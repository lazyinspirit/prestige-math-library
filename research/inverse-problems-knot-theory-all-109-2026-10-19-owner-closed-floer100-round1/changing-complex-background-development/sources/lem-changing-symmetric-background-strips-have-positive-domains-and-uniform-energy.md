## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix an actual complete pointed closed Heegaard diagram, a positive surface area form $\eta$, and one point $q_R$ in each complementary region, including its basepoint $z$. Let $j_a$ be a smooth compact finite-parameter family of positive surface structures and let $X_a=\operatorname{Sym}^g(\Sigma,j_a)$. Supply actual smooth relative transports $\Phi_a:X_*\to X_a$ with smooth equivariant ordered lifts, preserving the discriminant and every $E_{q_R}$, raw-identity near both tori and common exact generator charts. Such families may be constructed by the finite-parameter transport lemma along prescribed paths from a fixed reference parameter. No arbitrary boundary trivialization is presumed.

Let $a(s)$ be a smooth profile constant at both source ends. On $X_*$ consider an actual smooth finite-energy fixed-end solution
$$u_s+K_{s,t}(u)u_t=W_s(u),\qquad u(s,0)\in L_\alpha,\quad u(s,1)\in L_\beta.$$
Here $W$ is smooth, independent of $t$, compactly supported in $s$, zero near both tori and the common generator charts, and has a smooth permutation-equivariant ordered lift preserving all pairwise diagonals and coordinate incidence sets. The physical vertical structures $(\Phi_{a(s)})_*K_{s,t}$ equal $\operatorname{Sym}^g(j_{a(s)})$ near the protected discriminant and incidence divisors, tame the descended fixed product area form elsewhere, and have the stated common fixed full boundary structure and generator charts. Take uniform smooth bounds and taming constants on the actual compact parameter families under consideration. Uniform generator limits at both ends are required.

Every region multiplicity is nonnegative. There is a finite smooth branched ordered cover of the strip, and
$$E(u)\le C\left(\sum_R n_R(u)\operatorname{Area}_\eta(R)+\int_{\mathbb R}\bigl(|a'(s)|^2+\|\widetilde W_s\|_\infty^2\bigr)\,ds\right),$$
where $\widetilde W$ is the supplied ordered lift and $C$ is uniform on the stated compact families. If the fixed diagram is globally weakly admissible and $n_z=0$, only finitely many such integral domains occur across all endpoint pairs, without an index restriction; consequently these actual solutions have a uniform energy bound whenever the displayed forcing integral is uniformly bounded. Genus zero is the one-point case. No map counts, relative grading or invariance conclusion is included.

## Proof

**Given:** The actual data and Countable Choice in the Statement.

1.1 Pass to physical tuples $v=\Phi_{a(s)}u$. Their equation is $v_s+J_{s,t}v_t=B_s(v)$, where $B$ is the sum of the actual horizontal velocity of $\Phi_{a(s)}$ and $(\Phi_{a(s)})_*W_s$. By [F1] it has a smooth bounded equivariant ordered lift, with norm bounded by $C(|a'|+\|\widetilde W_s\|_\infty)$ and all prescribed coordinate incidences and diagonals invariant. In any relative support chart use its moving holomorphic coordinates $z_a$. The resulting coefficient equation is $c_s+i c_t=F(s,t,c)$ near the protected loci; the derivative of the coordinate with respect to $a$ is included in $F$, not discarded. Its ordered lift is again smooth and equivariant, with the same bound, since coordinate changes and their inverses have uniform derivatives on smaller compact charts. Every coordinate centered at $q_R$ keeps that point zero, so its corresponding lifted velocity vanishes when that coordinate is zero. [F1, A1, given, construct]

2.1 Prove the needed divisibility as a BOUNDED assertion, including higher collisions and arbitrary connection comparisons. Write the ordered velocity components as $V_i$. Swapping two roots gives $V_i(\mathbf z)=V_j(\operatorname{swap}_{ij}\mathbf z)$ by equivariance. The finite-dimensional mean-value inequality therefore gives $|V_i-V_j|\le C|z_i-z_j|$ on each smaller cluster chart. For the cluster discriminant $d=\prod_{i<j}(z_i-z_j)^2$, differentiation off its zero set gives $$\frac{V(d)}d=2\sum_{i<j}\frac{V_i-V_j}{z_i-z_j},$$ a uniformly bounded permutation-invariant function. It descends off the divisor and is defined to be zero on it, yielding a specified bounded measurable coefficient. The identity $V(d)=A d$ holds also at zeros: on the smooth discriminant part the field is tangent, and on its singular part the defining polynomial has zero differential. For an incidence equation $e=\prod_i z_i$, invariance first gives $V_i=0$ on $z_i=0$ where no other root is zero, then everywhere on that hyperplane by continuity. Hence $|V_i|\le C|z_i|$ and $V(e)/e=\sum_iV_i/z_i$ is bounded, with the same zero-set identity. Units from other support clusters add bounded logarithmic terms. Thus along the actual solution each local divisor equation satisfies $d_s+i d_t=A(s,t)d$, with locally bounded measurable coefficient, even when that coefficient has no smooth extension at collisions. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F2] in its scalar complex case. The local divisor equation is a continuous nonvanishing complex factor times a holomorphic scalar function. Its nonzero zeros are isolated and have positive integer local degree: the nonvanishing factor extends over the small disk and hence has zero boundary winding. Local containment propagates through each divisor neighborhood by this same factorization; a first exit from global containment is impossible, because the value at that point is still on the closed divisor and its protected neighborhood is open. Since both boundary tori and the uniform end generators miss every selected divisor and the discriminant, containment is excluded. Their preimages are consequently discrete. They lie in a bounded source interval by the uniform generator ends, and away from the physical boundary by compactness there and disjointness of the tori from the protected loci. Hence each preimage is finite. This proves nonnegative region multiplicities and the finite collision set without a transversality assumption. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Off that finite collision set take the cover of all ordered roots, of degree $d=g!$; disconnected components are allowed. A puncture has finite permutation monodromy, and each cycle is filled with a source coordinate $\pi(\zeta)=\zeta^k$. The cover is orientation preserving and holomorphic in its source coordinates. Root continuity supplies continuous bounded extensions: on a sufficiently small support disk all roots in that cluster converge to its single limiting support point. On the punctured cover, in the moving holomorphic target coordinates each root has bounded $\partial_{\bar\zeta}$ derivative prescribed by the actual smooth ordered forcing and the derivative of $\pi$. This distribution identity extends over the filled point: integrate against a test on a punctured disk, and the omitted boundary integral tends to zero by the root bound times its circle length. Subtract the [F2] Cauchy transform of that bounded right side. The remaining bounded distributionally holomorphic function is smooth and removable at the center. Thus the roots belong to every local $W^{1,p}$, $p>2$. Their coupled forced equation has smooth coefficients in all root variables; either repeated differentiation with the same Cauchy bounds, or [F2]'s continuous-weak smooth bootstrap on its graph almost complex structure, gives smoothness of the complete ordered lift. This argument does not assert smooth root extraction for arbitrary smooth polynomial coefficient maps. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 On this cover the lifted vertical structure is smooth and uniformly tame for $\Omega=\sum_i\eta_i$. Near collisions it is the product $j_{a(s)}$; off the protected diagonal neighborhood the quotient is a local finite covering on a compact set, so its inverse differential there has uniform bounds. The lifted equation is $f_s+\widetilde Jf_t=\widetilde B$. Tameness, bounded coefficients and Cauchy–Schwarz give $$c|f_t|^2\le\Omega(f_t,\widetilde Jf_t)=\Omega(f_s,f_t)-\Omega(\widetilde B,f_t),\qquad |df|^2\le C\bigl(\Omega(f_s,f_t)+|\widetilde B|^2\bigr).$$ The first identity fixes the sign convention $\Omega(w,Jw)>0$; absorb its last term by $xy\le\epsilon x^2+C_\epsilon y^2$, and use $f_s=-\widetilde Jf_t+\widetilde B$ for the second bound. Holomorphic source covering and conformal naturality multiply the integral forcing bound by $d$. The roots have finite energy on compact filled cores by step4.1 and on the tails by the local unramified generator charts and the assumed finite energy of $u$. The quotient differential itself is uniformly bounded in the actual compact relative charts, so $E(u)$ is bounded above by $C E(f)$ plus the same forcing integral. No inverse quotient bound at a collision is used. [F1, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The physical boundary roots lie on the ORIGINAL attaching circles, since the transports and forcing are raw-identity or zero near both tori. For a region $R$, choose a common regular value of the finitely many smooth root maps away from branch values. Their local product-definer identity counts exactly $d$ lifts of each physical incidence intersection. To identify this with the prescribed region coefficient, move that regular test point to $q_R$ along a finite path inside $R$. The incidence line construction works jointly in the surface-family parameter and the moving point: center each local root coordinate at that point, and its overlap ratios are the smooth nonvanishing unit products described below. Pull back this actual line family by the physical section $v(s,t)$. Its canonical section is nonzero on the boundary throughout the point path, because the path misses both attaching systems. Thus its relative degree is constant by the finite frame-winding argument in [F3], even though the transports need not preserve incidence divisors of UNCHOSEN points. At $q_R$ the chosen divisor is preserved by the transport and this degree is $n_R(u)$. Consequently the signed root-degree sum over $R$ is exactly $d n_R(u)$. The preimages of curve edges contribute zero two-form integral: the pullback vanishes on the rank-deficient set, and at a rank-two point the inverse image of an edge is a local smooth arc, covered by countably many source charts. Hence [F3]'s two-form degree formula over the regions gives $$\int_F\sum_i f_i^*\eta=d\sum_R n_R(u)\operatorname{Area}_\eta(R).$$ This is a signed topological identity, not a holomorphic-area assertion. Degrees agree with those on the fixed reference target, with an actual line-family construction as follows. In centered root coordinates the local incidence definer is the constant polynomial coefficient. On an overlap a root change $h_a$ fixes zero, so $h_a(z)/z$ is a nonvanishing holomorphic unit; the product of these units over the cluster is symmetric and has a smooth coefficient expression by [F1]'s contour transition formula. Noncontaining clusters supply ordinary nonvanishing units. These are actual smooth line transition functions on the relative family, with its canonical section and the physical incidence coorientation. Pull this line family back by the actual transports. Finite local projections and their smooth ODE trivialize it along each parameter path, taking the canonical nonzero section as the fixed frame on a common neighborhood of both tori. Such projections are obtained by embedding finitely many local frames with normalized finite chart weights into a trivial finite-rank bundle; projection onto their span is a finite invertible Gram-matrix formula. The same finite differentiated ODE proves the parameter-smooth identification. Relative boundary winding is invariant through this genuine line-family homotopy, by the frame argument in [F3]. Thus its degrees are those on the fixed reference target; raw identity near the tori retains the prescribed boundary frame. Truncate the strip in generator charts and cap by constants; their tail areas tend to zero by the actual finite root energy, and the caps contribute zero. Integrating step5.1 now proves the asserted energy bound, uniformly on the stated compact families. [F1, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 If $n_z=0$ and the diagram is globally weakly admissible, [F4] applies to these same actual domains: their boundary paths are on its fixed two circle systems, and their differences are normalized integral periodic chains. For clarity its real-cone step is essential: a nonzero nonnegative real periodic vector has a nonzero nonnegative rational vector in its rational face, and clearing denominators contradicts global weak admissibility. An unbounded normalized domain sequence, divided by its coefficient norm, would limit to such a vector; integral boundedness is finiteness. Positivity was proved in step3.1 before this use. There are finitely many endpoint pairs, so one finite domain set bounds all area sums without any index restriction. A uniform bound on the actual forcing integral then gives one energy bound by step6.1. For genus zero the target is a point, its operator and energies are zero and the claims are the literal empty sums. No moduli or invariance assertion has been used or deduced. [F3, F4, A1, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
