## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Suppose two complete globally weak pointed Heegaard diagrams for any closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold differ by a supplied attaching-circle isotopy avoiding the basepoint and the other circles of its own family. Their actual chosen hat complexes, with the auxiliary choices described below, have actual finite chain maps and inverse chain homotopies. The construction uses diagram transport on regular intervals and genuine triangle maps at finitely many births and deaths. The local inverse at a birth is an explicit unit-pair cancellation map composed with a finite action-triangular inverse. It does not assume that birth cancellation preserves counts or that arbitrary intermediate diagrams are admissible.

One fixed finite winding preparation makes the whole central isotopy globally weak. The preparation and its removal are themselves globally weak isotopies. All finite analytic comparisons are performed using the stated continuation suppliers and consistently retained actual generator charts. This proves the attaching-circle isotopy operation between globally weak hat diagrams for general Y, including both attaching families. It does not prove the sector-only input comparison, positive-basepoint minus invariance, absolute grading or naturality.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

1.1 Make the compact isotopy generic by an actual finite-dimensional perturbation relative its endpoint collars and marked-point neighborhoods. In finitely many graph charts write intersection with the fixed opposite curve as $f(t,\theta)=0$. Take finite chart bump functions and their coordinate products to embed the compact parameterized circles: a positive bump identifies its local chart, its coordinate products recover the point, and their differentials recover each tangent vector. These finitely many functions, together with polynomials through degree five in those coordinates and finite chart cutoffs, separates two-jets at each point and one-jets at any two distinct points: at two distinct embedded coordinate vectors choose a linear function taking distinct values and multiply its cubic interpolation factors by the local degree-two monomials. The conditions $f=f_\theta=0$ are therefore transverse on the two-dimensional parameter cylinder. The three conditions adding either $f_t=0$ or $f_{\theta\theta}=0$ are excluded, and the simultaneous two-point tangency condition has four equations on the three-dimensional configuration $(t,\theta,\theta')$, so is excluded as well. Apply [F2] on its countably many coordinate patches and take one common arbitrarily small perturbation. Compactness makes the tangency set finite, with distinct event times. Smallness retains the actual embedded circle family, its own-family disjointness and its avoided marks. Taylor expansion and the scalar parameter Morse completion give exactly the local ordinary fold $y=x^2-t$. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Prepare one uniformly admissible movie, rather than assuming all the original intermediate diagrams are admissible. Wind the FIXED opposite system using [F1]. Its paired dual loops can avoid the finite region representatives at BOTH endpoints. Choose the winding test points in open central plateaus of those annuli, away from the endpoints and common regular values of all the finitely many compact moving-curve trace maps $(t,\theta)\mapsto\Sigma$. Sard [F2] supplies such points. Their trace preimages are finite, so the multiplicities of every partial moving trace there are bounded by one finite integer K, uniformly in t. Homology relations among full circles remain constant along the isotopy. Since each complete family has independent homology classes, the moving boundary coefficients are rational linear functions of the fixed-system boundary coefficients, on their relation space; use a nonzero maximal minor. The original periodic evaluations are likewise bounded linear functions of these fixed coefficients by [F1]'s injectivity. Thus one finite C bounds every intermediate test evaluation by C times the maximum fixed boundary coefficient. At a time when a moving curve passes a test point use the two one-sided region evaluations; the extra jump is bounded by the same coefficient norm. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Take one integer $N>C+1$. The exact opposite winding traces of [F1] add $+Nb_i$ and $-Nb_i$ to the paired evaluations of every periodic domain, where $b_i$ is its fixed-system boundary coefficient. A nonzero domain has some $|b_i|$ maximal and positive, so the two resulting evaluations have opposite signs, uniformly along the WHOLE central path. If a selected point lies momentarily on a moving curve, sufficiently close one-sided points give the same strict signs. Hence the wound central movie is globally weak at every regular slice. The winding paths at the two ends are globally weak too: their supports avoid the selected old region representatives, so the inverse-trace correspondence leaves ALL their old coefficients unchanged at those points during EVERY partial winding. Nonnegativity would give an old nonnegative periodic domain, hence zero, and the inverse trace then makes the new one zero. Finally perturb the finite movies generically while retaining these open sign neighborhoods. The original endpoints are thus joined by winding, the uniformly weak central path and unwinding, all with finitely many ordinary events. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 On a closed interval containing no event, transverse intersection paths are smooth. Construct a compact surface vector field carrying the moving curves and preserving the fixed opposite curves SETWISE. At an intersection add a tangential velocity along the moving curve so that its total velocity is tangent to the fixed curve; transversality gives a unique scalar adjustment. In its local transverse axes extend that common prescribed value by compatible smooth fields tangent to the fixed axis and with the required normal component on the moving axis. Outside these finitely many patches use ordinary curve collars; a finite partition retains both linear conditions. The compact flow fixes the marks and transports both actual systems. By [F3] it gives an actual chain isomorphism with inverse for transported data. This proves the regular-interval operation without presuming that its action on homology is the identity. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 At one birth use the scalar normal form $y=x^2-t$ in a disk containing no other intersections or marks. The positive change of horizontal coordinate $x\mapsto x\sqrt{2R-x^2}$, with R fixed and the patch small, sends the parabola to the lower circle arc $y=R-\sqrt{R^2-x^2}-t$, while keeping horizontal lines horizontal. Use this circular-cap representative for the actual local complex model. A sufficiently small Hamiltonian displacement of the opposite horizontal arc crosses that cap once and creates exactly the same two new intersections. Its compensating zero-mean displacement can be supported in a second collar arc missing the moving family. The endpoint representatives are joined to those of the given fold by regular-interval isotopies, since no further tangency occurs after shrinking the two parameter intervals. Choose a positive area form annihilating all old periodic domains by the finite-simplex construction in [F4], and balance the small opposite-system displacement for this form. The old/new triple is globally weak: its periodic chains are old chains plus signed thin annuli; retained old-region points first kill the old part, and the two private lune signs kill each remaining coefficient. Thus [F5] supplies actual finite triangle maps and actual square-zero differentials for this local comparison. [F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 One surface tangency can create several generator pairs. Enumerate the finitely many complementary matchings by $r=1,\ldots,k$; write their new generators $a_r,b_r$, with the positive small bigon directed $a_r\to b_r$. The remaining coordinates agree in that pair. A strip supported entirely in the thin comparison collar is this small bigon with constant other roots: every other component of the moving curves in that collar has only one old crossing, so has no nonconstant two-ended root. For an index-one strip the local root has degree one by the scalar index computation in [F4]; its other roots are constant. Thus EVERY other strip contributing to the differential contains a fixed old-region core. Choose its area lower bound $c>0$, and take the birth small enough that all small-bigon areas $\delta_r$ and all nearest-triangle areas are below $c/(10\cdot2^{k+1})$. The bounded-density choice in [F4] makes this possible without shrinking c. The actual coefficient $a_r\to b_r$ is one; every other such domain would have the same tiny area, since periodic areas vanish, and could not contain that core. Its reverse and its diagonal coefficients vanish by negative or zero area. These assertions persist for small regular protected perturbations by the actual inverse and zero-mark compactness. Nonlocal entries between DIFFERENT pairs are retained rather than set to zero. [F4, F5, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Here is the actual one-pair algebra, applied while the other pairs remain in the complementary vector space O. If the chosen coefficient is one, its reverse and both diagonal coefficients are zero, write the CURRENT finite differential as $$do=Ao+a\lambda(o)+b\mu(o),\qquad da=u+b,\qquad db=v.$$ The equality $d^2=0$ gives $\mu(u)=\lambda(u)=0$, $Au=v$, and $\mu A=\lambda$. Put $D=A+u\mu$ on O, and set $$p(o)=o,\quad p(a)=0,\quad p(b)=u;\qquad i(o)=o+a\mu(o);\qquad h(b)=a,\quad h(a)=h(o)=0.$$ Direct multiplication gives $D^2=0$, $pd=Dp$, $di=iD$, $pi=I$, and $$ip+I=dh+hd.$$ On b the last equality is $u+b=da$, on a it is $a=h(u+b)$, and on O it is $a\mu(o)=hdo$. This identity concerns actual finite matrices and does not require the other new generators to be absent. [F5, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Cancel the pairs successively using step 6.1. Its formula $D=A+u\mu$ expresses each new coefficient as a finite sum of directed paths of ORIGINAL differential entries, with one subtraction of the small-bigon area for each eliminated pair. Paths may revisit an earlier eliminated pair, so count these occurrences: each cancellation concatenates two previous paths and adds one reversed unit edge. Starting with zero such edges, after j cancellations there are at most $2^j-1$. Thus every coefficient path subtracts at most $(2^k-1)\max\delta_r$. A path which uses only local small bigons cannot pass between distinct pairs. Hence every correction to a remaining pair coefficient, its reverse or its diagonal includes a nonlocal original entry of area at least c. Its total area is at least $c-2^{k+1}\max\delta_r>c/2$. Areas telescope along the path, since the original periodic areas vanish. Such a correction cannot have the required area $\delta_s$, $-\delta_s$ or zero. Thus each remaining unit coefficient stays one and its reverse and diagonal coefficients stay zero. This proves inductively that ALL k cancellations are defined. Denote their composite projection and inclusion by P and I, and their final differential on the OLD generator space by D. Composing the displayed single-pair homotopies gives an actual finite K with $$PI=I_{\mathrm{old}},\qquad IP+I_{\mathrm{new}}=dK+Kd.$$ For two cancellations the homotopy is $h_1+i_1h_2p_1$; finite induction proves the formula. No block of nonlocal inter-pair entries has been discarded. [F4, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Let H be the actual small-isotopy triangle map from the original old complex to the new one, with its two-lune top cycle. It is a chain map by [F5]. Set $T=PH$. Its nearest old-generator coefficient is one by [F4]'s actual triangle inverse. A triangle to ANY $b_r$ cannot stay in the thin opposite-system collar: the isolated tangent component has no old crossing, whereas every other moving-curve collar component has only its one old crossing. It therefore contains an old-region core, as does every other nonnearest direct old-output triangle. Every additional term in PH is a path of such a triangle and original positive strips, subtracting at most $2^{k+1}$ small-bigon areas (the same two-path recurrence bounds the composite projection). It still has core cost at least $c-2^{k+1}\max\delta_r>c/2$; nearest triangles have smaller area. Old periodic areas vanish, so these differences define relative action on each finite old connectivity component. More explicitly any cycle of nonnearest transitions, closed by the reverse nearest triangles, would be a normalized periodic domain of strictly positive area, contrary to the chosen zero periodic areas. Therefore $T=I_0+N$, where $I_0$ is the nearest-generator bijection and $I_0^{-1}N$ is strictly action triangular on a finite set. The finite geometric series is a literal chain inverse of T. This proves the needed assertion after ALL cancellations, not merely one surface pair. [F4, F5, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 The actual triangle map H now has explicit inverse $T^{-1}P$. Its left composite is literally the identity. Put $X=HT^{-1}P$ on the new complex. Then $PX=P$; using step 7.1 gives $$X+I=d(KX+K)+(KX+K)d.$$ Indeed $(IP+I)X+(IP+I)=X+I$, since $IPX=IP$. This proves both inverse assertions with every complementary matching included. A death uses the inverse maps and homotopies. All sums, cancellations and inverses are finite actual count matrices; no moving-boundary compactification has been substituted for the calculation. [step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 The finite concatenation can be made with actual analytic comparisons satisfying their stated hypotheses. First fix all local geometric intervals and their finitely many balanced area forms. At each regular interval transport the actual generator charts by the flow of step 4.1. At a birth its new normal-form patch misses every existing crossing, so its local complex model may be inserted while retaining all those chart germs; retain the new pair's charts in the next interval. Thus shared endpoint diagrams have common prescribed actual generator charts. Choose the finite regular perturbations close enough to their standard symmetric references that each is tame for ALL the finitely many positive area forms that will be used there; standard surface complex structures are compatible with every positive surface area form, and compactness gives one positive margin for this finite choice. At a shared endpoint first use the fixed-background full-boundary theorem in [F5] to align each actual boundary tensor with one common compatible tensor, retaining the prescribed right-angle charts of this external pair. Then its changing-background theorem compares the backgrounds with that boundary tensor, the common fixed area form and the retained charts. Internal auxiliary-circle pairs keep their arbitrary actual angles and require no such external chart change. No independence under a previously unsupported change of generator charts or arbitrary simultaneous change of area forms is asserted. [F3, F4, F5, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 9.1, construct, algebra]

11.1 Compose the finitely many regular-interval isomorphisms, birth/death equivalences and analytic comparisons. Compose their literal inverse maps in reverse order. For two consecutive maps the inverse homotopies are obtained by adding the first homotopy to the conjugate of the second by the intervening chain maps; direct expansion of $gf+I=dK+Kd$ verifies this rule, and finite induction gives the asserted global inverse homotopies. For the other attaching family conjugate the source and surface structures and interchange the families as in the actual handleslide proof [F4]. This preserves domains, operator indices, counts and homotopies and gives the other-family movie. Genus zero has no attaching-circle isotopy and gives the identity. This proves exactly the stated globally weak hat operation. It does not convert ordinary sector weakness into global weakness without an actual sector-relative comparison. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, construct, algebra] ∎
