## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. There is an explicit genus-two complete pointed handleslide model $(\Sigma,\boldsymbol\beta,\boldsymbol\gamma,z,j)$ with four generators $x=(x_1^{\pm},x_2^{\pm})$ and the following properties. Both systems bound complete disk systems of the same handlebody; $\gamma_1$ is the actual slide of $\beta_1$ over $\beta_2$, and $\gamma_2$ is an isotopic copy of $\beta_2$. Their normalized periodic lattice has basis
$$P_1=D_1-D_2-D_3,\qquad P_2=D_3-D_4,$$
where $D_1,D_3,D_4$ are bigons, $D_2$ is an annulus with four corners, and the remaining region contains $z$. In particular the diagram is globally weakly admissible.

For $J=\operatorname{Sym}^2(j)$ the two annular strip domains $D_2+D_3,D_2+D_4$, from $(x_1^+,x_2^+)$ to $(x_1^-,x_2^+)$, have actual pretranslation Fredholm index one. Every actual representative is regular, and their rigid translation-quotient counts are finite and have sum one modulo two. The same assertion holds with the second fixed coordinate $x_2^-$ instead. These are actual holomorphic strips, not merely smooth Whitney-domain representatives. The bigon counts are one. The only possible nonconstant index-zero domain is $D_2$, and it has no holomorphic representative in the chosen model.

Consequently the actual hat differential is zero on all four generators. The relative index grading is the number of plus coordinates, and $\Theta_{\beta\gamma}=x_1^+\times x_2^+$ is its top cycle. These actual index-one counts persist for sufficiently small regular protected perturbations of the constant integrable path. Adding standard separated two-intersection handle factors gives the corresponding top-cycle calculation in every genus $g\ge2$. No handleslide map, polygon identity, absolute grading or positive-basepoint minus invariance is inferred without its actual additional proof.

## Proof

1.1 Fix a sufficiently large integer $L$ and put $R=(4L^2-1)^{1/2}$. In the plane take unit circles $\beta_1,\beta_2$ centered at $-L,L$, the circle $\gamma_1$ centered at $L$ of radius $R$, and the unit circle $\gamma_2$ centered at $L-\sqrt2$. Then $\beta_1,\gamma_1$ are orthogonal, as are $\beta_2,\gamma_2$; the other opposite pairs are disjoint. Both right-hand unit circles lie strictly inside $\gamma_1$. Delete tiny round holes around $-L+1/2$ and $L-1/\sqrt2$, lying respectively inside $\beta_1\cap\gamma_1$ and $\beta_2\cap\gamma_2$, and two other tiny holes far below all four circles. Join corresponding positive and negative rims by the holomorphic annular plumbing coordinates $uv=\epsilon$. This is an actual genus-two complex surface. Its standard ball-with-two-handles filling makes the beta circles a complete disk system. Join the two beta meridians by a straight thin real-axis band and take its outer parallel boundary. This dumbbell boundary and gamma1 are symmetric upper/lower positive graphs on an interval. Reparametrize their horizontal intervals and interpolate their positive heights away from rounded endpoint charts, retaining the two tiny positive-hole neighborhoods and the whole beta2 disk inside. The rounded endpoint profiles can be chosen equal before this interpolation. This is an actual smooth curve isotopy avoiding all four holes and beta2; its finite graph velocity extends by collar cutoffs to a supported plane flow. Thus gamma1 is the actual slide boundary followed by an allowed isotopy, not only the same homology class. Gamma2 is the direct small-disk isotope of beta2 retaining its positive hole. Choose z in the outside region. [F7, given, construct]

2.1 The five regions are $D_1=\operatorname{int}\beta_1\setminus\overline{\operatorname{int}\gamma_1}$, $D_3=\operatorname{int}\gamma_2\setminus\overline{\operatorname{int}\beta_2}$, $D_4=\operatorname{int}\beta_2\setminus\overline{\operatorname{int}\gamma_2}$, $D_2=\operatorname{int}\gamma_1\setminus\overline{\operatorname{int}\beta_1\cup\operatorname{int}\beta_2\cup\operatorname{int}\gamma_2}$, and the connected remaining region $D_5$. The glued holes connect its three planar pieces. The first, third and fourth are ordinary bigons; $D_2$ is an annulus with four convex right-angle corners. A full-circle boundary chain on the planar part is a sum of the four circle-interior indicators plus a constant. Equality at the paired hole rims gives $b_1+g_1=0$, $b_2+g_1+g_2=0$. Normalize its D5 coefficient to zero. Its integral basis is therefore $P_1=1_{\beta_1}+1_{\beta_2}-1_{\gamma_1}$ and $P_2=1_{\gamma_2}-1_{\beta_2}$, the displayed chains. The coefficients of $aP_1+bP_2$ are $(a,-a,-a+b,-b,0)$. Nonnegativity forces $a=b=0$, and the same applies to its negative. This proves global weakness. Label the crossings so that the positive bigon strips go from $x_i^+$ to $x_i^-$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $A=D_2$, let h have boundary values0 on its outer component and1 on its inner component, and let C be its total positive inner flux. [F1] constructs h; circular reflection and the power coordinate at a right-angle corner give finite flux and smooth extension in the corresponding corner chart. Write $\nu_1,\nu_2$ for $2\pi/C$ times the absolute flux on its beta1 outer arc and beta2 inner arc. We choose L so that $\nu_1<\nu_2$, and prove the inequality rather than assume it generic. Put $K=\overline{B(L,1)}\cup\overline{B(L-\sqrt2,1)}\subset B(L,3)$ and $v=1-h$. Radial logarithmic comparison gives $h\le\log(R/|z-L|)/\log(R/3)$. Inside $|z-L|<R-3$, compare h from below with $\log((R-3)/|z-L|)/\log(R-3)$; it is at most1 on K and zero on that outer comparison circle. Hence, on the fixed collar $4<|z-L|<6$, $0\le v\le C_0/\log R$, while $v\ge c_0/\log R$ on the radius5 circle. The interior derivative estimate [F1] gives $C\le C_1/\log R$ by evaluating the flux on that circle. On $B(L,5)\setminus K$ compare v with $c_0/\log R$ times its fixed harmonic measure0 on K,1 on the radius5 circle. Hopf on a fixed open beta2 subarc gives beta2 flux at least $c_1/\log R$; thus $C\ge c_1/\log R$ and $\nu_2\ge c_2>0$. Near the beta1 outer arc $|z-L|\ge R-C_2$, so the first comparison bounds h by $C_3/(R\log R)$. The reflection/corner charts there have fixed uniform size and bounds as $L\to\infty$; their derivative estimate bounds its beta1 flux by $C_4/(R\log R)$. Consequently $\nu_1\le C_5/R$. Fix an integer L making this smaller than $c_2$. All constants in the fixed inner comparisons are independent of L. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 Consider first the domain $D_2+D_3$. Start at $x_2^+$ and cut along a proper initial segment of the gamma2 arc in this domain, ending at a tip with parameter $0<t<1$; parameter1 is the other crossing. Its normalization $B_t$ is an annulus. The second family, for $D_2+D_4$, cuts instead along beta2. Let $H_t$ be bounded harmonic with boundary value0 on beta edges and1 on gamma edges. It is obtained from a prescribed increasing sequence of continuous boundary functions tending to the indicated edge values away from the four corners, applying [F1]. The bounded increasing harmonic solutions converge locally uniformly: Harnack applied to their nonnegative differences controls each compact disk by their convergent center values. The limit is harmonic by its circle mean values. The maximum principle and logarithmic barriers at the four jump corners give uniqueness, so no sequence of arbitrary Perron witnesses is chosen. At such a corner subtract its explicit angle function. Reflection gives the holomorphic remainder and hence a primitive with its prescribed logarithmic leading term. At the slit tip the square-root coordinate makes the boundary smooth with a constant edge value. The closed conjugate differential of $H_t$ has one independent period $G(t)$. Its vanishing is exactly the existence of a single-valued holomorphic $p_t$ with imaginary part $H_t$, unique up to a real constant: cut the annulus along one transverse arc, integrate there by Green's identity, and the rejoining jump is that period. 4.1 At a zero of G, the strong principle gives $0<\operatorname{Im}p_t<1$. Hopf in each smooth normalized boundary chart, including the slit-tip chart, makes its boundary derivative nonzero. The four corner logarithms give two negative and two positive strip ends, one of each on each boundary component. Compactness away from the corners makes p proper. A sufficiently negative strip value has one simple inverse near each negative corner, by exponentiating the nonzero leading logarithm; there are no others by compactness of the remaining domain. Thus its degree is2. A finite tree joining its critical values to the base boundary gives $\chi(B_t)=2\chi(\overline S)-\sum(r-1)$ by lifted cells; hence the total branch defect is2. A degree-two map has only simple branch points, so there are two. Assigning the two target points above each strip point gives the actual holomorphic map $u:S\to\operatorname{Sym}^2\Sigma$. At a branch point its elementary symmetric coordinates are holomorphic, and at the tip the ordinary root coordinate is $a+\zeta^2$. Its ends decay exponentially by the corner logarithms. It is therefore a smooth finite-energy strip of the required domain. Real additive constants of p are exactly its longitudinal translations. [F1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

4.2 Let $h_t$ be ordinary harmonic measure0 on the outer component of $B_t$ and1 on the inner, and $C_t>0$ its total inner flux. Let $f_1(t),f_2(t)$ be the absolute beta-edge fluxes multiplied by $2\pi/C_t$. Green's identity gives $G(t)=C_t(f_1(t)-f_2(t))/(2\pi)$, choosing the inner-period orientation. Compute it first on interior parallels and truncate each of the four jump corners in its fixed angle coordinate. The two opposite logarithmic edge divergences at each corner cancel with the SAME cutoff radius; the radial derivative of the leading angle function on its truncation arc is zero. The remaining arc terms tend to zero, because h_t has the same constant value on the two adjoining edges and its reflected remainder has positive power order. Thus the right boundary integral is the finite integral of the beta/gamma indicator against the flux of h_t: the outer and inner total fluxes cancel, leaving exactly the displayed difference. No separately divergent edge flux of H_t was assigned a value. When the first slit shrinks, $f_2\to2\pi$; for the complementary slit $f_2\to0$. In either case the fixed outer beta arc has angle strictly between0 and$2\pi$. At the other endpoint the annulus tends to A and the detached cap, so $(f_1,f_2)\to(\nu_1,\nu_2)$. Here is an explicit justification of these limits. A changed slit or a shrinking attachment gap lies in a disk of radius r about one boundary point a. On every remaining compact subdomain the difference of the bounded harmonic measures is bounded by $\log(C/|z-a|)/\log(C/r)$. The maximum principle gives convergence to h_A on A and to1 on the cap, or to the uncut annulus measure at the shrinking-slit endpoint. Reflection gives smooth convergence along the fixed outer boundary, including its fixed corner charts, hence convergence of total flux. The inner fluxes are positive measures. Their limits away from the shrinking disk already have that total mass, so no mass can remain as an atom there. All marked arc fluxes therefore converge. By step3.1 the two families have signs $(-,-)$ and $(+,-)$ at their endpoints. [F1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Conversely take any actual strip in either stated annulus domain. [F3] gives its two-sheet incidence cover with holomorphic root maps. Their combined degree is1 over every point of the prescribed domain and0 outside it. Positivity excludes interior ramification or two interior preimages of a generic root value. Every nonconstant root component is therefore an interior embedding, and distinct components have disjoint open images. If its base-cover degree were1 it would be a disk, and its embedded interior could not contain that essential loop. It uses at least2 sheets; the TOTAL budget is2, so it uses all sheets. There is no constant sheet or second active component. Its interior has genus0, since it embeds in the target annulus. It has at most2 boundary components, by its proper degree2 base cover, and at least2 because of the essential loop. Hence it is an annulus, each boundary component covering the base boundary once. 5.2 Compute the ACTUAL index, not merely the dimension of the parameter set. The incidence base cover has its two positive simple branch points from step4.1; [F4]'s squared determinant model contributes2. A nonzero boundary-tangent field on an annulus contributes no Euler defect; it can be supplied by [F7]'s annulus parametrization, rotating its sign through the radial coordinate to match both boundary orientations. There is no interior root-map branch. The four convex generator sectors give the fixed-end corner correction -2: in the two negative-end factors the actual cap minus the rounded tangent turn is $-\pi$ in line angle, while the positive-end factors give zero. This follows directly from the cap angles, just as for the strip end; a continuous change of transverse end angles inside $(0,\pi)$ leaves the Fourier gap nonzero and changes neither integer. At the slit tip choose a half-plane coordinate with root map $g(\zeta)=a+\zeta^2$ and field constant there. On a clockwise inward indentation its pushed squared derivative phase makes one negative full turn, whereas the actual physical circle tangent line has constant squared phase. Their difference contributes +1. Finite excision therefore gives the actual pretranslation index $2-2+1=1$. 6.1 The incidence annulus in step 5.1 has an outer boundary which has exactly the two prescribed beta1/gamma1 generator corners; the inner has exactly two prescribed beta2/gamma2 corners, BOTH at the same fixed crossing. All boundary changes of attaching-family label occur at those four corners. The only attaching-curve portion INTERNAL to the target domain is the indicated crosscut. 7.1 An ACTUAL $H^1$ kernel section is smooth by the local weak Cauchy--Riemann bootstrap. Express it as the infinitesimal base-coordinate change $\delta p$ at a fixed root point. At an interior cover branch with local equation $z=a^b$, a symmetric-coordinate variation $\delta F$ gives $\delta p(a)=\delta F(a,a^b)$. Grouping power-series exponents into their b residue classes proves that no holomorphic infinitesimal section is lost at that collision. At the moving slit tip its only possible singularity is a real-coefficient simple pole in the square-root coordinate; its coefficient is the tip displacement times a nonzero factor, since p has nonzero tip derivative. At each fixed generator corner it is bounded: the actual twisted Fourier expansion of the kernel section decays at least as fast as the root map's first corner exponent, so multiplication by $dp/dg$, whose leading order is the inverse root displacement, stays bounded. The common logarithmic term has fixed coefficient. Elsewhere $\operatorname{Im}\delta p=0$ on both physical boundary lines. Thus these statements hold for the actual Hilbert kernel, not just for formal slit functions. [F2, F4, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 A single-valued meromorphic real-boundary function on this annulus cannot have just one nonzero boundary simple pole. The imaginary part of any candidate function differs from a real multiple of this kernel by a bounded zero-boundary harmonic function and hence is exactly that multiple. But the conjugate differential of a single-valued holomorphic function has zero period on the other boundary. Its nonzero kernel flux forces that real multiple to vanish. Apply this to $\delta p$. Its remaining imaginary part is zero by the maximum principle, so it is a real constant, exactly longitudinal translation. Conversely the actual translation section lies in $H^1$ by the same corner exponents. Its kernel is precisely one-dimensional. Step5.2's actual Fredholm index proves surjectivity.
