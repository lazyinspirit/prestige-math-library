## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. In the explicit genus-two circular handleslide model of the named supplier, take a sufficiently small upward circle translate $\delta_1$ of $\beta_1$ and downward circle translate $\delta_2$ of $\beta_2$. There are choices of the top crossings and of sufficiently small circle translates $\gamma'_i$ of $\gamma_i$ such that the two products
$$F_{\beta\gamma\delta}(\Theta_{\beta\gamma},\Theta_{\gamma\delta})=\Theta_{\beta\delta},\qquad F_{\gamma\delta\gamma'}(\Theta_{\gamma\delta},\Theta_{\delta\gamma'})=\Theta_{\gamma\gamma'}$$
hold for the actual index-zero, zero-basepoint triangle counts in the integrable model and for all sufficiently small regular protected coherent perturbations. Here the top generator is the one defined by the actual relative pair index, and the first input is traversed in reverse at its incoming boundary corner: the positive boundary order for $F_{ABC}$ is $A,C,B$.

Each contributing integrable product triangle is the unique representative of its positive domain, has an actual boundedly invertible $H^1\to L^2$ linearization, and contributes one over $\mathbb F_2$. No triangle translation quotient is taken. The transverse circle angles may be arbitrary elements of $(0,\pi)$; no holomorphic right-angle normalization is asserted. Adding separated two-intersection handle factors gives both identities in every genus $g\ge2$. The products here are the specified finite local triangle counts. Their use as chain maps and associativity identities requires the actual polygon analytic theorem, not merely this model calculation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The circular model, its five regions, its actual strip indices and top-generator labels. All circle translates are chosen small enough to retain the stated region cores and avoid the marked holes.

[F1] The actual circular model has periodic basis $P_1=I_{\beta_1}+I_{\beta_2}-I_{\gamma_1}$, $P_2=I_{\gamma_2}-I_{\beta_2}$, both of index zero. Its bigon counts, annular counts, actual relative grading and finite-energy end models have been proved. Here $I_c$ denotes the plane interior indicator of the circle $c$. ([[lem-circular-handleslide-slit-annuli-give-actual-top-cycles]])

[F2] The Countable-Choice gradient Hilbert construction solves the zero-Dirichlet correction of a compactly supported smooth forcing; its local weak bootstrap and reflected harmonic estimates give the smooth open-edge and straight-sector estimates. ([[lem-round-planar-real-boundary-pole-problem-has-a-bounded-normalized-response]])

[F3] The actual compact scalar disk Riemann--Hilbert operator with boundary $T\mathbb S^1$ is onto of index three and has kernel $a+ibw-\overline a w^2$. The whole-strip operator with any two transverse constant real lines has a bounded inverse, with the corresponding positive angular gap and Fourier end estimates. ([[lem-scalar-disk-riemann-hilbert-fredholm-index]], [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]])

[F4] Relative indices of finite-ended actual operators are differences of their commonly capped determinant windings; transverse sewing adds actual operator indices without a component-surjectivity assumption. Equal-domain winding differences are computed by labelled boundary paths; a zero-domain sphere has determinant degree zero. ## Proof

1.1 Choose the translates so that the two desired first-product regions are the ordinary triangles $$\Delta_1=I_{\beta_1}I_{\delta_1}(1-I_{\gamma_1}),\qquad \Delta_2=I_{\beta_2}I_{\delta_2}(1-I_{\gamma_2}).$$ They are trimmed portions of the disjoint bigons D1 and D4. Their boundary orientations are beta,delta,gamma, precisely the positive order for the first displayed product. This also specifies the two beta-delta crossings to be used. Each is a top crossing: in its two-lune pair it is the source of both positive bigons. For the reversed gamma-delta pair use the crossing which becomes the bottom crossing before reversing the two boundaries; the strip transformation $(s,t)\mapsto(-s,1-t)$ transposes the relative pair grading. This gives exactly the two gamma-delta vertices of the displayed triangles. All angles are strictly between zero and pi. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 Let $\Delta$ be one of these circular triangles. Construct its conformal parameter under Countable Choice. Fix an interior point a and a cutoff chi equal to one near a and zero near the boundary. Put $g_0=\chi\log|z-a|$. Its classical Laplacian away a is smooth and compactly supported. Apply the gradient-Hilbert construction in [F2] on this actual triangle: define $H^1_0(\Delta)$ as the closure of its compact smooth functions, extend them by zero into a fixed box, and integrate one coordinate derivative to obtain Poincare. Gradient-norm Hilbert representation therefore solves $\Delta v=\Delta g_0$ for that ordinary forcing with zero trace. Put $G=g_0-v$. Then G is harmonic off a, has its logarithmic pole, has zero boundary values and is negative inside by the maximum principle. Its conjugate differential has period $2\pi$ about a and no other periods, so $q=\exp(G+iG^*)$ is a single-valued proper holomorphic map to the disk with one simple zero. Its degree is one and it is biholomorphic. On an open edge, weak tangential bootstrap and Hopf give a nonzero boundary derivative. At a vertex a Mobius coordinate sends its two intersecting circles to rays. The sector power transports the finite Dirichlet-energy harmonic zero-edge remainder to a half-disk; odd reflection is a weak finite-energy harmonic function and its central point is removable by the cutoff energy identity. The interior weak bootstrap in [F2] then makes it smooth. Hopf on this half-disk gives the same nonzero derivative. Thus its inverse f has the nonzero leading power $(w-r_j)^{\theta_j/\pi}$ at vertex j. A disk automorphism matching the three ordered boundary vertices supplies the unique marked triangle. This argument uses the proved weak Dirichlet construction, not a full-Choice Riemann mapping theorem. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For each additional handle use four unit circles whose centers on the real axis are $0,\epsilon,2\epsilon,3\epsilon$, labelled beta,gamma,delta,gamma-prime. Put its positive plumbing hole in their common interior and its negative hole in their common exterior, avoiding the thin regions. Each pair has its top crossing on the upper semicircle: both oriented positive bigons start there. The upper triangle for the first three circles is the component of $I_\gamma(1-I_\beta)(1-I_\delta)$ between their three upper crossings; its positive order is beta,delta,gamma. The second three circles have the identical translated construction. For the first triple its periodic basis is $I_\gamma-I_\beta,I_\delta-I_\beta$. Outside the reference triangle, a beta-only far-left patch gives $-a-b\ge0$, a delta-only far-right patch gives $b\ge0$, and the gamma-only patch at its LOWER central arc gives $a\ge0$. Thus $a=b=0$, proving positive-domain uniqueness and global weakness. The same test applies to the translated second triple. The paired holes and the common outside component separate these factors from the genus-two model. Their top triangles and pair bigons therefore add no periodic ambiguity. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 For the gamma-delta pair, the labels and actual counts are transported from [F1] by a genuine small-deformation argument. A smooth surface flow tending to the identity sends the two delta circles to beta and preserves the gamma circles SETWISE: at their finitely many transverse intersections adjust tangential velocities on gamma, then extend in the transverse axes and along the remaining collars. Its pullback complex structures converge smoothly to the original one, with the actual end angles converging inside $(0,\pi)$. The finite positive-domain list and energy bound, absence of zero-mark bubbles and the positive-index integrable limit levels in [F1] give one-level compactness for index-one strips, and exclude nonconstant nonpositive-index strips. Whole-strip inverses with the converging angles promote core convergence to the global chart. The actual onto model strips therefore persist uniquely by their phase-slice inverse, without assuming that curve deformation preserves counts by definition. In particular this pair has the same zero periodic indices and top cycle as the model. Apply the same argument to its sufficiently small gamma-prime perturbations. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 The normalized integral triply-periodic lattice has basis $P_1,P_2,Q_1,Q_2$, where $Q_i=I_{\delta_i}-I_{\beta_i}$. This follows by the same paired-hole equations as for [F1], followed by subtraction of the delta-beta boundary coefficients; there is no rational division. Let $D=\Delta_1+\Delta_2$. If $D+aP_1+bP_2+cQ_1+dQ_2$ is nonnegative, a retained D2 core gives $a\le0$. The delta1-only upper-left thin lune gives $c\ge0$, while the beta1-only lower-left lune gives $a-c\ge0$. Thus $a=c=0$. A retained D3 core gives $b\ge0$; the delta2-only lower-right lune gives $d\ge0$, while the beta2-only upper-right lune gives $-b-d\ge0$. Thus $b=d=0$. Consequently D is the only positive domain for the prescribed top output. The same witness points applied to a periodic domain with zero reference chain force all four coefficients to vanish, proving global weakness of this triple. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.4 Choose the directions of gamma-prime translation so the second-product triangles are $$\Delta'_1=I_{\delta_1}(1-I_{\gamma_1})(1-I_{\gamma'_1}),\qquad \Delta'_2=I_{\gamma_2}I_{\gamma'_2}(1-I_{\delta_2}).$$ These are trimmed D1 and D3, with positive edge order gamma,gamma-prime,delta and the required top vertices. Its periodic basis is $\widetilde P_1=I_{\delta_1}+I_{\delta_2}-I_{\gamma_1}$, $\widetilde P_2=I_{\gamma_2}-I_{\delta_2}$, and $Q'_1,Q'_2$, with $Q'_i=I_{\gamma'_i}-I_{\gamma_i}$. For a nonnegative $D'+a\widetilde P_1+b\widetilde P_2+cQ'_1+dQ'_2$, a D2 core gives $a\le0$; a gamma-prime1-only strip inside delta1 gives $a+c\ge0$, and a gamma1-only strip gives $-c\ge0$. Hence $a=c=0$. A D4 core gives $b\le0$; a gamma2-only strip outside delta2 gives $b-d\ge0$, while a gamma-prime2-only strip gives $d\ge0$. Hence $b=d=0$. Again the positive domain is unique and the same zero-reference tests prove global weakness. These are separate tests for the second product, not an assumed symmetry of the first. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.5 Prove actual operator invertibility. At each end the target Mobius chart gives the exact operator $D_0$ with its actual two constant transverse lines. For a given $L^2$ forcing, extend its end part by zero to the whole strip, apply [F3]'s bounded inverse, and cut off the result. Pulling back its holomorphic target frame adds no zeroth-order error: $D(h'(u)\xi)=h'(u)D\xi$ since $Du=0$. Subtraction leaves a compactly supported forcing. For this residual write $\xi=f'\eta$ on the disk. Its boundary condition is $\eta\in T\mathbb S^1$. By [F3] solve the compact operator, whose kernel is $\eta=a+ibw-\overline a w^2$. The residual forcing is zero near all three marked boundary points, so its solutions are holomorphic and reflected there; their evaluations are bounded by local reflected Cauchy estimates, not by a nonexistent arbitrary $H^1$ point trace. Evaluation of the three-real-dimensional kernel at the three tangent fibers is an isomorphism: after division by $iw$ its columns are $1,\cos\vartheta,\sin\vartheta$, and three distinct circle points are not collinear. Subtract the matching kernel vector. Now eta vanishes at the three marks, and $\xi=f'\eta$ has the positive corner exponents and bounded cylindrical $H^1$ norm. This is a bounded right inverse. Conversely an actual homogeneous xi gives a holomorphic eta whose Fourier end bounds make it extend and vanish at those three marks. It is in the displayed kernel, hence zero. The right inverse is the actual inverse, and the triangle has index zero. Higher realizations follow from the same end inverses and weak local bootstrap. [F2, F3, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 A product of the two triangles, and of the additional handle triangles in step 2.1 when present, is off the diagonal, and its operator is the direct sum of the inverses in step 2.5. It contributes one. Conversely any actual representative of D has its finite root cover from [F5]. The combined root degree is one on each triangle and zero outside their disjoint closures. A nonconstant root component cannot cross their separating degree-zero open set. Each triangle must be covered by one active component, and each requires at least one base sheet. The total sheet budget is exactly the number of these disjoint triangles, so each uses exactly one and there are no constant components. Its root map has degree one and is the biholomorphism of step 1.2; its base cover has degree one as well. Therefore the product just constructed is the only representative. The same reasoning applies to D-prime. [F5, step 2.1, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 1.2, step 2.5, construct, algebra]

4.1 There are no other index-zero output terms. The first pair periodic indices P1,P2 are zero by [F1], and the second pair indices tilde-P1,tilde-P2 are zero by step 2.2. Each Q is the difference of the two actual index-one bigons in its two-lune pair. Insert these actual smooth based strip differences at the corresponding incoming or outgoing vertex of the reference triangle. Common-cap excision in [F4] adds their zero indices. Every remaining equal-domain ambiguity also has index zero, for the following explicit extension of the equal-domain proof. Its local coefficient-jump/Stokes test gives zero winding difference on EACH labelled boundary path on each of the three edges. Interpolate their real argument lifts relative endpoints. Join the three edge homotopies by their CONSTANT corner tracks, giving a homotopy of the ENTIRE boundary circle. Gluing the two disks to the two ends of this boundary cylinder gives a sphere, not a three-interval gluing with an introduced source handle. The cylinder lies in the boundary tori and misses all region divisors, so the sphere has every region degree zero. Its determinant degree is zero by [F4]. The common three cap arcs cancel, so their actual finite-ended indices agree. This repeats the full labelled-path and sphere proof with three edges; it does not infer a polygon index formula merely from the strip Statement. Thus the displayed periodic generators exhaust the relative ambiguities with zero triangle-index change. Glue to the triangle of step 3.1 a smooth realized sequence of the ordinary output bigons taking its top output to any other generator. [F4] gives its index equal to the number of lost plus coordinates. Every other realized triangle with that output has the same relative index, because its domain differs by the displayed periodic basis and the common-cap calculation. A lower output therefore has strictly positive index and contributes nothing to the index-zero map. The argument applies to both products. [F1, F4, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Finally take sufficiently small regular protected perturbations of the integrable data. Global weakness and [F5] give a uniform energy bound. Any limiting representative of D or D-prime cannot have a nonconstant strip break: across the third-family edge of each triangle its coefficient must be zero for a strip whose boundary uses only the other two families; propagating that zero through the triangle forces the whole supported strip domain to vanish. The triangles are disjoint, so the same argument applies separately to each one. There is no conformal degeneration for a three-punctured source and no nonconstant bubble by the zero-basepoint condition. Compactness thus gives only the unique product triangle, with exact end inverses promoting core convergence to its global Hilbert chart. Its bounded inverse in step 3.1 gives one and only one perturbed solution. Positive-domain uniqueness and the relative-index calculation are unchanged, so no lower-output index-zero term appears. This proves both displayed finite model products and their perturbative count assertions, without assuming the separate chain-map or associativity theorem. [F4, F5, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 2.5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
