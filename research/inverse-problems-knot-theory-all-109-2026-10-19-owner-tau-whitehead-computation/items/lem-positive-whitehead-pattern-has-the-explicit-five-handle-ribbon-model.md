## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Construct an oriented closed surface $C$ from the following signed ribbon diagram. At an intersection of two oriented curves listed in the order $(c_1,c_2)$ and having sign $s\in\{+1,-1\}$, the positive cyclic order of outgoing germs is $(c_1+,c_2s,c_1-,c_2(-s))$. The curve cycles are
$$\begin{array}{c|l}\gamma&Q,n\\\lambda&Q,b,a,a',b'\\\alpha_1&d,e,r,m\\\alpha_2&g,p,l\\\alpha_3&f,q\\\alpha_4&c,h,k\\ N&g,h\\\beta_1&m,n,k,l\\\beta_2&p,q,r\\\beta_3&e,a,f,a'\\\beta_4&d,b,c,b'.\end{array}$$
At $Q$ use the order $(\gamma,\lambda)$ and sign $+1$; at $n$ use $(\gamma,\beta_1)$ and sign $-1$. At every other point use its alpha curve (including $\lambda$) first and beta curve (including $N$) second, with signs
$$\begin{array}{c|rrrrrrrrrrrrrrrr}\text{point}&a&a'&b&b'&c&d&e&f&g&h&k&l&m&p&q&r\\\hline\text{sign}&-&+&-&+&-&-&-&-&-&+&+&-&-&-&+&-.\end{array}$$
Thicken this finite oriented ribbon graph and cap each of its ten boundary cycles by a disk. The resulting surface has genus five. Attach compression two-handles along $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4$ on one side and $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_4$ on the other. The resulting compact oriented manifold has two torus boundary components. With the outer torus marked by $(\gamma,\lambda)$ and the inner meridian marked by $N$, it is orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic to the exterior of the positive Whitehead pattern in a solid torus $V$. Here $\gamma$ is the longitude of $V$ and $\lambda$ its meridian. Thus inserting $V$ around any oriented companion knot with its Seifert zero framing gives the exterior of $D_+(K,0)$, with the actual positive clasp and no extra longitudinal twist.

Meridional filling of the inner boundary and marks on opposite sides of a short $N$ arc give actual doubly pointed bridge data for the filled pattern. The usual connected surface $\Sigma\#C$ with mixed attaching curves $\lambda\#m$ and $\gamma\#l$ admits actual product compression disks when $(m,l)$ are the meridian and zero longitude of the companion exterior. This geometric assertion supplies neither any holomorphic count nor an absolute Alexander evaluation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The displayed finite signed ribbon data, with the indicated cap disks. No three-manifold or knot is identified merely from a group presentation.

[F1] In an oriented annulus with local coordinates $(s,\theta)$ and normal height $z$, the band $B(s,u)=(s,u\cos2\pi s,C\sin\pi s-u\sin2\pi s)$, $C>2a$, $|u|\le a$, attaches to the two different annulus boundaries. Its two boundary crossings are positive and its boundary is precisely the positive Whitehead pattern in the annulus framing. ([[lem-positive-whitehead-clasp-band-gives-genus-one-surface]])

[F2] Smooth torus curves and annulus strips admit finite coordinate normalization and prescribed collar extensions. ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-torus-curves-and-annulus-strips]])

## Proof

1.1 The ribbon vertex/edge/face calculation is literal. Every one of the eighteen vertices has four germs, giving thirty-six edges; following an edge and then the next positive germ gives ten boundary cycles. Hence capping gives $\chi(C)=18-36+10=-8$ and genus five. The generic vertices belong to exactly the two named curves, while $Q$ is the single gamma/lambda intersection. The curves in either alpha or beta family are mutually disjoint. The following explicit reductions will identify the partial compressions geometrically, rather than infer their topology from this Euler count. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Use the following local band rule for a cancelling pair $A_i,B_j$ meeting once at $v$. An intersection $p$ of another beta curve with $A_i$ can be cleared by sliding that beta curve over $B_j$ along an empty $A_i$ interval from $p$ to $v$, of oriented direction $\delta=\pm1$. The copied $B_j$ has orientation $\sigma=-s(p)s(v)$ and is pushed to its left side with sign $\epsilon=\delta s(v)$. At another $B_j$ intersection $x$, insert its copy before $x$ on the alpha circle when $-\epsilon s(x)<0$, and after otherwise; its intersection sign is $\sigma s(x)$. Indeed the band arrives at $v$ from displacement $-\delta A_i'$, whose side relative to $B_j'$ is $\delta s(v)$, and its transverse displacement along the other alpha curve has sign $-\epsilon s(x)$. The old $p$ and the copied $v$ then bound the band-strip bigon and cancel. After all other beta curves are cleared, slide the remaining alpha curves over $A_i$ along the adjacent $B_j$ intervals; $A_i$ then has no other beta crossings, so these dual slides simply remove the original $B_j$ points. The remaining one/one handle pair is the product cancelling pair. On its rectangular handle coordinates this deletes a ball with a collar and restores that collar. Thus this is an actual relative handle move, fixed on the negative boundary tori, not a relator substitution. [given, construct, algebra]

1.3 Construct a genuine positive-band exterior to compare with this reduced diagram. Work in $V=S^1_\theta\times D^2_{(s,z)}$, oriented by $ds\wedge d\theta\wedge dz$, with annulus $0\le s\le1,z=0$. Choose $0<a\ll1$ and $2a<C\ll1$, and use [F1]'s band in the local theta interval. Let $H$ be a rounded regular neighborhood of two parallel core circles just outside the annulus rims and the arched joining edge $I_B(s)=(s,0,C\sin\pi s)$. At the bridge use normal disk coordinates $(\theta,z-C\sin\pi s)$ of radius $a$, so the two band edges lie on its boundary with angular parameters $\psi=-2\pi s$ and $\psi=\pi-2\pi s$. At the ends choose the core tubes with their inner-facing boundaries at $s=0,1,z=0$; the finite vertex rounding carries these boundary arcs and the band corners. Thus the positive Whitehead boundary $L$ lies on $\partial H$. The disk $$D_X=\{(s,\theta,C\sin\pi s):0\le s\le1,\ a\le\theta\le2\pi-a\}$$ is proper in $V\setminus H$: its two long edges lie on the core tubes and its two short edges on the bridge tube at $\theta=\pm a$. Its interior misses all three tubes. Adding its product neighborhood to $H$ gives a regular neighborhood of the annulus $I_B\times S^1$, a core solid torus. The vertical flow equal to $-C\sin\pi s\,\partial_z$ near this annulus and cut off away from its compact track straightens it; its neighborhood stays in the product-coordinate disk. Its complement in $V$ is $T^2\times I$. Consequently $V\setminus H$ is the outer-torus compressionbody with single compression disk $D_X$. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply step 1.2 to $(\alpha_3,\beta_3)$ at $f$, clearing $q$ with direction $+1$; to $(\alpha_4,\beta_4)$ at $c$, clearing $h$ then $k$ with directions $-1,+1$; and to $(\alpha_2,\beta_2)$ at $p$, clearing $g$ then $l$ with directions $+1,-1$. The displayed cycles make each chosen interval empty when it is used. Keep a subscript recording the cleared point on each copied intersection. Direct insertion gives respectively surface genera four, three and two. The final $N$ cycle is $(r_g,a_{qg},e_{qg},a'_{qg},b'_h,d_h,b_h)$. Three successive pairs $(a'_{qg},b'_h)$, $(e_{qg},d_h)$, $(a_{qg},b_h)$ are actual two-edge face cycles: their endpoints are consecutive on both participating curves with opposite signs, and following the positive germs returns after those two edges. Push across these three empty disk faces. Thus no free-word cancellation is being used in place of a geometric bigon. Abbreviate $d_k=D$, $e_{ql}=E$, $r_g=T$, $r_l=R$, $b_k=B$, $a_{ql}=A$, $a'_{ql}=A'$, $b'_k=B'$. The reduced cycles are $$X=(D,E,T,R,m),\quad\gamma=(Q,n),\quad\lambda=(Q,B,A,A',B'),\quad N=(T),\quad J=(m,n,B',D,B,R,A,E,A').$$ Their signs are $X\cap J:(D-,E+,R+,m-)$, $X\cap N:T+$, $\lambda\cap J:(B-,A+,A'-,B'+)$, $\gamma\cap J:n-$, and $\gamma\cap\lambda:Q+$. These finite lists reproduce every surviving port, including the peripheral curves. [step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

2.2 Let $D_0,D_1$ be the meridian disks of the two core tubes at $\theta=\pi$. Band-sum them along the boundary arc which follows the first core from $\pi$ down to $a$, then the $u=a$ band edge, then the second core up from $a$ to $\pi$. Push this narrow band just inside $H$. Its resulting disk $D_J$ is disjoint from $L$: the two original transverse intersections with $L$ have opposite signs and its band follows the intervening $L$ arc. This is a disk slide of the standard two-disk system of the handcuff handlebody $H$. A parallel copy $D_N$ of $D_1$ is disjoint from $D_J$, meets $L$ once, and together with $D_J$ cuts $H$ into the original cut ball. Therefore cutting $H$ along $D_J$ gives a solid torus with meridian $D_N$, and $L$ is its core after a collar push, since it meets this meridian once. This constructs the inner-torus compressionbody, its actual disk $D_J$ and the specified inner meridian, without identifying a knot from its group. [F1, F2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 Here is the complete port trace in these product coordinates. Orient $X=\partial D_X$ by $ds\wedge d\theta$. In the bridge cylinder $\Sigma=\partial(V\setminus H)$ has orientation $ds\wedge d\psi$; its $\psi=0$ edge goes in increasing $s$ and its $\psi=\pi$ edge in decreasing $s$. Choose a narrow $D_J$ band of angular half-width $\delta$, and a smaller push of the long $X$ edges inside its removed meridian-disk footprints. No extra meridian-circle intersection remains there. Its two bridge edges have $\psi=-2\pi s+\delta$ in increasing $s$ and $\psi=-2\pi s-\delta$ in decreasing $s$. Write $u_0,u_1$ for their crossings of $\psi=0$ and $v_0,v_1$ for their crossings of $\psi=\pi$, respectively. Then $$X=(u_0,u_1,T,v_0,v_1),\qquad J=(u_0,L_1,v_0,L_2,u_1,L_3,v_1,L_4,n).$$ The $X/J$ signs are $(-,+,+,-)$ in the order $(u_0,u_1,v_0,v_1)$, by the determinants of $(\pm\partial_s,(1,-2\pi))$ or $(\pm\partial_s,(-1,2\pi))$. Take $\lambda$ to be the boundary of the bridge-and-two-disks cross-section $\theta=0$, with the positive meridian orientation, and $\gamma$ the positive core longitude on the outer face of the first tube. On the upper bridge edge lambda goes in increasing $s$ and on the lower in decreasing $s$. Thus $$\lambda=(Q,L_3,L_2,L_1,L_4),\qquad\gamma=(Q,n).$$ The lambda/J signs at $(L_1,L_2,L_3,L_4)$ are $(+,-,+,-)$, the gamma/J sign is $n+$, and gamma/lambda has sign $Q+$. For example the two lambda crossings on each band edge occur at $s=1/4\pm\delta/(2\pi)$ and $3/4\pm\delta/(2\pi)$; this fixes their order. Orient $N=\partial D_N$ so that its sole $X$ crossing $T$ is negative. These computations include every port and both peripheral directions. [step 1.3, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Identify this product diagram with the reduced signed diagram by $$u_0\mapsto m,\ u_1\mapsto R,\ v_0\mapsto E,\ v_1\mapsto D,\quad L_1\mapsto A',\ L_2\mapsto A,\ L_3\mapsto B,\ L_4\mapsto B'.$$ Reverse the parametrizations of $X$ and $J$, retain gamma, lambda and $N$. Step 3.1 then gives exactly every cycle and every oriented intersection in step 2.1. This is a signed ribbon isomorphism, not just equality of words. Extend it over the vertex disks and edge strips. The shared graph has genus two and nine boundary cycles; its complement in each genus-two surface is nine disks, since their total Euler characteristic is nine with nine boundary components. The cap disks were specified with product collars, so the isomorphism extends over them by their disk parameters. It extends across the compression two-handles by the same disk and normal interval coordinates. Hence it identifies the actual two compressionbodies and their negative torus boundaries. Reversing the three moves of step 2.1 now identifies the original four-disk-per-side exterior with the positive-band exterior, preserving its peripheral curves. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The longitude in step 3.1 is the positive $S^1_\theta$ direction on a product solid torus. Its product parallel has linking framing zero after that torus is inserted using the companion Seifert framing. Lambda is its actual meridian; no replacement $\gamma\mapsto\gamma+k\lambda$ has occurred, because the full marked cycles were retained in steps 2.1 and 4.1. The knot in step 1.3 is literally the boundary of the zero-framed companion annulus with [F1]'s positive band. Gluing this marked solid torus to any supplied companion neighborhood therefore gives exactly $D_+(K,0)$, including clasp sign and framing. The proof has not used an Alexander polynomial, a homology rank or a peripheral group isomorphism to identify it. [F1, step 1.3, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The companion connected-surface construction has equally explicit disks. In the boundary collar identify the two marked tori by the meridian/zero-longitude gluing. Delete a small disk around their common meridian-longitude crossing and connect the two level surfaces by its product tube. The complementary collar is $(T^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2)\times I$, a genus-two handlebody. Its two product disks are $a\times I$ and $b\times I$, where $a,b$ are disjoint proper arcs cutting the punctured torus to a disk. Their boundaries are precisely $\lambda\#m$ and $\gamma\#l$, with the common crossing opened at the tube, so they are disjoint. Together with the existing compression disks on both sides these cut the actual glued exterior into its prescribed product pieces. Finally fill the inner torus along $N$. In its cut solid torus choose the straight core arc and its product boundary-parallel witness disk; a short opposite bridge across the $N$ interval lies in the other compressionbody and misses its disks. Place $w,z$ on its two sides, and round the two endpoint jets in disjoint balls. These are actual bridge witnesses for the filling core. The exterior identification above carries that core to the indicated positive Whitehead pattern. Thus the doubled-pointed and companion clauses follow as well. No holomorphic or absolute-grading comparison has been asserted. [F2, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
