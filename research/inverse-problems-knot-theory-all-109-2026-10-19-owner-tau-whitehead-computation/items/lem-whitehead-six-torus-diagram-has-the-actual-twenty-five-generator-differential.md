## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the smoothed explicit diagram of the geometric-admission lemma, its intersection labels $x_i$ at $(X_i,0)$ on a fixed lift, and the time-independent integrable structure $j=-i$ in physical $x+iy$ coordinates. Its actual Maslov-index-one strips are regular. There are exactly thirty-six translation orbits, one for each term in the following full filtered minus differential over $\mathbb F_2[U]$:
$$\begin{aligned} \partial x_2&=x_1+Ux_5,&\partial x_3&=Ux_2+x_4,&\partial x_4&=Ux_1+U^2x_5,\\ \partial x_r&=x_{r-1}+x_{r+3},&\partial x_{r+1}&=Ux_r+x_{r+2},&\partial x_{r+2}&=U(x_{r-1}+x_{r+3})\quad(r=6,10,14,18),\\ \partial x_{22}&=x_{25}+Ux_{21},&\partial x_{23}&=Ux_{22}+x_{24},&\partial x_{24}&=Ux_{25}+U^2x_{21}. \end{aligned}$$
The other columns vanish. All $z$ multiplicities are retained. The relative Alexander values can be centered by $A(x_1)=0$ so that $A=1$ on $x_2,x_6,x_{10},x_{14},x_{18},x_{22}$, $A=-1$ on $x_4,x_8,x_{12},x_{16},x_{20},x_{24}$, and $A=0$ on the others. With this specified centering, the actual hat complex has one-dimensional homology, detected by the chain map $p(x_i)=1$ for $i=5,9,13,17,21$ and $p(x_i)=0$ otherwise. Its inclusion threshold is zero.

The centering here is a relative grading choice. No absolute Alexander normalization, auxiliary-data or diagram invariance, two-twisted Whitehead calculation, or skein-triangle map is asserted.

## Proof

1.1 Retain the surface orientation, rather than silently reversing source and target. Its positive complex coordinate is $x-iy$. A canonical strip $u$ satisfies $u_s-i u_t=0$ in physical coordinates, with alpha below and beta above. Put $v(s,t)=u(s,1-t)$. Then $v_s+i v_t=0$, its lower boundary lies on horizontal beta and its upper boundary on the wavy alpha. Its negative and positive limits remain the source and target. The domain reflection and target orientation reversal also preserve each positive divisor multiplicity. This is an isomorphism of the actual real linearized operators. A positively oriented physical domain therefore has alpha boundary from target to source and horizontal beta boundary from source to target. On the universal cover, every endpoint pair has exactly one relative class: changing alpha and beta arcs changes their displacement by independent vertical and horizontal periods, and closure determines both; $\pi_2(T^2)=0$ leaves no additional two-sphere class. The unique proper alpha lift meeting beta-zero at all twenty-five $X_i$ gives these arcs explicitly. A self-endpoint class is the constant class and has zero area, so contributes no nonconstant strip. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We prove the scalar zero formula before assuming transversality or embeddedness. For a nonconstant integrable strip $v$, let $\tau=v_s$. It is holomorphic. Its lower boundary is real, and its upper boundary lies in $e^{i\theta(s)}\mathbb R$, where $\theta(s)$ is a continuous lift of the actual alpha tangent-line angle, constant for sufficiently large $|s|$ because the curves are exactly straight near the generators. The end convergence and exact Fourier theorem [F2] apply. Write $\theta_-=a\pi+r_-$ and $\theta_+=b\pi+r_+$, with $0<r_\pm<\pi$. The first nonzero end modes of $\tau$ have rates $$\lambda_-=r_-+k_-\pi>0,\qquad \lambda_+=r_+-\pi-k_+\pi<0,\qquad k_-,k_+\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.$$ Boundary zeros away from these straight charts also have finite integer order: extend the smooth tangent angle on a small real interval to a compactly supported smooth real function $f$ on the line. The holomorphic Cauchy integral $G(z)=\pi^{-1}\int f(t)/(t-z)\,dt$ has imaginary trace $f$ from the upper half-plane. Subtracting the finite Taylor polynomial in its local principal-value integrals proves smoothness of all boundary derivatives. Thus $e^{-G}\tau$ has real trace and extends by Schwarz reflection. Its zeros are isolated integer-order zeros unless $\tau$ vanishes identically. The same argument works in the lower-half chart at the upper edge. There are consequently finitely many boundary and interior zeros on a compact strip core and none sufficiently far out the ends. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Denote their total multiplicities by $Z_\partial$ and $Z_{\mathrm{int}}$. Apply the ordinary argument principle on a truncated strip, indenting clockwise around every boundary zero. Each such indentation contributes $-m\pi$ for a zero of order $m$; each interior zero contributes $2m\pi$. On the upper edge, traversed from right to left, the net nonzero tangent-line phase is $\theta_- -\theta_+$. The right and left end segments contribute $\lambda_+$ and $-\lambda_-$ by their actual leading Fourier modes. Hence $$2\pi Z_{\mathrm{int}}=\lambda_+-\lambda_-+\theta_- -\theta_+-\pi Z_\partial.$$ The scalar Fredholm formula [F2] gives $\mu=a-b$, so this identity is $$\mu=1+k_-+k_++Z_\partial+2Z_{\mathrm{int}}.$$ In particular an index-one strip has no derivative zero and both minimal end modes. Its boundary is monotone along each lifted curve; its two end sectors have angles strictly between zero and $\pi$. The total turning of the closed lifted boundary, with those convex corners, is one. This argument did not assume regularity and does not conclude that an arbitrary unbranched immersion is embedded. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We now perform the finite global-image exclusion, including immersed candidates. For each unordered pair among the twenty-five crossings, take the arc on the proper alpha lift and its horizontal closing segment, in both directions. Keep it only if both corner determinants are positive and its tangent turning is one. Compute turning by recording crossings of the ray $(7,1)$ by consecutive edge vectors: for a vector $(a,b)$ use coordinates $(7a+b,7b-a)$ and count signed crossings of the positive first axis. No edge vector lies on this ray and no consecutive vectors are opposite. Smoothing preserves this integer. Of the $600$ oriented boundaries, exactly $96$ survive these two tests. A boundary is Jordan precisely when none of its intermediate alpha–beta crossings lies in the open horizontal closing interval; alpha itself is embedded by [F1]. Exactly $36$ survive this test. For each of the other $60$, evaluate winding at $((X_j+X_{j+1})/2,\epsilon/100)$, in lexicographic order $j=1,\ldots,24$ and $\epsilon=1,-1$, stopping at the first negative value. Every one has a value $-1$. Take the corner smoothing disks also disjoint from these finitely many rational test points; sufficiently small disks have this property. The winding evaluation is the integer sum over edges $a\to b$ of $+1$ if $a_y\le q_y<b_y$ and $\det(b-a,q-a)>0$, of $-1$ if $b_y\le q_y<a_y$ and that determinant is negative, and zero otherwise. These are finite rational tests on the Statement's twenty-six vertices and their necessary vertical translates. The accompanying exact certificate records all sixty first witnesses and all thirty-six polygons; the rules above specify and reproduce every entry. A holomorphic strip has nonnegative winding, equal to its local degree at every regular interior point by the argument principle. Thus all sixty non-Jordan candidates are excluded, without a false assertion that unbranched holomorphic immersions are globally embedded. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For completeness each remaining smooth Jordan bigon has an actual conformal strip parametrization under Countable Choice. Solve the harmonic problem with boundary value zero on the horizontal edge and one on the alpha edge, using increasing continuous boundary data that vanish in shrinking neighborhoods of the two corners. Perron [F3], monotonicity, the interior Poisson formula and bounded convergence give a harmonic limit $H$ strictly between zero and one, with the indicated edge limits. Smooth edge pieces have exterior-circle logarithmic barriers. They give $|H-c|\le C\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,\partial\Omega)$ locally on an edge with value $c$, hence the interior gradient estimate gives bounded gradient there. This gives genuine local $H^1$ regularity up to that edge. Flatten the edge by its actual smooth graph chart. Its zero-boundary remainder satisfies $\partial_i(a^{ij}\partial_j h)=0$, with a smooth symmetric uniformly positive matrix. Testing with $-D_{-q}(\eta^2D_qh)$ in a tangent direction gives $$\lambda\|\eta\nabla D_qh\|_2^2\le C(\|\nabla h\|_2^2+\|D_qh\|_2^2)+\tfrac\lambda2\|\eta\nabla D_qh\|_2^2.$$ The fundamental theorem bounds the last zeroth-order difference quotient by $\|\partial_s h\|_2$. A sequence $q\to0$ and weak subsequence in the explicit separable Hilbert space therefore give the two derivatives with at most one normal derivative. The equation recovers the second normal derivative since $a^{22}\ge\lambda$. Repeating with differentiated equations and nested cutoffs gives every Sobolev order. Countable Choice suffices for these sequences and Hilbert projections [F2]. Explicit extension across a half-plane by $\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}c_j h(s,-jn)$, with $\sum c_j(-j)^r=1$ for $0\le r\le m$, followed by Fourier Cauchy–Schwarz for $m>k+1$, gives $C^k$ regularity. This proves smoothness up to the edge and justifies applying Hopf [F3]; it does not import a full-choice boundary-regularity theorem. [F2, F3, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The bigon is simply connected, so integrate $H_y\,dx-H_x\,dy$ to obtain its single-valued conjugate $K$, and put $F=K+iH$. At each corner the two edges are straight. If its angle is $\omega\in(0,\pi)$, subtract the angular harmonic function with the same zero/one side values and use $z^{\pi/\omega}$; odd reflection gives a bounded harmonic function with a removable corner. Thus $$F=\pm\omega^{-1}\log z+c+O(|z|^{\pi/\omega}),$$ with the opposite signs at the two corners and the differentiated versions of this expansion. Consequently $F$ maps properly to $\mathbb R\times(0,1)$; every sufficiently far negative real point has exactly one preimage by this expansion and the argument principle. Proper holomorphic degree is therefore one, so $F$ is biholomorphic. Its inverse gives a finite-energy strip with the specified source and target, unique up to real translation: two such maps differ by a strip automorphism fixing its ordered ends, and exponentiation reduces that automorphism to a positive dilation of the upper half-plane fixing zero and infinity. Its two end modes are minimal, and its derivative has no zeros, so step 3.1 gives index one. Smooth edge extension and Hopf from step 5.1 make this an actual smooth strip up to both boundaries. [F3, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Each index-one strip is regular. For an actual $H^1$ kernel section $\xi$ of its integrable linearization, the quotient $h=\xi/\tau$ is holomorphic, has real trace on both edges, and has no poles because $\tau$ has no zero. Its boundedness on compact cores follows from local real-boundary reflection in the gauge of step 2.1. At the ends [F2]'s Fourier series show that $\xi$ has no slower allowable decaying mode than the minimal mode of $\tau$; their quotient is bounded and has real limits. The imaginary part of $h$ has zero boundary values and tends to zero at both ends. The maximum principle on expanding rectangles gives $\operatorname{Im}h=0$ and hence $h$ is a real constant. Thus the real kernel is exactly the translation line. Its Fredholm index one and closed range [F2] give zero cokernel. The inverse on the translation-fixed complement is a genuine bounded inverse. This is a proof for the autonomous integrable operator; no multiplication of kernel sections for a non-complex-linear time-dependent operator has been used. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 It remains to count the marks in the thirty-six actual domains, not merely identify their shapes. For each positive polygon use the winding rule of step 4.1 at every $w+(25m,16n)$ and $z+(25m,16n)$ in its bounding box. The first six arrows are $$4\to1:(1,0),\quad2\to1:(0,1),\quad3\to4:(0,1),\quad4\to5:(2,1),\quad3\to2:(1,0),\quad2\to5:(1,2),$$ where parentheses are $(n_w,n_z)$. For each $r=6,10,14,18$ the six arrows are $$r\to r-1:(0,1),\ r\to r+3:(0,1),\ r+1\to r:(1,0),\ r+1\to r+2:(0,1),\ r+2\to r-1:(1,0),\ r+2\to r+3:(1,0).$$ The final six are $$24\to21:(2,1),\ 22\to21:(1,2),\ 23\to24:(0,1),\ 24\to25:(1,0),\ 23\to22:(1,0),\ 22\to25:(0,1).$$ The displayed marks and polygons lie within the finite ranges $|m|\le5,|n|\le8$; bounding boxes exclude all others. Each orbit counts one by steps 6.1 and 5.1. Weighting by $U^{n_w}$ gives exactly the stated matrix, including both $U^2$ end terms. This establishes actual counts for this chosen datum; the equality is not inferred from the paper's matrix. [F1, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 The relative Alexander equations $A(x)-A(y)=n_z-n_w$ in this list give precisely the centered values in the Statement, and multiplication by $U$ lowers them by one. Their consistency can be checked on every listed arrow. This arrow graph is connected; concatenating its classes and their reverses gives every endpoint class by uniqueness in step 1.1. Additivity of winding then gives the same relative equation for all classes, not only the counted arrows. [F4] or direct multiplication gives $\partial^2=0$, so this regular finite count defines the actual filtered complex for the chosen datum. After $U=0$, the functional $p$ in the Statement kills every differential: the only columns involving its nonzero coordinates contain the pairs $(x_5+x_9),(x_9+x_{13}),(x_{13}+x_{17}),(x_{17}+x_{21})$. It sends the cycle $x_5$ to one and detects the unique hat homology line by [F4]. Every centered negative generator is the boundary of $x_3,x_7,x_{11},x_{15},x_{19},x_{23}$, respectively, whereas $x_5$ already lies at level zero. This proves the threshold statement for the specified relative centering. It proves no missing absolute normalization or two-twist skein operation. [F4, step 8.1, construct, algebra] ∎
