## Statement

Assume the hypotheses of
[[def-latent-variable-joint-and-observed-data-factorization]]. Then
$m(\theta,x)=\int p(\theta,x,z)\,\mu(dz)$ is jointly measurable in
$(\theta,x)$. For each fixed $\theta$, it is an observed-data probability
density: it is $\mathcal A$-measurable and
$$\int_{\mathsf X}m(\theta,x)\,\nu(dx)=1.$$
Consequently $m(\theta,x)<\infty$ for $\nu$-almost every $x$. On every valid
fibre $0<m(\theta,x)<\infty$, $q(z\mid\theta,x)=p(\theta,x,z)/m(\theta,x)$ is
a measurable $\mu$-density of total mass one. Finally, if $h:\Theta\times\mathsf X\times\mathsf Z\to\mathbb C$ is product
measurable and
$\int |h(\theta,x,z)|p(\theta,x,z)\,d(\nu\otimes\mu)<\infty$ for a fixed
$\theta$, Fubini permits exchanging the latent and observed integrals of
$hp$. No exchange is claimed without this absolute integrability.

## Facts & Assumptions


**Given:** The parameter and data spaces, sigma-finite measures $\nu,\mu$,
jointly measurable normalized $p$, and, for the last clause, a product
measurable complex-valued $h$ with $\int |h|p<\infty$.

[F1] [[def-latent-variable-joint-and-observed-data-factorization]]: $p$ is
jointly measurable, each fixed-$\theta$ joint density integrates to one,
$m(\theta,x)=\int p(\theta,x,z)\,\mu(dz)$, and $q=p/m$ is defined only when
$0<m<\infty$.

[F2] [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]: a uniformly sigma-finite
kernel comes with a specified exhaustion $(Z_n)$ for which each kernel mass
on $Z_n$ is finite.

[F3] [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]]: integrating a
nonnegative product-measurable function against a finite or uniformly
sigma-finite kernel gives a measurable function of the parameter.

[F4] [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: for
nonnegative product-measurable $p$, its section integrals are measurable and
the iterated and product integrals agree.

[F5] [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]: for an
$L^1(\nu\otimes\mu)$ function, almost every section is integrable and the
iterated integrals equal the product integral.

[F6] [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]: pointwise
order and multiplication by a positive finite scalar pass to nonnegative
integrals.

[F7] [[thm-of-archimedean]]: the positive integers are unbounded in $\mathbb R$.

[F8] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra-on-the-extended-real-line]]: the extended-real
Borel structure contains $[0,\infty]$ and its Borel subsets.

[F9] [[thm-threshold-characterisations-of-real-and-extended-real-measurability]]:
an extended-real function is measurable if all strict upper threshold preimages
are measurable.

[F10] [[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]:
composition of a measurable map with a Borel measurable outer map is measurable.

[F11] [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]:
products of extended-real measurable functions are measurable under its stated
zero-times-infinity convention.

[F12] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: the
finite product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles.

[F13] [[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]: sigma-finiteness
provides a countable measurable exhaustion by sets of finite measure.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $\theta$ and let $\mathcal D_\theta$ be the collection of subsets $E\subseteq\Theta\times\mathsf X\times\mathsf Z$ whose section $E_\theta:=\{(x,z):(\theta,x,z)\in E\}$ belongs to $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal C$. Sections commute with complements and countable unions, so $\mathcal D_\theta$ is a sigma-algebra. It contains each measurable rectangle $T_0\times A_0\times C_0$, whose section is $A_0\times C_0$ if $\theta\in T_0$ and empty otherwise. By [F12], every product-measurable set has a measurable section. Applying this to preimages of Borel sets shows that $p_\theta(x,z):=p(\theta,x,z)$ is product-measurable. For complex $h$, first compose with the continuous Borel coordinate maps $\operatorname{Re},\operatorname{Im}:\mathbb C\to\mathbb R$; the same section argument makes both $\operatorname{Re}h_\theta$ and $\operatorname{Im}h_\theta$ measurable, hence $h_\theta$ is complex-measurable. [F10, F12, given]

1.2 By [F13], choose a measurable exhaustion $Z_n\uparrow\mathsf Z$ with $\mu(Z_n)<\infty$. On $S:=\Theta\times\mathsf X$, the constant assignment $K(s,B):=\mu(B)$ is a measure kernel: each section is $\mu$ and each evaluation map is constant. The same exhaustion makes it uniformly sigma-finite by [F2]. [F2, F13, given, choose]

2.1 For fixed $\theta$, step 1.1 makes $p_\theta$ product-measurable, so Tonelli [F4] gives $$\int_{\mathsf X}m(\theta,x)\,\nu(dx)=\int_{\mathsf X\times\mathsf Z}p_\theta(x,z)\,d(\nu\otimes\mu)=1$$ by [F1]. Thus $m(\theta,\cdot)$ is a probability density. Also $E_\theta:=\{(x,z):p_\theta(x,z)=\infty\}$ is measurable. For every positive integer $n$, $n\mathbf1_{E_\theta}\le p_\theta$, so [F6] gives $n(\nu\otimes\mu)(E_\theta)\le1$. Since this holds for every $n$, [F7] implies $(\nu\otimes\mu)(E_\theta)=0$. [F1, F4, F6, F7, given, step 1.1]

2.2 The function $p$ is nonnegative and product-measurable by [F1]. Under the finite-product identification $S\times\mathsf Z=(\Theta\times\mathsf X)\times\mathsf Z$ from [F12], apply [F3] to the constant kernel $K$ from step 1.2. It follows that $s\mapsto\int p(s,z)\,\mu(dz)=m(s)$ is measurable on $S$. [F1, F3, F12, step 1.2]

3.1 For fixed $\theta$, write $u_\theta=\operatorname{Re}h_\theta$ and $v_\theta=\operatorname{Im}h_\theta$. Define the finite-valued measurable function $\widetilde p_\theta:=p_\theta\mathbf1_{\{p_\theta<\infty\}}$ and then $$H_\theta:=u_\theta\widetilde p_\theta+i v_\theta\widetilde p_\theta.$$ By [F11] this is complex-valued product-measurable; it is defined to be zero on the null set where $p_\theta=\infty$. It agrees with $h_\theta p_\theta$ off that null set, and the assumed absolute integrability gives $\int|H_\theta|\,d(\nu\otimes\mu)<\infty$. Fubini [F5] applies to this $L^1$ representative and gives $$\int_{\mathsf X\times\mathsf Z}H_\theta\,d(\nu\otimes\mu)=\int_{\mathsf X}\left(\int_{\mathsf Z}H_\theta(x,z)\,\mu(dz)\right)\nu(dx)=\int_{\mathsf Z}\left(\int_{\mathsf X}H_\theta(x,z)\,\nu(dx)\right)\mu(dz),$$ with its zero assignment on exceptional null sections. Since $H_\theta=hp$ almost everywhere on the fixed-$\theta$ product space, these are the asserted exchanges for $hp$. [F5, F11, given, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 Let $E:=\{x:m(\theta,x)=\infty\}$, measurable by step 2.2. For each positive integer $n$, $n\mathbf1_E\le m(\theta,\cdot)$, so [F6] and step 2.1 give $n\nu(E)\le1$. Since this holds for every $n$, [F7] forces $\nu(E)=0$. If $0<m(\theta,x)<\infty$, division by that scalar and the definition of $m$ give $\int q(z\mid\theta,x)\,\mu(dz)=m(\theta,x)/m(\theta,x)=1$. The valid-fibre set $V=m^{-1}((0,\infty))$ is measurable by step 2.2 and [F8]. For measurability, define $\rho:[0,\infty]\to[0,\infty]$ by $\rho(t)=1/t$ for finite $t>0$ and $\rho(0)=\rho(+\infty)=0$. For $a<0$, $\{\rho>a\}=[0,\infty]$; for $a=0$ it is $(0,\infty)$; and for $a>0$ it is $(0,1/a)$. These sets are Borel by [F8], so [F9] makes $\rho$ Borel. By [F10], $\rho\circ m$ is measurable on $\Theta\times\mathsf X$. Pulling it back along the measurable coordinate projection from the finite product space, which is measurable by [F12], gives a product-measurable function; multiplying by $p$ preserves measurability by [F11]. This product restricts to $q=p/m$ on $V\times\mathsf Z$, so $q$ is measurable on the valid-fibre set. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, step 2.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 2.2, 2.1, 3.2, and 3.1 prove the marginal measurability, normalization, valid-fibre density, and integrable-exchange clauses. [step 2.2, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 3.1] ∎
