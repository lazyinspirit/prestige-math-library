## Statement

Let G be a countable discrete amenable group. For every right CG-module M and every integer q>0, the N G-von Neumann dimension of Tor_q^{CG}(M,N G) is zero. This dimension-flatness statement does not say that the Tor module itself is zero.

## Statement references

[Cha–Orr, Theorem 6.6 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0910.3700)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A countable discrete amenable group $G$, a right $\mathbb CG$-module $M$, and $q>0$. Work in the standing full-AC convention of the L2-signature definitions; the spectral and Hilbert-module arguments below use that existing convention, not an additional group-theoretic hypothesis.

[F1] The group von Neumann algebra, its canonical trace and the extended projective-submodule dimension have the stated meanings. The defining page fixes full AC as its foundational convention. [[def-integral-half-solutions-strebel-and-l2-signatures]]

[F2] In a finite von Neumann projection module, Hilbert-dense submodules have dimension-zero quotient; zero-dimensional modules are closed under submodules, quotients, extensions and filtered colimits. [[lem-finite-von-neumann-module-dimension-and-dense-quotients]]

[F3] For a finite matrix over $\mathbb CG$, its algebraic kernel is Hilbert-dense in its full Hilbert kernel, and the quotient of its $\mathcal N G$-kernel by the right $\mathcal N G$-span of that algebraic kernel has dimension zero. [[lem-amenable-folner-kernel-approximation]]

[F4] Tensor products consist of finite sums of elementary tensors and preserve cokernels over arbitrary rings. Apply the stated left-module version to the opposite ring and flip elementary tensors to obtain the right-module version used here. [[lem-tensoring-with-a-right-module-is-additive-right-exact-and-preserves-direct-sums]]

[F5] Tor is homology after tensoring a projective resolution of the right module. Canonical free covers iterated on kernels give a free resolution; apply the left-module construction to the opposite ring for right modules. Under the standing AC convention it is projective. Any two projective resolutions are homotopy equivalent over the resolved module, under DC, which follows from this standing AC convention. [[def-tor-by-resolving-the-right-module]], [[prop-the-iterated-free-module-resolution-is-canonical-in-zf]], [[thm-projective-resolutions-of-the-same-object-are-homotopy-equivalent-over-that-object]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and standing foundational convention above. Put $R=\mathbb CG$ and $A=\mathcal N G$.

1.1 First let $K\subseteq R^n$ be generated by finitely many columns, and let $B:R^s\to R^n$ be the corresponding matrix map onto $K$. Right exactness of tensor products identifies $K\otimes_R A$ with $A^s/\operatorname{im}((\ker_R B)\otimes_R A)$. The kernel of its natural map to $A^n$ is therefore $\ker_A B/\operatorname{im}((\ker_R B)\otimes_R A)$. This quotient has dimension zero by [F3]. The tensor-product identifications follow directly from the presentation $\ker_R B\to R^s\to K\to0$: relations among the chosen generators are exactly the elements of $\ker_R B$. [F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 For an arbitrary submodule $K\subseteq R^n$, take its directed union of finitely generated submodules $K_j$. A tensor is a finite sum, so every element of $K\otimes_R A$ comes from some $K_j\otimes_R A$. If its image in $A^n$ is zero, its representative already lies in the kernel of the natural map $K_j\otimes_R A\to A^n$. Thus the kernel for $K$ is the directed union of images of the zero-dimensional kernels from step 1.1; it has dimension zero by [F2]. This also proves the assertion if relations identify different representatives, since images are quotients of those kernels. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $F=\bigoplus_{i\in I}R$ be any free module and $K\subseteq F$. Every finite tensor in $K\otimes_R A$ involves finitely many vectors of $K$, each of finite coordinate support in $F$. Their supports lie in one finite free summand $R^n\subseteq F$. A kernel element is therefore the image of a kernel for a submodule of that finite free summand, handled by step 2.1. Taking the directed union over those submodules and finite supports, and applying [F2], proves that $\ker(K\otimes_R A\to F\otimes_R A)$ has dimension zero for every free $F$. [F2, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Choose a free presentation $0\to K\to F\to M\to0$. In the resolution-dependent definition of Tor, first use a free resolution beginning with this presentation. For any other chosen projective resolution, [F5] gives chain maps inverse up to chain homotopy. Additive tensoring preserves the homotopy equation $f-g=dh+hd$, since tensoring respects sums and composition. Thus the tensor complexes are homotopy equivalent and their homology modules are isomorphic; the calculation below therefore applies to every retained resolution datum. A free resolution beginning with this presentation computes $\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(M,A)$ as $\ker(K\otimes_R A\to F\otimes_R A)$: resolving $K$ and splicing onto $F\to M$ gives this equality by right exactness at the last differential. Thus step 3.1 proves the assertion for degree one. For $q>1$, successively apply the same construction to the kernels in a free resolution of $M$; the positive-degree homology after splicing shifts by one, giving $\operatorname{Tor}_q^R(M,A)\cong\operatorname{Tor}_1^R(K_{q-1},A)$ for the appropriate syzygy $K_{q-1}$. The degree-one argument applies to every such module, so every asserted higher Tor group has dimension zero. No claim that these algebraic quotient modules are zero is used or obtained. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
