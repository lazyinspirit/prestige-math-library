## Definition

Keep the type-A pyramid data
$(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak p,\mathfrak h,\mathfrak m,e,\chi)$ of
[[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]], with
$\lambda=(0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n)$, transpose
$q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$, and $\chi(x)=\operatorname{tr}(xe)$ on $\mathfrak m$.
Let $\eta\colon U(\mathfrak p)\to U(\mathfrak p)$ be the algebra automorphism
with
$$\eta(e_{i,j})=e_{i,j}+\delta_{i,j}\bigl(n-q_{\mathrm{col}(j)}-q_{\mathrm{col}(j)+1}-\cdots-q_l\bigr)$$
for all matrix units $e_{i,j}\in\mathfrak p$. Let $I_\chi$ be the kernel of the
algebra homomorphism $\chi\colon U(\mathfrak m)\to\mathbb C$ extending the Lie
character $\chi$, that is, the two-sided ideal generated by $x-\chi(x)$,
$x\in\mathfrak m$ ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]],
[[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]),
and let $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ be the left ideal it generates in
$U(\mathfrak g)$.

The **type-A finite W-algebra** is
$$W(\lambda)=\{u\in U(\mathfrak p):[x,\eta(u)]\in U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi\ \text{for all }x\in\mathfrak m\}.$$
It is a subalgebra of $U(\mathfrak p)$, and
$Q_\chi=U(\mathfrak g)/U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ is a
$(U(\mathfrak g),W(\lambda))$-bimodule with right action
$u\cdot\bar v=\overline{v\,\eta(u)}$ ([[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]).

The **Whittaker category** $\mathcal C(\lambda)$ is the category of
$\mathfrak g$-modules on which $x-\chi(x)$ acts locally nilpotently for every
$x\in\mathfrak m$ ([[def-representation-of-a-lie-algebra]]). For
$M\in\mathcal C(\lambda)$ the **Whittaker vectors**
$$\mathrm{Wh}(M)=\{v\in M:xv=\chi(x)v\ \text{for all }x\in\mathfrak m\}$$
form a $W(\lambda)$-module with $u\cdot v=\eta(u)v$, and
$\mathrm{Wh}(M)=0$ implies $M=0$.

The verification below establishes the algebra and module structures just
stated. The associated graded of the loop filtration on $U(\mathfrak p)$ makes
$W(\lambda)$ a filtered deformation of $U(\mathfrak g^e)$; that statement is
proved later on this page
([[thm-the-loop-filtration-on-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-has-associated-graded-u-of-g-e]]),
and the filtration used is the loop (good) filtration, not the Kazhdan
filtration.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The pyramid data of [[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]], the automorphism $\eta$ of $U(\mathfrak p)$, the ideal $I_\chi$ of $U(\mathfrak m)$ generated by $x-\chi(x)$ and the left ideal $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ of $U(\mathfrak g)$.

[F1] The grading $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak g_r$ is multiplicative, $e\in\mathfrak g_1$, $\mathfrak p=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}\mathfrak g_r$, $\mathfrak m=\bigoplus_{r<0}\mathfrak g_r$, and $\chi([\mathfrak m,\mathfrak m])=0$ ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]]).

[F2] Enveloping algebras: $U(\mathfrak g)$ is the quotient of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by $xy-yx-[x,y]$; a Lie character on a Lie subalgebra extends to an algebra homomorphism of the enveloping algebra, and $U(\mathfrak g)$ is generated by $\mathfrak p\cup\mathfrak m$ ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-representation-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]]).

[F3] Quotients and ideals: multiplication in a quotient ring is well defined modulo a two-sided ideal, and left ideals are additive subgroups stable under left multiplication ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The pyramid data, the automorphism $\eta$, the ideal $I_\chi$ and the left ideal $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$.

1.1 $\eta$ is an algebra automorphism. On generators, $\eta(e_{i,j})=e_{i,j}+c_j\delta_{i,j}$ with $c_j=n-q_{\mathrm{col}(j)}-\cdots-q_l$ a constant attached to the column of $j$; these elements are again matrix units plus scalars, and the assignment extends to an algebra endomorphism of $U(\mathfrak p)$ by the universal property [F2]. Composing with the map $e_{i,j}\mapsto e_{i,j}-c_j\delta_{i,j}$ gives the inverse, because the column of $j$ is unchanged by adding a scalar to the diagonal; hence $\eta$ is an automorphism. [F2, algebra]

2.1 $W(\lambda)$ is a subalgebra. If $u,v\in W(\lambda)$ then for $x\in\mathfrak m$, $[x,\eta(uv)]=[x,\eta(u)\eta(v)]=[x,\eta(u)]\eta(v)+\eta(u)[x,\eta(v)]$; the first term lies in $(U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi)\eta(v)\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ because $\eta(v)\in U(\mathfrak p)$ and $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ is a left ideal, and the second lies in $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ because $\eta(u)(U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi)\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ (again a left ideal). Also $1\in W(\lambda)$ since $[x,1]=0$, and sums of elements of $W(\lambda)$ are in $W(\lambda)$ by additivity of the commutator; so $W(\lambda)$ is a subalgebra of $U(\mathfrak p)$, with the ring structure inherited from $U(\mathfrak p)$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 $Q_\chi$ is a bimodule. $Q_\chi=U(\mathfrak g)/U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ is a left $U(\mathfrak g)$-module by left multiplication. If $u\in W(\lambda)$ then the condition defining $W(\lambda)$ says precisely that $\eta(u)$ preserves $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ under right multiplication, $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi\cdot\eta(u)\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$; hence right multiplication by $\eta(u)$, followed by projection, is a well-defined endomorphism of $Q_\chi$, and the assignment $u\mapsto$ (right multiplication by $\eta(u)$) is an algebra homomorphism by step 1.1. Associativity of the two actions is associativity of multiplication in the quotient ring $U(\mathfrak g)/U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$, so $Q_\chi$ is a $(U(\mathfrak g),W(\lambda))$-bimodule with the stated right action. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 $\mathrm{Wh}(M)$ is a $W(\lambda)$-module. Let $M\in\mathcal C(\lambda)$ and $v\in\mathrm{Wh}(M)$. First, $uv=\chi(u)v$ for every $u\in U(\mathfrak m)$: this holds for $u\in\mathfrak m$ by definition and extends to products by induction, since if $yv=\chi(y)v$ then $(xy)v=x(yv)=\chi(y)xv=\chi(x)\chi(y)v$. Hence $I_\chi v=0$, because $I_\chi=\ker(\chi\colon U(\mathfrak m)\to\mathbb C)$ and every element of $I_\chi$ acts on $v$ by its $\chi$-value, which is zero. Consequently $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi\cdot v=0$, since a general element of $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ is a sum of products $a\,i$ with $a\in U(\mathfrak g)$, $i\in I_\chi$, and $(ai)v=a(iv)=0$. Now take $u\in W(\lambda)$, $x\in\mathfrak m$. Then $x(\eta(u)v)=\eta(u)xv+[x,\eta(u)]v=\chi(x)\eta(u)v+0$, because $[x,\eta(u)]\in U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$ by the definition of $W(\lambda)$. Hence $\eta(u)v\in\mathrm{Wh}(M)$, and the map $(u,v)\mapsto\eta(u)v$ is bilinear and associative by the algebra structure of $U(\mathfrak p)$ and step 1.1. Here the character $\chi$ and the parabolic data are those of [F1]. This verifies the module structure; the vanishing statement $\mathrm{Wh}(M)=0\Rightarrow M=0$ is the cohomological detection theorem proved in [[lem-gan-ginzburg-skryabin-inputs-hold-for-the-type-a-pyramid-good-grading]] and [[thm-skryabins-equivalence-for-the-type-a-whittaker-pair]], and is recorded here as part of the categorical interface. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The dictionary $\eta$ is the type-A $\rho$-shift of the source; it is the automorphism used (in the form $\eta_c$) on the deformed tensor space later on this page. The undoubled pyramid grading has $e$ in degree $1$; the doubled Gan-Ginzburg grading used for the Kazhdan filtration has $e$ in degree $2$ and vanishing degree $-1$ part.

- The ideal $I_\chi$ is two-sided by construction: it is the kernel of an algebra homomorphism, and equivalently the two-sided ideal generated by the elements $x-\chi(x)$.
