## Statement

Keep the conventions of
[[def-the-level-one-vector-representation-its-affinization-and-energy-function]].
Then:

(i) The derived level-$0$ module $V$ has **level-one perfect crystal**
$B=\{b_1,\dots,b_e\}$ in the sense of axioms (P1)-(P3): the weights of $V$ are
the finite Weyl orbit of $\lambda^\circ=\Lambda_1-\Lambda_0$ in $P_{\mathrm{cl}}$
with one-dimensional weight spaces, $B\otimes B$ is connected,
$\varepsilon(b)\in P_{\mathrm{cl}}^+$ and $\varphi(b)\in P_{\mathrm{cl}}^+$ have
level one for every $b$, the maps $\varepsilon,\varphi:B\to(P_{\mathrm{cl}}^+)_1$
are bijections onto the level-one dominant weights, and
$B_{\min}:=\{b:\langle c,\varepsilon(b)\rangle=1\}$ equals $B$.

(ii) The basis $G(b_j):=v_j$ is a **lower global basis** of $V$ for the ordinary
semilinear bar involution with coefficient bar $q\mapsto q^{-1}$ and
$\overline{v_j}=v_j$: the $A_0$-lattice $V_0=\bigoplus_j\mathbb Z[q]v_j$ is
bar-stable up to $q\mapsto q^{-1}$, the images $b_j+G(b_j)\bmod qV_0$ are the
crystal elements, $\bar{E_iv}=E_i\bar v$ and $\bar{F_iv}=F_i\bar v$ for all
$v\in V$, all rank-one strings have exactly two vertices with $E$- and
$F$-coefficients $1$, and the divided powers
$E_i^{(m)},F_i^{(m)}$ vanish for $m\ge2$. Here
$A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ and $A_0=\mathbb Z[q]$ are kept distinct.

(iii) The same statements hold on the affinization with
$G(z^ab_j)=z^av_j$, the loop variable fixed by the bar involution, and the full
$P$-weight $\Lambda_j-\Lambda_{j-1}+a\delta$; the finite $V$ is a
$U_q'(\mathfrak g)$-module with $P_{\mathrm{cl}}$-weights and is not a
level-one $P$-graded highest weight module.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The vector module $V$, its affinization $V_{\mathrm{aff}}$ with the operators $E_i,F_i,K_h$ and the crystal data $\varepsilon_i,\varphi_i$ of [[def-the-level-one-vector-representation-its-affinization-and-energy-function]], the Bar/global basis framework of [[def-crystal-lattice-and-crystal-basis]], and the rank-one string formulas of [[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]].

[F1] On $B$: $f_ib_j=b_{j+1}$ if $j\equiv i$ and $0$ otherwise; $e_ib_j=b_{j-1}$ if $j\equiv i+1$ and $0$ otherwise; $\varepsilon_i(b_j)=\delta_{j\equiv i+1}$, $\varphi_i(b_j)=\delta_{j\equiv i}$, $\varepsilon(b_j)=\Lambda^{\mathrm{cl}}_{j-1}$, $\varphi(b_j)=\Lambda^{\mathrm{cl}}_j$, and the weights of $V$ are the $\lambda_j=\Lambda_j-\Lambda_{j-1}$ with multiplicity one ([[def-the-level-one-vector-representation-its-affinization-and-energy-function]]).

[F2] $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ has the rank-one subalgebra generated by $E_i,F_i,K_i^{\pm1}$ with the standard $U_q(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$-relations; on the two-dimensional string space $V_i(m)$ spanned by two consecutive basis vectors the operators $E_i,F_i$ are nonzero in both directions with coefficient $1$ and $E_i^{(2)}=F_i^{(2)}=0$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] A crystal lattice is an $A_0$-lattice stable under the divided operators with residue basis given by the mod-$q$ images; a perfect crystal of level $l$ satisfies (P1)-(P3) with the level-$l$ dominant weights of $P_{\mathrm{cl}}^+$ ([[def-crystal-graph-epsilon-phi-and-weight]], [[def-crystal-lattice-and-crystal-basis]]).

[F4] $\mathbb Q(q)$ is a field and $q$ is transcendental; the finite Weyl group $W$ of the finite subdiagram acts on $P_{\mathrm{cl}}$ through its type $A_{e-1}$ permutation action, preserving levels.



## Verification

**Proof technique:** read the perfectness data off the explicit arrows, prove the connectedness of $B\otimes B$ by a two-sided raising procedure, and verify the lower-basis axioms by the coefficient-one string matrices.

1.1 Perfectness of $B$ except connectedness. By [F1] every $\varepsilon(b_j)=\Lambda_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $\varphi(b_j)=\Lambda_j^{\mathrm{cl}}$ has level one, and the two maps are bijections $\{1,\dots,e\}\to\{\Lambda_0^{\mathrm{cl}},\dots,\Lambda_{e-1}^{\mathrm{cl}}\}=(P_{\mathrm{cl}}^+)_1$ because both $j\mapsto\Lambda_j^{\mathrm{cl}}$ and $j\mapsto\Lambda_{j-1}^{\mathrm{cl}}$ run through the $e$ fundamental classes; hence $B_{\min}=\{b:\langle c,\varepsilon(b)\rangle=1\}=B$ and axiom (P3) holds. For (P1): the weights $\lambda_j$ of $V$ are pairwise distinct with multiplicity one by [F1], and the finite Weyl group of the finite subdiagram acts transitively on them (it permutes the indices $j$ of the fundamental classes and hence the differences $\Lambda_j-\Lambda_{j-1}$), so they form the finite Weyl orbit of $\lambda^\circ$ with one-dimensional weight spaces, contained in its convex hull. [F1, F3, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 Connectedness of $B\otimes B$ for the fixed coproduct. Use the tensor rule $f_i(x\otimes y)=f_ix\otimes y$ if $\varphi_i(x)>\varepsilon_i(y)$ and $x\otimes f_iy$ otherwise, and $e_i(x\otimes y)=e_ix\otimes y$ if $\varphi_i(x)\ge\varepsilon_i(y)$ and $x\otimes e_iy$ otherwise. For a pair $(b_a,b_b)$ with $a>b$ use $i=b$: $\varphi_b(b_a)=0\ge\varepsilon_b(b_b)=0$, so $e_b$ acts on the first factor and $(b_a,b_b)\mapsto(b_{a-1},b_b)$; iterating lowers the first index to $b$. For $a<b$ use $f_b$: $\varphi_b(b_a)=0\le\varepsilon_b(b_b)=0$, so the rule acts on the second factor, $(b_a,b_b)\mapsto(b_a,b_{b+1})$, and iterating raises the second index to $a$. Hence every pair reaches the diagonal $(b_a,b_a)$; from $(b_j,b_j)$ the two arrows $f_j$ (first factor, using $\varphi_j(b_j)=1>0=\varepsilon_j(b_j)$) and then $f_j$ (second factor, using $\varphi_j(b_{j+1})=0\le\varepsilon_j(b_j)=0$) reach $(b_{j+1},b_{j+1})$, so all diagonal pairs are connected to each other and $B\otimes B$ is connected. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

1.3 Lower global basis of $V$. Define $\overline{\cdot}$ by $q\mapsto q^{-1}$, $\overline{v_j}=v_j$. Since $E_iv_j,F_iv_j\in\{0\}\cup\{v_{j\pm1}\}$, one has $\overline{E_iv}=\overline{E_i}\overline{v}$ and $\overline{F_iv}=\overline{F_i}\overline{v}$ for all $v$ because the coefficients $1$ are bar-invariant; the $A_0$-lattice $V_0=\bigoplus\mathbb Z[q]v_j$ satisfies $L:=\sum_jA_0v_j=V_0$ and $B$ is recovered as $\{v_j\bmod qV_0\}$, so $(V_0,B)$ is a crystal lattice by [F3]. By [F2] the $i$-string through any $v_j$ is exactly two-dimensional with $E_i$ and $F_i$ coefficients $1$: $E_iv_{i+1}=v_i$, $F_iv_i=v_{i+1}$ (indices mod $e$), and $E_i^2=F_i^2=0$ because the residue condition of two consecutive applications fails; hence $E_i^{(m)}=F_i^{(m)}=0$ for $m\ge2$ and the divided operators have the integral coefficient matrices $e_i,f_i$ with entries in $\{0,1\}$. This is exactly the lower-basis condition of [F3] with $G(b_j)=v_j$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 The affinized chart. Put $L_{\mathrm{aff}}:=\bigoplus_m\mathbb Z[q]u_m$ and $G(z^ab_j)=z^av_j=u_{j-ae}$; extend the bar involution by $\overline z=z$. Then $\overline{\cdot}$ is semilinear with coefficient bar $q\mapsto q^{-1}$, fixes every $G(z^ab_j)$, and satisfies $\overline{E_iw}=E_i\overline w$, $\overline{F_iw}=F_i\overline w$ for $w\in V_{\mathrm{aff}}$: the only loop shift occurs for $i=0$, where $E_0(z^av_1)=z^{a+1}v_e$ and $\overline{z^{a+1}v_e}=z^{a+1}v_e=E_0\overline{z^av_1}$, and the remaining cases have coefficients in $\{0,1\}$. The weight of $G(z^ab_j)$ is $\Lambda_j-\Lambda_{j-1}+a\delta$, and the string bound of step 1.3 is unchanged because the $i$-orbits are the same two-vertex sets shifted by loops. The finite module $V$ carries only the $P_{\mathrm{cl}}$-weights $\lambda_j$ and no dominant level-one highest weight vector, so it is a level-$0$ $U_q'(\mathfrak g)$-module and not a level-one $P$-graded highest weight module; the $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ and $A_0=\mathbb Z[q]$ charts are used separately and are not identified. [step 1.3, F1, F3, given, algebra]

3.1 Conclusion. The level-$0$ vector module has the level-one perfect crystal $B=\{b_1,\dots,b_e\}$ with the explicit $\varepsilon,\varphi$, connected tensor square and $B_{\min}=B$; the basis $G(b_j)=v_j$ is a lower global basis of $V$ whose $i$-strings have two vertices with coefficients $1$ and whose divided operators of order $\ge2$ vanish; and the affinized chart $G(z^ab_j)=z^av_j$ has the same properties with the full $P$-weight. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
