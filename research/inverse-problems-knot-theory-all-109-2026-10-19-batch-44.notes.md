## Supplier hypotheses and mathematical limits

- Batch 25's actual `def-ip-nonlinear-forward-map` and tangential-cone statements/proofs were inspected. The forward-map interface is not a tangential-cone conclusion: TCC is substantive and is not inferred from differentiability here.
- Batch 26's actual Tikhonov functional and existence/stability statements/proofs were inspected with their weak-closedness and axiom hypotheses. This batch uses the functional convention only; it does not use minimizer existence/stability to claim the finite-budget algorithm has a minimizer or converges.
- Batch 4's actual linear compact-operator error-budget definition and output-error proposition/proof were inspected. Their Hilbert/compact-operator hypotheses do not certify generic nonlinear finite-element residuals and are not substituted for the local matrix lemma or supplied `eta_u, eta_p` bounds.
- The published finite-step discrete-adjoint theorem proves an exact gradient for a finite state-step graph. The published elliptic-adjoint, finite-element gradient-error and Strang residual results have their own elliptic and derivative-data assumptions. In particular, `lem-fem-control-gradient-residual-bound` controls a continuous-objective gradient error under its full Strang hypotheses; it is not a generic algebraic residual bound for the discrete objective.
- The local linear residual lemma handles exact finite-dimensional systems only. For a nonlinear residual model, an `eta_u, eta_p` estimate still requires a separate stability argument. The contract explicitly requires the certified adjoint residual to be for the exact state/adjoint system; evaluating a residual only at the approximate state is insufficient unless the model also bounds the state and derivative-data defects.
- The algorithm is conditional on finite, correctly certified model routines. `converged` means only the exact discrete projected-gradient residual meets tolerance; `discrepancy-stopped` concerns only the discrete forward map. There is no discretized-minimizer-to-true-parameter convergence theorem, global uniqueness claim, or generic nonlinear solver-error theorem.
