## Exact target

F is any field, q∈F× has finite order e≥2 and q≠1, Qₐ=q^cₐ for integer charges cₐ, 1≤a≤ℓ. The algebra is the arbitrary-level Ariki–Koike algebra, expressed in the chosen affine-Hecke convention. For each residue content α, eα is the sum of split joint generalized-JM-eigenvalue projectors of words whose eigenvalues have multiset {q^i with multiplicity αᵢ}. The target is that each eα is zero or primitive central and every block arises in this way.

The parent owns finite dimension, spectral existence/propagation, and centrality. The following construction does not silently reprove or presume those claims. The degenerate q=1 case is in the separate center draft.

## What the actual Lyle–Mathas proof uses

Authoritative full text: Lyle–Mathas, arXiv math/0607451v4, pp.1–20, fully read. The relevant proof is Theorem 2.11, pp.4–8, and §3, pp.9–17. The source does not prove the cyclotomic Jantzen sum formula or the complete arbitrary-level cellular/cyclotomic Schur theory within those pages; it names these suppliers explicitly. It also does not prove its Fayers core-block inputs locally. Thus importing only its citation would fail the repository's local-proof requirement.

The exact source proof can be assembled from the following upstream contracts, all at arbitrary level ℓ. None is supplied by a theorem limited to ℓ=1.

**Q: cyclotomic cellular/Schur setup.** Construct the Specht modules S(λ) of the Hecke algebra H, indexed by all ℓ-multipartitions of n, and a finite-dimensional split quasihereditary cyclotomic Schur algebra C=End_H(M), with M=⊕λ mλH and H a direct summand of M. Its standard modules Δ(λ) have simple heads L(λ), Δ(λ) has only composition factors L(μ) with μ≤λ, and [Δ(λ):L(λ)]=1. Every S(λ) and every Δ(λ) is supported in a single block. All H simples occur as nonzero D(μ), which are quotients of S(μ). The Schur functor gives [Δ(λ):L(μ)]=[S(λ):D(μ)] when D(μ)≠0. The standard/cell filtrations imply that block linkage is the equivalence relation generated by shared simple factors of these standard/cell modules. On S(λ), every joint JM generalized eigenword has content equal to the multiset of residues cₐ+j−i of the nodes (i,j,a) of λ. This last statement is spectral triangularity, not block classification.

**J: the cyclotomic Jantzen formula.** Over O=F[t,t^(−1)]_(t−1), deform q to qt and Qₐ to Qₐt^(na). Build Δ_O(λ) and its cellular bilinear form, with semisimple generic fiber. For its Jantzen filtration, Δ(λ)^1=rad Δ(λ), the filtration terminates, and in K₀(C),

Σ_(i>0)[Δ(λ)^i]=Σμ Jλμ[Δ(μ)].

For λ strictly dominating μ, the coefficient is the signed sum over rim hooks of equal length whose removal gives the same diagram:

Jλμ=Σ_(x,y with λ\rλ_x=μ\rμ_y)(−1)^(leg(rλ_x)+leg(rμ_y)) ν_(t−1)(res_O(foot(rλ_x))−res_O(foot(rμ_y))),

and is zero otherwise. A rim-hook foot has row equal to the height of its column; res_O(i,j,a)=(qt)^(j−i)Qₐt^(na). This is LM Theorem 2.6, p.6, cited from James–Mathas, Trans. AMS 352 (2000), 5381–5404, Theorem 4.3. That full source is not retrieved/read/proved in this construction. J must not be declared proved by LM's citation.

**K: component-core Jantzen connectivity.** At fixed sizes of all components and fixed component e-cores, all multipartitions are Jantzen-connected. This is LM Proposition 3.7, pp.13–14. Its proof uses its explicit coefficient formula Proposition 3.6(b), and the Carter-partition implications quoted from Mathas, Theorem 5.45. This construction has read that proof, but has not locally proved the Carter-partition classification/implications. K is kept as a distinct open combinatorial obligation, rather than hidden in a blanket “standard abacus argument.”

**F: finite-e core-combinatorics contracts.** Put Cᵢ(λ)=number of nodes of residue i. Let hᵢ be removable minus addable i-nodes (the hub), w_e(λ) the total component e-hook weight, and

wt(λ)=Σₐ C_(cₐ)(λ)−½Σᵢ(Cᵢ(λ)−Cᵢ₊₁(λ))².

The required statements are: same-size multipartitions have equal content iff equal hub; equal hubs imply |λ|≡|μ| mod e and wt(λ)−wt(μ)=ℓ(|λ|−|μ|)/e; a componentwise multicore κ is reduced iff every multipartition of its residue class is a multicore, equivalently its wt is minimal among multicores with its hub; a nonreduced multicore admits a finite sequence of two-component bead exchanges preserving hub, never increasing wt, ending at a strictly lower wt; reduced multicores of equal content admit a finite sequence of two-component bead exchanges preserving content at every stage. These are LM Proposition 3.13, Proposition 3.14, and Lemmas 3.17–3.18, pp.15–16, citing Fayers [14] Proposition 3.2/Lemma 3.3 and [15] Theorem 3.1/Proof of Proposition 3.7(1),(2). These Fayers full sources have not been retrieved/read/proved here. Their conclusions must remain open inputs.

No Morita reduction of parameter orbits is needed for this task: all Qₐ are already powers of q. No affine-center theorem, Ariki categorification theorem, or asserted equality of affine and cyclotomic blocks is required if Q,J,K,F are actually proved.

## Local bridge 1: Jantzen coefficients determine Schur blocks from Q and J

The classes [Δ(λ)] form a Z-basis of K₀(C), since the matrix to simple classes is triangular with diagonal one. If Jλμ≠0, project the Jantzen sum identity to all blocks other than the block of Δ(λ). Its left side is zero, because Δ(λ)^i are submodules of the block-pure module Δ(λ). Its right side is a Z-linear combination of standard classes in the other blocks. Basis independence forces their coefficients to vanish. Hence Δ(μ) is in the block of Δ(λ).

Conversely put dλμ=[Δ(λ):L(μ)]. For λ≠μ, Δ(λ)^1=rad Δ(λ) contains every factor L(μ) of Δ(λ). Since all higher filtration terms lie in this radical, the coefficient of [L(μ)] in the left side of J is nonzero iff dλμ≠0. On the right it is Σν Jλν dνμ. If this integer is nonzero, at least one ν has Jλν≠0 and dνμ≠0, with λ>ν≥μ. Repeating decreases strictly in the finite dominance poset until ν=μ. This makes λ and μ Jantzen-connected. Using Q's block-linkage property now proves that two Δ's share a block iff their indices are Jantzen-connected. This argument proves LM Proposition 2.9 rather than merely citing it.

## Local bridge 2: Hecke and Schur block partitions coincide from Q

If D(μ)≠0 is a factor of S(λ), the multiplicity equality in Q implies L(μ) is a factor of Δ(λ). Therefore chains connecting Specht modules inside a Hecke block connect their standards in one Schur block. Thus each Hecke block can support at most one corresponding Schur block.

In the other direction write 1_H=b₁+⋯+b_s as primitive central idempotents. Since H is a summand of M, every Mbᵢ is nonzero. We have M=⊕ᵢ Mbᵢ, and Hom_H(Mbᵢ,Mbⱼ)=0 for i≠j: an H-linear map satisfies f(m)=f(mbᵢ)=f(m)bᵢ, incompatible with the target's bⱼ support. Hence C=⊕ᵢ End_H(Mbᵢ), so there are at least s Schur blocks. The first paragraph gives at most s and the assignments agree. Thus the Hecke Specht blocks are exactly the Jantzen classes. This is LM Proposition 2.3's actual double-centralizer argument; Q's representation-theoretic hypotheses remain explicit upstream inputs.

## Local combinatorial bridge: what LM §3 proves once K and F hold

The valuation appearing in J is zero unless the foot residues agree. If they agree, write h=n(a−b)+j−i−l+k for foot nodes (i,j,a),(k,l,b). The residue difference is a unit times t^h−1. The integer h is nonzero (when a≠b its na−nb displacement exceeds the possible foot-content differences; when a=b it is the nonzero axial distance). In characteristic p, write h=p^v h' with p not dividing h'. Then t^h−1=(t^h'−1)^(p^v) up to a Laurent unit, so its (t−1)-valuation is p^v. In characteristic zero its valuation is 1. This is the complete elementary proof of LM Lemma 3.4.

For a Jantzen edge some contributing pair of hooks therefore has equal length and foot residue. Their residue multisets agree because residues advance by one modulo e along the rim. Removing either leaves the same diagram, so the contents of λ and μ agree. Thus Jantzen-connected implies same content, without F.

When a rim hook moves between distinct components and preserves its foot residue, its removed and added diagrams determine the pair of hooks uniquely. Their Jantzen contribution is ±p^v or ±1, a nonzero integer. Thus that move is a Jantzen edge in one direction; reversal handles the opposite dominance direction. This argument remains valid even when the nonzero integer is divisible by char(F), because J lives in the integer Grothendieck group, not F.

Consequently multipartitions of equal componentwise multicores are Jantzen-connected. First use K to redistribute hooks inside each fixed-size component. If the component weights differ, choose components b,c with the b-weight below its target and c-weight above its target; unwrap one e-hook in c and wrap an e-hook with the same foot residue in b (move a lowest bead down its runner). This is the preceding cross-component edge. The sum of squares of discrepancies of component weights strictly decreases, so finitely many moves equalize the component weights; K completes the argument. This proves LM Lemma 3.9 from K and the hook valuation computation.

For a two-component exchange of core beads, distribute all surplus e-hook weight onto the affected runner in the first component before the exchange and onto its matching runner afterwards. The two lifted multipartitions then differ by moving one rim hook between those two components with equal foot residue, hence are Jantzen-connected. The redistribution is permitted by the preceding paragraph. This is the lifting argument in LM Lemma 3.11; it requires the exchange be defined as in LM Definition 3.10, i.e. exchange one bead between runners in each component and repack the component core, with the reverse exchange in the other component.

Now fix a content class and let W=max w_e(λ) within it, which exists because the set of multipartitions of n is finite. By F's minimal-core characterization, a member ν has weight W exactly when its componentwise core is reduced. If w_e(ν)<W, F supplies a hub-preserving core-exchange sequence from its core to a smaller core, all intermediate cores no larger than the starting one. Lift each intermediate core to size n by adding (n−size(core))/e hooks. The previous lifting paragraph makes consecutive lifts Jantzen-connected. The final lift has strictly more e-hook weight than ν. Repeat; this terminates at W because 0≤w_e≤floor(n/e). If two same-content members have weight W, their reduced cores have equal content; F connects the cores by content-preserving exchanges, and lifting to size n connects the members. Every member of the content class is therefore Jantzen-connected to every other. This supplies the actual max-weight argument of LM Theorem 3.19; it does not pretend to prove F.

## Final local algebraic bridge to the assigned CRT idempotents

Assume the parent's spectral construction plus Q,J,K,F, with the bridges above. If eα≠0, the nonzero finite-dimensional algebra eαH has a simple module. By Q it is D(λ) for a multipartition λ, and the spectral part of Q implies that λ has node content α. Every other simple module of eαH similarly comes from content α. The combinatorial bridge and the block bridges show that all of these simple modules lie in a single Hecke block.

If eα were the sum of two nonzero orthogonal central idempotents u,v, each finite-dimensional summand uH,vH would have a simple module (take a maximal proper left ideal). These simple modules cannot lie in one block, since u acts as identity on one and zero on the other and any module or extension decomposes by the central idempotent u. This contradicts the preceding paragraph. Hence eα is primitive. Conversely the nonzero eα are orthogonal and sum to 1, so every primitive central idempotent is one of them, by decomposing it against this sum. This is the full CRT-to-primitive conclusion and uses no unproved assumption that equal JM central characters by themselves imply equal cyclotomic blocks.
