# Binding finite operational contracts for advanced QA procedures

Status: draft planning, 2026-10-14. This appendix is part of the `statement_or_definition` of each machine row whose `operational_contract_ref` points here. A named routine below denotes these instructions, not a bibliographic invocation. Mathematical correctness, probability, termination, approximation and resource consequences remain the separate result rows specified below. No active item or proof acceptance is created.

A circuit is a finite gate list with ordered registers. Products act right to left. A supplied oracle names its complete unitary extension on invalid encodings, inverse and any controlled version actually used. Every preparation, inverse, query, arithmetic circuit, comparison, phase gate, coefficient/angle calculation and written output is charged. All classical numerical inputs have rational binary encodings unless an exact-algebraic or ideal exact-real variant is explicitly selected. Real constants are evaluated by certified rational intervals. A strict inequality is decided only after disjoint intervals certify it; an equality test uses exact algebraic arithmetic or an explicitly declared ideal-real comparison resource. None of these models silently supplies an efficient Turing compiler.

## OP0 — Numerical arithmetic, roots, coefficient generation and finite amplification

**Certified elementary evaluation.** For rational x and error 2^-b, evaluate exp, sin and cos by their rational Taylor sums until the absolute remainder bound `3^ceil(|x|+1)|x|^(N+1)/(N+1)!` is below the error budget; sine/cosine may first reduce the argument by a certified interval for π. Compute π by the arctangent identity `π=16 arctan(1/5)−4 arctan(1/239)` with alternating-series bounds. For x≥0 compute square roots by rational bisection between 0 and `max(1,x)`; x=0 returns0 and x<0 returns INVALID in the real routine. Complex algebraic roots use ROOTS. For x>0 compute log x with `2Σ_(j=0)^(N−1)y^(2j+1)/(2j+1)`, y=(x−1)/(x+1), and tail `2|y|^(2N+1)/((2N+1)(1−y²))`. Each loop increases N or b until its explicit rational remainder test passes. Store the interval, not an uncertified floating result. Complex exponentials use the resulting sine/cosine intervals. For atan2(y,x), the zero vector returns INVALID; otherwise select its quadrant by certified signs (exact zero coordinates use their explicit axis angles), and bisect an angle interval in that quadrant using monotonic sine/cosine comparisons with the normalized vector until its width meets the requested error. If an interval straddles an equality, retain the adjoining angle intervals rather than request a real zero test. Return the midpoint plus its error interval. Whenever a numerical logarithm/angle appears in a ceiling or resource cutoff, compute an upper rational enclosure of width≤1/4 and take its ceiling; this defines a terminating conservative integer rule even at exact integer boundaries. Norms are square roots of explicit sums of squared coordinates; a zero norm takes the separately stated zero-output/INVALID branch before division. Bounds on the number/size of these operations are separate classical bit-cost obligations.

**ROOTS(p,b).** Input a nonzero polynomial with rational or exact-algebraic complex coefficients and b≥1. A nonzero constant polynomial returns the empty root list immediately; the zero polynomial returns INVALID. All candidate rational cuts/displacements are enumerated first by increasing power-of-two denominator, then increasing numerator, restricted to the stated middle-third interval. Remove repeated factors by the Euclidean gcd of p and p′, retaining multiplicities. Compute a rational Cauchy root bound `B>1+max_(j<d)|a_j/a_d|`. Enclose the square-free roots in the square `[-B,B]+i[-B,B]`. Subdivide rational rectangles. Before using a boundary, restrict p to each directed edge, and use the real polynomial gcd/Sturm signed-remainder sequence to certify that its real and imaginary parts never vanish simultaneously on that edge; if they do, move that cut to the first dyadic rational in the middle third that passes the check. Count the roots in a rectangle by its boundary winding number: isolate the finitely many zeros of the imaginary edge polynomial by Sturm bisection, retain crossings with positive real part, and add +1 for a negative-to-positive imaginary crossing, −1 for the reverse, and 0 for even contact. Boundaries are traversed counterclockwise. Constant zero imaginary edge polynomials are handled by the first displacement in that deterministic enumeration for which the entire new directed boundary is certified root-free; simultaneous complex zeros are already excluded. Also require that the imaginary value at every boundary vertex is nonzero and no edge imaginary restriction is identically zero, selecting the first enumerated displacement that certifies all these conditions. This removes vertex/corner crossing ambiguity; retain a nested partition and the certified outer root bound. Retain rectangles with positive winding count, discard zero-count rectangles, and subdivide until each retained rectangle has count one and diameter≤2^-b. Return the polynomial, disjoint isolating rectangles and original multiplicities. A Sturm sequence is formed by p,p′ and successive **negative** Euclidean remainders; endpoint sign variation differences count real roots after removing endpoint factors. The exact-algebraic variant uses the same finite field arithmetic, with algebraic values represented by their defining polynomial and isolator. Refinement selects the appropriate root after resultant field operations.

This defines the needed root isolation and selection operations. Their winding/Sturm correctness and bit bounds must be authored locally; they are not granted by a root count or source title. Refinement has finite termination on a nonzero finite-degree polynomial because distinct roots have positive separation and only finitely many forbidden cuts occur. ROOTS may take many refinements; no bound `poly(d,log(1/error))` is inferred without its separate certified-arithmetic proof.

**QA23 efficient numerical arithmetic variant.** The complete ROOTS schedule above remains the exact/complete-mode routine and has its finite termination guarantee. QA23 additionally has an efficient numerical mode in the explicitly selected exact-algebraic arithmetic model: one charged operation per exact field operation and equality/order test; all rational arithmetic, finite searches, quadratic-descriptor construction and emitted output are also charged. Intermediates may be finite extension tower/DAG descriptors, each new root defined over the existing field and selected by a rational isolator. Original coefficient-encoding access, arithmetic-operation implementation, expanded minimal-polynomial conversion and finite-basis compilation are separate resource coordinates, with no implied uniform bit-time bound.

The efficient mode rounds the strictly shrunken target to bounded-height rational coefficients before root generation. Its GRID-ROOTS variant retains the squarefree, winding, Sturm and multiplicity procedures, but tests at most N² normalized cut pairs per node, N=100(n+1): in each coordinate's open middle third, choose the least dyadic denominator admitting N+1 points and use its first N points. Choose outer half-widths from (B,B+1) for an integer Cauchy bound B. Test exactly the same root-free-edge, nonzero-vertex and nonzero-edge-imaginary-polynomial conditions. Edge signs are certified by rational Sturm isolation of the squarefree product of the edge real/imaginary polynomials, refining until the imaginary root's interval has no real-part root. Endpoint factors are removed before open-interval counts. Floors/ceilings and grid denominators use charged bounded binary search/doubling. The local bounded-height arithmetic theorem, not finite correctness alone, supplies the polynomial operation bound.

For a unit scalar u+iv, certified atan2 uses exact coordinate signs and explicit axis angles. In a nonaxis quadrant put a=|u|, b=|v|, initialize [L,U]=[0,2], and use midpoint m and rational sine/cosine approximations c,s of error ε=(U−L)/96. With q=bc−as, q>2ε retains the upper half, q<−2ε the lower half; otherwise retain the intersection with [m−12ε,m+12ε]. All equalities take this last branch. Stop at width h/8, h=min(η,1), combine with a Machin π enclosure of width h/8 for the quadrant, and dyadically round the midpoint within h/16. The certified phase distance is <h, with O(log²(1/h)+1) rational operations and O(log(1/h)+1) algebraic operations/comparisons. This makes OP0's adjoining-interval equality rule explicit without unbounded coordinate refinement.


**RawMean(V,M).** M=2^m≥4. V is a supplied coherent finite sampler, and the value circuit computes v(x)∈[0,1] to its allocated precision. Append a flag and rotate it to `sqrt(1−v(x))|0>+sqrt(v(x))|1>`, then uncompute the value scratch. Use the resulting preparation, its inverse and the flag reflection in the exact QA13 repeated-controlled-Q estimator. Output sin²(πy/M), or a certified rational enclosure when classical processing needs bits. `Mean(V,M,ρ)` repeats this circuit an odd r times, where r is the least positive odd integer at least `ln(1/ρ)/(2(8/π²−1/2)²)`, with an upper interval for the logarithm, and returns the median. The same scalar v is estimated each time; the output is not a median of unrelated spectral samples.

**RelProb(V,p_min,c,ρ).** Input 0<p_min≤1, 0<c≤1/100, and promise that V's designated good-or-unfinished probability p is either zero or at least p_min. Set `J=ceil(log₂(1024/sqrt(p_min)))`; for j=2,…,J estimate p with `Mean(V,2^j,ρ/(2(J+1)))`. Stop the rough loop at the first estimate with `(2^j)^2 estimate≥1024`; if none, test once more at 2^J and return ZERO if that estimate lies below `256/4^J`, otherwise retain J. With the retained scale M, run Mean at the least dyadic scale at least `256πM/c`, with failure ρ/2, and return its rational enclosure. If the intervals cannot certify a branch within the allocated numerical error, refine to error `min(c p_min/100,2^-j/100)` and use lower endpoint for the declared comparison. This is a bounded schedule; ZERO or a bad probability estimate can occur on a statistical failure branch. The relative-probability/zero-detection proof is a separate obligation. Count each repeated controlled Q and all confidence/bit costs, not merely the number of phase registers.

**CapAmplify(V,p_low,ρ).** A preparation V, its inverse and an ordinary or coherent verifier are explicit inputs. If p_low≥1/2, use direct samples; otherwise, in every round first make one direct verified sample and then, if it failed, use the finite QA11 rejection-sampled random-iteration rule with K the least positive integer satisfying p_low K²≥1. Run `ceil(16 ln(1/ρ))+16` fresh rounds; return the first verified good output and its success bit, or FAIL after the cap. Unknown actual success probability is not used to select an exact Grover peak. Output states are conditioned on the designated projector; classical output requires a diagonal costed predicate. This procedure is finite on every execution branch. Its success bound needs the declared lower-bound promise and the separate amplification results.

## OP1 — QSP completion and phase generation from a bare target

Binds `alg-qa-23-qsp-phase-synthesis`; also supplies generation for QA24, QA25, QA27, QA28 and QA30 when their input is a coefficient polynomial rather than a phase list.

**Inputs and modes.** A real rational/exact-algebraic coefficient polynomial p_orig, declared integer degree bound d and parity d mod 2, and supplied tolerance δ>0. Exact synthesis requires |p_orig|≤1 on [-1,1]. Modes are `exact`, `complete-numerical`, and `efficient-numerical` (the default numerical mode). Check degree/parity and the ORIGINAL interval promise before any shrink. The complete modes retain ROOTS on p_orig′, all real critical points and endpoints ±1. The efficient mode uses the locally proved exact multiplicity-stratum/Sturm decision: form f=1−p_orig²; successive gcds with derivatives separate multiplicity strata; Sturm-count only odd-multiplicity roots in the open interval, removing endpoint factors; check endpoint values and a nonroot sample among deg(f)+1 fixed rational interior points. This decides the same interval promise in O((d+1)^4) charged field/order operations, with no original-root isolation. INVALID is returned on failure. No unconstrained Cauchy-name zero test is used; ideal exact algebraic equality/order is an explicit charged resource.

Put τ=min(δ,8). In exact mode set p=p_orig. In complete-numerical mode set p=(1−τ/8)p_orig. In efficient-numerical mode shrink by 1−τ/16 and round each allowed coefficient to dyadic error at most τ/[64(d+1)], keeping forbidden parity coefficients zero. The interval promise implies every original coefficient is bounded by (d+1)d^d for d≥1 (bound1 for d=0), by equally spaced Lagrange interpolation; bounded binary search with exact coefficient comparisons therefore finds the dyadic rounding in polynomial(d,1+log_+(1/δ)) operations independently of original coefficient height. The rounded p has ||p−p_orig||∞≤5τ/64<τ/8 and ||p||∞≤1−3τ/64, and its rational complement has polynomial coefficient height. Steps1–5 below use this target p. The complete modes use the literal complete ROOTS schedule; efficient-numerical uses the separately proved bounded-grid GRID-ROOTS variant from OP0. All exact root-class, multiplicity, coefficient-identity, parity and failure checks remain in force.

1. Form R=1−p². If R is identically zero, set B=C=0. Otherwise factor R with complete ROOTS in the complete modes or the certified bounded-grid rational GRID-ROOTS in efficient-numerical mode, retaining multiplicities. Classify its roots into: zero; real s∈(0,1); real s≥1; purely imaginary s=ib,b>0; and s=a+ib,a,b>0. Negative and conjugate partners are represented once by these classes, with their multiplicities. A failed symmetry/nonnegativity certificate returns INVALID.
2. Start a polynomial pair `(B,C)=(K,0)`. K is the positive algebraic scale that makes the following root product equal R, obtained by comparing a nonzero polynomial coefficient after forming the unscaled product. For each zero pair multiply by x. For a real interior pair s∈(0,1), its multiplicity is even: multiply by `(x²−s²)` once for each two copies. For each real exterior pair use the pair `(sqrt(s²−1)x,s)`. For an imaginary pair ib use `(sqrt(b²+1)x,b)`. For each conjugate quadruple ±a±ib set `c=a²+b²+sqrt(2(a²+1)b²+(a²−1)²+b⁴)` and use `(c x²−(a²+b²),sqrt(c²−1)x)`.
3. Multiply two pairs by the rule `(B,C)*(D,E)=(BD−(1−x²)CE,BE+CD)`, which represents multiplication of `B+i sqrt(1−x²)C`. If the resulting B has the opposite parity to d, multiply once by `(x,1)`; its squared modulus is one and the degree slack permits this. Verify the exact coefficient identity `R=B²+(1−x²)C²`, the degree bounds and the two parities. A failed identity returns CERTIFICATE_ERROR.
4. Set P=p+iB and **Q=iC**. This is the source Theorem5 completion when the requested real part of Q is zero. Q=C also satisfies the norm identity and can be used as an admissible alternative in the reflected signal convention, but a source SU(2) transfer must keep its declared off-diagonal convention. Apply the current machine row's explicit leading-coefficient phase-peeling recursion, verifying its reflected polynomial matrix after every step. In efficient-numerical mode positive roots are counted quadratic tower descriptors with explicit Cauchy/radicand upper bounds; for unit peel ratio r=x+iy use i if x=-1 and otherwise s+iy/(2s), s the positive root of (1+x)/2 with known upper bound1. This changes only the square-root choice, and either root gives leading cancellation. Encode each phase by its algebraic unit-circle value; pad with W² pairs if the nominal degree exceeds the actual degree.
5. For numerical output use the explicit buffered certified atan2 variant in OP0 at tolerance τ/[16(d+1)] for each unit phase. Its scalar distance is exactly its diagonal phase operator error. A separately supplied terminating QC compiler receives a further τ/[16(d+1)] operator-error allocation; its work is separately charged. Target error is at most τ/8, total phase implementation error at most τ/8, and implemented real-part error at most τ/4≤δ. Generate a finite-basis circuit only through that selected compiler model. Return the phase list, complementary coefficient certificate, error certificates and charged operation counts. There is no hidden generation oracle.

**Separation of results/costs.** The efficient-numerical branch has a locally proved polynomial(d,1+log_+(1/δ)) count in the explicitly selected unit-charged exact-algebraic field/order tower/DAG arithmetic model, including validation, coefficient rounding, bounded-grid roots, completion, peeling, atan2 and referenced certificates. Original coefficient access/encoding, expanded minimal-polynomial conversion, implementation of exact field/equality/order operations and optional finite-basis compilation are separate resource coordinates; no hidden refinement, sign oracle, Turing-time or compiler bound follows. Complete modes retain finite termination without that height-independent count. ROOTS, coefficient products and phase peeling are finite classical instructions. Exact algebraic operations, their bit sizes, numerical root sensitivity, finite gate synthesis and ideal exact-real arithmetic are different cost coordinates. The existing QA23 completion/factorization/synthesis/error/query results must prove this procedure and its resource claims locally. Source: Gilyén et al., Theorem5/Lemma6, PDF pp.11–12, equations (9)–(12), and Theorem3 degree reduction, pp.8–10. All affected QSVT, inversion, matrix-function, Gibbs and simulation consumers retain their parity, normalization and phase conventions.

## OP2 — MNRS approximate reflection and finite tolerant recursive finding

Binds `alg-qa-18-reversible-walk-finding` and `thm-qa-18-reversible-search-cost`.

**Inputs.** Finite ergodic reversible row chain P; strictly positive π; lower absolute gap δ₀>0; marked predicate f with promise π(M)=0 or π(M)≥ε₀>0; stationary walk-state preparation A_π with cost S; update/row circuits, inverses and controlled walk W=U² with cost U; costed coherent bit check C; target failure ρ. The search acts on the declared `ran(T)+ran(ST)` signal space and its data registers. A row matrix alone supplies none of these circuits.

**Reflection R(β).** Set Δ₀=2sqrt(δ₀), s=ceil(log₂(4π/Δ₀)), k=max(1,ceil(log₂(2/β))). Append k separate s-bit zero phase registers. Coherently execute QA07 on W on each register, with controlled powers implemented by the declared repeated controlled-W calls. If at least one estimate register is nonzero apply −I. If all are zero apply the exact row-space reflection R_A=2TT†−I, using its costed row preparation/inverse and the ancilla-zero reflection. Then reverse all k QPE circuits. This full-space extension is deliberate: W has an additional zero-phase space A⊥∩B⊥ on which R_A=−I, whereas R_A=+I on the stationary state. Thus zero-phase complement states are not mistaken for the stationary vector. On nonzero signal eigenphases the exceptional all-zero-estimate amplitude is≤2^-k, so replacing −I by R_A there costs at most2^(1−k) in norm. Charge the controlled R_A call and its exact control predicate separately. This preserves the stationary state exactly and approximates plus-on-stationary/minus-on-every-orthogonal reflection; it is not a measurement of the walker. Charge 2k(2^s−1) controlled walk/inverse calls, phase-register gates and all-zero comparisons.

**Recursive circuit A_i.** Fix γ=1/120 and β_i=18γ/(4π³ i²), i≥1. A_0 is identity. For i>0, reserve a fresh K_i for R(β_i) and emit chronologically:

1. A_(i−1); marked phase flip on the first/data label; A_(i−1)†.
2. Coherently check every older K_j, j<i. If any is nonzero, apply −I; otherwise apply R(β_i) to walker and K_i. The branch is a block-diagonal unitary; compute/uncompute its test workspace.
3. A_(i−1).

The recursion decreases i and produces a finite list. Inverse means reversal and exact adjoints, not resetting a measured register.

**Tolerant wrapper.** If ε₀≥1/2, use capped direct stationary samples with membership verification. Otherwise let t be the least integer with `3^t arcsin(sqrt ε₀)≥π/4`; minimality also gives the upper endpoint 3π/4. Compute this by interval comparisons, or exact comparison with the algebraic threshold `sin²(π/(4·3^t))`; equality follows the ≥ branch. For each of `ceil(20 ln(1/ρ))+20` outer trials:

1. Prepare a stationary sample and verify its first label; return it if marked.
2. Prepare one fresh stationary walk state with all K_1,…,K_t zero. For i=1,…,t, apply A_i to the current state, measure marked/unmarked, and, if marked, measure/verify/output the first label. After an unmarked result **keep the conditional current state and auxiliary registers** and continue to i+1; do not reprepare or pretend the old ancillas are zero.
3. If all i fail, discard this trial and start the next fresh outer trial. After the outer cap return FAIL (under the empty/nonempty promise this may be reported as “no marked element” with the stated one-sided error).

Every trial is finite. Empty instances never return a false marked label. The success constant `1/12−3γ≥1/20`, recursion error and geometric cost sum belong to the separate MNRS results. Charges include old-ancilla tests and retained auxiliary space. Source: MNRS §3.2 Theorem6 and §4 ApproximateRAA/TolerantRAA, PDF pp.9,12,15; no-reset semantics appear in Lemma2's proof on p.15. This definition supplies the element-distinctness finding consumer rather than replacing it by detection.

## OP3 — Montanaro bounded-variance mean with finite dyadic subroutines

Binds `alg-qa-29-variance-aware-monte-carlo` and its guarantee/cost consumers.

**Inputs.** A finite coherent sampler V of a binary encoded real value v; V†, controlled access, value arithmetic and rotations; known rational σ≥0 with Var(v)≤σ²; ε>0; desired confidence 1−ρ. Encoding/rotation error is allocated separately to at most ε/8. If σ=0, one sample returns the constant value under the variance-zero promise. If ε≥σ/2, run the construction internally with ε′=min(ε,σ/2); otherwise ε′=ε. All range comparisons are on encoded finite values.

**PositiveMean(V,a,ρ₁).** v≥0, 0<a<1/2. Set k=ceil(log₂(1/a)), M the least power of two at least `1000 sqrt(k)/a`. For band 0 use `v 1_[0,1)`; for ℓ=1,…,k use `v 1_[2^(ℓ−1),2^ℓ)/2^ℓ`. For each band make its reversible comparator/value circuit and call Mean from OP0 at M with error probability `1/(10(k+1))`. Return `u₀+Σ_(ℓ=1)^k 2^ℓu_ℓ`; values≥2^k are not queried by a hidden tail routine. Repeat this whole positive estimator an odd number at least `ceil(2 ln(1/ρ₁)/(4/5−1/2)²)+1`, and return the median. Its tail bound and `a(1+||v||₂)²` error follow from the separate Cauchy–Schwarz/second-moment proof. The numerical constant1000 dominates the explicit QA13 constant `2π+π²<17` in that proof; no unspecified “sufficiently large D” remains.

**VarianceMean.** For one outer trial, measure a fresh sample m of v/σ. Define the coherent centered value B=v/σ−m. Use PositiveMean on `−B 1_(B<0)/4` and `B 1_(B≥0)/4`, each with a=ε′/(32σ) and failure1/9. Return `σ(m−4u₋+4u₊)`. Repeat an odd number at least `ceil(18 ln(1/ρ))+1` of independent outer trials and take the median. A bad coarse sample is a statistical error event, not an undefined branch; all loops still terminate at their caps. Include σ scaling, m storage, value comparisons, inverse circuits and final output bit precision.

The separate proof uses the event |m−E[v]/σ|≤3 (failure≤1/9), on which ||B/4||₂<1; two positive-estimation failures each cost1/9, and their errors sum to ε′. The resource rows must count the actual controlled-A/A† or reflection costs and logarithmic cutoffs. Source: Montanaro Algorithms1–3/Lemma4/Theorem5, PDF pp.8–12; the stronger mean-dependent QA13 error, not only its worst-case additive bound, is used in the dyadic sum.

## OP4 — Combined qubitized Hamiltonian simulation, including generation

Binds `alg-qa-25-qubitized-hamiltonian-simulation` and supplies the finite even/odd combination used by matrix-function/Gibbs consumers.

**Inputs.** Hermitian H with a costed exact block encoding of H/α, α>0; U,U†, controlled variants needed for real extraction and SELECT; both projector-phase circuits; rational t and 0<ε≤1. A block-error version receives an explicit certified block-error budget rather than ignoring it. Output is a clean-input dilation approximating |0_work>exp(−itH)|ψ>, with its error certificate; auxiliary states are only approximately zero. Return identity for t=0. No measurement of an unknown system input is used to “clean” it.

1. Put τ=αt and ρ=ε²/10000. Starting at d=max(1,ceil(|τ|)), increment d until `u=|τ|/(2(d+2))<1` and `2(|τ|/2)^(d+1)/((d+1)!(1−u))≤ρ/8`. This finite factorial-tail test bounds the Jacobi–Anger truncation. Each bound is evaluated with outward rational intervals.
2. For k=0,…,d generate `J_k(τ)=Σ_(j≥0)(−1)^j(τ/2)^(2j+k)/(j!(j+k)!)` by rational sums. Stop when the absolute next-term geometric tail, using ratio `τ²/[4(j+1)(j+k+1)]<1`, is≤ρ/(32(d+1)). Record a rational coefficient within that error. Generate Chebyshev T_k by `T₀=1,T₁=x,T_(k+1)=2xT_k−T_(k−1)`. Form the real even polynomial E from even k and the real odd polynomial O from odd k so that `E−iO` is the rounded truncation of exp(−iτx). Include the sign `(−i)^k` literally.
3. A certified combined polynomial error≤ρ implies |E|,|O|≤1+ρ on [-1,1]. Set B=1+2ρ and p_E=E/B,p_O=O/B; their exact rational descriptions have strict boundedness. Run OP1 separately on these two bare targets, with their actual parity/degree; execute each resulting real-QSVT extraction with its required controls. Pad the two ancillary interfaces explicitly to the same size.
4. Prepare a clean coefficient qubit in |+>. SELECT applies the E-block circuit in branch0 and `−i` times the O-block circuit in branch1. Unprepare the coefficient qubit. Its zero block is `M/2`, M=p_E(H/α)−i p_O(H/α), with ||M−exp(−itH)||≤3ρ. The coefficient phase −i is retained under any outer control.
5. Apply the explicit phase-corrected OAA circuit `−WRW†RW`, R=I−2Π_zero, as in OP1/QA21. Retain the resulting ancillary dilation and allocate total phase, gate and block implementation error≤ε/4. The QA21 robust isometry bound with η=3ρ is the separate route to the final ε claim. Under an outer control, control each **whole** block and the known correction; projective approximations with discarded scalar phases are inadmissible.

The generation loops, polynomial coefficients, two phase lists, LCU register and OAA list are outputs of finite classical construction. Charge degree d, both QSVT sequences, their controls, all PREPARE/SELECT/inverse calls, ROOTS and coefficient bit computation. The near-optimal degree theorem must prove the stated factorial-tail choice and local approximation budget; a sparse-row bound needs the actual sparse block-encoding construction and its location/value/precision costs. Source: Gilyén §5.1/Chebyshev–Jacobi–Anger construction; QA21 provides the explicitly proved robust OAA route rather than an undefined “combine even and odd” instruction.

## OP5 — HHL finite sine-window filter, uncomputation and capped output

Binds `alg-qa-26-hhl-phase-estimation`, its success/state/cost rows, and the supported Hermitian-dilation consumer.

**Inputs.** Costed preparation B of |b>, its inverse and controlled forms; Hermitian H with ||H||≤1 and supported eigenvalues `1/κ≤|λ|≤1`, κ≥1; controlled simulations exp(iHs) at declared times and precision; rational ε,ρ∈(0,1). Rectangular systems first use the explicit Hermitian dilation and codomain-supported input. A call S(s,η) is charged by its actual time/precision resource function; negative-time calls use its declared inverse.

1. Set t₀=2048κ/ε. Let T be the least power of two at least `8(t₀+1)`. Prepare the phase register's sine-window state `sqrt(2/T)Σ_(τ=0)^(T−1)sin(π(τ+1/2)/T)|τ>` from zero. This state has an on-demand coherent preparation: at bit layer j execute **one** reversible arithmetic kernel on the superposed j-bit prefix to determine its interval [a,b), then compute `W(a,b)=(b−a)/2−(sin(2πb/T)−sin(2πa/T))/(4 sin(π/T))`; apply the prefix-controlled rotation splitting weights W(a,mid),W(mid,b), then uncompute its arithmetic. Traverse the log₂T prefix layers, with total state-preparation error ε_Ψ=ε/(4096κ). Every nonempty prefix weight is at least sin²(π/(2T))≥1/T². Compute each W interval to absolute error at most `ε_Ψ²/(10^6(log₂T)²T^4)`; refine interval divisions until the conditional weight ratio has the corresponding certified error, clamp it to [0,1], and compute the Y-rotation angle to error≤ε_Ψ/(100 log₂T). Encode the angle in a binary register with at least `ceil(log₂(100π log₂T/ε_Ψ))+2` bits. Implement it by the list of fixed Y rotations controlled by those bits, then uncompute the angle and weight registers. Zero/one ratios use the explicit identity/π branch. This is one coherent kernel per layer, never enumeration of 2^j prefix gates. Its reversible arithmetic/gate/bit complexity must be proved and charged; the optional explicit table variant instead pays O(T) preparation/data cost. This computes weights on demand rather than assuming a loaded amplitude table. T=1 would be the identity boundary but is not reached here.
2. For each phase bit j=0,…,log₂T−1 apply controlled exp(iH·2^j t₀/T). The sum of evolution times is<t₀; divide the allocated simulation-error budget `ε/(4096κ)` among these calls. Apply the **negative-sign** Fourier transform. Decode y to signed k=y for y<T/2 and k=y−T otherwise, and λ̃=2πk/t₀. T≥8(t₀+1) keeps the supported spectrum away from alias endpoints.
3. Append a three-outcome register represented on two qubits, with labels nothing,well,ill and one unused basis label. For u=|λ̃| define:
   - if u≥1/κ: f=1/(2κu), g=0;
