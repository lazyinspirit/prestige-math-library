## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed connected Riemann surface with positive area form $\eta$, and let $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$, $m\ge1$, with standard complex structure $J_0$, quotient $q:\Sigma^m\to X$, and repeated-point locus $\Delta$. Off $\Delta$, the product form $\Omega=\sum_l\operatorname{pr}_l^*\eta$ descends to $\omega$. Fix a smooth target metric $g$ and finitely many attaching curves with finitely many complementary regions $R$. A product torus $L_\gamma$ uses one point on each of a labeled system of $m$ pairwise disjoint curves $\gamma_i$ among those curves. Choose a point $a_R$ in each region. Suppose a smooth path $J_t$ agrees with $J_0$ on a fixed neighborhood $V$ of $\Delta$ and on a fixed neighborhood of every $E_{a_R}=\{a_R\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}\Sigma$, and tames $\omega$ on $X\setminus V$.

Let $u$ be a smooth $J_\tau$-holomorphic map for any fixed $\tau\in[0,1]$, either from the oriented sphere, or from a compact disk with its entire boundary on $L_\gamma$. Let $n_R(u)=n_{a_R}(u)$ be the actual absolute or relative divisor degree constructed by the point-divisor intersection lemma. Then every $n_R(u)$ is a nonnegative integer, and a constant $C$ depending only on the fixed geometry and path satisfies
$$E_g(u)\le C\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\eta.$$
This includes spheres lying entirely in $\Delta$ or in a point divisor; no off-diagonal-end hypothesis is present.

For a sphere all region coefficients equal one nonnegative integer $d_0$, and $n_a(u)=d_0$ for every $a\in\Sigma$. For a one-torus disk, let $A_i$ be the connected components of $\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_j\gamma_j$. Its coefficient function is constant on each $A_i$, say $b_i\ge0$, and its domain is $\sum_i b_i[A_i]$; in particular its boundary has only $\gamma$ curves. If one marked point $z_i$ is chosen in each $A_i$ away from all attaching curves, then $n_{z_i}(u)=b_i$.

Consequently a sphere with one zero point-divisor degree is constant. A one-torus disk with all the degrees $n_{z_i}$ zero is constant. The disk conclusion uses one marked point in each actual complement component, as provided by a complete marked cut system, and does not require any admissibility assertion about two-torus periodic domains or a Maslov-index formula. Applying them to a zero-mark degeneration still requires a proved additivity statement for the divisor degrees of its limiting components.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compact source map, fixed almost complex path, symmetric-product geometry, region points, curve systems and optional one-per-component marked points in the statement.

[F1] [[lem-holomorphic-symmetric-product-curves-admit-finite-branched-ordered-lifts]] constructs a finite compact branched ordered lift for a holomorphic closed-source map into the symmetric product even when it lies wholly in $\Delta$. [[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]], [[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]] and [[thm-removable-singularity-characterizations]] give the local discriminant, simple-root and bounded-root filling arguments when the diagonal preimage is isolated.

[F2] [[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]] gives actual integer divisor degrees, invariant under all point movement for a sphere and under movement avoiding boundary coordinate curves for a disk; proper local holomorphic intersections compute these degrees positively, including after a justified point move from a contained divisor.

[F3] [[lem-degree-is-well-defined-and-independent-of-the-normalized-top-form]] and [[thm-regular-value-formula-for-compact-support-degree]] give the actual degree and area formula for proper oriented surface maps, including the signed regular-fiber formula. [[thm-morse-sard-for-euclidean-maps]] supplies common regular values by finite or countable charts under Countable Choice.



## Proof

1.1 First construct the ordered lift for these actual compact maps. If a sphere is wholly in $\Delta$, it is globally $J_0$-holomorphic because $J_\tau=J_0$ at every point of its image; the ordinary coordinate Cauchy–Riemann equation follows directly. Apply [F1] to get a compact branched covering $p:F\to S^2$ of degree $d\le m!$ and a globally holomorphic ordered lift $\widetilde u:F\to\Sigma^m$. Otherwise $u^{-1}(\Delta)$ is finite. Indeed the local discriminant composition is holomorphic near each inverse image, because $J_\tau=J_0$ near $\Delta$, and has isolated zeros unless it is identically zero there. The set of interior source points having a neighborhood mapped into $\Delta$ is open and closed: at a limit point the local discriminant and zero-order factorization force local vanishing. Connectedness would then make the whole image diagonal. This was excluded for the sphere case under consideration, and is impossible for a disk because the product torus consists of distinct coordinates and avoids $\Delta$. For a disk that boundary avoidance also gives a whole boundary collar disjoint from the inverse image. Compactness now makes the isolated interior set finite. Pull back the full ordered covering away from it, with degree $d=m!$. At each missing point complete its finitely many monodromy cycles by $p(\zeta)=\zeta^r$. Near that point the tuple map is $J_0$-holomorphic, so each lifted bounded root is holomorphic off the center by the simple-root theorem and extends by bounded removal [F1]. This gives a smooth compact possibly disconnected bordered or closed Riemann surface $F$ and smooth ordered lift with $q\widetilde u=up$. The covering is unbranched on disk boundaries, where its coordinate maps have boundary in the union of $\gamma$ curves. Let $B$ denote the finite exceptional base set in this construction, or that of the wholly diagonal lift in [F1]. The lift is holomorphic wherever $u$ maps into a region on which $J_\tau=J_0$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 There is a uniform energy bound $$E_g(u)\le\frac{C}{d}\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega.$$ For a wholly diagonal sphere the lift is holomorphic everywhere, so its product Hermitian energy is exactly $\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega$. The smooth map $q$ on compact $\Sigma^m$ has a bounded derivative, and conformal covering change of variables gives $E_g(up)=dE_g(u)$, including branch points since the scalar conformal factors cancel off that finite measure-zero set. This proves the bound in that case. In the other case choose $V_0$ with $\Delta\subset V_0\Subset V$, where $V$ is a common diagonal neighborhood on which all $J_t=J_0$; for $m=1$ take these diagonal sets empty. On compact $X\setminus V_0$, uniform tameness gives $\tfrac12(|v|_g^2+|J_tv|_g^2)\le C\omega(v,J_tv)$ for all vectors and parameters: outside $V$ this is assumed, and within $V\setminus\Delta$ it follows from the positive product form and $J_t=J_0$. Over that part, ordered-cover change of variables bounds the map energy by $C/d$ times the lifted product area. Over $u^{-1}(V_0)$ the ordered lift is holomorphic and the bounded derivative of $q$ gives the same bound as for the wholly diagonal case. Both total lifted area densities are nonnegative on their respective parts. Taking the larger fixed constants proves the displayed global estimate. All coordinate area integrals are absolutely integrable because the completed lift is smooth on compact $F$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Write $f_l=\operatorname{pr}_l\widetilde u$. For each complementary region $R$, the map $f_l:f_l^{-1}(R)\to R$ is proper: the full source is compact, and its possible boundary maps into attaching curves outside $R$. Applying [F3] to its connected components gives an integer $d_{l,R}$, the total signed count over any common regular value, with $$\int_{f_l^{-1}(R)}f_l^*\eta=d_{l,R}\int_R\eta.$$ Here the aggregate formula is justified even if the open preimage has many components. A compactly supported target form has compact preimage meeting only finitely many open components. Any nonzero-degree component contains a preimage of any supplied common regular value, by the degree formula, so only finitely many components have nonzero degree because that regular fiber is compact and discrete. Sum their compact-support formulas and exhaust $R$ by compactly supported cutoffs; absolute integrability in step 2.1 passes to the limit. The inverse image of the curve union contributes zero to each two-form integral: where $df_l$ has rank below two its pullback is zero, and at rank-two points the inverse function theorem identifies that inverse image locally with finitely many arcs and crossings, a measure-zero set. A countable coordinate cover preserves this property. Hence $$\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega=\sum_{l,R}d_{l,R}\int_R\eta.$$ [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We now identify these degrees with the actual integer divisor multiplicities and prove positivity, without assuming proper intersection at the original $a_R$. Since $E_{a_R}$ is compact and addition $\Sigma\times\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}\Sigma\to X$ is continuous, there is a small coordinate disk $U_R\Subset R$ about $a_R$ such that every $E_a$, $a\in U_R$, lies inside the fixed open region where $J_t=J_0$. Compactness also makes each such $E_a$ have a neighborhood with that same property. Choose $a\in U_R$ which is a common regular value of all finitely many $f_l$ and avoids the finitely many coordinate values of $\widetilde u$ on $p^{-1}(B)$; [F3] ensures such choices, including when a coordinate map is constant. Its inverse image under the coordinate maps is finite, and the tuple cannot lie entirely in $E_a$, since that would force its whole covering surface into the union of those finitely many fibers. Thus $u^{-1}(E_a)$ is finite and lies away from base branch values. Near each such source point the map is $J_0$-holomorphic, and its ordered roots are holomorphic. At an unbranched sheet, each coordinate attaining $a$ has a simple zero in its local coordinate, by regularity. The local defining function of $E_a$ is the product of these root differences times a nonzero factor. Its zero order is therefore the number of those coordinates, including repeated identical roots. Each of the $d$ ordered sheets contributes exactly that many to the sum of coordinate regular-fiber counts. Positivity and [F2] give $$\sum_l d_{l,R}=d\,n_a(u)=d\,n_{a_R}(u)=d\,n_R(u),\qquad n_R(u)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}.$$ The last equality is justified point movement: $a,a_R$ lie in $R$, which avoids the actual boundary coordinate curves, or the source is a sphere. This argument remains valid if the original sphere was wholly in $E_{a_R}$ or $\Delta$; the degree is defined there by [F2], and the chosen nearby divisor is proper. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Substitute the degree identity of step 4.1 into step 3.1 and then step 2.1. It gives $$E_g(u)\le C\sum_R n_R(u)\int_R\eta,$$ with the same constant independent of the particular compact map and fixed parameter. This is the actual compact-bubble energy inequality. It has not been obtained by applying an off-diagonal strip theorem to a contained sphere. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For a sphere each $f_l$ is a proper map from a closed oriented surface to the connected closed surface $\Sigma$, and has one global signed degree $d_l$ by [F3]. Its region degree $d_{l,R}$ is this same integer at any supplied regular value. Thus step 4.1 gives one nonnegative integer $d_0=d^{-1}\sum_l d_l$ for every region. By the absolute point-movement assertion [F2], $n_a(u)=d_0$ for every $a\in\Sigma$, including any contained point divisor. The whole coefficient function is $d_0[\Sigma]$. A zero degree at one point makes this entire function zero, and step 5.1 makes the map energy zero. Smoothness and connectedness then make $u$ constant. [F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For a one-torus disk apply the same proper-map degree argument to each connected component $A_i$ of $\Sigma\setminus\bigcup_j\gamma_j$, rather than to the smaller regions cut out by both curve systems. The source boundary of every $f_l$ lies in the $\gamma$ union, so its degree on $A_i$ is constant. Every region $R\subset A_i$ has this same degree for that map; step 4.1 therefore makes $n_R(u)$ equal to one nonnegative integer $b_i$ throughout $A_i$. This proves the actual domain identity $D(u)=\sum_i b_i[A_i]$ and that its chain boundary has only $\gamma$ curves. By [F2], relative degree is constant when a point moves inside $A_i$, since that movement avoids the disk's boundary coordinate curves; hence a marked $z_i\in A_i$ has $n_{z_i}(u)=b_i$, regardless of the other attaching curves. All these degrees zero makes every coefficient zero, and step 5.1 again forces zero energy and a constant map. This establishes the asserted individual compact-bubble exclusions without an admissibility or Maslov premise. Divisor-degree additivity in a degeneration remains a separate necessary input when using these exclusions on a limiting configuration. [F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
