## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact oriented surface with supplied finite smooth triangle data, with circular interior and interval boundary links. Let $E\to M\times[0,1]$ be a complex line bundle and $s$ a continuous section, smooth on each end slice. Assume $s$ is nonzero on $\partial M\times[0,1]$, and that $s_0,s_1$ have finitely many isolated zeros, none on the vertices or edges of the supplied triangles. At an isolated zero its index is the winding of its coordinate value in any local complex frame along a small positive coordinate circle. Then
$$\sum_{s_0(p)=0}\operatorname{ind}_p(s_0)=\sum_{s_1(p)=0}\operatorname{ind}_p(s_1).$$
Intermediate zero sets may be nonisolated. No genericity of the family is assumed.

Consequently, let $Z$ be a closed oriented embedded codimension-two submanifold without boundary in an oriented smooth manifold $W$, with a supplied metric oriented normal disk tube, and let $F:M\times[0,1]\to W$ be a continuous homotopy smooth on the end slices. Assume its boundary image avoids $Z$ for every parameter and its end slices have finitely many isolated intersections with $Z$ away from the triangle edges. The algebraic end intersection sums are equal. Here a local intersection number is the winding of the oriented normal coordinate of $F$ around a small source circle; at a transverse intersection it is the usual sign of the induced isomorphism from the oriented source tangent plane to the oriented normal plane of $Z$. No compactness assertion for a noncompact homotopy is implicit: this statement uses the compact parameterized surface $M\times[0,1]$.

The following boundary version is also valid with explicit extra data. Suppose the relative problem has a supplied smooth reflection double, on which the doubled two surfaces and homotopy meet the preceding hypotheses. Require that reflection interchange the two interior copies and preserve their local intersection signs, and that every boundary intersection have just one doubled local representative, with the indicated sign. Then the conserved quantity on one copy is
$$I=\sum_{\text{interior intersections}}\operatorname{sign}+\frac12\sum_{\text{boundary intersections}}\operatorname{sign},$$
or the same expression with isolated local multiplicities. The assertion includes no construction of a global reflection double for arbitrary boundary data.

An explicit local model realizes the half weight. In $\mathbb C_a\times\mathbb C_b$ with complex product orientation, take the graph $G=\{(z^{r+1},z):z\in\mathbb C\}$ and its translate $G_\varepsilon=\{(z^{r+1},z+\varepsilon):z\in\mathbb C\}$ for $r\ge0$ and real $\varepsilon\ne0$. Their finite intersections have total positive multiplicity $r$. Restricting both graphs to $\operatorname{Im}z\ge0$, with intersections on the real boundary counted by one half, gives total $r/2$. These local counts concern a neighborhood containing all those roots, and hence may be used in a sufficiently small translated power-branch model.

All assertions above require no choice principle. The finite tube and any reflection double are hypotheses, not unproved consequences about a general Whitney or cylindrical representative.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite surface and bundle data, continuous homotopy and nonvanishing boundary, and, in the geometric assertions, the supplied oriented tube and, when used, the specified reflection double.

## Proof

1.1 **Frames and local winding facts on finite disks.** Every complex bundle on a compact disk or disk times an interval has a continuous frame by a finite construction. Take a finite bundle-chart cover and scalar bumps forming a partition of unity, embed fibers complex linearly in $\mathbb C^N$, and form their continuous orthogonal projection $Q=V(V^*V)^{-1}V^*$ in local column matrices. In a star-shaped parametrization of the compact base, uniform continuity provides a finite radial subdivision $x_j=x_0+j(x-x_0)/N_0$ such that $\|Q(x_{j+1})-Q(x_j)\|<1/2$ for every $x,j$. Projection from one image to the next is injective because its change is less than half the input norm; dimensions agree, so it is an isomorphism. The product $Q(x_{N_0})\cdots Q(x_1)$ transports a fixed basis at $x_0$ to a continuous frame everywhere. For a nonzero complex loop define winding by finite local argument lifts; products add winding, reversing orientation negates it, and a continuous homotopy keeps it constant, using uniform continuity and local argument of neighboring ratios. A disk frame change has zero boundary winding because its nonzero determinant contracts radially, so local zero index is frame independent. An orientation-positive source coordinate change also preserves winding: on a sufficiently small circle it deforms to its positive derivative through invertible local coordinates, and a positive linear map deforms to the identity by real Gram–Schmidt, positive rescaling and rotation. Finally, on a Euclidean polygon minus finitely many small round disks, any nonzero complex map has outer winding equal to the sum of its positive hole windings: subdivide by a finite fine grid on which local arguments exist and telescope boundary changes, cancelling interior segments. Grid lines can avoid all circle tangencies and circle-grid vertices because there are finitely many forbidden positions. Thus on each supplied face the winding of a section nonzero on its boundary is the sum of its isolated zero indices inside. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Construct an auxiliary nonzero section on the entire edge graph times the interval.** At a boundary vertex use the given nonzero section $s$. At every other vertex choose a nonzero continuous path in its line bundle over $[0,1]$, using a frame from step 1.1 and the constant coordinate one. On boundary edges retain the prescribed $s$, which is nonzero there. On an interior edge times the interval use a frame from step 1.1. The two endpoint vertex paths are represented by nonzero functions $a(t),b(t)$. Each has a continuous complex logarithm on $[0,1]$, constructed by finite argument lifts. If $x\in[0,1]$ is the edge coordinate, $\exp((1-x)\log a(t)+x\log b(t))$ is a nonzero section matching both vertices. There are finitely many edges, so these prescriptions define a continuous nonzero section $r$ on the whole one-skeleton times the interval. It agrees with $s$ on the outer boundary. It is not required to agree with either end section on the interior edges. No family of choices indexed by paths or points has been used. [given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Compute the invariant from this graph, and compare the actual end sections.** For each face $D$ choose a complex frame on $D\times[0,1]$ by step 1.1. The winding $w_D(r_t)$ along its oriented boundary is constant in $t$, since $r$ is nonzero on that boundary cylinder. At either endpoint $t=0,1$, $s_t$ is nonzero on the graph and the ratio $s_t/r_t$ is therefore a globally defined nonzero complex function there, independent of the local line frame. In each face boundary, product winding gives $w_D(s_t)-w_D(r_t)=w_D(s_t/r_t)$. Sum over faces. On interior edges the ratio is the same and traversed in opposite directions, so its argument changes cancel; on the outer boundary it is one. Thus $\sum_Dw_D(s_t)=\sum_Dw_D(r_t)$. The finite face excision of step 1.1 identifies the left side with the end zero index sum. The right side is constant by its construction. This proves the first assertion even if $s$ has an arbitrary nonisolated intermediate zero set; no transversality or one-manifold trace was required. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **Build the actual normal divisor line.** Use the metric and orientation in the supplied normal disk tube to regard its normal rank-two bundle as a complex line. On the tube $N$ over $Z$, pull this line back by the tube projection and denote its tautological vector at a point of $N$ by $v$. On $W\setminus Z$ use the trivial complex line. Over $N\setminus Z$ glue these bundles by sending a normal vector $\lambda$ to the unique complex scalar $\lambda/v$. This is a smooth nonzero transition function in every complex normal frame, and it is independent of that frame. The sections $v$ and $1$ match under the transition and define a global section $\sigma$ whose zero set is exactly $Z$. Its normal derivative on $Z$ is the identity, with the prescribed normal orientation. Pull back this line and section by $F$. The result satisfies the first assertion on the compact $M\times[0,1]$, and its local end zero index is exactly the stated local intersection number. At a transverse intersection a sufficiently small circle is homotopic through nonzero normal vectors to its invertible derivative image; real positive and negative matrices have degree $+1,-1$, respectively, by the same matrix deformation as in step 1.1. This proves the ordinary geometric intersection assertion. [step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Double and then divide by two, with the data kept explicit.** Apply step 4.1 to the supplied doubled problem. Every interior intersection on one copy has a distinct reflected partner of the same local sign or multiplicity by hypothesis. A boundary intersection is fixed and gives the single doubled local intersection stipulated in the data. Hence the doubled total is exactly twice the interior total plus the boundary total, that is $2I$. It is invariant by step 4.1, so $I$ is invariant. This argument establishes the half weight whenever the indicated double is actually supplied; it neither infers a double from a boundary condition alone nor assumes compactness for ends not included in the finite domain. [step 4.1, given, algebra]

6.1 **Compute the translated branch model completely.** Intersections of the displayed graphs satisfy $w=z+\varepsilon$ and $w^{r+1}=z^{r+1}$. There is no solution with $z=0$. Write $w=\zeta z$, where $\zeta^{r+1}=1$ and $\zeta\ne1$. Then $z=\varepsilon/(\zeta-1)$, giving exactly $r$ distinct roots. For $r=0$ the root set is empty. Each root is simple: the complex Jacobian of the two intersection equations has determinant, up to a fixed sign, $(r+1)(w^r-z^r)$, which is nonzero since $w/z=\zeta\ne1$ and $\zeta^r=\zeta^{-1}\ne1$. The equations are holomorphic in $(w,z)$, so every nonzero complex Jacobian has positive real determinant; each local intersection sign is therefore $+1$. Complex conjugation pairs the nonreal roots because $\varepsilon$ is real. A real root occurs precisely for $\zeta=-1$, hence at most once. Every conjugate pair contributes one root to the upper-half restriction, and each real root has weight one half. The weighted sum is consequently $r/2$. Scaling $\varepsilon$ scales all these roots, so they lie in any prescribed small neighborhood for sufficiently small nonzero translation. This proves the final local assertion with its boundary convention. [step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
