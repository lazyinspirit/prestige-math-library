## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $U$ be a connected oriented smooth handlebody with boundary $\Sigma$. Supply $m$ pairwise disjoint smooth compression disks NEAT at their boundary, actual compatible disk-face collars, and compatible roundings of the cut corners. Suppose cutting along them gives $k\ge1$ parametrized smooth balls. Put $h=m-k+1$, and suppose $h\ge0$.

These finite data construct an orientation-compatible finite polygon presentation of the ACTUAL surface $\Sigma$, with word $\prod_{i=1}^h A_iB_iA_i^{-1}B_i^{-1}$ when $h>0$, and the standard finite sphere presentation when $h=0$. The construction gives the quotient map and finite subdivision into smooth face pieces, rather than inferring their existence from an abstract surface classification. In particular these are the actual finite polygon data required by the diagonal-degree and multipointed sphere-index suppliers.

The construction allows all pairs of faces of the cut balls, including loop pairs on the same ball. It does not require any knot arcs and does not change $\Sigma$ or its orientation. Neat disks, parametrized cut balls and their compatible face collars are hypotheses: no auxiliary tangential disk normalization or identification of a raw cusp cut with a smooth corner is asserted here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The connected oriented handlebody, finite neat disk system, compatible cut-face collars, and actual parametrizations of its $k$ rounded cut balls.

[F1] A parametrized smooth ball with a parametrized boundary disk can be put in the standard window form. Attaching such a ball along a supplied collared disk, with compatible rounding, preserves the smooth diffeomorphism type, with explicit local cap straightening. ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-parametrized-ball-windows-and-disk-absorption]])

[F2] Finite planar disk-with-holes boundary maps extend to diffeomorphisms with all prescribed collar germs, under Countable Choice. ([[lem-finite-planar-hole-boundary-maps-extend-with-prescribed-collars]])

## Proof

1.1 Form the finite dual graph with one vertex for each cut ball and one edge for each compression disk, joining its two disk faces; loop edges are retained. This graph is connected. Indeed the original connected handlebody is reconstructed from those disjoint balls by just these paired face identifications and their collar products. A disconnected graph would split that reconstruction into two disjoint open-and-closed unions. Choose a finite spanning tree. Undo the cuts corresponding to its $k-1$ edges one at a time from a leaf of the tree. At each step two distinct currently merged balls are joined through one product $D^2\times I$ of the given face collar. Absorb this product ball along its first end window and then the other cut ball along its opposite window by [F1]. Both are actual smooth window attachments with the supplied parameters. The result is again a parametrized smooth ball with all remaining faces and their collars transported by the resulting diffeomorphisms. After $k-1$ steps, the remaining $h=m-k+1$ disk cuts give ONE parametrized ball, with its $2h$ paired face disks. Loop edges cause no difficulty: they were not used as tree edges and remain among the paired faces. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Its boundary is an actual parametrized sphere. Remove the interiors of the $2h$ face disks to obtain a punctured sphere $P$; the original $\Sigma$ is recovered by reidentifying its paired rims. The pairing reverses the boundary orientation induced by $P$, because the two signed sides of a neat disk have opposite outward normals. Its collars are signed collars of the original attaching circle. If $h=0$, the ball parameter already identifies $\Sigma$ with the standard sphere, which has its explicit two-hemisphere finite cell decomposition and standard sphere polygon. Hence suppose $h>0$. Choose inside the finite plane of a standard target sphere $h$ disjoint round cluster disks $Q_i$, each containing two smaller disjoint round holes $F_i^+,F_i^-$ and no other hole; their sizes and centers can be specified by finitely many rational numbers. Pair the two inner rims in each cluster by a fixed orientation-reversing reflection between the two round circles. All the exterior cluster disks miss the point at infinity. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Send the source punctured sphere to this standard punctured sphere by [F2], carrying paired rims and signed collars to the assigned paired rims and collars. More explicitly, choose an orientation-preserving rim map $b_i^+$ on one face; if $g_i$ is its original orientation-reversing pairing and $r_i$ the target reflection, prescribe $b_i^-=r_i b_i^+g_i^{-1}$ on the other. This preserves the induced boundary orientation too. Prescribe the paired target collars using the same point parameter on the two rims and opposite signed normal parameters. To apply the planar supplier, put the source stereographic pole inside one source face and the target pole inside its assigned face; that face becomes the outer boundary of a bounded planar hole domain, with all other faces its inner holes. This change of sphere coordinates is only for the application of [F2]. The resulting diffeomorphism agrees with every specified collar and descends after gluing to a smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphism from the original $\Sigma$ to the target rim quotient. In signed collar coordinates this descended map is the same smooth map on the positive and negative half-collars, with its smooth inverse; no abstract quotient smoothness is being assumed. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We describe a finite cut graph of that explicit target. In the quotient of each $Q_i$ let $A_i$ be the identified inner rim, and choose an embedded arc in $Q_i$ joining its two inner rims, with its endpoints paired; its image is a loop $B_i$ meeting $A_i$ once. Round the arc at that crossing in the signed collar. Choose its orientation so the ordered tangent directions $(A_i,B_i)$ have positive surface orientation. Cutting first along $A_i$ recovers the disk with two holes $Q_i\setminus(\operatorname{int}F_i^+\cup\operatorname{int}F_i^-)$; cutting along the joining arc merges its two inner boundaries and gives an annulus. Thus a thin radial crosscut from the crossing vertex to $\partial Q_i$ opens this annulus to a disk. These operations can be checked directly in a planar picture-free slit model: the two arc banks become the two consecutive boundary intervals, and its endpoint half-circles join those intervals in the unique order with region on the left. The new inner boundary word is $A_iB_iA_i^{-1}B_i^{-1}$ with the chosen orientation. No other cluster is involved. [step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The exterior of the cluster disks in the standard sphere is a sphere with $h$ holes. Choose $h-1$ disjoint arcs joining its boundary circles in a tree: since the disks lie in specified disjoint round clusters, they may be placed along a line and joined in that order by short corridor arcs missing every other disk. Cutting those arcs opens the exterior to a disk. Together with the radial crosscuts of step4.1 they form a tree connecting the $h$ crossing vertices of the $A_i,B_i$ pairs. Its union with the $2h$ loops is an embedded finite graph with disk complement. Replace this tree by one vertex without collapsing the SURFACE. Take a thin regular neighborhood of each tree edge and its incident vertices, which is a planar disk obtained by joining two small vertex disks by a rectangle. Inside that disk replace the old incident graph by a star joining the same cyclically ordered boundary endpoints to one interior point. The boundary endpoints and their order are fixed; the sectors of either star construction correspond to the same disk-complement intervals. Thus the new graph still has disk complement. Iterating over the finite tree gives one vertex and $2h$ oriented loop edges on the same target surface. This is a finite replacement of an auxiliary graph inside disks, not a diffeomorphism collapsing an edge to a point. [step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Trace the boundary of its complement with the surface on the left. Inside the $i$th cluster step4.1 gives the four successive letters $A_i,B_i,A_i^{-1},B_i^{-1}$. The exterior tree corridors insert only paired connecting banks; the star replacements in step5.1 eliminate those banks and concatenate the cluster words in their chosen order. Hence the actual boundary word is $\prod_i[A_i,B_i]$, with its positive orientation. The disk-complement closure is obtained by finitely many planar slits and regular disk sectors; parameterize each sector as a smooth face piece and subdivide finitely at the slit endpoints and vertex rays. Their characteristic maps extend smoothly on each such piece and give the required finite polygon quotient data. Pull them back through the actual diffeomorphism of step3.1 to the original $\Sigma$. No change of surface or orientation occurred. Every graph, band, face, collar and replacement was finite, besides [F2]'s stated Countable Choice suppliers. This proves precisely the supplied-input polygon construction, retaining the distinction between these neat cut data and any still-unprepared tangential compression witnesses. [F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
