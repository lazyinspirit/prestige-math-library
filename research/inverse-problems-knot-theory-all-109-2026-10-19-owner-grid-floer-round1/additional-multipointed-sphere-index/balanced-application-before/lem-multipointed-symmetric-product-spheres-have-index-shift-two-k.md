## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed oriented genus-$h$ Riemann surface with a supplied orientation-compatible finite polygon presentation as in the finite diagonal-degree theorem, and let $m\ge1$. Put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$. For every smooth sphere map $u:S^2\to X$ and every $z\in\Sigma$, define $n_z(u)$ to be the degree of $u^*\mathcal O(E_z)$, where $E_z=\{z\}+\operatorname{Sym}^{m-1}(\Sigma)$ and degree is finite face winding, including when the sphere image is contained in $E_z$. Then
$$\deg(u^*\det_{\mathbb C}TX)=(m+1-h)n_z(u).$$
In particular this formula is valid for arbitrary smooth sphere classes, not only holomorphic maps or simple incidence curves. It is unchanged on replacing the standard complex tangent bundle by a homotopic complex structure on the same real tangent bundle.

Let a smooth fixed-end Whitney strip model with actual product end frames represent a class $\phi$, and let $\phi\#u$ be its interior connected sum with such a sphere, using a specified attaching path and keeping the physical boundary and end data fixed. Its actual real Fredholm indices, before source translation, satisfy
$$\mu(\phi\#u)-\mu(\phi)=2(m+1-h)n_z(u).$$
Thus when $m=h+k-1$ the sphere shift is exactly $2k\,n_z(u)$. This includes all positive and negative sphere degrees. No assertion that the supplied finite polygon follows automatically from an unspecified handlebody cut, no full domain/corner formula for a nonspherical Whitney class, and no boundary-degeneration count, absolute grading or invariance theorem is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual finite oriented surface polygon, standard symmetric product, arbitrary smooth sphere map, and the specified Whitney models when applying the index conclusion.

[F1] The quotient determinant identity is $q^*\det(TX)=(\bigotimes_i p_i^*T\Sigma)\otimes\mathcal O(\sum_{i<j}\Delta_{ij})$, with actual line and pullback-degree constructions at all collisions. ([[lem-symmetric-product-quotient-determinant-has-vandermonde-ramification]])

[F2] The finite polygon calculation constructs actual circle maps $a_r,b_r$ and proves the diagonal-degree and tangent-degree formulas without full AC. ([[lem-finite-polygon-surface-diagonal-degree-pairing]])

[F3] The symmetric-product point divisor has its actual coefficient-polynomial line and canonical section; its sphere degree is invariant under moving the point even for contained images. Finite disk frames and winding give homotopy invariance of that degree. ([[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]])

[F4] Smooth relative Whitney models have actual Fredholm index given by their squared determinant boundary phase, invariant under relative smooth homotopies and additive under matching-end concatenation. ([[lem-smooth-relative-whitney-homotopies-preserve-strip-fredholm-index]])

[F5] Finite-dimensional parametric transversality and smooth Sard hold under Countable Choice. We use a finite family of compactly supported coordinate variations and finitely many strata or maps. ([[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]])

[F6] Smooth compact-set bump localization and the fundamental theorem on flows supply the finite supported coordinate vector fields and their smooth parameter-dependent local evolutions. ([[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-on-flows]])

## Proof

1.1 We first construct an ordered lift after a harmless smooth homotopy; no holomorphic representative is assumed. The multiplicity partitions of $m$ stratify $X$ into finitely many smooth complex submanifolds. A partition with $r$ distinct support points has complex codimension $m-r$, as seen in the product coefficient charts. All strata except the distinct-root stratum and the single-double-root stratum have real codimension at least four. In finitely many target coordinate neighborhoods of the compact sphere image choose finitely many supported coordinate vector fields spanning its tangent fibers. Compose their small flows to vary the sphere; the parameter derivative at zero spans every target tangent space, and compactness preserves this spanning on one common small parameter ball. Thus its universal evaluation is a submersion. By [F5] a parameter arbitrarily close to zero makes the sphere transverse to every partition stratum. The resulting homotopic smooth map $u'$ misses all codimension-at-least-four strata and meets the simple-double-root stratum transversely in finitely many points $B$. Finiteness follows because the full repeated-point locus is closed, the higher strata are missed by the compact image, and its preimage is then a compact zero-dimensional manifold. The flow homotopy changes neither determinant-line degree nor point-divisor degree, using finite bundle frames over the compact parameterized sphere. [F1, F3, F5, F6, given, construct]

2.1 Over $S^2\setminus B$ take the finite set of all $m!$ orderings of the distinct tuple $u'(x)$. Locally its simple roots vary smoothly, so this set is an actual unbranched degree-$d$ covering with $d=m!$ and ordered map $F=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$; it may be disconnected. Near a point of $B$, the target coefficient chart has one colliding pair. Its quadratic discriminant $a$ is a complex normal coordinate to that stratum, and $a\circ u'$ has invertible real derivative. Complete this to a small source chart; all other roots and the colliding pair's center are smooth functions there. The monodromy exchanges just those two roots. Fill each two-cycle of ordered sheets by a disk: if $a\circ u'$ preserves orientation, set $a\circ u'\circ\pi=\zeta^2$; if it reverses orientation, set it equal to $\overline{\zeta}^{2}$. In both cases $\pi$ is an orientation-preserving smooth branched covering, and the ordered roots are the smooth center plus or minus $\zeta/2$ or $\overline{\zeta}/2$, with any fixed quadratic normalization absorbed into the coordinate. Unchanged labels give the same fill on their respective ordered sheets. This constructs a compact oriented surface $C$, smooth maps $f_i:C\to\Sigma$, and a degree-$d$ branched map $\pi:C\to S^2$ such that $qF=u'\pi$. There are finitely many filled disks. Cutting the punctured base sphere by finitely many arcs from its branch disks to one outer disk gives finitely many ordinary sheet polygons; lift and reglue those polygons and the power-coordinate filling sectors. Finite subdivisions give actual smooth face data for $C$. Thus [F1]–[F2] apply to this lift, without a general surface classification or an assumed incidence normalization theorem. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Put $N=\sum_i\deg f_i$, summed over all components of $C$. Choose a point $p\in\Sigma$ which is a regular value of all nonconstant $f_i$, misses every constant component value, and misses the finite support of $u'(B)$. Such a point exists by [F5]: the finite critical-value union is null and the additional excluded set is finite. In local tuple coordinates the canonical point-divisor equation is the product of its root coordinates minus $p$. Consequently the actual line identity $q^*\mathcal O(E_p)=\bigotimes_i p_i^*\mathcal O(p)$ holds: the local product definers have exactly the product transition units, as in [F1]'s divisor construction. Pulling back gives degree $N$, since each root's transverse inverse points have their signed local degrees. On the other hand $qF=u'\pi$, and pulling back a line on an oriented degree-$d$ branched covering multiplies its degree by $d$. This follows by choosing a graph and faces whose small degree-counting disks miss the finitely many branch values: each has $d$ orientation-positive lifts and all internal edges cancel. Hence $N=d\,n_p(u')=d\,n_z(u)$ by [F3] and step1.1. None of these signed degree equalities requires the sphere or root maps to be holomorphic. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The ordered lift has a vanishing trace in every circle direction of [F2]. For any continuous $a:\Sigma\to S^1$, define $A:X\to S^1$ by $A(z_1+\cdots+z_m)=\prod_i a(z_i)$. This is continuous because the product on $\Sigma^m$ is symmetric and $q$ is the quotient map. The composite $A u':S^2\to S^1$ has a continuous real logarithm. An explicit choice-free argument is to lift it on each of two slightly enlarged closed hemispherical disks: a continuous circle map on a disk has a logarithm by subdividing each radial segment into one common number of short-argument increments, supplied by uniform continuity. Sum those increments from the disk center; the formula is continuous and exponentiates to the map. On the connected annular overlap the two logarithms differ by a constant integer multiple of $2\pi$, so adjust one to glue them. Therefore $A u'\pi=\prod_i af_i$ also has a logarithm. Its circle class is zero, while the class of a product is the sum of its factors' classes: the path-increment cocycles of [F2] differ only by the coboundary of the principal argument corrections. For $\alpha_{ir}=f_i^*a_r$ and $\beta_{ir}=f_i^*b_r$ this proves $\sum_i\alpha_{ir}=\sum_i\beta_{ir}=0$ in degree-one cohomology of $C$, for each $r$. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Apply [F2] to every pair $f_i,f_j$. The sum of the first two terms of its diagonal formula is $(m-1)N$. For its cup correction, graded commutativity makes $\alpha_{ir}\smile\beta_{jr}-\beta_{ir}\smile\alpha_{jr}=\alpha_{ir}\smile\beta_{jr}+\alpha_{jr}\smile\beta_{ir}$. Summing over $i<j$ therefore gives $$(\sum_i\alpha_{ir})\smile(\sum_i\beta_{ir})-\sum_i\alpha_{ir}\smile\beta_{ir}=-\sum_i f_i^*(a_r\smile b_r).$$ Its evaluation is $-N$ by the positive polygon cup pairing and degree naturality in [F2]. Summing over $r$ gives correction $-hN$, so $$\sum_{i<j}\deg((f_i,f_j)^*\mathcal O(\Delta))=(m-1+h)N.$$ Also [F2] gives $\sum_i\deg(f_i^*T\Sigma)=(2-2h)N$. Now [F1] yields $$d\,\deg(u'^*\det(TX))=(2-2h)N+(m-1+h)N=(m+1-h)N.$$ Use step3.1 and divide by the positive integer $d$. Homotopy invariance from step1.1 gives the asserted identity for the original arbitrary sphere $u$, including negative degree and constant maps. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 A homotopy of complex structures identifies their determinant lines over the parameterized sphere. In detail, embed the finite family of bundle charts complex linearly in one finite trivial bundle by a finite partition, and use the continuous image projections. Uniform continuity allows finitely many parameter subdivisions on which consecutive projections differ by less than $1/2$; their restrictions are fiber isomorphisms. Their finite product identifies the two end complex bundles and hence the determinant degrees. This proves the asserted homotopic-complex-structure invariance, in particular for an explicitly supplied nearly-symmetric-to-standard homotopy; no assertion that arbitrary almost complex structures lie in that homotopy class is being made. [F1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally compare the actual relative determinant windings in [F4]. Flatten a small interior disk of the Whitney model to its attaching value, and flatten a small disk about the sphere attaching point, by relative coordinate homotopies; include the specified attaching path by a thin cylinder with a trivial bundle frame. Frame the determinant line on the flattened Whitney core, and on the sphere minus its attaching disk. On their common attaching circle the two sphere frames have transition loop of winding $c=\deg(u^*\det(TX))$, with the positive sphere orientation. Interior connected sum removes the attaching disks and glues their oppositely oriented circles. Extending the Whitney core frame over the inserted punctured sphere transfers precisely that transition of winding $c$ to the determinant trivialization along the physical boundary. This sign can be checked directly by face winding: all inner boundary contributions cancel except the positive sphere-face winding, so the relative determinant degree increases by $c$. The boundary Maslov phase in [F4] is the SQUARE of the complex determinant phase and hence increases by $2c$. The fixed end caps and physical boundary real planes have not changed. The actual Fredholm index formula of [F4] consequently gives $\mu(\phi\#u)-\mu(\phi)=2c$. Applying step4.1 and $m+1-h=k$ proves the claimed multipointed shift. The argument supplies the spherical correction only; it asserts neither the full nonspherical domain formula nor a boundary-disk count or normalized absolute grading. [F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
