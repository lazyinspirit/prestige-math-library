## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Take an actual knot grid of size $n\ge2$, its flat complex torus and the constant path $J_t=\operatorname{Sym}^n(j_{\mathrm{flat}})$. Use its actual row and column product tori, $w=O,z=X$, and their flat product generator charts. Every empty-state oriented rectangle $R:x\to y$, with arbitrary $O$ and $X$ markings in its interior, has exactly ONE orbit of actual finite-energy holomorphic strips of domain $R$ under longitudinal translation. That strip has actual fixed-end index one and its actual linearization is onto. Every actual index-one strip is one of these rectangle strips. Thus all actual index-one orbits, across all endpoints and classes, are finite and regular for this constant path.

Consequently the full filtered polynomial disk-count matrix for this chosen analytic datum is EXACTLY the full filtered grid differential, on the identical state basis over $\mathbb F_2[U_1,\ldots,U_n]$, with each rectangle weighted by $\prod U_i^{O_i(R)}$ and filtration drop $n_X(R)$. It squares to zero by the finite rectangle-composition proof. Taking its zero-drop part gives the associated-graded minus matrix; then setting all variables to zero gives the tilde matrix. The identity preserves the relative Maslov and Alexander differences when assigned by the grid grading formulas.

This is a chosen-data analytic count and chain identification. It does not assert independence from auxiliary data, normalized absolute analytic gradings, a singly pointed reduction or its stable factors, move invariance, or identification with an independently constructed invariant. Equal-domain Whitney homotopy classes are not presumed equal: uniqueness is for actual strip orbits and therefore also accounts for every class represented by such a strip.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual flat grid, its constant integrable symmetric-product path, and the specified rectangle or actual finite-energy strip with generator ends.

[F1] Every realized flat-grid class has actual index $n_x(D)+n_y(D)$; a nonnegative index-one domain is exactly an empty-state rectangle. Its full relative grading identities include all positive $X$ degrees. ([[lem-realized-flat-grid-index-one-positive-domains-are-empty-rectangles]], [[thm-realized-grid-whitney-classes-have-the-full-relative-grading-formula]])

[F2] An ordinary positive flat rectangle has an explicit proper holomorphic degree-two map to the strip, with its opposite corner pairs at the two ends, one simple branch and nonzero exponential end-coordinate derivatives. Its bounded imaginary part, with alternating side values zero and one, is unique. ([[lem-a-flat-rectangle-has-an-explicit-alternating-side-strip-cover]])

[F3] Actual integrable point-divisor intersections are nonnegative, equal to domain degrees, and additive under ordered lifts. The local lift proof uses finite root monodromy and bounded holomorphic removal to fill its branch points, including repeated roots. ([[lem-pointed-heegaard-integrable-divisor-positivity]], [[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]], [[lem-holomorphic-symmetric-product-curves-admit-finite-branched-ordered-lifts]])

[F4] The actual geometric strip operator is Fredholm; its index agrees in all integer fixed-end Hilbert realizations. Exact transverse end charts give smooth exponential asymptotics. The constant transverse real/imaginary-line operator has zero kernel. ([[lem-geometric-strip-linearization-frame-and-fredholm-index]], [[lem-strip-fredholm-index-agrees-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]], [[lem-product-integrable-transverse-strip-end-exponential-asymptotics]], [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]])

[F5] The compact-domain energy bound applies to the protected standard structure; its ordered-cover proof bounds actual energy by the fixed domain area. The plane harmonic maximum principle and the logarithmic-corner barrier argument apply without full Choice. ([[lem-nearly-symmetric-heegaard-strip-domain-energy-bound]], [[thm-maximum-and-minimum-principles-for-plane-harmonic-functions]])

[F6] The finite grid algorithm counts the stated empty rectangles and its three matrices square to zero by the proved equal-weight rectangle pairings. ([[alg-grid-knot-floer-complex]], [[lem-grid-rectangle-differential-squares-to-zero]])

## Proof

1.1 First record the actual incidence construction needed for integrable strips, rather than import a clean-cover hypothesis. The two compact product tori avoid the diagonal, and the generator ends do too. Thus an actual strip with uniform generator ends meets the diagonal only in a compact interior part. Holomorphic local discriminants make this intersection discrete unless the whole map lies in the diagonal; the latter is excluded by its boundary. It is therefore finite. Off those points order its distinct roots locally. Their continuations give the finite degree-$n$ root-label cover of the punctured base, with monodromy permutations of the $n$ labels. At each omitted point separate its permutation into cycles of lengths $r$. In a cycle use the positive source coordinate $b-b_0=\zeta^r$; each root is now a single bounded holomorphic function of $\zeta$, and extends across zero. Bounded holomorphic removal follows directly from its Cauchy coefficients: every negative Laurent coefficient has absolute value at most $M\rho^{|k|}$ and vanishes as $\rho\to0$. This is the local construction in [F3], applied here to the actual strip. Near a physical boundary the roots are distinct and lie on their labelled straight row or column, so they extend by ordinary real-line Schwarz reflection; reflection follows by conjugating the upper and lower holomorphic pieces, whose matching real traces cancel the interface term, and the Cauchy integral formula. At the two ends, each distinct root has the exact transverse Fourier expansion in [F4]; in the quadrant coordinate $q=e^{\pi(s+it)/2}$, or $q=e^{-\pi(s+it)/2}$ at the other end, it is a convergent series of odd nonnegative powers with its generator constant. Hence it extends holomorphically at that corner. We obtain an actual compact bordered incidence surface with corners, a positive degree-$n$ branched base projection and a holomorphic root map to the torus. It has $n$ distinct root corners at each end, and only finite interior branching. No surface representativity assertion for arbitrary nonholomorphic classes was used. [F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let an actual strip have domain the empty-state rectangle $R$, lifted by its proper row and column intervals to a Euclidean rectangle. For a generic point outside its closure the total root-map degree is zero; for a generic point inside it the degree is one, by [F3]. Every nonconstant holomorphic root component has positive local degree and open image. Consequently each such component has image in $\overline R$: hitting its open complement would give positive degree at nearby generic points there. Every inactive generator coordinate lies outside $\overline R$, since emptiness excludes its interior and one point per row and column excludes its four edges. Its local root near either end is therefore constant: otherwise its open holomorphic image near that outside coordinate would contradict the degree-zero assertion. The corresponding point-divisor factor holds on that open end and hence everywhere by holomorphic identity, so it factors out as a constant root of the whole strip. Doing this for the $n-2$ inactive coordinates leaves two active roots and no other constant component, since none of their source corner coordinates equals a target corner coordinate. Every active interior value lies in $\operatorname{Int}R$: a value on its boundary would have an open neighborhood crossing to the forbidden complement. Its boundary values lie on the four bounding curves, because all other coordinate labels have already been factored out. Hence the active root map to $\operatorname{Int}R$ is proper and has total degree one. It is one-to-one and unramified: two preimages or a ramified preimage would cover nearby regular points at least twice. Degree one also gives onto-ness; a missing interior point is excluded by properness and openness. This inverse also respects the four actual boundary sides and corners without a boundary-uniformization premise. In the reflected straight-side chart a boundary critical point of order at least two would map an inward half-disk across the forbidden side of $R$. At a corner, an end power of order greater than one would likewise cover points outside its one permitted quadrant. Both are impossible. Two distinct boundary preimages would cover nearby interior values twice. Hence the boundary root map is locally unramified and one-to-one, with exactly the four indicated corner values. Thus its active incidence surface is biholomorphic to $R$ with those actual boundary limits, and its base projection is an actual holomorphic map $p':R\to\{0<t<1\}$ with alternating boundary imaginary parts zero and one. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F2] to this lifted rectangle. Its explicit cover $p$ has exactly two active inverse-fiber values at every strip point, counted with multiplicity, and they form a holomorphic map into $\operatorname{Sym}^2(R)$. Away from its branch this is immediate from the two inverse sheets; at the one simple branch use a local target surface coordinate $z$ and base coordinate $b$ with $p=z^2=b$, so the unordered roots have elementary symmetric coordinates $(0,-b)$, holomorphic at the branch. Adjoin the fixed inactive generator roots. This gives an actual holomorphic strip in the original symmetric product, with the required torus boundaries, source at southwest/northeast and target at northwest/southeast, and domain exactly $R$. Its end-coordinate expansions in [F2] give uniform generator ends and exponential derivative decay. It has finite energy: on the degree-one active root surface the product area is exactly the area of $R$; the quotient derivative is bounded near its compact image, including the branch. Pulling back to the finite ordered cover and dividing by its degree bounds its energy by a finite constant times that area, exactly as in [F5]'s proof, without assuming finite energy to obtain that bound for this explicit map. Conversely the map $p'$ in step 2.1 has bounded imaginary part with precisely those side values. The uniqueness in [F2] gives $p'=p+c$ with $c\in\mathbb R$. Its inverse fibers therefore give just a longitudinal translate of this constructed strip. This proves actual orbit existence and uniqueness for domain $R$, across all represented classes. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Its actual index is one by [F1]. We compute its kernel before concluding onto-ness. The strip image has a product neighborhood separating its active two roots from the inactive constant roots, which lie a positive distance outside $\overline R$. Inactive components of a kernel section are holomorphic scalar functions on the strip, with real lower and imaginary upper boundary traces and zero ends; their constant transverse operator has zero kernel by [F4]. For the active section, vary the local monic root equation to first order. Away from the branch, its equivalent infinitesimal base variation on the incidence rectangle is $$\dot p(z)=-p'(z)\xi(z),$$ where $\xi$ is the root variation evaluated over $p(z)$. It is a single holomorphic function on $R$. At the branch choose the coordinates $z^2=b$ from step 3.1. A holomorphic symmetric-product tangent section varies that equation by $\dot F(z,b)=A(b)+B(b)z$; implicit variation of $z^2-b=0$ gives $\dot p(z)=A(z^2)+B(z^2)z$. This extends holomorphically across the branch and agrees with the regular-sheet formula. Thus no root-coordinate pole has been introduced at the repeated tuple. [F1, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Along an open rectangle side, the derivative of $p$ sends its tangent line to the real line, and the actual root variation $\xi$ is tangent to that same side. Therefore $\dot p$ has real boundary values. It is bounded near each corner. Indeed in its exact end chart every kernel root component solves the constant transverse equation and is in $H^1$; the Fourier coefficient ODEs in [F4] make it $O(e^{-\pi|s|/2})$ with all derivatives. The corresponding rectangle distance to the corner is comparable to $e^{-\pi|s|/2}$ by [F2], while $p'(z)=O(1/|z-c|)$. Their product is bounded. The imaginary part of $\dot p$ is consequently bounded harmonic on $R$, zero on its open sides. Apply [F5]'s maximum principle with $\epsilon\sum_c\log(C/|z-c|)$, then let $\epsilon\downarrow0$, as in [F2]'s uniqueness proof. It vanishes identically. Cauchy--Riemann therefore makes $\dot p$ a real constant. The map from an active kernel section to $\dot p$ is injective, since the regular inverse sheets determine the section away from the branch and continuity determines it there. Hence the full real kernel has dimension at most one. The nonzero longitudinal derivative of the constructed strip is in that kernel and its end decay puts it in the actual domain. Its dimension is exactly one; actual Fredholm index one then gives zero cokernel. The higher Sobolev conclusions agree by [F4], so regularity is the actual fixed-end one. [F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 For any actual index-one strip in this integrable datum, [F3] gives nonnegative degree at every chosen region divisor, since its boundary misses each and the map cannot be contained in it. Thus its full domain is nonnegative. Apply [F1] to get an empty-state rectangle, and steps 3.1--5.1 identify its unique regular orbit. There are finitely many states and finitely many rectangles for each pair, so this also proves finiteness across every actual represented class. Each actual orbit contributes one modulo two. Its point-divisor weight is exactly $\prod U_i^{O_i(R)}$ by its domain. Therefore the analytic matrix equals the full filtered matrix in [F6]. That finite matrix squares to zero by [F6], independently of any unproved generic index-two compactification for this integrable path. Its zero-drop part and variable quotient are exactly the other two algorithmic matrices. Finally the actual relative grading identities in [F1] give the identity's Maslov and Alexander differences and the precise drop $n_X(R)$. Absolute normalization, choice independence, move invariance and singly pointed stable reduction were not used or inferred. [F1, F3, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
