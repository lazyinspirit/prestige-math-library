## Statement

Let $S^1 = \{ z \in \mathbb{C} : |z| = 1 \}$ with basepoint $1$, and for
$n \in \mathbb{Z}$ let $\omega_n$ be the loop
$\omega_n(t) = e^{2\pi i n t}$, $t \in [0,1]$. Then

$$\Phi \colon \mathbb{Z} \to \pi_1(S^1, 1), \qquad \Phi(n) = [\omega_n]$$

is an isomorphism of groups. In particular $\pi_1(S^1, 1)$ is infinite cyclic,
generated by the class of $\omega_1$, and the circle is not simply connected.

Equivalently: the **winding number** (degree) of a loop in $S^1$ is a complete
invariant of its path-homotopy class, and every integer is realised.

## Remarks

**Not proved in this library.** The group $\pi_1(S^1,1)$ is defined by
[[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]], and its group structure is proved in
[[thm-fundamental-group-laws]]. What is not established is the winding-number
classification of its loop classes, the isomorphism with $\mathbb Z$, and hence
the conclusion that the circle is not simply connected in the sense of
[[def-simply-connected]].

**What would prove it.** The lifting theorems for the covering
$p \colon \mathbb{R} \to S^1$, $p(t) = e^{2\pi i t}$, recorded in
[[rem-covering-spaces-and-universal-cover]]. Surjectivity of $\Phi$ comes from
lifting a loop to a path in $\mathbb{R}$ from $0$ to some integer $n$;
injectivity comes from lifting a path homotopy, whose lifted endpoints are
constant in the homotopy parameter and integer valued. Both lifts are produced by
a Lebesgue number argument on $[0,1]$ and on $[0,1]^2$.

**Why it matters here.** This is the first nontrivial computation of a
fundamental group, and it is the engine behind almost every classical
application: Brouwer's fixed point theorem in the plane, the fundamental theorem
of algebra by a winding-number argument, the non-existence of a continuous
logarithm on $\mathbb{C} \setminus \{0\}$, and the two-dimensional case of the
Jordan curve theorem.

The library has the group but not this computation. A continuous argument along
a loop, and hence the winding number of a loop about a point, can be constructed
by hand: subdivide the parameter interval finely enough, using uniform
continuity, that each piece stays in a half plane where a branch of the argument
is available, and add up the increments. That construction is elementary and in
scope, and it is what the analysis pages use. What those pages must not say
without the covering-space lifting argument is that the resulting integer gives
a complete invariant of loop classes or proves $\pi_1(S^1,1)\cong\mathbb Z$.
