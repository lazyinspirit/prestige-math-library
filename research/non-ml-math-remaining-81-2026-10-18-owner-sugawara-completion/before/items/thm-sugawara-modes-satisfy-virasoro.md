## Statement

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex simple Lie algebra with invariant form $B$ normalized so long roots have square $2$. Let $H$ be an irreducible positive-energy unitary affine-current module of level $k\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ as in [[def-positive-energy-unitary-affine-module]], and let $L_m$ be the modes of [[def-sugawara-modes]]. Since $h^\vee>0$, this is noncritical. Then
$$[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+\frac{c}{12}(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}\operatorname{id}_H,\qquad c=\frac{k\dim\mathfrak g}{k+h^\vee}.$$
Thus $C$ in [[def-virasoro-algebra]] acts as the scalar $c$. The theorem does not assert this proof for arbitrary nonunitary modules at arbitrary complex level.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The compact mode relations, the nonnegative integral level $k$, and the restricted Sugawara operators on $H$.

[F1] [[def-positive-energy-unitary-affine-module]]: $H$ is irreducible, $H=\bigoplus_{r\ge0}H_r$ with finite-dimensional ground space $H_0\ne0$, positive modes lower degree and vanish below degree zero, and $X(n)^*=-X(-n)$ for compact $X$.

[F2] [[def-sugawara-modes]]: in a compact orthonormal basis with $B(X_i,X_j)=-\delta_{ij}$, $L_m=-\frac{1}{2(k+h^\vee)}\sum_{i,r}:X_i(r)X_i(m-r):$, with modes of nonnegative index ordered to the right.

[F3] [[lem-sugawara-modes-act-on-currents]]: $[L_m,X(n)]=-nX(m+n)$ for every compact current mode.

[F4] [[lem-irreducible-affine-module-has-irreducible-ground-space]]: $H_0$ is an irreducible finite-dimensional module for the zero-mode algebra.

[F5] [[def-untwisted-affine-central-extension]]: $[X(a),Y(b)]=[X,Y](a+b)+aB(X,Y)K$ when $a+b=0$, and the central term is zero otherwise.

[F6] [[def-dual-coxeter-number-for-normalized-form]]: $-\sum_i\operatorname{ad}(X_i)^2=2h^\vee\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak g}$ for a compact orthonormal basis.

[F7] [[lem-witt-central-cocycles-are-cubic]]: a scalar Witt central cocycle supported on $m+n=0$ and with value zero at $(1,-1)$ is a multiple of $(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}$.

## Proof

1.1 Since $k\ge0$ and [F6] gives $h^\vee>0$, $k+h^\vee>0$, so the modes in [F2] are defined. At $k=-h^\vee$, the denominator in [F2] is zero, so its normalized formula is undefined there. Define $D_{m,n}=[L_m,L_n]-(m-n)L_{m+n}$. By Jacobi and [F3], $\left[\, [L_m,L_n],X(p)\right]=[L_m,-pX(n+p)]-[L_n,-pX(m+p)]=-p(m-n)X(m+n+p)=[(m-n)L_{m+n},X(p)].$ Thus $D_{m,n}$ commutes with every current mode. Each term of $L_m$ shifts energy by $-m$ because its two current indices sum to $m$, so $D_{m,n}$ shifts energy by $-(m+n)$. If $m+n>0$, it kills $H_0$ because its image would have negative energy. If $m+n<0$ and $D_{m,n}\xi\ne0$ for some $\xi\in H_0$, then this positive-energy vector is killed by every positive current mode, since $D_{m,n}$ commutes with them and they kill $\xi$. Reordering finite current words into negative, zero, and positive modes using [F5] shows its generated submodule has no degree-zero part: positive modes annihilate it, zero modes preserve its positive degree, and negative modes raise degree. This contradicts irreducibility of $H$, so $D_{m,n}$ kills $H_0$ in this case. If $m+n=0$, $D_{m,n}$ preserves $H_0$ and commutes with zero modes. It has an eigenvalue $\lambda$ on the nonzero finite-dimensional complex space $H_0$; the eigenspace is zero-mode invariant, so [F4] implies it is all of $H_0$, and $D_{m,n}$ is scalar on $H_0$. Finally, the submodule generated by nonzero $H_0$ is all of $H$ by irreducibility; because $D_{m,n}$ commutes with every current, its action on that generated module is the same scalar (zero when $m+n\ne0$). [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 Write $D_{m,n}=\lambda(m,n)\operatorname{id}_H$. Jacobi for the $L_m$ gives $(m-n)\lambda(m+n,p)+(n-p)\lambda(n+p,m)+(p-m)\lambda(p+m,n)=0;$ the bracket antisymmetry makes $\lambda$ alternating, and step 1.1 shows it is supported on $m+n=0$. It remains to normalize $\lambda(1,-1)$. Let $\xi\in H_0$. Normal ordering gives $L_{-1}\xi=-\frac1{k+h^\vee}\sum_i X_i(-1)X_i(0)\xi$ and $L_0\xi=-\frac1{2(k+h^\vee)}\sum_iX_i(0)^2\xi$. By [F3], $[L_1,X_i(-1)]=X_i(0)$ and $[L_1,X_i(0)]=0$, while $L_1\xi=0$ by degree. Hence $L_1L_{-1}\xi=-\frac1{k+h^\vee}\sum_iX_i(0)^2\xi=2L_0\xi$. Since $H_0\ne0$, this proves $\lambda(1,-1)=0$. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F7] using the scalar cocycle established in steps 1.1–2.1. There is a scalar $a$ such that $[L_m,L_n]=(m-n)L_{m+n}+a(m^3-m)\delta_{m+n,0}\operatorname{id}_H$. To determine $a$, take $\xi\in H_0$. The normal-ordered index pairs with sum $-2$ that can act nontrivially on $\xi$ give $L_{-2}\xi=-\frac1{k+h^\vee}\sum_i\left(X_i(-2)X_i(0)+\frac12X_i(-1)^2\right)\xi.$ Using [F3], $[L_2,X_i(-2)]=2X_i(0)$ and $[L_2,X_i(-1)]=X_i(1)$. The positive mode $X_i(1)$ kills $\xi$, and [F5] gives $X_i(1)X_i(-1)\xi=-k\xi$. Therefore $L_2L_{-2}\xi=\left(\frac{2\Delta}{k+h^\vee}+\frac{k\dim\mathfrak g}{2(k+h^\vee)}\right)\xi,\qquad \Delta=-\sum_iX_i(0)^2,$ where the first term means the operator $2\Delta/(k+h^\vee)$ on $H_0$. Also $4L_0\xi=2\Delta\xi/(k+h^\vee)$. Comparing $[L_2,L_{-2}]\xi=4L_0\xi+6a\xi$ yields $6a=k\dim\mathfrak g/(2(k+h^\vee))$, so $a=k\dim\mathfrak g/(12(k+h^\vee))$ and $c=12a=k\dim\mathfrak g/(k+h^\vee)$. This is the asserted central charge and Virasoro relation. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
