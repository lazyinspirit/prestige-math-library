# Independent evidence audit: CSP coding and no-small-cuts induction

This is a read-only mathematical audit of Malliaris–Shelah, [*Cofinality Spectrum Theorems*](https://math.uchicago.edu/~mem/Malliaris-Shelah-CST-new.pdf), printed §§2, 5 and 8 (especially pp. 20–24 and 30–35). It supplements `frontier-35-pt-csp-source-audit-draft.md`; it is **not** a selected proof supplier or a readiness verdict. I checked the printed formulas and the logical dependencies, and distinguish repairs that can be written locally from interfaces that still require a proof. No current-run manifest, plan, receipt, scope, or engine state was changed.

## §5: the coding bridge is a theorem chain, not a convention

1. **What must be coded.** Convention 5.1 defines `|A| ≤_s |B|` by an *internally represented* partial injection in a definable subtree of `T_{a×b}`. It does not mean an external injection. The later strict comparison needs, at minimum, monotonicity for initial intervals, preservation under restrictions, and the finite pigeonhole fact that sufficiently larger internal intervals cannot inject into smaller ones. Lemma 5.3 obtains a nonstandard initial segment `X_a` whose `T_a`-objects have Gödel codes in a larger `X_b`; Claim 5.5 iterates this; Definition 5.6 and Corollary 5.7 use the well-behaved product-order axiom 2.3(6) to code pair-valued maps; Conclusion 5.8 then identifies trees of tuples and function-code coordinates with definable subtrees of an allowed `T_b`. These are the exact interfaces used to define `T_6` and to realize its six-coordinate nodes. One must also say explicitly that the chosen `X_a` is restricted to the bounded coding radius supplied by Corollary 5.7, so every marker subsequently selected in `X_a` is within the claimed coding domain. Corollary 5.7's one-sentence proof does not spell out that restriction or the pair-map encoding.

2. **Literal arithmetic formulas need normalization.** Printed Lemma 5.3 defines `φ_+(x,y,z)` by a sequence of length `y`, first entry `x`, last entry at `y−1` equal to `z`, with successor increments. With the paper's usual exclusive-length reading, that says `z=x+y−1` for positive `y` and gives no zero case. A corrected local formula can use length `S(y)`, first entry `x`, and final entry at `y` equal to `z`; multiplication/exponentiation need independently checked base cases. The printed multiplication display in Lemma 5.11 does not contain its claimed output `z` on the defining right-hand side, so it does not literally define a multiplication graph. These appear to be presentational defects rather than counterexamples to the intended theorem, but quoting the displayed formulas verbatim would not establish the arithmetic used in Claim 8.2 or Theorem 8.5. The local proof needs corrected partial-operation graphs, closure on the chosen initial segment for each finite calculation, and the internal interval cardinality laws. The paper's Definition 2.3(7)(b)(ii) also prints `lg(b)=max(dom(b))`, while its evaluation and concatenation clauses use `lg` as an exclusive endpoint; a local formalization must fix one convention throughout.

3. **The crucial finite-function code is conditional.** In Theorem 8.5, a finite symmetric function on `x_3×x_3` and an injection `x_3→X_a` must each be *elements of `X_{a'}`*, not merely externally definable finite graphs. Corollary 5.7 and Conclusion 5.8 are meant to provide those code objects after shrinking to the bounded, coverable pair order. A local proof should construct the tuple/pair encoding, show the code exists below the cover's tree ceiling, and prove that the decoded graph and domain are uniformly definable in `M_1^+`. The bare ESTT Cartesian-product axiom supplies a product order; by itself it does not establish that arbitrary finite graphs are represented in the particular `X_{a'}` used by `T_6`.

Claim 8.2 is a separate bridge into §8.5. Its successor instruction, “by inductive hypothesis (d), we may choose” a point above `2d_β` with strictly larger internal size **and** arbitrarily long future growth, does not follow by choosing just one witness to each finite growth demand. In the *application* `κ<λ=p_s<t_s`, so `p_s>ℵ_0` and Theorem 4.2 can supply the missing countable compactness step: let `P_n(x)` say an `n`-step spaced, strictly size-growing chain starts at `x`; arbitrarily long chains above `d_β` make every finite subset of `{x>2d_β, |2d_β|<_s|x|}∪{P_n(x):n<ω}` satisfiable by taking the second point of a sufficiently long chain. `Or`-saturation then gives one `x` satisfying every `P_n`. At a limit, the printed `m_k` argument must be expanded: each earlier `n_β` lies below each `m_k` because arbitrarily many later tree coordinates separate it from the final value, and an index between all `n_β` and all `m_k` has room for all finite iterates of doubling. To infer genuine strict *internal cardinal* growth from that room requires the §5 finite-pigeonhole and arithmetic laws. This is a possible scoped repair for the theorem's uncountable-`p_s` use, not a verification of Claim 8.2 in its full printed generality.

## §8.5: exact successor-stage repair

At successor `α=β+1`, the type `(p.1)`–`(p.9)` has fewer than `p_s` parameters because `|α|<λ=p_s`; Theorem 4.2 can realize it **only after** finite satisfiability in the allowed product order is proved. The printed witness is wrong for a permissible singleton finite set `Γ`: it sets `b_3={y_{γ+1}:γ∈Γ}` and `(p.6)` requires `b_1=min(range b_5)`, `b_2=max(range b_5)`, whereas `(p.2)` requires `b_1<b_2`. For `|Γ|=1`, these are incompatible; the issue already occurs when `α=1`. An arbitrary finite subtype can also mention no marker at all.

A concrete local repair for any finite subtype is as follows. Let `Γ` include every marker index mentioned by any `(p.8)` or `(p.9)` clause. Choose one fresh `z` outside the previous domain `c_β(n_β,3)` and outside the selected markers when `Γ` is nonempty; choose two fresh points when `Γ` is empty. The old domain is internally at most half of nonstandard `X_a`, so its complement still contains these finitely many fresh points. Set `b_3` to the selected markers plus the padding. On distinct selected-marker pairs put the prescribed `d_{g(ζ+1,γ+1)}` in symmetric `b_4`; on padding pairs and the diagonal put `0`. Let `D=max({1}∪{d_{g(ζ+1,γ+1)}:ζ<γ∈Γ})`, enumerate `b_3` as `u_0,…,u_k`, and map `u_i` to `e_{α+1}+1+iD`. The double-spacing of the right cut, together with `D` on the left of the cut, gives `e_{α+1}+1+kD<e_α` for each *standard finite* `k`: choose a sufficiently deeper `e_{α+r}` so that `2^r` dominates `k`, and use `2e_{j+1}<e_j`. The corrected §5 partial arithmetic must justify those finite operations and their order laws.

Now `(p.1)` follows **provided the §5 coding interface above is proved**; `(p.2)` and `(p.6)` hold because the padded domain has at least two points and its images are strictly inside `(e_{α+1},e_α)`; `(p.3)` holds because the domain is externally finite in a nonstandard pseudofinite order; `(p.4)` holds by symmetric definition; `(p.5)` holds because each prescribed distance is at most `D`, while distinct images differ by at least `D`, and padding distances are zero. For `(p.7)`, padding lies outside the previous domain, and the old selected-marker pairs have their prescribed value by the induction invariant; the newly introduced `y_{β+1}` also lies outside the old domain. This checks all seven base clauses and the finitely selected `(p.8)`–`(p.9)` clauses, subject to the coding and arithmetic proofs just named.

There is a second successor-stage gap: printed `(p.2)` permits `x_2=e_α`, while tree clause `T_6(a)` requires the new `x_2<c_β(n_β,0)`. The induction only gives `c_β(n_β,0)≥e_{β+1}=e_α`, and equality can occur at a prior successor stage. Thus an arbitrary realization of the printed type need not be appendable to `c_β`. Strengthen `(p.2)` to `x_2<e_α` (or add `x_2<c_β(n_β,0)`). The padded finite witness above already has the strict inequality, so the stronger type remains finitely satisfiable and yields the required immediate-predecessor clause; all earlier-node clauses then follow from the tree property of `c_β`.

At a limit `α<λ`, Lemma 2.15 gives a below-ceiling upper bound in the definable tree. For each *already selected* marker `β<min(α,κ^+)`, its last persistence index `n(β)` exists by pseudofiniteness. The left indices `n_γ` are below every `n(β)` because each older marker persists at all later stages. Were the resulting pre-cut unfilled, its regular cofinalities would each be at most `|α|<p_s`; hence a suitable `n^{**}` exists and the restriction through this index retains all markers and the limit-stage interval inequalities. The printed `β<max{α,κ^+}` in the definition of `n(β)` must read `β<min{α,κ^+}`: markers with indices beyond the stage have not been chosen. The source also alternates `n(β)` and `n_β` in the last inequality, and writes a restriction `c↾n_α` while calling `n_α` its last domain index; the local proof must use its declared inclusive/exclusive convention consistently. These are index/notation repairs, but the regularization of the pre-cut and persistence argument must be written, not elided.

## Final internal-size contradiction: bound the image interval

The final treetop `c_λ` and lower-cofinality uniqueness give a `(λ,κ)` cut in its index order, with right-side indices `m_ε` (`ε<κ`). For each `β<κ^+`, the definable set of indices through which `y_{β+1}` persists is an initial interval containing all sufficiently late `n_α`. If it contained no `m_ε`, its last point would fill the index cut. Thus it contains some `m_{ε(β)}`; pigeonholing `κ^+` markers into `κ` choices yields a fixed `m_{ε*}` and a set `W` of size `κ^+` whose markers all persist there. Tree clause (e) then fixes `F(y_{γ+1},y_{β+1})=d_{g(γ+1,β+1)}` for distinct `β,γ∈W`.

The printed last paragraph invokes internal-size growth beyond `|d_{ζ(ε*)+1}|` after locating only `c_λ(m_{ε*},0)` between two `d`'s. This does **not** yet bound distances between images of the injection: those images lie below `c_λ(m_{ε*},2)`, which is larger than coordinate 0. A sound replacement uses the original cut. Write `t=c_λ(m_{ε*},2)`. Since `m_{ε*}` is to the right of every `n_α`, tree clause (a) and successor-stage markers give `t<e_α` for every `α<λ`. As `(d,e)` is a cut, `t` lies on its left side, so `t≤d_η` for some `η<κ`. Claim 8.2(2) gives `δ>η` with `|d_η|<_s|d_δ|`. Fact 8.4 gives a pair in `W` with `g(γ+1,β+1)≥δ`. Its prescribed `d_g` cannot inject into `[0,d_η]`: otherwise restriction to `[0,d_δ]` would contradict `|d_η|<_s|d_δ|`. But tree clause (d) supplies `|d_g|≤_s|x_5(y_{γ+1})−x_5(y_{β+1})|`, and the difference is at most `t≤d_η`; this is the required contradiction. This argument uses monotonicity/restriction of the internal-cardinality codes and corrected partial arithmetic; it avoids relying on coordinate 0 as an unproved interval bound.

## Readiness consequence

The published proof has a repairable singleton finite-satisfiability error, a successor-type bound too weak for tree concatenation, and a missing bound in its final displayed cardinality argument, plus several arithmetic/indexing displays that cannot be copied literally. The replacement arguments above close those **conditional** steps, provided a local §5 coding-and-arithmetic supplier and the earlier CSP interfaces (treetops, uniqueness, local `Or`-saturation, Claim 8.2) are proved. I have not independently proved the full coding-radius/pair-function lemma from ESTT or the internal finite-pigeonhole and growth assertions required in Claim 8.2; those remain substantive prerequisites. Therefore this audit does **not** establish that a small finite Step-1 scaffold is ready. A rigorous scaffold would need explicit statements and proofs for each of these interfaces, including the corrected successor/limit/final arguments, rather than a citation to §§5 and 8.5.
