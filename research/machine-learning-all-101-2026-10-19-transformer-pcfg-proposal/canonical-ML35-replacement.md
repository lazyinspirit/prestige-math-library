### ML-35 — Attention, transformer universality, and sequence limits

**Pages:** attention-and-transformer-expressivity / attention-and-transformer-expressivity-examples. **Scope:** Define scaled dot-product attention softmax(QKᵀ/√dₖ)V, multi-head attention, transformer encoder/decoder layers, residual/feed-forward blocks, positional encoding, causal masks, and forward-pass algorithm. Dense full attention costs O(n²d) per layer and has constant sequential depth/path length in the source’s comparison with recurrent processing; do not extend this to sparse/linear attention or hardware claims. Without positions, unmasked self-attention and tokenwise feed-forward maps are permutation equivariant. State Yun et al.’s fixed-length theorem: for fixed sequence length n and embedding dimension d, continuous compactly supported permutation-equivariant maps ℝᵈˣⁿ→ℝᵈˣⁿ are approximable in integrated Lebesgue d_p error, any 1≤p<∞, by compositions with two heads, head size one, and tokenwise ReLU FFN width four; trainable positional embeddings extend the theorem to continuous maps on compact domains without permutation restriction. It is an existence result with potentially exponential construction size and no layer normalization in the theorem’s block. Separately state Hahn’s hard/soft attention limits. Fixed-depth hard attention in the formal model cannot represent unbounded-length PARITY and 2DYCK under its reduction. For softmax, fix the parameter matrices and layer count independently of sequence length, require uniformly bounded token and positional embeddings combined by addition, globally Lipschitz ReLU-MLP positionwise maps, and a fixed globally Lipschitz logit readout followed by softmax; changing one nonfinal input symbol then changes the last-layer output by O(1/n), with constants depending on the matrices and layer count. The source’s parity next-token distribution is generated by the two-state automaton with fixed p∈(0,1), which terminates with probability p when the number of 1s is even and otherwise emits 0 or 1 with equal probability; its cross-entropy tends to unigram chance level. Its 2DYCK source uses the PCFG with fixed p∈(0,1), S→(S)S and S→[S]S each of probability p/2 and S→ε of probability 1−p. Make the sampling law explicit: its original left-to-right output is a finite or infinite bracket stream; sample a length-k prefix conditional only on reaching k bracket tokens, then predict the ordinary next token. The next-token cross-entropy has liminf excess at least p²(1−p)² log 2 above its conditional optimum for every fixed p∈(0,1). The PCFG’s finite-word masses sum to q=min(1,(1−p)/p); when p>1/2 they form a subprobability law. Conditioning on eventual termination gives exactly the finite-word process with parameter a=pq=min(p,1−p), whose survival-conditioned prefix prediction has liminf excess at least a²(1−a)² log 2 for every fixed p∈(0,1). Both laws agree for p≤1/2, including the critical infinite-mean case; they differ for p>1/2. Exact-total-length conditioning is a separate law and does not retain these transition probabilities. The source leaves prefix conditioning implicit; the exact-law and equal-mass pairing proof is a local completion, not a proof supplied by Hahn. This is not an exact-classification impossibility theorem. These asymptotic limits do not contradict fixed-length universality with growing resources.
