## 0. THE DECISION THIS FORCES FIRST: declare our conventions

Four live convention forks in the literature. Every cross-source statement in the
library is ambiguous until we pick. This becomes `rem-topology-conventions`,
authored EARLY in the topology track, and it is a decision for the owner:

| fork | camp A | camp B |
|---|---|---|
| does "compact" include Hausdorff? | Bourbaki, Engelking (compact = quasi-compact + Hausdorff); algebraic geometry follows | Munkres, Willard, Kelley, Morris, Lee, nLab: no |
| does "neighbourhood" mean OPEN set? | Munkres, Lee: yes | Willard, Kelley, Engelking, Bourbaki: no |
| do "regular"/"normal" include T1? | varies by source | varies by source |
| convergence primitive | nets (Kelley, Willard) | filters (Bourbaki, Morris App. 6) |

Engelking also bakes Hausdorff into "paracompact". My recommendation: follow the
Munkres/Willard/nLab camp (compact does NOT include Hausdorff; neighbourhood need
NOT be open; regular/normal do NOT include T1, with T3/T4 reserved for the
T1-strengthened forms), and do BOTH nets and filters with the dictionary, since
we have a whole page for convergence. State the alternatives in the remark.

## 2. Content I did NOT have and must add

**Foundations / closure calculus (T2b)**
- Kuratowski closure axioms K1-K4 and the BIJECTION closure operators <-> topologies;
  the dual interior axioms I1-I4; Hausdorff's neighbourhood-filter axiomatization.
  Three equivalent ways to define a topology. **Munkres omits all three.**
- Kuratowski 14-set closure-complement theorem, with the explicit ℝ witness
  (0,1) u (1,2) u {3} u ([4,5] n Q).
- The lattice of topologies on X is COMPLETE: an intersection of topologies is a
  topology, a union is not; the join is the topology generated by the union.
- int(A n B) = int A n int B but int(A u B) only contains int A u int B; dually
  for closure. Both fail for infinite index sets. (The asymmetry students reverse.)
- Regular open sets form a COMPLETE BOOLEAN ALGEBRA under
  A v B = int cl(A u B), not A u B. Bridge to Stone duality and forcing.
- cl_A(S) = A n cl_X(S) but int_A(S) can strictly CONTAIN A n int_X(S):
  closure localizes, interior does not.
- Specialization preorder; Alexandrov topologies <-> preorders; finite spaces are
  exactly finite preorders.
- omega-accumulation point and condensation point (distinct from limit point).
- Regular open / regular closed; scattered; dense-in-itself.

**Maps (T2c)**
- Continuity need only be checked on a SUBBASIS. Reduces most product and
  order-topology continuity proofs to one line.
- The full TFAE list for continuity, six conditions including
  cl f^-1(B) subset f^-1(cl B) and f^-1(int B) subset int f^-1(B).
- Pasting lemma for LOCALLY FINITE CLOSED covers (not just two closed pieces),
  with the counterexample showing local finiteness is essential (Z in R covered
  by singletons, Z cofinite).
- Continuity is local (open-cover criterion).
- Initial and final topologies (Bourbaki's organizing idea), their characteristic
  properties and TRANSITIVITY; subspace and product are initial, quotient and sum
  are final. Munkres never names these.
- The EMBEDDING LEMMA (evaluation map): if a family separates points and
  separates points from closed sets, the evaluation map is an embedding. Shared
  engine of the Tychonoff-cube embedding and Urysohn metrization.
- Top is complete and cocomplete; discrete -| forgetful -| indiscrete, which is
  WHY underlying sets of products and quotients are the set-theoretic ones.
- f continuous => f(A') subset cl f(A), NOT f(A') subset f(A)'.

**Constructions (T3)**
- cl(prod A_i) = prod cl(A_i) in BOTH the product and box topologies (so the
  closure formula is not what distinguishes them); int does not behave.
- Whitehead's theorem: (quotient map) x id_Z is a quotient map when Z is locally
  compact Hausdorff; and the PRODUCT OF TWO QUOTIENT MAPS NEED NOT BE A QUOTIENT
  MAP (Dowker). This is precisely why compactly generated spaces exist.
- Open equivalence relations: q open => X/~ Hausdorff iff R is closed in X x X.
- Compact-Hausdorff quotient theorem: a continuous surjection from compact onto
  Hausdorff is a quotient map. THE tool for torus/Klein bottle/RP^n.
- Hewitt-Marczewski-Pondiczery: a product of at most continuum-many separable
  spaces is separable. Separability is far more productive than intuition says.
- Order topology on a SUBSET is generally coarser than the subspace topology;
  they agree on convex subsets. (Munkres warns; most sources bury it.)
- LOTS are hereditarily normal, indeed MONOTONICALLY normal.
- Linear continuum => connected; connected subsets of a linear continuum are
  exactly the convex sets.
- Topological groups (cheap, high yield, and REQUIRED for Bourbaki's definition
  of exp on page 8A): translations and inversion are homeomorphisms so G is
  homogeneous; the topology is determined by any neighbourhood base at e; for
  each nbhd U of e there is symmetric V with VV subset U; **T0 => Hausdorff =>
  completely regular**; closure of a subgroup is a subgroup; an open subgroup is
  closed; G -> G/H is always OPEN; G/H Hausdorff iff H closed.
- Inverse limits of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces are nonempty (uses AC);
  inverse limits of finite discrete spaces = profinite/Stone spaces.
- Adjunction space = pushout in Top; mapping cylinder, cone, suspension, wedge,
  smash, join.

**Connectedness (T4)**
- QUASICOMPONENTS: the intersection of all clopen sets containing x. Components
  are contained in quasicomponents, strictly in general. **Absent from Munkres;
  the most commonly missed item in this topic.**
- Sura-Bura theorem: components = quasicomponents in compact Hausdorff spaces,
  and in locally connected spaces.
- Connected im kleinen at x is STRICTLY WEAKER than locally connected at x
  (pointwise); the two agree globally.
- Locally connected <=> components of every open set are open.
- Connected + locally path-connected => path-connected. The actual reason open
  connected subsets of R^n are path-connected.
- Arbitrary products of connected spaces are connected (finite-support density
  argument; uses AC to pick base points).
- IVT stated TOPOLOGICALLY for order topologies (Munkres 24.3), not just for R.
- Cut points as homeomorphism invariants: (0,1), [0,1), [0,1] pairwise
  non-homeomorphic; S^1 not homeomorphic to [0,1]; R not homeomorphic to R^n.
- Sierpinski: a continuum is not a countable disjoint union of >= 2 nonempty
  closed sets.
- Boundary bumping lemma for continua.
- Totally disconnected vs totally separated vs zero-dimensional: all distinct in
  general (Erdos space, Knaster-Kuratowski fan), all coincide for locally compact
  Hausdorff, giving Stone/profinite spaces.
- Chain-connected (well-chained) <=> connected for COMPACT METRIC spaces;
  Q is well-chained and disconnected.
- Hyperconnected, ultraconnected, extremally disconnected.

**Compactness (T5)**
- KURATOWSKI-MROWKA: X is compact <=> the projection X x Y -> Y is closed for
  every space Y. A CHOICE-FREE, purely categorical characterization. Excellent
  item.
- The tube lemma and its generalized form; projection along a compact factor is
  closed. Choice-free.
- Alexander subbase lemma (Zorn/BPI) as a working tool; gives a short Tychonoff.
- Local compactness has FIVE inequivalent definitions, coinciding for Hausdorff
  (indeed preregular) spaces. We must declare which we use.
- A subspace of a locally compact Hausdorff space is locally compact <=> it is
  LOCALLY CLOSED (open in its closure).
- LCH => completely regular, and **LCH => Baire** (the second form of the Baire
  category theorem, routinely omitted in favour of the complete-metric form).
- The one-point compactification is the MINIMUM Hausdorff compactification and
  beta-X is the MAXIMUM: frame the compactification lattice, not just endpoints.
- Proper (preimages of compacta compact) vs universally closed (Bourbaki), and
  exactly when they agree (LCH or compactly generated Hausdorff codomain).
- Perfect maps and their preservation/reflection table (Engelking §3.7).
- k-spaces / compactly generated spaces, k-ification, CGHaus is cartesian closed.
- sigma-compact + locally compact => exhaustion by compacts with K_n subset
  int K_{n+1}: the lemma every manifold argument silently uses.
- Lindelof + countably compact <=> compact; countably compact <=> limit point
  compact FOR T1 (T1 is needed).
- Compact and sequentially compact are INDEPENDENT in general.
- A. H. Stone: every metric space is paracompact (needs AC, not a ZF theorem).
  Dieudonne: paracompact Hausdorff => normal. Michael's theorem.
- Pseudocompact, metacompact, orthocompact, hemicompact.

**Convergence (T6)**
- Sequential closure is NOT IDEMPOTENT; iterating transfinitely to omega_1 is
  required; the "sequential order" of a space.
- Franklin's theorem: sequential spaces = exactly the quotients of metric spaces.
- The strict hierarchy metrizable => first countable => Frechet-Urysohn =>
  sequential => k-space, with a witness for each strictness.
- US spaces: unique SEQUENTIAL limits does NOT imply Hausdorff; the correct
  characterization needs nets.
- The canonical failure list, to be taught as a SET: cocountable topology on an
  uncountable set; [0, omega_1]; [0, omega_1); beta-N; [0,1]^[0,1] and {0,1}^R;
  Arens-Fort space; Arens space S_2 (sequential but not Frechet-Urysohn); the
  topology of pointwise convergence; the box topology on R^omega.
- The product topology IS the topology of pointwise convergence; this fails
  badly in the box topology.
- Willard §4.1 is literally titled "Inadequacy of sequences": the canonical
  textbook justification, and our page summary should say so.

## 3. Choice bookkeeping, now exact (goes into F1's table)

NEEDS FULL AC: Tychonoff (equivalent to AC, Kelley 1950); "every vector space has
a basis"; "every metric space is paracompact" (A. H. Stone) is NOT a ZF theorem.
NEEDS BPI / ULTRAFILTER LEMMA (strictly weaker than AC): Tychonoff for Hausdorff
factors (Los-Ryll-Nardzewski, Rubin-Scott); compactness of the Tychonoff cube
[0,1]^I; existence of beta-X; the Alexander subbase lemma.
NEEDS COUNTABLE/DEPENDENT CHOICE: "second countable => Lindelof"; the metric
compactness equivalences (compact <=> sequentially compact <=> complete + totally
bounded); Baire category.
CHOICE-FREE: the tube lemma; FINITE Tychonoff; Heine-Borel for [a,b] (needs only
the least-upper-bound property); Kuratowski-Mrowka.

## 4. A deliverable no standard source supplies ready-made

Engelking's appendix has literal INVARIANCE TABLES ("Invariants of operations",
"Invariants and inverse invariants of mappings"): a hereditary / weakly
hereditary / open-hereditary / productive / divisible matrix over all the
properties. The sweep flags building this for the library as high value.
PROPOSAL: a dedicated page `topological-properties-invariance-tables` whose body
is that matrix, every cell citing the item that proves or refutes it. It would be
a genuinely novel artifact and a natural capstone for the topology track.
Recorded facts to seed it: hereditary = T0, T1, Hausdorff, regular, completely
regular, first and second countable, metrizable, totally disconnected,
zero-dimensional; weakly hereditary only = compact, countably compact, Lindelof,
sigma-compact, normal, paracompact; open-hereditary = local compactness
(Hausdorff), local connectedness, separability; not hereditary at all =
connectedness, compactness. Productive = T0, T1, Hausdorff, regular, Tychonoff,
connected, path-connected, compact, zero-dimensional, totally disconnected.
Not productive = normality, Lindelof, paracompactness, local compactness (unless
all but finitely many factors are compact), countable compactness, sequential
compactness (countable products only), metrizability and first/second
countability (countable products only).
