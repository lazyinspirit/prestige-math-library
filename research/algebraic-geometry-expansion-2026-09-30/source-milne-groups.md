# Milne source lane: algebraic groups and group schemes

Research report for the algebraic-geometry expansion, lane 3. This report proposes scaffold work only; it does not change the canonical plan or any item page.

## Full-text evidence and source boundary

I independently located the source from J. S. Milne’s official author site, [Algebraic Groups](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iag.html), and retrieved its complete author-hosted PDF, [iAG2022.pdf](https://www.jmilne.org/math/Books/iAG2022.pdf). The author page identifies the book as *Algebraic Groups: The Theory of Group Schemes of Finite Type over a Field*, published by Cambridge University Press in 2017 and corrected for the 2022 reprint; the PDF title page says “5th October 2021; corrected; © J.S. Milne 2017, 2021; As published by CUP, 2022.” The retrieved PDF is 4,838,013 bytes, SHA-256 `f2ddd8fa4d263085f173934664b246007a2c0bd539739b7c82de39bfb5d21f40`. Its 659 physical PDF pages carry the book’s printed pagination through p. 644. Locators below use printed pages and section/theorem numbers.

I retrieved the full file and extracted its full text. I read the complete table of contents and targeted the definitions, theorem statements, proofs, notes, and dependencies in Chapters 1–8, 10–18, 20–23 and Appendices B–C relevant to this proposal. I did not read unrelated chapters, examples, and exercises cover to cover. Some mathematical symbols are garbled in the text extraction, so theorem statements and proof routes were checked against the PDF pages. No second full text was used as a substitute for Milne.

Milne’s book is about finite-type group schemes over a **field** (the author explicitly says “algebraic group” abbreviates “algebraic group scheme”). It allows nonreduced schemes. It is not a general treatment of group schemes over arbitrary base schemes. Several chapter results impose smoothness, connectedness, algebraic closure, perfectness, or splitness; those conditions must remain attached to the proposed pages. Chapter 3’s Hopf algebra definition allows a commutative base ring, but its affine-group correspondence is stated for finitely generated algebras over a field.

## Proof-sufficiency audit and non-negotiable boundaries

Milne provides genuinely local proofs for the Hopf correspondence and closed subgroup/Hopf ideal dictionary (3.6–3.15), finite-dimensional faithful representations (4.7–4.10), the characteristic-zero Cartier smoothness theorem (3.23), the tangent Lie bracket (10.23), multiplicative-type criteria/classification (12.18/12.23), the unipotent triangularization criterion (14.5), Lie–Kolchin (16.30), Borel fixed point (17.3), Chevalley’s line-stabilizer theorem (4.27), the smooth-affine quotient theorem (7.18), and the split root/Bruhat/parabolic structure of Chapter 21. The field-level reduced-root-datum existence theorem (23.55) is developed in Ch. 23(h) and Ch. 24; do not mistake it for the stronger integral model theorem 23.74, whose proof is explicitly delegated to Demazure/SGA 3. “Full proof in Milne” still means using the stated earlier Milne prerequisites: for example, Thm. 7.18 consumes 4.27 and 7.17; Thm. 10.23 consumes 4.9 and 10.14; and Thm. 21.80 summarizes constructions proved earlier in the chapter.

The proof chain is not uniformly self-contained. Keep these explicit source boundaries in the scaffold:

1. **Characteristic zero is special, not default.** Cartier’s Thm. 3.23 only makes affine algebraic groups smooth in characteristic zero. `alpha_p` and `mu_p` in characteristic p are nonreduced counterexamples to treating all groups as varieties. Lie algebra is not a complete invariant for groups, and the exponential/Lie equivalence for unipotents (14.37) is characteristic zero and invokes Ado and Engel. Milne’s characteristic-p sections discuss additional phenomena, including nonsplit/wound unipotents.
2. **Quotient scope must be exact.** Thm. 7.18 proves a separated scheme quotient for smooth affine `G`; it does not imply projectivity. General group quotients are handled through Thm. 5.14/Appendix B. The alternate proof of the nonsmooth orbit statement 7.20 explicitly cites Demazure–Gabriel lemmas. A page should not conflate a set of `k`-point cosets, an fppf quotient sheaf, and its representing scheme.
3. **Nonaffine structure has two theorem levels.** Uniqueness in Barsotti–Chevalley 8.27 is stated over a perfect field for a connected group variety. Thm. 8.28 has arbitrary-field/connected-algebraic-group scope but only asserts existence; its affine normal subgroup can be non-smooth. Projectivity of abelian varieties (8.45) is stated but not proved in this book.
4. **Tori and finite diagonalizable groups differ.** Character lattices of tori are torsion-free; groups of multiplicative type can have torsion and can be non-smooth. The classification in 12.23 is by continuous Galois modules over the separable closure, not merely an abstract lattice over `k`.
5. **Borel and flag hypotheses matter.** Fixed points require a smooth connected solvable group and a nonempty complete scheme, with the point possibly only over `k^a`. Borel/maximal-torus conjugacy is stated over algebraically closed fields/group varieties. Do not transport the complex flag pages to arbitrary nonsplit groups without descent arguments.
6. **Split reductive results are not all reductive results.** Ch. 21’s root groups, Bruhat cells, and parabolics are for split reductive groups over a field. The root datum is not just the Lie-algebra root system; it includes character and cocharacter lattices and their pairing. Thm. 23.74’s integral group-scheme existence/uniqueness depends on SGA 3/Demazure, not a proof supplied by Milne.
7. **Highest weight is not complete reducibility.** Thm. 22.2 classifies simple rational modules in every characteristic for split reductive groups. It does not say every module is semisimple. Group/Lie representation equivalence in 22.53 is a characteristic-zero result, and the simple-connected condition is needed for essential surjectivity.

For any new theorem page, the proof should either reproduce the indicated local argument from Milne, prove each invoked earlier lemma, or explicitly retain the source result as an external dependency. A citation to Milne does not close a proof obligation. The strongest gaps relative to the current AG plan are the absence of group-object/functor-of-points pages, finite-type group schemes with nilpotents, Hopf structures and rational comodules, Lie/finite-group-scheme interfaces, quotient-scheme scope, the nonaffine affine/abelian decomposition, arithmetic tori, and split reductive theory across characteristics. Frontier-36 has already closed the complex flag-specialization gap and should be treated as a published downstream supplier.
