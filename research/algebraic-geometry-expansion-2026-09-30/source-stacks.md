## Algebraic groups, abelian varieties, and quotient limits

### Abelian-variety projectivity

The source concern raised against Milne's *Algebraic Geometry* Theorem 8.45
is covered directly in Stacks. In §39.9 [0BF9], Definition 39.9.1 defines an
abelian variety over a field `k` as a group scheme that is a proper,
geometrically integral variety over `k`. Lemma 39.9.2 [0BFA] proves that such
an `A` is projective. Its complete dependency route is:

1. Lemma 39.8.7 [0BF7] proves every algebraic group scheme over a field is
   quasi-projective; the proof constructs an ample invertible sheaf after
   reduction to the connected smooth case.
2. More on Morphisms, Lemma 37.50.1 [0B45] identifies a proper scheme over a
   base with an ample invertible sheaf as projective (the base is `Spec k`).
3. Properness is in the definition of abelian variety; combine it with (1).

The `0BF7` proof uses group-scheme component/reduction results in §§39.7–39.8
and the ample-open criterion; it is more than a one-line citation. Stacks
therefore supplies a complete proof under the exact field/abelian-variety
hypotheses. No new AG pair is needed solely to source this theorem, though the
local library item should spell out the definition and the projectivity
deduction.

### Quotient and descent statements actually covered

Stacks has broad group-scheme/groupoid coverage in Chapter 39, but it carefully
separates a quotient sheaf from a quotient scheme.

- §39.20 [02VE], Definition 39.20.1 [02VG] defines `U/R` as the sheafification
  of the orbit presheaf for the Zariski, étale, fppf, smooth or syntomic
  topology. Definition 39.20.2 [03BD] calls it representable only when that
  sheaf is represented by a scheme. Lemma 39.20.3 [03C5] is a useful
  representability test: a candidate `M` represents `U/R` if `U→M` is a
  sheaf-surjection and `R→U×_M U` is a sheaf-surjection.
- For affine `U=Spec A`, `R=Spec B` with `s,t` finite locally free and
  `(t,s)` an equivalence relation, Proposition 39.23.9 [03BM] constructs
  `C=A^R`, proves `U→Spec C` finite locally free and `R=U×_{Spec C}U`, and
  identifies `Spec C` with the fppf quotient sheaf. This is a fully proved
  affine quotient supplier with exact finiteness hypotheses.
- For nonaffine `U`, Lemma 39.24.1 [03JE] produces invariant affine
  neighborhoods when each relevant finite equivalence class lies in an
  affine open. *Properties of Algebraic Spaces* Proposition 66.14.1 [07S6]
  then gives a scheme quotient when `s,t` are finite locally free, `(t,s)` is
  an equivalence relation, and every nonempty closed subset contains a point
  whose equivalence class lies in an affine open. The proof glues the affine
  fppf quotients from [03BM]. An earlier comment questioned whether the
  closed-subset hypothesis supplied enough affine neighborhoods. The live
  statement was strengthened on 2025-07-22: every nonempty closed subset must
  contain at least one point whose equivalence class lies in an affine open.
  The Stacks Project response to that comment says this change provides
  “enough” such points. The comment is resolved by the current hypothesis;
  [07S6] remains limited to these finite-locally-free groupoid equivalence
  relations and is not a general arbitrary-group quotient theorem.
- §39.25 [0CCH], Lemma 39.25.3 [0CCJ] proves effectiveness of descent data for
  a scheme `V/X` along a surjective finite locally free map `X→Y` when `V/X`
  is projective, quasi-projective, or has the listed ample line bundle
  condition. This is not unrestricted fpqc descent of arbitrary schemes.
  Lemmas 39.21.2–.3 [0APE, 0APF] express groupoid data as descent data.

These results do not establish representability of `G/H` for an arbitrary
subgroup scheme `H`, nor the Borel quotient `G/B` for a semisimple group. The
current semisimple flag/root-subgroup/Schubert and Borel–Weil–Bott chain still
needs Milne, Conrad and Brion for its Lie/group structure. The Stacks Chapter
39 groupoid formalism is not a substitute for that structure theory.

## Other material outside the inspected Stacks supplier set

- The classical varieties half uses Artin/Milne proofs for Nullstellensatz,
  elimination/resultants, plane Bézout, classical Grassmannian/Plücker
  equations, and tangent-line geometry. Stacks gives a scheme foundation and
  has an entire *Intersection Theory* chapter, but no exact direct Bézout tag
  was verified for this report.
- The current Stacks crosswalk includes no full GIT quotient/linearization
  theory, moduli representability package, or reductive-group root/flag
  construction. The quotient sheaf results above cover narrower conditions.
- Chapter 54 proves resolution for curves on regular surfaces under finite
  normalization. That does not prove resolution for arbitrary higher
  dimensional schemes.
- Full Chow groups/GRR and intersection-theoretic Chern/Todd machinery remain
  outside the read Stacks range and the present AV dependency closure.
- The high-dimensional dualizing-complex/Serre material at [0FVV]–[0FW0] is
  available in Stacks; the library's curve residue pairing is still a
  separate, concrete proof in AV-25.
