## Sources consulted

- Stephen D. Smith, *The Classification of Finite Simple Groups: A User's
  Guide*, `https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~smiths/talkv.pdf`: the CFSG family
  list, the five Mathieu group names, the definitions of components and
  `F^*(G)`, and the theorem `C_G(F^*(G)) <= F^*(G)`.
- American Mathematical Society, *The Classification of the Finite Simple
  Groups, Number 10*, `https://bookstore.ams.org/The-Classification-Of-The-Finite-Simple-Groups`:
  Number 10 belongs to a series whose aim is a complete proof, while that
  volume completes only the bicharacteristic-type portion begun in Number 9.
- Oberwolfach Report 16/2025, *The Classification of Finite Simple Groups*,
  `https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/51362?nt=1`: the organizer
  report describes the Gorenstein--Lyons--Solomon project as ongoing and
  nearing completion in 2025.
- Ronald Solomon, *The Classification of Finite Simple Groups: A Progress
  Report*, `https://www.ams.org/journals/notices/201806/rnoti-p646.pdf`:
  historical scope and the then-planned future volumes; used only as context,
  not as evidence for the 2026 status conclusion.
- J. Michael Boardman, *Universal Coefficient Theorem for Cohomology*,
  `https://math.jhu.edu/~jmb/note/uctcoh.pdf`: the natural universal-coefficient
  short exact sequence for a free integral chain complex splits, but the
  splitting is not natural.
- Allen Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*,
  `https://math.uchicago.edu/~farb/algtop2023/Hatcher.pdf`: the UCT splitting
  is not natural, with the standard exercise establishing that no natural
  choice exists.
- Clara Löh, *Group Cohomology, SS 2019*,
  `https://loeh.app.uni-regensburg.de/teaching/grouphom_ss19/lecture_notes.pdf`:
  the Hopf formula requires specified free-presentation data and a universal
  central extension is initial among central extensions of its target.
- Pavel Etingof, *18.755 Lie Groups and Lie Algebras II* lecture notes,
  `https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-755-lie-groups-and-lie-algebras-ii-spring-2024/mit18_755_s24_lec_full.pdf`:
  the Verma-module universal property allows the target vector to be zero;
  nonzero is imposed only when calling it a highest-weight vector.
- Yanqi Lake Li, *Algebra I* notes,
  `https://www.wwli.asia/downloads/YAlg1.pdf`: additive extension of
  induction, the induction ideal, Banaschewski's finite-product argument, and
  the normal-series convention for supersolvable groups.
- Tammo tom Dieck, *Representation Theory*,
  `https://www.uni-math.gwdg.de/tammo/d01.pdf`: the finite-group setting for
  character theory, the faithful-character inertia argument, and compatibility
  of inflation with induction from a full preimage.

universal-coefficient interface used by this group's Schur-multiplier page.
