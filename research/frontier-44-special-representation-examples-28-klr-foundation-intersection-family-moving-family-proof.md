## Statement

Let $f:X\to Y$ be a projective morphism of complex projective varieties, $X$ smooth connected of dimension $n$, with image dimension $m$. Give $f$ a supplied finite algebraic Whitney stratification with smooth surjective stratum maps, adapted to its fibre-dimension loci. Put $K=Rf_*\mathbb Q_X[n]$ and $P={}^pH^0K$. For a connected stratum $S\subset f(X)$ of dimension $1\le s\le m-1$, there exists a dense algebraic open $T\subset S$, a smooth projective morphism $F:\mathcal X_T\to T$ of relative dimension $n-s$, and a surjective morphism of local systems
$$R^{n-s}F_*\mathbb Q\longrightarrow (\mathcal H^{-s}P)|_T.$$
The normalized comparison identifying its target uses only proper base change and smooth/transverse perverse pullbacks; its total shift is zero. The source local system is semisimple, also after complexification, by the independent smooth-projective Gauss–Manin theorem. Thus the stratum local system is semisimple over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb C$, and this extends from $T$ to $S$ by the actual dense-open fundamental-group supplier.

## Proof

1.1 Put $W=H^0(\mathbb P^N,\mathcal O(1))$, $\Pi=(\mathbb P(W))^s$ and $d=sN$. Define $\mathcal Y=\{(y,H_1,\ldots,H_s):y\in H_j\text{ for all }j\}\subset Y\times\Pi$ and $\mathcal X=\mathcal Y\times_YX\subset X\times\Pi$. Write $q:\mathcal Y\to Y$, $q_X:\mathcal X\to X$, $h:\mathcal Y\to\Pi$, $g:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y$ and $h_X=h g$. Evaluation of $W$ onto the line $\mathcal O_Y(1)|_y$ is surjective. On an affine chart where one coordinate is nonzero, each hyperplane equation solves its corresponding coefficient in terms of the remaining coefficients. Thus its kernel is a rank-$N$ algebraic vector bundle, locally trivial by this explicit solution, and $q$ is a product of $s$ projective bundles with fibre $(\mathbb P^{N-1})^s$ and relative dimension $d-s$. The same holds for $q_X$, so $\mathcal X$ is smooth of dimension $n+d-s$. The projection $h_X$ is projective because $X$ is projective and the incidence is closed in $X\times\Pi$. This constructs the family, not merely a map on individual fibres. [F1, algebra, construct]

2.1 For a smooth target stratum $R$ of dimension $u$, the corresponding incidence $\mathcal Y_R=q^{-1}(R)$ is smooth of dimension $u+d-s$. At $y=[1:0:\cdots:0]\in R$, hyperplanes through $y$ are linear forms in the affine coordinates; their differentials span $T_y^*R$ because $R\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N$ is a smooth locally closed embedding. If $u\ge s$, choose $s$ independent differentials. The incidence tangent equations $d(H_j|_R)(v)+\delta H_j(y)=0$ can then be solved for $v$ for every parameter variation $\delta H$. Hence $dh$ is surjective at this incidence point; its image contains an analytic open and is therefore Zariski dense. Thus $h|_{\mathcal Y_R}$ is dominant. If $u<s$, its source has dimension less than $d$, so its image is not dense. On the open smooth locus of $f$ over its top image stratum, $df$ has rank $m\ge s$. The same tangent equations with $df(v)$ prove that $h_X$ is dominant. No projective-degree count or nonempty-connected-Bertini assertion is hidden here. [F1, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 Apply target generic smoothness [F1] to $h_X$ and to each dominant $h|_{\mathcal Y_R}$. Intersect their finitely many dense parameter opens, including opens contained in their images. Remove the closed image closures of the nondominant strata. The resulting $\Pi^0$ is nonempty and dense. Over it, $h_X$ is smooth surjective of relative dimension $n-s$; every surviving stratum $\mathcal Y_R$ maps smoothly and surjectively to $\Pi^0$, with relative dimension $u-s$. Its tangent equations prove that every parameter complete intersection cuts $R$ transversely; strata of dimension less than $s$ are absent. The product-projective-bundle pullbacks of the original Whitney strata give Whitney stratifications of $\mathcal X$ and $\mathcal Y$. The map $g$ is stratified: on each source/target stratum its local form is the original smooth stratum map times the same projective-bundle coordinates. Restricting to $\Pi^0$ preserves these properties. The needed uniform parameter open has thus been obtained from the actual generic-smoothness theorem, not just pointwise smoothness of a few sections. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 In particular $b=h|_{\mathcal Y_S}:\mathcal Y_S\to\Pi$ is dominant and is smooth of relative dimension zero over $\Pi^0$. Since $S$ is connected smooth, it is irreducible; the product-projective bundle $q_S:\mathcal Y_S\to S$ is irreducible too. The dense open $b^{-1}(\Pi^0)$ therefore intersects the generic fibre of $q_S$. Let $k(S)$ be its infinite function field. A trivialization over a dense open of $S$ identifies that fibre with $(\mathbb P^{N-1}_{k(S)})^s$. Its nonempty open meets the standard dense affine chart. A nonzero polynomial over an infinite field cannot vanish at every rational affine point: induct on the number of variables and then avoid the finitely many roots in the last variable. Apply this to a defining nonzero polynomial of a principal open inside the given open. It gives an actual $k(S)$-rational point in the good generic fibre. Its finitely many rational coordinates extend to regular functions after removing their denominators; shrinking further removes the pullback of the bad closed complement. Thus a dense open $T\subset S$ has an algebraic section $\mu:T\to\mathcal Y_S$ of $q_S$ whose image lies in $b^{-1}(\Pi^0)$. Put $\rho=b\mu:T\to\Pi^0$. This is the missing generic-section construction. [step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra, choose, construct]

5.1 The source's quasi-finiteness assertion can also be checked directly, although the comparison below does not require it. For $p\in\Pi^0$, the intersection $S\cap Y_p$ is zero-dimensional and misses $\overline S\setminus S$, since that boundary is a union of lower-dimensional strata, all absent by step 3.1. It is therefore closed in the projective $\overline S$, and finite. Consequently $b^{-1}(p)$ is finite. The section $\mu$ is injective, so $\rho^{-1}(p)$ is a subset of this finite fibre; $\rho$ is finite type and quasi-finite. Also $db$ is invertible on the smooth zero-relative-dimensional incidence, while $d\mu$ is injective because $q_S\mu=1$. Thus $d\rho$ has rank $s$. We do not need to assume without construction that $\rho$ is smooth onto its image or that its image is closed; the graph factorization below supplies the needed pullback comparison for this actual $\rho$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

5.2 Form $\mathcal Y_T=\mathcal Y\times_{\Pi}T$, $\mathcal X_T=\mathcal X\times_{\Pi}T$, with $\pi:\mathcal Y_T\to T$, $\Phi:\mathcal X_T\to\mathcal Y_T$ and $F=\pi\Phi$. By step 3.1 and smooth base change, $F$ is smooth projective of relative dimension $n-s$ and $\mathcal X_T$ is smooth of total dimension $n$. Each induced target stratum maps submersively to $T$. There is a tautological section $\theta(t)=(t,\rho(t),t)\in\mathcal Y_T$, since $\mu(t)$ chooses hyperplanes through $t$. The stratum induced by $S$ has relative dimension zero over $T$, hence is étale; its section $\theta$ is an open-and-closed component, using separatedness for closedness and the local étale inverse for openness. Separate this component from its complement as strata; this does not change the local Whitney conditions. Thus $\theta(T)$ is an actual stratum and $\theta^*P_\Phi$ has locally constant cohomology. Every fibre $X_t$ is the smooth projective source $f^{-1}(Y_t)$ of an $s$-fold transverse target slice. Its defect does not increase: on each fibre-dimension stratum the dimension drops by $s$, while the smooth source dimension also drops by $s$, so $2a+\dim(Y_t)_a-(n-s)\le r(f)$. Its image dimension is at most $m-s<m$. Apply the lower joint induction to each connected component of $X_t$, if needed. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra, construct]

6.1 The comparison has exact total shift zero. First $q^*[d-s]$ is perverse-exact and proper base change gives $q^*K[d-s]=Rg_*\mathbb Q_{\mathcal X}[n+d-s]$. Next factor the base change to $T$ as $\mathcal Y_T\xrightarrow{\iota}\mathcal Y|_{\Pi^0}\times T\xrightarrow{e}\mathcal Y|_{\Pi^0}$. The projection $e$ is smooth of relative dimension $s$. The first map is the inverse image of the graph of $\rho$ in $\Pi^0\times T$; in parameter coordinates it is cut out by $h(z)-\rho(t)=0$, exactly $d$ equations. Their derivatives on every stratum are surjective by step 3.1, so this is a transverse restriction of a smooth ambient complex embedding of codimension $d$. Thus $e^*[s]$ and $\iota^*[-d]$ are perverse-exact by [F2]. Combining with $q^*[d-s]$ gives net shift $(d-s)+s-d=0$. For $p_Y:\mathcal Y_T\to Y$, proper base change therefore identifies $p_Y^*K=R\Phi_*\mathbb Q_{\mathcal X_T}[n]$ and $p_Y^*P_b={}^pH^b(R\Phi_*\mathbb Q_{\mathcal X_T}[n])$. The same oriented smooth/normal comparisons have exceptional shifts $2(d-s)+2s-2d=0$, so there is no omitted transverse-orientation shift. Pulling back the earlier normalized finite splitting gives a finite normalized splitting for this actual $\Phi$. This is not an invocation of middle semisimplicity or a new decomposition theorem for $\Phi$. [F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 5.2, algebra]

7.1 Write $K_\Phi=R\Phi_*\mathbb Q_{\mathcal X_T}[n]$ and $P_{\Phi,b}={}^pH^bK_\Phi$. The unit $K_\Phi\to\theta_*\theta^*K_\Phi$, followed by $R\pi_*$ and the identity $\pi\theta=1$, gives $a:RF_*\mathbb Q_{\mathcal X_T}[n]\to\theta^*K_\Phi$. Compose it with the projection $\theta^*K_\Phi\to\theta^*P_{\Phi,0}$ in the pulled-back splitting. Taking $H^{-s}$ gives a genuine sheaf morphism $\sigma:R^{n-s}F_*\mathbb Q\to\mathcal H^{-s}(\theta^*P_{\Phi,0})$. Step 6.1 and $p_Y\theta:T\hookrightarrow Y$ identify the target with $(\mathcal H^{-s}P)|_T$. Both sides are local systems: the source by smooth proper transport, the target by its restriction to the stratum. The choice of splitting is only used to construct this projection; it is normalized and its existence was established before the present semisimplicity stage. [F2, step 5.2, step 6.1, algebra, construct]

8.1 For $t\in T$, the inclusion $j_t:Y_t\hookrightarrow\mathcal Y_T$ is transverse to every stratum, of codimension $s$, because each stratum maps submersively to $T$. Hence $j_t^*[-s]$ is perverse-exact. Proper base change identifies $j_t^*K_\Phi[-s]=Rf_{t*}\mathbb Q_{X_t}[n-s]$ and $j_t^*P_{\Phi,0}[-s]={}^pH^0(Rf_{t*}\mathbb Q_{X_t}[n-s])$. Trace the adjunction and projection defining $\sigma$ through these canonical base-change maps. Its stalk is exactly the composite from $H^{n-s}(X_t)$ to its normalized middle perverse summand and then the graded restriction $H^{n-s}_0(X_t)\to H^{n-s}_0(f^{-1}(t))$. The latter is surjective by [F3] for the lower map in step 5.2; the summand projection is surjective. Thus every stalk of $\sigma$ is surjective, and $\sigma$ is a surjection of the actual local systems in the Statement. This supplies the moving-family identification, rather than assuming that the stratum coefficient system is itself a smooth-fibre cohomology group. [F2, F3, step 5.2, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 The independent Gauss–Manin theorem [F4] makes $R^{n-s}F_*\mathbb Q$ semisimple over $\mathbb Q$ and after complexification. Quotients of a semisimple representation split into simple constituents, so the target local system has both semisimplicity properties on $T$. Surjectivity of $\pi_1(T)\to\pi_1(S)$ from [F4] means that its monodromy image on $T$ equals the original image on $S$; every invariant complement over $T$ is therefore invariant on $S$. This proves both properties on $S$. For $s=0$ the local system is a finite vector space on points; for $s=m$ it is the ordinary smooth-projective fibre local system on the top smooth-image open, handled by the same independent Gauss–Manin interface. Strata outside $f(X)$ carry zero. These boundary cases add no claim about arbitrary local systems. [F4, step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
