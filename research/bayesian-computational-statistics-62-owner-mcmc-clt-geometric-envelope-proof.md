## A. Kernel-stable countably generated reduction

Let (X,F), P, pi, M0, r0 be as in the target. Thus M0 is finite and measurable everywhere, 0<r0<1, and d_n(x):=sup_A |P^n(x,A)-pi(A)| <= M0(x)r0^n for n>=1.

Start with the countable family of rational sublevel sets of M0. Let A0 be the algebra it generates. Inductively let A_{j+1} be the algebra generated by A_j together with every set {x:P(x,A)>q}, A in A_j, q rational. Every A_j is countable. Put A=union_j A_j and G=sigma(A). For every A in A, P1_A is G-measurable by construction. The class of B in G for which P1_B is G-measurable is a lambda-system containing the algebra A (complement and disjoint-union closure follow from probability additivity and limits). The pi-lambda theorem therefore makes P a probability kernel on (X,G). Its iterates are the restrictions of the original iterates: first prove the integration identity for indicators, then simple functions and monotone limits. M0 is G-measurable, and pi restricted to G is invariant.

This restricted chain is pi|G-irreducible: if B in G has pi(B)>0, then for every x the original geometric bound implies P^n(x,B)>0 for all sufficiently large n. No passage from the original phi to a maximal irreducibility measure is required. No aperiodicity or Harris inheritance theorem is required either: the argument below only needs this irreducibility, the invariant law, and the original bound. The target's additional hypotheses are harmless.

Apply the existing countably generated small-set supplier with irreducibility measure pi|G. It gives m>=1, D in G, pi(D)>0, epsilon0>0, and a probability nu0 on G such that P^m(x,.) >= epsilon0 nu0(.) for x in D and nu0(D)>0.

Choose K finite so C=D intersect {M0<=K} has both pi(C)>0 and nu0(C)>0. This is possible because these intersections increase to D. Choose L>=K finite with a:=nu0(M0<=L)>0. Put nu(B)=nu0(B intersect {M0<=L})/a, and choose 0<epsilon<min(1,epsilon0 a). Then, for Q=P^m restricted to G,

    Q(x,.) >= epsilon nu(.) for x in C,
    nu(C)>0,  nu(M0)<=L,  sup_C M0<=K.

This construction is on G only. It never asserts that nu extends to a full-F minorizing probability. In particular there is no unjustified lift of a factor small set to a small set of the original kernel.

## B. Split chain, scalar renewal, and exponential return

For the Q-chain on G use the standard explicit split construction. At each state x assign a flag i with probability epsilon 1_C(x) of i=1. From flag 1 the next x-coordinate has law nu. From flag 0 at x in C it has residual law (Q(x,.)-epsilon nu)/(1-epsilon); outside C it has law Q(x,.). Assign the next flag by the same rule. The flag-1 set alpha=C x {1} is an atom: its outgoing transition is independent of x. All kernels are measurable; zero-denominator cases are avoided by epsilon<1. The marginal x-chain, when initialized with the stated random flag, has kernel Q.

Its invariant probability is

    pi_tilde(dx,1)=epsilon 1_C(x)pi(dx),
    pi_tilde(dx,0)=(1-epsilon 1_C(x))pi(dx).

Check invariance by summing the flagged outgoing transitions: epsilon 1_C nu plus (1-epsilon 1_C) times the residual equals Q, and the next flag is sampled as prescribed. Write p=pi_tilde(alpha)=epsilon pi(C)>0.

Starting in alpha, the first next x-coordinate has law nu and thereafter evolves marginally by Q. Therefore the atomic renewal probabilities satisfy

    u_0=1,
    u_n=P_alpha(Z_n in alpha)=epsilon nu Q^(n-1)(C), n>=1.

For n>=2 the original geometric bound and nu(M0)<=L give

    |u_n-p| <= epsilon L r0^[m(n-1)].

The finitely many first indices can be absorbed into a constant. Thus u_n=p+O(theta^n), theta=r0^m<1.

Let tau be the first positive return time to alpha and f_n=P_alpha(tau=n), n>=1. First-return decomposition (including possible nonreturn mass) gives u_n=sum_{j=1}^n f_j u_{n-j}. Hence for |z|<1,

    U(z)=sum_{n>=0}u_n z^n=1/(1-F(z)),
    F(z)=sum_{n>=1}f_n z^n.

Since U(t)~p/(1-t) as t increases to 1, F(t) tends to 1; monotone convergence gives sum f_n=1. Moreover

    U(z)=p/(1-z)+H(z)

with H analytic for |z|<theta^(-1). Near z=1,

    F(z)=1-(1-z)/(p+(1-z)H(z)).

The denominator is nonzero on some neighborhood of 1. This analytically extends F through 1. To justify the crucial exponential-moment inference without silently assuming its radius of convergence: for every k, termwise differentiated F(t), 0<t<1, has nonnegative coefficients. Monotone convergence as t increases to 1 and continuity of the analytically continued derivative give

    sum_n f_n (n)_k = F^(k)(1) < infinity.

Choose a closed disk about 1 strictly inside that analytic neighborhood, of radius delta, with |F|<=B on its boundary. Cauchy's estimate gives F^(k)(1)/k! <= B delta^(-k). For 0<h<delta, Tonelli and the binomial formula imply

    E_alpha[(1+h)^tau]
      = sum_{k>=0} h^k F^(k)(1)/k!
      <= B/(1-h/delta) < infinity.

Thus E_alpha[b^tau]<infinity for some b>1. This is the complete scalar converse; a citation to a geometric drift characterization is unnecessary.

## C. Stationary occupation and integrable residual hitting moment

Here all expectations are for the split chain. Let T=inf{n>=0:Z_n in alpha}. First establish P_{pi_tilde}(T<infinity)=1 without assuming a Harris theorem for the split chain. Under stationarity let I_j=1_alpha(Z_j). Then E I_j=p and Cov(I_0,I_n)=p(u_n-p). The exponential u_n bound gives

    Var(N^(-1) sum_{j=0}^{N-1} I_j) <= c/N.

On {T>=N} this average is zero. Chebyshev implies P(T>=N)<=c/(Np^2), so P(T=infinity)=0.

For bounded nonnegative h, decompose h(Z_N) according to the last visit to alpha at times 0,...,N. Stationarity and the Markov property yield

    pi_tilde(h)
      = p sum_{k=0}^N E_alpha[h(Z_k); tau>k] + R_N,
    0<=R_N<=||h||_infinity P_{pi_tilde}(T>N).

At k=0, the initial alpha state is distributed as pi_tilde conditioned on alpha; future outgoing laws are independent of that state. Thus the displayed identity is valid even if h is not constant on the atom. Let N tend to infinity and then apply monotone convergence to h truncations. This proves the stationary cycle identity for every nonnegative measurable h:

    pi_tilde(h)=p E_alpha[sum_{k=0}^{tau-1} h(Z_k)].

Fix 1<s<b. Put h(z)=E_z[s^T], allowing infinity. It is measurable by the finite-horizon path construction and monotone convergence; h=1 on alpha. For k>=1 on {tau>k}, the Markov property identifies the conditional future moment with s^(tau-k). Consequently

    pi_tilde(h)
      =p (1+E_alpha[sum_{k=1}^{tau-1}s^(tau-k)])
      <=p E_alpha[tau s^tau] < infinity.

The last inequality uses sup_{j>=1} j(s/b)^j<infinity and E b^tau<infinity. This also explicitly explains why a slightly smaller exponential rate is convenient.

For the unsplit Q-chain let sigma=inf{j>=0:X_j in C} and H(x)=E_x[s^sigma]. Initialize the split flag conditionally on x. Every alpha visit is a C visit, so sigma<=T pathwise and

    pi(H)<=pi_tilde(h)<infinity.

This supplies exactly the hitting-time integrability required below. Neither finiteness of H at every state nor a full-F split construction has been asserted.

## D. Transfer to the original full total-variation norm

The original P-chain reaches C at the skeleton time S=m sigma. Its path law and sigma distribution on G equal those of the restricted chain. Choose

    max(r0,s^(-1/m)) < r1 < 1.

For n>=1 decompose at S. On {S<=n}, the strong Markov property and the original full-F bound on C give conditional full total-variation error at most K r0^(n-S), also when n-S=0 after replacing K by max(K,1). On {S>n} use the universal bound 1. This is justified for each A in F before taking its supremum, so no measurability assumption about the function x->d_n(x) is being made. Therefore, writing K1=max(K,1),

    d_n(x) <= P_x(S>n)+K1 E_x[r0^(n-S);S<=n]
           <= (1+K1) r1^n E_x[r1^(-S)]
           <= (1+K1) H(x) r1^n.

The last comparison follows from r1^(-m)<s. This estimate holds wherever H is finite.

The measurable set N={H=infinity} has pi(N)=0. Define

    M1(x)=(1+K1)H(x) for x outside N,
    M1(x)=M0(x) for x in N.

Then M1 is measurable and finite everywhere, pi(M1)<infinity, and the desired bound holds outside N by the stopped estimate and on N by the original bound and r0<r1. This is the required everywhere patch; replacing the claim by an almost-everywhere statement is unnecessary.

## Integration cautions

- The small set is a small set only for the restricted sigma-field; do not claim a full-space minorization.
- The full-space transfer uses the original finite measurable M0 and a G-measurable hitting time, so it controls all original measurable events.
- A naive integration of M0 is invalid. This proof never integrates it against pi. It integrates M0 only against an explicitly truncated minorizing probability.
- The scalar renewal analytic continuation at 1 needs the nonnegative-coefficient/Cauchy/Tonelli argument, not merely the statement that F is analytic near 1.
- The stationary occupation step proves its own almost-sure stationary accessibility using the variance calculation, avoiding a hidden split-Harris or skeleton-irreducibility premise.
- `lem-invariant-law-integrates-bounded-set-drift`, `lem-bivariate-drift-minorization-coupling-rate`, and the geometric-drift theorem are not needed for this shorter route.
