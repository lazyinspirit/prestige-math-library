## Statement

For a finite relay channel with relay-only causality, fix a finite law $P_{X_r}P_{X\mid X_r}$. Every positive rate
$$0<R<\min\{I(X;Y_r\mid X_r),\ I(X,X_r;Y)\}$$
is achievable, and rate $R=0$ is always achievable with a one-message code. Taking the closure of the union over such laws gives the decode-forward inner bound. Mutual informations use the relay channel law and nats per use.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite relay channel, a law $P_{X_r}P_{X|X_r}$, and either a positive rate strictly below both displayed mutual informations or the zero-rate case.

[F1] [[def-finite-discrete-memoryless-relay-channel-and-causal-code]]: the relay observes $Y_r$ and its input at time $i$ may depend on $Y_r^{i-1}$.

[F2] [[lem-finite-joint-iid-strong-typicality]]: IID blocks are strongly typical with probability tending to one.

[F3] [[lem-finite-conditional-typicality-packing]]: a wrong independent codeword passes the relevant conditional joint-typicality test with exponent given by the corresponding mutual information.

## Proof

1.1 Choose $\epsilon>0$ so that $R+\epsilon$ is below both mutual informations. For every block $b\in\{1,\ldots,B+1\}$, independently generate a relay codebook $x_{r,b}^n(w)$ with $w\in[\lceil e^{nR}\rceil]$, each word IID from $P_{X_r}$. Given each relay word, generate source words $x_b^n(m,w)$ independently according to $\prod_iP_{X|X_r}(x_i|x_{r,b,i})$. Set $w_0=w_{B+1}=1$ as fixed dummy indices and use $w_1,\ldots,w_B$ for the $B$ messages. In block $b$, the source sends $x_b^n(w_b,w_{b-1})$ and the relay sends $x_{r,b}^n(w_{b-1})$. This is causal under [F1]. [given, F1, choose]

1.2 At the end of block $b$, the relay tests candidate $w_b$ using $(x_{r,b}^n(w_{b-1}),x_b^n(w_b,w_{b-1}),y_r^n(b))$ and accepts the unique jointly typical candidate. The true triple has the IID law $P_{X_r}P_{X|X_r}W_{Y_r|X,X_r}$, so [F2] makes its test pass with probability tending to one. For a wrong candidate, its source word is conditionally independent of the relay observation given the known relay word; [F3] bounds a false test by $e^{-n(I(X;Y_r|X_r)-\epsilon)}$. There are at most $e^{nR}$ false indices, so the union bound tends to zero because $R+\epsilon<I(X;Y_r|X_r)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 The destination decodes backward over blocks $b=B+1,B,\ldots,2$. It knows $w_b$ from the next block (or as the fixed dummy $w_{B+1}$), and tests each candidate $w_{b-1}$ against the block-$b$ source and relay codewords and $y^n(b)$. The true tuple is IID with law $P_{X_r}P_{X|X_r}W_Y$ and passes by [F2]. For a wrong candidate, the pair of candidate codewords is independent of the output generated by the true pair; [F3] bounds a false test by $e^{-n(I(X,X_r;Y)-\epsilon)}$. The union bound over at most $e^{nR}$ wrong indices tends to zero because $R+\epsilon<I(X,X_r;Y)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

1.4 For positive $R$ there are $B$ message blocks and $B+1$ channel blocks, so the rate tends to $BR/(B+1)$ as $n\to\infty$. For fixed $B$, the ensemble-average error tends to zero by steps 1.2–1.3; then letting $B\to\infty$ approaches $R$. The strict slack permits the typicality tolerances to shrink with $n$, and closure gives the positive-rate part. [step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

1.5 At $R=0$, use a singleton message set and let the destination always return its sole element; the error probability is zero for every blocklength. Thus the zero-rate case is included even if one of the mutual informations vanishes. [given, algebra] ∎
