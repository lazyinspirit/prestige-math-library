## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Every non-braid-like Reidemeister move of type II
acting on one Seifert circle, and every non-braid-like Reidemeister move of type
III, is a composition of Reidemeister I moves, braid-like Reidemeister II and
III moves, Yamada-Vogel reducing moves, and their inverses. Hence any two
diagrams of the same link can be connected by a sequence of moves of the four
types I, braid-like II, braid-like III and reducing (and inverses).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, an oriented link diagram, its Seifert picture and coherence data ([[def-coherence-of-seifert-circles-and-the-height-of-a-diagram]], [[def-reducing-arc-and-yamada-vogel-reducing-move]]).

[F1] Coherence of two Seifert circles and the height of a diagram are defined through the annulus cobounded by the two circles; AC is consumed there ([[def-coherence-of-seifert-circles-and-the-height-of-a-diagram]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] A reducing move along an arc joining an incoherent pair replaces the pair by two coherent circles joined by two oppositely signed arcs, leaving all other circles unchanged ([[def-reducing-arc-and-yamada-vogel-reducing-move]]).

[F3] Reidemeister moves of type I are braid-like by definition; the moves of types II and III are classified by the Seifert circles their local picture meets, and braid-like means that the corresponding strands run monotonically about the axis of the nested-chain picture ([[def-oriented-reidemeister-moves]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **A non-braid-like R3.** In the local picture of a non-braid-like R3 the moving strand slides across a crossing of two strands that lie on the same side of the moving strand with respect to the axis. Replace the move by the following concatenation of four moves: first a non-braid-like R2 applied to the moving strand and one of the two crossing strands, creating two crossings of opposite signs; then a diagram isotopy sliding the new pair of crossings around the picture; then a braid-like R3 applied where the strands now run monotonically; and finally the non-braid-like R2 of the first step run backwards. The four local pictures compose to the original R3 because the two R2 moves cancel pairwise on the two sides of the diagram isotopy, and the intermediate picture has all strands monotone about the axis at the R3 site, so that move is braid-like. All sign and orientation variants are obtained by reflecting the picture and reversing the orientations, which preserves the composition and turns each R2 into its opposite sign variant. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 **A non-braid-like R2 on one Seifert circle.** Suppose the two strands of the R2 picture lie on arcs of the same Seifert circle, so the move acts on one circle. Replace the R2 by the composition of two R1 moves, one on each strand, followed by a reducing move and its inverse: the two R1 moves create two new Seifert circles (one for each strand) so that the two arcs now lie on distinct Seifert circles, the reducing move along an arc joining them is available precisely because the two new circles are incoherent, and the inverse reducing move restores the original circle with the two crossings of the R2. The intermediate picture has the two strands in distinct circles, and the R2 in that picture is the defining local picture of a Yamada-Vogel reducing move, so the composition realizes the original move. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 **A non-braid-like R2 on two distinct circles.** When the two arcs of the R2 picture lie on two distinct Seifert circles, the local band slide is already the local picture of a reducing move up to orientation: if the circles are incoherent the move is a reducing move as defined, and if they are coherent the same picture with the opposite choice of slide reverses the signs of the two crossings, so the move is the inverse of a reducing move. Either way the move belongs to the stated generating set. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 **Conclusion.** Steps 1.1-1.3 show that every non-braid-like R3 and every non-braid-like R2 is generated by the four allowed move types, while type I moves are braid-like and braid-like II and III moves are allowed by definition. Since by the Reidemeister equivalence theorem any two diagrams of the same oriented link are connected by the oriented moves R1, R2, R3, replacing each non-braid-like R2 and R3 by the compositions above and keeping the remaining moves gives the required sequence through the four move types. AC is inherited exactly from [F1]. ∎ [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3]
