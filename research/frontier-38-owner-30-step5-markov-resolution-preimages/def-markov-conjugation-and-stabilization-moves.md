## Definition

Let $(B_n)_{n\ge0}$ be the braid groups of the Artin presentation
([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]), with generators
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ and the two Artin relations, and let

$$\iota_n\colon B_n\longrightarrow B_{n+1},\qquad \iota_n(\sigma_i):=\sigma_i\quad(1\le i\le n-1),$$

be the **standard inclusion homomorphism**. It is well defined: the defining
relators of $B_n$ are among the defining relators of $B_{n+1}$ under the
assignment $\sigma_i\mapsto\sigma_i$, so von Dyck's theorem extends the
assignment to a homomorphism. Its image is generated by
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$, and we write $\iota_n(\beta)$ for it; whether
this homomorphism is injective is not needed anywhere on this page, and no
subgroup identification is made. Under the identification of the last generator $\sigma_n$ with the
elementary geometric half twist of [[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]],
which is licensed by the choice-free surjectivity of the Artin presentation
onto the geometric braid group
([[prop-the-artin-presentation-surjects-onto-geometric-braids]]), the added
strand is inserted **on the right**: the first $n$ strands are untouched and
the new strand number $n+1$ carries the half twist $\sigma_n$.

On the disjoint union $\bigsqcup_{n\ge0}B_n$ define two kinds of elementary
**Markov moves**.

**Conjugation.** For $\beta,\gamma\in B_n$, replace $\beta$ by
$\gamma\beta\gamma^{-1}$, an element of the same group $B_n$. The number of
strands does not change. When $\beta$ is a geometric braid and $\gamma$ the
class of a braid in $G_n$, this is realized on closures by reading $\gamma$
around the axis, as the closure-preservation lemma below shows.

**Stabilization and destabilization.** For $n\ge1$ and $\beta\in B_n$ the **positive
stabilization** is $\beta\sigma_n:=\iota_n(\beta)\cdot\sigma_n\in B_{n+1}$ and
the **negative stabilization** is
$\beta\sigma_n^{-1}:=\iota_n(\beta)\cdot\sigma_n^{-1}\in B_{n+1}$; the inverse
operations, from $B_{n+1}$ to $B_n$, are called **destabilizations**. Both
signs occur, and the sign is the sign of the exponent of the new generator. By
[[prop-the-artin-presentation-surjects-onto-geometric-braids]] and
[[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]] the product $\beta\sigma_n^{\pm1}$ is
the class of a geometric braid on $n+1$ strands obtained from any geometric
representative of $\beta$ by adjoining one half twist of the last two strands
in its fixed product position, glued to the trivial motion of the first $n-1$ strands. Inserting the generator inside a factorization need not produce a conjugate of that fixed stabilization. It nevertheless gives a Markov sequence: if $\beta=uv$, old conjugation by $v$ gives $vu$, its right stabilization is $(vu)\sigma_n^{\pm1}$, and conjugation by $v^{-1}$ gives $u\sigma_n^{\pm1}v$. Only moving a factor past the whole word is cyclic conjugacy. At $n=0$ no $\sigma_0$ exists, so the empty braid has only conjugation moves and is an isolated Markov class.

**Markov equivalence.** Two braids $\beta,\beta'$ (possibly with different
numbers of strands) are **Markov equivalent** when they can be connected by a
finite sequence of moves each of which is a conjugation, a positive or negative
stabilization, or a destabilization. Markov equivalence is an equivalence
relation on $\bigsqcup_n B_n$: it is reflexive, symmetric by allowing inverses
of the listed moves, and transitive by concatenating sequences.

**Convention on orientation and side.** All braids are read with the product
and side conventions fixed on the pages `geometric-braids-and-artin-generators`
and `artin-presentation-completeness-and-braid-combing`: generators multiply
left to right in the order of stacking, and stabilization always adds the new
strand on the right. The definition is choice-free: no choice principle is used
in the Artin presentation, in the inclusion, or in the identification of
$\sigma_n$ with the elementary geometric half twist.
