- Projected subgradient is finite-budget only. The local proof gives the projected one-step inequality, the exact best/weighted-average bound, and the fixed-step `R/(G sqrt(N))` schedule. The zero `R` and zero `G` cases are handled separately.
- The proximal bundle algorithm is full-memory and specifies the model, predicted decrease, serious/null tests, failures, and finite-budget behavior. The convergence theorem is local and conditional on compact initial sublevel, continuity, positive predicted decreases, the exact acceptance rule, and `e_k = F(z_k)-M_k(z_k) -> 0`. It does not infer this accuracy condition from accumulating cuts.
- Direct search uses a finite positive-spanning list and the plan’s forcing condition. Its theorem proves stationarity only for accumulation points of an unsuccessful-poll subsequence whose mesh tends to zero and whose centers lie in a compact subset of an open smoothness neighborhood. It does not claim every run creates that subsequence or that a finite budget certifies stationarity.
- Nelder–Mead has explicit branch comparisons, slot tie-breaking, budget accounting, diameter and spread tests, and distinct failure statuses. The plan requires both diameter and spread tolerances. Conn–Scheinberg–Vicente describes diameter-only stopping as an available variant; this draft follows the controlling two-tolerance contract. Its expansion tie is accepted in Algorithm 8.1 (`f_e <= f_r`); the controlling OPT10 contract requires strict `f_e < f_r`, so a tie reflects here. No general convergence claim is made.
- Conn/Kolda global stationarity statements require their own globalization and mesh assumptions. The direct-search result here states only the conditional subsequence claim above.
