## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, the rank-inequality relation on the zero-based $S_n$ of [[def-bruhat-order-on-the-symmetric-group]], the inversion length on the one-based realization of [[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]], and the strong Bruhat order on a finite Weyl group of [[def-bruhat-order-on-a-finite-weyl-group]].

[F1] For $x\in S_n$ and $0\le p,q<n$, the rank number is $r_x(p,q)=|\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}:i\le p,\ x(i)\le q\}|$; $x\le y$ means $r_x(p,q)\ge r_y(p,q)$ for every $p,q$ ([[def-bruhat-order-on-the-symmetric-group]]).

[F2] The shift $i\mapsto i+1$ identifies the zero-based and one-based permutation groups; adjacent transpositions generate $S_n$ and $\ell$ is inversion length ([[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]], [[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[F3] Strong Bruhat order on a finite Weyl group is the transitive closure of length-increasing reflection covers, and is equivalent to the reduced-subword condition for every fixed reduced expression ([[def-bruhat-order-on-a-finite-weyl-group]]).

[F4] A finite root system acts through its root reflections, and its Weyl group is generated by those reflections ([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]]).

## Statement

Let $\le$ be the rank-inequality order on the zero-based $S_n$ of [[def-bruhat-order-on-the-symmetric-group]], and identify it with the one-based realization by shifting inputs and values by $1$. (a) This order is the strong Bruhat order of type A: the transitive closure of covers $x\to tx$ where $t$ is a transposition and $\ell(tx)=\ell(x)+1$. (b) If $y\le w$ then $\ell(y)\le\ell(w)$, with equality iff $y=w$; every saturated chain has $\ell(w)-\ell(y)$ steps; and $y\le w$ iff $y^{-1}\le w^{-1}$. (c) $y\le w$ iff for every reduced expression $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$, $y$ is a reduced subword. (d) For every simple reflection $s$, if $y\le w$ and $\ell(sy)>\ell(y)$, then $y\le sw$; if $y\le w$ and $\ell(sw)<\ell(w)$, then $sy\le w$. (e) Every interval $[y,w]$ is finite and $[y,w]=\{y\}\cup\bigcup_{y\to a\le w}[a,w]$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct comparison of the rank matrices, followed by induction on the sum of lengths.

1.1 **Conventions and length.** Let $\bar x$ be the conjugate of the zero-based permutation $x$ under $i\mapsto i+1$, and put $R_x(m,h):=r_x(m-1,h-1)$ for $1\le m,h\le n$, with $R_x(0,h)=R_x(m,0)=0$. Then $R_x(m,h)=|\{i\le m:\bar x(i)\le h\}|$. For $n\ge2$, in the type-$A_{n-1}$ root realization on $E=\{(a_1,\ldots,a_n):\sum a_i=0\}$ the roots are $e_a-e_b$ $(a\ne b)$. Choose the positive roots $e_a-e_b$ for $a<b$; their simple roots are $e_i-e_{i+1}$. Each root has squared length $2$, all root pairings are integers, and coordinate swaps preserve the root set, so this is a finite reduced crystallographic root system. Its simple reflections swap adjacent coordinates, its root reflections are all coordinate transpositions, and its Weyl group is $S_n$. For $n=1$ the root system is empty and the group is trivial. Swapping adjacent entries changes inversion count by one; repeatedly swapping an adjacent descent reduces any nonidentity permutation to the identity, so Coxeter length is the inversion length $\ell$. A nonidentity permutation has a left simple descent because its inverse list is not increasing. Thus the strong order in [F3], transported by the shift, has covers $x\to tx$ with $t$ a transposition and $\ell(tx)=\ell(x)+1$; left multiplication by a simple $s_i$ raises or lowers $\ell$ by one. [F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 **A strong cover decreases every rank number.** Let $z=tx$ be a cover, with $t=(a\ b)$ and $a<b$. Write $p_x(c):=\bar x^{-1}(c)$. If $p_x(a)<p_x(b)$, swapping the values creates their inversion and changes each intermediate value $c$ with $a<c<b$ and $p_x(a)<p_x(c)<p_x(b)$ by two more inversions in the same direction; if $e$ is the number of such values, the total length change is $1+2e>0$. Reversing the positions gives the negative change, so $\ell(z)=\ell(x)+1$ implies $p_x(a)<p_x(b)$. For thresholds $h<a$ or $h\ge b$, swapping $a,b$ leaves $R_x(m,h)$ unchanged. For $a\le h<b$, a prefix changes only when $p_x(a)\le m<p_x(b)$; there it contains $a$ for $x$ and $b$ for $z$, so $R_x(m,h)=R_z(m,h)+1$, while outside that range the counts agree. Hence $R_x(m,h)\ge R_z(m,h)$ for every $m,h$, and every strong-order chain is rank-inequality increasing. The rank inequalities are a partial order: reflexivity and transitivity are immediate, and the differences $R_x(m,h)-R_x(m-1,h)$ for all $h$ determine each value $\bar x(m)$, so equal rank matrices imply equal permutations. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Rank lifting.** Write $p_x(i)=\bar x^{-1}(i)$. Left multiplication by $s_i$ changes no $R_x(m,h)$ for $h\ne i$, and at $h=i$ it adds $1$ on $p_x(i+1)\le m<p_x(i)$, subtracts $1$ on $p_x(i)\le m<p_x(i+1)$, and is unchanged otherwise. Suppose $u\le v$ in rank order and $s_iv<v$, so $p_v(i+1)\le m<p_v(i)$ is the window on which $R_{s_iv}(m,i)=R_v(m,i)+1$. If also $s_iu>u$, then $p_u(i)<p_u(i+1)$. For a prefix in that window, $R_v(m,i)=R_v(m,i-1)$ and $R_v(m,i+1)=R_v(m,i-1)+1$. If $R_u(m,i)=R_v(m,i)$, the inequalities at thresholds $i-1$ and $i$ force $R_u(m,i-1)=R_v(m,i-1)$ and $p_u(i)>m$; the ascent gives $p_u(i+1)>m$, contradicting $R_u(m,i+1)\ge R_v(m,i+1)$. Therefore $R_u(m,i)\ge R_v(m,i)+1=R_{s_iv}(m,i)$, proving $u\le s_iv$. Also, whenever $s_iu<u$, its left multiplication only adds $1$ to rank numbers at threshold $i$, so $s_iu\le u\le v$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Rank order is strong Bruhat order.** Induct on $\ell(u)+\ell(v)$ for $u\le v$ in rank order. If $v$ is the identity, then $R_v(m,h)=\min(m,h)$ is the largest possible prefix count, so $u=v$. Otherwise choose a simple left descent $s_i v<v$. If $s_i u>u$, step 2.2 gives $u\le s_i v$ in rank order; induction gives a strong chain $u\le s_i v$, and the cover $s_i v\to v$ completes it. If $s_i u<u$, step 2.2 gives $s_i u\le v$ in rank order; induction gives a strong chain $s_i u\le v$, and the cover $s_i u\to u$ completes it. Thus rank order is contained in strong Bruhat order. Combined with step 2.1, the orders agree, proving (a). [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, F3, algebra]

4.1 **Length, inversion symmetry, subwords, and lifting.** Every strong cover raises $\ell$ by one, so if $y\le w$ then $\ell(y)\le\ell(w)$, equality holds exactly when $y=w$, and every saturated chain has $\ell(w)-\ell(y)$ steps. Moreover $R_{x^{-1}}(h,m)=R_x(m,h)$, so $y\le w$ iff $y^{-1}\le w^{-1}$; this proves (b). Part (c) follows from the reduced-subword characterization in [F3] and the order identification in step 3.1, transported through the index shift. For the first lifting implication in (d), if $sw>w$ then $y\le w<sw$; if $sw<w$, choose a reduced expression for $w$ beginning with $s$. A reduced subword for $y$ cannot use that first letter when $sy>y$, since then its product would have left descent $s$; hence it is a subword for $sw$ and $y\le sw$. For the second implication, if $sy<y$ then $sy\le y\le w$; if $sy>y$, the same reduced-subword argument makes a reduced subword for $sy$ by prefixing $s$ to the subword for $y$, so $sy\le w$. This proves (d). [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **Intervals.** The group $S_n$ is finite, so each interval is finite. If $z\in[y,w]$ and $z\ne y$, a saturated chain from $y$ to $z$ has a first cover $y\to a$ with $a\le z\le w$, whence $z\in[a,w]$. Conversely, for every cover $y\to a\le w$, transitivity gives $[a,w]\subseteq[y,w]$. The point $y$ is not in any such upper interval, so $[y,w]=\{y\}\cup\bigcup_{y\to a\le w}[a,w]$. This proves (e), including $y=w$, when the union is empty. The arguments use only finite permutations and finite chains; no choice principle is needed. [F1, F3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
