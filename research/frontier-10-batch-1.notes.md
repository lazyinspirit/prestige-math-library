## Outcome and page summaries

No split is proposed. The two A pages contain 13 and 32 authored items, both below the hard 60-item ceiling. Their B companions contain 7 authored items each. All 59 manifest items were written in the settled order; nothing was dropped, renamed, merged, or removed to meet a size target.

`symmetric-groups-and-the-sign-homomorphism` begins from the already-published symmetric group and obtains cycle structure from the orbit partition for the cyclic subgroup generated by a permutation. It develops support, disjoint cycles, the unique disjoint-cycle decomposition, the order read from cycle lengths, and generation by transpositions. The identity and the cases `n=0,1` are part of the statements rather than suppressed conventions.

The second half uses inversion number, not a Vandermonde polynomial, to prove that a transposition reverses inversion sign and that every transposition factorisation has the same parity. It then proves that sign is a homomorphism, is onto `{+1,-1}` exactly for `n>=2`, reads sign from cycle structure, defines `A_n`, proves normality and the correct small-`n` count, and proves uniqueness of the nontrivial homomorphism to the two-element sign group. This is the determinant-facing spine requested in the dispatch.

`matrices-and-the-matrix-of-a-linear-map` first adds multiplication to the published matrix space and proves the full matrix algebra, including zero-sized shapes, the matrix ring, units, matrix units, noncommutativity, and transpose. Because the published linear-map page is thin, this page also builds the missing machinery it actually needs: the vector space of linear maps, linear isomorphisms, identity and composition of linear maps, coordinate columns, and matrix representation.

The representation half proves coordinate action, the vector-space and ring isomorphisms, dimensions, invertibility, `GL_n(F)`, change-of-basis matrices, the two-sided basis-change formula for a rectangular map, similarity and its exact operator interpretation, trace, cyclicity of trace, and basis-independent operator trace. The companion fixes one operator while changing its bases in a numbered example, as the owner requested.

## Convention decisions

1. The library's finite set `n={0,...,n-1}` replaces the sources' `{1,...,n}`. Every matrix and permutation index therefore starts at zero.
2. Function composition follows the published convention: the right-hand factor acts first. Some sources reverse the order when cycles act on the right; their displayed products must not be copied mechanically.
3. One-cycles are omitted from a disjoint-cycle product but counted in `c(sigma)` in the formula `sgn(sigma)=(-1)^(n-c(sigma))`.
4. Milne constructs sign through an alternating rational expression; the prose scaffold recommends and this batch uses the inversion-count route, avoiding a forward dependency on multivariable polynomial rings.
5. Several sources call sign onto `{+1,-1}` without discussing the trivial degrees. The authored theorem states surjectivity exactly for `n>=2`; for `n=0,1` its image is `{1}`.
6. Matrix sources use 1-based entries and various forms of `F^{m,n}`. The library keeps the published `M_{m times n}(F)` function-space model and its genuine zero-sized shapes.
7. Change-of-basis notation is fixed as `P_{C<-B}=[id_V]_B^C`, the matrix sending B-coordinate columns to C-coordinate columns. With that convention the rectangular formula is `[T]_{B'}^{C'}=P_{C'<-C}[T]_B^C P_{B<-B'}`. Sources using the inverse arrow or defining `P` in the opposite direction display the conjugation in the reverse order.
8. Similarity is fixed as `B=P^{-1}AP`. A source writing `B=PAP^{-1}` is using the inverse transition matrix, not asserting a different relation.
9. All matrix algebra and trace results are stated over an arbitrary field. Commutativity of scalar multiplication is spent explicitly in transpose-of-product and trace-cyclicity calculations.
10. The library's unital-ring convention allows the one-element ring, so `M_0(F)` is a ring and `GL_0(F)` is the trivial group. This boundary is retained throughout.

## Proof and boundary obligations discharged at Step 5

The complete 45-item machine map is `research/frontier-10-batch-1.proof-contracts.json`. The highest-risk obligations are:

1. The cycle-decomposition proof must identify cycle supports with intrinsic cyclic-subgroup orbits, prove uniqueness rather than only existence, and handle the identity as the empty product.
2. The inversion proof must show an arbitrary transposition flips sign, not merely an adjacent transposition. The adjacent-swap word has odd length `2(b-a)-1`.
3. The homomorphism proof must concatenate factors in the library's composition order and must not claim surjectivity for `n=0,1`.
4. The `A_4` no-order-six proof may use Lagrange and index-two normality, but not Cauchy's theorem from another in-flight batch. Its counting/conjugation route is closed with published dependencies.
5. Matrix associativity must show the double-sum interchange over an arbitrary field and treat a zero intermediate dimension as an empty sum.
6. Matrix representation must prove surjectivity by constructing a map from arbitrary columns and must separately close `m=0` and `n=0`.
7. The two-sided change formula must type-check every shape; the right transition changes domain coordinates and the left transition changes codomain coordinates.
8. The reverse direction of the similarity/operator characterisation must prove that the columns of an invertible `P` form a basis before using them.
9. `tr(AB)=tr(BA)` uses commutativity in the scalar field. It is stated for rectangular factors and must check both zero dimensions.
10. In the square-zero example, `d_i^2=0` implies `d_i=0` because the scalars lie in a field; this conclusion would be false over a general ring with nilpotents.

No finite-smoke entry is selected because the repository's available bounded smoke types do not cover permutations or matrices. The finite B-page witnesses provide independent hand-checkable tables but are not represented as unsupported smoke-test types.
