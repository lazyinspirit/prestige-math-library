# Circle-spine descent: actual local PL operations

This is the one mathematical continuation of the original capture assembly, not a theorem acceptance packet. The single-annulus argument is in Range's original-proof-working.md. Its simultaneous torus trace and finite circle-spine descent remain to be assembled. In particular no original rank-two certificate is inferred from an arbitrary auxiliary frontier torus.

## A direct PL solid-torus alternative

Let V be a supplied parametrized PL solid torus. Let F be a connected TWO-SIDED properly embedded orientable PL surface, with no sphere component, with no compressing disk, and with nontrivial boundary circles on ∂V. The two-sided hypothesis is essential: the one-sided Möbius-band case is not asserted here.

First suppose F has no boundary-compressing disk. Normalize its boundary to parallel affine circles on the product boundary torus, and make a product meridian disk E transverse. All circle intersections disappear: an innermost circle on E bounds a disk on F by incompressibility; their sphere bounds a PL ball inside V, since V has its standard embedding in a parametrized S³ and the connected exterior of V is on the other side. The remainder of F stays outside the ball, because it reaches ∂V. The disk exchange is consequently an actual relative isotopy, deleting all the intersections on the replaced disk and creating none near the clean E disk. Choose a minimum number of arcs after these moves. An outermost E arc cuts off a disk with other edge on ∂E. If its F edge is essential, it is a forbidden boundary compression. Otherwise its F edge cuts a disk off F, so its endpoints lie on one boundary circle of F. If that circle has zero intersection with the meridian there were no endpoints to begin with. Otherwise all crossings of that affine circle with the affine meridian have the same sign. The interval of ∂E between consecutive crossings of this one circle therefore approaches opposite normal sides of F at its two ends, whereas the outermost disk lies on one coherent side of its F edge. This is impossible. Thus F misses E. It lies in the product ball V cut at E. The PL Loop criterion makes its fundamental group trivial, so F is a disk; its nontrivial torus boundary makes it a meridian disk. The finite proper-disk-in-ball parameterization identifies it with a standard meridian.

If F does have a boundary compression, its boundary edge lies in one annulus between adjacent parallel boundary curves. A same-circle edge is homotopic in that annulus into the rim, so injection of F in V would make the F edge boundary-parallel, contrary to the definition of boundary compression. Hence its endpoints lie on two distinct adjacent boundary circles. A neighborhood in F of those two circles and the compression edge is a pair of pants. The boundary annulus between the two circles, with the two parallel copies of the compression disk, caps its third rim in V. That rim therefore bounds a disk in F, by incompressibility. This disk is on the side away from the pair of pants (the other side contains actual boundary circles of F). Connectedness now says that F is precisely this pair of pants capped by that disk, hence an annulus. The same two disk collars and the interior sphere-ball exchange exhibit its product with the boundary annulus. This proves the boundary-parallel-annulus alternative without importing a smooth isotopy.

For a finite disjoint family, the corresponding disk and annulus moves can be performed simultaneously. Parallel meridian disks cut V into a finite cyclic chain of parametrized balls; the parameters arise by comparing one disk with the standard product meridian, using innermost circle exchanges and disk-window gluing. Primitive longitudinal boundary-parallel annuli are ordered in their product collars. Choose an innermost collar first; any other family component inside it has the same boundary type and is normalized in its smaller product collar before that collar is moved. At a cut disk the two endpoint orders are the actual linear orders of disjoint arcs, so the maps agree on the shared windows. Boundary disk maps extend over the cut balls by the supplied finite PL window theorem. This gives relative PL normalizations fixed on smaller already-normal collars.

## Verticality in an explicitly supplied PL circle-fibered model

Take a compact connected orientable irreducible PL circle-fibered three-manifold N with nonempty boundary, presented by finitely many polygon×circle bundle charts and the finite cyclic fibered-solid-torus models. Let A be a finite disjoint family of connected two-sided essential PL annuli or tori. The following construction is intrinsic to these models; it does not smooth the inclusion N→E.

Normalize each boundary circle as a vertical fiber or an affine transverse circle on its torus. Use finite PL general position at the exceptional fiber cores, and include one regular core when there are no exceptional ones. Near each core, a transverse surface has finitely many meridian disk graphs. Shrink the supplied cyclic model, and straighten the graphs in its product-sector coordinates. The graphs are disjoint, so their core-coordinate order gives compatible finite PL prism maps. Extend through a larger model collar. Remove these small core tori. Choose disjoint proper base arcs cutting the remaining base to one polygon. Their inverse images are annuli, and cutting at them gives a supplied product solid torus. The nonorientable base case uses the actual reflected side identifications; no orientation of the base is asserted.

Minimize first the number of surface/core intersections, then the number of intersections with the cut annuli. A circle inessential on a cut annulus bounds a clean disk there; incompressibility gives its disk on the surface. Irreducibility and the infinite/nontrivial surface group exclude the side containing the whole closed component, while a proper component reaches ∂N. Thus the finite disk-ball exchange removes the circle. An outermost returning cut-annulus arc has its other shore on a fiber. On an original boundary torus the same-side/constant-crossing-sign argument above contradicts either essentiality or the affine boundary normalization. On a removed core torus its ends lie on two distinct meridian disk graphs. The interval of the boundary fiber between them has strictly monotone core coordinate of length less than one core period. Its radial ruling in the supplied cyclic model is therefore an embedded strip, even when the fiber winds meridionally. Joining this strip to the outermost disk gives a bigon with the exceptional core and cancels two core intersections. This contradicts the first minimum. Consequently every remaining cut-annulus arc spans, and every remaining circle is an essential fiber circle. They cannot coexist on one cut annulus.

The cut surface in the product solid torus is still incompressible. A supposed compressing disk has its capping disk on the original surface. If the cap leaves the cut piece, the same sphere-ball exchange replaces it by the compressing disk, deleting core intersections or, when there are none in that cap, cut-annulus intersections. The rest of the original surface is outside the ball for exactly the preceding boundary/infinite-group reason. This contradicts the lexicographic minimum. This is an assertion about the actual embedded cut surface, not an unrestricted immersed replacement.

A closed cut component would be an entire original torus lying in the product solid torus. The PL Loop criterion would inject its Z² into the solid torus Z, which is impossible; hence there is no such component. Apply the proved PL solid-torus alternative to each boundary-bearing cut component. A boundary-parallel annulus with transverse boundary would have a boundary compression on a vertical fiber interval away from the cut-face collars. Choose its compression disk outermost among intersections with the other components; its clean shore again gives either a forbidden original boundary compression or the exceptional-core bigon just excluded. Thus such annuli do not occur. The remaining pieces are meridian disks with transverse boundary or primitive longitudinal annuli with vertical boundary. A vertical boundary circle and a transverse boundary circle have nonzero torus intersection, so these two types cannot coexist in one product solid torus. Use the simultaneous finite disk/annulus normalization above, relative to the already normal cut-face and core collars. The maps glue over those collars. It follows that each original component is vertical or everywhere transverse to the fibers.

For a transverse component with n sheets, the finite lifted base cell count gives χ(A)=nχ(B)−Σ(n−n/q_i): ordinary cells lift n times and each cone vertex lifts n/q_i times. A closed transverse torus is impossible when B has boundary, since its local covering would have nonempty boundary over ∂B. A transverse annulus has zero orbifold Euler characteristic. The finite surface classification and positive terms 1−1/q_i leave precisely annulus or Möbius base without cone points, or a disk with two order-two cones.

Cut the original circle fibers at the transverse annulus. Their segments give actual interval-bundle charts, including the finite cyclic exceptional charts. If the cut is connected, its two annulus ends lie in different horizontal components, so its endpoint cover is disconnected and the bundle is A×I. Regluing gives a return g which is a deck map of A→B. Following an oriented fiber goes successively through all n intersection points, so g has actual order n. Orientability of N forces g to preserve the orientation of A, hence the base is orientable: annulus or disk-two-order-two. For the annulus, its standard strip covering has group Z and the subgroup nZ; lift the base polygon cells to conjugate g by a PL covering isomorphism to a rotation of the core-circle coordinate. For the disk-two-order-two case, unfold the finite base rectangle to the strip with half-turns a(x,u)=(−x,−u), b(x,u)=(2−x,−u). The annular covering subgroup contains no half-turn (which has a fixed point); it is therefore a translation subgroup generated by t^k, where t=ab. Its quotient is dihedral of order 2k, but this quotient is the CYCLIC group generated by the actual return g. Reduced words imply k=1: k>2 is nonabelian, while k=2 is the noncyclic four group. Thus this cover is the double annulus cover with g(θ,u)=(−θ,−u). The PL covering isomorphism follows by lifting the disk cut along its two branch arcs and gluing its two sheets; in the order-two local charts it extends by their given polygonal quadratic model. Both explicit return maps preserve the annulus core-circle foliation. Descend that foliation through A×I and its return to obtain the new circle fibration, in which the given annulus is vertical.

If the cut separates, each of its two components has just one horizontal copy of A. Each is an interval bundle with connected endpoint double cover A, so it is the mapping cylinder of a FREE involution τ_i on A. The involutions are orientation reversing by orientability of the total space, and both are deck maps of A→B. Their generated group is TRANSITIVE on the intersection points of any one original circle fiber: traversing that connected circle alternates the two cut components and therefore successively applies τ_1 and τ_2. Its quotient is consequently the entire original base B, not a further covering of that base. Their group acts faithfully on a finite regular covering fiber: a deck map fixing one regular point is identity by path lifting, including at the finitely many cone charts by continuity. The group is consequently finite and has the nonorientable base B as its quotient. The only base from the zero-Euler list is the Möbius band, whose strip group is Z. Hence this deck group is cyclic; generated by two involutions it has order two, so τ_1=τ_2. Lifting the usual Möbius strip cell model conjugates them SIMULTANEOUSLY to τ(θ,u)=(θ+1/2,−u). Each mapping cylinder therefore has the explicit model (A×[−1,1])/((θ,u,v)~(θ+1/2,−u,−v)). Translation in θ gives its circle fibration, with one multiplicity-two core, and the two fibrations agree on their common annulus A. Glue them there. All parameters are finite polygon/product-cell parameters. This is the alternate circle fibration of the orientable twisted Klein-bottle interval bundle and makes the original A vertical. There is no assumed arbitrary periodic representative: the only finite maps used are the actual deck maps furnished by the cut-fiber interval bundles.

The last paragraph supplies local PL verticality/refibering only. It does NOT yet supply a maximal-family theorem, mixed I/circle engulfing, a compatible smooth parameterization, or simultaneous descent retaining the original torus map. Those operations cannot be replaced by the statement that a frontier torus is essential in an auxiliary carrier.

## A finite embedded-atoroidal torus cutting, before any map capture

Here is the existence operation available in the PL category. Let M be any compact connected orientable irreducible finitely triangulated PL three-manifold with NONEMPTY boundary. Its boundary need not be toric or incompressible. There is a finite disjoint family of injected two-sided tori whose cut components are all embedded-atoroidal. This is not yet uniqueness or characteristic engulfing.

Start with no tori. If a component is not embedded-atoroidal, select an incompressible torus there which is not boundary-parallel. The PL Loop criterion converts this to injection on fundamental groups; the finite injective-surface cutting theorem injects each cut-piece group into M and preserves irreducibility. Thus every selected torus injects in the ORIGINAL M. Every cut component has nonempty boundary: a component with none would have neither an original boundary face nor a cut face and would be a closed component of connected M. At no first step can a T²×I cut component appear. Such a product must have one end a copy of the new torus. If its other end is an older boundary face, the product makes the new torus boundary-parallel in the old component, contrary to its selection. If both ends are the two copies of the new torus, restoring that cut makes the whole old component a CLOSED torus bundle, contradicting its nonempty boundary. Initial M=T²×I is already atoroidal and needs no cut.

Choose the original triangulation once, with t tetrahedra, and put b=dim H₂(M,∂M;F₂). A finite accumulated torus family has simultaneous normal position by the CLOSED-surface specialization of the actual finite normal-isotopy proof, not by its stronger Statement requiring every proper ambient disk to be boundary-parallel. Keep the original triangulation T fixed. Put this closed family in general position entirely in Int M and count its finite edge weight w. The tetrahedron capping steps use only interior surgery disks; injection gives their surface disks, and irreducibility gives the empty interior balls. The positive-genus closed remainder cannot lie in such a ball, by its essential loop. Returning-edge bigons only occur on interior edges, since the closed family misses every boundary edge. Their interior isotopies strictly decrease w; recapping never increases it. Finite iteration therefore gives the triangle/quadrilateral normal disks for this same T. No boundary surgery, boundary bigon or boundary-disk ball is used, so no boundary-compressibility restriction on M has been added. This ambient isotopy preserves the absence of product cut components. The accepted normal-family prism bound says that more than 10t+b components give two distinct adjacent components with an actual intervening product. That would be a forbidden T²×I cut component. Hence at most 10t+b selections can be made. If the current cutting were still nonatoroidal then one further selection would contradict this bound. A bounded finite induction therefore stops with all cut pieces embedded-atoroidal.

This operation has not used an injected torus MAP, preserved a particular source word through a noninjective auxiliary carrier, or selected circle fibrations on the atoroidal pieces. It establishes the finite embedded cutting needed before those separate steps. To obtain a maximal CIRCLE-family one must still identify the circle-fibered pieces, merge the matching ones by actual finite fiber parameters, and prove the corresponding frontier/spectacle engulfing. In particular an atoroidal cut piece is not thereby asserted to have map-level torus capture.

## The embedded annulus operation with only its incident boundary required toric

Let M be compact connected orientable irreducible finite PL and embedded-atoroidal. Suppose a two-sided essential proper annulus A has both boundary circles on TORUS components of ∂M; no condition is imposed on its other boundary components. Then M is a PL circle-fibered model, hence all its boundary is toric. This local statement concerns an embedded annulus, not an arbitrary annulus map.

Take collars of the one or two incident boundary tori and a product band neighborhood of A. Normalize the two simple boundary circles by the finite torus curve parameters. The neighborhood N is a circle bundle over a pair of pants, or over a punctured Möbius band, with at most two internal frontier tori. Its frontier groups inject in N: the base boundary loops have infinite order in the free rank-two base group, and the fiber is infinite by the explicit product/reflection model. If a frontier compresses in M, choose a compressing disk clean of all frontiers, by the innermost-circle disk replacements. It cannot lie inside N by that injection. Compressing outward gives a sphere. Its irreducibility ball is outside N, since N contains an original boundary torus whereas the ball is in the interior of M. Reversing the compression therefore attaches a one-handle to this ball along two standard disk windows, giving an EXTERIOR parametrized solid torus. Adjoin it.

Its meridian cannot be the attaching fiber: a meridian disk joined to the vertical annulus over a base arc from this frontier to A would compress A. Thus its primitive attaching fiber is pμ+qλ with q≠0. The polygonal cyclic solid-torus model extends the circle fibration across it; this caps the base boundary with at most one cone point. The only intermediate case with another frontier is an annulus base with one possible cone. Its group has central fiber h and quotient Z*C_q; the two boundary base words have infinite order. Also h has infinite order by the presentation map h↦1, c↦−p/q into the additive rationals. Therefore those frontier groups still inject, and the operation can be repeated. Each filling removes a frontier, so there are at most two.

All remaining frontiers are incompressible and hence boundary-parallel in M. Let T²×I be the product to its original boundary torus. If the collar is on the exterior side of N, absorb it using its product fiber coordinate. If N is on the collar side, it is contained in that entire product component. That product has just one original boundary torus, so this is already impossible when N contains two original boundary tori. With two frontier tori and one original boundary, N is the original pair-of-pants circle bundle; its other frontier T′ injects in the product. Cutting the product along T′ injects the cut-piece groups, so N would inject in Z², contradicting its nonabelian free base quotient.

For completeness that product-cut assertion is intrinsic PL. The supplied universal cover of T²×I is the Euclidean slab R²×I. Any PL sphere lifts; its bounded Schoenflies ball stays in the slab, since neither connected outside half-space can cross the sphere. Distinct deck translates of this ball are disjoint or nested; nesting is impossible by equality of their finite Euclidean volume. It therefore projects to a PL ball. This proves product irreducibility without a smoothing bridge, and the accepted injective PL surface-cut theorem gives the cut-group assertion. The frontier T′ injects in the product because its composite into M injects.

Finally, with one frontier and one original torus, containment on the collar side makes N equal that product. The punctured-Möbius base is excluded by its nonabelian free quotient. In the annulus-with-one-cone case, Z*C_q must be abelian, so q=1. The base is an ordinary annulus. The arc underlying A has both ends on the same original base boundary and cuts off a disk. Its product section is a forbidden boundary compression of A. Thus every remaining collar is on the exterior side and can be absorbed. N has no internal frontier and is nonempty and relatively open and closed in connected M, so N=M. No higher-genus original boundary could have been absorbed by the permitted torus fillings or torus collars; this in particular rules out the given annulus when such a component exists.

All boundary torus products, disk pushes, cyclic torus fillings and circle coordinate gluings in this argument are finite PL parameters. The latter are matched on a boundary torus by their common primitive fiber slope: cut along that affine circle, use increasing lifts on the remaining annulus, and extend in its product collar. There is no claim of a smooth fibration on the original ambient manifold in this local PL statement.

## The degree-two circle-spine collar, without a diagram-based ball claim

This operation has a narrower input than a general immersed annulus. Its ambient horizontal marking P consists of ACTUAL disjoint injected torus boundary components, obtained by cutting outside the chosen fundamental tori. In the actual twofold ambient cover, take one embedded connecting annulus whose two rims are the chosen circle fibers on those fundamental tori. Their projected supports are disjoint, or are parallel on the same torus. The entire annulus map and its transverse class are retained. If its transverse class is already P-captured, that homotopy lifts to the corresponding actual boundary components upstairs; the spectacle was collar-carried and provides no obstruction. Otherwise every spanning path of the projected annulus is P-essential, by injection of P and the vanishing second homotopy group. In particular none of the interval constructions below asserts essentialness merely from subgroup injection.

There cannot be exactly ONE rim which projects with degree two onto its simple support. Perturb such a lifted rim in its invariant collar by r=c+ε cos s, with the free deck map s↦s+π. It gives precisely one target boundary double point. A primitive other rim is embedded, and there are no cross-rim intersections on the disjoint or parallel supports. The projected proper annulus has only double curves, since its ambient cover has degree two. At a transverse boundary crossing there is exactly one inward double-curve germ. Its compact double locus would consequently have precisely one end, impossible for a compact one-manifold. This parity proof retains the original map; it makes no arbitrary annulus replacement.

When BOTH rims have degree two, perturb each in its own isolated collar. There is one boundary double point at each end. Inessential double circles are removed by the already described innermost clean-disk ball exchanges, using incompressibility in the actual ambient cover. A returning double arc would need two distinct boundary germs on the same rim, which has only one; hence all double arcs span. A spanning arc and an essential circle cannot coexist on an annulus. Thus the local graph×interval thickening gives an embedded interval region N which contains the SAME projected annulus map, with horizontal H⊂P. Its fibers are homotopic to the original spanning paths with endpoint tracks in P and are therefore P-essential. The annulus core has infinite current image because the previous ambient map was an actual covering; this is enough to exclude the disk-base case and any ambient ball containing N. No assertion about the original exterior rank of a chosen auxiliary torus has been made.

Make this I-region essential by the annular hole-cap operation, now with W EMPTY. No used-window double is needed. Double the ambient manifold along the actual injected torus marking P. Its irreducibility follows from the finite injective-face sphere-pocket proof. Doubling a P-essential interval fiber gives an infinite cyclically reduced two-piece word. For a minimal exterior horizontal hole disk, the doubled vertical frontier is therefore not in a ball, and its exterior compression produces the solid torus whose seam cuts are the two actual disjoint meridian disks. The two finite cut-ball parameters give the cap ball in the original half. The same essential/inessential opposite-rim argument excludes any nondisk seam region. There are no other enclosure components to swallow here, and N's infinite core excludes a cap ball containing it. Fill the base hole by this ball. Each cap removes horizontal boundary circles, so only finitely many are possible. All maps already in N remain there by inclusion.

At the end H injects into P by the finite surface subsurface criterion and hence into the ambient manifold. Its group has index one or two in π₁(N). The base of N is a compact connected surface. If it has boundary, its group is free, so a kernel element of π₁(N) has square in the injected horizontal subgroup and must be trivial. Thus N injects, without an assumption that it did so before the caps. Every horizontal component lies in a torus and is incompressible there. As it is not a disk, it is an annulus or a whole torus. If the base has boundary this forces it to be an annulus or Möbius band: in the connected endpoint-double-cover case the rank formula rank(H)=2 rank(base)−1 gives base rank one; in the disconnected endpoint cover a horizontal annulus is already the base. The explicit annulus×I model is a circle-fibered solid torus with both H annuli vertical. The explicit orientation I-bundle over a Möbius band is also a solid torus, with its horizontal annulus vertical for the multiplicity-two circle model. In both cases its primitive horizontal core is exactly the original chosen fiber slope on the fundamental boundary torus. Match those coordinates and glue to the existing circle pieces.

If the caps close the base, it is a torus or Klein bottle, because its orientable endpoint cover is a torus. For T²×I the two horizontal tori are actual different P components. The original annulus gives 2a=±2b in the product's torsion-free abelian group, so the two primitive boundary fiber slopes agree up to sign. The product circle fibration with this slope matches both. For the orientation I-bundle over the Klein bottle there is one horizontal torus, with group ⟨u,v²⟩ inside ⟨u,v | vuv⁻¹=u⁻¹⟩. Its transverse interval has odd v exponent. Write the common primitive boundary slope as a=u^r v^(2s). The retained annulus, whose boundary is twice this slope, conjugates a² to a^(±2). Odd-v conjugation sends (r,s) to (−r,s). Thus either r=0 or s=0. These are EXACTLY the two circle-fiber slopes v² and u of the explicit disk-two-order-two and Möbius-base Seifert models. Choose the indicated model and glue its common horizontal fiber coordinate. This uses the actual transverse word; it does not infer a suitable fiber slope for an arbitrary annulus in a Klein-bottle interval bundle.

When neither rim has degree two, the boundary double locus is empty. Remove inessential double circles as before; the remaining circles are parallel essential circles. Their circle-direction thickening is already a circle-fibered region, with the given primitive slopes on its two marked boundary annuli. Glue it to the existing circle pieces by these actual coordinates. In the both-degree-two case the essentialized and refibered I-region gives the same kind of circle-piece attachment. Thus in either case the whole connecting annulus map is carried through this local operation. This proves the exceptional collar operation; the global maximal-circle-family and simultaneous kernel-family assemblies are still separate steps.

## Minimal torus position and circle-piece graph data

From the finite embedded-atoroidal cutting above, select a subfamily of LEAST cardinality for which each component is embedded-atoroidal or has an explicit PL circle-fibered model. Such a subfamily exists because the original finite cutting has atoroidal components. Tag a component as circle-fibered whenever it has such a model; otherwise it is tagged nonSeifert and atoroidal. This convention does not claim any map theorem for the latter. The selected system G has no boundary-parallel torus and no T²×I cut component, except the vacuous whole-manifold product case: a product can be removed by absorbing its supplied collar into the next cut piece, which does not change that piece's good property and deletes a cutting torus. A self-identified product would make an entire closed torus-bundle component, contrary to ∂M≠∅.

At a torus shared by two circle pieces, their regular-fiber slopes cannot match. If they did, the explicit circle coordinates match after the primitive boundary annulus parameterization, and the two bundles glue to one circle-fibered model. The same is true for two boundary components of one piece. Removing that cutting torus would preserve goodness, contradicting the selected minimum. In a twisted-Klein piece either explicit fibration is allowed in this argument: if either matches its neighbor, use it and remove the edge. There is just one boundary torus in that exceptional piece, so changing its choice does not undo matching at a second boundary.

Here is the finite minimum-position move needed in comparing with any other incompressible torus system. After exterior solid-torus fillings, a circle carrier N has injected torus frontier and remains irreducible. Make G transverse to ∂N, remove inessential intersection circles by innermost disk-ball exchanges, and minimize the number of remaining curves. Each annulus of G cut by ∂N has nontrivial core, so it is incompressible in N. If it boundary-compresses in N, the compression shore lies on ONE actual torus component of ∂N. The pair-of-pants construction from its two rim circles and the compression disk gives a third rim nullhomotopic in N. Injection of ∂N makes it nullhomotopic on that very torus, where it bounds a finite PL disk. The two disks and their collars bound the actual ball supplied by irreducibility. Reversing the compression through its two disk windows gives a product from the annulus to the boundary annulus. Thus it is boundary-parallel, not a new essential type.

Choose an innermost such product pocket. Any other G sheets in it are parallel stacks: in its annulus×I parameter their rim order is the actual linear order, and an outermost returning sheet would cut off a smaller pocket. Process the inner pockets first, then move the whole finite stack through the supplied disk windows. No sheet is crossed. At least the selected annulus loses its two frontier intersection circles, and no new ones are introduced. This contradicts the chosen total minimum. All the remaining intersection annuli are consequently boundary-INcompressible, so the direct PL verticality/refibering proof applies. The same argument applies to a second incompressible torus T cut by G: its annular pieces in a good cut component have nontrivial core; a boundary compression has its shore on a torus cutting face and yields exactly the same innermost product pocket. No claim about arbitrary compressible higher-genus carrier boundary is used.

A second incompressible torus T can now be made DISJOINT from G. If it had a remaining intersection, its essential annuli in an atoroidal nonSeifert component would contradict the toric-end embedded-annulus operation above. In a circle component they are vertical, except for the explicitly refiberable twisted-Klein case. Their common intersection circle on a G torus is therefore the regular-fiber slope from BOTH incident circle pieces. In the exceptional case choose its refibering; it has no other boundary at which a match could be lost. This matches a G edge and contradicts the preceding least-cardinality argument. The self-edge case matches the two fiber coordinates of the same piece and likewise deletes that torus. Hence there are no intersections. This argument applies simultaneously to a finite incompressible frontier family, using the same finite product-stack minimum.

These are graph-of-pieces data, not an assertion that a union of arbitrary I- and circle pieces is one bundle. In particular the rooted I-bundle/solid-torus book model is not discarded. For an EMBEDDED circle carrier, the next step is to cut it at the now disjoint G tori lying in its interior, classify those tori vertically inside it, and retain its full carried map through the product collars. A nonproduct carrier subpiece in a circle vertex has that vertex's intrinsic boundary fiber slope; a genuinely product carrier subpiece with an arbitrary slope cannot join two distinct actual boundary tori of a nonproduct circle vertex, since its annular base would then be the whole base. Its graph word backtracks in the same collar. A twisted-Klein vertex retains its explicit two alternatives. These observations are the input to the remaining map-engulfing/finite-cover passage, rather than a claim that the passage is already proved here.

## Peripheral intersections in a negative circle-piece base

This is the local algebra needed for graph-of-pieces normal forms; boundary slope uniqueness alone would not imply it. Let B be a compact base orbifold with nonempty boundary, finite interior cone points, and χorb(B)<0. Its group G is the explicitly computed free product of a free group with the finite cone cyclic groups. Send each cone factor injectively to its factor in their finite direct product, and also retain the base orientation character in C₂. Take the kernel H of the map to its finite IMAGE. Its finite labelled polygon cover has no cone points: at a cone of order q the corresponding permutation has exactly q sheets in each cycle, and filling those q lifted sectors gives an ordinary disk. The orientation character makes this an oriented surface F. The finite cell count gives χ(F)=[G:H]χorb(B)<0. This constructs the cover and its local parameters, not merely an abstract torsion-free subgroup.

Put a hyperbolic metric with geodesic boundary on F by the finite polygon construction: cut F into pairs of pants, prescribe the same positive cuff length on the two sides of each cut, form each pair from two right-angled hexagons, and glue their geodesic cuffs. A sphere with holes and a handled surface are both obtained by the finite polygon-with-bands cutting; the low disk/annulus cases are absent because χ(F)<0. The usual hexagon can be constructed by three disjoint perpendicular geodesic segments with the prescribed positive alternate side lengths; the hyperbolic right-triangle formulas determine its other sides positively. The doubled glued surface has no cone angle at a cuff and is complete. Continuing its hyperbolic charts in its universal cover gives the hyperbolic plane; the actual lifts of F are the intersections of its cuff half-planes. Hence the lifts of boundary circles of F are distinct geodesic lines. This is an ordinary surface construction, with no periodic mapping-class realization and no metric invariance under G asserted.

View this same universal cover as the universal orbifold cover of B. The group G permutes its ACTUAL boundary components. Suppose two conjugate boundary cyclic subgroups have a nontrivial common element z. Boundary groups are infinite cyclic, so z has infinite order. Since H has finite index, some positive power z^k lies in H. It is a hyperbolic deck transformation of the metric cover of F and stabilizes both corresponding boundary lines. Each is its axis, because the restriction to that boundary line is a nonzero translation. Uniqueness of the hyperbolic axis forces the lines to be the SAME actual component. Its stabilizer in G is exactly the ordinary boundary cyclic subgroup: on that component the orbifold covering is just the universal line cover of one boundary circle, with no cone or mirror point. Therefore the two base boundary labels agree and the conjugating element is in that same boundary subgroup. The conclusion includes two different lifts of the same boundary. Only z^k in H needed to be an isometry; an outside element of G was never assumed to preserve the chosen metric.

In a circle-Seifert piece the quotient by the normal cyclic fiber is this base group. Thus an intersection of conjugate boundary torus groups outside the coincident-boundary/conjugator case is contained in the fiber subgroup. The only pieces with injected torus boundary and χorb≥0 are the explicit T²×I and twisted-Klein interval-bundle models (positive cases are solid tori and have noninjective torus boundary). Those exceptions retain their actual product or index-two normal forms instead of this negative-Euler argument.

## Keeping the root generators in the actual annulus spine

The spine used at a circle vertex must include returning annuli around its cone points. Merely cutting the base into fibered solid tori does not retain their root generators. Here is the finite construction that repairs that omission, before any projection or engulfing assertion.

In a parametrized fibered solid torus V, let x be its oriented core generator and let its primitive boundary fiber be h=pλ+qμ, where λ maps to x, μ maps to 1, p>0, and gcd(p,q)=1. Take a thin marked fiber annulus Y on its boundary. Choose another primitive boundary curve b=rλ+sμ with ps−qr=±1. Cutting the boundary torus along a fiber exhibits it as an annulus, and b crosses that annulus from one rim to the other. Consequently a spanning path of the complementary boundary annulus, closed through Y with the corresponding marked endpoint tracks, represents x^r. Its fiber circle represents x^p. The determinant equation gives gcd(p,r)=1. Push the complementary annulus slightly into V while retaining its two boundary circles in Y. This is an actual embedded proper annulus A. It is obtained in the finite product collar of the boundary torus, so neither its boundary maps nor its transverse path has been replaced by an annulus-existence theorem. If ap+br=1, the two actual based words from A∪Y give x=(x^p)^a(x^r)^b. In particular the inclusion of A∪Y surjects onto π₁(V), including the exceptional root x. The complementary annulus may be boundary-parallel relative to the WHOLE boundary torus; that does not make its spanning path captured by the smaller marked annulus Y. These are different relative conditions.

For a general supplied circle piece N with nonempty marked vertical boundary Y, the same construction is finite. Work in its base surface with its finitely many cone points deleted. Choose one small interval in each marked base boundary interval or circle. Join these intervals by a finite disjoint proper arc system, and add the usual polygon-cutting proper arcs for the handles, crosscaps and unmarked boundary components. All endpoints can be put in the chosen marked intervals: move the finitely many endpoints in their actual boundary collars, and join them to the chosen intervals by parallel boundary bands in their cyclic order. Before filling the cone punctures, add a returning arc separating each puncture from the other punctures. Equivalently, take the standard polygon-with-bands picture with each cone puncture attached by a separate boundary band; the two sides of that band give this returning arc. Different bands and their proper arcs are disjoint. Retain the whole marked boundary intervals, not just their endpoint sets.

The inverse image of each such proper arc is an embedded vertical annulus with both rims in Y. The family is disjoint. Its transverse paths, closed using the marked boundary paths and the connecting arcs already chosen, give the usual handle, crosscap, ordinary boundary and cone generators of the base orbifold. Together with the regular fiber h they generate π₁(N): cutting at these annuli leaves base disks with at most one cone point, and the only additional local relation is the explicitly parametrized cone relation c_i^{q_i}=h^{m_i}. At a remaining cone solid torus the returning-annulus calculation above retains its root as well. This verifies the generator assertion in the actual polygon presentation, including the orientation-reversing base generators that conjugate h to h⁻¹. No choice of an auxiliary boundary torus is involved. If there is no cone and the base is a disk, the marked fiber already generates the solid-torus group.

For use in the downward map, retain the finite union K of these annuli, the whole Y collars, and their actual endpoint and connecting paths. The induced map π₁(K)→π₁(N) is onto, with base paths chosen as in the polygon construction. A map on N cannot be replaced just by its values on the regular-fiber circles of K. If a proposed lower carrier L contains the complete mapped K and L→M is injective on π₁, then the surjection defines at most one factorization π₁(N)→π₁(L); it defines one precisely when the relators from N vanish there. They do vanish when the original map N→M and that inclusion give the mapped K, because injection L→M detects their triviality. For aspherical N, L and M, this factorization gives a map N→L homotopic to the original map in M. The homotopy can retain the specified annulus and boundary data when those maps already agree: extend successively over cells of (N,K), using the now verified π₁ relations and the vanishing higher homotopy groups. Thus the full spine, rather than an arbitrary selected frontier, is the object whose carriage would retain the original root and transverse words.

There is also a useful exact obstruction to losing a root into an I-piece. Suppose the entire A∪Y from the solid-torus calculation has been carried into an essential interval region I, with the marked annulus Y lying in one actual horizontal component H. Assume its core h is primitive as a simple element of the oriented surface H, and [π₁(I):π₁(H)]≤2. Write a for the retained transverse closure, so the actual relation is h^r=a^p with gcd(r,p)=1. This equality holds INSIDE π₁(I), since I→M is injective, and a² belongs to π₁(H). If p is odd, a=(a²)^{−(p−1)/2}h^r lies in π₁(H). The centralizer of a nontrivial element of this surface group is cyclic, and a primitive simple h is not a proper power; hence a^p=h^r forces p to divide r and p=1. If p is even, (a²)^{p/2}=h^r inside π₁(H) instead gives p/2 dividing r, hence p=2. For a horizontal torus the same calculation is just divisibility in its primitive rank-one summand of ℤ². Thus p>2 cannot be absorbed into such an I-piece after the full return annulus and whole Y have been retained. The multiplicity-two exception is the explicit Möbius-base interval model already described above. This argument does not apply merely because the image of a fiber circle is present: it uses the retained transverse closure a, the actual whole horizontal annulus, and injection of I.

In the actual twofold setting the same multiplicity bound does NOT require the projected h itself to be simple downstairs. Retain the based covering character χ:π₁(M)→C₂. The original upper x is a closed lifted loop, so χ(x)=0 after any of the retained based homotopies. The original h=x^p is a simple essential curve in the actual upper P surface. Write its image downstairs as w^k, with w not a proper power in its oriented P component. If e is the least positive exponent with χ(w^e)=0, then e is one or two. The lift of w^k is (the lift of w^e)^{k/e}. Since the actual upper h is simple in an oriented surface, it is not a proper power there. Therefore |k|=e≤2.

Suppose the whole root certificate has been carried into an injected I-piece, and its boundary word lies in its actual horizontal subsurface H⊂P. The subgroup π₁(H) is root-closed in π₁(P). Here is the precise surface argument. Cut the oriented P surface at the boundary of H. A boundary cyclic group is root-closed in each incident surface group: its boundary circle is not a proper power, and the reduced-word centralizer of a nontrivial element is cyclic. If an element g has a power fixing the H vertex of the cut-surface tree, g is elliptic. Choose a g-fixed vertex. The power fixes the path from it to the H vertex. At its first edge, root-closure in the fixed vertex group forces g to fix that edge and the next vertex; induct along the finite path. Thus g fixes the H vertex. The whole-surface and disk cases are immediate; for a torus the proper injected subsurface is an annulus with primitive slope, so the assertion is divisibility in ℤ². This proves the root-closure claim without assuming that every abstract subgroup of a surface group is geometric.

Consequently w belongs to π₁(H). Since [π₁(I):π₁(H)]≤2, x² belongs there too. The exact relation (x²)^p=w^{2k} holds in that SAME horizontal group, by injection of I→M. A primitive root w in this free or torus group forces p to divide 2k. With p>0 and 0<|k|≤2, the only possible p>2 is p=4, |k|=2. In that case x²=w or w⁻¹. But χ(x)=0 gives χ(w)=0. Then w already lifts to a closed loop in the actual upper P component, whereas h=w^{±2}; its upper lift is a proper square, contradicting the actual simplicity of upper h. Hence again p≤2. Every use of the relation and of the character retains its original base path. The statement is about a root already carried by its COMPLETE certificate into one injected lower I-piece; it is not a construction of that piece.

This repairs the missing ROOT WORD in a circle-piece spine. It does not assert the remaining simultaneous carriage. In particular a negative-base I-piece has no finite disjoint family of closed base curves generating its group; replacing it by such a family would be false. Its actual finite graph×interval spine has branching fibers and must be retained through the twofold positioning. Nor has the calculation supplied an embedded lower carrier containing an arbitrary projected collection of roots and those branched spines. Those are the remaining simultaneous trace operations in the surjective torus assembly, rather than consequences of the root calculation.


## Twofold vertical-frontier cut pieces and relative product-pocket moves

This local continuation concerns the ACTUAL twofold cover, with free PL deck map τ. Write A for the finite disjoint vertical-frontier annuli of the supplied essential I-region J, and τA for their actual mates. Their rims are in P′. The entire horizontal endpoint cover H is retained, H→P′ and J→M′ are injective, each annular core has infinite image, and every I-fiber is essential relative to P′ with endpoint tracks allowed in P′. Boundary isotopies inside the labelled P′ components are allowed and their tracks must be retained. There are no used windows in this local operation. A move of J is accompanied by its conjugate move of τJ, so that the resulting regions remain actual deck mates. This is a pair of conjugate embedding isotopies; it must NOT be described as one common ambient isotopy of the union, which would preserve its intersection set.

The following cut-type implications require no unproved simultaneous verticalization. A spanning path of any one frontier annulus is P′-essential: move its endpoints around its two rim circles to compare it with an actual I-fiber. These are precisely allowed endpoint tracks in P′. If an intersection arc is returning on one annulus, an outermost returning disk has its other edge in P′. If that arc were spanning on the other annulus, this disk would give a forbidden P′-homotopy of such a spanning path. Thus a returning/spanning intersection is impossible. An inessential intersection circle on just one frontier would likewise kill an essential core word of the other injected annulus in the ambient group; a circle is inessential on both or essential on both.

Here is also the boundary-bigon consequence before any assertion about equivariance. For an outermost returning disk D, its boundary-rim interval β has no other intersection endpoints in its interior. The intersection arc is returning on the other annulus, where it bounds a disk E with boundary-rim interval δ. The loop β∪δ is simple: β is disjoint from every other rim in its interior, and δ is embedded. The two disks supply its nullhomotopy in M′, so injection P′→M′ makes it nullhomotopic on P′. It bounds an actual finite PL disk there. If that disk contains other rim arcs, choose an innermost returning arc in that disk; this gives an empty rim bigon. No whole rim circle can lie inside it, since rim circles have infinite image and hence are essential on P′. Moving a rim across such an empty bigon has its ordinary finite disk-window extension in a P′ collar, with the actual endpoint tracks recorded. To use this as a move of the TWO decks one must still choose disjoint conjugate supports, or give their simultaneous window parameter; the existence of an ordinary bigon alone does not supply that last condition.

Consequently, once genuine paired moves have removed the inessential circles and returning arcs, an individual frontier annulus is cut either by parallel essential circles or by spanning arcs, never both. Its cut pieces are actual annuli or rectangles. The core of every annular piece still has infinite image in the ambient group. A rectangle's paths between its P′-end edges are P′-essential by the spanning-path observation above. This does NOT establish essentialness between its OTHER two edges relative to a whole lateral marking: those edges lie on actual annuli, and cutting or drilling that marking can change the condition. In particular these conclusions alone do not meet rectangle15's hypothesis of whole disjoint DISK windows, nor its second relative essentialness hypothesis. No injection of a new boundary-complement pattern is inferred from injection of P′ and of J separately.

Here is the completed paired disk move for a CLEAN configuration. “Clean” means that the disks and annular differences specified below have no intersections with any other member of either frontier family, apart from the indicated two disks and their rims. Their supplied collars are also chosen free of the other frontier pieces. This is an actual geometric hypothesis; innermostness of just one disk does not imply it.

First, a clean inessential circle cannot be fixed setwise by τ when its two capping disks are exchanged. Their union would be a τ-invariant embedded sphere. Irreducibility gives its ball side. The side meeting the actual nonempty ambient boundary cannot be a ball with that sphere as its entire boundary; thus τ preserves the ball side. A free PL involution cannot preserve a ball: common-refine its finite triangulation with its image and barycentrically subdivide, so a preserved simplex would have a fixed barycenter, whereas freeness pairs all simplices and makes the Euler characteristic even. This contradicts χ(ball)=1. The same argument rules out an invariant disk pocket. For two DISJOINT conjugate sphere pockets their balls cannot strictly nest, since applying τ reverses the strict containment. They are therefore disjoint, and their disk-window pushes can be made as conjugate moves, with their proper frontier remainders and all P′ traces outside the balls.

The clean OPPOSITELY NESTED configuration is in fact IMPOSSIBLE; no big-disk swap is asserted. It necessarily uses one annulus and its mate, since distinct annuli of the same frontier family are disjoint. Put D0=D⊂A, D1=τD⊂τA, let E⊂τA be the disk with ∂E=∂D0=c0, and suppose D1⊂Int E. Then D0⊂Int τE and c1=τc0 is the other rim. The annular differences are B′=E minus Int D1 and A′=τE minus Int D0. Cleanliness says that their interiors and those of the two big disks have no other frontier intersections. In particular the interiors of A′ and of the two retained remainders A\Int(τE), τA\Int E are disjoint from the sphere S0=D0∪E, apart from their stated boundary rims. Both retained remainders are connected annuli with an interior disk removed, and each has its two ACTUAL rims in P′.

Let Q be the irreducibility ball of S0. It is a compact interior ball and therefore misses P′. The connected interior of A′ is wholly on one side of S0. At c1, the sphere S0 is the FULL τA-plane, since c1 lies in Int E. The two A half-planes there are A′ and the retained A\Int(τE); they are on opposite sides of S0. If A′ is outside Q, the retained A remainder enters Q. That connected remainder cannot leave Q without meeting S0 again, and cleanliness excludes such a meeting. It would therefore have its actual P′ boundary inside the interior ball, a contradiction.

If instead A′ is inside Q, inspect the local link at c0. Use a normal disk to the intersection circle, with A given by z=0, D0 on the negative x ray, A′ on the positive x ray, τA given by x=0, E on the positive z ray, and the retained τA\Int E on the negative z ray. The sphere S0 uses the negative x and positive z rays. Its other two rays, positive x and negative z, are on the SAME three-quadrant side. Consequently the retained τA remainder enters Q whenever A′ does. It too is connected, misses S0 except at its boundary c0, and reaches the actual P′ boundary; this is the same contradiction. These finite PL quadrant statements persist after rounding the sphere seam in its chosen small collar. Thus neither ball-side choice is possible. This proves the exclusion using one actual sphere ball and the retained boundary tracks, without assuming an inside-meridian solid torus or an isotopy of the two big disks.

In this calculation c0,c1 are null in the ambient group, since they bound the actual frontier disks. They must never be substituted for a longitudinal root or transverse word. In a larger, NONCLEAN configuration an additional crossing is exactly what lets a retained remainder leave a putative pocket; the preceding contradiction then does not apply. Nor does it produce a new circle carrier to which those extra crossings may be silently assigned.

The finite-forest extraction is still separate. An A-innermost circle only makes D and τD clean; E can contain several additional inessential circles. A minimum sum of two cap complexities does not prove otherwise. One useful finite observation is to draw an arrow i→j when a large cap E_i contains a clean conjugate cap τD_j. In a shortest directed cycle the large caps E_i are pairwise disjoint: a strict containment E_i⊂E_j gives the shortcut j→successor(i). But a cap on that cycle can still contain OFF-cycle clean caps. Those branches must be retained and processed in actual multiwindow parameters; they cannot be deleted to apply the clean nested-pair exclusion just proved. This section proves the disjoint paired-pocket move, the clean nested-pair exclusion and the stated cut-type implications only. It neither selects such a clean branch from an arbitrary finite forest nor establishes the later simultaneous branched-spine enclosure or a common interval coordinate on separately positioned regions.


### Proper disk systems with the same labelled ports

The whole-forest construction can use the following actual finite isotopy operation. Let R be a compact orientable irreducible finite PL three-manifold. Let D_i and F_i be two finite systems of pairwise disjoint properly embedded PL disks, with the SAME labelled pairwise disjoint boundary circles γ_i on ∂R. There is an ambient PL isotopy of R fixed on ∂R which carries the whole old system D_i onto the corresponding target system F_i. Targets are fixed comparison subsets, not part of the source system being isotoped.

First separate the old and target collars at each common rim, leaving that rim pointwise fixed. This is a local finite operation: in a boundary-annulus times inward-interval chart the proper disk collars give neat half-disk rays. Their finite PL quadrant parameters straighten the collars and interpolate the old ray to a nearby distinct ray in the open half-plane, fixing the physical boundary line. Extend over the finite adjacent prisms with the prescribed window parameters. Distinct labelled rims have disjoint such charts. Relative finite general position outside these collars now gives only finitely many interior intersection circles; there are no intersection arcs ending on a rim, because the two collars are separated throughout a neighborhood of it.

Choose an innermost intersection circle on ANY target disk. Its target cap Δ has interior disjoint from ALL old disks. The same circle cuts a disk δ off the intersected old disk. Their union is an embedded interior sphere, so bounds a ball in R. The old remainder is connected and reaches its actual boundary circle on ∂R. It misses this sphere except at the chosen seam and hence is outside the interior ball. Every other old disk also misses the sphere: it misses δ by old-system disjointness and misses Δ by its choice. It reaches ∂R and is therefore outside the ball as well. The finite ball-window push carries old δ across this ball to a small push-off of Δ, supported away from these other old disks and from the old remainder except its attaching collar. The new cap misses EVERY target disk: the target disks are disjoint, and a sufficiently small normal collar of Δ misses all the other target disks. Thus the TOTAL number of old/target intersection circles decreases, not merely the count for one selected pair. Old-system disjointness and all labelled boundary circles remain unchanged. Iterate finitely. This argument does not assume that the old cap δ was clean of target intersections; all of those intersections are removed by its replacement.

The resulting systems have disjoint interiors. For a corresponding pair D_i,F_i, their common rim has the separated quadrant collars just described. Push its pinched rim slightly inward to obtain an interior sphere from the two disk faces and the short bridging collar. The sphere bounds an actual ball by irreducibility. Its ball side is the pocket BETWEEN the disk faces: the other side near the bridging collar connects to the physical boundary through the unoccupied boundary half-disk and therefore cannot be inside an interior ball. Restoring the short rim collar gives the usual pinched ball between D_i and F_i, whose two disk faces meet at their common rim. This is a parameter construction, not an assertion about an arbitrary three-ball diffeomorphism. Parametrize the sphere boundary by the two disk parameters and their bridging annulus, extend that boundary parameter over its ball by finite coning, and restore the supplied quadrant collar. The finite ball/window theorem retains all those parameters.

For explicit control of the final push, a pinched ball has the PL model

    {(x,z): x∈D², |z|≤δ(x)},

where δ is positive in the polygonal disk interior, zero on its boundary, and piecewise linear on a finite fan. Its disk faces are z=δ(x) and z=−δ(x). Extend its two faces by a small tapered exterior collar, giving |z|≤2δ(x); the neat quadrant collars allow this without meeting the physical boundary except at the fixed rim. The graphs z=(1−2t)δ(x), extended by increasing affine maps on the two vertical subintervals and by the identity outside, first describe a continuous ambient isotopy with the desired endpoint maps and fixed rim. This displayed formula is generally bilinear in (x,t); it is not asserted to be jointly PL.

Here is its actual finite PL replacement. Triangulate the fan times the time interval into finitely many prisms and simplices. Assign +δ at the time-zero vertices and −δ at the time-one vertices, and interpolate linearly to obtain a jointly PL graph u(x,t). On every base-time simplex δ is affine and the vertex inequalities give −δ(x)≤u(x,t)≤δ(x); at the rim all three functions vanish. Subdivide compatibly. For each such simplex, compare the two source vertical prisms with levels −2δ, δ, +2δ with the two target prisms having levels −2δ, u, +2δ. They have the same zero-height faces and the same strict vertical order elsewhere. Use the same ordered vertex-prism triangulation on both, with corresponding vertices having the SAME (x,t) coordinates; extend their correspondence affinely on each simplex. This is the finite zero-positive prism matching: the vertical-edge height ratios are positive, the collapsed rim edges coincide, and the maps on shared faces agree. The resulting homeomorphism preserves (x,t), is increasing on every noncollapsed z-fiber, and is the identity on the outer levels and rim. Thus it is a level-preserving jointly PL ambient isotopy. Its end graphs are exactly the prescribed disk faces. This supplies the relative PL isotopy, not merely a replacement disk with the same boundary or a collection of separately PL time slices.

All other old AND target disks are outside this pinched pocket: their boundaries are on ∂R, their interiors miss both disk faces, and their rims are distinct from γ_i. Thus the matched-pair push fixes them. Perform these finitely many pushes successively. The composite is the asserted ambient isotopy of the whole old disk system, relative the entire boundary and every port label. Every path or spine originally in the source region is carried by the same ambient isotopy. No root, transverse closure, or horizontal endpoint track is replaced by a separately selected boundary witness.

### Opposite-end annular families in an actual untwisted product

This operation has the literal additional input J=B×I. Normalize the lower and upper boundary multicurves SEPARATELY to parallel copies of the same geodesic class representatives, retaining their actual labels and the existing order within each parallel family.
