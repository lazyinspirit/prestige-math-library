## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable finite PL three-manifold, and suppose $\pi_2(M)=0$. Let $P,W\subset\partial M$ be compact finite PL boundary subsurfaces with disjoint interiors. Every component of $W$ is supplied as a rectangle whose two opposite end edges are its intersection with $P$; its other edges are disjoint from $P$. Assume that every component of $P$ injects on fundamental groups into $M$.

Let $p:M'\to M$ be a supplied twofold PL cover. Put $P'=p^{-1}P$, $W'=p^{-1}W$. Let $e_j:C_j=[0,1]\times[0,1]\to M'$, $1\le j\le q$, be a nonempty finite family of pairwise disjoint proper finite PL embedded rectangles, each with
$$E=[0,1]\times\{0,1\},\qquad F=\{0,1\}\times[0,1],$$
whose $E$ edges lie properly in $P'$ and whose $F$ edges span components of $W'$. For each rectangle only its four corners lie in $P'\cap W'$. Suppose every projected map $f_j=pe_j$ is essential for both pairs: an arc in $C_j$ joining its two $E$ edges is not homotopic into $P$ with its endpoints remaining in $P$, and an arc in $C_j$ joining its two $F$ edges is not homotopic into $W$ with endpoints remaining in $W$.

Then the family $(f_j)$ is homotopic through maps of these labelled marked rectangles, keeping every $E$ edge in $P$, every $F$ edge in $W$ and every rectangle interior off $\partial M$, to a family $(g_j)$ carried by a compact embedded finite PL interval bundle $N\to B$. Every component of its base $B$ is a compact connected finite PL surface with nonempty boundary; $N$ and $B$ may be disconnected. The full horizontal endpoint surface of the bundle is $N\cap P$, and $N\cap W$ is a union of its vertical boundary strips containing all $g_j(F)$. The remaining frontier has the finite plane/quadrant charts of a properly embedded interval-bundle region. Every component of $N$ is orientable and PL irreducible, and its horizontal endpoint surface injects on fundamental groups into $N$. Each actual original relative homotopy class, including both sets of endpoint tracks and its labelled external boundary, is retained; no disjointness of the intermediate homotopies is asserted.

If in addition $\pi_1(M)$ is torsion-free, $N$ contains either a properly embedded rectangle essential for both marked pairs, or a properly embedded $\pi_1$-injective annulus with boundary in $P$ whose interval-spanning path is not homotopic into $P$ with endpoints remaining there. This is an existence alternative, not a claim that its chosen surface represents each original map.

The hypothesis $\pi_2(M)=0$ is explicit. This lemma does not by itself produce that input, perform a full covering-tower induction, engulf an arbitrary finite family of pre-existing interval bundles, or realize a periodic return map.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The marked finite PL data and both relative essentialness conditions in the Statement.

[F1] A disjoint arc family in a genuine twofold boundary cover, with distinct projected endpoints, has a boundary-fixed PL isotopy into projected position without disk monogons or bigons. The resulting labelled graph has an ambient homeomorphic marking by geodesic arcs, every subarc being uniquely shortest in its endpoint-fixed class. [[lem-free-twofold-surface-covers-admit-bigon-free-marked-arc-position]]

[F2] The first three proof rows of the two-sheeted disk lemma remove double circles by finite disk substitutions and adjacent-arm resolutions, keeping the entire external boundary map and introducing no new double curve or value of multiplicity greater than two. Those rows do not use its subsequent boundary normal-subgroup test or double-arc elimination. [[lem-two-sheeted-pl-disk-immersion-has-essential-embedded-disk]]

[F3] Polygonal disk/strip parameters, prescribed boundary extensions, finite collars, compatible cell triangulations and standard disk windows are supplied by the listed finite PL lemmas. [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]] [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]] [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]] [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]]

[F4] A finite sphere in standard PL three-space bounds a parametrized PL ball. Universal covers exist, sphere maps lift, and the standard two-sphere is simply connected. [[thm-finite-pl-schoenflies-in-dimension-three]] [[thm-universal-cover-existence]] [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]] [[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]]

## Proof

1.1 Normalize all $2q$ labelled $F$ edges first. In each parametrized rectangle of $W'$, disjoint proper spanning arcs have product-strip parameters: cut along them, parametrize each resulting polygonal disk by [F3], and interpolate their transverse interval coordinates in their actual order. Choose all projected strip levels in each $W$ rectangle distinct, including when source edges lie in deck-paired components of $W'$. The finitely many levels can be interleaved in any order extending the separate orders in the two lifted rectangles. These choices give a PL boundary isotopy carrying these edges to disjoint projected spanning arcs. Its endpoint movements on the $P'\cap W'$ seams extend to small boundary collars in $P'$, matching the same increasing interval maps there. Thus it is an isotopy of the whole marked boundary pair, preserving $P'$ and $W'$. It extends over a collar of $\partial M'$: on each collar level use the boundary isotopy at time $\min(t,\lambda(r))$, where the PL cutoff $\lambda$ is one on the outer face and zero at the inner face. Finite prism subdivisions make this a PL ambient isotopy. Apply it to the whole disjoint family. In particular all $4q$ corners now have distinct projected images. The projected map of the disjoint union of the $F$ edges is an embedding and has no singular values. [given, F3, construct]

2.1 Apply [F1] to all $2q$ labelled $E$ arcs in $P'\to P$ with these fixed corners. Extend its boundary-fixed isotopy by the identity on $W'$ and then over the same boundary collar of $M'$ as in step 1.1. The projected $E$ arc family now has no disk bigon or monogon in $P$. Move the interior of the resulting embedded union of rectangles into general position with its deck translate, fixed near this boundary graph. Explicitly use finitely many chart balls $U$ disjoint from their deck translates, subdivide the disk pieces there, and move their vertices off the finitely many determinant-zero affine positions of the other disk. Extend each vertex move by coning its ambient star boundary; its deck-paired move occurs in the disjoint chart. Later moves are chosen small enough to retain all earlier transverse intersections and separations. This gives finitely many regular double circles and proper double arcs for the projection, with no triple values since a twofold projection of an embedded union of disks has at most two preimages. All endpoints of double arcs lie in $P$, since the entire projected $F$ family is embedded. These ambient isotopies project to genuine homotopies of the original marked map. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Use exactly [F2]'s circle operation. Two preimage circles bound disks. In the nested case substitute the inner disk for the region inside the outer circle; in the disjoint case interchange their disk maps. The two preimage circles may belong to different labelled rectangles: the interchange still leaves each original external boundary attached to a disk and uses each old interior region exactly once. The equal boundary-value identifications extend by [F3]. Resolve the normal cross along the joining circle by its two adjacent polygonal arm connections. The orientation of the disk and the ambient manifold forbids a sheet-exchange monodromy of a double circle, so these cross-section resolutions agree around it. Each retained old sheet region is used at most once; consequently there are no new double curves and no new triple values. The external boundary map is unchanged, and the number of double circles drops. Finite repetition gives proper immersed labelled rectangles $g_j$, each with its own unchanged boundary map; their union has only regular double arcs and at most two preimages per value. Write $g$ for this union map. This is a disk replacement, and we do not call it an isotopy. Each labelled rectangle is homotopic relative to its entire boundary to its preceding map: glue those two disk maps along that common boundary to obtain a sphere map; $\pi_2(M)=0$ makes it null. Cutting its nullhomotopy along the equator, or the disk-pair homotopy extension construction, gives the required disk homotopy fixed on its boundary. Do this separately for the finitely many labels; these homotopies need not be disjoint. To keep this homotopy proper, in a boundary collar with inward coordinate $s$ replace $s$ by $\max(s,h(x,u))$, cutting off the replacement before the inner collar face. Choose the continuous height $h$ zero on $\partial C$ and at homotopy times $u=0,1$, positive for $x\in\operatorname{int}C$ and $0<u<1$, and smaller than the collar width. This fixes both endpoint maps and the entire boundary homotopy, while pushing any interior boundary contact inward. Thus both relative essentialness conditions are retained. [given, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Every preimage of a double arc spans the two $E$ edges of its source rectangle. Otherwise choose a returning preimage arc $a$ outermost among the returning arcs. It cuts off a disk $D\subset C$ with other boundary shore $\beta\subset E$. Its interior contains no preimage double arc: an arc there would also return to that same $E$ edge and contradict outermostness. Let $a^*$ be the other preimage of its double arc. If $a^*$ spans the $E$ edges of its own rectangle, the map of that spanning arc is homotopic through $g(D)$ to $g(\beta)\subset P$, contradicting the preserved relative essentialness. Thus $a^*$ also returns and bounds a disk $D^*$ with other shore $\beta^*\subset E$. The disk maps $g(D)$ and $g(D^*)$, together with the common double-arc image, show that $g(\beta)$ and $g(\beta^*)$ are homotopic in $M$ relative to their endpoints (orient both shores between the same two double-arc endpoints). These two endpoint images lie in the same component of $P$, since each shore is connected. Injection of that component's fundamental group into $M$ therefore makes the two shores homotopic relative to their endpoints in $P$. Use the ambient geodesic marking supplied in [F1]. Both shores become subsegments of the labelled shortest geodesics and are shortest in their own endpoint-fixed classes. Uniqueness forces them to have the same parametrized image, allowing reversal of orientation. This is impossible at either endpoint: the two boundary branches there belong to distinct transverse branches of the projected boundary graph. This argument does not assume that $g(\beta^*)$ is simple; its lift and its shortest-path property suffice. The contradiction proves the assertion. [given, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 In each labelled source rectangle put its parallel spanning preimage arcs at distinct cross-sections of a product parameter $C_j=I_x\times I_t$, where $E$ is the endpoint set for $t$. Such a parameter is explicit: cut at the finitely many disjoint arcs, parametrize the remaining polygonal disks as consecutive rectangles by [F3], and paste their matching shore parameters. The two preimages of a given double arc are distinct disjoint source arcs, by the regular double-arc local model and multiplicity two. No deck action on the disk after the substitutions is asserted. Choose an interval parameter on one preimage of each pair and use its image under $g$ to parameterize the other, reversing $t$ when required. The same polygonal disk extensions make these choices agree on the intervening rectangles. Thus each double-arc identification is exactly $t\mapsto t$ or $t\mapsto1-t$. No arbitrary circle-return map occurs. The image of $g$ is the corresponding interval bundle over the graph obtained from the disjoint union of the $q$ base intervals $I_x$ by identifying their finitely many paired interior points. At a vertex it has four transverse base arms; at each original endpoint it has one arm and lies in a marked $W$ strip. [F3, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Thicken this graph description inside $M$, rather than inferring a neighborhood parameter from an abstract triangulation. A double arc has a normal product chart: cover its parameter interval by finitely many PL arc charts, join consecutive chart disks using their prescribed disk boundary maps from [F3], and retain the two endpoint half-ball charts in $P$. In these charts the image sheets are $I_t$ times the planar cross. Choose a small transverse polygonal disk around that cross, with its four arm windows, and take its product with $I_t$. Along a nonsingular sheet rectangle between two such charts use its finite normal collar to attach a transverse band times $I_t$. Choose all widths below the positive separations of the finitely many nonincident compact sheet pieces; the resulting pieces meet only at their specified windows. At $F$ use the supplied $W$ rectangle parameters, so the band-end windows are in $W$ and their endpoints in $P$. The interval maps in step 5.1 make all attachments fiber preserving. Orientation reversal of $t$ reverses the base disk orientation, since the ambient three-manifold is orientable; this is exactly the transition rule for a possibly twisted interval bundle over a surface. The transverse disks and bands therefore form an actual finite surface $B$, possibly disconnected, with boundary and the attached three-dimensional charts form an embedded interval bundle $N\to B$. Every graph component contains at least one original interval, hence its unpaired end windows give nonempty boundary in each component of $B$. The chart ends at $t=0,1$ are exactly $N\cap P$, with no other contact with $P$ or $\partial M$. Its selected vertical end strips are exactly $N\cap W$ and contain $g(F)$. Along a remaining vertical boundary, the cross-section disk has a half-disk chart; coning its polygonal link and taking its interval product gives the claimed plane/quadrant frontier charts. This explicitly constructs all bundle and seam parameters. [F3, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 The horizontal endpoint surface injects on fundamental groups into $N$. Each of its components is either one copy of $B$ in a trivial bundle or the endpoint double cover of $B$ in a twisted bundle. Retraction along the interval fibers is a homotopy equivalence $N\to B$, and the endpoint projection is respectively an identity or a covering map. Path lifting proves that a covering induces an injection, so the asserted injection follows. The region is orientable because its charts are embedded ambient charts. Its base is a disk-and-band neighborhood of a finite graph, and hence has that graph as a spine: radially collapse each vertex disk to its finite arm star and each band to its middle interval, using compatible piecewise linear parameters. Its interval bundle consequently has a finite three-dimensional handle description with ball vertex blocks and rectangular-prism edge blocks attached along disk windows. [F3, step 6.1, construct]

7.2 We prove the last existence alternative under its extra torsion-free hypothesis. Every bundle fiber of a connected component of $N$ is essential relative to $P$: moving a fiber along a base path is a homotopy whose two endpoint tracks lie in the full horizontal surface, hence in $P$, and this compares it, up to reversal, with a fiber of one of the original essential strips. Use the finite spine graph from step 5.1, with its degree-one endpoints labeled by the components of $W$ containing their strips. If two endpoint labels differ, an embedded base path between them gives an embedded rectangle. It is essential relative to $P$ by the fiber observation and relative to $W$ because its two end arcs lie in different components of $W$. Otherwise all labels in this graph component are one rectangle $W_0$. Choose a spanning tree and a distinguished endpoint $v_0$. If any simple tree path from $v_0$ to another endpoint is essential relative to $W$, its interval bundle is the required embedded rectangle. Suppose all these paths are inessential. Their image paths in $M$ are then homotopic, with endpoints staying in $W_0$, to paths in $W_0$. Since $W_0$ is a disk, any two such paths with matching endpoints are homotopic relative to those endpoints. If all fundamental cycles of the spine graph also had trivial image in $\pi_1(M)$, every original graph walk between marked endpoints, closed by a path in $W_0$, would be nullhomotopic: insert the tree paths to $v_0$ and reduce the remaining walk by the non-tree edges. This contradicts the preserved $W$-essentialness of any original rectangle in this component. Thus a fundamental graph cycle has nontrivial image in $\pi_1(M)$. That cycle is embedded: it is one non-tree edge together with the unique simple tree path joining its endpoints. Take its embedded narrow surface neighborhood in $B$. If it is an annulus, the interval bundle over its core is a properly embedded annulus with nontrivial core image. If it is a Möbius band, use the vertical annulus over the boundary of that band; its core image is the square of the cycle image, which is nontrivial because $\pi_1(M)$ is torsion-free. In either case the annulus core has infinite order, so its cyclic group injects into $\pi_1(M)$. Every spanning interval of this annulus is one of the bundle fibers already proved essential relative to $P$. In particular the annulus cannot be homotopic through pair maps into $P$. This constructs the asserted embedded surface without assuming that $N$ injects into $M$ or that its horizontal surface is incompressible in $P$. [given, F3, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 This handlebody is PL irreducible. Lift it to its universal cover. Its vertex/edge blocks form the tree which covers its finite spine graph. Each finite subtree thickening is a parametrized PL ball: delete leaves and apply the actual two-ball one-window gluing of [F3]; include each edge prism as a ball node. A compact lifted sphere lies in the interior of one such finite subtree thickening, enlarged by adjacent blocks if necessary. By [F4] it bounds a parametrized ball there. Distinct deck translates of that ball have disjoint sphere boundaries, since those boundaries lift the same embedded sphere downstairs. Put any two such compact balls and a joining path in a larger finite subtree ball. Standard PL sphere separation there makes their interiors disjoint or one ball contained in the other. Equality or strict nesting cannot occur for a nonidentity deck element: the deck group is the free group of the graph, hence torsion-free by reduced words; infinitely many powers would then take one compact ball to a set meeting itself, contrary to the finite lifted-cell transporter property. Thus the ball is disjoint from all its nonidentity translates and projects as a parametrized PL ball bounded by the original sphere in $N$. [F3, F4, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 The projected boundary isotopies of steps 1.1–2.1 and the labelled boundary-fixed disk homotopies of step 3.1 carry every actual initial marked map into this one finite union $N$. Steps 4.1–6.1 did not substitute another relative class: they only parameterized and thickened its image. For every label both kinds of endpoint tracks stay in their original marked subsurfaces. Steps 7.1–8.1 give the stated intrinsic properties of the enclosure. The only use of the supplied deck involution was the genuine finite twofold diagram and its boundary metric cover; no mapping-torus monodromy was asserted to have finite order. This proves precisely the local projected-strip operation and none of the larger conclusions excluded in the Statement. [given, step 3.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1] ∎
