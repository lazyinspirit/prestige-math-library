## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $S$ be a compact connected oriented finite PL surface with nonempty boundary. Let $U\subset\partial S$ be a finite union of disjoint boundary circles and nondegenerate closed boundary intervals. Let $t:(S,U)\to(S,U)$ be a PL homeomorphism; it may reverse orientation and permute the components of $U$. Let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty finite collection of nontrivial free homotopy classes of circle maps and homotopy classes of arc maps with endpoints in $U$. Arc homotopies keep their endpoints in their respective components of $U$. Suppose $t$ permutes $\mathcal F$, allowing reversal of parametrization.

There are a compact finite PL subsurface $Q\subset S$, with $V=Q\cap U$, and a PL homeomorphism $t_1$ isotopic to $t$ through homeomorphisms of $(S,U)$ such that $t_1(Q)=Q$ and $t_1|Q$ has finite order. Every class of $\mathcal F$ has a representative in $(Q,V)$. Each component of $Q$ injects on fundamental groups into $S$, and an arc in $Q$ with endpoints in $V$ which is homotopic in $(S,U)$ into $U$ is already homotopic in $(Q,V)$ into $V$. Thus the carrier is incompressible and boundary-incompressible in the marked-pair sense. Components of $Q$ may be permuted.

This is a theorem about the given return homeomorphism, not a claim that it was a finite-order deck map. The ambient surface has boundary; no realization theorem for a general closed surface without invariant seam data is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite PL pair, return map and invariant finite family in the Statement.

[F1] Finite polygonal surface reduction supplies genus-with-boundary models, finite disk and band parameters, and disk extensions of prescribed boundary maps. Endpoint-fixed homotopic simple proper arcs have finite bigon and endpoint-bigon isotopies; disjoint labelled systems are treated simultaneously by empty-bigon deletion. The same circle argument, with its cyclic-cover disk descent, gives annular position for finite simple multicurves. [F2] Finite PL collars extend a boundary isotopy by using its time $\min(s,\lambda(r))$ at collar height $r$, with cutoff $\lambda=1$ at the incident face and $0$ at the other face; compatible prism subdivisions give the PL parameters. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]]

[F3] Universal covers lift homotopies. Van Kampen and graph-of-groups normal forms describe a surface cut at finitely many essential circles or proper arcs, retaining both faces and their basepaths. An open arc face has trivial group and a circle face has its infinite cyclic group. Positive reduced edge length cannot become a vertex-group element: multiplying by the inverse alleged vertex coefficient would contradict the reduced-word identity test. [F4] Under AC a sphere Beltrami coefficient of essential norm less than one has a normalized quasiconformal solution. Equal coefficients cancel under the quasiconformal chain rule; zero coefficient gives a conformal map. A proper simply connected plane domain has a Riemann map. ## Proof

1.1 We record the elementary low-complexity models. On a disk a boundary homeomorphism preserving finitely many marked intervals has a finite permutation of their cyclically ordered endpoints. Choose a circle parameter in which that permutation is a rotation or reflection, interpolate the boundary parameters on the complementary intervals, and extend over the disk by its cone parameter. The original map is isotopic to this model: first isotope the boundary maps, then use the disk extension and the radial Alexander isotopy on the remaining boundary-fixed disk map. These are finite PL cone subdivisions, not an assertion that a radial smooth formula is PL. On an annulus, cut at a spanning arc. A map is specified up to isotopy by its boundary component and cyclic endpoint permutations and one integer transverse winding; this follows by matching the cut arc with [F1] and then extending over the cut disk. If a finite invariant family contains a spanning arc class, some power fixes that class and all boundary labels. Its transverse winding is then zero, so its relative mapping class has finite order. In circle coordinates a finite-order class has the model $(x,r)\mapsto(\epsilon x+a,r)$ or $(x,r)\mapsto(\epsilon x+a,1-r)$, with $\epsilon=\pm1$ and rational $a$ whenever the first-coordinate action preserves orientation. To verify the compatibility rather than assume it, lift the two boundary maps to real lines: their translations, fixed by the endpoint cycles modulo integers, differ by the integer winding of the cut arc. A finite power with zero winding forces the two translations to agree after the permitted integer adjustment; if the boundaries are exchanged apply this test to the square. Thus those same finite circle models realize the original relative class, including proper marked intervals. If there is no spanning class, all arc classes are carried in the appropriate boundary collars and all nontrivial circle classes in a core collar. Take disjoint collars of both boundaries as necessary. The boundary circle models make the map finite on these collars; the middle annulus absorbs the original integer twist. Whole-circle boundary components allow boundary motion; proper marked intervals do not. Disk and annular carriers therefore have the asserted result. [given, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 We construct the essential carrier without choosing an invariant intersection order for immersed curves. Put $\mu=\partial U$; whole circle components contribute no points. Move the finitely many arc endpoints into the interiors of their labelled $U$ components. Double $S$ along its boundary and delete the points $\mu$ from the seam. Write $D$ for this doubled punctured surface and $j$ for its reflection. Each circle class contributes its two reflected copies; each arc contributes the closed class obtained by traversing it in one half and traversing its reflected inverse in the other. Denote this finite reflected collection by $\widehat{\mathcal F}$. The doubled homeomorphism commutes with $j$ and preserves that collection. The seam pieces between points of $\mu$ are open intervals; unpunctured seam components are circles. By [F3], a doubled arc nontrivial relative to its labelled $U$ interval has a reduced two-edge word, unless it is peripheral about a deleted seam point. In particular no winding around a proper marked interval has been silently forgotten. [given, F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Suppose now $\chi(S)<0$; step 1.1 handled the other connected orientable boundary surfaces. Choose a reflection-invariant complete hyperbolic metric on $D$ by the following finite construction. Decompose $S$ into pants and build each from two right-angled hyperbolic hexagons, gluing equal cuffs. At a boundary circle bearing $m>0$ points of $\mu$, replace its outside collar by a crown: in the hyperbolic plane take a loxodromic axis of the cuff length, choose $m$ cyclically ordered ideal points in a fundamental interval on one side, repeat them by the translation, and join consecutive ideal points by geodesics. The region between the axis and this invariant chain has the required cuff and $m$ ideal boundary vertices. Glue its cuff to the old one. Doubling along the remaining geodesic boundary segments gives complete cusps at the deleted seam points and makes $j$ an isometric reflection. All choices use finite polygon parameters; no averaging of negatively curved metrics is used. The geodesic disk/bigon argument proves uniqueness of a closed geodesic in every nonperipheral free class. A peripheral class is represented by a sufficiently small horocycle. Completeness gives the usual disk development of each universal cover: continue the hyperbolic charts along paths, use cancellation on a finite homotopy grid for path independence, and lift each finite hyperbolic segment through complete charts. The resulting complete local isometry onto the hyperbolic disk is a covering and hence an isometry. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Take the finite graph of the geodesic representatives of $\widehat{\mathcal F}$, retaining multiplicity labels but using the geometric support. Equal primitive supports are one graph curve. Add small disjoint horocycles for its peripheral classes. Use a reflection-invariant finite disk-and-band neighborhood and fill its complementary disks which contain no puncture. A complementary annulus between two existing components is not filled: twists there must remain outside the carrier. Include a peripheral end only to the extent of its collar; its compactification will be understood below. Call the resulting surface $R$. Its frontier circles are essential or peripheral; the annular components are the explicit collar cases of step 1.1. Tightening the other frontier circles to geodesics gives convex lifted components. Any essential subsurface carrying $\widehat{\mathcal F}$ can be tightened in this manner. Its convex lifts contain the unique corresponding geodesics; peripheral representatives can be put arbitrarily far into their cusp collars. It therefore contains the graph neighborhood up to collars. It also contains each filled disk: a frontier component trapped inside that disk would be inessential. This proves that $R$ is contained, up to collars and isotopy, in every essential carrier. Applying this assertion to the doubled image of $R$ and then to its inverse image proves that the two have the same isotopy class. Parallel frontier curves are kept as separate boundary copies and moved into their incident sides by disjoint small collars; this does not fill the annulus between them. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 These carrier isotopies can be made reflection-equivariant. For paired frontier circles use paired bigons and their disk parameters. A reflection-invariant frontier circle cuts into proper arcs in a half-surface; use the corresponding endpoint-bigon and strip parameters there and double them. Endpoints stay in the same seam interval and never cross a point of $\mu$. This gives an ambient isotopy of $(S,U)$ which makes the original return preserve $Q=R\cap S$, after compactifying its boundary cusp corners. The geodesic representatives of a nonperipheral doubled arc meet the seam in its two reduced-word crossings: any further crossings would give a reducible edge pair and hence a geodesic bigon. Its half is a representative of the original arc. Peripheral doubled arcs use the actual half-cusp disk. Thus $Q$ carries the actual family, not only some other classes with the same endpoints. It is injective on fundamental groups by the cut normal forms in [F3]. For relative boundary incompressibility, an arc of $Q$ homotopic into $U$ has a null doubled word. Normal form forces its half to be homotopic into its incident seam interval or circle. Since the endpoint tracks lie in one actual $U$ component, this is a path in $Q\cap U$. This proves the marked relative assertion, including the disk half-cusp cases. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 We next prove finite order of the mapping class, before proving actual periodic realization. In each negative-Euler component of $R$, its graph representatives fill up to disks and peripheral collars. A simple curve with at most $b$ intersections with that graph has only finitely many isotopy classes. Cut it at its crossings and record the successive oriented graph edges and the connecting arcs in the complementary polygons. There are at most $b$ letters, finitely many edge choices and finitely many pairings in each disk. In a peripheral collar, endpoints free on the peripheral boundary absorb a full boundary turn; a simple returning arc has only the two possible boundary-side disk classes, not an arbitrary winding. Equivalently use shortest geodesic representatives: each individual geometric intersection number is attained simultaneously with the fixed geodesic graph. Hence a bound on the sum of intersection numbers with $\widehat{\mathcal F}$ gives this finite word set. The return permutes that sum. Choose a pants decomposition and finitely many simple transverse curves crossing each of its cuffs. Some power of the return fixes all these classes. Isotope it to fix the pants curves, their sides, and their cyclic labels. On a cut pant choose two seams; matching them by [F1] leaves only integer collar windings, followed by a boundary-fixed disk map. Consequently the original power is a product of cuff twists, up to allowed peripheral-boundary motion. Each transverse curve detects its own cuff winding in the cut annular parameter, so all these integers are zero. This power is isotopic to the identity. Pants components themselves have only the finite boundary permutations; annular and disk components are step 1.1. Taking a common power and the finite component permutation proves that the doubled return has finite order in the mapping class group of $R$, with punctures retained and its frontier boundaries allowed to move. [given, F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 For clarity we supply the finite realization construction required for that finite mapping-class group. The following finite graph fact has a constructive proof. A graph vertex $v$ is dominated by $w\ne v$ if its closed neighborhood is contained in that of $w$. A finite graph is dismantlable if successive deletions of dominated vertices leave one vertex. Deleting any dominated vertex preserves dismantlability: in a dismantling order redirect a reference to the deleted vertex to its dominator, and, whenever that dominator was deleted earlier, follow its own domination reference. If the reference reaches the vertex being considered, the two remaining closed neighborhoods are equal and exchange their positions in the order. Otherwise it reaches a later vertex. Each redirection retains the required neighborhood inclusion and terminates in the finite order. A dismantlable finite graph has an automorphism-invariant clique. If all distinct vertices have different closed neighborhoods, delete all dominated vertices simultaneously; each is dominated by an undeleted maximal neighborhood, so the smaller invariant graph remains dismantlable. If two neighborhoods coincide, identify all such equivalence classes, which are cliques; the quotient is an induced graph obtained by dominated deletions. Induct on vertex count and take the preimage of the final invariant clique. This proves the graph fact rather than citing a fixed-point theorem for an arbitrary contractible complex. [F1, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 Here is the surgery input for that graph fact. On a compact negative-Euler surface with boundary use a pants metric with geodesic boundary. Each essential proper arc class with endpoints free in its incident boundary components has a unique shortest orthogonal geodesic. The existence, uniqueness and disjointness arguments are the compact path-limit and geodesic-bigon arguments of [F1]; the endpoint variation gives orthogonality. Fix an arc $\sigma$. For an intersecting arc $\rho$, an initial segment of $\sigma$ up to its first meeting with $\rho$ cuts $\rho$ into two new essential arcs. Their classes form a surgery pair. Each is disjoint from $\rho$, their intersections with $\sigma$ decrease, and the pair depends only on the two original classes. If the classes are disjoint, use the repeated pair $(\sigma,\sigma)$. For any finite set of arc classes, use the first crossing on an initial segment of $\sigma$ meeting the whole geodesic family. The corresponding arc $\rho$ is exposed: every member disjoint from $\rho$ is disjoint from both surgery outputs, since the initial segment misses the entire family. Surgery has no directed cycle, because intersections with $\sigma$ strictly decrease, followed by the last step to $\sigma$. These statements are invariant under every surface homeomorphism, including reversals. [F1, F3, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 A finite set convex under this surgery rule induces a dismantlable graph: convex means that each surgery pair of an arc in the set has at least one output in the set. Choose exposed vertices successively in each remaining subset, omitting $\sigma$ until last. Its proposed dominator may have been removed already. Replace such a reference by the earlier removed vertex's proposed dominator. The directed surgery references have no cycles, so this replacement terminates at a remaining vertex. Neighborhood inclusion is preserved along the replacements: every remaining neighbor of the original vertex is a neighbor of the old dominator and hence of its replacement. Thus each deletion is genuinely dominated in the current induced graph. Convexity and acyclicity also show $\sigma$ is in the set: otherwise every vertex has an outgoing surgery reference in a finite set. Now let $H$ be a finite group of arc-graph automorphisms coming from mapping classes. Start with an orbit $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_k$ and let $L$ be the largest geodesic length in that orbit. The set $A$ of essential arc classes with shortest length at most $2L$ is finite. Indeed lift the initial endpoints to finitely many compact boundary intervals in a fundamental chart system. A length bound places the other endpoints in a compact region of the universal cover, meeting only finitely many lifted boundary sides. Each pair of distinct sides has one shortest orthogonal connection; a connection to the same side is inessential. Each surgery toward $\sigma_i$ has an output of length at most $2L$, since one of the two pieces of the old arc has length at most $L$. Hence $A$ is convex for all these surgery rules. Its finite orbit union $HA$ is convex for the rule toward $\sigma_1$: translate an alleged pair back by the orbit element and use convexity toward its inverse image of $\sigma_1$. It is therefore dismantlable and has an $H$-invariant clique by step 7.1. This is the promised actual invariant disjoint arc system. [F1, F3, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 We extend the invariant arc system to a finite invariant filling graph; its new vertices need not be old punctures. This distinction retains reversals. By [F1] choose representatives of the mapping classes preserving the initial arc system, with constant-speed edge maps. Their compositions agree on the graph. An identity-class map fixing it has an isotopy fixing it: its incident cut arcs retain their words after regluing, so [F3] and [F1] match them relative to the graph before extending on the remaining disks. A peripheral collar twist invisible to these words is unwound at its puncture. There is no unanchored closed graph component, since every initial edge ends at a puncture; any twist about a nonperipheral boundary of its regular neighborhood is detected by a crossing cut arc. The same test persists after graph refinement, since every new edge attaches to that anchored graph or to an old puncture. Thus we obtain the finite induced group on each cut component with its actual boundary graph parameters. For every non-disk cut component apply step 9.1 after collapsing its boundary circles to punctures; retain old punctures too. An annular component has its unique essential spanning arc class and is included directly. Choose simple disjoint representatives of the new invariant classes, retaining one edge per class and the cyclic order of their ends on each cut-boundary circle. That order is determined by their disjoint isotopy classes; the group preserves it or reverses it according to its surface orientation. Record each movable endpoint by its real winding coordinate on that circle. Simplicity and the specified noncrossing order are precisely the linear cyclic-order inequalities: for two ends based initially at the same point they are, in the appropriate order, $x_i\le x_j\le x_i+1$. Their interior version puts the finitely many ends at distinct positions in that cyclic order and is nonempty, by a small boundary slide of the original disjoint arcs. The group acts by translations and signed coordinate permutations with its actual boundary displacements. Its composition rule on these real coordinates follows from the graph-fixed isotopy test above; a hidden full boundary turn would be a detected collar twist. It preserves this interior order cell, not just its closure. Average an orbit in that cell. The fixed point remains in the cell, since a finite average preserves each strict linear inequality. If a new arc lies in an annular cut component, quotient the two endpoint coordinates by their simultaneous opposite shift; the same averaging applies to its slope, with its single essential edge. KEEP these endpoint positions, even if they are in old edge interiors. Subdivide the old graph there and adjoin the new arcs as edges. Distinct new ends on each cut boundary have not collapsed. The inherited cyclic orders therefore give genuine cyclic or reversed cyclic link maps, including when a new endpoint lies at an old vertex. The new finite graph action is exact: its old edge maps and its fixed endpoint data prescribe the new edge permutations and their orientations; [F1] matches the intervening disjoint arcs relative to those data. The cut-word test just given again makes the composition isotopies fix the enlarged graph. No rounding operation on endpoint coordinates is used. In particular a reflection fixing an edge midpoint causes no non-equivariant choice between its two endpoints. For termination, truncate the old punctures temporarily to boundary circles. For each current cut component $C$ use the lexicographic sum of $\max(0,-\chi(C))$ and the number of annular components, omitting disk components. A first essential arc in a nonannular component decreases the first integer. A first essential arc in an annulus cuts it to a disk and decreases the second. Further members of the same finite disjoint orbit can become boundary-parallel after the preceding cuts, but then only split off disks and never increase these integers. They cannot undo that first strict decrease. A decrease of the first integer creates at most finitely many annuli, so the lexicographic process is finite. We end with a finite graph whose complementary components are disks and whose cyclic orders, puncture labels, and edge parameters have a genuine $H$ action. This is the filling graph needed for realization, without asserting a stronger fixed filling clique with endpoints at the old punctures. [F1, F2, F3, step 9.1, construct]

11.1 Extend that finite graph action over its disk faces. Give each abstract face boundary the inherited edge-length parameter. Its stabilizer acts by rotations or reflections of this parametrized circle, because it preserves or reverses the cyclic order. Cone this circle action to a face center, using finite polygon subdivisions; do this once on each face orbit and transport it. Repeated vertices or sides of a cut-open face retain their labels before gluing. The resulting maps match on every graph edge and have the exact group law. Their cyclic or reversed cyclic link maps at graph vertices show they are homeomorphisms of the whole surface. At a puncture, give its finite ray fan equally spaced angular coordinates. Its finite stabilizer acts cyclically or dihedrally on that fan, so the maps can be chosen to be literal rotations or conjugate rotations in a small complex disk about the puncture. This gives an actual finite PL surface action representing the original classes. We can also make this action hyperbolic without adding an analytic realization theorem. Choose a reference complete pants-and-cusp hyperbolic model for the punctured surface and take the preceding finite polygon parameters piecewise smooth on its compact core and literal complex rotations or reflections near its punctures. The finitely many maps are quasiconformal with a common bound: their compact-core affine charts have bounded nonsingular linear parts, and their cusp-disk maps are conformal or anticonformal. Average the pullbacks of a reference metric tensor over this now actual group. The resulting invariant measurable conformal structure has a uniformly bounded Beltrami coefficient, zero in the chosen puncture disks. Lift it to the reference hyperbolic disk and extend it by zero outside that disk. By [F4] its normalized sphere solution maps the disk to a proper simply connected plane domain, and the invariant-coefficient chain rule makes deck maps and the actual group lifts conformal or anticonformal there. A reference deck transformation preserves the extended coefficient on the whole sphere, since it is Möbius and preserves both sides of the reference circle. Its conjugate by the sphere solution is therefore globally Möbius. The Riemann map of the plane domain to the disk supplies the invariant complete hyperbolic model. Its peripheral generators are parabolic: if one were hyperbolic, its cyclic quotient would be a finite-modulus annulus. The original punctured disk chart, where the coefficient is zero, would then give a holomorphic map from a punctured disk into that annulus with nonzero winding. In bounded annulus coordinates the map and its inverse-coordinate value are bounded, so their Laurent series extend across the puncture with a nonzero value, giving zero winding instead. This contradiction verifies the cusp type without presupposing the desired complete cusp metric. Pull this metric back to the original surface: its acting maps remain the already constructed finite PL maps; the metric is auxiliary. Boundary circles initially collapsed to punctures are restored by invariant horocycle truncations, using finite invariant cusp fans to identify the new boundary circles with PL circles. Finite cusp fans fill any marked boundary corners. This proves realization for surfaces with boundary or punctures; no assertion about fixed boundary points is inferred merely from collapsing a circle. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 10.1, construct]

12.1 We need one closed case, which has additional invariant seam data. A closed component of $R$ is the entire double and its nonempty disjoint seam multicurve is invariant in homotopy classes. Match this multicurve by [F1], cut there, and apply step 11.1 on the punctured cut pieces. Choose equal small invariant horocycles for paired punctures. Marked gluings across the paired horocycles are described by real twist coordinates, one for each seam. The group acts on these coordinates by signed affine permutations: the integer part is the actual collar winding of the original mapping class, not an arbitrary choice of lift. Composition of original mapping classes is exactly composition of these affine maps; this is checked by adding their lifted boundary displacements. The average of an orbit supplies a fixed gluing. At that gluing the already genuine piece actions match on the horocycles and paste to a genuine finite action representing the original classes. A nontrivial pure integer twist cannot lie in this finite group, since its powers have distinct winding, so no invisible twist kernel has been discarded. This constructs the action, before uniformization. To have isometries available below, give the collar gluings smooth product metrics and average over this now actual finite action. Relative to a reference pants hyperbolic structure the resulting compact conformal structure has Beltrami norm $k<1$. Lift its coefficient to the reference disk and put it zero outside that disk. By [F4] a normalized sphere solution maps the disk onto a proper simply connected plane domain. The invariant Beltrami chain rule makes both deck transformations and lifts of the finite action conformal or anticonformal there. Its Riemann map to the disk gives the required invariant hyperbolic metric, pulled back to the surface carrying the actual finite PL action. This uses [F4], not an unproved general uniformization theorem. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 11.1, construct]

13.1 Apply this construction to the finite group generated by the return class from step 6.1 and the reflection class $j$. They commute, so the group is finite. Use step 11.1 for components with boundary or punctures and step 12.1 only for the closed double case. If $j$ exchanges two components, choose the construction on one and transport it to the other by $j$; there is no fixed seam in this case. On a component meeting the seam, the actual reflected seams are retained for the following reason. A negative-Euler surface group is center-free: in the disk model a nontrivial element has its axis or its parabolic fixed point, and two noncommuting elements cannot have the same commuting one-parameter group. Choose a lift of the original reflection fixing a point of a seam. Its induced automorphism squares to the identity. The realized reflection has a unique lift inducing that same automorphism; its square is a central deck transformation, hence identity. It is an orientation-reversing order-two isometry of the disk and therefore a reflection. For a closed seam its automorphism fixes the seam generator, so its fixed line is that generator's axis. For a proper seam retain its two based peripheral subgroups with their actual marked end paths. The original reflection normalizes each, possibly inverting its primitive generator. The realized lift induces that same automorphism, so it preserves each corresponding parabolic subgroup. Each has its unique ideal fixed point by step 11.1; thus the lift fixes both ideal endpoints, and its fixed line is their joining geodesic. At a frontier endpoint use the restored peripheral cusp before filling its finite corner fan. No boundary extension of a general quasiconformal disk map is used. Thus the realized involution fixes the whole corresponding seam system pointwise, not by a possible glide along it. Its two incident sides have the original labels. Peripheral corner cases are the truncated half-cusp sectors, and disk or annular pieces are step 1.1. [F1, F3, step 6.1, step 11.1, step 12.1, construct]

14.1 We explain the passage back to the original pair, rather than restrict an arbitrary isotopy of doubles. First isotope the realized seam system to the old one by the finite circle and arc moves of [F1], retaining the puncture and boundary labels. Write $j'$ for the resulting reflection. Two reflections fixing this seam pointwise and exchanging its sides are conjugate by a map $h$ which is the identity on one half and the composite $jj'$ on the other. Since $[j']=[j]$, the class $[h]$ commutes with $[j]$. Thus the class of a map supported on one half equals that of its reflection, supported on the other half. Here is the resulting twist test. Every interior pants curve and transversal in the first half is fixed in class by the map supported on the other half. Its homotopy class within the first half is also fixed: a reduced conjugacy between nonperipheral vertex-group words in [F3] stays in that vertex group, as otherwise its first cyclic or trivial edge group would contain the nonperipheral word. Matching these pants curves and transversals as in step 6.1 leaves only twists parallel to boundary circles of the cut-open half. For a whole circular seam the two parallel curves are isotopic, and reflection takes its twist to the inverse twist. Commutation therefore gives $2n=0$ for its integer exponent. For a half-boundary circle interrupted by deleted seam points, its curve and the reflected curve are distinct: the intervening punctures prevent an annulus between them. Their twists have independent exponents, detected by the corresponding finite transverse arcs; a map supported on just one side has zero exponent on the other, so equality with its reflection forces both exponents zero. The same test applies to a half-boundary meeting a free frontier; if the two parallel curves cobound an unpunctured annulus, they are the whole-circle case already considered, or a disk/annular half covered by step 1.1. Twists at free frontier circles are allowed boundary motion. Open seam arcs introduce no additional integer, since their edge groups are trivial. The finite cut-arc and disk-extension test of [F1] now gives an isotopy of $h$ to the identity with the retained labels. We can consequently conjugate the realized reflection to the original $j$ without changing any mapping class. On reflection-exchanged components the conjugacy is instead prescribed on one component and transported to its mate. The realized return then commutes with $j$ and preserves the prescribed half and its $U$ segments. [F1, F2, F3, step 6.1, step 13.1, construct]

15.1 The restricted return has the original pair mapping class. Here is the relative test needed at proper marked intervals. Take a finite cut-arc system in the half, retaining paths from a seam basepoint to every other seam component. If two half maps commuting with reflection have the same doubled mapping class, the normal forms of these paths agree. Across an interval face the edge group is trivial, so the two half paths agree relative to their actual labelled endpoint intervals. Across a whole circular face the only discrepancy is a power of its boundary word, exactly the allowed motion on that whole circle. To see that no simultaneous conjugator has been lost, it must normalize both incident half groups. A nonabelian vertex group cannot fix a second vertex in the coset tree, since the first edge stabilizer on that path is cyclic or trivial. Its normalizer is therefore that vertex group. The simultaneous conjugator lies in the incident edge group, giving exactly the preceding alternatives. At all other seams the retained connecting paths give the same test, and detect twists at their marked intervals as well.
