## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $p:S'\to S$ be a supplied twofold PL covering of compact oriented finite PL surfaces with nonempty boundary, and let $\tau$ be its actual deck involution. The cover may be disconnected. Let $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ be pairwise disjoint properly embedded finite PL arcs in $S'$. Suppose their $2r$ endpoints have distinct images in $S$. There is an ambient PL isotopy of $S'$, fixed on its boundary, taking this arc family to $A$ such that the projected family $p(A)$ has only transverse double crossings and has no embedded disk bigon: no disk in $S$ has boundary two subarcs of $p(A)$ with distinct corners and otherwise disjoint shores. The projected family also has no disk monogon. The endpoint labels and the homotopy class of each arc relative to its two endpoints are retained. Moreover there is an ambient homeomorphism of $S$, fixed on its boundary, carrying the labelled projected PL graph to geodesic arcs for an auxiliary curvature $-1$ metric with strictly convex boundary on the components met by those arcs. Every subarc of each such labelled geodesic is the unique shortest path in its endpoint-fixed homotopy class.

This concerns arcs in a given free twofold action. It makes no periodic-representative assertion about an arbitrary surface homeomorphism preserving homotopy classes.

## Proof

1.1 We first record the finite arc form of the bigon argument. Proper simple arcs in a finite oriented surface, with distinct fixed endpoints, which are homotopic relative to their endpoints are ambient PL isotopic relative to the boundary. Here is the disk construction. Lift their endpoint-fixed homotopy to the universal surface. Every compact subset of a simply connected covering surface lies in a disk: take a finite connected vertex-disk/edge-band neighborhood, fill compact complementary components and add bands joining repeated boundary interfaces; its remaining boundary circles inject into the outside, so its graph-spine rank must be zero. The finite neighborhood is therefore a disk. In that disk a returning excursion between two lifted arcs cuts off a disk by planar separation. Choose an innermost such disk, first with respect to the full lifted second family and then the full lifted first family. Its interior misses both families, and its two shores project injectively, since the original arcs are simple; its corners are distinct. Its boundary projects injectively as well, by the empty-shore choice. The disk projects injectively: two deck translates of its lifted closed disk with disjoint boundaries would be nested or disjoint; strict nesting gives infinitely many translates meeting a compact disk, contrary to the finite lifted-cell transporter property. Equality would identify its boundary points, also impossible. This is an actual embedded bigon downstairs. Its disk parameter slides one shore across it and removes two interior crossings. When the two shores share a prescribed endpoint, the innermost disk may instead have one corner at that endpoint and one at an interior crossing. The same disk slide, fixed on its boundary-side vertex, removes that one interior crossing. These endpoint bigons decrease the same finite crossing count. If no crossings remain, the homotopy lifts to a disk strip between the two disjoint arcs, and the same disk-descent argument supplies the strip downstairs. Sliding through it finishes the isotopy, fixed on the arc endpoints. Small boundary collars make the slides fixed on the entire surface boundary. For finitely many disjoint labelled arcs, choose the bigon empty for the two whole families: an additional arc enters through one shore and an innermost such excursion gives a smaller bigon. The finite crossing count drops. The final disjoint strips are then slid in their order, retaining previously matched arcs. Boundary-parallel arcs use their actual boundary-side disks in this same construction; they require no essential-circle convention. [given, F1, F4, construct]

1.2 Choose an auxiliary constant-curvature $-1$ metric with strictly convex boundary on each component of $S$ met by the projected arcs. Such a component has nonempty boundary because the arcs are proper. This has an explicit finite construction. A disk is a hyperbolic round disk. An annulus is a closed small metric tube around the core of a hyperbolic cylinder. For an orientable genus-$g$, $b$-boundary model with $2g+b>2$, cut its $g$ handle meridians and then split the resulting planar surface into $2g+b-2$ pairs of pants; these are the finite polygon cuts of [F1]. In the Poincare disk take a regular hexagon. Its angle depends continuously on its circumradius, tends to $2\pi/3$ as that radius tends to zero and to zero as it tends to one, so one radius gives angle $\pi/2$. Two copies glued along alternating sides give a hyperbolic pair of pants with three equal geodesic cuffs. Glue these equal cuffs according to the given pants cuts. Reflection across a geodesic cuff gives the matching smooth hyperbolic charts. Add a sufficiently small outward collar to each remaining cuff. In normal coordinates its metric is $dr^2+\cosh^2(r)\,ds^2$; the outer curve $r=-\delta$ has inward geodesic curvature $\tanh\delta>0$. Reparametrizing the collar identifies the enlarged surface with the original one. The construction uses a finite polygonal model and its finitely many cell parameters; the metric is auxiliary and does not assert that a radial formula or a geodesic is PL. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 Each fixed-endpoint homotopy class of paths has a shortest representative for this metric. Take paths with lengths decreasing to the infimum and parametrize them with uniformly bounded speed on $[0,1]$. Compactness and [F2] give a uniform limit. In finitely many sufficiently small normal balls, including the locally convex boundary half-balls, uniformly close paths have the same endpoint-fixed homotopy class by joining corresponding points inside those balls. Thus the limit is in the original class. Length is lower semicontinuous, since every finite partition sum is continuous in the uniform topology, so it attains the infimum. Local replacement by the unique short geodesic proves that it is a geodesic; strict boundary convexity excludes interior contact with the boundary. Two distinct representatives of the same class would have a monogon or a bigon in the lifted compact disk of step 1.1. A geodesic monogon has one exterior angle less than $2\pi$; a nondegenerate geodesic bigon has two exterior angles whose sum is less than $2\pi$. Since $K=-1$, either contradicts [F3]. A coincident geodesic segment extends uniquely until the endpoints by the local geodesic equation and gives the same representative. Thus the shortest representative is unique. For clarity, disjointness uses the endpoint order in that disk. Two lifted original simple arcs with four distinct boundary endpoints have noninterlacing endpoints. The same holds for distinct translates of one arc. A transverse crossing of their corresponding geodesic lifts therefore has even parity, whereas two crossings would give an innermost geodesic bigon. Thus there are no crossings. A self-crossing would be an intersection with a distinct translate, so is excluded as well. This proves that classes having disjoint simple representatives have disjoint simple geodesic representatives. A subsegment of a shortest representative is shortest in its own endpoint-fixed class, since replacing it by a shorter path would shorten the original representative; the uniqueness argument just given applies to these subsegments too. These arguments involve the two arcs and their compact homotopies, not a simultaneous straightening of arbitrary closed immersed curves. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 Pull the metric back to $S'$. Its deck involution is an isometry because it is a deck map of this supplied metric cover. Replace the $a_i$ by their unique geodesics $g_i$ in their fixed-endpoint classes. They are pairwise disjoint by step 2.1; their translates are precisely the corresponding geodesics $\tau g_i$. No member can share a nontrivial segment with a translated member. Unique continuation would make them the same maximal boundary-to-boundary geodesic, which would identify their endpoint sets after projection, contrary to the Given endpoint condition. The compact geodesic arcs therefore have finitely many crossings: in hyperbolic charts intersections of distinct geodesic segments are isolated, and an accumulation on their compact parameter products would give a common tangent and then a shared segment. Each of the two families is disjoint, so there are no triple crossings. A projected disk monogon or bigon lifts to one in this metric cover or its universal cover and contradicts step 2.1. [given, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Convert this finite geodesic graph into a finite PL graph with exactly the same labelled crossing data. Around each crossing choose a small disjoint chart disk meeting just its two transverse arcs, and give it two crossing polygonal diameters in the same cyclic order. Choose these disks in deck pairs. There is no invariant crossing disk: an invariant crossing point would be fixed by the free deck involution. Outside them, choose disjoint thin strip neighborhoods of the finitely many remaining embedded arc segments and replace each by a polygonal core with the same endpoints; lift these choices from quotient chart disks or take one representative of each two-element deck orbit. Subdivide the finite polygons and fill their matching boundary parameters by [F1]. The replacements can be made by ambient homeomorphisms supported on these disks and strips and fixed on their outside boundaries: in a crossing disk extend the four matching radial sectors across their disk parameters; in a strip extend the two matching rectangles across their disk parameters. Extend each map by the identity outside its support. Their composition is an ambient homeomorphism, fixed on the surface boundary, between the original geodesic graph and the new graph. No assertion about the topology of the complementary regions is needed. Thus the projected crossing pattern and absence of disk monogons or bigons persist, and the inverse homeomorphism supplies the stated geodesic marking. Let $A$ be the resulting PL family upstairs; its translates are exactly the other half of the graph. [F1, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The labelled $A$ arcs are simple, disjoint and in the same endpoint-fixed homotopy classes as the initial $a_i$. Step 1.1 supplies the simultaneous ambient PL isotopy of $S'$ to them, fixed on $\partial S'$. The projected endpoint labels remain distinct, and step 4.1 proves every asserted crossing and bigon conclusion. The empty family requires the identity isotopy. Components not met by an arc require no metric or isotopy. We used a metric lifted from the quotient, never the unsupported claim that averaging an arbitrary metric preserves negative curvature, and never a periodic realization theorem for an abstract mapping class. [given, step 1.1, step 4.1] ∎
