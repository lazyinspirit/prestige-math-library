## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable irreducible finite PL three-manifold with nonempty boundary. Let $P\subset\partial M$ be a finite PL subsurface, each component injective on fundamental groups. Let $W\subset\partial M$ be a finite disjoint union of parametrized rectangles with two opposite end intervals in $P$ and no other intersection with $P$. Every component of $W$ has a spanning path which is not homotopic into $P$ with its endpoint tracks in $P$.

Let $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ be a nonempty finite family of maps of rectangles. Each maps its two opposite end edges into $P$ and its other two edges as spanning paths into labelled components of $W$. Suppose its paths between the end edges are not homotopic into $P$, and its paths between the other edges are not homotopic into $W$, with the respective endpoint tracks in that marked surface. These are the two relative essentialness conditions. Let $W$ consist of the components actually used by this family; extra unmarked boundary windows impose no requirement. The rectangle maps need not be embedded, immersed, disjoint, or mutually inequivalent.

There is a compact embedded finite PL interval-bundle region $N\to B$ with finitely many components and with every base component a compact surface with nonempty boundary. Its whole horizontal boundary is $N\cap P$, and $N\cap W$ is a union of vertical rectangles. Every interval fiber is essential relative to $P$. Every $f_i$ is homotopic through such marked rectangle maps into $N$. Every component of $N$ injects on fundamental groups into $M$. In addition, the horizontal pair $(N\cap P,N\cap P\cap W)$ is incompressible and boundary-incompressible in $(P,P\cap W)$: a path in the former horizontal surface homotopic in $P$ into one actual end interval of $W$ is already homotopic into its intersection with that interval.

The conclusion is a carrier for the actual finite family. It does not assert uniqueness of a characteristic submanifold, nor descent of a circle-fibered Seifert region.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite PL data and both relative essentialness conditions in the Statement, with the actual whole used windows as the marking $W$.

## Proof

1.1 We first specify the elementary sphere and disk operations used throughout. A proper disk in a parametrized ball cuts it into two parametrized balls. Cap its boundary by either boundary disk; those boundary disks have the finite polygon parameters of [F4]. Push the cap slightly into the boundary collar and use finite PL Schoenflies in the supplied standard ball compactification. The side adjacent to the boundary window is a parametrized ball; window removal in [F4] parametrizes the other side. This also supplies finite disk slides and boundary-pocket pushes. In particular, if an innermost disk of a transverse sphere lies in an irreducible piece and its boundary bounds a disk in an injective gluing face, their union gives a boundary-attached ball. Shrink that ball in its window parameter and push it across the opposite face collar. The sphere cap moves across, while any other sheets entering the window are carried to that same side. The chosen circle disappears; circles inside its window disappear or persist without a new circle outside the window. Thus the total circle count strictly decreases. Minimizing that count proves that gluing irreducible pieces along injective surfaces preserves irreducibility. It includes disk windows, whose groups are trivial. This is the actual pocket move, rather than an inference from a general handle attachment. Applying it backwards to any finite lift of the hierarchy in [F3] proves that every connected finite cover of $M$ is irreducible: its final pieces are covers of balls and hence balls. The same holds for the irreducible carriers used below. Their second homotopy groups vanish by [F3]. [given, F2, F3, F4, F5, construct]

2.1 Make the rectangle maps proper and finite PL by relative approximation and boundary collars. The common-carrier proof of [F2] applies simultaneously to the finitely many end, side and corner subcomplexes. A finite bottom-and-side prism retraction extends the edge homotopies over each rectangle, retaining those marks. Normalize their $W$ sides as disjoint levels, retaining their whole original $W$ labels. For each such side attach to its source rectangle a SEPARATE copy of the entire corresponding $W$ disk along that spanning interval. Distinct copies remain distinct even when their images agree. A disk attachment along an interval preserves simple connectedness by van Kampen. Connect these finitely many complexes by a tree of source intervals mapped to interior paths. The resulting fixed finite connected source $C$ is simply connected. Denote its image by $J$. The image contains every whole used window, not just disconnected thin bands in it. Triangulate the image and the source once so the map is simplicial, retaining all source vertices and their image-simplex labels for the rest of the proof. The extra disk copies and connecting tree are part of this fixed source; they will never be dropped from the complexity count. [given, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Choose a compact manifold neighborhood of $J$ by [F2]. Near a used window its trace includes the whole window: in its finite product charts choose the boundary neighborhood first and extend it into an inward collar before applying the interior level-set neighborhood construction. The finite polygon subdivisions match the two traces. Thus no thin-band component has been substituted for an actual window. Enlarge this neighborhood by adding a parametrized ball whenever its relative frontier is either a sphere or one disk in the relative interior of the current neighborhood, with the complementary boundary disk in $P$ in the latter case. Its window charts keep the union a finite PL manifold. Such a ball contains every component of the current complement which it meets: a path in that complement cannot cross its frontier. It contains at least one such component if it enlarges the neighborhood. The finite number of initial complement components therefore strictly decreases, so the process stops. Call the carrier $V$, with $P_V=V\cap P$. The window trace is unchanged. The image group $\pi_1(J)\to\pi_1(V)$ is onto. The initial neighborhood retracts onto $J$; at a ball addition, its intersection with the connected current carrier is connected. Indeed a path in the carrier from a point inside the ball to its connected frontier first leaves the ball through that frontier. All intersection components therefore meet it. Van Kampen for attachment of this simply connected ball adds no generator, even if the intersection is larger than the stated frontier disk or sphere. [F2, F4, F5, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 This carrier is irreducible. Any interior sphere bounds a parametrized ball in the ambient irreducible manifold, and maximality in step 3.1 puts that ball in $V$. Also $P_V$ injects on fundamental groups into $V$. Otherwise apply the actual Loop theorem [F2] to a nontrivial kernel word to obtain a proper disk whose boundary is essential in $P_V$. That boundary is null in ambient $M$, hence in $P$ by its assumed injection, so it bounds a disk there. The two disks give a boundary-attached parametrized ball by the pocket construction of step 1.1. Maximality would put its boundary disk in $P_V$, contradicting essentialness. This argument uses Loop to obtain the simple kernel curve; it does not assume arbitrary kernel words are simple. Used window spans and all original rectangles remain essential, since a homotopy in the smaller marked carrier would be a homotopy in the original marked manifold. Its boundary is nonempty, and [F3] applies. [given, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 If $V$ has a connected twofold cover, lift the fixed simply connected source $C$. Every attached full window disk lifts with its rectangle side, so the lifted image again contains every whole window it uses. Form its augmented carrier as in steps 3.1–4.1 inside that cover; the cover is irreducible by step 1.1. This process terminates. For an explicit bound count pairs of distinct ORIGINAL source vertices with equal images. A lift can separate an equal-image pair but cannot identify a previously distinct pair. If none separates in a genuine twofold step, the lifted source map gives a well-defined section on its current image $J$: equal image vertices have equal lifts, and unique lifting on each labelled image simplex makes the simplex sections agree. The cover character would vanish on $\pi_1(J)$, contradicting its surjection onto $\pi_1(V)$. At least one pair therefore separates. The count is bounded by the square of the fixed original vertex count. Carrier triangulations may subdivide image cells; the counted original vertices and their labelled simplices remain unchanged. At the last carrier no connected twofold cover exists, so [F2] makes all its boundary components spheres. An irreducible connected manifold with a spherical boundary component is a parametrized ball: push that component inward, take its interior ball side, and restore the boundary collar. Thus the tower has an actual ball endpoint. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 In this last ball the horizontal components are disks, by their injection from step 4.1 and finite surface reduction. A closed spherical component would be the whole boundary sphere, leaving no essential window span, so does not occur. Each whole window joins two DISTINCT such disks, since its span is essential relative to them. Collapse the horizontal disks to vertices and the windows to edges in the boundary sphere; keep its actual planar cyclic orders. Each original essential rectangle uses two distinct parallel edges: its horizontal paths force the same two end vertices, and using one edge twice would be inessential relative to that whole window in a ball. Groups of parallel used edges do not interleave at a vertex. Indeed two parallel pairs alternating there have the ribbon rotation of a positive-genus bouquet, whereas their embedded regular neighborhood lies in the sphere. Split each horizontal disk into disjoint subdisks for these noncrossing edge groups, using finite polygonal trees joining the incident intervals. The noncrossing partition has an interval block; peel it off and induct to obtain those disjoint trees and subdisks. Thus each group gives a disjoint boundary patch consisting of two horizontal disks and its parallel window bands. It is a sphere with finitely many holes. [given, F4, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Here is simultaneous placement even for nested groups. Choose a point on the boundary sphere off all frontier circles of these patches. Each circle bounds its disk not containing that point; these disks are disjoint or nested. In a finite inward boundary collar push each disk in by a PL roof, zero on its boundary and positive inside. Choose the scales from the outside inward so an inner disk's entire roof is shallower than the positive minimum of each outer roof over that compact inner disk. The proper pushed disks are disjoint. Cut the supplied ball along all of them. Step 1.1 and the window constructions parametrize every piece as a ball. Its boundary contact patches are exactly the components of the boundary sphere cut at the circles, so distinct parallel-edge groups get distinct ball pieces even when nested. Each group's ball has two prescribed horizontal disks; their complement in its boundary is an annulus. Its disjoint bands have the planar matching cyclic order, so finite annulus parameters make them vertical in a disk-times-interval parameter. In this product ball every repeated rectangle with those same window labels is homotopic into the product: adjust its paths in the horizontal disks and whole windows, then compare the disk fillings using $\pi_2(\text{ball})=0$. We have obtained a finite interval-bundle carrier at the tower endpoint. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 We describe one downward step, initially allowing a noninjective enclosure. In an interval-bundle carrier with nonempty marked vertical boundary, choose a finite geometric basis of the relative base-path groupoid. A polygon-with-bands model gives disjoint proper arcs with endpoints in the marked boundary intervals: join those intervals by a tree, then take the handle and hole generators with separated endpoint slots in a chosen interval. The same construction on a twisted base uses its one-sided band arcs. The lifted rectangles are pairwise disjoint. Here the ambient space is the WHOLE twofold cover of the preceding carrier, containing the smaller augmented carrier; all inessentialness tests in this step take place in that whole cover. Every component meets an actual whole used window. A fiber is homotopic to that window's spanning path with its endpoint tracks in the lifted original $P$, so a contrary $P$-inessentialness homotopy would project to one for the original used-window span. Thus these fibers remain $P$-essential without assuming that the smaller carrier injects into the original manifold. If such a basis rectangle is inessential relative to a whole lifted ambient window, its two horizontal paths cannot connect opposite window ends, since that would make the window span inessential relative to $P$. Their endpoints are therefore in the same respective end intervals. Injection of $P$ lets both paths move into those actual intervals. The remaining disk fillings agree up to $\pi_2=0$, so this whole rectangle can move into that window and be discarded from the essential basis. Every original rectangle map in the carrier straightens to an interval times a base path: lift its simply connected domain to the fiber-orientation cover, match its horizontal ends and its vertical-window paths, and use the base surface's vanishing second homotopy group. Its path is a word in the remaining basis arcs and the discarded window paths. [F3, F4, F5, step 7.1, construct]

9.1 Project the remaining disjoint basis rectangles through the next twofold covering. Both essentialness conditions survive projection: a contrary marked homotopy lifts into the WHOLE twofold cover used for the tests in step 8.1, with the same whole lifted window labels. It is not required to stay in the smaller augmented carrier. Apply [F1] to get a finite embedded interval-bundle enclosure. Compose its actual basis-rectangle homotopies along the words of step 8.1; the joining edges are in the specified windows, and horizontal endpoint tracks stay in $P$. The comparison of the resulting disk fillings uses $\pi_2=0$ from [F3]. Thus every original rectangle has its required marked homotopy into this enclosure. Discard components carrying no retained rectangle. Every remaining component carries a rectangle essential in BOTH marked senses. We next make this enclosure essential without asserting it was already injective. [F1, F3, F5, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 Double this ambient carrier along its whole USED windows. Write $M^\dagger,P^\dagger$ for the window double and doubled horizontal marking. The double is irreducible by the disk-window pocket move of step 1.1. The horizontal surface $P^\dagger$ injects. To verify this, take a null disk for a loop there and make it transverse to the window disks. Remove circular preimages by disk replacements into contractible windows. An outermost remaining preimage arc cuts off a path in one original $P$. If its endpoints are in opposite ends of a window it contradicts the essential span. If they are in the same end, compare with the path in that end interval and use original $P$ injection to cancel this excursion. Induct until the loop lies in one original $P$, where injection applies. Double the enclosure along its vertical window rectangles; it is still an interval bundle. Its fibers stay essential relative to $P^\dagger$: folding the double onto the original pair sends a contrary homotopy to a contrary original homotopy. Each enclosure component contains the double of a selected rectangle. Its annular core has a cyclically reduced positive-length two-piece word, precisely because the rectangle was essential relative to the WHOLE windows; it consequently has infinite order by [F5]. Thus no component, or its horizontal component, can be a disk-type ball. Nor can a whole component lie in an ambient ball. [given, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 9.1, construct]

11.1 We cap horizontal holes in this window double. Choose an eligible hole disk in $P^\dagger$, disjoint from its incident horizontal component on its interior side, minimizing the number of horizontal boundary circles inside. Its opposite rim on the same vertical frontier annulus is not inside that disk. Otherwise the opposite nondisk horizontal component lies wholly inside; its planar inner hole provides a smaller eligible disk. Every horizontal component is nondisk by the infinite-core conclusion of step 10.1. Double the ambient again, now along $P^\dagger$. Its seam injects and the double is irreducible by step 1.1. Double the vertical frontier annulus to a torus. Its fiber has infinite order: an essential interval fiber gives a reduced positive-length word across the two copies. The chosen hole disk is an exterior compression disk for this torus. Compressing gives a sphere and an explicitly parametrized punctured solid torus. Irreducibility caps that sphere by a ball. The ball cannot contain the original torus, by its infinite fiber. Hence its EXTERIOR side is a solid torus $V$. This argument is the explicit torus-times-interval plus meridian two-handle and cap model, not a claim that arbitrary handle attachments preserve irreducibility. The fiber's infinite image also makes $\pi_1(V)\to\pi_1(\text{double})$ injective. [F4, F5, step 1.1, step 10.1, construct]

12.1 The seam meets $V$ in exactly two meridian disks. The first is the chosen hole disk; at its rim the other seam side is outside $V$, so no other seam region in $V$ can have that rim as boundary. Any remaining region therefore has only the opposite rim as boundary, and is a disk as well: if its rim is essential in the seam, a nondisk one-boundary side has noncyclic group injecting into the seam and hence into the ambient group, impossible inside the cyclic group of $V$. If its rim is inessential, the other possibility is the whole seam component minus a disk; it either meets the actual ambient boundary, impossible inside $V$, or surjects onto a closed positive-genus seam group, again impossible inside cyclic $V$. A spherical boundary-seam component would make this connected irreducible ambient a ball, contradicting the infinite core. Whole closed seam components inside $V$ are excluded by the same noncyclic-group argument. Cutting $V$ at either meridian gives a parametrized ball: make its boundary parallel to a standard product meridian, remove intersection circles by innermost disk-ball isotopies in the standard solid torus, and cut at the standard meridian. The given disk cuts that standard ball into two balls whose standard meridian windows lie on opposite sides; gluing those windows joins two balls and is [F4]'s ball gluing. The second seam meridian then cuts this ball into two parametrized balls. The seam reflection preserves the exterior $V$ and reverses the normal at both disks, so it exchanges these two balls. The original window-double half therefore contains a ball bounded by the vertical annulus and the two cap disks. No other enclosure component can enter that ball, since it carries an infinite core. Fill the base hole using this product ball; new fibers are homotopic to old frontier fibers with endpoint tracks in its cap disks. They remain essential. [F4, F5, step 10.1, step 11.1, construct]

13.1 Perform these caps equivariantly for the ORIGINAL window reflection. Minimal eligible disks form disjoint orbits: nested distinct disks would have unequal enclosed-circle counts. Their opposite cap disks are clean by step 12.1, since any incident enclosure component trapped there has an infinite core. Thus the cap boundaries are disjoint unless the caps coincide. Cap balls cannot nest, because nesting would trap the incident enclosure component with its infinite core. Choose the product parameter on one member of a paired orbit and reflect it to the other. An invariant cap, corresponding to an original frontier strip, has one fixed arc in each cap disk. Indeed the disk involution reverses orientation and its boundary has just the two original strip-corner fixed points. In a common invariant subdivision each interior fixed link is a reflected circle with two fixed directions, so its fixed set is a PL one-manifold. A closed fixed circle would exchange its disk side and the side meeting the disk boundary, impossible. Thus there is exactly one proper fixed arc. That arc joins the two strip-side endpoints in the fixed seam, forcing them to lie in ONE used window. The boundary fixed loop of the three-ball consists of the two cap-disk arcs and those two strip sides. It bounds its actual polygonal disk in that window. No other window piece can enter the ball without further boundary intersections, and a whole proper window disk cannot lie internally there. This is the entire fixed set in the ball. Step 1.1 cuts it into two parametrized balls, exchanged by reflection. Parametrize one half with its four boundary disks as a half-disk-times-interval, and reflect it. The resulting product parameter makes the reflection preserve interval fibers. This constructs the half-cap downstairs with its actual window face; no classification of arbitrary ball involutions is used. [F4, F5, step 10.1, step 12.1, construct]

14.1 Every cap removes two horizontal boundary circles and adds none, so the finite process ends. The horizontal surface upstairs in the window double now injects into $P^\dagger$. Otherwise the finite surface criterion supplies an inessential rim; since its horizontal component is not a disk, that rim or an inner planar hole would give an eligible disk for a further cap. Its marked boundary injection downstairs follows by doubling an arc: an arc in a horizontal component homotopic in $P$ into one actual window-end interval doubles to a null loop in $P^\dagger$. Injection of the doubled horizontal surface makes it null there as well. The cut normal form for doubling along its end intervals forces the arc into its own intersection with that interval. This treats multiple traces in the same whole window correctly. In particular every horizontal component downstairs injects into $P$ and then into its ambient manifold. Its inclusion in its interval-bundle component has index one or two. If $g$ is in the kernel of that component's ambient group map, $g^2$ belongs to a horizontal subgroup and is trivial by its injection. The base has boundary, since the component still meets a used window; its group is free by the finite polygon model and is torsion-free by reduced words. Hence $g=1$. The enclosure is now genuinely injective and has the asserted horizontal pair properties. Every previous marked rectangle homotopy was retained by inclusion through the cap operations. [given, F4, F5, step 10.1, step 11.1, step 12.1, step 13.1, construct]

15.1 Repeat steps 8.1–14.1 down the finite tower of step 5.1. Each output is the essential interval-bundle input to the next step; its marked window components are the actual whole-window labels inherited from the fixed source, and every base still has boundary. The discarded basis factors had actual homotopies into those windows, so none of the original rectangle words or endpoint tracks was lost. At the original level all $f_i$ therefore have the required marked homotopies into the final embedded region. Step 14.1 proves injection and both horizontal assertions there. Only the unpared hierarchy and the stated twofold position interface used AC; no characteristic-submanifold or circle-fibered descent theorem was invoked. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, step 8.1, step 14.1, construct] ∎
