## 1. Relative surface Euler calculation

Here is a finite topological calculation, independent of Floer moduli spaces. Let S be a connected oriented compact surface with nonempty boundary, properly embedded in an oriented smooth three-manifold M. Give M a metric, a fixed unit boundary field v0, and a trivialization t of v0-perpendicular. Require v0 not parallel to the chosen positive normal nu of S along its boundary. Put w0=projection of v0 to TS. Let I be the winding of w0 relative to the oriented boundary tangent, and r the winding of projection(nu) to v0-perpendicular relative to the first vector of t. A class is outer if it has a representative v agreeing with v0 on the boundary and avoiding -nu on all of S. Then

    <c1(v-perpendicular,t),[S]> = chi(S)+I-r

for every outer representative.

Proof. Projection of nu into v-perpendicular is nonzero on the boundary. Perturb the interior field, retaining avoidance of -nu, so its tangencies with nu are isolated transverse ones. One can do this in finitely many trivialized surface disks, with compactly supported perturbations; transversality is only for a section of the rank-two tangent bundle of this compact surface. At a tangency v=nu, both TS and v-perpendicular have the same orientation. The two projections, nu to v-perpendicular and v to TS, have the same zero index: in an oriented orthonormal frame with nu=(0,0,1), write v=(a,b,sqrt(1-a^2-b^2)); the two tangent coordinate maps differ by multiplication by -1 and an orientation-preserving identification. Thus the relative Euler obstruction with boundary frame projection(nu) is the sum of the indices of projection(v) to TS.

Compute that sum by extending the oriented boundary tangent, rotating it into w0 through I full turns on a boundary collar, and applying the disk definition of Euler obstruction to the remaining surface. The tangent framing has obstruction chi(S): on a finite polygonal handle presentation of S, a disk contributes 1 and each attached oriented one-handle contributes -1. A positive extra boundary turn increases the obstruction by 1. Hence the sum is chi(S)+I. Replacing the boundary frame projection(nu) by t subtracts its winding r. This proves the displayed formula, including its boundary signs.

The converse also holds at the level of restriction to S. Trivialize TM|S using an oriented frame of TS and nu; an oriented surface with boundary has such a tangent frame, obtained by cancelling the zeros of a field along disjoint arcs leading to the boundary in a finite disk-band presentation. The bundle of unit vectors excluding -nu is fiberwise a plane. Stereographic projection with pole -nu extends the prescribed boundary field to an avoiding field v1 over S: extend its two real coordinate functions on the boundary across a collar and use a cutoff to zero in the interior. To compare v and v1 relative to the boundary, first homotope them to agree on a finite relative one-skeleton. The extension across a two-cell is measured by the degree of the sphere obtained by gluing their two disks with opposite orientations. These integers form a relative two-cocycle; changing the one-skeleton homotopy changes it by a coboundary. The perpendicular oriented plane of a unit vector is the pullback of TS2, whose Euler number is 2, so the difference of the two relative Chern evaluations is twice this integer obstruction. Since H2(S,boundary S;Z)=Z for connected oriented S, equal Chern evaluations force the obstruction to vanish. Fill the two-cell homotopies and extend through a product neighborhood of S by a cutoff in the normal coordinate. This gives an avoiding representative near S without changing the boundary field. The argument is finite; it does not use a Floer invariance theorem.

## 2. The meridional boundary normalization is independent of Y

Take the meridionally sutured exterior M of a null-homologous knot and a Seifert surface F of genus g. Its longitudinal boundary meets the two oppositely oriented meridian sutures once each. In a collar of that boundary, straighten the longitude and meridian to the two product coordinates of the boundary torus. The prescribed sutured boundary field has no component in the meridian direction: it points inward on R-minus, outward on R-plus, and follows the transverse annulus height in the two transition annuli. Therefore the meridional unit vector is a nonzero first vector for v0-perpendicular and realizes the stated meridional trivialization t_mu. The positive normal of F has meridional component of constant sign; after the collar is straightened its projection is that meridional vector or its negative. Consequently r(F,t_mu)=0. This assertion is about the boundary collar, so neither b1(Y)=0 nor an S3 normalization is used.

For completeness the boundary index has an explicit signed count. The normalized tangent projection w0 agrees with the oriented longitude tangent only when the longitude passes from R-minus to R-plus. At each such transition its angle relative to the longitude decreases through zero: just before the transition the projection points toward the interior of F and just afterward toward its exterior. There are two suture crossings, alternating the direction of passage, so the degree of w0 relative to the boundary tangent is minus one. Equivalently each transverse suture crossing contributes -1/2 to a lifted angle difference; their two half-turns give I(F)=-1. A small longitudinal shear makes the transition transverse without changing the degree. The same computation applies to -F, because reversing the boundary orientation also reverses the parameter in the degree, while the normal and tangent frame signs change together.

Thus the surface in section1 has outer Chern value chi(F)-1=-2g. For S=-F this says

    <c1(s,t_mu),[F]>=2g,

exactly Alexander grading g under the ACTUAL general-Y Definition. No HFK duality or orientation-reversal theorem has been invoked.

## 3. Count actual generator crossings, rather than infer them from a grading formula

Suppose a surface-adapted sutured Heegaard diagram is supplied. Its polygon P and boundary partition A union B obey alpha disjoint B and beta disjoint A; its decomposing surface is the rounded union P at middle height, A times the upper half interval, and B times the lower half interval. Its coorientation is the ascending Morse direction. Let x be a generator and let n be the number of its coordinates in P. With S=-F as above, its Morse Spin-c label satisfies

    A_F(s(x)) = g-n.

Here is the actual local calculation. The gradient before the generator modifications agrees with the positive normal along the horizontal P, and, after the specified crease roundings, avoids its negative normal on the remaining bands. A trajectory corresponding to a coordinate outside P misses S entirely. For a coordinate in P its trajectory crosses S once positively. Trivialize TM near this trajectory using the oriented surface frame and its normal, and split its trajectory ball along the middle disk D in S. The negative half-ball contains the index-one critical point. The normalized gradient has boundary degree -1 on that half-ball, and its restriction to D before replacement is the north pole. A nonvanishing replacement has total boundary degree zero. Since the boundary hemisphere away from D was kept fixed, the new map on D, constant on its boundary, has south-pole degree +1. Its perpendicular plane therefore changes the relative Euler obstruction by +2. All n balls can be chosen disjoint, so their contributions add. The unchanged field has the outer Euler obstruction from section1; hence its final evaluation on S is -2g+2n. Reverse S=-F to get the formula displayed above. This is the same actual critical-point degree operation used by the accepted general-Y Morse Chern supplier, not a Maslov-index assumption.

In particular top grading g is equivalent, in this SUPPLIED adapted diagram, to avoiding P altogether. If that diagram has exactly one generator outside P, the top graded free group is Z. Any defined integral Spin-c-preserving differential on that single-generator group is zero: its square would otherwise be multiplication by the square of a nonzero integer, contradicting d^2=0. Thus, once such a differential and actual diagram comparison are supplied, its top homology is Z, with no orientation-dependent sign ambiguity in the isomorphism type. This algebra does not prove existence of the coherent orientation system or d^2=0.

## 4. What the fibration gives without Floer theory

Cut the compact exterior bundle at a fiber. Its bundle trivialization over the cut base interval gives F times [0,1], with the two horizontal faces marked by the actual return map. The longitudinal boundary collar supplies the product vertical annulus; no closed component of F or extra cap is introduced. Reversing the fiber coorientation exchanges the two horizontal faces and retains the same underlying product. This is the actual sutured product obtained by the decomposition along -F. Its product sutured diagram is (F,empty,empty), with the unique empty generator and zero differential over Z. The marked return map is retained when the exterior is reconstructed; it is not asserted to be isotopic to the identity.

## 5. Direct doubled-page diagram: a finite admissibility proof

An actual additional primary reading is Honda--Kazez--Matic, *On the contact class in Heegaard Floer homology*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0609734v2, complete construction in section3.1, PDF4--6, together with its explicit handlebody description PDF3. This is not a reading of its full invariance proof. The following argument expands its periodic-domain sign observation into an all-domain proof; it does not import the paper's invariance assertion.

Give the page F a finite disk-band presentation with 2g bands. Choose disjoint cocore arcs a_i cutting F into a disk. In disjoint narrow neighborhoods of these arcs and the boundary, choose b_i by shifting their endpoints a short positive distance along the oriented boundary and crossing a_i exactly once positively. The neighborhoods can be chosen mutually disjoint because all arc endpoints are distinct. Each b_i is disjoint from every a_j for j different from i, and all b_i are mutually disjoint. Cutting along the a_i gives a disk with paired edge intervals; in that disk the b_i consist of short parallel edge arcs joined by a single transverse crossover at their midpoint. This is a finite prescription for the local model, not a generic-intersection assertion.

Double the page along its boundary, with one copy orientation-reversed. On the upper copy take a_i and b_i. On the lower copy take a_i and phi(b_i), where phi is the actual monodromy and is the identity in a fixed boundary collar. Their doubles are closed alpha_i and beta_i curves. The two systems bound the actual disks a_i times a half base interval and b_i times the other half interval in the open-book presentation. Cutting either handlebody along these disks leaves the disk obtained by cutting F along its cocores, times the half interval, with only the vertical intervals over the original boundary arcs collapsed along the binding. The new cut-edge faces are retained. This quotient is a ball by a local collar parametrization: in a rectangle coordinate (s,r) with the original boundary r=0, replace the interval coordinate t by r(2t-1), so the quotient is the solid wedge |z|<=r. At the finitely many rectangle ends use the rounded corner version of the same coordinate. These charts give the usual ball obtained by rounding a disk product; their agreement on the retained cut faces is literal. Thus the assertion is about the marked cut product, not an arbitrary three-ball diffeomorphism. Thus these are complete disk systems for the actual open-book manifold, not merely curves of the correct cardinality. The upper-page complement of the thin a_i/b_i ribbons has a connected large region: cutting along the a_i identifies it with a disk with narrow boundary rectangles removed. All the remaining thin regions abut this large region on each side of their central crossover. Put z in the large region.

Let D be an integral closed-diagram periodic domain with n_z(D)=0, so its boundary is an integral sum of full alpha and beta curves. In the local upper-page crossover x_i, the two opposite large-region quadrants both have coefficient zero. Write k_i for one of the remaining two coefficients. The equality of opposite quadrant sums, which follows from the absence of an endpoint in the full-curve boundary, makes the other coefficient -k_i. The jump across alpha_i is therefore plus or minus k_i. This jump is the SAME integer along every segment of alpha_i because the domain boundary contains a full alpha_i with a single coefficient. If every k_i is zero, all alpha boundary coefficients vanish. Its boundary is then a sum of full beta curves which is null-homologous on the closed doubled surface. A complete handlebody disk system is homologically independent there: the arc joining the two copies of a point on a beta disk across the connected cut surface gives a closed dual curve meeting that beta once and the others zero. Pairing with those dual curves makes every beta coefficient zero. A region chain with zero boundary on the connected closed surface is constant, and its z coefficient is zero, so D is zero. Consequently every NONZERO periodic domain has some k_i nonzero, and already its two upper-page coefficients k_i and -k_i have opposite signs.

This proves global weak admissibility for every Spin-c label simultaneously and for arbitrary phi. It proves it without winding, without a rational-homology-sphere assumption, and without identifying the periodic lattice with H2(Y). For the two-punctured sutured diagram it continues to apply whenever one puncture lies at z in the indicated large region: a sutured periodic domain extends by zero over BOTH punctures to a closed periodic domain with n_z=0. The second puncture may lie in another region; its forced zero coefficient only imposes an additional restriction. Its actual knot marking and the decomposing fiber polygon must still be verified, as recorded below.

In the upper page the product-torus intersections have the unique tuple x_1 times ... times x_(2g), because a_i meets b_j there exactly when i=j, and then once. All other generator coordinates lie on the lower page. Thus the remaining geometric marking task is precise: realize the properly embedded -F as an adapted polygon whose interior contains exactly the lower-page intersections and excludes the upper tuple, while locating the two punctures so the sutured manifold is the actual meridional exterior. Once this marking is constructed, sections2--3 give the absolute grading g for the unique upper tuple and strictly smaller grading for every other generator. This marking is NOT inferred from a drawing, and is not yet claimed here.

## 6. Orientability of the actual strip determinant lines

This is a further concrete part of the integral extension, not the completed signed comparison. The actual primary proof read is Ozsvath--Szabo, *Holomorphic disks and topological invariants for closed three-manifolds*, https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0101206, v4, full Proposition3.10 proof and section3.6, PDF28--29, together with section4.2.4, PDF40--41. Its statement concerns closed Heegaard data; the argument below only uses the attaching-torus tangent frames and hence applies unchanged to the sutured strip operator once that operator is supplied.

Order and orient the attaching circles. Each product torus then has a global oriented tangent frame. Since it is totally real, complexification gives a complex frame of the ambient tangent bundle along that torus. Fix two generators x and y and the usual strip-like source, compactified at its two ends. Fix a contraction of this source to its alpha boundary arc, stationary at the two ends, and a connection on the ambient complex tangent bundle. Parallel transport along the contraction extends the alpha complex frame across the pullback bundle of each map u. Relative to that extension, the beta tangent frame is a path of complex invertible matrices A_u(t). At the two ends this comparison is fixed entirely by the two tangent frames at x and y; hence its endpoints a_x and a_y are fixed, independently of u. The construction is continuous for families of maps and operators. Straightening the totally real boundary conditions reduces the family of real Cauchy--Riemann operators to the family parametrized by such matrix paths, together with affine spaces of lower-order terms. The affine factors are contractible and cannot contribute orientation monodromy to the determinant line.

The fixed-endpoint path space in GL_m(C) has simply connected components. Indeed a loop in it is a square of matrices whose two vertical edges are fixed; collapsing its boundary after a fixed reference path gives a sphere in GL_m(C). Polar decomposition retracts GL_m(C) onto U(m). The first-column map U(j) to S^(2j-1) has fiber U(j-1): over a coordinate neighborhood, apply Gram--Schmidt to the fixed remaining coordinate vectors to obtain an explicit local section, so the usual local-trivialization lifting argument gives the homotopy exact sequence. Induction gives pi2(U(j))=0. The base U(1)=S1 has pi2 zero by lifting to its real-line cover. For j>=2 the sphere has pi2 zero: approximate a sphere map smoothly, choose a point outside its image because its source has dimension two and the target has dimension at least three, and contract it in the complement of that point. The approximation is homotopic to the original by short metric geodesics. Thus the above square is nullhomotopic in the matrix path space. This is a proof of the required pi2 vanishing, not a stable-unitary classification assumption.

The determinant line of a continuous Fredholm family is locally a finite-dimensional determinant: choose a finite-dimensional target subspace V meeting every cokernel in a small neighborhood, take the determinant of the inverse image of V, and tensor with det(V)^*. Over a loop in the fixed-endpoint path space this real line extends over the filling disk, and is therefore orientable. Enlarging V gives the same line by the elementary determinant exact-sequence identification. Consequently the pullback determinant line over EVERY component of the strip-map space has zero orientation monodromy. Choose an orientation at one operator in such a component and extend it by paths; the extension is path-independent. This proves existence of orientations on the individual components of the actual strip operator family. Rank zero is the point-target operator and has its canonical determinant orientation.

What this paragraph does NOT yet prove is compatibility of those independently chosen component orientations under every disk/triangle/parameter gluing. That requires the actual determinant gluing identifications, their associativity and the signs in the oriented one-manifold boundary identities. Even after such coherence is established, signed sutured diagram-change maps and their inverses must be constructed. Orientability alone must not be presented as the integral invariant.
