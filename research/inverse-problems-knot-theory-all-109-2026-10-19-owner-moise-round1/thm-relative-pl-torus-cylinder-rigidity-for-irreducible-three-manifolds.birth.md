## Statement

Let $W$ be a compact connected orientable PL three-manifold with a supplied finite compatible triangulation, and assume it is PL irreducible. Let $V=T^2\times[0,1]$ with its supplied standard finite PL structure. Suppose $f:W\to V$ is a PL map inducing an isomorphism on fundamental groups and restricting to a homeomorphism $\partial W\to\partial V$. Assume $f^{-1}(\partial V)=\partial W$. Then $f$ is homotopic relative to the boundary to a PL homeomorphism $g:W\to V$. The homotopy has inverse image of $\partial V$ equal to $\partial W$ at every time. All constructions are finite and introduce no choice hypothesis.

Here irreducibility means every finite embedded standard PL two-sphere in $\operatorname{Int}W$ bounds a parametrized PL ball in $\operatorname{Int}W$; it is an explicit hypothesis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite irreducible PL manifold, standard product and boundary-proper map inducing the stated fundamental-group isomorphism.

[F1] Finite orientable surface compression produces fundamental-group-injective components, preserving boundary and making the new group images subgroups of the original ones ([[lem-finite-pl-surface-compressions-produce-pi-one-injective-components]]).

[F2] Cutting along a connected two-sided fundamental-group-injective nonseparating finite PL surface in a manifold with abelian group gives cut group equal to the edge group and injective in the ambient group ([[lem-abelian-finite-pl-cut-manifolds-have-edge-group-fundamental-group]]).

[F3] A boundary homeomorphism from an irreducible finite PL manifold to a standard solid torus extends whenever its inverse meridian is nullhomotopic ([[thm-relative-pl-solid-torus-rigidity-for-irreducible-three-manifolds]]).

[F4] Compact regular PL height bands have global finite PL product parameters; polygonal disks and annuli admit prescribed-boundary finite PL parameters ([[lem-finite-pl-level-collars-give-global-band-products]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]).

[F5] Finite surface polygon reductions give orientable disk-and-band models, using only finite cuts and polygonal disk identifications ([[lem-finite-triangulated-surface-reduces-to-a-one-polygon-schema]], [[lem-polygonal-schema-reduction-moves]], [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]]).

[F6] A circle-valued map with trivial induced fundamental-group map has a real lift after fixing one value ([[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The stated data. Write $V=S^1_\mu\times S^1_\lambda\times[0,1]$ in PL angle coordinates modulo one. Label the two boundary tori of $W$ by their images at heights zero and one.

1.1 Choose a value $c$ of the $\lambda$ circle coordinate avoiding the finitely many vertex images of a common triangulation of that coordinate composed with $f$. Its preimage $F$ is a finite properly embedded cooriented PL surface. Indeed, on each source simplex it is a convex affine slice. Near a point in the smallest mixed source face, translation along a direction in that face with nonzero coordinate slope gives the same height change on every incident coface; it therefore gives the local product chart. Boundary faces give the corresponding half-space chart. This proves the PL surface and properness assertions without a transversality theorem. Its boundary consists of exactly one circle $C_i$ on each boundary torus, since the boundary map is a homeomorphism. Each maps once to the $\mu$ circle. Under $f_*$, every component's group image lies in the cyclic subgroup generated by $\mu$. Compress $F$ by [F1]. Each resulting component with boundary is injective in the same cyclic subgroup. A connected compact orientable surface with boundary has free fundamental group of rank $2g+b-1$: use the finite disk-and-band model of [F5] and collapse its disk and band cores to a graph. A free group embedded in a cyclic group has rank at most one, since a commutator of two distinct free generators is a nonempty reduced word. Thus each boundary component surface is a disk or annulus, with its finite PL model supplied by [F5]. A disk is impossible because either $C_i$ represents the nonzero $\mu$ element in $\pi_1(W)$. There is consequently one annulus $A$ joining $C_0$ to $C_1$. Discard the closed surface components; $A$ remains fundamental-group-injective. [given, F1, F4, F5, construct]

2.1 Cut $W$ along $A$, obtaining $N$. The cut is connected: every cut component must meet one of the two copies of $A$, and the annulus obtained by cutting either boundary torus along its essential $C_i$ joins those copies. Each cut boundary torus is parametrized by the given boundary map. The boundary of $N$ is a single torus made from these two annuli and the two copies of $A$. Its finite standard parameter will also be supplied explicitly below. The annulus is two-sided and has a bicollar by the finite local-link and collar construction in [F1]. Apply [F2] to it. Since $\pi_1(W)\cong\mathbb Z^2$, we obtain $\pi_1(N)\cong\mathbb Z$, generated by the annulus circle, with injective map to $\pi_1(W)$. Irreducibility passes to $N$: a sphere in its interior bounds a ball in $W$ by hypothesis; the connected annulus $A$, disjoint from the sphere and reaching $\partial W$, cannot enter that ball, so the ball lies on the cut side and is contained in $N$. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 We match the annulus map, including its possible winding in the other circle direction. Parametrize $A$ as $S^1\times[0,1]$, respecting the boundary labels. The $\lambda$ coordinate of $f|_A$ is constant $c$ on its boundary and has trivial induced group map, so [F6] lifts it to a real coordinate with boundary values $c$ and $c+m$ for some integer $m$. Let $$A_m=\{(u,c+ms,s):u\in S^1,\ 0\le s\le1\}\subset V.$$ Its boundary circles are the prescribed target circles. The boundary $\mu$ maps on the two ends of $A$ have the same degree, either one or minus one, since their loops are homotopic through $A$ and $f_*$ is injective. They extend to a PL annulus homeomorphism to $A_m$: subdivide both boundary circle maps compatibly in their cyclic order and match the intervening rectangular cells by the finite polygonal disk extensions of [F4]. If necessary compose with an integer twist in the $\mu$ coordinate, fixed on both boundary circles, to match the relative winding of $f_\mu|_A$. In detail the ratio of this chosen $\mu$ map and $f_\mu$ has a real lift by [F6], constant integers on the two boundary circles; one twist changes their difference by any prescribed integer. Choose that integer so both constants agree, and subtract it from the lift. Interpolation of the real lifts, of the interval coordinate, and of the two circle coordinates now gives a homotopy $f|_A\simeq a$ relative to its boundary, where $a:A\to A_m$ is the chosen PL homeomorphism. For the interval coordinate use its annulus parameter; for the $\lambda$ lift interpolate to $c+ms$. All boundary values are fixed. [F4, F6, step 1.1, construct]

4.1 Cut $V$ along $A_m$, obtaining a standard solid torus $V'$. Explicitly the PL shear $(u,v,s)\mapsto(u,v-ms,s)$, which is the identity on both boundary tori, takes $A_m$ to the constant-$\lambda$ annulus; its cut is $S^1\times[0,1]^2$. On $\partial N$ prescribe the original boundary maps on its two outside annuli and $a$ on both copies of $A$. They agree at the seams and give a PL homeomorphism $b:\partial N\to\partial V'$, supplying the promised torus parameter. Let $C=b^{-1}(\partial D^2\times\{s_0\})$ for a standard meridian of this cut solid torus. Its image is nullhomotopic in $V'$, hence in $V$. The map of this boundary loop under $f$ is homotopic in $V$ to its map under $b$: on the annulus copies use the relative homotopy of step 3.1 and elsewhere keep the original boundary map fixed. Since $\pi_1(N)\to\pi_1(W)\xrightarrow{f_*}\pi_1(V)$ is injective by step 2.1, $C$ is nullhomotopic in $N$. Apply [F3] to extend $b$ over a PL homeomorphism $N\to V'$. Its maps on the two annulus copies agree with their regluing identifications, so it descends to a PL homeomorphism $g:W\to V$ equal to $f$ on the boundary and to $a$ on $A$. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The maps $f,g$ induce the same map on fundamental groups, because they agree on a boundary torus whose inclusion induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$: its composite with $f_*$ is the boundary inclusion isomorphism in $V$. Each circle-coordinate ratio $f_jg_j^{-1}$ therefore has a real lift by [F6], chosen zero on the first boundary torus. It is also zero on the second. To verify this, take a path in $A$ joining the two boundaries; step 3.1 gives a relative-boundary homotopy of the ratio on $A$ to the constant map, so its lift has zero endpoint difference on that path. The constant value on the second connected boundary is thus zero. Interpolate these real lifts in the two circle coordinates from $g$ to $f$, and linearly interpolate their interval coordinates. This gives a homotopy fixed on both boundary tori. At an interior point both interval coordinates lie strictly between zero and one, so every interpolated point does too; hence it is boundary-proper at every time. The lifts and twists involve finitely many coordinates and integers. The finite compression sequence, canonical transversal choices of [F2], cell maps and gluings introduce no choice principle. [given, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct] ∎
