## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $(k,n)=(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$. Let $M$ be the underlying topological product $B^k\times T^n$, where $B^j=[-1,1]^j$ and $T^n=\mathbb R^n/(32\mathbb Z)^n$, equipped with a supplied finite orientable PL structure $\Sigma$ agreeing with the standard product PL structure near its entire boundary. The product identification in the INTERIOR need only be topological. Suppose its local handle $B^k\times\operatorname{Int}(2B^n)$ has the pulled-back chart data of [[lem-two-boundary-handle-shells-admit-protected-pl-ball-carvings]], and let $D,T,L$ be one of the actual PL carvings supplied there. In particular $\partial D=T\cup L$, $L=\partial B^k\times\tfrac32 B^n$, and $D$ contains $B^k\times B^n$ in its relative interior.

Suppose every finite PL interior two-sphere of $M$ has a complementary closure that is a parametrized TOP three-ball contained in $\operatorname{Int}M$. This is a supplied topological sphere-ball-side property, not a PL irreducibility hypothesis.

There is a finite PL submanifold $Q\subset\operatorname{Int}M$, disjoint from $D$, which is a parametrized TOP three-ball, such that replacing $Q$ by a standard PL cap gives a finite PL manifold $M_0$ and a homeomorphism $r:M\to M_0$ fixed outside $\operatorname{Int}Q$. The new structure agrees with $\Sigma$ on a neighborhood of $D$ and of $\partial M$. Moreover $M_0$ admits a finite PL homeomorphism to the standard PL product $B^k\times T^n$, fixed on the original product boundary, and homotopic relative to that boundary to the underlying TOP marking $r^{-1}:M_0\to M$. In particular $M_0$ is PL irreducible.

This proves the special boundary-attached reassembly. It does not assert that arbitrary2handle addition preserves irreducibility, that $Q$ is a PL ball, or that a TOP manifold without the supplied PL structure has been triangulated.

## Proof

1.1 Put $N=\overline{M\setminus D}$, rounding its finite PL boundary corners by the quadrant face-cone maps of [F3]. It is a compact finite PL manifold. For $k=1$, its boundary consists of the two punctured tori $F_\pm=\{\pm1\}\times(T^2\setminus\operatorname{Int}(\tfrac32 B^2))$, joined along their boundary circles by $T$; it is one closed genus-two surface. For $k=2$, the annulus $\partial M\setminus\operatorname{Int}L$ is capped by the two disks $T$, giving one sphere boundary. These identifications are finite product-cell identifications with the supplied annulus/disk parameters, so include actual PL parameters for the stated boundary models. The manifold $N$ is connected. Every component has boundary: a component without boundary would be both open and closed in connected $M$, disjoint from $D$. Since the total boundary of $N$ is connected, it belongs to only one component. Thus there is only that component. A PL interior sphere in $N$ has its TOP ball side in $N$: its ball in $M$ cannot contain any point of the connected $D$, since $D$ misses the sphere and meets $\partial M$, whereas that ball lies in $\operatorname{Int}M$. It consequently misses $D$ entirely. Every sphere in $N$ therefore separates and has an actual TOP ball-side parameter in $\operatorname{Int}N$. [given, F2, F3, construct]

1.2 We treat $k=1$ first and prove the needed relative path control. In the coordinate handle put $G=B^1\times(\operatorname{Int}(2B^2)\setminus B^2)$, whose TOP fundamental group is $\mathbb Z$. The proper annulus $T$ has both boundary circles equal to the standard generators on the two boundary annuli of $G$, so $\pi_1(T)\to\pi_1(G)$ is an isomorphism. It separates $G$ into exactly two components. Indeed its relative sides are the inside and outside of the carved ball, both nonempty, with small boundary strips at radii respectively below and above $3/2$. There are at most two components: every component's relative frontier meets $T$ by connectedness of $G$, and each of the two collars of the connected $T$ is connected. Denote by $G_o$ the outer CUT CLOSURE in $G$, including its boundary copy of $T$; use the analogous inner cut closure for the other side. The edge maps from $\pi_1(T)$ to BOTH incident cut-component groups are injective, since their composite into $\pi_1(G)$ is injective. Thickened collars give the amalgam van Kampen description. By [F5] both component groups inject into $\pi_1(G)$. Since either image already contains the whole image of $\pi_1(T)$, they and their edge maps are isomorphisms onto $\mathbb Z$. In particular $\pi_1(T)\to\pi_1(G_o)$ is an isomorphism. This uses no claim that $G_o$ was already a standard PL solid torus. [A1, F2, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F1] to $N$. Its cutting spheres all separate, so their cut graph is a finite tree. The connected original boundary of $N$ lies in exactly one distinguished cut piece. Each edge's TOP ball side lies on the branch AWAY from that piece, because its ball is contained in $\operatorname{Int}N$ and cannot contain the original boundary. Take only the edges incident to the distinguished vertex. Their branches are pairwise disjoint parametrized TOP balls $B_i$, with finite PL sphere boundaries, absorbing all the other pieces. Replace them by the supplied standard PL cap balls to obtain the distinguished prime $P$. It has nonempty boundary and is therefore irreducible by [F1]; it cannot be the closed $S^1\times S^2$ exception. Join the $B_i$ by disjoint thin finite PL arc tubes along a tree inside the remaining distinguished piece. The finite construction [F3] works inside its interior and misses a common boundary collar. In $N$ these TOP balls and tubes form one parametrized TOP ball $Q$; in $P$ the standard cap balls and IDENTICAL tubes form a parametrized PL ball. The ball/window boundary maps are matched by coning, as in the listed construction. Replacing $Q$ by a standard cap therefore gives $N_0$ PL homeomorphic to $P$, and a homeomorphism $N\to N_0$ fixed on the common complement and a boundary collar. If there are no such branches, take a small interior PL chart ball for $Q$ instead. Thus $N_0$ is PL irreducible, has the IDENTICAL marked boundary, and retains every boundary nullhomotopy class of $N$. Extend this replacement by the identity on $D$ to obtain $M_0=N_0\cup_TD$ and $r:M\to M_0$. The new PL structure is identical near $D$ and near $\partial M$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 Choose $3/2<\rho<2$ such that the entire coordinate cylinder $P_\rho=B^1\times\rho\partial B^2$ lies outside $D$. Such a $\rho$ exists: $D$ is compact in the outer block, its PL boundary $T\cup L$ misses the outer coordinate faces, and no interior point of a ball contained in that block can touch one of those faces. Hence its maximum square radius is strictly below2. The cylinder is a continuous annulus in $G_o$, and its boundary circles and their radial joining strips to $\partial T$ lie in the KNOWN standard boundary PL structure. Its core also generates $\pi_1(G_o)$, by step 1.2 and inclusion into $G$. Give $T$ a finite PL annulus parameter whose end-circle maps are the actual coordinate circle maps. They have compatible degree, since both represent the same generator; cutting a spanning strip and applying [F3]'s polygonal disk extension supplies this parameter. Its circle family of crossing arcs, followed by the two radial boundary strips, can be compared to the vertical crossing arcs of $P_\rho$. The resulting based loops have one constant integer winding $m$ in $\pi_1(G_o)\cong\mathbb Z$. For a precise independence check, vary the angular parameter continuously: the comparison loops vary by a homotopy with the corresponding basepoint path, and conjugation is trivial in this cyclic group. Thus the integer is constant. Precompose the annulus parameter with the twist $(\theta,u)\mapsto(\theta-mu,u)$. It fixes both boundary circles pointwise, is finite PL after subdividing the finitely many wrap lines in the angular cover, and changes the comparison winding by $-m$. Every crossing arc in this new family, extended by the radial strips, is therefore homotopic in $G_o$ relative its endpoints to the corresponding vertical arc in $P_\rho$. Only finitely many of these arcs will be used. [F3, F5, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 In $F_\rho=T^2\setminus\operatorname{Int}(\rho B^2)$ choose the standard two disjoint proper cutting arcs $a,b$: the coordinate horizontal and vertical generator circles, with their portions inside the central square removed. They have four distinct cyclic endpoints on $\rho\partial B^2$, are finite PL, and cutting along both leaves a polygonal disk; this is the ordinary square-quotient torus cell calculation. Extend them radially to $3/2\partial B^2$ in the two boundary copies. The four crossing arcs from step 2.2 join the corresponding endpoints across $T$. Their unions with $a_+,a_-$ and $b_+,b_-$ give two DISJOINT simple PL circles $c_a,c_b$ on $\partial N$. They are nullhomotopic in $N$: replace their crossing arcs by the homotopic outer vertical arcs in $G_o$, then fill by the two coordinate rectangles $a\times B^1$ and $b\times B^1$ in the untouched outside product $B^1\times F_\rho$. These are continuous nullhomotopies, which suffice. The finite PL boundary map just constructed also identifies the pattern $(\partial N,T,c_a,c_b)$ with the boundary of $F\times I$, its annular part $\partial F\times I$, and the boundaries of the two product disks $a\times I,b\times I$, where $F$ is a once-punctured torus. It is the original product marking on the two punctured boundary tori. By step 2.1, these same circles are nullhomotopic on the unchanged marked boundary of $N_0$. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct]

4.1 Apply [F4]'s exact-boundary Dehn lemma in $N_0$ to obtain disks $E_a,E_b$ with these exact boundaries. Make their interiors transverse by finite PL vertex perturbations, leaving disjoint short boundary collars fixed. In local affine simplex charts the excluded nontransverse positions are finitely many proper affine conditions; a sufficiently small permitted perturbation retains embeddedness. Their intersections are finitely many circles, because their boundaries are disjoint. Make the disks disjoint by finite disk exchanges. Choose an intersection circle innermost on $E_a$. Its disk in $E_a$ has interior disjoint from $E_b$. Replace the disk it bounds in $E_b$ by a close parallel copy of that $E_a$ disk, attached with its appropriate side collar. Its interior misses the rest of $E_b$, and the side push separates it from $E_a$; the replaced disk contained at least that intersection circle. Thus the number decreases and the boundary remains exactly $c_b$. Finite polygonal collar maps of [F3] verify embeddedness at the seam. Repeat finitely many times. This gives disjoint proper PL disks with the two prescribed boundary circles and no use of irreducibility for the exchanges. [F3, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Cut $N_0$ along these two disks, giving $Z$. The marked boundary pattern from step 3.1 shows explicitly that its boundary is ONE standard PL sphere: cut the two punctured tori along $a,b$ to disks, retain the cut annulus rectangles, and cap the four new disk faces. This is exactly the boundary of the standard ball obtained by cutting $F\times I$ along its two vertical disks. The cut manifold is connected. Each successive boundary-cutting circle is nonseparating in the boundary pattern, so paths on that boundary join its two disk copies; every cut component must meet such a copy, and induction retains connectedness. Irreducibility passes from $N_0$ to $Z$: a sphere in its interior bounds a PL ball in $N_0$, and either connected cutting disk is disjoint from that sphere and has boundary on the original boundary, outside the ball. Neither disk can enter it, so the ball lies in the cut manifold. Finally a connected irreducible PL manifold with its single boundary a supplied sphere is a parametrized PL ball. Push that boundary slightly inward by its finite collar. Its inner sphere bounds a PL ball by irreducibility; the ball cannot be on the boundary-collar side, which meets the original boundary. The other side is precisely this ball, and adjoining the outer sphere collar gives a parametrized ball by the finite face-frustum parameters in [F3]. Thus $Z$ has an ACTUAL PL ball parameter. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The boundary identification in step 3.1 extends across each pair of disk faces of $Z$ by the SAME polygonal disk extension, copied to its two shores. It therefore gives a finite PL homeomorphism from $\partial Z$ to the boundary of the standard cut ball of $F\times I$, respecting the two face-pair identifications. Extend over the balls by coning in their supplied ball parameters. Regluing those paired faces gives a PL homeomorphism $N_0\to F\times I$ whose annular marking is the parameter of step 2.2 and whose maps on the two punctured tori are the original product markings. Extend its map on $T$ across the two complementary disks $L$ by their ORIGINAL coordinate disk maps; the end-circle maps agree. These define a PL sphere map on $\partial D$, which extends over its supplied PL ball parameter by coning. Pasting across $T$ gives a PL homeomorphism $g:M_0\to B^1\times T^2$. It fixes the entire original boundary, on the punctured-torus portions and the two disk portions. Thus the annular2handle reattachment is proved by an explicit marked product model. No general2handle irreducibility assertion was used. [F2, F3, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct]

6.2 For $k=2$, step 1.1 gives a single sphere boundary for $N_0$. Its irreducibility from step 2.1 and the collar argument of step 5.1 make it a parametrized PL ball. It and $D$ are glued along the two disjoint disks $T$. Parametrize each ball as $D^2\times I$ with those disks as its two end windows. Such a two-window parameter follows by mapping the two complementary boundary disks, parametrizing the intervening annulus by a strip cut, and coning the resulting boundary sphere map. The two end disk maps can be prescribed to match the actual attachments: orientability gives exactly the compatible boundary orientations, and the annulus boundary extension supplies the rest. After these choices both attaching maps are the identity in the disk coordinates. Two interval blocks joined at both ends form $S^1$, so their union is the explicit standard PL $D^2\times S^1$. This supplies a PL product model for $M_0$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

7.1 Both standard PL products just obtained are irreducible. Their explicit cover is $B^k\times\mathbb R^n$ with integral translations in the second factor. A finite PL sphere lifts to a sphere in this cover, embedded in standard $\mathbb R^3$, since it is simply connected. Its compact PL ball from [F6] stays in the product: each boundary cylinder or plane is connected, unbounded and disjoint from the sphere, so stays outside that bounded ball. Distinct deck translates of the lifted sphere are disjoint, and their bounded balls are disjoint or nested by the standard complementary-component description. Proper nesting of a compact ball and its nonzero translation is impossible: maximize the linear coordinate in the translation direction, or minimize it for the opposite containment. Thus the ball has disjoint nontrivial deck translates and projects injectively to a parametrized PL ball. This proves irreducibility of $M_0$ in either case. For $k=2$ the original boundary identity sends a standard meridian to the boundary of either given disk of $T$, so it is nullhomotopic. Apply [F4]'s relative solid-torus extension to replace the product model from step 6.2 by one fixing the ENTIRE original product boundary. This is a legitimate use AFTER irreducibility has been proved, not a circular target-rigidity assumption. [F4, F5, F6, step 6.1, step 6.2, construct]

8.1 Write $f=r^{-1}:M_0\to B^k\times T^n$ for the underlying TOP marking. It and $g$ induce the same fundamental-group map, since they agree on the original boundary, whose inclusion surjects onto that group in either product. Hence each circle-coordinate ratio $g_j-f_j$ has a real lift by [F5]. For $k=1$ choose it zero on the first boundary torus; on the other it is a constant integer multiple of the period32. Compose $g$ with the standard PL shear subtracting those integer periods times its interval coordinate rescaled from0 to1. This shear fixes both boundary tori pointwise. The corrected ratios then have zero lifts on both boundary components. For $k=2$ the boundary is connected, so a lift chosen zero there is zero on all of it. Interpolate the real lifts to zero in the circle coordinates and interpolate the $B^k$ coordinates linearly between $g$ and $f$. These maps agree on the boundary throughout and are proper there: both disk/cube coordinates are interior for every interior source point, and a convex combination of interior points is interior. Thus this is an actual homotopy relative boundary from the final PL homeomorphism to $f$. All selected disks, exchanges, arc positions, winding integers, windows and tree tubes were finite. The only use of the TOP sphere-ball-side property was the honest boundary-root removal in steps 1.1 and 2.1; no removed TOP ball was asserted PL. [A1, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 6.1, step 6.2, step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎
