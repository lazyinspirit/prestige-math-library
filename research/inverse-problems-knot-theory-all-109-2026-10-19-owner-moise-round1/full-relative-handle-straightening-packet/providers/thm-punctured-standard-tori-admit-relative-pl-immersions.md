## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. For $1\le n\le3$, let $T^n=\mathbb R^n/(32\mathbb Z)^n$ have its standard affine smooth and PL structures, write $q$ for the quotient, and set $p=q(12,0,\ldots,0)$. On $q([-2,2]^n)$ use the displayed flat coordinates. There is an orientation-preserving PL immersion
$$a_n:T^n\setminus\{p\}\longrightarrow\mathbb R^n$$
which is exactly $a_n(q(x))=x$ for $x\in[-2,2]^n$. Here PL immersion means that every source point has a neighborhood on which the map is a PL homeomorphism onto an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$; global injectivity is not asserted. Its domain has an explicit locally finite subdivision of the standard PL structure, finite on compact subsets.

Consequently, for $k+n=3$ with $0\le k\le2$, the map $\operatorname{id}_{B^k}\times a_n$ is a PL immersion of $B^k\times(T^n\setminus\{p\})$ into $B^k\times\mathbb R^n$, respecting its boundary faces and equal to the identity on $B^k\times2B^n$, where $B^d=[-1,1]^d$. Rescaling the flat lattice identifies this with the standard torus convention. This supplies the punctured-torus immersion used by handle straightening; it gives no PL structure to an arbitrary topological manifold.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard affine torus, its explicit global translation frame, puncture and protected cube. In its flat puncture chart write $C_r=p+[-r,r]^n$, and put $M=T^n\setminus\operatorname{Int}C_{1/4}$ and $V=T^n\setminus\operatorname{Int}C_{7/32}$.

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] A compact connected smooth triad with nonempty outgoing boundary has a finite handle presentation relative to its incoming boundary, with no top-index handles. Its proof reverses the triad, eliminates the finitely many superfluous zero-handles and dualizes. [[prop-dual-elimination-of-top-index-handles]]

[F2] A full-rank formal immersion already genuine near a compact incoming piece can be integrated over a finite handle of core index $j<n$, retaining every one of its $n$ source columns and fixing a smaller incoming germ. The actual core restriction proof supplies the positive normal direction because $j<n$; realization uses the transverse columns and positive cocore compression, not a rank-dropping collapse. [[lem-formal-immersion-homotopies-extend-over-a-subcritical-handle]] [[lem-parametric-immersion-extension-on-a-disk]] [[lem-restriction-of-formal-immersion-data-has-the-parametric-lifting-property]] Formal columns need not equal the derivative of the base map during the deformation. [[def-formal-immersion-between-smooth-manifolds]]

[F3] Finite convex cells have compatible triangulations by coning their faces in increasing dimension, with their vertex averages as specified cone points. Segment integration controls the value error by the derivative error. Euclidean closed balls are complete and a strict contraction of one into itself has a unique fixed point. [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]] [[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]] [[thm-euclidean-space-complete]] [[thm-banach-fixed-point]] The ordinary inverse theorem applies to the initial smooth local immersion charts. [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]]

## Proof

1.1 For $n=1$, represent the punctured circle uniquely by $-20<t<12$ and set $a_1(q(t))=t$. This is an orientation-preserving PL homeomorphism onto that interval, and is the identity on the protected interval. Suppose henceforth $n=2$ or $3$. In the flat chart at zero let $A$ be the closed round ball of radius eight. Let $Q$ be the torus with the open round ball of radius $1/8$ about $p$ removed. These balls are disjoint and lie in injective affine charts. Choose a smooth radial scalar cutoff equal to one on the radius-nine ball and zero outside the radius-ten ball, and set $f_0(q(x))=\chi(x)x$ there, zero elsewhere. Its support lies strictly inside the fundamental cube, so this is a globally smooth map into $\mathbb R^n$. Give it the formal columns $F_0$ that send the global translation frame of the torus to the coordinate frame of $\mathbb R^n$. They are isomorphisms everywhere, and equal $df_0$ on a neighborhood of $A$. Thus these are genuine formal input data, with an actual identity immersion on that neighborhood. [A1, F2, given, construct]

1.2 There is an explicit PL homeomorphism $\rho:T^n\setminus\{p\}\to\operatorname{Int}M$, fixed outside $C_1$. For $j=0,1,\ldots$ use source shell radii $r_j=2^{-j}$ and target radii $t_j=1/4+(3/4)2^{-j}$. Triangulate the boundary of the unit cube once by compatible face cones. For each face simplex $\sigma$ of that boundary, the convex hull of $r_j\sigma$ and $r_{j+1}\sigma$ is a radial frustum; these finitely many frusta cover the closed cubical shell and meet along their faces. The corresponding target frusta use $t_j,t_{j+1}$ and have the same face incidences. Apply the face-coning construction of [F3] to these convex cells, choosing the average of each face's vertices. Send each source face barycenter to the corresponding target face barycenter, and extend affinely on every flag simplex. These are nondegenerate simplex maps with matching face inverses, hence a PL homeomorphism of that shell. On its two boundary cubes the map is just the indicated scalar dilation, so adjacent shell maps agree. At the outermost cube $r_0=t_0=1$, it is the identity and pastes to the identity outside $C_1$. The source shells exhaust $C_1\setminus\{p\}$, and the target shells exhaust $C_1\setminus C_{1/4}$. Each compact subset of either punctured/interior domain meets only finitely many shells, since the omitted point or boundary cube has positive distance from that compact set. Thus the pasted map and inverse are locally finite PL maps. All vertices and cone points were specified by the displayed radii and finite vertex averages; no countable selection is hidden in this construction. [F3, construct, algebra]

2.1 The compact triad $W=Q\setminus\operatorname{Int}A$, with incoming face $\partial A$ and outgoing face $\partial Q$, is connected. To see this without a classification theorem, join two points by a finite coordinate polygonal path in the torus and detour its portions inside either deleted ball along a slightly enlarged sphere; those spheres are path connected for $n\ge2$, and the two enlargements can be disjoint. Its outgoing face is nonempty. Apply [F1], then attach its finitely many handles to $A$. Every core index is $j<n$, so [F2] successively integrates the actual global formal data, fixing a smaller already genuine incoming collar at each stage. To retain formal data on the unprocessed region, extend each compact handle formula into a sufficiently thin outgoing buffer and substitute its deformation time multiplied by a source cutoff. Use the same substituted time in the base map and its full bundle monomorphism. Rank is retained because a formal monomorphism is not required to be the derivative in that transition buffer. The formulas are the identity near the incoming frozen piece and outside the outgoing buffer; the open rank condition and compact time interval give one buffer width. This is precisely the full-source rather than core-only construction in [F2]. After finitely many steps there is a smooth immersion $g:Q\to\mathbb R^n$, equal to the identity near $A$. Its differential has positive determinant: its formal homotopy starts in the positive translation frame and never loses rank. Only its restriction to the interior of $Q$ will be used below. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 We now make this map PL on the ORIGINAL flat torus structure, not on a newly chosen abstract triangulation. Use the flat polyhedra $M,V$ in Given. They satisfy $M\subset\operatorname{Int}V\subset V\subset\operatorname{Int}Q$, and $g$ is smoothly defined on a neighborhood of $V$. The least singular value of $dg$ has a positive minimum $s$ there after a small compact neighborhood is fixed. Its derivatives have one modulus of continuity $\omega(r)\to0$ in the affine charts; translations are the chart transitions, so their derivative comparisons agree. This follows by finite compact covers, rather than a choice of infinitely many local margins. [F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Subdivide the period-32 flat grid with spacing $h=1/(32m)$ by the ordered-coordinate Kuhn simplices. The diagonals on every cube face agree, including identified period faces. This gives a finite periodic triangulation of the torus; $M,V$ and the protected flat cube are subpolyhedra. There are finitely many simplex shapes up to translation and scale, each nondegenerate, so their inradius divided by diameter has one positive lower bound $\vartheta$. On every simplex of $V$, define $S_m$ by affine interpolation of the actual values of $g$ at its vertices. These maps agree on faces and on period identifications. For a simplex of diameter $d$, expand $g$ about its incenter $b$. Segment integration bounds its error from $g(b)+dg_b(x-b)$ by $d\omega(d)$. The affine secant of these errors has absolute value at most that bound, and its derivative norm is at most $2\omega(d)/\vartheta$: evaluate its difference between $b$ and $b+r v$, where $r$ is the inradius and $v$ a unit direction. Adding the derivative oscillation gives $$\|dS_m-dg_x\|\le(2/\vartheta+1)\omega(d)$$ on every full simplex and its face tangent directions. Thus the piecewise derivative error tends uniformly to zero. On every simplex contained in the identity region of $g$, the interpolant is exactly the identity. A sufficiently fine grid therefore fixes the whole protected cube pointwise. [F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Choose $m$ so large that the preceding derivative error is less than $s/8$. Choose one small radius $r>0$ such that every flat radius-$r$ ball about a point of $M$ lies in the interior of $V$, its quotient coordinate is injective, and $\omega(2r)<s/8$. Such a common radius exists because $M$ is compactly contained in that interior and the lattice period is fixed. In one such convex ball centered at $x_0$, put $L=dg_{x_0}$. On each grid simplex its map has derivative differing from $L$ by less than $s/4$. The error $S_m-Lx$ is consequently $s/4$-Lipschitz on the ball. Indeed subdivide a segment into its finitely many grid-simplex pieces and integrate the derivative on each; if it runs in a face use the common face derivative, or approximate it by transverse segments and pass to the continuous limit. Hence $$|S_m(x)-S_m(y)|\ge(3s/4)|x-y|.$$ In particular this proves local injectivity on whole neighborhoods, across every face, edge and vertex, not just injectivity on individual simplices. It also gives full-rank affine derivatives there, and their determinant is positive by the same small perturbation bound from $dg$. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Its local image is open with a genuine PL inverse. For a smaller closed ball of radius $r_0$ centered at $x_0$, and $y$ sufficiently close to $S_m(x_0)$, consider $$T_y(x)=x+L^{-1}(y-S_m(x)).$$ The preceding derivative-error bound and $\|L^{-1}\|\le1/s$ make this a contraction with factor at most $1/4$. If $|L^{-1}(y-S_m(x_0))|<3r_0/4$, it maps the closed ball into itself. [F3] gives its unique fixed point, which satisfies $S_m(x)=y$. The lower-distance bound of step 5.1 gives continuity of this local inverse. In a sufficiently small star it is PL: each affine simplex map is invertible, and local injectivity implies that images of adjacent simplices meet just in their common faces. Restrict to a smaller polyhedral neighborhood if necessary. The finite inverse maps on these image simplices are affine and agree on all faces. This establishes an orientation-preserving PL local homeomorphism on a neighborhood of every point of $M$. Denote its restriction to $M$ by $b$. This argument did not assume a global embedding of $g$ or of $S_m$. [F3, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Set $a_n=b\rho$. Every point has the explicit PL inverse charts of Steps 6.1 and 1.2, so this is a PL immersion on the standard punctured torus. The end homeomorphism fixes the protected cube because its support is in the disjoint puncture chart, and $b$ is the identity there by step 4.1. Its orientation is positive: the shell face maps interpolate outward-ordered nested cubes and are orientation preserving, and $b$ has positive affine derivatives by step 5.1. Composing the shell triangulations with the finite subdivisions for $b$, by taking finite face intersections and coning, gives a locally finite source triangulation realizing the composite; compact subsets require only finitely many such intersections. Finally products with $B^k$ are triangulated by finite product-cell cones on each locally finite piece. Since the first factor map is the identity, these maps preserve its boundary faces and their local half-space charts, and are the identity on $B^k\times2B^n$. This proves exactly the asserted punctured-torus and product immersion. The construction assumes only the standard smooth and PL torus and its translation frame; no step assigns either structure to a general topological three-manifold. Countable Choice enters through [F1]–[F2], and that qualification has not been removed from an unqualified ambient consumer. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 1.2, construct] ∎
