## Statement

Assume full AC. Every compact topological three-manifold $M$, with boundary allowed, is homeomorphic to the realization of a finite combinatorial three-manifold triangulation, with boundary allowed (the empty complex when $M=\varnothing$). Its boundary is the realization of a subcomplex. Thus $M$ admits a finite compatible PL structure, without any previously supplied smooth or PL structure on its interior.

If a TOP product collar of the boundary, together with a finite PL structure on its boundary surface, has already been supplied, the new PL structure may be chosen equal to that product PL structure on a smaller boundary neighborhood. No smooth marking or smooth ambient isotopy is asserted. Full AC remains the hypothesis of this theorem; no choice-free theorem is obtained by omitting that premise.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC and the compact Hausdorff second-countable TOP three-manifold with its ordinary Euclidean and half-space charts.

[A1] Full AC. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A compact connected boundaryless TOP surface has a finite triangulation under full AC. [[lem-compact-surface-admits-a-finite-triangulation]]

[F2] A compact TOP3-manifold with a supplied finite PL boundary surface has an actual TOP product collar; no ambient PL structure is required. [[lem-compact-topological-three-manifolds-with-triangulated-boundary-have-collars]]

[F3] A TOP embedding of an open3-dimensional $k$-handle in a coordinate $\mathbb R^3$, already PL near its attaching boundary, can be straightened on its prescribed fixed compact core by a source ambient TOP isotopy fixed on the attaching boundary and outside twice that core. All $k=0,1,2,3$ are included. [[thm-three-dimensional-topological-handles-admit-relative-pl-straightenings]]

[F4] Finite convex cell-face maps and compatible marked polyhedra admit finite common triangulations and subdivisions. The local finite PL chart-gluing construction in the handle supplier's Proof2.1 gives a locally finite compatible triangulation once a PL atlas is ALREADY supplied; it does not manufacture a PL atlas on a TOP manifold. [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]] [[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]] [[thm-three-dimensional-topological-handles-admit-relative-pl-straightenings]]

## Proof

1.1 We first give the finite PL handle construction needed on a compact part of a boundaryless PL3-manifold $O$ with its supplied compatible triangulation. Given a compact set $C\subset O$, finitely many closed tetrahedra and their finite stars contain it; include all their faces. These stars are compact and contained in $O$. In a further barycentric subdivision, truncate the selected simplex stars in increasing dimension. At a vertex, the region with that vertex coordinate at least $1-\epsilon_0$ is a cone on its standard PL sphere link and is a parametrized PL ball. Along an edge, outside these vertex caps, take the tube where the sum of the coordinates off that edge is at most $\epsilon_1\ll\epsilon_0$. Its core is the remaining edge interval and its normal section is the cone on the edge's circle link, a parametrized disk. Along a triangle outside those earlier regions, take the slab where its opposite coordinate is at most $\epsilon_2\ll\epsilon_1$ in each incident tetrahedron. Its remaining central polygon is convex and its normal link has two points, so it is a disk times an interval. Finally each tetrahedral remainder is a convex polytope: its inequalities are the complements of the vertex, edge and face cuts just listed. Choose all positive constants small enough that each remainder contains the barycenter and the face incidence stays fixed. Every displayed product parameter is actual finite PL: match the finitely many convex sections and side faces, then cone their boundary subdivisions as in [F4]. The circle or sphere link parameters supply the corresponding normal disk or ball parameters at edge and vertex stars. Thus these are parametrized handles $B^k\times B^{3-k}$, attached along $\partial B^k\times B^{3-k}$ to the lower-index pieces. They cover the selected tetrahedra. Each has a slightly extended open-handle parameter $B^k\times\mathbb R^{3-k}$ with compact twice-core image: extend the transverse product a little and use a locally finite PL cubical radial map from $\mathbb R^{3-k}$ onto its open transverse disk. Such a map is obtained from successive matching cube frusta with radii tending to the finite outer radius, by [F4]. All selected families are finite. Their widths may be made smaller, and their attaching ends placed in arbitrarily small collars of the lower-index pieces, before the next index is treated. No general handle-existence theorem or global TOP triangulation has been assumed. [given, F4, construct]

1.2 Initialize the construction at the boundary. Its components are open in the compact surface $\partial M$, so there are finitely many; each is compact, since it is also closed. Each is a boundaryless TOP2-manifold by the given half-space charts. Apply [F1] to obtain finite PL structures on them and take their finite disjoint union. By [F2] there is a TOP collar $c:\partial M\times[0,1]\to M$. Transport the product PL structure to its half-open image. This is a PL atlas on a full relatively open boundary neighborhood, without having asserted that the TOP collar was smooth or PL in some previously supplied ambient structure. If the boundary is empty, start with the empty processed set. If a collar and its finite boundary PL structure were already supplied in the Statement's relative case, use those instead. Cover the complement of a smaller collar by finitely many interior TOP coordinate domains whose closures miss $\partial M$; it is compact, so a finite list suffices. Together with the initial boundary neighborhood these form a finite cover. [A1, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 Let $\Sigma$ be a PL structure on $O$, and let $\phi:O\to\mathbb R^3$ be a TOP embedding, so its coordinate structure is a second PL atlas. For every compact $C\subset O$ there is a homeomorphism $H:O\to O$ supported in a compact subset of $O$ such that $\phi H$ is PL from $\Sigma$ on a neighborhood of $C$. Construct it using step 1.1. For a zero-handle, [F3] applies without an attaching condition. After that stage $\phi H$ is PL on neighborhoods of the finite zero-handle cores. Choose the next tubes' attaching collars in those open neighborhoods. Leave a smaller lower-index core neighborhood protected. Trim a short band off each attaching end before applying [F3], and extend its isotopy by the identity on the trimmed band. This is legitimate: the entire transverse closure at an attaching end lies in the already PL open region, so compactness provides a uniform band; the smaller rescaled handle has PL attaching boundary. The supports lie outside the protected lower-index cores. Apply [F3] to $\phi H\eta$ in each of the finite open-handle parameters $\eta$, and update $H$ by precomposition with $\eta P_t\eta^{-1}$, extended by the identity. Its support is in the compact twice-core image, and the attaching boundary is fixed. The protected earlier regions remain PL; the new cores become PL and their end bands were already PL. Continue through indices1,2,3, choosing the thin transverse widths after each preceding finite stage. At index3 the residual convex ball pieces cover whatever remains of the selected tetrahedra. Hence the final map is PL near all of $C$. Every extension is continuous across the handle boundary because it is fixed there and has compact support; its inverse has the identical pasting property. Finitely many such maps give the stated compactly supported homeomorphism (indeed an ambient TOP isotopy). This uses [F3]'s FIXED-core conclusion, not an uncontrolled transported image of the core. [A1, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 This gives a precise finite chart-gluing operation. Suppose a PL atlas has already been defined on an open set $A\subset M$, and $B\subset\operatorname{Int}M$ is an open coordinate domain with TOP chart $\phi:B\to\mathbb R^3$. Suppose $A,B$ and some finitely many other open sets cover $M$. Shrink all those cover members to open sets whose closures lie in their old members and which still cover $M$. Such a finite shrinking requires no metric theorem: for every point take a small closed chart ball or half-ball inside an allowed cover member, extract finitely many whose interiors cover by compactness, and group these interiors by their assigned member. Denote the shrunken old processed set by $A'$ and the new chart domain by $B'$. The compact set $C=\overline{A'}\cap\overline{B'}$ lies in $O=A\cap B$. The old PL atlas restricts to $O$ and has the compatible locally finite triangulation of [F4]. Apply step 2.1 to $\phi|_O$ and this $C$. Extend the resulting $H$ by the identity from $O$ to $B$; its support was compact in $O$, so this is a homeomorphism of $B$. Give $B'$ the single chart $\phi H$, and keep the old atlas on $A'$. On their entire overlap, which is contained in $C$, the new chart is PL in the old structure. Thus all transition maps and their inverses are PL there. The two atlases give a PL atlas on $A'\cup B'$. The remaining shrunken cover members still cover everything else. This is an actual compatible atlas extension, not an assertion that unrelated TOP chart transitions were PL. [A1, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Process those finitely many interior charts in order by step 3.1. At each stage the processed open set carries the PL atlas already constructed, every remaining chart retains its ordinary TOP coordinates, and all shrunken members still cover the original $M$. A finite union of the processed members plays the role of $A$; its closure after shrinking lies in that previous processed set. Thus the compact-overlap condition of step 3.1 holds at every stage. After the last chart the processed union is all of $M$, and its transitions are PL. All correcting homeomorphisms were supported in the interiors of the chosen charts, whose closures miss the boundary. There are only finitely many supports, so one smaller original boundary collar misses their union. The atlas there is exactly the initial product PL atlas. This proves the asserted relative boundary control. There is no infinite limit of changing TOP charts in this compact argument. [step 3.1, step 1.2, construct]

5.1 Finally realize this PL atlas by a finite compatible simplicial complex, using the finite chart-gluing construction of [F4] on closed coordinate cubes and boundary half-cubes whose interiors cover $M$. On compact chart overlaps the PL transition maps have finite subdivisions, so their intersection polyhedra receive common finite subdivisions; extend these across their incident convex cells by face-coning. Identify the equal overlap subcomplexes, making them full by a barycentric subdivision first. The finite quotient then has an abstract simplicial subdivision with no unintended vertex identifications and a continuous bijective realization map to $M$; compactness and Hausdorffness make it a homeomorphism. Retain the boundary faces in every common subdivision, so they form a subcomplex. A further finite subdivision puts each vertex star in one PL coordinate chart. In that chart its incident simplices form a finite linear fan filling a ball, or a half-ball at the boundary. Intersecting its rays with a small polyhedral sphere, or half-sphere, and coning matching face subdivisions gives the standard PL link sphere or disk. The same transverse fan argument treats links of higher-dimensional simplices. This verifies the stated combinatorial manifold condition. The empty manifold has the empty finite triangulation. Every nonempty case follows from the actual TOP boundary collar and the compact-overlap handle corrections, with full AC used exactly as declared in [A1] and its suppliers. No prior interior PL/smooth structure and no unqualified TOP prime theorem was used. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, construct] ∎
