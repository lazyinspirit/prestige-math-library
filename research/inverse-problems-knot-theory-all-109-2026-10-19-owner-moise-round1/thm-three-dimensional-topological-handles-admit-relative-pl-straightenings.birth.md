## Statement

Assume full AC. Put $B^j=[-1,1]^j$. Let $k+n=3$, $0\le k\le3$, and let $h:B^k\times\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^3$ be a topological embedding which is PL on a neighborhood of $\partial B^k\times\mathbb R^n$. There is an isotopy of embeddings $h_t$, $0\le t\le1$, starting at $h$, such that $h_1$ is PL on a neighborhood of $B^k\times B^n$, and
$$h_t=h\quad\text{on}\quad(\partial B^k\times\mathbb R^n)\cup\bigl(B^k\times(\mathbb R^n\setminus\operatorname{Int}(2B^n))\bigr).$$
For $n=0$ this means a boundary-relative straightening of the whole three-ball. More precisely $h_t=hP_t$ for an ambient TOP isotopy of the source with jointly continuous inverses and the displayed fixed set. No smooth isotopy is asserted.

This is a full-AC-qualified TOP-to-PL handle statement, including handles whose interior was only topologically parametrized. It does not by itself provide a finite triangulation of an arbitrary compact TOP manifold or discharge an original choice-free consumer.

## Proof

1.1 If $k=3$, the image boundary $h(\partial B^3)$ is a finite PL sphere. By [F7] its bounded side is a parametrized PL ball, and this side is exactly $h(B^3)$: invariance of domain makes $h(\operatorname{Int}B^3)$ open, compactness makes it closed relative to the sphere complement, and it is connected and bounded. The boundary PL map $h|_{\partial B^3}$ extends over the balls by coning compatible boundary subdivisions. Call that PL extension $j:B^3\to h(B^3)$. The homeomorphism $P=h^{-1}j$ fixes the source boundary. Its explicit Alexander family $P_t(x)=tP(x/t)$ for $x\in tB^3$, identity elsewhere, and $P_0=\operatorname{id}$ is jointly continuous with jointly continuous inverse: every moving point moves by at most $t\operatorname{diam}B^3$. Then $hP_1=j$, proving this case. Henceforth $k\le2$. [A1, F7, given, construct]

2.1 Let $\Sigma=h^{-1}(\mathrm{standard})$ on $X=B^k\times\mathbb R^n$, understood as a pulled-back PL atlas; $h$ is an actual PL chart map in that atlas. Let $a=\operatorname{id}_{B^k}\times a_n$ be [F1]'s immersion of $B^k\times(T^n\setminus\{p\})$ into $X$, where the torus has period32 and $p=(12,0,\ldots,0)$. The map $ha$ is a local homeomorphism, with boundary half-space charts; its inverse charts pull back the ambient standard PL atlas to an orientable PL atlas $\Sigma_1$. Transitions between two such developing charts are the identity wherever both branches agree. On the protected $B^k\times2B^n$ this atlas is exactly $\Sigma$. For $k>0$, boundedness of the image of $a_n$ is essential: its closure is compact in $\mathbb R^n$, so the boundary PL neighborhood of $h$ contains $\{x:|x|_\infty>1-\delta\}\times\overline{a_n(T^n\setminus p)}$ for some fixed $0<\delta<1$. There $ha$ is PL in the ORIGINAL standard product structure. Consequently $\Sigma_1$ agrees with the standard structure on this uniform collar, and it extends consistently over the whole boundary collar, including over $p$. Put $M=B^k\times T^n$ and $V=M\setminus C_0$, where $C_0=(1-\delta)B^k\times\{p\}$; for $k=0$ put $C_0=\{p\}$. The extended atlas on $V$ is standard near its compact boundary and still agrees with $\Sigma$ on the protected local handle. [A1, F1, given, construct]

2.2 We record why a locally finite compatible triangulation is actually available for this PL atlas, without assigning a PL atlas to an arbitrary TOP manifold. Choose a countable locally finite cover by closed chart cubes, each contained in a slightly larger chart cube; choose it using the explicit metrizable countable topology of $V$ and [A1]. Interiors cover. Every cube meets only finitely many others. On a compact intersection the PL transition has a finite subdivision, so the images of all the other incident cube faces are finite polyhedra in that cube's coordinates. A finite union of such cubes is triangulated inductively: subdivide the finite overlap polyhedra compatibly in both charts, extend those subdivisions across the incident convex simplices by face-coning, and identify the equal overlap subcomplexes. One barycentric subdivision makes the marked overlaps full, so the glued simplicial complex has no unintended simplex identifications. This finite construction changes an existing triangulation only on the finite stars of simplices incident to the new cube. Apply it along the enumerated cover. It stabilizes near every compact set. Indeed finitely many chart cubes cover that set; they and their incident finite stars form a compact finite union. Local finiteness leaves only finitely many later cubes meeting this union, and a refinement has no support beyond these incident stars. Later subdivisions only shrink simplices, so cannot create a new distant incidence. The stabilized subdivisions give a locally finite compatible manifold triangulation of $V$. The same argument retains any already supplied locally finite subcomplex after using its finite common subdivisions at each attachment. This is ordinary finite PL overlap gluing, not a TOP triangulation assertion; its hypothesis is the atlas explicitly constructed in step 2.1. [A1, F7, step 2.1, construct]

3.1 The manifold $V$ has the precise end conditions of [F2]. In a standard puncture chart take nested small rectangular neighborhoods $U_j$ of the missing cell $C_0$, with closures in $\operatorname{Int}M$, intersection $C_0$, and side radii decreasing to zero in the $n$ puncture directions and to the radius $1-\delta$ in the $k$ other directions. For $k=0$ these are just nested cubes. Each $U_j\setminus C_0$ deformation retracts onto its outer boundary $S^2$: push points radially away from the center to that rectangular boundary. A ray not lying in the missing cell's linear span never meets $C_0$, and on a ray in that span the initial point is already beyond the missing cell, so this homotopy never crosses it. Thus this end neighborhood is connected and simply connected by [F2]. Every compact subset of $V$ misses some $U_j$. For a compact PL core containing $\partial V$, this connected end neighborhood belongs to exactly one complementary component; every other component has compact closure in $M\setminus U_j\subset V$. This verifies both required end conditions. Choose a protected compact set containing $B^k\times2B^n$, the entire boundary and, for $k=0$, finite standard coordinate generator loops for $\pi_1(T^3)$ avoiding $p$. Apply [F2] to obtain a connected compact PL core $K$ retaining it, with a single interior PL sphere frontier $S$. [F2, step 2.2, construct]

4.1 By [F3] that sphere has a parametrized TOP ball side in the underlying product $M$. This side is the closure of $M\setminus K$. For $k>0$ it cannot contain $K$, since $K$ contains $\partial M$ whereas the ball side lies in $\operatorname{Int}M$. For $k=0$ it cannot contain $K$, since the protected coordinate generator loops in $K$ have nontrivial image in $\pi_1(T^3)$ and cannot lie in a ball. The two components are the relative interior of $K$ and its outside: $K$ is connected, has exactly this frontier, and the sphere has exactly two sides by [F3]. Cap $S$ by a standard PL ball, matching its given boundary parameter. The actual TOP ball parameter extends that same boundary map by a cone, so replacing the outside ball gives a homeomorphism to a compact finite PL manifold $M_{11}$, identical on $K$ and near the original boundary. Identify its underlying TOP space back with $M$ by this marking. Thus $M_{11}$ has a finite PL structure equal to $\Sigma$ on a neighborhood of the protected local handle. Every PL interior sphere of $M_{11}$ has the TOP ball-side property by [F3], now applied to its supplied global PL structure. [A1, F3, F7, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 For $k=0$ choose the protected PL ball $D$ inside the local handle as follows. In $h(\operatorname{Int}(2B^3))$, with its inherited STANDARD PL structure, apply [F2] to the compact set $h(B^3)$. Its underlying TOP space is an open ball, so its one end has the same explicit simply connected radial neighborhoods as in step 3.1. The resulting compact core has one PL sphere frontier and is a parametrized PL ball by [F7], since it sits in standard $\mathbb R^3$. Pull it back by $h$ to obtain a finite $\Sigma$-PL interior ball $D$ containing $B^3$ and lying in $\operatorname{Int}(2B^3)$. Include $D$ in the protected compact set when choosing $K$. Apply [F5]'s finite prime presentation and interior-ball core replacement to $M_{11}$; its fundamental group is the nontrivial abelian group $\mathbb Z^3$, and [F3] supplies all ball sides. This gives an irreducible finite PL manifold $M_2$ and TOP product marking unchanged near $D$. A finite simplicial approximation of that marking induces the same fundamental-group isomorphism by [F7]. The $T^3$ rigidity in [F5] therefore gives a PL homeomorphism $g:M_2\to T^3$ homotopic to that TOP marking. No unqualified TOP prime theorem or PL Poincare claim was used. [A1, F2, F3, F5, F7, step 4.1, construct]

5.2 For $k=1,2$, apply [F4] in the ORIGINAL local handle, obtaining its protected PL ball $D$ with exact lateral trace and one annulus or two frontier disks. It is contained in the protected part of $K$. Apply [F4]'s special boundary-prime replacement to $M_{11}$ and this actual $D$, using the TOP sphere-ball sides of step 4.1. It gives a finite PL product model $M_2$ and a PL homeomorphism $g:M_2\to B^k\times T^n$ fixed on the ENTIRE original boundary and homotopic relative to it to the TOP marking; the new structure is unchanged near $D$. For use with [F6], the $k=1$ carving has the required cyclic exterior in the universal-cover coordinates. In $G=B^1\times(\operatorname{Int}(2B^2)\setminus B^2)$, its frontier annulus $T$ has both end circles equal to the generator, so $\pi_1(T)\to\pi_1(G)=\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism. Its two sides are connected, distinguished by the inside and outside of $D$; every component meets one of the two connected side collars. The edge group injects into both side groups because its composite into $\pi_1(G)$ injects. Amalgam normal form [F7] then injects those side groups into $\mathbb Z$, and each already contains the whole edge image. Thus each side group is $\mathbb Z$. On the outside choose $\rho<2$ above the maximum radius of $D$; the standard cylinder $B^1\times\rho\partial B^2$ is its generator. Adding the untouched outer product $B^1\times(\mathbb R^2\setminus\rho B^2)$ along that cylinder changes neither connectedness nor the cyclic group. This proves $\pi_1(X\setminus\operatorname{Int}_X D)=\mathbb Z$ with the outer generator. It is the exact shell argument of [F4]'s proof, now giving the [F6] input, not a claim that an unrecognized PL shell was already standard. [A1, F3, F4, F7, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 In all cases identify the underlying $M_2$ with the TOP product through its actual marking. Let $e:X\to M$ be the standard period32 cover and let $\Sigma_3=e^{-1}(\mathrm{PL}(M_2))$. Lift the finite triangulation simplex by simplex; this is a locally finite compatible triangulation because the coordinate translation cover has only finitely many translates meeting a compact coordinate set. Near $D$ it agrees with $\Sigma$, since the flat inclusion of the protected handle in the torus cover is injective and every preceding replacement was identical there. Lift the homotopy from the TOP marking to $g$, starting with the identity cover lift, using [F7]. Its final lift $\widetilde g:(X,\Sigma_3)\to X_{\rm std}$ is a PL homeomorphism, obtained by lifting the finite local PL pieces of $g$. For $k>0$ the whole lifted boundary stays fixed through the homotopy: its base homotopy is constant and its initial lift is the identity. In every case $g_*$ is the identity in the marked coordinates, so $\widetilde g(x,y+32z)=\widetilde g(x,y)+(0,32z)$. The second-coordinate displacement is consequently periodic, and its continuous absolute value has a finite maximum on the compact fundamental block $B^k\times[0,32]^n$. This proves the actual boundedness needed by [F6], not just properness. [F4, F5, F7, step 5.1, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Apply [F6] to $\Sigma_3$, the bounded boundary-fixed PL model $\widetilde g$ and the retained $D$. For $k=0$ its interior-ball case applies. For $k=1$ step 5.2 gives the exact cyclic complement and generator. For $k=2$ the two marked disk windows are those of [F4]. The original embedding $h$ is PL from $\Sigma_3$ near $D$, since there $\Sigma_3=\Sigma=h^{-1}(\mathrm{standard})$. Thus [F6] gives an ambient TOP isotopy $P_t$ fixed on the original boundary and outside $B^k\times\operatorname{Int}(2B^n)$, with $hP_1$ PL on a neighborhood of the prescribed fixed core. Put $h_t=hP_t$. Its joint continuity, embedding property and boundary/support equalities follow from that actual ambient family and its joint inverse. Together with step 1.1 this proves all four handle indices with the original TOP interior scope. Full AC remains explicit in the declared Brown, immersion, covering/atlas and special reassembly uses; no choice-free TOP consumer or global atlas theorem has been claimed. [A1, F6, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 6.1, construct] ∎
