## Statement

Let $G$ be an orientable PL three-manifold, possibly noncompact, with a GIVEN locally finite compatible manifold triangulation and boundary a subcomplex.

(a) Any finite PL circle $C\subset\partial G$ nullhomotopic in $G$ bounds a compact properly embedded finite PL disk in $G$.

(b) Suppose $G$ is connected and $\pi_1(G)\cong\mathbb Z$. Let $C_0,C_1\subset\partial G$ be disjoint oriented finite PL circles with nonzero images in $\pi_1(G)$. Suppose there is a continuous map of an oriented annulus into $G$ whose two boundary maps parametrize exactly $C_0,C_1$ with opposite induced boundary orientations. Require that on sufficiently short source boundary collars this map is the specified embedded PL product collar of each $C_i$ in $G$, pointing into $\operatorname{Int}G$, and that the remaining source interior maps into $\operatorname{Int}G$. Then $C_0\sqcup C_1$ bounds a compact orientable properly embedded finite PL annulus in $G$, retaining a smaller one of these specified PL collars.

The annulus is not required to be isotopic to the original continuous map. No irreducibility hypothesis is needed. This is an assertion inside an ALREADY supplied locally finite PL manifold. It supplies no triangulation or smoothing of an arbitrary TOP shell, and no protected handle neighborhood or prime decomposition is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The locally finite oriented PL manifold and the circle or annulus-map data in the Statement.

[F1] Finite compact orientable PL manifolds satisfy the marked-subsurface Loop theorem and the exact-boundary Dehn lemma. [[thm-loop-theorem-for-finitely-triangulated-orientable-pl-three-manifolds]] [[thm-dehn-lemma-for-finitely-triangulated-orientable-pl-three-manifolds]]

[F2] Finite maps of pairs have simplicial approximations with common-carrier homotopies, but no positive-dimensional pointwise-relative conclusion is presumed. [[thm-finite-simplicial-approximation-for-maps-of-pairs]]

[F3] Compact PL subpolyhedra have collars when their local charts supply collars. Finite subcomplex neighborhoods are cut out by the barycentric zero-one vertex function and have the explicit mixed-face product charts, also at the ambient boundary. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]] [[lem-finite-subpolyhedra-have-compact-pl-manifold-neighborhoods]]

[F4] Polygonal disks, prescribed-boundary disk extensions, standard boundary windows and finite convex cell triangulations have actual finite PL parameters. [[lem-finite-polygonal-disk-and-collar-surgery]] [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]] [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]

[F5] The finite surface-compression proof cuts a nullhomotopy along a two-sided surface, removes surface-null inverse-image circles, and applies the marked Loop theorem on an innermost remaining cut face. It gives an embedded compression disk, preserves orientability and the boundary, and strictly decreases its stated nonnegative finite surface weight. [[lem-finite-pl-surface-compressions-produce-pi-one-injective-components]]

[F6] Reduced words in a finite graph free group are unique; a free group injecting into $\mathbb Z$ has rank at most one. [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products]]

## Proof

1.1 Every compact set $K\subset G$ has a compact finite PL manifold neighborhood $V$, with $K\subset\operatorname{Int}_G V$, and with every sufficiently small prescribed compact boundary collar included in its boundary trace. Here is the localization needed to use the COMPACT suppliers. Local finiteness makes $K$ meet only finitely many simplex stars. Choose finitely many smaller PL chart neighborhoods with compact closures covering it and take a finite subdivision of these finitely many stars so their closed union $P$ contains a neighborhood of $K$; if a particular relatively open neighborhood is prescribed, choose the stars within it. Include any finite marked subpolyhedra and their collars in $P$. After one more barycentric subdivision $P$ is full. Assign one to its vertices and zero to all other vertices of the locally finite triangulation, extending linearly to $r$. Its support lies in the finite closed star of $P$. The set $V=\{r\ge1/2\}$ is consequently compact and finite. The proof of [F3] applies verbatim on these finitely many mixed simplices: translation along a difference of a one-vertex and a zero-vertex identifies $r$ with a product coordinate, preserving boundary faces; the quadrant flattening makes $V$ a PL manifold, with $V\cap\partial G$ a boundary subsurface. This verifies the needed noncompact-ambient extension from its actual local construction, without asserting that the compact-neighborhood supplier's Statement had a noncompact ambient hypothesis. Connected $K$ is placed in one connected $V$ by retaining that component, and finitely many components may instead be joined by finite chart paths before applying the construction. [F3, F4, given, construct]

1.2 For (a), take the compact image of a nullhomotopy of $C$ and a short finite boundary collar of $C$. Step 1.1 gives a finite compact orientable PL neighborhood $V$ containing this image and collar, with $C\subset\partial V\cap\partial G$. The circle is still nullhomotopic in $V$. Apply [F1]'s exact-boundary Dehn lemma in $V$. Its proper disk has boundary exactly $C$ and interior in $\operatorname{Int}V\subset\operatorname{Int}G$; it is therefore proper in $G$ too. This proves (a). [F1, step 1.1, construct]

2.1 We turn the annulus map in (b) into a finite integral PL two-chain with its EXACT boundary and an unaltered short product collar. Choose an inward collar height $d>0$ small enough that the given annulus map is its specified PL product near each boundary. Cut its source at height $d$. The remaining source is a compact annulus mapping into $\operatorname{Int}G$. Its compact image has a finite triangulated neighborhood there by step 1.1. Use [F2] to approximate its map as a finite map of pairs, the two boundary curves lying in sufficiently small finite triangulated neighborhoods of the corresponding height-$d$ circles. Make the relevant stars small enough that all these boundary carriers lie at collar heights greater than $d/2$. Its approximation need not fix the boundary. Correct this explicitly. The original height-$d$ boundary maps and the approximating ones are PL and have a common carrier on every simplex after finite subdivisions. Triangulate the corresponding circle-times-interval prisms and send their vertices by the two endpoint maps; every prism simplex has its image in that common convex target simplex. Affine extension therefore gives a finite PL boundary homotopy in the interior of $G$, with the exact two endpoint maps, still at heights greater than $d/2$. Attach these two mapped annuli and then the original vertical collars from height zero to $d$. The resulting finite PL annulus MAP has exactly the required boundary, and below height $d/4$ its image is exactly the original two embedded product collars, with no other image there. Its oriented triangular pushforward chain $c$ is finite and integral, and its boundary is exactly the oriented chain $C_1-C_0$: the internal source edges cancel in pairs. Degenerate image triangles contribute zero. We shall resolve this chain, not assume that this map is embedded. [F2, F3, F4, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Take a finite ambient subdivision carrying $c$ and its boundary collars. Away from smaller fixed collars, the coefficients of its oriented two-faces are integers. Around every ambient vertex choose a small disjoint convex link ball, around each remaining open edge a disjoint narrow edge tube, and in each remaining face a thin face slab. This is a finite construction in the already given simplex charts; compatible truncations are supplied by [F4]. For an oriented face with coefficient $n$, put $|n|$ parallel copies of its truncated triangular disk in its face slab, oriented by the sign of $n$. Their short edge strips end on the boundaries of the adjacent edge tubes. At an interior edge take its transverse polygonal disk. The incident face copies give finitely many points on its boundary, signed by their induced oriented boundary at that edge. Their signed sum is zero because $\partial c$ has no interior edge term. Pair them by disjoint polygonal arcs joining opposite signs as follows: a nonempty balanced cyclic sign list has two adjacent opposite signs; join that pair in a narrow boundary collar, remove the pair, and repeat inside the remaining disk. The successive arcs are disjoint, and finitely many repetitions exhaust the list. Product these arcs with the trimmed edge interval to obtain disjoint bands. Opposite signs are exactly what makes their surface orientations match across each band. Where $c$ is already the single embedded fixed boundary collar, leave that collar unchanged; its incident two face sheets pair in its original product chart. Thus all changes are away from $\partial G$ and the retained smaller collars. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The remaining boundary in each small interior vertex ball is a finite disjoint family of polygonal circles in its link two-sphere. These can be capped by disjoint parametrized PL disks inside that ball. To check this without an unproved disk-system theorem, choose an innermost circle in a planar chart of the link sphere, use [F4]'s polygonal Jordan disk, and push its disk a short distance inward in the boundary collar. Its contact disk and collar form the standard boundary window. The complement of this small boundary ball is again a parametrized PL ball by [F4]'s boundary-window absorption. Retain that complementary ball and repeat on the remaining circles, which are still on its boundary. This finite induction supplies mutually disjoint cap disks, each with its prescribed polygonal boundary. Choose the cap orientation to agree with the already oriented face-band surface; a disk boundary has either of the two induced orientations. Vertex neighborhoods meeting the fixed collars keep their already embedded half-disk patches, and all other resolutions are made outside a still smaller boundary collar, so there is no new boundary component at $\partial G$. Pasting the face copies, edge bands, vertex disks and the retained product collars gives a compact finite orientable properly embedded PL surface $F\subset G$ with EXACT boundary $C_0\sqcup C_1$. The vertex links of this constructed surface are circles, or intervals along its boundary, by the disk and band models; hence embeddedness and the surface claim have been verified locally, rather than inferred from its two-chain alone. Closed components may be kept. No comparison of its genus with that of the original annulus map is claimed. [F3, F4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 We apply finite compressions in the noncompact ambient $G$, localizing each required witness. If a component $S$ of the current compact finite surface is not injective in $\pi_1(G)$, take one nontrivial kernel loop and its nullhomotopy in $G$, placing its boundary loop in $\operatorname{Int}S$. The union of the surface, its finite bicollar and this compact disk image has a finite compact PL neighborhood $V$ by step 1.1; its boundary trace contains the original surface boundary collars, so the surface is proper in $V$. The same kernel loop is nullhomotopic in $V$. The ACTUAL proof in [F5], applied in $V$, then supplies a compression disk for some component of this surface: its disk-map cutting and innermost-face Loop argument needs precisely this nontrivial kernel witness, not an assumption that $V$ itself has cyclic group. The obtained embedded disk has interior disjoint from the whole surface. Compress in its product neighborhood, preserving all boundary collars. If further components fail injection into $G$, localize their individual witnesses in a new finite neighborhood. No common compact neighborhood with cyclic fundamental group is asserted. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 This procedure terminates after finitely many steps, independently of how its compact neighborhoods enlarge. Use exactly [F5]'s weight: $2g+b-1$ for a component with $b>0$, $2g-1$ for a positive-genus closed component, and zero for a sphere. It is a nonnegative integer sum on our initial finite surface. Every essential nonseparating compression lowers it by two, except a torus-to-sphere compression lowers it by one. Every essential separating compression lowers it by at least one; a sphere cut off by a separating curve would say that curve already bounded a disk and was not essential. Hence the initial weight bounds the number of successive choices. Formally, induction on that nonnegative integer proves the existence of a terminal finite compression sequence: if the current surface is already injective stop, otherwise step 5.1 supplies one disk and the smaller-weight induction applies. This is finite induction and does not select an infinite sequence of witnesses. The final compact orientable proper PL surface $F'$ retains exactly $C_0,C_1$ and their smaller original collars, with every component injective in $\pi_1(G)\cong\mathbb Z$. [F5, step 5.1, induction, algebra]

7.1 A connected compact orientable finite PL surface with nonempty boundary and group injecting into $\mathbb Z$ is a parametrized PL disk or annulus. For an explicit finite verification, root its triangle dual graph at an exterior vertex across all boundary edges, choose a finite spanning tree, and remove triangles in parent-before-child order, collapsing each across its now free parent edge. The remaining complex is a finite connected graph. Its regular disk-and-strip neighborhood has the same PL surface type: reversing each triangle collapse adds a boundary flap along consecutive sides, absorbed by the polygonal disk boundary extension in [F4]. Collapse a graph spanning tree to read its free generators. Two distinct generators have a nonempty reduced commutator by [F6], so injection into $\mathbb Z$ forces graph rank at most one. A tree thickening is a disk, deleting leaf disks and strips by [F4]. A rank-one graph has a single circuit with finite trees attached; remove these leaf pieces to obtain an orientable circle band, which is an annulus. Cutting one transverse strip gives its actual polygonal disk parameter and hence its PL annulus parameter by [F4]. This proves the classification needed here without a general surface classification or an ambient category change. [F4, F6, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Neither component containing a $C_i$ can be a disk, since that would nullhomotope its stipulated nonzero class in $\pi_1(G)$. Thus these boundary components lie on annuli by step 7.1. There are exactly two boundary circles in total, so they lie on ONE annulus component of $F'$. Retain it and discard all closed components. This gives the asserted properly embedded finite PL annulus, with its original smaller product collars. Step 1.2 already proved the disk assertion, and all noncompact uses were reduced to individually finite compact neighborhoods. The argument supplies the required cyclic-shell existence assertion without assuming that an arbitrary continuous annulus can be ambiently isotoped to this one, and without a TOP-to-PL triangulation theorem. [given, step 1.2, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎
