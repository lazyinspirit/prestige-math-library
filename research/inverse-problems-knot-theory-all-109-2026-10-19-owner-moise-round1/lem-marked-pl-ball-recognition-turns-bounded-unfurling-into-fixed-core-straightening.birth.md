## Statement

Let $0\le k\le2$, $n=3-k$, $X=B^k\times\mathbb R^n$, $B^j=[-1,1]^j$, and $C=B^k\times2B^n$. Supply a locally finite PL structure $\Sigma$ on $X$, standard near $\partial X$, and a PL homeomorphism $f:(X,\Sigma)\to X_{\rm std}$, fixed on $\partial X$, whose second-coordinate displacement is bounded.

Supply a parametrized finite $\Sigma$-PL ball $D\subset X$ containing $B^k\times B^n$ in its relative interior, contained in $B^k\times r_0B^n$ for some $r_0<2$, with these marked boundary data. For $k=0$, $D\subset\operatorname{Int}C$. For $k=1,2$, $D\cap\partial X=L=\partial B^k\times\tfrac32 B^n$, its remaining frontier $T$ is respectively one proper PL annulus or two proper PL disks, and the two faces have standard PL quadrant-window charts along their common rim. For $k=1$ assume also that $X\setminus\operatorname{Int}_X D$ is connected with fundamental group $\mathbb Z$, generated by a core circle of the outer cylinder $B^1\times\partial(2B^2)$.

There is an ambient TOP isotopy $P_t:X\to X$, from the identity, fixed on $\partial X$ and outside $\operatorname{Int}C$, such that $P_1:X_{\rm std}\to(X,\Sigma)$ is PL on a neighborhood of the FIXED set $B^k\times B^n$. Thus for any topological embedding $h:X\to\mathbb R^3$ with $\Sigma=h^{-1}(\mathrm{standard})$, the embeddings $hP_t$ straighten $h$ on that fixed core with exactly this boundary and support control.

No isotopy between arbitrary TOP and PL structures is assumed: the bounded PL model, protected marked PL ball and cyclic-complement condition are the explicit inputs.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stated structures, bounded PL model and protected marked ball.

[F1] The controlled PL unfurling exists, with its uniform end control and boundary-fixed joint Alexander isotopy. [[lem-bounded-product-homeomorphisms-admit-controlled-pl-unfurlings]]

[F2] A finite PL sphere in standard $\mathbb R^3$ bounds a parametrized PL ball. Parametrized PL balls admit marked boundary-disk extensions, standard-window ball removal and finite collars. [[thm-finite-pl-schoenflies-in-dimension-three]] [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]] [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]]

[F3] In a compact orientable irreducible finite PL manifold with one torus boundary, a prescribed PL boundary map to a standard solid torus extends when its meridian inverse is nullhomotopic. [[thm-relative-pl-solid-torus-rigidity-for-irreducible-three-manifolds]]

[F4] The finite ball-shrinking and cone-center point-push constructions of the listed source move an interior parametrized PL ball into any supplied interior open set while fixing the ambient boundary. These constructions do not use that source's abelian-group hypotheses. Convex cell-face maps have finite compatible PL realizations. [[lem-prime-pl-core-can-retain-a-supplied-interior-ball]] [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]

## Proof

1.1 Arrange [F1]'s PL compactification $\gamma:X\to C\setminus E$, where $E=\{0\}\times\partial(2B^n)$, to be the identity on a neighborhood of $D$. Choose $r_0<s<2$. A finite standard PL radial shell map $d:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ takes $sB^n$ to $B^n$ and fixes the complement of $\operatorname{Int}(2B^n)$: map the inner cubes affinely, and the face frusta between $sB^n$ and $2B^n$ to their matching frusta between $B^n$ and $2B^n$, fixed on the outer boundary, by compatible face-coning. Use $\operatorname{id}_{B^k}\times d$ and replace $\gamma$ by $d^{-1}\gamma d$, with the product identity understood. This remains locally finite PL, fixes $B^k\times sB^n$, has the same end control since $d$ fixes the outer sphere, and has the same range. By [F1] the conjugate $\Theta=\gamma f\gamma^{-1}$ extends to a boundary-fixed homeomorphism of $C$, isotopic to the identity there. On a neighborhood of $D$, its rightmost factor is the identity, its middle factor is PL from $\Sigma$ to standard, and its left factor is standard PL. Thus $\Theta$ is PL from $\Sigma$ to standard near $D$, and $D'=\Theta(D)$ is an ACTUAL finite standard PL ball. Compactness of $f(D)$ meets only finitely many cells of $\gamma$. The ball $D'$ misses the outer coordinate faces, since $\Theta$ fixes $\partial C$, $D$ misses those faces, and a homeomorphism preserves the boundary. For $k>0$ its lateral trace is exactly the unchanged $L$, with the stated marked windows. [given, F1, F4, construct]

2.1 We will construct a finite standard PL homeomorphism $\lambda:C\to C$, fixed on its boundary, for which $B^k\times B^n\subset\operatorname{Int}_C\lambda(D')$. For $k=0$, take the standard inner cube $J=\tfrac54B^3$. By [F4] a boundary-fixed PL self-homeomorphism $v$ of $C$ moves $J$ into $\operatorname{Int}D'$. The actual construction shrinks $J$ using its short outer collar, moves its center by finitely many cone-center point pushes in interior convex charts, and chooses the shrink small enough to fit in that open set. Put $\lambda=v^{-1}$. Then $J\subset\operatorname{Int}\lambda(D')$, giving the required fixed-core inclusion. This does not assert a false unrestricted radial interpolation formula is PL; it uses the finite frustum and fan maps of [F4]. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 For $k=1$, put $W=\overline{C\setminus D'}$, where the set difference removes the whole closed $D'$ before taking its closure. It is a finite PL manifold with boundary, by the given window charts and [F2]'s local complementary-link construction. Its boundary is a torus: the outer annulus $A=\overline{\partial C\setminus L}$ and the frontier annulus $T'=\Theta(T)$ meet along their two end circles. The noncompact complement $X\setminus\operatorname{Int}_X D'$ is homeomorphic under $\Theta$ to the supplied connected cyclic complement. Radial clamping of $|y|_\infty>2$ to2 is a deformation retraction of that complement onto $W$; it misses $D'$ because $D'$ has radius strictly less than2 away from its lateral trace. Thus $W$ is connected and $\pi_1(W)=\mathbb Z$, with generator the outer cylinder core, which $\Theta$ fixes. It is PL irreducible. A PL interior sphere bounds a standard PL ball inside $\operatorname{Int}C$ by [F2], since the connected unbounded exterior of $C$ is on its outside. The connected $D'$ misses that sphere and touches $\partial C$, so stays outside that ball. Hence the ball lies in $\operatorname{Int}W$. [given, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Put $D_* =B^1\times\tfrac32 B^2$ and $W_* =\overline{C\setminus D_*}$. This is the supplied standard PL solid torus, an interval times a square annulus, with explicit finite face-prism parameters. Construct a PL boundary map $b:\partial W\to\partial W_*$ equal to the identity on $A$. Parametrize $T'$ by an annulus whose two end-circle maps are the actual coordinate maps; the prescribed-circle annulus extension in [F2] supplies this finite parameter. The circle obtained by one crossing arc of $T'$, radial strips at the two ends, and the reverse outer crossing arc on the outer cylinder maps to some integer $m$ in $\pi_1(W)=\mathbb Z$. Twist the annulus parameter by $m$ full turns, with sign chosen to subtract that integer, keeping both end circles pointwise fixed. The comparison loop now maps to zero. The twist is finite PL: on the angular cover it is affine, and only finitely many wrap lines occur. Complete $b$ by this twisted annulus map. Its inverse of the standard meridian of $W_*$ is exactly that comparison loop and is nullhomotopic in $W$. Apply [F3], with irreducibility already proved in step 3.1, to obtain a PL homeomorphism $W\to W_*$ extending $b$. Its map on $T'$, together with the identity on $L$, is a PL sphere map $\partial D'\to\partial D_*$. Cone it in their supplied PL ball parameters to extend over $D'$. Pasting the two maps gives $\lambda:C\to C$, fixed on $\partial C$, with $\lambda(D')=D_*$. Its marked solid-torus application is not a general handle-addition assertion. [F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For $k=2$, the two disjoint frontier disks $T'$ cut the standard PL ball $C=B^2\times[-2,2]$ into three parametrized PL balls, with middle one $D'$. Here is the precise disk-cut verification. For either frontier disk, its boundary circle splits $\partial C$ into two parametrized PL disks by [F2]. Join the frontier disk to the boundary disk on its outer-end side. This is a finite PL sphere; [F2] gives its bounded PL ball. The connected exterior of $C$ is on the unbounded side, so this ball stays in $C$. At the chosen outer-end boundary disk, inward collar points lie on the bounded side and points outside $C$ on the unbounded side; thus its trace on $\partial C$ is exactly that boundary disk. The given rim windows are the standard boundary-window charts. Its complement in $C$ is a PL ball by [F2]'s window-removal assertion. The other disjoint frontier disk lies in the appropriate remaining ball; apply the same argument there. This proves the three-ball decomposition without invoking irreducibility for a general handle attachment. Assign the two disk-face maps arbitrarily with their prescribed rim-circle values, using [F2]. Together with the identity on the outer boundary these give sphere maps from the two end balls to $B^2\times[3/2,2]$ and $B^2\times[-2,-3/2]$, and from $D'$ to $D_* =B^2\times[-3/2,3/2]$. Extend over all three balls by coning their finite parameters. The maps agree on their shared disks and paste to a PL homeomorphism $\lambda:C\to C$, fixed on $\partial C$, taking $D'$ to $D_*$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct]

6.1 Extend $\lambda$ by the identity off $C$ and give it the boundary-fixed Alexander isotopy $\lambda_t$ displayed in [F1]. Let $\Theta_t$ be [F1]'s corresponding isotopy to $\Theta$. Then $R_t=\lambda_t\Theta_t$ is an ambient isotopy, fixed on $\partial X$ and outside $\operatorname{Int}C$, with jointly continuous inverse. The terminal $R=\lambda\Theta$ is PL from $\Sigma$ to standard on a neighborhood of $D$, by step 1.1 and the standard PL nature of $\lambda$. In every case steps 2.1,4.1,5.1 put the FIXED core $B^k\times B^n$ in the relative interior of $R(D)$. Consequently $P_t=R_t^{-1}$ is the desired isotopy, and $P_1$ is PL from standard to $\Sigma$ on a neighborhood of that fixed core. Finally $hP_t$ has the stated support and boundary equalities, and $hP_1$ is PL there because $h:(X,\Sigma)\to\mathbb R^3_{\rm std}$ is PL by the supplied pullback definition. This explicitly controls the region under inversion; it does not identify the original core with an arbitrary conjugate image. [given, F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct] ∎
