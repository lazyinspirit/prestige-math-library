## Statement

Let $M$ be a compact connected orientable finite PL three-manifold, with boundary allowed, whose fundamental group is nontrivial and abelian. Suppose that every finite PL embedded two-sphere in $\operatorname{Int}M$ separates $M$, and that the closure of at least one of its two complementary components is a parametrized topological three-ball contained in $\operatorname{Int}M$. This is a supplied topological ball-side hypothesis, not a PL ball assertion.

Suppose also that a finite family $\mathcal S$ of disjoint finite PL embedded two-spheres in $\operatorname{Int}M$ is supplied, together with its PL cut pieces and standard PL cap balls, such that every capped cut piece is PL prime. Here prime means that an interior PL connected-sum splitting has at least one capped side PL homeomorphic to the standard PL three-sphere. The empty family is allowed. Let $D\subset\operatorname{Int}M$ be a supplied parametrized finite PL three-ball.

There is a compact finite PL submanifold $Q\subset\operatorname{Int}M$ disjoint from $D$, whose underlying space is a parametrized TOP three-ball, such that $A=\overline{M\setminus Q}$ has boundary $\partial M\sqcup\partial Q$ and contains $D$ in its interior. Capping $\partial Q$ by a standard PL three-ball gives a PL irreducible manifold $P$, retaining the original PL structure on $A$. Its original boundary is exactly $\partial M$. A homeomorphism from $M$ to this capped manifold is the identity on $A$, including on $D$ and $\partial M$.

All PL maps used on compact polyhedra have finite compatible subdivisions. The conclusion is conditional on the supplied finite prime presentation. It asserts neither the existence of that presentation in a TOP manifold nor a PL parameter of $Q$. The protected ball is an INTERIOR PL ball; this statement does not protect a boundary-attached topological handle or construct a PL ball around one.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite PL manifold, its prime splitting, the nontrivial abelian fundamental group, the topological ball-side property and the supplied interior PL ball $D$.

[F1] A finite PL sphere in the standard PL three-sphere bounds two parametrized finite PL balls. [[thm-finite-pl-schoenflies-in-dimension-three]]

[F2] Marked finite PL boundary disks extend over parametrized PL balls, and gluing parametrized PL balls along such disks gives a parametrized PL ball. Its proof also gives the complementary boundary-disk and the two-boundary annulus parameters used below. [[lem-parametrized-pl-balls-absorb-standard-boundary-windows]]

[F3] Compact locally collared PL subpolyhedra have PL collars; compatible subpolyhedra and convex cell complexes admit finite subdivisions. [[lem-compact-locally-collared-pl-subpolyhedron-has-pl-collar]] [[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]] [[lem-finite-convex-cell-complexes-admit-compatible-triangulations]]

[F4] A connected simply connected overlap gives the inclusion-induced free-product fundamental group. Reduced syllable words give injectivity of the factors and make $aba^{-1}b^{-1}$ nontrivial for nonidentity elements in distinct factors. [[cor-seifert-van-kampen-simply-connected-overlap]] [[thm-normal-form-for-free-products]] The standard two-sphere is simply connected. [[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]]

## Proof

1.1 We first give the finite PL ball-moving construction used twice below. If $E$ is a parametrized PL ball in the interior of a connected finite PL three-manifold $X$, its boundary is locally bicollared. Indeed take a common subdivision of its parameter and an ambient chart. Its boundary link is a polygonal circle in a standard sphere link; the two complementary link disks are the finite polygonal disks constructed in [F2]. Coning their parameters gives the two local half-space sides. The chosen ball labels its inside side, so its outside closure is a PL manifold near that boundary. Apply [F3] on the outside to obtain an exterior collar. The union $N$ of $E$ and a sufficiently short closed exterior collar is a parametrized PL ball: transfer the boundary of the supplied convex model of $E$ to the collar, subdivide its face prisms, and map them to the shell between that convex model and its double. On each shell face prescribe the two end maps and previously assigned side maps, then cone the boundary triangulation from an interior point. These finite maps have finite PL inverses and agree on common faces. Thus $N$ has coordinates in which $E$ is a convex ball $B$ and $N=2B$, with both dilations centered at an interior point $x$. For $0<\epsilon<1$, use the affine homothety on $B$ taking it to $\epsilon B$, and send each face frustum between $B$ and $2B$ to its corresponding frustum between $\epsilon B$ and $2B$, fixed on the outer boundary, by the same finite face-coning construction. This defines a PL self-homeomorphism of $N$, fixed on $\partial N$, and extends by the identity to $X$. It shrinks $E$ into an arbitrarily small neighborhood of $x$. This is a finite cell map, not a claim that radial interpolation itself is PL. [F2, F3, given, construct]

1.2 Each sphere of $\mathcal S$ separates $M$. Its cut graph, whose vertices are cut components and whose edges are those spheres, is therefore a finite tree: it is connected, and deleting any edge disconnects it, since a path avoiding that sphere cannot join its two sides. In particular there are $|\mathcal S|+1$ components. Sphere collars provide open van Kampen covers with intersection $S^2\times(-1,1)$, which is simply connected. Here the simply connected sphere in [F4] transfers through its supplied PL parameter, and projection contracts the interval factor. Attaching a standard cap ball similarly changes no fundamental group. Apply [F4] successively from the leaves of this finite tree to get the inclusion-induced isomorphism $\pi_1(M)\cong\ast_v\pi_1(P_v)$ for the capped pieces $P_v$. There is exactly one vertex $v_0$ with nontrivial group. At least one exists because the free product of trivial groups is trivial. Two nontrivial factors would give a nontrivial reduced commutator by [F4], contrary to abelianness. Put $P_0=P_{v_0}$ and let $A_0$ be its uncapped cut piece. [F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For any two interior points of connected $X$ there is a finite polygonal path between them: the union reachable through finitely many connected linear charts is open and closed, and finite subdivisions make a compact chart path polygonal. Cover it by finitely many relatively compact convex PL chart balls and divide it into short steps inside them. In such a ball triangulate its boundary, cone it from the current interior point and cone the same boundary triangulation from the next point. The affine maps of the two cone fans give an orientation-preserving PL homeomorphism fixed on the ball boundary, carrying the first point to the second. Compose these finitely many point pushes, extending by the identity outside their charts. Given an interior open set $U$, first choose a point push $p$ taking $x$ to a point of $U$, then choose $\epsilon$ in step 1.1 small enough that $p(\epsilon E)\subset U$. This gives a PL self-homeomorphism taking $E$ into $U$, supported in the interior of $X$. The same construction can fix any prescribed finite disjoint balls: take its collars and paths in their complement. That complement is connected, because a path meeting a ball can have its portion between its first and last encounters with a short outer collar replaced by a polygonal path in that collar's connected sphere cross interval. Finitely many disjoint collars make these replacements compatible. Consequently finitely many disjoint supplied cap balls can be moved, one after another while fixing the others, off any given interior PL surface: put each small image in an open chart ball off that surface and the other balls. Equivalently an inverse image of the surface is disjoint from all the original cap balls. These constructions require no infinite family of choices or isotopy theorem. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Apply steps 1.1 and 2.1 in $M$ to move $D$ wholly into a small interior chart ball of $A_0$. Pull the entire splitting and its cap identifications back by this PL self-homeomorphism, which fixes $\partial M$. The capped pieces are unchanged up to PL homeomorphism and the distinguished cut piece now contains the ORIGINAL $D$. Rename the pulled-back data $\mathcal S,P_0,A_0$. For any edge of its cut tree, the topological ball side from the hypothesis cannot contain $A_0$: a nontrivial loop in its group would then lie in a simply connected ball and become trivial in $\pi_1(M)$, contradicting the factor injection in step 1.2. Hence that ball side is precisely the branch away from $v_0$. In particular take the $r$ edges incident to $v_0$. Their corresponding closed branches $B_1,\ldots,B_r$ are parametrized TOP balls, pairwise disjoint, with PL sphere boundaries and with interiors disjoint from $D$. They contain all the other cut pieces and no point of $\partial M$, since each is contained in $\operatorname{Int}M$. Thus $A_0=\overline{M\setminus\bigcup_i B_i}$, and $P_0$ is obtained by attaching the supplied standard PL cap balls $C_i$ to its boundary spheres $\partial B_i$. All original boundary components lie in $A_0$. No assertion that a $B_i$ has a PL ball parameter has been made. [F4, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 We prove $P_0$ irreducible before combining those branch balls. For any PL sphere $T\subset\operatorname{Int}P_0$, step 2.1 moves the finitely many cap balls $C_i$ off $T$, and therefore carries $T$ by the inverse homeomorphism to a sphere $T'$ disjoint from the original caps. Suppose $P_0\setminus T'$ were connected. Deleting the cap interiors from that connected complement preserves connectedness by the collar rerouting in step 2.1, because their boundary spheres miss $T'$. Thus $A_0\setminus T'$ is connected. Replacing those cap balls by the connected $B_i$ preserves connectedness too, giving connected $M\setminus T'$. This contradicts the separating-sphere hypothesis. Every sphere in $P_0$ consequently separates. Since $P_0$ is PL prime, one capped side of any such sphere is the standard PL $S^3$. In that supplied standard sphere, its cap ball is a finite PL embedded ball, and its boundary sphere bounds two PL balls by [F1]. The closure of its complementary side is therefore a parametrized PL ball; this is precisely the original uncapped side of $T$. Hence $T$ bounds a PL ball in $P_0$. This proves irreducibility without using a TOP-to-PL identification of any $B_i$ or a simply connected three-manifold classification. [F1, F2, F3, given, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

4.2 Assume first that $r\ge1$. The interior of $A_0$ minus $D$ is connected by the same ball-collar rerouting. Choose disjoint finite polygonal arcs in $A_0\setminus D$ joining its $r$ cap-sphere boundary components in a tree, with distinct endpoints and product endpoint collars. Here are finite general-position details: first take finitely many chart paths connecting the required boundary components and entering them in their collars. Subdivide until each segment lies in a linear three-chart. Perturb the finitely many internal vertices so disjoint nonadjacent segments miss each other and the fixed earlier arcs. In each chart a segment intersection imposes a proper affine plane condition on one varying vertex, and there are finitely many pairs; choose a point in the open allowed ball outside these finitely many planes. Remove path loops and give different arcs distinct endpoint disks. All paths stay away from $D$ and unwanted boundary components by choosing the perturbations within their compact positive margins. A sufficiently thin PL neighborhood of each arc is an actually parametrized $D^2\times[0,1]$ tube. Construct it with disjoint small convex vertex balls and disjoint narrow prisms around the intervening straight segments; a segment prism meets its endpoint ball in a standard boundary window. The ball and window maps of [F2] successively give the tube ball with its two end disks marked. Their complementary boundary is an annulus, parametrized by the boundary-disk/annulus construction in [F2], so its two end markings extend to the stated tube parameter. Choose all tube widths after the finite disjoint arcs have been fixed. The resulting tubes $H_1,\ldots,H_{r-1}$ have disjoint interiors, miss $D$, and meet $B_i$ or $C_i$ exactly at the corresponding specified boundary disks. Their attaching windows are standard because the sphere collars and arc endpoint collars are product charts. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Put $Q=\bigcup_i B_i\cup\bigcup_j H_j$ in $M$. This is a finite PL submanifold with boundary: all its pieces are finite PL cut regions or tubes, and the just described window charts give the half-space boundary links at their seams. Its underlying space is a parametrized TOP ball. To verify the marked gluing, adjust each given TOP parameter of $B_i$ to have the supplied PL parameter on its boundary sphere. An arbitrary boundary homeomorphism of the standard sphere extends over the standard ball by the explicit cone formula $(s,t)\mapsto(f(s),t)$, with the center collapsed; continuity and the formula for its inverse are immediate. Thus this adjustment does not require that the original TOP parameter be PL. With the attaching disks now given their finite PL markings, joining two ball parameters along a specified marked disk gives a TOP ball parameter exactly as in [F2]: extend the disk map across the complementary boundary disks, cone the resulting boundary map, and identify the two balls with the two halves of a cube. Apply this first to the tube and one endpoint ball and then its other endpoint ball. Delete leaves of the finite joining tree and repeat. This constructs an actual TOP ball parameter of $Q$. The same construction inside $P_0$, with $C_i$ in place of $B_i$ and the SAME tubes, uses exclusively PL parameters and [F2]; it gives a parametrized PL ball $C=\bigcup_i C_i\cup\bigcup_j H_j$. Both constructions preserve the identical outer boundary marking. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.2, construct]

6.1 The common complement $A=\overline{M\setminus Q}=\overline{P_0\setminus C}$ has exactly the boundary $\partial M\sqcup\partial Q$. Its original PL structure is identical in both constructions, and it contains $D$ in its interior because every branch and tube missed $D$. Replace $C$ by a standard PL cap using its supplied PL ball parameter. Extending its boundary identification by finite coning gives a PL homeomorphism from the capped $A$ to $P_0$ fixed on $A$. The capped manifold is consequently PL irreducible by step 4.1. Likewise the TOP parameter of $Q$ extends the same boundary map by the cone formula in step 5.1, so replacing $Q$ by that cap gives a homeomorphism from $M$ to the capped $A$, fixed on $A$. In particular it fixes $D$ and all original boundary points. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 If $r=0$, then $M=P_0$ and step 4.1 already makes it irreducible. Choose a small convex PL chart ball $Q$ in $\operatorname{Int}M\setminus D$. Such an open set is nonempty: if $D$ were all of $M$, its nontrivial fundamental group would contradict its supplied ball parameter, and a point outside $D$ has a nearby interior point outside it, using a boundary half-chart if needed. Replacing this parametrized PL ball by a standard PL cap is PL homeomorphic to $M$, fixed on the complement, by [F2]'s boundary coning. This supplies all the same assertions in the empty splitting case. The preceding proof used only finitely many charts, subdivisions, cap balls, arcs, tubes, group elements and ball parameters. The supplied TOP ball-side property was used only to identify the branch balls, and no choice hypothesis or general TOP triangulation theorem was added. The interior-ball limitation in the Statement is therefore essential to the exact interface proved here. [F2, given, step 4.1, construct, cases] ∎
