## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $M$ be a given compact smooth oriented three-manifold, possibly disconnected and with boundary allowed. Let $b_i:S^1\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Int}M$, $1\le i\le r$, be a finite family of pairwise disjoint locally flat oriented circles, with chosen rank-two TOP normal-microbundle trivializations. A representative of each trivialization is a homeomorphism germ of a neighborhood of $S^1\times\{0\}$ in $S^1\times\mathbb R^2$ into $M$, equal to $b_i$ on the zero section; the class is its homotopy class through such normal trivializations, rather than just the class of one normal section. Let $U\subset\operatorname{Int}M$ be any open neighborhood of their union.

There is an orientation-preserving ambient TOP isotopy $H_t$ of $M$, with jointly continuous inverse, starting at the identity and fixed outside $U$, such that every $H_1(b_i(S^1))$ is smooth. There are orientation-preserving circle homeomorphisms $\theta_i$ for which $H_1b_i\theta_i$ are smooth regular parametrizations. Each transported normal-microbundle framing class has a smooth normal-framing representative. Relative to any chosen oriented smooth reference frame, its orientation sign and one integer winding determine the full transported class; the proof supplies actual trivialization homotopies to that representative. No preservation of an old transverse TOP ribbon or four-dimensional collar germ is asserted.

## Proof

1.1 The following construction supplies the separation facts needed by the local handle argument without invoking general Alexander duality, Poincare duality, UCT, an ANR theorem, or an arbitrary choice of neighborhoods. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 **Finite dual-cell calculation.** Let T be a finite oriented triangulation of the standard three-sphere and L a subcomplex. Subdivide once barycentrically. The dual block D(sigma) of a simplex sigma has dimension 3-dim(sigma); orient it by requiring its transverse intersection with sigma to have sign +1. The union of the closed dual blocks with sigma not in L is a finite cell complex: every coface of a simplex not in L is again not in L. It is a deformation retract of S^3 minus |L|. Indeed a simplex of the barycentric subdivision is a chain of original faces. At a point outside |L| the sum of its barycentric coordinates on faces not in L is positive. Decrease the other coordinates to zero and renormalize the retained ones. The linear homotopy stays in the complement, agrees on faces, and fixes the displayed dual-block union. [given, construct, algebra]

1.3 The cellular boundary matrix of that union is, up to the displayed orientation signs, the transpose incidence matrix of T with the rows and columns of L removed. Thus its cellular chain complex is C^{3-*}(T,L;Z). This is a finite integer matrix identity: a codimension-one dual face corresponds exactly to an original incidence sigma < tau. Opposite orientations at the same intersection give the cochain/cellular boundary signs. Subdivide the finitely many dual blocks into simplices; coning successively over their boundary faces gives the ordinary singular comparison and the same incidence maps. Consequently H_0(S^3 minus |L|;Z) is H^3(T,L;Z). The map to H^3(T;Z)=Z is the coefficient-sum augmentation: a positively oriented dual vertex has intersection +1 with the fundamental cycle. The pair sequence, together with H^2(S^3;Z)=0, therefore identifies `reduced H_0(S^3 minus |L|;Z) = H^2(|L|;Z).` [given, construct, algebra]

1.4 These identifications commute with shrinking L and finite subdivisions. This can be checked in a common subdivision: the oriented dual of a subdivided simplex is the sum of its oriented subblocks; interior faces cancel in pairs, and intersection with the same subdivided fundamental cycle still gives the same augmentation. All chain groups and all comparisons here are finite. [given, construct, algebra]

1.5 **Cofinal finite neighborhoods.** Put a supplied compact set K in a fixed Euclidean chart of S^3. On the standard dyadic cubical grids take the finite union of closed cubes meeting K and one full adjacent layer. Every such union contains K in its interior. Their mesh tends to zero, so they are cofinal among neighborhoods of K. Take a nested subsequence by always choosing the least finer grid whose full closed union is contained in the previous interior. Such an index exists by compactness and the positive margin of K inside that interior; the least-integer rule requires no dependent choice. Triangulate the cubes by the fixed coordinate ordering, complete the bounded box to a finite S^3 triangulation by coning its outer boundary to infinity, and apply the preceding finite dual-cell calculation. [given, construct, algebra]

1.6 The increasing complements have union S^3 minus K. Every zero-cycle and every finite collection of paths witnessing its relations have compact image disjoint from K, and hence belong to some one complement. It follows directly on those finite cycles and paths that the direct limit of their H_0 groups is H_0(S^3 minus K). No simultaneous choice of representatives is needed. [given, construct, algebra]

1.8 is defined on the entire Euclidean chart and approximates f on K with error at most 2a/3. If A is empty use the constant a/3, and if B is empty use -a/3; if both are empty use zero.
