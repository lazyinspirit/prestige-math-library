### 2.1 `def-invertible-element-and-general-linear-group-of-a-banach-algebra` — false claim (remark)

* Defect. The remark asserted: “**Two-sided inverses are required, and in a Banach algebra
  one-sided inverses already suffice.** … In a unital complex Banach algebra, however,
  $ab = 1$ alone forces $ba = 1$”. That is false: on $\ell^2(\mathbb N_0)$ the right shift
  $Te_n = e_{n-1}$ ($n \ge 1$), $Te_0 = 0$ and the left shift $Se_n = e_{n+1}$ satisfy
  $TS = 1$ and $ST = 1 - P \ne 1$ with $P$ the projection onto $\mathbb C e_0$; $T$ has a right
  inverse and is not invertible (it is not injective).
* Evidence. Direct computation $TS e_n = T e_{n+1} = e_n$ and $STe_0 = S0 = 0 \ne e_0$; the
  witness is the shift of `ex-spectrum-of-the-unilateral-shift`, opened this pass. The
  accompanying “standard boundary argument … rules this out” reasoning is invalid, since
  $1 - ba$ is a nonzero idempotent in the shift algebra.
* Repair. The remark now states the true fact, exhibits the shift witness, retains the
  statement that both inverses are always verified explicitly below, and
  `forward_refs` gained `ex-spectrum-of-the-unilateral-shift`.

### 2.2 `lem-resolvent-identity` — false supporting sentence in step 1.1

* Defect. Step 1.1 claimed “all these elements commute with one another because each is a
  complex multiple of $1$ minus one of the commuting elements $a,b$ and inverses of commuting
  elements commute”. In a general Banach algebra $a$ and $b$ need not commute, so
  $z1-a$ and $z1-b$ need not commute, and neither need $R(z,a)$ and $R(z,b)$.
* Evidence. The equalities actually used in steps 2.1 and 2.2 are only
  $R(u)u = uR(u) = 1$ for $u \in \{z1-a, w1-a, z1-b\}$ together with
  $(z1-a)-(w1-a) = (z-w)1$ and $(z1-a)-(z1-b) = b-a$; multiplying each resolvent against its
  own argument gives $R(z,a)(z1-a)R(w,a) = R(w,a)$ and $R(z,a)(w1-a)R(w,a) = R(z,a)$, and
  likewise in the second identity. No commutativity of $a$ and $b$ or of the two resolvents is
  ever needed, so the stated conclusion (the two identities, plus commutation of
  $R(z,a)$ with $R(w,a)$) is correct.
* Repair. Step 1.1 now records exactly those two-sided inverse relations and says explicitly
  that no commutativity between $a$ and $b$ or between $R(z,a)$ and $R(z,b)$ is claimed or
  needed.

### 2.3 `lem-submultiplicative-root-limit` — false inequality in step 2.2

* Defect. Step 2.2 justified $u_k^q \le t^n t^{-k}$ (where $t = u_k^{1/k}$, $n = qk+r$, $r<k$)

  $t \ge 1$ one has $t^{-k} \le 1$, so the chain $1 \le t^{-k}$ is false; e.g. $k = 2$, $r = 1$,
  $t = 2$: $u_k^q = t^{n-1}$ is not $\le t^n t^{-2} = t^{n-2}$.
* Evidence. $t^{-r} \le t^{-k} \iff t^{k-r} \le 1 \iff t \le 1$ for $t>0$, so the asserted bound
  can only hold for $t \le 1$; the correct replacement constant is $B_k := \max(1, t^{-k})$
  (for $t \ge 1$ one uses $u_k^q = t^{kq} \le t^n$ since $kq \le n$).
* Repair. Step 2.2 now proves $u_k^q \le t^n B_k$ with $B_k := \max(1,t^{-k})$, splitting
  $t \le 1$ and $t \ge 1$ correctly, and step 3.1 defines

### 2.4 `thm-spectral-radius-formula` — wrong difference quotient in step 3.1

* Defect. Step 3.1 asserted that the resolvent identity gives the difference quotient
  $\frac{h(z)-h(z_0)}{z-z_0} = \frac{R(z^{-1})R(z_0^{-1})}{z z_0}$ with limit
  $R(z_0^{-1})^2/z_0^2$ for $h(z) = (1-za)^{-1} = z^{-1}R(z^{-1},a)$. The formula is missing the
  factor $a$: with $u = z^{-1}$, $v = z_0^{-1}$,
  $uR(u) - vR(v) = (u-v)R(u)[1-vR(v)] = (v-u)\,a\,R(u)R(v)$, because
  $1 - vR(v) = -aR(v)$; the as-written identity would make
  $z^{-1}R(z^{-1}) - z_0^{-1}R(z_0^{-1}) = R(z^{-1}) - R(z_0^{-1})$, which is false (scalar check
  with $a=\alpha$: the quotient is $\alpha R(u)R(v)/(zz_0)$).
* Evidence. Direct expansion: $h(z)-h(z_0) = uR(u)-vR(v)$ and
  $v-u = (z-z_0)/(zz_0)$; independently $h'(z) = a(1-az)^{-2} = aR(z^{-1})^2/z^2$ from the
  power series $h = \sum a^n z^n$, which fixes the missing factor.
* Repair. Step 3.1 now derives $h(z)-h(z_0) = (v-u)aR(u)R(v)$ from the resolvent identity plus
  the rearrangement $1-vR(v) = -aR(v)$, obtains the difference quotient
  $aR(z^{-1})R(z_0^{-1})/(zz_0)$ and the limit $aR(z_0^{-1})^2/z_0^2$. Only holomorphy (not the
  value of the derivative) is used afterwards in steps 4.1–6.1 and in step 5.1, so the
  theorem's conclusion is unchanged.

### 2.6 `ex-spectrum-of-the-unilateral-shift` — false density claim in step 3.1

* Repair. Step 3.1 is now “Density on the unit circle”: the recurrence is solved as
  $c_n = \overline\lambda^{\,n}c_0$, density is proved for $|\lambda| = 1$, and for
