## Statement

Assume AC. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete, torsion-free, nonelementary and geometrically finite in the convex-core-neighborhood-volume sense. Let $\Lambda$ be its limit set and $\Omega=\widehat{\mathbb C}\setminus\Lambda$. Assume its components are uniform quasidisks: there is $Q<\infty$ and, for every component $V$, a supplied orientation-preserving $Q$-quasiconformal sphere homeomorphism $q_V$ taking $D$ onto $V$.

Supply representatives $\Omega_j$ of all component orbits, their full stabilizers $\Gamma_j$, and their actual free holomorphic covering quotients $X_j=\Omega_j/\Gamma_j$. Let $f,g$ be orientation-preserving quasiconformal sphere homeomorphisms conjugating $\Gamma$ to Möbius groups, with induced representations $\rho_f,\rho_g$. Write $X^f_j=f(\Omega_j)/\rho_f(\Gamma_j)$ and $X^g_j=g(\Omega_j)/\rho_g(\Gamma_j)$, marked by the maps $\overline f_j,\overline g_j$ from $X_j$.

Suppose for every $j$ there is a biholomorphism $a_j:X^f_j\to X^g_j$ for which $a_j\overline f_j$ is homotopic to $\overline g_j$. When marked punctures are supplied, use homotopies of punctured surfaces extending jointly continuously to the compactifications with all puncture labels fixed. Then there is a Möbius transformation $M$ satisfying
$$g|_\Lambda=M f|_\Lambda,\qquad \rho_g(\gamma)=M\rho_f(\gamma)M^{-1}\quad(\gamma\in\Gamma).$$
Thus these actual marked conformal-boundary data determine the marked quasiconformal deformation up to Möbius conjugacy. If $f,g$ have the same values on a common triple of distinct points of $\Lambda$, then $M=\mathrm{id}$, their representations are equal and their boundary restrictions on $\Lambda$ are equal. Normalization at an arbitrary triple outside $\Lambda$ does not assert this stronger conclusion. Equality of $f$ and $g$ inside the discontinuity components is not claimed.

The proof also establishes $\Lambda(\Gamma_j)=\partial\Omega_j$ and nonelementarity of each stabilizer from the stated geometric-finiteness and uniform-quasidisk assumptions. No extra full-limit hypothesis, Ahlfors-finiteness theorem, pre-existing boundary-parameterization bijection or interior extension theorem is imported.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the geometrically finite group, uniform quasidisk maps, all actual quotient covers, both quasiconformal conjugacies, and the marked biholomorphisms and homotopies in the Statement.

A sphere homeomorphism with coefficient zero is Möbius, by measurable-mapping uniqueness relative to the identity. ## Proof

1.1 If $\Omega$ is empty, its limit set is the whole sphere and $C(\Lambda)=\mathbb H^3$. Geometric finiteness therefore gives finite nonzero hyperbolic volume of $\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$. We verify the Haar hypothesis of [F6] explicitly. The model group $G=\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ is the oriented orthonormal frame bundle, with compact fiber $K=\mathrm{SO}(3)$. Give $K$ its invariant probability volume from its bi-invariant matrix metric, and give the frame bundle the product of base Riemannian volume and this fiber volume in local trivializations. Changes of section are rotations, so these local measures agree. Model isometries preserve the base metric and fiber rotations, making this a left-invariant smooth positive measure on $G$. Right translation multiplies such a measure by a positive scalar: its pullback is still left invariant and its density is determined at identity. These scalars form a continuous homomorphism $\Delta:G\to\mathbb R_{>0}$. Conjugation leaves $\Delta$ unchanged. The diagonal matrices $a_t=\operatorname{diag}(e^{t/2},e^{-t/2})$ contract each upper or lower unipotent subgroup to identity in one time direction, so $\Delta(n)=1$ for every unipotent $n$. These subgroups generate $G$: the identity $n_+(\lambda)n_-(-\lambda^{-1})n_+(\lambda)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\lambda\\-\lambda^{-1}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ gives diagonal matrices by multiplying two such matrices, and Gaussian elimination gives every determinant-one matrix from diagonal and unipotent matrices. Thus $\Delta=1$, so our frame measure is right invariant as well. Its locally defined quotient measure on $\Gamma\backslash G$ has total mass equal to the finite positive volume of $\mathbb H^3/\Gamma$, since the fiber has mass one. Apply [F6]'s contraction-rigidity theorem separately to $f$ and $g$: each is Möbius. Then $M=gf^{-1}$ gives the entire stated conclusion, including the common-triple clause. This handles the empty boundary case without a zero-area assertion. For the remainder assume $\Omega\ne\varnothing$; [F6] then gives $\operatorname{area}\Lambda=0$. [A1, F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We prove a uniform interior-ball estimate for quasidisks. There is $c=c(Q)>0$ such that, whenever a $Q$-quasidisk $V$ has $p\in\partial V\cap\mathbb C$ and its boundary meets the Euclidean circle of radius $r>0$ about $p$, the set $V\cap B(p,r)$ contains a Euclidean ball of radius $cr$. Normalize by a translation, rotation and dilation so that $p=0$ and one boundary point is $1$. On either boundary arc from $0$ to $1$, continuity gives another boundary point $b$ with $|b|=1/2$. Then $|b-1|\ge1/2$. The triples $(0,b,1)$ lie in a compact set of distinct sphere triples. Their Möbius transformations $N_b$ to a fixed triple of circle points $(-1,i,1)$ therefore form a compact family, with compact inverse family; this follows directly from the fractional-linear triple formula. Precompose the supplied sphere map $q_V$ by a disk automorphism $A$ sending $(-1,i,1)$ to the corresponding preimages of $(0,b,1)$, respecting their cyclic order by choosing the boundary arc and the ordered triple accordingly. Such a disk automorphism is obtained from the same triple formula and preserves the disk on the orientation-preserving side of its circle. The map $F=N_bq_VA$ is $Q$-quasiconformal and fixes the fixed triple. By [F1], $F,F^{-1}$ are uniformly equicontinuous in the spherical metric. Choose one point $a=(1-t)(-1)\in D$, with $t>0$ uniformly small, so that $N_b^{-1}F(a)\in B(0,1/2)$ for every such pair: $F(a)$ is uniformly close to $-1=N_b(0)$ and the inverse Möbius family is uniformly continuous. The fixed positive distance from $a$ to the circle, together with uniform equicontinuity of $F^{-1}$, gives a uniform spherical ball about $F(a)$ contained in $F(D)$. Uniform continuity of the compact family $N_b$ then gives a fixed $0<c<1/4$ for which the Euclidean ball of radius $c$ about $N_b^{-1}F(a)$ lies in $N_b^{-1}F(D)\cap B(0,1)$. Undo the normalization. This proves the stated estimate without assuming a quasidisk roundness theorem. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.3 We prove the round-circle gluing step used for each component. If $h:D\to D$ is $K'$-quasiconformal with boundary values identity, its extension $R$ by identity to the exterior is a $K'$-quasiconformal sphere homeomorphism. Pasting the two closed discs makes it a homeomorphism. The area and distortion formula [F2] gives $\int_D|Dh|_{HS}^2\le2K'\pi$. For almost every horizontal chord its local ACL representative has integrable derivative on the entire chord, by Fubini and this energy estimate. Continuous endpoint values and integration from an interior point then show it is absolutely continuous on the closed chord. For $h-\mathrm{id}$ those endpoints are zero, so its zero extension is absolutely continuous along that full line. The same holds vertically. Integration by parts on these lines gives local weak derivatives in $L^2$, equal to the piecewise derivatives. The quasiconformal inequality holds almost everywhere, because the circle is null and the exterior derivative is identity. This is the analytic quasiconformal definition in [F2]. Thus no trace or removability theorem has been assumed in this gluing step. [F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Only uniformly finitely many component closures contain a fixed point $p$. Make a fixed Möbius coordinate with $p$ finite. Given any finite collection of distinct components with $p$ on their boundaries, choose $r>0$ sufficiently small so every one of these boundaries meets the circle of radius $r$ about $p$. This is possible because each boundary is a Jordan curve containing some point other than $p$. Step 1.2 gives a ball of radius $cr$ in each component inside $B(p,r)$. These balls are pairwise disjoint, so if there are $N$ of them, $N\pi c^2r^2\le\pi r^2$. Thus $N\le c^{-2}$, independent of the collection and of $p$. An infinite collection would have finite subcollections of arbitrarily large size, so the total collection is finite with that uniform bound. The same argument works after sending an original point at infinity to a finite point, because Möbius changes preserve the quasiconformal constant. [step 1.2, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 The components form a null family in spherical diameter. Choose one component $V_0$ and send an interior point of it to infinity by a fixed Möbius map. It then contains a neighborhood of infinity, so every other component lies in a common bounded Euclidean disk $B(0,R)$. Each is a bounded Jordan domain. Its Euclidean diameter equals its boundary diameter: the maximum distance on its compact closure cannot have an interior endpoint, since moving that endpoint slightly away from the other increases the distance. If infinitely many of these domains had diameter at least $d>0$, for each choose a boundary point and a second one at distance at least $d/2$. Step 1.2 at radius $d/4$ would give a ball of radius $cd/4$ inside each domain. Infinitely many disjoint balls of that fixed area cannot lie in $B(0,R)$. Hence there are finitely many components above each Euclidean diameter threshold; the same holds for spherical diameter on this bounded chart, and a fixed sphere homeomorphism preserves the null-family property by uniform continuity. The one excluded component is irrelevant. There are at most countably many components, since each contains a distinct first member of a countable sphere base. The remaining case has $\Omega$ nonempty by step 1.1, so $V_0$ exists. [A1, step 1.2, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 For every component $V$ with stabilizer $G_V$, we now prove $\Lambda(G_V)=\partial V$. First every ideal accumulation point of a $G_V$ orbit lies in $\Lambda(\Gamma)$, since it is a subgroup orbit. A sequence of distinct matrices has, after normalizing determinant-one lifts by their Hilbert–Schmidt norm, a rank-one limit along a subsequence: bounded lifts would contradict discreteness, and the normalized determinant tends to zero. If its kernel and image are $b,a$, projectivizing gives convergence to $a$ locally uniformly off $b$, and the normalized Hermitian products show its interior orbit converges to $a$ by [F5]. Choose a point of $V$ other than $b$. Its images stay in $V$, so $a\in\overline V$. Since $a\in\Lambda(\Gamma)$, it is not in $V$, proving $\Lambda(G_V)\subseteq\partial V$. Conversely fix $p\in\partial V\subset\Lambda(\Gamma)$. If $p$ is ambient conical, [F4] gives distinct $g_n$ with $g_np\to u$ and $g_nq\to v\ne u$ for all $q\ne p$. Choosing another boundary point of $V$ shows that the boundaries of $g_nV$ have spherical diameter bounded below. Step 2.2 makes their collection finite; pass to a subsequence with $g_nV=W$ and fix one $g_*$ mapping $V$ to $W$. Then $k_n=g_*^{-1}g_n$ are distinct elements of $G_V$ with the same separated boundary-collapse property. Their normalized rank-one matrix limit must have kernel $p$: otherwise $k_np$ would have the same limit as the other boundary points. The inverse normalized matrices have image $p$, so $k_n^{-1}o\to p$ by the Hermitian computation. Hence $p\in\Lambda(G_V)$. If $p$ is ambient parabolic, any nonidentity parabolic generator fixing it permutes the finitely many components whose boundaries contain $p$, by step 2.1. A positive power preserves $V$, and its orbit tends to its fixed point $p$, again giving $p\in\Lambda(G_V)$. These cases exhaust boundary points by [F4]. The equality is proved, and each $G_V$ is nonelementary since a Jordan curve contains at least three points. [F4, F5, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Loxodromic fixed points of each $G_V$ are dense in $\partial V$. A nonelementary torsion-free group has a loxodromic element: if none were present, it would contain parabolics with different fixed points. Normalize one to $z\mapsto z+a$ and represent another by a trace-two matrix with lower-left entry $c\ne0$. The products with the first raised to the $n$th power have trace $2+nac$, whose absolute values eventually exceed two; such a matrix is loxodromic, since a nonloxodromic torsion-free element has repeated eigenvalues or unit-modulus eigenvalues with trace of absolute value at most two. Choose one loxodromic element with endpoints $x,y$. For any $p\in\Lambda(G_V)$, a sequence of distinct group elements with interior orbit tending to $p$ has, by the rank-one argument in step 3.1, sphere convergence to $p$ off one exceptional point. At least one of $x,y$ is not exceptional, so the corresponding endpoint of the conjugated loxodromic elements tends to $p$. Thus their endpoints are dense. Each endpoint is on $\partial V$ by step 3.1. If a homeomorphism $h:V\to V$ extends continuously to its boundary and commutes with $G_V$, it fixes these endpoints: apply $h\gamma^n=\gamma^nh$ to an interior point and take positive or negative iterates. Continuity and density force its boundary restriction to be the identity. [F5, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Fix a representative $\Omega_j$. Compose its supplied marked homotopy with $\pi_j:\Omega_j\to X_j$ and lift it through $g(\Omega_j)\to X^g_j$, starting with $g|_{\Omega_j}$, by [F3]. The endpoint $A_j:\Omega_j\to g(\Omega_j)$ satisfies $A_j\gamma=\rho_g(\gamma)A_j$ for every $\gamma\in\Gamma_j$, by uniqueness of lifted time paths. Thus $F_j=A_j(f|_{\Omega_j})^{-1}:f(\Omega_j)\to g(\Omega_j)$ is an equivariant lift of $a_j$. It is biholomorphic: covering sheet inverses make it holomorphic; the simply connected quasidisk domains are universal covers, and lifting $a_j^{-1}$ with the corresponding base point gives an inverse whose two composites are the identity by lift uniqueness. The domains are Jordan domains, since global sphere homeomorphisms take their Jordan boundaries to Jordan boundaries. By [F3], Riemann maps and their boundary extensions make $F_j$ extend homeomorphically to their closures. Therefore $h_j=g^{-1}F_jf:\Omega_j\to\Omega_j$ is quasiconformal, extends to the boundary, commutes with $\Gamma_j$ and has quasiconformal constant at most $K=K_gK_f$. Step 4.1 makes it the identity on $\partial\Omega_j$. All homotopy lifting uses exactly the supplied puncture-relative homotopy when labels are present. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 Extend this definition equivariantly to each component $V=\gamma\Omega_j$ by $h_V=\gamma h_j\gamma^{-1}$. It is well-defined because $h_j$ commutes with its full stabilizer. It has the same bound $K$, maps $V$ onto itself and is the identity on $\partial V$. On $\Lambda$ prescribe the identity. This gives a pointwise bijection $H$ of the sphere, with a componentwise-defined inverse, commuting with $\Gamma$. Global continuity and Sobolev regularity have not yet been asserted; they are proved next. [F2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Apply step 1.3 to $q_V^{-1}h_Vq_V$ on $D$. Its quasiconformal bound is at most $KQ^2$, and it has identity boundary values. Conjugating its global identity extension back by $q_V$ gives a sphere homeomorphism $H_V$ equal to $h_V$ on $V$ and identity off $V$, with bound $K_0=KQ^4$. This is uniform over all components. Enumerate the components and form the finite compositions $H^{(N)}=H_{V_1}\cdots H_{V_N}$. The supports are disjoint and each factor preserves its own component, so this is precisely $h_{V_i}$ on the included components and identity elsewhere. Its actual quasiconformal bound is $K_0$, independently of $N$. Indeed composition gives quasiconformality first; off the finitely many quasicircle boundaries its local differential is the differential of one factor or identity. Each such boundary is null by the quasiconformal N-property applied to the circle under $q_V$. Hence its analytic inequality has bound $K_0$ almost everywhere, rather than the crude product of constants. [F2, step 6.1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

8.1 Every $H^{(N)}$ fixes three distinct points of $\Lambda$, since $\Gamma$ is nonelementary. Conjugate by one fixed Möbius transformation sending these points to $0,1,\infty$ and apply [F1] with $K_0$. A subsequence converges uniformly to a $K_0$-quasiconformal sphere homeomorphism. On each component its values eventually agree with $h_V$, because all later factors have other supports; on $\Lambda$ they always equal identity. Thus its pointwise limit is exactly the prescribed map $H$ from step 6.1, proving that map is a global quasiconformal homeomorphism. For finitely many components use their finite composition directly. Equivariance is the pointwise one already established in step 6.1. This supplies global regularity from the proved local ACL extension and actual normalized compactness, without assuming that a piecewise homeomorphism is automatically quasiconformal across a null set. [A1, F1, F2, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Put $G=gHf^{-1}$. It is a quasiconformal sphere homeomorphism by [F2]. On $f(\Omega_j)$ it equals $F_j$, conformal by step 5.1, and on every translated component its corresponding translate is conformal by the Möbius equivariance. These are all components of the complement of $f(\Lambda)$ by [F4]. The set $f(\Lambda)$ is null: $\Lambda$ is null by step 1.1 and [F6], and [F2] supplies the quasiconformal N-property in sphere charts. Thus $G$ has coefficient zero almost everywhere. The measurable sphere mapping theorem's uniqueness relative to the identity in [F2] makes $G$ a Möbius transformation $M$. Since $H$ fixes $\Lambda$ pointwise, $g|_\Lambda=M f|_\Lambda$. Since $H$ commutes with $\Gamma$, conjugating by $G=gHf^{-1}$ gives $M\rho_f(\gamma)M^{-1}=gH\gamma H^{-1}g^{-1}=\rho_g(\gamma)$ for every $\gamma$. Finally, if $f,g$ agree on three distinct points of $\Lambda$, the equation for their restrictions makes $M$ fix their three distinct common image points, so $M=\mathrm{id}$. This proves exactly the marked deformation uniqueness and normalization assertions, without asserting equality of the original maps in the components. [F2, F4, F6, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, given, algebra] ∎
