## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Fix finitely many labeled stable input and opposite surface types. Consider ALL supplied boundary models of [[thm-supplied-geometrically-finite-boundary-models-have-holomorphic-skinning-and-spot-sum-coderivative]] with these types: $\Gamma$ is discrete, torsion-free, nonelementary and geometrically finite; ALL its ordinary components are uniform quasidisks; $D_i$, $1\le i\le m$, represent every component orbit, with full stabilizers $H_i$ and actual cofinite quotient surfaces
$$X_i=D_i/H_i,\qquad Z_i=D_i^*/H_i,\qquad D_i^*=S^2\setminus\overline{D_i}.$$
All complex orientations are inherited from the sphere. The uniform quasidisk bound is supplied within each model; no common such bound over this class is assumed. Cofinite models, compact cusp truncations and primitive peripheral labels are supplied as in that theorem. Empty $m$ makes every unit-quadratic assertion below vacuous.

For each $i$ and $H_i$-orbit of actual components $E\ne D_i$, put $K_E=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma E$ and use the actual spot
$$U_{i,E}=E/(H_i\cap K_E)\hookrightarrow Z_i,$$
covering the appropriate $X_b$ after a supplied translate $E=\gamma D_b$. Require the subgroup of $\pi_1(X_b)$ defining EACH such cover to be either trivial or cyclic, or the image of an actual compact connected essential incompressible subsurface, with cusp truncations and paired parallel rims allowed. This geometric-or-elementary cover condition is an additional supplied hypothesis, not a consequence asserted from quasidisks alone.

Write $T= (D\Sigma)^*$ for the ACTUAL spot-sum operator of the supplied model. For $0<\varepsilon<\eta_*/2$, let $\mathcal L_\varepsilon\subset\bigsqcup_i Z_i$ be the union, inside every spot, of all degree-ONE components lying over either a geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin annulus of its base $X_b$, or a connected component of $X_b$ minus its OPEN geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin part. Degree one means the restricted covering is a homeomorphism, also at the boundary of the latter components. Thus this is the actual geodesic lifted part, including cusp ends attached to such lifted complementary regions; it is not defined by abstract subgroup containment alone.

For every $h>0$ there is $\varepsilon_h>0$ such that for EVERY $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_h$ there is $\delta=\delta(h,\varepsilon)>0$, depending only on the fixed finite type list and the displayed supplied class, with
$$\sum_i\|q_i\|_1=1,\qquad \|Tq\|_1>1-\delta
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\int_{\mathcal L_\varepsilon}|q|>1-h.$$
The constants are uniform over every such model, its countably many possibly nonregular spots and every integrable holomorphic $q=(q_i)$ on the opposite surfaces.

Moreover, if a primitive geodesic $\alpha$ on some $X_b$ has length $\ell<\varepsilon/2$ and its geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin annulus has $|Tq|$ mass $\mu>h$, then some opposite $Z_i$ has a primitive geodesic $\beta$ of length at most $\ell$, obtained from an ACTUAL degree-one spot lift of $\alpha$, whose geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin annulus carries $|q_i|$ mass at least
$$\frac{\mu-h}{R},\qquad R=\sum_i(3g(Z_i)-3+n(Z_i))\ge1.$$
These conclusions give the complete local liftable-mass and one-step curve/mass propagation operations under the stated boundary/cover model. They do not construct that model for an arbitrary pared manifold, identify cylinders in a three-manifold, or turn a repeated unoriented curve class alone into an essential torus.

## Proof

1.1 Work in a nonempty model and abbreviate all spots by $s$, with covering $p_s:U_s\to X_{b(s)}$ and embedding into $Z_{i(s)}$. Put $r_s=q_{i(s)}|_{U_s}$ in its spot coordinates. By [F1], the spots are disjoint and cover up to a null set, so $$\sum_s\|r_s\|_1=1,\qquad \phi=Tq=\sum_s\Theta_{p_s}r_s,\qquad 0\le J:=1-\|\phi\|_1\le1.$$ On each base choose its measurable phase tensor $\nu_b=\overline{\phi_b}/|\phi_b|$ off the zeros and zero at the zeros. Intrinsically this is a Beltrami tensor, since a quadratic coordinate change contributes exactly the conjugate-derivative/derivative factor. Assemble these tensors $\Gamma$-invariantly on its ordinary components and set zero on the limit set, by [F1]'s actual linear extension. Denote the resulting sphere tensor by $\nu$ and its descent to $Z_i$ by $\nu_i$. It has modulus at most one. Countably many sheets and components ensure that exceptional null sets can be discarded simultaneously for each group element. Its global invariance, not membership of a point in one spot, will be used in comparison balls. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We verify the primitive target class needed for the final assertion, rather than infer it from holomorphic embedding. Let a primitive base core $\gamma\in K_E$ lift with degree one in a spot. At an appropriate representative point it belongs to $H_i\cap K_E$. If $\gamma=\eta^k$ in $H_i$ for $|k|>1$, apply [F2]'s root-closure with $D_i,E$ interchanged: $\eta\in H_i$ and $\eta^k\in K_E$ imply $\eta\in K_E$, contrary to primitivity in $K_E$. Thus the image class is primitive in $H_i$. The base core is disk-hyperbolic in $E$, so its ambient transformation is loxodromic by [F2]. The opposite domain $D_i^*$ is also a Jordan invariant disk; applying the same type correspondence to the group $H_i$ on it shows that this element is hyperbolic in its supplied disk action, hence gives a nontrivial primitive loxodromic surface class on $Z_i$. This uses actual full component stabilizers on both sides, not an arbitrary abstract intersection. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The exact absolutely integrable pairing identity [F1] gives $$\sum_i\int_{Z_i}\nu_iq_i=\sum_b\int_{X_b}\nu_b\phi_b=\sum_b\|\phi_b\|_1=1-J.$$ Thus the nonnegative individual defects $$D_i=\int_{Z_i}(|q_i|-\operatorname{Re}(\nu_iq_i))$$ satisfy $\sum_iD_i=J$. There is no conjugation of the complex-bilinear pairing beyond the explicit choice of the phase tensor. Also, on each base let $S$ be the sum of absolute values of all inverse-branch quadratic coefficients, interpreted as an intrinsic density. By [F1] and [F8], $$S\ge|\phi|,\qquad\int_{\bigsqcup X_b}S=1,\qquad\int(S-|\phi|)=J.$$ In particular every measurable base subset $B$ satisfies $$\sum_s\int_{p_s^{-1}B}|r_s|\le\int_B|\phi|+J.$$ This local triangle-defect bound already includes cancellation between different spots. [F1, F8, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 There exist $\varepsilon_*>0$ and $c_*<1$, uniform over the fixed types, such that every point of a non-degree-one geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin preimage with $\varepsilon<\varepsilon_*$ admits an inefficient ball as in [F4] for ANY given nonzero $q_i$ and globally invariant tensor of modulus at most one. To prove this carefully, lift the point to $x\in E$ and let $\gamma\in K_E$ be the primitive displacement generator of its base annulus. Its disk displacement at $x$ is $<2\varepsilon$, and its ambient type is loxodromic by [F2]. It is not in $H_i$: if it were, the annulus would lift with degree one at this point. Conversely any finite positive degree greater than one would give $\gamma^k\in H_i$ and $\gamma\notin H_i$, excluded by root-closure [F2]. If no uniform constants existed, choose $\varepsilon_n\to0$ and counterexamples with no ball of efficiency at most $1-1/n$, passing to a single pair of the finitely many input/opposite types. Apply [F3] to their ACTUAL components and elements. It supplies a common $D_2$ injection, centers tending to zero, and maps converging to one nonidentity parabolic $G$ fixing zero. With $r=1/1000$, $s=3/2$, $R=2$, $K=\overline D_1$ and the compact parabolic family $\{G\}$, [F4] supplies a constant $c_*<1$ and then the required metric balls for all late $n$. On $D_{3/2}$ the normalized restriction of the global tensor satisfies its invariance equation because both that disk and its image lie in $D_2$. This contradicts the chosen counterexamples. Finite many type pairs give common constants by minima and maxima. Balls are allowed to cross spot boundaries; invariance holds there because the coefficient was defined globally on the sphere. [A1, F2, F3, F4, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 The input areas are bounded by a common number determined by their fixed cofinite types. For fixed $\varepsilon$ and $a>0$, take [F5]'s uniform threshold $t>0$ for all the supplied geometric-or-elementary covers. Let $m_s=\|r_s\|_1$ and $d_s=m_s-\|\Theta_{p_s}r_s\|_1$. By step 1.1 and triangle inequality, $\sum_sm_s=1$ and $\sum_sd_s\le J$. On a positive-mass spot with $d_s/m_s<t$, apply [F5] to $r_s/m_s$: its mass outside its intrinsic amenable set $\mathcal A_\varepsilon(p_s)$ is less than $a m_s$. The total mass of all remaining spots is at most $J/t$. Zero-mass spots contribute nothing. Consequently $$\int_{\bigsqcup Z_i\setminus\bigcup_s\mathcal A_\varepsilon(p_s)}|q|\le a+J/t.$$ Here the uncovered complement is null by [F1]. This proves the needed uniform concentration on the full thin preimage plus all degree-one thick lifts directly from the established individual-cover modulus, including arbitrary cancellations and a countable nonregular spot family. [F1, F5, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $F_i(\varepsilon)$ be the union in $Z_i$ of the non-degree-one geodesic $\varepsilon$-thin preimages in its spots. These are measurable countable unions of open annular or universal-strip components. Ignore $i$ with $q_i=0$, which contribute no mass. Step 2.2 gives a family of actual inefficient metric balls covering $F_i(\varepsilon)$, all with radius at most the center's injectivity radius and all with the same inefficiency. Normalize $q_i$ by its norm and apply [F4]'s disjoint-ball/doubling conclusion, then rescale. It gives $$\int_{F_i(\varepsilon)}|q_i|\le\frac{C(\operatorname{type}(Z_i),5)}{1-c_*}D_i.$$ Taking the maximum over the finite type list and summing, there is $C_*<\infty$ with $$\int_{\bigcup_iF_i(\varepsilon)}|q|\le C_*J\qquad(0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_*).$$ No separate covering constants were maximized over countably many covers; one fixed-type ball estimate controls their entire union. [F4, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, construct, algebra]

3.2 Fix once $0<a_0<\eta_*/2$ and take $\varepsilon<a_0$. For the union $C_\varepsilon$ of the cuspidal $\varepsilon$-thin parts of the BASE surfaces $X_b$, [F6] and $\|\phi\|_1\le1$ give $\int_{C_\varepsilon}|\phi|\le u(\varepsilon)$. Step 2.1 therefore yields $$\sum_s\int_{p_s^{-1}(C_\varepsilon)}|r_s|\le u(\varepsilon)+J.$$ This estimates all cusp preimages, including universal covers of cusp tails. It does not assert that such a preimage is an actual cusp of $Z_i$, nor apply a source cusp-tail estimate to an arbitrary embedded spot. [F6, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Every degree-one lift of a closed $\varepsilon$-thick component $T$ extends to a degree-one lift of the connected component of $X_b$ minus its OPEN geodesic thin part which contains $T$. Indeed that latter component is $T$ with exactly its attached cusp tails; cusp tails each have one rim and cannot connect different thick components. The explicit height-coordinate deformation retracts each tail onto that rim and is identity on $T$. Lift this deformation by [F6], or equivalently lift its vertical height rays. Over a degree-one $T$, each rim's primitive loop fixes its sheet, so its adjacent cusp lift is itself degree one. It cannot attach to a different sheet or another thick lift by unique path lifting. Thus the entire component maps homeomorphically, including boundary, to the indicated complementary region. This identifies the chosen good thick lifts with actual members of $\mathcal L_\varepsilon$, without claiming all sheets are one-sheeted. Every point of $\mathcal A_\varepsilon$ which is not in $\mathcal L_\varepsilon$ therefore lies over a cusp or in a non-degree-one geodesic thin component. Boundary level curves have zero quadratic mass in their local smooth annulus coordinates; alternatively the definition included their closed thick lifts. Combining steps 3.1, 3.2 and 2.3 gives $$\int_{\bigsqcup Z_i\setminus\mathcal L_\varepsilon}|q|\le a+u(\varepsilon)+(1+C_*+t^{-1})J.$$ This is the complete thin/thick/degree-one decomposition, with no thin-geodesic mass discarded as negligible. [F6, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

5.1 Given $h>0$, choose $a<h/4$ and $\varepsilon_h<\min(a_0,\varepsilon_*,\eta_*/2)$ so small that $u(\varepsilon)<h/4$ for all $0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_h$. For EACH such fixed $\varepsilon$, [F5] supplies the threshold $t$ used in step 2.3. Choose $0<\delta<1$ with $(1+C_*+t^{-1})\delta<h/4$. If $\|Tq\|_1>1-\delta$, then $J<\delta$, and step 4.1 gives mass outside $\mathcal L_\varepsilon$ less than $3h/4<h$. Hence $\int_{\mathcal L_\varepsilon}|q|>1-h$. All constants are uniform over the precise supplied model class. The dependence $\delta(h,\varepsilon)$ is permitted; no moduli-uniform covering spectral gap or norm continuity was assumed. [F5, F6, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 For the base short annulus in the last assertion, select ALL degree-one components of its spot preimages. Any omitted component is disjoint from $\mathcal L_\varepsilon$: it cannot lie over the complementary base region and was not a degree-one annulus lift. By step 5.1 its total omitted $|q|$ mass is less than $h$. Its selected components have precisely the primitive loxodromic target images proved in step 1.2. Apply the complete annulus propagation theorem [F7] to this actual spot family, with omitted-mass bound $b=h<\mu$. It supplies a primitive target core of length at most $\ell$, in the image class of an actual selected lift, and a target annulus of mass at least $(\mu-h)/R$, with $R\ge1$. This proves the stated one-step curve/mass operation. It retains the actual selected cover component and class correspondence for later topology, and supplies no assertion that finite repetition without transverse gluing data makes a torus.
