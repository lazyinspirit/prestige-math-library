## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $\Gamma\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb C)$ be discrete and torsion-free, and let $D,E$ be DISTINCT actual ordinary components with Jordan boundaries. Put
$$H=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma D,\qquad K=\operatorname{Stab}_\Gamma E,\qquad J=H\cap K.$$
Supply the cofinite disk quotient $X=E/K$, with its complete finite-area metric. Then exactly the following alternative is needed, with no finite-generation assumption on $J$: $J$ is trivial or cyclic, or it is the image of an ACTUAL compact connected smoothly bounded essential incompressible subsurface $N\subset X$, after the usual basepoint conjugacy. In the latter case inclusion identifies $\pi_1(N)$ with $J$. The possibility $J=K$ is allowed; if $X$ is compact then $N=X$ is allowed. Every boundary circle of $N$ is essential in $X$.

More precisely, for nonelementary $J$ let $L$ be its limit set in the ideal circle of the uniformizing disk of $E$, and let $C=\operatorname{CH}_{H^2}(L)$. The interior quotient $\operatorname{int}C/J$ embeds isometrically in $X$ and has area at most $\operatorname{area}(X)$. If $C=H^2$, then $J=K$. Otherwise $C/J$ is a complete smooth hyperbolic surface with locally geodesic boundary, of finite area, and its double is a complete finite-area hyperbolic surface without boundary. Folding that double onto $C/J$ makes $J$ a QUOTIENT of a finitely generated group and proves finite generation. A compact essential core is then constructed INSIDE $\operatorname{int}C/J$ and its actual embedding in $X$ yields $N$.

Only the original component $E/K$ is assumed cofinite; no finite generation, geometric finiteness, quasidisk bound or cofinite opposite domain for $H$, $K$ or $J$ is imported. This proves the geometric-or-elementary spot-cover condition for the stated actual component data. It does not construct a boundary model from arbitrary pared topological data.

## Proof

1.1 Use [F1] to uniformize $E$ by the disk and regard $K,J$ as real disk groups. If $J$ is elementary, [F1] makes it trivial or cyclic: a disk parabolic stabilizer is a discrete real translation group of rank one, and a loxodromic fixed-pair group is cyclic; an endpoint swap would have order two. Hence suppose $J$ is nonelementary. Its closed disk limit set $L$ has at least three points. Its physical image under the closed-$E$ uniformizer is contained in $\partial D\cap\partial E$. Here is the actual orbit check. For any sequence of distinct $j_n\in J$, choose determinant-one matrices $A_n$; their norms tend to infinity along a divergent subsequence, since the discrete subgroup has only finitely many elements in a compact matrix set. Normalize by those norms and take a unit-matrix limit $A\ne0$ of determinant zero. It has rank one. The Hermitian orbit formula $A_n I A_n^*$, after projective normalization, tends to the rank-one image point $q$, and the normalized inverse matrices tend to the adjugate of $A$, giving the kernel point $p$ as another limit-set point of $\Gamma$. Thus $p,q$ are outside the ordinary set. At every ordinary point, in particular at any fixed $u\in E$ and $v\in D$, the Möbius maps tend to the same image point $q$, since neither point is the kernel. A subsequence tending to a chosen point of $L$ therefore tends physically to that same point from BOTH $D$ and $E$. It lies on both boundaries. This proves the contact containment and identifies the disk and physical limit sets, without a finite-generation assumption. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix ANY finite set $A\subset L$ of at least three points, now viewed on $\partial E\cap\partial D$. A closed-$D$ Jordan uniformizer from [F1] maps the radial star joining zero to its finitely many distinct boundary preimages to an embedded finite star $T_A\subset\overline D$. Its hub and open arms lie in $D$, its leaves are exactly $A$, and it meets $\partial E$ only at those leaves because the ordinary domain $D$ misses $\partial E$. It lies in the closed outside-$E$ Jordan disk $O=S^2\setminus E$. The complement $\operatorname{int}O\setminus T_A$ consists of $|A|$ open Jordan sectors. To prove the precise sector statement, start with two arms: their union is a crosscut of $O$, which [F2] splits into two Jordan disks. Insert one remaining arm at a time. Its interior avoids the previous tree, so is in one existing sector, and its endpoints are the hub on that sector's boundary and a new point of its $\partial E$ interval. It is a crosscut of that Jordan disk, splitting just that sector. Induction gives sectors whose closures meet $\partial E$ in EXACTLY the closed intervals between successive leaves in the $E$-circle order. This works for arbitrary Jordan arms; no regularity or metric angle at a leaf is assumed. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, induction]

3.1 If $k\in K\setminus J$, then $kD\ne D$, since it preserves $E$ and would otherwise lie in both full stabilizers. Distinct ordinary components are disjoint. Thus connected $kD\subset\operatorname{int}O$ avoids the entire star $T_A$: its arms and hub are in $D$, while its leaves are limit-set points and hence not ordinary. It lies in ONE sector from step 2.1. Consequently its contact set with $\partial E$, in particular the physical set $kL\subset\partial(kD)\cap\partial E$, lies in the corresponding one closed interval $I$ between consecutive points of $A$. Transport this conclusion by the closed-$E$ uniformizer. The hyperbolic hull of $kL$ is contained in the closed half-plane facing $I$ and bounded by the geodesic joining the endpoints of $I$, whereas the interior of the finite ideal polygon $\operatorname{CH}(A)$ lies in the opposite half-plane. Hence $$\operatorname{int}\operatorname{CH}(A)\cap kC=\varnothing.$$ The contact conclusion uses the whole crosscut tree, not merely absence of alternating four distinct endpoints; it therefore covers shared three or more contact points as well. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Every point of $\operatorname{int}C$ belongs to the interior of the hull of some finite $A\subset L$. One direct verification uses the Klein disk, where hulls and their half-planes are Euclidean convex hulls intersected with the disk. The closed convex hull is the closure of the union of finite convex hulls. Around a given interior point choose three small noncollinear surrounding points still in that interior. Approximate them sufficiently closely by points of finite convex hulls; their triangle still surrounds the original point. The union of the finitely many ideal points used in those three approximations gives the required finite polygon. Enlarge its ideal vertex set if needed to have at least three points. Apply step 3.1 to that $A$. Thus for EVERY $k\in K\setminus J$, $$\operatorname{int}C\cap kC=\varnothing.$$ In particular the interiors of the distinct $K$ translates of $C$ are disjoint. The set $\operatorname{int}C$ is nonempty since $L$ contains an ideal triangle, is convex, and is simply connected by geodesic contraction to an interior point. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The quotient $W=\operatorname{int}C/J$ therefore embeds isometrically and openly in $X=E/K$. Indeed any two interior points identified by $k\in K$ would lie in $\operatorname{int}C\cap k\operatorname{int}C$, forcing $k\in J$ by step 4.1. This is an open embedding because quotient sheets are local isometries. Its induced map on fundamental groups is the inclusion $J\hookrightarrow K$: the simply connected interior is its universal cover with deck group $J$, and lifting a projected loop has exactly that deck endpoint. Hence $\operatorname{area}(W)\le\operatorname{area}(X)<\infty$. If $C=H^2$, step 4.1 immediately excludes every $k\in K\setminus J$, so $J=K$. In that case use a compact standard cusp truncation of $X$ from [F3], or $X$ itself if compact, as $N$. Its cusp tails retract onto their rims, so its inclusion induces all of $K$ and its boundary circles are essential primitive cusp loops. This proves the full-hull case. From now on $C$ is proper and has nonempty boundary. [F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

5.2 The boundary of $C$ in $H^2$ consists of the complete geodesic lines joining the endpoints of the complementary gap intervals of $L$ in the ideal circle. This follows directly by intersecting all their closed inner half-planes in the Klein disk: their ideal boundary is $L$ and their intersection is exactly its closed convex hull. The gap lines have disjoint interiors and are locally finite. In fact a gap chord meeting a fixed compact disk has a gap interval of circle length bounded below by some positive constant. Otherwise its endpoints converge to one ideal point; the explicit orthogonal-circle geodesic formula, or the Klein straight-chord formula on compact subdisks, makes those chords escape every compact subset. The disjoint gap intervals have total circle length at most $2\pi$, so only finitely many can meet the compact disk. Thus every boundary point of $C$ has a neighborhood where $C$ is a single closed geodesic half-disk. There are no finite vertices or accumulated boundary lines in that neighborhood. [F1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Give $M=C/J$ its intrinsic quotient metric. The action is free and proper by restriction of [F1]'s model action; half-disk quotient charts and step 5.2 make it a smooth oriented hyperbolic surface with geodesic boundary. The convex closed set $C$ is complete and proper in its path metric, which is its ambient hyperbolic distance. The same holds for its quotient: for a quotient Cauchy sequence choose a subsequence with successive distances $<2^{-r}$ and lift successively so successive lift distances are $<2^{1-r}$. The lifts are Cauchy in $C$, hence converge, and their projections give the limit of the original Cauchy sequence. Thus $M$ is complete. Its area is $\operatorname{area}(W)\le\operatorname{area}(X)$, since its geodesic boundary has zero area. To see this last null assertion even before a bound on its components, a second-countable half-disk chart cover is countable and its boundary is a countable union of coordinate intervals of planar area zero. The interior universal cover is convex and the closed hull is contractible; [F4] consequently identifies $\pi_1(M)$ and $\pi_1(W)$ with $J$, compatibly with inclusion. Equivalently, finite collar pushes of any loop and homotopy into the interior give the same identification. [A1, F1, F4, F6, step 5.1, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Double $M$ by gluing two copies along the identity on ALL their boundary components, reversing the orientation on the second copy. Reflection in a local geodesic boundary gives a full hyperbolic disk chart. These charts and the old interior charts fit to a smooth oriented boundaryless hyperbolic surface $\mathcal D M$; they are compatible because hyperbolic reflection fixes the boundary line pointwise and is its local metric reflection. The fold $f:\mathcal D M\to M$ is continuous, is identity on each labeled copy, and is 1-Lipschitz in path distance. This double is complete. A Cauchy sequence folds to a limit in complete $M$. If the limit is interior, a small closed disk about it stays a positive distance from the boundary, so a Cauchy sequence cannot keep switching copies: any crossing path must meet the boundary. It eventually stays in one interior chart and converges. If the folded limit is a boundary point, the two local half-disk charts glue to one full reflected disk and their points converge to the same seam point. This proves completeness without a uniform global collar width. Its area is $2\operatorname{area}(M)<\infty$. It is connected since $M$ is connected and its boundary is nonempty. The fold is a RETRACTION onto either copy, so the induced map $\pi_1(\mathcal D M)\to\pi_1(M)$ is surjective. [F6, step 5.2, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 We explicitly justify the two-dimensional complete model needed for [F3]. Apply general-dimensional [F5] to the oriented complete curvature-$-1$ surface $\mathcal D M$. Its exponential universal covering has polar metric $dr^2+\sinh^2r\,d\theta^2$: along a unit radial geodesic extend one perpendicular unit vector by parallel transport, and $V(r)=\sinh r\,e(r)$ has the prescribed zero value and unit perpendicular initial derivative and solves $D_r^2V+R(V,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0$. Jacobi uniqueness and the exponential differential formula give the angular coefficient; radial unit length and orthogonality follow from the same radial geodesics and the perpendicular parallel field. Thus the complete covering plane is the standard hyperbolic plane, via $(r,\theta)\mapsto(\cosh r,\sinh r\cos\theta,\sinh r\sin\theta)$ and then the disk model. Its orientation-preserving isometries are precisely disk Möbius maps: the explicit maps in [F4] move a point and its oriented tangent frame arbitrarily, and an isometry fixing that frame fixes every emanating geodesic by uniqueness, hence every point by the polar model. Deck transformations are free and form a discrete group, as an evenly covered disk excludes nonidentity nearby transformations. A finite-order isometry has a fixed point by summing its finite orbit vectors in the hyperboloid and normalizing the future timelike sum, so the deck group is torsion-free. This writes $\mathcal D M$ as a complete torsion-free Fuchsian quotient, with its given metric. No three-dimensional theorem was applied to a surface. [F1, F4, F5, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Its finite area and [F3] now supply a compact standard-cusp truncation of $\mathcal D M$. Its fundamental group is finitely generated: [F6] triangulates that compact smooth region, a spanning tree of its finite one-skeleton gives finitely many edge-loop generators, and cellular approximation moves every loop into the one-skeleton. The finitely many cusp tails retract by height onto their boundary rims, so adding them changes no fundamental group. The fold of step 7.1 therefore proves that $\pi_1(M)=J$ is a QUOTIENT of a finitely generated group, and hence finitely generated by the images of its finite generating list. No assertion that an arbitrary subgroup of a finitely generated group is finitely generated is made. [F3, F4, F6, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 It remains to obtain the compact core INSIDE the embedded interior $W$, where the inherited metric need not be complete. In the disk of $E$, $\operatorname{int}C$ is a proper simply connected domain; its geodesic contraction makes every loop contractible and hence every complex cycle have zero winding about omitted points. Apply [F4] to give it a disk uniformizer. Conjugating $J$ by that uniformizer gives a discrete torsion-free disk group: if its distinct maps approached identity, their values at three interior points would make the original Möbius maps approach identity at three physical points of $E$, contrary to discreteness. Its invariant disk metric descends to a COMPLETE Poincare metric on $W$, with deck group $J$ and the same complex smooth structure. Completeness follows directly by lifting metric Cauchy subsequences as in step 6.1 to the complete disk model. Topologically $W$ is noncompact: if it were compact, it would be closed in Hausdorff $M$ as well as open; connectedness of $M$ would force $W=M$, contradicting the nonempty boundary. This uses a new complete conformal metric on $W$, not completeness of its inherited convex-interior metric. [F1, F4, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 9.1, construct, algebra]

11.1 Apply [F3]'s actual compact-core construction to this noncompact complete Fuchsian surface $W$ and its finitely generated nontrivial group $J$. It gives a compact connected smoothly bounded $N\subset W$ with essential boundary and $\pi_1(N)\cong J$. Under the actual open embedding $W\hookrightarrow X$ of step 5.1, this is an embedded compact smooth surface; the two conformal metrics used on $W$ have the same smooth coordinates. Its induced group map into $X$ is the inclusion $J\hookrightarrow K$, so it is injective. Every boundary class, nontrivial in $J$ by the core theorem, is nontrivial in $K$, hence essential in $X$. This proves the required genuine geometric subgroup realization, with no claim that merely finitely generated subgroups are geometrically realized by an arbitrary immersion. Rims were chosen strictly in $W$, so paired limiting boundary lines of the convex hull cause no identification of compact boundary circles. Together with the full-hull and elementary cases this proves every assertion. [F3, step 5.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
