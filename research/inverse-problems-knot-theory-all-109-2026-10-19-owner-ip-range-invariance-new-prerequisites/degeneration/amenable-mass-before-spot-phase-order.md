## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $X_n$ be connected complete finite-area hyperbolic surfaces, with $\operatorname{area}(X_n)\le A<\infty$. Let $p_n:Y_n\to X_n$ be connected holomorphic unbranched covers. For each $n$, require its subgroup $H_n\le\pi_1(X_n)$ either to be trivial or cyclic, or to be the image of an actual compact connected essential incompressible subsurface $S_n\subset X_n$. Cusps may first be truncated to obtain this compact subsurface; annular complementary components and paired boundary copies are allowed. No normality or uniform bound on the topology of $S_n$ is required.

Fix $0<\varepsilon_0<\eta_*/2$, where $\eta_*$ is the uniform local thin displacement threshold. For any $0<\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0$, let $\mathcal A_\varepsilon(p_n)$ be the union of $p_n^{-1}\{\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}<\varepsilon\}$ with every connected component of $p_n^{-1}(T)$ that maps with degree one to a connected component $T$ of the closed thick set $\{\operatorname{inj}_{X_n}\ge\varepsilon\}$. Here degree one means that the restricted covering is a homeomorphism, including boundary; these sets are measurable. The union includes every degree-one thick lift, independently of a chosen representative of the subgroup.

For integrable holomorphic quadratic differentials $\phi_n$ on $Y_n$, if
$$\|\phi_n\|_1=1,\qquad \|\Theta_{p_n}\phi_n\|_1\longrightarrow1,$$
then
$$\int_{Y_n\setminus\mathcal A_{\varepsilon_0}(p_n)}|\phi_n|\longrightarrow0.$$
This holds for arbitrary such varying surfaces and subsurfaces under the displayed area bound. In particular it includes every fixed marked finite-type geometric-cover family. For nonelementary proper geometric subgroups there is at most one degree-one lift over each thick component. The other components need only have cyclic or uniformly bounded-rank infinite-index stabilizers, and are not asserted to be universal covers.

The following countable-spot consequence is included. Let $W_n$ be second-countable Riemann surfaces, let $U_{n,j}\subset W_n$ be pairwise disjoint connected open sets in a finite or countable family, and supply holomorphic coverings $p_{n,j}:U_{n,j}\to B_{n,\alpha(j)}$ satisfying the same geometric-or-elementary subgroup condition. Here $B_n$ is a finite or countable disjoint union of finite-area complete hyperbolic surfaces, each of area at most $A$. For $q_n\in Q(W_n)$ of norm one, form the absolutely integrable holomorphic spot sum
$$T_nq_n=\sum_j\Theta_{p_{n,j}}(q_n|_{U_{n,j}})\in Q(B_n).$$
If $\|T_nq_n\|_1\to1$, then the $|q_n|$ mass outside the union of the intrinsic $\mathcal A_{\varepsilon_0}(p_{n,j})$ tends to zero. No finite bound on the number of spots, no absence of cancellation between their pushforwards, and no full-measure assumption on their union are needed. Their geometric-cover models themselves remain supplied hypotheses.

The conclusion uses the intrinsic degree-one-lift definition just given. Identifying it with a definition phrased using ambient isotopies into a chosen $S_n$ requires the corresponding surface-isotopy supplier; no such identification or full Iteration gluing conclusion is assumed in this theorem.

## Proof

1.1 Put $\psi_n=\Theta_{p_n}\phi_n$ and $I_n=1-\|\psi_n\|_1$. By [F1], these are globally defined holomorphic integrable quadratics, $I_n\ge0$ and $I_n\to0$. All covering sheets and components are countable since the surfaces are second countable. All the sets in the statement are measurable: the thick set is a compact surface with finitely many boundary circles and components by [F3], its inverse is locally a countable union of relative coordinate sheets, and a degree-one component is a union of such sheets. In particular all restricted mass integrals below are defined. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The intrinsic amenable sets are monotone in the threshold: if $0<e<E\le\varepsilon_0$, then $\mathcal A_e(p)\subset\mathcal A_E(p)$. A point over $\{\operatorname{inj}<e\}$ is already over $\{\operatorname{inj}<E\}$. Otherwise a point of $\mathcal A_e$ lies in a degree-one lift of an $e$-thick component $T_e$. If its projection is not $E$-thick it is in the larger thin part. If it is $E$-thick, its $E$-thick component $T_E$ is a connected subset of $T_e$. The inverse image of $T_E$ inside that degree-one lift maps homeomorphically to $T_E$. It is an entire component over $T_E$: local unique lifting, also in boundary half-discs, forbids attachment to another sheet. Thus the point belongs to $\mathcal A_E$. This proves the inclusion including points on the threshold boundaries; no geometric representative of $S$ was used. [F3, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Suppose the conclusion fails. Passing to a subsequence, fix $b>0$ with mass at least $b$ outside $\mathcal A_{\varepsilon_0}(p_n)$ for every $n$. That set lies over the closed $\varepsilon_0$-thick set. There are at most $M=\max(1,\lceil A/(2\pi)\rceil)$ components. Choose one component $T_n$ whose bad mass is at least $b/M$; here and below bad means outside the specified intrinsic amenable set. By the local defect bound, $$\int_{T_n}|\psi_n|\ge b/M-I_n.$$ Choose any $x_n\in T_n$, lift it to $i\in H^2$ and use its oriented hyperbolic frame. Its injectivity radius is at least $\varepsilon_0$, while $\|\psi_n\|_1\le1$. Apply [F2] and pass to a subsequence to obtain a finite-area thick-pointed limit $X$ with a nonzero UNscaled holomorphic quadratic $\psi$. Nonvanishing follows from the displayed positive mass on $T_n$ and the uniformly bounded ambient radius of $T_n$, exactly as proved in [F2]. No projective rescaling or source norm-continuity assertion is being used. [A1, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose an admissible $0<e<\varepsilon_0$ for this limiting group. Let $Z$ be its base $e$-thick component and $Z_n$ the corresponding entire component on $X_n$. The admissible threshold and actual whole-component marking $P_n:Z\to Z_n$ exist by [F3]. The connected set $T_n$ lies in $Z_n$, since all its points have injectivity radius at least $\varepsilon_0>e$ and it contains the basepoint. By step 1.2, $$\int_{p_n^{-1}Z_n\setminus\mathcal A_e(p_n)}|\phi_n|\ge b/M.$$ The marking fixes the homeomorphism type of $Z_n$. Put $R=\max(1,1-\chi(Z))$, a finite integer. Each $K_n=\pi_1(Z_n)$ has negative Euler characteristic, hence is either a free group of the fixed rank $1-\chi(Z)\ge2$ when it has boundary, or the fixed marked closed surface group when it has none. In the latter case $Z_n=X_n$: a nonempty compact boundaryless region of a connected surface is open and closed. The resulting entire surfaces converge smoothly to the compact $Z=X$. [F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Consider a connected component of $p_n^{-1}Z_n$ which is not degree one. Its subgroup $L\le K_n$ has infinite index and is generated by at most $R$ elements. For nonelementary geometric $H_n$ this is precisely [F4], applied to the actual incompressible thick subsurface $Z_n$; its boundary curves are essential by [F3]. If $H_n$ is trivial or cyclic, its intersection subgroup is trivial or cyclic and hence is generated by at most one element. Such a subgroup cannot have finite index in $K_n$: a finite-index cover of the negative-Euler compact core multiplies Euler characteristic by its positive degree, whereas a cyclic or trivial surface core has nonnegative Euler characteristic. Equivalently the finite triangulation lifts to that degree and a free cyclic group has rank at most one; in the closed case a finite-index subgroup is the fundamental group of a closed negative-Euler surface. Thus both input alternatives give exactly the claimed infinite-index, at-most-$R$ class. Components of degree one are exactly those for which $L=K_n$, by pointed unique path lifting. No claim that the other components are universal is needed. [F3, F4, F6, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Fix any $d>0$. Apply [F3] to the nonzero limit $\psi$ and compact $Z$ to choose finite transported equal flat nets $U\subset\operatorname{int}Z$ and $U_n\subset\operatorname{int}Z_n$, with $$\limsup_n\int_{Z_n\setminus U_n}|\psi_n|<d.$$ They have one fixed finite graph $Q$ with $s$ vertices, common disc area and one common positive lower lens fraction. The graph maps onto $K_n$ by the actual monodromy and marking; its based edge-loop labels are fixed under $P_n$. Since $\pi_1(U_n)\to K_n$ is onto, connected components of the lifted graph correspond bijectively to components of $p_n^{-1}Z_n$. Indeed the monodromy orbits on a root fiber agree, by [F6], and each lifted disc is attached to that root orbit by lifted graph paths. Thus the chosen bad graph components have precisely the subgroups classified in step 4.1. [F3, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

5.2 Fix finite generators of the marked group $K=\pi_1(Z)$, and identify all $K_n$ with it by $P_n$. In the free case choose a free basis; [F5] gives one positive Schreier outer-vertex expansion for every infinite-index subgroup generated by at most $R$. In the closed case use [F5]'s uniform smooth-near-compact-model conclusion, since step 3.1 supplies exactly its global smooth comparison and fixed marking. It gives the same uniform positive expansion conclusion, with respect to one fixed finite marked generating set. Take the minimum of the constants if necessary. Thus there is $\gamma_0>0$, independent of $n$ and of the bad component, for all these marked Schreier graphs. This is a local compact-model assertion only, not a bound over all of moduli space. [F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 We spell out why that bound transfers to every actual lifted $Q$, without requiring the covering to be regular. Choose a root and a spanning tree in finite connected $Q$, and gauge the tree labels to identity by the root paths. Vertices of a component are then pairs $(v,Lg)$, and traversing an oriented base edge multiplies the coset on the right by its fixed monodromy label. Let $D$ bound tree-path lengths. Since the finite based edge-loop labels generate $K$, each of the fixed group generators is represented by a based $Q$ loop of some uniformly bounded length $L_0$. For a finite vertex set $W$ in a lifted component, let $V$ be its set of touched cosets; $|V|\ge|W|/s$. From a vertex of $W$ with coset $v$, follow its tree path to the root, then a generator loop to any specified Schreier neighbor $v'$ outside $V$. The final root vertex is outside $W$. Its first outside vertex is in the outer border of $W$, and the final root is within distance $D+L_0$ of that border. If $\Delta$ bounds degree of $Q$, each border vertex can account for at most $B=\sum_{j=0}^{D+L_0}\max(1,\Delta)^j$ such endpoints. Distinct outside cosets give distinct root endpoints, so $$\gamma_0|W|/s\le\gamma_0|V|\le|\partial V|\le B|\partial W|.$$ Consequently every bad lifted net graph has expansion at least $\gamma=\gamma_0/(sB)>0$. The same bound holds on their countable disjoint union, by summing over the finitely many components touched by a finite vertex set. This explicitly proves the graph comparison for both group types. [F5, F6, step 5.1, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Apply [F1]'s quantitative flat-net theorem with the union of all graph components which are not degree-one components over $Z_n$. All bounds in steps 5.1–8.1 are fixed for the chosen $d$, hence its constant $C_d<\infty$ is independent of $n$. Every point of $p_n^{-1}U_n\setminus\mathcal A_e(p_n)$ lies in one of those bad components: a degree-one component over $Z_n$ was included in $\mathcal A_e$. Therefore $$\int_{p_n^{-1}U_n\setminus\mathcal A_e(p_n)}|\phi_n|\le C_d I_n.$$ On the remaining part over $Z_n\setminus U_n$, the local defect estimate gives total mass at most $\int_{Z_n\setminus U_n}|\psi_n|+I_n$. Combining and passing to limsup yields $$\limsup_n\int_{p_n^{-1}Z_n\setminus\mathcal A_e(p_n)}|\phi_n|\le d.$$ Since $d>0$ was arbitrary, choose $d<b/(2M)$ to contradict step 3.1. This contradiction proves the asserted mass concentration. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 For a nonelementary proper geometric subgroup, the uniqueness of any full lift over a thick component is [F4]; full ambient covers need no uniqueness assertion beyond the one-sheet identity case. The proof retained the possible cyclic intersections of other geometric translates throughout. The thin part, all cusps, both free and closed limiting thick groups, arbitrary changing subsurface topology, and nonregular covers have therefore all been included in the exact intrinsic statement. The only separate issue mentioned in the statement is the interpretation by ambient isotopies into a chosen subsurface; it is not an assumption in, or a consequence silently borrowed by, this analytic concentration proof. [F4, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 7.1, given, algebra]

11.1 The sequential result just proved gives a UNIFORM modulus over the entire class in the Statement. Precisely, for every $a>0$ there is $t=t(A,\varepsilon_0,a)>0$ such that every permitted cover $p:Y\to X$ and every unit quadratic $\phi$ with $1-\|\Theta_p\phi\|_1<t$ have $\int_{Y\setminus\mathcal A_{\varepsilon_0}(p)}|\phi|<a$. Otherwise for each integer $k$ choose a violating cover and unit quadratic with defect less than $1/k$. They satisfy all hypotheses of the already proved sequential theorem with the same $A,\varepsilon_0$ but retain bad mass at least $a$, a contradiction. Countable choice in [A1] licenses this countersequence. The modulus is not asserted effective or obtained from a global moduli norm-continuity theorem. It follows from the precise quantifiers of the proved sequential concentration result. [A1, step 9.1, given, construct, algebra]

12.1 For the countable-spot data set $m_{n,j}=\int_{U_{n,j}}|q_n|$ and $d_{n,j}=m_{n,j}-\|\Theta_{p_{n,j}}(q_n|_{U_{n,j}})\|_1\ge0$. Disjointness and [F1] give $\sum_jm_{n,j}\le1$. The pushforwards sum normally on compact coordinate discs: their absolute integral sum is at most one, so the same holomorphic mean estimate used in [F1]'s construction bounds the sum of their suprema on smaller discs. Hence $T_nq_n$ is holomorphic and its norm is at most $\sum_j(m_{n,j}-d_{n,j})$. Put $J_n=1-\|T_nq_n\|_1\to0$. The last inequality yields BOTH $1-\sum_jm_{n,j}\le J_n$ and $\sum_jd_{n,j}\le J_n$; any cancellation only increases $J_n$. Discard zero-mass spots. For each fixed $a>0$ use step 11.1's threshold $t>0$. On spots with $d_{n,j}/m_{n,j}<t$, the normalized restriction $q_n/m_{n,j}$ has bad mass less than $a$, so their total bad mass is at most $a\sum_jm_{n,j}\le a$. On all other spots the total mass is at most $t^{-1}\sum_jd_{n,j}\le J_n/t$. The uncovered part of $W_n$ has mass exactly $1-\sum_jm_{n,j}\le J_n$. Thus the mass outside the union of all intrinsic amenable spot parts is at most $a+(1+t^{-1})J_n$. Taking limsup and then $a\downarrow0$ proves the countable-spot conclusion, with every sum justified by its nonnegative absolute mass bound. This is the claimed analytic spot-sum concentration, without asserting existence or classification of the supplied spots. [F1, step 11.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
