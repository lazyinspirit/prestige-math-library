## Statement

Assume AC. Fix $\varepsilon>0$, the hyperbolic upper half-plane $H$ of curvature $-1$, its basepoint $o=i$ and one positive unit frame there. Let $\Gamma_n\le\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ be discrete torsion-free groups satisfying
$$d_H(o,\gamma o)\ge2\varepsilon\qquad(\gamma\in\Gamma_n\setminus\{1\}).$$
Thus the pointed complete surfaces $X_n=H/\Gamma_n$ have base injectivity radius at least $\varepsilon$. No finite-generation or finite-area assumption is needed for the following compactness conclusion.

A subsequence has a discrete torsion-free limit group $\Gamma$ with the same displacement bound, with $X=H/\Gamma$ a connected pointed complete surface. The limit is in the following explicit closed-set sense: every $\gamma\in\Gamma$ is a limit of $\gamma_n\in\Gamma_n$, and every finite matrix limit of elements $\gamma_n\in\Gamma_n$ belongs to $\Gamma$. The surfaces admit actual convergent holomorphic atlases with overlap convergence and asymptotic separation exactly as in [[thm-convergent-holomorphic-atlases-support-projective-quadratic-compactness]]. The distinguished points and frames are those projected from $o$ and its fixed frame. On each fixed lifted coordinate chart, all pulled-back hyperbolic metrics are the same model metric.

For every compact $K\subset X$ there is an open neighborhood $N_K$ of $K$ and, for all sufficiently large $n$, smooth embeddings $F_n:N_K\to X_n$ converging to the identity in these local charts, with $F_n^*g_{X_n}\to g_X$ in every $C^k$ norm on compact subsets. These embeddings are not claimed holomorphic. If $\operatorname{area}(X_n)\le A<\infty$, then $\operatorname{area}(X)\le A$.

If also supplied subgroups $L_n\le\Gamma_n$, after a further subsequence they have a discrete torsion-free limit $L\le\Gamma$. The pointed covers $H/L_n\to H/\Gamma_n$ converge to the actual holomorphic cover $H/L\to H/\Gamma$, in compatible quotient charts in which their expressions are the identity. This conclusion includes arbitrary nonregular covers and arbitrary subgroups $L_n$; no bound on their generators is imposed. It makes no faithful convergence or mass-tightness assertion.

For a sequence of cofinite finite-type marked models of one genus $g$ and $N$ punctures with supplied complete standard cusp ends and compact complement, the uniform area bound may be taken as $A=2\pi(2g-2+N)$. Consequently their nonzero simple-pole quadratic differentials have, on this connected pointed limit, a nonzero holomorphic projective limit by the actual analytic theorem cited above. No integrability of that limit follows from projective normalization alone.

This is the fixed-curvature thick-point regime. Compactness after rescaling at a thin point to a Euclidean or cylindrical model, the topology of the limiting finite-area surface, and faithful quadratic mass localization remain distinct subsequent operations.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the normalized groups, their positive base displacement bound, and the extra subgroup, area or finite-type model data only for the corresponding conclusions.

[A1] Full AC is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. It supplies all subsequence, frame, chart and finite-cover selections.

[F1] A discrete torsion-free subgroup of the hyperbolic isometry group acts freely and properly with a complete proper quotient. Model point stabilizers are compact; isometries taking one point into a fixed compact set form a compact set modulo that compact frame fiber. Nonidentity elements are parabolic or loxodromic. These local assertions do not assume finite volume. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F2] The Hermitian model realizes the real group on its invariant hyperbolic plane; the induced metric is $y^{-2}(dx^2+dy^2)$ and its real hyperboloid is the future unit sheet for a form of signature $(2,1)$. Group maps act linearly on it, and $-\langle u,v\rangle=\cosh d(u,v)\ge1$. Matrix multiplication and inversion are continuous. [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]]

[F3] An equicontinuous pointwise relatively compact family on a nonempty compact metric space has compact uniform closure under AC. [[cor-ascoli-arzela-for-compact-metric-domains]]

[F4] Supplied connected convergent holomorphic atlases, with overlap and asymptotic-separation conditions, support bounded-degree meromorphic normalization and projective convergence of nonzero simple-pole quadratic differentials of fixed genus and puncture count. [[thm-convergent-holomorphic-atlases-support-projective-quadratic-compactness]]

[F5] Holomorphic chart changes and local isometries obey measurable change of variables with their smooth Jacobians. [[thm-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-lebesgue-measurable-functions]]

[F6] Standard cofinite-model cusps are actual disjoint tails $\{y>T\}/\langle z\mapsto z+\ell\rangle$ with area $\ell/T$, smooth boundary horocycles, and compact complement. The metric connection in its orthonormal frame has $\nabla_{e_1}e_1=e_2$, $e_1=y\partial_x,e_2=y\partial_y$. [[thm-marked-quasiconformal-surfaces-have-cofinite-fuchsian-models]] Smooth-boundary Gauss–Bonnet is $\int K+\int_{\partial}k_g=2\pi\chi$. A compact genus-$g$ surface has Euler characteristic $2-2g$. [[cor-gauss-bonnet-for-compact-oriented-surfaces-with-smooth-boundary]], [[def-genus-and-euler-characteristic-compact-riemann-surface]]

## Proof

1.1 We prove the required closed-set compactness instead of citing a subgroup compactness theorem. Give $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ an explicit closed proper embedding into a Euclidean space: for a determinant-one real matrix $M$, regard its four entries as a vector $v$ and send its sign class to the symmetric matrix $vv^t$. This is injective modulo sign. Bounded image implies bounded $v$, so convergent images have convergent determinant-one lifts along a subsequence and lie in the same image; the image is closed and its topology is the quotient matrix topology. One-point compactification, using inverse stereographic projection of that closed Euclidean image, gives a compact metrizable space $T=\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)\cup\{\infty\}$. Each discrete subgroup is closed: if distinct group elements converged in the matrix group, pairwise quotients would give nonidentity elements tending to identity, contradicting discreteness. Thus $C_n=\Gamma_n\cup\{\infty\}$ is compact in $T$. The distance functions $a_n(t)=d_T(t,C_n)$ are uniformly bounded and one-Lipschitz. By [F3], a subsequence converges uniformly to a continuous function $a$. Let $C=\{a=0\}$. It is nonempty since it contains identity and infinity. In fact $a(t)=d_T(t,C)$. The inequality $a(t)\le d_T(t,C)$ follows from one-Lipschitz continuity and vanishing on $C$. For the reverse, take nearest points in the compact $C_n$ to fixed $t$, extract a convergent subsequence, and use uniform convergence to see their limit lies in $C$ with distance $a(t)$. Consequently $C_n\to C$ in Hausdorff distance: each side's distance to the other is bounded by $\|a_n-a\|_\infty$. [A1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put $\Gamma=C\setminus\{\infty\}$. Every $\gamma\in\Gamma$ is approximated by $\gamma_n\in\Gamma_n$, since the Hausdorff approximants to a finite point cannot eventually be the distinct infinity point. Conversely any finite matrix limit of $\gamma_n\in\Gamma_n$ lies in $\Gamma$, again by Hausdorff convergence. For $\gamma,\eta\in\Gamma$ choose simultaneous approximants. Continuity gives $\gamma_n\eta_n^{-1}\to\gamma\eta^{-1}\in\Gamma$, so $\Gamma$ is a subgroup. It is closed in the matrix group. If $\gamma\ne1$, its approximants are nonidentity for late $n$, hence $d(o,\gamma o)\ge2\varepsilon$. Thus identity has a neighborhood meeting $\Gamma$ only at identity, and $\Gamma$ is discrete. It is torsion-free: a nonidentity finite-order real Mobius transformation is elliptic, while every nonidentity element of $\Gamma_n$ has squared trace at least four. Locally choose matrix signs near a nonidentity limit; this trace condition passes to it and excludes ellipticity. Hence no nontrivial finite-order limit element exists. By [F1], its quotient is a free proper surface. More explicitly the invariant plane quotient is a closed isometric subspace of the complete proper three-dimensional quotient: its inverse image is the closed invariant plane and its distances are the same orbit infima of model distances. It is therefore complete and proper as well. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 A uniform isolation observation will be used repeatedly. If $g_n\in\Gamma_n$ tends to identity, then $g_n=1$ for all sufficiently large $n$, by the base displacement bound. If two sequences in $\Gamma_n$ tend to the same finite matrix, their pairwise quotients tend to identity, so they are eventually equal. Consequently approximants of a fixed finite matrix are locally unique. Any finite collection of exact relations among finitely many elements of $\Gamma$ holds among their chosen approximants for all sufficiently large $n$: evaluate each relation word; its value lies in $\Gamma_n$ and tends to identity, so it equals identity eventually. This supplies finite cocycle relations, without a finite presentation or finite-generation assumption on $\Gamma$. [F2, step 2.1, given, algebra]

3.2 Choose countably many small Euclidean coordinate discs $U_a\Subset H$ whose projections $\pi:H\to X$ are injective on their closures and a slightly larger neighborhood, and whose projected interiors cover $X$. Such discs exist by properness and freeness, and a rational-coordinate subcollection covers by second countability. Their projections are the desired charts $\phi_a=\pi|_{U_a}$. For each fixed compact $K\subset H$, every sequence of group elements with $g_nK\cap K\ne\varnothing$ has a finite matrix subsequence: taking the intersection points in $K$ and using compact frame fibers in [F1] bounds the matrices in a compact set. If $\pi_n|_{\overline U_a}$ failed to be injective infinitely often, choose nonidentity $g_n\in\Gamma_n$ taking a point of that compact set to another. A finite subsequence tends to $g\in\Gamma$ by step 2.1. It cannot be identity by step 3.1. But it takes one point of $\overline U_a$ to another, contradicting the source chart's injectivity there. Thus $\phi_{a,n}=\pi_n|_{U_a}$ is eventually an actual holomorphic embedding. The coordinate at $o$ can be included explicitly, giving the projected distinguished point and frame. All its pulled-back metrics equal the same upper-half-plane metric, since quotient maps are local isometries. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The atlas has the precise convergence properties in [F4]. On a component of a source overlap, its transition is the restriction of the unique $g\in\Gamma$ carrying the chosen lift in $U_a$ to its lift in $U_b$. On any compact portion of an overlap only finitely many such matrices occur, by the compact-matrix argument of step 3.2. Approximate each by $g_n\in\Gamma_n$. The maps $g_n$ converge holomorphically, with all derivatives, to $g$. On each compact part their images lie in $U_b$ for large $n$, so they are exactly the actual quotient transition $\phi_{b,n}^{-1}\phi_{a,n}$; uniqueness follows from injectivity of the target chart. This proves local uniform overlap convergence with its eventual domains. For asymptotic separation take finitely many closed coordinate subdiscs with disjoint projected images in $X$. If two corresponding images met in $X_n$ infinitely often, a sequence $g_n$ would carry a point of one compact lifted disc to the other. The compact-matrix argument gives a subsequence with limit $g\in\Gamma$, making their limit images meet in $X$, a contradiction. Hence they are eventually pairwise disjoint. This proves every supplied-atlas condition of [F4], rather than substituting only matrix convergence for surface convergence. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 We construct actual smooth compact-region embeddings to record the stronger geometric content needed for later transport. Cover a fixed compact $K\subset X$ and a neighborhood by finitely many of the preceding charts with relatively compact supports. In their real coordinates choose smooth nonnegative bump functions with positive sum near $K$, and divide by that sum to get weights $\chi_a$ summing to one there. Such bumps can be built directly from $e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$, zero for $t\le0$, in smaller coordinate discs. Write $\sigma_a$ for the chosen local lift of the source chart into $H$. On an overlap $\sigma_a=g_{ab}\sigma_b$, with $g_{ab}\in\Gamma$ locally constant. Only finitely many values occur on these compact supports, by properness. Choose approximants $g_{ab,n}$, including inverses and all finite triple-overlap relations. Step 3.1 makes $g_{ac,n}=g_{ab,n}g_{bc,n}$ exactly whenever the corresponding source lifts overlap, for all sufficiently large $n$. In the real future hyperboloid from [F2], define on a base chart $b$ $$u_{b,n}(x)=\frac{\sum_a\chi_a(x)g_{ab,n}^{-1}\sigma_a(x)}{\sqrt{-\left\langle\sum_a\chi_a(x)g_{ab,n}^{-1}\sigma_a(x),\sum_a\chi_a(x)g_{ab,n}^{-1}\sigma_a(x)\right\rangle}}.$$ Sum only the indices whose weights are nonzero there. The positive combination is future timelike, since the Lorentz product of two future unit vectors is at most minus one, so the denominator is positive. The exact cocycles give $u_{c,n}=g_{bc,n}^{-1}u_{b,n}$. Therefore $F_n(x)=\pi_nu_{b,n}(x)$ is independent of base chart and is smooth. As $n\to\infty$, every $g_{ab,n}^{-1}\sigma_a$ tends smoothly to $\sigma_b$ on compact pieces. The normalized sum consequently tends to $\sigma_b$ in every derivative. Thus $F_n$ is locally a diffeomorphism for large $n$, with pullback metric tending to the source metric in every $C^k$ norm. No conformality of this smooth blending is asserted. [A1, F1, F2, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 This local map is injective on a compact neighborhood of $K$ for late $n$. Otherwise take a subsequence of distinct pairs $x_n,y_n$ in such a compact neighborhood with $F_n(x_n)=F_n(y_n)$ and pass to source limits $x,y$. If $x\ne y$, take disjoint closed coordinate neighborhoods about them. Step 5.1's local convergence places their images inside the corresponding images from step 4.1 for late $n$, contradicting asymptotic separation. If $x=y$, use one smaller convex coordinate disc about that point. In its coordinate expression $F_n$ tends in $C^1$ to the identity and its derivative differs from identity by less than $1/2$ on that disc. The line-segment integral then gives $|F_n(x')-F_n(y')|\ge|x'-y'|/2$ for source points in the smaller disc, contradicting equality for $x_n,y_n$. Here the target expression is taken through the corresponding injective quotient chart, so the inequality is an ordinary coordinate inequality. Restrict to the interior of that compact neighborhood to obtain $N_K$ and the asserted embeddings. Local convergence and its metric conclusion remain those of step 5.1. [F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Suppose $\operatorname{area}(X_n)\le A$. We prove the area bound without a hidden measure-tightness assertion. Take the closed Dirichlet domain $D(o)$ for $\Gamma$ in $H$. Nearest points of the proper orbit exist, so its translates cover $H$. Its interior $D^\circ$ is precisely invariant under identity, and its boundary is contained in the countable union of geodesic bisectors comparing $o$ with nonidentity orbit points. Such curves have area zero in the smooth plane metric. Quotient charts preserve those null sets, so $\operatorname{area}(X)=\operatorname{area}(D^\circ)$. For every compact $L\subset D^\circ$, the map $\pi_n$ is injective on $L$ for late $n$: otherwise a nonidentity $g_n$ meeting that compact set has a matrix limit in $\Gamma$ by step 3.2; the limit cannot be identity by step 3.1, and would contradict Dirichlet-interior disjointness. Local isometry and [F5] now give $\operatorname{area}(L)=\operatorname{area}(\pi_nL)\le A$. Exhaust $D^\circ$ by increasing compact sets, for example bounded metric balls with distance at least $1/k$ from its complement, and use monotone convergence of their nonnegative area integrals. Thus $\operatorname{area}(X)\le A$. This bounds total area, not the tails of any independently normalized quadratic. [A1, F1, F2, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

8.1 For supplied $L_n\le\Gamma_n$, apply step 1.1 again to $L_n\cup\{\infty\}$ along the chosen subsequence. The resulting finite part $L$ is a subgroup by step 2.1 and lies in $\Gamma$, since all its approximants lie in $\Gamma_n$. It inherits discreteness, torsion-freeness and the same base displacement bound. Construct its quotient charts by steps 3.2 and 4.1. Around any lift in $H$, choose a disc injective for the larger limit group $\Gamma$; for large $n$ it is injective for $\Gamma_n$ and hence for $L_n$ as well. Its quotient charts in source and target make $H/L_n\to H/\Gamma_n$ the identity coordinate map, as they do for $H/L\to H/\Gamma$. Such discs cover the source and target quotients. This proves compatible local holomorphic convergence of actual pointed covering maps. All groups and covers here may be infinitely generated or nonregular; no extra convergence of the omitted sheets' mass is inferred. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Finally consider the fixed finite-type cofinite models with supplied standard cusps. Cut each at deep disjoint horocycles, leaving a compact smooth region $M_n$ homeomorphic to a genus-$g$ compact surface with $N$ open discs removed. Its Euler characteristic is $2-2g-N$: filling the $N$ boundary circles adds $N$ two-cells to a finite cell structure, and [F6] gives $2-2g$ for the filled surface. On a boundary $y=T$ the outward-normal-first orientation of $y\le T$ has unit tangent $-e_1$ and inward normal $-e_2$. Since $\nabla_{-e_1}(-e_1)=e_2$ by [F6], its signed geodesic curvature is $-1$. Its length and the removed cusp area are both $\ell/T$. Gauss–Bonnet [F6] therefore gives $$-\operatorname{area}(M_n)-\sum\ell/T=2\pi(2-2g-N).$$ Adding the cusp areas yields exactly $\operatorname{area}(X_n)=2\pi(2g-2+N)$. When $N=0$ the same calculation is the closed-surface formula. Step 7.1 supplies the uniform area conclusion, and steps 3.2 and 4.1 supply exactly the connected atlas required by [F4]. Applying that full analytic theorem to any nonzero simple-pole quadratics gives the stated nonzero holomorphic projective limit on $X$. Its scaling need not preserve integrability or mass, and the theorem's optional pole statement needs a limiting finite-type compactification separately. This completes all the asserted thick-pointed compactness, cover and analytic consequences. [F4, F6, step 4.1, step 7.1, given, algebra] ∎
