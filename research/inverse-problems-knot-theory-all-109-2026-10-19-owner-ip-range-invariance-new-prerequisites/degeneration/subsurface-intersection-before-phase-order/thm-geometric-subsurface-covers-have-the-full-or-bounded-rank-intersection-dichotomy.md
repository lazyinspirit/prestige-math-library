## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $X=H^2/\Gamma$ be a finite-area torsion-free Fuchsian surface. Let $S,T\subset X$ be compact connected smoothly bounded surface regions, with every boundary circle essential in $X$ and with injective fundamental-group inclusions. Empty boundary is allowed. Suppose their image subgroups $H,K\le\Gamma$ are nonelementary. Fix lifts to identify those subgroups and put $\chi_T=\chi(T)<0$.

Their convex cores $C_H=C(\Lambda_H)$ and $C_K=C(\Lambda_K)$ project as CONVEX CUT-SURFACES to $X$: their interiors modulo $H,K$ are embedded, there are finitely many geodesic rim and standard cusp classes, and the marked cores have the topology of $S,T$ with their parabolic rims opened to standard cusp ends. Distinct closed rims MAY project to the same ambient geodesic; shared rim lines and annular complementary regions are permitted. Distinct translates $gC_H$, indexed by $gH$, have disjoint interiors, and likewise for $C_K$.

For every $g\in\Gamma$ set $L_g=K\cap gHg^{-1}$. Then either $L_g=K$, or $[K:L_g]=\infty$ and $L_g$ is free of rank at most $1-\chi_T$. There is at most one coset $gH$ for which $L_g=K$. If such a coset exists, all other $L_{g'}$ are trivial or cyclic. Thus the components of the covering $H^2/H\to X$ pulled back over the marked $K$-subsurface have either no full component, in which case all stabilizers have uniformly bounded rank and infinite index, or one full component and only trivial/cyclic stabilizers on the rest. This is a precise group-theoretic full-lift statement; no assertion that those latter covers are always universal is made.

For an actual small-threshold thick component $T$, the preceding topology/count theorem gives $1-\chi_T\le1+\operatorname{area}(X)/(2\pi)$. This is the uniform generator input needed for common graph expansion, for both punctured free-group and closed-surface bases. Expansion itself and the final amenable-mass theorem are not part of this statement.

## Proof

1.1 We begin with the topological arrangement behind the cut-surface geometry. The full inverse image of $\partial S$ in $H^2$ is a locally finite family of pairwise disjoint proper smooth lines. Essentiality makes each boundary monodromy nontrivial. Its compact period arc and translates by that element form a proper line, since nontrivial parabolic or loxodromic powers leave compact sets and compact model sets meet only finitely many translated period arcs, by [F1]. Local finiteness of the whole family follows by the same finite-translate argument on the finitely many boundary period arcs. A proper embedded line separates the plane into two regions: add its point at infinity in the one-point compactification, obtaining a Jordan circle on the sphere, and apply [F3], using a point off the circle as the plane's infinity. The arrangement's complementary-region dual graph is a tree. It is connected because a compact path can be chosen to cross finitely many of the locally finite smooth lines, transversely in finitely many small straightening charts. Each line borders exactly one region on each side, since a narrow variable-width strip along it avoids all other lines and is connected on each side. Its dual edge is a bridge, because no path of regions on opposite sides can avoid crossing that separating line. A connected graph all of whose edges are bridges is a tree. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $D$ be the closure of the lifted region belonging to $S$. Its stabilizer is precisely $H$: a path in $D$ from a lifted basepoint to a translate projects to an $S$-loop representing that translate; conversely all elements represented by $S$-loops preserve the component. Its quotient is compact $S$, so every point of $D$ lies within one fixed distance of an $H$-orbit, by finitely many compact covering-chart lifts of $S$. Consequently its ideal boundary is exactly $\Lambda_H$. For $g\notin H$, the vertices $\operatorname{int}D$ and $\operatorname{int}gD$ of the arrangement tree are distinct. The first edge on the unique joining path is a line $\ell\subset\partial D$ that separates these two regions. If $\ell$ has loxodromic period, its ideal endpoints are distinct and its two sides have the two corresponding closed ideal arcs; Jordan separation gives this after adjoining either ideal-circle arc to the lifted line. Thus $\Lambda_H$ and $g\Lambda_H$ lie on opposite sides of the geodesic with those endpoints. If the period is parabolic, normalize its fixed point to infinity. The lifted line is periodic horizontally and has heights bounded above and bounded away from zero. Its upper side has ideal boundary only infinity; its other side has the remaining ideal circle. Since both $H$ and $gHg^{-1}$ are nonelementary, neither $D$ nor $gD$ can have its ideal boundary in that singleton. A parabolic line therefore cannot be the separating line. We have proved that distinct translates of $C_H$ lie in opposite closed geodesic half-planes, and hence have disjoint interiors. The same argument applies to $T$ and $C_K$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 These subgroups are finitely generated: triangulate compact $S,T$ by [F3], use cellular approximation to move every based loop to the finite one-skeleton, and choose its finitely many non-tree edge loops. If either subgroup has finite index in $\Gamma$, it has the same full-circle limit set. Indeed finitely many coset representatives put every $\Gamma$-orbit point within a fixed distance of its subgroup orbit, and conversely. If it were a proper finite-index subgroup, step 2.1's separator would force that full circle to lie in a proper closed ideal arc, an impossibility. Hence a finite-index one must be all of $\Gamma$. If the subgroup is proper, its covering is noncompact: a compact covering has a compact discrete fiber and therefore finite degree. If it is all of $\Gamma$ and $X$ is noncompact, the covering is again noncompact. The only remaining compact case is a whole compact $X$, whose convex hull is $H^2$ by [F1]. It has no parabolics, since its positive continuous injectivity radius has a positive minimum, whereas parabolic displacements tend to zero along a vertical ray. In that case a compact region carrying the whole group cannot have an essential loxodromic boundary: its lifted region has full-circle ideal boundary and cannot lie on the inner side of that boundary line. Thus the closed case is simply the whole compact surface itself. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 In each noncompact covering, [F4] lifts $S$ or $T$ to a compact embedded essential core: the lift is injective because its projection is the original embedding, and its fundamental-group image is the entire defining subgroup. Apply [F2] to this supplied core. This constructs $C_H/H$ and $C_K/K$ with their original marked topology, finitely many geodesic rims and standard cusps. Step 2.1 makes their interior projections into $X$ injective: an identification would give overlapping interiors of two distinct translates, which is impossible. Their boundary projections are the geodesic or peripheral classes of the original essential embedded boundary circles. Such a circle is primitive in the FULL ambient cyclic stabilizer, not only in its subgroup: lift it to the annular cover for that full stabilizer, identify that annulus with $\mathbb C^*$, and apply the Jordan-disc argument of the finite-generator theorem's first two steps. Its class has winding $\pm1$, since a small circle about zero lies in its punctured Jordan disc. Thus each projected geodesic rim is a simple closed ambient geodesic and each cusp tail maps with degree one to its labeled ambient cusp. Several rims can have the same ambient geodesic image when the original curves are parallel. Within either rim family geodesics are disjoint or coincide, by nonalternation of endpoints of the disjoint original boundary lifts. This proves exactly the asserted convex CUT-surface interpretation, retaining all paired rims. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Fix $g\in\Gamma$ and put $E=C_K\cap gC_H$, $L=K\cap gHg^{-1}$. If $L$ is nontrivial, a loxodromic element in it has its full axis in both convex hulls. A parabolic element in it forces both hulls to contain a common deep full horodisc: in normalized coordinates take one finite limit point and all its primitive translates; the ideal triangles with infinity and two consecutive translates contain $y>\ell/2$. This is the direct computation in [F2]. Thus an empty $E$ implies $L$ trivial. If $E$ is nonempty but has empty interior, it is contained in a geodesic line, since it is convex. Any group preserving such a set is trivial or a subgroup of a cyclic axis stabilizer; if the set is a point, an interior-point stabilizer in a torsion-free discrete group is trivial. Hence in this case $L$ is trivial or cyclic. These possibilities include shared rim lines and cannot be replaced by a claim of universal covers only. [F1, F2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Suppose now $E$ has nonempty interior. Projection gives an injective local isometry $$\operatorname{int}E/L\longrightarrow\operatorname{int}C_K/K.$$ Indeed if $k\in K$ identifies two points of $\operatorname{int}E$, then those points lie in $g\operatorname{int}C_H$ and in $kg\operatorname{int}C_H$. Step 2.1 forces $kgH=gH$, so $k\in gHg^{-1}$ and hence $k\in L$. Its image is a connected component of the intersection of the two embedded open cut-surface interiors in $X$: a path in that intersection lifts in $\operatorname{int}C_K$, and its chosen $H$-translate cannot change without crossing a rim, so it stays in $\operatorname{int}E$. The convex interior is simply connected, so this component $W$ has fundamental group $L$ and its inclusion into the $K$-surface induces the actual subgroup inclusion. This argument asserts interior injectivity; the whole boundary can identify along shared seams and is NOT presumed embedded. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The component $W$ is finite type, by actual finite boundary geometry. Take common deep ambient cusp truncations avoiding the finitely many projected closed geodesic rims from step 4.1. Each present cusp is an entire degree-one tail on both sides of any such truncation. In the compact ambient remainder there are finitely many simple geodesic rim curves, allowing coincident supports. Two different such geodesics have finitely many transverse intersections: compact period arcs in the model have only finitely many relevant deck translates, and two distinct model geodesics meet at most once. Combine coincident supports but retain their side labels. Cutting along this finite graph gives finitely many compact surface pieces with ordinary geodesic corners. At a boundary vertex split its incident sectors into separate interval links; at an interior vertex keep the full circle link. This is the usual direct finite cutting construction in the local disc, and yields finite closed pieces with genuine boundary, even when their maps to $X$ have a boundary pinch. Apply the actual cornered relative triangulation in [F3] to these pieces. The components of the selected open intersection are interiors of these finite cut pieces, with a finite selection of removed cusp discs restored as punctured tails. Thus a compact connected surface with boundary and finitely many corners models a core of $W$. Round its finitely many corners inside their wedges and shave a small boundary collar to get a compact SMOOTH core $A\subset W$ with $\pi_1(A)\cong\pi_1(W)=L$. Because step 6.1 embeds the interior, this is a closed embedded compact surface in the interior of the $K$-surface. This constructs finite generation instead of assuming it for subgroup intersections. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Choose a sufficiently deep smooth cusp truncation $M_K$ of $C_K/K$ containing that $A$ in its interior. It has $\chi(M_K)=\chi_T$ by [F2], or is the whole compact $K$-surface in the closed case. Its complement to $\operatorname{int}A$ has finitely many compact smooth pieces, each with a nonempty boundary and meeting a circle of $\partial A$; the finite boundary-circle count and connectedness prove finiteness. None is a disc. If one were a disc $B$, its boundary circle would be nullhomotopic in the $K$-surface. Injectivity $\pi_1(A)\to K$ would make it nullhomotopic in $A$. Lift that nullhomotopy to the simply connected convex universal $K$-domain in $H^2$. The lifted disc $B$ is disjoint from the lifted $A$ on its interior. Its boundary is a Jordan circle, which cannot contract while avoiding an interior point of that disc: by [F3] the punctured disc is a cylinder with boundary generator, and its inclusion into the punctured plane is surjective on that generator because it contains a small circle about the point. This contradicts the purported lifted nullhomotopy in $A$. Hence all complementary pieces are nondiscs. Each has Euler characteristic at most zero: cap its $b\ge1$ boundaries and use $\chi=2-2a-b$ from [F3]; the only positive possibility is $a=0,b=1$, a disc by Jordan–Schoenflies. [F1, F2, F3, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Boundary-compatible finite cellulations of $A$ and those complementary pieces, refining their common finite boundary marks, give $$\chi_T=\chi(M_K)=\chi(A)+\sum_j\chi(B_j)\le\chi(A).$$ The cell justification is the finite fan-refinement and capping argument in [F4]; glued boundary circles have equal numbers of vertices and edges and hence zero Euler characteristic. If $A$ is closed, its embedding is open and closed in the connected $K$-surface, so it is that whole surface; step 6.1 then gives $L=K$. Otherwise triangulate $A$ relative to its boundary, choose a face-dual spanning tree rooted at the outside boundary, and remove faces with parents before children. Each triangle collapses across its free parent edge, leaving a connected graph spine, exactly as in the explicitly proved compact-core construction. A graph spanning tree and its remaining edge loops then give $\pi_1(A)$ free of rank $1-\chi(A)$. Therefore whenever $L\ne K$ and $E$ has interior, $$\operatorname{rank}L=1-\chi(A)\le1-\chi_T.$$ The rank bound from step 5.1 covers the empty-interior cases too, since $\chi_T<0$. The negativity itself follows from the same boundary graph spine for $T$ when it has boundary and its nonelementary image, or from positive hyperbolic area and closed Gauss–Bonnet when $T=X$ is closed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 5.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 A proper $L$ cannot have finite index in $K$. If $[K:L]=d<\infty$, finite coset representatives show $\Lambda_L=\Lambda_K$: the two corresponding orbit sets stay within fixed distance of each other, and bounded-distance sequences have the same ideal limits in the hyperbolic model. Since $L\le gHg^{-1}$, it follows that $\Lambda_K\subset g\Lambda_H$ and thus $C_K\subset gC_H$. Hence $E=C_K$, and the map in step 6.1 is the actual finite-degree cover $\operatorname{int}C_K/L\to\operatorname{int}C_K/K$ of degree $d$. Its proven injectivity forces $d=1$, so $L=K$. This establishes the full-versus-infinite-index dichotomy without a multiplicativity assumption on an unconstructed intersection core. [F1, F2, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

11.1 If $K\subset gHg^{-1}$ for one coset $gH$, then $C_K\subset gC_H$. The full-dimensionality of $C_K$ follows from nonelementarity. Any other coset $g'H\ne gH$ has $g'\operatorname{int}C_H$ disjoint from $g\operatorname{int}C_H$ by step 2.1. Thus $C_K\cap g'C_H$ has empty interior. Step 5.1 makes its stabilizer $L_{g'}$ trivial or cyclic. In particular it cannot be all of the nonelementary $K$, so the full coset is unique. If no full coset exists, steps 9.1 and 10.1 say that every stabilizer is infinite-index and has the displayed uniform free rank bound. This is exactly the stated two-case intersection description. [F1, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, construct, algebra]

12.1 Finally the covering pullback interpretation is literal subgroup monodromy. Its fiber can be indexed by cosets $gH$, with the left $K$ action; the stabilizer is $K\cap gHg^{-1}$ and components are its $K$-orbits, equivalently the associated double cosets. A full stabilizer gives a singleton orbit and an isomorphic lift; all others have the subgroup covers just described. Changing to right-coset/right-monodromy conventions inverts the labels and gives the same component statement. For a small-threshold thick component $T$, essentiality of its horocycle/hypercycle boundaries comes from their full nontrivial cyclic stabilizers [F1]; the proper-line Jordan argument of the compact-core theorem proves its injectivity. The topology/count theorem [F4] gives $-\chi_T\le\operatorname{area}(X)/(2\pi)$. Thus the common rank bound is at most $1+\operatorname{area}(X)/(2\pi)$. All claimed geometric and subgroup interfaces are proved; no uniform graph expansion or final amenable-mass conclusion has been inferred merely from them. [F1, F4, step 9.1, step 10.1, step 11.1, given, algebra] ∎
