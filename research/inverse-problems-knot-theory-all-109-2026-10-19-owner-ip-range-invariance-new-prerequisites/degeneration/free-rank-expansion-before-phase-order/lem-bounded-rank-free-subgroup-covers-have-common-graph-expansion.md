## Statement

Assume AC. Let $F_r=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_r\rangle$ be free on its displayed basis, $r\ge2$, and let $R\ge0$ be an integer. For EVERY infinite-index subgroup $H\le F_r$ generated by at most $R$ elements, its labeled right Schreier graph on left cosets $Hg$, with one positive edge $Hg\to Hga_i$ for every $i$, has outer vertex expansion at least
$$\gamma_{r,R}=\min\left\{\frac{r-1}{2r},\frac1{T}\right\}>0,\qquad T=\max\left\{1,\left\lceil\frac{2R}{r-1}\right\rceil\right\}.$$
Expansion means that every nonempty finite vertex set $V$ satisfies $|bV|\ge\gamma_{r,R}|V|$, where $bV$ is the set of vertices OUTSIDE $V$ adjacent to it. Multiple edges and loops are retained for edge counts, with a loop counted twice in incidence degree; expansion itself counts distinct outside vertices. No free factor, cyclic, geometric, normal or regular-cover assumption is made about $H$.

The bound passes explicitly to fixed finite labeled net graphs. Let $Q$ be a finite connected graph with $s$ vertices, with loops and multiple edges allowed, and directed edge labels $c_e\in F_r$, $c_{\bar e}=c_e^{-1}$. Choose a root and a spanning tree, and suppose tree-edge labels have been gauged to identity and based loop labels generate $F_r$. Let $D$ bound the lengths of root-to-vertex tree paths, let $L$ bound lengths of based edge loops in $Q$ labeled by every $a_i$ and $a_i^{-1}$, and let $\Delta\ge1$ bound the incidence degree of $Q$. On vertices $(v,Hg)$ define the labeled lift $\mathcal G_H$ by edges $(v,Hg)\to(w,Hg c_e)$ for each directed edge $e:v\to w$. This graph is connected and has outer vertex expansion at least
$$\frac{\gamma_{r,R}}{s B_{\Delta,D+L}},\qquad B_{\Delta,N}=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\Delta^j.$$
All constants depend only on the displayed finite base data and $r,R$, uniformly over all such $H$.

This supplies the genuine common expansion operation for bounded-generator subgroups of a free group. It does not assert that every subgroup arising from intersecting geometric surface subsurfaces has the required generator bound, or settle the analogous CLOSED surface-group case. Those are distinct remaining topological inputs to the general degeneration theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the free basis, bounded subgroup generator number, infinite index and the extra finite labeled base graph for the second conclusion.

[A1] Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. Only countable spanning-tree selection is needed below; it can also be specified by breadth-first search and the finite alphabet order.

[F1] The free group has its unique freely reduced words and universal property. A word evaluates to identity exactly when successive adjacent inverse cancellations reduce it to the empty word. [[def-free-group]], [[thm-reduced-words-form-the-free-group]]

[F2] All graph conventions are specified here, including infinite graphs and loops: each edge has two formal opposite orientations; a finite-support rational edge chain reverses sign when orientation is reversed, and its boundary is terminal vertex minus initial vertex. Its cycle space is the kernel of this boundary on finite-support chains. A spanning tree has a unique finite simple path between two vertices. No topological graph- or surface-classification theorem is assumed for the elementary cycle calculations below.

## Proof

1.1 Write $\mathcal S$ for the Schreier graph in the Statement. It is connected because any coset is reached by reading a finite basis word, and is infinite because the index is infinite. Its incidence degree is exactly $2r$. A path starting at the root coset $H$ ends at the coset given by its label word; thus it is closed precisely when that word represents an element of $H$. Form the group of finite root-based edge loops modulo insertion and deletion of an edge immediately followed by its reverse. Reading labels identifies this loop group with $H$: it is surjective by the preceding path construction, and if the label word is identity in $F_r$, the cancellations in [F1] cancel actual reverse edges because the outgoing edge with any given signed label is unique at each vertex. Hence its kernel is trivial. This is a direct combinatorial proof of the subgroup identification, without importing a covering-space presentation theorem. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose a spanning tree $\mathcal T$ of $\mathcal S$, for example by shortest word distance from the root and the first incoming edge in a fixed finite signed-label order. For each unoriented edge outside $\mathcal T$ choose one orientation $e$ and form the loop $\lambda_e$ that follows the tree path to its origin, traverses $e$ and returns by the tree path from its terminus. These loops freely generate the edge-loop group. Indeed any loop factors into their product by inserting the tree path and its reverse at every intermediate vertex. Reading only nontree edge names, with tree edges sent to the empty word, defines an inverse homomorphism from the loop group to the free group on those names: a loop followed by its reverse cancels, and the loop $\lambda_e$ reads exactly its one edge name. Conversely the factorization proves that these maps are mutual inverses. Since $H$ is generated by at most $R$ elements, there can be at most $R$ such edge names. To see this without an unproved rank comparison for subgroups, send their free group to the rational vector space with one coordinate per edge name by exponent sums; it is onto the direct-sum vector space. The images of $R$ generators must span it, so its dimension is at most $R$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The rational cycle space of $\mathcal S$ has dimension equal to the number of its nontree edges, hence at most $R$. Explicitly the edge chains of the $\lambda_e$ are independent, because each has coefficient one on its own nontree edge and zero on the other nontree edges. They span: subtract those chains with the coefficients of any finite-support cycle on its nontree edges; the remainder is a finite-support cycle in a tree and therefore zero. The latter assertion follows by taking a terminal edge in the finite subtree spanned by its support, whose endpoint boundary coefficient forces its coefficient zero, and repeating finitely many times. This proves the needed cycle-space bound. In particular it does not rely on the false assertion that the rank of an arbitrary subgroup of a rank-$R$ free group is at most $R$. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $V$ be nonempty finite, let $v=|V|$, and let its full induced finite subgraph have $e$ unoriented edges and $c\ge1$ connected components, isolated vertices included. A spanning forest in that finite graph has $v-c$ edges. Each remaining edge gives an independent cycle, and these span by the same finite-tree elimination as in step 3.1. Its cycle dimension is therefore $e-v+c$. This cycle space injects into the whole graph's cycle space simply by extending its edge coefficients by zero; there are no two-dimensional cells or additional boundaries. Thus $e-v+c\le R$. Counting all incidences at $V$, the number $E_{\rm out}$ of crossing edges is $$E_{\rm out}=2rv-2e\ge2(r-1)v+2c-2R.$$ Every outside vertex receives at most $2r$ such incidences, so $|bV|\ge E_{\rm out}/(2r)$. Loops and parallel edges satisfy these same formulas with the stated incidence convention. [F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 If $v\ge T$, then $v\ge2R/(r-1)$ and step 4.1 gives $|bV|\ge(r-1)v/(2r)$. If $v<T$, connectedness and infinitude of $\mathcal S$ imply $bV\ne\varnothing$: take a path from a vertex of $V$ to any vertex outside $V$ and its first exit. Hence $|bV|\ge1\ge v/T$. Both cases give $|bV|\ge\gamma_{r,R}v$. The argument covers $R=0$ with $T=1$, the trivial subgroup, all nonnormal subgroups, and every finite nonempty $V$. Finite-index subgroups were correctly excluded: their entire finite vertex set would have empty outer boundary. [step 1.1, step 4.1, given, algebra]

6.1 Consider the finite labeled graph $Q$. Its gauged tree edges keep cosets fixed, so every $(v,Hg)$ connects to $(v_0,Hg)$ by a path of length at most $D$. The supplied based loops labeled by the signed free generators realize all Schreier edges between root-fiber vertices by paths of length at most $L$. Since the Schreier graph is connected, this proves $\mathcal G_H$ connected as well. Its incidence degree is at most $\Delta$. A radius-$N$ ball contains at most $B_{\Delta,N}$ vertices, since each reached vertex is an endpoint of some length-$j$ edge path and there are at most $\Delta^j$ such paths for each $j$. This upper estimate is valid for loops, multiple edges and $\Delta=1$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 For a nonempty finite vertex set $W$ of $\mathcal G_H$, let $V$ consist of all cosets $Hg$ appearing in some $(v,Hg)\in W$. Then $|V|\ge|W|/s$. For every $Hh\in b_{\mathcal S}V$, choose a neighboring $Hg\in V$ and one $(v,Hg)\in W$. Follow the gauged tree path to $(v_0,Hg)$, then the supplied based loop realizing the relevant generator edge to $(v_0,Hh)$. This path has length at most $D+L$ and ends outside $W$, because the coset $Hh$ does not appear in $W$ at all. Its first outside vertex belongs to $b_{\mathcal G_H}W$, and its endpoint is within $D+L$ of that vertex. Distinct $Hh$ give distinct endpoint vertices in the root fiber. The ball bound in step 6.1 therefore yields $$|b_{\mathcal S}V|\le B_{\Delta,D+L}|b_{\mathcal G_H}W|.$$ Apply step 5.1 to $V$ and use $|V|\ge|W|/s$ to get $$|b_{\mathcal G_H}W|\ge\frac{\gamma_{r,R}}{s B_{\Delta,D+L}}|W|.$$ Every selection here is finite for the chosen $W$. This proves the exact claimed common expansion on the lifted finite net graph. [step 5.1, step 6.1, given, construct, algebra]

8.1 The generator loops whose lengths define $L$ exist whenever the based loop labels generate $F_r$: express each $a_i$ as a finite product of those labels and their inverses and concatenate the corresponding loops, then take the maximum of their finitely many lengths. If a different initial gauge is used, vertex-wise coset relabeling along the chosen spanning tree makes its edge labels identity and gives an isomorphic lifted graph. Thus the explicit assumptions on $Q$ are intrinsic finite data, not restrictions to regular covers. Steps 5.1 and 7.1 establish all conclusions. They require the actual uniform generator bound for $H$ and free base group; they do not manufacture either of these hypotheses from a geometric-subsurface statement still awaiting proof. [F1, F2, step 5.1, step 7.1, given, algebra] ∎
