## Statement

Assume AC, hence Countable Choice. Let $X$ be a connected Riemann surface, let $q\ne0$ be a holomorphic quadratic differential, and let $K\subset X$ be a nonempty compact connected smoothly bounded surface region. Empty boundary is allowed. For every $\delta>0$ there is a finite collection of embedded equal-radius $q$-flat discs $D_1,\ldots,D_s\subset\operatorname{int}K$ with zero-free embedded extended flat neighborhoods such that:

- their union $U$ is connected and $\int_{K\setminus U}|q|<\delta$;
- each has the same mass $A=\pi r^2$, each nonempty pairwise intersection is an ordinary connected flat lens, and all such intersections have mass at least $aA$ for some $a>0$;
- distinct disc centers are used, and each pair of CLOSED discs either is disjoint or has positive open overlap; thus no pair has only boundary contact;
- the image of $\pi_1(U)$ in $\pi_1(K)$ is the whole group, and therefore its image in $\pi_1(X)$ equals that of $\pi_1(K)$.

A finite connected graph $Q$, whose vertices are these discs and edges are their nonempty pairwise overlaps, consequently carries all that monodromy: using chosen center paths in discs and overlaps, its loop labels surject onto the image of $\pi_1(K)$ in $\pi_1(X)$. For any covering of $X$, the lifted-disc intersection graph is a graph covering of $Q$; its connected components have the usual coset descriptions for the corresponding monodromy action. Covers need not be regular. If $\partial K\ne\varnothing$, then $\pi_1(K)$ is free of rank $1-\chi(K)$; this is proved by the actual finite boundary-relative triangulation below, not inferred from a disk-and-band assertion.

Under the actual thick-pointed quadratic convergence of [[lem-finite-quadratic-flat-nets-transport-under-thick-holomorphic-convergence]], the net transports to nearby quadratics with these fixed discs' exact masses and a common positive overlap lower bound, the same finite intersection graph, and convergent finite monodromy labels. This statement by itself does NOT identify that monodromy with the fundamental group of a whole varying thick component, or prove any subgroup rank or global expansion property.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the Riemann surface, nonzero holomorphic quadratic, compact connected smooth surface region and positive omitted-mass tolerance.

[A1] Choice and Countable Choice are [[def-axiom-of-choice]] and [[def-countable-choice]].

[F1] A compact smooth metric-extended surface region has a finite boundary-relative curvilinear triangulation, including a closed region. Smooth boundary collars exist under Countable Choice. [[thm-finite-geodesic-triangulation-of-a-compact-riemannian-surface]], [[thm-collar-neighborhood-theorem]]

[F2] Zeros of a nonzero holomorphic function are isolated, by the identity theorem; applying it to local quadratic coefficients gives finitely many zeros on any compact neighborhood of $K$. Near a nonzero coefficient its square root exists by the holomorphic inverse theorem for $z\mapsto z^2$, integration on a small coordinate disc gives a primitive, and the nonzero derivative makes that primitive a local coordinate. Thus at every nonzero point there is an embedded natural coordinate in which $q=dw^2$. [[thm-identity-theorem-holomorphic-functions]], [[thm-holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem]], [[thm-holomorphic-primitive-on-star-shaped-domain]]

[F3] Quadratic mass has density $|f(z)|\,dx\,dy$ in a complex chart, and the usual smooth measurable change-of-variables rule holds under Countable Choice. [[thm-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-lebesgue-measurable-functions]]

[F4] A supplied finite family of equal flat discs with extended embedded zero-free neighborhoods transports under the stated thick quadratic convergence. All finite intersection masses converge; positive overlap fractions stay positive, disjoint closed discs stay disjoint, and finite monodromy words converge with actual finite relations. [[lem-finite-quadratic-flat-nets-transport-under-thick-holomorphic-convergence]]

## Proof

1.1 Choose a smooth Riemannian metric on a neighborhood of $K$ by finite coordinate bump functions and apply [F1] to triangulate $K$. Its finite one-skeleton contains a connected finite graph $G$ whose inclusion surjects onto $\pi_1(K)$. To justify the latter directly, a loop may be approximated by finitely many coordinate arcs transverse to the finitely many triangle edges and missing vertices, and each of its finitely many passages across an open triangular disc can be pushed to the two boundary arcs between its entry and exit points. One can first subdivide its compact parameter interval into finitely many triangle-star neighborhoods; the same contraction inside each closed triangular disc gives the edge-loop replacement without a global transversality assertion. Thus every loop is homotopic to an edge loop. A spanning tree of the connected one-skeleton and its non-tree edges gives finitely many such generator loops. If $\partial K\ne\varnothing$, push the whole finite graph slightly into the interior using a smooth collar from [F1]; this is homotopic in $K$ to its original inclusion. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Move this finite graph off the finitely many zeros of $q$, retaining its monodromy. Here is an explicit local move rather than a generic-position invocation. The graph consists of finitely many piecewise regular smooth arcs and has coordinate area zero: bounded derivative parametrizations cover their radius-$h$ strips with area tending to zero. Around a zero in the graph choose a small coordinate disc compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}K$, containing no other zero. Take a smooth bump $\chi$ equal to one on a smaller disc about that zero and supported in the first. For a sufficiently small constant vector $v$, the maps $\Phi_t(z)=z+t\chi(z)v$, $0\le t\le1$, have $\|D\Phi_t-I\|<1/2$ and are diffeomorphisms, extended by identity outside the coordinate disc. Injectivity follows from the Lipschitz bound for $\Phi_t-\operatorname{id}$; surjectivity follows by iterating $z\mapsto y-t\chi(z)v$, whose successive increments decrease geometrically on the complete plane. Smooth local inverses then follow from the real derivative formula, or that same contraction differentiated. Choose $v$ so small that any preimage of the zero lies where $\chi=1$. The forbidden translations are the negatives of graph points in that smaller disc, an area-zero subset, so some arbitrarily small $v$ avoids them. This isotopy removes the zero from the graph and does not meet any other zero. Repeating finitely many times gives a finite piecewise smooth graph $G'\subset\operatorname{int}K$ disjoint from all zeros, whose image still surjects onto $\pi_1(K)$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a compact connected zero-free surface region $A\subset\operatorname{int}K$ containing $G'$ and with $\int_{K\setminus A}|q|<\delta$. Concretely shave a sufficiently thin boundary collar, empty if the boundary is empty, and remove small pairwise disjoint coordinate discs about the remaining finitely many zeros. Take the collar thin enough and the zero discs small enough to avoid the compact graph $G'$. Their omitted mass is arbitrarily small: on a fixed compact coordinate cover $|q|$ has bounded continuous density, collar areas tend to zero with width, and disc areas tend to zero with their radii. The shaved region remains connected by the collar product. Removing each interior disc preserves path connectedness: for any path between two remaining points, replace the portion between its first entry and last exit through that disc by a boundary-circle arc. This avoids the entire disc; doing so finitely many times introduces no other disc because their closures are disjoint. Thus the resulting $A$ is connected. It has a compact zero-free neighborhood inside $\operatorname{int}K$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Natural flat charts exist near every point of that neighborhood by [F2]. By compactness, choose finitely many such chart discs so that smaller concentric discs cover $A$ and their larger closures stay zero-free inside $\operatorname{int}K$. There is $\rho>0$ so that every point $x\in A$ has an embedded natural disc of radius $20\rho$ about it inside one of the larger charts: take the minimum of the finitely many positive Euclidean natural-coordinate margins between the smaller and larger discs. Translations of those natural coordinates give the centered discs. A path of $|q|^{1/2}$-length less than $20\rho$ starting at $x$ cannot exit that chart disc, since up to its first exit its Euclidean length is at least the radius. This supplies the local metric-ball property needed below without assuming completeness of the punctured flat metric. Choose finitely many distinct centers $x_j\in A$ whose radius-$\rho$ discs cover $A$. [A1, F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Enlarge their common radius slightly to some $r\in(\rho,11\rho/10)$, avoiding the finitely many values at which two closed discs first meet only at their boundaries. These exceptional values are precisely $d_{ij}/2$ for the relevant flat center distances $d_{ij}\le22\rho/10$. To check this in one chart, if two such discs meet, join their centers through a common point by two flat radial segments of total length at most $2r$. The first-exit property in step 4.1 puts that whole path inside the radius-$20\rho$ chart about the first center, and then every radial segment to a point of the second disc has total length less than $3r<20\rho$. Hence BOTH discs are contained in that one flat chart. Their transition there has derivative whose square is one, hence is $w\mapsto\pm w+c$ on the connected disc, so they are ordinary equal-radius Euclidean discs. They have a connected lens intersection when $d_{ij}<2r$, are disjoint with disjoint closures when $d_{ij}>2r$, and have boundary-only contact only at $d_{ij}=2r$. The same argument with a potential contact point proves that only these finitely many distances can be exceptional anywhere on the surface. Choose $r$ outside them. Each selected disc has an embedded extended flat neighborhood of radius at least $2r$, since $2r<20\rho$. [A1, F2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Put $D_j=D(x_j,r)$ and $U=\bigcup_jD_j$. They lie in $\operatorname{int}K$ and cover $A$, so $\int_{K\setminus U}|q|<\delta$ by step 3.1. The relative cover $D_j\cap A$ has connected nerve: otherwise the unions of two nonempty disconnected graph classes would partition connected $A$ into disjoint nonempty relatively open sets. Each member meets $A$ at its center. Thus the ordinary intersection graph $Q$ is connected, and $U$ is connected as a union of connected discs along its edges. Every disc has mass $\pi r^2$ by [F3]. Finitely many positive lens intersections give a positive minimum fraction $a$; if there are no edges, set $a=1$, a vacuous condition. The union contains $G'$, so the image of $\pi_1(U)$ in $\pi_1(K)$ contains the surjective image of $\pi_1(G')$ from step 2.1. It is therefore all of $\pi_1(K)$, and composing with inclusion gives exactly the same image in $\pi_1(X)$. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 To realize the monodromy by the finite graph $Q$, choose one point in each nonempty lens and paths from the two centers to it within the corresponding discs. They give an edge path in $U$ for each abstract edge. Any loop in $U$ has a subdivision into finitely many arcs each lying in one of the open discs, by compactness of its parameter interval. At a transition choose the actual point in the overlap. Since the overlap is connected, connect it within the lens to the chosen edge point; within each disc all paths with the same endpoints are homotopic because the disc is simply connected. These replacements identify the loop's class with the image of an edge loop in $Q$, after joining its basepoint to a center. Thus the loop labels of $Q$ surject onto $\pi_1(U)$ and hence onto the claimed image group. No equality between $\pi_1(Q)$ and $\pi_1(U)$ is asserted; two-dimensional nerve relations may be present. [A1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 For a covering $p:Y\to X$, each lift of a disc maps homeomorphically to it. For an edge $i$–$j$, its base lens is connected and simply connected. Inside any fixed lift of $D_i$ its inverse lens is connected and is contained in exactly one lift of $D_j$; conversely every meeting lift arises this way. Hence each lifted disc vertex has exactly one adjacent lift over every incident edge of $Q$. The lifted intersection graph is therefore a graph covering of $Q$. Following the center-edge paths of step 7.1 identifies the graph components with orbits of the monodromy image on the actual covering fiber. Fixing one fiber point gives its stabilizer $H$ in that image group $K_0$; the orbit is described by its cosets, and choosing tree paths in $Q$ gives the finite-labeled-graph model with vertex set $(v,Hg)$ and edge right multiplication by the corresponding loop labels. This uses the actual monodromy action and works for arbitrary nonnormal stabilizers, rather than presuming that every cover has a deck group acting transitively on fibers. [step 5.1, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 For completeness the boundary case has the promised free rank. The dual adjacency graph of the finite triangulation of a connected surface with boundary, augmented by one outside vertex incident to its boundary edges, is connected: interior paths between face points cross finitely many edges, and a path to the boundary connects it to the outside vertex. Choose a spanning tree rooted there. Remove faces in an order with every parent before its children. The edge toward a face's tree parent is free when that face is removed: for a root child it is a boundary edge; for any other face its parent has already been removed and no third face meets that edge. Collapse that closed triangle across its free edge onto its other two edges. This is a deformation retraction of the remaining complex, given in the reference triangle by pushing along line segments away from the free edge; all its other edges are fixed. Finite repetition removes all faces and yields a connected finite graph spine of $K$. A spanning tree in that graph then shows its loop group free on the non-tree edges, by the explicit tree-path insertion and cancellation argument. Its rank is $E-V+1=1-\chi(K)$, since each triangle/free-edge collapse preserves Euler characteristic. Thus $\pi_1(K)$ is free of that rank. This separate argument is not applied when $K$ is closed. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Finally apply [F4] to the finite extended flat discs constructed above. Their exact areas stay $\pi r^2$, positive lens fractions have a common positive lower bound for large $n$, and pairs whose closed source discs are disjoint stay disjoint. Step 5.1 excluded boundary-only contacts, so every source nonedge has disjoint closed discs and every source edge has positive overlap. The finite graph is therefore exactly the same for large $n$. The actual transported center paths have the finite monodromy convergence and finite-relation stability asserted in [F4]. This establishes all analytic and finite graph inputs claimed in the Statement, without asserting any global subgroup or expansion conclusion. [F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, given, algebra] ∎
