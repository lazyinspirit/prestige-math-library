## Statement

Assume AC and all the full jointly blocked Jordan HNN hypotheses of [[thm-blocked-jordan-hnn-extensions-have-nested-limit-sets-and-conformal-boundary-gluing]], with notation $G_0,J_i,B_i,S_i,U_i,f,A_0,G$. Impose the following explicit seam-parabolic compatibility: for $i=1,2$, whenever $p$ is a parabolic point of $J_i$ and its full ambient stabilizer $G_p$ has rank one, normalize that stabilizer as a real translation lattice and require some finite $R_p$ with
$$\Lambda_G\setminus\{p\}\subset\{|\operatorname{Im}z|\le R_p\}.$$
No condition is imposed when the ambient stabilizer has rank two. Rank-one edge cusps may therefore enlarge to rank two; contacts of gluing boundaries remain allowed.

Under exactly these hypotheses, without geometrical finiteness of $G_0$, every point of
$$\Lambda_G\setminus\bigcup_{g\in G}g\Lambda_{G_0}$$
is conical. If in addition $G_0$ is elementary or nonelementary geometrically finite, then $G$ is elementary or nonelementary geometrically finite. In the nonelementary case every positive-radius neighborhood of its convex hull has finite quotient volume; the same finite-volume assertion holds for the elementary convex hull convention described below.

Every parabolic point of $G$ is a translate of a base parabolic point and is bounded parabolic. If a base parabolic point $p$ lies on no $G_0$ translate of $S_1\cup S_2$, then $G_p=(G_0)_p$. In rank one it also has a precisely invariant ideal peak $P'=\{|\operatorname{Im}z|>R'\}\subset A_0\cap\Omega_G$ for that full stabilizer. Seam parabolics may have enlarged rank, subject to the explicit compatibility only when their final rank remains one. Elementary $J_i$ and elementary $G_0$ are included. For an elementary parabolic or trivial group the hull of its at-most-one-point limit set is empty, and for an elementary loxodromic group it is its axis.

The compatibility is an additional application interface to be proved from the given peripheral gluing. This theorem does not establish it automatically, does not assert the reverse combination implication, and does not replace any original general hyperbolization hypothesis by it.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the full torsion-free blocked Jordan HNN data and the explicit seam compatibility; elementary or geometrically finite $G_0$ only for the full forward conclusion.

[A1] Full choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] The actual HNN group is discrete and torsion-free. Its walls have $S_i\cap\Lambda_G=\Lambda_{J_i}$ and retain rank-one strip clearance. Every new limit point has shrinking nested forward discs along an infinite nonbacktracking tree path. At the base vertex, distinct outgoing closed discs have disjoint interiors and null distinct-coset diameters, and can accumulate only in $\Lambda_{G_0}$. The regular retained cell $R\cap\Omega_{G_0}$ lies in $\Omega_G$. [[thm-blocked-jordan-hnn-extensions-have-nested-limit-sets-and-conformal-boundary-gluing]]

[F2] The actual gap $A_0$ is precisely invariant under $G_0$ in $G$, even at its limit points. [[thm-jointly-invariant-jordan-domains-give-discrete-kleinian-hnn-extensions]]

[F3] A nonelementary geometrically finite group has a closed Dirichlet domain whose ideal closure meets its limit set in finitely many parabolic points. At a normalized rank-one cusp its ideal points have bounded parallel coordinate, and at rank two both horizontal coordinates are bounded. Rank-one bounded parabolics have precisely invariant ideal peaks contained in the discontinuity domain. [[thm-geometrically-finite-kleinian-groups-have-separated-standard-cusps-and-finite-dirichlet-limit-vertices]]

[F4] Full parabolic stabilizers of arbitrary discrete torsion-free Kleinian groups are translation lattices of rank one or two. A nonidentity element is loxodromic or parabolic. [[lem-local-thin-components-of-torsion-free-kleinian-quotients]]

[F5] Matrix normalization gives rank-one convergence outside an exceptional point in the actual group limit set; the attracting point is also a limit point. At a normalized parabolic point with some translation length $\ell$, the complex Shimizu bound and the height formula bound every ambient orbit height by $\max(Y_0,\ell^2/Y_0)$. No ambient geometrical finiteness or equal cusp ranks are needed. [[thm-geometrically-finite-subgroup-cusp-clear-sets-have-null-coset-translates]]

[F6] A nonelementary geometrically finite group has every limit point conical or bounded parabolic. Conversely that condition gives finite volume of every positive-radius convex neighborhood for a nonelementary discrete torsion-free group. [[thm-finite-convex-neighborhood-volume-implies-conical-bounded-parabolic-limit-set]], [[thm-conical-bounded-parabolic-kleinian-groups-have-finite-convex-neighborhood-volume]]

[F7] The geometric-to-boundary and boundary-to-geometric conical criteria are proved by rank-one matrices and distances to two geodesics. [[thm-limit-set-conjugacies-preserve-geometric-finiteness-in-hyperbolic-three-space]] The model, geodesic, distance and Möbius derivative formulas are supplied by [[lem-complete-curvature-minus-one-manifolds-are-kleinian-quotients]].

## Proof

1.1 Two elementary geometric observations will be used for arbitrary $G$, including elementary output groups. A parabolic point is never conical: put it at infinity and use the bound $H$ from [F5]. Every orbit point has height at most $H$, whereas the point of height $Y$ on its vertical ray has distance at least $\log(Y/H)$ from that orbit. This diverges. A subgroup conical point is conical for the ambient group by the same orbit sequence and ray. We will also use the whole-sphere form of [F7]: if distinct $k_n$ satisfy $k_nx\to u$, $k_ny\to v$ for every sphere point $y\ne x$, with $u\ne v$, then $x$ is geometrically conical. This version does not require a nonelementary group. Choose any two distinct sphere points $y,z\ne x$. The geodesics $k_n[x,y]$ and $k_n[x,z]$ have endpoints tending to the distinct pair $u,v$, so meet a fixed-radius ball: Möbius maps sending the varying endpoint pairs to $(0,\infty)$ converge to a fixed nonsingular map, giving that bound directly. Thus $k_n^{-1}o$ stays within bounded distance of both fixed geodesics. It escapes compact sets by properness. A bounded-radius neighborhood of either geodesic has only its endpoints as ideal accumulation points, by the upper-half-space distance formula. Their only common endpoint is $x$. Hence $k_n^{-1}o\to x$ and its nearest points on $[x,y]$ eventually lie on a ray toward $x$, proving conicality. [F4, F5, F7, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $x\in\Lambda_G\setminus G\Lambda_{G_0}$. By [F1] choose nested shrinking forward discs along its tree path. After restricting to one of the two directed incidence types, write them $h_nB_i$ for one fixed $i\in\{1,2\}$ and their boundaries as $h_nS_i$. Put $y_n=h_n^{-1}x$. These points lie in $\Lambda_G\setminus\Lambda_{J_i}$, since membership in a translated edge limit set would give an old vertex limit point. We seek $j_n\in J_i$ and $q_n\in G$, the latter either identity or a parabolic translation, so that $$\operatorname{dist}_{\rm sph}(q_nj_ny_n,q_nS_i)\ge\delta>0$$ on an infinite subsequence. All cusp coordinate changes below are fixed after passing to one cusp, so their uniform continuity and inverse uniform continuity preserve a positive lower separation. [A1, F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 If $J_i$ is nonelementary, ideal regular points have representatives in its closed ideal Dirichlet domain. We justify this operation. In a ball model centered at the chosen Dirichlet center $o$, the spherical conformal scale of $j$ at any $y\in\Omega_{J_i}$ tends to zero as $j$ escapes. Indeed normalize its matrices to rank one using [F5]; the exceptional point belongs to $\Lambda_{J_i}$, and on a small neighborhood of $y$ the normalized denominator stays bounded away from zero while the normalized determinant tends to zero. The fractional-linear derivative formula gives the scale tending to zero. Therefore the positive scale attains its maximum at some $j$. At $z=jy$, every $a\in J_i$ has spherical scale at most one by the derivative chain rule. This means $z\in\overline D(o)$: for the ball model that inequality is precisely the ideal limit of the Dirichlet comparisons with $a^{-1}o$, whose Poisson factor is $(1-|a^{-1}o|^2)/|z-a^{-1}o|^2$. Equivalently take the distance difference on a ray to $z$. This proves ideal representative coverage without inferring it from coverage of model interior points. Choose such $j_n$ for $y_n$ and write $z_n=j_ny_n$. [A1, F3, F5, F7, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose first $J_i$ is nonelementary. If $\operatorname{dist}_{\rm sph}(z_n,S_i)$ is bounded below along a subsequence, set $q_n=1$ there. Otherwise take a subsequence $z_n\to p\in S_i$. Closedness of $\Lambda_G$ and [F1] give $p\in\Lambda_{J_i}$. By [F3] it is one of the finitely many parabolic vertices of this Dirichlet domain. Rank two in $J_i$ cannot occur: after putting $p=\infty$, the two horizontal ideal Dirichlet-coordinate bounds prevent finite ideal points $z_n$ from escaping to infinity. Thus $(J_i)_p$ has rank one. Normalize its generator to $z\mapsto z+\ell$, $\ell>0$. The two Dirichlet comparisons with its positive and negative generator bound $\operatorname{Re}z_n$. Hence $z_n\to\infty$ forces $|\operatorname{Im}z_n|\to\infty$. If $G_p$ has rank one, compatibility excludes this because $z_n\in\Lambda_G$. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 The remaining escape case has rank-one $(J_i)_p$ and rank-two $G_p$. Its full translation lattice contains $\ell$ and another vector $v$ with $\operatorname{Im}v\ne0$. Integer multiples of $v$ reduce the imaginary part of $z_n$ into a fixed bounded interval, and integer multiples of $\ell$ then reduce its real part into $[0,\ell]$. Let $q_n$ be their combined translation. Thus $q_nz_n$ stays in a fixed finite compact rectangle and $|\operatorname{Im}q_n|\to\infty$. The original clearance puts $S_i\setminus\{p\}$ in a fixed horizontal strip. All finite points of $q_nS_i$ therefore have imaginary parts tending uniformly in absolute value to infinity; the point infinity is unchanged. Thus $q_nS_i$ tends uniformly to infinity spherically, while $q_nz_n$ remains in a finite compact set. Their distance has a positive lower bound. This gives the required separation in the escaping case, allowing the extra ambient lattice direction rather than ruling out rank enlargement. [F1, F4, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.2 Handle elementary $J_i$ explicitly. Torsion-free discreteness excludes elliptics; a finite invariant limit set of size at least three would make the group finite by its faithful permutation action and hence trivial. An empty limit set forces triviality. A two-point limit set is fixed pointwise because its interchange has order two, so the group is diagonal cyclic: infinitely many multipliers in a compact annulus contradict discreteness, and a nontrivial modulus-one element is elliptic. A one-point limit set gives a parabolic translation lattice by [F4]. For trivial $J_i$, take $j_n=q_n=1$. Since $S_i\cap\Lambda_G=\varnothing$, compactness separates all $y_n$ from $S_i$. For cyclic loxodromic $J_i$, normalize its generator to $z\mapsto az$, $|a|>1$; powers put $z_n=j_ny_n$ in the compact annulus $1\le|z|\le|a|$. Any limit there lies in $\Lambda_G$ and avoids $\Lambda_{J_i}=\{0,\infty\}$, so cannot lie in $S_i$ by [F1]; take $q_n=1$. For rank-two parabolic $J_i$, normalize infinity and use a fixed finite closed lattice parallelogram, which similarly avoids its unique limit point and is separated from $S_i$. For rank-one parabolic $J_i$, real powers bound $\operatorname{Re}z_n$. A subsequence with bounded imaginary coordinate is again separated by compactness and the wall limit-set equality. If its imaginary coordinate is unbounded, compatibility excludes ambient rank one, and ambient rank two is exactly step 4.1's translation argument. Thus the separation is proved for all elementary edge groups too. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 On the selected subsequence set $k_n=q_nj_nh_n^{-1}$. The curve $k_n(h_nS_i)=q_nS_i$ stays at distance at least $\delta$ from $k_nx$. The disc $h_nB_i$ shrinks to $x$ and contains $x$ in its interior, so its boundary separates $x$ from every fixed sphere point $y\ne x$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The transformed curve separates $k_nx$ from $k_ny$. The spherical ball of radius $\delta$ about $k_nx$ misses this curve and lies in one of its complementary components; consequently $d_{\rm sph}(k_nx,k_ny)\ge\delta$ for every such fixed $y$ eventually. This follows from Jordan separation, not a distance-monotonicity claim for arbitrary transformed boundary points. The matrices $k_n$ escape compact sets: otherwise compact-family equicontinuity would send the shrinking curves $h_nS_i\to\{x\}$ to curves approaching $k_nx$, contradicting their positive separation. A rank-one subsequence from [F5] must have kernel $x$, since a different kernel would make $k_nx$ and $k_ny$ converge to the same point for some $y$ distinct from both. A further subsequence has $k_nx\to u$ and $k_ny\to v$ for every sphere point $y\ne x$, with $u\ne v$ by the separation. Step 1.1 proves $x$ conical, including any elementary output case. This proves the first assertion without a base geometrical-finiteness assumption. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Now assume the base is elementary or nonelementary geometrically finite. Every nonparabolic point of $\Lambda_{G_0}$ is conical: this is [F6] in the nonelementary case, or the explicit orbit on a loxodromic axis in the elementary two-point case; elementary parabolic groups have no such points. Let $p$ be a base parabolic point on $gS_i$, $g\in G_0$. Its preimage $g^{-1}p\in\Lambda_{J_i}$ by [F1]. It cannot be conical for $J_i$, because that would make it ambient conical and contradict its ambient parabolicity by step 1.1. It is therefore parabolic for $J_i$, by [F6] when $J_i$ is nonelementary and by the elementary alternatives proved in step 4.2 otherwise. If its ambient stabilizer has rank one, compatibility bounds the transverse coordinate of the full ambient limit set. The quotient of $\Lambda_G\setminus\{g^{-1}p\}$ is a closed subset of the compact cylinder $(\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z)\times[-R_p,R_p]$ and is compact. To see closedness, the limit set minus infinity is closed in the finite plane and invariant under the lattice; the covering quotient takes invariant closed sets to closed sets, since their complements are invariant open sets. If the ambient stabilizer has rank two, the same argument uses the compact two-dimensional lattice torus. Conjugating back proves every seam base parabolic bounded, with no restriction against rank enlargement. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.1, step 4.2, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 Let $p$ instead be a base parabolic point lying on no $G_0$ translate of $S_1\cup S_2$. The base block equality $gB_i\cap\Lambda_{G_0}=g\Lambda_{J_i}\subset gS_i$ shows $p\in A_0$. If $h\in G_p$, then $p\in hA_0\cap A_0$, so precise gap invariance [F2] forces $h\in G_0$. Therefore $G_p=(G_0)_p$ for either rank. Rank two is bounded by the compact lattice-torus argument of step 6.1. For rank one, put $p=\infty$ and write $H=(G_0)_p$ as real translations of period $\ell$. By [F3] there is a precisely invariant base peak $P=\{|\operatorname{Im}z|>R\}\subset\Omega_{G_0}$; if the base itself is elementary rank-one parabolic this follows directly from its translation action. Each $B_i$ excludes infinity and is compact in the finite plane. Moreover every $gB_i$, $g\in G_0$, excludes infinity by the same block equality and the assumption on translated seams. The $H$ translates of each $B_i$ have uniformly bounded transverse coordinate. We next prove that all outgoing translates, for both labels, satisfy one finite transverse bound. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 6.1, given, construct, algebra]

8.1 Otherwise choose one fixed label $i$ and $g_n\in G_0$ such that $g_nB_i$ contains a point with imaginary coordinate unbounded in absolute value. Discard elements of $H$, whose translates have bounded transverse extent. Fix one $y_0\in P$. Precise invariance of $P$ implies $g_ny_0\notin P$, so these imaginary parts stay in $[-R,R]$. Left-multiply by real translations $t_n\in H$ to put the real parts of $t_ng_ny_0$ in $[0,\ell]$. The resulting test images remain in a fixed finite compact rectangle, while the discs $t_ng_nB_i$ still have unbounded transverse extent. Their right cosets modulo $J_i$ cannot belong to any finite set: each fixed coset image is one compact finite-plane disc, because every translate excludes infinity by step 7.1. Pass to a subsequence of distinct cosets. The outgoing-disc nullity in [F1] and a point in each disc escaping to infinity force every point of the disc to tend spherically to infinity. Hence images under $t_ng_n$ of two fixed distinct points of $B_i$ tend to infinity. These are distinct group elements; [F5] then makes their attracting limit infinity and their exceptional point a base limit point. But $y_0\in\Omega_{G_0}$ is not exceptional, so $t_ng_ny_0\to\infty$, contradicting its fixed finite rectangle. This proves the uniform transverse bound for each label, and taking their maximum gives it for the full outgoing family. [F1, F5, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Increase $R$ to $R'$ larger than this common bound. Then $P'=\{|\operatorname{Im}z|>R'\}\subset P$ avoids every outgoing closed disc, so lies in $A_0\cap\Omega_{G_0}$ and hence in $\Omega_G$ by [F1]. Inside $G_0$ it is precisely invariant under $H$, because $P'$ is $H$-invariant and contained in the precisely invariant $P$. Outside $G_0$, disjointness follows from $P'\subset A_0$ and [F2]. Thus it is a genuine ideal peak for the full ambient stabilizer $H$, already shown equal to $G_p$ in step 7.1. Its inclusion in $\Omega_G$ bounds the transverse coordinate of $\Lambda_G\setminus\{p\}$ by $R'$. The compact-cylinder argument of step 6.1 proves $p$ bounded parabolic. Both the stabilizer equality and the full two-tail precisely invariant peak have now been established, rather than inferred from formal tree stabilizers alone. [F1, F2, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Let $p$ be any ambient parabolic point. It cannot lie outside all translated base limit sets: step 5.1 would make it conical, contradicting step 1.1. Translate it into $\Lambda_{G_0}$. If it were not base parabolic, step 6.1 would make it base conical and hence ambient conical, again impossible. Thus every ambient parabolic point is a translated base parabolic point, and steps 6.1 and 9.1 prove it bounded. Every ambient limit point in a translated base limit set is now either conical by the base conclusion in step 6.1 or is a bounded parabolic point. All remaining limit points are conical by step 5.1. If $G$ is nonelementary, the actual forward criterion [F6] proves finite volume of every positive-radius convex neighborhood. If $G$ is elementary, step 4.2's classification applies equally to $G$. For a trivial or parabolic group the hull of its empty or singleton limit set is empty. For a cyclic loxodromic group it is the axis; let $L>0$ be its translation length. In Fermi coordinates about that axis the hyperboloid parametrization $(\cosh r\cosh s,\cosh r\sinh s,\sinh r\cos\theta,\sinh r\sin\theta)$ gives metric $dr^2+\cosh^2r\,ds^2+\sinh^2r\,d\theta^2$ and volume density $\sinh r\cosh r$. A fundamental screw-translation interval $0\le s<L$, $0\le\theta<2\pi$ therefore gives radius-$\varepsilon$ tube volume $\pi L\sinh^2\varepsilon<\infty$. This handles the elementary output cases without asserting an unwarranted nonelementary conclusion. All full forward and cusp assertions follow. [F1, F4, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 9.1, algebra] ∎
