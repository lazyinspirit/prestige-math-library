## Statement

Let $\Theta=\{1/3,2/3\}$ have prior mass $1/2$ at each parameter. Given $\theta$, observe three independent Bernoulli$(\theta)$ variables $Y=(Y_1,Y_2,Y_3)$, and summarize by $S(Y)=Y_1+Y_2+Y_3$. For observed data $y_0=(1,1,0)$ and any $0\le\epsilon<1$, the ABC target based on $S$ equals the full-data posterior, with masses $1/3$ at $1/3$ and $2/3$ at $2/3$. The count statistic is sufficient.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite prior-simulator model, the statistic $S$, and the conventions in [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]].

[F1] A probability kernel is a probability measure in its event argument and a measurable function in its parameter argument. [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F2] A statistic is a measurable map from data to its summary space. [[def-statistic-and-estimator]]

[F3] Sufficiency means that one parameter-independent probability kernel $Q(t,D)$ reconstructs the conditional data law given the summary for every parameter. [[def-sufficient-statistic]]

[F4] A Polish presentation is separable and has a compatible complete metric; the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by its open sets. [[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-polish-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F5] The usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is complete, the rationals are countable and dense, and its metric balls generate the usual topology. [[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]], [[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]], [[def-metric-ball]], [[def-metric-topology]]

[F6] The ABC target conditions the prior-predictive law on the tolerance event, and rejection sampling returns that target when acceptance probability is positive. [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]], [[alg-abc-rejection]], [[thm-abc-rejection-target-is-tolerance-conditioned-posterior]]

[F7] A Dirac set function is a probability measure. [[def-dirac-measure]], [[prop-dirac-measure-is-a-probability-measure]]

[F8] A sufficient statistic has a posterior depending only on its value, up to the posterior version convention; the finite common-kernel verification below proves sufficiency directly. [[thm-sufficient-summary-recovers-full-posterior]]

## Example

Take $\Theta=\{1/3,2/3\}$, $\mathsf Y=\{0,1\}^3$, $\Pi(\{\theta\})=1/2$, $K(\theta,\{y\})=\theta^{S(y)}(1-\theta)^{3-S(y)}$, and $S(y)=y_1+y_2+y_3$.

## Verification

**Given:** The finite prior-simulator model and summary in the Example.

1.1 Give each finite set its discrete metric $d(x,x')=0$ for equality and $1$ otherwise. Since Cauchy means that tail distances are eventually below every positive radius, a Cauchy sequence is eventually constant at radius $1/2$; the metric is complete. Each finite set is countable and dense in itself, and its singletons are open, so its Borel sigma-algebra is the power set. The identity is a Borel isomorphism to this Polish presentation, so [F4] gives that $\Theta$ and $\mathsf Y$ are standard Borel. For $\mathbb R$, [F5] gives a complete usual metric and countable dense subset $\mathbb Q$; each metric ball is an ordinary open interval, so this metric induces the usual topology. Thus $\mathbb R$ is Polish and its usual Borel space is standard Borel by [F4]. [given, F4, F5, construct]

2.1 The prior is proper. For each $\theta$, summing the likelihood over the eight binary triples gives $(\theta+(1-\theta))^3=1$, so $K$ is a probability kernel by [F1]. The count $S$ is measurable because its domain is finite. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $k\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ let $F_k=\{y\in\mathsf Y:S(y)=k\}$ and define $Q(k,D)=|D\cap F_k|/\binom3k$. For $t\notin\{0,1,2,3\}$ set $Q(t,\cdot)=\delta_{(0,0,0)}$. Each value is a probability measure by [F7], and for fixed $D$ the map $t\mapsto Q(t,D)$ is Borel, so $Q$ is a probability kernel. For every $\theta$, $D\subseteq\mathsf Y$, and Borel $C\subseteq\mathbb R$, $K(\theta,D\cap S^{-1}(C))=\sum_{k\in C\cap\{0,1,2,3\}}|D\cap F_k|\theta^k(1-\theta)^{3-k}$ $=\sum_{k\in C\cap\{0,1,2,3\}}Q(k,D)\binom3k\theta^k(1-\theta)^{3-k}=\int_C Q(t,D)K_S(\theta,dt).$ This is the common-kernel criterion [F3], so $S$ is sufficient. [given, F1, F3, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The full-data likelihood at $y_0=(1,1,0)$ is $\theta^2(1-\theta)$. The prior-weighted likelihoods at $1/3$ and $2/3$ are respectively $1/27$ and $2/27$, so their normalization gives posterior masses $1/3$ and $2/3$. For $0\le\epsilon<1$, the condition $|S(Y)-2|\le\epsilon$ is exactly $S(Y)=2$. Its likelihood is $3\theta^2(1-\theta)$, three times the full-data likelihood at either parameter; the prior-weighted masses are $1/9$ and $2/9$, giving the same normalized posterior. [given, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The event in step 3.2 has positive prior-predictive probability $1/3$, so the ABC target is defined. By [F6], the rejection algorithm's accepted parameter has that target law; hence it equals the full-data posterior just computed. The result also agrees with the sufficient-summary posterior conclusion [F8]. [given, F6, F8, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
