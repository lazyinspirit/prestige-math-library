## Mathematical caveats

- Posterior-predictive definitions take a globally supplied measurable probability kernel. The dominated formula provides one such kernel only with its stated proper-prior/joint-density hypotheses and explicit filler; it does not endorse forming a quotient at zero/infinite evidence.
- Posterior predictive moments require finite integrated second moment. The kernel-valued moment decomposition is choice-free. AC appears only in the separately stated optional identification with conditional-expectation equivalence classes; the squared-loss posterior-mean theorem explicitly assumes AC because its proof uses the published L² projection theorem.
- Equal-tailed intervals are proved credible for every 0 < alpha < 1; credibility is not a frequentist coverage statement. HPD regions require a density relative to a specified measure and may be disconnected/nonunique.
- Posterior risk is defined only for finite loss integrals. A Bayes action rule must be measurable and attain the minimum; no generic existence theorem is asserted. The median and finite-class results include measurable selectors. The exact continuous zero–one example shows that a density mode need not distinguish point actions.
