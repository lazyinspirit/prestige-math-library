# Period frontier: complete local construction

Research proposal only; canonical manifest, receipts and state unchanged.

Choose a finite foliation-box cover of the compact image of h and a finite C² source partition of unity subordinate to its pullback. The local cooriented transverse covectors du_i differ by positive nonvanishing C¹ factors on overlaps. Their weighted sum β therefore has exactly the characteristic kernel and singularities, and is C¹. Equivalently β=h*ω if a global defining form ω has already been fixed. Choose an oriented source area form μ and define X by ι_Xμ=β. The local foliation transverse functions u=z∘h are C², and β=f du with nonzero C¹ f, so X is C¹ and its regular line foliation has the C² first-integral atlas used below. The given generic disk has finitely many centers and saddles, all interior, and a nonsingular boundary collar. Extend X as a C¹ field to an open neighborhood U of the compact source disk by [[lem-c1-planar-fields-on-a-closed-disk-extend-to-a-neighborhood]]. This is an extension of the field only.

Fix the specified center c. Let P_c be the set of all points on regular periodic X-orbits contained in int D whose bounded Jordan interiors contain c. A nonconstant periodic orbit is an embedded circle: choose its least positive period using a local flow box and compactness, and uniqueness rules out a repeated point before that period. Its time parametrization is C² since X is C¹. The center-level picture supplied by the generic-position lemma gives a punctured neighborhood of c consisting of such circles, so int P_c is nonempty. The flow preserves P_c and its interior. Let A be the connected component of int P_c containing that small center collar. Components of an open planar set are open (a small connected ball through any point belongs to its component), and A is flow-saturated because each orbit is connected. Two of its circles are disjoint by uniqueness and both contain c in their bounded interiors; Jordan separation therefore puts one compact circle strictly inside the bounded interior of the other. To spell out this familiar nesting use: one circle, being connected and disjoint from the other, is entirely in one of its two complementary components. If both circles lay in each other's exteriors, their bounded components would be disjoint: the bounded component of the first is connected, disjoint from the second circle, and contains points on its own collar on the same exterior side of the second; hence it lies in the second's exterior. This would contradict their common point c. Thus the circles in A are strictly nested. Orient them by X; their agreement or disagreement with boundary orientation is a locally constant sign on connected A and hence consistent.

The local and global construction in [[lem-c2-first-integral-period-annuli-have-c2-products]], with the explicit connected-open-set formulation recorded in this proposal, now proves that A is an annulus and gives a C² product Ψ:S¹×(0,1)→A increasing in the nesting order. Its hypotheses are exactly the C² atlas, compact circle leaves, nesting, consistent orientation, and connected openness just established. For clarity the crucial local return step is not an assumption of open periodic stability: a short everywhere-transverse interval in a first-integral box meets each global circle at most once, because every crossing has one fixed boundary-orientation sign, whereas entry and exit across a Jordan curve alternate. The finite cyclic plaque return therefore fixes every point of a sufficiently short section. The finite phase gluing and the ordered one-dimensional quotient then produce local and global products. A is maximal among connected open regular circle families continuing the chosen center collar, because any such extension is an open subset of P_c and hence lies in this same component. This proves existence and maximality without choosing a maximal chain or using Zorn. In product coordinates X=a(s,θ)∂θ with a positive C¹ function, using the product supplier's finite-regularity clause, not a C² flow of X.

Write C_s=Ψ(S¹×{s}), Ω_s for its bounded open Jordan component, and Ω=⋃_{0<s<1}Ω_s. All closed bounded domains lie in D: their exterior contains the connected complement of the source disk, so their bounded interiors cannot leave the source disk. For s<t the entire compact closed domain of C_s lies in Ω_t, and C_s⊂Ω_t. Put Γ=∂Ω in the plane. This is a nonempty compact subset of D, and it separates c from any specified point q outside D: c lies in a fixed Ω_s, q lies outside cl Ω, and every path between them has a first exit from Ω, on Γ. No connectedness or graph regularity of Γ is assumed at this stage.

Here is the complete Hausdorff exhaustion argument. For r∈(0,1) set T_r=cl(⋃_{s≥r}C_s), a nonempty compact subset of D. These sets decrease with r. Every C_s is in Ω, so their tail intersection K∞ is contained in cl Ω. If x∈Ω_t, a ball about x lies in Ω_t, and every C_s with s>t avoids that ball; hence K∞∩Ω=∅. Thus K∞⊂Γ. For any ε>0, if arbitrarily late C_s had points at distance ≥ε from Γ, the nested compact sets T_r∩{x∈D:dist(x,Γ)≥ε} would all be nonempty and have a common point, contrary to K∞⊂Γ. This proves sup_{x∈C_s}dist(x,Γ)→0. Conversely fix p∈Γ and ε>0. Choose x∈Ω∩B_{ε/2}(p) and t with x∈Ω_t. For s>t, x∈Ω_s while p∉Ω_s; along the segment from x to p, the boundary C_s is met, at a point within ε/2 of p. Cover compact Γ by finitely many balls B_{ε/2}(p_i), make these finitely many choices x_i,t_i, and take s>max t_i. Then every point of Γ is within ε of C_s. Consequently C_s→Γ in Hausdorff distance, and Γ⊂cl A. These are finite choices for each ε, not a chosen sequence of witnesses.

No center belongs to Γ. The original center c lies in the fixed open set Ω_t. For any other center d, take a sufficiently small saturated center disk V disjoint from c. Every regular orbit meeting V is a complete small level circle in V, by uniqueness, so it does not enclose c and cannot belong to A. The smaller center disk is therefore disjoint from cl A and from Γ. All equilibria on Γ are consequently among the finite nondegenerate saddles of the characteristic disk.

If ∂D is transverse, use a source boundary collar coordinate r≥0 measuring inward distance. Since β is nonzero on the boundary tangent, Xr is nonzero on ∂D. Its sign is constant along this connected compact circle, and continuity gives one δ>0 and k>0 with |Xr|≥k and that same sign throughout 0≤r≤δ. A periodic orbit in D cannot enter this collar: if Xr<0 its forward trajectory cannot leave the collar through r=δ and must exit D within time δ/k; if Xr>0 the same assertion holds in backward time. Hence A and its closure avoid a smaller boundary collar. In the leafwise-boundary case the nonsingular collar makes ∂D one regular periodic X-orbit. If Γ meets it, invariance obtained below includes that entire orbit in Γ, and the generalized PB alternatives force Γ to equal it: a regular boundary orbit cannot be a saddle-to-saddle trajectory. This verifies both boundary clauses without assuming a leafwise boundary collar is foliated by closed circles.

All hypotheses of [[lem-flat-drift-realizes-a-period-annulus-frontier-as-an-omega-limit]] have now been checked: compact disk, C¹ neighborhood field, C² nested leaf product, and compact Γ=∂Ω. It supplies a C¹ field Y on U (shrink U if needed) and a positive orbit with compact closure in D⊂⊂U and ω_Y(y)=Γ, with Y=X and DY=DX on Γ. In particular Γ is compact, connected and invariant; its regular Y-trajectories are X-trajectories because equality on invariant Γ and uniqueness identify them. Apply [[lem-local-generalized-poincare-bendixson-for-a-precompact-planar-orbit]]. A singleton equilibrium is impossible because Γ separates c and q, whereas the complement of one point in the plane is polygonally connected. If Γ has no equilibria, it is one regular periodic orbit. Otherwise every regular point is on a trajectory with saddle alpha- and omega-limits. The branch and terminal-component proof in [[lem-finite-saddle-omega-graph-is-strongly-connected]], corrected explicitly in this proposal, then proves that Γ is a finite embedded strongly connected directed saddle graph, covered by finitely many directed saddle polycycles. This applies also to loops, parallel edges and repeated vertices. It uses the four local characteristic branches at each saddle, not an assertion that the general PB theorem has finitely many connections.

Finally, for any fixed phase θ₀, s↦h(Ψ(θ₀,s)) is C² and transverse to F: its pulled-back characteristic covector is nonzero on ∂s because ∂s is transverse to ker β. These are precisely the prescribed closed characteristic loops C_s; their holonomy can be identity or nonidentity, and no holonomy conclusion is needed. The only countable selection here is the product supplier's ACω selection of a countable chart and gluing system. Compact-tail intersections, finite collar covers, the field extension, the explicit flat drift, and finite graph arguments require no AC or DC. This completes every clause of the stated frontier result.

## Finite graph closure

Here is a direct finite-graph proof of strong connectivity. Suppose the finite directed graph has more than one strongly connected component. Its finite condensation graph has a terminal component S: starting from any component and following an outgoing edge must stop, since a repeated component would be a directed cycle and hence one strongly connected component. Include in N all vertices and all edges internal to S, and a short terminal segment of every edge entering S from outside. Choose each trimming point in the regular part of that incoming edge. There is no edge leaving S. Thus N is compact and positively invariant relative to Γ, and S is contained in int_Γ N, since all incident half-edges at its vertices are included near those vertices. For every T>0, Φ_T(N)⊂int_Γ N: points of S stay in S, and each added incoming segment moves strictly toward its terminal saddle. No finite-time arrival at that saddle is possible by uniqueness, and its sole outside trimming endpoint moves strictly past the trimming point. Compactness gives δ=dist(Φ_T(N), Γ\int_Γ N)>0. Because N is positively invariant, Φ_t(N)⊂Φ_T(N) for all t≥T. Every (ε,T)-chain in Γ starting in S, for ε<δ, therefore remains in N by induction: the flowed point is in Φ_T(N), and its next jump cannot leave N. There is a vertex outside N, since the terminal component is proper. This contradicts the exact-endpoint internal chain transitivity proved above. Thus all vertices lie in one strongly connected component. This argument controls arbitrarily many jumps and requires no informal claim that a chain itself traces a directed itinerary. 

## Local product applicability

Near a compact leaf C choose a short C² transverse interval σ inside a first-integral box, and shrink it so it is transverse to the line field everywhere. Orient each circle as the boundary of its bounded Jordan domain, using the consistent orientation in the hypotheses. Along σ the determinant of a locally chosen positively oriented tangent and σ′ is continuous and nonzero, hence has one sign. Consequently all intersections of any fixed leaf circle with this particular σ have the same inward/outward crossing direction. On the compact section subarc between two hypothetical intersections, transversality makes intersections isolated and finite, while the Jordan separation conclusion of [[lem-finitely-cornered-regular-plane-curve-separates-without-choice]] makes entry and exit crossings alternate. Thus this section meets each circle at most once. This argument uses everywhere-transversality and consistent boundary orientation; it makes no sign assertion for an arbitrary curve. Cover C by a finite cyclic chain of C² foliated rectangles. Finite plaque transport defines a C² return map H on a smaller interval in σ. Its returned point lies on the same global circle as the starting point and on σ, so the just-proved uniqueness of section intersection gives H(t)=t. Mere proximity in the starting box would not suffice to identify two plaques. Identity holonomy now makes the transported transverse parameter a well-defined C² first integral on the local saturated neighborhood.

Here is the finite phase gluing. Choose a C² once-around parametrization $\gamma_0:S^1\to C$ and a finite cyclic cover by plaque arcs whose slightly enlarged arcs lie in the chosen rectangles; refine so only adjacent enlarged arcs overlap and each overlap lies in a common rectangle. In each rectangle, hold its transported transverse coordinate fixed at $t$ and keep the reference leaf-coordinate of $\gamma_0$ fixed. This gives a C² candidate $\gamma_i(t,\theta)$ on the enlarged arc. On an adjacent overlap express both candidates in the common plaque coordinate $x$; they have the same transverse coordinate and agree as functions of $\theta$ at $t=0$. Choose a C² cutoff $\chi$ equal to one on an open collar at one end and zero on an open collar at the other, and set $x(t,\theta)=\chi(\theta)x_i(t,\theta)+(1-\chi(\theta))x_{i+1}(t,\theta)$. The point with plaque coordinate $x(t,\theta)$ and the shared transverse coordinate remains on that leaf. At $t=0$, both candidates equal $\gamma_0$, so $\partial_\theta x$ is the positive reference derivative; compactness of the finitely many closed overlaps lets us shrink the transverse interval once so $\partial_\theta x>0$ on every blend. On the two open collars the formula equals the corresponding candidate exactly, so adjacent pieces and all their derivatives agree there. The cyclic product closes because $H$ is the identity. Thus the resulting $\gamma(t,\cdot)$ is a C² regular circle map, positive in phase, and of degree one: the finitely many oriented plaque arcs are traversed once in their cyclic order, and their exact collar transitions preserve that order. Each $\gamma_t$ is a local diffeomorphism into its connected compact leaf, hence is onto; degree one makes it one-to-one. Since $t$ is a submersion, $(t,\theta)\mapsto\gamma(t,\theta)$ is a C² local diffeomorphism and is a C² product over a neighborhood of the leaf.

For the global base, quotient $A$ by its circle leaves. The local products make the quotient map open and give increasing C² interval charts. Disjoint compact leaves have disjoint sufficiently small saturated product collars, so the quotient $B$ is Hausdorff. Images of a countable basis of $A$ form a countable basis of $B$; $B$ is connected and has no endpoints. The nested leaf order agrees with its interval-chart topology. A bounded nonempty subset has a supremum: if its order cut had no least upper bound, the lower and upper sides of the cut would be disjoint nonempty open sets separating connected $B$. Every closed order segment $[a,b]$ is compact by the open-cover supremum proof: for an open cover let $S$ be the points $x$ for which $[a,x]$ has a finite subcover. A cover member at $a$ makes $S$ nonempty; if $c=\sup S<b$, a cover member containing $c$ extends a finite subcover past $c$, a contradiction, while if $c=b$ that same member finishes a finite cover of $[a,b]$.

Apply [[thm-second-countable-implies-lindelof]] with ACω to select countably many local product charts with precompact interval cores. Their finite order hulls exhaust $B$. Replace the exhaustion by a strictly expanding one: at each step take the least later finite hull extending both endpoints of the previous hull. On each compact shell choose a finite cover by precompact interval charts and explicit C² bumps $\eta((t-t_i)/r_i)$, where $\eta(v)=e^{-1/(1-v^2)}$ for $|v|<1$ and zero outside. Choose each support inside the next shell; the family is locally finite. Normalizing its positive sum gives a C² partition of unity. For increasing local coordinates $t_i$, $\alpha=\sum_i\rho_i\,dt_i$ is a positive C¹ one-form. Apply [[cor-primitives-of-a-continuous-function]] in each interval chart to the positive C¹ coefficient of $\alpha$; each local primitive is C² because its derivative is C¹. On overlaps the primitives differ by constants, so fixing one value and continuing across the compact order segments gives a strictly increasing C² local diffeomorphism $B\to J$ onto an open interval; injectivity follows from the leaf order and the image is an interval by connectedness. An explicit increasing smooth reparametrization takes $J$ to $(0,1)$.

Choose a countable locally finite chain of compact base slabs inside the selected product intervals, with consecutive open overlap collars. Select the local products, seams and transition lifts together using ACω before gluing. On an overlap the transition is a C² family $g_s$ of orientation-preserving circle diffeomorphisms; lift it to $G(s,\theta+2\pi)=G(s,\theta)+2\pi$ fixing one seam value. Extend over the next slab by $G_{\rm ext}(s,\theta)=\chi(s)G(s,\theta)+(1-\chi(s))G(s_*,\theta)$, with a fixed C² cutoff equal to one on the old-side open collar and zero before the overlap ends. Its $\theta$ derivative is a convex combination of positive derivatives. Compose with the already-built lift before each extension. This is deterministic recursion along the preselected chain; local finiteness and exact collar agreement give a global C² product. Fiberwise bijectivity and the local C² inverses prove it is a C² diffeomorphism. Because $D\Psi^{-1}$ and $X\circ\Psi$ are C¹, the tangent coefficient is C¹; connectedness permits one global phase orientation making it positive. This is a foliation-atlas product, not a constant-speed phase obtained from a C¹ flow.

The construction proves the stronger connected-open-set formulation just stated; a pre-existing annulus homeomorphism is never used. In particular the quotient is Hausdorff because two distinct compact leaves have disjoint saturated collars: choose disjoint small Euclidean neighborhoods of the leaves, then shrink each finite local product uniformly so its entire circle fibers lie in its prescribed neighborhood. The quotient map is open because the saturation of an open subset in each local product is open. Thus the images of a countable Euclidean basis are a countable basis. A missing cut point would split the connected quotient into its two open order rays; and the supremum open-cover proof gives compact order segments. The selected local product cores have compact closures strictly inside their full product intervals. All later auxiliary data must be selected without dependent choice: after choosing the countable product system by ACω, enumerate its cores, take finite order hulls, and pass to the least later hull extending both endpoints. These compact hulls exhaust the base because each quotient point is in a core. For each compact shell the set of admissible finite coordinate covers, bumps and positive-form data is nonempty by compactness and the displayed explicit bump. Use ACω once for this countable family of nonempty finite-data sets. After integrating the resulting positive C¹ one-form, use ACω on the countable family of finite slab covers, orientation-preserving local product charts and adjacent transition lifts. The real base coordinate makes rational subdivisions available. One can insist on precompact overlap collars, threefold or finer refinements so every seam lies in a common local product, and a locally finite chain of slabs. The choices are all made before phase gluing, and the later recursion is the specified cutoff formula on that preselected chain, not a repeated selection dependent on the previously glued map. Each extension is made relative to an open seam collar. The product is therefore C², its inverse is C² by the scalar-root inverse supplier, and A is an annulus as a conclusion. This supplies exactly the connected nested family needed in the frontier application.

## Additional interface: decreasing regular cycles

This is a conditional extension for the parent’s minimal-cycle construction, not an addition to the manifest. Let C_n be distinct regular periodic characteristic circles bounding decreasing closed Jordan disks B_n in a fixed compact source disk. Put K=intersection B_n and G=boundary K. Assume K contains a fixed small center disk, and every equilibrium on G is a nondegenerate characteristic saddle. Then the following construction needs no additional choice beyond a subsequence of natural numbers and finite local choices.

First C_n converge in Hausdorff distance to G. Every tail limit lies in K by nesting. No point of int K can be a boundary limit, since its small ball lies in every B_n. Conversely, for p in G and ε>0 choose q outside K within ε of p. Some B_N excludes q; for n≥N the segment from p∈B_n to q∉B_n crosses C_n within ε of p. A finite cover of compact G makes this uniform. The usual nested compact tail argument gives the other Hausdorff inclusion without selecting a convergent sequence. Each B_n is invariant: uniqueness prevents crossing its periodic boundary, and compactness gives complete forward and backward flow there. Hence K and its boundary G are invariant under the flow homeomorphisms. In particular G is compact and connected, as a Hausdorff limit of connected compact circles: a hypothetical separation into disjoint compact nonempty pieces has positive distance, and a sufficiently close circle would be split into their disjoint neighborhoods. K contains a fixed open disk, so G separates an inside point from a point outside the source disk.

At a regular point of G take a short everywhere-transverse section. Each C_n meets it at most once by oriented Jordan crossing signs, exactly as in the product proof. Every point of G on the section is a limit of projected near points on C_n: a flow box moves those near points onto the section by a uniformly small signed time. Therefore G meets each such short section at most once. For every regular trajectory in invariant G, its alpha- and omega-limit sets are nonempty compact connected subsets of G. If one contains a regular point, arbitrarily late crossings of a local section must all be the unique G point on that section, and repeated crossing makes the trajectory periodic. A periodic component is open in G by the same section property (a finite tube projects nearby points of G onto that unique section point), and closed by compactness. Connectedness then makes it all of G. Otherwise every one-sided limit is a singleton equilibrium. The finite four-branch argument and the terminal-SCC trapping argument above thus classify G as a periodic orbit or a finite strongly connected saddle graph. Internal chain transitivity needed for the latter follows directly from periodic approximants: for p,q∈G and ε,T>0 choose nearby p_n,q_n on one sufficiently close C_n; traverse that periodic circle from p_n to q_n, adding sufficiently many full turns that the traversal time is >2T; subdivide into finitely many intervals in [T,2T], and approximate intermediate points by points of G. Uniform finite-time flow continuity makes the resulting jumps <ε and keeps the exact endpoints p,q. All these selections are finite for the fixed ε,T.

Trim disjoint small saddle disks from the finite graph and put a short transverse midpoint section on each trimmed edge. Hausdorff convergence and flow-box projection give at least one section intersection for every sufficiently late C_n; oriented Jordan signs give at most one. Each edge is therefore traversed once, and not with growing multiplicity. The complementary compact set away from the trimmed edge tubes and saddle disks is disjoint from G and hence eventually disjoint from C_n. In each saddle disk the characteristic transverse function has Morse saddle coordinates; each regular nearby level joins one incoming port to one outgoing port, with two possible sector pairings. The finite port set permits only finitely many once-per-edge itineraries, so one itinerary occurs along an infinite subsequence (take its indices in increasing order, determined by that itinerary). Choose each source saddle disk small enough that its image under h lies inside one ambient foliation box. Every approximating saddle passage has constant ambient transverse coordinate z. Its plaque continuation is consequently the same single-box transverse map as the limiting piecewise characteristic passage through that saddle. This ambient passage is defined on a full open transverse interval; smooth extendibility of the source saddle-passage map is irrelevant.

A finite plaque word along the trimmed edges, joined by these single-box saddle passages, consequently gives one ambient holonomy representative H on a common interval about 0. At the approaching base-section parameters t_n, the approximating cycles follow exactly this word; their holonomy germs are the germs of H at t_n, up to the fixed transversal-coordinate conjugacy. If every selected C_n has nonidentity holonomy, H cannot be identity near 0, because that would make the germ identity at all sufficiently late t_n. This is a fixed-word argument, rather than convergence of independently chosen germs.

If a separate singular-image separation hypothesis forces G to have just one saddle, its once-per-edge itinerary is one homoclinic loop or the concatenation of its two homoclinic loops. Nonidentity of the concatenation forces at least one lobe word to be nonidentity. The lobe is an embedded simple characteristic cycle. Its boundary lies in every B_n, and its entire bounded Jordan domain lies there as well: the connected unbounded complement of B_n is disjoint from the lobe boundary, so is contained in its unbounded complementary component. That lobe is therefore a genuine nonidentity cycle lower bound for the nested domains. For a regular G the same conclusion holds with G itself. This closes this conditional regular-sequence lower-bound interface; it does not assert a one-center/no-saddle filled interior or classify all possible singular approximating sequences without the required finite-singular-cycle reduction.
