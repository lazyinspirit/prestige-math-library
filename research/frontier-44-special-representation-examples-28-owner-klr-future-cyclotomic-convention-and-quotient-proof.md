## Graded quotient and projective covers

End_A^0(P)→Hom_A^0(P,P_B)=End_B^0(P_B).

The equality is the ordinary quotient adjunction: any A-map into a B-module kills JP. If P is graded indecomposable with finite-dimensional local End_A^0(P), then P_B is zero or indecomposable: its nonzero degree-zero endomorphism algebra is a quotient of that local algebra and has no nontrivial idempotent. This applies to the already proved generic KLR Krull–Schmidt/projective-cover context.

If P→S is a graded projective cover and JS=0, its quotient map P_B→S is again a projective cover. Indeed, a submodule N⊂P_B mapping onto S has a preimage in P mapping onto S; essentiality of the original cover makes that preimage all of P and hence N=P_B. If JS≠0 and P_B is known to have a graded simple quotient, then P_B=0: every simple quotient of P factors through its unique simple head S, which cannot be a B-module. In the finite-dimensional cyclotomic setting, a nonzero P_B certainly has such a simple quotient. Hom_B(P_B,B)^ψ ≅ (Hom_A(P,A)⊗_A B)^ψ ≅ B⊗_A Hom_A(P,A)^ψ.

The first isomorphism is finite-projective Hom base change; the second sends n⊗b to ψ(b)⊗n and uses ψ(J)=J. It preserves degrees.
