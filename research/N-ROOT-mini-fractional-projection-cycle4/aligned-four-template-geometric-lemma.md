# Globally aligned toothed fans using four templates

2026-10-18. This is a geometric refinement of the verified full-mass construction, suggested by the coordinating root. It does not supply a pressure-correction estimate.

Fix a small angle delta>0. The two unoriented line-direction bands are centered at angles +delta and -delta relative to vertical, each with aperture delta/10. A fan of type + has all its main long tooth-side line directions in the + band; type - uses the - band. Each type has an upward and downward variant. These four fan orientations are sufficient at every recursion depth. Complete container/filler templates additionally record the incoming base direction. There are only finitely many such directions, because they are long-side directions of these four fixed fan polygons (N is fixed before depth varies). Thus one uses a finite collection of templates, not necessarily only four full container triangulations.

To see this, take the alternating-radius fan with radii 1/2 and 1, and put all its radial directions in an angular interval of width a centered on its chosen upward or downward central line. For consecutive radial vectors v,w, the long-side vector is v-w. Its component along the central line has absolute value at least 1/2-O(a), whereas its transverse component is O(a). Hence its unoriented line angle differs from the central line by at most C a, with C absolute. Choose a so small that C a<delta/10. Subdividing a straight tooth side and inserting reserve-base endpoints does not change its line direction. The short root ramps remain guards and need no descendants.

For a parent long-side line in the + band, the upward central vector of type - has nonzero signed normal component relative to that line, with magnitude at least c delta. Its negative, the downward vector, has the opposite sign. Thus precisely one of the upward/downward type - sectors lies wholly in the exterior half-plane of that parent side, provided the radial aperture a is chosen as above. The strict angle separation is uniform. Interchange + and - for the other parent type. A sufficiently small scaled half-container in that exterior side then contains the chosen child sector, with its flat base on the parent tooth side. The sector is oblique relative to the base normal, which changes only fixed geometric constants. Rectangular or parallelogram reserve boxes with that base and a sufficiently large fixed aspect ratio accommodate it.

The alternating radii still give each long side length comparable to one. Consecutive ray angles are comparable to a/N, and their triangle's distance from the root is comparable to a/N. The spacing ell can be chosen comparable to b0 a/N, so parent fan triangle areas are comparable to ell a/N=ell^2/b0. The number of middle-third attachment points is comparable to N/ell=N^2/(b0 a). Exterior clearance is comparable to a/N, and reserve diameters are a fixed small multiple of ell, with constants allowed to depend on delta and a. Consequently the same choice of small b0 gives the attachment area ratio required by G1. The filler and buffer arguments use only fixed finite template geometry and remain applicable to these four oblique variants.

In particular, if r is a fixed multiple of ell, the amplification factor in the physical fractional lower bound is

    B r^(2-2s) qminus^2 = c(delta,a,b0,sigma,s) N^(2s).

It exceeds one for sufficiently large fixed N whenever s>0. Thus restricting every main long-side direction to a fixed narrow cone around a global vertical line does not remove the verified full-mass counterexample mechanism. This is purely a geometric assertion: uniform smallness or persistence of the actual constrained-pressure correction in a depth-dependent cone norm has not been proved.
