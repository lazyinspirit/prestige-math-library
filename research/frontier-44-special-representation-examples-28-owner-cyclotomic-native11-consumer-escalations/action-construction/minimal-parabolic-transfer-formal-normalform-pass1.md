## Finite spanning reduction

Start with a finite diagram and its finitely many dots, crossings and local extrema. Only i components can have local extrema or be closed. Use dot cyclicity on i curves and the ordinary KLR dot moves elsewhere to place dots away from extrema. A dot moved through an equal-color crossing produces its usual lower-crossing correction; an unequal-color dot move produces none. There are finitely many dots, and each correction removes that crossing, so the process terminates after a finite linear expansion.

Sort cup/cap vertices along their i components toward the boundary or toward a designated far-left closed-component position. Each time a vertex passes a crossing, use the specified cap/cross or cup/cross mate equation. At an unlike color the formal sideways inverse/cancellation gives its explicit nonzero scalar; at color i the actual rank-one cross-and-cap equation gives the straight term and a finite sum of dot/bubble terms. This sorting is a sequence of the specified local equations, not an assumed free isotopy of the actual module maps. The correction terms have fewer unsorted extrema on open components, or fewer crossings meeting those extrema. Every sum is finite: dots on an open component have nonnegative count, while negative-degree bubbles vanish, giving a lower bound on the bubble index and hence an upper bound on the open dot counts in the fixed-index cross-and-cap sum. The ordinary triangle equations cancel adjacent opposed vertices when reached. In the main term a moved vertex passes one of the finitely many crossings, reducing its number of remaining obstacles; in every correction term the lexicographic pair (unsorted open-component extrema, crossing obstacles at those extrema) decreases. Thus sorting and its correction expansions terminate.

After this step the open arcs are the fixed matching arcs, with any crossing word superimposed. A closed component has color i. Remove its excess adjacent maximum/minimum pairs by the two triangles, and move it to the far left by the model-verified bubble slides. These slides are explicit finite coefficient equations. For an i root x, the normalized series changes by the factor (1−xt)^{±2}. For a spectator j root y, it changes by

$$q_{ij}^{\mathrm{rev}}(y,t)=t^{-a_{ij}}\frac{Q_{ij}(t^{-1},y)}{t_{ij}},$$

or its reciprocal, according to the direction of the slide. This polynomial has constant term1 by Q4. On any fixed coefficient, multiplication or formal inversion uses finitely many lower coefficients; formal inversion does not invert a dot or Q. The equal-i slide follows the flag quotient p/c_i² under adding a root; the spectator slide follows replacing p by pQ_ij/t_ij. The trace model verifies both as actual coefficient equations, and the generic faithfulness argument below verifies their formal relations on the downward sector. Consequently each closed component contributes a finite polynomial in far-left bubble generators and dots, with no undisclosed spectator-bubble generator. Bubble inversion expresses the opposite-orientation family as the reciprocal series of the chosen one, coefficient by coefficient.

For each matching choose a fixed generic drawing and fixed dot positions. The remaining crossing word can be straightened to the selected reduced drawing: the Coxeter deletion algorithm first applies braid/far moves until a nonreduced word has an adjacent repeated reflection; its quadratic relation removes that pair, and the equal-color quadratic gives zero. A braid defect is a polynomial times a word with fewer crossings. Induct on crossing length for all defects, and use the already described dot moves to restore the fixed dot positions. This is exactly PBW straightening on the open strands after vertex sorting. Crossing an i extremum during comparison of the selected matching drawings is handled by the same finite mate equations above. Hence arbitrary diagrams lie in the stated span.

For all-downward boundaries no open arc can match two glyphs on the same boundary: their orientations require a bottom-to-top match. A matching is therefore a colored permutation, and the selected reduced diagram is κ_w e(ν). Its dot positions give the PBW monomial. The preceding reduction yields the explicit surjection

where b_a are the independent positive i-bubble coefficients at the chosen source region. Reciprocal/fake coefficients have already been eliminated, so no second polynomial ring is asserted.

## Independent generic action and degree separation

The trace helper's flag model gives all generators and the local equations just used. Before truncating its monic i-root relations, the downward action is the generic KLR polynomial realization, with the source crossing factorization that becomes native −π and −Q. PBW gives a distinct leading permutation and nonzero polynomial coefficient for each chosen κ_w. Thus a nonzero finite PBW/bubble combination has a polynomial test vector on which it is nonzero, provided the bubble coefficients are independent.

In that model b_a are the coefficients of the normalized p/c_i² series or its reciprocal. Up to any finite bound D they are a unit triangular polynomial change from the first D elementary symmetric functions in the existing i alphabet, with the spectator symmetric functions and universal monic coefficients as parameters. In particular the first appearance of the new elementary function has coefficient ±1. Taking at least D existing i roots makes these independent. Take spectator alphabets large enough for the finitely many symmetric coefficients used by the test vector. This proves nonzero generic action without invoking a geometric flag variety or a one-dimensional Hom statement.

All defining monic-factor relations after at most L added i flags have internal degree at least 2d_i(N−r−L+1), where r is the initial i-root count and N the total monic i degree. The coefficient variables are nonnegatively graded and all dot degrees are positive. Choose N−r so large that these relation degrees exceed the degrees of the chosen test vector and every term in its nonzero output. The quotient ideal cannot meet those degrees. Therefore that output remains nonzero in the actual finite flag model. For a fixed source region weight μ one can increase r and choose auxiliary framing λ=μ+Σm_jα_j, so N−r=μ_i+r increases too. Only i dominance is required in this minimal-parabolic model; no spectator upward functor is present, so negative auxiliary spectator framing components cause no restriction. This supplies arbitrarily large degree separation for every symmetrizable finite-index datum, including infinite Cartan type.

For inhomogeneous monic field specialization, perform this formal faithfulness calculation first over the universal graded coefficient ring, assigning each coefficient its missing monic-polynomial degree. The later quotient/kernel proof is algebraic and specializes the explicit monic identities; it does not assert that a degree-separation argument survives after forgetting those degrees or infer generic deformation flatness.

Thus the all-downward PBW/bubble spanning family is independent. In particular specializing the far-left coefficient variables alone has exactly the tensor-product effect on the downward sector; it cannot create additional ordinary KLR relations.

## General boundary matching independence

Apply the two already specified formal adjunctions to bend each upward i boundary glyph to the other boundary. These are mutually inverse Hom correspondences by the actual triangles. A chosen perfect matching becomes a matching between all-downward glyphs, hence a permutation. Dots remain assigned to the same connected components by cyclicity; far-left bubbles remain coefficients. If the selected reduced drawing changes, the preceding straightening has its unit leading permutation coefficient and lower crossing corrections. Therefore the finite matching/dot filtration changes to the downward PBW/bubble filtration by a triangular matrix with unit diagonal. The all-downward independence just proved then gives independence of the general matching family. This establishes formal P_i nondegeneracy with its explicit model, before any cyclotomic quotient is taken.
