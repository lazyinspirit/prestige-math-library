## Parameter adaptation for the two-color supplier

Here is the algebra calculation needed to use the direct §6.3.1 route for general Q, rather than the unproved p5 extension sentence. Fix i!=j, a=-a_ij and t the nonzero coefficient of u^a in Q_ij. The one-j simple has its dot acting by0. Homogeneity gives Q_ij(u,0)=t u^a. For a triple i,j,i whose j-dot acts by0, the divided braid polynomial is, up to the fixed presentation sign,

    t sum_(r=0)^(a-1) x_left^r x_right^(a-1-r).

Mixed Q monomials with positive j-exponent vanish. The coefficient t remains invertible over every field. In the nil-Hecke simple of m strands each dot has nilpotence index exactly m: the coinvariant algebra model k[x_1,...,x_m]/(positive symmetric polynomials) has x_s^m=0 and x_s^(m-1)!=0; the latter follows from the monic Artin basis after permuting s to the chosen last coordinate. Its polynomial representation contains a vector on which this power is nonzero.

For m+n<=a, the length-decreasing quadratic terms at either i/j boundary vanish, since x_i^a=0 on a block of size m or n. The divided braid term vanishes since every r has r>=m or a-1-r>=n. Thus the positive-shuffle span in Ind(L(i^m),L(j),L(i^n)) is invariant under dots, idempotents and crossings: dot slides keep j in the same position; far moves preserve positive length; only the boundary square and i,j,i divided braid can remove the last unequal crossing, and their correction terms just vanished. The quotient supported on i^m j i^n is precisely the simple tensor of the two nil-Hecke simples and L(j), hence is simple, with character [m]!_i[n]!_i i^m j i^n. Any simple of ci+j has a nonzero such corner; a simple submodule in that corner induces onto it, so these exhaust the simples for c<=a. The tensor quotient is generated by its defining corner, giving the indicated quotient map. This constructs the small two-color simples without Serre minimality or Gabber–Kac imports.

For m+n=a+1 with m,n>0, the same divided braid polynomial has a single possibly nonzero term on a tensor of top-dot vectors: r=m-1, a-1-r=n-1. Its coefficient is the unit t. Both neighboring unequal crossings belong to the kernel of a hypothetical quotient supported only at i^m j i^n, so the braid difference would belong to that kernel; its correction instead equals t times a nonzero defining-corner vector generating the tensor simple. This forces the whole induced module into the kernel and contradicts its being proper. For m=0 or n=0 the boundary square replaces the braid: t x_i^a acts nontrivially on the block of a+1 strands. Consequently at the critical degree the induction head cannot remain on a single word. Its two-word character, zero-jump equivalence and all larger-degree two-color structure still need the C1 nonreduced head/once properties; this calculation alone does not establish them.

## What is proved once C1–C3 are available

Write bar M=(e_i^L)^c M, c=c_i(M). Define Psi_i(M)=bar M tensor b_i(-c). The map is injective because M=(f_i^L)^c bar M. The Mackey/unique-head argument giving cross-end stripping is local: Ind(L(i^c),bar M,L(j)) surjects onto f_j M. Restrict the first c i-strands. If j!=i only uncrossed i-stripping quotients survive since bar M has no first i. Their quotients are Ind(bar M,L(j)), forcing the stripped simple to be f_j bar M. For j=i there are also Ind(L(i),bar M) quotients, but these cannot map to a first-i-reduced target by reciprocity; the same conclusion follows whenever c_i(f_i M)=c. If c_i(f_i M)=c+1 the zero-jump equivalence gives e_i^L f_i M=M.

The jump identity gives, by first-end symmetry and repeated stripping,

    epsilon_i(M)=max(epsilon_i(bar M),c-<h_i,wt(bar M)>),

where generic wt=-beta. If epsilon_i(bar M)>=c-<h_i,wt(bar M)>, raising preserves c and strips to e_i bar M. If the inequality is strict in the other direction, raising decreases c by1 and strips to bar M. This is exactly the tensor raising rule including its equality convention. Cross colors commute with stripping by the preceding argument; zero raising is checked using the same epsilon formula. Partial inverses then prove the lowering rule: f_j M always exists, and its epsilon/weight identifies the correct tensor branch; the inverse raising branch just checked returns Psi_i(M). Thus Psi_i is strict and preserves weight, epsilon and phi. Every nonvacuum M has a word beginning with some i, so c_i(M)>0 for some i. The elementary-recognition hypotheses are now actually established; C4 identifies the generic graph with B(infinity).

For a cyclotomic survivor M, define actual lowering length p_i^Lambda(M)=max{n:pr_Lambda f_i^n M!=0}. C2 and cross-color invariance show that only c_i matters along this string. The jump rule says c_i is constant through J_i(M) steps and then increases by1 at every further step. Consequently

    p_i^Lambda(M)=J_i(M)+lambda_i-c_i(M)
                 =epsilon_i(M)+lambda_i-<h_i,beta>.

This proves finite normal rank-one lengths and the corrected phi formula, without the false uniqueness assertion. The generic identification carries c_i to the elementary-embedding coordinate epsilon_i^star. C2 and C5 now identify exactly the surviving vertex set and its cut-off arrows with B(Lambda). This is the full intended identification route; C4/C5 are essential.

## A complete comparison lemma replacing abstract recognition C4

There is a smaller recognition contract for this application, whose proof is elementary. Suppose C and D are crystals, each generated from its unique weight-zero vertex by lowering words, every f_i is everywhere nonzero, and each has strict elementary embeddings Psi_i with nonpositive elementary coordinates and a positive coordinate for some i on every nonvacuum vertex. Assume the weight support of a vertex is finite and extraction subtracts a nonnegative multiple of alpha_i from its root weight. Then there is a unique crystal isomorphism C=D carrying the two vacua to each other.

Proof. Fix a finite color set containing the support of a lowering word. Cycle through these colors, applying Psi_i to the current first factor, and leave every elementary tensor factor already extracted in place. Tensoring a strict embedding with identity maps is strict: for each color the tensor max formulas preserve epsilon/phi and weight, hence preserve the branch inequality including equality. Therefore every finite composite is strict and injective. Each extraction lowers the nonnegative height of the first factor by its elementary coordinate c. If an entire cycle makes no extraction from a nonvacuum factor, that factor is unchanged throughout the cycle, yet its support has a color with positive extraction, contradiction. Thus after at most the starting height many cycles (and one harmless final cycle) the first factor is the vacuum. Extraction of absent colors is zero: a nonzero alpha_i extraction would give a residual root weight with negative alpha_i coefficient, forbidden. Consequently this finite procedure works in both crystals using the same finite color cycle.

For a word w of length h, use h+1 cycles. Every prefix has height at most h, so both images of every prefix have vacuum first factor. Strictness computes the image of w by applying that same lowering word to the same tensor of a vacuum and elementary zero vertices. While the underlying vacua belong to different crystals, their weights and epsilon/phi are identical and their raising operators vanish; at each intermediate prefix the first factor is still the vacuum, so a nonzero lowering operation cannot take place in that first factor. All operations occur in the identical elementary factors, and the resulting elementary coordinates are identical. If two words w,w' produce the same vertex in C, use a common finite color set and enough cycles for both lengths; their identical C images give identical coordinate tensors, hence identical D images and, by injectivity, identical D vertices. Interchanging C and D gives the converse. Mapping a lowering word in C to that word in D is therefore a well-defined bijection. It preserves lowering by construction, raising by partial inverses, weights by word content, and epsilon/phi because the common strict finite tensor embeddings preserve these statistics. Uniqueness follows from lowering generation. This proves the lemma.

For the module crystal, lowering generation is proved by repeatedly stripping a nonzero last color, and nonzero generic f_i is C1. For canonical B(infinity) these properties follow from its existing grand-loop crystal theorem. What still must be locally supplied is the existence and positive extraction of canonical strict elementary embeddings; the comparison lemma does not manufacture them. It replaces importing the full Kashiwara–Saito recognition theorem with that exact canonical-embedding supplier. It also removes any need for a general rank-one-normal highest-weight uniqueness assertion.
