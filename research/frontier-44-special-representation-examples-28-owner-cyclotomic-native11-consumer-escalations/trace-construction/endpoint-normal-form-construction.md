## Setup and basic sliding identity

Use G,H,λ in Rouquier's rank-one orientation, G⊣H with unit u and counit v. Thus G raises λ by2. Let NH_m act on G^m; taking mates gives NH_n^op on H^n. Put u_i:1→H^iG^i for the iterated ordinary unit. Define σ_{m,n}:G^mH^n→H^nG^m by sliding one G past one H using the crossing mate σ, recursively σ_{m,0}=σ_{0,n}=id, σ_{m,1}=(σG^{m-1})∘(Gσ_{m-1,1}) and σ_{m,n}=(H^{n-1}σ_{m,1})∘(σ_{m,n-1}H).

The only sliding identity needed is

Hσ_{a+1,b}∘(uG^aH^b) = (T_b...T_1 G^{a+1})∘(H^buG^a)∘σ_{a,b}.

Here T_j are same-color crossings on the H block, interpreted as mates. This follows by expanding σ as (H G^a v)∘(H(T_1...T_a)H)∘(uG^{a-1}H), cancelling the adjacent ordinary unit/counit by its triangle and using T_{j+1}T_1...T_a=T_1...T_aT_j. Induction on b gives the displayed identity; it also proves σ_{m,n} commutes with both finite nil-Coxeter actions. These operations use only the supplied ordinary adjunction and nil-Hecke relations; no second cup/cap appears.

## Explicit coefficient modules and maps

Put r=m-n+λ. For r<0 and 0≤i≤min(m,n), define L_i=L(m,n,i,λ) inside NH_m⊗_{NH_i}NH_n with basis

T_w⊗m_μ(X_{n-i+1},...,X_n)X_{n-i+1}^{i-1}X_{n-i+2}^{i-2}...X_{n-1}T_{w'},

where w ranges over minimal left S_{m-i}-coset representatives in S_m, w' over minimal right S_{n-i}-coset representatives in S_n, and μ ranges over partitions with at most i parts each≤−r-i. The NH_i tensor actions use the final i positions of both blocks. For i=0 set L_0=k; if r=0 set L_i=0 for i>0. PBW and the nonzero staircase corner in the full NH_i matrix algebra prove the listed coefficient vectors independent. Its rank is (m!/(m-i)!)(n!/(n-i)!) binom(−r,i).

Let b_i:G^{m-i}H^{n-i}1_λ→H^nG^m1_λ be (H^{n-i}u_iG^{m-i})∘σ_{m-i,n-i}. The desired comparison is the sum of the action maps

Φ_{m,n,λ}:⊕_i L_i⊗G^{m-i}H^{n-i}1_λ{i(i-2m-λ)d}→H^nG^m1_λ.

The degree follows by adding the known unit/crossing degrees and the staircase exponent, independently of the second adjunction. For r≥0 the dual comparison is obtained by exchanging G,H and replacing λ with−λ through ordinary mate duality; no second adjunction is needed for that operation.

## Why finite nil-Coxeter socles suffice

The finite nil-Coxeter algebra has basis T_w, radical generated by the T_j and socle kT_{w_m}; its pairing T_w,T_{w'}↦coefficient of T_{w_m} in T_wT_{w'} is perfect because it pairs complementary reduced words. A projective module over this finite local algebra is free. For a map between relatively projective modules, isomorphism on T_{w_m} times the modules implies isomorphism: it is isomorphism on tops after the perfect pairing, hence surjective by nilpotence of the radical; its split kernel is projective and has zero socle, so vanishes. The same argument works for the tensor product of two finite nil-Coxeter algebras.

The NH_m matrix presentation established in `rank-one-universal-model.md` makes restriction to finite nil-Coxeter relatively projective. Explicitly, P_m has the finite-nil-Coxeter basis T_w(staircase), with the staircase normalized by (-1)^{m(m-1)/2}; PBW/Artin independence gives a free module over the symmetric coefficients. Thus it suffices to compare Φ after multiplication by Ω=T_{w_m}⊗T_{w_n}. The coefficient modules also have this relative projectivity: multiplying the stated L_i basis by the remaining finite nil-Coxeter algebras produces free coset modules. This is a concrete matrix-algebra test, not a faithfulness inference from K_0.

## Induction and the polynomial filtration

The base cases m=0 or n=0 are identities; m=n=1 is exactly the supplied canonical rho.

For n=1 and m+λ≤0, iterating the canonical rho from left to right gives the comparison with G^mH plus the coefficient module

M=(⊕_{0≤l<−λ}X_1^lT_1...T_{m-1}NH_{m-1})⊕(⊕_{w∈S_{[2,m-1]}\backslash S_{[2,m]},,0≤l<−m-λ}T_wX_m^lNH_{m-1}).

The sliding identity proves that each summand maps by its coefficient action on uG^{m-1}; there are no unspecified extra maps. On the Ω socle, use T_w f T_{w_m}=∂_w(f)T_{w_m} and the divided-difference leading-term calculation to obtain

T_{w_m}M=T_{w_m}Σ_{0≤l≤−λ-m}X_m^lNH_{m-1}=T_{w_m}L(m,1,1,λ)NH_{m-1}.

The high powers from the first family successively supply the missing upper bound, with unit leading coefficients, and the second family supplies the lower powers. Both coefficient modules have m(−λ-m+1) rightNH_{m-1} generators. Thus the change of spanning families is triangular with unit diagonal, and the socle criterion proves Φ when n=1.

For r=0 and n>1, use induction first on (m,n-1,λ-2), then on (m,1,λ). The extra component in the resulting composite has factor T_{n-1} on the H block by the sliding identity. After multiplying by Ω it vanishes, since T_{w_n}T_{n-1}=0. The remaining component is σ_{m,n}; the projective socle criterion proves it invertible, as required when L_i=0 for i>0.

For n>1 and r<0, apply induction to (m,n-1,λ) and then the already-proved n=1 comparison to each term. This gives an isomorphism with recursively generated coefficient modules M_i. More explicitly M_i is the sum of the old L(m,[2,n],i,λ) and the new family

(⊕_{l<−r+n-i}T_{n-i}...T_1 X_1^l k) L(m,[2,n],i-1,λ) (⊕_{1≤j≤m-i}T_j...T_{m-i}k).

Each map is again coefficient action on b_i by the sliding identity. Filter the target by decreasing number i of paired units. On its Ω socle, the old family gives symmetric polynomials m_μ in the final i variables with max exponent≤−r-i-1. The new family is computed by

∂_{n-1}...∂_{n-i+1}(∂_{n-i}...∂_1(X_1^l)m_μ(X_{n-i+2},...,X_n)),

where μ has at most i−1 parts≤−r-i and l<−r+n-i. Expand the inner divided difference in X_{n-i+1} with coefficients symmetric in the preceding variables. For any term of exponent k, the parabolic bound proved by the action helper gives final max exponent≤max(k-i+1,−r-i-1). Therefore all terms with k<−r-1 stay in the old bound. At l=−r+n-i-1, the leading exponent is k=−r-1 with coefficient1; its outer divided difference is

m_{μ∪{−r-i}}(X_{n-i+1},...,X_n)+R,

with max exponent of R≤−r-i-1. These precisely add the partitions whose largest part is the previously missing upper bound−r-i. Moving the symmetric coefficients in preceding variables through σ_{m-i,n-i} is resolved by the smaller (m-i,n-i) normal form; terms with extra paired units land in filtration levels j>i. The parabolic bound shows their final coefficient max exponent≤−r-j, so each lies in the stated L_j family.

Here is the missing equal-rank count explicitly. Put R=−r>0 and K=(m!/(m-i)!)((n-1)!/(n-i)!). The old recursive family has rank K(n-i)binom(R-1,i). The new family has rank K(R+n-i)binom(R-1,i-1): the smaller n−1 coefficient has coset factor m!/(m-i+1)! times (n-1)!/(n-i)!, and its one-strand step has factor (m-i+1)(R+n-i). Their sum is

K[(n-i)binom(R-1,i)+(R+n-i)binom(R-1,i-1)]=Kn binom(R,i),

because i binom(R-1,i)=(R-i)binom(R-1,i-1). This is exactly rank L_i. At i=0 or outside the rectangle use binom(R,j)=0 for j<0 or j>R. The recursive family is treated as its original free direct-sum coefficient module, rather than assuming its written PBW images independent. After extending by the remaining nil-Coxeter actions both source and target are relatively projective of the same rank; the socle ranks agree as well, since both divide by the same finite nil-Coxeter ranks.

Modulo already-handled levels j>i, the old-bound monomials and the unit-leading new-bound monomials span every partition of the rectangle, with an explicitly triangular change of basis. The equal-rank computation shows that this spanning change has no extra multiplicity: its diagonal blocks are square with unit diagonal. Therefore it is an isomorphism on the Ω socle at each level. Descending induction on i and the projective socle criterion give the general Φ. This completes the iterated-rho normal-form argument, including the rank check compressed in the source.

## Endpoint and transfer without asserting End(vacuum)=S

Take m=n=N, λ=−N and a vacuum M with HM=0. Every summand of Φ except i=N contains a positive rightmost H-power and vanishes. For i=N the partition rectangle is P(N,0), hence a singleton; applying the standard NH_N primitive idempotents on both sides reduces the (N!)² matrix coefficient space to k. Thus the divided-power ordinary unit M→H^{(N)}G^{(N)}M is an isomorphism. This is an actual map statement, not quantum-action decategorification. Also HG^{N+1}M=0 by the n=1,r=0 normal form; ordinary adjunction then makes End(G^{N+1}M)=0, so G^{N+1}M=0.

There is a useful stronger comparison avoiding a separate assertion that the universal vacuum endomorphism ring equals the polynomial coefficient ring. In the formal universal vacuum category M is specifically the generating identity1-morphism, and let A=End(M); it is commutative by the interchange law on endomorphisms of that identity1-morphism. This commutativity is not asserted for an arbitrary actual H-annihilated object, such as a direct sum of vacua; those objects are reached only by evaluation of the formal identities. The equal-rank determinant argument below is confined to this formal commutative A. The n=1 normal form identifies HGM with N shifted copies of M, with basis x^ju, 0≤j<N. Write x^Nu uniquely in this basis. Its coefficients give a monic degree-N polynomial a(z) over A. The relation a(x)u=0 implies a(x)GM=0 by the ordinary adjunction Hom(GM,GM)=Hom(M,HGM). Consequently NH_a acts on G^aM through the deformed monic cyclotomic quotient NH_a(a), where a(X_1)=0. Coefficients act centrally by right composition on M.

For m=n=a,λ=−N, the only nonzero summand in the normal form after evaluating on M is i=a. It makes H^aG^aM a direct sum of (a!)²binom(N,a) shifted copies of M. Ordinary adjunction therefore makes End(G^aM) a freeA-module of that rank. Its displayed basis consists of coefficient actions on the iterated unit u_a. Taking mates of the H-block actions turns them into NH_a actions on G^aM, so the NH_a(a) and A action surjects onto this endomorphism algebra. Depending on chronological right-module conventions this is a map from NH_a(a)^op; retain that harmless opposite throughout the comparison.

The universal model in `rank-one-universal-model.md`, specialized along S→A to the coefficients of a(z), identifies NH_a(a) with H_a⊗_S A. This is freeA of the same rank. The surjection is therefore an isomorphism: its matrix between free modules of equal rank has a right inverse, hence unit determinant over commutative A. The n=1 normal form keeps the additive idempotent-complete subcategory generated by the G^aM closed under H; G sends its regular generator to the next regular generator. Thus these endomorphism isomorphisms give an equivalence of this extremal string with the universal H_a model after coefficient basechange to A. The ordinary unit/counit match by their adjunction characterization, and x/crossings match by construction. Since rho and its inverse match, all four candidate second maps match the explicit model maps, so both triangle composites are invertible on every G^aM.

Any actual lowest object in the representation is obtained by evaluating the formal vacuum category; evaluation preserves these identities and inverses. The integrable detection proof in `candidate-maps-and-rank-one-detection.md` then gives an actual second adjunction everywhere, after the triangle normalization described there. The supplied graded dictionary restores E_i{t_i(β)}⊣F_i⊣E_i with t_i(β)=−2d_i(〈h_i,Λ−β〉−1). This closes the rank-one second-adjunction route independently of a generic cyclotomic Frobenius theorem. Different-color crossing-mates remain a separate supplier calculation.
