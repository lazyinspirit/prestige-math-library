## Scope and unconditional quotient lemma

Let A=R(β) with the existing graded KLR conventions over a degree-zero field k, and let B=A/J be a finite-dimensional homogeneous quotient with generator-fixing anti-involution ψ preserving J. The standard cyclotomic quotient has J generated by x₁^{λν₁}e(ν). Arbitrary monic polynomials with lower terms over a degree-zero field define an ungraded deformation; the graded statements below do not assert that such a deformation is homogeneous.

The generic native10 projective-cover/maximal-tail and duality suppliers give a finite graded projective cover P(S) for every finite-dimensional graded simple A-module S, absolute End(S)=k, and the normalized involutions P↦P#=Hom_A(P,A)^ψ and S↦DψS. They are genuine existing inputs, rather than conclusions inferred from the cyclotomic basis theorem. In this paragraph no geometric scope restriction is used.

1. For a finite graded projective P, B⊗_A P is finite graded projective: tensor a finite graded free-module retract. Every homogeneous endomorphism of B⊗P lifts to an endomorphism of P. Indeed compose it with P→B⊗P and use projectivity of P to lift through that surjection. Consequently End_A^0(P)→End_B^0(B⊗P) is surjective. For indecomposable P its domain is local, so a nonzero quotient module has local degree-zero endomorphism ring and is indecomposable. This conclusion concerns indecomposable P; it does not hold for an arbitrary projective.

2. If JS=0, the map P(S)→S factors through P(S)/JP(S). Its kernel remains superfluous: the preimage of any graded submodule whose image together with that kernel is the whole quotient, together with the old cover kernel, is all P(S), hence the preimage is all P(S). Thus the quotient is the B-projective cover of S. If JS≠0 and P(S)/JP(S) were nonzero, it would have a graded simple quotient T, by finite dimensionality. The composite P(S)→T implies T is a shift of S, since the original cover has only that simple head. But a B-simple is annihilated by J, contradiction. Thus P(S)/JP(S)=0. Conversely every graded B-simple is an inflated A-simple, and step 2 supplies its projective cover. Therefore the extension map on projective Grothendieck groups is surjective, sends each generic indecomposable class to its same-head cover or zero, and never merges two distinct surviving simple labels (up to shifts).

3. Finite projective evaluation gives the natural isomorphism
   Hom_B(B⊗_A P,B) ≅ Hom_A(P,A)⊗_A B.
   Prove it for a shifted free A-module by evaluation at its generators, and pass to retracts. Applying ψ to the right modules gives
   (B⊗_A P)# ≅ B⊗_A P#.
   The vector dual Dψ of an inflated finite B-module is exactly its inflated A-vector dual. Neither assertion requires B to be Frobenius or a second adjunction.

4. Transport generic normalized P#≅P and DψS≅S through steps 2–3. Write h(P,S)=gdim Hom_B(P,S), first-bar sesquilinear, and T(P,S)=gdim(P^ψ⊗_B S), bilinear. Finite projective evaluation proves h(P,S)=T(P#,S) and D(P^ψ⊗S)≅Hom_B(P,DψS). For a normalized cover and simple, h(P,S)=q^a for a unique integer a, since End(S)=k. These identities and both self-dualities give q^{-a}=q^a, hence a=0. Thus normalized covers and normalized simples pair by δ under both h and T. For a different simple the Hom group is zero. This distinguishes projective Hom duality from vector duality; a vector-self-dual shifted regular module is not automatically the canonical projective normalization.

This four-step lemma is independent of the cyclotomic crystal-recognition theorem and the symmetric trace theorem. It must precede the vacuum, based-quotient, and lower/upper basis consumers.
