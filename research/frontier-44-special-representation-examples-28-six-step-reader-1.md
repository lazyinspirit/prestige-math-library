### Elementary argument resolving the real-power uncertainty

Let a>1 and let E_a(z) be the supremum in the remark. Its values are positive and finite by the density, monotonicity and completeness arguments already written there. For any two nonempty bounded positive sets U,V, sup(UV)=sup(U)sup(V) follows without limits: the product of the suprema is an upper bound; conversely, if L bounds UV, then L/v bounds U for each v in V, so sup(U) v<=L; dividing by the positive sup(U) and taking the least upper bound over V yields sup(U)sup(V)<=L. This is also the complete argument in the MIT handout, p. 2.

Set A=E_a(x), B=E_a(y), C=E_a(x+y). Every product a^s a^t with rational s<=x and t<=y equals a^(s+t) and belongs to the defining family for C. The supremum-product fact therefore gives AB<=C.

Conversely, if rational r<x+y, density gives rational s in (r-y,x), and t=r-s is rational with t<y. Then a^r=a^s a^t<=AB. The only remaining case is a rational r=x+y. For natural n>=1, put h=a^(1/n)-1>=0. Integer Bernoulli gives a=(1+h)^n>=1+nh, hence a^(1/n)<=1+(a-1)/n. Since r-1/n<x+y, the strict-case argument gives a^(r-1/n)<=AB; multiplying by a^(1/n)>0 yields a^r<=AB(1+(a-1)/n). If a^r>AB, the Archimedean property supplies n>AB(a-1)/(a^r-AB), making the displayed upper bound strictly smaller than a^r, a contradiction. Thus every rational r<=x+y has a^r<=AB, so C<=AB. Therefore E_a(x+y)=AB. Reciprocal bases give the corresponding result for 0<a<1, and a=1 is immediate. This argument quantifies over finite choices of n; it invokes no notion of sequence convergence, derivative, series, exponential or continuity.
