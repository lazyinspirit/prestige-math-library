## Repairs and mathematical evidence

### Separate inner parity

The original IA statement and proof steps 2.1/3.1 silently replaced an odd
scalar factor by an even tensor unit. The defined category has separate
modules M0/M1 and parity-preserving morphisms; its internal shifts are module
shifts, so they do not perform this replacement. The canceled curl leaves
the odd scalar summand with internal shift `{-1,1}` in outer degree 1.
Thus the categorical relation is

$$C(D_1)\simeq\Pi C(D_2)\{1,1\}[1].$$

The base definition now defines the unsigned parity reversal Pi and states
the homotopy equation explicitly. The kink item retains it in every relevant
formula. This corrects a false categorical claim in the stated convention,
not a change to the Euler normalization. Termwise cohomology with parity
labels forgotten has the original three grading shifts.

The Markov theorem proof now accumulates `Pi^nu` along the move sequence and
forgets it at CH/H. For the published normalization, put `r=(s-e)/2`:
positive stabilization leaves r fixed, negative stabilization raises r by 1,
and braid relations/IIa cancellation/conjugation preserve r. Consequently
`Pi^{floor(r)} C_pub` is invariant in the explicit parity convention, while
the parity-forgotten `CH_pub` is invariant with the stated published grading
correction. The original claim that internal/outer shifts alone made the
explicit-parity `C_pub` invariant was overstrong and is repaired. The Euler
formula uses grading k rather than a separate parity label and is unchanged.

Evidence: [KR II v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0505056v2), subsection 4,
printed pp. 20–21, gives the surviving odd-row calculation. The
[published version](https://msp.org/gt/2008/12-3/gt-v12-n3-p04-p.pdf), printed
p. 1391, explicitly suppresses separate Z2 bookkeeping by relating it to the
first internal degree. Its formulas (12)–(14), printed pp. 1393–1395, give
the half-integer regrading. This suppressed convention was missing from the
original translation into the library's explicit-parity category.
