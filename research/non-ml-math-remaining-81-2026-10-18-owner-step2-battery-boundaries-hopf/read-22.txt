## Statement

Assume the inherited Axiom of Choice. Use the finite simply-laced full weight datum $P$, root lattice $Q$, positive roots $\Phi^+$ and root normalization $(\alpha,\alpha)=2$. Let $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta^2)=\ell$ be odd and greater than the Coxeter number. Both $\operatorname{ord}(\zeta)=\ell$ and $2\ell$ are allowed. Work in the full toral-weight Lusztig category of [[def-root-of-unity-weyl-and-dual-weyl-module-families]].

Define $\eta\preceq_\ell\lambda$ to mean that $\eta+\rho$ is obtained from $\lambda+\rho$ by a finite sequence, possibly empty, of reflections
$$
r_{\alpha,k}(x)=x-((x,\alpha)-k\ell)\alpha,\qquad \alpha\in\Phi^+,\quad k\in\mathbb Z,
$$
where each step is strictly downward: $(x,\alpha)>k\ell$. Intermediate labels need not be dominant. Every composition label $\eta$ of $\Delta_\zeta(\lambda)$ or $\nabla_\zeta(\lambda)$ satisfies $\eta\preceq_\ell\lambda$. Every Weyl section and every composition label of the indecomposable tilting $T_\zeta(\lambda)$ also satisfies $\eta\preceq_\ell\lambda$.


## Proof

**Given:** These data. All small modules and their composition factors below belong to the category of finite dimensional formally $P$-graded $u$-modules; repeated $K$ eigenvalues do not identify their labels. Put $[a]=(\zeta^a-\zeta^{-a})/(\zeta-\zeta^{-1})$, define $Z(\kappa)=u\otimes_{u^0u^+}\mathbb C_\kappa$, and write $Z^w(\kappa)$ for its transformed-Borel versions.

1.1 Put $R=u^-$ and $H=R\rtimes\mathbb C[T]$. This is an actual finite Hopf algebra: $\Delta(F_i)=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i$, the torus is group-like, and the antipode preserves this Borel. The odd-order type-1 toral quotient is a Hopf quotient because the $K_i^\ell-1$ generate a Hopf ideal. The positive-height ideal $R_+$ is nilpotent, and $J=R_+H=HR_+$ is nilpotent with quotient $\mathbb C[T]$. This quotient is semisimple: its commuting toral operators diagonalize simultaneously, since their finite-order minimal polynomials divide separable polynomials $X^d-1$. A nilpotent ideal annihilates every simple module, and a semisimple quotient has zero radical; hence $J=\operatorname{rad}H$ and $R_+=\operatorname{rad}R$. [F1, algebra]

1.2 In rank one of highest coordinate $m=(\kappa,\alpha)$, take $v_j=F^{(j)}v_0$ for $0\le j<\ell$; in the opposite module of lowest coordinate $m'=m-2(\ell-1)$ take $v'_j=E^{(j)}v'_0$. Their actions are $Fv_j=[j+1]v_{j+1}$, $Ev_j=[m-j+1]v_{j-1}$, $Ev'_j=[j+1]v'_{j+1}$ and $Fv'_j=-[m'+j-1]v'_{j-1}$, with out-of-range vectors zero. The map $v_j\mapsto c_jv'_{\ell-1-j}$, where $c_0=1$ and $c_{j+1}=-[m-\ell-j]c_j/[j+1]$, intertwines these actions. For $E$ compatibility use $[\ell-j]=-s[j]$ and $[a-\ell]=s[a]$, where $s=\zeta^\ell\in\{1,-1\}$; $F$ compatibility is its defining recurrence, and the endpoint and toral relations follow from the displayed actions and weights. All denominators are nonzero. If $1\le n\le\ell$ is congruent to $m+1$ modulo $\ell$, then $c_j\ne0$ exactly for $j<n$. The kernel is zero for $n=\ell$ and otherwise is spanned by $v_n,\ldots,v_{\ell-1}$, generated by the singular vector $F^{(n)}v_0$. It is an image of the baby Verma of label $\kappa-n\alpha$: the $i$-th positive generator kills that vector because $[m-n+1]=0$, and the other simple positive generators commute with $F_i$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 By [F2], choose a Frobenius functional $f$ on $H$. Then $b\mapsto[a\mapsto f(ab)]$ identifies the left regular module $H$ with $H^*$ under $(h\phi)(a)=\phi(ah)$. The socle of $H^*$ is the annihilator of $J$, of dimension $\dim(H/J)=|T|$. On the other hand $\operatorname{soc}_H H=\{h:Jh=0\}=\{h:R_+h=0\}=\operatorname{soc}_R H$, since $J=HR_+$. As a left $R$-module, $H=\bigoplus_{t\in T}RK_t$, so $|T|\dim\operatorname{soc}_R R=|T|$. Thus $R$ has one-dimensional regular socle, spanned by its nonzero maximum-height PBW monomial; every positive-height factor annihilates it. This transports to braid-transformed halves and to $u^+$ by algebra isomorphisms; transformed Borels need not be Hopf subalgebras for the original coproduct. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Free right induction from the minimal parabolic in [F1] transports this exact rank-one kernel calculation. For adjacent Borels $w,ws_i$, $\beta=w\alpha_i$ and $w$-highest label $\kappa$, it gives a homomorphism $Z^w(\kappa)\to Z^{ws_i}(\kappa-(\ell-1)\beta)$. If $1\le n\le\ell$ is congruent to $(\kappa+w\rho,\beta)$ modulo $\ell$, its kernel is zero for $n=\ell$ and otherwise is an image of $Z^w(\kappa-n\beta)$. This is exact induction of an actual module map; it is not deduced from character equality. [F1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Each baby Verma is regular over its transformed negative half. Every nonzero submodule therefore contains its lowest PBW line by step 2.1, and that line generates its simple socle. Also $Z(\kappa)$ has a unique simple head $L_u(\kappa)$: the sum of all submodules missing its one-dimensional highest line still misses that line and is the unique maximal submodule. Every finite graded simple has such a highest label, by taking a maximal label in its finite support. Their formal characters are linearly independent, since a maximal highest label in a finite relation occurs with coefficient one only in its own simple character. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Choose a reduced expression $w_0=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_N}$, write $w_j=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}$ and $\beta_j=w_j\alpha_{i_j}>0$, and put $\kappa(w)=\kappa+(\ell-1)(w\rho-\rho)$. Compose step 2.2 along these successive Borels from $Z(\kappa)$ to $Z^{w_0}(\kappa-2(\ell-1)\rho)$. Every module in this chain has the character of $Z(\kappa)$: the transformed root-product character changes by $e^{(\ell-1)(\rho-w\rho)}$, canceled by its label shift. Each map is nonzero on the unique label-$\kappa$ line. Indeed in its source that line has exponent $\ell-1$ along already inverted roots and zero along not-yet-inverted roots. The current positive root $\beta_j$ is not yet inverted, so its rank-one exponent is zero and its image coefficient is $c_0=1$; the complementary free parabolic basis makes the resulting vector nonzero. [F1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The composite image is generated by its label-$\kappa$ line. In the opposite-Borel target this is the maximum-height line of its regular $u^+$ representation, so step 2.1 makes its $u$-span the simple socle. Thus the composite image is the simple head $L_u(\kappa)$ of its source. Every other composition factor $L_u(\eta)$ of $Z(\kappa)$ consequently occurs in its kernel. The successive-image filtration of the kernel of a finite composite places it among the composition factors of one of the individual kernels. Step 2.2 makes that kernel an image of $Z^{w_j}(\kappa(w_j)-n_j\beta_j)$ with $1\le n_j<\ell$ and $n_j\equiv(\kappa+\rho,\beta_j)\pmod\ell$, because $\kappa(w_j)+w_j\rho-(\kappa+\rho)=\ell(w_j\rho-\rho)$. This transformed baby Verma has the character of $Z(\kappa-n_j\beta_j)$; independence of simple characters in step 3.1 transfers the composition factor to that ordinary baby Verma. Repeat: the nonnegative root height of the current highest label minus $\eta$ strictly decreases, so the procedure terminates. Each change $\kappa\mapsto\kappa-n_j\beta_j$ is a strict downward affine reflection, in the level $k\ell=(\kappa+\rho,\beta_j)-n_j$. Hence every baby-Verma composition label satisfies $\eta\preceq_\ell\kappa$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, induction]

4.2 Let $p(\beta)$ denote the Kostant partition numbers, defined by $\sum_{\beta\in Q_+}p(\beta)e^{-\beta}=\prod_{\alpha>0}(1-e^{-\alpha})^{-1}$. The baby PBW character and the actual Weyl formula in [F3] give $\operatorname{ch}Z(\kappa)=e^\kappa\prod_{\alpha>0}\frac{1-e^{-\ell\alpha}}{1-e^{-\alpha}},\qquad \operatorname{ch}\Delta_\zeta(\lambda)=\sum_{w\in W,\,\beta\in Q_+}\det(w)p(\beta)\operatorname{ch}Z(w\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda-\ell\beta).$ The second identity follows by multiplying the finite Weyl numerator by $\prod_{\alpha>0}(1-e^{-\ell\alpha})^{-1}$. It holds in the completion on $\lambda-Q_+$: $w\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda\le\lambda$, and only finitely many $\beta$ contribute to a fixed label. Simple graded characters are a unitriangular basis in that completion: recursively subtract their highest coefficients by increasing root height; every bounded-height interval has finitely many lattice labels, so the recursion is well defined and unique. In particular the coefficient of a fixed simple character on the right is a finite signed sum, since its contributing highest labels must satisfy $w\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda-\ell\beta\ge\eta$. [F1, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 If $L_\zeta(\eta)$ is a composition factor of $\Delta_\zeta(\lambda)$, restriction to $u$ is exact and its highest vector generates a baby-Verma quotient with simple head $L_u(\eta)$. Thus $L_u(\eta)$ has positive multiplicity in the actual restricted Weyl module. The finite signed character coefficient in step 4.2 must therefore have some nonzero baby multiplicity, giving $\eta\preceq_\ell w\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda-\ell\beta$ by step 4.1. This uses virtual characters only to locate a composition factor, not to assert a module filtration. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 For every $x$ and positive root $\alpha$, the translation $x\mapsto x-\ell\alpha$ is a downward chain. Write $(x,\alpha)=k\ell+r$, $0\le r<\ell$. If $r>0$, reflect in levels $k\ell$ and $(k-1)\ell$, subtracting respectively $r\alpha$ and $(\ell-r)\alpha$; if $r=0$, reflect directly in level $(k-1)\ell$, subtracting $\ell\alpha$. For dominant $\lambda$, $w\mathbin{\cdot}\lambda\preceq_\ell\lambda$ as well: build a reduced word by adjoining simple reflections on the left. At a length-increasing step $s_i v$, $v^{-1}\alpha_i$ is positive, so $(v(\lambda+\rho),\alpha_i)>0$ and the level-zero reflection is downward. Apply the negative translations to the positive-root summands of $\beta$. Thus step 5.1 proves $\eta\preceq_\ell\lambda$ for Weyl composition factors. The isometry $-w_0$ preserves positive roots and these downward chains; exact duality in [F3] proves the same assertion for $\nabla_\zeta(\lambda)$. [F3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Work in the finite dominance ideal $\Gamma=\{\mu\in P^+:\mu\le\lambda\}$ and put $S=\{\mu\in\Gamma:\mu\preceq_\ell\lambda\}$. Reciprocity in [F4] and step 6.1 show that every Weyl section $\Delta_\zeta(\nu)$ of the projective kernel $K_\mu$ has $\mu\prec_\ell\nu$. Suppose $X$ has all composition labels in $S$ and $\mu\notin S$. Such a section label $\nu$ cannot belong to $S$, since transitivity would give $\mu\preceq_\ell\lambda$. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta_\zeta(\nu),X)=0$: a nonzero image is a highest quotient with simple head $L_\zeta(\nu)$, absent from $X$. Induction along the kernel flag gives $\operatorname{Hom}(K_\mu,X)=0$. The projective sequence consequently gives $\operatorname{Ext}^1(\Delta_\zeta(\mu),X)=0$. [F4, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 The actual finite universal-extension construction in [F4] starts with $X=\Delta_\zeta(\lambda)$, whose composition labels lie in $S$ by step 6.1. Step 7.1 shows that it never adds a Weyl quotient with label outside $S$. Adding a section with label in $S$ preserves this composition support by step 6.1 and transitivity. The final tilting and its indecomposable highest-label-$\lambda$ summand therefore have all Weyl labels in $S$, using summand closure in [F4]. Their composition labels also lie in $S$ by the Weyl result already proved. This proves every assertion, for both possible orders of $\zeta$ and without primality of $\ell$; the only scalar zeros used were $[a]=0$ exactly when $\ell\mid a$. [F4, A1, step 6.1, step 7.1, induction] ∎
