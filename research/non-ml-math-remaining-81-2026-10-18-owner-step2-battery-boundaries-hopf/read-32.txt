## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$ and let $\theta:A\to k$ be a unital homomorphism to a field. Set $\mathfrak p=\ker\theta$, $k_0=\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p)\subseteq k$, and let $R$ be the maximal-adic completion of $A_{\mathfrak p}$. Then $R$ is a complete separated Noetherian local domain with residue field $k_0$, and $A\to R$ is injective. Thus $\mathbb Q(v)$ embeds in $F=\operatorname{Frac}R$, and $A\to R\to k_0\to k$ equals $\theta$.

Every finite projective $R$-module is free, with equal fraction-field and residue-field dimensions. In every finite associative unital $R$-algebra $C$, every idempotent of $C/\mathfrak m_R C$ lifts to an idempotent of $C$. For any right $C$-module, multiplication by such an idempotent is an exact projection commuting with scalar extension. In particular, a filtration with finite free layers remains exact on its projected layers after passing to $F$ or $k_0$. If $J_R=\bigoplus_E M_{d_E}(R)$, every $R$-finite-free $J_R$-module has the same simple multiplicities in its generic and residue fibres.


## Proof

1.1 Put $B=A_{\mathfrak p}$. It is a nonzero Noetherian local domain by [F1]. Its residue is $\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p)$: modulo $\mathfrak p$, the inverted elements are exactly the nonzero elements of $A/\mathfrak p$. This identifies that field with $k_0\subseteq k$. Localizing a domain is injective. The elementary prime correspondence used below follows directly from fractions: a prime disjoint from the denominators extends to a prime, and contraction recovers it because a fraction belongs to the extended ideal exactly when its numerator does. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 If $\mathfrak p\cap\mathbb Z=0$, every nonzero integer is a unit in $B$, so fraction arithmetic identifies $B$ with a prime localization of $\mathbb Q[v,v^{-1}]$. The latter is a PID: ideals in its localization are generated by the image of a generator of their contracted ideal in $\mathbb Q[v]$. Thus $B$ is a field or has a nonzero principal maximal ideal $(a)$. In the second case [F2] and the chain $(0)\subsetneq(a)$ give dimension one. The class of $a$ modulo $(a)^2$ is nonzero, since $a=a^2b$ in the domain would imply $1=ab$ with $a$ a nonunit. Hence its embedding dimension is one. In the field case both dimensions are zero. Thus $B$ is regular in both cases. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Otherwise $\mathfrak p\cap\mathbb Z=(\ell)$ for a prime integer $\ell$. The prime $\mathfrak p/(\ell)$ in the PID $\mathbb F_\ell[v,v^{-1}]$ is zero or $(f)$ for an irreducible Laurent polynomial $f$. If it is zero, $\mathfrak p=(\ell)$ and the principal-maximal-ideal argument of step 2.1 proves regularity. Otherwise choose a Laurent lift $G$ of $f$; then the maximal ideal of $B$ is $\mathfrak m=(\ell,G)$. The chain $(0)\subsetneq(\ell)B\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ and [F2] give $\dim B=2$. The quotient $B/(\ell)$ is the domain $\mathbb F_\ell[v,v^{-1}]_{(f)}$. If $\ell=r\ell^2+s\ell G+tG^2$ in $B$, reduction modulo $\ell$ gives $\bar t f^2=0$, hence $t=\ell t'$. Cancellation of $\ell$ then gives $1=r\ell+sG+t'G^2\in\mathfrak m$, impossible. Thus $\ell\notin\mathfrak m^2$. If $a\ell+bG\in\mathfrak m^2$, reducing modulo $\ell$ gives $\bar b f\in(f^2)$, so $b\in\mathfrak m$. It follows that $a\ell\in\mathfrak m^2$, and $\ell\notin\mathfrak m^2$ forces $a\in\mathfrak m$, since otherwise $a$ is a unit. The two displayed generators therefore give a basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$; its dimension is two. Thus $B$ is regular in this final case. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 By [F3], $R=\widehat B$ is complete separated Noetherian local with residue $k_0$, is regular, and is a domain. Faithful flatness makes $B\to R$ injective: if $b$ maps to zero, flat tensoring the inclusion $bB\hookrightarrow B$ gives an injection $bB\otimes_B R\hookrightarrow R$ whose image is zero; faithfulness forces $bB=0$, so $b=0$. Therefore $A\hookrightarrow R$ induces $\mathbb Q(v)\hookrightarrow F$, with the required residue map. If $\mathfrak p=0$, this also covers the field $B=R=\mathbb Q(v)$ with zero maximal ideal. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For a finite projective module $P$ over any local ring, lift a basis of $P/\mathfrak mP$ to $r$ elements of $P$. Nakayama on the finite cokernel makes $R^r\to P$ surjective. Projectivity splits it as $R^r=P\oplus Q$, with $Q$ finite. Reduction of this direct sum shows $Q/\mathfrak mQ=0$, so Nakayama gives $Q=0$. Hence $P$ is free of rank $r$. Over our domain this rank equals both $\dim_F(F\otimes_RP)$ and $\dim_{k_0}(k_0\otimes_RP)$. [F4, step 4.1, choose, algebra]

5.2 Let $x_0\in C$ lift an idempotent modulo $\mathfrak m_RC$. Define $\delta_i=x_i^2-x_i$ and $x_{i+1}=x_i+(1-2x_i)\delta_i$. All factors here are polynomials in $x_i$, so integer-polynomial expansion gives $\delta_{i+1}=\delta_i^2(4\delta_i-3)$. Thus $\delta_i\in\mathfrak m_R^{2^i}C$ and $x_{i+1}-x_i\in\mathfrak m_R^{2^i}C$. The finite-module completeness in [F4] gives a limit $q\equiv x_0$ modulo $\mathfrak m_RC$. Multiplication is continuous, since these ideal powers are two-sided ideals, so separatedness gives $q^2=q$. This construction involves no division and works in every characteristic. [F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 For any right $C$-module $X$, the $R$-linear decomposition $X=Xq\oplus X(1-q)$ shows that projection $X\mapsto Xq$ commutes with every scalar extension. It is exact: under a surjection $X\to Y$, lift $y\in Y$ to $x$, and then $xq$ lifts $yq$; kernels and injections follow by restriction of the same projection. If $0\to I_{i+1}\to I_i\to B_i\to0$ is a filtration sequence, projection gives $0\to I_{i+1}q\to I_iq\to B_iq\to0$. When $B_i$ is finite free, $B_iq$ is finite projective and hence free by step 5.1; the sequence splits as $R$-modules. It therefore retains its exact layer after scalar extension, including reduction to $k_0$. [step 5.1, step 5.2, algebra]

7.1 Finally let $J_R=\bigoplus_E M_{d_E}(R)$ act on an $R$-finite-free module $X$. With matrix units $e^E_{ij}$, put $Q_E=e^E_{11}X$. It is a direct summand of $X$, hence finite free by step 5.1. The map $R^{d_E}\otimes_RQ_E\to e_EX$ sending $u_i\otimes x$ to $e^E_{i1}x$ has inverse $y\mapsto\sum_i u_i\otimes e^E_{1i}y$, by the matrix-unit identities. Therefore the generic and residue multiplicities of the $E$-standard simple are both $\operatorname{rank}_RQ_E$. This proves the final assertion and completes the lemma. [step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
