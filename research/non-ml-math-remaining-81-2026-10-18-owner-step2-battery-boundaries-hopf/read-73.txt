## Statement

Let $Y=\pi T_1$ be the fundamental coweight translation in the polynomial representation of [[def-daha-a1-polynomial-representation]]. On the symmetric Laurent polynomials $V$, the restriction of $Y+Y^{-1}$ is the operator $\mathcal D$ of [[def-daha-a1-symmetric-macdonald-operator]]. Here Weyl-invariant means fixed by the involution $Y\mapsto Y^{-1}$ induced by the rank-one finite reflection. Every such Laurent polynomial in $Y$ restricts to a polynomial in $\mathcal D$. Thus the symmetric rank-one DAHA translations give a commuting family of difference operators on $V$.


## Proof

1.1 Let $f\in V$. Since $\sigma_1f=f$, the divided-difference term in [F1] vanishes and $T_1f=tf$. Since $f$ is symmetric, $(\pi f)(z)=f(q^{-1}z^{-1})=f(qz)$. Hence $(Yf)(z)=t f(qz)$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Write $c=t-t^{-1}$ and $T_1=a(z)\sigma_1+b(z)$, where $a(z)=(t^{-1}z^2-t)/(z^2-1)$ and $b(z)=cz^2/(z^2-1)$. By [F4], $T_1^{-1}=T_1-c$, so $Y^{-1}=T_1^{-1}\pi$. For $f\in V$, $\sigma_1\pi f(z)=f(q^{-1}z)$ and $\pi f(z)=f(qz)$, giving $(Y^{-1}f)(z)=\frac{t^{-1}z^2-t}{z^2-1}f(q^{-1}z)+\frac{c}{z^2-1}f(qz)$. Adding the formula from step 1.1 yields exactly [F2], so $(Y+Y^{-1})|_V=\mathcal D$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The $Y$-Laurent algebra is commutative because it is generated by the single invertible element $Y$. By [F5], the finite reflection negates the rank-one translation exponent, so it acts by $Y\mapsto Y^{-1}$. If $p(Y)=\sum_{k=-N}^{N}a_kY^k$ is invariant under this involution, coefficient comparison gives $a_k=a_{-k}$, hence $p(Y)=a_0+\sum_{k=1}^{N}a_k(Y^k+Y^{-k})$. The elements $S_n=Y^n+Y^{-n}$ satisfy $S_0=2$, $S_1=Y+Y^{-1}$, and $S_{n+1}=(Y+Y^{-1})S_n-S_{n-1}$ by multiplication; induction therefore expresses each $S_n$ as a polynomial in $Y+Y^{-1}$. By [F3], $Y+Y^{-1}$ preserves $V$, and by step 2.1 its restriction is $\mathcal D$. Thus every invariant $p(Y)$ restricts to the corresponding polynomial $p_0(\mathcal D)$, and these restrictions commute. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
