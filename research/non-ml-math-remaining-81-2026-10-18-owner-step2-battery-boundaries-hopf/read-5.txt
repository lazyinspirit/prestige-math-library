## Statement

Let $z$ be a primitive root of odd order $\ell>1$. Write $N=\ell N_1+N_0$ for $N\in\mathbb Z$ and $r=\ell r_1+r_0$ for $r\ge0$, with $0\le N_0,r_0<\ell$. The symmetric quantum binomial, interpreted by its Laurent-polynomial product for negative upper index, satisfies
$$\begin{bmatrix}N\\r\end{bmatrix}_z=\binom{N_1}{r_1}\begin{bmatrix}N_0\\r_0\end{bmatrix}_z.$$
For a specialized family $X^{(n)}$ with the divided-power multiplication of [[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]],
$$X^{(a\ell+r)}=\frac{(X^{(\ell)})^aX^r}{a![r]_z!}\qquad(a\ge0,\ 0\le r<\ell).$$
In particular the family is generated by $X$ and $X^{(\ell)}$; modulo the ideal generated by $X$, a divided power vanishes unless its index is divisible by $\ell$, and $X^{(a\ell)}=(X^{(\ell)})^a/a!$.


## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 For an indeterminate $Q$ put $P_N(t)=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}(1+Q^jt)$ for $N\ge0$ and $P_N(t)=\prod_{j=N}^{-1}(1+Q^jt)^{-1}$ for $N<0$, with inverses expanded as formal power series. Define $G(N,r;Q)$ by $P_N(t)=\sum_{r\ge0}Q^{r(r-1)/2}G(N,r;Q)t^r$. Multiplying the last factor gives the Gaussian Pascal recurrence, so for $N\ge0$ this is the Gaussian of [F1]. For $N<0$, the same recurrence downward from $N=0$ agrees with the generalized Gaussian product; each fixed coefficient uses finitely many terms of finitely many geometric series and is a Laurent polynomial in $Q$. Thus evaluation at a root of unity is legitimate, including negative $N$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Set $Q=z^2$, which has exact order $\ell$. Since $\ell$ is odd, $\prod_{j=0}^{\ell-1}(1+Q^jt)=1+t^\ell$. Periodicity of the factors gives $P_N(t)=(1+t^\ell)^{N_1}P_{N_0}(t)$, also for $N_1<0$ by formal inversion. The coefficient of $t^r$ is $\binom{N_1}{r_1}Q^{r_0(r_0-1)/2}G(N_0,r_0;Q)$: the second factor has degree less than $\ell$, so its only eligible degree is $r_0$. The difference $r(r-1)/2-r_0(r_0-1)/2$ is divisible by $\ell$, because $r-r_0$ is divisible by the odd integer $\ell$. Consequently $G(N,r;Q)=\binom{N_1}{r_1}G(N_0,r_0;Q)$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In the symmetric conversion of [F1], the difference $r(N-r)-r_0(N_0-r_0)$ is divisible by $\ell$. Therefore the factors $z^{-r(N-r)}$ and $z^{-r_0(N_0-r_0)}$ agree, proving the displayed quantum Lucas identity. For $0<r<\ell$, $[r]_z\ne0$, since otherwise $z^{2r}=1$ would contradict the exact order of $z^2$. Hence $[r]_z!\ne0$ for $r<\ell$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Lucas gives $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a\ell+r\\r\end{smallmatrix}\right]_z=1$ and $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}(a+1)\ell\\\ell\end{smallmatrix}\right]_z=a+1$. Apply [F2] first repeatedly to $X^{(\ell)}$, obtaining $(X^{(\ell)})^a=a!X^{(a\ell)}$, and then to $X^{(a\ell)}X^{(r)}$, obtaining $X^{(a\ell+r)}=X^{(a\ell)}X^{(r)}$. Finally $X^{(r)}=X^r/[r]_z!$ for $r<\ell$. These prove the generation and quotient assertions, including $r=0$ and $a=0$, using only finite computations. [F2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
