## Statement

Let $G$ be the group of substitutions of $K[z,z^{-1}]$ generated by $s_0,s_1,\pi$ from [[def-daha-a1-root-datum-and-parameter-conventions]]. If $g_1,\ldots,g_N\in G$ are distinct and $f_1,\ldots,f_N\in K(z)$ satisfy

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N} f_j(z)\,g_j(h)(z)=0\qquad\text{for every }h\in K[z,z^{-1}],
$$

then every $f_j$ is zero. This holds over $K=\mathbb Q(q,t)$ because $q$ is indeterminate; it is not asserted when distinct translations collapse at a root of unity.


## Proof

1.1 Write $\gamma_j=g_j(z)\in K(z)$. Distinct elements of $G$ give distinct $\gamma_j$: if the signs differ, one is a nonzero scalar multiple of $z$ and the other of $z^{-1}$; if the signs agree, equality would give $q^r=q^s$, hence $r=s$ because $q$ is indeterminate. Thus all differences $\gamma_j-\gamma_i$ for $i\ne j$ are nonzero in $K(z)$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Apply the assumed operator relation to $h=1,z,\ldots,z^{N-1}$. The resulting linear system for $(f_j)$ has matrix $(\gamma_j^k)_{0\le k<N,1\le j\le N}$, whose determinant is the Vandermonde product $\prod_{i<j}(\gamma_j-\gamma_i)\ne0$ by step 1.1. The matrix is invertible over $K(z)$, so every $f_j=0$. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
