## Statement

For the complex affine Hecke algebra of any reduced based root datum with positive real affine parameters, every irreducible finite dimensional module $V$ is $L(P,\sigma)$ for a Langlands datum $(P,\sigma)$ of [[def-hh-affine-hecke-weight-temperedness-and-positive-position]]. The datum is unique: $L(P,\sigma)\cong L(Q,\rho)$ implies $P=Q$ and $\sigma\cong\rho$ as $H_P$-modules. The standard module has the unique irreducible quotient proved in [[thm-hh-affine-hecke-standard-module-unique-irreducible-quotient]].

The tempered irreducibles are exactly the data with $P=B$ and real exponent zero. In general $\sigma=\tau\circ\psi_\nu$ for a tempered $H_P$-module $\tau$ and $\nu\in C_P^{++}$, so this is a classification by tempered parabolic modules in strictly positive real position. It includes reducible root systems, nontrivial central lattice directions, arbitrary isogeny type, and unequal positive real parameters.


## Proof

**Given:** The above $V$ and the fixed invariant inner product.

1.1 Its finite nonempty weight set admits a weight $t$ maximizing $r(\lambda(t))$. Put $\nu=p(\lambda(t))$, let $P$ be its simple-root zero set and write $\lambda(t)=\nu+\eta$ with $\eta\in K_P$ by [F1]. Then $\nu\in C_P^{++}$. Let $U=H_PV_t\subset V$. Every weight $t'$ of $U$ is $wt$ for some $w\in W_P$ by [F2]; because $W_P$ fixes $\nu$, its logarithmic absolute value is $\nu+w\eta$, with $w\eta\in E_P$. By [F1] its $r$-value is at least $\|\nu\|$, whereas maximality of $t$ in $V$ gives the reverse inequality. The equality clause in [F1] therefore forces $w\eta\in K_P$. Thus every weight of $U$ lies in $\nu+K_P$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Choose a nonzero $H_P$-submodule of $U$ of least dimension and denote it by $\sigma$; it is irreducible, and its weights are among those of $U$. step 1.1 proves that $\sigma$ is essentially tempered of real exponent $\nu$ and in strictly positive position. The inclusion $\sigma\hookrightarrow V$ defines an $H$-linear map $I_P(\sigma)\to V$, $h\otimes v\mapsto hv$: the equality $ha\otimes v=h\otimes av$ for $a\in H_P$ proves that this formula is well defined. Its image is nonzero and $H$-stable, so irreducibility of $V$ makes it surjective. By [F3] it identifies $V$ with $L(P,\sigma)$. This proves exhaustivity. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Suppose $V\cong L(P,\sigma)\cong L(Q,\rho)$ with real exponents $\nu,\mu$. The largest $r$-value among the weights of $V$ is intrinsic, and [F3] identifies it with both $\|\nu\|$ and $\|\mu\|$. Every weight attaining that value has projection both $\nu$ and $\mu$, so $\nu=\mu$. Their zero sets are therefore equal, giving $P=Q$. The sum of the generalized weight spaces attaining that maximum is also intrinsic; [F3] identifies its $H_P$-action with both $\sigma$ and $\rho$. Hence $\sigma\cong\rho$, proving full uniqueness rather than uniqueness modulo an unspecified Weyl conjugacy. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 A vector belongs to $K_B$ exactly when its projection to $C$ is zero by [F1]. Therefore $V$ is tempered exactly when the largest $r$-value of its weights is zero. In its unique Langlands datum this value is $\|\nu\|$ by [F3]; vanishing implies $\nu=0$ and its zero set is $B$. Conversely $P=B$ and $\nu=0$ mean $\sigma$ is tempered and $I_B(\sigma)=\sigma=V$. The twisting assertion follows from [F2]. All selections in this proof concern a finite set or a finite-dimensional space; no arbitrary-index choice principle is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
