## Statement

Assume AC. Let $d\ge1$ and $B=K(C_d\times\mathbb Z,1)$. For geometric oriented **smooth** bordism of maps,
$$\Omega^{SO}_3(B)\cong\mathbb Z/d.$$
In degree four, forgetting the map splits the constant-map inclusion, and its kernel is a finite group isomorphic to $\mathbb Z/d$. In particular, for every closed smooth oriented four-manifold $Z$ and map $f:Z\to B$,
$$d\bigl([Z,f]-[Z,\mathrm{constant}]\bigr)=0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega^{SO}_4(B).$$
Thus that displayed disjoint union, with reversed orientations for its negative terms, bounds an actual compact smooth oriented five-manifold over $B$. No TOP bordism calculation or generalized-homology axiom for geometric bordism is presupposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and geometric smooth oriented bordisms, with product collars at every boundary face. All maps to a CW target are continuous.

[F1] Disjoint union gives the geometric bordism group; zero means an actual filling with its extending map. [[def-oriented-bordism-group-of-a-space]], [[lem-oriented-bordism-classes-over-a-space-form-an-abelian-group]]

[F2] The coefficient groups in dimensions one, two and three vanish integrally: the actual single-copy smooth fillings are proved by [[lem-closed-oriented-smooth-one-and-two-manifolds-bound]] and [[thm-every-closed-oriented-smooth-three-manifold-bounds-a-compact-oriented-smooth-four-manifold]]. Dimension-zero oriented bordism is $\mathbb Z$: signed point count is unchanged by an oriented compact one-manifold, and oppositely signed points bound intervals. Negative-dimensional groups are zero.

[F3] Relative smooth approximation, finite-dimensional transversality and collars permit cutting a compact source at a regular level of a real function and smoothing its product corners. The Euclidean approximation has arbitrarily small prescribed continuous pointwise error and fixes a neighborhood where the input was already smooth. [[thm-relative-whitney-approximation-for-manifold-valued-maps]], [[thm-relative-whitney-approximation-for-euclidean-valued-maps]], [[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[thm-collar-neighborhood-theorem]] A compact smooth submanifold in the interior has a normal tube; the inverse-function theorem applies to its smooth finite-dimensional coordinates. [[thm-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem-in-a-smooth-ambient-manifold]], [[thm-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-on-manifolds]] The sequence of an exact couple, including its page subquotients but not an automatic abutment, is [[thm-an-exact-couple-generates-a-spectral-sequence]].

[F4] Compact smooth sources and their specified boundary faces have compatible finite triangulations under AC. Cellular approximation and the compact-image property put their maps and bordisms into finite skeleta and finite subcomplexes, relative to the boundary. [[thm-compact-smooth-manifolds-with-corners-admit-relative-finite-triangulations]], [[thm-cellular-approximation-for-maps-of-cw-pairs]], [[cor-the-image-of-a-compact-space-lies-in-a-finite-cw-subcomplex]] Cellular chains compute singular homology. [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]]

## Proof

1.1 Define $\Omega_n(X,A)$ using compact smooth oriented $n$-manifolds with boundary mapped to $A$, modulo bordisms with a vertical boundary face mapped to $A$. Rounding product corners does not alter the maps, since they are constant in the normal collar coordinate. Composition of maps, disjoint union and taking oriented boundary are well-defined on these classes. The sequence $\Omega_n(A)\to\Omega_n(X)\to\Omega_n(X,A)\to\Omega_{n-1}(A)$ is exact. At the middle absolute group, a relative nullbordism has its other boundary in $A$, giving precisely the required absolute preimage. At the relative group, fill a nullbordant boundary in $A$ and glue this filling onto the source to obtain an absolute cycle. At the boundary group, a nullbordism in $X$ is itself the required relative cycle. Cylinders prove homotopy invariance. The same arguments with a protected vertical face give the exact sequences of triples. These are constructions of manifolds and extending maps, rather than an invocation of an unproved homology-theory structure. [F1, F3, construct]

2.1 Here is the excision operation actually needed for CW pairs. If $\overline U\subset\operatorname{int}A\subset X$, first make the map on a smaller boundary collar constant in the collar direction. For one compact source put $A_0=\overline{f^{-1}(X\setminus A)}$ and let $B_0$ be $f^{-1}(\overline U)$ together with a smaller closed boundary collar. These closed sets are disjoint: the collar maps to $A$, while the open set $f^{-1}(\operatorname{int}A)$ separates $f^{-1}(\overline U)$ from $A_0$. Separate them by a continuous real function, equal to zero near $A_0$ and one near $B_0$, and approximate it smoothly retaining these values; for this scalar approximation, double the source along its constant boundary collar and retain that collar. Choose a regular intermediate value by [F3]. The part on the first side maps to $X\setminus U$, and its new boundary maps to $A\setminus U$. In the cylinder on the source, round the corner at the cut and declare the removed part a vertical face: its map lies in $A$. This is a relative bordism from the original cycle to the cut one. Apply exactly the same operation to a purported nullbordism, protecting its prescribed incoming face, to prove injectivity. Thus excision is an isomorphism. The separating function exists on the compact smooth source by a finite partition of unity; only a finite choice of local bump data is needed. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 For the pair of a $p$-cell and its boundary, a representative can be made smooth and transverse to the cell center on the compact inverse image of a smaller interior ball. Here is the supported approximation in those target coordinates. Extend the coordinate map continuously to the source by a target cutoff equal to one on the half-radius ball and zero outside a larger interior ball. Apply [F3] with a uniformly small Euclidean error, then multiply the correction by a smooth source cutoff equal to one near the inverse image of a smaller center ball and supported inside the inverse image of the half-radius ball. Such a cutoff is a finite sum of chart bumps on this compact interior subset. On its support the extended coordinates are the original coordinates. Choose the error smaller than both the distance of that support image from the cell boundary and the gap between the smaller center ball and the complement of the cutoff-one region. The straight-line homotopy then stays inside the cell, fixes the complement, and every possible new center preimage lies where the replacement is smooth. A further arbitrarily small finite bump family, whose coordinate translations span the target near those preimages, is transverse as a total family. [F3] therefore supplies a slice transverse to the center. Its center preimage is a compact oriented $(n-p)$-manifold $N$, with its normal framing given by the $p$ target coordinates. In [F3]'s normal tube, use that framing as the fiber coordinate $v$; the map to the cell is $v+O(|v|^2)$, with fiber derivative the identity at zero. The parameter inverse-function theorem and compactness make $(x,v)\mapsto(x,f(x,v))$ a diffeomorphism on one uniform small neighborhood of $N\times0$. The compact complement of a smaller such tube has image separated from the center. Consequently choose $0<\epsilon<1$ so that the entire inverse image of the radius-$\epsilon$ target ball is this product $N\times D^p_\epsilon$. Before cutting, change the target map as follows. On the unit disk set $h_s(0)=0$, and for $x=r\omega$ put $h_s(r\omega)=R_s(r)\omega$, where $R_s(r)=(1-s)r+s\min(r/\epsilon,1)$ and $0\le s\le1$. The formula is continuous, is the identity when $s=0$, and fixes the unit boundary for every $s$. At $s=1$ it expands the small target ball by $1/\epsilon$ and sends its entire complement to the unit boundary. Therefore $h_1\circ f$ maps the removed source complement into $S^{p-1}$, while on $N\times D^p_\epsilon$ it is projection followed by this positive rescaling. The homotopy $h_s\circ f$ is rel the prescribed target boundary. Only now apply the source cylinder cut of step 2.1: its removed face really maps into the relative subspace. The surviving relative cycle is precisely $N\times D^p$ with its projection map and the center's original orientation. Conversely $N\times D^p$ maps by projection to the cell. Transverse inverse images in a bordism give bordisms of $N$; products of a bordism with $D^p$ prove the reverse implication. Therefore $\Omega_n(D^p,S^{p-1})=\Omega_{n-p}(*)$, including zero when $n<p$. For finitely many top-dimensional cells, choose such an $\epsilon_e$ in each cell, after making all center preimages transverse. Perform the displayed radial homotopy in each characteristic disk and fix the entire lower skeleton. This descends even for nonregular attaching maps: every boundary point of every characteristic disk is fixed throughout, so identified boundary points retain their same image in the lower skeleton. The quotient characteristic maps and the CW cylinder topology give one continuous homotopy of the whole finite skeleton, rel that lower skeleton. At its endpoint the complement of the disjoint small cell balls maps into the lower skeleton. Thus the corresponding disjoint source complements may legitimately be declared vertical relative faces, leaving the finite disjoint union of $N_e\times D^p$ with their actual characteristic maps. On a relative bordism, make the same center approximations transverse rel its already transverse endpoint collars; their center preimages give bordisms of the $N_e$. Conversely products of these bordisms with the characteristic disks give a relative bordism. These two constructions prove both directions of the direct-sum identification, rather than assuming that the complement originally mapped to the lower skeleton. They give $$\Omega_{p+q}(X^p,X^{p-1})=\bigoplus_{e^p}\Omega_q(*).$$ For $p=0$ this is directly the finite disjoint-union decomposition. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Apply step 1.1's exact sequences to the skeletal pairs. With $D_{p,q}=\Omega_{p+q}(X^p)$ and $E_{p,q}=\Omega_{p+q}(X^p,X^{p-1})$, inclusion, quotient and boundary form an exact couple. In step 3.1's product coordinates, taking the boundary and projecting onto an adjacent cell multiplies its coefficient bordism by the attaching incidence degree. One can verify this by choosing a regular point in that cell: each signed inverse image contributes one copy of $N$, and a negatively oriented normal disk contributes $-N$. In degree one this is the terminal-minus-initial endpoint rule. Consequently $d^1$ is the cellular boundary, and $$E^2_{p,q}=H_p(X;\Omega_q(*)),\qquad d^r:(p,q)\longmapsto(p-r,q+r-1).$$ The exact-couple construction in [F3] gives these pages and their specified subquotients; convergence is verified in the next step. No normalization of a separate boundary or suspension map has been assumed. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 These computations apply to a CW complex finite in each degree, even if it is infinite-dimensional. By [F4], a map from an $n$-manifold is homotopic to a map into $X^n$; a bordism between two such maps can be moved relative to its boundary into $X^{n+1}$. Hence $\Omega_n(X^{n+1})\to\Omega_n(X)$ is an isomorphism, and all constructions occur in finite subcomplexes. Put $F_p=\operatorname{im}(\Omega_n(X^p)\to\Omega_n(X))$. This is exhaustive at $p=n$ and zero at $p<0$. In [F3]'s page-subquotient formula, for fixed $(p,q)$ of total degree $n$, the stable numerator is $\ker k$: its defining earlier-skeleton image is zero when $p-r<0$. The stable denominator is $j\ker(\Omega_n(X^p)\to\Omega_n(X))$, since its later-skeleton map stabilizes once $p+r-1\ge n+1$. Exactness gives $\ker k=\operatorname{im}j$. Send $e=jx$ in this numerator to the image of $x$ modulo $F_{p-1}$. Different lifts differ by a class from $X^{p-1}$, so this is well-defined and onto $F_p/F_{p-1}$. Its kernel is precisely the displayed stable denominator: if the image of $x$ comes from $X^{p-1}$, subtract such a preimage to make its image in $\Omega_n(X)$ zero. Thus $E^\infty_{p,q}=F_p/F_{p-1}$, proving the needed finite exhaustive abutment rather than assuming it for an arbitrary exact couple. [F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For clarity the necessary ordinary homology is computed for every integer $d$, not just primes. Put $R=\mathbb Z[C_d]$, write its generator as $a$, and put $N=1+a+\cdots+a^{d-1}$. The augmented periodic free resolution of the trivial module is $$\cdots\xrightarrow{a-1}R\xrightarrow{N}R\xrightarrow{a-1}R\xrightarrow{\epsilon}\mathbb Z\to0.$$ Writing an element as $\sum c_ja^j$ shows $\ker(a-1)=\mathbb ZN=\operatorname{im}N$, $\ker N=\ker\epsilon$, and every coefficient list of sum zero is $(a-1)$ times a list, by successive partial sums. This proves exactness. The usual free simplicial model $EC_d$ has simplices $(g_0,\ldots,g_k)$, face deletion, diagonal free group action, and a contraction obtained by prepending $1$. Its quotient is a finite-in-each-degree model of $BC_d$. Its augmented chain resolution and the displayed one compute the same homology: recursively lift each basis image across the preceding differential's surjection; exactness supplies the lift, and the identical recursion supplies chain homotopies between the two composites and the identities. There are finitely many basis vectors in each degree, and AC permits the countable recursion. Tensoring the displayed resolution with the trivial module makes its positive differentials alternate $0,d$. Therefore $$H_0(BC_d;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z,\quad H_{2j+1}(BC_d;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/d,\quad H_{2j}(BC_d;\mathbb Z)=0\ (j\ge1).$$ The simplicial contraction above also proves contractibility of $EC_d$: its prepending operation is an extra degeneracy and the corresponding prism contracts its geometric realization. [F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Take $B=BC_d\times S^1$, whose universal cover $EC_d\times\mathbb R$ is contractible. The product CW structure is finite in each degree. Its cellular complex is the direct sum of the cellular complex of $BC_d$ and its degree-one shift, because the circle has one zero-cell, one one-cell and zero differential; the product boundary formula follows by orienting each product cell in factor order. Thus $$H_3(B;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/d,\qquad H_4(B;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/d.$$ All higher positive-degree groups are finite as well. This identifies the target as the stated $K(C_d\times\mathbb Z,1)$ and computes the exact rows used below. [F4, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 In total degree three the only possibly nonzero $E^2$ term is $(3,0)$, equal to $\mathbb Z/d$, since [F2] kills coefficient degrees one through three. Its outgoing differentials have zero targets, and all incoming differentials have negative coefficient degree. It therefore survives and is the sole filtration quotient. This proves $\Omega_3(B)=\mathbb Z/d$. In total degree four, the only terms are $(0,4)$ and $(4,0)$. The latter is $\mathbb Z/d$ and again has no nonzero incoming or outgoing differential. The former is exactly the constant-map subgroup: inclusion of a point has the forgetful map as a left inverse on geometric bordism. Hence it is injected, so no incoming differential can kill any of it. The resulting filtration gives a short exact sequence $$0\to\Omega_4(*)\to\Omega_4(B)\to\mathbb Z/d\to0.$$ The same forgetful map splits it. No computation or torsion-freeness of $\Omega_4(*)$ was required. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For $[Z,f]-[Z,\mathrm{constant}]$, forgetting the map is zero. By step 8.1 it lies in a subgroup of order $d$, hence multiplication by $d$ is zero. By the geometric definition [F1], this equality is an actual smooth oriented five-bordism with the extending map, including the stated orientation signs. The empty manifold uses the empty bordism. When $d=1$, the finite group is trivial and the same proof gives zero third group and zero fourth kernel. For prime powers all conclusions follow without altering any step. AC was explicitly retained for the smooth and cellular suppliers and the resolution comparison. [F1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
