## Statement

Assume AC. Let $X$ be a smooth knot exterior or the exterior of a smooth proper disk in the standard four-ball. Its meridian-coordinate homomorphism is $\ell:\pi_1X\to\mathbb Z$. Let $X_q$ be its connected $q$-fold cyclic cover, for any $q\ge1$, and put $\psi=\ell/q$ on its group. For any homomorphism $c:\pi_1X_q\to C_{p^a}$, where $p$ is prime and $a\ge1$, give $\mathbb C(t)$ the rank-one unitary coefficient system
$$\rho(g)=\zeta_{p^a}^{c(g)}t^{\psi(g)},\qquad \bar t=t^{-1}.$$
Then $H_*(X_q;\mathbb C(t)_\rho)=0$. The same conclusion holds for a trivial finite character.

If $M_{0,q}$ is the $q$-fold cover of zero surgery on a smooth knot, and $c$ is any prime-power-order character on its first homology, the same conclusion holds on $M_{0,q}$ with its meridian free coordinate. In particular every smooth four-bordism whose boundary consists of such data has a nonsingular twisted middle Hermitian form, with no change to its absolute twisted $H_2$. The regular $C_{p^a}\times\mathbb Z$ cover in this assertion is generally infinite; the assertion concerns its specified rank-one localization.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite smooth geometric spaces, indicated homomorphisms and AC.

[F1] Finite relative smooth triangulations and local cellular chains give finite based chain complexes for each space and its boundary, over the group ring of any supplied covering. [[thm-compact-smooth-manifolds-with-corners-admit-relative-finite-triangulations]], [[thm-cellular-chains-compute-homology-with-local-coefficients]]

[F2] The knot exterior has first homology generated by the meridian. Its deformation retraction from the complement and Alexander duality with the embedded circle also give zero higher reduced homology: $\widetilde H_i(S^3\setminus K;\mathbb Z)=\widetilde H^{2-i}(S^1;\mathbb Z)$, which vanishes for $i\ne1$. [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]], [[thm-alexander-duality-for-compact-locally-contractible-subsets-of-a-sphere]] The smooth disk exterior has integral homology of the meridian circle by [[lem-slice-disk-exterior-has-homology-of-a-circle]]. The homology universal coefficient sequence therefore gives the same assertion over every $\mathbb F_p$, with the meridian generator. [[thm-universal-coefficient-theorem-for-homology-over-a-pid]] Local chains of collared gluings satisfy Mayer–Vietoris, and short exact chain sequences give homology exact sequences. [[thm-excision-and-mayer-vietoris-with-local-coefficients]], [[thm-long-exact-sequence-in-homology]]

[F3] The free coordinate on $M_{0,q}$ sends the lift of $\mu^q$ to one; its filling solid torus has precisely this core. [[lem-zero-surgery-cyclic-cover-homology-splitting]]

[F4] Local Poincare–Lefschetz duality and the unitary identification of dual systems identify the absolute-to-relative middle map with the twisted Hermitian intersection form. [[thm-poincare-lefschetz-duality-with-local-coefficients]], [[lem-involutive-twisted-chain-duals-realize-local-coefficient-duality]] Full AC is retained. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F5] A primitive $p^a$-th root has monic minimal polynomial $\Phi_{p^a}(z)=1+z^{p^{a-1}}+\cdots+z^{(p-1)p^{a-1}}$. Thus $\mathbb Z[\zeta_{p^a}]=\mathbb Z[z]/(\Phi_{p^a})$. [[thm-cyclotomic-polynomials-are-irreducible-over-the-rationals]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix any prime $p$ and put $k=\mathbb F_p$. The infinite cyclic chain complex of $X$ is finite free over $\Lambda=k[u,u^{-1}]$ by [F1]. Its quotient by $u-1$ is the ordinary cellular complex of $X$: each base cell has the free orbit of its lifts. Multiplication by $u-1$ is injective on chain groups, giving a short exact sequence with that quotient. The connecting map $H_1(X;k)\to H_0(X_\infty;k)$ sends the meridian to one: lift its edge loop to a path from a vertex $v$ to $uv$, and divide its boundary by $u-1$. By [F2] this is an isomorphism. The exact sequence therefore makes $u-1$ an isomorphism on every positive-degree $H_i(X_\infty;k)$. The degree-zero module is $k$ with trivial deck action. These assertions follow in degree one from the displayed connecting isomorphism and $H_2(X;k)=0$, and in higher degrees from the two adjacent vanishing base groups. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 All these homology modules are finitely generated over the noetherian PID $\Lambda$: kernels and images are submodules of finite free modules, and polynomial division proves the PID property of the localization of $k[u]$. If $v_1,\ldots,v_s$ generate a module $L$ on which $u-1$ is onto, write $v_i=(u-1)\sum_j A_{ij}v_j$. The adjugate identity says that $\det(I-(u-1)A)$ kills every generator. This Laurent polynomial takes value one at $u=1$, so is nonzero. Thus $L$ is killed by a nonzero polynomial. The same is true for degree zero, with annihilator $u-1$. A finitely generated module killed by a nonzero Laurent polynomial is finite-dimensional over $k$: multiply the polynomial by a unit so its first and last coefficients are nonzero, and use its relation to reduce both positive and negative powers to a finite interval. Consequently all $H_i(X_\infty;k)$ are finite-dimensional. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The infinite cyclic cover of $X_q$ for $\psi$ is literally $X_\infty$, now with positive deck generator $u^q$. Each finite-dimensional vector space in step 2.1 is torsion for this new invertible operator: on a finite-dimensional space its characteristic polynomial annihilates it, by the adjugate identity. Its homology therefore becomes zero after localization to $k(t)$, where $t$ denotes $u^q$. Localization commutes with homology: multiplying a cycle relation by one common nonzero denominator proves both kernel and image comparisons directly. Hence the finite cellular chain complex of $X_q$ with coefficient $g\mapsto t^{\psi(g)}$ is acyclic over $k(t)$. This argument does not require that $q$ be a power of $p$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Put $m=p^a$, $O=\mathbb Z[\zeta_m]$ and $K=\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)(t)$. The cellular matrices for $\rho$ have entries in $O[t,t^{-1}]$. By [F5], evaluation at one modulo $p$ gives a well-defined ring map $O\to k$ sending $\zeta_m$ to one, since its monic minimal polynomial has value $p$ there. Reducing every matrix by this map gives exactly step 3.1's acyclic complex. A minor nonzero over $k(t)$ comes from a Laurent-polynomial determinant with nonzero reduction, and is therefore nonzero over $K$. Thus each differential's $K$-rank is at least its reduced rank. In degree $i$ the reduced adjacent ranks sum to the number of $i$-cells. The two unreduced adjacent ranks cannot exceed that number because their composite is zero. They consequently have the same sum, and the $K$ homology vanishes in every degree. Extension to $\mathbb C(t)$ is an extension of fields and is exact, proving the assertion for $X_q$. For a trivial finite character use the same proof with $O=\mathbb Z\to k$; no cyclotomic generator is needed. Inverse-deck conventions replace the displayed monomials by their inverses and do not change any rank argument. [F1, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For $M_{0,q}$, restrict its character to the exterior cover and use step 4.1. In the zero filling the longitude is killed, and its core is the lifted meridian by [F3]. Its coefficient is $\alpha t$, for a root of unity $\alpha$ (in the specified splitting $\alpha=1$). The cellular circle differential $\alpha t-1$ is an invertible scalar in $\mathbb C(t)$; multiplication by its inverse gives a contraction. The solid torus retracts to this circle with its coefficient transport. The boundary torus is the product of this circle with its longitude circle and has the same contraction on the first factor, with the tensor differential sign. Thus exterior, filling and overlap are all acyclic. Local Mayer–Vietoris [F2] proves $H_*(M_{0,q};\mathbb C(t)_\rho)=0$. [F2, F3, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Write $F=\mathbb C(t)$ with its stated involution. For a four-bordism $W$ with these boundary components, the pair sequence and step 5.1 give an isomorphism $H_2(W;F)\to H_2(W,\partial W;F)$. Finite field-valued chain duality [F4] identifies the target with the conjugate dual of the source, and the map with its Hermitian intersection form. This proves nonsingularity on the absolute module. At chain level an acyclic finite-dimensional boundary complex has a contraction: choose complements to its cycle spaces and invert each differential on its chosen complement. The absolute-to-relative quotient then has contractible kernel, so is a chain homotopy equivalence. This is an explicit algebraic boundary cap, preserving the absolute middle homology; no geometric handle attachment is required to turn an already nonsingular twisted form into one. Finally, $\mathbb C[C_m\times\mathbb Z]=\mathbb C[C_m][t,t^{-1}]$ splits into the finite character idempotent summands. Projection onto the specified character is exact and localization on that Laurent-polynomial summand is flat, by clearing denominators. Hence this chain homology is exactly $H_2(Y;\mathbb C)\otimes_{\mathbb C[C_m\times\mathbb Z]}F$ in the original Witt construction. [F1, F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
