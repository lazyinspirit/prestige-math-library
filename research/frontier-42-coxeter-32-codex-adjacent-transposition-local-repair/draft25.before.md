## Statement

Fix the following numbering of the classical diagrams, and let $(W,S)$ be the irreducible finite Coxeter system of that type with its canonical reflection representation $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$, $V=\mathbb R^S$, and Coxeter form $B$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]):
* $A_n$ ($n\ge1$): $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_n\}$, $m(s_i,s_j)=3$ if $|i-j|=1$ and $2$ otherwise;
* $B_n$ ($n\ge2$): $m(s_i,s_{i+1})=3$ $(1\le i\le n-2)$, $m(s_{n-1},s_n)=4$, other pairs $2$;
* $D_n$ ($n\ge4$): $m(s_1,s_3)=m(s_2,s_3)=3$, $m(s_i,s_{i+1})=3$ $(3\le i\le n-1)$, other pairs $2$;
* $I_2(m)$ ($m\ge3$): $S=\{s_1,s_2\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=m$.
Write $[k]_t:=1+t+\cdots+t^{k-1}$ and $[k]_t!:=[1]_t[2]_t\cdots[k]_t$. Then:

**(1) Permutation and signed-permutation models.** For each type the canonical representation is carried by a linear isometry onto the following model representation, in such a way that $\rho(s)$ corresponds to the model's displayed simple reflection:
(a) $A_n$: the standard representation of the symmetric group $S_{n+1}$ on $V\cong\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\sum_ix_i=0\}$ (equivalently on the quotient $\mathbb R^{n+1}/\mathbb R(1,\dots,1)$), where $\rho(s_i)$ corresponds to the adjacent transposition $(i\ i+1)$ ([[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]], [[thm-adjacent-transpositions-generate-the-symmetric-group]]); under the resulting isomorphism $W\to S_{n+1}$ the length is the inversion number ([[def-inversions-inversion-number-and-sign]]), as proved in step 3.1.
(b) $B_n$: the group of signed permutations of $[n]$, acting on $\mathbb R^n$ with orthonormal basis $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n$, where $\rho(s_i)$ $(i\le n-1)$ exchanges the $i$-th and $(i+1)$-st coordinates and $\rho(s_n)$ changes the sign of the $n$-th coordinate; equivalently the Weyl group $W(B_n)$ of the classical root system $B_n=\{\pm\varepsilon_i\}\cup\{\pm\varepsilon_i\pm\varepsilon_j\}$ ([[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).
(c) $D_n$: the group of signed permutations with an even number of sign changes, where $\rho(s_1)$ exchanges coordinates $1,2$, $\rho(s_2)$ maps $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(-x_2,-x_1)$, and $\rho(s_i)$ $(i\ge3)$ exchanges coordinates $i-1,i$; equivalently the Weyl group $W(D_n)$ of the classical root system $D_n=\{\pm\varepsilon_i\pm\varepsilon_j:i<j\}$ ([[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).
(d) $I_2(m)$: the dihedral group of order $2m$ acting on $\mathbb R^2$ generated by the reflections in two lines meeting at angle $\pi/m$, in the standard coordinates attached to the two simple roots.

**(2) Orbit and quotient polynomial.** Choose $s_0=s_n$ in type $A_n$, $s_0=s_1$ in type $B_n$, $s_0=s_n$ in type $D_n$, and $s_0=s_1$ in type $I_2(m)$. Put $T:=S\setminus\{s_0\}$ and $W_T:=\langle s:s\in T\rangle$ ([[def-cg-parabolic-quotient-and-two-sided-minima]] (1)), and let $f_0$ be the dual fundamental functional of [[lem-cg-fundamental-weight-orbit-and-schreier-distance]]. In type $B_n$, $s_1$ is the long-root endpoint and $s_n$ is the sign-change endpoint. In type $D_n$, $s_n$ is the far endpoint of the chain $s_1,s_3,s_4,\dots,s_n$. Then the orbit $W\cdot f_0$, described in the model coordinates, has the following distance function $d$, which satisfies $|d(sv)-d(v)|\le1$ at every generator and is attained by the explicit words displayed in the proof:
(a) $A_n$: if $n=1$, $T=\emptyset$ and $W_T=\{1\}$; if $n\ge2$, $W_T\cong A_{n-1}$. Writing $y_j(x)=x_j|_H$ and $z_j=-\sqrt2\,y_j$, the orbit is $\{z_1,\dots,z_{n+1}\}$ on $H=\{x:\sum_i x_i=0\}$, with Schreier graph the path $z_1-z_2-\cdots-z_{n+1}$; $d$ takes each value $0,1,\dots,n$ exactly once and $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=[n+1]_t$.
(b) $B_n$ ($n\ge2$): $W_T\cong B_{n-1}$. Writing $y_i(x)=x_i$ and $z_i=\sqrt2\,y_i$ on $\mathbb R^n$, $W\cdot f_0=\{\pm z_1,\dots,\pm z_n\}$ and the Schreier graph is the path $z_1-z_2-\cdots-z_n-(-z_n)-(-z_{n-1})-\cdots-(-z_1)$; $d$ takes each value $0,1,\dots,2n-1$ exactly once and $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=[2n]_t$.
(c) $D_n$ ($n\ge4$): $W_T\cong D_{n-1}$. Writing $y_i(x)=x_i$ and $z_i=\sqrt2\,y_i$ on $\mathbb R^n$, $W\cdot f_0=\{\pm z_1,\dots,\pm z_n\}$, with the Schreier graph obtained from the paths $z_1-z_2-\cdots-z_n$ and $(-z_1)-(-z_2)-\cdots-(-z_n)$ by the cross edges $z_1-(-z_2)$ and $(-z_1)-z_2$; the distances are $0,1,\dots,n-2$ and $n,\dots,2n-2$, each once, together with $n-1$ twice, so $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=[n]_t(1+t^{n-1})$.
(d) $I_2(m)$ ($m\ge3$): $W_T\cong A_1$; writing $r=s_1s_2$ and $v_k:=r^k\cdot f_0$ for $k\in\mathbb Z/m$, the Schreier graph is the path $v_0-v_1-v_{-1}-v_2-v_{-2}-\cdots$, $d$ takes each value $0,\dots,m-1$ exactly once and $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=[m]_t$.

**(3) Products.** With the usual low-rank diagram identifications $A_1=B_1$, $A_2=I_2(3)$, $B_2=I_2(4)$, $D_2=A_1\times A_1=I_2(2)$, and $D_3=A_3$ (verified from their Coxeter presentations in step 3.2),
$$P_{A_n}(t)=[n+1]_t!\quad(n\ge1),\qquad P_{S_n}(t)=P_{A_{n-1}}(t)=[n]_t!\quad(n\ge2),$$
$$P_{B_n}(t)=\prod_{i=1}^n[2i]_t\quad(n\ge1),\qquad P_{D_n}(t)=[n]_t\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}[2i]_t\quad(n\ge2),\qquad P_{I_2(m)}(t)=[2]_t[m]_t\quad(m\ge3),$$
where $P_{B_1}$ uses $B_1=A_1$, and $P_{D_2},P_{D_3}$ use the displayed low-rank identifications. Here $P_{S_n}$ refers to the type $A_{n-1}$ presentation of $S_n$. In particular $P_{A_n}(1)=(n+1)!$, $P_{B_n}(1)=2^nn!$, and $P_{D_n}(1)=2^{n-1}n!$. No invariant degrees and no exceptional-case computation enter this item; the products are proved by the explicit recurrences of (2), with $D_2$ treated directly. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An irreducible finite Coxeter system of type $A_n$, $B_n$, $D_n$ or $I_2(m)$ with its numbering as displayed, its canonical representation $\rho$ on $V=\mathbb R^S$ with Coxeter form $B$, the Euclidean model space $E$ with orthonormal basis $\varepsilon_1,\dots$, and the dual fundamental functional $f_0$ of the terminal node.

[F1] $B$ is the symmetric bilinear form with $B(e_s,e_s)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ for $s\ne t$; the reflection with normal $a$ is $r_a(v)=v-\frac{2B(v,a)}{B(a,a)}a$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (2),(3)).

[F2] $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ is the canonical homomorphism with $\rho(s)=r_{e_s}$, and for $s\ne t$ the product $\rho(s)\rho(t)$ has order exactly $m(s,t)$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (3)(iv)).

[F3] The assignment $s\mapsto g_s$ into a group extends uniquely to a homomorphism $W\to G$ whenever $g_s^2=1$ and $(g_sg_t)^{m(s,t)}=1$ for all $s\ne t$ with $m(s,t)<\infty$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[def-group-homomorphism]]).

[F4] The adjacent transpositions $(i\ i+1)$ generate $S_{n+1}$ for $n\ge1$; the inversion number of a permutation is the number of pairs $i<j$ whose values are inverted ([[thm-adjacent-transpositions-generate-the-symmetric-group]], [[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]], [[def-inversions-inversion-number-and-sign]]).

[F5] The coordinate root sets displayed for $B_n$ and $D_n$ in Statement (1) are the root systems of those types ([[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]]); the Weyl group of a root system is generated by its root reflections ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).

[F6] For the orbit of the dual fundamental functional of $s_0\in S$ with $T=S\setminus\{s_0\}$, the orbit map $W/W_T\to W\cdot f_0$ is a bijection, $d(v)$ is the minimal length in the corresponding coset and the graph distance in the Schreier graph, and $|d(s\cdot v)-d(v)|\le1$ for all generators ([[lem-cg-fundamental-weight-orbit-and-schreier-distance]] (1)-(3)).

[F7] Isometries carry reflections to reflections: if $\phi$ is a linear isometry and $B(a,a)\ne0$, then $\phi r_a\phi^{-1}=r_{\phi(a)}$; $r_{-a}=r_a$; and $g r_ag^{-1}=r_{ga}$ for every orthogonal $g$ ([[def-linear-isometry-and-orthogonal-or-unitary-operator]], [[def-linear-isomorphism-and-invertible-linear-map]], [[def-inner-product-space]], [[def-linear-basis]], [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (3), [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (2)).

[F8] The group generated by a set, a group homomorphism and its isomorphisms, and the order of an element are as in [[def-generated-subgroup]], [[def-group-homomorphism]], [[def-group-isomorphism-and-automorphism]], [[def-order-in-a-group]], [[def-group-power]].

[F9] For every $T\subseteq S$, $W_T=\langle s:s\in T\rangle$ is the standard parabolic; in particular $W_\emptyset=\{1\}$ ([[def-cg-parabolic-quotient-and-two-sided-minima]] (1)).

[F10] The order of a finite group $G$ means the finite cardinality $|G|$ of its underlying set ([[def-finite-cardinality]]).

[F11] For a polynomial $f$ over a commutative ring, $f(1)$ is its value at $1$ under the polynomial evaluation map ([[def-polynomial-evaluation-and-root]]).

[F12] $|S_n|=n!$ for every $n\in\mathbb N$ ([[cor-symmetric-group-has-factorial-cardinality-again]]).

[F13] For $A\subseteq W$, $P_A(t)=\sum_{w\in A}t^{\ell(w)}$ ([[def-cg-length-series-descent-generating-polynomial]] (1)).

[F14] For $J\subseteq S$, put $W^J:=\{w\in W:\ell(ws)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in J\}$. Multiplication gives a length-additive bijection $W^J\times W_J\to W$, so $W^J$ consists of the unique minimal representatives of the sets $wW_J$, which are left cosets by [[def-coset]], and $P_W=P_{W^J}P_{W_J}$ ([[thm-cg-parabolic-growth-factorization-and-rationality]] (2), [[def-coset]]).

[F15] The canonical reflection homomorphism $\rho$ is injective ([[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] (3)).

[F16] For each $J\subseteq S$, $(W_J,J)$ is the Coxeter system of the restricted matrix and its intrinsic length agrees with the restriction of $\ell$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (2)).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Coordinate roots and the Gram isometry.** In type $A_n$ take $E=H=\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:\sum_ix_i=0\}$ and $\alpha_i=\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_{i+1}$; these roots have squared norm $2$, inner product $-1$ for adjacent indices and $0$ otherwise. In type $B_n$ take $E=\mathbb R^n$, $\alpha_i=\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_{i+1}$ for $i<n$ and $\alpha_n=\varepsilon_n$; their normalized adjacent inner products are $-\frac12$ along the $3$-edges and $-\frac1{\sqrt2}=-\cos(\pi/4)$ at the $4$-edge. In type $D_n$ take $\alpha_1=\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_2$, $\alpha_2=-(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)$ and $\alpha_i=\varepsilon_{i-1}-\varepsilon_i$ for $3\le i\le n$; the two fork inner products and chain inner products are $-1$, and the other pairs are orthogonal. For $I_2(m)$ take two normals of length $\sqrt2$ with angle $\pi-\pi/m$. In each case the normalized roots $\hat\alpha_s$ have Gram matrix $B(e_s,e_t)$. They form a basis: the type-$A$ differences form a basis of $H$, the type-$B$ roots are a coordinate-triangular basis, a relation among the type-$D$ roots has $c_n=0$ and successively $c_{n-1}=\cdots=c_3=0$, after which $c_1-c_2=-c_1-c_2=0$ forces $c_1=c_2=0$, and the two $I_2(m)$ normals are independent. Since $(e_s)_{s\in S}$ is a basis of $V$, $\phi(e_s)=\hat\alpha_s$ extends to a linear isometry $\phi:V\to E$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 **Conjugating the canonical action.** By [F7], $\phi\rho(s)\phi^{-1}=r_{\hat\alpha_s}$. The reflection formula gives the adjacent coordinate swaps in types $A_n,B_n,D_n$, the sign change of the last coordinate for $s_n$ in type $B_n$, the signed swap $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(-x_2,-x_1)$ for $s_2$ in type $D_n$, and the two line reflections for $I_2(m)$. Let $G=\langle r_{\hat\alpha_s}:s\in S\rangle$. Its displayed generators are involutions, and [F2] transported by $\phi$ shows that each pair product has the required Coxeter order, so [F3] gives a surjective homomorphism $\theta:W\to G$. The conjugate representation $\psi(w)=\phi^{-1}\theta(w)\phi$ agrees with $\rho$ on every generator; uniqueness in [F3] gives $\psi=\rho$. Since $\rho$ is injective by [F15], $\theta$ is injective as well, hence $W\cong G$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3, F7, F8, F15, algebra]

2.2 **Type $A_n$ ($n\ge1$), $s_0=s_n$, $T=\{s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}\}$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]** If $n=1$, then $T=\emptyset$ and $W_T=\{1\}$ by [F9]; if $n\ge2$, deleting $s_n$ from the displayed chain leaves type $A_{n-1}$. Let $y_j(x)=x_j|_H$ and $z_j=-\sqrt2\,y_j$. Then $f_0\circ\phi^{-1}=z_{n+1}$: $z_{n+1}$ vanishes on $\hat\alpha_i=(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_{i+1})/\sqrt2$ for $i\le n-1$ and has value $1$ on $\hat\alpha_n$. By step 1.1 the model group is $S_{n+1}$ acting by permuting the $z_j$, so $W\cdot f_0=\{z_1,\dots,z_{n+1}\}$; each $s_i$ swaps $z_i,z_{i+1}$ and fixes the others, giving the path $z_1-z_2-\cdots-z_{n+1}$. For $j\le n$ the word $s_j s_{j+1}\cdots s_n$ carries $z_{n+1}$ to $z_j$ and has length $n+1-j$; for $j=n+1$ the empty word gives $z_{n+1}$. Conversely $d'(z_j):=n+1-j$ changes by at most one at every generator and vanishes at $z_{n+1}$, so $d'\le d$ by the graph-distance clause of [F6]. Hence $d=d'$ and $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}t^{n+1-j}=[n+1]_t$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]

2.3 **Type $B_n$ ($n\ge2$), $s_0=s_1$, $T=\{s_2,\dots,s_n\}$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]** Deleting $s_1$ leaves the type-$B_{n-1}$ chain; for $n=2$ the remaining one-generator Coxeter presentation is type $A_1$, also denoted $B_1$. Put $y_i(x)=x_i$ and $z_i=\sqrt2\,y_i$ on $\mathbb R^n$; then $f_0\circ\phi^{-1}=z_1$, since it takes value $1$ on $\hat\alpha_1$ and vanishes on every $\hat\alpha_i$ for $i\ge2$. By step 1.1 the model group is the signed permutation group, so $W\cdot f_0=\{\pm z_1,\dots,\pm z_n\}$. The generators $s_i$ for $2\le i\le n-1$ swap $z_i,z_{i+1}$ together with their negatives, $s_1$ swaps $z_1,z_2$, and $s_n$ exchanges $z_n$ with $-z_n$; hence the Schreier graph is the path $z_1-z_2-\cdots-z_n-(-z_n)-\cdots-(-z_1)$. For $i=1$ the empty word carries $z_1$ to itself; for $2\le i\le n$, $s_{i-1}\cdots s_2s_1$ carries $z_1$ to $z_i$. For each $1\le i\le n$, $s_i s_{i+1}\cdots s_{n-1}s_n s_{n-1}\cdots s_1$ carries $z_1$ to $-z_i$. These words have lengths $i-1$ and $2n-i$. Thus $d(z_i)\le i-1$ and $d(-z_i)\le2n-i$. The function $d'(z_i)=i-1$, $d'(-z_i)=2n-i$ vanishes at $z_1$ and changes by at most one along every edge, so $d'=d$ by [F6]. Hence $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=\sum_{i=1}^n(t^{i-1}+t^{2n-i})=[2n]_t$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]

2.4 **Type $D_n$ ($n\ge4$), $s_0=s_n$, $T=\{s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}\}$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]** Deleting $s_n$ leaves the type-$D_{n-1}$ diagram; when $n=4$ the remaining three nodes form the path $s_1-s_3-s_2$, the type-$A_3$ presentation denoted $D_3$ in the low-rank convention. Put $y_i(x)=x_i$ and $z_i=\sqrt2\,y_i$ on $\mathbb R^n$; then $f_0\circ\phi^{-1}=-z_n$, since $-\sqrt2\,y_n$ vanishes on $\hat\alpha_i$ for $i\le n-2$, on $\hat\alpha_{n-1}=(\varepsilon_{n-2}-\varepsilon_{n-1})/\sqrt2$ and on $\hat\alpha_2=-(\varepsilon_1+\varepsilon_2)/\sqrt2$, while it takes value $1$ on $\hat\alpha_n=(\varepsilon_{n-1}-\varepsilon_n)/\sqrt2$. By step 1.1 the model group is the even signed permutation group, so $W\cdot f_0=\{\pm z_1,\dots,\pm z_n\}$. The generators $s_i$ for $i\ne2$ swap adjacent coordinate functionals, while $s_2$ sends $z_1\mapsto-z_2$ and $z_2\mapsto-z_1$ and fixes $z_j$ for $j\ge3$; thus the Schreier graph consists of the paths $z_1-\cdots-z_n$ and $(-z_1)-\cdots-(-z_n)$ joined by $z_1-(-z_2)$ and $(-z_1)-z_2$. The word $s_{j+1}s_{j+2}\cdots s_n$ carries $-z_n$ to $-z_j$ for $2\le j\le n$, so $d(-z_j)\le n-j$; the word $s_1s_3s_4\cdots s_n$ carries $-z_n$ to $-z_1$, giving $d(-z_1)\le n-1$. For $2\le j\le n$, $s_j s_{j-1}\cdots s_3s_2s_1s_3s_4\cdots s_n$ carries $-z_n$ to $z_j$ (the initial string is empty for $j=2$), and has length $n+j-2$. Also $s_2s_3\cdots s_n$ carries $-z_n$ to $z_1$, so $d(z_1)\le n-1$. The function $d'(-z_j)=n-j$ and $d'(z_j)=n+j-2$ vanishes at $-z_n$; each generator changes it by at most one along the displayed graph, so $d'=d$ by [F6]. Its values are $0,\dots,n-2$ once each, $n-1$ twice, and $n,\dots,2n-2$ once each. Therefore $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=(1+t+\cdots+t^{n-2})+2t^{n-1}+(t^n+\cdots+t^{2n-2})=[n]_t(1+t^{n-1})$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]

2.5 **Type $I_2(m)$, $s_0=s_1$, $T=\{s_2\}$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]** Here $T$ has one node and is type $A_1$. Let $\eta_1\in E^*$ be dual to $\hat\alpha_1,\hat\alpha_2$, so $\eta_1(\hat\alpha_1)=1$ and $\eta_1(\hat\alpha_2)=0$; then $f_0\circ\phi^{-1}=\eta_1$. By step 1.1 the group is dihedral of order $2m$, and the cosets $r^kW_T$ map bijectively to $v_k=r^k\cdot f_0$ by [F6]. Since $s_2r=r^{-1}s_2$ and $s_1=rs_2$, the left action is $s_2\cdot v_k=v_{-k}$ and $s_1\cdot v_k=v_{1-k}$. Omitting loops, these edges form the path $v_0-v_1-v_{-1}-v_2-v_{-2}-\cdots$ through all $m$ vertices. The vertex at position $2a$ is $(s_2s_1)^a\cdot f_0=v_{-a}$ and the vertex at position $2a+1$ is $s_1(s_2s_1)^a\cdot f_0=v_{a+1}$; these alternating words attain each path position. Thus [F6] gives distances $0,1,\dots,m-1$ and $\sum_vt^{d(v)}=[m]_t$. [step 1.1, F6, F9, algebra]

3.1 **Identifying the model groups.** In type $A_n$, the adjacent swaps generate $S_{n+1}$ by [F4]; if a permutation acts trivially on $H$, it fixes every $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$, which forces it to fix each index, so this action is faithful and $G\cong S_{n+1}$. In one-line notation, right multiplication by an adjacent transposition swaps two adjacent entries, changing only their mutual inversion and hence changing $\operatorname{inv}$ by exactly $1$ or $-1$. Therefore every word for $\pi$ has at least $\operatorname{inv}(\pi)$ letters. If $\pi\ne1$, its one-line list has an adjacent descent; swapping that pair on the right reduces the inversion number by one. Repeating this operation reaches the increasing identity list after exactly $\operatorname{inv}(\pi)$ swaps, and reversing the swaps expresses $\pi$ by a word of that length. Thus $\ell(\pi)=\operatorname{inv}(\pi)$. In type $B_n$, the reflections in roots $\varepsilon_i$ negate coordinate $i$, those in $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ swap coordinates $i,j$, and those in $\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j$ swap and negate coordinates $i,j$; these formulas show every root reflection is a signed permutation. Conversely the simple reflections $s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}$ are adjacent swaps and $s_n$ is one coordinate sign change, so they generate all coordinate permutations and all individual sign changes, hence every signed permutation. As each is in the Weyl group and every root reflection is one of these signed permutations, $G=W(B_n)$. In type $D_n$, reflections in $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ swap coordinates and those in $\varepsilon_i+\varepsilon_j$ swap and negate them; each has even sign parity, which is multiplicative under composition. The simple reflections $s_1,s_3,\dots,s_n$ generate all coordinate permutations, and $s_1s_2$ negates coordinates $1,2$; conjugating this pair negation by permutations gives a negation of any pair of coordinates, and partitioning any even set into pairs gives every even sign pattern. Thus $G$ is the group of even signed permutations, and every root reflection lies in it, so $G=W(D_n)$. In type $I_2(m)$, $r=r_{\hat\alpha_1}r_{\hat\alpha_2}$ is a rotation by $2\pi/m$ and has order $m$. Every word in two involutions reduces to an alternating word, hence to one of at most the $m$ powers $r^k$ or the $m$ elements $r^k r_{\hat\alpha_1}$. The powers are distinct; the latter elements are distinct and have determinant $-1$, unlike the powers, so $|G|=2m$ and $G$ is dihedral. [step 2.1, F4, F5, F8, F10, algebra]

3.2 **Parabolic recurrences and low-rank bases.** By [F6], the orbit is in bijection with the cosets $wW_T$ and $d(v)$ is the length of the unique minimal representative of its coset. These are left cosets by [F14]. By [F16], each parabolic $W_T$ has the restricted Coxeter presentation and its intrinsic length equals the ambient length; deleting the selected node gives the smaller type named in each recurrence. The factorization in [F14] gives $P_W=P_{W^T}P_{W_T}$; the minimal representatives in $W^T$ therefore give $P_{W^T}=\sum_vt^{d(v)}$, so each quotient polynomial in (2) yields the corresponding recurrence. The one-generator presentations for $A_1$ and $B_1$ coincide; the two-generator presentations for $A_2$ and $I_2(3)$ both have one edge labelled $3$, and those for $B_2$ and $I_2(4)$ both have one edge labelled $4$. The standard $D_3$ diagram is the three-node path $A_3$, so the generator bijection gives a length-preserving presentation isomorphism. Step 2.2 gives $P_{A_1}=[2]_t$ because $W_T=\{1\}$, and for $n\ge2$ gives $P_{A_n}=P_{A_{n-1}}[n+1]_t$. For $n\ge2$, $P_{B_n}=P_{B_{n-1}}[2n]_t$ with $P_{B_1}=P_{A_1}$. In type $D_2$, its two generators commute and are involutions. The presentation map $s_1\mapsto(1,0)$, $s_2\mapsto(0,1)$ into $\{0,1\}^2$ under componentwise addition modulo $2$, and the map $(a,b)\mapsto s_1^a s_2^b$ back are inverse homomorphisms: the first respects the Coxeter relators, and the second is a homomorphism because the generators commute and square to $1$. Hence its elements have lengths $0,1,1,2$, so $P_{D_2}=[2]_t^2$; this is the same two-generator presentation as $I_2(2)$ under the extended notation and the direct product $A_1\times A_1$. Also $P_{D_3}=P_{A_3}$ by the path-presentation isomorphism above, while for $n\ge4$ step 2.4 gives $P_{D_n}=P_{D_{n-1}}[n]_t(1+t^{n-1})$. Step 2.5 gives $P_{I_2(m)}=P_{A_1}[m]_t$ for $m\ge3$. 4.1 **Telescoping the recurrences.** The type-$A$ recurrence gives $P_{A_n}=\prod_{k=2}^{n+1}[k]_t=[n+1]_t!$ and hence $P_{S_n}=P_{A_{n-1}}=[n]_t!$ for $n\ge2$. The type-$B$ recurrence with $B_1=A_1$ gives $P_{B_n}=\prod_{i=1}^n[2i]_t$ for $n\ge1$. In type $D$, the identity $(1+t^{n-1})[n-1]_t=[2n-2]_t$ changes the recurrence for $n\ge4$ into $P_{D_n}=[n]_t\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}[2i]_t$; this formula also holds for $D_3=A_3$ and for $D_2$ by step 3.2. Step 3.2 gives $P_{I_2(m)}=[2]_t[m]_t$. [step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 **Evaluation at one and group orders.** By [F11] and [F13], $P_W(1)=\sum_{w\in W}1=|W|$ (with cardinality as in [F10]); the polynomial evaluation is valid because the groups identified in step 3.1 are finite. The formulas give $P_{A_n}(1)=(n+1)!$, $P_{B_n}(1)=2^nn!$, $P_{D_n}(1)=2^{n-1}n!$ for $n\ge2$, and $P_{I_2(m)}(1)=2m$. These are the group orders: [F12] gives $|S_{n+1}|=(n+1)!$ and $|S_n|=n!$; each permutation has $2^n$ sign assignments in type $B_n$ and $2^{n-1}$ even sign assignments in type $D_n$ (the first $n-1$ signs determine the last); the direct $D_2$ presentation in step 3.2 has four elements; and the dihedral group has order $2m$ by step 3.1. The low-rank coincidences are respected: $P_{A_1}=P_{B_1}$, $P_{A_2}=[2]_t[3]_t=P_{I_2(3)}$, $P_{B_2}=[2]_t[4]_t=P_{I_2(4)}$, $P_{D_2}=[2]_t^2$, and $P_{D_3}=[2]_t[3]_t[4]_t=P_{A_3}$. No invariant degrees and no exceptional diagrams are used. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, F10, F11, F12, F13, algebra] ∎
