## Statement

Let $G\leq\operatorname{GL}(V)$ be a finite complex reflection group, $n=\dim V$, $S=\mathbb C[V]$, and $R=S^G$. Let $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ be minimal homogeneous invariant generators of $I=SR_+$, with $d_i=\deg f_i>0$. Their common zero set is $\{0\}$, $m=n$, and they form an $S$-regular sequence. Every homogeneous lift of a homogeneous complex basis of $S/I$ is a graded free $R$-basis of $S$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite reflection action and basic invariants in the statement.

[F1] Invariants separate distinct finite orbits ([[lem-finite-linear-group-invariant-polynomials-separate-orbits]]).

[F2] The basic invariants generate $R$ and are algebraically independent ([[lem-finite-reflection-invariant-generators-are-algebraically-independent]]).

[F3] Regularity means successive injectivity and a nonzero terminal quotient ([[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]).

[F4] Depth is the supremum of lengths of regular sequences in the maximal ideal ([[def-depth-with-respect-to-an-ideal]]); equality with support dimension defines Cohen--Macaulayness ([[def-cohen-macaulay-local-module-and-ring]]).

[F5] Depth is bounded by support dimension for a nonzero finite module over a Noetherian local ring ([[thm-depth-bounded-by-support-dimension]]).

[F6] Support dimension is the least length of a tuple with finite-length quotient, and tuples of that length are parameter systems ([[thm-dimension-and-parameters-for-modules]]).

[F7] Parameter systems on a nonzero finite local Cohen--Macaulay module are regular ([[cor-every-system-of-parameters-is-regular-in-a-cohen-macaulay-module]]).

[F8] A polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

## Proof

1.1 Choose coordinates $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. For each coordinate $x_j$, the polynomial $\prod_{g\in G}(T-gx_j)$ is monic of degree $q=|G|$, has invariant coefficients, and vanishes at $T=x_j$. Its coefficient of $T^{q-a}$ is homogeneous of degree $a$. Reduce powers $x_j^q$ using these equations. Induction on the sum of exponents shows that the monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ with $0\leq a_j<q$ generate $S$ over $R$; the reductions preserve total degree. Thus $S/I$ is finite dimensional. If all $f_i(v)$ vanish, every invariant takes its constant value at $v$ by F2. F1 applied to $Gv$ and $\{0\}$ forces $v=0$. Conversely positive-degree polynomials vanish at zero. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 F2 identifies $R$ with a polynomial algebra in $m$ variables of positive weights $d_i$. The number of its monomials of weighted degree at most $N$ grows between positive constants times $(N+1)^m$: when $m>0$, all exponents at most $\lfloor N/(m\max d_i)\rfloor$ give the lower bound, and all exponents are at most $N$ for the upper bound; for $m=0$ the count is one. The analogous count for $S$ grows as $(N+1)^n$, by the same coordinate argument. Since $R_{\leq N}\subseteq S_{\leq N}$, the lower bound forces $m\leq n$. The finite homogeneous spanning family of 1.1 gives a graded surjection from finitely many degree shifts of $R$ onto $S$, so the upper bound forces $n\leq m$. Hence $m=n$, without a transcendence-degree or Nullstellensatz appeal. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Put $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ and $A=S_{\mathfrak m}$. This is a nonzero Noetherian local ring: F8 applies over the field, and any ideal in a localization is generated by the images of generators of its contraction. The coordinate tuple is regular on $A$: after quotienting by its first $j$ terms the ring is the localization at the origin of the polynomial domain in the remaining coordinates, and multiplication by the next coordinate is injective. The terminal quotient is $\mathbb C$. Therefore F4 gives $\operatorname{depth} A\geq n$, F6 gives $\dim A\leq n$, and F5 forces equality. Thus $A$ is Cohen--Macaulay. The quotient $A/IA$ is nonzero, since $I\subseteq\mathfrak m$, and finite dimensional over $\mathbb C$ by 1.1. It has finite length as an $A$-module: every strict module chain is a strict complex-subspace chain. Now 2.1 and F6 make $(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ a parameter system, so F7 makes it regular on $A$. [F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Regularity descends here to the graded ring $S$. Indeed, for a homogeneous ideal $J$ and nonzero homogeneous class $u\in S/J$, localization at $\mathfrak m$ cannot kill $u$: if $su=0$ with $s\notin\mathfrak m$, the lowest-degree component is $s(0)u\ne0$. If multiplication by a homogeneous $f_j$ had a kernel on $S/(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})$, one of the homogeneous components of a nonzero kernel element would therefore give a nonzero kernel after localization, contradicting 3.1. Every quotient remains nonzero because the ideals have positive-degree generators. F3 proves global regularity. [F3, step 3.1]

5.1 Let $H_M(t)=\sum_{a\geq0}\dim_{\mathbb C} M_a t^a$ for these graded spaces, whose components are finite dimensional. The injective multiplication by $f_j$ on the preceding quotient gives, degree by degree, $H_{S/(f_1,\ldots,f_j)}(t)=(1-t^{d_j})H_{S/(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})}(t)$. Counting polynomial monomials gives $H_S(t)=(1-t)^{-n}$ and, by F2, $H_R(t)=\prod_j(1-t^{d_j})^{-1}$. Thus $H_{S/I}(t)H_R(t)=H_S(t)$. [F2, step 4.1]

6.1 Choose any homogeneous basis $\bar b_1,\ldots,\bar b_l$ of the finite-dimensional graded quotient and homogeneous lifts $b_a$. They span $S$ over $R$ by degree induction: for homogeneous $s$ subtract the combination of lifts representing its quotient class; the remainder is $\sum_j f_j s_j$ with $\deg s_j<\deg s$, so apply induction. The resulting graded surjection $\bigoplus_a R(-\deg b_a)\longrightarrow S$ has equal source and target dimensions in every degree by 5.1; its degreewise kernels are zero. Hence it is an isomorphism and the lifts are a free basis. For $n=0$, $S=R=\mathbb C$, $I=0$, the empty regular sequence has nonzero terminal quotient and the basis is any nonzero constant. Degree-one invariants and fixed directions are allowed throughout. All basis/lift selections concern one finite-dimensional space; no AC is used. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 5.1] ∎
