Full proof draft:

1.1 The posterior coordinate variances satisfy $C^{-4}i^{2p}/(i^q+n)\le v_i\le C^4 i^{2p}/(i^q+n)$, and the noise weights satisfy $t_i=n\kappa_i^{-2}/(i^{1+2\alpha}\kappa_i^{-2}+n)^2\le C^6 n i^{2p}/(i^q+n)^2$. Split at $N$. For $i\le N$, $t_i\le C^6 i^{2p}/n$ and $\sum_{i\le N} i^{2p}\le2N^{1+2p}$ for $N\ge2$. For $i>N$, $t_i\le C^6 n i^{-2-4\alpha-2p}$; the decreasing-function integral comparison bounds its sum by $2C^6nN^{-1-4\alpha-2p}\le2C^6R$. Thus $\sum_i t_i\le4C^6R$ and $\sup_i t_i\le C^6R/N$. Standard normal moments give $E Z_i^2=1$, $\operatorname{Var}(Z_i^2)=2$, so the variance of $\sum t_iZ_i^2$ is at most $8C^{12}R^2/N$. For completeness, the fourth-moment identity follows by twice integrating $\phi'(x)=-x\phi(x)$ by parts: $\int x^4\phi(x)dx=3\int x^2\phi(x)dx=3$, with boundary terms zero. Finite sums pass to the nonnegative infinite sum by monotone convergence; the centered sums are Cauchy in $L^2$ since $\sum t_i^2<\infty$.

1.2 All $t_i(\alpha)$ decrease in $\alpha$. Thus the same $Z_i$ couple the process and $\sup_{a\le\alpha\le b}\|W(\alpha)\|^2=\sum_i t_i(a)Z_i^2$. Chebyshev at threshold $R_a$ above its mean yields $P\{\sup\|W\|^2>(4C^6+1)R_a\}\le8C^{12}/N_a$, proving (iii).

2.1 For the posterior-radius lower bound, retain only integer indices $N/2\le i\le N$. For $N\ge6$ there are at least $N/3$ such indices, and each has $v_i\ge C^{-4}2^{-2p-1}N^{2p}/n$. The retained quadratic sum $U_*$ therefore has mean at least $d_0R$, where $d_0=C^{-4}2^{-2p-1}/3$. Each retained variance weight is at most $C^4N^{2p}/n=C^4R/N$, so $\operatorname{Var}U_*\le2(C^4R/N)E U_*$. Also $E U_*\le C^4R$, hence $P\{U_*<d_0R/2\}\le8C^8/(d_0^2N)$. Since the full posterior squared norm dominates $U_*$, for sufficiently large $N$ its $1-\gamma$ quantile is at least $d_0R/2$. This proves (i), with $d=d_0/2$, independently of $A_n$. The full norm is finite a.s. when $\alpha>0$ because $\sum v_i\le\sum i^{-1-2\alpha}<\infty$. At zero use only the defined whole-space branch.

3.1 Each squared bias coefficient increases with $\alpha$, so it suffices to consider $b$. Set $q=q_b$, $N=N_b$. By the bounds on $\kappa_i$, $\|B(b)\|^2\le C^8\sum_i i^{2+4b+4p}\theta_i^2/(i^q+n)^2$. For $2\le i\le N$ and $N\ge4$, $i^{q+2p}/\log i\le N^{q+2p}/\log N$. Indeed $x^r/\log x$ for $r\ge1$ has its maximum on $[2,N]$ at an endpoint, and its value at $N\ge4$ exceeds its value at $2$. The contribution of these indices is consequently at most $C^8R h_n(b;\theta)$. The index $1$ is zero by hypothesis.

3.2 Put $m=\lfloor N\rfloor$. For large $n$, $m\ge N_0$ and uniformly for $b\le A_n$, $m^{1+2b}\ge N^{1+2b}/2$ and $\log m\ge\log N/2$, because $(1+2A_n)/N_{A_n}\to0$. On the block $m\le i\le\lfloor\rho m\rfloor$, $i^q+n\le(\rho^q+1)n\le2\rho^q n$. Therefore $h_n(b;\theta)\ge N^{2b}\rho^{-2q}\sum_{i=m}^{\lfloor\rho m\rfloor}\theta_i^2/16$. The polished-tail inequality bounds $\sum_{i>N}\theta_i^2$ by $16L_0\rho^{2q}R h_n(b;\theta)$. Each actual bias coefficient has magnitude at most one, so this bounds the remaining squared bias. Together with step 3.1 and $h_n(b)\le8C^8$, this proves (ii).
