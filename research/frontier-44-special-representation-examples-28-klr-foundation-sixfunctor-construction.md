# Constructing analytic derived operations

Author: `/root/klr_six_functor_construction`. This is new construction under the informed KLR reopening. It preserves the earlier rounds and does not assert native acceptance. All new content remains draft. There are no canonical item, page, plan, state or gate edits.

## Contracts and dependency boundary

Let k be a characteristic-zero field. The constructions below work over any field. Assume AC where injective replacements or a vector-space basis is selected. Spaces are locally compact Hausdorff, second countable, and have finite covering dimension. In the intended application they are analytic spaces of separated finite-type complex varieties. Ordinary sheaves are sheaves of k-vector spaces, equivalently modules over the locally constant ring sheaf k_X. The derived category is formed in all k-sheaves; constructibility is a subsequent full-subcategory condition. There is no coherent/quasicoherent sheaf substitution.

The geometric inputs needed to restrict the construction to D^b_c are: finite compatible Whitney/conical stratifications for each algebraic map with separately typed source/target occurrences; local finite cochain models on links, strata and fibres; local contractible balls on smooth strata; and finite triangulation of compact fibres. These are the topology author's new supplier, not established by naming it. A general algebraic map is not assumed globally simplicial. Constructibility of all outputs and smooth nonproper base change remain dependent on this geometric input.

The local proofs below separate the elementary support calculus from that input. In particular the exceptional adjoint is constructed through a finite fibrewise-soft resolution. It is not introduced by assuming Verdier duality or a six-operation package.

## A. Ordinary derived operations (new lemma)

Proposed ID `lem-bounded-analytic-field-sheaves-have-derived-tensor-hom-and-pullback-pushforward-adjunctions`. Dependencies: `lem-ringed-space-module-sheaves-enough-injectives`, `lem-bounded-below-complexes-admit-injective-replacements`, `thm-injective-complexes-model-the-bounded-below-derived-category`, `lem-stalk-inverse-image-sheaf`, `thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise`, `thm-inverse-direct-image-adjunction`, `def-sheaf-hom`, `thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness`, `def-axiom-of-choice`.

**Given:** a continuous f:X→Y; bounded k-sheaf complexes A,B; bounded-below target complexes where indicated; supplied k-linear injective replacements.

1.1 The inverse-image stalk is (f^{-1}F)_x=F_{f(x)}. Thus f*=f^{-1} is exact, sends quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms, and gives an exact functor on derived categories. The sheaf inverse/direct image adjunction is k-linear: its two transpositions send a section to its restriction/colimit class, preserving scalar multiplication. Therefore f*⊣f_* in k-sheaves. For an injective I on X, Hom_Y(-,f_*I)=Hom_X(f*(-),I) is exact, so f_*I is injective. Applying the chain-Hom adjunction to injective target complexes gives f*⊣Rf_* on D^+. The triangle identities are the section-level restriction/gluing identities; no separate choices of derived unit or counit are made. [given, construct]

1.2 Define F⊗_kG by sheafifying the sectionwise tensor presheaf. Its stalk is F_x⊗_kG_x: filtered colimits commute with finite tensor expressions. Define internal Hom on U by k-linear sheaf morphisms F|U→G|U. The elementary sheaf adjunction Hom_X(H⊗F,G)=Hom_X(H,Hom(F,G)) follows locally from the vector-space tensor-Hom transposition and gluing; the unit is h↦(f↦h⊗f), and evaluation sends h⊗φ to φ(h). All k-vector spaces are flat, hence tensoring sheaves is exact on stalks. A bounded complex A is K-flat: filter the finite-column total tensor with an acyclic complex by A-degree, whose successive columns are acyclic exact tensors; finite induction makes the total complex acyclic. Therefore A⊗^L B is the ordinary signed total tensor of bounded representatives, independent of those representatives. [construct, algebra]

1.3 Let B→I be a bounded-below injective replacement and A a bounded representative. Define RHom(A,B)=Hom•(A,I), with degree-n term the finite product of Hom(A^p,I^{p+n}) and differential d(φ)=d_Iφ−(−1)^nφd_A. Each sheaf Hom(A^p,I^q) is injective, since it is right adjoint to the exact functor −⊗A^p. The total complex is bounded below and termwise injective (only finitely many p occur). It is therefore K-injective. Changing A by a quasi-isomorphism changes this total Hom by a quasi-isomorphism: for every open U, its cone's sections are Hom•_X(j_U!Cone(A)|U,I), which is acyclic because j_U! is exact and I is K-injective. A sectionwise acyclic complex is stalkwise acyclic because stalks are exact filtered colimits. Changing I is handled by its homotopy-equivalence comparison. Thus this is the actual internal derived Hom, not sectionwise Hom of stalks. [step 1.2, construct]

1.4 The signed chain transposition Hom•(C⊗A,I)=Hom•(C,Hom•(A,I)) is an isomorphism: a degree-n map out of C^r⊗A^p corresponds to the same bilinear map with its Koszul reordering; both differentials are d_Iφ−(−1)^nφd_source. Since I and Hom•(A,I) are K-injective and bounded C is K-flat, H^0 gives tensor⊣RHom in D^b, with D^+ target. Restriction to opens gives the internal version. Associativity, symmetry, evaluation and currying commute because they send pure tensors to the same maps with the same permutation sign. Pentagon, symmetry and triangle identities hold on pure tensors, hence on sheaves and their derived complexes. This supplies an actual dg model for these operations. [step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra] ∎

## B. Compact neighbourhood and soft support calculus (new pair)

Proposed A page `compact-support-sheaf-calculus`; B page `compact-support-sheaf-calculus-examples`. New items `lem-sections-near-a-compact-set-are-sections-on-that-set`, `lem-c-soft-field-sheaves-are-compact-support-acyclic`, `lem-finite-polyhedral-covering-dimension-bounds-all-sheaf-cohomology`, and `lem-finite-fibre-dimension-gives-bounded-fibrewise-soft-resolutions`. The following proofs do not use constructible objects to bound arbitrary-sheaf cohomology.

### B1. Compact neighbourhood sections

**Given:** a compact K in a locally compact Hausdorff X and a k-sheaf F. Claim: colim_{U⊃K} F(U)=Γ(K,F|K). A section on K is locally represented by ambient sections. Compactness chooses finitely many domains U_i and compact subsets C_i⊂U_i∩K covering K, by finite shrinking in the compact Hausdorff K. On C_i∩C_j the ambient representatives agree as germs, hence on an ambient open W_ij containing this compact intersection. Shrink their ambient domains around C_i so pairwise intersections lie in W_ij: to see the two-set shrinking, take a relatively compact neighbourhood of C_i∪C_j and a smaller W_0 with closure∩(C_i∪C_j) inside W_ij; the disjoint compact parts C_i\W_0 and C_j\W_0 have disjoint neighbourhoods, by compact-Hausdorff normality. Add W_0 to those neighbourhoods, shrink within U_i,U_j, and repeat for the finitely many pairs. The representatives now glue on a neighbourhood of K. Injectivity is equally local: two representatives equal on K agree on a neighbourhood of each point of K, and the union of finitely many such neighbourhoods is a neighbourhood of K. All constructions are k-linear and natural. No cohomological comparison is assumed.

A useful strengthened form is simultaneous finite gluing with prescribed zero germs on a compact collar: apply the same argument to the zero representative on that collar. This form is used below to ensure compact support rather than just an unrestricted extension.

### B2. c-soft acyclicity

A sheaf F is c-soft when Γ(X,F)→Γ(K,F|K) is onto for every compact K. In a locally compact Hausdorff space this is equivalent to extension with compact support: put K inside an open W with compact closure, choose a smaller neighbourhood W_0 of K with closure inside W, prescribe the given section near K and zero on the compact collar closure(W)\W_0, extend that section on the disjoint compact subsets by c-softness, restrict to W and glue zero across the exterior. The result is compactly supported and has the prescribed germs on K. The same argument gives an extension supported in any prescribed neighbourhood of K.

**Given:** 0→F→G→H→0 and F c-soft. Every section of H on a compact K lifts to a section of G on some neighbourhood of K. Choose finitely many local lifts over a compact shrinking C_1,…,C_m of K. Inductively patch the lift on the previous compact union with the next lift: their difference lies in F on a neighbourhood of the compact intersection. Extend its restriction on that compact intersection to a section of F over the new domain, using the support-controlled c-soft extension. Correct the new lift by that section. They then agree on a neighbourhood of the compact intersection. The finite neighbourhood shrinking of B1 makes their domains meet only where they agree, so they glue. This produces the claimed neighbourhood lift. If G is also c-soft, its extension gives a global lift; consequently H is c-soft.

For a compactly supported section s of H, choose a relatively compact W containing its support and perform the same finite patching including the zero lift on a compact collar near ∂W. The errors on overlaps lie in F and are corrected by extensions supported in the corresponding neighbourhoods. The lift is zero near the collar and can be glued to zero outside W. Thus Γ_c(G)→Γ_c(H) is onto when F and G are c-soft. Injectivity at Γ_c(F) and exactness at Γ_c(G) follow by support and sheaf kernels. This proves compact-support exactness on short exact sequences of c-soft sheaves.

An injective k-sheaf I is flasque: j_U!k_U→j_V!k_V is monic for U⊂V, and injectivity extends a morphism corresponding to a section on U. B1 extends any section on compact K to a neighbourhood, and flasqueness extends that section to X, so I is c-soft. Resolve a c-soft F by k-linear injectives. The successive quotients remain c-soft by the preceding patching lemma. Applying Γ_c gives an exact positive-degree complex. Hence R^qΓ_c(F)=0 for q>0. This argument also proves acyclicity for Γ on compact spaces, where Γ_c=Γ. Restriction of a c-soft sheaf to an open set is c-soft by support-controlled extension. Restriction to a closed subset is c-soft, since any compact subset is also compact in X and B1 lifts its section to the ambient restriction. Extension by zero of a c-soft sheaf on an open subset is c-soft: a section on a compact set has zero germs near its compact boundary part, leaving a compact support set in the open subset, to which the preceding support-controlled extension applies. Direct sums preserve c-softness: a section on a compact set has, locally, only finitely many nonzero summands; a finite cover produces a finite global set of summands, each extended separately. In particular tensor with a constant vector space preserves c-softness after choosing a basis. AC is declared for that last basis and for injectives.

### B3. Čech comparison on compact metric spaces, and the dimension bound

**Given:** a compact metric X and any k-sheaf F. Let Čech cohomology mean the directed refinement limit of finite-cover Čech groups, with the usual chain-homotopic refinement maps. This is not the fixed-cover comparison theorem alone.

To lift an n-cochain through an epimorphism G→H after refinement, first take a compact shrinking V_i with closure(V_i)⊂U_i of its finite parent cover. For each tuple J of distinct parent indices the cochain's section on U_J has a local lift cover. For x put I_x={i:x∈closure(V_i)}. Choose W_x avoiding every closure(V_i) with i∉I_x, and contained in one lift domain for each tuple J⊂I_x. There are finitely many such requirements and x lies in every relevant U_J, so W_x exists. Take a finite star-refinement of {W_x} and refine it by {V_i}. If a tuple of refined members intersects, its union lies in one W_x; every parent label belongs to I_x, because a member meets W_x but W_x avoids the other parent closures. Its intersection therefore lies in a chosen lift domain. This gives a lift of every component of the refined cochain. The same refinement argument applies to equality of lifted cochains.

For a cocycle in H, lift it as above to a cochain g in G; dg is a cocycle in F. Its class is independent of the lifts since their difference is an F-cochain, and independent of refinement by common refinement and the alternating prism homotopy between label maps. This defines the connecting homomorphism. Exactness is the usual elementary cochain calculation: a class with zero image is, after refinement, the image of a cocycle in the kernel; a zero connecting class permits subtracting an F-cochain to make its chosen lift a cocycle. Degree zero is sheaf gluing. Thus refinement-limit Čech groups form a δ-functor. They vanish on injectives: an injective is flasque, and the augmented finite-cover Čech complex of a flasque sheaf is exact, proved by induction on the number of opens using the restriction surjection on their union/intersection (equivalently the standard two-cover cone). Embedding F in an injective therefore effaces all positive groups. Both this δ-functor and injective derived cohomology are effaceable and agree in degree zero. Dimension shifting along F→I→Q recursively supplies the unique natural comparison isomorphism in every degree. This proves global Čech-derived comparison here without assuming any acyclic cover of F.

If X is the realization of a finite simplicial complex of dimension d, arbitrarily fine barycentric subdivisions exist: each simplex's mesh decreases by at most d/(d+1) at each subdivision, by its barycentre formula. A Lebesgue number for a given finite cover ensures the open vertex stars of a sufficiently fine subdivision refine it. Every nonempty intersection of these stars has at most d+1 distinct vertices, because such vertices belong to one simplex. Hence the normalized Čech complex is zero in degrees>d. These covers are cofinal. The preceding comparison gives H^q(X,F)=0 for all q>d and every sheaf F, with no constructibility assumption. The same bound applies to compactly supported cohomology on every open U in a finite compact polyhedron P, not only a semialgebraic open: j!F on P is an ordinary sheaf, and RΓ_c(U,F)=RΓ(P,j!F), as follows by applying the c-soft resolution and B2 extension-zero acyclicity. If a fibre is P minus a closed subcomplex, every open in the fibre is also open in P. Thus the dimension bound applies to all fibre-opens required in B4. The last equality is a proved resolution identity, not an unproved definition swap.

### B4. Finite fibrewise-soft resolutions

**Given:** f:X→Y, with each fibre locally compact Hausdorff, and a uniform integer d bounding H^q_c(V,F)=0 for q>d for every open V in every fibre and every k-sheaf F on V. The topology supplier must establish this using actual finite triangulations/covering dimension; constructible cochain finiteness alone does not meet this hypothesis.

Resolve any k-sheaf A on X by global injectives. Their restrictions to each closed fibre are c-soft by B2. For the cokernel Q after d steps, dimension shifting on every fibre-open V gives H^q_c(V,Q|V)=H^{q+d}_c(V,A|V)=0 for q>0. A sheaf E on a locally compact Hausdorff space with H^1_c(V,E)=0 for every open V is c-soft: take compact K⊂V with compact closure, use the sheaf-exact sequence j!E|V\K→E|V→i*E|K→0. The compact-support long exact sequence and H^1_c(V\K,E)=0 lift every section on K to a compactly supported section on V. The exact sequence is stalkwise exact. Thus Q restricted to each fibre is c-soft. Truncating the resolution at Q gives a length-d resolution by fibrewise-c-soft sheaves. The same applies to k_X and gives the finite K used below. On a bounded complex, totalize such resolutions; the bounded number of resolution columns ensures there is no infinite-diagonal convergence issue.

## C. Proper-support direct image and its exceptional adjoint

Proposed definition `def-derived-operations-on-analytic-sheaves-over-locally-compact-spaces`; proposed theorem `thm-compactly-supported-derived-pushforward-has-exceptional-inverse-image-adjoint`. Required new suppliers are B1–B4; ordinary derived suppliers are A. The theorem's locally compact contract requires finite fibrewise cohomological dimension. The algebraic specialization additionally requires the separately owned geometric stratification input.

For a sheaf A, define (f!A)(V) to be sections on f^{-1}V whose closed support is proper over V. Local properness of that support is local on the base: preimages of a compact set are covered by finitely many relatively compact base neighbourhoods; hence compactness is checked locally. This proves sheaf gluing. The functor is left exact by the sheaf kernel and its support. If f is proper, f!A=f_*A. For an open inclusion it is exact extension by zero; for a closed inclusion it is exact direct image.

### C1. Compact-support fibre stalk

The map (f!A)_y→Γ_c(X_y,A|X_y) restricts a representative to the fibre. It is injective: if that restriction is zero, it vanishes on a neighbourhood of every point of its fibre support. A section with support proper over a base neighbourhood has compact fibre support; remove that neighbourhood of the support and use properness/closedness of its remaining support to shrink the base neighbourhood until the section is zero.

For surjectivity, represent a compactly supported fibre section by ambient sections near its compact support, using B1. Choose a relatively compact ambient neighbourhood W of this support. On the compact collar closure(W)\W_0 its fibre germs are zero. The strengthened B1 gluing with the zero section gives a section near X_y∩closure(W) that is zero near this collar. The compact collar can be avoided after shrinking a neighbourhood of y: each collar point not on the fibre has a base-image neighbourhood missing y, and the finitely covered compact collar yields a single such shrink. Restrict to W, glue zero outside closure(W), and shrink the base as just described. The resulting section has support in a fixed compact set, so is proper over the base neighbourhood and represents the fibre section. This proves the stalk identity for arbitrary sheaves. Naturality is restriction of representatives.

For injective A the restriction to X_y is c-soft, so an injective resolution restricts to a Γ_c-acyclic resolution. Exactness of stalks yields
(Rf!A)_y=RΓ_c(X_y,A|X_y).
In particular B4 gives finite cohomological dimension≤d and Rf!:D^b(X)→D^b(Y). A fibrewise-c-soft sheaf is f!-acyclic by the same stalk computation. Open extensions and direct sums of such sheaves remain fibrewise-c-soft, by B2.

### C2. Actual adjoint construction

Choose the finite fibrewise-soft resolution k_X→K of B4, in degrees0,…,d. For an open U⊂X write K_U=j_U!j_U*K. Let I be a bounded-below injective complex on Y. Set
H_K(I)(U)=Hom•_Y(f!K_U,I),
with signed total-Hom differential. Each degree has finitely many K-columns. A bounded-below flasque complex on X results, as follows.

For a cover U=⋃U_i, the augmented extension-zero Čech chain complex of K_U is exact on stalks: at a point the simplex of indices containing that point has the elementary contracting homotopy that adds a fixed containing index. Every term is a direct sum of fibrewise-soft open extensions and is f!-acyclic. Its cycles are also f!-acyclic. Indeed dimension shifting along its short exact cycle sequences identifies R^qf! of a cycle with R^{q+r}f! of an earlier cycle for arbitrarily large r; choose r>d. Therefore applying f! preserves exactness. Applying Hom_Y(-,I^q) preserves exactness by injectivity; in particular the first three terms give the sheaf equalizer for H_K(I). For V⊂U, f!K_V→f!K_U is monic because K_V→K_U is monic and f! left exact. Injectivity of each I^q gives surjective restriction on every Hom term; finite products over K-degree give surjectivity in every total degree. Thus H_K(I) is indeed a sheaf complex and termwise flasque, with lower bound inf(I)−d.

Let E_U=j_U!k_U. The complex K_U resolves E_U by f!-acyclic terms, so f!K_U represents Rf!E_U. Also RHom_X(E_U,H_K(I))=RΓ(U,H_K(I))=Γ(U,H_K(I)), since the bounded-below flasque complex is Γ(U)-acyclic. This is exactly Hom•_Y(f!K_U,I), representing RHom_Y(Rf!E_U,I). The comparison is evaluation, natural in U and in maps of generators.

Every k-sheaf is a quotient of a sum of E_U: sum over all nonzero pairs (U,s∈A(U)) and send the generator 1 to s. At a stalk any germ has such a representative; the reduced cover of the zero sheaf is zero. For a bounded-above complex A, construct M descending from its upper bound, without assuming these generators projective or lifting its differential through unrelated covers. Maintain that Z^n(M)→Z^n(A) is stalk-surjective and cohomology is already isomorphic above n. Put Q=ker(A^{n−1}⊕Z^n(M)→A^n), where the map is (a,m)↦d_Aa−ε(m), and take the reduced generator cover M^{n−1}→Q. The first projection defines ε and the second defines d_M. At a stalk a cycle a lifts using the pair (a,0), proving cycle surjectivity in the next degree. The image of d_M in Z^n(M) is exactly the ε-preimage of the A-boundaries, proving the cohomology isomorphism in degree n. The defining kernel relation proves εd_M=d_Aε and d_M²=0. Starting with zero above the bound and iterating gives a bounded-above generator complex M→A, termwise stalk-surjective and a quasi-isomorphism. This is the k-linear kernel construction of the actual bounded-above flat-resolution supplier, with j!k generators; it uses no map lifting through a nonprojective object. Direct sums of generators are acyclic against the flasque terms of H_K(I). To justify computing derived Hom using M, replace H_K(I) by injectives J. For each generator E_U the map Hom•(E_U,H_K(I))→Hom•(E_U,J) is a quasi-isomorphism by Γ(U)-acyclicity; products over the summands are exact over a field and preserve these quasi-isomorphisms. The Hom bicomplex has bounded diagonals (M is bounded above; H,J are bounded below), so row comparison proves the total comparison. Hom•(M,J) computes RHom(A,H_K(I)) by K-injectivity of J.

Termwise evaluation on generator summands gives the chain identity
Hom•_X(M,H_K(I))=Hom•_Y(f!(K⊗M),I).
The bounded K is K-flat over k, so K⊗M resolves A. Its terms are direct sums of K_U and hence f!-acyclic. Applying f! computes Rf!A even though M is bounded above rather than below: if an acyclic complex has f!-acyclic terms, dimension shift across its cycle sequences and the bound d show its f!-image is acyclic. Truncate below any prescribed degree before a comparison; the finite cohomological bound makes the omitted tail invisible in that degree. This proves the needed acyclic-resolution comparison without an unbounded-resolution assertion. Consequently the displayed chain identity proves
RHom_X(A,H_K(I))=RHom_Y(Rf!A,I)
for bounded A and bounded-below I. H^0 gives the natural exceptional Hom transposition for bounded A and bounded-below B, with f^!B represented by H_K(I_B). Its counit is constructed directly rather than transposing an identity of a possibly unbounded source: for each closed Q⊂f^{-1}V proper over V, the complex of Q-supported sections of H_K(I) is Hom•_Y(f_!(K|Q),I), by the exact open/closed support sequence and fibrewise acyclicity. Since Q→V is proper, f_!(K|Q)=f_*(K|Q). Precompose with k_V→f_*k_Q→f_*(K|Q), induced by the constant-section unit and K coaugmentation. This gives a map to I(V); the maps commute with support enlargement and restriction, and hence give f_!H_K(I)→I. The flasque terms of H_K(I) are f_!-acyclic, so this is a derived counit. On every generator E_U its induced Hom transposition is exactly the defining evaluation above. On the bounded constructible categories, once their geometric bounded-output theorem makes f^!B a bounded object, this Hom transposition is a genuine adjunction and its two triangle identities follow by inverse transposition. In that category the unique identity-transposition comparisons between choices of K and I satisfy identity and cocycle laws. The topological lemma does not infer an unrestricted D^+-source adjunction solely from its bounded-source generator argument. This constructs the adjoint, rather than defining it circularly as Df*D.

### C3. Closed inclusion and localization

For i:Z→X closed, with open complement j, define Γ_ZF=ker(F→j_*j*F). It has zero stalks off Z, hence is i*i^{-1}Γ_ZF. Maps i*A→F land in Γ_ZF because the source has zero restriction to the open complement. Thus i^!_0F=i^{-1}Γ_ZF is right adjoint to exact i*. It preserves injectives. Ri^!_0, computed on k-linear injectives, agrees with the preceding constructed exceptional adjoint by uniqueness. The first localization triangle j!j*A→A→i*i*A follows from the stalk-exact short sequence. For injective I, the restriction map I→j_*j*I is onto sections by flasqueness; its kernel is Γ_ZI. Restriction j* preserves injectives because j! is exact, so the corresponding short exact complex sequence gives the second triangle i*i^!A→A→Rj_*j*A. This also proves local RHom(i*k_Z,A)=RΓ_ZA: tensor-Hom with i*k_Z localizes the preceding adjunction on opens. Hence support cohomology in the Thom/diagonal argument is exactly the localization construction used here.

## D. Base change, projection and coherent traces

Proposed ID `thm-analytic-sheaf-six-operations-have-coherent-base-change-projection-and-trace`. The following clauses are constructions from A–C. Restriction to bounded constructible outputs and smooth nonproper base change remain the geometric input described at the start.

For a Cartesian square X'→X over Y'→Y, inverse image of an injective I on X restricts on every fibre of f' to the restriction of I on the canonically homeomorphic original fibre. It is therefore fibrewise-c-soft and f'!-acyclic. The section restriction map u*f!I→f'!v*I is an isomorphism on stalks by C1. Applying it to an injective complex proves u*Rf!≈Rf'!v*. If f is proper, this is ordinary proper base change u*Rf*≈Rf'*v*. These are the canonical restriction/exchange maps, not arbitrary chosen isomorphisms.

For proper maps ordinary compact fibre cohomology can also be verified independently: restriction of injectives to compact fibres is c-soft and Γ-acyclic by B2; compact-neighbourhood sections are B1; preimage neighbourhoods are cofinal because f is closed, with V=Y\f(X\U). This recovers exactly the Stacks full proof and ensures the exchange is the canonical derived adjunction map.

Projection formula is obtained on fibres. For bounded A,B choose A→I injective. On X_y, I is c-soft and f*B is the constant complex B_y. Tensor with each B_y term is a direct sum of I|X_y, hence c-soft; Γ_c(E⊗V)=Γ_c(E)⊗V because a compactly supported section has a finite local tensor presentation and a finite compact cover uses only finitely many coefficient vectors. This proves the canonical map Rf!A⊗B→Rf!(A⊗f*B) is an isomorphism on stalks. Its map is the adjoint of the tensor of the f*⊣Rf* counit with id, equivalently sectionwise s⊗b↦s⊗f*b. The signed totalization supplies the bounded-complex version.

The support definition proves (gf)!=g!f!: a section proper over Z has support proper over Y, since the inverse image of a compact L⊂Y is a closed subset of the inverse image of the compact g(L); its image support is proper over Z. Conversely, if support is locally proper over Y and its image support proper over Z, the inverse image of a compact in Z lies over a compact image support and is compact. The support of f!s is f(support s), by the fibre-stalk identity and local properness, so these are inverse descriptions. Direct images of globally c-soft sheaves by f! are c-soft: a section over a compact base set is represented on a neighbourhood by B1, its total support over that compact set is compact by properness of the representing support, and support-controlled c-soft extension on X extends it; the compact extension pushes forward to an extension on Y. Thus f! sends injectives to g!-acyclic sheaves. Applying the two ordinary support functors to an injective resolution gives the canonical derived composition R(gf)!=Rg!Rf!; the same comparison proves associativity for triples, since the section support bijection is literally associative.

Exceptional composition (gf)^!≈f^!g^! is the unique adjoint transpose of that composition isomorphism. Traces tr_f:Rf!f^!→Id are the actual counits from C2. Under the composition identification,
tr_{gf}=tr_g ∘ Rg!(tr_f)g^!.
This equality is obtained by transposing the identity of f^!g^!; both sides have that transpose, as can be checked using the two triangle identities. Thus traces compose without a new axiom. Identity units and counits are the identity transpositions.

The two composites of base-change maps in a pasted rectangle restrict a representative section to the same fibre section; hence they agree before deriving. Resolution comparisons preserve that equality. Projection formulas for composites both send pure tensors s⊗b to the same pullback tensor. Projection/trace compatibility follows by transposition: both composites transpose to evaluation on (s⊗b) followed by the same counit. This proves the projection, base-change, unit and trace squares used in pull-push convolution. These identities hold in the dg resolution model; no triangulated-level isomorphism is chosen independently. The extraordinary proper exchange u^!Rf*≈Rf'*v^! is **not** claimed to follow from simply taking a right-adjoint mate of the ordinary base-change isomorphism. Its actual proof needs the biduality result below. That distinction prevents exchanging the wrong adjunction pairs.

## E. Dualizing complex and constructible biduality: exact remaining interface
