## Scope, conventions, and proof route

- The quasilinear Cauchy datum is a `C^1` immersion `gamma: V subset R^(n-1) -> R^n` and `phi: V -> R`, for `a(x,z) dot Du = b(x,z)`. A compatible initial covector `p_0(y)` obeys `p_0 Dgamma = Dphi`; the augmented characteristic ODE is `Xdot=a(X,Z)`, `Zdot=b(X,Z)`, `Pdot=b_x-(D_xa)^T P+(b_z-a_z dot P)P`. Equivalently, component `j` is `Pdot_j=b_(x_j)+b_z P_j-sum_i P_i(a_(i,x_j)+a_(i,z)P_j)`. This fixes the convention now rather than copying a source with an incompatible sign convention.
- The local graph exists only after the projection `(s,y) mapsto X(s,y)` is locally invertible and the characteristic value is independent of the chosen parameters. The initial rank condition is `rank D_(s,y)X(0,y)=n`, equivalently the determinant with columns `a(gamma(y),phi(y))` and `Dgamma(y)` is nonzero in the codimension-one parametrisation. The initial-Jacobian lemma keeps this rank computation distinct from the datum tangency compatibility lemma.
- A characteristic crossing/caustic is loss of local injectivity or rank of this projected map, not blow-up of the lifted ODE. For Burgers `u_t+u u_x=0`, `X(t,xi)=xi+t u_0(xi)` and the first crossing convention is the infimum in `[0,infinity]` of times at which `1+t u_0'(xi)=0` for some `xi`; it is `infinity` when no such time exists, in particular when `u_0' >= 0`.
- A fully nonlinear equation is `F(x,z,p)=0`, with `F` of class `C^2` on an open subset. The Lagrange–Charpit system is `Xdot=F_p`, `Zdot=p dot F_p`, `pdot=-F_x-p F_z`. The two required invariants are separate: `F(X,Z,P)` and the contact compatibility `Z_y-P dot X_y`. The alternative formula `Zdot=p dot F_p-F` agrees only while `F=0`; it is not silently substituted. The full nonlinear theorem must check the initial projection rank before invoking the inverse theorem.
- A complete integral is only a definition. The envelope lemma adds the explicit hypothesis `D^2_(alpha alpha) S` invertible at a stationary point before using the implicit function theorem. It does not say that every complete integral has a differentiable envelope.
- The Cauchy–Kovalevskaya remark records the exact analytic class: a real-analytic hypersurface, analytic coefficients and normal data through order `m-1`, a PDE in noncharacteristic normal form for the `m`th normal derivative, and local uniqueness only among real-analytic solutions. It is not proved here and is never a dependency target.
