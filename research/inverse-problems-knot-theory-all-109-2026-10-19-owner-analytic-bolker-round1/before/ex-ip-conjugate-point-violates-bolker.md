## Example

Let $X=G=S^2$ be the unit round sphere with its standard real-analytic structure, and give $Z$ the density induced by the product round metric. Let
$$
Z=\{(n,x)\in S^2\times S^2:n\cdot x=0\},
$$
so $n\in G$ labels the oriented great circle $C_n=\{x\in X:n\cdot x=0\}$. The transform uses the analytic density induced on $Z$ and the constant analytic weight $1$. Then $G\leftarrow Z\to X$ is an analytic double fibration, and the geodesic from $x$ to its antipode $-x$ along $C_n$ has conjugate endpoints. Moreover, for every $(n,x)\in Z$, the nonzero data covector $x^\flat\in T_n^*G$ is paired by the canonical relation with both $(x,-n^\flat)$ and $(-x,n^\flat)$. Thus the left projection of the canonical relation is not injective, so the Bolker condition fails globally.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The unit round sphere, the incidence hypersurface $Z$, and the round arclength density on its great-circle fibers.

[F1] [[def-ip-analytic-double-fibration-transform]]: both incidence projections must be analytic submersions, and the required support condition is properness over compact object sets.

[F2] [[def-ip-bolker-condition]]: Bolker requires the left cotangent projection to be globally injective.

[F3] [[lem-ip-double-fibration-conormal-kernel]]: for a defining function $F$, the conormal bundle is generated by $dF$ and the relation uses $(\eta,-\xi)$.

[F4] [[def-riemannian-metric-and-riemannian-manifold]]: the unit round metric identifies tangent vectors and cotangent vectors by $v\mapsto v^\flat$.

[F5] [[ex-sphere-as-a-regular-level-set]]: $T_xS^2=x^\perp$ for every $x\in S^2$.

[F6] [[thm-real-analytic-inverse-and-implicit-function-theorems]]: a regular zero of a real-analytic scalar function is locally an analytic graph.

[F7] [[def-geodesic-variation]]: a smooth family whose parameter curves are affinely parametrized geodesics is a geodesic variation.

[F8] [[def-jacobi-field]]: a Jacobi field satisfies $D_t^2J+R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0$.

[F9] [[def-conjugate-points-along-a-geodesic-and-their-multiplicity]]: endpoints are conjugate when a nonzero Jacobi field vanishes at both ends.

[F10] [[ex-great-circles-as-round-sphere-geodesics]]: constant-speed great-circle parametrizations are geodesics of the round sphere.

[F11] [[thm-variation-field-of-a-geodesic-variation-is-a-jacobi-field]]: the variation field of a smooth geodesic variation is Jacobi; this implication uses no choice axiom.

## Proof

**Given:** The incidence $Z$ and its round geometry.

1.1 Put $F(n,x)=n\cdot x$ on $S^2\times S^2$. At a point with $F=0$, the vector $x$ lies in $T_nS^2$ by [F5], and the tangent variation $(\dot n,\dot x)=(x,0)$ satisfies $dF(\dot n,\dot x)=x\cdot x=1$. Thus $dF\ne0$; [F6] makes $Z=F^{-1}(0)$ an analytic hypersurface of dimension $3$ in the $4$-dimensional product. [F5, F6, given, algebra]

1.2 A tangent vector to $Z$ satisfies $x\cdot\dot n+n\cdot\dot x=0$. Given any $\dot n\in T_nS^2$, set $\dot x=-(x\cdot\dot n)n$; then $\dot x\in T_xS^2$ by [F5], and the tangent equation holds. Hence $\pi_G$ is a submersion. Given any $\dot x\in T_xS^2$, set $\dot n=-(n\cdot\dot x)x$; the same calculation proves $\pi_X$ is a submersion. Both maps are analytic restrictions of product projections. Since $Z$ is compact, $\pi_G$ is proper on $\pi_X^{-1}(K)$ for every compact $K\subset X$. The dimensions satisfy $4>3>2\ge2$, so [F1] applies. [F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra, choose]

2.1 The defining differential on tangent vectors to the product is $dF=(x^\flat,n^\flat)$ by the metric identification [F4]. Therefore by [F3] the relation at $(n,x)$ consists of
$$
(n,\eta;x,\xi)=(n,\tau x^\flat;x,-\tau n^\flat),\qquad \tau\ne0.
$$
At the antipodal point $-x$, choose multiplier $-\tau$. Its data covector is $(-\tau)(-x)^\flat=\tau x^\flat$, the same as at $x$, while the object point changes from $x$ to $-x$. Both are nonzero relation points. Hence the left projection is not injective, and [F2] implies failure of global Bolker. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $v=n\times x$, a unit tangent vector at $x$. For $s$ near zero put $n_s=\cos(s)n+\sin(s)v$, $v_s=n_s\times x$, and
$$
\gamma_s(t)=\cos(t)x+\sin(t)v_s,\qquad 0\le t\le\pi.
$$
Each $n_s$ is a unit vector orthogonal to $x$, and $v_s$ is a unit vector orthogonal to $x$; hence $\gamma_s$ parametrizes a great circle at unit speed and is a geodesic by [F10]. Thus [F7] makes this a geodesic variation. Its endpoints are fixed: $\gamma_s(0)=x$ and $\gamma_s(\pi)=-x$. Differentiating in $s$ at zero gives
$$
J(t)=\left.\partial_s\gamma_s(t)\right|_{s=0}
=\sin(t)(v\times x)=-\sin(t)n,
$$
because $(n\times x)\times x=-n$ when $n\cdot x=0$ and $|x|=1$. By [F11] this field is Jacobi and hence satisfies the Jacobi equation in [F8]. It is nonzero for $0<t<\pi$ and vanishes at both endpoints, so [F9] makes the endpoints conjugate. The proof uses no choice axiom; the sphere has no boundary, so this example lies outside the simple-surface hypotheses. [F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, given, algebra] ∎

## Geometric diagram

The great circle is drawn as a circle in its plane, with $n$ normal to the plane and $x,-x$ antipodal. At $n$, the data covector is represented by the same tangent vector $x$ for both relation points; the object covectors at the antipodes are opposite. The calculation in step 2.1, rather than the schematic, establishes noninjectivity.
