Read the actual published statements and complete local proofs for `thm-hessian-characterises-convexity`, `thm-least-squares-solutions-and-normal-equation`, and `thm-singular-value-decomposition`. Their scopes match the intended uses: the Hessian criterion requires a C2 function on an open convex Euclidean domain; least-squares minimizers exist in finite-dimensional inner-product spaces and differ by the kernel; SVD applies to finite-dimensional real or complex inner-product spaces, including rank zero. None declares AC. The indicator-response lemma uses the real finite-dimensional case and handles rank zero explicitly.

Read the actual Gaussian density theorem and its proof. Both it and the published multivariate-normal definition assume AC; LDA/QDA statements and consumers will state AC and carry `def-axiom-of-choice`. The theorem's covariance premise is positive definite, so its Lebesgue density formula applies; singular covariance is excluded from these discriminants.

Read the published Bernoulli GLM definitions and complete score/information and IRLS proofs. The specialization used here has response support `{0,1}`, canonical logit, common scale `a(phi)=1`, `b(eta)=log(1+exp(eta))`, mean `p=sigmoid(eta)`, and variance/derivative `p(1-p)>0` at finite logits. Finite support supplies the required fixed support and moments; continuity of logistic derivatives gives the local derivative envelopes. Full column rank makes `X^T W X` positive definite. The local proof will also derive the Bernoulli log-likelihood gradient and observed Hessian, so Newton equals Fisher scoring for this model. The generic source theorem is used only with its stated admissibility and invertibility hypotheses.

Read `thm-cauchy-schwarz-for-real-and-complex-inner-product-spaces` and its exact norm convention for the perceptron margin argument. It is a choice-free finite-dimensional application.
