## Finding

The factorization assertion is not refuted. Its finite-quotient, local-DVR, and local-to-global parts have a plausible ZF proof. The current published proof, however, does **not** establish its no-Choice claim: it uses a general finite-free PID-submodule supplier whose published proof reaches an explicitly AC-qualified PID-to-UFD theorem. The gap is in the supplier route used to establish the integral lattice is free, not in the finite prime enumeration or the finite-quotient membership test.

Thus the current proof route imports AC at the precise step that supplies freeness. The fact that the corollary's *statement* is unqualified does not remove the cost of its proof. This is a genuine used dependency, not an unrelated transitive branch.

The repo's published `lem-subgroups-of-free-abelian-groups-are-free` does not currently repair this route: its statement assumes AC (lines 27–30), and its proof well-orders the basis and makes a set-indexed family of lift choices (lines 48–50). The already-published `lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic` is choice-free: its least-positive-element proof uses the well-ordering of the natural numbers, with no AC premise (lines 42–47, 79–84). It is a suitable base case for the missing finite-rank argument.

## Choice-free finite-rank lattice route

The following supplies the needed `O_K` lattice facts using only finite choices and one-witness existential choices. It specializes to the number-field setting and does not call the generic PID-submodule corollary.

1. **Put an integral lattice around `O_K`.** Let `K/Q` have finite degree `n`. Choose one finite `Q`-basis and, for each of its finitely many elements, one integer clearing the coefficients of a monic polynomial over `Q`; the resulting scaled basis elements `u_1,...,u_n` are integral. Put `M=Σ Z u_i`, so `M⊂O_K`. The trace pairing is nondegenerate. Choose its finite trace-dual basis `u_i^*`, and one nonzero integer `c` clearing the finitely many rational coordinates of every `u_i^*` relative to the `u_j`. Then `c u_i^*∈M⊂O_K`. For `x=Σ a_i u_i∈O_K`,
   `c a_j = Tr_{K/Q}(x c u_j^*) ∈ Z`,
   because the product is integral and its rational trace is an integer. Hence `M⊂O_K⊂c^{-1}M`. These are finite selections, not a choice from an infinite family.

2. **Use an explicit subgroup-of-`Z^n` induction.** Every subgroup `H≤Z^n` is finite free by induction on `n`. Project to the first coordinate. Its image is `0` or `dZ`, where in the nonzero case `d>0` is the least positive member, by `lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic`. If the image is zero, apply induction to `H≤Z^{n-1}`. Otherwise choose one `h∈H` with first coordinate `d`. The kernel of the projection on `H` is a subgroup of `Z^{n-1}`, so by induction it has a finite basis. For any `g∈H`, its first coordinate is a unique `q d`; then `g-qh` is in the kernel. The chosen `h` together with the kernel basis spans `H` and is independent, since the first coordinate of `h` is nonzero. This uses one lift at each finite induction stage, not simultaneous or countably many choices. Applying this to the additive subgroup `O_K≤c^{-1}M≅Z^n` makes `O_K` finite free. Since it contains the full lattice `M`, its rank is `n=[K:Q]`.

3. **Obtain Noetherianity and finite quotients.** A finite additive basis of `O_K` makes each ideal an additive subgroup of a finite-rank free abelian group, so the same induction makes it finitely generated over `Z`; those generators also generate it as an `O_K`-ideal. Thus `O_K` is Noetherian without the generic PID-freeness route. For a nonzero ideal `a`, take one `0≠a∈a`. Multiplication by `a` on the free rank-`n` lattice is injective with nonzero integral determinant `d`. The adjugate identity gives `d O_K⊂a O_K`; the quotient `O_K/a O_K` is a quotient of `(Z/dZ)^n`, hence finite, and `O_K/a` is a quotient of it. This is the argument of the published `lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient` (steps 1.1–2.1), after supplying the specialized finite-free lattice proof above. The determinant/adjugate argument is finite integer linear algebra.

4. **Continue with the target's finite local-global route.** In `O_K/a`, the finite ideal lattice gives finitely many maximal ideals over `a`; finite sets admit finite enumerations in ZF. For a nonzero prime `p` of `O_K`, the finite-quotient result makes `O_K/p` a finite domain, hence a field, so `p` is maximal and has height one. Integral closure gives normality (`thm-integral-closure-is-integrally-closed`). The target can then use `thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr`. At each prime above `a`, the localized nonzero proper ideal has a unique positive exponent by the DVR ideal theorem. The product is finite. If membership fails, the nonzero class of `x` in the finite quotient has a proper annihilator; a maximal ideal containing it exists by finite cardinality. Its inverse image contradicts the asserted equality after localization. This proves ideal equality without a general maximal-ideal theorem. Localizing a second finite factorization gives the same primes and exponents by DVR uniqueness. The only selections here are from finite sets or a single explicitly nonempty set; the exponents are unique.

This route also repairs the dependency of `lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient`, whose current dependency is `thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree` (frontmatter line 7) and whose proof assumes an integral basis (line 30). The finite-free theorem need not be used through the current generic corollary.

## Audit of the height-one/DVR route actually used

The target uses `thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr` (target line 36). That theorem has an unqualified statement (supplier lines 32–36), and its proof at lines 58–64 establishes one-dimensionality, Noetherianity of the localization, preservation of integral closure, and nonfield status, then uses condition 3 of `thm-equivalent-characterisations-of-a-dvr`.

The DVR equivalence has a large dependency closure, including `thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations`, whose broad statement mentions DC (lines 30–48). The condition actually used is choice-free. The Noetherian-to-finitely-generated direction is identified as choice-free in that theorem (lines 45–49, 73–81); the quotients/localizations supplier uses only finite generators (steps 1.2–3.1, especially its remark at line 76); the nilradical-nilpotence proof chooses finitely many generators and one natural nilpotence exponent per generator (steps 1.1–3.1); and the adjugate identity is a finite matrix calculation. The Noetherian-to-ACC direction used to stop repeated division is the choice-free direction identified in `thm-equivalent-characterizations-of-noetherian-modules` and restated at `thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations` lines 45–49, 59, and 81. There is no need to invoke the DC-qualified ACC-to-maximal-condition implication.

In DVR-equivalence step 2.1, if division by a generator `π` continued forever, it would give a strict ascending chain of principal ideals. At each stage the quotient in `x_i=π x_{i+1}` is unique because the ring is a domain and `π≠0`, so this recursion does not select from a family of possible quotients. ACC rules it out. The minimal exponent in step 3.1 is a least natural number; steps 1.3 and 4.1–6.1 make only one-at-a-time or finite-list choices.

There is a small proof-formulation point separate from Choice: steps 2.1 and 3.1 initially say they apply in the situations of conditions 2 and 4; the condition-3 route (steps 4.1–6.1) establishes a principal maximal ideal at its end and then cites step 3.1. The same deterministic division and least-exponent argument applies once that principal maximal ideal is obtained, but a local sentence extending steps 2.1/3.1 to this point would make the condition-3-to-4 derivation explicit. This is not the AC defect in the target's finite-free supplier.

## Authoritative full-text source review

Full relevant text was read, not just search snippets:

- J. S. Milne, [Algebraic Number Theory](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANT.pdf), Proposition 2.29 (PDF p. 37; book p. 35) gives the trace-dual sandwich `M⊂O_K⊂M'` and concludes finiteness. Its freeness step refers generically to the theorem that submodules of free modules over a PID are free; it does not spell out the finite-rank `Z^n` induction needed for this ZF audit. Theorem 3.7/Lemma 3.8 (PDF pp. 51–52; book pp. 49–50) use a maximal bad ideal for general Dedekind factorization, so that generic argument is not the finite-quotient route used here.
- J. S. Milne, [A Primer of Commutative Algebra](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/CA.pdf), Proposition 20.2 (pp. 94–95) chooses a maximal annihilator in a quotient using Noetherianity. That general maximal-element route is unnecessary for the target's finite quotient, where a maximal ideal can be found by finite cardinality. Proposition 20.5 and Corollary 20.6 / Theorem 20.7 (pp. 95–96) provide standard localization and Dedekind context; the factorization discussion refers back to the ANT proof.
- A. B. Altman and S. L. Kleiman, [A Term of Commutative Algebra, 13th ed.](https://web.mit.edu/18.705/www/13Ed.pdf), Theorem (23.10) (PDF pp. 148–149; book pp. 140–141) gives a generic one-dimensional normal local DVR criterion. It does not audit choice costs for the specialized finite quotient.
- The [Stacks Project, Lemma 10.120.17](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/034X) and Section 10.120 ([tag 034O](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/034O)) give standard Dedekind-domain equivalences/factorization using Noetherian induction and maximal-prime steps; those generic proofs do not furnish the choice audit needed here.
- The [Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.7](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AUU) and [Lemma 15.22.11](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0AUW) give full proofs for finite torsion-free modules over a PID, including an embedding into a finite free module and a rank-induction freeness proof. Reading the proof steps shows only finite basis/generator lists, finite denominator clearing, and finitely many lifts in the finite-rank route; the Stacks text itself does not label the result as a ZF theorem.

None of these external texts explicitly presents the complete specialized number-field factorization proof as a ZF result. The trace-dual lattice plus finite-rank subgroup induction above is the route identified by this audit; external sources corroborate its algebraic ingredients, not a claim that one source already proves the foundation-specific conclusion.

## Local helper needed for a clean published route

The missing route ingredient is a choice-free, finite-rank formulation, for example:

> **Subgroups of finite-rank free abelian groups are finite free.** For every `n∈N` and subgroup `H≤Z^n`, there is a finite basis of `H`; the proof is induction on `n` using `lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic`, one lift of the least positive first coordinate, and induction on the kernel.

This helper (or an equivalent specialized theorem that combines the trace-dual sandwich with the finite-free conclusion for `O_K`) would let the target avoid the AC-qualified generic PID route while preserving its statement and its finite quotient/DVR factorization proof. Whether and how to amend the published dependency chain is outside this read-only audit.
