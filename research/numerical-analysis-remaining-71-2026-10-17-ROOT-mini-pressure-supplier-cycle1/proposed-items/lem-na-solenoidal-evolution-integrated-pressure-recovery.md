## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be nonempty, bounded, connected and Lipschitz, $d\in\{2,3\}$, $0<T<\infty$ and $\nu>0$. Write $V=H^1_0(D)^d$, $\|v\|_V=\|\nabla v\|_2$, $Q=L^2_0(D)$ and use $a_\nu$, $b$, $V_\sigma$ and $H_\sigma$ from [[def-na-stokes-zero-mean-pressure-and-velocity-spaces]] and [[def-na-solenoidal-energy-weak-ns-velocity]]. Let $C_P$ be the Poincare constant, $C_{\rm div}$ the divergence-right-inverse constant, and choose $C_c$ so that
$$|c(a;b,v)|\le C_c\|a\|_V\|b\|_V\|v\|_V,\qquad \|c(a;a,\cdot)\|_{V^*}\le C_c\|a\|_2^{1/2}\|a\|_V^{3/2}.$$
For $f\in L^2(0,T;L^2(D)^d)$ and $u_0\in H_\sigma$, let $u$ be a solenoidal energy weak velocity in [[def-na-solenoidal-energy-weak-ns-velocity]]. Put $U=\|u\|_{L^\infty L^2}$, $E=\|u\|_{L^2V}$ and $F=\|f\|_{L^2L^2}$. There is a unique $P\in L^\infty(0,T;Q)\cap C_w([0,T];Q)$, $P(0)=0$, satisfying, for every $t\in[0,T]$ and $v\in V$,
$$(u(t)-u_0,v)_2+\int_0^t\bigl(a_\nu(u(s),v)+c(u(s);u(s),v)\bigr)\,ds+b(v,P(t))=\int_0^t(f(s),v)_2\,ds.$$
Moreover,
$$\|P\|_{L^\infty Q}\le C_{\rm div}\bigl(C_P(U+\|u_0\|_2)+C_P\sqrt T F+2\nu\sqrt T E+C_cT^{1/4}U^{1/2}E^{3/2}\bigr).$$
Here $C_w$ means continuity for the weak topology of $Q$. Define the $Q$-valued time distribution $p=\partial_tP$ by
$$\langle p,\eta\rangle=-\int_0^TP(t)\eta'(t)\,dt\quad\bigl(\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)\bigr).$$
Thus a $Q$-valued distribution here is a continuous real-linear map from scalar test functions to $Q$; continuity is in the usual test-function topology and the norm topology of $Q$. This $p$ is the unique mean-zero distributional pressure for $u$. In particular, for every $\varphi\in C^1([0,T];V)$ vanishing near $T$,
$$-\int_0^T(u,\varphi')_2+\int_0^T\bigl(a_\nu(u,\varphi)+c(u;u,\varphi)\bigr)-\int_0^Tb(\varphi',P)=\int_0^T(f,\varphi)_2+(u_0,\varphi(0))_2.$$

For a mixed MINI solution on a mesh and time partition as in [[def-na-mini-navier-stokes-backward-euler-scheme]], define $P_h^0=0$, $P_h^m=\sum_{n=1}^m\tau_np_h^n$, and let $P_h$ be its continuous piecewise-affine interpolant. If $\max_{0\le n\le N}\|u_h^n\|_2\le U_h$ and $\|\overline u_h\|_{L^2V}\le E_h$, then, with the uniform MINI inf-sup lower bound $\beta>0$,
$$\sup_t\|P_h(t)\|_2\le\beta^{-1}\bigl(2C_PU_h+C_P\sqrt T F+2\nu\sqrt T E_h+C_cT^{1/4}U_h^{1/2}E_h^{3/2}\bigr).$$
For the sequence and subsequential velocity limit of [[thm-na-mini-navier-stokes-be-subsequential-limit]], a further subsequence satisfies $P_j\rightharpoonup P$ in $L^2(0,T;Q)$. Its piecewise-constant pressures converge to $p$ weakly as $Q$-valued time distributions: for every $q\in Q$ and $\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)$,
$$\int_0^T(\overline p_j(t),q)_2\eta(t)\,dt\longrightarrow(\langle p,\eta\rangle,q)_2.$$
No relation between mesh size and maximum time step is required. These conclusions do not assert $p\in L^{4/3}(0,T;Q)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The domain, viscosity, data, solenoidal energy velocity, and, for the discrete clauses, actual mixed MINI solutions and refining sequence in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice implies the Countable Choice used by divergence lifting, Riesz representation and Bochner-space compactness, and permits the subsequence extractions below. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[lem-ac-supplies-countable-and-dependent-choice-for-banach-integration]])

[F1] The solenoidal energy definition gives weak continuity in $H_\sigma$, $u(0)=u_0$, the scalar time-tested equation on $V_\sigma$, and the indicated energy norms. The skew form is the trilinear integral difference in its definition and obeys the energy-class estimate in the Statement; Poincare also gives its $V^3$ bound. ([[def-na-solenoidal-energy-weak-ns-velocity]], [[def-na-evolutionary-skew-convective-form]], [[lem-na-evolutionary-skew-convective-form-bound]], [[thm-poincare-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]])

[F2] There is a fixed bounded linear $B:Q\to V$ with $\operatorname{div}Bq=q$ and $\|Bq\|_V\le C_{\rm div}\|q\|_2$. Every $v\in V$ has $\operatorname{div}v\in Q$, and $b(v,q)=-(q,\operatorname{div}v)_2$. Riesz represents bounded functionals on $Q$; equivalently an $L^sV^*$ residual annihilating $V_\sigma$ has a unique $L^sQ$ pressure. ([[thm-na-bounded-lipschitz-domain-divergence-right-inverse]], [[lem-na-zero-mean-of-zero-boundary-divergence]], [[def-na-stokes-zero-mean-pressure-and-velocity-spaces]], [[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]], [[lem-na-continuous-pressure-recovery-from-solenoidal-residual]])

[F3] Strong measurability and integrability of the norm give Bochner integrability; its norm obeys the integral inequality and bounded linear maps commute with integration. Scalar $W^{1,1}$ functions have absolutely continuous representatives and satisfy the fundamental theorem; an $L^1$ function inducing zero distribution vanishes almost everywhere. Distributional differentiation uses the negative test-derivative convention. ([[thm-bochner-integrability-criterion]], [[lem-bochner-integral-norm-inequality]], [[thm-bounded-linear-maps-commute-with-bochner-integration]], [[cor-one-dimensional-w-one-p-functions-have-absolutely-continuous-representatives]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]], [[thm-locally-integrable-functions-embed-in-distributions]], [[def-distributional-derivative]])

[F4] The mixed scheme holds on all $V_h$ tests with interval-average forcing. Its energy theorem gives uniform $U_h,E_h$, the jump-square sum bound, and $\|f_h\|_{L^2L^2}\le F$. MINI has a uniform inf-sup lower bound $\beta$ and its Fortin map satisfies $\Pi_hv\to v$ in $V$ for each fixed $v\in V$. ([[def-na-mini-navier-stokes-backward-euler-scheme]], [[thm-na-mini-be-step-existence-energy]], [[thm-na-mini-element-fortin-stability]], [[lem-na-mini-fortin-l2-approximation]])

[F5] The existing limit theorem provides strong $L^2L^2$ convergence, weak $L^2V$ convergence, a uniform $L^\infty L^2$ bound, and strong convergence of initial projections. Force averages converge in $L^2L^2$. The zero-boundary Sobolev inequality gives the $L^6$ estimate in dimensions two and three, Holder gives interpolation and the time estimates used here, and scalar Fubini applies to integrable functions on the finite time square. ([[thm-na-mini-navier-stokes-be-subsequential-limit]], [[lem-pe12-bochner-lp-mollification]], [[cor-sobolev-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]], [[thm-holder-inequality-for-integrals]], [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]])

[F6] Scalar $L^2(D)$ is Hilbert and separable, by restriction of the rational-box dense family. Its mean-zero subspace $Q$ is closed; subtracting means from a scalar dense family gives a dense family in $Q$. Therefore $Q$ and $L^2(0,T;Q)$ are separable Hilbert spaces, and bounded sequences in the latter have weakly convergent subsequences. Hilbert orthogonal decomposition also projects $V$ tests onto the closed subspace $H_\sigma$ for purposes of pairing with $u$. ([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]], [[thm-rational-box-step-functions-form-a-countable-dense-subset-of-l-p-of-rn]], [[thm-orthogonal-decomposition-by-a-closed-subspace]], [[lem-parabolic-weak-compactness-separable-bochner-hilbert]])

## Proof

1.1 Set $g(t)(v)=(f(t),v)_2-a_\nu(u(t),v)-c(u(t);u(t),v)$. The continuous trilinear map and strongly measurable velocity give a strongly measurable $V^*$-valued convection term, by approximation with simple $V$-valued functions. The other terms are bounded linear images of strongly measurable functions. By Poincare and [F1], $\|g(t)\|_{V^*}\le C_P\|f(t)\|_2+2\nu\|u(t)\|_V+C_c\|u(t)\|_2^{1/2}\|u(t)\|_V^{3/2}$. Holder puts this norm in $L^{4/3}(0,T)$ and gives $\|g\|_{L^1V^*}\le C_P\sqrt T F+2\nu\sqrt T E+C_cT^{1/4}U^{1/2}E^{3/2}$. Define $G(t)=\int_0^tg(s)\,ds$; [F3] makes $G$ norm-continuous in $V^*$. [A1, F1, F3, F5, given, construct]

1.2 Define $R(t)(v)=G(t)(v)-(u(t)-u_0,v)_2$. This is strongly measurable and essentially bounded in $V^*$, with norm at most $C_P(U+\|u_0\|_2)+\|g\|_{L^1V^*}$. For every fixed $v\in V$, $R(t)(v)$ is continuous in $t$: the $u$ pairing equals its pairing with the Hilbert projection of $v$ onto $H_\sigma$, to which weak continuity applies. For each $z\in V_\sigma$, the scalar weak equation and [F3] identify $(u(t),z)_2$ with the absolutely continuous function $(u_0,z)_2+G(t)(z)$; weak continuity makes this identity hold at every time. Thus $R(t)(z)=0$ for every $z\in V_\sigma$ and every $t$, and $R(0)=0$. [F1, F3, F6, step 1.1, construct]

2.1 For each $t$ use Riesz to define $P(t)\in Q$ by $(P(t),q)_2=-R(t)(Bq)$ for all $q\in Q$. The map from $R$ to $P$ is bounded linear of norm at most $C_{\rm div}$; hence $P$ is strongly measurable and the claimed $L^\infty Q$ bound follows from steps 1.1–1.2. For each $q$, the right side is continuous in $t$, so $P\in C_w([0,T];Q)$ and $P(0)=0$. For $v\in V$ put $q=\operatorname{div}v$. Since $v-Bq\in V_\sigma$, $R(t)(v)=R(t)(Bq)=-(P(t),q)_2=b(v,P(t))$, proving the integrated identity. If two primitives satisfy it, testing their difference with $Bq$ for all $q$ proves equality at every time. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

2.2 The formula $\langle p,\eta\rangle=-\int P\eta'$ defines a continuous $Q$-valued distribution since its norm is at most $\|P\|_{L^\infty Q}\|\eta'\|_{L^1}$. Differentiate the integrated identity on scalar time tests to obtain $u_t+a_\nu(u,\cdot)+c(u;u,\cdot)+b(\cdot,p)=f$ in $V^*$-valued distributions. Any other $Q$-valued pressure distribution for this equation differs by a distribution $r$ satisfying $b(Bq,\langle r,\eta\rangle)=-(q,\langle r,\eta\rangle)_2=0$ for every $q,\eta$, hence equals $p$. For finite-rank time-dependent $V$ tests, scalar integration by parts in the integrated identity gives the stated initial-trace formula. Such tests are dense in $C^1([0,T];V)$: approximate the continuous derivative uniformly by continuous piecewise-linear maps with values in a finite-dimensional span, integrate those approximants and retain the initial value; multiply by a fixed smooth cutoff supported before $T$ and equal to one on the support of the original test. This preserves convergence in $C^1$ and vanishing near $T$. All terms are continuous for this norm by the bounds above, so the identity passes to every stated test. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $\widehat u_h$ be the affine velocity interpolant and let bars denote right-constant reconstructions. On each cell $\partial_tP_h=\overline p_h$ and $\partial_t\widehat u_h=D_\tau u_h^n$. Integrating the mixed equation, including a possible partial terminal cell, gives for all $t$ and $v_h\in V_h$ the identity $(\widehat u_h(t)-u_h^0,v_h)_2+\int_0^t[a_\nu(\overline u_h,v_h)+c(\overline u_h;\overline u_h,v_h)]+b(v_h,P_h(t))=\int_0^t(f_h,v_h)_2$. Since $P_h(t)\in Q_h$, apply the uniform inf-sup bound. Convexity bounds $\|\widehat u_h(t)\|_2$ by $U_h$; Holder and [F1] bound the three integrals by $C_P\sqrt T F$, $2\nu\sqrt T E_h$ and $C_cT^{1/4}U_h^{1/2}E_h^{3/2}$ times $\|v_h\|_V$. This proves the discrete primitive estimate. [F1, F4, F5, given, algebra]

4.1 Now use the sequence of [F5]. Its energy bound implies $\sum_n\|u_j^n-u_j^{n-1}\|_2^2\le C$ and therefore $\|\widehat u_j-\overline u_j\|_{L^2L^2}^2=\sum_n(\tau_n/3)\|u_j^n-u_j^{n-1}\|_2^2\le C\tau_{\max,j}/3\to0$. Thus $\widehat u_j\to u$ strongly in $L^2L^2$. Step 3.1 uniformly bounds $P_j$ in $L^\infty Q$, hence in $L^2Q$. By [F6] and [A1], extract a further subsequence $P_j\rightharpoonup P_*\in L^2Q$. If the uniform bound is $K$, weak convergence gives $|\int(P_*,w)_2|\le K\int\|w\|_2$ for every $w\in L^2Q$. Taking $w=\mathbf1_A P_*$ on measurable subsets of $\{\|P_*\|_2>K\}$ proves $\|P_*\|_2\le K$ almost everywhere, so $P_*\in L^\infty Q$. [A1, F4, F5, F6, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

4.2 The uniform $L^2L^6$ bound and strong $L^2L^2$ convergence give strong $L^2L^3$ and $L^2L^4$ convergence of $\overline u_j$ by Holder interpolation. For any fixed $v\in V$, the first integral defining the skew form is a weak $L^2L^2$ gradient paired with $\overline u_j\otimes v\to u\otimes v$ strongly in $L^2L^2$; the other integral pairs $\nabla v\in L^2$ with $\overline u_j\otimes\overline u_j\to u\otimes u$ strongly in $L^1L^2$. Hence the convection terms converge when multiplied by any fixed bounded scalar time weight. Replacing $v$ by $\Pi_jv$ introduces a term bounded by $C\|\Pi_jv-v\|_V\|\overline u_j\|_{L^2V}^2$, which tends to zero. This reasoning applies to arbitrary $v\in V$; it never assumes $\Pi_jv\in Z_j$. [F1, F4, F5, step 4.1, algebra]

5.1 Test the integrated identity of step 3.1 with $\Pi_jv$ and integrate in time against $\eta\in C_c^\infty(0,T)$. Each primitive integral becomes its original integrand multiplied by the bounded continuous weight $s\mapsto\int_s^T\eta(t)\,dt$; this scalar interchange follows by absolute integrability and can also be obtained first for time-step simple functions and then by $L^1$ approximation. Strong convergence of $\widehat u_j$, the initial values, force averages and Fortin tests, weak $L^2V$ convergence of velocities, and step 4.2 pass every term to the limit. The pressure term converges because $P_j\rightharpoonup P_*$ in $L^2Q$ and $\operatorname{div}\Pi_jv\to\operatorname{div}v$ in $L^2$. Thus $b(v,P_*)=G(t)(v)-(u(t)-u_0,v)_2$ as scalar time distributions for every $v\in V$. Subtract step 2.1 and use $v=Bq$ to obtain $(P_*-P,q)_2=0$ as a scalar distribution for every $q\in Q$. A countable dense subset of $Q$ and [F3] give $P_*=P$ almost everywhere, proving the claimed weak $L^2Q$ convergence. Finally $\int(\overline p_j,q)_2\eta=-\int(P_j,q)_2\eta'$ converges to $-\int(P,q)_2\eta'=(\langle p,\eta\rangle,q)_2$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 Steps 2.1–2.2 prove continuous reconstruction, uniqueness, the bound and the initial-trace identity, while steps 3.1–5.1 prove the uniform discrete estimate and subsequential distributional pressure convergence under exactly the stated vanishing mesh/time assumptions. These give every asserted conclusion. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎

## Remarks

For the same velocity define $g$ as in step 1.1. If $1<s<\infty$ and additionally $g\in L^s(0,T;V^*)$, the pressure distribution belongs to $L^s(0,T;Q)$ if and only if the **actual full distributional derivative** $u_t$ belongs to $L^s(0,T;V^*)$. Indeed the full equation gives $u_t=g-b(\cdot,p)$ in one direction. In the other, $g-u_t$ annihilates $V_\sigma$: its evaluation on each fixed solenoidal test is zero as a distribution, hence almost everywhere. To get a common null set, embed $V$ isometrically with its full Sobolev norm into a finite product of separable scalar $L^2(D)$ spaces by $v\mapsto(v,\nabla v)$. A separable metric space has a countable base; intersect that base with any subspace and use [A1] to choose one point in each nonempty intersection. Thus $V_\sigma$ is separable in its inherited norm, and a countable dense family gives the common null set by continuity of the functional. The existing [[lem-na-continuous-pressure-recovery-from-solenoidal-residual]] gives $p_s\in L^sQ$, and distributional uniqueness above identifies it with $p$. Consequently this criterion is also equivalent to the primitive having an $L^sQ$ weak derivative. At $s=4/3$ the hypothesis on $g$ already holds. The explicit additional regularity $u\in W^{1,4/3}(0,T;L^2(D)^d)$ suffices, since $L^2(D)^d\hookrightarrow V^*$; it is not supplied by the energy argument. Extending a derivative known only on $V_\sigma$ does not establish this full-derivative condition.

The source's pressure-free weak solution and the primitive conclusions here do not discharge the stronger instantaneous pressure regularity or instantaneous smooth pressure-rate obligations in the PE06 plan.
