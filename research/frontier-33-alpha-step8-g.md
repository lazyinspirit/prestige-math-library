## Sources consulted

- Huang–Ju–Zhou, [Erdős–Hajnal beyond the five-vertex
  path](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06258v2), §6.2, Claim 6.5.2 and its proof:
  the initial blockade consists of the equivalence classes generated by
  overlapping induced copies of the $E$-graph, later blockades coarsen it, and
  an induced $E$ in the terminal pattern would put all six representatives in
  one initial class.
- Hoory–Linial–Wigderson, [Expander Graphs and Their
  Applications](https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/BOOKS/expanderbookr1.pdf),
  Construction 8.1 (printed p. 68): the four displayed affine maps are exactly
  the forward Margulis neighbours and the other four are their inverses.
  Section 3.1 (printed pp. 24–26) defines an $(n,d,\alpha)$-graph, identifies
  normalized adjacency as the walk transition matrix, uses symmetry for the
  uniform stationary law, and proves mean-zero contraction by $\alpha$.
- Arora–Barak, [Computational Complexity: A Modern
  Approach](https://theory.cs.princeton.edu/complexity/book.pdf), §5.2.2,
  Example 5.9 (printed pp. 93–94): bounded-alternation completeness uses fixed
  polynomial-length quantified blocks. This supports compiling a single fixed
  input-length circuit after choosing an explicit total fixed-width encoding.
- Thaler, [Proofs, Arguments, and
  Zero-Knowledge](https://people.cs.georgetown.edu/jthaler/ProofsArgsAndZK.pdf),
  Lemmas 3.7–3.8 (printed pp. 30–32): both multilinear-evaluation algorithms
  take a table with values in a field and a point in a power of that field.
  Section 4.1, Discussion of costs (printed pp. 37–38), counts
  $\sum_i(d_i+1)$ prover field elements for a full well-formed sum-check
  transcript and one terminal polynomial evaluation.
