## Statement

Let $Y_1,\ldots,Y_m$ be centered sub-Gaussian real random variables with a common proxy $\sigma^2\ge0$, where $m\ge1$. No independence is assumed. For $t>0$, if $\sigma^2>0$ then
$$\mathbb P\left(\max_{1\le j\le m}|Y_j|\ge t\right)\le2m\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),$$
and if $\sigma^2=0$ the probability is $0$.

Let $Z$ be a centered sub-Gaussian vector in $\mathbb R^d$ with proxy $\sigma^2$, where $d\ge1$. For $0<\epsilon<1$, let $\mathcal N$ be the sphere net supplied by [[lem-epsilon-net-of-unit-sphere-has-cardinality-bound]]. Then for $t>0$, if $\sigma^2>0$,
$$\mathbb P(\|Z\|_2\ge t)\le2(1+2/\epsilon)^d\exp\!\left(-\frac{(1-\epsilon)^2t^2}{2\sigma^2}\right),$$
and if $\sigma^2=0$ this probability is $0$. The deterministic reduction is $\|z\|_2\le(1-\epsilon)^{-1}\max_{u\in\mathcal N}|\langle u,z\rangle|$ for every $z\in\mathbb R^d$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Centered scalar or vector random elements satisfying the sub-Gaussian proxy bounds; a finite integer $m\ge1$; and, in the vector clause, $d\ge1$ and $0<\epsilon<1$.

[F1] A centered scalar $X$ is sub-Gaussian with proxy $\sigma^2$ when $\mathbb E e^{\lambda X}\le e^{\lambda^2\sigma^2/2}$ for every real $\lambda$; the vector condition bounds each projection ([[def-subgaussian-variable-and-vector]]).

[F2] For $W\ge0$ and $r>0$, Markov gives $\mathbb P(W\ge r)\le\mathbb E[W]/r$ ([[cor-markov-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

[F3] Finite unions satisfy $\mathbb P(\bigcup_jA_j)\le\sum_j\mathbb P(A_j)$ ([[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]).

[F4] For $d\ge1$ and $0<\epsilon<1$, there is a finite $\epsilon$-net $\mathcal N\subseteq S^{d-1}$ with $|\mathcal N|\le(1+2/\epsilon)^d$ ([[lem-epsilon-net-of-unit-sphere-has-cardinality-bound]]).

[F5] $S^{d-1}=\{u\in\mathbb R^d:\|u\|_2=1\}$ and $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Euclidean norm ([[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]]).

[F6] The Euclidean inner product satisfies $|\langle u,z\rangle|\le\|u\|_2\|z\|_2$ ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-finite]]).

[F7] Every nonempty finite set of real numbers has a maximum ([[lem-finite-set-has-max]], [[def-max-min]]).

[F8] The exponential is continuous and strictly increasing, positive, and satisfies $e^{-x}=1/e^x$ and $e^{x+y}=e^xe^y$ ([[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]]).

[F9] For every $a>0$ and natural $m$, $x^m/e^{ax}\to0$ as $x\to+\infty$; use $m=0$ to get $1/e^{ax}\to0$ ([[thm-exponential-beats-every-polynomial]]).

## Proof

1.1 For each $\lambda>0$, $h_\lambda(x)=e^{\lambda x}$ is continuous by [F8] and continuity of scalar multiplication, hence Borel: the Borel sets with Borel preimage form a sigma-algebra containing all opens, so contain the sigma-algebra generated by opens ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]). For each Borel $B$, $\{h_\lambda(Y_j)\in B\}=Y_j^{-1}(h_\lambda^{-1}(B))$ is measurable, and $h_\lambda(Y_j)\ge0$ by [F8]. If $\sigma^2>0$, then $Y_j\ge t$ implies $e^{\lambda Y_j}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$ by [F8]; Markov [F2], the MGF bound [F1], and the reciprocal and addition formulas in [F8] give $\mathbb P(Y_j\ge t)\le\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda^2\sigma^2/2)$. Choosing $\lambda=t/\sigma^2$ gives $\mathbb P(Y_j\ge t)\le e^{-t^2/(2\sigma^2)}$; Using parameter $-\lambda$, $Y_j\le-t$ implies $e^{-\lambda Y_j}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$; the same Markov calculation gives $\mathbb P(Y_j\le-t)\le\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda^2\sigma^2/2)$, and $\lambda=t/\sigma^2$ gives the same lower-tail bound. If $\sigma^2=0$, Markov gives $\mathbb P(Y_j\ge t)\le1/e^{\lambda t}=e^{-\lambda t}$ for every $\lambda>0$. By [F9] with $m=0$ and $a=t$, the right side tends to $0$ as $\lambda\to\infty$, so the tail is zero, and at parameter $-\lambda$ the same calculation gives $\mathbb P(Y_j\le-t)\le e^{-\lambda t}\to0$. [F1, F2, F8, F9, def-borel-sigma-algebra, algebra, construct]

1.2 Fix $z\ne0$ and put $u=z/\|z\|_2\in S^{d-1}$ by [F5]. By [F4], some $v\in\mathcal N$ satisfies $\|u-v\|_2\le\epsilon$. Then [F6] gives $|\langle v,z\rangle|\ge\langle u,z\rangle-|\langle u-v,z\rangle|\ge(1-\epsilon)\|z\|_2$. For $z=0$ the claimed reduction is immediate. Thus the deterministic net inequality holds. [given, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 By the finite maximum convention in [F7], the event $\{\max_j|Y_j|\ge t\}$ is the union of the $2m$ upper- and lower-tail events from step 1.1. Applying [F3] gives the stated finite-maximum bounds, without any independence assumption. [F3, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Apply step 1.2 to $Z$. If $\|Z\|_2\ge t$, then at least one of the $|\mathcal N|$ projections has absolute value at least $(1-\epsilon)t$. Each projection is centered sub-Gaussian with proxy $\sigma^2$ by [F1] because its direction is a unit vector. Step 2.1 and [F4] therefore give the displayed vector tail bound; when $\sigma^2=0$, step 2.1 gives probability zero. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
