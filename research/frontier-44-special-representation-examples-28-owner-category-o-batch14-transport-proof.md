## Complete categorical proof

The actual general biadjunction Statement, not merely its wall-specialized consequence, makes both functors exact adjoints in both directions. Put a=lambda+rho and b=mu+rho, both strictly dominant. The tensor Verma flag of E Delta(y dot lambda) has factors Delta(y dot lambda+gamma). A retained factor in the mu block has y a+gamma=x b. The dominant norm-distance inequality gives |x b-y a|>=|b-a|, with equality only x=y because both a and b are regular. The finite-module weight bound gives |gamma|<=|nu|=|b-a|, with equality only on W(mu-lambda), whose weights have multiplicity one. Thus exactly gamma=y(mu-lambda) survives, once, and F Delta_y^lambda=Delta_y^mu. The dual argument gives G Delta_y^mu=Delta_y^lambda. For F left adjoint G, the unit on Delta_y^lambda corresponds to id of the nonzero F Delta_y^lambda, so is nonzero. Its target GF Delta_y^lambda is isomorphic to the same Verma; the Verma Hom dimension-one theorem makes it an isomorphism. Similarly the counit on Delta_y^mu corresponds to id of G Delta_y^mu and is an isomorphism. Exactness and induction through short exact flag sequences extend both natural isomorphisms to all Verma-filtered objects, in particular all projectives. Every object X admits a finite presentation P1->P0->X->0, since O is abelian and has enough projectives. The natural diagrams for the exact composites GF and FG show their unit/counit are isomorphisms on the cokernel X. Hence F and G are inverse equivalences on the entire blocks; no Morita presentation or numerical-matrix inference is used. ## Weighted inverse witness and consumers

The Grothendieck group of $\mathcal O_\lambda$ has the classes of the simples and of the standards as bases ([[prop-the-grothendieck-group-of-o-has-simple-and-standard-bases]], [[def-grothendieck-group-and-character-of-category-o]]), and the character map is additive, so it suffices to prove the matrix identity. By [[thm-simple-verma-composition-multiplicities-are-kazhdan-lusztig-values]], $\mathrm{ch}\,M(y\cdot\lambda)=\sum_w [M(y\cdot\lambda):L(w\cdot\lambda)]\,\mathrm{ch}\,L(w\cdot\lambda)=\sum_w P_{y,w}(1)\,\mathrm{ch}\,L(w\cdot\lambda)$; the transition matrix is $A:=(P_{y,w}(1))$, the specialisation at $v=1$ of the KL coefficient matrix $P=(p_{y,w})$, which is unitriangular for the finite Bruhat order with $p_{y,w}=0$ unless $y\le w$. Specialisation is a ring homomorphism, and [[def-inverse-kazhdan-lusztig-polynomials]] with [[thm-kazhdan-lusztig-inversion-formula]] give the two-sided inverse $Q'=P^{-1}$ defined by the finite chain sum, so $A^{-1}=(q'_{x,w}(1))$ and inverting the triangular system gives $\mathrm{ch}\,L(w\cdot\lambda)=\sum_x q'_{w,x}(1)\mathrm{ch}\,M(x\cdot\lambda)$ (row $w$ of $Q'(1)$ applied to the column of Verma characters). Supports: $q'_{x,w}\ne0$ only for $x\le w$, so row $w$ is supported in columns $x\ge w$, and the inversion formula is proved from bar-duality, not from finite examples. The signed form follows by substituting $q'_{x,w}=\mathrm{sgn}(x)\mathrm{sgn}(w)q_{x,w}$ and using $\mathrm{sgn}(w)\mathrm{sgn}(x)=(-1)^{\ell(w)-\ell(x)}$. All sums are finite. The chain formula is a weighted inverse, not ordinary Bruhat Mobius inversion unless every interval coefficient is1. In the ten-element interval [1324,3412], the only nonconstant KL polynomial is the endpoint 1+q. Thus the interval multiplicity matrix is Z+E, where Z is its zeta matrix and E has only the endpoint entry1. Since Z^-1 E Z^-1=E and E Z^-1 E=0, its inverse is Z^-1-E; the endpoint entry is -2 rather than the ordinary Mobius value -1. The S3 all-one interval example remains ordinary Mobius inversion.
