## Mathematical route and caveats

The A page defines finite parameterized state-step objectives and real elliptic/parabolic variational states before proving sensitivities. The ODE tangent lemma works on an explicit common compact tube, proves a uniform difference bound and first-order remainder, and uses Gronwall for the derivative and its continuity. The finite reverse recursion is proved from the finite C1 graph and a tangent/cotangent telescoping identity. The continuous ODE formula separately accounts for initial, terminal, running-cost, and vector-field parameter terms. The order-p comparison is conditional: the global-error theorem and explicit RK source supply state-error/local-defect facts only; the adjoint error, derivative-data consistency, endpoint consistency, and quadrature error remain separate hypotheses.

The implicit RK stage Jacobian is shown nonsingular from the contraction derivative bound and finite-dimensional rank-nullity before the selected-branch implicit-function formula is applied. No derivative of a finite-budget nonlinear solver is claimed. The multiple-shooting B example verifies the scalar exponential flow directly and computes its residual Jacobian and adjoint sum.

The elliptic state map uses operator-norm C1 data and uniform coercivity. The adjoint formula uses the transposed form and does not assume symmetry. The conforming finite-element gradient estimate retains state/adjoint energy errors and separate direct-objective, load-derivative, and form-derivative defects; the L2 rate adds separate dual approximation and L2-bounded parameter-derivative assumptions and states \(0<h\le1\). The one-dimensional diffusion example proves its weak identity by density from \(C_c^\infty\), product rule, FTC, Holder, and the declared Poincare bound. The parabolic gradient estimate uses the reverse-time transposed equation and retains \(P_h'u+P_hu'-u'\) in the Ritz residual; the reverse-time derivative is \(-P_{p,h}'(T-s)\). Countable Choice is declared and linked where the Lax–Milgram, Ritz, and energy-pairing suppliers use it.

The finite-time QR theorem proves the determinant/log-volume identity and a conditioned finite numerical log bound only. It makes no infinite-time Lyapunov, covariant-vector, or shadowing claim. The counterexample keeps state convergence separate from gradient convergence.
