### Load-bearing repair checks

- **Weak identities and entropy signs.** The viscous equation pairs as `weak residual = -epsilon * integral(u Delta(phi))`, with its initial term. The Riemann entropy test integral has sign `>= 0`, corresponding to distributional production `<= 0`. Both signs are now consistent through the existence and example arguments.
- **Shock criterion.** With right-minus-left jumps, `C_eta = [q] - s'[eta] = - integral_{u^-}^{u^+} eta'' F`. Ordinary oriented integration gives sufficiency, and an explicitly constructed nonnegative entropy-second-derivative bump gives necessity. For a Kruzhkov pair between the states the coefficient is `-2 sign(u^+-u^-) F(k)`. This closes the previously incorrect sign inference and removes the unsupplied Stieltjes representation.
- **Nonlinear diagonal and time compactness.** The Kato limit is controlled by local L1 translation continuity and the Lipschitz two-state flux, with distinct time/space mollifiers supported within the open slab. Compactness uses the correct convolution dual pairing, spatial translates, and a uniform local time modulus, including the initial endpoint. Limits of slice norms no longer compare a liminf with one unspecified subsequence member.
- **Oleinik.** Smooth normalized fluxes converge in C2 on the fixed state interval with convexity constants tending to kappa. Heat-potential cancellation supplies a classical differentiated solution for these smooth fluxes. A quadratic spatial barrier and an elementary maximum argument prove the slope bound; varying-flux compactness and entropy uniqueness pass it back to the C2 flux. The former Sobolev positive-part test and Gronwall invocation are removed. The converse retains the signed convex mollification commutator and strong-trace hypothesis.
- **Hamilton–Jacobi.** The complete new local argument proves the conjugate inverse derivative, constructs viscous primitives by the mild heat formula, establishes uniform tails by entropy cutoffs, upgrades local compactness to uniform-in-time L1 convergence, and passes the viscous primitives through viscosity test contacts. L1 approximation closes compactly supported bounded data. Constant-tail truncations of a bounded Lipschitz primitive, together with the explicit `|x-y| <= Ct` minimizer bound, close nonintegrable derivative data by local equality of Hopf–Lax formulas. No viscosity-to-entropy equivalence is imported as an unproved fact.
- **Gradient catastrophe.** For t>1, the positive root a(t) exceeds sqrt(t-1); the two monotone outer branches of `y-t arctan(y)` cover their respective half-lines. Their states at x=0 satisfy the stationary descending shock criterion. Bounded convergence supplies matching local traces at t=1, cancelling time-interface residuals. This establishes the advertised continuation without applying the integrable-data existence theorem to the nonintegrable datum.
- **Trace geometry and computations.** Shock trace error bounds use interval length without an extra measure(K) factor. Rarefaction discrepancies are supported between `min(0,left speed)t` and `max(0,right speed)t`, including cases with both speeds of the same sign. The Burgers shock computation now uses the negative sign of the right-state Kruzhkov flux inside the state interval. Twenty rational shock cases and the cubic counterexample's positive 3/4 production were checked with exact rational arithmetic.
