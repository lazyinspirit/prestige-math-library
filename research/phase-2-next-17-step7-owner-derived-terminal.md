Here those restrictions are already part of the repository's meanings of smooth manifold: `def-smooth-manifold` builds on `def-topological-manifold-without-boundary`, and `def-topological-manifold-with-boundary` likewise requires Hausdorff, second-countable, finite-dimensional local models. The statement's “smooth manifolds, possibly with boundary” is within the comparison theorem's scope.
- `ex-a-two-sheeted-orientation-preserving-cover-has-degree-two` — **judge claim rejected; unchanged**. The displayed quotient circle is equipped with the oriented smooth-manifold structure in the cited `prop-degree-of-the-power-map-on-the-circle`, so its topology is Hausdorff under `def-smooth-manifold`. Independently, distinct classes in `R/Z` have disjoint short quotient arcs: choose an arc radius smaller than one third of their positive circular separation, and note that integer translates of an open interval make its quotient image open. Thus compact target subsets are closed, their inverse images are closed in compact `S^1`, and F3's properness argument is valid.
- `fs-naturality-alone-implies-that-two-comparison-maps-commute-with-mayer-vietoris-connectors` — **judge claim rejected; unchanged**. Proof 1.1 explicitly cites F1, not F2, for Mayer–Vietoris exactness under the assumed `AC_omega`: the two connected arcs have diagonal restriction image in `H^0(U∩V)=R²`, so the off-diagonal class has nonzero connector. F2 is used only in the explanatory step 3.1. Its supplied-partition choice-free aside is also justified transitively: `lem-the-de-rham-map-commutes-with-mayer-vietoris-connectors` invokes `lem-de-rham-mayer-vietoris-with-boundary-and-an-explicit-partition-lift`, whose statement/proof establish exactness with a supplied partition. - `lem-the-de-rham-map-commutes-with-mayer-vietoris-connectors` — **judge claim rejected; unchanged**. Although the cited `prop-barycentric-subdivision-and-prism-preserve-smooth-singular-chains` statement emphasizes smoothness, its F2 and proof 1.1 explicitly say each simplex of `Sσ` and `Tσ` is an affine precomposition of `σ`, with affine image inside the original simplex, and conclude that both operators preserve every specified image-containing subset.
