## Mathematical conventions and sources

The A-page definition uses randomized expected replacement stability at a fixed sample size: for fixed neighboring ordered samples and test point, the absolute difference is outside expectation over the algorithm's independent internal randomness. The general theorem then proves the absolute expected population-minus-empirical gap by a ghost sample and coordinate exchange. No sample permutation-invariance assumption is made.

The convex SGD claim uses totally differentiable convex per-example losses with beta-Lipschitz gradients, scalar L-Lipschitz losses, a fixed initialization, jointly measurable loss and gradient maps, and data-independent sample indices with each marginal explicitly satisfying P(I_t=i)=1/n. The step condition is `0 <= alpha_t <= 2/beta`. The nonconvex claim retains `n >= 2`, losses in `[0,1]`, beta-smoothness, L-Lipschitzness, measurable displayed risk variables, and a nonincreasing schedule `0 <= alpha_t <= c/t`; it states the same explicit uniform-marginal law. Both conclusions concern only the expected generalization gap for that stated randomized procedure.

The two independent full treatments selected for the A page are Hardt, Recht, and Singer's full arXiv preprint and Matni's complete Lecture 16 notes. Hardt et al. §2.2 Theorem 2.2/proof supports randomized stability and expected generalization; §3.3 arXiv Theorem 3.8 (conference Theorem 3.7) is the complete convex proof; §3.4 arXiv Theorem 3.12 (conference Theorem 3.8) and Lemma 3.11 give the complete nonconvex proof.
