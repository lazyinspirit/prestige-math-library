## Exact batch5 contracts

- `lem-proper-normal-curve-rational-function-map`: assume AC, with `C` a normal proper integral curve over a field `k` and `f ∈ k(C)^×`. If `f` is algebraic over `k`, both `f` and `f⁻¹` are global regular units. Otherwise `f` gives a finite locally free map `C → P¹_k` of degree `[k(C):k(f)]`, with affine coordinate `f`. The strategy is codimension-one DVR extension to `P¹`, then proper plus quasi-finite implies finite, then finite torsion-free modules over the two polynomial PID charts are free.
- `lem-finite-flat-curve-fibre-degree`: for the preceding map of rank `d`, the zero and pole fibres have divisor degrees `d`, with coefficients `ord_x(f)` and `−ord_x(f)` weighted by `[κ(x):k]`. The strategy is base change to `0` and `∞`, decompose the finite Artinian fibres locally, and compute each local quotient length in the DVR.
- `thm-principal-divisor-degree-zero-proper-curve`: for an AC-assuming normal proper integral curve and `f ∈ k(C)^×`, `deg_k div_W(f)=0`. The strategy handles algebraic `f` by the unit clause; otherwise it subtracts the degree-`d` zero and pole fibres.
- `cor-degree-descends-picard-curve`: for an AC-assuming normal proper integral curve, `deg_k O_C(D):=deg_k D` is a homomorphism `Pic(C) → Z`. The strategy makes the curve locally factorial, represents Picard classes by divisors, and uses principal-divisor degree zero for independence.

## Complete local argument routes and current suppliers

### 1. Extension and finite locally free map to `P¹`

For a normal proper integral curve, properness gives finite type. The published `cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian` makes affine charts Noetherian. Every non-generic point of a one-dimensional integral curve is closed and has height one; the published `thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr` makes its local ring a DVR. The generic local ring is `k(C)`. If `f` is algebraic over `k`, both `f` and `f⁻¹` satisfy monic polynomials over `k`; they are integral over every local ring and hence lie in every integrally closed local ring. They therefore define global regular units.

If `f` is not algebraic over `k`, it is transcendental. At every closed point the DVR valuation makes either `f` or `f⁻¹` regular. On the open locus where `f` is regular use the chart coordinate `t=f`; where `f⁻¹` is regular use the infinity chart coordinate `u=f⁻¹`. These opens cover the generic point as well; on their overlap `f` is a unit and the chart transition is `u=t⁻¹`. The two maps glue to `φ_f:C→P¹_k`. This explicit chart construction is the required extension step; normality is used to get the DVR at each codimension-one point.

The degree is finite by a fully published route. Choose a nonempty affine chart `Spec A⊂C`; `lem-integral-finite-type-scheme-function-field` identifies `k(C)=Frac(A)`, and `thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree` gives `trdeg_k k(C)=1`. Since `f` is transcendental, `trdeg_k k(f)=1`; every algebraically independent set over `k(f)` is also algebraically independent over `k`, so its relative transcendence degree in `k(C)` is finite. `cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity` gives `trdeg_{k(f)}k(C)=0`, hence `k(C)/k(f)` is algebraic. The chart makes `k(C)` finitely generated over `k(f)`, so `thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite` gives `[k(C):k(f)]<∞`.

The morphism is proper because `C/k` is proper, `P¹_k` is separated (the published `thm-projective-space-proper-over-base` supplies properness and thus separatedness), and the published `lem-proper-source-to-separated-target-proper` applies. To verify quasi-finiteness in the library's pointwise definition, a closed-target fibre is a proper closed subset of the curve and is a finite set of closed points by published `lem-curve-closed-subsets-finite`. The residue fields of closed points on both finite-type `k`-schemes are finite over `k` by published `lem-finite-type-jacobson-residue-extension`, so each extension `κ(x)/κ(y)` is finite. The generic fibre has only the generic point (proper maps send closed points to closed points), and its residue extension is `k(C)/k(f)`, already finite. Thus points are isolated in their fibres with finite residue extensions; published `lem-quasi-finite-morphism-fibre-characterization` gives quasi-finiteness. The published `thm-proper-quasi-finite-is-finite` now gives finiteness.

On either target chart the finite morphism gives a finite algebra `B` over `k[t]` (or `k[u]`). Dominance makes `k[t]→B` injective, and integrality makes `B` a domain, so `B` is torsion-free. The published `cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid` and `cor-finitely-generated-torsion-free-modules-over-a-pid-are-free` make `B` free. Its rank is the generic fibre dimension `[k(C):k(f)]`, since a nonempty affine open of the integral source has function field `k(C)`. Thus the map is finite locally free of the claimed degree. The two charts use the same generic field extension, so their ranks agree.

The following route suppliers are current published items: `def-axiom-of-choice`, `thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr`, `cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian`, `thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree`, `lem-integral-finite-type-scheme-function-field`, `cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity`, `thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite`, `thm-projective-space-proper-over-base`, `lem-proper-source-to-separated-target-proper`, `lem-curve-closed-subsets-finite`, `lem-finite-type-jacobson-residue-extension`, `lem-quasi-finite-morphism-fibre-characterization`, `thm-proper-quasi-finite-is-finite`, `cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid`, and `cor-finitely-generated-torsion-free-modules-over-a-pid-are-free`. The degree definition is an earlier-owned batch5 item.

### 2. Fibre degree, including arbitrary residue fields and inseparable maps

For `y=0` and `y=∞`, the finite locally free rank-`d` algebra base-changes to a finite-dimensional `k`-algebra of dimension `d`. It is Artinian because a descending chain of ideals is a descending chain of finite-dimensional `k`-subspaces. The published AC-qualified `thm-structure-theorem-for-artinian-rings` decomposes it into local Artinian factors indexed by points `x` of the fibre. At a zero, the local factor is `O_{C,x}/(f)`; at a pole it is `O_{C,x}/(f⁻¹)`. The local ring is a DVR, and `thm-dvr-ideal-and-module-length` gives the quotient length `ord_x(f)` or `−ord_x(f)`. A uniformizer filtration has that many successive quotients isomorphic to `κ(x)`, each of `k`-dimension `[κ(x):k]`. Hence the fibre algebra dimension is the sum of the stated weighted orders and equals `d`.

This works over non-algebraically-closed `k`: it counts `k`-vector-space dimension using residue degrees, not geometric point count. It also works for inseparable function-field extensions: rank `d=[k(C):k(f)]` is the full field degree, and local lengths retain the scheme-theoretic multiplicity. No separability or geometric-integrality hypothesis is needed. The published `thm-dvr-ideal-and-module-length` and `cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian` are present; the map and degree/order definitions are exact batch5 suppliers.

**Manifest dependency omission to reconcile:** the fibre manifest does not list the published `thm-structure-theorem-for-artinian-rings` used for the local product decomposition. Its AC hypothesis is covered by the target's stated AC. The residue-degree conversion can be proved inline with the uniformizer filtration; no new local helper is needed.

### 3. Principal divisor degree zero

If `f` is algebraic over `k`, the map lemma makes it a global unit, so every DVR order is zero. Otherwise the Weil principal divisor is the zero fibre minus the pole fibre: positive orders are exactly the pullback multiplicities at `0`, and negative orders are exactly those at `∞`. The fibre lemma gives degree `d` for both, so their difference has degree zero. `def-degree-divisor-proper-curve` and `def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group` are the exact earlier-owned batch5 IDs.

`def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group` is explicitly DC-qualified. AC discharges this through already published `thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice`; the target manifest includes `def-axiom-of-choice` but omits that implication supplier. Add/cite it rather than treating DC as implicit. The local proof route inherits the AC cost of `lem-proper-source-to-separated-target-proper`, `thm-projective-space-proper-over-base`, `thm-proper-quasi-finite-is-finite`, and the AC-qualified closed-subset and Artinian suppliers. This is the cost of the library route; Stacks 42.18.3 proves the mathematical formula through a different route.

### 4. Degree on `Pic(C)`

The normal Noetherian curve is locally factorial: at its generic point the local ring is a field; at every closed point it is a DVR. The current published `cor-dvr-is-a-pid` and AC-qualified `thm-principal-ideal-domains-are-unique-factorisation-domains` show those closed local rings are UFDs. This establishes the `def-locally-factorial-scheme` hypothesis (an earlier-owned batch5 definition). The earlier-owned batch5 `thm-cartier-weil-isomorphism-locally-factorial` then gives `Pic(C)≅Cl(C)`, with its DC assumption discharged using the same published AC⇒DC theorem.

The degree definition is a homomorphism on Weil divisors. The principal-degree theorem makes it vanish on the principal subgroup, so it descends to `Cl(C)` and composes with `Pic(C)≅Cl(C)`. For a Weil divisor `D`, local factoriality makes `D` Cartier, and the Cartier/Weil map identifies the Picard class of `O_C(D)` with the class of `D`; therefore the resulting homomorphism is precisely `O_C(D)↦deg_k D`. The earlier-owned batch5 `thm-cartier-divisors-mod-principal-to-picard` and `thm-cartier-weil-isomorphism-locally-factorial` are the required Cartier/Picard suppliers.

**Manifest dependency omissions to reconcile:** `cor-degree-descends-picard-curve` does not list the published `cor-dvr-is-a-pid`, the published AC-qualified `thm-principal-ideal-domains-are-unique-factorisation-domains`, the batch5 `def-locally-factorial-scheme`, or published `thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice`. No new helper is required. Its statement should make clear that `D` is Cartier, or that a Weil divisor is being used and is Cartier because the normal one-dimensional curve is locally factorial.

## Hypotheses and remaining uncertainty

- No route assumes `k` algebraically closed or `C` geometrically integral. The algebraic-over-`k` branch is important: the constant field of `k(C)` may be larger than `k`, and an element algebraic over `k` is handled as a global unit rather than as a dominant map to `P¹`.
- Normality is essential to this promised local route: it supplies DVRs at the closed codimension-one points and makes Weil order equal to the DVR valuation. Stacks 42.18.3 states a broader degree-zero result, but its graph-closure/local-length proof is not a replacement for the batch5 normal-curve statements.
- The missing manifest links above point to published existing suppliers (or exact earlier-owned batch5 IDs), not to a missing mathematical lemma. The Artinian fibre-to-residue-degree calculation can be written directly using a uniformizer filtration. I identified no need to add a new local helper.
- Source review is not a proof audit of the live batch5 author files. In particular, the existence or absence of the three target files noted at the start is not evidence for or against the correctness of their eventual proofs.
