## Statement

Under the hypotheses and notation of [[lem-generic-pfaffian-straightening]], the ring $k[a_{ij}:i<j]/J_{m+2}$ is an integrally closed domain for every even $m$ with $0\le m\le t$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, integers, Pfaffians and quotient $R$ in the Statement.

[F1] [[lem-generic-pfaffian-straightening]] supplies a basis of standard products and an injective Gram substitution into $k[x_{ij}:1\le i\le t,1\le j\le m]$; the leading monomial of $P_S$ is $d_S=\prod_{j=1}^{|S|}x_{s_j,j}$.

## Proof

1.1 The Gram embedding in [F1] makes $R$ a domain. We describe its monomial degeneration explicitly. Choose positive integer weights on the finitely many variables $x_{ij}$ which realize the column-first lexicographic comparisons between all terms in all the finitely many Gram images $P_S$, $|S|\le m$. Such weights exist: list the variables in that lexicographic order, give successive variables weights $N^{tm-1},N^{tm-2},\ldots,1$, and take $N>2t+1$, larger than the total degree of each polynomial being compared. Thus $d_S$ is the unique term of largest weight in each image. Filter $R$ by these ambient polynomial weights. A standard product has unique term of largest weight equal to the corresponding product of the $d_S$; these monomials are distinct by [F1]. In a linear combination of standard products the terms of maximum weight therefore do not cancel. Consequently the associated graded ring is exactly $k[\Gamma]$, where $\Gamma$ is the additive semigroup generated by the exponent vectors of all $d_S$, for even $|S|\le m$. In particular this is a filtration of the entire ring, not merely a computation of the initial terms of its generators. [F1, algebra, choose]

2.1 Write an exponent array as $b=(b_{ij})$, put $\lambda_j=\sum_i b_{ij}$, and put $C_j(u)=\sum_{i\le u}b_{ij}$, with $C_j(0)=0$. The semigroup $\Gamma$ consists exactly of the integer arrays satisfying [step 1.1, algebra]
$$
b_{ij}\ge0,\qquad \lambda_{2r-1}=\lambda_{2r},\qquad \lambda_j\ge\lambda_{j+1},\qquad C_{j+1}(u)\le C_j(u-1)\quad(1\le j<m,\ 1\le u\le t).
$$
Necessity follows by adding these inequalities for individual increasing even rows. For sufficiency sort the row indices in each column increasingly, with multiplicities. The cumulative inequalities say that the $a$th entry of column $j$ is strictly less than the $a$th entry of column $j+1$ whenever the latter exists: a failure at value $u$ would give $C_{j+1}(u)>C_j(u-1)$, and the converse follows by taking $a=C_{j+1}(u)$. The inequalities on column lengths make a left-justified tableau; their pairwise equalities say that every row length is even. Each row is therefore an allowed $S$. Reading these rows from top to bottom gives a standard product and expresses $b$ as a sum of exponent vectors of $d_S$. All displayed conditions are homogeneous integral linear inequalities or equalities. Thus if $b$ is in the group $L$ generated by $\Gamma$ and $qb\in\Gamma$ for a positive integer $q$, then $b\in\Gamma$. More precisely $\Gamma=L\cap\mathcal C$, where $\mathcal C$ is the rational polyhedral cone cut out by the displayed conditions. [step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 This proves normality of $k[\Gamma]$ directly. The group algebra $k[L]$ is a Laurent polynomial ring after choosing an integral lattice basis and is integrally closed. Each defining integral linear inequality $\ell\ge0$ gives the valuation $v_\ell(\sum c_b X^b)=\min_{c_b\ne0}\ell(b)$ on its fraction field (combine equal-weight terms first); its nonnegative valuation ring is integrally closed. The equality $\Gamma=L\cap\mathcal C$ gives [step 2.1, algebra]
$$
k[\Gamma]=k[L]\cap\bigcap_{\ell}\{f:v_\ell(f)\ge0\}.
$$
Indeed for a Laurent polynomial nonnegative minimum under every $\ell$ means that every exponent lies in $\mathcal C$. An intersection of integrally closed subrings of one field is integrally closed, by applying a monic equation in every member of the intersection. Hence the special ring of the filtration is a normal domain. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 We give the lifting argument to avoid assuming normality from distinct initial terms. Let $\mathcal B=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}F_dR\,z^d\subset R[z]$ be the Rees ring of step 1.1. It is generated by $z$ and $P_Sz^{w(d_S)}$: expand an element in the standard basis, and use the absence of highest-weight cancellation in step 1.1. It is a finitely generated domain, $\mathcal B/(z)=k[\Gamma]$, and $\mathcal B[z^{-1}]=R[z,z^{-1}]$. Retain also the ordinary grading $\deg(P_S)=|S|/2$ and $\deg z=0$; then $\mathcal B_0=k[z]$ and all other generators have positive ordinary degree. We use the following local normality argument. For a finite-type domain $D$ over a field and a nonzero element $z$, if $(D/(z))_{\mathfrak p}$ is normal, then $D_{\mathfrak p}$ is normal for every prime $\mathfrak p$ containing $z$. To check this by Serre's criterion, localize at such $\mathfrak p$. At a prime $Q$ containing $z$, depth increases by one on adjoining the nonzerodivisor $z$, so $S_2$ follows from $S_2$ of the quotient; height-one primes containing $z$ are regular since their quotient is a field. For $Q$ not containing $z$, choose a prime $P$ minimal over $Q+(z)$ in this local ring. The dimension formula for finite-type domains and the principal ideal theorem give $\operatorname{ht}P=\operatorname{ht}Q+1$ and $\dim(D_P/QD_P)=1$. If $\operatorname{ht}Q=1$, the quotient of $D_P$ by $z$ is a normal one-dimensional local domain, hence a discrete valuation ring; lifting a parameter shows $D_P$ is regular of dimension two, and its localization $D_Q$ is regular. If $\operatorname{ht}Q\ge2$, the quotient has depth at least two, so $D_P$ has depth at least three; the depth-localization inequality $\operatorname{depth}D_Q\ge\operatorname{depth}D_P-\dim(D_P/QD_P)$ gives depth at least two at $Q$. Thus $R_1$ and $S_2$ hold throughout the local ring, which proves the assertion. These are the usual local depth and dimension statements for noetherian rings; the argument identifies exactly their uses. Applied to $\mathcal B$, step 3.1 proves normality at every point of the fiber $z=0$. [step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For completeness, this local conclusion controls every fiber here. The normalization of a finitely generated domain over a characteristic-zero field is finite; thus its nonnormal locus is closed (it is the support of the finite normalization quotient). This finiteness follows from Noether normalization and the trace-dual bound for the integral closure in the finite separable fraction-field extension. The nonnormal locus of $\mathcal B$ is stable under both its ordinary grading action and its filtration grading action. If it contained a point over $z=c\ne0$, its closure under the ordinary grading action would contain the point of the section with all positive-ordinary-degree generators zero and $z=c$: the orbit map extends to scaling parameter zero. The filtration grading action then gives all points of that section with $z\ne0$; closedness gives its point at $z=0$, contradicting step 4.1. Over the algebraically closed field $k$, a nonempty closed subset has a closed point, so this excludes the entire nonnormal locus. Hence $\mathcal B$ and $R[z,z^{-1}]$ are normal. Finally an element of $\operatorname{Frac}R$ integral over $R$ is integral over this Laurent extension and therefore belongs to it; its Laurent expansion has only the degree-zero coefficient, so it belongs to $R$. This proves normality of $R$. When $m=0$, $R=k$ directly; all other boundary cases are included in the filtration argument. [step 4.1, algebra] ∎
