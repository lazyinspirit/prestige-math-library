## Definition

A rational homology sphere is a closed connected oriented three-manifold $Y$ with $H_*(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong H_*(S^3;\mathbb Q)$. Let $\mathfrak t$ be a Spin$^c$ structure on $Y$, meaning a principal $\mathrm{Spin}^c(3)$ lift of the oriented frame bundle. A pointed Heegaard diagram for $(Y,\mathfrak t)$ consists of a closed oriented surface $\Sigma$ of genus $g$, two systems $\boldsymbol\alpha=(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_g)$ and $\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_g)$ of pairwise disjoint simple closed curves which bound complete disk systems in the two genus-$g$ handlebodies of a Heegaard splitting of $Y$, and a basepoint $z\in\Sigma\setminus(\boldsymbol\alpha\cup\boldsymbol\beta)$; the diagram is restricted to the generators assigned to $\mathfrak t$. A periodic domain is an integral two-chain in the complementary regions whose boundary is an integral sum of attaching curves. The diagram is strongly admissible for $\mathfrak t$ when every nonzero periodic domain $P$ with $n_z(P)=0$ and $\langle c_1(\mathfrak t),[P]\rangle\ge0$ has at least one positive and one negative region coefficient.

For such a diagram, put $\mathbb T_\alpha=\alpha_1\times\cdots\times\alpha_g$ and $\mathbb T_\beta=\beta_1\times\cdots\times\beta_g$ in $\operatorname{Sym}^g(\Sigma)=\Sigma^g/S_g$. A generator is an intersection point $\mathbf x\in\mathbb T_\alpha\cap\mathbb T_\beta$. A Whitney disk class $\phi\in\pi_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)$ is a relative homotopy class of disks in $\operatorname{Sym}^g(\Sigma)$ with boundary arcs on the two product tori and endpoints $\mathbf x,\mathbf y$; its Maslov index is $\mu(\phi)$ and its local multiplicity at $z$ is $n_z(\phi)$. For a regular almost-complex datum for which every relevant count is finite and the disk-count operator squares to zero, define $CF^\infty(Y,\mathfrak t)$ over $\mathbb F_2[U,U^{-1}]$ on generators $[\mathbf x,i]$, $i\in\mathbb Z$, with differential
$$
\partial^\infty[\mathbf x,i]=\sum_{\mathbf y}\sum_{\substack{\phi\in\pi_2(\mathbf x,\mathbf y)\\\mu(\phi)=1,\ n_z(\phi)\ge0}}\#_2\bigl(\mathcal M(\phi)/\mathbb R\bigr)[\mathbf y,i-n_z(\phi)].
$$
Here $\mathbb F_2$ is the field with two elements, $U[\mathbf x,i]=[\mathbf x,i-1]$, and $\#_2$ is the finite count modulo two. The submodule generated by $[\mathbf x,i]$ with $i<0$ is $CF^-(Y,\mathfrak t)$, $CF^+(Y,\mathfrak t)=CF^\infty/CF^-$, and $HF^\infty$ and $HF^+$ denote their homology when the differential is established. Relative Maslov gradings are fixed by the Fredholm index and the shift under $U$ is $-2$.

When the standard absolute rational Maslov grading is available, normalize it so that the generator of $HF^\infty(S^3)$ has degree zero. For a smooth Spin$^c$ cobordism $(W,\mathfrak s)$ between rational homology spheres, the induced map is assigned degree shift
$$
\frac{\langle c_1(\mathfrak s)^2\rangle-2\chi(W)-3\sigma(W)}{4},
$$
where $c_1(\mathfrak s)$ is the first Chern class, $\chi(W)$ is Euler characteristic, $\sigma(W)$ is the signature, and its square is evaluated using the rational intersection form of $W$. This is a convention for a Floer package when constructed; it asserts neither existence of generic regular data nor that the disk-count differential squares to zero. Heegaard invariance, the absolute grading on general $(Y,\mathfrak t)$, and cobordism-map properties are separate claims.
