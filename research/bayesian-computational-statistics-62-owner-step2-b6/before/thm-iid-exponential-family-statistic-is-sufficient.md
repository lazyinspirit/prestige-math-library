## Statement

Assume AC. Let $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$ be standard Borel, let $\mu$ be a
sigma-finite measure, and let $(P_\eta)_{\eta\in\Omega}$ be a nonempty
exponential family of [[def-exponential-family]] with density
$p_\eta(y)=h(y)\exp\{\eta^TT(y)-A(\eta)\}$. For each finite $n\ge1$, the IID
sample law $P_\eta^{\otimes n}$ has density
$$\prod_{i=1}^nh(y_i)\exp\!\left\{\eta^T\sum_{i=1}^nT(y_i)-nA(\eta)\right\}$$
relative to $\mu^{\otimes n}$, and
$T_n(y_1,\ldots,y_n)=\sum_{i=1}^nT(y_i)$ is sufficient for the sample.
No full-rank, completeness, or converse characterization is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the standard-Borel sample space, sigma-finite $\mu$, and a
nonempty family with the displayed exponential-family density.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed by the common-reference factorization criterion and its conditional-law suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] An exponential-family density is $p_\eta=h\exp\{\eta^TT-A(\eta)\}$ and integrates to one against $\mu$. [[def-exponential-family]].

[F2] Finite product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles, and finite products of sigma-finite measures are sigma-finite and satisfy the rectangle formula. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]] [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]].

[F3] Tonelli integrates nonnegative product densities by iterated integrals. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]].

[F4] A nonnegative measurable density induces a measure by integration. [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]].

[F5] Measures agreeing on a generating pi-system with a finite exhaustion agree on the product sigma-algebra. [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]].

[F6] The finite sample product of standard-Borel spaces is standard Borel, and the finite-dimensional Euclidean statistic space $\mathbb R^k$ with its Borel sigma-algebra is standard Borel. [[lem-finite-products-of-standard-borel-spaces-are-standard-borel]] [[lem-finite-euclidean-space-is-standard-borel]].

[F7] If every likelihood ratio relative to one in-model reference law is a Borel function of a statistic, the statistic is sufficient on standard-Borel spaces. [[thm-common-reference-factorization-criterion]].

[F8] Sufficiency means there is one conditional-law kernel of the data given the statistic that works for every parameter. [[def-sufficient-statistic]].

[F9] Measurable functions are closed under finite products and sums. [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]].

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 For fixed $\eta$, each coordinate pullback of $p_\eta$ is measurable on the product sigma-algebra [F2], and the product density $g_\eta(y_{1:n})=\prod_i p_\eta(y_i)$ is measurable by [F9]. Its integral against $\mu^{\otimes n}$ is one by [F2], repeated Tonelli [F3], and $\int p_\eta\,d\mu=1$. Thus $g_\eta\,d\mu^{\otimes n}$ is a probability measure by [F4]. On every measurable rectangle it equals $\prod_iP_\eta(A_i)$ by the rectangle formula and Tonelli, so the sigma-finite pi-system uniqueness theorem [F5] identifies it with the IID product law. Expanding $g_\eta$ gives the density displayed in the Statement. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F9, given]

2.1 Since $\Omega$ is nonempty, choose $\eta_0\in\Omega$. The density ratio on the common support is $\frac{g_\eta(y_{1:n})}{g_{\eta_0}(y_{1:n})} =\exp\{(\eta-\eta_0)^TT_n(y_{1:n})-n(A(\eta)-A(\eta_0))\}.$ If any $h(y_i)=0$, both product densities vanish, so the same positive Borel function of $T_n$ satisfies $g_\eta=r_\eta(T_n)g_{\eta_0}$ everywhere. Integrating this identity on each measurable set proves that it is a version of the Radon–Nikodym derivative under $P_{\eta_0}^{\otimes n}$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra, choose]

3.1 By [F6] both the sample and the finite-dimensional statistic spaces are standard Borel. The reference $P_{\eta_0}^{\otimes n}$ belongs to the family, and step 2.1 makes every likelihood ratio a Borel function of $T_n$. Applying [F7] gives a common conditional-law kernel, which is sufficiency by [F8]. The sole choice-axiom use is inherited from [A1] and [F7]. [A1, F6, F7, F8, step 2.1, given] ∎
