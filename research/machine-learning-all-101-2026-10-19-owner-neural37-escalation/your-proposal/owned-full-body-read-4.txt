
===== lem-pinn-residual-loss-from-fill-distance =====


## Statement

For $d\ge2$, let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ and $\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^d$ be measurable, and let $\mu_r$ and $\mu_b$ be probability measures on them. For positive integers $m_r,m_b$, let the residual samples $x_1^r,\ldots,x_{m_r}^r$ and boundary samples $x_1^b,\ldots,x_{m_b}^b$ form $\varepsilon_r$- and $\varepsilon_b$-nets of $U$ and $\Gamma$ in Euclidean distance. Assume that for every center $x\in U$ and $y\in\Gamma$,
$$\mu_r(\overline B_{\varepsilon_r}(x)\cap U)\le C_r\varepsilon_r^d,\qquad \mu_b(\overline B_{\varepsilon_b}(y)\cap\Gamma)\le C_b\varepsilon_b^{d-1}.$$
Suppose $[L[h]]_{\alpha;U}$, $[B[h]]_{\alpha;\Gamma}$, $[f]_{\alpha;U}$, and $[g]_{\alpha;\Gamma}$ are finite, where $[v]_{\alpha;E}=\sup_{x\ne y\in E}|v(x)-v(y)|/|x-y|^\alpha$. Let $\lambda_r,\lambda_b>0$, and use the population and empirical weighted-square PINN losses
$$\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}(h;\lambda)=\lambda_r\int_U|L[h]-f|^2\,d\mu_r+\lambda_b\int_\Gamma|B[h]-g|^2\,d\mu_b,$$
$$\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}_m(h;\lambda)=\frac{\lambda_r}{m_r}\sum_{i=1}^{m_r}|L[h](x_i^r)-f(x_i^r)|^2+\frac{\lambda_b}{m_b}\sum_{j=1}^{m_b}|B[h](x_j^b)-g(x_j^b)|^2.$$
Define
$$C_m=3\max\{C_rm_r\varepsilon_r^d,\ C_bm_b\varepsilon_b^{d-1}\},\quad \lambda^R_{r,m}=\frac{3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}}{C_m},\quad \lambda^R_{b,m}=\frac{3\lambda_b\varepsilon_b^{2\alpha}}{C_m}.$$
Then
$$\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}(h;\lambda)\le C_m\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}_m(h;\lambda)+C_m\left(\lambda^R_{r,m}[L[h]]_{\alpha;U}^2+\lambda^R_{b,m}[B[h]]_{\alpha;\Gamma}^2\right)+3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}[f]_{\alpha;U}^2+3\lambda_b\varepsilon_b^{2\alpha}[g]_{\alpha;\Gamma}^2.$$

For $d=1$, assume $\mu_r$ is a probability measure on measurable $U\subset\mathbb R$, and $\Gamma$ is a nonempty finite measurable set with probability measure $\mu_b$. Every point of $\Gamma$ is available deterministically. For a positive integer $m_r$, let $x_1^r,\ldots,x_{m_r}^r$ form an $\varepsilon_r$-net of $U$, and assume $\mu_r(\overline B_{\varepsilon_r}(x)\cap U)\le C_r\varepsilon_r$ for every center $x\in U$. Suppose the same four Hölder seminorms are finite. Use the exact weighted boundary loss
$$\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN},b}(h;\lambda_b)=\lambda_b\int_\Gamma|B[h]-g|^2\,d\mu_b=\lambda_b\sum_{y\in\Gamma}\mu_b(\{y\})|B[h](y)-g(y)|^2$$
and the empirical loss $\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}_m(h;\lambda)=\frac{\lambda_r}{m_r}\sum_{i=1}^{m_r}|L[h](x_i^r)-f(x_i^r)|^2+\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN},b}(h;\lambda_b)$. Set
$$C_m=3C_rm_r\varepsilon_r,\qquad \lambda^R_{r,m}=\frac{3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}}{C_m},\qquad \lambda^R_{b,m}=0.$$
Then
$$\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}(h;\lambda)\le C_m\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN}}_m(h;\lambda)+C_m\lambda^R_{r,m}[L[h]]_{\alpha;U}^2+3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}[f]_{\alpha;U}^2.$$
There is no random boundary term or boundary-data error term in this branch because the full boundary contribution is evaluated exactly.

## Statement references
[Shin et al., Appendix B, Lemmas B.1–B.2: deterministic fill-distance inequality](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

[Shin et al., Appendix B, proof of Lemma B.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The trial function, residual and boundary operators, sample sets, fill distances, measures, and positive loss weights in the Statement.

[F1] The population and empirical PINN losses use the weighted-square conventions in the local definition ([[def-physics-informed-neural-network-loss]]).

[F2] For $d\ge2$, the residual cells have mass at most $C_r\varepsilon_r^d$ and the boundary cells have mass at most $C_b\varepsilon_b^{d-1}$ by the upper ball-mass assumptions. For $d=1$, each residual cell has mass at most $C_r\varepsilon_r$ by the residual upper ball-mass assumption in the Statement.

[F3] The Hölder seminorm convention is $|v(x)-v(y)|\le[v]_{\alpha;E}|x-y|^\alpha$ ([[def-physics-informed-neural-network-loss]]).

[F4] In the one-dimensional branch, the exact full-boundary empirical contribution equals the population boundary integral, and a finite union of sample-centered $\varepsilon_r$-balls covers $U$ ([[def-physics-informed-neural-network-loss]], [[thm-finite-and-countable-subadditivity-of-measures]]).

## Proof

**Proof strategy:** integrate local collocation bounds over nearest-sample cells.

1.1 Order the finite residual sample set and assign each point of $U$ to a nearest residual sample, breaking ties by the least index. This produces a measurable finite Voronoi partition $V_1,\ldots,V_{m_r}$; each cell lies in the radius-$\varepsilon_r$ ball about its assigned sample. For $d\ge2$, also assign each point of $\Gamma$ to a nearest boundary sample to obtain cells $W_j$; for $d=1$, use every point of the finite boundary deterministically and make no boundary partition. [given, choose]

1.2 By [F2], $\mu_r(V_i)\le C_r\varepsilon_r^d$ in the $d\ge2$ branch and $\mu_r(V_i)\le C_r\varepsilon_r$ in the $d=1$ branch. If $x\in V_i$, the three-term square inequality and [F3] give $$|L[h](x)-f(x)|^2\le3|L[h](x_i^r)-f(x_i^r)|^2+3\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}[L[h]]_{\alpha;U}^2+3\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}[f]_{\alpha;U}^2.$$ For $d\ge2$, each boundary cell satisfies $\mu_b(W_j)\le C_b\varepsilon_b^{d-1}$, and its analogous local square bound holds. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Integrate the residual inequality over the cells. The collocation terms contribute at most $3C_rm_r\varepsilon_r^d\lambda_r m_r^{-1}\sum_i|L[h](x_i^r)-f(x_i^r)|^2$ for $d\ge2$, and $3C_rm_r\varepsilon_r\lambda_r m_r^{-1}\sum_i|L[h](x_i^r)-f(x_i^r)|^2$ for $d=1$. The seminorm and data terms contribute $3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}([L[h]]_{\alpha;U}^2+[f]_{\alpha;U}^2)$ because $\mu_r(U)=1$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, algebra]

2.2 For $d\ge2$, integrating the boundary inequality gives the estimate with $3C_bm_b\varepsilon_b^{d-1}$ multiplying the empirical boundary term and $3\lambda_b\varepsilon_b^{2\alpha}([B[h]]_{\alpha;\Gamma}^2+[g]_{\alpha;\Gamma}^2)$ for the remaining terms. For $d=1$, the weighted boundary term in the population loss is exactly $\operatorname{Loss}^{\mathrm{PINN},b}(h;\lambda_b)$ from [F4]. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F4, algebra]

3.1 For $d\ge2$, by the definition of $C_m$, the empirical residual and boundary coefficients in steps 2.1–2.2 are at most $C_m$. The seminorm coefficients equal $C_m\lambda^R_{r,m}$ and $C_m\lambda^R_{b,m}$ by definition, while the data terms already have their displayed coefficients. Adding the estimates yields the result. For $d=1$, the net and upper ball-mass bound imply $1=\mu_r(U)\le\sum_{i=1}^{m_r}\mu_r(\overline B_{\varepsilon_r}(x_i^r)\cap U)\le m_rC_r\varepsilon_r$, so $C_m\ge3$. Thus $C_m$ dominates the exact boundary term, the residual empirical coefficient from step 2.1 equals $C_m$, and $C_m\lambda^R_{r,m}=3\lambda_r\varepsilon_r^{2\alpha}$. Combining these bounds with the residual data term proves the one-dimensional inequality. [step 2.1, step 2.2, F4, algebra] ∎


===== lem-shin-bounded-open-set-hilbert-evolution-triple =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded and open, $d\ge1$, and put $H=L^2(U;\mathbb R)$ and $V=H^1_0(U;\mathbb R)$, where the latter is the $H^1$-closure of $C_c^\infty(U)$. Then $V$ is a separable real Hilbert space, its inclusion into $H$ is continuous, injective and dense, and $(V,H,V^*)$ is a real evolution triple. The gradient norm and the full $H^1$ norm are equivalent on $V$. No regularity of $\partial U$ is required.

## Statement references

[Clason, Chapter 11: the Sobolev evolution-triple setting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1709.08618v2.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, a bounded open $U\subset\mathbb R^d$, and the stated real spaces.

[A1] Countable Choice is carried by the $L^2$ completeness and density suppliers and by the countable selections below ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] $L^2(U)$ is a real Hilbert space; rational box-step functions are countable and dense in $L^2(\mathbb R^d)$ ([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]], [[thm-rational-box-step-functions-form-a-countable-dense-subset-of-l-p-of-rn]]).

[F2] Weak derivatives are defined by integration against compactly supported smooth tests; $H^1_0(U)$ is the $H^1$-closure of those smooth functions ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]).

[F3] On an open set bounded in one direction, $\|v\|_2\le C\|Dv\|_2$ for $v\in H^1_0(U)$ ([[thm-poincare-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]]).

[F4] $C_c^\infty(U)$ is dense in $L^2(U)$ for every open $U$ ([[lem-smooth-compactly-supported-functions-are-dense-in-ltwo-of-an-open-set]]).

[F5] A continuous dense injective Hilbert inclusion $V\hookrightarrow H$ defines the canonical real evolution triple ([[def-parabolic-evolution-triple-and-energy-space]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $U=\varnothing$, both spaces are zero and the conclusions hold. Otherwise choose finite $a<b$ with $U\subset\{a<x_1<b\}$. The zero extension of any $h\in H$ belongs to $L^2(\mathbb R^d)$ with the same norm. Restricting the countable dense box-step family in [F1] to $U$ therefore gives a countable dense subset of $H$. The product $Z=H^{d+1}$ is a separable Hilbert space. [given, A1, F1, construct]

2.1 The map $J:H^1(U)\to Z$, $J(v)=(v,D_1v,\ldots,D_dv)$, is an isometry for the full Sobolev norm. Its range is closed: if $J(v_n)\to(v,g_1,\ldots,g_d)$ in $Z$, then for every $\phi\in C_c^\infty(U)$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives $\int vD_i\phi=\lim_n\int v_nD_i\phi=-\lim_n\int D_iv_n\phi=-\int g_i\phi$. Thus $g_i=D_iv$ weakly, so the limit is $J(v)$. A closed subspace of a Hilbert space is complete, hence $H^1(U)$ is Hilbert. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By the closure definition, $V$ is a closed subspace of $H^1(U)$ and is Hilbert. The Poincare inequality applies because $U$ is bounded in the first direction, and yields $\|Dv\|_2^2\le\|v\|_{H^1}^2\le(1+C^2)\|Dv\|_2^2$ on $V$. This proves norm equivalence and continuity of the inclusion $V\hookrightarrow H$. Injectivity holds because these spaces use the same almost-everywhere equivalence classes. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 A subspace $Y$ of a separable metric space $Z$ is separable under [A1]: if $(z_j)$ is dense in $Z$, the sets $Y\cap B(z_j,r)$ for positive rational $r$ form a countable base of $Y$; choose one point from every nonempty such set. Any relatively open neighborhood contains one of these sets, so the chosen points are dense. Apply this first to $J(H^1(U))\subset Z$ and then to $J(V)$; transport the dense subsets through the isometry $J$. Thus $V$ is separable. [A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 By [F4], $C_c^\infty(U)$ is dense in $H$, and it is contained in $V$ by [F2]. Thus $V\hookrightarrow H$ is dense. The Hilbert, separability, continuity and injectivity conclusions already proved give the canonical evolution triple by [F5]. Every argument used an arbitrary bounded open $U$, so none requires a boundary chart or a polyhedral boundary. [F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

This reproduces the actual argument of the read published polyhedral Sobolev-evolution supplier on the larger bounded-open-set domain class. Its closed-graph, separability and compact-smooth-density arguments do not use polyhedral geometry. The bibliographic reference records the evolution-space context; no new reading of Clason's full text is claimed here.

===== lem-shin-elliptic-energy-estimate =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded Lipschitz. Let $A=(a_{ij})$ be a bounded measurable real symmetric matrix field, uniformly elliptic with eigenvalues at least $\lambda_0>0$, and let $b,c,d$ be bounded measurable real coefficient fields satisfying
$$\lambda_0^{-2}(\|b(x)\|^2+\|c(x)\|^2)+\lambda_0^{-1}|d(x)|\le v^2\qquad(x\in U).$$
For the divergence operator
$$L[e]=\sum_{i,j}D_i(a_{ij}D_j e)+\sum_iD_i(b_i e)+\sum_i c_iD_i e+de,$$
every real $e\in H^1_0(U)\cap C^2(U)\cap C^0(\bar U)$ with $L[e]\in L^2(U)$ satisfies
$$\|e\|_{H^1(U)}^2\le C_E\bigl(\|L[e]\|_{L^2(U)}^2+\|e\|_{L^2(U)}^2\bigr),$$
where $C_E$ depends only on $\lambda_0$ and $v$. The zero-trace condition is required for the integration by parts.

## Statement references
[Shin et al., Appendix E: source elliptic H1 error estimate and coefficient bound](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

[Shin et al., Appendix E: energy estimate, with the lower-order coefficient absorption written out here](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The bounded Lipschitz domain, uniformly elliptic symmetric matrix, coefficient bound, zero-trace function, and divergence operator in the Statement.

[A1] Countable Choice is the explicit premise of the zero-boundary Sobolev closure convention ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]).

[F1] $H^1(U)=W^{1,2}(U)$ has norm squared $\|e\|_{L^2(U)}^2+\|\nabla e\|_{L^2(U)}^2$ ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[F2] The divergence operator uses the positive-principal-part convention displayed in [[def-shin-elliptic-divergence-form-operator]].

[F3] $H^1_0(U)$ is the $H^1$-closure of $C_c^\infty(U)$, and the $H^1$ norm contains the $L^2$ norm and weak-gradient norm ([[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]], [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

## Proof

**Proof strategy:** integrate against the zero-trace error and absorb lower-order terms with Young's inequality.

1.1 By [F3], choose $\phi_n\in C_c^\infty(U)$ with $\phi_n\to e$ in $H^1(U)$. The distributional definition of each divergence term and the ordinary test-function integration by parts give $$\int_U\phi_nL[e]\,dx=-\int_U a_{ij}D_j eD_i\phi_n\,dx-\int_U b_i eD_i\phi_n\,dx+\int_U c_iD_i e\,\phi_n\,dx+\int_U d e\,\phi_n\,dx.$$ All coefficients are bounded and $e,\nabla e,L[e]\in L^2$, so each term is continuous under $H^1$ convergence. Taking $n\to\infty$ and using [F2] yields $$-\int_U eL[e]\,dx=\int_U a_{ij}D_j eD_i e\,dx+\int_U b_i eD_i e\,dx-\int_U c_i eD_i e\,dx-\int_U d e^2\,dx.$$ The principal term is at least $\lambda_0\|\nabla e\|_2^2$ by ellipticity. [given, A1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The coefficient bound gives $\|b\|^2+\|c\|^2\le\lambda_0^2v^2$ and $|d|\le\lambda_0v^2$. Hence the absolute value of the two first-order terms is at most $\sqrt2\lambda_0v\|e\|_2\|\nabla e\|_2$, and the zero-order term is at most $\lambda_0v^2\|e\|_2^2$. Young's inequality bounds the first-order contribution by $\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\|\nabla e\|_2^2+\lambda_0v^2\|e\|_2^2$. Therefore $$-\int_U eL[e]\,dx\ge\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\|\nabla e\|_2^2-2\lambda_0v^2\|e\|_2^2.$$ [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $L[e]\in L^2(U)$, Cauchy–Schwarz and step 2.1 imply $$\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\|\nabla e\|_2^2\le\|L[e]\|_2\|e\|_2+2\lambda_0v^2\|e\|_2^2\le\frac{1}{2\lambda_0}\|L[e]\|_2^2+\left(\frac{1}{2\lambda_0}+2\lambda_0v^2\right)\|e\|_2^2.$$ Thus $\|\nabla e\|_2^2\le\lambda_0^{-2}\|L[e]\|_2^2+(\lambda_0^{-2}+4v^2)\|e\|_2^2$. By [F1], adding $\|e\|_2^2$ gives the claimed $H^1$ estimate with $C_E=\max\{\lambda_0^{-2},1+\lambda_0^{-2}+4v^2\}$. [step 2.1, F1, algebra] ∎


===== lem-shin-elliptic-residual-stability =====


## Statement

Let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded and open, and let
$$L[u]=\sum_{i,j=1}^d a_{ij}D_iD_j u+\sum_{j=1}^d\widetilde b_jD_j u+\widetilde c\,u$$
on $U$, where $a(x)$ is symmetric, $\xi^Ta(x)\xi\ge\lambda_0|\xi|^2$ for all $x\in U$ and $\xi\in\mathbb R^d$ with $\lambda_0>0$, the coefficients are continuous and bounded, and $\widetilde c\le0$. There is a constant $C_L<\infty$, depending only on $U$, $\lambda_0$, and the coefficient bounds, such that every $w\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\bar U)$ with $L[w]\in C^0(U)$ satisfies
$$\|w\|_{C^0(U)}\le C_L\left(\|L[w]\|_{C^0(U)}+\|w\|_{C^0(\partial U)}\right).$$
In particular, for any two functions $u^*,h\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\bar U)$,
$$\|u^*-h\|_{C^0(U)}\le C_L\left(\|L[u^*]-L[h]\|_{C^0(U)}+\|u^*-h\|_{C^0(\partial U)}\right).$$

## Statement references
[Shin et al., Appendix D, Lemma D.2: residual and boundary stability estimate](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

[Shin et al., Appendix D, Lemma D.2: the stability interface that this barrier proof reconstructs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The bounded domain, uniformly elliptic coefficient matrix, bounded lower-order coefficients with $\widetilde c\le0$, and $w\in C^2(U)\cap C^0(\bar U)$.

[F1] The operator expands with the positive principal part and zero-order coefficient shown in [[def-shin-elliptic-divergence-form-operator]].

[F2] A continuous function on the compact set $\bar U$ attains its maximum; a $C^2$ function at an interior maximum has zero gradient and negative-semidefinite Hessian.

## Proof

**Proof strategy:** construct a strict positive barrier and apply the weak maximum principle by perturbation.

1.1 Since $U$ is bounded, choose real numbers $a_1<b_1$ with $a_1\le x_1\le b_1$ on $\bar U$. Let $B=\|\widetilde b_1\|_{C^0(U)}$ and $C_0=\|\widetilde c\|_{C^0(U)}$. Choose $\kappa>0$ large enough that $\kappa^2\lambda_0-\kappa B-C_0\ge1$. For $\psi(x)=e^{\kappa x_1}$, [F1] gives $$L[\psi](x)=e^{\kappa x_1}\bigl(\kappa^2a_{11}(x)+\kappa\widetilde b_1(x)+\widetilde c(x)\bigr)\ge e^{\kappa a_1}>0.$$ Choose $M>e^{\kappa b_1}$ and set $\Phi=M-\psi$. Then $\Phi>0$ on $\bar U$ and $L[\Phi]=M\widetilde c-L[\psi]\le-e^{\kappa a_1}$. [given, F1, algebra, choose]

2.1 Put $R=\|L[w]\|_{C^0(U)}$, $Q=\|w\|_{C^0(\partial U)}$, and $\eta=e^{\kappa a_1}$. For $z=w-Q-(R/\eta)\Phi$, the boundary values satisfy $z\le0$. In $U$, $$L[z]=L[w]-Q\widetilde c-(R/\eta)L[\Phi]\ge -R+R=0,$$ because $-Q\widetilde c\ge0$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $z(x_0)>0$ at some $x_0\in U$. Since $z\le0$ on $\partial U$ and $z$ is continuous on $\bar U$, its positive maximum is attained at an interior point. For sufficiently small $\delta>0$, $z+\delta\psi$ has an interior positive maximum as well: its value at $x_0$ is at least $z(x_0)>0$, while its boundary values are at most $\delta e^{\kappa b_1}<z(x_0)$. At that interior maximum [F2] and $\widetilde c\le0$ give $L[z+\delta\psi]\le0$, contradicting $L[z+\delta\psi]>0$. Hence $z\le0$ on $U$, and therefore $$w\le Q+(R/\eta)\|\Phi\|_{C^0(\bar U)}.$$ Applying the same argument to $-w$ yields the corresponding lower bound. Taking $C_L=\max\{1,\|\Phi\|_{C^0(\bar U)}/\eta\}$ proves the first estimate; applying it to $u^*-h$ proves the second. [step 2.1, F2, algebra] ∎


===== lem-shin-interior-parabolic-schauder-estimate =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $0<\alpha<1$, and let $O\subset\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R$ be open. Use $\rho((x,t),(y,s))=\max\{|x-y|,\sqrt{|t-s|}\}$ and $[v]_{\alpha;E}=\sup_{z\ne z'\in E}|v(z)-v(z')|/\rho(z,z')^\alpha$. Suppose the symmetric matrix $A$, vector $\beta$ and scalar $q$ are locally $\alpha$-Hölder for this metric, and $\lambda I\le A\le\Lambda I$ with $0<\lambda\le\Lambda<\infty$. For every compact parabolic cylinder $K$ contained in the interior of a larger compact cylinder $K'\subset O$ there is $C<\infty$, depending on these cylinders, $d,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda$ and the coefficient Hölder norms on $K'$, such that every smooth $u$ satisfying $u_t-A:D^2u-\beta\cdot Du-qu=F$ near $K'$ obeys
$$\|u\|_{C^{2+\alpha,1+\alpha/2}(K)}\le C\bigl(\|u\|_{L^\infty(K')}+\|F\|_{C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(K')}\bigr).$$
Here the left norm consists of the suprema and parabolic $\alpha$-Hölder seminorms of $u,Du,D^2u,u_t$. The constant does not depend on derivatives of the coefficients above their displayed Hölder regularity. The assertion is an estimate for smooth solutions, not a prior assumption of classical solvability.

## Statement references

[Shin et al., §2.1 and Appendix F: parabolic regularity setting](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, the cylinders, coefficients, smooth solution and source in the Statement.

[A1] Countable Choice is inherited from the Gaussian integration and distribution suppliers ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The causal heat kernel has unit spatial mass, parabolic scaling, Gaussian derivative bounds and distributional equation $(\partial_t-\Delta)\Gamma=\delta$ ([[lem-heat-kernel-normalisation-scaling-and-derivatives]], [[thm-heat-kernel-is-the-causal-fundamental-solution]]).

[F2] A smooth cutoff equal to one near a compact set and supported inside a prescribed open neighborhood exists ([[lem-euclidean-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]]).

## Proof

1.1 Fix a constant symmetric matrix $A_0$ with eigenvalues in $[\lambda,\Lambda]$. The linear substitution $x=A_0^{1/2}y$ in [F1] gives $$G(x,t)=\mathbf1_{t>0}(4\pi t)^{-d/2}(\det A_0)^{-1/2}\exp\bigl(-x^TA_0^{-1}x/(4t)\bigr),\qquad (\partial_t-A_0:D^2)G=\delta.$$ Put $N=d+2$ and $K_{ij}=D_{ij}G$. Direct differentiation gives $|K_{ij}(z)|\le C\rho(z,0)^{-N}$, $|D_xK_{ij}(z)|\le C\rho(z,0)^{-N-1}$ and $|\partial_tK_{ij}(z)|\le C\rho(z,0)^{-N-2}$ away from zero, with Gaussian decay as $|x|/\sqrt t$ increases. The extension across $t=0$ is smooth away from $x=0$, since that decay dominates every power. The constants are uniform over $A_0$ in the given ellipticity interval. [A1, F1, given, algebra]

1.2 For use with cutoffs, the following interpolation estimate holds on nested cylinders, with norms scaled to their outer radius $r$: for each $\varepsilon>0$, the scaled lower-order $\alpha$ norms of $u$ and $rDu$ are at most $\varepsilon r^2(\|D^2u\|_{\alpha;r}+\|u_t\|_{\alpha;r})+C_\varepsilon\|u\|_\infty$, provided the finite difference segments lie in the larger cylinder. To verify it, write $D_iu(x,t)$ as a centered first difference of step $\theta r$ plus its Taylor integral remainder, bounded by $C\theta r\|D^2u\|_\infty$. Apply the same identity to differences between two points. For separations smaller than $\theta r$ use the top Hölder norms, and for larger separations use the supremum estimate and divide by the separation to the power $\alpha$. For time differences of $u$ use $|t-s|\|u_t\|_\infty$. For time differences of $Du$ use spatial first differences of step $\sqrt{|t-s|}$, bounding their time difference by $C\sqrt{|t-s|}\|u_t\|_\infty$ and the two remainders by $C\sqrt{|t-s|}\|D^2u\|_\infty$; then split at $|t-s|=\theta^2r^2$. The coefficients of the top norms are positive powers of $\theta$ because $\alpha<1$. Choosing $\theta$ small makes them at most $\varepsilon$; the larger-separation terms have finite constants depending on that $\theta$ and the cylinder separation. [given, algebra, choose]

2.1 Each positive-time spatial integral of $K_{ij}$ is zero. Its improper integrals on parabolic balls have a bound independent of the radius. Indeed integration of $D_{ij}G$ over $|x|<R$ gives a boundary integral of $D_jG$; its absolute value is at most $CR^dt^{-d/2-1}\exp(-cR^2/t)$. Integration for $0<t<R^2$, followed by $t=R^2s$, is bounded by $C\int_0^1s^{-d/2-1}e^{-c/s}ds<\infty$. The integral is independent of $R$ by scaling. Differences of two such truncations, hence the integrals on centered parabolic annuli, are bounded uniformly as well. This is the cancellation needed at the singularity; an absolute integral of $|K_{ij}|$ there is not asserted. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $v\in C_c^\infty$ have support in a parabolic cylinder of radius at most $r$ and put $F_0=v_t-A_0:D^2v$. Integrating the derivative of the Gaussian convolution from a time before the support gives $v=G*F_0$: the initial convolution is zero, its derivative is convolution with $F_0$, and the positive-time approximate identity gives the upper endpoint. This uses only [F1] and differentiation of smooth compactly supported functions. Differentiation of this identity expresses $D_{ij}v$ as the principal-value convolution with $K_{ij}$, with a fixed bounded constant multiple of $F_0$ if the truncation is by parabolic balls rather than positive time strips. That constant is the radius-independent truncation integral in step 2.1. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The singular-integral estimates follow directly as follows. At a pole subtract $F_0$ evaluated at that pole. On an annulus of radius $s$, the difference is bounded by $[F_0]_\alpha s^\alpha$, and its absolute integral against $K_{ij}$ is at most $C[F_0]_\alpha s^\alpha$. For two poles at parabolic distance $h$, the parts within distance $4h$ of either pole are therefore bounded by $C[F_0]_\alpha\int_0^{4h}s^{\alpha-1}ds\le C[F_0]_\alpha h^\alpha$. On the complement use the same subtracted constant at both poles. The kernel difference is at most $Chs^{-N-1}$: spatial displacement follows from $D_xK_{ij}$, and time displacement, of size at most $h^2$, from $\partial_tK_{ij}$ and $h^2s^{-N-2}\le hs^{-N-1}$. Its integral is bounded by $C[F_0]_\alpha h\int_{4h}^\infty s^{\alpha-2}ds\le C[F_0]_\alpha h^\alpha$. Changing the near-region truncation produces annular pieces controlled by these same estimates; changing the subtracted constant produces at most $C|F_0(z)-F_0(z')|$ by step 2.1. Thus $[D^2v]_\alpha\le C[F_0]_\alpha$. On the support neighborhood, integrating only up to radius comparable with $r$ and using the bounded truncation term gives $\|D^2v\|_\infty\le C(\|F_0\|_\infty+r^\alpha[F_0]_\alpha)$; outside that neighborhood the kernel is nonsingular and its Gaussian bound gives the same estimate. The compact time support may be used to truncate all time integrals before subtraction, so no absolutely divergent integral at infinite time is introduced. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Since $v_t=F_0+A_0:D^2v$, the same bounds hold for $v_t$. In the scaled notation $\|g\|_{\alpha;r}=\|g\|_\infty+r^\alpha[g]_\alpha$, the result is $$r^2\bigl(\|D^2v\|_{\alpha;r}+\|v_t\|_{\alpha;r}\bigr)\le Cr^2\|v_t-A_0:D^2v\|_{\alpha;r}.$$ All constants depend only on $d,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda$ and the fixed support-radius multiple. The strict inequality $\alpha<1$ is used in the convergent far integral of step 4.1. [step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Choose a cutoff $\eta$ supported in a cylinder of radius $r$ near $z_0$, and put $v=\eta u$, extended by zero. Freeze $A_0=A(z_0)$. Leibniz's formula gives $$v_t-A_0:D^2v=\eta F+(A-A_0):D^2v+\beta\cdot Dv+qv+u(\eta_t-A:D^2\eta-\beta\cdot D\eta)-2(A D\eta)\cdot Du.$$ The scaled product norm of $(A-A_0):D^2v$ is bounded by $Cr^\alpha[A]_\alpha\|D^2v\|_{\alpha;r}$. Choose $r$ so that this coefficient, multiplied by the constant in step 5.1, is at most $1/4$, and absorb it there. The remaining drift, zero-order and cutoff terms involve only $u,Du$ and $F$; step 1.2 absorbs their top-norm contributions after choosing its $\varepsilon$ sufficiently small. Cutoffs with derivatives bounded by powers of the inverse gap between inner and outer cylinders are supplied by [F2] after rescaling. [F2, step 5.1, step 1.2, given, construct]

7.1 The resulting nested-cylinder inequality has the form $M(s)\le\varepsilon M(S)+C_\varepsilon(S-s)^{-m}B$, where $M$ is the full scaled top norm, $m<\infty$ is fixed and $B=\|u\|_\infty+\|F\|_\alpha$. To remove the outer top norm, choose radii $s_j=s_0+a(1-2^{-j})$ whose limiting cylinder is strictly inside the domain, and take $\varepsilon<2^{-m-1}$. Iteration gives a convergent geometric sum of the $B$ terms. The remainder $\varepsilon^jM(s_j)$ tends to zero because $u$ is smooth on the fixed larger compact cylinder. Thus $M(s_0)\le CB$. A finite cover of $K$ by the small cylinders in step 6.1, and step 1.2 for the lower derivatives, gives the stated estimate. The choices used coefficient Hölder bounds and ellipticity only, proving the asserted independence from higher coefficient derivatives. [step 1.2, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
