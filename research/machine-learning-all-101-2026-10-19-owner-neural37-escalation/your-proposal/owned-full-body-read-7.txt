
===== lem-shin-zero-data-parabolic-classical-energy-identification =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice. Let $U\subset\mathbb R^d$ be bounded and open with an exterior tangent ball at every boundary point, and let $T>0$. Consider the intended divergence operator
$$L_tu=\sum_{i,j}D_i(a_{ij}D_ju)+\sum_iD_i(b_iu)+\sum_i c_iD_iu+du.$$
Assume $a=a^T\ge\lambda_0I$, $\lambda_0>0$, and $a,b,c,d$ bounded; the spatial derivatives of $a,b$ agree with distributional derivatives locally, and the expansion
$$\beta_j=\sum_iD_ia_{ij}+b_j+c_j,\qquad q=\sum_iD_ib_i+d$$
is locally valid with bounded $a,\beta,q$, locally parabolic $\alpha$-Hölder up to the final face, $0<\alpha<1$, and $q\le0$. Let $r$ be bounded and locally parabolic $\alpha$-Hölder up to that face. If $e$ is locally classical, bounded, continuous on the closed cylinder, zero on the initial and lateral faces, and satisfies $-e_t+L_te=r$, then
$$e\in L^2(0,T;H^1_0(U)),\qquad e_t\in L^2(0,T;H^{-1}(U)),\qquad e(0)=0\text{ in }L^2(U).$$
It is the unique zero-initial variational solution of that equation, and for a finite $C_E$ depending only on $T,\lambda_0$ and the divergence coefficient bounds,
$$\sup_{0\le t\le T}\|e(t)\|_2^2+\int_0^T\|De(t)\|_2^2dt\le C_E\|r\|_{L^2(U\times(0,T))}^2.$$
Consequently a sequence of such errors with $\|e_m\|_\infty\to0$ and $\|r_m\|_\infty\to0$ converges strongly to zero in $L^2(0,T;H^1(U))$. No closed-cylinder derivative regularity is assumed.

## Statement references

[Shin et al., Appendices F–G: parabolic consistency and energy estimates](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01806)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The choice assumptions, domain, coefficients, distributionally compatible expansion, source and classical zero-data error in the Statement.

[A1] Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice are retained from the approximation, truncation and Sobolev suppliers ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The bounded-open-set Sobolev triple is separable; $H^1_0(U)$ is the $H^1$-closure of compactly supported smooth functions ([[lem-shin-bounded-open-set-hilbert-evolution-triple]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]).

[F2] Smooth weak approximants on exhausting polyhedral domains have uniform compact-interior classical estimates and converge to the unique bounded classical solution with the prescribed continuous data; compatible divergence-coefficient convolutions are permitted ([[lem-shin-parabolic-classical-approximation-and-boundary-attainment]]).

[F3] Evolution-space solutions have continuous $L^2$ traces and satisfy the time energy-pairing identity ([[lem-parabolic-energy-pairing-and-trace]]).

[F4] A bounded sequence in a separable Hilbert space has a weakly convergent subsequence, and the corresponding Bochner spaces are separable Hilbert spaces ([[lem-parabolic-weak-compactness-separable-bochner-hilbert]]).

[F5] Every $L^1$ approximate identity converges strongly to the identity in finite $L^p$, in particular $p=2$; the kernel may be anisotropic provided its mass, uniformly bounded $L^1$ norm and shrinking-tail conditions hold ([[thm-l-one-approximate-identities-converge-in-l-p]]).

## Proof

1.1 Use the domains $U_n$ and divergence-compatible smooth convolution approximants from [F2], with zero initial and lateral data and source $r_n$. Their spatial mollifier support stays inside $U$ at every point of $U_n$; their temporal mollifier is supported at positive past lags, sampling $t-s$ with $s>0$. Thus $a_n$ retains ellipticity and its bound, $b_n,c_n,d_n$ retain their separate bounds, and the expanded $\beta_n,q_n$ are the convolutions of the bounded original expanded coefficients, retaining their bounds and $q_n\le0$. No endpoint limit of the original $b,c,d$ at $T$ is used. Each fixed smooth approximant may be extended locally past $T$ for its construction; compactness is used only at times below $T$. The resulting weak solutions $e_n$ are smooth in the interior, uniformly bounded, and have a locally classical subsequential limit with zero continuous initial/lateral data at every time below $T$ by [F2]. Classical comparison on cylinders ending below $T$ identifies the limit with $e$. [A1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Test the weak equation $e_{n,t}-L_ne_n=-r_n$ with $e_n$. The divergence terms have no boundary contribution because $e_n\in H^1_0(U_n)$ in time-integrated sense. In particular the lower-order spatial form is $$\int_{U_n}a_nDe_n\cdot De_n+\int_{U_n}(b_n-c_n)\cdot De_n\,e_n-\int_{U_n}d_ne_n^2.$$ The cross term is $b_n-c_n$, since the $b$ term is integrated by parts and the $c$ term is not. Write $B=\|b\|_\infty+\|c\|_\infty$ and $D=\|d\|_\infty$. With $y_n=\|e_n\|_2^2$, [F3], ellipticity and Young's inequality give $$\frac12y_n'+\frac{\lambda_0}{2}\|De_n\|_2^2\le\left(\frac{B^2}{2\lambda_0}+D+\frac12\right)y_n+\frac12\|r_n\|_2^2.$$ The constants are uniform in $n$; derivatives of the smoothed coefficients do not occur. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $C_0=B^2/\lambda_0+2D+1$. The previous inequality gives $y_n'+\lambda_0\|De_n\|_2^2\le C_0y_n+\|r_n\|_2^2$. Multiply by $e^{-C_0t}$ and integrate, using $y_n(0)=0$. Then $\sup_ty_n\le e^{C_0T}\|r_n\|_{L^2}^2$. Integrating the same differential inequality without the factor bounds $\lambda_0\int\|De_n\|_2^2$ by $C_0T\sup y_n+\|r_n\|_{L^2}^2$. The boundedness of $r$ and the finite cylinder give a uniform right side. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Extend each $e_n$ by zero from $U_n$ to $U$. For almost every time this lies in $H^1_0(U)$: extend its compactly supported smooth $H^1(U_n)$ approximants by zero, which preserves their norms, and use the closure definition in [F1]. The bounds in step 3.1 therefore give a bounded sequence in $L^2(0,T;H^1_0(U))$. Apply [F4] and pass to a weakly convergent subsequence. The local uniform convergence from step 1.1 identifies this weak limit with $e$ almost everywhere, by testing on compact interior cylinders. Such cylinders exhaust $U\times(0,T)$; a locally zero $L^2$ function is zero almost everywhere on their union. Hence $e\in L^2(0,T;H^1_0(U))$. [A1, F1, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Fix a compact positive-time cylinder $K\Subset U\times(0,T)$ and a smooth spacetime cutoff $\chi$ equal to one near $K$. Extend each of $\chi b,\chi c,\chi d$ by zero to $\mathbb R^{d+1}$; these bounded compactly supported functions belong to $L^2$. The nonnegative product convolution kernels used in step 1.1 have mass one, $L^1$ norm one, and support shrinking to zero in both space and time (with positive past time lag). Thus they satisfy the approximate-identity conditions, although the spatial and temporal scales differ. Apply [F5] with $p=2$ to these extensions. For large $n$ their convolutions agree on $K$ with $b_n,c_n,d_n$, respectively, so $b_n\to b$, $c_n\to c$ and $d_n\to d$ strongly in $L^2(K)$, with their common uniform supremum bounds. Only $a_n,\beta_n,q_n,r_n$ are asserted to converge uniformly on such compact cylinders, by their local Hölder regularity. For a smooth test $\phi$ supported in $K$, the $a_nDe_n\cdot D\phi$ term passes by uniform coefficient convergence and weak $L^2$ gradient convergence. In the term $c_n\cdot De_n\,\phi$, the test vector $c_n\phi$ converges strongly in $L^2(K)$, so its pairing with the bounded weakly convergent gradients tends to $\int c\cdot De\,\phi$. In the $b_n e_n\cdot D\phi$ and $d_ne_n\phi$ terms, strong local coefficient convergence and local uniform convergence of $e_n$ imply strong $L^2$ convergence of the products. The source and time-test terms pass by local uniform convergence. These arguments prove the limiting weak identity on every compact positive-time cylinder without extra spatial $C^1$ assumptions on $b,c,d$. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For a smooth spacetime test with compact spatial support in $U$ but not necessarily vanishing at initial time, first perform the passage above on $[\delta,T]$, taking its support strictly below $T$ when necessary. The common bounded coefficient and source norms, supremum bounds and energy bounds control the omitted interval $(0,\delta)$ uniformly: terms linear in gradients are at most a fixed constant times $\sqrt\delta\|De_n\|_{L^2}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, and the zeroth-order, source and time-test terms are at most a constant times $\delta$. The same bounds hold for the limit by step 4.1. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ therefore gives the identity with zero initial boundary term, inherited from the approximants. Density of spatial compact-smooth functions in $H^1_0(U)$ extends the positive-time identity to all variational tests, since the divergence coefficients are bounded and the limit has the membership established in step 4.1. It follows that $e_t=L_te-r\in L^2(0,T;H^{-1}(U))$. [F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 By [F3], this evolution-space function has a continuous $L^2$ trace. Its integration-by-parts identity compared with the weak identity in step 6.1, for tests $\eta(t)\phi(x)$ with $\eta(0)\ne0$ and $\phi\in C_c^\infty(U)$, gives $(e(0),\phi)_{L^2}=0$. Density in $L^2(U)$ gives $e(0)=0$. Thus $e$ is a zero-initial variational solution as claimed. If two such weak solutions exist, their difference has zero source and zero initial value. The same test and integrating-factor argument as steps 2.1–3.1 on $U$ gives zero $L^2$ norm at every time, proving weak uniqueness. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Apply [F3] to the now identified weak solution $e$, repeating step 2.1 on $U$ with its actual source $r$. The integrating-factor and integrated gradient arguments in step 3.1 yield the stated estimate with, for example, $C_E=e^{C_0T}+\lambda_0^{-1}(C_0Te^{C_0T}+1)$. The exact value is immaterial, but its dependence is only on the declared quantities, not on boundary derivatives of $e$. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For the final convergence assertion, $\|r_m\|_{L^2}^2\le T|U|\|r_m\|_\infty^2\to0$. The estimate in step 8.1 gives $\int\|De_m\|_2^2\to0$, and either that estimate or $\int\|e_m\|_2^2\le T|U|\|e_m\|_\infty^2$ gives the zeroth-order term. Their sum is the squared $L^2(0,T;H^1(U))$ norm. This proves strong convergence and completes the result. [step 8.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The expansion assumption is deliberately distributional as well as pointwise. Local $C^1$ spatial coefficients imply it. The source's word “differentiable” should not be used to conceal an unproved weak integration-by-parts identity for pathological pointwise derivatives. This helper imposes no extra boundary smoothness and no $C^{2,1}$ regularity on the closed cylinder.

The intended operator has a separately indexed $\sum_iD_i(b_iu)$ term. The source's printed $\sum_{i,j}D_i(a_{ij}D_ju+b_iu)$ repeats that term $d$ times and is incompatible with its own expanded drift and potential; it is not the operator used in this helper.

===== lem-smooth-integral-functionals-approximate-on-compact-function-sets =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain, and let $X=L^p(D)$, $X=W^{m,p}(D)$, or $X=C^m(\bar D)$, where $1\le p<\infty$ and $m\ge0$ wherever applicable. For every bounded linear functional $L:X\to\mathbb R$, every compact $K\subset X$, and every $\varepsilon>0$, there is $\kappa\in C_c^\infty(D)$ such that
$$\sup_{a\in K}\left|L(a)-\int_D\kappa(y)a(y)\,dy\right|<\varepsilon.$$

The input function in this integral is its zeroth derivative. The conclusion is compact-uniform, without an assertion of operator-norm density of these functionals.

## Statement references

[Kovachki et al., Appendix C, Lemmas 28–31; the present compact-set proof uses inward smoothing](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08481)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $D,X,L,K,\varepsilon$ as in the Statement.

[F1] The inward smoothing construction supplies $S_ta(x)=\int_Dk_t(x,y)a(y)\,dy$, where $k_t$ is ambient smooth, has fixed compact support in $x$, and $S_ta\to a$ uniformly on each compact subset of $X$. Its explicit kernel is $k_t(x,y)=\sum_j\psi_j(x)\rho_t(x+tv_j-y)$ ([[lem-inward-mollification-and-finite-rank-approximation-on-lipschitz-domains]]).

[F2] All listed $X$ embed continuously into $L^1(D)$; this follows directly from Hölder's inequality on the bounded domain, or the sup norm in the continuous case. Countable Choice is used by the local smoothing supplier ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F3] The finite normalized-bump construction gives an ambient smooth cutoff equal to one near any compact subset of an open set, taking values between zero and one and compactly supported in that open set ([[lem-finite-ambient-partitions-for-euclidean-boundary-integration]]).

## Proof

1.1 Empty $K$ or $L=0$ is handled by $\kappa=0$. Otherwise [F1] permits choosing $t>0$ with $\sup_K\|S_ta-a\|_X<\varepsilon/(2\|L\|)$. For fixed $t$, the map $y\mapsto k_t(\cdot,y)|_D$ is ambient $C^\infty$ with values in $X$: every mixed $x$ and $y$ derivative is uniformly continuous on compact products, so the ordinary finite-dimensional Taylor remainders in $y$, uniformly for $x$ derivatives through order $m$, tend to zero in the $C^m$ norm and hence in the $X$ norm. Thus $\kappa_t(y)=L(k_t(\cdot,y)|_D)$ is ambient smooth, with $D_y^\alpha\kappa_t=L(D_y^\alpha k_t(\cdot,y)|_D)$. It is bounded on $\bar D$. [given, F1, F2, choose, construct]

1.2 Compactness of $K$ in $X$ implies its compactness in $L^1(D)$ by [F2]. Given $\theta>0$, take a finite $\theta/2$ net $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ in that $L^1$ image. By integrability and an exhaustion of $D$ by compact subsets, there is one compact $F\subset D$ with $\int_{D\setminus F}|a_i|<\theta/2$ for all $i$. Therefore $\sup_{a\in K}\int_{D\setminus F}|a|<\theta$. Choose a smooth cutoff $0\le\zeta\le1$, compactly supported in $D$, equal to one on $F$; use [F3] for the one-set open cover $\{D\}$ of $F$. Then $\sup_K\int_D(1-\zeta)|a|<\theta$. This uses a finite net and finitely many integrable tails, including at $p=1$; it requires no $L^\infty$ approximation of a dual coefficient. [F2, F3, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 For each $a\in X$, one has $L(S_ta)=\int_D\kappa_t(y)a(y)\,dy$. To justify the identity without assuming a vector-integration theorem, partition a box containing $D$ into cells of diameter tending to zero and replace $k_t(\cdot,y)$ on each cell by $k_t(\cdot,y_l)$. Uniform continuity of every $x$ derivative through order $m$ makes the error in $X$ at most $c_X\eta\|a\|_1$, where $\eta\to0$ is the uniform kernel derivative error and $c_X$ converts a $C^m$ bound to an $X$ bound. Applying bounded $L$ to the finite sums is legitimate by linearity. Their scalar integral errors are at most $\|L\|c_X\eta\|a\|_1$ as well, so passing to the limit proves the identity. Consequently $\sup_K|L(a)-\int_D\kappa_ta|<\varepsilon/2$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $\|\kappa_t\|_{L^\infty(D)}=0$, choose $\kappa=0$. Otherwise use step 1.2 with $\theta=\varepsilon/(2\|\kappa_t\|_\infty)$ and set $\kappa=\zeta\kappa_t\in C_c^\infty(D)$. The cutoff error satisfies $\sup_K|\int_D(\kappa_t-\kappa)a|\le\|\kappa_t\|_\infty\sup_K\int_D(1-\zeta)|a|<\varepsilon/2$. Combining with step 2.1 proves the claim. [step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra] ∎ 
===== thm-neural-operator-compact-uniform-universality =====


## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $D\subset\mathbb R^d$ and $D'\subset\mathbb R^{d'}$ be bounded Lipschitz domains. Let $A$ be $L^{p_1}(D)$, $W^{m_1,p_1}(D)$, or $C(\bar D)$, with $1\le p_1<\infty$ and $m_1\ge1$ where applicable. Let $U$ be $L^{p_2}(D')$, $W^{m_2,p_2}(D')$, or $C^{m_2}(\bar D')$, with $1\le p_2<\infty$, with $m_2=0$ in the Lebesgue case, $m_2\ge1$ in the Sobolev case, and $m_2\ge0$ in the continuous case. Let $G^\dagger:A\to U$ be continuous, $K\subset A$ compact, and $\varepsilon>0$. Suppose $\sigma_1\in\mathcal A_0^L$, $\sigma_2\in\mathcal A_0$, and $\sigma_3\in\mathcal A_{m_2}$ in every finite input dimension used below. Then some finite-layer neural operator $G$ in the specified integral architecture satisfies
$$\sup_{a\in K}\|G^\dagger(a)-G(a)\|_U<\varepsilon.$$

The printed additional source implication asserts that, if $U$ is Hilbert, $M>0$, $\|G^\dagger(a)\|_U\le M$ on all $A$, and $\sigma_1\in\mathrm{BA}$, then $\|G(a)\|_U\le4M$ on all $A$. This exact implication is vacuous because its diameter-bounded class is empty. A useful corrected implication replaces only that additional activation premise by the following explicitly different property: for every compact $S\subset\mathbb R^q$, every $C\ge\sup_{z\in S}|z|_2$ with the convention that the radius of the empty set is zero, and every $\eta>0$, there is a $\sigma_1$ MLP $b:\mathbb R^q\to\mathbb R^q$ with $\sup_S|b(z)-z|_2<\eta$ and $\sup_{\mathbb R^q}|b(z)|_2\le C$. Under this replacement the same global bound $4M$ holds. This replacement does not change the printed diameter-based definition.

## Statement references

[Kovachki et al., Theorem 11, Definitions 5–7 and Appendices A, C, D, F](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.08481)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The spaces, compact set, target, activation hypotheses and error in the Statement.

[F1] The activation hypotheses supply fixed-depth finite MLP density in the indicated finite-dimensional $C^m$ norms; stacking scalar networks of a common depth gives vector outputs. Their affine output layers allow linear combinations and constant functions ([[def-neural-operator-activation-classes]]).

[F2] The architecture uses affine integral maps on $D$, coordinatewise $\sigma_1$ between its intermediate affine maps, and a final kernel on $D'\times D$ integrated over $D$ ([[def-neural-operator]]).

[F3] Smooth restrictions are norm-dense in all listed output spaces, including $C^m(\bar D')$, by the constructive smooth-range approximation in [[lem-inward-mollification-and-finite-rank-approximation-on-lipschitz-domains]]. Thus the targets subsequently approximated using the $\mathcal A_m$ premise can be chosen ambient smooth; no extension of an arbitrary $C^m(\bar D')$ function is assumed.

[F4] Under Countable Choice, if $a,b\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(D)$ are distinct modulo null functions then some real $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(D)$ has $\int_D\varphi(a-b)\ne0$, by injectivity of regular distributions ([[thm-locally-integrable-functions-embed-in-distributions]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F6] ReLU networks are compact-uniformly dense for continuous finite-dimensional targets under Countable Choice ([[thm-single-hidden-layer-universal-approximation]]).

[F5] The source's diameter-bounded class is empty ([[lem-neural-operator-ba-class-is-empty-as-defined]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $K$ is empty choose the zero operator. Otherwise put $T=G^\dagger|_K$ and choose $0<\tau<\varepsilon/5$. Every listed input space injects continuously into $L^1(D)$: for finite $p$, $\|a\|_1\le |D|^{1-1/p}\|a\|_p\le |D|^{1-1/p}\|a\|_{W^{m,p}}$ when applicable, and for $C(\bar D)$ use $\|a\|_1\le|D|\|a\|_\infty$. Thus each smooth moment $a\mapsto\int_D\varphi a$ is continuous on $A$ and such moments separate distinct members of $K$, using [F4]; distinct continuous functions also differ on an interior set of positive measure. [given, F4, choose, algebra]

1.2 The corrected property has concrete instances. For ReLU and a compact $S\subset\mathbb R^q$, suppose $C\ge\sup_S|z|_2$. If $S$ is empty choose zero. If $C=0$, then $S\subset\{0\}$ and again choose zero. If $C>0$, the coordinate clamp $c_j(z)=-C+\operatorname{ReLU}(z_j+C)-\operatorname{ReLU}(z_j-C)$ is an exact ReLU network onto $[-C,C]^q$ and fixes $S$. The radial projection $P$ onto the Euclidean ball of radius $C$ is continuous and fixes $S$. By [F6] approximate $P$ on the cube by a ReLU MLP $p$ with Euclidean error $<\xi$, where $0<2\xi<\eta$. Then $b(z)=Cp(c(z))/(C+\xi)$ is a finite ReLU MLP, globally bounded by $C$, and for $z\in S$ its error is less than $\xi+\xi C/(C+\xi)<2\xi<\eta$. Composition of layered MLPs is a layered MLP because consecutive affine maps merge. ReLU also has linear growth and belongs to every relevant $\mathcal A_0$ by [F6]. Thus this corrected branch is nonvacuous. [F1, F6, construct, algebra]

2.1 The set $Q=\{(a,b)\in K^2:\|T(a)-T(b)\|_U\ge\tau\}$ is compact. For each pair in $Q$ a separating moment from step 1.1 remains nonzero on a neighborhood of that pair. A finite subcover supplies moments $\varphi_1,\ldots,\varphi_J$ such that $E(a)=(\int_D\varphi_ja)_{j=1}^J$ has $E(a)\ne E(b)$ for all $(a,b)\in Q$. If $Q$ is nonempty, the positive continuous function $|E(a)-E(b)|_2$ on $Q$ has a positive minimum $r$; if $Q$ is empty set $J=1$, $\varphi_1=0$, and $r=1$. In both cases $|E(a)-E(b)|_2<r$ implies $\|T(a)-T(b)\|_U<\tau$. This is the complete finite-moment reduction and does not presume a dual representation theorem. [step 1.1, construct, choose]

3.1 Cover the compact $E(K)$ by finitely many open balls of radius $r/4$ with centers $z_i=E(a_i)$, $a_i\in K$, $1\le i\le s$. Put $h_i(z)=\max(0,r/2-|z-z_i|_2)$, $h_0(z)=\operatorname{dist}(z,E(K))$, and $w_i(z)=h_i(z)/(h_0(z)+\sum_{k=1}^s h_k(z))$. The denominator is positive everywhere: off $E(K)$ its first term is positive, and on $E(K)$ some $h_i>r/4$. Consequently $w=(w_1,\ldots,w_s)$ is continuous on all $\mathbb R^J$, nonnegative, and $\sum_iw_i\le1$, with equality on $E(K)$. For $a\in K$, $w_i(E(a))>0$ implies $|E(a)-E(a_i)|_2<r/2$ and hence $\|T(a)-T(a_i)\|_U<\tau$. The convex combination $\sum_iw_i(E(a))T(a_i)$ therefore differs from $T(a)$ by less than $\tau$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For every listed output space choose smooth neighborhood restrictions $\phi_i$ with $\|\phi_i-T(a_i)\|_U<\tau$, using [F3]. Define $Bv=\sum_i v_i\phi_i$ and $L=\max(1,\|B\|_{\ell^1\to U})$. Since the weights in step 3.1 sum to one on $E(K)$, $\sup_K\|T(a)-Bw(E(a))\|_U<2\tau$. This is the output finite-dimensional factorization. [step 3.1, F3, choose, construct, algebra]

5.1 Let $Z=\{z:\operatorname{dist}(z,E(K))\le1\}$, a compact subset of $\mathbb R^J$. Uniform continuity of $w$ on $Z$ gives $0<\delta<1$ such that $z,z'\in Z$ and $|z-z'|_2<\delta$ imply $|w(z)-w(z')|_1<\tau/L$. Set $R=\max(1,\sup_{a\in K}\|a\|_1)$. By the $\sigma_2$ premise [F1] choose common-depth scalar networks $p_j$ with $\sup_{\bar D}|p_j-\varphi_j|<\delta/(2R\sqrt J)$. Then $\widetilde E(a)=(\int_Dp_j(y)a(y)\,dy)_j$ satisfies $\sup_K|\widetilde E-E|_2<\delta/2$, so $\widetilde E(K)\subset Z$ and $\sup_K\|Bw(E(a))-Bw(\widetilde E(a))\|_U<\tau$. The input layer kernel $(x,y)\mapsto(p_j(y))_j$ ignores $x$ and has exactly constant spatial output. The estimate uses only $L^1$ bounds and covers $p_1=1$. [step 1.1, step 4.1, F1, choose, construct, algebra]

6.1 Apply the $\sigma_1\in\mathcal A_0$ premise to the continuous map $w$ on compact $\widetilde E(K)$, obtaining a finite MLP $f:\mathbb R^J\to\mathbb R^s$ such that $\sup_{z\in\widetilde E(K)}|f(z)-w(z)|_1<\tau/L$. Then $\sup_K\|Bw(\widetilde E(a))-Bf(\widetilde E(a))\|_U<\tau$. Only $C^0$ approximation of this middle map is used; no differentiability of $\sigma_1$ is needed. [step 5.1, F1, choose, algebra]

7.1 Put $V=\max(1,\sup_{a\in K}|f(\widetilde E(a))|_1)$, finite by continuity and compactness. Define $c_U=1$ for $U=C^{m_2}(\bar D')$ with the maximum derivative norm, and $c_U=(J_{m_2}|D'|)^{1/p_2}$ for $U=W^{m_2,p_2}(D')$ with the usual sum-of-$p_2$-powers norm, where $J_m$ counts multi-indices of order at most $m$; this also treats $L^{p_2}$ with $J_0=1$. For $C^{m_2}$ functions $u$, directly integrating each derivative gives $\|u\|_U\le c_U\|u\|_{C^{m_2}}$. By $\sigma_3\in\mathcal A_{m_2}$ choose common-depth networks $q_i$ such that $\|q_i-\phi_i\|_{C^{m_2}(\bar D')}<\tau/(c_UV)$. These approximants are chosen from the $C^{m_2}$ network subfamily supplied by that density premise, so their derivatives exist. With $\widetilde Bv=\sum_i v_iq_i$, the estimate $\|(\widetilde B-B)v\|_U\le c_U|v|_1\max_i\|q_i-\phi_i\|_{C^{m_2}}$ yields $\sup_K\|\widetilde Bf(\widetilde E(a))-Bf(\widetilde E(a))\|_U<\tau$. Equivalent choices of standard Banach norms merely change $c_U$. [step 4.1, step 6.1, F1, choose, construct, algebra]

8.1 Write the middle MLP as $f(z)=A_{n+1}\sigma_1(A_n\sigma_1(\cdots\sigma_1(A_1z+b_1)\cdots)+b_n)+b_{n+1}$ with $n$ hidden layers. On a spatially constant field $v(y)=z$, the affine map $z\mapsto Az+b$ is represented exactly by the integral kernel $A/|D|$ and constant bias $b$. Such constants are exact $\sigma_2$ networks through their output biases.
