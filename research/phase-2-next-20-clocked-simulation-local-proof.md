## 1. Integral buffers and exact order invariant

For each virtual tape retain its scanned symbol in a centre record. A record also has a permanent origin bit, initially1 precisely at the original cell0. Writing changes the tape symbol, never the bit. Treat the virtual tape as two ordered stacks extending outwards from the centre. The positive stack initially consists of input cells1,2,..., then blanks; the negative stack initially consists of blanks. The origin bit will prevent entering the negative part of the auxiliary two-sided representation.

Put h_i=2^i for i>=0. On each side, zone i has **capacity2h_i**, and contains0,h_i,or2h_i genuine records; the remaining slots contain a distinct hole code. Ordinary blank tape symbols count as genuine records. In each zone genuine records are packed nearest the centre, in their stack order. For each i the left and right occupancies sum to2h_i. Ignoring holes and concatenating zones0,1,... gives exactly the two stacks. Initially both occupancies are h_i. The centre is always genuine. Thus the smallest zone has capacity2 and half-occupancy1; no fractional base zone occurs.

Here is a move towards the positive stack after writing the old centre c. Choose the least i whose right zone is nonempty. Such an i exists because untouched zones are half-full. For j<i, right zone j is empty and left zone j is full. Extract the first h_i records of right zone i. Make the first extracted record the new centre, and distribute the remaining h_i-1 records among the right zones j<i with h_j records in zone j, preserving order. This is possible because sum_{j<i}h_j=h_i-1. Right zone i loses h_i records, so its occupancy changes from h_i or2h_i to0 or h_i.

Prepend c to the concatenation of the full left zones j<i. This list has1+2sum_{j<i}h_j=2h_i-1 records. Keep its first h_i-1 records in the lower left zones, h_j in each zone j<i, and prepend the remaining h_i records to left zone i. The previous left occupancy at i was2h_i minus the right occupancy, hence0 or h_i; the new occupancy is h_i or2h_i. All other zones are unchanged. Both order and complementary occupancy are preserved. All zones below i end half-full on both sides.

The negative-stack move is the same construction with left and right interchanged. At i=0 the lower lists are empty: one right record becomes the centre and the old centre is prepended to left zone0. These formulas still apply literally.

If M requests a left move while the centre has its origin bit set, perform only the requested symbol write and leave the buffers unchanged. Otherwise perform the appropriate stack move. Because records carry the origin bit unchanged, this realizes the clamped boundary at0 exactly. The auxiliary negative cells are never scanned. All k virtual writes/moves use the transition already selected from the original tuple of scanned symbols; processing their buffer operations successively does not mix simulated transitions.

## 2. Reset counting, including higher resets

Fix one virtual tape and i>=1. Let q_i be the total right occupancy in its zones0,...,i-1, and put H_i=sum_{j<i}h_j=2^i-1. Initially q_i=H_i. Every operation whose chosen level is at least i resets all these zones to half-full, so it again leaves q_i=H_i, **even when the operation's level is strictly greater than i**.

Between two such resets, each positive-stack move with level<i decreases q_i by exactly1: it removes one record to the centre and only redistributes the remaining lower records. Each negative-stack move with level<i increases q_i by exactly1. A clamped move or a write without a head move leaves it unchanged. This follows directly by counting the records on the two sides of the redistribution described above.

An operation can choose a level>=i only when all its lower source-side zones are empty. For a positive move this means q_i=0. For a negative move complementary occupancy gives q_i=2H_i. Starting from H_i, reaching either endpoint takes at least H_i prior lower-level moves, and the triggering operation is one more move. Consequently the first operation at level>=i, and each subsequent one after any such reset, requires at least H_i+1=2^i simulated transitions of this virtual tape. Intervening higher resets do not evade the charge: they are themselves members of this counted sequence and start a new interval at H_i.

Among at most b simulated transitions, the number N_i of operations whose chosen level is exactly i is therefore at most floor(b/2^i), for i>=1. At level0 it is at most b. No level i with2^i>b can be reached. A constant multiple of2^i work per level-i operation consequently totals at most

C_M b + C_M sum_{i=1}^{floor(log_2 b)} 2^i floor(b/2^i)
<= C_M b(1+floor(log_2 b))

for b>=1. The finite k virtual tapes change only the M-dependent constant. At b=0 no buffer operation is performed. This is a charging proof for all operations, not an assumption that intervening higher resets cannot occur.

## 3. Fixed finite-alphabet implementation and lazy initialization

A single interpreter must not literally acquire k physical tapes or the alphabet Gamma^k when M changes. Instead use a fixed binary alphabet with finitely many marker codes and a fixed number of physical work tapes. Decode the finite program into a saved table. For this fixed M, its k, symbol/state widths and table length are constants. A physical buffer slot is a delimited block containing k fixed-width symbol/origin-or-hole fields. Two physical tapes hold the left and right zone sequences; another holds centre fields, and a fixed collection holds the table, input archive, clock and scratch copies. This number is independent of M.

A zone has endpoint and midpoint markers and k occupancy flags from {empty,half,full}. Each genuine sublist is packed at its near end. To find the least source zone for a chosen virtual tape, scan the successive headers and occupancy flags. Up to level i, the total capacities on one side are sum_{j<=i}2h_j=2^{i+2}-2, so the scanned physical length is O_M(2^i). Header storage for binary level labels, if used, adds only O(sum_{j<=i}log(j+2))=O(2^i).

To redistribute, copy the affected genuine sublists of the chosen field to scratch tapes in stack order, retaining the other k-1 fields in place. Scan the affected zones again and write the specified new lists, holes and occupancy flags. Midpoints and endpoints delimit the exact half-capacities. Each extraction/copy/write/return is a linear scan of this prefix and a scratch list of at most a constant multiple of its length. A fixed number of scans suffices for the explicit list operations in section1. Fixed-width fields and table lookups cost O_M(1) per processed record. Hence one redistribution costs O_M(2^i), without a full-tape sweep or a search beyond level i.

For completeness, growing the allocated prefix needs no unit-cost arithmetic assumption. Zone0 has a literal fixed layout. A unary length marker for the most recent zone can be copied twice to make the next zone's layout, with its midpoint placed between copies; scratch heads scan each copy once and return. Updating that unary marker and writing the fields costs the next zone's O_M(2^i) length. Finite headers and fixed-width record routines are controlled by loops whose table/width parameters are saved in the finite program area. Reading a parameter or scanning the entire M-table costs a constant for fixed M. Thus this describes one finite interpreter, with constants depending on M, rather than a family of physical alphabets.

First validate the given program/input/clock syntax and create a sequential archive of the decoded input symbols in O_M(n+log(b+2)) time on valid inputs. The existing machine code fixes a one-tape syntax; the theorem's written multitape extension adds the tape count and lexicographic k-symbol transition tuples. This is an explicit new syntax extending, not changing the meaning of, the published one-tape encoding. For fixed M the table and symbol widths are bounded constants. Unary length delimiters can be copied and consumed by linear scans; each input block is checked against the fixed alphabet. Canonical binary clock validation is a single scan. On malformed headers use the already-written finite saturated count check and reject; the theorem asserts total rejection there, not a uniform malformed-input time bound.

Put input symbol0, or blank for empty input, in the first virtual centre. Other virtual centres are blank. All centres initially have their origin bit. Keep the archive head at input symbol1. **Do not distribute the entire input into buffers initially.** Unallocated zones are interpreted as their original half-full contents. On first reaching a new global highest level i, initialize its right half-lists using the next h_i symbols of the input archive for the first virtual tape, then blanks after archive exhaustion; other virtual tapes and the left half-lists receive blanks. Fill the other halves with hole fields.

Why is the sequential archive exact? An untouched right zone i initially represents positions2^i,...,2^{i+1}-1, and lower zones represent the earlier positions. Operations confined to lower levels do not change that untouched suffix. Allocations happen in increasing level order, so the archive is always at the start of the next untouched interval. If another virtual tape forces the allocation first, initialize every field of the new zone then; the same invariant holds for the first virtual tape. Reading beyond the input terminator simply generates blanks, without repeatedly scanning the input.

The first encounter with level i is part of a redistribution charged in section2. Its initialization also costs O_M(2^i). Thus a long input and a tiny b cost O_M(n) for the one input scan/archive, followed by work only on the O(b) lazily reached buffer prefix. There is no O(n log n) preprocessing term.

The convenient physical routines above allow stationary heads. To compile the fixed multitape interpreter into the left/right-only model, store each logical cell at physical position twice its index. Implement one logical step by two physical steps, storing the finite tuple of intended moves in finite control. A right move is R,R; a left move is L,L; a stationary move is R,L. Write the desired logical symbol at the even cell in the first step, and preserve the scanned symbol in the second. At the clamped left boundary, L,L stays at0. Intermediate odd cells have no logical interpretation and are ignored. The resulting heads are again at even cells, including stationary and clamped cases. The fixed number of physical heads means their pending move vector fits in finite control. Initial raw input can be copied into this spaced representation in linear time on a spare tape. Time changes by a constant factor plus input preparation; visited cells by a constant factor. No impossible mixed-parity schedule on unspaced tapes is assumed.

## 4. Clock, outcomes and resource separation

Copy b to a binary countdown. At each simulated configuration check the simulated state first: report its acceptance or rejection if halting. Otherwise reject if the countdown is zero; otherwise execute exactly one M transition, decrement, and repeat. A straightforward entire-clock scan costs O(log(b+2)) per simulated step, already within the required b log(b+2) allowance. This reports a halt on the bth transition and rejects only a still-nonhalting configuration at expiry. For b=0, the normalized initial state is nonhalting and rejects before any transition. For b=1, exactly one transition is allowed.

The total valid-input time is O_M(n+log(b+2)+b log(b+2))=O_M(n+b log(b+2)); the bounded b=0 term is absorbed by the nonempty encoded-input syntax. No O(S) bound for this interpreter is asserted. Use the distinct current-configuration interpreter for the linear visited-space claim, including its separate binary clock and decoder workspace. A supplied constructor still incurs its own declared time/workspace costs.
