## Canonical representation and Tits cone

Use finite rank for the geometry branch. Let `V = ⊕_{s∈S} R e_s`, `B(e_s,e_t) = −cos(π/m_st)` with `π/∞ = 0`. Its existence and uniqueness are extension from a basis; degeneracy is allowed. Expanding the formula proves each `r_s` is an involution preserving B. For finite `m_st`, B is positive definite on the two-generator plane, the product is a rotation of angle `2π/m_st`, and the B-orthogonal complement is fixed; this proves its order is exactly `m_st`. For infinity the product has a nontrivial unipotent action: `(r_s r_t)^n e_s = e_s + 2n(e_s+e_t)`. Relator verification plus the universal property of presentations supplies the homomorphism (Davis 6.12.3–6.12.5).

**Faithfulness is not relator verification.** Choose one complete route: (a) independent root-sign and length criterion, then a reduced nonidentity word sends an appropriate positive root negative; or (b) Davis D.1's half-space route, which needs Tits' combinatorial Lemma 4.8.3 proved earlier. Route (b) checks a precise rank-two half-space property, applies the earlier combinatorial lemma to show distinct open dual chambers disjoint, and concludes faithfulness. Do not use geometric faithfulness to establish exchange and then exchange to establish faithfulness without an independently proved bridge. The classical-source agent should own this upstream choice.

Define the dual action by `(w f)(v)=f(w^{-1}v)`, the chamber `C={f:f(e_s)≥0}`, `U=⋃wC`, spherical-face complement `C^f`, and `I=⋃wC^f`. Supply these contracts separately:

1. Closed-chamber collision: if `x,y∈C` and `wx=y`, then `x=y` and `w∈W_{S(x)}`. Prove by length descent/wall separation, including boundary points; open-chamber disjointness alone does not suffice.
2. Tits-cone convexity and finite segment face crossings: translate one endpoint into C, induct on the length of a chamber containing the other endpoint, and reflect the portion beyond the first exiting wall to reduce length (Davis D.2.7). Specify ties at intersections of walls and all boundary cases.
3. `I=int(U)` and `U(W,C^f)→I` a homeomorphism: prove local finite parabolic charts. The all-face basic-construction map `U(W,C)→U` is a bijection but generally **not** a homeomorphism (Davis D.2.6 remark).
4. Stabilizers are conjugates of `W_{S(x)}`; for finite rank the interior action is proper in the compact-set sense, through locally finite chamber charts. Finite point stabilizers alone do not imply properness for an arbitrary action.
5. Convexity makes I contractible by straight-line contraction. Averaging a finite orbit gives a fixed point, whose stabilizer yields containment of every finite subgroup in a spherical parabolic. This route supplies that consequence before CAT(0), with no fixed-point theorem dependency.

No residual-finiteness/virtual-torsion-free theorem should be added just from the phrase 'linear': those require separate commutative-ring specialization and Selberg arguments with characteristic-zero hypotheses.

## Finite, affine and hyperbolic distinctions

**Finite criterion.** Finite rank: W finite iff B positive definite (Davis 6.12.9). In the positive-definite direction, prove the canonical image discrete using disjoint open dual chambers, then use compactness of O(B). A subgroup of a compact group is not necessarily finite without discreteness. In the converse direction, average an inner product over finite W. Invariance under `r_s` makes `e_s` orthogonal to its fixed hyperplane, so the averaged inner product has row s proportional to B's row s. Symmetry propagates the positive proportionality constants along each connected component. Thus B positive definite on each component. This elementary proof avoids silently assuming irreducibility of a potentially degenerate representation.

**Affine criterion.** For connected finite-rank Coxeter diagrams, positive semidefinite B of corank one gives a Euclidean simplex action; treat the rank-two infinity edge as its own `\widetilde A_1` case. The source's theorem 6.8.12 assumes all off-diagonal entries finite and so does not cover it verbatim. Supply the positive radical vector lemma without blanket Perron–Frobenius: for nonzero kernel x, off-diagonal entries ≤0 imply `B(|x|,|x|)≤B(x,x)=0`; positive semidefiniteness forces equality and hence `|x|∈ker B`. If a coordinate vanishes, the corresponding row equation propagates zeros along every negative edge, contradicting connectedness. Thus the radical has a strictly positive generator. Every proper principal submatrix is positive definite, since a kernel vector padded with zero would contradict this generator. Quotient V by the radical, obtain unit normals with their positive dependence, and construct the bounded simplex from half-spaces using Davis 6.8.5–6.8.8. Prove reflection tessellation/presentation with a preceding chamber-development result rather than assuming it. Reducible systems are products of finite/affine/indefinite components, not corank-one by definition.

**Hyperbolic examples.** Indefinite B does not mean word-hyperbolic W or that W has a faithful geometric hyperbolic reflection realization of dimension `|S|−1`. For a compact hyperbolic simplex require Lorentz signature `(n,1)` and every proper principal submatrix positive definite; construct the normals and check all extremal rays timelike in the same time cone (Davis 6.8.4). For finite-volume ideal simplices, allow appropriate positive-semidefinite deleted submatrices and prove cusp/volume conditions separately. Lorentz signature alone guarantees neither compactness nor finite volume. Polygon reflection groups need not have their canonical representation in the same dimension as the geometric realization. General word hyperbolicity is a different statement.

A later Moussong criterion for finite S needs two forbidden configurations: an irreducible affine special subgroup with at least three generators; and disjoint non-spherical subsets whose cross-labels are all two (a product of two infinite special subgroups). Rank-two infinite dihedral is allowed. Do not promise the criterion from bare CAT(0): its proof also requires the extra-large link deformation theorem and/or a proved flat-plane route. Davis/Moussong notes §6.8 explicitly states these without proofs; this source reading does not close that extension.

## Crystallographic affine alcoves

Published local facts actually inspected: `def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions`, `prop-highest-root-exists-and-is-unique-in-an-irreducible-finite-root-system`, and `prop-affine-weyl-group-is-a-coroot-lattice-semidir-product`.

The last item is an **untwisted Kac–Moody full-dual-Cartan formula**, with a large Lie-theory dependency closure. It is not automatically a supplier of the Euclidean affine hyperplane action. Keep its existing home. A Euclidean-first affine page should supply the following direct bridge (finite reduced crystallographic Φ with simple roots and proved highest root θ):

- `H_{α,k}={x:(α,x)=k}`, `s_{α,k}(x)=x−((α,x)−k)α^∨ = t_{kα^∨}s_α(x)`; verify it is the actual isometric reflection and involution.
- Root/coroot lattices are discrete full-rank lattices by simple-root/coroot bases, not just finitely generated subgroups of a real vector space. State the chosen Euclidean identification. Integrality proves all affine reflections preserve the full arrangement; finiteness of Φ proves local finiteness of the hyperplanes.
- The reflection-generated affine group is exactly `Q^∨⋊W`: products lie in it, and `s_{α,1}s_{α,0}=t_{α^∨}` supplies its translations. Uniqueness follows by comparing the linear part and then translation vector. This short proof is independent of Kac–Moody realization.
- For irreducible Φ, the open alcove `A={x:(α_i,x)>0, (θ,x)<1}` is a connected component of the complement: highest-root dominance gives `0<(α,x)<1` for every positive α on A; convexity and bounding hyperplanes prove it is a component. Positive coefficients of θ make its closure a bounded simplex. This alone does not yet prove wall reflections generate the full affine group or that all alcoves are its translates.
- To prove transitivity, join regular points by a generic finite path; local finiteness allows crossing one hyperplane at a time, each crossing maps adjacent alcoves by its reflection. Generic-path perturbation must be proved (finite bad codimension-two intersections on a compact neighborhood). To prove simple transitivity, use an independently proved Coxeter chamber collision/faithfulness theorem or a complete gallery homotopy proof; a picture of the tessellation is insufficient. To prove generation by A's walls, unfold the gallery using already visited wall conjugates. Identify all simple wall relations and apply the chamber-development/presentation theorem.
- Distinguish extended affine group `P^∨⋊W` from the affine Coxeter group; nontrivial alcove stabilizers are possible in the extended group. Work out rank one and type A2, and distinguish B/C root-length conventions explicitly.

This direct argument supplies the affine page without importing Lie-algebra structure as a silent premise.

**G2. Coxeter cells and the complex.** Prove nerve downward closure from finite parabolic inclusion; set `P={wW_T:T spherical}` ordered by inclusion; define Σ as its order realization. For each spherical T and fixed positive distances d_s to simple mirrors, form `C_T=conv(W_T x_T)`. These contracts establish a coherent coarser polyhedral structure on Σ. Its vertex set is W, edges are labeled S, finite rank gives finitely many shapes and finite local incidence. Its 2-cells are the rank-two `2m_st` polygons. Write the simply-connected Cayley 2-complex argument with cancellation of immediate backtracks and relator disks; the involution relation is represented by backtracking, not a separate loop edge. **G3. CAT foundations and local charts.** Define CAT(0), CAT(1) comparison triangles with strict perimeter `<2π` for CAT(1), local CAT, and local geodesics separately. Derive the Euclidean squared-distance convexity inequality and geodesic uniqueness. Prove cone metric triangle inequality and cone/CAT(1) equivalence for the links used, plus joins and the local product-cone chart at a point in a face. For disconnected links use the angular extended-distance/truncation convention; do not require global finite-distance connectedness of a nerve of a universal Coxeter system. Charts + cone comparison prove the link criterion; higher-face links are links in a vertex link, so vertex tests suffice after the induction is proved. Prove local-to-global under **complete, connected, simply connected length/geodesic** hypotheses: local-geodesic continuation/development in homotopy classes, uniqueness by convex local distance, continuous dependence, and subdivision/gluing comparison. Davis/Moussong §1.5 sketches Birkhoff shortening but its claimed uniform convergence is not justified merely by Ascoli (which gives subsequences). Either prove full convergence/continuity and uniqueness or use a fully proved continuation route. A CAT(0) geodesic contraction is continuous directly from `d(H_t x,H_t y)≤t d(x,y)`; no compactness is needed for this last step.

**G4. Spherical metric flags, the real bottleneck.** A Gram matrix with diagonal one and positive definite supplies a unique spherical simplex by a square-root/Cholesky construction. Edge lengths of Coxeter links are `π−π/m_st`, not `π/m_st`. Define metric flag with *cosines* of pairwise edge lengths and positive definite matrix test. Prove links inherit metric flag by Schur complements: `c'_{ij}=(c_{ij}−c_{i0}c_{j0})/sqrt((1−c_{i0}²)(1−c_{j0}²))`; nonpositive off-diagonal signs persist. Prove size ≥π/2 links and the finite Coxeter positive-definite criterion make the nerve metric flag.

To prove the finite large-simplex metric-flag theorem, supply every missing ingredient in Davis I.7's proof: finite-complex compactness; local CAT(1) by link induction; uniform short-geodesic radius; Bowditch's short-loop/curve-shortening criterion; existence of a minimizing nonshrinkable short loop with controlled parametrization; radius-π/2 spherical-cone balls in stars; radial replacement by two radii through a constant-length homotopy; preservation of nonshrinkability/local geodesicity; termination/maximal vertex passage and reduction to a 3-edge circuit; and the Gram-matrix proof that a 3-circuit of perimeter `<2π` fills a spherical triangle. All must be proofs, not 'standard chamber geometry'. Bowditch is not an external fallback: its exact local theorem must be proved if used. Source sketches are insufficient evidence to mark this contract supplied. The all-right cubical case has a simpler star-development proof, but its completion does not prove the non-right general theorem.

**G5. Assembly and consequences.** Once G1–G4 are actually supplied, every Σ vertex link is the finite metric-flag nerve with edge lengths above; link criterion gives local CAT(0), G1 gives completeness/geodesicity, G2 gives simple connectivity, and G3 local-to-global gives CAT(0). Geodesic contraction gives contractibility. A complete CAT(0) circumcenter theorem supplies fixed points for finite isometry groups: derive the midpoint inequality, show centers minimizing maximum distance form a Cauchy sequence, take the limit, prove uniqueness and invariance. The completeness hypothesis must remain stated; CAT(0) alone is not sufficient. This recovers containment in spherical parabolics and convex contractible fixed sets. ### CAT(0) globalization: use endpoint stability, not unproved Birkhoff convergence

Supply the following ordered local contracts, adapting BH II.4.3–4.11.

**C0.1 Endpoint stability.** Given a local geodesic `c:[0,1]→X` in a locally complete, locally convex metric space, choose ε so every closed `2ε`-ball along c is complete and convex. For endpoint perturbations of size `<ε`, construct the unique nearby local geodesic with convex pointwise separation from c. Cover first short subintervals where it is immediate. Extend a solvable interval of size A to size `3A/2` by splitting into thirds and alternately connecting perturbed left endpoint to the current second interior point and current first interior point to perturbed right endpoint. Their interior points contract by factor `1/2` at each iteration; completeness gives limits. Convexity gives uniform convergence on the two overlapping subintervals and identifies their limits on the overlap. This proves existence, uniqueness, continuous dependence and `length(c')≤length(c)+endpoint displacement sum`. It is an actual geometric-series contraction, not an Ascoli claim of convergence.

**C0.2 Local-geodesic path space.** Let `G_{x0}` consist of constant-speed local geodesics from x0, with sup metric. Endpoint evaluation is a local isometry by C0.1: two nearby paths have their maximal separation at the endpoint because separation is convex and they share their initial endpoint. Truncation `c(t)↦c(st)` gives a continuous contraction. A sup-Cauchy sequence converges uniformly because X complete; C0.1 bounds lengths locally and preserves local-geodesic charts, so its limit lies in `G_{x0}`. Thus this path space is complete and contractible. To apply a length-space covering criterion, equip it with its induced length metric: contraction paths give finite distances, local convex charts identify that metric locally with the sup metric, and an induced-length Cauchy sequence converges first in the sup metric and then in one common chart, proving completeness for the length metric as well.

**C0.3 Covering criterion.** A complete local isometry onto a connected locally uniquely geodesic length space is a covering: lift rectifiable paths maximally, use their remaining length to make lifted values Cauchy at a finite endpoint, then completeness extends the lift; uniqueness is the open/closed equality-set argument. For a small uniquely geodesic base ball, lift its radial segments from each point of the fiber to construct continuous inverse sheets; show sheets disjoint by uniqueness. Connectedness supplies surjectivity. The existing covering/lifting suppliers may provide the purely topological conclusion; this metric completeness criterion itself needs a local proof.

**C0.4 Global geodesics.** C0.2–C0.3 make endpoint evaluation the universal covering. If X simply connected, it is a homeomorphism and every pair has a unique local geodesic. C0.1's length bound, extended along any rectifiable competing curve by an open/closed continuation argument, makes that local geodesic globally minimizing. Partition a geodesic joining varying endpoints finely and sum the local convex-distance inequalities to get global convexity (BH II.4.8).

**C0.5 CAT patchwork.** For a geodesic triangle, sweep geodesics from one vertex to the opposite edge. Continuous endpoint dependence makes this a continuous square map. Compactness gives a finite grid whose image rectangles lie in CAT(0) neighborhoods. Triangulate each strip and use the elementary comparison-triangle gluing lemma successively within strips and across strips (BH II.4.10–4.11). Prove the gluing lemma by the Euclidean law of cosines and the angle comparison/straightening inequality; no infinitesimal Riemannian argument is used. This yields global CAT(0). Completeness and simple connectivity are explicit, and the polyhedral metric suppliers prove the hypotheses.

### Finite CAT(1) injectivity radius and systole: exact source instead of shortest-loop folklore

For a compact locally CAT(1) geodesic space, let r be the uniform injectivity radius. Compact local charts give `r>0`. If CAT(1) fails, BH II.4.9–4.12 patchwork imply `r<π`. Choose nondegenerate digons with side lengths decreasing to r. Ascoli in the compact target gives a limit digon of side length r. Prove the limit is nondegenerate: if the sides merged, midpoint separation would tend to zero; each of the two small triangles joining their midpoints to an endpoint has perimeter `<2r`, so patchwork and Alexandrov comparison force both sides to coincide, contradiction. For opposite points of the limit digon, any distance `<r` gives the same two-triangle contradiction. Thus the limit is an isometrically embedded circle of length `m=2r<2π`. This proves the exact minimum embedded-circle statement (BH II.4.16), with finite compactness sufficient; finite-shape noncompact extensions are unnecessary here.

Bowditch pp.13–19 supplies the matching **local** geodesic and loop statements: local geodesics of length `≤r` are global, distinct local geodesics with common endpoints and total length `≤2r` give a minimum embedded circle, and every closed local geodesic has length at least m. ### Quantitative polygonal shortening and the short-loop class

These are explicit local proof obligations derived from Bowditch §§3.3–3.4, not an instruction to 'apply Bowditch'.

1. Choose a uniform small local radius l and a polygon mesh bound h<l. On cyclic n-tuples define mesh M, length L, energy `E=Σd(x_i,x_{i+1})²`, and midpoint map `f(x)_i=mid(x_i,x_{i+1})`. Prove continuity from unique local geodesics; prove `E(fx)≤E(x)` and `L(fx)≤L(x)` by local comparison and Cauchy–Schwarz. Equality characterizes a constant polygon or equally spaced vertices on a closed local geodesic (Bowditch 3.3.1–3.3.4). Repeated maps need only subsequences in compact targets; never assume general convergence.
2. Prove that polygons whose length limit is zero are exactly those whose iterate eventually has length `<l/2`; once inside one small CAT(1) ball, energy equality and compactness force a constant subsequential limit. The whole even-iterate sequence then converges to a constant because subsequent polygons remain inside arbitrarily small balls (3.3.5–3.3.6). This set of polygons is open by continuity.
3. Prove a quantitative energy decrement for shrinkable polygons with length in any compact interval `[a,b]⊂(0,2π)`: after a bounded number of midpoint operations, energy drops by a positive amount depending continuously on current length. Bowditch 3.3.9–3.3.14 constructs the finite comparison disk from the midpoint-operation cellulation. Verify its interior cone angles are ≥2π, its outer turning angles are <π and the needed intermediate boundary angles are ≥π; use comparison-gluing and the disk's finite triangulation to prove CAT(1) on the comparison disk. The spherical radius estimate for a perimeter `<2π` loop and the midpoint quadrilateral estimate give the positive decrement. Degenerate comparison triangles are handled by taking the product with a small Euclidean regular polygon and then sending its radius to zero; prove positive-curvature product CAT comparison by the model-sphere and sine-comparison calculation in the audit supplement below. Summing squared CAT(0) inequalities would not prove this CAT(1) assertion. These geometric helper lemmas must be stated, proved and ordered, not elided as an energy argument.
4. The decrement gives a uniform iteration bound lowering every shrinkable polygon of length≤b below a. Therefore the set of polygons converging to a point is also closed inside the polygon space of lengths `<2π` (3.3.8,3.3.15). Hence a short-loop homotopy cannot connect a closed local geodesic to a constant polygon: a local-geodesic polygon has constant energy and is outside the zero-limit component.
5. Pass between arbitrary rectifiable loops and polygons by sufficiently fine subdivisions, local unique geodesic chord replacement, and the explicit local triangle homotopies. Their lengths never increase. A short-loop homotopy has a uniformly fine polygon mesh after compactness of the parameter square; polygon lengths stay `<2π` by chord bounds. This proves a closed local geodesic cannot be homotoped to a point through loops of length `<2π` (3.1.4/3.4 proof), and every short loop in its constant-loop component has a homotopy with nonincreasing length to a point (3.1.5).
6. In compact X, a subsequence of midpoint iterates converges; equality of limiting energies forces the limit polygon to represent a constant or closed local geodesic. It can be linked back to the original polygon by local vertex homotopies with arbitrarily small length excess. Taking the excess smaller than the gap to 2π yields the same short-loop component (proof of 3.1.6, printed p.32). Therefore every loop shorter than m is shrinkable (3.1.7). The embedded circle of length m is a shortest nonshrinkable loop. This gives a minimum without assuming closure of an unspecified collection of nonshrinkable curves.

This expands the original Davis appeal to Bowditch into a finite supplier tower. Quantitative comparison-disk and product-limit helpers are essential prerequisites; omitting them would reintroduce the original gap.

### Metric-flag proof by bounded radial insertion

Normalize a finite metric-flag complex as the nerve of an almost-negative matrix: diagonal one, assigned edge cosine when an edge exists, and −1 for a nonedge. Its PD principal subsets are exactly its simplices. Gram/Schur complement proves all face links remain metric flag and have edges≥π/2. Induct on dimension using cone charts to get **local** CAT(1). Assume global CAT(1) fails and take the shortest nonshrinkable circle of length `m<2π` above.

For vertex v, the radial spherical cone of radius π/2 on its angular link embeds in its star: every opposite face in a large simplex lies at angular distance ≥π/2 from v. A path of length `<π/2` from v cannot leave that star; radial distance is 1-Lipschitz in its cell charts. Hence its open radial cone is the open π/2-ball and contains no other vertex. These open cones cover the complex: for a unit point `x=Σa_i v_i` in a simplex, `1=Σa_i B(x,v_i)` forces a positive pairing for at least one support vertex. Do not require an unproved global convexity of this ball; the argument uses its local cone charts.

A locally geodesic loop entering such an open cone has an arc in its closed cone which develops to a great semicircle in a hemisphere. Its endpoints are antipodal equatorial points, and its length is π. It cannot have two such excursions on a loop of length `<2π`. Developing the radial ruled surface gives a closed hemisphere/lune of angular width π; rotate this semicircle continuously toward the two meridian radii through v. Every curve in this homotopy has length exactly π and stays in that ruled surface. Thus the loop remains in the same short-loop component. All previous vertices are preserved (none is inside the open cone), and v is inserted if absent.

Every deformed loop still has length m and is nonshrinkable. It is therefore locally geodesic: any strict local shortening in a CAT(1) chart gives a shorter loop in its short-loop component, which would be shrinkable. Distinct vertices are at least π/2 apart by the same first-star-exit argument. Hence there are at most three vertex visits on a loop of length `<2π`, and successive insertions terminate after at most three additions.

Expand the maximum-vertex step explicitly. If an outgoing radial ray from a visited vertex v lies in the interior of a simplex σ of dimension≥2, then before it leaves σ through an opposite face some other support vertex w has positive pairing with the ray (at the exit v's pairing is nonpositive and the unit-norm identity forces another positive pairing). Thus the ray enters w's open cone. At a maximum-vertex loop its cone excursion already passes through w. Within σ, the segment toward w is the unique spherical radial segment and remains in convex σ; local continuation forces the developed ray through v and w to be their edge, contradicting interior dimension≥2. If the loop has no visited vertex, any cone excursion inserts one. Thus the maximizing loop lies in the 1-skeleton. A locally geodesic edge loop of length `<2π` has three distinct edges, since every edge is ≥π/2, and immediate backtracking is not locally geodesic. The three vertices are pairwise adjacent. Their spherical triangle inequalities hold because every side is `<π` and the sum of the other two is≥π; perimeter `<2π` makes the Gram matrix positive definite. Metric flag fills this triangle. Its boundary has an interior turn smaller than π at each corner, contradicting local geodesicity. Therefore global CAT(1) holds.

This route avoids Moussong 9.11 completely and gives a bounded termination measure (at most three vertex additions) rather than an indefinite deformation process. The independent auditor must check the explicit hemisphere development and maximum-vertex continuation when adapting it to local item contracts.

### Exact Bowditch 3.3.9–3.3.15 formulas

Fix the polygon vertex count n. Let h<l, where every triangle of perimeter `<2l` is CAT(1), and set `C_h^0` to be polygons whose iterated-length limit is zero. Bowditch 3.3.9 asserts

`E(f x) ≤ E(x) − λ(L(x))`,

where λ is positive and continuous on `(0,2π)`, depends on n **but not h**, and is allowed to tend to zero at both endpoints. The comparison-disk argument is initially for nondegenerate comparison triangles and polygons in `C_h^0`.

Put `r=L(x)/2`, `Δ=E(x)−E(fx)`, and `ξ_i=d(x_i,x_{i+1})`. The cyclic averaging estimate is

`E(fx)≤Σ((ξ_i+ξ_{i+1})/2)^2`,

so `Σ(ξ_i−ξ_{i+1})²≤4Δ` and `|ξ_i−ξ_{i+1}|≤2√Δ`. Summing along the shorter cyclic route and averaging gives `|ξ_i−2r/n|≤n√Δ`. If `Δ≤r²/n⁴`, every ξ_i is at least r/n.

Choose `ε=π−r`. Lemma 3.3.10 gives a positive η=η(ε) such that any loop of length≤2r in a **globally** CAT(1) comparison target lies in a ball of radius `π/2−η`. This uses the spherical midpoint-of-ellipse estimate (Proposition 1.3): divide the loop into two arcs of length≤r and compare each point to the midpoint of the two division points. The estimate can be specified without a closed-form η by maximizing midpoint distance over the compact family of spherical triangles with sum of the two sides≤r; its maximum is strictly below π/2 by the spherical cosine formula. A continuous positive minorant η(r) is sufficient.

Set **`μ=min{r/(2n),η}`**, so μ<2η. Lemma 3.3.11 gives δ=δ(η,μ)>0: in a CAT(1) target, if `d(a,x)≤π/2−η`, `d(a,y),d(a,z)≤d(a,x)` and `d(x,y)=d(x,z)=μ`, then `d(y,z)≤2μ−δ`. Its proof develops the two comparison triangles along the a–x side into S² and gives a compact-family separation bound away from the degenerate straight angle. Choosing a smaller continuous positive δ if necessary is allowed.

In the midpoint comparison disk, choose the outer boundary vertex b farthest from its radius center a. The interior-adjacent boundary vertices cannot maximize this distance because their boundary angles are≥π; therefore b is an original outer-row vertex. Its two neighbors v1,v2 each have distance from b equal to half an original side, hence≥r/(2n)≥μ. Cut these sides at distance μ from b and apply the previous δ estimate. If `ζ_i=d((fx)_i,(fx)_{i+1})`, this yields for some k

`ζ_k≤(ξ_k+ξ_{k+1})/2−δ`,

while `ζ_i≤(ξ_i+ξ_{i+1})/2` for all i. Since δ≤(ξ_k+ξ_{k+1})/2, the resulting energy improvement is at least

`δ(ξ_k+ξ_{k+1})−δ² ≥ (δ/2)(ξ_k+ξ_{k+1}) ≥ δ r/n`.

Thus a safe source-consistent choice is

`λ(2r)=min{r²/n⁴, δ(η(π−r), min{r/(2n),η(π−r)}) r/n}`,

or any positive continuous minorant, for example half of this bound after choosing continuous minorants for η and δ. Do not claim the stronger bound `2δr/n`: the −δ² term must be retained. For a compact interval `[r0,R]⊂(0,2π)`, let `λ0=min λ>0`. Since `E≤L²≤R²`, any integer `M>R²/λ0` forces every zero-limit polygon with initial length≤R to have `L(f^M x)<r0`. This is 3.3.8 and gives the uniform bound needed in 3.3.15's closed-class argument. At a limit polygon take an upper length bound R strictly below 2π and choose r0<l; continuity of the fixed finite iterate then keeps its length below l. This is the exact local/uniform hypothesis used, not compactness of an unspecified curve class.

The finite comparison disk has vertices `v(i,j)` for rows i=0,…,M and cyclic labels j. Consecutive rows are connected by the midpoint-operation triangles `v(i,j),v(i−1,j),v(i−1,j+1)`. Their sides are the actual X-distances. The piecewise boundary map to X is distance nonincreasing. For each interior-row vertex v, adjacent previous-row vertices v1,v2 satisfy

`ρ(v1,v2)≥d(gv1,gv2)=d(gv1,gv)+d(gv,gv2)=ρ(v1,v)+ρ(v,v2)`.

The triangle inequality gives equality, placing v in a developed straight segment and forcing interior cone angle≥2π. The last interior boundary row has boundary angle≥π; the outer row has angle<π. The cellulation's finite row induction rules out a simple closed geodesic in this disk (Bowditch 3.3.13); its explicit no-short-circle/patchwork suppliers establish global CAT(1). Preserve this row-induction proof as a required local comparison-disk lemma, rather than merely calling the disk CAT(1) from the cone-angle statement.

### Correct CAT(1) product theorem without extra Riemannian comparison prerequisites

Bowditch 3.2.1 uses the product model S²×S² and its curvature upper bound; the printed claim 'curvature≤−1' is a typo for ≤1. Here is an independent explicit model calculation that supplies the needed product result without importing a full Riemannian CAT comparison theorem.

In a product-model triangle, parameterize its opposite side c(t), t∈[0,1], by constant speed. Its factor curves c_i are constant-speed great-circle segments with speeds v_i, and `v²=v_1²+v_2²` is the squared product side length. Fix the other triangle vertex p. Put `r_i(t)=d_{S²}(p_i,c_i(t))` and `r(t)=sqrt(r_1²+r_2²)`. Triangle perimeter<2π implies throughout the side

`r(t)≤min{a+tv,b+(1−t)v}<π`,

where a,b are the distances to its endpoints. Thus factor antipodes are excluded. The spherical law of cosines differentiated along a great circle gives

`r_i''=cot(r_i)(v_i²−r_i'^2)`

away from r_i=0. Set A=r cot r and A_i=r_i cot r_i. The elementary function u cot u is decreasing on (0,π), so A_i≥A; also `1−A≥0` and `|r_i'|≤v_i`. Direct differentiation gives

`r r''−A(v²−r'^2)=Σ(A_i−A)v_i²+Σ(1−A_i)r_i'^2−(1−A)r'^2`

`≥(1−A)(Σr_i'^2−r'^2)≥0`,

the last step being Cauchy–Schwarz with weights r_i/r. Therefore F(t)=cos r(t) satisfies

`F''+v² F≤0`.

The scalar Dirichlet sine-comparison lemma for 0<v<π implies

`F(t)≥[sin((1−t)v)cos a+sin(tv)cos b]/sin v`.

Its right side is the cosine of the vertex-to-side distance in the spherical comparison triangle. Since cosine decreases on [0,π], this is the vertex-to-side CAT(1) inequality. Prove the scalar lemma by the positive Green kernel for `−d²/dt²−v²` on [0,1], or its elementary Wronskian/sine integration formula. Zero factor distances are removable: squared spherical distance is smooth away from antipodes, and `cos sqrt(r_1²+r_2²)` extends smoothly at r=0, so the inequality extends by continuity there. Zero side speed gives the degenerate constant-side case.

For arbitrary CAT(1) factors, replace each projected factor triangle by its S² comparison triangle. Their paired boundary map bounds the original product distance above by the corresponding S²×S² model distance and preserves product side lengths (each factor segment is parameterized proportionally to length). Apply the model inequality. This is a legitimate sum of **factor distance bounds followed by the model proof**, not a sum of squared CAT(0) convexity inequalities. The usual vertex-to-side/full comparison equivalence completes the product theorem and must be an earlier CAT supplier.

Degenerate comparison-disk triangles are then handled by taking the product polygon with an n-gon of small circumradius e in E². Its midpoint iterates stay regular and their adjacent triples are noncollinear, so every paired comparison triangle has strict triangle inequalities. The product midpoint map is componentwise; product energy is the sum of factor energies. The decrement λ depends on n and the total polygon length, not on e or the mesh bound. As e→0 the E² energies vanish and the lengths converge to those of the original polygon, giving the same λ inequality by continuity.

### Maximal-vertex continuation: the exact injectivity radius use

A shortest nonshrinkable loop of length m entering an open radius-π/2 radial cone has a semicircle excursion of length π. Its equatorial endpoints are distinct, so its complement has positive length. Consequently **m>π**, and the injectivity radius `r=m/2` is **strictly greater than π/2**. Every local-geodesic subarc of length<π/2 is therefore globally minimizing and unique (Bowditch 2.16 and the injectivity-radius contract).

Suppose an outgoing ray from a visited vertex v lies in the interior of a simplex σ of dimension≥2. Before it exits σ at an opposite face, some other vertex w of σ has positive pairing with a point x of the ray; at the exit v's pairing is nonpositive and the unit-norm identity forces another positive pairing, persisting immediately before exit. Thus x lies inside w's open radial cone. At maximum vertex count that excursion already passes through w. Its x–w subarc has length<π/2 and is globally unique by r>π/2. The spherical x–w segment stays inside convex σ and has the same length as its ambient distance: a path from w leaving its star costs at least π/2, whereas this radial segment is shorter. Thus it is another global geodesic of length<r and must coincide with the loop subarc. Local spherical continuation makes the outgoing ray from v the great-circle line through v and w. This puts the interior point x in span{v,w}, impossible for an interior point of a simplex with at least three linearly independent vertices. This supplies uniqueness **before** the simplex exit and does not assume global convexity of the star.

A safe explicit radius constant is available: in the spherical comparison triangle set `b=d(x,y)`, `d=d(x,z)`, `c=d(y,z)`. The midpoint formula gives

`cos d(a,x)=(cos b+cos d)/(2cos(c/2)) = cos((b+d)/2) cos((b−d)/2)/cos(c/2) ≥ cos(r/2)`,

because `b+d≤r<π` and `|b−d|≤c`. Therefore in 3.3.10 one may take **`η=(π−r)/2`**, radius r/2. For the quadrilateral separation δ, retain the source's compact-family **intrinsic developed quadrilateral** bound, rather than automatically bounding by the ambient spherical chord: that chord may leave the two glued comparison triangles and then a path through their common vertex must be considered. The local δ contract must verify this containment or supply the corresponding two-case intrinsic path estimate.
