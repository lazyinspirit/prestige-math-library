## Safe foundational order and the avoided circle

A proof-first scaffold must not establish exchange from root positivity while establishing root positivity from exchange without an independent starting proof. A clean route, independently checked against both sources, is:

1. Construct the presented group using the free group and normal closure of the relators. State its universal property and check it proves the descent and uniqueness of every proposed action.
2. Construct the canonical linear action and establish exact rank-two product orders, without yet asserting faithfulness.
3. Construct the signed-reflection permutation action, prove word-independent inversion sets, and deduce exchange. This is purely algebraic and needs no root-sign theorem.
4. Deduce deletion and Matsumoto's braid connectivity; then standard-parabolic presentation, length restriction and coset decomposition.
5. Prove the rank-two half-space property; use the simultaneous induction of Davis Lemma 4.8.3 to obtain the chamber half-space property in the dual canonical action. This yields root sign coherence, length-sign correspondence, disjoint chamber interiors and faithfulness.
6. Develop finite chamber theory, the finite/positive-definite criterion and diagram classification; use actual existing finite-reflection invariant suppliers for invariant theory.

For finite-rank theory say explicitly that S is finite. Finite-rank is enough for the proposed category. An arbitrary-rank extension must use finitely supported vector sums, distinguish the algebraic dual from a finite-dimensional dual, and avoid finite-dimensional compactness assertions. Do not silently generalize every statement to infinite S.

## Foundational proof contracts and silent prerequisites

### Presentation, length, parity, reflection set

Lusztig §1.1–1.2 and Davis §3.3 supply the definitions, but the author must justify: the quotient is a group; generator maps exist; every element is represented by a finite S-word (s inverse equals s); the nonempty set of representing word lengths has a least natural number; reversing a word shows length of the inverse equals length; concatenation gives subadditivity. Empty S yields the trivial group. A sign character exists because each relator has even word length. This proves length changes by exactly ±1 under simple multiplication, rather than merely asserting an inequality. Define T as the conjugates of S, not as every involution of W. After the canonical action, each simple generator is nontrivial and distinct; exact product orders show the original matrix is recovered from the system.

### Canonical action and rank-two relations

Davis Lemma 6.12.3, Corollary 6.12.4–6.12.6; Lusztig Proposition 1.3. Let E have basis alpha_s, with symmetric form B(alpha_s,alpha_t) = -cos(pi/m_st), convention cos(pi/infinity)=1. Then B(alpha_s,alpha_s)=1 and r_s(x)=x-2B(x,alpha_s)alpha_s. Calculate r_s squared equals identity, B is preserved, the fixed space is the hyperplane ker B(-,alpha_s), and the -1 eigenspace is R alpha_s. These remain valid when B is degenerate or indefinite. Calling this an orthogonal reflection must not imply a positive Euclidean inner product exists in every type.

For finite m_st, the rank-two restricted form is positive definite: determinant sin²(pi/m)>0. E decomposes as the rank-two plane plus its B-orthogonal complement even if the full B is degenerate; prove this by solving the invertible 2-by-2 Gram equations. The product fixes the latter summand and is rotation through 2pi/m on the plane, hence has exact order m. For infinity, the two-plane product is a nonidentity unipotent with square-zero nilpotent part; its kth power is identity plus kN, which is nonidentity over R for k nonzero. This proves infinite order. The relations therefore descend to a **unique** homomorphism W -> GL(E). Faithfulness remains a separate theorem.

Covert prerequisites: finite-dimensional linear extension from basis data; bilinear extension; kernels; matrix products/determinants; a proof of the two-plane rotation/trigonometric calculation; eigenvalues or an explicit rotation-power computation; direct sum via invertible Gram matrix; characteristic zero. These can be local elementary lemmas if existing supplier claims do not exactly cover them.

### Signed action, inversions, exchange and deletion

Lusztig Propositions 1.5–1.7; Davis Lemma 3.3.5 and §3.2.16–17. On {±1} x T define U_s(epsilon,t)=(epsilon*(-1)^[s=t],sts). Check U_s squared equals identity. For finite rank-two relation, compute the 2m conjugates in its sign exponent: each reflection in the relevant dihedral orbit occurs an even number of times, so (U_s U_t)^m is identity. This proof does not assume the canonical action faithful. Define the inversion set of a reduced word as the conjugated letters r_i=s_1...s_(i-1)s_i s_(i-1)...s_1. If two r_i coincide, multiply the equality out to delete two letters, contradicting minimality. The signed action makes this set independent of reduced expression. For a descent sw, compare its expression beginning with s to the arbitrary chosen reduced expression of w: s occurs among its inversion reflections, and solving that equality gives exchange. State left/right conventions explicitly and derive the right form by inversion.

Deletion for a nonreduced word: choose a shortest nonreduced prefix and apply exchange to its reduced preceding prefix. This yields deletion of exactly two letters with the same product; repeating terminates because word length drops by two. This is the subword supplier needed for parabolics and Bruhat theory.

### Matsumoto and an actual word-problem algorithm

Davis Theorem 3.4.2 supplies the complete exchange-based induction; Lusztig Theorem 1.9 supplies a different complete induction. Recommended proof: reduced expressions with the same first letter reduce to shorter length. With different first letters s,t, exchange iteratively constructs an alternating initial segment until its length is m_st; if m were infinite, this would contradict the finite length of w. One braid move interchanges the initial alternating segment, then the shorter induction completes the connection. For algorithmic claims, merely knowing a finite sequence exists is insufficient explanation: finitely enumerate all words of a fixed length reachable by braid moves; if one contains ss, cancel it and repeat at shorter length; if not, the word is reduced by the theorem. Each fixed-length search is finite because S is finite. Two reduced words are equal exactly when their finite braid-connectivity search connects. Do not promise polynomial complexity.

Lusztig Corollary 1.4's assertion that rank-two alternating word lengths are “immediate” should not be copied as a proof of full-W length. Exact rank-two group order by itself does not generally prove no shorter expression using other generators exists. Use Davis's exchange/deletion approach and prove the restricted rank-two length comparison before the final Matsumoto inference. Likewise when a simple generator is said to lie in a rank-two subgroup, justify that it is one of those two generators: its canonical action has its displacement image R alpha_s, whereas a word in r_a,r_b has displacement in span(alpha_a,alpha_b). This closes the basis-support argument without assuming faithfulness.

### Standard parabolics, cosets and longest elements

Lusztig §§9.1–9.8; Davis §§4.1–4.3 and 4.6–4.7. Support of a reduced word is independent of the word by braid connectivity. W_I consists exactly of elements whose support lies in I; its restricted length equals ambient length. The canonical map from the abstract submatrix presentation onto W_I is an isomorphism: define its inverse with reduced I-words, use Matsumoto to prove representative independence, and use exchange to check multiplicativity. All intersections W_I intersect W_J = W_(I intersect J) follow from support, including the empty-intersection case. Connected diagram components commute and the multiplication map of their factors is an isomorphism; show both surjectivity and trivial cross-factor intersections rather than just saying “natural product.”

Each left/right standard-parabolic coset has a unique minimal element because lengths are natural numbers. The length-additive expression w=uv (u in W_I, v minimal in W_I\W) follows by deletion: deleting a letter from v would contradict its minimality; deleting letters solely from the reduced u word would contradict that word's minimality. Uniqueness then follows by additivity. The no-I-descent characterization is proved from the first letter of a nontrivial u. For double cosets the minimal representative is unique and a length-additive factorization exists, but arbitrary pairs of parabolic factors are **not unique**; intersections control their ambiguity. State the side conventions consistently.

In finite W the unique longest w0 satisfies length(w0)=length(u)+length(u^-1 w0), is an involution and conjugates S to S. Davis Lemmas 4.6.1–2 prove existence and uniqueness and the criterion “every simple generator is a left descent” implies W finite. In infinite W do not assert a longest element. Every finite set of descents generates a finite standard parabolic, Davis Lemma 4.7.2. Distinguish standard parabolic W_I from its conjugate (parabolic subgroup) and prove the conjugacy-invariance of rank if that rank is defined for all parabolics; Davis Proposition 4.5.10 relies on an omitted boundary-edge exercise, so supply that argument locally or restrict the early rank definition to standard parabolics.

### Roots, chamber half-spaces and faithfulness

Davis §§4.8 and D.1 give an exact independent route. Use the contragredient action on E*: (w phi)(x)=phi(w^-1 x). Define C by phi(alpha_s)>=0 and its interior by strict inequalities. This cone exists and has nonempty interior because the alpha_s form a basis: assign value 1 to each basis vector. Do not identify E and E* through B when B is degenerate.

For each simple s use the open half-space H_s={phi:phi(alpha_s)>0}. On each rank-two quotient of E* the two reflection chambers are either Euclidean sectors (finite m) or the interval tiling on an invariant affine line (infinite m). Verify the half-space alternative and descent length there, as in Davis Lemma D.1.5. Davis Lemma 4.8.3 then proves property P for all of W using **two** simultaneous inductions P_n and Q_n, not just an unproved “rank-two reduction.” Record the intermediate Q_n factorization: for every w of length n and s != t, wC° lies in u(H_s intersect H_t) for rank-two u with length(w)=length_rank2(u)+length(u^-1 w). The two implications (P_n,Q_n) => P_(n+1) and (P_(n+1),Q_n) => Q_(n+1) are explicitly proved on printed p.56. Property P says wC° is entirely on one side of each simple wall, and negative-side placement implies simple left descent.

If wC° intersects C° for w != 1, write w=sw' with length(w') one lower. The half-space alternative for w' contradicts the given intersection and the descent lengths. Thus chamber interiors are disjoint. If w acts trivially it preserves C° and must be 1, proving faithful dual action, hence faithful canonical action. Root set Phi={w alpha_s}; root sign follows because every root's evaluation on C° has constant nonzero sign: apply the half-space property to w^-1 C°. A linear functional represented by a root has constant positive sign on all positive coordinate vectors exactly when its simple-basis coordinates are all nonnegative and not all zero; prove this by making any putative negative coordinate arbitrarily dominant. Hence Phi=Phi+ disjoint-union -Phi+ and length(ws)>length(w) iff w alpha_s is positive. Reflection conjugacy formula w r_s w^-1 = r_(w alpha_s) is a direct B-invariance calculation. Inversion root counts follow by translating the signed inversions or chamber walls; do not equate reflection/root data before proving the root-to-reflection identification modulo sign.

Definition obligations: roots need not be integral combinations of simple roots; general Coxeter root systems do not satisfy the crystallographic axiom. A root lattice over Z is therefore inappropriate without additional crystallographic hypotheses. Tits cone is the union of wC, not automatically all of E*. Convexity is a theorem (Davis D.2.7), and boundary topology differs from the CW chamber-gluing topology (explicit warning after D.2.6).

### Finiteness criterion and full diagram classification

Davis Theorem 6.12.9 and Appendix C, with the following economical local replacement for the geometric existence direction. The disjoint-interior theorem also isolates identity in rho(W): for fixed phi in C°, {g:g phi in C°} is an identity neighborhood meeting rho(W) only in identity. If B is positive definite, rho(W) lies in the compact orthogonal group. Were it infinite, a sequence of distinct elements would have a convergent subsequence; quotients of consecutive terms approach identity, contradicting isolation. This proves finite W without importing the developing-map theorem, covering-space theory or spherical simplex tessellation. Prerequisites: finite-dimensional matrix topology, compactness of orthogonal group (closed and bounded), Bolzano–Weierstrass, continuity of inversion/product; give short local proofs if no exact suppliers exist.

Conversely average any inner product over finite W. For connected diagram, an invariant inner product Q has alpha_s perpendicular to ker B(-,alpha_s), because r_s acts by -1 on alpha_s and +1 there. Thus Q(alpha_s,-)=c_s B(alpha_s,-), with c_s=Q(alpha_s,alpha_s)>0. Symmetry implies c_s=c_t along every edge where B(alpha_s,alpha_t) is nonzero; connectedness gives Q=cB. Hence B is positive definite. Reduce disconnected diagrams by factor blocks. This elementary argument avoids an unproved real-version Schur lemma.

Appendix C's classification proof is a concrete determinant/domination proof. List A_n (n>=1), B_n (n>=2), D_n (n>=4), E6,E7,E8,F4,H3,H4,I2(m) (m>=3); note A1 x A1=I2(2), A2=I2(3), B2=I2(4), G2=I2(6), B_n and C_n have the same Coxeter system. Do not list only Weyl types. ## Supplement: complete uniform bipartite root-enumeration proof route

Added after scaffold-author request and sent to the independent auditor. **Davis does not supply this Coxeter-element theorem:** its Steinberg citation concerns growth series. The route below is an independently constructed and checked finite-dimensional proof, grounded in the previously read canonical-action, finite-chamber/stabilizer and positive-root suppliers. It is not represented as a reading of an unavailable Steinberg proof. The theorem statement should retain its actual consulted source citation; the local proof provenance is an alteration/construction of the argument, not a literature-proof reading claim.

### Exact hypotheses and supplier closure

Let (W,S) be a finite irreducible Coxeter system of rank n>=2. Use its canonical unit simple roots alpha_s and positive-definite form B. Let S=J disjoint-union K be a two-coloring of its tree diagram; both classes are nonempty. Order J before K and put a=product_(s in J)s, b=product_(s in K)s, c=ab. Within each class generators commute. Repeated generators s_(i+n)=s_i give prefix roots rho_i=s_1...s_(i-1)alpha_(s_i). The desired conclusion is that h=ord(c), |Phi+|=nh/2, and Phi+=(rho_1,...,rho_(nh/2)) without repetitions.

Suppliers needed and actually accounted for by this proof: faithful canonical action; positive-root coefficients and length-sign criterion; root-to-reflection bijection modulo sign; chambers with disjoint interiors and unique closed-chamber representatives; point stabilizer W_I for the set I of zero simple-root pairings; the longest-element characterization by sending all positive roots negative; finite-dimensional real spectral theorem or an explicit Rayleigh minimum argument; compact unit sphere; elementary dihedral group normal form. No crystallographic lattice, Weyl classification, invariant exponents, Coxeter-number table, root height, or crystallographic integrality is used. This therefore includes H3,H4 and I2(m) uniformly.

### 1. Positive eigenvector and invariant plane, with all existence checks

In simple-root coordinates let A=2(I-B): its diagonal is zero, off-diagonal entries are 2cos(pi/m_st)>=0, and its support graph is connected. Because B is positive definite, every eigenvalue of A is <2. The largest eigenvalue lambda is positive: a vector supported with two positive coordinates on any edge has positive A-quadratic value. Existence of a maximum of x^tAx on the Euclidean unit sphere follows from compactness. Its stationary equation is Ap=lambda p (derive by differentiating along all vectors perpendicular to p, so no unproved multiplier theorem is required). Replacing p by its coordinatewise absolute value cannot decrease the quadratic value; take p>=0. If p_s=0, the equation 0=sum_t A_st p_t forces p_t=0 at every adjacent vertex, hence everywhere by connectedness, contradiction. Thus every p_s>0. We have 0<lambda<2. This proves exactly the Perron fact needed without invoking an unproved Perron–Frobenius theorem.

Define u=sum_(s in J)p_s alpha_s and v=sum_(t in K)p_t alpha_t. The vectors within each class are B-orthonormal. Write d_J=B(u,u)=sum_J p_s² and d_K=B(v,v)=sum_K p_t². Multiplying the J-coordinate eigen-equations by p_s and summing, and doing the same for K, gives lambda d_J=sum_(s in J,t in K)A_st p_s p_t=lambda d_K. Hence d_J=d_K=d>0 and B(u,v)=-lambda d/2. The plane P=span(u,v) is genuinely two-dimensional because their supports are disjoint and both vectors are nonzero. Its Gram determinant d²(1-lambda²/4)>0.

The reflection formula gives a(u)=-u, a(v)=v+lambda u, b(v)=-v, b(u)=u+lambda v. In particular a,b preserve P and act as orthogonal line reflections there. For x=x_Ju+x_Kv, B(x,alpha_s)=p_s(x_J-lambda x_K/2) for s in J, and B(x,alpha_t)=p_t(x_K-lambda x_J/2) for t in K. Consequently the intersection D=C intersect P of the fundamental closed chamber with P is precisely the sector bounded by the fixed lines of a,b, of angle theta=arccos(lambda/2) in (0,pi/2). Its relative interior lies inside the full chamber interior: both displayed common factors are strictly positive there. Thus there is a point of P with trivial W stabilizer, supplied by the full chamber-interior theorem. An element of <a,b> acting trivially on P fixes this point, so is identity. The restriction of <a,b> to P is faithful. This is also why a,b are nonidentity and distinct.

### 2. Exact order, not only an eigenvalue divisor

Let h be the exact order of the rotation c|P. Finite W ensures h is finite. Faithfulness on <a,b> makes this the exact order of c in W as well. The elementary two-reflection calculation makes c|P a rotation by 2theta up to orientation, so theta=k*pi/h for integers k>=1 coprime to h. The dihedral group generated on P has 2h elements and its images of D have pairwise disjoint relative interiors: each lies inside the respective full W chamber, whose interiors are disjoint. Their angular measures total 2h*theta and cannot exceed 2pi. Therefore theta<=pi/h, forcing k=1. In particular **theta=pi/h**, with no assumption that the eigenvalue originally gave the primitive angle. The 2h sectors now cover P: their total angles equal 2pi, and the dihedral action places them consecutively with no overlap. This is a direct fundamental-sector proof of the Coxeter-plane angle.

### 3. Every root wall occurs at a sector boundary, and exact block multiplicities

For a root beta, B(beta,x) is nonzero for any x in D's relative interior because x is in C° and beta or -beta has nonnegative simple coefficients. Thus the root hyperplane H_beta does not contain P, and its intersection with P is a line. It cannot meet the relative interior of ANY translated dihedral sector: an interior point of that sector lies in the corresponding full chamber interior and is on no root hyperplane. Since the sectors cover P, H_beta intersect P is a sector-boundary line.

Take a nonzero point x on one of D's two boundary rays. Its zero simple-root pairings are exactly J or exactly K, by the explicit coordinate inequalities in step 1 (the other factor is positive because lambda<2). The closed-chamber stabilizer theorem gives W_x=W_J or W_K. Because the simple reflections in a color commute and are independent, W_J is an elementary abelian group, and its canonical action changes sign independently on the orthonormal alpha_s, s in J, fixing their common orthogonal complement. A product of q distinct such generators has -1 eigenspace dimension q. Thus the only reflections in W_J are the individual generators, and the only root walls through x are their simple walls, using the already supplied root-to-reflection bijection modulo sign. The same holds for K. Transport this statement by <a,b> to every boundary ray. It proves that each crossed ray has exactly the root walls in the relevant transported color block; no hidden extra walls or roots occur at a simultaneous crossing.

### 4. Half-turn, reduced word, no repetition and all positive roots

Starting in D, move around P in the direction whose first crossed ray is the J-wall ray. Its consecutive adjacent chambers are obtained by right multiplication of their labels by a,b,a,b,..., so the labels are 1,a,ab,aba,... . After h boundary crossings the sector is -D, since each sector has angle pi/h. During this half-turn every root-wall line is crossed exactly once. Each transported color block consists of the individual root walls just proved in step 3. The root attached to a particular simple generator in that block is w alpha_s, where w is the product of previous blocks; preceding letters inside the block commute with alpha_s, so they do not change this root.

Every such root is positive with respect to the initial chamber: its wall has not been crossed earlier, and the initial interior point lies on the positive side before its first crossing. More explicitly, the current chamber's interior is on the positive side of its outward simple-root wall, and the initial and current chamber interior lie on the same side until the crossing; evaluate w alpha_s at the initial regular point and apply sign coherence. Therefore the expanded alternating-block word is reduced by the right-multiplication root-sign criterion, and its prefix roots are exactly the successive crossed positive roots rho_i. They cannot repeat because each root wall is crossed only once and each root wall corresponds to exactly one positive root. Every positive root is encountered because each of its lines meets the half-turn path, and step 3 accounts for every wall at every crossed boundary ray. This proves exhaustiveness and no repetitions, not just a count.

The resulting word sends the full chamber C to -C: it takes the regular plane point in C° to a point in -C°, and the unique chamber containing that point is -C°. Equivalently its inversions are all positive roots. Hence it is the longest element w0. This supplies the opposition assertion required by the enumeration, without assuming in advance a formula for w0.

### 5. Odd h is a real separate case

For even h the h successive blocks comprise h/2 copies of each color, so their total number of simple letters is nh/2 and w0=(ab)^(h/2)=c^(h/2).

For odd h, elementary dihedral normal form shows a,b are conjugate inside <a,b> (explicitly use the alternating word of h letters, which conjugates one to the other). Conjugate real linear involutions have the same -1 eigenspace dimension. On E, the -1 eigenspaces of a and b have dimensions |J| and |K|, respectively, by their commuting orthonormal-root formulas. Thus |J|=|K|=n/2; n is necessarily even. The first h blocks, a,b,...,a, have (h+1)/2 J-blocks and (h-1)/2 K-blocks, but equal block sizes give exactly nh/2 letters. Here w0=c^((h-1)/2)a; the corresponding other-direction word is c^-((h-1)/2)b. This includes odd dihedral m and even-rank A types and does not illegally use c^(h/2).

We have proved |Phi+|=nh/2 and exact h=2|Phi+|/n as consequences. Numerical h tables, if wanted, should follow from separately proved root counts or explicit matrices; the proof does not assume them. For rank one treat A1 separately: a=s,b=1,c=s,h=2,P is a line, and the root list has the one positive root. For reducible W do not claim a single nh/2 formula without additional hypotheses: distinct components have different Coxeter numbers.
