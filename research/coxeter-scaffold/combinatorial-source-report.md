## Dependency order and foundational repairs

HH-11 must first prove abstract generators have their prescribed orders, reflections are involutions, length has a minimum, parity forces length to change by exactly one under a simple generator, strong exchange (including the version for an arbitrary expression if used), deletion, support invariance, Matsumoto and word reduction. Signed-permutation action alone must not be silently promoted to faithful geometric action. Downstream combinatorial proofs use strong exchange and deletion, not a general geometric root-positivity theorem.

The order-theoretic prerequisites can be proved locally in one A page or an earlier foundations page: a poset's reflexivity/antisymmetry/transitivity; strict order; covers; chains and saturated chains; interval; locally finite; rank/gradedness; directedness; minimum versus merely minimal; maximum versus maximal; order map/isomorphism/anti-isomorphism; lower ideal; meet/join existence versus uniqueness; semilattice/lattice and distributive lattice. Every finite poset has a maximal/minimal element by a terminating chain, and a finite directed poset has a greatest element by successively taking upper bounds. A finite acyclic precedence relation has a linear extension by repeatedly removing a minimal element. These are finite arguments; no arbitrary choice assumption is needed.

Do not define Bruhat order by “some reduced expression contains a subword” and then assume expression independence. Define by the reflexive transitive closure of length-increasing reflection multiplication and prove the subword theorem for EVERY fixed reduced expression.

## Distinct extension pairs and exact contracts

### C1. Bruhat order, the subword theorem, and lifting

Definition contracts: reflection set `T={wsw^{-1}}`; directed Bruhat graph; reflexive transitive closure; subword means subsequence, while contiguous segment/factor is a different term; covers and rank. Prove the graph closure is a poset because every nontrivial edge strictly raises integer length. Inversion gives left/right equivalence directly by conjugating reflections or by reversing words.

Supplier chain (BB §§2.1–2.2): strong exchange + deletion → augmentation lemma 2.2.1 → subword theorem 2.2.2 → expression independence 2.2.3, finite intervals 2.2.4 and rank-one chain property 2.2.6 → lifting 2.2.7 → directedness 2.2.9. The augmentation lemma is substantive: choose a reduced subword for `u` whose final omitted position is as early as possible; restore that position; if length goes down, strong exchange gives either a shorter expression for `w` or a competing subword contradicting minimality. Supply both contradictions, not “exchange implies augmentation.”

Finite intervals are bounded by `2^{ell(w)}`; use a finite surjection from subwords rather than invoke arbitrary choices of subwords. A cover has rank difference one by augmentation. Lifting has explicit hypotheses `u≤w`, `sw<w`, `su>u`; take a reduced word for `w` starting with `s`, observe the reduced subword for `u` cannot use that initial `s`, then prove both `u≤sw` and `su≤w`. Include the corresponding right-hand formulation by inversion.

B companion: full I2(m) diagrams as descriptions of ranked elements; S3 comparability; counterexample that Bruhat order is a lattice (two distinct minimal common upper bounds of the two simple generators in S3); counterexample weak and Bruhat are the same; type-A rank/dot criterion only if its detailed monotone-rectangle proof BB 2.1.5 is supplied rather than a citation.

### C2. Standard parabolic subgroups, quotients, and double cosets

Definition contracts: `W_J`, right-descents, left-descents, `W^J`, `^J W`, quotient SET versus quotient GROUP, actual left coset `wW_J`, right coset `W_Jw`; fix notation consistently. A nonnormal parabolic quotient is not a group.

Supplier chain (BB §2.4): deletion → ambient/restricted length equality; support invariance → `W_I∩W_J=W_{I∩J}`; exchange characterization of Coxeter systems → inherited parabolic Coxeter presentation (or direct Matsumoto/support proof of presentation injectivity). Descents-free characterization uses “no reduced expression ends/begins in J,” not one arbitrarily chosen expression.

Prove unique length-additive factorization `w=u v`, `u∈W^J`, `v∈W_J`: repeatedly strip right descents in J; termination at most `ell(w)`; length additivity also requires the collected word for v has the counted reduced length. For uniqueness BB 2.4.4 extracts a reduced subword of `u v y^{-1}` for another descents-free representative x; it must avoid all suffix J letters, hence x≤u, and symmetry yields equality. Consequently multiplication `W^J×W_J→W` is a bijection; length addition for ALL `u∈W^J,v∈W_J` is then obtained by uniqueness applied to the actual product. Prove projection to W^J is Bruhat order-preserving using right lifting and induction (BB 2.5.1). Quotient saturated chains require the additional augmentation-in-quotient check BB 2.5.5.

Double-coset minimum route, independent elementary elaboration of the premise used in Stembridge §1.3: strip length-decreasing left I and right J descents to reach u∈`^I W^J`. Prove u≤every x∈`W_IuW_J`: along ANY sequence of left I and right J multiplications from u, maintain u≤current w. Ascents preserve it by transitivity; descents preserve it by lifting because u has no corresponding I/J descent. Thus u is the unique Bruhat minimum and therefore unique length minimum. This avoids a root-theoretic parabolic-intersection assumption. If v is another descents-free representative, the same reasoning gives v≤u≤v. Do not claim arbitrary triple factors `a u b` are simultaneously unique or length-additive. A refined parametrization needs a separately proved conjugated-parabolic intersection theorem and transversal restriction.

B companion: Young subgroups as consecutive-position-block permutations; coset minima by sorting each position block; Grassmannian representatives and partitions in a rectangle. For the latter define partitions, coordinate order, and the bijection explicitly; prove its inverse and inversion/area equality. Double-coset type-A contingency matrices: map a permutation to intersection counts of I-value and J-position blocks; prove row/column sums, orbit invariance, surjectivity by ordered allocation, and injectivity up to both block actions. This is an elementary classification supplier, not a count asserted from a diagram.

### C3. Weak order, reflection inversion sets, and lattice structure

BB §3.2 closure route: choose z of maximal length among common lower bounds of x,y (this finite set is contained in the finite Bruhat interval below x). Show every common initial simple generator is below z, otherwise exchange extends z to a longer common lower bound. ### C5. Heaps and fully commutative elements

Heap definition on POSITIONS of a word: take reflexive transitive closure of `i<j` with equal or noncommuting labels (`m(s_i,s_j)≠2`). Antisymmetry follows strictly increasing position on every nontrivial path. Repeated equal labels are comparable. Define heap equivalence by label-preserving poset isomorphism, not literal equality of position sets. Show a commuting adjacent swap gives an isomorphic heap; incomparable positions have distinct commuting labels. All labeled linear extensions are exactly the commutation class: use terminal maximal-position induction as Stembridge 1.2, or prove any two linear extensions connect by adjacent incomparable swaps. ### CG16: absolute order and moved-space rigidity

The following is a complete elementary route for absolute-order transitivity, independent of geometry. If `u≤_T v≤_T w`, concatenate reduced factorizations for u, u^{-1}v, v^{-1}w. Triangle inequality gives `ell_T(w)≤ell_T(u)+ell_T(u^{-1}w)≤ell_T(u)+ell_T(u^{-1}v)+ell_T(v^{-1}w)=ell_T(w)`. Both inequalities are equalities, proving transitivity. Reflexivity uses the empty word, and antisymmetry follows from the strict increase of length unless the complementary factor is e. Every reduced T-word has reduced prefixes and factors: otherwise replace a nonminimal factor to shorten the whole word. Thus absolute order is graded, covers differ by one in reflection length, and its intervals are finite when W is finite. Conjugation and inversion preserve reflection length because T is conjugation-stable and consists of involutions.

Reordering any selected subword into a prefix requires Hurwitz moves, NOT Coxeter-generator exchange: adjacent reflections `a b` can be replaced by `b (b a b)` or `(a b a) a`. Both preserve product and remain reflection letters; repeated moves bring any selected subsequence to a prefix, conjugating unselected factors as needed. Supply the exact direction and verify the selected-factor product remains unchanged. Then the absolute-order subword characterization follows. Do not quote the weak-order subword theorem here.

Geometric interface requires separately proved faithful positive-definite reflection realization, finite W reflection-length equality `ell_T(w)=dim M(w)`, and the orthogonal-group restriction theorem from Brady–Watt's refs [7] and [8]. Those two papers were not accessed in this assignment, so these imports are NOT source-closed by reading math/0501502. State exact required claim: for fixed A∈O(V), and subspace U⊂M(A), there is a UNIQUE orthogonal B≤A with M(B)=U, where ≤ is orthogonal reflection-length prefix order. In W the B need not belong to W. This uniqueness gives, for α,β below the SAME upper element δ, `M(α)⊂M(β) iff α≤β`; it does NOT give this equivalence for arbitrary α,β. Prove orthogonal identities `M(A)=F(A)^perp`, `M(A^{-1})=M(A)`, rank bounds and product length before using the theorem. If a Wall-form proof is chosen, define the restricted bilinear form, prove nondegeneracy and its orthogonal compatibility, construct B explicitly, and prove uniqueness; naming “Wall form” alone is insufficient.

A reflection R(root)≤α iff its normal lies in M(α) depends on the restriction theorem plus reflection-length equality and root realization; prove both implications before using root sets to characterize elements. The formula `R(v)α=αR(α^{-1}v)` is just conjugation and does not itself need v∈M(α); the length/decreasing-rank conclusions do.

### CG18: sortable recursion, quotient homomorphism and exact imported gaps

A sorting word is a lexicographically minimum tuple of positions of FIXED length ell(w), among embeddings of reduced words in c-infinity. It exists because any finite reduced word embeds by taking successive occurrences, and a nonempty subset of N^k has a lex minimum by k successive least choices; N^k lex order is not globally a well-order for arbitrary variable/infinite tuples. Prove each greedy selected letter is a left descent of the remaining group element; this gives a concrete scanning algorithm and proves the existence of a reduced completion. Stop when remainder=e. Every reduced expression for c uses each generator once; no long braid can be applied to it, so Matsumoto implies all its reduced expressions differ by commutations only. Adjacent commuting swaps inside EACH repeated block induce sorting-word swaps without crossing dividers; give the local greediness proof, then sortable block-inclusion is independent of the chosen reduced c-word.
