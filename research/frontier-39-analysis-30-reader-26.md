## Mathematical evidence and edits

1. **Inversion: false local-to-global prerequisite, repaired without narrowing the theorem.** In `thm-lca-fourier-inversion-for-integrable-transform`, old [F7] asserted that a locally integrable function annihilating every C_c test must vanish globally almost everywhere, and old proof 2.1 used it. The repository's Radon convention requires outer regularity on Borel sets and inner regularity on open sets, not arbitrary Borel inner regularity. Those hypotheses do not justify the asserted global density-measure uniqueness.

   A direct counterexample is G = R × D with D an uncountable discrete abelian group and E = {0} × D. Every compact set projects into finitely many points of D, so E meets each compact set in finitely many Haar-null singletons. Thus w = 1_E is locally integrable and every compactly supported test pairs to zero. But every open set containing E has positive measure in each of uncountably many disjoint open cosets R × {d}. Such an open set has infinite measure: among uncountably many positive masses some threshold 1/n is exceeded infinitely often, and finite additivity gives arbitrarily large lower bounds. Outer regularity therefore gives m(E) = infinity. Consequently w does not vanish globally almost everywhere. Compact localizations of w m cannot recover that global information, and the published RMK uniqueness theorem does not license the old step.

   Authoritative confirmation read: Yulia Kuznetsova, *On continuity of measurable group representations and homomorphisms*, arXiv:1010.0999v3, Section 1, “Definitions and notations”, printed p. 3, paragraphs distinguishing outer-regular Haar measures from inner-regular Haar measures and defining locally null sets (PDF page index 2, extracted lines 89–101). The source states that the former convention can have locally null measurable sets of infinite measure, whereas the two conventions agree in the sigma-finite case. URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.0999 . This was the complete relevant source passage; the unrelated automatic-continuity arguments were not needed or audited.

   Repair: [F7] now cites the Riesz–Fischer subsequence theorem, with complex functions handled through real and imaginary parts and arbitrary specified representatives handled by a countable union of null sets. Proof 1.4 now explicitly combines uniform convergence with an L1 subsequence to establish that H_U represents f*q_U. Proof 2.1 obtains uniform H_U → f^vee from ||fhat(qhat_U − 1)||_1 → 0 and L1 f*q_U → f, selects countably many admissible pairs achieving both errors, and applies a globally a.e. convergent L1 subsequence. No sigma-finiteness or countable neighbourhood base is assumed. Proof 3.1 explicitly proves equality at a continuity point of an arbitrary representative by Haar positivity.
