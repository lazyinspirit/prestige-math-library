# Divisor Voronoï / exponent-pair source-chain review

**Review date:** 2026-10-23. **Owner:** analytic-NT R3. **Purpose:** supplementary source review for R1's `exponent-pairs-and-classical-voronoi-summation` and `dirichlet-divisor-and-gauss-circle-discrepancy` pairs. This note adds no inventory or library item and does not edit R1's files.

## Finding

The primary proof-source gap for the **pointwise divisor Voronoï identity is closed** by Tom Meurman's complete short proof, read in full below. Its theorem is for every `x>0`, includes the half-weight at integral `x`, gives an explicit conditional integral kernel, and proves bounded convergence on compact positive intervals and uniform convergence away from integers. The conversion of this integral kernel to the familiar `K₁,Y₁` expression is stated in the same paper with a Watson citation; it is not used in Meurman's proof.

The broader **weighted finite-interval `K₀,Y₀` formula** has an exact authoritative statement in Ivić's Chapter 3 and a proof for a restricted Laplace-transform test class in Egger–Steiner arXiv:1001.3556v4. Ivić's chapter explicitly delegates the difficult convergence details at integral points to Dixon–Ferrar/Titchmarsh; Egger–Steiner's Theorem 1.1 states but does not prove Hejhal's all-`C²_c(ℝ)` version, and its own Theorem 2.6 proves only the stated Laplace-transform class. Thus a weighted identity for smooth compact support strictly inside `(0,∞)` is buildable from Meurman's pointwise identity by distributional differentiation and the displayed Bessel recurrences; the full finite-interval `C²[a,b]` endpoint-half theorem still needs the endpoint extension proof to be stated locally if that is the intended library item.

The initial Chandrasekharan source search remains part of the access history: K. It is not needed as the pointwise proof supplier now that Meurman's full article is local.

## Primary pointwise proof: Meurman

- **Source:** Tom Meurman, “A simple proof of Voronoi's identity,” *Astérisque* 209 (1992), 265–274, full publisher/JSTOR-hosted scan at <https://numdam.org/item/AST_1992__209__265_0.pdf> (record: <http://www.numdam.org/item?id=AST_1992__209__265_0>).
- **Local full text:** `meurman-asterisque-209-voronoi-1992.pdf/.txt` (11 physical PDF pages; complete printed article pp. 265–274, all §§1–8 and references read).
- **SHA-256:** PDF `8470bd54a894b5fe4e727b82eabf759501e5dd08598020499c532ce506b44526`; text `b41b35ba2559e91750bde5f45b0462b986a7a8e18ee03253d66418275fd41d8b`.
- **Exact normalization and theorem (printed p. 265, (1.1)–(1.2)):** with `d(x)=0` unless `x` is an integer, define
  `Δ_M(x) := Σ_{n<x} d(n) + (1/2)d(x) − x log x − (2γ−1)x − 1/4`.
  Then for every real `x>0`,
  `Δ_M(x) = (2/π) Σ_{m≥1} [d(m)/m] ∫₀^∞ cos(2πu) sin(2πmx/u) du`.
  The series is boundedly convergent on every compact interval `[x₁,x₂]⊂(0,∞)` and uniformly convergent on such intervals that contain no integers. This is an endpoint-averaged pointwise identity; at nonintegral `x`, `Δ_M(x)` differs from R1's unstarred `Σ_{n≤x}d(n)−x(log x+2γ−1)` normalization by the fixed `−1/4` constant.
- **Proof harvest:** §2 introduces a damping parameter `v∈[0,1/2]` and the integral `F(t,v)=∫₀∞e^{-v(u+t/u)}cos(2πu)sin(2πt/u)du`, proves its `v→0+` limit, and reduces the identity to a uniform stationary-phase estimate. §3 proves that estimate by splitting the cosine/sine product, integrating the nonstationary term by parts, and expanding about the stationary point `u=1`; the error is uniform in `v`. §4 expands the damped divisor sum as an absolutely convergent double integral/geometric-series expression. §§5–6 truncate at `N>2x`, bound the tail, and evaluate the sawtooth/main integral. §7 treats separately `x/n∉ℤ` and `x/n∈ℤ`, derives the uniform approximation of `A(x,v)` by `A(x)`, and passes `v→0+`. §8 proves bounded convergence of the undamped series by partial summation and controls the term closest to `n=x`.
- **Bessel normalization (printed pp. 266–267):** Meurman states that the integral in (1.2) equals `−√(mx)[K₁(4π√(mx))+(π/2)Y₁(4π√(mx))]`, citing Watson, *A Treatise on the Theory of Bessel Functions*, 2nd ed. This conversion is not used in Meurman's proof. It agrees with Ivić (3.1) and the standard pointwise divisor Voronoï formula.
- **Disposition:** full proof supplier for the pointwise endpoint-averaged identity and convergence contract. A DDP application can consume this exact statement; do not silently erase the half endpoint or the `−1/4` normalization shift.

## Exact existing-library reuse and remaining suppliers

The local library has a useful elementary base but no divisor-Voronoï theorem:

- `thm-dirichlet-hyperbola-method`, `thm-divisor-counting-summatory-estimate`, and `cor-average-order-of-divisor-counting-function` prove the standard `O(√x)` divisor-summatory remainder. This is an elementary baseline, not a Voronoï estimate.
- `cor-divisor-dirichlet-series-zeta-square` supplies the coefficient identity for `ζ(s)^2` in its half-plane of absolute convergence.
- `thm-riemann-zeta-functional-equation`, `thm-completed-riemann-zeta-functional-equation`, `thm-riemann-zeta-meromorphic-continuation`, the Mellin representation of completed zeta, and the Perron items provide pieces of a possible contour proof. They do not by themselves prove the required contour shifts, convergence of the transformed series, or identify its Bessel kernel.
- `thm-two-square-representation-count` gives the exact arithmetic relation `r₂(n)=4 Σ_{d|n}χ₄(d)`, and `cor-average-order-of-two-square-representations` gives `Σ_{n≤x}r₂(n)=πx+O(√x)`. Since the full lattice count includes `(0,0)`, the R1 normalization `R(x)=#{(a,b)∈Z²:a²+b²≤x}−πx` is `R(x)=1+Σ_{1≤n≤x}r₂(n)−πx` (with the usual real-cutoff convention). The existing corollary is only the `O(√x)` baseline.
- `thm-poisson-summation-for-schwartz-functions` and `thm-poisson-summation-for-a-full-rank-lattice` supply the general Poisson framework. Miller–Schmid §6 supplies the full specific smoothing/Fourier/Bessel estimates for the classical circle `O_ε(x^(1/3+ε))` route.
- `lem-bessel-integral-representations-and-scaled-range` covers integer-order `J_n` and `I_n` representations. It does **not** cover the `K₀,Y₀` divisor kernel, Mellin transforms for that kernel, or its oscillatory asymptotics. The GL2 spectral/Bessel interfaces in R4 are different transforms and are not direct suppliers for this GL1 divisor identity.

For a fully proved divisor-transform item, the minimum local chain still needs the exact test-function and endpoint-averaging convention; a derivation of the `ζ(s)^2` functional-equation multiplier and residue at `s=1`; justified Perron/Mellin inversion and contour displacement for the stated smooth compactly supported test function; an evaluated inverse Mellin transform giving `4K₀(4π√{nx})−2πY₀(4π√{nx})`; and convergence/regularization for the resulting `d(n)` series. To obtain a truncated pointwise formula for `Δ(x)`, the truncation error and integer endpoint correction also need their own statement. The source preview is insufficient to check these obligations. The circle route should remain separate: its Miller–Schmid Poisson proof does not logically require the divisor Voronoï theorem.
