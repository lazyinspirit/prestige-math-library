# R2: sieve methods, prime distribution, and bounded gaps

Research dossier for the new analytic-number-theory category. This is a finite, buildable A/B plan; it creates no library items and no shared plan-spec. The unique machine-readable fragments are r2-sources/pair-fragment.json and r2-sources/proposed-inventories.json.

## Scope, reuse, and ownership

The proposed pathway is: finite inclusion–exclusion and local-density sieve data → Brun, Selberg, linear, and weighted sieves → additive/multiplicative large sieve and finite-group spectral sieve → Siegel–Walfisz and Bombieri–Vinogradov (BV) → GPY and Maynard–Tao criteria → Chen’s weighted switching theorem and Rankin-type prime-gap constructions. The finite residue-class algorithm and its correctness theorem are separate inventory entries. Conjectures and research-status remarks are nonlogical endpoints.

Reuse the exact existing items rather than restating them: def-prime, def-prime-counting-function, def-von-mangoldt-function, def-chebyshev-psi-function, def-chebyshev-theta-function, def-number-theoretic-mobius-function, cor-classical-mobius-inversion, cor-von-mangoldt-mobius-inversion-formula, thm-canonical-prime-factorisation, def-unit-group-modulo-n-and-euler-totient, def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q, thm-dirichlet-character-orthogonality, thm-prime-number-theorem-arithmetic-progressions, thm-prime-number-theorem-logarithmic-integral, cor-chebyshev-theta-prime-number-theorem-error, def-a-sieve-family-and-its-intersections, thm-inclusion-exclusion, thm-the-bonferroni-inequalities, cor-inclusion-exclusion-is-boolean-mobius-inversion, lem-finite-sum-reindexing-and-fubini, thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm, thm-bessel-inequality-and-finite-parseval-identity, def-additive-character-of-a-finite-abelian-group, lem-additive-character-orthogonality-from-representation-orthogonality, and thm-standard-representatives-modulo-n.

thm-prime-number-theorem-arithmetic-progressions is explicitly fixed-modulus only; it is not a supplier for SW or BV. R1 owns the zero-free, Landau–Page, ineffective Siegel, and primitive-character explicit-formula suppliers. The SW item depends on R1’s A-page suppliers; BV uses SW, the multiplicative large sieve, Vaughan decomposition, and the prime-power/PNT error estimate. BV does not require the R1 zero-density pair. R3 owns Green–Tao, transference, Gowers/relative Szemerédi, finite-field trace functions, and Deligne inputs. R4 owns automorphic/property-τ spectral applications. R2’s “spectral sieve” scope is Kowalski’s finite-group/conjugacy/coset framework; it does not claim an automorphic or Deligne-based spectral sieve.

The 2026 claims in the status ledger are provenance/status records, never logical suppliers. In particular, the newly found 182 source is not a proposed theorem item.

### Contract details that matter at build time

- For SW, independently quantify modulus exponent A and requested error power B, but hold both fixed before x grows. The Λ-sum is over all integers, including prime powers. The residue maximum is over units only. q=1 is included with its one residue class. Use the R1 truncated explicit formula with its own half-weighted endpoint convention internally, then explicitly convert to the full-weighted progression sum at cost O(log x); do not conflate those conventions.
- For BV, specify B=B(A), not a freely chosen pair. The classical statement is an average of maxima over reduced classes. There is no outer max over y≤x in the item. Stronger E*(x,q)=max over y≤x of E′(y,q) formulations in GPY/Polymath are separate results.
- Maynard’s Definition (1.3) is the exact level contract: for fixed θ>0 and every A>0,
  Σ_{q≤x^θ} max_{(a,q)=1}|π(x;q,a)−π(x)/φ(q)|≪_A x(log x)^−A.
  The theorem proof then inserts a fixed W-pre-sieve and needs the corresponding uniformity in the reduced residue classes used by the weighted tuple sum. The threshold M_k>2m/θ is not a stand-alone implication from M_k; it is a theorem with these distribution hypotheses. Do not silently add a logarithmic cutoff to the published level definition; for EH, only each fixed θ<1 is conjectured.
- The library should not turn Polymath8b §8’s heuristic discussion into an impossibility theorem for every sieve method. Its no-go discussion is specifically about the linear-sieve information class and sieve arguments described there.

## 2025 smooth-modulus theorem suppliers

These are proved theorem items on the new pair `smooth-modulus-prime-distribution-and-harman-gaps` / `smooth-modulus-prime-distribution-examples`. The complete Type I/II/III and Harman proof chain is part of the A-page scope; the results are not merely a dated status note.

- For a coefficient sequence f supported on [x,2x], define
  Δ(f;a mod q)=Σ_{n≡a (q)}f(n)−φ(q)⁻¹Σ_{(n,q)=1}f(n). Its exponent θ to smooth moduli means: for every ε>0 there exists δ=δ(ε)>0 such that for every A>0, uniformly in a coprime to P(x^δ),
  Σ_{q≤x^(θ−ε), q|P(x^δ)} |Δ(f;a mod q)| ≪_{ε,A} x(log x)^−A.
  Here P(z)=∏_{p<z}p, so q is squarefree and z-smooth. Include q=1. This is Stadlmann’s and Baker–Irving’s function-level definition; it is distinct from the classical all-moduli BV cutoff.
- A coefficient sequence β at scale N has the Polymath8a Siegel–Walfisz property when, for every q,r≥1, primitive a mod q, and fixed A>0,
  |Δ(β·1_{(·,r)=1};a mod q)| ≪ τ(qr)^O(1)N(log x)^−A.
  Its implied constants are uniform in q,r,a, with the displayed divisor factor. The Type I/II estimate assumes this property for β; the Heath–Brown coefficient-factor verification is a separate theorem item using R1’s zero-free, Landau–Page, ineffective Siegel, primitive-L analytic continuation/functional equation, truncated Perron, and character-orthogonality inputs.
- For y≥1, i-tuply y-densely divisible means recursively: for each j+k=i−1 and 1≤R≤yn, factor n=qr with y^−1R≤r≤R, q j-tuply and r k-tuply y-densely divisible. Every y-smooth n has this property for every i. The imported Polymath Type III statement uses squarefree q in D_I^(1)(x^δ), q≪x^(1/2+2ω), with a primitive class mod P_I.
- Polymath8a Theorem 2.8(v), proved in its §7, gives Type III for MN₁N₂N₃≈x, N_iN_j≫x^(1/2+σ) for i≠j, x^(2σ)≪N_i≪x^(1/2−σ), smooth ψ_i, and 0<ω<1/12, σ>1/18+28ω/9+2δ. For every A>0 the reduced-class discrepancy sum is ≪x(log x)^−A. The required Type I/II supply is instead Stadlmann Proposition 1; do not substitute Polymath8a’s older estimates for it.
- Stadlmann Proposition 1 assumes
  72ω+24δ<1, 48ω+16δ+4σ<1, and 64ω+20δ+2σ<1.
  For coefficient sequences α,β at M,N with MN≈x, x^(1/2−σ)≤N≤x^(1/2), M≈x/N, and β Siegel–Walfisz, it proves
  Σ_{q≤x^(1/2+2ω), q|P(x^δ), (q,a)=1}|Δ(α*β;a mod q)|≪_{A,ω,δ}x(log x)^−A.
  The implied constant may depend on the coefficient and SW constants, not on the particular sequence values. This is Stadlmann’s Type∞ I/II estimate.
- Stadlmann Lemma 1 combines that estimate and Type^(1) III into MPZ[ω,δ]. Corollary 2 permits 80ω+(80/3)δ<1. The local finite Heath–Brown identity is
  Λ=Σ_{j=1}^k(−1)^(j−1) C(k,j) μ^{*j}*1^{*(j−1)}*log
  for fixed k≥1; its expansion supplies the Type I/II/III coefficient convolutions, whose Siegel–Walfisz factors are verified separately. Partial summation, the prime-power ledger, and the MPZ conversion prove Theorem 1: for every ε>0 some δ(ε)>0 exists such that, for all A>0 and every integer a,
  Σ_{q≤x^(1/2+1/40−ε), q|P(x^δ), (q,a)=1}|π(x;q,a)−π(x;q)/φ(q)|≪_{A,ε}x(log x)^−A,
  where π(x;q)=Σ_{p≤x,(p,q)=1}1. This includes q=1 and is a smooth-modulus average, not arbitrary moduli through x^(1/2+1/40).
- For Theorem 2, Stadlmann Proposition 2 gives an explicit minorant ρ(n;x)≤1_P(n), supported on numbers all of whose prime factors exceed x^(1/5−2η) with η=0.00481, with exponent θ=0.5253 to smooth moduli and Σ_{x≤n≤2x}ρ(n;x)≥(1−2·10^−5+o(1))x/log x. Its two subtracted Buchstab regions are exactly the regions printed in Proposition 2 (local source pp. 9–10): first n=p₁p₂p₃p₄n₅ with 0.19038≤α₄<α₃<α₂<α₁<0.40481, α₁+α₂<0.40481, α₂+α₃+α₄>0.59519; second n=p₂p₃p₄p₅p₆ with 0.19038≤α₂,…,α₆≤0.23848, α₂,α₄>α₃, α₂+α₄<0.40481, α₂+α₃+α₅>0.59519, α₆≥α₅. Here α_i=log p_i/log x and ψ(n,y)=1 iff every prime divisor of n is ≥y.
- Baker–Irving Lemma 1 says that such a minorant with prime-factor cutoff x^ξ, smooth-modulus exponent θ, and mass (1−c₁+o(1))x/log x gives H_m≪_{c₀}exp(c₀m) for every c₀>2/(θ(1−c₁)). Their proof adapts Maynard’s divisor-sum weight and uses the M_k≥log k−O(1) lower bound; this raw variational bound is separately inventoried. With θ=0.5253 and c₁=2·10^−5, 2/(θ(1−c₁))<3.8075, proving Stadlmann Theorem 2, H_m≪exp(3.8075m).

The finite-field proof edges are local R2 items: rank-three Kloosterman sheaf rank/conductor/connected monodromy; the squarefree CRT correlation (Polymath8a Lemma 6.25 and Corollary 6.26); and the rational-phase incomplete-sum/q-van-der-Corput specialization (Polymath8a Proposition 4.12/Corollary 4.16 and Stadlmann Lemmas 12–13). These depend on R3’s `thm-deligne-quasi-orthogonality-for-geometrically-irreducible-trace-functions`. The generic Deligne theorem alone does not assert the composite-modulus or rational-phase estimates.

The R2 finite-field item contracts follow the source statements. In Corollary 6.26, for squarefree s,r₁,r₂ with (s,r₁r₂)=1, a_i∈(ℤ/r_i sℤ)^×, and a smooth cutoff Ψ_H with H≪(s[r₁,r₂])^O(1), the sum over (h,s[r₁,r₂])=1 of Ψ_H(h)Kl₃(a₁h;r₁s)Kl₃(a₂h;r₂s) is
≪(s[r₁,r₂])^ε(H/([r₁,r₂]s)+1)s^(1/2)[r₁,r₂]^(1/2)(a₂−a₁,r₁,r₂)^(1/2)(a₂r₁³−a₁r₂³,s)^(1/2).
Its proof is the CRT factorization of Lemma 6.25, Deligne’s complete rank-three correlation, completion (Lemma 4.9), and Möbius inversion for the coprimality cutoff.

For the rational-phase item, Polymath8a Proposition 4.12 assumes squarefree q, f=P/Q∈ℚ(X) with P,Q∈ℤ[X], deg P<deg Q and deg(Q mod p)=deg Q for every p|q. For smooth compactly supported ψ_N at scale N≪q^O(1), it gives both Pólya–Vinogradov/Weil completion and one q-van-der-Corput bounds when q=rs. Corollary 4.16 specializes this to q=[d₁,d₂] y-densely divisible, d|q and n≡a mod d, with phases e_{d₁}(c₁/(n+l₁))e_{d₂}(c₂/(n+l₂)); writing δ_i=d_i/(d₁,d₂) and δ'_i=δ_i/(d,δ_i), the first bound is
≪q^ε(d^−1/2N^1/2q^1/6y^1/6+d^−1(c₁,δ'_1)/δ'_1·(c₂,δ'_2)/δ'_2·N),
and the variant replaces q^1/6y^1/6 with q^1/2. These are the precise complete-sum inputs that Stadlmann modifies in Lemmas 12–13; they are not implicit consequences of citing Deligne alone.

## Open problems and pathway note

The open-problem items have these exact meanings, and are never theorem dependencies:

- **Twin primes:** H₁=2, where H₁=liminfₙ(pₙ₊₁−pₙ).
- **Hardy–Littlewood prime tuples:** for every fixed admissible finite tuple H, the conjectured asymptotic is `sum_{n <= x} product_{h in H} 1_P(n+h) ~ S(H) x/(log x)^|H|`, with the usual singular series.
- **Elliott–Halberstam EH(θ):** for each fixed 0<θ<1 and A≥1, with `Lambda_[x,2x](n) = Lambda(n) 1_[x,2x](n)`, the discrepancy bound is
  `sum_{q <= x^θ} max_{(a,q)=1} |Delta(Lambda_[x,2x]; a mod q)| <<_A x/(log x)^A`.
  The conjecture asserts EH(θ) for every fixed θ<1; it does not assert the endpoint θ=1.
- **Generalized Elliott–Halberstam GEH(θ):** A4 definition item `def-generalized-elliott-halberstam-hypothesis` states that, for each fixed 0<θ<1, ε>0, A≥1, sequences α,β supported on [N,2N] and [M,2M], with x^ε ≪ N,M ≪ x^(1−ε), NM ≈ x, divisor bounds `|alpha(n)|, |beta(n)| << tau(n)^O(1)(log x)^O(1)`, and, for every q,r≥1 and primitive a mod q, the Siegel–Walfisz condition
  `|Delta(beta·1_(gcd(·,r)=1); a mod q)| << tau(qr)^O(1) M/(log x)^A`,
  the convolution discrepancy satisfies
  `sum_{q <= x^θ} max_{(a,q)=1} |Delta(alpha*beta; a mod q)| <<_A x/(log x)^A`.
  The open-problem item `op-generalized-elliott-halberstam-conjecture` depends only on this definition. GEH is conjectured for every fixed θ<1. Maynard’s conditional H₁≤12 theorem assumes EH(θ) for every θ<1; Polymath8b’s DHL[3,2] result and H₁≤6 consequence state GEH(θ) explicitly as a hypothesis (their proofs note that a single sufficiently large fixed θ suffices); they do not depend on the open-problem item.

The interval between the peer-reviewed explicit H₁≤246 bound and newer 2026 source claims remains a research-status matter; no 2026 claim is silently promoted to the theorem inventory. The source report contains the category overview and pathway, but no library _pathway carrier is proposed.

## Chen weighted sieve and switching pair

The A page chen-double-sieve-and-almost-primes has a linear item chain in the order listed in r2-sources/proposed-inventories.json. The B page contains only examples and source/status remarks. These are the build contracts:

1. def-prime-factor-count-with-multiplicity defines Ω(n)=Σₚ vₚ(n). Use Pⱼ={n>1 : Ω(n)≤j}; repeated prime factors count repeatedly and 1 is excluded. Thus the main Chen conclusion says “prime or a product of two primes.”
2. lem-chen-singular-series-specialization identifies the local product for fixed even h≠0:
   \[
   \mathfrak S(h)=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac{1}{(p-1)^2}\right)
   \prod_{\substack{p\mid h\\p>2}}\frac{p-1}{p-2}.
   \]
   The even Goldbach specialization uses h=N. State absolute convergence and positivity; the local factor at 2 is already incorporated in this normalization.
3. def-linear-sieve-level-of-distribution packages the sequence, multiplicative local density, level and remainder sums. thm-linear-sieve-fundamental-lemma is the two-sided approximation of the sifted sum by its Euler-product main term with level cutoff and uniform remainder budget explicit. Its proof obligations include the finite Brun/Richert weights and the general-dimensional fundamental-lemma construction. It is not a lower bound for arbitrary sequences without a distribution hypothesis.
4. def-chen-kuehn-weighted-sifted-sum states Kuhn’s weight and sifted sum over |p+h|, including the prime-factor intervals, multiplicity convention, and parameters u,v,λ. thm-kuehn-weighted-prime-translate-sieve reproduces Richert’s Theorem 12.2: for even h, 0<|h|≤x, fixed 2<u<v, 4≤v≤8 and λ≥2, the weighted sifted sum over p≤x is bounded below by
   \[
   \frac{4\mathfrak S(h)x}{\log^2 x}\left(
   \log\!\left(\frac v2-1\right)
   -\frac1\lambda\log\!\left(\frac{v-2}{u-2}\right)
   +O_{u,v}((\log x)^{-1/15})\right).
   \]
   The item statement must reproduce Richert’s exact weight, not just this lower-bound formula. Its proof uses the linear-sieve fundamental lemma, Bombieri–Vinogradov, and the Siegel–Walfisz small-modulus estimate.
5. thm-chen-weighted-sifted-lower-bound records the corrected proof’s explicit specialization W(N)≥2.6408 S(N)N/log²N for sufficiently large even N. W(N) counts the sifted candidates with Chen’s specified one-prime medium range.
6. thm-chen-switched-triple-product-upper-bound proves Ω_switch(N)≤3.94033 S(N)N/log²N for the switched count of representations N−a p₃ prime, where a=p₁p₂ lies in Chen’s factor ranges. Its dependencies include the Selberg upper sieve, multiplicative large sieve, BV, and SW. lem-chen-switching-count-lower-bound uses the fact that each three-factor obstruction contributes to the switch at least twice, so the number of surviving P₂ representations is at least
   \[
   W(N)-\frac12\Omega_{\rm switch}(N)
   \ge (2.6408-3.94033/2)\mathfrak S(N)\frac{N}{\log^2N}
   >0.67\mathfrak S(N)\frac{N}{\log^2N}.
   \]
   Strict positivity is a finite numerical inequality; the theorem does not infer that P₂ is prime.
7. thm-chen-goldbach-prime-plus-p2 states Chen’s quantitative lower count for all sufficiently large even N and hence N=p+P₂. thm-chen-prime-shift-plus-p2 states that, for every fixed nonzero even h, infinitely many primes p satisfy p+h∈P₂, with the source’s positive lower count. The open Goldbach and twin-prime conjectures remain separate; neither follows from these items.
8. def-weighted-switching-level-hypotheses encodes Matomäki–Zúñiga-Alterman, Definition 2.4 and Assumption 3.1, for a set A⊆([1,x]∩Z)² and parameters δ>0, S∈N, v≥u≥2, v≥S+1. For each original or switched sequence (vₙ)ₙ≤x, density g multiplicative with 0≤g(p)<p and size X, level θ means
   \[
   \sum_{d\le x^\theta}\mu^2(d)
   \left|\sum_{n\le x/d}v_{dn}-\frac{g(d)}dX\right|
   =O\!\left(\frac{X}{(\log x)^{100}}\right),
   \]
   together with the local product bound
   \[
   \prod_{w\le p<z}\left(1-\frac{g(p)}p\right)^{-1}
   \le\left(1+O((\log w)^{-1})\right)\frac{\log z}{\log w}
   \quad(z>w\ge2).
   \]
   Here the implied constants are fixed parts of the contract. Define aₙ=Σ_{p≤x,(p,n)∈A}1, and
   \[
   W(n)=1-\sum_{\substack{p\mid n\\x^{1/v}\le p<x^{1/u}}}
       w\!\left(\frac{\log p}{\log x}\right),
   \]
   where w:R≥0→[0,1] vanishes off [1/v,1/u]. θ₁∈[1/u+δ,1); θ₂:[1/v,1/(S+1)]→[δ,1); X₁≥1 and X₂(α)≥1. The formal assumptions are:
   - A1: (aₙ) has level θ₁, size X₁, density g₁.
   - A2: W(n)1_(n,P(x^(1/v)))=1≤0 whenever ω(n)>R.
   - A3: Σ_{p≥x^(1/v)} Σ_{n≤x/p²,(n,P(x^(1/v)))=1} a_(np²)≤X₁/(log x)².
   - A4: θ₂ and w are Lipschitz, and for each α∈[1/v,1/(S+1)], the switched sequence
     \[
     b_{P,\ell}=\sum_{\substack{p_1m\le x\\(\ell,p_1m)\in A\\P<p_1\le2P\\p\mid m\Rightarrow p>p_1\\S\le\Omega(m)\le R-1}}
       \left(|\mu(m)|W_P^+(m)+\frac{C_w}{\log x}\right),
     \quad P=x^\alpha,
     \]
     has level θ₂(α), size X₂(α), density g₂; W_P is the source’s local approximation to W(p₁m).
   - A5: for each such α, X₂(α)=(1+o(1))(X₁/π(x)) times the sum of |μ(m)|W_(x^α)^+(m)+C_w/log x over p₁m≤x, x^α<p₁≤2x^α, every prime divisor of m greater than p₁, and S≤Ω(m)≤R−1.
   
   Theorem 3.2 specializes to S=3,v=20,u=6, w=(1/2)1_[1/20,1/6], θ₁=θ₂(α)=0.267 and g₁=g₂, and concludes Σ_(p,P₃)∈A 1≫X₁/log x. This is conditional on the original and switched level/density hypotheses, not an unconditional level statement.
   
   Source regularity issue: Assumption 3.1 requires w to be Lipschitz, while Theorem 3.2 prints the discontinuous step profile above. Section 7 directly evaluates the step-weight integrals, but the broad Assumption 3.1 proof uses C_w and its Lipschitz property. Keep the 0.267 theorem as an A result only with an explicit proof obligation to justify this specialization (for example, a smooth approximation plus endpoint-error estimates); do not treat it as a literal instance of A1–A5 without that argument.

## Rankin, Erdős–Rankin, and large-prime-gap pair

The A page rankin-erdos-rankin-large-prime-gaps has these contracts in dependency order:

1. def-iterated-logarithms-and-prime-gap-scale defines log₁x=log x, logⱼ₊₁x=log(logⱼx), for sufficiently large x, and
   \[
   L(x)=\frac{\log x\,\log_2 x\,\log_4 x}{(\log_3 x)^2}.
   \]
2. def-prime-gap-functions lets pₙ be the increasing sequence of primes and defines
   \[
   G(X)=\max_{p_{n+1}\le X}(p_{n+1}-p_n),\qquad
   G_k(X)=\max_{p_{n+k}\le X}\min_{1\le i\le k}(p_{n+i}-p_{n+i-1}).
   \]
   Thus Gₖ describes a chain of k consecutive gaps.
3. def-prime-residue-covering-length defines P(x)=∏_{p≤x}p and Y(x) as the largest H for which one residue class aₚ mod p for every prime p≤x covers [1,H].
4. lem-crt-residue-covering-to-prime-gap says that if such classes cover [1,H], CRT gives m∈(x,x+P(x)] with m≡−aₚ mod p; every m+j, 1≤j≤H, is composite. Hence G(P(x)+H+x)≥H, with the endpoint convention matching G.
5. lem-rankin-covering-system-lower-bound gives the three-stage covering count: intermediate primes leave only smooth integers and a prime-times-small-cofactor set; greedy small-prime classes reduce the residual count by the Mertens product; remaining primes cover the residual set once the number of unused large primes exceeds it. thm-rankin-classical-prime-gap-lower-bound states the classical Rankin bound limsup_(X→∞) G(X)/L(X)>0.
6. thm-erdos-rankin-arbitrarily-large-scale-prime-gaps records Maynard’s 2016 theorem limsup_(X→∞) G(X)/L(X)=∞. The proof changes the last stage of Rankin’s construction: random residue classes on small primes leave a regular sifted prime set, Maynard/GPY weights give many prime progressions in residue classes for almost all large primes, and a probabilistic covering step uses the unused classes. This differs from the classical positive-constant theorem.
7. thm-fgmt-quantitative-large-prime-gap-bound states the published 2018 result G(X)≫log X log₂X log₄X/log₃X for all sufficiently large X, with an effective implied constant. Its proof contract is the FGMT hypergraph-covering theorem, its sieve-weight/distribution estimates, and the residue-covering-to-gap transfer. It is a separate stronger quantitative scale, not a consequence of the qualitative limsup statement alone.
8. thm-ford-maynard-tao-fixed-length-large-gap-chains states that for each fixed k≥1, and all sufficiently large X (threshold may depend on k),
   \[
   G_k(X)\gg k^{-2}\frac{\log X\,\log_2X\,\log_4X}{\log_3X},
   \]
   with an effective absolute implied constant. The full Maier-matrix argument is required; the scalar k=1 bound alone does not imply the chain theorem.

The B companion contains a CRT worked example and a date-stamped status remark. No status claim in that remark is an item dependency.
