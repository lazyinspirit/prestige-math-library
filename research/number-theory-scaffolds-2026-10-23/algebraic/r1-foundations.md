### Conventions and hypotheses

Use \(\mathcal O_K\) for the ring of integers, \(\mathfrak a,\mathfrak p\) for ideals, \(d_K\) for the field discriminant, \(r_1,r_2\) for signature, and \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]=r_1+2r_2\). The current Minkowski embedding does not rescale complex coordinates by \(\sqrt2\). Thus its covolume is \(2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|d_K|}\,N\mathfrak a\), and the ideal-class constant is
\[
M_K=(4/\pi)^{r_2}\frac{n!}{n^n}\sqrt{|d_K|}.
\]
Do not mix this convention with a scaled embedding.

The exhaustive procedures below are finite and exact. No complexity claim is made for maximal-order, prime-decomposition, HNF enumeration, or bounded-height searches. The LLL source has a complexity result under explicit integer-lattice hypotheses; include it only after checking its printed exponent in the PDF.

### A0. Exact integer column-Hermite basis extraction

**Planned IDs:** alg-integer-column-hermite-normal-form; thm-integer-column-hnf-terminates-and-is-canonical.

**Input:** a full-row-rank matrix \(M\in\mathbb Z^{n\times m}\), \(m\ge n\), whose columns generate a full-rank sublattice \(L\subseteq\mathbb Z^n\).

**Procedure:** for \(i=n,n-1,\ldots,1\), put \(q_i=m-n+i\) and work only with the first \(q_i\) columns. Their rows below \(i\) are zero by the preceding stages. If necessary swap columns to put a nonzero entry of row \(i\) in column \(q_i\). For each \(j<q_i\), use the extended Euclidean algorithm on the row entries \(a=M_{i,q_i}\), \(b=M_{ij}\): compute \(g>0\) and \(s,t\in\mathbb Z\) with \(sa+tb=g\), then apply to columns \(q_i,j\) the unimodular matrix
\[
\begin{pmatrix}s&-b/g\\ t&a/g\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It replaces the row pair by \((g,0)\). After all \(j<q_i\) have been processed, make the pivot positive. At the end, the first \(m-n\) columns are zero; take the last \(n\) columns as \(H\). For \(j=n,n-1,\ldots,2\), and then \(i=j-1,j-2,\ldots,1\), replace column \(j\) by
\[
\operatorname{col}_j-\left\lfloor h_{ij}/h_{ii}\right\rfloor\operatorname{col}_i.
\]
Return \(H\).

**Correctness and termination:** each column operation is unimodular and preserves the generated lattice. At stage \(i\), Euclidean descent reduces the active row entries to their positive gcd and zeroes that row in all earlier active columns; it terminates because a nonzero remainder is strictly smaller in absolute value. Induction down the rows gives zero columns followed by a nonsingular upper-triangular \(H\). The final reductions give \(h_{ii}>0\) and \(0\le h_{ij}<h_{ii}\) for \(i<j\), without changing earlier reduced rows. Milne's HNF uniqueness result then makes this the unique canonical column-HNF basis of \(L\). The procedure uses finitely many Euclidean steps and finitely many reductions. No complexity claim is made.

**Source/provenance:** ai-altered rectangular column version of Milne, Lemma 2.44 and Remark 2.45(b), pp. 41–42. The source proves triangularization by Euclidean row operations and uniqueness of the HNF; the input/output convention and exact column-operation version above are stated locally. Planned dependencies: def-euclidean-domain, thm-euclidean-algorithm, thm-division-algorithm-in-z.

### A1. Finite-coset integral-closure search

**Planned IDs:** alg-integral-closure-by-discriminant-cosets; thm-integral-closure-coset-search-correct.

**Input:** monic irreducible \(f\in\mathbb Z[X]\), degree \(n\), root \(\alpha\), and \(K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)\). Let \(A=\mathbb Z[\alpha]\) in its power basis.

**Procedure:** form the companion matrix \(C\) for multiplication by \(\alpha\). Compute the exact trace Gram matrix
\[
G_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(C^{i+j}),\qquad 0\le i,j<n,
\]
and \(D=|\det G|>0\). This fixes the discriminant computation to the encoded input. Enumerate all \(D^n\) representatives
\[
\beta_{\mathbf a}=D^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_i\alpha^i,\qquad 0\le a_i<D.
\]
For each representative form \(M_{\beta_{\mathbf a}}=D^{-1}\sum_i a_iC^i\) and compute its exact rational characteristic polynomial \(\chi_{\beta_{\mathbf a}}(T)=\det(TI-M_{\beta_{\mathbf a}})\). Retain it iff \(\chi_{\beta_{\mathbf a}}\in\mathbb Z[T]\). Return the finite coset presentation
\[
\mathcal O_K=\bigcup_{\mathbf a\ \mathrm{retained}}(\beta_{\mathbf a}+A)
\]
with multiplication inherited from \(K\), and pass the integer numerator matrix with columns \(D e_1,\ldots,D e_n\) and retained vectors \((a_0,\ldots,a_{n-1})^t\) to A0. Return the resulting integral basis as well.

**Correctness and termination:** Milne Prop. 2.43 gives \(A\subseteq\mathcal O_K\subseteq D^{-1}A\), so the finite representatives contain every integral coset. Integrality is constant on an \(A\)-coset. An element is integral iff its multiplication characteristic polynomial has integer coefficients: integrality makes the rational characteristic coefficients algebraic integers, hence integers; conversely Cayley–Hamilton gives a monic integral annihilating polynomial. Thus retained cosets are exactly \(\mathcal O_K/A\), and the integer numerator columns generate \(D\mathcal O_K\). A0 returns a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis of that lattice; dividing by \(D\) gives an integral basis of \(\mathcal O_K\). The search terminates after \(D^n\) tests, followed by the terminating A0 procedure. No complexity bound is claimed.

**Source/provenance:** Milne, Prop. 2.43 and “Algorithms for finding the ring of integers,” pp. 40–44. The characteristic-polynomial test is an ai-altered implementation of Milne's minimal-polynomial test. A0 supplies the basis-extraction step. Planned dependencies: alg-integer-column-hermite-normal-form, def-number-field, def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer, def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field, def-integral-basis-and-power-integral-basis, def-field-norm-and-trace, def-discriminant-of-a-number-field-basis-and-order, thm-discriminant-as-an-embedding-determinant, cor-trace-and-norm-of-an-algebraic-integer, thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences.

This is not a trace-dual subgroup search. It uses Milne's discriminant-denominator bound. Do not call it “exponential” without an input encoding and a derived bit-complexity bound.

### A2. General rational-prime decomposition in \(\mathcal O_K\)

**Planned IDs:** alg-prime-ideal-decomposition-from-integral-basis; thm-prime-ideal-decomposition-algorithm-correct.

**Input:** number field with explicit integral basis and exact multiplication table, plus rational prime \(p\). Input is the maximal order, not merely a power order; no condition \(p\nmid[\mathcal O_K:\mathbb Z[\alpha]]\).

**Procedure:** reduce the multiplication table modulo \(p\) to the \(n\)-dimensional commutative \(\mathbb F_p\)-algebra \(A=\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\). Enumerate its \(p^n\) coordinate vectors and test \(x^n=0\). This detects the nilradical \(J\) exactly: if \(x\) is nilpotent, its multiplication operator on an \(n\)-dimensional vector space is nilpotent and has nilpotence index at most \(n\); conversely \(x^n=0\) makes \(x\) nilpotent. Row-reduce the nilpotent vectors to a basis of \(J\), form the quotient multiplication table for \(A/J\), and represent all subsequent vectors by coordinate tuples in \(0,\ldots,p-1\), ordered lexicographically. Recursively split this finite reduced algebra using Stein Alg. 4.3.7: compute the exact \(\mathbb F_p\)-linear kernel \(V=\ker(x\mapsto x^p-x)\); if \(\dim_{\mathbb F_p}V=1\), the component is a field. Otherwise choose the lexicographically first \(\alpha\in V\setminus\mathbb F_p\), compute its minimal polynomial \(m\) by the first linear dependence among \(1,\alpha,\alpha^2,\ldots\), scan \(c=0,1,\ldots,p-1\) for a root in \(\mathbb F_p\), set \(m_1=X-c\) and \(m_2=m/m_1\), find \(u_1,u_2\in\mathbb F_p[X]\) with \(u_1m_1+u_2m_2=1\), and take the nontrivial idempotent \(\bar\epsilon=(u_1m_1)(\alpha)\). Split recursively. Each split lowers the dimensions of the two components; stop when all reduced components are fields. Lift each nontrivial split idempotent to the corresponding finite component \(C\) of \(A\): choose the lexicographically first vector \(x\in C\) lifting \(\bar\epsilon\), put \(f=x^2-x\), \(u=2x-1\), find the first \(u^{-1}\) by lexicographic finite search, and replace \(x\) by \(x-u^{-1}f\) until \(x^2=x\). Since \(u^2=1+4f\) is a unit and the new idempotent error is \(u^{-2}f^2\), nilpotence of the component radical makes this iteration terminate. Split \(C\) as \(xC\times(1-x)C\). At each terminal component \(C_i\), its nilradical \(J_i\) is the unique maximal ideal and \(C_i/J_i\) is a field. The kernel of the composite map \(\mathcal O_K\to C_i\to C_i/J_i\) is therefore the prime \(P_i\) over \(p\). Residue degrees are \(f_i=\dim_{\mathbb F_p}(\mathcal O_K/P_i)\).

At each terminal component \(C_i\), compute its nilradical \(J_i\) and its powers until the first zero power; return that least exponent as \(e_i\). By CRT and ideal factorization, the component is \(\mathcal O_K/P_i^{e_i}\); its maximal ideal has nilpotency index exactly \(e_i\). Construct a \(\mathbb Z\)-basis for the preimage \(P_i\subseteq\mathcal O_K\) by taking \(p e_j\) and lifts of a vector-space basis of its residue subspace, then applying A0. Return \(P_i\) both as the residue-subspace preimage and this integral basis, together with \((e_i,f_i)\). The nilpotence bound in the correctness argument makes this scan terminate; the ideal-factorization result gives \(e_i\le n\).

**Correctness and termination:** a finite-dimensional algebra over \(\mathbb F_p\) is Artinian because its ideals are vector subspaces and descending chains stabilize when dimensions do. The quotient is reduced: if \((x+J)^k=0\), then \(x^k\in J\), so some further power of \(x\) is zero and hence \(x\in J\). More directly for the finite ring \(A/J\), it has finitely many maximal ideals, and every prime is maximal because a finite integral domain is a field. Their intersection is zero: if \(x\) is not nilpotent, the finite multiplicative set \(\{1,x,x^2,\ldots\}\) omits zero; among the finitely many ideals disjoint from that set choose a maximal one, which is prime by the maximal-disjoint-ideal argument. Thus the pairwise-comaximal maximal ideals and the finite Chinese remainder theorem identify \(A/J\) with a product of finite residue fields. In that product, \(V=\ker(x\mapsto x^p-x)\) is exactly the product of the prime fields, and a component is a field exactly when \(\dim V=1\); for \(\dim V>1\), the chosen \(\alpha\) has a minimal polynomial that splits into distinct linear factors over \(\mathbb F_p\), so the scan finds \(c\), and Bezout gives the stated idempotent. Each split strictly lowers component dimension. Stein Alg. 4.3.7 gives the factor-splitting construction and Alg. 4.3.9 identifies the projection kernels with the primes above \(p\). Stein explicitly does not compute exponents. Our finite idempotent-lift and exponent procedure is a separately identified ai-altered completion. For any component of dimension \(m\le n\), every nilpotent element has \(m\)-th power zero by the multiplication-operator argument. There are at most \(p^m\) such elements, so every product of \(p^m(m-1)+1\) elements of the nilradical is zero by pigeonhole; hence the radical is nilpotent with an explicit finite bound \(N=p^m(m-1)+1\). In the lift iteration, \(u^2=1+4f\) is a unit and the new error is \(u^{-2}f^2\), so after at most \(\lceil\log_2N\rceil\) iterations the error vanishes. In the CRT component corresponding to \(P_i\), the quotient is \(\mathcal O_K/P_i^{e_i}\); unique ideal factorization and the local DVR powers show its maximal ideal has nilpotency index exactly \(e_i\). Thus the computed exponents give \(p\mathcal O_K=\prod_iP_i^{e_i}\). Every element, root, idempotent, inverse, and subspace scan is finite; recursion strictly splits dimensions, so the algorithm terminates. No complexity claim is made.

**Source/provenance:** Stein Ch. 4 §4.3.4, Algs. 4.3.7–4.3.9, pp. 57–59; the idempotent-lift and exponent procedure is a separate ai-altered completion proved by the finite-algebra argument above. The proof uses the library definition of the nilradical but gives finite-ring proofs of its needed decomposition facts, avoiding Artinian nilpotence/prime-existence results with stronger choice hypotheses. Planned dependencies: alg-integer-column-hermite-normal-form, def-number-field, def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field, def-integral-basis-and-power-integral-basis, thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree, thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf, thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals, def-nilradical-and-reduced-ring, def-radical-of-an-ideal, def-prime-above-and-residue-degree, def-ramification-index, thm-fundamental-identity-for-primes-in-number-fields, def-finite-field-and-its-order, cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-euclidean, thm-polynomial-division-algorithm-over-a-field, thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd.

This is the general route when \(p\) divides the power-order index. Existing Dedekind–Kummer remains the simpler conditional method only under its exact index hypothesis.

### A5. Full unit and class-group computation from bounded S-units

This computation uses Lenstra's finite S-unit generator method and then recovers ordinary units as a kernel and the class group as a cokernel. It does not apply a bounded-height theorem directly to ordinary units.

**Planned IDs:** lem-archimedean-product-body-minkowski-bound; thm-lenstra-integral-s-unit-height-generators; thm-s-unit-valuation-exact-sequence; alg-integer-smith-normal-form; thm-integer-smith-normal-form-computes-kernel-and-cokernel; alg-compute-units-and-class-group-from-bounded-s-units; thm-bounded-s-unit-matrix-computation-correct.

Write \(n=[K:\mathbb Q]=r_1+2r_2\), \(\Delta=d_K\), \(S_\infty\) for the archimedean places, and
\[
d=(2/\pi)^{r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}.
\]
This is Lenstra's bound parameter; it is distinct from the ideal-class constant \(M_K\) used by A4.

**Height convention.** Use the shared planned item def-absolute-multiplicative-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field. Its relative height at \([x:1]\in\mathbb P^1(K)\) is
\[
H_K([x:1])=\prod_v\max\{1,|x|_v\},
\]
where \(|2|_v=2\) at real places, \(|2|_v=4\) at complex places, and \(|x|_v=(Nv)^{-\operatorname{ord}_v(x)}\) at finite places. This agrees with Lenstra's unrooted product height \(H_L(x)=H_K([x:1])\); R3 separately defines the absolute height \(H_{\rm abs}=H_K^{1/n}\), which is not the bound used here. For an integral element, all finite-place factors are \(1\), so
\[
H_K([x:1])=\prod_{i=1}^{r_1+r_2}\max\{1,|\sigma_i(x)|\}^{n_i},
\qquad n_i=1\text{ at real places},\quad n_i=2\text{ at complex places}.
\]

**Local product-body lemma.** For a full lattice \(L\subset\mathbb R^{r_1}\times\mathbb C^{r_2}\) of covolume \(c_L\), there is a nonzero point \(b\in L\) with every real absolute value and complex modulus at most \(t\), where \(t^n=(4/\pi)^{r_2}c_L\). In the unscaled embedding, use the symmetric convex body with real intervals \([-t,t]\) and complex disks of radius \(t\). Its volume is
\[
2^{r_1}\pi^{r_2}t^n=2^n c_L,
\]
so the existing Minkowski equality case gives the point. For an ideal lattice \(L=\mathfrak a\), \(c_L=2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}N\mathfrak a\), and the bound becomes \(t^n=dN\mathfrak a\).

**Lenstra integral S-unit height bound.** Let \(S\) be finite, contain every archimedean place, and contain every finite prime ideal \(\mathfrak p\) with \(N\mathfrak p\le d\). Put \(m_S=\max(\{1\}\cup\{N\mathfrak p:\mathfrak p\in S_f\})\). Then \(K_S\) is generated by its integral S-units \(x\in\mathcal O_K\cap K_S\) satisfying \(H_K([x:1])\le d^2m_S\).

**Proof plan:** prove Lenstra 1992 Thm. 6.2 locally rather than treating that citation as a logical supplier. ### A6. LLL basis reduction

**Planned IDs:** alg-lll-reduced-lattice-basis; thm-lll-algorithm-terminates-and-returns-reduced-basis.

**Input:** ordered basis of a full-rank lattice \(L\subseteq\mathbb Q^n\). Clear a positive common denominator \(q\), perform inner products, Gram–Schmidt, and basis updates exactly on \(qL\), then divide every returned vector by \(q\). This returns a basis of \(L\); the LLL inequalities are invariant under this common scaling.

**Procedure:** use LLL algorithm (1.15) of Lenstra–Lenstra–Lovász with \(\delta=3/4\). Initialize Gram–Schmidt vectors and \(\mu_{ij}\), put \(k=2\), and first replace \(b_k\) by \(b_k-rb_{k-1}\), where \(r=\lfloor\mu_{k,k-1}+1/2\rfloor\). After every basis update recompute all Gram–Schmidt data exactly from the current basis. This makes \(|\mu_{k,k-1}|\le1/2\). Test
\[
\|b_k^*+\mu_{k,k-1}b_{k-1}^*\|^2\ge(3/4)\|b_{k-1}^*\|^2.
\]
If the test fails, swap \(b_k,b_{k-1}\) and set \(k=\max(2,k-1)\). If it passes, size-reduce \(b_k\) against \(b_{k-2},b_{k-3},\ldots,b_1\), in that descending order, using the same nearest-integer rule and recomputing all Gram–Schmidt data after each update; then increase \(k\). Stop at \(k=n+1\).

**Correctness and termination:** every update is an invertible integer basis operation. At termination the size-reduction and Lovász inequalities are the source's reduced-basis conditions. The termination potential is \(D=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}d_i\), where \(d_i\) is the squared determinant of the prefix lattice of rank \(i\). A failed test reduces \(D\) by a factor \(<3/4\); \(D\) has a positive lower bound for the fixed lattice. Between swaps \(k\) increases and is bounded by \(n+1\), hence termination.

### A9. Malle's weak field-counting conjecture as a dated open-problem branch

**Planned IDs:** def-permutation-index-of-a-transitive-group-element; def-finite-cyclotomic-action-on-conjugacy-classes; def-malle-counting-function-and-exponents; op-weak-malle-counting-conjecture-for-number-fields.

**Definition obligations.** Define the index of $g\in S_n$ as $\operatorname{ind}(g)=n-\#\operatorname{Orb}(g)$, where $\operatorname{Orb}(g)$ is the set of cycles in the specified permutation action. Let $\operatorname{Cl}(G)$ be the set of conjugacy classes of $G$. For $C\in\operatorname{Cl}(G)$ define $\operatorname{ind}(C)=\operatorname{ind}(g)$ for $g\in C$; this is well-defined because conjugation preserves cycle type. For a nontrivial transitive subgroup $G\le S_n$, put $\operatorname{ind}(G)=\min_{1\ne g\in G}\operatorname{ind}(g)$ and $a(G)=1/\operatorname{ind}(G)$. For a fixed number field $K$, define $N(K,G;X)$ to count $K$-isomorphism classes of degree-$n$ extensions $L/K$ whose Galois closure has group $G$ in the specified transitive action and whose relative discriminant ideal $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ has absolute ideal norm at most $X$. A8 supplies the trace-discriminant ideal definition used in this count.

Let $K$ be a number field and put $m=|G|$. Define $\Gamma_K(G)$ as the image of the finite Galois group $\operatorname{Gal}(K(\mu_m)/K)$ in $\operatorname{Perm}(\operatorname{Cl}(G))$ under the power action: if $\sigma(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^a$ for a primitive $m$th root, then $\sigma$ sends $[g]$ to $[g^a]$. The exponent class $a\in(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$ is independent of the chosen primitive root. Since $G$ is finite, cor-order-of-element-divides-group-order gives $\operatorname{ord}(g)\mid m$ for every $g\in G$; consequently the power map is well-defined, conjugacy-preserving, and defines the stated action. Define the strong-form orbit exponent by counting the $\Gamma_K(G)$-orbits on the conjugacy classes of minimal index:
$$
b(K,G)=\#\Bigl(\Gamma_K(G)\backslash\{C\in\operatorname{Cl}(G):\operatorname{ind}(C)=\operatorname{ind}(G)\}\Bigr).
$$
The strong form predicts
$$
N(K,G;X)\sim c(K,G)X^{a(G)}(\log X)^{b(K,G)-1},\qquad c(K,G)>0.
$$
The weak form predicts
$$
N(K,G;X)=X^{a(G)+o(1)},
$$
equivalently, for every $\epsilon>0$,
$$
X^{a(G)-\epsilon}\ll_{K,G,\epsilon}N(K,G;X)\ll_{K,G,\epsilon}X^{a(G)+\epsilon}.
$$
This formulation includes a lower bound and therefore would imply existence of $G$-extensions; it is not conditional on a separately assumed realization.

**Status/evidence boundary.** Plan an open-problem item only for the weak exponent statement; the strong logarithmic prediction is known to fail in general, as shown by the full Klüners 2004 counterexample. Alberts's full 2021 article, §1 and its results §§3.4 and 5.1–5.2, states the weak conjecture, reports no known weak counterexamples as of its publication, and proves lower bounds for substantial abelian-normal-subgroup families. Wang's full 2025 paper, §§1–4, studies the strong and Türkelli-modified logarithmic predictions and gives counterexamples to the modification; it does not claim a counterexample to the weak exponent. Ray's full 2026 paper, §§1–7, proves only a lower bound for specified symplectic groups, with natural-action exponents asymptotic to a factor $1/(4n)$ of the weak-Malle prediction as $\ell\to\infty$. This is a specific partial result, not a proof or refutation of the general weak form. The 2014 Jones–Roberts database article supplies dated examples with explicitly bounded completeness regions. Malle's original 2002/2004 articles remain unavailable to this reader (publisher PDF HTTP 403; Project Euclid interstitial), so use the later full-text formulations rather than implying that the originals were read. The source/status review is dated 2026-10-23 and covers full texts through Ray 2026 (arXiv:2609.23093, posted 2026-09-19); the search was not exhaustive, so the final item must not assert frontier completeness or claim that no later resolution exists. This open-problem branch is never a logical supplier or a dependency of the enumeration algorithms.
