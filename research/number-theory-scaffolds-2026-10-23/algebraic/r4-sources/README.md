Local PDFs and text extracts are in this directory unless another path is given. SHA-256 hashes identify the PDFs. Text extraction is a reading aid; for scanned formulas the OCR status is stated explicitly.

## Reading convention

“Read” means the stated section or result was inspected in the cited text, including the local definitions needed to interpret its hypotheses. It does not mean every proof in a cited volume was reconstructed. Any unread proof input that a proposed item depends on is listed as a local proof/source gate. “Reuse” means an existing library contract can supply a prerequisite; “defer” means no R4 theorem is claimed for that heading.

## Exact finite-field cohomology and trace contracts

These are the intended contracts for the four étale pages. They fix conventions and avoid collapsing prerequisites into a “Deligne bound” label.

1. **Étale site, derived category, and lisse sheaf / π₁.** SGA5 VI §1.1 gives constructible ℓ-adic sheaves as compatible projective systems of constructible Zℓ/ℓⁿ-sheaves; define the bounded constructible Qℓ derived category from these systems by inverting ℓ. Milne Chapter I §§5–9 (printed pp. 39–69) gives the small étale site, sheaf categories, image functors, and derived-functor cohomology context. Deligne §3.1 gives the lisse Qℓ fibre-functor equivalence. The precise passage from compatible torsion systems to the Qℓ derived category is an item-level construction obligation. For connected finite-type X/F_q, geometric base point x̄, and ℓ≠char(F_q), a lisse finite-rank Qℓ-sheaf corresponds to a continuous finite-dimensional Qℓ-representation of π₁^et(X,x̄). Constructible means a finite locally closed stratification on which each restriction is lisse. A curve middle extension is j_{!*}(F[1])[-1] from lisse F on dense open j:U↪X.

2. **Trace function convention.** For a lisse sheaf on its lisse open, use geometric Frobenius Frob_x^geom, inverse arithmetic Frobenius, on the stalk; in the associated local Galois representation the trace is on inertia invariants. For arbitrary constructible sheaves, the separate stalk trace is s_F(x)=tr(Frob_x^geom | F_x̄), the term used by the general trace formula. Arithmetic Frobenius is used separately in global Galois representation polynomials.

3. **Compact support and trace formula.** For a separated finite-type X/F_q, choose a Nagata compactification j:X↪X̄ with proper structure map f̄:X̄→Spec(F_q). Define RΓ_c(X,F):=RΓ(X̄,Rj_!F)=R f̄_*Rj_!F and H_c^i(X,F):=H^i(RΓ_c(X,F)); the compact-support object is independent of compactification. The chain of local suppliers is the separated finite-type compactification theorem, definition of Rf_!, compactification-independence theorem, proper base change, and finiteness. For constructible Qℓ-sheaf F, H_c^i is finite dimensional and vanishes outside finitely many degrees, subject to the stated Qℓ-system passage. For each rational point x define the separate constructible-stalk trace s_F(x)=tr(Frob_x^geom | F_x̄); with geometric Frobenius on cohomology,
   Σ_{x∈X(F_q)} s_F(x) = Σ_i (−1)^i tr(Frob_q^geom | H_c^i(X̄,F)).
   SGA5 XV §3 proves the equivalent Euler-factor/product formula for every constructible sheaf; Milne §18 and §§19.1–19.2 supply compactification and finiteness context. The derived Qℓ/Rf_! formalism is an explicit local definition/proof obligation where the read sources give compatible torsion systems or flat Zℓ-sheaves. This stalk trace is not the lisse-open trace-function interface, which is used for the FKM correlation theorem.

4. **GOS and conductor.** For smooth geometrically connected open curve U, smooth proper completion X̄, j:U↪X̄ and lisse finite-rank Qℓ-sheaf F, ℓ≠p,
   χ_c(U,F)=rank(F)χ_c(U,Qℓ) − Σ_{x∈X̄\U} Swan_x(F).
   Swan is the upper-numbering wild inertia conductor, zero in the tame case. Artin conductor is dim(F)−dim(F^{I_x})+Swan_x(F). The FKM complexity c(F) is a separate curve invariant, not the Artin conductor.

5. **Degree 0, degree 2, and correlation main term.** On a smooth geometrically connected nonproper curve U over an algebraically closed field of characteristic p, for ℓ≠p and lisse finite-rank Qℓ-sheaf F, H_c^0(U,F)=0. Poincaré duality identifies H_c²(U,F) with the dual of H^0(U,F∨(1)), the geometric invariants of F∨(1). For geometrically irreducible pointwise weight-zero middle-extension sheaves F₁,F₂ on A¹/F_p, ℓ≠p, Schur’s lemma makes the invariants in F₁⊗D(F₂) zero when the two sheaves are geometrically nonisomorphic and one-dimensional when they are geometrically isomorphic. FKM Theorem 4.1 states
   |Σ_{x∈F_p} t_F₁(x) overline{t_F₂(x)}| ≤ 3c(F₁)²c(F₂)²√p
   in the nonisomorphic case; in the isomorphic case t_F₁=αt_F₂, |α|=1, and
   |Σ_{x∈F_p} t_F₁(x) overline{t_F₂(x)} − αp| ≤ 3c(F₁)²c(F₂)²√p.
   The scanned theorem page (PDF p. 13) was visually checked because the OCR dropped the conjugation bar. The explicit factor 3c₁²c₂² needs a separate tensor-conductor estimate in addition to GOS; its local derivation remains a proof gate.
