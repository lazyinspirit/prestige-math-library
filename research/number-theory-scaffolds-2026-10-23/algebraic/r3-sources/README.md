# R3 arithmetic-geometry source ledger

This directory holds the R3-only planning evidence for arithmetic geometry and
Diophantine geometry. The companion report is
[`../r3-arithmetic-geometry.md`](../r3-arithmetic-geometry.md). The machine
interfaces are `pair-fragment.json` and `proposed-inventories.json`; neither
constitutes an authored or proved library item. Sources below are listed with
the exact text actually consulted and the limits of that consultation. A PDF
download or text extraction is not counted as a full read by itself.

## Proof and source gates retained

* **Faltings, bounded height, Tate, and isogeny finiteness.** The complete 18-page 1983 primary article and the original one-page 1984 erratum were read; see the source rows below. The correction replaces a faulty stabilization assertion in Theorem 2 by stationarity of the height sequence and an eventual choice of the abelian variety in the sequence. Faltings–Wüstholz *Rational Points*, Ch. IV and Ch. V §§1–5 were also read for the proof chain and the Mordell/Siegel applications. Thus this is no longer an unread primary-source gate. Keep height comparison, moduli/Northcott, Tate homomorphism, semisimplicity, Chebotarev, and Shafarevich as distinct contracts; a future library proof still has to reconstruct and audit each implication, including the erratum correction.
* **Baker linear forms.** Waldschmidt, IMSc Report 116 (1992), Theorems 7.1 and 10.1, provides fully read proofs of explicit *homogeneous* bounds. Theorem 10.1 has the exact explicit constant recorded below and is sufficient, after using its weaker bound in Evertse’s height argument, for an effective homogeneous unit-equation/Thue chain. The general *inhomogeneous* bound with an arbitrary algebraic additive term `γ` is recalled but not proved in Evertse Ch. 5, and Waldschmidt XI.1 explicitly says Baker’s qualitative proof is omitted. The full proof source for that stronger inhomogeneous statement remains a gate; it is not a prerequisite of the effective homogeneous subchain. The 1966 primary MathNet endpoint timed out. The Baker 1975 book is listed in Internet Archive as `transcendentalnu0000bake`; the unencrypted PDF and OCR endpoints returned HTTP 401 (borrow required), so that text was not acquired/read. The 106 MB IMSc download is a duplicate copy of Waldschmidt Report 116, not Baker’s 1975 book.
* **Siegel integral points.** The full proof in Faltings–Wüstholz *Rational Points*, Ch. V §5, printed pp.198–200, was read. Theorem 5.1 applies to arbitrary finite S; the proof reduces genus-zero and genus-one cases with enough punctures to Mordell–Faltings by covers, choosing a finite extension independent of the integral point. It uses no Roth theorem and proves finiteness, not an effective bound or a general integral-point listing algorithm.
* **Lang–Néron.** Kahn 2007’s complete note was read, together with Kahn 2006 §2 Néron–Severi theorem, §3 Theorem 3 proof, Appendix A, and Hindry’s Appendix B proof of Lang–Néron. The imported finite-generation lemma is no longer unread. The dependencies on classical Mordell–Weil, Néron–Severi, and resolution/alteration results remain explicit and are not represented as reproved by Kahn.
* **Height machine and Arakelov inputs.** Schanuel is fully read for the number-field normalization and count (R5 owns that count). The general Weil height-machine theorem is not treated as fully proved by Silverman’s MSRI slides; a future proof page needs its complete theorem source and exact bounded-error hypotheses. Gillet–Soulé arithmetic Chow sections and the Faltings–Wüstholz height chain support the planned Arakelov interfaces but do not erase their separate imports.
* **Absolute anabelian theorem.** Tamagawa’s relative affine-curve theorem over one fixed finitely generated field is source-read. His absolute comparison for distinct fields uses Pop’s birational theorem; Pop’s source was not read, so the absolute statement is excluded from the source-complete contract. The proper section conjecture is recorded as a conjecture, not a theorem. Carlson–Stix 2026 concerns reconstruction from étale topoi and does not settle the Galois section conjecture.
* **Open-problem status.** Open items are never theorem suppliers. Source dates are preserved: one 2018 abc critique with recorded dissent, the September 2026 Pasten Szpiro bound, a 2024 special-family Manin–Peyre proof, 2010 dynamics conjecture formulations, and Stix’s dated section-conjecture manuscript. No current-status survey covering all these conjectures was read, so present-tense status labels must be refreshed before publication.
