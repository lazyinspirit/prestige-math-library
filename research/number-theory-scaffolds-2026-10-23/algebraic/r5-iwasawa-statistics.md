## Existing library reuse and computational boundary

Existing suppliers are reused by ID and remain in their current homes:

- Cyclotomic and analytic inputs: thm-special-values-of-riemann-zeta-at-integers, thm-primitive-dirichlet-l-analytic-continuation, thm-primitive-dirichlet-l-functional-equation, and the published Dirichlet-series and primitive-character pages. The p-adic special-value pair cites the ζ special-value theorem where it interpolates classical values. It does not create a second complex analytic continuation or prime number theorem proof.
- Number-field class groups: def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain, thm-finiteness-of-the-number-field-class-group, cor-class-group-generated-by-small-primes, and the published Minkowski/class-group page. No class-group items are rehomed.
- Binary forms: def-binary-quadratic-form-over-integers, def-positive-definite-binary-quadratic-form, def-primitive-binary-quadratic-form, def-discriminant-of-binary-quadratic-form, def-reduced-positive-definite-binary-quadratic-form, thm-reduction-of-positive-definite-binary-quadratic-forms, thm-unique-reduced-positive-definite-binary-quadratic-form, lem-leading-coefficient-bound-for-a-reduced-form, and def-binary-quadratic-form-class-number. R5 adds a form-class-number enumerator using these contracts.
- Existing arithmetic examples include the class groups of Q(√−5), Q(√10), and reduced forms for discriminants −4, −8, −20, and −23. The new −23 example is an algorithm execution trace, not a replacement for the published example.

R5’s exact class-number algorithm returns the number of proper-equivalence classes of primitive positive-definite binary forms of a negative fundamental discriminant. It does not compute an ideal class group’s structure, decide whether an ideal is principal, calculate an integral basis, implement Dedekind–Kummer, enumerate Minkowski-bounded HNF ideals, or duplicate R1’s candidate/class-covering list. The λ routine is independent of those tasks. It consumes finite character data, a chosen p-adic embedding and an explicit μ=0 certificate, then uses exact ideal valuations of finite generalized-Bernoulli sums. It does not claim a bit-complexity result beyond the finite sum count and exact arithmetic procedures.

### 6. Reduced-form class-number algorithm

**Pages:** reduced-binary-forms-and-exact-class-number-computation / companion. **Direct DAG:** existing positive-definite-binary-quadratic-forms-and-reduction page → pair 6 A.

- **Algorithm contract.** Input a negative fundamental discriminant D. Set A=floor(sqrt(|D|/3)). For each integer 1≤a≤A, iterate b∈[-a,a] with b≡D mod 2; set c=(b²-D)/(4a) when integral. Retain exactly those triples with a≤c, gcd(a,b,c)=1, and canonical boundary convention b≥0 when |b|=a or a=c. Output the triples and their count, which is the binary-form class number.
- **Correctness.** Every retained triple is a primitive positive-definite form of discriminant D. Conversely every proper class has a unique reduced form; for reduced forms, 1≤a≤sqrt(|D|/3), |b|≤a≤c. Thus the loop visits every class once and the output size is the class number. This uses the existing reduction, uniqueness, and leading-coefficient-bound items. It stops because the outer and inner loops are finite.
- **Bit cost.** There are at most ∑_{a=1}^A(2a+1)=O(|D|) candidate (a,b) pairs. Each candidate performs a constant number of integer arithmetic/divisibility operations and one gcd on O(log|D|)-bit integers. With schoolbook arithmetic and Euclid, this is O(|D|(log|D|)^2) bit operations; working storage is O(log|D|), plus O(h(D)log|D|) if the list is retained. These bounds are derived locally, not attributed to Stein. No ideal class-group structure or principality decision follows.

### 11. Borel regulators, higher K-groups, and zeta special values

**Pages:** `borel-regulators-higher-k-groups-and-zeta-values` / companion. Reuses number fields, Dirichlet units, the DR Siegel-rationality theorem from pair 7, and the existing classical zeta/Dirichlet-L interfaces. It adds no PNT or complex continuation supplier.

  \zeta_F(4)=\pi^{4(r_1+r_2)}|D_F|^{-1/2}\det\!\left(\mathcal L_4(\sigma_{r_1+i}(y_j))\right)_{1\le i,j\le r_2}.

### 12. Beilinson, Bloch–Kato, and Tamagawa conjectures

**Pages:** `beilinson-bloch-kato-tamagawa-conjectures` / companion. This pair is the motive/regulator frontier supplier and the consumer of pairs 3, 9, and 11; it does not claim a general solution to the Beilinson or Bloch–Kato conjectures.

- **Modular motive and completed L-function scope.** Let $f$ be a normalized newform in $S_k(\Gamma_0(N))$ with trivial nebentypus, even $k\ge4$, $N\ge3$; let $F=\mathbb Q(a_n(f))$ be its totally real Hecke field. Scholl’s construction in Longo–Vigni §2.2 forms the self-dual Grothendieck motive $M(f)$ from the weight-$k$, level-$N$ Kuga–Sato variety and its Hecke projector. Its Betti, de Rham and étale realizations include the self-dual twist $V_f(k/2)$. For the classical modular-form normalization, Martin Definition 7.3.4 has $\Lambda_f(s)=(\sqrt N/(2\pi))^s\Gamma(s)L(f,s)$. The Fricke involution is $\check f(z)=N^{-k/2}z^{-k}f(-1/(Nz))$; for a normalized eigennewform it acts by an eigenvalue $\lambda_N\in\{\pm1\}$ (Martin Theorem 8.4.7), and Mellin continuation gives $\Lambda_f(s)=(-1)^{k/2}\Lambda_{\check f}(k-s)$, entire for cusp forms (Theorem 7.3.6). Thus the self functional equation is $\Lambda_f(s)=(-1)^{k/2}\lambda_N\Lambda_f(k-s)$. Longo–Vigni shifts the variable: $L(M,s)$ corresponds to $(L(f^\sigma,s+k/2))_\sigma$ and $\Lambda(M,s)=L_\infty(M,s)L(M,s)$; define $r_{\rm an}(M)=\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}L(M,s)$ and $L^*(M,0)$ as its leading Taylor coefficient, with $\Lambda^*(M,0)$ related by their equation (2.25). The Fricke/Mellin suppliers are for trivial nebentypus and even weight; do not extend the formula to general nebentypus without its character factor and source normalization.
- **Motivic cohomology, height and $p$-adic regulator.** For the Kuga–Sato variety $X$ and $f$-projector $\theta_f$, Longo–Vigni define $H^0_{\rm mot}(K,M)=0$ and $H^1_{\rm mot}(K,M)=CH^{k/2}_{\rm arith}(X/K)_F[\theta_f]$. Here $CH_{\rm arith}$ is the projector image of homologically trivial codimension-$k/2$ cycles admitting an integral model with zero intersection against all dimension-$k$ cycles on special fibres; it is a specified arithmetic Chow subgroup, not asserted equal to all homologically trivial cycles. The algebraic rank is $\dim_F H^1_{\rm mot}(K,M)$, conjecturally finite. The Gillet–Soulé pairing on these cycles defines $\operatorname{Reg}_{\mathcal B}(M/K)=\det(\langle t_i,t_j\rangle_{\rm GS,\infty})$ for an $F$-basis (Reg $=1$ in rank 0); nondegeneracy makes it nonzero. Their $p$-adic Abel–Jacobi map gives $\operatorname{reg}_{K,p}:H^1_{\rm mot}(K,M)_p\to H^1_f(K,V_p)$, conjecturally an isomorphism for every $p$ and number field $K$. Keep these as the article’s precise definitions and assumptions (LV §§2.6–2.8).
- **Beilinson rationality and rank conjectures.** The analytic/algebraic rank conjecture is $\operatorname{ord}_{s=0}L(M/K,s)=\dim_F H^1_{\rm mot}(K,M)$. The fundamental line is $\Delta(M)=\det_F^{-1}H^1_{\rm mot}(\mathbb Q,M)\otimes\det_F H^1_{\rm mot}(\mathbb Q,M)^*\otimes\det_F t(M)\otimes\det_F^{-1}V_B^+$ (LV Definition 2.36). Under the height nondegeneracy and comparison-map hypotheses that give $\theta_\infty:\Delta(M)\otimes_F F_\infty\simeq F_\infty$, the rationality conjecture says there is a unique $\zeta_f\in\Delta(M)$ with $\theta_\infty(\zeta_f)=L^*(M,0)^{-1}$ (LV Conjecture 2.40). Equivalent formulations include the regulator/period relation in Proposition 2.43. Record finite dimensionality, height nondegeneracy, rank equality, rationality, and $p$-regulator bijectivity as open-problem contracts, never as logical dependencies or established consequences. The motive-specific zeta element is defined only conditional on the rationality conjecture.
- **Fontaine–Perrin-Riou $p$-TNC and exact fractional-ideal form.** For an $F$-stable projective Betti lattice $T_B$, finite $S\supset\{p,\infty\}$, its associated $p$-adic lattice $T_p$, and assuming the zeta-element rationality plus the $p$-adic regulator isomorphism, LV Conjecture 2.56 is the equality of $\mathcal O_p$-lattices $\theta_{p,S}(\zeta_f^*)\,\mathcal O_p=\operatorname{Det}_{\mathcal O_p}R\Gamma_c(G_S,T_p)$ (their equation (2.51) for $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_F$). Its explicit rank-$r$ reformulation, LV Theorem 2.83, additionally assumes height nondegeneracy. In fractional $\mathcal O_p$-ideals it is
  $$
  \left(\frac{\Lambda^*(M,0)}{\Omega_M\,\operatorname{Reg}_{\mathcal B}(M)}\right)=
  \frac{\mathcal I(\mathrm{III}^{\mathrm{BK}}_p(\mathbb Q,M))\,\mathcal I_p(\gamma_f)\,\prod_{v\in S}\operatorname{Tam}^{(p)}_v(M)}
  {(\det A_{\widetilde{\mathcal B}})^2\,\operatorname{Tors}_p(M)}.
  $$
  Here $\Omega_M=\Omega_\infty/(2\pi i)^{k/2}$ is the period of a chosen nonzero $\gamma_f\in T_B^+$; $\mathcal I_p(\gamma_f)$ is the index ideal of its Betti-to-étale image in $T_p^+$; $A_{\widetilde{\mathcal B}}$ is the transition matrix from the $p$-regulator images of a motivic basis to an $\mathcal O_p$-basis of $H^1_f(\mathbb Q,T_p)$; $\mathrm{III}^{\mathrm{BK}}_p(\mathbb Q,M)$ is the finite Bloch–Kato Shafarevich–Tate group; and $\operatorname{Tors}_p(M)=\mathcal I^{-1}(H^0(G_S,A_p)^\vee)\,\mathcal I^{-1}(H^1(\mathbb Q,T_p)_{\rm tors})$. The right side retains local Tamagawa ideals, finite Selmer-group order, period-index and torsion factors. This is the exact statement; it is not a bare equality of L-value and regulator.
- **Original Bloch–Kato motivic-pair formulation.** Separately, let $(V,D)$ be a motivic pair over $\mathbb Q$ of weights $\le-3$, and choose a $G_\mathbb Q$-stable lattice $M$. The source chooses a finite-dimensional $\mathbb Q$-space $\Phi$ with period comparison $\Phi\otimes\mathbb R\simeq D_\infty/(D_\infty^0+V_\infty^+)$ and Galois comparison $\Phi\otimes\mathbb A_f\simeq H^1_{f,\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb Z)}(\mathbb Q,V\otimes\mathbb A_f)$. Bloch–Kato define $\operatorname{Tam}(M)$ as the Tamagawa measure of the adelic quotient $\left(\prod_v A(\mathbb Q_v)\right)/A(\mathbb Q)$. Conjecture 5.15 asserts finiteness of $\mathrm{III}(M)$ and
  $$
  \operatorname{Tam}(M)=\frac{\#H^0(G_\mathbb Q,M^*\otimes\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z(1))}{\#\mathrm{III}(M)}.
  $$
  Its special-value form (5.15.1), for sufficiently large finite $S\ni\infty$, is
  $$
  L_S(V,0)=\frac{\#\mathrm{III}(M)}{\#H^0(G_\mathbb Q,M^*\otimes\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z(1))}\,
  \mu_{\infty,w}\!\left(A(\mathbb R)/A(\mathbb Q)\right)\prod_{p\in S-\{\infty\}}\mu_{p,w}\!\left(A(\mathbb Q_p)\right).
  $$
