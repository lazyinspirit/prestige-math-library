2.1 We first prove invariance of the actual path generators for both column arrangements, independently of generation. For a box a write c(a) for its column, $\rho_c=n-\sum_{d\ge c}q_d$ and $\widetilde E_{ab}=(-1)^{c(b)-c(a)}(E_{ab}+\delta_{ab}\rho_{c(a)})$. The negative PBW projection $\operatorname{pr}:U(\mathfrak g)=U(\mathfrak p)U(\mathfrak m)\to U(\mathfrak p)$ applies χ to the right factor. Its elementary rules are $\operatorname{pr}(ZB)=\chi(Z)B+\operatorname{pr}([Z,B])$ and $\operatorname{pr}(BZ)=B\chi(Z)$ for a negative matrix unit Z and a p-factor B. The bracket is $[\widetilde E_{ab},\widetilde E_{hk}]=\delta_{bh}(\widetilde E_{ak}-\delta_{ak}\rho_{c(a)})-\delta_{ak}(\widetilde E_{hb}-\delta_{hb}\rho_{c(b)})$, and $\chi(\widetilde E_{ab})=-\delta_{\operatorname{row}(a),\operatorname{row}(b)}$ at adjacent negative columns, zero at greater distance. The path sum $P_{ij;x}^{(r)}$ has total degree $\sum(c(b_t)-c(a_t)+1)=r$, p-factors, shared intervening rows, starting/ending rows i,j, strict forward joins when the intervening row exceeds x and weak reverse joins otherwise, and sign $(-1)^{r-s}$ times one minus for every intervening row at most x. Its ηtail image is the same path sum in the displayed shifted factors. Invariance means $\operatorname{pr}([z,\eta_{\rm tail}(P)])=0$. If an element B is invariant, then $(z-\chi(z))B=B(z-\chi(z))+[z,B]$ proves $U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi B\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)I_\chi$; hence invariant elements form a subalgebra, and products, commutators and coefficients of inverse series with identity constant are invariant. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.2 Group equal row lengths into the minimal admissible parabolic blocks ν, with final nonempty block m of size t. D/E/F below are its actual Gauss path coefficients. Deleting the last column embeds the smaller shifted matrix units into the current ones; on unshifted diagonal units this compensates the rho difference by a scalar translation, and on negative units it preserves χ. A dot denotes the smaller coefficients. With final column A, preceding column B, $L_{kj}=\widetilde E_{(A,k),(A,j)}$ and $C_{hj}=\widetilde E_{(B,h),(A,j)}$, the right end recursions, for any fixed bottom row h, are $D_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot D_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m}(\sum_k\dot D_{a;ik}^{(r-1)}L_{kj}+[\dot D_{a;ih}^{(r-1)},C_{hj}])$, $E_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot E_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m-1}(\sum_k\dot E_{a;ik}^{(r-1)}L_{kj}+[\dot E_{a;ih}^{(r-1)},C_{hj}])$, and $F_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot F_{a;ij}^{(r)}$. The E boundary formula is used for r>1. Deleting the FIRST column of the reflected arrangement has the ordinary embedding on the remaining shifted factors. With first column A, next B, $L_{ik}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(A,k)}$ and $C_{ih}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(B,h)}$, its LEFT recursions are $D_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot D_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m}(\sum_kL_{ik}\dot D_{a;kj}^{(r-1)}+[C_{ih},\dot D_{a;hj}^{(r-1)}])$, $E_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot E_{a;ij}^{(r)}$, and $F_{a;ij}^{(r)}=\dot F_{a;ij}^{(r)}+\delta_{a,m-1}(\sum_kL_{ik}\dot F_{a;kj}^{(r-1)}+[C_{ih},\dot F_{a;hj}^{(r-1)}])$, with r>1 in the F boundary formula. To verify these identities, cut each path at its final or initial visit to the added column. Paths missing that column give the dotted coefficient; paths ending or starting in its block give the product sum. For a crossing path the two neighboring cut positions at the common bottom row h have opposite signs and give the two orders in the commutator; further visits pair by moving to the neighboring cut. The identical cut rule in a changed row interval proves the Gauss identities: paths without an intermediate visit occur once; paths with such a visit have the two neighboring cuts with opposite sign. Thus these are identities for the actual Gauss coefficients. In particular LEFT deletion corrects F and leaves E unchanged; it does not use the interchanged E/F display in the source. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 Induct on the number of columns, deleting the last column in the original left-justified diagram and the first in the reflected diagram. A one-column diagram has no negative algebra, so every mode is invariant. Empty rows have only formal constants and zero positive coefficients. All earlier D blocks, all nonboundary E/F blocks, and F in right deletion or E in left deletion are unchanged by step2.2. Their path monomials have no forbidden preceding-bottom target in right deletion, or following-bottom source in left deletion; commuting a new negative into such a monomial produces a negative unit of greater column distance with zero χ, and moving it to the right keeps this absent endpoint. Thus their projected brackets vanish. Induction supplies old-negative invariance of every dotted coefficient. The old negative algebra commutes with L and C in step2.2, so its character ideal is preserved by those factors, proving old-negative invariance of the changed coefficients. The complete negative Lie algebra is generated by the old one and the new adjacent-column matrices: brackets through a row present in intervening bottom rows produce each longer negative matrix. Jacobi and the normalizer identity in step2.1 propagate invariance from these generators. Only the new adjacent matrices need be checked below. [step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

2.4 The changed D^(1) is a sum of the row-pair diagonal-column blocks; its two adjacent-column projected commutators cancel. Let the neighboring column have height u≥t, with b=u−t shorter rows and cutoff x=n−t. In RIGHT deletion put $z=\widetilde E_{(A,f),(B,g)}$, f bottom and g arbitrary. In D^(2), and in boundary E^(2) when the shift is1, the single bridge contributes scalar $-u\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$. The bottom plus paths $\sum_kB_{ik}A_{kj}$ contribute $+t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and matrix terms $-\delta_{gi}A_{fj}+\delta_{fj}B_{ig}$ for g bottom. The shorter minus paths in B contribute $+b\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and $+\delta_{fj}B_{ig}$ for g shorter. Moving their negative factor through the following block yields one additional χ correction for each shorter row; this is the scalar b term. The single bridge gives the negative of all the matrix terms, and $-u+t+b=0$. In LEFT deletion put $z=\widetilde E_{(B,f),(A,g)}$, f arbitrary and g bottom. The bridge scalar is $-t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$; bottom plus paths $\sum_kA_{ik}B_{kj}$ give $+t\delta_{gi}\delta_{fj}$ and $-\delta_{gi}B_{fj}+\delta_{fj}A_{ig}$ for f bottom. Shorter minus paths $-\sum_hB_{ih}B_{hj}$ give $-\delta_{gi}B_{fj}$ for f shorter and NO scalar: the extra negative χ involves a shorter row h and bottom row g. The bridge has the opposite matrix terms. Therefore $-t+t=0$ and all matrices cancel, for D^(2) and boundary F^(2). Paths outside these adjacent columns have zero projection by the support argument in step2.3. This proves the exact jump-one base cases in both actual LEFT reductions. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra]

2.5 At a RIGHT jump-one boundary, for a fixed earlier-block row g and r>1 the coefficient recursions in U(p) are $D_{m;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(2)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(r)}]+\sum_{s=0}^r\widetilde D_{m-1;gg}^{(r+1-s)}D_{m;ij}^{(s)}$ and $E_{m-1;ij}^{(r+1)}=[D_{m-1;ig}^{(2)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(r)}]-\sum_fD_{m-1;if}^{(1)}E_{m-1;fj}^{(r)}$. Here $\widetilde D(u)=-D(u)^{-1}$, with order-zero coefficient −I. The corresponding LEFT identities are $D_{m;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(r)},E_{m-1;gj}^{(2)}]+\sum_{s=0}^rD_{m;ij}^{(s)}\widetilde D_{m-1;gg}^{(r+1-s)}$ and $F_{m-1;ij}^{(r+1)}=[F_{m-1;ig}^{(r)},D_{m-1;gj}^{(2)}]-\sum_fF_{m-1;if}^{(r)}D_{m-1;fj}^{(1)}$. These are algebraic coefficient identities, independent of invariance and generation. Expanding the E identity by the right end recursion gives $\dot E^{(r+1)}+\sum\dot D^{(1)}\dot E^{(r)}$, its product with L, and its commutator with C; collecting with step2.2 gives $E^{(r+1)}+\sum D^{(1)}E^{(r)}$. The left F expansion instead gives $\dot F^{(r+1)}+\sum\dot F^{(r)}\dot D^{(1)}$, its LEFT product with L and $[C,\,\cdot\,]$, which collect to $F^{(r+1)}+\sum F^{(r)}D^{(1)}$. The unchanged earlier D^(2) has no boundary endpoint and commutes with L,C. Expanding the boundary E–F bracket similarly gives the D identity: the coefficient convolution $D\widetilde D=-I$ cancels paired split paths with opposite cut signs, leaving the changed D coefficient. Thus the recursions hold in both product orders. Every earlier D/inverse-D coefficient and the unaffected E or F coefficient is invariant by step2.3; the changed base coefficients are invariant by step2.4. Since the sum uses s≤r, induction on r proves invariance of ALL changed jump-one modes. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, algebra]

2.6 For a larger boundary shift, or a rectangle, the first two end columns have equal height t. A double dot deletes both and $K_{s;ij}=\ddot D_{m;ij}^{(s)}$; replacing K with the corresponding boundary $\ddot E$ in RIGHT deletion or $\ddot F$ in LEFT deletion gives the same calculation. In RIGHT deletion call the last positions C,B,A, and write $B_{ck}=\widetilde E_{(B,c),(B,k)}$, $A_{kj}=\widetilde E_{(A,k),(A,j)}$, $C_{hk}=\widetilde E_{(C,h),(B,k)}$, $J_{cj}=\widetilde E_{(B,c),(A,j)}$ and $H_{hj}=\widetilde E_{(C,h),(A,j)}$. Twice expanding step2.2 expresses the changed r-mode as the sum of eight groups $A_0=K_{r;ij}$, $B_0=\sum_cK_{r-1;ic}B_{cj}$, $C_0=[K_{r-1;ih},C_{hj}]$, $D_0=\sum_kK_{r-1;ik}A_{kj}$, $E_0=\sum_{k,c}K_{r-2;ic}B_{ck}A_{kj}$, $F_0=\sum_k[K_{r-2;ih},C_{hk}]A_{kj}$, $G_0=\sum_cK_{r-2;ic}J_{cj}$, $H_0=[K_{r-2;ih},H_{hj}]$. For $z=\widetilde E_{(A,f),(B,g)}$ their projected brackets, in that order, are $0,-\delta_{fj}K_{r-1;ig},0,+\delta_{fj}K_{r-1;ig},R,S,-R,-S$, where $R=t\delta_{fj}K_{r-2;ig}-K_{r-2;ig}A_{fj}+\delta_{fj}\sum_cK_{r-2;ic}B_{cg}$ and $S=\delta_{fj}[K_{r-2;ih},C_{hg}]$. The bracket in step2.1 proves this table by moving each negative factor to the RIGHT. The t summation rows give the scalar in R; the bridge J gives its opposite because $\rho_A-\rho_B=t$. K lies in the twice-deleted p and commutes with z. The eight expressions cancel for EVERY r>1. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra]

2.7 The REFLECTED calculation is a direct LEFT-ideal calculation, not an anti-map reversal of step2.6. Call its first positions A,B,C, and write $L_{ik}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(A,k)}$, $B_{kc}=\widetilde E_{(B,k),(B,c)}$, $C_{kh}=\widetilde E_{(B,k),(C,h)}$, $J_{ic}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(B,c)}$, $H_{ih}=\widetilde E_{(A,i),(C,h)}$. The eight groups are $A_0=K_{r;ij}$, $B_0=\sum_kB_{ik}K_{r-1;kj}$, $C_0=[C_{ih},K_{r-1;hj}]$, $D_0=\sum_kL_{ik}K_{r-1;kj}$, $E_0=\sum_{k,c}L_{ik}B_{kc}K_{r-2;cj}$, $F_0=\sum_kL_{ik}[C_{kh},K_{r-2;hj}]$, $G_0=\sum_cJ_{ic}K_{r-2;cj}$, $H_0=[H_{ih},K_{r-2;hj}]$. For $z=\widetilde E_{(B,f),(A,g)}$ their projected brackets are $0,+\delta_{ig}K_{r-1;fj},0,-\delta_{ig}K_{r-1;fj},R',S',-R',-S'$, with $R'=t\delta_{ig}K_{r-2;fj}-\delta_{ig}\sum_cB_{fc}K_{r-2;cj}+L_{ig}K_{r-2;fj}$ and $S'=-\delta_{ig}[C_{fh},K_{r-2;hj}]$. For example $[z,L_{ik}]=\delta_{gi}\widetilde E_{(B,f),(A,k)}$, and moving this negative through B gives $-\delta_{fk}B_{kc}+\delta_{fc}$; summing k gives the t term. The other part of the product bracket gives the L term. The bridge J has the opposite matrix terms and scalar $\rho_A-\rho_B=-t$; the F/H terms cancel because $[z,H_{ih}]=\delta_{gi}C_{fh}$ and z commutes with K. Thus every group is accounted for and cancels in the reflected LEFT reduction for EVERY r>1. If there are only two columns, K has only its formal constant coefficients and terms involving the third column vanish, so the same table applies. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra]

2.8 Steps2.3–2.7 cover every allowed parabolic D/E/F mode for the original and reflected arrangements. Refining a minimal block is its formal Gauss decomposition: coefficients of its inverse and resulting factors are polynomials in its previous coefficients, hence invariant by step2.1. This gives all ordinary simple modes; higher-root commutator definitions give all higher roots. Therefore the actual untwisted-chart images $P_{ii;i-1}^{(r)},P_{i,i+1;i}^{(r)},P_{i+1,i;i}^{(r)}$ and all their higher-root polynomials belong to their actual left reductions. This conclusion covers the infinite allowed mode families and does not rely on finite generation, finite truncation or a reflected W identification. [step 2.1, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 2.5, step 2.6, step 2.7, algebra]
