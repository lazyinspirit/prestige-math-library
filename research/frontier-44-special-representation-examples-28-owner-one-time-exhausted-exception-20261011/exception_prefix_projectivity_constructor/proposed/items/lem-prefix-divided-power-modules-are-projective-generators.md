## Statement

Assume AC. Let $q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$ be bottom-aligned column heights, $n=q_1$, $N=\sum q_j$, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{gl}_N(\mathbb C)$, and $\mathfrak p$ the upper column parabolic. Fix any admissible complex origin $c$, meaning $c_i-c_j\in\mathbb Z$ implies $c_i=c_j$. The ground column $j$ has top-to-bottom entries $c_j+q_j-n,\ldots,c_j+1-n$. Let $\mathrm{Col}_c^d$ consist of strict-column tableaux whose differences from these ground entries are nonnegative partitions of total size $d$, and let $\mathcal O_c^d$ be the full Serre summand on these labels. Read columns **top to bottom**, and set $\eta_i=b_i+i-1$ for the category-O highest weight. Write $M(B),L(B),P(B)$ for the associated generalized standards, simples, and projective covers. For each polynomial filling $A=(a_i)$ of total $d$, put
$$Z_A=\bigotimes_{i=1}^N\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}(V_{\le\operatorname{col}(i)}),\qquad Z(A,c)=U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak p)}(\mathbb C_{-\rho}\otimes\mathbb C_c\otimes Z_A),$$
where $V_{\le j}$ is the actual natural prefix submodule, and the characters $\mathbb C_{-\rho}$ and $\mathbb C_c$ have diagonal scalars $q_j-n$ and $c_j$ on column $j$. Set $P_c=M(A_c)$ and $T_d=P_c\otimes V^{\otimes d}$.

Every $Z(A,c)$ is projective in the **whole** finite summand $\mathcal O_c^d$. For each $B\in\mathrm{Col}_c^d$, its projective cover $P(B)$ is a direct summand of $Z(B-A_c,c)$; hence the finite sum $G_d=\bigoplus_{A\in\mathrm{Tab}^d}Z(A,c)$ is a projective generator of that whole summand. There is an actual inclusion $Z(A,c)\hookrightarrow T_d$ whose quotient has a finite generalized-standard flag. These conclusions persist under exact projection to any union of whole central/linkage blocks. Arbitrary deletion of labels is not an asserted finite-cut operation.

The proof also establishes the maximal-induction sublemma: split the columns into an initial and final segment, define the two-block induction $R$ with shift $\beta=(0^{N'},(N')^{N''})$, and let $B$ have degree split $(d',d'')$. If every $C\ge B$ has the same degree split, then $R(P(B')\boxtimes P(B''))\cong P(B)$. In particular this applies when the final segment is the last ground column. The detector $T_d$ and the whole-summand generator $G_d$ are different objects.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The pyramid, arbitrary admissible origin, top-to-bottom reading, polynomial labels, and induced modules of the Statement. Symmetric powers are symmetric tensors via the characteristic-zero averaging idempotent.

[F1] Genuine parabolic category O has projective covers, finite length, restricted duality, standard flags on projectives, standard/costandard Hom and Ext vanishing, and BGG reciprocity on integral blocks ([[def-parabolic-category-o-for-a-standard-parabolic]], [[thm-parabolic-category-o-is-a-highest-weight-category]], [[thm-every-category-o-object-has-finite-length]], [[prop-projective-covers-in-o-are-indecomposable-and-unique]], [[prop-restricted-duality-is-an-exact-involution-on-category-o]]). On arbitrary admissible mixed-coset blocks these same properties transport from the integral factor blocks by the actual exact equivalence [[lem-separated-integral-cosets-give-typed-parabolic-category-equivalences]]; its product generator construction supplies the tensor-product block category. Thus no generic integral theorem is directly applied outside its integral contract.

[F2] PBW and finite-dimensional reductive complete reducibility ([[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[thm-weyls-complete-reducibility-theorem]]). A simple highest-weight module has the nondegenerate transposition-contravariant pairing obtained by quotienting its highest-weight pairing by its radical; equivalently restricted duality fixes its simple head.

[F3] Polynomial Levi simples of partition highest weight are the nonzero Schur modules, with the Young-symmetrizer realization and length cutoff ([[def-schur-module-and-schur-polynomial-character]], [[thm-schur-weyl-decomposition-with-length-cutoff]]). AC is inherited from these suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).





## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: construct the maximal-induced-cover epimorphism before comparing multiplicities, induct on the number of columns, and detect every projective summand by an actual Young-symmetrizer quotient.

**Given:** Use the data and conventions of the Statement and [F1]–[F3]. Let $E_j$ denote the natural column Levi module. Write $V(C)$ for the polynomial column-Levi simple of highest weight $C-A_0$, inflated with radical zero, where $A_0$ is the ground tableau with $c=0$; set $V_c^{\otimes d}=\mathbb C_c\otimes V^{\otimes d}$. In the proof $Z_{A,c}$ means $\mathbb C_c\otimes Z_A$; $\nabla(C)$ is the restricted dual of $M(C)$.

1.1 Central characters in this dictionary record the entry multiset. In a fixed charge coset, every polynomial entry is at least $c+1-n$. If another strict tableau of the same multiset had a negative ground difference in row $r$, its bottom entry would be smaller than $c+1-n$, because entries decrease strictly by integral steps down that column. This is impossible. Total differences from the ground tableau are fixed by the multiset, so they still sum to $d$. Admissibility identifies any columns sharing a coset with the same $c$, and is exactly what makes the preceding minimum common. Thus $\mathrm{Col}_c^d$ is closed under its central-character/linkage labels; its Serre summand is a sum of whole blocks. It is finite because the ground differences are nonnegative integer partitions of total $d$. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra, construct]

1.2 Split off the rightmost column: $N'=N-q_l$, $\mathfrak g'=\mathfrak{gl}_{N'}$, $\mathfrak g''=\mathfrak{gl}_{q_l}$, and write $B=(B',B'')$. Fix the degree split $d'+d''=d$. Bruhat comparisons preserve charge cosets. More concretely, the generating move toward a larger tableau swaps a smaller entry on the left with a larger same-coset entry on the right, then sorts both columns. It never decreases the sum of the entries in the columns left of a given cut, counted separately in each coset. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra, construct]

1.3 For any $B'\in\mathrm{Col}_{c'}^{d'}$, adjoin the ground last column $G_l=(c_l+q_l-n,\ldots,c_l+1-n)$ and call the result $B$. If $C\ge B$, its last-column sum cannot exceed the sum in $G_l$: use the previous cut inequality in the coset $c_l+\mathbb Z$, and the fixed total multiset. But admissibility and polynomiality say that the $q_l$ strictly decreasing last-column entries are respectively at least the entries of $G_l$, so their sum cannot be smaller. Equality forces all of them to be exactly the ground entries. Consequently every $C\ge B$ still has degree split $(d',0)$. This is the precise maximality condition required below. Entries in other charge cosets cannot be swapped into this column. This argument allows arbitrary admissible complex $c$, repeated entries across columns, and singular central characters. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra, construct]

1.4 Let $\mathfrak q$ be the two-block upper parabolic with Levi $\mathfrak g'\oplus\mathfrak g''$ (dimensions $N',N''$ for any column cut) and upper off-diagonal radical $\mathfrak r$. Put $\beta=(0^{N'},(N')^{N''})$ and define  $R(X)=U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak q)}(\mathbb C_\beta\otimes X),\qquad H(Y)=\mathbb C_{-\beta}\otimes Y^{\mathfrak r}.$ $R$ is exact by PBW and left adjoint to $H$. The shift makes concatenation of the two local highest-weight readings agree with the global reading. The following calculations are independent of projectivity of $R(X)$. Induction in stages gives $R(M(C')\boxtimes M(C''))=M(C)$. Moreover $H(L(C))=L(C')\boxtimes L(C'')$. The top two-block Levi slice of $L(C)$ is generated by the global highest vector. A proper Levi submodule of that slice would generate a proper global submodule: PBW orders negative off-diagonal roots first, Levi roots next, positive off-diagonal roots last, and the last act by zero on the slice. Thus its generated global submodule cannot contain the highest vector. Simplicity shows that this top slice is the indicated Levi simple. There are no additional $\mathfrak r$-invariants below it. Indeed $L(C)$ has its nondegenerate contravariant highest-weight pairing, induced by the Shapovalov pairing on its standard. Every vector below the top cut is spanned by positive-degree opposite-radical PBW monomials applied to the top Levi slice. If $v$ there were $\mathfrak r$-invariant, moving any such monomial to the first argument by transposition would pair $v$ to zero, since the corresponding positive-radical operator kills $v$. Weight orthogonality handles the other cut weights. Nondegeneracy forces $v=0$. Hence the entire invariant module is exactly the top slice, not merely a module with that character. Moreover $H(\nabla(C))=\nabla(C')\boxtimes\nabla(C'')$. Under PBW, $M(C)$ is free over the enveloping algebra of the opposite off-diagonal radical, with coefficient module $M(C')\boxtimes M(C'')$. Its restricted dual is the weightwise coinduced module. An invariant functional vanishes on every positive-degree opposite-radical monomial, leaving exactly the restricted dual of the coefficient module. Transposition supplies precisely this invariance condition. Finally, if $C$ and $B$ have the same degree split, then  $[M(C):L(B)]=[M(C'):L(B')][M(C''):L(B'')].$ To see this last identity, take the exact weight slice at the maximal eigenvalue of the first-block trace $z'=\sum_{i\le N'}e_{ii}$. Every off-diagonal negative root lowers that eigenvalue by one, whereas both within-block root systems preserve it. The slice of $M(C)$ is its top coefficient module $M(C')\boxtimes M(C'')$. In a composition series, a simple with smaller top eigenvalue contributes nothing to this slice. A simple with the same top eigenvalue contributes its top Levi simple, computed above. Such a top Levi simple determines the concatenated label uniquely. Comparing its multiplicity proves the formula. Equal degree split is exactly equality of the two top eigenvalues after the fixed ground and $\beta$ shifts. This argument works separately inside the finitely many lattice/coset components and needs no integrality between distinct $c_j$. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra, construct]

2.1 These formulas also show that $R$ sends the fixed two-factor polynomial component into $\mathcal O_c^d$: its projectives have standard flags, which induce exactly to standards in this polynomial summand. For labels with another degree split, the displayed $H$ simple/costandard modules belong to the corresponding other two-factor component. Hom from a projective in the fixed component is zero. Block closure from step 1.1 makes these component separations legitimate; no exactness of the full invariant functor $H$ is assumed. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra, construct]

2.2 Let $B$ satisfy the maximality condition that $C\ge B$ forces the same degree split $(d',d'')$. Set $X=R(P(B')\boxtimes P(B''))$. Adjointness and the simple invariant calculation give, for every polynomial simple,  $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(X,L(C))=\delta_{BC}.$ Therefore the finite-length object $X$ has simple head $L(B)$. Lift the head epimorphism $X\twoheadrightarrow L(B)$ along the cover $P(B)\twoheadrightarrow L(B)$: projectivity of $P(B)$ supplies a map $P(B)\to X$ whose composite is the cover. Its image maps onto the entire head of $X$, so it is all of $X$ (the radical of a finite-length object is superfluous). Thus there is a **genuine epimorphism** $P(B)\twoheadrightarrow X$. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, algebra, construct]

2.3 $X$ has a standard flag by exact induction and the flags on the two projectives. For a standard $M(C)$ in the fixed degree split, induction in stages gives  $(X:M(C))=(P(B'):M(C'))(P(B''):M(C''))=[M(C'):L(B')][M(C''):L(B'')].$ For other degree splits this multiplicity is zero. On the other hand BGG reciprocity gives $(P(B):M(C))=[M(C):L(B)]$, zero unless $C\ge B$. Maximality puts every possible nonzero case into the fixed split, where step 1.4 identifies it with the same product. Hence $P(B)$ and $X$ have identical standard-flag multiplicities and therefore identical composition multiplicities. The kernel of the already constructed epimorphism has composition multiplicity zero at every simple; finite length makes that kernel zero. We have proved  $R(P(B')\boxtimes P(B''))\cong P(B).$ This is stronger than character matching: the map whose kernel is proved zero exists first. The two-factor projective used here is projective because Hom from an external product of projectives is exact in the tensor product finite block category (equivalently use the tensor product of their finite basic block algebras). No full faithfulness is involved. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, algebra, construct]

3.1 Induct on $l$. For $l=1$, $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak g$, and the polynomial summand consists of finite-dimensional reductive modules with semisimple Cartan/centre. Complete reducibility makes every $Z(A,c)$ projective. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra, construct]

3.2 For $l>1$, remove the last column from $A$ to obtain $A'$. The tensor identity, induction in stages, and $V_{\le l}=V$ give the actual module isomorphism  $Z(A,c)\cong R\bigl(Z(A',c')\boxtimes\mathbb C_{c_l+q_l-n}\bigr)\otimes\bigotimes_{i=N-q_l+1}^{N}\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}(V).$ The scalar on the one-column module is the ground scalar in the stated ordinary highest-weight convention; adding $\beta$ verifies the global highest-weight shift. By the induction hypothesis $Z(A',c')$ is a finite direct sum of the projectives $P(B')$. Step 1.3 makes each label formed from $B'$ and the ground last column maximal. Step 2.3 thus identifies each summand $R(P(B')\boxtimes\mathbb C_{c_l+q_l-n})$ with its actual projective cover $P(B',G_l)$. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra, construct]

3.3 Tensoring by the finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-module $E=\bigotimes_{i=N-q_l+1}^N\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}(V)$ preserves projectives: its right adjoint is the exact functor tensoring by the distinct dual $E^*$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(P\otimes E,-)=\operatorname{Hom}(P,-\otimes E^*)$. This is in the full parabolic category, where the polynomial components are whole-block summands by step 1.1; a projective of such a summand is projective in that category. All resulting standard labels have total polynomial degree $d$, so the resulting projective belongs to $\mathcal O_c^d$. This proves assertion 1 for arbitrary $A$, not only column-sorted fillings. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra, construct]

4.1 Fix any $B\in\mathrm{Col}_c^d$ and take $A_B=B-A_c$, whose entries in each column are a nonnegative partition $\mu^{(j)}$. The prefix projections $V_{\le j}\twoheadrightarrow E_j=V_{\le j}/V_{\le j-1}$ induce a $\mathfrak p$-epimorphism  $Z_{A_B,c}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb C_c\otimes\bigotimes_j\bigotimes_r\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu_r^{(j)}}(E_j).$ The radical acts trivially on this quotient. For each $j$, the tensor product of row symmetric powers has a nonzero equivariant map onto the simple polynomial Levi module of highest weight $\mu^{(j)}$: apply row symmetrization and column antisymmetrization to the tensor having row $r$ filled by the $r$th basis vector of $E_j$. The column antisymmetrization is nonzero because basis vectors in each column are distinct; the resulting highest vector has weight $\mu^{(j)}$. Its span generates the simple Schur module, and complete reducibility makes the map onto that simple summand an epimorphism. Tensoring these maps produces a $\mathfrak p$-epimorphism onto $V(B)$. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

4.2 Exact induction gives an actual epimorphism $Z(A_B,c)\twoheadrightarrow M(B)\twoheadrightarrow L(B)$. Since $Z(A_B,c)$ is projective by step 3.3, lift this map through $P(B)\twoheadrightarrow L(B)$ to a map $Z(A_B,c)\to P(B)$. Its image surjects onto $L(B)$; essentiality of the cover makes the lift surjective. Projectivity of $P(B)$ splits it. Therefore $P(B)$ is an actual direct summand of $Z(A_B,c)$. This proves assertion 2 without needing generalized Kostka numbers or inference of projectivity from a character. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

4.3 There are finitely many fillings $A$ of degree $d$ ($\binom{d+N-1}{N-1}$), so their direct sum is finite. It contains every indecomposable projective cover and hence generates every finite-length object by epimorphisms from finite sums. Arbitrary projectives in the finite summand are summands of finite sums of these covers, hence of finite sums of the $Z(A,c)$. Exact projection to any union of its whole blocks gives the same assertion for that finite central cut. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

5.1 The averaging inclusion of each $\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}(V_{\le\operatorname{col}(i)})$ into its ordered tensor power gives a $\mathfrak p$-monomorphism $Z_{A,c}\hookrightarrow V_c^{\otimes d}$. PBW makes $U(\mathfrak g)$ free as a right $U(\mathfrak p)$-module, so induction preserves this monomorphism and its cokernel. The tensor identity identifies its ambient induction with $P_c\otimes V^{\otimes d}=T_d$. Thus  $T_d/Z(A,c)\cong U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak p)}\bigl(\mathbb C_{-\rho}\otimes(V_c^{\otimes d}/Z_{A,c})\bigr).$ The finite quotient on the right is semisimple over the reductive column Levi and polynomial of total degree $d$ after removing the $c$ twist. Its radical acts nilpotently (it strictly lowers the column filtration in tensor space). Every finite simple $\mathfrak p$-section has radical invariants by Engel; these are Levi-stable, hence the entire simple section, so that radical acts by zero. Its sections are consequently precisely polynomial Levi simples $V(C)$ for $C\in\mathrm{Col}_c^d$. A finite $\mathfrak p$-composition filtration, induced exactly, is the desired finite standard flag of $T_d/Z(A,c)$. This proves the inclusion and standard-filtered quotient assertion. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra, construct] ∎
