4.2 **Simple images and standard heads.** The exact corner functor kills precisely off-standard simples and identifies its other simple images with the distinct exhaustive $B$-simples; the genuine equivalence of step 4.1 transports these to the corresponding finite-W simples $L_W(A)$. For standard $A$, $P(A)\in\operatorname{add}(T)$, so $\pi P(A)$ is an indecomposable $B$-projective with head $\pi L(A)$: its Hom to each simple is the corresponding Hom from $P(A)$ by the elementary add$(T)$ equivalence. Lift $P(A)\to L(A)$ through $N(A)\to L(A)$; the image surjects onto the standard's head and is therefore the whole standard. Applying exact $\pi$ makes $\pi N(A)$ a nonzero quotient of this indecomposable projective, so it has the same unique simple head. Through Morita, the actual finite-Levi standard $V(A)=V_{\!\mathrm{Wh}}N(A)$ likewise has unique simple head $L_W(A)$. This does not assert that a specified finite-Levi highest vector generates it. 5.1 **Independent row compression.** Add $t$ full ground rows until the enlarged smallest column has height at least $d$; all roots $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$ remain fixed. In $U^+$ let $e$ project onto tensors using only retained rows. The actual dot formula preserves row multisets, so $e$ commutes with dots and crossings and belongs to the left image by the independent raw first centralizer of [F1]. Each right matrix restricts identically under the retained-box embedding: left neighbours and row/column indices agree and its scalar $a_j$ is unchanged. Therefore $eU^+=U$ as right Hecke modules. Extending a commuting endomorphism of $eU^+$ by zero shows $eA^+e=A$, where $A^+,A$ are the large and small left image algebras. For the finite-Levi standard, the zero-deleted-occupancy part of $\mathbb C_{c_j}\otimes S_{\mu^{(j)}}(\mathbb C^{q_j+t})$ is $\mathbb C_{c_j}\otimes S_{\mu^{(j)}}(\mathbb C^{q_j})$ by its tensor Young-symmetrizer realization, and vanishes exactly when a deleted-row partition entry is nonzero. In the original path expansion, Levi projection kills off-column factors and each surviving strictly-forward path has at most one factor in each column. A deleted intermediate row changes removed-coordinate occupancy in a column with no later factor there to undo it; compression therefore kills that monomial. Remaining paths are exactly the small paths. The head shift in column $j$ changes by $-(j-1)t$, cancelling the retained source-reading shift $+(j-1)t$; its normalized highest weight remains the entry plus row index minus one. The corner action and its Gauss coefficients consequently give $eV(A^+)=V(A)$ for contractible labels and zero for noncontractible labels, as actual module identifications. 5.2 **Simple contraction without highest-vector assumptions.** Every noncontractible standard simple is killed by $e$, because it is a quotient of the standard annihilated in step 5.1. For a finite algebra and an idempotent $e$, restriction gives a bijection between its simple modules surviving $e$ and the simples of $eAe$: any nonzero vector in $eL$ generates $eL=eAe v$, and for a small simple $M$ the module $Ae\otimes_{eAe}M$ has a unique simple quotient surviving $e$. Indeed its $e$-part is $M$; every proper submodule has zero $e$-part, since a submodule containing that part generates the whole induced module. The sum of all such proper submodules is still annihilated by $e$ and is the unique maximal submodule. Apply this to the independent corner in step 5.1. By step 4.2 both simple lists are complete, and small standard labels are in bijection with contractible large standard labels by adjoining full ground rows. Thus the survivors form a subset of the contractible list with exactly the same finite cardinality, so every contractible simple survives. Its restriction is a simple quotient of $eV(A^+)=V(A)$, which has the unique head from step 4.2, hence $eL_W(A^+)=L_W(A)$. [F1, step 4.2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

5.3 **Fullness and the central Hecke corner.** If a contractible $A^+$ and another label have the same entry-multiset central character, admissibility separates charge cosets and identifies all charges within each coset. Its minimal bottom entry $c_j+1-(n+t)$ appears in each column of that coset; strictness and nonnegative ground differences force the same entries into the bottom row of the other label. Remove that row and repeat for every deleted row. Thus the contractible labels form a union of whole central-character blocks. Let $f$ be their central support in $A^+$; then $e=ef=fe$, since $e$ kills all complementary simples. Every simple of $A_f=fA^+f$ survives $e$ by step 5.2. If $A_feA_f$ were proper, its nonzero quotient would have a simple killed by $e$, a contradiction. Hence $f=\sum_k a_keb_k$. Multiplication $A_fe\otimes_{eA_fe}eM\to M$ has inverse $m\mapsto\sum_k a_ke\otimes eb_km$; balancing and this identity verify both composites. This gives a faithful Morita equivalence on all supported-block modules. The operator $f$ belongs to the right centralizer and is central there, so restriction identifies $fB^+f=\operatorname{End}_{A_f}(fU^+)^{\mathrm{op}}$ with $B=\operatorname{End}_{eA_fe}(eU^+)^{\mathrm{op}}$. The enlarged stable action is faithful by the independent filtered construction in [F1], giving $B=fH_d(\mathbf a)f$. Its trace is therefore symmetric and nondegenerate by [F4]. This local proof precedes, and does not depend on, a later stable-range definition. [F1, F2, F4, step 5.1, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 **The actual duality comparison.** Let $\beta:T\to DT$ be the tensor contravariant form. Put $\delta M=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M,T)$ with $(bf)(m)=f(m)b^*$, and $X^\#=\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(X,B)$ with the same twist. Transposition gives the natural isomorphism $\delta M\to\pi DM$, $f\mapsto Df\circ\beta$, whose inverse is $g\mapsto\beta^{-1}\circ Dg\circ\operatorname{bidual}_M$. This uses only $T\cong DT$, not individual projective self-duality. Tensor-Hom gives $\delta\pi^*\cong\#$, and the symmetric trace of step 5.3 gives $\#\cong D_B$ by $h\mapsto\operatorname{tr}\circ h$. Define $\theta_M:M\to D\pi^*\delta M$ by $\theta_M(m)(t\otimes f)=(t,f(m))$. The form identity $(xtb,t')=(t,x^{\mathrm{tr}}t'b^*)$ makes this balanced and $\mathfrak g$-linear. Applying $\pi$ gives a natural map $\pi\to\pi D\pi^*\delta\cong\#\pi D$ between exact functors; exactness of $\#$ follows from the symmetric algebra's injective regular module. On $T$ it is the regular trace-duality isomorphism, and hence is invertible on add$(T)$. Use the copresentations of step 3.1 and kernels to prove it invertible on every projective; use finite projective presentations and cokernels to prove it invertible on every object. Dualizing gives the genuine module isomorphism $\pi D\cong D_B\pi$. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 5.3, construct, algebra]

6.2 **Individual standard prinjectives are self-dual.** For standard $A$, $I(A)=DP(A)\in\operatorname{add}(T)$ by step 1.3. Step 6.1 gives $\pi I(A)=D_B\pi P(A)$ and fixes its simple label because $DL(A)=L(A)$. In a symmetric algebra the primitive projective $Be_A$ is also the injective hull of its corresponding simple: the perfect trace identifies its linear dual with the corresponding right ideal, and the $*$ twist sends the primitive to the primitive carrying the dual simple. Since that simple is fixed here, $D_B\pi P(A)\cong\pi P(A)$. Both $I(A)$ and $P(A)$ are projectives, so full faithfulness of step 3.2 lifts this isomorphism and its inverse; their composites are identities by faithfulness. Hence $I(A)\cong P(A)$, so each standard projective-injective is individually self-dual. This last conclusion has been derived after the copresentation, central-corner and duality constructions, and was not used to prove them. [F2, step 1.3, step 3.2, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
