1.1 **A regular corner in the stable realization.** Add full ground rows until there are $d$ distinct rows of length $l$, and choose one rightmost box in each such row; let $v_S$ be their ordered tensor and $e_S$ the row-weight projection onto tensors using these rows once each. The actual tensor matrices in [F3] preserve row multisets, so this projection commutes with the right Hecke action and belongs to the left image by the first centralizer. The vectors $v_Sx_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}w$, $0\le r_i<l$, $w\in S_d$, have distinct leading tensors: the leading dot moves its box one column left, while a permutation changes the ordered distinct rows. They therefore form a basis of $e_SU$ by triangularity, and its cardinality is $l^dd!$. Thus $b\mapsto v_Sb$ identifies $e_SU$ with the right regular $H_d(\mathbf a)$-module. Put $A=\operatorname{End}_{H_d(\mathbf a)}U$. Since $e_SU$ is regular, $Ae_S=\operatorname{Hom}_{H_d}(e_SU,U)\cong U$ by evaluation at $v_S$. Consequently $h:H_d(\mathbf a)\to e_SAe_S$, characterized by $h(b)v_S=v_Sb$, is an algebra isomorphism, and $\theta\mapsto\theta(v_S)$ identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(U,M)$ with $e_SM$ for every finite-image module $M$. The left Hecke action on this corner is $h$, not the naive right action on $M$. [F3, F5, construct, algebra]

1.2 **The cyclic symmetric generator and its bottom quotient.** Write $A-A_c=(a_1,\ldots,a_N)$ and form the actual cumulative-column $\mathfrak p$-module $Z=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Sym}^{a_i}(V_{\le\operatorname{col}(i)})$. Its bottom column degree is $b=\sum_i a_i(l-\operatorname{col}(i))$; the higher-degree part is a $\mathfrak p$-submodule, and $Z/Z_{>b}$ is the inflated Levi module $Z_b=\bigotimes_j\bigotimes_r\operatorname{Sym}^{\mu_r^{(j)}}(E_j)$. Put $w_A=\sum_{w\in\prod_iS_{a_i}}w$ and $x_A=\prod_i\prod_{k=1}^{a_i}\prod_{j>\operatorname{col}(i)}(x_{a_1+\cdots+a_{i-1}+k}-a_j)$. Apply these factors in order starting with the first tensor place. The actual dot formula gives $v_Sx_A=v_{\mathbf j}$, where $\mathbf j$ has the ordered rows of $v_S$ and column sequence prescribed by $A-A_c$; subtracting $a_j$ cancels the scalar at column $j$ and moves that box one column left. Hence $v_Sx_Aw_A=v_{\mathbf j}w_A\in e_SZ$. More generally the vectors $v_Swx_1^{r_1}\cdots x_d^{r_d}x_Aw_A$, with $0\le r_k<\operatorname{col}(\mathbf j_k)$ and $w$ ranging over cosets of $\prod_i S_{a_i}$, have distinct leading symmetrized tensors and span $e_SZ$ by the ordinary symmetric-tensor basis. Thus this vector generates $e_SZ$ as a corner module, and its image generates $e_S(Z/Z_{>b})$. These are actual vector identities and triangular basis statements, rather than a character identification. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 **The affine colour fibre.** Ordinary Levi Schur-Weyl gives $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak h}(U,Z_b)\cong\bigotimes_j M^{\mu^{(j)}}$ on the canonical colour fibre, where $M^{\mu}$ is the symmetric-group permutation module. Its vectors in the corner are $v_{\mathbf j}ww_A+e_SZ_{>b}$ with $w\in S_{d_1}\times\cdots\times S_{d_l}$. For $k=d_1+\cdots+d_{j-1}+1$, the identity $v_{\mathbf j}=v_Sx_A$ and commutativity of dots give $h(x_k)v_{\mathbf j}ww_A=v_Sx_Ax_kww_A=v_{\mathbf j}x_kww_A$. In the bottom quotient the dot formula has scalar $a_j=c_j+q_j-n$: its left-neighbour term has higher degree, within-column terms involving a preceding place in this block are absent for its first place, and the remaining terms have higher degree or another colour fibre. Thus $x_k$ acts as $a_j$ on that canonical fibre. The affine cross relations then determine the remaining dots as $a_j$ plus their within-block Jucys-Murphy sums. This proves that the fibre inclusion is a homomorphism for the affine Young subalgebra $H_{d_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{d_l}$, with module $\bigotimes_j\mathrm{ev}_{a_j}^*M^{\mu^{(j)}}$, and its image generates the entire corner by step 1.2. 1.4 **The genuine Specht isomorphism.** The Levi Young-symmetrizer map gives an actual quotient $Z_b\twoheadrightarrow V(A)$ and hence a $\mathfrak p$-quotient $Z/Z_{>b}\twoheadrightarrow V(A)$. The canonical colour fibre quotient is $\bigotimes_j S^{\mu^{(j)}}$, by ordinary Levi Schur-Weyl. The affine fibre map in step 1.3 descends through this actual quotient and still generates: $e_S$ is exact and the generating vector of its source maps onto a generator of its quotient. Affine induction therefore gives a surjection $S(A)\twoheadrightarrow e_SV(A)$. The multilinear row-weight part of $\bigotimes_j S_{\mu^{(j)}}(E_j)$ has one summand for each allocation of the $d$ distinct rows into ordered colour subsets of sizes $d_j$, and on each subset its weight space is the polytabloid Specht space. Its dimension is $d!/(d_1!\cdots d_l!)\prod_j\dim S^{\mu^{(j)}}$. This is also the induced module's dimension, since the affine algebra is free over its affine Young subalgebra with the finite shuffle basis. The existing surjection is therefore an isomorphism. Steps 1.1 and [F2] identify its target with $\operatorname{Hom}_{W_d}(U,V(A))=\pi(N(A))$, preserving the actual Hecke maps. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

1.5 **Descent to the original pyramid.** Extend the original label $A$ by the added ground rows, producing a contractible large label $A^+$. Its column partitions $\mu^{(j)}$ and roots $a_j$ are unchanged. The actual original-path/zero-occupancy compression in [F3] gives $eV(A^+)=V(A)$ and $eU^+=U$, with the faithful supporting-block Morita equivalence and natural Hom comparison. Every simple constituent of $V(A^+)$ has the same entry-multiset central character as $A^+$; the bottom-row minimum argument forces every such label to be contractible. Hence the supporting central idempotent satisfies $fV(A^+)=V(A^+)$, and the Hom comparison identifies the original $\operatorname{Hom}_{W_d}(U,V(A))$ with the entire large Hom module, rather than an arbitrary quotient of it. Step 1.4 therefore descends the same intrinsic Specht isomorphism to the original pyramid. [F3, step 1.4, construct, algebra]
