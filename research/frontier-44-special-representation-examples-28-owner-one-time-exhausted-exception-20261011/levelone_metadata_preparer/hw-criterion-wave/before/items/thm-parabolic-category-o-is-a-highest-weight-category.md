## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let
$\mathfrak p=\mathfrak l_J\oplus\mathfrak u_J$ and $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$
be as in [[def-parabolic-category-o-for-a-standard-parabolic]]. Then:

**(1) (Simples.)** Within an integral block, $L(\lambda)\in\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$
if and only if $\lambda$ is $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral; every simple
object of $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ is of this form.

**(2) (Standards.)** For $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral $\lambda$ the
generalized Verma $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ lies in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$,
has finite length, has $L(\lambda)$ as its unique simple quotient (head), and
$[M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda):L(\mu)]\neq0$ forces $\mu\le\lambda$ in the
dominance order; $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ is the largest quotient of
$M(\lambda)$ lying in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$.

**(3) (Enough projectives.)** The inclusion
$\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}\subseteq\mathcal O$ is exact and has a left adjoint
$L_{\mathfrak p}$ (the largest $\mathfrak p$-locally-finite quotient);
$L_{\mathfrak p}$ is right exact and sends projective objects of $\mathcal O$
to projective objects of $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$. Consequently, for every
$\mathfrak p$-dominant integral $\lambda$ the object
$P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda):=L_{\mathfrak p}(P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda))$ is a
projective cover of $L(\lambda)$ in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$, and
$\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ has enough projectives and injectives.

**(4) (Generalized-standard flags, BGG reciprocity and duality.)** Every
projective in a fixed integral block has a finite generalized Verma flag. Its
projective cover surjects to its labelled generalized standard with a kernel
filtered by higher-labelled generalized standards. The simple and
generalized-standard classes form bases of the finite block Grothendieck
group, and characters are injective on this group. For $\mathfrak p$-dominant
integral $\lambda,\mu$,
$$(P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda):M_{\mathfrak p}(\mu))=[M_{\mathfrak p}(\mu):L(\lambda)],$$
the multiplicities being finite; moreover restricted duality restricts to an
exact contravariant involution of $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ exchanging
$M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ and $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$, and
$\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}}(M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda),\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\mu))=0$
for all $\lambda,\mu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The standard parabolic $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak l_J\oplus\mathfrak u_J$ of [[def-standard-parabolic-and-levi-subalgebras-from-simple-roots]], the category $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ of [[def-parabolic-category-o-for-a-standard-parabolic]] with its generalized Vermas $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ and costandards $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)=DM_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$, and the ambient category $\mathcal O$ with standards $\Delta(\lambda)=M(\lambda)$ and costandards $\nabla(\lambda)=D\Delta(\lambda)$.

[F1] $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ is a full Serre subcategory of $\mathcal O$ containing $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ for $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral $\lambda$; $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ is the largest quotient of $M(\lambda)$ in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ with unique simple head $L(\lambda)$ and finite length; the simples of $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ are the $L(\lambda)$ with $\lambda$ $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral; restricted duality preserves $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ and $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ is a submodule of $\nabla(\lambda)$ with socle $L(\lambda)$ ([[def-parabolic-category-o-for-a-standard-parabolic]] (3)-(7) and its remarks).

[F2] The ambient category: every object has finite length; $\mathcal O$ is abelian and closed under extensions; enough projectives exist; projective covers are indecomposable and unique; simples are the $L(\lambda)$; restricted duality $D$ is an exact contravariant involution fixing simple classes; $\operatorname{Hom}(\Delta(\lambda),\nabla(\mu))$ is $0$ unless $\lambda=\mu$, when it is one-dimensional, and $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O}(\Delta(\lambda),\nabla(\mu))=0$ for all $\lambda,\mu$; finite-length objects decompose into indecomposables; $\mathcal O$ splits as the direct sum over central characters, which are constant exactly on dot-Weyl orbits; dominant weights are the maxima of their Weyl orbits ([[thm-every-category-o-object-has-finite-length]], [[thm-category-o-is-abelian-and-extension-closed]], [[thm-category-o-has-enough-projectives]], [[prop-projective-covers-in-o-are-indecomposable-and-unique]], [[thm-simple-objects-of-category-o-are-highest-weight-modules]], [[prop-restricted-duality-is-an-exact-involution-on-category-o]], [[lem-standard-costandard-hom-and-ext-vanishing]], [[lem-finite-length-objects-decompose-into-indecomposables]], [[thm-category-o-decomposes-by-generalized-central-character]], [[cor-central-characters-are-dot-weyl-orbits]], [[lem-dominant-weights-are-maxima-of-their-weyl-orbits]], [[def-standard-and-costandard-objects-in-category-o]], [[def-bgg-category-o]]).

[F3] The parabolic Levi data: $\mathfrak l_J$ is reductive with center $\mathfrak z_J$, $\mathfrak u_J$ is the nilradical of $\mathfrak p$, and the element $\zeta$ of [[def-standard-parabolic-and-levi-subalgebras-from-simple-roots]] (5) gives a positive integral grading on $\mathfrak u_J$, degree $0$ on $\mathfrak l_J$, and finite-dimensional fixed-degree PBW slices of $U(\mathfrak u_J)$.

[F4] Weyl's complete reducibility theorem for the semisimple part $\mathfrak s_J$ and the PBW basis theorem ([[thm-weyls-complete-reducibility-theorem]], [[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F5] Projectivity and injectivity mean exactness of the represented Hom functors ([[def-projective-object]], [[def-injective-object]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: build the largest locally finite quotient as a minimal kernel, construct bounded representable projectives in an integral block, prove the generalized-standard flag and Hom/$\operatorname{Ext}^1$ vanishing inside $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$, and finish with BGG reciprocity and unitriangular Grothendieck bases.

1.1 **Simples and standards (parts (1),(2)).** By [F1], within an integral block $L(\lambda)$ lies in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ exactly when $\lambda$ is $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral, and every simple of $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ arises this way; this is (1). Also by [F1], for such $\lambda$ the generalized Verma $M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ lies in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$, is a quotient of $M(\lambda)$, is the largest quotient in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$, and has unique simple head $L(\lambda)$ and finite length; since all its composition factors are composition factors of $M(\lambda)$, whose simple factors are the $L(\mu)$ with $\mu\le\lambda$, the support condition $\mu\le\lambda$ holds. This is (2). [F1, F2]

1.2 **The reflector $L_{\mathfrak p}$.** Let $M\in\mathcal O$ have finite length and let $\mathcal K$ be the set of subobjects $K\subseteq M$ with $M/K\in\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$; it is nonempty ($K=M$ gives the zero quotient, which lies in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$) and the lengths $\ell(M/K)$ are bounded, so choose $K_0\in\mathcal K$ with $\ell(M/K_0)$ minimal. If $K\in\mathcal K$, then the diagonal $M\to M/K\oplus M/K_0$ has kernel $K\cap K_0$, so $M/(K\cap K_0)$ embeds into $M/K\oplus M/K_0$, which lies in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ by [F1] (closed under finite sums, subobjects and quotients); hence $K\cap K_0\in\mathcal K$ and minimality gives $\ell(M/(K\cap K_0))\ge\ell(M/K_0)$, so $K\cap K_0=K_0$, that is, $K_0\subseteq K$. Thus $K_0$ is the intersection of all members of $\mathcal K$ and $L_{\mathfrak p}M:=M/K_0$ is the largest quotient of $M$ lying in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$. Any morphism $f:M\to N$ with $N\in\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ has $M/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f\subseteq N$ in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$, so $\ker f\in\mathcal K$, hence $K_0\subseteq\ker f$ and $f$ factors uniquely through $L_{\mathfrak p}M\to N$. This universal property makes $L_{\mathfrak p}$ a functor left adjoint to the inclusion, and a left adjoint is right exact; the inclusion is exact because $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ is a full abelian subcategory closed under kernels and cokernels computed in $\mathcal O$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 **Bounded representable projectives in a block.** Fix an integral central-character block $\mathcal O_\chi^{\mathfrak p}$ and its finite dot orbit in the ambient Weyl group; let $\kappa$ be the dot-dominant representative of the orbit, so every label $\lambda$ of the block satisfies $\lambda\le\kappa$: indeed $\kappa+\rho$ is dominant, so $\kappa+\rho-w(\kappa+\rho)\in Q^+$ for every $w$, and every object of the block has all simple composition factors in the orbit, hence all its weights are $\le\kappa$; its $\zeta$-weights (for the grading element $\zeta$ of [F3]) therefore satisfy $\eta(\zeta)\le\kappa(\zeta)$. For a $\mathfrak p$-dominant integral label $\eta$ in the block put $N:=\kappa(\zeta)-\eta(\zeta)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and let $E=E(\eta)$ be its finite-dimensional Levi simple; form the finite-dimensional $\mathfrak p$-module $B_N(E):=U(\mathfrak p)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak l_J)}E$ modulo the subspace spanned by all $\mathfrak u_J$-PBW monomials of $\zeta$-degree $>N$; the cutoff is $\mathfrak p$-stable because $\mathfrak u_J$ raises the $\zeta$-degree and $\mathfrak l_J$ preserves it. The module $B_N(E)$ represents $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak l_J}(E,-)$ on $\mathfrak p$-finite modules whose $\zeta$-weights are at most $\kappa(\zeta)$: a $\mathfrak p$-linear map $B_N(E)\to W$ is the same as a $\mathfrak l_J$-linear map $E\to W$ because $E$ generates $B_N(E)$, and every excluded monomial of degree $>N$ maps $E$ into a weight space whose $\zeta$-weight exceeds $\kappa(\zeta)$, hence zero in such $W$. The functor $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak l_J}(E,-)$ is exact on this class: given an epimorphism of $\mathfrak p$-finite modules in the class, lift the finitely many generators of $E$'s image through it inside a finite-dimensional Levi submodule containing those generators and split the resulting Levi surjection by complete reducibility [F4]. Hence $Q(E):=\operatorname{pr}_\chi\bigl(U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{\mathfrak p}B_N(E)\bigr)$, the projection of the induced module to the block $\chi$, represents an exact functor on the $\mathfrak p$-finite part of the block and is projective there; $U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{\mathfrak p}-$ is exact because $U(\mathfrak g)$ is free as a right $U(\mathfrak p)$-module by PBW [F4], and block projection is exact by [F2]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 **Parabolic projective covers and enough projectives (part (3)).** If $P\in\mathcal O$ is projective then, by the adjunction of step 1.2, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}}(L_{\mathfrak p}P,-)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O}(P,-)$ is exact on $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ (exactness in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ coincides with exactness in $\mathcal O$ by fullness and Serre closure), so $L_{\mathfrak p}P$ is projective in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$. Let $P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda)$ be the projective cover of $L(\lambda)$ in $\mathcal O$; right exactness gives a surjection $P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda):=L_{\mathfrak p}P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L_{\mathfrak p}L(\lambda)=L(\lambda)$. It is essential: if $S\subseteq P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ maps onto $L(\lambda)$ and $S'$ is the preimage of $S$ in $P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda)$, then $S'\twoheadrightarrow L(\lambda)$ along the projective cover $P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L(\lambda)$, so $S'=P^{(\mathcal O)}(\lambda)$ and $S=P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$. Hence $P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ is a projective cover of $L(\lambda)$ in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$. Every $M\in\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ is a quotient of a projective: choose a projective $P\in\mathcal O$ with $P\twoheadrightarrow M$ and apply the right exact $L_{\mathfrak p}$, using $L_{\mathfrak p}M\cong M$. Finally, restricted duality $D$ preserves $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ by [F1] and is an exact contravariant involution, so dualizing the projectivity statement gives enough injectives. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 **Hom and $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ inside $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ (part (4), last clause).** The natural maps $M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)\twoheadrightarrow\Delta(\eta)$ (quotient) and $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi)\hookrightarrow\nabla(\xi)$ (submodule) induce an injection $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak p}(M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta),\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi))\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(\Delta(\eta),\nabla(\xi))$, which by [F2] is zero unless $\eta=\xi$, where it is at most one-dimensional; for $\eta=\xi$ the composite $M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)\to L(\eta)\to\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)$ is a nonzero map, so the dimension is one. For an extension $0\to\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi)\to X\to M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)\to0$ in $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ with $\eta$ not strictly below $\xi$: $X$ has no weight strictly above $\eta$ (otherwise a composition factor of $X$ above $\eta$ would have to be the head or come from $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi)$, whose weights are $\le\xi$), so lifting the top $\eta$-weight vector of $X$ produces a highest weight vector whose Levi span is $E(\eta)$, annihilated by $\mathfrak u_J$, and the universal property of $M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)$ splits the standard quotient; the case $\eta<\xi$ dualizes to the first case using the duality of [F1]. This includes incomparable labels, so $\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}}(M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta),\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi))=0$ for all $\eta,\xi$; the vanishing is proved inside $\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}$ and not by restricting the ordinary $\operatorname{Ext}^1$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Generalized-standard flags of parabolic projectives (part (4), first clause).** In the construction of step 1.3, $B_N(E)$ carries the radical-degree filtration by $\zeta$-degree, whose slices are finite-dimensional semisimple $\mathfrak l_J$-modules on which $\mathfrak u_J$ acts by zero; inducing each slice therefore yields a finite direct sum of generalized Verma modules, and after block projection the object $Q(E)$ has a finite generalized-standard flag. Every simple $L(\eta)$ of the block is a quotient of some $Q(E_\eta)$ because its highest-weight vector spans a Levi simple $E_\eta$ mapping to the simple head. For a parabolic projective cover $P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ of step 2.1, a morphism $Q(E_\eta)\to P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ hitting the head $L(\lambda)$ is onto by essentiality of the cover and its projectivity makes it split; hence every parabolic projective cover, and therefore after finite sums and direct summands every projective of the block, is a direct summand of a finite sum of the $Q(E)$'s. The class of modules with a finite generalized-standard flag is closed under finite direct sums and under direct summands: for a maximal flag label $\eta$ of a flagged module $X$, the canonical trace $U(\mathfrak g)X^\eta$ is $M_{\mathfrak p}(\eta)\otimes X^\eta$ with generalized-standard quotient (proved by induction on the original flag: a last non-$\eta$ quotient has zero $\eta$-weight and a last $\eta$ quotient splits by lifting its top weight, which is highest and whose Levi span is $E(\eta)$ with $\mathfrak u_J$ acting by zero; inducting on the shorter flag gives the claim), every endomorphism preserves this trace and its projector acts on the finite multiplicity space, so direct summands retain flags. Hence every projective in the block has a finite generalized Verma flag. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 1.3, algebra]

4.1 **BGG reciprocity and the highest-weight axioms (part (4), middle).** Let $(P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda):M_{\mathfrak p}(\xi))$ be the multiplicity in a generalized-standard flag of step 3.1. Induction on the flag and the vanishing of step 2.2 identify this multiplicity with $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O^{\mathfrak p}}(P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda),\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi))$; projectivity and a composition series of $\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi)$ whose factors are the $L(\eta)$ with $\eta\le\xi$ compute this dimension as $[\nabla_{\mathfrak p}(\xi):L(\lambda)]=[M_{\mathfrak p}(\xi):L(\lambda)]$, the equality by duality of [F1]. This proves BGG reciprocity. By part (2) the multiplicity $[M_{\mathfrak p}(\xi):L(\lambda)]$ is nonzero only for $\lambda\le\xi$, and it equals $1$ for $\xi=\lambda$, so the reciprocity formula gives the standard support $\xi\ge\lambda$ with diagonal multiplicity one; hence the simple and generalized-standard classes form bases of the finite block Grothendieck group by unitriangularity, and characters are injective on it. Removing the maximal higher traces from the flag of $P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ gives the surjection $P^{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow M_{\mathfrak p}(\lambda)$ with kernel filtered by higher-labelled generalized standards, which is the highest-weight axiom; the duality clause and the $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ vanishing are those of [F1] and step 2.2. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra]

5.1 Parts (1), (2), (3) and (4) are proved in steps 1.1, 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1 respectively; the statements about enough injectives and the duality exchange are in steps 2.1 and 2.2 with [F1]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

The proof is the owner's local Levi/PBW/flag/Weyl route: it does not import
the properly stratified parabolic-presentable category of
Mazorchuk--Stroppel, and no Koszul duality, Koszul grading or
singular/parabolic equivalence is used. The bounded module $B_N(E)$ and the
trace argument supply the projective flags, which are not obtained by applying
the merely right-exact reflector to ordinary Verma flags. Injectivity of
characters and the Grothendieck bases are consequences of the finite
unitriangular multiplicity matrix, exactly as stated.
