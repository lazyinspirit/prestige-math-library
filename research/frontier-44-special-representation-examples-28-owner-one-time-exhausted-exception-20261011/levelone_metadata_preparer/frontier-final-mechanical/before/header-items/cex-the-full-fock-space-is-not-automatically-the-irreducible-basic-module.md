## Statement refuted

**False statement.** The full level-one Fock space is automatically its own
irreducible basic module: at $e = 3$ the transported Fock space
$F_{\mathrm{sym}}$ coincides with its cyclic submodule
$M_{\mathrm{sym}} = J^{-1}V(\Lambda_0)$ generated by the empty partition, so
every standard vector $M_\lambda$, and every ambient lower global basis vector
$G_{\mathrm{sym}}(\lambda)$, would lie in the basic module; equivalently, on the
quantum-group side every standard vector $|\lambda\rangle$ and every ambient
$G(\lambda)$ would lie in $M = U_q|\mathrm{empty}\rangle$.

This is the natural expectation that would follow from reading the basic
crystal's label set and the ambient standard chart as one and the same.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $e = 3$, charge $0$, the transport and signature conventions of
[[def-symmetric-group-level-one-residue-and-partition-label-convention]], and
the axiom of choice as declared in the contract ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The basic module in the symmetric-group convention is
$M_{\mathrm{sym}} = J^{-1}V(\Lambda_0) = J^{-1}M_{\mathrm{QG}}$, its crystal is
the empty component and its labels are exactly the $3$-restricted partitions;
$(4)$ is not $3$-restricted and $(4)' = (1,1,1,1)$ is not $3$-regular
([[thm-restricted-partition-component-is-the-basic-crystal-in-the-symmetric-group-convention]],
[[def-symmetric-group-level-one-residue-and-partition-label-convention]]).

[F2] The full Fock crystal has crystal basis $(F_0, B_F)$ with
$F_0 = \bigoplus_{\text{all }\lambda} A_0|\lambda\rangle$ and
$B_F = \{|\lambda\rangle + qF_0\}$; its empty connected component is precisely
the $e$-regular partitions, and the basic module $M = U_q|\mathrm{empty}\rangle$
has lower $A_0$ lattice $M \cap F_0$
([[thm-the-empty-component-is-the-highest-weight-crystal-b-lambda-zero]]).

[F3] Top-isotypic splitting: for an integrable module with a crystal basis
$(L,B)$ and a maximal weight $\lambda$ with $N_0 := U_qM_\lambda$,
$M = N_0 \oplus N_1$ and $L = (L \cap N_0) \oplus (L \cap N_1)$, with
$B \cap (L \cap N_0)/q(L \cap N_0)$ a crystal basis of $N_0$
([[thm-crystal-bases-of-integrable-modules-decompose-into-highest-weight-crystals]]).

[F4] The ambient lower global basis satisfies
$G(\lambda) = |\lambda\rangle + \sum_{\mu} d_{\mu\lambda}|\mu\rangle$ with
$d \in q\mathbb{Z}[q]$, so $G(\lambda) \equiv |\lambda\rangle \bmod qF_0$, and
the transported $G_{\mathrm{sym}}(\lambda) = J^{-1}G(\lambda')$ satisfies
$G_{\mathrm{sym}}(\lambda) \equiv M_\lambda \bmod q(F_0)_{\mathrm{sym}}$
([[thm-the-basic-representation-global-basis-is-unitriangular-in-the-partition-basis]]).

[F5] The standard residue classes $\{|\lambda\rangle + qF_0\}$ form a basis of
$F_0/qF_0$ over $A_0/qA_0$, so they are linearly independent; in particular the
class of $|\lambda\rangle$ lies in the span of the classes of a subset $S$ of
labels only if $\lambda \in S$. [F2]

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The two finite checks. The shape $(4)$ is not $3$-restricted, because $\lambda_1 - \lambda_2 = 4 - 0 = 4 \ge 3$; its conjugate is $(1,1,1,1)$ (four columns of height one), not $(1,1,1)$, and it is not $3$-regular because the positive part $1$ occurs four times, and $4 \ge 3$. By [F1], therefore, neither $(4)$ nor $(1,1,1,1)$ is a label of the basic component in its respective convention: $(4)$ is not a basic label in the symmetric-group model, and $(1,1,1,1)$ is not an $e$-regular label on the quantum-group side. [given, F1]

2.1 $|1111\rangle$ lies outside the field basic module. The vacuum is the unique maximal-weight vector of $F_{\mathrm{QG}}$ at weight $\Lambda_0$ (its label weight space is one-dimensional, spanned by $|\varnothing\rangle$), so [F3] applies with $N_0 = M = U_q|\mathrm{empty}\rangle = V(\Lambda_0)$ and gives $F_0 \cap M = F_0 \cap N_0$ as the lower lattice of $N_0$, whose residue space is spanned by the classes of the labels of the empty component, that is, by the $e$-regular labels by [F2]. Suppose $|1111\rangle \in M$; then $|1111\rangle \in F_0 \cap M$, so its class $|1111\rangle + qF_0$ would be a linear combination of classes of $e$-regular labels; but the standard classes are independent by [F5] and $|1111\rangle$ is not $e$-regular by step 1.1, a contradiction. Hence $|1111\rangle \notin M$, and in particular the raw standard vector is nonzero and excluded. [given, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The ambient lower lift is excluded as well. By [F4] $G(1111) = |1111\rangle \bmod qF_0$, so $G(1111)$ has the same residue class as $|1111\rangle$; if $G(1111) \in M$, then $G(1111) \in F_0 \cap M$ and its residue class would lie in the span of the $e$-regular classes, contradicting the independence of [F5] exactly as in step 2.1. Hence $G(1111) \notin M$, i.e. the ambient lower global lift is outside the field basic module as well. [given, F2, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 Transport to the symmetric-group convention. Applying $J^{-1}$ to the field module $M$ gives $M_{\mathrm{sym}} = J^{-1}M$, and $J^{-1}|1111\rangle = M_{(1111)'} = M_{(4)}$; since $J^{-1}$ is a linear isomorphism, $M_{(4)} = J^{-1}|1111\rangle \notin M_{\mathrm{sym}}$ by step 2.1, and likewise $G_{\mathrm{sym}}(4) = J^{-1}G(1111) \notin M_{\mathrm{sym}}$ by step 3.1. Since also $M_{(4)} \in F_{\mathrm{sym}}$, the transported full Fock space is strictly larger than its cyclic basic module, refuting the false statement. [given, F1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The full signature remains meaningful on these shapes. At $(4)$ the $0$-nodes are the removable node $(1,4)$ of residue $3 \equiv 0$ and the addable nodes $(1,5)$ of residue $4 \equiv 1$ and $(2,1)$ of residue $-1 \equiv 2$; the residue-$0$ word is the single $-$, so the modular signature has good node $(1,4)$ and $\tilde e_0(4) = (3)$, an edge of the full transported crystal. Both $(4)$ and $(3)$ are non-$3$-restricted, so this edge joins two vertices of other components, not a route into the empty component, in agreement with the component identification of [F1]; modular signature statements restricted to restricted labels do not apply to $(4)$ as a basic label. [given, F1, step 1.1] ∎
