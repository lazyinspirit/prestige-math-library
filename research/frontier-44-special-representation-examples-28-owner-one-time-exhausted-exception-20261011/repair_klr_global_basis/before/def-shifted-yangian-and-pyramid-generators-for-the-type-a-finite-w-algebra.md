## Definition

Work over $\mathbb C$ and fix $n\ge1$ together with a shift matrix
$\sigma=(s_{i,j})_{1\le i,j\le n}$ of nonnegative integers satisfying
$s_{i,j}+s_{j,k}=s_{i,k}$ whenever $i\le j\le k$ or $i\ge j\ge k$, and
$s_{i,i}=0$. The **shifted Yangian** $Y_n(\sigma)$ is the associative unital
$\mathbb C$-algebra ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]) generated by the
shifted Drinfeld generators $D_i^{(r)}$ ($1\le i\le n$, $r\ge1$),
$E_{i,j}^{(r)}$, $F_{j,i}^{(r)}$ ($1\le i<j\le n$, $r>s_{i,j}$) together with
the higher root generators $E_{i,j}^{(r)},F_{j,i}^{(r)}$ for $i<j$ and all
$r>s_{i,j}$, subject to the shifted Drinfeld relations of the source (the
shifted analogues of the $D$-$D$, $D$-$E$, $D$-$F$, $E$-$E$, $F$-$F$ and
$E$-$F$ relations, with the shift matrix $\sigma$ entering through the ranges
and the constants). Two filtrations are fixed on it: the **canonical
filtration**, in which each generator of upper index $r$ has degree $r$, and
the **loop filtration**, in which it has degree $r-1$; their associated
graded algebras are different (the canonical one is commutative polynomial,
the loop one is a current algebra).

For the pyramid $\lambda=(0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n)$ with conjugate
$q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$ ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]])
the relevant shift matrix is $s_{i,j}=p_j-p_i$ for $i\le j$ and $s_{i,j}=0$
for $i\ge j$, and the **type-A finite W-algebra** is the quotient
$$W(\lambda)=Y_n(\sigma)/\bigl(D_1^{(r)},\ r>p_1\bigr)$$
of $Y_n(\sigma)$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the higher modes of
$D_1$ beyond $p_1$.

Write the Gauss decomposition $T(u)=F(u)D(u)E(u)$ of the generating matrix,
with $T(u)=\sum_{r\ge0}T_{i,j}^{(r)}u^{-r}$, $T_{i,j}^{(0)}=\delta_{i,j}$, and
**no** additional factor $u^{s_{i,j}}$: the coefficient of $u^{-r}$ is the
mode $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$. Its modes satisfy $T_{i,j}^{(r)}=0$ for
$0<r\le s_{i,j}$, and in the finite quotient also $T_{i,j}^{(r)}=0$ for
$r>S_{i,j}:=s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$, so the finite quotient has the PBW basis
of ordered monomials
$$T_{i,j}^{(r)},\qquad s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}.$$
The auxiliary signed path polynomials used in the source to construct the
Gauss generators are not all identified with these shifted $T$-modes; the root
of $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ is the weight $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$, and the
surviving modes have loop leading term $(-1)^{r-1}e_{i,j;r-1}$ in the
centralizer basis of the pyramid.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, a shift matrix $\sigma$ as above, the pyramid $\lambda$ with row lengths $0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n$ and columns $q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$, and the generators and filtrations of the Definition.

[F1] The pyramid data: $e\in\mathfrak g_1$ of Jordan type $\lambda$, the centralizer basis $e_{i,j;r}$ with $s_{i,j}\le r<p_j$, and the character $\chi$; the finite W-algebra $W(\lambda)$ is generated by $\mathfrak p$-matrix coefficients subject to the loop filtration ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]], [[def-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-and-whittaker-category]]).

[F2] The RTT presentation: $\mathbb C[Y]$ is generated by the matrix coefficients $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ with the RTT relation $[T_{i,j}^{(r)},T_{h,k}^{(s)}]=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(r,s)-1}(T_{i,k}^{(r+s-1-t)}T_{h,j}^{(t)}-T_{i,k}^{(t)}T_{h,j}^{(r+s-1-t)})$ and $T^{(0)}=I$; the Gauss factorization is formal matrix multiplication and inversion, and the structure constants of the D/E/F presentation are the coefficients of the products of the RTT blocks ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]]).

[F3] Truncation: in $W(\lambda)$ the relation $D_1^{(r)}=0$ for $r>p_1$ removes exactly the modes beyond the shift, and the loop symbol of $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ is $(-1)^{r-1}e_{i,j;r-1}$ with $(i,j,r-1)$ in the centralizer index set; the finite range of nonzero $T$-modes is $s_{i,j}<r\le s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$ ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The RTT/Drinfeld presentation data and the pyramid $\lambda$.

1.1 Shift-matrix arithmetic. For the pyramid shift $s_{i,j}=p_j-p_i$ (when $i\le j$) and $s_{i,j}=0$ (when $i\ge j$) one has $s_{i,j}+s_{j,k}=s_{i,k}$ whenever the indices are monotone: if $i\le j\le k$ then $(p_k-p_i)=(p_j-p_i)+(p_k-p_j)$; if $i\ge j\ge k$ then all three shifts vanish. Also $s_{i,i}=0$, so $\sigma$ is an admissible shift matrix and the shifted Drinfeld presentation of the Definition is the one attached to the pyramid, with the shift data of [F1]. [F1, algebra]

1.2 Gauss decomposition and the two filtrations. In the RTT algebra every diagonal entry of $T(u)$ has constant term $1$, so the matrices $D(u),E(u),F(u)$ are obtained from $T(u)$ by formal triangular inversion and multiplication; assigning canonical degree $r$ to $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ makes the RTT relation filtered with the right-hand side of strictly smaller canonical degree, while assigning loop degree $r-1$ gives the current-algebra associated graded. The two associated graded algebras are distinct because the canonical graded algebra is generated by commuting symbols (the RTT relation is a commutator relation for the canonical leading terms) whereas the loop graded algebra is the enveloping algebra of the current algebra generated by the loop symbols $e_{i,j}t^{r-1}$; in particular the loop algebra is noncommutative. [F2, algebra]

1.3 Vanishing below the shift. From the Gauss product $T=FDE$ and the shifted Drinfeld relations, the coefficient $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ with $0<r\le s_{i,j}$ expresses as a combination of products of generators whose shifted ranges are not reached; multiplying the block matrices and comparing coefficients of $u^{-r}$ shows that every contributing product has total shift at least $s_{i,j}\ge r$, so the coefficient vanishes. [F2, F3, algebra]

1.4 The truncation quotient. The two-sided ideal generated by $D_1^{(r)}$, $r>p_1$, is stable under the adjoint action of the diagonal generators $D_i^{(s)}$ by the shifted Drinfeld relations, so the quotient $W(\lambda)$ is the filtered algebra with generators $D_i^{(r)}$ ($r\le p_1$ when $i=1$), $E_{i,j}^{(r)}$, $F_{i,j}^{(r)}$ in the shifted ranges and the induced relations; this is exactly the presentation of the finite W-algebra in terms of $\mathfrak p$-matrix coefficients. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Vanishing above the shift and the finite index set. In the quotient, the same coefficient comparison applied to the highest modes shows $T_{i,j}^{(r)}=0$ for $r>s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$: the finite truncation bounds the loop degree of every surviving symbol by the length of the corresponding centralizer string, and the coefficient recursion of the source's column argument uses $S_{i,j}-s_{h,j}=S_{i,h}$ to reduce each remainder to a smaller column, with the last-column terms vanishing because their index exceeds the bound. Hence the surviving modes are exactly $s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}$, and by the PBW theorem for $Y_n(\sigma)$ restricted to this finite range they form an ordered monomial basis of the quotient $W(\lambda)$. [F3, step 1.3, step 1.4, algebra]

3.1 Symbols and weights. In the loop filtration the symbol of $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ has degree $r-1$ and is $(-1)^{r-1}e_{i,j;r-1}$ in the centralizer basis $(i,j,r-1)$ of the pyramid: the sign comes from the alternating sum in the Gauss inversion, and the matrix-unit part is the coefficient of $t^{r-1}$ in the corresponding current $e_{i,j}(t)$. The root attached to $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ is $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ by the $\mathfrak p$-weight of the matrix unit $e_{i,j}$. The auxiliary signed path polynomials are merely a construction device: their signs and column cuts do not all agree with the signs $(-1)^{r-1}$ of the $T$-modes, so they are not identified with the shifted $T$-series. [F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The lowest nonzero shifted mode. For the smallest pyramid $p=(1,2)$, $n=2$, the shift is $s_{1,2}=1$, so $T_{1,2}^{(1)}=0$ and the first nonzero mode is $T_{1,2}^{(2)}$, whose loop leading term is $-e_{1,2;1}\ne0$; this realizes the general index rule $s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}$ with $S_{1,2}=1+1=2$ and confirms that the coefficient index $r$ is the unshifted FDE index. [step 1.3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The index convention is deliberately unshifted: the coefficient of $u^{-r}$ is $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$, no power of $u$ is inserted, and the filtrations only change the assignation of degrees. Rescaling the series by $u^{s_{i,j}}$ would shift the ranges and is not used anywhere on this page.

- The finite quotient keeps the higher diagonal modes $D_i^{(r)}$ as polynomial expressions rather than setting them to zero; only the modes $D_1^{(r)}$ with $r>p_1$ are truncated.
