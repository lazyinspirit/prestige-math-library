## Exact theorem interfaces

**Lemma 5.3.** Given any nontrivial `b∈Or(s)`, find a nontrivial `a∈Or(s)` whose order is a definable initial segment of `X_b`, with `T_a` definably injectible into `X_b` by Gödel codes. The needed quantifier is `∀η∈T_a ∃x∈X_b` with a definable **injective** choice of code, not “every external function on `X_a` exists in the tree.” A node may have multiple syntactic code names as long as no one code decodes to two nodes; pseudofiniteness then selects the least code for each node; the printed prime-power display does not by itself give one, because it neither tags the length nor forbids extra prime factors.

**Corollary 5.7.** Choose a nontrivial `a` and a cover `d` such that the well-behaved pair order `a×a` has its relevant tree codes in `X_d`. Also choose a nonstandard radius `r∈X_a` for which codes of pair-valued functions on `[0,r]_a` can be represented in `X_d`; then `a` itself is coverable by `d`. The last two assertions require an explicit interpretation of function domains and codes. The source's one-sentence proof cites Claim 5.5 and Definition 5.6(a), but does not display that interpretation.

## A corrected Lemma 5.3 route, conditional on initial-segment tree restriction

1. **Uniform partial arithmetic.** Require the local ESTT contract to include the empty node and standard finite concatenations (the printed Definition 2.3(7) states extension from an existing node but not an explicit base node). Use the exclusive-length convention and corrected `Add` from the bounded-radius audit. Define `Mul(x,y,z)` by a length-`S(y)` sequence beginning at `0`, ending at `z`, and adding `x` at each of its `y` successor steps. Define `Pow(x,y,z)` by a length-`S(y)` sequence beginning at `1`, ending at `z`, and multiplying by `x` at each step. These are first-order *partial* graphs on `X_b`; at every standard finite argument the expected standard output exists by finite concatenation and is unique by finite induction. At arbitrary arguments, functionality on a chosen radius must be included in the bounded formula or proved by the least-disagreement argument; do not use the printed addition formula, which is off by one, or assume these graphs are globally total.
2. **A definable, length-tagged code.** Define `Prime(p)` using the corrected partial multiplication graph and a bounded divisor quantifier; define `NextPrime(p,q)` to say `q` is the least `Prime` above `p`. A prime-list witness of length `S(ℓ)` starts with `p_0=2` and follows `NextPrime` to `p_ℓ`. For a node `η` of exclusive length `ℓ`, define `Code(x,η)` by existential prime and product-sequence witnesses satisfying `q_0=2^{ℓ+1}`, `q_{i+1}=q_i·p_{i+1}^{η(i)+1}` for `i<ℓ`, and `q_ℓ=x`, with every multiplication/power witness explicitly in `X_b`. The factor `2^{ℓ+1}` tags length, and the `p_{i+1}` are odd. The final equality excludes extra prime factors. For a standard finite `η`, all witnesses and `x` are standard finite successors in `X_b`; unique prime valuations make one `x` decode at most one standard finite `η`.
3. **Put existence *and* injectivity into the overspill formula.** Let `Short_w(η)` mean `η∈T_b`, `lg(η)<w`, and `η(i)<w` for every `i<lg(η)`. The single first-order formula

   `Ψ(w) := [∀η(Short_w(η)⇒∃x∈X_b Code(x,η))] ∧ [∀η,ν,x ((Short_w(η)∧Short_w(ν)∧Code(x,η)∧Code(x,ν))⇒η=ν)]`

   may also conjoin the finite list of functionality and prime-list properties used by the decoder. For each standard finite `w`, every short, low node is an actual standard finite tuple: its entries and length lie in finite initial intervals. The explicit finite prime product witnesses `Ψ(w)`, and ordinary unique factorization verifies the injectivity conjunct. Therefore `Ψ(S^n(0_b))` holds for every standard `n`. Pseudofiniteness of `X_b` yields a nonstandard `w_*` satisfying `Ψ`; add the familiar below-ceiling margin clause to `Ψ` if `w_*` must lie far enough below `d_b`. For each short, low node, choose the least `x` satisfying `Code(x,η)`; the second conjunct makes this a definable injection into `X_b` even if one node has several codes.
4. **The exact transfer step.** Define `X_a=[0,w_*)_b` and `d_a=min(d_b,max X_a)` as in Lemma 5.3. To conclude that `Ψ(w_*)` codes **all** of `T_a`, one must prove the uniform initial-segment tree interpretation: every `η∈T_a` is represented by a `T_b` node of length `<w_*` and entries `<w_*`, with the representation first-order definable and injective. Corollary 3.10 constructs a definable initial-segment *order* by enlarging `Δ`; its displayed proof does not spell out the associated tree-embedding map or fully track the relevant `p_s,t_s` thresholds through that expansion. A targeted induced-tree construction for a single initial segment is given below. The remaining issue is extending that construction through the ESTT product closure while preserving the threshold claims. If the local ESTT supplier does this, steps 1–3 give a source-correct code-radius proof without a Recorded supplier.

The route does not assume global prime factorization in `M_1^+`: uniqueness is asserted only for codes of short, low nodes inside the chosen radius and follows by overspill from standard finite correctness. This is why a length tag and a full product equality are essential. The literal source's formula `φ₆` says only that each listed prime divides `x` to an indicated exponent; the same `x=6` satisfies the listed exponents for one-node `(0)` and two-node `(0,0)` descriptions, so that display alone cannot identify `T_a` with a subset of `X_b`.

## Corollary 3.10: an initial-segment repair and the threshold condition

For one initial segment `Y=[0,w)_b`, define its tree **explicitly** by

`T_Y={t∈T_b : lg_b(t)≤d_Y and (∀i<lg_b(t)) t(i)<w}`, with `d_Y=min(d_b,max Y)`.

Keep the same exclusive length, evaluation, and initial-segment order as in `T_b`. Membership and the identity embedding `T_Y↪T_b` are uniformly first-order definable in parameter `w`. An initial segment of a member still satisfies the predicate. If `t∈T_Y` has `lg(t)<d_Y` and `z∈Y`, the old ESTT concatenation gives `t⌢⟨z⟩∈T_b`; its length remains `≤d_Y` and all values lie in `Y`, so it belongs to `T_Y`. This proves the required tree clauses for this **one** restricted order, provided the local ESTT normal form includes the empty node and restriction closure that the printed axiom list leaves implicit. The identity embedding then makes the last step of Lemma 5.3 valid for this order.

The threshold arithmetic is also explicit **if every newly admitted order comes with such an induced definable-subtree embedding into an old tree**. A cut `(L,R)` in any definable suborder `Y⊆X_b` gives the same cut in `X_b`: if an ambient `x` lay between the two infinite arms, the definable set `Y∩(-∞,x]` would have a greatest member by pseudofiniteness, furnishing a point of `Y` between the arms. Thus no new cut of smaller total cofinality appears, and, since all old orders remain, `p_{s'}=p_s`. Likewise, for a chain in a new tree `T_Y` of regular length `ρ<t_s`, Claim 2.14 gives an upper bound inside that definable subtree of the old `T_b`; hence no new treetop failure occurs below `t_s`, and old trees give `t_{s'}=t_s`. These are proof steps, not consequences of Corollary 3.9 alone: Corollary 3.9 equates `lcf` only in its stated common range and does not state equality of either threshold.

The **remaining closure check** is that Corollary 3.10 enlarges `Δ` to all definable suborders while ESTT also requires products of every pair of new orders. A product of two initial segments is generally a definable *rectangle* in an old product order, not an initial segment under Definition 2.3(5)'s arbitrary product ordering. The simple restriction formula above then no longer gives a tree indexed by the rectangle order: advancing one step in the rectangle can skip a nonstandard-length gap in the ambient product index, whereas Definition 2.3(7) only promises one-step concatenation. The distinguished well-behaved pair order of Definition 2.3(6) does make its own square rectangles initial segments, which is enough for the order-radius argument below, but it does not settle arbitrary iterated products. A complete local proof of the full Corollary 3.10 expansion must specify those product/tree interpretations and show their embeddings preserve initial-segment order; the primary two-line proof does not. No counterexample to the intended theorem is asserted, but a bare appeal to Corollary 3.9 cannot discharge this step.

## Corollary 5.7: what the pairing axiom does and does not supply

Let `a₀` be the nontrivial order from Definition 2.3(6), `c₀=a₀×a₀`, and `Pr:X_{a₀}²→X_{c₀}` the definable bijection. The special order condition says that for every `x∈X_{a₀}` there is `y(x)∈X_{c₀}` with

`Pr([0,x]_{a₀}²)=[0,y(x)]_{c₀}`.

`y(x)` is unique. For every **standard finite** `x=S^n(0_{a₀})`, its right-hand initial interval has exactly `(n+1)²` external elements, so `y(x)` is the standard successor at rank `(n+1)²−1`; this is the concrete force of the pairing axiom, not merely existence of a Cartesian product. Apply Lemma 5.3 to `c₀` to obtain a nonstandard initial segment `c'=[0,r)_{c₀}` with `T_{c'}` codes in some cover `X_d`. The first-order set `{x∈X_{a₀}:y(x)<r}` contains every standard finite successor; pseudofiniteness gives a nonstandard `x_*` in it. Shrink `x_*` further by a below-ceiling clause if needed. Then `[0,x_*]_{a₀}²` maps into `X_{c'}`, and its pair order is an initial segment of `c₀`.

This establishes the **order-radius** part of Corollary 5.7, conditional on the same initial-segment tree interpretation in Lemma 5.3. It does **not** yet prove that every required map `[0,x_*]_{a₀}→[0,x_*]_{a₀}²` has a code in `X_d`. A map's graph has an input coordinate and two output coordinates, while `T_{c'}` is a tree of sequences indexed by `X_{c'}` and valued in `X_{c'}`; the source does not state the reindexing/padding map or a three-coordinate graph encoding that identifies the two. A natural candidate is the increasing boundary embedding `j↦y(j)` from `X_a` into the pair order, storing `Pr(f(j))` at index `y(j)` and padding the intervening indices. But the gaps between `y(j)` and `y(S(j))` can have nonstandard length; Definition 2.3(7) gives one-step concatenation, not a code constructor for that padded node. This is the exact missing reindexing operation. The statement also needs to distinguish an arbitrary external map, which need not be internal, from a definable/internal tree function. In particular, the corollary cannot quantify over **all external** maps: in a countable nonstandard model an infinite `[0,x_*]` has uncountably many external maps into its square, while `X_d` is countable. This is a counterexample to that reading, not to the intended internal-code claim. A local proof must give a uniform first-order injection from the **specific internal pair-function nodes used later** into `T_{c'}` (or into a separately covered triple-product tree), show its length is below the cover ceiling, and define the decoder for domain, graph, and range. Likewise `a`-coverability by the **same** `d` needs a uniform unary-to-pair tree embedding, not just the order inclusion. These are the next exact unverified steps after the initial-segment tree restriction.

The bounded arithmetic/PHP route for Claim 8.2 can avoid this general pair-function cover, but Theorem 8.5's six-coordinate tree cannot. No part of this draft changes selected scope, plan, manifests, readiness receipts, or engine state; `p=t` remains escalated.
