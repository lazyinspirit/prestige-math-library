## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $W\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open. Then $W$ is a smooth
$n$-manifold: the one-chart atlas $\{(W,\mathrm{id}_W)\}$ is a smooth atlas, and the
smooth structure it generates is the one induced on the open subset $W$ of
$\mathbb R^n$. A chart $(U,\varphi)$ on $W$ belongs to this structure exactly
when both transition maps between $\varphi$ and the identity are smooth, that
is, exactly when $\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)$ and its inverse are smooth as maps
between Euclidean open sets.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$ and an open subset $W\subseteq\mathbb R^n$.

[F1] $\mathbb R^n$ is Hausdorff, being metrizable, and its rational open boxes form a countable basis, so it is second countable ([[def-hausdorff-space]], [[def-second-countable-space]], [[thm-rational-points-and-boxes-in-rn]]).

[F2] A chart is a homeomorphism from an open set of the manifold onto an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ ([[def-manifold-chart-coordinate-domain-and-coordinate-functions]]).

[F3] Two charts are smoothly compatible when their domains are disjoint or both transition maps are smooth ([[def-smoothly-compatible-charts]]).

[F4] A smooth atlas is a family of pairwise smoothly compatible charts whose domains cover the manifold ([[def-smooth-atlas]]).

[L1] If $(M,\mathcal S)$ is a smooth $n$-manifold and $U\subseteq M$ is open with the subspace topology, then $U$ is a topological $n$-manifold, and the restricted charts $(V\cap U,\varphi|_{V\cap U})$ form a smooth atlas whose generated maximal atlas is independent of the presenting atlas ([[prop-an-open-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold-has-a-canonical-restricted-smooth-structure]]).

[A1] The identity map $\mathrm{id}_W$ on an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is smooth: each coordinate function has every iterated coordinate derivative equal to a constant function, hence existing and continuous.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The one-chart family $\{(\mathbb R^n,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb R^n})\}$ is a smooth atlas on $\mathbb R^n$: [F1] gives the topological-manifold hypotheses, [F2] makes the identity a chart, and [A1] makes the identity transition smooth, so [F3] and [F4] apply. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, A1]

2.1 Since $W\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is open, [L1] applied to the smooth manifold $\mathbb R^n$ from step 1.1 gives the restricted smooth structure on $W$. [L1, step 1.1]

3.1 If a chart $(U,\varphi)$ on $W$ belongs to that restricted structure, then it is smoothly compatible with the identity chart $(W,\mathrm{id}_W)$; hence the two transition maps are exactly $\varphi$ and $\varphi^{-1}$, and [F3] makes both smooth as maps between Euclidean open sets. [F3, step 2.1]

4.1 Conversely, if $\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)$ and $\varphi^{-1}$ are smooth as maps between Euclidean open sets, then $(U,\varphi)$ is smoothly compatible with $(W,\mathrm{id}_W)$ by [F3], so it belongs to the maximal atlas generated by the identity chart on $W$. Together with steps 2.1 and 3.1 this proves the stated characterization and identifies it with the restricted smooth structure. [F3, L1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
