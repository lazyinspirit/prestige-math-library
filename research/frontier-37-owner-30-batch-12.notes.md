## Mathematical dependency audit

The near/far lemma checks `Lp` local integrability with published complex Hölder, the positive finite ball measure, the published centered maximal definition, and dilation/change of variables. Dyadic near shells sum as `R^alpha Mf(x)` because `alpha>0`. The far kernel has radial exponent `(alpha-n)p'+n-1`; its integral from `R` to infinity is finite exactly when `(n-alpha)p'>n`, equivalent to `p<n/alpha`, and its norm has power `R^{alpha-n/p}`. The published polar formula is applied only to Borel radial kernels, which is within its actual statement. The lemma separates representative independence for each convergent integral. Hedberg's radius is `(||f||p/Mf(x))^{p/n}`; it treats `||f||p=0` and `Mf(x)=0` separately, without optimizing an undefined expression.

For HLS, `theta=alpha p/n` and `q=p/(1-theta)`, so `q(1-theta)=p`. Apply the published strong centered-maximal estimate to the real modulus of complex `f`; `Mf` is finite almost everywhere and the Hedberg bound integrates to `||I_alpha f||q<=C||f||p`. The published theorem assumes Countable Choice and only `1<p<infinity`, both met here. Measurability of the integral for an arbitrary Lebesgue representative follows directly: `(x,y)->K_alpha(x-y)` is Borel, while `(x,y)->f(y)` is product measurable by the second coordinate projection; Euclidean Lebesgue measure is sigma-finite and Tonelli applies to the positive and negative real/imaginary components. This avoids the unrelated convolution issue involving `f(x-y)`. Absolute convergence and equality almost everywhere give a linear, representative-independent `Lq` class. The norm bound is proved on the smooth core before using published complex smooth density, complex `Lq` completeness, and bounded dense-subspace extension. The same bound for all `f` identifies the extension uniquely with the almost-everywhere integral.

The B scaling example uses the published smooth bump and `C^1` change-of-variables theorem. A nonzero positive test has a nonzero finite potential norm under any hypothesized bound; applying the bound to all dilates and sending the scale both to zero and infinity forces `1/q=1/p-alpha/n`. At `p=1`, a normalized ball already has a `|x|^{alpha-n}` far tail, whose `n/(n-alpha)` power gives the divergent radial integral `dr/r`. The balls tend to a point mass against continuous tests, but no limit argument is required for the failure. At `p=n/alpha`, the logarithmically corrected radial `f` has finite `Lp` norm because the logarithmic power is `p>1`; its potential has logarithmic power 1 and diverges at zero. A lower bound on every sufficiently small punctured ball proves essential unboundedness, not merely failure at one null point.
