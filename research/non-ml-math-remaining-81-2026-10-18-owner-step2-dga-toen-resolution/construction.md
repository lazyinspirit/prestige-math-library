## Toën prerequisite and tensor convention

The native finding is a real omitted premise/convention, not a false Hall theorem. The original Toën definition fixes a finite field k, then calls endomorphism complexes DGAs and modules right dg modules. Those interfaces require k as a commutative ring and a particular tensor differential. The proposed first paragraph names the existing field-to-ring lemma explicitly, with the same field operations and unit; add that exact direct dependency.

For arbitrary, possibly unbounded cochain complexes U,V over k, define

`(U tensor V)^n = direct_sum_{p+q=n} U^p tensor_k V^q`

and on homogeneous f∈U^p, g∈V^q,

`d(f tensor g) = df tensor g + (-1)^p f tensor dg`.

The differential is k-balanced because d is k-linear, so it descends through the tensor relations; linear extension on the total direct sum is well-defined. It raises total degree by one. Applying it twice leaves the pure terms `d²f tensor g` and `f tensor d²g`, both zero, and mixed coefficients `(-1)^(p+1)+(-1)^p=0`. Thus it is a cochain differential, including characteristic2. Direct-sum finite support makes the construction valid without imposing a boundedness hypothesis.

Use the original composition order: first tensor factor f∈T(b,c)^p, second factor g∈T(a,b)^q, and product `fg=f∘g`. Composition being a degree-zero chain map then says exactly

`d(fg) = (df)g + (-1)^p f(dg)`.

This derives the DGA Leibniz rule from the chain-map requirement rather than assuming it as an unexplained sign convention. Endomorphism multiplication remains associative/unital/k-bilinear. The central scalar map is `lambda -> lambda 1_a`, since k-bilinearity and the unit give `(lambda1_a)f=lambda f=f(lambda1_a)`. The original closed degree-zero identity and cochain square-zero conditions supply the remaining DGA axioms over the now-explicit commutative base.

For a right module, make the original action order explicit:

`M(b) tensor_k T(a,b) -> M(a)`, `(m,f) -> mf`.

The same tensor chain-map convention gives

`d_M(mf) = d_M(m) f + (-1)^|m| m d_T(f)`.

This is exactly the original batch41 **right** dg-module rule; it is not the left-module sign determined by |f|. Unitality and action/composition compatibility are retained. For the representable `h_b=T(-,b)`, its right action is composition, so the previously derived composition identity verifies this rule with m as the first factor.
