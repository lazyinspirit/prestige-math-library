The envelope is integrable and controls the full closed tolerance ball. Countable Choice is used only to establish closed-ball measurability and positive finite volume through the Borel and box-measure suppliers; no full AC is used. - `cex-abc-zero-tolerance-limit-fails-without-integrable-envelope` uses a proper telescoping prior and explicit moving density spikes. Each interval has width `2w_n` and height `1/(4w_n)`, so each spike has mass `1/2`; every component density is continuous at zero and the averaged density is `1/4`. The ABC mass of `{1}` along epsilon `1/q` tends to `1/4` while the density-selected mass is `1/2`, and the local envelope integral diverges. The explicit construction is choice-free; only its comparison with the stated zero-tolerance theorem carries that theorem's Countable Choice assumption.
- The finite Bernoulli example computes full-data and count likelihood normalizers (`1/9` and `1/3`) and verifies the common uniform-on-fibres conditional kernel for every parameter, data event, and summary event. The identifiable counterexample has sampling laws `delta_0` and `delta_1`, but its constant summary leaves the ABC target at the prior. The adjacent-uniform example has acceptance probability exactly epsilon and target `delta_0` for `0 < epsilon < 1/2`.
