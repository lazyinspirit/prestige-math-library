## Statement

Let $T$ and $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ be the free $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra and defining ideal of [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]], and write $K_i=K_{d_i h_i}$. Let $\Delta:T\to T\otimes_{\mathbb Q(q)}T$ and $\varepsilon:T\to\mathbb Q(q)$ be the algebra homomorphisms determined by

$$\Delta(K_h)=K_h\otimes K_h,\quad \Delta(E_i)=E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes E_i,\quad \Delta(F_i)=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i,\quad \varepsilon(K_h)=1,\quad\varepsilon(E_i)=\varepsilon(F_i)=0.$$

Let $S:T\to T$ be the $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear algebra anti-homomorphism determined by

$$S(K_h)=K_{-h},\qquad S(E_i)=-E_iK_i,\qquad S(F_i)=-K_i^{-1}F_i.$$

Then

$$\Delta(I_{\mathrm{DJ}})\subseteq I_{\mathrm{DJ}}\otimes T+T\otimes I_{\mathrm{DJ}},\qquad \varepsilon(I_{\mathrm{DJ}})=0,\qquad S(I_{\mathrm{DJ}})\subseteq I_{\mathrm{DJ}}.$$

Hence these maps descend to $U_q(\mathfrak g)=T/I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ and make it a Hopf algebra over $\mathbb Q(q)$ ([[def-bialgebra-counit-and-antipode]]): $\Delta$ is coassociative, $\varepsilon$ is a counit, and

$$m(S\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta=\eta\circ\varepsilon=m(\operatorname{id}\otimes S)\Delta.$$

The antipode $S$ is unique for this multiplication, unit, coproduct and counit ([[lem-an-antipode-is-unique]]), and its square satisfies

$$S^2(E_i)=q_i^{-2}E_i,\qquad S^2(F_i)=q_i^{2}F_i,\qquad S^2(K_h)=K_h.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum over $\mathbb Q(q)$ and its Drinfeld–Jimbo presentation.

[F1] The symmetrizer satisfies $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$, so the two crossing factors $q_i^{a_{ij}}$ and $q_j^{a_{ji}}$ are equal ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F2] The defining ideal is generated by the toral, toral-action, mixed $E_iF_j$, and positive and negative Serre relation families ([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F3] The $q_i$-Gaussian coefficients are symmetric in $r$ and $m-r$, and $q_i-q_i^{-1}\ne0$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F4] In the toral-action algebras, the Serre coproducts are the quasiprimitive formulas stated in [[lem-coproduct-preserves-the-positive-and-negative-quantum-serre-ideals]]; their images in $U_q(\mathfrak g)\otimes U_q(\mathfrak g)$ are zero.

[F5] The tensor product of algebras has multiplication $(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d)=ac\otimes bd$ ([[thm-tensor-product-of-algebras-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F6] Generator assignments extend uniquely to algebra maps on the free tensor algebra ([[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]).

[F7] A map that kills a two-sided ideal factors through the quotient ring ([[def-quotient-ring]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[F8] A two-sided ideal generated by a subset is as in [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]].

[F9] Tensoring an exact sequence ending in zero preserves exactness at the two rightmost terms ([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).

[F10] Tensor products over $\mathbb Q(q)$ have the symmetry isomorphism $x\otimes y\mapsto y\otimes x$ ([[thm-symmetry-and-associativity-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F11] A bialgebra has algebra maps $\Delta,\varepsilon$ satisfying coassociativity and the counit equations; a Hopf algebra has an antipode satisfying both convolution-inverse equations ([[def-bialgebra-counit-and-antipode]]).

[F12] An antipode, if it exists, is unique ([[lem-an-antipode-is-unique]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Check the defining relations after mapping into $U_q(\mathfrak g)\otimes U_q(\mathfrak g)$, use tensor right exactness to recover the free-ideal coideal inclusion, and verify the Hopf equations on generators with an explicit word-induction step for convolution.

1.1 The displayed assignments extend from the free generators to an algebra map $\Delta:T\to T\otimes T$ by [F6], using the tensor product algebra structure in [F5]. The counit assignment extends to an algebra map by [F6], and reversing words extends the assignment for $S$ to a $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear anti-homomorphism. [given, F5, F6, construct]

1.2 Let $\pi:T\to U:=T/I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ be the quotient map. Write $I\otimes T$ and $T\otimes I$ for the images of the corresponding tensor-product maps in $T\otimes T$. Right exactness [F9] gives $\ker(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_T)=\operatorname{im}(I\otimes T\to T\otimes T)$; by applying [F9] again after the flip [F10], $\ker(\operatorname{id}_U\otimes\pi)=\operatorname{im}(U\otimes I\to U\otimes T)$, and $\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_I:T\otimes I\to U\otimes I$ is surjective. If $z\in\ker(\pi\otimes\pi)$, then $(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_T)z$ lies in this second kernel, so choose $y\in T\otimes I$ with the same image; then $z-y\in\ker(\pi\otimes\operatorname{id}_T)$. Thus $\ker(\pi\otimes\pi)=I\otimes T+T\otimes I$. This uses only a lift for one tensor at a time, not a choice function. [given, F7, F9, F10, algebra]

1.3 The counit sends $K_0-1$ and $K_hK_{h'}-K_{h+h'}$ to $1-1=0$. It sends each toral-action relation to $0-0$, each mixed relation to $0-0-\delta_{ij}(1-1)/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=0$, and every Serre sum to zero because each monomial contains an $E$ or an $F$. Thus $\varepsilon(I_{\mathrm{DJ}})=0$. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

1.4 The anti-homomorphism $S$ sends $K_0-1$ to itself and $K_hK_{h'}-K_{h+h'}$ to $K_{-h'}K_{-h}-K_{-(h+h')}$, another toral generator. For $b=\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle$, applying $S$ to the $E_i$ toral-action relation gives $-E_iK_iK_{-h}+q^bK_{-h}E_iK_i=0$ in $U$ by the relation at $-h$; applying it to the $F_i$ relation gives $-K_i^{-1}F_iK_{-h}+q^{-b}K_{-h}K_i^{-1}F_i=0$ by the same relation and commutation of the toral elements. [given, F2, F7, algebra]

2.1 In $U\otimes U$, the toral relations are preserved: $\Delta(K_0)=1\otimes1$ and $\Delta(K_hK_{h'})=K_{h+h'}\otimes K_{h+h'}$. For $b=\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle$, the two terms in $\Delta(K_h)\Delta(E_i)-q^b\Delta(E_i)\Delta(K_h)$ cancel by $K_hE_i=q^bE_iK_h$ in each tensor factor and commutation of toral elements; the two terms in $\Delta(K_h)\Delta(F_i)-q^{-b}\Delta(F_i)\Delta(K_h)$ cancel by the negative toral-action relation in each factor. Therefore $(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta$ kills the toral and toral-action generators of $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. [step 1.1, F2, F5, algebra]

2.2 Applying $S$ to $R_{ij}=E_iF_j-F_jE_i-\delta_{ij}C_i$ and moving toral factors with the defining relations gives $S(R_{ij})=q_j^2q_i^{-a_{ij}}(F_jE_i-E_iF_j)K_iK_j^{-1}-\delta_{ij}S(C_i)$ in $U$. For $i\ne j$ the first factor vanishes by $E_iF_j=F_jE_i$; for $i=j$ its scalar is $q_i^2q_i^{-2}=1$, $F_iE_i-E_iF_i=-C_i$, and $S(C_i)=-C_i$, so the two terms cancel. Hence $S(R_{ij})=0$ in $U$. [step 1.4, F1, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

2.3 Let $m=1-a_{ij}$. In $S(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})$, each of the $m+1$ substituted generators contributes a minus sign; moving the inserted $K_i,K_j$ to the right contributes exponent $s(s-1)+(m-s)(m-s-1)+a_{ij}m+2s(m-s)=m(m-1+a_{ij})=0$. Reindexing $s\mapsto m-s$ and using the symmetric Gaussian coefficients [F3] contributes the remaining sign, so $S(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})=-\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij}K_i^mK_j$. For the negative family, $K_i^{-1}F_i=q_i^2F_iK_i^{-1}$ gives $(K_i^{-1}F_i)^r=q_i^{r(r+1)}F_i^rK_i^{-r}$ by induction. Moving all toral factors to the right contributes exponent $s(s+1)+(m-s)(m-s+1)+2s(m-s)+a_{ij}m=m(m+1+a_{ij})=2m$; the equality $q_j^{a_{ji}}=q_i^{a_{ij}}$ from [F1] is used when $K_j^{-1}$ crosses $F_i$, and $K_j^{-1}F_j=q_j^2F_jK_j^{-1}$ supplies the additional factor $q_j^2$. Reindexing $s\mapsto m-s$ and using [F3] gives $S(\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij})=-q_i^{2m}q_j^2\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}K_i^{-m}K_j^{-1}$. These identities are computed modulo the toral and toral-action relations only; their right sides lie in the two-sided Serre ideal. Thus both Serre families map into $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. [step 1.4, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.1 Put $a=a_{ij}$ and $C_i=(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$. Expanding the commutator of the coproducts, the cross terms cancel because $E_iK_j=q_i^{-a}K_jE_i$ and $K_i^{-1}F_j=q_i^aF_jK_i^{-1}$, where the equality $q_i^{a_{ij}}=q_j^{a_{ji}}$ is [F1]. Hence $[\Delta(E_i),\Delta(F_j)]=(E_iF_j-F_jE_i)\otimes K_i^{-1}+K_j\otimes(E_iF_j-F_jE_i)$. In $U$, $E_iF_j-F_jE_i=\delta_{ij}C_i$, and direct expansion gives $\Delta(C_i)=(K_i\otimes K_i-K_i^{-1}\otimes K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=C_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+K_i\otimes C_i$. Thus the image under $(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta$ of every mixed relation is zero. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

3.2 For each positive or negative Serre generator, [F4] maps its coproduct to zero in $U\otimes U$. Therefore $(\pi\otimes\pi)\Delta$ kills all remaining generators of $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. [step 2.1, F4, algebra]

3.3 Steps 1.4, 2.2 and 2.3 show that $S$ sends every generator of $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ into $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. Since $S$ reverses products and $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ is a two-sided generated ideal, $S(I_{\mathrm{DJ}})\subseteq I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$; the quotient property [F7] gives the descended anti-homomorphism on $U$. [step 1.4, step 2.2, step 2.3, F2, F7, F8, algebra]

4.1 By step 1.2, steps 2.1, 3.1 and 3.2 put every defining generator's coproduct in $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}\otimes T+T\otimes I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. This sum is a two-sided ideal: for pure tensors, left and right multiplication preserve each summand because $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ is a two-sided ideal; the product formula in [F5] gives these products. Since $\Delta$ is an algebra map, it contains the coproduct of every element of the generated ideal $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, F2, F5, F8, algebra]

4.2 For $K_h$, both antipode products are $S(K_h)K_h=K_{-h}K_h=1$ and $K_hS(K_h)=K_hK_{-h}=1$. For $E_i$, they are $S(E_i)K_i^{-1}+E_i=-E_iK_iK_i^{-1}+E_i=0$ and $E_iS(K_i^{-1})+S(E_i)=E_iK_i-E_iK_i=0$. For $F_i$, they are $S(F_i)+S(K_i)F_i=-K_i^{-1}F_i+K_i^{-1}F_i=0$ and $F_i+K_iS(F_i)=F_i-K_iK_i^{-1}F_i=0$. Since $S(1)=1$ by construction, the equations hold on the unit as well. These are the two convolution equations of [F11] on every generator and on the unit. [step 1.3, step 3.3, F11, algebra]

5.1 The maps $\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ descend by steps 4.1 and 1.3. For $K_h$, both iterated coproducts equal $K_h\otimes K_h\otimes K_h$. For $E_i$, each equals $E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes1\otimes E_i$; for $F_i$, each equals $F_i\otimes1\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes K_i\otimes F_i$. Thus coassociativity holds on generators. On $K_h$ both counit composites equal $K_h$; on $E_i$ they give $0+E_i=E_i$ and $E_i\cdot1+0=E_i$; on $F_i$ they give $0+F_i=F_i$ and $F_i\cdot1+0=F_i$. Each side of these identities is an algebra homomorphism, so equality on the generators and unit proves equality on all of $U$, giving the bialgebra axioms in [F11]. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 1.3, F5, F7, F11, algebra]

5.2 Write $\Delta(x)=\sum x_{(1)}\otimes x_{(2)}$. If the two convolution equations hold for words $x$ and $y$, then anti-multiplicativity of $S$ and multiplicativity of $\Delta$ give $\sum S(x_{(1)}y_{(1)})x_{(2)}y_{(2)}=\sum S(y_{(1)})(\sum S(x_{(1)})x_{(2)})y_{(2)}=\varepsilon(x)\varepsilon(y)1$. Likewise $\sum x_{(1)}y_{(1)}S(x_{(2)}y_{(2)})=\sum x_{(1)}(\sum y_{(1)}S(y_{(2)}))S(x_{(2)})=\varepsilon(y)\varepsilon(x)1$. Since $\varepsilon$ is multiplicative and all words are products of generators, induction gives both identities on every word and hence every element of $U$. Thus $S$ is an antipode as defined in [F11]. [step 4.2, F11, algebra]

6.1 Uniqueness follows from [F12]. Since $S$ reverses products, $S^2$ is an algebra homomorphism, and on generators $S^2(E_i)=K_i^{-1}E_iK_i=q_i^{-2}E_i$, $S^2(F_i)=K_i^{-1}F_iK_i=q_i^2F_i$, and $S^2(K_h)=K_h$. This proves the statement. [step 3.3, step 5.2, F12, algebra] ∎

## Source note

JKK §1, display (1.6), printed p. 6, gives exactly the coproduct, counit and antipode convention used here but asserts the Hopf structure without proving relation preservation. Berkeley Ch. 13 §13.1.3, printed pp. 308–309, gives the positive Serre quasiprimitivity/Hopf-ideal results and a different coproduct convention; the convention-sensitive relation calculations are local. The scaffold's claim that the convolution antipode equations extend from generators because all maps involved are algebra or anti-algebra homomorphisms is invalid: $m(S\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta$ is not generally an algebra homomorphism. Step 5.2 supplies the required word-induction argument. The free-ideal coideal claim uses the explicitly proved tensor-quotient kernel calculation in step 1.2, rather than an unproved freeness or choice argument.
