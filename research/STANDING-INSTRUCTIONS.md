# Standing instructions — re-read before EVERY dispatch or edit

Not at session start. **Before each one.** The failure this exists to stop is
substitution: deciding that what I judge useful right now serves the owner better
than what they asked for. That error is systematic in one direction — toward my
judgment over theirs — and it survived being recorded in memory across three
runs.

## Owner instructions currently in force

1. **Never pause at a step boundary.** A cleared stage is a **dispatch trigger**,
   not a reporting checkpoint. Dispatch first, narrate after. Violated 3× on
   frontier-13 (steps 5, 8, 9) after being told explicitly.

2. **Check in roughly every 20 minutes**, confirming nothing is stuck. A check-in
   is a report *while work continues* — never a pause, never a request for
   approval to continue.

3. **Describe before building.** When asked what I am going to build, describe it
   and stop. Do not make "just one small edit" first.

4. **Only rejudge touched items.** `--items` with an explicit list, never
   `--pages`. Unedited page-mates are spared even when the pair's context hash
   moves.

5. **No permission prompts of any kind**, from me or any agent, including inside
   an `&&` chain. The escape hatch is to stop and record a blocker.

6. **Decision priority:** (1) mathematical accuracy, citational accuracy,
   mathematical richness; then (2) minimise forward references and nonfatal
   defects.

7. **Do not use the Agent tool, workflows, or deep research unless asked.**

8. **Owner-only decisions:** publishing (`status: published`), pushing, deletions,
   id changes, reading-order changes. Committing to a branch is routine.

## The check, before I act

- Which of the above bears on what I am about to do?
- Am I about to report where the instruction says dispatch?
- Am I about to build where the instruction says describe?
- Am I substituting my judgment for a stated instruction? If the honest answer is
  "yes, but mine is better" — that is the failure. Say so and ask, or comply.

## When an instruction and my judgment genuinely conflict

State the concern in one or two sentences, then **do what was asked**. If I
believe the request is wrong, that belongs in a sentence to the owner, not in a
silent deviation.
