## 1. Physical-load statement

Let the actual assembled interior equations be

    C_phys v+H_phys p=L_v,
    H_phys^*v-(7/10)Kp=L_p,                               (1)

where C_phys=d^2 C_0 and H_phys=d H_0. The arrays L_v,L_p are the actual row moments, not densities. They are supported at distance at least R from the origin in the upper half-plane. Define

    A=sum |L_p|,    B=sum |L_v|.

For d<=rho<=R/8 there is one affine polynomial a such that at all uniform pressure nodes |x|+y<=rho,

    |p-a|<=C[(rho^2/R^2+d/R)A
                         +(rho^2/R^3+d/R^2)B].             (2)

In particular d<=rho^2/R gives

    max_ports |p-a|<=C(rho/R)^2(A+B/R).                    (3)

The scalar-load sum need not vanish. Any additive pressure gauge is absorbed by a. A logarithmic fundamental term from a nonzero scalar moment is smooth near the child because its source is distant, and its second derivative has the R^(-2) bound used in (2). No outgoing inner monopole has been set to zero.

## 2. Actual low-frequency source orders

Divide the velocity rows by d^2 and pressure rows by d, so the normalized loads are

    f_v=L_v/d^2,    f_p=L_p/d.

On the full plane the exact block inverse gives

    S P=H_0^* C_0^(-1)f_v-f_p,
    S=(7/10)K+H_0^* C_0^(-1)H_0,
    v=C_0^(-1)(f_v-H_0P).                                (4)

After tangential Fourier transform, integrate the vertical Fourier variable by its stable/unstable roots. Near zero the physical pressure factor is a nonzero analytic multiple of theta^2+eta^2. Its simple vertical residue therefore has order |theta|^(-1). The exact H_0 factor vanishes to first order, while C_0 tends to 3/10. Consequently the full-plane physical residue orders are

    source f_p: pressure order -1, velocity order 0;
    source f_v: pressure order 0, velocity order 1.         (5)

Here order q means a one-sided analytic symbol bounded by C|theta|^q, with its corresponding differentiated bounds. The leading scalar-pressure residue is

    -exp(-|theta||j-m|)/(2|theta|),                        (6)

for a unit scalar row impulse at layer m. The leading velocity-impulse pressure residues are the appropriate first derivatives of this physical Green factor. The exact discrete roots replace the displayed exponent by their analytic root factors and change the amplitudes by lower orders; (5) is unchanged. All nonphysical source residues are analytic and bounded near zero, with exponential decay in |j-m|.

To enforce zero base data, add the actual half-plane Poisson response to minus the full-plane boundary trace. The physical response amplitude from base data is P_base-k_n v_base,n plus analytic order-one corrections, as independently derived from the three-column boundary matrix. Thus a scalar source retains physical order -1, and a velocity source physical order 0, also for the reflected term. The scalar leading physical factor is the Dirichlet image combination

    -[exp(-|theta||j-m|)
                       -exp(-|theta|(j+m))]/(2|theta|).    (7)

The difference in (7) makes the zero-frequency half-plane value finite for each fixed j,m. Neither its two separate logarithmic terms nor an arbitrary inner flux is individually discarded.

One order distinction is essential for mesh pressure layers. The mesh pressure amplitude from prescribed pressure alone is order one: at theta=0 the pure pressure datum is exactly the physical/gauge column, with no mesh pressure. Combining this with (5) gives the target-side mesh pressure amplitudes

plus source-mesh terms exponentially small in m. Each target mesh branch has the additional exp(-cj). Pressure derivatives across this mesh-width layer need not obey a macroscopic Hessian bound. Its VALUE is estimated below, rather than differentiating it twice and losing powers of d.

The high-frequency part is controlled directly by the uniformly invertible half-line saddle operator and exponential conjugation, exactly as in the preceding half-plane note. ## 3. Kernel bounds and scaling

Let n be tangential separation in lattice units. Split the pressure into a cutoff physical part and the remaining mesh part. For sources outside radius R and targets inside radius rho, all direct and reflected physical separations are at least a fixed multiple of R/d.

For the physical part, two target spatial derivatives multiply its symbols by order two. The orders -1 and 0 in (5) therefore become orders one and two. Fourier integration by parts, separately on either side of zero, gives

    |D_lattice^2 G_ph,p|<=C distance^(-2),
    |D_lattice^2 G_ph,v|<=C distance^(-3).                 (9)

For horizontal separation dominating the vertical separation, integrate twice or three times respectively; possible jumps of the first or second derivative at zero give exactly those powers. If vertical separation dominates, rescale theta by that separation in the exponentially decaying physical factor. This is the same elementary kernel argument as for the distant-base note, with one additional inverse power of |theta| in the scalar-source case. There is an additional detail when a target vertical difference crosses the SOURCE HEIGHT j=m. The switched full-line factor can jump there. Insert the two residues on either side before taking that difference. For a scalar source, the resulting leading contact term is a constant times Q, followed by an order-one remainder. For a normal velocity source it is a constant times F_n, followed by an order-two remainder; for a tangential velocity source it is a constant times i theta F_t, followed by an order-two remainder. These facts follow immediately from the residue expansions in the supplier's (4): the apparent order-one normal-force terms cancel between A_+ and A_-, while the tangential sign(theta)|theta| term is the smooth theta term. Smooth cutoff polynomials decay faster than any prescribed inverse power, and the remaining orders give exactly (9). Such a source is far horizontally. This permits discrete second differences; it does not assume a continuously C2 physical part through every source-height line.

From (8), the mesh VALUE kernels are bounded by

    |G_m,p|<=C exp(-cj)/(1+m+|n|),
    |G_m,v|<=C exp(-cj)/(1+m+|n|)^2,                      (10)

plus C_M exp(-c|j-m|)(1+|n|)^(-M) direct source-mesh terms and C_M exp(-c(j+m))(1+|n|)^(-2) source-mesh/base-correction terms. For the first terms in (10), use one or two integrations by parts when |n| dominates m, and direct rescaling when m dominates. One-sided jumps are retained; an order-zero symbol need only have a 1/|n| tail. This is sufficient. Choose M>=3 for the remaining terms.

Return now to physical loads and physical pressure. The prefactor is d f_p=L_p for scalar loads and d f_v=L_v/d for velocity loads. Two physical spatial derivatives add d^(-2). Hence (9), convolved with the sources, gives

    |D^2 p_ph|<=C(A/R^2+B/R^3).                           (11)

Equation (10) gives, without differentiating the target layer,

    |p_m|<=C(d A/R+d B/R^2).                              (12)

The exponentially localized remaining terms satisfy the same bounds, whether the source is far vertically or horizontally. Use the affine polynomial determined by p_ph at the origin and its two coordinate forward differences there. The discrete Taylor estimate along lattice paths bounds its error by C rho^2 times the supremum of physical second differences in (11). Together with (12), this proves (2). This discrete Taylor argument also covers source-height crossings and makes no continuously C2 extension claim for that part of the Green decomposition.

## 4. Exact cutoff moments and ring-energy bound

Suppose (v,p) solves the homogeneous actual uniform equations on a half-annular ring of radii comparable to R, with zero residual base velocity and pressure. Choose a smooth nodal cutoff eta equal to one inside the ring and zero outside, with |D eta|<=C/R, |D^2 eta|<=C/R^2. Its exact commutator loads on the ring are

    L_v=[C_phys,eta]v+[H_phys,eta]p,
    L_p=[H_phys^*,eta]v-(7/10)[K,eta]p.                   (13)

Let V=||v||_L2(ring), E=||grad p||_L2(ring), using the P1 nodal reconstruction for v and the actual P1 pressure. The nodal reconstruction norm is bounded by the full MINI velocity mass norm on a one-cell enlargement, by the uniformly positive local reduced mass form; thus V may be replaced by that actual full-velocity norm. Shape equivalence on this fixed uniform lattice gives

    sum |L_p|<=C(V+E),
    sum |L_v|<=C(d V+R E).                               (14)

Here is the direct scaling argument, including the gauge issue. The zero residual pressure trace on the flat portions of the half-ring gives ||p||_L2<=C R E by anchored Poincare. C_phys has off-diagonal size O(d^2), so its commutator contributes at most C(d/R)||v||_L1<=C d V. H_phys has coefficients O(d), so its commutator contributes C R^(-1)||p||_L1<=C R E to the velocity load, and C R^(-1)||v||_L1<=C V to the scalar load. Finally, the five-point K commutator is the sum of p K eta and products of first differences. Its scalar-load l1 norm is at most C[R^(-2)||p||_L1+R^(-1)||grad p||_L1]<=C E. Every coefficient is from the actual coupled operator; no pressure row or bubble-induced term is dropped.

Combining (3) and (14), for d<=rho^2/R, gives the concrete incoming estimate

    max_ports |p_incoming-a|<=C(rho/R)^2(V+E).             (15)

The scalar commutator moment is not presumed zero in deriving (14)--(15).
