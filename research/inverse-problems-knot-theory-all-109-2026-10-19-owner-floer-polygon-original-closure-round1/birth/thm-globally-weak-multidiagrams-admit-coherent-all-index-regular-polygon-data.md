## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a finite ordered list of distinct attaching systems with pairwise transverse product tori in $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$, $m\ge1$, and no triple intersection. Suppose every nonzero full-curve multi-periodic domain vanishing at the nonempty finite basepoint set $Z$ has both signs. Suppose also that each component of the complement of any one attaching system contains a basepoint. Fix a finite maximum arity $N$. Common compatible protected geometry with already supplied exact product-angle generator charts may be retained; their planes are $\mathbb R^m$ and $\prod_j e^{i\theta_j}\mathbb R$ for arbitrary fixed $0<\theta_j<\pi$. Then there is a choice of common nearly symmetric target geometry and coherent source-dependent polygon structures for every ordered sub-list with at most $N$ inputs, having the following properties.

All structures fix whole common neighborhoods of the diagonal, every chosen region divisor and the basepoint divisors, and whole neighborhoods of the boundary tori and generator charts. They have genuine time-constant endpoint collars on each autonomous strip end. Every nonconstant zero-$Z$ end strip is regular, at every actual index. Every nonconstant zero-$Z$ polygon, with its conformal parameters included, is regular at every actual index. There are no constant stable polygons. On each finite-tree conformal face the limiting structures are precisely the already chosen structures on its vertices; their comparisons on retained cores and after every fixed number of neck derivatives are exponentially small. The uniform neck bounds and autonomous translated-neck limits required for the actual polygon compactness and gluing theorems hold.

When a compatible common protected tensor and an admissible coherent reference system have been supplied, the choices can be made arbitrarily close to that system in any prescribed finite collection of smooth coefficient norms on its compactified-family charts; the prescribed protected tensor and its actual corner angles remain unchanged. In particular a supplied integrable circular model is a legitimate reference. No count-preservation claim follows merely from this closeness.

The same relative construction gives generic smooth one-parameter coherent families between two such regular coherent choices with the same end paths and protected geometry. The parameter is included in the universal dimension, fixed endpoints are retained, and no regularity of every intermediate slice is asserted. This is a construction of analytic choices. It does not yet assert finite counts or an independence theorem for the resulting operations, and it does not replace sector admissibility by the stated stronger global hypothesis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite transverse triple-free multidiagram, global admissibility, basepoints, finite maximum arity and target nearly symmetric convention.

[F1] The actual zero-mark domains of all sub-polygons, all indices and all conformal parameters form a finite set, giving one energy bound before any genericity choice. Nonconstant maps have a free interior site outside the protected divisors and all boundary tori. ([[lem-globally-weak-pointed-polygons-have-finite-all-index-domains]], [[thm-source-dependent-nearly-symmetric-polygons-admit-countable-choice-universal-regularity]])

[F2] The actual polygon universal Hilbert construction gives source-localized surjectivity and Countable Choice Sard–Smale regularity over every finite-dimensional source parameter space. The explicit time-only smoothing-series and Cayley construction in the flat-jet proof gives flat endpoint jets; its separated-protected-set surjectivity Statement is not applied to the larger protected union here. The free-support and surjectivity argument for that union is given in steps2.1–3.1 below. Finite-energy autonomous strips have dense time-slice injective sites. ([[thm-source-dependent-nearly-symmetric-polygons-admit-countable-choice-universal-regularity]], [[thm-countable-choice-flat-endpoint-jet-universal-strip-regularity]], [[lem-finite-energy-strip-time-slice-injective-points-are-dense]])

[F3] Actual finite-tree conformal charts, bounded-energy compactness, the exact transverse inverse, and unconditional finite-ended linear matching are available. Zero-mark spheres and one-torus disks are excluded when every one-system cut component has a basepoint. ([[lem-boundary-marked-disks-have-finite-tree-conformal-neck-charts]], [[thm-bounded-energy-polygons-have-stable-source-bubble-and-strip-limits]], [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]], [[lem-finite-ended-cauchy-riemann-relative-index-is-compact-determinant-winding]])

## Proof

1.1 If common compatible protected geometry and actual product-angle corner charts have already been supplied, retain them unchanged in this step. Otherwise choose finitely many disjoint generator neighborhoods for ALL the pairs. No point belongs to two different pair-generator sets: such a point would belong to three tori, contrary to the hypothesis. On each neighborhood straighten its two transverse curve germs in the underlying separate surface factors and prescribe the product tensor $i$, with either chosen transverse product angles or right angles as an auxiliary choice. No supplied integrable chart is changed to force a different angle. Away from the diagonal these are compatible with the descended product area form. Extend these prescriptions to a compatible tensor on its complement by the following explicit compatible-metric construction: on each finite patch choose a positive metric, average it with its pullback by the prescribed tensor where that tensor is fixed, and use a finite positive partition; for a general resulting positive metric $g$, the skew-adjoint invertible map $A$ given by $g(Au,v)=\omega(u,v)$ has positive $-A^2$, and $A(-A^2)^{-1/2}$ is compatible. The inverse square root is smooth by a convergent power series on finitely many spectral intervals, or its positive-matrix integral, and agrees with a prescribed compatible tensor on its fixed patches. Prescribe the symmetric surface tensor on the protected diagonal neighborhood and match it in its outside collar by the same compatible construction. Choose closed disjoint protected neighborhoods $D,N$, where $D$ contains all the incidence divisors and the diagonal and $N$ contains ALL the tori and generator charts. They are disjoint because all labelled circles avoid the region test points and all tuples on one torus have distinct roots. Fix the resulting tensor on a neighborhood of $N$ for every subsequent choice. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 First construct regular autonomous end paths for every pair. Start with the common tensor of step1.1 and use time-dependent variations supported outside $D\cup N$, with flat endpoint jets as in [F2]. For a nonconstant strip, [F1]'s area argument supplies an open free-image source region. Dense time-slice injective sites provide a point there at which the universal time-only variation can be localized without another preimage at that time. Uniform convergence at the two ends excludes remote preimages of its free target neighborhood. The local adjoint and unique-continuation argument in [F2] therefore kills every nonzero cokernel: choose an anti-$J$ endomorphism sending $u_t$ to the obstruction direction at that site, with a nonnegative bump of sufficiently small target and time support. Dense Hilbert tangent fields preserve its nonzero pairing. The actual fixed Fredholm operator and the finite-cokernel quotient give a split universal right inverse exactly as in the polygon proof of [F2]. Countably many second-countable map charts and integer index strata permit one residual choice regular at EVERY actual zero-mark index. Its nonconstant strips have index at least one because their nonzero translation vector belongs to their kernel. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Turn the flat jets into genuine endpoint collars without assuming that the shrinking-support Hilbert sum already has a common collar. Cut the chosen path to its endpoint values in time intervals of width tending to zero. Flatness gives convergence in every smooth norm; the protected geometry is unchanged. If nonregular nonconstant zero-mark strips existed for infinitely many late cut paths, [F1] gives a common energy bound across ALL their indices. Apply the strip case of [F3]'s actual compactness. No zero-mark sphere or boundary disk can occur. A nonconstant limiting chain consists of finitely many strips of the all-index regular auxiliary path. Augmenting is unnecessary: their actual component operators are onto, and the squared-cutoff inverse from [F3], with the small core coefficient difference and sufficiently long exact generator necks, is a right inverse for the nearby strip operator. Its error norm is the finite sum of $C/T_e$ plus a term tending to zero; a Neumann series contradicts nonregularity. If the whole limit is constant with no bubbles or levels, degree additivity makes every region degree eventually zero, so [F1]'s energy estimate makes the original strip constant as well. Equivalently inside the exact generator chart the whole transverse inverse has no nonzero decaying solution. Thus a late cut path is regular for ALL nonconstant zero-mark strips. All exact transverse inverses and Fourier tails here use the positive finite minimum of $\theta_j$ and $\pi-\theta_j$ for these pairs; the general-angle inverse supplies these estimates without an orthogonal normalization. There are finitely many pairs; choose such paths and, for the opposite order, use their actual reversed strip data. No finite lower-index hypothesis was needed. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, contradiction]

4.1 Construct the polygon coefficients by finite induction on the number of inputs. The three-punctured source has no conformal modulus; its ends are the already chosen paths. For a higher arity prescribe on each finite-tree boundary face the structures already chosen on its vertices. They agree on intersections by the inductive face rule. In the explicit source charts of [F3], place these vertex structures on the component cores, move to the common autonomous end data along the middle of each matching strip and use a smooth fixed transition there. All structures coincide with the common tensor on the generator chart. On a fixed retained core this sewn choice equals the vertex choice; on overlapping conformal charts its difference from that choice is $O(\rho_e)$, including every fixed derivative in $T_e=-\log\rho_e/\pi$. Extend and patch these coefficients using a finite partition in the compactified conformal base. Here patch the CAYLEY matrices relative to the compatible tensor of step1.1, rather than averaging almost-complex tensors. On the free target region a tame tensor has Cayley matrix $B$ anti-commuting with that reference and satisfying $\|B\|<1$: putting $v=(I-B)w$ gives $\omega(v,Jv)=|w|^2-|Bw|^2$. This is a convex condition. All prescribed values are zero on the protected sets, so partitions retain them. Multiple edges are handled by the disjoint sewings in the actual finite-tree charts; finite partitions preserve their agreeing face limits. Smoothness up to each conformal face in its real scale variables gives the required exponential core comparisons and uniform unit-window bounds. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 At this arity make a generic perturbation which is positive at EVERY interior conformal point and vanishes to all orders on every conformal face. Such a multiplier is explicit: choose boundary defining functions $r_j$ in the finitely many face collars and take $h=\exp(-\sum_j1/r_j^2)$ near their union, extending positively through the interior by finite smooth partitions. On each conformal coordinate patch use the localized source/target fields of [F2] multiplied by $h$. They still span densely on every compact interior set since $h>0$ there. To obtain an actual common smooth perturbation, constrain the countable smoothing coefficients additionally by every seminorm $\sup |\partial^\alpha A_j|/h'$ on its support, where $h'$ is any chosen slower positive boundary-flat function, and by every rapid end seminorm. These numbers are finite for each compactly supported bump. The explicit $2^{-j}$ smoothing choice of [F2] then makes the sum and its fixed-order derivatives boundary-flat, with uniform unit-window bounds, and rapidly decaying on each external end. At each finite neck length the multiplier is strictly positive, so universal surjectivity holds there. In particular a large but finite neck is not silently frozen before regularity has been proved. Sard–Smale over all actual index strata gives regular nonconstant polygons at this arity. The perturbation changes no lower face data and is smaller than every fixed exponential there. There are finitely many arities and label lists, so the induction uses finitely many residual choices. [F2, F3, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Every stable polygon has at least three distinct boundary labels. A constant solution would give a point in their three product tori, which is impossible by the hypothesis. Thus step5.1 gives regularity for every stable polygon which actually occurs. A source-dependent coefficient may decay towards an end rather than become exactly autonomous on one whole source tail; translated unit windows nevertheless converge to the specified autonomous path by the rapid seminorms. Whenever the map is in a generator chart its equation is EXACTLY the common product equation. These are precisely the hypotheses used for the local regularity, concentration, transverse-neck collapse and gluing of [F3]. The finite all-index energy bound remains valid for all these data by [F1]. No assertion about a constant stable polygon's universal cokernel has entered the construction. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra]

6.2 The construction is relative to a supplied compatible protected tensor and can be arbitrarily small about a supplied coherent reference system. At the end-path step start with its reference paths, take the Hilbert ball inside the desired smooth neighborhood, and choose a late collar cutoff still in that neighborhood. At the finite face induction extend the DIFFERENCE from the reference Cayley matrices; its face values are precisely the already chosen lower differences. The finite collar partitions have bounded derivatives in each stipulated finite collection of norms, so choose the lower perturbations within successively smaller positive budgets before proceeding to the next arity. Finitely many arities permit these budgets to be fixed in advance by the finite partition bounds. Take each subsequent Hilbert ball inside its remaining budget. Residual parameters are dense in each such open ball by [F2]. This retains the supplied full protected tensor and every supplied angle, while giving the desired closeness. Source-local free support is unaffected: its nonconstant-map argument uses only the fixed protected geometry and area bound, and its localized tangent can be scaled arbitrarily small without losing the nonzero obstruction pairing. This proves relative smallness, not invariance of any unproved integrable count. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For the stated relative one-parameter construction, interpolate the two coherent Cayley coefficient systems by their convex combination, performing the same finite face induction; their ends and protected values agree. Retain the given choices on fixed parameter endpoint collars. Perturb on the interior with the multipliers of step5.1 and an additional parameter cutoff positive away from those endpoint collars. Universal surjectivity there is [F2]; on the collars it follows from endpoint regularity, with the parameter appended as a free domain coordinate. Apply Sard–Smale to the whole parameterized source section, including the one parameter in its index. The resulting families have exactly the prescribed endpoints, face compatibility and coefficient bounds. The conclusion is universal regularity, so individual critical slices may fail fixed-parameter regularity and no stronger slice assertion is being made. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
