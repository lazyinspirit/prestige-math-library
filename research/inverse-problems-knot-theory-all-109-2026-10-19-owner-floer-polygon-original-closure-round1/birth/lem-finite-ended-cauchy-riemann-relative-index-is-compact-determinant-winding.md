## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $S$ be the standard disk with finitely many boundary punctures and specified holomorphic strip ends. Let $P$ and $P'$ be actual smooth real-linear Cauchy–Riemann operators on complex rank $m$ bundles over $S$, with oriented totally real boundary conditions. On each end assume specified frames identify both boundary conditions with the same transverse product planes and both operators with $D_0=\partial_s+i\partial_t$ outside a compact set. Use the actual $H^1$ boundary domains and $L^2$ targets. Cap the ends by the same framed boundary arcs and the same local coefficients to obtain two actual operators $A,A'$ on compact disks. If $w(A)$ denotes the winding of the squared determinant of their oriented real boundary frames in any global complex disk frame, then
$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P-\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P'=w(A)-w(A').$$
The difference is independent of the common caps. The same identity holds in the integer higher Sobolev realizations and for coefficients tending to the specified end operators with all needed derivatives. It is a relative operator-index statement, not a domain or Chern evaluation formula for Heegaard diagrams.

There is also unconditional linear index additivity at transverse nodes. Suppose finitely many such operators are sewn along matching exact transverse ends, with the sewn operator agreeing on fixed cores up to a norm error tending to zero and having the exact common product operator on the sufficiently long matching tails. Then its index, with each pre-existing finite parameter column included on both sides and the new neck lengths held fixed, equals the sum of the component indices. No component surjectivity is assumed. Appending an actual new conformal parameter adds one to this index; removing the actual translation of a nonconstant strip subtracts one. These assertions concern the actual operators and do not supply regularity of a moduli space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual operators, specified matching end frames, compact caps and exact matching-tail hypotheses in the statement.

[F1] Actual finite-ended disk operators are Fredholm, and their higher integer Sobolev realizations have the same kernel, cokernel and index. Their proofs construct the compact core and exact end parametrices rather than assuming a puncture index formula. ([[lem-finite-transverse-strip-ended-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm]], [[lem-transverse-strip-ended-disk-fredholm-realizations-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]])

[F2] An actual oriented real-boundary disk operator has real index $m+w$, where $w$ is its squared determinant boundary winding. Its proof also supplies the finite-cell-average approximation for compactly supported multiplication from $H^1$ to $L^2$, and actual weak higher regularity. ([[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F3] Norm limits of finite-rank Hilbert perturbations preserve Fredholmness and index, with a bounded inverse on the kernel complement; finite-dimensional Schur blocks prove local constancy without arbitrary Choice. ([[lem-countable-choice-hilbert-parametrices-and-index-stability]])

[F4] The exact transverse whole-strip inverse and Fourier expansion give a bounded inverse and exponential tails for homogeneous kernel sections. ([[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]])

## Proof

1.1 Choose a crosscut in each exact strip end. In the original standard compact disk fill the omitted boundary interval behind that crosscut; these finitely many fillings give a compact disk, without a new uniformization assertion. Along the crosscut the two bundle frames and operators are identical. Extend this frame through the filling. Join its two oriented real edge frames by any one smooth path of complex invertible matrices and use the same path for both caps; a finite path exists by elementary complex plane rotations and positive rescaling. Make the cap coefficients agree on a full band across the crosscut. The resulting $A,A'$ have the asserted exact Cauchy–Riemann symbols, oriented boundary conditions and identical cap pieces. Their core bundles need not be identified. They are Fredholm by [F2], and $P,P'$ are Fredholm by [F1]. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For linear node matching first remove the surjectivity restriction by actual finite stabilization. Each component has finite-dimensional cokernel by [F1]. Smooth compactly supported target sections are dense in its $L^2$ target, so their images span this finite quotient: a dense linear subspace of a finite-dimensional space equals that space. Choose finitely many such sections whose images form a basis, and append their coefficients as finite-dimensional domain variables. The augmented operator is onto. Its kernel-complement inverse is bounded by [F3], and the actual higher estimate in [F1] puts its $H^2$ forcing outputs in $H^3$. Both its kernel sections and the section outputs of its finite columns satisfy the homogeneous exact end equation beyond their compact supports; [F4] supplies their exponential tails. This is a finite choice of actual smooth sections, not the assertion that an abstract cokernel basis is pointwise smooth. [F1, F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 Prove the required excision rather than assuming it. Compare the disconnected operators $P\oplus A'$ and $P'\oplus A$. Cut each at the specified crosscuts. The four kinds of pieces are the two possibly different cores, the common infinite ends, and the common finite caps. On every pair of matching exterior bands identify fibers by the specified frame. For $0\le\theta\le\pi/2$ impose the transmission rule that the two exterior traces equal $R_\theta$ times the two core traces, where $R_\theta$ is the real two-by-two rotation through $\theta$, acting on the two copies of every complex fiber. This defines a closed $H^1$ transmission domain. An explicit identification with the domain at zero is obtained by leaving both cores fixed and multiplying the two exterior sections on the paired bands by $R_{\theta\chi(r)}$, where $\chi$ is one at the crosscut and zero before the other edge of the band. The same real rotation preserves the identical totally real planes at both physical boundary endpoints. Weak integration by parts shows that equality of the two transmission traces is precisely the absence of an interface delta derivative; thus these are the actual glued $H^1$ domains. Rotate the target on these exterior bands by the same multiplier. Since the two symbols there are identical and complex scalar in the fiber, conjugation changes the operator only by multiplication by derivatives of $R_{\theta\chi}$. This is compactly supported and bounded. On a rectangle meeting a physical boundary, extend across its straight edge by the bounded $H^1$ reflection and use finite square-cell averages; on interior rectangles use ordinary extension and the same averages. The coordinate-segment variance estimate gives operator-norm finite-rank approximation of these multiplications, exactly as in [F2]. Therefore [F3] gives constant index throughout this explicitly identified path. At zero the pieces assemble $P\oplus A'$; at $\pi/2$ they assemble $P'\oplus A$, with one harmless constant minus sign on a core. Consequently $\operatorname{ind}P+\operatorname{ind}A'=\operatorname{ind}P'+\operatorname{ind}A$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 On each long matching strip choose real cutoffs with squared sum one, supported on its two overlapping component pieces, with all first derivatives $O(1/T)$ and higher derivatives bounded by the corresponding powers. Cut a forcing, apply the two augmented component inverses and cut the section outputs again; keep each finite variable in its original core coordinate. This gives $P_T$ with uniformly bounded norm. Its composition with the augmented glued operator is $I+K_T$, where $\|K_T\|\le C/T+o(1)$: the squared cutoffs give the identity, their derivatives give the first term, and the assumed core comparison gives the second. The compactly supported finite-column contributions match on their cores and vanish on the tails. A Neumann series therefore gives an actual right inverse $Q_T$. Transplant component kernel vectors and project by $I-Q_TD_T$. The reverse map cuts a glued kernel vector and projects it by the component operators' $I-Q_vD_v$. On the component kernels the composition is $I+o(1)$, using their exponential tails; on the glued kernel it is $I+O(1/T)+o(1)$, using the squared-cutoff decomposition and the bounded inverses. Both compositions are invertible by Neumann series. Hence the glued augmented operator is onto with kernel dimension the sum of the component augmented kernel dimensions, and its index is the sum of their indices. The same construction applies to finitely many edges using the finite sum of the error bounds. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 By [F2], $\operatorname{ind}A=m+w(A)$ and $\operatorname{ind}A'=m+w(A')$. Substitute these equalities in step2.1 to obtain the stated relative formula. Choosing different common cap paths repeats precisely the same identity with the same left side, so the difference of the windings is unchanged. A change of global complex disk frame has zero determinant winding because it extends to the disk; a change of oriented real boundary frame has positive real determinant, also with zero winding. Thus these windings have their specified meaning. For asymptotic coefficients cut their difference from the end limit off increasingly far out. The omitted multiplication tends to zero in $H^1\to L^2$ norm, and in $H^{q+1}\to H^q$ norm when the derivatives through $q$ tend to zero. The resulting Fredholm operators converge in norm to the original operator. Local constancy in [F3] and higher-index agreement in [F1] give the same identity. No homotopy of a nontransverse end operator is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Remove the finite columns introduced in step1.2. Adding $r$ domain variables to a Fredholm operator increases its index by $r$: split its domain into its kernel and a closed complement, split the target into its range and finite cokernel, eliminate the invertible complementary block, and compute the remaining finite matrix, whose index is its number of columns minus its number of rows. This same finite-block computation includes any pre-existing finite parameter columns and shows that an appended free parameter adds one independently of its column. The number of artificial columns on the glued source is the sum of those on the components. Subtracting it from step2.2 proves unconditional node index additivity. Higher-index agreement follows from [F1], and a smooth nonconstant strip's free translation has the nonzero kernel vector $u_s$; a compact-source phase with nonzero value on that vector removes exactly one real dimension. No such phase or quotient is applied to a stable polygon. This completes the asserted relative-index and node-index statements without a Heegaard domain formula or a regularity assumption. [F1, F3, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
