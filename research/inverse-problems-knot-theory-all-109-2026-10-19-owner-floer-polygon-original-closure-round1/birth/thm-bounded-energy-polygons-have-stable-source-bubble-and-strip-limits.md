## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix finitely many compact attaching product tori with pairwise transverse finite intersections, common exact product generator charts, fixed full boundary tensors on each torus and fixed nearly symmetric protected target geometry. Consider actual polygons on the sources in the finite-tree conformal charts. Suppose their source-dependent coefficient families have uniform smooth bounds on vertex compact subsets and unit neck windows. On every neck and external end they equal the common product structure near each relevant generator. Along any sequence receding from both vertex cores on a neck, all translated coefficient derivatives on compact windows converge to the specified autonomous end path. On an external end assume the same end convergence. Coefficient convergence is uniform in the target. On each labelled physical boundary its full tensor on that torus is the fixed boundary tensor, independently of the longitudinal source coordinate. Families assembled by the explicit vertex sewings with rapidly convergent end coefficients have these properties.

Every sequence of actual smooth polygons with fixed external corner labels and uniformly bounded energy has a subsequence with a finite stable limit: polygon components on a finite conformal source tree; finite chains of autonomous strips on its internal edges and external ends; and finite trees of nonconstant spheres and one-torus boundary disks at concentration points, with stable constant components where needed. Convergence is smooth on compact subsets away from the finite nodal points after the actual conformal rescalings and strip translations. All bubble and strip necks have vanishing lost energy and vanishing image diameter; attached values agree. Total energy is the sum of the component energies. For the fixed protected incidence points their nonnegative degrees add exactly across these components. No regularity, dimension or index assumption is used.

In particular, if every prescribed basepoint degree is zero and each attaching-system complement component contains one such basepoint, all sphere and one-torus disk bubbles are constant and are removed from the stable description. Only polygon vertices and strip chains remain. The theorem does not classify low-dimensional ends, prove transversality or construct gluing collars.

The exact end charts may have arbitrary constant product angles $0<\theta_j<\pi$. All transverse Fourier and cutoff estimates below use the positive finite minimum of $\theta_j$ and $\pi-\theta_j$; no orthogonal crossing normalization is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual source, target, coefficient bounds, end convergence, full boundary tensors, fixed corners and energy bound. A finite family of conformal combinatorial types is understood for the fixed number of external labels.

[F1] Actual conformal tree extraction and exact strip necks were constructed from normalized real configurations. ([[lem-boundary-marked-disks-have-finite-tree-conformal-neck-charts]])

[F2] The actual interior and totally real boundary bubble-tree proofs give local smooth extraction, removable punctures, positive energy quanta, finite bubble trees, matched values and no lost energy. Their proofs allow smooth explicitly source-dependent coefficients with uniform local bounds and fixed full boundary tensor. ([[thm-countable-choice-interior-gromov-bubble-tree-compactness]], [[thm-countable-choice-totally-real-boundary-gromov-bubble-tree-compactness]])

[F3] The actual strip-breaking proof extracts a finite list of separated energy clusters, uses local bubble trees at each, and proves transverse-neck collapse using the exact generator model. Its autonomous strip quantum and exponential generator estimates are proved locally. ([[thm-countable-choice-bubbled-transverse-strip-breaking-compactness]], [[lem-small-local-energy-transverse-strip-neck-collapse]], [[lem-product-integrable-transverse-strip-end-exponential-asymptotics]])

[F4] Actual incidence degrees are nonnegative, admit relative line connections flat near boundary and generator images, and add through no-neck limits. Compact sphere and disk positivity includes maps contained in protected loci. A zero-basepoint sphere has zero domain and energy; a one-torus disk domain is constant on each attaching-system complement component. ([[lem-nearly-symmetric-polygons-have-positive-domains-and-degree-energy-bounds]], [[lem-divisor-degree-additivity-and-zero-marked-strip-bubble-exclusion]])

## Proof

1.1 Extract a conformal source tree by [F1], with finitely many vertex configurations in relatively compact normalized charts. Exhaust each vertex punctured source by compact cores. First extract smooth limits of the coefficient matrices on these cores times the compact target: uniform bounds on every derivative give equicontinuity of each derivative in finitely many target charts, and extraction at countably many rational coordinate points followed by the derivative bounds gives uniform convergence on smaller compact sets. A countable derivative and core diagonal yields actual smooth limit tensors, still squaring to minus identity and retaining all protected and boundary values. On these cores the actual coefficient bounds and equations satisfy the local estimates in [F2]. Cover a core by finitely many interior disks and physical-boundary half-disks. Its small-energy estimates give smooth convergence away from finitely many concentration points; each concentration point costs a common positive quantum. At each such point apply the actual rescaling and finite tree constructions in [F2]. Their rescaled coefficients freeze at the source point, and their full boundary values remain the prescribed tensor; hence their nonconstant limits are actual spheres or one-torus boundary disks, not formal energy atoms. Use successively larger integer cores and the countable simultaneous subsequence extraction of those proofs. Overlaps have the same smooth limits since they agree on their common compact sets. The energy bound and uniform local quanta bound the total number of nonconstant bubbles by a fixed integer. Limits on each vertex have removable nodal punctures where no strip end is retained, exactly as in [F2]. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Treat every long conformal neck and every external end by the actual finite-cluster argument, including possible moving concentration points. Fix a sufficiently small positive window threshold below the local regularity and strip quantum constants. A window with at least that energy contains one cluster. Choose a maximal collection of separated such windows on each neck; there are at most a fixed integer because the windows can be chosen with bounded overlap and their energies sum to at most a fixed multiple of the total bound. The selection is finite after passing to a subsequence: order the windows longitudinally, and extract limits of their pairwise distances in the compact interval $[0,\infty]$ after applying $r\mapsto r/(1+r)$. Bounded-distance clusters form one group; different groups recede from each other. Translate one representative of each group to zero. The assumed coefficient convergence makes its limit equation the actual autonomous end-path equation. Local smooth convergence and [F2] give an autonomous strip with its finite bubble trees; if that strip is constant but the group has energy, the group contains a nonconstant bubble. Each group therefore consumes a positive quantum. This is precisely the finite ordered grouping argument of [F3], now applied to the finitely many finite or semi-infinite neck intervals from [F1]. No source translation symmetry is imposed on the polygon vertex itself. [F1, F2, F3, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The remaining transverse neck regions have uniformly small energy in every fixed window; otherwise step2.1 did not extract a maximal list. Their actual source-dependent version of [F3]'s neck estimate follows by its same explicit proof. Uniform local source bounds and fixed full boundary tensors give the same target-global small-energy gradient estimate on each window. Each angular slice then has small length and its two endpoints lie on the two transverse compact tori. Compactness forces those endpoints near their finite intersection. For a small enough threshold each slice lies in one of the disjoint generator charts, and connectedness chooses one chart along the whole neck. There the ACTUAL equation is exactly $D_0$, even though the coefficients elsewhere depend on the source. The real Fourier basis and its positive gap give the exponential energy and distance bounds of [F3] on the central region, uniformly in length. The same proof covers a semi-infinite outer tail. Thus all remaining transverse neck energies vanish after the cores expand, their images collapse to the matching generator, and all intervening strip ends agree. This reconstructs the needed source-dependent neck proof rather than invoking the autonomous Statement of the neck supplier outside its scope. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Bubble annuli and boundary half-annuli have no remaining energy or image jump by [F2]'s actual no-neck estimates: in their logarithmic coordinates the explicit source derivatives tend to zero under rescaling, and the same full boundary tensor is retained on both banks in the boundary case. The small-gradient reflection and exponential cylinder estimates used in those proofs therefore apply. If an annulus failed the small-window premise it would contain an additional positive energy cluster and hence an additional nonconstant bubble; the finite-energy extraction already included all such clusters. Combining these bubble estimates with step3.1 gives the exact energy identity by first deleting fixed nodal neighborhoods, passing to the smooth limit there, then expanding the rescaled cores and shrinking the deleted neighborhoods. The residual integrals tend to zero. All attached values agree by the image-diameter part of the same estimates. Constant components are inserted only to keep distinct attachment points separated; each retained constant sphere has at least three special points, and each retained constant disk at least three boundary-weighted special points. The finite marked-point and nonconstant-component lists bound their number by the elementary finite-tree valence count. Thus one cannot obtain an infinite zero-energy ghost chain by this construction. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Degree additivity is an actual topological assertion on these maps. For a protected incidence divisor use its relative complex line and the canonical nonzero section on every boundary torus; choose its connection flat on a neighborhood of their union, and on the disjoint generator charts. Such a flat choice is compatible even where tori meet, because the same canonical divisor section is nonzero on their entire union. Cut along the finitely many small nodal circles and half-circles, and cap in the target charts where neck images collapse. The connection curvature identity for the actual line degree gives the sum of the component degrees plus the integrals on the discarded necks and caps. The latter tend to zero by their vanishing energy and diameter, exactly the local Stokes calculation in [F4]. Degrees are integers, so the equality is exact for all sufficiently far members of the sequence. The argument does not differentiate the source-dependent almost-complex tensor. Nonnegativity on every bubble, including a bubble contained in the diagonal or an incidence divisor, is the compact-map proof in [F4]. Thus no chosen protected degree can be lost in a hidden sphere or boundary disk. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 In the zero-basepoint case every nonconstant sphere would have the same positive incidence degree in all surface regions by [F4], contrary to additivity and the zero degree at any prescribed basepoint. A boundary disk on one attaching torus has constant domain coefficient on each component of that attaching-system complement. Each such component contains a prescribed basepoint. Its nonnegative coefficient is zero there by step5.1, so its entire domain and its energy are zero by the compact-map energy theorem in [F4]; it is constant. Remove those components and any unstable constants. These arguments use the actual complete attaching-system condition and do not replace ordinary weak Spin-c admissibility by a stronger hypothesis to exclude bubbles. Periodic-domain finiteness is needed separately to obtain the initial energy bound, but not for this zero-basepoint bubble exclusion. [F4, given, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 All subsequence selections are among a countable exhaustion of finitely many vertex and neck charts, as in [F2, F3], with the same Countable Choice simultaneous diagonal argument. Every iterative extraction of positive-energy objects stops after the fixed integer quantum bound; no dependent infinite choice or unproved abstract stable-map compactification has entered. The result records every actual polygon vertex, autonomous strip level and bubble, their matched values, exact degree and energy additivity, and no loss on any neck. Identifying which of these limits occur in a given low-dimensional regular moduli space, constructing collars, and proving finite counts remain further tasks. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
