## 1. TOPOLOGICAL SPACES

### DEFINITIONS
- **Topology / topological space**: family τ ⊆ P(X) containing ∅, X, closed under arbitrary unions and finite intersections.
- **Open set / closed set**: member of τ; complement of an open set. Equivalent axiomatization by closed sets (De Morgan dual).
- **Clopen set**: simultaneously open and closed; the clopen sets form a Boolean subalgebra of P(X).
- **Standard examples**: discrete, indiscrete/trivial, cofinite (finite-closed), cocountable, Sierpiński, Euclidean/metric, Sorgenfrey (lower-limit), Alexandrov.
- **Basis (base)**: B ⊆ τ such that every open set is a union of members of B.
- **Basis criterion (for a family to *be* a basis)**: B covers X, and for x ∈ B₁∩B₂ there is B₃ ∈ B with x ∈ B₃ ⊆ B₁∩B₂.
- **Subbasis (subbase)**: S ⊆ P(X); the generated topology has as basis the finite intersections of members of S.
- **Topology generated by a family**: the coarsest topology containing it = intersection of all topologies containing it.
- **Coarser / finer (weaker / stronger, smaller / larger)**: τ ⊆ τ′ means τ coarser, τ′ finer; comparable if one contains the other.
- **Neighbourhood**: N is a nbhd of x if x ∈ U ⊆ N for some open U. (Munkres/Lee restrict "neighbourhood" to *open* sets; Willard, Kelley, Engelking, Bourbaki do not — a live convention split.)
- **Neighbourhood filter / neighbourhood system** N(x): all neighbourhoods of x; a filter on X.
- **Neighbourhood base (local base, local basis)** at x: B(x) ⊆ N(x) with every N ∈ N(x) containing some B ∈ B(x).
- **First countable / second countable**: countable local base at every point / countable base. (Full countability-axiom theory is the sibling's; needed here for §5–§6.)
- **Interior** int A = largest open subset of A = ⋃{U open : U ⊆ A}.
- **Closure** cl A = Ā = smallest closed superset = ⋂{C closed : A ⊆ C}.
- **Boundary (frontier)** ∂A = cl A ∩ cl(X∖A) = cl A ∖ int A.
- **Exterior** ext A = int(X∖A) = X ∖ cl A.
- **Limit point (accumulation / cluster point)** of A: every open nbhd of x meets A∖{x}.
- **ω-accumulation point**: every nbhd meets A in an infinite set. **Condensation point**: every nbhd meets A in an uncountable set.
- **Derived set** A′ = set of limit points of A. **Isolated point** of A: point of A that is not a limit point of A.
- **Perfect set**: closed and dense-in-itself (A = A′). **Scattered**: every nonempty subset has an isolated point.
- **Dense**: cl D = X. **Nowhere dense**: int(cl A) = ∅. **Dense-in-itself**: no isolated points. **Separable**: has a countable dense subset.
- **Meagre (first category) / comeagre (residual)**: countable union of nowhere dense sets / complement of such.
- **Regular open / regular closed**: A = int(cl A) / A = cl(int A).
- **Kuratowski closure axioms** [K1] c(∅)=∅, [K2] A ⊆ c(A), [K3] c(c(A))=c(A), [K4] c(A∪B)=c(A)∪c(B).
- **Dual interior axioms** [I1] i(X)=X, [I2] i(A) ⊆ A, [I3] i(i(A))=i(A), [I4] i(A∩B)=i(A)∩i(B).
- **Čech closure operator**: drops idempotence [K3]. **Moore closure**: drops [K1].
- **Hausdorff's neighbourhood axioms**: the original axiomatization of a topology by assigning filters N(x) with the "shrinking" axiom.
- **Subspace topology (relative topology)** on A ⊆ X: {A ∩ U : U ∈ τ}; a **relatively open** set.
- **Hereditary property**: passes to every subspace. **Weakly hereditary**: passes to closed subspaces. **Open-hereditary**: passes to open subspaces.
- **Topological property (invariant)**: preserved by homeomorphism.
- **Specialization preorder** x ≤ y iff x ∈ cl{y}; **Alexandrov topology**: arbitrary intersections of opens are open (⟺ topology from a preorder).
- **Weight, density, cellularity (ccc), π-base, π-weight**: standard cardinal functions.

### THEOREMS
- **Closed-set axiomatization**: τ ↔ the family of complements satisfying arbitrary intersections / finite unions.
- **Lattice of topologies**: an arbitrary intersection of topologies on X is a topology; the topologies on X form a complete lattice (meet = intersection, join = topology generated by the union). The union of two topologies is generally *not* a topology.
- **Generation theorem**: every S ⊆ P(X) generates a unique coarsest topology; finite intersections of S ∪ {X} form a basis.
- **Basis comparison lemma** (Munkres 13.3): τ′ ⊇ τ iff for each x and each B ∈ B with x ∈ B there is B′ ∈ B′ with x ∈ B′ ⊆ B.
- **Closure characterization**: x ∈ cl A iff every open (equivalently every basic, equivalently every subbasic-∩-finite) nbhd of x meets A.
- **cl A = A ∪ A′**; A is closed iff A′ ⊆ A iff A = cl A; A is open iff A = int A.
- **Kuratowski's theorem**: closure operators on X correspond bijectively to topologies on X; likewise for the dual interior operators and for neighbourhood systems.
- **Kuratowski closure-complement (14-set) theorem**: at most 14 distinct sets from a subset under closure and complement; achieved in ℝ by (0,1) ∪ (1,2) ∪ {3} ∪ ([4,5] ∩ ℚ).
- **Interior/closure identities**: int(A∩B)=int A ∩ int B and cl(A∪B)=cl A ∪ cl B (equalities); int(A∪B) ⊇ int A ∪ int B and cl(A∩B) ⊆ cl A ∩ cl B (inclusions only). Infinite versions give only inclusions for *both*.
- **Monotonicity + idempotence** of int and cl; exterior reverses inclusions and is *not* idempotent.
- **Partition**: X = int A ⊔ ∂A ⊔ ext A (disjoint). A closed ⟺ ∂A ⊆ A; A open ⟺ A ∩ ∂A = ∅; A clopen ⟺ ∂A = ∅.
- **∂(A) is closed; ∂(∂(∂A)) = ∂(∂A)**; the boundary of an open (or closed) set is nowhere dense.
- **Density criteria**: D dense ⟺ D meets every nonempty open ⟺ meets every nonempty basic open ⟺ int(X∖D) = ∅.
- **Regular open algebra**: the regular open sets of any space form a *complete Boolean algebra* under A∧B = A∩B, A∨B = int cl(A∪B), ¬A = int(X∖A).
- **Subspace theorems**: closed sets of A are A ∩ (closed in X); cl_A(S) = A ∩ cl_X(S); int_A(S) ⊇ A ∩ int_X(S), and the inclusion can be strict.
- **Basis for a subspace**: {B ∩ A : B ∈ B}. **Transitivity**: a subspace of a subspace is a subspace of the ambient space.
- **Open/closed transfer**: if A is open (closed) in X, then subsets open (closed) in A are open (closed) in X.
- **Hereditary**: T0, T1, Hausdorff, regular, completely regular, first/second countability, metrizability, totally disconnected, zero-dimensional. **Weakly hereditary only**: compact, countably compact, Lindelöf, σ-compact, normal, paracompact. **Open-hereditary**: local compactness (Hausdorff), local connectedness, separability. **Not hereditary at all**: connectedness, compactness.
- **Alexandrov correspondence**: Alexandrov topologies on X ↔ preorders on X, via the specialization preorder; finite spaces are exactly finite preorders.

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Munkres §§12–17** is the canonical undergraduate ordering (spaces → basis → order topology → product on X×Y → subspace → closed sets/limit points). He introduces the **order topology as its own §14** early, and defines "neighbourhood" to mean *open* set.
- **Willard Ch. 2** (§2.1 Fundamental Concepts, §2.2 Neighborhoods, §2.3 Bases and Subbases) is the best textbook treatment of the neighbourhood-filter axiomatization and of subbases.
- **Engelking §1.1–§1.3** is the reference-grade version: bases, closure/interior, "methods of generating topologies" (§1.2 — a *separate section* on generating a topology from a base, subbase, closure operator, or neighbourhood system, which most books scatter), then boundary/derived set/dense/nowhere dense/Borel sets in §1.3.
- **Bourbaki I §1** does open sets → neighbourhoods → closed sets → *fundamental systems of neighbourhoods* → bases → **locally finite families** → interior/closure/frontier, i.e. it puts local finiteness in the very first section, far earlier than anyone else.
- **Kelley Ch. 1** and **Morris Ch. 1–3** are the gentlest; Morris does topology (Ch.1), Euclidean topology + bases (Ch.2), limit points/closure/neighbourhoods (Ch.3).
- **Cambridge IB** (Rasmussen) is the minimal viable core: definition, metric topologies, neighbourhoods, closed sets, closure and interior — nothing on derived sets, Kuratowski axioms, or regular open sets.
- **Kuratowski axioms** appear in Engelking §1.2, Kelley Ch.1 problems, and Wikipedia; **Munkres omits them entirely** — a well-known gap in the standard undergraduate curriculum.

## 2. CONTINUITY AND MAPS

### DEFINITIONS
- **Continuous map**: f: X → Y with f⁻¹(V) open for every open V ⊆ Y.
- **Continuous at a point x**: for every nbhd V of f(x) there is a nbhd U of x with f(U) ⊆ V.
- **Sequentially continuous**: xₙ → x ⟹ f(xₙ) → f(x).
- **Homeomorphism**: continuous bijection with continuous inverse; **homeomorphic (topologically equivalent)** spaces.
- **Open map / closed map**: image of every open (closed) set is open (closed).
- **Embedding (imbedding)**: f: X → Y injective and a homeomorphism onto f(X) with the subspace topology.
- **Quotient map (identification map)**: surjective f with "U open in Y ⟺ f⁻¹(U) open in X".
- **Saturated set**: S = f⁻¹(f(S)); equivalently a union of fibres.
- **Local homeomorphism**: every point has an open nbhd mapped homeomorphically onto an open set.
- **Retraction / retract / section**: r: X → A ⊆ X with r|A = id; a right inverse of a surjection.
- **Initial (weak/projective) topology** induced by {fᵢ: X → Yᵢ}: coarsest topology making all fᵢ continuous; subbasis {fᵢ⁻¹(V)}.
- **Final (strong/inductive) topology** induced by {fᵢ: Xᵢ → Y}: finest topology making all fᵢ continuous.
- **Universal / characteristic property** (Lee's term): the mapping-out (quotient, coproduct, final) or mapping-in (product, subspace, initial) factorization property that determines the topology up to unique homeomorphism.
- **Evaluation map** e: X → ∏ Yᵢ, e(x) = (fᵢ(x)); **separating points**; **separating points from closed sets**.
- **Perfect map**: continuous, closed, surjective, with compact fibres. **Proper map**: see §5.

### THEOREMS
- **Continuity TFAE**: (i) preimages of opens are open; (ii) preimages of closeds are closed; (iii) f(cl A) ⊆ cl f(A) for all A ⊆ X; (iv) cl f⁻¹(B) ⊆ f⁻¹(cl B); (v) f⁻¹(int B) ⊆ int f⁻¹(B); (vi) f is continuous at every point.
- **Subbasis criterion**: f is continuous iff f⁻¹(S) is open for every S in a *subbasis* of Y (equivalently a basis). This is the workhorse for products and order topologies.
- **Closure/limit-point characterization**: f continuous ⟹ f(A′) ⊆ cl f(A) (note: *not* f(A′) ⊆ f(A)′).
- **Composition, restriction, corestriction, expansion**: composites of continuous maps are continuous; f|_A is continuous; corestricting to any subspace containing the image is continuous; expanding the codomain is continuous. Constants and inclusions are continuous.
- **Continuity is local**: if {Uᵢ} is an open cover and each f|_{Uᵢ} is continuous, then f is continuous.
- **Pasting (gluing) lemma**: X = A ∪ B with A, B both closed (or both open) and f|_A, f|_B continuous and agreeing on A∩B ⟹ f continuous. Extends to arbitrary open covers and to **locally finite closed covers**. Fails for a general infinite closed cover (Wikipedia's counterexample: ℤ ⊆ ℝ covered by singletons, with ℤ given the cofinite topology).
- **Homeomorphism criteria**: a continuous bijection is a homeomorphism ⟺ it is open ⟺ it is closed ⟺ it is a quotient map.
- **Quotient map recognition**: continuous open surjections and continuous closed surjections are quotient maps; the converse fails — quotient maps are neither open nor closed in general.
- **Characteristic property of the quotient**: for q: X → Y quotient, g: Y → Z is continuous ⟺ g∘q is continuous. **Passing to the quotient**: f: X → Z constant on fibres of q factors uniquely through q.
- **Quotient composition/cancellation**: a composite of quotient maps is a quotient map; if q∘f is a quotient map and f is a continuous surjection, then q is a quotient map.
- **Initial topology**: exists and is unique; **characteristic property** (g: Z → X continuous ⟺ every fᵢ∘g continuous); **transitivity** (initial of initials is initial); the initial topology of a single inclusion is the subspace topology; of the projections, the product topology.
- **Final topology**: dual statements; final of inclusions of a partition into open pieces = disjoint-union topology; final of a single surjection = quotient topology.
- **Embedding lemma (evaluation map)**: if {fᵢ} separates points and separates points from closed sets, then e: X → ∏Yᵢ is an embedding; if it merely separates points and X is compact Hausdorff, e is still an embedding. (This is the engine of Tychonoff-cube embeddings and of Urysohn metrization — statement level here; metrization is the sibling's.)
- **Open-map criterion**: f is open iff f(B) is open for every B in a basis of X.
- **Projections** πᵢ: ∏Xⱼ → Xᵢ are continuous, surjective and **open**, but generally **not closed** (π₁: ℝ² → ℝ and the hyperbola xy = 1).
- **Categorical facts**: **Top** is complete and cocomplete; the forgetful functor Top → Set has both a left adjoint (discrete) and a right adjoint (indiscrete), hence preserves all limits and colimits — which is exactly why underlying sets of products/quotients are the set-theoretic ones.
- **Sequential continuity**: continuous ⟹ sequentially continuous; converse holds iff the domain is a sequential space (§6).
- **Compact-to-Hausdorff trick**: a continuous map from a compact space to a Hausdorff space is closed, hence proper, hence a quotient map onto its image; if bijective, a homeomorphism (§5).

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Munkres §18** ("Continuous Functions") bundles continuity, homeomorphism, open/closed maps, the pasting lemma and the "maps into products" rule into one section; §22 is the quotient topology, starred as optional in the 2e — a curricular oddity, since quotients are indispensable.
- **Lee, *Introduction to Topological Manifolds* Ch. 3** ("New Spaces from Old") is the clearest modern source for **characteristic properties**: he states and uses them for subspaces, products, disjoint unions and quotients uniformly, and explicitly makes them the *definition* up to homeomorphism.
- **Bourbaki I §2** is the source of the initial/final language ("initial topologies", "final topologies", "pasting together of topological spaces", "canonical decomposition of a continuous mapping" f = (injection)∘(bijection)∘(surjection)). Bourbaki treats these as the organizing principle; Munkres never names them.
- **Willard §3.2 (Continuous functions), §3.3 (Product spaces, weak topologies)** — Willard uses "weak topology induced by a family" for the initial topology; Kelley likewise. Engelking §1.4 uses "continuous, closed and open mappings".
- **Dugundji** is the classical reference for weak/strong topologies and for the **Dugundji extension theorem**; its section structure could not be retrieved.
- **nLab** gives the pure categorical framing (Top's limits/colimits, adjoint triple, quotient = coequalizer, adjunction = pushout) and is the best source for the universal-property statements as *definitions*.

## 3. CONSTRUCTIONS

### DEFINITIONS
- **Product topology** on ∏ᵢXᵢ: initial topology for the projections; basis = ∏Uᵢ with Uᵢ = Xᵢ for all but finitely many i.
- **Box topology**: basis = *all* ∏Uᵢ with Uᵢ open; strictly finer than the product topology for infinite index sets.
- **Disjoint union (coproduct, topological sum, direct sum)** ⨆Xᵢ: final topology for the injections; U open iff U ∩ Xᵢ open in each Xᵢ.
- **Quotient topology / quotient space** X/∼: final topology for the canonical projection; **X/A** = collapse a subspace to a point.
- **Adjunction (attaching) space** X ∪_f Y: for A ⊆ Y closed and f: A → X continuous, (X ⊔ Y)/(a ∼ f(a)).
- **Mapping cylinder** M_f = (X×I) ∪_f Y; **mapping cone**; **cone** CX = (X×I)/(X×{1}); **suspension** ΣX = (X×I)/(X×{0}, X×{1} each collapsed) = CX ∪_X CX.
- **Wedge sum** X ∨ Y (one-point union); **smash product** X ∧ Y = (X×Y)/(X∨Y); **join** X * Y.
- **Order topology** on a totally ordered set: subbasis of open rays (a,→) and (←,b); basis of open intervals plus the rays.
- **Left / right order topology**: bases of rays in one direction only (used for semicontinuity).
- **Lexicographic (dictionary) order topology** on a product of ordered sets; the **long line**; **ordinal spaces** [0,λ), [0,λ].
- **Linear continuum**: totally ordered set with the least-upper-bound property and densely ordered.
- **Topological group**: group with a topology making (x,y) ↦ xy⁻¹ continuous; **homogeneous space**; **coset space** G/H.
- **Inverse (projective) system and inverse limit**; **direct (inductive) system and colimit**; **coherent / weak topology** determined by a family of subspaces (CW complexes).
- **Topological sum vs. product duality**; **cylinder** X×I; **orbit space** X/G.

### THEOREMS
- **Product = initial topology for projections**; subbasis πᵢ⁻¹(Uᵢ); for finite products basis = ∏Uᵢ.
- **Characteristic property of the product**: f: Z → ∏Xᵢ is continuous ⟺ every πᵢ∘f is continuous. **This fails for the box topology** — Munkres's canonical counterexample f(t) = (t, t, t, …): ℝ → ℝ^ω is continuous into the product, not into the box.
- **Box vs product**: they agree iff the index set is finite (or all but finitely many factors are indiscrete); the box topology is finer.
- **cl(∏Aᵢ) = ∏ cl(Aᵢ)** in *both* the product and box topologies; but int(∏Aᵢ) = ∏ int(Aᵢ) only when all but finitely many Aᵢ = Xᵢ.
- **Subspaces and products commute**: ∏(Aᵢ ⊆ Xᵢ) as a subspace of ∏Xᵢ carries the product of the subspace topologies. Products are associative and commutative up to canonical homeomorphism.
- **Productive properties**: T0, T1, Hausdorff, regular, Tychonoff, connected, path-connected, compact (Tychonoff), zero-dimensional, totally disconnected.
- **Non-productive**: normality (Sorgenfrey plane), Lindelöf (Sorgenfrey plane), paracompactness, local compactness (a product is locally compact iff every factor is and all but finitely many are compact), countable compactness, sequential compactness (only countable products), σ-compactness (only finite products), metrizability and first/second countability (only countable products).
- **Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery**: a product of at most 2^κ spaces of density ≤ κ has density ≤ κ; in particular a product of at most 𝔠 separable spaces is separable — separability is far more productive than intuition suggests.
- **Disjoint union**: each summand is clopen in ⨆Xᵢ; characteristic property (maps out of a sum = families of maps); ⨆ preserves local properties (local compactness, local connectedness) and destroys global ones (connectedness; compactness only for finite sums).
- **Quotient theorems**: quotients preserve compactness, connectedness, path-connectedness, separability, Lindelöf; they do **not** preserve Hausdorffness, local compactness, first/second countability, or metrizability.
- **Open equivalence relations**: if the quotient map q is open, then X/∼ is Hausdorff ⟺ the relation R ⊆ X×X is closed; X/∼ is T1 ⟺ every equivalence class is closed.
- **Compact-Hausdorff quotient theorem**: if X is compact Hausdorff and R is a closed equivalence relation, X/R is compact Hausdorff; equivalently, a continuous surjection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a quotient map — the standard tool for building the torus, Möbius band, Klein bottle and ℝPⁿ from squares/disks.
- **Whitehead's theorem**: if q: X → Y is a quotient map and Z is locally compact Hausdorff, then q × id_Z : X×Z → Y×Z is a quotient map. **The product of two quotient maps need not be a quotient map** (Dowker's example).
- **Adjunction space = pushout in Top**; if A is closed in Y, then X embeds as a closed subspace of X ∪_f Y and Y∖A embeds as an open subspace; adjunction of a normal space to a normal space along a closed set is normal.
- **Cone/suspension**: CX is always contractible; ΣSⁿ ≅ Sⁿ⁺¹; X ↪ CX; the suspension is the double mapping cylinder of X → pt, X → pt.
- **Order topology theorems**: any LOTS is Hausdorff and **hereditarily normal (completely normal)**, in fact monotonically normal; the order topology on a subset is generally **coarser** than the subspace topology, and they agree on convex subsets.
- **Linear continuum ⟹ connected** (Munkres 24.1), and the connected subsets of a linear continuum are exactly the convex sets (intervals and rays).
- **Ordinal spaces**: [0,λ] is compact for every ordinal λ; [0,ω₁) is countably compact, sequentially compact, first countable, but not compact and not Lindelöf; [0,ω₁] is compact but not first countable at ω₁ and not sequentially "reachable".
- **Topological groups (elementary)**: translations and inversion are homeomorphisms ⟹ G is a **homogeneous space**; the topology is determined by any nbhd base at e; for each nbhd U of e there is a symmetric V with VV ⊆ U; **T0 ⟹ Hausdorff ⟹ completely regular (Tychonoff)**; the closure of a subgroup is a subgroup and of a normal subgroup is normal; an open subgroup is closed; G → G/H is always an **open** map; G/H is Hausdorff ⟺ H is closed; products of topological groups are topological groups; locally compact groups carry a Haar measure (statement level).
- **Inverse limits**: an inverse limit of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces over a directed index set is nonempty and compact Hausdorff (uses Tychonoff, hence AC); inverse limits of finite discrete spaces = profinite/Stone spaces.

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Munkres** splits the product topology in two: §15 (finite, X×Y) and §19 (arbitrary, product vs box) — pedagogically deliberate, and §19 is where the box-topology counterexamples live. Quotients are §22 and marked optional.

- **Engelking Ch. 2 "Operations on topological spaces"** is the most complete: §2.1 Subspaces, §2.2 **Sums**, §2.3 Cartesian products, §2.4 Quotient spaces and quotient mappings, §2.5 **Limits of inverse systems**, §2.6 Function spaces I. He also supplies literal **invariance tables** in the appendix ("Invariants of operations", "Invariants and inverse invariants of mappings") — the single most useful curricular artifact for this section.
- **Bourbaki I §2–§4** covers subspaces, locally closed subspaces, quotient spaces, canonical decomposition, products *and* inverse limits, then Ch. III is topological groups in full — Bourbaki is the reference for the "pasting together of topological spaces" (gluing along open subsets).

The chapter-9 build-up (Cantor set → Hilbert cube → Urysohn's embedding → Peano's theorem) is unusual and excellent.

## 4. CONNECTEDNESS

### DEFINITIONS
- **Separation** of X: a pair of disjoint nonempty open sets with union X. **Connected**: no separation exists. **Disconnected**: otherwise.
- **Separated sets**: A, B with A ∩ cl B = cl A ∩ B = ∅ (weaker than disjoint closures being disjoint).
- **Connected subset**: connected in the subspace topology (equivalently, not the union of two nonempty separated sets).
- **{0,1}-criterion**: X is connected iff every continuous map X → {0,1} (discrete) — equivalently X → ℤ — is constant.
- **Connected component** of x: the union of all connected subsets containing x; the maximal connected subset containing x.
- **Quasicomponent** of x: the intersection of all clopen sets containing x.
- **Path**: continuous γ: [0,1] → X. **Path-connected**; **path component**.
- **Arc**: an embedding [0,1] ↪ X. **Arcwise (arc-)connected**.
- **Locally connected (at x)**: every nbhd of x contains a connected *open* nbhd of x.
- **Connected im kleinen (weakly locally connected) at x**: every nbhd of x contains a connected (not necessarily open) nbhd of x.
- **Locally path-connected**; **semi-locally simply connected** (for covering space theory).
- **Totally disconnected**: all components are singletons. **Hereditarily disconnected**: same (terminology varies).
- **Totally separated**: for x ≠ y there is a clopen U with x ∈ U, y ∉ U (equivalently all quasicomponents are singletons).
- **Zero-dimensional**: has a base of clopen sets. **Extremally disconnected**: the closure of every open set is open.
- **Chain-connected / well-chained** (metric): for every ε>0 any two points are joined by a finite ε-chain x = p₀, …, pₙ = y with d(pᵢ,pᵢ₊₁) < ε.
- **Continuum**: compact connected Hausdorff space (metric continuum if metrizable). **Peano continuum**: connected, locally connected, compact metric.
- **Cut point**: x such that X∖{x} is disconnected. **Dispersion point**; **non-cut point**.
- **Hyperconnected (irreducible)**: no two nonempty opens are disjoint. **Ultraconnected**: no two nonempty closeds are disjoint.

### THEOREMS
- **Equivalent formulations**: X connected ⟺ the only clopen subsets are ∅ and X ⟺ every continuous X → {0,1} is constant ⟺ X is not a union of two nonempty separated sets ⟺ ∂A ≠ ∅ for every proper nonempty A.
- **Continuous images of connected spaces are connected** — hence connectedness is a topological invariant, and hence the topological **Intermediate Value Theorem**.
- **Intermediate Value Theorem (topological form, Munkres 24.3)**: X connected, Y an ordered set with the order topology, f: X → Y continuous, and r between f(a) and f(b) ⟹ r = f(c) for some c.
- **Unions**: a union of connected sets with a point in common is connected; a union of a chain (or pairwise-intersecting family) of connected sets is connected; if {Cᵢ} are connected and C₀ meets each, ⋃Cᵢ is connected.
- **Closure**: if C is connected and C ⊆ D ⊆ cl C then D is connected; in particular cl C is connected.
- **Products**: an arbitrary product of connected spaces is connected (finite case by the "cross" argument; infinite case by density of finite-support sub-products — **requires AC to choose a base point in each factor**).
- **Quotients and sums**: quotients of connected spaces are connected; a disjoint sum of ≥2 nonempty spaces is disconnected.
- **Components**: partition X; are connected, maximal, and **always closed**; are *not* open in general; components are open ⟺ (locally, for each point) the space is locally connected.
- **Quasicomponents**: each component ⊆ its quasicomponent; **components = quasicomponents in compact Hausdorff spaces (Šura-Bura)** and in locally connected spaces; the inclusion is strict in general (standard example: {0,1}×{0} ∪ ⋃ₙ({1/n}×[0,1]) style constructions).
- **Path components ⊆ components**; path-connected ⟹ connected; **converse false** (topologist's sine curve).
- **Locally connected ⟺ components of every open set are open** ⟺ the space is connected im kleinen at every point. (Pointwise, "connected im kleinen at x" is strictly weaker than "locally connected at x".)
- **Locally path-connected ⟺ path components of every open set are open**; locally path-connected ⟹ components = path components.
- **Connected + locally path-connected ⟹ path-connected.** Corollary: connected open subsets of ℝⁿ (and of manifolds) are path-connected, indeed polygonally connected.
- **Connected subsets of ℝ are exactly the intervals** (including rays, ℝ, singletons, ∅); more generally the connected subsets of a linear continuum are exactly the convex sets.
- **A linear continuum is connected**; ℝ is connected; [a,b] is connected.
- **Cut point invariants**: homeomorphisms preserve cut points and the number of components of complements — used to prove (0,1) ≇ [0,1) ≇ [0,1], S¹ ≇ [0,1], ℝ ≇ ℝⁿ (n>1), and to distinguish letters/graphs.
- **Sierpiński's theorem**: a continuum cannot be partitioned into countably many (≥2) pairwise disjoint nonempty closed sets.
- **Boundary bumping lemma**: if C is a component of a closed proper subset K of a continuum X, then cl(C) ∩ ∂K ≠ ∅.
- **Totally disconnected vs totally separated vs zero-dimensional**: totally separated ⟹ totally disconnected; zero-dimensional + T0 ⟹ totally separated; **for locally compact Hausdorff spaces all three coincide**; the counterexamples in general are the Knaster–Kuratowski fan and Erdős space.
- **Stone spaces**: compact Hausdorff + totally disconnected ⟺ zero-dimensional compact Hausdorff ⟺ profinite (inverse limit of finite discrete spaces) ⟺ Stone dual of a Boolean algebra.
- **Chain-connectedness**: connected ⟹ well-chained; the converse holds for **compact** metric spaces (ℚ is well-chained but not connected) — the standard bridge result usually left as an exercise.
- **Local connectedness is preserved by**: open continuous surjections, quotient maps, disjoint sums, and finite products; **not** by arbitrary continuous maps or by arbitrary products (products of locally connected are locally connected iff all but finitely many factors are connected).
- **Hahn–Mazurkiewicz theorem** (statement level, on the boundary of this section and §5): a Hausdorff space is a continuous image of [0,1] iff it is a Peano continuum.

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Munkres §§23–25** is the reference ordering: connected spaces, connected subspaces of the real line (incl. the linear continuum and IVT), then components + local connectedness. He puts the topologist's sine curve in §24's exercises.
- **Cambridge IB** explicitly teaches the **integer-valued-function criterion** for connectedness alongside the open-set definition — a formulation Munkres relegates to an exercise but which is far easier to apply.
- **Engelking Ch. 6** ("Connected spaces": §6.1 Connected spaces, §6.2 **Various kinds of disconnectedness**) is the only standard text with a systematic section on the disconnectedness hierarchy (hereditarily disconnected / totally disconnected / totally separated / zero-dimensional / extremally disconnected).
- **Willard Ch. 8** places connectedness *after* compactness and metrization — a minority ordering; **Morris introduces connectedness in Ch. 3**, before continuity, which is the earliest of any source.
- **Lee Ch. 4** ("Connectedness and Compactness") merges both; Lee is unusually careful about locally path-connected ⟹ components = path components, because he needs it for covering spaces.
- **Kelley** treats connectedness lightly (problems in Ch. 1 and Ch. 3) — Kelley's focus is convergence/uniformities; do not use Kelley as the connectedness source.
- **Quasicomponents** are in Willard, Engelking, and Pete Clark's notes, but **absent from Munkres and from most first courses** — the single most commonly missed item in this section.

## 5. COMPACTNESS

### DEFINITIONS
- **Cover / open cover / subcover / refinement**; **locally finite**, **point-finite**, **star-finite** families.
- **Compact**: every open cover has a finite subcover. **Quasicompact** (Bourbaki/EGA): the same property without Hausdorff.
- **Finite intersection property (FIP)**: every finite subfamily has nonempty intersection.
- **Compact subset**: compact in the subspace topology ⟺ every cover by open sets *of the ambient space* has a finite subfamily covering it.
- **Relatively compact (precompact)**: closure is compact.
- **Lindelöf**: every open cover has a countable subcover. **Hereditarily Lindelöf**.
- **σ-compact**: a countable union of compact subsets. **Hemicompact**: a sequence of compacts cofinal in the compacts under inclusion. **Exhaustion by compacts**: Kₙ ⊆ int Kₙ₊₁, ⋃Kₙ = X.
- **Countably compact**: every countable open cover has a finite subcover.
- **Sequentially compact**: every sequence has a convergent subsequence.
- **Limit point compact (weakly countably compact, Bolzano–Weierstrass property)**: every infinite subset has a limit point.
- **Pseudocompact**: every continuous real-valued function is bounded.
- **Locally compact** — at least five inequivalent versions: (a) every point has a compact nbhd; (b) every point has a *closed* compact nbhd; (c) every point has a relatively compact open nbhd; (d) every point has a local base of compact nbhds; (e) every point has a local base of closed compact nbhds.
- **Paracompact**: every open cover has a locally finite open refinement. **Metacompact**: point-finite open refinement. **Orthocompact**: interior-preserving open refinement. **Countably paracompact**.
- **Partition of unity subordinate to a cover**; **support**.
- **Compactification**: a compact space containing X as a dense subspace. **One-point (Alexandroff)**; **Stone–Čech βX**; **Wallman**.
- **k-space (compactly generated)**: topology coherent with the compact (or compact Hausdorff) subspaces; **k-ification**.
- **Proper map**: preimages of compacta are compact (Munkres/analysis convention) vs **universally closed**: f × id_Z is closed for every Z (Bourbaki).
- **Perfect map**: continuous closed surjection with compact fibres.
- **Alexander subbasis**; **Lebesgue number**; **totally bounded** (metric).

### THEOREMS
- **FIP characterization**: X compact ⟺ every family of closed sets with the FIP has nonempty intersection.
- **Closed subspaces of compact spaces are compact**; **compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed**; a compact subset of a non-Hausdorff space need not be closed.
- **Compact Hausdorff ⟹ regular and normal**; more precisely disjoint compact sets in a Hausdorff space have disjoint open nbhds.
- **Continuous images of compact spaces are compact**. **Extreme value theorem**: a continuous real (or order-valued) function on a nonempty compact space attains its supremum and infimum.
- **Compact-to-Hausdorff**: a continuous map from compact to Hausdorff is **closed**, hence proper, hence a quotient map onto its image; if injective it is an embedding; if bijective it is a **homeomorphism**.
- **Finite unions of compacts are compact**; a compact ∩ closed is compact; in Hausdorff spaces an arbitrary intersection of compacts is compact.
- **Tube lemma**: if K is compact and N ⊆ X×K is open with {x₀}×K ⊆ N, then there is an open W ∋ x₀ with W×K ⊆ N. **Choice-free.** Generalized tube lemma: for compact A ⊆ X, compact B ⊆ Y, and open N ⊇ A×B, there are opens U ⊇ A, V ⊇ B with U×V ⊆ N.
- **Projection along a compact factor is closed**; **Kuratowski–Mrówka theorem**: X is compact ⟺ πY: X×Y → Y is closed for every space Y — a **choice-free**, purely categorical characterization.
- **Finite products of compact spaces are compact** (no choice needed; proved by the tube lemma).
- **Tychonoff's theorem**: an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact. **Equivalent to AC over ZF (Kelley 1950).** The **Hausdorff-factors version is equivalent to the ultrafilter lemma / Boolean prime ideal theorem (BPI)** (Łoś–Ryll-Nardzewski, Rubin–Scott), as is compactness of the Tychonoff cube [0,1]^I.
- **Alexander subbase lemma**: if every cover by members of a fixed subbasis has a finite subcover, X is compact. **Uses Zorn's lemma; BPI suffices.** Gives a short proof of Tychonoff.
- **Heine–Borel**: A ⊆ ℝⁿ is compact ⟺ closed and bounded. (Compactness of [a,b] itself needs only the least-upper-bound property — no choice.)
- **Metric equivalences**: compact ⟺ sequentially compact ⟺ limit point compact ⟺ countably compact ⟺ complete + totally bounded. **These use countable/dependent choice.**
- **Lebesgue number lemma** and the **uniform continuity theorem** for compact metric spaces.
- **Countably compact TFAE**: countable open covers have finite subcovers ⟺ every countable family of closed sets with FIP has nonempty intersection ⟺ every sequence has a cluster point ⟺ every infinite set has an ω-accumulation point.
- **Implication chart**: compact ⟹ countably compact ⟹ limit point compact and ⟹ pseudocompact; sequentially compact ⟹ countably compact. **Countably compact ⟺ limit point compact for T1 spaces**; **countably compact ⟺ sequentially compact for first-countable spaces**. Compact and sequentially compact are **independent** in general.
- **Lindelöf + countably compact ⟺ compact.**
- **Second countable ⟹ Lindelöf and separable** (uses AC_ω); for metric spaces second countable ⟺ Lindelöf ⟺ separable.
- **Lindelöf regular ⟹ normal** (Tychonoff's lemma) and ⟹ paracompact; Lindelöf is closed-hereditary and preserved by continuous images; **not productive** (the Sorgenfrey line is Lindelöf, the Sorgenfrey plane is not).
- **σ-compact ⟹ Lindelöf**; **locally compact Hausdorff + Lindelöf ⟹ σ-compact ⟹ admits an exhaustion by compacts**; σ-compact + locally compact Hausdorff ⟹ paracompact.
- **Local compactness**: definitions (a)–(e) coincide for Hausdorff (indeed preregular) spaces but not in general; **a subspace of a LCH space is locally compact ⟺ it is locally closed** (open in its closure); open and closed subspaces of LCH are LCH; **LCH ⟹ completely regular (Tychonoff)** and **LCH ⟹ Baire**.
- **Baire category theorem, second form**: every locally compact Hausdorff space is a Baire space (not just complete metric spaces).
- **One-point compactification X\***: always compact; X open and dense in X\* (when X is noncompact); **X\* is Hausdorff ⟺ X is locally compact Hausdorff**; it is the unique *minimum* Hausdorff compactification; Sⁿ = (ℝⁿ)\*; functorial for proper maps.
- **Stone–Čech βX** (statement level): for Tychonoff X, a compact Hausdorff space with a dense copy of X such that every continuous f: X → K (K compact Hausdorff) extends uniquely to βX; it is the **maximum** Hausdorff compactification; βX = X ⟺ X is compact Hausdorff; C(βX) ≅ C_b(X); βℕ = the space of ultrafilters on ℕ. **Existence over ZF requires the ultrafilter lemma (BPI).**
- **Paracompactness**: **A. H. Stone's theorem** — every metric space is paracompact (needs AC; not a ZF theorem). **Dieudonné** — every paracompact Hausdorff space is normal. **Michael's theorem** — a regular space is paracompact if every open cover has a locally finite (not necessarily open) refinement. **Partitions of unity** exist subordinate to every open cover ⟺ paracompact Hausdorff. Closed-hereditary; **not productive** (Sorgenfrey plane). compact ⟹ paracompact ⟹ metacompact ⟹ orthocompact.
- **k-spaces**: locally compact Hausdorff, first-countable (hence metric), and sequential spaces are k-spaces; quotients and disjoint sums of k-spaces are k-spaces; **CGHaus is cartesian closed** (products/function spaces behave); k-ification is a coreflection.
- **Proper maps**: for the Bourbaki definition, proper ⟺ universally closed ⟺ closed with compact fibres; the two definitions agree when the codomain is locally compact Hausdorff (or compactly generated Hausdorff); a proper map into a LCH space is closed; X is compact ⟺ X → pt is proper; proper maps extend to the one-point compactifications.
- **Perfect maps**: perfect preimages of compact spaces are compact; perfect maps preserve compactness, Lindelöf, paracompactness, normality, and *reflect* metrizability and second countability (Engelking §3.7).
- **Compactness is: closed-hereditary, preserved by continuous images / quotients / finite unions / arbitrary products; not hereditary, not preserved by inverse images without properness.**

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Convention split on Hausdorff in "compact"** — this is the single most important source variation in the whole subject. **Bourbaki** (and French tradition, and Engelking §3.1) define *compact = quasi-compact + Hausdorff*, reserving **quasi-compact** for the bare cover property; **algebraic geometry (EGA/Stacks)** follows Bourbaki. **Munkres, Willard, Kelley, Morris, Lee, nLab** do **not** build Hausdorff into compactness. Engelking likewise builds Hausdorff into **paracompact**. Any curriculum must fix this convention explicitly and say so.
- **Munkres §§26–29** (compact spaces, compact subspaces of ℝ, limit point compactness, local compactness) + **Ch. 5 §§37–38** (Tychonoff, Stone–Čech) + **Ch. 6 §§39–42** (local finiteness, Nagata–Smirnov, paracompactness, Smirnov) + **§45** (compactness in metric spaces). Munkres proves Tychonoff **twice**: once via the "maximal FIP family" (Zorn) in §37 and once via the Alexander subbase lemma in the exercises.
- **Willard Ch. 6** ("Compactness") is the fullest single-chapter treatment: compactness, countable/sequential compactness, compactification, local compactness, and the compactness–uniformity link. **Kelley Ch. 5** ("Compact spaces") proves Tychonoff via ultrafilters and is the historical source for **Tychonoff ⟺ AC**.
- **Engelking Ch. 3** is the reference inventory: §3.1 Compact, §3.2 Operations on compact spaces, §3.3 **Locally compact spaces and k-spaces**, §3.4 compact-open topology, §3.5 Compactifications, §3.6 **Čech–Stone compactification and Wallman extension**, §3.7 **Perfect mappings**, §3.8 Lindelöf, §3.9 Čech-complete, §3.10 Countably compact / pseudocompact / sequentially compact, §3.11 Realcompact. Nothing else covers perfect maps, realcompactness and k-spaces in one place.
- **Morris Ch. 7 (compactness, Heine–Borel), Ch. 10 (Zorn's lemma → Tychonoff → Stone–Čech)** is the best free source that spells out the choice-theoretic dependencies as part of the exposition.
- **Cambridge IB** covers only: open-cover compactness, [0,1], closed subsets of compact, compact subsets of Hausdorff are closed, compact subsets of ℝ, continuous images, EVT, product of two compacts, compact subsets of ℝⁿ, sequential compactness. That is the realistic *floor*.
- **nLab** is the place for the Kuratowski–Mrówka characterization, the choice-free vs choice-dependent bookkeeping, and CGHaus cartesian closedness.

## 6. CONVERGENCE BASICS (AND WHERE SEQUENCES FAIL)

*Full net/filter theory is the sibling agent's. This section covers sequences in topological spaces and the precise failure points that motivate nets/filters.*

### DEFINITIONS
- **Convergent sequence**: xₙ → x iff every nbhd of x contains xₙ for all large n. **Limit** (not unique in general).
- **Cluster (accumulation) point of a sequence**: every nbhd contains xₙ for infinitely many n.
- **Eventually / frequently in a set** — the vocabulary that generalizes verbatim to nets.
- **Sequential closure** [A]_seq = {limits of sequences from A}; **sequentially closed** (closed under sequential limits); **sequentially open** (complement sequentially closed).
- **Sequential space**: every sequentially open set is open (⟺ every sequentially closed set is closed).
- **Fréchet–Urysohn space**: for every A and every x ∈ cl A there is a sequence in A converging to x (⟺ hereditarily sequential).
- **Sequentially continuous** map; **sequentially compact**; **sequentially discrete** (every convergent sequence is eventually constant).
- **Sequential order** of a space: the least ordinal at which iterating the sequential closure stabilizes (≤ ω₁).
- **Sequential coreflection (sequentialization)**; the analogous **k-ification** for k-spaces.
- **US space (unique sequential limits)**: strictly between T1 and Hausdorff.

### THEOREMS
- **One-sided containment**: if xₙ ∈ A and xₙ → x then x ∈ cl A. Hence [A]_seq ⊆ cl A, **and the inclusion can be proper**.
- **Sequential closure is not idempotent** in general — iterating it transfinitely (up to ω₁) is required to reach the closure, which is exactly why the naive "closure = limits of sequences" intuition breaks.
- **Continuous ⟹ sequentially continuous**; the converse holds **iff the domain is a sequential space** — this is the defining property of sequential spaces.
- **Hierarchy** (all implications strict): metrizable ⟹ first countable ⟹ Fréchet–Urysohn ⟹ sequential ⟹ k-space (compactly generated).
- **First-countable payoffs**: x ∈ cl A ⟺ some sequence in A converges to x; f continuous at x ⟺ sequentially continuous at x; compact ⟹ sequentially compact (uses countable choice).
- **Franklin's theorem**: a space is sequential ⟺ it is a quotient of a metric space. (Quotients and disjoint sums of sequential spaces are sequential; subspaces are not.)
- **Uniqueness of limits**: Hausdorff ⟹ every sequence has at most one limit; the **converse is false** (US spaces). The correct converse needs *nets*: X is Hausdorff ⟺ every net has at most one limit.
- **Where sequences fail — the canonical failure list**:
  - **Cocountable topology on an uncountable set**: only eventually-constant sequences converge, so every subset is sequentially closed while the space is not discrete ⟹ **not sequential**; every map out of it is sequentially continuous but not every map is continuous; limits are non-unique (space is T1, not Hausdorff).
  - **[0, ω₁] (order topology)**: ω₁ ∈ cl[0,ω₁) but **no sequence from [0,ω₁) converges to ω₁**; compact but not first countable at ω₁, so closure is not sequentially detectable.
  - **[0, ω₁)**: sequentially compact and countably compact but **not compact**, and not Lindelöf — shows sequential compactness ⇏ compactness.
  - **βℕ**: ℕ is dense but no nontrivial sequence in ℕ converges in βℕ; βℕ is compact but **not sequentially compact** — shows compactness ⇏ sequential compactness.
  - **[0,1]^[0,1] and {0,1}^ℝ**: compact (Tychonoff) but not sequentially compact; sequences cannot detect the topology of uncountable products.
  - **Arens–Fort space**: every convergent sequence is eventually constant, so it is sequentially discrete but not discrete ⟹ not sequential and not first countable.
  - **Arens space S₂**: sequential but **not** Fréchet–Urysohn — separates the two neighbouring classes.
  - **ℝ^ℝ = the topology of pointwise convergence**: convergence of nets is exactly pointwise convergence, but the space is not sequential; sequential closure of the bounded functions etc. is strictly smaller than the closure.
  - **Box topology on ℝ^ω**: the map t ↦ (t,t,…) is sequentially continuous where relevant yet not continuous into the box, and the box topology is not first countable.
- **Product convergence**: in the product topology, a net (or sequence) converges ⟺ every coordinate converges — the product topology *is* the topology of pointwise convergence. In the **box** topology this fails badly.
- **Bridge to nets/filters (hand-off to sibling)**: the fixes are (i) cl A = set of limits of nets in A; (ii) f continuous ⟺ f preserves net convergence; (iii) X compact ⟺ every net has a convergent subnet ⟺ every ultrafilter converges; (iv) X Hausdorff ⟺ net limits are unique. Each of these is *false* with "sequence" in place of "net".

### SOURCE NOTES
- **Willard §4.1 is literally titled "Inadequacy of sequences"** — the canonical textbook justification for nets, and the best single reference for this section. Willard then does nets (§4.2) and filters (§4.3).
- **Kelley Ch. 2 "Moore–Smith Convergence"** puts convergence *second*, before products and quotients — a deliberate structural choice that makes the whole book net-based; Kelley coined/popularized "net" and "subnet".
- **Engelking §1.6** ("Convergence in topological spaces: nets and filters. Sequential and Fréchet spaces") covers the sequential/Fréchet–Urysohn distinction explicitly — most books omit it.
- **Munkres has no dedicated convergence chapter**: sequences appear inside §17 (limit points) and §21 (metric topology), and the failure of sequences is confined to exercises. This is the most-cited gap in the Munkres-only curriculum, and it is exactly why §21 has the "sequence lemma" (first-countable ⟹ closure detected by sequences) as a numbered result.
- **Morris Appendix 6 "Filters and Nets"** and **Bourbaki I §6–§7** are filter-first; Bourbaki never uses nets at all, which is a genuine terminological fork (filter/ultrafilter vs net/subnet) that any curriculum must declare.
- **Wikipedia "Sequential space"** gives the hierarchy and Franklin's theorem cleanly; note that its summary of the cocountable topology is misleading — the cocountable topology on an uncountable set is **not** sequential.

## RESULTS GOOD CURRICULA INCLUDE THAT ARE EASY TO MISS

**Foundations / closure calculus**
1. **Kuratowski closure axioms ↔ topologies** as a bijection, plus the dual **interior axiomatization** and **Hausdorff's neighbourhood axiomatization** — three equivalent ways to define a topology. Munkres omits all three.
2. **Kuratowski's 14-set closure-complement theorem**, with the explicit witness (0,1) ∪ (1,2) ∪ {3} ∪ ([4,5]∩ℚ) in ℝ.
3. The **lattice of topologies on a set** is complete: intersections of topologies are topologies, unions are not; the join is the topology *generated by* the union.
4. **int(A∩B)=int A ∩ int B but int(A∪B) ⊋ int A ∪ int B**, and both fail for infinite index sets — the asymmetry students consistently get backwards.
5. **Regular open sets form a complete Boolean algebra** (with A∨B = int cl(A∪B)); this is the bridge to forcing and to Stone duality.
6. **cl_A(S) = A ∩ cl_X(S) but int_A(S) can strictly contain A ∩ int_X(S)** — closure localizes, interior does not.
7. The **specialization preorder** and the correspondence **Alexandrov topologies ↔ preorders** (hence finite spaces = finite preorders).

**Bases, comparison, maps**
8. **Munkres's Lemma 13.3 basis comparison criterion** — the only practical way to compare two topologies given by bases.
9. **Continuity need only be checked on a subbasis** — this alone reduces most product/order-topology continuity proofs to one line.
10. **Pasting lemma for locally finite closed covers** (not merely two closed pieces), with the counterexample showing local finiteness is essential.
11. **Quotient maps are neither open nor closed in general**, yet π₁: ℝ²→ℝ is an open quotient that is not closed — plus the composition/cancellation lemmas for quotient maps.
12. **Whitehead's theorem**: (quotient map) × id_{locally compact Hausdorff} is a quotient map — **and the product of two quotient maps need not be a quotient map** (Dowker). This is the reason CGHaus exists.
13. **Compact-to-Hausdorff trick**: any continuous surjection from compact onto Hausdorff is a closed map, hence a quotient map — the single most-used tool for identifying quotient constructions (torus, Klein bottle, ℝPⁿ) with familiar spaces.
14. **Initial/final topology language and their transitivity**, with subspace/product as initial and quotient/sum as final — Bourbaki's organizing idea, almost always skipped in Anglophone first courses.
15. **The embedding lemma / evaluation map** (separating points + separating points from closed sets ⟹ embedding into a product) — the shared engine of Tychonoff-cube embedding and Urysohn metrization.
16. **Top's adjoint triple** discrete ⊣ forgetful ⊣ indiscrete, which explains why underlying sets of limits and colimits are the set-theoretic ones.

**Products, orders, groups**
17. **Box vs product**: f(t)=(t,t,t,…): ℝ → ℝ^ω continuous into the product, discontinuous into the box — and **cl(∏Aᵢ)=∏cl Aᵢ in *both*** topologies (the closure formula is not what distinguishes them).
18. **The order topology on a subset ≠ the subspace topology in general**, with agreement on convex subsets — Munkres warns explicitly; almost everyone else buries it.
19. **LOTS are hereditarily normal (completely normal), indeed monotonically normal** — a strong free theorem about ordered spaces.
20. **Hewitt–Marczewski–Pondiczery**: products of ≤ 𝔠 separable spaces are separable — separability is vastly more productive than first/second countability.
21. **Topological groups: T0 ⟹ Tychonoff, plus homogeneity and "topology determined by nbhds of e"** — three cheap, high-yield theorems most first courses never state.
22. **Inverse limits of nonempty compact Hausdorff spaces are nonempty** — the fact that makes profinite/Stone theory work.

**Connectedness**
23. **Quasicomponents**, their containment of components, and the theorems that they coincide in compact Hausdorff (Šura-Bura) and in locally connected spaces — with an explicit strict example.
24. **Locally connected ⟺ components of open sets are open**, and the pointwise gap between "connected im kleinen at x" and "locally connected at x".
25. **Connected + locally path-connected ⟹ path-connected** — the actual reason open connected subsets of ℝⁿ are path-connected.
26. **Arbitrary products of connected spaces are connected**, and the finite-support-density proof technique (plus its use of AC).
27. **IVT stated topologically for order topologies**, not just for ℝ (Munkres 24.3).
28. **Cut-point arguments as homeomorphism invariants**: (0,1) ≇ [0,1) ≇ [0,1], S¹ ≇ [0,1], ℝ ≇ ℝⁿ.
29. **Sierpiński's theorem**: a continuum is not a countable disjoint union of ≥2 nonempty closed sets.
30. **Boundary bumping lemma** for continua, and the coincidence of components with intersections of clopen sets in compact Hausdorff spaces.
31. **Totally disconnected ≠ totally separated ≠ zero-dimensional in general** (Erdős space, Knaster–Kuratowski fan), but all three coincide for locally compact Hausdorff — giving Stone/profinite spaces.
32. **Chain-connected ⟺ connected for compact metric spaces** (ℚ is well-chained but disconnected).

**Compactness**
33. **Kuratowski–Mrówka**: X compact ⟺ X×Y → Y is closed for all Y — a choice-free definition of compactness that generalizes to topos theory.
34. **Choice bookkeeping**: Tychonoff ⟺ AC; Tychonoff for Hausdorff factors ⟺ BPI/ultrafilter lemma; Alexander subbase from Zorn/BPI; "every metric space is paracompact" is not a ZF theorem; "second countable ⟹ Lindelöf" and the metric compactness equivalences need countable/dependent choice; but the tube lemma, finite Tychonoff, and Heine–Borel for [a,b] are choice-free.
35. **Alexander subbase lemma** as a working tool (it turns Tychonoff into a short proof and is reusable for order topologies).
36. **Compact ⇏ sequentially compact** ([0,1]^[0,1], {0,1}^ℝ, βℕ) **and sequentially compact ⇏ compact** ([0,ω₁)) — genuinely independent notions.
37. **Lindelöf + countably compact = compact**, and **countably compact ⟺ limit point compact for T1** (T1 is needed).
38. **Local compactness has five inequivalent definitions**, coinciding under Hausdorff; and **a subspace of a LCH space is locally compact ⟺ it is locally closed**.
39. **LCH ⟹ completely regular** and **LCH ⟹ Baire** — the second form of the Baire category theorem, routinely omitted in favour of the complete-metric form only.
40. **One-point compactification is the minimum Hausdorff compactification; βX is the maximum** — framing the compactification lattice, not just the two endpoints.
41. **Proper = universally closed (Bourbaki) vs preimage-of-compact-is-compact**, and exactly when they agree (LCH or compactly generated Hausdorff codomain).
42. **Perfect maps** and their preservation/reflection table (Engelking §3.7) — the right notion for transporting properties both ways.
43. **k-spaces / compactly generated spaces**, k-ification, and the cartesian closedness of CGHaus — the standard repair for Top's failure to be cartesian closed.
44. **Lindelöf and paracompactness are not productive**, with the Sorgenfrey plane as the single counterexample settling both plus non-normality.
45. **σ-compact + locally compact ⟹ exhaustion by compacts with Kₙ ⊆ int Kₙ₊₁** — the technical lemma every manifold-theory argument silently uses.

**Convergence**
46. **Sequential closure is not idempotent**; the sequential order of a space; the transfinite iteration to ω₁.
47. **Franklin's theorem**: sequential spaces = quotients of metric spaces.
48. **Unique sequential limits does not imply Hausdorff** (US spaces); the correct characterization requires nets.
49. **The full failure list** (cocountable topology, [0,ω₁], βℕ, [0,1]^[0,1], Arens–Fort, Arens space S₂) taught as a *set* rather than a single anecdote — it is the only honest motivation for nets and filters.
50. **first countable ⟹ Fréchet–Urysohn ⟹ sequential ⟹ k-space**, all strict, with a witness for each strictness — the hierarchy that ties §6 to §5's k-spaces.

**Curricular bookkeeping**
51. **Engelking's invariance tables** ("Invariants of operations"; "Invariants and inverse invariants of mappings") — an explicit hereditary / weakly hereditary / productive / divisible matrix. Building this table for the library is high value and no other standard source supplies it ready-made.
52. **Declare the Hausdorff-in-compactness convention up front** (Bourbaki/Engelking bake it in; Munkres/Willard/Kelley/nLab do not), and likewise the neighbourhood convention (open-only in Munkres/Lee vs general in Willard/Kelley/Engelking/Bourbaki), and net-vs-filter (Kelley/Willard vs Bourbaki). Every cross-source theorem statement in the library will be ambiguous otherwise.
