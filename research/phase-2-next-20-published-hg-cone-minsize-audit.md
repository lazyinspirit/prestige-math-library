## Ultralimit and represented-side clauses

The bounded-real ultralimit proof deterministically bisects a bounded interval
according to the supplied ultrafilter, proves uniqueness by disjoint
neighbourhoods, and checks sums, scalar multiples, products, absolute values,
order and large-set changes. AC is used only to extend the cofinite filter to a
free ultrafilter; the calculus for a supplied ultrafilter constructs no further
choice. Its reciprocal supplement uses an eventual lower bound for a non-null
rational Cauchy sequence and gives a valid Cauchy inverse.

The rescaled-distance lemma then passes the metric identities to bounded real
ultralimits, proves zero-distance transitivity and representative independence,
and treats sequences bounded only on a large set by replacing other coordinates
with basepoints. This gives the required quotient metric without a hidden
separation assumption.

For a family of oriented geodesic sides meeting a bounded rescaled region, the
side-limit lemma chooses bounded origins under the stated AC, takes finite or
infinite ultralimits of its two endpoint parameters, and clamps every fixed
finite parameter to the current side. Exact distance preservation gives an
isometric interval, ray or line. Conversely, every admissible represented side
point has a bounded parameter sequence and equals its clamped limit point.
The final pairwise side choice proves geodesicity of the whole cone.

## Tree and sublinear-minsize clauses

The triangle-extrema lemma obtains minima and maxima without countable choice
by a fixed enumeration of finite dyadic grids and deterministic nested boxes.
The unique-arc, tripod and zero-slim equivalences follow from closed initial
side intersections. Projection to a segment, ray or line is attained and
unique; this gives the stated common-tail and equal-line consequences for
finite Hausdorff distance.

The tree-cone two-side lemma argues by violating side pairs, bases at a point
of maximal Hausdorff separation and scales by that separation. The two
represented limit sides have finite Hausdorff distance but retain a point at
distance one. Their endpoints give exactly the segment, common-origin-ray and
line cases, each impossible in a real tree. All countable selections are under
the explicit AC hypothesis.

The all-tree-cones theorem first extends that bound to short opposite endpoints.
From triangles of unbounded slimness it takes a maximally distant point and
splits according to whether the second-side distance ratio has finite or
infinite ultralimit. In the finite case the three pairs of half-sides make a
finite cone tripod contradicting the retained distance-one gap. In the infinite
case the third side escapes; maximality keeps an entire surviving negative
half-ray within distance one of the other limit side, contradicting the real-
tree ray/line tail rules. The empty space is separated explicitly.

The sublinear side-identification lemma uses the bound
`m_X(P)<=epsilon P` above a threshold and `m_X(P)<=P_0` below it, so every
bounded-scale represented triangle has scaled minsize tending to zero.
Minimizing triples coalesce; distance additivity forces an arbitrary point of
any cone segment onto the prescribed side limit. Isometric parameterization
then proves equality and uniqueness. The sublinear-hyperbolicity theorem uses
this for every cone under one fixed free ultrafilter, proves each cone triangle
is a tripod, and invokes the preceding all-tree-cones result. Its AC uses are
stated.

Finally, the coarse square-root lemma correctly retains the edge loss in the
third-side barrier `h=m/2-r`. The affine crossing estimate gives
`h^2<=Nr^2`; the nonpositive-barrier case and zero data are separate. Moving
from finite marked sets to continuous sides costs exactly `2e` by a limiting
Hausdorff argument.
