## Definition

Assume AC. The input is either (a) a based $r$-component string-link diagram
with its endpoint collars and product-tube parallels equal to the diagram's
blackboard push-offs, or (b) a based finite regular
diagram of a closed $r$-component labeled oriented link with chosen product
tubes, one initial strand on each component, and a common basepoint and access
paths. The finite input records its compatible common-upper-collar reference accesses. In the compatible branch the actual accesses are these, up to a common base-path change, and the parallels are the blackboard parallels already specified. In the arbitrary-choice branch it additionally records finite words in the compatible strand meridians for each access difference $g_e$, the parallel endpoint differences $h_{0,j},h_{1,j}$, and its relative meridian winding $k_j$. These are word certificates for the chosen paths, part of the supplied based-word data; no evaluation of an unencoded smooth path is claimed. In closed-link mode the preferred longitude uses its own recorded access $h_j$, which may differ from the initial-meridian access $g_j$. Arbitrary parallels are allowed in this branch, with the recorded $k_j$. The input also includes a maximum index length $N\geq2$. The output is
the integer coefficients $\mu_\tau(I)$ in string-link mode, or the based
coefficients $\mu_L^b(I)$, their lower indeterminacy data $\Delta_I^b$, and
residues $\bar\mu_L^b(I)$ in closed-link mode, for every index sequence $I$
with $2\leq |I|\leq N$.

Set $d=N-1$ and work in the truncated noncommutative ring
$$R_d=\mathbb Z\langle X_1,\ldots,X_r\rangle/\mathfrak m^{d+1},$$
where $\mathfrak m$ is the ideal generated by the letters. Carry out the
following finite procedure.

1. Verify the finite crossing, orientation, over/under, and component data. If
   a closed-link component has no underpassing point, insert a small type-I
   curl on that component in a disk meeting no other component and record the
   resulting based diagram. Give every strand $a$ the component label $j(a)$.

2. First compute in the compatible reference root symbols $x_j$, irrespective of the actual access choices. For every strand $a$, initialize its meridian series by
   $Y_a^{(1)}=1+X_{j(a)}$. For each degree $\ell=2,\ldots,d$, copy every
   $Y_a^{(\ell-1)}$ into the constant and lower-degree part of
   $Y_a^{(\ell)}$, with new degree-$\ell$ terms initially zero, and reset each
   chosen root strand to $1+X_j$. Then do one pass over each component in its
   oriented order. In closed-link mode start at the chosen initial strand and
   omit the final undercrossing back to that strand; in string-link mode start
   at the bottom endpoint and use every undercrossing through to the top
   endpoint. At an undercrossing with over-strand $o$ and source and target
   under-arcs $s,t$, use the already available $Y_o^{(\ell-1)}$ and the
   current $Y_s^{(\ell)}$ to set
   $$Y_t^{(\ell)}=\operatorname{trunc}_{\ell}\bigl((Y_o^{(\ell-1)})^{-1} Y_s^{(\ell)}Y_o^{(\ell-1)}\bigr)$$
   when the traversal is source-to-target, and set
   $$Y_s^{(\ell)}=\operatorname{trunc}_{\ell}\bigl(Y_o^{(\ell-1)} Y_t^{(\ell)}(Y_o^{(\ell-1)})^{-1}\bigr)$$
   when it is target-to-source. Here $\operatorname{trunc}_{\ell}$ discards
   monomials of degree greater than $\ell$, and every inverse is the finite
   geometric series in the nilpotent ideal of $R_d$.

3. For each component $j$, traverse its undercrossings in order and form
   $W_j=\prod_c (Y_{o(c)}^{(d)})^{\epsilon_c}$, where the factors are in
   traversal order and $\epsilon_c=+1$ for source-to-target and $-1$ for
   target-to-source. In string-link mode set $\Lambda_j=W_j$. In closed-link
   mode set
   $$\Lambda_j=(1+X_j)^{-\operatorname{wr}_j(D)}W_j,$$
   where $\operatorname{wr}_j(D)$ is the sum of the determinant signs at
   self-crossings of component $j$.

4. In the arbitrary-choice branch evaluate each supplied transport word by the strand series from step 2. Write $G_e$ for the resulting unit series, and $H_{0,j},H_{1,j}$ for the endpoint transport series. In string-link mode replace the compatible $\Lambda_j=W_j$ by
   $$H_{0,j}(1+X_j)^{k_j}W_jH_{1,j}^{-1}.$$
   In closed-link mode replace the compatible preferred series by
   $$H_j(1+X_j)^{-\operatorname{wr}_j(D)}W_jH_j^{-1},$$
   where $H_j=H_{0,j}=H_{1,j}$ is the actual longitude access difference, independently of the initial-meridian difference $G_j$. The arbitrary parallel winding cancels in the preferred correction. All powers and inverses are finite truncated operations. To express this result in the actual component-meridian symbols, form
   $$G_j(1+X_j)G_j^{-1}=1+X_j+R_j(X),\qquad \deg R_j\ge2.$$
   With new letters $V_j$, initialize $Z_j^{(1)}=V_j$ and iterate
   $$Z_j^{(s+1)}=\operatorname{trunc}_d\bigl(V_j-R_j(Z^{(s)})\bigr),\qquad 1\le s<d.$$
   Substitute $X_j=Z_j^{(d)}(V)$ in the corrected longitude series. This is its expansion in the chosen actual meridians. For the compatible branch this step is the identity. Rename the final letters $V_j$ as $X_j$.

5. For every $I=(i_1,\ldots,i_k,j)$ with $1\leq k\leq d$, read the coefficient
   of $X_{i_1}\cdots X_{i_k}$ in $\Lambda_j$. In string-link mode return this
   integer. In closed-link mode, also compute the gcd of the coefficients for
   all proper subsequences of cyclic permutations of $I$ of lengths at least
   two, with empty gcd equal to zero, and return the corresponding residue
   class.

All scans, including the supplied transport words and the $d-1$ inverse-substitution iterations, are over finite crossing, strand, degree, and index sets. The
procedure makes no polynomial-time claim. If a finite-input validation fails,
return `invalid-input`.

## Remarks

The per-degree passes use over-strand series only through degree $\ell-1$:
their degree-$\ell$ terms cancel against the constant term of the conjugated
meridian. The output records the based residue data; its independence of
closed-link basing is the separate theorem on Milnor invariants.
