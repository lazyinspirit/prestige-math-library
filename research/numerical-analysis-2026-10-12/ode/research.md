## One-step error contracts

Reuse P-NUM-ODE-01/02 for the generic route. On a fixed interval and compact tube, the step defect must be uniform in the starting time and exact state, and the numerical step map must have a stated Lipschitz bound in the same norm. The error recursion is

$$e_{n+1}\le (1+Lh)e_n+Ch^{p+1},$$

and iteration on $O(h^{-1})$ steps gives a global $O(h^p)$ error on that fixed interval. Hairer Ch. III proves the one-step version by propagating each local defect through the step-map stability bound and summing; FNC Ch. 6 gives the scalar recurrence proof. For explicit Euler, bounded first derivatives of $f$ on the tube yield the local $O(h^2)$ defect and the stability factor $1+Lh$. For a Runge–Kutta method, its name is insufficient: state its order conditions and the derivatives needed for a uniform Taylor remainder. The discrete Grönwall estimate is an authoring obligation unless a separate local supplier is added. A state error alone does not control an augmented log-density or adjoint variable; those need their own defects and stability estimates.

## Linear multistep methods

For a fixed-step $k$-step formula, define the first and second generating polynomials $\rho$ and $\sigma$, then define local truncation error by inserting the exact IVP solution into the entire recurrence. Order $p$ means residual per unit step $O(h^p)$, equivalently a one-step recurrence defect $O(h^{p+1})$. Consistency is order at least one. Under the standard coefficient convention this requires $\rho(1)=0$ and $\rho'(1)=\sigma(1)$; higher orders come from matching further Taylor moments.

The root condition is separate: every root $\zeta$ of $\rho$ satisfies $|\zeta|\le1$, and each root on the unit circle is simple. Repeated roots strictly inside the unit disk are allowed; their polynomial-in-$n$ factors remain bounded after multiplication by $|\zeta|^n$. A repeated unit root produces unbounded parasitic growth. Hairer Ch. III §III.7 derives the root test from the homogeneous difference recurrence. FNC Ch. 6 states the same criterion, but its printed proof explicitly omits repeated roots; that proof must be completed locally rather than copied as complete.

For the sufficiency proof on an equally spaced grid, assume a uniform local defect $O(h^{p+1})$, the root condition, a Lipschitz field on a fixed solution tube, existence of each implicit step on the selected branch, and starting values with error $O(h^p)$. Rewrite the recurrence as a first-order companion system. The root condition gives uniformly bounded powers of its zero-dynamics matrix; variation of constants then sums the local residuals and yields a global $O(h^p)$ error through a fixed finite time. Hairer Ch. III §III.8 carries this proof for equidistant steps and explicitly says the variable-step case is more technical. The full Dahlquist equivalence statement needs the converse as well: prove that convergence for the declared class forces consistency and the root condition. Do not replace this chain by a one-step Grönwall theorem or call the root test a consistency condition.

## Stiffness and stability regions

On the test equation $y'=\lambda y$ with $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\le0$, a one-step method produces $y_{n+1}=R(z)y_n$, $z=h\lambda$. Its stability region is the set where the iterates remain bounded; A-stability means that the full closed left half-plane is included. For multistep methods, stability is instead tested by the roots of $\rho(\zeta)-z\sigma(\zeta)$, with the same unit-circle simplicity condition. Hairer distinguishes method order from this stability geometry: explicit Euler is stable only on its bounded disk, backward Euler is A-stable, and BDF methods cover large portions of the left half-plane (with the stated order-dependent limits). These claims concern the scalar test equation, not a universal stiffness definition or an accuracy guarantee for every system.

L-stability is not named in the read Hairer sections. If included, define it for a rational one-step stability function as A-stability together with $R(z)\to0$ as $|z|\to\infty$ in the left half-plane; prove examples from their stability functions (backward Euler is L-stable, the trapezoidal rule is A-stable but not L-stable). Do not infer this limit from A-stability alone. The FNC chapter uses “stiff” operationally for cases where implicit methods can take useful larger steps, and explicitly says a complete mathematical description is deferred to another chapter. Treat stiffness as a model-dependent motivation, not as a theorem with a universal scalar criterion.

## Adaptivity and estimators

Hairer Ch. III and FNC Ch. 6 both describe embedded Runge–Kutta pairs. If methods of orders $p$ and $p+1$ share stages, their output difference estimates the local error of the lower-order value when its leading defect is present. The proposed step multiplier follows the asymptotic model $E(h)\approx Ch^{p+1}$, giving $q\approx(\mathrm{Tol}/E)^{1/(p+1)}$ for a local target; a global-error-per-step argument gives exponent $1/p$. FNC explicitly calls this a speculation/guess and reports that recommendations differ. The estimate can fail when its leading coefficient cancels or the asymptotic regime is not reached. Acceptance/rejection, safety factors, norm scaling, minimum/maximum steps, and stiffness detection are algorithm choices. They do not certify a global error bound. Any adaptive algorithm definition should expose its controller inputs and rejection/update rules; a theorem about global adaptive accuracy needs new mesh-regularity and estimator-reliability hypotheses and proof.

## BVPs, shooting, and nonlinear collocation

FNC Ch. 10 demonstrates linear finite-difference/collocation BVPs and gives the algebraic identity $e_h=A_h^{-1}\tau_h$. It says the solution error follows the truncation-error rate when $\|A_h^{-1}\|$ is uniformly bounded, but expressly omits the stability proof and its concrete BVP hypotheses. A local theorem must fix one operator, grid family, norm, boundary rows, consistency residual (including the boundary conditions), and a uniform inverse or maximum-principle estimate. Then the error proof is the residual identity plus that stability bound. An interior stencil order without boundary consistency is insufficient.

Shooting turns unknown initial data into a nonlinear residual evaluated through an IVP solution. Its Jacobian contains the IVP sensitivity map, so root conditioning and IVP discretization error both enter; the FNC source gives an unstable shooting example and no general convergence theorem. Nonlinear collocation similarly produces a finite-dimensional residual system; the source supplies an algorithm and examples, not convergence of the quasi-Newton iteration or mesh convergence. Keep three errors separate: continuous BVP conditioning, discretization error, and inexact nonlinear-solver error. A claimed nonlinear BVP convergence theorem needs a locally unique solution branch, consistent discretization, a uniformly controlled discrete Jacobian/inverse, and a solver error small enough relative to the discretization defect.

## Geometric integration

Hairer’s lectures 1–2 establish the canonical two-form, prove that a Hamiltonian flow is symplectic using the variational equation, and derive symplectic Euler and Störmer–Verlet. Störmer–Verlet is the composition of two symplectic Euler updates and is symmetric, which gives order two. For Runge–Kutta methods, the coefficient identity $b_i a_{ij}+b_j a_{ji}=b_i b_j$ implies preservation of quadratic first integrals and hence symplecticity. Use the existing Hamiltonian-flow supplier instead of restating its continuous conclusion.

Symplecticity is preservation of the two-form; it does not mean that the numerical map exactly preserves the original Hamiltonian. Hairer lecture 3 constructs a formal modified equation (the series may diverge, so truncate it), proves that a symplectic method has a locally Hamiltonian modified equation, and proves a finite-order near-energy theorem. Its checked hypotheses are smooth $H$, a symplectic method with a globally defined modified Hamiltonian on $U$, and a numerical orbit contained in a compact $K\subset U$. For any fixed truncation order $N$, the modified energy changes by $O(t h^N)$ and the original $H$ by $O(h^r)$ for order-$r$ methods over $t=nh\le C h^{-N+r}$. The source’s analytic/exponential refinement is noted but not needed for the scaffold contract. It requires analyticity and its own truncation argument; do not promote it from the short remark to an independently proved local supplier.
