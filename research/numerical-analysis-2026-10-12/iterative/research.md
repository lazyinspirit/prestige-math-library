## Source-backed coverage and proof routes

For a stationary splitting $A=M-N$ with $M$ invertible, the error obeys $e_{j+1}=Ge_j$ with $G=M^{-1}N$. The existing local theorem has the strong quantifier: convergence to the unique solution for every right-hand side and every start is equivalent to $\rho(G)<1$. It does not assert that one fixed initial error must diverge whenever $\rho(G)\ge1$; that error may miss an unstable invariant subspace. Saad proves the all-start criterion and norm sufficient condition and explains the asymptotic rate from the Jordan form. For a defective dominant eigenvalue, finite-step bounds can carry a polynomial factor in $j$; the spectral radius is an asymptotic root rate, not a uniform one-step contraction in every norm. Jacobi, Gauss–Seidel, SOR, and Richardson are standard concrete splittings. Strict or irreducible diagonal dominance supplies sufficient convergence for Jacobi/Gauss–Seidel; for symmetric matrices with positive diagonal, SOR converges for all starts exactly when $A$ is positive definite and $0<\omega<2$. Claims about “optimal” relaxation parameters require the additional ordering/model hypotheses stated by the source.

The exact-arithmetic Krylov route is already local. A Krylov iterate is a polynomial in $A$ applied to the initial residual. Arnoldi builds an orthonormal basis and a Hessenberg factorization until exact breakdown, which is equivalent to Krylov invariance. Unrestarted GMRES minimizes the Euclidean residual over $x_0+K_j(A,r_0)$ and terminates by the relative grade for invertible $A$. Restarted GMRES preserves cyclewise minimization but discards the full-space termination guarantee. For Hermitian positive-definite $A$, CG minimizes the energy error and has the Chebyshev bound

$$\|e_j\|_A\le 2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_2(A)}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_2(A)}+1}\right)^j\|e_0\|_A.$$

MINRES is the Euclidean-residual minimizer over the Hermitian Krylov affine space and does not require positive definiteness. The exact source proof route for a new sparse-cost result is to count the sparse matrix–vector product and vector kernels separately from the Krylov convergence theorem: a CSR matrix–vector product costs $O(n+\operatorname{nnz}(A))$, whereas Arnoldi orthogonalization through $j$ vectors costs $O(jn)$ per step and full-basis storage costs $O(jn)$. For a square sparse pattern with at least one stored entry per row, $n\le\operatorname{nnz}(A)$, so an $m$-step unrestarted implementation costs $O(m\operatorname{nnz}(A)+m^2n)$ arithmetic and $O(mn)$ basis storage, apart from the small least-squares solve. This is a complexity claim about the implementation, not a convergence bound; the number of iterations still depends on the operator, right-hand side, stopping rule, and preconditioner.

Left and right preconditioning preserve the solution set but measure different residuals. Left-preconditioned GMRES minimizes $\|M^{-1}(b-Ax)\|_2$; right-preconditioned GMRES minimizes the physical residual $\|b-Ax\|_2$ while searching the transformed space. Their transformed operators share a spectrum, which alone does not force equal convergence histories for nonnormal matrices. SPD-preconditioned CG is CG on the symmetric transformed matrix $C^{-1}AC^{-*}$, so its bound uses that transformed matrix's spectral condition number. A preconditioner has no generic monotonic improvement guarantee; the existing published counterexample explicitly rules out such a claim. Flexible GMRES is required when the preconditioning operation changes between steps.

For eigenvalue computations, the local statements already give (i) power iteration under diagonalizability, a simple strictly dominant eigenvalue, and a nonzero starting component, with projective ratio rate $|\lambda_2/\lambda_1|$; (ii) shifted inverse iteration when the shift is not an eigenvalue and one simple eigenvalue is uniquely closest; (iii) local cubic Rayleigh-quotient iteration for Hermitian matrices near a simple eigenvector; and (iv) subspace iteration under a strict spectral gap and an invertible initial leading-eigenspace block. The Cornell notes additionally derive orthogonal iteration/QR, Hessenberg QR costs, shifted and double-shifted steps, deflation, and Lanczos Ritz residuals. In floating point, Lanczos orthogonality can be lost near small recurrence residuals, producing duplicate or “ghost” Ritz values; reorthogonalization controls this. A useful missing proved result is a local Lanczos extremal-Ritz convergence bound with its starting-angle and spectral-gap hypotheses, using the already-published Lanczos/Ritz suppliers.

For an $m\times n$ sparse matrix in CSR, values and column indices take $O(\operatorname{nnz}(A))$ storage plus $O(m)$ row pointers, and a matrix–vector product costs $O(m+\operatorname{nnz}(A))$ operations. For square patterns with at least one stored entry per row, this simplifies to $O(\operatorname{nnz}(A))$. Saad's sparse-matrix chapter explains why graph ordering controls fill in direct factorization; fill and factorization cost are graph-dependent and must not be stated as a universal function of dimension alone. Existing local content includes an example showing that sparse Gaussian elimination can create fill, but not a general fill-complexity theorem. If the scaffold needs a complexity claim, use a fixed mesh family and ordering with its hypotheses stated.

Geometric multigrid rests on two complementary facts: a smoother damps error components that oscillate on the fine mesh, while interpolation/restriction carry smooth components to a coarse problem where they become resolvable. Let $P$ prolong coarse vectors, $R$ restrict fine residuals, and define the Galerkin operator $A_H=RA_hP$. The coarse correction

$$C_h=I-P A_H^{-1}R A_h$$

is an $A_h$-orthogonal projector onto the complement of $\operatorname{Ran}(P)$ when $A_h$ is SPD and the transfer/Galerkin identities hold. Saad's two-grid theorem supplies a precise proof route: assume the smoothing estimate $\|S_he\|_{A_h}^2\le\|e\|_{A_h}^2-\alpha\|A_he\|_{D^{-1}}^2$ and approximation estimate $\min_{v_H}\|e-Pv_H\|_D^2\le\beta\|e\|_{A_h}^2$, with constants $0<\alpha\le\beta$ uniform over mesh levels. Then the one-sided two-grid error operator has $A_h$-norm at most $\sqrt{1-\alpha/\beta}<1$. For a symmetric pre/post cycle, derive the corresponding bound from the $A_h$-adjoint smoother rather than silently reusing the one-sided formula. Mesh-independent convergence requires uniform constants; naming an iteration “multigrid” does not establish them.

For work estimates, if each level costs $O(N)$ and coarsening reduces the unknown count by $b=2^d$, then a cycle with $\gamma$ recursive visits obeys $T(N)=cN+\gamma T(N/b)$. The recursion gives $T(N)=\Theta(N)$ for $\gamma<b$, $\Theta(N\log N)$ for $\gamma=b$, and $\Theta(N^{\log_b\gamma})$ for $\gamma>b$. This is separate from convergence. FMG's discretization-accuracy theorem additionally assumes an inter-level approximation bound, a uniform multigrid contraction, and bounded interpolation; Saad's theorem states the exact constants and condition $c_2\xi^\mu<1$. AMG defines coarsening and interpolation from the matrix and can be connected to the same Galerkin proof, but an arbitrary algebraic coarsening does not automatically satisfy the approximation property.

Randomized range approximation has no existing local algorithm or theorem. HMT's fully read source supplies the Gaussian range-finder theorem and detailed derivations, but some underlying Gaussian matrix estimates are stated or cited rather than proved there. Given a fixed real $m\times n$ matrix $A$, target rank $k\ge2$, oversampling $p\ge2$, and $k+p\le\min(m,n)$, draw $\Omega$ with independent $N(0,1)$ entries, set $Y=A\Omega$, and take an orthonormal basis $Q$ for $\operatorname{Ran}(Y)$. The expected spectral error satisfies

$$\mathbb E\|A-QQ^*A\|_2\le\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{k}{p-1}}\right)\sigma_{k+1}+\frac{e\sqrt{k+p}}{p}\left(\sum_{j>k}\sigma_j^2\right)^{1/2}.$$

The irreducible baseline is $\sigma_{k+1}$ by the local Eckart–Young theorem. For $p\ge4$, HMT gives explicit high-probability bounds (Theorem 10.8 and Corollary 10.9), with failure probabilities $5t^{-p}+e^{-u^2/2}$ in the parameterized form and $6p^{-p}$ in one simplified form. These are probabilities over the independent Gaussian test matrix; they are not deterministic guarantees for every draw. HMT derives the probabilistic bounds by composing the deterministic SVD-partition bound, independence of $\Omega_1$ and $\Omega_2$, and Gaussian pseudoinverse-moment, pseudoinverse-tail, spectral-norm, and concentration estimates. The source states or cites some of those Gaussian estimates rather than proving them; a local proof must supply those lemmas before treating the probabilistic theorem as self-contained. The power scheme samples $B=(A A^*)^qA$ and orthonormalizes between applications in floating point; its expected error bound follows from the same Gaussian theorem and a power inequality. HMT explicitly warns that the raw power formula can erase small singular modes through roundoff.

Claims for SRFT sampling should remain outside the initial proved scope unless their local proof is supplied: HMT states the geometry-preservation theorem and uses it, but explicitly omits its proof. The same caution applies to the a-posteriori Gaussian error estimator, whose cited proof is outside HMT. The deterministic range-finder bound and power inequality have complete proofs; the main probabilistic bounds have detailed derivations but require local proofs of the listed Gaussian lemmas. Numerical experiments in the paper are empirical evidence, not proof.

## Exact arithmetic and finite precision

Exact algebraic identities, convergence assumptions, and probabilistic error bounds are separate from floating-point behavior. The local CG remark states that residual orthogonality, $A$-conjugacy, and finite termination can fail in floating point. The read lecture notes and Saad chapter explain loss of Lanczos/Arnoldi orthogonality and the role of reorthogonalization. Use the local dot-product and QR stability suppliers when an explicit floating-point result is needed. For randomized power iterations, the source's exact-arithmetic theorem does not imply numerical accuracy unless the basis is reorthogonalized between applications of $A$ and $A^*$.

## Open-problem status

There are no proposed open-problem claims in this packet. Multigrid convergence, Gaussian range-finder bounds, Krylov termination, and Lanczos convergence are established results with source-backed proof routes. Missing local proofs or authorship are prerequisite gaps, not evidence that these statements are unresolved.
