Line bundles
Let X be a complex manifold. A line bundle π : L →X is a 1-dimensional
holomorphic vector bundle.
This means there exists a collection of trivializations of L over charts Ui
on X, say Li ∼= Ui×C. Viewing L in two diﬀerent charts, we obtain clutching
data gij : Ui ∩Uj →C∗such that (x, yj) ∈Lj is equivalent to (x, yi) ∈Li iﬀ
gij(x)yj = yi. This data satisﬁes the cocycle condition gijgjk = gik.
Holomorphic and meromorphic sections. In terms of charts, a holo-
morphic section s : X →L is encoded by holomorphic functions si = yi◦s(x)
on Ui, such that
si(x) = gij(x)sj(x).
These sections form a sheaf OL.
A meromorphic section is given by a collection of si ∈M(Ui) satisfying
the coboundary condition above. Alternatively, a meromorphic section is
given locally by the product fs of a holomorphic section s and a meromor-
phic function f.
This means that, technically speaking, a meromorphic section is not
a section at all, since it does not take values in L at its poles.
On the
other hand the sheaf of meromorphic sections ML is well–deﬁned and locally
isomorphic to the sheaf M.
Examples. Two basic examples of line bundles, on any complex manifold
M, are the trivial bundle X × C, and the canonical bundle ∧n T∗X.
