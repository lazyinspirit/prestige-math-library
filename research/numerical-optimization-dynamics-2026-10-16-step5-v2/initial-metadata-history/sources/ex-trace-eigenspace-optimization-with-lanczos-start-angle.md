## Example

Let $A=\operatorname{diag}(5,2,0)$ and minimize
$f(x)=-x^{\mathsf T}Ax$ on $\operatorname{St}(3,1)=S^2$. The top eigenspace is
$\operatorname{span}(e_1)$, so the optimum is $-5$. Start Lanczos at
$b=(1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt3$ and use the two-dimensional Krylov space. The
normalized vector generated by $p(A)b$, with $p(\lambda)=(\lambda-1)/4$, has
Rayleigh quotient $41/9$ for $A$, objective value $-41/9$, and objective gap
$4/9$. The Chebyshev start-angle theorem bounds the largest Ritz-value gap by
$5/8$. Direct arithmetic also gives $4/9\le5/8$ for this explicit candidate;
the candidate need not maximize the Rayleigh quotient on the Krylov space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The diagonal matrix $A$, unit starting vector $b$, and the Stiefel trace objective above.

[F1] The Stiefel gradient is the tangent projection of the ambient Euclidean gradient ([[prop-stiefel-grassmann-riemannian-gradient-formulas]]).

[F2] For a simple top eigenvalue, a nonzero top-eigenspace component of the start vector gives the stated Chebyshev Ritz-value error bound ([[thm-lanczos-largest-ritz-value-chebyshev-bound-with-degenerate-cases]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** explicit polynomial filtering and exact Rayleigh-quotient arithmetic.

1.1 The ambient gradient of $f$ is $-2Ax$. At $x=e_2$, the Stiefel projection in [F1] is zero, so the eigendirection is stationary; at the global minimizer $e_1$, $f(e_1)=-5$. For the displayed start, $p(A)b=(1,1/4,-1/4)^{\mathsf T}/\sqrt3$ is nonzero and belongs to $K_2(A,b)=\operatorname{span}\{b,Ab\}$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 The squared norm of this vector is $(1+1/16+1/16)/3=3/8$, and its $A$-quadratic form is $(5+2/16)/3=41/24$. Dividing gives Rayleigh quotient $41/9$, hence objective $-41/9$ and gap $5-41/9=4/9$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Here $\lambda_1=5$, $\lambda_2=2$, $\lambda_3=0$, the top multiplicity is one, and $\tau=\|(I-P)b\|/\|Pb\|=\sqrt2$. Since step 1.1 places the candidate in $K_2$, its quotient shows $\theta_2\ge41/9$. For Krylov dimension $m=2$, the theorem [F2] gives $$\lambda_1-\theta_2\le\frac{(5-0)(\sqrt2)^2}{T_1(1+2(5-2)/(2-0))^2}=\frac{10}{4^2}=\frac58.$$ Since $\theta_2\ge41/9$, its Ritz gap obeys $0\le\lambda_1-\theta_2\le4/9\le5/8$, so the candidate comparison and source bound agree. Also $b$ and $Ab$ are independent because the third coordinate of $Ab$ is zero while that of $b$ is nonzero, so the two-dimensional Krylov space is attained without breakdown. [F2, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
