## Full independent counterexample verification

Work in ZFC, hence in particular with the theorem's Countable Choice assumption. Set T=1 and V=H=l2(N0;R), with the identity evolution embedding. Let e0=(1,0,...), and define w(t)=(t^n/n!)_(n>=0), 0<=t<=1. The coordinate series for w and w' converge uniformly in l2 since the squared tails are bounded by tails of sum_(n>=0) 1/(n!)^2; coordinatewise differentiation is therefore legitimate. In fact w is smooth, but C1 suffices. Thus w is in W, w(0)=e0, and (e0,w(t))=1, (e0,w'(t))=0.

For any finitely many distinct t1,...,tk, a linear relation among the vectors w(ti) gives, in coordinates n=0,...,k-1 after multiplying by n!, a Vandermonde system. Its determinant is product_(i<j)(tj-ti), nonzero. Therefore the uncountable family {w(t):0<=t<=1} is algebraically linearly independent. By the Hamel-basis extension theorem in ZFC, extend it to a basis B of the real vector space l2. For each t let lambda_t be the coefficient of w(t) in a vector's finite B expansion. Then lambda_t(w(t))=1, and lambda_t(w(s))=0 if s differs from t. Every fixed v has a finite Hamel expansion, so lambda_t(v) is nonzero at only finitely many t. In particular it vanishes almost everywhere; its time dependence for fixed v is Lebesgue measurable. For each t, continuity of w(s) as s tends to t through distinct s also shows lambda_t is not continuous: its values on those w(s) are zero, whereas its value at w(t) is one. The bilinear form below is therefore genuinely unbounded. Even scalar measurability for each fixed x,y would not fix this example.

Let Theta=R, a_theta(t;x,y)=lambda_t(x)(e0,y), u_theta(t)=theta w(t), f_theta(t)=theta(w'(t)+e0), u0_theta=theta e0. The form is independent of theta; its parameter derivative is zero. All forcing and initial data derivatives exist in their required Hilbert/Bochner spaces. The state map is bounded linear from R into W, hence Frechet differentiable, with w_eta=eta w and initial derivative eta e0. For every t and every v, the state identity holds because lambda_t(theta w(t))=theta, so the two sides are theta(w'(t),v)+theta(e0,v). Its linearized identity holds for every t and v by the same computation. There is no issue of differentiating an unbounded operator in this construction: a_theta has zero parameter derivative identically.

Set L=0 and phi(x,theta)=(e0,x). The objective assumptions hold, including the common integrable majorant, and its H terminal gradient is e0. Set p(t)=e0, so p is in W, p'=0 and p(1)=e0. Its purported adjoint equation for a fixed v is a_theta(t;v,e0)=lambda_t(v)=0, which holds except at finitely many times depending on v. Thus p satisfies the original written adjoint requirement. It does not satisfy the repaired simultaneous-test requirement: at every t the test v=w(t) gives 1=0. This is exactly where original Proof 1.2 substituted illegally.

The actual reduced objective is j(theta)=(e0,theta w(1))=theta, hence Dj(theta)[1]=1. The original claimed right-hand side is (p(0),e0)+integral_0^1(e0,w'(t)+e0)dt=1+1=2. All terms in this example are separately integrable. The defect is therefore not merely a failure to define the integral; it contradicts the asserted numerical derivative. The use of a full Hamel basis belongs only to this research counterexample, not to the repaired theorem's proof or axiom dependency list. No new active counterexample item is proposed.

## Repaired proof and exact extent

Fix theta and eta. On the common full-measure time set, insert v=p(t) in the linearized equation and v=w_eta(t) in the adjoint equation. Bilinearity gives cancellation, with no symmetry assumption:

    <f'_theta[eta],p> - a'_theta[eta](u_theta,p)
      = <w'_eta,p> + <p',w_eta> + D_uL[w_eta].

Each term on the right is integrable: w'_eta,p' and D_uL belong to L2(V*) and the paired p,w_eta belong to L2(V). Their norm products are L1 by Cauchy--Schwarz; continuity of the pairing gives measurability. Consequently the displayed difference on the left has a measurable L1 representative (Lebesgue measure is complete), even when the two summands on the left are not individually integrable. This observation preserves the largest conditional claim without introducing arbitrary derivative bounds. The draft explicitly interprets that difference together inside the original gradient integral. Null-set values may be defined arbitrarily without affecting the integral.

The pairing lemma now integrates <p',w_eta>+<w'_eta,p> into the two endpoint terms. Initial w_eta(0)=u0'_theta[eta] and terminal p(T)=gradient_H phi give the signs shown in the theorem. The assumed objective differentiation, chain rule and continuous trace map give its preliminary derivative formula; cancellation of integral D_uL[w_eta] proves the original gradient conclusion. The new hypothesis is all that changes the argument. In particular no solution existence, coercivity, symmetry, or state-map differentiability is inferred.

A bounded-form repair would also be possible, but requires a little more care than saying 'bounded': both residual maps in the test v must be continuous almost everywhere, including the parameter-derivative slice in the tangent equation. For time-dependent tests whose Bochner representatives have separable essential ranges, countably many fixed tests dense in those ranges suffice: discard their countable union of exceptional sets and extend the residual by continuity at each remaining time. Hence global separability of V and uniform time bounds are unnecessary just for this test substitution. This alternative is recorded to explain why the proposed direct simultaneous-test formulation is both smaller and less restrictive than a global bounded-form regime. It is not used as an unproved source for the draft.
