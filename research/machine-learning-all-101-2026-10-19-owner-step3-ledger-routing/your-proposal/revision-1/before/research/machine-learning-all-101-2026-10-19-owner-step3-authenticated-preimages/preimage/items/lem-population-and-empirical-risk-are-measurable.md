## Statement

In a supervised prediction problem, for every measurable predictor $f$, the function $(x,y)\mapsto\ell(f(x),y)$ is measurable, so its population risk is a defined extended nonnegative integral. For every sample size $m\ge1$, the empirical risk $S\mapsto\widehat R_S(f)$ is an extended nonnegative measurable function on the finite product sample space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A supervised prediction problem, a measurable predictor $f\in\mathcal F$, and a positive integer $m$. The sample domain is $(\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y)^m$ with its recursively formed product sigma-algebra.

[F1] Predictors are measurable, and the loss is measurable on the action-label product. [[def-supervised-learning-problem]]

[F2] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles and finite products are formed recursively. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F3] Population risk is the nonnegative integral of $(x,y)\mapsto\ell(f(x),y)$, and empirical risk is its finite sample average. [[def-population-and-empirical-risk]]

[F4] Finite sums of measurable extended-real functions are measurable when pointwise defined; real scalar multiples of real-valued measurable functions, functions $f\mathbf1_E$ for nonnegative measurable extended-real $f$ and measurable $E$, countable infima, and increasing pointwise limits of measurable extended-real functions are measurable. [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]]

[F5] A map is measurable when preimages of measurable sets are measurable; preimages under a composition are taken successively. [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

[F6] An extended-real-valued function is measurable when all of its strict-superlevel preimages are measurable. [[def-extended-real-valued-measurable-function]]

## Proof

1.1 Define $\Phi_f(x,y)=(f(x),y)$. For each measurable rectangle $U\times V$ in $\mathsf A\times\mathsf Y$, its preimage is $f^{-1}(U)\times V$, which belongs to $\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y$ by measurability of $f$. Since the rectangles generate the target product sigma-algebra by [F2], $\Phi_f$ is measurable by [F5]. Therefore $\ell_f:=\ell\circ\Phi_f$ is measurable by successive preimages in [F5] and nonnegative; [F3] defines $R_P(f)=\int\ell_f\,dP$. [F1, F2, F3, F5]

2.1 For each coordinate $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$, the sample projection $\pi_i: (\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y)^m\to\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y$ is measurable by the finite product construction in [F2]. Hence $S\mapsto\ell_f(\pi_i(S))$ is measurable by [F5]. The finite sum $s(S):=\sum_{i=1}^m\ell_f(\pi_i(S))$ is a defined nonnegative extended-real function and is measurable by [F4]. Since $m>0$, for every real $a$ we have $\{S:s(S)/m>a\}=\{S:s(S)>ma\}$, including when $s(S)=+\infty$. Thus $s/m$ is measurable by the strict-superlevel criterion [F6], and [F3] gives measurability of $S\mapsto\widehat R_S(f)$. [given, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1] ∎
