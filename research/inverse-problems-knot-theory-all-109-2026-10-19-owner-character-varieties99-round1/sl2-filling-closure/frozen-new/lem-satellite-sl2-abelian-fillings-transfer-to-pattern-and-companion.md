## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $K=P(C)$ be a zero-framed oriented satellite, with signed pattern winding $w$, and let $P(U)$ be its zero-framed pattern closure. If a slope $p/q$ is $SL_2(\mathbb C)$-abelian for $K$, it is $SL_2(\mathbb C)$-abelian for $P(U)$.

If $w\ne0$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$ and $q>0$, put $d=\gcd(q,w^2)$. If the primitive satellite filling of slope
$$\frac{p w^2/d}{q/d}=w^2\frac pq$$
is $SL_2(\mathbb C)$-abelian, then the companion filling of slope $p/q$ is $SL_2(\mathbb C)$-abelian. The meridional case $q=0$ also holds, since both meridional filling groups are trivial. This is an assertion about ALL representations, including reducible nonabelian ones, not merely irreducible characters. Consequently infinitely many distinct rational $SL_2(\mathbb C)$-abelian satellite slopes give infinitely many such slopes for the pattern closure and, when $w\ne0$, for the companion after division by $w^2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the marked zero-framed satellite and primitive slopes of the Statement.

[F1] The actual pattern group has abelianization $\mathbb Z[\mu_P]\oplus\mathbb Z[\lambda_V]$, with $[\mu_V]=w[\mu_P]$ and $[\lambda_P]=w[\lambda_V]$. Satellite gluing identifies $(\mu_V,\lambda_V)$ with $(\mu_C,\lambda_C)$ and the inner pair with the preferred $(\mu_K,\lambda_K)$. The actual exterior group is the pushout, the pattern-closure group is $\pi_1(X_P)/\langle\!\langle\lambda_V\rangle\!\rangle$, and a knot meridian normally generates its group. These geometric/group assertions, including their basings, are proved in [[lem-satellite-peripheral-representations-transfer-thinness]], independently of its generic diagonal transfer.

[F2] Filling glues the solid-torus meridian to the actual primitive peripheral word $\mu^p\lambda^q$. The overlap pushout is valid without injectivity of either torus map. [[def-oriented-slope-and-knot-dehn-filling]] [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]]

[F3] The companion abelianization is generated by its positive meridian and kills its preferred longitude. [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] The meaning of every representation having abelian image is [[def-sl-two-abelian-surgery]]. The choice assumption is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof

1.1 In the filling pushout of [F2], choose a second torus slope completing $(p,q)$ to a unimodular basis. Its image is the attached solid-torus core generator, while $\mu^p\lambda^q$ is killed. Eliminate that core generator. This gives the actual group identity $\pi_1(E_J(p/q))=\pi_1(E_J)/\langle\!\langle\mu_J^p\lambda_J^q\rangle\!\rangle$ for every marked knot $J$. In particular a representation descends exactly when that actual matrix word is $I$, not when merely one eigenvalue of a power is 1. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The quotient map from the pattern group onto the pattern-closure group extends to the satellite group by sending the companion's meridian-exponent class $n$ to the image of $\mu_V^n$. Its companion longitude maps to $I$, matching the killed $\lambda_V$ in [F1]. The pushout gives a surjection $\pi_1(E_K)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(E_{P(U)})$ preserving the inner meridian and preferred longitude. Step1.1 therefore gives a surjection of the same-slope filling groups. Any nonabelian representation of the latter composed with this surjection remains nonabelian, proving the pattern assertion for every primitive slope and every $w$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 For the companion assertion work contrapositively. Take a representation $\rho$ of $\pi_1(E_C)$ with nonabelian image and $\rho(\mu_C)^p\rho(\lambda_C)^q=I$, using step1.1. Write these commuting matrices as $A,B$. Normal meridian generation in [F1] shows $A\ne\pm I$: a central meridian would force the whole image to be central and hence abelian. Conjugating the whole representation puts $A$ either in a diagonal form with distinct eigenvalues or in a noncentral Jordan form. We will construct a possibly centrally twisted companion representation together with a matching abelian pattern representation that kills the PRIMITIVE satellite word $\mu_P^{p w^2/d}\lambda_P^{q/d}$. The integers in this word are coprime, since $d=\gcd(q,w^2)$ and $\gcd(p,q)=1$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 In the distinct-eigenvalue case, $A=\operatorname{diag}(\alpha,\alpha^{-1})$ and $B=\operatorname{diag}(\beta,\beta^{-1})$. Write $\alpha=s e^{i\theta}$ and $\beta=t e^{i\phi}$, with $s,t>0$. The actual filling identity gives $s^p t^q=1$ and $p\theta+q\phi=2\pi m$ for an integer $m$. As $d\mid q$, $\gcd(p,d)=1$, so choose $k\in\mathbb Z$ with $m+pk\equiv0\pmod d$ and put $z=s^{1/w}\exp(i(\theta+2\pi k)/w)$. This works for signed $w\ne0$ and satisfies $z^w=\alpha$. Send $[\mu_P]$ to $T=\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1})$ and $[\lambda_V]$ to $B$. By [F1] these match $A,B$ on the outer torus and give inner longitude $B^w$. The first eigenvalue of the primitive filling word is $$z^{p w^2/d}\beta^{wq/d}=(s^p t^q)^{w/d}\exp(2\pi i(m+pk)w/d)=1.$$ Both matrices are diagonal, so its second eigenvalue is the inverse and the WHOLE filling word is $I$. This is the needed slope-adapted root; an arbitrary $w$th root would not suffice. [F1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 Next write $A=\epsilon(I+aN)$, $B=\eta(I+bN)$ with $N^2=0$, $N\ne0$, $a\ne0$, and $\epsilon,\eta\in\{1,-1\}$. Solving the commutant of a nonzero two-by-two nilpotent shows that this includes every commuting $B$, also when $b=0$. The identity $(I+tN)^n=I+ntN$ holds for every integer $n$, using $I-tN$ for the inverse, so the following computations also cover negative winding. Suppose first $\epsilon^w=\epsilon$; this covers $\epsilon=1$ and also $\epsilon=-1$ with $w$ odd. The original filling equation gives $\epsilon^p\eta^q=1$ and $pa+qb=0$. Put $T=\epsilon(I+(a/w)N)$ and assign $[\lambda_V]\mapsto B$. Then $T^w=A$, so [F1] matches the outer torus, and the inner longitude is $B^w$. The primitive satellite word equals $$\epsilon^{p w^2/d}\eta^{wq/d}\left(I+\frac{w}{d}(pa+qb)N\right).$$ Its unipotent part is $I$. Its sign $S$ is 1 as well: if $d$ is odd, $S^d=(\epsilon^p\eta^q)^w=1$, using $\epsilon^w=\epsilon$, and $S\in\{1,-1\}$. If $d$ is even, both $q,w$ are even; the present case then forces $\epsilon=1$, while $\eta^w=1$, so again $S=1$. Thus the whole word is $I$. [F1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.3 The remaining case is $A=-(I+aN)$ and $w$ even. This matrix need not have a commuting $w$th root in $SL_2(\mathbb C)$, so do not assert one. Use the actual central character $\chi(g)=(-1)^{\operatorname{ab}(g)}$ from [F3] and replace $\rho$ by $\rho'=\chi\rho$. Commutators are unchanged by this twist, so its image remains nonabelian. Its meridian is $I+aN$ and its longitude remains $B=\eta(I+bN)$. Put $T=I+(a/w)N$ and assign $[\lambda_V]\mapsto B$. Then $T^w=\rho'(\mu_C)$ and $B^w=I+wbN$ since $w$ is even, giving the correct gluing to $\rho'$. The original relation still implies $pa+qb=0$, so the primitive satellite word is $I+(w/d)(pa+qb)N=I$. The twisted representation is not asserted to descend to the original companion filling: only its actual satellite extension must kill the satellite word, as this calculation proves. [F1, F3, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 In each of steps3.1–3.3, the pattern representation factors through its actual free abelianization, agrees with the indicated companion representation on BOTH outer generators, and kills the primitive inner filling word. The exterior pushout and step1.1 therefore give an actual representation of the satellite filling. Its image contains the chosen companion image, which is nonabelian even in the twisted case. This proves the companion contrapositive and hence the asserted implication. The cases exhaust all noncentral determinant-one meridian matrices, and step2.2 excludes precisely the central ones; no semisimplicity or irreducibility hypothesis was imposed on the original representation. For the meridional slope, step1.1 kills the normally generating meridian, so both filling groups are trivial and the remaining assertion is automatic. Finally division of rational slopes by the fixed nonzero integer $w^2$ is injective, while the pattern transfer leaves the slope unchanged. Hence the two infinitude conclusions follow without an accumulation-slope theorem. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 3.3, construct, algebra] ∎
