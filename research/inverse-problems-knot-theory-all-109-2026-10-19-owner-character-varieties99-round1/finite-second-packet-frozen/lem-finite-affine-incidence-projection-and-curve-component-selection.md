## Statement

Assume full AC. Put $T=(\mathbb C^*)^n$ and $R=\mathbb C[z_1^{\pm1},\ldots,z_n^{\pm1}]$, with $n\ge0$. For a finite index set $B$, let $J_b$ be an ideal of $R[u_1,\ldots,u_{m_b}]$, where $m_b\ge0$, and let $X_b$ be its zero set in $T\times\mathbb C^{m_b}$. Let $S$ be the union of the coordinate projections of the $X_b$ to $T$. Then its Zariski closure $Z$ has radical vanishing ideal $$I(Z)=\bigcap_{b\in B}\bigl(\sqrt{J_b}\cap R\bigr).$$ Write the finite irreducible-component decomposition as $Z=\bigcup_i Z_i$, and let $C$ be the union of those $Z_i$ whose dimension is one. Then $$I(C)=\bigcap_{\dim Z_i=1}I(Z_i).$$ Thus one must select the one-dimensional components after radical elimination; the first intersection alone also retains any isolated or higher-dimensional components. Empty intersections are the unit ideal and an empty selected union is empty. If $S$ is preserved by every coordinatewise field automorphism of $\mathbb C$ fixing $\mathbb Q$ (in particular, if all $J_b$ are generated by rational-coefficient Laurent polynomials), both $I(Z)$ and $I(C)$ are scalar extensions of radical ideals over $\mathbb Q$. No closedness of the projections or bound on their dimension is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC and the indicated finite incidence charts and coordinate projections.

[F1] The affine and relative Nullstellensatz identifies the vanishing ideal of a zero set with the radical of its defining ideal ([[thm-classical-affine-nullstellensatz-correspondence]]).

[F2] Every affine algebraic set has a unique finite irredundant decomposition into maximal nonempty irreducible closed subsets, including the empty decomposition ([[lem-classical-affine-algebraic-set-finite-irreducible-components]]).

[F3] Geometric dimension equals the dimension of the coordinate ring; the proof identifies both dimensions with lengths of strict chains of irreducible closed sets or prime ideals ([[thm-affine-variety-dimension-coordinate-ring]]).

[F4] A Laurent vanishing ideal stable under every field automorphism fixing $\mathbb Q$ is the scalar extension of its rational contraction, which is radical ([[lem-automorphism-stable-laurent-ideals-descend-to-rational-coefficients]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Exact radical contraction.** Realize $T\times\mathbb C^{m_b}$ as the closed affine set with coordinates $z_i,v_i,u_j$ and equations $z_iv_i=1$. Its coordinate ring is $R[u_1,\ldots,u_{m_b}]$: replace each $z_i^{-1}$ by $v_i$, and reduce any mixed positive powers using $z_iv_i=1$. Thus the relative assertion of [F1] gives $I(X_b)=\sqrt{J_b}$ in exactly this coordinate ring. A Laurent polynomial $f\in R$ vanishes on the projection of $X_b$ exactly when its pullback, the same expression independent of $u$, vanishes on $X_b$. Its ideal is consequently $\sqrt{J_b}\cap R$. Vanishing on a finite union means vanishing on every member, so $I(S)$ is the stated intersection. Moreover $I(S)=I(\overline S)$: each polynomial zero set containing $S$ contains its closure, and $S\subseteq\overline S$ gives the reverse inclusion. This proves the first formula without asserting that a projection is closed. For no charts, $S$ is empty and every polynomial vanishes on it. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Select actual curve components.** The set $Z$ is a closed subset of the displayed affine model for $T$, so [F2] gives its finite component list $Z_i$. Select precisely the members of dimension one, with dimension as in [F3]. This is a finite union of closed sets, hence closed. A polynomial vanishes on that union exactly when it vanishes on each selected member, giving the second formula directly. Each component ideal is radical, since $f^k$ vanishing at all its points forces $f$ to vanish; their intersection is radical as well. If no component is selected its vanishing ideal is the whole ring. The need for selection is literal: in $(\mathbb C^*)^2$, the closed set $V(L-1)\cup\{(2,2)\}$ has ideal $(L-1)\cap(M-2,L-2)$, whereas its selected curve ideal is $(L-1)$. These differ since $L-1$ has value $1$ at $(2,2)$. The line ideal formula follows by substitution $L=1$ in a Laurent polynomial (multiply by a monomial and divide by $L-1$); the point ideal follows by subtracting its value successively in $M$ and $L$. Thus even radical elimination does not itself discard isolated points. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Rational descent commutes with selection.** A field automorphism $\sigma$ fixing $\mathbb Q$ induces a bijection of $T$ taking $V(f_1,\ldots,f_r)$ to $V(\sigma f_1,\ldots,\sigma f_r)$; the same assertion for arbitrary collections of equations makes it a Zariski homeomorphism. If $S$ is stable, its closure $Z$ is stable as well. This homeomorphism permutes the maximal irreducible closed subsets by maximality and preserves their dimensions by bijecting all strict chains of irreducible closed subsets, the convention in [F3]. Therefore it preserves $C$. Apply [F4] separately to the stable point sets $Z$ and $C$ to obtain both asserted radical rational contractions. Finally, rational generators for each $J_b$ make $X_b$ stable by applying $\sigma$ to the defining equations and their inverse implications; projection commutes with $\sigma$, so their union $S$ is stable. All arguments include $n=0$, empty charts, empty zero sets and the whole torus. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, algebra] ∎
