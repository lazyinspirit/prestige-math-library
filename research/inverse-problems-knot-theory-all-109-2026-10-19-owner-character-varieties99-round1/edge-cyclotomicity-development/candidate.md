## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth knot and let $A(M,L)$ be its reduced ordinary or enhanced A-polynomial, with exponent point $(a,b)$ representing $L^aM^b$. For every one-dimensional Newton face, every nonzero complex root of its edge polynomial in a primitive edge coordinate is a root of unity. This includes the entire Newton segment when the Newton polygon has dimension one. The assertion is vacuous for a constant polynomial.

More precisely, let $(u,v)$ be a primitive integral minimum normal to the face and put $y=L^{-v}M^u$. After dividing the face polynomial by a Laurent monomial and a nonzero scalar, regard it as a polynomial in $y$ with nonzero constant term. For each of its roots $c\in\mathbb C^*$ there is an integer $d>0$ such that $c^{2d}=1$.

Consequently, if the Newton support is contained in an integral line of primitive direction $(q,p)$, every one-dimensional irreducible peripheral graph $M^pL^q=c$ contributing to $A$ has a root of unity as its constant. No nonzero condition on $q$ is asserted or inferred by this conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the actual knot exterior $X$, its meridian and preferred longitude, and the polynomial/face in the Statement.

[A1] Full AC. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A normalized discrete valuation has valuation ring $O$, a uniformizer $\pi$, and ideals $\pi^nO$; division by an element of minimum valuation proves the elementary ideal assertions used below. [[def-discrete-valuation-ring]]

[F2] The finite signed determinant is alternating, multilinear and normalized over a commutative ring. Expanding the columns of a product and using alternation gives $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$; hence the basis relations below are consistent. Thus finite complex vector spaces have determinant lines: ordered bases are generators with change-of-basis relation given by that determinant. Concatenating a subspace basis and lifted quotient basis gives the exact-sequence determinant identification; its independence follows by a block upper-triangular change of basis. These finite constructions also cover the zero vector space with determinant line $\mathbb C$. [[thm-leibniz-determinant-is-alternating-multilinear-and-normalized]]

[F3] Homogeneous group bars have alternating vertex-deletion boundary. In increment coordinates their coinvariant differential in degrees two and three is $\partial[g|h]=[h]-[gh]+[g]$ and $\partial[g|h|k]=[h|k]-[gh|k]+[g|hk]-[g|h]$. We use these explicit complexes, without a derived-resolution or asphericity assumption. [[def-unnormalized-homogeneous-bar-resolution]]

[F4] The compact oriented exterior has a finite singular relative fundamental cycle whose boundary represents the oriented boundary-torus class. The pair connector is the boundary of a representative, and its boundary class is the outward-normal-first fundamental class. [[def-relative-fundamental-class-and-boundary-orientation]] [[lem-a-collar-identifies-boundary-local-homology-with-the-pair-fundamental-class]]

[F5] The actual ordinary/enhanced eigenvalue incidence and the toric normalization construction supply a dominant finite-type representation incidence over each selected peripheral factor, an actual finite representation field, and extension of a local normalized-curve valuation with residue field $\mathbb C$. In the enhanced case the chosen representations can be absolutely irreducible. The construction at a specified face root is reproduced in steps6.1–7.1, so the existential edge witness is not silently taken to cover all roots. [[def-knot-a-polynomial]] [[def-enhanced-a-polynomial]] [[lem-newton-edges-have-valued-finite-peripheral-representation-models]]

## Proof
 1.1 We first construct the required valuation operation. Let $F$ contain $\mathbb C$, let $w:F^*\to\mathbb Z$ be a normalized discrete valuation trivial on $\mathbb C^*$, and suppose that the residue map identifies $O/(\pi)$ with $\mathbb C$. Successive residue extraction shows that $1,\pi,\ldots,\pi^{n-1}$ is a complex basis of $O/\pi^nO$: subtract the constant residue, divide by $\pi$, and repeat; independence follows by taking the least nonzero coefficient's valuation. Every pair of full $O$-lattices in $F^2$ has a common lower lattice and finite-dimensional complex quotients. Indeed finitely many matrix entries bound both inclusions between suitable $\pi$-powers of $O^2$. The following two-dimensional diagonal reduction also computes their quotient dimensions. After scaling a matrix to be integral, move an entry of minimum valuation to its first position. It divides every entry, so integral row/column subtraction clears its row and column; the remaining diagonal entry completes the reduction. Unit changes of basis leave the quotient unchanged. It follows that, for $N\subset L$, $\dim_{\mathbb C}(L/N)$ is the valuation of the determinant of an $O$-basis of $N$ measured in an $O$-basis of $L$. In particular all lattices $L=A O^2$, $A\in SL_2(F)$, have equal dimensions modulo any common lower lattice $N$. Only full free lower lattices are needed: a sufficiently small $\pi^NO^2$ is a common lower lattice for any finite collection, including any previously chosen lower lattices. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 For lattices $L,L'$ of determinant valuation zero define the line $$\mathcal D(L,L')=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C}\bigl(\det(L/N),\det(L'/N)\bigr),$$ where $N\subset L\cap L'$ is any common lower lattice. If $N'\subset N$, the exact sequences identify the two determinant lines with $\det(N/N')\otimes\det(L/N)$ and $\det(N/N')\otimes\det(L'/N)$, respectively. Identify a map $T$ with $1\otimes T$. This is independent of a basis for the common first factor: rescaling it rescales source and target equally. Block-basis concatenation proves that two successive refinements give the same identification as their composite. A common further lower lattice therefore identifies any two choices of $N$ uniquely. For three lattices choose the same $N$ and compose the maps of their one-dimensional determinant lines. Refinement preserves composition because $(1\otimes T')(1\otimes T)=1\otimes(T'T)$. It follows that the transitivity maps $\mathcal D(L,L')\otimes\mathcal D(L',L'')\to\mathcal D(L,L'')$ are associative. No permutation of a source-only determinant factor is used: both quotient dimensions are equal, and all maps have degree zero. This supplies the sign convention, including when the common lower lattice is changed. Every $A\in SL_2(F)$ acts naturally on these lines by its complex-linear quotient maps, compatibly with refinement and composition. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Set $L_0=O^2$. Pairs $(A,\phi)$ with $A\in SL_2(F)$ and $0\ne\phi\in\mathcal D(L_0,AL_0)$ form a group under $$(A,\phi)(B,\psi)=(AB,(A\psi)\circ\phi).$$ Step2.1 proves associativity. The identity is $(1,1)$ and the inverse is obtained by applying $A^{-1}$ to $\phi^{-1}$. The kernel of projection to $SL_2(F)$ consists of the nonzero scalars in $\operatorname{Hom}(\det(L_0/N),\det(L_0/N))$; it is $\mathbb C^*$ and is central. The projection is onto since each determinant line is nonzero. For $A\in SL_2(O)$ there is the canonical lift $(A,1)$, and these lifts multiply correctly since $AL_0=L_0$. Thus this is an actual central extension, with a supplied splitting on $SL_2(O)$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Write $H_a=\operatorname{diag}(\pi^a,\pi^{-a})$ and $U_z=\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1})$, $z\in O^*$. For $a\ge0$, take $N=\pi^aOe_1\oplus Oe_2$. On $L_0/N$ the determinant of $U_z$ is $\bar z^a$; on $H_aL_0/N$ it is $\bar z^{-a}$. Hence its action on $\mathcal D(L_0,H_aL_0)$ is multiplication by $\bar z^{-2a}$. Comparing the two products in step3.1 gives $$[\widetilde H_a,\widetilde U_z]=\bar z^{2a},\qquad [s,t]=sts^{-1}t^{-1}.$$ Commutators of lifts of a commuting subgroup are central and bimultiplicative: expanding $[s_1s_2,t]$ and $[s,t_1t_2]$ proves this directly. Choose the lifts of all $H_a$ as powers of one lift of $H_1$; their mutual commutators vanish. Lifts of units commute by the splitting. Bimultiplicativity proves the displayed identity also for negative $a$ and yields, for $f=\pi^a z$ and $g=\pi^b t$, $$[\widetilde{\operatorname{diag}(f,f^{-1})},\widetilde{\operatorname{diag}(g,g^{-1})}]  =\bar t^{2a}\bar z^{-2b}  =\left(\overline{f^b/g^a}\right)^{-2}.$$ Changing either lift by a central scalar leaves this commutator unchanged. The usual tame-symbol factor $(-1)^{ab}$ also disappears on squaring. [step 3.1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 For any representation $\rho:\pi_1(X)\to SL_2(F)$ the lifts of its two peripheral elements commute in this extension. Here is the actual boundary argument, without assuming that $X$ or a presentation complex is aspherical. Choose a section $s$ of the extension with $s(1)=1$ and put $c(g,h)=s(g)s(h)s(gh)^{-1}\in\mathbb C^*$. Associativity gives $c(g,h)c(gh,k)=c(h,k)c(g,hk)$. Choose paths from a fixed basepoint to the points of $X$. For an oriented singular simplex, label its edge $ij$ by the represented loop going to vertex $i$, traversing that edge, and returning from vertex $j$. The triangular face supplies $g_{ij}g_{jk}=g_{ik}$. Send its degree-two and degree-three simplices to $[g_{01}|g_{12}]$ and $[g_{01}|g_{12}|g_{23}]$; the degree-one version and [F3] show, face by face, that this is a chain map. Evaluating $c$ multiplicatively on a bar two-chain gives 1 on the boundary of every bar three-simplex by the cocycle identity. By [F4], the standard fundamental two-cycle of the boundary torus is a boundary of a finite singular three-chain in $X$: start with a relative representative and subtract a boundary-torus three-chain correcting its boundary representative. Triangulate the torus square by its diagonal. Its two oriented triangles have bars $[\rho(\mu)|\rho(\lambda)]-[\rho(\lambda)|\rho(\mu)]$, since both edge orders have the same diagonal element. The product of cocycle values is therefore $c(\rho\mu,\rho\lambda)/c(\rho\lambda,\rho\mu)=1$. This quotient is exactly the commutator of the two chosen lifts. Reversing the torus orientation only inverts 1. [A1, F3, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Suppose the peripheral eigenvalues are $f,g\in F^*$ and at least one has nonzero valuation. Its matrix has distinct eigenvalues, and the other commuting matrix preserves its two eigenspaces. A common eigenvector for $f,g$ can therefore be completed to a simultaneous eigenbasis; rescale its second vector to give determinant 1. Conjugating the entire representation by this basis change makes the peripheral matrices the two diagonals in step4.1. Step4.2 then proves $$\left(\overline{f^{w(g)}/g^{w(f)}}\right)^2=1.$$ If both valuations are zero, the same expression is 1 without diagonalization. In particular, if $d=\gcd(|w(f)|,|w(g)|)>0$, the unit $f^{w(g)/d}g^{-w(f)/d}$ has residue $\zeta$ with $\zeta^{2d}=1$. This conclusion is for actual representations, whether reducible or absolutely irreducible. It does not require extension of the representation over $O$. [step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Now prescribe ANY nonzero root $c$ of the selected face polynomial. Factor $A$ into its peripheral Laurent factors as in [F5]. The initial form of a product is the product of initial forms, because the least weights add and their product is nonzero in a domain. Evaluation at $c$ therefore makes the initial form of some factor $P$ zero. That factor's initial form is not a monomial. Choose $r,s\in\mathbb Z$ with $ru+sv=1$, and use the unimodular coordinates $$x=L^rM^s,\qquad y=L^{-v}M^u,\qquad L=x^u y^{-s},\quad M=x^v y^r.$$ Multiplying the transformed $P$ by a Laurent monomial gives an irreducible $Q\in\mathbb C[x,y^{\pm1}]$ not divisible by $x$, with $Q(0,c)=0$. The affine curve $Q=0$ consequently has the actual point $(0,c)$. Normalize and choose a point over it. Its local DVR has $v(x)>0$, $v(y)=0$, and residue $y=c$. These are exactly the finite toric/normalization constructions of [F5], now made at the prescribed root rather than at an unspecified root. [F5, step 5.1, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 To retain actual representations, select a dominant irreducible component of the finite peripheral incidence over the curve $P=0$. Its generic fiber is a nonzero finite-type algebra over $k=\mathbb C(P=0)$. A maximal ideal gives a finite field extension $F/k$ carrying actual generator matrices and a common eigenvector with eigenvalues $M,L$. For the enhanced incidence retain its nonzero four-word matrix-algebra certificate, so this step remains valid even when the originating character component has dimension larger than one. Extend the valuation of step6.1 to this finite field as in [F5]. Its residue field is still $\mathbb C$, the residue of $y$ is still $c$, and $$(w(L),w(M))=d(u,v),\qquad d>0.$$ Apply step5.1 with $f=L,g=M$. Since $(u,v)$ is primitive, the primitive unit there is $L^vM^{-u}=y^{-1}$. Thus $c^{-2d}=1$, proving the claimed order bound. This proof applies to every prescribed face root separately; it makes no common representation-field or uniform ramification assertion for different roots. [A1, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally suppose the support lies in a line of primitive direction $(q,p)$ in the stated exponent order. A one-point support gives a Laurent unit and no peripheral graph, so assume the support has at least two points. After a Laurent monomial is removed, $A$ is a nonconstant one-variable Laurent polynomial in $y=L^qM^p$. Its zero set in the torus is the finite union of graphs $y=c$ for its nonzero roots: the unimodular completion of $(q,p)$ makes $(x,y)$ torus coordinates, so each such graph is an irreducible copy of $\mathbb C^*$. The whole Newton segment is the minimum face for the primitive normal $(u,v)=(p,-q)$. Steps6.1–7.1 therefore make every one of these constants a root of unity. This also covers $p=0$ or $q=0$; excluding either geometric possibility would require a separate argument. Laurent translations and reversal of the primitive edge coordinate only multiply the face polynomial by a monomial or replace its roots by inverses, and hence preserve the conclusion. All ordinary and enhanced branches asserted in the Statement have now been proved. [F5, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
