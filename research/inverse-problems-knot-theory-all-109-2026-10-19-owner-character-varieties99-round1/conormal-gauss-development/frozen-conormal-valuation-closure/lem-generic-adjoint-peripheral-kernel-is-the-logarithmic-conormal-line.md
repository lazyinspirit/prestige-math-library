## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $E$ be the compact exterior of a smooth oriented knot in $S^3$, with positive meridian $\mu$ and preferred longitude $\lambda$. Let $X$ be an irreducible one-dimensional component of its complex $SL_2$ character variety. Fix a one-dimensional peripheral eigenvalue-image component $Y$ arising from $X$, with coordinates $(M,L)$ ordered as (meridian,longitude), and an irreducible Laurent equation $P(M,L)=0$ for $Y$. Write $K=\mathbb C(X)$.

There is a finite extension $F/K$ with an actual generic representation $\rho:\pi_1(E)\to SL_2(F)$ and a choice of common peripheral eigenline whose eigenvalues are $M,L\in F^*$. These give the specified generic point of $Y$. Its actual intrinsic adjoint peripheral homology has a two-dimensional boundary basis ordered (longitude,meridian) and a one-dimensional inclusion kernel. With $\delta\in\{\lambda,\mu\}$ having distinct peripheral eigenvalues, set $u=\rho(\delta)-\operatorname{tr}(\rho(\delta))I/2$. In that basis its kernel is exactly
$$\ker\left\{F^2\longrightarrow\Omega^1_{F/\mathbb C},\quad (a,b)\longmapsto a\,\frac{\mathrm dL}{L}+b\,\frac{\mathrm dM}{M}\right\}.$$
The two displayed differentials are not both zero. Thus its projective slope, with convention $s=-b/a$ for the kernel line, is
$$s=\left[\frac{\mathrm dL}{L}:\frac{\mathrm dM}{M}\right]=[-M P_M:L P_L]\ \in\mathbb P^1(F).$$
A ratio of one-forms here means their coordinate pair in any nonzero basis of the one-dimensional $F$-space $\Omega^1_{F/\mathbb C}$. The result is independent of that basis, the finite field model, the common eigenline and simultaneous conjugation, and is defined over $K$. It includes zero when $\mathrm dL=0$ and infinity when $\mathrm dM=0$. In particular a nonconstant meridian eigenvalue is not assumed.

This is an identity of rational projective maps. It agrees with the actual specialized homology slope on a nonempty dense rank-chart open. At every point where the rational extension and the peripheral restriction are evaluated and $(P_M,P_L)\ne(0,0)$, its projective value is $[-M P_M:L P_L]$; when $P_L\ne0$ this is the ordinary value $-M P_M/(L P_L)$. For the square-free ordinary A-polynomial, replacing $P$ by the full polynomial gives the same ratio at a point where its gradient is nonzero. No assertion that all specialized exterior homology ranks are constant outside that open, nor any ideal-point tree or essential-surface construction, is part of this lemma.

## Proof

1.1 **An actual field model and its eigenvalue curve.** First suppose $X$ contains an irreducible character. Apply [F1], with $K=\mathbb C(X)$, to get an actual generic $\sigma$ over a field $F_0$ of degree at most two over $K$. The chosen one-dimensional eigenvalue image is not contained in the finite set $M,L\in\{1,-1\}$. Thus one of $\lambda,\mu$, denoted $\delta$, has $\operatorname{tr}(\sigma(\delta))^2-4\ne0$ in $F_0$. Adjoin one root of its quadratic characteristic polynomial. Its two distinct eigenlines are preserved by both commuting peripheral matrices, so they simultaneously diagonalize over this extension $F$ and give eigenvalues $M,L$. Choosing the line corresponding to the specified component gives its generic point; the other choice inverts both coordinates. This field has degree at most four over $K$. All word traces are unchanged. Every field just used is finitely generated of transcendence degree one over $\mathbb C$. Since at least one of $M,L$ is nonconstant, choose such a coordinate $t$. It is transcendental over the algebraically closed field $\mathbb C$, and $F/\mathbb C(t)$ is finite: every element of a finite generating list of $F/\mathbb C(t)$ is algebraic, and a finite tower of finite simple algebraic extensions is finite. In particular the two eigenvalue differentials will not both vanish. The case of a component consisting of reducible characters is treated in step8.1. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **One-dimensional differentials, with an actual derivation.** Formal differentiation on $\mathbb C(t)$ extends uniquely to $F$ with $D(t)=1$. To check this directly, build the finite algebraic tower from step1.1. If $\alpha$ has monic minimal polynomial $p(T)=\sum_j c_jT^j$ over a preceding field with its already defined derivation, then $p'(\alpha)\ne0$: characteristic zero makes the derivative of the nonconstant irreducible $p$ nonzero, and its smaller degree prevents it from vanishing at $\alpha$. Define $D\alpha=-\sum_j(Dc_j)\alpha^j/p'(\alpha)$. Differentiate polynomials in $\alpha$ by the product rule with this value. The ideal $(p)$ is preserved, because differentiating $p$ gives zero at $\alpha$; hence this descends to the simple-extension field and extends to quotients by the quotient rule. This proves existence and uniqueness at every step. In the universal module [F6], differentiating the same relations writes every $\mathrm d\alpha$ as a multiple of $\mathrm dt$, and the quotient rule does the same for every element of $F$. The functional induced by $D$ takes $\mathrm dt$ to1, so $\mathrm dt\ne0$. Thus $\Omega^1_{F/\mathbb C}=F\,\mathrm dt$, and $\mathrm df=(Df)\mathrm dt$. In particular $\mathrm dt/t\ne0$, which proves the asserted nonvanishing. Every other derivation is its value at $t$ times $D$, by the same uniqueness calculation. [F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 **The actual boundary inclusion has rank one over this field.** Use the field $F$ as a constant coefficient ring for the intrinsic adjoint local system. It is self-dual by the nondegenerate transport-invariant form $\langle V,W\rangle=\operatorname{tr}(VW)$ on $\mathfrak{sl}_2(F)$. Intrinsic cochains with this dual system are the full $F$-linear dual of intrinsic chains: chains are a direct sum over simplices and cochains the corresponding product, and the exceptional first-face transports are inverse adjoints. Under full AC a functional on a subspace extends to the whole vector space by extending a basis. Consequently $H^1(E;\operatorname{Ad}\rho)=H_1(E;\operatorname{Ad}\rho)^*$: a cocycle is precisely a functional killing boundaries, and a functional zero on cycles is a coboundary after extending the functional on the image of the preceding boundary. The same holds on the boundary. The first homology here is finite-dimensional by [F2]; the boundary has dimension two by [F3]. Apply [F4]. Cap with the relative exterior class identifies $H^1(E)$ with $H_2(E,\partial E)$, while boundary cap identifies $H^1(\partial E)$ with $H_1(\partial E)$. The cap boundary identity identifies the latter cap after restriction with the pair connector $\partial:H_2(E,\partial E)\to H_1(\partial E)$, up to the degree sign. Restriction is the transpose of inclusion $i_*$ under the evaluation pairings just described. Hence $\operatorname{rank}\partial=\operatorname{rank}i_*$. Pair exactness gives $\ker i_*=\operatorname{im}\partial$, so $2=\dim\ker i_*+\operatorname{rank}i_*=2\operatorname{rank}i_*$. Both image and kernel therefore have dimension one. The two classes with the common vector $u$ in the Statement are the actual basis by [F3], also in the inverse-forward-transport convention: applying [F3] to inverse peripheral matrices replaces $u$ by $-u$, a common harmless rescaling. This proves generic admissibility on the actual exterior; it is not assumed from an abstract matrix family. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Differentiate the actual representation and annihilate every relator boundary.** Put $Z(g)=D(\rho(g))\rho(g)^{-1}$, with entrywise $D$. Differentiating determinant1 gives $\operatorname{tr}Z(g)=0$; for a two-by-two matrix this follows immediately by differentiating its four-entry determinant identity and multiplying by its adjugate. The product and inverse rules give $Z(gh)=Z(g)+\operatorname{Ad}\rho(g)Z(h)$ and $Z(g^{-1})=-\operatorname{Ad}\rho(g)^{-1}Z(g)$. In the finite presentation complex of [F2], define an $F$-linear functional on generator-edge chains by $\ell(e_j\otimes v)=\operatorname{tr}(vZ(x_j))$. For a positive letter $x_j$ after prefix $q$, [F2]'s word chain has coefficient $\operatorname{Ad}\rho(q^{-1})v$; its pairing is $\operatorname{tr}(v\operatorname{Ad}\rho(q)Z(x_j))$. For a negative letter the coefficient is $-\operatorname{Ad}\rho((qx_j^{-1})^{-1})v$, giving the corresponding negative term in $Z(qx_j^{-1})$. Summing the finite word gives exactly $\ell(c_w\otimes v)=\operatorname{tr}(vZ(w))$. For every relator $r$, the actual identity $\rho(r)=I$ implies $Z(r)=0$, so $\ell$ annihilates the entire relator-boundary image, not just its invariant-coefficient part. Restricting $\ell$ to cycles therefore gives a functional on the actual exterior $H_1$ through [F2]. For every loop fixing $v$ its value on the actual loop class is the displayed word pairing, with the prescribed initial coefficient. No identification of all character tangents with cohomology has been invoked. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Compute both peripheral pairings, including a moving eigenbasis.** Choose $Q\in GL_2(F)$ simultaneously diagonalizing the commuting peripheral pair in step1.1. In this basis $\widetilde u=Q^{-1}uQ=\operatorname{diag}(c,-c)$ with $2c=\eta-\eta^{-1}\ne0$, where $\eta$ is the eigenvalue of $\rho(\delta)$ on the chosen line. Write $H=Q^{-1}DQ$ and $\widetilde A=Q^{-1}AQ$. Differentiation gives $D\widetilde A\,\widetilde A^{-1}=Q^{-1}(DA\,A^{-1})Q+\operatorname{Ad}(\widetilde A)H-H$. Because $\widetilde u$ commutes with $\widetilde A$, the last two terms have zero trace pairing with $\widetilde u$. For $A=\rho(\lambda)$ the diagonal derivative has entries $DL/L,-DL/L$, so $\ell([u\otimes\lambda])=2c\,DL/L$. For $A=\rho(\mu)$ it similarly gives $\ell([u\otimes\mu])=2c\,DM/M$. The same factor $2c$ occurs for both; it is nonzero. This explicitly accounts for derivatives of the eigenbasis rather than assuming a constant diagonalization. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Identify the full peripheral conormal kernel.** By step2.1 at least one of $DL/L,DM/M$ is nonzero, so the functional in step4.1 is nonzero on the two-dimensional peripheral homology. It factors through its actual image in exterior homology by step3.1. Its kernel consequently contains $\ker i_*$. Both kernels are one-dimensional by step2.2 and the nonzero functional, hence they are equal. Translating $Df$ back to $\mathrm df=(Df)\mathrm dt$ gives exactly the Statement's kernel map. If its coefficients in the ordered basis are $(\alpha,\beta)=(DL/L,DM/M)$, the kernel is $F(-\beta,\alpha)$, and the slope $-\text{second}/\text{first}$ is $[\alpha:\beta]$. Rescaling $\mathrm dt$ or $u$ changes both coordinates by one common nonzero scalar. Swapping the common eigenline replaces both logarithmic differentials by their negatives, and preserves this projective point. Thus zero and infinity have not been lost by dividing by a potentially zero meridian differential. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **The Laurent equation gives the projective Gauss ratio.** Apply $D$ to the actual equation $P(M,L)=0$. It yields $(L P_L)(DL/L)+(M P_M)(DM/M)=0$. The two coefficients $L P_L,M P_M$ are not both zero at the generic point of $Y$. Indeed multiply $P$ by a Laurent monomial to make an irreducible polynomial $P_0$ not divisible by either coordinate: a nontrivial polynomial factorization would give a Laurent factorization, and the only additional Laurent units among polynomial factors are coordinate monomials. Suppose $P_0$ depends on both variables. Its content as a polynomial in either variable over the other polynomial ring is a unit, since a nonunit content would factor $P_0$. By [F8] it stays irreducible over that coefficient ring's fraction field. In characteristic zero its derivative in either occurring variable is nonzero and has smaller degree; hence it is not divisible by $P_0$, even over that fraction field. Its class in the coordinate domain of $Y$ is consequently nonzero. If $P_0$ depends on just one variable, irreducibility over $\mathbb C$ makes it linear in that coordinate, with a nonzero constant derivative. This proves the claim in all cases. Laurent units do not affect the derivative along $P=0$. Therefore the vector $(DL/L,DM/M)$ is proportional to $(M P_M,-L P_L)$, and its projective slope is $[-M P_M:L P_L]$. If $DM=0$, the equality and $DL\ne0$ give $P_L=0$, while $P_M\ne0$; the formula is infinity. The $DL=0$ case gives zero. These cases include meridian-constant and longitude-constant curve components. [F8, step 2.1, step 5.1, given, algebra]

7.1 **Descent and actual specialization, without changing the scope of a rational map.** Before adjoining the peripheral eigenvalues, the finite word matrices and invariant coefficient $u=\sigma(\delta)-\operatorname{tr}(\sigma(\delta))I/2$ are defined over $F_0$. The rank-one assertion of step2.2 holds over $F_0$ as well; equivalently compute ranks over $F_0$ and then extend scalars to $F$, which preserves them by finite elimination. A kernel generator is therefore obtained by finite field operations over $F_0$, as in [F5]. Field automorphisms commute with the word matrices and $u$, and simultaneous conjugation intertwines them while leaving the two input coordinates unchanged. The explicit quadratic descent in [F1] gives this projective kernel, and hence its slope, over $K$. It is independent of the model: on a common dense principal open actual specializations have identical irreducible characters and are conjugate by [F1], so the two rational maps agree on a dense set and their homogeneous cross products vanish identically. Applying [F5] to these actual exterior presentation and peripheral matrices, and clearing its finitely many denominators using [F1], gives a dense open on which this rational map is exactly the actual specialized homology slope. The equality with the Gauss pair is an equality of rational projective maps. At a point where that rational extension is evaluated and the Gauss pair is nonzero, equality persists in the local ring: choose a nonzero Gauss coordinate, whose ratio is regular there, and use the rational cross-product equality. This does not infer constancy of exterior homology ranks at points outside the rank-chart open. [F1, F2, F5, step 2.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 **Components of reducible characters and the full ordinary polynomial.** If $X$ contains no irreducible character, triangularize any representative over $\mathbb C$. Its diagonal entries give a homomorphism to $\mathbb C^*$, hence factor through the abelianization in [F7]. Its word traces are those of $\mu\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(m,m^{-1})$, with $\lambda\mapsto I$. The functions $m^n+m^{-n}$ are polynomials in $t=m+m^{-1}$ by the recurrence $T_{n+1}=tT_n-T_{n-1}$ with $T_0=2,T_1=t$. Conversely every complex $t$ has a root $m\ne0$ of $m^2-tm+1$. Since the meridian trace is a character coordinate, these characters form a closed irreducible affine line. Thus the one-dimensional component $X$ consisting of them is that line. Over its quadratic field $F=K(m)$ take this actual diagonal generic representation. The globally fixed diagonal coefficient line is a direct summand of its adjoint local system; its homology is ordinary homology with $F$ coefficients. By [F7] the meridian class there is nonzero and the longitude class zero. The actual torus basis [F3] therefore has kernel the longitude line and slope zero. Here $L=1$, $M=m$ is nonconstant and the whole differential and conormal conclusion is $[0:\mathrm dM/M]=[0:1]$, agreeing with $P=L-1$. Finally, for the square-free ordinary polynomial write $A=P R$ near a point of $Y$. If another factor also vanishes, both partial derivatives of $A$ vanish there. At a point with nonzero gradient, $R\ne0$ and $(A_M,A_L)=R(P_M,P_L)$, so the same projective ratio results. A Laurent-unit factor has the same effect because its differentiated term contains $P$. All formulas retain the stated variable and boundary-basis orders. Only this affine conormal calculation and its explicit rank-chart specializations have been proved; no ideal-point or essential-surface assertion has been inferred. [F3, F7, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
