## Open problem

If a nontrivial knot $K\subset S^3$ has exactly two distinct boundary slopes, must $K$ be a torus knot? The two slopes of a nontrivial torus knot $T_{p,q}$ are $0$ and $pq$ in the meridian-longitude convention where $a[\lambda]+b[\mu]$ has slope $b/a$.

## Open problem references

[Baldwin and Sivek, discussion following Question 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.19197v2)

## Status

Open. Baldwin–Sivek describe the two-boundary-slope statement as a folklore conjecture and give only a partial result for nontrivial fibered non-torus knots. A web search on 2026-10-19 found 2-bridge partial results but no resolution for all knots. No proof is asserted here.
