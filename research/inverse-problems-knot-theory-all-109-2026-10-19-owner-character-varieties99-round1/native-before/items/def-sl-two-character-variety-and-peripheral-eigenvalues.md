## Definition

Assume full AC. Let $G=\pi_1(E_K)$ be the group of a smooth oriented knot exterior, with positive meridian $\mu$ and preferred longitude $\lambda$. For a finite presentation $G=\langle g_1,\ldots,g_n\mid r_1,\ldots,r_m\rangle$, the **$SL_2(\mathbb C)$ representation variety** is the algebraic set of tuples $(A_1,\ldots,A_n)\in SL_2(\mathbb C)^n$ for which every relator matrix $r_j(A_1,\ldots,A_n)$ equals the identity. The group $SL_2(\mathbb C)$ acts on this set by simultaneous conjugation. Its affine categorical quotient, whose coordinate ring is the invariant ring of regular functions, is the **$SL_2(\mathbb C)$ character variety** $X(E_K)$. For a representation $\rho$, its character is the function $\gamma\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(\rho(\gamma))$. A representation is **reducible** if its matrices preserve a common one-dimensional subspace of $\mathbb C^2$; otherwise it is irreducible. The peripheral torus group is $\pi_1(T_K)\cong\mathbb Z^2$, generated by the meridian and preferred longitude; its image in $G$ is generated by their commuting classes and need not be isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^2$. Their image under any representation is a commuting pair of matrices, so it has a common invariant line and is simultaneously triangularizable over $\mathbb C$. Choosing a common invariant line gives meridian and longitude eigenvalues $(M,L)\in(\mathbb C^*)^2$; the unordered boundary character identifies $(M,L)$ with $(M^{-1},L^{-1})$. Simultaneous diagonalizability is stronger than simultaneous triangularizability.
