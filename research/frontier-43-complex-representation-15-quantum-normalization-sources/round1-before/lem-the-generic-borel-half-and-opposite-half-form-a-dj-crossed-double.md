## Statement

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum for a quantum group, let $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ be the Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra of [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]], and let $U_q^-,U_q^0,U_q^+$ be the $\mathbb Q(q)$-subalgebras generated by the $F_i$, the $K_h$ and the $E_i$ ([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

(a) **The crossed double presented by cross-relations.** Let $W:=U_q^-\oplus U_q^0\oplus U_q^+$ be the direct sum of the three underlying vector spaces, let $T(W)$ be its tensor algebra with unit $1_{T(W)}$ (the empty word), and let $J\subseteq T(W)$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the following elements:

$$u\otimes v-uv\qquad(u,v\ \text{in one common factor }U_q^-,U_q^0,U_q^+),$$
$$1_{U_q^-}-1_{T(W)},\qquad 1_{U_q^0}-1_{T(W)},\qquad 1_{U_q^+}-1_{T(W)},$$
$$K_hE_i-q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_iK_h,\qquad K_hF_i-q^{-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_iK_h\qquad(h\in P^{\vee},\ i\in I),$$
$$E_iF_j-F_jE_i-\delta_{ij}\frac{K_i-K_i^{-1}}{q_i-q_i^{-1}}\qquad(i,j\in I),$$

where in the last three lines the symbols denote the corresponding elements of $W$. Then $D:=T(W)/J$ is an associative unital $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra, and the inclusions of the three factors define unital algebra homomorphisms $\iota_-:U_q^-\to D$, $\iota_0:U_q^0\to D$, $\iota_+:U_q^+\to D$. The algebra $D$ has the following **universal property**: for every unital $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra $B$ and every triple of unital algebra homomorphisms $f_-:U_q^-\to B$, $f_0:U_q^0\to B$, $f_+:U_q^+\to B$ whose images satisfy

$$\text{(b)}\quad f_0(K_h)f_\pm(x)f_0(K_h)^{-1}=q^{\pm\beta(h)}f_\pm(x)\quad(x\in U_q^\pm[\pm\beta]\ \text{homogeneous},\ h\in P^{\vee}),$$
$$\text{(c)}\quad f_+(E_i)f_-(F_j)-f_-(F_j)f_+(E_i)=\delta_{ij}\frac{f_0(K_i)-f_0(K_i^{-1})}{q_i-q_i^{-1}}\qquad(i,j\in I),$$

there is a unique unital algebra homomorphism $F:D\to B$ with $F\circ\iota_-=f_-$, $F\circ\iota_0=f_0$, $F\circ\iota_+=f_+$. In particular $D$ is determined by this property up to a unique isomorphism.

(b) **Toral cross-relations in $D$.** For every $h\in P^{\vee}$ and every homogeneous $x\in U_q^\pm[\pm\beta]$, $\iota_0(K_h)\iota_\pm(x)\iota_0(K_h)^{-1}=q^{\pm\beta(h)}\iota_\pm(x)$; also $\iota_0(K_h)^{-1}=\iota_0(K_{-h})$.

(c) **Identification with $U_q(\mathfrak g)$.** The assignments $\psi(E_i):=\iota_+(E_i)$, $\psi(F_i):=\iota_-(F_i)$, $\psi(K_h):=\iota_0(K_h)$ extend uniquely to algebra homomorphisms $\psi:U_q(\mathfrak g)\to D$ and $\phi:D\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ with $\phi(\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+))=x_-x_0x_+$ for all $x_-\in U_q^-$, $x_0\in U_q^0$, $x_+\in U_q^+$. They are mutually inverse, so $D\cong U_q(\mathfrak g)$ as $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebras and $\iota_-,\iota_0,\iota_+$ are injective.

(d) **Spanning.** Every element of $D$ is a finite sum of products $\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+)$; equivalently, the linear map $U_q^-\otimes U_q^0\otimes U_q^+\to D$, $x_-\otimes x_0\otimes x_+\mapsto\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+)$ is surjective.

**Scope of the claim.** Neither the injectivity of the map in (d) -- equivalently, the identification of the underlying vector space of $D$ with $U_q^-\otimes U_q^0\otimes U_q^+$ and the freeness of this presentation as a crossed (Drinfeld) double -- nor the description of the general cross product $\iota_+(x)\iota_-(y)$ by the pairing formula of the Drinfeld double is proved here; the proof uses neither the formal shuffle model nor the Hopf pairing. Both statements are the open inputs of [[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]. The construction uses no choice principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite symmetrizable Cartan datum, the Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ and its three generated subalgebras.

[F1] $U_q(\mathfrak g)=T(V)/I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$, where $T(V)$ is free on the symbols $E_i,F_i,K_h$ and $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ is generated by the toral relations $K_0-1$, $K_hK_{h'}-K_{h+h'}$, by $K_hE_i-q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_iK_h$ and $K_hF_i-q^{-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_iK_h$, by $E_iF_j-F_jE_i-\delta_{ij}(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$, and by both quantum Serre sums; $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ has the induced universal property, and $K_i^{-1}=K_{-d_ih_i}$ ([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F2] $U_q^\pm,U_q^0$ are the subalgebras generated by the $E_i$, the $F_i$, the $K_h$; $U_q^\pm$ is spanned by finite monomials in its generators (including the empty monomial $1$), $U_q^0$ is spanned by the $K_h$, and $K_hxK_h^{-1}=q^{\beta(h)}x$ for every $x\in U_q^\pm[\pm\beta]$ ([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F3] The tensor algebra $T(W)$ has the universal property that every linear map $W\to B$ into a unital associative algebra extends uniquely to a unital algebra homomorphism $T(W)\to B$; in particular every element of $T(W)$ is a finite linear combination of words in elements of $W$ ([[def-tensor-algebra-of-a-vector-space]], [[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]).

[F4] For a two-sided ideal $J$ of an associative unital algebra $T$, maps out of $T/J$ correspond bijectively to maps out of $T$ whose kernel contains $J$, and quotients of associative algebras are associative ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[def-quotient-ring]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[F5] The datum gives $\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle\in\mathbb Z$, $q_i=q^{d_i}$ with $d_i>0$, so $q_i-q_i^{-1}\ne0$ in $\mathbb Q(q)$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** Construct $D$ as a presented algebra and record its universal property; build comparison maps in both directions from the universal properties of $T(W)$ and $U_q(\mathfrak g)$; show that they are mutually inverse on generators; then transfer the toral cross-relations and the spanning property from generators by multiplicativity and a finite rearrangement induction.

1.1 Define $D:=T(W)/J$ and let $\pi:T(W)\to D$ be the quotient map; write $\iota_-,\iota_0,\iota_+$ for $\pi$ composed with the inclusions of the three factors, and $1_D:=\pi(1_{T(W)})$. By [F4] $D$ is associative and unital, and the maps $\iota$ are unital algebra homomorphisms: multiplicativity is the relation $u\otimes v-uv\in J$ and unitality is the relation $1_{U_q^\bullet}-1_{T(W)}\in J$. [F3, F4, construct]

1.2 Universal property of $D$. Let $B$ and $f_-,f_0,f_+$ be as in the statement, and let $g:W\to B$ be the linear map that is $f_-,f_0,f_+$ on the three summands. By [F3] $g$ extends uniquely to a unital algebra homomorphism $\tilde g:T(W)\to B$. Every listed generator of $J$ lies in $\ker\tilde g$: the relations $u\otimes v-uv$ and $1_{U_q^\bullet}-1_{T(W)}$ because $f_\bullet$ are unital algebra homomorphisms, the two cross-relations because of (b) and (c). Hence $J\subseteq\ker\tilde g$, and [F4] gives a unique unital algebra homomorphism $F:D\to B$ with $F\circ\pi=\tilde g$, i.e. $F\circ\iota_\bullet=f_\bullet$. Since a homomorphism with the required properties must take the prescribed values on the images of $W$, which generate $D$, it is unique. This also shows that a second algebra with the same universal property is canonically isomorphic to $D$. [F3, F4, given]

1.3 Construction of $\phi:D\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$. Let $g_0:W\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ be the linear map given by the three inclusions. Each listed generator of $J$ has image zero: for $u,v$ in one factor, $uv$ denotes the product in that factor, which coincides with the product in $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ because the factor is a subalgebra, so $u\otimes v-uv\mapsto uv-uv=0$; the unit relations map to $1-1=0$; the cross-relations map to the defining relations of $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$ in [F1]. By [F3,F4] there is a unique unital algebra homomorphism $\phi:D\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ with $\phi\circ\iota_\bullet=g_0$, that is, $\phi(\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+))=x_-x_0x_+$. [F1, F3, F4, algebra]

1.4 Construction of $\psi:U_q(\mathfrak g)\to D$. Define elements $E_i':=\iota_+(E_i)$, $F_j':=\iota_-(F_j)$, $K_h':=\iota_0(K_h)$ of $D$. By [F1] it suffices to check the generators of $I_{\mathrm{DJ}}$. The toral relation $K_0-1$ holds because $K_0=1$ in $U_q^0$ and $\iota_0$ is unital; $K_h'K_{h'}'-K_{h+h'}'=0$ because $\iota_0$ is multiplicative and $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$ in $U_q^0$. The relations $K_h'E_i'-q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_i'K_h'$ and $K_h'F_i'-q^{-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_i'K_h'$ are the imposed relations (b) of $J$, and $E_i'F_j'-F_j'E_i'-\delta_{ij}(K_i'-K_i'^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})=0$ is the imposed relation (c). Finally the Serre sums hold: the element $\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij}\in U_q^+$ is zero in $U_q(\mathfrak g)$, hence zero in the subalgebra $U_q^+$; since $\iota_+$ is an algebra homomorphism, the corresponding polynomial in the $E_i'$ equals $\iota_+(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})=0$, and likewise for $\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}$ with $\iota_-$. By the universal property [F1] there is a unique unital algebra homomorphism $\psi:U_q(\mathfrak g)\to D$ with $\psi(E_i)=E_i'$, $\psi(F_i)=F_i'$, $\psi(K_h)=K_h'$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 $\phi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}_{U_q(\mathfrak g)}$. Both sides are unital algebra homomorphisms $U_q(\mathfrak g)\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$; they agree on the generators $E_i,F_i,K_h$ by 1.3 and 1.4, and the generators generate. [step 1.3, step 1.4, algebra]

2.2 Toral cross-relations. The multiplication rule $K_hE_i=q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_iK_h$ in $D$ is the imposed relation, and $K_h^{-1}=K_{-h}$ in $U_q^0$ gives $\iota_0(K_h)^{-1}=\iota_0(K_{-h})$, so $\iota_0(K_h)\iota_+(E_i)\iota_0(K_h)^{-1}=q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}\iota_+(E_i)$; the same computation gives the $F_i$-case. For a monomial $x$ of degree $\beta$ in the $E_i$ (respectively $F_i$), multiplicativity of $\iota_+$ (respectively $\iota_-$) and additivity of $h\mapsto\langle\beta,h\rangle$ give $\iota_0(K_h)\iota_\pm(x)\iota_0(K_h)^{-1}=q^{\pm\beta(h)}\iota_\pm(x)$; an arbitrary homogeneous element of $U_q^\pm[\pm\beta]$ is a $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear combination of such monomials, so the formula extends by linearity. [step 1.1, F2, F5, algebra]

3.1 $\psi\circ\phi=\mathrm{id}_D$. Both sides are unital algebra homomorphisms $D\to D$. For $x\in U_q^-$, write $x$ as a finite linear combination of monomials in the $F_i$; since $\psi$ and $\iota_-$ are multiplicative and agree on the $F_i$, $\psi(x)=\iota_-(x)$, and similarly $\psi(x_0)=\iota_0(x_0)$ and $\psi(x_+)=\iota_+(x_+)$ for $x_0\in U_q^0$, $x_+\in U_q^+$. Hence $\psi(\phi(\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+)))=\psi(x_-x_0x_+)=\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+)$. The elements $\iota_-(U_q^-)$, $\iota_0(U_q^0)$, $\iota_+(U_q^+)$ generate $D$ because their union contains the images of $W$; hence the two homomorphisms agree on all of $D$. Together with 2.1 this proves (c) and the injectivity of $\iota_-,\iota_0,\iota_+$, since $\phi\circ\iota_-\!, \phi\circ\iota_0,\phi\circ\iota_+$ are the inclusions. [step 1.3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Spanning. Let $S$ be the span of the products $\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+)$; we prove by induction on the length of a word in elements of the images of $W$ that every such word lies in $S$. The empty word is $1=\iota_-(1)\iota_0(1)\iota_+(1)$. For the induction step write the word as $p\cdot q$ with $p\in S$ and $q$ one further factor element, and treat the three cases for $q$. If $q=\iota_+(e)$, then $\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\iota_+(x_+e)\in S$ by multiplicativity of $\iota_+$. If $q=\iota_0(K)$, then 2.2 applied homogeneous piece by homogeneous piece gives $\iota_+(x_+)\iota_0(K)=\iota_0(K)\iota_+(x_+')$ for a suitable $x_+'\in U_q^+$, so the product is $\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0K)\iota_+(x_+')\in S$; the same applies with $\iota_-$ in place of $\iota_+$. If $q=\iota_-(y)$, it suffices by linearity to treat monomials $x_+$ and $y$. Moving the letters of $y$ one at a time to the left past the letters of the monomial $x_+$ and rewriting each adjacent copy of $E_iF_j$ by the imposed relation $E_iF_j=F_jE_i+\delta_{ij}(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$ expresses $\iota_+(x_+)\iota_-(y)$ as a finite sum of products $\iota_-(y')\iota_0(K)\iota_+(x_+')$: each rewrite strictly decreases the number of pairs (an $E$-letter to the left of an $F$-letter), so the procedure terminates, and the $K$-factors produced are moved to the middle using 2.2. Hence the whole product is $\iota_-(x_-)\iota_0(x_0)\bigl(\sum\iota_-(y')\iota_0(K)\iota_+(x_+')\bigr)$; moving each $\iota_-(y')$ to the left past $\iota_0(x_0)$ by 2.2 and absorbing it with $\iota_-(x_-)$ exhibits every term in $S$. Every element of $D$ is a finite $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear combination of such words by [F3], so the linear map of (d) is surjective. [step 2.2, F3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The presentation of $D$ by the three factor algebras and the cross-relations (b),(c) is the algebra presented by the Drinfeld double of the Borel (Enriquez §2.3, Berkeley Definition 13.1.3.12). What is proved above is its universal property and its identification with $U_q(\mathfrak g)$; the tensor-product freeness of the presentation is deferred to [[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]].
