## A local padding lemma that repairs one earlier worry

There is a useful *definable* long-concatenation principle that follows from ESTT and pseudofiniteness, provided the local tree contract has the empty node and the usual restriction operation (both tacit in the source's finite-sequence arguments). Fix an old order `b`, a definable map `g:[0,L)_b→X_b` with parameters in `M_1^+`, and `L≤d_b`. Let

`P(n) := ∃t∈T_b [lg_b(t)=n ∧ ∀i<n (t(i)=g(i))]`, for `0≤n≤L`.

`P` is first-order definable, `P(0)` holds, and ESTT's one-step extension makes `P(n)⇒P(n+1)` whenever `n<L`. If some `n≤L` failed, pseudofiniteness would supply the least failing `n`; it cannot be zero or a successor after a successful predecessor. Thus `P(L)`. Tree extensionality makes the resulting node unique. This argument does **not** construct a node for an arbitrary *external* map; it applies individually to every internally definable `g`, with arbitrary fixed parameters. In particular, the nonstandard gaps between successive pairing boundaries are not a padding obstruction for such a map. The previous audit's “one-step concatenation cannot fill the gaps” concern should be read as a missing displayed argument, not as a failure of this definable route.

Here is the precise pair-function use. In Definition 2.3(6), let `a₀` be the distinguished order, `c₀` its special square product, `Pr` its pairing bijection, and `y(j)` the unique boundary satisfying `Pr([0,j]_{a₀}²)=[0,y(j)]_{c₀}`. The map `y` is definable and strictly increasing. Apply a corrected Lemma 5.3 to obtain a codeable initial segment `c'=[0,R)_{c₀}` with bound `d_{c'}`. Since `y(n)<R,d_{c'}` and `n<d_{a₀}` for every standard finite `n`, pseudofiniteness yields nonstandard `r∈X_{a₀}` with `r<d_{a₀}` and `y(r)<R,d_{c'}`. Put `a=[0,r]_{a₀}` and `c=[0,y(r)]_{c₀}`, so `Pr(X_a²)=X_c` and the restricted pair order is an **initial segment**, not a general rectangle.

For **each** internally definable `f:[0,r]_a→[0,r]_a²`, define `G:[0,y(r)]_{c₀}→X_c` by `G(y(j))=Pr(f(j))` for `j≤r` and `G(i)=Pr(0,0)` at other indices. Its graph is first-order definable from that of `f`; the padding lemma gives the unique node `t_f∈T_{c'}` of length `y(r)+1` with these values. A uniform decoder reads `Pr^{-1}(t_f(y(j)))=f(j)`, so distinct `f` yield distinct nodes and the corrected Lemma 5.3 code gives distinct least codes in `X_d`. This establishes the **individual definable-function** interpretation used by the later proof; it does not assert that every external map has a code, nor a first-order quantifier over all formulas defining `f`.

The same construction supplies the `a`-coverability clause by the **same** `d`, conditionally on the induced initial-segment trees: for `η∈T_a` of length `ℓ≤r`, set `G_η(y(j))=Pr(η(j),ℓ)` for `j<ℓ` and the fixed default elsewhere. Since `ℓ>0` makes `Pr(η(j),ℓ)` distinct from the default, the embedded node determines both `ℓ` and every entry; `ℓ=0` has the unique all-default image. This is a first-order uniform injection `T_a→T_{c'}→X_d`. Likewise `T_c⊆T_{c'}` under the induced initial-segment interpretation, so `d` covers the special square pair order. The length/ceiling condition `y(r)<d_{c'}` is indispensable. (The generic term “Gödel codes for functions” in Corollary 5.7 remains ambiguous if read as a quantification over functions not represented by a definable graph or an internal tree node.)

## Threshold preservation still needs a product-closure proof

For a **single** definable subset `Y⊆X_b` whose relevant elements lie at ambient indices `<d_b`, the padding lemma also suggests an induced tree: encode a `Y`-indexed sequence at its successive ambient positions and use a fixed default in gaps. One-step extension across a possibly nonstandard ambient gap follows from the lemma, and an ambient definable-subtree embedding would carry chains of length `<t_s` to old treetops. This strengthens the prior audit's restricted-tree sketch. Its hypothesis cannot simply be dropped for the full Corollary 3.9 expansion.

The exact universal obligation is: **for every** pair of newly admitted definable suborders `Y⊆X_u`, `Z⊆X_v`, exhibit an order `q∈Or(s')` with `X_q=Y×Z`, a nonstandard `d_q`, and a definable initial-segment-preserving tree embedding `T_q` into an old `T_w` (or otherwise prove directly that every chain of regular length `<t_s` in `T_q` has an upper bound). Definition 2.3(5) only supplies an arbitrary old product order `w` with a nonstandard `d_w`; it does not require `Y×Z` to meet the old initial interval `[0,d_w]` cofinally or even in a nonstandard-length set. The special square axiom 2.3(6) fixes this for the **one** distinguished square above, not for arbitrary products. The elementary finite pattern of ordering the complement of a proper rectangle first and the rectangle after `d_w` shows why the naive choice `d_q=max((Y×Z)∩[0,d_w])` need not be nonstandard; this is a counterexample to that proposed bound-selection argument, not to Corollary 3.9 itself.

Corollary 3.8/3.9 states preservation of lower cofinality in a stated range and simply says the enlarged family remains a CSP; it does not prove `t_{s'}=t_s` or supply the product-tree embedding. The earlier draft's Discussion 3.48 flags a related small-tree invariance proof as omitted. Consequently the new padding observation closes the **local definable pair-map and unary-cover coding step, conditional on the selected initial-segment interpretation**, but not the global ESTT/product and treetop-threshold proof needed to turn the expansion into a complete locally authorable `p=t` Step-1 chain. No readiness conclusion follows.
