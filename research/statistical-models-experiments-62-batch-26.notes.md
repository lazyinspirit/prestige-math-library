- Read the complete relevant statements/proofs for the conditional-independence definition, conditional-expectation versions, conditional-expectation algebra, tower property, monotone convergence, integrability and expectation. The published conditional-independence definition is bounded-function only. The local lemma proves the integrable extension by truncation, L1 convergence, the conditional-expectation L1 contraction derived from the order bound and expectation preservation, and uniqueness of an L1 limit. AC is named and cited where used.
- The weighting theorem fixes a probability space, integrable real Y, binary response R, G=sigma(X), the true version pi=E[R|G], and pi>0 almost surely. For nonnegative S=|Y|, Y+, Y-, it first establishes the bounded simple-weight identity, then applies monotone convergence to truncated reciprocal weights and the conditional-expectation tower. The S=|Y| case proves absolute integrability of RY/pi; subtracting positive and negative parts then proves the signed identity. The source conditional-independence premise is never applied directly to unbounded Y.
- The algorithm is a statistic on supplied records and known positive probabilities. It does not assume iid sampling, fitted probabilities, finite-sample stability or estimated-weight inference. A joint treatment/response weight is not defined.
- The pattern-mixture definition assumes standard-Borel X, real Y, positive mass in both response groups, and respondent-covariate support covering the nonrespondent support. It states the setwise overlap condition explicitly. Standard-Borel disintegration supplies the respondent kernel under AC; the translation is a probability kernel by pushforward and kernel-integration measurability. A finite mean target additionally needs integrability under the induced law.
- The finite B laws were checked by direct atom sums. For 0<epsilon<=1, the weighted contribution is 1/epsilon with probability epsilon/2 and zero otherwise, yielding mean 1/2 and variance 1/(2 epsilon)-1/4. At epsilon=0, two full-data laws have the same observed law and means 0 and 1/2. In the sensitivity family, P(R=1,Y=0)=0.3, P(R=1,Y=1)=0.2, and P(R=0)=0.5 stay fixed while mu(Delta)=0.4+0.5 Delta. For Delta>0, the missing-pattern response probability varies across Y=0 and Y=Delta; at Delta=0 the respondent/nonrespondent laws agree. The interval of induced target values is not assigned a coverage interpretation.
