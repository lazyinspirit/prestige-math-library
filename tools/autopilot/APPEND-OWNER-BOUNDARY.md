# Native append planning under owner supervision

`owner-supervised-compact.mts` leaves native `1-append-drift-N` and
`1-append-apply-N` stages intact. Their native reviewer, metadata recertification,
plan validation, manifest integrity, and drift-application gates still run.
These are planning boundaries for explicitly appended pairs, before their new
carriers exist. They do not certify mathematical authoring or resolve an older
frontier's proof escalations.

The original planning stages and every authored-content battery keep their
owner-supervised holds. Step 1 cannot close until its actual current artifacts,
readiness records, preparation, and complete native gates pass. Appending never
changes worker concurrency or grants publication authority.

The immutable `1-baseline` stage may sit between original drift application
and scaffolding. Appended planning is inserted after that baseline; its existing
snapshot and history are retained.

Metadata recertification preserves a prior page that is explicitly empty in
both its original manifest and current plan. It records the page separately as
an outstanding authoring obligation, grants no item/readiness credit, and still
rejects missing inventory where the plan declares items.

Append planning selects only the explicitly appended pages for metadata and
plan validation, retaining the full plan and actual prerequisite inputs as
context. Existing manifests remain guarded by native preimage/integrity checks;
old content escalations stay held. This follows the owner's minimal-check
direction and avoids turning a new-pair planning gate into a whole-run audit.

Owner drift resolutions continue to bind the immutable original native report.
When native append materialization adds reviewed sections, the loader verifies
each exact report/preimage/merged digest against its native apply receipt and
reconstructs the entire current report. Unrecorded edits still fail closed.
The owner separately refreshes current planning scope and document bindings;
this grants no new finite computation exception or content acceptance.
