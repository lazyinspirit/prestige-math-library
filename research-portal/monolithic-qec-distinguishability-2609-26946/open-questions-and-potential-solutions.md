## 1. Research insights beyond the article

### Research insight 01 · Jump-time variance controls recoverable phase loss

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proposed

Let $S$ have a Borel probability law supported on a bounded interval $[a,b]$, let $S'$ be an independent copy, and set $v=\operatorname{Var}(S)$ and $\phi(\delta)=\mathbb E e^{i\delta S}$ for real $\delta$. On a logical qubit define $\mathcal D_\phi$ to preserve populations and multiply the $01$ coherence by $\phi$. For $0\le p\le1$, consider the orthogonally flagged channel $\mathcal N(X)=(1-p)X\oplus p\mathcal D_\phi(X)$. Its support-completed transpose recovery, with whole-input code and maximally mixed input $I/2$, has entanglement infidelity $L_{\rm tr}=p(1-|\phi|^2)/2$. It satisfies $0\le L_{\rm tr}\le\min\{p/2,p\delta^2v/2\}$ and the explicit remainder bound $0\le p\delta^2v/2-L_{\rm tr}\le p\delta^4\mathbb E(S-S')^4/48$. Consequently, for a fixed timing law and fixed $p$, $L_{\rm tr}=p\delta^2v/2+O(\delta^4)$. The quadratic coefficient is sharp, deterministic shifts of $S$ do not change the loss, and $L_{\rm tr}\le p\delta^2(b-a)^2/8$. Conditional on the article's four-level decay postulate, the dwell-time density is $\gamma e^{-\gamma(\tau-s)}/(1-e^{-\gamma\tau})$ on $[0,\tau]$, with variance $\gamma^{-2}-\tau^2e^{-\gamma\tau}/(1-e^{-\gamma\tau})^2$ for $\gamma,\tau>0$. At fixed $\gamma,\delta$, this gives $L_{\rm tr}=\gamma\delta^2\tau^3/24+O(\tau^4)$.

#### Why it matters

The loss separates into a branch probability and uncertainty in the phase accumulated within that branch. This gives a quantitative bound for arbitrary bounded timing laws, explains why a deterministic delay is harmless to transpose fidelity, and identifies the variance underlying the four-level cubic coefficient.

#### Argument and next steps

For an independent copy $S'$, bounded product integration gives $|\phi|^2=\mathbb E\cos(\delta(S-S'))$ and $\mathbb E(S-S')^2=2v$. The elementary inequality $0\le x^2/2-(1-\cos x)\le x^4/24$ yields both the variance bound and its remainder. Independently, support normalization makes transpose recovery act as the identity on the first positive-weight flag and as $\mathcal D_{\overline\phi}$ on the second. Composition therefore leaves coherence $|\phi|^2$ on that branch, giving the stated entanglement infidelity. For the four-level postulate, direct matrix evolution produces the normalized dwell-time mixture; integration of its first two moments supplies the variance and cubic coefficient without using the article's fidelity claim as proof.

#### Research question

#### Potential solution

**Solution scope:** full

**Evidence status:** Proposed approach

For the explicit recovery in F3, with every unused outcome reset to $I_2/2$, fix $\gamma>0$, $T>0$ and $|\Delta_0|\le D<\infty$. For either displayed code and $\tau=T/N$, let $\mathcal Q(X)=\operatorname{tr}(X)I_2/2$ and $A=(\gamma^2/2)(\mathcal Q-\mathrm{id})$. Then $S_\tau=\mathrm{id}+\tau^2A+O_D(\tau^3)$ and $S_\tau^N=\exp(T\tau A)+O_{T,D}(\tau^2)$ in diamond norm. Thus the first nonzero per-physical-time logarithmic generator is $\tau^{-1}\log S_\tau=\tau A+O_D(\tau^2)$, and the maximally mixed logical entanglement infidelity is $1-F_e(S_\tau^N)=3\gamma^2T\tau/8+O_{T,D}(\tau^2)$. Both leading coefficients are independent of the unknown lift and identical for the two codes.
