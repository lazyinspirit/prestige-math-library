## Statement

Under the thermal-envelope hypotheses, let $\chi_m:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb C$ be Borel, $|\chi_m|\leq1$, and $|\chi_m(\omega)|^2\to1$ almost everywhere. Write $f_t$ and $W$ for the functions in the envelope lemma. Then $\int |\chi_m|^2 f_t\to\int f_t$, uniformly for all real $t$, with error bounded by $\int(1-|\chi_m|^2)W\to0$. In particular the compact-shell choices $\chi_m=1_{[1/m,m]}$ suffice. If the integrability hypothesis fails, this lemma asserts no unregulated correlation or distributional limit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The thermal-envelope hypotheses, including Countable Choice and its Borel measure conventions, and the cutoff functions in the Statement.

[F1] The envelope supplies the measure on $I=(0,\infty)$, its Borel conventions and inherited Countable Choice hypothesis, the formulas for $N_\beta,f_t,W$, measurable $W\geq0$ with $\int W=M<\infty$, and absolutely integrable $f_t$ for every real $t$. [[lem-author-ef1b96c593db-thermal-integrable-envelope]]

[F2] Dominated convergence applies to measurable complex functions under one nonnegative integrable majorant. [[math:thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F3] The modulus of an integral is bounded by the integral of its modulus for an integrable complex function. [[math:thm-integral-triangle-inequality]]

[F4] The integral is complex-linear on integrable functions. [[math:thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets; a measurable map has measurable inverse images of all measurable codomain sets. [[math:def-borel-sigma-algebra]] [[math:def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

[F6] The real indicator of a measurable set is measurable. [[math:prop-indicator-function-is-measurable-iff-its-set-is-measurable]]

[F7] Continuous maps have Borel preimages, measurable maps compose, and maps into $\mathbb R^n$ are measurable exactly when all real coordinates are measurable. [[math:thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]] [[math:thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]] [[math:thm-coordinatewise-measurability-into-r-n]]

[F8] Finite sums and products of continuous real-valued maps are continuous. [[math:lem-algebra-of-continuous-real-maps-on-a-space]]

[F9] The nonnegative integral is monotone, and a measurable real or complex function with finite integral of its modulus is integrable. [[math:prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]] [[math:def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]

[F10] The real exponential is strictly increasing, and $|e^{ix}|=1$ for real $x$. [[math:thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]] [[math:cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]

[F11] Complex modulus is multiplicative and satisfies the triangle inequality. [[math:lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]

## Proof

**Goal:** Prove the uniform cutoff-error estimate, its convergence to zero, and applicability of the compact-shell indicators.

1.1 Put $q_m=|\chi_m|^2$, $h_m=(1-q_m)W$, $g_{m,t}=q_m f_t$, and $r_{m,t}=(q_m-1)f_t$. The real and imaginary parts of $\chi_m$ and $f_t$ are Borel by [F1], [F5] and [F7]. For $\chi_m=a_m+ib_m$, the polynomial $a_m^2+b_m^2$ gives Borel $q_m$: the coordinate tuple is measurable, the polynomial is continuous by [F8], and composition is measurable by [F7]. The same argument applied to the tuples of real coordinates of $q_m,f_t,W$ gives Borel $h_m$ and both real coordinates of $g_{m,t},r_{m,t}$; the coordinate criterion then gives their complex measurability. Modulus is continuous because [F11] gives $\big||z|-|w|\big|\leq|z-w|$, so their moduli are also Borel by [F7]. All these functions are finite-valued. [F1, F5, F7, F8, F11, algebra]

2.1 Since $\beta\omega>0$, [F10] gives $e^{\beta\omega}>e^0=1$ and hence $N_\beta(\omega)>0$. The formula in [F1] and the unit modulus of both phases give $|f_t|\leq|\kappa|^2\omega^2[N_\beta+(N_\beta+1)]=W$ by [F11]. Since $0\leq q_m\leq1$, we obtain $|g_{m,t}|\leq W$ and $|r_{m,t}|\leq h_m\leq W$. Step 1.1 supplies measurability, so monotonicity in [F9] gives $\int|g_{m,t}|\leq M$, $\int|r_{m,t}|\leq M$, and $\int h_m\leq M$. Thus these cutoff products and error densities are integrable by [F9]; $f_t$ is integrable by [F1]. [F1, F9, F10, F11, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By linearity on the integrable functions just established and by the integral triangle inequality, $\left|\int q_m f_t-\int f_t\right|=\left|\int r_{m,t}\right|\leq\int|r_{m,t}|\leq\int h_m$. The last comparison uses [F9]. This bound holds for every real $t$ with the same right-hand side. [F3, F4, F9, step 2.1]

4.1 The hypothesis $q_m\to1$ almost everywhere and finiteness of $W$ give $h_m\to0$ almost everywhere. The functions $h_m$ and the zero limit are measurable, and $0\leq h_m\leq W$ with $\int W=M<\infty$. Hence [F2] yields $\int h_m\to0$. Together with step 3.1, this is uniform convergence on the entire real time axis with the asserted error bound. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 For every positive integer $m$, the interval $[1/m,m]$ is closed in $I$: its complement is the union of two relatively open intervals. It is therefore Borel by [F5]. Its real indicator is measurable by [F6], and adjoining its identically zero imaginary coordinate makes it Borel into $\mathbb C\simeq\mathbb R^2$ by [F7]. It is bounded by one, and for each $\omega>0$ every sufficiently large integer $m$ satisfies $1/m\leq\omega\leq m$; thus its squared modulus converges pointwise to one. These shell cutoffs satisfy all hypotheses of the preceding steps. If $W$ is nonintegrable, the domination hypothesis used here fails and the lemma makes no claim about unregulated or distributional limits. [F1, F5, F6, F7, step 4.1] ∎
