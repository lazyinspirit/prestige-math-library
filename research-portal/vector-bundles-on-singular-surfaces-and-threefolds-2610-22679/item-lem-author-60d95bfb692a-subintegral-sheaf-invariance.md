## Statement
If $p:T\to X$ is a finite subintegral morphism of separated finite-type schemes over a field and $F$ is a set-valued cdh sheaf, then restriction $F(X)\to F(T)$ is a bijection. In particular this applies to the finite seminormalization map when it belongs to this category.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $p$ and $F$ as stated.

**Goal:** Prove bijectivity of restriction.

[F1] The singleton $\{p\}$ is a cdh cover. [lem-author-60d95bfb692a-finite-subintegral-cdh-cover](item-lem-author-60d95bfb692a-finite-subintegral-cdh-cover)

[F2] Every $s\in F(T)$ has equal pullbacks to $T\times_X T$. [lem-author-60d95bfb692a-subintegral-matching](item-lem-author-60d95bfb692a-subintegral-matching)

[F3] A sheaf has unique amalgamations for covering sieves; restriction to a covering singleton is injective. [def-scheme-sites-and-sheafification](item-def-scheme-sites-and-sheafification), [lem-author-60d95bfb692a-cover-restriction-injective](item-lem-author-60d95bfb692a-cover-restriction-injective)

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Glue the matching section along the generated sieve.

1.1 Given $s\in F(T)$, assign to each arrow $v:V\to X$ in the sieve generated by $p$ the pullback $a^*s$ for any factorization $v=pa$. This does not depend on the factorization: two such maps $a,b:V\to T$ define a map $V\to T\times_X T$, and [F2] identifies $a^*s$ and $b^*s$. These assignments are compatible with precomposition. [F2, construct]

2.1 By [F1] and [F3], this matching family has a unique amalgamation in $F(X)$, whose restriction to $T$ is $s$. Restriction is therefore surjective; [F3] also gives injectivity. Thus it is a bijection. [F1, F3, step 1.1] ∎
