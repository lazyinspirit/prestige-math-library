## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed compatible site and universe bounds of the supported perfect-$K$-theory constructions. Let $k$ be a field, let $U$ be a smooth finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three, and let $i:C\hookrightarrow U$ be an integral closed curve of codimension two. Let $\eta$ be the generic point of $C$ and let $E\in\operatorname{Perf}_{|C|}(U)$ be an object of the specified current supported perfect category. Neither smoothness nor normality of $C$, nor global quasi-projectivity of $U$, is required. Identify its spectral degree-zero class with $G_0(C)$ by the completed coherent-cohomology Euler and ideal-power devissage comparison. Its generic-rank coordinate is
$$\chi_\eta(E)=\sum_n(-1)^n\ell_{\mathcal O_{U,\eta}}H^n(E)_\eta.$$
This is a finite sum of finite integers. It is additive under exact triangles, invariant under restricting the ambient scheme to an open neighbourhood of $\eta$, sends $[i_*\mathcal O_C[0]]$ to $+1$, and sends every class supported at finitely many closed points of $C$ to zero. The current support spectrum and the open-complement fiber are identified by the actual localization map, not by a model-free notation change.

## Facts & Assumptions
[A1] The assumed [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice) is used through the smoothness characterization, finite-type algebra and Noetherian-topology results below, and through the essentially small supported perfect-category and K-theory comparisons. The fixed universes satisfy [math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention), contain $\omega$ and the scheme data, and admit the enlargements required by the supplied constructions; universe existence is a separate standing size assumption. The compatible site bounds are those of [F1]–[F3] and [F7]. These premises apply in both proof steps; the finite stalk calculation requires no additional choice.

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the stated site and universe bounds; a field $k$; a smooth finite-type $k$-scheme $U$ of pure dimension three; an integral codimension-two closed curve $i:C\hookrightarrow U$ with generic point $\eta$; and a supported perfect complex $E\in\operatorname{Perf}_{|C|}(U)$.

**Goal:** Identify the precise coordinate in the current K-theory model and its positive test object.

[F1] The completed supported comparison maps the current $K_0^{|C|}(U)$ to $G_0(C)$ by the coherent Euler class followed by ideal-power quotients, and sends $[F]$ back to $[i_*F[0]]$. Here $G_0(C)$ has coherent-sheaf generators and short-exact-sequence relations; closed direct image is $(i_*F)(V)=F(i^{-1}V)$ with the induced structure-sheaf action. [prop-supported-k-zero-devissage](item-prop-supported-k-zero-devissage)

[F2] Generic rank on $G_0(C)$ sends $[F]$ to $\dim_{k(\eta)}F_\eta$, where $k(\eta)=\mathcal O_{C,\eta}$, and corresponds under [F1] to the alternating generic length displayed above. Its kernel is generated by closed-point residue-field classes. The supplier includes finiteness of the displayed sum and lengths and the identity $\mathcal O_{U,\eta}/\mathcal I_\eta=k(\eta)$ for the defining ideal $\mathcal I$ of $C$. [lem-curve-generic-rank-localization](item-lem-curve-generic-rank-localization)

[F3] Current support-category K-theory maps naturally equivalently to the fiber of restriction for a qcqs scheme and quasi-compact open complement. This is the actual category-defined support-to-fiber map and is natural for open restriction. [prop-perfect-k-localization-input](item-prop-perfect-k-localization-input)

[F4] For a smooth finite-type scheme over any field, the smoothness characterization in [math:def-smooth-morphism-to-field-classical](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-smooth-morphism-to-field-classical) says that all local rings after every field extension are regular. In particular the identity extension $k/k$ gives regularity of every local ring of $U$. Regularity at a point is the local-ring condition of [math:def-regular-local-ring-geometric-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-regular-local-ring-geometric-point). No perfect-field assumption enters this implication.

[F5] Finite type means locally of finite type and quasi-compact, by [math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism); a quasi-compact morphism has quasi-compact inverse images of quasi-compact opens, by [math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism). Thus a finite-type field scheme has a finite affine cover with finitely generated $k$-coordinate algebras. [lem-author-14463ef2f4e5-finite-type-dimension](item-lem-author-14463ef2f4e5-finite-type-dimension) makes each such algebra Noetherian. A finite affine cover of this kind makes the scheme Noetherian by [math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme). By [math:lem-closed-immersion-affine-quotient-and-base-change](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-closed-immersion-affine-quotient-and-base-change), the inverse image under $i$ of a chart $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$; the images of a finite list of $k$-algebra generators still generate $A/I$.

[F6] By [math:thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum), the affine spectra just described are Noetherian topological spaces under AC. The ascending-open-chain convention is [math:def-noetherian-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-noetherian-topological-space). By [math:lem-noetherian-open-subsets-are-quasi-compact](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-noetherian-open-subsets-are-quasi-compact), all opens of a Noetherian space are quasi-compact under AC. The qcqs condition means quasi-compactness and quasi-compact intersections of affine opens, as in [math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme).

[F7] [def-scheme-perfect-complexes](item-def-scheme-perfect-complexes) supplies the full stable idempotent-complete category $\operatorname{Perf}_{|C|}(U)$: its objects are locally equivalent to bounded cochain complexes of finite locally free modules and have cohomology sheaves vanishing off $C$. Its triangles are the cofiber triangles in the homotopy category. [def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface](item-def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface) supplies its essentially small current realization, presentation independence and $K_0^{|C|}(U)=\pi_0\mathbb K(\operatorname{Perf}_{|C|}(U))$. For a small stable idempotent-complete presentation, [thm-author-3a003d7b5208-nonconnective-k-zero-input](item-thm-author-3a003d7b5208-nonconnective-k-zero-input) gives the object-class cofiber presentation of this degree-zero group, and [lem-author-3a003d7b5208-triangle-presentation](item-lem-author-3a003d7b5208-triangle-presentation) identifies it with the distinguished-triangle presentation.

[F8] Fields and schemes over a base use [math:def-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-field) and [math:def-scheme-over-base](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-scheme-over-base). An integral scheme is nonempty, reduced and irreducible, as in [math:def-integral-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-integral-scheme); a closed immersion identifies a closed subspace and has surjective structure-sheaf map, as in [math:def-closed-immersion-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-closed-immersion-schemes). Dimension is the length supremum of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets, as in [math:def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space). Here a curve has dimension one; pure dimension three stipulates that $U$ has dimension three and each irreducible component has that dimension. Codimension at the generic point of $C$ is the Krull dimension of $\mathcal O_{U,\eta}$; its stipulated value two is not used to infer any regularity. The generic point has dense closure, and [F2] supplies its field and generic stalk. Length $\ell_R(M)$ counts simple factors in a finite composition series, with length zero for the zero module, as in [math:def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module). Restriction to an open subspace evaluates a sheaf on the same smaller opens, as in [math:def-restriction-sheaf-open-subspace](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-restriction-sheaf-open-subspace); hence it preserves stalks at points of that open. The notation $H^n(E)$ denotes cohomology sheaves in [F7], and $F[0]$ is the sheaf $F$ placed in degree zero.

## Proof

1.1 By [F5], choose a finite affine cover of $U$ with finitely generated $k$-coordinate algebras; the one-point base $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is quasi-compact. These algebras are Noetherian, so $U$ is Noetherian. The inverse images of the cover in $C$ are affine quotient spectra with finitely generated $k$-algebras, so the same argument makes $C$ Noetherian. Its integrality and dimension one are hypotheses, and $\dim U=3$ gives finite dimension. Apply [F4] with the identity extension $k/k$ to make every local ring of $U$ regular. For completeness, $U$ is also Noetherian as a topological space: any increasing sequence of opens stabilizes on each of the finitely many Noetherian affine charts by [F6], and the maximum of their stabilization indices makes it stabilize on $U$. Every open of $U$ is therefore quasi-compact by [F6]; this includes intersections of affine opens and $U\setminus C$. Consequently $U$ and every open neighbourhood $W$ of $\eta$ are qcqs, and both open complements of the closed supports in use are quasi-compact. These checks discharge the ambient hypotheses of [F1]–[F3] without separatedness, quasi-projectivity or a perfect ground field. [A1, F4, F5, F6, F8, given, choose]

2.1 Apply [F1] to $(U,C)$ and [F2] to its $G_0(C)$ class. Their composite has precisely the displayed finite alternating generic-length formula. To check triangle additivity, use the stable idempotent-complete supported category in [F7] and one of its equivalent small presentations. The degree-zero and triangle-presentation comparisons in [F7] give $[B]=[A]+[D]$ for every exact triangle $A\to B\to D\to A[1]$ in this very category. The composite homomorphism therefore gives $\chi_\eta(B)=\chi_\eta(A)+\chi_\eta(D)$; being a homomorphism alone was not the missing triangle relation. If $\eta\in W\subseteq U$ is open, the stalk restriction convention in [F8] identifies its local ring and all $H^n(E)_\eta$ with their restrictions. Thus the formula is invariant under ambient open restriction. For $i_*\mathcal O_C[0]$, [F1] supplies the actual perfect object and [F2] identifies its only nonzero generic cohomology with $k(\eta)$ in degree zero. This residue module is simple: a nonzero element generates it under the local ring's surjection onto the field. Its one-factor series has length one by [F8], fixing the value $+1$. An object supported at finitely many closed points of the integral curve has zero generic stalk, since $\eta$ is not closed on a dimension-one integral scheme, so it has coordinate zero; linearity gives the same assertion for such classes. Finally apply [F3] using step 1.1. Its natural support-category-to-fiber equivalence transports these same classes and is compatible with open restriction. No new sign or abstract group identification is inserted in the open-complement fiber. This proves every assertion. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F7, F8, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
