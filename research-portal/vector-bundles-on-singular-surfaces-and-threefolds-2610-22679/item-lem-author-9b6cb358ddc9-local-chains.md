## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed compatible universe and site-size bounds of the supplied perfect-K-space and derived-sheaf constructions. Let $X$ be any qcqs scheme over a field. On its small Zariski site let $\mathcal K=\Omega^\infty K_{\rm Perf}$ and $j:\underline{\mathbb Z}\times BGL\to\mathcal K$ be the actual stable free-class map, with locally constant ranks. The maps $j$, $j\times j$, and the component-zero map $j_0:BGL\to S$ into the actual rank-zero fiber induce local quasi-isomorphisms on normalized free integral chains. Therefore precomposition detects homotopy classes of derived sheaf maps into each actual abelian EM coefficient target $A_i=\Omega^\infty(\mathbb Z(i)_{\rm mot}[2i])$. No noetherianness, dimension bound, or continuity of motivic complexes is assumed.

Here qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated: every open cover has a finite subcover, and intersections of affine open subsets are quasi-compact. The small Zariski site consists of open subschemes with open coverings; one may use its affine basis for stalk calculations. Use the qcqs presentation by quasi-compact opens for the perfect-K presheaf, and extend its local sheaf model to the full open-set site. These opens are qcqs because $X$ is quasi-separated. A perfect complex is locally quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite locally free modules, and $K_{\rm Perf}$ is the supplied nonconnective K-theory of the actual enhanced perfect category. The symbol $\mathcal K$ uses the supplied stalkwise local replacement of its space presheaf; the canonical unit from perfect K-spaces is retained. All derived sheaf maps and fibers use this same local homotopy convention. It does not identify ordinary space-valued sheafification with hypercompletion. The sheaf $\underline{\mathbb Z}$ consists of locally constant integer functions. The rank map $\mathcal K\to\underline{\mathbb Z}$ is induced locally by alternating ranks of bounded perfect representatives; $S$ denotes its homotopy fiber over zero. Put $GL=\operatorname*{colim}_nGL_n$ with stabilization $g\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(g,1)$, and let $BGL$ be the local replacement of the nerve of this group presheaf. The free-class map sends stable automorphisms to their actual K-space loops, translated into each integer-rank component; its zero-rank part is $j_0$.

Normalized chains and unbounded derived coefficients have the sheaf-chain conventions stated below. More precisely, restrict the actual coherent coefficient functor $D_i(U)=\mathbb Z(i)_{\rm mot}(U)$ to the quasi-compact open basis $\mathcal B$ of $X$. Here $A_i$ is the stalkwise local replacement of $U\mapsto\Omega^\infty H(D_i(U)[2i])$, with the actual fixed coherent Eilenberg–Mac Lane realization. Let $P_i$ be a strict complex presentation of that entire coherent diagram and put $C_i=a_{\mathcal B}P_i$, its degreewise associated sheaf complex, independent up to derived equivalence. The coefficient comparison below identifies $A_i$ naturally with $E_X(C_i[2i])$, the target of the derived free-chain adjunction, retaining restriction arrows and zero basepoints. Thus the initial coefficient shorthand denotes this local object; it does not assert $D_i(U)=R\Gamma(U,C_i)$ or identify ordinary coefficient sections with hypercomplete sections. For pointed maps, the same detection assertion uses reduced free chains and target basepoint zero. Universes contain $\omega$ and the site data, with larger universes for the categories; their existence is a separate assumption from Choice.

[F10] [math:lem-equality-in-a-filtered-colimit-of-sets-is-eventual](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-equality-in-a-filtered-colimit-of-sets-is-eventual) supplies the eventual-equality criterion for a small filtered diagram of sets. For a filtered diagram of commutative unital rings, construct its underlying colimit set from representatives modulo this eventual equality. Define a sum or product by moving its two representatives to a common stage; eventual equality makes the result independent of the representatives and common stage. Zero, one and negatives are defined from their stage values. Each ring axiom involves finitely many representatives and holds after moving them to one stage, so these operations give a commutative unital ring. A compatible cocone of ring maps induces the unique set map out of this quotient, and the stage formulas make it a ring map; hence this is the ring colimit. Filteredness, in [F6]'s finite-diagram convention, puts any finite list of elements at one stage. The eventual-equality criterion then makes any finite list of polynomial equations true at a single later stage: move the finitely many equality witnesses to a common stage and equalize any parallel transition arrows. This applies to the idempotent, inverse, composition and splitting equations below.

## Proof
1.1 Finite projective modules admit finite idempotent-matrix presentations: choose finite generators giving a surjection $q:A^n\to P$, and use projectivity to split it by $s:P\to A^n$. Then $e=sq$ satisfies $e^2=e$ and has image isomorphic to $P$. Conversely an idempotent image is a finite projective summand; morphisms between two such images are precisely matrices $u$ with $u=e'ue$. Over a filtered colimit of rings, [F10] first places the finitely many matrix entries at one stage and then makes all entries of $e^2-e$ zero at a common later stage. Thus the idempotent matrix and its finite projective image descend. Morphisms, their equalities, inverses, and split exact sequences descend by the same finite-matrix argument. A Quillen Q morphism is a finite admissible diagram modulo an isomorphism of such diagrams; the diagram, its splitting data, and any witnessing isomorphism descend. Thus every finite simplex of the Q nerve descends, with equality after a further index. Matrix-idempotent presentations give equivalent exact categories, so this identifies the filtered nerve colimit up to the canonical equivalence of categories, rather than requiring compatible independent skeletons. By [F6] its filtered simplicial colimit computes its colimit as spaces. Based loops are the homotopy pullback of the two zero-basepoint arrows; [F6] commutes that finite limit with the filtered colimit. Thus $K^Q(P(-))=\Omega BQ(P(-))$ commutes with these ring colimits, including its components and natural free-object maps. [A1, F1, F4, F6, F10, given, construct]

1.2 The full Q/Waldhausen comparison of [F1] applies because surjections of finite projectives split. For a cochain map $f:P\to Q$, the graph inclusion $P\to Q\oplus\operatorname{Cone}(\mathrm{id}_P)$, $p\mapsto(f(p),(p,0))$, is a degreewise split cofibration; projection to $Q$ is a quasi-isomorphism since the identity cone is contractible. Thus factorization stays inside bounded finite projectives. Their acyclic cones are contractible by [F4]. A quasi-isomorphism is therefore a homotopy equivalence; conversely a map invertible in the localization induces an isomorphism on cohomology, so DHKS-saturation holds. Their actual localization is the affine enhanced perfect category by [F4], and extension of scalars preserves split cofibrations and contractible cones. Hence [F5] applies naturally. The resulting comparison to the actual nonconnective perfect K-space is an isomorphism in every positive degree. On degree zero, the Q and Waldhausen object classes generate their Grothendieck groups, and [F3] identifies their image with the same finite-projective class in actual perfect $K_0$. Thus this canonical comparison is also an isomorphism on components. Translation in these grouplike K-spaces identifies all components, so it is an equivalence of the full K-spaces. Choice is used in the supplied small presentations and enhanced realizations, not as a substitute for the component argument. [A1, F1, F3, F4, F5]

2.1 Combine the natural full-space comparison in step 1.2 with the Q continuity in step 1.1. This identifies the connective space of actual perfect K-theory of a filtered colimit ring with the filtered colimit of the corresponding spaces, compatibly with free-object maps. Only this K-theoretic continuity is used. No assertion about continuity of motivic complexes enters. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 At a Zariski point $x$, affine neighborhoods are cofinal among neighborhoods: two such neighborhoods contain a third affine neighborhood of $x$. Their rings have colimit $\mathcal O_{X,x}$. By [F7], the actual local replacement has the stalk of this affine perfect-K presheaf; by [F6] the stalk is its filtered homotopy colimit. Step 2.1 therefore identifies that stalk with $\Omega^\infty K_{\rm Perf}(\mathcal O_{X,x})$, compatibly with the free-object map. Finite matrices and their inverse equations descend in the same way, giving the source stalk $\mathbb Z\times BGL(\mathcal O_{X,x})$. Finite projectives over this local ring are free, and the actual free-class map is componentwise the acyclic plus map of [F1]. Its integral homology isomorphism gives a normalized free-chain quasi-isomorphism: use singular-complex models of the plus arrow by [F6], normalize its singular chains without changing homology by [F8], and transfer along weakly equivalent simplicial representatives by [F9]. Its rank map on $K_0$ is the isomorphism to $\mathbb Z$. Stalks commute with the finite homotopy limit defining $S$ by [F6]; hence its zero fiber is the zero component of this local K-space, and the same plus comparison proves the chain assertion for $j_0$. This selects a discrete rank component and does not assume preservation of arbitrary homotopy fibers by homology equivalences. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1]

4.1 Apply [F8]'s natural normalized simplicial Eilenberg–Zilber comparison to each stalk of $j\times j$. Its terms are normalized free chains in nonnegative homological degrees, equivalently bounded-above flat complexes after cochain reindexing. The two chain quasi-isomorphisms for $j$ from step 3.1 therefore give a quasi-isomorphism on the tensor product, by [F8]'s precise bounded-above tensor interface. Naturality identifies it with the chains of the product map. Stalks commute with finite products by [F6] and with normalized free-chain constructions by [F9]. Thus this proves the local quasi-isomorphism for $j\times j$, without imposing a dimension or connectedness hypothesis. [F6, F8, F9, step 3.1]

5.1 Exact abelian sheafification and the stalk criterion in [F9] turn steps 3.1 and 4.1 into quasi-isomorphisms $L_X(j)$, $L_X(j_0)$ and $L_X(j\times j)$ of complexes of sheaves. First use [F2] to identify the actual locally replaced coefficient target with $E_X(C_i[2i])$, naturally with restrictions, basepoints and the actual Chern maps. Apply [F9]'s derived adjunction to each map and $C_i[2i]$ of [F2]. Precomposition by these quasi-isomorphisms is an equivalence on derived mapping spaces into $C_i[2i]$, and hence on derived sheaf mapping spaces into $A_i$. It therefore detects their homotopy classes. For pointed maps, use the cofiber of $\mathbb Z\to L_X(Y)$ induced by the source basepoint; [F9] shows that these maps remain quasi-isomorphisms on reduced chains and supplies the pointed adjunction. No boundedness of $C_i$ or filtered-colimit assertion about motivic coefficients was used. Derived evaluation on $X$ preserves an equality of these sheaf-map classes and its descent data. [F2, F9, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
