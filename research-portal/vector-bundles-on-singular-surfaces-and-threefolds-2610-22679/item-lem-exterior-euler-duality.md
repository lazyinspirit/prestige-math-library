## Statement

Let $X$ be a scheme and $E$ a locally free $\mathcal O_X$-module of constant finite rank $r\geq 0$. Write $E^\vee=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_X}(E,\mathcal O_X)$ and $\det E=\Lambda^r E$. For every $0\leq i\leq r$ there is a canonical isomorphism
$$\Lambda^{r-i}E\cong\det E\otimes\Lambda^iE^\vee.$$

Let $K^0_{\mathrm{vb}}(X)$ be the vector-bundle Grothendieck ring: its additive group is generated by isomorphism classes $[F]$ of finite locally free $\mathcal O_X$-modules, subject to $[F]=[F']+[F'']$ for every short exact sequence $0\to F'\to F\to F''\to0$ of such modules; multiplication is induced by tensor product and its identity is $[\mathcal O_X]$. Define the exterior Euler class of a rank-$r$ bundle by
$$\lambda_{-1}(E)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^i[\Lambda^i E].$$
Then
$$\lambda_{-1}(E)=(-1)^r[\det E]\,\lambda_{-1}(E^\vee).$$
The class $[\det E]$ is a unit, so $\lambda_{-1}(E)=0$ if and only if $\lambda_{-1}(E^\vee)=0$.

The exterior powers, their local increasing-wedge bases, and the determinant line are as in [def-exterior-determinant-bundles](item-def-exterior-determinant-bundles). A line bundle is a locally free module of rank one; a unit in a ring is an element with a multiplicative inverse.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$ and a locally free $\mathcal O_X$-module $E$ of constant finite rank $r\geq0$.

**Goal:** Construct the stated canonical exterior-power isomorphisms and prove the Euler-class identity and simultaneous vanishing.

[F1] On a trivializing open set for $E$ with ordered basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$, the increasing wedges of these basis vectors form bases for the exterior powers; $\det E=\Lambda^rE$ is a line bundle. These constructions are functorial in $E$. [def-exterior-determinant-bundles](item-def-exterior-determinant-bundles)


[F2] Internal Hom has sections $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_U}(F|_U,G|_U)$ on $U$; the sheaf tensor product is the sheafification of $U\mapsto F(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X(U)}G(U)$. In particular Hom out of $\mathcal O_U^n$ is computed by the images of its $n$ standard sections. [math:def-sheaf-hom](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sheaf-hom) [math:def-sheaf-tensor-product](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sheaf-tensor-product)

[F3] Exactness means equality of image and kernel sheaves and can be tested on stalks. A surjective sheaf map therefore permits local lifts of each prescribed section near any point: lift its germ and shrink until its image equals that section. [math:def-exact-sequence-sheaves](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-exact-sequence-sheaves) [math:thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise)

[F4] Balanced pairings induce unique maps from module tensor products. Over a commutative ring, tensor products have natural symmetry and associativity maps. Maps from the tensor-product presheaf to a sheaf extend uniquely through sheafification. Compatible local maps of module sheaves glue uniquely by applying the sheaf axiom to their values on sections. [math:thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products) [math:thm-symmetry-and-associativity-over-a-commutative-ring](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-symmetry-and-associativity-over-a-commutative-ring) [math:thm-sheafification-universal-property](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-sheafification-universal-property) [math:def-sheaf-on-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sheaf-on-topological-space)

[F5] A scheme has a structure sheaf; module sheaves and their morphisms have sectionwise module operations and scalar-compatible restrictions. Finite locally free means locally isomorphic to $\mathcal O_U^n$, allowing the integer $n$ to vary between trivializing opens. [math:def-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-scheme) [math:def-module-on-ringed-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-module-on-ringed-space) [math:def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank)

[F6] For a positive-size square matrix over a commutative ring, $\det$ is the signed permutation sum. For the size-zero matrix in the empty-wedge case, set its determinant equal to $1$. [math:def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix)

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Use the perfect exterior-product pairing and then reindex the finite alternating sum.

1.1 The presentation of $K^0_{\mathrm{vb}}(X)$ is well defined: isomorphism classes of finite locally free modules form a set, since bundles can be encoded by open covers, finite ranks and transition matrices satisfying the overlap identities. Explicitly, the free abelian group here consists of integer-valued functions of finite support on the set of bundle classes; quotient by the subgroup of finite integer combinations of the stated exact-sequence relations. Tensoring an exact sequence of finite locally free modules with another such module preserves exactness: near each point trivialize the quotient, locally lift its finitely many basis sections by [F3], and intersect the finitely many resulting neighbourhoods. Those lifts define a local splitting, so the sequence is locally a split sequence of modules. On a further trivializing open for the tensor factor, tensoring is a finite direct sum, which preserves that split sequence; [F3] then gives global exactness. The resulting tensor sheaf is finite locally free, with local basis the pairs of basis vectors of the two factors, by [F2] and [F4]. Consequently tensor product respects the relations in each variable and gives the stated ring multiplication; associativity and commutativity follow from the module tensor maps in [F4], extended through sheafification and glued. The identity map is induced by $a\otimes s\mapsto as$, with inverse $s\mapsto1\otimes s$. [given, F2, F3, F4, F5, construct, algebra]

1.2 For each $i$, exterior multiplication gives a canonical pairing $\Lambda^{r-i}E\otimes\Lambda^iE\longrightarrow\det E.$ It induces a map $\Lambda^{r-i}E\to\mathcal Hom(\Lambda^iE,\det E)$. On a trivializing open set, the increasing wedge indexed by a subset $J$ of size $r-i$ pairs to zero with every increasing $i$-wedge except the one indexed by the complementary subset, with which it pairs to $\pm e_1\wedge\cdots\wedge e_r$. Thus its matrix is a signed permutation matrix, including in characteristic two, and the induced map is an isomorphism. Because the pairing is defined by exterior multiplication, these local isomorphisms agree on overlaps. [F1, F2, F4, algebra]

2.1 Locally, $E^\vee$ has the dual basis $e_1^\vee,\ldots,e_r^\vee$. The canonical map $\Lambda^i E^\vee\to(\Lambda^i E)^\vee$ sends $\phi_1\wedge\cdots\wedge\phi_i$ to the functional $v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_i\mapsto\det(\phi_a(v_b))$. It sends each increasing dual wedge to the dual of the corresponding increasing wedge basis vector, hence is an isomorphism. Likewise the evaluation map $\det E\otimes(\Lambda^i E)^\vee\to\mathcal Hom(\Lambda^iE,\det E)$ is locally an isomorphism. The signed permutation formula in [F6] is multilinear and alternating (terms with repeated rows or columns cancel in pairs), so the displayed functional factors through the exterior quotient in [F1]. These formulas commute with restrictions and are independent of the chosen frames; their local inverses consequently agree on overlaps and glue by [F4]. Combining their inverses with step 1.2 gives the asserted canonical isomorphism. The same argument covers $i=0$, $i=r$, and $r=0$, using the empty wedge. [F1, F2, F4, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Taking classes in the ring and substituting $j=r-i$ gives $\begin{aligned} \lambda_{-1}(E)&=\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i[\Lambda^iE] =\sum_{j=0}^r(-1)^{r-j}[\det E][\Lambda^jE^\vee]\\ &=(-1)^r[\det E]\lambda_{-1}(E^\vee). \end{aligned}$ The dual line $(\det E)^\vee$ has the property that the evaluation map $\det E\otimes(\det E)^\vee\to\mathcal O_X$ is an isomorphism locally and hence globally. Thus $[(\det E)^\vee]$ is the inverse of $[\det E]$. Multiplication by the unit $(-1)^r[\det E]$ is bijective, proving simultaneous vanishing. This establishes all claims. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
