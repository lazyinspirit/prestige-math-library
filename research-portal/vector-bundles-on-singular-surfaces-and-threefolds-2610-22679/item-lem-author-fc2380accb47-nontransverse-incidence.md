## Statement
Assume AC. Use the first-jet-spanning family on the finite-type affine $k$-scheme $X$, over the algebraically closed field $k$ of arbitrary characteristic, with $W\hookrightarrow X$ locally closed, smooth and of pure dimension three, and $E$ a rank-three bundle. On $W$, $E$ abbreviates its pullback from $X$. Index the chosen family as $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$. Write $T=\mathbb A_k^N$ and $\Phi=\sum_{a=1}^N t_a\phi_a$ for the universal section on $W\times_k T$, and let $I_W\subset W\times_k T$ be its scheme-theoretic zero incidence. Define the tangent sheaf by $T_{W/k}=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_W}(\Omega^1_{W/k},\mathcal O_W)$, where $\Omega^1_{W/k}$ carries the universal $k$-derivation $d$ and $\mathcal Hom$ is the sheaf of module homomorphisms on open subsets. Let $p:I_W\to W$ be the projection; $p^*$ denotes pullback of module sheaves.

Let $e_a$ be the standard basis of $\mathcal O_W^N$ and $j:\mathcal O_W^N\twoheadrightarrow J^1E$ its first-jet evaluation map. The universal first jet $\sum_a t_a j(e_a)$, formed after pullback to $W\times_k T$, has zero value on $I_W$. The locally split jet exact sequence therefore identifies its restriction with a section $\theta$ of $p^*(\Omega^1_{W/k}\otimes E)$. Contraction with the dual cotangent factor defines the intrinsic linear map $d_W\Phi:p^*T_{W/k}\to p^*E$: on pure tensors it sends $v$ to $v(\omega)e$ for a tensor $\omega\otimes e$ of $\theta$. In local frames its coefficients are $\sum_a t_a\,d(\phi_a)$, so parameter coordinates are held constant when taking this derivative. Let $B\subset I_W$ be the closed subscheme defined locally by the determinant of this rank-three map. Then $\dim B\leq N-1$. At a point of any specialized zero scheme, the point is outside $B$ exactly when the induced derivative over its residue field is an isomorphism. These are equations for the scheme and its derivative, rather than only a count of set-theoretic fibers.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The smooth three-dimensional $W$, rank-three $E$, and surjective first-jet map of the first-jet family supplier, retaining its Axiom of Choice assumption. All products are over $k$.

**Goal:** Bound the complete incidence of nontransverse zeros in every characteristic.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the first-jet family in [F1], where it supplies affine global generation. It also meets the explicit AC hypotheses of the ideal-sheaf construction in [F5] and the Noetherian subspace result in [F6]. The determinant and pivot calculations themselves use only finite algebra. [F1] [lem-author-fc2380accb47-coordinate-first-jets](item-lem-author-fc2380accb47-coordinate-first-jets) supplies this indexed family, the rank-three cotangent bundle on $W$, the rank-twelve bundle $J^1E$, its locally split sequence $0\to\Omega^1_{W/k}\otimes E\to J^1E\to E\to0$, and the surjection $j:\mathcal O_W^N\twoheadrightarrow J^1E$ under [A1]. [F2] [lem-regular-section-parameter-family](item-lem-regular-section-parameter-family) constructs the actual universal zero ideal and its parameter fibers. [F3] Finite-type field schemes are Noetherian. For an irreducible such scheme its dimension is the transcendence degree of its function field; in particular affine $a$-space and its nonempty principal opens have dimension $a$. Dimensions add for nonempty products, and image closure has dimension at most the source dimension, applied componentwise. Use chain dimension with $\dim\varnothing=-\infty$. These are precisely the finite-type dimension inputs and conventions in [thm-author-fc2380accb47-finite-type-geometric-input](item-thm-author-fc2380accb47-finite-type-geometric-input); no generic-smoothness result is used.

[F4] The universal derivation is additive, kills $k$ and obeys $d(ab)=a\,db+b\,da$. The tangent sheaf is the internal Hom dual of the locally free cotangent sheaf, so dual frames give rank three and the local contraction $\omega\otimes e\mapsto(v\mapsto v(\omega)e)$. Pullback is extension of scalars after inverse image; in free frames it preserves the tensor contraction and a split exact sequence, since the splitting identities remain identities after extension of scalars. ## Proof
1.1 Pull the locally split jet sequence in [F1] to $I_W$ using [F4]. The value component of $\sum_a t_a j(e_a)$ is $\Phi$, which vanishes as a section on the actual zero subscheme by [F2]; its jet therefore lies in the pulled-back kernel, giving $\theta$ and the contraction in the Statement. In a coefficient frame its derivative is $\sum_a t_a d(\phi_a)$. On a change of frame it becomes $G\sum_a t_a d(\phi_a)+(dG)\Phi$ by [F4]. The second term vanishes scheme-theoretically on $I_W$, not only at its closed points. Changes of frames for $E$ and $\Omega^1$ thus change the derivative matrix by invertible left and right matrices. By [F5] its determinant changes by a unit, so the principal ideals agree on overlaps and define the stated closed subscheme $B$. In jet coordinates the equations are zero value and singular $3\times3$ derivative, pulled back by the actual jet-evaluation morphism in [F1]. Quotienting the local coefficient rings by these equations also shows that $B$ is finite type over $k$ by [F6]. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, given, construct]

1.2 Let $M=\operatorname{Spec}k[z_{ij}]/(\det(z_{ij}))$ be the scheme of singular $3\times3$ matrices. On a chart where a chosen $2\times2$ block $A$ is invertible, write a matrix as $\begin{pmatrix}A&b\\c&e\end{pmatrix}$. Multiplication by invertible block triangular matrices reduces it to $\operatorname{diag}(A,e-cA^{-1}b)$, so by [F5] the determinant-zero equation is $e=cA^{-1}b$. Its chart is a nonempty principal open of affine eight-space, parametrized by $A,b,c$. These finitely many charts cover rank two. On the remaining rank-at-most-one locus, a nonzero entry used as pivot expresses every entry as the corresponding column entry times the corresponding row entry divided by that pivot. There are five independent coordinates, namely the pivot, its other two row entries and its other two column entries, with the pivot inverted; the zero matrix is one additional point. These are scheme parametrizations of the rank-at-most-one loci, by their $2\times2$ minor equations; every point of $M$ belongs to a rank-two chart, a rank-one chart or the zero point, with rank computed over its residue field. By [F3], the closures of the images of these finitely many charts have dimensions at most eight, five and zero respectively. They cover $M$, so its dimension is at most eight. The nonempty rank-two chart has dimension eight, so $\dim M=8$. This argument uses no characteristic restriction. The bad jets in a twelve-dimensional jet chart have zero value and derivative in $M$. [F3, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 If $W$ is empty then $B$ is empty and the claims hold with the convention in [F3]. Otherwise locally split $j$ by [F1] and [F2], and simultaneously trivialize its rank-$(N-12)$ kernel, $E$ and $\Omega^1$. The resulting invertible linear change of parameter coordinates identifies the inverse image of the bad-jet equations, over each such open $V\subset W$, with $V\times_k M\times_k\mathbb A_k^{N-12}$. Hence its dimension is at most $\dim V+8+N-12\leq N-1$ by [F3]. A finite such open cover exists by [F6], and the open-cover dimension formula there proves the global bound. Finally, after specialization the same determinant equations define the pulled-back bad locus. At any point outside it the determinant has nonzero residue, and [F5] says precisely that the derivative over that residue field is an isomorphism; conversely an isomorphism has nonzero determinant. This proves the specialized assertion as well. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
