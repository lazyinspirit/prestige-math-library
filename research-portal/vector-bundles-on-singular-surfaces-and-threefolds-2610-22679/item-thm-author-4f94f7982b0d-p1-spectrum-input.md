## Statement
Fix compatible size universes. Let $S$ be a quasi-compact quasi-separated (qcqs) derived scheme, let $\mathrm{Sm}_S$ be its smooth qcqs derived schemes with the Zariski topology, and let $\mathrm{St}_S$ be the infinity-category of sheaves of spaces on this site, with cartesian tensor product. Write $\mathrm{St}=\mathrm{St}_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}$. Use the source's presentable infinity-categories and coherent monoidal structures: $\mathrm{Pr}^L$ consists of presentable infinity-categories and colimit-preserving functors; a presentably symmetric monoidal category is a commutative algebra object in $\mathrm{Pr}^L$, and a category presentably tensored over it is a module object there. Let $\mathcal V$ be presentably tensored over $\mathrm{St}$; being defined over $S$ means having the corresponding $\mathrm{St}_S$-action. For the ring assertions, require multiplicativity: $\mathcal V$ is presentably symmetric monoidal with a compatible symmetric monoidal left adjoint $\mathrm{St}\to\mathcal V$. The case of motivic spectra over $S$ uses $\mathcal V=\mathrm{St}_S$. A subscript $*$ means pointed objects, $X_+$ adds a disjoint basepoint, and $E^T$ denotes the cotensor right adjoint to $T\otimes-$. The pointed tensor product is smash. Put $\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}=\mathbb P^1\otimes-$ with $\mathbb P^1$ pointed at infinity.

A symmetric sequence is a functor from the groupoid of finite sets and bijections, with tensor product given by Day convolution along disjoint union; equivalently it has levels $E_n$ with coherent symmetric-group actions. Let $F_1(\mathbb P^1)$ be the free symmetric sequence generated by $\mathbb P^1$ in degree one, and let $\mathbb S_{\mathbb P^1}$ be its free commutative algebra in symmetric sequences of pointed stacks. A symmetric lax $\mathbb P^1$-spectrum in $\mathcal V_*$ is a module over this algebra. It is a motivic spectrum, an object of $\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal V)$, exactly when every adjoint bonding map $E_n\to(E_{n+1})^{\mathbb P^1}$ is an equivalence. Localization to these spectra is monoidal; in multiplicative coefficients a commutative algebra over the image of the sphere algebra therefore gives a motivic commutative ring spectrum after localization, and is already such a model if the bonding condition holds.

The adjunction $\Sigma^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}:\mathcal V_*\rightleftarrows\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal V):\Omega^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}$ has right adjoint evaluation at level zero. On an Omega-spectrum $E$, the shift $s_-$ evaluates at $E_{n+1}$ in level $n$ and agrees with $\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}$, so $\Omega^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}^n E\simeq E_n$ for $n\geq0$. For $Y\in\mathrm{Sm}_S$, the diagonal convention is $E^{2i,i}(Y)=\pi_0\operatorname{Map}(\Sigma^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}Y_+,\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}^iE)$, which evaluates at $\pi_0E_i(Y)$ for $i\geq0$ when $\mathcal V=\mathrm{St}_S$. Separately, ordinary coefficient stabilization, where $\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal V)$ is the formal inversion of the pointed circle in $\mathcal V_*$, induces $\Sigma^\infty:\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal V)\rightleftarrows\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal V)):\Omega^\infty$. In multiplicative coefficients its left adjoint is symmetric monoidal and its right adjoint lax monoidal; the latter applies the ordinary coefficient right adjoint at each level. This $\Omega^\infty$ is distinct from level evaluation $\Omega^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}$.

The Picard stack $\mathrm{Pic}$ classifies line bundles and their isomorphisms and is pointed by the trivial line; $[\mathcal O(1)]:\mathbb P^1\to\mathrm{Pic}$ is its pointed tautological-line map. An orientation of $E$ is a retraction of $[\mathcal O(1)]\otimes\mathrm{id}_E:\mathbb P^1\otimes E\to\mathrm{Pic}\otimes E$. For a motivic ring spectrum in multiplicative coefficients, a pointed class $c_1:\mathrm{Pic}\to\Omega^\infty_{\mathbb P^1}\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}E$ whose restriction along $[\mathcal O(1)]$ is the $\mathbb P^1$-suspended unit determines a linear orientation, namely such a retraction as a right $E$-module map. Its first Chern operations are multiplication by those very classes; left $E$-modules inherit the orientation.

Let $\partial:\mathbb P^1\to S^1\otimes\mathbb G_m$ be the boundary of the pointed Zariski square of §2.3.1, from the cover by the two affine-line charts with overlap $\mathbb G_m$, the multiplicative-group stack; $S^1\otimes-$ denotes pointed suspension. A motivic spectrum $E$ is fundamental when $\partial\otimes\mathrm{id}_E$ has a retraction, whose composite with that map is homotopic to the identity of $\mathbb P^1\otimes E$. Orientable spectra are fundamental. For fundamental coefficient stabilization, assume $\mathcal V$ is compactly generated and $(-)^{\mathbb P^1}:\mathcal V\to\mathcal V$ preserves filtered colimits, as in §§2.2.1–2.2.2. Writing $\mathrm{fd}$ for the full subcategory specified by this retraction condition, Theorem 2.4.5 gives the adjoint equivalence $\Sigma^\infty:\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\mathcal V)^{\mathrm{fd}}\simeq\operatorname{Sp}_{\mathbb P^1}(\operatorname{Sp}(\mathcal V))^{\mathrm{fd}}:\Omega^\infty$. With these hypotheses and multiplicative $\mathcal V$, left modules over any fundamental motivic $E_1$-ring form a stable infinity-category; this applies in particular to an oriented motivic $E_1$-ring. The compactness assumptions are used for Theorem 2.4.5 and this stability application, rather than being requirements for the symmetric-spectrum construction alone.

For an oriented motivic spectrum $E$, the projective bundle formula (PBF) is the additional condition of Definition 3.2.1: for every $n\geq1$, writing $\xi_n=c_1(\mathcal O(1))$ on $\mathbb P^n$ pointed by a fixed coordinate section (the infinity section when $n=1$), the actual finite class-power map is an equivalence
$$\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_n^i:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}^{-i}E\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }E^{\mathbb P^n}.$$
Here powers mean iterated shifted Chern operations, and in the linearly oriented ring case they are the actual products. If $\mathcal V$ is defined over $S$, $E$ satisfies this PBF, $V$ is a vector bundle of finite constant rank $r$ on an $S$-stack $X$, and $p:\mathbb P(V)\to X$ is the projective bundle of line quotients with $\xi=c_1(\mathcal O(1))$, Lemma 3.2.3 gives the equivalence
$$\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\xi^i p^*:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{r-1}\Sigma_{\mathbb P^1}^{-i}E^{X_+}\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }E^{\mathbb P(V)_+}.$$
The first formula is reduced and uses powers $1,\ldots,n$; the bundle formula uses disjoint basepoints and includes power zero. Orientation alone does not assert PBF. These constructions and conditional results apply over arbitrary qcqs derived bases without imposing $\mathbb A^1$-invariance or finite quasi-smooth transfers.
