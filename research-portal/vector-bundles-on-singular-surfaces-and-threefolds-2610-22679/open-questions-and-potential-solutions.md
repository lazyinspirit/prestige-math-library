### Research insight 01 · Chern coordinates detect self-duality of critical-rank bundles

#### Insight

**Kind:** corollary

**Evidence status:** proposed

Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $X$ a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme of dimension $d=2$ or $3$, with the foundational assumptions of the supplied motivic constructions. Assume the applicable Chern-class bijection of Theorem A or B and the specified motivic splitting, Whitney and determinant identities. Write $H_i(X)=H^{2i,i}_{\mathrm{mot}}(X,\mathbb Z)$, $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[2]=\{[L]:L^{\otimes2}\cong\mathcal O_X\}$, and $G[2]=\{g\in G:2g=0\}$. Duality on $\mathrm{Vect}_d(X)$ corresponds to $(a_1,a_2)\mapsto(-a_1,a_2)$ for $d=2$ and $(a_1,a_2,a_3)\mapsto(-a_1,a_2,-a_3)$ for $d=3$. Consequently, isomorphism classes of underlying self-dual rank-two bundles on a surface are classified by $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[2]\times H_2(X)$. Before imposing any top-group torsion assertion, the analogous rank-three fixed locus is $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[2]\times H_2(X)\times H_3(X)[2]$. If multiplication by $2$ on $H_3(X)$ is injective, as asserted by Proposition 2.6, the rank-three parameter set reduces to $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[2]\times H_2(X)$, with $c_3=0$. Assuming also the splitting implication established in the proof of Corollary 6.10, every such rank-three bundle is $E'\oplus\mathcal O_X$ for a rank-two bundle $E'$. Every such complement satisfies $E'\oplus\mathcal O_X\cong E'^\vee\oplus\mathcal O_X$; cancellation of that summand is not asserted.

#### Argument and next steps

Dualizing a locally split flag reverses its line quotients and replaces each quotient line by its inverse. Line-class additivity therefore negates every Chern root. Whitney and injective flag pullback give $c_i(E^\vee)=(-1)^ic_i(E)$ in the actual integral motivic groups. Theorem A or B then identifies self-dual isomorphism classes with fixed coordinate tuples. Weight one identifies the first fixed coordinate with a two-torsion determinant. In rank three the remaining odd coordinate belongs to $H_3(X)[2]$; the additional two-torsion hypothesis makes it zero. The splitting implication then applies, and dualizing any resulting decomposition proves stable self-duality of its rank-two complement.

A short exact sequence of vector bundles splits locally: in a frame for its locally free quotient, lift the finitely many basis sections after restricting to a sufficiently small open neighbourhood. Its dual sequence is therefore exact. Thus duality is a well-defined involution on bundle isomorphism classes, and it takes a flag with line quotients $L_1,\ldots,L_r$ to a reversed flag with line quotients $L_r^\vee,\ldots,L_1^\vee$. 2. Line-class additivity gives $c_1(\mathcal O)=2c_1(\mathcal O)$, hence $c_1(\mathcal O)=0$. Evaluation gives $L\otimes L^\vee\cong\mathcal O$, so $c_1(L^\vee)=-c_1(L)$. Choose the supplied motivic flag pullback $q:Y\to X$ for $E$, and write $x_j=c_1(L_j)$. By Whitney and step 1, $q^*c_i(E)=e_i(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ and $q^*c_i(E^\vee)=e_i(-x_1,\ldots,-x_r)=(-1)^ie_i(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$. Each term of $e_i$ contains exactly $i$ factors. ### Research question 01

#### Research question

Let $X$ be a geometrically integral normal affine surface of finite type over $\mathbb R$. Is the map $E\mapsto(c_1(E),c_2(E),[E(\mathbb R)])$ injective on isomorphism classes of rank-two algebraic vector bundles, where $c_i(E)\in H^{2i}_{\mathrm{mot}}(X,\mathbb Z(i))$ uses Elmanto–Morrow motivic cohomology and the last coordinate records the isomorphism class of the continuous, unoriented real vector bundle on the possibly singular or empty Euclidean space $X(\mathbb R)$?

#### Why it matters

The article's Theorem A addresses algebraically closed fields. Independently, Lerbet proves the proposed criterion for smooth real affine surfaces. The singular extension therefore asks whether real realization still supplies the information missing from motivic Chern classes. Normality gives an additional useful reduction: an isomorphism on the smooth locus extends uniquely across the singularities.

#### Potential solution

**Solution scope:** none

**Evidence status:** Unresolved

No proof or counterexample for the singular case is supplied. Two rigorous reductions are established: restriction of vector bundles to the smooth locus is fully faithful, and the real forms of a fixed complex bundle are classified by explicit semilinear involution orbits. These reductions do not constitute a partial solution to injectivity.

#### Argument and next steps

Write $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, $U=X_{\mathrm{sm}/\mathbb R}$ and $B=A[i]$. Normality gives extension of bundle morphisms from $U$ by the height-one intersection description of a normal domain. Thus producing an algebraic isomorphism on $U$ suffices. However, if $X$ is singular, $U$ is not affine: its global functions are $A$, so an affine $U$ would make its inclusion into $X$ an isomorphism. For the descent approach, fix an actual real form of a rank-two projective $B$-module $P$, with conjugate-semilinear involution $\tau$. Set $G=\operatorname{Aut}_B(P)$ and $\sigma(h)=\tau h\tau^{-1}$. Other real forms are represented by $g\in G$ satisfying $g\sigma(g)=1$, modulo $g\sim hg\sigma(h)^{-1}$. Their modules are $M_g=P^{g\tau}$. The unresolved calculation is whether equality of the three proposed invariants of $M_g$ and $M_{g'}$ forces this equivalence. If complex Chern classes classify the relevant complexifications, naturality reduces every equal-invariant pair to such a fixed fiber. That last bridge is explicitly conditional here; the article's Theorem A motivates it but is not used as independent proof of its own prerequisite chain.

#### Proof sketch or overall strategy

Establish the formulation independently of the article, verify the smooth boundary, then develop two complementary reductions: extension of morphisms across codimension at least two, and an elementary calculation of effective real descent. Isolate the remaining invariant-separation problem without presenting the conditional strategy as a solution.
