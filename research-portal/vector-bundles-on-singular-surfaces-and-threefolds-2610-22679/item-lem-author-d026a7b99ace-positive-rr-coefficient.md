## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the compatible site-size and universe bounds of the supplied motivic and perfect K-theory constructions. For the RR polynomial of codimension $d=3$ and class index $q=3$, $P^3_3(n)=2n$ for every integer rank $n$. In particular the supported third Chern class of the positive point K-generator is twice the positive motivic point generator. The coefficient is integral and the same in every characteristic.

[F0] For this test choose an algebraically closed field $k$: every nonconstant polynomial over $k$ has a root. Every irreducible nonconstant polynomial is then linear and separable, so $k$ is perfect, as required by the smooth comparisons below. Use the standard charts of [math:def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts): $X=\mathbb P^3_k$, with homogeneous coordinates $X_0,X_1,X_2,X_3$, is covered by $U_j=D_+(X_j)=\operatorname{Spec}k[X_a/X_j:a\ne j]$. Set $x=[1:0:0:0]$ and $i:\operatorname{Spec}k\hookrightarrow X$. By [math:ex-polynomial-ring-flat-smooth](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/ex-polynomial-ring-flat-smooth), the charts are smooth three-dimensional affine spaces. Smoothness is tested at each point on such a source chart, so $X$ is smooth over $k$. Their coordinate rings are noetherian by [math:cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian), since every ideal in a field is either zero or the whole field. By [math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme), this finite Noetherian affine cover makes $X$ a Noetherian scheme and in particular quasi-compact. The chart algebras are finitely generated over $k$, so the structural morphism is locally of finite type and, being quasi-compact, finite type. Apply [lem-adjudicate-a6bf10648fa2-finite-type-field-qcqs](item-lem-adjudicate-a6bf10648fa2-finite-type-field-qcqs): every open subset of $X$ is quasi-compact and $X$ is qcqs. In particular the punctured complement and the neighbourhoods used for support excision are in its domain. The charts are irreducible, have nonempty pairwise overlaps, and their common open where all coordinates are nonzero is dense, so $X$ is irreducible. The dimension calculation of [math:cor-dimension-affine-and-projective-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-dimension-affine-and-projective-space) makes it pure of dimension three. The twists $\mathcal O(m)$ are those of [math:def-twisting-sheaf-proj](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-twisting-sheaf-proj). The polynomial graded ring is generated in degree one, so [math:thm-twisting-sheaf-invertible-standard-graded](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-twisting-sheaf-invertible-standard-graded) makes them lines, with frame $X_j^m$ on $U_j$ and $\mathcal O(m)\otimes\mathcal O(n)\cong\mathcal O(m+n)$. To match the quotient convention in [F2], a quotient of $\mathcal O^4$ to a line is locally specified by four generating coordinate sections. On the locus where its $j$th section is a frame, normalize that section to $1$; the remaining three coefficients give exactly the chart $U_j$ and its stated ratio transitions. The quotient line frames have the same transitions as $X_j$, identifying the tautological quotient line with $\mathcal O(1)$. Thus the projective bundle in [F2] is this same $X$.

[F7] For a smooth closed point $z$ in the positive-coordinate interface, use its smooth affine neighbourhood $V$ of pure dimension three. The relative dimension of $V\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ at $z$ is three: by [math:def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism) it is the local dimension of the geometric fiber, here $V$ itself since $k$ is algebraically closed, and the component formula there gives three. The local-on-source formulation of [math:thm-smooth-local-standard-form](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-smooth-local-standard-form) therefore gives, after shrinking about $z$, an affine open $W_0=\operatorname{Spec}B_0\subseteq V$ and an étale $k$-map $q_0:W_0\to\mathbb A^3_k$. Open restriction preserves local finite type by [math:lem-finite-type-local-on-source-and-target](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-finite-type-local-on-source-and-target); these opens are quasi-compact by the finite-type field lemma in [F0], so $W_0$ is finite type over $k$ and $B_0$ is a finite-type $k$-algebra. Since $z$ is closed, [math:lem-maximal-ideal-residue-field-of-an-affine-algebra-is-finite](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-maximal-ideal-residue-field-of-an-affine-algebra-is-finite) makes $\kappa(z)/k$ finite. Every element of this extension has an irreducible minimal polynomial over $k$, which must be linear by algebraic closedness, so $\kappa(z)=k$. Thus $q_0(z)=a\in k^3$. Subtract $a_j$ from each coordinate function of $q_0$ to obtain $q_1(z)=0$. The inverse target translation adds $a_j$; it is a $k$-isomorphism, so merely reidentifies the local rings and geometric fibers in the étale definition, and $q_1$ remains étale. The map $q_1$ is finite type: generators of $B_0$ over $k$ also generate it over the image of $k[t_1,t_2,t_3]$. By [math:lem-etale-stable-base-change-composition](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-etale-stable-base-change-composition), its scheme-theoretic fiber $F=q_1^{-1}(0)$ is étale over $k$, and it is finite type because its coordinate algebra is the quotient $B_0/(q_{1,1},q_{1,2},q_{1,3})$. By [math:thm-etale-over-algebraically-closed-field-discrete-smooth-points](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-etale-over-algebraically-closed-field-discrete-smooth-points), $F$ is a finite disjoint union of reduced copies of $\operatorname{Spec}k$, one being $z$. Each other point $z_\ell$ is closed in $W_0$, since $F$ is closed and its points are closed in $F$. Let $\mathfrak m_z,\mathfrak m_\ell$ be the corresponding distinct maximal ideals of $B_0$. For each other point choose $g_\ell\in\mathfrak m_\ell\setminus\mathfrak m_z$ and put $g=\prod_\ell g_\ell$, using $g=1$ when there are no other points. This uses only finite choice. Then $g\notin\mathfrak m_z$ and $g$ vanishes at every other fiber point. By [math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion), $W=\operatorname{Spec}((B_0)_g)=D(g)$ is an affine open neighbourhood of $z$ in $W_0$. Its restricted map $q=q_1|_W$ remains étale by the germ convention, and its scheme-theoretic fiber over zero is exactly $\operatorname{Spec}k$ at $z$, with no nilpotent thickening. Localization adjoins the single generator $g^{-1}$, so $W$ is finite type over $k$. Both $W$ and $\mathbb A^3_k$ are smooth separated finite-type $k$-schemes: smoothness of $W$ is inherited from $V$, and their affineness gives separatedness by [math:cor-affine-schemes-separated](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-affine-schemes-separated). They have pure dimension three, so their rational point immersions have codimension three. The selected support comparison [lem-adjudicate-1f4b2e0378b1-flat-natural-supported-cycle-comparison](item-lem-adjudicate-1f4b2e0378b1-flat-natural-supported-cycle-comparison) is natural for this étale map and gives $q^*\delta_0=\delta_z$ for the actual positive classes of [F1]. Étale implies flat by [math:def-etale-morphism-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-etale-morphism-schemes). Flatness on local rings has the meaning of [math:def-flat-morphism-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-flat-morphism-schemes), and tensor with a flat module preserves exact sequences by [math:def-flat-and-faithfully-flat-modules-and-ring-maps](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-flat-and-faithfully-flat-modules-and-ring-maps). The pair pullback of [def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface](item-def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface) therefore carries $u_0$ to $u_z$: compute it by the free coordinate Koszul complex of the origin, tensor stalkwise with the flat local rings of $W$, and use the reduced one-point fiber to identify its sole cohomology sheaf with the residue sheaf at $z$. This uses the stalk criterion in [F4]. The full natural pointed construction in Proof 1.1 and 2.2 of [lem-author-d026a7b99ace-supported-chern-map](item-lem-author-d026a7b99ace-supported-chern-map), evaluated on the map of restriction arrows for $(W,\{z\})\to(\mathbb A^3_k,\{0\})$, gives the commuting square for the actual EM supported operation. ## Proof
**Given:** [A1], any algebraically closed field $k$ of arbitrary characteristic, $X,x,i$ and $U_j$ from [F0], and integer $n$.

**Goal:** Determine the universal integer polynomial value without a sign convention guess or division.

1.1 The coordinate sections $X_1,X_2,X_3$ of $\mathcal O(1)$ define the sheaf Koszul complex with terms $\mathcal O(-p)^{\binom3p}$ in homological degrees $p=0,1,2,3$ and differential the alternating deletion formula of [F4], multiplying a deleted basis vector by its coordinate section. These global bundle maps commute with restriction, so their chart descriptions agree on overlaps. On $U_0$ the complex is $K(y_1,y_2,y_3;k[y_1,y_2,y_3])$, where $y_a=X_a/X_0$. Quotienting by the first $a-1$ coordinates gives the polynomial ring in the remaining coordinates; multiplication by $y_a$ is injective by comparison of monomial coefficients, and the final quotient is $k\ne0$. Thus [F4] resolves the reduced point on $U_0$. On $U_j$ for $j=1,2,3$, the $j$th coefficient is $X_j/X_j=1$; wedging with its basis vector gives a contraction $ds+sd=1$, so the complex is contractible there and $i_*k$ restricts to zero. By localization and stalkwise exactness in [F4] these chart calculations give the global resolution $0\to\mathcal O(-3)\to\mathcal O(-2)^3\to\mathcal O(-1)^3\to\mathcal O\to i_*k\to0$. The construction also checks that $i$ is regular of codimension three. The object-class rule [F5] gives $[i_*k]=1-3[\mathcal O(-1)]+3[\mathcal O(-2)]-[\mathcal O(-3)]$ in actual perfect $K_0(X)$, where $1=[\mathcal O]$. By [F0] and additive first-Chern normalization in [F3], $c_1(\mathcal O(-a))=-ah$ and a line has no higher Chern coefficients. Consequently [F3] gives $c_t([i_*k])=(1-2ht)^3/((1-ht)^3(1-3ht))$. 2.1 Expand only through degree three in the integral formal-series ring of [F3]. The numerator is $1-6ht+12h^2t^2-8h^3t^3$; the denominator is $1-6ht+12h^2t^2-10h^3t^3$ modulo $t^4$. Thus their quotient is $1+2h^3t^3$ modulo $t^4$, with first two coefficients zero. For $n$ times this perfect class, actual total Cartan gives the $n$th power, whose degree-three coefficient is $2nh^3$: modulo $t^4$ the square of $2h^3t^3$ is zero, so positive powers multiply that term by $n$, and inversion changes its sign for negative powers. No integer is inverted in the coefficient ring, including in positive characteristic; $H^{6,3}(X)=\mathbb Z h^3$ is integral motivic cohomology. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply the imported RR identity of [F1] to the classical regular pair $(X,x)$, with $X=\mathbb P^3_k$, $q=d=3$, $r=0$ and $a=n[\mathcal O_x]$. This use is within the general classical finite-dimensional Noetherian theorem; the regularity and its length-three resolution were checked in step 1.1. The classical refined K pushforward is $n\bar u_x$, where $\bar u_x$ is the residue-object generator, and its refined motivic pushforward is $P^3_3(n)\bar\delta_x$. Forgetting support commutes with $c^{\rm RR}_{3,x}$ by [F6]. By [F2], the latter class maps to $P^3_3(n)\bar h^3$. By [F6], the same Koszul expression and the integral expansion in step 2.1 compute its ordinary classical third Chern class as $2n\bar h^3$. Since $\bar h^3$ freely generates the classical degree-six group, $P^3_3(n)=2n$. Separately, on this projective pair the actual EM support operation on $nu_x$ forgets to $c_3^{\rm EM}(n[i_*k])=2nh^3$ by step 2.1 and its support-forgetting square in [F1]. Injectivity in [F2] therefore gives $c^{\rm EM}_{3,x}(nu_x)=2n\delta_x$. This proves the actual projective supported calculation without identifying it with the RR operation. 4.1 Restrict the actual projective supported identity of step 3.1 to the standard affine chart $(\mathbb A^3_k,0)$, using the open-excision square in [F1]. It gives $c^{\rm EM}_{3,0}(nu_0)=2n\delta_0$ for every integer $n$. Now let $z$ be any smooth closed point on a pure-three-dimensional finite-type $k$-scheme in the positive-coordinate interface. Choose its smooth affine neighbourhood and the étale map $q:W\to\mathbb A^3_k$ of [F7]. Pullback sends $nu_0$ to $nu_z$ by its flat Koszul calculation and sends $\delta_0$ to $\delta_z$ by its specified cycle comparison. Its actual-operation naturality square consequently gives $c^{\rm EM}_{3,z}(nu_z)=2n\delta_z$ on $W$. Open excision in [F1] transports the equality to the original ambient scheme. No support-forgetting injectivity on that ambient scheme is required. In particular the positive point generator has third class twice the positive motivic generator. The classical universal polynomial value in step 3.1 and this actual EM point calculation are integral for every characteristic and every integer $n$.
