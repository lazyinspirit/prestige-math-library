## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed universe bounds of the completed comparison suppliers. Let $k$ be a perfect field, $U$ a smooth finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three, and $i:C\hookrightarrow U$ an integral closed curve of codimension two, with generic point $\eta$. No smoothness or normality of $C$ is assumed. Use the actual Elmanto–Morrow filtered nonconnective perfect-complex $K$-theory and write $K_C(U)=\operatorname{fib}(K(U)\to K(U\setminus C))$ and $F_C^jK(U)=\operatorname{fib}(F^jK(U)\to F^jK(U\setminus C))$.

The group $\pi_0K_C(U)$ is canonically the Grothendieck group of perfect complexes on $U$ supported on $C$. Under this identification, the arrow in the supported exact sequence
$$\pi_0F_C^3K(U)\longrightarrow\pi_0K_C(U)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z[C]\cong\mathbb Z$$
is generic localization: it sends a supported perfect complex $E$ to $\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{U,\eta}}H^n(E)_\eta$, and the positive cycle $[C]$ corresponds to $1$. Its kernel, equivalently the image of $\pi_0F_C^3K(U)$, is the subgroup generated by the classes of perfect complexes supported at closed points of $C$. Indeed it is generated by the individual residue-sheaf classes $[i_{x*}\kappa(x)]$, where $i_x:\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x)\hookrightarrow U$ and $x$ ranges over closed points of $C$.

If $U$ is an open subscheme of a finite-type $k$-scheme $X$, these points are closed smooth points of $X$. In particular this applies to a curve contained in the smooth locus in the article's algebraically closed-field setting.

Here integral means reduced and irreducible; a curve is one-dimensional. A perfect complex is a complex locally quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite locally free sheaves. It is supported on a subset when all its cohomology stalks vanish outside that subset. Its Grothendieck group has one generator for each perfect object and the usual distinguished-triangle relations. The length of a finite-length module is the number of simple factors in a composition series. Generic localization is the map induced by restriction to the generic point, expressed through coherent $G$-theory as generic rank; the alternating-length formula specifies the same map on supported perfect objects.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the fixed universe bounds, $k$, $U$, $C$ and the actual supported filtration as in the Statement. For the final assertion, also let $U\subseteq X$ be an open immersion into a finite-type $k$-scheme.

**Goal:** Identify the actual supported sequence and its second arrow, compute its kernel by closed-point classes, and establish the stated smooth-point conclusion.

[F1] [lem-curve-supported-second-stage](item-lem-curve-supported-second-stage) supplies the actual equivalence $\epsilon_2:F_C^2K(U)\simeq K_C(U)$ and the exact sequence after transport along $\epsilon_2$. [lem-smooth-leading-cycle-normalization](item-lem-smooth-leading-cycle-normalization) identifies the transported projection with alternating generic length times the positive supported cycle $[C]$. These suppliers require the stated perfect-field, smoothness, dimension, AC and universe hypotheses.

[F2] [prop-perfect-k-localization-input](item-prop-perfect-k-localization-input) identifies the category-defined supported nonconnective $K$-spectrum with the actual restriction fiber for a qcqs scheme and a quasi-compact open complement. [prop-supported-k-zero-devissage](item-prop-supported-k-zero-devissage) identifies its degree-zero group with $G_0(C)$ and sends the coherent class $[F]$ inversely to the class of $i_*F[0]$. Here $G_0(C)$ is generated by coherent sheaves with short-exact-sequence relations. The ambient scheme must be regular Noetherian and finite-dimensional.

[F3] [lem-curve-generic-rank-localization](item-lem-curve-generic-rank-localization) states, under AC, that for an integral Noetherian one-dimensional scheme the kernel of generic rank on $G_0$ is generated by the residue sheaves of closed points. Under the supported comparison it identifies generic rank with alternating generic length; its localization description is the filtered limit over nonempty opens.

[F4] [lem-koszul-point-support-class](item-lem-koszul-point-support-class) proves under AC that the residue sheaf of a closed smooth point of a finite-type scheme over a perfect field is perfect and supported at that point, using a local Koszul resolution.

## Proof

1.1 A finite-type scheme over a field is Noetherian, quasi-compact and quasi-separated; $U\setminus C$ is quasi-compact because $U$ is Noetherian. Smoothness over the perfect field makes $U$ regular, and its dimension is finite. Thus the hypotheses of [F2] hold. Its localization equivalence identifies the actual fiber $K_C(U)$ with the $K$-spectrum of the full category of perfect complexes supported on $C$. The degree-zero comparison and triangle presentation in the supported dévissage identify its $\pi_0$ with the stated Grothendieck group and canonically with $G_0(C)$. [given, F2]

2.1 The pair $C\subset U$ satisfies all hypotheses of [F1]. Transporting the supported graded exact sequence along the actual augmentation $\epsilon_2$ gives the displayed sequence. By [F1] its second arrow takes $[E]$ to the alternating generic-length coefficient times $[C]$, with positive generator. By [F3], under the specific $G_0(C)$ comparison of step 1.1 this coefficient is generic rank, equivalently localization to the generic point in the stated filtered-limit sense. This identifies the actual arrow, rather than inferring it from an abstract isomorphism of its target with $\mathbb Z$. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3], the kernel of generic rank on $G_0(C)$ is generated by $[\kappa(x)]$ for closed points $x$ of $C$. Since $C$ is closed in $U$, each such $x$ is closed in $U$. The inverse in [F2] sends $[\kappa(x)]$ to the class of the same residue sheaf, viewed as a perfect complex on $U$ in degree zero. It is supported at $x$; [F4] independently supplies its local perfectness. Consequently the kernel is generated by these point classes. Conversely every perfect complex supported at a closed point has zero stalk at $\eta$, so its alternating generic length is zero. Hence the subgroup generated by all such complexes is exactly the kernel. Exactness in step 2.1 identifies this kernel with the image of $\pi_0F_C^3K(U)$. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 Suppose $U$ is open in a finite-type $k$-scheme $X$. A closed point of the finite-type $k$-scheme $U$ has residue field finite over $k$. Its image in $X$ is therefore a closed point: on an affine neighbourhood its prime has field quotient finite over $k$, hence is maximal. Since $U$ is smooth over $k$, $X$ is smooth over $k$ at each such point by locality of smoothness on the source. Thus all generators in step 3.1 are supported at closed smooth points of $X$. This proves the final assertion and the lemma. [given, step 3.1] ∎
