## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X$ be a finite-type scheme over a perfect field $k$, and let $x$ be a closed point at which $X$ is smooth over $k$. Write $i:\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x)\to X$ for the closed immersion of the point. There is a smooth affine open neighbourhood $V$ of $x$ on which the ideal of $x$ is generated by a regular sequence whose images in $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ form a regular system of parameters. Its Koszul complex is a finite free resolution of $i_*\kappa(x)|_V$. Thus $i$ is a regular immersion, and the residue sheaf $i_*\kappa(x)$, placed in degree zero, is perfect on $X$ and supported on $\{x\}$.

Consequently it has a distinguished class $[i_*\kappa(x)]$ in $K_{0,\{x\}}(X)$, whose image in $K_0(X)$ is its ordinary perfect-complex class. This class is independent of the choice of neighbourhood and parameters. No claim that it generates the supported group is made here; that identification requires a separate purity or dévissage result.

A perfect field is a field whose algebraic extensions are separable. A regular local ring is a noetherian local ring whose dimension equals the dimension of its maximal ideal modulo its square over its residue field. A regular system of parameters is a minimal generating sequence of that maximal ideal of length equal to the dimension. A sequence is regular if each element is a non-zero-divisor modulo its predecessors and the final quotient is nonzero. A regular immersion is a closed immersion whose ideal is locally generated by a regular sequence. The Koszul complex on a sequence $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ has terms $\bigwedge^j A^d$ and differential given by contraction with $(f_1,\ldots,f_d)$; for the empty sequence it is $A$ in degree zero. The residue sheaf $i_*\kappa(x)$ has stalk $\kappa(x)$ at $x$ and zero stalks elsewhere.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a finite-type $k$-scheme $X$ over a perfect field, and a smooth closed point $x$. Use the same universe bounds as the supplied perfect categories and nonconnective K-theory.

**Goal:** Construct the local Koszul resolution, prove global perfectness and support, and identify the resulting supported class.

[F1] A smooth finite-type scheme over a perfect field has regular local rings under AC. A finite-type algebra over a field is a quotient of a finite-variable polynomial algebra, hence is noetherian; quotients and localizations of noetherian rings remain noetherian. ([math:thm-regular-equals-smooth-over-perfect-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-regular-equals-smooth-over-perfect-field), [math:def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras), [math:lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct), [math:thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations))

[F2] Under AC, lifts of a cotangent-space basis in a regular local ring form a regular system of parameters. Every such system is a regular sequence, and its Koszul complex resolves the residue field. ([math:lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis), [math:thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay), [math:lem-regular-local-residue-field-koszul-resolution](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-regular-local-residue-field-koszul-resolution), [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice))

[F3] Perfectness is the local condition of being represented by a bounded complex of finite locally free modules. The supported perfect category consists of perfect objects whose cohomology stalks vanish outside the specified subset; it is stable and idempotent complete. ([def-scheme-perfect-complexes](item-def-scheme-perfect-complexes))

[F4] For qcqs schemes the supported K-spectrum is the nonconnective K-theory of the supported perfect category, and its exact inclusion induces the support-to-global map. The interface supplies essential smallness and permits passage to an equivalent small presentation. For an idempotent-complete stable category, the degree-zero comparison sends each object to its K-theory class and is natural for exact functors. ([def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface](item-def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface), [thm-author-3a003d7b5208-nonconnective-k-zero-input](item-thm-author-3a003d7b5208-nonconnective-k-zero-input))

[F5] A $k$-scheme means a scheme over $\operatorname{Spec}k$. An affine open has its restricted structure sheaf and an affine presentation; a finite-type morphism is locally of finite type and quasi-compact. Quasi-compactness of a morphism means that inverse images of quasi-compact opens are quasi-compact. Smoothness uses local standard smooth presentations, so a smooth point has a principal affine neighbourhood on which the morphism is smooth. Principal localization identifies $\operatorname{Spec}A_g$ with $D(g)$, and the stalk at a prime is the corresponding prime localization. ([math:def-scheme-over-base](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-scheme-over-base), [math:def-affine-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-scheme), [math:def-affine-open-subscheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-open-subscheme), [math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism), [math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism), [math:def-smooth-morphism-classical](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-smooth-morphism-classical), [math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion), [math:thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization))

[F6] Closed affine points correspond to maximal ideals, and their residue fields are $\kappa(x)=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x\cong A/\mathfrak m$. The quotient map defines a closed immersion into the affine chart. A closed immersion means a homeomorphism onto a closed subset with surjective structure-sheaf map. On a noetherian affine chart the associated sheaf of $A/\mathfrak m$ is its closed-point skyscraper. ([math:cor-closed-points-of-spectrum-are-maximal-ideals](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-closed-points-of-spectrum-are-maximal-ideals), [math:def-residue-field-scheme-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-residue-field-scheme-point), [math:lem-quotient-spectrum-map-is-a-closed-immersion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-quotient-spectrum-map-is-a-closed-immersion), [math:def-closed-immersion-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-closed-immersion-schemes), [math:ex-skyscraper-coherent-closed-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/ex-skyscraper-coherent-closed-point))

[F7] In a noetherian ring every ideal is finitely generated, and every submodule of a finite module is finitely generated. Localization is exact and commutes with quotient modules and direct sums. In the fraction construction, $m/1=0$ in $S^{-1}M$ exactly when some $s\in S$ annihilates $m$. Thus a finite module whose localization at $\mathfrak m$ is zero is killed by a single element outside $\mathfrak m$: take the product of annihilating denominators for a finite generating list. ([math:def-noetherian-ring-and-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-noetherian-ring-and-module), [math:thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian), [math:thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact), [math:thm-localisation-of-modules-commutes-with-quotients-and-sums](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-localisation-of-modules-commutes-with-quotients-and-sums), [math:def-localisation-of-a-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-localisation-of-a-module))

[F8] Over any commutative ring, a finite regular sequence with finite free coefficient module has a finite free Koszul resolution of its quotient. This interface does not require the ring to be local. The empty sequence resolves the ring itself. ([math:cor-koszul-complex-resolves-a-regular-quotient](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-koszul-complex-resolves-a-regular-quotient))

[F9] Associated sheaf is the module-to-quasi-coherent-sheaf equivalence on an affine scheme. For an $A$-module $M$, its associated sheaf $\widetilde M$ has sections $M_g$ on $D(g)$, with canonical localization restrictions. Its stalk at a prime is the localized module, naturally in the module; a sequence of sheaves is exact if and only if all its stalk sequences are exact. In particular, localizing an exact module resolution and then using these stalk identifications proves exactness of its associated sheaf resolution. ([math:thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence), [math:lem-associated-sheaf-sections-basic-open](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-associated-sheaf-sections-basic-open), [math:lem-associated-sheaf-stalk-localization](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-associated-sheaf-stalk-localization), [math:thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise))

[F10] A noetherian ring has noetherian spectrum. Under AC every open subspace of a noetherian topological space is quasi-compact. A scheme is quasi-separated when every intersection of two affine opens is quasi-compact, and qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated. ([math:thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum), [math:lem-noetherian-subspaces-and-compact-opens](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-noetherian-subspaces-and-compact-opens), [math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme))

## Proof

1.1 Start with a finite-type affine chart at $x$ from [F5] and shrink it to a principal affine neighbourhood on which the structure morphism is smooth. Its coordinate ring is still of finite type over $k$: adjoining the inverse of the shrinking element adds one algebra generator. Write this neighbourhood as $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. The closed point corresponds to a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ by [F6]. By [F1] and [F5], $A$ is noetherian and $R=A_{\mathfrak m}=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is regular local. Choose a basis of its finite-dimensional cotangent space and lift it to a regular system of parameters $t_1,\ldots,t_d$ by [F2]. Write each $t_i=a_i/s_i$ with $a_i\in\mathfrak m$ and $s_i\notin\mathfrak m$, and put $f_i=a_i$. Multiplication of individual basis vectors by these units preserves the cotangent basis, so the images of $f_1,\ldots,f_d$ are also a regular system of parameters by [F2]. For $d=0$ use the empty tuple. To construct the stated morphism $i$, compose $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak m)\hookrightarrow U$ from [F6] with the open inclusion into $X$. Its underlying image is the closed singleton $\{x\}$. On $U$ its structure-sheaf map is surjective by the quotient construction; on $X\setminus\{x\}$ it is the map to the zero sheaf. These opens cover $X$, so the stalk criterion [F9] and the closed-immersion definition [F6] make $i$ a closed immersion into $X$. The identification $A/\mathfrak m\cong\kappa(x)$ is canonical. [given, F1, F2, F5, F6, F9, choose, construct]

2.1 The finite $A$-module $\mathfrak m/(f_1,\ldots,f_d)$ localizes to zero at $\mathfrak m$, since the $f_i$ generate $\mathfrak mR$. By [F7] it is killed by some $h_0\notin\mathfrak m$. For each $j$, put $M_j=A/(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})$. The kernel of multiplication by $f_j$ on $M_j$ is finite by [F7]. Exactness of localization identifies its localization at $\mathfrak m$ with the multiplication kernel over $R$, which is zero by [F2]. Thus some $h_j\notin\mathfrak m$ kills this kernel. Set $h=h_0h_1\cdots h_d$ and $V=D(h)=\operatorname{Spec}A_h$ using [F5]. On $V$ the $f_i$ generate $\mathfrak mA_h$, and exactness of localization and its compatibility with quotients make every multiplication by $f_j$ on $A_h/(f_1,\ldots,f_{j-1})$ injective. The quotient $A_h/\mathfrak mA_h$ is canonically $A/\mathfrak m\ne0$: localization commutes with this quotient and $h$ has nonzero, hence invertible, image in the field $A/\mathfrak m$. Therefore all the preceding quotients are nonzero as well, and the sequence is regular on $A_h$. This includes $d=0$, when $\mathfrak mA_h=0$ and the empty sequence is regular. [F2, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F8] over the actual ring $A_h$, with coefficient module $A_h$, to the regular sequence from step 2.1. It gives a finite free resolution of $A_h/\mathfrak mA_h$. Place its exterior-power term $\bigwedge^j A_h^d$ in cochain degree $-j$, with contraction differential, and augment degree zero to the quotient. By [F9] its associated sheaf complex is exact: at every prime its augmented stalk complex is the localization of that exact module resolution, which remains exact by [F7]. A free module $A_h^r$ has associated sheaf $\mathcal O_V^r$, since on each basic open both have sections given by the same $r$-fold free module over the localized ring, with the same restriction maps. The resolved sheaf is $i_*\kappa(x)|_V$ by [F6], applied to the noetherian ring $A_h$ and its maximal ideal $\mathfrak mA_h$. Explicitly, for any open $W\subseteq X$, the globally defined residue sheaf has sections $\kappa(x)$ if $x\in W$ and zero otherwise, with identity restrictions between opens containing $x$ and scalar action through $\mathcal O_X(W)\to\mathcal O_{X,x}\to\kappa(x)$. This is the sheaf direct image along the constructed $i$; its restriction to $V$ is precisely that skyscraper. Thus the Koszul sheaf complex resolves this restriction. Since its ideal is generated by a regular sequence on a neighbourhood of the only point of the closed subscheme, $i$ is a regular immersion under the inline definition in the Statement. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 The stalks of $i_*\kappa(x)$ vanish away from $x$: because $\{x\}$ is closed, each other point has a neighbourhood disjoint from it. Its stalk at $x$ is the nonzero field $\kappa(x)$ by the explicit section formula in step 3.1. On $V$ step 3.1 gives a bounded finite free resolution; on $X\setminus\{x\}$ the sheaf is zero, represented by the zero complex. These opens cover $X$, so [F3] makes the residue sheaf, placed in degree zero, perfect with support exactly $\{x\}$. The globally defined object is this sheaf; no extension by zero of a locally free resolution is used. [F3, step 3.1, given]

5.1 Since $\operatorname{Spec}k$ has one point, it is quasi-compact; the quasi-compact structure morphism in [F5] therefore makes $X$ quasi-compact. Local finite type gives an affine cover with finite-type coordinate rings; choose a finite subcover $U_1,\ldots,U_n$. All these rings are noetherian by [F1], so each $U_a$ has noetherian underlying space by [F10]. For any open $W\subseteq X$, each $W\cap U_a$ is quasi-compact by [F10]. An open cover of $W$ restricts to a cover of each such intersection; take its finite subcover there and unite these finitely many finite subcovers. Thus every open $W$ is quasi-compact. In particular all intersections of affine opens are quasi-compact, proving quasi-separatedness by [F10]; hence $X$ is qcqs. Apply [F4] to the supported perfect object of step 4.1, using the stable idempotent-complete supported category from [F3] and its equivalent small presentation supplied by [F4]. Its object class lies in $K_{0,\{x\}}(X)$, and naturality for the exact inclusion sends it to the class of the same object in $K_0(X)$. Every choice of parameters and neighbourhood in steps 1.1 through 3.1 resolves the same globally defined sheaf, so the supported object and its class are independent of those choices. AC is retained through [F1], [F2], [F4], [F6], [F9] and [F10], and the universe bounds are those of [F4]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F9, F10, step 4.1, choose] ∎
