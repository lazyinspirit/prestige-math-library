## Statement
For every finite commutative ring $\Lambda$, the functor $X\mapsto D^b_{\mathrm{cons}}(X_{\mathrm{et}},\Lambda)$ on all qcqs schemes, with its actual derived pullbacks, is a hypercomplete arc sheaf valued in $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$. The same holds for each full subcategory of cohomological amplitude $[-n,n]$, $n\geq0$. Bounded constructible has the meaning below; no invertibility, noetherianity, regularity, finite-dimension or finite Tor-dimension hypothesis is imposed.

For a prime field $F$, an ordinary $D(\mathbb Z)$-valued cdh sheaf on all qcqs $F$-schemes is precisely a presheaf $P$ such that $P(\varnothing)\simeq0$ and every distinguished Nisnevich square and finitely presented abstract blowup square specified below is sent to a homotopy-cartesian square. Here ordinary means covering-family/Čech descent; this criterion alone asserts no unrestricted hypercompletion comparison. All constructions use suitable nested universes and Choice for the imported derived-category constructions.

## Definition
A qcqs scheme is quasi-compact and quasi-separated. Work with schemes in one universe, a skeleton of that category in a larger universe, and further universes containing the coefficient infinity-categories and their limits. A presheaf is contravariant; covering sieves and their generated Grothendieck topology have the scheme-site meanings. The notation $X_{\mathrm{et}}$ denotes the small étale site: its objects are étale morphisms $U\to X$, its arrows are $X$-morphisms, and its covers are jointly surjective families of étale morphisms. A sheaf of $\Lambda$-modules is a sheaf of sets on this site with module operations and restriction maps preserving those operations. The constant sheaf $\underline\Lambda_X$ is the sheaf associated to the constant presheaf $\Lambda$.

An arc cover $Y\to X$ of qcqs schemes is a morphism such that each map $\operatorname{Spec}V\to X$ from a valuation ring of rank at most one lifts to $Y$ after an injective local extension $V\to W$ of valuation rings, with $W$ also of rank at most one. Rank here is Krull dimension. The arc topology is generated by these singleton covers and finite disjoint-union covering families. A valuation ring and its dimension have their usual algebraic meanings; the injective extension is local when the inverse image of the maximal ideal of $W$ is the maximal ideal of $V$.

For $F=\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$ with $p$ prime, the cdh site is the qcqs prime-field site, with no finite-type or separatedness condition over $F$. Its generators are Nisnevich covering families and the families $\{Z\to X,Y\to X\}$ from cartesian squares

$$\begin{matrix}E&\longrightarrow&Y\\\downarrow&&\downarrow p\\Z&\xrightarrow{i}&X.\end{matrix}$$

Here $E=Y\times_X Z$, $i$ is a finitely presented closed immersion, $p$ is finitely presented and proper, and $Y\setminus E\to X\setminus Z$ is an isomorphism. Finitely presented means locally of finite presentation, quasi-compact and quasi-separated as a morphism; proper has its scheme-theoretic meaning. A distinguished Nisnevich square has the same pattern with $Z$ replaced by an open subscheme $U\subseteq X$, $E=Y\times_XU$, $p:Y\to X$ étale, and $Y\times_X(X\setminus U)_{\mathrm{red}}\to(X\setminus U)_{\mathrm{red}}$ an isomorphism. The residue-field lifting definition of a Nisnevich covering family is retained.

A $\Lambda$-sheaf $M$ is constructible if on each affine open of $X$ there is a finite partition into constructible locally closed subschemes on which $M$ becomes a constant finitely generated $\Lambda$-module after an étale covering. Constructible subsets are finite unions of differences of retrocompact opens, with retrocompact meaning that intersection with every quasi-compact open is quasi-compact. For finite $\Lambda$, finitely generated coefficient modules are finite sets. This definition applies to nonnoetherian schemes as well.

For the abelian category $\mathcal A_X$ of $\Lambda$-sheaves, $D(X_{\mathrm{et}},\Lambda)$ means the infinity-localization of cochain complexes in $\mathcal A_X$ at quasi-isomorphisms, not merely its homotopy category. Here a quasi-isomorphism induces isomorphisms on every $H^j$, where $H^j=\ker d^j/\operatorname{im}d^{j-1}$. The analogous localization for abelian groups is $D(\mathbb Z)$. The ringed-site Grothendieck property and the stable, presentable derived enhancements are imported below. The full subcategory $D^b_{\mathrm{cons}}$ consists of objects whose cohomology sheaves are constructible and vanish outside some finite interval; its $[-n,n]$ subcategory consists of objects with $H^j=0$ for $j\notin[-n,n]$. For a morphism $f:Y\to X$, the derived pullback is induced by exact étale inverse image with its constant $\Lambda$-action. It preserves this bounded constructible subcategory and its amplitude subcategories, with the coherent composition maps of inverse image. It is not pullback of $\mathcal O_X$-modules.

An infinity-category is modeled by a simplicial set with fillers for all inner horns; a horn is the union of all faces of a simplex except one, and inner means the omitted face is neither the first nor the last. The simplicial indexing category $\Delta$ has objects the nonempty finite ordered sets $[n]=\{0,\ldots,n\}$ and order-preserving maps; a simplicial set is a functor $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}\to\mathrm{Set}$. The infinity-category $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$ has small infinity-categories as objects, functors as arrows, and as mapping spaces the maximal subgroupoids of their functor infinity-categories. Limits below are homotopy limits: they represent compatible cones with all higher coherence, or equivalently induce the corresponding homotopy limits of mapping spaces from each test object. Thus homotopy-cartesian means that the upper left value is equivalent to the homotopy pullback of the other three values.

For a topology $\tau$, a site hypercover $U_\bullet\to X$ is an augmented simplicial family of objects over $X$ such that $U_0$ covers $X$ and each map from $U_m$ to the matching family determined by its lower faces is locally surjective for $\tau$. Equivalently this is the covering condition for $U_m\to(\operatorname{cosk}_{m-1}U_{<m})_m$; the matching object is the limit imposing all compatible proper-face restrictions. Families may be regarded as formal coproducts of representable presheaves. Local surjectivity means that each section over a test object lifts after a covering family. Evaluate a contravariant functor on a formal family by the product of its values on the members. It satisfies hyperdescent when the natural comparison

$$P(X)\longrightarrow\lim_{[m]\in\Delta}P(U_m)$$

is an equivalence for every such hypercover, and takes the empty family to the terminal object. Hypercomplete here means this hypercover descent condition. Ordinary descent requires only covering-family Čech nerves, whose degree $m$ consists of the $(m+1)$-fold fiber products over $X$. In $D(\mathbb Z)$ the terminal object is $0$; in $\mathrm{Cat}_\infty$ it is the category with one object and contractible mapping space.

## Source qualification
The explicit empty-scheme condition retains the existing correction to the literal square criterion. A constant presheaf with fixed nonzero value $C\in D(\mathbb Z)$ and identity restrictions sends every square to a homotopy-cartesian square, but its value on the empty scheme is $C$, whereas descent for the empty covering requires $0$. The categorical arc theorem and its finite-ring scope are unaffected.
