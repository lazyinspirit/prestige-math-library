## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and compatible universes. Retain the native derived geometry and the usual projective line of the supplied framework. Classical truncation $t_0Y$ of a derived scheme $Y$ has the same underlying Zariski topological space, and its open subschemes correspond to the open derived subschemes of $Y$. A derived scheme is qcqs exactly when its classical truncation is qcqs. If $S$ is qcqs and $Y$ is a smooth qcqs derived $S$-scheme, each quasi-compact open derived subscheme of $Y$ is again smooth and qcqs over $S$. The projective line $\mathbb P^1_S$ and the derived product $Y\times_S\mathbb P^1_S$ are smooth qcqs $S$-schemes. The assertions hold in animated derived geometry and in connective spectral geometry with its flat, fiber-smooth convention; no equivalence between these categories is asserted.

## Proof
**Given:** The native geometric convention, Choice, universes, and $S,Y$ in the statement.

**Goal:** Establish the truncation comparison and the two closure properties needed by smooth-site descent and projective-line evaluation.

1.1 In spectral geometry [F1] gives the same topological space for $Y$ and $t_0Y$ and identifies open restrictions; its schematic qcqs comparison gives the claimed qcqs equivalence. In animated geometry [F2] identifies opens on every affine chart with opens of its ordinary truncation. The local identifications respect restriction to an overlap, since they come from base change to the truncation and the universal property of localization. They therefore glue along an affine Zariski cover to identify the posets of all opens. A chosen derived affine cover truncates to an ordinary affine cover. Quasi-compactness agrees because all open covers and their finite subcovers correspond. To test quasi-separatedness, pull the diagonal back to the affine target charts formed by pairs of these affine charts; the pullbacks are exactly the open intersections. Quasi-compactness of a morphism is checked on an affine-open cover of its target, so this diagonal test is precisely quasi-compactness of these intersections. Their quasi-compactness agrees under the open comparison already proved. The same diagonal test for the ordinary affine cover gives the qcqs equivalence of [F3]. This argument uses the chosen affine cover and does not infer affineness of a lift from an equivalence of posets. An open subset of a quasi-separated scheme is quasi-separated: its affine opens are affine opens of the whole scheme and have the same intersections. Consequently a quasi-compact open of a qcqs $Y$ is qcqs. Its inclusion is smooth: in the spectral instance it is etale by [F1]; in the animated instance it is locally a principal localization. The induced map on $\pi_0$ is an ordinary principal open immersion, hence smooth by [F4]; the homotopy groups localize by the Lemma 4.2 input of [F2]. These two facts satisfy the animated smoothness criterion of [F2]. Smoothness is local and preserved by composition in both instances. Its composite to $S$ is therefore smooth. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, given]

2.1 The usual projective line over an affine base has two affine charts with affine localization overlap. Each of these ordinary affine schemes is quasi-compact by the affine compactness input in [F3]. The two charts therefore make the ordinary projective line quasi-compact: an open cover restricts to a cover of each chart, and the union of their two finite subcovers is finite and covers the whole scheme. Their intersection is the indicated affine localization, hence is quasi-compact by [F3]. The affine-cover diagonal test of [F2], used in step 1.1, now proves quasi-separatedness. Thus its ordinary truncation is qcqs. The ordinary base $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ is itself qcqs by the two affine-scheme inputs of [F3]. Over a general qcqs $S$, its ordinary truncation is $\mathbb P^1_{t_0S}$: this follows chartwise from the native construction and truncation, and the chart identifications glue. The ordinary scheme $\mathbb P^1_{t_0S}=t_0S\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}$ is qcqs by the ordinary fiber-product assertion of [F3]: $t_0S$ is qcqs by step 1.1 and the hypothesis on $S$, while $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb P^1_{\mathbb Z}$ are qcqs by the preceding affine and two-chart checks. Thus both factors and their base meet the hypotheses of that input before it is applied. Step 1.1 now makes $\mathbb P^1_S$ qcqs. Its smoothness is the projective-space assertion of [F1] in spectral geometry; in animated geometry the two ordinary charts $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z[T]\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ are first smooth by [F4]. The ordinary-scheme inclusion preserves smooth maps by [F2], so their animated realizations are smooth. Base change along $S\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$ gives the two polynomial charts over $S$, and smoothness is local on the source by [F2]. These are the charts of the usual projective line by [F3], so $\mathbb P^1_S\to S$ is smooth. The product $Y\times_S\mathbb P^1_S$ exists in the native category. In the spectral instance it is schematic and qcqs by [F1]. In the animated instance its truncation is $t_0Y\times_{t_0S}\mathbb P^1_{t_0S}$ by [F2], which is qcqs by [F3], and step 1.1 applies again. Finally its projection to $Y$ is a base change of the smooth projective line, and composition with $Y\to S$ is smooth. All hypotheses of the ordinary fiber-product input are met: the two factors and the base are qcqs. This proves both site-closure claims without a Noetherian, dimension or separatedness bound. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1] ∎
