## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and fix compatible Grothendieck universes $\mathcal U\in\mathcal V$ with $\omega\in\mathcal U$, the scheme data in $\mathcal U$, and the supplied perfect K-theory categories in the larger universes. Universe existence is a separate assumption. Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a noetherian affine scheme, with $A$ commutative and unital, and let $r\geq0$ and $E$ be a bundle of constant rank $r$. Noetherian means that $X$ has a finite affine open cover by spectra of rings in which every ideal is finitely generated. Write $K_0(X)=\pi_0K(X)$ for the supplied perfect spectral K-zero group and $[F]\in K_0(X)$ for the object class of a bundle placed in degree zero. Put $\varepsilon(E)=\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i[\Lambda^iE^\vee]$.

A finite projective $A$-module is a finitely generated module with the lifting property through surjective module maps. Define $K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$ as the free abelian group of finite formal integer sums of finite-projective isomorphism classes $[Q]_{\mathrm{proj}}$, divided by the subgroup of finite integer combinations of $[Q]_{\mathrm{proj}}-[Q']_{\mathrm{proj}}-[Q'']_{\mathrm{proj}}$ for short exact sequences $0\to Q'\to Q\to Q''\to0$, meaning that the first map is injective, the last is surjective and the image of the first equals the kernel of the last. For any $A$-module $M$, use the exterior quotient $\Lambda_A(M)=T_A(M)/(m\otimes m:m\in M)$, where $T_A(M)=\bigoplus_{n\geq0}M^{\otimes_A n}$ has concatenation multiplication and $M^{\otimes_A0}=A$; the denominator denotes the homogeneous two-sided ideal generated by these squares. Its degree-$i$ part is $\Lambda_A^iM$. For $P=\Gamma(X,E)$ put $P^*=\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,A)$ and $C^K_r(P)=\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i[\Lambda_A^iP^*]_{\mathrm{proj}}$. Every module in this sum is finite projective. The affine comparison $\alpha:K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)\to K_0(X)$, sending $[Q]_{\mathrm{proj}}$ to $[\widetilde Q]$, is an isomorphism and satisfies $\alpha(C^K_r(P))=\varepsilon(E)$.

In $K_0(X)$ put $\lambda_{-1}(E)=\sum_{i=0}^r(-1)^i[\Lambda^iE]$. Then $\lambda_{-1}(E)=(-1)^r[\det E]\varepsilon(E)$, and the determinant factor is a unit, so these two exterior conventions vanish simultaneously. For a section $t:\mathcal O_X\to E$, form the functional $t^\vee:E^\vee\to\mathcal O_X$, $\varphi\mapsto\varphi(t(1))$. Call $t$ regular if, at every point of its zero scheme, some local frame expresses its coordinate germs as a regular sequence of length $r$: successively each is a non-zero-divisor modulo its predecessors and the final quotient is nonzero; the empty sequence is allowed for $r=0$. For such a section its Koszul complex has $\Lambda^iE^\vee$ in degree $-i$ and ambient perfect K-zero class $\varepsilon(E)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The noetherian affine scheme, constant-rank bundle, Choice and universe assumptions in the Statement; a regular section only for the final assertion.

**Goal:** Construct the module exterior classes and their affine comparison, and establish the duality and Koszul identities.

[F1] The affine comparison identifies vector-bundle Grothendieck classes with perfect spectral object classes. The affine module–bundle equivalence preserves rank, direct sums, duals and tensor products; short exact sequences of affine bundles split. A finite projective module has precisely the stated finite-generation and lifting-property meaning. [def-vector-bundle-classes](item-def-vector-bundle-classes), [lem-affine-bundle-module-interface](item-lem-affine-bundle-module-interface)

[F2] The exterior construction is the tensor-algebra quotient for arbitrary modules, followed by sheafification for bundles. Its bundle exterior powers are functorial and finite locally free. For a finite free module the increasing wedges are a basis for precisely this quotient, including the empty wedge in degree zero. [def-exterior-determinant-bundles](item-def-exterior-determinant-bundles), [math:def-exterior-algebra-of-a-finite-free-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-exterior-algebra-of-a-finite-free-module), [math:lem-exterior-algebra-basis-monomials](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-exterior-algebra-basis-monomials)

[F3] In the vector-bundle Grothendieck ring, $\lambda_{-1}(E)=(-1)^r[\det E]\lambda_{-1}(E^\vee)$ and $[\det E]$ is a unit. [lem-exterior-euler-duality](item-lem-exterior-euler-duality)

[F4] For a regular section $t$ of $E$, its associated functional $t^\vee$ has a Koszul complex with $\Lambda^iE^\vee$ in degree $-i$. Its ambient perfect K-zero class is their alternating sum. This includes the empty sequence in rank zero. [lem-regular-zero-koszul-euler-class](item-lem-regular-zero-koszul-euler-class)

[F5] For a multiplicative subset $S$ of a commutative ring, module localization uses fractions $m/s$, with $m/s=n/t$ precisely when $u(tm-sn)=0$ for some $u\in S$. Localization commutes with quotient modules and arbitrary direct sums. A map from a module to an $S^{-1}A$-module extends uniquely to localization by $m/s\mapsto s^{-1}h(m)$. [math:def-localisation-of-a-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-localisation-of-a-module), [math:thm-localisation-of-modules-commutes-with-quotients-and-sums](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-localisation-of-modules-commutes-with-quotients-and-sums), [math:thm-universal-property-localisation-of-a-module](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-universal-property-localisation-of-a-module)

[F6] Module tensors are generated by elementary tensors with additivity and balance relations. Balanced pairings induce unique additive maps; over a commutative ring the scalar action is $a(m\otimes n)=(am)\otimes n=m\otimes(an)$, so scalar-compatible formulas induce linear maps. [math:def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations), [math:thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products), [math:thm-commutative-ring-module-structure-on-a-tensor-product](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-commutative-ring-module-structure-on-a-tensor-product)

[F7] An affine scheme is a spectrum with its structure sheaf, and the opens $D(f)$ form a basis. Its structure sheaf has $\mathcal O_X(D(f))=A_f$ with localization restrictions. For every module $M$, $\widetilde M(D(f))=M_f$, naturally in module maps and restrictions; in particular $\Gamma(X,\widetilde M)=M$. Compatible local maps of sheaves glue uniquely by gluing their values on sections. [math:def-affine-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-scheme), [math:def-affine-scheme-spectrum](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-scheme-spectrum), [math:lem-associated-sheaf-sections-basic-open](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-associated-sheaf-sections-basic-open), [math:thm-sections-basic-open-affine-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-sections-basic-open-affine-scheme), [math:def-sheaf-on-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sheaf-on-topological-space)

[F8] The spectral K-theory product is induced by derived tensor product, with unit $[\mathcal O_X]$. Choice and the compatible universe assumptions are inherited premises of the supplied K-theory and affine equivalence. [def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface](item-def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface), [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice)

[F9] A noetherian scheme has a finite affine open cover by spectra of noetherian rings. Affine schemes are quasi-compact and quasi-separated, so the qcqs hypotheses of the perfect K-theory suppliers hold here. Universes have the supplied closure convention, with $\omega$ explicitly included and their existence separately assumed. [math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme), [math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact), [math:thm-affine-schemes-quasi-separated](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-affine-schemes-quasi-separated), [math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention)

[F10] The sheaf tensor product is the sheafification of the sectionwise tensor presheaf; a presheaf map to a sheaf extends uniquely through sheafification. Exactness of sheaves can be tested on stalks. A complex $B$ is K-flat if tensoring any acyclic complex with it, using the direct-sum total tensor complex, remains acyclic. The supplied derived tensor is computed by that total tensor on K-flat representatives. ## Proof

1.1 First the finite-projective isomorphism classes form a set: for a finite generating surjection $A^n\to Q$, projectivity supplies a section, so $Q$ is isomorphic to the image of an idempotent $n\times n$ matrix over $A$. All these matrices, for all finite $n$, form a set, and quotienting their images by module isomorphism gives the required set. Thus the finite-formal-sum presentation in the Statement defines an abelian group. We next construct the exterior comparison. For arbitrary $A$-modules $M,N$ and a multiplicative subset $S$, the formulas $(m\otimes n)/s\mapsto(m/s)\otimes(n/1)$ and $(m/s)\otimes(n/t)\mapsto(m\otimes n)/(st)$ give inverse maps between $S^{-1}(M\otimes_A N)$ and $(S^{-1}M)\otimes_{S^{-1}A}(S^{-1}N)$. Indeed the second formula is additive and balanced by a common-denominator calculation; replacing a fraction by an equal fraction changes its value by zero under the fraction criterion in [F5]. It is $S^{-1}A$-linear by the scalar formula in [F6]. The first map is obtained from the balanced pairing $(m,n)\mapsto(m/1)\otimes(n/1)$ and localization's universal property, and the two composites fix the elementary tensors. Iterating, and applying the direct-sum and quotient assertions of [F5], identifies $S^{-1}T_A(M)$ with $T_{S^{-1}A}(S^{-1}M)$ and localizes its defining ideal. That ideal becomes exactly the ideal of squares: $(m/s)\otimes(m/s)$ is $s^{-2}$ times the image of $m\otimes m$, and original squares remain squares. This gives $(\Lambda_A^iM)_f\cong\Lambda_{A_f}^i(M_f)$, naturally in $M$ and localization, also for $i=0$. If $\widetilde M$ is finite locally free, put $N_i=\Lambda_A^iM$. Send global wedges to their images under the canonical presheaf-to-sheaf map defining $\Lambda^i\widetilde M$, restrict to $D(f)$ and extend to fractions by [F5]. The resulting $A_f$-linear maps $N_{i,f}\to(\Lambda^i\widetilde M)(D(f))$ commute with restrictions, hence define a sheaf map $\widetilde{N_i}\to\Lambda^i\widetilde M$ by [F7]. Refine free charts by basic opens. On each such $D(f)$, $M_f$ is finite free by [F7]; the localization just proved and [F2] identify this map with the identity on increasing-wedge bases, compatibly on all smaller basic opens. It therefore has local inverses, which glue by [F7] to a global inverse. Thus $N_i\cong\Gamma(X,\Lambda^i\widetilde M)$ is finite projective by [F1] and [F2]. Apply this to $M=P^*$, whose associated bundle is $E^\vee$ by [F1], to obtain finite projectivity of every $\Lambda_A^iP^*$ and the canonical comparison $\widetilde{\Lambda_A^iP^*}\cong\Lambda^iE^\vee$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, construct]

2.1 A short exact sequence of finite projectives splits: lift the identity of its projective quotient through its surjection. Its exact-sequence relation is therefore a direct-sum relation; conversely every direct-sum relation comes from a split short exact sequence. Affine bundle sequences split by [F1], so the direct-sum-preserving equivalence there induces mutually inverse homomorphisms on these presented groups. Composing with its supplied bundle-to-spectral comparison gives $\alpha$. By step 1.1 it sends every $[\Lambda_A^iP^*]_{\mathrm{proj}}$ to $[\Lambda^iE^\vee]$, giving $\alpha(C^K_r(P))=\varepsilon(E)$; functoriality in [F2] makes these finite sums isomorphism invariant. To transfer the ring identity, the product in [F8] agrees with ordinary bundle tensor: on a chart where a bundle $B$ is $\mathcal O^b$, the tensor presheaf of [F10] identifies tensoring with $B$ with taking $b$ copies, by sending tensors with each standard basis vector to that component. The inverse sends a tuple to the sum of those tensors. These maps extend through sheafification and commute with restrictions. Thus tensoring an acyclic complex with $B[0]$ is locally a finite direct sum of that complex and stays acyclic, by the stalk criterion in [F10]. This makes every degree-zero bundle K-flat; the supplied total-tensor formula consequently computes the derived tensor of two degree-zero bundles by their ordinary tensor in degree zero. Hence the bundle-to-spectral comparison respects multiplication on all generators, and [F3] gives the asserted determinant-unit identity and simultaneous vanishing in $K_0(X)$. Finally apply [F4] to the specified regular section: its Koszul terms are $\Lambda^iE^\vee$ in degree $-i$, whose alternating ambient class is $\varepsilon(E)$. All assertions include $r=0$ and the empty affine scheme. Affineness supplies the qcqs hypotheses by [F9]. Choice is inherited through [F1], [F4], [F7]–[F10]; universe existence is the separate assumption in [F9]. The finite sums and explicit fraction maps require no additional choices. This proves every stated conclusion. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, F8, F9, F10, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
