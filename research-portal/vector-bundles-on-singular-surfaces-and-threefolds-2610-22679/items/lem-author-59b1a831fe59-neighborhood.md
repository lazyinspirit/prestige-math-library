## Statement
Assume AC and the completed universe bounds. Let $X$ be a qcqs $k$-scheme of pure finite dimension $d$, where $k$ is perfect, and let $x$ be a closed point at which $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth. There is an affine smooth separated finite-type neighbourhood $V$ of $x$, pure of dimension $d$. Its residue field $L=\kappa(x)$ is finite separable over $k$, and $\operatorname{Spec}L\hookrightarrow V$ has pure codimension $d$. Both $X\setminus\{x\}$ and $V\setminus\{x\}$ are qcqs. For every $j\geq0$, actual EM Nisnevich descent gives the support-excision equivalence $C_{j,x}(X)\simeq C_{j,x}(V)$, compatibly with support-to-global maps. Here $C_{j,x}$ is the unshifted cohomological restriction fiber, not its $[2j]$ spectral shift.

## External input
Reference 0 records the finite-type dimension premise used through the declared geometric supplier in [F2]. Only finitely generated coordinate domains, their quotient spectra and finite-type components of $V$ are subject to that input. The bound on a possibly non-Noetherian component $T$ of $X$, the verification of the cover, and the enhanced support-excision application are proved locally below; no published proof of that combined conclusion is claimed.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** Exactly the hypotheses of the Statement, with smoothness at $x$ understood by the germwise scheme definition in [F3].

**Goal:** Verify all geometric and descent hypotheses for the actual point-support pair.

[A1] Assume [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]] and exactly the compatible site and universe bounds in [[lem-author-b747b28c359f-elmanto-morrow-complexes]]. Choice is used through the Jacobian, regularity, component-existence and finite-type compactness suppliers below, and the enhanced motivic construction; the finite witness selections introduce no stronger assumption.

[F1] [[def-article-conventions]] supplies qcqs schemes, residue fields and chain dimension. Pure dimension $d$ means that every irreducible component has dimension $d$. [[def-cohomology-with-support]] supplies the support-to-global convention. Write $C_j(T)=\mathbb Z(j)_{\mathrm{mot}}(T)$ for the unshifted coefficient functor of [[lem-author-b747b28c359f-elmanto-morrow-complexes]], in its supplied stable enhancement, and define $C_{j,x}(T)=\operatorname{fib}(C_j(T)\to C_j(T\setminus\{x\}))$ whenever the displayed opens are qcqs. Fibers and pullbacks here are coherent limits in that enhancement. [[lem-open-excision-for-support-fibers]] supplies the pullback-pasting argument for support excision; below that same argument is written out in the enhanced complex target, rather than assuming a strict cochain square without a model.

[F2] [[thm-author-fc2380accb47-finite-type-geometric-input]] supplies the finite-type dimension formulas: an irreducible finite-type $k$-scheme with generic residue field $K$ has dimension $\operatorname{trdeg}_kK$, and a closed point with finite residue extension has codimension equal to the dimension of each containing component. Here codimension is the supremum of lengths of strict irreducible-closed chains from the point to that component. These facts are applied only to finite-type schemes and finitely generated $k$-domains, never directly to a possibly non-Noetherian component of $X$. The exact dimension import is also recorded in reference 0.

[F3] [[math:def-smooth-morphism-schemes]] defines smoothness at an arbitrary scheme point by local finite presentation, flatness and geometric regularity of the fiber. [[math:def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]] defines the resulting standard smooth presentation and permits permutation to a leading invertible Jacobian minor. [[math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]] and [[math:def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]] specify finite type, finite algebra generation and the generated subalgebras used below. Open subschemes have restricted structure sheaves by [[math:def-affine-open-subscheme]].

[F4] For a standard smooth $k$-algebra with $n$ variables and $c$ equations and leading minor invertible, [[math:lem-ag-standard-smooth-regular-geometric-fibres]] applied to $R=k$, $\mathfrak p=(0)$ and the identity field extension makes every component have dimension $n-c$ and every local ring regular. ## Proof
1.1 Smoothness at $x$ gives an affine finitely presented chart $U\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ around $x$ by [F3]; restriction preserves the pointwise smoothness condition. Apply the Jacobian criterion to this locally finitely presented morphism and shrink to its standard smooth affine chart $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ containing $x$, with $n$ variables, $c$ equations and, after permutation, leading minor a unit. Thus $V$ is smooth, separated and finite type, regular Noetherian, and pure of dimension $e=n-c$ by [F3]–[F4]. To prove $e=d$ without global Noetherianness, choose an irreducible component $Z$ of $V$. Its closure $T$ in $X$ is an irreducible component of $X$: [F5] puts it in some component $T'$, and $T'\cap V$ is irreducible and contains $Z$, so equals $Z$ by maximality; this nonempty open is dense in $T'$, giving $T'=T$. In particular $T\cap V=Z$. By the affine generic-point interface in [F5], $Z$ has generic point $\eta$, whose closure in $X$ is $T$; put $K=\kappa(\eta)$. In any affine open $W=\operatorname{Spec}A$ meeting $T$, the generic point belongs to $W$, and $T\cap W$ is the prime-closed subset for its prime $\mathfrak p$. By [F5], $D=A/\mathfrak p$ embeds in $K\cong\operatorname{Frac}D$, with these identifications all through the same residue field of $X$ at $\eta$. On $V$, the domain $B/\mathfrak p_\eta$ is finitely generated, so [F2] gives $e=\dim Z=\operatorname{trdeg}_kK$. Given any chain $\mathfrak q_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak q_r$ of primes in any such $D$, choose $a_i\in\mathfrak q_i\setminus\mathfrak q_{i-1}$ for $1\leq i\leq r$. Their contractions to the finitely generated domain $D_0=k[a_1,\ldots,a_r]\subseteq D\subseteq K$ remain strict. Hence $r\leq\dim D_0=\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}D_0\leq e$ by [F2]; the last inequality holds because an algebraically independent tuple in a subfield remains so in $K$. For any finite strict chain of irreducible closed subsets of $T$, choose an affine open of $X$ meeting its smallest member. Its traces remain nonempty irreducible and strict, since each trace is dense in its original member; [F5] turns them into a prime chain in the corresponding $D$. Thus $\dim T\leq e$. Conversely, chains in $Z=T\cap V$ have distinct closures in $T$, so $\dim T\geq\dim Z=e$. Purity of $X$ gives $\dim T=d$, and therefore $e=d$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 Since $x$ is closed in $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$, its prime is maximal, and [F5] identifies $L=\kappa(x)$ with the field $B/\mathfrak m_x$. The quotient-spectrum result in [F6] makes $\operatorname{Spec}L\to V$ the closed immersion at $x$. This field is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, so [F6] makes it finite over $k$. Each $a\in L$ is algebraic because sufficiently many powers of $a$ are linearly dependent in the finite-dimensional $k$-space $L$; perfectness and [F6] therefore make $L/k$ separable. A finite $k$-basis also generates $L$ as a $k$-algebra. The scheme $\operatorname{Spec}L$ is regular by the field assertion in [F4], so the perfect-field regular/smooth equivalence makes it smooth of finite type over $k$, and affineness makes it separated. For each component of the pure-$d$ finite-type $V$ containing $x$, [F2] gives codimension $d$. Set $U_0=X\setminus\{x\}$ and $W_0=V\setminus\{x\}$. By [F7], $W_0$ is quasi-compact. For any open cover of $U_0$, adjoining $V$ covers quasi-compact $X$, so finitely many members together with $V$ suffice; finitely many members of the original cover also cover $W_0$. Their union covers $U_0$, proving it quasi-compact. Both complements inherit quasi-separatedness from $X$ by [F7], so all four opens are qcqs. The cover $\{V,U_0\}$ is Nisnevich, with intersection $W_0$. For completeness, let $R_V,R_{U_0}$ be the full subcategories of arrows to $X$ factoring through the indicated opens, and $R=R_V\cup R_{U_0}$ the covering sieve. Their intersection is $R_{W_0}$. Every composable string in $R$ belongs wholly to one of these subcategories: the last object factors through one open, and every preceding arrow then does also. Hence its nerve is their union along the intersection. A coherent cone on its restriction diagram is consequently a pair of coherent cones agreeing on that intersection. Each $R_O$ has terminal object $O\hookrightarrow X$, so the corresponding opposite-diagram limit is $C_j(O)$. The descent equivalence in [F1] thus gives $C_j(X)\simeq C_j(V)\times_{C_j(W_0)}C_j(U_0)$ with the actual restrictions. Now perform the pullback-pasting argument of [F1] in this supplied stable enhancement: pulling back its projection to $C_j(U_0)$ along $0\to C_j(U_0)$ gives $(C_j(V)\times_{C_j(W_0)}C_j(U_0))\times_{C_j(U_0)}0\simeq C_j(V)\times_{C_j(W_0)}0$. Indeed, a map into either side is exactly a map into $C_j(V)$ with a coherent nullhomotopy of its restriction to $C_j(W_0)$. This proves the restriction-induced equivalence $C_{j,x}(X)\simeq C_{j,x}(V)$ for each $j\geq0$; its projection to $C_j(V)$ is the support-to-global projection followed by restriction, so the claimed compatibility is coherent. The construction uses the unshifted complexes throughout. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1] ∎
