## Statement
Every field is a regular local ring.

A regular local ring is a noetherian local ring whose Krull dimension equals the dimension of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ over its residue field, where $\mathfrak m$ is its maximal ideal. Krull dimension is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of prime ideals.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $K$.

**Goal:** Prove that $K$ is regular local.

[F1] The displayed equality of Krull dimension and embedding dimension characterizes regular noetherian local rings ([[math:def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]).

[F2] A field has $0\ne1$, and every nonzero element $a$ has an inverse $a^{-1}$ with $a^{-1}a=1$ ([[math:def-field]]).

[F3] With its field operations, a field is a nonzero commutative ring without zero divisors ([[math:lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F4] In a commutative ring an ideal is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by ring elements ([[math:def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]). A proper ideal $P$ is prime if $ab\in P$ implies $a\in P$ or $b\in P$; a proper ideal is maximal if no proper ideal strictly contains it ([[math:def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]]). An ideal generated by $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ consists of the sums $r_1a_1+\cdots+r_na_n$ with coefficients in the ring; this specifies finite generation here.

[F5] A local ring is a nonzero commutative ring with a unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, and its residue field is $R/\mathfrak m$ ([[math:def-local-ring]]). A commutative ring is noetherian if every ideal is finitely generated ([[math:def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]); only this definition is used, without any choice-dependent equivalence with ascending-chain conditions.

[F6] The ideal product $IJ$ consists of finite sums of products $ij$ with $i\in I$ and $j\in J$; in particular $\mathfrak m^2=\mathfrak m\mathfrak m$ ([[math:def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]). The quotient ring $R/I$ consists of additive cosets, with $(a+I)+(b+I)=(a+b)+I$ and $(a+I)(b+I)=ab+I$ ([[math:def-quotient-ring]]).

[F7] For a finite-dimensional vector space, dimension is the number of elements of a finite basis; in particular the zero vector space over any field has dimension zero ([[math:def-dimension]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [F2] and [F3], $K$ is a nonzero commutative ring without zero divisors. If an ideal $I$ contains $a\ne0$, then $1=a^{-1}a\in I$ by [F2] and [F4], and every $r\in K$ belongs to $I$ because $r=r\cdot1$; hence $I=K$. Thus the only ideals are $(0)$ and $K$, generated respectively by $0$ and $1$. The ideal $(0)$ is proper since $0\ne1$, is prime since $K$ has no zero divisors, and is maximal since there is no other proper ideal. By [F5], $K$ is noetherian and local, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(0)$. The length-zero prime chain consisting of $(0)$ exists, and there is no strict inclusion between prime ideals, so the inline definition of Krull dimension gives $\dim K=0$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, given]

2.1 By [F6] and step 1.1, $\mathfrak m^2=(0)$, and the map $K/(0)\to K$ given by $a+(0)\mapsto a$ is a bijection preserving addition and multiplication: each coset is the singleton $\{a\}$. This identifies the residue field with $K$. The quotient $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=(0)/(0)$ has one element, so its addition and residue-field scalar multiplication are the unique operations on the zero vector space. By [F7] its dimension over $K$ is zero. Consequently $\dim K=\dim_K(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)=0$, and [F1] makes $K$ regular local. [F1, F6, F7, step 1.1] ∎
