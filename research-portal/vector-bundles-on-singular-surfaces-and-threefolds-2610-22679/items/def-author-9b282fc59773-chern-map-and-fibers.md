## Definition
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the suitable universes used by the bundle-class supplier, with the supplied Grothendieck universe closure convention; their existence is assumed separately from Choice. Let $X$ be a smooth affine scheme of finite type and pure dimension three over a field. Here smoothness and finite type refer to the structure morphism to the spectrum of that field. Pure dimension three means that every irreducible component has dimension three; the dimension of a nonempty affine scheme is the Krull dimension of its coordinate ring, and components carry their reduced induced scheme structures. Write $V=\mathrm{Vect}_2(X)$ for the set of isomorphism classes of rank-two finite locally free sheaves. The determinant of $E$ is the invertible sheaf $\det E=\bigwedge^2E$, using the supplied sheaf exterior-power construction. The group $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ is the group of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves, under tensor product, with identity $[\mathcal O_X]$. The group $\mathrm{CH}^i(X)$ is the free abelian group on integral codimension-$i$ closed subschemes modulo rational equivalence: the subgroup generated by divisors of rational functions on integral codimension-$(i-1)$ closed subschemes. In the supplied dimension grading this is $A_{3-i}(X)=A^i(X)$; divisor multiplicities and rational equivalence have the supplied Chow-group meaning. Use the supplied Chow-theoretic Chern classes, and use the determinant convention for the first invariant in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$. Define
$$C_X:V\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pic}(X)\times\mathrm{CH}^2(X),\qquad [E]\longmapsto([\det E],c_2(E)).$$
For a line-bundle class $L$ put $V_L=\{e\in V:[\det e]=L\}$. For $a\in\mathrm{CH}^2(X)$ put $V_{L,a}=\{e\in V_L:c_2(e)=a\}$. These are sets of bundle isomorphism classes, without choices of a determinant trivialization. The target basepoint is $([\mathcal O_X],0)$ and the source basepoint is $[\mathcal O_X^{\oplus2}]$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** $X$ and the conventions in the Definition.

**Goal:** Justify the map and its fiber notation.

[F1] An affine scheme is quasi-compact (under the assumed Choice) and quasi-separated. Thus $X$ meets the qcqs hypothesis of the bundle-class supplier, and bundle isomorphism classes form a set under its Choice and universe conventions. Choice is inherited here from this set construction, the affine quasi-compactness result and the Chern-class supplier; no further selection is used in the argument below. [[math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]] [[math:thm-affine-schemes-quasi-separated]] [[math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme]] [[def-vector-bundle-classes]] [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]] [[math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention]]

[F2] On a smooth equidimensional finite-type scheme over a field, the supplied projective-bundle relation defines isomorphism-invariant Chern classes in the Chow ring. Its codimension grading is the supplied Chow-group grading. The normalization at the zero Cartier divisor gives $c_1(\mathcal O_X)=0$, and the line-summand formula therefore makes every positive Chern class of a sum of trivial lines zero. [[math:def-chern-classes-of-a-vector-bundle]] [[math:def-chow-group-of-cycles-mod-rational-equivalence]]

[F3] The sheaf exterior-power construction is functorial on bundle maps. For rank two its second exterior power has rank one; on free charts the wedge basis gives the determinant of the transition matrix. [[def-exterior-determinant-bundles]]

[F4] Tensor products and duals of invertible sheaves give a well-defined abelian group of their isomorphism classes, with identity $[\mathcal O_X]$. [[math:def-picard-group-scheme]]

## Justification
1.1 By [F3], applying the second exterior-power functor to the local rank-two trivializations of $E$ gives rank-one trivializations with transition functions the determinants of the original matrices. Functoriality verifies their cocycle condition and sends a bundle isomorphism to an isomorphism of second exterior powers. Thus $[\det E]$ depends only on $[E]$. By [F4], tensor products and duals of invertible sheaves give the stated group law and inverses on their isomorphism classes. [F1, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 By [F2], $c_2(E)\in\mathrm{CH}^2(X)$ is invariant under bundle isomorphism. Combining this with step 1.1 defines $C_X$ on the set $V$ supplied by [F1]; separation defines the subsets $V_L$ and $V_{L,a}$. The global wedge of the two standard basis sections trivializes the determinant of $\mathcal O_X^{\oplus2}$ by [F3], and its second Chern class is zero by [F2], so the chosen basepoints are respected. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1] ∎

## Source notes
For context, Asok–Fasel Theorem 1 uses the two Chern invariants under the additional hypotheses of an algebraically closed base field of characteristic different from two. No assertion that arbitrary pairs are realized, or uniquely realized, is part of this definition. Identification of the determinant coordinate with the usual $c_1$ coordinate in $\mathrm{CH}^1$ is a separate comparison result and is not asserted here.
