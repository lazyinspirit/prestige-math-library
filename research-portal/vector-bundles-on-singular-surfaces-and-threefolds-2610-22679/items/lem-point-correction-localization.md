## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the site and universe bounds of the cited filtration and support constructions. Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $X$ be a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme of dimension three, and let $\alpha\in F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. Choose $P,S,C,T$ as in [[lem-bertini-support-reduction]]: $\alpha=[P]-[\mathcal O_X^3]$, $S$ is a finite set of smooth closed points on the three-dimensional components, $C$ is the reduced closure of the componentwise curve constructed there, $T=C\cup S$, and $\alpha$ restricts to zero on $X\setminus T$. Put $D=S\setminus C=\{y_1,\ldots,y_r\}$, allowing $D=\varnothing$.

The square of open immersions
$$\begin{matrix}X\setminus T&\longrightarrow&X\setminus C\\\downarrow&&\downarrow\\X\setminus D&\longrightarrow&X\end{matrix}$$
is cartesian and cocartesian. The actual localization and restriction maps form a commutative diagram with exact rows
$$\begin{matrix} \bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb Z&\xrightarrow{f}&K_0(X)&\longrightarrow&K_0(X\setminus D)\\ \Vert&&\downarrow i&&\downarrow\\ \bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb Z&\xrightarrow{g}&K_0(X\setminus C)&\xrightarrow{j}&K_0(X\setminus T). \end{matrix}$$
The two copies of the direct sum identify with the respective supported $K_0$ groups by actual restriction and point dévissage; the $i$th basis vector maps under $f$ to $[\kappa(y_i)]$. Moreover, for both pairs the actual map from the third supported filtration stage to supported $K$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_0$.

There is $a\in\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z$ with $i(\alpha)=g(a)$. Thus $\beta=\alpha-f(a)$ restricts to zero in $K_0(X\setminus C)$, and $f(a)$ belongs to the zero-extended top-dimensional smooth-point subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)\subseteq F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. Consequently $\beta$ belongs to the kernel of the natural restriction $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)\to F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X\setminus C)$. If $X$ is pure three-dimensional, the top-dimensional subgroup is the smooth-point subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ of [[def-smooth-point-filtration]].

Here $K_0$ is zeroth homotopy of nonconnective perfect-complex $K$-theory, and $K(A/B)$ is the fiber of restriction $K(A)\to K(B)$. Its supported third stage is the corresponding fiber at stage $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K$. The image filtration $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0$ is the image of that stage in $K_0$. The top-dimensional smooth-point subgroup is generated by the residue-sheaf classes of smooth closed points on the three-dimensional clopen components, extended by zero to $X$. No point on a lower-dimensional component is included merely because it is smooth.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The field, scheme, class, support data, Choice and size bounds in the statement. A finite-type scheme over a field is Noetherian, so all its open subsets are quasi-compact; an algebraically closed field is perfect.

**Goal:** Establish the actual finite-support localization diagram, the supported third-stage comparison, and the correction without assuming either support-to-global map injective.

[F1] [[lem-bertini-support-reduction]] supplies the stated data, including $T=C\cup S$, the location of $S$ on three-dimensional components, and $\alpha|_{X\setminus T}=0$. Its componentwise construction applies to disconnected $X$ and uses Choice.

[F2] [[prop-perfect-k-localization-input]] gives natural localization, Mayer–Vietoris and supported open excision for these qcqs schemes and opens. [[prop-smooth-point-excision-purity]] identifies the actual supported $K_0$ at each smooth closed point with $\mathbb Z$, with positive generator the supported residue-sheaf class and with its stated global image.

[F3] [[def-motivic-filtration]], [[def-cohomology-with-support]], [[prop-motivic-graded-spectral-sequence]] and [[lem-filtered-support-exactness]] give the natural supported tower, stage-zero $K$, and its graded restriction fibers. [[lem-author-b747b28c359f-elmanto-morrow-complexes]] supplies the derived Nisnevich sheaves underlying each motivic graded term, hence their Zariski descent. [[lem-smooth-point-motivic-purity]] makes every homotopy group of each supported graded term of weight $0,1,2$ vanish at a smooth point of a pure three-dimensional scheme.

[F4] [[lem-author-80f982ded420-clopen-filtered-products]] gives the actual stagewise finite clopen product decomposition. [[lem-smooth-point-filtration]] gives the point-supported stage-three surjection in the pure case. [[lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope]] identifies the zero-extended top-dimensional point subgroup and its containment in the third image filtration; [[def-smooth-point-filtration]] gives the pure-dimensional convention. The filtration is natural for restriction by [[def-motivic-filtration]].

## Proof
1.1 Since $D=S\setminus C$ and $T=C\cup S=C\cup D$, the upper-left open is $(X\setminus C)\cap(X\setminus D)$ and the other two opens cover $X$. Intersections of open immersions are their fiber products, and morphisms out of the two covering opens that agree on their intersection glue uniquely. Therefore the displayed scheme square is cartesian and cocartesian. All four opens are quasi-compact and quasi-separated. [F1, given]

2.1 Apply localization to $D\subset X$ and to the same closed set $D\subset X\setminus C$. It is closed in the latter because $D$ is a finite set of closed points and is disjoint from $C$. Open excision identifies the two supported spectra by actual restriction, compatibly with their maps to the ambient spectra. Their long exact homotopy sequences give the two exact rows and the commutative square of maps. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 For disjoint finite closed subsets $A,B\subset X$, the cover $X=(X\setminus A)\cup(X\setminus B)$ and Mayer–Vietoris identify the support spectrum for $A\cup B$ with the product of the support spectra for $A$ and $B$: take the fiber over $K(X\setminus(A\cup B))$ of the cartesian restriction square and use the universal property of the pullback. The two factors map to the union-support spectrum by the corresponding maps of support fibers. Iterating, including the empty case, identifies $K_D(X)$ with the finite product of the one-point support spectra. By point dévissage each zeroth group is $\mathbb Z$, and the inverse generator maps to the residue-sheaf class. Open excision gives the same identification on $X\setminus C$ and preserves these generators. This proves the displayed diagram and its generator normalization. [F2, step 2.1]

4.1 Each motivic stage has Zariski Mayer–Vietoris on the opens used here. Indeed, stage zero has it by [F2]. If stage $q$ has it, compare its Mayer–Vietoris square with the square of the weight-$q$ graded spectrum. The latter is cartesian by derived Zariski descent in [F3]; taking fibers in the natural graded triangle shows that the stage-$(q+1)$ square is cartesian. Induction gives the assertion through stage three. The same finite-support pullback argument as in step 3.1 therefore splits the supported stage and its stage-to-$K$ map into the corresponding one-point maps. [F2, F3, step 3.1]

5.1 Let $y\in D$. It lies on a three-dimensional clopen component, and the union $X_3$ of those components is pure three-dimensional. The stagewise clopen product equivalence identifies the support tower at $y$ on $X$ with that on $X_3$: the other component contributes the fiber of an identity map. On $X_3$, [F3] makes the supported graded spectra in weights $0,1,2$ contractible, since all their homotopy groups vanish. The supported graded triangles therefore make the transitions from stage three to stage zero equivalences. Stage zero is supported $K$. Thus the actual one-point stage-three map is an equivalence. By step 4.1, so is the map for the finite set $D$ on $X$; the analogous map on $X\setminus C$ follows by stagewise open excision from its Mayer–Vietoris square. Taking $\pi_0$ proves the two stated isomorphisms. [F3, F4, step 4.1]

6.1 By [F1], $j(i(\alpha))=\alpha|_{X\setminus T}=0$. Exactness of the lower row gives an element $a$ with $i(\alpha)=g(a)$. Commutativity gives $i(f(a))=g(a)$, hence $i(\beta)=0$. By step 3.1, $f(a)$ is a finite integral combination of residue classes at points of $D$; every such point is smooth and lies on a three-dimensional component. Therefore [F4] puts $f(a)$ in $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ and hence in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. Since this is a subgroup, $\beta$ lies in it too. Natural restriction of the image filtration then makes its vanishing in ambient $K_0(X\setminus C)$ exactly the asserted kernel condition. For pure $X$, [F4] identifies the point subgroup with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}$. No injectivity of $f$ or $g$ is used. [F1, F4, step 3.1, step 5.1] ∎
