## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the size conventions of the cited perfect K-theory and smooth-point constructions. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a normal affine $k$-scheme of finite type and dimension $3$. Let $E$ be a rank-three vector bundle on $X$, and put
$$\beta(E)=[\mathcal O_X]-[E]+[\Lambda^2E]-[\det E]\in K_0(X).$$
If $\beta(E)=0$ in $K_0(X)$, then $E\cong\mathcal O_X\oplus E'$ for a rank-two vector bundle $E'$.

In particular, this applies when $\beta(E)$ is trivial in $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ for pure-dimensional $X$. If $X$ also has lower-dimensional components, the corresponding hypothesis may instead be stated using the zero-extended top-dimensional subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$; it does not include all smooth points on those lower-dimensional components.

Here a vector bundle is a finite locally free sheaf, and rank three means locally isomorphic to $\mathcal O_X^3$. Normal means that all local rings are integrally closed domains. The group $K_0(X)$ is the degree-zero perfect K-theory group in [[def-rank-three-euler-k-class]]. The pure smooth-point subgroup in [[def-smooth-point-filtration]] is generated by the classes of residue sheaves at smooth closed points. Its top-dimensional zero extension for a nonpure normal scheme is specified in [[lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, scheme, bundle, choice and size hypotheses in the statement, and $\beta(E)=0$ in $K_0(X)$.

**Goal:** Construct a decomposition of $E$ into a trivial line and a rank-two bundle, and establish the stated subgroup implications.

[F1] [[lem-affine-bundle-module-interface]] identifies vector bundles on $X$ with finite projective $A$-modules, compatibly with rank, direct sums and duals. [[lem-affine-perfect-k-zero-bridge]] identifies the projective-module Grothendieck group with the specified perfect $K_0(X)$ and sends a bounded finite-projective resolution to its alternating class.

[F2] For $P=\Gamma(X,E)$, [[lem-author-394a5b71198d-exterior-dual-interface]] identifies $C^K_3(P)=\sum_{i=0}^3(-1)^i[\Lambda^iP^*]$, where $P^*=\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,A)$, with the dual exterior class in perfect $K_0(X)$. It also proves that this class and $\beta(E)$ vanish simultaneously.

[F3] [[prop-smooth-cycle-unique-divisibility]] gives unique divisibility, in particular absence of $2$-torsion, for the pure smooth-point subgroup and for its zero-extended top-dimensional counterpart. [[lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope]] identifies the latter's generators as the residue classes of smooth closed points on the three-dimensional components.

[F4] [[thm-author-394a5b71198d-murthy-module-splitting-input]] imports Murthy's criterion: for a reduced affine algebra of dimension $n\geq2$ over an algebraically closed field, if the subgroup of projective $K_0$ generated by residue-field classes of height-$n$ smooth closed points has no $(n-1)!$ torsion, then a rank-$n$ finite projective module splits off a free rank-one summand when its exterior-dual class vanishes. Its external-input section and reference give the precise source and reading limits.

## Proof

1.1 A finite-type algebra over a field is noetherian, and normality makes $A$ reduced. By [F1], $P=\Gamma(X,E)$ is finite projective of constant rank $3$, and $E$ is its associated bundle. The affine K-zero comparison in [F1] is an isomorphism. [F1, given]

2.1 Let $G\subset K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$ be the subgroup in [F4] for $n=3$. A height-three smooth closed point lies on a three-dimensional component, and conversely a smooth closed point on a three-dimensional component has height three. Over the perfect field $k$, these are the point classes in [F3]. Their residue modules admit finite projective resolutions: at such a point the local ring is regular, while the residue module is supported only there; the associated perfect residue sheaf has the same class as the alternating resolution by [F1]. Hence the comparison in [F1] identifies $G$ with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ in the pure case and with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ in the general normal case. By [F3], multiplication by $2$ is injective on that image, and therefore on $G$. Thus $G$ has no $(3-1)!=2$ torsion. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F2], $\beta(E)=0$ implies that the image of $C^K_3(P)$ in perfect $K_0(X)$ is zero. The comparison is injective by [F1], so $C^K_3(P)=0$ in projective $K_0(A)$. The reducedness, dimension, field, rank and factorial-torsion hypotheses of [F4] now hold. It follows that $P\cong A\oplus Q$ for a finite projective module $Q$ of rank two. Applying the affine equivalence gives $E\cong\mathcal O_X\oplus\widetilde Q$, where $\widetilde Q$ is a rank-two vector bundle. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 The pure smooth-point subgroup and the zero-extended top-dimensional subgroup are subgroups of $K_0(X)$ by their definitions. Thus an element trivial in either subgroup is zero in $K_0(X)$, and step 3.1 applies. The stated nonpure convention follows from [F3]. This proves the splitting implication and its subgroup formulations. [F3, step 3.1] ∎
