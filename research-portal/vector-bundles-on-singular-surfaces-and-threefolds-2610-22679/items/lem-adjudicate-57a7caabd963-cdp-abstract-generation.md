## Statement
Let $k$ be a field. On the category of separated finite-type $k$-schemes let $J$ be the topology generated by Nisnevich covering families and abstract blowup families whose proper map is an actual isomorphism on the open complement of the closed center. Every completely decomposed proper morphism is a $J$-cover. Consequently $J$ is the cdh topology generated by Nisnevich covering families and singleton completely decomposed proper morphisms. No reducedness hypothesis on the schemes is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, category and generated topology in the statement, and a completely decomposed proper morphism $p:Y\to X$.

**Goal:** Prove the reverse generation implication with actual open-complement isomorphisms.

[F1] The meanings of complete decomposition, properness, abstract blowup family and generated topology, and the fact that every actual abstract blowup family is a Nisnevich-plus-cdp cover, are supplied by [[def-cdh-topology-interface]], including its disjoint-union sieve justification.

[F2] A proper birational map of integral finite-type $k$-schemes is an isomorphism over a dense open of its target, with its entire inverse image. [[thm-adjudicate-57a7caabd963-birational-dense-open-input]]

[F3] Finite-type schemes over a field are noetherian of finite dimension, with finitely many irreducible components. A closed subset missing all generic points has smaller dimension. A reduced zero-dimensional such scheme is a finite disjoint union of spectra of fields. These follow from the affine noetherian dimension statements; all closed immersions and finite-type maps here are finitely presented.

## Proof

1.1 For every $X$, the reduction $X_{\mathrm{red}}\to X$ is a $J$-cover: take the abstract blowup family with closed center $X_{\mathrm{red}}$ and proper member $\varnothing$. Both open complements are empty, so the required actual isomorphism holds. Properness and complete decomposition survive base change. For the latter, a residue-field lift over $x$ base changes along $x'\to x$ to a $\kappa(x')$-valued lift. Thus, once the assertion is proved for reduced targets, apply it to $Y\times_X X_{\mathrm{red}}\to X_{\mathrm{red}}$ and then use local character along the reduction cover. It suffices to treat reduced targets. [F1, F3, construct]

1.2 Prove the assertion for reduced targets by induction on $d=\dim X$. The empty case is immediate. If $d=0$, each of the finitely many field components of $X$ has a residue-field-preserving lift in $Y$. These lifts define a section of $p$ over each component and hence over $X$. A map with a section generates the maximal sieve and covers. This proves the base case. [F1, F3, given]

1.3 For the induction step, for each generic point $\eta_i$ of the reduced $X$ choose a lift $y_i\in Y$ with $\kappa(y_i)=\kappa(\eta_i)$. Let $X_i$ be the corresponding reduced irreducible component and $Y_i$ the reduced closure of $y_i$ in $Y$. The map $Y_i\to X$ factors through $X_i$, since $Y_i$ is reduced and its image lies in that closed component. The induced map $Y_i\to X_i$ is proper and birational: the closure is integral with generic residue field $\kappa(y_i)$, and it is closed in the proper $X$-scheme $Y$. Apply [F2] to each such map. After deleting component intersections and the finitely many closed complements of the resulting dense opens, there is an open $U\subseteq X$ meeting every component such that $Y'=\coprod_iY_i\to X$ is an actual isomorphism over $U$. Let $Z$ be the reduced closed complement of $U$. Then $\dim Z<d$ and $\{Y'\to X,Z\to X\}$ is an actual abstract blowup family. [F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 The base change $Y\times_XZ\to Z$ is completely decomposed proper and therefore covers by the induction hypothesis, since $Z$ is reduced and has smaller dimension. For the sieve $R$ generated by $p$, its pullback to $Y'$ is maximal because $Y'\to X$ factors through $Y$. Its pullback to $Z$ is covering because it contains the covering sieve generated by $Y\times_XZ\to Z$. Local character along the abstract blowup family in step 1.3 makes $R$ covering. This proves the induction, and step 1.1 then supplies all nonreduced targets. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3]

3.1 Every generator of $J$ covers for the Nisnevich-plus-cdp topology by [F1]; conversely every cdp generator covers for $J$ by step 2.1, and the Nisnevich generators are common. Minimality of the two generated topologies proves equality. Only finitely many lifts and dense opens were selected in each finite-dimensional induction, so the local argument adds no arbitrary-index choice assumption. [F1, step 2.1] ∎
