## Remark
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the size conventions of the smooth-point filtration definition. Let $k$ be a perfect field and let $X$ be a quasi-compact, quasi-separated $k$-scheme of pure finite dimension $d\geq 0$, as in that definition. The subgroup of $K_0(X)$ generated by the classes of the residue sheaves of closed points where $X$ is smooth over $k$ is often written $F^dK_0(X)$. The article writes it $F^d_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ to distinguish this notation from $F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$.

Here $K_0(X)=\pi_0K(X)$ for the supplied nonconnective perfect-complex $K$-theory spectrum, and $F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$ is the image of $\pi_0F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X)\to K_0(X)$. The stated distinction is a distinction of notation; the subgroup inclusion below does not assert that the two groups are unequal.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The field, scheme, dimension, choice and size hypotheses in the remark.

**Goal:** Explain the two notations and their stated relationship.

[F1] [[def-smooth-point-filtration]] defines $F^d_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ as the subgroup generated by the indicated residue-sheaf classes and places it inside $F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$.

[F2] [[def-motivic-filtration]] defines the latter group as the image of the stage-to-$K$ map stated above.

[F3] The assumed [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]] discharges the explicit Choice hypotheses when invoking the smooth-point construction in [F1] and the perfect-complex $K$-theory and motivic-filtration construction in [F2]. This is an inherited construction premise; the notation argument makes no further selection.

## Justification
1.1 Under the given hypotheses, including the inherited Choice premise in [F3], [F1] identifies the smooth-point subgroup with the group whose alternative notation is described in the article. [F1, F3, given]

2.1 By [F1] and [F2], $F^d_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)\subseteq F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)\subseteq K_0(X)$. Writing the two subscripts makes their definitions distinguishable without asserting that the inclusion is strict. This establishes the remark. [F1, F2, step 1.1] ∎
