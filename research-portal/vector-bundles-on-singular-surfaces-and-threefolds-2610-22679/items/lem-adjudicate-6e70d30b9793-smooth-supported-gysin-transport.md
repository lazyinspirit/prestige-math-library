## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the compatible nested Grothendieck universes of the supplied smooth coefficient constructions, with $\omega$ in the smaller universe. Fix a perfect field $k$. For a regular closed immersion $i:Z\hookrightarrow V$ of codimension $d$ between smooth finite-type $k$-schemes, the specified integral Spitzweck/classical-to-EM field comparison preserves the refined motivic Gysin map and its forget-support map. Separatedness is not required. In particular the comparison preserves $i_!(1)$ for every such pair, including smooth closed-point pairs. For the literal positive-cycle normalization used here, assume additionally that $k$ is algebraically closed, $V$ is pure of dimension three and $x$ is a closed point of $V$: it takes the classical refined point class to the actual EM class $\delta_x$ represented by the reduced supported cycle $[x]$ with coordinate $+1$. This includes the supported codimension-three RR application; no Gysin formalism on singular normal qcqs schemes is asserted.

## Definition
Use the supplied universe-bounded smooth site and its Nisnevich-local, $\mathbb A^1$-invariant coefficient sheaves. Smoothness and finite type concern the morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ and have the scheme-theoretic meanings in the declared definitions; perfect means that nonconstant irreducible polynomials over $k$ are separable. A regular immersion of codimension $d$ means a closed immersion whose ideal sheaf $I$ is locally generated by a regular sequence of length $d$: each term is a non-zero-divisor modulo its predecessors and the generated ideal is proper at points of $Z$. Its normal bundle $N=(I/I^2)^\vee$ is the dual conormal bundle. The Thom space is the pointed quotient $\operatorname{Th}(N)=N/(N\setminus Z)$, with $Z$ its zero section. The coefficient and vector-bundle constructions, including this normal-bundle interface, are those of the primary-input supplier.

Write $E^{a,b}(Y)$ for the represented classical or EM coefficient theory, and $E_Z^{a,b}(V)$ for its closed-support theory, evaluated on $V/(V\setminus Z)$, equivalently the corresponding homotopy fiber of restriction. The refined map has type $i_!:E^{a,b}(Z)\to E_Z^{a+2d,b+d}(V)$. Forgetting support is induced contravariantly by $V_+\to V/(V\setminus Z)$. Here the EM map on the smooth site uses its supplied represented coefficient comparison and the oriented Thom/purity construction; the theorem identifies it with the classical map under the specified comparison, rather than postulating a Gysin construction on singular schemes. The comparison is the unit zigzag followed by the inverse of the structured EM comparison, with inverses taken in the functor category. The support-fiber and cohomological grading convention is the existing motivic support interface, and coherent spectral limits have the meaning of the stable-spectra interface. Empty supports have zero support theory; a codimension-zero clopen pair has the corresponding summand map with Thom class $1$.

## Proof
**Given:** The smooth regular pair, its normal bundle, the specified natural coefficient comparison, Choice and the compatible size bounds above.

**Goal:** Compare the actual supported maps and establish the stated positive-cycle consequence.

1.1 First check the domain of [F3]. For either smooth finite-type scheme $Y=V$ or $Y=Z$, choose a finite affine open cover $Y=\bigcup_a\operatorname{Spec}A_a$ with each $A_a$ generated by a finite number $n_a$ of elements over $k$. By [F6] each $A_a$ is Noetherian of Krull dimension at most $n_a$, so $Y$ is a Noetherian scheme. Its underlying space is Noetherian by [F5]. On each affine chart, a strict chain of irreducible closed subsets gives, by the radical defining ideals in [F6], a reversed strict chain of primes; thus its chain length is at most $n_a$. The open-cover dimension formula in [F6] bounds $\dim Y$ by the maximum of these finitely many $n_a$. Empty $Y$ has dimension $-\infty$ and needs no chart. Smoothness makes $Y$ regular by [F6]. The same check applies to the smooth finite-type projective completions and opens in the supplied Thom construction. Thus the source Noetherian, finite-dimension and regularity hypotheses hold, without a separatedness assumption. The empty and codimension-zero cases follow from the conventions in the Definition. Otherwise let $\overline N=\mathbb P_{\rm lines}(N\oplus\mathcal O)=\mathbb P_{\rm quot}(N^\vee\oplus\mathcal O)$ with its infinity divisor $\mathbb P_{\rm lines}(N)$. By [F3], restriction from its coefficient cohomology to that of its divisor at infinity is split surjective in every bidegree, by the projective bundle formula. The localization sequence consequently makes the map from Thom cohomology into $E(\overline N)$ injective. Use the actual top Chern class defining the Thom class in [F3], with the same line bundle after changing projectivization convention; [F1] preserves this universal class and all projective-bundle powers. Naturality on that restriction diagram identifies the unique refined Thom lifts. This comparison is natural: the projective-bundle section is given by the first $d$ powers of the common first Chern class, and the coefficient map commutes with that section.
