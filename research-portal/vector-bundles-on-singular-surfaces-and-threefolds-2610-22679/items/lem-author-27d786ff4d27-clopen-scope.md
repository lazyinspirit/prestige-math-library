## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC). Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three, and let $P$ be a rank-three vector bundle on $X$. Fix a functional $s:P\to\mathcal O_X$ whose scheme-theoretic zero locus $S=V(\operatorname{im}s)$ is finite and smooth over $k$ and lies in $X_{\mathrm{sm}/k}$; $S$ may be empty. Put $Q=\ker s$ and $U=X\setminus S$. Then $Q$ is coherent on $X$, and $Q|_U$ is a rank-two vector bundle in the exact sequence $0\to Q|_U\to P|_U\xrightarrow{s}\mathcal O_U\to0$. Fix a closed subset $Z\subset U$ of codimension at least two before choosing the next functional.

Write $X=\coprod_a X_a$ for its finite clopen integral-component decomposition, and put $U_a=U\cap X_a$, $Z_a=Z\cap U_a$, $E_a=(Q|_{U_a})^\vee$ and $W_a=U_a\setminus(Z_a\cup(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}})$. The integral construction uses on each component a finite indexed family of actual global sections of $E_a$ which generates $E_a$, spans $E_{a,x}/\mathfrak m_x^2E_{a,x}$ at every smooth closed point $x\in U_a$, and contains a controlling family on every member of a finite principal cover $W_a=\bigcup_i D(g_{a,i})$. Here each chart is a smooth affine open of $X_a$ on which $E_a$ has a frame $e_1,e_2$; its controlling family consists of that frame and all products $f_\ell e_r$, where the $f_\ell$ generate the chart's coordinate algebra over $k$. These families can be chosen for every fixed setup above by taking global generators of the coherent dual of $Q$ on each affine component and adjoining the required coordinate multiples; the chart families are restrictions of the same global indexed family on $U_a$.

There is a functional $t:Q|_U\to\mathcal O_U$ whose scheme-theoretic zero locus is a finite disjoint union of nonempty smooth geometrically integral curves of pure codimension two in $U$, one on each $X_a$, disjoint from $Z$ and contained in $U_{\mathrm{sm}/k}$. In particular it is a single such curve when $X$ is integral. The functional may depend on the fixed $Z$. A globally irreducible zero scheme cannot in general be demanded for an arbitrary fixed preceding pair $(U,Q)$ on a disconnected normal threefold, even when $P$ is trivial and its first two Chern classes vanish. This obstruction concerns that fixed pair, rather than the global theorem or every possible preceding point functional. Here the Chern classes in this comparison are the integral motivic Chern classes, with the fixed compatible site and universe bounds of their construction; on the smooth example the ordinary integral Chow classes vanish as well. These conventions concern the Chern comparison only and impose no Chern-vanishing assumption on the componentwise construction.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC and the field, affine finite-type pure threefold, bundle, fixed point functional, kernel, open set and prescribed avoidance locus specified in the Statement. A functional on $Q|_U$ is a section of its dual, and its zero scheme is defined locally by its coefficients. The finite-smooth-to-reduced-rational assertion is proved in step 1.1 from [F10].

**Goal:** Establish the componentwise curve construction with all its actual hypotheses, and retain the precise disconnected fixed-pair obstruction.

[F1] [[thm-author-fc2380accb47-normal-geometric-input]] gives the finite clopen integral decomposition and normality's codimension-two bound for the singular locus, with regular and smooth loci equal over the perfect field $k$. In particular, $(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}}=U_a\setminus(U_a)_{\mathrm{sm}/k}$ is closed, and each of its irreducible components has codimension at least two: its generic point is nonregular, so $(R_1)$ excludes local-ring dimension at most one, using the supplier's codimension convention. The properties of the arbitrary fixed $s$ and of its finite smooth zero scheme are established from the general interfaces [F9] and [F10], rather than inferred from an existential point-section construction.

[F2] [[thm-author-27d786ff4d27-determinantal-height]] bounds the height of the proper ideal of maximal minors of a $2\times3$ matrix by two, with prime height defined as $\dim A_{\mathfrak p}$. [[math:cor-primes-of-a-prime-local-ring]] identifies primes of $A_{\mathfrak p}$ with primes of $A$ contained in $\mathfrak p$, preserving inclusion. This compares that local dimension with the lengths of prime chains ending at $\mathfrak p$ in the example.

[F3] [[lem-author-27d786ff4d27-dual-global-controls]] supplies the actual finite global dual-section families, their first jets and their controlling chart restrictions. [[lem-author-27d786ff4d27-chartwise-integrality]] is applied with the enlarged fixed avoidance set $Z'_a=Z_a\cup(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}}$ to those families on the same $W_a$, using its common-core nonemptiness and fixed-locus avoidance opens. By [F1], $Z'_a$ is closed of codimension at least two. The supplier then produces a nonempty smooth geometrically integral zero curve avoiding $Z'_a$, hence avoiding $Z_a$ and contained in $(U_a)_{\mathrm{sm}/k}$. The AC assumption of these suppliers, the coherence theorem [F9], and the finite étale classification [F10] is carried here through [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]. It is inherited at these invocations; the subsequent finite clopen assembly introduces no arbitrary-index choice requirement.

[F4] [[math:def-affine-scheme]] fixes affine schemes with their structure sheaves, and [[math:def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]] fixes a rank-$r$ vector bundle as an $\mathcal O_X$-module locally isomorphic to $\mathcal O_X^r$; a frame is such a basis. The dual on an open $V$ is the sheaf assigning to $T\subseteq V$ the $\mathcal O_T$-linear maps from the restricted module to $\mathcal O_T$. Kernels are sectionwise kernels, images are sheaf images, and exactness is equality of image and kernel sheaves. A functional's zero ideal is generated by its coefficients in a frame; an invertible change of frame gives the same ideal, so these coefficient-ideal subschemes glue. [[math:def-smooth-morphism-schemes]] fixes smoothness of the structure map to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ and its preservation by open restriction. [[math:def-coherent-module-scheme]] fixes coherence, including quasi-coherence and finite type. [[math:def-sheaf-on-topological-space]] supplies locality and unique gluing of compatible sections on an open cover, including the empty cover. We use it to glue the clopen idempotents and the component functionals; for the latter, glue their values on each local section, which preserves linearity and commutes with restriction by uniqueness.

[F5] [[math:def-field]] fixes the field operations, and [[math:def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]] fixes the polynomial rings and monomial expansions used in the example. Here algebraically closed means that every nonconstant polynomial in one variable has a root; hence every irreducible polynomial is linear and separable, so $k$ is perfect. Finite type on the affine scheme means $A=k[f_1,\ldots,f_d]$ for a finite list. A ring is Noetherian if every ideal is finitely generated; a field is Noetherian since it has just two ideals. [[math:cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]] therefore makes $A$ Noetherian, and its single affine chart makes $X$ a Noetherian scheme by [[math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]]. Normal means that all local rings are integrally closed domains; integral means nonempty, irreducible and reduced; clopen means both closed and open. Dimension is supremum of strict irreducible-closed chains, and pure dimension three means that every irreducible component has dimension three. We use [F1]'s local-ring convention for codimension. A finite $k$-scheme is affine with finite-dimensional coordinate algebra over $k$. A curve is pure dimension one. Geometrically integral means integral after every field extension, as in the curve supplier [F3]. [[math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion]] fixes $D(g)=\{\mathfrak p:g\notin\mathfrak p\}$ with its coordinate ring $A_g$. The first-jet space at $x$ is $E_x/\mathfrak m_x^2E_x$; spanning means surjectivity from the finite coefficient space over $k$. Generation means stalkwise spanning as a module. These are the same conventions used by [F3].

[F6] [[math:ex-normal-affine-space]] gives normality of $\mathbb A_k^3$. [[math:def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]] includes the polynomial presentation with three variables, no equations and empty Jacobian minor equal to 1. [[math:thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]] makes this presentation smooth in the sense of [F4]. [[math:cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]] gives $\dim k[x,y,z]=3$. [[math:cor-multivariate-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]] makes the coordinate polynomial rings and their variable-quotient polynomial rings domains. These verify the geometric hypotheses and the primality assertions in the example.

[F7] [[lem-adjudicate-c48bc07f0450-punctured-affine-lifting]] supplies the polynomial representatives of all dual-kernel sections on $\mathbb A_k^3\setminus\{0\}$, including $\Gamma(\mathcal O)=k[x,y,z]$, vanishing of $H^1$, the ordered localization/Koszul comparison and explicit gluing of local lifts. It assumes AC, already carried in [F3].

[F8] [[def-motivic-chern-classes]] defines the actual integral motivic classes $c_i(P)\in H^{2i,i}_{\mathrm{mot}}(X,\mathbb Z)$ by the signed projective-bundle relation. Under AC and its compatible site and universe bounds, [[lem-author-b02e51f9da03-trivial-chern]] gives $c_t(\mathcal O_X^m)=1$ on qcqs schemes over a field, and the same identity for ordinary integral Chow classes on smooth equidimensional finite-type schemes, with the latter classes defined by [[math:def-chern-classes-of-a-vector-bundle]]. Here $c_t=1+\sum_{i\geq1}c_it^i$ is the total Chern series in a formal variable $t$. Quasi-compact means that every open cover has a finite subcover; quasi-separated means that intersections of two affine opens are quasi-compact. The comparison example is affine, hence qcqs by [[math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]] and [[math:thm-affine-schemes-quasi-separated]], and smooth of pure dimension three. [[math:ex-spectrum-product-ring-disjoint-union]] identifies the two-copy union, including its structure sheaf, with the spectrum of the product of the two polynomial rings. Thus this union is affine and qcqs as well; smoothness and pure dimension three hold on its two open components. Thus these are the actual Chern theories in the comparison, not freely assigned symbols. Their site and universe bounds are retained for this comparison; they are not consequences of AC.

[F9] Under AC, [[math:thm-coherent-sheaves-abelian-noetherian-scheme]] identifies finite-type quasi-coherent sheaves with coherent sheaves on a locally Noetherian scheme and makes kernels of morphisms of coherent sheaves coherent. Finite locally free sheaves are quasi-coherent by [F4]'s bundle definition and are of finite type on their frame charts, so this applies to $P$ and $\mathcal O_X$ after [F5]. [[lem-surjection-kernel-bundle]] applies to any morphism from a rank-$r$ vector bundle to $\mathcal O_X$: off its coefficient zero locus it is surjective, splits locally, and its sectionwise kernel restricts to a rank-$(r-1)$ vector bundle. Thus its interface applies to the particular fixed $s$, without requiring that $s$ be chosen by a Bertini construction.

[F10] [[math:def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]] defines relative dimension using the local dimensions of the fibers after every field extension; local dimension is the infimum of dimensions of open neighborhoods. [[math:def-etale-morphism-schemes]] defines étale as smooth of relative dimension zero. Under AC, [[math:thm-etale-over-algebraically-closed-field-discrete-smooth-points]] states that every finite étale $k$-scheme over algebraically closed $k$ is a finite disjoint union of copies of $\operatorname{Spec}k$, including the empty case. For our arbitrary finite smooth $S=\operatorname{Spec}B$, [[math:thm-affine-fibre-product-tensor-ring]] identifies $S_K$ with $\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_k K)$ for every field extension $K/k$. The tensor product in [[math:def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]] consists of finite sums of elementary tensors subject to additivity and balancing. Thus a finite spanning list $b_i$ of $B$ over $k$ gives the spanning list $b_i\otimes1$ over $K$, with the scalar action supplied by this tensor-ring identification. The finite-dimensional rank-nullity formula of [[math:thm-rank-nullity]] shows that every prime quotient of this finite-dimensional $K$-algebra is a field, as proved below. Under AC, [[math:thm-irreducible-closed-subsets-and-prime-ideals]] identifies nonempty irreducible closed subsets of an affine spectrum with the vanishing sets of prime ideals. These inputs establish relative dimension zero in step 1.1, so the classification applies to the stipulated $S$.

## Proof
1.1 By [F5], $X$ is Noetherian. Its finite locally free sheaves $P$ and $\mathcal O_X$ are finite-type quasi-coherent on their frame charts, hence coherent by [F9] under AC. Apply its coherent-kernel theorem to this fixed morphism $s$ to obtain coherent $Q=\ker s$. Its coefficient zero locus is the stipulated $S$ by [F4]. Thus the off-zero kernel lemma in [F9] applies to the same $s$: the sequence in the Statement is exact and locally split on $U$, with rank-two kernel $Q|_U$. To justify the finite-smooth assertion for this arbitrary $S$, write $S=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ finite dimensional over $k$. For every field extension $K/k$, [F10] identifies $S_K=\operatorname{Spec}C$ for the finite-dimensional $K$-algebra $C=B\otimes_k K$. For each prime $\mathfrak p$ of $C$, the quotient $D=C/\mathfrak p$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional $K$-domain. Multiplication by any nonzero $d\in D$ is injective, hence surjective by rank-nullity in [F10]; an inverse image of $1$ is an inverse of $d$. Thus every prime of $C$ is maximal. By the irreducible-closed-subset correspondence in [F10], every nonempty irreducible closed subset of $\operatorname{Spec}C$ is consequently a singleton. Every nonempty open also has dimension zero: an irreducible closed subset of an open has irreducible closure in the whole space, and is therefore a singleton as well. Hence the local dimension at every point of $S_K$ is zero, for every $K/k$. The empty case is vacuous. Together with the given smoothness, [F10] gives relative dimension zero and therefore étaleness; its general classification now makes this $S$ a finite disjoint union of reduced $k$-rational points. By [F1] and [F5], the components $X_a$ are finitely many clopen normal integral threefolds. To verify affineness and finite type, glue the functions $1$ on $X_a$ and $0$ on its complement to $e_a\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=A$. Then $e_a^2=e_a$, $X_a=D(e_a)$, and [F5] identifies it with $\operatorname{Spec}A_{e_a}$. Since $e_a$ is idempotent, $A_{e_a}=A/(1-e_a)$, a finite-type $k$-algebra. Restriction of $s$ has zero scheme $S\cap X_a$, still finite and smooth in the smooth locus; its coherent kernel restricts to $Q|_{X_a}$ and has rank two on $U_a$. By [F1], $(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}}$ is closed of codimension at least two. Set $Z'_a=Z_a\cup(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}}$, a closed subset fixed before choosing the next functional. Every irreducible component of a union of two closed subsets is an irreducible component of one of them: an irreducible subset of that union must lie in one member, and maximality then gives the assertion. Thus $Z'_a$ has codimension at least two. Neither member contains the generic point of $U_a$, so $W_a=U_a\setminus Z'_a$ is nonempty. Moreover $U_a\setminus(Z'_a\cup(U_a)_{\mathrm{sing}})=W_a$, the same smooth integral open and controlling chart cover as in the Statement. Apply the global dual construction of [F3] on each component with this particular $W_a$, obtaining the indexed family described in the Statement. In particular, in its construction a frame $g_{a,i}\psi_1,g_{a,i}\psi_2$ on $D(g_{a,i})$ has globally extendable controlling products even for the coordinate $1/g_{a,i}$, whose products with the frame are $\psi_1,\psi_2$. Thus apply the chartwise lemma in [F3] with avoidance subset $Z'_a$ and the actual global family on this unchanged $W_a$. Its common-core and avoidance conditions apply to this enlarged fixed set, and it gives a functional on each $Q|_{U_a}$ whose entire zero scheme is a nonempty smooth geometrically integral curve of pure codimension two avoiding $Z'_a$. Consequently the curve avoids $Z_a$ and lies in $(U_a)_{\mathrm{sm}/k}$. The sheaf gluing axiom on the disjoint clopen cover identifies sections with tuples, so these functionals assemble into $t$. The coefficient ideals agree with those on each component by [F4]. Its zero scheme is precisely the disjoint union of the component zero schemes; smoothness, geometric integrality of each curve, pure codimension two and avoidance hold componentwise. When $X$ is integral this is a single curve. The fixed $Z$ was chosen before these parameters. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F9, F10, given, algebra]

2.1 For the obstruction set $R=k[x,y,z]$ and take two copies of $X_0=\mathbb A_k^3=\operatorname{Spec}R$, with $P=\mathcal O^3$ and point functional $(x,y,z)$ on each. By [F5] and [F6] these are normal affine finite-type pure threefolds, smooth over $k$. Their point zero scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}(k[x,y,z]/(x,y,z))=\operatorname{Spec}k$, the reduced origin, so it satisfies the required finite smooth condition. On $U_0=X_0\setminus\{0\}$ let $Q_0$ be its rank-two kernel. The locally split dual sequence is $0\to\mathcal O_{U_0}\xrightarrow{(x,y,z)^t}\mathcal O_{U_0}^3\to Q_0^\vee\to0$. By [F7], every section of $Q_0^\vee$ is represented by a polynomial vector $b\in k[x,y,z]^3$; its proof retains the regular-sequence Koszul calculation, identifies the augmented localization degree two with ordered Čech degree one, applies the affine-cover sheaf-cohomology comparison, and proves the necessary lifting exactness by explicit gluing. The zero scheme of this section on $U_0$ is defined by the $2\times2$ minors of the matrix with rows $(x,y,z)$ and $b$: locally one of $x,y,z$ is invertible, and elementary row/column elimination identifies the two residual coordinates with those minors up to that unit. Its minor ideal $J\subset k[x,y,z]$ is proper because every minor vanishes at the origin. The ring is commutative Noetherian by [F5], so [F2] applies and gives a prime over $J$ of height at most two. The origin's maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)$ has height three: the displayed ideals in $(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq(x,y)\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ are prime, since their quotients are polynomial domains or $k$ by [F6]. By the prime-localization correspondence in [F2], this chain gives a chain of length three in $R_{\mathfrak m}$, and every chain there contracts to a chain in $R$. Therefore [F6]'s ring-dimension bound gives $3\leq\dim R_{\mathfrak m}\leq\dim R=3$. Thus the prime supplied by [F2] is not the origin, and $V(J)\cap U_0$ is nonempty. No functional $Q_0\to\mathcal O_{U_0}$ is therefore nowhere zero. On two copies every global functional has zeros in both clopen components, and its zero locus is disconnected and cannot be irreducible. By [F8], trivial $P$ has $c_1=c_2=0$ in the stated motivic theory, as well as in the ordinary Chow theory on this smooth example, so that additional article hypothesis does not remove this fixed-pair counterexample. This does not obstruct first reducing the global theorem to components, or assert that every possible preceding point section gives the same pair. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, given, algebra] ∎
