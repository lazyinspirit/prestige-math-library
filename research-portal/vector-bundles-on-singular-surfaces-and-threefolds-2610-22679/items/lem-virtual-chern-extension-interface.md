## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $H$ be an additively oriented bigraded theory with the projective bundle contract on a category $\mathcal C$ of schemes. For each $X$ in $\mathcal C$, suppose that every finite locally free $\mathcal O_X$-module $E$ is assigned a series
$$c_t(E)=1+\sum_{i\geq 1}c_i(E)t^i,\qquad c_i(E)\in H^{2i,i}(X),$$
depending only on its isomorphism class. Assume that these series are natural under morphisms in $\mathcal C$ and satisfy the Whitney identity $c_t(E)=c_t(E')c_t(E'')$ for every short exact sequence $0\to E'\to E\to E''\to0$ of finite locally free modules.

Write $K^0_{\mathrm{vb}}(X)$ for the abelian group generated by the isomorphism classes $[E]$ of finite locally free modules, with the relations $[E]=[E']+[E'']$ for all such exact sequences. Then there is a unique group homomorphism
$$c_t:K^0_{\mathrm{vb}}(X)\longrightarrow \left\{1+\sum_{i\geq1}a_i t^i:a_i\in H^{2i,i}(X)\right\},$$
where the target group uses multiplication of formal series, taking $[E]$ to $c_t(E)$. Every element can be written $[E]-[F]$, and its image is $c_t(E)c_t(F)^{-1}$. The construction is natural in $X$. When $X$ is locally Noetherian, $K^0_{\mathrm{vb}}(X)$ is the vector-bundle Grothendieck group $K^0(X)$ of the cited library definition. The extension is conditional on the stated exact-sequence Whitney identity; the individual coefficients are not asserted to be additive. In particular, the second coefficient obeys the cross-term formula $c_2(x+y)=c_2(x)+c_1(x)c_1(y)+c_2(y)$.

A finite locally free module is an $\mathcal O_X$-module locally isomorphic to a finite direct sum of copies of $\mathcal O_X$. Naturality of the given series means $f^*c_i(E)=c_i(f^*E)$ for every morphism $f:Y\to X$ in $\mathcal C$. A short exact sequence here is an exact sequence of sheaves whose three terms are finite locally free. Formal-series multiplication uses the finite coefficient sums $(ab)_n=\sum_{j=0}^n a_jb_{n-j}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The category, theory, natural bundle series and exact-sequence Whitney identity in the statement, together with the Axiom of Choice.

**Goal:** Construct the unique natural extension, establish the difference formula and the stated coefficient identity, and identify the locally Noetherian case.

[F1] The theory has associative unital bigraded rings, multiplication of bidegree $(0,0)$, graded commutativity in the first degree, and degree-preserving contravariant unital ring pullbacks. These are part of the given theory's contract ([[def-bigraded-oriented-theory]]).

[F2] Under Choice, the published vector-bundle Grothendieck group for a locally Noetherian scheme has exactly the generator-and-exact-sequence presentation stated above ([[math:def-grothendieck-group-of-coherent-sheaves-and-vector-bundles-on-a-scheme]], [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F3] Compatible sheaves of modules on an open cover of a fixed ringed space glue, with the specified identifications, uniquely up to unique compatible isomorphism. Apply this to finite free local modules and invertible transition matrices satisfying the identity and cocycle conditions ([[math:thm-gluing-sheaves]]).

[F4] For a scheme morphism $f:Y\to X$, module pullback is $f^*E=\mathcal O_Y\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_X}f^{-1}E$, a construction on the underlying ringed spaces ([[math:def-pullback-module-ringed-spaces]]). On any morphism of ringed spaces, pullback carries a finite free module of rank $n$ to a finite free module of rank $n$, including $n=0$ ([[math:ex-pullback-free-module]]). This module pullback is distinct from the cohomology pullback in [F1].

[F5] The Axiom of Choice permits a simultaneous choice of a trivializing neighbourhood and a frame at each point of a fixed bundle's underlying scheme ([[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[A1] The assigned bundle series are invariant under isomorphism, natural, and multiplicative in every short exact sequence, as stated in the hypotheses.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Construct the target group coefficient by coefficient and descend a map through the presentation.

1.1 Put $R_X=\bigoplus_{i\geq0}H^{2i,i}(X)$. By [F1], this is a commutative graded ring: the first degree of each homogeneous summand is even. The displayed target consists of series with constant coefficient $1$ and coefficient of $t^i$ in $H^{2i,i}(X)$. Finite coefficient sums show that it is closed under associative, commutative multiplication and contains $1$. For $a(t)=1+\sum_{i\geq1}a_i t^i$, define $b_0=1$ and recursively $b_n=-\sum_{j=1}^n a_jb_{n-j}$. Each $b_n$ has bidegree $(2n,n)$, and the coefficient of $t^n$ in $a(t)b(t)$ is zero for $n>0$. Thus $b(t)$ is an inverse in the displayed set, which is therefore an abelian group. [F1, construct]

1.2 The bundle isomorphism types form a set. For a given bundle, use [F5] to choose for each point $x$ a trivializing open $U_x$ containing $x$, a finite rank $n_x$ and a frame. Record the opens, ranks and invertible transition matrices satisfying the identity and cocycle conditions. All such recorded data form a set: their index set is the fixed underlying set of points of $X$, the opens belong to its topology, the ranks belong to $\mathbb N$, and each matrix has finitely many entries in the set of sections $\mathcal O_X(U_x\cap U_y)$. By [F3], the modules $\mathcal O_{U_x}^{n_x}$ and these overlap maps glue to a finite locally free module. Every bundle is isomorphic to the gluing of its recorded data. Quotienting the set of data by isomorphism of the glued modules therefore gives a set $S$ of bundle isomorphism types; the empty scheme is covered by the empty datum and gives its unique module. Form the abelian group of finitely supported functions $S\to\mathbb Z$ with pointwise addition, and quotient by the subgroup generated by the exact-sequence relation vectors. This constructs the presented group. The split sequence $0\to E\to E\oplus F\to F\to0$ gives $[E\oplus F]=[E]+[F]$, and the zero sequence gives $[0]=0$. Collecting positive and negative terms in a finite expression now writes every element as $[E]-[F]$, using the zero bundle for an empty sum. [F3, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 By [A1] and step 1.1, the rule sending a finitely supported function $m:S\to\mathbb Z$ to the finite product $\prod_{s\in S}c_t(s)^{m(s)}$ defines a homomorphism to the target. Isomorphism invariance makes $c_t(s)$ well defined; the product is finite and negative powers exist by step 1.1. This sends the generator $[E]$ to $c_t(E)$. For every defining exact-sequence relation its image is $c_t(E)c_t(E')^{-1}c_t(E'')^{-1}=1$ by the Whitney identity. It therefore descends to the quotient. Its value on $[E]-[F]$ is $c_t(E)c_t(F)^{-1}$. Since the classes $[E]$ generate the quotient, these prescribed values also make the extension unique. [A1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For $f:Y\to X$, trivialize $E$ on an open $U\subseteq X$ and put $V=f^{-1}U$. The module-pullback construction in [F4] commutes with restriction to $V$: the same neighbourhood representatives, their sheafification and tensor restrictions define pullback along $f|_V:V\to U$. The finite-free calculation in [F4] therefore gives $(f^*E)|_V\cong\mathcal O_V^n$ when $E|_U\cong\mathcal O_U^n$. These inverse-image opens cover $Y$, so $f^*E$ is finite locally free even when ranks vary. Pullback preserves isomorphisms by applying the construction to a map and its inverse. A local matrix for a bundle morphism is pulled back by applying the structure-sheaf map to its entries, as follows by evaluating the tensor formula on each basis vector. To treat an exact sequence, at each point trivialize its quotient term and lift its finitely many basis germs through the sheaf epimorphism; after shrinking to a common neighbourhood these lifts map to the basis sections and define a splitting. Inverse image and tensor on module morphisms preserve sums, compositions and identities by their additive defining operations, so preserve the splitting equations and hence the resulting split exact sequence. Exactness is local, and thus pullback preserves every relation in the presentation. It follows that $[E]\mapsto[f^*E]$ defines a homomorphism on the presented groups. For composable scheme morphisms, identify the frames of the iterated and composite pullbacks on inverse-image trivializing opens. Their transition matrices are the images of the original matrices under the same composite structure-sheaf map, so these identifications agree on overlaps and glue by [F3]. Identity pullback similarly preserves each frame. Hence the maps on bundle classes are contravariantly functorial. None of these opens needs to be an object of $\mathcal C$; these are computations on underlying ringed spaces. By [F1], cohomology pullback acts coefficientwise on the target series and preserves products and inverses. The assumed naturality gives $f^*c_t(E)=c_t(f^*E)$ on generators, hence on every virtual class by step 2.1. For locally Noetherian $X$, [F2] identifies this same presentation with the published $K^0(X)$; no locally Noetherian hypothesis is used in the preceding construction. [F1, F2, F3, F4, A1, step 2.1]

4.1 Comparing the coefficient of $t^2$ in $c_t(x+y)=c_t(x)c_t(y)$ gives $c_2(x+y)=c_2(x)+c_1(x)c_1(y)+c_2(y)$. Thus coefficientwise additivity does not follow from the group homomorphism: for example, in the formal series group over the ring $\mathbb Z[u]$ of finite polynomials in $u$ with integer coefficients, the homomorphism $n\mapsto(1+ut)^n$ has second coefficient zero at $1$ and $u^2$ at $2$. This formal example illustrates the cross term; it makes no additional assertion about a particular cohomology theory. The construction and all claimed properties follow. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
