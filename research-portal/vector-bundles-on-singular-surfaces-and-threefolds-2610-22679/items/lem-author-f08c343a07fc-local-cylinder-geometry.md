## Statement
Assume AC. Let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be a nonempty smooth affine surface over a field $k$: here surface means a finite-type $k$-scheme of dimension two whose irreducible components all have dimension two, and smooth means that its structure morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth. Put $\mathbb A^1_k=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$. Then the canonical identification $Y=\operatorname{Spec}A[t]\cong X\times_k\mathbb A^1_k$ makes $Y$ a smooth affine finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three. The projection $p:Y\to X$ and zero section $s:X\to Y$, induced respectively by the inclusion $A\to A[t]$ and evaluation $A[t]\to A$ at $t=0$, are $k$-morphisms satisfying $p\circ s=\operatorname{id}_X$. For every vector bundle $E$ on $X$, meaning a finite locally free $\mathcal O_X$-module, the sheaf pullbacks $p^*E$ and $s^*p^*E$ are finite locally free and there is a canonical isomorphism $s^*p^*E\cong E$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the field $k$ and the scheme $X$ in the statement, with the dimension and vector-bundle conventions stated there.

**Goal:** Prove the cylinder, section and pullback assertions.

[F0] A field has the operations and axioms of [[math:def-field]]; in particular its axioms make it a commutative unital ring. Rings here are commutative with identity, and ring maps preserve identity. [[math:def-commutative-ring]], [[math:def-ring-homomorphism]] A commutative $k$-algebra is such a ring with a unital structure map from $k$; algebra maps commute with this map. [[math:def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]] An affine scheme is a locally ringed space isomorphic to a spectrum with its structure sheaf. The points of $\operatorname{Spec}R$ are prime ideals, with distinguished-open basis $D(h)=\{\mathfrak p:h\notin\mathfrak p\}$. [[math:def-affine-scheme]], [[math:def-affine-scheme-spectrum]] The polynomials $R[t]$ are finite coefficient sequences with coefficientwise addition and convolution multiplication. [[math:def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]] A scheme is locally affine; its morphisms are morphisms of locally ringed spaces, and a $k$-scheme has a specified morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$. A $k$-morphism commutes with these structure maps. [[math:def-scheme]], [[math:def-morphism-of-schemes]], [[math:def-scheme-over-base]] The fibre product is characterized by compatible pairs of morphisms to its factors; the base change of the affine-line structure map to $X$ is its projection to $X$. [[math:def-fibre-product-schemes-universal-property]]

[F1] An irreducible subset is nonempty and cannot be written as the union of two proper closed subsets in its subspace topology. [[math:def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset]] An irreducible component of a scheme is a maximal irreducible closed subset of its underlying topological space; only this underlying closed subset is needed here. [[math:def-irreducible-component-scheme]] Scheme and component dimension mean the supremum of lengths of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets, and pure dimension $d$ means that every irreducible component has dimension $d$, as in [[lem-adjudicate-fa9c7180089a-polynomial-spectrum-dimension]]. For a nonzero commutative ring $R$, its Krull dimension $\dim R$ is the supremum of the integers $n\geq0$ for which a strict prime chain $\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n$ exists. [[math:def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]] Under AC a noetherian ring of finite Krull dimension gains exactly one dimension on adjoining a variable. [[math:thm-dimension-of-a-polynomial-ring-over-a-noetherian-ring]] The comparison of ring and scheme dimensions, including preservation of pure dimension after adjoining a variable, is supplied by [[lem-adjudicate-fa9c7180089a-polynomial-spectrum-dimension]].

[F2] Smooth morphisms are stable under base change and composition. [[math:thm-smooth-morphisms-stable-base-change-composition]]

[F3] Smoothness has the scheme-theoretic meaning of [[math:def-smooth-morphism-schemes]]. The presentation of $k[t]$ with one variable and no equations is a finite presentation, so its spectrum map is locally of finite presentation by [[math:def-locally-finite-presentation-morphism]]. Its empty Jacobian minor is $1$; under AC the relative Jacobian criterion therefore makes $\mathbb A^1_k\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ smooth. [[math:thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]]

[F4] An affine finite-type $k$-scheme has finitely generated coordinate algebra under AC. [[math:lem-affine-finite-type-scheme-coordinate-ring-finitely-generated]] A field is noetherian, and a finite-type algebra over a noetherian ring is noetherian. [[math:lem-field-is-noetherian]], [[math:cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]] Finite type for a scheme morphism means locally finite type and quasi-compact; under AC affine schemes are quasi-compact. [[math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]], [[math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]] Here finite type for an algebra means generation by finitely many elements, and noetherian means every ideal is finitely generated. [[math:def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]], [[math:def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]

[F5] Affine fibre products are spectra of tensor products, with the canonical ring inclusions giving their projections; ring homomorphisms give affine scheme morphisms contravariantly and respect identities and composition. [[math:thm-affine-fibre-product-tensor-ring]], [[math:thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]] The tensor product is the coproduct of commutative algebras, and a coefficient map together with the image of $t$ determines a unique map from a polynomial ring. [[math:thm-coproduct-property-of-tensor-products-of-commutative-algebras]], [[math:thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]

[F6] A finite locally free sheaf is locally a finite direct sum of the structure sheaf and is quasi-coherent. [[math:def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]] Module pullback is $f^*\mathcal F=\mathcal O_T\otimes_{f^{-1}\mathcal O_S}f^{-1}\mathcal F$ for $f:T\to S$. [[math:def-pullback-module-ringed-spaces]] Under AC, on an affine scheme a quasi-coherent sheaf is canonically the associated sheaf of its module of global sections; pullback along $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ takes $\widetilde M$ to $\widetilde{B\otimes_R M}$ and preserves quasi-coherence. [[math:thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence]], [[math:lem-pullback-qc-module-quasi-coherent]] Additive balanced formulas on elementary tensors define unique homomorphisms. [[math:prop-elementary-tensor-formulas-descend-exactly-when-balanced]]

[F7] AC is assumed through the cited dimension, affine equivalence, quasi-compactness and smoothness results. The explicit coefficient and tensor maps introduce no additional infinite choices. [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F8] Modules are unital; an $\mathcal O_T$-module is a sheaf with sectionwise module actions compatible with restrictions. [[math:def-left-and-right-modules]], [[math:def-module-on-ringed-space]] The tensor product is generated additively by elementary tensors subject to additivity in each factor and the balancing relation; every tensor is a finite sum of elementary tensors. For commutative scalars its module action is $r(m\otimes n)=(rm)\otimes n=m\otimes(rn)$. [[math:def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], [[math:thm-commutative-ring-module-structure-on-a-tensor-product]] The associated sheaf $\widetilde M$ has sections $M_h$ on $D(h)$; a quasi-coherent module is one that is locally associated to a module on affine opens. [[math:def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]], [[math:def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]

[F9] An affine open carries the restricted structure sheaf. Every point of a scheme lies in an affine open by the scheme definition in [F0]. Within such a chart, the distinguished opens in [F0] refine any open neighborhood, and each $D(h)$ is affine because $\operatorname{Spec}R_h\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ identifies it with its open locally ringed subspace. Thus affine opens refine all open neighborhoods. [[math:def-affine-open-subscheme]], [[math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F4], $A$ is finitely generated over the noetherian field $k$, hence noetherian. It is nonzero since $X$ is nonempty. The dimension comparison in [F1] gives $\dim A=2$, and its polynomial-dimension theorem gives $\dim A[t]=3$; its purity assertion gives pure dimension three for $Y$. If $A=k[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$, then $A[t]=k[a_1,\ldots,a_n,t]$, so the affine chart of $Y$ is locally of finite type, and its quasi-compactness gives finite type by [F4]. The universal properties in [F5] give inverse $k$-algebra maps $A\otimes_k k[t]\to A[t]$, $a\otimes f(t)\mapsto af(t)$, and $A[t]\to A\otimes_k k[t]$, $\sum_i a_it^i\mapsto\sum_i a_i\otimes t^i$. Their composites fix coefficients and $t$, hence are identities. Thus [F5] identifies $Y$ with the fibre product and $p$ with the base change of $\mathbb A^1_k\to\operatorname{Spec}k$. By [F3] and [F2], $p$ is smooth and its composite with the smooth structure map of $X$ is smooth. This proves all geometric assertions about $Y$. [F0, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F7, given]

2.1 Put $B=A[t]$ and let $\varepsilon:B\to A$ be evaluation at zero. Polynomial evaluation in [F5] makes this a $k$-algebra map, with $\varepsilon|_A=\operatorname{id}_A$. The contravariant correspondence in [F5] therefore gives the stated $k$-morphisms $p,s$ and $p\circ s=\operatorname{id}_X$. By [F6] write $E\cong\widetilde M$ canonically, where $M=\Gamma(X,E)$; then $p^*E\cong\widetilde{B\otimes_A M}$ and $s^*p^*E\cong\widetilde{A\otimes_B(B\otimes_A M)}$, with the $B$-action on $A$ given by $\varepsilon$. The formula $a\otimes(b\otimes m)\mapsto a\varepsilon(b)m$ is additive and balanced in both tensor operations: the inner relation uses $\varepsilon(c)=c$ for $c\in A$, and the outer relation uses $\varepsilon(b'b)=\varepsilon(b')\varepsilon(b)$. By [F6] it defines an additive map; the scalar action in [F8] shows that it respects multiplication by each $a_0\in A$, so it is $A$-linear, inverse to $m\mapsto1\otimes(1\otimes m)$, since the balancing relations reduce $a\otimes(b\otimes m)$ to $1\otimes(1\otimes a\varepsilon(b)m)$. Taking associated sheaves gives the canonical isomorphism with $E$, naturally in $M$. Finally let $f:T\to S$ be either $p$ or $s$, and let $\mathcal F$ be respectively $E$ or $p^*E$, the latter after the former case has been established. For a point $x\in T$, choose an open trivializing neighborhood of $f(x)$ for $\mathcal F$ by [F6], and refine it to an affine neighborhood $V=\operatorname{Spec}R$ by [F9]. The inverse image $f^{-1}(V)$ is open since scheme morphisms are continuous; [F9] gives an affine neighborhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}C$ of $x$ inside it. On $V$, restriction of the trivialization gives $\mathcal F|_V\cong\mathcal O_V^r\cong\widetilde{R^r}$. The affine pullback formula in [F6] gives $(f^*\mathcal F)|_U\cong\widetilde{C\otimes_R R^r}$. Its coordinate isomorphism with $\mathcal O_U^r$ is induced by $c\otimes(r_i)_{i=1}^r\mapsto(c\varphi(r_i))_{i=1}^r$, where $\varphi:R\to C$ is induced by $f$; the inverse sends $(c_i)_{i=1}^r$ to $\sum_{i=1}^r c_i\otimes e_i$, with $e_i$ the coordinate unit vectors. These formulas are additive and balanced by [F8], and their composites are identities by the balancing relations, including for $r=0$. Every $x$ has such a neighborhood $U$, so first $p^*E$ and then $s^*p^*E$ are finite locally free. [F0, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, construct, algebra] ∎
