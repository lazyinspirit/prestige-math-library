## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Fix a field $k$. If $p:T\to X$ is a finite subintegral morphism of separated finite-type $k$-schemes and $F$ is a set-valued sheaf on the cdh site of separated finite-type $k$-schemes, then restriction $F(X)\to F(T)$ is a bijection. In particular this applies to the finite seminormalization map when it belongs to this category.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the field $k$, and $p$ and $F$ as stated, with the small-site conventions in [F4].

**Goal:** Prove bijectivity of restriction.

[F1] Under the Axiom of Choice, the singleton $\{p\}$ is a cdh cover. Choice is inherited from the universal-closedness use in the finite-proper argument of this supplier. [[lem-author-60d95bfb692a-finite-subintegral-cdh-cover]] The Axiom of Choice says that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F2] Every $s\in F(T)$ has equal pullbacks along the two projections $q_1,q_2:T\times_X T\rightrightarrows T$. [[lem-author-60d95bfb692a-subintegral-matching]]

[F3] A set-valued sheaf is a contravariant functor: for composable arrows $a,b$, $(a\circ b)^*=b^*\circ a^*$, and $\operatorname{id}^*=\operatorname{id}$. It has unique amalgamations for covering sieves; restriction to a covering singleton is injective. [[def-scheme-sites-and-sheafification]] [[lem-author-60d95bfb692a-cover-restriction-injective]]

[F4] The cdh site uses a suitably small category equivalent to separated finite-type $k$-schemes, with the indicated fibre products represented in it, and the topology generated by Nisnevich covers and completely decomposed proper morphisms. [[def-cdh-topology-interface]] Subintegral means integral, bijective on points and inducing residue-field isomorphisms; finite means affine with coordinate algebras finite as modules. The seminormalization map is subintegral with seminormal source. Its application here is conditional on that map being finite and belonging to this category. [[def-seminormal-scheme-interface]]

[F5] Scheme fibre products exist. For maps $a,b:V\to T$ with $pa=pb$, their universal property gives a unique $h:V\to T\times_X T$ with $q_1h=a$ and $q_2h=b$. [[math:thm-fibre-products-of-schemes-exist]] [[math:def-fibre-product-schemes-universal-property]] Their representatives belong to the chosen site by [F4].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Glue the matching section along the generated sieve.

1.1 Given $s\in F(T)$, the sieve generated by $p$ consists of all arrows $v:V\to X$ admitting a factorization $v=pa$. Assign to such $v$ the pullback $a^*s$. This is independent of the factorization: if also $v=pb$, [F5] supplies $h:V\to T\times_X T$, and [F2] and functoriality in [F3] give $a^*s=h^*q_1^*s=h^*q_2^*s=b^*s$. For any arrow $w:W\to V$, the factorization $vw=p(aw)$ gives the section $(aw)^*s=w^*(a^*s)$ by [F3]. Thus the assignments form a compatible family. Independence makes the assignment well-defined without selecting a factorization for each arrow. [F2, F3, F5, construct]

2.1 By [F1] and [F3], this matching family has a unique amalgamation in $F(X)$. Its restriction along $p$ is $s$, because the factorization $p=p\operatorname{id}_T$ gives that section by [F3]. Restriction is therefore surjective; [F3] also gives injectivity. Thus it is a bijection. The seminormalization specialization follows from [F4] under its stated finiteness and category qualification. The use of Choice is inherited solely through [F1]. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1] ∎
