## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and fixed compatible site and universe size bounds for the constructions below. Let $k$ be any field. On its qcqs schemes, the actual EM weight-zero map $q_0:K\to\operatorname{gr}^0K$ induces the locally constant Euler rank on $K_0$. Moreover $\Omega^\infty\operatorname{gr}^0K$ is the discrete Nisnevich sheaf $\underline{\mathbb Z}$ of locally constant integer functions. Thus $S=\Omega^\infty\operatorname{Fil}^1K$ is the actual rank-zero fiber of $\Omega^\infty K\to\underline{\mathbb Z}$, and $\pi_0\operatorname{Fil}^1K(X)$ identifies injectively with $\ker(\operatorname{rank}:K_0(X)\to\underline{\mathbb Z}(X))$ for every qcqs $X$.

## Proof

**Given:** Any field $k$, the stated foundational bounds, and qcqs $X/k$ with the supplied actual tower. Qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated, as in [F1].

**Goal:** Identify weight-zero sections, the actual map and the whole first-stage fiber without a smoothness, finite-type or connectedness hypothesis.

1.1 Define $L(V)=\operatorname{LC}(|V|,\mathbb Z)$, with pointwise addition and restriction by composition with the continuous underlying scheme map. This is a presheaf. For any Nisnevich covering family in the site, the map from the topological disjoint union of its sources is surjective, by its residue-field lifting condition, and open by [F2]. For an abstract blowup covering $\{Y\to V,V'\to V\}$, the corresponding disjoint-union map is surjective: $Y$ covers the closed locus and $V'$ is isomorphic over its complement. It is closed because the images of closed subsets in both summands are closed by [F2], and their union is finite. Both maps are quotient maps by [F2]. By reference 1 these fiber products are qcqs and thus lie in the site. The argument also applies after arbitrary base change within the site: openness and closedness are universal, closed immersions stay closed immersions, and joint surjectivity persists because [F3] lifts any point of the new base over an original preimage to a common field-valued point in the fiber product. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F8, construct]

1.2 We calculate the field group needed for the map. On the one-point scheme $\operatorname{Spec}E$, a vector bundle is a finite-dimensional $E$-vector space: a local trivialization is already global. By the finite-basis and dimension inputs in [F6], it is isomorphic to $E^d$ for a unique $d$, and dimension respects every exact-sequence relation. Thus dimension induces $K^0(\operatorname{Spec}E)\to\mathbb Z$, while $n\mapsto n[E]$ is inverse, since direct-sum exact sequences give $[E^d]=d[E]$ on every generator. Compose with the bridge to obtain $K_0(\operatorname{Spec}E)\cong\mathbb Z$, with $[E[0]]$ sent to $1$. For a bounded field complex $P$, the bridge gives $\sum_n(-1)^n\dim_E P^n$. Put $Z^n=\ker d^n$ and $B^n=\operatorname{im}d^{n-1}$. Rank-nullity in the sequences $0\to Z^n\to P^n\to B^{n+1}\to0$ and $0\to B^n\to Z^n\to H^n(P)\to0$ shows that this alternating sum equals $\sum_n(-1)^n\dim_E H^n(P)$, by cancellation of the finitely many boundary terms. The bridge and this identity give the unit and Euler-characteristic normalizations, independent of the representative. [A1, F6, F7, construct, algebra]

2.1 A matching family in $L$ gives a continuous function on the topological disjoint union. Any two points with the same base image have a common field-valued point in their scheme fiber product by [F3]; equality of the two restricted functions there forces equality of the original integer values. The function is therefore constant on each fiber. The quotient universal property in [F2] gives a unique continuous descended function, hence a unique section in $L(V)$. This works after every base change by step 1.1. Matching data on the sieve generated by a family are the same data: restrict to its arrows, and extend to any factoring arrow by pullback; the fiber-product matching condition makes the extension independent of the factorization. Reference 0 now gives a topology for which $L$ is a sheaf containing every specified generating sieve. The cdh topology is the least topology with those generators, so $L$ is a cdh sheaf. In particular it is a Nisnevich sheaf. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 The map from the constant presheaf $P$ to $L$ sends an integer to its constant function. To verify that it is ordinary abelian sheafification, let $P\to M$ be an additive natural map to any cdh abelian sheaf. A locally constant function on a qcqs $V$ has finite image: its open value fibers cover the quasi-compact space. Each fiber is clopen, hence quasi-compact and quasi-separated with its open subscheme structure, so these fibers form a finite Zariski, hence Nisnevich, cover, interpreted on the skeleton through the chosen representatives. On a fiber with value $n$, use the image of $n\in P$ in $M$ and glue. The fibers are disjoint, so compatibility holds; uniqueness of sheaf gluing gives independence of refinements and naturality under pullback. Addition is respected after the common finite refinement by pairs of value fibers. Any extension must agree on these fibers, so it is unique. The empty scheme has its empty cover and exactly one section, the zero element. Thus [F5] identifies $L$ with the ordinary constant integer sheaf. [F1, F5, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Consequently $H^0_{\mathrm{cdh}}(X,\mathbb Z)=L(X)$, and negative cohomology vanishes by [F5]. Interpret the actual weight-zero comparison of [F1] by the coherent realization in [F4], so $\pi_i\operatorname{gr}^0K(X)=H^{-i}_{\mathrm{cdh}}(X,\mathbb Z)$. For any spectrum $E$ and $i\geq0$, the adjunction in [F4] identifies pointed sphere maps $S^i\to\Omega^\infty E$ with maps $\mathbb S[i]\to E$: the left adjoint preserves the suspension pushout, starting with $\Sigma^\infty S^0=\mathbb S$. Taking components gives $\pi_i\Omega^\infty E=\pi_iE$ (components for $i=0$). The tower in [F4] gives $\Omega^\infty E\simeq\Omega\Omega^\infty(E[1])$. Loop multiplication and reversal translate any component to the zero component by a homotopy equivalence, so positive groups vanish at every basepoint when they vanish at zero. Applied to the graded spectrum, all positive groups vanish and its components are naturally $L(X)$. The canonical map to its component set is consequently a weak equivalence by [F4], and hence an equivalence to the discrete space $L(X)$. This is natural in $X$, hence identifies the whole presheaf with the discrete Nisnevich sheaf $L$, on the actual big site. Higher positive cdh cohomology contributes only negative spectrum degrees and does not change this conclusion. [A1, F1, F4, F5, step 3.1]

5.1 For every point $x\in X$, apply naturality of the actual $q_0$ to $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(x)\to X$ from [F3]. Its source and target on $\pi_0$ are both $\mathbb Z$ by steps 1.2, 3.1 and 4.1 applied to the one-point field scheme. The source unit and target constant section $1$ correspond under these identifications; the actual unital comparison in [F1] therefore makes the field map the identity. Pullback of a perfect class is its derived fiber by [F7], whose integer in step 1.2 is its Euler rank. Thus the value at every $x$ of $\pi_0q_0$ is that Euler rank. Functions in $L(X)$ are determined by all their point values, so the actual map is precisely the locally constant Euler-rank homomorphism, even when a perfect complex has no global bundle presentation. The empty scheme gives the same identity into the zero group. [A1, F1, F3, F7, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Finally, by [F4] the square defining the cofiber of $\operatorname{Fil}^1K(X)\to K(X)$ is also a pullback, so its actual cofiber projection is in the fiber sequence $\operatorname{Fil}^1K(X)\to K(X)\to\operatorname{gr}^0K(X)$. The right adjoint $\Omega^\infty$ preserves this pullback: applying its mapping-space adjunction to any pointed test space turns it into the same limit universal property. Hence its first space is the actual homotopy fiber over zero of the map in step 5.1 to the discrete space $L(X)$. A homotopy fiber over a discrete target consists of the source components mapping to zero; the path to zero is unique up to a contractible space of choices. Consequently its component map is injective with image exactly $\ker(K_0(X)\to L(X))$. This proves the stated rank-zero fiber and injective identification naturally on every qcqs $X$. [F4, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
