## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and suitable universes. For a commutative unital ring A and X=Spec A, there is a canonical isomorphism π₀K(X)≅K⁰(X), where K(X) is the nonconnective spectrum of [[def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface]] and K⁰(X) is the vector-bundle Grothendieck group. Under this isomorphism the class of the associated sheaf complex of a bounded complex P of finite projective A-modules maps to $\sum_n(-1)^n[\widetilde P^n]$, and the spectral class of a vector bundle E placed in degree zero maps to [E]. Equivalently, the inverse sends [E] to the class of E[0]. No noetherian or regularity hypothesis is imposed.

A vector bundle is a locally finite free $\mathcal O_X$-module. Its Grothendieck group is generated by isomorphism classes of vector bundles with [E]=[E′]+[E″] for every short exact sequence 0→E′→E→E″→0 of vector bundles. A finite projective A-module is a finitely generated module having the lifting property through surjective module maps; a bounded complex has finitely many nonzero terms. This is a comparison with vector-bundle K zero, not an identification with coherent-sheaf G-theory.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC, suitable universes, a commutative unital ring A, and X=Spec A.

**Goal:** Identify the degree-zero group of the specified nonconnective spectrum with the vector-bundle group and establish the stated formulas.

[A1] Choice is assumed for the essentially small perfect-category and K-theory constructions invoked in [F1], and for the supplied affine quasi-compactness result; it is not inferred from affineness. The suitable universes in the Statement are fixed with the supplied closure convention; their existence is assumed, not deduced from AC. [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention]]

[F1] Under the assumed AC, the affine scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is quasi-compact by [[math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]] and quasi-separated by [[math:thm-affine-schemes-quasi-separated]], hence qcqs. The supplied interface therefore defines $K(X)=\mathbb K(\operatorname{Perf}(X))$, with an essentially small stable idempotent-complete perfect category and invariance under an equivalent small presentation. [[def-nonconnective-k-spectrum-interface]]

[F2] The cofiber Grothendieck group of a small stable infinity-category is canonically the triangle Grothendieck group of its homotopy category. [[lem-author-3a003d7b5208-triangle-presentation]]

[F3] For a small idempotent-complete stable infinity-category, the object-class map identifies its cofiber Grothendieck group with π₀ of the specified nonconnective K-theory. This is an external input with the source and reading limits recorded there. [[thm-author-3a003d7b5208-nonconnective-k-zero-input]]

[F4] The triangle group of Perf(X) identifies with the vector-bundle group; it takes a bounded finite-projective representative to the alternating sum of its associated bundle terms and E[0] to [E]. The exact affine equivalence used there is degreewise associated sheaf. [[lem-author-3a003d7b5208-affine-triangle-euler]], [[def-scheme-perfect-complexes]]


[F5] The commutative unital ring convention is [[math:def-commutative-ring]]. The affine scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ includes its structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X$, as in [[math:def-affine-scheme]]. The associated-sheaf construction $M\mapsto\widetilde M$ is functorial in module maps, and is applied degreewise to complexes. [[math:def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]]

[F6] Modules mean unital left modules; module maps are linear, and finite generation and finite freeness have their supplied algebraic meanings. The lifting-property definition of a finite projective module is given inline in the Statement. [[math:def-left-and-right-modules]], [[math:def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]], [[math:def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]

[F7] A bundle is the finite locally free module sheaf defined inline in the Statement, with module sheaves and their linear morphisms understood as in [[math:def-module-on-ringed-space]]. Short exact sequences use the sheaf exactness convention of [[math:def-exact-sequence-sheaves]]. Complexes use cochain differentials of degree $+1$ whose consecutive composites vanish; $E[0]$ means $E$ in degree zero and zero elsewhere. [[math:def-cochain-complex-in-an-abelian-category]], [[def-scheme-perfect-complexes]]

## Proof
**Proof technique:** Compose the spectral, triangulated and affine Euler comparisons.

1.1 Choose a small stable idempotent-complete presentation C equivalent to Perf(X), available by [F1]. The equivalence identifies the corresponding K-theory spectra and hence their π₀ groups. Apply [F3] to C and invert its object-class isomorphism; then apply [F2] to obtain an isomorphism from π₀K(X) to the triangle Grothendieck group of hPerf(X). The construction is independent of the presentation by the naturality and equivalence invariance in [F1] and [F3]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Compose the isomorphism of step 1.1 with the affine Euler isomorphism of [F4]. This gives π₀K(X)≅K⁰(X). By [F3], the spectral class of an object is sent in step 1.1 to its triangle class. Therefore [F4] sends the class represented by P to $\sum_n(-1)^n[\widetilde P^n]$, and sends the class of E[0] to [E]. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 The classes [E] generate K⁰(X), so the final formula also describes the inverse on its generators. The hypotheses of all invoked suppliers are satisfied by the given commutative unital ring, AC and affine qcqs scheme; none requires noetherianity or regularity. This proves the bridge and both formulas. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1] ∎
