## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed compatible site and universe bounds of the supplied constructions. Let $k$ be a perfect field, let $V$ be a smooth affine $k$-scheme of finite type and pure dimension $d$, and let $x\in V$ be a closed point of codimension $d$. Set $L=\kappa(x)$; this is a finite separable extension of $k$, so $\operatorname{Spec}L$ is smooth of finite type over $k$. Pure dimension $d$ means that every irreducible component has dimension $d$; dimension is the supremum of lengths of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets. For $r\geq0$, write $C_r(T)=\mathbb Z(r)_{\mathrm{mot}}(T)$ for the unshifted Elmanto–Morrow motivic complex, evaluated contravariantly on qcqs $k$-schemes with its supplied coherent restriction maps. Put $C_r(L)=C_r(\operatorname{Spec}L)$ and $C_{r,x}(V)=\operatorname{fib}(C_r(V)\to C_r(V\setminus\{x\}))$ in the supplied stable derived enhancement. Let $z_x^r(V,\bullet)$ be the subcomplex of Bloch's unnormalized cycle complex generated by admissible integral cycles with set-theoretic support on $\{x\}\times_k\Delta_k^\bullet$. All products are over $k$. Homological degree $n$ is placed in cochain degree $-n$ before shifting, with $A[a]^q=A^{q+a}$ and differential $(-1)^a d_A$.

For every $n\geq0$ one has $z_x^j(V,n)=0$ if $0\leq j<d$. If $j\geq d$, the closed immersion $x\times\Delta^n=\Delta_L^n\hookrightarrow V\times\Delta_k^n$ identifies integral generators and gives an isomorphism of chain complexes
$$z^{j-d}(\operatorname{Spec}L,\bullet)\xrightarrow{\sim}z_x^j(V,\bullet).$$
It preserves the actual face-intersection multiplicities and the alternating boundary signs. Consequently
$$C_{j,x}(V)\simeq C_{j-d}(L)[-2d]\quad(j\geq d),\qquad C_{j,x}(V)\simeq0\quad(0\leq j<d),$$
using the actual smooth comparisons on both sides. At $j=d$, the positive cycle $[x]$ corresponds to $1\in z^0(L,0)$, with no residue-field degree factor.

## Facts & Assumptions
[A1] The assumed [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]] and fixed compatible site and universe bounds discharge the inherited premises of the neighbourhood and actual-support comparison in [F2] at the annotated applications below. Concretely, use the universes of [[math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention]] with $\mathcal U\in\mathcal V$ and $\omega\in\mathcal U$, containing the scheme and site data, and larger bounds for the categories as in the actual-support supplier. Their existence is separately assumed. The chain, dimension and formal fiber computations introduce no additional choice principle.

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the fixed compatible site and universe bounds, a perfect field $k$, a smooth affine finite-type $k$-scheme $V$ of pure dimension $d$, a closed point $x\in V$ of codimension $d$, its residue field $L=\kappa(x)$, the supported-cycle and unshifted coefficient conventions in the Statement, and an integer $j\geq0$.

**Goal:** Prove the point-cycle calculation, including the complete low-weight range and coefficients of the boundary.

[F0] Perfectness is that of [[math:def-perfect-field]]. ## Proof
1.1 Write $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and let $\mathfrak m$ be the maximal ideal corresponding to $x$; then $A/\mathfrak m=L$ by [F0]. By [F3], $V\times\Delta^n$ has ring $R_n=A[t_0,\ldots,t_n]/(\sum t_a-1)$ and its closed point-support subscheme has quotient $R_n/\mathfrak m R_n=L[t_0,\ldots,t_n]/(\sum t_a-1)$. An integral closed cycle has quotient $R_n/P$ with $P$ prime by [F1] and [F3]. Its set-theoretic support lies in this point-support subscheme exactly when its generic point $P$ contains $\mathfrak m R_n$. The quotient-prime correspondence therefore identifies these cycles with the integral closed subschemes of $\Delta_L^n$, with the very same quotient rings and reduced structures. Every such cycle has dimension at most $n$, hence ambient codimension at least $d$ by [F2]. Thus there are no generators in weights $j<d$. For $j\geq d$, a cycle of dimension $e$ has codimensions $d+n-e$ and $n-e$ in the two ambients, differing by exactly $d$. [A1, F0, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Consider a coordinate face of simplex dimension $m$. For every nonempty irreducible component $W$ of its intersection with a cycle, [F2] gives codimensions $d+m-\dim W$ in $V\times\Delta^m$ and $m-\dim W$ in $\Delta_L^m$. Thus the exact admissibility requirement of [F1], codimension $j$ in the ambient face, is equivalent to codimension $j-d$ in the residue-field face; empty intersections agree as well. Hence the cycles of step 1.1 are admissible on one side exactly when they are on the other. For a codimension-one face, [F3] uses the same coordinate equation on the identical quotient ring of the integral cycle. Consequently the face intersections have identical local rings and the same generic quotient-local lengths in [F1]. The coordinate order and alternating signs are unchanged. Extending this generator bijection linearly in each degree gives inverse group homomorphisms, and equality of the face coefficients makes them commute with the chain differential. This proves the asserted chain isomorphism, before passing to any rational-equivalence quotient. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Under [A1], [F4] and [F2] identify the actual support complex with $z_x^j[-2j]$. For $j\geq d$, apply step 2.1 and the cochain shift identity $[-2j]=[-2(j-d)][-2d]$. By [F2], $\operatorname{Spec}L$ is a smooth affine finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension zero; its affine structure morphism is separated by [F0]. The actual-support source comparison therefore applies to its unique closed point and empty open complement in weight $q=j-d\geq0$. It identifies $z^{j-d}(\operatorname{Spec}L,\bullet)[-2(j-d)]$ with the specified $C_{j-d}(L)$ of [F4]. This proves the shifted derived equivalence. If $0\leq j<d$, step 1.1 gives the zero cycle complex in every degree and hence the zero actual fiber. In degree zero and $j=d$, the same generator is $\operatorname{Spec}L$ itself, so its coefficient is $+1$, not $[L:k]$; no pushforward to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ has occurred. [A1, F0, F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
