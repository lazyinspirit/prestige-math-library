## Statement
Let $F=\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$. Choose universes large enough to contain the schemes in use, and a suitably small skeleton $\mathcal C_F$ of the qcqs $F$-schemes in that universe. On $\mathcal C_F$ use the topology generated by Nisnevich covers and families $\{Y\to X,X'\to X\}$ from cartesian squares with $Y\to X$ a finitely presented closed immersion, $X'\to X$ finitely presented and proper, and $X'\setminus(X'\times_XY)\cong X\setminus Y$. This is the precise general cdh site used here. No separated finite-type hypothesis over $F$ or $k$ is imposed. Its evaluation object may be any qcqs $k$-scheme, viewed over its prime field $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The hypotheses in the statement.

**Goal:** Establish the displayed construction or conclusion with its actual maps.

[F1] The universe and generated-topology constructions are those of [[def-scheme-sites-and-sheafification]]. EM §3.3 p.14 specifies the displayed generators.

## Proof
1.1 A topology with these generating sieves exists: intersect all Grothendieck topologies containing them. The topology axioms survive intersection, and the maximal topology belongs to the collection. This constructs the least such topology on the chosen small category. [F1, construct]

2.1 These are exactly the square and covering conventions specified in EM §3.3 p.14: in particular both finiteness conditions on the square are retained. A qcqs $k$-scheme is an object after composing its structure morphism with $\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}F$; quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness here are properties of the underlying scheme. Thus the site supplies every evaluation object required. The previously completed separated finite-type site is a different specified site and is left unchanged; no equivalence between their cohomologies is asserted. [given, step 1.1] ∎
