## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $F=\mathbb Q$ or $F=\mathbb F_p$ for a prime $p$. Fix Grothendieck universes $U\in V$ with $\omega\in U$ and $F\in U$, large enough for the scheme data under consideration. Let $\mathcal D_F$ be the category of all qcqs $F$-schemes whose defining data lie in $U$, regarded as a $V$-small category, and choose a full skeleton $\mathcal C_F$. Here qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated. For every $T\in\mathcal D_F$ choose its representative $rT\in\mathcal C_F$ and an $F$-isomorphism $\epsilon_T:rT\to T$, taking $rT=T$ and $\epsilon_T=\mathrm{id}_T$ on $\mathcal C_F$.

Here $V$-small means that the object set and the set of all morphisms belong to $V$. On this $V$-small category use the following site conventions. A sieve $S$ on $T$ is a set of arrows into $T$ closed under precomposition. For $f:T'\to T$, its pullback sieve is $f^*S=\{g:Z\to T'\mid fg\in S\}$. A Grothendieck topology assigns to each $T$ a collection $J(T)$ of covering sieves, subject to these axioms: the maximal sieve of all arrows into $T$ covers; if $S\in J(T)$, then $f^*S\in J(T')$ for every $f:T'\to T$; and if $S\in J(T)$ and $R$ is a sieve on $T$ for which $f^*R\in J(T')$ for every $f:T'\to T$ in $S$, then $R\in J(T)$. A family of arrows into $T$ generates the sieve of all arrows factoring through a member of that family. The topology generated by specified families is the least topology containing those generated sieves. A site here is this category equipped with such a topology. A set-valued presheaf $P$ means a contravariant functor from $\mathcal C_F$ to $\mathrm{Set}_V$, the category of sets belonging to $V$ and functions between them; thus it assigns restriction functions $P(f):P(T)\to P(T')$ respecting identities and reversed composition. These definitions apply directly at size $V$ and assert no sheafification or derived-localization result.

On $\mathcal C_F$ use the Grothendieck topology generated by its Nisnevich covering families and families $\{Y\to X,X'\to X\}$ from cartesian squares of its objects, with fourth vertex $Y'=X'\times_XY$, such that $Y\to X$ is a finitely presented closed immersion, $X'\to X$ is finitely presented and proper, and the induced map $X'\setminus Y'\to X\setminus Y$ is an isomorphism. Complements mean the open subschemes with their restricted structure sheaves.

Here a morphism is finitely presented if it is quasi-compact, quasi-separated and locally of finite presentation. The last condition means that over every affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A$ of the target there is an affine open cover of its inverse image by $\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B\cong A[t_1,\ldots,t_n]/(g_1,\ldots,g_m)$ as an $A$-algebra for finite $n,m$.

This gives the precise general qcqs cdh site used here. No separated finite-type hypothesis over $F$ or $k$ is imposed. Let $k\in U$ be any field with a specified identification of its prime subfield with $F$, and let $X/k$ be any qcqs scheme within the chosen size bound. Write $X_F$ for $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}F$. Evaluation of a presheaf $P$ on $\mathcal C_F$ at this $X$ means $P(rX_F)$. On an $F$-morphism $f:T\to S$ use $r(f)=\epsilon_S^{-1}f\epsilon_T$; thus the convention transports evaluation functorially to all the stated schemes, without treating them as literal objects of the skeleton. No equivalence with the cohomology of a separated finite-type site is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The universe bounds, Choice, schemes, morphisms and generating families in the statement.

**Goal:** Construct the least topology with these generators and give the representative-based evaluation convention.

[F1] Relative smallness is defined inline using [[math:def-small-locally-small-and-large-category]] and [[math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention]]. The sieve, topology, family-generated sieve and set-valued presheaf definitions are the inline conventions in the Statement; contravariant functors and precomposition use [[math:def-functor-and-contravariant-functor]]. Skeletons are full subcategories containing exactly one representative of every isomorphism class by [[math:def-skeletal-category-and-skeleton]]. Under [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]], [[math:prop-every-small-category-has-a-skeleton-under-choice]] supplies a skeleton; Choice also selects the set-indexed isomorphisms $\epsilon_T$. Natural transformations have the meaning of [[math:def-natural-transformation]]; a natural isomorphism means one whose components are isomorphisms.

[F2] The qcqs condition is [[math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme]]. We use only the pointwise definition of a Nisnevich covering family in the first paragraph of [[def-nisnevich-covering-interface]], on the specified category; the topology and presheaf sizes are the inline ones above. Schemes and morphisms over $\operatorname{Spec}F$ or $\operatorname{Spec}k$ are those of [[math:def-scheme-over-base]]. The specified prime subfield of $k$ is [[math:def-prime-subfield]], and its inclusion $F\hookrightarrow k$ induces $\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}F$ by [[math:thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]].

[F3] The square conditions use [[math:def-closed-immersion-schemes]], [[math:def-proper-morphism]] and [[math:def-fibre-product-schemes-universal-property]]. Open complements and affine open charts carry their restricted structure sheaves as in [[math:def-affine-open-subscheme]]. The generators are cartesian squares of the stated objects; no existence claim for an arbitrary fibre product in this skeleton is needed.

[F4] The imported naming and generator convention is recorded in source reference 0: [Elmanto–Morrow, §3.3, printed p. 14, (3.4) and the following paragraph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.08463v1). Its prime-field and finiteness hypotheses match those stated here.

## Proof
1.1 The encoded object and morphism data lie in $U$; their collections are subsets of fixed finite products of $U$, and hence belong to $V$ by power-set and finite-product closure, since $U\in V$. This establishes the stated $V$-smallness. Apply the supplied skeleton proposition to $\mathcal D_F$, and use Choice to select the set-indexed isomorphisms $\epsilon_T$, with the stated normalization on the skeleton. The skeleton's object set $O$ and total arrow set $M$ belong to $V$. Each sieve is a subset of $M$, so all pairs $(T,S)$ consisting of an object and a sieve form a subset of $O\times\mathcal P(M)$ in $V$. Candidate topologies are subsets of this set, and their collection is again a set in $V$ by power-set closure. Thus we may intersect all Grothendieck topologies containing the specified generating sieves. This collection is nonempty because it contains the topology in which every sieve covers. The maximal sieve covers in the intersection, and pullback stability holds in each topology. For transitivity, a sieve covering in the intersection covers in each topology, and if every pullback of another sieve along its arrows covers in the intersection, those pullbacks cover in each topology; transitivity there makes the other sieve cover in every topology. The intersection therefore satisfies the inline topology axioms and is the least topology containing the generators. [F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Replacing vertices and arrows by isomorphic representatives preserves the cartesian universal property and the stated covering and morphism conditions. By the recorded source convention, these generators therefore specify the source cdh topology on the skeleton, with both finite-presentation conditions retained. For $X/k$ as stated, composing the structure morphisms produces $X_F\in\mathcal D_F$: the underlying scheme is unchanged, so it remains qcqs, and the composite stays within the universe bound. Its representative is $rX_F$, related to it by $\epsilon_{X_F}$. For composable $f:T\to S$ and $g:S\to R$, cancellation gives $r(g)r(f)=\epsilon_R^{-1}gf\epsilon_T=r(gf)$ and $r(\mathrm{id}_T)=\mathrm{id}_{rT}$. These are $F$-morphisms in the full skeleton, so $r$ is a functor and precomposition evaluates $P$ at $P(rX_F)$ with the stated restriction maps. Changing the chosen isomorphisms for the fixed skeleton gives representative functors $r,r'$ with comparison components $\alpha_T=\epsilon_T'^{-1}\epsilon_T:rT\to r'T$. Direct cancellation gives $r'(f)\alpha_T=\alpha_Sr(f)$ for $f:T\to S$; each component is an isomorphism, so this is a natural isomorphism. This supplies every evaluation object in the claimed scope. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1] ∎
