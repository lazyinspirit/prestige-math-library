## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $C$ be an integral Noetherian scheme of dimension one, with generic point $\eta$ and function field $k(\eta)=\mathcal O_{C,\eta}$. The generic-rank homomorphism
$$\operatorname{rk}_\eta:G_0(C)\longrightarrow K_0(k(\eta))\cong\mathbb Z$$
sends the class of a coherent sheaf $F$ to $\dim_{k(\eta)}F_\eta$. Its kernel is generated by classes of coherent sheaves supported at closed points. In fact it is generated by the residue-field sheaves $\kappa(x)$ at closed points $x$.

For every nonempty open subscheme $V\subseteq C$, restriction defines a directed system as opens shrink, and the natural homomorphism
$$\varinjlim_{\eta\in V\subseteq C}G_0(V)\longrightarrow K_0(k(\eta))\cong\mathbb Z$$
is an isomorphism. Thus the displayed kernel is the kernel of localization at the generic point, computed as this filtered limit.

For the following additional supported comparison, fix the compatible site and universe bounds of the supplied perfect-$K$-theory construction. If $i:C\hookrightarrow U$ is a closed immersion into a regular Noetherian finite-dimensional scheme, the supported comparison $K_0^{|C|}(U)\cong G_0(C)$ identifies generic rank with the alternating generic length of a supported perfect complex $E$:
$$[E]\longmapsto\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{U,\eta}}H^n(E)_\eta.$$
The sum and each length are finite. No regularity or smoothness of $C$ is assumed.

For a field $L$, $K_0(L)$ is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of finite-dimensional $L$-vector spaces, with the short-exact-sequence relations. Here $G_0(X)$ is the abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of coherent $\mathcal O_X$-modules, with relation $[F]=[F']+[F'']$ for every short exact sequence $0\to F'\to F\to F''\to0$. An integral scheme is nonempty, reduced and irreducible; its generic point has dense closure and its local ring there is a field. A specialization of $y$ is a point of $\overline{\{y\}}$; dimension is the supremum of lengths of chains of strict specializations. Write $\kappa(x)=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x$ for the residue field at a point of a scheme $X$; at a closed point the sheaf also denoted $\kappa(x)$ is its pushforward along the closed immersion of the point. A sheaf is supported at $x$ if its stalks vanish away from $x$. Length is the number of simple factors in a finite composition series, independent of the series. A perfect complex is locally quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite locally free sheaves, and support in $|C|$ means that its cohomology sheaves vanish away from $C$. We use the supplied supported perfect category and its specified $K_0$ comparison; regularity of $U$ means regularity of every local ring. The colimit is in abelian groups, indexed by the set of nonempty opens, with one arrow $V\to W$ when $W\subseteq V$.

## Facts & Assumptions
[A1] The assumed [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]] discharges the coherent dévissage, coherence, affine, associated-sheaf and coherent-group premises in [F1]–[F2], [F7]–[F8] and [F10]. For step 6.1, the additional fixed site and universe bounds discharge the selected supported perfect-$K$ comparison in [F3]. No such supported-model premise is needed for the preceding coherent generic-rank and filtered-colimit assertions. The group constructions in [F5] and the finite basis and filtration arguments add no further choice or universe assumption.

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and $C,\eta$ as in the Statement. For the final supported assertion, also fix the stated closed immersion, a supported perfect complex $E$, and the compatible site and universe bounds of that comparison.

**Goal:** Compute generic localization and its filtered limit, and identify it under supported dévissage with alternating generic length.

[F1] The coherent one-generic-generator dévissage theorem says that on a Noetherian scheme a property of coherent sheaves holds for all coherent sheaves if it holds for zero, is two-out-of-three in coherent short exact sequences, and has a coherent witness of generic length one supported on each integral closed subscheme. It assumes AC. [[math:lem-coherent-devissage-one-generic-generator]]

[F2] Coherent sheaves on a Noetherian scheme form an abelian category; kernels, cokernels and finite direct sums are coherent, and coherence is local. The residue-field supplier establishes coherence on a Noetherian affine neighbourhood of a closed point. Step 2.1 extends this to the global point sheaf by the local criterion. [[math:thm-coherent-sheaves-abelian-noetherian-scheme]] [[math:ex-skyscraper-coherent-closed-point]]

[F3] For a regular Noetherian finite-dimensional ambient scheme and any closed subscheme, supported perfect-complex $K_0$ is identified with the coherent $G_0$ of the closed subscheme by the coherent-cohomology Euler class followed by the finite filtration by powers of the defining ideal. [[prop-supported-k-zero-devissage]]

[F4] A module sheaf is a sheaf of abelian groups with compatible module structures on sections. [[math:def-module-on-ringed-space]] Exactness means equality of image and kernel sheaves, also for module sheaves. [[math:def-exact-sequence-sheaves]] Exactness can be tested on every stalk, by applying the abelian-sheaf theorem to the underlying sheaves of abelian groups. [[math:thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]]

[F5] A small category is filtered if it is nonempty, any two objects map to a common object and parallel arrows can be equalized. A colimit has compatible canonical maps and the universal property that every compatible family of maps to a fixed target factors uniquely through it. [[math:def-filtered-category-and-filtered-colimit]] [[math:def-limit-and-colimit-of-a-diagram]] Abelian groups are exactly $\mathbb Z$-modules, including their homomorphisms, subgroups and quotients. [[math:prop-abelian-groups-are-z-modules]] The direct sum consists of finitely supported families with coordinatewise operations and canonical summand inclusions. [[math:def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]] A module map killing a submodule factors uniquely through its quotient. [[math:thm-quotient-module-universal-property]] Step 4.1 constructs the needed colimit from these inputs rather than assuming its existence.

[F6] A composition series is a finite strictly increasing submodule chain from zero to the module, with simple factors; length is its number of factors and is independent of the series. [[math:def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module]] In a short exact sequence of modules, the middle term has finite length if and only if both outer terms do, and then the lengths add. [[math:cor-length-is-additive-in-short-exact-sequences]]

[F7] A ring is Noetherian when every ideal is finitely generated; ascending ideal chains then stabilize, since their union is an ideal whose finite generating set is contained in one term. [[math:def-noetherian-ring-and-module]] Noetherian for a scheme means locally covered by spectra of Noetherian rings and quasi-compact; an integral scheme has domain coordinate rings on all nonempty affine opens. [[math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]] [[math:def-integral-scheme]] In a spectrum, $V(J)$ is the set of primes containing the ideal $J$. [[math:def-prime-spectrum-and-vanishing-sets]] Topological Noetherianity is the descending chain condition on closed subsets, and under AC every subspace of a Noetherian space is Noetherian and quasi-compact. [[math:def-noetherian-topological-space]] [[math:lem-noetherian-subspaces-and-compact-opens]] Coherence is the local finite-type quasi-coherent condition with finite-type relation kernels; in particular restrictions are coherent. [[math:def-coherent-module-scheme]] Finite type means locally associated to a finitely generated module; equivalently under AC there are local surjections from finite free module sheaves. [[math:def-finite-type-finite-presentation-module-sheaf]] Closed immersion means a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and a surjection on structure sheaves; its stalk quotient describes the closed subscheme's local ring. [[math:def-closed-immersion-schemes]] The residue field at a prime is the fraction field of its domain quotient. [[math:def-residue-field-scheme-point]] Regularity is regularity of each local ring. [[math:def-regular-local-ring-geometric-point]] Distinguished opens form a basis of an affine spectrum, each is the spectrum of a principal localization, and localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian. [[math:def-affine-scheme-spectrum]] [[math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion]] [[math:thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]] The perfect objects, their cohomology and support are those of the fixed supplied scheme-derived category. [[def-scheme-perfect-complexes]]

[F8] The open subscheme on an open subset has the restricted structure sheaf. [[math:def-affine-open-subscheme]] Restriction of a sheaf to an open is evaluation on its open subsets. [[math:def-restriction-sheaf-open-subspace]] Its stalk consists of germs of sections on neighbourhoods, with two representatives identified when they agree on a smaller neighbourhood. [[math:def-stalk-of-presheaf]] Thus restriction leaves each stalk at a point of the open unchanged.

[F9] Finite-dimensional vector spaces over a fixed field are classified up to linear isomorphism by dimension. Thus a space of dimension $d$ is a direct sum of $d$ copies of that field. [[math:cor-finite-dimensional-vector-spaces-are-isomorphic-iff-equal-dimension]]

[F10] Under AC the coherent category of a locally Noetherian scheme is essentially small and abelian, so its isomorphism-class generators and short-exact-sequence relations define an abelian group. We denote this coherent group by $G_0(X)$; the supplier denotes it by $K_0(X)$. This notation does not identify it with the perfect-complex group on a singular scheme. ## Proof
**Proof technique:** Apply coherent dévissage using only points distinct from the generic point, then localize on shrinking opens and compute supported ideal-filtration lengths.

1.1 First the underlying space of $C$ is Noetherian: on a Noetherian affine chart, write a descending chain of closed subsets as $Z_j=V(I_j)$ and put $J_j=I_0+\cdots+I_j$. Then $V(J_j)=Z_0\cap\cdots\cap Z_j=Z_j$, while the ascending ideal chain $J_j$ stabilizes by [F7]. A finite affine cover gives a common stabilization index for a chain on $C$. By [F7], an open is therefore quasi-compact. Around any of its points, choose inside a Noetherian affine chart a distinguished open contained in that open; its ring is a Noetherian localization by [F7]. Thus the open is also locally Noetherian and is a Noetherian scheme. Every nonempty open $V\subseteq C$ is integral and Noetherian, has dimension at most one, and contains $\eta$. Every point $x\neq\eta$ of $V$ is closed in $V$: a further proper specialization would give a chain of length at least two starting at $\eta$. Consequently, for any integral closed subscheme $Z$ of $V$ with generic point $\xi$, either $\xi=\eta$ and $|Z|=|V|$, or $\xi\neq\eta$ and $|Z|=\{\xi\}$ is a closed point. Only these support descriptions are needed for the witnesses below. The possibility that $V=\{\eta\}$ is open is allowed; then an affine neighbourhood of its only point is all of $V$, and its ring of sections is the unique stalk $k(\eta)$, so $V=\operatorname{Spec}k(\eta)$ and there are no points $x\neq\eta$ in $V$. On any nonempty affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $C$, the generic point is the zero prime and [F7] identifies its local ring with $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. [F7, given]

2.1 By [F10], $G_0(V)$ is a well-defined abelian group with the stated presentation. Let $H_V\subseteq G_0(V)$ be generated by $[\mathcal O_V]$ and $[\kappa(x)]$ for $x\in V\setminus\{\eta\}$. These point sheaves are coherent: on an affine neighbourhood $W=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $x$, the global closed-point direct image restricts to $\widetilde{A/\mathfrak m_x}$, which is coherent by [F2]; on $V\setminus\{x\}$ it is zero. These two opens cover $V$, so locality of coherence gives the global assertion. Apply [F1] under [A1] to the property $P(F):[F]\in H_V$. It holds for zero and is two-out-of-three by the exact relations in $G_0(V)$. For any integral closed subscheme with generic point $\eta$, use $\mathcal O_V$: it is coherent by [F2], its support is contained in that subscheme by step 1.1, and its generic stalk $k(\eta)$ is annihilated by the zero maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{V,\eta}$. For an integral closed subscheme with generic point $x\neq\eta$, use $\kappa(x)$: its support is the singleton underlying that subscheme, its stalk at $x$ is one-dimensional over the residue field and it is annihilated by the maximal ideal. All witness hypotheses hold, so $H_V=G_0(V)$, including when $V=\{\eta\}$. [A1, F1, F2, F7, F8, F10, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Taking stalks at $\eta$ is exact by [F4]. A coherent sheaf locally admits a surjection from a finite free module sheaf by [F7]. Taking its stalk using [F4] gives a finite generating family over the field $k(\eta)$ identified in step 1.1, so the generic stalk is finite-dimensional. For a short exact sequence of finite-dimensional vector spaces, a basis of the subspace together with lifts of a basis of the quotient is a basis of the middle space: projecting a linear relation proves independence, and subtracting lifts proves spanning. Dimension is therefore additive and defines the generic-rank homomorphism. It sends $[\mathcal O_V]$ to $1$ and each $[\kappa(x)]$ with $x\neq\eta$ to zero. By step 2.1, an element of its kernel is an integral linear combination of precisely these point classes. Conversely all such point classes have generic rank zero. For $V=C$, the generic point is not closed because $\dim C=1$, so these are exactly the closed-point classes in the Statement. Coherent sheaves supported at closed points have generic rank zero, and each residue sheaf is such a sheaf. For the inline definition of $K_0(L)$, the same basis argument makes dimension a homomorphism. By [F9] and the split exact sequences for finite direct sums, $[W]=(\dim_LW)[L]$, including $[0]=0$. Hence $m\mapsto m[L]$ is inverse to dimension on all virtual classes; apply this with $L=k(\eta)$ for the stated normalization. [A1, F2, F4, F7, F9, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The nonempty opens form a small filtered poset under reverse inclusion: they are a set of subsets of $C$, every finite intersection contains $\eta$, and parallel arrows in a poset coincide. Restriction preserves coherence by [F7], is exact by [F4] and [F8], and preserves the generic stalk and structure-sheaf class, so it gives the asserted diagram of groups. Construct its colimit as follows, using [F5]: put $S=\bigoplus_VG_0(V)$, with summand maps $j_V$, and let $N$ be the subgroup generated by all $j_V(a)-j_W(a|_W)$ for $W\subseteq V$. Explicitly $N$ consists of finite integral sums of these differences. Set $Q=S/N$ and let $q_V:G_0(V)\to Q$ be the induced maps. For any compatible family $f_V:G_0(V)\to B$, the formula $s\mapsto\sum_V f_V(s_V)$ on finitely supported families is additive, kills $N$ and factors uniquely through $Q$. Conversely a map out of $Q$ gives such a family. This proves the colimit universal property and its existence. Its canonical images generate $Q$, because elements of $S$ have finite support. The generic-rank maps form a compatible family, hence induce a well-defined map $\rho:Q\to\mathbb Z$. [F4, F5, F7, F8, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 For $x\in V\setminus\{\eta\}$, the open $V\setminus\{x\}$ still contains $\eta$ and kills the residue-sheaf class. Each class in step 2.1 involves only finitely many such points, so one nonempty open removes them all. Thus the canonical image of every element of $G_0(V)$ is an integer multiple of $t=q_V([\mathcal O_V])$. This $t$ is independent of $V$, by restriction from $C$. The canonical images generate $Q$ by step 4.1, so $Q$ is generated by $t$, and $\rho(t)=1$ by step 3.1. The homomorphism $\mathbb Z\to Q$, $m\mapsto mt$, is consequently inverse to $\rho$, proving the colimit isomorphism. If $V=\{\eta\}$, its only generator is $[\mathcal O_V]$; the generic point is never treated as a removable point class. Composing $\rho$ with the inverse dimension isomorphism $\mathbb Z\to K_0(k(\eta))$ identifies $Q$ with the displayed field group. The localization map is $q_C$ followed by this isomorphism; its composition with field dimension is the rank map from step 3.1, so it has exactly the stated kernel. [F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Under the additional bounds in [A1], apply [F3] and let $\mathcal I$ define $C$ in $U$. The image of $[E]$ in $G_0(C)$ is the finite sum of the classes of $\mathcal I^rH^n(E)/\mathcal I^{r+1}H^n(E)$ with signs $(-1)^n$. Put $R=\mathcal O_{U,\eta}$ and $\mathfrak m=\mathcal I_\eta$. The closed immersion and [F4] identify $R/\mathfrak m$ with $\mathcal O_{C,\eta}=k(\eta)$, so $\mathfrak m$ is the maximal ideal of $R$. Each stalk quotient in this filtration is finite-dimensional over this residue field, by coherence on $C$ and the finite-type argument in step 3.1. By [F4], taking stalks preserves each short exact sequence in the finite ideal-power filtration. A finite-dimensional vector space over $k(\eta)$ has length equal to its dimension as an $\mathcal O_{U,\eta}$-module: a basis flag has one-dimensional factors, each simple since the action factors through this residue field. Starting with the zero terminal filtration term, apply [F6] to each short exact filtration sequence. This proves that $H^n(E)_\eta$ has finite length and that its length is the sum of these quotient dimensions. Only finitely many $n$ contribute. Applying generic rank gives exactly the alternating generic-length formula, without requiring regularity or smoothness of $C$. [A1, F3, F4, F6, F7, step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
