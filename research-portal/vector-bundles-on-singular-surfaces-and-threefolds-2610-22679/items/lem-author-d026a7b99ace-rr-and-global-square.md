## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed site and universe bounds of the motivic and support constructions. Let $k$ be algebraically closed of arbitrary characteristic, let $X$ be a finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three, and let $x$ be a smooth closed point, with closed immersion $i:\operatorname{Spec}k(x)\hookrightarrow X$. Write $u_x=[i_*k(x)]$ for the positive supported residue class and $\delta_x$ for the positive fundamental point-cycle class. For this pair, $c_{3,x}(nu_x)=2n\delta_x$ and $\epsilon_{3,x}c_{3,x}(nu_x)=2nu_x$ for every $n\in\mathbb Z$. If, in addition, $X$ is normal and affine, write $\iota_K:K_{0,x}(X)\to K_0(X)$ and $\iota_H:H^{6,3}_x(X,\mathbb Z)\to H^{6,3}_{\mathrm{mot}}(X,\mathbb Z)$ for forgetting support. For every $z\in K_{0,x}(X)$ and $b\in H^{6,3}_x(X,\mathbb Z)$, both squares with support-to-global commute:
$$c_3(\iota_Kz)=\iota_Hc_{3,x}(z),\qquad \epsilon_3(\iota_Hb)=\iota_K\epsilon_{3,x}(b),$$
where the second equality is viewed in $K_0(X)$ via the image-filtration inclusion. Thus the global composite equals twice the residue-sheaf class on every such point generator, whether or not support-to-global is injective. The same supported formulas and global equalities hold for a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme $X$ of dimension three with lower-dimensional components, provided $x$ lies on a three-dimensional component: compute on the pure clopen union $X_3$ of those components and extend the point class by zero.

## Proof
1.1 By [F6], $X$ and its punctured open are qcqs and $X$ is Noetherian. By [F8], the algebraically closed field is perfect in both supplier conventions, and the neighbourhood lemma applies with $d=3$ under [F0] and [F4]'s size and Choice assumptions. Choose its smooth affine pure-three-dimensional finite-type neighbourhood $V$. The same [F6] argument makes $V$ and the finite-type point scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k(x)$ Noetherian; their dimensions are three and zero. The neighbourhood lemma gives codimension three, and the Koszul lemma in [F8] makes the point immersion regular with the actual perfect residue object. Apply [F1]'s local point calculation to this $V,x$ to obtain $c_{3,x}(nu_x)=2n\delta_x$ for every integer $n$. The Chern support excision in [F2] and coordinate identifications in [F1] transport this equality to $X$, with the same positive generators. Since the supported edge in [F2] is a homomorphism taking $\delta_x$ to $u_x$, it follows that $\epsilon_{3,x}c_{3,x}(nu_x)=2nu_x$. Since [F1] also makes every supported K-zero class an integer multiple of $u_x$, this proves additivity of this supported $c_3$, without asserting general additivity of higher Chern operations or comparing independently constructed RR operations. [F0, F1, F2, F4, F6, F8]

1.2 The first global square is the one constructed in [F2]. For the edge square, use [F7]: the support-to-global maps of $F^3K$, its graded cofiber, and $K$ commute with the actual projection and augmentation. Since both supported and global $p_3$ are isomorphisms, their inverse homomorphisms commute on $\pi_0$ by the inverse-square identity in [F7]. Compose with stage augmentation to get precisely the edge equality of the statement. In particular $\iota_Ku_x=[i_*k(x)]$ lies in the third image filtration. [F2, F3, F6, F7]

1.3 For the nonpure case, [F4] gives $X=X_3\amalg Y$ with $x\in X_3$. Put $U=X\setminus\{x\}$ and $U_3=X_3\setminus\{x\}$, so $U=U_3\amalg Y$. By [F6] these schemes and opens are qcqs, even though $U_3$ need not be affine. The current clopen-filtered-products supplier in [F4] applies to every qcqs field-scheme and finite clopen partition, so applies directly to $U=U_3\amalg Y$, including an empty $Y$. It gives actual restriction equivalences $F^jK(U)\simeq F^jK(U_3)\times F^jK(Y)$ for all $j\geq0$, with their transitions and augmentations. At stage zero these give $K(U)\simeq K(U_3)\times K(Y)$. The derived sheaf and clopen descent in [F5] give the corresponding product of motivic complexes in each nonnegative weight, including zero on the empty scheme. The exact zero extension of the residue object is the one supplied in [F4]; no new perfect-category construction is assumed on the punctured open. [F4, F5, F6]

2.1 Combine step 1.3 with the same restriction products on $X$ from [F4]. They identify restriction from $X$ to $U$ with the product of restriction from $X_3$ to $U_3$ and the identity on $Y$, compatibly at every stage and at stage zero. By [F7], taking fibers gives the product of the first fiber and the fiber of that identity, which is zero. Thus actual restriction identifies the supported augmented towers at $x$ on $X$ and $X_3$, preserving projection and augmentation. The motivic restriction products of step 1.3 give the same support identification: their coherent spectral realization is the one fixed in [F7], and its fibers also preserve these finite products. This proves the support identifications on the possibly nonaffine open, with no induction across untyped derived and spectral products. [F4, F5, F7, step 1.3]

3.1 These restrictions preserve the residue objects and fundamental point classes. Apply steps 1.1–1.2 on the pure normal affine threefold $X_3$. The supported Chern operations and their support-to-global squares transport along step 2.1 by [F2]. Globally, the Chern operations are pointed and natural for restriction, so a zero-extended point class has zero Chern coordinate on $Y$. For the edge, the global $p_3$ maps on $X$ and $X_3$ are isomorphisms by [F3]. Given a motivic class with coordinates $(b_3,0)$, the stage class with coordinates $(p_{3,X_3}^{-1}b_3,0)$ maps to it by naturality of the stage projections and the product decompositions. Uniqueness of the inverse of $p_{3,X}$ identifies this as its inverse image, using the unique inverse homomorphisms in [F7]; no isomorphism assertion on $Y$ is needed. Augmentation also preserves these coordinates. Thus both global squares and the supported identities extend from $X_3$ to $X$ exactly as stated. [F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Apply the two squares to $z=nu_x$ and its supported Chern image. In the pure case steps 1.1–1.2, and in the nonpure case step 3.1, give $\epsilon_3c_3(n[i_*k(x)])=\iota_K\epsilon_{3,x}c_{3,x}(nu_x)=2n[i_*k(x)]$. This applies a support-forgetting map to a supported equality and never cancels one, so it remains valid when the global point class vanishes. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1] ∎
