## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field and let $X$ be a scheme of finite type over $k$. Every open subset of $X$ is quasi-compact, meaning that every open cover of that subset has a finite subcover. In particular, $X$ is quasi-compact and quasi-separated: intersections of affine open subschemes are quasi-compact. The empty scheme is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a field $k$, and a $k$-scheme $X$, meaning a scheme equipped with its structure morphism $f:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$; assume $f$ is of finite type. [[math:def-scheme-over-base]], [[math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]]

**Goal:** Prove quasi-compactness of every open subset and the stated qcqs consequence.

[F1] A field has an inverse for every nonzero element. A commutative ring is Noetherian if every ideal is finitely generated. [[math:def-field]], [[math:def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]

[F2] A finite-type morphism is quasi-compact and locally of finite type; the latter means that every point has an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ mapping into an affine open of the base with a finite-type coordinate algebra map. Finite type for a $k$-algebra means generated by finitely many elements as an algebra. The scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k$ has one point and its ring of global functions is canonically $k$, so its only nonempty open is the whole base. Consequently the charts for $X$ have coordinate rings that are finitely generated $k$-algebras. [[math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]], [[math:def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]], [[math:ex-spectrum-field-one-point]]

[F3] A finite-type commutative algebra over a Noetherian commutative ring is Noetherian. [[math:cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]

[F4] Under AC, the spectrum of a Noetherian commutative ring is a Noetherian topological space. [[math:thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum]]

[F5] Under AC, every open subset of a Noetherian topological space is quasi-compact, including the empty subset. [[math:lem-noetherian-open-subsets-are-quasi-compact]]

[F6] A scheme is quasi-compact if its underlying space is quasi-compact, and quasi-separated if the intersection of every two affine open subschemes is quasi-compact. [[math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme]]

[F7] A scheme morphism $f:X\to S$ is quasi-compact when $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subseteq S$. [[math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]]

[A1] AC is assumed for the Noetherian-spectrum and open-subset suppliers. [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Any nonzero ideal of $k$ contains some $a\ne0$, hence contains $a^{-1}a=1$ and is all of $k$. Thus its only ideals are $(0)$ and $(1)$, both finitely generated, and $k$ is Noetherian by [F1]. Every open cover of the one-point base $\operatorname{Spec}k$ has a member containing its sole point and hence a one-member subcover; thus the base is quasi-compact by [F2]. The structure morphism $f$ is quasi-compact by [F2], so [F7] applied to the whole base gives that $X=f^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}k)$ is quasi-compact. By [F2], $X$ has an affine open cover with finite-type coordinate $k$-algebras; take a finite subcover $U_1,\ldots,U_N$. Each coordinate algebra is Noetherian by [F3], so each $U_j$ is a Noetherian topological space by [F4]. For empty $X$ take the empty subcover. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, A1]

2.1 Let $W\subseteq X$ be open and take any open cover of $W$. For each $j$, restricting this cover to the open subset $W\cap U_j$ gives a finite subcover by [F5]. The union of these finitely many finite subcovers covers $W$, since the $U_j$ cover $X$. Thus every $W$ is quasi-compact; for empty $W$ the empty subcover suffices. Apply this to $W=X$ and to each intersection of two affine opens. The definitions in [F6] give precisely the claimed qcqs conclusion. [F5, F6, A1, step 1.1] ∎
