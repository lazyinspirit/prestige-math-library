## Statement
Let $A$ be a commutative ring with unit. Call a finite projective $A$-module $P$ cancellative if, for every finite projective $Q$ and every integer $N\geq0$, $P\oplus A^N\cong Q\oplus A^N$ implies $P\cong Q$. This is equivalent to cancellation of an arbitrary common finite projective summand: $P\oplus T\cong Q\oplus T$ implies $P\cong Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data and hypotheses in the statement.

**Goal:** Establish the stated conclusion.

[F1] A finite projective module is generated by finitely many elements and has the lifting property for homomorphisms through surjections. The choice-free module clause gives every finite projective $T$ a finite projective complement $T'$ and an integer $N\geq0$ with $T\oplus T'\cong A^N$. Only this module clause is used; the supplier's later scheme assertions and their additional assumptions are not invoked. [lem-finite-projective-complements](item-lem-finite-projective-complements)

[F2] The ring is commutative with unit and all modules are unital left modules. Finite direct sums have coordinatewise operations; $A^N$ is the free module on the finite index set $\{1,\ldots,N\}$, empty when $N=0$, with its indexed standard basis. The notation $\cong$ means an $A$-linear bijection. [math:def-commutative-ring](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-commutative-ring) [math:def-left-and-right-modules](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-left-and-right-modules) [math:def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules) [math:def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis) [math:def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel)

[F3] A free module with a finite indexed basis is projective without assuming the Axiom of Choice: only finitely many basis values need preimages through a surjection. For the empty basis the lift is the unique map from zero. This is the finite-basis clause, rather than the arbitrary-basis assertion requiring Choice. [math:thm-free-modules-are-projective-with-choice-boundary](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-free-modules-are-projective-with-choice-boundary)

## Proof

1.1 If $P$ is cancellative in the free-summand sense and $\phi:P\oplus T\to Q\oplus T$ is an isomorphism, choose $T'$ and $\alpha:T\oplus T'\to A^N$ by [F1]. The map $\phi\oplus\operatorname{id}_{T'}$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\phi^{-1}\oplus\operatorname{id}_{T'}$. Reassociation $(m,(t,t'))\mapsto((m,t),t')$ and its inverse are $A$-linear coordinate maps. Reassociate on both sides and apply $\alpha$ on the last two coordinates to obtain $P\oplus A^N\cong Q\oplus A^N$. Free-summand cancellativity now gives $P\cong Q$. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 Conversely suppose cancellation holds for every common finite projective summand and let $P\oplus A^N\cong Q\oplus A^N$. The $N$ standard basis vectors generate $A^N$ (the empty family generates $A^0=0$), and the finite-basis clause of [F3] makes $A^N$ projective without assuming Choice. Thus $A^N$ is finite projective, so the assumed cancellation with $T=A^N$ gives $P\cong Q$. Together with step 1.1 this proves the equivalence. [F1, F2, F3, given, step 1.1] ∎
