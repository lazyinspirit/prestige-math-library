## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the site and universe size bounds required by the cited motivic filtration, support, and comparison constructions. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and $X$ a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three. Then
$$F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)=F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X).$$

More generally, if $X$ is normal affine of finite type and dimension three but is not assumed pure-dimensional, the same equality holds with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ replaced by the zero-extended subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ generated by smooth closed points on its three-dimensional components.

Here normal means that every local ring is an integrally closed domain; affine finite type means $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ for a finitely generated $k$-algebra $A$. Dimension is the supremum of lengths of chains of distinct irreducible closed subsets, and pure dimension three means that every irreducible component has dimension three. A point is smooth if $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth in a neighbourhood of it. The group $K_0(X)=\pi_0K(X)$ is the zeroth homotopy group of nonconnective $K$-theory of perfect complexes, where a perfect complex is locally quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite locally free sheaves. The motivic image piece $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$ is the image of $\pi_0F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X)\to K_0(X)$. For pure $X$, $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ is the subgroup generated by the perfect residue-sheaf classes $[\kappa(x)]$ at smooth closed points. In the nonpure case the top-dimensional subgroup uses only points on three-dimensional components, with classes extended by zero along the clopen component inclusions. Choice means that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; the size bounds fix the sites and universes of the cited constructions.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Reduce to componentwise curve support, lift to the actual supported third stage, and apply the generic-rank kernel calculation with its hypotheses checked.

1.1 By [F6], $k$ is perfect and $X$ is Noetherian. By [F7], $X$, its smooth open $W$, all component opens and the open complements of the closed supports used below are qcqs and quasi-compact. These opens are of finite type by [F0] and [F7]. By [F1], $X$ is a finite disjoint union of clopen integral components, and the top-dimensional smooth-point subgroup is contained in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. It remains to prove the reverse inclusion. Let $\alpha$ be an arbitrary element of that image piece. Choice and the fixed compatible size bounds are retained in all these supplier applications. [F0, F1, F6, F7, A1, given]

2.1 Apply [F2] to $\alpha$. It supplies a closed subset $C\subset W=X_{\mathrm{smooth}}$ and a class $p\in F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ such that $\beta=\alpha-p$ lies in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$ and restricts to zero in $K_0(X\setminus C)$. For every three-dimensional component $X_e$, $C_e=C\cap X_e$ is an integral closed curve in $X_e$ contained in $W_e$; on every lower-dimensional component $C$ is empty. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3], a representative of $\beta$ in $\pi_0F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X)$ lifts to $\widetilde b\in\pi_0F_C^3K(X)$. Write $z\in\pi_0K_C(X)$ for its augmentation, so its support-to-global image is $\beta$. The opens $W$ and $X\setminus C$ cover $X$, and their intersection is $W\setminus C$; step 1.1 checks the quasi-compactness hypotheses. Hence [F5] supplies a homotopy-cartesian $K$-restriction square and the actual excision equivalence $r_K:K_C(X)\simeq K_C(W)$. The supplied stage restriction is the group isomorphism $r_F:\pi_0F_C^3K(X)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi_0F_C^3K(W)$ of [F3]; put $\widetilde b_W=r_F(\widetilde b)$. Naturality of the augmentation gives $r_K(z)$ as the augmentation of $\widetilde b_W$, where $r_K(z)$ denotes the induced map on $\pi_0$. Both restriction maps commute with the support-to-global maps. In particular $z$ is the inverse-excision image of this supported class on $W$; no inverse to global restriction $K_0(X)\to K_0(W)$ is assumed. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Restrict the finite clopen partition of $X$ to $W$ and $W\setminus C$. By [F5], both the stage-three maps and the $K$ maps decompose by these restrictions. Taking their fibers decomposes the two support spectra into finite products: by [F3], a fiber is a pullback over zero, and the universal property identifies a product of such pullbacks with the pullback of the product maps. Mapping the sphere into this product gives the corresponding product on $\pi_0$. A component with empty support contributes the fiber of an identity and hence zero. For a remaining component, $W_e$ is nonempty because it contains $C_e$; it is integral since a nonempty open of an integral scheme remains reduced and irreducible. By [F6], $\dim W_e=\dim X_e=3$, so it is pure three-dimensional; it is smooth by its definition and finite type by step 1.1. Let $\eta_e$ be the generic point of $C_e$. By [F6], $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(\eta_e)=1$, since $C_e$ is an integral curve. The closed immersion gives the same residue field at $\eta_e$ in $C_e$ and $W_e$. The dimension formula in [F6] therefore gives $\dim\mathcal O_{W_e,\eta_e}=3-1=2$. This is exactly the required codimension of $C_e$ in $W_e$, including when the reduced closure has singular boundary points. Thus [F4] applies to each component of $\widetilde b_W$ and expresses the corresponding component of $r_K(z)$ as a finite integer combination of supported residue-sheaf classes at closed points of $C_e$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By the final assertion of [F4], applied to $W_e\subset X_e$, every point in step 4.1 is closed and smooth in $X_e$, hence in $X$ because $X_e$ is clopen. By [F5], its residue sheaf is perfect on $X_e$ and its restriction to $W_e$ is the same residue sheaf there, with the same residue field by [F0]. Restriction between the supported perfect categories consequently sends its point class on $X_e$ to the point class on $W_e$, also when viewed with support in the larger closed curve $C_e$. The excision equivalence for $C_e\subset W_e\subset X_e$ in [F5] therefore takes the inverse image of each class occurring in step 4.1 to that actual residue class on $X_e$; all quasi-compactness hypotheses hold by step 1.1. The clopen support decomposition and [F1] identify its subsequent extension to $X$ with the residue class at the same point, with zeros on other components. Hence inverse excision sends the combination $r_K(z)$ to a supported class whose global image is in $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$. Step 3.1 identifies that inverse image with $z$ and its global image with $\beta$. Consequently $\alpha=p+\beta$ belongs to $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$. [F0, F1, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary, step 5.1 and the inclusion in step 1.1 prove the general equality. If $X$ is pure three-dimensional, all its components are three-dimensional, so [F1] identifies the top-dimensional subgroup with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$. This proves the article assertion as well as the separate componentwise extension. [F1, step 1.1, step 5.1] ∎
