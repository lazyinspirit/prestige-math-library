## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the site and universe size bounds required by the cited motivic filtration, support, and comparison constructions. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and $X$ a normal affine finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three. Then
$$F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)=F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X).$$

More generally, if $X$ is normal affine of finite type and dimension three but is not assumed pure-dimensional, the same equality holds with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ replaced by the zero-extended subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ generated by smooth closed points on its three-dimensional components.

Here normal means that every local ring is an integrally closed domain; affine finite type means $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ for a finitely generated $k$-algebra $A$. Dimension is the supremum of lengths of chains of distinct irreducible closed subsets, and pure dimension three means that every irreducible component has dimension three. A point is smooth if $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth in a neighbourhood of it. The group $K_0(X)=\pi_0K(X)$ is the zeroth homotopy group of nonconnective $K$-theory of perfect complexes, where a perfect complex is locally quasi-isomorphic to a bounded complex of finite locally free sheaves. The motivic image piece $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$ is the image of $\pi_0F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X)\to K_0(X)$. For pure $X$, $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ is the subgroup generated by the perfect residue-sheaf classes $[\kappa(x)]$ at smooth closed points. In the nonpure case the top-dimensional subgroup uses only points on three-dimensional components, with classes extended by zero along the clopen component inclusions. Choice means that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; the size bounds fix the sites and universes of the cited constructions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $k$ and $X$ as in the general statement, the Axiom of Choice, and the stated size bounds.

**Goal:** Show that the third motivic image piece is precisely the top-dimensional smooth-point subgroup, and recover the pure-dimensional assertion.

[F1] [lem-author-80f982ded420-normal-point-strata](item-lem-author-80f982ded420-normal-point-strata) gives a finite disjoint clopen decomposition of $X$ into integral components and identifies zero extension of their point classes. [lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope](item-lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope) defines the top-dimensional point subgroup and proves its inclusion in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. In the pure case it is the subgroup of [def-smooth-point-filtration](item-def-smooth-point-filtration).

[F2] For any $\alpha$ in the third image piece, [lem-bertini-support-reduction](item-lem-bertini-support-reduction) constructs the componentwise curve-and-point support: the reduced closed curve $C$ lies in the smooth locus $W$; on each three-dimensional integral component its intersection is an integral closed curve, and on lower-dimensional components it is empty. [lem-point-correction-localization](item-lem-point-correction-localization) gives a top-dimensional smooth-point class $p$ such that $\beta=\alpha-p$ remains in the third image piece and restricts to zero in $K_0(X\setminus C)$.

[F3] [lem-lift-curve-supported-filtration](item-lem-lift-curve-supported-filtration) gives an actual third-stage support lift of the stage representative of $\beta$ and an actual restriction equivalence between the support fibers on $(X,X\setminus C)$ and $(W,W\setminus C)$, compatible with the maps to the global stages. The image-piece convention is that of [def-motivic-filtration](item-def-motivic-filtration).

[F4] On a smooth pure three-dimensional finite-type scheme over the perfect field $k$, for an integral closed curve of codimension two, [lem-supported-generic-rank-kernel](item-lem-supported-generic-rank-kernel) identifies the image of the actual supported third stage in supported $K_0$ with the subgroup generated by residue-sheaf classes of closed points of that curve. The maps are the actual stage and support maps, with the normalization specified in that supplier.

[F5] [lem-author-80f982ded420-clopen-filtered-products](item-lem-author-80f982ded420-clopen-filtered-products) supplies finite clopen product decompositions of the actual stages and their maps. [lem-open-excision-for-support-fibers](item-lem-open-excision-for-support-fibers) identifies support fibers under restriction to an open neighbourhood of the support, compatibly with support-to-global maps. [lem-koszul-point-support-class](item-lem-koszul-point-support-class) supplies the perfect residue-sheaf classes at smooth closed points. The cited suppliers use the stated Choice and size hypotheses; an algebraically closed field is perfect. [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice)

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Reduce to componentwise curve support, lift to the actual supported third stage, and apply the generic-rank kernel calculation.

1.1 By [F1], $X$ is a finite disjoint union of clopen integral components. The top-dimensional smooth-point subgroup is contained in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$. It remains to prove the reverse inclusion. Let $\alpha$ be an arbitrary element of that image piece. [F1, given]

2.1 Apply [F2] to $\alpha$. It supplies a closed subset $C\subset W=X_{\mathrm{smooth}}$ and a class $p\in F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$ such that $\beta=\alpha-p$ lies in $F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)$ and restricts to zero in $K_0(X\setminus C)$. For every three-dimensional component $X_e$, $C_e=C\cap X_e$ is an integral closed curve in $X_e$ contained in $W_e=W\cap X_e$; on every lower-dimensional component $C$ is empty. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3], a representative of $\beta$ in $\pi_0F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X)$ lifts to an element of the actual support group $\pi_0F^3_{\mathrm{mot}}K(X/(X\setminus C))$. Restrict this lift to $W$ using the support equivalence in [F3]. Its image in $\pi_0K(W/(W\setminus C))$, followed by the support-to-global map and the compatible comparison back on $X$, represents $\beta$. This uses the maps of support fibers, rather than identifying unrelated groups merely by isomorphism. [F3, step 2.1]

4.1 The finite clopen decomposition in [F1] restricts to $W$ and to its complement of $C$. Taking fibers of the componentwise stage and $K$ maps in [F5] decomposes both supported groups into the corresponding finite products. A component with empty support contributes the fiber of an identity map and hence zero on $\pi_0$. For each remaining component, $W_e$ is smooth of pure dimension three and $C_e\subset W_e$ is an integral closed curve of codimension two. Applying [F4] to the component of the lifted element shows that its supported $K_0$ image is a finite integer combination of the residue-sheaf classes at closed points of $C_e$. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Each such point is closed and smooth in $X_e$, hence in $X$: closedness follows because a closed point of the finite-type $k$-scheme $W_e$ has finite residue field over $k$, and smoothness is local on the source. By [F5], excision for a neighbourhood of each point identifies its supported class on $W_e$ with its supported class on $X_e$, compatibly with support-to-global maps; [F1] identifies subsequent clopen zero extension with the point class on $X$. Thus the image on $X$ of the combination in step 4.1 is a top-dimensional smooth-point class. Compatibility in step 3.1 shows that this image is $\beta$. Consequently $\alpha=p+\beta$ is in $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)$. [F1, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Since $\alpha$ was arbitrary, step 5.1 and the inclusion in step 1.1 prove the general equality. If $X$ is pure three-dimensional, all its components are three-dimensional, so [F1] identifies the top-dimensional subgroup with $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$. This proves the article assertion. [F1, step 1.1, step 5.1] ∎
