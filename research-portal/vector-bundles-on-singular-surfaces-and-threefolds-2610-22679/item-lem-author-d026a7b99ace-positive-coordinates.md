## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and fix the compatible sites and completed universe bounds of the supplied motivic, support and K-theory constructions. In particular, assume compatible nested Grothendieck universes $\mathscr U\in\mathscr V\in\mathscr W$ with $\omega\in\mathscr U$, containing the scheme and site data, with larger bounds for the enhanced categories as required by those constructions; their existence is a separate assumption. Let $k$ be algebraically closed of any characteristic, $X$ a finite-type $k$-scheme of pure dimension three, and $x$ a smooth closed point. Write $k(x)=\kappa(x)$ and let $i:\operatorname{Spec}k(x)\hookrightarrow X$ be the closed immersion of the point. There is a smooth affine neighbourhood $V$ of $x$, and actual open-excision identifications give $K_{0,x}(X)\cong\mathbb Z$ with positive generator $u_x=[i_*k(x)]$, and $H^{6,3}_x(X,\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ with positive generator $\delta_x$, the fundamental point cycle. Explicitly extend the nonnegative weight system by $C_j=0$ and $H^{a,j}=0$ for $j<0$, and likewise for its support fibers. With this convention, $H^{2j,j}_x(X)=0$ for every integer $j<3$. These are supported generators, even when their images in global groups vanish. The positive motivic generator is selected by the specified comparison $\Psi=\lambda\rho^{-1}\theta$ of the étale-natural supported-cycle lemma: on a smooth affine neighbourhood it is the inverse image of the literal reduced supported cycle $[x]$, and on $X$ it is transported by actual open excision. This fixes the normalization of the comparison, rather than choosing an unspecified fixed-ambient equivalence.

## Facts & Assumptions
[F0] A $k$-scheme has a structure morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}k$, as in [math:def-scheme-over-base](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-scheme-over-base); finite type and smoothness concern this morphism, as in [math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism) and [math:def-smooth-morphism-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-smooth-morphism-schemes). A closed point has closed singleton by [math:def-closed-point-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-closed-point-scheme), and an affine scheme has a presentation as the spectrum of a ring by [math:def-affine-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-scheme). The residue field is $k(x)=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x$, as in [math:def-residue-field-scheme-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-residue-field-scheme-point). Pure dimension three means that every irreducible component has dimension three; dimension counts lengths of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets. Algebraically closed means that every nonconstant polynomial in $k[t]$ has a root in $k$. Perfectness has the irreducible-separable-polynomial meaning of [math:def-perfect-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-perfect-field).

[A1] The assumed [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice) is inherited by the geometric, perfect-K, cycle and motivic comparison suppliers below. The nested universes have the closure conditions of [math:def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-grothendieck-universe-closure-convention); their existence, compatible sites and the additional category bounds are assumed independently of AC. These are precisely the size premises used in the neighbourhood, actual-support and cycle-complex applications in [F2], and in the supported constructions in [F1], [F3] and [F4]. The elementary cycle calculation introduces no additional choice principle.

[F1] [prop-smooth-point-excision-purity](item-prop-smooth-point-excision-purity) and [lem-koszul-point-support-class](item-lem-koszul-point-support-class) give the actual K-support fiber and its dévissage coordinate, normalized by the residue sheaf. The latter supplier constructs the closed immersion $i$, the globally defined perfect residue object, its parameter-independent class and a regular-sequence Koszul resolution on a smooth affine neighbourhood. For the neighbourhood $V$ supplied in [F2], [lem-author-59b1a831fe59-neighborhood](item-lem-author-59b1a831fe59-neighborhood) gives $\operatorname{codim}_V\{x\}=3$. The convention in [def-bloch-higher-cycle-complex](item-def-bloch-higher-cycle-complex) identifies this codimension with $\dim\mathcal O_{V,x}$, since the generic point of the reduced closed point is $x$ itself. Open subschemes carry the restricted structure sheaf by [def-cohomology-with-support](item-def-cohomology-with-support): on every open subset of $V$, the sections and restriction maps of $\mathcal O_V$ and $\mathcal O_X$ coincide. Intersecting any neighbourhood of $x$ in $X$ with $V$ gives a smaller neighbourhood contained in $V$. These neighbourhoods are therefore cofinal for the stalk, giving $\mathcal O_{X,x}\cong\mathcal O_{V,x}$. The geometric-regularity clause of [math:def-smooth-morphism-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-smooth-morphism-schemes), applied to the structure morphism over $k$ and the identity extension $k/k$, makes this local ring regular. Thus it is regular of dimension three.

[F2] Under [A1], [lem-author-59b1a831fe59-neighborhood](item-lem-author-59b1a831fe59-neighborhood) supplies a smooth affine separated pure-three-dimensional finite-type neighbourhood and actual motivic support excision. Its residue field $L=k(x)$ is finite separable over the perfect base field, so $\operatorname{Spec}L$ is smooth of finite type over $k$. [lem-author-59b1a831fe59-actual-support](item-lem-author-59b1a831fe59-actual-support) compares the actual smooth restriction arrow and its fiber with the supported cycle complex. To select the positive class, use the particular spectral arrow/fiber comparison $\Psi$ of [lem-adjudicate-1f4b2e0378b1-flat-natural-supported-cycle-comparison](item-lem-adjudicate-1f4b2e0378b1-flat-natural-supported-cycle-comparison), rather than an arbitrary equivalence allowed by the fixed-ambient statement. This comparison is compatible with open and étale pullback between the stated smooth separated ambients and with the support projection. [lem-author-59b1a831fe59-cycle-complex](item-lem-author-59b1a831fe59-cycle-complex) identifies that complex with $C_0(L)[-6]$ in weight three, carrying the literal reduced point cycle $[x]$ to $1$, and proves zero for $0\leq j<3$. [lem-smooth-point-motivic-purity](item-lem-smooth-point-motivic-purity) gives the same lower-weight vanishing and permits the explicit negative-weight zero extension. [prop-smooth-motivic-comparison](item-prop-smooth-motivic-comparison) supplies $C_0(L)\simeq z^0(\operatorname{Spec}L,\bullet)$, with homological degree $n$ in cochain degree $-n$.

[F3] [lem-open-excision-for-support-fibers](item-lem-open-excision-for-support-fibers) preserves support-to-global arrows under restriction when its stated descent hypothesis holds. For K-theory, [F1] supplies the restriction equivalence to the smooth locus $S=X_{\mathrm{smooth}}$ and the positive coordinate directly on $X$. For the chosen affine neighbourhood $V$, [F2] supplies actual motivic support excision, compatibly with support-to-global maps. Only this motivic excision is used to transport a generator from $V$ to $X$; no K-descent square for this particular $V$ is assumed.

[F4] [def-cohomology-with-support](item-def-cohomology-with-support) defines $K_{0,x}(T)=\pi_0\operatorname{fib}(K(T)\to K(T\setminus\{x\}))$, its support-to-global map, and the actual motivic support convention. Write $C_j(T)=\mathbb Z(j)_{\mathrm{mot}}(T)$ for its unshifted coefficient complex. In the compatible cohomological convention of [def-supported-complex-indexing](item-def-supported-complex-indexing), $C_{j,x}(T)=\operatorname{fib}(C_j(T)\to C_j(T\setminus\{x\}))$ and $H^{a,j}_x(T,\mathbb Z)=H^a(C_{j,x}(T))$. The shift is $A[m]^q=A^{q+m}$ with differential $(-1)^m d_A$. In particular $H^6(C_0(L)[-6])=H^0(C_0(L))$. For $j<0$ the Statement defines both coefficient objects to be zero, so their explicit fiber model is zero as well.

[F5] [lem-adjudicate-a6bf10648fa2-finite-type-field-qcqs](item-lem-adjudicate-a6bf10648fa2-finite-type-field-qcqs) proves under AC that a finite-type field-scheme and all its opens are qcqs, with every open quasi-compact. This checks the domain of [F1], [F2] and [F4] without a global separatedness assumption.

[F6] [def-bloch-higher-cycle-complex](item-def-bloch-higher-cycle-complex) defines the unnormalized cycle groups, admissibility, integral generators, face-intersection lengths, the alternating boundary $\partial_n=\sum_{a=0}^n(-1)^a d_a$ and $H_0=\ker\partial_0/\operatorname{im}\partial_1$, with $\partial_0=0$. [lem-author-89250b1d710c-face-coordinate-geometry](item-lem-author-89250b1d710c-face-coordinate-geometry) supplies the simplex rings and ordered faces. Over $L$, $\Delta_L^0=\operatorname{Spec}L$ and $\Delta_L^1=\operatorname{Spec}L[t]$, with the two faces given by $t=0$ and $t=1$.

[F7] [math:cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable) states that every algebraic extension of a perfect field is separable. Apply it to perfectness in the irreducible-polynomial convention of [math:def-perfect-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-perfect-field) used in [F0]. This verifies the all-algebraic-extensions convention for the perfect-field hypothesis explicitly stated by [lem-koszul-point-support-class](item-lem-koszul-point-support-class) and [prop-smooth-point-excision-purity](item-prop-smooth-point-excision-purity) in [F1], as well as the convention of the motivic suppliers in [F2]. No characteristic restriction is introduced.

**Given:** $X,k,x,i$ as stated, AC, the fixed compatible sites and completed universe bounds, and the support, indexing and negative-weight conventions above.

**Goal:** Fix the local pair and its actual positive coordinates, preserving the full integer vanishing range and support-to-global maps.

## Proof
1.1 Every nonconstant irreducible polynomial over the algebraically closed field $k$ is linear, hence separable, so $k$ is perfect by [F0]. Applying [F7] to each algebraic extension of $k$ proves that every such extension is separable. Thus $k$ satisfies the perfect-field convention of the K-theory suppliers in [F1] as well as that of the motivic suppliers in [F2]. By [F5], $X$ and its open complement are qcqs. Under [A1], choose the smooth affine separated pure-three-dimensional finite-type neighbourhood $V$ supplied by [F2]. Independently, [F1] supplies a regular-sequence Koszul resolution of the residue sheaf on a smooth affine neighbourhood of $x$; this neighbourhood need not be the chosen $V$. The codimension-three conclusion of [F2] and the local-ring codimension convention in [F1] give $\dim\mathcal O_{V,x}=3$. As explained in [F1], restriction to this open neighbourhood identifies $\mathcal O_{X,x}\cong\mathcal O_{V,x}$, and the identity field extension in the smoothness condition gives regularity. Thus the local ring at $x$ is regular of dimension $3$. The residue sheaf is the globally defined perfect object, independently of the resolution, and [F1] identifies its actual K-support coordinate with $+1$. Thus $K_{0,x}(X)\cong\mathbb Z$ is normalized by $u_x=[i_*k(x)]$, with the specified support-to-global map of [F4]. [A1, F0, F1, F2, F4, F5, F7, given]

2.1 Apply the actual arrow/fiber comparison and chain isomorphism of [F2] with $d=j=3$ under [A1]. By [F4], they identify $H^{6,3}_x(V)$ with $H^0(C_0(L))=H_0(z^0(\operatorname{Spec}L,\bullet))$. In degrees zero and one, [F6] gives exactly one codimension-zero integral generator, the whole simplex. Indeed $L$ is a field, so the product of nonzero elements is nonzero; the product of the leading coefficients of two nonzero polynomials shows that $L[t]$ also has no zero divisors. Thus both rings are domains, and every nonzero prime contains the strictly smaller prime $(0)$, so cannot have codimension zero. These whole simplices meet all faces admissibly. Each degree-one face has coefficient $1$: the quotient of the localized ring $L[t]_{(t)}$ by $t$, or of $L[t]_{(t-1)}$ by $t-1$, is its residue field, a simple module of length one. The two face images therefore agree, and their alternating difference is zero. Since $\partial_0=0$, degree-zero homology is $\mathbb Z$, positively generated by the reduced point. Define $\delta_x$ on $V$ by $\Psi_{V,\{x\}}^{-1}[x]$, using the specified spectral comparison in [F2]. Its target is the realization of the same supported cycle complex just computed; [F4] and the realization/indexing assertion in [F2] identify its $\pi_{-6}$ with that complex's $H^6$. Thus the chain calculation gives this actual class coordinate $+1$. The same construction commutes with restriction to smaller smooth affine neighbourhoods by the open naturality of $\Psi$, so the normalization is independent of that neighbourhood: compare any two on a smaller affine neighbourhood in their intersection and use actual open excision. For $0\leq j<3$, [F2] gives zero supported cycle complexes in all degrees; for $j<0$, [F4] and the declared extension give zero support fibers. The actual motivic excision equivalence of [F2] transports $\delta_x$ from $V$ to $X$, preserving support-forgetting arrows as in [F3]. The K-coordinate and its residue-sheaf generator have already been obtained directly on $X$ in step 1.1 from [F1], without using K-excision for the chosen $V$. Thus $H^{2j,j}_x(X)=0$ for every integer $j<3$, and both positive coordinates hold even when a global image vanishes. No identification of an unspecified abstract purity isomorphism with Gysin pushforward is used. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, step 1.1] ∎
