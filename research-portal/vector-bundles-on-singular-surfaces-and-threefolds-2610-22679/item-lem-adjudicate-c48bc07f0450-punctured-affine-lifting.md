## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC). Let $k$ be a field, $A=k[x,y,z]$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, $\mathfrak m=(x,y,z)$, and $U=X\setminus\{\mathfrak m\}$. Let $v=(x,y,z)^t:\mathcal O_U\to\mathcal O_U^3$ and let $F$ be its cokernel sheaf. Then $v$ is injective, $F$ is finite locally free of rank two, $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)=A$, and $H^1(U,\mathcal O_U)=0$. Every section of $F$ lifts to a vector in $A^3$. In particular, for $Q=\ker((x,y,z):\mathcal O_U^3\to\mathcal O_U)$ one has $F\cong Q^\vee$, so every functional on $Q$ has a polynomial-vector representative. Here $H^1$ is sheaf cohomology, identified with ordered affine-cover Čech cohomology by the supplied comparison theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC, the field, polynomial ring, punctured spectrum and displayed maps in the Statement. The dual of an $\mathcal O_U$-module $M$ assigns to each open $V\subseteq U$ the $\mathcal O_V$-linear sheaf morphisms $M|_V\to\mathcal O_V$, with restriction of morphisms as restriction maps; these morphisms glue uniquely by the sheaf axiom in [F5]. Kernels are sectionwise kernels, cokernels are sheaf cokernels, and exactness means equality of image and kernel sheaves. Global sections are denoted by $\Gamma$. On a cover indexed by $1<2<3$, ordered Čech cochains have alternating restriction differential: $(dc)_{ij}=c_j-c_i$ and $(dc)_{123}=c_{23}-c_{13}+c_{12}$. Cohomology means the kernel of a differential modulo the image of its predecessor.

**Goal:** Justify the polynomial lift, including the comparison and exactness bridge used in the fixed-pair obstruction.

[F1] [math:def-field](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-field) fixes fields, and [math:def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates) defines $A=k[x,y,z]$ as finite sums of monomials, with coefficientwise addition and convolution multiplication. [math:def-affine-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-affine-scheme) fixes spectra with their structure sheaves. [math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion) identifies $D(f)$ with $\operatorname{Spec}A_f$, including the section ring $A_f$. Under AC, [math:cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact) makes these affines quasi-compact. [math:thm-separatedness-gluing-overlap-criterion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-separatedness-gluing-overlap-criterion) tests separatedness on an affine cover by affineness of overlaps and surjectivity of their coordinate-ring tensor maps. [math:def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank) fixes finite locally free sheaves and the quasi-coherence of such sheaves, including $\mathcal O_U$.

[F2] Under AC, [math:thm-cech-computes-qc-cohomology-separated-scheme-affine-cover](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-cech-computes-qc-cohomology-separated-scheme-affine-cover) identifies ordered Čech cohomology with sheaf cohomology for a quasi-coherent sheaf on a quasi-compact separated scheme with a finite affine open cover. Its sheaf-cohomology convention is used for $H^1$ above; no derived-category or long-exact-sequence theorem is needed for the lifting argument below, which is explicit sheaf gluing.

[F3] [math:def-koszul-complex-of-a-sequence-with-coefficients](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-koszul-complex-of-a-sequence-with-coefficients) fixes the homological Koszul complex and its alternating differential. [math:thm-regular-sequences-give-acyclic-koszul-complexes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-regular-sequences-give-acyclic-koszul-complexes) says that a finite regular sequence on a module has Koszul homology zero in positive degrees. Regular means that each successive element is a nonzerodivisor on the quotient by its predecessors and that the final quotient is nonzero. [math:lem-filtered-colimits-of-abelian-groups-are-exact](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-filtered-colimits-of-abelian-groups-are-exact) allows cohomology of a small filtered system of cochain complexes to commute with its colimit; we use only the ordered positive integers as the indexing category.

[F4] [math:def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves) defines kernels sectionwise and defines cokernel and image sheaves by sheafifying the sectionwise cokernel and image presheaves, also for modules over a structure sheaf. This is the construction of $F$, $Q$ and the image of $v$ used here. On a chart where a coordinate of $v$ is a unit, elementary elimination identifies the quotient presheaf with a free rank-two sheaf; thus its sheafification is that same quotient.

[F5] [math:def-sheaf-on-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-sheaf-on-topological-space) supplies locality and gluing: sections equal on an open cover are equal, and sections on a cover which agree on every pairwise overlap glue uniquely to a global section. These apply to the structure sheaf and its finite powers, as well as to the locally constructed morphisms.

[A1] [math:def-axiom-of-choice](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-axiom-of-choice) is inherited at affine quasi-compactness and the Čech comparison in [F1] and [F2]. All subsequent coordinate choices and gluing use only this finite three-member cover.

## Proof
1.1 Set $u_1=x,u_2=y,u_3=z$. The opens $D(u_i)$ cover $U$, because the only prime containing all three variables is $\mathfrak m$; all intersections are $D(\prod_{i\in I}u_i)$ with their localization rings by [F1]. Thus $U$ is quasi-compact. Apply the overlap criterion of [F1] over $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$: each map $A_{u_i}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}A_{u_j}\to A_{u_iu_j}$ is surjective, since its image contains $A$, $1/u_i$ and $1/u_j$. Hence $U$ is separated in the absolute sense required by [F2]. On each chart a coordinate of $v$ is a unit; eliminating that coordinate proves injectivity of $v$, local splitting of its image, and a free rank-two quotient $F$ using the sheaf-cokernel construction in [F4]. It also splits the row map with kernel $Q$. Dualizing that split finite free sequence chartwise gives $F\cong Q^\vee$, with the identifications agreeing on overlaps by evaluation. Each localization embeds in $A_{xyz}$: multiplication by a monomial just shifts the exponents in the coefficient description of [F1], so it is injective. By locality and gluing in [F5], $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)$ is $A_x\cap A_y\cap A_z$ inside $A_{xyz}$. Write this common localization as $k[x^{\pm1},y^{\pm1},z^{\pm1}]$, meaning finite sums of monomials with integer exponents. Multiplication by a sufficiently large power of $xyz$ gives a polynomial, and uniqueness of polynomial coefficients proves uniqueness of this expression. Membership in all three subrings permits no negative exponent of any variable, so this intersection is $A$. The augmented ordered localization complex is $L^0=A$, $L^1=A_x\oplus A_y\oplus A_z$, $L^2=A_{xy}\oplus A_{xz}\oplus A_{yz}$, $L^3=A_{xyz}$, with alternating inclusions as differentials. Define $K_n^p=\bigoplus_{|I|=p}A$ for $0\leq p\leq3$, with differential adding $j\notin I$ with coefficient $u_j^n$ and the usual alternating sign. This is the cochain Koszul complex on $x^n,y^n,z^n$. The complement-of-subsets pairing in $\bigwedge A^3$ identifies it, with degree signs, with the homological Koszul complex of [F3] in reverse degrees: the coefficient of a subset and its complement is their wedge coefficient of $e_1\wedge e_2\wedge e_3$, and deleting an index on one side corresponds to adding it on the other. The sequence is regular: the quotients successively have monomial bases with the preceding variable exponents bounded by $n-1$, so multiplication by the next variable's $n$-th power is injective; the final quotient has basis $x^ay^bz^c$ with $0\leq a,b,c<n$. Thus [F3] makes $H^p(K_n^\bullet)=0$ for $p<3$. The transition from $n$ to $n+1$ multiplies the summand indexed by $I$ by $\prod_{i\in I}u_i$ (the empty product is 1). It commutes with the differentials, and $a\mapsto a/(\prod_{i\in I}u_i)^n$ identifies its colimit summand with the corresponding localization, hence identifies the colimit complex with $L^\bullet$. Exactness of this filtered colimit gives $H^2(L^\bullet)=0$. Removing $L^0$ shifts the degrees by one and gives precisely the ordered Čech complex for $D(x),D(y),D(z)$ and $\mathcal O_U$. Therefore its first cohomology vanishes, and [F2] applies to this quasi-coherent sheaf on the verified quasi-compact separated scheme to give $H^1(U,\mathcal O_U)=0$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, A1, given, algebra]

2.1 For a section $\sigma$ of $F$, the explicit local splittings in step 1.1 and the quotient description in [F4] give lifts $b_i\in A_{u_i}^3$. On an overlap, their difference has the unique form $b_j-b_i=c_{ij}v$ with $c_{ij}\in A_{u_iu_j}$, since it maps to zero in $F$ and $v$ is a split injection there. On the triple overlap, cancellation of the three differences gives $c_{23}-c_{13}+c_{12}=0$. The vanishing of first ordered Čech cohomology proved in step 1.1 yields $c_i\in A_{u_i}$ with $c_{ij}=c_j-c_i$. Consequently $b_i-c_iv$ agree on overlaps. The gluing axiom in [F5] glues them to a section of $\mathcal O_U^3$, and step 1.1 identifies that section with a vector $b\in A^3$ lifting $\sigma$. This proves the surjectivity on sections directly, including the exactness consequence needed from the displayed locally split sequence; it does not infer surjectivity merely from sheaf exactness. The identification $F\cong Q^\vee$ then proves the functional assertion. [F4, F5, step 1.1, given, algebra, construct] ∎
