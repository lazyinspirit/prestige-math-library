## Open problem
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the fixed compatible universes of the motivic-filtration and smooth-point constructions. Let $k$ be algebraically closed, meaning that every nonconstant polynomial in $k[t]$ has a root in $k$. Let $A$ be a commutative unital $k$-algebra generated by finitely many elements as a $k$-algebra, and assume that $A$ is reduced: $a^n=0$ for some integer $n\geq1$ implies $a=0$. Set $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, with its structure sheaf and the structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ induced by the algebra map $k\to A$. This is the affine $k$-variety meant here; it need not be irreducible.

Assume that $X$ has pure dimension $d>3$: every irreducible component has dimension $d$. An irreducible closed subset is a nonempty closed subset that is not a union of two proper closed subsets; components are maximal such subsets. Dimension is the supremum of the lengths $n$ of strict chains $Z_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq Z_n$ of irreducible closed subsets. Affineness makes $X$ quasi-compact and quasi-separated, and algebraic closedness makes $k$ perfect; in particular every algebraic extension of $k$ is separable. Thus these objects meet the scheme and field hypotheses of both filtration constructions.

Under what precise restrictions on the singularities of $X$, for example normality, can the geometric support method in the proof of the article’s Theorem 6.7 be generalized to establish
$$F^d_{\mathrm{mot}}K_0(X)=F^d_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)?$$
Normality means that the local rings of $X$ are integrally closed domains. Here $K_0(X)$ is the Grothendieck group of perfect complexes. The left side is the image in $K_0(X)$ of the degree-zero homotopy group of the $d$th stage of the motivic filtration; the right side is the subgroup generated by the classes of the residue sheaves of closed points at which $X$ is smooth over $k$.

In particular, formulate hypotheses under which the proof’s reduction to support inside the smooth locus, correction by smooth closed points, and analysis of the remaining supported classes can be carried out in dimension $d$. A perfect complex is supported on a closed subset $T$ when its restriction to $X\setminus T$ is acyclic, as in the perfect-complex support construction; a supported class here means the image in $K_0(X)$ of a class of such complexes. The smooth locus means the points having an open neighbourhood smooth over $k$. The problem asks for a method and exact hypotheses; it does not assert that normality suffices.

## Status
The article proves the corresponding third-piece statement for normal affine threefolds and expresses the belief that its geometric proof may generalize to higher dimensions for sufficiently mild singularities, mentioning normality as an example. Remark 6.8 separately reports a communicated alternative argument without normality and in arbitrary dimension, to appear in ongoing work; that report is not a proof of the requested geometric extension. No independent current-status review or solution is claimed, and this question is not a logical premise.
