## Statement
Assume AC. Let $A$ be a regular integral affine algebra of dimension $n\geq2$ over an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic, and put $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Write $K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$ for the Grothendieck group of finite projective modules, with exact-sequence relations. Let $F^nK_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$ be its subgroup generated by the classes $[A/\mathfrak m]$ of the residue fields of smooth maximal ideals of height $n$; a finite projective resolution defines each such class. Then this subgroup has no $(n-1)!$ torsion, meaning that $(n-1)!x=0$ forces $x=0$. The natural cycle map $\phi:\mathrm{CH}^n(X)\to F^nK_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$ sending each closed-point cycle $[x]$ to $[k(x)]$ is an isomorphism. For a rank-$n$ finite projective module $P$ and associated bundle $E$, the actual equality is $\phi(c_n(E))=C^K_n(P)=\sum_i(-1)^i[\Lambda^iP^*]$. Thus this class belongs to $F^n$, and $c_n(E)=0$ exactly when $C^K_n(P)=0$.

## Proof
1.1 Write $\mathcal P$ for finite projective $A$-modules and $\mathcal H$ for finite modules admitting finite resolutions by objects of $\mathcal P$. Give both groups short-exact-sequence relations. With the explicit group presentations in [F7], the latter category is extension closed: for $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ and finite projective resolutions of the end terms, lift the map $P''_0\to M''$ to $M$ by projectivity and combine it with $P'_0\to M'$. This gives a surjection $P'_0\oplus P''_0\to M$. Its kernel projects onto the first syzygy of $M''$: for a syzygy element in $P''_0$, its lift in $M$ lies in $M'$, and a preimage in $P'_0$ cancels it. The kernel of this projection is the first syzygy of $M'$. Thus these three kernels form a short exact sequence. Repeating, with resolutions padded by zeros, gives a resolution of $M$ with terms $P'_i\oplus P''_i$; the last extension of projective syzygies splits by the lifting property. Thus the inherited exact structure is defined, and inclusion induces $j:K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)\to K_0(\mathcal H)$. [F4, F7, construct]

1.2 For $M\in\mathcal H$, choose a finite resolution $P_\bullet\to M$ and put $\chi(M)=\sum_i(-1)^i[P_i]$. Given two resolutions, lift the identity of $M$ to a chain map by lifting successively through the surjections onto kernels in the target resolution. It induces the identity on degree-zero homology and both resolutions have zero higher homology, so [F8] makes its finite mapping cone acyclic. Every bounded acyclic complex of finite projectives is split exact: in its lowest nonzero degree $q$, the map from degree $q+1$ surjects onto the projective degree-$q$ term and splits by [F4]. The residual kernel in degree $q+1$ is a finite projective direct summand by [F4]. Remove this two-term identity complex and continue through the finitely many degrees. The alternating projective class of the cone is zero, while its termwise alternating sum is the difference of the two resolution sums. Hence $\chi(M)$ is independent of the chosen resolution. [F4, F7, F8, construct, algebra]

2.1 The resolution in step 1.1 gives $\chi(M)=\chi(M')+\chi(M'')$ for every defining exact relation, using independence from step 1.2. Hence $\chi$ induces a homomorphism $K_0(\mathcal H)\to K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$. Length-zero resolutions give $\chi j([P])=[P]$. Each successive syzygy of a finite projective resolution is in $\mathcal H$, because the tail is its finite resolution. The corresponding short exact sequences telescope to $(M)=\sum_i(-1)^i(P_i)$ in $K_0(\mathcal H)$, so $j\chi((M))=(M)$. These are mutually inverse group maps, respecting every exact relation. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 By [F6], $A$ is Noetherian, regular and of finite dimension $n$, and $X$ is smooth, integral and equidimensional of dimension $n$ over $k$. Hence both the codimension Chow group and the ordinary Chern classes used here have their declared constructions and hypotheses. By [F5] every finite $A$-module has a resolution by finite projectives of length at most $n$. Hence $\mathcal H$ is the whole category of finite $A$-modules, with exactly the same short exact sequences, so its exact-relation group is $G_0(A)$, the all-finite-module group in the source convention [F4]. Step 2.1 therefore makes $j:K_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)\to G_0(A)$ an isomorphism with inverse $\chi$. Write $(M)$ for the source class of a finite module; its image under $\chi$ is the resolution Euler class, and length-zero resolutions identify projective classes by $j$. This establishes the comparison with the source's full category, rather than only its finite-projective-dimension subcategory. By [F9], the selected maximal ideals in the source convention are exactly all closed points of $X$: they are smooth and their regular local rings have dimension $n$. Thus the point-indexed subgroup used by $\phi_H$ and the target in the Statement have the same generators. For the subgroup $F^n_H$ generated by the selected $(A/\mathfrak m)$, $\chi$ restricts to an isomorphism onto the stated $F^nK_0^{\mathrm{proj}}(A)$, since it takes each residue module to its resolution Euler class and $j$ is its inverse.
