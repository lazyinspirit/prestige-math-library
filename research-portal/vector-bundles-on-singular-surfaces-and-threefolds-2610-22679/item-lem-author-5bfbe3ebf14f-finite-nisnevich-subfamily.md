## Statement
In the specified category of separated finite-type schemes over a field $k$, every Nisnevich covering family $\{U_i\to X\}_{i\in I}$ contains a finite subfamily which is still Nisnevich. The empty family suffices if $X=\varnothing$. This does not assert that the residue-field-lifting locus of a given étale map is Zariski open.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The site above and the pointwise residue-field-lifting definition of a Nisnevich family.

**Goal:** Obtain a finite subfamily with that same pointwise lifting property.

[F1] The site and covering condition are those of [def-cdh-topology-interface](item-def-cdh-topology-interface) and [def-nisnevich-covering-interface](item-def-nisnevich-covering-interface). A finite-type structural map is locally of finite type and quasi-compact: its affine charts over $k$ have finitely generated coordinate algebras, and a finite number cover $X$. A field is Noetherian since its only ideals are zero and the whole field. The finite-type algebra theorem therefore makes these chart rings Noetherian, so $X$ is a Noetherian scheme. [math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism), [math:def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism), [math:cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian), [math:def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme)

[F2] The imported Noetherian-scheme facts give a Noetherian underlying space and finitely many irreducible components for $X$ and each closed subscheme. Each component has a unique generic point, by the imported generic-point theorem. Dimension means the supremum of lengths of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets, with empty-space dimension $-\infty$. The imported dimension formula gives a finite bound for $\dim X$: for each of the finitely many generic points choose one of the finite-type affine charts from F1, with ring $B$ and corresponding prime $\mathfrak p$. Its residue field is $\operatorname{Frac}(B/\mathfrak p)$, generated as a field by the images of a finite algebra generating list. Here a transcendence basis is an algebraically independent set over which the field extension is algebraic; algebraic means each element satisfies a nonzero polynomial over the base field, and algebraic independence means no nonzero polynomial relation among the chosen elements. Transcendence degree is the size of such a basis. The finite-generator form of the imported transcendence-basis theorem gives a basis inside the specified finite field-generating set, hence finite transcendence degree. Every closed subset has dimension at most $\dim X$, since its irreducible closed chains are also chains in $X$. [math:def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space), [math:def-residue-field-scheme-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-residue-field-scheme-point) The four precise imported premises are recorded in External input.

[F3] Every closed subset has its unique reduced induced closed subscheme structure by [thm-adjudicate-d1c21a67502d-reduced-closed-subscheme-input](item-thm-adjudicate-d1c21a67502d-reduced-closed-subscheme-input). A component is equipped with that structure and is integral: it is nonempty, irreducible and reduced, so its nonempty affine opens have domain coordinate rings. The reduction $X_{\mathrm{red}}$ has the same underlying points as $X$. A closed immersion induces an isomorphism on residue fields, as proved in the Justification of the Nisnevich interface; this applies to reductions and to components. Thus all their pointwise lifts can be compared with lifts over $X$. A generic point $\eta$ of a component $C$ means $\overline{\{\eta\}}=C$; every nonempty open of $C$ contains it. [math:def-irreducible-component-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-irreducible-component-scheme), [math:def-integral-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-integral-scheme), [math:def-reduction-of-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-reduction-of-scheme), [def-nisnevich-covering-interface](item-def-nisnevich-covering-interface)

[F4] An étale morphism is locally of finite presentation and hence locally of finite type, since a finite presentation has finitely many generators. Arbitrary base change preserves the latter property. Thus $U_i\times_X C\to C$ is locally of finite type, and at a point above a component generic point one may choose witnessing affine charts $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $B$ a finitely generated $A$-algebra. The Nisnevich interface supplies existence and the universal property of the scheme fibre product. [math:def-etale-morphism-schemes](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-etale-morphism-schemes), [math:def-locally-finite-presentation-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-presentation-morphism), [math:lem-base-change-locally-finite-type-presentation](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-base-change-locally-finite-type-presentation), [math:def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism), [def-nisnevich-covering-interface](item-def-nisnevich-covering-interface)

[F5] Finite generation permits a presentation $B=A[t_1,\ldots,t_n]/J$ with an arbitrary ideal $J$, without a claim that $J$ is finitely generated. Polynomial evaluation is a ring map and a map annihilating $J$ factors through the quotient. Affine scheme maps correspond contravariantly to ring maps. For a domain $A$, its fraction field contains $A$ injectively; for $a\ne0$ the localization maps into that field by $b/a^m\mapsto b/a^m$, injectively by the domain fraction equality criterion. Its spectrum is the open $D(a)$. At the generic point $(0)$ of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ the residue field is $\operatorname{Frac}A$. [math:def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras), [math:thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family), [math:thm-quotient-ring-universal-property](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-quotient-ring-universal-property), [math:thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence), [math:thm-field-of-fractions-is-a-field-and-the-domain-embeds](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-field-of-fractions-is-a-field-and-the-domain-embeds), [math:thm-universal-property-of-localisation](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-universal-property-of-localisation), [math:lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion), [math:def-residue-field-scheme-point](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-residue-field-scheme-point)

[F6] Here is the local spreading argument used below. Let $C$ be integral and let $Y\to C$ be locally of finite type with a section over its generic point. Choose the affine charts of F4 around the image of this section, so $A$ is a domain and the section gives an $A$-algebra map $B\to K=\operatorname{Frac}A$. For the presentation in F5, choose $0\ne a\in A$ equal to a product of denominators of the finitely many images of the $t_j$ (use $a=1$ for an empty list). Evaluation then maps $A[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ to $A[1/a]$. Every element of $J$ maps to zero in $K$, hence already in $A[1/a]$ by injectivity. The quotient property gives an $A$-algebra map $B\to A[1/a]$ and therefore a section over the nonempty open $D(a)\subset C$, extending the prescribed generic section. There is no finite-relations assumption. Also, a retraction $K\to L\to K$ of field maps makes $K\to L$ an isomorphism: the second map is injective and its image contains all of $K$, so both are inverse isomorphisms. Field-map injectivity is supplied by [math:lem-ring-homomorphism-of-fields-is-a-field-homomorphism](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/lem-ring-homomorphism-of-fields-is-a-field-homomorphism). These are local arguments using F4 and F5, not an imported rational-section theorem.

[F7] Open and closed sets obey the topology axioms; finite unions of closed sets are closed. On a subset, open and closed sets are traces of ambient open and closed sets. A nonempty space is irreducible if it cannot be written as a union of two proper closed subsets, and an irreducible subset carries the subspace topology. For $A\subseteq Z$, write $\overline A$ for the intersection of all closed subsets of $Z$ containing $A$. This family contains $Z$, so the intersection is closed, contains $A$, and lies in every closed set containing $A$. A component means a maximal nonempty irreducible closed subset, as in F3. These conventions suffice for the local containment and coverage argument in step 1.1; no arbitrary-space component-existence theorem is assumed. [math:def-topological-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-topological-space), [math:def-subspace-topology-top](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-subspace-topology-top), [math:def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset), [math:def-irreducible-component-scheme](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-irreducible-component-scheme)

## Proof
1.1 Let $Z\subset X$ be any nonempty reduced closed subscheme. First establish component containment and coverage using F2, F3 and F7. Given a nonempty irreducible closed subset $H\subseteq Z$, the lengths of strict irreducible closed chains starting at $H$ form a nonempty subset of the finite set $\{0,\ldots,\dim X\}$. Choose a chain of greatest length. Its last member $C$ cannot be properly contained in another irreducible closed subset, since appending that subset would give a longer chain starting at $H$; thus $C$ is a component containing $H$. For each point $z\in Z$, its closure $H=\overline{\{z\}}$ is nonempty and irreducible: if $H=E_1\cup E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ closed in $H$, F7 makes them closed in $Z$ because $H$ is closed, and $z\in E_j$ for some $j$ forces $H\subseteq E_j$ by the defining closure property. Hence $H=E_j$. Applying containment to this $H$ puts $z$ in a component, proving that the components cover $Z$. By F2 and F3 these finitely many components $C$ are integral and have generic points $\eta$. For each choose an original index $i$ and a residue-field-preserving lift of $\eta$ in $U_i$. The residue fields of $C,Z,X$ at this point agree by F3. The corresponding map $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(\eta)\to U_i$ is constructed on an affine neighbourhood by its coordinate-ring map to that residue field; the affine correspondence in F5 and the fibre-product universal property give a generic section of $U_i\times_X C\to C$. By F4 and F6 it extends over a nonempty open $V_C\subset C$. Remove from $V_C$ all other components. The resulting open $V'_C$ still contains $\eta$, since distinct maximal irreducible components do not contain each other's generic points. It is open also in $Z$: if $V_C=C\cap W_C$ for an open $W_C\subset Z$, then $V'_C=W_C\cap(Z\setminus\bigcup_{C'\ne C}C')$, because the components cover $Z$. Their finite union $O$ is open in $Z$ and contains every component generic point. The sections give points in the original $U_i$ above every point of $O$. At such a point $z$ the section followed by projection to $U_i$ gives field maps $\kappa(z)\to\kappa(u)\to\kappa(z)$ with identity composite, hence an isomorphism by F6; F3 identifies the base residue field with that in $X$. Thus finitely many original indices provide Nisnevich lifts over $O$. All choices here are finite. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, given]

2.1 Put $T=(Z\setminus O)_{\mathrm{red}}$, which exists by F3. If it is nonempty, any chain of nonempty irreducible closed subsets of $T$ is a chain in $Z$ by F7, since $T$ is closed in $Z$; its largest member is contained in a component $C$ of $Z$ by the containment argument of step 1.1. That containment is strict because $T$ omits the generic point of $C$. Appending $C$ lengthens the chain by one, so $\dim T\le\dim Z-1$. The finite bound in F2 now permits induction on $\dim Z$: the empty complement needs no indices, and otherwise apply the induction hypothesis to $T$ with the original family, whose pointwise lifts remain available by F3. Together with the indices of step 1.1 this gives finitely many original indices lifting every point of $Z$. At dimension zero the complement must be empty, providing the base case. Apply the result to $Z=X_{\mathrm{red}}$; F3 identifies all points and residue fields with those of $X$. Each selected original map remains étale, hence this finite subfamily is Nisnevich. If $X$ is empty, choose the empty family. No openness of a whole residue-field-lifting locus was used. [F1, F2, F3, F7, step 1.1] ∎
