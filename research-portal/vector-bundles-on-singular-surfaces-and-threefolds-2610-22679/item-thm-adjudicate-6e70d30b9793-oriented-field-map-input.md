## Statement
The following established inputs are used integrally, with the versions, hypotheses and reading limits in the source records. Throughout, the coefficient functors, filtrations and comparison maps are evaluated on qcqs schemes over the indicated bases.

For any qcqs scheme $X$ with structure map $g:X\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z$, the unit induces an equivalence of motivic $E_\infty$ rings $s^0\mathbf1_X\to s^0KGL_X$, compatible with the equivalences obtained by base change from $\mathbb Z$. Over $\mathbb Z$, the canonical framed vector-bundle map is an equivalence $\Sigma^\infty_{\rm fr}\mathrm{Vect}\simeq f_0KGL$. Its rank-induced Bott-cofiber map to $\Sigma^\infty_{\rm fr}\mathbb Z$ is an equivalence. The latter object is naturally Spitzweck's absolute integral $HZ$ as an $E_\infty$ ring, by the adjunction construction from constant integers; constant $1$ is the ring unit. The required condition $f_1HZ=0$ holds over the Dedekind base $\mathbb Z$. No characteristic inversion or $1/2$ assumption is imposed on this KGL assertion.

For a prime field $F$, EM supplies natural multiplicative augmented comparison maps from its motivic K-filtration to its cdh KH-filtration, hence natural graded coefficient maps $\eta:\mathbb Z(\star)_{\rm mot}\to\mathbb Z(\star)_{\rm cdh}$. For $j\geq0$ these are the maps of EM Construction 4.36, and their regular-Noetherian equivalence is Theorem 6.1(2). For $j<0$, the source coefficients vanish by EM Theorem 4.10(1) if $F=\mathbb Q$ and Theorem 4.24(1) if $F=\mathbb F_p$. In that range use $\mathbb Z(j)_{\rm cdh}(X):=\operatorname{gr}^j_{\rm cdh}KH(X)[-2j]=0$, from the N-indexed cdh filtration of Theorem 3.5(1), and let $\eta_j$ be the canonical zero-to-zero equivalence induced by the same filtered comparison. Thus $\eta$ denotes this graded comparison in every integer weight, with its existing products and unit. Each $\eta_j(X)$ is an equivalence when $X/F$ is regular Noetherian. On smooth $F$-schemes the composite of the classical-to-EM map with $\eta$ is the canonical classical left-Kan/cdh comparison. EM's actual first-Chern map is obtained from the classical weight-one map on smooth $F$-algebras by left Kan extension and Nisnevich sheafification, as in Lemma 5.1.

BEM supplies natural multiplicative graded maps from cdh coefficients through its base-changed represented coefficients to $\mathbb Z(\star)^{\mathbb A}$ represented by the KGL slices. Their composite is an equivalence on qcqs schemes over any field, in every integer weight. Its cdh coefficients restrict along every field extension by the canonical base-independent left-Kan/cdh construction. The represented theory has zero negative weights, the projective bundle formula and the Picard first class induced by $1-[L^\vee]$. On regular Noetherian bases its spectral Picard map is the determinant/unstable-slice-edge map of Construction 4.35; the cdh-local left Kan extension in Theorem 4.39(4), followed by Remark 4.45, supplies the indicated map on all qcqs schemes. For an $\mathbb A^1$-invariant Nisnevich graded ring theory with zero negative weights in every degree and PBF after every smooth base change, the mapping-limit argument in the proof of BEM Lemma 3.27 detects the Picard-space maps in (3.28) by the reduced $\mathbb P^1$ class: the projective-space tower has zero $\lim^1$ obstruction. This is a statement about these Picard-space maps, not arbitrary higher spectral maps.

Over a perfect field, Spitzweck's integral field spectrum identifies with ordinary Voevodsky motivic cohomology and the Bloch cycle model, compatibly with the product and actual $\mathbb G_m$ bonding map. Its canonical orientation classifies its tautological Picard line. Integral positive-characteristic specialization and arbitrary field extension have the hypotheses of Theorem 9.16 and Lemma 9.17. The occurrence of the tautological subline $\mathcal O(-1)$ in its line-projectivization convention does not replace the class of a given line by the class of its dual.

For a closed immersion $Z\hookrightarrow V$ between smooth separated finite-type schemes over a perfect field, smooth homotopy purity gives the coefficient-independent pointed equivalence $V/(V\setminus Z)\simeq\operatorname{Th}(N_{Z/V})$ by the canonical deformation maps. On these regular smooth pairs the refined motivic Gysin map is the Thom isomorphism followed by purity; its forget-support map is the quotient projection. For each positive Chern degree $i\geq1$, Navarro's supported Chern maps arise from the actual universal pointed K-space map and commute with forgetting support. Only this smooth-pair Gysin construction is used in the field-comparison application.

## Definition
Here qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated. A Noetherian scheme has a finite affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings; a regular Noetherian scheme has regular local rings at every point, with regularity meaning that the dimension of the maximal ideal modulo its square equals the Krull dimension. A perfect field is one whose irreducible polynomials are separable. Smoothness, finite type, separatedness and closed immersion have the scheme-theoretic meanings supplied in Statement references, relative to the indicated base. A prime field is $\mathbb Q$ or $\mathbb F_p$, and an equal-characteristic scheme here is a scheme over a field. The domain of the coefficient constructions and comparisons is restricted to qcqs schemes; their equal-characteristic locus therefore consists of qcqs schemes over a field. A Dedekind domain here is a Noetherian integrally closed domain of Krull dimension one; fields are excluded by this convention. The Dedekind bases in the imported statements are the spectra of such domains, with the mixed-characteristic and residue-characteristic qualifications recorded separately in the source hypotheses.

The imported motivic category $\mathrm{SH}(X)$ is the stable symmetric monoidal $\infty$-category of Nisnevich-local, $\mathbb A^1$-invariant spectra on smooth $X$-schemes stabilized by the reduced pointed projective line. Its tensor unit is $\mathbf1_X$; write $\mathbb T_X$ for the invertible reduced projective-line motive. A motivic $E_\infty$ ring is a coherently commutative unital algebra object in this category. The effective subcategory is generated under colimits and stable operations by suspension spectra of smooth $X$-schemes; its $j$-effective version is its tensor product with $\mathbb T_X^{\otimes j}$. The effective cover $f_jE\to E$ is universal for maps from $j$-effective objects, and $s^jE=\operatorname{cofib}(f_{j+1}E\to f_jE)$. Thus $s^0$ is the zeroth slice, not an arbitrary objectwise truncation. These constructions, their existence, multiplicative refinements and base-change comparison maps are imported from BEM §§3.1–3.3.

$K(X)$ denotes algebraic K-theory of perfect complexes, $KH(X)$ its $\mathbb A^1$-invariant homotopy K-theory, and $KGL_X$ its representing motivic spectrum. The decreasing filtrations used here have graded pieces $\operatorname{gr}^j=\operatorname{cofib}(\operatorname{Fil}^{j+1}\to\operatorname{Fil}^j)$. An N-indexed filtration has its augmentation $\operatorname{Fil}^j\to E$ an equivalence for $j\leq0$, so its negative graded pieces are zero (EM §2.2). In negative weights the cdh coefficients mean these shifted graded pieces, and the EM-to-cdh map means the component of the same augmented filtered map (EM Construction 4.36); multiplicativity means an $E_\infty$ algebra in filtered or graded objects with Day convolution, retaining units and coherent products. In particular the shifted coefficient grading is $F^j=\mathbb Z(j)[2j]$. The canonical Bott class uses the reduced virtual class $1-[\mathcal O(-1)]$ on $(\mathbb P^1,\infty)$; division by $\beta$ denotes the cofiber of its multiplication map, rather than inversion of a prime. The framed suspension $\Sigma^\infty_{\rm fr}$ is the framed motivic suspension functor in the cited Bachmann and Hoyois constructions. Here $\mathrm{Vect}$ is the groupoid of finite locally free sheaves and isomorphisms; its rank map has values in locally constant integers, with tensor unit of rank one.

The Picard space is the nerve of the groupoid of rank-one locally free $\mathcal O$-modules and their isomorphisms, pointed by $\mathcal O$, with tensor product as its commutative group structure. The first-Chern maps in the Statement are natural maps on this space, or the specified maps of associated spectra, rather than merely maps on isomorphism classes. For shifted coefficients, PBF means that powers of $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$ and pullback induce $\bigoplus_{a=0}^r F^{j-a}(Y)\simeq F^j(\mathbb P^r_Y)$ on every indicated smooth base $Y$. The symbol $\lim^1$ denotes the first derived inverse-limit obstruction in the projective-space mapping tower used in BEM Lemma 3.27.

$L_\tau$ denotes sheafification for $\tau=\mathrm{Nis}$ or $\mathrm{cdh}$. The Nisnevich topology uses étale covering families lifting each point with its residue field; cdh adds covers from finitely presented abstract blowup squares, as in BEM Definition 2.5. The left Kan extensions are the universal extensions along the indicated smooth-scheme or smooth-algebra inclusions, in the cited presentable $\infty$-categories; they are formed with compatible site and universe bounds. Their canonical comparison maps are the adjunction counits. The coefficient theories $\mathbb Z(\star)_{\rm mot}$, $\mathbb Z(\star)_{\rm cdh}$ and $\mathbb Z(\star)^{\mathbb A}$ are respectively EM's shifted graded motivic K-filtration, the cdh-local left Kan extension of classical coefficients, and the KGL-slice coefficient construction specified in the Statement. Their existence and the structured comparisons, including equivalence on the stated loci, are the imported EM and BEM inputs.

For the smooth closed pair $i:Z\hookrightarrow V$, let $\mathcal I=\ker(\mathcal O_V\to i_*\mathcal O_Z)$. Its conormal sheaf is $\mathcal C_{Z/V}=i^{-1}(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2)$, regarded as an $\mathcal O_Z$-module; the quotient inside $i^{-1}$ is formed on $V$. A regular closed immersion means that its ideal is locally generated along $Z$ by a finite regular sequence in the ambient local ring. The imported smooth-immersion and conormal results supply regularity of $i$ and finite local freeness of $\mathcal C_{Z/V}$ on this smooth-pair scope. The normal sheaf is $\mathcal N_{Z/V}=\mathcal C_{Z/V}^{\vee}=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_Z}(\mathcal C_{Z/V},\mathcal O_Z)$; duality of finite locally free sheaves supplies its finite local freeness. Write $N_{Z/V}$ for the corresponding geometric normal vector bundle in the imported purity theorem. Then $\operatorname{Th}(N)=N/(N\setminus Z)$, with $Z$ its zero section, is its total space modulo the complement of that section. Cohomology with support is evaluated on $V/(V\setminus Z)$; forgetting support is induced contravariantly by the pointed quotient map $V_+\to V/(V\setminus Z)$. The refined Gysin operation uses the oriented Thom isomorphism and the canonical purity equivalence on this smooth-pair scope, exactly as imported below.
