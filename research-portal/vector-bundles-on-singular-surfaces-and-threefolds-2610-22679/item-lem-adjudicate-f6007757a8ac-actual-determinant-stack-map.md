## Statement
Assume AC and the fixed compatible universes. For every qcqs scheme $X$ there is a natural pointed map from its specified nonconnective K-space to the stack of graded lines, whose value on a perfect complex $E$ is its Knudsen–Mumford graded determinant and whose value on an object automorphism is its determinant automorphism. Restricting to the locally constant rank-zero fiber gives the actual map to $B\mathbb G_m$. On vector bundles it is the frame-torsor map induced by $\det:GL_n\to\mathbb G_m$; on locally bounded finite-projective representatives it is the alternating graded determinant. Its additivity uses enhanced cofiber sequences and their specified diagrams; the induced class in $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ kills every cofiber relation. No equality of global vector-bundle and perfect K zero, or arbitrary ordinary triangle-diagram functoriality, is assumed.

1.2 The Nisnevich families give a covering-family presentation: identities cover; residue-field-preserving lifts compose; and a lift of $x$ base-changes to a lift of any point $v$ over $x$ because its residue tensor is $\kappa(x)\otimes_{\kappa(x)}\kappa(v)\cong\kappa(v)$. Étaleness is preserved in these operations by [F5]. Zariski open covers are included by [F4]. The target $\mathcal G$ is a stack for this presentation. For a Nisnevich family $\{T_i\to T\}$, [F3] descends compatible lines and their isomorphisms effectively and fully faithfully. The descended quasi-coherent module is finite locally free; its rank is one since pullback to the jointly surjective family has rank one: on an affine chart where the descended module is free of rank $r$, its pullback is free of rank $r$, by $B\otimes_A A^r\cong B^r$; a point of the covering above each target point forces $r=1$. For compatible integer gradings $n_i$, a residue-field-preserving lift $u$ of $x\in T$ and any $v$ over $x$ have a common point on their overlap: [F5] identifies its residue tensor with $\kappa(x)\otimes_{\kappa(x)}\kappa(v)\cong\kappa(v)$. Compatibility therefore makes their integer values equal. Thus the $n_i$ define a unique function $n:T\to\mathbb Z$ with the prescribed pullbacks. Each degree locus is the union of images of the corresponding open degree loci in the $T_i$, so it is open by [F5]; hence $n$ is locally constant. The empty scheme has its unique grading and line up to unique isomorphism. Tensor, unit and sign symmetry descend with these data: their local formulas are preserved by pullback, and full faithfulness makes their compatibility equalities global. Taking nerves gives a sheaf of 1-truncated spaces, still denoted $\mathcal G$: for a sieve containing a covering family, a coherent family over that sieve supplies the preceding descent datum. Its descent object identifies with every member of the sieve after pulling back the cover; full faithfulness glues those identifications and their equations uniquely. Thus the covering-sieve condition of [F4] holds, as well as covering-family descent. [F1, F3, F4, F5, A1]

1.3 For a Picard groupoid $\mathcal P$ with unit $\mathbf 1$, define $T_n\mathcal P$ as follows. An object consists of labels $p_{ij}$ for $0\leq i\leq j\leq n$, with $p_{ii}=\mathbf 1$, and isomorphisms $\mu_{ijk}:p_{jk}\otimes p_{ij}\to p_{ik}$. For $i\leq j\leq k\leq l$, the two composites from $p_{kl}\otimes p_{jk}\otimes p_{ij}$ to $p_{il}$ agree after the specified associator. Repeated-index maps are the prescribed unitors. A morphism is a family of label isomorphisms, identity on repeated indices, commuting with every $\mu$. Precomposition by an order-preserving map of finite ordinals defines the simplicial operators; repeated vertices use the unit labels and unitors. The monoidal triangle and pentagon identities make these operators well defined, including on repeated-index tetrahedra, and precomposition makes their identities exact. Thus $T_\bullet\mathcal P$ is a simplicial groupoid with $T_0\mathcal P=*$ and $T_1\mathcal P=\mathcal P$. Restriction to consecutive edges is an equivalence $T_n\mathcal P\to\mathcal P^n$: extend labels by ordered parenthesized tensor products; associators give the $\mu$, and their pentagon gives the tetrahedron equations. Conversely those equations identify any interval label with this product, and uniquely extend each family of consecutive-edge isomorphisms. More generally restriction to two subsets of vertices meeting at a single vertex $s$ is an equivalence to the product of their label groupoids. To see essential surjectivity, regard each given label as an arrow from its first vertex to its second, with composition in the displayed tensor order. Choose inverse labels for the edges incident to $s$, and label each missing edge by the composite through $s$. Tensoring by any label is an equivalence, so the cancellation isomorphisms can be chosen with the triangle identities; associativity and cancellation give all required tetrahedron equations. The original triangle data identify this extension with the given labels on each subset. For full faithfulness, an isomorphism on the two subsets determines the missing-edge isomorphisms uniquely from their triangles through $s$, since tensoring is fully faithful; their remaining compatibilities follow from the tetrahedron equations. This argument includes $s$ outside the interval of a missing edge by solving its triangle through the same tensor equivalence. Nerves therefore satisfy the restriction-square criterion in [F8], since the common-vertex groupoid is terminal. Put $B_\otimes\mathcal P=|NT_\bullet\mathcal P|$. Effectiveness supplies a canonical equivalence $\eta_{\mathcal P}:N\mathcal P\to\Omega B_\otimes\mathcal P$. This uses the target's explicit groupoid object, with no comparison to a crossed-complex model. [F1, F7, F8, construct]

2.1 For every ring map $A\to B$, termwise tensor takes finite projectives to finite projectives and preserves split cofibrations, their pushouts and contractible cones, so it is exact on $\mathcal W(A)$. Use the actual models in compatible larger universes before making any small presentations. For $A\to B\to C$, the comparison $C\otimes_B(B\otimes_A P)\cong C\otimes_A P$ sends $c\otimes(b\otimes p)$ to $cb\otimes p$. Its units and associativity equations hold on elementary tensors, hence on all modules and chain maps. Direct-sum comparisons are the canonical maps on the summands; their unit and associativity equations hold on the inclusions of each summand. These are the coproduct comparisons retained in the S-construction and Picard model. These define a pseudofunctor of relative categories, not just a list of ringwise functors. Quasi-isomorphism markings are saturated because they are inverse images of cohomology isomorphisms; tensor preserves them by the contraction argument. The relative associated-sheaf functors into the K-flat perfect family are connected by the canonical chain isomorphisms in [F6]. Their composition equation also holds before localization: for $A\to B\to C$, both comparisons send the pulled-back elementary tensor to $cb\otimes p$; identity comparisons give $1\otimes p\mapsto p$. Naturality in module maps makes these chain comparisons commute with every differential and with split cofiber diagrams. Thus the Grothendieck constructions give a map of relative families preserving cocartesian arrows. Over invertible base maps the transports are relative equivalences, and the other marked-family conditions in [F7] follow from the preceding marking checks. Equivalently apply its localization functor to this whole transformation of marked diagrams. This gives a homotopy-coherent natural transformation $e:\mathcal W(-)_\infty\to\widehat{\operatorname{Perf}}(\operatorname{Spec}(-))$; its components are exact equivalences by [F6]. The target is exactly the restriction of the specified common family, not an unrelated pullback diagram. [F1, F2, F6, F7, A1, step 1.1]

2.2 For $X\in\mathcal C$, let $\mathcal A_X$ be the category of all affine schemes $U$ in $\mathcal C$ equipped with any morphism $U\to X$, and triangles over $X$ as morphisms. Here the homotopy limit of this groupoid diagram means the nerve of the groupoid of objects $g_U\in\mathcal G(U)$ and isomorphisms $h^*g_U\cong g_V$ for every $h:V\to U$ over $X$, satisfying identity and composition equations with the canonical pullback comparisons; morphisms are compatible families of isomorphisms. This constructs an inverse equivalence of groupoids, hence of their nerves, naturally in $X$. Restricting or repeating indices restricts or repeats exactly those quotient and determinant diagrams, so the $q_{A,n}$ form a simplicial functor. 3.1 First normalize each strong symmetric tensor functor $F$ at the unit: replace $F(\mathbf 1)$ by the target unit, and conjugate its arrow and tensor maps by its unit isomorphism at that object. This produces an isomorphic strictly unital tensor functor. Normalize tensor natural isomorphisms by the same conjugations; their component at the unit is then identity. Retain the induced composition comparisons between normalized functors, rather than asserting that normalization commutes strictly with composition. Their coherence equations follow by conjugation from the original equations. The construction $T_\bullet$ now acts on these normalized functors: apply the functor to labels and compose its tensor comparisons with the images of $\mu$. Strict unitality makes this commute with repeating indices, including off-diagonal labels created by a repetition. The tensor coherence equations preserve the tetrahedron and unit equations. A tensor natural isomorphism acts componentwise on labels, and its defining tensor and unit equations make it a morphism of these simplicial groupoids. For $A\to B$, the KM base-change isomorphisms give a natural isomorphism between $q_{B,\bullet}S_\bullet(B\otimes_A-)$ and $T_\bullet(g_{AB})q_{A,\bullet}$, where $g_{AB}$ is pullback of graded lines. They commute with every $\mu$ by KM's base-change compatibility with actual cofiber diagrams. For $A\to B\to C$, the two composites agree on every label and every additivity map by KM's pullback-composition law and the tensor comparisons checked in step 2.1; their identity equations hold as well. These are pseudonatural maps over the product of the affine index category and the simplicial index category. The Grothendieck-construction convention of [F7] therefore gives transformations of the whole coherent diagrams. Taking nerves and geometric realizations is performed on these diagrams. Explicitly, for ordinary category-valued diagrams, a string of natural transformations gives a functor on the product with $[m]$; its nerve is a map on the product with $\Delta^m$.
