## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and compatible Grothendieck universe bounds, with $\omega$ in the smaller universe. Their existence is a standing size assumption. Let $F=\mathbb Q$ or $F=\mathbb F_p$ for a prime $p$. Work on the bounded big Nisnevich site of qcqs $F$-schemes and its slices, using the same site for derived evaluation and for line bundles. Here qcqs means quasi-compact and quasi-separated. All complexes and maps have their supplied stable derived enhancement. For a cochain complex, $C[a]^q=C^{q+a}$ with differential $(-1)^a d_C^{q+a}$; write $HC$ for its Eilenberg–Mac Lane realization and $\Omega^\infty$ for the zeroth space of a spectrum.

For every such scheme $Y$ and $n\geq0$, put $A_n(Y)=H(\mathbb Z(n)_{\rm mot}(Y)[2n])$, using the actual EM graded-piece identifications and products, and put $\mathbb Z(n)_{\rm mot}=0$ and $A_n=0$ for $n<0$. These form a natural unital commutative graded algebra in spectra: the unit $\mathbb S\to A_0$ and products $A_a\wedge A_b\to A_{a+b}$ include all associativity, symmetry and unit coherences. Equivalently the grading uses the convolution product $(A\otimes B)_n=\bigoplus_{a+b=n}A_a\wedge B_b$.

Let $\mathcal{Pic}(Y)$ be the nerve of the groupoid of invertible $\mathcal O_Y$-modules and their isomorphisms, pointed by $\mathcal O_Y$. For $\mathbb G_m(U)=\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_U)^\times$, use the natural pointed equivalence $\mathcal{Pic}(Y)\simeq\Omega^\infty H(R\Gamma_{\rm Nis}(Y,\mathbb G_m)[1])$ identifying the loop sheaf by the identity on $\mathbb G_m$. Shifting the actual EM map $c_1:R\Gamma_{\rm Nis}(Y,\mathbb G_m)[-1]\to\mathbb Z(1)_{\rm mot}(Y)$ by two, realizing it and taking zeroth spaces therefore gives a natural pointed map $\mathcal{Pic}\to\Omega^\infty A_1$. Its map on components is the EM first Chern class.

For each integer $d\geq0$, let $\pi:\mathbb P^d_Y\to Y$ be standard relative projective space. In the line convention it classifies rank-one subbundles of $\mathcal O_Y^{d+1}$; $\mathcal O(-1)$ is the tautological subline and $\mathcal O(1)$ its dual. On $\mathbb P^1_Y$ take $\infty_Y=[1:0]$. The first standard basis vector trivializes $\infty_Y^*\mathcal O(-1)$, hence its dual trivializes $\infty_Y^*\mathcal O(1)$. Applying the pointed Picard map gives $b=c_1(\mathcal O(1))$ with this specified nullhomotopy at infinity. For every integer $j$, the actual complex map is an equivalence:
$$\bigoplus_{m=0}^{d}\mathbb Z(j-m)_{\rm mot}(Y)[-2m]\xrightarrow{\sum_{m=0}^{d}c_1(\mathcal O(1))^m\pi^*}\mathbb Z(j)_{\rm mot}(\mathbb P^d_Y).$$
For $j<0$ every term is zero. More generally, for every finite locally free $E$ of constant rank $d+1>0$, the same formula holds for the line projectivization $P_Y^{\rm lin}(E)$ with its dual tautological line. It also holds for the quotient projectivization $Q_Y(E)=\operatorname{Proj}_Y\operatorname{Sym}(E)$ with its tautological quotient $\mathcal O_Q(1)$: use $Q_Y(E)\simeq P_Y^{\rm lin}(E^\vee)$, which identifies the two $\mathcal O(1)$ lines without a sign change. These total schemes are qcqs.

For a field extension $F\subseteq k$ and a qcqs $k$-scheme $S$, all these structures restrict naturally to $\mathrm{Sm}^{\rm qcqs}_S$, defined here as the full category of smooth $S$-schemes whose underlying schemes are qcqs, with $S$-morphisms as arrows. No finite-generation hypothesis on $k/F$ or smoothness hypothesis on $S$ is imposed.
