## Statement
Assume the Axiom of Choice and the size conventions of the smooth-point construction. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and $X$ a normal, pure-dimensional affine $k$-scheme of finite type and dimension $3$. The smooth-point subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$, generated by the perfect residue-sheaf classes of smooth closed points, is uniquely divisible: multiplication by every positive integer is an isomorphism. In particular multiplication by $2$ is an isomorphism.

More generally, if normal affine $X$ has dimension $3$ but has lower-dimensional components, let $X_3$ be the union of its three-dimensional components. Its zero-extended subgroup $F^3_{\mathrm{sm,top}}K_0(X)=(i_3)_*F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X_3)$ is uniquely divisible. This assertion does not include smooth points on lower-dimensional components in fixed weight $3$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The field and scheme hypotheses, choice and size conventions in the statement.

**Goal:** Prove unique divisibility in the pure case and for the stated zero-extended subgroup, including the multiplier $2$.

[F1] [thm-author-b87d598e83f3-krishna-cycle-input](item-thm-author-b87d598e83f3-krishna-cycle-input) gives unique divisibility of the Levine–Weibel group and a kernel killed by $2$ for its actual cycle map on a reduced pure affine threefold over $k$.

[F2] [rem-levine-weibel-cycle-image](item-rem-levine-weibel-cycle-image) identifies the actual cycle-map image with the smooth-point subgroup, using the residue-sheaf point classes. [def-smooth-point-filtration](item-def-smooth-point-filtration) defines the latter for pure schemes.

[F3] [lem-author-b87d598e83f3-cycle-image-roots](item-lem-author-b87d598e83f3-cycle-image-roots) gives unique divisibility of an image when its domain is uniquely divisible and its kernel is torsion.

[F4] [lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope](item-lem-author-80f982ded420-dimension-weight-scope) supplies the pure top-dimensional stratum and the zero-extended subgroup; [lem-author-80f982ded420-clopen-filtered-products](item-lem-author-80f982ded420-clopen-filtered-products) makes zero extension an injective coordinate inclusion. [lem-author-b87d598e83f3-isomorphism-transfers-roots](item-lem-author-b87d598e83f3-isomorphism-transfers-roots) transports unique divisibility across an isomorphism.

## Proof
1.1 A normal finite-type affine scheme is reduced, so the pure $X$ satisfies [F1]. The kernel killed by $2$ is torsion. Applying [F3] to the cycle map yields unique divisibility of its image. By [F2] that image is exactly $F^3_{\mathrm{sm}}K_0(X)$ with the stated point-class normalization. Thus multiplication by every positive integer, in particular $2$, is an isomorphism on that subgroup. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 In the dimension-only case, [F4] makes $X_3$ a pure normal affine threefold and identifies zero extension with an injective coordinate inclusion. Step 1.1 applies to $X_3$; its zero-extension map is an isomorphism onto the subgroup defined in the statement. The transport result in [F4] therefore gives unique divisibility there. The definition selects top-dimensional components, so it makes no fixed-weight assertion about points on smaller components. [F4, step 1.1] ∎
