### Research insight 01 · An explicit contraction rate after entry into a common arc

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proved

Let $A=(A_{x,y})$ be a fixed symmetric nonnegative weight matrix on $n\ge2$ vertices whose positive off-diagonal support is connected. Put $D=\operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf1)$ and $L=D-A$, and let $\lambda_2(L)$ denote its second-smallest eigenvalue. Let $\theta:[T,\infty)\to\mathbb R^V$ solve the homogeneous rotating-frame equations $\dot\theta_x=-\sum_y A_{x,y}\sin(\theta_x-\theta_y)$. Suppose the chosen real lifts have diameter $\gamma=\max_x\theta_x(T)-\min_x\theta_x(T)<\pi$. Then the initial interval remains invariant, the mean $\bar\theta=n^{-1}\sum_x\theta_x(T)$ is conserved, and, writing $E_T=\|\theta(T)-\bar\theta\mathbf1\|_2$ and $r=\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)>0$, one has $\|\theta(t)-\bar\theta\mathbf1\|_2\le E_Te^{-r(t-T)}$. Here $\operatorname{sinc}(u)=\sin(u)/u$, with value $1$ at zero. For $E_T>0$, an error tolerance $\varepsilon>0$ is therefore attained by $T+r^{-1}\max\{0,\log(E_T/\varepsilon)\}$. If additionally $d^*>0$, $c^->-1$ and $\Delta_L=L-d^*I+(d^*/n)J\succeq c^-d^*I$, then $\lambda_2(L)\ge(1+c^-)d^*$, giving the certified rate $(1+c^-)d^*\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)$. This includes the article’s two-sided expanders satisfying these conditions.

#### Argument and next steps

Symmetry conserves the arithmetic mean. For $y=\theta-\bar\theta\mathbf1$, pairwise cancellation gives $\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|y\|_2^2=-\sum_{x<z}A_{x,z}(y_x-y_z)\sin(y_x-y_z)$. ### Research insight 02 · The random-initialization cost of an arc-only argument

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proposed

Let $n\ge2$ and let $(\theta_x)_{x\in V}$ be independent uniform phases on $S^1=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$, where $|V|=n$. For $0<D<\pi$, let $C_D$ be the event that all phases lie in some closed clockwise arc of angular length $D$. Then $\mathbb P(C_D)=n(D/(2\pi))^{n-1}$. Moreover, $\mathbb P(\bigcup_{0<D<\pi}C_D)=n/2^{n-1}$. Consequently, a criterion requiring initial containment in an arc shorter than a semicircle can apply directly to at most $n/2^{n-1}$ of these initial states, even if the arc and its permitted length are selected after observing the phases. This bound tends to zero exponentially as $n\to\infty$.

#### Why it matters

The authors ask how quickly a random initial state synchronizes in Section 7, PDF p. 39. The calculation quantifies how few uniform initial states already satisfy a short-arc hypothesis. A contraction estimate under that hypothesis therefore needs an additional argument controlling entry into the arc region before it can address typical random initialization. The benchmark is independent of graph expansion.

#### Argument and next steps

For each vertex $x$, consider the event that every phase lies in the clockwise $D$-arc starting at $\theta_x$. Any covering arc can be shifted until its starting point is an occupied phase. Away from coincidences, two different occupied starting points cannot both work: their clockwise separations would each be at most $D$, although those separations sum to $2\pi$. Each anchored event has probability $(D/(2\pi))^{n-1}$ by product integration. Their probabilities therefore add. Increasing $D$ to $\pi$ gives the probability of fitting in any arc strictly shorter than a semicircle. This bounds the applicability of any criterion requiring such initial containment, while leaving subsequent entry and convergence unrestricted.

#### Proof sketch or overall strategy

Use a direct decomposition into occupied-starting-point events. Prove their equivalence to arc containment and their disjointness outside a null set, compute each event by finite product integration, and pass to the increasing union of all strictly subsemicircular arc events.

#### Numbered argument

1. Work on $[0,2\pi)^V$ with the product of normalized angular-length probability measures, identifying coordinates modulo $2\pi$. Every singleton has measure zero. For distinct vertices $x,y$, the section of $\{\theta_x=\theta_y\}$ at a fixed $\theta_x$ is a singleton in the $y$ coordinate, so product integration gives probability zero. A finite union shows that all phases are distinct almost surely. [hyp-uniform-phase-law, math:thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces, math:def-measure]
2. For each $x\in V$, define $A_x(D)$ by requiring $(\theta_y-\theta_x)\bmod 2\pi\in[0,D]$ for every $y\ne x$. These are Borel events because modular subtraction is piecewise continuous. Clearly $A_x(D)\subseteq C_D$. Conversely, lift any covering clockwise $D$-arc to a real interval $[a,a+D]$, choose the smallest occupied lift $b$, and shift the interval to $[b,b+D]$. All occupied lifts remain inside, so $C_D=\bigcup_{x\in V}A_x(D)$. This also proves measurability of the initially existential event $C_D$. [def-clockwise-covering-arc]
3. On the distinct-phase event, suppose $A_x(D)$ and $A_y(D)$ both hold for $x\ne y$. Write $a=(\theta_y-\theta_x)\bmod 2\pi\in(0,2\pi)$. The two anchored conditions imply $a\le D$ and $2\pi-a\le D$, hence $2\pi\le2D<2\pi$, a contradiction. Thus the anchored events are pairwise disjoint outside the null set established in step 1. **Solution scope:** partial

**Evidence status:** Proposed approach

Consider the article’s homogeneous, unnormalized dynamics on a finite connected undirected graph with $n\ge2$, symmetric adjacency matrix $A$, degree matrix $D$ and Laplacian $L=D-A$. In the rotating frame a real lift satisfies $\dot\phi_x=-\sum_y A_{x,y}\sin(\phi_x-\phi_y)$. Suppose that at time $T$ its diameter is $\gamma<\pi$. Set $\bar\phi=n^{-1}\sum_x\phi_x(T)$, $R=\|\phi(T)-\bar\phi\mathbf1\|_2$, and $k=\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)$, where $\operatorname{sinc}(u)=\sin(u)/u$ and $\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1$. Then $\|\phi(t)-\bar\phi\mathbf1\|_2\le R e^{-k(t-T)}$ for every $t\ge T$. For $R>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$, permanent error at most $\varepsilon$ is guaranteed by $T+k^{-1}\max\{0,\log(R/\varepsilon)\}$. If $R=0$, synchronization already holds. The one-vertex case is already synchronized. More generally, fix $0<\gamma<\pi$ and let $T_\gamma$ be the first time the phases lie in a closed arc of length $\gamma$. Let $\tau_\varepsilon$ be the first time after which their flat-torus distance to the synchronized set remains at most $\varepsilon$. For every $s\ge0$, $\Pr\{\tau_\varepsilon>s+b\}\le\Pr\{T_\gamma>s\}$, where $b=[\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)]^{-1}\max\{0,\log(\sqrt n\,\gamma/(2\varepsilon))\}$. On an article-type two-sided expander with $d^*>0$ and $c^->-1$, one may replace $\lambda_2(L)$ in these sufficient upper bounds by $(1+c^-)d^*$.

#### Argument and next steps

The extreme lifted phases move inward while their spread is below $\pi$, so the containing interval is invariant. Symmetry preserves the lifted mean. Pairing the two orientations of each edge gives a quadratic dissipation identity; the sine-sector inequality and the Laplacian Rayleigh bound yield $q'\le-2kq$ for the squared centered norm $q$. An integrating factor proves the exponential estimate. At short-arc entry, the variance bound $q\le n\gamma^2/4$ makes the remaining time independent of the particular entering configuration. Applying this deterministic implication on the event $T_\gamma\le s$ proves the probability transfer. The numbered argument below also verifies an exact complete-graph family whose entrance times diverge near an antipodal two-cluster equilibrium.
