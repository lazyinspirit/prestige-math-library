## 1. Research insights beyond the article

### Research insight 01 · An explicit contraction rate after entry into a common arc

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proposed

Let $A$ be a fixed symmetric nonnegative weight matrix on $n\ge2$ vertices whose positive off-diagonal support is connected. Put $D=\operatorname{diag}(A\mathbf1)$ and $L=D-A$, and let $\lambda_2(L)$ denote its second-smallest eigenvalue. Consider continuous real lifts solving the homogeneous first-order Kuramoto system in the rotating frame, $\dot\theta_x=-\sum_y A_{x,y}\sin(\theta_x-\theta_y)$. Suppose their diameter at time $T$ is $\gamma<\pi$. Their initial interval remains invariant, their arithmetic mean $\bar\theta$ is conserved, and, for all $t\ge T$,
$$\|\theta(t)-\bar\theta\mathbf1\|_2\le e^{-\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)(t-T)}\|\theta(T)-\bar\theta\mathbf1\|_2,$$
where $\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)=\sin\gamma/\gamma$ and $\operatorname{sinc}(0)=1$. If $0<\varepsilon<\gamma$, their real-lift diameter is at most $\varepsilon$ by time
$$T+\frac{\log\!\bigl(\gamma\sqrt{n/2}/\varepsilon\bigr)}{\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)}.$$
For an $(n,d^*,\alpha,c^-,c^+)$-expander with $d^*>0$ and $c^->-1$, one may replace $\lambda_2(L)$ in these guarantees by $(1+c^-)d^*$. In particular, a $d$-regular adjacency expander with $d>0$ and $\alpha<1$ has guaranteed contraction rate $(1-\alpha)d\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)$. The factor $\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)$ is sharp for exponential norm bounds with prefactor one over the general connected weighted class.

#### Why it matters

The article asks how quickly a random initial state synchronizes. This separates that problem into time to enter a common arc shorter than a semicircle and a quantitatively controlled contraction afterward. Only a lower Laplacian bound is needed for the second stage.

The contribution here is an explicit specialization to the article’s expander parameters, a tolerance-time consequence, and a sharpness check. #### Argument and next steps

The maximum lift cannot increase and the minimum cannot decrease while their separation is below $\pi$; continuity preserves this regime. Symmetry cancels the sum of the derivatives, conserving the mean. For $y=\theta-\bar\theta\mathbf1$, pairing the two orientations of each edge gives $\frac12\frac{d}{dt}\|y\|_2^2=-\sum_{x<y}A_{x,y}(\theta_x-\theta_y)\sin(\theta_x-\theta_y)$. Since sinc decreases on $[0,\pi)$, this is at most $-\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)y^TLy$, hence at most $-\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)\|y\|_2^2$. Integration proves contraction. The inequalities $\operatorname{diam}(\theta)\le\sqrt2\|y\|_2$ and $\|y(T)\|_2\le\sqrt n\gamma/2$ give the tolerance time. Testing the assumed centered-Laplacian lower bound on $\mathbf1^\perp$ gives the expander rate. For two vertices joined with weight $a>0$, the phase difference satisfies $\dot\delta=-2a\sin\delta$; its initial logarithmic contraction rate is exactly $\lambda_2(L)\operatorname{sinc}(\gamma)$.

### Research insight 02 · The random-initialization cost of an arc-only argument

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proved

Let $n\ge2$ and let $(\theta_x)_{x\in V}$ be independent uniform phases on $\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$, where $|V|=n$. For $0<D<\pi$, let $C_D$ mean that all phases lie in some closed arc of angular length $D$. Then $q_n(D):=\mathbb P(C_D)=n(D/(2\pi))^{n-1}$. The probability of fitting in any arc of length strictly below $\pi$, allowing its width to be selected after observing the phases, is $n/2^{n-1}$. For independent full resamplings, the probability of meeting a fixed $C_D$ condition within $R$ trials is $1-(1-q_n(D))^R$, and the expected number of trials through the first success is $1/q_n(D)$. Consequently, polynomially many uniform starts have vanishing probability of satisfying any short-arc initialization certificate as $n\to\infty$, even when its permitted width approaches $\pi$.

#### Why it matters

The article asks how quickly a random initial state synchronizes. Established short-arc synchronization theorems control trajectories whose phases already occupy a suitable arc. This benchmark quantifies how rarely their arc hypothesis holds at a uniform start, independently of graph expansion. It identifies the need for an additional argument controlling entry into that region.

#### Originality

The coverage formula is classical: Golomb’s June 2002 puzzle-column solution explicitly states and proves it for arc widths at most a semicircle. The contribution here is its application to initialization certificates and the accompanying restart calculation. No worldwide novelty is claimed for either consequence. No such benchmark was found in the supplied article’s text searches or the examined original-PDF passages; this author did not perform an independent line-by-line novelty assessment of all 41 pages.

#### Argument and next steps

proof_strategy: direct

**Given:** The independent uniform phases and events $C_D$ in the statement; independent full resampling for the restart assertions.

[F1] Product integration computes probabilities by integrating measurable sections; probability measures are additive on disjoint events and continuous from below.

[F2] For $0\le r<1$, $\sum_{k\ge0}r^k=1/(1-r)$; nonnegative double sums may be interchanged.

1.1 Except on a null set of coincidences, a covering arc shorter than a semicircle has a unique first sampled point: the complementary empty gap exceeds a semicircle and is unique. For a specified first point, each other phase independently falls in its clockwise $D$-arc with probability $D/(2\pi)$. These anchoring events are disjoint, and their union is $C_D$ outside that null set. Hence $q_n(D)=n(D/(2\pi))^{n-1}$. [given, F1]

1.2 Increasing $D$ to $\pi$ gives probability $n/2^{n-1}$ for membership in some arc of width strictly below $\pi$. Across $R$ starts, the union bound therefore gives probability at most $Rn/2^{n-1}$ of encountering any such state. For $R\le Cn^k$ with fixed $C>0$ and integer $k\ge0$, this bound tends to zero. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 For fixed $D$, independence makes the probability of no success in $R$ trials equal to $(1-q_n(D))^R$. Thus the first successful trial $N_D$ has tail $\mathbb P(N_D>r)=(1-q_n(D))^r$. Summing this tail, justified by nonnegative summation, yields $\mathbb E N_D=1/q_n(D)$. These are initialization costs; they do not measure the subsequent evolution of a trajectory. [given, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

These events are Borel measurable, since modular interval conditions are finite unions of ordinary interval conditions in angular representatives. Coincident phases form a null set: after fixing one coordinate, coincidence restricts another coordinate to a singleton, of uniform measure zero. ## 2. Potentially fruitful questions

### Research question 01

#### Research question

Fix $0<a<0.0816$. For every positive-degree $d$-regular binary $(n,d,a)$-expander, let $T$ be the first rotating-frame time at which all phases fit in an arc of length $\pi/3$. From independent uniform initial phases, does a constant $C_a$ exist such that $\mathbb P(dT>C_a[\log n+\log(1/\delta)])\le\delta$ for every $0<\delta<1/2$, uniformly over these graphs?

#### Why it matters

This makes the authors’ synchronization-speed direction precise. The graph is deterministic; only the initial phases are random. The factor $d$ fixes the coupling normalization. Entry into a short common arc separates the difficult global transient from a subsequent deterministic contraction estimate. The lower bounds below show why dependence on both $n$ and the failure probability is needed.

#### Originality

Section 7, PDF p. 39, asks how quickly random initial states synchronize, without specifying this entrance event or logarithmic quantile. This formulation sharpens that direction. The accompanying lower bounds are elementary local deductions; no worldwide novelty is claimed for them. The literature check was targeted, not exhaustive.

#### Current knowledge

The uniform upper bound remains unresolved in this assessment. Targeted web searches concerning Kuramoto expanders, random initial phases and convergence or entrance times did not identify a verified resolution. Dörfler–Bullo Theorems 4.1 and 4.8 were checked in the primary PDF: their relevant estimates assume phase cohesion and do not establish random entry from the entire torus. #### Potential solution

**Solution scope:** partial

**Evidence status:** Proposed approach

Put $\gamma=\pi/3$ and $c_a=(1-a)\sin(\gamma)/\gamma$. Whenever $T<\infty$, compatible real lifts $u$ after entry satisfy $\|u(T+s)-\bar u\mathbf1\|_2\le e^{-c_a ds}\|u(T)-\bar u\mathbf1\|_2$ for $s\ge0$, where $\bar u$ is their conserved mean. Two necessary-scale bounds also hold. On the loopless complete graph $K_n$, eligible whenever $n-1\ge1/a$, for every $0<\eta<1$, $\mathbb P(dT\ge\frac{n-1}{2n}\log(3n\eta/4))\ge1-\eta$. On every graph in the question with $n\ge2$, $\mathbb P(dT>\tau)\ge6^{-(n-1)}e^{-2(n-1)\tau}$ for all $\tau\ge0$. Thus a uniform bound cannot omit the $\log n$ scale, and on a fixed admissible graph sufficiently small failure probabilities require a $\log(1/\delta)$ scale.

#### Argument and next steps

The common arc is forward invariant. Symmetry conserves the lifted mean, the sine sector bound controls energy dissipation, and expansion gives a mean-zero Laplacian gap of at least $(1-a)d$. For the complete-graph lower bound, the exact evolution of $\rho_1$ gives $|\rho_1(t)|\le e^{nt}|\rho_1(0)|$, whereas entry forces $|\rho_1(T)|\ge\sqrt3/2$; independent uniform phases have $\mathbb E|\rho_1(0)|^2=1/n$. For the delay-tail lower bound, an explicit positive-volume neighborhood of an antipodal equilibrium remains outside the entrance set for a prescribed time by a $2d$ Lipschitz comparison. The numbered argument supplies the details.

#### Proof sketch or overall strategy

Prove the post-entry contraction directly, then use complete-graph order parameters and explicit neighborhoods of antipodal equilibria to establish necessary scales. Finally isolate a sufficient uniform survival-volume estimate as a research target. That final target is unproved and is not counted as a solution.

#### Potential solution

**Solution scope:** full

**Evidence status:** Proposed approach

Assume countable choice. Start the uniform edge-addition process on $n\ge2$ vertices and put $N=\binom n2$ and $\tau=\min\{m:G(n,m)\text{ is connected}\}$. Then $\mathbb P(\forall m\in\{\tau,\ldots,N\},\ G(n,m)\text{ is globally synchronizing})\to1$. For each graph held fixed, this means convergence of $\dot\theta_i=\sum_jA_{ij}\sin(\theta_j-\theta_i)$ to a fully synchronized point for normalized-volume almost every initial phase.

#### Argument and next steps

Use the independent-clock representation of the process. An isolated-vertex estimate puts connectivity after a deterministic spectral-onset time with high probability. A uniform normalized-spectrum theorem then bounds every subsequent graph’s nonzero condition number below two. Applying the deterministic synchronization criterion and transferring through all edge-arrival times gives the simultaneous conclusion.

#### Proof sketch or overall strategy

Import the uniform process spectrum and deterministic synchronization criterion, then verify their composition through the existing clock coupling and isolated-vertex lemma. The essential point is uniform control on one event, allowing evaluation at random arrival times without assuming synchronization is monotone.

#### Numbered argument

1. Assign independent uniform labels $U_e\in[0,1)$ to all possible edges and set $T_e=-\log(1-U_e)$. The graph $H_t$ containing edges with $T_e\le t$ has edge probability $p(t)=1-e^{-t}$. Almost surely the clocks are distinct and their order is uniform. At the $m$th arrival $s_m$, $H_{s_m}=G(n,m)$, and connectivity persists after $s_\tau$. [math:def-countable-choice, def-expander-prereq-edge-order-process-coupling, lem-author-fc0d6c95270f-exponential-process-transfer]
2. Put $p_0=3\log n/(4n)$ and $t_0=-\log(1-p_0)$ for sufficiently large $n$. The isolated-vertex lemma gives $\mathbb P(H_{t_0}\text{ is disconnected})\to1$, hence $s_\tau>t_0$ with high probability. Its quantitative certificate is $\mathbb P(X=0)\le1/(nq)+p_0/(1-p_0)$, where $X$ counts isolated vertices and $q=(1-p_0)^{n-1}$; eventually $nq\ge n^{1/8}$. [lem-author-fc0d6c95270f-isolated-vertex-before-onset, step:1]
3. Apply the uniform spectral input with $k=1$ and $\delta=1/4$. On one event of probability tending to one, its estimate holds for every $t\ge t_0$. Whenever $H_t$ is connected, deleting isolated vertices changes nothing. Thus its nonzero normalized-Laplacian eigenvalues satisfy $|1-\lambda_i|<C/\sqrt{(n-1)p(t)}$ simultaneously. [input-hkp-process-spectrum, def-author-fc0d6c95270f-normalized-laplacian, step:1]
4. Intersect the two high-probability events from steps 2 and 3; their intersection still has probability tending to one by the union bound on their complements. On it, every $t\ge s_\tau$ is a connected time after $t_0$. Write $\varepsilon_n=C/\sqrt{(n-1)p_0}$, which tends to zero. For sufficiently large $n$, $\varepsilon_n<1/3$, and every such graph satisfies $\lambda_n(\mathcal L)/\lambda_2(\mathcal L)\le(1+\varepsilon_n)/(1-\varepsilon_n)<2$. Connectedness also gives positive degrees and $\lambda_2(L)>0$. [def-author-fc0d6c95270f-normalized-laplacian, prop-expander-prereq-laplacian-support-kernel, input-elementary-event-calculus, step:2, step:3]
5. The deterministic synchronization criterion now applies to every $H_t$ with $t\ge s_\tau$, with global coupling constant one. Evaluate this single pathwise conclusion at each $s_m$ for $\tau\le m\le N$. Step 1 identifies these graphs with the uniform edge-addition process, proving the required simultaneous event.
