## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system and $J\subseteq S$. Write $W_J$ for the subgroup generated by $J$ and $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$. The relative arrangement groupoid $\mathcal D=\mathcal D(J)$ has relative chambers as objects and is generated by directed crossings of adjacent chambers and their inverses, with geodesic paths having the same endpoints identified. The universal wall groupoid $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}$ has chamber labels $(x,I)$ as objects and exactly one arrow between any ordered pair, labelled $x^{-1}y$ from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$. The Brink–Howlett groupoid $\mathcal{BH}$ has the associates of $J$ as objects, where $I$ is an associate when $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$, and arrows $I\to K$ are the elements carrying the simple-root set of $K$ onto that of $I$.

For arbitrary Coxeter type, the natural functors $H:\mathcal D\to\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}$ and $\mathcal F:\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}\to\mathcal{BH}$ exist; $H$ is the identity on chamber objects and $\mathcal F$ sends $(x,I)$ to $I$ and the arrow labelled $x^{-1}y$ to the same element of $W$.

Suppose now that $W$ is finite, also called spherical type. Let $A$ be the associated Artin–Tits group, $A_I$ its standard parabolic subgroup, and $\pi:A\to W$ the homomorphism sending each Artin generator to its corresponding simple reflection. The reduced ribbon groupoid $\mathcal R$ has the associates of $J$ as objects and is generated by elementary ribbons $\mu_{a,I}=\Delta_I^{-1}\Delta_{I\cup\{a\}}$ and their inverses, where $a\in S\setminus I$ and $\Delta_L$ is the positive lift of the longest element of $W_L$. The functor $\mathcal G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal R$ sends $(x,I)$ to $I$ and each directed crossing indexed by $a$ at $(x,I)$ to $\mu_{a,I}$.

The homomorphism $\pi$ induces a functor $P:\mathcal R\to\mathcal{BH}$, identity on objects, sending $\mu_{a,I}$ to the corresponding wall-crossing element $v_{a,I}$. The square commutes: $P\mathcal G=\mathcal F H$. Both vertical functors $\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal F$ are normal coverings with deck group isomorphic to $N(W,J)$. A covering has a unique lift from each specified source object for each arrow starting at its image; it is normal when its deck transformations act transitively on a fibre, and a deck transformation is an automorphism over the covering.

At the chamber labelled $(1,J)$ and its image $J$, the respective vertex groups of $\mathcal D$, $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}$, $\mathcal R$ and $\mathcal{BH}$ are isomorphic to $\pi_1(\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C},x)$, the trivial group, $N(A,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)/A_J$, and $N(W,J)$. Here $x$ is a chamber basepoint corresponding to $(1,J)$ under the arrangement-groupoid equivalence, and $\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$ is the relative complexified hyperplane complement.

## Proof


**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system and subset $J$ in the Statement. For the square and vertex identifications assume additionally that $W$ is finite.

[F1] The arrangement groupoid is generated by directed adjacent crossings and their inverses. The functor $H$ maps a crossing from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$ to the unique universal-wall arrow with those endpoints. [def-arrangement-groupoids-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-arrangement-groupoids-artin-tits-normalisers) [rem-arrangement-to-universal-artin-tits-normalisers](item-rem-arrangement-to-universal-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F2] The functor $\mathcal F$ exists in arbitrary type and sends a universal-wall arrow labelled $x^{-1}y$ to that element in $\mathcal{BH}$. It is a universal covering with deck group $N(W,J)$; its adjacent-crossing arrows project to the corresponding elements $v_{a,I}$. [thm-universal-brink-howlett-cover-artin-tits-normalisers](item-thm-universal-brink-howlett-cover-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F3] In spherical type $\mathcal G$ sends a crossing indexed by $a$ to $\mu_{a,I}$ and is a normal covering with deck group $N(W,J)$. [def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers) [cor-deligne-normal-cover-artin-tits-normalisers](item-cor-deligne-normal-cover-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F4] The elementary ribbon $\mu_{a,I}$ is the positive reduced-word lift of $v_{a,I}$. The reduced ribbon groupoid is generated by these arrows and their inverses. [lem-ribbon-generator-lift-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-ribbon-generator-lift-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-reduced-ribbon-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-reduced-ribbon-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F5] In spherical type $\mathcal D$ is equivalent to the fundamental groupoid of $\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$, and the vertex group of $\mathcal R$ at $J$ maps isomorphically to $N(A,J)$. [thm-spherical-deligne-topology-artin-tits-normalisers](item-thm-spherical-deligne-topology-artin-tits-normalisers) [thm-artin-normaliser-splitting-artin-tits-normalisers](item-thm-artin-normaliser-splitting-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F6] The vertex group of $\mathcal{BH}$ at $J$ is the subgroup $N_J$ permuting the simple roots indexed by $J$, and the normaliser quotient maps $N_J$ isomorphically to $N(W,J)$. Every universal-wall hom-set is a singleton. [def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-normaliser-complement-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-normaliser-complement-artin-tits-normalisers) [cor-coxeter-normaliser-splitting-artin-tits-normalisers](item-cor-coxeter-normaliser-splitting-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-universal-wall-groupoid-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-universal-wall-groupoid-artin-tits-normalisers)



[F7] The Artin–Tits group has its standard Artin generators and a homomorphism $\pi:A\to W$ sending each generator to its corresponding simple reflection. The positive reduced-word lift $\sigma_w$ satisfies $\pi(\sigma_w)=w$. [def-artin-pure-lifts-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-artin-pure-lifts-artin-tits-normalisers)

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Construct the bottom functor, prove the square commutes and establish the covering and vertex-group assertions.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], $H$ and $\mathcal F$ exist under the finite-rank hypotheses without assuming $W$ finite. The action of $\mathcal F$ on objects and labels is as stated. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 Assume $W$ finite. For each elementary ribbon, its image under $\pi$ is $v_{a,I}$ by [F4], and this is a Brink–Howlett arrow with the corresponding endpoints by [F2]. A composite of elementary ribbons and inverses therefore maps to a composite of such arrows and inverses. Because each ribbon arrow is an element of $A$ tagged by its endpoints and $\pi$ is a homomorphism, equal ribbon arrows have equal images in $W$; hence this assignment is well defined and preserves identities and composition. It defines the asserted functor $P$. [F2, F4, F7, given]

1.3 The left vertical functor is the normal cover of [F3]. The right vertical functor is universal by [F2], hence normal, with the stated deck group. This establishes both covering assertions. [F2, F3]

2.1 On a chamber $(x,I)$ both composites have value $I$. For a directed crossing indexed by $a$, $P\mathcal G$ gives $\pi(\mu_{a,I})=v_{a,I}$ by [F3] and [F4], while $\mathcal F H$ gives the same element by [F1] and [F2]. Functors preserve inverses and composition, and the crossings and their inverses generate $\mathcal D$; therefore the two composites agree on every arrow. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F7, step 1.2]

3.1 By [F5], the arrangement-groupoid equivalence identifies the vertex group at the chosen chamber with the fundamental group at its corresponding basepoint, and identifies the ribbon vertex group at $J$ with $N(A,J)$. The unique endomorphism of a universal-wall object is its identity, so that vertex group is trivial. Finally [F6] identifies the Brink–Howlett vertex group at $J$ with $N(W,J)$. Together with steps 1.1–1.3 and 2.1, this proves the statement. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 1.3] ∎
