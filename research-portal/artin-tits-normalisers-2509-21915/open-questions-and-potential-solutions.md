### Research insight 01 · Normalizer cohomology from the restricted intersection lattice

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proved

Work in ZFC. Let $(W,S)$ be a finite Coxeter system, $J\subseteq S$, $A$ its Artin–Tits group, and $P=\ker(A\to W)$. Put $G=N(W,J)$ and let $\mathcal H^J$ consist of the distinct proper restricted root hyperplanes in $E=\Theta_J$. With trivial rational coefficients there are graded-algebra isomorphisms $H^*(N(P,J);\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{OS}(\mathcal H^J;\mathbb Q)$ and $H^*(N(A,J);\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{OS}(\mathcal H^J;\mathbb Q)^G$. Consequently $b_1(N(P,J))=|\mathcal H^J|$ and $b_1(N(A,J))=|\mathcal H^J/G|$. In the article’s $D_4$, $J=\{2\}$ example, the complete rational Poincaré polynomials are respectively $1+7t+15t^2+9t^3$ and $1+4t+6t^2+3t^3$. The pure algebra is determined by the intersection lattice; the quotient algebra additionally requires the induced $G$-action on that lattice.

#### Why it matters

The geometric normaliser model yields computable cohomology rings even when the restricted arrangement is not a reflection arrangement. The D₄ calculation demonstrates that the method determines higher Betti numbers as well as abelianization ranks.

2. For each hyperplane choose a defining complex-linear form $\ell_H$ and put $c_H=\ell_H^*u$, where $u\in H^1(\mathbb C^*;\mathbb Q)$ evaluates to one on a positive circle. The finite CW model and cellular cohomology imply $H^*(M;\mathbb Q)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb C\cong H^*(M;\mathbb C)$: the finite cellular cochain matrices extend scalars, and kernels and images commute with field extension. Send $e_H$ to $c_H$. After extension, this is the normalized logarithmic comparison. ### Research insight 02 · Vanishing Euler characteristic outside the zero-dimensional case

#### Insight

**Kind:** corollary

**Evidence status:** proved

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite Coxeter system, let $A$ be its Artin–Tits group, and let $P=\ker(A\to W)$. For $J\subsetneq S$, the groups $N(P,J)$ and $N(A,J)$ have finite classifying spaces and satisfy $\chi(N(P,J))=\chi(N(A,J))=0$. Consequently, every finite CW classifying model for either group has equally many even-dimensional and odd-dimensional cells. More precisely, choosing a restricted hyperplane equation $\ell$ gives $N(P,J)\cong\mathbb Z\times\pi_1(F)$, where $F=\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}\cap\{\ell=1\}$. The scalar loop also determines a central element of infinite order in $N(A,J)$. When $J=S$, both normaliser quotients are trivial and their Euler characteristics equal $1$.

#### Why it matters

The article constructs finite classifying spaces in spherical type. Their Euler characteristics provide a numerical constraint on any proposed finite classifying model. The scalar-loop description explains the vanishing geometrically and supplies additional information about the groups.

#### Argument and next steps

For $J\subsetneq S$, a simple root outside $J$ gives a nonzero defining functional $\ell$ for a hyperplane of the relative arrangement. Centrality gives an explicit homeomorphism $\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}\cong\mathbb C^*\times F$ by $z\mapsto(\ell(z),z/\ell(z))$. Since $F$ is a retract of a space of finite CW homotopy type, its rational Betti numbers are finite and eventually zero. The homology calculation for a product with a circle therefore makes the alternating sum vanish. The finite covering of the normaliser quotient multiplies Euler characteristic by $|N(W,J)|$, so its Euler characteristic also vanishes. The article's classifying-space identifications give the group assertions. The product identifies the scalar loop with the generator of a direct $\mathbb Z$ factor in the pure quotient; the descended scalar action makes its image central in the full quotient.

#### Proof sketch or overall strategy

Decone the nonempty central arrangement by normalising one linear equation. Derive the circle-product homology formula directly from Mayer–Vietoris, transfer Euler-characteristic vanishing through the finite cover, and apply the spherical classifying-space theorem. Track the scalar loop to obtain the additional group-theoretic conclusions.

#### Numbered argument

1. Put $E=\Theta_J\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, $M=\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$, $G=N(W,J)$ and $B=M/G$. Choose $s\in S\setminus J$. Because the simple roots form a basis, the functional $\ell(z)=z(\alpha_s)$ on $E$ is nonzero: the dual coordinate taking value $1$ on $\alpha_s$ and $0$ on the other simple roots belongs to $\Theta_J$. Thus $\ker\ell$ is one of the restricted hyperplanes. In particular, $\ell$ never vanishes on $M$. [D-data, D-arrangement]
2. Define $F=M\cap\{\ell=1\}$. Since every deleted hyperplane is linear, multiplication by any nonzero complex scalar preserves $M$. Consequently $h:M\to\mathbb C^*\times F$, $h(z)=(\ell(z),z/\ell(z))$, is a homeomorphism with inverse $(t,u)\mapsto tu$. The maps are continuous and their compositions are identities. The model input makes $M$ nonempty and path-connected, so $F$, its continuous image under normalisation, is also nonempty and path-connected. [step:1, D-arrangement, D-models]
3. The normalisation map $r:M\to F$, $r(z)=z/\ell(z)$, restricts to the identity on $F$. Hence inclusion induces an injection $H_n(F;\mathbb Q)\to H_n(M;\mathbb Q)$, because the composite with $r_*$ is the identity. Since $M$ has finite CW homotopy type, the numbers $a_n=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_n(F;\mathbb Q)$ are finite and vanish in sufficiently high degrees. [step:2, D-models, D-homology]
4. Radial contraction identifies the homotopy type of $M$ with $S^1\times F$. Cover $S^1$ by two open arcs whose intersection has two contractible components. In the resulting Mayer–Vietoris sequence, the map in degree $n$ from $H_n(F;\mathbb Q)^2$ to itself is $(a,b)\mapsto(a+b,-a-b)$, after identifying each arc component with $F$. Its kernel and cokernel are each isomorphic to $H_n(F;\mathbb Q)$. Exactness gives $b_n(M)=a_n+a_{n-1}$, with $a_{-1}=0$. The finite alternating sum therefore cancels: $\chi(M)=\sum_n(-1)^n(a_n+a_{n-1})=0$. This argument requires no finite CW assertion for $F$. [step:2, step:3, D-homology]
5. The covering $M\to B$ has finite degree $d=|G|$. Pull it back along a finite CW model $K\to B$. Homotopy lifting identifies the pullback total space with $M$ up to homotopy. Lifting the cells of $K$ gives exactly $d$ cells above each cell, so $\chi(M)=d\chi(K)=d\chi(B)$. Since $d>0$, step 4 implies $\chi(B)=0$. The identifications $M\simeq K(N(P,J),1)$ and $B\simeq K(N(A,J),1)$ now give both asserted group Euler characteristics. [step:4, D-models, D-covering, D-euler]
6. By the definition of Euler characteristic through cell counts, either finite classifying model has equal total numbers of even-dimensional and odd-dimensional cells. For the excluded case $J=S$, the root-basis definition gives $\Theta_J=0$ and the restricted arrangement is empty, so $M$ is a point. Directly, $A_J=A$ and $W_J=W$, hence $N(A,J)$ and $N(P,J)$ are trivial and have Euler characteristic $1$. [step:5, D-data, D-arrangement, D-euler]
7. For a basepoint $u\in F$, the product homeomorphism identifies the loop $c(t)=e^{2\pi it}u$ with the standard generator of the $\mathbb C^*$ factor. Coordinatewise loops and homotopies give $N(P,J)\cong\mathbb Z\times\pi_1(F,u)$. The linear $G$-action commutes with complex scalar multiplication, so scalar multiplication descends to $B$. For any based loop $\gamma$ in $B$, the square $(s,t)\mapsto e^{2\pi is}\gamma(t)$ has boundary the commutator of the projected scalar loop with $\gamma$; hence that scalar loop is central. Its order is infinite because $\pi_1(M)\to\pi_1(B)$ is injective and $c$ generates an infinite cyclic factor upstairs. [step:2, D-arrangement, D-models, D-circle-product, D-covering]

#### Potential solution

**Solution scope:** partial

**Evidence status:** Proposed approach

Work in ZFC with finite-rank Coxeter systems and the canonical geometric representation. Put $X(W,J)=(I_J\times I_J)\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal H^J}(H\times H)$, where $I_J=\operatorname{int}_{\Theta_J}(\mathrm{Cone}\cap\Theta_J)$. Let $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$, $N(A,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)/A_J$, and let $N(P,J)$ be the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ in $N(A,J)$. Say that a pair satisfies the relative conclusion when its algebraic and covering exact sequences are isomorphic with identity on $N(W,J)$, and $X(W,J)$ and its quotient are respectively classifying spaces for $N(P,J)$ and $N(A,J)$. This conclusion is closed under finite direct products of Coxeter systems. Consequently it holds for every finite product of pairs whose factors satisfy at least one of: $W_i$ is finite; $W_i$ is irreducible affine and $J_i=\varnothing$; $W_i$ is infinite dihedral and $J_i$ is a singleton; or $J_i=S_i$ with arbitrary finite-rank $W_i$. In the singleton infinite-dihedral case, $X(W_i,J_i)\cong(0,\infty)^2$ and all three normaliser quotients are trivial. In particular, every Coxeter system of rank at most two satisfies the conclusion for every $J$.
