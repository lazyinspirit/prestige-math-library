## 1. Research insights beyond the article

### Research insight 01 · Normalizer cohomology from the restricted intersection lattice

#### Insight

**Kind:** connection

**Evidence status:** proved

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter system, $A$ its Artin–Tits group, $P=\ker(A\to W)$, and $J\subseteq S$. Put $G=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$, $N(A,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)/A_J$, and let $N(P,J)$ be the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ in $N(A,J)$. Let $\mathcal A=\mathcal H^J$ be the set of distinct proper restrictions of root hyperplanes to $\Theta_J$. Then, as graded rational algebras, $H^*(N(P,J);\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{OS}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathcal A)$ and $H^*(N(A,J);\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{OS}_{\mathbb Q}(\mathcal A)^G$, where $g(e_H)=e_{gH}$. Consequently $b_1(N(P,J))=|\mathcal A|$ and $b_1(N(A,J))=|\mathcal A/G|$. For the article's $D_4$ example with trivalent $J=\{2\}$, the complete rational Poincaré polynomials are $P_{N(P,J)}(t)=1+7t+15t^2+9t^3$ and $P_{N(A,J)}(t)=1+4t+6t^2+3t^3$. Here $b_k(\Lambda)=\dim_{\mathbb Q}H^k(\Lambda;\mathbb Q)$ and $P_\Lambda(t)=\sum_k b_k(\Lambda)t^k$.

#### Why it matters

The spherical normaliser models turn a restricted arrangement, including one that is not a reflection arrangement, into computable group-cohomology data. The full quotient requires the intersection lattice together with the induced normaliser action; the unlabelled lattice alone does not determine its invariant algebra.

#### Argument and next steps

Apply the frozen spherical classifying-space theorem, then the natural [Orlik–Solomon comparison](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/561.fall.2021/past.articles/orlik.solomon.1980.pdf). Normalized logarithmic generators are rational winding classes, so the complex comparison descends to rational coefficients using a finite Salvetti model. For the finite regular cover, averaging invariant cochains identifies downstairs cohomology with upstairs invariants, multiplicatively. In D₄, suitable coordinates make the eight-element normaliser quotient act by independent sign changes. Its seven hyperplanes are three coordinate planes and four diagonal planes. Their nine rank-two intersections determine the pure polynomial; the fixed-lattice trace formula and averaging determine the quotient polynomial.

#### Numbered argument

1. Write $E=\Theta_J$, $M=E_{\mathbb C}\setminus\bigcup_{H\in\mathcal A}H_{\mathbb C}$ and $Y=M/G$. The spherical model input gives a regular $G$-cover $M\to Y$ and classifying-space models for $N(P,J)$ and $N(A,J)$ respectively. Thus their rational cohomology rings are those of $M$ and $Y$. For the ring comparison, the universal-cover singular chains form a free resolution; the front/back simplex diagonal defining the cup product is the same upstairs and downstairs. Applying equivariant Hom with trivial coefficients gives the usual classifying-space comparison, including its rational coefficient version. [D-choice, D-groups, D-arrangement, D-spherical-model, D-group-cohomology, D-cohomology-basics]
2. For each $H$, choose a nonzero complex linear form $\alpha_H$ with kernel $H_{\mathbb C}$. Let $\lambda_H=\alpha_H^*\eta$, where $\eta\in H^1(\mathbb C^*;\mathbb Q)$ evaluates to one on a positive circle. Its complex image is represented by $d\alpha_H/(2\pi i\alpha_H)$. The finite Salvetti model and finite cellular cochains give $H^*(M;\mathbb Q)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb C\cong H^*(M;\mathbb C)$. The complex Orlik–Solomon theorem therefore makes the rational dependency relations vanish and makes $e_H\mapsto\lambda_H$ an isomorphism: its kernel and cokernel vanish after the faithful field extension. For the left cohomological action $g\cdot u=(g^{-1})^*u$, multiplication of defining forms by nonzero constants does not change their logarithmic classes, so $g\lambda_H=\lambda_{gH}$. This proves the rational equivariant comparison. [D-os-definition, D-os-comparison, D-finite-salvetti, D-cohomology-basics, D-circle-generator, step:1]
3. Every singular simplex of $Y$ lifts to $M$, and its lifts form a $G$-torsor. Consequently pullback identifies $C^*(Y;\mathbb Q)$ with $C^*(M;\mathbb Q)^G$, compatibly with differentials and cup products. Averaging by $|G|^{-1}\sum_g g$ shows that taking invariants commutes with cohomology: average a cycle representing an invariant class for surjectivity, and average a primitive for injectivity. Hence $H^*(Y;\mathbb Q)\cong H^*(M;\mathbb Q)^G$ as graded algebras. This argument uses the finite cover directly and requires no unsupported equivariance assertion for a relative Salvetti equivalence. [D-cover-cochains, D-group-cohomology, D-cohomology-basics, step:1]
4. Combining steps 1–3 proves both asserted algebra isomorphisms. Distinct proper central hyperplanes have no dependent singleton or dependent pair, so the Orlik–Solomon ideal has no degree-one part. Its degree-one basis is therefore the set of $e_H$. The action permutes this basis, and an invariant vector has one independent coefficient per orbit. This proves both general first-Betti-number formulas, including the empty-arrangement case. [D-os-definition, step:1, step:2, step:3]
5. Use orthonormal coordinates with $\alpha_1=e_1-e_2$, $\alpha_2=e_2-e_3$, $\alpha_3=e_3-e_4$, $\alpha_4=e_3+e_4$, and Coxeter form one-half the dot product. The simple reflections generate exactly the even signed permutations: the first three generate all coordinate permutations, and the product of the last two changes two signs, whose conjugates generate all even sign changes. Faithfulness identifies this group with $W(D_4)$, of order $24\cdot8=192$, with roots $\{\pm e_i\pm e_j:i<j\}$. Its twelve root lines form one orbit. Normalising $\langle s_{\alpha_2}\rangle$ means preserving the line $\mathbb R\alpha_2$, so its normaliser has order 16 and $G$ has order 8. The three roots $\beta_1=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3=e_1-e_4$, $\beta_2=\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_4=e_1+e_4$, and $\beta_3=\alpha_3+\alpha_2+\alpha_4=e_2+e_3$ are mutually orthogonal and orthogonal to $\alpha_2$. Their reflections give eight distinct quotient elements and hence all of $G$. [D-d4-realisation, D-faithfulness, D-groups]
6. Put $X=f(\alpha_1)$, $Z=f(\alpha_3)$ and $V=f(\alpha_4)$ on $E$. Restricting the root set from step 5 gives precisely the seven normal forms $X,Z,V,X+Z,X+V,Z+V,X+Z+V$. Set $y_1=X+Z$, $y_2=X+V$, $y_3=Z+V$. Their inverse expressions are $2X=y_1+y_2-y_3$, $2Z=y_1+y_3-y_2$ and $2V=y_2+y_3-y_1$. The arrangement therefore has normals $y_1,y_2,y_3,y_1+y_2+y_3,y_1+y_2-y_3,y_1-y_2+y_3,-y_1+y_2+y_3$, numbered $H_1,\ldots,H_7$ in that order. Since $y_i=f(\beta_i)$, the three reflections act by independent sign changes of the $y_i$. Each coordinate plane is fixed, while the four diagonal planes form one orbit. The orbit sizes are consequently $1,1,1,4$. [D-d4-realisation, D-arrangement, step:5]
7. The rank-two intersections are exhausted by the three double incidences $\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}$ and the six triple incidences $\{1,4,7\},\{1,5,6\},\{2,4,6\},\{2,5,7\},\{3,4,5\},\{3,6,7\}$. Substitution in the displayed forms verifies each incidence and distinguishes the nine lines. These incidences account for all $3+6\cdot3=21$ pairs of hyperplanes. In the reverse-inclusion intersection lattice, the rank-one Möbius values are −1, a line lying in $m$ planes has value $m-1$, and the origin has value $-(1-7+15)=-9$. The identity-element case of the character formula therefore yields $P_M(t)=1+7t+15t^2+9t^3$. [D-lattice-character, step:6]
8. For $g=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$, the fixed lattice contains all three coordinate planes and five rank-two lines: the three coordinate axes and the two lines $y_3=0$, $y_1=\pm y_2$. In this fixed poset the axes have Möbius value 1; each additional line contains just one fixed hyperplane and has value 0. The origin then has value $-(1-3+3)=-1$. The fixed-lattice formula, using ranks from the full lattice, gives trace polynomial $1+3t+3t^2+t^3$. Coordinate permutations and multiplication by scalar −1 reduce every non-scalar sign change to this case. Both scalar elements fix the full lattice and have trace polynomial $P_M(t)$. Averaging the eight traces gives $(2P_M(t)+6(1+3t+3t^2+t^3))/8=1+4t+6t^2+3t^3$. Steps 1–4 identify these two polynomials with the asserted group-cohomology polynomials. ### Research insight 02 · Vanishing Euler characteristic outside the zero-dimensional case

#### Insight

**Kind:** corollary

**Evidence status:** proved

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system with $W$ finite, let $A$ be its Artin–Tits group, and let $J\subsetneq S$. Define $N(P,J)$ as the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ in $N(A,J)$. Then $\chi(N(P,J))=\chi(N(A,J))=0$, where group Euler characteristic means the ordinary Euler characteristic of a finite classifying-space model. More precisely, put $E=\Theta_J$ and $M=\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$. Choose $s\in S\setminus J$, extend $\lambda(f)=f(\alpha_s)$ complex-linearly, and set $F=\{z\in M:\lambda(z)=1\}$. Then $M\cong\mathbb C^*\times F$ and $P_{N(P,J)}(t)=(1+t)P_F(t)$, with $P_F(t)=\sum_{n\geq0}\dim_{\mathbb Q}H_n(F;\mathbb Q)t^n$ a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients. When $J=S$, both normaliser quotients are trivial and their Euler characteristics equal $1$.

#### Why it matters

The finite classifying models in the article acquire a numerical constraint: their alternating cell counts must vanish whenever the relative space has positive dimension. The polynomial refinement explains this cancellation through a scalar circle factor and provides additional checks on proposed homology computations.

#### Argument and next steps

The relative arrangement is finite and central. Since J is proper, the restriction of a simple-root evaluation outside J is nonzero, and its kernel belongs to that arrangement. Consequently lambda never vanishes on M. Normalization gives mutually inverse maps z↦(lambda(z),z/lambda(z)) and (a,w)↦aw between M and C*×F.
