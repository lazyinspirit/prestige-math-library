## Definition

Let $\Gamma_0$ be finite, with Coxeter group $W$, real simple-root basis $(\alpha_i)_{i\in\Gamma_0}$, root set $\Phi=\Phi^+\sqcup\Phi^-$ and dual space $\Theta$. For $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$, let $\Theta_J=\{f\in\Theta:f(\alpha_j)=0\text{ for every }j\in J\}$ and let $\Phi_J=\{w\alpha_j:w\in W_J,\ j\in J\}$, where $W_J$ is the subgroup generated by the simple reflections indexed by $J$. Write $H_\alpha=\{f\in\Theta:f(\alpha)=0\}$ and $\mathcal H=\{H_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$, and let $\mathrm{Cone}$ be the Tits cone, the union of the $W$-translates of the closed fundamental chamber.

The **Tits cone intersection**, or **$J$-cone**, is $\mathrm{Cone}(J)=\mathrm{Cone}\cap\Theta_J$. Its relative interior $\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ$ is its interior in $\Theta_J$, equipped with the topology induced from the coordinate Euclidean topology on $\Theta$.

The **relative roots**, or **$J$-roots**, and their positive and negative parts are
$$\Phi^J=\{\alpha\in\Phi:\Theta_J\not\subseteq H_\alpha\}=\Phi\setminus\Phi_J,\qquad \Phi^J_\pm=\Phi^J\cap\Phi^\pm.$$
The **relative arrangement** is the set of distinct hyperplanes in $\Theta_J$
$$\mathcal H^J=\{H\cap\Theta_J:H\in\mathcal H,\ \Theta_J\not\subseteq H\}=\{H_\alpha\cap\Theta_J:\alpha\in\Phi^J_+\}.$$
Different positive relative roots may give the same restricted hyperplane. When $J=\varnothing$, the pair $(\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ,\mathcal H^J)$ is $(\mathrm{Cone}^\circ,\mathcal H)$, the Coxeter arrangement in the interior of the Tits cone.

## Justification

The coordinate subspace and parabolic roots are defined by [def-parabolic-faces-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-parabolic-faces-artin-tits-normalisers) and [def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers), and the Tits cone, root hyperplanes and ambient topology by [def-coxeter-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-coxeter-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers). The relative interior is therefore well-defined in the induced topology, as specified by [prop-relative-linear-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-relative-linear-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites).

Put $V_J=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_j:j\in J\}$. By [prop-relative-linear-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-relative-linear-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites), $\Theta_J\subseteq H_\alpha$ exactly when $\alpha\in V_J$. By [prop-parabolic-minimum-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-parabolic-minimum-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites), $\Phi_J=\Phi\cap V_J$. These two facts prove the displayed equality defining $\Phi^J$, without assuming $W_J$ finite. The same linear-interface proposition shows that each included intersection is a hyperplane of $\Theta_J$.

By [prop-root-inversion-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-root-inversion-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites), every root is positive or the negative of a positive root. Since $H_\alpha=H_{-\alpha}$, and the condition that $\Theta_J$ is not contained in the hyperplane is unchanged by sign, every member of the first description of $\mathcal H^J$ occurs in the second; the reverse inclusion is immediate. The arrangement is a set, so equal restricted hyperplanes are counted once.

For a concrete coincidence, take $\Gamma_0=\{1,2\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=3$ (type $A_2$) and $J=\{1\}$. The form and reflection formula in [def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers) give $B(\alpha_2,\alpha_1)=-\cos(\pi/3)=-1/2$ and $s_1\alpha_2=\alpha_2+\alpha_1$. Thus $\alpha_2$ and $\beta=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$ are distinct roots with nonnegative simple-root coordinates, so both are positive. By [def-parabolic-faces-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-parabolic-faces-artin-tits-normalisers), every $f\in\Theta_J$ has $f(\alpha_1)=0$, hence $f(\beta)=f(\alpha_2)$. The functional specified on the basis by $f(\alpha_1)=0$ and $f(\alpha_2)=1$ belongs to $\Theta_J$, so neither restricted evaluation is zero and both roots are relative. Their restricted hyperplanes coincide: $H_{\alpha_2}\cap\Theta_J=H_\beta\cap\Theta_J=\{0\}$, since a functional vanishing on both basis vectors is zero.

If $J=\varnothing$, then $\Theta_J=\Theta$ and $\Phi_J=\varnothing$. Every root hyperplane is proper, so $\mathcal H^J=\mathcal H$; the two cone interiors agree because they are taken in the same space. All these conclusions also cover the empty vertex set.
