## Definition

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system with $S=\{s_i:i\in\Gamma_0\}$, where the index set $\Gamma_0$ may be infinite. Let $\ell(w)$ be the least number of simple generators in a word representing $w\in W$. For $K\subseteq\Gamma_0$, let $W_K=\langle s_k:k\in K\rangle$ be the standard parabolic subgroup generated by those simple generators. Fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$.

The **algebraic chamber-label set** is $\mathrm{Cham}(J)=\{(x,I)\in W\times\mathcal P(\Gamma_0): W_Jx=xW_I\text{ and }\ell(x)\leq\ell(xu)\text{ for every }u\in W_I\}$. Here $\mathcal P(\Gamma_0)$ is the set of all subsets of $\Gamma_0$, and $W_Jx=\{vx:v\in W_J\}$ and $xW_I=\{xu:u\in W_I\}$. Thus the length condition says that $x$ has minimal length in the coset $xW_I$.

## Justification

The Coxeter-system definition supplies the group $W$ and its finite-valued word-length function. The standard parabolic definition supplies the subgroups $W_J$ and $W_I$ for every indicated subset, including the empty subset. Group multiplication therefore defines both displayed cosets, and the equality and length inequalities specify a property of each member of the set $W\times\mathcal P(\Gamma_0)$. The displayed comprehension consequently defines a set. It is nonempty: the identity element $1$ has length zero, $W_J1=1W_J$, and $(1,J)$ satisfies the length condition. No uniqueness of a minimal coset representative or geometric identification of these labels is needed for the definition.
