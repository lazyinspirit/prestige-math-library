## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system with vertex set $\Gamma_0$, canonical geometric representation $V=\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma_0}\mathbb R\alpha_i$, and positive roots $\Phi^+$. For $I\subseteq\Gamma_0$, put $V_I=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\alpha_i:i\in I\}$ and let $\Phi_I^+$ be the positive roots of the standard parabolic subgroup $W_I$. Fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$ and $(y,K)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$; explicitly, $W_Jy=yW_K$ and $y$ has minimum length in $yW_K$.

Then $y^{-1}\alpha_j\in\Phi_K^+$ for every $j\in J$, and $y\alpha_k\in\Phi_J^+$ for every $k\in K$. The maps $y:V_K\to V_J$ and $y^{-1}:V_J\to V_K$ are inverse isomorphisms. Their matrices in the indicated simple-root bases are entrywise nonnegative and are permutation matrices. Consequently $y\{\alpha_k:k\in K\}=\{\alpha_j:j\in J\}$. The assertions include empty $J$ and $K$; neither $W$ nor the parabolic subgroups are assumed finite.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The geometric representation has symmetric bilinear form $B$ with $B(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=1$ and $s_i(v)=v-2B(v,\alpha_i)\alpha_i$. Its roots are the $W$-translates of simple roots, and positivity means nonnegative simple-root coordinates. [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The label condition is $W_Jy=yW_K$, with $y$ of minimum length in this common set. The standard parabolic $W_I$ is generated by the $s_i$ for $i\in I$, and $\Phi_I$ consists of their translates of the simple roots indexed by $I$. [[def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] For finite rank, $\Phi\cap V_I=\Phi_I$. A minimum $d$ in $dW_I$ sends $\Phi_I^+$ into $\Phi^+$, and a minimum $d$ in $W_Id$ has $d^{-1}\Phi_I^+\subseteq\Phi^+$. [[prop-parabolic-minimum-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

[F4] An invertible real square matrix whose entries and inverse entries are nonnegative is a product of a permutation matrix and a strictly positive diagonal matrix; the empty matrix is included. [[lem-nonnegative-inverse-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

[F5] Relative to ordered domain and codomain bases, the column indexed by a domain basis vector is the coordinate column of its image under the linear map; empty bases give the corresponding zero-sized matrices. [[math:def-coordinate-column-and-matrix-of-a-linear-map]]

## Proof
**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system, $J$, and label $(y,K)$ in the Statement.


**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove the two positive parabolic-root inclusions and the permutation-matrix conclusion.

1.1 For any subset $I$, each generator $s_i$ with $i\in I$ acts as the identity modulo $V_I$, by the reflection formula. The same is therefore true of every element of $W_I$. From $W_Jy=yW_K$, we have $y^{-1}W_Jy=W_K$ and $yW_Ky^{-1}=W_J$. Fix $j\in J$ and put $\beta=y^{-1}\alpha_j$. The element $y^{-1}s_jy\in W_K$ sends $\beta$ to $-\beta$; since it is the identity modulo $V_K$, its difference on $\beta$ shows $2\beta\in V_K$. Thus $y^{-1}V_J\subseteq V_K$. Interchanging $J$ and $K$ and replacing $y$ by $y^{-1}$ gives $yV_K\subseteq V_J$. Applying $y$ to the first inclusion shows that both inclusions are equalities, so the restrictions of $y$ and $y^{-1}$ are inverse isomorphisms. [F1, F2]

2.1 Each $y^{-1}\alpha_j$ is a root in $V_K$, hence belongs to $\Phi_K$ by [F3]. Since $y$ is minimum in $W_Jy$, [F3] also makes it positive. Similarly, each $y\alpha_k$ is a root in $V_J$ and is positive because $y$ is minimum in $yW_K$. This proves both parabolic-root inclusions. Their nonnegative simple-root coordinates are precisely the columns of the respective matrices of the inverse isomorphisms in 1.1. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 Choose orderings of the two finite simple-root bases. Their cardinalities agree by 1.1, and the two matrices are inverse and entrywise nonnegative by steps 1.1 and 2.1. By [F4], the matrix of $y$ is a permutation matrix times a strictly positive diagonal matrix. Thus each $y\alpha_k$ is a positive scalar multiple $c\alpha_j$ of one simple root, with the indices matched by a permutation. The reflection formula and $B(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=1$ show by expansion that each $s_i$ preserves $B$; hence every element of $W$ preserves $B$, and every root has self-pairing $1$. Consequently $1=B(y\alpha_k,y\alpha_k)=c^2$, so $c=1$. The matrix of $y$ is therefore a permutation matrix, as is its inverse, and the displayed equality of simple-root sets follows. For empty bases the same conclusion is vacuous and the matrices are the unique $0\times0$ matrices. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
