## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be the Coxeter system indexed by a set $\Gamma_0$, possibly infinite, with its standard geometric representation and positive and negative roots $\Phi^+$ and $\Phi^-$. For $L\subseteq\Gamma_0$, let $W_L$ be the subgroup generated by the simple generators indexed by $L$, let $\Phi_L$ be its associated parabolic root set, and put $\Phi_L^\pm=\Phi_L\cap\Phi^\pm$. An associate of $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$ is a subset whose standard parabolic subgroup is conjugate to $W_J$.

Let $I$ be an associate of $J$, let distinct $a,b\in\Gamma_0\setminus I$ satisfy $|\Phi_{I\cup\{a,b\}}\setminus\Phi_I|<\infty$, and put $T=I\cup\{a,b\}$. Let $v_{a,b,I}$ be the two-generator element constructed in the proof of the minimal common left multiple lemma. Then $v_{a,b,I}^{-1}$ is the unique $w\in W_T$ satisfying $w(\Phi_T^+\setminus\Phi_I^+)\subseteq\Phi_T^-$ and $w(\Phi_I^+)\subseteq\Phi_T^+$.

## Facts & Assumptions


[F1] The construction in the minimal common left multiple lemma takes $v_{a,b,I}=q$, where $q=r^{-1}$ and $r=w_Dw_{D\cap I}$, with $D$ the union of the components of the diagram induced on $T$ that meet $\{a,b\}$. The relative-longest lemma makes $D$ spherical and gives $\Phi^+\cap q\Phi^-=\Phi_T^+\setminus\Phi_I^+$. Here $w_L$ denotes the longest element of a finite standard parabolic subgroup $W_L$. [[lem-two-generator-minimal-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[lem-author-a7d202611130-finite-relative-longest]]

[F2] In a finite-rank Coxeter system, every root is positive or negative, with the two signs disjoint, and every nonidentity element sends at least one simple root to a negative root. [[math:thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]] [[math:thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]]

[F3] Every group element has a finite word in the simple generators. For each finite $H\subseteq\Gamma_0$, the canonical map from the Coxeter group presented by the restricted matrix on $H$ into $W$ is injective with image $W_H$, by part 1 of [[math:thm-arbitrary-rank-finite-parabolics-and-bruhat-subword]]. Thus $W_H$, with the generators indexed by $H$, is a finite-rank Coxeter system. The geometric reflection formulas in [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]] restrict to its standard representation on $V_H=\operatorname{span}\{\alpha_h:h\in H\}$. Its roots identify with $\Phi_H=\{x\alpha_h:x\in W_H,h\in H\}$, and their positive and negative signs agree with the ambient signs because their coordinates outside $H$ are zero.

## Proof


**Given:** The Coxeter system, roots, subsets and finite relative-root hypothesis in the Statement.

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove the two root-sign conditions for $r=v_{a,b,I}^{-1}$ and show that they determine it in $W_T$.

1.1 By [F1], $r$ lies in $W_D\subseteq W_T$, and for a positive root $\alpha$ the condition $r\alpha\in\Phi^-$ is equivalent to $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap q\Phi^-=\Phi_T^+\setminus\Phi_I^+$. Indeed $q=r^{-1}$. The action of $W_T$ preserves $\Phi_T$, directly from its definition as the $W_T$-orbit of the simple roots indexed by $T$. Consequently $r$ sends the stated difference into $\Phi_T^-$ and sends $\Phi_I^+$ into $\Phi_T^+$; for the latter, write a root under consideration as $x\alpha_i$ and choose a finite set of indices containing $i$ and the letters in words for $x$ and $r$. By [F3], both $x\alpha_i$ and $rx\alpha_i$ are roots of that finite-rank Coxeter system with their ambient signs, so [F2] gives root-sign dichotomy. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Let $w\in W_T$ satisfy both displayed conditions. Choose a finite $H\subseteq T$ containing the indices in words for both $w$ and $r$. By [F3], $W_H$ is the Coxeter group for the restricted matrix, its roots are $\Phi_H\subseteq\Phi_T$, and the actions of $w$, $r$ and $wr^{-1}$ on them are the finite-rank geometric actions with the same signs. For every positive root $\alpha$ of the finite subsystem on $H$, the two conditions for $w$ and step 1.1 show that $w\alpha$ and $r\alpha$ have the same sign: $\alpha$ belongs either to $\Phi_I^+$ or to its complement in $\Phi_T^+$. These images remain roots of the subsystem on $H$. Thus $wr^{-1}$ sends every positive root of that subsystem to a positive root: write such a root as either $r\alpha$ or $-r\alpha$, with $\alpha$ positive, and use the matching signs. By [F2] applied to this finite-rank Coxeter system, $wr^{-1}=1$ in $W_H$, hence also in $W$, so $w=r$. This proves uniqueness and the assertion. [F2, F3, step 1.1, given] ∎
