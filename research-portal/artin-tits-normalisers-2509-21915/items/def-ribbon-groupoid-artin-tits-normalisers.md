## Definition

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter diagram, meaning that its Coxeter group $W$ is finite, with vertex set $\Gamma_0$. Let $A$ be its Artin–Tits group, with generators $\sigma_i$ indexed by $i\in\Gamma_0$, and let $A_I^+$ be the submonoid generated by the $\sigma_i$ for $i\in I$. Fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. An associate of $J$ is a subset $I\subseteq\Gamma_0$ whose standard Coxeter parabolic subgroup $W_I=\langle s_i:i\in I\rangle$ is conjugate to $W_J$ in $W$.

The **ribbon groupoid** $\mathcal R'(J)$ has the associates of $J$ as objects. For associates $I,K$, its arrows $I\to K$ are tagged by source and target and have labels

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal R'(J)}(I,K)=\{\beta\in A:A_I^+\beta=\beta A_K^+\}.$$

Here equality is equality of subsets of $A$. For $\beta:I\to K$ and $\gamma:K\to L$, their composite, read left to right, has label $\beta\gamma$. The identity at $I$ has label $1\in A$.

## Justification

The positive parabolic monoids and the associate condition are supplied by [[def-positive-artin-monoids-artin-tits-normalisers]] and [[def-associates-artin-tits-normalisers]]. The objects form a set, and the arrows form a set of triples $(I,\beta,K)$. If $A_I^+\beta=\beta A_K^+$ and $A_K^+\gamma=\gamma A_L^+$, associativity of multiplication in $A$ gives $A_I^+\beta\gamma=\beta A_K^+\gamma=\beta\gamma A_L^+$. The identity satisfies the defining equality, and multiplying that equality for $\beta$ by $\beta^{-1}$ on both sides gives $A_K^+\beta^{-1}=\beta^{-1}A_I^+$. Associativity, identity and inverse laws therefore follow from the group laws of $A$. These are the groupoid and left-to-right composition conventions of [[def-groupoid-conventions-artin-tits-normalisers]].

The canonical reflection representation, its simple-root basis and positive-root convention are those of [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]. It remains to justify connectedness. By [[def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers]], for any two associates $I,K$ there is $x\in W$ with $x\{\alpha_k:k\in K\}=\{\alpha_i:i\in I\}$ in the canonical reflection representation. For each $k\in K$, write $x\alpha_k=\alpha_i$; the basis property makes $k\mapsto i$ a bijection from $K$ to $I$. The reflection formula and invariance of the Coxeter form in [[math:lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] show that $x s_k x^{-1}=s_i$ as linear transformations; faithfulness in [[math:thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] makes this an equality in $W$.

The same root-length criterion gives $\ell(xs_k)=\ell(x)+1$ because $x\alpha_k=\alpha_i$ is positive. It also gives $\ell(x^{-1}s_i)=\ell(x^{-1})+1$ because $x^{-1}\alpha_i=\alpha_k$ is positive; reversing words in the involutive Coxeter generators gives $\ell(s_i x)=\ell(x)+1$. The reduced-word lift $\sigma_x$ and its length-additive multiplication rule from [[prop-positive-lift-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]] therefore yield $\sigma_i\sigma_x=\sigma_{s_i x}=\sigma_{x s_k}=\sigma_x\sigma_k$. Since the corresponding generators are in bijection, taking all finite positive products gives $A_I^+\sigma_x=\sigma_x A_K^+$. Thus $\sigma_x$ labels an arrow $I\to K$. The object $J$ exists, so the groupoid is nonempty and connected.
