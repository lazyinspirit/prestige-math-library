## Example

Take the $D_4$ Coxeter diagram with central vertex $2$ and $J=\{2\}$. Let $W_I$ denote the subgroup generated by the simple reflections indexed by $I$, and put $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$. The relative chambers are labelled by pairs $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ the minimum-length element of $xW_I$; write $(x,i)$ for $(x,\{i\})$.

The action of $N(W,J)$ on these chambers has four orbits, each containing eight chambers. Their second labels are respectively $\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{4\}$: these are precisely the associates of $J$, meaning the objects of the Brink–Howlett groupoid. In Figures 2–3 of the article, equal colours denote equal second labels: black denotes $1$, red $2$, green $3$ and blue $4$.

The universal Brink–Howlett covering sends the object $(x,I)$ to $I$ and the arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$ labelled $x^{-1}y$ to the arrow with the same label $x^{-1}y$ from $I$ to $K$.

## Facts & Assumptions


[F1] The completed chamber table lists all 32 relative chambers, numbered 1 through 32, with their labels $(x,i)$. [[ex-d4-labelled-chamber-graph-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The normaliser quotient acts on chamber labels, preserves their second component, and is transitive on labels with any fixed second component. [[lem-normaliser-chamber-action-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The universal Brink–Howlett groupoid has the chamber labels as objects and one arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$ labelled $x^{-1}y$. The base objects are the associates of $J$, and the covering sends $(x,I)$ to $I$ and retains the arrow label. [[thm-universal-brink-howlett-cover-artin-tits-normalisers]]

## Verification


**Given:** The $D_4$ data and chamber labels in the Example.

**Goal:** Verify the four orbits, their sizes, and the stated covering map.

1.1 Read the second components in the complete table [F1]. For $i=1,2,3,4$, respectively, the numbered chambers are $\{2,3,4,6,27,29,30,31\}$, $\{1,5,7,9,24,26,28,32\}$, $\{10,12,13,16,18,19,22,25\}$, and $\{8,11,14,15,17,20,21,23\}$. These four disjoint sets exhaust the table and each has eight entries. By [F2], each set is one orbit and different sets are different orbits. [F1, F2]

2.1 By [F3], the covering maps each chamber label to its second component and preserves the stated arrow label; its base objects are exactly the associates of $J$. The four nonempty second-component sets in step 1.1 therefore give exactly the four associates listed in the Example. This verifies the orbit count, orbit sizes and covering formula. [F3, step 1.1] ∎
