## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter system, meaning that $W$ is finite, and let $J\subseteq S$. Let $A$ be the associated Artin–Tits group, let $\pi:A\to W$ be its standard projection and let $P=\ker\pi$. Write $W_J$ and $A_J$ for the subgroups generated by the standard generators indexed by $J$. For $G=W,A$, put $N(G,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_G(G_J)/G_J$, where $\operatorname{Norm}_G(G_J)=\{g\in G:gG_Jg^{-1}=G_J\}$. Let $N(P,J)$ be the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ in $N(A,J)$.

Let $X=\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$ be the relative complexified hyperplane complement, let $x\in X$, and let $\bar x$ be its image in $X/N(W,J)$. The algebraic exact sequence $1\to N(P,J)\to N(A,J)\xrightarrow{\bar\pi}N(W,J)\to1$ is isomorphic to the covering exact sequence $1\to\pi_1(X,x)\to\pi_1(X/N(W,J),\bar x)\to N(W,J)\to1$, with the identity on $N(W,J)$. In particular, $N(P,J)\cong\pi_1(X,x)$ and $N(A,J)\cong\pi_1(X/N(W,J),\bar x)$. Fundamental groups use first-path-first multiplication.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The normaliser quotients and $N(P,J)$ are the groups described in the Statement, and the algebraic sequence displayed there is exact. [[def-normaliser-quotients-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[lem-normaliser-exact-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] In spherical type, $X$ is the complement in the complexification of a finite-dimensional real vector space of finitely many complexified real hyperplanes. [[def-relative-complement-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The action of $N(W,J)$ on $X$ gives a normal covering $q:X\to X/N(W,J)$ and, at every point of $X$, the displayed topological exact sequence. Its final map sends a loop to the unique element carrying the initial point of its lift to its endpoint. [[prop-relative-cover-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] With the Axiom of Choice, the completed groupoid comparison provides a point $x_0\in X$ and an isomorphism from the algebraic exact sequence to the topological exact sequence based at $x_0$, inducing the identity on $N(W,J)$. It identifies the algebraic kernel with the kernel of the topological endpoint map. [[lem-cover-exact-comparison-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F5] Endpoint-fixed path classes compose by concatenation and have inverse classes represented by reversed paths. A path downstairs has a unique lift from a specified point over its initial point. [[math:def-fundamental-groupoid-of-a-space]] [[math:thm-path-lifting-for-covering-maps]]

[F6] A morphism of group extensions consists of group homomorphisms making the injection and quotient squares commute. [[math:def-morphism-of-group-extensions]]

[F7] No finite family of proper linear subspaces covers a finite-dimensional vector space over an infinite field. We apply this over $\mathbb R$ to the underlying real vector space of the complexification in [F2]. [[math:lem-finite-dimensional-space-over-an-infinite-field-is-not-a-finite-union-of-proper-subspaces]]

## Proof

**Given:** The Coxeter system, subset, groups, complement and basepoint in the Statement, and the Axiom of Choice.

**Goal:** Transport the comparison at $x_0$ to the arbitrary basepoint $x$, preserving the quotient map.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $V$ be the underlying finite-dimensional real vector space of the ambient complexification in [F2], and let $L$ range over the deleted complexified hyperplanes. Each $L$ has real codimension two: a real basis of the corresponding real hyperplane, together with its multiples by $i$, is a real basis of $L$. For $p\in X$, the subspace $L+\mathbb R p$ has dimension $\dim_{\mathbb R}V-1$, since $p\notin L$, and is proper. For two endpoints $p,q\in X$, [F7] therefore gives $r\in V$ outside all the finitely many subspaces $L+\mathbb R p$ and $L+\mathbb R q$. If there are no deleted hyperplanes, no avoidance condition on $r$ is needed. [F2, F7, choose]

2.1 The segment from $p$ to $r$ avoids every deleted $L$: if $(1-t)p+tr\in L$ for $0\le t\le1$, then $t\ne0$ because $p\notin L$, and solving for $r$ gives $r\in L+\mathbb R p$, contrary to step 1.1. The same argument applies to the segment from $q$ to $r$. Both segment maps are continuous, since their real coordinates are affine functions of $t$; traversing the first and the reverse of the second gives a path in $X$ from $p$ to $q$. The construction also covers an empty arrangement, while in dimension zero $X$ is a point. Hence $X$ is path-connected. Take $p=x_0$ from [F4] and $q=x$, and choose the resulting path $c:x_0\to x$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Write $\bar c=q\circ c$. By [F5], conjugation by path classes gives isomorphisms $u:\pi_1(X,x_0)\to\pi_1(X,x)$ and $v:\pi_1(X/N(W,J),q(x_0))\to\pi_1(X/N(W,J),\bar x)$: with first-path-first products they send $[\alpha]$ to $[c^{-1}*\alpha*c]$ and $[\beta]$ to $[\bar c^{-1}*\beta*\bar c]$, respectively. Their inverses use the reversed path. Projection preserves concatenation and reversal, so $v q_*=q_*u$. [F3, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 Let a loop $\beta$ at $q(x_0)$ lift from $x_0$ to $g x_0$, where $g\in N(W,J)$. The lift of $\bar c^{-1}*\beta*\bar c$ starting at $x$ is $c^{-1}$ followed by that lift of $\beta$ and then by $g c$. Indeed, $g c$ projects to $\bar c$ and starts at $g x_0$; uniqueness in [F5] identifies it with the final lifted segment. Its endpoint is $g x$. By the endpoint description in [F3], the last maps of the two covering sequences therefore agree after applying $v$. [F3, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 3.1 and 4.1 give an isomorphism of the two covering exact sequences, with identity on $N(W,J)$. Compose it with the comparison at $x_0$ from [F4]. The result is an isomorphism from the algebraic sequence of [F1] to the covering sequence based at $x$, with identity on the rightmost nontrivial group. Its kernel and middle components give the two stated group isomorphisms. [F1, F3, F4, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
