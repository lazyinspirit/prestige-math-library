## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter system, meaning that $W$ is finite, and let $J\subseteq S$. Let $A$ be the associated Artin–Tits group and $P$ the kernel of its standard projection to $W$. For $G=W,A$, let $G_J$ be the standard parabolic subgroup generated by the generators indexed by $J$, and put $N(G,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_G(G_J)/G_J$, where the normaliser consists of the elements conjugating $G_J$ to itself. Let $N(P,J)$ be the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ in $N(A,J)$.

Let $X=\mathcal X_J^{\mathbb C}$ be the relative complexified hyperplane complement. The induced action of $N(W,J)$ gives a quotient $X/N(W,J)$. Then $X$ is a $K(N(P,J),1)$ and $X/N(W,J)$ is a $K(N(A,J),1)$. Here $K(G,1)$ means a path-connected space whose fundamental group is isomorphic to $G$ and whose homotopy groups in all degrees at least two vanish.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The normaliser quotients and $N(P,J)$ are the groups defined in the Statement, and $X$ is the relative complement defined there. [[def-normaliser-quotients-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-relative-complement-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] In spherical type, $X$ is path-connected and its homotopy groups in degrees at least two vanish. [[thm-finite-simplicial-asphericity-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The orbit map $q:X\to X/N(W,J)$ is a covering map. [[prop-relative-cover-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] For a covering of nonempty path-connected spaces, the induced maps on homotopy groups in degrees at least two are isomorphisms. [[prop-cover-asphericity-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

[F5] Assuming the Axiom of Choice, the algebraic and topological normaliser exact sequences are isomorphic. In particular, for $x\in X$ and $\bar x=q(x)$, $\pi_1(X,x)\cong N(P,J)$ and $\pi_1(X/N(W,J),\bar x)\cong N(A,J)$. [[thm-normaliser-topological-exact-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[math:def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F6] A path is a continuous map from $[0,1]$, and path-connectedness means that any two points can be joined by a path. Fundamental groups consist of based-loop homotopy classes, and higher homotopy groups are defined by based cubes. [[math:def-path-connected]] [[math:def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]] [[math:def-higher-homotopy-group-by-based-cubes]]

[F7] A map of topological spaces is continuous when it is continuous at every point; equivalently, the inverse image of every open set is open. [[math:def-continuous-map-top]] [[math:thm-continuity-characterisations-top]]

## Proof
**Given:** The spherical Coxeter system, subset $J$, associated groups and relative complement in the Statement, and the Axiom of Choice.

**Goal:** Identify both fundamental groups and prove that both spaces have vanishing higher homotopy groups.


**Given:** The data and assumption in the Statement.

**Goal:** Establish the two stated $K(G,1)$ assertions.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F2], $X$ is nonempty and path-connected and has trivial homotopy groups in degrees at least two. Choose $x\in X$. By [F5], its fundamental group at $x$ is isomorphic to $N(P,J)$. Thus $X$ is a $K(N(P,J),1)$ under the convention in the Statement. [F2, F5, choose]

2.1 For any $b_0,b_1\in X/N(W,J)$, surjectivity of $q$ gives $x_0,x_1\in X$ with $q(x_i)=b_i$. By [F2] and [F6], choose a path $\gamma:[0,1]\to X$ from $x_0$ to $x_1$. Then $q\circ\gamma$ is a path from $b_0$ to $b_1$: it is continuous since for every open $U$ in the quotient, $(q\circ\gamma)^{-1}(U)=\gamma^{-1}(q^{-1}(U))$ is open, by continuity of $q$ and $\gamma$ and [F7]. Thus the quotient is path-connected and is nonempty because $X$ is nonempty. By [F3] and [F4], $q$ induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups in degrees at least two, so those groups vanish for the quotient by step 1.1. By [F5], its fundamental group at $q(x)$ is isomorphic to $N(A,J)$. Hence the quotient is a $K(N(A,J),1)$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, choose, construct] ∎
