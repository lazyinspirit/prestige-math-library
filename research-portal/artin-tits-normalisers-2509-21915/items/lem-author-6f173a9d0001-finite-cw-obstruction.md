## Statement

The space $M$ of [[lem-author-6f173a9d0001-winding-independence]] is not homotopy equivalent to a finite CW complex. A finite CW complex is a space obtained by attaching finitely many cells in finitely many dimensions. A finite CW model for $M$ means such a complex homotopy equivalent to $M$.

## External input

Hatcher, §2.2, Theorem 2.35 (p.139) identifies cellular with singular homology; consequence (ii) (p.140) bounds the generators of $H_1(K;\mathbb Z)$ by the finite number of one-cells of a CW complex $K$. Corollary 2.11 (p.111) gives homology isomorphisms under homotopy equivalence. These are imported premises, with integer coefficients.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] **Given:** The infinite independent family in $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$ from [[lem-author-6f173a9d0001-winding-independence]]. **Goal:** Obstruct finite CW homotopy type, rather than merely count cells in one chosen model.

[F2] The cellular-homology bound and homotopy-invariance consequence in the External input section are imported from Hatcher at the cited locators.

[F3] CW complexes have the cell-attachment, closure-finiteness and weak-topology structure of [[math:def-cw-complex-with-closure-finiteness-and-weak-topology]]. Homotopy equivalence has the meaning of [[math:def-homotopy-equivalence]]. Finiteness means finitely many cells in total.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $K$ is a finite CW complex, let $d$ be its number of one-cells. By [F2], $H_1(K;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by at most $d$ elements. A homotopy equivalence $K\to M$ would give an isomorphism $H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$, making the latter generated by at most $d$ elements. [F2, F3]

1.2 We prove by induction on $d\geq0$ that any $d+1$ vectors $v_0,\ldots,v_d$ in $\mathbb Z^d$ have a nonzero integral relation. For $d=0$, $v_0=0$ gives one. For $d>0$, write $a_i$ for the first coordinate of $v_i$. If all $a_i$ vanish, apply the induction hypothesis to the last $d-1$ coordinates of $v_0,\ldots,v_{d-1}$ and give $v_d$ coefficient zero. Otherwise reorder so $a_0\ne0$. For $1\leq i\leq d$, the vectors $w_i=a_0v_i-a_iv_0$ have zero first coordinate. Their last $d-1$ coordinates admit an integral relation $\sum_{i=1}^d b_iw_i=0$ with some $b_i\ne0$, by induction. Thus $a_0\sum_{i=1}^d b_iv_i-(\sum_{i=1}^d b_ia_i)v_0=0$ is a nonzero integral relation, since at least one coefficient $a_0b_i$ is nonzero. [algebra]

2.1 Let an abelian group $A$ be generated by $g_1,\ldots,g_d$ and let $h_0,\ldots,h_d\in A$. Write each $h_i$ as $\sum_{j=1}^d c_{ji}g_j$ with integers $c_{ji}$, and let $v_i=(c_{1i},\ldots,c_{di})\in\mathbb Z^d$. Step 1.2 gives integers $n_i$, not all zero, with $\sum_i n_iv_i=0$. Substituting the expressions for $h_i$ gives $\sum_i n_ih_i=0$. Hence a group generated by $d$ elements contains no independent family of $d+1$ elements; the argument includes $d=0$. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 If a finite CW complex $K$ were homotopy equivalent to $M$, step 1.1 would make $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$ generated by some finite number $d$ of elements. Choose the $d+1$ meridian classes indexed by $0,\ldots,d$ from [F1]. Their integral independence contradicts step 2.1. Therefore $M$ has no finite CW model. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
