## Example

Take the $D_4$ Coxeter system whose central vertex is $2$ and whose leaves are $1,3,4$, and put $J=\{2\}$. Let $W$ be its Coxeter group and $A$ its Artin–Tits group. The canonical homomorphism $\pi:A\to W$ sends each standard Artin generator to the corresponding simple reflection.

Let $\mathcal D$ be the Deligne groupoid of the relative chamber arrangement: its objects are the relative chambers, and it is the groupoid completion of the directed chamber-path category modulo identifications of geodesic paths with the same endpoints. A geodesic is a path of minimum length among paths with those endpoints. A chamber label $(x,I)$ consists of $x\in W$ and a subset $I$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$, where $W_I$ is generated by the simple reflections indexed by $I$ and $x$ has minimum length in $xW_I$. The universal wall groupoid $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}$ has these labels as objects and exactly one arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$, labelled $x^{-1}y$.

Let $\mathcal R$ be the reduced ribbon groupoid. Its objects are the associates of $J$, meaning subsets $I$ for which $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$; its arrows are endpoint-tagged Artin-group elements generated by elementary ribbons and their inverses. The Brink–Howlett groupoid $\mathcal{BH}$ has the same objects, with an arrow $I\to K$ labelled by an element of $W$ carrying the simple-root set indexed by $K$ onto that indexed by $I$. In this example the objects of $\mathcal R$ are $\{1\},\{2\},\{3\},\{4\}$.

There is a functor $H:\mathcal D\to\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}$ that is the identity on objects. For a directed chamber path $p:(x,I)\to(y,K)$, it sends the represented arrow to the unique arrow labelled $x^{-1}y=v_p$, where $v_p$ is the ordered product of the simple wall-crossing elements along $p$. On formal inverses, $H$ takes the inverse arrow. There is also a functor $P:\mathcal R\to\mathcal{BH}$, identity on objects, induced on arrow labels by $\pi:A\to W$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] For a finite-rank Coxeter system and fixed $J$, the natural functor from the arrangement groupoid to the universal wall groupoid is identity on chamber objects, sends a directed chamber path from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$ to the arrow labelled $v_p=x^{-1}y$, and sends formal inverses to inverses. [[rem-arrangement-to-universal-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] In spherical type, meaning that $W$ is finite, the canonical homomorphism $A\to W$ induces an identity-on-objects functor from the reduced ribbon groupoid to the Brink–Howlett groupoid. [[rem-groupoid-square-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The specified $D_4$ example has the stated Deligne and reduced ribbon groupoids, and the reduced ribbon groupoid has the four singleton objects listed above. [[ex-d4-deligne-ribbon-presentations-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] For a Coxeter system on a finite set with connected diagram, its group is finite if its labelled diagram is a standard $D_n$ diagram with $n\ge4$: one degree-three vertex with arms of $1,1,n-3$ vertices and all edges labelled $3$. [[math:thm-cg-finite-coxeter-classification-including-h-and-dihedral]]

## Verification



**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The $D_4$ data, $J$, and groupoids in the Example.

**Goal:** Verify both comparison functors and the stated formulas.

1.1 The $D_4$ system is finite-rank, so [F1] applies to its arrangement groupoid. It supplies $H$ and its identity action on objects. For a directed chamber path with endpoints $(x,I)$ and $(y,K)$, [F1] gives the unique target arrow with label $x^{-1}y$, equal to its wall word $v_p$; it also gives the action on formal inverses. [F1, given]

2.1 The diagram in the Example is connected on four vertices, with central vertex $2$, three arms of one vertex each, and all three edges labelled $3$. It is therefore the $n=4$ case of [F4], so $W$ is finite. This supplies the spherical hypothesis required for the reduced ribbon groupoid and for [F2]. Thus [F2] supplies the functor $P$, identity on objects and induced by $\pi$ on the endpoint-tagged arrow labels. The four-object description is [F3]. Together with step 1.1, this proves the example. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, given] ∎
