## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system with vertex set $\Gamma_0$, and fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. Let $\Theta_J=\{f:f(\alpha_j)=0\text{ for every }j\in J\}$, let $\mathrm{Cone}(J)=\mathrm{Cone}\cap\Theta_J$, and let $\mathcal H^J$ be the set of distinct proper root-hyperplane traces on $\Theta_J$. Here $\mathrm{Cone}$ is the Tits cone, and $\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ$ is the interior in $\Theta_J$. A chamber is a connected component of the arrangement complement, and distinct chambers are adjacent when they share a wall.

Write $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ for the pairs $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ of minimum length in $xW_I$. The chamber-labelling bijection identifies these pairs with the chambers of $(\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ,\mathcal H^J)$. Restriction from the full relative cone to its interior identifies the chambers and preserves adjacency. The universal wall groupoid $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}(J)$ has these labels as objects; for $A=(x,I)$ and $B=(y,K)$ its unique arrow is the endpoint-tagged triple $(A,B,x^{-1}y)$. Composition multiplies labels from left to right.

For any $A,B\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ there is a finite chamber path $p=\omega_{a_1,I_1}*\cdots*\omega_{a_m,I_m}:A\to B$ in the relative-interior arrangement $(\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ,\mathcal H^J)$, consisting of simple wall crossings between adjacent chambers. Its wall word is $v_p=v_{a_1,I_1}\cdots v_{a_m,I_m}=x^{-1}y\in W$. Defining the tagged arrow $[p]=(A,B,v_p)$, we have $\operatorname{Hom}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}(J)}(A,B)=\{[p]\}$; after taking arrow labels this singleton is $\{v_p\}$. The path need not be unique. If $A=B$, it may be the one-chamber path of length zero, with $v_p=1$. Thus the adjacent-crossing morphisms generate the groupoid.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The relative-cone definition supplies $\Theta_J$, $\mathrm{Cone}(J)$, its interior in $\Theta_J$, and the distinct proper root-hyperplane traces $\mathcal H^J$. Chambers are connected components of the complement; distinct chambers are adjacent when they share a wall. [[def-relative-cone-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-chambers-walls-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The algebraic set $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ consists of the pairs $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ of minimum length in $xW_I$. The chamber-labelling theorem identifies these pairs bijectively with the relative-interior chambers for finite rank and arbitrary $J$, without a spherical-type hypothesis. [[def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[thm-chamber-labelling-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] Restriction from $\mathrm{Cone}(J)$ to $\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ$ identifies the chambers and preserves adjacency. [[rem-same-chambers-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] Any two relative chambers are connected by a finite sequence of simple wall crossings; the empty sequence is allowed for equal endpoints. [[lem-finite-wall-path-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F5] A chamber path is a finite nonempty sequence of chamber labels with consecutive chambers adjacent. Each crossing $\omega_{a_i,I_i}$ has a defined element $v_{a_i,I_i}\in W$; its wall word is their ordered product, with empty product $1$ for the one-chamber path. [[def-chamber-paths-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-simple-wall-crossing-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F6] For $A=(x,I)$ and $B=(y,K)$, the universal wall groupoid has the sole arrow $(A,B,x^{-1}y)$. Composition multiplies labels from left to right. For adjacent labels its unique arrow has their simple wall-crossing element as label. [[def-universal-wall-groupoid-artin-tits-normalisers]]

## Proof

**Given:** Two labels $A=(x,I)$ and $B=(y,K)$ for the stated finite-rank Coxeter system and subset $J$.

**Goal:** Construct the stated interior crossing path and identify its tagged composite, proving generation.

1.1 By [F2], identify $A$ and $B$ with their unique relative-interior chambers in the arrangement supplied by [F1]. By [F3], they are also chambers of the full relative arrangement, with the same adjacency relation. Thus the geometric connectivity assertion applies to these specified algebraic endpoints. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Apply [F4] to the chambers in step 1.1 and choose a finite simple-crossing sequence between them. By [F3], every chamber and adjacency in the sequence is also in the relative-interior arrangement. By [F2], their labels give exactly the path $p$ of [F5], with endpoints $A,B$ and wall word $v_p$. For equal endpoints choose the one-chamber path, whose word is $1$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, choose]

3.1 Write the successive labels as $A_i=(x_i,I_i)$ for $0\leq i\leq m$, with $A_0=A$ and $A_m=B$. By [F6], each adjacent arrow is $(A_{i-1},A_i,x_{i-1}^{-1}x_i)$ and its label is the corresponding crossing element. Their composite has label $v_p=(x_0^{-1}x_1)\cdots(x_{m-1}^{-1}x_m)=x^{-1}y$ and is exactly $[p]=(A,B,v_p)$. For $m=0$ the composite is $(A,A,1)$, the identity. Since [F6] makes the hom-set a singleton, it equals $\{[p]\}$, with label set $\{v_p\}$. Every ordered pair of objects admits the path constructed in step 2.1, so every arrow is a finite composite of adjacent-crossing arrows. No uniqueness of the path is required. [F5, F6, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
