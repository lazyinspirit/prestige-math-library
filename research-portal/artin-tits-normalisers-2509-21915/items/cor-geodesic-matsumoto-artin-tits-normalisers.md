## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system with diagram vertex set $\Gamma_0$, and fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. Write $W_L$ for the subgroup generated by the simple reflections indexed by $L$. Let $\Theta$ be the dual of the real geometric representation, let $\Theta_J$ be the subspace annihilating the simple roots indexed by $J$, and let $\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ$ be the interior in $\Theta_J$ of the intersection of $\Theta_J$ with the Tits cone. The relative arrangement $\mathcal H^J$ consists of the distinct restrictions to $\Theta_J$ of root hyperplanes not containing $\Theta_J$.

A chamber is a connected component of the complement of these hyperplanes in $\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ$; chambers are adjacent when they share a wall, a codimension-one intersection of a chamber closure with an arrangement hyperplane. A chamber path is a finite nonempty sequence of adjacent chambers, and it is geodesic when its number of crossings is minimal among paths with the same endpoints. The chamber labels $(x,I)$ belong to $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$: thus $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ has minimum Coxeter length in $xW_I$.

A codimension-two relation replaces one of the two geodesic paths supplied by [[def-codimension-two-artin-tits-normalisers]] by the other, with the same endpoints, inside a longer path. Any two geodesic paths $p,p':(x,I)\to(y,K)$ in $(\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ,\mathcal H^J)$ are related by a finite sequence of codimension-two relations.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The endpoint labels determine a Brink–Howlett morphism $w=x^{-1}y:I\to K$. A morphism $w:I\to K$ carries the simple roots indexed by $K$ onto those indexed by $I$; composable labels multiply from left to right. [[prop-chambers-morphisms-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-standard-expressions-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The wall word of a geodesic is a standard expression, meaning a composable sequence of elementary wall-crossing elements whose Coxeter lengths sum to the length of their product. Conversely, each standard expression lifts uniquely from a prescribed source chamber to a geodesic, whose endpoint is obtained by right multiplication by the product. [[lem-standard-geodesic-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-standard-expressions-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] Any two standard expressions for the same Brink–Howlett morphism are connected by finitely many atomic braid relations. An atomic braid relation exchanges the two standard expressions for the same two-generator morphism $q$, associated with distinct vertices $a,b$ outside its source type and a finite relative parabolic-root difference. [[thm-atomic-matsumoto-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-atomic-braid-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] At any chamber of that source type, the two standard expressions for $q$ lift to the two paths identified by the associated codimension-two relation. They have the same endpoint. [[lem-two-geodesics-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-codimension-two-artin-tits-normalisers]]

## Proof
**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system, subset $J$, and geodesics $p,p'$ with the same labelled endpoints in the Statement.


**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Connect $p$ and $p'$ by finitely many codimension-two replacements.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], the wall words of $p$ and $p'$ are standard expressions for the same morphism $w=x^{-1}y$. By [F3], there is a finite chain of standard expressions for $w$ joining them, with consecutive expressions differing by one atomic braid relation. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Lift every expression in this chain from $(x,I)$. By [F2] each lift is a geodesic ending at $(xw,K)=(y,K)$, and uniqueness identifies the first and last lifts with $p$ and $p'$. Consider one move, replacing a consecutive block by the other expression for $q$. The common prefix lifts to the same intermediate chamber in both paths. Each of the two blocks is a standard expression for $q$, so [F2] and [F4] identify its lift from that chamber with one of the two paths in the corresponding codimension-two relation. Both blocks end at the same chamber, so the unchanged suffix lifts identically after either block. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Thus each atomic move lifts to one codimension-two replacement inside the geodesic path. The finite chain from step 1.1 therefore gives a finite sequence of such replacements from $p$ to $p'$, as required. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
