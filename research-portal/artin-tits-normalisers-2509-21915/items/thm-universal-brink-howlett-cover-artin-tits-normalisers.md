## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system of arbitrary rank in its standard real root representation, with diagram vertex set $\Gamma_0$, and fix arbitrary $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. Put $W_I=\langle s_i:i\in I\rangle$ and $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$. Let $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ be the algebraic label set of pairs $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ of minimum length in $xW_I$. Write $\mathcal{BH}=\mathcal{BH}(J)$ for the Brink–Howlett groupoid and $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}=\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}(J)$ for the groupoid with these labels as objects and one arrow $(x,I)\to(y,K)$ labelled $x^{-1}y$. The functor $\mathcal F:\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}\to\mathcal{BH}$ defined by $(x,I)\mapsto I$ and $x^{-1}y\mapsto x^{-1}y$ is a universal groupoid covering, with $\operatorname{Deck}(\mathcal F)\cong N(W,J)$ and $\mathcal{BH}\cong\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}/N(W,J)$.

For the geometric interpretation, use the finite-rank relative-cone convention: the set $\Gamma_0$ is finite, while neither $W$ nor $W_J$ is required to be finite and $J$ is arbitrary. Then the objects upstairs are exactly the relative chambers, and the adjacent-wall arrows map to the Brink–Howlett generators $v_{a,I}:I\to K$. Here $I$ is an associate of $J$, $a\in\Gamma_0\setminus I$, $D=[I,a]$ is the component containing $a$ in the diagram induced on $I\cup\{a\}$, and $W_D$ is finite. The family is given by $v_{a,I}=w_{D\setminus\{a\}}w_D$ and $K=(I\cup\{a\})\setminus\{\iota_D(a)\}$, where $w_L$ is the longest element of a finite standard parabolic $W_L$ and $w_D\alpha_i=-\alpha_{\iota_D(i)}$ defines $\iota_D$. The projected adjacent crossings generate $\mathcal{BH}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The standard real root representation, positive roots and Coxeter length use the arbitrary-rank conventions of [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]. The arbitrary-rank label set has exactly the coset and length conditions in the statement. The base groupoid is small and connected, with arrow $w:I\to K$ exactly when $wA_K=A_I$, where $A_L=\{\alpha_l:l\in L\}$. The upstairs groupoid is small and nonempty, has singleton hom-sets and left-to-right label multiplication, and contains $(1,J)$. [F2] The minimum-coset, canonical-simple-system transport, standard-parabolic identification and canonical-system generation assertions recorded in External input apply at arbitrary rank.

[F3] The root-permuting subgroup $N_J=\{n:nA_J=A_J\}$ is a subgroup of the normaliser. The quotient homomorphism restricts to an isomorphism $N_J\to N(W,J)$. ## External input

The following premises are imported from Dyer–Gimenez, with exact locators and reading limits in the second source reference. Here a reflection subgroup is a subgroup generated by reflections in roots, and $\Pi_U$ denotes its canonical positive simple-root system in the ambient standard based root system; $\Phi^+$ is the ambient positive root set.

Dyer–Gimenez permits arbitrary rank in §1.4. For any reflection subgroup $U$ and $z\in W$, Proposition 2.10(b),(c) gives a unique minimum $d$ in $zU$ and says that $y\in zU$ equals $d$ exactly when $y\Pi_U\subseteq\Phi^+$. Proposition 2.10(d) gives $\Pi_{zUz^{-1}}=d\Pi_U$. Proposition 2.11(c) identifies $\Pi_{W_L}=A_L$ for every subset $L$ of the simple-root indices, where $A_L=\{\alpha_l:l\in L\}$. A reflection subgroup is generated by the root reflections indexed by its canonical simple system, as also stated in Theorem 2.9 with $U=W_\Delta=\langle s_\beta:\beta\in\Delta\rangle$.

## Proof

**Given:** The arbitrary-rank algebraic data in the first paragraph of the Statement, and the additional finite-rank convention only for its second paragraph. **Goal:** Prove the universal covering, deck and quotient assertions, and the stated geometric interpretation on its specified domain.

1.1 If $(x,I)$ is a label, then $xW_Ix^{-1}=W_J$ and $x$ is the minimum of $xW_I$. Apply [F2] to $U=W_I$ and $z=x$. Canonical-system transport and the standard-parabolic identification give $xA_I=A_J$. Conversely, an element $x$ with $xA_I=A_J$ is the minimum of $xW_I$ by the positivity criterion. Its conjugate subgroup $xW_Ix^{-1}$ has canonical simple system $xA_I=A_J$, again by transport. The generation assertion in [F2] identifies this subgroup with $W_J$. Thus such an $x$ is exactly a label with second component $I$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Under the finite-rank convention, [F5] identifies all objects upstairs with the relative chambers. For an adjacent arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$, [F6] supplies the finite component, index, target and equality $y=xv_{a,I}$. Its image has label $x^{-1}y=v_{a,I}$ and exactly the target in the indexed family. Thus the adjacent arrows project to the stated Brink–Howlett generators. [F5, F6]

2.1 For two labels $(x,I),(y,K)$, step 1.1 gives $x^{-1}yA_K=A_I$, so $x^{-1}y$ is a base arrow. Conversely, if $w:I\to K$ is a base arrow and $(x,I)$ is a label, then $xwA_K=xA_I=A_J$. Step 1.1 makes $(xw,K)$ a label. These statements hold at arbitrary rank. [F1, step 1.1]

2.2 For $n\in N_J$, define $n\cdot(x,I)=(nx,I)$. Step 1.1 shows that this is a label, since $nxA_I=nA_J=A_J$. It is an action by invertible permutations and extends uniquely to the singleton-hom-set groupoid. It preserves every projected arrow label because $(nx)^{-1}(ny)=x^{-1}y$. It is free on objects because $nx=x$ implies $n=1$. Two labels with the same second component are in one orbit: $n=yx^{-1}$ satisfies $nA_J=yA_I=A_J$ and $nx=y$. Conversely the action preserves the second component. Thus its orbits are exactly the fibres of $\mathcal F$. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The proposed functor is well-defined by step 2.1. Its identity and composition laws follow from $x^{-1}x=1$ and $(x^{-1}y)(y^{-1}z)=x^{-1}z$. Fixing a source $(x,I)$ and a base arrow $w:I\to K$, step 2.1 gives its lift to $(xw,K)$ with label $w$. Any lift must end at $(y,K)$ with $x^{-1}y=w$, hence $y=xw$, and its hom-set is a singleton. This proves the source-star bijection. Base connectedness and $(1,J)$ give a lift to an object over every base object. [F1, F4, step 2.1]

3.2 Transfer this action along the group isomorphism $N_J\to N(W,J)$ in [F3]. In finite rank it agrees with the geometric action in [F7]: the representative $n$ of a quotient class sends $(x,I)$ to $(nx,I)$, and step 1.1 makes $nx$ the minimum of $nxW_I$, exactly the formula of [F7]. Hence the quotient action used here has the claimed geometric meaning on that domain. [F3, F7, step 1.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The upstairs groupoid is nonempty and connected and its vertex groups are trivial, by the singleton hom-sets in [F1]. Consequently the covering is universal by [F4]. [F1, F4, step 3.1]

4.2 Apply [F4] to the free $N(W,J)$ action and write $Q$ for its quotient covering. By step 2.2, $\mathcal F$ factors through $Q$. The induced functor is bijective on objects, since its orbits are exactly the fibres and step 3.1 gives every base object. Each arrow orbit has a unique representative with any fixed source in its source orbit by [F4]; from that source the source-star bijection in step 3.1 gives exactly the base arrows. The induced functor is therefore also bijective on arrows and is an isomorphism. This proves the quotient assertion. [F4, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2]

5.1 The quotient-cover theorem gives $\operatorname{Deck}(Q)\cong N(W,J)$. Since the induced quotient-to-base functor in step 4.2 is an isomorphism, $Q\phi=Q$ holds exactly when $\mathcal F\phi=\mathcal F$. Their deck groups coincide, proving the asserted deck-group identification. [F4, step 4.2]

6.1 Every base arrow lifts by step 3.1 and its lift factors into adjacent arrows by [F5]. Projecting that factorization proves that the projected adjacent crossings generate the base. This establishes the geometric paragraph of the statement without imposing any sphericity condition on $W$ or $W_J$. [F5, F6, step 3.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Source notes

The article's Theorems 1.3 and 4.7 and their covering, deck and quotient conclusions remain the attribution. The article omits an explicit global rank qualifier. Its §2.2 follows Iyama–Wemyss for the dual representation; Proposition 3.6 invokes finite dimension, and Lemma 4.2 says that $I,K$ are finite. The current chamber-labelling supplier records that its independently sourced Iyama–Wemyss foundation starts with an $n\times n$ Coxeter matrix and allows infinite Coxeter groups. That independent source was not directly read in this adjudication. These observations are consistent with the finite-rank geometric convention already explicit in the current relative-cone definition; omission of a rank qualifier alone does not establish an intended infinite-dimensional geometric realization.

The local proof establishes the covering, deck and quotient conclusions at arbitrary rank, using the exact independent Dyer–Gimenez inputs and the existing algebraic normaliser splitting. The current splitting proof and its five algebraic helpers were read: they construct the complement by minimum coset representatives, without this covering theorem. Thus using that supplier here creates neither a logical nor a declared dependency cycle, despite the original article deriving its splitting corollary from the covering theorem. The arbitrary-rank lifting correspondence is proved locally, rather than applying the finite-rank chamber-morphism proposition outside its scope.

In finite rank, the retained chamber, adjacent-generator and generation conclusions apply the existing geometric suppliers and the constructed action agrees with the geometric normaliser action. No topology or chamber theorem for infinitely many generators is asserted. The original unqualified formulation remains recorded in the attribution reference; no original-article defect is established. The older Brink–Howlett, Lusztig, Howlett and Vinberg proofs have not been read for this adjudication.
