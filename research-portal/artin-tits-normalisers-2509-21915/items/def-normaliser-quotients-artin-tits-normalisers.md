## Definition

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system indexed by $S=\{s_i:i\in\Gamma_0\}$, let $A$ be its Artin–Tits group, and let $P=\ker(\pi:A\to W)$ be its pure Artin–Tits group. For $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$, let $W_J=\langle s_j:j\in J\rangle$ and $A_J=\langle\sigma_j:j\in J\rangle$ be the standard parabolic subgroups; the brackets denote the subgroups generated by the displayed elements.

For $G=W$ with $G_J=W_J$, or $G=A$ with $G_J=A_J$, define the **normaliser** of $G_J$ in $G$ by $\operatorname{Norm}_G(G_J)=\{g\in G:gG_Jg^{-1}=G_J\}$. Define the normaliser quotient groups $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$ and $N(A,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)/A_J$.

Define $N(P,J)=\{\beta A_J\in N(A,J):P\cap\beta A_J\ne\varnothing\}$. Equivalently, $N(P,J)$ is the image of $P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ under the quotient homomorphism $\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)\to N(A,J)$.

## Justification

The parabolic-subgroups definition supplies $W_J\leq W$ and $A_J\leq A$. The Artin–pure-lifts definition supplies $A$, the pure subgroup $P$ and the homomorphism $\pi$. The exact sequence in [[prop-classical-cover-artin-tits-normalisers]], with $P$ identified with its image in $A$ as in [[def-artin-pure-lifts-artin-tits-normalisers]], gives $P=\ker\pi$. By [[prop-normaliser-quotient-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]], each normaliser is a subgroup and the corresponding parabolic subgroup is normal in it; consequently both displayed quotient groups exist, with elements the indicated cosets.

By [[math:lem-intersection-of-subgroups]], applied to the two subgroups of $A$, the intersection $I=P\cap\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ is a subgroup of $A$. Let $q:\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)\to N(A,J)$ be the quotient homomorphism. Its restriction $q|_I:I\to N(A,J)$ is a homomorphism, since for $x,y\in I$ one has $q|_I(xy)=q(xy)=q(x)q(y)=q|_I(x)q|_I(y)$. By [[math:thm-image-subgroup-and-kernel-normal]], its image $q(I)$ is a subgroup of $N(A,J)$. If a coset $\beta A_J$ in $N(A,J)$ meets $P$ at $p$, then $p$ also belongs to $\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ because $\beta$ and $A_J$ do, and $pA_J=\beta A_J$. Conversely, the coset of any element of that intersection meets $P$. Thus the displayed set is exactly the stated image.
