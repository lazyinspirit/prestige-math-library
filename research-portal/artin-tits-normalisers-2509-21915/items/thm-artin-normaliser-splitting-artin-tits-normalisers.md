## Statement

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter diagram, meaning that its Coxeter group $W$ is finite, and let $J$ be a subset of its vertex set. Let $A$ be the associated Artin–Tits group and $A_J$ its standard parabolic subgroup. Let $\mathcal R(J)$ be the reduced ribbon groupoid, and write $\mathcal R_J$ for its vertex group at $J$, consisting of its arrows from $J$ to $J$ under composition.

The normaliser $\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)$ is the internal semidirect product $A_J\rtimes\mathcal R_J$: each normaliser element has a unique expression $pr$ with $p\in A_J$ and $r\in\mathcal R_J$. Consequently the quotient homomorphism restricts to an isomorphism $\mathcal R_J\cong N(A,J)$, where $N(A,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)/A_J$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The independent spherical ribbon splitting gives a groupoid on all subsets whose elementary arrows are $\Delta_I^{-1}\Delta_{I\cup\{a\}}:I\to\iota_{I\cup\{a\}}(I)$ for $a\notin I$. Its arrows conjugate the source standard parabolic subgroup to the target, using $g^{-1}A_Ig=A_K$ for an arrow $g:I\to K$. Its vertex group $R^{\mathrm{all}}_J$ satisfies $\operatorname{Norm}_A(A_J)=A_J\rtimes R^{\mathrm{all}}_J$, with unique factorisation $pr$. [[thm-ribbon-normaliser-input-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

[F2] The objects of $\mathcal R(J)$ are the associates of $J$, meaning the subsets $I$ for which $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$ in $W$. Its arrows are finite composable products of the same elementary ribbons and their inverses, with left-to-right composition. The target of an elementary ribbon is $\iota_{I\cup\{a\}}(I)$. [[def-reduced-ribbon-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The homomorphism $\pi:A\to W$ sends each standard Artin generator $\sigma_i$ to $s_i$; the subgroups $A_I$ and $W_I$ are generated by these respective generators. Thus $\pi(A_I)=W_I$. [[def-artin-pure-lifts-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] $A_J$ is normal in its normaliser, and $N(A,J)$ is the corresponding quotient group. [[def-normaliser-quotients-artin-tits-normalisers]]

## Proof
**Given:** The spherical diagram, subset $J$, Artin group, and reduced ribbon groupoid in the Statement.


**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Identify the vertex group in the independent splitting with $\mathcal R_J$ and prove the quotient assertion.

1.1 Every generating arrow of $\mathcal R(J)$ is an elementary arrow in the all-subsets groupoid of [F1], with the same element, source, and target; the targets agree because the opposition permutation carries $I$ onto $(I\cup\{a\})\setminus\{\iota_{I\cup\{a\}}(a)\}$. The same holds for inverses and products. Hence $\mathcal R_J\subseteq R^{\mathrm{all}}_J$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Conversely, express an arrow in $R^{\mathrm{all}}_J$ as a finite composable product of elementary arrows and their inverses. For any intermediate object $I$, the initial segment $g:J\to I$ satisfies $g^{-1}A_Jg=A_I$ by [F1]. Applying $\pi$ and [F3] gives $\pi(g)^{-1}W_J\pi(g)=W_I$, so $I$ is an associate of $J$. Every factor of the product therefore occurs between objects of $\mathcal R(J)$ and is one of its generators or an inverse. Thus $R^{\mathrm{all}}_J\subseteq\mathcal R_J$. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 By 1.1 and 1.2 the two vertex groups agree. Substituting this equality into [F1] gives the asserted internal semidirect product and unique factorisation. The quotient map in [F4] is surjective on $\mathcal R_J$: the coset of $pr$ is the coset of $r$. Its kernel on $\mathcal R_J$ is $A_J\cap\mathcal R_J=\{1\}$ by uniqueness of the factorisation. It is therefore an isomorphism onto $N(A,J)$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
