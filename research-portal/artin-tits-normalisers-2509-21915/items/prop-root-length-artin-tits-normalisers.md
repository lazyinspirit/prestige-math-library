## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be the Coxeter system of the stated Coxeter diagram, with geometric representation on $V=\bigoplus_{i\in\Gamma_0}\mathbb R\alpha_i$, roots $\Phi$, positive roots $\Phi^+=\Phi\cap\sum_i\mathbb R_{\geq0}\alpha_i$, negative roots $\Phi^-=-\Phi^+$, length $\ell$, and contragredient action on the algebraic dual $V^*$. The index set may be infinite.

The roots decompose as $\Phi=\Phi^+\sqcup\Phi^-$. The contragredient action faithfully realizes $W$ as a group generated by linear reflections. For $w\in W$, $\ell(w)=|\{\alpha\in\Phi^+:w\alpha\in\Phi^-\}|=\ell(w^{-1})$. If $w^{-1}\alpha_i\in\Phi^-$, then $\ell(s_iw)=\ell(w^{-1}s_i)<\ell(w)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] For a finite set of generators, roots have exactly one sign, the number of positive roots made negative by an element is its length, and length is invariant under inversion. If $x\alpha_i$ is negative, then $\ell(xs_i)<\ell(x)$. [[prop-root-inversion-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

[F2] For a finite set of generators, the geometric representation is faithful. [[math:thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]]

[F3] In a finite Coxeter system, a standard parabolic subgroup has the Coxeter presentation restricted to its generating subset, and its intrinsic length agrees with ambient length. [[math:thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]

## Proof
**Given:** The Coxeter presentation, geometric representation, roots, length and contragredient action in the Statement. [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]


**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove the sign, reflection, inversion-count and descent assertions for the given system.

1.1 **Finite subsystems.** For finite $J\subseteq S$, let $W_J^*$ be the group presented by the restricted Coxeter matrix, and let $V_J$ be the span of its simple roots. The natural map $W_J^*\to W$ is injective: an equality of two finite $J$-words in the presentation of $W$ has a derivation using finitely many defining relators and letters, all contained in some finite $K\supseteq J$; [F3] then gives the equality already in $W_J^*$. Similarly, the intrinsic length of an element of $W_J^*$ equals its length in $W$: any shorter ambient word and a finite derivation of its equality lie in some finite $K$, contradicting [F3]. The action of $W_J^*$ on $V_J$ is its finite-rank geometric representation, because the defining reflection formulas restrict to those for $J$. [F3, given]

2.1 **Root signs and inversion counts.** Each root $u\alpha_i$ belongs to a finite subsystem containing the letters of a word for $u$ and $s_i$; [F1] therefore gives it exactly one sign in the full basis. Fix $w$, choose finite $J$ containing a word for it, and write $n=\ell(w)$. By [F1] and 1.1 the subsystem on $J$ supplies $n$ distinct positive roots made negative by $w$. Conversely, any finite collection of such full-system roots, together with words representing them and $w$, is contained in a finite subsystem $K$. Their signs in that subsystem agree with their signs in the full basis, and [F1] and 1.1 show that at most $n$ of them occur. Hence the full inversion set has exactly $n$ elements. Reversing words in the involutory generators gives $\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(w)$. [F1, step 1.1, given]

2.2 **Faithful dual reflection action.** If $w\ne1$, choose finite $J$ containing a word for $w$. By 1.1 its image in $W_J^*$ is nontrivial, so [F2] shows that $w$ acts nontrivially on $V_J$, and hence on $V$. The algebraic dual separates vectors: a nonzero finite linear combination of basis vectors is detected by a coordinate functional. Thus a nonidentity invertible operator on $V$ has nonidentity contragredient action on $V^*$. For each generator, the dual formula is $(s_i f)(v)=f(v)-2B(v,\alpha_i)f(\alpha_i)$; it fixes pointwise the hyperplane $f(\alpha_i)=0$ and negates the nonzero functional $B(-,\alpha_i)$, so it is a linear reflection. These generator actions faithfully realize $W$. [F2, step 1.1, given]

3.1 **Descent.** If $w^{-1}\alpha_i$ is negative, take a finite subsystem containing $s_i$ and a word for $w$. By [F1] its intrinsic lengths satisfy $\ell(w^{-1}s_i)<\ell(w^{-1})$; 1.1 transfers this inequality to $W$. Reversal of words gives $\ell(w^{-1}s_i)=\ell(s_iw)$ and $\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(w)$. Together with 2.1 and 2.2, this proves every assertion. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
