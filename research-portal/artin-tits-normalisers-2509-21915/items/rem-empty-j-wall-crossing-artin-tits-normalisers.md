## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system with simple generators $s_i$ indexed by the vertices $i$ of a Coxeter diagram $\Gamma$. For $K\subseteq\Gamma_0$, write $W_K=\langle s_k:k\in K\rangle$. An associate of $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$ is a subset $I$ for which $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$, as in [[def-associates-artin-tits-normalisers]]. The empty subset is its only associate.

For every $a\in\Gamma_0$, the connected-support notation of [[def-connected-support-artin-tits-normalisers]] gives $[\varnothing,a]=\{a\}$, the group $W_{[\varnothing,a]}$ is cyclic of order two, and the simple wall-crossing element of [[def-simple-wall-crossing-artin-tits-normalisers]] is $v_{a,\varnothing}=s_a$.

## Proof


**Given:** the Coxeter system and vertex $a$ in the Statement.

[F1] The generators satisfy $s_i^2=1$, and in the standard geometric representation $s_i\alpha_i=-\alpha_i$ for a nonzero basis vector $\alpha_i$. Word length is the least length of a word in the simple generators. [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The standard parabolic $W_K$ is generated by the $s_k$ for $k\in K$; in particular $W_\varnothing=\{1\}$. [[def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F3] The set $[I,a]$ is the component containing $a$ in the diagram induced on $I\cup\{a\}$. [[def-connected-support-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F4] For finite $W_L$, $w_L$ is its maximum-length element; $w_\varnothing=1$. [[def-longest-elements-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F5] A pair $(x,I)$ lies in $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ exactly when $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ has minimum word length in $xW_I$. For such a label, if $a\in\Gamma_0\setminus I$, $D=[I,a]$ and $W_D$ is finite, the wall-crossing definition gives $v_{a,I}=w_{D\setminus\{a\}}w_D$. [[def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers]] [[def-simple-wall-crossing-artin-tits-normalisers]]



**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** prove the associate, component, group-order and wall-crossing assertions.

1.1 **Associates.** By [F1], $s_i$ acts nontrivially on $\alpha_i$, so $s_i\ne1$ for every vertex $i$. By [F2], a nonempty $I$ has $s_i\in W_I$ for some $i\in I$, so $W_I$ is nontrivial. Since a conjugate of $W_\varnothing=\{1\}$ is again $\{1\}$, no nonempty $I$ is an associate of $\varnothing$. The empty subset is an associate of itself by conjugation by $1$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 **Rank one.** The induced diagram on $\{a\}$ has only the vertex $a$, so [F3] gives $D=[\varnothing,a]=\{a\}$. By [F1] and [F2], $W_D=\{1,s_a\}$ and its two elements are distinct; hence it is cyclic of order two. In particular, $W_D$ is finite. The identity has length zero, while the nonidentity generator $s_a$ has length one. Thus [F4] gives $w_D=s_a$ and $w_{D\setminus\{a\}}=w_\varnothing=1$. [F1, F2, F3, F4]

2.1 **Chamber label and wall crossing.** Set $J=I=\varnothing$ and $x=1$. By [F2], $W_\varnothing 1=1W_\varnothing=\{1\}$. The identity is the only element in this coset and has word length zero by [F1], so it has minimum length there. The chamber-label criterion in [F5] therefore gives $(1,\varnothing)\in\mathrm{Cham}(\varnothing)$. Also $a\in\Gamma_0\setminus\varnothing$ by the given choice of vertex. Step 1.2 establishes the remaining finiteness hypothesis, so [F5] applies and gives $v_{a,\varnothing}=w_\varnothing w_{\{a\}}=1s_a=s_a$. Together with steps 1.1 and 1.2 this proves the Statement. [F1, F2, F5, given, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
