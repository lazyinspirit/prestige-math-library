## Statement

Suppose $\mathcal A(W)$ is an irreducible finite real reflection arrangement on $E$, with $\dim E>1$, in the sense of [[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-invariant-metric-essentiality]]. Its chamber-wall Coxeter system from [[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-chamber-wall-rank]] has connected Coxeter diagram in the convention of [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]. In particular, if $\dim E=3$, this system has connected rank three.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The wall normals form a basis, and $S$ generates $W$ ([[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-chamber-wall-rank]]).

[F2] An absent diagram edge means $m(s,t)=2$, hence $s$ and $t$ commute ([[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]).

[F3] Reflections are orthogonal and uniquely determined by their hyperplanes; a nontrivial product decomposition contradicts irreducibility ([[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-invariant-metric-essentiality]]).

## Proof
**Given:** The irreducible arrangement, invariant metric and essentiality of [[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-invariant-metric-essentiality]], the wall-normal basis and Coxeter generators from [[lem-author-fcf1e9b45588-chamber-wall-rank]], and the diagram convention of [[def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]. **Goal:** Exclude a disconnected diagram.


**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If the diagram is disconnected, split its vertices as nonempty unions of components $S_1\sqcup S_2$. For $s\in S_1$, $t\in S_2$, [F2] gives $st=ts$. The $-1$ eigenline of $s$ is therefore $t$-invariant. An orthogonal reflection has invariant lines either in its hyperplane or equal to its normal line: its restriction to an invariant line is $+1$ or $-1$. The normal lines of distinct wall reflections cannot coincide, by [F3]. Their normals are consequently perpendicular. Thus the spans $E_1,E_2$ of the two sets of wall normals are orthogonal and, by [F1], $E=E_1\oplus E_2$ with both factors nonzero. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The generators in $S_i$ act trivially on the other factor, so $W=W_1\times W_2$ on this orthogonal direct sum, where $W_i$ is generated by $S_i$. For $(w_1,w_2)$ the codimension of the fixed subspace is the sum of the codimensions for $w_i$. If it is a reflection, exactly one summand is one and the other zero; faithfulness of each factor's action makes the zero-codimension factor the identity. Hence every reflection hyperplane is $H_1\oplus E_2$ or $E_1\oplus H_2$, and all such factor reflection hyperplanes occur. This is a product arrangement, contradicting [F3]. The diagram is connected. When $\dim E=3$, [F1] also gives $|S|=3$. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra] ∎
