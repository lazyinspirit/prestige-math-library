## Example

Let $W$ be the Coxeter group with the $D_4$ diagram whose central vertex $2$ is joined to $1,3,4$. In its standard real reflection representation let $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_4$ be the simple-root basis, and let $\Phi=\{w\alpha_i:w\in W,\ 1\leq i\leq4\}$. A positive root is a root whose coordinates in this basis are all nonnegative. Then $\Phi^+=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_3,\alpha_4,\alpha_1+\alpha_2,\alpha_3+\alpha_2,\alpha_4+\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3,\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_4,\alpha_3+\alpha_2+\alpha_4,\alpha_1+\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4,\alpha_1+2\alpha_2+\alpha_3+\alpha_4\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The generators $s_i$ act by $s_i(x)=x-2B(x,\alpha_i)\alpha_i$, where $B(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=1$, $B(\alpha_2,\alpha_j)=-1/2$ for $j\in\{1,3,4\}$, and the other off-diagonal basis pairings are zero. [[def-d4-example-data-artin-tits-normalisers]]

[F2] The root set consists of the $W$-translates of the simple roots, and $W$ is generated by the simple reflections. [[def-real-coxeter-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites]]

## Verification



**Given:** the D4 representation and positive-root convention in the Example.

**Goal:** prove that its positive roots are exactly the twelve displayed vectors.

1.1 Write $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{12}$ for the vectors in the displayed order, and put $R=\{\pm\beta_1,\ldots,\pm\beta_{12}\}$. For a vector with coordinates $(c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4)$, [F1] gives $c_i'=-c_i+\sum_{j\sim i}c_j$ for the coordinate changed by $s_i$, leaving the others fixed; $j\sim i$ means that $j$ and $i$ are joined in the D4 diagram. Direct substitution gives the following images of $\beta_k$, where $-j$ denotes $-\beta_j$ and $j$ denotes $\beta_j$. In the order $s_1,s_2,s_3,s_4$, the rows for $k=1,\ldots,12$ are $(-1,5,1,1)$, $(5,-2,6,7)$, $(3,6,-3,3)$, $(4,7,4,-4)$, $(2,1,8,9)$, $(8,3,2,10)$, $(9,4,10,2)$, $(6,8,5,11)$, $(7,9,11,5)$, $(11,10,7,6)$, $(10,12,9,8)$, and $(12,11,12,12)$. Thus each $s_i$ preserves $R$: the table covers positive entries and linearity covers their negatives. Since $R$ contains every simple root, [F2] gives $\Phi\subseteq R$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 Conversely, $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_4$ are simple roots. The table gives $\beta_5=s_2\beta_1$, $\beta_6=s_2\beta_3$, $\beta_7=s_2\beta_4$, $\beta_8=s_3\beta_5$, $\beta_9=s_4\beta_5$, $\beta_{10}=s_4\beta_6$, $\beta_{11}=s_4\beta_8$, and $\beta_{12}=s_2\beta_{11}$. Hence all $\beta_k$ are roots. Each negative $-\beta_k$ is also a root: for $\beta_k=w\alpha_i$, it equals $ws_i\alpha_i$ by [F1]. Therefore $R\subseteq\Phi$. The twelve $\beta_k$ have nonnegative, nonzero coordinate tuples, whereas their negatives have a negative coordinate, so the stated positive roots are exactly those twelve. [F1, F2, step 1.1] ∎
