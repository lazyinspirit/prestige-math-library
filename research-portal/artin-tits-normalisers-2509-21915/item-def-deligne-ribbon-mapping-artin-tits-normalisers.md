## Definition

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter diagram, meaning that its Coxeter group $W$ is finite, with vertex set $\Gamma_0$, and fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. For $I\subseteq\Gamma_0$, write $W_I$ for the subgroup generated by the simple reflections indexed by $I$. A chamber label is a pair $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ of minimum Coxeter length in $xW_I$. Identify these labels with the chambers of the relative arrangement. Let $\mathcal D=\mathcal D(J)$ be its arrangement groupoid: its objects are the chambers, its directed generating arrows are the simple crossings between adjacent chambers, and its positive-category relations identify geodesic paths with common endpoints. Let $\mathcal R=\mathcal R(J)$ be the reduced ribbon groupoid, whose objects are the subsets $I$ for which $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$ and whose elementary arrows are the ribbons $\mu_{a,I}$ for $a\in\Gamma_0\setminus I$.

Define $\mathcal G$ on the directed chamber graph by $\mathcal G(x,I)=I$ on vertices and $\mathcal G(\omega_{a,I})=\mu_{a,I}$ on a generating arrow $\omega_{a,I}:(x,I)\to(y,K)$. Explicitly, with $T=I\cup\{a\}$, the target is $K=T\setminus\{\iota_T(a)\}$, where $\iota_T$ is the opposition permutation of the finite parabolic $W_T$. The elementary ribbon is $\mu_{a,I}=\Delta_I^{-1}\Delta_T:I\to K$, where $\Delta_L$ is the positive reduced-word lift of the longest element of $W_L$.

Let $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$ act on $\mathcal D$ through its action on relative chambers. Write $\mathcal Q:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D/N(W,J)$ for the quotient functor: it sends each object and arrow to its orbit under this action. Composition is read from left to right.

## Justification

The chamber-label definition and the chamber-labelling theorem identify every object of $\mathcal D$ with one such pair. Its second component $I$ is an object of $\mathcal R$ because $W_Jx=xW_I$ implies $W_I=x^{-1}W_Jx$. By the adjacent-wall formula, each directed generating arrow has a unique crossing index $a$ at its source. The simple-crossing definition uses the opposition permutation of the component containing $a$ in $T=I\cup\{a\}$. Since $W$ is finite, its subgroup $W_T$ is finite. The finite-parabolic wall-formula lemma therefore identifies the crossing target as $T\setminus\{\iota_T(a)\}$. The ribbon-generator lift lemma gives this same target $K$ for $\mu_{a,I}$, and the reduced ribbon groupoid contains this elementary arrow. Thus the displayed rule is a well-defined map of directed graphs, preserving sources and targets.

The label $(1,J)$ exists, so the chamber-labelling theorem gives an object of $\mathcal D$. The finite-wall-path lemma connects any two chamber objects by a finite sequence of simple crossings; their composite in $\mathcal D$ is an arrow between those objects. Thus $\mathcal D$ is nonempty and connected. It is small: the chamber labels form a subset of the set $W\times\mathcal P(\Gamma_0)$, the directed chamber graph has a set of arrows, and the arrangement-groupoid construction presents its morphisms as equivalence classes of finite composable words in these arrows and formal inverses. These words form a set, so their quotient is a set as well.

The normaliser chamber-action lemma gives an action free on chambers, and the induced-action result gives an action by automorphisms of $\mathcal D$. The connected small groupoid $\mathcal D$ therefore satisfies the hypotheses of the quotient-groupoid construction: orbit representatives can be aligned uniquely for composition, so taking object and arrow orbits defines the stated functor $\mathcal Q$.
