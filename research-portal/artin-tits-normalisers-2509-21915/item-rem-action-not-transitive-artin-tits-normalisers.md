## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system, and fix $J\subseteq S$. For $I\subseteq S$, let $W_I$ be the subgroup generated by $I$, and let $\ell$ be word length in $S$. A chamber label is a pair $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ for which $x$ has minimum length in $xW_I$. The normaliser quotient $N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$ acts on these labels, and hence on the corresponding relative chambers. An action is transitive if any chamber can be carried to any other by an acting element.

When $J=\varnothing$, this action is transitive. In general it need not be transitive: for type $A_2$, with $S=\{s,t\}$, $m(s,t)=3$ and $J=\{s\}$, the labels $(1,\{s\})$ and $(ts,\{t\})$ lie in different orbits.

## Facts & Assumptions


[F1] Labels correspond bijectively to relative chambers. The normaliser quotient preserves the second component of a label and acts transitively on all labels with a fixed second component. [thm-chamber-labelling-artin-tits-normalisers](item-thm-chamber-labelling-artin-tits-normalisers) [lem-normaliser-chamber-action-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-normaliser-chamber-action-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F2] In the canonical real representation, the simple roots $e_u$ form a basis, $u(e_v)=e_v-2B(e_v,e_u)e_u$, and $B(e_u,e_v)=-\cos(\pi/m(u,v))$ for distinct generators with finite label. Each simple reflection is nonidentity. [def-real-coxeter-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-def-real-coxeter-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites)

[F3] A root is positive when its simple-root coordinates are nonnegative. For $w\in W$ and $u\in S$, $\ell(wu)>\ell(w)$ when $we_u$ is positive. [prop-root-inversion-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-root-inversion-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites)

[F4] The Coxeter presentation has $s^2=t^2=1$ and, when $m(s,t)=3$, $sts=tst$. The label and quotient conventions are those in the Statement. [def-real-coxeter-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-def-real-coxeter-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites) [def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-normaliser-quotients-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-normaliser-quotients-artin-tits-normalisers)

## Proof


**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system and labels in the Statement.

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

**Goal:** Prove transitivity for empty $J$ and verify the stated nontransitive example.

1.1 If $J=\varnothing$, then $W_J=\{1\}$. For any label $(x,I)$, its defining equality gives $xW_I=\{x\}$, hence $W_I=\{1\}$. Each simple generator is nonidentity by [F2], so $I=\varnothing$. All labels therefore have the same second component. By [F1], the action is transitive on labels and hence on chambers. [F1, F2, F4]

1.2 In type $A_2$, put $J=\{s\}$ and $x=ts$. The pair $(1,\{s\})$ is a label: $W_s1=1W_s$, and $1$ has length zero. The braid relation gives $sx=sts=tst=xt$, so $W_sx=xW_t$. Since $B(e_s,e_t)=-1/2$, the reflection formula gives $se_t=e_t+e_s$ and $t(se_t)=e_s$. Thus $xe_t=e_s$ is positive, and [F3] gives $\ell(xt)>\ell(x)$. As $W_t=\{1,t\}$, $x$ is the minimum in $xW_t$, so $(x,\{t\})$ is also a label. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 The two labels have different second components. By [F1], the action preserves that component, so they cannot lie in the same orbit. Their corresponding chambers are distinct by the chamber bijection, proving the claimed failure of transitivity. [F1, step 1.2] ∎
