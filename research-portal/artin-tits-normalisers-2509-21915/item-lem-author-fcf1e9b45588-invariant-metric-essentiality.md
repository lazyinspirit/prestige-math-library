## Statement

A finite real reflection arrangement in a finite-dimensional real vector space $E$ means the set $\mathcal A(W)=\{\operatorname{Fix}(r):r\in W\text{ is a reflection}\}$ for a finite subgroup $W\leq\mathrm{GL}(E)$ generated by reflections. Here a reflection is an involution with a codimension-one fixed subspace. A product arrangement on $E=E_1\oplus E_2$ consists of the hyperplanes $H_1\oplus E_2$ and $E_1\oplus H_2$ from arrangements on the two factors; a positive-dimensional empty factor is allowed. The arrangement is irreducible when it has no such decomposition with both $E_i\ne0$. For every such finite real reflection group $W$, the space $E$ admits a $W$-invariant positive-definite inner product. In that metric every reflection is the orthogonal reflection in its fixed hyperplane, and distinct reflections have distinct fixed hyperplanes. If additionally $\dim E>1$ and $\mathcal A(W)$ is irreducible, then $E^W=0$ (the action is essential).

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A linear hyperplane has codimension one, and arrangements record distinct hyperplanes themselves, as in [def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers).

[F2] A real inner product is a symmetric bilinear form with $(x,x)>0$ for $x\ne0$ and $(0,0)=0$ ([math:def-inner-product-space](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-inner-product-space)).

[F3] Orthogonality means $(x,y)=0$, and $K^\perp=\{x\in E:(x,k)=0\text{ for every }k\in K\}$ ([math:def-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-orthogonality-and-orthogonal-complement)).

[F4] For two subspaces, $E=U\oplus V$ means $U+V=E$ and $U\cap V=\{0\}$ ([math:def-internal-direct-sum](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-internal-direct-sum)).

[F5] For any subspace $K$ of a finite-dimensional real inner product space $E$, one has $E=K\oplus K^\perp$, with unique decompositions into the two subspaces ([math:thm-finite-dimensional-orthogonal-decomposition](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/thm-finite-dimensional-orthogonal-decomposition)).

[F6] For the natural action of $W$ on $E$, $E^g=\{x\in E:gx=x\}$ and $E^W=\{x\in E:gx=x\text{ for every }g\in W\}$; the notation $\operatorname{Fix}(r)$ means $E^r$ ([math:def-fixed-point-sets-of-a-group-action](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-fixed-point-sets-of-a-group-action)).

[F7] If $R$ is the set of reflections in $W$, saying that $W$ is generated by reflections means $W=\langle R\rangle$, the smallest subgroup of $W$ containing $R$ ([math:def-generated-subgroup](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-generated-subgroup)).

[F8] For a real linear endomorphism $T$ of $E$ and $\lambda\in\mathbb R$, its $\lambda$-eigenspace is $E_\lambda(T)=\{v\in E:T(v)=\lambda v\}$, including the zero vector ([math:def-eigenvalue-eigenvector-eigenspace-and-spectrum](https://alphabetamath.cc/item/def-eigenvalue-eigenvector-eigenspace-and-spectrum)).

## Proof

**Given:** A finite group $W$ generated by real linear reflections on $E$, its arrangement as in [def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-hyperplane-arrangement-artin-tits-normalisers), and the product and irreducibility conventions in the Statement. **Goal:** Construct the invariant metric and prove reflection uniqueness for every such finite group, and prove essentiality under the additional dimension and irreducibility hypotheses.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose a basis of $E$, let $(\ ,\ )_0$ be its Euclidean inner product, and set $(x,y)=\sum_{w\in W}(wx,wy)_0$. This is bilinear and symmetric; for $x\ne0$ its identity summand is positive and the other diagonal summands are nonnegative, so it is an inner product by [F2]. Reindexing by $w\mapsto wg$ proves $W$-invariance. For an involutive reflection $r$, the $+1$ and $-1$ eigenspaces are perpendicular by [F3]: $(u,v)=(ru,rv)=-(u,v)$. The identity $x=(x+rx)/2+(x-rx)/2$ decomposes $E$ as their direct sum. Their dimensions are therefore $\dim E-1$ and $1$. Thus $r$ is uniquely determined by its fixed hyperplane, acting as $+1$ there and $-1$ on its orthogonal line. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F8, algebra]

2.1 Put $K=E^W=\bigcap_{H\in\mathcal A(W)}H$, the equality holding because reflections generate $W$. In the finite-dimensional real inner product space constructed in step 1.1, $K$ is a subspace, so [F5] gives $E=K\oplus L$ for $L=K^\perp$. The space $L$ is $W$-stable: for $g\in W$, $\ell\in L$ and $k\in K$, invariance gives $(g\ell,k)=(\ell,g^{-1}k)=(\ell,k)=0$. For each arrangement hyperplane $H$, one has $K\subseteq H$. Writing $h=k+\ell$ by [F5] gives $\ell=h-k\in H\cap L$; hence $H=K\oplus(H\cap L)$ by [F4]. Since $H$ has codimension one in $E$, $H\cap L$ has codimension one in $L$. [step 1.1, given, F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

3.1 For the essentiality assertion, now assume $\dim E>1$ and $\mathcal A(W)$ is irreducible. If $0\ne K\ne E$, then both $K$ and $L$ are nonzero. The hyperplanes described in step 2.1 constitute the product of the empty arrangement on $K$ and the arrangement $\{H\cap L:H\in\mathcal A(W)\}$ on $L$, contrary to irreducibility. If $K=E$, then $W=1$ and the arrangement is empty; for $\dim E>1$, a basis line and the span of the remaining basis vectors are nonzero complementary subspaces, and decompose that empty arrangement as a product, again contradicting irreducibility. Hence $K=0$. This proves essentiality and all metric assertions. [step 1.1, step 2.1, given, F1, F4, F6, algebra] ∎
