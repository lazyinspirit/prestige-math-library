## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank spherical Coxeter system, meaning that $W$ is finite, and fix $J\subseteq S$. Write $W_J$ for the subgroup generated by $J$ and $N=N(W,J)=\operatorname{Norm}_W(W_J)/W_J$. Let $\mathcal D$ be the relative arrangement groupoid and $\mathcal R$ the reduced ribbon groupoid. With the functors $\mathcal G:\mathcal D\to\mathcal R$ and $\mathcal Q:\mathcal D\to\mathcal D/N$ of [def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers), there is an isomorphism of groupoids $\mathcal G':\mathcal D/N\to\mathcal R$ satisfying $\mathcal G=\mathcal G'\circ\mathcal Q$.

The coverings $\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal G$ are isomorphic via the identity on $\mathcal D$ and $\mathcal G'$ on their targets. In particular, $\mathcal G$ is a normal covering and its deck transformation group is isomorphic to $N$ via the action of $N$ on $\mathcal D$. A covering is a functor with a unique lift from each specified source object for every arrow starting at its image; a deck transformation is an automorphism of the source groupoid over the functor. Normality means that deck transformations act transitively on a fibre.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The objects of $\mathcal D$ are the relative chambers, labelled by pairs $(x,I)$ with $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ of minimum length in $xW_I$. Its arrows include the simple wall crossings. The functor $\mathcal Q$ takes objects and arrows to their $N$-orbits. [def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-deligne-ribbon-mapping-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-arrangement-groupoids-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-arrangement-groupoids-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F2] Any two relative chambers are joined by a finite sequence of simple wall crossings. [lem-finite-wall-path-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-finite-wall-path-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F3] The action of $N$ on the chamber labels is free, and it induces an action by automorphisms of $\mathcal D$. [lem-normaliser-chamber-action-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-normaliser-chamber-action-artin-tits-normalisers) [rem-normaliser-deligne-action-artin-tits-normalisers](item-rem-normaliser-deligne-action-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F4] For a group acting by automorphisms on a connected small groupoid, freely on objects, the orbit functor is a normal covering and the action identifies the acting group with its deck transformation group. [prop-quotient-cover-artin-tits-normalisers](item-prop-quotient-cover-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F5] In the present spherical setting, $\mathcal G$ factors as $\mathcal G'\circ\mathcal Q$, with $\mathcal G':\mathcal D/N\to\mathcal R$ an isomorphism of groupoids. [prop-deligne-ribbon-quotient-artin-tits-normalisers](item-prop-deligne-ribbon-quotient-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F6] A covering, deck transformation, and normal covering have the meanings stated above. [def-groupoid-cover-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-groupoid-cover-artin-tits-normalisers)

## Proof
**Given:** The finite-rank spherical Coxeter system and subset $J$ in the Statement.


**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove that the two coverings are isomorphic and identify the normal covering and deck group of $\mathcal G$.

1.1 **Apply the orbit-cover theorem.** The label $(1,J)$ is an object: $W_J1=1W_J$, and the identity has minimum length in $W_J$. Thus $\mathcal D$ is nonempty. By [F2], every two of its objects are joined by a sequence of simple crossings, which compose to an arrow by [F1]. Hence $\mathcal D$ is connected. It is small by its construction in [F1]. The action in [F3] is therefore an action by automorphisms on a connected small groupoid, free on objects. By [F4], $\mathcal Q$ is a normal covering and $n\mapsto(C\mapsto nC,\,a\mapsto na)$ is an isomorphism from $N$ to its deck group. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given]

2.1 **Transport the covering.** By [F5], $\mathcal G=\mathcal G'\circ\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal G'$ is an isomorphism. For an arrow $r$ of $\mathcal R$ starting at $\mathcal G(C)$, its unique preimage under $\mathcal G'$ starts at $\mathcal Q(C)$; the unique lift of that preimage from $C$ under $\mathcal Q$ is precisely the unique lift of $r$ from $C$ under $\mathcal G$. Thus $\mathcal G$ is a covering. The identity functor upstairs and $\mathcal G'$ downstairs form the asserted isomorphism of coverings. [F5, F6, step 1.1]

3.1 **Transport deck transformations and normality.** For an automorphism $d$ of $\mathcal D$, the equality $\mathcal Gd=\mathcal G$ is equivalent, after composing with $(\mathcal G')^{-1}$, to $\mathcal Qd=\mathcal Q$. Hence the two deck groups are the same subgroup of automorphisms of $\mathcal D$. Since $\mathcal G'$ is injective on objects, the fibres of $\mathcal G$ and $\mathcal Q$ coincide. The deck group therefore acts transitively on a fibre of $\mathcal G$ because it does so for $\mathcal Q$. Consequently $\mathcal G$ is normal and its deck group is identified with $N$ by the action described in 1.1. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
