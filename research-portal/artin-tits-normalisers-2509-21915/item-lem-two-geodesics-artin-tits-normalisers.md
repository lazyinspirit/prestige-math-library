## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system with diagram vertex set $\Gamma_0$, geometric root set $\Phi$, and Coxeter length $\ell$. Fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. For $L\subseteq\Gamma_0$, let $W_L$ be the subgroup generated by its simple reflections and $\Phi_L=\{w\alpha_i:w\in W_L,\ i\in L\}$. An associate of $J$ is a subset whose standard parabolic subgroup is conjugate to $W_J$. The Brink–Howlett groupoid $\mathcal{BH}(J)$ has these associates as objects; its morphisms $I\to K$ are the elements $w\in W$ satisfying $\{w\alpha_k:k\in K\}=\{\alpha_i:i\in I\}$, with composition by multiplication from left to right.

In the relative arrangement $(\mathrm{Cone}(J)^\circ,\mathcal H^J)$, write $(x,I)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ for a chamber label. A chamber path is a finite sequence of adjacent chambers; it is geodesic if its number of crossings is minimal among paths with the same endpoints. For $a\notin I$, when the component $D=[I,a]$ containing $a$ in the diagram induced on $I\cup\{a\}$ has finite $W_D$, put $v_{a,I}=w_{D\setminus\{a\}}w_D$, where $w_L$ is the longest element of the finite parabolic $W_L$. Write $\omega_{a,I}$ for the associated simple wall crossing. A standard expression is a composable sequence of these wall-crossing elements whose Coxeter lengths sum to the length of their product.

Let distinct $a,b\in\Gamma_0\setminus I$ satisfy $|\Phi_{I\cup\{a,b\}}\setminus\Phi_I|<\infty$, and let $(x,I)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$. Let $q=v_{a,b,I}:I\to K$ be the two-generator element supplied by [lem-two-generator-minimal-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-two-generator-minimal-artin-tits-normalisers). Its two standard expressions lift to exactly two geodesic paths of equal length from $(x,I)$ to $(xq,K)$, beginning respectively with $\omega_{a,I}$ and $\omega_{b,I}$. If a chamber $C$ admits geodesics from $(x,I)$ beginning with each of these crossings, then some geodesic from $(x,I)$ to $C$ passes through $(xq,K)$.

## Facts & Assumptions


[F1] The two-generator lemma supplies $q:I\to K$, exactly two standard expressions for it, one beginning with each of $v_{a,I}$ and $v_{b,I}$, and equal numbers of factors in those expressions. [lem-two-generator-minimal-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-two-generator-minimal-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F2] Standard expressions from $I$ lift uniquely from $(x,I)$ to geodesics, with endpoint obtained by right multiplication by their product; conversely every geodesic has a standard expression as its wall word. [lem-standard-geodesic-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-standard-geodesic-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F3] A chamber $C=(y,L)$ determines the morphism $w=x^{-1}y:I\to L$ associated with the endpoints. [prop-chambers-morphisms-artin-tits-normalisers](item-prop-chambers-morphisms-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F4] If a morphism $w:I\to L$ has both $v_{a,I}$ and $v_{b,I}$ as length-additive left divisors, then $w=qz$ with $z:K\to L$ a morphism and $\ell(w)=\ell(q)+\ell(z)$. [lem-author-a7d202611130-simultaneous-prefix](item-lem-author-a7d202611130-simultaneous-prefix)

[F5] A standard expression is a composable wall-element sequence whose factor lengths add to the Coxeter length of its product. A left divisor $u$ of $w$ means $w=ut$ with $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(t)$. [def-standard-expressions-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-standard-expressions-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers)

## Proof


**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system, relative chamber label, and distinct $a,b$ with finite relative root difference in the Statement.

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove the two-path assertion and the geodesic-through-$(xq,K)$ assertion.

1.1 By [F1] the two standard expressions for $q$ have equal factor counts and start with the two specified elements. By [F2] they lift uniquely from $(x,I)$ to geodesics ending at $(xq,K)$, with the specified initial crossings. Conversely, the wall word of any geodesic to $(xq,K)$ is a standard expression for the endpoint morphism $q$ by [F2] and [F3]. The two expressions in [F1] therefore give all such geodesics, and their equal factor counts give equal path lengths. [F1, F2, F3, given]

1.2 Write $C=(y,L)$ and $w=x^{-1}y:I\to L$. The two given geodesics have standard wall words for $w$ by [F2] and [F3]. Since these words begin with $v_{a,I}$ and $v_{b,I}$, respectively, each is a length-additive left divisor of $w$: the length equality for a standard word and subadditivity of Coxeter length force additivity between its first factor and the product of its remaining factors. By [F4], $w=qz$ for a morphism $z:K\to L$ and $\ell(w)=\ell(q)+\ell(z)$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, given]

2.1 Choose a standard expression for $q$ and one for $z$; these exist by [F1] and [F2], including the empty expression if $z$ is an identity. They are composable, and the length equality in step 1.2 makes their concatenation a standard expression for $w$. By [F2] its lift from $(x,I)$ is a geodesic ending at $(y,L)=C$. After its initial expression for $q$, it passes through $(xq,K)$, again by [F2]. This proves the remaining assertion. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.2] ∎
