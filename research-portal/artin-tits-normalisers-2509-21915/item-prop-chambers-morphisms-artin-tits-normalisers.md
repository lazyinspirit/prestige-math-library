## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a finite-rank Coxeter system, with vertices $\Gamma_0$, simple roots $\alpha_i$, length $\ell$, and standard parabolic subgroups $W_I=\langle s_i:i\in I\rangle$. Fix $J\subseteq\Gamma_0$. A subset $I$ is an associate of $J$ when $W_I$ is conjugate to $W_J$ in $W$. The chamber-label set $\mathrm{Cham}(J)$ consists of pairs $(x,I)$ such that $W_Jx=xW_I$ and $x$ has minimum length in $xW_I$. The Brink–Howlett morphisms $I\to K$ are the elements $w\in W$ satisfying $w\{\alpha_k:k\in K\}=\{\alpha_i:i\in I\}$. The universal wall groupoid has these labels as objects and exactly one arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(y,K)$, labelled $x^{-1}y$.

For associates $I,K$ of $J$, if $(x,I),(y,K)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$, then $x^{-1}y\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{BH}(J)}(I,K)$. Conversely, if $w\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{BH}(J)}(I,K)$ and $(x,I)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$, then $(xw,K)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$, and $w$ labels the unique arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(xw,K)$ in $\widetilde{\mathcal{BH}}(J)$. Under the relative-chamber labelling bijection, the same assertions apply to the corresponding geometric chambers.

## Facts & Assumptions


[F1] A chamber label $(z,L)$ satisfies $W_Jz=zW_L$ and is minimal in $zW_L$. Conversely these two properties define a label. The subgroup $W_L$ is generated by the simple reflections indexed by $L$. [def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-chamber-labels-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-parabolic-subgroups-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F2] For each label $(z,L)$, the element $z$ maps the set of simple roots indexed by $L$ bijectively onto the set indexed by $J$. [lem-nonnegative-root-bases-artin-tits-normalisers](item-lem-nonnegative-root-bases-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F3] An element $d$ is minimal in $dW_L$ exactly when $\ell(ds_i)>\ell(d)$ for every $i\in L$; equivalently, it sends all positive roots of $W_L$ to positive roots. [prop-parabolic-minimum-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites](item-prop-parabolic-minimum-interface-artin-tits-prerequisites)

[F4] The geometric representation is faithful, and for each simple generator $s_i$ its action is the reflection $v\mapsto v-2B(v,\alpha_i)\alpha_i$, where $B(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=1$. These reflections preserve $B$, hence so does every element of $W$. A root is positive when its simple-root coordinates are nonnegative. [def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-coxeter-data-artin-tits-normalisers) [prop-root-length-artin-tits-normalisers](item-prop-root-length-artin-tits-normalisers)

[F5] Brink–Howlett arrows are defined by the stated equality of simple-root sets, and the universal wall groupoid has the stated singleton hom-sets. The labelling theorem identifies the algebraic labels with the relative geometric chambers in the finite-rank setting. [def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-brink-howlett-artin-tits-normalisers) [def-universal-wall-groupoid-artin-tits-normalisers](item-def-universal-wall-groupoid-artin-tits-normalisers) [thm-chamber-labelling-artin-tits-normalisers](item-thm-chamber-labelling-artin-tits-normalisers)

## Proof


**Given:** The finite-rank Coxeter system, subsets and labels in the Statement; neither $W$ nor its standard parabolic subgroups are assumed finite.

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Goal:** Prove both implications and the asserted uniqueness of the universal-groupoid arrow.

1.1 For labels $(x,I)$ and $(y,K)$, [F2] gives $x\{\alpha_i:i\in I\}=\{\alpha_j:j\in J\}=y\{\alpha_k:k\in K\}$. Applying $x^{-1}$ shows that $x^{-1}y$ sends the simple-root set of $K$ onto that of $I$. By [F5], it is a Brink–Howlett morphism from $I$ to $K$. [F2, F5]

1.2 Now let $w:I\to K$ be a Brink–Howlett morphism. For each $k\in K$, write $w\alpha_k=\alpha_i$ for its corresponding $i\in I$. Preservation of $B$ and the reflection formula in [F4] show that $ws_kw^{-1}$ and $s_i$ have the same action on every vector; faithfulness makes them equal in $W$. Since $w$ bijects the two simple-root sets, these equalities give $wW_Kw^{-1}=W_I$. Therefore, using $W_Jx=xW_I$, we obtain $W_Jxw=xwW_K$. [F1, F4, F5, given]

1.3 By minimality of $x$ in $xW_I$, [F3] makes each $x\alpha_i$ with $i\in I$ positive. For every $k\in K$, the root $xw\alpha_k$ is one of these positive roots. Let $\beta$ be any positive root of $W_K$. The reflection formula in [F4] shows that each $s_k$ with $k\in K$ preserves $V_K=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\alpha_k:k\in K\}$. By the definition of parabolic roots, $\beta$ therefore lies in $V_K$; its positivity gives $\beta=\sum_{k\in K}c_k\alpha_k$ with $c_k\geq0$. By linearity, $xw\beta=\sum_{k\in K}c_k(xw\alpha_k)$ has nonnegative simple-root coordinates. It is a root, since $xw\in W$, and hence is positive by [F4]. Thus $xw$ sends every positive root of $W_K$ to a positive root. [F1, F3, F4, F5, given]

2.1 Apply the all-positive-parabolic-roots criterion in [F3] to step 1.3: $xw$ is minimal in $xwW_K$. Together with step 1.2, [F1] gives $(xw,K)\in\mathrm{Cham}(J)$. The singleton arrow from $(x,I)$ to $(xw,K)$ has label $x^{-1}(xw)=w$. Finally, [F5] transfers these assertions to the corresponding geometric chambers. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎

## Source notes

The proof follows the article's reduction of the first assertion to the simple-root sets associated with individual labels. The converse uses the same positivity and coset-minimality mechanism, with the conjugation of standard parabolic subgroups made explicit. The geometric identification used here is supplied only in finite rank; no infinite-rank topological extension is asserted.
