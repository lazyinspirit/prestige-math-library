Regular two-level fractions are selected from the coded $\{-1,+1\}^k$ run cube by independent generator equations. Their signed defining subgroup records all exact and opposite-column relations; resolution is the shortest nonidentity defining word. The proofs establish the run count and show that an equal-column alias gives only a combined coefficient in an unrestricted linear model.

A complete mirror foldover reverses every factor sign, so a word of order $r$ changes by $(-1)^r$. It separates opposite-parity aliases while same-parity relations may remain. Reversing a selected set $J$ retains exactly the original defining words with even intersection with $J$; this formula lists residual aliases for partial follow-ups. Resolution and aliasing are design-matrix properties. Sparsity or hierarchy assumptions used to interpret a combined effect are additional modeling assumptions, not consequences of the design.
