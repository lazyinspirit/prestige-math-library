This page defines survival, reliability, density hazards, cumulative hazards,
and exponential and Weibull lifetime families. For a nonnegative event time with
a finite-valued Lebesgue density and $S_T(0)=1$, it proves the cumulative-hazard
identity under Countable Choice and Dependent Choice, up to the first zero of
survival; when that zero occurs at a finite time, the improper cumulative
hazard diverges there. Atom jumps satisfy a separate multiplicative relation.
For iid right-censored exponential observations, it derives the dominated
likelihood and rate maximizer under a common sigma-finite censor dominator and
independent, parameter-distinct censoring. These results are conditional on
their sampling and observation assumptions; they do not establish that a real
process is noninformative or that a fitted family is adequate. Recurrent
failures of repairable systems are outside the single-lifetime model.
