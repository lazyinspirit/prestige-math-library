These worked cases separate the ratio's arithmetic from the conditions that
give it a causal interpretation. A small first stage can magnify changes in
the reduced form; a direct instrument effect breaks exclusion; defiers can
make the ratio differ from the complier effect; and heterogeneous effects can
make a valid LATE differ from the ATE. The final counterexample gives two
full-data laws with the same observed law and different population ATEs when
monotonicity and effect homogeneity are not assumed. None of these examples
turns model assumptions into empirical facts.
