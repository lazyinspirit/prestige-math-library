Published pages define finite-dimensional quantum mathematics: complex inner-product spaces, ordered registers and tensor products; pure and mixed states, measurements, channels and gates. Circuit syntax, denotational semantics, and resource costs are explicit. Other pages study reversible computation, gate universality, exact and approximate synthesis, state preparation, uniform circuit families, promise problems, and complexity. Branches treat stabilizer circuits and error correction, fault tolerance, teleportation, measurement-based computation, topological codes and braid gates, circuit optimization, and lower bounds. No claim of physical realization or experimental outcomes is made.

Linear Algebra and Probability define finite states, measurements, and circuit semantics; Computability Theory supports uniform families and complexity. Abstract Algebra and Number Theory supply group and arithmetic methods for gates and algorithms; Braid Groups support the topological anyon branch. Core circuits, states, and channels underpin Quantum Algorithms and Information Theory; fusion and anyon constructions connect to Categorification and Geometric Representation Theory. The theory stays within the finite-dimensional mathematical model.
