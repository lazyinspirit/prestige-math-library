These examples use the plus-$Q$ convention and the proved A-page basis. They show a one-generator multiplication table, the quotient identity for the zero form, and why the PBW theorem does not imply semisimplicity.

## Examples

- [[ex-one-dimensional-clifford-algebra]]: for $V=ke$ with $B(e,e)=a$, the basis is $1,\bar e$ and $\bar e^2=a1$, including the degenerate value $a=0$.
- [[ex-zero-quadratic-clifford-algebra-is-exterior]]: when $B=Q=0$, the Clifford and exterior quotient ideals coincide; the ordered square-free bases match.
- [[cex-clifford-algebra-need-not-be-semisimple]]: with $V=ke$ and $Q=0$, the nonzero element $\varepsilon=\bar e$ satisfies $\varepsilon^2=0$, and the left regular module is not semisimple.

These boundary cases do not supply a classification for nonzero coefficients or degenerate forms of higher rank.
