## Parameters and the standard basis

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system, with length $\ell$, and let $\Gamma$ be a totally ordered abelian group, written additively. Its group algebra is $A=\mathbb Z[\Gamma]$, with basis symbols $v^\gamma$, involution $\overline{v^\gamma}=v^{-\gamma}$, and negative part $A_{<0}$ spanned by $v^\gamma$ for $\gamma<0$.

A weight function $L:W\to\Gamma$ satisfies $L(xy)=L(x)+L(y)$ whenever $\ell(xy)=\ell(x)+\ell(y)$. Its simple-generator labels must agree across every finite odd-labelled edge; even-labelled edges allow independent values. The weighted Hecke algebra uses
$$
(T_s-v^{L(s)})(T_s+v^{-L(s)})=0
$$
and the Coxeter braid relations. The arbitrary-rank standard-basis proof uses finite support to extend the finite-generator basis theorem and proves the bar involution and its strict Bruhat triangularity.

The local theorem [[thm-unequal-parameter-kazhdan-lusztig-basis]] constructs the unique bar-invariant basis $C_w=T_w+\sum_{y<w}p_{y,w}T_y$ with $p_{y,w}\in A_{<0}$. It covers positive, zero, and negative values of $L(s)$; the simple-generator normalization is stated separately in that theorem.

## Cells and quotient modules

The preorders are generated by nonzero coefficients in $C_sC_y$ and $C_yC_s$; the two-sided preorder uses either kind of step. Mutual comparability gives the left, right, and two-sided cells. For each cell, the lower span modulo the lower span outside that cell is a module on the appropriate side. [[thm-unequal-parameter-cell-quotients-are-modules]] proves ideal closure and shows that the quotient basis is indexed exactly by the cell.

These constructions do not establish Lusztig's P1–P15, positivity, boundedness, semisimplicity, or a classification of irreducible modules. The exact boundedness convention and the conjectural status of P1–P15 in the general setting are recorded in [[rem-general-unequal-parameter-cell-conjectures-are-regime-dependent]].

## Finite-Weyl cells and modular basic sets

For $A=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$, finite Weyl $W$, and a nonnegative integral weight, [[def-finite-weyl-lusztig-basic-set-data]] states the split generic setup and precisely P2–P8 and P15′. These properties are explicit hypotheses. The theorem does not assert them for arbitrary unequal weights.

[[thm-finite-weyl-canonical-basic-sets-from-lusztig-properties]] applies to every $\theta:A\to k$ with parameter $u=\theta(v)\ne0$, multiplicative parameter $\xi=u^2$, and characteristic zero or L-good characteristic. Here L-good means that all leading Schur integers $f_E$ are nonzero in $k$, with $c_E(v)=f_Ev^{-2\alpha_E}$ plus higher powers. The full Schur elements are allowed to vanish: the result includes nonsemisimple specializations.

The local proof constructs the asymptotic algebra $J$, its integral map $\phi:H\to J_A$, and its compatible filtered layers. Explicit integral matrix units give the good-characteristic split $J$-models. The generic Hecke models are free globally over $A$, obtained by restricting those integral models through $\phi$. Lifted projective-cover idempotents compare their generic ranks and special dimensions. Thus the proof does not infer freeness of arbitrary torsion-free modules over a two-dimensional local ring or assume exactness of arbitrary lattice reduction.

The result selects a unique generic label $E(M)$ for every special simple, with multiplicity one and least Schur exponent; all other contributing labels have strictly larger exponent. [[cor-weighted-cell-quotients-and-finite-weyl-basic-set-labels]] proves its cell connection, explicitly retaining the dagger twist between the asymptotic and native cell conventions. It asserts neither that all cell modules are irreducible nor that all cell labels remain distinct.

## The A1×A1 worked specialization

For $W=A_1\times A_1$, $L(s)=1,L(t)=2$, and the specialization $O=\mathbb Z_p$, $v\mapsto1+p$ for an odd prime $p$, [[thm-a1xa1-unequal-parameter-cells-give-a-canonical-basic-set]] computes all four singleton cells and their one-dimensional quotients. The specialized order is $O^4$, the decomposition matrix is the identity, and the four cell modules form a canonical basic set under the stated definition.

This example lies in Geck's finite-Weyl setting. In his normalization $U_s=v^{L(s)}T_s$, the rank-one Schur elements are $1+v^{\pm2L(s)}$; for $A_1\times A_1$ their products specialize to $4$, which is nonzero at odd residue characteristic. This verifies the nonvanishing hypothesis of Geck's Theorem 4.11. The worked theorem computes these lattice reductions directly. The separate general theorem above supplies the broader finite-Weyl modular connection under its explicit hypotheses.
