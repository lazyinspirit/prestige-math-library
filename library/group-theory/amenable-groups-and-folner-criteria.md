This page treats amenability through three linked lenses: invariant means,
Folner sets, and paradoxical decompositions. The early permanence results are
proved without hiding them inside Tarski's theorem, and the steps that pass
from Folner or paradoxical data back to invariant means record their
ultrafilter and matching-extension costs explicitly.

The landmark theorem is the Folner criterion. After that, the page extracts
Folner sequences in the countable case, derives amenability from
subexponential growth, and then turns to paradoxical decompositions and the
nonamenability of the rank-two free group. The closing theorem records that, for
finitely generated groups, amenability depends only on quasi-isometry type.
