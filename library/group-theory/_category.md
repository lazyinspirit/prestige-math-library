Group theory examines finite and infinite groups through subgroups, actions, extensions,
presentations and representations. The finite-group strand develops Frattini and
extraspecial p-groups, complements, primitivity, socles, modular characters and
decomposition matrices. A second strand follows free groups through Schreier rewriting,
HNN extensions, small cancellation and decision problems, then studies Cayley graphs,
word metrics, growth and hyperbolicity. Trees and graphs of groups lead to Bass–Serre
theory, while inverse systems and pro-p groups describe completions. Cohomology, Schur
multipliers and central extensions organize further extension problems.

Foundational arguments draw on Abstract Algebra, Set Theory and Topology, while
specialized results use tools from Commutative Algebra, Homological Algebra and Number
Theory. Group actions, presentations, finite-group structure and cohomology support work
in Algebraic Geometry, Braid Groups, Knot Theory and Representation Theory of Groups.
Connections to Coxeter Groups and Homological Algebra also reuse group-action and
extension methods across their geometric and algebraic pages. In particular, free-group
presentations support Braid Groups, while finite-group characters and structure inform
Representation Theory of Groups.
