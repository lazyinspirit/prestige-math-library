These examples make the genericity statements concrete. The torus and circle
models show how a finite-dimensional parameter can move a function from Morse
to degenerate behavior, while the bump-separation example isolates the extra
step needed to pass from Morse to excellent Morse.

The two counterexamples mark the sharp boundaries. A Morse function can still
have repeated critical values, and on a noncompact manifold tiny perturbations
whose supports drift to infinity can create new critical points unless the
topology controls the perturbation at infinity strongly enough.
