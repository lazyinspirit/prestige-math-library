These examples test the empty presentation, the index exchange of duality, the
reordering of equal-index handles, the exact scope of the interchange lemma, and
the nonempty-incoming-boundary hypothesis of the elimination proposition. The
cylinder $M\times[0,1]$ presents itself with no handles at all; the genus-$g$
surface presentation shows the dual of a $0$-handle and a $2$-handle swapping
roles while the $2g$ one-handles stay fixed; two disjoint one-handles on a disk
may be attached in either order; on the circle, keeping the field fixed, the two
critical values cannot be swapped across the connecting trajectory, so the
disjointness hypothesis of the interchange lemma is genuinely needed; and a
nonempty triad with empty incoming boundary must begin with a $0$-handle, so the
nonempty-boundary hypothesis of the zero-handle elimination cannot be dropped.
