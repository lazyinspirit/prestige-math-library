Categorification and geometric representation theory studies algebraic structures through categories, functors, and geometry. The collection develops Hopf and Hecke algebras, their modules and tensor structures, and affine and cyclotomic constructions. KLR algebras, highest-weight categorification, crystal bases, and Ariki theorems connect representation theory with decomposition numbers. Kazhdan–Lusztig theory, Soergel methods, projective functors, category O, and localization provide geometric and categorical approaches to characters and multiplicities. Hall algebras organize extension data in finitary, derived, motivic, and cohomological settings, with further topics in stability, wall crossing, modular categories, sewing, and defects.

Abstract Algebra, Category Theory, and Homological Algebra supply algebraic and categorical foundations; Coxeter Groups, Lie Theory, and Representation Theory of Groups provide reflection and highest-weight methods. Algebraic Geometry supports descent and convolution, while Quantum Groups and Crystal Bases, Symmetric Groups and Symmetric Functions, and Braid Groups supply further constructions and comparisons. These connections lead through Fock spaces and Schur–Weyl duality to categorification, and through Hall integration and tensor categories to geometric and topological applications. The reading pathway follows the prerequisite chains across these branches, with example companions accompanying the theory pages.
