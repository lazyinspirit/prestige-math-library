## algebraic-and-geometric-foundations

Begin with tensor coherence, quantum enveloping algebras, ordinary Hecke Specht constructions, Kazhdan–Lusztig bases, proper descent, and equivariant convolution. These are independent entry points within the collection; their external prerequisites remain on the linked subject pages.

## hecke-bases-and-integral-forms

Develop coalgebras and generic Hecke bases alongside divided powers, integral forms, and quantum highest-weight modules. Soergel Hodge theory and the quantum-symmetry interface prepare the categorical and structural branches.

## affine-actions-and-crystal-lattices

Continue to comodules, bialgebras, Hecke induction and traces, affine Hecke algebras, and Yangians. Kashiwara operators and crystal lattices begin the crystal branch. Projective functors and noninvertible symmetries develop their categorical applications.

## hopf-modules-characters-and-tensor-crystals

Study cyclotomic quotients, specialization, Hopf modules, and duality. Tensor-product crystals and highest-weight uniqueness build on crystal lattices, while character formulas, unequal-parameter cells, and modular categories continue their respective branches.

## global-bases-and-finite-structures

Balanced triples construct global crystal bases with their lattice and normalization conventions. Finite Hopf integrals, quasitriangular structures, singular and parabolic multiplicities, and higher defects extend the structural and geometric branches.

## categorification-and-quantum-affine-representations

Use global bases in KLR induction and quantum-Serre categorification before cyclotomic highest-weight constructions. Quantum affine type A develops its Fock-space action; root-of-unity quantum groups lead to tilting quotients. Parabolic category O, Specht modules, Drinfeld doubles, and Frobenius sewing follow their prerequisites.

## cyclotomic-structures-and-fock-space-comparisons

Develop cyclotomic KLR categorification, cellular Murphy bases, Hecke tensor actions, and affine Hecke algebras beyond type A. Residue crystals and higher-level Schur–Weyl duality supply the Fock-space comparisons used in the decomposition-number theorems.

## hecke-klr-and-hall-constructions

The cyclotomic Hecke–KLR bridge follows its Specht, quantum affine, and categorification inputs. Finitary Hall algebras begin the counting branch after their declared prerequisites. Double-affine and rational Cherednik constructions continue the affine representation-theory routes.

## decomposition-numbers-integration-and-localization

Read Ariki categorification and its separate degenerate theorem before the category-O/KLR/Hecke route and localization. Green coproducts, derived and cohomological Hall algebras precede Hall modules and motivic integration; stability and wall crossing follow the integration constructions.
