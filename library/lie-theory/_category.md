Lie theory links smooth groups with Lie algebras and their representations. Foundational
topics develop invariant vector fields, the exponential map, Lie subgroups, actions and
homogeneous spaces, then construct representations, enveloping algebras and the
Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem. Solvable, nilpotent and semisimple Lie algebras lead to
Cartan subalgebras, root decompositions, root systems, Dynkin diagrams and
classification. Other topics treat highest-weight theory, Weyl character and
multiplicity formulas, compact groups, maximal tori, Peter–Weyl theory and real forms.
Further developments include Verma modules, Category O, BGG resolutions, Lie algebra
cohomology, Kac–Moody algebras, superalgebras and geometric interfaces.

Foundational Lie-group topics use smooth-manifold, tangent-bundle and flow theory from
Differential Geometry; other topics draw on Abstract Algebra, Linear Algebra,
Homological Algebra, Algebraic Geometry and symmetric-function methods. Lie-theoretic
results are used in Representation Theory of Groups, Categorification and Geometric Representation Theory, Coxeter Groups, Quantum Algebra and Representation Theory and
related geometric applications. The connections run through root systems, highest
weights, enveloping algebras and representations of Lie groups. Related constructions
also appear in Symmetric Groups and Symmetric Functions.
