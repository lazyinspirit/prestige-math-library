These examples compute Borel-Weil-Bott in the smallest ranks. On the
projective line for $SL_2$ the line bundles $\mathcal L_{m\omega_1}$ are the
twists $\mathcal O(m)$, and the three regimes are displayed: global sections
for $m\ge0$; vanishing of sections with degree-one cohomology realising the
dual irreducible for $m\le-2$; and total vanishing at the singular wall
$m=-1$.

The $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ examples exhibit higher degree: a weight whose unique
nonvanishing cohomology sits in degree one and realises the eight-dimensional
module $L(\rho)^*\cong L(\rho)$, and the top-degree case $\lambda=-3\rho$, where the Serre
pairing against $H^0(\mathcal L_\rho)$ is perfect and both sides are
eight-dimensional.

The counterexample records a convention trap rather than a mathematical
alternative: replacing $\mathbb C_{-\lambda}$ by $\mathbb C_{+\lambda}$ in
the associated bundle dualises the line bundle. For $\lambda=m\omega_1$
with $m>0$ on the projective line, this changes its sections from the nonzero
module $L(m\omega_1)^*$ to zero; it does not dualise the section space.
