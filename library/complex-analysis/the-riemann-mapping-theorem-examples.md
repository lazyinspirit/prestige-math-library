These examples make the extremal theorem concrete by writing normalized Riemann
maps for standard domains and by solving the extremal problem explicitly on the
disc itself. The sharpness example is the Koebe function, whose slit-plane
image shows that the quarter-disc constant cannot be improved.

The counterexample and false statements isolate the main geometric cautions: the
punctured disc is not conformally equivalent to the disc, normalization is what
makes the Riemann map unique, and conformal equivalence does not preserve
Euclidean area.
