category: quantum-algorithms
title: Quantum Algorithms
---

This category studies quantum algorithms as finite mathematical procedures on
explicitly encoded finite-dimensional complex vector spaces. It develops the
register, circuit, oracle, probability, and cost interfaces needed to state an
algorithm precisely, then proves correctness, termination, success probability,
query complexity, gate complexity, and bit complexity as separate results.
Early pages cover reversible computation, Fourier sampling, phase estimation,
order finding, search, and query lower bounds. Later branches treat quantum
walks, Hamiltonian simulation, block encodings, signal processing, singular
value transformation, linear systems, sampling, optimization, complexity,
communication, and adiabatic computation. The path is deliberately branching:
Fourier methods support hidden-subgroup and number-theoretic algorithms, while
walk and simulation methods have their own mathematical prerequisites. Each
algorithm states its input representation and access model, including the cost
of controlled or inverse queries, state preparation, precision, and classical
post-processing. A speedup claim is tied to those explicit costs and proved
hypotheses. Physical implementations and empirical motivation may be discussed
as context, but they do not supply mathematical assumptions or proof steps.
