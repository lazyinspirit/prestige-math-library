The diagonal example gives an exact one-ancilla block encoding of $\operatorname{diag}(1,1/2)$, a degree-one bounded odd polynomial with an explicit QSP phase list, its scalar error on the promised gap, two input-dependent success probabilities, and a finite Bernstein reciprocal certificate. The second example gives a three-stage stopping distribution, computes $T_{\max}$, $T_{\rm av}$, and $p_{\rm succ}$, then evaluates the cost expressions for a successful phase-certified fixed-time attempt and the variable-time profile. The arithmetic does not claim the variable-time expression is faster for every instance.
