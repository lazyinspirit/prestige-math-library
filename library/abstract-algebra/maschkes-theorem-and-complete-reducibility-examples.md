These examples keep the RT-2 statements concrete. The first group-algebra
decompositions are worked out from conjugacy-class counts and the
sum-of-squares formula, so the abstract Wedderburn theorem turns into explicit
products for $C_3$, $S_3$, $Q_8$, and $\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)$.

The companion page also isolates the two easy failure modes the A page warns
about. In characteristic $p$, the unipotent $2\times2$ representation of $C_p$
shows exactly where Maschke fails, and the trivial $C_2$ action on a
two-dimensional space shows why uniqueness belongs to isotypic blocks rather
than to individual irreducible summands.
