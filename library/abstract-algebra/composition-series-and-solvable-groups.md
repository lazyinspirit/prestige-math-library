Normal subgroups, quotient groups, isomorphism theorems, automorphisms, commutators, centres, direct products, and the well-ordering principle for $\mathbb N$ provide the language used here. Earlier results also supply the simplicity of $A_n$ for $n\ge5$, the derived subgroups of $A_n$ and $S_n$, and the structure of cyclic groups. These declared dependencies support comparisons of subgroup chains and tests for solvability and nilpotence.

Subnormal and composition series lead through the modular and butterfly lemmas to Schreier refinement and the Jordan–Hölder theorem. Characteristic subgroups and the derived series then give the universal abelianization and the main closure criteria for solvable groups. Lower and upper central series characterize nilpotence, yielding closure under subgroups, quotients, and finite products, the nilpotence of finite $p$-groups, and the resulting solvability and central-extension bounds.
