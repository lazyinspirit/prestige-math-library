The finite spherical metric-flag proof needs quantitative short-loop homotopy control that existing generic homotopy and compactness suppliers do not establish. This pair expands Bowditch’s actual energy/comparison-disk proof into explicit ordered local suppliers.

This is a prose scaffold for future local item authoring. Its empty item lists do not assert proof completion. Every construction below is a named supplier contract; definitions are justified by the separately named existence, descent or uniqueness proofs before any application consumes their properties. Source reading supports the selected proof route and is not a substitute for a library proof.

## Ordered construction and proof contracts

**def-cg-short-loop-homotopy-and-nonshrinkability.** In a compact locally CAT(1) metric complex define normalized rectifiable short loops by length<2pi. Use uniform-plus-length loop topology (equivalently the controlled normalized constant-speed Lipschitz family used in the source), and require short-loop homotopies continuous in this topology. A compact parameter family then has a uniform length bound b<2pi and a common fine mesh; uniform continuity of the map alone does not bound rectifiable lengths. Define nonshrinkable as not connected to a constant through such short loops, distinct from ordinary null-homotopy. Midpoint-iteration zero-limit polygons are defined separately; their equivalence with the constant short-class is proved, not assumed.

Definition justification: `lem-cg-bowditch-quantitative-short-loop-control`.

**def-cg-cyclic-small-mesh-polygon-and-midpoint-energy.** Choose a uniform local CAT(1) radius l small enough that model balls are complete and convex, and mesh h<l. For fixed cyclic n-tuples define mesh max d(x_i,x_(i+1)), L=Σd(x_i,x_(i+1)), E=Σd(x_i,x_(i+1))² and f(x)_i=mid(x_i,x_(i+1)); use modulo-n indices. Define the zero-limit basin by lim L(f^k x)=0, without identifying it yet with a short-homotopy class. Existence, uniqueness and continuity of each short midpoint and the periodic conventions follow from the supplied local charts. Fixed n is essential to the quantitative energy constants.

Definition justification: `lem-cg-polygon-midpoint-drop-and-equality`.

**lem-cg-polygon-midpoint-drop-and-equality.** Prove mesh does not grow, L(fx)≤L(x) and E(fx)≤E(x) from local spherical comparison and Cauchy–Schwarz. Analyze equality exactly: constant tuple or equally spaced vertices of a closed local geodesic. Prove the set of tuples eventually shortened below l/2 is open, its even iterates converge to a constant inside shrinking convex balls, and its converse by compact subsequential limits. Supply zero-energy and degenerate-edge cases explicitly (Bowditch3.3.1–3.3.6).

**lem-cg-finite-spherical-comparison-disks-and-radius-estimates.** For a zero-limit n-polygon choose finite m with L(f^m x)<2l. Build the Bowditch midpoint-operation disk G(m,n): the first/last row cyclic vertices bound it, intermediate triangles follow consecutive midpoint rows, and a final fan caps the last small polygon. Assign each triangle its spherical comparison metric when inequalities are strict. The map from the disk graph to X is distance-nonincreasing, because each face perimeter<2l permits local comparison. At each interior row vertex v, its images are the midpoint of adjacent prior-row v1,v2; hence ρ(v1,v2)≥d(gv1,gv2)=ρ(v1,v)+ρ(v,v2), and triangle inequality forces equality and cone angle≥2pi. Last-row boundary angles are≥pi, first-row angles<pi. Finite cellulation induction excludes simple closed local geodesics: a curve outside the first-row vertices would have to remain inside a comparison triangle in an open hemisphere. The compact local CAT(1) short-circle criterion then makes this disk pi-CAT(1). Prove Bowditch3.3.10 quantitatively: for loop length2r<2pi split at arclength r with endpoints y,z and chord midpoint a. For any point x write b=d(x,y),d=d(x,z),c=d(y,z); b+d≤r and c≥|b−d|. Spherical midpoint comparison gives cos d(a,x)≥(cos b+cos d)/(2cos(c/2))≥cos(r/2), so radius≤R=r/2=pi/2−η with η=(pi−r)/2. At a radius-maximizing boundary vertex x and equal outgoing segments of length μ=min{r/(2n),η}, two endpoint distances to a are≤D=d(a,x)≤R. Glue the spherical comparison triangles on opposite sides of ax; their perimeters are≤2R+μ<pi because μ≤η. The spherical cosine law makes each direction angle at x strictly below pi/2, so the intrinsic quadrilateral distance d_Q(y,z)<2μ by an explicit local corner shortcut near x. On the compact parameter family satisfying these inequalities and μ>0, this strict defect has a positive continuous minorant δ(η,μ); prove continuity by the two finite spherical triangles and their chord-or-via-apex shortest-path cases, including collapsed triangles, with μ<2η excluding the equality corner. This is the source quadrilateral constant. Ambient sphere chord distance is not substituted for d_Q unless the chord lies in Q. Obtain the estimate for longer unequal midpoint segments by selecting the initial equal μ pieces and adding the remaining lengths. Treat D=0 as an impossible nonconstant configuration. The source compact-family constant and the explicit radiusη feed the exact energy inequality. Include strict triangle, cone-angle, boundary and perimeter hypotheses; degeneracies wait for the next supplier.

**lem-cg-local-cat-one-products-from-sine-comparison.** Prove local CAT(1) of the l2 product without importing smooth-manifold comparison. Project a sufficiently small product triangle to its factor triangles and pair their S² comparison triangles at proportional side parameters; their boundary maps bound original product distances. It suffices to prove model S²×S² vertex-side comparison (a Euclidean factor has the stronger zero-curvature formula). Along a constant-speed model product geodesic use factor speeds v_i, radial distances r_i from a fixed product vertex, R²=Σr_i²,V²=Σv_i² and f(u)=u cot u, f(0)=1. On 0<r_i<pi derive r_i″=cot(r_i)(v_i²−(r_i′)²) by twice differentiating the spherical cosine formula. Because f decreases and |r_i′|≤v_i, sum to obtain RR″−f(R)(V²−(R′)²)≥(1−f(R))(Σ(r_i′)²−(R′)²)≥0 by Cauchy. Thus F=cos R satisfies F″+V²F≤0. Triangle perimeter<2pi bounds R(t) by min{a+tV,b+(1−t)V}<pi, excluding factor antipodes. Sine Green-kernel comparison on V<pi gives F(t)≥[sin((1−t)V)cos a+sin(tV)cos b]/sin V, the spherical vertex-side inequality. Prove the scalar comparison directly by the positive Dirichlet kernel or interior-minimum test and sine addition formulas. At r_i=0 use smooth squared spherical distance and continuity; cos sqrt(q) is smooth at q=0. Alexandrov subdivisions give all side-point comparisons, and paired factor-model boundary maps give the asserted product inequality on uniform local charts. No distributions, Riemannian curvature theorem or CAT(0) squared-distance surrogate is required.

**lem-cg-comparison-product-perturbation-and-degenerate-limits.** For potentially degenerate comparison triangles attach to the n-polygon a regular Euclidean n-gon of circumradius ε>0 and apply midpoint iteration in the l2 product. The preceding sine-comparison supplier proves uniform local CAT(1) on small product charts. Its Euclidean triangle coordinates are noncollinear, giving strict comparison triangle inequalities; the regular polygon lies in the zero-limit basin by explicit midpoint contraction. Construct the nondegenerate comparison disk and apply its quantitative bound. As ε→0, product edge lengths and both energies converge to those of the original fixed-n tuple, while λ_n(L) is continuous and independent of ε. Pass the inequality to the limit. Prove strictness for every finite cellulation triangle and preserve the local mesh/perimeter hypotheses by choosing ε sufficiently small; do not replace CAT(1) product comparison by summing CAT(0) squared inequalities.

**lem-cg-uniform-energy-decrement-and-short-class-closedness.** Work first in the zero-limit basin, with fixed n and r=L/2∈(0,π). Write ξ_i=d(x_i,x_(i+1)), ζ_i=d(fx_i,fx_(i+1)), Δ=E(x)−E(fx). The triangle bound ζ_i≤(ξ_i+ξ_(i+1))/2 gives Σ(ξ_i−ξ_(i+1))²≤4Δ. Cyclic telescoping and mean 2r/n imply ξ_i≥2r/n−n√Δ; if Δ≤r²/n⁴ then ξ_i≥r/n. Set η=η(π−r) from the comparison disk radius lemma and μ=min{r/(2n),η}, meeting μ<2η. The radius-maximizing boundary point lies on the first row (last-row angles≥pi), so the quadrilateral estimate gives one ζ_k≤(ξ_k+ξ_(k+1))/2−δ(η,μ). Choose δ no larger than this average. Its square deficit is≥δ(ξ_k+ξ_(k+1))/2≥δ r/n. Thus Δ≥λ_n(L), where a safe continuous positive choice is half min{r²/n⁴,δ(η(π−r),min(r/(2n),η(π−r))) r/n}. The same bound survives the supplied product perturbation limit because n,L and radius constants converge independently of perturbation size. On a compact length band [a,b]⊂(0,2pi), λ has a positive minimum and E≤b², so after at most ceil(b²/minλ)+1 iterates every basin tuple has length<a. Hence the zero-limit basin is closed as well as open in the fixed-n short-polygon space. It cannot contain an equally spaced local-geodesic tuple. Only after this clopen result identify basin membership with the constant short-homotopy class; no circular shrinkability assumption enters the disk construction.

**lem-cg-bowditch-quantitative-short-loop-control.** Use normalized constant-speed rectifiable loops and uniform-plus-length continuous families. Fine chord subdivisions and local chord homotopies have nonincreasing length; a compact short family has uniform b<2pi and speed bound, hence one fixed fine polygon mesh. Local interpolation defines a continuous short-polygon family, so the zero-limit basin clopen theorem separates closed local geodesics from constants. Midpoint iterations have convergent subsequences in the compact fixed-n tuple space; limiting energy equality makes their limits constant or equally spaced local-geodesic polygons. Near-limit tuples connect by small local vertex homotopies with length excess below the uniform gap to2pi, so the original loop shares the limit short class. Zero-limit loops contract through nonincreasing-length midpoint homotopies; therefore any loop shorter than the minimal embedded-circle length is shrinkable, and its realizing circle is minimum nonshrinkable. Prove finite subdivision, interpolation continuity, normalization at zero length and nonincreasing contraction claims explicitly; continuity of the parameter square alone is never used as a length bound.
