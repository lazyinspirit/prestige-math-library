The examples begin from the explicit coordinate Hopf algebras of the additive
and multiplicative groups, $\mathbf G_a=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ and
$\mathbf G_m=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$, and of their infinitesimal
closed subgroup schemes in characteristic $p$: $\alpha_p$, defined by $t^p=0$,
and $\mu_p$, the group of $p$-th roots of unity. All four coordinate rings,
their group laws and their point functors are computed, showing that
$\alpha_p$ and $\mu_p$ are finite nonreduced schemes of length $p$ cut out of
reduced ambient groups.

The Lie algebras of these groups are then computed from the cotangent spaces at
the identity: all four are one-dimensional with zero bracket, generated by
the dual functionals taking the cotangent classes of $t$, $t-1$ and their
respective images in $k[s]/(s^p)$ to $1$, while
$\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\mathfrak{gl}_n$ with the matrix
commutator. This produces the pair of isomorphisms
$\operatorname{Lie}(\alpha_p)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_a)$ and
$\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_p)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_m)$.

The counterexample records the consequence: the finite scheme $\alpha_p$ is
nonsmooth over $k$ while the larger group $\mathbf G_a$ is smooth, yet their
Lie algebras agree, so the Lie algebra does not determine smoothness. The same
contrast holds for $\mu_p\subseteq\mathbf G_m$. This is exactly the failure
which Cartier's theorem precludes in characteristic zero.
