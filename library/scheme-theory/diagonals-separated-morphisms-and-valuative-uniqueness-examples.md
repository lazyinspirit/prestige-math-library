These calculations make the diagonal concrete. The affine line is cut out by $x-y$, the projective line by its
bihomogeneous equation in every pair of standard charts, and the graph of a polynomial map by $y_j-g_j(x)$ with
first projection an isomorphism.

The counterexamples isolate what fails. For the doubled-origin line the diagonal image is dense but not closed in
the cross chart, and one discrete-valuation-ring diagram has two lifts. A separated scheme can have non-Hausdorff
Zariski points; an open immersion has uniqueness without existence; and gluing two copies of a rank-one
non-discrete valuation ring shows that restricting the criterion to discrete valuation rings is unsound without
extra hypotheses.
