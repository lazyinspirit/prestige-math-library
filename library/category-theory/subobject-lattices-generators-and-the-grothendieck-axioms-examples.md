These examples keep the A-page abstractions visible. The two subobject-lattice
examples show both sides of the modularity story: a tame divisor lattice for
$C_{12}$ and the diamond $M_3$ already present in $\mathbf F_2^2$. The module
examples spell out the Galois connection, composition-factor bookkeeping, the
generator role of the ring itself, and the AB5 lattice identity on an explicit
directed chain.

The counterexample on finite abelian groups isolates a separate warning. An
abelian category can be perfectly concrete and still have no nonzero projective
objects at all, so "has a generator", "has enough projectives", and "projective
generator" are genuinely different hypotheses.
