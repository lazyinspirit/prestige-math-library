For a compact measurable domain and a unique interior mode, the Laplace theorem
gives the leading asymptotic equivalent under a positive amplitude, a positive-
definite negative Hessian, local quadratic domination, and an explicit global
gap. Its proof rescales the local integral, applies dominated convergence, and
bounds the off-mode contribution exponentially. It makes no generic claim for
noncompact tails or a relative $O(n^{-1})$ remainder. The deterministic
quadrature result defines the equal-panel composite trapezoidal value and proves
its $C^2$ error bound by integrating the pointwise linear-interpolation error on
each panel.
