The examples compute a two-sample Bernoulli permutation orbit with tied
scores, exhibit a uniform-subset Monte Carlo design without replacement, and
compare equal-mean but unequal-distribution sampling with the exchangeable
case. They also compare a weighted sharp-null assignment test with
group-invariance inference and enumerate a two-point model where the
uncorrected Monte Carlo fraction is zero with positive probability. A
published finite-group example records why the invariant-null premise is
essential: [[cex-noninvariant-null-invalidates-group-pvalue]].
