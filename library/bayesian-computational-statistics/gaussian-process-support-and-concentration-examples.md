The first example shows that the independent-coordinate Gaussian cylinder law
on $\mathbb R^{\mathbb N}$ is not a Borel prior on $\ell^2$: almost surely
its coordinates fail to be square summable. The second gives a rank-one
finite-dimensional Gaussian process whose explicit realization is a tight
Borel Gaussian law on $\mathbb R^2$, with its covariance RKHS, support,
and Fernique moment computed directly. The examples distinguish cylinder
existence from Banach-valued tightness without asserting posterior
contraction.
