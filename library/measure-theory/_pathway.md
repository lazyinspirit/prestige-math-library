## sigma-algebras-and-borel-sets

A sigma-algebra is the closure needed for countable additivity, Borel sets are the
sigma-algebra generated by the open sets, and measurable functions, pi-lambda arguments and
monotone continuity supply the basic language of measure. Outer measures and Caratheodory
measurability convert covering costs into complete measures, and half-open boxes lead to
Lebesgue measure with regularity, completion, translation invariance, determinant scaling
and Steinhaus's theorem. Inner measure then records what can be captured from inside, and
Vitali's construction shows that translation invariance and countable additivity cannot
extend to all subsets of $\mathbb R$. Bernstein sets, Cantor-function homeomorphisms and
free ultrafilters add measurable non-Borel sets and other standard nonmeasurable
constructions.
