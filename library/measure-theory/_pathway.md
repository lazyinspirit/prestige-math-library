## sigma-algebras-and-borel-sets

A sigma-algebra is the closure needed for countable additivity, Borel sets are the
sigma-algebra generated by the open sets, and measurable functions, pi-lambda arguments and
monotone continuity supply the basic language of measure. Outer measures and Caratheodory
measurability convert covering costs into complete measures, with sigma-finite extension and
uniqueness controlling when the construction is canonical. In Euclidean space half-open
boxes and elementary sets give the premeasure whose outer measure becomes Lebesgue measure,
and dyadic decompositions connect that construction back to Borel sets. Regularity,
completion, translation invariance, determinant scaling under linear maps, Steinhaus's
theorem and the positive-measure subgroup corollary then identify Lebesgue measure as the
geometric measure compatible with the earlier null-set and Jordan-content notions.
