These examples compute the rank-one instances of
[[divided-powers-integral-forms-and-quantum-highest-weights]] and record the
boundary where naive specialization fails.

[[ex-divided-powers-and-lusztig-form-in-quantized-sl-two]] multiplies divided
powers in quantized $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, verifies
$F^{(2)}F^{(2)}=\binom{4}{2}_iF^{(4)}$ with the explicit Laurent polynomial
$q^{-4}+q^{-2}+2+q^{2}+q^{4}$, and works out the commutator and the $a=b=2$
toral reordering with the integral binomials.
[[ex-quantum-verma-and-simple-modules-for-sl-two]] computes the rank-one Verma
string, the finite-dimensional simple quotient in the dominant case, and the
irreducible infinite-dimensional, non-integrable Verma modules for negative
labels, including the boundary case $m=-1$.
[[ex-specializing-a-quantum-highest-weight-lattice-at-q-one]] exhibits the
divided-power lattice inside $L(m)$, proves it free by the explicit string
basis, and computes its classical specialization to the $(m+1)$-dimensional
$\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation with the coroot diagonal action.
[[cex-direct-q-one-substitution-in-quantum-denominators]] shows that
substituting $q=1$ into numerator and denominator of the toral element
$T_{0,1}$ gives $0/0$ while the integral-form specialization acts by the
coroot labels, with the two-dimensional witness
$\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$.
