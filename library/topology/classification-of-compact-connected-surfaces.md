Every nonempty compact connected surface in this chapter has no boundary. A finite triangulation supplies a polygonal model; a dual-tree cut places its faces in one polygon. The polygon moves then reduce its paired boundary word to the sphere digon, a product of handle blocks, or a product of crosscap blocks. The theorem [[thm-classification-of-compact-connected-surfaces]] distinguishes the resulting surfaces by integral orientability and Euler characteristic.

The plane-topology chain proves the Jordan–Schönflies extension and the finite graph facts needed to construct a finite triangulation. The one-polygon and reduction lemmas make every cut and gluing explicit. The sphere, torus, and projective-plane examples establish the three base quotients used by the classification proof. The empty reduced word is a notation for the genuine paired sphere digon, not a zero-sided polygon. The classification theorem gives $\chi=2-2g$ for the orientable genus-$g$ model and $\chi=2-k$ for the nonorientable $k$-crosscap model; the two corollaries record the genus formula and the complete invariant.
