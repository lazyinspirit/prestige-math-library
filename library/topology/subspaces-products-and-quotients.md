**Objective.** The previous topology page fixed the axioms and defined the
subspace topology. This page builds the rest of the standard supply of new spaces
from old: products, disjoint unions and quotients, together with what remains to
be said about subspaces. The organising observation is that all four are
instances of just two constructions, an *initial* topology on the source of a
family of maps and a *final* topology on their target, and that each is
characterised by a single statement about which maps into or out of it are
continuous.

**Subspaces, continued.** The subspace topology itself is not redefined here: it
is [[def-subspace-topology-top]], which already proves its closed sets, its
bases, transitivity, the continuity of the inclusion, the characteristic property
of a map into a subspace and the continuity of a restriction. What this page adds
is the behaviour of closure and interior.
[[thm-subspace-closure-and-interior]] shows that closure traces exactly,
$\operatorname{cl}_S(A) = \overline{A} \cap S$, while the interior only contains
the trace of the ambient interior, with equality when $S$ is open; and that a
dense subset of $X$ traces to a dense subset of every **open** subspace, the
unqualified form being false. [[def-hereditary-property]] then names the pattern,
distinguishing hereditary from open-hereditary and closed-hereditary, and
[[cor-metrizability-and-first-countability-are-hereditary]] gives the two
positive results this library needs: metrizability and first countability pass to
every subspace, the metric case being the subspace metric already identified with
the subspace topology inside [[def-metrizable-space]].

**Initial and final topologies.** [[def-initial-and-final-topology]] defines both
at once: the initial topology of maps $f_i : X \to Y_i$ is generated by the
preimages of the open sets, and the final topology of maps $g_i : Z_i \to X$
consists of the sets all of whose preimages are open. The subspace topology is
the initial topology of the inclusion, and that identification is made
explicitly. [[thm-initial-and-final-characteristic-properties]] then proves the
two statements that everything else on the page reads off: a map *into* a space
with the initial topology is continuous exactly when every composite with the
defining family is, and a map *out of* a space with the final topology is
continuous exactly when every composite with the defining family is; the two
topologies are respectively the coarsest and the finest making the family
continuous.

**Products and boxes.** [[def-product-topology]] builds the product set as a set
of functions, defines the projections, and puts two topologies on it: the **box**
topology, generated by all products of open sets, and the **product** topology,
the initial topology of the projections, whose basic open sets are the boxes with
all but finitely many factors unrestricted. The empty product is a one-point
space, and the nonemptiness of a product of nonempty factors is a theorem of ZF
for a finite index set and the Axiom of Choice in general; both are stated where
they are used. [[thm-product-universal-property]] gives the characteristic
property, proves that projections are continuous and open for both topologies,
and isolates surjectivity of a projection as the one place where choice is spent.
[[thm-box-finer-than-product]] compares the two: the box topology is finer, the
two agree for a finite index set, and the box topology is strictly finer whenever
infinitely many factors have a nonempty proper open subset.
[[thm-product-of-subspaces-and-closures]] proves that products commute with
subspaces and that $\overline{\prod A_i} = \prod \overline{A_i}$.

**The seam with $\mathbb{R}^n$.** [[lem-product-topology-on-rn]] exists so that
one symbol does not name two spaces. For $n \ge 1$ the product topology on $n$
copies of the usual topology of $\mathbb{R}$ is the metric topology of
$d_\infty$, whose balls are exactly the basic boxes, and $d_1$ and $d_2$ induce
the same topology by Lipschitz equivalence
([[def-equivalent-metrics]], [[thm-metric-equivalence-hierarchy]]). So
$\mathbb{R}^n$ as a product and $\mathbb{R}^n$ as the metric space of
[[lem-metrics-on-rn]] are one space, and every later statement about open sets,
closed sets and convergence in $\mathbb{R}^n$ may be read either way.

**Disjoint unions.** [[def-disjoint-union-topology]] realises the coproduct
concretely, as the union of the sets $X_i \times \{i\}$, so that the canonical
injections are injective and the summands disjoint by construction; the topology
is the final topology of the injections, and a set is open exactly when each of
its traces is. [[thm-coproduct-universal-property]] gives the dual of the product
statement: a map out of a disjoint union is continuous exactly when each
restriction is, each injection is an open and closed embedding, and each summand
is clopen.

**Quotients.** [[def-quotient-topology]] defines the quotient topology of a
surjection as the final topology of that one map, together with quotient maps,
saturated sets, the quotient of a space by an equivalence relation and the
collapse $Z/B$ of a nonempty subset to a point.
[[thm-quotient-universal-property]] gives the characteristic property, the
factorisation of a map constant on the fibres, and the fact that a composite of
quotient maps is a quotient map.
[[lem-open-or-closed-surjection-is-quotient]] supplies the three practical
sufficient conditions, an open surjection, a closed surjection and a surjection
with a continuous section, none of which is necessary.
[[thm-quotient-canonical-factorisation]] is the first-isomorphism-theorem
analogue: every quotient map induces a homeomorphism from the quotient by its own
fibre relation onto its target, so up to homeomorphism the quotient maps out of
$X$ are exactly the canonical projections.
[[def-adjunction-cone-suspension]] then glues: the adjunction space
$Y \cup_f X$, and the cone and the suspension of a space as quotients of
$X \times [0,1]$.

**Separation, minimally.** [[def-hausdorff-space]] is introduced here in its
smallest useful form, for one purpose: to state and refute the claim that a
quotient of a Hausdorff space is Hausdorff. It records the definition, quotes
from [[def-metrizable-space]] that every metrizable space is Hausdorff, and
exhibits the indiscrete topology on two points as a space that is not. No
separation condition beyond this one is defined on this page, and no statement on
this page uses or anticipates one; the graded family that the Hausdorff condition
belongs to is developed later in the reading order.

**Five false statements.** The product and box topologies do not agree, witnessed
by a shrinking box in $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$
([[fs-product-topology-equals-box-topology]]); a product of open sets need not be
open in the product topology, witnessed by $\prod_k (0,1)$
([[fs-arbitrary-product-of-open-sets-is-open]]); projections need not be closed,
witnessed by the hyperbola in $\mathbb{R}^2$
([[fs-projections-are-closed-maps]]); a quotient map need not be open, witnessed
by collapsing $[0,1]$ inside $\mathbb{R}$ ([[fs-quotient-map-is-open]]); and a
quotient of a Hausdorff space need not be Hausdorff, witnessed by the line with
two origins ([[fs-quotient-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-hausdorff]]). Each witness is
worked inline. The companion page returns to all of them but one, the product of
open sets that is not open, which is settled here and nowhere else; and where it
returns it sometimes carries the same witness further and sometimes replaces it by
a stronger one, as with the quotient map that is neither open nor closed.

**What is left open, and said so.**
[[rem-constructions-this-page-stops-short-of]] records four questions this page
deliberately does not answer: whether a product of quotient maps is a quotient
map, which needs a compactness hypothesis later in the reading order; separation
beyond the Hausdorff condition; the invariants that would tell the cone, the
suspension, the cylinder and the Mobius band apart, so that nothing here claims
any two of them differ; and metrizability of products, which is settled on these
pages only in named instances and never in general. The worked spaces and the
counterexamples behind every failure above are on
[[subspaces-products-and-quotients-examples|the companion examples page]].
