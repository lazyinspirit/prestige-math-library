The published function-space material supplies the two topologies compared throughout: [[def-topology-of-pointwise-convergence]] on $Y^{X}$, and [[def-topology-of-uniform-convergence]], whose uniform metric is defined only for a nonempty domain. [[def-equicontinuity]] and [[def-compact-open-topology]] state equicontinuity, pointwise boundedness and the compact-open topology for a metric domain, which is narrower than the setting here. From the compactness development come [[thm-tychonoff]], [[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]], [[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]] and [[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], together with [[thm-metric-hausdorff-separation]] and [[thm-compactness-agrees-with-metric-compactness]], which is what allows a metric compactness hypothesis and a topological one to be exchanged.

The compact-open topology and equicontinuity are first defined for a topological domain and shown to agree with the published metric notions where both apply. Continuity of evaluation, a pointwise compactness criterion, and lemmas on pointwise closure and on the agreement of the two topologies on an equicontinuous family give the sufficient direction; compactness of a family is then shown to return equicontinuity and pointwise relative compactness, and the two combine into the general theorem under the Axiom of Choice. Compact Hausdorff domains, nonempty compact metric domains, proper targets and compact targets follow as successive specialisations.
