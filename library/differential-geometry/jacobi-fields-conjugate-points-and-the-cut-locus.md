The page keeps the three layers of the Jacobi theory separate. Variation fields of geodesic variations satisfy the Jacobi equation $D_t^2J+R(J,\dot\gamma)\dot\gamma=0$, and conversely every Jacobi field is realized by a variation: existence and uniqueness from initial data, the dimension $2n$ of the solution space, the fact that tangential Jacobi fields are exactly the affine multiples of the velocity, the restriction of Killing fields, and the constancy of the Wronskian are proved in turn. The differential of the exponential map is then the endpoint value of the Jacobi field vanishing at the base point.

Conjugate points are introduced as the parameters at which a nonzero Jacobi field vanishes twice; they are the critical values of the exponential map along the geodesic, are isolated unless the geodesic is constant, and their multiplicity is invariant under affine reparametrization. The second variation of energy defines the index form, integration by parts identifies the Jacobi fields with its null solutions at fixed endpoints, the index lemma bounds the index form from below on a conjugate-free geodesic, and the local length-comparison estimates of the smooth and $H^1$ settings convert these statements into the theorem that a geodesic does not minimize past its first conjugate point. Degeneracy of the index form at a conjugate endpoint and the Morse index theorem for geodesics record the corresponding global count.

The third layer is the cut locus. A short unit sphere lemma and a change of variables that is analytic around the diagonal give the positivity and continuity of cut time, which does not exceed the first conjugate time, and the injectivity radius is the infimum of the cut times. The characterization of a cut point, the initial-interval property of minimizing segments, and the sequential compactness of finite-dimensional unit spheres yield the diffeomorphism from the open tangent cut domain onto the complement of $\{p\}$ and the cut locus, the closedness and nullity of the cut locus, the smoothness of the distance off $p$ and off the cut locus with its unit radial gradient and its expression in radial Jacobi fields, and the polar integration formula that may discard the cut locus. Six false statements mark the boundary of the theory: not every vector field along a geodesic is Jacobi, conjugacy is not a property of two points independent of the geodesic, a geodesic need not stop minimizing exactly at its first conjugate point, the distance need not be smooth on $M\setminus\{p\}$, the cut locus need not be a smooth hypersurface, and nullity of the cut locus does not follow merely from empty interior.

The Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is inherited through the completeness, exponential, cut-time and measure-theoretic suppliers and is declared wherever it is used; the smooth local arguments are kept choice-free.
