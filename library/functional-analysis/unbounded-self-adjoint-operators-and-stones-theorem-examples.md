The companion computes the unbounded theory on its basic models and exhibits
the three boundary phenomena the main page must not gloss over.

The multiplication operator $M_mf=mf$ on $L^2(X,\mu)$ of a sigma-finite space,
with domain $\{f:\int|m|^2|f|^2<\infty\}$, is proved self-adjoint, with spectral
projections $E(B)=M_{\mathbf 1_{m^{-1}(B)}}$, calculus $g(M_m)=M_{g\circ m}$
and spectrum the essential range of $m$; specialising to $X=\mathbb R$,
$m(x)=x$ gives the position operator, with $\sigma(Q)=\mathbb R$, proper dense
domain, and the unitary group $e^{itQ}f(x)=e^{itx}f(x)$ generated by $iQ$. The
periodic derivative $P=-i\,d/dx$ on the domain $f(0)=f(1)$ is proved
self-adjoint by an explicit solution of $(P\pm i)u=g$, and its unitary group is
identified with the translation family $f(x+t\bmod1)$, whose generator is
exactly $P$ on $D(P)$.

On the counterexample side, the minimal derivative has deficiency indices
$(1,1)$ with $K_\pm=\mathbb C e^{\mp x}$, and its self-adjoint extensions are
computed as the boundary conditions $f(1)=\mu f(0)$ with $|\mu|=1$, the
one-parameter family promised by the von Neumann parameterization; an
everywhere-defined closed operator on a Banach space is bounded by the closed
graph theorem, so a self-adjoint unbounded operator never has domain $H$; and
the group $f(x)\mapsto e^{itx}f(x)$ is strongly continuous but satisfies
$\|U(t)-I\|=2$ for every $t\ne0$, so strong continuity does not upgrade to
norm continuity when the generator is unbounded. The closing remark records the
extension interface of the main page and warns that equal deficiency
dimensions alone do not exhibit a unitary.
