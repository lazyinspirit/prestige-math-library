Linear algebra develops vector spaces, linear maps and matrices over fields, then
follows the structural and computational consequences of these objects. Core topics
include dimension, bases, rank-nullity, change of basis, Gaussian elimination,
determinants, bilinear and quadratic forms, inner products and spectral theory. Further
topics include complexification and realification, exterior powers and Hodge duality,
LU, Cholesky and QR factorizations, Krylov methods, conjugate gradients, MINRES, GMRES
and pseudoinverses. Matrix norms, conditioning and regularized least squares connect the
theory to numerical stability, while matrix differentiation and perturbation pages
analyze changing operators.

Some topics draw on Abstract Algebra, Real Analysis, Topology and Combinatorics. Linear
algebra supplies methods used in Abstract Algebra, Representation Theory of Groups,
Coxeter Groups, Algebraic Geometry and Differential Topology. Its inner-product,
spectral, matrix and subspace tools support Numerical Linear Algebra and Approximation,
Quantum Algorithms, Quantum Computing and Statistical Learning and Classical Models.
Other applied connections include Machine Learning Algorithms, Neural Networks and
Learning Dynamics, and Inverse Problems.
