This collection studies neural architectures, their approximation properties and the mathematical behavior of training. It begins with feedforward and ReLU networks, convolution and equivariance, graph message passing, recurrent and state-space models, attention and neural operators. Architecture-specific results distinguish representational capacity from trainability and predictive performance. Selected risk, complexity and kernel results from Statistical Learning and Classical Models provide prerequisites without requiring that entire collection to be read first.

Automatic differentiation leads to nonconvex gradient descent, stochastic training, momentum, adaptive methods and initialization. Each algorithm retains its precise update convention, smoothness assumptions and cost model. Exact differentiation, a finite execution trace and convergence of an optimization procedure are separate claims. The later efficient-training part covers implicit differentiation, tiled attention and low-rank parameter updates.

Training theory connects implicit bias, interpolation, double descent, neural tangent kernels and mean-field learning. The sample-covariance prerequisite pairs belong with the interpolation results they support. Wasserstein geometry and gradient flows similarly precede mean-field dynamics. Norm, margin, compression and stability arguments then address generalization under their distinct hypotheses. These advanced branches can be read selectively. Representation Learning and Generative Models uses the relevant neural foundations; preference optimization in Online and Reinforcement Learning also draws on differentiation.
