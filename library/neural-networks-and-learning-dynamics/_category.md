Published pages study network architectures and expressivity—feedforward and ReLU networks, convolution, graph, recurrent and state-space models, attention, and neural operators—and training through automatic differentiation, stochastic and nonconvex gradient descent, momentum, adaptive methods, initialization, efficient differentiation, implicit bias, and interpolation. Further results cover neural tangent kernels, mean-field feature learning, covariance limits, Wasserstein gradient flows, and norm, margin, compression, and stability generalization bounds. They distinguish approximation, optimization, and predictive guarantees under their hypotheses.

Statistical Learning and Classical Models provides risk and complexity bounds for generalization; Probability supplies stochastic foundations. Linear Algebra and Functional Analysis underpin approximation and gradient-flow results; Numerical Optimization, Inverse Problems and Uncertainty Quantification informs efficient model training; Partial Differential Equations supports neural-operator branches. These foundations carry into Representation Learning and Generative Models, where architectures support learned features, and Online and Reinforcement Learning, where differentiation supports policy gradients and preference methods. Probability also reuses selected gradient-flow results in its analysis.
