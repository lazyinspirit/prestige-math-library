Published pages distinguish learned representations from learned data distributions. They cover self-supervised and contrastive objectives, reconstruction and masked autoencoders, latent-variable models and variational bounds, energy and adversarial models, probability metrics and transport duality, exact-density and continuous flows, flow matching, discrete and continuous diffusion, multimodal alignment, and foundation-model transfer. The statements separate finite or idealized mathematical guarantees from empirical scaling evidence and retain assumptions on sampling, density, and solver error.

Neural Networks and Learning Dynamics supplies architectures and differentiation; Statistical Learning and Classical Models supplies risk and generalization tools. Probability and Measure Theory underpin sampling and latent-variable analysis, while Numerical Differential Equations and Stochastic Dynamics supports continuous-flow and diffusion methods. These foundations serve distinct routes: contrastive and multimodal learning uses statistical bounds, while generation uses probability and solver theory. The category brings these tools together while keeping representation objectives separate from distribution modeling. Readers can follow contrastive, transport, and diffusion routes separately.
