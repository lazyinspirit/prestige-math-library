Representation learning seeks useful features from supervision, reconstruction or relationships among observations. Generative modeling instead specifies or approximates a data distribution and a procedure for sampling or evaluating it. This collection connects these goals while keeping their mathematical objectives distinct. Its starting points are contrastive learning, reconstruction and masked autoencoders, followed by variational latent-variable models. Selected neural architectures, differentiation and statistical-learning results serve as prerequisites from the earlier collections.

Energy models, score matching and adversarial objectives lead into probability metrics and transport duality. Finite-metric duality precedes the compact-metric Kantorovich-Rubinstein result and its Wasserstein-GAN application. Exact-density flows and continuous normalizing flows form a separate route through change of variables and differential equations; flow matching connects transport paths with continuous generation.

Diffusion pages distinguish finite chains, masked discrete generation and continuous score-based sampling. The reverse-time diffusion prerequisite belongs immediately before the score-SDE application. Forward SDE well-posedness, Gaussian smoothing and numerical Euler convergence remain external probability and numerical prerequisites. A solver-error theorem does not control learned-score bias. Multimodal alignment and foundation-model transfer finish the pathway, separating mathematical guarantees from empirical scaling evidence. Online and Reinforcement Learning is a parallel branch; its preference and policy objectives can be consulted for later alignment applications.
