The Real Analysis pages develop the real line and calculus from completeness. They compare Cauchy-sequence and Dedekind-cut constructions, establish order and topological properties of the real line, and build the theory of sequences, limits, series, continuity, and differentiation. Integration proceeds through the Riemann and gauge integrals, the fundamental theorems, improper integrals, and bounded variation. Further pages treat uniform convergence and power series, define the exponential, logarithm, and trigonometric functions, and establish results on multivariable differentiation, inverse and implicit maps, Jordan content, change of variables, line and surface integrals, and classical integral theorems. The collection also covers ordinary differential equations, convexity, and real special functions.

Set Theory, Topology, Linear Algebra, Combinatorics, and Abstract Algebra support arguments throughout these pages. Measure Theory and Probability draw on its integration results; Functional Analysis, Complex Analysis, Fourier Analysis, and Partial Differential Equations use completeness, limits, and series; Differential Geometry relies on multivariable calculus and change of variables.
