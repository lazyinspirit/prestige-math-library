The space $C(K,\mathbb R)$ is formed from continuous real-valued functions on a compact metric space. The supremum metric, compactness criteria, uniform convergence, and the finite binomial identities used by Bernstein polynomials provide the background for its approximation theory. Compactness is expressed through equicontinuity and boundedness, while uniform limits preserve continuity.

The development defines the family conditions needed for Arzelà--Ascoli, proves polynomial approximation through Bernstein polynomials, and gives the real Stone--Weierstrass argument for unital point-separating algebras. It also proves the closed-set form of Baire category needed for the dense nowhere-differentiability result and establishes the Takagi function as an explicit witness.
