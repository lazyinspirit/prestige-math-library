[[def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero]] defines $\pi$ as twice the least positive zero of cosine, while [[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]] and [[thm-sine-cosine-zero-sets-and-fundamental-period]] determine its sine-zero and periodicity properties. The polygonal definition of path length, its refinement monotonicity, and the speed-integral theorem from [[def-path-polygonal-length-and-rectifiability-in-rn]] provide the geometric background.

Circular length and a local Riemann-area convention lead to the circumference, regular-polygon, and disc-area characterizations of $\pi$. Finite integral identities then give the Gregory-Leibniz series, the Wallis product and its central-binomial consequence, and Viète's cosine and nested-radical product. The concluding equivalence theorem collects the zero, period, geometric, series, and product formulas without changing the original definition.
