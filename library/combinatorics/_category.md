Combinatorics studies finite structures through counting, order, graphs and probability.
Foundational topics develop finite counting, binomial coefficients, inclusion–exclusion,
double counting, partitions, generating functions and linear recurrences. Order theory
covers chains, antichains, Sperner and Dilworth; graph theory proceeds through
connectivity, trees, matchings, flows, colourings, planarity, extremal problems and
Ramsey theory. Other topics develop finite probability and the probabilistic method,
block designs, finite projective planes, algebraic and spectral graph theory, incidence
algebras and set partitions. The collection also develops induced subgraphs, regularity,
sparse configurations, blockades and the Erdős–Hajnal property.

Some algebraic and representation-theoretic topics use cyclic groups, characters and the
complex exponential from Abstract Algebra and Real Analysis. Combinatorial methods are
used in later work in Commutative Algebra, Computability Theory, Mathematical
Statistics, Quantum Algorithms, Quantum Computing and Statistical Learning and Classical
Models. The connections include finite abelian Fourier analysis, extremal and induced
graph arguments, counting methods and probabilistic constructions. Linear Algebra and
Number Theory contribute further algebraic inputs.
