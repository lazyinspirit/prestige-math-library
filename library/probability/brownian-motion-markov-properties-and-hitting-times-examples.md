These examples accompany
[[brownian-motion-markov-properties-and-hitting-times]]. The Gaussian
convolution underlying the semigroup is computed in closed form, the maximum
crossing probability is evaluated with its two limits, and the one-sided
hitting-time density is shown to have divergent first moment, which separates
almost-sure finiteness from integrability.

The exit-side computation substitutes into the two-sided exit probability, the
successive-hit example restarts independent Brownian excursions and reads off a
simple symmetric random walk, and the planar example contrasts coordinate
recurrence with the polarity of single points in the plane.

The counterexamples locate the boundaries of the main page: the raw natural
filtration is not right-continuous before augmentation, the strong Markov
conclusion fails at the non-stopping last zero before a fixed time, and
almost-sure finiteness of a hitting time does not imply its integrability.
