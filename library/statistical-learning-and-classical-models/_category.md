Statistical learning studies how predictions are defined, fitted and evaluated from data. This collection begins with loss, risk and empirical minimization, then develops finite-class guarantees, VC dimension, Rademacher complexity, stability, compression and PAC-Bayes bounds. These results distinguish fitting the observed sample from controlling performance on new observations. Probability, mathematical statistics and linear algebra provide the external foundations; the learning-specific guarantees retain their own sampling and measurability assumptions.

The modeling pathway covers linear and sparse prediction, classification, kernels, Gaussian processes, trees and ensembles. It continues through clustering, spectral methods, dimension reduction, latent factors, graphical models and causal discovery. Active querying and semi-supervised methods explain what additional information a learning protocol can use, and what unlabeled data alone cannot identify. Approximate kernel solves retain their numerical error contracts.

The final parts address distribution shift, adversarial risk, calibration, fairness, deployment guarantees, privacy and federated learning. These are distinct mathematical questions: calibration does not imply accuracy or fairness, and privacy does not establish generalization by itself. Read the relevant theory and model prerequisites before each application rather than treating the whole collection as a compulsory linear course. Neural Networks and Learning Dynamics builds on selected parts here; online decision methods can branch from the same classical foundations.
