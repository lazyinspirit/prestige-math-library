The first example computes the correspondence for the one-dimensional trivial module of S3 in characteristic two. Its normalizer and relative traces establish both vertices and identify the unique restriction summand.

The TI Sylow specialization computes both exceptional families and identifies the full nonprojective domains, retaining the Sylow and proper-normalizer hypotheses. The final example gives explicit tensor maps for identity induction and explains why a trivial chosen vertex forces the full-group boundary. These examples inherit the stated AC assumption only where they invoke the general correspondence or projectivity comparison.
