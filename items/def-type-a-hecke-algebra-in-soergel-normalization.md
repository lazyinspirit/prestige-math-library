## Definition

**Coefficients.** Let $A:=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$ be the ring of Laurent polynomials
in one indeterminate with integer coefficients, a commutative ring containing
$\mathbb Z$ as the constant Laurent polynomials, and put $q:=v^{-2}\in A$. Both
$v$ and $q$ are units of $A$, indeed $A^\times=\{\pm v^m:m\in\mathbb Z\}$, while
$q-1=v^{-2}-1$ is a non-zero, non-invertible element of the domain $A$ and is
never inverted below; the only element of $A$ inverted anywhere on this page is
$v$ itself, through $q=v^{-2}$.

**The algebra.** For $n\ge2$ let $H_n=H_{S_n}$ be the unital associative
$A$-algebra presented by generators $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}$ and relations
$$T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q,\qquad T_iT_{i+1}T_i=T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1},\qquad T_iT_j=T_jT_i\ \ (|i-j|>1).$$
The braid and commutation relations are those of the Coxeter presentation
of $S_n$ ([[lem-type-a-reduced-words-are-connected-by-braid-moves]]), while
its involution relations are replaced by the displayed Hecke quadratic relations;
$H_n$ is the quotient of the *free* associative
$A$-algebra on $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}$ by these relations. It is *not* the quotient
of the group algebra $A[S_n]$ by the ideal generated by the quadratic relations.
Indeed, in $A[S_n]$ the involutions satisfy $T_i^2=1$, so the ideal generated
there by the relations $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$ is generated by
    $$(T_i-q)(T_i+1)=(T_i^2-1)+(1-q)(T_i+1)=(1-q)(T_i+1).$$
    Already for $n=2$, the generic algebra
    $H_2=A[T_1]/((T_1-q)(T_1+1))$ is free over $A$ on $1,T_1$, since its
    defining polynomial is monic of degree two. Thus
    $(1-q)(T_1+1)\ne0$ in $H_2$, whereas that element vanishes in the indicated
    quotient of $A[S_2]$; the generator-preserving presentation through the group
    algebra is impossible. Since $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$ is
equivalent over $A$ to $(T_i-q)(T_i+1)=0$, we may use either form.

**Normalized generators.** Put
$$H_i:=v(T_i+1)=vT_i+v\in H_n.$$
Then $H_i$ is a unit-free normalization: it is triangular, with leading term
$vT_i$. Expanding the quadratic relation gives $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$, so
$$H_i^2=v^2(T_i+1)^2=v^2\bigl(T_i^2+2T_i+1\bigr)=v^2\bigl((q-1)T_i+q+2T_i+1\bigr) =v^2\bigl((q+1)T_i+(q+1)\bigr)=(v^2(q+1)\bigr)(T_i+1).$$
Since $v^2q=1$, the scalar is $v^2q+v^2=1+v^2$, so
$$H_i^2=(1+v^2)(T_i+1)\cdot v\cdot v^{-1}=v^{-1}(1+v^2)H_i=(v^{-1}+v)H_i,$$
that is $H_i^2=(v+v^{-1})H_i$. Conversely $T_i=v^{-1}H_i-1$, so the two
generating sets determine each other over $A$. The element $H_i$ is the
bar-invariant generator of Elias–Williamson, §2.1, where it is written with the
same scalar $v+v^{-1}$.

**Small $n$ and scope.** For $n\le1$ there are no generators and we set
$H_n:=A$; the statements about $T_i,H_i$ are vacuous. This presentation is the
internal normalization used by this page: it records no specialization to a
finite field, no Tits deformation theorem and no positivity or Kazhdan–Lusztig
statement. The standard basis $\{T_w\}$ indexed by reduced words, its
multiplication rule by simple generators, and the comparison with products of
the $H_i$ are supplied by
[[lem-type-a-hecke-standard-basis-for-soergel-comparison]], which also makes the
notation $T_w$ for $w\in S_n$ well defined.
