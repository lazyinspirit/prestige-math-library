## Statement

Let $X$ be a topological space in which the intersection of any two
compact open subsets is compact ([[def-compact-space]]), and let $\mathbb Z_X$
be the constant sheaf on $X$ associated to the constant presheaf with value
$\mathbb Z$ ([[def-sheafification]]). Let $K\subseteq\mathbb Z_X$ be a subsheaf
generated by finitely many sections over compact open subsets: there are
$t\ge0$, compact open subsets $U_1,\dots,U_t\subseteq X$ and sections
$s_i\in\mathbb Z_X(U_i)$ with $K=\langle s_1,\dots,s_t\rangle$ in the sense of
[[lem-subsheaf-generated-by-sections]]. Then there are a natural number $N$ and
a chain of subsheaves
$$0=F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_N=K$$
of $\mathbb Z_X$ such that for every $1\le n\le N$ there are compact open
subsets $V\subseteq U\subseteq X$ and a short exact sequence
$$0\longrightarrow j_{V!}\mathbb Z_V\longrightarrow j_{U!}\mathbb Z_U\longrightarrow F_n/F_{n-1}\longrightarrow 0$$
of sheaves of abelian groups on $X$, where $j_V:V\hookrightarrow X$ and
$j_U:U\hookrightarrow X$ are the inclusions, $\mathbb Z_V$ and $\mathbb Z_U$ are
the constant sheaves associated to the constant presheaves with value
$\mathbb Z$ on $V$ and $U$ ([[def-sheafification]]), $j_{!}$ is extension by
zero ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]]) and $F_n/F_{n-1}$ is the cokernel
sheaf of the inclusion $F_{n-1}\subseteq F_n$
([[def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves]]). If $K=0$ one may take $N=0$, so that
the chain is trivial. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] $\mathbb Z_U$ denotes the sheaf on an open $U$ associated to the constant presheaf with value $\mathbb Z$ ([[lem-abelian-sheaves-form-a-grothendieck-category]]).

[F2] For every presheaf $\mathcal F$ the sheafification map induces a bijection on stalks, $\mathcal F_x\to(a\mathcal F)_x$ ([[thm-sheafification-preserves-stalks]]).

[F3] Every morphism of presheaves from $\mathcal F$ into a sheaf factors uniquely through the sheafification map $\eta_{\mathcal F}$ ([[thm-sheafification-universal-property]]).

[F4] The sheaf $\underline A_{\mathrm{loc}}$ of locally constant $A$-valued functions is a sheaf of sets whose stalk at $x$ is canonically $A$ by evaluation at $x$ ([[lem-locally-constant-functions-form-a-sheaf]]).

[F5] Extension by zero along an open inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ is left adjoint to restriction, $\operatorname{Hom}_X(j_!\mathcal F,\mathcal G)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_U(\mathcal F,j^{-1}\mathcal G)$, and $j_!$ is exact on sheaves of abelian groups ([[thm-extension-by-zero-adjunction-exactness]]).

[F6] Restriction maps of $j_!\mathcal F$ are inherited from $\mathcal F$, and $j_!\mathcal F$ is a subsheaf of $j_*\mathcal F$ ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]]).

[F7] For a family of sections the generated subsheaf is given by the stalk condition $\langle s_\alpha\rangle(W)=\{s\in\mathcal F(W):s_x\in G_x\text{ for every }x\in W\}$, where $G_x$ is generated by the germs of the generators defined at $x$, and $\langle s_\alpha\rangle_x=G_x$ ([[lem-subsheaf-generated-by-sections]]).

[F8] A subsheaf $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ with $\mathcal H_x=G_x$ for every $x$ equals the subsheaf generated by the family, and if a further family lies in $\langle s_\alpha\rangle$ then the generated subsheaf is unchanged ([[lem-subsheaf-generated-by-sections]]).

[F9] Every subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$ equals $n\mathbb Z=\langle n\rangle$ for exactly one natural number $n$ ([[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]]).

[F10] The subgroup generated by a subset $S$ of a group is the smallest subgroup containing $S$, hence a subgroup that contains every $s\in S$ contains $\langle S\rangle$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F11] A sequence of sheaves of abelian groups is exact if and only if all its stalk sequences are exact ([[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]]).

[F12] The cokernel sheaf of a morphism of abelian sheaves is the sheafification of the cokernel presheaf ([[def-kernel-cokernel-image-sheaves]]).

[F13] The category of sheaves of abelian groups on $X$ is an abelian category, so kernels and cokernels give short exact sequences ([[thm-abelian-sheaves-form-abelian-category]], [[def-exact-sequence-sheaves]]).

[F14] A subset $A\subseteq X$ is a compact subset when the subspace $(A,\mathcal T_A)$ is a compact topological space ([[def-compact-space]]).

[F15] Compactness of a subset can be read in the ambient space: $A$ is compact if and only if every family of open subsets of $X$ covering $A$ has a finite subfamily covering $A$ ([[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]]).

[F16] The open subsets of a subspace $S$ are the traces $U\cap S$ of open subsets of the ambient space ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]).

[F17] Stalks are computed as the filtered colimit of the section groups over the open neighbourhoods of the point, and germs of restrictions agree ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]], [[def-germ-of-section]]).

## Proof

**Given:** A topological space $X$ whose compact open subsets are closed under pairwise intersections, the constant sheaf $\mathbb Z_X$ associated to the constant presheaf with value $\mathbb Z$, compact open subsets $U_1,\dots,U_t$ and sections $s_i\in\mathbb Z_X(U_i)$ generating a subsheaf $K=\langle s_1,\dots,s_t\rangle$, the fibre decomposition of each $s_i$, and the subgroups $J_x=\{i:x\in U_i\}$.

1.1 Fix the data $U_1,\dots,U_t$ and $s_1,\dots,s_t$ of the statement. If $t=0$ then $K=\langle\varnothing\rangle=0$ by [F7] and we take $N:=0$, $F_0:=0$; this is the case covered by the boundary discussion below, so assume from now on that $t\ge1$ and that each $U_i$ is a compact open subset of $X$ [F14]. [F7, F14, given] [F7, F14]

1.2 For $i\le t$ and $k\in\mathbb Z$ put $A_{i,k}:=\{x\in U_i:(s_i)_x=k\cdot 1_x\}$, where $1_x$ denotes the germ at $x$ of the constant section $1$. The sheafification map identifies $\mathbb Z_X$ with the sheaf of locally constant $\mathbb Z$-valued functions [F1, F2, F4], and under this identification $s_i$ is a locally constant function whose value at $x$ is the integer $k$ with $(s_i)_x=k\cdot1_x$; hence each $A_{i,k}$ is open in $U_i$ and therefore in $X$ [F16], and the nonempty $A_{i,k}$ cover $U_i$. Since $U_i$ is compact [F14], by [F15] finitely many of the open sets $A_{i,k}$ cover $U_i$, so only finitely many of the sets $A_{i,k}$ are nonempty. Each nonempty $A_{i,k}$ is a compact subset of $X$: if a family of open subsets of $X$ covers $A_{i,k}$, then adjoining one open set of $X$ whose trace on $U_i$ is the open subset $U_i\setminus A_{i,k}=\bigcup_{l\ne k}A_{i,l}$ of $U_i$ [F16] produces a family of open sets covering $U_i$, so by [F15] and compactness of $U_i$ finitely many of them cover $U_i$ and, discarding the possibly adjoined set, finitely many members of the original family cover $A_{i,k}$; hence $A_{i,k}$ is compact by [F15]. Moreover the sections $k$ over the sets $A_{i,k}$ generate the same subsheaf as the original sections: for $x\in U_i$ exactly one $k$ has $x\in A_{i,k}$, and there $(k\cdot 1_{A_{i,k}})_x=k\cdot 1_x=(s_i)_x$ by [F17], so the subgroups generated by the germs of the two families at $x$ coincide, and [F7, F8] give $\langle s_1,\dots,s_t\rangle=\langle k\text{ over }A_{i,k},\ i\le t,\ k\in\mathbb Z\rangle$. Relabel the nonempty fibres and their constant values as $(U_1,n_1),\dots,(U_m,n_m)$ with $n_j\in\mathbb Z$, so that $U_j$ is compact open and $K$ is generated by the constant sections $n_j$ over $U_j$. [F1, F2, F4, F7, F8, F14, F15, F16, F17, given] [F1, F2, F4, F7, F8, F14, F15, F16, F17]

1.3 For every nonempty subset $I\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}$ put $U_I:=\bigcap_{j\in I}U_j$ and let $d_I\ge0$ be the unique natural number with $\langle n_j:j\in I\rangle=d_I\mathbb Z$ in $(\mathbb Z,+)$ [F9]. Each $U_I$ is compact open by the hypothesis on $X$ and induction on the number of elements of $I$ [F14], and the constant section $d_I$ over $U_I$ lies in the subsheaf generated by the sections $n_j$ over $U_j$: at a point $x\in U_I$ one has $d_I\in\langle n_j:j\in I\rangle\subseteq\langle n_j:x\in U_j\rangle$, so $d_I$ is a finite integer combination of the integers $n_j$ with $x\in U_j$ [F10] and therefore $(d_I\cdot1)_x=d_I\cdot1_x$ lies in the subgroup generated by the germs $(n_j\cdot1)_x$ [F10]; by the stalk description of generated subsheaves [F7] the section $d_I$ over $U_I$ belongs to the generated subsheaf. Hence [F8] shows that the subsheaf generated by the enlarged family consisting of all the original sections $n_j$ over $U_j$ together with all the sections $d_I$ over $U_I$ is again $K$. For $x\in X$ write $J_x:=\{j:x\in U_j\}$; the values of the enlarged family at $x$ are exactly the integers $d_I$ with $\varnothing\ne I\subseteq J_x$, because $x\in U_I$ holds precisely when $I\subseteq J_x$. For such $I$ the inclusion $\langle n_j:j\in I\rangle\subseteq\langle n_j:j\in J_x\rangle=d_{J_x}\mathbb Z$ shows that $d_I$ is a nonnegative multiple of $d_{J_x}$ whenever $d_{J_x}>0$, and $d_{J_x}$ is itself one of the values at $x$; if $J_x\ne\varnothing$ and not all integers $n_j$ with $j\in J_x$ are zero then $d_{J_x}>0$, and if all of them are zero then every $d_I$ with $I\subseteq J_x$ is $0$. [F7, F8, F9, F10, F14, given] [F7, F8, F9, F10, F14]

1.4 Let $W\subseteq X$ be open with inclusion $j_W:W\hookrightarrow X$. By [F6] a section of $j_{W!}\mathbb Z_W$ over an open $Y\subseteq X$ is a section of $\mathbb Z_W$ over $Y\cap W$ with support closed in $Y$; for $x\in W$ the open neighbourhoods of $x$ contained in $W$ are cofinal among all neighbourhoods of $x$, so the colimit description of stalks [F17] gives $(j_{W!}\mathbb Z_W)_x=(\mathbb Z_W)_x=\mathbb Z$ generated by the germ of the constant section $1$, while for $x\notin W$ every section of $j_{W!}\mathbb Z_W$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of $x$, so $(j_{W!}\mathbb Z_W)_x=0$. For $V\subseteq U$ the morphism $\iota:j_{V!}\mathbb Z_V\to j_{U!}\mathbb Z_U$ obtained from the adjunction [F5] and the identification $(j_{U!}\mathbb Z_U)|_V=\mathbb Z_V$ is a monomorphism, and on stalks at $x\in V$ it is the identity $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ while at $x\notin V$ both stalks are $0$. [F5, F6, F17]

2.1 Put $N:=\max\{d_I:\varnothing\ne I\subseteq\{1,\dots,m\}\}$ and, for $0\le n\le N$, let $F_n\subseteq\mathbb Z_X$ be the subsheaf generated by the sections $d_I$ over $U_I$ with $d_I\le n$ ([[lem-subsheaf-generated-by-sections]]). Then $F_0=0$, because the sections with $d_I\le0$ are the zero sections of the pieces with $d_I=0$; the $F_n$ form a chain $F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_N$ of subsheaves of $\mathbb Z_X$, since by [F7, F8] adding sections already contained in a generated subsheaf does not change it; and $F_N=K$ because every $d_I\le N$ and the pieces of [step 1.3] generate $K$. [F7, F8, step 1.3]

3.1 Fix $x\in X$ and $0\le n\le N$. The indices of generators of $F_n$ active at $x$ are the nonempty $I\subseteq J_x$ with $d_I\le n$ [step 2.1]. If $J_x=\varnothing$, or if $d_{J_x}=0$, every such generator has zero germ and $(F_n)_x=0$ [F7, step 1.3]. Suppose $d_{J_x}>0$. Every positive $d_I$ for $I\subseteq J_x$ is a positive multiple of $d_{J_x}$, since $d_I\mathbb Z$ contains $d_{J_x}\mathbb Z$ [F9, step 1.3]. If $d_{J_x}>n$, no positive $d_I\le n$ is active and $(F_n)_x=0$. If $0<d_{J_x}\le n$, the generator indexed by $I=J_x$ is active and all other active values are multiples of it, so $(F_n)_x=d_{J_x}\mathbb Z$. Thus $$ (F_n)_x=\begin{cases}d_{J_x}\mathbb Z,&0<d_{J_x}\le n,\\0,&d_{J_x}=0\text{ or }d_{J_x}>n\text{ or }J_x=\varnothing.\end{cases} $$ Zero gcd values never suppress a positive generator. [F7, F9, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 For $1\le n\le N$ put $Q_n:=F_n/F_{n-1}$ [F12, F13] and define $$E_n:=\bigcup_{d_I=n}U_I,\qquad W_{<n}:=\bigcup_{0<d_I<n}U_I,\qquad V_n:=E_n\cap W_{<n}.$$ All unions are over the finite set of nonempty subsets of $\{1,\dots,m\}$; empty unions are permitted. Each $E_n,W_{<n},V_n$ is compact open: finite unions of compact subsets are compact by [F15], and finite intersections of compact opens are compact by the hypothesis on $X$ [F14, given]. At $x\in E_n$ one has $J_x\ne\varnothing$ and $0<d_{J_x}\le n$ because the positive gcd $d_{J_x}$ divides a value $d_I=n$ active at $x$ [step 1.3]. If $x\in V_n$, some positive $d_I<n$ is active, so $0<d_{J_x}<n$; hence [step 3.1] gives $(Q_n)_x=0$. If $x\in E_n\setminus V_n$, then $d_{J_x}=n$: otherwise $0<d_{J_x}<n$ and $x\in U_{J_x}\subseteq W_{<n}$, a contradiction. Thus $(F_n)_x=n\mathbb Z$, $(F_{n-1})_x=0$, and $(Q_n)_x=n\mathbb Z$. Conversely, if $x\notin E_n$ then $d_{J_x}\ne n$ whenever $J_x\ne\varnothing$, since $d_{J_x}=n$ would put $x$ in $U_{J_x}\subseteq E_n$; [step 3.1] therefore gives $(Q_n)_x=0$. The stalks of $Q_n$ are consequently $n\mathbb Z$ exactly on $E_n\setminus V_n$ and zero elsewhere. [F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, step 1.3, step 3.1]

5.1 The constant section $n$ of $\mathbb Z_X$ on $E_n$ belongs to $F_n(E_n)$: every point of $E_n$ belongs to some $U_I$ with $d_I=n$, and there its germ is the germ of the generator $d_I$ of $F_n$ [F7, step 2.1]. Let $\bar n\in Q_n(E_n)$ be its image. The group homomorphism $\mathbb Z\to Q_n(E_n)$ sending $1$ to $\bar n$ induces a morphism $\mathbb Z_{E_n}\to Q_n|_{E_n}$ by the sheafification universal property [F3]: a locally constant integer-valued function acts locally as that integer times $\bar n$. Extension-by-zero adjunction [F5] yields $$\varphi_n:j_{E_n!}\mathbb Z_{E_n}\longrightarrow Q_n.$$ For $x\in E_n$ the stalk map sends $1$ to the class of $n$ in $(Q_n)_x$; outside $E_n$ its source stalk is zero [step 1.4]. This construction uses a single constant section, without assuming that membership in compact open subsets is locally constant. [F3, F5, F7, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Since $V_n\subseteq E_n$, step 1.4 gives the canonical monomorphism $j_{V_n!}\mathbb Z_{V_n}\to j_{E_n!}\mathbb Z_{E_n}$. Its composite with $\varphi_n$ is zero: at $x\in V_n$ the target stalk $(Q_n)_x$ is zero by [step 4.1], and at every other point the source stalk is zero by [step 1.4]; stalkwise zero implies the morphism is zero [F11]. The resulting sequence $$0\longrightarrow j_{V_n!}\mathbb Z_{V_n}\longrightarrow j_{E_n!}\mathbb Z_{E_n}\xrightarrow{\ \varphi_n\ }Q_n\longrightarrow0$$ is exact on stalks. Outside $E_n$ all three stalks vanish. On $V_n$ it is $0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}}\mathbb Z\to0$. On $E_n\setminus V_n$ it is $0\to0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{1\mapsto n}n\mathbb Z\to0$, which is exact [steps 1.4, 4.1, 5.1]. Exactness of sheaves follows from [F11]. [F11, step 1.4, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 Take $U=E_n$ and $V=V_n$ in [steps 4.1 and 6.1]. They are compact open with $V\subseteq U$, and the chain $0=F_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_N=K$ of [step 2.1] has precisely the quotient sequences required in the statement. If $N=0$ the chain is $F_0=K=0$ and there are no quotients to check. Only finitely many fibres of the original sections, subsets $I$, and finite unions and intersections of the resulting compact opens were used; no choice principle is used. ∎ [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 6.1]
