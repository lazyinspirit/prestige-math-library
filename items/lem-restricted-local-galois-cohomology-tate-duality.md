## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice and DC. Let $K$, $S_0\subseteq S$, the finite continuous $G_K$-module $M$ and its Cartier dual $M^D$ satisfy the hypotheses of the restricted-local-cohomology definition, including the positive-characteristic prime-to-characteristic condition. For $r=0,1,2$, the sum of the actual normalized local Tate cup pairings induces a continuous perfect duality
$$P^r_S(K,M^D)\times P^{2-r}_S(K,M)\longrightarrow\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z.$$
In particular each restricted product is locally compact Hausdorff, and each identifies topologically with the continuous character group of the other, equipped with the compact-open topology. At real places the prescribed Tate cohomology is used and at complex places the local groups are zero. This is a local and topological result; it asserts no global localization exactness.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the fields, place sets, finite modules, local conventions and explicit choices of the Statement.

[A1] AC, Countable Choice and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The restricted products and their local unramified reference subgroups outside the finite set $S_0$ are as in the stated definition. [[def-restricted-product-of-local-galois-cohomology-groups]]

[F2] The two unramified degree-one local subgroups are exact mutual annihilators when the module is unramified and its order is prime to the residue characteristic. [[lem-unramified-local-tate-annihilators-prime-to-residue-characteristic]]

[F3] For every finite place, the actual normalized local Tate pairing is perfect on finite modules in the relevant characteristic scope. [[thm-local-finite-module-tate-duality-in-characteristic-zero]] [[thm-local-finite-module-tate-duality-prime-to-positive-characteristic]]

[F4] Products of compact spaces are compact under AC; the compact-open topology is generated by constraints on compact sets. [[thm-tychonoff]] [[def-compact-open-topology-for-topological-domains]]

[F5] The finite Galois Brauer comparison identifies the real degree-two cohomology with the Brauer group, whose invariant is $\frac12\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z$. Finite-group Tate cohomology uses the supplied complete-resolution convention. [[thm-archimedean-brauer-groups-and-invariant-maps]] [[lem-finite-galois-obstruction-brauer-cohomology-comparison]] [[def-tate-cohomology-for-finite-groups]]

[F6] $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is injective as an abelian group, so finite subgroup characters extend. [[thm-injective-modules-over-a-pid-are-exactly-divisible]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify local reference annihilators and apply the actual restricted-product topology theorem.

1.1 At every finite $v\notin S_0$, the module and its Cartier dual are unramified and their orders are prime to the residue characteristic. Degree-zero unramified cohomology is the entire local invariant group. The procyclic unramified quotient has zero degree-two cohomology for finite coefficients: a finite cyclic degree-two class has an invariant representative, and inflation to a quotient enlarged by a multiple of the coefficient exponent kills it. Hence the degree-two unramified reference subgroup is zero. By [F2] the degree-one unramified groups are mutual annihilators. By [F3], in degrees zero and two the full group and zero subgroup are mutual annihilators under local duality. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 At a real place write $G=\{1,c\}$ and identify the Cartier dual with $D=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ with the extra sign from complex conjugation. Put $d=c-1$ and $N=1+c$. The two-periodic complete resolution with alternating $c-1$ and $1+c$ gives the cyclic Tate formulas $\widehat H^{2j}(G,M)=\ker d/NM$ and $\widehat H^{2j+1}(G,M)=\ker N/dM$. Under evaluation, the adjoints of $d$ and $N$ are respectively $-N$ and $-d$ on $D$. Thus evaluation descends to the indicated even-even and odd-odd pairings. Their values lie in $\{0,\frac12\}$, and extending characters by [F6] identifies the annihilator of each kernel with the appropriate image. This proves perfectness on the finite quotient groups. The explicit cyclic cup cocycle gives that same evaluation, with the generator of $H^2(G,\mathbb C^\times)$ sent to $1/2$ by [F5]; the real local pairing is therefore the stated actual normalized pairing. At a complex place the Tate cohomology of the trivial group is zero. [F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For each $v\in S_0$ choose the zero reference subgroup in the first local group and its full annihilator in the second. This changes only finitely many reference subgroups, so it does not change either restricted product or its topology. At all remaining places use the unramified subgroups; step 1.1 shows these are mutual annihilators. Local perfectness follows from [F3] at finite places and step 1.2 at archimedean places. Denote the finite local groups by $(A_v,B_v)$ and their annihilator reference subgroups by $(U_v,V_v)$. For a finite set $T\subseteq S$, the subgroup $P_T=\prod_{v\in T}A_v\times\prod_{v\notin T}U_v$ is compact and open by [F4]. These subgroups cover the restricted product, and every compact subset lies in one of them by a finite subcover and union of the finitely many index sets. The same applies to the other restricted product, so both are locally compact Hausdorff. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 For $x$ and $y$ in the two restricted products, only finitely many local pairings can be nonzero because $U_v$ and $V_v$ annihilate each other. Their sum is continuous in each variable: for a fixed tuple, finitely many coordinate constraints kill its pairing with a sufficiently small reference neighborhood. It vanishes on the product of the two open reference subgroups, so bilinearity also makes it jointly continuous. Local perfection makes the induced map to continuous characters injective. Conversely, restrict a continuous character of the first product to each finite local group and use local perfection to obtain elements $b_v\in B_v$. Its open kernel contains the elements of $\prod_v U_v$ that vanish at finitely many selected indices, so $b_v\in V_v$ outside that finite set. The finite-support elements are dense in the compact product $\prod_v U_v$, and the character agrees there with the sum of its coordinate restrictions; continuity extends the agreement to the full compact reference subgroup and then to all of the restricted product. Thus every continuous character arises from a unique element of the other restricted product. The annihilators of the compact open subgroups $P_T$ form a neighborhood basis for the compact-open character topology: every compact subset is contained in some $P_T$ by step 2.1. Under the bijection these annihilators are precisely the standard restricted-product neighborhoods, proving a homeomorphism. The same argument with the roles reversed gives the other duality. All $r=0,1,2$ and an empty place set are included. [F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra] ∎
