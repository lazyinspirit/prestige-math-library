## Statement

Let $K$ be a number field, let $\mathcal O_K$ be its ring of integers, and fix $0\ne a\in\mathcal O_K$. Define
$$D(a):=\{b\in\mathcal O_K:\text{there exists }c\in\mathcal O_K\text{ with }bc=a\}.$$
For $b,b'\in D(a)$, set $b\sim b'$ if $b/b'$ is a unit of $\mathcal O_K$. Then the quotient set $D(a)/\!\sim$ is finite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A number field $K$, its ring of integers $\mathcal O_K$, and $0\ne a\in\mathcal O_K$.

[F1] Every nonzero integral ideal of $\mathcal O_K$ factors uniquely as a finite product of nonzero prime ideals with positive integer exponents. ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]])

[F2] For $b,c\in\mathcal O_K$, $(b)$ is the ideal generated by $b$ and $(b)(c)$ is the ideal consisting of finite sums of products from those two ideals. ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]])

[F3] The units of $\mathcal O_K$ are its invertible elements. ([[def-s-integers-and-s-units-of-a-number-field]])

## Proof

1.1 If $a$ is a unit and $bc=a$, then $b$ has inverse $ca^{-1}\in\mathcal O_K$, so every $b\in D(a)$ is a unit by [F3] and $D(a)/\!\sim$ has one class. Otherwise $(a)$ is a proper nonzero integral ideal. For $b\in D(a)$ choose $c\in\mathcal O_K$ with $bc=a$. By [F2], $(b)(c)=(bc)=(a)$, so $(b)$ is an integral ideal divisor of $(a)$. By [F1], write $(a)=\prod_{j=1}^t\mathfrak p_j^{e_j}$ with finite $t\ge1$. Unique factorization implies every integral ideal divisor of $(a)$ is $\prod_{j=1}^t\mathfrak p_j^{f_j}$ with $0\le f_j\le e_j$, so there are at most $\prod_{j=1}^t(e_j+1)$ possible ideals $(b)$. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 If $(b)=(b')$, then $b\in(b')$ and $b'\in(b)$, so $b=ub'$ and $b'=vb$ for some $u,v\in\mathcal O_K$ by the principal-ideal description in [F2]. Since $b,b'\ne0$, cancellation gives $uv=1$, hence $u$ is a unit by [F3] and $b\sim b'$. Conversely, if $b/b'$ is a unit, then $(b)=(b')$. Thus distinct equivalence classes in $D(a)/\!\sim$ give distinct ideal divisors of $(a)$, which are finite by step 1.1. [given, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
