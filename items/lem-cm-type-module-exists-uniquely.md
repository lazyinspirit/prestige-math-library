## Statement

Let $(E,\Phi)$ be a CM pair and let $k$ be a number field containing the reflex field $E^r$. There is a finite-dimensional $E\otimes_{\mathbb Q}k$-module $V_\Phi$ such that
$$\operatorname{Tr}_k(a\mid V_\Phi)=\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi(a)\qquad(a\in E),$$
and any two such modules are isomorphic as $E\otimes_{\mathbb Q}k$-modules. Here $\operatorname{Tr}_k$ is the trace of the $k$-linear action of $a$.

## Statement references

[J. S. Milne, *Complex Multiplication*, Proposition 1.21](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/CM.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A CM pair $(E,\Phi)$, its reflex field $E^r$, and a number field $k\supseteq E^r$.

[F1] The reflex field is generated by the type traces, and its Galois stabilizer fixes the CM type. [[def-reflex-field-and-reflex-type-of-a-cm-type]]

[F2] Distinct embeddings are linearly independent as functions on the nonzero elements of their field. [[thm-dedekind-linear-independence-of-characters]]

[F3] Every finite separable extension is simple. For a primitive element with separable minimal polynomial $f$, factor $f$ into distinct irreducibles over $k$; the polynomial Chinese remainder theorem then expresses $E_j\otimes_{\mathbb Q}k\cong k[T]/(f)$ as a finite product of fields. If $f$ splits in $L$, the same argument gives one copy of $L$ per embedding. [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]] [[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]]

## Proof

1.1 Choose a finite Galois extension $L/k$ containing every conjugate of every factor of $E$. For each factor choose a primitive element; its separable minimal polynomial splits into distinct linear factors in $L$, so [F3] and the Chinese remainder theorem give $E\otimes L\cong\prod_{\varphi:E\to L}L$. The module $W=\bigoplus_{\varphi\in\Phi}L_\varphi$, with $a\in E$ acting on $L_\varphi$ by $\varphi(a)$, has trace $\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi(a)$. Since $k$ contains $E^r$, its Galois group preserves $\Phi$ by [F1]. Permuting these summands and acting on scalars gives a semilinear Galois action on $W$ commuting with $E$. [F1, F3, given, construct]

1.2 The invariant vectors span $W$ over $L$: otherwise a nonzero $L$-linear functional vanishing on their span, applied to $\sum_{g\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)}g(c)g(w)$ for all $c\in L,w\in W$, would give a nontrivial linear relation among distinct field automorphisms, contrary to [F2]. A basis selected from the invariant vectors is an $L$-basis of $W$, and its invariant linear combinations have coefficients in $k$. Therefore $V_\Phi=W^{\operatorname{Gal}(L/k)}$ base-changes to $W$ and has the required trace. [F2, step 1.1, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 By [F3], $B=E\otimes k$ is a finite product of fields. Every finite $B$-module is therefore a direct sum of finite-dimensional vector spaces over those field factors. After extending to $L$, the dimension on each embedding coordinate is determined by the trace character. Equality of trace functions and [F2] force multiplicity one on precisely the embeddings in $\Phi$ and zero elsewhere. Thus the dimensions on every field factor agree, and the two $B$-modules are isomorphic. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
