## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the self-intersection and Thom
suppliers. Let $W=W_{h,j}=D(\xi_{h,j})$, $M=M_{h,j}=S(\xi_{h,j})$ and let
$\varepsilon=h+j$.

1. If $\varepsilon=\pm1$, then the boundary middle form of
   [[def-boundary-middle-form-and-signature]] on $W$ is the rank-one form
   $[\varepsilon]$ generated by the zero section, and the boundary signature is
   $\sigma(W)=\varepsilon$.
2. For arbitrary $h,j$ the zero section of the disk bundle has
   self-intersection $h+j$ in the boundaryless interior; if $h+j=0$ the
   rank-one homological zero-section form is degenerate, while the
   cohomological image $I$ is the zero space and its form has signature zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The bundle $\xi_{h,j}$ over $S^4$, the disk bundle $W=D(\xi_{h,j})$ with projection $\pi$, zero section $s$, sphere bundle $M$, the classes $x=\pi^*u$ and the Thom generator $U$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The Thom isomorphism gives $H^4(W,M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\cdot U$ and $H^4(W;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\cdot x$, and $j(U)=e(\xi_{h,j})\cdot(\text{generator})= (h+j)x$ in the calibrated conventions ([[thm-thom-isomorphism-for-oriented-vector-bundles]], [[lem-euler-and-first-pontryagin-classes-of-xi-h-j]], [[def-milnor-sphere-bundle-m-h-j]]).

[L2] When $\varepsilon=\pm1$, the normalized Thom evaluation satisfies $\langle U\smile x,[W,M]\rangle=1$ ([[lem-thom-class-of-a-disk-bundle-pairs-with-the-base-generator]]).

[L3] The boundary middle form is $Q_W(y,y')=\langle\tilde y\smile\tilde y',[W,M]\rangle$ on $I=\operatorname{im}j$, with signature defined by inertia ([[def-boundary-middle-form-and-signature]]).

[L4] Assume AC. For a compact boundaryless oriented embedded $a$-submanifold in an oriented boundaryless $2a$-manifold, with the induced orientation on its normal bundle $\nu$, the self-intersection number equals the evaluation of the Euler class of $\nu$ ([[thm-self-intersection-is-the-euler-number-of-the-normal-bundle]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the map $j$ is multiplication by $\varepsilon=h+j$ on the infinite cyclic group generated by $U$; hence $I=\operatorname{im}j$ over $\mathbb R$ is $\mathbb R\cdot x$ when $\varepsilon=\pm1$ and $I=0$ when $\varepsilon=0$, and the relative lift of $x$ is $\varepsilon U$ in the first case. [L1, A1]

2.1 If $\varepsilon=\pm1$, then $Q_W(x,x)=\langle(\varepsilon U)\smile x,[W,M]\rangle=\varepsilon\langle U\smile x,[W,M]\rangle=\varepsilon$ by [L2] and [L3]; the form is therefore rank one on $I=\mathbb R\cdot x$ with matrix $[\varepsilon]$, and its inertia signature is $\varepsilon$. [step 1.1, L2, L3]

3.1 If $\varepsilon=0$, then $j=0$ by step 1.1, so $I=0$; the induced form on the zero-dimensional space $I$ is nondegenerate with no positive or negative directions, so its signature is zero, while the homological rank-one zero-section form has matrix $[0]$ and is degenerate. The radical of this zero-space form is zero; the radical of the homological rank-one form is its entire one-dimensional space. [step 2.1, L3]

4.1 For arbitrary $h,j$ the zero section $s:S^4\to W$ is a closed oriented embedded submanifold of the boundaryless interior with normal bundle $\xi_{h,j}$, so by [L4] its self-intersection number is $\langle e(\xi_{h,j}),[S^4]\rangle=h+j$. [step 3.1, L1, L4]

5.1 Therefore for $\varepsilon=\pm1$ the middle form is $[\varepsilon]$ with signature $\varepsilon$, and for $h+j=0$ the cohomological image form is zero-dimensional with signature zero while the homological zero-section form is degenerate, as asserted. [step 4.1] ∎
