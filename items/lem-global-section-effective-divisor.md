## Statement

Let $X$ be a scheme, let $\mathcal L$ be an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module
([[def-invertible-sheaf]]) and let $s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$ be a global
section. Let $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ be an open cover of $X$ with generators
$e_i\in\mathcal L(U_i)$, so that $\mathcal O_{U_i}\to\mathcal L|_{U_i}$,
$a\mapsto ae_i$, is an isomorphism, and let $f_i\in\mathcal O_X(U_i)$ be the
coefficient defined by $s|_{U_i}=f_ie_i$. Suppose that $s$ is a **regular
section** of $\mathcal L$, meaning that the morphism of sheaves
$\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal L$, $a\mapsto as$, is injective; equivalently, suppose
that each coefficient $f_i$ is a regular section of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$
([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]]), that is, multiplication by the germ
$(f_i)_x$ is injective on $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ for every $x\in U_i$. Then:

1. (independence) the coefficient $f_i$ of $s$ is regular for **every**
   trivializing open cover and every choice of generators, so the hypothesis
   is a property of $s$ alone;
2. (gluing) $f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j)^{\times}$ for all $i,j$, so
   the equations $f_i$ glue to an effective Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ with
   local-equation datum $\{(U_i,f_i)\}$ and vanishing subscheme $Z_D$
   ([[def-effective-cartier-divisor]],
   [[thm-effective-cartier-divisor-closed-immersion]]);
3. (the pair) the local isomorphisms
   $\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}\to\mathcal L|_{U_i}$ with $f_i^{-1}\mapsto e_i$
   glue to a canonical isomorphism $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L$
   ([[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]],
   [[lem-cartier-divisor-sheaf-invertible]]) which carries the canonical
   global section $1_D$ of $\mathcal O_X(D)$ to $s$; consequently
   $(\mathcal L,s)\cong(\mathcal O_X(D),1_D)$;
4. (zero locus) the ideal sheaf $I_D$ of $Z_D$ satisfies
   $I_D|_{U_i}=f_i\mathcal O_{U_i}$; that is, $Z_D$ is locally cut out by the
   coefficient of $s$ in each trivialization.

No integrality, reducedness or Noetherian hypothesis is imposed on $X$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$, an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal L$ ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]), a global section $s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$, an open cover $\{U_i\}_{i\in I}$ of $X$ with generators $e_i\in\mathcal L(U_i)$, and coefficients $f_i\in\mathcal O_X(U_i)$ with $s|_{U_i}=f_ie_i$.

[F1] $\mathcal L$ is locally free of rank one: each generator $e_i$ induces an isomorphism $\mathcal O_{U_i}\to\mathcal L|_{U_i}$, $a\mapsto ae_i$; if two sections $e,e'$ generate $\mathcal L$ on an open $V$ then $e'=we$ for a unique $w\in\mathcal O_X(V)$, and $w$ is a unit of $\mathcal O_X(V)$, while any unit multiple of a generator is again a generator ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F2] With $S_X(U)$ the set of regular sections of $\mathcal O_X$ over $U$, the sheaf $\mathcal K_X$ of meromorphic functions is the sheafification of $U\mapsto S_X(U)^{-1}\mathcal O_X(U)$; the canonical maps $\mathcal O_X(U)\to S_X(U)^{-1}\mathcal O_X(U)\to\mathcal K_X(U)$ are ring maps that send every element of $S_X(U)$ to a unit, and $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal K_X$ is injective. A germ is regular exactly when multiplication by it is injective ([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]]).

[F3] A Cartier divisor on $X$ is a global section of $\mathcal K_X^{\times}/\mathcal O_X^{\times}$, represented by meromorphic units $f_i\in\mathcal K_X(U_i)^{\times}$ on an open cover with $f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j)^{\times}$; it is effective when it admits such a representation with $f_i\in\mathcal O_X(U_i)$ regular ([[def-cartier-divisor]], [[def-effective-cartier-divisor]]).

[F4] For a Cartier divisor $D$ with datum $(U_i,f_i)$ the sheaf $\mathcal O_X(D)\subseteq\mathcal K_X$ satisfies $\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}$ and is invertible, freely generated by $f_i^{-1}$; if $D$ is effective, the constant meromorphic function $1$ is a global section of $\mathcal O_X(D)$, called the canonical section $1_D$, and $1_D|_{U_i}=f_i\cdot f_i^{-1}$ ([[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]], [[lem-cartier-divisor-sheaf-invertible]]).

[F5] An effective Cartier divisor $D$ determines a closed immersion $i_D:Z_D\hookrightarrow X$ whose ideal sheaf $I_D$ satisfies $I_D|_{U_i}=f_i\mathcal O_{U_i}$ for every local-equation datum $(U_i,f_i)$ of $D$ ([[thm-effective-cartier-divisor-closed-immersion]]).

[F6] Sections of a sheaf, and morphisms of sheaves, that agree on the members of an open cover glue uniquely ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Coefficients and regularity.** Fix $i$ and a point $x\in U_i$. Since $e_i$ generates $\mathcal L$ on $U_i$, the map $\mathcal O_{X,x}\to\mathcal L_x$, $a\mapsto ae_{i,x}$, is an isomorphism by [F1], and the germ of the structure map $\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal L$, $a\mapsto as$, at $x$ is the composite of $a\mapsto af_{i,x}$ with it. Hence the structure map is injective at $x$ if and only if multiplication by $f_{i,x}$ on $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is injective, i.e. if and only if the coefficient $f_i$ is regular at $x$ in the sense of [F2]. Therefore $s$ is a regular section of $\mathcal L$ exactly when every coefficient $f_i$ is a regular section of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$. [F1, F2]

2.1 **Unit ratios.** For each pair $i,j$ both $e_i$ and $e_j$ generate $\mathcal L$ over $U_i\cap U_j$, so $e_i=u_{ij}e_j$ for a unique unit $u_{ij}\in\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j)^{\times}$ by [F1]. Restricting $s|_{U_i}=f_ie_i$ and $s|_{U_j}=f_je_j$ to the overlap and substituting gives $f_iu_{ij}e_j=f_je_j$, and since $a\mapsto ae_j$ is injective on $U_i\cap U_j$ by [F1] we get $f_iu_{ij}=f_j$, that is, $f_i/f_j=u_{ij}^{-1}\in\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j)^{\times}$. The equations therefore have unit ratios and, by [F3], their images in $\mathcal K_X$ are meromorphic units with unit ratios on overlaps. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 **Independence of the trivialization.** Let $\{V_k\}_{k\in J}$ be any other open cover with generators $e'_k\in\mathcal L(V_k)$ and coefficients $h_k\in\mathcal O_X(V_k)$, $s|_{V_k}=h_ke'_k$, and suppose the $f_i$ are regular. Fix $k$ and a point $x\in V_k$; choose $i$ with $x\in U_i$. On $U_i\cap V_k$ both $e_i$ and $e'_k$ generate $\mathcal L$, so $e'_k=we_i$ with $w$ a unit of $\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap V_k)$ by [F1], and comparing coefficients as in step 2.1 gives $h_kw=f_i$, so $h_k|_{U_i\cap V_k}=w^{-1}f_i|_{U_i\cap V_k}$ is a unit multiple of the regular section $f_i|_{U_i\cap V_k}$, hence is regular. As $x\in V_k$ was arbitrary, $h_k$ is a regular section of $\mathcal O_{V_k}$; thus regularity of the coefficients is independent of the cover and of the chosen generators, and by step 1.1 it is equivalent to regularity of $s$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 **The effective Cartier divisor.** By the hypothesis of the Statement (equivalently, by steps 1.1 and 3.1) the coefficients $f_i$ are regular sections of $\mathcal O_{U_i}$, hence their images in $\mathcal K_X(U_i)$ are units by [F2], and their ratios are units of $\mathcal O_X$ on the overlaps by step 2.1. Therefore $(U_i,f_i)$ is a local-equation datum of a Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ in the sense of [F3], and $D$ is effective because the representing equations lie in $\mathcal O_X(U_i)$ and are regular. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

3.3 **The glued isomorphism.** By [F4] the sheaf $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is freely generated by $f_i^{-1}$ on each $U_i$, and $\mathcal L$ is freely generated by $e_i$ there; let $\varphi_i:\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}\to\mathcal L|_{U_i}$ be the unique $\mathcal O_{U_i}$-linear isomorphism with $\varphi_i(f_i^{-1})=e_i$. On $U_i\cap U_j$ we have $f_i^{-1}=u_{ij}f_j^{-1}$ and $e_i=u_{ij}e_j$ by step 2.1, so $\varphi_i(f_j^{-1})=\varphi_i(u_{ij}^{-1}f_i^{-1})=u_{ij}^{-1}e_i=e_j=\varphi_j(f_j^{-1})$; the two isomorphisms agree on a generator, hence on all sections. By the gluing axiom [F6] the $\varphi_i$ glue to a morphism $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L$, which is an isomorphism because it restricts to an isomorphism on each member of the cover. [F1, F4, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 **The canonical section realizes $s$.** The constant meromorphic function $1$ restricts to $f_i\cdot f_i^{-1}\in\mathcal O_X(D)(U_i)$ for every $i$ by [F4], so it is the global section $1_D\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X(D))$ and these local expressions glue. Under $\varphi$ its restriction to $U_i$ maps to $f_ie_i=s|_{U_i}$ by step 3.3; the local sections $s|_{U_i}$ agree on overlaps because they are restrictions of $s$, so $\varphi(1_D)=s$ by the uniqueness part of [F6]. Hence $({\mathcal L},s)\cong(\mathcal O_X(D),1_D)$, and by [F5] the ideal sheaf of the vanishing subscheme satisfies $I_D|_{U_i}=f_i\mathcal O_{U_i}$, i.e. $Z_D$ is locally cut out by the coefficient of $s$ in each trivialization. [F4, F5, F6, step 3.3]

5.1 **Conclusion.** Every regular global section $s$ of an invertible sheaf $\mathcal L$ has regular coefficients on every trivialization (steps 1.1 and 3.1); those coefficients have unit ratios and glue to an effective Cartier divisor $D$ (steps 2.1 and 3.2); the local isomorphisms on the free generators glue to a canonical isomorphism $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L$ with $\varphi(1_D)=s$ (steps 3.3 and 4.1); and $Z_D$ is locally cut out by the coefficients of $s$ (step 4.1). This proves all four assertions of the Statement. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.1] ∎

The construction uses no choice principle: the cover, the generators and the section are given, the coefficients and the glueing isomorphisms are uniquely determined by them, and no trivialization or divisor is selected. The zero divisor arises exactly from unit coefficients: if $f_i=1$ for all $i$ then $s$ generates $\mathcal L$, the divisor $D$ is the empty effective divisor with $Z_D=\varnothing$ and $\mathcal O_X(D)=\mathcal O_X$, and $\varphi$ is the inverse of the given trivialization. Conversely a regular section is never identically zero on a nonempty open: if $U\neq\varnothing$ then injectivity of $\mathcal O_X(U)\to\mathcal L(U)$ forces $s|_U\neq0$, so the zero section is regular only on the empty scheme. On the empty scheme the cover is empty, the data are vacuous, $D=0$, $\mathcal O_X(D)=\mathcal O_X$ is the zero module sheaf, which is invertible there, and the canonical isomorphism is the identity. The statement is local in $X$: it applies to a section defined on any open subscheme, and it imposes no condition on the singularities, the reducedness or the integrality of $X$; a coefficient may be any regular section of $\mathcal O_X$, including a non-zero-divisor that vanishes at a closed point of a nonreduced scheme.
