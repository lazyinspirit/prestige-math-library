## Statement

Fix $n\ge0$, a commutative ring $R$, and $\delta\in R$. For $1\le i<n$, let $d_i\in\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ be the elementary cup-cap diagram of [[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]]. Then

$$d_i^2=\delta d_i\ (1\le i<n),\qquad d_i d_{i+1}d_i=d_i,\quad d_{i+1}d_i d_{i+1}=d_{i+1}\ (1\le i\le n-2),\qquad d_i d_j=d_jd_i\ (1\le i,j<n,\ |i-j|>1).$$

Consequently the assignment $u_i\mapsto d_i$ extends to a unital $R$-algebra homomorphism

$$\pi_n:P_n(R,\delta)\longrightarrow\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge0$, a commutative ring $R$, $\delta\in R$, the endomorphism algebra $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$, its product convention, and the presented algebra $P_n(R,\delta)$.

[F1] $d_i$ has a cap joining top points $i,i+1$, a cup joining bottom points $i,i+1$, and vertical through strands at all other positions. ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]])

[F2] Stacking removes each closed cycle with one coefficient $\delta$ and leaves the exterior matching. ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]])

[F3] Products $xy=x\circ y$ stack the left factor above the right factor; composition is associative and bilinear. ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]])

[F4] $P_n(R,\delta)$ is the quotient of the free associative $R$-algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by the listed square, adjacent triple, and distant commutation relations; maps satisfying them factor uniquely through the quotient. ([[def-temperley-lieb-generator-presentation]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In the stack $d_i^2$, all strands outside positions $i,i+1$ pass straight through. In the two affected positions, the cup of the upper copy joins to the cap of the lower copy to form exactly one closed cycle; the remaining exterior path connections are the same cap and cup as in $d_i$. The stack rule [F2] therefore gives $d_i^2=\delta d_i$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 Restrict to positions $i,i+1,i+2$ in the stack with upper factor $d_i$, middle factor $d_{i+1}$ and lower factor $d_i$. The upper cap and lower cup are the exterior cap and cup of the result. The remaining path starts at the exterior top point $i+2$: it follows the upper through strand to the first interface, the middle cap to position $i+1$, the upper cup to position $i$, the middle through strand to the second interface, the lower cap to position $i+1$, the middle cup to position $i+2$, and the lower through strand to exterior bottom point $i+2$. Thus the two remaining exterior endpoints are joined exactly as in $d_i$, and all six interface points lie on this path; no closed component is formed. The product is therefore $d_i d_{i+1}d_i=d_i$. Reflecting this three-position stack horizontally about its middle strand preserves stacking and loop counts while interchanging $d_i$ and $d_{i+1}$, so it gives $d_{i+1}d_i d_{i+1}=d_{i+1}$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.3 If $|i-j|>1$, the two cup-cap diagrams modify disjoint pairs of positions. In either stack order, each affected pair has the same cap and cup and every other strand is vertical; no component joins the two disjoint position intervals and no closed cycle forms. Hence the resulting boundary matching is the same and $d_i d_j=d_jd_i$. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 The images $d_i$ satisfy every defining relation of $P_n(R,\delta)$ by steps 1.1–1.3. The quotient universal property in [F4] gives the unique unital $R$-algebra homomorphism $\pi_n$ with $\pi_n(u_i)=d_i$. For $n=0$ and $n=1$ the generator and relation sets are empty, and the unique map sends the scalar unit to the diagram identity. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎

## Remarks

The three-strand calculation only follows connected paths and cycles in the finite stacked graph. It makes no appeal to a drawing as a proof and uses no invertibility assumption on $\delta$.

The following local panels illustrate steps 1.1 and 1.2. Factors are read from top to bottom; strands outside the displayed positions are vertical and omitted. The dots at intermediate levels mark glued boundary points, not crossings.

On positions $i,i+1$, the two middle arcs in $d_i^2$ form one closed cycle. Removing that cycle contributes the scalar $\delta$ and leaves the exterior cap and cup of $d_i$.
