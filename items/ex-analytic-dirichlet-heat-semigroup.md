## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be nonempty and bounded open and let $A=\Delta_D$ be the Dirichlet Laplacian with its eigenbasis $\{e_j\}_{j\ge1}$ and eigenvalues $-\lambda_j$ of [[thm-discrete-spectrum-of-a-symmetric-elliptic-dirichlet-operator]] ([[cor-dirichlet-laplacian-generates-an-analytic-heat-semigroup]]), so that $a(e_j,v)=\lambda_j(e_j,v)$ for every $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $\lambda_j\to+\infty$. Then the heat semigroup is given by the spectral series
$$T(t)f=\sum_{j\ge1}e^{-\lambda_jt}(f,e_j)_{L^2}\,e_j,\qquad f\in L^2(\Omega),$$
converging in $L^2(\Omega)$ for every $t>0$, with $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\lambda_1t}$ when $\lambda_1>0$, and for every $m\ge1$
$$A^mT(t)f=\sum_{j\ge1}(-\lambda_j)^me^{-\lambda_jt}(f,e_j)_{L^2}\,e_j,\qquad \|A^mT(t)\|\le\Bigl(\frac{m}{et}\Bigr)^m.$$
The bound is the spectral-coefficient form of [[thm-analytic-semigroup-smoothing-estimates]] and exhibits the $t^{-m}$ singularity as the supremum of $s^me^{-st}$, $s\ge0$. For every nonempty bounded open $\Omega$ and every $t>0$, the abstract conclusion is $T(t)f\in D(A^m)$. Its spatial consequence $T(t)f\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$ also holds when $n\ge2$ and $\Omega$ is a bounded $C^{2m}$ domain, by [[thm-global-h-two-dirichlet-regularity]] and [[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]]; when $n=1$, it holds for every bounded open $\Omega$ by the distributional derivative argument in step 4.1. The statement carries the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice inherited from [[thm-discrete-spectrum-of-a-symmetric-elliptic-dirichlet-operator]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonempty bounded open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$; the symmetric Dirichlet form $a(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx$ on $V=H^1_0(\Omega)$ with associated operator $A$ defined by $a(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$ ([[def-ltwo-operator-associated-with-a-symmetric-elliptic-form]]), identified with $\Delta_D$; the eigenbasis $\{e_j\}_{j\ge1}\subseteq H^1_0(\Omega)$ of $L^2(\Omega)$ and eigenvalues $\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$ with $\lambda_j\to+\infty$, orthonormal in $L^2(\Omega)$ and satisfying $a(e_j,v)=\lambda_j(e_j,v)_{L^2}$ for every $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; the coefficients $c_j:=(f,e_j)_{L^2}$ of $f\in L^2(\Omega)$; and the semigroup $T$ generated by $A$.

[L1] The form-norm expansion: for every $u\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ the series $\sum_j(u,e_j)_{L^2}e_j$ converges to $u$ in the $H^1_0$ norm and $a(u,u)=\sum_j\lambda_j|(u,e_j)_{L^2}|^2$, while for every $f\in L^2(\Omega)$ the series $\sum_j(f,e_j)_{L^2}e_j$ converges to $f$ in $L^2(\Omega)$ with $\|f\|_{L^2}^2=\sum_j|(f,e_j)_{L^2}|^2$; this assumes the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice ([[lem-eigenbasis-expansion-in-the-form-norm]]).

[L2] The eigenbasis of the symmetric elliptic Dirichlet operator: there is an orthonormal basis $\{e_j\}$ of $L^2(\Omega)$ with $e_j\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $a(e_j,v)=\lambda_j(e_j,v)_{L^2}$ for every $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, where $\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$ are real with $\lambda_j\to+\infty$, each repeated according to finite multiplicity; the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice are assumed ([[thm-discrete-spectrum-of-a-symmetric-elliptic-dirichlet-operator]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[L3] For the principal Dirichlet form $a_0$ the operator $A$ of the weak identity $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$ is densely defined and self-adjoint with $\langle Au,u\rangle=-a_0(u,u)\le0$, hence $A=\Delta_D$ and generates a contraction analytic semigroup of maximal allowed angle $\pi/2$ ([[cor-dirichlet-laplacian-generates-an-analytic-heat-semigroup]]).

[L4] For a strongly continuous semigroup $S$ with generator $G$ and $\|S(t)\|\le Me^{\omega t}$, every real $\lambda>\omega$ lies in $\rho(G)$ and $R(\lambda,G)x=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}S(t)x\,dt$ as an improper Bochner integral ([[thm-laplace-transform-formula-for-the-semigroup-resolvent]]).

[L5] A generator of a strongly continuous semigroup is closed and densely defined ([[thm-generators-are-closed-and-densely-defined]]).

[L6] For a sectorial $A$ of angle $\delta$ with vertex $0$ and generated semigroup $T$, one has $T(t)X\subseteq D(A^m)$ for every $t>0$, $m\ge1$, and the contour semigroup is the unique exponentially bounded strongly continuous semigroup with generator $A$ ([[thm-analytic-semigroup-smoothing-estimates]], [[lem-generator-of-the-contour-semigroup-is-the-sectorial-operator]]).

[L7] For the Dirichlet Laplacian on a bounded domain the first eigenvalue satisfies $\lambda_1>0$ ([[cor-poincare-constant-and-first-dirichlet-eigenvalue]]).

[L8] Global $H^2$ Dirichlet regularity, under Countable Choice: for $n\ge2$, a bounded $C^2$ domain, $a^{ij}\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$, bounded lower-order coefficients, and $f\in L^2$, a weak zero-Dirichlet solution lies in $H^2$; the constant coefficients of $\Delta_D$ meet these coefficient hypotheses ([[thm-global-h-two-dirichlet-regularity]]).

[L9] Higher-order boundary regularity, under Countable Choice: for $n\ge2$, a bounded $C^{k+2}$ domain, $a^{ij}\in W^{k+1,\infty}$, lower-order coefficients in $W^{k,\infty}$, and $f\in H^k$, a weak zero-Dirichlet solution lies in $H^{k+2}$; the constant coefficients of $\Delta_D$ meet these coefficient hypotheses ([[thm-higher-order-boundary-regularity-for-dirichlet-problems]]).

[L10] If strongly measurable $X$-valued functions converge pointwise almost everywhere in norm and are dominated in norm by one integrable scalar function, then their Bochner integrals converge in norm ([[thm-bochner-dominated-convergence]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Diagonal action and domain characterisation. Since $a(e_j,v)=\lambda_j(e_j,v)_{L^2}$ for all $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, the defining identity gives $e_j\in D(A)$ with $Ae_j=-\lambda_je_j$; for $u\in L^2(\Omega)$ with coefficients $c_j=(u,e_j)_{L^2}$ one has $u\in D(A)$ if and only if $\sum_j\lambda_j^2|c_j|^2<\infty$, and then $Au=-\sum_j\lambda_jc_je_j$: if $u\in D(A)$ then $(Au,e_j)_{L^2}=-a(u,e_j)=-\lambda_j(u,e_j)_{L^2}$ by symmetry and [L1] gives $\sum_j\lambda_j^2|c_j|^2=\|Au\|_{L^2}^2<\infty$ together with $Au=-\sum_j\lambda_jc_je_j$, while conversely $\sum_j\lambda_j^2|c_j|^2<\infty$ makes $u_N:=\sum_{j\le N}c_je_j$ Cauchy in the form norm by [L1], hence convergent in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ to a class equal to $u$ in $L^2(\Omega)$, and for $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ continuity of $a$ in the $H^1$ norm gives $a(u,v)=\lim_Na(u_N,v)=\sum_j\lambda_jc_j\overline{(v,e_j)_{L^2}}$, the scalar series converging absolutely by Cauchy-Schwarz and [L1], so $u\in D(A)$ with $Au=-\sum_j\lambda_jc_je_j$. [L1, L2, given, algebra]

2.1 The spectral series defines the semigroup. For $t>0$ put $S(t)f:=\sum_je^{-\lambda_jt}c_je_j$ and $S(0)f:=f$: the series converges in $L^2(\Omega)$ because the weights $e^{-\lambda_jt}$ are bounded in $j$ and $\sum_j|c_j|^2=\|f\|_{L^2}^2$ by [L1], the family is linear with $\|S(t)\|\le\sup_j e^{-\lambda_jt}\le e^{-\lambda_1t}$ when $\lambda_1>0$ by [L7], $S(t)S(s)=S(t+s)$ is coefficientwise, and $\|S(t)f-f\|_{L^2}^2=\sum_j(e^{-\lambda_jt}-1)^2|c_j|^2\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$ by dominated convergence; each $S(t)f$ lies in $D(A)$ with $AS(t)f=-\sum_j\lambda_je^{-\lambda_jt}c_je_j$ by the criterion of [step 1.1], since $\sum_j\lambda_j^2e^{-2\lambda_jt}|c_j|^2\le\sup_j\bigl(\lambda_j^2e^{-2\lambda_jt}\bigr)\|f\|_{L^2}^2<\infty$. [step 1.1, L1, L7, given, algebra]

3.1 Powers and smoothing constants. For $m\ge1$ the criterion of [step 1.1] applied inductively along the diagonal action gives $A^mS(t)f=\sum_j(-\lambda_j)^me^{-\lambda_jt}c_je_j$ with $S(t)f\in D(A^m)$, and $\lambda_j\ge\lambda_1>0$ by [L7] gives $\|A^mS(t)\|\le\sup_{s\ge0}s^me^{-st}=(m/(et))^m$, the supremum being at $s=m/t$ by one-variable calculus; the same identities hold for $T$ once $T=S$ is established. [step 2.1, L7, given, algebra]

3.2 The generator is $A$, so $S=T$. For $\lambda>0$ and $g\in L^2(\Omega)$ with coefficients $g_j$, the series $u:=\sum_jg_j(\lambda+\lambda_j)^{-1}e_j$ satisfies $\sum_j\lambda_j^2|u_j|^2\le\sum_j|g_j|^2<\infty$, so $u\in D(A)$ with $(\lambda I-A)u=\sum_j(\lambda+\lambda_j)u_je_j=g$ by [step 1.1], while $(\lambda I-A)u=0$ forces $(\lambda+\lambda_j)(u,e_j)_{L^2}=0$ for all $j$ and hence $u=0$; therefore $R(\lambda,A)g=\sum_j(\lambda+\lambda_j)^{-1}g_je_j$ with $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le1/(\lambda+\lambda_1)\le1/\lambda$. To compare with the Laplace transform of $S$, for $N\ge1$ put $S_N(t)g:=\sum_{j\le N}e^{-\lambda_jt}g_je_j$. Parseval and $\lambda_j\ge0$ give $\|S_N(t)g\|_{L^2}^2=\sum_{j\le N}e^{-2\lambda_jt}|g_j|^2\le\|g\|_{L^2}^2$; by [L1], $S_N(t)g\to S(t)g$ in $L^2$ for every $t\ge0$. Thus the functions $t\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}S_N(t)g$ converge pointwise in norm and are dominated by the integrable scalar function $t\mapsto e^{-\lambda t}\|g\|_{L^2}$, so [L10] gives   $$\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}S(t)g\,dt=\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}S_N(t)g\,dt=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j\le N}(\lambda+\lambda_j)^{-1}g_je_j=R(\lambda,A)g,$$ where the last limit holds in $L^2$ since $\sum_j|g_j|^2/(\lambda+\lambda_j)^2<\infty$. By [L4] the left side is $R(\lambda,G_S)g$, where $G_S$ is the generator of $S$. Since $G_S$ is closed as a generator by [L5] and $A$ is closed as the generator of $T$ by [L3, L5], their common everywhere-defined inverse $R(\lambda,A)=R(\lambda,G_S)$ gives $D(G_S)=R(\lambda,A)L^2=D(A)$ and $G_S=A$; finally [L6] supplies the unique exponentially bounded semigroup with generator $A$, and both $S$ and the heat semigroup $T$ of [L3] are exponentially bounded strongly continuous semigroups with generator $A$, so $S=T$ and the spectral series represents the heat semigroup. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L3, L4, L5, L6, L10, given, algebra]

4.1 Abstract smoothing and the spatial reading. By [L6] the semigroup $T$ generated by the sectorial operator $A$ of [L3] satisfies $T(t)L^2\subseteq D(A^m)$ for every $t>0$ and $m\ge1$, which is the abstract content of the identities of [step 3.1]. For $n\ge2$ and a bounded $C^{2m}$ domain, put $v:=T(t)f$ and $w_r:=A^rv$ for $0\le r\le m$. Since $v\in D(A^m)$, each $w_r\in D(A)\subseteq H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $r<m$, $w_m\in L^2$, and $Aw_r=w_{r+1}$. Thus $-\Delta w_{m-1}=-w_m\in L^2$, so [L8] gives $w_{m-1}\in H^2$. Descending from $r=m-2$ to $r=0$, if $w_{r+1}\in H^{2(m-r-1)}$, then [L9] with $k=2(m-r-1)$ applies to $-\Delta w_r=-w_{r+1}$; its domain requirement is $C^{2(m-r)}$, supplied by $C^{2m}$, and its coefficient requirements hold for the Laplacian. It follows that $w_r\in H^{2(m-r)}$, in particular $v\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$. For $n=1$ and any bounded open $\Omega$, $A=\partial_x^2$ distributionally. Each $w_r\in D(A)\subseteq H^1_0(\Omega)$ for $r<m$ satisfies $w_r''=w_{r+1}\in L^2$; induction gives $w_r=v^{(2r)}$ distributionally, while $w_r'\in L^2$ for $r<m$ and $w_m\in L^2$. Hence all weak derivatives of $v$ through order $2m$ lie in $L^2$, so $v\in H^{2m}(\Omega)$ without boundary regularity. The abstract $D(A^m)$ conclusion remains valid in every dimension for arbitrary bounded open $\Omega$. [step 3.1, L3, L6, L8, L9, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The series for $T(t)f$ is the coefficientwise functional calculus of the self-adjoint generator along its eigenbasis; the universal scalar bound $(m/(et))^m$ gives an explicit instance of the generic estimate $C_mt^{-m}$ of [L6]. The actual norm is $\sup_j\lambda_j^me^{-\lambda_jt}$, which can be strictly smaller because the eigenvalues are discrete. The statement inherits the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice from the spectral suppliers [L2] and [L7] and no further choice principle beyond Dependent Choice is used in the verification.
