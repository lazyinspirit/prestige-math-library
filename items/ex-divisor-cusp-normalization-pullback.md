## Example

Let $k$ be a field, let
$$A=k[t^{2},t^{3}]\subseteq k[t]$$
be the $k$-subalgebra generated by $x:=t^{2}$ and $y:=t^{3}$, and let
$\nu:\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\to C$ with $C=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be the
morphism induced by the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow k[t]$, so that
$\nu^{\#}(x)=t^{2}$ and $\nu^{\#}(y)=t^{3}$. Since $t=y/x$ in
$\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, the fraction field of $A$ is $k(t)$, and $k[t]$ is
its integral closure in $k(t)$: the morphism $\nu$ is the normalization of
the cusp $C$. Let $D=V(x)\subseteq C$ be the closed subscheme cut out by $x$,
viewed as the effective Cartier divisor with ideal sheaf $x\mathcal O_{C}$.
Then:

1. $D$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $C$ and the pullback $\nu^{*}D$ is
   defined;
2. $\nu^{*}D$ is the effective Cartier divisor on the affine line
   $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ cut out by $t^{2}$, and the source
   $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ is a normal affine line;
3. the equation $t^{2}$ of $\nu^{*}D$ has order $2$ at the origin $[0]=V(t)$
   and order $0$ at every other prime divisor of the affine line;
4. the invertible sheaves satisfy
   $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}k[t]}(\nu^{*}D)\cong\nu^{*}\mathcal O_{C}(D)$,
   with the constant sections corresponding.

The point of the example is that $t^{2}$ is not a generator of $A$'s
fractional structure by accident: the cusp is not normal precisely because
$t\notin A$, while along the divisor $V(x)$ the pullback recovers the
vanishing of order two that the equation $x$ encodes only "half" of, the
ring $A/(x)$ being a nonreduced thickening of the origin of the cusp.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$, the $k$-subalgebra $A=k[t^{2},t^{3}]\subseteq k[t]$ with $x=t^{2}$, $y=t^{3}$, the affine schemes $C=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, the morphism $\nu:\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\to C$ induced by the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow k[t]$, and the closed subscheme $D=V(x)\subseteq C$ cut out by $x$.

[F1] $k[t]$ is a unique factorisation domain, in particular a domain ([[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]]), and $A$ is a subring of it.

[F2] A field has exactly the two ideals $(0)$ and $(1)$ and is therefore a Noetherian ring ([[def-field]], [[lem-field-is-noetherian]]); by Hilbert's basis theorem $k[t]$ is a Noetherian ring ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

[F3] $A=k[x,y]$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra, so it is a Noetherian ring ([[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[F4] Points of an affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}R$ are the prime ideals of $R$, the basic opens $D(f)$ form a basis of the topology, the stalk at a prime $\mathfrak p$ is the localisation $R_{\mathfrak p}$, and a homomorphism $\varphi:A\to B$ induces the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ whose sheaf map is localisation ([[def-affine-scheme]], [[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]], [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]], [[def-morphism-affine-schemes-from-ring-map]]).

[F5] An effective Cartier divisor on a scheme is a Cartier divisor with a local-equation representation $(U_{i},f_{i})$ in which each equation is a regular section, with ideal sheaf $\mathcal I_{D}$ generated locally by the $f_{i}$; Cartier divisors themselves are the global sections of $\mathcal K_{X}^{\times}/\mathcal O_{X}^{\times}$ ([[def-cartier-divisor]], [[def-effective-cartier-divisor]]).

[F6] A section is regular exactly when multiplication by each of its germs is injective; on a domain $R$ the nonzero elements of $R$ and the nonzero elements of every localisation $R_{\mathfrak p}$ are regular ([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]]).

[F7] $k[t]$ is an integrally closed domain ([[lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed]]).

[F8] $k[t]$ is a principal ideal domain ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid]]).

[F9] $\dim k[t]=1$ ([[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]]).

[F10] An integral scheme is a nonempty reduced irreducible scheme, and equivalently a nonempty scheme whose every nonempty affine open is the spectrum of a domain; in particular the spectrum of a domain is integral; a scheme is Noetherian when it has a finite affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings; a Noetherian scheme is normal when every local ring is an integrally closed domain, and the integral closed subschemes of codimension one of a normal Noetherian scheme are its prime divisors ([[def-integral-scheme]], [[def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]], [[def-weil-divisor-normal-noetherian-scheme]]).

[F11] A localization $S^{-1}R$ of an integrally closed domain $R$ is
integrally closed. Indeed, if $z\in\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ satisfies
$z^n+\sum_{i<n}(a_i/s_i)z^i=0$, put $s=\prod_{i<n}s_i$. Then $sz$
satisfies the monic equation
$$(sz)^n+\sum_{i<n}a_i(s^{n-i}/s_i)(sz)^i=0$$
over $R$, so $sz\in R$ and $z\in S^{-1}R$
([[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[F12] The integral closure of a domain $A$ in a ring $B$ is the set of elements of $B$ integral over $A$; $A$ is integrally closed in its fraction field when it equals its integral closure there ([[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[F13] The height of a prime is the Krull dimension of the local ring $R_{\mathfrak p}$, and the Krull dimension of a ring is the supremum of the lengths of chains of prime ideals ([[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]]).

[F14] Contraction along a localisation map $R\to S^{-1}R$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection onto the primes disjoint from $S$; for the localisation $R_{\mathfrak p}$ at a prime this makes $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$ the unique maximal ideal and identifies the local ring with fractions $r/s$, $s\notin\mathfrak p$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]], [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

[F15] In a Noetherian integrally closed domain, the localisation at a prime ideal of height one is a discrete valuation ring ([[thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr]]).

[F16] On a normal locally Noetherian scheme, a prime divisor $Z$ with generic point $\xi$ has discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O_{X,\xi}$, and the order of a global meromorphic unit $f$ along $Z$ is $\operatorname{ord}_{Z}(f)=v_\xi(f_\xi)$ for the normalised valuation $v_\xi$ of that ring; $\operatorname{ord}_{Z}(f)=0$ exactly when $f_\xi$ is a unit of $\mathcal O_{X,\xi}$ ([[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]]).

[F17] Let $f:X\to Y$ be a morphism and $D$ a Cartier divisor on $Y$ with an $f$-admissible local-equation datum; then $f^{*}D$ is defined, independently of the datum, and if $D$ is effective with regular equations $f_{i}$ on $U_{i}$, the datum $f_{i}/1$ is admissible exactly when the pulled-back regular equations $f^{\#}(f_{i})$ are again regular, in which case $f^{*}D$ is the effective Cartier divisor cut out locally by the $f^{\#}(f_{i})$ ([[def-pullback-cartier-divisor]]).

[F18] For a morphism $f:X\to Y$ and a Cartier divisor $D$ on $Y$ with $f^{*}D$ defined there is a canonical isomorphism $\mathcal O_{X}(f^{*}D)\cong f^{*}\mathcal O_{Y}(D)$, which for effective $D$ matches the constant sections $1$ and is independent of the admissible datum ([[lem-pullback-cartier-divisor-line-bundle]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** exhibit the effective equation $x=t^{2}\in A$, observe that its pullback $t^{2}\in k[t]$ is a nonzerodivisor because $k[t]$ is a domain, and compute the order of $t^{2}$ along the prime divisors of the affine line with the discrete valuation of the local ring at the origin.

1.1 $A$ is a subring of the domain $k[t]$ by [F1], so $A$ is a domain, and $x=t^{2}\neq0$; $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra by construction and is Noetherian by [F3]; $k[t]$ is Noetherian by [F2], and the inclusion $A\hookrightarrow k[t]$ induces the morphism $\nu:\operatorname{Spec}k[t]\to C$ with $\nu^{\#}(x)=t^{2}$ and $\nu^{\#}(y)=t^{3}$ by [F4], where $C=\operatorname{Spec}A$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given]

1.2 Since $A$ is a subring of the domain $k[t]$ by [F1] and $0\neq x=t^{2}\in A$, multiplication by $x$ on $A$ and on every localisation of $A$ is injective, so $x$ is a regular section in the sense of [F6]; hence $D=V(x)$ is the effective Cartier divisor on $C$ with ideal sheaf $x\mathcal O_{C}$ and local equation $x$ on the whole of $C$, in particular a Cartier divisor by [F5]. [F1, F5, F6]

1.3 The polynomial ring $k[t]$ is an integrally closed domain by [F7]; every prime localisation $k[t]_{\mathfrak p}$ is therefore integrally closed by [F11]; since $k[t]$ is Noetherian by [F2] and of dimension $\dim k[t]=1$ by [F9], the affine line $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ is a normal Noetherian integral scheme of dimension one in the sense of [F10]. [F2, F7, F9, F10, F11]

2.1 As $A$ is a domain and Noetherian, $C=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is an integral Noetherian scheme by [F10]. [F10, step 1.1]

2.2 The element $t^{2}=\nu^{\#}(x)$ is a nonzero element of the domain $k[t]$, hence a nonzerodivisor, so the pulled-back regular equation $\nu^{\#}(x)$ is regular; by the effective case of [F17] the datum $x/1$ for $D$ of step 1.2 is $\nu$-admissible and $\nu^{*}D$ is the effective Cartier divisor on $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ cut out by the global equation $t^{2}$. [F17, step 1.1, step 1.2]

2.3 The fraction field $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ equals $k(t)$, because $t=y/x$ is a fraction of elements of $A$ and conversely $A\subseteq k[t]$; the element $t$ is integral over $A$ since $t^{2}=x\in A$, so $k[t]=A+A t$ is a finite $A$-module; every $z=a+bt$ with $a,b\in A$ satisfies $z^2-2az+(a^2-b^2x)=0$, so $k[t]$ is integral over $A$, and every element of $k(t)$ integral over $A$ is integral over $k[t]$ and hence lies in $k[t]$ because $k[t]$ is integrally closed by [F7]; therefore $k[t]$ is the integral closure of $A$ in $k(t)$ by [F12], and $\nu$ is the normalization of $C$, the source being normal by step 1.3. [F7, F12, step 1.1, step 1.3]

2.4 Since $\dim k[t]=1$ by [F9] and height is computed by [F13], a prime $(\ne(0))$ of $k[t]$ has height one exactly when it is nonzero, and $(0)$ has height zero; by [F8] every nonzero prime of $k[t]$ is principal, say $(g)$ with $g$ a prime element of $k[t]$. Hence the prime divisors of $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, which by [F10] are its height-one integral closed subschemes, are exactly the closed points $V(g)$ for prime elements $g\in k[t]$. [F8, F9, F10, F13, step 1.3]

3.1 The element $t$ is prime in $k[t]$ because $k[t]/(t)\cong k$ is a field, so $[0]=V(t)$ is a prime divisor by step 2.4; the local ring $k[t]_{(t)}$ is a discrete valuation ring by [F15], since $k[t]$ is a Noetherian integrally closed domain by [F2] and [F7] and $(t)$ has height one by step 2.4, and its maximal ideal is the extension $(t)k[t]_{(t)}$ by [F14], generated by the image of $t$; consequently $v_{[0]}(t)=1$ for the normalised valuation of [F16]. [F2, F7, F14, F15, F16, step 2.4]

3.2 Let $Z=V(g)$ be a prime divisor of $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ with $(g)\neq(t)$, as in step 2.4. If $t^{2}\in(g)$ then $g\mid t^{2}$, and since the prime element $g$ divides the product $t\cdot t$ it divides $t$, so $(t)\subseteq(g)$; both are height-one primes by step 2.4, so $(t)=(g)$, contradicting $Z\neq[0]$. Hence $t^{2}\notin(g)$, and $t^{2}$ lies outside the maximal ideal $(g)k[t]_{(g)}$ of the local ring, so it is a unit there by [F14] and $v_{Z}(t^{2})=0$, that is, $\operatorname{ord}_{Z}(t^{2})=0$ by [F16]. [F14, F16, step 2.4]

3.3 By [F18] applied to the morphism $\nu$ and the divisor $D$ of step 1.2, whose pullback is defined by step 2.2, there is a canonical isomorphism $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}k[t]}(\nu^{*}D)\cong\nu^{*}\mathcal O_{C}(D)$ matching the constant sections $1$. [F18, step 2.2]

4.1 The element $t^{2}$ is a global meromorphic unit of the integral scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, and its order at the prime divisor $[0]$ is $\operatorname{ord}_{[0]}(t^{2})=v_{[0]}(t^{2})=2v_{[0]}(t)=2$ by [F16] and step 3.1. [F16, step 3.1]

5.1 In summary, $D=V(x)$ is an effective Cartier divisor on the cusp $C$ by step 1.2; the pullback $\nu^{*}D$ is defined and is cut out by the global equation $t^{2}$ on the normal affine line $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ by steps 1.3 and 2.2; the equation $t^{2}$ has order $2$ at the origin and order $0$ at every other prime divisor by steps 3.2 and 4.1; $k[t]$ is the integral closure of $A$ by step 2.3, so that $\nu$ is the normalization of the cusp; and the associated invertible sheaves agree by step 3.3. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.1] ∎

The order computed at the origin is the local multiplicity of the pulled-back equation: there $t^{2}$ is the square of the uniformiser $t$, while away from the origin it is a unit in the local rings of the affine line. On the cusp, $x=t^{2}$ is a nonzerodivisor and $A/(x)\cong k[y]/(y^{2})$. The nonnormality of $A$ is confined to the vertex: $V(x)$ is that vertex and $A_x=k[t,t^{-1}]$ is normal. It does not prevent $V(x)$ from being an effective Cartier divisor.
