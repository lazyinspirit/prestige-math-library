## Statement

Let $T$ be a small triangulated category with a bounded nondegenerate t-structure, abelian heart $E$, and a specified bounded heart-valued cohomological functor realizing its t-cohomology. Let $V(E)$ be the exact determinant Picard category: its generators are objects of $E$, their isomorphisms and short exact sequence comparisons, with naturality, filtration associativity and split-commutativity relations. Inclusion of the heart induces an equivalence $V(E)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal W(T)$ with the universal triangulated determinant category. In particular it induces isomorphisms on component groups and on unit automorphism groups. The assertion does not require the Axiom of Choice. In addition, if $S$ is a unital left Noetherian ring and every finitely generated left $S$-module has a finite projective resolution (with no uniform bound required), the canonical map $K_1(S)\to K_1^{\det}(D_{\mathrm{perf}}(S))$ is an isomorphism.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite signed words, kernel flags, the two-subobject refinement identity, and truncation-rotation induction.

1.1 Construct $V(E)$ exactly as the finite-word construction in [F1], replacing triangles by short exact sequences and octahedra by their three-step filtration diagrams. Objects are signed finite words in symbols $[M]$; an inverse reverses the word and reverses the signs. Include evaluation, associativity, symmetry, isomorphism and exact-sequence generators and impose their typed Picard and exact determinant relations. All are sets because $E$ is small. Quotient the set of finite paths by the generated congruence. Formal evaluations make tensoring invertible, and all generators are invertible arrows. The resulting category is Picard. Sending the generators to any specified exact determinant data extends uniquely to paths, and respects the quotient precisely when those data satisfy the displayed relations; transformations extend termwise. Thus it has the asserted universal property. Inclusion of $E$ into $T$ sends its exact sequences to triangles: the cone of a monomorphism between heart objects has cohomology only in degree zero, equal to its cokernel, by the supplied cohomological property, so [F11] identifies it with that cokernel. The connecting map is unique by orthogonality, and three-step filtration diagrams are their octahedra. Thus inclusion gives $j:V(E)\to\mathcal W(T)$. [F1, F2, F4, F11, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We record the finite calculation rules, including signs. For a Picard object $L$ with specified inverse $L^v$ and evaluation $\delta_L:L\otimes L^v\to1$, an arrow $u:L\to M$ induces the covariant arrow $u^v:L^v\to M^v$ defined by $\delta_M(u\otimes u^v)=\delta_L$. The inverse-tensor comparison is defined by shuffling labelled factors beside their inverses and evaluating. These operations respect composition and tensor associativity: after evaluation both sides are literally the same labelled permutation and evaluations, and tensor cancellation is faithful. Adjacent shuffles satisfy involution, disjoint commutation and braid by the symmetry and hexagon equations. Let $\varepsilon(L)$ be the unit automorphism obtained by cancelling the self-interchange of two copies of $L$. Cancelling the four crossings of $L,M,L,M$ shows $\varepsilon(L\otimes M)=\varepsilon(L)\varepsilon(M)$ and $\varepsilon(L)^2=1$. For exact determinant data, $[-1_M]=\varepsilon([M])$: the block unipotents on $M\oplus M$ act trivially by naturality on $0\to M\to M\oplus M\to M\to0$, and $E_{12}(1)E_{21}(-1)E_{12}(1)=S\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$ gives the assertion. Consequently $\varepsilon([B])=\varepsilon([A])\varepsilon([C])$ in an exact sequence, by its comparison and the first identity. For triangulated data the rotation rule and multiplicativity of shift evaluations also follow from the axioms, as follows. For $A\xrightarrow aB\xrightarrow bC\xrightarrow cTA$, use the octahedron on $A\xrightarrow aB\xrightarrow bC$, whose composite is zero, and the split cone $C\oplus TA$. Its two triangles on this split object have maps $(1,c)$ and $(c,-1)$, compared with $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ by the automorphism $(u,v)\mapsto(u,cu-v)$. Its determinant is $\varepsilon([A])$, since its off-diagonal block is a unipotent and its diagonal is $1,-1_{TA}$; $\varepsilon([TA])=\varepsilon([A])$ follows by cancelling the shift pair. The other octahedron, on $A\to0\to C$, says that inserting the $A,TA$ evaluation pair and the split $C$ comparison is the unit operation. Substitution into the first octahedral equation proves the rotation rule: the comparison for $A\to B\to C$ equals insertion of the $A,TA$ pair followed by the inverse rotated comparison on $B,TA$. Finally apply the octahedron on $A\xrightarrow aB\to0$. Its other two triangles are the shift evaluations of $A,B$ and the twice-rotated triangle $C\to TA\to TB$; applying the just-proved rotation rule to the latter identifies its comparison with that of $TA\to TB\to TC$ after the labelled middle shuffle. Thus the shift evaluation on $B,TB$ equals the product of those on $A,TA$ and $C,TC$ after the two triangle comparisons. This proves shift-unit multiplicativity from the axioms, with the actual negative map retained. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Here is the refinement identity used below, proved without an external additivity theorem. For subobjects $U,V\subset B$, put $I=U\cap V$ and $J=U+V$. The two refinements through $I\subset U\subset J\subset B$ and $I\subset V\subset J\subset B$ have common factors $I,U/I,V/I,B/J$, with the two middle factors interchanged. Indeed $J/I=(U/I)\oplus(V/I)$: the sum map is epic and has zero kernel. Filtration associativity reduces both refinements to this split sequence, and split commutativity supplies precisely that middle interchange. This also proves the three-by-three rule for any exact grid: refine the middle object by the two inverse-image subobjects, apply this identity, and use the quotient identifications from kernels and images. In particular no unnoticed scalar or suppressed self-symmetry occurs. The identity applies equally to bounded graded objects, degree by degree. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.3 The unit automorphism group of $V(\operatorname{Proj}_{\mathrm{fg}}(S))$ is $K_1(S)$, by the following finite presentation calculation. Given a loop, tensor every vertex with one finite positive projective sum containing a labelled copy for each negative factor occurring anywhere. Cancel those negatives against their own labelled copies. A vertex is now the determinant of an ordered positive direct sum. An isomorphism letter becomes its summand isomorphism; a negative isomorphism becomes its inverse on the remaining labelled copy. An exact comparison becomes a chosen split-sequence isomorphism; structural symmetries become block permutation matrices. The inverse-evaluation interchange becomes $-1$ by step 2.1. Thus the path becomes actual projective isomorphisms whose composite is an automorphism $u$ of one projective $P$. Add a finite complement making $P\oplus P'$ free and take its stable $K_1(S)$ class. This class is independent of every normalization choice: extra labelled summands and complements add identity blocks; relabelling and different vertex identifications conjugate all adjacent matrices and hence conjugate the final matrix; changing a splitting is a block unipotent. After adjoining free complements a projective block unipotent has matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&v\\0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ on finite free sums and is a product of elementary matrices. Each elementary matrix is a stable commutator, by $[E_{ik}(x),E_{kj}(1)]=E_{ij}(x)$ with a new index $k$, so is zero in stable abelianization. The defining relations also preserve this class: functoriality is matrix composition, naturality is the split block-triangular identity, filtration associativity identifies the same ordered three-fold sum, and split commutativity is exactly the displayed block permutation, including its $-1$ self-interchange. All Picard structural and evaluation equations become identical labelled permutations and cancellations. Therefore the construction is a well-defined homomorphism on the quotient of finite loop paths. Its composite with free-module automorphisms is their usual stable class, and the normalization proves its reverse composite is identity on every loop. This proves the asserted identification without importing a presentation theorem for algebraic K-theory. [F5, F10, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

9.2 Let $\mathcal A$ be the finite left $S$-modules. Every such module has a resolution by finite projectives: take finite free covers of syzygies, which remain finite by [F7]. Explicitly the pullback of two projective covers with kernels $K,L$ splits in both directions and gives $K\oplus Q\cong L\oplus P$; iteration compares the two resolutions with projective summands added. Thus the last finite syzygy is a summand of projectives. No uniform bound on $d$ is needed. For a finite module isomorphism lift it between finite resolutions one degree at a time by projectivity; two lifts are chain homotopic by the same lifting applied to their difference, starting at the augmentation. For $0\to L\to M\to N\to0$, construct the horseshoe resolution of $M$ from the chosen finite resolutions of $L,N$. In degree zero lift the projective cover of $N$ through its pullback along $M\to N$, adjoin the projective cover of $L$, and repeat on kernels. Its terms are the direct sums of the two projective terms, its kernel sequences remain exact, and it terminates after their finite lengths. Compare it with any chosen resolution of $M$ by step 9.2, and apply $I$ from step 4.2. For a finite filtration, repeated horseshoes give a resolution filtered by split projective summands resolving its successive quotients. Such a split filtered projective lifts through a strict filtered epimorphism: lift the summand born at filtration index $j$ through the epimorphism at that index and sum these finitely many lifts. Inducting on degree therefore gives filtered comparison maps, unique up to filtered homotopy, for every finite filtered diagram. Applying $D,I$ proves independence of horseshoes and naturality. A three-piece filtration gives precisely associativity; a two-piece split filtration and its block interchange give symmetry. All defining finite relations of $V(\mathcal A)$ are therefore preserved. Conversely for exact determinant data already on $\mathcal A$, the syzygy sequences $0\to K_{i+1}\to P_i\to K_i\to0$, $K_0=M$, telescope to its alternating resolution determinant. 12.1 First check triangle compatibility of $\eta$ when $A$ is concentrated in degree $i$ and $B,C$ have no cohomology below $i$.
