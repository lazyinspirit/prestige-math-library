## Statement

Let $K/k$ be a finitely generated field extension, and set
$$k_{\mathrm{alg}}:=\{a\in K:a\text{ is algebraic over }k\}.$$
Then $k_{\mathrm{alg}}$ is a finite extension of $k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field extension $K/k$ generated as a field by a finite list.

[F1] There are $a_1,\ldots,a_m\in K$ with
$K=k(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$; the list may be empty
([[def-finitely-generated-field-extension]]).

[F2] $F(S)$ denotes the smallest subfield containing $F$ and a set $S$; in
particular $k(T)$ and $L(a)$ have their generated-field meanings
([[def-field-extension-generated-subfields-and-simple-extension]]).

[F3] The set $k_{\mathrm{alg}}=\operatorname{acl}_K(k)$ consists of the elements
of $K$ algebraic over $k$ and is a subfield
([[def-relative-algebraic-closure]], [[thm-algebraic-elements-form-a-subfield]]).

[F4] A field generated by finitely many elements algebraic over a base field is
finite over that base
([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

[F5] A finite field extension is algebraic
([[thm-finite-field-extensions-are-algebraic]]).

[F6] An element $x$ is algebraic over $F$ exactly when $F(x)/F$ is finite
([[cor-element-algebraic-iff-simple-extension-finite]]).

[F7] In a tower of finite field extensions degrees multiply:
$[M:k]=[M:F][F:k]$ for $k\subseteq F\subseteq M$
([[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]).

[F8] Algebraicity is transitive in a tower of field extensions
([[thm-transitivity-of-algebraicity]]).

[F9] A set $T$ is algebraically independent over a field exactly when the
evaluation map from the polynomial ring with variables indexed by $T$ is
injective ([[def-algebraically-independent-finite-tuples-over-a-field]]).

[F10] An element is algebraic over a field when it is a root of a nonzero
polynomial over that field; transcendental means not algebraic
([[def-algebraic-and-transcendental-elements]]).

[F11] A linearly independent subset of a vector space with an $n$-element
spanning set has at most $n$ elements
([[cor-independent-set-is-no-larger-than-a-finite-spanning-set]]).

[F12] $[M:F]$ is the finite dimension of $M$ as an $F$-vector space when
$M/F$ is finite ([[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix a finite generating list $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ from [F1]. [construct]
Scan the list in order, starting with $T_0=\varnothing$. At stage $i$, adjoin $a_i$ to the
selected set exactly when it is transcendental over the field generated by the
previously selected elements. The selected set stays algebraically independent:
if adjoining $a_i$ created a nonzero polynomial relation, its degree in $a_i$
would be positive, since a relation of degree zero would contradict independence
of the preceding tuple. Write it as a polynomial in $a_i$; independence of the
preceding tuple makes a nonzero coefficient remain after evaluation, so this
would make $a_i$ algebraic over its generated field, a contradiction. Every omitted generator is
algebraic over the field generated at its stage and stays algebraic after
enlarging that field. Thus the final finite set $T$ is algebraically
independent over $k$, and every $a_i$ is algebraic over $k(T)$. This finite scan
uses no choice principle. [F1, F2, F9, F10, algebra]

2.1 The field $K=k(T)(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ is generated over $k(T)$ by finitely many elements algebraic over that field. [step 1.1, algebra]
Hence [F4] makes $K/k(T)$ finite. Set
$n:=[K:k(T)]$; since both are fields, $n\ge1$. If $m=0$, then $K=k$, $T$ is
empty and $n=1$. [F1, F2, F4, F12, step 1.1]

2.2 Let $E$ be any algebraic intermediate field $k\subseteq E\subseteq K$. The tuple $T$ remains algebraically independent over $E$. [step 1.1, algebra]
Otherwise choose a
nonempty subset $T'\subseteq T$ of least cardinality that is algebraically
dependent over $E$, and choose $t\in T'$. The tuple $T'\setminus\{t\}$ is
independent over $E$. A nonzero polynomial relation on $T'$ can be viewed as a
polynomial in $t$ with coefficients in the other variables; independence of
$T'\setminus\{t\}$ ensures at least one coefficient stays nonzero after
evaluation. The relation must have positive degree in $t$, since degree zero
would give a relation on the independent tuple $T'\setminus\{t\}$. Hence it
gives a nonzero polynomial over $E(T'\setminus\{t\})$ vanishing at $t$, so
$t$ is algebraic over that field.

The extension $E(T'\setminus\{t\})/k(T'\setminus\{t\})$ is algebraic. Each
of its elements belongs to the field generated over $k(T'\setminus\{t\})$ by
some finite list $e_1,\ldots,e_r\in E$. Each $e_j$ is algebraic over $k$ and
therefore over the larger base $k(T'\setminus\{t\})$. By [F4], the field
generated by this finite list is finite over that base, and [F5] makes each
of its elements algebraic over the base. This proves the extension is
algebraic. Transitivity [F8] would then make $t$ algebraic over
$k(T'\setminus\{t\})$, contradicting the algebraic independence of $T$ over
$k$. If $T$ is empty there is no nonempty dependent subset, so the same
conclusion holds vacuously. [F2, F4, F5, F8, F9, F10, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $L$ be any finite intermediate extension $k\subseteq L\subseteq K$, write $d=[L:k]$, and choose a $k$-basis $b_1,\ldots,b_d$ of $L$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]
Since $L/k$ is
finite, [F5] makes it algebraic, so step 2.2 applies and $T$ is algebraically
independent over $L$. Suppose
$$\sum_{i=1}^d b_i\lambda_i=0,\qquad \lambda_i\in k(T).$$
Let $P=k[X_t:t\in T]$ and let $\operatorname{ev}_k:P\to K$ evaluate each
$X_t$ at $t$. By step 1.1 and [F9], $\operatorname{ev}_k$ is injective, so
$\operatorname{ev}_k(g)\ne0$ for every nonzero $g\in P$. The quotients
$\operatorname{ev}_k(f)/\operatorname{ev}_k(g)$, where $f,g\in P$ and $g\ne0$,
form the subfield generated by $k$ and $T$:
they form a subfield containing those elements, and every subfield containing
them contains all these quotients. By [F2], this is $k(T)$. Choose a common
denominator $g$ and numerators
$f_1,\ldots,f_d\in P$ with $\lambda_i=\operatorname{ev}_k(f_i)/
\operatorname{ev}_k(g)$. Multiplying the displayed relation by its nonzero
denominator gives $\sum_i b_i\operatorname{ev}_k(f_i)=0$. Algebraic independence over $L$
means the evaluation map $L[X_t:t\in T]\to K$ is injective, so the polynomial
$\sum_i b_i f_i(X)$ in that ring is zero. For each monomial its coefficient is
a $k$-linear combination of $b_1,\ldots,b_d$; their basis independence makes
every coefficient in every $f_i$ zero. Thus all $\lambda_i$ are zero, and
$b_1,\ldots,b_d$ are linearly independent over $k(T)$ inside $K$. Since $K$
has an $n$-element spanning set over $k(T)$, [F11] gives
$$[L:k]=d\le n.$$ [F2, F9, F11, F12, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Let $D$ be the set of degrees $[L:k]$ of finite intermediate fields $k\subseteq L\subseteq K$. [step 3.1, choose]
It is nonempty because $k$ itself has degree $1$. Every degree in $D$ is a
positive integer, since an intermediate field contains $1\ne0$. Step 3.1
therefore puts $D$ inside $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Start with the attained value
$1\in D$ and inspect $2,\ldots,n$ in order, replacing the current value by
$r$ exactly when $r\in D$. This finite scan yields a greatest member
$d_0\in D$. By the definition of $D$, there is a finite intermediate field
$L$ with $[L:k]=d_0$; fix one such witness.
This is one existential choice from a nonempty set, not a choice function on a
family. [F12, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 Take any $a\in k_{\mathrm{alg}}$. [step 4.1, algebra]
It is algebraic over $k$ and therefore
over $L$, since its nonzero polynomial over $k$ remains nonzero over $L$. By
[F6], $L(a)/L$ is finite; since $L/k$ is finite, [F7] makes $L(a)/k$ finite
and
$$[L(a):k]=[L(a):L][L:k].$$
Because $L(a)\subseteq K$, this degree lies in $D$, so it is at most
$d_0=[L:k]$. The tower formula and
$[L(a):L]\ge1$ force $[L(a):L]=1$, whence $L(a)=L$ and $a\in L$. Thus
$k_{\mathrm{alg}}\subseteq L$. Conversely [F5] makes every element of the
finite extension $L/k$ algebraic over $k$, so $L\subseteq k_{\mathrm{alg}}$.
Therefore $k_{\mathrm{alg}}=L$ and is a finite extension of $k$. [F2, F3, F5,
F6, F7, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 This proof includes the empty generator list and empty transcendence tuple. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]
If $n=1$, the only possible finite intermediate degree is $1$ and the maximum
argument gives $k_{\mathrm{alg}}=k$. No characteristic, separability, or
perfectness hypothesis is used. AC is not used: the generator scan and degree
bound are finite, and the basis and maximal-degree witness are each fixed only
for one arbitrary finite intermediate field. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2,
step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
