## Statement

Assume AC. Work on a complete filtered probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal F,(\mathcal F_t)_{0\le t\le T},P)$ whose filtration is
right-continuous and contains every $P$-null subset of $\mathcal F$ in
$\mathcal F_0$. Let $m\ge1$ be finite, let
$W=(W^1,\dots,W^m)$ be an adapted standard $m$-dimensional Brownian motion
[[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]] whose increments after time $s$ are
independent of $\mathcal F_s$, and let $H=(H^1,\dots,H^m)$ be a predictable
row-vector process [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]] with
$\mathbb E\int_0^T\|H_s\|_2^2ds<\infty$. For
$$M_t=\sum_{j=1}^m\int_0^tH_s^j\,dW_s^j,$$
the continuous version satisfies
$$\mathbb E\sup_{0\le t\le T}|M_t| \le16\sqrt m\,\mathbb E\left(\int_0^T\|H_s\|_2^2ds\right)^{1/2}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the complete usual filtered probability space, adapted Brownian motion and increment-independence hypotheses above; and the predictable finite-energy integrand.

[F1] For a real Brownian Itô integral $N_t=\int_0^tK_s\,dB_s$ with finite energy and Brownian increments independent of the current filtration, $\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T}|N_t|^2\le4\mathbb E\int_0^TK_s^2ds$. [[thm-doob-maximal-bound-for-the-ito-integral]]

[F2] Stopping a finite-energy Itô integral at a stopping time agrees, up to indistinguishability, with integrating the predictable stopped integrand. [[thm-stopping-an-ito-integral]]

[F3] The coordinates of a standard finite-dimensional Brownian motion are independent standard one-dimensional Brownian motions. [[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]]

[F4] The predictable sigma-algebra is generated by rectangles $(s,u]\times C$ with $C\in\mathcal F_s$ and by the time-zero sets; for any stopping time $\rho$, the indicator process $1_{[0,\rho]}$ is predictable. [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]] [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]

[F5] The finite-energy Itô integral is an isometry from predictable $L^2$ into mean-zero $L^2$ random variables. [[thm-ito-isometry-and-linearity-in-predictable-l2]]

[F6] An elementary predictable integrand has integral equal to its defining finite sum over Brownian increments. [[def-ito-integral-of-an-elementary-predictable-process]]

[F7] Bounded elementary predictable integrands are dense in predictable $L^2([0,T]\times\Omega)$. [[thm-density-of-elementary-predictable-processes-in-predictable-l2]]

[F8] For a nonnegative measurable function, the integral over a product space equals either iterated integral; applied to $1_{\{(\lambda,\omega):\lambda<Z(\omega)\}}$, this gives $\mathbb EZ=\int_0^\infty\mathbb P(Z>\lambda)\,d\lambda$. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F9] A map $\rho$ is a stopping time when $\{\rho\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$ for every $t\ge0$, and $A\in\mathcal F_\rho$ when $A\cap\{\rho\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$ for every $t\ge0$. [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]]

[F10] For a continuous adapted real process, its supremum on a compact interval equals its supremum over rational times in the interval together with the right endpoint, and is measurable in the terminal sigma-algebra. [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]]

[F11] Dominated convergence applies to the product-measure limit below: the squared difference of the stopped-interval indicators times $H^2$ tends to zero almost everywhere and is bounded by the integrable function $H^2$. [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[A1] AC is stated because the Brownian, Itô-integral, stopping and maximal-inequality interfaces in [F1]–[F4] adopt it. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof


1.1 If $T=0$, both sides vanish. Henceforth $T>0$. For stopping-time notation only, extend the filtration by $\mathcal F_t=\mathcal F_T$ for $t>T$; it remains complete and right-continuous, and the predictable sigma-algebra on $[0,T]$ is unchanged by [F4]. All Brownian integrals and estimates below are on $[0,T]$; no Brownian property for this extension after $T$ is asserted or used. First take $m=1$ and write $B=W^1$. On the common null exceptional set, set $M$ and $Q$ to zero; completeness keeps them adapted and makes their paths continuous everywhere. This changes no integral class. Put $Q_t=\int_0^tH_s^2ds$ and $M^*=\sup_{t\le T}|M_t|$. The continuous version makes $M^*$ measurable, and [F1] gives $\mathbb E(M^*)^2\le4\mathbb E Q_T<\infty$, so $\mathbb EM^*<\infty$. [F1, F4, given]

1.2 Fix $\lambda>0$. Let $\tau$ be the first time $|M_t|\ge\lambda$, capped at $T$, and put $A_\lambda=\{M^*\ge\lambda\}$. Continuity and adaptation make $\tau$ a stopping time by [F9]–[F10], and $A_\lambda\in\mathcal F_\tau$: for each $t<T$, $A_\lambda\cap\{\tau\le t\}=\{\sup_{s\le t}|M_s|\ge\lambda\}$, whose supremum is measurable by [F10]; for $t\ge T$, the intersection is $A_\lambda\in\mathcal F_T\subseteq\mathcal F_t$ by the same terminal supremum fact. Let $R_t=Q_t-Q_{t\wedge\tau}$; this is continuous and adapted. Let $\sigma$ be its first hitting time of $\delta^2\lambda^2$, capped at $T$; it is a stopping time by [F9]–[F10], where $0<\delta\le1/4$. The set $A_\lambda\cap\{\tau<s\}$ is the countable union, over rational $u>0$, of $(u,\infty)\times(A_\lambda\cap\{\tau\le u\})$. Each second factor belongs to $\mathcal F_u$ by $A_\lambda\in\mathcal F_\tau$. Thus it is predictable; intersecting with $\{s\le\sigma\}$ preserves predictability by [F4]. [given, F4, F9, F10]

2.1 Define $N_t=\int_0^t1_{A_\lambda}1_{(\tau,\sigma]}(s)H_s\,dB_s.$ For each $n$, let $G_n=\{jT2^{-n}:0\le j\le2^n\}$ and round $\tau,\sigma$ upward to their least grid points $\tau_n,\sigma_n\in G_n$. They are stopping times because $\{\tau_n\le t\}=\bigcup_{q\in G_n,\ q\le t}\{\tau\le q\}\in\mathcal F_t$, and likewise for $\sigma_n$; monotonicity of the ceiling map gives $\tau_n\le\sigma_n$ and $\tau_n\downarrow\tau$, $\sigma_n\downarrow\sigma$. Since $A_\lambda\in\mathcal F_\tau$ and $\tau\le\tau_n$, one has $A_\lambda\in\mathcal F_{\tau_n}$: for every $t$, $A_\lambda\cap\{\tau_n\le t\}=(A_\lambda\cap\{\tau\le t\})\cap\{\tau_n\le t\}\in\mathcal F_t$. For an elementary predictable integrand on a refinement of this grid, the coefficient on $(t_k,t_{k+1}]$ after multiplying by $1_{A_\lambda}1_{(\tau_n,\sigma_n]}$ is measurable at $t_k$: the active event $\{\tau_n\le t_k<\sigma_n\}$ is in $\mathcal F_{t_k}$, and $A_\lambda\cap\{\tau_n\le t_k\}$ is in $\mathcal F_{t_k}$. Thus the finite-sum definition [F6] gives $\int_0^t1_{A_\lambda}1_{(\tau_n,\sigma_n]}H_s\,dB_s=1_{A_\lambda}(M_{t\wedge\sigma_n}-M_{t\wedge\tau_n})$ for elementary $H$. For general predictable $H$, approximate in predictable $L^2$ by elementary $H^k$ using [F7]. The isometry [F5] passes the left side to its limit in $L^2(P)$, and [F1] applied to $H^k-H$ gives $\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T}|M_t^k-M_t|^2\to0$, passing the stopped evaluations on the right to their limits for this fixed grid. As $n\to\infty$, the interval indicators converge outside the graphs of $\tau$ and $\sigma$, which are $dt\otimes P$-null by Tonelli [F8]. Dominated convergence [F11] with majorant $H^2$ and the isometry [F5] pass the identity to $(\tau,\sigma]$. Continuity gives convergence of the right side to $1_{A_\lambda}(M_{t\wedge\sigma}-M_{t\wedge\tau})$ almost surely for each fixed $t$, proving the required stopped-integral identity entirely on $[0,T]$. Both sides have continuous versions, so their equality at the countable rational times and continuity give equality up to indistinguishability. The accumulated energy of the integrand is at most $\delta^2\lambda^2 1_{A_\lambda}$ on every path. Thus [F1] gives $\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T}|N_t|^2 \le4\delta^2\lambda^2\mathbb P(A_\lambda).$ On $\{M^*>2\lambda,\ Q_T^{1/2}<\delta\lambda\}$ the threshold defining $\sigma$ is not reached before $T$, and after the first hit of $\lambda$ the continuous path moves by more than $\lambda$. Hence $\sup_{t\le T}|N_t|>\lambda$ on that event. Markov's inequality and the preceding display yield $\mathbb P(M^*>2\lambda,\ Q_T^{1/2}<\delta\lambda) \le4\delta^2\mathbb P(M^*\ge\lambda).$ [F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, F8, F11, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Split the event $\{M^*>2\lambda\}$ according to whether $Q_T^{1/2}\ge\delta\lambda$. The bound in step 2.1 gives $\mathbb P(M^*>2\lambda) \le\mathbb P(Q_T^{1/2}\ge\delta\lambda) +4\delta^2\mathbb P(M^*\ge\lambda).$ For any nonnegative $Z$, the integrals of $1_{\{\lambda<Z\}}$ and $1_{\{\lambda\le Z\}}$ over $\lambda>0$ agree pathwise, since they differ at at most one point. Integrating this inequality over $\lambda>0$ and applying Tonelli [F8] to $1_{\{(\lambda,\omega):\lambda<M^*(\omega)\}}$ and $1_{\{(\lambda,\omega):\lambda<Q_T(\omega)^{1/2}\}}$ gives $\mathbb EM^*\le\frac{2}{\delta}\mathbb E Q_T^{1/2} +8\delta^2\mathbb EM^*.$ With $\delta=1/4$, the last coefficient is $1/2$, so $\mathbb EM^*\le16\mathbb E Q_T^{1/2}$. [F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For general finite $m$, write $M=\sum_{j=1}^m M^j$ with $M_t^j=\int_0^tH_s^j\,dW_s^j$. Apply the scalar result of step 3.1 to each coordinate and then the finite-sum Cauchy–Schwarz inequality pathwise: $\mathbb E\sup_{t\le T}|M_t| \le16\sum_{j=1}^m\mathbb E\left(\int_0^T|H_s^j|^2ds\right)^{1/2} \le16\sqrt m\,\mathbb E\left(\int_0^T\sum_{j=1}^m|H_s^j|^2ds\right)^{1/2}.$ This is the claimed estimate. AC enters only through [A1] and the cited Brownian and Itô interfaces. [A1, F3, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
## Source notes

The proof is a local good-lambda derivation from the published $L^2$ Doob maximal bound and a finite-grid proof of the stopped-integral identity on $[0,T]$. It supplies the $L^1$ maximal estimate used in the H-M-S energy proof; no general BDG theorem is imported without proof.
