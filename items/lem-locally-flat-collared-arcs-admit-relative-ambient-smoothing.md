## Statement

Let $M=S^3\times[0,1]$ carry its standard smooth structure. Suppose $f:[0,1]\to M$ is a locally flat proper arc and is a smooth embedding on neighborhoods of its two source endpoints. Then an ambient topological isotopy, equal to the identity on neighborhoods of the two boundary levels of $M$, carries $f$ to a smooth parametrized embedding. It can preserve any smaller prescribed source end collars pointwise.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The indicated parametrized locally flat arc with smooth end collars. No choice axiom is assumed.

[F1] Smooth Euclidean arc embeddings are dense among continuous curves and form a locally solvable class under arbitrarily small ambient smooth pushes. The relative form permits a finite union $C$ of closed source intervals where the map is already smooth on a neighborhood and is to remain fixed. The ambient push may also fix a closed protected set $B$ separated from the moving part of the curve, by putting the extended velocity field equal to zero near $B$. [[lem-smooth-arc-embeddings-form-a-dense-controlled-solvable-class]]

[F2] Two countably presented dense locally solvable embedding classes, supplied with specified controlled-isotopy selectors, can be meshed without choice. This includes control of both inverse limits and preservation of a fixed compact carrier and protected set. [[lem-countably-presented-controlled-embedding-classes-can-be-meshed]]

[F3] Euclidean finite-parameter transversality and selection of a first rational good parameter for compact embedding families require no choice. [[lem-euclidean-parametric-transversality-and-canonical-good-parameters]]

## Proof

1.1 At an interior point of the arc take a local-flatness chart $q:U\to\mathbb R^4$ carrying its intersection with the entire arc onto the first coordinate axis, parametrized by a source open interval $V$. Shrink the chart inside an ambient smooth coordinate chart $\Omega\to\mathbb R^4$. If $qf(s)=(u(s),0,0,0)$, choose a smooth increasing diffeomorphism $r:V\to\mathbb R$ and replace $q$ by $(r\circ u^{-1},\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R^3})q$. Thus the new chart satisfies $qf(s)=(r(s),0,0,0)$ and makes the parametrized arc smooth. The operation is a homeomorphism of the chart, not an assertion that it is smooth in the ambient structure. For a compact interval $P\subset V$, this gives two smooth structures on $U$: the ambient one and the one transported by $q$. The curve $f|P$ is smooth in the second. [given, construct]

1.2 Choose the prescribed smaller closed end collars inside the given smooth source neighborhoods. Choose a larger right end collar $D$ still inside the smooth right end neighborhood, leaving a strict buffer before the smaller right collar. Choose a final patch meeting the initial point of $D$ and extending into the smaller right collar; its left endpoint lies before $D$. All preceding patches may be chosen to end before $D$, their union reaching into the left overlap of that final patch. Cover the remaining compact source interval by finitely many local-flatness intervals $V_i$ as above. Choose each chart inside an ambient smooth coordinate chart $\Omega_i$. Choose an increasing subdivision $t_0<\cdots<t_N$ of that remaining interval fine enough that each segment $[t_{i-1},t_i]$ has a slightly larger compact interval $P_i=[l_i,r_i]$ lying in one $V_i$. Arrange $t_0$ and $t_N$ in the interiors of the given smooth end neighborhoods. Choose $\eta>0$ so small that the $\eta$-neighborhood of every original compact image $f(P_i)$ lies in $\Omega_i$, and require the sum of all subsequent ambient displacements to be less than $\eta/2$. Process these intervals from left to right. Inductively the arc is smooth on a source neighborhood of $[0,t_{i-1}]$; until the final patch it agrees with the original arc on all of $D$. In the current $P_i$ freeze a left collar $[l_i,c_i]$ with $c_i>t_{i-1}$ still in that smooth neighborhood. At the final patch also freeze a right collar beginning inside $D$ and strictly before the smaller prescribed right collar. These sets $C$ are finite unions of nondegenerate closed source end intervals, as required for the relative small-push construction. Protect the entire prior arc $f([0,t_{i-1}])$ and, before the final patch, all of $f(D)$; at the final patch protect only the smaller prescribed right end collar; the moving part has positive distance from this protected set, because the frozen collars provide strict source buffers and the full current arc is an embedding of a compact interval. Also protect fixed small neighborhoods of the boundary levels, disjoint from the remaining moving interior arc. [given, construct, choose]

2.1 Here are explicit dense lists for the relative smooth classes needed below. In either coordinate system choose a smooth base curve $a$ agreeing with the frozen curve near the finite union $C$ of closed end intervals. In the transported system use $qf$ itself. In the ambient system multiply the known smooth curve near $C$ by a smooth source cutoff equal to one near $C$ and supported in its known smooth neighborhood, extending the product by zero. This is a finite construction and gives a supplied extension across the endpoints. Choose a fixed smooth $\chi$ flat zero exactly on $C$ and positive elsewhere, using the usual exponential bump on each of the finitely many complementary intervals. Enumerate the curves $a+\chi P$, with $P$ a vector of rational polynomials, retaining those that are embeddings and satisfy the required open neighborhood restrictions. This list is dense among continuous embeddings agreeing with the frozen curve on $C$: for any such $g$, first cut off $g-a$ close to $C$, introducing arbitrarily small uniform error by continuity; divide the cutoff difference by $\chi$, now continuous on the whole interval, and approximate it uniformly by rational polynomials. Such approximation follows explicitly from Bernstein polynomials followed by rational approximation of their finitely many coefficients. To make the resulting smooth curve an embedding, add $\chi(u+sv)$ with arbitrarily small rational vectors $u,v$. Where $\chi>0$, the derivative coefficients $\chi'$ and $\chi+s\chi'$ cannot both vanish; for $s\ne r$, simultaneous vanishing of $\chi(s)-\chi(r)$ and $s\chi(s)-r\chi(r)$ forces both points into $C$. On $C$ the fixed derivative is nonzero and two distinct fixed points remain distinct. Thus [F3], in source dimensions at most one and two below four, gives good embedding parameters; their set is open by compactness, so rational ones exist. The resulting curve is still in the enumerated polynomial family. Every retained entry has its specified extension germ. With these germs, [F1] supplies a specified small-push selector for pairs of entries, including the relative and protected-set constraints. All motion and closeness moduli may be taken as the first positive reciprocal integers satisfying the required inequalities; this is specified data rather than countable choice. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 We first smooth one such $P$, relative to a finite union $C$ of closed source end intervals contained in a region where $f$ is already ambient smooth. Let $B\subset M$ be a closed protected set disjoint from $f(P\setminus\operatorname{Int}_P C)$. Choose an open set $W$ with compact closure in $U\setminus B$ containing that compact moving-part set. Consider embeddings of $P$ into $U$ agreeing with $f$ on $C$, sufficiently close to $f$ that their moving parts remain in a smaller such set inside $W$. Use the two dense lists of step 2.1 in this relative embedding space, taking their retained entries as the two classes. Both classes are locally solvable by their specified [F1] selectors, applied in the ambient coordinates of $\Omega$ for the first class and the transported coordinates of $q$ for the second. In either set of coordinates protect the closed complement of $W$ and take the motion bound small enough that the moving compact part remains separated from that complement. This confines the isotopies to $W$. For the transported class conjugate its Euclidean pushes by $q$; uniform continuity of $q$ and $q^{-1}$ on the relevant compact carriers makes the conjugate pushes arbitrarily small in the ambient metric. Use the protected-set version to keep the pushes supported in $W$ and fixed on $f(C)$, and extend them by the identity over $M\setminus W$. Each local solvability application may take its bound still smaller to remain in this relative embedding space. Their allowed homeomorphism group is the group fixing $B\cup f(C)$ and equal to the identity outside $\overline W$; these are closed conditions under uniform convergence of maps and inverses. The transported list contains $f|P$ as its zero-polynomial entry. Apply [F2] to that embedding and a nearby ambient-smooth member. It produces an ambient isotopy supported in $\overline W$, fixed on the protected set and $f(C)$, making $f|P$ ambient smooth. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Apply step 3.1 to the current interval $P_i$, using its current local-flatness chart, namely the original chart transported by the already performed ambient isotopies and then restricted to a small product box around the compact image of $P_i$. The displacement bound ensures that image remains in $\Omega_i$; the product box can therefore be chosen inside $\Omega_i$. Those isotopies preserve local flatness. The result makes the arc smooth on the interior of $P_i$, in particular on a source neighborhood of $[t_{i-1},t_i]$, and preserves the preceding smooth initial interval and the protected right collar pointwise. At the next step the same induction hypothesis therefore holds. After finitely many steps every point of the source belongs to an open region of smoothness. Each resulting parametrized curve is globally injective, since it is the ambient image of the original embedding; its derivative is everywhere nonzero on those smooth regions. It is therefore a smooth embedding of the compact interval. Concatenating the finitely many ambient isotopies gives the asserted isotopy, and all fixed boundary neighborhoods and prescribed smaller source collars remain fixed throughout. [step 3.1, step 1.2, construct] ∎

## Remarks

This donor proves the local-flatness-to-smoothness bridge using the separately proved choice-free controlled smooth-arc supplier, explicit countable lists with extension germs, and the choice-free meshing lemma. It does not cite an unproved four-dimensional taming theorem or strengthen the canonical target with a choice assumption. The finitely many local charts and interval cutoffs can be selected by finite choice, which is a theorem of ZF; all infinite selections inside a patch use the specified least-index rules.

For a collared arc with arbitrary source parametrization at its ends, this lemma first applies after reparametrizing the source by an increasing homeomorphism so that its endpoint height parameters become smooth. The image is unchanged. No boundary-fixed ambient isotopy is claimed to change a nonsmooth or nonstandard source parameter in a fixed end neighborhood.
