## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice and DC. For a characteristic-zero complete discretely valued field $F$ with finite residue field, let
$$h_F\{a,b\}=\operatorname{rec}_F(a)(\sqrt b)/\sqrt b\in\{\pm1\}$$
be the canonical quadratic Hilbert homomorphism on $K_2^M(F)$, with the distinguished arithmetic local Artin map. For every finite field extension $E/F$, the canonical Milnor transfer satisfies
$$h_F(N_{E/F}x)=h_E(x)\qquad(x\in K_2^M(E)).$$
This includes residue characteristic two and non-Galois extensions. Separately, set $h_{\mathbb C}=1$ and $h_{\mathbb R}\{a,b\}=-1$ exactly when $a<0$ and $b<0$. These define homomorphisms on Milnor $K_2$, and the same transfer identity holds for each finite extension of $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields and canonical maps in the Statement, under the stated three choice axioms.

[A1] AC, Countable Choice and DC are explicit hypotheses. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] Canonical Milnor transfers exist under AC, are transitive, and satisfy $N_{E/F}j_{F/E}=[E:F]$. [[thm-bass-tate-kato-norm-transfer-for-milnor-k-groups]]

[F2] For a finite-residue nonarchimedean local field, the Moore quotient of $K_2^M$ is its finite cyclic roots-of-unity group, with divisible kernel. [[thm-local-field-k2-moore-sequence-and-divisible-kernel]]

[F3] The canonical local Hilbert symbol is Steinberg and defines a surjective Milnor homomorphism to the roots of unity of the given order. [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F4] For a finite abelian extension of finite-residue local fields the Milnor norm is surjective in every degree at least two. Only degree two and quadratic extensions are used here. [[lem-finite-abelian-local-milnor-k-norms-are-surjective]]

[F5] A finite extension of a complete discrete valued field is complete discretely valued, its residue extension is finite, and $[E:F]=ef$. Thus a finite extension of a field in the nonarchimedean Statement is again in that scope. [[lem-complete-discrete-valuation-extension-degree-and-tame-norm]]

[F6] A finite field extension has a finite normal closure inside an algebraic closure. In characteristic zero this closure is separable and Galois. [[thm-finite-normal-closures-exist-and-are-finite]]

[F7] A finite group has a Sylow two subgroup; a nontrivial finite two-group has nontrivial centre. Finite Galois fixed fields have the subgroup/index degrees, and normal subgroups give Galois quotient extensions. [[thm-sylow-first-theorem]] [[thm-nontrivial-center-of-a-finite-p-group]] [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]] [[thm-normal-subgroups-and-quotients-in-the-galois-correspondence]]

[F8] Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a root. Milnor groups are generated by multilinear symbols subject to the Steinberg relations. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-minimum-modulus-proof]] [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify the local order-two Milnor quotient, prove norm surjectivity on it by finite Sylow reduction, and treat the archimedean groups directly.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 For any nonarchimedean field $A$ in the Statement, write the Moore exact sequence from [F2] as $0\to D\to K_2^M(A)\to\mu(A)\to0$. Divisibility gives $D\subseteq2K_2^M(A)$. Hence the induced map $K_2^M(A)/2\to\mu(A)/2$ is an isomorphism: if a class maps to $2z$, lift $z$ and subtract twice that lift to obtain an element of $D$, which itself is twice an element. In characteristic zero $-1\ne1$, so the finite cyclic group $\mu(A)$ has even order and $\mu(A)/2$ has order two. By [F3], $h_A$ is surjective and kills doubles. It is consequently the unique nontrivial homomorphism from this group of order two to $\{\pm1\}$. This applies also in residue characteristic two. [A1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 If $B/A$ has odd degree, [F1] gives $N_{B/A}j(x)=[B:A]x$, congruent to $x$ modulo two, so its norm is onto modulo two. If $B/A$ is quadratic, it is separable in characteristic zero, its nonidentity root conjugation makes it Galois cyclic, and [F4] makes its norm onto already integrally. All fields involved are in the needed finite-residue complete scope by [F5]. [A1, F1, F4, F5, given, algebra]

1.3 Every nonzero complex number has a square root by [F8]. Thus each generator $\{a,b\}$ of $K_2^M(\mathbb C)$ equals $2\{\sqrt a,b\}$, and the whole group is two-divisible. For $\mathbb R$, positive entries are squares, so modulo two every symbol is a multiple of $\{-1,-1\}$. The sign formula in the Statement is bilinear on $\mathbb R^\times$ and is Steinberg because $u$ and $1-u$ cannot both be negative. It therefore defines a homomorphism on $K_2^M(\mathbb R)$ with value $-1$ on $\{-1,-1\}$. Hence $K_2^M(\mathbb R)/2$ has order two, with exactly this sign character, whereas $K_2^M(\mathbb C)/2=0$. [F8, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 A finite Galois extension with two-group $P$ has a tower with quadratic steps. Indeed a nontrivial $P$ has a nonidentity central element by [F7]; its order is a power of two, so a suitable power generates a central subgroup $Z$ of order two. The top step $M/M^Z$ is quadratic and $M^Z/A$ has the smaller Galois group $P/Z$. Induction gives the rest of the tower; for $P=1$ the tower is empty. Applying step 1.2 at each step and transitivity [F1] proves that the norm of any such two-group extension is onto modulo two. [F1, F5, F7, step 1.2, given, induction, algebra]

3.1 For arbitrary finite $E/F$, take a finite normal closure $M/F$ containing $E$, using [F6]. In characteristic zero an irreducible polynomial has nonzero derivative of smaller degree and is coprime to that derivative; hence all its roots are distinct. Thus the finite normal closure is separable and Galois. Choose a Sylow two subgroup $P$ of its Galois group using [F7]. With $D=M^P$, the degree $[D:F]$ is odd and $M/D$ is Galois with two-group $P$. Steps 1.2 and 2.1 make $N_{M/F}=N_{D/F}N_{M/D}$ onto modulo two. Since also $N_{M/F}=N_{E/F}N_{M/E}$, the map $N_{E/F}:K_2^M(E)/2\to K_2^M(F)/2$ is onto. Both groups have order two by step 1.1, so $h_F\circ N_{E/F}$ and $h_E$ are the same unique nontrivial homomorphism on $K_2^M(E)/2$. This proves the asserted identity without a cross-base Artin norm-functoriality or cup-transfer premise. Identity extensions and trivial Sylow subgroups are included. [A1, F1, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, given, choose, algebra]

4.1 By [F8], every real irreducible polynomial has degree one or two, and adjoining a root of an irreducible quadratic gives $\mathbb C$; thus a finite extension of $\mathbb R$ is $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, and a finite extension of $\mathbb C$ is itself. This follows for a finite extension by choosing an element outside the base: the quadratic already supplies $i$, and any remaining algebraic element has a minimal polynomial split over $\mathbb C$, hence lies there. Transfers in the identity cases are identities by [F1]. For $\mathbb C/\mathbb R$, the norm is a homomorphism from the two-divisible group in step 1.3, so its image is contained in $2K_2^M(\mathbb R)$. Consequently $h_{\mathbb R}(Nx)=1=h_{\mathbb C}(x)$. This proves every archimedean clause separately from the finite-residue providers. [F1, F8, step 1.3, given, algebra] ∎
