## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be any field,
let $n\ge1$ be finite, let $0\ne f\in k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ be nonconstant,
and let $a\in k^n$ satisfy $f(a)=0$. Put
$$X=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(f)\bigr),$$
and let $x\in X(k)$ be the point defined by evaluation at $a$. Then the
structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth at $x$ if and only
if $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)=1$. Here “smooth at $x$” means that the structure
map is standard smooth at the corresponding prime after shrinking to an open
neighborhood of $x$ ([[def-smooth-morphism-classical]],
[[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]]). The equation is used with its actual
scheme structure; no reducedness, perfectness, or characteristic assumption
is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, finite $n\ge1$, a nonzero nonconstant polynomial
$f\in P=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, a point $a\in k^n$ with $f(a)=0$, the affine
$k$-scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}(P/(f))$, the corresponding point $x$, and
the Axiom of Choice. Set $\mathfrak m_a=(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)$,
$A=P/(f)$, $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak m_a/(f)$, and
$R=P_{\mathfrak m_a}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_aR$.

[F1] [[def-multiplicity-hypersurface-point]]: the least nonzero homogeneous
part of $f(a+t)$ has degree $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)$, and this is the
$\mathfrak m$-adic order of $f$ in $R$. Thus multiplicity one means exactly
that the class of $f$ in $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ is nonzero.

[F2] [[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]]: a finite-presentation map is
standard smooth at $\mathfrak q$ when, after localizing away from
$\mathfrak q$, it has a presentation with an invertible Jacobian minor; for
the one equation $f$, the $1\times1$ minor is a partial derivative of $f$.

[F3] [[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]: smoothness is defined locally by
standard smooth presentations at the source points. In particular, the
pointwise condition at $x$ is precisely standard smoothness at $\mathfrak q$
after a neighborhood shrinking.

[F4] [[thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]]: for a finite-presentation
map $R_0\to S$ and $\mathfrak q_0$ over $\mathfrak p_0$, standard smoothness
at $\mathfrak q_0$ is equivalent to flatness of
$(R_0)_{\mathfrak p_0}\to S_{\mathfrak q_0}$ and geometric regularity of the
fiber at $\mathfrak q_0$.

[F5] [[def-ag-geometrically-regular-algebra-and-fibre]]: geometric regularity
of a fiber at a point requires that, after every field extension, all local
rings at points above it are regular; it therefore implies regularity after
the extension $k/k$ itself.

[F6] [[prop-modules-over-a-field-are-projective-flat-and-injective]]: under
AC every module over a field is flat, so the local $k$-algebra map to any
local ring of a chart is flat.

[F7] [[lem-regular-local-regular-quotient-ideal-is-parameter-generated]]:
under AC, if $(R_0,\mathfrak n,\ell)$ is regular local, $J\subseteq\mathfrak n$,
and $R_0/J$ is regular, then $J$ is generated by an initial part of a regular
system of parameters.

[F8] [[lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis]]: under AC, the
classes of a regular system of parameters form a basis of
$\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2$; hence the classes in any initial part are linearly
independent.

[F9] [[thm-localisation-and-polynomial-extension-of-regular-rings]]: under
AC, finite polynomial extensions and localizations of regular Noetherian
rings are regular; in particular $R=P_{\mathfrak m_a}$ is regular local.

[F10] [[thm-localisation-commutes-with-quotients]]:
$(S^{-1}P)/(S^{-1}I)\cong\bar S^{-1}(P/I)$ for an ideal $I$ and a
multiplicative set $S$.

[F11] [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]: the local ring of
$\operatorname{Spec}A$ at $\mathfrak q$ is canonically $A_{\mathfrak q}$.

[F12] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC supplies a choice function for every family
of nonempty sets; its uses here are only those explicitly inherited through
[F4], [F6], [F7], [F8], and [F9].

## Source qualification

Milne, *Algebraic Geometry* v6.10, §4b, Definition 4.9 and the following
paragraph (printed pp. 83–84 / PDF pp. 83–84), defines the leading form as the
least-degree nonzero homogeneous summand and uses its degree as the
multiplicity of a plane-curve singularity. The book's global convention is
that $k$ is algebraically closed; this is terminology and classical context,
not proof of the present arbitrary-field scheme statement. Milne's Chapter 10
supplement, §f, 10.58 (PDF p. 16) and 10.64 (PDF p. 18), treats
algebraic schemes over a field and states that a rational point is nonsingular
exactly when its local ring is regular. These source statements motivate the
criterion; the argument below proves the equation-level result for every
field and retains nonreduced hypersurfaces.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Translate to $t_i=X_i-a_i$ and write $f(a+t)=L(t)+$ terms of degree at least two. By [F1], $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)=1$ exactly when $L\ne0$. For each $i$, the coefficient of $t_i$ in $L$ is $\partial f/\partial X_i(a)$: expanding each monomial $(a_j+t_j)^{e_j}$, its linear $t_i$ coefficient is the formal derivative coefficient, with the integer exponent interpreted in $k$. Thus multiplicity one is equivalent, in every characteristic, to at least one partial derivative being nonzero at $a$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)=1$, and choose the least index $i$ with $h=(\partial f/\partial X_i)(a)\ne0$. The class of the polynomial $\partial f/\partial X_i$ is outside $\mathfrak q$ in $A$, so localizing there gives the one-equation presentation $k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]/(f)$ with its $1\times1$ Jacobian minor invertible. Since $n\ge1$, this is a standard smooth chart by [F2]; hence the structure morphism is smooth at $x$ by [F3]. [F2, F3, step 1.1, given]

2.2 Conversely, suppose the structure morphism is smooth at $x$. By [F2]–[F3], shrink once around $x$ to a finite-presentation standard smooth chart $B$ and let $\mathfrak q_B$ be its prime for $x$. The local $k$-module $B_{\mathfrak q_B}$ is flat by [F6]. Apply [F4] with base field $k$ and its zero prime: the fiber is $B$, and it is geometrically regular at $\mathfrak q_B$. Taking the extension $k/k$ in [F5] shows that $B_{\mathfrak q_B}$ is regular. The chart does not change the stalk, so [F11] gives $B_{\mathfrak q_B}\cong A_{\mathfrak q}$; then [F10] identifies this ring with $R/(f)R$. By [F9], $R$ is regular local, and $J=(f)R\subseteq\mathfrak m$ is nonzero by the finite order in [F1]. By [F7], $J$ is generated by an initial part $u_1,\ldots,u_r$ of a regular system of parameters. Their classes in $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ are linearly independent by [F8]. Since $J=(f)R$ and $f\in\mathfrak m$, every $sf$ modulo $\mathfrak m^2$ is the residue of $s$ times the class of $f$, so the image of $J$ in $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ has dimension at most one. Hence $r\le1$. If $r=0$, [F7] makes $J=0$, contrary to [F1]; therefore $r=1$, and the image of $J$ is nonzero. It is spanned by the class of $f$, so $f\notin\mathfrak m^2$. By [F1], $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)=1$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, step 1.1, given]

3.1 In one variable, at $a=0$, $f=t$ has multiplicity $1$ and derivative $1$, giving the standard smooth chart; $f=t^p$ in characteristic $p>0$ has multiplicity $p$ and derivative $0$, and step 2.2 rules out smoothness. The zero polynomial is excluded because it has no least nonzero homogeneous part; a zero-variable polynomial cannot meet the nonconstant hypothesis. If the hypersurface has no $k$-rational points there is no instance of this pointwise claim, and there is no interval or endpoint parameter. Steps 2.1 and 2.2 establish both iff directions. The coefficient and chart arguments make no family of choices; AC is used through [F4], [F6], [F7], [F8], and [F9], and no additional DC assumption is used. [F1, F4, F6, F7, F8, F9, F12, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, algebra] ∎
