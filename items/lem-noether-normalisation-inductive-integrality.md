## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra. Then
there exist algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in A$ such that
$A$ is integral over the polynomial subalgebra $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra $A$.

[L1] Finite type means generated by finitely many algebra elements ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] Integrality is transitive in towers of rings ([[thm-transitivity-of-integrality]]).

[L3] Over an infinite field, a triangular change can make a nonzero relation monic in the last variable ([[lem-noether-normalisation-nonzero-polynomial-made-monic]]).

[L4] Over an arbitrary field, the exponent-substitution trick isolates a unique highest $x_n$-term ([[lem-noether-normalisation-finite-field-exponent-substitution]]).

[L5] A monic relation makes the last generator integral over the subalgebra generated by the earlier ones ([[lem-noether-normalisation-one-relation-reduction]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction on the number of algebra generators.

1.1 By [L1], choose generators $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ of $A$ as a $k$-algebra. We prove the theorem by induction on $n$. [L1, given, choose]

1.2 Inductive hypothesis: assume the statement for every $k$-algebra generated by at most $n-1$ elements. [ih]

2.1 Base case $n=0$: then $A$ is the image of $k$. Because $A\ne 0$ and $k$ is a field, the structure map $k\to A$ is injective and hence identifies $A$ with $k=k[\,]$. Thus $A$ is integral over the empty polynomial algebra. [base, step 1.1, given]

2.2 If $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ are algebraically independent over $k$, then $A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ already has the required form. [step 1.1, given]

2.3 Otherwise there is a nonzero polynomial relation among $x_1,\ldots,x_n$. If $k$ is infinite, apply [L3] to make such a relation monic in the last variable after a triangular change. If $k$ is finite, apply [L4] to make such a relation monic in the last variable after the exponent substitution. In either case we obtain new generators $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ of $A$ such that $A$ is integral over $B:=k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$ by [L5]. [L3, L4, L5, step 1.1]

3.1 The algebra $B$ is generated over $k$ by $n-1$ elements, so the induction hypothesis yields algebraically independent elements $z_1,\ldots,z_d\in B$ such that $B$ is integral over $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. Then [L2] and step 2.3 show that $A$ is integral over $k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. [L2, step 1.2, step 2.3]

4.1 Step 2.2 handles the algebraically independent case, while steps 2.3 and 3.1 handle the dependent case. Therefore the theorem holds for $n$ generators, and hence for every finite-type $k$-algebra. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, discharge-induction] ∎
