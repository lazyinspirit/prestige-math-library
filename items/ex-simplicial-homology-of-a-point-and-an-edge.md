## Example

For the one-vertex complex $\{ \varnothing,\{v\}\}$, one has
$C_0 \cong \mathbb Z[v]$ and $C_n=0$ for $n \geq 1$. Hence
$H_0^{\mathrm{simp}}\cong \mathbb Z$ and $H_n^{\mathrm{simp}}=0$ for $n>0$.
The augmentation $\varepsilon([v])=1$ is an isomorphism, so
$\widetilde H_n^{\mathrm{simp}}=0$ for every $n$.

For the one-edge complex with vertices $v_0,v_1$ and edge $[v_0,v_1]$, one has
$C_1 \cong \mathbb Z[v_0,v_1]$, $C_0 \cong \mathbb Z[v_0] \oplus \mathbb Z[v_1]$,
and $\partial[v_0,v_1]=[v_1]-[v_0]$. The kernel of $\partial_1$ is $0$, so
$H_1^{\mathrm{simp}}=0$. The quotient of $C_0$ by the subgroup generated by
$[v_1]-[v_0]$ is $\mathbb Z$, so $H_0^{\mathrm{simp}}\cong \mathbb Z$. The
augmented complex is exact, hence the reduced homology also vanishes.
