## Statement

Let $A$ be a commutative ring with $1$ and let $M$ be a finitely generated
$A$-module ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).
Let $k\ge0$ and let
$$A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }A^n\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }M\longrightarrow0$$
be a presentation in which $n\ge0$ is finite and the index set $J$ is
arbitrary, possibly infinite. For $r\ge0$ let $I_r(\varphi)$ be the ideal of
$A$ generated by the $r\times r$ minors of the matrix of $\varphi$, with the
conventions
$$I_r(\varphi)=\begin{cases}A,& r\le0,\\ 0,& r>n,\end{cases}$$
and with $I_r(\varphi)=0$ also when $r$ exceeds a finite number of columns.
Then $I_{n-k}(\varphi)$ depends only on $M$ and $k$, not on the presentation;
it is written $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)$.

Moreover $\operatorname{Fitt}_k$ is compatible with base change. If $A\to B$
is a ring homomorphism, then
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M\otimes_AB)=\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)\cdot B,$$
the ideal of $B$ generated by the image, and if $f\in A$, then
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M_f)=\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)\cdot A_f,$$
so the ideals localise. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $A$; a finitely generated $A$-module $M$; an
integer $k\ge0$; a presentation $A^{(J)}\to A^n\to M\to0$ with $n$ finite.

[F1] A module is finitely generated when it is generated by a finite subset,
and $A^n$ is the free module on $n$ standard basis elements
([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[F2] Localisation of modules is exact: every short exact sequence of
$R$-modules localises to a short exact sequence of $S^{-1}R$-modules
([[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]]).

[F3] Tensoring is right exact, so it preserves cokernels and surjections
([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct comparison of two presentations by elementary
shears.

1.1 Put $F=A^n$ and $K=\ker(F\xrightarrow{\pi}M)=\operatorname{im}\varphi$, and for a submodule $N\subseteq F$ and $r\ge0$ let $J_r(N)$ be the ideal generated by the determinants of all $r\times r$ matrices whose columns are $r$ elements of $N$, with $J_r(N)=A$ for $r\le0$ and $J_r(N)=0$ for $r>n$; multilinearity of the determinant in the columns shows that the ideal generated by the $r\times r$ minors of a matrix whose columns generate $N$ equals $J_r(N)$, so $I_r(\varphi)=J_r(K)$ for every $r$ and the stated conventions match, and it suffices to prove $J_{n-k}(K)=J_{n'-k}(K')$ for the kernels of two surjections $F=A^n\twoheadrightarrow M$ and $F'=A^{n'}\twoheadrightarrow M$ from finite free modules. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Determinant calculus, three elementary facts. (a) Nestedness: $J_r(N)\subseteq J_s(N)$ for $s\le r$, by Laplace expansion of an $r\times r$ determinant along a row. (b) Cauchy--Binet: every $r\times r$ minor of a product of matrices $CA$ is an $A$-linear combination of $r\times r$ minors of $A$, obtained by expanding the determinant of the product and regrouping terms according to the $r$ rows of $A$ used; consequently $J_r(CN)=J_r(N)$ for every automorphism $C$ of $F$, by applying this inclusion to $C$ and to $C^{-1}$. (c) Block Laplace expansion: for a square submatrix of $\begin{pmatrix} M_1 & 0\\ M_2 & M_3\end{pmatrix}$ with $h$ selected lower rows, expand its determinant along those $h$ rows. Every term is a product of an $h\times h$ minor from the selected lower rows and a complementary minor from $M_1$; expanding the former further in columns separates contributions from $M_2$ and $M_3$. In particular, whenever a term uses $j$ columns from the $M_3$ block, its other columns contribute a minor of $M_1$ of size equal to the number of selected upper rows. [given, algebra]

2.1 Augmentation identity: for $N\subseteq A^n$ and $m\ge0$, with $N\oplus A^m\subseteq A^n\oplus A^m$, one has $J_r(N\oplus A^m)=J_{r-m}(N)$, and the same identity holds with the two blocks interchanged, $J_r(A^m\oplus N)=J_{r-m}(N)$. If $r\le m$, the right side is $A$ by convention and an $r\times r$ identity minor in the free block shows that the left side is $A$. If $r>m$, adjoining the $m$ standard free-block vectors to any $r-m$ vectors of $N$ gives a block identity minor, so $J_{r-m}(N)\subseteq J_r(N\oplus A^m)$. Conversely, expand any $r\times r$ minor of vectors $(v_i,c_i)\in N\oplus A^m$ along its selected free-block rows. Every resulting term is a free-block minor times a minor of the $v_i$ of size at least $r-m$, hence lies in $J_{r-m}(N)$ by nestedness in step 1.2(a); thus $J_r(N\oplus A^m)\subseteq J_{r-m}(N)$. Swapping the two blocks proves the second identity. [given, algebra, step 1.2]

2.2 Now let $p:F\twoheadrightarrow M$ and $p':F'\twoheadrightarrow M$ be two surjections from finite free modules, with kernels $K$ and $K'$ as in step 1.1; choose an $A$-linear lift $w:F'\to F$ with $p\circ w=p'$ by choosing values on the $n'$ standard basis elements of $F'$ one at a time, a finite and choice-free construction, and define $L=\{(x,y)\in F\oplus F':p(x)=p'(y)\}\subseteq F\oplus F'$, which is the kernel of the $A$-linear map $p-p'\circ\mathrm{pr}_2$ on $F\oplus F'$. [F1, given, step 1.1]

3.1 The endomorphisms $C(x,y)=(x-w(y),y)$ and $D(x,y)=(x,y-u(x))$ of $F\oplus F'$, where $u:F\to F'$ is an $A$-linear lift with $p'\circ u=p$ (which exists by choosing values on the finite standard basis of $F$), are automorphisms with inverses $(x,y)\mapsto(x+w(y),y)$ and $(x,y)\mapsto(x,y+u(x))$; for $(x,y)\in L$ one has $p(x-w(y))=p(x)-p'(y)=0$, so $C(L)\subseteq K\oplus F'$, while $p(k+w(y))=p'(y)$ for $k\in K$ shows $C(L)=K\oplus F'$, and likewise $p'(y-u(x))=p'(y)-p(x)=0$ on $L$ gives $D(L)=F\oplus K'$. [given, step 2.2]

4.1 By step 1.2(b) applied to the automorphisms $C$ and $D$ of step 3.1, $J_{n+n'-k}(L)=J_{n+n'-k}(C(L))=J_{n+n'-k}(K\oplus F')$ and $J_{n+n'-k}(L)=J_{n+n'-k}(D(L))=J_{n+n'-k}(F\oplus K')$; the augmentation identity of step 2.1, with $m=n'$ and $m=n$ respectively, gives $J_{n+n'-k}(K\oplus F')=J_{n-k}(K)$ and $J_{n+n'-k}(F\oplus K')=J_{n'-k}(K')$, whence $J_{n-k}(K)=J_{n'-k}(K')$; combined with step 1.1, this proves $I_{n-k}(\varphi)=I_{n'-k}(\psi)$ for any two presentations. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Base change: let $A\to B$ be a ring homomorphism and apply $-\otimes_AB$ to the presentation, so that by [F3] the sequence $B^{(J)}\to B^n\to M\otimes_AB\to0$ is exact, hence is a presentation of $M\otimes_AB$ whose matrix has the images of the entries of $\varphi$; its $(n-k)\times(n-k)$ minors are the images of those of $\varphi$, and the two ideals are generated by these images, giving $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M\otimes_AB)=\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)\cdot B$; for the localisation statement apply [F2] to the presentation, so that the localised sequence $A^{(J)}_f\to A^n_f\to M_f\to0$ is exact, its matrix is the localisation of the matrix of $\varphi$, and minors commute with the coefficient map, giving $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M_f)=\operatorname{Fitt}_k(M)\cdot A_f$; all steps used finitely many choices on standard bases and universal constructions only, so no choice principle is invoked. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1] ∎
