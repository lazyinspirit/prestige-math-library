## Definition

Two constructions are defined here, one for maps *into* spaces and one for maps
*out of* spaces. Every further construction on this page is an instance of one of
them.

**Initial topology.** Let $X$ be a set, let $I$ be an index set, let
$(Y_i, \mathcal{T}_i)$ be a topological space for each $i \in I$
([[def-topological-space]]), and let $f_i : X \to Y_i$ be a function for each
$i$. The **initial topology** on $X$ induced by the family $(f_i)_{i \in I}$ is

$$\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{in}} \;:=\; \big\langle\, \{\, f_i^{-1}[V] : i \in I,\ V \in \mathcal{T}_i \,\} \,\big\rangle ,$$

the topology generated by the preimages of the open sets of the $Y_i$
([[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

*This is well posed, and the obligation is discharged by the item cited.* For an
arbitrary family $\mathcal{S}$ of subsets of $X$, the family
$\langle \mathcal{S} \rangle$ is a topology on $X$, contains $\mathcal{S}$, and
is contained in every topology on $X$ containing $\mathcal{S}$
([[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]); no further verification is needed here. By
[[thm-basis-criterion]] a **basis** for $\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{in}}$ is the family
of intersections of finitely many sets $f_i^{-1}[V]$, the intersection of none
being $X$.

**Final topology.** Let $X$ be a set, let $(Z_i, \mathcal{S}_i)$ be a topological
space for each $i \in I$, and let $g_i : Z_i \to X$ be a function for each $i$.
The **final topology** on $X$ induced by the family $(g_i)_{i \in I}$ is

$$\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{fin}} \;:=\; \{\, U \subseteq X : g_i^{-1}[U] \in \mathcal{S}_i \text{ for every } i \in I \,\} .$$

*This is a topology, and the verification is carried out here rather than
assumed.* Preimage commutes with the three set operations named in the axioms:
$g_i^{-1}[\varnothing] = \varnothing$ and $g_i^{-1}[X] = Z_i$, which gives (T1);
$g_i^{-1}[\bigcup \mathcal{U}] = \bigcup \{\, g_i^{-1}[U] : U \in \mathcal{U} \,\}$
for every family $\mathcal{U} \subseteq \mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{fin}}$, which with
(T2) in $Z_i$ gives (T2); and
$g_i^{-1}[U \cap V] = g_i^{-1}[U] \cap g_i^{-1}[V]$, which with (T3) in $Z_i$
gives (T3) ([[def-topological-space]]).

**Two degenerate cases, stated because they are used.** For $I = \varnothing$ the
initial topology is $\langle \varnothing \rangle = \{\varnothing, X\}$, the
indiscrete topology, and the final topology is $\mathcal{P}(X)$, the discrete
topology, the defining condition being vacuous
([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]). For a family
consisting of a single map the definitions read the same way with the index
dropped.

**The subspace topology is the model initial topology.** Let $(X,\mathcal{T})$ be
a space, let $S \subseteq X$ and let $\iota : S \to X$ be the inclusion. Then
$\iota^{-1}[U] = U \cap S$ for every $U \subseteq X$, so the generating family for
the initial topology of the one-element family $(\iota)$ is exactly
$\mathcal{T}_S = \{\, U \cap S : U \in \mathcal{T} \,\}$
([[def-subspace-topology-top]]). That family is already a topology, so generating
adds nothing and the initial topology of $\iota$ **is** the subspace topology.
Everything [[def-subspace-topology-top]] proves about it is therefore quoted here
and not reproved: its closed sets are the traces of the closed sets, a basis of
$X$ traces to a basis of $S$, the inclusion is continuous
([[def-continuous-map-top]], [[thm-continuity-characterisations-top]]),
$\mathcal{T}_S$ is the coarsest topology making $\iota$ continuous, and a map
$g : Z \to S$ is continuous if and only if $\iota \circ g$ is.

**Terminology.** A family $(f_i)$ as above is the *defining family* of the
initial topology, and likewise for the final one; both topologies depend on the
whole family and not on any one member. The characteristic properties that make
these two constructions worth naming are the subject of the next item.

## Remarks

- **The direction of the arrows is what distinguishes the two.** An initial
  topology is put on the *source* of its defining maps and is the coarsest making
  them continuous; a final topology is put on the *target* and is the finest
  making them continuous. Both facts are proved in the next item rather than
  built into the definitions above.

- **Why the initial topology is generated and the final one is not.** The
  preimages $f_i^{-1}[V]$ need not be closed under unions or finite
  intersections, so a topology has to be generated from them; the final family is
  closed under both operations already, because preimage commutes with both, so
  it is a topology as it stands. The asymmetry is a fact about preimages and not
  a choice of presentation.

- **Both constructions are determined by a universal property, so they are unique
  once that property is stated.** That is the content of the next item, and it is
  what lets the product, the coproduct and the quotient be treated as three
  instances of two constructions rather than as three separate theories.
