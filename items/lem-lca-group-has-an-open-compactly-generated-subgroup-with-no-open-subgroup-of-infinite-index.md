## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) and Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $G$ be a locally compact Hausdorff abelian topological group ([[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-hausdorff-space]], [[def-topological-group]]). Then $G$ contains an open (hence closed) subgroup $H$ which is compactly generated and contains no open subgroup of infinite index. For instance, if $G$ is discrete one may take $H=\{0\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A locally compact Hausdorff abelian group $G$, and the Axiom of Choice together with Dependent Choice.

[F1] **Classification of compactly generated abelian groups.** Every compactly generated locally compact Hausdorff abelian group is isomorphic as a topological group to $\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb Z^n\times K$ for some $m,n\ge0$ and some compact group $K$. This is Hewitt-Ross, *Abstract Harmonic Analysis I*, Theorem 9.8, quoted and attributed in the Ross article recorded in the sources (Theorem 3 proof, p. 3); the primary volume is not available here, so the classification is used as a cited theorem and no minimality claim about its axiom basis is made beyond the declared AC and DC.

[F2] An open subgroup is closed because its complement is a union of open cosets; a closed subgroup of an LCA group is LCA by [[lem-local-compact-subgroups-of-hausdorff-groups-are-closed]]. A compactly generated group is one that contains a compact set generating it as a group; the subgroup $\langle C\rangle$ generated by a symmetric set $C$ containing the identity is the union of the sets $C^k$ of sums of $k$ elements of $C$, and it is open as soon as $C$ is a neighbourhood of the identity. A locally compact space has compact neighbourhoods, and sums and finite products of compact sets are compact ([[thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]], [[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]]); closed Euclidean balls are compact ([[thm-heine-borel-rn]]). ([[def-generated-subgroup]], [[def-compact-space]], [[def-neighbourhood-top]], [[def-locally-compact-space]], [[lem-a-locally-compact-hausdorff-space-has-a-base-of-open-sets-with-compact-closure]])

[F3] $\mathbb Z$ and $\mathbb R^m$ carry their standard topologies, $\mathbb Z$ being discrete and $\mathbb R^m$ connected, since the real line is order-convex and finite products of connected spaces are connected ([[thm-connected-subsets-of-r-are-intervals]], [[thm-product-of-connected-spaces]]); the quotient $G/L$ of a topological group by a subgroup $L$ carries the quotient topology, which makes the quotient map continuous, and for an open subgroup $L$ every coset is open (translations are homeomorphisms), so $G/L$ is discrete. ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-connected-space]], [[def-quotient-group]], [[def-quotient-topology]], [[def-subgroup]], [[def-homeomorphism-and-open-maps]], [[lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]])

[F4] Continuous images of compact sets are compact, and the continuous image of a connected space is connected ([[thm-continuous-image-of-a-connected-space]]). The discrete topology on a finite set is compact while the discrete topology on an infinite set is not. A group homomorphism out of a product that is independent of one factor descends to the quotient by the kernel of that factor ([[thm-quotient-universal-property]], [[def-product-topology]]). ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[def-compact-space]], [[def-connected-space]])

## Proof

1.1 Choose a compact symmetric neighbourhood $C$ of the identity $e$ of $G$ and put $H_0:=\langle C\rangle=\bigcup_{k\ge1}C^k$. Then $H_0$ is an open subgroup of $G$ and is generated by the compact set $C$, so $H_0$ is compactly generated. [F2]

2.1 By [F1] there are $m,n\ge0$, a compact group $K$ and a topological isomorphism $\varphi:H_0\to\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb Z^n\times K$. Let $H:=\varphi^{-1}\big(\mathbb R^m\times\{0\}\times K\big)$. Since $\{0\}$ is open in the discrete group $\mathbb Z^n$, the set $\mathbb R^m\times\{0\}\times K$ is open in $\mathbb R^m\times\mathbb Z^n\times K$, so $H$ is an open subgroup of $H_0$ and hence open in $G$; it is closed as well, because its complement in $G$ is the union of the remaining cosets of $H$, each of which is open by [F3]. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 $H$ is compactly generated. Indeed $H$ is isomorphic under $\varphi$ to $\mathbb R^m\times\{0\}\times K$, which is homeomorphic to $\mathbb R^m\times K$; a closed ball of large radius in $\mathbb R^m$ is compact and generates $\mathbb R^m$ as a group, because every $v\in\mathbb R^m$ is an integer multiple of a vector of sufficiently small norm, and $K$ generates itself, so the compact product of that ball with $K$ generates $\mathbb R^m\times K$. [F2, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $L\le H$ be an open subgroup of $H$. Then $H/L$ is discrete, because every coset of the open subgroup $L$ is open. The composite $\mathbb R^m\to H\to H/L$ of the inclusion of the connected factor (under the identification of step 2.1) with the quotient map is continuous, so its image is connected in the discrete space $H/L$, hence a single point; therefore $\mathbb R^m\subseteq L$. It follows that every coset of $L$ meets $\{0\}\times K$, so the quotient map restricts to a continuous surjection $K\to H/L$, and $H/L$ is compact as a continuous image of the compact group $K$; being compact and discrete it is finite. Hence every open subgroup $L$ of $H$ has finite index in $H$. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The subgroup $H$ of steps 1.1-4.1 is open in $G$, compactly generated, and contains no open subgroup of infinite index. If $G$ is discrete, then $H=\{0\}$ is open, compactly generated as the subgroup generated by the compact set $\{0\}$, and its only subgroup is itself, of index $1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
