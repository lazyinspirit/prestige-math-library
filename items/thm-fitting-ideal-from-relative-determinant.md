## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative integral domain with fraction field $K$, let $n\ge1$, and let $A\in M_n(R)$ have nonzero determinant. Regard $C^\bullet=(R^n\xrightarrow{A}R^n)$ as a cochain complex in degrees $-1,0$. Its scalar extension to $K$ is acyclic. Under the standard basis identification of its determinant line with $R$, the canonical acyclicity trivialization sends $1$ to $(\det A)^{-1}\in K$; hence the inverse fractional ideal of its determinant lattice is $(\det A)R$. The cokernel $M=\operatorname{coker}(A)$ is a finitely presented torsion module and $\operatorname{Fit}_0(M)=(\det A)R$. Thus the relative determinant ideal equals the zeroth Fitting ideal.


Let $\Lambda$ be a commutative ring, let $\Lambda\hookrightarrow Q$ be an injective homomorphism to a finite product of fields, and let $x\in K_0(\Lambda,Q)$ belong to the image of the boundary $\partial:K_1(Q)\to K_0(\Lambda,Q)$. Choose $y\in K_1(Q)$ with $\partial(y)=x$. The principal $\Lambda$-submodule $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(x)=\det(y)\Lambda\subset Q$ is independent of the choice of $y$. For boundary classes $x,x'$, one has $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(x+x')=\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(x)\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(x')$ and $\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(-x)=\operatorname{Fitt}_{\Lambda}(x)^{-1}$. This applies in particular to the relative Euler class of a torsion perfect complex whenever that class is a boundary. Over a commutative local noetherian domain $\Lambda$ with fraction field $Q$, for a finite torsion module $M$ of projective dimension at most one, its class from a finite projective presentation is a boundary and this construction gives its ordinary zeroth Fitting ideal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** In the square case, a commutative domain $R$, its fraction field $K$, and $A\in M_n(R)$ with $\det A\ne0$. For the boundary-class case, an injective map $\Lambda\hookrightarrow Q$ to a product of fields, a relative boundary class $x$, and a lift $y\in K_1(Q)$. In the module case, $\Lambda$ is a local noetherian domain and $M$ is finite torsion of projective dimension at most one.

[F1] [[def-determinant-line-of-a-perfect-complex]]: for a bounded finite-projective cochain complex, the determinant line is the alternating tensor product of the top exterior powers of its terms, and a complex acyclic over $K$ has a canonical determinant trivialization.

[F2] [[def-fitting-ideal-of-a-finitely-presented-module]]: for a finite presentation $R^m\xrightarrow{B}R^n\to M\to0$, $\operatorname{Fit}_0(M)$ is generated by all $n\times n$ minors of $B$ and is independent of presentation.

[F3] [[def-field-of-fractions]]: every nonzero element of $R$ is invertible in $K$.

[F4] [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]]: a square matrix over a commutative ring is invertible if and only if its determinant is a unit.

[F5] [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]: the determinant is given by the alternating sum over permutations, so for a $1\times1$ matrix $[a]$ it equals $a$.

[F6] [[thm-relative-k-theory-localization-sequence]] gives exactness at K1 of the target.

[F7] [[def-relative-algebraic-k0-group]] gives the presentation class of a torsion module.

[F8] [[def-projective-module]] and [[lem-finite-projective-modules-over-a-domain-have-constant-rank]] give the choice-free local-freeness argument: a finite projective over a local ring is free.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The determinant $\det A$ is nonzero in $K$, hence is a unit there by [F3]. Applying [F4] over $K$ shows that $A_K$ is invertible, so the two-term complex is acyclic. In degrees $-1,0$, the determinant line is $\det_R(R^n)\otimes\det_R(R^n)^\vee\cong R$. The acyclicity trivialization is induced by $A_K:R^n_K\to R^n_K$; with the fixed standard top wedge $e$, the relation $\det(A_K)e=(\det A)e$ sends $e\otimes e^\vee$ to $(\det A)^{-1}$. Hence the image lattice in $K$ is $(\det A)^{-1}R$, whose inverse fractional ideal is $(\det A)R$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 If $y,y'$ have boundary $x$, their difference in additive K1 notation is the image of some $z\in K_1(\Lambda)$, by exactness at $K_1(Q)$. Its determinant lies in $\Lambda^\times$, since an invertible matrix over $\Lambda$ has unit determinant. Therefore $\det(y')\Lambda=\det(y)\det(z)\Lambda=\det(y)\Lambda$. Multiplication and inversion of representatives give the formulas for sums and negatives. Since $\det(y)$ is a unit in $Q$, the resulting principal module is invertible as a fractional ideal, with the displayed inverse. [F6, given, algebra]


2.1 The cokernel has the finite presentation $R^n\xrightarrow{A}R^n\twoheadrightarrow M\to0$. In this square presentation the only $n\times n$ minor is $\det A$, so [F2] gives $\operatorname{Fit}_0(M)=(\det A)R$. Comparing with step 1.1 proves the assertion. [F2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 For the module clause, choose a finite free cover $\Lambda^n\to M$. Its kernel $P$ is finite projective: finite generation follows from noetherianity, and projectivity follows directly from a length-one projective resolution $0\to L\to E\to M\to0$. Indeed the fiber product $H=\Lambda^n\times_M E$ has split projections giving $H\cong P\oplus E$ and $H\cong L\oplus\Lambda^n$; the latter is projective, so its direct summand $P$ is projective. Over the local ring it is finite free. Tensoring with the fraction field makes the injection $P\to\Lambda^n$ an isomorphism, so $P$ has rank $n$ and is isomorphic to $\Lambda^n$. In these bases the presentation is $0\to\Lambda^n\xrightarrow{A}\Lambda^n\to M\to0$, with $\det A\ne0$. Its relative class is $[\Lambda^n,A,\Lambda^n]=\partial[A]$, and step 1.2 assigns it $(\det A)\Lambda$. The defining maximal-minor formula for the zeroth Fitting ideal gives exactly $(\det A)\Lambda$. [F2, F7, F8, step 1.2, construct, algebra] ∎
