## Statement

Let $(S,m)$ be a Coxeter matrix with $S$ finite, $W$ its presented group ([[def-group-presentation]], [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), and let $\Sigma$ be the Davis realization of [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] with the CW structure of [[lem-cg-davis-cellulation-cw-structure-and-cayley-skeleta]]. Then $\Sigma$ is simply connected ([[def-simply-connected]], [[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]]). More precisely, for every vertex $v\in\Sigma^0=W$, inclusion $j\colon\Sigma^2\hookrightarrow\Sigma$ induces an isomorphism $j_*\colon\pi_1(\Sigma^2,v)\to\pi_1(\Sigma,v)$, these groups are trivial, and in particular $\pi_1(\Sigma^2,1)$ is trivial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, its presented group $W$, and the Davis realization $\Sigma$ with its CW structure and skeleta.

[F1] The CW structure has $\Sigma^0=W$, $\Sigma^1$ the undirected $S$-labelled Cayley graph, and $\Sigma^2$ the Cayley $2$-complex whose $2$-cells are the cosets $wW_{\{s,t\}}$ for distinct $s,t$ with finite $m(s,t)$, each a $2m(s,t)$-gon with the Coxeter-relator boundary ([[lem-cg-davis-cellulation-cw-structure-and-cayley-skeleta]] (2)-(3)).

[F2] The skeleta of a CW complex are subcomplexes, so $(\Sigma,\Sigma^0)$ and $(\Sigma,\Sigma^2)$ are CW pairs and $\Sigma^2$ is itself a CW complex ([[def-cw-complex-with-closure-finiteness-and-weak-topology]], [[def-skeleta-cw-subcomplex-and-relative-cw-complex]], [[lem-cg-davis-cellulation-cw-structure-and-cayley-skeleta]] (2)-(3)).

[F3] For CW pairs $(X,A)$ and $(Y,B)$, if $X\setminus A$ has finitely many cells and a map of pairs is cellular on $A$, it is homotopic rel $A$ through maps of pairs to a cellular map; for a finite relative source this clause uses no Choice ([[thm-cellular-approximation-for-maps-of-cw-pairs]]).

[F4] The Coxeter presentation is $W=F(S)/N$, with $N$ the normal closure of $R=\{s^2:s\in S\}\cup\{(st)^{m(s,t)}:s\ne t,\ m(s,t)<\infty\}$; a word represents $1$ in $W$ iff it lies in $N$, and every element of $N$ is a finite product of conjugates of relators and their inverses ([[def-group-presentation]], [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[prop-equality-of-words-in-a-presentation]], [[prop-normal-closure-is-products-of-conjugates]]).

[F5] In $F(S)$, words use the alphabet $S\cup S^{-1}$; equality is generated by insertion and deletion of adjacent inverse pairs, and each element has a reduced-word representative ([[def-free-group]], [[def-alphabet-words-and-reduction]], [[thm-reduced-words-form-the-free-group]]).

[F6] Based loop classes form a group with the constant loop as identity and reverse paths as inverses; a continuous pointed map induces a homomorphism on $\pi_1$; a space is simply connected when it is nonempty, path-connected, and its fundamental groups are trivial ([[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]], [[thm-fundamental-group-laws]], [[def-induced-homomorphism-on-fundamental-groups]], [[def-simply-connected]], [[def-path-connected]]).

[F7] Each spherical-coset cell is a convex polytope, and for $q=wW_T$ its face indexed by $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ is a vertex; $S$ generates $W$, so the Cayley graph on $W$ is connected ([[thm-cg-davis-complex-cell-incidence-and-stabilizers]] (1)-(2), [[def-cg-spherical-nerve-coset-poset-and-davis-realization]] (1)-(2), [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F8] For each $s\in S$, the one-generator parabolic is $W_{\{s\}}=\{1,s\}$: its restricted presentation reduces every word to $1$ or $s$, and the map to the two-element group separates them ([[lem-cg-davis-cellulation-cw-structure-and-cayley-skeleta]] Fact [F10]).

[F9] For a finite simplicial source and subcomplexes $A,B$, a map of pairs has a simplicial approximation after sufficiently many barycentric subdivisions and a homotopy through maps of pairs; the proof uses only finite choice ([[thm-finite-simplicial-approximation-for-maps-of-pairs]]). If $B$ is the singleton basepoint, this homotopy fixes it.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 A loop in either $X=\Sigma$ or $X=\Sigma^2$ based at a vertex $v$ is homotopic rel $v$ in $X$ to an edge loop. Give $S^1$ the CW structure with one $0$-cell $*$ and one $1$-cell, and view the loop as a map of pairs $(S^1,*)\to(X,X^0)$. It is cellular on the $0$-skeleton because it sends $*$ to $v\in X^0=\Sigma^0$; the relative source $S^1\setminus\{*\}$ is one cell, so [F3] gives a cellular approximation rel $*$, whose image lies in $X^1=\Sigma^1$. [given, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Suppose a loop $\beta$ in $\Sigma^2$ based at a vertex $v$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$, so it has a based homotopy $H\colon I^2\to\Sigma$ with $H(s,0)=\beta(s)$, $H(s,1)=v$, and $H(0,t)=H(1,t)=v$. Use [step 1.1] with $X=\Sigma^2$ to homotope $\beta$ rel endpoints to an edge loop $\beta'$ by $F\colon I^2\to\Sigma^2$, where $F(s,0)=\beta(s)$ and $F(s,1)=\beta'(s)$. Define $H'(s,t)=F(s,1-2t)$ for $0\le t\le\frac12$ and $H'(s,t)=H(s,2t-1)$ for $\frac12\le t\le1$; the two formulas agree at $t=\frac12$, and $H'$ is a based null-homotopy of $\beta'$. Its boundary is cellular for $(I^2,\partial I^2)\to(\Sigma,\Sigma^2)$: the bottom edge maps into $\Sigma^1$, and the other boundary edges and vertices map to $v\in\Sigma^0$. The relative source has one open $2$-cell, so [F3] gives a cellular approximation rel boundary; because the source has dimension $2$, its image lies in $\Sigma^2$. Thus $\beta'$ and then $\beta$ are null-homotopic in $\Sigma^2$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3]

2.2 Every continuous loop in $\Sigma^1$ based at $1$ is based-homotopic to a finite edge walk. Barycentrically subdivide the Cayley graph to an abstract simplicial graph: every original edge is split at its midpoint, so distinct original edges give distinct simplicial edges. Triangulate the circle with the basepoint as a vertex, and apply [F9] with the singleton basepoint as each distinguished subcomplex. The resulting map on a finite subdivided circle is a finite walk in the subdivided graph, with a homotopy fixing the basepoint. Delete stationary traversals and immediate reversals; at each midpoint the two incident half-edges either reverse or join to one full original edge, so compressing gives a finite based edge walk in the original graph. We now show that each such walk is null-homotopic in $\Sigma^2$. Orient each traversal and record $s$ or $s^{-1}$ according to its direction, obtaining a word $w\in F(S)$ whose image in $W$ is $1$ by [F1]. By [F4], $w$ is a finite product $g_1r_1^{\epsilon_1}g_1^{-1}\cdots g_kr_k^{\epsilon_k}g_k^{-1}$ in $F(S)$, with $r_i\in R$ and $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$; $k=0$ is allowed. The path for this product is a concatenation of conjugate relator loops, and [F5] turns equality in $F(S)$ into finitely many insertions or deletions of adjacent inverse letters. Since each Coxeter generator satisfies $s=s^{-1}$ in $W$, each such pair is an immediate backtrack in the undirected Cayley graph; [F8] ensures the $s$-edge has distinct endpoints. A relator $s^2$ traverses that edge out and back, while a relator $(st)^{m(s,t)}$ with distinct $s,t$ and finite label is the boundary of the corresponding $2m(s,t)$-gon in $\Sigma^2$ by [F1]; inverse relators reverse these loops. Thus every conjugate relator loop contracts in $\Sigma^2$ (conjugation preserves the identity class by [F6]), and the finite concatenation contracts by the group law [F6]. If $S=\emptyset$, then $W=1$ and $\Sigma^1$ is one vertex, so the only edge loop is constant; the same argument also allows the empty relator product $k=0$. If no finite rank-two label occurs, there are no polygon relators and the only relator loops are involution backtracks. Finally [step 1.1] reduces every loop in $\Sigma^2$ at $1$ to an edge loop, proving $\pi_1(\Sigma^2,1)=1$. [step 1.1, F1, F4, F5, F6, F8, F9]

3.1 For every vertex $v$, inclusion $j\colon\Sigma^2\hookrightarrow\Sigma$ induces an isomorphism $j_*\colon\pi_1(\Sigma^2,v)\to\pi_1(\Sigma,v)$. Every class represented by a loop in $\Sigma$ has an edge-loop representative by [step 1.1], hence lies in the image. If a loop in $\Sigma^2$ maps to the identity, [step 2.1] makes it null-homotopic in $\Sigma^2$, so $j_*$ is injective. Its induced homomorphism is defined by [F6]. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F6]

3.2 The graph $\Sigma^1$ is connected because its vertices are $W$ and $S$ generates $W$ [F1, F7]. Every closed cell is a convex polytope containing its vertex $wW_\emptyset=\{w\}$ as a face [F7], and each point of $\Sigma$ lies in such a cell; a segment in that cell joins the point to a vertex of $\Sigma^1$. Hence $\Sigma$ is path-connected. For an arbitrary basepoint $x$, choose a path $p$ from $x$ to $1$ and a loop $\alpha$ at $x$. The loop $\bar p*\alpha*p$ at $1$ is homotopic rel basepoint to an edge loop by [step 1.1], then contracts by [step 2.2]. Prepending $p$ and appending $\bar p$ to that based null-homotopy gives a homotopy rel $x$ from $(p*\bar p)*\alpha*(p*\bar p)$ to $p*\bar p$, after reparameterizing concatenations. The loop $p*\bar p$ contracts rel $x$ by the homotopy $G(u,t)=p(2u(1-t))$ for $u\le\frac12$ and $G(u,t)=p(2(1-u)(1-t))$ for $u\ge\frac12$: the formulas agree at $u=\frac12$, are continuous, and keep both endpoints at $x$. Hence $(p*\bar p)*\alpha*(p*\bar p)$ is null-homotopic and is also homotopic rel $x$ to $\alpha$ by contracting its two outer copies of $p*\bar p$. Thus $\alpha$ is null-homotopic, and every fundamental group of $\Sigma$ is trivial. [step 1.1, step 2.2, F1, F6, F7]

4.1 The CW complex $\Sigma$ is nonempty, path-connected and has trivial fundamental groups by [step 3.2], so it is simply connected; [step 3.1] then gives triviality of $\pi_1(\Sigma^2,v)$ and the asserted inclusion isomorphism for every vertex $v$, while [step 2.2] gives the explicit basepoint case $\pi_1(\Sigma^2,1)=1$. The cellular approximations use finite relative sources and [F3] is explicitly choice-free in that case; the normal-closure product and all word reductions are finite, and the path $p$ in [step 3.2] is chosen separately for one basepoint. No Axiom of Choice is used. [step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, F3, given] ∎
