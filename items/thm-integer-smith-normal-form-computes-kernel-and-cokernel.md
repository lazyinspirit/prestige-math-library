## Statement

For every integer matrix $A\in\mathbb Z^{r\times m}$, the Smith-normal-form algorithm returns unimodular matrices $U\in\operatorname{GL}_r(\mathbb Z)$ and $Q\in\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb Z)$ such that $D=UAQ$ is diagonal with nonzero entries $d_1,\ldots,d_s>0$, $d_i\mid d_{i+1}$, and all other entries zero. The last $m-s$ columns of $Q$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\ker(A:\mathbb Z^m\to\mathbb Z^r)$, and
$$\operatorname{coker}(A)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^s\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z^{r-s}.$$

The zero matrix and rectangular matrices are included; an empty direct sum is the zero group.

## Statement references

[Stein, Proposition 2.1.5 and proof, pp. 17–19](https://wstein.org/books/ant/ant.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The integer matrix and algorithm in the Statement.

[F1] The algorithm uses integer row and column swaps, sign changes, and integer-multiple additions; each is invertible over $\mathbb Z$ ([[alg-integer-smith-normal-form]]).

[F2] Integer division and Euclidean remainder descent terminate ([[thm-division-algorithm-in-z]], [[thm-euclidean-algorithm]]).

## Proof



1.1 Each operation in [F1] is multiplication on the left or right by an integer matrix whose inverse is another integer elementary-operation matrix. Thus every intermediate matrix has the same kernel and cokernel up to the accumulated unimodular coordinate changes. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 At a pivot, Euclidean division clears entries in its row and column. A nonzero remainder becomes a strictly smaller positive pivot. If the pivot does not divide the remaining block, adding a column containing a nondivisible entry to the pivot column forces another Euclidean remainder and again strictly decreases the pivot. By [F2] this can happen only finitely often. [F2, F1, algebra]

2.1 Once a pivot divides every entry in the remaining block, recursive operations within that block preserve divisibility by the fixed pivot. Induction on the smaller row and column dimensions therefore produces a diagonal matrix with positive nonzero entries, each dividing the next; zero active blocks terminate immediately. This proves that the algorithm terminates with the stated form, including zero and rectangular cases. [step 1.2, F1, induction]

3.1 Since $D=UAQ$, both transformations are isomorphisms of the domain and codomain integer lattices. For $x=Qy$, $Ax=0$ exactly when $Dy=0$. The diagonal equations force $y_1=\cdots=y_s=0$ and leave $y_{s+1},\ldots,y_m$ arbitrary integers, so the corresponding last columns of $Q$ form a basis of $\ker A$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The same codomain transformation identifies $\operatorname{coker}A$ with $\operatorname{coker}D$. The diagonal image is generated by $d_i e_i$ for $1\le i\le s$, while the remaining $r-s$ codomain coordinates are free. The quotient is therefore the displayed direct sum. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
