## Example

Use the standard rank-one datum $P=\mathbb Z\omega$, $P^\vee=\mathbb Zh$, $\alpha=2\omega$, $\langle\omega,h\rangle=1$ and $d_1=1$. Thus $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is generated by $E,F,K^{\pm1}$ with $q_1=q$. For $m\in\mathbb Z$, put $\lambda=m\omega$ and write $[t]=(q^t-q^{-t})/(q-q^{-1})$.

The Verma module $M(m)$ has ordinary-power basis $u_t=F^tu_0$, $t\ge0$, where $u_0=v_\lambda$. Its actions are
$$Ku_t=q^{m-2t}u_t,\qquad Fu_t=u_{t+1},\qquad Eu_t=[t][m-t+1]u_{t-1}\quad(t\ge1),\qquad Eu_0=0.$$
The divided-power basis $v_t=F^{(t)}u_0=u_t/[t]!$ instead satisfies
$$Kv_t=q^{m-2t}v_t,\qquad Fv_t=[t+1]v_{t+1},\qquad Ev_t=[m-t+1]v_{t-1}\quad(t\ge1),\qquad Ev_0=0.$$

(a) For $m\ge0$, the submodule generated by $F^{m+1}u_0$ is the tail spanned by $u_t$, $t\ge m+1$. Its quotient $L(m)$ is simple of dimension $m+1$, with divided-power basis $v_0,\ldots,v_m$ and the second set of actions, with $v_{-1}=v_{m+1}=0$. For $m=0$ it is trivial.

(b) For $m=-1$, the raising coefficients in the divided-power basis are $[-t]=-[t]\ne0$ for every $t\ge1$. Hence $M(-1)=L(-1)$ is irreducible, infinite-dimensional and not integrable. Its highest weight is integral and is not dominant.

(c) For every $m<0$, $m-t+1\ne0$ at all $t\ge1$, so the same conclusions hold. Thus dominance in [[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]] is essential.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-one Cartan datum, the Verma module $M(m)=U_q^-v_0$ with $U_q^-=\mathbb Q(q)[F]$, and the generators $E,F,K^{\pm1}$.

[F1] $M(\lambda)$ is free of rank one over $U_q^-$ with basis $u_t=F^tu_0$, has weights $\lambda-Q_+=\{\lambda-t\alpha_1\}=\{m-2t:t\ge0\}$, and satisfies $Kv_0=q^mv_0$, $Ev_0=0$, $KF=q^{-2}FK$ ([[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]], [[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]).

[F2] $EF^t=F^tE+F^{t-1}\frac{1}{q-q^{-1}}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(q^{-2s}K-q^{2s}K^{-1})$ for $t\ge1$, and $[m-t+1]=\frac{q^{m-t+1}-q^{-m+t-1}}{q-q^{-1}}$ ([[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]] (ii), [[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] $[j]=0$ for an integer $j$ if and only if $j=0$, and $[j]\ne0$ otherwise; more precisely $[-t]=-[t]$ for $t\ge1$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F4] For $\lambda\in P^+$ the Serre quotient $M(\lambda)/\sum_iU_q(\mathfrak g)F_i^{\lambda(h_i)+1}v_\lambda$ is the simple module $L(\lambda)$ and is universal among integrable cyclic highest weight modules of weight $\lambda$ ([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** read the rank-one Verma string from the commutation formula, split off the finite string when the highest weight is nonnegative, and prove irreducibility in the negative cases by the largest-index raising argument.

1.1 Basis and action. By [F1], the ordinary-power vectors $u_t=F^tu_0$ are a basis, with $Ku_t=q^{m-2t}u_t$ and $Fu_t=u_{t+1}$. Evaluating [F2] on $u_0$ gives $Eu_t=\bigl(\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(q^{m-2s}-q^{-m+2s})/(q-q^{-1})\bigr)u_{t-1}$. The geometric sums satisfy $\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q^{-2s}=q^{-(t-1)}[t]$ and $\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q^{2s}=q^{t-1}[t]$, so $Eu_t=[t][m-t+1]u_{t-1}$. Dividing by $[t]!$ gives the displayed actions on $v_t=F^{(t)}u_0$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The dominant quotient. For $m\ge0$, $Eu_{m+1}=0$ by step 1.1. The tail spanned by $u_t$, $t\ge m+1$, is stable under $E,F,K^{\pm1}$ by those formulas, and it is exactly the submodule generated by $u_{m+1}$ because repeated $F$ generates every tail vector. Therefore its quotient has basis $v_0,\ldots,v_m$ and the stated truncated divided-power actions. For a nonzero vector $x=\sum_{t=0}^T c_tv_t$ with largest occupied index $T\le m$, $E^Tx=c_T\prod_{j=1}^T[m-j+1]v_0\ne0$: lower-index terms are killed. Any submodule containing $x$ contains $v_0$ and then every $v_t$, so the quotient is simple. The empty product covers $T=0$ and $m=0$ gives the trivial module. [step 1.1, F1, F3, F4, algebra]

2.2 The boundary $m=-1$. Here $Ev_t=-[t]v_{t-1}$ for $t\ge1$. For a nonzero finite sum $x=\sum_{t=0}^T c_tv_t$ with $c_T\ne0$, $E^Tx=c_T\prod_{j=1}^T(-[j])v_0\ne0$, including the empty product when $T=0$. A submodule containing $x$ therefore contains $v_0$ and every $v_t$; $M(-1)$ is simple. It is infinite-dimensional by its basis, and $F^tu_0=u_t\ne0$ for all $t$, so it is not integrable. The label $-1$ is integral and is not dominant. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra]

3.1 For $m<0$, all scalars $[m-j+1]$, $j\ge1$, are nonzero. The same largest-index argument gives $E^Tx=c_T\prod_{j=1}^T[m-j+1]v_0\ne0$ for each nonzero finite sum. Thus $M(m)=L(m)$ is simple, infinite-dimensional and non-integrable, with integral non-dominant highest weight. The explicit dominant strings in step 2.1 and these negative cases prove all stated conclusions. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, F1, F3, F4, algebra] ∎
