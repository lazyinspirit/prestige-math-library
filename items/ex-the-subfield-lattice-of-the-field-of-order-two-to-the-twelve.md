## Example

Let $E$ be a field with $\mathbb F_2$ as a subfield and $[E:\mathbb F_2]=12$, so
that $|E|=2^{12}$. Its intermediate fields over $\mathbb F_2$ are exactly

$$\mathbb F_2,\quad \mathbb F_{2^{2}},\quad \mathbb F_{2^{3}},\quad \mathbb F_{2^{4}},\quad \mathbb F_{2^{6}},\quad \mathbb F_{2^{12}}=E,$$

one for each of the six positive divisors $1,2,3,4,6,12$ of twelve, with
$\mathbb F_{2^{d}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{2^{e}}$ exactly when $d$ divides $e$.
Neither of $\mathbb F_{2^{4}}$ and $\mathbb F_{2^{6}}$ contains the other, and

$$\mathbb F_{2^{4}}\cap\mathbb F_{2^{6}}=\mathbb F_{2^{2}},\qquad \mathbb F_{2^{4}}\,\mathbb F_{2^{6}}=\mathbb F_{2^{12}} .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $E$ with subfield $\mathbb F_2$ and $[E:\mathbb F_2]=12$ ([[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]); divisibility as in [[def-divides-in-z]], with $\gcd$ and $\operatorname{lcm}$ as in [[def-common-divisor-and-gcd]] and [[def-lcm]].

[L1] $E/\mathbb F_2$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by $x\mapsto x^{2}$ ([[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]]), and $|E|=2^{12}$ ([[lem-order-of-the-relative-frobenius]], [[def-finite-field-and-its-order]]).

[L2] The intermediate fields of $\mathbb F_{q^{n}}/\mathbb F_q$ are exactly the $\mathbb F_{q^{d}}=\{x:x^{q^{d}}=x\}$ for the positive divisors $d$ of $n$, one for each divisor, with $[\mathbb F_{q^{d}}:\mathbb F_q]=d$ and $\mathbb F_{q^{d}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{q^{e}}$ if and only if $d\mid e$ ([[thm-intermediate-fields-of-an-extension-of-finite-fields]]).

[L3] A field of order $p^{m}$ has, for each positive divisor $e$ of $m$, exactly one subfield of order $p^{e}$, namely $\{a:a^{p^{e}}=a\}$, and these are all of its subfields ([[thm-subfield-lattice-of-a-finite-field]]).

[L4] Every common divisor of two integers divides their greatest common divisor, and their least common multiple divides every common multiple ([[cor-common-divisor-divides-gcd]], [[thm-gcd-lcm-product]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The positive divisors of twelve are $1,2,3,4,6,12$, six in all, since a positive divisor $d$ of $12$ satisfies $d\le12$ and direct inspection of $1,\dots,12$ leaves exactly these. [given, algebra]

2.1 By [L1] and [L2] the intermediate fields of $E/\mathbb F_2$ are the $\mathbb F_{2^{d}}$ for those six $d$, one for each, with $\mathbb F_{2^{d}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{2^{e}}$ exactly when $d\mid e$. [step 1.1, L1, L2]

3.1 Neither $4\mid6$ nor $6\mid4$, so by step 2.1 neither of $\mathbb F_{2^{4}}$ and $\mathbb F_{2^{6}}$ contains the other. [step 2.1, given]

4.1 Their intersection is an intermediate field of $E/\mathbb F_2$, being a subfield of $E$ containing $\mathbb F_2$, so it is $\mathbb F_{2^{c}}$ for a unique divisor $c$ of $12$ by step 2.1; from $\mathbb F_{2^{c}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{2^{4}}$ and $\mathbb F_{2^{c}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{2^{6}}$ one gets $c\mid4$ and $c\mid6$, so [L4] gives $c\mid\gcd(4,6)=2$; and $2\mid4$ and $2\mid6$ put $\mathbb F_{2^{2}}$ inside both, so $2\mid c$. Hence $c=2$ and the intersection is $\mathbb F_{2^{2}}$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L4]

5.1 Their compositum is likewise an intermediate field $\mathbb F_{2^{c'}}$, and it contains both, so $4\mid c'$ and $6\mid c'$ by step 2.1. Thus [L4] gives $\operatorname{lcm}(4,6)=12\mid c'$; since $c'\mid12$, $c'=12$ and the compositum is $E=\mathbb F_{2^{12}}$. [step 2.1, step 4.1, L4]

6.1 The same six fields are what [L3] produces for $E$, whose order is $2^{12}$: its subfields are the $\{a:a^{2^{e}}=a\}$ for the divisors $e$ of $12$, and these are the sets named in [L2]. So the Galois indexing and the elementary one agree here. [step 2.1, step 5.1, L2, L3] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where the two descriptions coincide.** Because the base field is the prime field, the divisors of $[E:\mathbb F_2]$ and the divisors of $\log_2|E|$ are the same list; over a larger base field the two indexings differ by the factor $[\mathbb F_q:\mathbb F_p]$, and [[rem-the-two-descriptions-of-the-subfield-lattice-of-a-finite-field]] records the translation.
