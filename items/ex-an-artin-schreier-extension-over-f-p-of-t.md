## Example

Let $F=\mathbb F_p(t)$. Then the polynomial

$$x^p-x-t$$

is irreducible over $F$, and for any root $\alpha$ the extension $F(\alpha)/F$
is cyclic of degree $p$. Its full root set is

$$\alpha,\ \alpha+1,\ \dots,\ \alpha+(p-1).$$

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The rational function field $F=\mathbb F_p(t)$ and the polynomial $x^p-x-t$.

[L1] The rational function field is a field of fractions ([[cor-rational-function-field-as-a-fraction-field]]).

[L2] In characteristic $p$, a degree-$p$ extension is cyclic exactly when it is generated by a root of an irreducible polynomial $x^p-x-a$ ([[thm-artin-schreier-characterization-of-cyclic-degree-p-extensions]]).

[L3] A finite extension is Galois when it is the splitting field of a separable polynomial, and then the order of its Galois group equals its degree ([[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Suppose $t=b^p-b$ for some $b\in\mathbb F_p(t)$. Write $b=u/v$ in lowest terms. At the pole $t=\infty$, if $b$ has pole order $m>0$ then $b^p-b$ has pole order $pm$, a multiple of $p$; if $b$ has no pole there, neither does $b^p-b$. Both alternatives contradict the simple pole of $t$ at infinity. Therefore no such $b$ exists. [L1, algebra]

2.1 Let $\alpha$ be a root in an algebraic closure and let $m_\alpha$ have degree $d$ over $F$. Every conjugate of $\alpha$ is a root of $x^p-x-t$, hence has the form $\alpha+i$ with $i\in\mathbb F_p$ and therefore lies in $F(\alpha)$. The polynomial $m_\alpha$ thus splits in $F(\alpha)$, and it is separable because it divides a polynomial with derivative $-1$. By [L3], $F(\alpha)/F$ is Galois. Its automorphisms inject into the additive group of $\mathbb F_p$ by $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha+i$, so $d$ is $1$ or $p$. Step 1.1 excludes $d=1$, hence $d=p$ and $m_\alpha=x^p-x-t$. Thus this polynomial is irreducible. [L3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Now [L2] makes $F(\alpha)/F$ cyclic of degree $p$. Its full root set is $\alpha+i$ for $i\in\mathbb F_p$, since $(\alpha+i)^p-(\alpha+i)=\alpha^p-\alpha=t$. [L2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
