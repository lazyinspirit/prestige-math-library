## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain and let $I\subseteq R$ be a nonzero ideal. Then for every nonzero element $a\in I$ there exists $b\in I$ such that $I=(a,b)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Dedekind domain $R$, a nonzero ideal $I\subseteq R$, and a chosen nonzero element $a\in I$.

[L1] Nonzero ideals factor uniquely into prime powers in a Dedekind domain ([[thm-unique-factorisation-of-ideals-in-dedekind-domains]]).

[L3] The Chinese remainder theorem solves simultaneous congruences modulo finitely many pairwise comaximal ideals ([[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Form the integral ideal $J:=(a)I^{-1}$. By [L1], only finitely many prime ideals $\mathfrak p$ satisfy $v_{\mathfrak p}(a)>v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$; call this finite set $S$. For each $\mathfrak p\in S$, choose $b_{\mathfrak p}\in I$ with $v_{\mathfrak p}(b_{\mathfrak p})=v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$. By [L3], choose elements $u_{\mathfrak p}\in R$ such that $u_{\mathfrak p}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p}$ and $u_{\mathfrak p}\equiv0\pmod{\mathfrak q}$ for every distinct $\mathfrak q\in S$. Put $b:=\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S}u_{\mathfrak p}b_{\mathfrak p}$, and if $S=\varnothing$ put $b=0$. Then $b\in I$. [L1, L3, given, choose]

2.1 For $\mathfrak p\in S$, the term $u_{\mathfrak p}b_{\mathfrak p}$ is a unit multiple of $b_{\mathfrak p}$ in $R_{\mathfrak p}$, while every other summand lies in $\mathfrak pI_{\mathfrak p}$. Hence $v_{\mathfrak p}(b)=v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$. For $\mathfrak q\notin S$, one has $v_{\mathfrak q}(a)=v_{\mathfrak q}(I)$ by definition of $S$. Therefore $(a,b)$ and $I$ have the same prime valuations. [L1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [L1], ideals with the same prime valuations are equal. Hence $(a,b)=I$. [L1, step 2.1] ∎
