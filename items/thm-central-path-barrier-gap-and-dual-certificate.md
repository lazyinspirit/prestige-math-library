## Statement

For the smooth convex inequality problem of [[def-barrier-central-path-and-newton-residuals]], suppose a strict feasible exact minimizer $x_t$ of the barrier objective exists, the primal value $p^*$ is finite, and the equality multiplier is included. Use the sign convention $L(x,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(x)+\lambda^Tg(x)+\nu^T(Ax-b)$, and define $q_D(\lambda,\nu)=\inf_{x\in D}L(x,\lambda,\nu)$ on the common open domain $D$. Then the Lagrangian dual pair generated by $\lambda_i=-1/(t g_i(x_t))$ has value $q_D(\lambda,\nu)=f_0(x_t)-m/t$, so
$$0\le f_0(x_t)-p^*\le m/t.$$
For the conic model of [[def-conic-optimization-primal-dual-models]], suppose $x_t$ is an exact barrier minimizer and $F$ is a $\nu$-log-homogeneous barrier. Its barrier multiplier $z_t=-\nabla F(s_t)/t$ and equality multiplier give a dual feasible pair with gap $\nu/t$, and hence
$$0\le c^Tx_t-p^*\le\nu/t.$$
No primal attainment or strong-duality assumption is used; existence of the exact barrier minimizer and finiteness of $p^*$ are required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The smooth and conic barrier objectives, their strict feasibility and exact minimizers, and a finite primal value.

[F1] The smooth inequality convention is $g_i\le0$, with multiplier $\lambda_i\ge0$ ([[def-inequality-constrained-program-and-kkt-geometry]]).

[F2] For differentiable convex $L$ on an open convex domain, $L(y)\ge L(x)+\nabla L(x)^T(y-x)$ ([[thm-differentiable-convex-functions-and-gradient-inequality]]).

[F3] The barrier identity is $DF(s)[s]=-\nu$ and $-\nabla F(s)\in K^*$ ([[lem-loghom-barrier-gradient-is-dual]]).

## Proof

1.1 In the smooth case, differentiating $\Psi_t$ at its affine-constrained minimizer gives $t\nabla f_0(x_t)+Dg(x_t)^T(-1/g(x_t))+A^Tw=0$. Since $g_i(x_t)<0$, $\lambda_i=-1/(t g_i(x_t))>0$, and $\nu=w/t$ make this $\nabla f_0+Dg^T\lambda+A^T\nu=0$. Also $\lambda_i g_i(x_t)=-1/t$ for every $i$. Thus $\nabla_x L(x_t,\lambda,\nu)=0$ and $L(\cdot,\lambda,\nu)$ is convex; [F2] proves that $x_t$ globally minimizes this Lagrangian on $D$. Therefore $q_D(\lambda,\nu)=L(x_t,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(x_t)-m/t$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 For every primal feasible $x\in D$, [F1] gives $\lambda_i\ge0$ and $g_i(x)\le0$, hence $L(x,\lambda,\nu)=f_0(x)+\lambda^Tg(x)\le f_0(x)$. Thus $q_D(\lambda,\nu)\le f_0(x)$ for every feasible $x$, so $q_D(\lambda,\nu)\le p^*$. Since $x_t$ is feasible, $p^*\le f_0(x_t)$. Combining this with step 1.1 yields $0\le f_0(x_t)-p^*\le m/t$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In the conic case set $z_t=-\nabla F(s_t)/t\in K^*$ by [F3] and $y_t=-w/t$. The stationarity equation in [[def-barrier-central-path-and-newton-residuals]] gives $A^Ty_t-G^*z_t=c$, so the pair is dual feasible. The conic gap identity in [[def-conic-optimization-primal-dual-models]] gives $c^Tx_t-(b^Ty_t-\langle h,z_t\rangle)=\langle z_t,s_t\rangle=-DF(s_t)[s_t]/t=\nu/t$. For every primal feasible $x$, the same identity and $z_t\in K^*$ make its objective at least the dual value, so $b^Ty_t-\langle h,z_t\rangle\le p^*$. Since $x_t$ is primal feasible, $p^*\le c^Tx_t$; combining these inequalities with the exact gap gives $0\le c^Tx_t-p^*\le\nu/t$. [F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
