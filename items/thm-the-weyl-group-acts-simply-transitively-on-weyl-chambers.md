## Statement

Let $\Phi\subseteq E$ be a reduced crystallographic root system and let
$W=W(\Phi)$ be its Weyl group
([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]). Then $W$ acts simply transitively on
the set of open Weyl chambers of $\Phi$
([[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]]): for any two open chambers $C,C'$
there is exactly one $w\in W$ with $w(C)=C'$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The open chambers of $\Phi$ (the connected components of the complement of the finitely many root hyperplanes $L_\alpha$, $\alpha\in\Phi$), the Weyl group $W\subseteq O(E)$ generated by the root reflections, and the fundamental chamber $C_+=\{x:(x,\alpha_i)>0,\ i=1,\dots,r\}$ of a base $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$.

[L1] Each root reflection $s_\alpha$ is orthogonal, fixes $L_\alpha$ pointwise, and negates $\alpha$; $W$ is finite and acts faithfully on $\Phi$ ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]], [[prop-the-weyl-group-is-finite-and-acts-faithfully-on-the-root-system]]).

[L2] The complement of $\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi}L_\alpha$ is a finite union of open convex cones, its components are the open chambers, and each chamber is defined by a system of strict inequalities $\pm(x,\alpha)>0$; the fundamental chamber $C_+$ is a chamber, and every $x\in C_+$ satisfies $(x,\alpha)>0$ for every positive root $\alpha$ ([[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

[L3] For $w\in W$, the inversion set is $N(w)=\{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}:w(\alpha)\in\Phi^{-}\}$ and its length is $\ell(w)=|N(w)|$; the number of root hyperplanes separating two chambers equals the number of roots that are positive on the first and negative on the second ([[def-length-and-longest-element-of-a-finite-weyl-group]]).

[L4] Every open chamber has exactly $r=\dim E$ walls, and each wall lies in some root hyperplane $L_\alpha$; every root hyperplane is a wall of some chamber ([[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $C,C'$ be open chambers, and let $x\in C$, $x'\in C'$ be points such that the segment $[x,x']$ meets no intersection $L_\alpha\cap L_\beta$ of two distinct root hyperplanes; such points can be chosen because a chamber is open and dense in its closure and only finitely many bad affine subspaces occur. The finitely many parameters $t$ with $x+t(x'-x)\in L_\alpha$ are then distinct, and they split the segment into subsegments lying in chambers $C=C_0,C_1,\dots,C_m=C'$; consecutive chambers $C_{k-1},C_k$ share the hyperplane $L_{\alpha_k}$ just crossed and lie on opposite sides of it, so $s_{\alpha_k}(C_{k-1})=C_k$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 Every chamber is of the form $u(C_+)$ for some $u$ in the subgroup generated by the simple reflections: applying step 1.1 with $C=C_+$, the wall crossed from $C_{k-1}$ into $C_k$ is of the form $u(L_{\alpha_j})$ with $u$ the product of the reflections already crossed and some simple root $\alpha_j$ (its normal is a simple root because it is a wall of $u(C_+)$), so $s_{\alpha_k}=us_{\alpha_j}u^{-1}$ and $C_k=us_{\alpha_j}(C_+)$; induction on the number of crossed hyperplanes proves the claim. [L4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Consequences of step 2.1: (i) the subgroup generated by the simple reflections contains every root reflection, because every root hyperplane $L_\alpha$ is a wall of some chamber $u(C_+)$ by [L4], hence is $u(L_{\alpha_j})$ for a simple root $\alpha_j$ and $s_\alpha=us_{\alpha_j}u^{-1}$; since the simple reflections generate $W$, so do the simple reflections alone. (ii) $W$ acts transitively on chambers: any chamber is $u(C_+)$. [L1, L2, L4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The length $\ell(w)=|N(w)|$ equals the minimum number $\ell'(w)$ of simple reflections in an expression $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_l}$: the expression of step 2.1 applied to $C=C_+$ and $C'=w(C_+)$ uses one reflection per crossed hyperplane, and a generic segment from $C_+$ to $w(C_+)$ crosses exactly the $\ell(w)$ separating hyperplanes, each once, so $\ell'(w)\le\ell(w)$; conversely any expression with $l$ factors gives a chain of chambers $C_k=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}(C_+)$ in which at most one hyperplane is added at each step, so at most $l$ hyperplanes separate $C_+$ from $w(C_+)$ and $\ell(w)\le l$. Hence $\ell(w)=\ell'(w)$. [L1, L3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 $W$ acts freely on chambers. Suppose $w(C_+)=C_+$. If $\alpha\in\Phi^{+}$ and $w\alpha\in\Phi^{-}$, then $-w\alpha\in\Phi^{+}$; for $x\in C_+$ one has $wx\in C_+$ and hence $(x,\alpha)>0$ while $(wx,-w\alpha)=-(x,\alpha)<0$, contradicting that every positive root has positive inner product with every point of $C_+$ (which holds by [L2] because positive roots are nonnegative integral combinations of the simple roots). Hence $w(\Phi^{+})\subseteq\Phi^{+}$, so $N(w)=\varnothing$ and $\ell(w)=0$; by step 4.1 the shortest simple-reflection expression of $w$ has length $0$, that is, $w=1$. [L2, L3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Combining transitivity (step 3.1) with freeness (step 5.1): for any chambers $C,C'$ there is $w$ with $w(C)=C'$, and if also $v(C)=C'$ then $v^{-1}w(C)=C$ forces $v^{-1}w=1$ and $v=w$. This is simple transitivity. [step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
