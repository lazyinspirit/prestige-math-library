## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a
split reductive group over $k$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$. Then every dominant
$\lambda\in X(T)^+$ is the highest weight of a simple finite-dimensional
rational representation of $G$; equivalently, there is a rational
representation of $G$ containing a primitive vector of weight $\lambda$
([[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, derived subgroup $G_{\mathrm{der}}$, and a dominant $\lambda\in X(T)$.

[F1] *Decomposition of a reductive group.* $Z(G)_t$ is a torus contained in $T$, the derived subgroup $G_{\mathrm{der}}$ is semisimple, $T_{\mathrm{der}} =(T\cap G_{\mathrm{der}})_t$ is a split maximal torus of $G_{\mathrm{der}}$, and the multiplication morphism $m:Z(G)_t\times G_{\mathrm{der}}\to G$ is surjective with finite central kernel $N$, so $m$ is a central isogeny; moreover every maximal torus of $G$ contains $Z(G)$ ([[lem-reductive-center-radical-and-semisimple-quotient]], [[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]], [[def-borel-subgroup-and-maximal-torus]], [[lem-maximal-tori-extension-conjugacy-and-derived-group]], parts (d) and (e)). The maximal torus $T_{\mathrm{der}}\subseteq T$ is split because a subtorus of a split torus has a character lattice that is a torsion-free quotient of the original lattice with trivial Galois action ([[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]]). The product $H=Z(G)_t\times G_{\mathrm{der}}$ is split reductive with maximal torus $T_H=Z(G)_t\times T_{\mathrm{der}}$ and $m(T_H)=T$ ([[def-split-reductive-algebraic-group]]).

[F2] *Dominant characters of the product.* Let $Z$ be a split torus and let $T_0$ be a maximal torus of a split semisimple group $G_0$. Every dominant character of $Z\times G_0$ is the weight of a primitive vector of a rational representation ([[lem-dominant-characters-of-products-of-tori-and-split-semisimple-groups-arise-as-primitive-weights]]).

[F3] *Descent along a central isogeny.* Let $\pi:(H',T')\to(H'',T'')$ be a central isogeny of split reductive groups with kernel $N'$, let $\mu\in X(T')$ and let $V'$ be a simple $H'$-module of highest weight $\mu$. Then $V'$ factors through $H''$ if and only if $\mu\in X(T'')$; if so, $N'$ acts trivially and the descended $H''$-module is simple with highest weight $\mu$ relative to compatible Borel pairs ([[lem-centre-central-characters-and-descent-along-central-isogenies]]).

[F4] *Modules generated by a primitive vector.* If a rational representation of a split reductive group is generated by a primitive vector of weight $\nu$, then it has a largest proper submodule and the quotient by it is a simple module generated by the image of the vector, of highest weight $\nu$ ([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]]).

[F5] *Simple modules.* Every simple rational representation of $G$ contains a primitive vector whose weight is its highest weight, and every simple rational representation of the affine group scheme $G$ of finite type is finite-dimensional ([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]], [[lem-simple-rational-representations-are-finite-dimensional]]).

[F6] *Root groups, character descent and scheme images.* The zero adjoint weight space is $\operatorname{Lie}T$ ([[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Each root group is a smooth copy of $\mathbf G_a$ with its root tangent weight; a smooth torus-stable subgroup contains that root group if its Lie algebra contains the corresponding root space. The rank-one normalizer represents the reflection $s_\alpha(x)=x-\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$, and positive root groups multiply to the Borel's unipotent radical ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). A subgroup scheme both unipotent and diagonalizable is trivial ([[lem-unipotent-and-diagonalizable-intersection-is-trivial]]); a group homomorphism with trivial scheme kernel is an isomorphism onto its closed scheme image ([[lem-nonaffine-group-image-exact-quotient-properties]]). The character anti-equivalence for split diagonalizable groups sends a scheme kernel to the cokernel of the character map; thus characters trivial on the kernel of a torus isogeny are precisely those pulled back from its target ([[lem-diagonalizable-character-antiequivalence]]).

[F7] *Borels and positive systems.* Borels containing a split maximal torus correspond bijectively to positive root systems and are the cocharacter subgroups for regular cocharacters ([[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]]). The cocharacter decomposition gives $B=T\ltimes R_u(B)$ in this regular reductive case ([[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]]).

## Proof

**Given:** AC; a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, derived subgroup $G_{\mathrm{der}}$, and a dominant $\lambda\in X(T)$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] form $H=Z(G)_t\times G_{\mathrm{der}}$ with maximal torus $T_H=Z(G)_t\times T_{\mathrm{der}}$ and central isogeny $m:H\to G$ with finite kernel $N$. Its torus restriction maps $T_H$ onto $T$ and induces an injection $m^*:X(T)\hookrightarrow X(T_H)$ with finite cokernel. Put $\lambda_H=m^*\lambda$, which lies in that image. [F1]

2.1 We establish the root correspondence, without assuming the total Lie differential is an isomorphism. The central kernel lies in $Z(H)\subseteq T_H$ by [F1] applied to $H$. For a root $\beta$ of $H$, $N\cap U_\beta$ is both unipotent and diagonalizable, so it is trivial; hence $m|_{U_\beta}$ is an isomorphism onto a smooth closed image $I_\beta$ by [F6]. Centrality makes $N$ act trivially on the root tangent line, so $\beta|_N=1$ and exact character duality gives a unique $\alpha\in X(T)$ with $m^*\alpha=\beta$. The image is $T$-stable, with nonzero tangent weight $\alpha$, so $\alpha$ is a root of $G$, and the root-group containment criterion gives $U_\alpha\subseteq I_\beta$. Both are smooth connected curves, hence equal. This gives an injection of root sets; it is a bijection since $\dim H=\dim G$, $\dim T_H=\dim T$, and the root decomposition with one-dimensional root spaces gives $|\Phi|=\dim G-\dim T$ for either group. [F1, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The torus isogeny maps the maximal subtorus of $\ker\beta$ onto that of $\ker\alpha$, so it maps the source rank-one centralizer into the target one. Hence a rank-one reflection representative $n_\beta$ maps into that target rank-one subgroup. Its action on $T$ is nontrivial, because torus pullback is injective and intertwines conjugation, so it represents $s_\alpha$ by [F6]. Comparing the two reflection formulas and using $m^*\alpha=\beta$ gives $\langle m^*x,\beta^\vee\rangle=\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ for every $x\in X(T)$. The root bijection preserves addition, so the inverse images of $\Phi^+(B)$ form a positive system in $H$. Choose its Borel $B_H$ by [F7]; it is a product Borel since the central torus has no roots. By [F6] and [F7], $B_H$ and $B$ are generated by their maximal tori and positive root groups; their identified generators give $m(B_H)=B$. All highest weights below use these compatible pairs. [F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For every positive root $\beta=m^*\alpha$ of $H$, step 3.1 gives $\langle\lambda_H,\beta^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\ge0$. Hence $\lambda_H$ is dominant for the product Borel $B_H$, so the hypothesis of [F2] is satisfied. [given, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 By [F2] there is a rational representation $W$ of $H$ containing a primitive vector $v$ of weight $\lambda_H$. Let $V_H\subseteq W$ be the $H$-submodule generated by $v$; applying [F4] to $V_H$ and $v$, the quotient $Q=V_H/V_H'$ by the largest proper $H$-submodule $V_H'$ is a simple $H$-module, and the image of $v$ is a nonzero primitive vector of weight $\lambda_H$ in $Q$, so $\lambda_H$ is the highest weight of the simple module $Q$. [F2, F4, step 4.1]

6.1 By [F3] applied to the central isogeny $m:H\to G$ (with $\pi=m$) and the simple $H$-module $Q$ of highest weight $\lambda_H\in X(T)$, the kernel $N$ acts trivially on $Q$ and $Q$ descends along $H/N=G$ to a simple $G$-module of highest weight $\lambda_H$, which is $\lambda$ under the identification $X(T)\subseteq X(T_H)$. This descended module is finite-dimensional by [F5], since $G$ is affine of finite type. Hence every dominant $\lambda$ is the highest weight of a simple finite-dimensional rational representation of $G$. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 5.1]

7.1 For the equivalence: a simple finite-dimensional $G$-module of highest weight $\lambda$ contains a primitive vector of weight $\lambda$ by [F5], while conversely a rational representation containing a primitive vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$ has, by [F4] applied to the submodule generated by $v$, a simple quotient of highest weight $\lambda$, finite-dimensional by [F5]. Thus the two formulations are equivalent. [F4, F5, step 6.1] ∎

## Remarks

- This is the reduction in Milne's proof of Theorem 22.20: the product $Z(G)_t\times G_{\mathrm{der}}$ is handled by the semisimple case plus the torus factor, and the central isogeny of (19.25) performs the descent.
- The hypothesis that $\lambda$ is a character of $T$ is used exactly through the identification $X(T)\subseteq X(T_H)$; a dominant element of $X(T_H)$ not lying in $X(T)$ would produce a simple module of the covering group that does not descend.
