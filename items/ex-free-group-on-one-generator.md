## Example

For a one-element set $X=\{x\}$, the free group on $X$ is isomorphic to
$(\mathbb Z,+)$ by the isomorphism that sends $[x]$ to $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The word-quotient free group $F_{\mathrm{word}}(\{x\})$.

[L1] Every class in $W(X)/{\sim}$ contains exactly one reduced word ([[thm-normal-form-for-the-word-quotient-model]]).

[L2] A cyclic group generated by an element of infinite order is isomorphic to $(\mathbb Z,+)$ ([[thm-classification-of-cyclic-groups]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 A reduced word on $\{x,x^{-1}\}$ cannot contain both letters, since a change from one to the other creates an adjacent inverse pair; hence every reduced word is uniquely $x^n$ for $n\geq0$ or $x^{-n}$ for $n\geq1$, with the empty word corresponding to exponent $0$. [L1, given]

2.1 Thus $[x]$ generates the group, and no positive power $[x]^n$ is the identity because its reduced representative $x^n$ is nonempty; therefore $[x]$ has infinite order. [L1, step 1.1]

3.1 By [L2], construct the isomorphism $F_{\mathrm{word}}(\{x\})\cong(\mathbb Z,+)$ carrying $[x]$ to $1$ and $[x]^k$ to $k$. [L2, step 2.1, construct, discharge-construct] ∎
