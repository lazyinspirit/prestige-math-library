## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with
standard basis $e,f,h$ and Casimir element $\Omega$, so that
$Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\mathbb C[\Omega]$. The annihilator of the trivial module $\mathbb C$ is
the augmentation ideal
$\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C=U(\mathfrak g)\mathfrak g=(e,f,h)$,
the two-sided ideal generated by $\mathfrak g$; it is a primitive ideal whose
central character $\chi_0$ is the one with $\chi_0(\Omega)=0$, and
$\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C\cap Z(U(\mathfrak g))=(\Omega)$ is
a maximal ideal of $Z(U(\mathfrak g))$. The central ideal
$U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_0=(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)$ is strictly smaller than the
annihilator: the highest vector of the simple Verma module $M(-2)$ is an
eigenvector of $h$ with eigenvalue $-2$, so $h\in\operatorname{Ann}\mathbb C$
but $h\notin(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)$. Thus at the trivial central character the
annihilator of the trivial module is not generated by the Casimir relation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with basis $e,f,h$, the trivial module $\mathbb C$, and the ideal $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C$ of operators acting by $0$ on $\mathbb C$.

[F1] The trivial module is the unital module on which $e,f,h$ act by $0$; the corresponding algebra homomorphism is the augmentation $\varepsilon\colon U(\mathfrak g)\to\mathbb C$ with $\varepsilon(\mathfrak g)=0$, so its kernel is the two-sided ideal generated by $\mathfrak g$: after killing $e,f,h$, every nonempty tensor word is zero and the quotient is spanned by $1$; $\varepsilon(1)=1$ makes the remaining scalar quotient exactly $\mathbb C$ ([[def-annihilator-ideal-of-a-lie-algebra-module]], [[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]]).

[F2] Under AC the center is $\mathbb C[\Omega]$, so $\ker\chi_0=(\Omega)$ and $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_0=(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)$; the trivial module is simple and nonzero, so its annihilator is primitive and the center acts on it through the central character $\chi_0$ with $\chi_0(\Omega)=0$, the Casimir eigenvalue of the trivial module ([[lem-the-central-reduction-of-usl2-is-simple-away-from-finite-dimensional-characters]], [[cor-the-center-is-a-polynomial-algebra-of-rank-many-generators]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-primitive-ideal-of-an-enveloping-algebra]], [[prop-a-primitive-ideal-determines-a-central-character]], [[def-central-character-of-a-lie-algebra-module]], [[prop-casimir-eigenvalue-on-a-highest-weight-module]]).

[F3] $M(-2)$ is a simple Verma module by the Verma irreducibility criterion, since $-2+1=-1\notin\mathbb Z_{>0}$; its highest vector $v_{-2}$ satisfies $hv_{-2}=-2v_{-2}$, and its central character is $\chi_{-2}$, which equals $\chi_0$ because the normalized Casimir $\Omega=ef+fe+\tfrac12h^2$ acts on both by $0$, by the eigenvalue formula $\Omega\mapsto\lambda(\lambda+2)/2$ and $-2\cdot0/2=0$ ([[def-verma-module]], [[cor-verma-irreducibility-criterion-from-shapovalov-determinants]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-cyclic-highest-weight-module]], [[lem-the-central-reduction-of-usl2-is-simple-away-from-finite-dimensional-characters]]).

[F4] $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)$ for every $\lambda$ ([[prop-verma-annihilator-contains-the-central-character-ideal]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C=\ker\varepsilon=U(\mathfrak g)\mathfrak g=(e,f,h)$; this is a proper two-sided ideal, and since $\mathbb C$ is simple and nonzero it is primitive by [F2]. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 The trivial module has Casimir eigenvalue $\Omega\mapsto0$, so its central character is $\chi_0$ with $\chi_0(\Omega)=0$; by [F2], $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C\cap Z(U(\mathfrak g))=\ker\chi_0$, which contains $\Omega$ and is a maximal ideal of $Z(U(\mathfrak g))$. The two-sided ideal generated by $\ker\chi_0$ is $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_0=(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)$, contained in the annihilator by [F2]. [F2, algebra]

1.3 By [F3], $\chi_{-2}=\chi_0$, so [F4] applied to $M(-2)$ gives $(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(-2)$. The highest vector satisfies $hv_{-2}=-2v_{-2}\ne0$, so $h\notin\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(-2)$ and hence $h\notin(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)$. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 On the other hand $h$ acts on the trivial module by $0$, so $h\in\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C$ by [F1]. Thus $(\Omega)U(\mathfrak g)\subsetneq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}\mathbb C$: the central ideal does not exhaust the annihilator. [step 1.1, step 1.3, F1, algebra] ∎
