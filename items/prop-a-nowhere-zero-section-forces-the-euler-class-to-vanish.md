## Statement

Assume AC. Let $E\to B$ be an $R$-oriented numerable real vector bundle of
rank $n\geq1$ over a base in the scope of the general Thom theorem. If $E$
admits a nowhere-zero section, then
$$e(E)=0\qquad\text{in }H^n(B;R).$$
No converse is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, an $R$-oriented numerable rank-$n$ bundle $E\to B$ with $n\geq1$ over a base in the general Thom scope, and a nowhere-zero section $\sigma$ of $E$.

[F1] The Euler class is $e(E)=s^*j^*(u_E)$, and the Gysin sequence of an oriented bundle in the Thom scope is the exact and natural sequence $$\cdots\to H^{k-n}(B;R)\xrightarrow{\smile e(E)}H^k(B;R) \xrightarrow{p^*}H^k(S(E);R)\xrightarrow{\partial_G}H^{k-n+1}(B;R)\to\cdots,$$ where $p:S(E)\to B$ is the sphere bundle projection ([[def-euler-class-by-zero-section-pullback-of-the-thom-class]], [[thm-gysin-long-exact-sequence-of-an-oriented-sphere-bundle]]).

[F2] Under AC every numerable real bundle carries a bundle metric ([[thm-numerable-vector-bundles-admit-bundle-metrics]]).

[F3] Pullback of cohomology is contravariantly functorial, so $(p\sigma)^*=\sigma^*p^*$ and the identity map induces the identity on cohomology ([[prop-singular-cohomology-is-contravariantly-functorial]]).

[F4] The zero section and pullback sections have the displayed coordinate formulas; in particular the sphere bundle is the locus of unit vectors ([[def-pullback-vector-bundle-and-pullback-section]]).

[A1] AC is the Axiom of Choice in the form fixed by [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof
1.1 The section can be normalised. Choose a bundle metric on the numerable bundle $E$ by [F2] and put $\widehat\sigma(b)=\sigma(b)/\|\sigma(b)\|$; this is continuous because $\sigma$ is nowhere zero, and it is a section of the sphere bundle $p:S(E)\to B$ in the sense of [F4], that is, $p\widehat\sigma=\operatorname{id}_B$. The normalisation is a specified function of the supplied section and metric, not a choice. [F2, F4]

2.1 The sphere projection is injective on cohomology. Since $p\widehat\sigma=\operatorname{id}_B$, functoriality [F3] gives $\widehat\sigma^*p^*=(\operatorname{id}_B)^*=\operatorname{id}$ on $H^*(B;R)$; a map with a left inverse is injective, so $p^*:H^k(B;R)\to H^k(S(E);R)$ is injective for every $k$. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The Euler class vanishes. Take $k=n$ in the Gysin sequence of [F1]: $$H^0(B;R)\xrightarrow{\smile e(E)}H^n(B;R)\xrightarrow{p^*}H^n(S(E);R).$$ Exactness at $H^n(B;R)$ says that the kernel of $p^*$ is the image of $\smile e(E)$, which is the cyclic subgroup generated by $e(E)$, because $e(E)=1\smile e(E)$ is the image of the unit. By step 2.1 the kernel is zero, so $e(E)=0$. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 Boundary cases and the missing converse. The hypothesis $n\geq1$ is used in two places: the sphere bundle has nonempty fiber $S^{n-1}$, and the unit normalisation of step 1.1 divides by the positive norm of a nonzero vector of a positive-dimensional fiber. For $n=0$ every section is the zero section, $e(0_B)=1\neq0$, and the statement is excluded. If $\sigma$ is the zero section of a positive-rank bundle the normalisation is undefined, and indeed the conclusion can fail. The proposition asserts no converse: the vanishing of $e(E)$ does not in general produce a nowhere-zero section, and no such section is constructed here. Over the empty base the unique class is zero. AC is used through the metric of [F2] and the Gysin sequence, as recorded. [F1, F2, A1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
