## Statement refuted

**False claim:** Let $n\ge2$ and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a bounded
$C^1$ domain. Every pair of smooth data, a smooth $f$ on $\overline\Omega$ and
a smooth $g$ on $\partial\Omega$, admits a solution $u\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$
of the classical Neumann problem
$$-\Delta u=f\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad \partial_\nu u=g\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,$$
with no compatibility condition relating $f$ and $g$.

The claim fails already at the level of a necessary equation: every such
solution must satisfy
$$\int_\Omega f\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS,$$
and the smooth constant data $f\equiv1$, $g\equiv0$ violate it, because the
left-hand side is $\lambda(\Omega)>0$ while the right-hand side is $0$. The
counterexample below proves both facts. Compatibility is thus necessary, not
sufficient: this item asserts no existence result for compatible data.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, an integer $n\ge2$, a bounded $C^1$ domain
$\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ in the convention of
[[def-bounded-c-one-domain-boundary-charts-and-outward-normal]], and the
constant data $f\equiv1$ on $\Omega$, $g\equiv0$ on $\partial\Omega$. Write
$\lambda$ for Lebesgue measure on $\mathbb R^n$.

[A1] Countable Choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, says that every sequence
of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]). It is the
only choice principle used below, through the divergence theorem and the
Lebesgue-measure interfaces.

[F1] For $n\ge2$, a bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega$ and
$F\in C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$,
$\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}F\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}F\cdot\nu\,dS$, both
integrals finite, with the outward normal on every boundary component
([[thm-divergence-theorem-for-bounded-c-one-euclidean-domains]]).

[F2] The Laplacian is $\Delta u=\operatorname{div}\nabla u$ for
$u\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$, and the classical normal derivative is
$\partial_\nu u=Du\cdot\nu$ for the outward unit normal
([[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-classical-normal-derivative]]).

[F3] A bounded $C^1$ domain is a nonempty bounded open subset of
$\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge2$ and locally $C^1$ boundary; for $C^2(\overline\Omega)$
the derivatives through order two extend continuously to $\overline\Omega$, and
$F\in C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$ means $F$ and its first derivatives
extend continuously ([[def-bounded-c-one-domain-boundary-charts-and-outward-normal]]).

[F4] A subset $U$ of a metric space is open exactly when every $x\in U$ has a
real $r>0$ with $B(x,r)\subseteq U$ ([[def-metric-topology]]); here
$B(a,r)=\{y:\lVert y-a\rVert_2<r\}$ is the Euclidean metric ball
([[def-metric-ball]]).

[F5] Every Euclidean ball $B(a,r)$ with $r>0$ is Lebesgue measurable and
satisfies $0<\lambda(B(a,r))<\infty$, under Countable Choice
([[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]]).

[F6] The Borel $\sigma$-algebra is generated by the open sets
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), and under Countable Choice every Borel subset of
$\mathbb R^n$ is Lebesgue measurable; in particular every open set is
([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]).

[F7] For a measurable set $E$ the integral over $E$ is
$\int_E h\,d\lambda=\int h\chi_E\,d\lambda$
([[def-integral-over-a-measurable-set]]); the simple integral of
$\chi_E=1\cdot\chi_E$ is $1\cdot\lambda(E)$
([[def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]]), and the nonnegative
integral of a nonnegative simple measurable function equals its simple
integral ([[prop-the-nonnegative-integral-agrees-with-the-simple-integral]]).
Thus $\int_E 1\,d\lambda=\lambda(E)$ for measurable $E$.

[F8] If $A\subseteq B$ are measurable subsets of a measure space, then
$\mu(A)\le\mu(B)$ ([[prop-measure-monotonicity]]).

[F9] Surface integration on a compact embedded $C^1$ hypersurface is chart
integration of the signed integrand through its positive and negative parts,
so an integrand that vanishes identically integrates to zero
([[def-surface-integral-on-a-compact-c-one-hypersurface]]); the boundary
$\partial\Omega$ of a bounded $C^1$ domain is such a hypersurface with the
outward normal of [F3].

[F10] For $k\in\mathbb N$ a map is of class $C^k$ when all iterated partial
derivatives of words of length at most $k$ exist and are continuous
([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]); a constant map has
all positive-order iterated partial derivatives equal to $0$ and is therefore
smooth.

[L1] The refuted claim: every pair of smooth Neumann data on a bounded $C^1$
domain admits a $C^2(\overline\Omega)$ solution, with no compatibility
condition between the source $f$ and the boundary datum $g$.

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F3] the domain $\Omega$ is a nonempty bounded open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge2$; fix a point $a\in\Omega$. Applying the openness definition [F4] to $a$ gives $r>0$ with $B(a,r)\subseteq\Omega$, a Euclidean metric ball in the sense of [F4]. [given, F3, F4]

1.2 The source datum $f$ is the constant one on $\Omega$, and the boundary datum $g$ is the identically zero function on $\partial\Omega$, the restriction of the zero constant function on $\mathbb R^n$. By [F10] a constant map has every positive-order iterated partial derivative equal to $0$ and is of class $C^k$ for every $k$, hence smooth; both data are therefore smooth, and $f$ is constant on the open set $\Omega$. [given, F3, F10]

1.3 Suppose $u\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$ satisfies $-\Delta u=f$ in $\Omega$ and $\partial_\nu u=g$ on $\partial\Omega$. By [F3] the gradient field $\nabla u$ lies in $C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$, so the divergence theorem [F1] applies to it, with Countable Choice [A1]: $\int_\Omega\Delta u\,dx=\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}\nabla u\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nu\,dS=\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS$, the middle identity by [F1], the first by [F2], and the last because $\nabla u\cdot\nu=\partial_\nu u=g$ by [F2]. Since $f=-\Delta u$ pointwise, this says $\int_\Omega f\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS$: every classical solution forces the compatibility equation. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 For the constant one datum of step 1.2, $\int_\Omega f\,dx=\int_\Omega 1\,d\lambda=\lambda(\Omega)$: by [F7] the integral over the measurable set $\Omega$ of the constant one is the simple integral of the indicator $\chi_\Omega$, namely $1\cdot\lambda(\Omega)$. Since $\Omega$ is open it is Lebesgue measurable by [F6], and $B(a,r)\subseteq\Omega$ by step 1.1 with both sets measurable, so the monotonicity [F8] gives $\lambda(\Omega)\ge\lambda(B(a,r))$, while [F5] gives $\lambda(B(a,r))>0$. Hence $\int_\Omega f\,dx>0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F5, F6, F7, F8]

2.2 For the zero datum of step 1.2 the boundary integrand $y\mapsto g(y)$ is identically zero on the compact $C^1$ hypersurface $\partial\Omega$, so by [F9] its surface integral vanishes: $\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS=0$, and therefore $-\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS=0$. [step 1.2, F3, F9]

3.1 If a solution $u\in C^2(\overline\Omega)$ existed, step 1.3 would give $\int_\Omega f\,dx=-\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS$, whereas step 2.1 gives $\int_\Omega f\,dx>0$ and step 2.2 gives $-\int_{\partial\Omega}g\,dS=0$; the resulting $0<\int_\Omega f\,dx=0$ is a contradiction. Hence the smooth constant data $f\equiv1$, $g\equiv0$ admit no classical solution on any bounded $C^1$ domain, although by step 1.2 each datum is smooth, and the claim [L1] is refuted. The compatibility equation of step 1.3 is necessary only; its sufficiency for existence is not asserted, and the nonempty domain, positive radius and both boundary orientations are the ones fixed in [F3], [F4] and [F9]. The only choice principle used is $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ of [A1], through the divergence and measure interfaces. [step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, L1, algebra, cases] ∎

## Source notes

Teschl, *Partial Differential Equations: From Classical to Modern* (2025
archived author manuscript), §5.4 equation (5.43), printed p.128, records the
Neumann compatibility identity in the present sign convention
$-\Delta u=f$. Hunter, *Notes on Partial Differential Equations* (2014), §2.5
Theorem 2.24 and the surrounding Green identities, printed p.32, supplies the
divergence-theorem derivation of the same identity. Neither source is used as
a proof here: the necessity is derived directly from the local divergence
theorem, and the violation by the constant data $f\equiv1$, $g\equiv0$ is
computed from the positivity of the Lebesgue measure of the nonempty open
domain. The sibling corollary in this pair (draft at the time of writing)
records the same compatibility equation; the derivation here is self-contained
and also covers domains that are not connected, which is why no connectedness
hypothesis appears. This item asserts no existence theorem for compatible data.
