## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Suppose a sequence of actual bounded-energy nearly symmetric polygons has the stable-source, strip and bubble limit of the named compactness theorem, with common oriented boundary tori, protected integrable geometry and exact transverse end charts. Write $\mu_v$ for the actual fixed-map index of EVERY stable polygon component, including a constant one, and each nonconstant strip component. For a boundary bubble $b$ put $\nu_b=\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}(P_b)-m$, using its actual compact disk operator. For a sphere bubble $c$ put $\sigma_c=2\langle c_1(TX),[c]\rangle$. Then the sequence's fixed-map index is eventually
$$\mu=\sum_v\mu_v+\sum_b\nu_b+\sum_c\sigma_c.$$
No regularity of the sequence or its limiting components is needed. A constant stable polygon retains its actual, possibly negative, fixed-map index and is never deleted by a zero-energy argument. A constant compact sphere or one-torus disk has zero sphere or disk index contribution; redundant such ghosts and removable constant strip levels may be omitted.

If the target is the balanced symmetric product $\operatorname{Sym}^{h+k-1}\Sigma$ and each boundary system cuts into the stated $k$ marked ball components, the actual positive-domain formulas give $\nu_b\ge2$ for every nonconstant boundary bubble and $\sigma_c\ge2k$ for every nonconstant sphere. Thus, after including conformal parameters and quotienting only actual nonconstant strip translations, the count-index budget is
$$a=\sum_v a_v+q+\sum_b\nu_b+\sum_c\sigma_c,$$
where $q$ is the number of nodes of the polygon/strip tree after deleting the sphere/disk bubble trees and removable constant strip levels. The $a_v$ include the actual count indices of constant stable polygon vertices; these indices are not asserted nonnegative. A common auxiliary parameter of dimension $p$ is included only ONCE in this formula; equivalently add $p$ to the total and distribute it to the one component carrying that parameter. These are operator and degree identities, not transversality assertions or a classification of bubble counts.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual convergent sequence and its no-neck finite bubble tree, matching corner frames, oriented boundary systems, and the stated target when positivity is invoked.

[F1] Actual common-cap determinant excision computes relative finite-ended indices. Actual node index additivity holds without regularity by finite cokernel stabilization. ([[lem-finite-ended-cauchy-riemann-relative-index-is-compact-determinant-winding]])

[F2] An actual oriented real-boundary compact disk has index $m$ plus its squared determinant boundary winding. ([[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F3] Actual stable-source polygon compactness supplies smooth core convergence, finite bubble trees and quantitative collapse of every intervening image neck. The conformal source dimension is the sum of the vertex dimensions plus one for each stable-source edge. ([[thm-bounded-energy-polygons-have-stable-source-bubble-and-strip-limits]], [[lem-boundary-marked-disks-have-finite-tree-conformal-neck-charts]])

[F4] The supplied neat Morse cut balls give the actual finite oriented surface polygon needed for the sphere formula, by the finite spanning-tree and paired-rim construction. ([[lem-neat-balanced-handlebody-cuts-supply-finite-surface-polygons]]) For actual balanced one-torus disk classes the compact fixed-node index is twice the sum of the component coefficients; for actual sphere classes the sphere attachment shift is $2k$ times the incidence degree. These statements do not require that the ambient three-manifold be $S^3$. ([[lem-balanced-boundary-disk-domains-have-twice-component-sum-maslov-index]], [[lem-multipointed-symmetric-product-spheres-have-index-shift-two-k]])

## Proof

1.1 In rank zero every fixed-map bundle and operator is zero, so every fixed-map contribution in the asserted formula is zero; the count-index formula is exactly the finite-tree conformal-dimension identity in [F3]. Thus that case holds directly. For positive rank first use ONE fixed reference complex structure on the target tangent bundle. All the source-dependent nearly symmetric tensors are tame off the common integrable region. Choose a compatible reference agreeing with the integrable tensor there and with the exact product tensor in the generator charts; no preservation of a possibly noncompatible boundary tensor is needed for this index homotopy. Relative to that reference their Cayley matrices have norm less than one, since the taming inequality in the variables $v=(I-B)w$ is $|w|^2-|Bw|^2>0$. Contract these matrices to the reference, keeping the integrable values and exact generator tensors and retaining the boundary totally real planes. The common exact end tensors remain fixed. Along each given actual map this produces a norm-continuous Fredholm operator homotopy, so [F1]'s finite-block local constancy preserves its fixed-map index. The same contraction applies to every limit component. The disk and sphere bundle classes in the resulting computation are therefore pullbacks of the SAME complex bundle $TX$; their indices have not been replaced by a different formal index. On a boundary edge total reality is retained because its torus is Lagrangian for the tame form. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The compactness in [F3] gives an actual topological cutting description, as follows. Fix large but finite smooth cores on ALL stable polygon vertices, on nonconstant strip levels and on the nonconstant bubbles, with small coordinate disks about their attachment points. Retain every constant stable polygon with its labelled real boundary planes and specified corner caps. By no-neck image collapse, every remaining connecting annulus or half-annulus has image in one contractible target chart; on a boundary neck use a chart straightening its one torus to $\mathbb R^m$. Smooth convergence on the core boundary circles makes the sequence and the limiting core maps uniformly close there. In these charts join them by straight interpolation, with a cutoff supported near the corresponding cuts. This is an actual homotopy of maps and keeps the boundary on its assigned torus; along a transverse-corner end use the exact product chart which straightens both tori. On each intervening annulus contract the map to its matching constant value, keeping the corrected boundary loops and using the same interpolation on its two collars. A loop may first be filled in that chart, so no nontrivial target homotopy class is discarded. Perform this finitely many times from the leaves of the bubble tree inward. It identifies the sequence's pullback bundle and real boundary condition, up to actual relative homotopy, with the bundles of the limiting component cores connected by trivial matching tubes. A constant compact sphere has zero determinant Chern degree, and a constant one-torus disk has constant real boundary plane and compact index $m$, hence zero disk insertion index. Such redundant bubble ghosts can be contracted or retained with zero contribution. A removable constant transverse strip has its exact invertible product operator and index zero, so deleting it changes no fixed-map index. In contrast a constant STABLE POLYGON keeps its boundary planes and corner caps: triviality of its complex bundle does not make their capped index zero, and this vertex is retained in the remaining tree. There is no assertion that an arbitrary map has an embedded incidence representative. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Compute a boundary disk insertion in this description. Use a complex frame on its compact disk and an oriented frame on its real boundary. Cut a short boundary attachment interval inside the straight chart from step2.1, where both frames can be made constant by a homotopy on that contractible interval. Glue the complementary real boundary loop into the base component's boundary, with the positive source boundary orientation. Squaring its determinant makes this a literal product of the old boundary loop and the inserted loop. The new squared winding minus the old one is exactly the inserted winding, namely $\operatorname{ind}P_b-m$ by [F2]. Common caps at all unchanged punctures cancel by [F1]; hence the change in the actual finite-ended index is $\nu_b$. For a sphere choose frames on its two hemispheres, constant at its attachment disk. Their determinant transition on the equator has winding $\langle c_1(TX),[c]\rangle$ by the clutch definition of the first Chern number. Inserting this sphere changes the squared determinant clutch by twice that winding, with the positive sphere orientation. The same common-cap excision therefore changes the actual index by $\sigma_c$. These computations use the full determinant line, so ramification or containment of a bubble in the diagonal causes no missing coordinate contribution. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Delete bubble trees successively using step3.1. The remaining polygon/strip tree, including ALL its stable constant polygon vertices, has actual fixed-map index the sum of its component indices by [F1]'s unconditional node matching. This proves the first formula. For the balanced target use its actual finite cut-ball surface polygon from [F4], not an assumed homotopy classification. Bubble positivity and degree-energy identities give nonnegative integer component coefficients for a one-torus disk; a nonconstant disk has at least one positive coefficient because zero domain has zero energy. Its index contribution is therefore $2\sum b_i\ge2$ by [F4]. A sphere has a constant nonnegative incidence coefficient on the connected surface; coefficient zero again gives zero energy. Thus a nonconstant sphere has positive integral degree and contribution at least $2k$ by [F4]. No assumption of generic simplicity or regularity of a bubble was used in this budget. [F1, F3, F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Finally include the genuine source parameters. For a stable tree, [F3] gives the original conformal dimension as the sum of the stable vertex conformal dimensions plus the number of stable-source edges. For a tree with inserted strip levels, each such NONCONSTANT strip has its unquotiented operator index and loses one actual translation in its component count; recovering its position supplies one additional node coordinate. In a connected finite tree the number of nodes is the number of vertices minus one, so these contributions give precisely the count-index formula stated. A sphere or disk bubble does not replace any original boundary puncture and its removed compact bundle contribution has already been computed in step3.1, not counted a second time as a conformal parameter. A common external parameter must be added once to the whole operator; adding it independently at every vertex would repeat the same domain variable. Equivalently attach that parameter to one component before applying the finite-column node formula of [F1]. A constant stable polygon still has its ordinary source conformal parameters and actual fixed-map index; no regularity or nonnegativity is inferred for that vertex. This explains the auxiliary-parameter convention and finishes the actual index budget. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
