## Statement

Let $k$ be a nonarchimedean local field, $\mathfrak o$ its valuation ring, $\varpi$ a uniformizer, $q=|\mathfrak o/\varpi\mathfrak o|$, $G_v=\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)$ and $K_v=\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathfrak o)$. Let a group containing $G_v$ act on a measure space $X$ by measure-preserving translations $U(g)$, with $U(g)U(h)=U(gh)$. Let $\mathcal A$ be a translation-stable complex vector space of measurable scalar functions containing $L^1(X)$.

Supply a finite list of finite-dimensional complex vector spaces $W_j$ and invertible operators $Z_j$ with $(Z_j-z_j)^{r_j}=0$, where $r_j\ge1$ and $|z_j|=q^{-2}$. The list may be empty. For upper triangular $b$ with diagonal entries $a,d$, put $\nu(b)=\operatorname{ord}_v(a/d)$; this is well-defined on $\mathrm{PGL}_2(k)$. Define $I(W_j)$ to be the space of locally constant $W_j$-valued functions on $G_v$ satisfying
$$F(bg)=Z_j^{\nu(b)}F(g).$$
Its action is right translation, with no normalized-induction factor. Supply a $G_v$-equivariant linear map
$$\beta:\mathcal A\longrightarrow\bigoplus_j I(W_j),\qquad \ker\beta\subset L^1(X).$$
The map and its kernel condition are hypotheses, not assertions about automorphic forms. Let $\mathcal P$ be any family of $K_v$-fixed functions in $\mathcal A$. Let $\Gamma$ be a subgroup commuting elementwise with $G_v$.

Choose residue representatives $b\in\mathfrak o/\varpi\mathfrak o$ and put
$$h_b=\begin{pmatrix}\varpi&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\quad h_\infty=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&\varpi\end{pmatrix},\quad T=\sum_b U(h_b)+U(h_\infty),\quad d_v=q+1.$$
Set
$$\lambda_j=qz_j+z_j^{-1},\qquad P(t)=\prod_j\big((t-\lambda_j)(t-\overline{\lambda_j})\big)^{r_j},\qquad D=P(T).$$
Then $T$ is the unnormalized spherical Hecke action on $K_v$-fixed functions. On all of $L^1(X)$ it has norm at most $d_v$ and integral multiplier $d_v$. The operator $D$ is convolution by an explicitly finite real signed atomic measure supported in $G_v$, commutes with $\Gamma$, and has nonzero integral multiplier $P(d_v)$. Every $p\in\mathcal P$ satisfies $Dp\in L^1(X)$, with the same $D$ for the entire family. Consequently $P(d_v)^{-1}\int_X Dp$ is defined for all these functions and their $\Gamma$-translates and extends ordinary integration on $L^1$. No positivity is claimed.

There is also the following common-place assertion. In a restricted product with infinitely many finite-place factors and specified compact open groups $K_v$, a compact set $C$ and finitely many vectors fixed by open subgroups impose nontrivial local conditions at only finitely many places. After excluding these places and any supplied finite set of ramified boundary data, one can choose $v$ for which every element of $C$ has component in $K_v$ and all the vectors are $K_v$-fixed. In particular, at that place the products of translates of the vectors by elements of $C$ are $K_v$-fixed. If the boundary-map hypotheses above hold at that place with one finite list for the entire family, the construction gives a common regularizer commuting with those translations. This assertion requires only that their $v$-components lie in $K_v$; it does not require trivial $v$-components.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The invariant measure, action, local field, finite generalized boundary modules, equivariant map with integrable kernel, and family in the Statement. In the common-place assertion the restricted product topology, infinitude of the place set, open stabilizers and finiteness of the excluded set are supplied. The automorphic construction of $\beta$ and its uniform finite list is not supplied by a citation.

[F1] [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]] gives complex linearity and closure of $L^1$ under finite linear combinations.

## Proof

**Given:** Exactly the data of the Statement.

1.1 The right cosets of $K_v$ in $K_v\operatorname{diag}(\varpi,1)K_v$ are $h_bK_v$ and $h_\infty K_v$. Indeed these cosets correspond to index-$q$ sublattices of $\mathfrak o^2$, equivalently to lines in $(\mathfrak o/\varpi)^2$. The columns of $h_b$ span the inverse image of the line generated by $(b,1)$, and those of $h_\infty$ the inverse image of the line generated by $(1,0)$. These are all $q+1$ lines and are distinct. An index-$q$ sublattice has elementary divisors $1,\varpi$: choose a lift of a nonzero residue vector on the line, extend it to an integral basis, and multiply the second basis vector by $\varpi$. This proves the asserted double-coset description. Scalar homotheties cause no further identifications among these lattices of equal index. Left multiplication by $K_v$ permutes these right cosets. Hence the displayed $T$ preserves $K_v$-fixed functions and is exactly the spherical Hecke action there, independently of representatives. On arbitrary functions it denotes this particular finite sum, not a bi-$K_v$-invariant measure. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Each point of a compact subset $C$ of a restricted product has an open restricted-product neighborhood whose components outside a finite set lie in $K_v$. Finitely many such neighborhoods cover $C$; the union of their exceptional sets is finite. An open stabilizer of each of the finitely many vectors contains a basic identity neighborhood with all $K_v$ factors outside a finite set, so the vector is fixed by each such $K_v$. Taking the finite union with the excluded ramification set leaves a place, since the index set is infinite. At that place $U(c)$ preserves a $K_v$-fixed vector for every $c\in C$: write its local component in $K_v$, where it acts trivially, and its remaining component away from $v$, where it commutes with $K_v$. Products and conjugates remain fixed. [given, choose, algebra]

2.1 Write $B$ for the upper triangular subgroup. One has $G_v=BK_v$: integral column operations send the bottom row of an invertible matrix to $(0,d)$, by first scaling it to a primitive integral row and then extending a primitive vector to an integral basis. Thus a right $K_v$-fixed function in $I(W_j)$ is determined by $w=F(1)$, and is $F(bk)=Z_j^{\nu(b)}w$. This is well-defined since $B\cap K_v$ has diagonal ratio a unit. Conversely every $w$ defines such a function. For the sum of right translations defining $T$, evaluation at $1$ gives $qZ_jw+Z_j^{-1}w$, since $\nu(h_b)=1$ and $\nu(h_\infty)=-1$. By step 1.1 its output is again $K_v$-fixed, so this calculation determines the whole output. The local boundary action is therefore $L_j=qZ_j+Z_j^{-1}$. This proves the local action formula with the unnormalized convention used here. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Set $N_j=Z_j-z_j$. The finite geometric series for $(z_j+N_j)^{-1}$ is valid because $N_j^{r_j}=0$. In particular $L_j-\lambda_j=N_j(q-z_j^{-1}Z_j^{-1})$, where the factors commute. Thus $(L_j-\lambda_j)^{r_j}=0$. This also includes logarithmic generalized terms: translation on polynomials of degree less than $r_j$ in a logarithmic coordinate is unipotent, since each finite difference lowers degree. An exponent with $\xi(\varpi)=z_j$ has translation $Z_j=z_j$ times that unipotent operator. No semisimplicity or deletion of repeated factors is justified or used. [step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 Since $|z_j|=q^{-2}$, the reverse triangle inequality yields $|\lambda_j|\ge q^2-q^{-1}>q+1$ for every integer $q\ge2$. The last inequality holds at $q=2$ and the difference $q^2-q-1-q^{-1}$ increases thereafter. Hence $\lambda_j\ne d_v$ and $\overline{\lambda_j}\ne d_v$. The polynomial $P$ has real coefficients, annihilates every $L_j$, and satisfies $P(d_v)=\prod_j|d_v-\lambda_j|^{2r_j}>0$. Including conjugate factors is harmless and ensures a real measure even when exponents are nonreal. Collisions between eigenvalues do not invalidate annihilation. [step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 Let $\eta=\sum_b\delta_{h_b}+\delta_{h_\infty}$. For $P(t)=\sum_{n=0}^N a_nt^n$, define $\mu=\sum_{n=0}^N a_n\eta^{*n}$, with $\eta^{*0}=\delta_1$. This is a finite real signed atomic measure, and the action convention gives $U(\mu)=P(T)=D$. Measure preservation implies $\|Tp\|_1\le d_v\|p\|_1$ and $\int Tp=d_v\int p$ for $p\in L^1$. By induction and [F1], $D$ maps $L^1$ to itself with norm at most $\sum_n|a_n|d_v^n$ and integral multiplier $\sum_n a_nd_v^n=P(d_v)$, also the total mass of $\mu$. All supports are finite and hence compact. [F1, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 For $p\in\mathcal P$, equivariance makes $\beta(p)$ right $K_v$-fixed, and $\beta(Dp)=P(T)\beta(p)$. Steps 2.1–4.1 show this is zero on every component, so the supplied kernel condition gives $Dp\in L^1$. Every $U(\gamma)$ with $\gamma\in\Gamma$ commutes with every summand of $T$, hence with $D$. Therefore $DU(\gamma)p=U(\gamma)Dp$ is integrable with the same integral. By step 5.1 the quotient $P(d_v)^{-1}\int Dp$ equals $\int p$ whenever $p\in L^1$. An empty module list means $\ker\beta=\mathcal A\subset L^1$ and $D=1$, consistently with all assertions. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, given, algebra]

7.1 The spherical $T$ commutes with $K_v$ on the $K_v$-fixed subspace, because both $U(k)T$ and $TU(k)$ there equal $T$ by step 1.1. It commutes on all functions with every away-from-$v$ translation. Consequently $D$ commutes with the translations in step 1.2 on the $K_v$-fixed subspace. This subspace is stable under $D$ and these translations. The argument of step 6.1 therefore applies on it. For compatibility with a scalar space containing all of $L^1$ as in the earlier expansion lemma, restrict instead to the subgroup of away-from-$v$ translations, and replace the support elements by their away-from-$v$ components; their actions on all sections and densities at issue coincide. This supplies full commutation on that scalar space without claiming the atomic $T$ commutes with arbitrary local translations. The common finite list and kernel condition give simultaneous integrability for the whole family. [step 1.1, step 6.1, step 1.2, given, algebra] ∎

## Application boundary

This construction proves the local Hecke action, generalized annihilation, nonzero mass, $L^1$ multiplier and common-place commutation. Its global application still requires an actual equivariant asymptotic map with integrable kernel and one finite list of generalized multiplicities for the mixed densities. That is a stronger requirement than assigning a numerical exponent to each separate function. No bound on the norm or conductor of the chosen place is asserted; no small-prime or amplifier-eigenvalue estimate is supplied. The real-part-one growth-killing measure of §4.3.7 is a different construction.

For the intended $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ quotient, a boundary module with exponent $\xi$ of real part two has $|\xi(\varpi)|=q^{-2}$. The sign of this exponent uses $|\varpi|=q^{-1}$ and unnormalized induction; inserting a normalized-induction factor would change the formula. The quotient measure, controlled-increase map, spherical boundary identification and uniform multiplicities remain application obligations. This lemma does not establish the parent subconvexity theorem or assert that those hypotheses already hold.
