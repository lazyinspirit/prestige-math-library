## Example

Let $k$ be any field and let $a\in k$. Let
$$ C=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\bigr)\subseteq\mathbb A^2_k $$
be the parabola, and let $P=(a,a^2)\in C(k)$ be the point corresponding to the
maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$ of $A=k[x,y]/(y-x^2)$. Then:

1. the Jacobian matrix of the defining equation $f=y-x^2$ at $P$ is the
   one-row matrix $(-2a,\,1)$, and the Jacobian-kernel isomorphism identifies
   the tangent space with its kernel,
   $$ T_PC\cong\ker(-2a,\,1)=\{(u,2au):u\in k\}=k\cdot(1,2a), $$
   a one-dimensional $k$-vector space; in the translated coordinates
   $(X-a,\,Y-a^2)=(u,v)$ this is the line $v=2au$, that is
   $Y-a^2=2a(X-a)$, the tangent line of the parabola at $P$;
2. the maximal ideal is principal, $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$, so the local
   parameter $t=\bar x-a$ generates $\mathfrak m$, and the class of $t$ is a
   $k$-basis of the cotangent space
   $$ C_PC=\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m}/(\mathfrak m A_{\mathfrak m})^2\cong\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong k, $$
   so that $T_PC\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(C_PC,k)\cong k$ is one-dimensional as
   well.

Both computations hold in every characteristic, including characteristic $2$,
where $(-2a,1)=(0,1)$, the kernel is $k\cdot(1,0)=\{(u,0)\}$, and the tangent
line is the horizontal line $Y=a^2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, an element $a\in k$, the polynomial rings $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$, the polynomial $f=y-x^2$, the principal ideal $I=(f)$, the quotient ring $A=k[x,y]/I$, the parabola $C=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq\mathbb A^2_k$, the point $P=(a,a^2)\in k^2$, and the evaluation map $\varepsilon:k[x,y]\to k$, $g\mapsto g(a,a^2)$.

[F1] [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]: $R[x]$ is the set of finitely supported coefficient functions with $(a+b)_n=a_n+b_n$ and $(ab)_n=\sum_{i+j=n}a_ib_j$, and the commutative-ring axioms hold, so products expand by distributivity and the iterated ring $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$ is a commutative ring.

[F2] [[def-monomials-multidegree-and-total-degree]]: every polynomial in $F[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ has a unique finite expansion $f=\sum_{\mathbf t}c_{\mathbf t}x^{\mathbf t}$ over finitely many multi-indices, each scalar $c_{\mathbf t}$ being the coefficient of the monomial $x^{\mathbf t}$ in exactly this expansion, so two polynomials are equal exactly when all their coefficients agree.

[F3] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: for a subset $S\subseteq R$, $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals of $R$ containing $S$, and for $a\in R$ the ideal $(\{a\})$ is written $(a)$ and is called **principal**.

[F4] [[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]: for two-sided ideals $I,J$ the product is $IJ=\{\sum_{k=1}^m i_kj_k:m\ge0,\ i_k\in I,\ j_k\in J\}$, the empty sum being $0$, so the product of two principal ideals is generated by the product of the generators.

[F5] [[def-jacobian-matrix-affine-algebraic-set]]: for an ideal with a specified finite generating list $f_1,\dots,f_r$ and a point $a$ satisfying $f(a)=0$ for every $f\in I$, the **Jacobian matrix at $a$** has rows $(\partial f_i/\partial t_j(a))$, with formal monomial derivatives whose integer coefficients are read in $k$, and it uses the actual scheme ideal.

[F6] [[thm-zariski-tangent-space-jacobian-kernel]]: for any field, ideal $I\subseteq k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}(k[t]/I)$, rational point $a\in X(k)$ and any finite generating list of $I$, the coordinate-velocity map gives a canonical $k$-linear isomorphism $T_aX\cong\ker J(a)$, independent of the chosen generating list, with no reducedness, perfectness or characteristic hypothesis.

[F7] [[lem-evaluation-ideal-is-maximal]]: for $a\in k^n$ the evaluation map $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to k$ has kernel $(x_1-a_1,\ldots,x_n-a_n)$, which is a maximal ideal.

[F8] [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]: a ring homomorphism whose kernel contains a two-sided ideal factors uniquely through the quotient ring.

[F9] [[thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]]: ring maps $A\to B$ correspond contravariantly to morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, so $k$-algebra homomorphisms $A\to k$ are the $k$-rational points of $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

[F10] [[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]: for a commutative ring $R$ and a monic $g\in R[x]$, every $f\in R[x]$ has unique $q,r\in R[x]$ with $f=qg+r$ and $r=0$ or $\deg r<\deg g$.

[F11] [[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family]]: a ring homomorphism $\varphi:R\to S$ and a family $(s_i)_{i\in I}$ in $S$ determine a unique ring homomorphism $R[x_i:i\in I]\to S$ restricting to $\varphi$ and sending $x_i\mapsto s_i$.

[F12] [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]: if $R$ is an integral domain, then $R[x]$ is an integral domain.

[F13] [[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]]: for every ring homomorphism $f:R\to S$ one has $R/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f$, so a surjective homomorphism with kernel $J$ presents $R/J$ as its image.

[F14] [[def-zariski-cotangent-space-point]]: the intrinsic cotangent space of a scheme $X$ at a point $x$ is $C_xX:=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_x$ of the local ring $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ modulo its square, a vector space over the residue field $\kappa(x)$.

[F15] [[lem-cotangent-localization-at-rational-point]]: for a commutative $k$-algebra $A$ and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ with $A/\mathfrak m=k$ via the structure map, the canonical maps $\theta_r:\mathfrak m^r/\mathfrak m^{r+1}\to\mathfrak n^r/\mathfrak n^{r+1}$ with $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$ are isomorphisms; at $r=1$ this is the localization comparison for the intrinsic cotangent space.

[F16] [[def-zariski-tangent-space-point]]: the intrinsic Zariski tangent space is the dual $T_xX:=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(C_xX,\kappa(x))$, and at a $k$-rational point it agrees with the relative tangent space over $k$.

[F17] [[def-ring-characteristic]]: the characteristic of $R$ is the least $n\ge1$ with $n\cdot1_R=0_R$, and $0$ when there is none, so $\operatorname{char}k=2$ means precisely that $2\cdot1_k=1+1=0$.

[F18] [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: every field is a commutative ring with $1\ne0$ and is an integral domain.






## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Setup: $k$ is a field and $a\in k$; the iterated ring $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$ is a commutative ring by [F1]; the polynomial $f=y-x^2$ is nonzero because in its unique expansion [F2] the coefficient of $y$ is $1\ne0$ in $k$ by [F18]; $I=(f)$ is the principal ideal generated by $f$ in the sense of [F3], $A=k[x,y]/I$ is the quotient ring, $C=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq\mathbb A^2_k$ is the corresponding closed subscheme, and the point $P=(a,a^2)$ satisfies $f(P)=a^2-a^2=0$; the evaluation map $\varepsilon:k[x,y]\to k$, $g\mapsto g(a,a^2)$ has kernel $(x-a,y-a^2)$ by [F7]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F7, F18, algebra]

2.1 The substitution homomorphism and the structure of $A$: by [F11] with the coefficient map $k\to k[x]$ and assigned images $x\mapsto x$, $y\mapsto x^2$ there is a unique $k$-algebra homomorphism $\sigma:k[x,y]\to k[x]$ with $\sigma(x)=x$ and $\sigma(y)=x^2$, and $\sigma$ is surjective because it is the identity on the subring $k[x]$; for an arbitrary $g\in k[x,y]=k[x][y]$, division by the monic polynomial $f=y-x^2$ of degree one in $y$ over the commutative ring $k[x]$ gives, by [F10], unique $q,r\in k[x,y]$ with $g=qf+r$ and $r=0$ or $\deg_y r<1$, so that $r\in k[x]$, and applying $\sigma$ gives $\sigma(g)=r$ because $\sigma$ fixes $k[x]$ pointwise and $\sigma(f)=x^2-x^2=0$; hence $g\in\ker\sigma$ if and only if $r=0$, if and only if $g\in(f)=I$, so $\ker\sigma=I$ and by [F13] the quotient $A=k[x,y]/I$ is isomorphic to $k[x]$ as a $k$-algebra, with $\bar x\mapsto x$ and $\bar y\mapsto x^2$. [step 1.1, F10, F11, F13, algebra]

3.1 Consequences for $A$: $k$ is an integral domain by [F18], so $k[x]$ is an integral domain by [F12] and hence $A\cong k[x]$ is an integral domain; the element $t=\bar x-a\in A$ is nonzero, because its image $x-a$ in $k[x]$ has $x$-coefficient $1\ne0$ in the unique expansion [F2]; therefore $t$ is a nonzerodivisor in $A$, so $tc=0$ with $c\in A$ forces $c=0$. [step 2.1, F2, F12, F18, algebra]

4.1 The point $P$ and its maximal ideal: since $f(P)=0$ by step 1.1, the polynomial $f$ lies in the kernel $(x-a,y-a^2)$ of $\varepsilon$, so [F8] factors $\varepsilon$ uniquely through the quotient as a $k$-algebra homomorphism $\delta:A\to k$; $\delta$ is surjective because $\varepsilon$ is, so by [F13] one has $A/\mathfrak m\cong k$ for $\mathfrak m=\ker\delta$, and $\mathfrak m$ is the image of $(x-a,y-a^2)$ in $A$, namely $(\bar x-a,\bar y-a^2)$; in $A$ one has $\bar y=\bar x^2$, so distributing over the difference [F1] gives $\bar y-a^2=\bar x^2-a^2=(\bar x-a)(\bar x+a)$, and hence $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$ is the principal ideal generated by $t=\bar x-a$ in the sense of [F3]; by [F9] the $k$-algebra homomorphism $\delta$ exhibits $P$ as the $k$-rational point of $C$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F1, F3, F8, F9, F13, algebra]

5.1 The cotangent space $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$: with $t=\bar x-a$ and $\mathfrak m=(t)$, the product ideal $\mathfrak m^2=\mathfrak m\cdot\mathfrak m$ equals $(t^2)$ by [F4]; the multiplication map $\mu:A/(t)\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, $[c]\mapsto[ct]$, is well defined because $c\in(t)$ implies $ct\in(t^2)=\mathfrak m^2$, is surjective because $\mathfrak m=(t)$ consists exactly of the elements $ct$, and is injective because $ct=t^2d$ for some $d\in A$ gives $t(c-td)=0$, which the domain $A$ with $t\ne0$ of step 3.1 turns into $c=td\in(t)$; hence $\mu$ is an isomorphism of $k$-vector spaces, and since the surjective map $\delta:A\to k$ of step 4.1 has kernel $(t)$, [F13] gives $A/(t)\cong k$, so that $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong k$ is one-dimensional with $k$-basis the class $[t]$ of the local parameter $t=\bar x-a$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F4, F13, algebra]

5.2 The Jacobian matrix and the tangent space: $I=(y-x^2)$ has the finite generating list $(f)$, and every element of $I$ is a multiple of $f$ with $f(P)=0$, so the Jacobian matrix of this list at $P=(a,a^2)$ is the $1\times2$ matrix of formal partial derivatives $(\partial f/\partial x,\partial f/\partial y)$ evaluated at $P$ by [F5]; since $\partial f/\partial x=-2x$ and $\partial f/\partial y=1$, the matrix is $(-2a,\,1)$ with the integer $2$ read in $k$, and a vector $(u,v)\in k^2$ lies in its kernel exactly when $-2au+v=0$, that is $v=2au$, so $\ker J=\{(u,2au):u\in k\}=k\cdot(1,2a)$ is one-dimensional because the first coordinate $1$ of $(1,2a)$ is nonzero in $k$ by [F18]; by [F6] the coordinate-velocity map gives a canonical $k$-linear isomorphism $T_PC\cong\ker J$, hence $T_PC\cong\{(u,2au):u\in k\}$; in the translated coordinates $(X-a,Y-a^2)=(u,v)$ the tangent line of the parabola at $P$ is therefore $v=2au$, that is $Y-a^2=2a(X-a)$, Milne's equation (20) for $F=Y-X^2$; if the characteristic is $2$, so that $2=0$ in $k$ by [F17], the same formula gives the row $(0,1)$, the kernel $k\cdot(1,0)=\{(u,0)\}$, and the horizontal tangent line $Y=a^2$. [step 4.1, F5, F6, F17, F18, algebra]

6.1 Intrinsic cotangent space and duality: the local ring of $C$ at $P$ is $\mathcal O_{C,P}=A_{\mathfrak m}$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n=\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$, and the intrinsic cotangent space is $C_PC=\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2$ by [F14]; the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ satisfies $A/\mathfrak m=k$ via the structure map by step 4.1, so [F15] with $r=1$ applies and its canonical map $\theta_1:\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2$ is an isomorphism, giving $C_PC\cong\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong k$ with the class of the local parameter $t$ as a $k$-basis; by [F16] the tangent space is the dual $T_PC=\operatorname{Hom}_k(C_PC,k)$, so $T_PC\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(k,k)\cong k$ is one-dimensional, in agreement with the kernel $k\cdot(1,2a)$ of step 5.2. [step 5.1, step 5.2, F14, F15, F16, algebra]

7.1 Conclusion: for the parabola $C=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x,y]/(y-x^2))$ over any field $k$ and every $a\in k$, at $P=(a,a^2)$ the tangent space is $T_PC\cong\{(u,2au):u\in k\}=k\cdot(1,2a)$, equivalently the tangent line $Y-a^2=2a(X-a)$ through $P$, and the maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$ is generated by the local parameter $\bar x-a$, whose class is a $k$-basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong C_PC\cong k$; both spaces are one-dimensional, in every characteristic, with no perfectness, algebraic-closedness or reducedness hypothesis. [step 5.2, step 6.1]

8.1 Boundary and scope dispositions: $C$ is nonempty because $P$ is a $k$-rational point of it by step 4.1, $k$ is nonempty since it is a field with $0\ne1$ by [F18], and the cotangent space $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong k$ is nonzero by step 5.1, so no object here is empty and the case of an empty instance does not arise; the zero cases are the vertex $a=0$, where the tangent space is $k\cdot(1,0)=\{(u,0)\}$ and the tangent line is $Y=0$, the vanishing $f(P)=0$ of step 1.1, the vanishing of the entries $-2a$ and $2a$ when $a=0$, and the nonzero class $[t]$ of step 5.1; there is one equation, one generator of $I$, one Jacobian row, one local parameter $t$ generating $\mathfrak m$, one basis class $[t]$ and one point $P$, and the tangent space has dimension one rather than zero; the degenerate case is characteristic $2$, where $(-2a,1)=(0,1)$, $T_PC=k\cdot(1,0)$ and the formula $v=2au$ collapses to $v=0$, and since no characteristic hypothesis is used or needed elsewhere, all fields and all $a$ are covered; the endpoints of the parameter are $a=0$, the vertex, where the tangent is horizontal, and the values with $2a\ne0$, where it is not, while the degree endpoints of $f=y-x^2$ are its least degree $1$ and top degree $2$, differentiated in step 5.2 to the constant $1$ and the linear polynomial $-2x$; the two directions of the membership equivalence $\ker\sigma=I$ are proved in step 2.1, the forward one because $\sigma(g)=0$ forces $r=0$ and hence $g=qf\in I$, and the reverse one because $g=hf$ gives $\sigma(g)=\sigma(h)\sigma(f)=0$, while both inclusions of the ideal equality $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$ are proved in step 4.1, since $t$ is the first generator and conversely every element of $(x-a,y-a^2)$ is $t(a_1+(\bar x+a)a_2)$, so the two iff axes are checked rather than vacuous; no Axiom of Choice or dependent choice is used, since $a$, $f$, the substitution $\sigma$, the division, the class $[t]$ and the Jacobian row are all exhibited explicitly and every cited supplier is choice-free, with no def-axiom-of-choice dependency declared. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, F18] ∎

## Source qualification

Milne, *Algebraic Geometry* v6.10, §4a (printed pp. 81–82) defines the tangent
space $T_PV$ of a plane curve $V:F(X,Y)=0$ at a point $P$ to be the algebraic
subset cut out by equation (20),
$\frac{\partial F}{\partial X}(a,b)(X-a)+\frac{\partial F}{\partial Y}(a,b)(Y-b)=0$,
which is a line through $P$ unless both partials vanish, and works with a
nonconstant $F$ whose ideal is assumed radical. For the parabola, $F=Y-X^2$
gives $-2a(X-a)+(Y-a^2)=0$, that is $Y-a^2=2a(X-a)$, the line whose direction
space in the translated coordinates $(u,v)=(X-a,Y-a^2)$ is
$\{(u,2au)\}$; the item states both the scheme-theoretic tangent space of the
library (the dual of the cotangent space, a space of velocity vectors) and this
affine tangent line. Milne's curves are classical varieties over an
algebraically closed field with $I(V)=(F)$ assumed radical; the item works with
the actual scheme ideal $I=(y-x^2)$ in $k[x,y]$ over an arbitrary field, and
the two readings coincide here because $A=k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\cong k[x]$ is a
domain, so $(f)$ is prime and radical and no reduction by the nilradical is
needed. Examples 4.2–4.5 of the same section assume $\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2,3$;
the item computes the Jacobian row $(-2a,1)$ in every characteristic and shows
that only the geometric reading $v=2au$ degenerates when $2=0$, the tangent
line becoming $Y=a^2$.

Arapura, *Notes on Basic Algebraic Geometry* §5.1 (printed pp. 34–35) gives in
Exercise 5.1.1 the $\varepsilon$-expansion
$f(a_1+b_1\varepsilon,\ldots)=f(a)+\sum_ib_i\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}(a)\varepsilon$
and in Theorem 5.1.2 the identification of $T_aX$ with the kernel of the
Jacobian matrix; this is the route of the scaffold citation and of the
library's [[thm-zariski-tangent-space-jacobian-kernel]] used in the item. For
the local-parameter and cotangent statement, Arapura Lemma 5.2.2 records
$T_a\cong(\mathfrak m_a/\mathfrak m_a^2)^*$ with Exercise 5.2.3 constructing
the isomorphism from a linear map killing $\mathfrak m_a^2$, and Milne §4f
item 4.30(d) records the canonical isomorphism
$\mathfrak m_P/\mathfrak m_P^2\to\mathfrak n_P/\mathfrak n_P^2$ between the
maximal-ideal quotients of the coordinate ring and of the local ring. The item
derives the local-parameter statement from the substitution isomorphism
$k[x,y]/(y-x^2)\cong k[x]$, exhibits the class $[\bar x-a]$ as a $k$-basis of
$\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, and passes to the local ring with the library's
localization lemma [[lem-cotangent-localization-at-rational-point]]; the
scaffold's phrase "the local parameter $x-a$ generates $\mathfrak m$" is made
precise as the principal ideal equality $\mathfrak m=(\bar x-a)$ in
$A=k[x,y]/(y-x^2)$ together with the basis statement in the cotangent space,
and no Lie-theoretic or higher-order structure is claimed.
