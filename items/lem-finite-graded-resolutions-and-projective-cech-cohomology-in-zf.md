## Statement

Work in ZF. Let $k$ be any field, $N\ge0$, $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_N]$ with its usual grading, and $M$ a finitely generated graded $S$-module. It has a finite resolution
$$0\to F_{N+1}\to\cdots\to F_0\to M\to0,\qquad F_j=\bigoplus_a S(-a)^{\beta_{j,a}},$$
with finite sums and degree-zero maps. The finite standard-projective-chart Cech complex of $\widetilde M(t)$ computes finite-dimensional, cover-independent cohomology groups $H^q$; they vanish for $q>N$. For all integers $t$,
$$\chi(\widetilde M(t))=\sum_{j,a}(-1)^j\beta_{j,a}\binom{t-a+N}{N}.$$
For sufficiently large $t$, every positive cohomology group is zero and the natural map $M_t\to H^0(\widetilde M(t))$ is an isomorphism.

More generally every coherent sheaf $F$ on a specified closed projective $k$-scheme $X\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_k$, expressed by finite affine presentations and their finite gluing, is $\widetilde M$ on the ambient projective space for such a finite graded module. Its cohomology is finite, large twists are generated by finitely many global sections with positive cohomology zero, and its Euler characteristic is the corresponding rational polynomial for every integer twist. These groups and the polynomial commute with any field extension. The actual affine section interpretation and finite affine Cech comparison are proved here for finite-type field charts and their principal localizations. No enumeration of $k$, general unenumerated Noetherian-base comparison or injective-resolution construction is asserted or used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The arbitrary field, finite variable list, finite graded module or finite coherent affine presentation data, and specified projective embedding in the Statement.

[F1] Finite-variable polynomial algebras over a field are Noetherian by finite generators, with a choice-free proof. [[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct]]

[F2] The Koszul complex of a finite regular sequence on a finite free module is a finite free resolution of the quotient. [[cor-koszul-complex-resolves-a-regular-quotient]]

[F3] Localization of modules preserves short exact sequences. [[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]]

[F4] Every nonzero finite-type algebra over a field is finite over a polynomial subalgebra. [[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]]

## Proof

1.1 We first establish the actual finite-type affine interpretation, without a prime-existence choice principle. For a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra $A$, use [F4] to make it finite over $R=k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. The quotient by $(z_1,\ldots,z_d)A$ is nonzero: otherwise the determinant trick on a finite $R$-generating list produces an element of $1+(z_1,\ldots,z_d)$ annihilating the faithful $R$-module $A$, impossible. This quotient is finite-dimensional over $k$. A nonzero quotient of least positive vector-space dimension is a field. Thus every proper ideal of a finite-type $k$-algebra is contained in a maximal ideal by applying this argument to its nonzero quotient. The same holds on principal localizations, which remain finite type. Also $D(g)\subseteq D(f)$ forces $f$ to be a unit in $A_g$: otherwise a maximal ideal containing it there would give a point of $D(g)$ outside $D(f)$. Thus the principal-open restriction maps are the genuine localization maps. It follows that a covering family of principal opens has a finite unit-ideal witness: otherwise the generated proper ideal lies in an uncovered maximal ideal. For any module $Q$, its fraction presheaf on principal opens is a sheaf with sections $Q_f$. To check it, refine a cover by all subordinate principal opens and take a finite unit witness. For a finite cover, clear its finitely many fraction denominators and overlap discrepancies; an identity $1=\sum_i a_if_i^m$ glues compatible fractions by $q=\sum_i a_if_i^m q_i$ and also proves uniqueness. In positive Cech degrees the same calculation gives the contraction $b_J=\sum_i a_if_i^m c_{iJ}$ for a localization cocycle, after increasing $m$ to clear its finitely many identities. This proves affine section recovery and affine Cech acyclicity for finite-type field charts, without enumerating the field or selecting lifts on an infinite cover. [F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 By [F1], a submodule of a finite free $S$-module is finitely generated: prove this by induction on the rank, projecting to its first coordinate ideal and finitely lifting its generators; the remaining kernel lies in a free module of smaller rank. Submodules and quotients of a finite module are finite by taking a finite free presentation and preimages. Graded kernels admit finite homogeneous generating lists by replacing each of finitely many generators with its finitely many homogeneous components. A finite graded module is bounded below in degree, and its quotient by $m=(x_0,\ldots,x_N)$ is a finite-dimensional graded $k$-space. Lift a finite homogeneous basis of that quotient. These lifts generate: their cokernel $Q$ satisfies $Q=mQ$, and a nonzero homogeneous element of least possible degree in $Q$ would express itself using elements of lower degree, a contradiction. This proves the graded Nakayama assertion needed here without a local-ring regularity theorem. A resulting minimal graded free cover $F_0\to M$ has kernel contained in $mF_0$. Repeat on the kernel a specified finite number of times, through $F_{N+3}$, obtaining an augmented complex exact through degree $N+2$, with every differential landing in $m$ times the preceding free module. Only finitely many bases and lifts are chosen. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 The variables form an $S$-regular sequence: after each initial quotient the next variable acts injectively on a polynomial ring. Thus their Koszul complex $K$ is a free resolution of $k$ of length $N+1$ by [F2]. Compare the two finite complexes in $K\otimes_S F_\bullet$ from step 2.1. In the $K$ direction, each $F_p$ is free, so the homology is $k\otimes_SF_p$ in degree zero. In the other direction the augmented $F$ complex is exact through degree $N+2$, and tensoring with each finite free $K_q$ preserves that exactness. Hence in total degrees at most $N+2$ the same total-complex homology is that of $K\otimes_SM$. This finite comparison can be obtained by eliminating exact rows, or the bounded filtration kernel/image chase; no infinite resolution has been selected. In total degree $N+2$ the latter homology is zero, since $K$ has length $N+1$. On the former complex every differential is zero modulo $m$, so that homology is $F_{N+2}/mF_{N+2}$. It vanishes; step 2.1 makes $F_{N+2}=0$. Exactness through $N+1$ now proves that $F_0,\ldots,F_{N+1}$ is the stated finite resolution. The extra finite stages were used precisely so that truncation creates no spurious top homology in this comparison. [F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Compute the standard chart Cech complex for $O(t)$. Its term on a nonempty index set $I$ is the degree-$t$ part of $S[(\prod_{i\in I}x_i)^{-1}]$. Split its monomial basis by exponent vectors $u\in\mathbb Z^{N+1}$ with $\sum u_i=t$, and put $A(u)=\{i:u_i<0\}$. The coefficient complex admits exactly those $I$ with $A(u)\subseteq I$. If $A(u)$ is nonempty and proper, toggle the least vertex outside it, with its alternating sign; the two-deletion cancellation gives $\delta h+h\delta=1$. If $A(u)$ is empty the same contraction on the augmented simplex leaves one copy of $k$ in degree zero. If it is every vertex, the only term is one copy of $k$ in degree $N$. All operations preserve finite monomial support, so the direct-sum contractions use only finite lifts for any given cochain. For $N\ge1$ this gives $H^0(O(t))$ with basis the nonnegative monomials of degree $t$, and $H^N(O(t))$ with basis the all-negative monomials of degree $t$; no other groups occur. These sets are finite. Their dimensions are respectively $\binom{t+N}{N}$ for $t\ge0$ and $\binom{-t-1}{N}$ for $t\le-N-1$, with zero elsewhere. The alternating Euler value is the polynomial $\binom{t+N}{N}$ for every integer $t$. If $N=0$, the one chart has one monomial of every integer degree, giving one copy of $k$ in degree zero for all $t$, and that same polynomial is $1$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Localizing the resolution of step 3.1 and taking degree zero is exact by [F3] and the direct-sum grading. Hence it is a finite resolution of $\widetilde M$ on every standard chart and overlap by the finite sums of twists $\widetilde F_j$. Use the finite double complex of their standard-chart Cech complexes. Its augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism to the Cech complex of $\widetilde M$, by exactness of those rows. Each column has finite-dimensional cohomology by step 4.1. A finite filtration consequently gives finite-dimensional total cohomology and the alternating Euler sum of the column Euler characteristics, proving the displayed polynomial. The original Cech complex has no terms above $N$, so neither does its cohomology. For sufficiently large $t$, every summand $O(t-a)$ in the finite resolution has nonnegative twist, so all its positive groups vanish. Its global sections are exactly the degree-$t$ parts of $F_j$, by step 4.1. Their complex resolves $M_t$, proving both positive-cohomology vanishing and the natural $H^0=M_t$ identification. These identifications include the zero module and resolutions with empty free summands. [F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Now start with finite coherent chart data for $F$ on the specified closed projective $X$. Regard its chart modules as modules over the ambient polynomial chart rings through their finite quotient presentations. They and their kernels remain finitely presented by [F1] and the module argument of step 2.1. A chart generator extends to a global section after a large twist: on each of the finitely many other charts, its overlap image is a fraction with denominator a power of the first chart coordinate. Clear these finitely many denominators. The discrepancies on pair overlaps vanish after again inverting that coordinate, so a common further power kills all the finitely many discrepancies. The corrected sections glue by step 1.1, with the standard $O(t)$ transition factors. Doing this for the finite chart-generator list gives a surjection $E_0=\bigoplus O(-a)\to F$. Its kernel has finite coherent chart data by step 2.1 and [F3]. Repeating once gives a surjection $E_1=\bigoplus O(-b)\to\ker(E_0\to F)$; the shifts $b$ may be taken larger than every $a$. By step 4.1 every component $O(-b)\to O(-a)$ is a homogeneous polynomial of degree $b-a$: it is a section of $O(b-a)$. Therefore this map is the sheafification of a map between finite graded free $S$-modules. Its finite graded cokernel $M$ has sheafification exactly $F$, by exact chart localization. This constructs the asserted module, rather than presuming an arbitrary-base sheaf-to-graded-module equivalence. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Any two finite affine covers in this finite-type field category give canonically the same cohomology. Their intersections are quasi-compact and have finite principal refinements, by the finite unit-cover argument in step 1.1. Apply the finite double Cech complex of a common such refinement. On every affine intersection its positive comparison rows contract by step 1.1 and its degree-zero part is the original module. Eliminating these finite acyclic rows in either order gives the canonical refinement isomorphisms; a third refinement proves compatibility. Finite short exact sequences of the chart modules give termwise exact Cech complexes by [F3], so the elementary cycle-lift-and-differentiate construction gives their long exact sequences. Thus the groups in step 5.1 are actual intrinsic finite affine Cech cohomology of the supplied coherent sheaf, with additive Euler characteristic, without identifying them through a chosen injective resolution. For sufficiently large twists the finite module construction of step 6.1 and step 5.1 give vanishing. Also $F(t)$ is finitely globally generated: the images of the finitely many degree-$t-a$ monomials in $E_0(t)$ generate on every chart when $t\ge\max a$. [F3, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For any field extension $k'/k$, the chart localizations, polynomial matrices and all their alternating restriction maps base change to their corresponding data over $k'$. Tensoring any exact $k$-vector-space sequence with $k'$ is exact without an infinite basis choice: every tensor relation uses only a finite-dimensional coefficient subspace of $k'$, choose its finite basis, and apply coordinatewise injectivity. Thus kernels, images and cohomology of these finite Cech complexes commute with the field extension. Finite dimensions, their alternating Euler polynomial and the same finite resolution formula are preserved. The case $N=0$ is explicit in step 4.1, negative twists remain included in the polynomial calculation, and all zero data use the zero complexes. The only prime-existence argument was the finite-type-field calculation in step 1.1; no result for an arbitrary unenumerated Noetherian base, injective resolution or infinite succession of module covers has been used. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
