## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice.

Let $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ be a sigma-finite measure space whose sigma-algebra is
countably generated. Then there is a countable algebra
$\mathcal A_0 \subseteq \mathcal A$ such that for every $E \in \mathcal A$ with
$\mu(E) < \infty$ and every $\varepsilon > 0$ there exists $A \in \mathcal A_0$
with

$$
\mu(E \triangle A) < \varepsilon.
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice, a sigma-finite measure space $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ with $\mathcal A$ countably generated.

[L1] A countable generator yields a countable algebra ([[lem-countable-generators-yield-countable-set-algebras]]).

[L2] Sigma-finite measures admit an increasing exhaustion $X_1 \subseteq X_2 \subseteq \cdots$ by measurable finite-measure sets ([[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]).

[L3] If two families contain one another inside generated sigma-algebras, then they generate the same sigma-algebra ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-comparison-criterion]], [[def-generated-sigma-algebra]]).

[L4] Continuity from below and the symmetric-difference measure calculus are available ([[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]], [[prop-measure-of-a-set-difference]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\mathcal G$ be a countable generator of $\mathcal A$, and choose a [L1, L2, L3, given, choose] sigma-finite exhaustion $(X_m)$ by [L2]. Apply [L1] to the countable family $\mathcal G \cup \{X_m : m \ge 1\}$ to obtain a countable algebra $\mathcal A_0 \subseteq \mathcal A$. Because $\mathcal G \subseteq \mathcal A_0 \subseteq \mathcal A$, [L3] gives $\sigma(\mathcal A_0)=\mathcal A$. [L1, L2, L3, given, choose]

1.2 Fix $m$ and let $\mathcal M_m$ be the family of measurable subsets [L4, algebra] $B \subseteq X_m$ for which every $\varepsilon > 0$ admits $A \in \mathcal A_0$ with $A \subseteq X_m$ and $\mu(B \triangle A) < \varepsilon$. Because $X_m \in \mathcal A_0$, the family $\mathcal M_m$ contains $\mathcal A_0 \cap X_m$. On the finite-measure space $X_m$, the class $\mathcal M_m$ is a sigma-algebra: complements are handled inside $X_m$, and increasing unions are handled by approximating one large stage and using continuity from below. Therefore $\mathcal M_m$ is a sigma-algebra containing the trace of $\mathcal A_0$, so it contains every measurable subset of $X_m$. [L4, algebra]

2.1 Now let $E \in \mathcal A$ with $\mu(E) < \infty$ and let $\varepsilon > 0$. [L4, choose, algebra] By [L4], choose $m$ so large that $\mu(E \setminus X_m) < \varepsilon/2$. Since $E \cap X_m$ is a measurable subset of $X_m$, the construction above yields $A \in \mathcal A_0$ with $A \subseteq X_m$ and $\mu((E \cap X_m) \triangle A) < \varepsilon/2$. Then $$ \mu(E \triangle A) \le \mu(E \setminus X_m) + \mu((E \cap X_m) \triangle A) < \varepsilon. $$ So finite-measure sets are approximable by members of the countable algebra $\mathcal A_0$. [L4, choose, algebra] ∎
