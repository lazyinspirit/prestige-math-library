## Statement

The map $(x,\mu)\mapsto\theta_x\mu$ from $\mathbb R^d$ times the evaluation-measurable space of locally finite counting measures to that space is measurable.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The evaluation sigma-algebra on locally finite counting measures and the recentering convention in [[def-spatial-recentering-action]].

[F1] A point process is a measurable random element into locally finite counting measures, and their sigma-algebra is generated by evaluations $\mu\mapsto\mu(B)$ for Borel $B$. ([[def-random-measure-and-locally-finite-point-process]])

[F2] A measure kernel has measurable evaluations, and parameterized integration of a nonnegative product-measurable function is measurable when the kernel has a common finite-mass exhaustion. ([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]], [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]])

[A1] AC and Countable Choice are carried by the transitive well-definedness dependencies of the point-process supplier; the evaluation calculation below uses neither. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** $d\ge1$, AC, Countable Choice, and a locally finite counting measure $\mu$ with its evaluation sigma-algebra.

1.1 Fix a Borel set $B\subseteq\mathbb R^d$. On parameter space $\mathbb R^d\times\mathcal N_{\rm lf}(\mathbb R^d)$ define the evaluation kernel $K((x,\mu),C)=\mu(C)$. Its evaluations are measurable by [F1]; the fixed compact exhaustion $[-n,n]^d$ has finite $K((x,\mu),\cdot)$-mass for every $(x,\mu)$ by local finiteness. The function $((x,\mu),y)\mapsto\mathbf1_B(y-x)$ is product measurable because subtraction is continuous and $B$ is Borel. [given, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 Applying [F2] to this kernel gives measurability of $$(x,\mu)\longmapsto\int\mathbf1_B(y-x)\,\mu(dy)=\mu(B+x)=(\theta_x\mu)(B).$$ Since this holds for every Borel $B$ and evaluations generate the target sigma-algebra [F1], $(x,\mu)\mapsto\theta_x\mu$ is measurable. [step 1.1, F1, F2]

3.1 The argument applies to every Borel evaluation and uses no selection; the AC and Countable Choice tags record only the carried supplier conventions [A1]. [A1, step 2.1] ∎
