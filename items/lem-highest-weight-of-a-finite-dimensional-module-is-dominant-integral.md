## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional irreducible
representation of $\mathfrak g$. Then the highest weight of $V$ is dominant
integral
([[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$ and a chosen base of simple roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ with coroots $h_{\alpha_i}$, and a nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible module $V$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory supplying [L1] and through [L2] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] For each simple root $\alpha_i$ there are $e_i\in\mathfrak g_{\alpha_i}$, $f_i\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}$ with $[e_i,f_i]=h_{\alpha_i}$, $[h_{\alpha_i},e_i]=2e_i$ and $[h_{\alpha_i},f_i]=-2f_i$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

[L2] $V$ contains a highest weight vector $v$ of some weight $\lambda$, and then $v$ generates $V$; in particular $e_i\cdot v=0$ and $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v=\lambda(h_{\alpha_i})v$ ([[lem-every-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representation-has-a-highest-weight-vector]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[L3] A finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module is a direct sum of irreducible submodules, and an irreducible submodule has a highest weight $m\ge0$ with respect to $h$, with eigenvalues $m,m-2,\dots,-m$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix a simple root $\alpha_i$, its triple $(e_i,f_i,h_{\alpha_i})$ from [L1], and a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$ generating $V$ as in [L2]; then $e_i\cdot v=0$ and $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v=m_iv$ with $m_i=\lambda(h_{\alpha_i})$. [A1, L1, L2]

2.1 Let $W=U(\operatorname{span}\{e_i,f_i,h_{\alpha_i}\})v$ be the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-submodule generated by $v$; it is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $V$, so $W$ is finite dimensional, and by [L3] it is a direct sum of irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-submodules. [L1, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Write the direct-sum decomposition from step 2.1 as $W=\bigoplus_{a=1}^sW_a$ and decompose $v=\sum_av_a$ accordingly.  Each $W_a$ is stable under $e_i$ and $h_{\alpha_i}$, so uniqueness of the direct sum and step 1.1 give $e_i\cdot v_a=0$ and $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v_a=m_iv_a$ for every $a$.  Since $v\ne0$, some component $v_a$ is nonzero.  That component is a highest weight vector of the irreducible module $W_a$ with highest weight $m_i$, so [L3] implies $m_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$. [L3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 The argument of steps 1.1–3.1 applies to every simple root, so $\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=m_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ for every $i$, which by definition means that the highest weight $\lambda$ is dominant integral. [step 3.1] ∎
