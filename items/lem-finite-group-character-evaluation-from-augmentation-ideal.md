## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group, let $\mathbb Z[G]$ have augmentation ideal
$I_G$, and let $G^{\mathrm{ab}}=G/[G,G]$, where $[G,G]$ is the subgroup
generated by commutators. For a homomorphism $\chi:G\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$,
define the $\mathbb Z$-linear map
$$
f_\chi:I_G\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,
\qquad
f_\chi\!\left(\sum_{g\in G}a_g[g]\right)=\sum_{g\in G}a_g\chi(g),
$$
where $\sum_g a_g=0$. Then $f_\chi$ is $G$-invariant, the map
$$
G^{\mathrm{ab}}\longrightarrow I_G/I_G^2,
\qquad \bar\sigma\longmapsto [\sigma]-[1],
$$
is an isomorphism, and the resulting evaluation is
$$
f_\chi([\sigma]-[1])=\chi(\sigma).
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite group $G$, its group ring, augmentation ideal $I_G$,
and a homomorphism $\chi:G\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$.

[F1] Every element of $\mathbb Z[G]$ has a unique finite expansion in the
basis $\{[g]:g\in G\}$, and $I_G$ consists of the sums whose coefficients
sum to zero; it is generated by $[g]-[1]$. [[def-augmentation-map-and-augmentation-ideal-of-a-group-ring]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify the augmentation ideal modulo its square and
evaluate the dual map on its generators.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Since $I_G$ is generated by $[g]-[1]$, the indicated map onto
$I_G/I_G^2$ is surjective. It is a homomorphism because
$$
[gh]-[1]-([g]-[1])-([h]-[1])=([g]-[1])([h]-[1])\in I_G^2;
$$
as the target is abelian, it factors through $G^{\mathrm{ab}}$. Define
$$
\psi:I_G\to G^{\mathrm{ab}},
\qquad
\psi\!\left(\sum_g a_g[g]\right)=\sum_g a_g\bar g,
$$
where the target abelian group is written additively. If $x,y\in I_G$, it
suffices to check a product of generators:
$$
\psi\bigl(([g]-[1])([h]-[1])\bigr)
=\overline{gh}-\bar g-\bar h+\bar 1=0.
$$
Thus $\psi$ vanishes on $I_G^2$ and descends to $I_G/I_G^2$. On every
generator, $\psi([g]-[1])=\bar g$, so the descended map is inverse to the
stated surjection. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The unique basis expansion in [F1] makes $f_\chi$ well-defined and
$\mathbb Z$-linear. For $h,g\in G$,
$$
h([g]-[1])=([hg]-[1])-([h]-[1]),
$$
so $f_\chi(h([g]-[1]))=\chi(hg)-\chi(h)=\chi(g)$. Since the differences
$[g]-[1]$ generate $I_G$, $f_\chi$ is $G$-invariant. Finally,
$f_\chi([\sigma]-[1])=\chi(\sigma)-\chi(1)=\chi(\sigma)$, proving the
evaluation formula. [F1, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
