## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $(S,\Sigma)$ be a measurable space. For each $j<n$, let
$K_j$ be a probability kernel from $(S,\Sigma)$ to $(T_j,\Tau_j)$, let
$\nu_j$ be sigma-finite on $(T_j,\Tau_j)$, and let
$f_j:S\times T_j\to[0,\infty]$ be a specified
jointly measurable function such that
$$K_j(s,B)=\int_B f_j(s,t_j)\,\nu_j(dt_j)\qquad(B\in\Tau_j).$$
On the finite product spaces, let $K=\bigotimes_{j<n}K_j$ be the product
probability kernel, let $\nu=\bigotimes_{j<n}\nu_j$, and put
$$f(s,t)=\prod_{j<n}f_j(s,t_j),$$
using the extended-product convention $0\cdot\infty=0$. Then $f$ is jointly
measurable and, for every $s\in S$, it is a density of $K(s,\cdot)$
with respect to $\nu$ on the entire product sigma-algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A positive finite number of probability kernels $K_j$, sigma-finite measures $\nu_j$, and specified nonnegative jointly measurable densities $f_j$ as in the Statement.

[F1] Finite products of probability kernels exist, are probability kernels, and have the product rectangle formula. [[lem-finite-products-of-probability-kernels]]

[F2] Finite products of sigma-finite measures exist, are sigma-finite, and have the product rectangle formula. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F3] For a nonnegative product-measurable function, Tonelli gives measurable section integrals and identifies the product integral with either iterated integral. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F4] Integrating a nonnegative measurable function over measurable sets defines a measure. [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]

[F5] Measures agreeing on a generating pi-system are equal when that pi-system supplies an increasing finite-measure exhaustion. [[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]]

[F6] Extended nonnegative measurability is determined by strict superlevel sets; between any two distinct real numbers there is a rational. [[def-extended-real-valued-measurable-function]], [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F7] The finite product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles, and its coordinate projections are measurable. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** construct the product density and compare its induced measure with the product kernel on rectangles.

**Given:** The hypotheses and conventions in the Statement.

1.1 The product kernel $K$ is a probability kernel by [F1]. Construct the finite product measure recursively: the one-factor case is $\nu_0$, and each next product is sigma-finite by [F2] applied to the preceding sigma-finite product and the next $\nu_j$. By [F7], each coordinate pullback $(s,t)\mapsto f_j(s,t_j)$ is measurable on $S\times\prod_{j<n}T_j$. For two nonnegative extended-measurable functions $u,v$ and $a\ge0$, $$\{uv>a\}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q,\ q>0}\{u>q\}\cap\{v>a/q\};$$ when $uv>a$, one can choose a positive rational $q$ with $a/v<q<u$, with the evident choice of any $0<q<u$ when $a=0$; this also covers infinite factors and excludes a zero factor under the stated $0\cdot\infty=0$ convention. For $a<0$ the superlevel set is the whole domain. Thus finite multiplication preserves measurability by [F6], and $f$ is jointly measurable. [F1, F2, F6, F7, given]

1.2 Fix $s$ and define $\lambda_s(A)=\int_A f(s,t)\,\nu(dt)$. By [F4], $\lambda_s$ is a measure. Repeated Tonelli [F3] and the probability-kernel density identities give $$\lambda_s\!\left(\prod_{j<n}T_j\right)=\int\prod_{j<n}f_j(s,t_j)\,d\nu=\prod_{j<n}\int f_j(s,t_j)\,\nu_j(dt_j)=\prod_{j<n}K_j(s,T_j)=1.$$ Hence $\lambda_s$ is a probability measure. [F3, F4, given]

2.1 For a measurable rectangle $R=\prod_{j<n}B_j$, repeated Tonelli and the density identities yield $$\lambda_s(R)=\prod_{j<n}\int_{B_j}f_j(s,t_j)\,\nu_j(dt_j)=\prod_{j<n}K_j(s,B_j)=K(s,R),$$ where the last equality is [F1]. Rectangles form a generating pi-system containing the whole product. Both measures are probabilities by step 1.2 and [F1], so the constant exhaustion by the whole product has common finite mass one. [F5] therefore gives $\lambda_s=K(s,\cdot)$ on the full product sigma-algebra. This holds for every $s$, proving the density assertion. [F1, F5, step 1.2] ∎
