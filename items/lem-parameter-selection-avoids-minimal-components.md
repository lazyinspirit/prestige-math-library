## Statement

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local ring of positive dimension $d$. Then there exists $x\in\mathfrak m$ outside every minimal prime of $R$. For every such $x$ one has
$$
\dim(R/(x))\le d-1.
$$
In particular $x$ misses the top-dimensional minimal components.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$ with $d=\dim R>0$.

[L1] A Noetherian ring has finitely many minimal primes ([[thm-noetherian-ring-has-finitely-many-minimal-primes]]).

[L2] Finite prime avoidance chooses an element of $\mathfrak m$ outside finitely many proper prime ideals ([[lem-finite-prime-avoidance]]).

[L3] If a prime is minimal over a principal ideal generated by a nonzerodivisor, then it has height $1$ ([[cor-minimal-prime-over-a-nonzerodivisor-has-height-one]]).

[L4] Prime ideals of a quotient correspond to primes upstairs containing the quotient ideal ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], the minimal primes of $R$ form a finite set; because $d>0$, none equals $\mathfrak m$. Hence [L2] provides $x\in\mathfrak m$ outside every minimal prime. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 Let $\mathfrak p/(x)$ be a minimal prime of $R/(x)$. By [L4], the prime $\mathfrak p$ is minimal over $(x)$ in $R$. If $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p$ is a minimal prime of $R$, then step 1.1 gives $x\notin\mathfrak q$, so the image of $x$ in the domain $R/\mathfrak q$ is a nonzerodivisor. Therefore [L3] shows that $\mathfrak p/\mathfrak q$ has height $1$, and every chain in $R/\mathfrak p$ extends upward to a chain in $R/\mathfrak q$ longer by one step. [L3, L4, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Since $\dim(R/\mathfrak q)\le d$, step 2.1 implies $\dim(R/\mathfrak p)\le d-1$. Taking the supremum over all minimal primes $\mathfrak p/(x)$ of $R/(x)$ yields $\dim(R/(x))\le d-1$. [step 2.1, given]

4.1 Thus one can choose a first parameter outside the minimal components, and every such choice lowers dimension by at least one. [step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
