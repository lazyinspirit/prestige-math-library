## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice, and DC. Let $S$ be a finite set, let $P_s$ be finite sets of odd rational primes with $P_s\cap P_t=\varnothing$ for $s\ne t$, and suppose the primes in each $P_s$ have one fixed residue class modulo $8$. Let $\chi_a$ denote the quadratic character of $\mathbb Q(\sqrt a)/\mathbb Q$ for $a\in\mathbb Q^\times$. Fix $\chi_0=a\chi_{-1}+b\chi_2$, with $a,b\in\mathbb F_2$. For $x=(p_s)_{s\in S}\in\prod_sP_s$, put $\chi_x=\chi_0+\sum_s\chi_{p_s}$, and let $N=\mathbb Q_2/\mathbb Z_2$ have trivial Galois action with $\xi=-1$. At $2$ and $\infty$ fix local conditions depending only on the local character, and at other places use the automatic unramified-or-zero twist-family conditions.

For $x$ define
$$\Psi_x:\mathbb F_2^2\oplus\bigoplus_{s\in S}\mathbb F_2\longrightarrow H^1(G_{\mathbb Q},\mathbb F_2),\qquad (u,v,(m_s))\longmapsto u\chi_{-1}+v\chi_2+\sum_sm_s\chi_{p_s}.$$
This is an isomorphism onto the subgroup unramified outside $\{2,\infty\}\cup\{p_s:s\in S\}$. The inverse image of the fixed-point Selmer subgroup is determined by the local conditions at $2$ and $\infty$ and the equations
$$(-1/p_s)^u(2/p_s)^v\,\chi_0(\operatorname{Frob}_{p_s})^{m_s}\prod_{t\ne s}(p_t/p_s)^{m_t+m_s}=1\qquad(s\in S),$$
where the quadratic character value $\chi_0(\operatorname{Frob}_{p_s})$ is written multiplicatively as $\pm1$.

If $x=(p_s)$ and $x'=(p'_s)$ satisfy $p_s\equiv p'_s\pmod8$ for every $s$ and $(p_t/p_s)=(p'_t/p'_s)$ for every $s\ne t$, these inverse-image Selmer subgroups are identical under the displayed coordinates. The preimage of the rational-torsion connecting line is also independent of $x$, so the corresponding fixed-point Selmer ranks agree. No analogous assertion is made here for arbitrary base fields or twistable modules.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite prime grid, quadratic characters, and local Selmer conditions in the Statement.

[F1] The twist, fixed-point Selmer subgroup and rational-torsion conventions are those in the twist-family definition. [[def-twistable-galois-module-and-favored-twist-family]]

[F2] Over a characteristic-zero field, Kummer theory identifies $H^1(G_F,\mu_2)$ with $F^\times/(F^\times)^2$. [[lem-kummer-cohomology-of-roots-of-unity]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F2], a global quadratic character unramified outside a finite set of rational primes has a square-class representative with even valuation at every other odd prime: adjoining a square root of an odd-unit nonsquare gives the unramified quadratic extension by its separable irreducible residue polynomial. Unique factorization therefore makes the displayed characters of $-1$, $2$, and the selected primes a basis for this subgroup: their valuations at the selected primes and at $2$, followed by the sign, show independence. Hence $\Psi_x$ is an isomorphism onto the stated unramified subgroup. For an odd prime $p$, the character $\chi_p$ is ramified at $p$ and unramified at every other odd prime. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix a selected $p_s$. The twist $N_{\chi_x}$ has inertia invariants exactly $N[2]$, since $\chi_x$ is ramified there. The long exact sequence for $0\to N[2]\to N_{\chi_x}\xrightarrow{2}N_{\chi_x}\to0$ makes the kernel of $H^1(\mathbb Q_{p_s},N[2])\to H^1(\mathbb Q_{p_s},N_{\chi_x})$ the line generated by the boundary of the nonzero local invariant. Lifting this invariant to an element of order four shows directly that the boundary is $\chi_x|_{G_{\mathbb Q_{p_s}}}$. Thus the zero local Selmer condition requires the localized class $\alpha=\Psi_x(u,v,(m_t))$ to equal $m_s\chi_x$, since their inertia restrictions agree exactly for this coefficient $m_s$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The difference $\alpha-m_s\chi_x$ is the unramified character $$u\chi_{-1}+v\chi_2+m_s\chi_0+\sum_{t\ne s}(m_t+m_s)\chi_{p_t}$$ at $p_s$. It vanishes exactly when its value on Frobenius is trivial. For an odd prime $q\ne p_s$, the Frobenius value of $\chi_q$ is the Legendre symbol $(q/p_s)$; the corresponding values for $-1$ and $2$ are their Legendre symbols. This gives the displayed equation. Here the exponents are elements of $\mathbb F_2$, and the character values in the product are interpreted as signs. [F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 An odd $2$-adic unit is a square exactly when it is congruent to $1$ modulo $8$: necessity follows by squaring, and successive square-root lifting proves sufficiency. Therefore $p_s\equiv p'_s\pmod8$ makes $\chi_{p_s}$ and $\chi_{p'_s}$ agree at $2$; both are trivial at infinity because the primes are positive. The local restrictions of $\chi_x$ and $\Psi_x(u,v,(m_s))$ at $2$ and $\infty$ are consequently identical for $x$ and $x'$. Their selected-place equations agree by step 3.1, the fixed residue classes modulo $8$, and the assumed Legendre-symbol equalities. At all other finite places both the twist and the class are unramified, so the automatic condition holds. This proves equality of the inverse-image Selmer subgroups. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 The global invariants of a nontrivial quadratic twist $N_{\chi_x}$ are $N[2]$, and the boundary of their nonzero element is $\chi_x$; its preimage under $\Psi_x$ is the fixed vector $(a,b,(1)_{s\in S})$. If $S=\varnothing$ and $\chi_0=0$, the global module is untwisted and divisible, so its connecting image is zero, again independent of $x$. Quotienting the equal preimage Selmer groups by this fixed connecting image gives equal fixed-point ranks. [F1, step 1.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
