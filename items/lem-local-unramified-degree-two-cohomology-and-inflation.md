## Statement

Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field, fix a separable closure $K_s$, its maximal unramified subextension $U=K^{\mathrm{ur}}$, and $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(U/K)$. Use normalized cochains and normalized continuous cochains. The finite crossed-product maps induce a canonical isomorphism
$$b_U:\operatorname{Br}_{\mathrm{alg}}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,U^\times),$$
normalized by $b_U([A(c)])=[c]$ on each unramified finite-level crossed product. Every algebraic Brauer class is split by a finite unramified extension. Valuation and the connecting map are isomorphisms
$$H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,U^\times)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,\mathbb Z),\qquad\delta:H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,\mathbb Z).$$
Evaluation at arithmetic Frobenius identifies $H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ with $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. On a finite unramified level of degree $n$, the cyclic valuation class $r\bmod n$ corresponds to $r/n\bmod\mathbb Z$; these classes form $(\frac1n\mathbb Z)/\mathbb Z$.

Under AC and DC with the supplied derived-cohomology conventions, inflation also induces an isomorphism
$$H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,U^\times)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(G_K,K_s^\times).$$
Only this last absolute comparison has the additional choice hypotheses.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The local field, chosen extensions and groups in the Statement.

[F1] For compact profinite groups, normalized continuous cohomology is the direct limit of finite-quotient cohomology with its actual inflation/coefficient maps. Under AC and DC the same holds in the supplied derived convention for absolute Galois groups. [[thm-continuous-profinite-cohomology-as-direct-limit-of-finite-quotients]]

[F2] Every finite Galois factor set is the obstruction of an algebra split by that field, with zero obstruction exactly for a matrix algebra, compatibly with enlargement and stabilization. [[lem-finite-galois-factor-set-obstruction]]

[F3] Every local central division algebra splits over a finite unramified extension. [[lem-local-division-algebra-has-unramified-maximal-subfield]]

[F4] Positive unramified unit cohomology vanishes. The unramified Galois group and each finite power are compact without choice. [[lem-unramified-local-unit-norms-and-cohomology]]

[F5] Finite cyclic cohomology is computed by norm quotients and norm kernels; inflation from order $n$ to order $an$ multiplies degree-two representatives by $a$. [[lem-cyclic-group-cohomology-norm-and-inflation]]

[F6] Finite unramified levels have cyclic Galois groups generated by arithmetic Frobenius, and all positive degrees occur. [[thm-finite-unramified-local-extensions-classified-by-residue-extensions]]

[F7] Central simple algebras have division representatives, remain central simple under scalar extension, and satisfy $A\otimes_K A^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\operatorname{End}_K(A)$. [[lem-finite-density-and-central-simple-base-change]]

[A1] Only for the final absolute inflation comparison, assume AC and DC with the supplied derived-cohomology convention. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]] [[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite crossed products and compact procyclic cochains.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Algebraic similarity classes form an abelian group here by a finite construction. Matrix stabilization gives an equivalence relation, and any two local central simple algebras split over a common finite unramified field by [F3,F6,F7]. Their tensor product becomes a matrix algebra there; a nonzero proper ideal would remain nonzero proper after field extension, and the center descends by the finite linear commutator equations. Thus the tensor product is central simple. Associativity and factor swap give the abelian operation, matrix algebras its identity, and [F7] gives inverse $A^{\mathrm{op}}$. The classes form a set by coding all finite-dimensional algebra structures with finite arrays over $K$. The zero class consists exactly of matrix algebras: if $M_r(A)\cong M_s(K)$, the corner cut out by $\operatorname{diag}(1_A,0,\ldots,0)$ becomes $P M_s(K)P\cong\operatorname{End}_K(\operatorname{im}P)$ for an idempotent $P$, so $A$ is a matrix algebra. This uses only a finite basis for the image and its complementary kernel. [F3, F6, F7, given, construct, algebra]


1.2 Now assume [A1] only for the absolute assertion. By [F1], every absolute continuous degree-two class is represented at a finite Galois $E/K$ by a cocycle $c$ with coefficients in $E^\times$. By [F2] choose an algebra $B$ with obstruction $[c]$, write it as $M_r(D)$ by [F7], and split $D$ over a finite unramified $L/K$ by [F3]. In the common finite Galois field $EL$, the obstructions computed over $E$ and $L$ agree by [F2]. Thus the absolute class inflates from the unramified tower, proving surjectivity. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F7, construct, algebra]

2.1 For any finite Galois splitting field $E/K$, the obstruction $o_E$ of [F2] is a homomorphism on the relative algebraic group. Indeed split $A_E=\operatorname{End}_E(V)$ and $B_E=\operatorname{End}_E(W)$, and use their finite semilinear implementations $T_\sigma,S_\sigma$ from the factor-set construction in [F2]. On $V\otimes_EW$ the implementation $T_\sigma\otimes S_\sigma$ has the product of their scalar factors. Hence $o_E(A\otimes B)=o_E(A)+o_E(B)$. It is injective because [F2] has zero obstruction exactly for a matrix algebra, the zero class in step 1.1. It is onto because [F2] realizes every class as $o_E(A(c)^{\mathrm{op}})=[c]$. Since $A\otimes A^{\mathrm{op}}$ is split by [F7], the tensor law gives $o_E(A^{\mathrm{op}})=-o_E(A)$. Thus $b_E=-o_E$ is an isomorphism with $b_E([A(c)])=[c]$. The matrix/cochain enlargement formula in [F2] proves its inflation and coefficient compatibility. This proves the finite comparison and its sign directly, without a separate Brauer-comparison supplier. [F2, F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every algebraic class splits over a finite unramified level by [F3,F7]. The compatible finite maps $b_E$ from step 2.1 therefore give a homomorphism onto the direct limit of $H^2(\operatorname{Gal}(E/K),E^\times)$ over these levels. Surjectivity holds at each level by step 2.1. If an algebraic class maps to zero in the direct limit, its obstruction is zero at some later finite level, whence it was already a matrix algebra over $K$ by [F2]. Thus the homomorphism is injective. Compactness from [F4] and the finite-level description [F6] let [F1] identify this direct limit with $H^2_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,U^\times)$. These are precisely the claimed $b_U$, crossed-product normalization, and relative unramified identification. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, step 2.1, algebra]


4.1 The sequence $1\to\mathcal O_U^\times\to U^\times\to\mathbb Z\to0$ is exact as discrete continuous $\Gamma$-modules: the base uniformizer realizes every integer valuation. It gives a long exact cohomology sequence directly on normalized continuous cochains. Indeed a continuous cochain has finite image by [F4]'s compactness, so finitely many coefficient lifts give a continuous lift, taking the identity lift for zero to preserve normalization. The kernel equality is pointwise; the cocycle-lift construction gives the connecting homomorphisms. By [F4], the unit terms in degrees two and three vanish, so valuation induces the claimed degree-two isomorphism. This finite-image lifting argument also applies to $0\to\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\to0$. [F4, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The trivial $\Gamma$-module $\mathbb Q$ has zero positive cohomology: at every finite cyclic level, [F5] gives zero odd groups because multiplication by its order is injective and zero positive even groups because it is onto. Pass to the compact cochain direct limit [F1]. The second sequence in step 4.1 therefore makes $\delta$ an isomorphism. By [F6], $\Gamma=\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with arithmetic Frobenius $\varphi$. A continuous degree-one cocycle in the trivial module $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ is a continuous homomorphism. It is determined by its value at the dense cyclic subgroup generated by $\varphi$; every rational class of order $n$ gives one through $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. Hence Frobenius evaluation identifies $H^1$ with $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. [F1, F5, F6, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 At order $n$, lift the character taking Frobenius to $r/n$ by $i\mapsto ir/n$ for $0\le i<n$. Its coboundary is $r$ when $i+j\ge n$ and zero otherwise. The degree-two periodic-to-bar comparison sends its generator to $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}[\varphi^i\mid\varphi]$, with degree-one generator $[\varphi]$: the boundary of this sum is $N[\varphi]$ by telescoping. Evaluating the carry cocycle on it gives $r$, so its cyclic degree-two coordinate is exactly $r\bmod n$, with the stated sign. By [F5], enlargement $n\to an$ sends it to $ar\bmod an$, preserving $r/n\bmod\mathbb Z$. This proves the finite-level coordinates, including $n=1$ and the zero class. [F5, F6, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 If a finite unramified-level class maps to zero absolutely, [F1] makes it zero at some finite Galois extension containing that level. Realize it as an obstruction by [F2]; its vanishing at the later level says the underlying algebra is split already over $K$, so its original obstruction is zero. Every continuous unramified class has a finite representative by [F1,F4]. Inflation is therefore injective. This proves the final absolute isomorphism exactly under [A1], while step 1.1 and steps 2.1–6.1 use no such assumption. [A1, F1, F2, F4, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
