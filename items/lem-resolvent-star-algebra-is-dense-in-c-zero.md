## Statement

Fix $z\notin\mathbb R$ and let $r(x)=(x-z)^{-1}$. Let $\mathcal A$ be the
$*$-algebra generated by $r$ in $C_0(\mathbb R)$, that is, the uniform closure
of the linear span of the products $r^k\overline r^{\,j}$ with $k+j\ge1$.
Then $\mathcal A=C_0(\mathbb R)$. Under Countable Choice
([[def-countable-choice]]), consequently, if self-adjoint $A_n,A$
satisfy $R_{A_n}(z)\to R_A(z)$ strongly (respectively in norm) at one nonreal
$z$, then the same convergence holds at every nonreal $z'$, in particular at
$\overline z$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] $\mathcal A$ is a self-adjoint algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity, $r$ is injective on $\mathbb R$, and $r$ does not vanish at any point of $\mathbb R$; the one-point compactification $\mathbb R^*=\mathbb R\cup\{\infty\}$ is a compact Hausdorff space and $C_0(\mathbb R)=\{f\in C(\mathbb R^*):f(\infty)=0\}$ ([[def-one-point-compactification]], [[thm-one-point-compactification-properties]], [[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-continuous-map-top]]).

[A2] Let $X$ be a compact Hausdorff space and let $B\subseteq C(X,\mathbb C)$ be a self-adjoint complex function algebra containing the constants, separating points, with no common zero. Then $B$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\mathbb C)$ ([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]]).

[A3] Under Countable Choice, with $D_z:=R_A(z)-R_{A'}(z)$ for self-adjoint $A,A'$ and nonreal $z$, the resolvent identity gives $D_{z'}=[I+(z'-z)R_A(z)]^{-1}D_z[I-(z'-z)R_{A'}(z')]$ for nonreal $z'$, the first factor being $I+(z-z')R_A(z')$ and the second $I+(z-z')R_{A'}(z')$; these affine transforms of resolvents are bounded with norms at most $1+|z'-z|/|\operatorname{Im}z'|$ ([[def-resolvent-and-spectrum-of-a-closed-unbounded-operator]], [[thm-self-adjoint-resolvent-estimate]], [[def-norm-and-strong-resolvent-convergence]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** $z\notin\mathbb R$, $r(x)=(x-z)^{-1}$ and the $*$-algebra $\mathcal A$ generated by $r$.

1.1 Extend $r$ to $\mathbb R^*$ by $r(\infty)=0$; then $r\in C_0(\mathbb R)\subseteq C(\mathbb R^*)$ and $r$ separates the points of $\mathbb R^*$: it is injective on $\mathbb R$, and $r(x)\ne0=r(\infty)$. [A1]

1.2 Parameter independence: for nonreal $z'$ the difference $D_{z'}=R_A(z')-R_{A'}(z')$ factors as in [A3] through $D_z$, with both outer factors of norm at most $1+|z'-z|/|\operatorname{Im}z'|$, since they are $I+(z-z')R_A(z')$ and $I+(z-z')R_{A'}(z')$ and $\|R_A(z')\|,\|R_{A'}(z')\|\le1/|\operatorname{Im}z'|$; hence $D_{z'}\to0$ in norm whenever $D_z\to0$ in norm, and $D_{z'}x\to0$ for every $x$ whenever $D_zy\to0$ for every $y$, because bounded operators preserve both modes of convergence. [A3]

2.1 The algebra $B:=\{f+c\mathbf 1:f\in\mathcal A,c\in\mathbb C\}\subseteq C(\mathbb R^*)$ contains the constants and $r,\overline r$, is self-adjoint, separates points by step 1.1, and has no common zero because of the constant function $\mathbf 1$; hence $B$ is uniformly dense in $C(\mathbb R^*)$ by [A2]. [A1, A2, step 1.1]

3.1 Therefore $\mathcal A=C_0(\mathbb R)$: given $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$ and $\varepsilon>0$, density of $B$ gives $f=c\mathbf 1+g$ with $g\in\mathcal A$ and $\|f-c\mathbf 1-g\|_\infty<\varepsilon$; evaluating at $\infty$, where $f(\infty)=0$, gives $|c|<\varepsilon$, so $\|f-g\|_\infty<2\varepsilon$ and $g\in\mathcal A$. [A1, step 2.1]

4.1 The density claim is step 3.1 and the consequence is step 1.2, which also covers $z'=\overline z$. ∎
