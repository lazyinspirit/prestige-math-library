## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj sheaf construction
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $S=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}S_d$ be a commutative
nonnegatively graded ring which is generated as an $S_0$-algebra by its degree
one part $S_1$, and let $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$ with twisting sheaves
$\mathcal O_X(n)=\widetilde{S(n)}$ ([[def-twisting-sheaf-proj]]). Then:

1. For every integer $n$ the sheaf $\mathcal O_X(n)$ is invertible
   ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]).
2. For all integers $m,n$ the multiplication map
   $$\mathcal O_X(m)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal O_X(n)\longrightarrow\mathcal O_X(m+n)$$
   of [[def-twisting-sheaf-proj]] is an isomorphism of
   $\mathcal O_X$-modules.

If $X=\varnothing$ (for instance if $S_+=0$) both statements are vacuous and
hold.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative nonnegatively graded ring $S$ generated by $S_1$ over $S_0$, the scheme $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$, and the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] Every element of $S_k$ is a sum of products of $k$ elements of $S_1$, so if $S_1\subseteq\mathfrak p$ for a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ then $S_+\subseteq\mathfrak p$. ([[def-proj-graded-ring-points]])

[F2] $X$ is covered by the standard opens $D_+(f)$, for homogeneous $f\in S_+$ of positive degree; each is an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$. ([[thm-proj-structure-sheaf-scheme]])

[F3] $\mathcal O_X(n)=\widetilde{S(n)}$ and on $D_+(f)$ its sections are $S(n)_{(f)}=\{a/f^k:a\in S(n)_{kd}\ \text{homogeneous}\}$; the multiplication maps $\mathcal O_X(m)\otimes\mathcal O_X(n)\to\mathcal O_X(m+n)$ are induced by $(a/f^k,b/f^l)\mapsto ab/f^{k+l}$ on charts. ([[def-twisting-sheaf-proj]], [[lem-proj-associated-sheaf-basic-sections]])

[F4] An $\mathcal O_X$-module is invertible if and only if it is locally free of rank one: every point has an open neighbourhood $U$ with $\mathcal L|_U\cong\mathcal O_U$. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: cover $X$ by the charts $D_+(x)$ with $x\in S_1$, exhibit the unit $x^n$ as a frame for $\mathcal O_X(n)$ on each such chart, and check that multiplication of frames is an isomorphism chart by chart.

1.1 The degree-one charts cover. Let $\mathfrak p\in X=\operatorname{Proj}S$. If $S_1\subseteq\mathfrak p$ then $S_+\subseteq\mathfrak p$ by [F1], contradicting $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}S$; hence there is $x\in S_1$ with $x\notin\mathfrak p$. Therefore every point of $X$ lies in some $D_+(x)$ with $x\in S_1$, and the family $\{D_+(x):x\in S_1\}$ is an open cover of $X$. [F1, algebra]
1.2 A frame on each degree-one chart. Fix $x\in S_1$ and an integer $n$. In the localised ring $S[x^{-1}]$ the element $x$ is a unit, so $x^n$ is defined for every integer $n$ and is a unit; as an element of the graded module $S(n)[x^{-1}]$ it has degree $0$. Every class of $S(n)_{(x)}$ has the form $a/x^k$ with $a\in S(n)_k=S_{n+k}$ homogeneous of degree $n+k$, and $a/x^k=(a/x^{k+n})\,x^n$ with $a/x^{k+n}\in S_{(x)}$; hence multiplication by $x^n$ maps $S_{(x)}$ onto $S(n)_{(x)}$, and it is injective because $x^n$ is a unit of $S[x^{-1}]$, so $S(n)_{(x)}$ is free of rank one with basis $x^n$. By [F3] this basis is a frame of $\mathcal O_X(n)$ on $D_+(x)$. [F3, algebra]
2.1 Invertibility. By step 1.1 the degree-one charts $D_+(x)$ with $x\in S_1$ cover $X$, and by step 1.2 the restriction of $\mathcal O_X(n)$ to each of them is free of rank one; hence $\mathcal O_X(n)$ is locally free of rank one, that is invertible, by [F4]. This is claim (1). [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]
2.2 Multiplication on a chart is an isomorphism. Fix $x\in S_1$ and integers $m,n$. On $D_+(x)$ the multiplication map of [F3] sends $S(m)_{(x)}\otimes_{S_{(x)}}S(n)_{(x)}\to S(m+n)_{(x)}$ and, in terms of the frames of step 1.2, $x^m\otimes x^n\mapsto x^{m+n}$, which is a unit of $S(m+n)_{(x)}$; being a map of free rank-one modules carrying a generator to a generator, it is an isomorphism. The identifications are compatible with restrictions to smaller standard opens, since both sides are given by the same localisation maps. [F3, step 1.2, algebra]
3.1 Global isomorphism. The maps of step 2.2 on the charts $D_+(x)$, $x\in S_1$, glue: on overlaps $D_+(x)\cap D_+(y)=D_+(xy)$ both restrictions are the multiplication maps computed in the localisation at $xy$, so they agree by the canonicity of the localisation maps in [F3]; the cover of step 1.1 and the local isomorphisms of step 2.2 therefore yield a global isomorphism $\mathcal O_X(m)\otimes\mathcal O_X(n)\to\mathcal O_X(m+n)$, which is claim (2). [F3, step 1.1, step 2.2]
4.1 Conclusion. Step 2.1 proves invertibility of every $\mathcal O_X(n)$ and step 3.1 proves that all multiplication maps are isomorphisms. If $S_+=0$ then $S=S_0$ is generated by $S_1=0$ over $S_0$, and $X=\operatorname{Proj}S=\varnothing$ because no homogeneous prime satisfies $S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$; the statements are then vacuous, and the empty scheme is covered by the empty family of charts. The Axiom of Choice [A1] is inherited from the Proj construction [F2], [F3]; no choice is made here. [A1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, cases: empty X]
\qed
