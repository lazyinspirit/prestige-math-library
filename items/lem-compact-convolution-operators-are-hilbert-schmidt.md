## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a compact Hausdorff group with
normalized Haar probability measure $\mu$
([[cor-normalized-haar-probability-on-a-compact-group]]) and let
$f\in L^2(K,\mu;\mathbb C)$ be a class
([[def-complex-haar-lp-spaces-and-compactly-supported-functions]]). Then the
left convolution $C_f$ on $L^2(K,\mu;\mathbb C)$,

$$(C_fh)(x)=\int_K f(xy^{-1})h(y)\,d\mu(y),$$

is a well-defined bounded linear operator whose definition does not depend on
the chosen measurable representative of $f$ and which is Hilbert–Schmidt with
$\|C_f\|_{HS}=\|f\|_2$; in particular $C_f$ is compact. The operator acts on the
regular representation only; no assertion is made about integrating a
convolution kernel on an arbitrary irreducible representation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a compact Hausdorff group $K$ with normalized Haar probability $\mu$, a class $f\in L^2(K,\mu;\mathbb C)$, and Countable Choice as a consequence of AC.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] Assume AC. For a Radon measure $\mu$ on an LCH space $X$ the complex spaces $L^1(X,\mu;\mathbb C)$ and $L^2(X,\mu;\mathbb C)$ are complete, and $C_c(X;\mathbb C)$ is dense in both. ([[lem-complex-haar-l1-and-l2-are-complete-and-cc-dense]])

[F2] Under AC a class of the completed product of two sigma-finite measure spaces defines a bounded kernel operator, with $\sum_e\|T_ke\|^2=\|k\|_2^2$ for every Hilbert basis, so $T_k$ is Hilbert–Schmidt with $\|T_k\|_{HS}=\|k\|_2$; the operator is independent of the chosen representative of $k$. ([[thm-l-two-kernels-give-hilbert-schmidt-operators]])

[F3] Under Countable Choice every Hilbert–Schmidt operator is compact. ([[thm-hilbert-schmidt-operators-are-compact]])

[F4] For sigma-finite measure spaces and a product-measurable nonnegative function the iterated integrals exist and agree with the product integral. ([[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]])

[F5] The normalized Haar probability is a left Haar measure, is inversion invariant, and left translations and inversion preserve it; integrals of nonnegative measurable functions and of integrable real or complex functions are invariant under measure-preserving maps. ([[cor-normalized-haar-probability-on-a-compact-group]], [[def-left-haar-integral-and-left-haar-measure]], [[thm-integrals-are-invariant-under-measure-preserving-maps]])

[F6] AC implies Countable Choice, the hypothesis needed for the Hilbert-space and completed-product $L^2$ results [F9, F10] and the compactness conclusion [F3]. ([[lem-ac-supplies-countable-and-dependent-choice-for-banach-integration]], [[def-countable-choice]])

[F7] A topological group has continuous multiplication and inversion, and a finite product of compact spaces is compact; a composite of continuous maps is continuous. ([[def-topological-group]], [[def-product-topology]], [[thm-finite-products-of-compact-spaces]], [[lem-continuity-is-local-and-pastes]], claim 1)

[F8] The product sigma-algebra is the sigma-algebra generated by measurable rectangles; pointwise limits of measurable functions are measurable. ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]], [[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]])

[F9] Under Countable Choice $L^2$ of a measure space is a Hilbert space for the integral pairing, so Cauchy–Schwarz holds for it ([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]], [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[F10] The completed product measure is the completion of the product measure on the product sigma-algebra. ([[def-completed-product-measure]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $u\in C(K)$ and put $q_u(x,y):=u(xy^{-1})$. Inversion and multiplication are continuous, so $q_u$ is continuous on the compact space $K\times K$. [F7]

2.1 For each $\epsilon>0$, continuity gives an open-rectangle cover of $K\times K$ on each member of which $q_u$ varies by less than $\epsilon$; compactness gives a finite subcover. Disjointify its rectangles by successive differences and on each nonempty piece use a value of $q_u$ from its rectangle. These pieces are measurable in the product sigma-algebra, so this gives a measurable simple function within $\epsilon$ of $q_u$ everywhere. Taking $\epsilon=1/n$ and applying pointwise-limit measurability to the real and imaginary parts shows that $q_u$ is product-measurable. [F7, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 For fixed $x$, the map $y\mapsto xy^{-1}$ preserves $\mu$ by [F5]. Tonelli applied to the measurable function $|q_u|^2$ therefore gives $\|q_u\|_{L^2(\mu\times\mu)}^2=\int_K\int_K|u(xy^{-1})|^2\,d\mu(y)d\mu(x)=\|u\|_2^2$. [F4, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 The map $u\mapsto q_u$ is an isometry from $C(K)$ into $L^2$ of the completed product measure by step 3.1. Since $C(K)$ is dense in $L^2(K)$ and the completed-product $L^2$ space is complete, it extends to an isometry $U:L^2(K)\to L^2(\overline{\mu\times\mu})$. Choose $u_n\in C(K)$ with $\|f-u_n\|_2\to0$ and put $\kappa:=Uf=\lim_nq_{u_n}$; then $\|\kappa\|_2=\|f\|_2$, and the class $\kappa$ is independent of the approximating sequence. [A1, F1, F9, F10, step 3.1]

5.1 Choose measurable representatives of $f$ and $h\in L^2(K)$ and define $C_fh(x):=\int_K f(xy^{-1})h(y)\,d\mu(y)$. For each $x$, the change of variables $y\mapsto xy^{-1}$ preserves $\mu$, so Cauchy–Schwarz makes this integral finite and gives $|C_fh(x)-C_{u_n}h(x)|\le\|f-u_n\|_2\|h\|_2$ for every continuous approximant $u_n$ chosen in step 4.1, uniformly in $x$. Each $C_{u_n}h$ is continuous: joint continuity of $q_{u_n}$ from step 1.1 and compactness of $K$ give $\sup_y|q_{u_n}(x,y)-q_{u_n}(x_0,y)|\to0$ as $x\to x_0$, and $h\in L^1(K)$ since $\mu(K)=1$. Thus $C_fh$ is a measurable uniform limit. By [F2], $T_{q_{u_n}}=C_{u_n}$ and $\|T_{q_{u_n}}-T_\kappa\|\le\|q_{u_n}-\kappa\|_2\to0$, so the uniform limit also gives $C_fh=T_\kappa h$ as an $L^2$ class. [F2, F5, F7, F9, step 1.1, step 4.1]

6.1 The kernel theorem [F2] makes $T_\kappa$ Hilbert–Schmidt with $\|T_\kappa\|_{HS}=\|\kappa\|_2=\|f\|_2$. By step 5.1 this operator is exactly $C_f$, so $C_f$ is bounded and has the asserted Hilbert–Schmidt norm. [F2, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 If $f$ is changed on a null set $N$, then for each $x$ the convolution integrand changes only for $y\in N^{-1}x$, since $xy^{-1}\in N$ iff $y\in N^{-1}x$; this set is null by inversion and right invariance of $\mu$. Changing the representative of the input $h$ also leaves every section integral unchanged. Thus $C_f$ is representative-independent; since Countable Choice holds, [F3] makes this Hilbert–Schmidt operator compact. [F3, F5, F6, step 6.1] ∎
