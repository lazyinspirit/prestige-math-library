## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the canonical smooth tangent-bundle structures and global differentials. Let $M^m,N^n$ be smooth manifolds with $m\le n$ (no compactness of $M$ is
needed for this invariance) and let $H:M\times[0,1]\to N$ be a regular
homotopy, that is, a smooth map whose restriction $H_t$ to every slice is an
immersion. Then $t\mapsto(H_t,dH_t)$ is a continuous path in
$\operatorname{FImm}(M,N)$ for the weak compact-open $C^\infty$ topology, and
consequently every homotopy invariant of formal data is constant along a
regular homotopy: the path component of $(H_t,dH_t)$ in
$\operatorname{FImm}(M,N)$ does not depend on $t$. For $M=S^m,N=\mathbb R^n$
this invariant is the Gauss frame class of the preceding items (the difference
class in $\pi_m(V_m(\mathbb R^n))$ when $n\ge m+2$); for $M=S^1,N=\mathbb R^2$
it is the winding number, i.e. the rotation number. In particular two regularly
homotopic immersions have homotopic formal data, which is the necessity half
of every classification on this page.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, smooth manifolds $M^m,N^n$ with $m\le n$ and a regular homotopy $H:M\times[0,1]\to N$.

[F1] A regular homotopy is a smooth $H$ with every slice $H_t$ an immersion, with $H_0$ and $H_1$ the prescribed immersions; the space $\operatorname{Imm}(M,N)$ carries the subspace topology of the weak compact-open $C^\infty$ topology. [[def-regular-homotopy-of-immersions]], [[def-space-of-immersions-and-space-of-formal-immersions]]

[F2] The weak compact-open $C^\infty$ topology is generated by finitely many chart data, compact chart pieces and derivative tolerances. [[def-weak-compact-open-smooth-topology-on-mapping-spaces]]

[F3] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the smooth tangent-bundle structures, the derivative map $D:\operatorname{Imm}(M,N)\to\operatorname{FImm}(M,N)$, $f\mapsto(f,df)$, is continuous for the weak topologies. [[lem-the-derivative-map-is-continuous]]

[F4] For compact $M$, path components of $\operatorname{Imm}(M,N)$ are exactly the regular homotopy classes. A continuous family of immersions on a compact parameter domain can be smoothed through immersions relative to the parameter boundary provided its adjoint is smooth on a neighbourhood of that boundary times $M$; these assertions inherit the countable-choice assumption of the relative approximation theorem. [[lem-smooth-families-and-path-components-in-the-weak-topology]], [[def-countable-choice]]

[F5] Points joined by a continuous path lie in the same path component, and path components are the equivalence classes of the relation "joined by a path". [[def-homotopy-relative-and-path-homotopy]]

[F6] The adjoint and evaluation correspondences for smooth families of maps identify a smooth $H:M\times[0,1]\to N$ with a map $[0,1]\to C^\infty(M,N)$. [[def-smooth-family-of-maps-and-evaluation-map]], [[thm-the-exponential-law]]

[F7] The angular winding invariant of formal circle immersions equals rotation number on derivatives. [[lem-formal-immersions-of-the-circle-in-the-plane-are-classified-by-the-winding-number]].

[F8] For sphere sections with simply connected Stiefel fibre, the evaluation lemma identifies components by the difference class. [[lem-the-basepoint-evaluation-of-the-stiefel-section-space-is-a-fibration]].

## Proof

1.1 The adjoint $\widehat H:[0,1]\to C^\infty(M,N)$, $\widehat H(t)=H_t$, is continuous for the weak compact-open $C^\infty$ topology directly from the definition: a basic weak neighbourhood of $H_{t_0}$ is determined by finitely many charts, compact pieces $K_i\subseteq M$ and tolerances $\varepsilon_i$ on derivatives of order at most $r_i$, and compactness with continuity gives a closed time interval $J$ about $t_0$ relative to $[0,1]$ on which $H(K_i\times J)$ lies in every prescribed target chart. For each of the finitely many occurring multi-indices $\alpha$ the local expression $D^\alpha(\psi_i\circ H_t\circ\varphi_i^{-1})(x)$ is continuous on the compact set $\varphi_i(K_i)\times J$, hence uniformly continuous there, so for $t$ close to $t_0$ every one of the finitely many derivatives stays within its tolerance; therefore $\widehat H$ is continuous. Its image lies in $\operatorname{Imm}(M,N)$ because every slice of $H$ is an immersion by [F1]. [F1, F2, F6]

2.1 Composition with the continuous derivative map [F3] gives the continuous path $t\mapsto D(H_t)=(H_t,dH_t)$ in $\operatorname{FImm}(M,N)$, since the composite of continuous maps is continuous and $\widehat H$ takes values in $\operatorname{Imm}(M,N)$ by step 1.1. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 A continuous path has all its points in a single path component [F5], so the path component of $(H_t,dH_t)$ in $\operatorname{FImm}(M,N)$ is independent of $t$; hence every invariant of formal data that is constant on path components takes the same value at $(H_0,dH_0)$ and $(H_1,dH_1)$, which is the necessity half of the classifications: regularly homotopic immersions have homotopic formal data. [F5, step 2.1]

4.1 The two instances: for $M=S^m$, $N=\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge m+2$, the path component of the formal data is detected by the difference class of the basepoint-evaluation lemma through the section-space description of $\operatorname{FImm}(S^m,\mathbb R^n)$, so the class in $\pi_m(V_m(\mathbb R^n))$ is constant along a regular homotopy; for $M=S^1$, $N=\mathbb R^2$ the corresponding invariant of a path component is the winding number, which for the derivative $(f,df)$ is the rotation number of the immersion, as established in [[lem-formal-immersions-of-the-circle-in-the-plane-are-classified-by-the-winding-number]]. The sphere difference-class conclusion is [[lem-the-basepoint-evaluation-of-the-stiefel-section-space-is-a-fibration]]. The compact-source identification of path components with regular homotopy classes used in the converse direction is [F4] with its countable-choice hypothesis; the invariance proved here uses steps 1.1–3.1 and inherits the structural countable-choice hypothesis of [F3]. No approximation or additional choice is used in this direct argument. [F4, F7, F8, step 3.1] ∎
