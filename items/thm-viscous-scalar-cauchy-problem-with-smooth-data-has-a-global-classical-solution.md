## Statement

Assume Countable Choice (CC) for the heat-kernel and $L^1$ interfaces below. Let
$n\ge1$, $\varepsilon>0$, let $f\colon\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^n$ be $C^2$ with
$f(0)=0$, and let $u_0\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$. For every $T>0$ there is a
mild solution $u$ of
$$u_t+\operatorname{div}_x f(u)=\varepsilon\Delta u\qquad\text{on }\mathbb R^n\times(0,T),\qquad u(x,0)=u_0(x),$$
with $u\in C([0,T];C_b(\mathbb R^n))\cap C([0,T];L^1(\mathbb R^n))\cap C^{1,2}(\mathbb R^n\times(0,T))$.
The solution is global in the sense that these solutions are compatible on
finite time intervals; for every $t\in[0,T]$,
$$\sup_{x\in\mathbb R^n}u(t,x)\le\sup_xu_0(x),\qquad \inf_{x\in\mathbb R^n}u(t,x)\ge\inf_xu_0(x).$$
In particular it is bounded and remains in the initial range. No uniqueness
beyond the constructed mild solution is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, $n\ge1$, $\varepsilon>0$, $f\in C^2(\mathbb R;\mathbb R^n)$ with $f(0)=0$, $u_0\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$, and $T>0$.

[F1] The heat evolution $H_t$ is the convolution with the heat kernel $\Gamma$: $H_tg=\Gamma(\cdot,t)*g$ is defined for $g\in L^p$ with $\|H_tg\|_p\le\|g\|_p$, and $\Gamma(\cdot,t)$ has unit mass, is strictly positive, is $C^\infty$ with $\partial_t\Gamma=\Delta\Gamma$, and satisfies the Gaussian derivative bounds $|D^\alpha\Gamma(x,t)|\le C_{n,\alpha}t^{-(|\alpha|+n)/2}e^{-|x|^2/(8t)}$ ([[def-heat-evolution-of-initial-data]], [[lem-heat-kernel-normalisation-scaling-and-derivatives]]).

[F2] For every multi-index $\alpha$ and $1\le p\le q\le\infty$ there is $C_{\alpha,p,q}$ with $\|D^\alpha H_tg\|_q\le C_{\alpha,p,q}t^{-|\alpha|/2-\frac n2(1/p-1/q)}\|g\|_p$ for all $g\in L^p$ and $t>0$; moreover the spatial and time derivatives pass through heat convolution for positive time, and Young's inequality bounds convolutions ([[thm-spatial-derivative-estimates-for-heat-flow]], [[thm-lp-to-lq-heat-kernel-estimate]], [[lem-spatial-and-time-derivatives-pass-through-heat-convolution-for-positive-time]], [[thm-young-convolution-inequality]]).

[F3] $H_tu_0$ is bounded and uniformly continuous with $\|H_tu_0\|_\infty\le\|u_0\|_\infty$, and $C([0,\tau];C_b(\mathbb R^n))$ with the supremum norm is complete: a uniformly Cauchy sequence of bounded functions converges pointwise in $\mathbb R$, its Cauchy bound then gives uniform convergence and boundedness of the limit; uniform limits preserve spatial continuity. Applying the same argument to continuous paths with values in this complete $C_b$ space gives a uniform limit continuous in time (the triangle inequality with one approximating path proves continuity). Thus this path space is complete, the uniform limit of continuous functions is continuous, and the Banach fixed point theorem applies to a contraction of a nonempty complete metric space ([[thm-heat-cauchy-solution-for-bounded-continuous-data]], [[thm-uniform-limit-continuous-real-functions]], [[thm-complete-subspace-iff-closed]], [[thm-banach-fixed-point]]).

[F4] Differentiable calculus: the chain rule, the sum and product rules, and the Laplacian of a $C^2$ function; parabolic cylinders and their parabolic boundary are as defined for the maximum principle ([[thm-chain-rule]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]], [[def-parabolic-cylinder-and-parabolic-boundary]]).

[F5] Convergence tools: dominated convergence, Riesz--Fischer completeness of $L^p$, and the $L^p$ class convention; the extreme value theorem on compact sets and Heine--Borel in $\mathbb R^n$ ([[thm-dominated-convergence]], [[thm-riesz-fischer-completeness-of-l-p]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

[F6] Write $BUC(\mathbb R^n)$ for the bounded uniformly continuous functions. The Gaussian kernels form an $L^1$ approximate identity, so $H_{\varepsilon t}g\to g$ in $L^1$ for $g\in L^1$ by the approximate-identity theorem. For $g\in BUC$, define $\omega_g(\rho):=\sup_{|z|\le\rho}\|g(\cdot-z)-g\|_\infty$, which tends to zero with $\rho$. Unit mass and the Gaussian tail give
$$\|H_{\varepsilon t}g-g\|_\infty\le\omega_g(\rho)+2\|g\|_\infty\int_{|z|>\rho}\Gamma(z,\varepsilon t)\,dz,$$
so $H_{\varepsilon t}g\to g$ uniformly as $t\downarrow0$. The Gaussian convolution identity $\Gamma_t*\Gamma_s=\Gamma_{t+s}$ follows by completing the square in its integrand and using Gaussian unit mass. The contraction and this identity give strong continuity of $t\mapsto H_{\varepsilon t}g$ in both $L^1$ and $BUC$ on $[0,\tau]$ ([[lem-heat-kernel-normalisation-scaling-and-derivatives]], [[thm-l-one-approximate-identities-converge-in-l-p]]). The product-space rearrangements use [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]], kernel time integrations use [[thm-ftc-second-part]], and the flux Lipschitz bound uses [[cor-mean-value-theorem]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Setup and kernel estimates.** Put $M_0:=\|u_0\|_\infty$, $R:=2M_0+1$ and $L:=\sup_{|s|\le R}|f'(s)|<\infty$. Since $f(0)=0$ and $f$ is $C^1$, the mean value theorem gives $|f(s)|\le L|s|$ for $|s|\le R$. By [F2] there are constants $C_1,C_2$ with $\|\nabla H_\sigma g\|_p\le C_1\sigma^{-1/2}\|g\|_p$ for $p\in\{1,\infty\}$. The Gaussian first moment gives $\|H_\sigma g-g\|_\infty\le C_2\sigma^{1/2}\|\nabla g\|_\infty$ for $g\in C^1\cap L^\infty$ with $\nabla g$ bounded. Also, $\|\nabla\Gamma_\sigma(\cdot+h)-\nabla\Gamma_\sigma\|_1\le\min\{2C_1\sigma^{-1/2},C_2|h|\sigma^{-1}\}$ and $\|\Gamma_\sigma(\cdot+h)-\Gamma_\sigma\|_1\le\min\{2,C_2|h|\sigma^{-1/2}\}$ for every $h\in\mathbb R^n$ and $\sigma>0$. For the scaled kernel, $\|\partial_\sigma\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\sigma^{-3/2}$, so for $\Delta>0$ and $\sigma>0$, $$\|\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon(\sigma+\Delta)}-\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\min\{\sigma^{-1/2},\Delta\sigma^{-3/2}\}.$$ [F1, F2, F6]


2.1 **A local mild solution for bounded uniformly continuous $L^1$ data.** Fix a starting datum $g\in BUC(\mathbb R^n)\cap L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ with $\|g\|_\infty\le M_0$. Choose $\tau_0\in(0,T]$ with $q:=2C_1L\sqrt{\tau_0/\varepsilon}\le\tfrac12$ and let $X_{\tau_0}:=C([0,\tau_0];C_b(\mathbb R^n))$ with the supremum norm ([[thm-complete-subspace-iff-closed]]). For $v\in X_{\tau_0}$ with $\|v\|_{X_{\tau_0}}\le R$ define $$(\mathcal T_gv)(t):=H_{\varepsilon t}g-\int_0^t\nabla H_{\varepsilon(t-s)}*f(v(s))\,ds .$$ By [F6], $t\mapsto H_{\varepsilon t}g$ is continuous in the supremum norm at $t=0$; by [F1] the Duhamel term is continuous in $X_{\tau_0}$, since its integrand is norm continuous for $s<t$ and has the integrable majorant $C_1(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-1/2}\|f(v(s))\|_\infty$. The bounds $\|H_{\varepsilon t}g\|_\infty\le M_0$ and $\|f(v(s))\|_\infty\le LR$ give $$\|\mathcal T_gv\|_{X_{\tau_0}}\le M_0+qR\le R,\qquad \|\mathcal T_gu-\mathcal T_gv\|_{X_{\tau_0}}\le q\|u-v\|_{X_{\tau_0}}.$$ Thus $\mathcal T_g$ is a contraction of the closed radius-$R$ ball; the Banach fixed point theorem gives a unique fixed point $u\in X_{\tau_0}$ satisfying $u(t)=H_{\varepsilon t}g-\int_0^t\nabla H_{\varepsilon(t-s)}*f(u(s))ds$. The original datum $u_0\in C_c^\infty$ satisfies these assumptions. [F1, F3, F6, step 1.1]


2.2 **Hölder regularity on strips.** Fix $0<\delta<\tau_0$ and let $t,t'\in[\delta,\tau_0]$. Since $f(u)$ is bounded with $\|f(u)\|_\infty\le LR$, the mild identity and the kernel bounds of step 1.1 give, for every $\alpha\in(0,1)$, $$\|u(t,\cdot+h)-u(t,\cdot)\|_\infty\le C_\delta|h|^\alpha,\qquad \|u(t,\cdot)-u(t',\cdot)\|_\infty\le C_\delta|t-t'|^{1/2}.$$ For the spatial estimate, $\|\nabla H_{\varepsilon t}g\|_\infty\le C_{\varepsilon,\delta}\|g\|_\infty$ on the strip. The Duhamel term is bounded by $LR\int_0^t\min\{C_\varepsilon\sigma^{-1/2},C_\varepsilon|h|\sigma^{-1}\}\,d\sigma$, with $t\le\tau_0$; splitting at $\sigma=|h|^2$ gives at most $C_\varepsilon(|h|+|h|\log_+(\tau_0/|h|^2))\le C_{\varepsilon,\tau_0,\alpha}|h|^\alpha$ for $0<|h|\le1$, where $\log_+(r):=\max\{\log r,0\}$. Larger $|h|$ are covered by boundedness of $u$. For the temporal estimate assume $t=t'+\Delta$ with $\Delta>0$. The initial heat term is Lipschitz in $t$ on $[\delta,\tau_0]$ by the positive-time estimate for $\partial_tH_{\varepsilon t}g=\varepsilon\Delta H_{\varepsilon t}g$. On the common Duhamel interval, with $\sigma=t'-s$, the kernel difference is a **time difference** and satisfies $$\|\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon(\sigma+\Delta)}-\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\min\{\sigma^{-1/2},\Delta\sigma^{-3/2}\}.$$ Splitting its integral at $\sigma=\Delta$ gives $\int_0^{t'}\|\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon(\sigma+\Delta)}-\nabla\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\,d\sigma\le C_\varepsilon\sqrt\Delta$. The remaining time slab has norm at most $LR\int_{t'}^tC_1(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-1/2}ds\le2C_1LR\sqrt{\Delta/\varepsilon}$. Thus both estimates hold with $C_\delta$ depending only on $\delta,\tau_0,\varepsilon,n,\|g\|_\infty,LR$, and $u$ is continuous in $(t,x)$ for $t>0$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]


3.1 **$L^1$ continuity and identification of the limits.** For the arbitrary starting datum $g\in BUC\cap L^1$ of step 2.1, take Picard iterates $u^{(0)}:=g$, $u^{(k+1)}:=\mathcal T_gu^{(k)}$. By [F6], $u^{(0)}\in C([0,\tau_0];L^1)$. If $u^{(k)}\in C([0,\tau_0];L^1)$, the integral defining $u^{(k+1)}$ is defined by norm limits of Riemann sums on truncated intervals $s\le t-\eta$. Completeness supplies these limits, and the bound on the omitted interval is at most $2C_1L\|u^{(k)}\|_{C_tL^1}\sqrt{\eta/\varepsilon}$, which tends to zero. Its integrand has integrable majorant $C_1(\varepsilon s)^{-1/2}L\|u^{(k)}\|_{C_tL^1}$, so $u^{(k+1)}$ is $L^1$-continuous and $\|u^{(k+1)}\|_{C_tL^1}\le\|g\|_1+q\|u^{(k)}\|_{C_tL^1}\le2\|g\|_1$. Successive differences obey $\|u^{(k+1)}-u^{(k)}\|_{C_tL^1}\le q\|u^{(k)}-u^{(k-1)}\|_{C_tL^1}$, so the iterates converge in the complete space $C([0,\tau_0];L^1)$ ([[thm-riesz-fischer-completeness-of-l-p]]) to some $w$. They also converge uniformly on $[0,\tau_0]\times\mathbb R^n$ to the fixed point $u$ of step 2.1. On every bounded ball $B_m$, this uniform convergence implies convergence to $u$ in $C([0,\tau_0];L^1(B_m))$, while the global $L^1$ convergence gives convergence to $w$ in the same local space. Uniqueness of the local $L^1$ limit yields $u=w$ a.e. on every $B_m$, hence a.e. on $\mathbb R^n$; thus $u\in C([0,\tau_0];L^1)$ and $\|u(t)\|_1\le2\|g\|_1$. [F2, F5, F6, step 2.1]


3.2 **The equation in distributions.** Testing the mild identity and using Fubini (the bound $(t-s)^{-1/2}$ is integrable on each finite time triangle) gives $$\int_{\Pi}(u\varphi_t+f(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi)+\int g\varphi(\cdot,0)=-\varepsilon\int_{\Pi}u\Delta\varphi.$$ Indeed the initial heat term pairs as $-\int g\varphi(\cdot,0)$ against $\varphi_t+\varepsilon\Delta\varphi$, and the divergence Duhamel term pairs as $-\int f(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi$ against the same expression, by integrating $\partial_tH_{\varepsilon(t-s)}=\varepsilon\Delta H_{\varepsilon(t-s)}$ in $t$. Thus $u_t+\operatorname{div}f(u)=\varepsilon\Delta u$ distributionally, with datum $g$. [F1, F2, step 2.1]


3.3 **Hölder continuity and boundedness of the spatial gradient.** Fix $0<\delta_0<\delta<\tau_0$ and use the mild identity restarted at $\delta_0$: $$u(t)=H_{\varepsilon(t-\delta_0)}u(\delta_0)-\operatorname{div}W(t),\qquad W(t):=\int_{\delta_0}^tH_{\varepsilon(t-s)}f(u(s))\,ds.$$ By step 2.2, $f(u)$ is spatially $C^\alpha$ and temporally $C^{\alpha/2}$ on each positive strip (the $C^{1/2}$ bound implies the weaker $C^{\alpha/2}$ bound). Componentwise, differentiation of the divergence heat potential gives $$\partial_ku(t,x)=\partial_kH_{\varepsilon(t-\delta_0)}u(\delta_0,x)-\sum_i\int_0^{t-\delta_0}\int_{\mathbb R^n}D_{ki}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}(z)\bigl(f_i(u(t-\sigma,x-z))-f_i(u(t,x))\bigr)\,dz\,d\sigma,$$ where the frozen value is subtracted using $\int D_{ki}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}=0$. Gaussian scaling gives $\|D^2\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\sigma^{-1}$, $\|\nabla D^2\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\sigma^{-3/2}$, and $\|\partial_\sigma D^2\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}\|_1\le C_\varepsilon\sigma^{-2}$. With the parabolic $C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}$ modulus of $f(u)$, a spatial increment $h$ is bounded after splitting at $\sigma=|h|^2$ by $$C\int_0^{|h|^2}\sigma^{-1+\alpha/2}\,d\sigma+C|h|\int_{|h|^2}^{\tau_0-\delta_0}\sigma^{-3/2+\alpha/2}\,d\sigma\le C|h|^\alpha,$$ and a time increment $\Delta=|t-t'|$ is bounded after splitting at $\sigma=\Delta$ by $$C\int_0^\Delta\sigma^{-1+\alpha/2}\,d\sigma+C\Delta\int_\Delta^{\tau_0-\delta_0}\sigma^{-2+\alpha/2}\,d\sigma\le C\Delta^{\alpha/2}.$$ If either split point exceeds the finite integration horizon, the same bounds follow by increasing $C$. The heat initial term is smooth with bounded derivatives on $t\ge\delta$. Thus for each $0<\alpha<1$, $$[\nabla u]_{C^\alpha_x(\mathbb R^n\times[\delta,\tau_0))}+[\nabla u]_{C^{\alpha/2}_t(\mathbb R^n\times[\delta,\tau_0))}\le C_{\delta_0,\delta}.$$ These are seminorm bounds; in particular they establish continuity of $\nabla u$ before any absolute bound is used. The absolute gradient bound follows from boundedness of $u$. If $\nabla u(t,x)\ne0$, put $e=\nabla u(t,x)/|\nabla u(t,x)|$. Along the unit segment $x+se$, $0\le s\le1$, the fundamental theorem of calculus and the spatial seminorm $M_\delta:=[\nabla u]_{C^\alpha_x}$ give $$|\nabla u(t,x)|\le |u(t,x+e)-u(t,x)|+\int_0^1|\nabla u(t,x+se)-\nabla u(t,x)|\,ds\le 2R+\frac{M_\delta}{1+\alpha}.$$ Thus $\nabla u$ is bounded on the positive-time strip. Since $f''$ is bounded on $[-R,R]$, $f'(u)$ has the same parabolic Hölder regularity as $u$ there; hence $h:=-f'(u)\cdot\nabla u$ is bounded and belongs to $C^{\alpha,\alpha/2}(\mathbb R^n\times[\delta,\tau_0))$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.2]


4.1 **Heat-potential cancellation and $C^{1,2}$.** Fix $0<\delta<\tau_0$, use the bounds of step 3.3 on a slightly larger positive strip, and restart the heat equation at $\delta$. Then $$u(t)=H_{\varepsilon(t-\delta)}u(\delta)+w(t),\qquad w(t,x):=\int_\delta^tH_{\varepsilon(t-s)}h(s)(x)\,ds.$$ For $\sigma>0$, $\int_{\mathbb R^n}D_{ij}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}(z)\,dz=0$, so cancellation gives $$D_{ij}w(t,x)=\int_0^{t-\delta}\int_{\mathbb R^n}D_{ij}\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}(z)\bigl(h(t-\sigma,x-z)-h(t,x)\bigr)\,dz\,d\sigma.$$ The parabolic Hölder bound from step 3.3 yields $|h(t-\sigma,x-z)-h(t,x)|\le C(|z|^\alpha+\sigma^{\alpha/2})$. Gaussian scaling therefore gives $$\int_{\mathbb R^n}|D^2\Gamma_{\varepsilon\sigma}(z)|\bigl(|z|^\alpha+\sigma^{\alpha/2}\bigr)\,dz\le C_{\varepsilon,n,\alpha}\sigma^{-1+\alpha/2},$$ which is integrable at $\sigma=0$. Truncating the heat-potential integral at $\sigma=\eta>0$, differentiating, and letting $\eta\downarrow0$ with this integrable dominator shows that $D_{ij}w$ exists and is continuous; the heat-kernel identity also gives $w_t-\varepsilon\Delta w=h$ in distributions. Since $h$ and $\Delta w$ are continuous, this identity gives a continuous classical time derivative. The first term $H_{\varepsilon(t-\delta)}u(\delta)$ is smooth for $t>\delta$, so $u\in C^{1,2}(\mathbb R^n\times(\delta,\tau_0))$ and satisfies $u_t=\varepsilon\Delta u-f'(u)\cdot\nabla u$, hence $u_t+\operatorname{div}_x f(u)=\varepsilon\Delta u$ pointwise. Since $\delta>0$ is arbitrary, $u\in C^{1,2}(\mathbb R^n\times(0,\tau_0))$. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.3]


5.1 **The range bound by a barrier.** Let $a<b$ in $[0,\tau_0)$ and put $M_a:=\sup_xu(a,x)$. Write $B:=f'(u(t,x))$, which is bounded on $[a,b]\times\mathbb R^n$ by $L$. For $\delta_1>0$ and $\lambda>0$ set $\Phi(t,x):=\delta_1e^{\lambda(t-a)}(1+|x|^2)$; then, using steps 4.1 and [F4], $$(\partial_t-\varepsilon\Delta+B\cdot\nabla)\Phi=\delta_1e^{\lambda(t-a)}\bigl(\lambda(1+|x|^2)-2\varepsilon n+2B\cdot x\bigr)>0$$ for $\lambda$ large enough, because $|B|\le L$ and $-\varepsilon\Delta(1+|x|^2)=-2\varepsilon n$. The function $z:=u-M_a-\Phi$ is negative at $t=a$ and, by step 2.1, $|u|\le R$ while $\Phi\ge\delta_1(1+|x|^2)$, so $z$ is negative on the lateral boundary of every sufficiently large ball intersected with $[a,b]$; if $z$ had a positive maximum in the closed cylinder, then at that point $z_t\ge0$, $Dz=0$, $\Delta z\le0$ ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]]), contradicting $(\partial_t-\varepsilon\Delta+B\cdot\nabla)z<0$ there. Letting $\delta_1\downarrow0$ gives $u\le M_a$ on $[a,b]$; applying the same argument to $-u$, whose flux is $\tilde f(s)=-f(-s)$ and whose range bound is the same, gives $u\ge m_a:=\inf_xu(a,x)$. In particular, with $a=0$, $\sup_xu(t,x)\le\sup_xu_0$ and $\inf_xu(t,x)\ge\inf_xu_0$ on $[0,\tau_0)$, the range of $u$ is contained in $I_0:=[\inf u_0,\sup u_0]$. [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1]


6.1 **Global continuation and conclusion.** For every $t_1>0$, the positive-strip spatial modulus of step 2.2 makes $u(t_1,\cdot)$ bounded and uniformly continuous, step 3.1 gives $u(t_1)\in L^1$, and step 5.1 places its values in $I_0$. Thus $g:=u(t_1)$ is an admissible $BUC\cap L^1$ starting datum for the local fixed-point and Picard iteration of steps 2.1 and 3.1. The strong heat continuity required for this restart is [F6]: Gaussian approximate-identity convergence gives $H_{\varepsilon t}g\to g$ in the supremum norm for BUC data and in $L^1$ for $L^1$ data. With the same global constants $R,L$, the local solution on $[t_1,t_1+\tau_0)$ agrees with $u$ at $t_1$; local uniqueness in step 2.1 makes the pieces agree on overlaps, and $\|u(t)\|_\infty\le\max\{|\inf u_0|,|\sup u_0|\}\le R$ throughout. Iterating finitely many times covers $[0,T]$, and the same local uniqueness shows that two solutions obtained with different terminal times $T,T'$ agree on their common interval. The range bounds of step 5.1 hold on all of $[0,T]$; $L^1$ continuity holds by step 3.1 and $C^{1,2}$ regularity on $\mathbb R^n\times(0,T)$ by step 4.1. This is the asserted global mild solution. [F3, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
