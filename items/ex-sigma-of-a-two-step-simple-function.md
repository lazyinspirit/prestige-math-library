## Example

Let $\varnothing\ne A\ne X$, let $\alpha \ne \beta$, and define

$$f := \alpha\,\mathbf{1}_A + \beta\,\mathbf{1}_{X \setminus A}.$$

Then $f$ takes exactly the two values $\alpha$ and $\beta$, and

$$\sigma(f)=\{\varnothing,\ X,\ A,\ X \setminus A\}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A set $X$, a subset $A$ with $\varnothing\ne A\ne X$, distinct real numbers
$\alpha \ne \beta$, and the two-step function
$f=\alpha\,\mathbf{1}_A + \beta\,\mathbf{1}_{X \setminus A}$.

[L1] The sigma-algebra generated by a function is the collection of its Borel
preimages. ([[def-sigma-algebra-generated-by-a-function]])

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Because $f$ takes only the two values $\alpha$ and $\beta$, every preimage [L1, given]
$f^{-1}(B)$ is one of $\varnothing$, $X$, $A$, or $X \setminus A$. So
$\sigma(f)$ is contained in that four-set family. [L1, given]

2.1 Since $\alpha \ne \beta$, choose a real threshold strictly between them. The [step 1.1, L1]
corresponding open ray has preimage $A$ or $X \setminus A$, and the other three
sets arise as in the previous example from $\mathbb{R}$ and $\varnothing$. Hence
all four sets belong to $\sigma(f)$, so they are exactly its members. [step 1.1,
L1] ∎
