## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let $\Sigma$ be an oriented genus-$h$ surface, let $k\ge1$ and $m=h+k-1$, and give $q\ge3$ balanced attaching systems $\boldsymbol\eta^0,\ldots,\boldsymbol\eta^{q-1}$ of $m$ circles. Supply their disjoint compression disks in oriented handlebodies $U_i$, their compatible Morse functions with $k$ index-zero and $m$ index-one critical points, and $k$ distinct points $\mathbf w$ with one in each cut component of every system. Distinct systems meet transversely and finitely. Use the usual product tori in $\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$. Let $W$ be the oriented polygon cobordism obtained from the polygon times $\Sigma$ by attaching $e_i\times U_i$ along $e_i\times\Sigma$, with rounded corners. A realized Whitney polygon is a continuous disk map with these successive torus edges and specified corner generators; no holomorphic representative is assumed.

The finite Morse construction below assigns such a class $u$ a Spin-c structure $\mathfrak s_{\mathbf w}(u)$ on $W$, restricting to the specified Morse labels of its corners. If $u,v$ have the same corners, their domain difference $P=D(u)-D(v)$ has boundary an integral sum of full attaching circles. Capping those circles with the supplied disks along polygon legs gives a class $H(P)\in H_2(W;\mathbb Z)$. With the common source/surface orientations, their Spin-c difference is
$$\mathfrak s_{\mathbf w}(u)-\mathfrak s_{\mathbf w}(v)=j\operatorname{PD}_W H(P),$$
where $j:H^2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)\to H^2(W;\mathbb Z)$. The sign is fixed by the positive index-one/index-two gradient modification having determinant degree $+2$ on a positive transverse disk.

In particular, if the two Spin-c structures and every $w$-multiplicity agree, then $P$ is a sum of actual doubly-periodic domains for the adjacent Heegaard pairs, each with all $w$-multiplicities zero. For a triangle these are the $\alpha\beta$, $\beta\gamma$ and $\alpha\gamma$ pairs. Their capped classes need not be zero individually. Fixed all $w,z$ multiplicities imply this hypothesis without any extra normalization convention.

The construction is compatible with gluing polygons along a common Heegaard boundary. Compatible Spin-c structures on the pieces have the usual extension orbit under the image of $H^1$ of that common boundary in $H^2$ of the glued cobordism; no unique extension is asserted. Along an actual continuous class-preserving family and its actual neck-smoothing, the assigned total Spin-c structure is constant, and the glued pieces recover that total extension, not an arbitrarily chosen member of the orbit. Point-divisor multiplicity vectors at every supplied $w,z$ mark add under these actual gluings. These assertions do not supply an analytic compactification or a gluing existence theorem. For supplied small attaching-curve isotopies away from the marks, transport the compatible Morse data in the actual handlebody collars and supply a continuous transport of the polygon maps. On the unchanged cobordism this carries their Spin-c labels and capped periodic classes, including torsion information. If one witness point in every old region misses the swept curves, all coefficients at those witnesses are unchanged. Hence strict strong admissibility, and the corresponding sector or global weak witness conditions, pass to the transported lattice. No existence of the continuous polygon-map transport is inferred from the mere isotopy of its boundary curves.

The statement does not identify all literal Whitney classes with their domains, divide genus-two classes by a sphere action, assert finiteness of classes with one domain, or supply any analytic count or Maslov-index formula. The point target $m=0$ has only the constant map and the domain conclusion is zero.

## Proof

1.1 **Finite Spin-c difference cochains, without full choice.** For two lifts of the same frame bundle, the fiberwise equivariant maps covering the identity form a principal $U(1)$ bundle: in common local frame charts their transition quotient is central, and the cocycle identities multiply those quotients. Evaluation identifies twisting the first lift by this difference line with the second. On the given finite handle CW structure, a line has its integer obstruction cochain on the two-cells after trivialization on the one-skeleton. Its coefficient is the winding of the boundary circle transition. Extension over a three-cell says precisely that these integers form a cocycle, because the face boundary windings cancel. Changes of one-skeleton trivializations change it by an integral coboundary. No higher obstruction remains, since a circle map on a sphere of dimension at least two has a real logarithm and contracts through that logarithm. Conversely glue the degree-specified transitions over the finitely many two-cells and extend in these same higher steps. Thus difference lines, also with fixed boundary trivialization, are classified by the actual finite cellular $H^2$. The determinant line of a Spin-c lift changes by the SQUARE of this difference line: the central $z$ has determinant character $z^2$. This finite construction, not the full-AC classification on arbitrary CW-type bases, is all that is needed below. [F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 **Construct the lift attached to the actual polygon.** First obtain boundary-preserving smoothing, rather than attributing it to transversality. At each corner the two transverse product tori have simultaneous smooth coordinates: the sum of their local parametrizations has invertible derivative, so [F7] gives a chart taking them to the two coordinate planes. Multiplication by a scalar cutoff in that chart contracts a smaller corner neighborhood to the generator, preserving both edge constraints. On each remaining compact edge, lift its finitely many circle-factor paths to real angles and smooth those angles relative their constant ends; this supplies a homotopy within that same torus. Extend this boundary homotopy to the disk explicitly by shrinking its original disk into radius $1-t/2$ and using the annular remainder to run the boundary homotopy from time zero on its inner circle to time $t$ on its outer circle. Reparametrize the boundary homotopy to be constant near its final time. At time one the resulting map is smooth on a full outer collar, with the prescribed smooth edge paths and constant corner neighborhoods. Reflect these constant-normal collars to an open source neighborhood if needed and apply [F7] relative a smaller closed outer collar. The resulting smooth map and its relative homotopy preserve every boundary torus and corner, and hence the original continuous Whitney class. This finite construction uses the given product-circle parametrizations and a relative smoothing theorem, not an assumed continuous/smooth quotient identification. By [F2] arrange finitely many simple double-root and $w$ incidences and no higher coincidence. Its root incidence surface in polygon times $\Sigma$ is joined along each edge to the chosen gradient trajectories ending at the index-one critical points. Add the $k$ fixed $w$ tracks, joined to the index-zero trajectories. Away from these surfaces the vertical oriented two-plane is tangent to $\Sigma$ and equals the kernel of the handlebody height differential on its edge piece. Foliating the polygon by the pairwise edge leaves of a central radial star gives three-manifold leaves near every corner. On a regular leaf the incidence tracks pair each index-one with one index-two zero and each index-zero with one index-three zero, with no shared endpoint. A small tube around each selected trajectory is a ball. Its boundary gradient degree is the sum of two opposite-parity local indices, hence zero, so [F3] extends its unit vector field without a zero. The perpendicular oriented plane extends the vertical plane over that tube. The construction is continuous on each compact interval of regular leaves: carry the finitely many disjoint tubes by their smooth moving coordinates, extend once and transport that extension. [F1, F2, F3, F7, given, construct]

1.3 **The capped-domain and finite homology maps.** The ordered-root calculation [F1], on each of the finitely many polygon edges, gives $\partial P=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}\eta^i_j$ as a full curve-chain identity. Fix one radial leg to each edge, attach those circle cylinders, and cap with the supplied disks in its handlebody. This is the actual integral two-cycle defining $H(P)$. A constant surface domain caps to zero: it bounds the corresponding handlebody cross-section. In one system write $A^i_1,\ldots,A^i_k$ for its cut components, labelled by their $w$ marks. Each $A^i_l$ capped with its disk-face boundaries is the boundary of the corresponding cut ball, so $H(A^i_l)=0$. Let $L_i$ be the kernel of $H_1(\Sigma)\to H_1(U_i)$, equivalently the lattice of compression-disk boundaries; it has rank $h$. The finite relative handle chains for $(W,\text{polygon}\times\Sigma)$ are the direct sum of those for $(U_i,\Sigma)$ shifted along the edges. The boundary map therefore gives $$H_2(W;\mathbb Z)=\ker\bigl(\bigoplus_i L_i\longrightarrow H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\bigr).$$ Here the map from $H_2(\Sigma)$ is zero by the cross-section bounding just noted. Disk-boundary coefficient relations in a balanced system are exactly the boundaries of its cut components: after cutting to the $k$ balls, the only relations among its $m$ oriented disk faces are the vertex-boundary relations of their connected dual graph. Eliminate a spanning tree of that finite graph to check this assertion integrally, including loop edges. Consequently the kernel of the capped-domain map is the span of the one-system domains $A^i_l$. No domain/homotopy-class bijection was used. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Only balls remain excluded, and their lifts extend.** Choose the radial star to avoid the finitely many incidence events and separate their leaf parameters. On its central leaf the incidence tracks are disjoint finite stars, one for each distinct root or $w$ track: a covering over a star has no monodromy. On a leaf with one simple root collision, two otherwise disjoint tracks meet at one vertex with four distinct ends. At a root/$w$ collision the same description holds for that root track and the $w$ track. All these graphs are trees; their endpoints lie on distinct critical-point copies, and the distinct leaf parameters keep different event graphs disjoint. A small regular neighborhood of each tree in the four-manifold is a ball: prune a leaf, absorbing its vertex ball and connecting product one-handle into the neighboring ball, then repeat finitely. Remove such neighborhoods and small parameter collars; step1.2 now supplies an oriented two-plane everywhere on their complement. Rotation in it and its oriented orthogonal complement gives a complex structure and its actual canonical Spin-c lift. Explicitly, lift a path in $U(2)$ from the identity to its real orthogonal path in $\operatorname{Spin}(4)$, and pair that lift with the square root of its complex determinant along the path. A loop of determinant winding $n$ ends with spin sign $(-1)^n$: the determinant-one part is a loop in $SU(2)=S^3$, while a single complex-coordinate rotation lifts as $\exp(\theta e_1e_2/2)$. The square root has the same sign, so their class in $\operatorname{Spin}^c(4)$ is independent of the path and defines a homomorphism. Its determinant line has transition $\det_{\mathbb C}$, as required. Over the boundary of each removed ball the oriented frame bundle is trivial. Its lifted fiber over that frame is a circle bundle on $S^3$, trivial by step1.1 since $H^2(S^3;\mathbb Z)=0$. It extends over the ball, uniquely up to lift isomorphism there. Thus a Spin-c lift on all of $W$ is constructed. Near its boundary the leaf construction is exactly the chosen corner-gradient modification, giving the asserted restrictions. Local extension choices differ inside balls and therefore do not alter the lift class. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 **The coefficient-one local lift comparison.** Fix $u,v$ with the same corners and use identical corner-gradient fields, so their lifts have a relative difference line. Restrict its finite two-cell obstruction to the cylinders dual to the compression disks on each $U_i\times e_i$. A track moving positively once across such a dual cylinder changes the central disk vector field by degree one. To check the integer, divide the boundary of its trajectory ball into the two critical-point hemispheres and the transverse mid-disk. The index-one hemisphere contributes degree $-1$, while extension without a zero has total degree zero; the new mid-disk therefore has degree $+1$. Its perpendicular plane is the pullback of $TS^2$, whose Euler number is $2$, so the determinant change on that disk is $+2$. By step1.1 the difference LINE winding on this individual integral cell is $+1$; this division by two takes place in $H^2(D^2,S^1)=\mathbb Z$, not in a possibly two-torsion global group. Negative crossings give $-1$. Track winding differences are the coefficients of $\partial P$ by [F1]. The resulting relative cochain is therefore exactly $\operatorname{PD}_W H(P)$. This identifies the whole class: the restriction to these dual cylinders is injective, because under [F4] it is the inclusion of the kernel $\bigoplus_i L_i\to H_1(\Sigma)$ in step1.3 into its direct sum of disk coordinates. For redundant disks pass to their cut-component relations first. Applying $j$ proves the stated absolute Spin-c difference. In particular relative homotopies and sphere insertions change no assigned lift, so the assignment descends to the literal realized classes. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Equal Spin-c classes force a sum of boundary-pair domains.** If the lifts agree, step3.1 gives $j\operatorname{PD}H(P)=0$. By [F4], $H(P)$ lies in the image of $H_2(\partial W;\mathbb Z)$ in $H_2(W;\mathbb Z)$. Every class of a boundary Heegaard pair is represented by a doubly-periodic domain: its finite pair sequence identifies its $H_2$ with the kernel of the two disk-boundary lattices mapping to $H_1(\Sigma)$, and a null-homologous integral circle chain bounds an integral region chain. Subtract the appropriate $A^i_l$ to set every $w_l$ coefficient to zero; these subtractions cap to zero. Choose such normalized domains $Q_i$ for the finitely many boundary classes, with the boundary orientation signs. Then $H(P-\sum_iQ_i)=0$. Step1.3 writes this remainder as $\sum_{i,l}c_{il}A^i_l$. Since every $w$ degree of $P$ and $Q_i$ is zero, $\sum_i c_{il}=0$ for each $l$. Hence the remainder is $\sum_{i>0,l}c_{il}(A^i_l-A^0_l)$. Each difference is a sum of adjacent one-system differences along the finite edge cycle. Each adjacent difference is itself a doubly-periodic domain and has every $w$ coefficient zero. This proves the required actual lattice conclusion, including redundant curves and multiple marks. [F4, step 1.3, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.2 **Gluing preserves labels and records the extension orbit.** The field construction is a product near the Heegaard boundary collar. Glue those collars using any chosen isomorphism between the agreeing boundary lifts. The resulting lift is the one obtained by gluing the actual polygon maps: their fixed corner and gradient neighborhoods match, and the finite local constructions coincide elsewhere. Two seam isomorphisms differ by a circle-valued gauge function on the seam. Its integer path windings give its $H^1$ class; gauges extending to either piece do not change the glued lift. The finite line-clutching construction of step1.1 changes the glued difference class by precisely the connecting map $\delta:H^1(Y;\mathbb Z)\to H^2(W_{\rm glued};\mathbb Z)$. Conversely identical restricted lifts differ only by this seam clutching, as seen by trivializing their difference line separately on the two pieces. Thus the extension set is exactly the stated image-$\delta$ orbit, without asserting uniqueness. In a continuous family of actual fixed-end maps, the finite relative two-cell obstruction coefficients are integer windings and hence locally constant; finite parameter charts give the same assigned lift throughout every connected class-preserving family. For an actual neck-smoothing, use the common product collar frames just constructed: its seam gauge is the one carried by that smoothing. Its glued lift is therefore the specified total lift, rather than another member of the orbit. The same conclusion holds in an actual degeneration once its continuous topological neck identification has been supplied; no analytic existence is inferred. Multiplicities at any $w$ or $z$ are degrees of the corresponding actual divisor lines. On the cut and glued source faces their boundary winding terms cancel along the seams, so their vectors add, with every actual disk or sphere component included. In particular a sphere domain is a multiple of $\Sigma$ and a one-system disk domain is a sum of cut components; these cap to zero by step1.3, so such insertions change no total Spin-c label. All operations were finite beyond the stated countable-choice smooth suppliers. For $m=0$ the target map and all domains are zero, and the domain assertion is immediate. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Carry the actual data and their admissibility witnesses.** In each system [F6] extends its disjoint-circle isotopy to a surface isotopy $\phi_{i,t}$, supported away from the fixed mark neighborhoods. Extend over the handlebody boundary collar by $(x,r)\mapsto(\phi_{i,t\chi(r)}(x),r)$, where $\chi$ is one near its boundary and zero deeper inside. This is a diffeomorphism at every $t$, with the inverse obtained by inverting the surface map at the same $r$. Carry its Morse function, metric and compression disks by this actual collar family. The underlying handlebodies, surface and polygon cobordism have not changed. With the supplied actual polygon-map family, the finite construction above is continuous also in these carried data; the integer difference-line cochains remain constant. Thus the whole Spin-c lift, and not only its rational Chern class, is carried. For a periodic domain with boundary coefficients $a_{ij}$ orient each traced curve annulus $T_{ij}$ by $\partial T_{ij}=\eta^i_j(t)-\eta^i_j(0)$ and set $P_t=P_0+\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}T_{ij}$, interpreted by its region degrees. Then its boundary is the required new full-curve chain. Its caps are transported by the disk isotopies; their prism chains have boundary exactly the differences of the caps and these annuli, so its capped homology class is unchanged. At every point missed by the swept curves the annulus degree is zero, so its region coefficient is unchanged. Reverse the annuli to obtain the inverse lattice transport; the two annulus chains cancel, so this is an integral lattice isomorphism, including the mark-normalized and $j\operatorname{PD}$ kernels. Its Chern evaluations agree because the actual lift and capped class agree. Every old strict or two-sign witness remains at its same point with the same coefficient. The transported lattice therefore has precisely the claimed preserved admissibility condition, even if new small regions appear. This argument transports supplied data and maps; it asserts no unproved existence of map transport, no holomorphic invariance and no count comparison. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
