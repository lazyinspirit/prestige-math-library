## Definition

Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be a finite extension and let $\alpha\in K$. Let $m_\alpha\in\mathbb Q[T]$ be the unique monic irreducible polynomial satisfying $m_\alpha(\alpha)=0$. Let $P_\alpha\in\mathbb Z[T]$ be the unique polynomial that is a nonzero rational multiple of $m_\alpha$, whose integer coefficients have greatest common divisor $1$, and whose leading coefficient is positive. If
$$P_\alpha(T)=a_dT^d+\cdots+a_1T+a_0,$$
the **naive coefficient height** of $\alpha$ is
$$H_{\mathrm{naive}}(\alpha):=\max_{0\le j\le d}|a_j|.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite extension $K/\mathbb Q$ and $\alpha\in K$.

[F1] For an algebraic element over a field, the evaluation kernel is generated by a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial. ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]])

[F2] An integer polynomial is primitive when the greatest common divisor of its coefficients is $1$. ([[def-content-and-primitive-integer-polynomial]])

## Proof

1.1 Clear a positive common denominator of the finitely many rational coefficients of $m_\alpha$, divide the resulting integer polynomial by the positive greatest common divisor of its coefficients, and choose the sign with positive leading coefficient. This constructs a primitive integer polynomial proportional to $m_\alpha$. [given, F1, F2, construct]

2.1 If $P$ and $Q$ are two primitive integer polynomials proportional to $m_\alpha$, write $Q=(u/v)P$ with relatively prime integers $u,v$ and $v>0$. Since $vQ=uP$, coprimality forces $v$ to divide every coefficient of $P$, so $v=1$; primitiveness of $Q$ then gives $|u|=1$, and positive leading coefficients force $u=1$. Thus $P=Q$. The finite nonzero coefficient list has a well-defined positive maximum absolute value, so $H_{\mathrm{naive}}(\alpha)$ is well-defined. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
