## Statement

Assume AC. Every nonempty irreducible separated complex algebraic variety of
finite type is connected in its analytic topology. For every locally closed
algebraic subset $A$ of a separated finite-type complex variety $X$, the closure
of $A$ in the analytic topology equals its Zariski closure as a set of classical
complex points. In particular smooth irreducible algebraic strata are connected
complex manifolds once their holomorphic charts have been supplied.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the classical complex varieties and locally closed algebraic subsets in the Statement, with the analytic topology obtained from affine Euclidean charts.

[F1] A nonzero finite-type complex algebra is finite over a polynomial subalgebra ([[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]]). A finite ring extension is integral: its underlying finite module is faithful for the subalgebra generated by any one element ([[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]). Polynomial algebras over fields are integrally closed, and an integral element's monic minimal polynomial over their fraction field has polynomial coefficients ([[lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed]], [[lem-integrally-closed-minimal-polynomial-coefficients]]).

[F2] Characteristic-zero fields are perfect, so their finite extensions are separable; a finite separable extension has a primitive element ([[cor-fields-of-characteristic-zero-and-finite-fields-are-perfect]], [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]). Coprime polynomials over a field admit a polynomial Bezout identity ([[thm-bezout-identity-for-polynomials]]), and every nonconstant complex polynomial has a root ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-liouville-proof]]).

[F3] A simple root of a holomorphic equation is a holomorphic function of its parameters on a small neighbourhood ([[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]]). A holomorphic function off the zero set of a nonzero holomorphic function extends uniquely if it is locally bounded at that zero set ([[thm-riemann-extension-across-hypersurface-zero-sets]]).

[F4] Holomorphic functions are continuous and separately holomorphic. On every polydisc their power-series coefficients satisfy $|c_\alpha|\le M R^{-|\alpha|}$ when every boundary radius equals $R$; the coefficients are unique on overlapping polydiscs ([[prop-holomorphic-functions-are-continuous-and-separately-holomorphic]], [[thm-power-series-expansion-in-several-complex-variables]], [[cor-uniqueness-of-multivariable-power-series-coefficients]]).

[F5] In a positive-dimensional irreducible complex variety, every point is the value at zero of a holomorphic disk whose nonzero parameters lie in any specified nonempty Zariski open subset ([[lem-complex-algebraic-boundary-points-admit-normalized-curve-arcs]]). Classical varieties have finitely many irreducible components and finite affine covers ([[lem-classical-variety-noetherian-components]], [[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]]).

[A1] AC is carried by the algebraic and normalized-curve suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). No choice of infinitely many covering sheets is made: their symmetric functions are defined intrinsically by a given clopen partition.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 First let $Z$ be irreducible affine with coordinate domain $B$. By [F1] choose $R=\mathbb C[z_1,\ldots,z_d]\subseteq B$ with $B$ finite over $R$. Write $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. The localization $(R\setminus\{0\})^{-1}B$ is a finite-dimensional $K$-domain; multiplication by a nonzero element is injective and hence surjective, so this domain is a field, equal to $E=\operatorname{Frac}(B)$. If $d=0$, $B$ is a finite-dimensional complex domain and therefore a field; [F2] forces every algebraic element over $\mathbb C$ to lie in $\mathbb C$, so $B=\mathbb C$ and $Z$ is one point. Otherwise [F2] supplies a primitive element $\eta\in E$ over $K$. Write $\eta=b/s$ with $b\in B$, $0\ne s\in R$ and put $\theta=b$; then $K(\theta)=E$, and $\theta$ is integral over $R$ by [F1], using the finite faithful $R[\theta]$-module $B$ (faithfulness follows by evaluating an annihilator at $1$). By [F1] its monic minimal polynomial is $P(z,T)\in R[T]$, of degree $m=[E:K]\ge1$. It is irreducible and separable over $K$. For finitely many $R$-module generators of $B$, express each as a $K$-linear combination of $1,\theta,\ldots,\theta^{m-1}$ and clear the finitely many denominators. This gives $0\ne a\in R$ with $B_a=R_a[\theta]$. Since $P$ is monic, division by $P$ and linear independence of these powers over $K$ identify this algebra with $R_a[T]/(P)$. [given, F1, F2, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 By separability and [F2], there are polynomials $H,J\in K[T]$ with $HP+J\partial_TP=1$. Clear denominators to obtain $H_0,J_0\in R[T]$ and $0\ne\delta\in R$ with $H_0P+J_0\partial_TP=\delta$. Put $g=a\delta$ and $V=\mathbb C^d\setminus Z(g)$. This set is nonempty because a nonzero polynomial cannot vanish everywhere on $\mathbb C^d$ (induction on $d$ using the finiteness of roots of a nonzero one-variable polynomial). It is path connected: for distinct $v,w\in V$, the polynomial $t\mapsto g(v+t(w-v))$ is nonzero at $t=0,1$, hence has only finitely many zeros; the complex parameter plane minus those zeros has a path from $0$ to $1$, obtained from the straight segment by finitely many small circular detours. Its image connects $v$ to $w$ in $V$. The principal open $U\subseteq Z$ where $g$ does not vanish is, by step 1.1's coordinate algebra, the space $\{(z,t):z\in V,\ P(z,t)=0\}$. All its coordinate identifications and inverses are polynomial quotients, hence analytic homeomorphisms. For each $z\in V$, $P(z,T)$ is monic of degree $m$ and has exactly $m$ distinct complex roots by the Bezout identity and [F2]. The implicit-function theorem [F3] gives disjoint holomorphic graphs near these roots on one common small connected base ball. They exhaust every nearby fibre because there are already $m$ distinct roots of its degree-$m$ polynomial. Thus $U\to V$ is an actual $m$-sheeted holomorphic covering, with no appeal to an unproved normalization or covering comparison. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $U=U_1\amalg U_2$ is a nontrivial analytic clopen partition. On each connected small graph of step 2.1 its membership in $U_1$ or $U_2$ is constant. Consequently the number $m_i$ of roots over $z$ belonging to $U_i$ is locally constant on $V$, hence constant since $V$ is connected. Nonemptiness of both pieces implies $1\le m_i<m$ and $m_1+m_2=m$. Define on $V$ the monic polynomials $Q_i(z,T)$ whose roots are precisely the roots on $U_i$ over $z$. Their coefficients are elementary symmetric functions of those roots; the local holomorphic graphs make them holomorphic, and changing the enumeration changes no symmetric function, so these coefficients glue without any chosen global root functions. Pointwise on $V$ one has $P(z,T)=Q_1(z,T)Q_2(z,T)$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Write $P(z,T)=T^m+p_1(z)T^{m-1}+\cdots+p_m(z)$. Every root satisfies $|t|\le1+\sum_j|p_j(z)|$: if $|t|$ exceeds this quantity, then $|t|>1$ and $|t|^m\le\sum_j|p_j(z)||t|^{m-j}\le(\sum_j|p_j(z)|)|t|^{m-1}<|t|^m$, a contradiction. The polynomial coefficients therefore give a bound $|t|\le C(1+\|z\|_\infty)^D$ for some integer $D\ge0$ and $C\ge1$, valid also near $z=0$. Each coefficient of $Q_i$ is a sum of finitely many products of at most $m_i$ such roots; it is locally bounded near $Z(g)$ and has a bound $C'(1+\|z\|_\infty)^{Dm_i}$ on $V$. Apply [F3] to extend each coefficient to all of $\mathbb C^d$. The same bound holds after extension by continuity: the nonzero polynomial $g$ cannot vanish on an ordinary open ball, since that ball contains a product of disks and successively restricting each variable would force all its coefficients to vanish. Hence $V$ is analytically dense in $\mathbb C^d$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 An entire function $h$ on $\mathbb C^d$ satisfying $|h(z)|\le C'(1+\|z\|_\infty)^N$ for an integer $N\ge0$ is a polynomial. Indeed [F4] on the radius-$R$ polydisc centred at zero gives $|c_\alpha|\le C'(1+R)^N/R^{|\alpha|}$; uniqueness makes the coefficients independent of $R$ by comparison on the smaller common polydisc. Letting $R\to\infty$ forces $c_\alpha=0$ for $|\alpha|>N$. Its expansion therefore is the finite polynomial $\sum_{|\alpha|\le N}c_\alpha z^\alpha$ on every polydisc, hence on all of $\mathbb C^d$. Apply this to step 4.1's coefficients: $Q_i\in R[T]$. The equality $P=Q_1Q_2$ on $V$ is a polynomial identity, since each coefficient of their difference vanishes on a nonempty ordinary open ball and the preceding one-variable restriction argument makes that coefficient the zero polynomial. Both factors have positive degree smaller than $m$, contradicting the irreducibility of $P$ in $K[T]$. Thus $U$ is analytically connected. This argument factors $P$ itself; it does not infer that one of two polynomial factors is zero from their vanishing on different analytic pieces. [F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The open $U$ is Zariski nonempty in the irreducible affine $Z$. If $Z$ has positive dimension, [F5] supplies at each $x\in Z$ an arc tending to $x$ with punctured image in $U$, so $U$ is analytically dense in $Z$. The zero-dimensional case was already a singleton in step 1.1. The closure of a connected set is connected: any clopen separation of its closure would restrict to a separation of the set, putting the set in one closed side and hence its closure there. Therefore $Z$ is analytically connected. For a general irreducible $X$, choose a nonempty affine open $Z\subseteq X$. It is irreducible and connected by the affine case; [F5] makes it analytically dense in $X$ when $X$ has positive dimension. If $X$ has dimension zero, its nonempty irreducible affine opens are singletons; irreducibility forces them to meet, so $X$ is one point. Again connectedness passes to the closure, proving the first assertion. [F5, step 1.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Finally let $A\subseteq X$ be locally closed algebraic, and write $C$ for its Zariski closure. Algebraic closed sets are analytically closed, because their polynomial equations are continuous on affine charts; hence the analytic closure of $A$ lies in $C$. There is a Zariski open $O\subseteq X$ with $A=C\cap O$. For each of the finitely many irreducible components $C_j$ of $C$, the set $A\cap C_j$ is a nonempty open subset: otherwise the dense set $A$ would be contained in the union of the other components, a proper closed subset of $C$. If $\dim C_j>0$, [F5] gives at every point of $C_j$ an arc with punctured image in $A\cap C_j$, proving analytic density there. If $\dim C_j=0$, it is a point by the affine argument and the finite affine-cover property, and nonemptiness of $A\cap C_j$ puts that point in $A$. Thus every $C_j$ lies in the analytic closure, which is therefore exactly $C$. The empty subset has empty closure in both topologies. The final manifold clause only combines this connectedness with supplied holomorphic charts; no chart existence is inferred from irreducibility. [given, F5, step 1.1, step 6.1] ∎

$1+\|z\|_\infty$ bound; the polynomial factor identity replaces its incorrect
unevaluated-factor domain inference. Analytic density uses the locally proved
normalized-curve-arc supplier, including its finite curve-normalization and
chart arguments, rather than an external comparison theorem.
