## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $I,J\trianglelefteq R$ be ideals. Then
$$
\sqrt{IJ}=\sqrt{I\cap J}=\sqrt{I}\cap\sqrt{J}.
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$ and ideals $I,J\trianglelefteq R$.

[L1] The radical of a finite intersection is the intersection of the radicals ([[lem-radical-of-an-intersection]]).

[L2] The product ideal $IJ$ is generated by finite sums of products $ij$ with $i\in I$ and $j\in J$ ([[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every generator $ij$ of $IJ$ lies in both $I$ and $J$, so $IJ\subseteq I\cap J$. Therefore $\sqrt{IJ}\subseteq\sqrt{I\cap J}=\sqrt{I}\cap\sqrt{J}$ by [L1]. [L1, L2, given]

1.2 If $x\in\sqrt{I}\cap\sqrt{J}$, choose $m,n\ge 1$ with $x^m\in I$ and $x^n\in J$. Then $x^{m+n}=x^m x^n\in IJ$, so $x\in\sqrt{IJ}$. [L2, choose, algebra]

2.1 Step 1.1 gives $\sqrt{IJ}\subseteq\sqrt{I}\cap\sqrt{J}$, step 1.2 gives the reverse inclusion, and [L1] identifies that common ideal with $\sqrt{I\cap J}$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L1] ∎
