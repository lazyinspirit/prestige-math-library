## Statement

Let k be a field and C a normal integral proper finite-type k-curve. For every t∈k(C) transcendental over k, the rational function t induces a finite dominant morphism C→P¹_k; such t exists. Its restrictions over the two standard affine opens of P¹ and their intersection have affine coordinate rings finite over k[t], k[t⁻¹] and k[t,t⁻¹], respectively, with the evident finite localization and gluing data. No choice principle is assumed. In particular this supplies a finite algebra presentation of C over P¹; it does not assert a separately constructed projective embedding.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** k and C as stated.

[F1] A finite-type k-domain has finite integral closure in its fraction field. [[thm-integral-closure-finite-finite-type-domain-over-field]]

[F2] A height-one localization of a normal Noetherian domain is a DVR. [[thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr]]

[F3] Projective space is proper, and proper morphisms are stable under base change and composition without a choice premise. [[lem-projective-properness-and-finite-type-surjectivity-without-choice]]

[F4] A field extension generated by finitely many algebraic elements is finite. [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]

[F5] Properness means separatedness, finite type and universal closedness; separatedness is the closed-diagonal condition. [[def-proper-morphism]] [[def-separated-morphism-schemes]]

## Proof

1.1 Let K=k(C). Since C is a finite-type integral curve, K/k is finitely generated of transcendence degree one. Choose one transcendental t∈K. A finite generating list of K/k is algebraic over k(t), so K/k(t) is finite by [F4]. On either standard affine chart A=k[t] or k[t⁻¹] of P¹, multiply a finite field-generating list by nonzero elements of A so that it is integral over A. The A-algebra A' generated by these elements is a finite-type k-domain, finite over A and with fraction field K. By [F1], its normalization in K is finite over A'; transitivity of integrality identifies it with the integral closure of A in K. These two finite algebras agree after localization to k[t,t⁻¹], and glue to a normal integral finite k-curve B→P¹ with function field K. [F1, F4, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 A finite morphism is proper without a choice premise, by the following finite algebra calculation. For a finite A-algebra D and ideal J⊂D, put A'=A/ker(A→D/J); then A' injects into the finite A'-algebra D/J. At any prime p of A', its localization is nonzero and finite over A'_p. The determinant form of Nakayama makes its fibre over κ(p) nonzero. A nonzero finite-dimensional κ(p)-algebra has a maximal ideal of maximal vector-space dimension, giving a prime over p. Thus the image of V(J) is the closed set V(ker(A→D/J)). This proof survives arbitrary base change, because finiteness survives base change. A finite map is separated and finite type, so [F5] makes it proper. Step 1.1 and [F3] therefore make B proper over k. [F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 We prove the needed valuative extension directly. Let X be a proper separated k-scheme, let R be a DVR containing k with fraction field L, and let Spec L→X be given. Take the reduced closure Z of its graph in X×_k Spec R. The base change of X is proper by [F3], so its projection is closed. The image of Z contains the generic point and hence the closed point of Spec R. Choose one point z of Z over that closed point. The integral local ring O_{Z,z} is a subring of L, contains R and dominates R. If a∈O_{Z,z} were not in R, the DVR property would put a⁻¹ in the maximal ideal of R; its image would lie in the maximal ideal of O_{Z,z}, contradicting a·a⁻¹=1. Hence O_{Z,z}=R, giving a lift Spec R→X. Two lifts agreeing on Spec L agree by the closed diagonal of [F5]. This uses one specified point z, not a family of choices. [F3, F5, given, choose, algebra]

4.1 For any closed point b of B, [F2] makes O_{B,b} a DVR with fraction field K. Apply step 3.1 to the generic map Spec K→C. The resulting map from Spec O_{B,b} extends to an open neighbourhood of b: select one affine target chart around the image of its closed point, write the finitely many coordinate images and relations in the local ring, and clear their finitely many denominators. The extension is unique on overlaps because C is separated and B is integral. The union of all opens on which the generic map extends is therefore B, and the unique local maps glue to a morphism B→C without choosing one neighbourhood per point. [F2, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Interchange B and C, using properness of B from step 2.1 and the DVRs on C from [F2], to obtain C→B. Both composites agree with the identity at the generic point. Since the targets are separated and the sources integral, their equalizers are closed and contain the generic point; the equalizer ideals vanish in the function field and hence vanish. Thus both composites are identities, and B≅C. Composing C→B with the finite map of step 1.1 gives the asserted finite dominant C→P¹, whose generic coordinate pulls back to the specified t. Its two affine preimages and overlap are the finite integral-closure algebras constructed in step 1.1. [F2, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Scope

This gives the finite P¹ presentation required for finite-code descent. It does not select a projective embedding or prove any Picard or cohomological statement.
