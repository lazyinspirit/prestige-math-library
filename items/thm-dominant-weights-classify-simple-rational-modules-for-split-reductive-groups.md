## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a
split reductive group over a field $k$, let $B\supseteq T$ be a Borel subgroup,
and let $X(T)^+$ be the set of dominant characters of the maximal torus $T$
([[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]]). For every
$\lambda\in X(T)^+$ there exists a simple rational representation $V(\lambda)$
of $G$, unique up to isomorphism, whose $T$-weight decomposition is
$$V(\lambda)=V(\lambda)_\lambda\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V(\lambda)_\mu$$
with $\dim_kV(\lambda)_\lambda=1$, and every simple rational representation of
$G$ is isomorphic to $V(\lambda)$ for a unique $\lambda\in X(T)^+$
([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]],
[[thm-simple-modules-with-equal-highest-weight-are-isomorphic]],
[[lem-dominant-characters-arise-as-primitive-weights-for-split-reductive-groups]]).
The map sending a simple representation to its highest weight is a bijection
from the set of isomorphism classes of simple rational representations of $G$
to $X(T)^+$; it holds in every characteristic.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a split reductive group $(G,T)$ over $k$ with Borel
$B\supseteq T$, and a dominant $\lambda\in X(T)^+$.

[F1] *Existence of primitive vectors of dominant weight.* Every dominant
$\lambda\in X(T)^+$ is the highest weight of a simple finite-dimensional
rational representation of $G$, and equivalently there is a rational
representation of $G$ containing a primitive vector of weight $\lambda$
([[lem-dominant-characters-arise-as-primitive-weights-for-split-reductive-groups]]).

[F2] *Modules generated by a primitive vector.* If a rational representation
$W$ of $(G,T)$ is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector $v$ of weight
$\lambda$, then $W=kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}W_\mu$ with $W_\lambda=kv$
one-dimensional, and $W$ has a largest proper $G$-submodule $W'$, the quotient
$W/W'$ being a simple $G$-module generated by the image of $v$
([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]],
[[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F3] *Highest weight of a simple module.* Every simple rational representation
$V$ of $G$ contains a primitive vector $v$, unique up to a nonzero scalar, its
weight $\lambda$ is dominant, $V_\lambda=kv$, every weight $\mu$ of $V$
satisfies $\mu\le\lambda$, and any two primitive vectors of $V$ have the same
weight ([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]]).

[F4] *Uniqueness.* Simple rational representations of $(G,T)$ with equal
highest weight are isomorphic
([[thm-simple-modules-with-equal-highest-weight-are-isomorphic]]).

[F5] *Finite dimensionality.* Simple rational representations of the affine
group scheme $G$ of finite type are finite-dimensional
([[lem-simple-rational-representations-are-finite-dimensional]]).

## Proof

**Given:** AC; a split reductive group $(G,T)$ over $k$ with Borel
$B\supseteq T$, and a dominant $\lambda\in X(T)^+$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Existence: by [F1] there is a rational representation $W$ of $G$ containing a primitive vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$. Let $W_0\subseteq W$ be the $G$-submodule generated by $v$; by [F2] applied to $W_0$ and $v$, one has $W_0=kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}(W_0)_\mu$ with $(W_0)_\lambda=kv$ one-dimensional, and the quotient $V(\lambda)=W_0/W_0'$ by the largest proper submodule is a simple $G$-module generated by the image of $v$. The image of $v$ is nonzero of weight $\lambda$, and the weight spaces of the quotient are the images of the weight spaces of $W_0$, so $V(\lambda)_\lambda$ is the line generated by the image of $v$ and every other weight $\mu$ of $V(\lambda)$ satisfies $\mu<\lambda$. By [F5] $V(\lambda)$ is finite-dimensional. [F1, F2, F5]

1.2 Uniqueness and exhaustiveness: let $V$ be any simple rational representation of $G$. By [F3] $V$ contains a primitive vector $v$, unique up to scalar, whose weight $\lambda_V$ is dominant and is the highest weight of $V$; the weight $\lambda_V$ is therefore uniquely determined by $V$. If $V_1,V_2$ are simple with $\lambda_{V_1}=\lambda_{V_2}$, then $V_1\cong V_2$ by [F4]. Hence the map $V\mapsto\lambda_V$ induces a bijection from the set of isomorphism classes of simple rational representations of $G$ to the set of dominant characters realized as highest weights. [F3, F4]

2.1 Combining steps 1.1 and 1.2: for every $\lambda\in X(T)^+$ the module $V(\lambda)$ of step 1.1 is simple with $V(\lambda)=V(\lambda)_\lambda\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V(\lambda)_\mu$ and $\dim_kV(\lambda)_\lambda=1$, and any simple rational representation is isomorphic to $V(\lambda)$ for the unique $\lambda\in X(T)^+$ given by its highest weight. No step used a hypothesis on the characteristic of $k$, so the classification holds in every characteristic. [step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Remarks

- The two halves of the argument are independent: existence comes from the construction of a primitive vector of weight $\lambda$ followed by the quotient by the largest proper submodule, and uniqueness comes from the comparison of two simple modules with the same highest weight.
- No separability, characteristic-zero, or algebraic-closure hypothesis appears, in accordance with Milne's Theorem 22.2; the only finiteness input is that simple rational representations of a finite-type affine group scheme are finite-dimensional, used to make $V(\lambda)$ finite-dimensional.
