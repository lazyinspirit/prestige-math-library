## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from the proper finiteness theorem and
the basis-extraction corollary cited below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$
be a field ([[def-field]]), let $X$ be a scheme proper over $k$, that is, the
structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is proper
([[def-proper-morphism]]), and let $\mathcal F$ be a coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module
([[def-coherent-module-scheme]]). Then for every $q\ge0$ the $k$-vector space
$H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ of sheaf cohomology
([[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]) is finite-dimensional over
$k$ ([[def-dimension]]), and only finitely many of the groups $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$
are nonzero. More precisely, choose a finite affine open cover of $X$ with no
empty members when $X\ne\varnothing$, and use the empty cover when
$X=\varnothing$. If $n$ is its number of members, then
$H^q(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every $q\ge n$, so the vanishing bound is the length
of the cover and does not depend on $\mathcal F$.

The empty source $X=\varnothing$, the zero sheaf $\mathcal F=0$, the degree
$q=0$ and every field $k$ are included.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** A field $k$, a proper morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ and a coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal F$; the Axiom of Choice is inherited from the cited suppliers.

[F1] A field is a Noetherian ring: its only ideals are $(0)$ and the whole field, so every ideal is finitely generated. ([[lem-field-is-noetherian]])

[F2] Finite generation and vanishing for proper coherent cohomology: for a Noetherian ring $A$, a proper morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ and a coherent $\mathcal F$, each $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ is a finitely generated $A$-module; choose a finite affine open cover with no empty members when $X$ is nonempty and use the empty cover when $X$ is empty, and let $n$ be its number of members. Then $H^q(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every $q\ge n$. The cited theorem assumes both AC and DC; AC in this corollary supplies DC by the choice-implication theorem. ([[thm-serre-finiteness-projective-cohomology]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]])

[F3] A module over a field is a vector space: the axioms for a $k$-module are exactly the vector-space axioms over $k$, so every $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ is a $k$-vector space. ([[def-vector-space]], [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]])

[F4] Finitely generated modules and finite spanning sets: a module $M$ over a ring is finitely generated when it is generated by finitely many of its elements, that is, when there are $m_1,\dots,m_s\in M$ with every element of $M$ a finite linear combination of the $m_j$; a set of vectors spans a vector space when its linear combinations fill it. Hence a finitely generated $k$-module is a $k$-vector space with a finite spanning set. ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[def-linear-combination-and-span]])

[F5] Basis extraction: assuming the Axiom of Choice, every spanning subset of a vector space contains a basis. ([[cor-every-spanning-set-contains-a-basis]])

[F6] Finite-dimensionality: a vector space is finite-dimensional over its field when it has a finite basis, and the zero space is finite-dimensional of dimension $0$ via the empty basis. ([[def-dimension]])

[F7] The Axiom of Choice is the choice principle named in the statement. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: specialise the proper finiteness theorem to the base field, where a finitely generated module is a vector space with a finite spanning set, extract a finite basis, and read off the finite vanishing range from the cover-length bound.

1.1 The base is Noetherian. By [F1] the field $k$ is a Noetherian ring, and AC supplies the DC premise of [F2] by the choice-implication theorem cited there. If $X$ is empty, choose the empty cover; if $X$ is nonempty, choose a finite affine open cover and discard any empty members, leaving at least one member. Write $n$ for the number of members of this chosen cover. Thus [F2] applies to the proper morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ and the coherent module $\mathcal F$: each $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ is a finitely generated $k$-module, and $H^q(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every $q\ge n$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Finite-dimensionality in every degree. By [F3] each $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ is a $k$-vector space, and by 1.1 it is finitely generated as a $k$-module, hence has a finite spanning set by [F4]; [F5] produces a basis of $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ inside that finite spanning set, which is a finite basis, so $H^q(X,\mathcal F)$ is finite-dimensional over $k$ by [F6]. [F3, F4, F5, F6, 1.1]

1.3 Only finitely many nonzero groups. The cover chosen in 1.1 exists because $X$ is proper, hence quasi-compact, over the affine base $\operatorname{Spec}k$. By 1.1, $H^q(X,\mathcal F)=0$ for every $q\ge n$, so the only degrees that can be nonzero are $q=0,1,\dots,n-1$, a finite set (empty when $X=\varnothing$). [F2, 1.1]

2.1 Boundaries and choice accounting. If $X=\varnothing$ then $n=0$, all groups vanish by 1.1, and each is finite-dimensional of dimension $0$ by [F6]; if $\mathcal F=0$ all groups vanish in the same way. Degree $q=0$ is covered by 1.2 together with all other degrees. The field $k$ is arbitrary, in particular $k=\mathbb F_2$ and fields of every characteristic are allowed, and only the trivial ring $A=0$ is excluded by the hypothesis that $k$ is a field. The Axiom of Choice [F7] supplies DC for the proper finiteness theorem through [F2] and is also used by the basis extraction of [F5], which is the only place where a basis is selected; the finite spanning sets of [F4] are supplied by 1.1, so no selection from an infinite family occurs. [F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, 1.1, 1.2] ∎
