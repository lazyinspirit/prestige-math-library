## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) inherited from the
regular-local and smooth differential suppliers. For a closed immersion
$i:X\hookrightarrow Y$ of smooth finite type schemes over a field, with pure
dimensions $n,m$ and constant codimension $d=m-n$, the ideal is locally
generated by a regular sequence of length $d$, $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2$ is
locally free of rank $d$, and
$$0\to\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\to i^*\Omega^1_{Y/k}\to\Omega^1_{X/k}\to0$$
is exact. Its dual gives $0\to T_X\to i^*T_Y\to N_{X/Y}\to0$. Locally in the
étale topology the embedding is the inverse image of the coordinate inclusion
$\mathbb A^n\subset\mathbb A^m$. Consequently
$\operatorname{Bl}_{X\times\{\infty\}}(Y\times\mathbb P^1)$ is smooth; if $Y$
is quasi-projective, this blowup is quasi-projective. The same immersion and
normal assertions hold on locally closed presentations, by restriction. More
generally, a section $s:T\to P$ of a smooth morphism of locally Noetherian
schemes of constant relative dimension $r$ is a regular immersion of
codimension $r$. Its regular equations and normal bundle commute with every
base change on $T$, including singular bases.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice; a field $k$; a closed immersion $i:X\hookrightarrow Y$ of smooth finite type $k$-schemes with $X$ of pure dimension $n$, $Y$ of pure dimension $m$ and constant codimension $d=m-n$; its ideal sheaf $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_Y$; and, in the last part, a section $s:T\to P$ of a smooth morphism $P\to T$ of locally Noetherian schemes of constant relative dimension $r$.

[F1] Jacobian criterion. A morphism $f$ locally of finite presentation is smooth at $x$ if and only if near $x$ it admits a standard smooth chart: affine opens $\operatorname{Spec}C$ of $x$ and $\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $f(x)$ with $f(\operatorname{Spec}C)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ and an isomorphism $C_h\cong(A[t_1,\dots,t_N]/(g_1,\dots,g_r))_\beta$ in which some $r\times r$ minor of the Jacobian matrix $(\partial g_j/\partial t_i)$ is a unit; such a chart is flat over $A$, exhibits relative dimension $N-r$ at $x$, and its fibres are geometrically regular ([[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]], [[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]], [[def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]]).

[F2] Smoothness of a finite type $k$-algebra and regularity. A finite type $k$-algebra admitting a standard smooth presentation at every prime is geometrically regular over $k$ ([[thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]], [[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]], [[def-ag-geometrically-regular-algebra-and-fibre]]); geometric regularity over $k$ implies that every local ring is regular local, since the empty generating list exhibits $k$ itself as a finitely generated field extension of $k$. At a prime admitting a standard smooth chart, the local ring is the localization of that chart and is regular. Moreover every local ring of a standard smooth $k$-algebra at a prime $Q$ is regular of dimension $\operatorname{ht}(Q')-c$, where $Q'$ is the corresponding prime of the polynomial ring and $c$ the number of equations ([[lem-ag-standard-smooth-regular-geometric-fibres]]). A field is Noetherian, and a finite type algebra over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[lem-field-is-noetherian]], [[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[F3] Local dimension formula. For a finite type $k$-scheme $Z$, a point $z$ and the infimum $\dim_zZ$ of the dimensions of open neighbourhoods of $z$, one has $\dim_zZ=\dim\mathcal O_{Z,z}+\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(z)$ ([[lem-affine-local-dimension-residue-transcendence]]). A smooth pure-dimensional $k$-scheme of dimension $e$ has local scheme dimension $e$ at every point.

[F4] Regular quotients. If $(R,\mathfrak m)$ is regular local and $I\subseteq\mathfrak m$ is an ideal with $R/I$ regular, then $I$ is generated by an initial segment of a regular system of parameters, of length $\dim R-\dim(R/I)$, so its members form an $R$-regular sequence ([[lem-regular-local-regular-quotient-ideal-is-parameter-generated]]).

[F5] Regular sequences and Koszul. Every finite regular sequence is Koszul-regular: its positive-degree Koszul homology vanishes, so every relation $\sum_j a_jf_j=0$ among the sequence elements is a combination of the Koszul relations $f_je_k-f_ke_j$ ([[thm-regular-sequences-give-acyclic-koszul-complexes]]).

[F6] Conormal sequence. For a closed immersion $i:X\to Y$ of $S$-schemes with ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$, the sequence $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\to i^*\Omega^1_{Y/S}\to\Omega^1_{X/S}\to0$ of $\mathcal O_X$-modules is exact ([[thm-conormal-sequence-closed-immersion]]).

[F7] Differential sheaves. A morphism smooth at a point has locally free relative differentials of rank equal to the relative dimension there ([[thm-differentials-smooth-locally-free]], [[def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]]). A morphism is formally unramified if and only if its relative differentials vanish; unramified means locally of finite type plus formally unramified; and étale means flat plus unramified for a locally finitely presented morphism ([[thm-formally-unramified-differentials-zero]], [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]], [[thm-etale-equivalent-flat-unramified-fp]]). Smooth and étale morphisms are stable under base change and composition ([[thm-smooth-morphisms-stable-base-change-composition]], [[lem-etale-stable-base-change-composition]]).

[F8] Smooth local structure. A smooth morphism of relative dimension $r$ at a point is, after shrinking the source and the base Zariski locally, the composite of an étale morphism to $\mathbb A^r_T$ and the projection ([[thm-smooth-local-standard-form]], [[def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]]). An étale morphism is locally standard étale: $B\cong(A[z]/(P))_g$ with $P$ monic and $P'$ a unit ([[thm-etale-locally-standard-etale]]).

[F9] Blowups. Blowing up a finite type quasi-coherent ideal sheaf is locally H-projective; if the ideal is globally generated by $f_0,\dots,f_r$ the blowup embeds as a closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^r_X$ ([[thm-blowup-projective]], [[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]], [[def-rees-algebra-ideal-sheaf]]). Blowups commute with flat base change ([[thm-blowup-base-change-flat]]).

[F10] Ampleness. On a quasi-projective $k$-scheme a very ample invertible sheaf exists, hence an ample one ([[lem-very-ample-implies-ample]], [[def-quasi-projective-morphism]]); for an ample invertible sheaf $L$ and a coherent sheaf $\mathcal F$, the twist $\mathcal F\otimes L^{\otimes\nu}$ is globally generated for all $\nu\gg0$ ([[thm-serre-criterion-ampleness]]). The Segre embedding exhibits $\mathbb P^{m_1}_k\times_k\mathbb P^{m_2}_k$ as a closed subscheme of a projective space, so a product of quasi-projective $k$-schemes is quasi-projective when the embedding is quasi-compact ([[cor-segre-veronese-embedding]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; reduce the immersion to its local regular-sequence model, compute the conormal sequence and its dual, exhibit the étale-local coordinate model, and read the deformation and section statements off that model.

1.1 Local invariants at a point. Fix $x\in X$ and put $R=\mathcal O_{Y,x}$, $S=\mathcal O_{X,x}$, so that the closed immersion identifies $S$ with $R/\mathcal I_x$ and $\kappa(x)$ with the residue field of both. Since $Y$ and $X$ are smooth of finite type over $k$ at $x$, [F1] provides standard smooth charts through $x$ and through $x$ in $X$; by [F2] the local rings $R$ and $S$ are regular local rings. Write $t=\operatorname{trdeg}_k\kappa(x)$. Both $Y$ and $X$ are pure-dimensional and smooth, so their local scheme dimensions at $x$ equal $m$ and $n$; by [F3] applied to each, $\dim R=m-t$ and $\dim S=n-t$, hence $\dim R-\dim S=m-n=d$. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 A regular sequence locally generating the ideal. By step 1.1 the local ring $R$ is regular and its quotient $S=R/\mathcal I_x$ is regular, so [F4] applied to $I=\mathcal I_x$ produces a regular system of parameters of $R$ whose first $d$ members $f_1,\dots,f_d$ generate $\mathcal I_x$; in particular $f_1,\dots,f_d$ is an $R$-regular sequence. Each germ has a representative on a common affine neighbourhood $U$ of $x$. That neighbourhood is Noetherian by [F2], so its ideal of definition of $U\cap X$ is finitely generated, and clearing denominators in the finitely many generation equalities shrinks $U$ until the representatives generate the ideal sheaf and their germs at $x$ remain the same regular sequence. [F2, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The conormal sheaf is free with basis the classes of the $f_j$. Let $S^d\to I/I^2$ be the surjection sending the $j$-th basis vector to the class of $f_j$, which is onto by step 2.1. If $\sum_j\bar a_j[f_j]=0$ in $I/I^2$, lift the $\bar a_j$ to $a_j\in R$; the relation says $\sum_j a_jf_j\in I^2$, say $\sum_j a_jf_j=\sum_j b_jf_j$ with all $b_j\in I$, so $\sum_j(a_j-b_j)f_j=0$. By [F5] every $a_j-b_j$ lies in $I$, whence every $\bar a_j=0$. Thus $S^d\to I/I^2$ is an isomorphism, and since $x$ was arbitrary $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2$ is locally free of rank $d$ with local basis the classes of $f_1,\dots,f_d$. [F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Exactness of the conormal sequence and its dual. By [F6] the conormal map $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2\to i^*\Omega^1_{Y/k}$ surjects onto the kernel $K$ of $i^*\Omega^1_{Y/k}\to\Omega^1_{X/k}$. At $x$, [F7] makes $i^*\Omega^1_{Y/k,x}$ and $\Omega^1_{X/k,x}$ free over $S$ of ranks $m$ and $n$; the surjection onto the free target splits, so $K$ is free of rank $m-n=d$. By step 3.1 the conormal module is free of rank $d$, so the surjection between free modules of equal rank has nonzero determinant modulo the maximal ideal, hence is an isomorphism. Therefore the conormal sequence is short exact. Dualizing it at each point, the dual of a short exact sequence of finite free modules over a local ring is short exact, so $0\to T_X\to i^*T_Y\to N_{X/Y}\to0$ is exact with $N_{X/Y}=(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2)^\vee$ locally free of rank $d$. [F6, F7, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The étale-local coordinate model. Shrinking around $x$, choose $u_1,\dots,u_n\in\mathcal O_Y(U)$ whose differentials on $X$ form a basis of $\Omega^1_{X/k}$ near $x$: differentials of local functions generate this sheaf, so select $n$ of them whose residues form a basis at $x$, then shrink until their determinant is a unit. Lift these functions through the surjection $\mathcal O_Y(U)\to\mathcal O_X(U\cap X)$. Form the $k$-morphism $\varphi=(\varphi_1,\dots,\varphi_m):U\to\mathbb A^m_k$ with coordinates $\varphi_j=f_j$ for $j\le d$ and $\varphi_{d+j}=u_j$. By step 4.1 the classes of $df_1,\dots,df_d$ form a basis of $I/I^2\otimes\kappa(x)$ and the classes of $du_1,\dots,du_n$ a basis of $\Omega^1_{X/k}\otimes\kappa(x)$; the exact conormal sequence of step 4.1 then shows that $d\varphi_1,\dots,d\varphi_m$ span the $m$-dimensional $\kappa(x)$-vector space $\Omega^1_{Y/k,x}\otimes\kappa(x)$. Choosing a presentation of $U$ as an étale-locally standard smooth chart as in [F1] and adjoining the $m$ equations $\varphi_j-t_j$ exhibits a standard smooth presentation of $U\to\mathbb A^m_k$ at $x$ whose Jacobian matrix has an invertible full $m\times m$ minor, because the corresponding row space together with the rows of the chart presentation spans. Hence $\varphi$ is smooth of relative dimension $0$ at $x$ by [F1]. Its relative differentials vanish at $x$ by [F7], so $\varphi$ is unramified and, being flat and locally of finite presentation by [F1], étale at $x$ by [F7]. Finally, $\varphi^{-1}(\mathbb A^n)\cap U$, where $\mathbb A^n\subset\mathbb A^m$ is the coordinate subspace $t_1=\cdots=t_d=0$, is exactly $U\cap X$, because $X$ is locally defined in $U$ by $f_1=\cdots=f_d=0$ by step 2.1. [F1, F7, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

5.2 The locally closed case. If $i:X\hookrightarrow Y$ is only a locally closed immersion, write it as a closed immersion $X\hookrightarrow U$ followed by an open immersion $U\hookrightarrow Y$; the ideal sheaf, the conormal sheaf and the conormal sequence are those of the closed part, computed in $U$, and the assertions are local near points of $X$, so they follow from the closed case applied to $X\hookrightarrow U$. [F6, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Smoothness of the deformation blowup. On the affine chart of $\mathbb P^1$ containing $\infty$, use the coordinate $t$ vanishing at $\infty$; the centre is cut out by $(f_1,\dots,f_d,t)$. On its complement the centre is empty, so the blowup is the identity and is smooth. Near the centre the coordinate model is the blowup of $\mathbb A^m\times\mathbb A^1$ along $(f_1,\dots,f_d,t)$. Its charts are computed from the Rees algebra: the $t$-chart has coordinates $(f_1/t,\dots,f_d/t,t,u_1,\dots,u_n)$ and the $f_j$-charts have coordinates $(f_j,f_l/f_j\ (l\ne j),t/f_j,u_1,\dots,u_n)$, each an affine space of dimension $m+1$; the chart ideals are generated by regular sequences, so each chart is smooth over $k$, and smoothness is local on the source, so the model blowup is smooth. Now let $\psi=\varphi\times\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb P^1}:U\times\mathbb P^1\to\mathbb A^m\times\mathbb P^1$. It is étale, hence flat, and the ideal of $X\times\{\infty\}$ in $U\times\mathbb P^1$ is the inverse image of the model ideal because $X\cap U=\varphi^{-1}(\mathbb A^n)$. By [F9] (flat base change of blowups) the blowup of $U\times\mathbb P^1$ along $X\times\{\infty\}$ is the base change of the smooth model blowup along $\psi$; by [F7] base changes of smooth morphisms are smooth, and a composition of smooth morphisms is smooth, so this base change is smooth over $k$. As $x\in X$ was arbitrary and $X$ is covered by the opens $U\cap X$, the blowup $\operatorname{Bl}_{X\times\{\infty\}}(Y\times\mathbb P^1)$ is smooth over $k$. [F1, F9, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Quasi-projectivity of the blowup. Assume now that $Y$ is quasi-projective over $k$. Then $Y\times\mathbb P^1_k$ is quasi-projective: $Y$ is a quasi-compact locally closed subscheme of some projective space, and the Segre embedding [F10] realizes the product of projective spaces as a closed subscheme of a projective space, so the product immerses quasi-compactly. Let $Z=Y\times\mathbb P^1_k$ and let $L$ be very ample on $Z$, hence ample [F10]. The ideal sheaf $\mathcal J$ of $X\times\{\infty\}$ is coherent, so by [F10] the twist $\mathcal J\otimes L^{\otimes\nu}$ is globally generated for some $\nu\ge1$; the graded algebra $\bigoplus_{q\ge0}\mathcal J^q\otimes L^{\nu q}$ has the same relative Proj as $\bigoplus_{q\ge0}\mathcal J^q$, since a trivialization of $L$ identifies their Proj charts and changing the trivialization rescales degree $q$ by the $q$-th power of a unit. A finite set of generators of $\mathcal J\otimes L^\nu$ therefore gives a graded surjection from $\mathcal O_Z[T_0,\dots,T_r]$ onto this twisted Rees algebra. Its relative Proj is a closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^r_Z$, by the same homogeneous-quotient chart calculation as [F9], and a projective bundle over the quasi-projective $Z$ is quasi-projective, so the blowup is quasi-projective over $k$. [F9, F10, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Sections of smooth morphisms. Let $s:T\to P$ be a section of a smooth morphism $P\to T$ of constant relative dimension $r$, with $T$ locally Noetherian. Near a point $t\in T$, [F8] factors $P\to T$ Zariski locally as an étale morphism $P'\to\mathbb A^r_T=\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_T[x_1,\dots,x_r]$ followed by the projection, after shrinking around $s(t)$ and $t$; by [F7] the étale morphism is flat. The composite $T\to P'\to\mathbb A^r_T$ is the graph of the $T$-point with coordinates $c_i=s^*(x_i)$. This graph is cut out by the regular sequence $x_1-c_1,\dots,x_r-c_r$: after quotienting by the first $i$ terms, the next element is a monic linear polynomial in a new variable and hence a nonzerodivisor over any base ring. Pulling the graph back along the flat étale map $P'\to\mathbb A^r_T$ gives a regular immersion $Q=T\times_{\mathbb A^r_T}P'\hookrightarrow P'$ of codimension $r$, and $Q\to T$ is étale. The section factors through a section $T\to Q$ of this étale morphism. By [F8], after shrinking near the chosen point, this section is described by a standard étale chart $\operatorname{Spec}((A[z]/(P))_g)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $z\mapsto a$, $P(a)=0$ and $P'(a)$ a unit. Factoring $P(z)=(z-a)Q_0(z)$ gives $Q_0(a)=P'(a)$; after restricting to the open neighborhood where $Q_0$ is invertible, the equation $P(z)=0$ forces $z=a$, so the section is an open immersion there. Hence near its image in $P'$, the pulled-back regular equations cut out $s(T)$, proving that $s$ is a regular immersion of codimension $r$. These equations remain regular after every base change $T'\to T$, since the section of the smooth base change $P\times_TT'\to T'$ is again locally obtained by this graph construction, including when $T'$ is singular. The normal bundle also commutes with base change: for a section of a smooth morphism, the conormal bundle identifies with $s^*\Omega^1_{P/T}$, and this relative differential bundle pulls back to $s'^*\Omega^1_{(P\times_TT')/T'}$. Hence the codimension and normal bundle are preserved by arbitrary base change on $T$. [F7, F8, algebra] ∎
