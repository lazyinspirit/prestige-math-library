## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For every $n\ge1$ let
$Q^{(n)}=(q^{(n)}_1,\dots,q^{(n)}_n)$ be the canonical base configuration of
[[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], so that
$h_n=\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$ and $q^{(n)}_j=((2j-n-1)h_n,0)$. Let
$A_{ij}\in PB_n=\pi_1(F_n(D^2),Q^{(n)})$ be the standard pure braid
generators of [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]] for $1\le i<j\le n$. Then
$$PB_n=\bigl\langle A_{ij}:1\le i<j\le n\bigr\rangle ,$$
the subgroup generated by the classes $A_{ij}$; for $n=1$ the family is empty
and generates the trivial group $PB_1$. This asserts generation only: no
presentation, no completeness of any list of relations, and no statement about
the Artin presentation of $PB_n$ is claimed. Once the statement is known at
the canonical base configuration, a path in $F_n(D^2)$ from $Q^{(n)}$ to any
other base configuration conjugates it to the corresponding statement there
([[lem-path-conjugation-isomorphism-of-fundamental-groups]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice and an integer $n\ge2$ with the canonical base configurations $Q^{(n)}$, $Q^{(n-1)}$ of [[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], the truncated configuration $q'=(q^{(n)}_1,\dots,q^{(n)}_{n-1})\in F_{n-1}(D^\circ)$, the open-disc configuration spaces $F_n(D^\circ)\subseteq F_n(D^2)$ and $F_{n-1}(D^\circ)\subseteq F_{n-1}(D^2)$, and the half twists $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ at $Q^{(n)}$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice holds ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] In ZF, AC implies DC ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F2] $PB_m=\pi_1(F_m(D^2),q^{(m)})$ is the pure braid group of the closed-disc convention at the configuration $q^{(m)}$; the inclusion $\iota^F:F_m(D^\circ)\to F_m(D^2)$ induces an isomorphism $\iota^F_*:\pi_1(F_m(D^\circ),c)\to\pi_1(F_m(D^2),c)$ at every configuration $c$ of interior points, $PB_0$ and $PB_1$ are trivial, and for $m\ge2$ the last-coordinate forgetting map $\varphi:PB_m\to PB_{m-1}$ fits into the short exact sequence $1\to F_{m-1}\xrightarrow{\kappa}PB_m\xrightarrow{\varphi}PB_{m-1}\to1$ with $\kappa$ injective, $\varphi$ surjective and $\operatorname{im}\kappa=\ker\varphi$, where the base configurations are $q\in F_m(\operatorname{int}D^2)$ and its truncation $q'$; under the isomorphism $\iota^F_*$ the map $\varphi$ corresponds to the open-disc forgetting map $p:(x_1,\dots,x_m)\mapsto(x_1,\dots,x_{m-1})$ ([[def-pure-braid-group-from-ordered-configurations]], [[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]], [[lem-interior-and-closed-disk-configuration-spaces-are-homotopy-equivalent]]).

[F3] At the base configuration $Q^{(n)}$ the kernel of the forgetting map $\varphi:PB_n\to PB_{n-1}$ is free with free basis $A_{1n},\dots,A_{n-1,n}$, the standard generators of the last column, interpreted through the isomorphism $\Psi$ of [F4] ([[lem-standard-pure-braids-generate-each-free-kernel]]).

[F4] The standard generators are the classes $A_{ij}=\Psi([W_{ij}])$ of the words $W_{ij}=\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i^2\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}$ (first-under-second stacking, empty outer blocks for $j=i+1$), where $\Psi([\beta])=(\iota^F_*[z_\beta])^{-1}$ for the coordinate path $z_\beta$ of a pure geometric braid $\beta$; $\Psi$ is an isomorphism $G_m^{\mathrm{pure}}\to PB_m$, so $A_{ij}=\iota^F_*((a_{ij})^{-1})$ for the open-disc class $a_{ij}=[z_{W_{ij}}]$, and the word identity gives $[\gamma\star\beta]=[\gamma][\beta]$ ([[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]], [[cor-pure-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-ordered-configurations]], [[def-pure-braid-group-from-ordered-configurations]]).

[F5] For the configuration $Q^{(m)}$ with $h_m=\frac{1}{4(m+1)}$ the half twist $\sigma_r$ ($1\le r\le m-1$) is the motion $(\sigma_r)_r=m_r+\rho$, $(\sigma_r)_{r+1}=m_r-\rho$, all other strands fixed, with midpoint $m_r=q_r+h_m$ and diamond path $\rho^{(m)}(t)=h_m\cdot(2t-1,-2t)$ for $t\le\frac12$ and $h_m\cdot(2t-1,2t-2)$ for $t\ge\frac12$; the opposite half twist $\sigma_r^-$ replaces $\rho$ by the reflection $\rho^-(t)=(\rho_1(t),-\rho_2(t))$, and $[\sigma_r^-]=[\sigma_r]^{-1}$ ([[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]]).

[F6] For a path $\gamma$ from $x_0$ to $x_1$ the radial-shell transport is an isomorphism $\beta_\gamma:\pi_m(X,x_1)\to\pi_m(X,x_0)$ with inverse transport by $\bar\gamma$, and in degree one $\beta_\gamma[a]=[\gamma*a*\bar\gamma]$; if $H$ is a homotopy of based cubes with basepoint track $\gamma$, meaning that every boundary face of the cube is mapped to $\gamma$, then $[H(-,0)]=\beta_\gamma[H(-,1)]$ (that is, $f_*=\beta_\gamma g_*$ for the corresponding maps). For $m=1$ the cube model is the loop model of the fundamental group ([[prop-higher-homotopy-basepoint-transport-and-moving-homotopies]], [[def-higher-homotopy-group-by-based-cubes]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction on $n$.

1.1 **Base case.** For $n=1$ the index set $\{1\le i<j\le1\}$ is empty, and the subgroup generated by the empty family is the trivial group, which equals $PB_1$ by [F2]. [base, F2]

1.2 **Induction hypothesis.** Fix $n\ge2$ and assume that the open-disc group $\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),Q^{(n-1)})$ is generated by the classes $a^{(n-1)}_{kl}=[z_{W_{kl}}]$ of the standard words at $Q^{(n-1)}$, $1\le k<l\le n-1$; equivalently, by [F4], that $PB_{n-1}$ is generated by the classes $A_{kl}$. [ih]

1.3 **Choice, the exact sequence, and the kernel.** By [F1] the Axiom of Choice [A1] yields DC, so the short exact sequence of [F2] is available at level $n$ and base configuration $Q^{(n)}$, with truncation $q'=(q^{(n)}_1,\dots,q^{(n)}_{n-1})$: the forgetting map $\varphi:PB_n\to PB_{n-1}$ has $\operatorname{im}\kappa=\ker\varphi$ and is surjective, and under the isomorphism $\iota^F_*$ it corresponds to the open-disc forgetting map $p:F_n(D^\circ)\to F_{n-1}(D^\circ)$, so $\ker p_*=(\iota^F_*)^{-1}(\ker\varphi)$. By [F3] the kernel $\ker\varphi$ is generated by $A_{1n},\dots,A_{n-1,n}$, and by [F4] $(\iota^F_*)^{-1}(A_{in})=(a^{(n)}_{in})^{-1}$; since a subgroup generated by elements equals the one generated by their inverses, $\ker p_*=\langle a^{(n)}_{1n},\dots,a^{(n)}_{n-1,n}\rangle$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.4 **The affine comparison of the two configurations.** Write $h_m=\frac{1}{4(m+1)}$ for the configurations of [F5], so that $h_{n-1}=\frac{n+1}{n}h_n$, and for $t\in I$ define the similarity $g_t(z):=(1+\frac{t}{n})z+t\,(h_{n-1},0)$ of $\mathbb R^2$, so that $g_0=\operatorname{id}$ and $g_1$ is the scaling $z\mapsto\frac{n+1}{n}z$ followed by the translation by $(h_{n-1},0)$. For $1\le j\le n-1$ one computes $g_1(q^{(n)}_j)=\frac{n+1}{n}(2j-n-1)h_n+(h_{n-1},0)=((2j-n-1)h_{n-1}+h_{n-1},0)=((2j-n)h_{n-1},0)=q^{(n-1)}_j$, and the same computation gives $g_1(m^{(n)}_r)=m^{(n-1)}_r$ for the midpoints, $1\le r\le n-2$. Since the diamond paths of [F5] are $\rho^{(m)}=h_m\rho^{(1)}$ for the fixed normalised shape $\rho^{(1)}$ given by $\rho^{(1)}(t)=(2t-1,-2t)$ for $t\le\frac12$ and $\rho^{(1)}(t)=(2t-1,2t-2)$ for $t\ge\frac12$, one gets $\frac{n+1}{n}\rho^{(n)}=h_{n-1}\rho^{(1)}=\rho^{(n-1)}$. Consequently $g_1(m_r^{(n)}\pm\rho^{(n)}(s))=g_1(m_r^{(n)})\pm\frac{n+1}{n}\rho^{(n)}(s)=m_r^{(n-1)}\pm\rho^{(n-1)}(s)$, and the same equality holds for the reflected displacement $\rho^-$; the similarity $g_1$ has real coefficients and therefore commutes with the reflection $R(x,y)=(x,-y)$ that defines $\sigma^-_r$ in [F5]. [F5]

2.1 **The comparison homotopy.** Fix $1\le i<j\le n-1$ (there are no such pairs when $n=2$). Let $z=z_{W^{(n)}_{ij}}$ be the coordinate path of the representative of $W_{ij}$ at $Q^{(n)}$ built from the motions of [F5], a loop in $F_n(D^\circ)$, and put $u:=[\upsilon]$ for the loop $\upsilon(s):=(z_1(s),\dots,z_{n-1}(s))$ in $F_{n-1}(D^\circ)$, so that $u=p_*(a^{(n)}_{ij})$; let $\zeta$ be the corresponding coordinate path of $W_{ij}$ at $Q^{(n-1)}$ and $v:=[\zeta]=a^{(n-1)}_{ij}$. Every strand of the word moves only inside the support discs $U_r$ of the half twists with $r\le n-2$ and off the last strand, so $|z_k(s)|\le(n-2)h_n+\frac32h_n=(n-\frac12)h_n$ for all $k,s$ (the fixed strands are the base points, of norm at most $(n-1)h_n$). Because each factor of the word and each factor of $\zeta$ is built from the same normalised diamond paths and the midpoints correspond under $g_1$ by step 1.4, and because $g_1$ respects stacking and time reparametrisation, the identities $g_1\circ\sigma_r^{(n)}=\sigma_r^{(n-1)}$, $g_1\circ(\sigma_r^-)^{(n)}=(\sigma_r^-)^{(n-1)}$ for $r\le n-2$ imply $g_1(\upsilon(s))=\zeta(s)$ for every $s$. Define $H(s,t):=g_t(\upsilon(s))$. By step 1.4 each $g_t$ is injective, so the $n-1$ coordinates of $H(s,t)$ are pairwise distinct, and $|H_k(s,t)|\le(1+\frac tn)(n-\frac12)h_n+t\,h_{n-1}\le\frac{n+1}{n}(n-\frac12)h_n+h_{n-1}=\frac{n+1/2}{4n}<1$, so $H$ takes values in $F_{n-1}(D^\circ)$ and is continuous; moreover $H(-,0)=\upsilon$, $H(-,1)=\zeta$, and the path $\eta(t):=g_t(q')$ satisfies $H(0,t)=g_t(\upsilon(0))=g_t(q')=\eta(t)=H(1,t)$ because $\upsilon(0)=\upsilon(1)=q'$. Thus $H$ is a homotopy of based $1$-cubes from $\upsilon$ to $\zeta$ whose boundary value is the path $\eta$ in $F_{n-1}(D^\circ)$ from $q'$ to $Q^{(n-1)}$. [step 1.4, F4, F5]

3.1 **Transporting along the affine path.** By step 2.1 the homotopy $H$ has basepoint track $\eta$, so the moving-homotopy transport identity of [F6] in degree one gives $u=\beta_\eta(v)$, that is $p_*(a^{(n)}_{ij})=\beta_\eta(a^{(n-1)}_{ij})$ for every $1\le i<j\le n-1$, where $\beta_\eta:\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),Q^{(n-1)})\to\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),q')$ is the isomorphism of [F6]. [step 2.1, F6]

4.1 **The images of the older generators generate the quotient.** Let $K:=\langle a^{(n)}_{ij}:1\le i<j\le n\rangle\le\pi_1(F_n(D^\circ),Q^{(n)})$ be the subgroup generated by all the raw standard words at level $n$. Since $p_*$ is a homomorphism and the index set splits into the cases $j\le n-1$ and $j=n$, one has $p_*(K)=\langle p_*(a^{(n)}_{ij}):1\le i<j\le n-1\rangle=\langle\beta_\eta(a^{(n-1)}_{ij}):1\le i<j\le n-1\rangle=\beta_\eta(\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),Q^{(n-1)}))$, where the last equality uses that the classes $a^{(n-1)}_{ij}$ generate the group by the induction hypothesis of step 1.2 and that $\beta_\eta$ is an isomorphism; hence $p_*(K)=\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),q')$. [step 1.2, step 3.1, F6]

5.1 **Lifting generation to level $n$.** By step 1.3 the classes $a^{(n)}_{1n},\dots,a^{(n)}_{n-1,n}$ lie in $K$ and generate $\ker p_*$, so $\ker p_*\subseteq K$. Let $g\in\pi_1(F_n(D^\circ),Q^{(n)})$. By step 4.1 there is $h\in K$ with $p_*(h)=p_*(g)$, hence $gh^{-1}\in\ker p_*\subseteq K$ and therefore $g=(gh^{-1})h\in K$. So $K=\pi_1(F_n(D^\circ),Q^{(n)})$. [step 1.3, step 4.1]

6.1 **The closed-disc statement and induction conclusion.** Applying the isomorphism $\iota^F_*$ of [F2] to the equality of step 5.1 gives $PB_n=\iota^F_*(K)=\langle\iota^F_*(a^{(n)}_{ij})\rangle$, and by [F4] $\iota^F_*(a^{(n)}_{ij})=A_{ij}^{-1}$, so $PB_n=\langle A_{ij}^{-1}:1\le i<j\le n\rangle=\langle A_{ij}:1\le i<j\le n\rangle$, which is the statement at level $n$; step 1.1 is the base case, so by induction $PB_n$ is generated by the standard generators for every $n\ge1$. [step 1.1, step 5.1, F2, F4, discharge-induction]

The comparison of the two base configurations is carried out by the explicit similarities $g_t$, so no Artin-presentation completeness is used: only the short exact sequence, the free kernel with its standard basis, and the geometric words enter. ∎
