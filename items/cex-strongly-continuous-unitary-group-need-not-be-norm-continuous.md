## Statement refuted

Assume the Axiom of Choice. On $H=L^2(\mathbb R)$ let $U(t)f(x)=e^{itx}f(x)$.
Then $U$ is a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group that is not norm
continuous: $\|U(t)-I\|=2$ for every $t\ne0$. Its generator is $iQ$, where $Q$
is the position operator, and no strongly continuous semigroup that is norm
continuous at zero has an unbounded generator; since $iQ$ is unbounded, norm
continuity fails, confirming the computation below.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] A family $U:\mathbb R\to\mathcal B(H)$ with $U(0)=I$, $U(s+t)=U(s)U(t)$ and unitary values is a strongly continuous unitary group exactly when the orbit maps are continuous; for $L^2$ functions, continuity follows from dominated convergence ([[def-strongly-continuous-one-parameter-unitary-group]], [[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[A2] For the position operator $Q$ the spectral PVM is $E(B)f=\mathbf 1_Bf$, so $e^{itQ}f(x)=e^{itx}f(x)$ by the functional calculus ([[ex-position-operator-on-l-two-of-r]]).

[A3] The group generated by a self-adjoint $T$ has generator $iT$ ([[lem-self-adjoint-operator-generates-a-strongly-continuous-unitary-group]]).

[A4] For a bounded Borel $h$ the norm of the multiplication operator $M_h$ on $L^2$ is the essential supremum of $|h|$: the upper bound is the norm identity of the calculus, and the lower bound follows by testing on indicator functions of sets where $|h|$ is close to that supremum; for $h_t(x)=e^{itx}-1$ with $t\ne0$ the essential supremum is $2$ ([[ex-unbounded-multiplication-operator-and-its-domain]], [[def-operator-norm]]).

[A5] A strongly continuous semigroup that is norm continuous at $0$ has a bounded generator (the integral $\int_0^\tau U(s)ds$ is invertible for small $\tau$ and $G=(\frac1\tau(U(\tau)-I))(\int_0^\tau U(s)ds)^{-1}$), so an unbounded generator forces failure of norm continuity ([[def-strongly-continuous-one-parameter-unitary-group]], [[def-operator-norm]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** $H=L^2(\mathbb R)$ and $U(t)f(x)=e^{itx}f(x)$.

1.1 $U$ is a unitary group: $|e^{itx}|=1$ makes each $U(t)$ isometric and onto, and $U(s)U(t)=U(s+t)$, $U(0)=I$ follow from the scalar identities. [A1, given]

1.2 $U$ is strongly continuous: for $f\in L^2$ one has $\|U(t)f-f\|^2=\int|e^{itx}-1|^2|f(x)|^2dx\to0$ by dominated convergence, the integrand being bounded by $4|f|^2$ and tending to $0$ pointwise. [A1, given]

1.3 $U(t)=e^{itQ}$ with $Q$ the position operator: by the position-operator example, $U(t)=\int e^{it\lambda}dE(\lambda)$ for the spectral PVM of $Q$, and the calculus identifies this with multiplication by $e^{itx}$; hence the generator of $U$ is $iQ$ by the generation lemma. [A2, A3]

1.4 Norm discontinuity: $U(t)-I$ is multiplication by $h_t(x)=e^{itx}-1$, an operator whose norm is the essential supremum $\|h_t\|_\infty=\sup_{x\in\mathbb R}|e^{itx}-1|=2$ for $t\ne0$; the upper bound is clear. For every $\varepsilon>0$ the set $E_\varepsilon:=\{|h_t|>2-\varepsilon\}$ has positive measure, so for some $N$ its intersection $E_{\varepsilon,N}:=E_\varepsilon\cap[-N,N]$ has positive finite measure. Testing on $1_{E_{\varepsilon,N}}/\sqrt{|E_{\varepsilon,N}|}\in L^2(\mathbb R)$ gives the reverse inequality. [A4, given]

2.1 The generator $iQ$ is unbounded by step 1.3, while the generator of a strongly continuous semigroup that is norm continuous at $0$ is bounded; hence $U$ is not norm continuous, and step 1.4 computes the norm distance $2$ directly. [A5, step 1.3, step 1.4] ∎
