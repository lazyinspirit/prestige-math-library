## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be an
algebraically closed field, let $X$ be a smooth finite-type $k$-scheme, let
$L$ be an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module, and let
$W\subseteq\Gamma(X,L)$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional linear system with
$\dim_kW=r+1$, base locus $\operatorname{Bs}(W)$ and
$X^\circ=X\smallsetminus\operatorname{Bs}(W)$
([[def-linear-system-base-locus]]).

Then there is a finite-type $k$-scheme $I$, the **incidence** of the linear
system, with $k$-morphisms $\pi:I\to X^\circ$ and
$p:I\to\mathbf P(W)=\mathbf P^r_k$, determined by $L$ and $W$ up to canonical
isomorphism, such that:

1. *(Local product.)* Let $s_0,\ldots,s_r$ be a $k$-basis of $W$ and let
   $X^\circ_j=X^\circ\smallsetminus|Z(s_j)|$ be the open subset on which
   $s_j$ is invertible. If $r\ge1$, then $\pi$ restricted over $X^\circ_j$
   exhibits $\pi^{-1}(X^\circ_j)$ as isomorphic over $X^\circ_j$ to the
   projection $X^\circ_j\times_k\mathbf P^{r-1}_k\to X^\circ_j$.
2. *(Fibres.)* For every $[s]\in\mathbf P(W)(k)$ the fibre $p^{-1}([s])$ is
   isomorphic over $X^\circ$ to the zero subscheme $Z(s)\cap X^\circ$ of the
   section $s$ ([[lem-zero-scheme-of-line-bundle-section]]).
3. *(Smoothness.)* The structure morphism $I\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is
   smooth in the local-standard-smooth sense
   ([[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]).
4. *(Small parameter space.)* If $r=0$, that is $\dim_kW=1$, then
   $I=\varnothing$.

The construction uses the actual equations $\sum_i\xi_ig_i$ of the members,
with no reducedness or nonzerodivisor hypothesis on the local equations.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An algebraically closed field $k$; a smooth finite-type $k$-scheme $X$; an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module $L$; a nonzero finite-dimensional linear system $W\subseteq\Gamma(X,L)$ with $\dim_kW=r+1$, base locus $\operatorname{Bs}(W)$ and $X^\circ=X\smallsetminus\operatorname{Bs}(W)$; and a $k$-basis $s_0,\ldots,s_r$ of $W$.

[F1] [[def-linear-system-base-locus]]: $Z(s)$ is the zero subscheme of a nonzero section and $\operatorname{Bs}(W)=\bigcap_{0\ne s\in W}|Z(s)|$; the base locus is closed.

[F2] [[lem-zero-scheme-of-line-bundle-section]]: on an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $L|_V$ generated by $e$ and $s|_V=fe$, the zero subscheme $Z(s)\cap V$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/(f))$; the construction is independent of the trivialization.

[F3] [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]]: the standard charts $U_j=\operatorname{Spec}k[x^{(j)}_\ell:\ell\ne j]$ of $\mathbf P^r_k$ are affine, cover $\mathbf P^r_k$, and on $U_j\cap U_{j'}$ the coordinates satisfy $x^{(j)}_\ell=x^{(j')}_\ell/x^{(j')}_j$ for $\ell\ne j,j'$ and $x^{(j)}_{j'}=1/x^{(j')}_j$.

[F4] [[thm-gluing-affine-schemes]]: compatible open immersions of affine schemes along principal opens glue to a scheme with the given affine cover.

[F5] [[thm-fibre-products-of-schemes-exist]]: fibre products of $k$-schemes exist, and over affine charts the product has the affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_kB)$.

[F6] [[def-closed-immersion-schemes]]: a quotient $A\to A/J$ of a commutative ring presents a closed immersion $\operatorname{Spec}(A/J)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Spec}A$.

[F7] [[def-smooth-morphism-classical]]: a finite-type morphism is smooth when at every source point there are affine neighbourhoods on which the ring map is standard smooth at the corresponding prime; the condition is local on the source.

[F8] [[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]]: a polynomial algebra $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is standard smooth over $k$ with no equations ($c=0$), and the case $c=0$ is a localisation of a polynomial ring.

[F9] [[lem-smoothness-stable-under-product-classical]]: the scheme-theoretic product of finite-type $k$-schemes smooth over a field $k$ is smooth over $k$.

[F10] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is assumed and is spent through the declared suppliers.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Local affine models. Let $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$ be an affine open on which $L$ has a generator $e$, and write $s_i|_V=g_ie$ with $g_i\in A$; such charts exist because $L$ is invertible and $X$ is quasi-compact. For each chart $U_j=\operatorname{Spec}k[x^{(j)}_\ell:\ell\ne j]$ of $\mathbf P^r_k$ put $$I_{V,j}:=\operatorname{Spec}\!\left(A[x^{(j)}_\ell:\ell\ne j]\big/\bigl(g_j+\textstyle\sum_{\ell\ne j}g_\ell x^{(j)}_\ell\bigr)\right)\ \subseteq\ V\times_kU_j,$$ the closed subscheme of the product cut out by the equation $\sum_i\xi_ig_i=0$ written in the chart $x^{(j)}_j=1$. This is a quotient presentation of a closed subscheme of the affine product [F5, F6], and the equation is the local equation of the general member in the sense of [F1] and [F2]. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, given, construct]
1.2 Independence of the choices. If $e'=ue$ is a second generator on $V$ with $u\in A^\times$ and $s_i|_V=g_i'e'$, then $g_i'=u^{-1}g_i$, so the ideal $\bigl(g_j'+\sum_{\ell\ne j}g_\ell'x_\ell\bigr)=u^{-1}\bigl(g_j+\sum_{\ell\ne j}g_\ell x_\ell\bigr)$ is unchanged, and the two closed subschemes of $V\times_kU_j$ coincide. On the overlap $U_j\cap U_{j'}$ of the two standard charts, the transition formulas of [F3] identify the coordinates $x^{(j)}_\ell=\xi_\ell/\xi_j$ and $x^{(j')}_\ell=\xi_\ell/\xi_{j'}$ for the same homogeneous coordinates $\xi$, so substituting $x^{(j)}_\ell=x^{(j')}_\ell/x^{(j')}_j$ and $x^{(j)}_{j'}=1/x^{(j')}_j$ into the first equation and multiplying by the unit $x^{(j')}_j$ gives exactly the second equation $$g_{j'}+\sum_{\ell\ne j'}g_\ell x^{(j')}_\ell =x^{(j')}_j\Bigl(g_j+\sum_{\ell\ne j}g_\ell x^{(j)}_\ell\Bigr).$$ Hence the local models agree on all overlaps of base charts and projective charts. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]
2.1 Gluing. The affine schemes $I_{V,j}$, indexed by a finite trivializing affine cover of $X$ and by $j=0,\ldots,r$, have pairwise compatible open immersions on their overlaps by step 1.2, so they glue along the principal opens of [F4] to a $k$-scheme $I_X$ together with a closed immersion $I_X\hookrightarrow X\times_k\mathbf P^r_k$ commuting with the two projections $\pi_X:I_X\to X$ and $p:I_X\to\mathbf P^r_k$. The scheme $I_X$ is finite type over $k$ because it is covered by the finitely many affine charts $I_{V,j}$, each a quotient of a finitely generated polynomial algebra over $k$. Define the incidence of the linear system to be the open subscheme $I:=I_X\times_XX^\circ$ obtained by base change along the open immersion $X^\circ\hookrightarrow X$, and keep $\pi:I\to X^\circ$ and $p:I\to\mathbf P^r_k$ for the restrictions. [F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]
3.1 Local product structure. Fix $j$ and let $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X^\circ_j$ be an affine chart trivializing $L$ by $e$, with $s_i|_V=g_ie$. Since $V\cap|Z(s_j)|=\varnothing$ and $Z(s_j)\cap V$ is cut out by $g_j$ by [F2], the function $g_j$ lies in no maximal ideal of $A$, hence $g_j\in A^\times$. Assume $r\ge1$. For an incidence point $[\xi_0:\cdots:\xi_r]$ over $V$, the equation $\sum_i g_i\xi_i=0$ and invertibility of $g_j$ imply that some $\xi_\ell$ with $\ell\ne j$ is nonzero: otherwise also $\xi_j=0$. Thus projection to the other coordinates defines an everywhere-defined map to $\mathbf P^{r-1}_k$, and its inverse is $$[\eta_\ell]_{\ell\ne j}\longmapsto\left[\xi_j=-g_j^{-1}\sum_{\ell\ne j}g_\ell\eta_\ell:\ \xi_\ell=\eta_\ell\ (\ell\ne j)\right].$$ To check scheme morphisms rather than only point maps, choose $\ell_0\ne j$ and work on the chart $\eta_{\ell_0}\ne0$, equivalently the source chart $\xi_{\ell_0}\ne0$. Normalize $\xi_{\ell_0}=1$. Its incidence ring is $$A[y_m:m\ne\ell_0]\big/\bigl(g_{\ell_0}+g_jy_j+\textstyle\sum_{m\ne j,\ell_0}g_my_m\bigr)\ \cong\ A[y_m:m\ne j,\ell_0],$$ where the isomorphism eliminates $y_j$ by $y_j=-g_j^{-1}(g_{\ell_0}+\sum_{m\ne j,\ell_0}g_my_m)$. These charts cover the incidence over $V$, and their maps agree on overlaps because the displayed homogeneous formulas are scale invariant. They glue to $\pi^{-1}(V)\cong V\times_k\mathbf P^{r-1}_k$ over $V$, and the identifications agree under a change of trivialization of $L$ since all $g_i$ acquire the same unit factor. The affine $V$ cover $X^\circ_j$, giving the asserted product over $X^\circ_j$. In particular, the chart $\xi_j\ne0$ alone need not cover the incidence; the eliminated coordinate is $\xi_j$, while the covering charts have $\xi_{\ell_0}\ne0$ for $\ell_0\ne j$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]
3.2 Fibres. Let $[s]\in\mathbf P(W)(k)$, choose $0\ne s\in W$ representing it and a $k$-basis $s_0,\ldots,s_r$ with $s=s_0$. Over an affine chart $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X^\circ$ trivializing $L$ by $e$ with $s_i|_V=g_ie$, the point $[s]$ lies in the chart $U_0$ of $\mathbf P^r_k$; the fibre of $p$ over it is obtained by substituting the coordinates $(1,0,\ldots,0)$ into $g_0+\sum_{\ell\ne0}g_\ell x^{(0)}_\ell$, that is, it is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/(g_0))=Z(s_0)\cap V$ by [F2]. Since the trivializing charts cover $X^\circ$, this identifies the fibre with $Z(s)\cap X^\circ$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]
4.1 Smoothness. Let $x\in I$. By step 3.1 some open neighbourhood of $x$ in $I$ is isomorphic over a smooth open subscheme of $X^\circ$ to $U\times_k\mathbf P^{r-1}_k$ when $r\ge1$, while for $r=0$ the incidence is empty and the claim is vacuous. The open subscheme $X^\circ_j$ of the smooth $X$ is smooth over $k$ because smoothness is local on the source [F7]. The projective space $\mathbf P^{r-1}_k$ is smooth over $k$: its standard charts are polynomial algebras $k[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}]$, which are standard smooth with $c=0$ by [F8], and smoothness is local on the source [F7]. By [F9] each product $X^\circ_j\times_k\mathbf P^{r-1}_k$ is smooth over $k$, and these open pieces cover $I$, so $I\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth by [F7]. The Axiom of Choice enters only through the declared suppliers, notably [F9]; the finitely many charts and basis elements chosen here are finite choices and need no choice principle. [F7, F8, F9, F10, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]
5.1 The case $r=0$, and the boundary dispositions. If $r=0$ then $W=ks_0$ for $0\ne s_0\in W$, so $\operatorname{Bs}(W)=|Z(s_0)|$ and $X^\circ=X\smallsetminus|Z(s_0)|$. On every trivializing affine chart $V\subseteq X^\circ$ the coefficient $g_0$ is a unit by the argument of step 3.1, so the equation $g_0=0$ cuts out the empty subscheme; as the charts cover $X^\circ$, indeed $I=\varnothing$, and the structural claims are vacuous. If $X=\varnothing$ then $\Gamma(X,L)=0$, so the hypothesis $W\ne0$ has no instance. If $X^\circ=\varnothing$, the incidence $I$ is empty by its definition, and the local product and fibre clauses are vacuous. The construction is canonical: a change of $k$-basis of $W$ multiplies the vector of coefficients $(\xi_i)$ by an invertible constant matrix and hence induces an automorphism of $\mathbf P^r_k$ carrying the equation $\sum_i\xi_ig_i=0$ to itself, and a change of trivialization multiplies all $g_i$ by a unit; so $I$ is determined up to unique isomorphism compatible with both $\pi$ and $p$, equivalently over $X^\circ\times_k\mathbf P(W)$. Uniqueness follows because the glued quotient maps define a closed immersion into that product: a morphism over the product must be the identity on each quotient chart. This completes the proof. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
