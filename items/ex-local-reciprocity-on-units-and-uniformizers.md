## Example

Assume AC and DC. Let $K$ be a finite-residue nonarchimedean local
field with uniformizer $\pi$, and let $L/K$ be the unramified extension of
degree $d\ge1$. Under arithmetic-Frobenius normalization, every unit of
$\mathcal O_K^\times$ maps trivially to $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$,
$\pi$ maps to arithmetic Frobenius, and
$$N_{L/K}L^\times=\pi^{d\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_K^\times.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and DC; the unramified extension; its degree; and the local Artin normalization.

[F1] A uniformizer maps to arithmetic Frobenius on every finite unramified extension. [[thm-local-artin-unramified-frobenius-normalization]]

[F2] The residue field extension is Galois of degree $d$, and its Galois group is cyclic generated by arithmetic Frobenius. [[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]]

[F3] Reduction identifies the Galois group of a finite unramified extension with the Galois group of its residue extension. [[thm-finite-unramified-local-extensions-classified-by-residue-extensions]]

[F4] The field norm is the product of the Galois conjugates in this finite Galois extension. [[def-field-norm-and-trace]] [[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]]

[F5] The multiplicative group of each finite residue field is cyclic. [[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

[F6] The unique extension of the absolute value is preserved by each $K$-automorphism, so each such automorphism is continuous. [[thm-unique-extension-of-a-nonarchimedean-absolute-value]]

[A1] Dependent Choice is inherited from the finite local reciprocity convention. [[def-dependent-choice]]

[A2] AC is required by the local class-formation supplier used in the local Artin theorem. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[A3] AC implies Countable Choice, which supplies the hypothesis of the finite unramified-extension classification used here. [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]]

## Verification

1.1 Let $k_L/k_K$ have degree $d$ and $|k_K|=q$. By [F4], the residue norm is the finite-field norm $x\mapsto x^{(q^d-1)/(q-1)}$. A generator of $k_L^\times$ exists by [F5], and its norm has order $q-1$, so the residue norm is onto. For $r\ge1$, write $U_{r,L}=1+\mathfrak m_L^r$. Since $L/K$ is unramified, the same $\pi$ is a uniformizer in both fields. For $x\in\mathcal O_L$, [F4] gives $$N_{L/K}(1+\pi^r x)=\prod_{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)}(1+\pi^r\sigma x)\equiv1+\pi^r\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x)\pmod{\pi^{r+1}}.$$ The reduced trace is $z\mapsto z+z^q+\cdots+z^{q^{d-1}}$ on $k_L$. This is a nonzero polynomial of degree $q^{d-1}<q^d$, so the trace is a nonzero $k_K$-linear map to the one-dimensional space $k_K$ and is onto. Starting with a lift of a residue-norm preimage, correct a target unit successively at orders $r=1,2,\ldots$ using this trace surjectivity. The correcting factors lie in $U_{r,L}$, so their partial products converge in the complete field $L$. By [F4] and [F6], the norm is a finite product of continuous automorphisms and hence continuous; its value at the limit is the target unit. Thus every unit of $K$ is a norm. [A1, A2, A3, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, algebra, choose]

2.1 The valuation of a norm is the sum of the valuations of the $d$ conjugates by [F4]. Each conjugate preserves the valuation by [F6], so every norm has valuation in $d\mathbb Z$. The same uniformizer $\pi$ in $L$ is fixed by every $K$-automorphism, hence has norm $\pi^d$; every unit of $K$ is a norm by step 1.1. Therefore the norm group is $\pi^{d\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_K^\times$. [F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Reduction identifies the Galois group with the cyclic group of order $d$, generated by arithmetic Frobenius, by [F2], [F3], and [F5]. By [F1], $\pi$ maps to that generator. The norm quotient has order $d$ by the valuation description in step 2.1, so its kernel is exactly the norm group; the unit subgroup maps trivially. [A1, A2, A3, F1, F2, F3, F5, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
