## Statement

Let $F$ be any field, let $q\in F^\times$ have finite quantum characteristic
$e=e(F,q)\ge2$, let $\Lambda\in P^+$ be dominant and let $\alpha\in Q_+$ have
height $d$ with $e_\alpha\ne0$, in the conventions of
[[def-affine-and-cyclotomic-type-a-hecke-algebras]] and
[[def-residue-sequence-idempotents-and-cyclotomic-hecke-blocks]]. Then the
homomorphisms $\rho\colon R^\Lambda_\alpha\to H^\Lambda_\alpha$ of
[[lem-hecke-intertwiners-produce-local-klr-generators]] and
$\sigma\colon H^\Lambda_\alpha\to R^\Lambda_\alpha$ of
[[lem-cyclotomic-klr-block-satisfies-the-hecke-relations]] are mutually
inverse $F$-algebra isomorphisms. Equivalently, $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ is
generated by the Khovanov–Lauda generators
$\{e(i)\}\cup\{y_r\}\cup\{\psi_r\}$ subject only to the relations
(1.6)–(1.15), and the isomorphism is normalized by
$\rho(e(i))=e(i)$ and
$$\rho(y_re(i))=(1-q^{-i_r}X_r)e(i)\quad(q\ne1),\qquad \rho(y_re(i))=(x_r-i_r)e(i)\quad(q=1).$$
The degenerate ($q=1$, $\operatorname{char}F=e$) and nondegenerate
($q\ne1$) cases are proved separately, and both hold in finite quantum
characteristic $e$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The residue-projector/block definition [F1], the early central
sector isomorphism [F2], the local KLR generator construction [F3], and the
inverse Hecke-relations map [F4].

[F1] The residue-sequence idempotents $e(\nu)$, the central sums $e_\alpha$
and the direct decomposition
$H^\Lambda_d=\bigoplus_\alpha H^\Lambda_\alpha$, with every nonzero
$e_\alpha$ primitive and every nonzero $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ an actual block; at
level one the JM generators correspond to $X_r=q^j(1+(q-1)L_r)$
respectively $x_r=j+L_r$
([[def-residue-sequence-idempotents-and-cyclotomic-hecke-blocks]]).

[F2] The explicit maps $\rho\colon R^\Lambda_\alpha\to H^\Lambda_\alpha$ and
$\sigma\colon H^\Lambda_\alpha\to R^\Lambda_\alpha$ are mutually inverse for
every content $\alpha$, including zero sectors, with $H^\Lambda_\alpha=0$ iff
$R^\Lambda_\alpha=0$ and
$H^\Lambda_d\cong\bigoplus_\alpha R^\Lambda_\alpha$; on idempotents the maps
are the literal identity and the dot image is $x_r-i_r$ in the degenerate
branch and $1-q^{-i_r}X_r$ in the nondegenerate branch
([[thm-cyclotomic-hecke-central-sectors-are-isomorphic-to-type-a-cyclotomic-klr-algebras]]).

[F3] The elements $\{e(i)\}\cup\{y_r\}\cup\{\psi_r\}$ satisfy the relations
(1.6)–(1.15) and define the unique homomorphism $\rho$ with
$e(i)\mapsto e(i)$, $y_r\mapsto y_r$, $\psi_r\mapsto\psi_r$
([[lem-hecke-intertwiners-produce-local-klr-generators]]).

[F4] The elements $x_r,s_r$ respectively $X_r,T_r$ satisfy the affine Hecke
and cyclotomic relations, so there is a unique homomorphism $\sigma$ killing
$e_\beta$ and every corner $e_\beta H^\Lambda_de_\gamma$ with
$(\beta,\gamma)\ne(\alpha,\alpha)$
([[lem-cyclotomic-klr-block-satisfies-the-hecke-relations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The field $F$, the finite quantum characteristic $e$, the dominant

weight $\Lambda$, the content $\alpha$ of height $d$ with $e_\alpha\ne0$, and

the suppliers of [F1]–[F4].

1.1 The mutually inverse early maps. By [F3] the local KLR generators of the nonzero sector satisfy the KLR relations, so there is a unique algebra homomorphism $\rho\colon R^\Lambda_\alpha\to H^\Lambda_\alpha$ sending $e(i)\mapsto e(i)$, $y_r\mapsto y_r$ and $\psi_r\mapsto\psi_r$; by [F4] the Hecke generators $X_r,T_r$ (respectively $x_r,s_r$) of the sector satisfy the affine Hecke and cyclotomic relations, so there is a unique homomorphism $\sigma\colon H^\Lambda_\alpha\to R^\Lambda_\alpha$ with $\sigma(e(i))=e(i)$ for $i\in I_\alpha$. The early central sector isomorphism [F2] proves, by explicit generator composites that are literally the identity, that $\rho$ and $\sigma$ are mutually inverse over $F$ for every content, including the zero sectors, with $H^\Lambda_\alpha=0$ if and only if $R^\Lambda_\alpha=0$. No algebraic closure, root extraction, dimension argument or primitive-block premise was used to construct these maps. [F2, F3, F4]

1.2 Primitivity of the sector. By [F1] every nonzero $e_\alpha$ is primitive and every nonzero $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ is an actual block. Hence for the content $\alpha$ of the Statement the sector $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ is a block of $H^\Lambda_d$, and the block terminology in the Statement is justified rather than assumed. [F1]

2.1 The blockwise isomorphism and its normalization. Restricting the mutually inverse maps of step 1.1 to the nonzero sector $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ gives mutually inverse unital $F$-algebra isomorphisms $\rho\colon R^\Lambda_\alpha\to H^\Lambda_\alpha$ and $\sigma\colon H^\Lambda_\alpha\to R^\Lambda_\alpha$. The defining formulas of $\rho$ give $\rho(e(i))=e(i)$ and the stated dot normalization $\rho(y_re(i))=(1-q^{-i_r}X_r)e(i)$ in the nondegenerate case and $\rho(y_re(i))=(x_r-i_r)e(i)$ in the degenerate case, matching the dot images of [F2]. Because $R^\Lambda_\alpha$ is the algebra generated by $\{e(i)\}\cup\{y_r\}\cup\{\psi_r\}$ subject only to the defining KLR relations and $\rho$ is an isomorphism, $H^\Lambda_\alpha$ is generated by the images of those generators subject only to those relations. The degenerate and nondegenerate cases were proved separately by [F2]–[F4] and both hold in finite quantum characteristic $e$, with no characteristic-zero or level-one restriction. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎

## Remarks

The theorem is the Brundan–Kleshchev identification of each nonzero cyclotomic
Hecke block with the corresponding cyclotomic Khovanov–Lauda block. The
isomorphism is exhibited by explicit mutually inverse maps, and the later
primitivity theorem is used only to justify the block terminology; no choice
principle is used.
