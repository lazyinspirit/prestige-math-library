## Statement

For finite Galois L/K and nonzero $P\mid p$, the arithmetic Frobenius coset has order $f(P/p)$ in D/I. If P is unramified, $\operatorname{Frob}_P$ has the same order in D.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data and hypotheses of the statement.

[F1] [[def-arithmetic-frobenius-coset]]: For finite Galois L/K and nonzero $P\mid p$, the **arithmetic Frobenius coset** is the unique element of $D(P/p)/I(P/p)$ corresponding under the residue isomorphism to $x\mapsto x^{Np}$ on $\kappa(P)$, where $Np=|\kappa(p)|$. It is defined also when P is ramified. Its inverse is called geometric Frobenius. The quotient element is distinguished; a representative in D need not be unique.

[F2] [[thm-unramified-frobenius-element-exists-uniquely]]: For finite Galois L/K and a nonzero prime $P\mid p$ with $e(P/p)=1$, there is a unique $\operatorname{Frob}_P\in D(P/p)$ satisfying $\operatorname{Frob}_P(a)\equiv a^{Np}\pmod P\qquad(a\in\mathcal O_L).$ It is the arithmetic Frobenius element, the unique lift of the arithmetic Frobenius coset.

[F3] [[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]]: Let $\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field of order $q$ and let $E$ be a finite field having $\mathbb F_q$ as a subfield, with $[E:\mathbb F_q]=n$ (def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension). Then $E/\mathbb F_q$ is a finite Galois extension (def-finite-galois-extension-and-galois-group) and $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_q)=\langle\sigma_q\rangle$ is cyclic of order $n$, generated by the relative Frobenius $\sigma_q\colon x\mapsto x^{q}$ (def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension).

## Proof

1.1 The isomorphism defining the coset carries it to the $Np$-power automorphism of $\kappa(P)/\kappa(p)$. That automorphism has order $[\kappa(P):\kappa(p)]=f(P/p)$, and an isomorphism preserves the order of every element. [F1, F3]

2.1 In the unramified case the lift is through the isomorphism $D\to D/I$, so its order is also f. When f=1 the coset, and its unramified lift, are identities. [F2, step 1.1] ∎
