## Example

Let $X_1,X_2,\ldots$ be IID with law $P$, where $P|X|<\infty$ and $PX^2<\infty$. Let $\Theta=\mathbb R$ with its usual metric and set $m_\theta(x)=-(x-\theta)^2$. For a finite observed sample $x_1,\ldots,x_n$, the empirical criterion $P_nm_\theta$ has the unique maximizer $\bar x_n=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i$. The population criterion $Pm_\theta$ has the unique maximizer $\mu=PX$. The map from a sample to $\bar x_n$ is measurable, so it is an exact M-estimator.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite real sample $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ and, for the population claim, a probability law $P$ on $\mathbb R$ with $P|X|<\infty$ and $PX^2<\infty$.

[F1] [[def-m-estimator]]: An M-estimator is a measurable exact or approximate maximizer or minimizer of the empirical criterion $P_nm_\theta$.

[F2] [[def-estimator-sequence-in-iid-experiments]]: An estimator sequence is a sequence of measurable maps on the finite IID product sample spaces.

[F3] [[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]: The usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is $d(x,y)=|x-y|$.

[F4] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]: IID means a common law and mutual independence of the whole family.

[A1] The sample space is $\mathbb R^n$ with product topology and Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets. For finite $n$, rational open boxes form a countable basis, so this Borel sigma-algebra is $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)^{\otimes n}$. The criterion is $m_\theta(x)=-(x-\theta)^2$.

[A2] The population law has finite first and second absolute moments as stated.

## Verification

1.1 Write $\bar x_n=n^{-1}\sum_i x_i$. Expanding both sides gives $\sum_i(x_i-\theta)^2=\sum_i(x_i-\bar x_n)^2+n(\theta-\bar x_n)^2$, because $\sum_i(x_i-\bar x_n)=0$. Thus $P_nm_\theta=P_nm_{\bar x_n}-(\theta-\bar x_n)^2$, which is uniquely maximized at $\bar x_n$. [A1, given, algebra]

1.2 Let $\mu=PX$. Expanding $X-\theta=(X-\mu)+(\mu-\theta)$ and using $P(X-\mu)=0$ gives $Pm_\theta=-P[(X-\mu)^2]-(\theta-\mu)^2$. The moment assumptions make the mean and centered second moment finite, so this is uniquely maximized at $\mu$. [A2, given, algebra]

2.1 The IID sequence in [F4] gives the repeated-sampling context. If every coordinate of $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$ differs by less than $\delta$, then $|\bar x_n(x)-\bar x_n(y)|\le n^{-1}\sum_i|x_i-y_i|<\delta$. Hence the sample-mean map is continuous for the product topology. Inverse images of open sets are open, so the map is Borel measurable under [A1]; it is an estimator sequence by [F2] and is an exact M-estimator by [F1] and step 1.1. The target metric is the usual metric in [F3]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎

This example identifies the finite-sample and population criteria. It does not claim the global uniform-convergence premise on the noncompact set $\mathbb R$; that premise must be checked separately before applying the consistency theorem.
