## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$ and let $B_0, \dots, B_{m-1}$ be a finite list of half-open boxes
in $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([[def-half-open-box]]), where $B_j = B(a^j,b^j)$. For $i < n$
put

$$C_i \;:=\; \{-\infty, +\infty\} \cup \{\, a^j_i : j<m \,\} \cup \{\, b^j_i : j<m \,\} \;\subseteq\; \overline{\mathbb{R}},$$

a finite set with at least two members, and let
$c_{i,0} < c_{i,1} < \dots < c_{i,N_i}$ be its increasing enumeration, so that
$c_{i,0} = -\infty$, $c_{i,N_i} = +\infty$ and $N_i \ge 1$. The **cells of the
grid generated by the list** are the half-open boxes

$$Q_k \;:=\; B\big((c_{i,k_i})_{i<n},\ (c_{i,k_i+1})_{i<n}\big), \qquad k_i < N_i \ \ (i<n).$$

Then:

1. the cells are pairwise disjoint and their union is $\mathbb{R}^n$;
2. for every $j < m$, a cell that meets $B_j$ is contained in $B_j$, and $B_j$ is
   the union of the cells contained in it;
3. consequently every elementary set ([[def-elementary-set-in-rn]]) is the union
   of a finite list of pairwise disjoint half-open boxes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n \ge 1$, a finite list $B_0,\dots,B_{m-1}$ of half-open boxes with parameter pairs $(a^j,b^j)$, and the sets $C_i$ and cells $Q_k$ displayed in the Statement.

[L1] $B(a,b) := \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : a_i < x_i \le b_i \ \text{ for every } i < n \,\}$ ([[def-half-open-box]]).

[L2] A subset $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ is an **elementary set** when there are a natural number $m$ and a list $B_0, \dots, B_{m-1}$ of half-open boxes with $E=\bigcup_{j<m}B_j$ ([[def-elementary-set-in-rn]]).

[F1] $(\overline{\mathbb{R}}, \le)$ is a totally ordered set, and the inclusion of $\mathbb{R}$ preserves and reflects the order; $-\infty$ is the least and $+\infty$ the greatest element of $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$, and $-\infty < x < +\infty$ for every $x \in \mathbb{R}$ ([[def-extended-reals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Each $C_i$ is a finite subset of the totally ordered set $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ containing the two distinct elements $-\infty$ and $+\infty$, so it has a unique strictly increasing enumeration $c_{i,0}<\dots<c_{i,N_i}$ with $N_i \ge 1$, and its least and greatest members are $c_{i,0}=-\infty$ and $c_{i,N_i}=+\infty$. [F1]

1.2 For claim 1, distinct multi-indices $k \ne k'$ differ at some $i$, say $k_i < k'_i$, whence $k_i + 1 \le k'_i$ and $c_{i,k_i+1} \le c_{i,k'_i}$; a common point $x$ would satisfy both $x_i \le c_{i,k_i+1}$ and $c_{i,k'_i} < x_i$, which is impossible, so the cells are pairwise disjoint. [L1, F1]

1.3 For claim 1 again, given $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$ and $i<n$, the set $\{\, r \le N_i : c_{i,r} < x_i \,\}$ contains $0$ because $c_{i,0} = -\infty < x_i$ and omits $N_i$ because $c_{i,N_i} = +\infty$ is not below the real $x_i$, so it has a greatest member $k_i$ with $k_i < N_i$, and then $c_{i,k_i} < x_i \le c_{i,k_i+1}$; the multi-index $k$ so obtained puts $x$ in $Q_k$. [L1, F1]

1.4 For claim 2, suppose $x \in Q_k \cap B_j$ and fix $i<n$. From $a^j_i < x_i \le c_{i,k_i+1}$ and $a^j_i \in C_i$ it follows that $a^j_i < c_{i,k_i+1}$, and the members of $C_i$ strictly below $c_{i,k_i+1}$ are exactly $c_{i,0},\dots,c_{i,k_i}$, so $a^j_i \le c_{i,k_i}$; from $c_{i,k_i} < x_i \le b^j_i$ and $b^j_i \in C_i$ it follows that $c_{i,k_i} < b^j_i$, and the members of $C_i$ strictly above $c_{i,k_i}$ are exactly $c_{i,k_i+1},\dots,c_{i,N_i}$, so $c_{i,k_i+1} \le b^j_i$. [L1, F1]

2.1 For claim 2, step 1.4 gives $a^j_i \le c_{i,k_i}$ and $c_{i,k_i+1} \le b^j_i$ in every coordinate, hence $Q_k \subseteq B_j$; and every point of $B_j$ lies in some cell by step 1.3, that cell then meeting $B_j$ and so contained in it, so $B_j$ is exactly the union of the cells contained in it. [step 1.3, step 1.4, L1]

3.1 For claim 3, let $E = \bigcup_{j<m}B_j$ be elementary; by step 2.1 each $B_j$ is the union of the cells contained in it, so $E$ is the union of those cells that are contained in at least one $B_j$, and by step 1.2 these finitely many cells are pairwise disjoint; listing them proves claim 3, while claims 1 and 2 are steps 1.2, 1.3 and 2.1. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, L2] ∎
