## Statement

Chow and Technau's Theorem 1.5 states the following.

For every $\epsilon>0$, every irrational $\alpha\in K$, and every $\gamma\in\mathbb R$, there is a set $G\subseteq\mathrm{Bad}$ with $\dim_HG=1$ such that for every $\beta\in G$ and every $\delta\in\mathbb R$, infinitely many integers $n\ge3$ satisfy
$$n\,\|n\alpha-\gamma\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}\,\|n\beta-\delta\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}<\frac{(\log\log n)^{3+\epsilon}}{\log n},$$
This is a partial inhomogeneous bound, not a proof of Littlewood’s conjecture for every pair.

Here $K=\{\alpha\in\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Q:\sup_j j^{-1}\log q_j(\alpha)<\infty\}$ for the regular continued-fraction denominators $q_j(\alpha)$, and $\mathrm{Bad}=\{\beta:\inf_{q\ge1}q\|q\beta\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}>0\}$.



Also, $\dim_H$ denotes Hausdorff dimension.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** explicit finite-prefix expectations, compact exceptional-set avoidance, and the translated rational-convergent grid.

1.1 We establish auxiliary Claim 1: Let $B$ be a nonempty finite set with a fixed linear order, let $\nu:B\to(0,1]$ satisfy $\sum_{b\in B}\nu(b)=1$, and put $\Omega=B^{\mathbb N}$ with its prefix metric $d(\omega,\omega')=2^{-m}$, where $m$ is the first position at which they differ, and distance zero for equal sequences. For every real continuous $f$ on $\Omega$ define $$L(f)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{w\in B^n}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^n\nu(w_i)\Bigr)f(wb_0b_0\cdots),$$ where $b_0$ is the least element of $B$. The limit exists, and $L$ is a positive normalized linear functional with $|L(f)|\le\|f\|_\infty$. For each integer $k\ge0$, $$L(f)=\sum_{w\in B^k}\Bigl(\prod_{i=1}^k\nu(w_i)\Bigr)L(f_w),\qquad f_w(\omega)=f(w\omega).$$ For every length-$k$ prefix cylinder $[w]$, its indicator is continuous and has $L(\mathbf1_{[w]})=\prod_{i=1}^k\nu(w_i)$. The same construction applies componentwise to complex continuous functions. No form of Choice is assumed. Input (a): Determined recursion on the natural numbers produces a unique sequence from an explicit initial value and successor rule. [[thm-recursion]] Input (b): Every real Cauchy sequence converges, without a choice principle. [[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]] [F1, F2, given]

2.1 We first prove uniform continuity without choosing infinitely many witnesses. For a fixed $\epsilon>0$, call a finite word $w$ bad if there are two sequences extending $w$ on which $f$ differs by at least $\epsilon$. If bad words occur at every length, the empty word has bad extensions at arbitrarily large lengths. A word with arbitrarily long bad extensions has at least one child with arbitrarily long bad extensions, because it has only finitely many children. By input (a) choose the least such child at every stage, obtaining a determined infinite sequence $\omega$. Continuity of $f$ at $\omega$ supplies a prefix length $m$ on whose cylinder $f$ differs from $f(\omega)$ by less than $\epsilon/2$. No two sequences in that cylinder can differ in $f$ by $\epsilon$, contradicting the choice of the length-$m$ prefix. Thus for every $\epsilon>0$ some $m$ makes the oscillation of $f$ on every length-$m$ cylinder less than $\epsilon$. The finitely many length-$m$ representatives then show that $f$ is bounded. [given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $L_n(f)$ be the finite sum in auxiliary Claim 1. Its coefficients are nonnegative and sum to1. For $n\ge m$, refine a length-$n$ prefix to any length $n'\ge n$: all refined representative sequences have the same length-$m$ prefix as the original representative, and the total refined weight equals the original weight. step 2.1 therefore gives $|L_{n'}(f)-L_n(f)|<\epsilon$. Hence $(L_n(f))$ is Cauchy and has a real limit by input (b). Finite positivity, linearity, normalization and the uniform bound pass to the limit. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Fix $k$ and write a word of length $n+k$ uniquely as $wv$ with $|w|=k$ and $|v|=n$. Each $f_w$ is continuous, so taking limits in this finite sum proves the prefix-tail identity. If $f=\mathbf1_{[w]}$, the indicator is constant on each length-$k$ cylinder, and every $L_n(f)$ with $n\ge k$ is exactly $\nu(w)$; hence its limit is that value. Real and imaginary parts give the complex version. Every branch above was constructed by the fixed least-child rule, and all other selections are finite. 5.1 We establish auxiliary Claim 2: For a finite word $w$ of positive integers write its continued-fraction convergent as $p(w)/q(w)=[0;w]$ and its preceding convergent as $p'(w)/q'(w)$. For every $N\ge1$, the continued-fraction evaluation of the finite-alphabet code space $\{1,\ldots,N\}^{\mathbb N}$ is a continuous map with compact image. 6.1 Multiplication of the symmetric matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ gives the convergent matrix $\begin{pmatrix}q&q'\\p&p'\end{pmatrix}$. [step 5.1, given, algebra]

7.1 For a code in $\{1,\ldots,N\}^{\mathbb N}$, the nested closed prefix intervals have diameters tending uniformly to zero by step 6.1. The finite ordered code space is compact: if an open cover has no finite subcover, at least one child of a prefix cylinder lacking a finite subcover has the same property. [step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 For any fixed prefix $w$, its children with next digit $1\le a\le N$ are the images under $T_w$ of the intervals $[1/(a+1),1/a]$. 9.1 Every infinite tail has value in $[a,b]$, with $a=1/(N+1)$ and $b=1/(1+a)<1$: the first digit gives the lower bound and the next digit gives the upper bound. 10.1 Iterating this finite inequality for $m$ levels gives total cylinder length at least $(1-2/(N+1))^m$.
