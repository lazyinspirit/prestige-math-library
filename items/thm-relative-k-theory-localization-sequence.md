## Statement

Let $f:A\to B$ be a homomorphism of unital associative rings. For $g\in\operatorname{GL}_n(B)$, define $\partial([g])=[A^n,g,A^n]$, which stabilizes to a map from $K_1(B)$; define $\rho([P,\alpha,Q])=[P]-[Q]$ and $f_*([P])=[B\otimes_AP]$. These maps give a sequence
$$
K_1(A)\longrightarrow K_1(B)\xrightarrow{\partial}K_0(A,B)\xrightarrow{\rho}K_0(A)\xrightarrow{f_*}K_0(B),
$$
which is exact at $K_1(B)$, $K_0(A,B)$, and $K_0(A)$. Here exactness at a term means that the image of the preceding map equals the kernel of the following map; no assertion of exactness at the two displayed endpoints is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A homomorphism $f:A\to B$ of unital associative rings and the definitions of $K_0(A)$, $K_1(A)$, and $K_0(A,B)$ above.

[F1] [[def-algebraic-k0-and-k1-of-a-ring]]: $K_0(A)$ is the group completion of the direct-sum monoid of finitely generated projective left $A$-modules, and $K_1(A)$ is the abelianization of the stable general linear group.

[F2] [[def-relative-algebraic-k0-group]]: $K_0(A,B)$ is generated by triples $(P,\alpha,Q)$ with $\alpha:B\otimes_A P\cong B\otimes_A Q$, modulo additivity for exact sequences of triples and the composition relation.


[F3] [[lem-kernel-of-relative-boundary-is-image-of-ring-k1]] identifies coker(K1(A) to K1(B)) with the kernel of the relative forgetful map.

## Proof

1.1 The boundary is well defined and exactness at $K_1(B)$ and $K_0(A,B)$ follows from [F3]. The maps $\rho$ and $f_*$ are well defined by the defining direct-sum and triple relations; $f_*\rho=0$ because a generic triple isomorphism identifies its two scalar extensions. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $[P]-[Q]$ map to zero in $K_0(B)$. There is a finite projective $B$-module $W$ with $BP\oplus W\cong BQ\oplus W$. Choose a projective complement $W'$ making $W\oplus W'\cong B^n$. Adding it gives an isomorphism $B(P\oplus A^n)\cong B(Q\oplus A^n)$ and therefore a relative triple mapping to $[P]-[Q]$. This proves the reverse inclusion at $K_0(A)$ and completes the three exactness claims. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra] ∎
