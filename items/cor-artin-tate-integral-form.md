## Statement

Let $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$ be commutative rings, each a subring of the next
([[def-subring]]), with $A$ Noetherian. Suppose $C$ is of finite type over $A$
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]) and every element of $C$ is
integral over $B$ ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]]). Then $C$ is
module-finite over $B$, and $B$ is of finite type over $A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Commutative rings $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$, each a subring of the next, with $A$ Noetherian, $C$ of finite type over $A$, and every element of $C$ integral over $B$.

[L1] $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is the smallest subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_n$; an algebra is **of finite type** over $R$ when it equals $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some finite list, and **module-finite** over $R$ when it is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] For a homomorphism of commutative rings $A\to B$, an element $b\in B$ is **integral over $A$** when it is a root of a monic polynomial in $A[X]$ ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]]).

[L3] For commutative rings $A\subseteq B$ with $A$ a subring of $B$ and $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ integral over $A$, the subalgebra $A[b_1,\ldots,b_n]$ is module-finite over $A$ ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

[L4] For commutative rings $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$, each a subring of the next, with $A$ Noetherian, $C$ of finite type over $A$ and $C$ module-finite over $B$, the ring $B$ is of finite type over $A$ ([[thm-artin-tate-lemma]]).

[L5] A subring contains the identity of the ambient ring and is closed under sums, additive inverses and products ([[def-subring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $r\in\mathbb N$ and $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in C$ with $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$. The subring $B[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ of $C$ contains $B$, hence contains $A$, and contains every $t_i$; since $A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ is the smallest subring of $C$ with those two properties, $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]\subseteq B[t_1,\ldots,t_r]\subseteq C$. So $C=B[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ is of finite type over $B$, generated by the same elements. [L1, L5, given]

2.1 Every element of $C$ is integral over $B$, so in particular each $t_i$ is; $B$ is a subring of $C$; hence $B[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ is module-finite over $B$. By step 1.1 that ring is $C$, so $C$ is module-finite over $B$. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 The hypotheses of the Artin–Tate lemma are now all in place for $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$: $A$ is Noetherian, $C$ is of finite type over $A$, and $C$ is module-finite over $B$. Therefore $B$ is of finite type over $A$. [L4, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Finite type over $A$ gives finite type over $B$ for free.** Step 1.1 uses only that $A\subseteq B$: the same algebra generators work over the larger base ring. That is what lets the integrality hypothesis be applied to the finitely many $t_i$ rather than to all of $C$ at once.

- **Integrality of every element is more than integrality of the generators, and only the generators are used.** The hypothesis as stated is the usual one and is what the applications supply; the proof needs it only at $t_1,\ldots,t_r$.
