## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $X$ be a real or complex Banach space and let
$F:[0,1]\to X$ be Lipschitz with constant $L$ and $F(0)=0$. There is a unique
$X$-valued vector measure $\nu_F$ on the Lebesgue sigma-algebra such that

$$\nu_F((a,b])=F(b)-F(a)\qquad(0\leq a<b\leq1),$$

and $|\nu_F|\leq L\lambda$. Conversely, if an $X$-valued vector measure $\nu$
satisfies $|\nu|\leq L\lambda$, then $F(t)=\nu((0,t])$ is Lipschitz, is based
at zero, and induces $\nu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] Countable Choice holds ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[L1] A Lipschitz map with constant $L$ satisfies the uniform distance bound
([[def-lipschitz-holder-contraction]]).

[L2] Vector measures are norm-countably additive and their variation is a
finite-partition supremum
([[def-banach-valued-vector-measure-and-variation]]).

[L3] Continuity from below and set-difference measure calculus hold
([[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]], [[prop-measure-of-a-set-difference]]),
and under Countable Choice the Lebesgue sigma-algebra is the completion of
Borel Lebesgue measure
([[cor-lebesgue-sigma-algebra-is-the-completion-of-borel-lebesgue-measure]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The Banach space and the curve or vector measure in the corresponding
part of the Statement, and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$.

1.1 Define the increment measure on the rational interval algebra. [given, L1, construct]
On the algebra generated by rational half-open intervals and $\{0\}$, set
$\nu_0(\{0\})=0$. For a finite disjoint union
$A=\bigsqcup_j(a_j,b_j]$ with rational endpoints in $[0,1]$, put
$\nu_0(A)=\sum_j(F(b_j)-F(a_j))$. Common endpoint refinement and telescoping
make this representation-independent and finitely additive. By [L1],
$\|\nu_0(A)\|\leq L\lambda(A)$ and, more generally,
$\|\nu_0(A)-\nu_0(B)\|\leq L\lambda(A\triangle B)$.

2.1 Extend to every Lebesgue set. [A1, L2, L3, step 1.1]
The class of Borel sets approximable in symmetric-difference measure by the
rational interval algebra is a sigma-algebra: complements preserve the
distance, and countable unions reduce by continuity from below in [L3] to one
large finite union. It contains the rational intervals and hence all Borel
sets; the completion assertion in [L3] adds every Lebesgue set. Use [A1] to
select an approximating sequence $A_n$ for $E$. Step 1.1 makes
$\nu_0(A_n)$ Cauchy, so completeness of $X$ defines
$\nu_F(E)=\lim_n\nu_0(A_n)$ independently of the approximants. The bound
$\|\nu_F(E)\|\leq L\lambda(E)$ follows by passage to the limit. Finite
additivity and this bound show norm countable additivity: for disjoint $E_k$,
the unaccounted tail has norm at most
$L\lambda(\bigcup_{k>N}E_k)\to0$. Thus [L2] applies.

3.1 Verify variation, all endpoints, and uniqueness. [L1, L2, L3, step 2.1]
Summing the bound from step 2.1 over any finite partition gives
$|\nu_F|(E)\leq L\lambda(E)$. Rational endpoints satisfy the increment formula
by construction; rational approximation to arbitrary $a,b$, the same measure
bound, and continuity from [L1] give it for all endpoints. In particular
$\nu_F(\{0\})=0$. Any other dominated vector measure agreeing on rational
intervals agrees on their algebra, and [L3] plus its domination gives equality
on every Lebesgue set.

4.1 Recover a Lipschitz curve from a dominated vector measure. [L1, L2, step 3.1]
Conversely let $|\nu|\leq L\lambda$ and define $F(t)=\nu((0,t])$. Then
$F(0)=0$ and, for $a<b$,
$\|F(b)-F(a)\|=\|\nu((a,b])\|\leq|\nu|((a,b])\leq L(b-a)$, so [L1] makes $F$
Lipschitz. Its increment measure agrees with $\nu$ on intervals and hence,
by uniqueness in step 3.1, everywhere.

5.1 Combine both directions and record the choice boundary. [A1, step 3.1, step 4.1]
Steps 1.1--3.1 and step 4.1 are inverse constructions. If $L=0$, both the
curve and measure are zero; the empty interval and singleton endpoints have
zero increment. A one-interval algebra element is the defining case. The exact
choice cost is [A1] in the countable-algebra approximation supplied by [L3]; all
other selections are finite or least-indexed. [A1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
