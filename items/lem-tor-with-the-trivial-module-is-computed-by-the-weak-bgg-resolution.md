## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and let $B_\bullet(\lambda)$ be the weak BGG resolution of [[thm-weak-bgg-resolution]], with $\Pi_\lambda=L(\lambda)$. Then for every $k\ge1$ the induced differential $B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\to B_{k-1}(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_{k-1}(\lambda)$ is zero, $\operatorname{Wt}\bigl(B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\bigr)=\{w\circ\lambda:\ell(w)=k\}$ with each weight occurring once, and consequently

$$\operatorname{Tor}_k^{U(\mathfrak n^-)}\bigl(\mathbb C,\Pi_\lambda\bigr)\cong B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\cong\mathbb C^{|W_k|},$$

where $W_k=\{w\in W:\ell(w)=k\}$. This is the dimension statement used by BGG in the form needed here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a dominant integral weight $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$, the weak BGG resolution $B_\bullet(\lambda)$ of $\Pi_\lambda=L(\lambda)$ with its differentials, and the right $U(\mathfrak n^-)$-module $\mathbb C$ with trivial action.

[F1] $0\to B_{|\Phi^+|}(\lambda)\to\cdots\to B_1(\lambda)\to B_0(\lambda)\to\Pi_\lambda\to0$ is exact, each $B_k(\lambda)$ is an object of $\mathcal O$, and $\operatorname{Typ}B_k(\lambda)=\{w\circ\lambda:\ell(w)=k\}$ with each weight occurring once ([[thm-weak-bgg-resolution]], [[def-verma-type-of-a-module-with-a-standard-filtration]], [[def-bgg-category-o]]).

[F2] Verma modules satisfy $M(\psi)\cong U(\mathfrak n^-)$ as $U(\mathfrak n^-)$-modules, generated by the highest weight vector $v_\psi$, and $M(\psi)/\mathfrak n^-M(\psi)\cong\mathbb C v_\psi$ is one-dimensional of weight $\psi$; the weights $w\circ\lambda$ for $w\in W$ are pairwise distinct ([[thm-pbw-model-of-a-verma-module]], [[def-verma-module]], [[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]]).

[F3] The functor $F=\mathbb C\otimes_{U(\mathfrak n^-)}(-)$ is right exact, and $F(M)\cong M/\mathfrak n^-M$. Objects of $\mathcal O$ that are Verma-filtered are $F$-acyclic: $\operatorname{Tor}_j^{U(\mathfrak n^-)}(\mathbb C,N)=0$ for all $j>0$ and every Verma-filtered $N\in\mathcal O$ ([[lem-verma-filtered-objects-are-acyclic-for-n-minus-coinvariants]], [[prop-tor-zero-is-the-tensor-product-in-either-construction]], [[def-tor-by-resolving-the-left-module]]).

[F4] The acyclic-resolution theorem: if $P$ is a supplied projective resolution datum on a class $\mathcal D$, $F$ is additive and right exact, and $\cdots\to Q_1\to Q_0\to A\to0$ is an exact complex with every $Q_q\in\mathcal D$ $F$-acyclic and all syzygies $Z_q$ in $\mathcal D$, then $L_n^PF(A)\cong H_n(F(Q_\bullet))$ for all $n\ge0$; projective resolutions exist and, under the Axiom of Choice, a resolution datum may be supplied. Moreover $\operatorname{Tor}_n^{U(\mathfrak n^-)}(\mathbb C,-)$ is the left derived functor of $F$ computed with such a datum ([[thm-acyclic-resolution-theorem-for-left-derived-functors]], [[def-f-acyclic-resolution]], [[def-balanced-tor-bifunctor]], [[cor-every-module-admits-a-projective-resolution]], [[thm-long-exact-tor-sequence-in-the-left-module-variable]]).

[F5] $\mathcal O$-objects are $\mathfrak h$-semisimple with finite-dimensional weight spaces, and the quotient of an $\mathfrak h$-stable submodule is $\mathfrak h$-semisimple; nonzero eigenvectors of pairwise distinct weights in a vector space are linearly independent ([[def-bgg-category-o]]). The number of elements of $W$ of length $k$ is denoted $|W_k|$; for $k>|\Phi^+|$ both sides below are zero ([[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]], [[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]]).

## Proof

1.1 We compute the weight multiset of $B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)$ from the Verma filtration of [F1]. Fix a filtration $0=N_0\subseteq N_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq N_n=B_k(\lambda)$ with $N_j/N_{j-1}\cong M(w_j\circ\lambda)$, where $w_1,\dots,w_n$ list $W_k$. For each $j$, the vanishing $\operatorname{Tor}_1(\mathbb C,M(w_j\circ\lambda))=0$ from [F3] and its long exact sequence give a short exact sequence $0\to N_{j-1}/\mathfrak n^-N_{j-1}\to N_j/\mathfrak n^-N_j\to (N_j/N_{j-1})/\mathfrak n^-(N_j/N_{j-1})\to0$; the last term is $\mathbb C v_{w_j}$ by [F2], Choose a weight-vector lift in $N_j$ of the highest weight vector in $N_j/N_{j-1}$; such a lift exists by $\mathfrak h$-semisimplicity. Its coinvariant class $\bar v_{w_j}$ has weight $w_j\circ\lambda$ and maps to the generator of the last term. Together with the embedded earlier classes it spans $N_j/\mathfrak n^-N_j$; induction on $j$ gives a spanning set for $B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)$. [F2, F3, F5]

1.2 The induced map $\bar d_k\colon B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\to B_{k-1}(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_{k-1}(\lambda)$ is $\mathfrak h$-equivariant: the differential $d_k$ is a $\mathfrak g$-homomorphism, $\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)$ and $\mathfrak n^-B_{k-1}(\lambda)$ are $\mathfrak h$-stable, and the induced map on quotients commutes with the action of $\mathfrak h$. [F1, F5]

1.3 The homology of $(B_\bullet(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_\bullet(\lambda),\bar d_\bullet)$ computes Tor: by [F1] and [F3] the complex $B_\bullet(\lambda)$ is an $F$-acyclic resolution of $\Pi_\lambda$ with all terms and all syzygies in $\mathcal O$, so the acyclic-resolution theorem of [F4] gives $\operatorname{Tor}_n^{U(\mathfrak n^-)}(\mathbb C,\Pi_\lambda)\cong H_n\bigl(B_\bullet(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_\bullet(\lambda)\bigr)$ for every $n$. [F1, F3, F4]

2.1 These classes have weights $w_j\circ\lambda$, which are pairwise distinct by [F2], and $B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)$ is $\mathfrak h$-semisimple by [F5]; each newly lifted class has nonzero image in the corresponding one-dimensional quotient of the short exact sequence in step 1.1, and the earlier classes embed injectively. Induction therefore proves these classes nonzero, linearly independent and a basis. Therefore $\operatorname{Wt}\bigl(B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\bigr)=\{w\circ\lambda:\ell(w)=k\}$ with each weight occurring once, and $\dim_{\mathbb C}B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)=|W_k|$. [F2, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 For every $k\ge1$ the map $\bar d_k$ is zero. Indeed, its source has weights $\{w\circ\lambda:\ell(w)=k\}$ and its target has weights $\{w'\circ\lambda:\ell(w')=k-1\}$ by step 2.1, and these two sets are disjoint by the pairwise distinctness in [F2]; an $\mathfrak h$-equivariant map sends a weight vector of weight $\mu$ into the weight-$\mu$ space of the target, so every basis vector of the source maps to $0$. [F2, step 2.1, step 1.2]

4.1 Consequently, for $k\ge1$, the degree-$k$ homology of $B_\bullet(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_\bullet(\lambda)$ equals $B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)$ (all incoming and outgoing induced differentials at degree $k$ vanish by step 3.1), so
$$\operatorname{Tor}_k^{U(\mathfrak n^-)}(\mathbb C,\Pi_\lambda)\cong B_k(\lambda)/\mathfrak n^-B_k(\lambda)\cong\mathbb C^{|W_k|}$$
by steps 1.3 and 2.1. The claims about the vanishing induced differential and the weight multiset are steps 3.1 and 2.1, and for $k>|\Phi^+|$ the module is zero by [F5]. [F5, step 2.1, step 1.3, step 3.1] ∎
