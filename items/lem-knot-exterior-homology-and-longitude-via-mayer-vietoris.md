## Statement

Let $K:S^1\to S^3$ be a smooth oriented embedding and suppose a product tubular embedding $\Phi:S^1\times D^2_{2\epsilon}\to S^3$ is supplied, where $D^2_r=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z|\le r\}$. Define $N=\Phi(S^1\times D^2_\epsilon)$, $E=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N$, $T=\partial E$, and let $i:T\hookrightarrow E$ be inclusion. Fix $s_0\in S^1$ and $z_0\in\partial D^2_\epsilon$. Let $\mu=\Phi(\{s_0\}\times\partial D^2_\epsilon)$ be the fibre meridian and let $\sigma:S^1\to T$ be the product section $s\mapsto\Phi(s,z_0)$. Then $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)$ is infinite cyclic generated by $i_*[\mu]$. The kernel of $i_*:H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(E;\mathbb Z)$ has a unique generator $[\lambda]$ whose image under the product projection $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)$ is $1$, and $([\mu],[\lambda])$ is a basis of $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)$. The proof uses no choice axiom beyond the supplied tube.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $K$, the supplied product tube $\Phi$, and the sets and curves in the statement.

[F1] [[def-oriented-link-in-s-three-and-ambient-isotopy]]: $K$ is a smooth embedding of the oriented circle in the fixed oriented $3$-sphere.

[F2] [[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]]: for an open cover $X=U\cup V$, the sequence $H_1(U\cap V;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(V;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ is exact.

[F7] [[thm-short-exact-two-open-singular-chain-mayer-vietoris-sequence]]: the chain map from the intersection sends $c$ to $(i_U(c),-i_V(c))$, so the homology map has the same opposite-sign convention.

[F3] [[cor-homology-of-spheres]]: $H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z)=H_2(S^3;\mathbb Z)=0$.

[F4] [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]]: the finite product CW structures on $S^1$, $T=S^1\times S^1$, and $S^1\times D^2$ compute their singular homology.

[F5] [[def-cellular-homology]] and [[thm-cellular-boundary-is-the-incidence-degree-matrix]]: cellular groups are free on cells and their differentials are the signed incidence maps.

[F6] [[thm-homotopy-equivalences-induce-isomorphisms-on-singular-homology]]: a deformation retraction induces an isomorphism on singular homology.

## Proof

1.1 Give $S^1$ its one-vertex, one-edge CW structure. By the oriented-knot convention [F1], the core circle has the specified positive orientation used for the section generator. The solid tube $S^1\times D^2$ retracts radially to the core circle, whose cellular boundary is zero, so $H_1(S^1\times D^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. The product torus $T=S^1\times S^1$ has one $0$-cell, two $1$-cells and one $2$-cell; the two endpoint incidences cancel in degree $1$, and the attaching commutator of the $2$-cell has total signed incidence zero on each $1$-cell. Thus $H_1(T;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$, with basis the fibre class $[\mu]$ and section class $[\sigma]$. The projection to the first $S^1$ sends $[\mu]$ to $0$ and $[\sigma]$ to $1$. [given, F1, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

1.2 Set $U=\Phi(S^1\times\{|z|<3\epsilon/2\})$ and $V=S^3\setminus\Phi(S^1\times D^2_{\epsilon/2})$. They are open and cover $S^3$. Radial contraction in the disk coordinate retracts $U$ to the core circle and $U\cap V$ to $T$. The homotopy $r_t(r)=(1-t)r+t\epsilon$ for $\epsilon/2<r<\epsilon$, fixed for $r\ge\epsilon$, retracts $V$ onto $E$. The intersection map in the Mayer–Vietoris sequence is therefore the map $H_1(T)\to H_1(S^1)\oplus H_1(E)$ given by $x\mapsto(p_*x,-i_*x)$, where $p$ is product projection. [given, F2, F6, F7, construct]

2.1 By exactness and [F3], this intersection map is an isomorphism. Its restriction to $\ker p_*=\mathbb Z[\mu]$ is an isomorphism onto $0\oplus H_1(E)$: injectivity follows from the isomorphism, and any $(0,y)$ has a preimage $x$ with $p_*x=0$. Hence $H_1(E)\cong\mathbb Z$, generated up to sign by $i_*[\mu]$. [F2, F3, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Write $i_*[\sigma]=c\,i_*[\mu]$ for the unique integer $c$, and define $[\lambda]=[\sigma]-c[\mu]$. Then $i_*[\lambda]=0$ and its product projection is $1$. Every class in $\ker i_*$ has the form $a[\mu]+b[\sigma]$ and satisfies $a+bc=0$, so it is exactly $b[\lambda]$; the projection condition forces $b=1$. The change-of-basis matrix from $([\mu],[\sigma])$ to $([\mu],[\lambda])$ has determinant $1$, so the latter pair is a basis. All choices were the supplied finite product data and integer operations; no choice axiom was used. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

This calculation is deliberately local to a supplied product tube. It does not use the separate batch-57 theorem whose proof assumes full AC.
