## Example

Let $N>1$, let $k$ contain a primitive $N$th root of unity $\zeta$, and assume $\operatorname{char}(k)\nmid N$. For the Taft Hopf algebra $T_N$ with basis $g^i x^j$ ($0\le i,j<N$), the left-integral space is generated by
$$
\Lambda_N=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}g^i x^{N-1}.
$$
Its counit is zero, so $T_N$ is not semisimple.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $N>1$, a field $k$ with a primitive $N$th root $\zeta$ and $\operatorname{char}(k)\nmid N$, and the Taft Hopf algebra $T_N$.

[F1] [[lem-hh-taft-algebra-pbw-and-hopf-structure]]: the basis elements multiply by $(g^i x^j)(g^a x^b)=\zeta^{-aj}g^{i+a\bmod N}x^{j+b}$ when $j+b<N$, and give zero otherwise; $\varepsilon(g)=1$, $\varepsilon(x)=0$, and $T_N$ is generated by $g,x$.

[F2] [[def-hh-left-right-integrals-and-integral-functionals]]: $t$ is a left integral exactly when $ht=\varepsilon(h)t$ for all $h\in T_N$.

[F3] [[thm-hh-hopf-maschke-integral-averaging]]: a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra is semisimple iff some left integral has nonzero counit.

## Verification

1.1 The multiplication rule gives $g\Lambda_N=\Lambda_N$ by reindexing $i\mapsto i+1\pmod N$. It also gives $xg^i=\zeta^{-i}g^i x$, so $x\Lambda_N=\sum_i\zeta^{-i}g^i x^N=0$. These are the left-integral equations for the generators $g,x$; because they generate $T_N$ and $\varepsilon$ is multiplicative, the equations extend to every product and then every linear combination. Thus $\Lambda_N$ is a left integral. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Write any left integral as $t=\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}g^ix^j$. The equation $gt=t$ forces $c_{ij}=c_{i-1,j}$ for every $i,j$, so for each $j$ the coefficient is independent of $i$. The equation $xt=0$ has coefficient $\zeta^{-i}c_{ij}$ at each distinct basis vector $g^ix^{j+1}$ for $j<N-1$; since $\zeta^{-i}\ne0$, all those $c_{ij}$ vanish. Thus only the $j=N-1$ coefficients can remain, and they are all equal, proving $\int_{T_N}^{\ell}=k\Lambda_N$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $N>1$, each summand of $\Lambda_N$ contains a positive power of $x$, and multiplicativity of the counit gives $\varepsilon(\Lambda_N)=0$. Every left integral is a scalar multiple of $\Lambda_N$ by step 2.1, so none has nonzero counit. The Hopf–Maschke criterion [F3] therefore implies that $T_N$ is not semisimple. [F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
