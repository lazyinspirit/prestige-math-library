## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field and let $C$ be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve
over $k$. Let
$$0\longrightarrow\mathcal L\longrightarrow\mathcal M\longrightarrow\mathcal Q\longrightarrow0$$
be an exact sequence of $\mathcal O_C$-modules in which $\mathcal L$ and
$\mathcal M$ are invertible and $\mathcal Q$ is a torsion sheaf: its stalk at
the generic point is zero, and at each closed point $p$ its stalk
$\mathcal Q_p$ is a module of finite length
$l_p=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{C,p}}(\mathcal Q_p)$, zero for all but
finitely many $p$. Then $\mathcal M$ is isomorphic to
$$\mathcal L\Bigl(\sum_p l_p[p]\Bigr)=\mathcal L\otimes_{\mathcal O_C}\mathcal O_C(D),\qquad D=\sum_p l_p[p],$$
for the effective divisor $D$ of the closed points with the lengths $l_p$ as
coefficients, and the quotient $\mathcal M/\mathcal L$ is isomorphic to the
quotient $\mathcal O_C(D)/\mathcal O_C$ of the twist by $\mathcal O_C(D)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a smooth proper geometrically integral curve $C$ over a field $k$ with function field $k(C)$ and generic point $\eta$, and an exact sequence $0\to\mathcal L\to\mathcal M\to\mathcal Q\to0$ of $\mathcal O_C$-modules with $\mathcal L,\mathcal M$ invertible and $\mathcal Q$ torsion with generic stalk zero and finite lengths $l_p$ at the closed points $p$, zero for all but finitely many $p$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is used through the stated local-ring theorem to obtain the discrete valuation ring structure at each closed point; it places no restriction on the field $k$. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]])

[F1] A curve over $k$ is geometrically integral, separated, of finite type and of chain dimension one; every nonempty open of $C$ contains its generic point $\eta$. Under the Choice premise [A1], the local ring of the smooth curve $C$ at a closed point $p$ is a discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O_{C,p}$ with maximal ideal generated by a uniformizer $t_p$, and the local ring at $\eta$ is the function field $k(C)$. ([[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]], [[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]])

[F2] An invertible sheaf is a locally free $\mathcal O_C$-module of rank one; its stalk $\mathcal L_p$ at a closed point is free of rank one over $\mathcal O_{C,p}$, and its stalk at the generic point is a one-dimensional $k(C)$-vector space. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-rational-section-line-bundle]])

[F3] In a discrete valuation ring every nonzero element is a unit times a power of a uniformizer, and for a discrete valuation ring $V$ with uniformizer $\pi$ the quotient $V/(\pi^k)$ has length $k$ over $V$. ([[thm-dvr-element-normal-form]], [[thm-dvr-ideal-and-module-length]], [[def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module]])

[F4] The stalk of an invertible sheaf at the generic point is nonzero and one-dimensional over $k(C)$, so a nonzero morphism $\mathcal O_C\to\mathcal N$ from the structure sheaf to an invertible sheaf is injective and exhibits a rational section of $\mathcal N$; more generally a pair $(\mathcal N,s)$ of an invertible sheaf and a nonzero rational section is the data used by the rational-section dictionary. ([[def-rational-section-line-bundle]], [[def-invertible-sheaf]])

[F5] For an invertible sheaf $\mathcal N$ on an integral scheme, a nonzero rational section $s$ determines a Cartier divisor $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$ and a global isomorphism $\mathcal O_C(D)\to\mathcal N$ carrying the canonical rational section $1_D$ to $s$. ([[def-rational-section-line-bundle]], [[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]])

[F6] For a nonzero regular section $s$ of an invertible sheaf on $C$, its coefficient on a trivializing open is a regular function and is the local equation of $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$ in [F5]. The local equations differ by units on overlaps ([[def-cartier-divisor]]). Each germ is nonzero: if it vanished on a neighborhood, the section would vanish at the generic point, contrary to the nonzero rational section and the generic-point property in [F1, F4]. Since $C$ is integral, its local rings are domains, so multiplication by each coefficient is injective. The equations are regular nonzerodivisors and hence define an effective Cartier divisor by [[def-effective-cartier-divisor]]. At a closed point $p$, their orders are independent of the chosen frame; if these orders vanish outside a finite set, their formal sum on closed points is the divisor notation of [[def-divisor-smooth-proper-curve]]. This local equation and coefficient description does not use a global equivalence theorem for all Cartier and Weil divisors. ([[def-cartier-divisor]], [[def-effective-cartier-divisor]], [[def-divisor-smooth-proper-curve]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; compare the two invertible sheaves at every closed point by localizing the sequence at the discrete valuation ring, convert the resulting local divisibility data into a rational section of $\mathcal L^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal M$, and read off its divisor.

1.1 Local structure at a closed point. Fix a closed point $p$ and write $V=\mathcal O_{C,p}$. Choose bases $e_L$ of $\mathcal L_p$ and $e_M$ of $\mathcal M_p$. The injection sends $e_L$ to $a e_M$ for a nonzero $a\in V$; writing $a=u t_p^k$ with $u\in V^\times$ by [F3], its image is $t_p^k V e_M$. Thus $\mathcal M_p/\mathcal L_p\cong V/(t_p^k)$, whose length is $k$ by [F3]. Since this quotient is $\mathcal Q_p$, $k=l_p$. [F1, F2, F3, A1]

1.2 The generic point. Localizing the exact sequence at the generic point $\eta$ of the integral curve $C$ gives an exact sequence whose last term is the hypothesis-zero stalk $\mathcal Q_\eta=0$, so the morphism $\mathcal L\to\mathcal M$ restricts to an isomorphism $\mathcal L_\eta\to\mathcal M_\eta$ at the generic point. By [F2] both stalks are one-dimensional over $k(C)$, so this isomorphism is a nonzero rational trivialisation of the invertible sheaf $\mathcal N=\mathcal L^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal O_C}\mathcal M$: the inclusion $\mathcal L\hookrightarrow\mathcal M$ is a nonzero morphism $\mathcal L\to\mathcal M$, hence a nonzero rational section $s$ of $\mathcal N$ in the sense of [F4]. [F2, F4]

2.1 The global divisor isomorphism. Let $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$ and use [F5] to obtain the global isomorphism $\psi:\mathcal O_C(D)\to\mathcal N=\mathcal L^{\vee}\otimes\mathcal M$ carrying $1_D$ to $s$. Tensoring by $\mathcal L$ and composing with the evaluation isomorphism $\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal L^{\vee}\cong\mathcal O_C$ gives a global isomorphism $\Phi:\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)\to\mathcal M$. By the definition of $s$ from the original injection, the square comparing $\mathcal L\to\mathcal M$ with $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$, $\ell\mapsto\ell\otimes1_D$, commutes: both maps send the generic section $\ell$ to the original image of $\ell$, and equality of maps to the locally free sheaf $\mathcal M$ can be checked at the generic point. Thus the isomorphism identifies the given subsheaf $\mathcal L$ with the canonical copy $\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C\subseteq\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$. On a trivializing open, the local equation of $D$ is the coefficient of the original regular morphism $\mathcal L\to\mathcal M$; its order at $p$ is $l_p$ by step 1.1. Thus the finite closed-point divisor notation for these local coefficients is $D=\sum_p l_p[p]$ by [F6]. Its local equations are regular and nonzero, so it is effective by [F6]. Hence $\mathcal M\cong\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$ as claimed. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The quotient sheaf and the finite-support trivialization. Put $N=\mathcal O_C(D)/\mathcal O_C$. Since step 2.1 identifies the inclusion $\mathcal L\to\mathcal M$ with $\mathcal L\to\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$, taking cokernels gives the global isomorphism $\mathcal Q\cong\mathcal L\otimes N$. Its support is the finite set $S=\{p:l_p>0\}$, because $N_p\cong\mathcal O_{C,p}/(t_p^{l_p})$ by the local divisor equation and [F3]. For each $p\in S$, choose an open neighborhood $U_p$ on which $\mathcal L$ is trivial and which contains no point of $S\setminus\{p\}$; such a neighborhood is obtained by intersecting a trivializing open with the complements of the finitely many other closed points of $S$. Also let $U_0=C\setminus S$. These opens cover $C$. On each $U_p$, a chosen frame of $\mathcal L$ gives an isomorphism $(\mathcal L\otimes N)|_{U_p}\cong N|_{U_p}$, and on $U_0$ both sheaves vanish. For distinct $p,q\in S$, the intersection $U_p\cap U_q$ misses all of $S$, and $U_0\cap U_p$ also misses $S$, so both $N$ and $\mathcal L\otimes N$ vanish on every overlap between distinct members of this cover. The local isomorphisms therefore agree on overlaps and glue to a global (generally noncanonical) isomorphism $\mathcal Q\cong N=\mathcal O_C(D)/\mathcal O_C$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, F6]

4.1 Conclusion. For the effective divisor $D=\sum_p l_p[p]$, the original inclusion and the rational-section isomorphism give $\mathcal M\cong\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_C(D)$, and the finite-support open-cover argument gives the noncanonical global isomorphism $\mathcal M/\mathcal L\cong\mathcal O_C(D)/\mathcal O_C$. The local lengths determine the coefficients, while the latter isomorphism also uses the finite support and chosen trivializations of $\mathcal L$ near that support. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
