## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathcal O$ be the ring of integers of a finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, with uniformizer $\varpi$ and finite residue field $k$. Let $M$ be a finite free $\mathcal O$-module and let $\{T_i\}_{i\in I}$ be any pairwise commuting family of $\mathcal O$-linear endomorphisms. Let $\mathbb T\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O}(M)$ be the unital $\mathcal O$-subalgebra generated by these endomorphisms. Suppose a unital $\mathcal O$-algebra homomorphism $\bar\lambda:\mathbb T\to k$ is given, where $\mathcal O$ acts on $k$ by reduction, and put $\mathfrak m=\ker\bar\lambda$.

Then $\mathbb T$ is a finite free $\mathcal O$-module, and $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}$ is a finite free $\mathcal O$-algebra which is Noetherian and local with residue field $k$. It is complete and separated for its maximal-ideal topology, so $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}\cong\widehat{\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}}$. More explicitly, there is an idempotent $e\in\mathbb T$ such that $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}\cong e\mathbb T$ and $M_{\mathfrak m}\cong eM$; the displayed algebra acts faithfully on $eM$.

This conditional construction applies to a Hecke-stable integral lattice only after such a lattice, commuting integral operators, and the residual eigencharacter have been supplied. It does not construct the minimal modular lattice or prove the residual ideal proper, and it asserts no Hecke-valued Galois representation, local deformation condition, complete-intersection property, or $R=T$ theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The complete DVR, finite residue field, lattice, commuting endomorphisms and residual homomorphism in the Statement, and AC.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is assumed.

[F1] [[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]] gives finite freeness of every submodule of a finite free module over a PID.

[F2] [[thm-nakayama-lemma]]: under AC, a finite module $N$ over a commutative ring with $JN=N$ for an ideal $J$ contained in the Jacobson radical is zero.

## Proof

1.1 Since a DVR is a PID, [F1] applied to $\mathbb T\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O}(M)\cong\mathcal O^{r^2}$ shows that $\mathbb T$ is finite free over $\mathcal O$. It is commutative because the generators commute. It is $\varpi$-adically complete and separated by a finite basis and the corresponding properties of $\mathcal O$. Any ideal of $\mathbb T$ is an $\mathcal O$-submodule of this finite free module, so [F1] also proves that it is finitely generated over $\mathbb T$; thus $\mathbb T$ is Noetherian. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 For $b\in\mathbb T$ the geometric series $\sum_{j\ge0}(\varpi b)^j$ converges in $\mathbb T$ and inverts $1-\varpi b$. Therefore every maximal ideal contains $\varpi$: otherwise its image would make $\varpi$ invertible, so some $b$ would satisfy $1-\varpi b$ in that maximal ideal. The quotient $B=\mathbb T/\varpi\mathbb T$ is a finite-dimensional algebra over the finite field $k$ and hence a finite ring. Its maximal ideals are finitely many, their inverse images are exactly the maximal ideals of $\mathbb T$, and the chosen one is $\overline{\mathfrak m}=\mathfrak m/\varpi\mathbb T$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Let $J$ be the intersection of all maximal ideals of $B$. The descending sequence $J\supseteq J^2\supseteq\cdots$ stabilizes because $B$ is finite-dimensional over $k$. Its stable term is a finite $B$-module $N$ with $JN=N$, so [F2] gives $J^r=0$ for some $r\ge1$. Enumerate the maximal ideals $\mathfrak n_1=\overline{\mathfrak m},\mathfrak n_2,\ldots,\mathfrak n_q$. Their powers $\mathfrak n_i^r$ are pairwise comaximal: expand $(a+b)^{2r-1}=1$ for $a\in\mathfrak n_i$, $b\in\mathfrak n_j$ with $a+b=1$. Their product lies in $J^r=0$, so the elementary Chinese remainder construction yields an idempotent $\bar e\in B$ which is $1$ modulo $\mathfrak n_1^r$ and $0$ modulo every other $\mathfrak n_i^r$. Its image in the factor $\bar e B$ has the single maximal ideal $\bar e\mathfrak n_1$, whose $r$th power is zero. [A1, F2, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Lift $\bar e$ to $e_0\in\mathbb T$ and set $\delta_i=e_i^2-e_i$, $e_{i+1}=e_i+(1-2e_i)\delta_i$. Polynomial expansion gives $\delta_{i+1}=\delta_i^2(4\delta_i-3)$; hence $\delta_i\in\varpi^{2^i}\mathbb T$ and $e_{i+1}-e_i\in\varpi^{2^i}\mathbb T$. Completeness gives an idempotent $e$ lifting $\bar e$. The direct summand $A=e\mathbb T$ is finite free over $\mathcal O$ by [F1], complete for $\varpi$, and has special fibre $A/\varpi A\cong\bar eB$. In a $\varpi$-adically complete ring, an element whose reduction modulo $\varpi$ is a unit is a unit: lift its inverse and invert the resulting $1+\varpi c$ by a geometric series. Therefore $A$ is local, with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n$ the inverse image of $\bar e\mathfrak n_1$, and $\mathfrak n^r\subseteq\varpi A\subseteq\mathfrak n$. Its residue is $B/\overline{\mathfrak m}\cong k$ by the given $\bar\lambda$. The inclusions show that the $\mathfrak n$-adic and $\varpi$-adic filtrations are cofinal, so $A$ is complete and separated for the maximal-ideal topology. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Every $s\in\mathbb T\setminus\mathfrak m$ maps to a unit of $A$, since its reduction in the unique residue field of $A$ is nonzero. The map $t/s\mapsto et/(es)$ is therefore a homomorphism $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}\to A$. Conversely $e\notin\mathfrak m$ and $e^2=e$, so $e$ becomes $1$ in $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}$; localization gives an inverse $A\to\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}$. Thus $\mathbb T_{\mathfrak m}\cong A$. The finite free $\mathcal O$-module $A$ is Noetherian by the ideal argument of step 1.1, and the completed local-ring and residue claims follow from step 4.1. [step 1.1, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 Because $e\notin\mathfrak m$ and $e(1-e)=0$, localization kills $(1-e)M$, while $eM$ is naturally an $A$-module. The same inverse maps as in step 5.1 identify $M_{\mathfrak m}$ with $eM$. If $a=e t\in A$ annihilates $eM$, then for every $x\in M$ one has $ax=etx=te x=0$ because $e$ and $t$ commute as elements of $\mathbb T$. Faithfulness of the original inclusion $\mathbb T\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal O}(M)$ forces $a=0$. [step 5.1, given, algebra] ∎
