## Statement

In ZFC, a $P*\dot Q$-generic $K$ factors as a $P$-generic $G$ and $\dot Q_G$-generic $H$ with $M[K]=M[G][H]$, and conversely $G*H$ is generic. If $P$ is ccc and $P\Vdash\dot Q$ is ccc, then $P*\dot Q$ is ccc.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a two-step iteration in a transitive ground model, and the stated chain hypotheses for the last clause.

[F1] [[def-two-step-forcing-iteration]] fixes the set-sized order and, under
AC, supplies a bounded-carrier name forced equal below any condition to an
arbitrary local name for a member of $\dot Q$.

[F2] [[thm-forcing-theorem]] supplies name evaluation and the truth lemma; [[def-forcing-relation-for-formulas]] supplies the existential and negation clauses, [[def-forcing-relation-for-atomic-formulas]] supplies atomic membership, and [[lem-forcing-monotonicity-density-and-decision]] supplies dense decisions and density closure.

[F3] [[thm-chain-condition-preserves-cofinalities-and-cardinals]] preserves $\omega_1$ through ccc forcing.

[F4] [[thm-forcing-preserves-ordinals]] says each ordinal of a generic extension is a ground ordinal.

## Proof

1.1 From $K$ put $G=\{p:\exists\dot q\in R\ (p,\dot q)\in K\}$ and $H=\{\dot q_G:\exists p\in G\ (p,\dot q)\in K\}$. Preimages of ground dense subsets of $P$ are dense in the iteration, so $G$ is generic. If $\dot D_G$ is dense in $\dot Q_G$, the forcing theorem produces below each pair a name for a second-coordinate extension in $\dot D$; [F1] replaces that local name by a forced-equal name in the set carrier $R$. The resulting pairs are dense, and meeting them proves $H$ generic over $M[G]$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Conversely, for generics $G,H$ take the filter generated by pairs $(p,\dot q)$ in the restricted iteration with $p\in G$ and $\dot q_G\in H$. Every quotient condition has a name occurring in $\dot Q$, or a locally forced-equal representative in $R$ by [F1], so the same dense-set translation meets every ground dense subset of $P*\dot Q$. Recursive evaluation of names first by $G$ and then by $H$ proves $M[K]=M[G][H]$. [F1, F2]

1.3 Suppose $A=\{(p_\alpha,\dot q_\alpha):\alpha<\omega_1^M\}$ were an antichain and put $\dot I=\{(\check\alpha,p_\alpha):\alpha<\omega_1^M\}$. We prove syntactically that $1_P$ forces the map $\alpha\mapsto\dot q_\alpha$ from $\dot I$ into $\dot Q$ to have pairwise incompatible, hence distinct, values. Otherwise some $s$ forces distinct $\alpha,\beta\in\dot I$ and a common $\dot Q$-extension of their values. The forcing membership clause lets us strengthen below $p_\alpha$ and $p_\beta$; the existential clause of [F2] then supplies a further condition $t$ and a name $\dot r$ with $t\Vdash\dot r\le_{\dot Q}\dot q_\alpha,\dot q_\beta$. By [F1], replace $\dot r$ below $t$ by a forced-equal $\rho\in R$. Then $(t,\rho)$ belongs to the restricted iteration and extends both members of $A$, a contradiction. Thus $1_P\Vdash\dot I$ injects into a $\dot Q$-antichain. Since $1_P\Vdash\dot Q$ is ccc, $1_P\Vdash\dot I$ is countable. [F1, F2]

Because $P$ is ccc, [F3] preserves the ground regular $\omega_1^M$. Hence $1_P\Vdash\dot I$ is bounded below $\check\omega_1^M$. The forcing existential clause supplies, densely below any condition, a name for such a bound; F4 says that bound is a ground ordinal, and the check-name membership clause then densely decides the name equal to some ground $\check\beta$ with $\beta<\omega_1^M$. Thus $D=\{p\in P:\exists\beta<\omega_1^M\ p\Vdash\dot I\subseteq\check\beta\}$ is dense. Choose a maximal antichain $C\subseteq D$; it is countable by ccc of $P$. For each $c\in C$ choose a ground bound $\beta_c$, and put $\beta^*=\sup_{c\in C}(\beta_c+1)<\omega_1^M$. Predensity of $C$ and the forcing negation clause give $1_P\Vdash\dot I\subseteq\check\beta^*$. But the definition of $\dot I$ gives $p_\alpha\Vdash\check\alpha\in\dot I$ for every $\alpha<\omega_1^M$, contradicting the bound when $\alpha\ge\beta^*$. Therefore $P*\dot Q$ is ccc. AC supplies the antichain indexing, the maximal antichain $C$, and its bound choices; no external generic is used. [F1, F2, F3, F4] ∎
