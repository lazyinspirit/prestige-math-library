## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $M$ be an $R$-module. The
following are equivalent.

1. $M$ is a Noetherian module ([[def-noetherian-module]]).
2. $M$ is finitely generated
   ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).
3. $M$ is finitely presented ([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]).

The proof below shows exactly where the Noetherian hypothesis is used to make
conditions 2 and 3 agree.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$ and an $R$-module $M$. For $n\in\mathbb N$, $R^n$ denotes the free module on an $n$-element set with standard basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$.

[L1] A left $R$-module $M$ is **Noetherian** when every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-module]]).

[L2] $M$ is finitely generated when $M=\langle S\rangle_R$ for some finite $S\subseteq M$, and $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $S$ ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[L3] Every finitely generated left module over a left Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]]).

[L4] In the free module $R^{(X)}$ every element has a unique expression $\sum_{x\in F}r_xe_x$ with $F\subseteq X$ finite, where $e_x$ has coordinate $1_R$ at $x$ and zero elsewhere; for $X=\varnothing$ the module is $0$ ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[L5] Every set map $u\colon X\to M$ extends uniquely to an $R$-module homomorphism $\bar u\colon R^{(X)}\to M$ with $\bar u(e_x)=u(x)$, given by $\bar u(\sum_{x\in F}r_xe_x)=\sum_{x\in F}r_xu(x)$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-free-modules]]).

[L6] For a ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_i\in R$ and $s_i\in S$, the term with $k=0$ being $0_M$ ([[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

[L7] A sequence of modules and homomorphisms is exact at a module where two arrows meet when the image of the incoming map equals the kernel of the outgoing one ([[def-exact-and-short-exact-sequences-of-modules]]).

[L8] An $R$-module $M$ is finitely presented when there are $m,n\in\mathbb N$ and an exact sequence $R^m\to R^n\to M\to0$ ([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Condition 1 implies condition 2, because $M$ is a submodule of itself and a Noetherian module has all of its submodules finitely generated. [L1, L2, given]

1.2 Condition 2 implies condition 1, since $R$ is a Noetherian ring and a finitely generated module over such a ring is Noetherian. [L3, given]

1.3 Condition 3 implies condition 2: an exact sequence $R^m\to R^n\to M\to0$ has its right-hand map $\beta$ surjective, because exactness at $M$ says the image of $\beta$ is the kernel of the zero map $M\to0$, which is all of $M$; every element of $R^n$ is $\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_ie_i$, so every element of $M$ is $\sum_{i=1}^{n}r_i\beta(e_i)$ and $M$ is generated by the finite set $\{\beta(e_1),\ldots,\beta(e_n)\}$. [L2, L4, L6, L7, L8, given]

1.4 Assume condition 2 and fix a finite generating set $m_1,\ldots,m_n$ of $M$, with $n\in\mathbb N$. The universal property of the free module gives an $R$-module homomorphism $\beta\colon R^n\to M$ with $\beta(e_i)=m_i$, and its image is the set of all finite sums $\sum_ir_im_i$, which is $\langle m_1,\ldots,m_n\rangle_R=M$; so $\beta$ is surjective. [L2, L4, L5, L6, given]

2.1 Still assuming condition 2, the module $R^n$ is generated by the finite set $\{e_1,\ldots,e_n\}$, hence is Noetherian over the Noetherian ring $R$; therefore its submodule $K:=\ker\beta$ is finitely generated, say by $k_1,\ldots,k_m$ with $m\in\mathbb N$. [L1, L2, L3, L4, step 1.4]

3.1 Still assuming condition 2, the universal property gives $\alpha\colon R^m\to R^n$ with $\alpha(e_j)=k_j$, whose image is $\langle k_1,\ldots,k_m\rangle_R=K=\ker\beta$; together with the surjectivity of $\beta$ this makes $R^m\to R^n\to M\to0$ exact, so $M$ is finitely presented and condition 2 implies condition 3. [L5, L6, L7, L8, step 1.4, step 2.1]

4.1 Steps 1.1, 1.2, 1.3 and 3.1 close the cycle: condition 1 gives condition 2, condition 2 gives condition 1 and condition 3, and condition 3 gives condition 2. The three conditions are therefore equivalent. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where the Noetherian hypothesis is spent.** Only in step 1.2, through [[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]], and in step 2.1, to make $\ker\beta$ finitely generated. Step 1.3 and step 1.4 hold over any commutative ring.

- **The presentation is not canonical.** It depends on the chosen generating set of $M$ and on the chosen generating set of $\ker\beta$; different choices give different $m$ and $n$, and nothing above claims either is minimal.
