## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $X$ be a
normed space that is not spanned by any finite list, and let $0<\alpha<1$. Then
there exists a sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ of unit vectors in $X$ such that

$$\|x_n-x_m\|>\alpha \qquad \text{for all } n\ne m.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Dependent Choice, a normed space $X$ that is not the span of any
finite list, and a real $\alpha$ with $0<\alpha<1$.

[L1] Dependent Choice produces an $\mathbb N$-indexed chain for an entire
relation on a nonempty set ([[def-dependent-choice]]).

[L2] If a subspace admits an ordered basis of finite length, then it is closed
([[cor-finite-dimensional-subspaces-are-closed]]).

[L3] Riesz's lemma gives a unit vector at distance $>\alpha$ from every proper
closed subspace ([[lem-riesz-lemma]]).

[L4] The span of a subset is the set of its finite linear combinations
([[def-linear-combination-and-span]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $S$ be the set of all finite lists $s=(u_0,\dots,u_m)$ of unit vectors in $X$ such that $\|u_j-u_k\|>\alpha$ for all $j\ne k$. The set $S$ is nonempty because any single unit vector lies in it: choose any nonzero $u\in X$ and normalize it. [L4, choose]

1.2 Define a relation $R$ on $S$ by $s\,R\,t$ when $t$ is obtained from $s$ by appending one more unit vector $u$ with $\operatorname{dist}(u,\operatorname{span}(s))>\alpha$. If $s=(u_0,\dots,u_m)\in S$, then the finite span $M:=\operatorname{span}(s)$ is generated by a finite list, so by deleting dependent terms one obtains an ordered basis of finite length for $M$. Since $X$ is not the span of any finite list, $M\subsetneq X$; by [L2] it is closed. Now [L3] applies to $M$ and produces a unit vector $u$ with $\operatorname{dist}(u,M)>\alpha$, so $s$ has an $R$-successor. Thus $R$ is entire on $S$. [L2, L3, L4, choose]

1.3 By [L1], there is a sequence $(s_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ in $S$ with $s_n\,R\,s_{n+1}$ for every $n$. Because each successor appends exactly one new term, the first entries stabilize: if $x_n$ denotes the last entry appended when passing from $s_n$ to $s_{n+1}$, then every earlier $x_j$ remains in all later lists. [L1, construct]

2.1 For $m<n$, the vector $x_n$ was chosen with $\operatorname{dist}(x_n,\operatorname{span}(x_0,\dots,x_{n-1}))>\alpha$, and $x_m$ lies in that span. Hence $\|x_n-x_m\|>\alpha$. Every $x_n$ is a unit vector by the definition of $R$. Therefore $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ is the required separated sequence. [step 1.3, L4] ∎
