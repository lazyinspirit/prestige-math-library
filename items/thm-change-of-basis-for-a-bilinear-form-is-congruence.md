## Statement

Let $B$ be a bilinear form on a finite-dimensional space. If $A$ is its matrix in an old basis and the columns of an invertible matrix $P$ are the new basis vectors in old coordinates, then its matrix in the new basis is

$$P^{\mathsf T}AP.$$

Matrices related by $A'=P^{\mathsf T}AP$ with $P$ invertible are called **congruent**.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The form, the two bases, and the change-of-basis matrix $P$ described above.

[L1] If $x,y$ are coordinate columns, the matrix $A$ of $B$ satisfies $B(u,v)=x^{\mathsf T}Ay$ ([[def-matrix-radicals-rank-and-nondegeneracy-of-a-bilinear-form]]).

[L2] A representing matrix acts on coordinate columns by multiplication ([[thm-coordinate-action-of-a-linear-map]]).

[L3] Transpose reverses products: $(XY)^{\mathsf T}=Y^{\mathsf T}X^{\mathsf T}$ ([[prop-transpose-laws]]).

[L4] Matrix multiplication is associative and represents the relevant finite sums ([[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** coordinate calculation.

1.1 If $x,y$ are the new coordinate columns of $u,v$, respectively, [L2] makes their old coordinate columns $Px,Py$. [L2, given]

2.1 By [L1], $B(u,v)=(Px)^{\mathsf T}A(Py)=x^{\mathsf T}P^{\mathsf T}APy$, using [L3] and [L4]. [step 1.1, L1, L3, L4]

3.1 Since step 2.1 holds for every $x,y$, the new matrix is $P^{\mathsf T}AP$. The displayed relation is therefore exactly the equivalence generated by basis changes and is called congruence, not similarity. [step 2.1] ∎
