## Statement

Every finitely generated semisimple module is a finite direct sum of simple modules. See [[def-semisimple-module]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The hypotheses and objects in the Statement.

[L1] A left $R$-module is semisimple when it is an internal direct sum of simple submodules, allowing the empty direct sum. Hence the zero module is semisimple. ([[def-semisimple-module]]).

[L2] Let $M$ be a left $R$-module and $S\subseteq M$. The submodule generated by $S$ is $$\langle S\rangle_R:=\bigcap\{N\le M:S\subseteq N\}.$$ The family is nonempty because $M\le M$, and its intersection is a submodule by lem-submodule-criterion-sums-and-intersections. Thus $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $S$, just as def-generated-subgroup defines a generated subgroup. ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In a direct-sum decomposition, each generator has finite support; the union of those finite supports contains every generator and hence the whole module. [L1, L2, given, algebra]

2.1 The finite subfamily located in step 1.1 already consists of simple summands of the original internal direct sum, so no summand is zero and no index repeats; its internal sum is therefore a finite direct sum of simple modules, and by step 1.1 it is all of $M$. For a module generated by the empty set, $M=\langle\varnothing\rangle_R=0$, which is the empty direct sum and semisimple by [L1]. This proves the stated claim. [step 1.1, L1, L2, given, algebra] ∎
