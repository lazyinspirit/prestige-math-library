## Statement

Let $P$ be a finite $p$-group and let $Q\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}P$ be a nontrivial
normal subgroup ([[def-finite-p-group]], [[def-normal-subgroup]]). Then

$$[Q,P]<Q,$$

where $[Q,P]=\langle[q,u]:q\in Q,\ u\in P\rangle$ is the subgroup commutator of
[[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]] and
[[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]. Moreover $[Q,P]\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}Q$,
so the quotient $Q/[Q,P]$ is defined
([[def-quotient-group]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite $p$-group $P$ and a nontrivial normal subgroup $Q\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}P$.

[F1] $Q\cap Z(P)\ne\{1\}$: a nontrivial normal subgroup of a finite $p$-group meets the center nontrivially ([[thm-normal-subgroups-of-finite-p-groups-meet-the-center]], [[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F2] Commutators: $[a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}$, and $[A,B]=\langle[a,b]:a\in A,\ b\in B\rangle$; if $A\le B\le G$ and $D\le G$ then every generator $[a,d]$ of $[A,D]$ is a generator of $[B,D]$, so $[A,D]\le[B,D]$. If $N\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$, $A\le N$ and $B\le G$, then $[A,B]\le N$: for $a\in A$, $b\in B$ one has $bab^{-1}\in N$, so $[a,b]=a\,(bab^{-1})^{-1}\in N$ ([[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]], [[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]], [[def-generated-subgroup]], [[def-normal-subgroup]], [[lem-group-inverse-laws]], [[def-subgroup]]).

[F3] If $M\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$, the quotient map $\pi:G\to G/M$, $\pi(g)=gM$, is a surjective group homomorphism, $\pi(A)=AM/M$ for every subgroup $A\le G$, and images of generated subgroups are generated by the images of the generators ([[def-quotient-group]], [[lem-homomorphisms-respect-commutators-and-derived-series]], [[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F4] Order facts: for $M\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}Q$ one has $|Q/M|=[Q:M]=|Q|/|M|$; subgroups of finite $p$-groups are finite $p$-groups; $[Q:M]$ divides $|Q|$, so it is a power of $p$ when $Q$ is ([[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]], [[thm-lagrange]], [[lem-subgroups-of-finite-p-groups-are-p-groups]], [[def-finite-p-group]]).

[F5] Strong induction on the positive integer $|Q|$ ([[thm-strong-induction]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We prove by strong induction on $n=|Q|$ the statement: for every finite $p$-group $P$ and every nontrivial $Q\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}P$ with $|Q|=n$, one has $[Q,P]<Q$ and $[Q,P]\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}Q$. Let such $P$ and $Q$ be given. By [F1] the subgroup $Z_0:=Q\cap Z(P)$ is nontrivial; it is normal in $P$ as the intersection of the normal subgroups $Q$ and $Z(P)$ (the center is normal), and $[Z_0,P]=\{1\}$ because every element of $Z_0$ commutes with every element of $P$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 If $Z_0=Q$, that is $Q\le Z(P)$, then every generator $[q,u]$ of $[Q,P]$ equals $1$, so $[Q,P]=\{1\}<Q$ since $Q$ is nontrivial; also $[Q,P]=\{1\}\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}Q$. [F2, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 Otherwise $Z_0<Q$, so $\bar Q:=Q/Z_0$ is a nontrivial normal subgroup of the finite $p$-group $\bar P:=P/Z_0$ by [F4]; since $|\bar Q|=|Q|/|Z_0|<|Q|=n$, the induction hypothesis of step 1.1 applies to $\bar P$ and $\bar Q$ and gives $[\bar Q,\bar P]<\bar Q$. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $\pi:P\to\bar P$ be the quotient map. By [F3], $\pi(Q)=\bar Q$, $\pi(P)=\bar P$, and $\pi([Q,P])$ is generated by the elements $\pi([q,u])=[\pi(q),\pi(u)]$ for $q\in Q$, $u\in P$, that is $\pi([Q,P])=[\bar Q,\bar P]$; on the other hand $\pi([Q,P])=[Q,P]Z_0/Z_0$. [F2, F3, step 2.2]

4.1 The inclusion $[\bar Q,\bar P]<\bar Q$ of step 2.2 therefore says $[Q,P]Z_0/Z_0<Q/Z_0$, which means $[Q,P]Z_0<Q$; as $[Q,P]\le[Q,P]Z_0$, we get $[Q,P]<Q$. [F4, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Finally $[Q,P]\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}Q$: for $q\in Q$ and $a\in Q$, $b\in P$ one has $q[a,b]q^{-1}=[qaq^{-1},qbq^{-1}]=[a^{q},b^{q}]$, and $a^{q}\in Q$, $b^{q}\in P$ because $q\in Q\le P$ and $P^{q}=P$; hence conjugation by $q$ permutes the generators of $[Q,P]$, so $[Q,P]^{q}=[Q,P]$ for every $q\in Q$, which is normality of $[Q,P]$ in $Q$. This completes the induction and the proof. ∎ [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1]
