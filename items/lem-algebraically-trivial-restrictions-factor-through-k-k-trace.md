## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $k$ be algebraically closed, let $X/k$ be smooth integral of finite type with function field $K$, and let $p:Y\to X$ be an abelian scheme with generic fibre $A/K$. Let $A^\vee/K$ be the dual abelian variety and let $(T,\tau)$ be a supplied $K/k$-trace pair for $A^\vee$. Define $\operatorname{Pic}^{\rm alg}(Y)$ as the subgroup of $\operatorname{Pic}(Y)$ generated by $[\mathcal L_{c_1}]-[\mathcal L_{c_0}]$ for line bundles $\mathcal L$ on $Y\times_k C$, smooth connected $k$-curves $C$, and points $c_0,c_1\in C(k)$. Then generic-fibre restriction satisfies
$$\operatorname{res}\bigl(\operatorname{Pic}^{\rm alg}(Y)\bigr)\subseteq\tau\bigl(T(k)\bigr)\subseteq A^\vee(K)\subseteq\operatorname{Pic}(A).$$
In particular this includes the subgroup generated by differences of fibres of flat codimension-one cycle families over such curves. The assertion assumes the displayed model and trace pair; it does not construct the model, prove trace-cokernel equality or establish Lang–Néron finite generation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field, smooth integral model, abelian scheme, trace pair and curve-family subgroup in the Statement.

[A1] AC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] An abelian scheme is smooth, proper and has geometrically integral fibres and an identity section. [[def-abelian-scheme]]

[F2] The algebraically trivial rigidified Picard functor of an abelian variety is represented by its dual, with the universal Poincare bundle. [[def-rigidified-relative-picard-functor-and-dual-abelian-variety]] [[thm-abelian-variety-dual-and-polarization]]

[F3] Every smooth proper geometrically connected pointed curve has a Jacobian representing its degree-zero Picard functor, and every pointed morphism from it to an abelian variety factors uniquely through that Jacobian. [[thm-picard-zero-represented-by-jacobian]] [[thm-abel-jacobi-universal-property-for-pointed-curve]]

[F4] A one-variable function field over a perfect field with relatively algebraically closed constants has a smooth proper curve model; a rational map from a smooth curve to a proper target extends. [[thm-curves-function-fields-equivalence]] [[lem-rational-map-smooth-curve-to-proper-scheme-extends]]

[F5] A rational map from a smooth integral finite-type variety to an abelian variety extends uniquely. [[thm-nonaffine-rational-map-smooth-variety-to-abelian-variety-extends]]

[F6] The trace pair uniquely factors every homomorphism from the base change of an abelian variety over $k$. [[def-k-trace-of-an-abelian-variety]]

[F7] Flat ring maps satisfy going down; regular local rings are factorial, and on a locally factorial Noetherian integral scheme Weil divisors are Cartier. [[thm-flat-going-down]] [[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]] [[thm-cartier-weil-isomorphism-locally-factorial]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** turn a curve family into a pointed map to the dual, pass through the constant Jacobian, and apply the trace universal property.

1.1 By [F1], $Y\to X$ is smooth and flat with geometrically integral generic fibre. Every component of $Y$ dominates $X$ by flat going down in [F7], so the unique generic-fibre component makes $Y$ irreducible. Smoothness over the field makes $Y$ reduced and regular, hence integral. For every smooth connected curve $C/k$, the product $Y\times_k C$ is likewise smooth and integral, since $k$ is algebraically closed and both factors are geometrically integral. It is therefore locally factorial by [F7]. [F1, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix a generator of $\operatorname{Pic}^{\rm alg}(Y)$, represented by $C$, points $c_0,c_1$ and a line bundle $\mathcal L$ on $Y\times_k C$. Restrict to $A\times_K C_K$, rigidify along the zero section by tensoring with the inverse pullback of its restriction to that section, and tensor by the inverse of its fibre at $c_0$ pulled back from $A$. Every geometric fibre of the resulting family is algebraically equivalent to zero: over an algebraic closure of $K$, the base-changed curve remains connected and contains both the given fibre and $c_0$. By [F2], the family determines a $K$-morphism $f:C_K\to A^\vee$ with $f(c_0)=0$. The point $f(c_1)$ represents the restriction of $[\mathcal L_{c_1}]-[\mathcal L_{c_0}]$ to $A$; rigidifying only tensors by a line pulled back from the point and does not change its class in $\operatorname{Pic}(A)$. [F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Since $k$ is algebraically closed, $k(C)/k$ has relatively algebraically closed constants and $k$ is perfect. Choose by [F4] a smooth proper geometrically integral $k$-curve $\overline C$ with the same function field. The identity on the function field gives a rational map $C\dashrightarrow\overline C$, which extends by [F4] to a morphism $i:C\to\overline C$. Base-changing $i$ to $K$ is dominant and birational because $C$ and $\overline C$ are geometrically integral. Consequently $f$ gives a rational map $\overline C_K\dashrightarrow A^\vee$; by [F5] it extends to $\overline f:\overline C_K\to A^\vee$. The morphisms $f$ and $\overline f\circ i_K$ agree on a dense open and hence everywhere by separatedness. In particular $\overline f(i(c_0))=0$ and $\overline f(i(c_1))=f(c_1)$. [F4, F5, step 2.1, given, choose, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $J/k$ be the Jacobian of $\overline C$ and let $u:\overline C\to J$ be the Abel–Jacobi map based at $i(c_0)$, as in [F3]. Its base change $J_K$ represents the degree-zero Picard functor of $\overline C_K$: for every $K$-scheme $Z$, the curve $(\overline C_K)_Z$ is canonically $\overline C_Z$, and the geometric degree-zero condition and normalized universal bundle are unchanged. Thus $J_K$ is the Jacobian of $\overline C_K$ and $u_K$ its pointed Abel–Jacobi map. Applying [F3] over $K$ gives a homomorphism $h:J_K\to A^\vee$ with $\overline f=h\circ u_K$. By [F6] there is a homomorphism $h_0:J\to T$ over $k$ such that $h=\tau\circ(h_0)_K$. Therefore $f(c_1)=\tau(h_0(u(i(c_1)))_K)$ lies in $\tau(T(k))$. [F3, F6, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Step 4.1 applies to each curve-family generator and the trace image is a subgroup, proving the displayed containment for finite sums. For a flat codimension-one cycle family on $Y\times C$, step 1.1 and [F7] turn its finite integral combination of prime divisors into a Cartier divisor and hence a line bundle. Flatness over $C$ makes restriction of that Cartier divisor to each fibre equal the corresponding cycle fibre, including its multiplicities: locally a parameter of $C$ is a nonzerodivisor on every flat component, and the fibre Cartier construction is base change. Thus the associated fibre differences are among the generators already handled. The zero class is included. No finite generation or converse containment has been used. [F7, step 1.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
