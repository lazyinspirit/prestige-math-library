## Definition

Work in a transitive model of $\mathrm{ZFA} + \mathrm{AC}$
([[def-zfa-universe-atoms-and-kernel]], [[thm-fraenkel-mostowski-permutation-model]]).

**The permutation system.** Let $A$ be the set of atoms, carrying a linear order
$\le$ and, in the real-ordered instance, the order topology of
[[def-order-topology-on-a-linearly-ordered-set]]. Let $G$ be the group of all
automorphisms of $(A,\le)$ and, in the real-ordered instance, of the ordered
set with the intervals that the construction uses. A **support ideal**
$\mathcal{I}$ is a family of subsets of $A$ that contains every singleton, is
closed under subsets and finite unions, and is $G$-invariant:
$e\in\mathcal I$ implies $g[e]\in\mathcal I$ for every $g\in G$. The filter it
generates consists of the subgroups of $G$ that contain the pointwise
stabiliser $\operatorname{fix}(e)$ of some $e\in\mathcal I$. It is a normal
filter: finite intersections use
$\operatorname{fix}(e\cup f)\subseteq\operatorname{fix}(e)\cap
\operatorname{fix}(f)$, conjugation uses
$g\operatorname{fix}(e)g^{-1}=\operatorname{fix}(g[e])$, and the singleton
clause supplies every atom stabiliser. The associated **ordered Läuchli
permutation model**
$\mathcal{P}(A,G,\mathcal{I})$ is the hereditarily symmetric interpretation of
[[def-symmetric-and-hereditarily-symmetric-sets]] for that filter
([[def-permutation-support-system-and-normal-filter]]).

**The two instances.** Two choices are used below and they are not
interchangeable.

1. **Real-ordered, countable compact supports.** $A$ carries the order type of a
   dense linear order completed to a continuum, and $\mathcal{I}$ is the
   $G$-invariant ideal generated by the supports that are compact subsets of
   bounded intervals in the completion.
2. **Rational-ordered, finite supports.** $A$ carries the order type of the
   rationals in the completion, and $\mathcal{I}$ is the $G$-invariant ideal
   of finite subsets.

**The Läuchli continuum.** In each instance the completion of the ordered atom
set, with the order topology and ordered by its cuts, is a linearly ordered
topological space $L$ in which every bounded interval is relatively compact and
which is strongly connected: the order automorphisms supported in $\mathcal{I}$
act transitively on the intervals of the completion that avoid the supports.
$L$ is compact as an ordered space and Hausdorff in the order topology
([[def-compact-space]], [[def-subspace-topology-top]]); the two cuts at the ends
of the completion give two distinct closed subsets of $L$.

## Remarks

- **What is imported and what is proved here.** The construction of the
  completion, the relative compactness of supports in bounded intervals and the
  cut description are read off from Brunner's §§1–3 and recorded here as the
  definition of the models; the choice and separation properties of the two
  instances are proved in the next items of this page, not assumed from the
  construction.

- **Why two instances.** The real-ordered model with countable compact supports
  is the one in which countable choice holds, and the rational-ordered model with
  finite supports is the one in which the Boolean prime ideal principle holds;
  both contain the continuum $L$ described above. The supports are not
  interchangeable: a countable union of compact supports need not be compact, and
  the finite-support model does not support the countable-choice calculation.
