## Statement

Let $(M,\mathcal S)$ be a smooth manifold and let $(U,\varphi)$ be a smooth
chart. Give $U$ the restricted smooth structure of the open submanifold and
$\varphi(U)$ its standard smooth structure. Then the corestriction
$\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)$ is a diffeomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth manifold $(M,\mathcal S)$ and a smooth chart $(U,\varphi)$.

[F1] A smooth chart is a chart of the maximal atlas, hence a homeomorphism of
the open set $U$ onto the open subset $\varphi(U)\subseteq\mathbb R^n$
([[def-smooth-manifold]],
[[def-manifold-chart-coordinate-domain-and-coordinate-functions]]).

[F2] The open subset $\varphi(U)$ carries the standard smooth structure
generated by the identity chart
([[prop-open-subsets-of-euclidean-space-have-the-standard-smooth-structure]]),
and the open subset $U$ carries the restricted structure of $M$
([[prop-an-open-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold-has-a-canonical-restricted-smooth-structure]]).

[F3] A map between smooth manifolds is smooth when its representative with
respect to suitable smooth charts is smooth
([[def-c-r-and-smooth-maps-between-smooth-manifolds]]).

[F4] A diffeomorphism is a bijective smooth map with smooth inverse
([[def-diffeomorphism-and-local-diffeomorphism-of-manifolds]]).

[A1] The identity map of an open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is smooth, each
iterated coordinate derivative being a constant function.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] the corestriction $\varphi:U\to\varphi(U)$ is a bijective
homeomorphism. [given, F1]

1.2 $\varphi$ is smooth: with the smooth chart $(U,\varphi)$ of $U$ from [F2]
and the identity chart of $\varphi(U)$ from [F2], the representative is
$\mathrm{id}\circ\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{\varphi(U)}$, smooth by
[A1], so [F3] applies. [given, F2, F3, A1]

1.3 $\varphi^{-1}$ is smooth: with the identity chart of $\varphi(U)$ and the
chart $(U,\varphi)$ of $U$, the representative is
$\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}\circ\mathrm{id}=\mathrm{id}_{\varphi(U)}$, again
smooth by [A1], so [F3] applies. [given, F2, F3, A1]

2.1 Steps 1.1–1.3 give a bijective smooth map with smooth inverse, which is
exactly the diffeomorphism of [F4]. [given, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
