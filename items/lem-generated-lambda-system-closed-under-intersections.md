## Statement

If $\mathcal P$ is a pi-system on $X$, then its generated lambda-system
$\lambda_X(\mathcal P)$ is closed under binary intersections.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A pi-system $\mathcal P$ on $X$ and $\mathcal D:=\lambda_X(\mathcal P)$.

[L1] A pi-system is nonempty and closed under binary intersections ([[def-pi-system]]).

[L2] The family $\mathcal D$ is the smallest lambda-system on $X$ containing $\mathcal P$ ([[lem-generated-lambda-system-exists-and-is-minimal]]).

[L3] For $A$ in a lambda-system $\mathcal D$, the family $\mathcal D_A=\{B\in\mathcal D:A\cap B\in\mathcal D\}$ is a lambda-system ([[lem-lambda-good-sets]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $A\in\mathcal P$. By [L3], $\mathcal D_A$ is a lambda-system. If $B\in\mathcal P$, then $A\cap B\in\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal D$ by [L1] and [L2], so $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal D_A$. Minimality in [L2] yields $\mathcal D\subseteq\mathcal D_A$. [L1, L2, L3]

2.1 Now fix $B\in\mathcal D$. Symmetry of intersection and step 1.1 show $A\cap B\in\mathcal D$ for every $A\in\mathcal P$, so $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal D_B$. By [L3] and [L2], $\mathcal D_B$ is a lambda-system containing $\mathcal P$ and therefore contains $\mathcal D$. [step 1.1, L2, L3, algebra]

3.1 Thus for arbitrary $A,B\in\mathcal D$ one has $A\in\mathcal D_B$, which means $A\cap B\in\mathcal D$. [step 2.1] ∎
