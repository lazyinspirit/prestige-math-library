## Statement

Use $G=\{H,T,T^\dagger,S,S^\dagger,X,Y,Z\}$ and allow scalar phases $\omega^qI$. Only $H$ contributes to the H count; only $T,T^\dagger$ contribute to the T count. Every word can be rewritten, with its exact scalar retained, as

$$\omega^q T^a H T^{b_1}H\cdots H T^{b_{h-1}}H T^c X^e,$$

where all $b_i$ are odd, exponents are read modulo $8$, and $e\in\{0,1\}$. For $h=0$ interpret this as $\omega^qT^aX^e$. The rewriting never increases either resource count. Each odd power uses one T gate and even powers use none. A reduced word with $h\ge2$ has $\operatorname{sde}(|U_{00}|^2)=h+1$; with $h=1$ the exponent is $2$, and with $h=0$ the squared entry is $0$ or $1$. Consequently reduced words minimize H count among all words for the same matrix up to global phase.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact matrices of $G$, $S=T^2$, and the ring $O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$.

[F1] [[def-qc-clifford-t-gate-library]] supplies the matrices and $\omega^4=-1$.

[F2] [[def-qc-denominator-exponent]] defines the least exponent placing an element in $O$.

## Proof


1.1 Replace $Y$ by $iXZ$, and move all $X$ factors to the right using $XT^r=\omega^rT^{-r}X$ and $XH=HZ$. Combine adjacent diagonal powers. For an even interior exponent, use $H^2=I$, $HT^4H=X$, $HT^2H=\omega T^{-2}HT^{-2}$, or $HT^6H=\omega^{-1}T^2HT^2$. Each replacement strictly decreases the H count and introduces only free diagonal Clifford or Pauli factors. Repeat until all interior exponents are odd. Combining powers never increases the number of odd powers, since the parity of a sum is at most the sum of the parities. Thus both counts never increase. Exact scalar exponents are updated modulo $8$. A stack of diagonal blocks and H symbols implements this reduction in linear time in the input word length: each interior even-block replacement removes at least one H symbol and requires only bounded local stack updates. [F1, given, algebra, construct]

1.2 For a unit column $(u,v)^T$, set $x=2\operatorname{Re}(\bar uv)$, $y=2\operatorname{Im}(\bar uv)$, $z=|u|^2-|v|^2$. Direct matrix multiplication gives the H action $(x,y,z)\mapsto(z,-y,x)$ and the $T^r$ action $(x,y,z)\mapsto(x\cos(r\pi/4)-y\sin(r\pi/4),x\sin(r\pi/4)+y\cos(r\pi/4),z)$. For odd $r$, its sine and cosine are independently signed $1/\sqrt2$. The rightmost $T^cX^e$ starts the column at $(0,0,(-1)^e)$, and the leftmost $T^a$ does not change the final $z$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Ignore these endpoint factors temporarily. After one H the triple is $(\pm1,0,0)$. After two H gates with an odd intervening power it is $(0,\pm1/\sqrt2,\pm1/\sqrt2)$. After three it has $x=\pm1/\sqrt2$, $y=\pm1/2$, $z=\pm1/2$, so the respective least exponents are $1,2,2$. If two nonzero elements have unequal least exponents, their sum and difference have exponent equal to the larger one: scale by that larger power; one summand is primitive modulo $\sqrt2O$ and the other is divisible by $\sqrt2$, so their sum cannot be divisible. Inductively, after $h\ge3$ H gates the exponents of $(x,y,z)$ are $(h-2,h-1,h-1)$. Indeed an odd rotation makes both new transverse coordinates have exponent $h$, and H makes the new exponents $(h-1,h,h)$. The same transition from the displayed two-H triple establishes the induction base. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $|u|^2=(1+z)/2$, the positive exponent $h-1$ of $z$ for $h\ge2$ gives exponent $h+1$ for $|u|^2$ by the same primitive-numerator argument. For one H, $z=0$ and $|u|^2=1/2$ has exponent $2$; for zero H the column is a basis vector. Thus the squared-entry exponent distinguishes every possible reduced H count, including $0,1$. Any competing word reduces by step 1.1 without increasing H count, and its squared-entry exponent is unchanged by scalar phase. Its reduced H count therefore equals that of the given reduced word. This proves H minimality. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
