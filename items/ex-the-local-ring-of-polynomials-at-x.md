## Example

For a field $F$,
$$ F[x]_{(x)}=\left\{\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\in F(x):g(0)\ne0\right\}. $$
This is the ring of rational functions defined at $0$. Its maximal ideal is generated by $x$, and its residue field is canonically $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$ and the polynomial ring $F[x]$.

[F1] Evaluation at $0$ is the unique homomorphism $F[x]\to F$ fixing $F$ and sending $x$ to $0$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]).

[F2] For $g\in F[x]$, one has $g(0)=0$ exactly when $x$ divides $g$ ([[cor-factor-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F3] A quotient by an ideal is a field exactly when the ideal is maximal ([[thm-quotient-is-field-iff-ideal-maximal]]).

[F4] Localising at a prime yields a local ring with the extended prime as maximal ideal, and its residue field is the fraction field of the quotient domain ([[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]], [[cor-residue-field-of-a-localisation-at-a-prime]]).

[F5] The rational function field is $F(x)=\operatorname{Frac}(F[x])$ ([[cor-rational-function-field-as-a-fraction-field]]).

[F6] Every maximal ideal of a commutative ring is prime ([[cor-maximal-ideals-are-prime]]).

[F7] A homomorphism that sends every denominator to a unit factors uniquely through the localisation ([[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]]).

[F8] A localisation fraction $f/g$ is zero exactly when some denominator annihilates $f$ ([[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]]).

[F9] Localisation at a prime ideal $P$ uses the multiplicative set $R\setminus P$ ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], evaluation at $0$ has kernel $(x)$ and is onto. Explicitly, $f+(x)\mapsto f(0)$ is well defined and has inverse $c\mapsto c+(x)$, so $F[x]/(x)\cong F$. Thus [F3] makes $(x)$ maximal, and [F6] makes it prime. [F1, F2, F3, F6, algebra]

1.2 By [F9], the denominators are the elements outside $(x)$, which are exactly the polynomials $g$ with $g(0)\ne0$ by [F2]. They are nonzero and hence units in $F(x)$ by [F5], so [F7] gives a map from the localisation to $F(x)$ sending $f/g$ to the same fraction. If its image is zero, then $f=0$ because [F5] embeds the domain $F[x]$ in its fraction field, and [F8] makes $f/g=0$ in the localisation. The map is therefore injective, and its image is precisely the displayed fractions, which are exactly those admitting a representative with denominator nonzero at $0$. [F2, F5, F7, F8, F9]

2.1 By [F4], the maximal ideal is $(x)F[x]_{(x)}=xF[x]_{(x)}$, and the residue field is $\operatorname{Frac}(F[x]/(x))\cong\operatorname{Frac}(F)$. Every nonzero denominator in the field $F$ is already invertible, so $a/b=(ab^{-1})/1$ and the last fraction field is canonically $F$. [F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
