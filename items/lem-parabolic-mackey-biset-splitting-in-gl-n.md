## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $q$ be a prime power, put $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ and
let $\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ be compositions of $n$
with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$ and $J_1,\dots,J_s$ and standard parabolics
$P_\alpha=L_\alpha\ltimes U_\alpha$, $P_\beta=L_\beta\ltimes U_\beta$
([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]],
[[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]]). Let
$\tau\in S_n$ be any permutation and let $w:=P_\tau$ be its
permutation matrix. One may in particular choose the block-increasing
representative of a $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset
([[lem-standard-parabolic-double-cosets-are-young-double-cosets]],
[[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]), and put
$$M:=wL_\beta w^{-1},\qquad V:=wU_\beta w^{-1},\qquad X:=wP_\beta w^{-1},\qquad C:=L_\alpha\cap M,\qquad A:=L_\alpha\cap V,\qquad D:=U_\alpha\cap M .$$
Then:

1. **Subgroups and normalities.** $M,V,X$ are subgroups of $G$ with $X=MV$,
   $M\cap V=\{I_n\}$ and $V\trianglelefteq X$; and $C,A,D$ are subgroups of $G$
   with $C\le L_\alpha$, $C\le M$, $A\le V$, $D\le U_\alpha$,  $C$ normalising both $A$
   and $D$ and $A\cap C=\{I_n\}$.
2. **The biset splitting.** Let $(L_\alpha/A)\times_C(D\backslash M)$ be the set
   of equivalence classes of the pairs $(lA,Dm)$ with $l\in L_\alpha$ and
   $m\in M$ under the equivalence relation generated by
   $$(lcA,Dm)\sim(lA,Dcm)\qquad(l\in L_\alpha,\ m\in M,\ c\in C),$$
   the **balanced product** of the right $C$-set $L_\alpha/A$ and the left
   $C$-set $D\backslash M$, and write $[lA,Dm]$ for the class of $(lA,Dm)$. Then
   $$\Phi\bigl([lA,Dm]\bigr):=U_\alpha\,l\,m\,w\,U_\beta$$
   is well defined and is a bijection from $(L_\alpha/A)\times_C(D\backslash M)$
   onto the set $\{\,U_\alpha xU_\beta:x\in P_\alpha wP_\beta\,\}$ of
   $(U_\alpha,U_\beta)$-double cosets inside $P_\alpha wP_\beta$.
3. **Equivariance.** The formulas $a\cdot(U_\alpha xU_\beta):=U_\alpha axU_\beta$
   and $(U_\alpha xU_\beta)\cdot b:=U_\alpha xbU_\beta$ define a left action of
   $L_\alpha$ and a right action of $L_\beta$ on the set of claim 2; the formulas
   $$a\cdot[lA,Dm]:=[alA,Dm],\qquad [lA,Dm]\cdot b:=[lA,D\,m\,wbw^{-1}]$$
   define a left action of $L_\alpha$ and a right action of $L_\beta$ on the
   balanced product; and $\Phi$ is equivariant for them, that is
   $\Phi(a\cdot x)=a\cdot\Phi(x)$ and $\Phi(x\cdot b)=\Phi(x)\cdot b$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, a prime power $q$, the group $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$, compositions $\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ of $n$ with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$ and $J_1,\dots,J_s$, the standard parabolics $P_\alpha=L_\alpha\ltimes U_\alpha$ and $P_\beta=L_\beta\ltimes U_\beta$, a permutation $\tau\in S_n$, its permutation matrix $w=P_\tau$, and the groups $M,V,X,C,A,D$ displayed in the statement.

[L1] For every composition $\gamma$ of $n$ with blocks $K_1,\dots,K_t$, writing $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)$ for the index with $k\in K_{\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)}$, the standard parabolic $P_\gamma$, its Levi subgroup $L_\gamma$ and its unipotent radical $U_\gamma$ of [[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]] satisfy $L_\gamma\cap U_\gamma=\{I_n\}$, $U_\gamma\trianglelefteq P_\gamma$, the multiplication map $L_\gamma\times U_\gamma\to P_\gamma$ is a bijection (so that each $g\in P_\gamma$ has a unique factorisation $g=lu$ with $l\in L_\gamma$, $u\in U_\gamma$), $P_\gamma=L_\gamma\ltimes U_\gamma$, and, with the $\gamma$-block diagonal part $\operatorname{diag}_\gamma(g)$ of $g\in P_\gamma$ defined by $\operatorname{diag}_\gamma(g)_{kl}=g_{kl}$ when $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)=\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$ and $\operatorname{diag}_\gamma(g)_{kl}=0$ otherwise, one has $$P_\gamma=\{\,g\in G:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)\,\},\qquad L_\gamma=\{\,g\in P_\gamma:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)\,\},$$ $$U_\gamma=\{\,g\in P_\gamma:g_{kk}=1\text{ for all }k,\ g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }k\ne l\text{ and }\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\ge\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)\,\},$$ and $\operatorname{diag}_\gamma(g)$ is the Levi factor of $g$, that is $g=\operatorname{diag}_\gamma(g)\cdot u$ with $u\in U_\gamma$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]], [[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]]).

[L2] For $\sigma,\tau\in S_n$ the permutation matrix $P_\sigma$ satisfies $(P_\sigma)_{ij}=1$ exactly when $i=\sigma(j)$, $P_\sigma P_\tau=P_{\sigma\circ\tau}$ and $P_{\sigma^{-1}}=P_\sigma^{-1}$, and the classes $w_\sigma=P_\sigma T$ identify $S_n$ with the Weyl group $W=N/T\cong S_n$ ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]); consequently $w=P_\tau$ is invertible with $w^{-1}=P_{\tau^{-1}}$, and for every matrix $A$ and all $k,l$ one has $(wAw^{-1})_{kl}=A_{\tau^{-1}(k),\tau^{-1}(l)}$, the entry formula for the product coming from the permutation-matrix entries together with $(AB)_{kl}=\sum_i A_{ki}B_{il}$ ([[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

[L3] Matrix multiplication over a field is associative, distributes over addition and is compatible with scalar multiplication ([[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]]), and the $(k,l)$ entry of a product $AB$ of matrices is $(AB)_{kl}=\sum_i A_{ki}B_{il}$ ([[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

[L4] In a group, the intersection of a nonempty family of subgroups is a subgroup ([[lem-intersection-of-subgroups]]); a subgroup $N\le G$ is normal when $gNg^{-1}=N$ for all $g\in G$ ([[def-normal-subgroup]]); and for a subgroup $H\le G$ the left coset is $gH=\{\,gh:h\in H\,\}$ ([[def-coset]], [[def-subgroup]]).

[L5] Every $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset of $W$ contains exactly one $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing permutation $\tau$, which is its unique element of minimal inversion length, and $\tau\mapsto P_\alpha P_\tau P_\beta$ is a bijection from the set of these double cosets onto the set of $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double cosets of $G$ ([[lem-standard-parabolic-double-cosets-are-young-double-cosets]]).

[L6] A left action of a group $K$ on a set $S$ is a map $K\times S\to S$ with $e\cdot s=s$ and $(kk')\cdot s=k\cdot(k'\cdot s)$ for all $k,k'\in K$, $s\in S$ ([[def-group-action]]); a **right action** of $K$ on $S$ is a map $S\times K\to S$, written $(s,k)\mapsto s\cdot k$, with $s\cdot e=s$ and $s\cdot(kk')=(s\cdot k)\cdot k'$ for all $k,k'\in K$, $s\in S$, an axiom pair verified below directly from associativity in $G$.

[L7] The two extreme compositions give $P_{(n)}=G$ with $L_{(n)}=G$, $U_{(n)}=\{I_n\}$ and $P_{(1^n)}=B$ with $L_{(1^n)}=T$, $U_{(1^n)}=U$, where $T\cap U=\{I_n\}$ is the diagonal torus and $U$ the standard maximal unipotent subgroup; and for $n=1$ one has $G=B=T=\mathbb F_q^\times$ and $U=\{I_1\}$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]], [[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]], [[def-standard-subgroups-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The transported blocks and the groups $M,V,X$.** Put $\kappa(k):=\operatorname{blk}_\beta(\tau^{-1}(k))$ for $1\le k\le n$, so that $k\in\tau(J_j)$ exactly when $\kappa(k)=j$; by [L2] one has $(wAw^{-1})_{kl}=A_{\tau^{-1}(k),\tau^{-1}(l)}$ for every matrix $A$, so applying the criteria of [L1] to the composition $\beta$ gives $$X=\{\,g:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\kappa(k)>\kappa(l)\,\},\qquad M=\{\,g:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)\,\},\qquad V=\{\,g\in X:g_{kk}=1\text{ for all }k,\ g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }k\ne l\text{ and }\kappa(k)\ge\kappa(l)\,\},$$ and then $M,V\subseteq X$, $M\cap V=\{I_n\}$ (both contain $wI_nw^{-1}=I_n$, and an element $wpw^{-1}=wqw^{-1}$ with $p\in L_\beta$, $q\in U_\beta$ satisfies $p=q\in L_\beta\cap U_\beta=\{I_n\}$), $X=MV$ (as $P_\beta=L_\beta U_\beta$), $X,M,V$ are subgroups of $G$ and $V\trianglelefteq X$, because products and inverses are carried by $w(pq)w^{-1}=(wpw^{-1})(wqw^{-1})$ and $(wqw^{-1})^{-1}=wq^{-1}w^{-1}$ by [L3], and for $x=wpw^{-1}\in X$ and $v=wuw^{-1}\in V$ one has $xvx^{-1}=w(pup^{-1})w^{-1}\in V$ since $pup^{-1}\in U_\beta$ by [L1]. [L1, L2, L3]

2.1 **The subgroups $C,A,D$.** The sets $C=L_\alpha\cap M$, $A=L_\alpha\cap V$ and $D=U_\alpha\cap M$ are subgroups of $G$ by [L4], and $C\subseteq L_\alpha$, $C\subseteq M$, $A\subseteq V$, $D\subseteq U_\alpha$ hold by definition; moreover $A\cap C\subseteq V\cap M=\{I_n\}$ by step 1.1. [step 1.1, L4]

2.2 **The projection onto $M$.** Since $X=MV$ with $M\cap V=\{I_n\}$ and $V\trianglelefteq X$ by step 1.1, every $g\in X$ has a unique factorisation $g=m\,u$ with $m\in M$ and $u\in V$: if $m_1u_1=m_2u_2$ with $m_i\in M$, $u_i\in V$, then $m_2^{-1}m_1=u_2u_1^{-1}\in M\cap V=\{I_n\}$, so $m_1=m_2$ and $u_1=u_2$. Define $\pi:X\to M$ by $\pi(g):=m$ for $g=mu$; then $\pi(g)^{-1}g\in V$ for all $g\in X$, and $\pi$ is a group homomorphism with kernel $V$, because for $g=mu$ and $g'=m'u'$ one has $gg'=m\,m'\,(m'^{-1}um'\,u')$ with $m'^{-1}um'\in V$ and $u'\in V$ by [L3] and step 1.1, so the factorisation of $gg'$ is $(mm')\cdot(m'^{-1}um'u')$ and $\pi(gg')=mm'=\pi(g)\pi(g')$, while $\pi(g)=I_n$ holds exactly when $g\in V$. [step 1.1, L3]

2.3 **The components of $P_\alpha\cap X$ lie in $X$.** Let $g\in P_\alpha\cap X$ and let $g=l_0u_0$ be its factorisation from [L1] with $l_0\in L_\alpha$ and $u_0\in U_\alpha$; put $l:=l_0$ and $u:=gl_0^{-1}=l_0u_0l_0^{-1}\in U_\alpha$, so that $g=u\,l$ with $u\in U_\alpha$ and $l\in L_\alpha$. Then $l$ is the $\alpha$-block diagonal part of $g$: if $k,j$ lie in the same $\alpha$-block $I_t$, then, since $l$ is block diagonal and $u$ has identity diagonal blocks, $g_{kj}=(ul)_{kj}=\sum_m u_{km}l_{mj}=\sum_{m\in I_t}u_{km}l_{mj}=l_{kj}$; entries of $l$ between different $\alpha$-blocks are zero. Hence $l\in X$: if $\kappa(k)>\kappa(j)$ then either $\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(j)$ and $l_{kj}=0$, or $\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)=\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(j)$ and $l_{kj}=g_{kj}=0$ because $g\in X$ by the criterion of step 1.1. Finally $u=gl^{-1}\in X$, because $X$ is a subgroup containing $g$ and $l$ by step 1.1. [step 1.1, L1, L3]

2.4 **$\Phi$ is well defined.** First, changing representatives of the two cosets does not change the image. If $a\in A=L_\alpha\cap V$, then $m^{-1}am\in V$ because $M$ normalises $V$ by step 1.1; hence $U_\alpha l a m wU_\beta=U_\alpha l m(m^{-1}am)wU_\beta=U_\alpha l m wU_\beta$, because $w^{-1}(m^{-1}am)w\in U_\beta$. If $d\in D=U_\alpha\cap M$, then $ldl^{-1}\in U_\alpha$ by [L1], so $U_\alpha l d m wU_\beta=U_\alpha l m wU_\beta$. Second, for $c\in C$ the two sides of the generating balancing relation have the same image, since $(lc)m=l(cm)$ by [L3]: $$U_\alpha\,(lc)\,m\,w\,U_\beta=U_\alpha\,l\,(cm)\,w\,U_\beta .$$ Finally, $\Phi$ takes values in the specified $(U_\alpha,U_\beta)$-double cosets inside $P_\alpha wP_\beta$: writing $m=wm_0w^{-1}$ with $m_0\in L_\beta$ gives $lmw=lwm_0\in P_\alpha wP_\beta$. [step 1.1, L1, L3]

3.1 **Normalisation by $C$.** Let $c\in C$. Then $cL_\alpha c^{-1}=L_\alpha$ and $cU_\alpha c^{-1}=U_\alpha$, because $C\subseteq L_\alpha\subseteq P_\alpha$ and $U_\alpha\trianglelefteq P_\alpha$ by [L1]; and $cMc^{-1}=M$, because $C\subseteq M$; and $cVc^{-1}=V$, because $C\subseteq X$ and $V\trianglelefteq X$ by step 1.1. Hence $cAc^{-1}=c\,(L_\alpha\cap V)\,c^{-1}=(cL_\alpha c^{-1})\cap(cVc^{-1})=L_\alpha\cap V=A$ and $cDc^{-1}=c\,(U_\alpha\cap M)\,c^{-1}=(cU_\alpha c^{-1})\cap(cMc^{-1})=U_\alpha\cap M=D$, so $C$ normalises $A$ and $D$. Consequently right multiplication by $C$ on $L_\alpha/A$ and left multiplication by $C$ on $D\backslash M$ are well defined. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L1, L4]

3.2 **The $\kappa$-block diagonal part, and $L_\alpha\cap X$.** For $g\in X$ let $d$ be the $\kappa$-block diagonal part of $g$, that is $d_{kl}:=g_{kl}$ when $\kappa(k)=\kappa(l)$ and $d_{kl}:=0$ otherwise; then $d=\pi(g)$. Indeed, write $g=wpw^{-1}$ with $p\in P_\beta$ and $p=\lambda u$ with $\lambda\in L_\beta$, $u\in U_\beta$ as in [L1]; the conjugation formula of [L2] gives $d=w\lambda w^{-1}$, and $w\lambda w^{-1}\in M=\{\,g:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)\,\}$ by step 1.1, while $d^{-1}g=w(\lambda^{-1}p)w^{-1}\in V$ by [L1], so the factorisation $g=d\cdot(d^{-1}g)$ with $d\in M$ and $d^{-1}g\in V$ is the one of step 2.2 and $d=\pi(g)$. Consequently, for $g\in L_\alpha\cap X$ the element $\pi(g)$ lies in $L_\alpha$: its entries are entries of $g$ inside $\kappa$-blocks and zero elsewhere, and $g$ vanishes off the $\alpha$-block diagonal by [L1]; hence $\pi(g)\in C=L_\alpha\cap M$. Moreover $g=a\,\pi(g)$ with $a:=\pi(g)\,\bigl(\pi(g)^{-1}g\bigr)\,\pi(g)^{-1}$: here $\pi(g)^{-1}g\in V$ by step 2.2, and $a\in V$ because $\pi(g)\in M\subseteq X$ normalises the normal subgroup $V\trianglelefteq X$ of step 1.1, while $a\in L_\alpha$ as a product of elements of $L_\alpha$; so $a\in A=L_\alpha\cap V$. [step 1.1, step 2.2, L1, L2, L3]

3.3 **$\Phi$ is surjective.** Let $x\in P_\alpha wP_\beta$, say $x=p\,w\,q$ with $p\in P_\alpha$ and $q\in P_\beta$. By [L1] write $p=lu$ with $l\in L_\alpha$, $u\in U_\alpha$ and $q=m_0u_0$ with $m_0\in L_\beta$, $u_0\in U_\beta$, and put $m:=wm_0w^{-1}\in M$, so that $wm_0=mw$ and $x=l\,u\,m\,w\,u_0$. Then $$U_\alpha xU_\beta=U_\alpha\,l\,u\,m\,w\,u_0\,U_\beta=U_\alpha\,l\,m\,w\,U_\beta=\Phi\bigl([lA,Dm]\bigr),$$ because $lul^{-1}\in U_\alpha$ (as $U_\alpha\trianglelefteq P_\alpha$ and $l\in L_\alpha\subseteq P_\alpha$ by [L1]) makes $U_\alpha lu=U_\alpha l$, and $u_0U_\beta=U_\beta$ since $u_0\in U_\beta$. Hence every $(U_\alpha,U_\beta)$-double coset inside $P_\alpha wP_\beta$ lies in the image of $\Phi$. [step 1.1, step 2.4, L1, L3]

3.4 **Claim 3: the two biset structures agree.** For $a\in L_\alpha$ and $b\in L_\beta$ the formulas $a\cdot(U_\alpha xU_\beta):=U_\alpha axU_\beta$ and $(U_\alpha xU_\beta)\cdot b:=U_\alpha xbU_\beta$ are well defined (as $aU_\alpha a^{-1}=U_\alpha$ and $bU_\beta b^{-1}=U_\beta$ by [L1]) and satisfy the axioms of [L6], so they are a left action of $L_\alpha$ and a right action of $L_\beta$. On the balanced product the formulas $a\cdot[lA,Dm]:=[alA,Dm]$ and $[lA,Dm]\cdot b:=[lA,D\,m\,wbw^{-1}]$ are well defined, because they carry the generating relation to a relation of the same form, $((al)cA,Dm)\sim(alA,Dcm)$ and $(lcA,D\,m\,wbw^{-1})\sim(lA,D\,c\,m\,wbw^{-1})$ for $c\in C$; they are actions because $M$ is a subgroup and $b\mapsto wbw^{-1}$ is a homomorphism $L_\beta\to M$ (it is conjugation by the fixed element $w$ and $wL_\beta w^{-1}=M$, while $w(bb')w^{-1}=(wbw^{-1})(wb'w^{-1})$ and $wI_nw^{-1}=I_n$ by [L3]). Finally, for $b^w:=wbw^{-1}$ one has $b^ww=wb$, so $$\Phi\bigl(a\cdot[lA,Dm]\bigr)=U_\alpha\,a\,l\,m\,w\,U_\beta=a\cdot\Phi\bigl([lA,Dm]\bigr),\qquad \Phi\bigl([lA,Dm]\cdot b\bigr)=U_\alpha\,l\,m\,b^w\,w\,U_\beta=U_\alpha\,l\,m\,w\,b\,U_\beta=\Phi\bigl([lA,Dm]\bigr)\cdot b .$$ [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.4, L1, L3, L6]

4.1 **The $M$-component of $U_\alpha\cap X$.** For $u\in U_\alpha\cap X$ one has $\pi(u)\in D=U_\alpha\cap M$. Indeed $\pi(u)$ is the $\kappa$-block diagonal part $d$ of $u$ by step 3.2, so $d\in M$ and $d_{kk}=u_{kk}=1$ for all $k$, while $d_{kl}=0$ whenever $k\ne l$ and $\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ge\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)$: if $\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)$ then $d_{kl}=0$, and if $\kappa(k)=\kappa(l)$ then $d_{kl}=u_{kl}=0$ by the criterion for $U_\alpha$ in [L1]. Hence $d\in U_\alpha$ and $d\in D$. [step 3.2, L1]

5.1 **$\Phi$ is injective.** Let $l,l'\in L_\alpha$ and $m,m'\in M$ with $\Phi([l'A,Dm'])=\Phi([lA,Dm])$, that is $U_\alpha l'm'wU_\beta=U_\alpha lmwU_\beta$. Then there are $u_0\in U_\alpha$ and $u_2\in U_\beta$ with $l'm'wu_2=u_0lmw$; putting $v:=wu_2w^{-1}\in V$ gives $l'm'v=u_0lm$ and hence $$m'=l'^{-1}u_0lmv^{-1}=(l'^{-1}u_0l')\,(l'^{-1}l)\,m\,v^{-1}=u'\,\lambda\,m\,v_1,$$ where $u':=l'^{-1}u_0l'\in U_\alpha$, $\lambda:=l'^{-1}l\in L_\alpha$ and $v_1:=v^{-1}\in V$. Therefore $m'm^{-1}=u'\lambda\,(mv_1m^{-1})=u'\lambda v_2$ with $v_2:=mv_1m^{-1}\in V$, because $m\in M\subseteq X$ normalises $V\trianglelefteq X$ by step 1.1; so $u'\lambda=(m'm^{-1})v_2^{-1}\in M\cdot V\subseteq X$ by step 1.1 and $u'\lambda\in U_\alpha L_\alpha=P_\alpha$ by [L1], that is $u'\lambda\in P_\alpha\cap X$, and step 2.3 gives $u'\in X$ and $\lambda\in X$. Applying the homomorphism $\pi$ of step 2.2 to $m'm^{-1}=u'\lambda v_2$ gives $$m'm^{-1}=\pi(m'm^{-1})=\pi(u')\,\pi(\lambda)\,\pi(v_2)=\pi(u')\,\pi(\lambda),$$ since $m'm^{-1}\in M$ satisfies $\pi(m'm^{-1})=m'm^{-1}$ and $v_2\in V=\ker\pi$ by step 2.2; with $d:=\pi(u')\in D$ by step 4.1 and $c:=\pi(\lambda)\in C$ by step 3.2 this is $m'=d\,c\,m$. By step 3.2 applied to $\lambda\in L_\alpha\cap X$ we have $\lambda=a\,c$ with $a\in A$, so $l'=l\lambda^{-1}=l\,c^{-1}a^{-1}$. Hence $l'A=lc^{-1}A$ and $Dm'=Dcm$, and the pair $(l'A,Dm')=(lc^{-1}A,Dcm)$ is related to $(lA,Dm)$ by the generating relation, because $(lc^{-1})\,c=l$ and $c\,(Dm)=Dcm$; therefore $[l'A,Dm']=[lA,Dm]$, so $\Phi$ is injective. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.2, step 4.1, L1, L3, L4]

6.1 **Claims 1 and 2.** Steps 1.1, 2.1 and 3.1 prove claim 1. Step 2.4 shows that $\Phi$ is well defined on the balanced product, step 3.3 that it is surjective onto the set of $(U_\alpha,U_\beta)$-double cosets inside $P_\alpha wP_\beta$, and step 5.1 that it is injective; hence $\Phi$ is a bijection, which is claim 2, the set $P_\alpha wP_\beta$ being by [L5] the double coset attached to the $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset of $\tau$. The entry and projection arguments above used no block-increasing hypothesis. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.4, step 3.3, step 5.1, L5]

7.1 **The extreme cases.** For $\alpha=\beta=(n)$ one has $U_\alpha=U_\beta=\{I_n\}$ and $L_\alpha=L_\beta=G$ by [L7], so $M=X=G=L_\alpha$, $V=\{I_n\}$, $C=G$ and $A=D=\{I_n\}$; the balanced product is the quotient of $G\times G$ by $(gc,m)\sim(g,cm)$ and is in bijection with $G$ via the well-defined map $(l,m)\mapsto lm$ (with inverse $g\mapsto(g,I_n)$), while the double cosets inside $P_\alpha wP_\beta=G$ are the singletons, so claim 2 reads $G\cong G$ via $g\mapsto gw$, each target double coset being a singleton. For $\alpha=\beta=(1^n)$ one has $U_\alpha=U_\beta=U$ and $L_\alpha=L_\beta=T$ by [L7], so $M=wTw^{-1}=T$ (the conjugation formula of [L2] only permutes the diagonal positions), $V=wUw^{-1}$, $C=T\cap T=T$, and $A=T\cap V=\{I_n\}$, $D=U\cap T=\{I_n\}$ because $T\cap U=\{I_n\}$ by [L7] and $V$ is a conjugate of $U$; the balanced product is therefore $(T/\{I_n\})\times_T(\{I_n\}\backslash T)\cong T$ and claim 2 specialises to a bijection $T\to U\backslash(BwB)/U$.
