## Statement

Let $p$ be any prime, put $\mathcal O=\mathbb Z_p$ and $R=\mathcal O\llbracket T\rrbracket$. Then $R$ is a Noetherian local unique factorisation domain with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(p,T)$. Its prime-element classes are represented by $p$ and the monic distinguished irreducible polynomials in $\mathcal O[T]$, where distinguished means reduction modulo $p$ is a power of $T$. Every submodule of a finitely generated $R$-module is finitely generated, and every finitely generated $R$-module is finitely presented.

Suppose $N$ is a finitely generated $R$-module killed by a nonzero $f\in R$. For an irreducible factor $h$ of $f$, write $N_{(h)}$ for localization by the elements outside the prime ideal $(h)$. If $N_{(h)}=0$ for every irreducible factor $h$ of $f$, then $N$ is finite. More explicitly there exist integers $r,s\ge0$ with $p^rN=T^sN=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A prime $p$, the rings and modules in the Statement, and the finite generating and annihilator data when used.

[F1] The p-adic integers are the compatible residues modulo $p^n$, with their residue topology. [[def-p-adic-integers-as-compatible-residue-classes]]

[F2] Every nonzero integral p-adic power series is uniquely a power of its uniformizer times a unit times a monic distinguished polynomial. [[thm-p-adic-weierstrass-preparation-for-iwasawa-power-series]]

[F3] Division by a primitive series gives a unique polynomial remainder below its first unit-coefficient degree; in particular this holds for a monic distinguished polynomial. [[lem-weierstrass-division-for-p-adic-power-series]]

[F4] Over a given UFD, a primitive positive-degree polynomial is irreducible exactly when it is irreducible over the fraction field. The proof uses finite contents and primality of the given UFD factors. [[lem-gauss-lemma-over-a-ufd]]

[F5] Polynomial division by a monic polynomial over a commutative ring gives a unique remainder of smaller degree. [[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]

[F6] For a positive-sized square matrix over a commutative ring, its adjugate satisfies $A\operatorname{adj}(A)=\det(A)I$. [[thm-adjugate-identity-over-a-commutative-ring]]

[F7] Noetherian means that every submodule is finitely generated, applied to the regular module for a Noetherian ring. [[def-noetherian-module]], [[def-noetherian-ring]]

[F8] A UFD is a domain with finite factorization into irreducibles and uniqueness up to permutation and associates. [[def-unique-factorisation-domain]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], a nonzero $x\in\mathcal O$ has a first nonzero residue and is $p^ru$ with $u$ nonzero modulo $p$. The inverses of $u$ modulo $p^n$ are unique and compatible, giving its inverse in $\mathcal O$. In any nonzero ideal choose an element with least $r$; it generates that ideal. This also proves that $\mathcal O$ is a domain: a product of two such elements has the sum of their valuations and a unit factor. It is complete by compatible coefficient limits, a DVR with uniformizer $p$, and has the explicit unique single-prime factorization $p^r$ times a unit, for every prime including $2$. This is precisely the UFD property in [F8] for the coefficient ring; no general PID-to-UFD theorem is needed. Products of lowest nonzero coefficients now also show that $R=\mathcal O\llbracket T\rrbracket$ is a domain. [F1, F8, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Every submodule $U$ of $\mathcal O^d$ is finitely generated by induction on $d$. For $d=0$ it is zero. Its first-coordinate image is an ideal $(a)$ by step 1.1. If $a=0$, it lies in $\mathcal O^{d-1}$ and induction applies. Otherwise choose one vector over $a$; subtracting its multiples from any vector of $U$ leaves the first-coordinate kernel, a submodule of $\mathcal O^{d-1}$. The chosen vector and the finitely many inductive kernel generators generate $U$. There are only finitely many choices in this finite-rank induction. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 By finite induction on degree, a monic polynomial in $\mathcal O[T]$ is a finite product of monic irreducible polynomials: in a nontrivial factorization of a monic polynomial both leading coefficients are units, so normalize them to $1$, and both positive degrees decrease. A nonunit constant factor cannot occur. If the original polynomial is distinguished, reducing its monic factors modulo $p$ shows that each is a power of $T$ modulo $p$, hence distinguished. For a monic irreducible factor $P$, the explicit UFD of step 1.1 and [F4] show that $P$ is irreducible in $\mathbb Q_p[T]$. [F4, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 For a nonzero ideal $I$ of $R$, let $m$ be the least coefficient-content valuation among its nonzero elements; each series has such a minimum by the well-ordering of its nonzero coefficient valuations. Write $I=p^mJ$ by dividing every element by $p^m$. The ideal $J$ contains a primitive series, and [F2] gives a monic distinguished polynomial $P\in J$ after removing its unit factor. If $d=\deg P$, [F3] writes every $g\in J$ as $qP+r$, with $r\in J\cap\mathcal O[T]_{<d}$. This intersection is a submodule of $\mathcal O^d$, hence has finitely many generators by step 2.1. Together with $P$ they generate $J$ and, after multiplying by $p^m$, generate $I$. The zero ideal is finitely generated, and if $d=0$ then $P=1$. This proves Noetherianity in the finite-generation sense of [F7], without an ACC/maximal-condition equivalence. [F2, F3, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 For a monic distinguished irreducible factor $P$ of positive degree from step 2.2, [F3] identifies $R/(P)$ with $\mathcal O[T]/(P)$ by unique polynomial remainders, compatibly with sums and products. By [F5], a polynomial of degree below $\deg P$ cannot be divisible by $P$ even over $\mathbb Q_p$, so the latter quotient embeds into $\mathbb Q_p[T]/(P)$; this is a field by Euclidean polynomial division and the irreducibility from step 2.2. Thus $(P)$ is prime in $R$. Also $(p)$ is prime because $R/(p)=\mathbb F_p\llbracket T\rrbracket$ is a domain by lowest nonzero coefficients. Preparation [F2] and step 2.2 now factor every nonzero series into a unit times a power of $p$ times finitely many such prime $P$'s. Primality gives uniqueness by cancelling one prime factor at a time. Thus $R$ is a UFD and every irreducible is associate to one listed factor. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 A series is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is a unit of $\mathcal O$, by recursive coefficient inversion and the constant coefficient of a product. All remaining series belong to $\mathfrak m=(p,T)$, and $R/\mathfrak m=\mathbb F_p$, so $R$ is local. Also every submodule of $R^d$ is finitely generated: repeat the coordinate-image/kernel induction of step 2.1, now lifting the finitely many generators of the image ideal supplied by step 3.1. For a submodule of a finitely generated module, pull back along a finite free surjection and take images of these finitely many generators. In particular the kernel of a finite free surjection onto a finitely generated module is finitely generated, giving a finite presentation. This proves the module assertions directly under [F7]; no infinite sequence of choices is used. [F7, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 For the final assertion, if $N=0$ or $f$ is a unit there is nothing to prove. Otherwise list the distinct irreducible factors of $f$ as $h_1,\ldots,h_l$, with $l\ge1$, and choose finitely many generators $n_1,\ldots,n_v$ of $N$. Since $N_{(h_i)}=0$, for each generator there is an element outside $(h_i)$ killing it. A finite product gives $t_i\in\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)$ outside $(h_i)$. Set $g=\sum_{i=1}^l t_i\prod_{j\ne i}h_j$. Modulo $(h_i)$ every term but the $i$th is zero and the $i$th is nonzero, since $(h_i)$ is prime and no other $h_j$ is divisible by $h_i$. Thus $g$ is a nonzero annihilator with no irreducible factor in common with $f$. This is an explicit finite avoidance formula. [step 3.2, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 At least one of $f,g$ is primitive, since otherwise both have factor $p$. By [F2], its distinguished polynomial $P$ lies in $\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)$. If $P=1$ then $N=0$. Otherwise put $d=\deg P>0$ and $A=R/(P)$, finite free of rank $d$ over $\mathcal O$ by [F3]. Let $b$ be the other annihilator and replace it by its polynomial remainder modulo $P$. The polynomials $b$ and $P$ are coprime over $\mathbb Q_p$: a common factor of $P$ is represented by one of its monic distinguished factors from step 2.2, and the irreducibility from step 2.2 together with [F5] would make that factor divide the other annihilator in $R$, contradicting step 4.2. Euclidean Bezout in $\mathbb Q_p[T]$ therefore makes multiplication by $b$ on $\mathbb Q_p\otimes A$ invertible. Its integral $d$-by-$d$ matrix has nonzero determinant $\delta\in\mathcal O$. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.2, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 By [F6], applying the adjugate to the coordinate vector of $1$ in $A$ gives $\delta\cdot1=b y$ for some $y\in A$. Hence $\delta\in(P,b)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_R(N)$. Write $\delta=p^ru$ with $u$ a unit by step 1.1, so $p^rN=0$. Distinguishedness gives $T^d\in pA$, and therefore $T^{rd}N=0$. The ring $R/(P,p^r)$ is generated by $d$ elements over the finite ring $\mathcal O/(p^r)$, and a $v$-generated module over it has at most $p^{rdv}$ elements. If $r=0$ it is the zero module. This proves the claimed finiteness and the explicit annihilating powers, using only the finitely many irreducible factors of the given annihilator. [F3, F6, step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
