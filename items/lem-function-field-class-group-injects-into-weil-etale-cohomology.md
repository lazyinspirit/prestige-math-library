## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $k$ be finite, let $C/k$ be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve, let $T$ be a finite set of closed points, and put $U=C\setminus T$. Write $\operatorname{Cl}(U)$ for divisors on $U$ modulo principal divisors. For the Weil–étale site and $\mathbb G_m$ convention of the supplied Definition, there is a canonical injection
$$\iota_U:\operatorname{Cl}(U)\hookrightarrow H_W^1(U,\mathbb G_m).$$
For a finite étale map $f:U'\to U$ coming from a finite separable extension of their function fields, the injections commute with divisor norm and Weil–étale corestriction, including when the full finite constant fields differ. Consequently, for every prime $p$,
$$\mathbb Z_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Cl}(U)\hookrightarrow
\mathbb Z_p\otimes_{\mathbb Z}H_W^1(U,\mathbb G_m).$$
For an abelian algebraic function-field tower and a finite set containing all its ramified places, these maps give an injection on norm/corestriction inverse limits. In particular the class-group limit denoted $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K^\Sigma)_p$ in the supplied Definition is a submodule of $H^1((\mathcal O_K^\Sigma)_W,\mathbb Z_p(1))$, equivariantly for the tower's Galois group. This applies also to each intermediate infinite tower $K_n$. No finite presentation, torsion or determinant assertion about that cohomology is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the finite-field curve, open, choice assumptions, and, for the naturality and limit assertions, the finite étale maps and towers in the Statement.

[F1] The Weil–étale site uses finite-type étale schemes over $\bar U$ with arithmetic-Frobenius-twisted morphisms. Its $\mathbb G_m$ is invertible regular functions and its cohomology is sheaf cohomology. The function-field tower convention takes norm/corestriction limits and defines its $p$-cohomology by $\mathbb Z_p\otimes H_W^1(\mathbb G_m)$. [[def-weil-etale-class-group-and-cohomology-in-function-field-tower]]

[F2] On a smooth proper geometrically integral curve, closed-point local rings are DVRs, Weil and Cartier divisors agree, and principal divisors correspond. Restriction gives the same assertions on an open subcurve. [[thm-cartier-weil-divisors-curves-agree]]

[F3] A torsion-free abelian group is flat over $\mathbb Z$. [[prop-torsion-free-abelian-groups-are-flat]]

[F4] Finite extensions of finite fields are cyclic Galois, generated by arithmetic Frobenius. [[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]]

[F5] A finite splitting field of a separable polynomial is Galois and its full automorphism group has the original field as fixed field. [[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]

[A1] AC and DC are explicitly retained. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 On the site in [F1], let $\mathcal R^\times$ assign to an étale object the product of the multiplicative groups of the function fields of its integral components. The usual restriction of rational functions, including Frobenius on constants, defines its maps. It is a sheaf: the assertion that compatible rational functions on an étale covering descend may be checked at each generic point, where it is the equalizer assertion for a separable field extension and its two pullbacks. For a finite separable extension this equalizer consists exactly of the base field: after adjoining the finitely many conjugates to form a finite separable splitting field, the equalizer elements are fixed by every embedding, hence lie in the base field by [F5]. General coverings reduce to a finite subcover of each quasi-compact object; the resulting descended rational functions agree on overlaps and are unique. This also proves the assertion for a disconnected object by components. Put $\mathcal D=\mathcal R^\times/\mathbb G_m$, the quotient sheaf of abelian groups. Thus
$$0\longrightarrow\mathbb G_m\longrightarrow\mathcal R^\times\longrightarrow\mathcal D\longrightarrow0$$
is an exact sequence of Weil–étale sheaves, with multiplicative notation in the first two terms. [F1, F2, F5, A1, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The quotient $\mathcal D$ is the divisor sheaf. Indeed a nonzero rational function has orders at the closed points, unchanged by multiplication by a local unit; the kernel of all orders is exactly the local units by the DVR assertion in [F2]. Every integral coefficient at one point is locally the divisor of a power of a uniformizer. A finite divisor is therefore locally a rational function, so these orders give all sections of the quotient sheaf. Conversely these local orders glue, and on a quasi-compact curve their support is finite: finitely many local rational functions have only finitely many zeros and poles. The same statement holds on étale objects, whose local rings are unramified extensions of the curve DVRs and hence DVRs: an étale local extension has maximal ideal generated by the original uniformizer and separable residue extension, so the valuation orders and the uniformizer argument persist. Thus on $\bar U$, sections of $\mathcal D$ are exactly its finite divisors. Global sections on the Weil–étale site are those divisors invariant under arithmetic Frobenius. Its finite orbits of geometric closed points are precisely the closed points of $U$; the sum over the orbit of a point is its pullback divisor. Consequently
$$H_W^0(U,\mathcal D)=\operatorname{Div}(U).$$
Similarly $H_W^0(U,\mathcal R^\times)=\bar k(C)^{{\times},\phi}=k(C)^\times$: a rational function uses finitely many coefficients in $\bar k$, hence is defined over one finite extension of $k$; invariance under its Frobenius generator descends it to $k$ by [F4], or coefficientwise in a finite $k$-basis of that extension. This identifies the global-section map with the actual principal divisor map. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.1 The initial exact sequence of sheaf cohomology for step 1.1 now contains
$$k(C)^\times\xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}}\operatorname{Div}(U)
\xrightarrow{\delta_U}H_W^1(U,\mathbb G_m).$$
For completeness, exactness at the middle can also be read directly: represent a divisor by rational equations on a cover; their unit ratios are its $\mathbb G_m$ cocycle. It is zero exactly when multiplication of those equations by local units makes them glue to a global rational section. Thus the kernel is exactly the image of global rational functions, as asserted by the connecting homomorphism. Taking the quotient by these principal divisors defines the injection $\iota_U$. The resulting class is the class of the invertible sheaf $\mathcal O_U(D)$ with its canonical Weil descent. This construction works also for $T=\varnothing$ and the zero divisor. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $f:U'\to U$ be as in the Statement. Étale-locally on $U$ it is a disjoint union of finitely many copies of the base. On this cover, norm on rational functions and units is multiplication over these copies, and divisor norm is addition over them. These maps commute with the divisor map. They are invariant under permutation of the copies, so descend and give a morphism from the pushed-forward exact divisor sheaf sequence of $U'$ to that of $U$. Pushforward is exact here because after this étale base change it is a finite product functor on abelian sheaves, which is exact. On a closed point the descended divisor formula is $[y]\mapsto[\kappa(y):\kappa(f(y))][f(y)]$; it follows either by grouping the geometric points or by counting residue embeddings. Hence this is exactly the divisor norm. Naturality of connecting homomorphisms shows that $\delta_U$ of the norm divisor is the norm on cohomology of $\delta_{U'}$; the latter is corestriction, whose local transfer multiplies these same finite copies. The unit-ratio cocycle description of step 2.1 verifies this equality directly on any common splitting cover, without using a duality or perfect-complex comparison. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.2 If $U'$ has full constants $k'$ strictly larger than $k$, its base change to $\bar k$ has $[k':k]$ components, cyclically permuted by arithmetic Frobenius by [F4]. Choose the component corresponding to one embedding $k'\hookrightarrow\bar k$; its stabilizer is the subgroup generated by $\phi^{[k':k]}$. The Weil site on all the components with their $\phi$-action is equivalent to the Weil site over $k'$ on this component: restricting gives one direction, and making the finite disjoint union of its translates gives the inverse, with the last translate glued by $\phi^{[k':k]}$. Both composites carry canonical componentwise identifications. Replacing the chosen embedding permutes those translates, so the resulting norm is independent of that choice. Under this identification the multiplication and addition in step 3.1 include every constant-field component exactly once, and are the usual subgroup transfer on the Weil action. Thus the same norm/corestriction identity holds with changing full constants. [F1, F4, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 The additive group $\mathbb Z_p$ is torsion-free. Indeed multiplication by an integer prime to $p$ is invertible modulo every $p^r$, and if $pa=0$ then the coordinate $a\bmod p^r$ is zero by testing $pa=0\bmod p^{r+1}$; induction handles any power of $p$. By [F3], tensoring the injection of step 2.1 with $\mathbb Z_p$ preserves injectivity. Step 3.1 and step 3.2 preserve this injection under all norm/corestriction maps. An element of an inverse limit is a compatible family; if its image in the other limit is zero, each coordinate is zero by the corresponding finite-level injection, so the whole family is zero. This needs no surjectivity or Mittag–Leffler assertion about the transition maps. For the tower in the Statement, removing all ramified places makes each finite curve map on these opens finite étale, so these are exactly the systems in [F1]. Automorphisms of the tower are degree-one instances of the same natural construction, giving Galois equivariance. Restricting to the finite subfields of $K_n$ gives the asserted intermediate-tower injection. Thus the class-group annihilator in BDL's specialization is a submodule of the corresponding cohomology annihilator, with no claim that its missing presentations or torsion have been proved. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra] ∎
