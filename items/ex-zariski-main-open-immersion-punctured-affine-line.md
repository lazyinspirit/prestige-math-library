## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field, put
$R=k[t]$ and let $S=k[t,t^{-1}]=R_t$ be the principal localisation of $R$ at
$t$ ([[def-principal-localisation]]). Then:

1. The inclusion $R\to S$ is of finite type and quasi-finite
   ([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]). Its fibres are
   as follows: over the prime $(t)$ of $R$ the fibre is empty, and indeed
   $$ S\otimes_R\kappa((t))=0; $$
   over every prime $\mathfrak p$ of $R$ with $t\notin\mathfrak p$ the fibre
   ring is the residue field, $S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p)$,
   and the local fibre at the uniquely determined prime above $\mathfrak p$ is
   that same field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$.

2. The finite $R$-algebra $T:=R$ realizes the factorization of
   [[thm-quasi-finite-algebra-open-finite-factorization]] with the single
   element $g:=t$: the element $t$ lies in $T$ and avoids every prime of $S$,
   $$ T_t=R_t=S=S_t, $$
   and the contraction map
   $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(T)=\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is
   a homeomorphism onto $D_R(t)$
   ([[cor-principal-localisation-spectrum-is-distinguished-open]]). No cover
   by more than one principal open is needed.

So the inclusion of the punctured affine line over the affine line is the
simplest instance of Zariski's main theorem in its open form: the quasi-finite
algebra is already a principal localisation of the finite $R$-algebra $R$
itself, and the open image is the principal open $D(t)$. The Axiom of Choice
is recorded only because the general factorization theorem is invoked in part
2; the fibre computation and the display $T_t=S$ are explicit and choice-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the polynomial ring $R=k[t]$, the principal localisation $S=R_t=k[t,t^{-1}]$ of $R$ at $t$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[L1] The map $R\to S$ is **quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$** when $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, the map is quasi-finite when it is of finite type and quasi-finite at every prime, and the fibre of $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ over $\mathfrak p$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))$, the local ring at the prime over $\mathfrak p$ being $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ ([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]).

[L2] An $R$-algebra $A$ is of finite type over $R$ when $A=R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for finitely many elements, and module-finite over $R$ when it is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] For $f\in R$ the principal localisation is $R_f=S_f^{-1}R$ with $S_f=\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}$, and its elements may be written $r/f^n$; in particular $R_1$ is canonically isomorphic to $R$ ([[def-principal-localisation]]).

[L4] For $f\in R$ the localisation map $R\to R_f$ induces a homeomorphism from $\operatorname{Spec}(R_f)$ onto the distinguished open subset $D(f)$ ([[cor-principal-localisation-spectrum-is-distinguished-open]]).

[L5] For a unital ring map $A\to C$ and a multiplicative subset $M\subseteq A$ there is a ring isomorphism $(M^{-1}A)\otimes_AC\cong\overline M^{-1}C$, with no flatness, finite-generation or nonzero-ring hypothesis ([[lem-tensor-ring-presentations-for-base-change]]).

[L6] For multiplicative subsets $S,T\subseteq R$, with $\bar T$ the image of $T$ in $S^{-1}R$ and $U$ generated by $S\cup T$, there is an isomorphism $\bar T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)\cong U^{-1}R$ ([[prop-iterated-localisation]]).

[L7] For a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $R$ there is a canonical field isomorphism $R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\cong\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$, the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ ([[cor-residue-field-of-a-localisation-at-a-prime]]).

[L8] Contraction along the localisation map $R\to S^{-1}R$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}R)$ onto the primes of $R$ disjoint from $S$, with inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto S^{-1}\mathfrak p$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[L9] Assume the Axiom of Choice. If $R\to S$ is of finite type and quasi-finite at every prime of $S$ and $S'$ is the integral closure of the image of $R$ in $S$, then there are a finite $R$-subalgebra $T\subseteq S'$ and finitely many $g_1,\ldots,g_n\in T$ with the contraction map $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(T)$ a homeomorphism onto the open set $U=D_T(g_1)\cup\cdots\cup D_T(g_n)$, $T_{g_i}\cong S_{g_i}$, and $T_g\cong S_g$ for every $g\in T$ with $D_T(g)\subseteq U$ ([[thm-quasi-finite-algebra-open-finite-factorization]]).

[L10] An element $b$ of a commutative ring $B$ is integral over a subring $A\subseteq B$ when it is a root of a monic polynomial in $A[X]$ ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]]).

[L11] For a unital ring map $R\to S$ the relative integral closure $\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ is the subring of $S$ consisting of the elements integral over the map; it contains the image of $R$ and is the integral closure of that image in $S$ ([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]).

[L12] In the localisation $S^{-1}R$ two fractions are equal, $r/s=r'/s'$, if and only if $v(rs'-r's)=0$ for some $v\in S$, and every $s\in S$ maps to a unit ([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]).

[L13] If $R$ is an integral domain then so is $R[x]$; in particular $k[t]$ is a domain ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]).

[L14] The **Axiom of Choice** (AC) is the statement that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Take $R=k[t]$ and $S=R_t=k[t,t^{-1}]$, the principal localisation of $R$ at $t$ as in [L3], and note $S\ne0$ since $R$ is a domain by [L13] and a localisation of a nonzero ring at a nonzerodivisor is nonzero: if $r/t^n=0$ then $t^mr=0$ for some $m\ge0$ by [L12], whence $r=0$. The localisation map $R\to S$ is injective by the same computation, so we may view $R\subseteq S$. Every element of $S$ is of the form $r/t^n$, that is $r\cdot(1/t)^n$, so $S=R[1/t]$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by the single element $1/t$; hence $R\to S$ is of finite type by [L2]. [given, L2, L3, L12, L13]

2.1 We compute the fibres. Let $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ and apply [L5] with $A=R$, $C=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ and the multiplicative subset $M=\{1,t,t^2,\ldots\}$ of $R$, whose image in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is generated by the image of $t$: $$ S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)=(M^{-1}R)\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong\overline M^{-1}\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)_t. $$ By [L7] the field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is $\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$, so the image of $t$ in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is zero exactly when $t\in\mathfrak p$. If $t\in\mathfrak p$, then inverting the zero element of a ring gives the zero ring, so $\kappa(\mathfrak p)_t=0$ and therefore $S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)=0$; if $t\notin\mathfrak p$, then the image of $t$ in the field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is a nonzero element, hence a unit, and then every fraction $r/t^n=r(t^{-1})^n$ already lies in $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so $\kappa(\mathfrak p)_t=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. [given, step 1.1, L5, L7]

2.2 For part 2 put $T:=R$, viewed as a subring of $S$ by step 1.1. Then $T$ is module-finite over $R$ because it is generated as an $R$-module by the single element $1$, hence a finite $R$-algebra in the sense of [L2]. Every element $r\in R$ is integral over $R$, being a root of the monic polynomial $X-r\in R[X]$ by [L10]; so $R\subseteq\operatorname{Int}_R(S)=S'$ by [L11], and $T=R$ is a finite $R$-subalgebra of $S'$. [given, step 1.1, L2, L10, L11]

3.1 By the fibre form of [L1] the fibre of $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ over $\mathfrak p$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(S\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))$. Hence by step 2.1 the fibre over the prime $(t)$ is $\operatorname{Spec}(0)=\varnothing$, in particular $S\otimes_R\kappa((t))=0$ and no prime of $S$ lies over $(t)$, while over every $\mathfrak p$ with $t\notin\mathfrak p$ the fibre is $\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(\mathfrak p))$, a single point. This matches [L8], which shows that the primes of $S=R_t$ are exactly the primes of $R$ not containing $t$. [given, step 2.1, L1, L8]

3.2 With $g:=t\in T$ we have $T_t=R_t=S$ by [L3] and $S_t=S$, since $t$ is already invertible in $S=R_t$; and $g\notin\mathfrak q$ for every $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ by [L8]. The contraction map $\operatorname{Spec}(S)\to\operatorname{Spec}(T)=\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ is a homeomorphism onto $D_R(t)$ by [L4] applied to $f=t$, which is the open set $U=D_T(t)$; this is exactly the configuration of [L9] with $n=1$, $g_1=t$ and a single principal open. [given, step 2.2, L3, L4, L8, L9]

4.1 We compute the local fibres and quasi-finiteness. Let $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ and let $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$ be its contraction; by [L8] we have $t\notin\mathfrak p$. Localizing $S=R_t$ at $\mathfrak q$ is the same as localizing $R$ at the image of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$, because $t\in R\setminus\mathfrak p$ and therefore the multiplicative set generated by $t$ and $R\setminus\mathfrak p$ is just $R\setminus\mathfrak p$, by [L6]. Every $s=r/t^n\in S$ outside $\mathfrak q$ has $r\notin\mathfrak p$, so it becomes a unit after localizing by $R\setminus\mathfrak p$; conversely every element of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$ maps outside $\mathfrak q$. Thus the two localizations have the same universal property and $$ S_{\mathfrak q}=(R_t)_{\mathfrak q}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}, $$ an isomorphism of $R$-algebras carrying the extension of $\mathfrak p$ to the extension of $\mathfrak p$. It follows that $$ S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p) $$ by [L7]. This is finite over the field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, of dimension one; by [L1] the map $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$, and since $\mathfrak q$ was arbitrary and $R\to S$ is of finite type by step 1.1, the map is quasi-finite. [given, step 1.1, step 3.1, L1, L6, L7, L8]

5.1 Part 1 is proved by steps 1.1, 2.1 and 4.1: the map is finite type and quasi-finite, the fibre over $(t)$ is empty with $S\otimes_R\kappa((t))=0$, over every other prime the fibre ring is $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, and the local fibre at the prime above $\mathfrak p$ is that same field. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 The Axiom of Choice was recorded in the Statement for the same reason it appears in [L9], namely the general factorization theorem invoked in step 3.2; the computations of steps 1.1, 2.1, 4.1, 2.2 and 3.2 manipulate finitely many explicit elements ($t$, $1/t$, the fractions $r/t^n$) and no family of nonempty sets is selected anywhere. This proves both parts. ∎ [given, step 3.2, L9, L14]
