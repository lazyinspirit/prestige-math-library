## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be perfect of characteristic $p>0$, let $K/k$ be
finitely generated, and let $L/K$ be a finitely generated extension of
transcendence degree one. Suppose $L\otimes_K\overline K$ is a domain
for an algebraic closure $\overline K/K$. For $e\ge0$ put
$$L_e=K L^{p^e}\subseteq L.$$
Then $L_e/K$ is finitely generated of transcendence degree one with the
same geometric tensor-domain property, and
$$[L_e:L_{e+1}]=p,\qquad [L:L_e]=p^e.$$
Each $L_e/L_{e+1}$ is purely inseparable of exponent one. The statement
includes $\operatorname{trdeg}_k K=0$; geometric integrality is encoded by
the explicit tensor-domain hypothesis, not merely by exact constants.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the fields, their finite generation and transcendence
degrees, and the geometric tensor-domain hypothesis.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A finitely generated field $F$ of transcendence degree $d$ over
perfect characteristic-$p$ constants has degree $[F:F^p]=p^d$, with the
restricted monomials of a separating transcendence basis as a basis.
Finite transcendence degrees add in towers.
[[lem-function-field-coordinate-derivations-and-constant-kernel]]
[[cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity]]

[F2] Pure inseparability is the elementwise $p$-power condition.
Finitely many algebraic generators give a finite extension, and finite
degrees multiply in towers.
[[thm-purely-inseparable-extension-characterizations]]
[[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]
[[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]

[F3] Under AC every vector space is flat, so tensoring with a field
extension preserves injections and exact sequences.
[[prop-modules-over-a-field-are-projective-flat-and-injective]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited from the flatness supplier, through the vector-space
basis used to preserve injections. The degree computations themselves use
only the finite bases in [F1].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; tensor with one layer of constant
$p$th roots and compare degrees through Frobenius.

1.1 Set $n=\operatorname{trdeg}_kK$. Then $\operatorname{trdeg}_kL=n+1$ by [F1], and $L/k$ is finitely generated. Consequently $[K:K^p]=p^n$ and $[L:L^p]=p^{n+1}$. Inside $\overline K$ let $K^{1/p}$ be the field of $p$th roots of elements of $K$. Frobenius is a field isomorphism from $K^{1/p}$ onto $K$, carrying its subfield $K$ onto $K^p$. Therefore $[K^{1/p}:K]=[K:K^p]=p^n$; equivalently take the $p$th roots of the restricted monomial basis in [F1]. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 The algebra $T=L\otimes_K K^{1/p}$ injects into the given domain $L\otimes_K\overline K$ by [F3]. It is a domain of dimension $p^n$ as an $L$-vector space, using the finite $K$-basis of $K^{1/p}$. It is therefore a field: multiplication by a nonzero element is an injective endomorphism of this finite vector space, hence surjective and has an inverse image of $1$. Identify $L$ and $K^{1/p}$ with their subfields in $T$. Frobenius on $T$ identifies its subfield of $p$th powers with $L^pK\subseteq L$, since $T$ is generated by $L$ and $K^{1/p}$ and their $p$th-power images are $L^p$ and $K$. It carries the embedded $L$ onto $L^p$. Thus $[K L^p:L^p]=[T^p:L^p]=[T:L]=p^n$. The tower formula [F2] with $L^p\subseteq K L^p\subseteq L$ gives $[L:K L^p]=p$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 Each $L_e$ is finitely generated over $K$ by the $p^e$th powers of a finite generating tuple for $L/K$. The extension $L/L_e$ is algebraic by the elementwise $p^e$-power condition, so its transcendence degree over $K$ remains one by [F1]. Moreover $L_e\otimes_K\overline K$ injects into $L\otimes_K\overline K$ by [F3], and hence is a domain. Apply step 2.1 with $L_e$ in place of $L$. Its next subfield is $K L_e^p=K L^{p^{e+1}}=L_{e+1}$, giving degree $p$. Every element's $p$th power lies in that next field, so the extension is purely inseparable of exponent one by [F2]. Multiplying the finitely many degree-$p$ steps gives $[L:L_e]=p^e$ by [F2], including $e=0$ as degree one. When $n=0$ the intermediate tensor field of step 2.1 is simply $L$, and the same calculation applies. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra, step 2.1] ∎
