## Statement

If $A$ is a commutative unital $k$-algebra, every linear map $f:V\to A$
extends uniquely to a unital algebra homomorphism $\widetilde f:S(V)\to A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A vector space $V$, a commutative unital $k$-algebra $A$, and a
linear map $f:V\to A$.

[L1] The tensor-algebra universal property extends $f$ uniquely to a unital
algebra map $\widehat f:T(V)\to A$
([[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]).

[L2] A ring homomorphism killing an ideal factors uniquely through the quotient
([[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[L3] $S(V)=T(V)/J$, where $J$ is generated by commutativity relators
([[def-symmetric-algebra-of-a-vector-space]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Apply [L1] to obtain $\widehat f$. For every generator of $J$, commutativity of $A$ gives $\widehat f(v\otimes w-w\otimes v)=f(v)f(w)-f(w)f(v)=0$, so $J\subseteq\ker\widehat f$. [L1, L3, algebra]

2.1 By [L2], $\widehat f$ factors through a unique unital algebra map $\widetilde f:S(V)\to A$, and its restriction to the image of $V$ is $f$. [step 1.1, L2]

3.1 If $G:S(V)\to A$ is another such map, its composite with $T(V)\twoheadrightarrow S(V)$ is a unital algebra extension of $f$, hence equals $\widehat f$ by [L1]. Surjectivity of the quotient map gives $G=\widetilde f$. [step 2.1, L1, L3, algebra]

4.1 Therefore $\widetilde f$ exists uniquely, with no basis or finite-dimensional hypothesis on $V$. [step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
