## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$, let $C$ be a smooth connected curve over $k$, and let $x$ be a closed point of $C$. Put $R=\mathcal O_{C,x}$, let $\mathfrak m_x$ be its maximal ideal, and define its adic completion by $\widehat R=\varprojlim_{n\geq1}R/\mathfrak m_x^n$. The local ring $R$ is a discrete valuation ring; choose a uniformizer $t_x$. The natural map $k\llbracket T\rrbracket\to\widehat R$ sending $T$ to $t_x$ is an isomorphism. Define the **geometric local field at $x$** to be $K_x=\operatorname{Frac}(\widehat R)\cong k((t_x))$, with normalized valuation $v_x(t_x)=1$. Its valuation ring is $\widehat R$ and its residue field is $k$. Replacing $t_x$ by another uniformizer does not change $v_x$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, smooth curve $C/k$, closed point $x$, and the completed local ring in the Definition.

[F1] [[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]]: under the Axiom of Choice, $\mathcal O_{C,x}$ is a DVR and its maximal ideal is generated by a uniformizer.

[F2] [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]]: a curve over $k$ is geometrically integral, so the structure map embeds $k$ into each local ring.

[F3] [[lem-finite-type-jacobson-residue-extension]]: the residue field at a closed point of a finite-type $k$-scheme is a finite extension of $k$. Since $k$ is algebraically closed, that residue field is $k$.

[F4] [[def-adic-completion-of-a-module]]: the completion of $R$ at $\mathfrak m_x$ is $\varprojlim_n R/\mathfrak m_x^n$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; this is the axiom assumed by [F1] to obtain the DVR local-ring result.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], $R$ is a DVR and $\mathfrak m_x=(t_x)$. By [F2] and [F3], $k$ embeds in $R$ and identifies with $R/\mathfrak m_x$. Using [F4], recursively subtract from each element of $\widehat R$ its unique residue coefficient in $k$, then divide the remainder by $t_x$. This constructs a unique power series in $t_x$ for each element of $\widehat R$; a nonzero series has valuation equal to the index of its first nonzero coefficient. Therefore $k\llbracket T\rrbracket\to\widehat R$, $T\mapsto t_x$, is an isomorphism, and $\widehat R$ is a complete DVR with residue field $k$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, A1, given, algebra]

2.1 The fraction field of a complete DVR is complete for the extended discrete valuation: a Cauchy sequence is bounded below in valuation, multiplication by a fixed power of $t_x$ moves its tail into $\widehat R$, and completeness of $\widehat R$ supplies the limit. If $t'_x$ is another uniformizer, then $t'_x=u t_x$ for a unit $u$, so both have valuation one and define the same normalized valuation. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
