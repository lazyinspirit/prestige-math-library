## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and $X$ an integral smooth projective $k$-scheme of finite type. Define $\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$ as the subgroup of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ generated by differences of fibres in connected finite-type families of line bundles, and set $\operatorname{NS}(X)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)/\operatorname{Pic}^0(X)$. Let $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$ be prime. Then $\operatorname{NS}(X)/\ell^r\operatorname{NS}(X)$ is finite for every $r\ge1$. More precisely, with $M=|H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\mu_\ell)|$, one has
$$\left|\operatorname{NS}(X)/\ell^r\operatorname{NS}(X)\right|\le M^r.$$
This statement does not assert finite generation of $\operatorname{NS}(X)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field, smooth projective scheme, algebraic-equivalence subgroup, prime and exponent in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Higher direct images of finite constructible torsion sheaves under proper morphisms of Noetherian schemes are finite constructible. [[lem-proper-torsion-constructibility-under-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use Kummer to embed Picard quotients into finite proper cohomology.

1.1 Let $n=\ell^r$. Since $\ell$ is invertible on $X$, the morphism $u\mapsto u^n$ on $\mathbb G_m$ is surjective as an étale sheaf: adjoining an $n$th root of a unit gives a finite étale cover because $nu^{n-1}$ is invertible. Its kernel is $\mu_n$, so the Kummer sequence is exact. The associated long exact cohomology sequence contains an injection $\operatorname{Pic}(X)/n\operatorname{Pic}(X)\hookrightarrow H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\mu_n)$, using the standard identification of $H^1(\mathbb G_m)$ with line bundles. [A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is proper and Noetherian, and $\mu_n$ is a finite locally constant $\mathbb Z/n$-module sheaf. By [F1], its second direct image is finite constructible on the algebraically closed point. A constructible sheaf on that point is constant with finite stalk, so $H^2(X,\mu_n)$ is finite. In particular $M$ is a positive finite integer. [A1, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $r>1$, the étale sheaf sequence $0\to\mu_{\ell^{r-1}}\to\mu_{\ell^r}\xrightarrow{(-)^{\ell^{r-1}}}\mu_\ell\to0$ is exact. Its cohomology sequence shows that $|H^2(X,\mu_{\ell^r})|$ is at most $|H^2(X,\mu_{\ell^{r-1}})|\,|H^2(X,\mu_\ell)|$. Induction gives $|H^2(X,\mu_{\ell^r})|\le M^r$, including $r=1$. The quotient map $\operatorname{Pic}(X)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{NS}(X)$ induces a surjection on quotients by $\ell^r$. Combining this with steps 1.1–2.1 gives the asserted finite bound. [A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Scope

The result gives finite quotients modulo each power of each prime away from the characteristic. It does not imply that $\operatorname{NS}(X)$ is finitely generated: for example, the additive group $\mathbb Q$ has zero quotient by every positive integer but is not finitely generated. The missing global finite-generation argument remains open.
