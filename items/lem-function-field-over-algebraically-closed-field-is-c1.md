## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, let
$C/k$ be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve with a $k$-rational
point $P$, and put $K=k(C)$. Then for every integer $d\ge1$, every integer
$n>d$, and every nonzero homogeneous polynomial
$F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ of degree $d$, there are
$f_1,\ldots,f_n\in K$, not all zero, such that $F(f_1,\ldots,f_n)=0$.
This property is called the $C_1$ property.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $k$, $C$, the $k$-point $P$, the field $K=k(C)$, and $F$,
$d$, and $n$ as in the Statement.

[A1] AC supplies the hypotheses of the full Riemann–Roch and algebraic
Nullstellensatz suppliers used below. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] For a divisor $D$ on $C$, $L(D)$ is the finite-dimensional space of
rational functions with poles bounded by $D$, and $l(D)=\dim_k L(D)$.
([[def-riemann-roch-space-of-divisor]], [[def-little-l-divisor]])

[F2] For every divisor $D$, $l(D)-l(K_C-D)=\deg(D)+1-g$.
([[thm-full-riemann-roch-divisor]])

[F3] A canonical divisor has degree $2g-2$.
([[cor-canonical-degree-two-g-minus-two]])

[F4] Over an algebraically closed field, if $m<N$, then $m$ homogeneous
polynomials of positive degree in $N$ variables (with zero equations omitted) have a common nonzero zero: otherwise the
Nullstellensatz makes their ideal have radical $(X_1,\ldots,X_N)$, of height
$N$, contradicting the height bound $m$ for an ideal generated by $m$
elements. ([[cor-strong-nullstellensatz-two-inclusions]],
[[thm-krull-height-theorem]])

[F5] Under AC, a divisor of negative degree on a smooth proper geometrically integral curve has $L(D)=0$ and $l(D)=0$. [[cor-negative-degree-no-sections-rr]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Riemann–Roch dimension count and the homogeneous
Nullstellensatz.

**Given:** The data and hypotheses above.

1.1 Choose an effective divisor $A$ containing the poles of all coefficients of $F$; there are finitely many coefficients, and each rational function on a proper smooth curve has finitely many poles. For a positive integer $e$, put $E=eP$. If $e>2g-2$ and $de+\deg(A)>2g-2$, [F2], [F3], and [F5] give $l(E)=e+1-g,\qquad l(dE+A)=de+\deg(A)+1-g,$ because the divisors $K_C-E$ and $K_C-dE-A$ have negative degree and hence no nonzero sections. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F5, given]

2.1 Since $n>d$, choose $e$ large enough that both inequalities of step 1.1 hold and $N:=n\,l(E)>m:=l(dE+A),$ which follows from $N-m=(n-d)e+(n-1)(1-g)-\deg(A)\longrightarrow+\infty.$ Choose bases of $L(E)$ and $L(dE+A)$. [step 1.1, given, choose, algebra]

3.1 Write each unknown $f_i$ as a linear combination of the $l(E)$ basis vectors of $L(E)$ with $N$ scalar variables. Since each coefficient of $F$ lies in $L(A)$, every value $F(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ lies in $L(dE+A)$. Equating its $m$ coordinates to zero gives $m$ homogeneous degree-$d$ polynomial equations in $N$ variables. [F1, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 By [F4] and $m<N$, this homogeneous system has a nonzero common solution in $k^N$. The chosen basis expansions are injective, so the resulting functions $f_i\in L(E)\subset K$ are not all zero; their substitution makes all $m$ coordinates, and therefore $F(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$, zero. [F4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The construction applies to every $d,n,F$ in the Statement and supplies a nontrivial zero, which is exactly the $C_1$ property. AC is used only through [A1] and the cited Riemann–Roch and Nullstellensatz suppliers. [A1, step 4.1] ∎
