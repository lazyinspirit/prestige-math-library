## Statement

Let $N\trianglelefteq G$ and $N\le H\le G$. Then
$$H/N\le Z(G/N)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad [G,H]\le N.$$
Consequently a normal series $1=H_0\le H_1\le\cdots\le H_c=G$ is central, meaning $H_{i+1}/H_i\le Z(G/H_i)$, exactly when $[G,H_{i+1}]\le H_i$ for every $0\le i<c$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A normal subgroup $N\trianglelefteq G$ and a subgroup $H$ containing $N$.

[F1] $[G,H]$ is generated by all $[g,h]$ with $g\in G$ and $h\in H$ ([[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]]).

[F2] An element is central exactly when it commutes with every element of the group ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F3] Multiplication in $G/N$ is $(gN)(hN)=ghN$ ([[def-quotient-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Suppose $H/N\le Z(G/N)$. For every $g\in G$ and $h\in H$, the cosets $gN$ and $hN$ commute by [F2]. Expanding their products with [F3] gives $ghN=hgN$, equivalently $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\in N$; hence [F1] gives $[G,H]\le N$. [assume-hyp, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Conversely, suppose $[G,H]\le N$. Then [F1] gives $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}\in N$ for all $g\in G,h\in H$. Reversing the coset calculation in step 1.1 shows $gN$ and $hN$ commute, so $H/N\le Z(G/N)$ by [F2]. [assume-hyp, step 1.1, F1, F2, F3]

3.1 Applying the equivalence of steps 1.1 and 2.1 with $(N,H)=(H_i,H_{i+1})$ for each adjacent pair proves the series criterion, including $H_0=1$ and $H_c=G$. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
