## Statement

Assume AC and DC.

Let $F=\mathbb F_q(C)$ be a one-variable function field whose exact constant
field is $\mathbb F_q$, with $C/\mathbb F_q$ smooth, proper, and geometrically
integral. The greatest common divisor of the residue degrees
$[\kappa(v):\mathbb F_q]$ over all closed points $v$ of $C$ is one.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The curve and constant field in the Statement.

[A1] AC and DC are the choice assumptions of the degree-one Picard and
divisor suppliers. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]])

[F1] There is a line bundle $\mathcal L$ on $C$ with $\deg(\mathcal L)=1$.
([[thm-picard-degree-one-line-bundle-over-finite-field]])

[F2] A line bundle on a smooth integral curve has a nonzero rational section;
its Cartier divisor has associated Weil divisor of the same degree as the
line bundle. ([[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]],
[[cor-degree-descends-picard-curve]],
[[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement and Facts & Assumptions.

1.1 Choose a nonzero rational section of $\mathcal L$ by [F2]. Its divisor is
a finite integral combination $D=\sum_v n_v[v]$ of closed points, and
$\deg(D)=1$ by [F1] and [F2]. [A1, F1, F2, choose]

1.2 By the definition of divisor degree,
$1=\sum_v n_v[\kappa(v):\mathbb F_q]$. Hence the subgroup of $\mathbb Z$
generated by all closed-point degrees contains $1$, so its positive
generator, their greatest common divisor, is $1$. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
