## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. The following explicitly constructed compact oriented smooth surfaces admit finite smooth triangle data, with any specified finite set of boundary points as vertices:

1. A finite disjoint union of smoothly parametrized planar closed disk-with-holes regions, glued in finitely many pairs of boundary circles by specified smooth circle diffeomorphisms and compatible signed product collars, whenever the resulting surface is oriented. One may leave some circles unglued. A round sphere with finitely many round disk interiors removed is included by placing a stereographic pole in one removed disk.
2. A surface obtained from the first construction by deleting finitely many additional smoothly parametrized disks with closures inside the planar-piece interiors and gluing in specified smoothly parametrized annuli along some of their boundary circles, with compatible product collars. Before this operation one may separate finitely many closed subcollars of the original seams as explicit annular pieces.
3. The actual compact positive branched disk covers supplied by the finite-transposition construction, including the degree-one disk case.

Here finite smooth triangle data means finitely many face-to-face embedded triangular disks, each parametrized from a Euclidean closed triangle by a smooth map with nonsingular derivative in a surface chart on a neighborhood of that triangle. Interior vertex links are circles and boundary links are intervals. Parametrizations of the two occurrences of a common edge may differ by an increasing smooth interval map; the image cellulation has a compatible finite abstract simplicial model after finite subdivision. These are precisely the supplied smooth face and incidence data needed by the finite tangent-field index and finite smooth surface matching arguments.

No triangulation or smoothing result for an arbitrary topological surface or an arbitrary prescribed tangential graph is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite actual planar pieces, smooth attaching maps and product collars, optional additional disk and annulus parametrizations, or the actual finite branched-cover construction, and the finite marked boundary points.

[F1] A smooth planar disk-with-holes region admits an actual diffeomorphism to a chosen round-hole model with assigned boundary maps and collar germs under Countable Choice. Smooth disk boundary maps extend over a disk with prescribed collar. ([[lem-finite-planar-hole-boundary-maps-extend-with-prescribed-collars]], [[lem-smooth-jordan-disk-boundary-maps-extend-to-diffeomorphisms]])

[F2] The finite-transposition cover is an actual compact smooth surface. Its branch values are finite and distinct, with local charts $z=q+w^2$ at a simple ramification point and one-sheet coordinate charts at all other preimages. It is a finite unramified covering away from those values, including along its boundary. ([[lem-finite-transposition-data-realize-branched-disk-covers]])

## Proof

1.1 First construct the smooth data on a bounded round planar region. Around its outer circle choose a sufficiently close inscribed polygon, and around each inner circle a sufficiently close circumscribed polygon. All remain disjoint and correctly nested. To arrange finitely many prescribed radial directions, choose a finite angular mesh with successive gaps less than a sufficiently small positive number and containing those directions. Put polygon vertices on one concentric circle of radius $R$ for each boundary component. For an inner circle of radius $r$, require $R\cos(\delta/2)>r$, where $\delta$ is the largest mesh gap; for the outer circle take $R<r$. Thus the polygons and circle collars have positive separation and the polygonal core has the intended holes. Between successive radial directions its boundary collar is one curvilinear quadrilateral. Write $v(s)=(1-s)v_0+sv_1$, $0\le s\le1$, for the intervening polygon side, and choose its smooth argument $\theta(s)$. Since the angular gap is less than $\pi$, $\theta'(s)>0$ and $|v(s)|>0$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 On an outer collar use $$\Phi(s,t)=\big((1-t)|v(s)|+tr\big)e^{i\theta(s)},\qquad 0\le s,t\le1,$$ and on an inner collar interchange the two radii. These maps are smooth with nonzero Jacobian on a neighborhood of their closed parameter rectangles: the angular derivative is positive and the radial derivative has one fixed nonzero sign. Orient the parameter triangles so that their maps preserve the surface orientation, reversing their vertex order when necessary. The polygon side is parametrized AFFINELY by $v(s)$, and the circle side is its actual smooth circular arc. Split the rectangle by a diagonal. Each resulting triangle has the required smooth nonsingular parametrization. At the radial sides adjacent collar sectors use the same affine interpolation between the same circle and polygon vertices, so their image edges match. Every prescribed boundary point on an initial radial direction is a vertex. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Triangulate the remaining polygonal core explicitly, including its holes. Choose a direction with distinct projections of its finitely many vertices. In every slab between successive projections, the boundary pieces are ordered affine graphs; membership alternates at each boundary crossing. The portions of the core in a slab are therefore finitely many convex triangles or quadrilaterals. Subdivide their common walls at all incident vertices and intersections, and fan each convex piece from an interior point. This gives a finite face-to-face affine triangulation of the core. If a core edge was subdivided, subdivide the adjacent collar rectangle at the corresponding $s$ values and fan its smaller rectangles as in step2.1. Thus all images meet face-to-face. Finitely many triangles meet each point and fill its whole disk or half-disk neighborhood; their vertex links are consequently circles or intervals. By [F1] pull this actual finite smooth construction back to each supplied smooth planar piece, with all its prescribed boundary vertices and collars. This is a smooth pullback, not the pullback of a polygonal cellulation by an arbitrary homeomorphism. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For the finite gluings, first insert in every paired circle the union of both prescribed finite vertex sets, transported by its circle map. Triangulate each piece by step3.1 with those vertices. The two image edges on a paired circle are then the same actual arcs after identification. Their signed product collars make every face parametrization extend smoothly through that seam as a map into the resulting surface chart. Its derivative stays nonsingular there, since the original one-sided collar map extended diffeomorphically across zero. Joining the two boundary half-links makes a circular interior link. The unpaired boundary links stay intervals. This proves clause1. Additional deleted parametrized disks leave finite planar pieces with additional smooth disk holes; apply [F1] again to those pieces. An inserted parametrized annulus is itself a disk-with-one-hole model. Apply the same paired-collar construction to obtain clause2. To use a neighborhood of an original seam, first peel a thin closed signed subcollar around its entire circle from the neighboring pieces and regard that subcollar as one explicit annular piece. The remaining pieces are still planar regions with smooth boundary circles, and the new annulus joins them by its two product rims. A disk around a proper subarc of that seam can now have its whole closure in this annulus interior. Only this explicit finite collar refinement is used; arbitrary intersections of a new disk boundary with the old seams are not presumed finite. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For clause3 choose disjoint small round disks around the finitely many branch values, wholly inside the base disk. Make them small enough that [F2]'s local branch charts cover every preimage: compactness excludes extra remote preimages approaching a value, and the finite local charts at that fiber give its complete preimage over a smaller disk. Above such a disk are ordinary coordinate disks and, at its one ramified pair, a disk in the coordinate $w$ with projection $q+w^2$. On the complement the cover is unramified. Triangulate that round disk-with-holes by steps1.1–3.1, and lift every closed triangle to its finitely many sheets. A triangle has a simply connected small neighborhood avoiding the branch values; finite continuation of the explicit local sheet inverses supplies one smooth inverse branch on each lifted triangle neighborhood. Equivalently, follow its sheet labels across the finitely many cut banks in [F2]'s construction; no branch point is left there. Thus each lifted face has a smooth nonsingular parametrization in the source coordinate given by that inverse branch, up to its circle boundary. Fill each removed source coordinate disk by steps1.1–3.1 with the same finite boundary vertices. The circle image edges then match those of the lifted complement; their two parametrizations may differ but both are regular increasing parameters on that same smooth arc. Product collars from the actual covering charts give the required smooth seam extension. The degree-one case is simply the disk construction. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Finally convert the finite image incidence data to an abstract simplicial model if necessary. Subdivide each image edge with its own new point, and fan each face from its own interior point, making corresponding subdivisions in the incident Euclidean triangle parameters. A desired point on an image edge has a unique parameter on either of its two regular parametrizations, so the subdivisions match as images. Distinct vertex-edge-face flags now give distinct small triangles; any two intersect in exactly their common flags. Their vertex sets therefore form a finite abstract simplicial complex. Prescribe one common parameter on every image edge and extend each resulting face-boundary homeomorphism over its reference triangle by radial extension; this gives a homeomorphism from the abstract realization to the actual surface. Only this last ABSTRACT realization map is asserted to be topological. The actual smooth triangle parametrizations already constructed in the preceding steps remain available independently on every image face, so no differentiability of that radial extension is needed. All constructions use finite charts, meshes, sheet labels and collars, besides [F1]'s stated Countable Choice. This proves all three clauses with actual smooth face data and no arbitrary-surface smoothing inference. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
