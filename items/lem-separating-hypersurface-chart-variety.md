## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X$ be an
irreducible classical variety over $k$ of dimension $d$. There is a nonconstant
irreducible polynomial
$$P(T_1,\ldots,T_d,Z)\in k[T_1,\ldots,T_d,Z]$$
such that the hypersurface $H=V(P)\subseteq\mathbf A_k^{d+1}$ is irreducible,
$\partial P/\partial Z\ne0$, and $X$ and $H$ contain isomorphic nonempty open
subvarieties. In particular, $\dim H=d$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; an algebraically closed field $k$; and an
irreducible classical variety $X$ of dimension $d$ over $k$.

[F1] The Axiom of Choice says every family of nonempty sets has a choice
function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] Every algebraically closed field is perfect ([[cor-fields-of-characteristic-zero-and-finite-fields-are-perfect]]); every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root ([[def-algebraically-closed-field]]).

[F3] A classical variety has a finite affine-model cover; for an irreducible
variety its compatible affine atlas makes it an integral classical variety.
Thus a nonempty affine chart $U$ exists ([[def-classical-algebraic-prevariety-regular-maps-and-varieties]], [[def-classical-integral-affine-atlas-and-chartwise-morphism]]).

[F4] For an affine algebraic set $U$, its coordinate ring is the quotient of a
finite-variable polynomial ring by its vanishing ideal
([[def-coordinate-ring-affine-algebraic-set]]). In particular it is a
finitely generated $k$-algebra.

[F5] The fraction fields of the nonempty affine charts of an integral classical
variety identify canonically as $k(X)$
([[lem-general-variety-function-field-charts]]).

[F6] If $X$ is irreducible classical, then
$\dim X=\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(X)<\infty$
([[thm-dimension-equals-transcendence-degree]]).

[F7] A finitely generated field extension of a perfect field has a separating
transcendence basis: for some algebraically independent
$t_1,\ldots,t_r$, the extension over $k(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ is finite separable
([[thm-ag-separating-transcendence-basis-perfect-field]]).

[F8] Every finite separable field extension is generated by one element
([[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]).

[F9] The minimal polynomial of an algebraic element is monic irreducible and
generates the kernel of its evaluation map
([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[F10] An element of a field extension is separable when its minimal polynomial
is separable; an extension is separable when every element is
([[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]]).

[F11] For an irreducible polynomial $m$ over a field $L$, the quotient
$L[Z]/(m)$ is a field ([[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]).

[F12] An irreducible polynomial over a field is separable exactly when its
formal derivative is nonzero
([[cor-irreducible-polynomial-is-separable-iff-derivative-nonzero]]).

[F13] Every finite-variable polynomial ring over a field is a UFD, and its
irreducible elements are prime
([[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-rings-over-fields-are-ufds]]).

[F14] Over a UFD, a primitive positive-degree polynomial is irreducible exactly
when it is irreducible over the fraction field ([[lem-gauss-lemma-over-a-ufd]]).

[F15] Over algebraically closed $k$ and under AC, irreducible affine algebraic
sets correspond to proper prime ideals, and $I(V(J))=\sqrt J$
([[thm-classical-affine-nullstellensatz-correspondence]]).

[F16] For an affine classical variety $Y$, its function field is
$k(Y)=\operatorname{Frac}(k[Y])$
([[def-classical-variety-function-field]]).

[F17] Under AC, two integral classical varieties over algebraically closed $k$ are birationally equivalent exactly when their function fields are $k$-isomorphic ([[thm-classical-birational-equivalence-iff-function-fields-isomorphic]]).

[F18] For integral classical varieties with compatible affine atlases, birational equivalence means that they have isomorphic nonempty open subvarieties ([[def-classical-birational-equivalence]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [F3], choose a nonempty affine chart $U\subseteq X$. Its coordinate ring is finite type by [F4], so its fraction field is finitely generated over $k$; by [F5] this field is $K:=k(X)$. Equation [F6] gives $\operatorname{trdeg}_k K=d$. [F3, F4, F5, F6, choose]

2.1 By [F2] and [F7], choose a separating transcendence basis $t_1,\ldots,t_d$ of length $d$ for $K/k$; its residual extension $K/L$, for $L=k(t_1,\ldots,t_d)$, is finite separable. Here $A=k[T_1,\ldots,T_d]$ has fraction field $L$ under $T_i\mapsto t_i$. If $d=0$, then $K/k$ is finite and every element has an irreducible minimal polynomial over the algebraically closed field $k$; [F2] and [F9] force each such polynomial to be linear, so $K=k$ and we may take $\gamma=0$. In general, [F8] gives $\gamma\in K$ with $K=L(\gamma)$; when $K=L$, again take $\gamma=0$. [F2, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, choose]

3.1 Let $m(Z)\in L[Z]$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $\gamma$. It is irreducible by [F9] and separable by [F10], since $K/L$ is separable. Hence $m'(Z)\ne0$ by [F12]. For the trivial extension this is $m(Z)=Z$, so the same derivative conclusion holds. Also [F9] and [F11] identify $L[Z]/(m)$ with the field $L(\gamma)=K$. [F9, F10, F11, F12, step 2.1]

4.1 Clear the finitely many coefficient denominators of $m$ with a nonzero $q\in A$, obtaining $Q=q m\in A[Z]$. In the UFD $A$, factor the common irreducible divisors of the finitely many coefficients of $Q$ to write $Q=cP$, where $c\in A\setminus\{0\}$ is their common content and $P$ is primitive. The polynomial $P$ has positive $Z$-degree and is an associate of $m$ over $L$. Thus it is irreducible in $L[Z]$, and [F14] makes it irreducible in $A[Z]$. Since $q/c\in L^\times$ is independent of $Z$, $P=(q/c)m$ in $L[Z]$ and $P_Z=(q/c)m'\ne0$. [F13, F14, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Put $H=V(P)\subseteq\mathbf A_k^{d+1}$. By [F13] the irreducible polynomial $P$ is prime in $A[Z]$; it is a nonunit because it has positive $Z$-degree. Therefore $(P)$ is a proper prime ideal. By [F15], $H$ is nonempty and irreducible and $I(H)=\sqrt{(P)}=(P)$. Thus [F4] gives $k[H]=A[Z]/(P)$, a domain, and [F16] gives $k(H)=\operatorname{Frac}(A[Z]/(P))$. Localizing this coordinate ring at the nonzero elements of $A$ yields $L[Z]/(P)\cong L[Z]/(m)\cong L(\gamma)=K$. Here $A$ embeds in $A[Z]/(P)$ because $P$ has positive $Z$-degree, and the localized quotient is a field by [F11]; hence it is the fraction field of $A[Z]/(P)$. Consequently $k(H)\cong k(X)$ over $k$. [F4, F11, F13, F15, F16, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Both $X$ and $H$ are integral classical varieties by their hypotheses and step 5.1. Their function fields are $k$-isomorphic, so [F17] and [F18] supply isomorphic nonempty open subvarieties. By [F6], $\dim H=\operatorname{trdeg}_k k(H)=d$. Step 4.1 gives the required nonzero last-coordinate partial derivative, and the construction makes $H$ an irreducible hypersurface in $\mathbf A_k^{d+1}$. AC is propagated through the integral-atlas, chart-function-field, dimension, Nullstellensatz, affine-function-field and birational interfaces [F3, F5, F6, F15, F16, F17]; once these apply, the construction uses only the finite chart, basis, generator and denominator/content selections above. [F1, F3, F5, F6, F15, F16, F17, F18, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎

## Source note

Milne, *Algebraic Geometry* v6.10, §3k Proposition 3.36 and Theorem 3.37,
printed p. 74, reduces birationality of affine varieties to isomorphic
nonempty affine opens and constructs a birational hypersurface from a
$d$-dimensional function field generated by $d+1$ elements. Proposition 3.38,
printed pp. 74–75, supplies the separable last generator over a perfect base;
§4h, proof of Theorem 4.37, printed p. 95, uses the resulting hypersurface
chart and a nonvanishing partial derivative. The present proof obtains the
specific last-coordinate derivative directly: a primitive generator over a
separating transcendence basis has separable minimal polynomial, and clearing
denominators and removing content multiplies it only by a nonzero scalar in
$k(T_1,\ldots,T_d)$, so that derivative stays nonzero.
