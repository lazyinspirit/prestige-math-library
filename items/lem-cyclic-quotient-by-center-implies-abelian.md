## Statement

If the quotient group $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is abelian.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group $G$ such that $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic.

[L1] The center $Z(G)$ consists of the elements commuting with every element of $G$ ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[L2] The center is a normal subgroup of $G$ ([[lem-center-is-normal]]).

[L3] Multiplication in $G/Z(G)$ is $(gZ(G))(hZ(G))=ghZ(G)$ ([[def-quotient-group]]).

[L4] A cyclic group is generated by one element ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[L5] The subgroup generated by an element is the set of its integer powers ([[lem-cyclic-subgroup-is-the-set-of-powers]]).

[L6] Powers of one group element commute with one another ([[lem-group-power-laws]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose $aZ(G)$ generating $G/Z(G)$. By [L3], [L4], and [L5], arbitrary $x,y\in G$ have forms $x=a^m z$ and $y=a^n w$ for some integers $m,n$ and central elements $z,w\in Z(G)$. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, choose]

1.2 The elements $z,w$ commute with every element by [L1], and $a^m$ commutes with $a^n$ by [L6]. [L1, L6]

2.1 Therefore $xy=a^mza^nw=a^{m+n}zw=a^{n+m}wz=a^nwa^mz=yx$. Since $x,y$ were arbitrary, $G$ is abelian. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L6, algebra] ∎
