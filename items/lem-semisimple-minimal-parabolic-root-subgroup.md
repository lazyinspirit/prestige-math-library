## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be the connected simply connected complex
semisimple affine algebraic group with maximal torus $T$, root system $\Phi$,
simple roots $\Delta$ and positive system $\Phi^+$ of
[[def-complex-semisimple-algebraic-group-borel-and-flag-variety]], and let
$B=T\ltimes U$ be its Borel subgroup as in
[[lem-semisimple-borel-root-factorization]]. Fix a simple root
$\alpha\in\Delta$, let $U_{-\alpha}$ be the negative root subgroup, let
$\varphi_\alpha:SL_2(\mathbb C)\to G$ and
$n_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(w)$ be the rank-one homomorphism and Weyl
representative of [[lem-semisimple-rank-one-sl2-root-homomorphism]], and let
$P_\alpha$ be the subgroup generated by $B$ and $U_{-\alpha}$. Then:

(i) $P_\alpha$ is a closed connected algebraic subgroup of $G$ with
$$P_\alpha=B\sqcup Bn_\alpha B,$$
the two double cosets being disjoint;

(ii) $\operatorname{Lie}P_\alpha=\mathfrak b\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ and
$\dim P_\alpha=\dim B+1$;

(iii) the coset space $P_\alpha/B$ is $\mathbb P^1$: it is covered by the two
affine charts $z\mapsto u_{-\alpha}(z)B$ and $t\mapsto u_\alpha(t)n_\alpha B$,
each isomorphic to $\mathbb A^1$ and glued by $t=z^{-1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the group $G$, its maximal torus $T$, the simple root $\alpha$, the root subgroups $U_{\pm\alpha}$ with the morphism $\varphi_\alpha:SL_2\to G$ of [F1], and the Borel $B=T\ltimes U$ of [F2].

[F1] $\varphi_\alpha:SL_2(\mathbb C)\to G$ is a morphism of algebraic groups with differential sending the standard basis to $(e_\alpha,f_\alpha,h_\alpha)$, with $\varphi_\alpha\begin{pmatrix}1&z\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=u_\alpha(z)$, $\varphi_\alpha\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\z&1\end{pmatrix}=u_{-\alpha}(z)$, kernel contained in $\{\pm I\}$, and $\varphi_\alpha(diag(u,u^{-1}))=\alpha^\vee(u)\in T$. ([[lem-semisimple-rank-one-sl2-root-homomorphism]])

[F2] $T$ normalizes $U$ and $U^-$, $T\cap U=1$, $B=T\cdot U=T\ltimes U$ is a closed connected solvable subgroup, and $U=\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^+}U_\beta$ in every height-compatible order, with the corresponding product map an isomorphism of varieties onto $U$; the same holds for $U^-$. ([[lem-semisimple-borel-root-factorization]])

[F3] For every root $\beta$ and every nonzero $e_\beta\in\mathfrak g_\beta$ the curve $z\mapsto\exp_G(ze_\beta)$ is a morphism onto the closed one-dimensional subgroup $U_\beta$ with $\operatorname{Lie}U_\beta=\mathfrak g_\beta$, and $U_\beta$ depends only on $\beta$, not on the root vector. ([[lem-semisimple-root-exponential-algebraic-subgroups]])

[F4] $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $\mathfrak h=\operatorname{Lie}T$ and $\mathfrak n^+=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\mathfrak g_\alpha$, and for $\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$ the root space $\mathfrak g_\lambda$ consists of the $x$ with $[H,x]=\lambda(H)x$ for all $H\in\mathfrak h$. ([[def-complex-semisimple-algebraic-group-borel-and-flag-variety]], [[def-root-and-root-space-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]])

[F5] For every root $\beta$ the reflected functional $s_\alpha(\beta)$ of [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]] is again a root. ([[thm-root-reflections-preserve-the-root-set]])

[F6] $\Delta$ is a basis of the positive system $\Phi^+$, the root system is reduced, and every positive root is a sum of simple roots with nonnegative integer coefficients, so every root has simple-root coordinates of one sign. ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]], [[def-complex-semisimple-algebraic-group-borel-and-flag-variety]])

[F7] Every root space $\mathfrak g_\gamma$ of a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra is one-dimensional. ([[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]])

[F8] For $g\in G$ conjugation $C_g(h)=ghg^{-1}$ is an automorphism of Lie groups with $d(C_g)_e=\operatorname{Ad}_g$; the differential of a Lie group homomorphism is a Lie algebra homomorphism, and $C_g$ is invertible, so $\operatorname{Ad}_g$ is an automorphism of $\mathfrak g$; moreover $\operatorname{Ad}_{\exp_GX}=e^{\operatorname{ad}_X}$ for every $X\in\mathfrak g$. ([[def-conjugation-and-the-adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-group]], [[thm-differential-of-a-lie-group-homomorphism-is-a-lie-algebra-homomorphism]], [[prop-adjoint-exponential-identity]])

[F9] If $F:G\to H$ is a homomorphism of finite-dimensional real Lie groups, then $F(\exp_GX)=\exp_H(dF_eX)$ for every $X\in\operatorname{Lie}G$. ([[prop-exponential-map-is-natural-for-lie-group-homomorphisms]])

[F10] Every morphism of classical varieties over an algebraically closed field sends constructible subsets to constructible subsets, and for an irreducible classical variety $X$ and a morphism $f:X\to Y$ the closure $\overline{f(X)}$ is irreducible with $\dim X=\dim\overline{f(X)}+r$, where $r$ is the common dimension of the nonempty fibres over a nonempty open subset. ([[thm-chevalley-constructible-image-varieties]], [[cor-dimension-of-image-plus-generic-fibre]])

[F11] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it supplies countable choice for [F8] and [F9], and the full AC hypotheses of [F1]–[F3], [F5], [F7] and [F10].

[F12] $n_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(w)$ lies in $N_G(T)$, acts on $\mathfrak h$ by the involutive reflection $s_\alpha$, and $n_\alpha^2=\varphi_\alpha(-I)$ lies in $T$ and acts trivially on $T$. ([[lem-semisimple-rank-one-sl2-root-homomorphism]])

## Proof

1.1 The Gauss decomposition $SL_2=B_2\sqcup B_2wB_2$ holds, where $B_2$ is the upper triangular subgroup of determinant one and $w=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$: a matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ with $c=0$ lies in $B_2$, and one with $c\neq0$ equals $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}c^{-1}&a\\0&c\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&c^{-1}d\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Since $\varphi_\alpha$ maps the diagonal torus $D$ onto $\alpha^\vee(\mathbb C^\times)$ and the standard unipotent subgroups onto $U_{\pm\alpha}$ by [F1], it maps $B_2=D\ltimes U_2$ onto $B_\alpha:=T_\alpha U_\alpha\subseteq B$ with $T_\alpha=\alpha^\vee(\mathbb C^\times)$ and $U_\alpha\subseteq U$ by [F2]; hence $V_\alpha:=\varphi_\alpha(SL_2)=B_\alpha\sqcup B_\alpha n_\alpha B_\alpha$, and $V_\alpha\subseteq B\cup Bn_\alpha B$ because $B_\alpha\subseteq B$ and $B_\alpha n_\alpha B_\alpha\subseteq Bn_\alpha B$. In particular $U_{-\alpha}\subseteq B\cup Bn_\alpha B$ and $w\notin B_2$. [F1, F2]

1.2 For every root $\beta$ one has $\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)\mathfrak g_\beta=\mathfrak g_{s_\alpha(\beta)}$. Indeed by [F8] $\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)$ is a Lie algebra automorphism, so for $x\in\mathfrak g_\beta$ and $H\in\mathfrak h$ one has $[H,\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)x]=\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)[\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)^{-1}H,x]=\beta(\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)^{-1}H)\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)x=(s_\alpha\beta)(H)\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)x$, because $\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)$ preserves $\mathfrak h$ and acts there as the involutive reflection $s_\alpha$, so that $\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)^{-1}H=s_\alpha H$, and because the dual reflection satisfies $(s_\alpha\beta)(H)=\beta(s_\alpha H)$. By [F5] the functional $s_\alpha(\beta)$ is a root, so by [F4] and [F7] the target space $\mathfrak g_{s_\alpha(\beta)}$ is the one-dimensional root space of that functional; as $\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)$ is invertible and $\mathfrak g_\beta\ne0$, the image is all of it. [F1, F4, F5, F7, F8, F12]

1.3 For every $\beta\in\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$ one has $s_\alpha(\beta)\in\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$. Write $\beta=\sum_{\gamma\in\Delta}n_\gamma\gamma$ with $n_\gamma\ge0$ integers, using that $\beta$ is positive and [F6]; as $\beta\ne\alpha$ and the root system is reduced, some $n_\gamma$ with $\gamma\ne\alpha$ is nonzero. The reflected functional $s_\alpha(\beta)=\beta-\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ has the same coefficient $n_\gamma$ at every simple root $\gamma\ne\alpha$, hence has the positive coefficient $n_\gamma>0$ at $\gamma$ and is a root, so all its simple coefficients are nonnegative and it is a positive root; it is different from $\alpha$, whose coefficient at $\gamma$ is $0$. [F5, F6]

1.4 The two double cosets $B$ and $Bn_\alpha B$ are disjoint. It suffices to show $n_\alpha\notin B$, since $B\cap Bn_\alpha B\ne\emptyset$ would give $n_\alpha\in B$. Suppose $n_\alpha=tu$ with $t\in T$ and $u\in U$, using the decomposition $B=T\ltimes U$ of [F2]. Then $\operatorname{Ad}(u)|_{\mathfrak h}=\operatorname{Ad}(t)^{-1}\operatorname{Ad}(n_\alpha)|_{\mathfrak h}=s_\alpha$, because $t$ acts trivially on $\mathfrak h$; but writing $u=u_{\beta_1}(z_1)\cdots u_{\beta_m}(z_m)$ in the height-compatible order of [F2] and using $\operatorname{Ad}(u_\beta(z))=e^{z\operatorname{ad}_{e_\beta}}$ from [F8], each factor maps $\mathfrak h$ into $\mathfrak h+\mathfrak n^+$: indeed $[e_\beta,H]=-\beta(H)e_\beta\in\mathfrak n^+$ and $\mathfrak n^+$ is a Lie subalgebra, so every term $(\operatorname{ad}_{e_\beta})^kH$ with $k\ge1$ lies in $\mathfrak n^+$. A composition of maps of the form $\mathrm{id}+N$ with $N$ mapping $\mathfrak h$ into $\mathfrak n^+$ again maps $\mathfrak h$ into $\mathfrak h+\mathfrak n^+$ and induces the identity on the $\mathfrak h$-component, so $(\operatorname{Ad}(u)-\mathrm{id})(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak n^+$; comparing with $s_\alpha$ gives $(s_\alpha-\mathrm{id})(\mathfrak h)\subseteq\mathfrak h\cap\mathfrak n^+=0$ by [F4], hence $s_\alpha=\mathrm{id}$ on $\mathfrak h$, contradicting $s_\alpha(h_\alpha)=-h_\alpha\ne h_\alpha$. Therefore $n_\alpha\notin B$. [F1, F2, F4, F8, F12]

2.1 Fix a height-compatible order of $\Phi^+$ with $\alpha$ first and write $U''=\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}}U_\beta$ in the induced order, so that $U=U_\alpha\cdot U''$ by [F2]. For $u\in U$, written as $u=u_\alpha(z)u''$ with $u''\in U''$, one has $n_\alpha un_\alpha=C_{n_\alpha}(u)\,n_\alpha^2$ with $n_\alpha^2\in T$ by [F1]; by [F9] applied to the automorphism $C_{n_\alpha}$ of [F8] and the parametrization $u_\beta(s)=\exp_G(se_\beta)$ of [F3], step 1.2 gives $C_{n_\alpha}(u_\alpha(z))\in U_{-\alpha}$ and $C_{n_\alpha}(u_\beta)\subseteq U_{s_\alpha\beta}$ for each factor $u_\beta$ of $u''$, so by step 1.3 the element $C_{n_\alpha}(u'')$ is a product of elements of the subgroups $U_{s_\alpha\beta}\subseteq U$ and therefore lies in $U$; hence $n_\alpha Un_\alpha\subseteq U_{-\alpha}\cdot U\cdot T\subseteq U_{-\alpha}\cdot B$. [F1, F2, F3, F8, F9, F12, step 1.2, step 1.3]

2.2 Moreover $B\cap V_\alpha=B_\alpha$. The preimage $H:=\varphi_\alpha^{-1}(B)$ is a closed subgroup of $SL_2$ containing $B_2$, and $H\ne SL_2$ since $n_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(w)\notin B$ by step 1.4. If $H$ contained an element $g\notin B_2$, then $g\in B_2wB_2$ by step 1.1, so $w\in B_2gB_2\subseteq H$ and $H$ contains $\langle B_2,w\rangle=SL_2$, since every element of $B_2wB_2$ is a product of two elements of $B_2$ and one $w$ and $SL_2=B_2\cup B_2wB_2$; this contradicts $H\ne SL_2$, so $H=B_2$ and $B\cap V_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(B_2)=B_\alpha$. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.4]

3.1 Consequently $n_\alpha Bn_\alpha=(n_\alpha Tn_\alpha)(n_\alpha Un_\alpha)=T\cdot n_\alpha Un_\alpha\subseteq T\cdot U_{-\alpha}\cdot B\subseteq B\cdot U_{-\alpha}\cdot B\subseteq B\cdot(B\cup Bn_\alpha B)\cdot B=B\cup Bn_\alpha B$, where the middle inclusion uses step 2.1, the next uses $U_{-\alpha}\subseteq B\cup Bn_\alpha B$ from step 1.1, and the last uses $B\cdot B\subseteq B$ and $B\cdot Bn_\alpha B\cdot B=Bn_\alpha B$. [F1, F12, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 The simple double coset has a one-root chart: $Bn_\alpha B=U_\alpha n_\alpha B$. Indeed $B=TU_\alpha U''$ by [F2] and step 2.1, while $n_\alpha^{-1}U''n_\alpha\subseteq U\subseteq B$ by steps 1.2–1.3, so the $U''$ factor moves through $n_\alpha$ into the right $B$ factor and $T$ is absorbed there. The multiplication $U_\alpha\times B\to Bn_\alpha B$, $(u,b)\mapsto un_\alpha b$, has singleton complex-point fibres: equality of two outputs would put a nontrivial element of $U_\alpha$ in $n_\alpha Bn_\alpha^{-1}$, whereas conjugation by $n_\alpha^{-1}$ sends it into $U_{-\alpha}\cap B=1$ by step 2.2. Thus the image is irreducible of dimension $\dim B+1$ by the fibre-dimension formula [F10]. [F2, F10, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Define $Q=B\cup Bn_\alpha B$. Then $Q\cdot Q\subseteq Q$: one has $B\cdot B\subseteq B$ and $B\cdot Bn_\alpha B\cdot B\subseteq Bn_\alpha B$, so the only non-formal product is $(Bn_\alpha B)(Bn_\alpha B)=B(n_\alpha Bn_\alpha)B\subseteq B(B\cup Bn_\alpha B)B=Q$ by step 3.1. Also $Q^{-1}=Q$, because $B^{-1}=B$ and $(Bn_\alpha B)^{-1}=Bn_\alpha^{-1}B\subseteq Bn_\alpha B$: indeed $n_\alpha^{-1}=n_\alpha\,n_\alpha^{-2}\in n_\alpha T\subseteq Bn_\alpha B$ by [F1], using $T\subseteq B$. Hence $Q$ is a subgroup of $G$ containing $B$ and, by step 1.1, containing $U_{-\alpha}$. [F1, F12, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Therefore $P_\alpha=Q$: since $Q$ is a subgroup containing the two generators $B$ and $U_{-\alpha}$ of $P_\alpha$, one has $P_\alpha\subseteq Q$; conversely $B\subseteq P_\alpha$, and $n_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(w)\in V_\alpha=\varphi_\alpha(SL_2)$ is a product of elements of $U_\alpha\subseteq B$, of $U_{-\alpha}$ and of $T_\alpha\subseteq T\subseteq B$, so $n_\alpha\in P_\alpha$ and $Bn_\alpha B\subseteq P_\alpha$, whence $Q\subseteq P_\alpha$. Thus $P_\alpha=B\cup Bn_\alpha B$, and with step 1.4 this is the disjoint union $B\sqcup Bn_\alpha B$. [F1, step 1.4, step 4.1]

6.1 The set $P_\alpha=B\sqcup Bn_\alpha B$ is constructible because $B$ is closed and $Bn_\alpha B$ is the morphic image of $U_\alpha\times B$ of step 3.2. The latter image is irreducible. For every $z\ne0$, the rank-one Gauss decomposition of step 1.1 puts $u_{-\alpha}(z)$ in $Bn_\alpha B$, while $u_{-\alpha}(z)\to1$ algebraically as $z\to0$; hence $1$ and, by left $B$-translation, all of $B$ lie in the closure of $Bn_\alpha B$. Consequently $Z=\overline{P_\alpha}=\overline{Bn_\alpha B}$ is irreducible of dimension $\dim B+1$ by step 3.2. Since $P_\alpha$ is a dense constructible subgroup of $Z$ by step 5.1, it contains a nonempty open $O$ of $Z$. For any $z\in Z$, the two nonempty opens $zO^{-1}$ and $O$ meet, so $z\in OO\subseteq P_\alpha$; thus $P_\alpha=Z$ is closed and irreducible, hence connected, and has dimension $\dim B+1$. As a closed algebraic subgroup over $\mathbb C$ it is smooth. [F1, F10, step 1.1, step 3.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Construct the quotient as a scheme using two product charts. Put $s_0(z)=u_{-\alpha}(z)$ and $s_\infty(t)=u_\alpha(t)n_\alpha$, and let $m_0:\mathbb A^1\times B\to P_\alpha$ and $m_\infty:\mathbb A^1\times B\to P_\alpha$ send $(z,b)$ to $s_0(z)b$ and $(t,b)$ to $s_\infty(t)b$. The maps are injective on complex points: for $m_0$ this uses $U_{-\alpha}\cap B=1$ from step 2.2, and for $m_\infty$ it uses $n_\alpha^{-1}U_\alpha n_\alpha=U_{-\alpha}$ and the same intersection. Their differential at every point is an isomorphism onto $T P_\alpha$: at $1$ and $n_\alpha$, after left translation, its two summands are $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ and $\mathfrak b$, which sum directly to $\operatorname{Lie}P_\alpha$: that Lie algebra contains $\mathfrak b\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ because $P_\alpha$ contains $B$ and $U_{-\alpha}$, while $\dim P_\alpha=\dim B+1$ by step 6.1; translations handle all other points. Since source and target are smooth finite-type schemes over $\mathbb C$, the maps are étale. Their injectivity on closed points makes each an étale monomorphism: the complement of the open diagonal in its finite-type fibre product is closed and has no $\mathbb C$-point, hence is empty. Thus both $m_0$ and $m_\infty$ are open immersions. The image of $m_\infty$ is $Bn_\alpha B$ by step 3.2, and $B$ lies in the image of $m_0$, so the two open images cover $P_\alpha$ on closed points and therefore as schemes. In $SL_2$, with $s_0(z)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\z&1\end{pmatrix}$ and $s_\infty(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, one calculates $s_0(z)^{-1}s_\infty(t)=\begin{pmatrix}t&-1\\1-zt&z\end{pmatrix}$, which is upper triangular exactly when $zt=1$; the overlap transition is the upper-triangular matrix at $t=z^{-1}$, carried by $\varphi_\alpha$ into $B$. Therefore the local first-coordinate maps glue to a morphism $q:P_\alpha\to\mathbb A^1\cup_{z=t^{-1}}\mathbb A^1=\mathbb P^1$, and each preimage is $\mathbb A^1\times B$ with right $B$ acting on its second factor. These local products show directly on every test scheme that $q$ represents the fppf sheaf quotient $P_\alpha/B$ and is a Zariski-locally trivial right $B$-torsor. This proves (iii), including the $t=0$ point that the earlier calculation dividing by a matrix entry omitted. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct]

7.2 $\operatorname{Lie}P_\alpha=\mathfrak b\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$: since $P_\alpha$ is a closed subgroup containing $B$ and $U_{-\alpha}$, its Lie algebra contains $\operatorname{Lie}B+\operatorname{Lie}U_{-\alpha}=\mathfrak b+\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ by [F2] and [F3]; here $\mathfrak b\cap\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}=0$ by [F4], since $-\alpha\notin\Phi^+$, so $\mathfrak b+\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}=\mathfrak b\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ has dimension $\dim\mathfrak b+1=\dim B+1$. Connected algebraic groups over $\mathbb C$ of characteristic zero are smooth, so $\dim\operatorname{Lie}P_\alpha=\dim P_\alpha=\dim B+1$ by step 6.1, and the inclusion of vector spaces of the same dimension is an equality. [F2, F3, F4, step 6.1]

8.1 Collecting the steps proves (i), (ii) and the two-chart description of (iii): $P_\alpha=B\sqcup Bn_\alpha B$ is closed and connected by steps 5.1 and 6.1, $\operatorname{Lie}P_\alpha=\mathfrak b\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ with $\dim P_\alpha=\dim B+1$ by steps 6.1 and 7.2, and the two affine charts and their fppf quotient gluing $t=z^{-1}$ are established in step 7.1. The Axiom of Choice enters through [F11]: full AC is assumed by the root-subgroup suppliers [F1]–[F3], root results [F5], [F7], and constructibility and fibre dimension in [F10]; the Lie-group suppliers [F8] and [F9] require only countable choice; the argument selects only the fixed simple root $\alpha$, the fixed root vectors and the fixed height order of $\Phi^+$. [F11, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 7.2, discharge-construct] ∎
