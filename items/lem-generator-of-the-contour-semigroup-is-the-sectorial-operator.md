## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Let $A$ be a sectorial operator of angle $\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ with vertex $0$ on a complex Banach space
$X$ ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]) and let
$(T(z))_{z\in\Sigma_\delta\cup\{0\}}$ be the contour family of
[[lem-contour-definition-of-an-analytic-semigroup]], shown in
[[lem-dunford-contour-construction-satisfies-the-semigroup-law]] to be a
bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta$. Then the generator of the
strongly continuous semigroup $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$
([[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-c-zero-semigroup]]) is $A$, and
$(T(t))_{t\ge0}$ is the unique strongly continuous semigroup generated by $A$
within the class of exponentially bounded semigroups. No choice principle beyond Dependent Choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A sectorial operator $A$ of angle $\delta$ on the Banach space $X$, its contour semigroup $(T(t))_{t\ge0}$, the generator $B$ of that semigroup, a real $\omega$ with $\|T(t)\|\le Me^{\omega t}$ for all $t\ge0$, and a fixed real $\lambda>\max\{\omega,0\}$.

[L1] For the contour semigroup, $T(t)X\subseteq D(A)$, $AT(t)=T'(t)$, $t\mapsto T(t)$ is norm-$C^1$ for $t>0$, and $T'(t)=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma \lambda e^{\lambda t}R(\lambda,A)\,d\lambda$ ([[thm-analytic-semigroup-smoothing-estimates]], [[lem-contour-definition-of-an-analytic-semigroup]]).

[L2] For a strongly continuous semigroup with generator $B$ and exponential bound $\|T(t)\|\le Me^{\omega t}$ one has $R(\lambda,B)x=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\,dt$ for $\lambda>\omega$ ([[thm-laplace-transform-formula-for-the-semigroup-resolvent]]).

[L3] The fundamental theorem of calculus for Banach-valued curves, and average convergence: for continuous $g$, $h^{-1}\int_0^hg(s)ds\to g(0)$ ([[lem-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-banach-valued-continuous-curves]], [[lem-average-convergence-of-a-continuous-banach-valued-function]]).

[L4] Sectoriality with vertex $0$ makes $A$ closed and densely defined and puts every positive real $\lambda$ in $\rho(A)$ ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]).

[L5] For any strongly continuous semigroup with generator $C$, if $y\in D(C)$ then its orbit remains in $D(C)$ and $CS(t)y=S(t)Cy$ ([[lem-semigroup-generator-commutes-with-orbits-on-its-domain]]).

[L6] For $\lambda\in\rho(A)$, $R(\lambda,A)X=D(A)$, $AR(\lambda,A)=\lambda R(\lambda,A)-I$, and $R(\lambda,A)(\lambda I-A)y=y$ for $y\in D(A)$ ([[def-resolvent-of-a-closed-operator]]).

[L7] The generator $B$ is defined by $x\in D(B)$ exactly when $h^{-1}(T(h)x-x)$ has a limit as $h\downarrow0$, and that limit is $Bx$ ([[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-c-zero-semigroup]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The inclusion $A\subseteq B$. First let $y\in D(A)$ and $s>0$. The contour formula for $T(s)Ay$ and [L1] give $$T(s)Ay=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma e^{\lambda s}R(\lambda,A)Ay\,d\lambda=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_\Gamma e^{\lambda s}(\lambda R(\lambda,A)y-y)\,d\lambda=T'(s)y=AT(s)y.$$ Here $R(\lambda,A)Ay=\lambda R(\lambda,A)y-y$ follows from [L6], and $(2\pi i)^{-1}\int_\Gamma e^{\lambda s}d\lambda=0$: close a truncated keyhole, use the entire primitive $e^{\lambda s}/s$, and let its exponentially decaying outer arc tend to zero. Now for $x\in D(A)$ and $h>0$, the fundamental theorem [L3] on $[\varepsilon,h]$, followed by $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ using strong continuity at $0$, gives $T(h)x-x=\int_0^hT(s)Ax\,ds$. Dividing by $h$ and applying average convergence [L3] yields $h^{-1}(T(h)x-x)\to Ax$. By the generator definition in [L7], $x\in D(B)$ and $Bx=Ax$. [L1, L3, L6, L7, given, algebra]

1.2 The resolvent computation. Fix $x\in X$ and $\lambda>\max\{\omega,0\}$, and put $v_{\eta,R}:=\int_\eta^Re^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\,dt$ for $0<\eta<R$. On the compact interval $[\eta,R]$, both $t\mapsto T(t)x$ and $t\mapsto AT(t)x=T'(t)x$ are continuous, so the orbit is continuous in the graph norm of the closed operator $A$. Its graph-norm Bochner integral therefore lies in $D(A)$ and satisfies $Av_{\eta,R}=\int_\eta^Re^{-\lambda t}AT(t)x\,dt$. Since $(d/dt)[e^{-\lambda t}T(t)x]=e^{-\lambda t}(AT(t)x-\lambda T(t)x)$, the fundamental theorem [L3] gives $(\lambda I-A)v_{\eta,R}=\int_\eta^Re^{-\lambda t}(\lambda T(t)x-AT(t)x)\,dt=-\int_\eta^R\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(e^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\bigr)dt=e^{-\lambda\eta}T(\eta)x-e^{-\lambda R}T(R)x$. As $\eta\downarrow0$ and $R\to\infty$, $v_{\eta,R}\to v:=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\,dt$ by the exponential bound and strong continuity at $0$, while the displayed right side tends to $x$ because $\lambda>0$. Closedness of $A$ now gives $v\in D(A)$ and $(\lambda I-A)v=x$; since $\lambda>0$ and $A$ is sectorial, $\lambda\in\rho(A)$ and $v=R(\lambda,A)x$. [L1, L3, L4, given, algebra]

2.1 The resolvents agree and $B=A$. By [step 1.2] the vector $v=\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda t}T(t)x\,dt$ equals $R(\lambda,A)x$; by the Laplace formula [L2] the same integral equals $R(\lambda,B)x$. Hence $R(\lambda,B)x=R(\lambda,A)x$ for every $x\in X$. Their ranges agree and equal $D(B)=D(A)$; for each $y$ in this common domain, applying the inverses gives $(\lambda I-B)y=(\lambda I-A)y$, so $B=A$. [step 1.2, L2, L4, L6, given, algebra]

2.2 Uniqueness among exponentially bounded semigroups. Let $S$ and $T$ be two strongly continuous semigroups with generator $A$ and exponential bounds, fix $t>0$ and $x\in D(A)$, and set $g(s):=S(t-s)T(s)x$ for $s\in[0,t]$. For $0<s<t$, write the difference quotient as $$\frac{g(s+h)-g(s)}h=\frac{S(t-s-h)-S(t-s)}hT(s)x+S(t-s-h)\frac{T(s+h)x-T(s)x}h.$$ By [L5], $T(s)x\in D(A)$, the first term tends to $-S(t-s)AT(s)x$, and the second tends to $S(t-s)AT(s)x$; hence $g'(s)=0$. Continuity at the endpoints and the fundamental theorem [L3] show that $g$ is constant, so $S(t)x=T(t)x$. Since $D(A)$ is dense by [L4] and $S(t),T(t)$ are bounded, this extends to all $x\in X$. [step 1.1, L3, L4, L5, given, algebra]

3.1 Assembly. [step 2.1] identifies the generator of the contour semigroup with $A$, and [step 2.2] proves uniqueness in the exponentially bounded class; no choice principle beyond Dependent Choice was used, since only the contour construction, the Laplace representation and the fundamental theorem were invoked. [step 2.1, step 2.2, given] ∎
