## Statement

Every immersion of schemes is a monomorphism and is locally of finite type, and
is therefore separated as a morphism. No finite presentation is claimed: for a
closed immersion given by a non-finitely-generated ideal the argument supplies
only finite type.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An immersion $f:Z\to X$ of schemes with a factorization $f=j\circ i$ into a closed immersion $i:Z\to U$ and an open immersion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$.

[F1] An **immersion** is a morphism factoring as a closed immersion into an open subscheme of its target. ([[def-locally-closed-immersion]])

[F2] An **open immersion** identifies its source isomorphically with an open subscheme of its target. ([[def-open-immersion-schemes]])

[F3] A **closed immersion** has underlying map a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and surjective structure map. ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]])

[F4] Open immersions and closed immersions are monomorphisms of schemes, and any composite of these is a monomorphism; monomorphism means injectivity of the induced map on morphism sets. ([[lem-immersions-and-localizations-monomorphisms]])

[F5] A monomorphism $j:X\to Y$ is separated, its diagonal $\Delta_{X/Y}$ being an isomorphism. ([[lem-monomorphism-diagonal-isomorphism]])

[F6] A morphism is **locally of finite type** when every point of the source has an affine open neighbourhood mapping into an affine open of the target over which the corresponding ring map is of finite type. ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]])

[F7] Being locally of finite type is affine-local on source and target. ([[lem-finite-type-local-on-source-and-target]])

[F8] Every point of a scheme has an open neighbourhood which, with the restricted structure sheaf, is an affine scheme; hence the affine open subschemes form a basis of the topology. ([[def-scheme]])

[F9] For a ring $A$, closed immersions $Z\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ are, up to unique isomorphism over $\operatorname{Spec}A$, precisely the morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ for ideals $I\subseteq A$. ([[thm-affine-closed-immersions-quotient-rings]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The composite $f=j\circ i$ of the open immersion $j$ and the closed immersion $i$ is a monomorphism by [F4]; hence by [F5] the morphism $f$ is separated. [F1, F4, F5, given]

1.2 If $A\to B$ and $B\to C$ are ring maps of finite type, then $A\to C$ is of finite type: choosing finitely many generators of $B$ over $A$ and of $C$ over $B$, their union generates $C$ over $A$. [given]

1.3 An open immersion $j$ is locally of finite type: by [F2] it identifies its source with an open subscheme of the target, so a point $u$ of the source lies in some affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$ contained in the image of $j$, by [F8]; then $j^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)$ is an affine open neighbourhood of $u$ carrying an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{Spec}A$ via $j$, and the corresponding ring map is the identity of $A$, which is of finite type by [F6]. [F2, F6, F8]

1.4 A closed immersion is locally of finite type: for a point of its source choose an affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$ containing its image; the restriction $i^{-1}(\operatorname{Spec}A)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ again has underlying map a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and surjective structure map, so is a closed immersion by [F3], hence is $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ for some ideal $I\subseteq A$ by [F9]; the map $A\to A/I$ is generated as an $A$-algebra by the class of $1$, hence of finite type, with [F7] giving the general case. [F3, F7, F9]

2.1 Applying step 1.2 fibrewise over a common affine chart and using locality [F7], the composite of two morphisms that are locally of finite type is locally of finite type. [F7, step 1.2]

2.2 The argument for the closed immersion used only that $A\to A/I$ is generated by one element, so it gives finite type and not finite presentation; the ideal $I$ is not assumed finitely generated, and no finite presentation of $f$ should be read off from [F6]. [step 1.4]

3.1 By steps 1.3 and 1.4 both $i$ and $j$ are locally of finite type, so their composite $f$ is locally of finite type by step 2.1. [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1]

4.1 Steps 1.1 and 3.1 show that $f$ is a monomorphism, locally of finite type and separated, which is the assertion. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 2.2] ∎
