## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the residue suppliers. Let $C$
be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve over a perfect field $k$, let
$\mathcal L$ be an invertible $\mathcal O_C$-module, and let $s$ be a global
section of $\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}$. Then the functional
$$c\longmapsto\sum_p\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)$$
on finite-support families $c=(c_p)$ of local $\mathcal L$-principal parts
vanishes on the principal parts of global rational sections of $\mathcal L$
and on families of local regular sections, and therefore descends to a
$k$-linear functional $H^1(C,\mathcal L)\to k$. Consequently the residue
pairing
$$\langle\;,\;\rangle\colon H^1(C,\mathcal L)\times H^0(C,\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})\longrightarrow k$$
is well defined and $k$-bilinear, and it is functorial in $\mathcal L$; the
independence of the representing family is exactly the content of the global
residue theorem applied to the rational differential obtained by multiplying
a global rational section of $\mathcal L$ by $s$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a perfect field $k$, a smooth proper geometrically integral curve
$C$ over $k$, an invertible $\mathcal O_C$-module $\mathcal L$, and a global
section $s\in H^0(C,\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})$.

[F1] For a local principal part $c_p\in\mathcal L_\eta/\mathcal L_p$ and a
regular local section $s\in(\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})_p$ the product $c_ps$ is a
local principal part of $\omega_C$ and $\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)$ depends
only on $c_p$ and the germ of $s$ at $p$; the sum
$\langle c,s\rangle=\sum_p\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)$ over a finite-support
family is finite and $k$-linear in $c$ and in $s$ when $s$ is global and regular
([[def-residue-pairing-principal-parts]]).

[F2] The space of finite-support families of local $\mathcal L$-principal
parts surjects onto $H^1(C,\mathcal L)$ with kernel generated by the principal
parts of global meromorphic sections of $\mathcal L$ (including zero); families of regular lifts already give zero in each local quotient: $H^1(C,\mathcal L)$ is the cokernel of the diagonal map
$\mathcal L_\eta\to\bigoplus_p\mathcal L_\eta/\mathcal L_p$, and the diagonal image contains the principal parts of every global meromorphic section
([[lem-principal-parts-cech-h1-presentation]],
[[def-principal-parts-sheaf-line-bundle-curve]]).

[F3] If $c_p\in\mathcal L_p$ is regular at $p$ and $s$ is regular at $p$
(for instance a global section), then $c_ps$ is a regular differential at $p$
and $\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)=0$; the residue also vanishes on exact
differentials ([[lem-residue-exact-differential-zero]],
[[def-residue-pairing-principal-parts]]).

[F4] For every rational differential $\omega$ on $C$ the residues
$\operatorname{res}_p(\omega)$ vanish at all but finitely many closed points
and their sum vanishes ([[thm-global-residue-theorem-algebraic-curve]]).

[F5] The canonical bundle $\omega_C$ is invertible. Tensor evaluation sends a generic-fibre element $\theta\in\mathcal L_\eta$ and the generic germ of a global section $s$ of $\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1}$ to a rational differential $\theta s\in\Omega^1_{k(C)/k}$. Here $\theta$ is a meromorphic section, possibly zero; the differential is zero when either factor is zero ([[def-canonical-line-bundle-curve]], [[def-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F6] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; check the functional vanishes on the two kinds of
changes of representative and apply the global residue theorem.

1.1 The functional is k-linear. Fix the global section $s$. By [F1] each summand $c\mapsto\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)$ is $k$-linear in the principal part $c_p$ and the sum is finite for every finite-support family, so $c\mapsto\sum_p\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)$ is a $k$-linear functional on the space of finite-support families of local principal parts. [F1, F6]

1.2 Regular families are killed. Let $c$ be a family with $c_p\in\mathcal L_p$ for every closed point $p$. Since $s$ is a global section it is regular at every $p$, so $c_ps$ is a regular differential at $p$ by [F3] and $\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)=0$; hence the functional vanishes on $c$. [F1, F3]

1.3 Principal parts of meromorphic sections are killed. Let $\theta$ be a global meromorphic section of $\mathcal L$, that is, an element of $\mathcal L_\eta$, and let $c=(c_p)$ be its family of local principal parts $c_p=\theta+\mathcal L_p$. By [F5] the product $\theta s$ is a rational differential on $C$, and its local principal part at $p$ is $c_ps$, so $\sum_p\operatorname{res}_p(c_ps)=\sum_p\operatorname{res}_p(\theta s)$. By the global residue theorem [F4] the right-hand sum vanishes, so the functional kills the principal parts of every global meromorphic section of $\mathcal L$. [F1, F4, F5]

2.1 Descend to cohomology. By [F2] the kernel of the surjection from finite-support families of local principal parts onto $H^1(C,\mathcal L)$ is generated by the two kinds of families treated in steps 1.2 and 1.3, so the $k$-linear functional of step 1.1 factors through a $k$-linear functional $H^1(C,\mathcal L)\to k$. Letting $s$ vary, the construction is $k$-linear in $s$ by [F1] and natural in $\mathcal L$ because the product and the residue are, so the pairing $\langle\;,\;\rangle\colon H^1(C,\mathcal L)\times H^0(C,\omega_C\otimes\mathcal L^{-1})\to k$ is well defined and $k$-bilinear; this is the independence statement of the definition of the pairing, and it is exactly the global residue theorem applied to $\theta s$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
