## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $M$ be a finitely generated
$R$-module. Then $M$ is a Noetherian $R$-module.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$ and a finitely generated $R$-module $M$.

[L1] Noetherianity means that every ideal, and more generally every submodule in question, is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-ring-and-module]]).

[L2] The quotient module $M/N$ is defined for every submodule $N\le M$ ([[def-quotient-module]]).

[L3] Cyclic and finitely generated modules are those generated by one or finitely many elements ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 First suppose $M$ is cyclic, say $M=Rx$. Let $N\le M$ be a submodule and put $$I:=\{r\in R:rx\in N\}.$$ Then $I$ is an ideal of $R$, so [L1] gives generators $r_1,\dots,r_s$ for $I$. Every element of $N$ has the form $rx$ with $r\in I$, hence lies in the submodule generated by $r_1x,\dots,r_sx$. Thus every submodule of a cyclic module is finitely generated, so every cyclic module over $R$ is Noetherian. [L1, L3, construct]

2.1 Now let $M=\langle x_1,\dots,x_n\rangle_R$ with $n\ge1$, and argue by induction on $n$. The base case $n=1$ is step 1.1. For $n>1$, set $M':=\langle x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}\rangle_R$. Then $M/M'$ is cyclic, generated by $x_n+M'$, so step 1.1 makes $M/M'$ Noetherian. By the induction hypothesis, $M'$ is Noetherian. [step 1.1, L2, L3]

3.1 Let $N\le M$. Since $M'$ is Noetherian, the submodule $N\cap M'$ is finitely generated. Since $M/M'$ is Noetherian, the image $(N+M')/M'\le M/M'$ is finitely generated; choose lifts $y_1,\dots,y_t\in N$ of its generators. Every element of $N$ differs from an $R$-linear combination of the $y_i$ by an element of $N\cap M'$, so $N$ is generated by those $y_i$ together with generators of $N\cap M'$. Thus every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated. [step 2.1, L1, L2, choose, algebra]

4.1 Therefore $M$ is Noetherian. [step 3.1, L1] ∎
