## Statement

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field with residue cardinality $q$, let $r\geq t\geq1$, and fix a nontrivial continuous unitary additive character $\psi:K\to\mathbb C^\times$. Let $\pi,\pi'$ be admissible finite-length representations of $\mathrm{GL}_r(K)$ and $\mathrm{GL}_t(K)$ of Whittaker type, meaning that their respective spaces of $\psi_N$- and $\psi_N^{-1}$-equivariant functionals are one-dimensional. Let $Z(s,W,W',\Phi)$ and $Z_j(s,W,W')$ be the local integrals defined above, and let $L(s,\pi\times\pi')$ be the local factor of the tensor Weil–Deligne parameter. The equal-rank integrals $Z(s,W,W',\Phi)$, and, when $t<r$, each family $Z_j(s,W,W')$ for $0\leq j\leq r-t-1$, converge absolutely for $\operatorname{Re}(s)$ sufficiently large and extend to rational functions of $q^{-s}$. Their values span the fractional ideal generated by $L(s,\pi\times\pi')=P(q^{-s})^{-1}$, where $P\in\mathbb C[X]$ and $P(0)=1$; for $t<r$ this generator is independent of $j$.

For the functional equations, let $w_m$ be the anti-diagonal permutation matrix in $\mathrm{GL}_m(K)$, put $g^\iota={}^tg^{-1}$, and set $\widetilde W(g)=W(w_r g^\iota)$, $\widetilde W'(g)=W'(w_t g^\iota)$. For $t=r$, let $\widehat\Phi$ be the Fourier transform of $\Phi$ for the self-dual measure associated with $\psi$. Then

$$\frac{Z(1-s,\widetilde W,\widetilde W',\widehat\Phi)}{L(1-s,\pi^\vee\times(\pi')^\vee)} =\omega_{\pi'}(-1)^{r-1}\epsilon(s,\pi\times\pi',\psi)\cdot \frac{Z(s,W,W',\Phi)}{L(s,\pi\times\pi')}.$$

For $t<r$, set $k=r-t-1-j$. Put $w_{r,t}=\operatorname{diag}(I_t,w_{r-t})$, with $w_1=1$, and define the right translate $\rho(w_{r,t})W(g)=W(gw_{r,t})$. Then

$$\frac{Z_k(1-s,\rho(w_{r,t})\widetilde W,\widetilde W')}{L(1-s,\pi^\vee\times(\pi')^\vee)} =\omega_{\pi'}(-1)^{r-1}\epsilon(s,\pi\times\pi',\psi)\cdot \frac{Z_j(s,W,W')}{L(s,\pi\times\pi')}.$$

In particular, $w_{r,r-1}=I_r$, so the adjacent-rank equation has no additional right translation. The factor $\epsilon(s,\pi\times\pi',\psi)$ is a monomial in $q^{-s}$, independent of $j$. Here $\pi^\vee$ denotes the contragredient representation and $\omega_{\pi'}$ denotes the central character of $\pi'$.
