## Statement

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local domain, and let $x\in\mathfrak m$ be nonzero. If $\mathfrak m$ is minimal over $(x)$, then every prime ideal strictly contained in $\mathfrak m$ is zero. In particular $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\le 1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian local domain $(R,\mathfrak m)$ and a nonzero element $x\in\mathfrak m$ such that $\mathfrak m$ is minimal over $(x)$.

[L1] In a domain, the zero ideal is prime ([[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]], [[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]]).

[L2] Quotients and localizations of Noetherian rings are Noetherian, and every ideal of a Noetherian ring is finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[L3] The nilradical of a Noetherian ring is nilpotent ([[thm-nilradical-of-a-noetherian-ring-is-nilpotent]]).

[L4] Prime ideals of a localization correspond to primes disjoint from its denominator set ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[L5] If $IM=M$ for a finitely generated module, then $(1-a)M=0$ for some $a\in I$ ([[lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama]]).

[L6] In a local ring, every element outside the unique maximal ideal is a unit; in particular $1-a$ is a unit for $a$ in the maximal ideal ([[thm-local-ring-unit-characterisations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $\mathfrak m$ is both maximal and minimal among primes containing $(x)$, it is the only such prime. Hence the quotient $\overline R=R/(x)$ is Noetherian by [L2] and has the single prime $\overline{\mathfrak m}$. Its nilradical is therefore $\overline{\mathfrak m}$, so [L3] gives $\overline{\mathfrak m}^N=0$ for some $N$. Each layer $\overline{\mathfrak m}^j/\overline{\mathfrak m}^{j+1}$ is a finite-dimensional vector space over $R/\mathfrak m$: it is finitely generated by [L2] and is annihilated by $\overline{\mathfrak m}$. A descending chain of ideals in $\overline R$ induces descending chains in these finitely many finite-dimensional layers, so all layers, and therefore the original chain, stabilize. Thus $\overline R$ is Artinian. [L2, L3, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $\mathfrak p\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ be prime. Then $x\notin\mathfrak p$. For $r\ge1$, define the symbolic power $$\mathfrak p^{(r)}:=\mathfrak p^rR_{\mathfrak p}\cap R.$$ These form a descending chain. By step 1.1, the ideals $(\mathfrak p^{(r)}+(x))/(x)$ in $\overline R$ stabilize, so for some $r$, $$\mathfrak p^{(r)}+(x)=\mathfrak p^{(r+1)}+(x).$$ If $a\in\mathfrak p^{(r)}$, write $a=b+xc$ with $b\in\mathfrak p^{(r+1)}$. In $R_{\mathfrak p}$ the element $x$ is a unit, while $a-b\in\mathfrak p^rR_{\mathfrak p}$; hence $c\in\mathfrak p^rR_{\mathfrak p}\cap R=\mathfrak p^{(r)}$. Therefore $$\mathfrak p^{(r)}=\mathfrak p^{(r+1)}+x\mathfrak p^{(r)}.$$ [L4, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 The quotient module $M=\mathfrak p^{(r)}/\mathfrak p^{(r+1)}$ is finitely generated by [L2], and step 2.1 says $M=xM$. Apply [L5] with $I=(x)$. It gives $(1-a)M=0$ for some $a\in(x)\subseteq\mathfrak m$. Fact [L6] makes $1-a$ a unit, so $M=0$ and $\mathfrak p^{(r)}=\mathfrak p^{(r+1)}$. Localizing this equality at $\mathfrak p$ gives $$(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p})^r=(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p})^{r+1}.$$ [L2, L5, L6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Apply [L5] in the local ring $R_{\mathfrak p}$ to the finite module $(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p})^r$ and the ideal $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$. Step 3.1 says that ideal times the module is the module, so [L6] gives $(\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p})^r=0$. Because $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a domain, this forces $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}=0$, and contraction through [L4] gives $\mathfrak p=(0)$. Thus every prime strictly below $\mathfrak m$ is zero, and [L1] yields $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\le1$. [L1, L4, L5, L6, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
