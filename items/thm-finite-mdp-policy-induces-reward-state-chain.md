## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let a finite MDP have nonempty state set $S$, and let $\pi$ be a stationary Markov policy. Put $E=S\times(\mathcal R\cup\{0\})$ with its discrete sigma-algebra and define, for $x=(s,\eta)\in E$ and $B\subseteq E$,

$$ K_\pi(x,B)=\sum_{a\in A(s)}\pi(a\mid s) \sum_{\substack{s'\in S\\\rho\in\mathcal R}}1_B(s',\rho)\,p(s',\rho\mid s,a). $$

Then $K_\pi$ is a probability kernel on $E$. For each initial state $s\in S$, there is a probability law on $E^{\mathbb N}$ whose coordinate process starts at $(s,0)$ and is a time-homogeneous Markov chain with transition kernel $K_\pi$ relative to its natural filtration.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite MDP with $S\ne\emptyset$, a stationary policy $\pi$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[F1] [[def-finite-markov-decision-process]]: for every $(s,a)$, the finite joint masses $p(s',\rho\mid s,a)$ are nonnegative and sum to one; $\pi(\cdot\mid s)$ is a probability distribution.

[F2] [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]: a probability kernel assigns a probability measure at each input and has measurable event sections at every input.

[F3] [[thm-ionescu-tulcea-construction-of-a-markov-chain]]: under Choice, an initial probability and a sequence of probability kernels give a unique path-space probability law with the prescribed iterated finite-prefix laws; in the homogeneous last-coordinate case, the coordinate process is a homogeneous chain.

[F4] [[def-time-homogeneous-markov-chain-with-transition-kernel]]: a process is a homogeneous Markov chain relative to a filtration when its conditional next-state law equals the specified kernel applied to the current state, almost surely.

[F5] [[thm-subset-of-a-finite-set]], [[thm-sum-rule]], and [[thm-product-rule]]: subsets, finite unions, and finite Cartesian products of finite sets are finite; a sum over a finite partition splits into the sums over its parts.

[F6] [[def-sum-over-a-finite-index-set]] and [[lem-a-double-sum-over-finite-index-sets-may-be-interchanged]]: finite sums are independent of enumeration and the order of two finite sums may be interchanged.

[F7] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: finite product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles, so coordinate projections are measurable.

[F8] [[thm-well-ordering-principle]]: every nonempty subset of the natural numbers has a least element.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $x=(s,\eta)\in E$. By [F5], $E=S\times(\mathcal R\cup\{0\})$ is finite: $\{0\}\setminus\mathcal R$ is a subset of a finite singleton, $\mathcal R\cup\{0\}=\mathcal R\cup(\{0\}\setminus\mathcal R)$ is a finite union, and then take its product with $S$. For each $a\in A(s)$, the point masses $p(s',\rho\mid s,a)$ on $S\times\mathcal R\subseteq E$ (and zero on the added dummy-reward points) define a probability measure $\mu_a$ on $E$: they are nonnegative and total $1$ by [F1]. To check countable additivity, fix a bijection $e:n\to E$. For any disjoint sequence $(B_j)$, each nonempty $B_j$ has a least index $i_j<n$ with $e(i_j)\in B_j$, by [F8]; disjointness makes these indices distinct, so only finitely many $B_j$ are nonempty. Additivity then follows from finite-sum additivity over the partition of the finite support, as in [F5]. The displayed kernel is the finite mixture $K_\pi(x,B)=\sum_{a\in A(s)}\pi(a\mid s)\mu_a(B)$. Its values are nonnegative and $K_\pi(x,E)=\sum_a\pi(a\mid s)=1$ by [F1]. For a disjoint countable family $(B_j)$, only finitely many $B_j$ are nonempty by the same argument; additivity of each $\mu_a$ and finite-sum interchange [F6] give $K_\pi(x,\bigcup_jB_j)=\sum_jK_\pi(x,B_j)$. Thus $B\mapsto K_\pi(x,B)$ is a probability measure. Every subset of $E$ is measurable and every function on the discrete source $E$ is measurable, so [F2] makes $K_\pi$ a probability kernel. [given, F1, F2, F5, F6, F8, algebra]

2.1 For fixed $s\in S$, the point mass $\mu_0=\delta_{(s,0)}$ is a probability measure on $E$. At every $n\ge0$, use the history-dependent kernel $K_n(x_0,\ldots,x_n,B)=K_\pi(x_n,B)$. Its final-coordinate projection is measurable because the preimage of each measurable set is a product-measurable coordinate cylinder by [F7]; composition with the measurable section $x\mapsto K_\pi(x,B)$ is measurable. Each section is a probability measure by step 1.1, so $K_n$ is a probability kernel. [step 1.1, F2, F7, construct]

3.1 Apply [F3] to $(E,\mathcal P(E))$, $\mu_0$, and the sequence $(K_n)$. Its homogeneous specialization gives a probability law on $E^{\mathbb N}$ with initial state $(s,0)$ and the iterated finite-prefix laws of $K_\pi$. [given, A1, step 2.1, F3]

4.1 The coordinate process under that law is a homogeneous chain relative to its natural filtration by [F3], and its conditional transition identity is exactly the convention in [F4]. This proves the claimed chain construction for every initial $s\in S$. [step 3.1, F3, F4] ∎
