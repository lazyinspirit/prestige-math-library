## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and let $k$ be a field of characteristic zero. In projective coordinates $[X:Y:Z]$ on $\mathbb P^2_k$, let $C$ be the plane curve $Y^3=X(X-Z)Z$, and define $\pi:C\to\mathbb P^1_k$ by $\pi([X:Y:Z])=[X:Z]$. Put $U=\mathbb P^1_k\setminus\{[0:1],[1:1],[1:0]\}$, whose deleted points are $0,1,\infty$. Then $C$ is a smooth proper geometrically connected curve of genus $1$, and $\pi^{-1}(U)\to U$ is finite étale of degree $3$. Over an algebraic closure of $k$, the complement $C\setminus\pi^{-1}(U)$ consists of exactly three distinct points, one above each deleted point.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a characteristic-zero field $k$; the projective plane cubic $C$ and the projection $\pi$ specified in the Statement.

[A1] AC is the stated assumption. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A curve over a field is a separated finite-type geometrically integral scheme of dimension one; smoothness and properness are additional properties. A smooth plane curve of degree $d$ is proper and geometrically integral, and its genus is $(d-1)(d-2)/2$. Thus a smooth plane cubic is a smooth proper curve of genus one. [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]] [[cor-genus-degree-smooth-plane-curve]] [[def-genus-euler-characteristic-curve]]

[F2] A nonconstant morphism between proper integral curves is finite and surjective. A finite morphism is affine and module-finite on affine charts. [[thm-nonconstant-morphism-proper-curves-finite-surjective]] [[def-finite-morphism-schemes]]

[F3] A finite étale algebra is finite locally free; in particular, a finite étale morphism is finite and flat. For a monogenic algebra $R[T]/(f)$, its relative differentials are generated by $dT$ with relation $f'(T)dT=0$, so if $f'(T)$ is a unit, the algebra is étale. [[def-finite-morphism-schemes]] [[lem-finite-etale-algebra-module-presentation-and-rank]] [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]]

[F4] Relative projective space is covered by its standard coordinate charts, and a tuple of homogeneous coordinates with no common zero defines a projective morphism on its domain. [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]] [[def-morphism-to-projective-space-homogeneous-coordinates]]

## Proof

1.1 On the affine chart $Z=1$, the equation is $y^3=x(x-1)$. The polynomial $T^3-x(x-1)$ is irreducible over $\bar k(x)$: if it were reducible, a cubic would have a root, making $x(x-1)$ a cube, but its valuation at $x=0$ is $1$. Hence the cubic is geometrically integral. On this chart, simultaneous vanishing of the partial derivatives $2x-1$ and $-3y^2$ would force $x=1/2$ and $y=0$, which does not satisfy the equation. At infinity the equation forces the unique point $[1:0:0]$; the partial derivative with respect to $Z$ of $X(X-Z)Z-Y^3$ is nonzero there. Thus $C$ is smooth. It is a smooth proper geometrically integral plane cubic, so [F1] gives genus $1$. [A1, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 The pair $(X,Z)$ has no common zero on $C$: a point with $X=Z=0$ would satisfy $Y^3=0$ and hence would not be a projective point. By [F4], the homogeneous pair therefore defines the morphism $\pi([X:Y:Z])=[X:Z]$. It is nonconstant, so [F2] makes it finite and surjective. The function-field equation $y^3=x(x-1)$ and the irreducibility proved in step 1.1 give degree $3$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Over $U$, use $x=X/Z$ and $y=Y/Z$. The inverse image is the affine scheme with coordinate algebra $B=k[x,(x(x-1))^{-1},y]/(y^3-x(x-1)).$ This is free of rank $3$ over $A=k[x,(x(x-1))^{-1}]$, because its defining polynomial is monic of degree $3$. In $B$, $y$ and $3$ are units, so the derivative $3y^2$ is a unit. By [F3], $B$ is finite étale over $A$. This proves that $\pi^{-1}(U)\to U$ is finite étale of degree $3$. [A1, F3, step 2.1, given, construct]

4.1 Over $x=0$ the fibre equation is $y^3=0$, so it has the unique geometric point $(0,0)$. Over $x=1$ it likewise has the unique geometric point $(1,0)$. At infinity, $Z=0$ and the projective equation forces $Y=0$, leaving the unique point $[1:0:0]$. These three points are distinct and account for the full complement of $\pi^{-1}(U)$. Together with the preceding smoothness, degree, and étaleness calculations, this proves every assertion. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given] ∎
