## Example

Let $k$ be a field and let
$$
R=k[x]/(x^n)
$$
with $n\ge1$. Then $R$ is a local Artinian ring with maximal ideal $(x)$, its ideals are exactly $(x^r)$ for $0\le r\le n$, and its length as an $R$-module is $n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, an integer $n\ge1$, and the quotient ring $R=k[x]/(x^n)$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid]], every ideal of $k[x]$ is principal. The ideals of $R$ correspond by [[thm-correspondence-theorem-ideals]] to the ideals of $k[x]$ containing $(x^n)$, hence to the principal ideals $(f)$ with $f$ dividing $x^n$. Up to multiplication by a unit, these are exactly $(x^r)$ for $0\le r\le n$. Therefore the ideals of $R$ are precisely $R=(x^0)\supsetneq(x)\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq(x^{n-1})\supsetneq(x^n)=0$, so $(x)$ is the unique maximal ideal. [given, algebra]

2.1 The chain in step 1.1 shows directly that $R$ is Artinian and that $(x)^n=0$. For each $0\le r<n$, the quotient $(x^r)/(x^{r+1})$ is generated by the class of $x^r$ and annihilated by $x$, so it is one-dimensional over the residue field $R/(x)\cong k$. Hence each quotient has length $1$. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Applying [[cor-length-is-additive-in-short-exact-sequences]] successively to $0\to(x^{r+1})\to(x^r)\to(x^r)/(x^{r+1})\to0$ for $0\le r<n$ shows that $\ell_R(R)=n$. Thus $R$ is a local Artinian ring of length $n$. [step 2.1, given, algebra] ∎
