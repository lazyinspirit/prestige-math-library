## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A$ be a finite-type Cartan matrix of size
$r$, meaning the Cartan matrix of a based reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi(A)$ as in [[prop-finite-type-cartan-matrix-properties]], and let $\mathfrak g(A)$ be its Serre Lie algebra
([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]). Then
$\mathfrak g(A)$ is finite-dimensional and semisimple, has a Cartan
subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ spanned by the images of the $h_i$, has root system
$\Phi(A)$ with Cartan matrix $A$, and has the triangular decomposition
$\mathfrak g(A)=\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$, where
$\mathfrak n_\pm$ is generated by the $e_i$ respectively $f_i$. If $r>0$ and $A$ is the
Cartan matrix of an irreducible root system, then $\mathfrak g(A)$ is simple. Conversely, if $\mathfrak g$ is a
finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with base
$\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ and root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples
$(e_i,f_i,h_i)$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]), then the $e_i,f_i,h_i$ generate
$\mathfrak g$ and satisfy exactly the relations of
[[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]], so that
$\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak g(A)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-type Cartan matrix $A=(a_{ij})$ and its presented algebra $\mathfrak g(A)=\widetilde F/R$ in the sense of its definition; and, for the converse direction, a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, root system $\Phi$, base $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ and root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). It supplies ordered bases for PBW and the free-Lie construction and is also assumed in the cited semisimple root-space theory.

[L1] The presentation is the quotient of the free Lie algebra by the ideal of the displayed relations ([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]).

[L2] PBW gives the ordered-monomial basis and injectivity of a Lie algebra into its enveloping algebra ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]). The free-Lie and enveloping-algebra universal properties are [[thm-universal-property-of-the-free-lie-algebra]] and [[thm-universal-property-of-the-universal-enveloping-algebra]].

[L3] Every finite-dimensional complex $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module is completely reducible; its irreducible constituents have weights $m,m-2,\ldots,-m$, each of multiplicity one, for integers $m\ge0$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

[L4] In a finite-dimensional complex semisimple algebra, root triples satisfy the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ relations, root spaces are one-dimensional, and the root-space decomposition has zero space $\mathfrak h$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]], [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]], [[thm-root-space-decomposition-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]]). The roots span $\mathfrak h^*$ and form a reduced crystallographic root system ([[thm-roots-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]]). By [L5], the simple roots form a basis and their Cartan matrix is nonsingular; since $\alpha_j(h_i)=a_{ij}$ ([[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]]), the simple coroots $h_i$ are therefore a basis of $\mathfrak h$. Its dimension is $\dim\mathfrak h+|\Phi|$ ([[prop-dimension-formula-from-roots]]).

[L5] A base is linearly independent, and every root has integral coefficients of one sign in it ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]). Root reflections preserve the finite reduced root set ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]). The Cartan matrix is nonsingular and satisfies $a_{ij}=0$ iff $a_{ji}=0$, with nonpositive off-diagonal entries ([[prop-finite-type-cartan-matrix-properties]]).

[L6] A Cartan subalgebra is nilpotent and self-normalizing ([[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ be the Lie algebra with generators $e_i,f_i,h_i$ and all relations of [L1] except the Serre relations. It is $\mathbb Z$-graded by $\deg e_i=1$, $\deg f_i=-1$, $\deg h_i=0$, and Jacobi and the mixed relations reduce every bracket to a linear combination of brackets only in the $e$'s, only in the $f$'s, or single $h$'s. More explicitly, $[e_i,\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-]\subseteq\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-+\widetilde{\mathfrak h}$ follows by induction on bracket length; induction on positive bracket length then reduces $[\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+,\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-]$ to the same three summands. Their nonzero degrees have opposite signs, so the sum is direct. Thus $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}=\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak h}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$, where $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm$ is the subalgebra generated by the $e_i$ respectively the $f_i$ and $\widetilde{\mathfrak h}$ is spanned by the $h_i$. We claim that $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$ is free on the $f_i$, that $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+$ is free on the $e_i$, and that the $h_i$ are linearly independent. For the first claim let $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak h'\ltimes\mathbb FL(f'_1,\dots,f'_r)$ be the semidirect product of the abelian Lie algebra with basis $h'_1,\dots,h'_r$ and the free Lie algebra on $f'_1,\dots,f'_r$, with $[h'_i,h'_j]=0$ and $[h'_i,f'_j]=-a_{ij}f'_j$; the universal properties in [L2] identify the enveloping algebra of the free Lie algebra with the free associative algebra (both represent arbitrary choices of the generator images in a unital associative algebra). PBW, with a basis of the free Lie ideal placed before the $h'_i$, then identifies multiplication $U(\mathbb FL(f'_i))\otimes U(\mathfrak h')\to U(\mathfrak a)$ as a vector-space isomorphism, so $U(\mathfrak a)$ is identified with $\mathbb C\langle f'_1,\dots,f'_r\rangle\otimes\mathbb C[h'_1,\dots,h'_r]$. Write $\alpha_j(h_i)=a_{ij}$ for the simple roots and put $\alpha=\alpha_{j_1}+\cdots+\alpha_{j_s}$ for the weight of a word $w=f'_{j_1}\cdots f'_{j_s}$, so that $\alpha(h_i)$ is the sum of the $a_{ij_k}$. Define endomorphisms of $U(\mathfrak a)$ by $$h_i(w\otimes P)=w\otimes(h'_i-\alpha(h_i))P,\qquad f_i(w\otimes P)=f'_iw\otimes P,$$ $$e_i(f'_{j_1}\cdots f'_{j_s}\otimes P)=\sum_{k:\,j_k=i}f'_{j_1}\cdots\widehat{f'_{j_k}}\cdots f'_{j_s}\otimes\bigl(h'_i-(\alpha_{j_{k+1}}+\cdots+\alpha_{j_s})(h_i)\bigr)P,$$ the hat marking an omitted letter. These satisfy the relations of $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$: the operators $h_i$ are multiplications by commuting polynomials, and passing from $w$ to $f'_jw$ lowers the weight by $\alpha_j$, so $[h_i,f_j]=-a_{ij}f_j$; each summand of $e_j$ has its left factor $(h'_i-\alpha(h_i))$ replaced by $(h'_i-(\alpha-\alpha_j)(h_i))$, which differs by $a_{ij}$, giving $[h_i,e_j]=a_{ij}e_j$; and $e_if_j$ and $f_je_i$ differ only by the summand in which the leading letter is removed, present exactly when $i=j$, where it equals $w\otimes(h'_i-\alpha(h_i))P$, giving $[e_i,f_j]=\delta_{ij}h_i$. Hence $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ acts on $U(\mathfrak a)$. Let $\psi:\mathbb FL(f'_i)\to\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$ be the Lie homomorphism with $f'_i\mapsto f_i$, which is surjective, and let $\Theta$ be the inclusion of $\mathbb FL(f'_i)$ into $\mathbb C\langle f'_i\rangle\subset U(\mathfrak a)$, which exists by [L2]. The maps $z\mapsto$ (action of $\psi(z)$) and $z\mapsto$ (left multiplication by $\Theta(z)$) are Lie homomorphisms that agree on the generators $f'_i$, hence on $\mathbb FL(f'_i)$; evaluating both at $1$ gives $\psi(z)(1)=\Theta(z)=z$. So $\psi(z)=0$ forces $z=0$, and $\psi$ is an isomorphism: $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$ is free on the $f_i$. Applying the same construction with the roles of $e_i$ and $f_i$ exchanged, that is, to the automorphism $e_i\mapsto f_i$, $f_i\mapsto e_i$, $h_i\mapsto-h_i$ of the presentation, which preserves the listed relations, shows that $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+$ is free on the $e_i$. Finally $h_i(1)=h'_i$ for every $i$, and the $h'_i$ are linearly independent in $U(\mathfrak a)$; a relation $\sum_ic_ih_i=0$ in $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ therefore yields $\sum_ic_ih'_i=0$, hence $c_i=0$ for all $i$. [L1, L2, algebra]

1.2 For later use, every root of a based root system is carried to a simple root by a product of simple reflections. For a positive nonsimple root $\gamma=\sum k_i\alpha_i$, positivity of $(\gamma,\gamma)=\sum k_i(\gamma,\alpha_i)$ supplies $i$ with $\gamma(h_i)>0$. Reflection subtracts the positive integer $\gamma(h_i)$ from the $i$-th coefficient. Another coefficient is positive, since reducedness excludes a nonsimple root on a simple-root line. By the one-sign property the reflected root stays positive and has smaller height. Induction reaches a simple root; negative roots are reduced to positive ones by a final sign-changing simple reflection. [L5, algebra]

2.1 Write $E_i=\operatorname{ad}e_i$, $F_i=\operatorname{ad}f_i$, $H_i=\operatorname{ad}h_i$ in the algebra before the Serre quotient. For $i\ne j$ put $m=a_{ij}$ and $N=1-m$. The relations and induction give $F_iE_i^n e_j=-n(m+n-1)E_i^{n-1}e_j$, since $F_i e_j=0$. Hence $[f_i,S^+_{ij}]=0$ for $S^+_{ij}=E_i^N e_j$. For $k\notin\{i,j\}$ the commutator with $f_k$ vanishes termwise. For $k=j$ it equals $-E_i^N h_j$, which is zero for $N\ge2$, and for $N=1$ because then $a_{ij}=a_{ji}=0$. The involution exchanging $e,f$ and negating $h$ gives $[e_k,S^-_{ij}]=0$. Let $I_\pm$ be the ideals generated by these elements within the free positive and negative subalgebras. These are stable under $\mathfrak h$, since the generators and their iterated brackets are weight vectors. Jacobi induction on the number of positive generators bracketing $S^+_{ij}$ proves $[f_k,I_+]\subseteq I_+$: the base commutator is zero, and every new term $[f_k,e_l]=-\delta_{kl}h_k$ preserves the ideal. Similarly $[e_k,I_-]\subseteq I_-$. Thus $I_++I_-$ is an ideal in the full algebra and is exactly the Serre ideal. It has no zero-degree part, so $\mathfrak g(A)=\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$ and the $h_i$ remain independent. The simple generators remain nonzero because $[e_i,f_i]=h_i$. [L1, L5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For fixed $i$ the three generators give a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, since their nonzero distinct weights and independent $h_i$ exclude linear relations. For $j\ne i$, the span of $e_j,E_i e_j,\ldots,E_i^{-a_{ij}}e_j$ is a finite-dimensional module: $E_i$ kills its last vector by the Serre relation, $H_i$ acts by weights $a_{ij}+2n$, and the commutator formula in step 2.1 describes $F_i$. The analogous span generated by $f_j$ is also finite-dimensional. The span of $e_i,f_i,h_i,h_j$ is stable for every $j$. Every bracket of vectors in finite-dimensional modules is in a finite-dimensional module, because the bracket is an equivariant image of their tensor product. Since all elements are finite sums of iterated brackets, every element is in a finite-dimensional module for this triple. By [L3], $E_i,F_i$ act locally nilpotently. [L1, L3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 The root-lattice grading assigns degrees $\alpha_i,-\alpha_i,0$ to $e_i,f_i,h_i$. Each homogeneous bracket has that $\mathfrak h$-weight by the defining relations. Nonsingularity of $A$ makes different lattice elements distinct functionals on $\mathfrak h$. The triangular decomposition implies that the only possible weights are $Q_+$ and $-Q_+$, where $Q_+=\sum_i\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\alpha_i$, and that $\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak h$. Each nonzero weight space is finite-dimensional: it is spanned by the finitely many bracket words with its fixed multidegree. Furthermore $\mathfrak g_{m\alpha_i}=0$ for $|m|>1$, because a Lie algebra on a single generator has no bracket of length greater than one; $\mathfrak g_{\alpha_i}=\mathbb C e_i$ and $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}=\mathbb C f_i$. [L1, L5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The finite sums $\exp(E_i)$ and $\exp(F_i)$ are automorphisms: for any derivation $D$, induction gives $D^n[x,y]=\sum_{k=0}^n\binom nk[D^kx,D^{n-k}y]$, so exponentiation preserves brackets when $D$ is locally nilpotent, with inverse $\exp(-D)$. Define $w_i=\exp(E_i)\exp(-F_i)\exp(E_i)$. On the triple, direct substitution using $E_i f_i=h_i$, $E_i h_i=-2e_i$, $F_i e_i=-h_i$, $F_i h_i=2f_i$ gives $w_i(h_i)=-h_i$. It fixes every $h$ with $\alpha_i(h)=0$, so $w_i(h)=h-\alpha_i(h)h_i$ for all $h\in\mathfrak h$. Consequently $w_i$ maps $\mathfrak g_\lambda$ bijectively to $\mathfrak g_{s_i\lambda}$, where $s_i\lambda=\lambda-\lambda(h_i)\alpha_i$. It also preserves every ideal, because such an ideal is preserved by $E_i,F_i$ and their exponentials. [step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 Suppose $\mathfrak g_\lambda\ne0$ with $\lambda=\sum k_i\alpha_i\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$. If only one coefficient is positive, step 3.2 makes $\lambda$ a simple root. Otherwise $(\lambda,\lambda)=\sum k_i(\lambda,\alpha_i)>0$ supplies an $i$ with $c=\lambda(h_i)=2(\lambda,\alpha_i)/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)>0$. Here $c$ is an integer, not in general $k_i$. Step 4.1 gives a nonzero space at $s_i\lambda=\lambda-c\alpha_i$. At least one coefficient other than the $i$-th stays positive, so this weight is not in $-Q_+$. Step 3.2 therefore forces it into $Q_+$; its height is strictly smaller. Induction proves $s_i\lambda\in\Phi(A)$ and hence $\lambda\in\Phi(A)$. Negative weights follow by the same reflection argument or the presentation involution. Conversely every root occurs: step 1.2 carries it to a simple root, whose space is nonzero, and the automorphisms in step 4.1 carry that space back. The same isomorphisms show every root space has dimension one. Thus $\dim\mathfrak g(A)=r+|\Phi(A)|$. [L5, step 1.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Suppose the diagram of $A$ is connected. If $J\ne0$ is an ideal, invariance under $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak h$ makes it a sum of weight spaces: projections onto the finitely many joint eigenspaces are polynomials in the commuting diagonal operators (choose an $h$ separating their finitely many weights and use interpolation). A nonzero root component, by steps 1.2 and 4.1, puts $e_i$ in $J$ for some $i$. A nonzero component $h\in J\cap\mathfrak h$ instead gives $e_i\in J$ from $[h,e_i]=\alpha_i(h)e_i$ for some $\alpha_i(h)\ne0$, by nonsingularity of $A$. Then $h_i=[e_i,f_i]\in J$ and $f_i=-[h_i,f_i]/2\in J$. If $a_{ij}\ne0$, $[h_i,e_j]=a_{ij}e_j$ puts the next generator in $J$. Connectedness propagates this to all generators, so the algebra is simple and nonabelian. For disconnected $A$, generators in distinct blocks commute: mixed $e,f$ brackets and $h$ brackets vanish by the initial relations, and the zero Cartan entries give $[e_i,e_j]=[f_i,f_j]=0$ by the Serre relations. Jacobi extends this to the block subalgebras. The block inclusions and projections supplied by the presentations are mutually inverse maps with their direct sum, proving semisimplicity. Finally $\mathfrak h$ is abelian, and its normalizer equals itself: a nonzero root component of a normalizing vector would give a nonzero root component in its bracket with some $h$, contrary to normalization. Hence it is a Cartan subalgebra by [L6], with precisely the root system and Cartan matrix already established. [L1, L5, L6, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Conversely let $\mathfrak g$ be finite-dimensional complex semisimple with the given base and triples. For $i\ne j$, $[e_i,f_j]=0$ since $\alpha_i-\alpha_j$ has mixed signs and is not a root. The vector $f_j$ is killed by $E_i$ and has weight $-a_{ij}\ge0$. In a finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module a vector killed by $e$ is a highest-weight vector: for a vector of weight $m$, the commutator formula $ef^n v=n(m-n+1)f^{n-1}v$ proves this and gives $f^{m+1}v=0$. Complete reducibility therefore gives $F_i^{1-a_{ij}}f_j=0$; exchange $e,f$ to get the other Serre relation. All other presentation relations follow from [L4]. The generated subalgebra contains $\mathfrak h$, since the simple coroots form a basis. It is preserved by the exponentials of $E_i,F_i$, which are nilpotent on this finite-dimensional adjoint module by [L3]. The calculation in step 4.1 therefore applies to the actual algebra too. Step 1.2 and one-dimensionality of its root spaces show that the generated subalgebra contains every root space. By the root-space decomposition it is all of $\mathfrak g$, giving a surjection $\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$. Both dimensions equal $r+|\Phi|$ by step 5.1 and [L4], so the map is an isomorphism. For $r=0$ the presentation has no generators and is the zero algebra, with zero Cartan subalgebra and empty root system; the converse follows from [L4] as well. [L1, L3, L4, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra, A1] ∎
