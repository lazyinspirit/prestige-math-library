## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A$ be a finite-type Cartan matrix of size
$r$, and let $\mathfrak g(A)$ be its Serre Lie algebra
([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]). Then
$\mathfrak g(A)$ is finite-dimensional and semisimple, has a Cartan
subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ spanned by the images of the $h_i$, has root system
$\Phi(A)$ with Cartan matrix $A$, and has the triangular decomposition
$\mathfrak g(A)=\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$, where
$\mathfrak n_\pm$ is generated by the $e_i$ respectively $f_i$. If $A$ is the
Cartan matrix of an irreducible component of a reduced crystallographic root
system, then $\mathfrak g(A)$ is simple. Conversely, if $\mathfrak g$ is a
finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with base
$\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ and root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples
$(e_i,f_i,h_i)$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]), then the $e_i,f_i,h_i$ generate
$\mathfrak g$ and satisfy exactly the relations of
[[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]], so that
$\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak g(A)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-type Cartan matrix $A=(a_{ij})$ and its presented algebra $\mathfrak g(A)=\widetilde F/R$ in the sense of its definition; and, for the converse direction, a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, root system $\Phi$, base $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ and root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory of [[thm-root-space-decomposition-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]] and the string and one-dimensionality results, whose contract carries the assumption ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] $\mathfrak g(A)$ is the quotient of the free Lie algebra on $e_i,f_i,h_i$ by the ideal of the stated relations ([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]).

[L2] PBW: the ordered monomials in a totally ordered basis of a Lie algebra form a basis of its universal enveloping algebra; consequently the universal enveloping algebra of the free Lie algebra on a set is the tensor algebra on that set ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L3] Finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-modules are completely reducible, and an irreducible module has weights $m,m-2,\dots,-m$ on one-dimensional weight spaces; a cyclic vector of weight $m$ with $f$-killed generator gives $V_m$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

[L4] In a semisimple $\mathfrak g$ the elements $e_i,f_i,h_i$ of a root triple satisfy $[h_i,e_i]=2e_i$, $[h_i,f_i]=-2f_i$, $[e_i,f_i]=h_i$, root strings through a root are intervals, and root spaces are one-dimensional ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]], [[thm-root-string-property]], [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]]).

[L5] A semisimple $\mathfrak g$ has $\dim\mathfrak g=\dim\mathfrak h+|\Phi|$ ([[prop-dimension-formula-from-roots]]), and the Cartan matrix of the base is $a_{ij}=\alpha_j(h_i)$ ([[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ be the Lie algebra with generators $e_i,f_i,h_i$ and all relations of [L1] except the Serre relations. It is $\mathbb Z$-graded by $\deg e_i=1$, $\deg f_i=-1$, $\deg h_i=0$, so $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}=\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak h}\oplus\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$ with $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm$ the subalgebras generated by the $e_i$ respectively $f_i$. Both $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm$ are free on their generators and the $h_i$ are linearly independent: this is the source's triangular-decomposition lemma, proved by representing $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ on $U=\mathbb C\langle f_i\rangle\otimes\mathbb C[h'_i]$ by the displayed formulas for the action of $h_i,f_i,e_i$, which satisfy all the relations and restrict to an isomorphism $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-\cong\mathbb FL(f_i)$ and to an isomorphism of $\widetilde{\mathfrak h}$ onto $\mathbb Ch'_i$; the statement for $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+$ follows by the opposite grading. [L1, L2, algebra]

1.2 Conversely let $\mathfrak g$ be finite-dimensional complex semisimple with base $\Delta$ and root triples $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$. The $e_i,f_i,h_i$ generate $\mathfrak g$: the subalgebra they generate contains every simple root space and, by height descent along root strings, every root space; since $\mathfrak h$ is spanned by the $h_i$ and $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak g_\alpha$, it is all of $\mathfrak g$. The relations of [L1] hold by [L4]; the Serre relations hold because for $j\ne i$ the $(\mathfrak{sl}_2)_i$-submodule generated by $f_j$ is finite-dimensional with lowest weight $-a_{ij}$, so $(\operatorname{ad}f_i)^{1-a_{ij}}f_j=0$, and similarly for the $e$'s. Therefore the assignment $e_i\mapsto e_i$, $f_i\mapsto f_i$, $h_i\mapsto h_i$ defines a surjective Lie homomorphism $\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$. [L4, L5, algebra]

2.1 Let $S^+_{ij}=(\operatorname{ad}e_i)^{1-a_{ij}}e_j\in\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_+$ and $S^-_{ij}=(\operatorname{ad}f_i)^{1-a_{ij}}f_j\in\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_-$; the ideal of the Serre relations in $\widetilde{\mathfrak g}$ is $I_+\oplus I_-$ with $I_\pm$ the ideals generated by the $S^\pm_{ij}$ inside $\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm$ (the verification that $[f_k,S^+_{ij}]=0$ and $[e_k,S^-_{ij}]=0$ uses the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-representation theory of [L3]). Hence $\mathfrak g(A)=\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$ with $\mathfrak h$ spanned by the $h_i$ and $\mathfrak n_\pm=\widetilde{\mathfrak n}_\pm/I_\pm$, giving the asserted triangular decomposition and the linear independence of the $h_i$. [L1, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The Serre relations say that for $j\ne i$ the generator $e_j$ (respectively $f_j$) generates the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module $V_{-a_{ij}}$ over the subalgebra $(\mathfrak{sl}_2)_i$ spanned by $e_i,f_i,h_i$, that $h$-elements generate $V_0$ or $V_2$, and that $e_i,f_i$ generate $V_2$; consequently $\mathfrak g(A)$ is a sum of finite-dimensional $(\mathfrak{sl}_2)_i$-modules for every $i$, and every weight space $\mathfrak g_\alpha=\mathfrak g(A)\cap\{x:[h,x]=\alpha(h)x\ \forall h\in\mathfrak h\}$ is finite-dimensional, with $\mathfrak g_0=\mathfrak h$. [L3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $\alpha\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$ and $\mathfrak g_\alpha\ne0$, then $\alpha\in R$ and $\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha=1$: this is Etingof's height induction. Let $\alpha=\sum_ik_i\alpha_i$ with $k_i\ge0$ and minimal height among the nonzero $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $\alpha\notin R$; since $(\alpha,\alpha)>0$ for positive definite $\Phi$ there is $i$ with $(\alpha,\alpha_i^{\vee})=k_i>0$, so $(\alpha,\alpha_i^{\vee})>0$ and $k_i$ is the coefficient; the reflection $s_i\alpha=\alpha-(\alpha,\alpha_i^{\vee})\alpha_i$ has smaller height, and the module structure of step 3.1 gives $\mathfrak g_{s_i\alpha}\ne0$; by minimality $s_i\alpha\in R$, whence $\alpha=s_i(s_i\alpha)\in R$, a contradiction. For $\alpha\in R$, step 3.1 exhibits $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ as a weight space of the finite-dimensional $(\mathfrak sl_2)_i$-modules and Weyl-conjugacy of roots to simple roots reduces to the one-dimensional simple root spaces, so $\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha=1$. Hence $\dim\mathfrak g(A)\le r+|\Phi|$, with equality because the simple root spaces are nonzero. [L3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 There is an invariant bilinear form on $\mathfrak g(A)$: define it on generators by $(e_i,f_j)=\delta_{ij}(2/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i))$, $(h_i,h_j)=2(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)/(\alpha_j,\alpha_j)$ and extend using the adjoint action, as in the source's normalized form; the form is symmetric and invariant, and its restriction to $\mathfrak h$ is nondegenerate. Its radical is an ideal; height lowering (as in step 4.1) shows that an ideal meeting $\mathfrak h$ trivially is generated by height-zero relations and is zero, so the radical is zero and the form is nondegenerate. Hence the Killing form of $\mathfrak g(A)$ is nondegenerate and $\mathfrak g(A)$ is semisimple by Cartan's criterion, with the stated Cartan matrix and root system; if $A$ is irreducible then any nonzero ideal contains a root space, Weyl-conjugacy produces $e_i$ and $f_i$ for some $i$, and the connectedness of the Dynkin diagram propagates this to all generators, so $\mathfrak g(A)$ is simple. [L1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 The homomorphism of step 1.2 is injective: by step 5.1, $\dim\mathfrak g(A)=\dim\mathfrak h+|\Phi(A)|=r+|\Phi|$, while [L5] gives $\dim\mathfrak g=\dim\mathfrak h+|\Phi|=r+|\Phi|$; a surjection between spaces of equal finite dimension is an isomorphism. Hence every finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra is isomorphic to the Serre algebra of its Cartan matrix, which completes the proof. [L5, step 5.1, step 1.2, algebra, A1] ∎
