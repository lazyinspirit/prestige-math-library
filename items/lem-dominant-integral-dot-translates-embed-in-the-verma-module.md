## Statement

Let $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and $w,w'\in W$. Then $w\circ\lambda\uparrow\lambda$ in the strong linkage order, so $M(w\circ\lambda)$ embeds in $M(\lambda)$; the image is the submodule generated by the (unique up to scalar) singular vector of weight $w\circ\lambda$ in $M(\lambda)$. If $w\ge w'$ in Bruhat order, then $M(w\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(w'\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(\lambda)$, and the inclusion is the unique-up-to-scalar nonzero element of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M(w\circ\lambda),M(w'\circ\lambda))\cong\mathbb C$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A dominant integral weight $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and elements $w,w'\in W$; the dot action $w\circ\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$.

[F1] If $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ and $\langle\eta+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$, then there is an embedding $M(s_\alpha\circ\eta)\hookrightarrow M(\eta)$ ([[thm-verma-embedding-for-an-arbitrary-positive-root]]).

[F2] $\mu\uparrow\eta$ means that there are weights $\eta=\eta_0\succ\eta_1\succ\cdots\succ\eta_r=\mu$ and positive roots $\alpha_j$ with $\eta_j=s_{\alpha_j}\circ\eta_{j-1}$ and $\langle\eta_{j-1}+\rho,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$; the empty chain gives $\eta\uparrow\eta$. This is the definition in [[def-strong-linkage-order-on-weights]].

[F3] Every nonzero homomorphism between Verma modules is injective, and $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M(\mu),M(\eta))\le1$ for all weights $\mu,\eta$ ([[lem-a-nonzero-verma-homomorphism-is-injective]], [[thm-verma-homomorphism-spaces-have-dimension-at-most-one]]).

[F4] For $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and a positive root $\beta$, $\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$ and $\lambda+\rho$ is regular ([[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]]).

[F5] For a positive root $\beta\in\Phi^+$ and its reflection $s_\beta$: $\ell(s_\beta u)<\ell(u)$ if and only if $u^{-1}\beta<0$ ([[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]]).

[F6] A Bruhat relation $w\ge w'$ is witnessed by a saturated reflection chain $w=v_0,\dots,v_r=w'$ with $\alpha_j\in\Phi^+$, $v_j=s_{\alpha_j}v_{j-1}$ and $\ell(v_j)=\ell(v_{j-1})-1$, choosing each $\alpha_j\in\Phi^+$ since $s_{-\alpha_j}=s_{\alpha_j}$ ([[def-bruhat-order-on-a-finite-weyl-group]]).

[F7] A homomorphism $M(\eta)\to V$ is determined by the image of the highest weight vector, which must be a vector of weight $\eta$ killed by $\mathfrak n^+$; such nonzero vectors are exactly the singular vectors of weight $\eta$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-verma-modules]]).

## Proof

1.1 We prove by induction on $\ell(w)$ that $w\circ\lambda\uparrow\lambda$ and that $M(w\circ\lambda)$ embeds in $M(\lambda)$ with image generated by a singular vector of weight $w\circ\lambda$. For $w=1$ the chain is empty and the identity embeds $M(\lambda)$ in itself. If $w\ne1$, choose a reduced word $w=s_i u$ with $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+1$; then $w\circ\lambda=s_i\circ(u\circ\lambda)$ and $u\circ\lambda\uparrow\lambda$ by induction. Since $\ell(s_i u)=\ell(u)+1$, step [F5] gives $u^{-1}\alpha_i>0$; hence $\langle u\circ\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=\langle u(\lambda+\rho),\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda+\rho,u^{-1}\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$ by [F4]. So [F2] provides the one-step chain $u\circ\lambda\succ s_i\circ(u\circ\lambda)=w\circ\lambda$, which concatenated with the inductive chain gives $w\circ\lambda\uparrow\lambda$, and [F1] gives an embedding $M(w\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(u\circ\lambda)$ that we compose with $M(u\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$. [F2, F4, F5, induction]

1.2 Now let $w\ge w'$. By [F6] fix a saturated chain $w=v_0,\dots,v_r=w'$ with $v_j=s_{\alpha_j}v_{j-1}$ and $\ell(v_j)=\ell(v_{j-1})-1$. At each step the pairing $\langle v_j\circ\lambda+\rho,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda+\rho,v_j^{-1}\alpha_j^\vee\rangle$ is a positive integer: $v_j^{-1}\alpha_j=(s_{\alpha_j}v_{j-1})^{-1}\alpha_j=v_{j-1}^{-1}s_{\alpha_j}\alpha_j=-v_{j-1}^{-1}\alpha_j$, and $\ell(s_{\alpha_j}v_{j-1})=\ell(v_{j-1})-1$ gives $v_{j-1}^{-1}\alpha_j<0$ by [F5], so $v_j^{-1}\alpha_j>0$. Hence [F1] gives embeddings $M(v_{j-1}\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(v_j\circ\lambda)$ for all $j$, whose composite embeds $M(w\circ\lambda)$ in $M(w'\circ\lambda)$; the pair $(w,w')$ is nonzero in $\operatorname{Hom}$, which is one dimensional by [F3]. [F1, F3, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 By the embeddings constructed in step 1.1, $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M(w\circ\lambda),M(\lambda))\ne0$, of dimension exactly $1$ by [F3]; its image is the submodule generated by the image of the highest weight vector, which by [F7] is the unique-up-to-scalar singular vector of weight $w\circ\lambda$ in $M(\lambda)$. This proves the first two assertions. [F3, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Both the composite $M(w\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(w'\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$ and the embedding of step 2.1 are nonzero elements of the one-dimensional space $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M(w\circ\lambda),M(\lambda))$; rescaling the chosen embedding $M(w\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(w'\circ\lambda)$ by the reciprocal scalar makes the composite equal to the canonical embedding, so after this normalisation the image of $M(w\circ\lambda)$ lies inside the image of $M(w'\circ\lambda)$ inside $M(\lambda)$, i.e. $M(w\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(w'\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(\lambda)$ for the canonical singular-vector submodules. The stated uniqueness is exactly the one-dimensionality of [F3]. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
