## Statement

**False claim.** For every Noetherian commutative ring $R$ there is an
$N\in\mathbb N$ such that every ideal of $R$ can be generated by at most $N$
elements.

The Noetherian condition bounds the number of generators of **each** ideal
separately ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]) and asserts nothing
uniform across the ideals of the ring.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and the polynomial ring $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$ ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]), each of whose elements is uniquely a $k$-linear combination of the monomials $x^{a}y^{b}$, $a,b\in\mathbb N$, with finitely many nonzero coefficients. For an ideal $I$ its powers are $I^{0}:=k[x,y]$ and $I^{n+1}:=I^{n}I$, where $IJ$ is the ideal of finite sums $\sum_{l}i_lj_l$ with $i_l\in I$ and $j_l\in J$ ([[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]). For $f\in k[x,y]$ and $n\in\mathbb N$, write $f^{(n)}$ for the sum of the terms of $f$ whose monomials $x^{a}y^{b}$ have $a+b=n$.

[L1] For $S\subseteq R$, $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, so $S\subseteq(S)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[L2] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L3] $R[x]$ is the set of finitely supported functions $\mathbb N\to R$, with coefficientwise addition and the convolution product $(ab)_i=\sum_{j+k=i}a_jb_k$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L4] A **vector space over a field $F$** is a set $V$ with an addition making $(V,+,0_V)$ an abelian group and a scalar multiplication $F\times V\to V$ satisfying $\lambda(u+v)=\lambda u+\lambda v$, $(\lambda+\mu)v=\lambda v+\mu v$, $(\lambda\mu)v=\lambda(\mu v)$ and $1_Fv=v$ ([[def-vector-space]]).

[L5] The **span** of a subset of a vector space is the set of finite linear combinations of its elements, and a subset **spans** $V$ when its span is $V$ ([[def-linear-combination-and-span]]).

[L6] A subset $S\subseteq V$ is **linearly independent** when every injective finite list $v\colon n\to S$ satisfies: $\sum_{i<n}\lambda_iv_i=0_V$ implies $\lambda_i=0_F$ for every $i<n$ ([[def-linear-independence]]).

[L7] If a vector space $V$ over a field $F$ has a spanning subset $S$ with $S\approx n$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$, then every linearly independent subset $L\subseteq V$ is finite and the unique $m\in\mathbb N$ with $L\approx m$ satisfies $m\le n$ ([[cor-independent-set-is-no-larger-than-a-finite-spanning-set]]).

[L8] If $R$ is a Noetherian commutative ring then $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is Noetherian for every $n\in\mathbb N$ ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]).

[L9] Every field is a Noetherian ring ([[ex-noetherian-integers-and-fields]]).

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The ring $k[x,y]$ is Noetherian, being a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over the Noetherian ring $k$. [L8, L9, given]

1.2 Fix $n\in\mathbb N$ and let $V_n$ be the set of $k$-linear combinations of the $n+1$ monomials $x^{i}y^{n-i}$ for $0\le i\le n$. It is a $k$-vector space: it contains $0$, is closed under addition and under multiplication by elements of $k$ read as constant polynomials, and the vector-space axioms are the corresponding identities in $k[x,y]$. The monomials $x^{i}y^{n-i}$, $0\le i\le n$, are distinct and form a linearly independent subset of $V_n$, since a polynomial is a finitely supported family of coefficients and so vanishes exactly when all of its coefficients do; and they span $V_n$ by construction. [L3, L4, L5, L6, given]

2.1 Two facts about the ideal $\mathfrak m^{n}$, where $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$. First, $x^{i}y^{n-i}\in\mathfrak m^{n}$ for $0\le i\le n$: at $n=0$ the ideal is all of $k[x,y]$, and if a product of $n$ factors from $\{x,y\}$ lies in $\mathfrak m^{n}$ then multiplying it by $x$ or by $y$ lands in $\mathfrak m^{n}\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m^{n+1}$. Second, every element of $\mathfrak m^{n}$ has all its monomials of total degree at least $n$: this holds vacuously at $n=0$; every element of $\mathfrak m$ is $ax+by$ and so has all monomials of degree at least $1$; and an element of $\mathfrak m^{n+1}$ is a finite sum of products $uv$ with $u\in\mathfrak m^{n}$ and $v\in\mathfrak m$, whose monomials have degree at least $n+1$ because degrees add on monomials. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.2]

3.1 Suppose $\mathfrak m^{n}=(g_1,\ldots,g_m)$ with $m\in\mathbb N$. By step 2.1 every monomial of every $g_j$ has degree at least $n$, so $g_j=g_j^{(n)}+p_j$ with $g_j^{(n)}\in V_n$ and every monomial of $p_j$ of degree at least $n+1$. Fix $i$ with $0\le i\le n$ and write $x^{i}y^{n-i}=\sum_j h_jg_j$ with $h_j\in k[x,y]$. Splitting each $h_j$ into its constant term $\lambda_j\in k$ and a remainder whose monomials have degree at least $1$, every contribution to $h_jg_j$ other than $\lambda_jg_j^{(n)}$ has all monomials of degree at least $n+1$. Comparing the coefficients of the monomials of degree exactly $n$ on both sides gives $x^{i}y^{n-i}=\sum_j\lambda_jg_j^{(n)}$. [L2, L3, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 So the set $\{g_1^{(n)},\ldots,g_m^{(n)}\}$, which has at most $m$ elements and lies in $V_n$, spans a subset of $V_n$ containing all $n+1$ monomials $x^{i}y^{n-i}$, hence spans $V_n$. Those monomials form a linearly independent subset of $V_n$ with exactly $n+1$ elements, so $n+1\le m$. Every generating list of $\mathfrak m^{n}$ therefore has at least $n+1$ members. [L5, L6, L7, step 1.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Now suppose the claim held for $R=k[x,y]$, which is Noetherian by step 1.1, with bound $N$. Taking $n=N$ in step 4.1, the ideal $(x,y)^{N}$ needs at least $N+1$ generators, so it cannot be generated by at most $N$ elements. The claim is false. [step 1.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **What the Noetherian condition does say.** Each ideal has *some* finite generating list; the length of that list is allowed to depend on the ideal, and here it does, without bound.

- **The bound is sharp in this example and is not claimed to be sharp in general.** The ideal $(x,y)^{n}$ is generated by the $n+1$ monomials of degree $n$, so exactly $n+1$ generators suffice; the refutation needs only the lower bound.

- **A uniform bound does exist in some Noetherian rings.** In a principal ideal domain every ideal is generated by one element ([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]), so the false claim is not false for lack of any instance; it is false because it is asserted for every Noetherian ring.
