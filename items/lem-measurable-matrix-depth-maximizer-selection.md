## Statement

For every nonempty finite sample, both the bounded-class and global dense
and block-sparse argmax correspondences in
[[def-matrix-depth-covariance-estimator]] admit Borel measurable selectors
as functions of the sample. The covariance output obtained by division by
$\beta$ is Borel as well. No computational feasibility is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Fix integers $n,p\ge1$, a sample $x=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)\in E:=(\mathbb R^p)^n$, and, in the sparse case, $1\le s\le p/2$. Identify matrices with $\mathbb R^d$, $d=p^2$. First fix $c>0$ and let $K=\mathcal F_p(c)$ or $K=\mathcal F_{p,s}(c)$, with respectively $U=S^{p-1}$ or $U=U_s$. Write $D(x,\Gamma)=D_U(\Gamma,P_n(x))$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is inherited through the fixed Gaussian scale $\beta>0$ in [[def-matrix-depth-covariance-estimator]] and [[def-axiom-of-choice]]. The construction below makes only least choices from fixed finite orders and adds no choice axiom.

[F1] [[def-matrix-depth-covariance-estimator]] defines empirical depth as the directional infimum of the minimum of two weak-tail counts; both counts include ties.

[F2] [[lem-matrix-depth-maximizer-exists-on-compact-classes]] proves that $K$ and its empirical argmax fibers are nonempty compact. It applies to any fixed $c>0$ by taking the bound there to be $c/\beta$.

[F3] [[def-upper-semicontinuous-real-map-on-a-topological-space]] characterizes upper semicontinuity by open strict sublevels, equivalently closed weak superlevels.

[F4] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]] defines the Borel sigma-algebra generated by open sets; in particular closed sets and their finite or countable Boolean combinations are Borel.

[F5] [[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]]: a pointwise limit of measurable real functions is measurable.

[F6] [[thm-rational-box-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-rn]]: rational open boxes generate the finite-dimensional Borel sigma-algebra, so a map with Borel real coordinates is Borel.

[F7] [[cor-real-spectral-theorem-for-self-adjoint-endomorphisms]], [[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]], and [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]] supply norm-attaining eigenvectors for positive semidefinite symmetric matrices and $(u^Tx_i)^2\le\|x_i\|_2^2$ for unit $u$. The block-sparse class in [[def-block-sparse-covariance-class]] has one off-diagonal block of size at most $s$, with a diagonal complement.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** closed finite-level hit sets and a deterministic dyadic-cube construction, followed by a data-dependent compact cap for global maximizers.

1.1 For fixed $u$, each function $(x,\Gamma)\mapsto(u^Tx_i)^2-u^T\Gamma u$ is continuous. Its nonpositive and nonnegative sets are closed, so their indicators are upper semicontinuous: outside a closed set the indicator is locally zero, and at a point of the set its limsup is at most one. Finite sums and the minimum of two upper semicontinuous maps are upper semicontinuous. The infimum over fixed $u\in U$ is also upper semicontinuous because its strict sublevel is the union of the strict sublevels for individual $u$. Thus $D$ is jointly upper semicontinuous in $(x,\Gamma)$. Every directional count minimum belongs to $\{0,1/n,\ldots,1\}$; the infimum of a nonempty subset of this finite set belongs to the same set. Ties may give depth $1$, so the full grid is retained. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 Put $R(x)=\max_{i\le n}\|x_i\|_2^2$. If a positive semidefinite matrix $\Gamma$ has $\|\Gamma\|_{\rm op}>R(x)$, a top unit eigenvector $u$ gives $u^T\Gamma u=\|\Gamma\|_{\rm op}> (u^Tx_i)^2$ for every $i$, so the empirical upper weak tail is zero and the dense depth is zero. In the global block-sparse class, write $\Gamma$ as its symmetric block on some support $S$, $|S|\le s$, and its diagonal complement. The largest eigenvalue is attained either by a unit eigenvector in that block or by a coordinate vector in the complement; choose the former in a tie when the block is nonempty. Hence a norm-attaining vector has support of size at most $s$ or one, extends to a coordinate set of size $2s$, and belongs to $U_s$. The same strict inequality makes the sparse depth zero. This argument selects one appropriate eigenvector and does not claim every eigenvector is supported in a small block. [F1, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 For $0\le j\le n$ and any fixed closed cube $Q\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, let $H_{j,Q}:=\{x:\exists\Gamma\in K\cap Q,\ D(x,\Gamma)\ge j/n\}$. This is closed. Indeed its defining superlevel set is closed by step 1.1, and $K\cap Q$ is compact. If $x_0\notin H_{j,Q}$, product-open rectangles in the complement of that superlevel set cover $\{x_0\}\times(K\cap Q)$. Finitely many matrix-coordinate neighborhoods cover $K\cap Q$; intersect their sample-coordinate neighborhoods to obtain a neighborhood of $x_0$ outside $H_{j,Q}$. The empty $K\cap Q$ case gives the empty hit set. The same argument with $Q=\mathbb R^d$ gives a closed set $H_j:=\{x:\max_KD(x,\cdot)\ge j/n\}$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Set $H_{n+1}=\varnothing$ and $E_j=H_j\setminus H_{j+1}$ for $0\le j\le n$. These are Borel by [F4] and partition $E$, since the maximum exists by [F2] and takes a value in the finite grid by step 1.1. On $E_j$, the argmax fiber is $A(x)=\{\Gamma\in K:D(x,\Gamma)\ge j/n\}$, so $A(x)$ hits a fixed closed cube $Q$ exactly when $x\in H_{j,Q}$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Take a fixed closed cube $Q_0$ containing $K$; the entry bound in the proof of [F2] permits $Q_0=[-c,c]^d$. Repeatedly bisect each coordinate interval into two closed halves, ordering the $2^d$ child cubes lexicographically. On $E_j$, starting from $Q_0$, retain the first child cube $Q$ with $x\in H_{j,Q}$. A child exists because the children cover their parent and the parent meets $A(x)$. Inductively every selected-cube event is Borel: if $B$ is the event selecting a parent with ordered children $Q_1,\ldots,Q_{2^d}$, the event selecting $Q_r$ is $B\cap H_{j,Q_r}\setminus\bigcup_{\ell<r}H_{j,Q_\ell}$. Each level has finitely many cubes, so its selected center $a_m(x)$ is a finite-valued Borel map. The first-child rule remains unambiguous at overlapping cube boundaries. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 For fixed $x$, the sets $A(x)\cap Q_m(x)$ are nested nonempty compact sets and the cubes have diameter $2c\sqrt d\,2^{-m}\to0$. Their intersection is nonempty: otherwise their complements would cover the first compact set, and a finite subcover would contradict a later set's nonemptiness. The intersection has at most one point because its diameter is zero; denote the point by $\widehat\Gamma_c(x)$. Since both it and $a_m(x)$ lie in $Q_m(x)$, $\|a_m(x)-\widehat\Gamma_c(x)\|_2$ is at most the cube diameter and tends to zero. Thus each coordinate of $\widehat\Gamma_c$ is Borel by [F5], and the matrix-valued map is Borel by [F6]. It belongs to $A(x)$ for every sample. Taking $c=\beta M$ proves both original bounded-class selection claims. [A1, F2, F5, F6, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The continuous function $R$ has a Borel integer majorant $k(x)=1+\lfloor R(x)\rfloor$, with $\{k=\ell\}=\{\ell-1\le R<\ell\}$ for $\ell\ge1$. On that stratum, step 1.2 shows that every admissible matrix outside the fixed cap $\ell$ has depth zero. The maximum over the cap is nonnegative, so any cap maximizer is a global maximizer. Apply the explicit construction of steps 1.1–5.1 with $c=\ell$ for each integer $\ell\ge1$ and paste those Borel maps on the Borel strata $\{k=\ell\}$. For any open set of matrices, the preimage under this pasted map is the countable union, over $\ell\ge1$, of the Borel stratum $\{k=\ell\}$ intersected with the preimage under its fixed-cap Borel selector; hence the pasted map is Borel. This yields a Borel global maximizer in each class. Global argmax fibers need not be compact when the maximal depth is zero; only their compact capped subfibers are used. Division by the fixed $\beta>0$ preserves coordinatewise Borel measurability, proving the covariance-output claim for bounded and global selectors. [A1, F1, F4, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
