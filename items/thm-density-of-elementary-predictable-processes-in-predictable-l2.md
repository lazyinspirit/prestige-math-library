## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the standing hypothesis (H) of
[[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]]. Fix $T>0$ and let
$\mathcal P_T$ be the predictable sigma-algebra on $[0,T]\times\Omega$
[[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]], with the finite
measure $\mathrm dt\otimes P$. Then the classes of bounded elementary
predictable integrands
[[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]] are dense in
$L^2([0,T]\times\Omega,\mathcal P_T,\mathrm dt\otimes P)$: for every
$\mathcal P_T$-measurable $H$ with $\int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega H^2\,dP\,dt<\infty$
and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a bounded elementary predictable integrand
$G$ with
$$\Bigl(\int_0^T\!\!\int_\Omega(H-G)^2\,dP\,dt\Bigr)^{1/2}<\varepsilon .$$
Equalities are equalities of $(\mathrm dt\otimes P)$-classes, so two elementary
integrands whose difference vanishes almost everywhere represent the same
approximant.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $T>0$, the predictable sigma-algebra $\mathcal P_T$ generated by the sets $(s,u]\times C$ with $0\le s<u\le T$, $C\in\mathcal F_s$, and $\{0\}\times C$ with $C\in\mathcal F_0$, the finite measure $\mu:=\mathrm dt\otimes P$ on it, and the class $\mathcal E$ of $(\mathrm dt\otimes P)$-classes of bounded elementary predictable integrands.

[F1] $\mathcal E$ is a real vector space: on a common refinement of two elementary representations the coefficients of a sum, difference or scalar multiple are bounded and measurable at the left endpoints of the refinement, and the representation of $H$ on a refinement is elementary. The constant process $1$ is elementary, and every element of $\mathcal E$ is bounded, say by $\|G\|_\infty$. [[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]] [[def-ito-integral-of-an-elementary-predictable-process]]

[F2] The $L^2$ norm of step 2.2 satisfies the triangle inequality and is induced by an inner product on the quotient space of classes; distances are computed by $\|U-V\|_2=\bigl(\int(U-V)^2d\mu\bigr)^{1/2}$. [[thm-the-l-p-norm-descends-to-the-quotient-and-makes-l-p-a-normed-space]]

[F3] A finite intersection of generators of $\mathcal P_T$ is empty, or a generator $(s,u]\times C$, or a time-zero generator $\{0\}\times C$; the family of finite unions of disjoint generators is an algebra generating $\mathcal P_T$. [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]

[F4] If $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal D$ is a pi-system of sets and $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system, then $\sigma(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal D$. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F5] Every $H\ge0$ that is $\mathcal P_T$-measurable admits nonnegative simple $\mathcal P_T$-measurable $s_n\uparrow H$ pointwise; each $s_n$ is a finite nonnegative linear combination of indicators of $\mathcal P_T$-sets and is bounded, its values being finitely many. [[thm-increasing-simple-approximation-of-a-nonnegative-measurable-function]]

[F6] If measurable functions satisfy $|f_n|\le g$ for a single integrable $g$ and $f_n\to f$ pointwise, then $\int|f_n-f|^2d\mu\to0$ whenever the domination is square-integrable: here $0\le s_n\le H$ with $H\in L^2$, and $H-s_n\to0$ pointwise. [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F7] $\mu(\{0\}\times C)=0$ for every $C$: the section at $\omega$ is the singleton $\{0\}$, which is Lebesgue-null, so Tonelli gives $\mu(\{0\}\times C)=\int_C\!\!\int 1_{\{0\}}(s)\,ds\,dP=0$. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F8] AC is declared for uniformity with the ambient probability interfaces; the density argument itself is choice-free, and the implication bridge records the inherited obligations. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $\mathcal D:=\{A\in\mathcal P_T:1_A\in\overline{\mathcal E}\}$, where the closure is taken in the $L^2(\mu)$ metric of [F2]. By [F1] the set $\overline{\mathcal E}$ contains every constant $L^2(\mu)$ class (represented elementarily by its value on $(0,T]$ and by $0$ at time $0$) and is closed under finite linear combinations of its elements: if $U_1,\dots,U_k\in\overline{\mathcal E}$ and $a_1,\dots,a_k\in\mathbb R$, choose $G_i\in\mathcal E$ with $\|U_i-G_i\|_2<\delta$ and use the triangle inequality to bound $\|\sum_ia_iU_i-\sum_ia_iG_i\|_2$ by $\delta\sum_i|a_i|$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Every generator of $\mathcal P_T$ lies in $\mathcal D$, and $\mathcal D$ contains $\emptyset$ and $[0,T]\times\Omega$. Indeed, for $(s,u]\times C$ with $s>0$ the process $1_C1_{(s,u]}$ is elementary on the partition $0<s<u<T$ when $u<T$, with coefficients $0,1_C,0$, and on the partition $0<s<u=T$ when $u=T$, with coefficients $0,1_C$; all coefficients are measurable at the left endpoints since $1_C\in\mathcal F_s$ and constants are $\mathcal F_0$-measurable. For a generator $(0,u]\times C$ with $C\in\mathcal F_0$, use coefficients $1_C,0$ on $0<u<T$ when $u<T$, and the single coefficient $1_C$ when $u=T$. For $\{0\}\times C$ the zero process is elementary with $\|1_{\{0\}\times C}-0\|_2^2=\mu(\{0\}\times C)=0$ by [F7]. The one-block elementary process $1_{(0,T]}$ represents $1_{[0,T]\times\Omega}$ in $L^2(\mu)$ because the omitted time-zero slice is null, so the whole space belongs to $\mathcal D$; the zero elementary process represents $1_\emptyset$, so $\emptyset\in\mathcal D$. [F1, F7, given]

2.1 $\mathcal D$ is closed under complements: if $1_A$ is approximated by bounded elementary $G^n$, then the bounded elementary processes $1-G^n$ converge to $1-1_A=1_{A^c}$ in $L^2(\mu)$ by the linearity of [F1] and the norm axioms of [F2]. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 $\mathcal D$ is closed under countable unions of pairwise disjoint sets: let $A_1,A_2,\dots\in\mathcal D$ be pairwise disjoint with union $A$. Given $\varepsilon>0$, choose $m$ so large that $$\Bigl(\sum_{j>m}\mu(A_j)\Bigr)^{1/2}<\varepsilon/2,$$ which is possible because the disjoint additivity of the finite measure $\mu$ gives $\sum_j\mu(A_j)=\mu(A)\le T<\infty$; since each $A_j$ lies in $\mathcal D$, choose for $j\le m$ a bounded elementary $G_j$ with $\|1_{A_j}-G_j\|_2<\varepsilon/(2m)$; the finite sum $S:=\sum_{j\le m}G_j$ is bounded and elementary on a common refinement by [F1]; the complement of $\bigcup_{j\le m}A_j$ inside $A$ is $\bigcup_{j>m}A_j$, so [F2] and the triangle inequality give $\|1_A-S\|_2\le\bigl(\mu(\bigcup_{j>m}A_j)\bigr)^{1/2}+\sum_{j=1}^m\|1_{A_j}-G_j\|_2<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon$, hence $A\in\mathcal D$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.3 The family of finite intersections of generators of $\mathcal P_T$ is the pi-system generated by the generators, and it is contained in $\mathcal D$: by [F3] a finite intersection is empty, a generator, or a time-zero generator, and $\mathcal D$ contains $\emptyset$ and every generator by step 1.2. [F3, step 1.2]

3.1 The family $\mathcal D$ contains $\emptyset$, the whole space $[0,T]\times\Omega$ and is closed under complements (step 2.1) and under countable disjoint unions (step 2.2), so it is a lambda-system; step 2.3 shows that it contains the pi-system of finite intersections of generators, whose generated sigma-algebra is $\mathcal P_T$ by [F3]. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3]

4.1 By Dynkin's pi-lambda theorem [F4], $\mathcal P_T=\sigma(\text{generators})\subseteq\mathcal D$. Hence the indicator of every predictable set is an $L^2(\mu)$ limit of bounded elementary processes. [F4, step 3.1]

5.1 Consequently every bounded $\mathcal P_T$-measurable simple function $\sum_{i=1}^ka_i1_{A_i}$ is in $\overline{\mathcal E}$: approximate each indicator $1_{A_i}$ by bounded elementary processes and combine the finitely many approximants with the coefficients $a_i$ using the closure under finite linear combinations from step 1.1. [step 1.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Let $H\ge0$ be $\mathcal P_T$-measurable with $H\in L^2(\mu)$. By [F5] choose nonnegative simple $\mathcal P_T$-measurable $s_n\uparrow H$ pointwise; each $s_n$ is bounded and lies in $\overline{\mathcal E}$ by step 5.1. Since $0\le s_n\le H$ and $H\in L^2(\mu)$, [F6] gives $\|H-s_n\|_2\to0$, and for $\varepsilon>0$ one takes $n$ with $\|H-s_n\|_2<\varepsilon/2$ and then a bounded elementary $G$ with $\|s_n-G\|_2<\varepsilon/2$, so that $\|H-G\|_2<\varepsilon$ by the triangle inequality. [F5, F6, step 5.1]

7.1 For a general real $H\in L^2(\mu)$, decompose $H=H^+-H^-$; both parts are nonnegative and $\mathcal P_T$-measurable with $H^\pm\le|H|$, so both lie in $L^2(\mu)$ and each is approximated to within $\varepsilon/2$ by a bounded elementary process by step 6.1; their difference is bounded and elementary on a common refinement by [F1] and is within $\varepsilon$ of $H$ by the triangle inequality. [F1, step 6.1]

8.1 Steps 6.1 and 7.1 prove the density statement for nonnegative and for general predictable $L^2$ classes, with all approximants bounded and elementary; equalities of processes are equalities of $(\mathrm dt\otimes P)$-classes throughout, which is exactly the qualification "modulo product-almost-everywhere equality". For each fixed $m$, step 2.2 selects only the finite list $G_1,\dots,G_m$, so the density argument itself uses no choice principle; the declared AC is inherited from the standing hypothesis (H) and ambient probability interfaces as recorded in [F8]. [step 2.2, step 6.1, step 7.1, F8, given] ∎

## Source notes

Van der Vaart, Lemmas 5.21--5.23, obtains density by a monotone-class argument on the predictable rectangles followed by simple approximation. The version here separates the two steps: the lambda-system argument produces indicator approximants for every predictable set, and the increasing simple approximation plus dominated convergence produces the general $L^2$ approximant. No completeness of $L^2$ is used at this stage; the completion step is item 10.
