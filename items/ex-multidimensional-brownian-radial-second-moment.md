## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $d\ge1$ be finite, let
$B=(B_t)_{t\ge0}$ be a standard $d$-dimensional Brownian motion, and give it
its uncompleted natural filtration

$$\mathcal F_t=\sigma(B_u:0\le u\le t).$$

Then

$$E\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2=d\,t\qquad(t\ge0),$$

and the real process

$$M_t=\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2-d\,t$$

is an all-pairs continuous-time martingale: for every $0\le s\le t$,

$$E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=M_s\qquad\text{almost surely}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a finite integer $d\ge1$, and a standard $d$-dimensional Brownian motion $B$.

[F1] A standard $d$-dimensional Brownian motion starts at zero almost surely, has mutually independent vector increments with law $N_d(0,hI_d)$ on every finite increasing grid, and equivalently has independent standard scalar Brownian coordinate processes. [[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]]

[F2] The uncompleted natural filtration is generated by observations up to time $t$; an all-pairs martingale is adapted, integrable at each time, and satisfies the displayed conditional identity for every $s\le t$. [[def-continuous-time-filtration-and-all-pairs-martingale]]

[F3] Disjoint groups of independent sigma-algebras remain independent, and measurable functions applied separately to independent random elements remain independent. [[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]] [[lem-measurable-functions-preserve-independence]]

[F4] A pi-system contained in a lambda-system generates a sigma-algebra still contained in that lambda-system; probability is finitely additive and continuous on increasing event sequences. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]] [[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]

[F5] The squared Euclidean norm is the finite sum of coordinate squares. The Euclidean norm is continuous, finite algebra preserves continuity, and continuous maps have Borel preimages. [[def-euclidean-inner-product]] [[lem-every-norm-on-rn-is-continuous-for-the-euclidean-metric]] [[lem-algebra-of-continuous-real-maps-on-a-space]] [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]

[F6] A scalar $N(0,h)$ variable has mean zero and variance, hence second moment, $h$, including $h=0$. [[lem-characteristic-function-of-a-normal-law]]

[F7] A variable measurable for the conditioning sigma-algebra conditions to itself; an integrable variable independent of that sigma-algebra conditions to its mean. Conditional expectation is linear, and a finite known factor may be taken out when the products are integrable. Ordinary $L^1$ integration is linear on arbitrary measure spaces. [[lem-conditioning-a-known-variable-and-an-independent-variable]] [[thm-basic-algebra-and-order-properties-of-conditional-expectation]] [[thm-taking-out-what-is-known]] [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

[F8] Products of square-integrable real variables are integrable by Cauchy--Schwarz. [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-for-random-variables]]

[F9] AC is used through the Brownian, normal-law, and conditional-expectation suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $B_t=(B_t^1,\ldots,B_t^d)$. By [F1], each $B_t^\alpha$ has law $N(0,t)$, so [F6] gives $E[(B_t^\alpha)^2]=t$. The coordinate-square sum in [F5] and finite $L^1$ linearity in [F7] therefore give $$E\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2=\sum_{\alpha=1}^dE[(B_t^\alpha)^2]=d\,t.$$ Thus $\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2$ and $M_t$ are integrable. The map $x\mapsto\lVert x\rVert_2^2$ is continuous, hence Borel by [F5]; since $B_t$ is an observation generating $\mathcal F_t$, both $\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2$ and $M_t$ are $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. Consequently $M$ is adapted. At $t=0$, the same calculation gives expectation zero although $B_0=0$ is only an almost-sure identity. [F1, F2, F5, F6, F7]

1.2 Fix $0\le s<t$ and put $\Delta=B_t-B_s$. Let $\Pi_s$ consist of $\Omega$ and all finite intersections $$\bigcap_{j=1}^m\{B_{u_j}\in A_j\},\qquad 0\le u_j\le s,\quad A_j\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R^d).$$ It is a pi-system and its generated sigma-algebra is $\mathcal F_s$ by [F2]. Given one such cylinder, sort its distinct positive observation times and adjoin $0,s,t$. On the probability-one event $\{B_0=0\}$, every displayed past observation is a finite cumulative sum of the vector increments ending no later than $s$. Those increments and $\Delta$ are disjoint groups of the mutually independent increment family in [F1]; [F3] makes their grouped vectors, and then the past observation tuple and $\Delta$, independent. Replacing the tuple by its almost-surely equal cumulative-sum expression changes the cylinder event by a null set, so for every Borel $C\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ and every $A\in\Pi_s$, $$P(A\cap\{\Delta\in C\})=P(A)P(\Delta\in C).$$ Empty cylinders give $\Omega$. If $s=0$, every finite past tuple is almost surely the constant zero tuple, and the same null-set argument gives the identity. [F1, F2, F3, F9]

2.1 Fix a Borel $C\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ and let $\Lambda_C$ be the events $A\in\mathcal F_s$ satisfying the factorization in step 1.2. The class contains $\Omega$; finite differences of nested members follow by subtracting the two finite probability identities, and increasing countable unions follow from probability continuity in [F4]. Thus $\Lambda_C$ is a lambda-system containing $\Pi_s$. By [F4], it contains $\sigma(\Pi_s)=\mathcal F_s$. Since $C$ was arbitrary, $\Delta$ is independent of $\mathcal F_s$. For $s=t$, $\Delta=0$ identically and the same independence conclusion is immediate. [step 1.2, F4]

3.1 Let $h=t-s\ge0$. By [F1], the coordinates of $\Delta$ have laws $N(0,h)$; [F6] and [F7] give $$E[\Delta^\alpha\mid\mathcal F_s]=0,\qquad E[\lVert\Delta\rVert_2^2\mid\mathcal F_s]=E\lVert\Delta\rVert_2^2=d\,h.$$ Indeed each scalar coordinate and the squared norm are Borel functions of the independent vector from step 2.1, and the squared norm is integrable by [F5]--[F7]. Also $B_s^\alpha$ and $\Delta^\alpha$ are square-integrable by [F1] and [F6], so [F8] makes their product integrable. Because $B_s^\alpha$ is $\mathcal F_s$-measurable, taking out what is known yields $$E[B_s^\alpha\Delta^\alpha\mid\mathcal F_s]=B_s^\alpha E[\Delta^\alpha\mid\mathcal F_s]=0.$$ The formulas also hold when $h=0$, where $\Delta=0$ pointwise. [step 2.1, F1, F5, F6, F7, F8]

4.1 The pathwise Euclidean identity $$\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2=\lVert B_s\rVert_2^2+2\sum_{\alpha=1}^dB_s^\alpha\Delta^\alpha+\lVert\Delta\rVert_2^2$$ and conditional linearity now give $$E[\lVert B_t\rVert_2^2\mid\mathcal F_s]=\lVert B_s\rVert_2^2+d(t-s).$$ Subtracting the deterministic $dt$ proves $$E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=\lVert B_s\rVert_2^2+d(t-s)-dt=M_s.$$ With the adaptation and integrability from step 1.1, this is exactly the all-pairs martingale definition in [F2]. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F2, F5, F7, algebra]

5.1 Steps 1.1--4.1 prove both displayed claims. The case $d=1$ is Durrett's scalar square martingale; $d=0$ is excluded. Time zero, $s=0$, $s=t$, and $t=0$ are all covered, and no completed or right-continuously augmented filtration has been substituted for the stated natural filtration. There is no biconditional. AC is used only through [F1], [F6], and [F7]; the finite grid, pi-lambda promotion, and coordinate sum make no additional choices. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F1, F2, F6, F7, F9] ∎

## Source notes

Durrett, Section 7.5, Theorem 7.5.4 and its proof, printed p. 376, proves $B_t^2-t$ is a martingale by expanding across the future centered increment and conditioning on the Brownian past. The proof above supplies the finite $d$-coordinate extension and proves from the increment definition and a pi-lambda argument that the future vector increment is independent of the uncompleted natural filtration.
