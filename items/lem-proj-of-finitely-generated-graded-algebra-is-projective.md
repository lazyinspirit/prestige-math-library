## Statement

Assume AC inherited from the Proj construction. Let $S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n$ be a finitely generated graded commutative $\mathbb C$-algebra with $S_0=\mathbb C$ and every $S_n$ finite-dimensional, and suppose $S$ is generated as a $\mathbb C$-algebra by homogeneous elements $f_1,\dots,f_N$ of positive degrees $d_1,\dots,d_N$; put $L=\operatorname{lcm}(d_1,\dots,d_N)$ (with $L=1$ when $N=0$) and $d=kL$ with $k=\max(1,N-1)$.

Then the Veronese subalgebra $S^{(d)}=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}S_{dj}$ is generated in degree one by $S_d$: every element of $S^{(d)}$ is a polynomial in elements of the finite-dimensional space $S_d$. Consequently, if $S_d\ne0$, choosing a $\mathbb C$-basis $g_0,\dots,g_M$ of $S_d$ and the induced graded surjection $B=\mathbb C[x_0,\dots,x_M]\to S^{(d)}$, $x_i\mapsto g_i$, the canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}S^{(d)}$ exhibits $\operatorname{Proj}S$ as a closed subscheme of the projective space $\mathbf P^M_{\mathbb C}=\operatorname{Proj}B$ of finite type over $\mathbb C$, and $\mathcal O(1)$ pulls back to $\mathcal O_{S^{(d)}}(1)$.

If $S_+=0$, then $S=\mathbb C$; and if $S_d=0$, degree-one generation makes $S^{(d)}=\mathbb C$. In either case $\operatorname{Proj}S=\varnothing$ is the closed subscheme $V_+(x_0)$ of $\mathbf P^0_{\mathbb C}$, so the conclusion holds vacuously in that case as well.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction; a finitely generated graded $\mathbb C$-algebra $S$ with $S_0=\mathbb C$ and $\dim_{\mathbb C}S_n<\infty$ for all $n$; homogeneous generators $f_1,\dots,f_N$ of positive degrees $d_1,\dots,d_N$ when $S_+\ne0$; the numbers $L=\operatorname{lcm}(d_1,\dots,d_N)$, $k=\max(1,N-1)$ and $d=kL$.

[F1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; it is inherited by this item from the Proj construction, whose localization and chart-gluing data use it. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]])

[F2] *Finite homogeneous generation.* $S$ is generated as a $\mathbb C$-algebra by finitely many homogeneous elements, and every $S_n$ is finite-dimensional. If $S_+\ne0$ then the degree-zero part of a generating set may be omitted, so that $S$ is generated by finitely many homogeneous elements $f_1,\dots,f_N$ of positive degrees; every element of $S_n$ is then a $\mathbb C$-linear combination of monomials $f_1^{a_1}\cdots f_N^{a_N}$ with $\sum a_id_i=n$. ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]], [[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]])

[F3] *Proj dictionary.* For a graded ring $S$ with $S_0=\mathbb C$, the points of $\operatorname{Proj}S$ are the homogeneous primes not containing $S_+$, the standard opens are $D_+(f)=\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$ for homogeneous $f$ of positive degree, and these charts cover $\operatorname{Proj}S$. For the standard graded polynomial ring $B=\mathbb C[x_0,\dots,x_M]$ one has $\operatorname{Proj}B=\mathbf P^M_{\mathbb C}$. ([[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]], [[thm-projective-space-as-proj]])

[F4] *Closed subschemes of projective space.* For a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq B$ the canonical closed immersion $\operatorname{Proj}(B/I)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Proj}B=\mathbf P^M_{\mathbb C}$ identifies $\operatorname{Proj}(B/I)$ with $V_+(I)$; its chart rings are the quotients $B_{(g)}\to(B/I)_{(g)}$. ([[thm-closed-subschemes-projective-space-homogeneous-ideals]])

[F5] *Veronese invariance.* There is a canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}S^{(d)}$ mapping $D_+(f)$ to $D_+(f^d)$ with the same coordinate ring $S_{(f)}=S^{(d)}_{(f^d)}$, under which $\mathcal O_{S^{(d)}}(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal O_S(d)$. ([[lem-proj-veronese-invariance]])

[F6] *Twisting sheaves.* For a graded ring $A$ with $A_0=\mathbb C$, the sheaf $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}A}(1)$ is the associated sheaf of the shifted graded module $A(1)$, with sections $A(1)_{(g)}$ on the chart $D_+(g)$; a degree-zero homomorphism $M\to N$ of graded $A$-modules induces a morphism $\widetilde M\to\widetilde N$ of associated sheaves, computed on charts by the corresponding localization maps. ([[def-twisting-sheaf-proj]], [[def-associated-sheaf-graded-module-proj]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $S_+=0$ then $S_n=0$ for all $n\ge1$ and hence $S=S_0=\mathbb C$; since every homogeneous prime contains $S_+=0$, the set $\operatorname{Proj}S$ is empty, and $\varnothing$ is the closed subscheme $V_+(x_0)\subseteq\mathbf P^0_{\mathbb C}$, so the assertions about $\operatorname{Proj}S$ hold vacuously. Assume henceforth $S_+\ne0$ and fix homogeneous generators $f_1,\dots,f_N$ of $S$ of positive degrees $d_1,\dots,d_N$, with $L=\operatorname{lcm}(d_1,\dots,d_N)$, $k=\max(1,N-1)$ and $d=kL$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 *The generation claim.* We show that every monomial $f_1^{a_1}\cdots f_N^{a_N}$ of degree $md$ with $m\ge2$ is a product of $m$ monomials of degree $d$. Suppose not, and choose a counterexample with $m\ge2$ minimal; call it $M=f_1^{a_1}\cdots f_N^{a_N}$, so that $a=(a_1,\dots,a_N)$ has no decomposition $a=b_1+\cdots+b_m$ with $b_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N$ and $\sum_i b_{ji}d_i=d$. Then $a$ has no sub-vector of degree $d=kL$: if $c\le a$ had $\sum_i c_id_i=kL$, then $a-c$ would have degree $(m-1)kL$ and would, by minimality of $m$, decompose into $m-1$ vectors of degree $kL$, so $a$ would decompose into $m$ of them. [F2, algebra]

2.1 *Maximal $L$-blocks.* Call $v\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N$ an $L$-block of $a$ if $v\le a$ and $\sum_i v_id_i=L$, and choose a maximal-length family $v_1,\dots,v_\ell$ of $L$-blocks of $a$ with $\sum_{j\le\ell}v_j\le a$; such a maximum exists because every $L$-block has degree $L>0$. Then $\ell\le k-1$, since otherwise $v_1+\cdots+v_k$ would be a sub-vector of $a$ of degree $kL$, contradicting step 1.2. Put $u:=a-\sum_{j\le\ell}v_j\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}^N$. The vector $u$ contains no $L$-block: any $L$-block $v\le u$ would be disjoint from all $v_j$, contradicting maximality of $\ell$. Moreover $\sum_i u_id_i=md-\ell L\ge(mk-k+1)L=((m-1)k+1)L\ge(k+1)L\ge NL$, using $m\ge2$ and $k\ge N-1$. [choose, algebra, step 1.2]

3.1 *The box bound.* For each $i$, since $L/d_i$ is a positive integer and $(L/d_i)e_i$ has degree $L$, the absence of an $L$-block in $u$ forces $u_i<L/d_i$, i.e. $u_i\le L/d_i-1$. Hence $\sum_i u_id_i\le\sum_i(L/d_i-1)d_i=NL-\sum_i d_i<NL$, contradicting the lower bound $\ge NL$ from step 2.1. Therefore every monomial of degree $md$, $m\ge2$, is a product of $m$ monomials of degree $d$; since $S_{md}$ is spanned by such monomials and $S_0=\mathbb C$, the Veronese algebra satisfies $S^{(d)}=\mathbb C[S_d]$ with $S_d$ finite-dimensional. [F2, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 If $S_d=0$, step 3.1 gives $S^{(d)}=\mathbb C$, so its Proj is empty and is a closed subscheme of $\mathbf P^0$ as in step 1.1; [F5] gives the same conclusion for $\operatorname{Proj}S$. Otherwise choose a basis $g_0,\dots,g_M$ of the nonzero finite-dimensional space $S_d$. The graded map $B=\mathbb C[x_0,\dots,x_M]\to S^{(d)}$ sending $x_i$ to $g_i$ is surjective by step 3.1. Its homogeneous kernel $I$ identifies $S^{(d)}$ with $B/I$, and [F4] exhibits its Proj as $V_+(I)\subseteq\mathbf P^M$. The remaining chart arguments concern this nonempty-basis case. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 *The twisting sheaves.* On the covering charts $D_+(x_i)$ of $\operatorname{Proj}B$, the degree-zero module $B(1)_{(x_i)}$ is free with frame $x_i$: every fraction of shifted degree zero is $x_i$ times a degree-zero fraction. Its pullback to $D_+(g_i)$ is therefore free with the corresponding frame $g_i$, which likewise generates $S^{(d)}(1)_{(g_i)}$. On overlaps the transition ratios $x_j/x_i$ pull back to $g_j/g_i$. Thus these frame identifications glue to identify the pullback of $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}B}(1)$ with $\mathcal O_{S^{(d)}}(1)$ by [F6]. [F6, algebra, step 4.1]

5.2 *Finite type.* The charts $D_+(g_i)$ have rings obtained by quotienting the polynomial ring in the degree-zero ratios $x_j/x_i$, hence are finitely generated $\mathbb C$-algebras; finitely many charts suffice because $S^{(d)}$ is generated over $\mathbb C$ by the finite set $g_0,\dots,g_M$ by step 3.1, so $D_+(g_0),\dots,D_+(g_M)$ cover $\operatorname{Proj}S^{(d)}$. Thus $\operatorname{Proj}S^{(d)}$ is a $\mathbb C$-scheme of finite type. [F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Finally [F5] gives the canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}S^{(d)}$ mapping $D_+(f)$ to $D_+(f^d)$. Composed with step 4.1 it exhibits $\operatorname{Proj}S$ as a closed subscheme of $\mathbf P^M_{\mathbb C}$, of finite type over $\mathbb C$ by step 5.2, and by step 5.1 the twisting sheaf $\mathcal O(1)$ pulls back to $\mathcal O_{S^{(d)}}(1)$, as claimed; the degenerate case $S=\mathbb C$ was settled in step 1.1. [F5, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2] ∎

## Remarks

- **The correcting range of $d$.** The repaired statement singles out the common multiple $d=kL$ with $k=\max(1,N-1)$; the original scaffold claimed the conclusion for *every* common multiple of the degrees of a generating set, which is false. With $S=\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4]$ graded by $\deg x_1=2$, $\deg x_2=12$, $\deg x_3=15$, $\deg x_4=20$, one has $L=60$, and the monomial $x_1x_2^4x_3^2x_4^2$ has degree $120=2L$ although it is not a product of two monomials of degree $L$: no sub-multiset of the degrees $\{2,12,12,12,12,15,15,20,20\}$ sums to $60$. Hence $S^{(L)}$ is not generated in degree one, while the lemma supplies $k=N-1=3$ and then $S^{(3L)}$ is. (This is Deligne's classical phenomenon, quoted in the weighted-projective-space literature; the proof above is self-contained.)
- **Multiples of $d$.** If $A$ is a standard graded $\mathbb C$-algebra, then so is every Veronese $A^{(l)}$: $A_{lm}=(A_l)^m$. Applying this to $A=S^{(d)}$ shows that $S^{(d')}$ is generated in degree one for every multiple $d'$ of $d$, Moreover, the maximal-block and box-bound argument of steps 1.2–3.1 works for every integer $k\ge\max(1,N-1)$: its only bound on $k$ is $k\ge N-1$. Thus the conclusion holds for all sufficiently large multiples $kL$ of $L$.
- **No Hilbert--Mumford input.** The proof uses only the monomial combinatorics of the degrees $d_i$ and the definitions of $\operatorname{Proj}$ and its twisting sheaves; no criterion for (semi)stability is involved.
