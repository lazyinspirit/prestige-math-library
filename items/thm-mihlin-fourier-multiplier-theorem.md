## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $n\ge1$, put $q:=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$, and let
$m$ be a Mihlin symbol with constants $C_\alpha$, $|\alpha|\le q$
([[def-mihlin-symbol-with-more-than-half-dimension-derivatives]]); put
$A:=\max_{|\alpha|\le q}C_\alpha$, so that $\|m\|_\infty\le C_0\le A$. Then $m$
is an $L^p$ Fourier multiplier for every $1<p<\infty$
([[def-lp-fourier-multiplier-and-multiplier-norm]]), and there is a constant
$C_n$, depending only on $n$, with
$$\|m\|_{M_p}\le C_n\max\bigl(p,(p-1)^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(A+\|m\|_\infty\bigr) \qquad(1<p<\infty).$$
Equivalently, the operator $T_m$ of
[[def-translation-invariant-fourier-multiplier-on-schwartz-space]] satisfies
$\|T_mf\|_p\le C_n\max(p,(p-1)^{-1})(A+\|m\|_\infty)\|f\|_p$ for every
$f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C)$. Only strict-range bounds are asserted: no
endpoint $p=1$ or $p=\infty$ claim is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice; $n\ge1$, $q=n_0=\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1$, the Mihlin symbol $m$ with constants $C_\alpha$ and the constant $A=\max_{|\alpha|\le q}C_\alpha$; a compactly supported $f\in L^2(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C)$; the mollifier family $\varphi_\varepsilon$ generated by a unit-mass $\varphi\in C_c^\infty$ ([[def-mollifier-family-generated-by-a-unit-mass-smooth-bump]], [[prop-mollifier-families-are-l-one-approximate-identities]]).

[F1] In the dyadic-piece setting attached to $m$ — the annulus cutoff $\zeta$, the symbols $m_j=m\,\zeta(2^{-j}\cdot)$ and the functions $K_j=\mathcal F^{-1}(u_{m_j})$ — one has $\sup_j\int|K_j(x)|(1+2^j|x|)^{1/4}dx\le C_nA$ and $\sup_j2^{-j}\int|\nabla K_j(x)|(1+2^j|x|)^{1/4}dx\le C_nA$ whenever $A\ge\max\{\|m\|_\infty,\max_{|\alpha|\le n_0}C_\alpha\}$ ([[def-mihlin-symbol-with-more-than-half-dimension-derivatives]], [[lem-dyadic-mihlin-kernels-have-uniform-integral-hormander-control]]).

[F2] With $W:=\mathcal F^{-1}(u_m)$, a tempered distribution extending the off-origin kernel (no principal-value representation is asserted): the partial sums $\sum_{|j|\le N}K_j$ converge to $W$ in $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)$; the series $\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}K_j(x)$ converges for almost every $x\in\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$ to a function $k$ with $k=W$ on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$ (that is, $\langle W,\psi\rangle=\int k\psi$ for every $\psi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\})$); and $k$ satisfies the annular bound $\sup_{\delta>0}\int_{\delta\le|x|\le2\delta}|k|\le C_nA$ and Hörmander's condition $\sup_{y\ne0}\int_{|x|\ge2|y|}|k(x-y)-k(x)|dx\le C_nA$ ([[lem-mihlin-dyadic-pieces-sum-to-an-off-support-kernel-representation]]).

[F3] $\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)\subseteq D_m$ and, as $L^2$ classes, $T_mf=\mathcal F_2^{-1}(m\,\mathcal F_2f)$ for $f\in\mathcal S$, with $\|T_mf\|_2\le\|m\|_\infty\|f\|_2$; the Schwartz-core action extends uniquely to the bounded $L^2$ operator $T_m=\mathcal F_2^{-1}M_m\mathcal F_2$ of norm $\|m\|_\infty$ ([[lem-ltwo-fourier-multiplier-bound]], [[def-translation-invariant-fourier-multiplier-on-schwartz-space]]).

[F4] For $u\in\mathcal S'$ and Schwartz $f$, $\mathcal F(u*f)=(\mathcal Fu)(\mathcal Ff)$; the convolution $u*f$ is the smooth function of polynomial growth $x\mapsto\langle u_y,f(x-y)\rangle$ ([[thm-fourier-transform-converts-allowed-tempered-convolutions-to-products]], [[thm-tempered-convolution-is-smooth-with-polynomial-growth]], [[def-convolution-of-a-tempered-distribution-with-a-schwartz-function]]).

[F5] A Calderón–Zygmund kernel in the base sense with constants $A_1,A_2$ and its $L^2$-bounded operator with norm $B$ satisfy $\|Tg\|_p\le C_n(A_2+B)\max(p,(p-1)^{-1})\|g\|_p$ for $1<p<\infty$ ([[def-calderon-zygmund-kernel-and-principal-value-operator]], [[thm-calderon-zygmund-singular-integrals-are-bounded-on-lp]]); an $L^1$ approximate identity converges in $L^p$: $\|g*\varphi_\varepsilon-g\|_p\to0$ for $g\in L^p$, $1\le p<\infty$ ([[thm-l-one-approximate-identities-converge-in-l-p]]); and $g*\varphi_\varepsilon$ is smooth for locally integrable $g$ ([[thm-convolution-with-a-mollifier-is-smooth-and-differentiates-under-the-integral-sign]]).



[F6] Tonelli interchanges nonnegative product integrals; dominated convergence applies under an integrable majorant; locally integrable functions are determined almost everywhere by their distribution pairings. ([[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]], [[thm-dominated-convergence]], [[thm-locally-integrable-functions-embed-in-distributions]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The dyadic pieces and the kernel. The hypotheses of [F1] are satisfied, so with $\zeta$, $m_j$, $K_j$ as there the weighted bounds hold; [F2] then supplies the tempered distribution $W=\mathcal F^{-1}(u_m)$, the almost-everywhere convergent series $k=\sum_jK_j$ on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$ coinciding with $W$ off the origin, and the annular and Hörmander bounds $\le C_nA$ for $k$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 On Schwartz inputs, [F4] gives $\mathcal F(W*f)=u_{m\widehat f}$, so $W*f=T_mf$. Suppose $x\notin\operatorname{supp}f$ and put $\psi(y)=f(x-y)$, which vanishes near zero. Cut $\psi$ off at infinity using the fixed cutoff $\chi_R$ of [F1]. Then $\chi_R\psi\to\psi$ in Schwartz space, and [F2] gives $\langle W,\chi_R\psi\rangle=\int k\chi_R\psi$. The annular bound makes $\int|k\psi|$ finite: on $2^jd\le|y|\le2^{j+1}d$, its contribution is at most $C_nA\sup_{|y|\ge2^jd}|\psi(y)|$, summable for $j\ge0$ by Schwartz decay, where $d>0$ is smaller than the distance from zero to the support of $\psi$. Dominated convergence [F6] gives $T_mf(x)=\int k(y)f(x-y)\,dy$. Only annular size and local integrability are used. [F1, F2, F4, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 For compactly supported $f\in L^2$, its mollifications $f_\varepsilon=f*\varphi_\varepsilon$ are smooth with uniformly bounded compact support and converge to $f$ in $L^1$ and $L^2$ by [F5]. Fix a compact $E$ disjoint from $\operatorname{supp}f$; for sufficiently small $\varepsilon$ all these supports lie in a fixed compact set $S$ disjoint from $E$. The compact difference set $E-S$ avoids zero. Tonelli and [F2] give $\int_E\int_S|k(x-y)||f_\varepsilon(y)-f(y)|\,dy\,dx\le\|f_\varepsilon-f\|_1\int_{E-S}|k(z)|\,dz\to0$. The same estimate with $f$ proves absolute convergence of its kernel integral almost everywhere on $E$. Step 1.2 identifies $T_mf_\varepsilon$ there with the kernel integral of $f_\varepsilon$. By [F3], $T_mf_\varepsilon\to T_mf$ in $L^2$, hence in $L^1(E)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Uniqueness of the $L^1(E)$ limit identifies $T_mf$ with the kernel integral of $f$. A countable compact exhaustion proves the off-support representation on $(\operatorname{supp}f)^c$. [F2, F3, F5, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The kernel $k$ has annular and Hörmander constants at most $C_nA$, and $T_m$ has $L^2$ norm $B=\|m\|_\infty$. Step 2.1 proves its off-support representation, so $T_m$ is a Calderón–Zygmund operator. Apply the dimension-only estimate proved in the quantitative argument of [[thm-calderon-zygmund-singular-integrals-are-bounded-on-lp]] to get $\|T_mg\|_p\le C_n\max(p,(p-1)^{-1})(A+\|m\|_\infty)\|g\|_p$. The constant has no unrecorded dependence on $p$. [F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Consequently $m$ is an $L^p$ Fourier multiplier in the sense of the multiplier definition: [F3] gives $\mathcal S\subseteq D_m$, for $f\in\mathcal S$ the distribution $T_mf$ is the regular distribution of the $L^2$ class $\mathcal F_2^{-1}(m\mathcal F_2f)$, which is the class assigned to $f$ by the $L^p$ extension of step 3.1 (the two extensions of the Schwartz-core action agree on the dense subspace $\mathcal S$), and step 3.1 is exactly the required norm bound. Hence $\|m\|_{M_p}\le C_n\max(p,(p-1)^{-1})(A+\|m\|_\infty)$ for every $1<p<\infty$, which is the assertion. [F3, F5, step 3.1] ∎
