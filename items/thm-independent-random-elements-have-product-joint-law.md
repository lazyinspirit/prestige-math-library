## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, and let
$X_i:(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\to(S_i,\Sigma_i)$ for $i<n$ be independent
random elements. Define

$$X=(X_0,\dots,X_{n-1}):\Omega\to\prod_{i<n}S_i.$$

Then $X$ is a random element of
$\left(\prod_{i<n}S_i,\bigotimes_{i<n}\Sigma_i\right)$, and its law is the
finite product of the marginal laws:

$$\mathbb P_X=\bigotimes_{i<n}\mathbb P_{X_i}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Independent random elements $X_i:(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\to(S_i,\Sigma_i)$ for $i<n$.

[L1] Independence of random elements is equivalent to factorization on measurable rectangles. ([[thm-rectangle-criterion-for-independent-random-elements]])

[L2] The law of a random element is a probability measure. ([[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]], [[lem-law-of-a-random-element-is-a-probability-measure]])

[L3] For sigma-finite factors, the product measure is the unique measure on the product sigma-algebra having the rectangle formula. ([[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]])

[L4] The finite product sigma-algebra is generated recursively by measurable rectangles. ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]])

[L5] Measures agreeing on a generating pi-system with an increasing finite-measure exhaustion from that pi-system are equal ([[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 First prove that all-coordinate rectangles generate the recursively defined finite product sigma-algebra. This is immediate for one factor. Suppose it holds for $k$ factors, write $T_k=\prod_{i<k}S_i$ and $\mathcal A_k=\bigotimes_{i<k}\Sigma_i$, and let $\mathcal H$ be the sigma-algebra generated by the $(k+1)$-coordinate rectangles. These rectangles belong to $\mathcal A_k\otimes\Sigma_k$, so $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal A_k\otimes\Sigma_k$. Fix $B\in\Sigma_k$. The class $\mathcal C_B=\{A\in\mathcal A_k:A\times B\in\mathcal H\}$ contains $T_k$, since $T_k\times B$ is an all-coordinate rectangle. It is closed under complements relative to $T_k$, because $(T_k\setminus A)\times B=(T_k\times B)\setminus(A\times B)$, and under countable unions because product with $B$ commutes with them. Hence it is a sigma-algebra containing all $k$-coordinate rectangles. The induction hypothesis gives $\mathcal C_B=\mathcal A_k$. As this holds for every $B$, every binary rectangle $A\times B$ generating $\mathcal A_k\otimes\Sigma_k$ lies in $\mathcal H$, proving equality and completing the finite induction. [given, L4]

2.1 Let $\mathcal D:=\{E\subseteq\prod_{i<n}S_i:X^{-1}(E)\in\mathcal F\}$. Preimages preserve complements and countable unions, so $\mathcal D$ is a sigma-algebra. If $R=\prod_{i<n}B_i$ is a measurable rectangle, then $X^{-1}(R)=\bigcap_{i<n}X_i^{-1}(B_i)\in\mathcal F$. By step 1.1, the product sigma-algebra is generated by these all-coordinate rectangles, so $X$ is measurable for $\bigotimes_{i<n}\Sigma_i$. [given, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 For every measurable rectangle $R=\prod_{i<n}B_i$, [L1] gives $$\mathbb P_X(R)=\mathbb P(X_0\in B_0,\dots,X_{n-1}\in B_{n-1})=\prod_{i<n}\mathbb P(X_i\in B_i)=\prod_{i<n}\mathbb P_{X_i}(B_i).$$ [L1, L2, step 2.1]

3.2 By step 2.1, the law $\mathbb P_X$ is defined, and [L2] makes it a probability measure on the product sigma-algebra. [step 2.1, L2]

4.1 For every $n\ge1$, put $\nu_1:=\mathbb P_{X_0}$ and, for $1\le m<n$, define recursively $\nu_{m+1}:=\nu_m\otimes\mathbb P_{X_m}$. For $n=1$, step 3.1 already identifies $\mathbb P_X$ with $\nu_1$. For $n\ge2$, repeated use of the rectangle formula in [L3] shows that $\nu_n\left(\prod_{i<n}B_i\right)=\prod_{i<n}\mathbb P_{X_i}(B_i)$ for every measurable rectangle. Therefore $\mathbb P_X$ and $\nu_n$ agree on all measurable rectangles. [step 3.1, step 3.2, L3, algebra]

5.1 All-coordinate rectangles are closed under intersections, because intersections are taken coordinatewise; they contain the whole product and generate its sigma-algebra by step 1.1. By step 4.1, $\mathbb P_X$ and $\nu_n$ agree on this pi-system. Both have total mass one: [L2] gives this for the joint law, and the rectangle formula [L3] gives it at every stage of the product-law recursion. The constant exhaustion by the whole product therefore satisfies the finite-measure hypothesis of [L5], which gives $$\mathbb P_X=\nu_n=\bigotimes_{i<n}\mathbb P_{X_i}.$$ This is the claimed product joint law. [L2, L3, L5, step 1.1, step 3.2, step 4.1] ∎
