## Statement

Assume AC. Let $L$ have characteristic $p>0$, let $M/L$ be finite
purely inseparable, let $D/L$ be a smooth proper geometrically integral
curve, and let $C/L$ be a projective geometrically integral curve.
Suppose a nonconstant $M$-morphism $f:D_M\to C_M$ has an infinite set
$S\subseteq D(L)$ such that $f(S)\subseteq C(L)$ and $f(S)$ is infinite.
Then $f$ is the base extension of a unique nonconstant $L$-morphism
$D\to C$. Neither smoothness of $C$ nor projective linearity of $f$ is
assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the fields, curves, morphism and point set of the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Elements of a purely inseparable extension have a $p$-power in
the base field. In a finite extension, taking the maximum of the exponents
of a finite field generating list gives $q=p^a$ with $M^q\subseteq L$.
[[thm-purely-inseparable-extension-characterizations]]

[F2] A divisor space $L(B)$ on a smooth proper curve is finite-dimensional
and consists of rational functions with poles bounded by $B$. Its global
section space commutes with field extension. Cartier divisors and their
line bundles pull back using local rational equations; for a rational
function, this pulls back its principal divisor.
[[lem-riemann-roch-space-finite-dimensional]]
[[lem-proper-cohomology-field-extension]]
[[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]]
[[lem-pullback-cartier-divisor-line-bundle]]

[F3] A nonzero rational function on an integral curve has only finitely
many zeros where regular, and a proper closed subset of a curve is finite.
Finite homogeneous equations describe projective closed subschemes.
Finite-dimensional linear equations and their kernels can be computed
by row reduction and commute with field extension.
[[lem-curve-closed-subsets-finite]]
[[thm-closed-subschemes-projective-space-homogeneous-ideals]]
[[thm-gauss-jordan-elimination-produces-reduced-row-echelon-form]]

[F4] A rational map from a smooth curve to a proper scheme extends
uniquely to a morphism. A projective field scheme is proper: projective
space, closed immersions and compositions are proper.
[[lem-rational-map-smooth-curve-to-proper-scheme-extends]]
[[thm-projective-space-proper-over-base]] [[lem-closed-immersion-proper]]
[[lem-proper-stable-composition]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited from the curve and cohomology suppliers. The infinite
point conditions are reduced to finitely many linear equations in a finite
coefficient space; no infinite family of representatives is chosen.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; descend a finite pole space and use the
linear conditions of prescribed point values to recover the map.

1.1 Embed $C\subseteq\mathbf P^N_L$. A homogeneous coordinate is nonzero at the generic image of $f$; renumber it first, and write the resulting rational coordinate tuple as $f_0=1,f_1,\ldots,f_N\in M(D)$. Choose $q$ with $M^q\subseteq L$ by [F1]. Then $f_i^q\in L(D)$: the field $M(D)$ is generated by $M$ and $L(D)$, and taking $q$th powers puts all these generators into $L(D)$. Let $B$ be the sum on $D$ of the pole divisors of the nonzero $f_i^q$; an identically zero coordinate contributes no poles. It is an effective divisor defined over $L$. Its pullback $B_M$ bounds every pole divisor of $f_i$. Indeed [F2] and the identity $\operatorname{div}(f_i^q)=q\operatorname{div}(f_i)$ give coefficient at least $q$ times each pole order of $f_i$. Put $V=L(B)$ over $L$. By [F2], all $f_i$ belong to $V\otimes_L M$, identified with the functions whose poles are bounded by $B_M$. Remove from $S$ the finitely many points in the support of $B$. At all remaining points the tuple $[1:f_1:\cdots:f_N]$ is regular and is the given morphism's value, so the first coordinate of every corresponding $f(P)$ is nonzero. Their image is still infinite. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For every remaining $P\in S$, write its image in the fixed chart as $[1:c_1(P):\cdots:c_N(P)]$ with $c_i(P)\in L$. On the finite-dimensional $L$-space $V^{N+1}$ impose the linear conditions $b_i(P)=c_i(P)b_0(P)$ for $1\le i\le N$. These are well-defined linear conditions over $L$, since every function of $V$ is regular at $P$. Their functionals span a finite-dimensional subspace of the dual of $V^{N+1}$; choose a finite basis from their span. Thus their common kernel $T$ is given by finitely many linear equations and satisfies $T\otimes_L M$ equal to the common kernel of all the same conditions after field extension, by [F3]. The nonzero tuple $(f_0,\ldots,f_N)$ lies in that extended kernel by step 1.1. Hence $T\ne0$, and choose a nonzero tuple $(b_0,\ldots,b_N)\in T$. Necessarily $b_0\ne0$: otherwise all the $b_i$ vanish on infinitely many points by the constraints and are zero by [F3]. [F2, F3, given, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 At all but finitely many of these points, $b_0(P)\ne0$ by [F3]. The rational tuple $[1:b_1/b_0:\cdots:b_N/b_0]$ over $L$ therefore has the prescribed values $f(P)\in C(L)$ at infinitely many points. Pulling back any homogeneous equation of $C$ gives a rational function on $D$ with infinitely many zeros, so it is zero by [F3]. Thus this tuple defines a rational $L$-map $D\dashrightarrow C$. It is nonconstant because its prescribed image is infinite. By [F4] it extends uniquely to a nonconstant morphism $g:D\to C$. Over $M$, each rational function $f_i b_0-b_i$ vanishes at infinitely many of the same distinct points of $D_M$, so is zero by [F3]. Therefore $g_M=f$ as rational maps and hence as morphisms by uniqueness in [F4]. Any other $L$-descent has the same rational coordinates after scalar extension and therefore before it, again by injectivity of field extension; its morphism is the same by [F4]. This proves descent and uniqueness, without any basepoint-free assumption on the chosen coordinate tuple. [F3, F4, given, algebra, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
