## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be an LCH group. A closed two-sided ideal
$I\trianglelefteq C^*(G)$ is **primitive** if it is the kernel of an
irreducible nondegenerate star-representation of $C^*(G)$
([[def-c-star-algebra]],
[[thm-nondegenerate-representations-of-c-star-g-are-unitary-representations-of-g]],
[[def-full-group-c-star-algebra]]). The **primitive ideal space**
$\operatorname{Prim}(C^*(G))$ is the set of primitive ideals, equipped with the
**Jacobson topology**, whose closed sets are the sets
$$h(J):=\{I\in\operatorname{Prim}(C^*(G)):I\supseteq J\}$$
for closed two-sided ideals $J\trianglelefteq C^*(G)$.

Under the correspondence between unitary representations of $G$ and
nondegenerate star-representations of $C^*(G)$, an irreducible unitary
representation $\pi$ determines the primitive ideal
$C^*\ker\pi:=\ker_{C^*(G)}\pi$, and the assignment
$$\kappa:\widehat G\to\operatorname{Prim}(C^*(G)),\qquad\kappa([\pi]):=C^*\ker\pi,$$
is well defined on unitary equivalence classes
([[def-unitary-dual-of-a-locally-compact-group]]).

## Remarks

- **Kernels of equivalent representations agree.** If $U:H_\pi\to H_\rho$ is
  a unitary intertwiner, then $\pi(f)=U^{-1}\rho(f)U$ for every $f\in L^1(G)$
  and, by continuity of the extensions, for every element of $C^*(G)$; hence
  $\ker\pi=\ker\rho$ and $\kappa$ is well defined on classes.
- **The Jacobson closed sets satisfy the topology axioms.** Finite
  intersections of hulls are hulls of the closed ideals generated by the
  union; arbitrary intersections are hulls of the ideal generated by the
  union; $h(0)$ is the whole space and $h(C^*(G))=\varnothing$. Finite unions
  use that primitive ideals are prime: if $J_1J_2\subseteq P=\ker\pi$ for an
  irreducible $\pi$, then $\pi(J_1)H_\pi$ and $\pi(J_2)H_\pi$ are ideals
  images; if both were nonzero they would be dense invariant subspaces
  (irreducibility), and $\pi(J_1)\pi(J_2)H_\pi$ would be dense and zero at
  once; hence $J_1\subseteq P$ or $J_2\subseteq P$. Consequently
  $h(J_1)\cup h(J_2)=h(J_1\cap J_2)$, and the displayed family of closed sets
  is a topology. No assertion that primitive ideals are maximal is used.
- **Comparison with the Fell topology.** The set $\operatorname{Prim}(C^*(G))$
  with the Jacobson topology carries the quotient topology induced by
  $\kappa$ from the Fell topology on $\widehat G$ when the comparison is
  established; that identification is proved by the kernel-map theorem later
  on this page and is not assumed here
  ([[def-fell-topology-on-the-unitary-dual]]).
- **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the representation
  correspondence; taking kernels and forming hulls uses no further choice
  ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).
