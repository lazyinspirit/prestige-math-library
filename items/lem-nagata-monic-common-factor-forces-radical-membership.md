## Statement

Let $C$ be a commutative ring and $I$ an ideal. Suppose $n\geq1$ and
$$Q(X,T)=X^nT^n+\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_j(X)X^{n-j}T^{n-j},\qquad\mu_j(X)\in I C[X].$$
Let $G(X,T)=T^k+b_1(X)T^{k-1}+\cdots+b_k(X)$ be monic in $T$, with $k\geq1$ and $b_i(X)\in C[X]$, and suppose $G$ divides $Q$ in $C[X,T]$. Then every coefficient of every $b_i$ belongs to $\sqrt I$. Consequently, for any commutative $C$-algebra $D$ and $x,t\in D$ satisfying $G(x,t)=0$, one has $t\in\sqrt{ID}$.

In particular, localizing $D$ at the multiplicative subset $\{t^m+z:m\geq0,\ z\in ID\}$ gives the zero ring. Zero rings, zero or unit ideals, and zero coefficient polynomials are included; neither reducedness nor Noetherianity is required.

## Statement references

[Alonso–Coquand–Lombardi, Lemma2.19, pp.8–9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00597)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The coefficient ring, ideal, two polynomials, their stated monic divisibility and, for the consequence, the algebra and evaluation.

[F1] If one monic polynomial divides another, every nonleading coefficient of the divisor satisfies a monic equation with coefficients in the ideal generated by the nonleading coefficients of the dividend, in the subring generated by those dividend coefficients. [[lem-nagata-monic-factor-coefficients-are-integral-over-coefficient-ideal]]

[F2] Radical membership is witnessed by a positive power, and the radical of an ideal is an ideal. [[def-radical-of-an-ideal]] [[lem-radical-is-an-ideal]]

## Proof

1.1 Choose a positive integer $N$ larger than the degrees of all nonzero $b_i(X)$ and all nonzero $\mu_j(X)$. There are finitely many polynomials, so such a bound exists, including when all are zero. Substituting $T=X^N$ in the given divisibility gives $G(X,X^N)\mid Q(X,X^N)$ in $C[X]$. The first polynomial is monic of nominated degree $Nk$: each other block $b_i(X)X^{N(k-i)}$ has exponents in the disjoint interval $[N(k-i),N(k-i)+N-1]$, strictly below $Nk$. The second polynomial is monic of nominated degree $n(N+1)$: its $j$th other term has degree at most $N-1+(N+1)(n-j)<n(N+1)$ and all its coefficients belong to $I$. Thus every nonleading coefficient of $Q(X,X^N)$ lies in $I$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F1] to these monic polynomials. Every nonleading coefficient of $G(X,X^N)$ satisfies a monic equation whose nonleading coefficients lie in an ideal contained in $I$. Reducing that equation modulo $I$ makes it a pure power equation, so the coefficient belongs to $\sqrt I$ by [F2]. The disjoint exponent intervals in step 1.1 ensure that every coefficient of each $b_i(X)$ occurs unchanged as a nonleading coefficient of $G(X,X^N)$, without a collision with another block or the leading term. Hence all those coefficients lie in $\sqrt I$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Map to any $D$ as in the Statement. The image of $\sqrt I$ lies in $\sqrt{ID}$ by its power witnesses. Since this latter radical is an ideal by [F2], every $b_i(x)$ belongs to it. The equality $G(x,t)=0$ then gives $t^k\in\sqrt{ID}$; a further positive power gives $t\in\sqrt{ID}$. Thus some $t^m$ with $m\geq1$ belongs to $ID$, and $0=t^m+(-t^m)$ lies in the specified multiplicative subset. Localization at a subset containing zero is the zero ring. That subset is indeed multiplicative, because $(t^m+z)(t^r+w)=t^{m+r}+t^mw+t^rz+zw$ and the remaining terms lie in $ID$. If $I$ is unit the conclusion is automatic, and if $C$ is zero every algebra and equality here is zero. Zero polynomials among the finite lists require no degree choice in step 1.1. This covers all stated boundary cases. [F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
