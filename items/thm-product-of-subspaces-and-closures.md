## Statement

Let $(X_i, \mathcal{T}_i)_{i \in I}$ be topological spaces, let $A_i \subseteq X_i$
for each $i$, and give $\prod_{i} X_i$ the product topology
([[def-product-topology]]). Then:

1. **Subspaces commute with products.** The product of the subspace topologies on
   the $A_i$ ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]) is exactly the subspace topology
   that $\prod_i A_i$ inherits from $\prod_i X_i$. So the phrase "the product of
   the subspaces $A_i$" names one topology, whichever of the two routes is taken.
2. **Closure of a product is the product of the closures.** In $\prod_i X_i$,
   $$\overline{\prod_{i \in I} A_i} \;=\; \prod_{i \in I} \overline{A_i} ,$$
   closures being taken in $\prod_i X_i$ and in $X_i$ respectively
   ([[def-interior-closure-boundary-top]]). In particular $\prod_i A_i$ is closed
   in the product whenever every $A_i$ is closed in $X_i$.

No hypothesis of nonemptiness is imposed in claim 2: if some $A_{i_0}$ is empty
then both sides are empty, since $\overline{\varnothing} = \varnothing$. When
every $A_i$ is nonempty the inclusion $\supseteq$ of claim 2 uses the Axiom of
Choice for infinite $I$ ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) and [[lem-finite-choice]] for
$I$ a natural number, and that is the only place in the item where a choice
principle appears.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Topological spaces $(X_i,\mathcal{T}_i)_{i \in I}$, subsets $A_i \subseteq X_i$, the product $P := \prod_i X_i$ with the product topology, the subset $A := \prod_i A_i \subseteq P$, and the projections $\pi_j : P \to X_j$ and $\pi^A_j : A \to A_j$.

[A1] A basis for the product topology on $P$ is the family of boxes $\prod_i U_i$ with every $U_i$ open and $U_i = X_i$ off a set listed as $\{i_0,\dots,i_{n-1}\}$ for some natural $n$; the product topology is generated by the subbasis $\{\pi_i^{-1}[U] : U \in \mathcal{T}_i\}$ ([[def-product-topology]], [[thm-basis-criterion]]).

[A2] The subspace topology on $A \subseteq P$ is $\{\, O \cap A : O \text{ open in } P \,\}$, and likewise on each $A_i \subseteq X_i$ ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]).

[L1] A topology generated by a family is the coarsest topology containing it, and two families generating the same topology may be exchanged freely ([[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

[L2] The projections $\pi_j$ are continuous, and a map into a product is continuous exactly when all its components are ([[thm-product-universal-property]], [[thm-initial-and-final-characteristic-properties]]).

[L3] For a continuous $f$ one has $f[\overline{B}] \subseteq \overline{f[B]}$ for every subset $B$ of the source ([[thm-continuity-characterisations-top]], clause (e)).

[L4] $x \in \overline{B}$ if and only if every basic open set containing $x$ meets $B$; $\overline{B}$ is the smallest closed superset of $B$, and $\overline{\varnothing} = \varnothing$ ([[thm-closure-characterisation-top]], clauses (c) and (d) and claim 2; [[def-interior-closure-boundary-top]]).

[L5] A function on a natural number $n$ whose values are nonempty sets has a choice function for its family of values ([[lem-finite-choice]], [[def-choice-function]]); a family of nonempty sets indexed by an arbitrary set has one by the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $i \in I$ and $U \in \mathcal{T}_i$ one has $(\pi^A_i)^{-1}[U \cap A_i] = \{\, x \in A : x_i \in U \cap A_i \,\} = \{\, x \in A : x_i \in U \,\} = \pi_i^{-1}[U] \cap A$, the middle equality holding because every $x \in A$ already satisfies $x_i \in A_i$. [given, A2]

1.2 As $U$ ranges over $\mathcal{T}_i$ and $i$ over $I$, the sets $\pi_i^{-1}[U] \cap A$ are exactly the traces on $A$ of the subbasic open sets of $P$, and the sets $U \cap A_i$ are exactly the open sets of the subspace $A_i$. [A1, A2]

1.3 If some $A_{i_0} = \varnothing$ then $A = \varnothing$ and $\overline{A_{i_0}} = \varnothing$ by [L4], so $\overline{A} = \varnothing$ and $\prod_i \overline{A_i} = \varnothing$ as well. [given, L4]

1.4 Each $\pi_i$ is continuous and $\pi_i[A] \subseteq A_i$, so [L3] gives $\pi_i[\overline{A}] \subseteq \overline{\pi_i[A]} \subseteq \overline{A_i}$; hence every $x \in \overline{A}$ has $x_i \in \overline{A_i}$ for every $i$, that is $\overline{A} \subseteq \prod_i \overline{A_i}$. [given, L2, L3, L4]

1.5 Assume every $A_i$ is nonempty, and fix by [L5] a point $a \in \prod_i A_i = A$. [L5, choose]

1.6 Assume every $A_i$ is nonempty, let $x \in \prod_i \overline{A_i}$ and let $B = \prod_i O_i$ be a basic open set of $P$ with $x \in B$, with $O_i = X_i$ off $\{i_0,\dots,i_{n-1}\}$ as in [A1]. For each $m < n$ the set $O_{i_m} \cap A_{i_m}$ is nonempty, since $x_{i_m} \in \overline{A_{i_m}}$ and $O_{i_m}$ is an open set containing it. [A1, L4]

2.1 By step 1.1 and step 1.2 the initial topology on $A$ of the family $(\pi^A_i)$ and the subspace topology on $A$ are generated by the same family of subsets of $A$: the first by the sets $(\pi^A_i)^{-1}[W]$ with $W$ open in $A_i$, the second by the traces on $A$ of the open sets of $P$, whose subbasic members are the traces of the sets $\pi_i^{-1}[U]$. So the two topologies coincide, which is claim 1. [step 1.1, step 1.2, A1, A2, L1]

2.2 By [L5] applied to the function $m \mapsto O_{i_m} \cap A_{i_m}$ on $n$, choose $c_m \in O_{i_m} \cap A_{i_m}$ for each $m < n$, and define $y \in P$ by $y_{i_m} := c_m$ for $m < n$ and $y_i := a_i$ for every other $i$, with $a$ as in step 1.5. Then $y_i \in A_i$ for every $i$, so $y \in A$; and $y_i \in O_i$ for every $i$, since $O_i = X_i$ off the listed indices. So $y \in B \cap A$. [step 1.5, step 1.6, L5, choose]

3.1 By step 2.2 every basic open set containing $x$ meets $A$, so $x \in \overline{A}$ by [L4]; hence $\prod_i \overline{A_i} \subseteq \overline{A}$ when every $A_i$ is nonempty, and with step 1.4 the two sets are equal in that case. [step 1.4, step 2.2, L4]

4.1 Step 1.3 disposes of the case in which some $A_i$ is empty and step 3.1 of the case in which none is, so claim 2 holds in general; the final sentence of claim 2 follows because $A_i = \overline{A_i}$ for every $i$ then gives $\overline{A} = A$, which is closedness by [L4]. With step 2.1 both claims are proved. [step 1.3, step 2.1, step 3.1, L4] ∎

## Remarks

- **Claim 1 is what lets "$\prod A_i$" be written without a warning.** Every later item that forms a product of subspaces, the Hilbert cube and the Cantor set among them, silently uses it: the topology on $[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}$ obtained by taking the product of the subspaces $[0,1] \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ is the topology it inherits as a subset of $\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}}$.

- **Claim 2 fails for the box topology in the direction one might expect it to hold.** Nothing above is claimed for $\mathcal{T}^{\square}$, and the inclusion $\overline{A} \subseteq \prod \overline{A_i}$ is the only half that survives there, since it uses only continuity of the projections, which holds for the box topology as well.

- **The choice is spent on the coordinates that the basic open set leaves unrestricted.** Those are all but finitely many, and for each of them the point $y$ of step 2.2 needs *some* member of $A_i$; the finitely many restricted coordinates are handled by [[lem-finite-choice]] alone. That split is exactly why the finite case of claim 2 is a theorem of ZF.
