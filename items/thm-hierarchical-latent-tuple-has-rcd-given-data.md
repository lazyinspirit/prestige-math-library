## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the hypotheses of
[[def-hierarchical-bayesian-model]]. On the canonical joint probability space,
the latent tuple $\xi=(\eta,\theta_1,\ldots,\theta_m)$ has a regular
conditional distribution given the sigma-algebra generated by the observed
data $Y$. The conditional law is a probability kernel at every sample point,
including exceptional points. This existence conclusion alone does not assign
a normalized likelihood posterior to a fixed observation fiber on which the
evidence is zero or infinite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-group hierarchical model and AC.

[F1] The model supplies a proper hyperprior, a finite product group-parameter kernel, and a finite product observation kernel, all with the stated measurable spaces. [[def-hierarchical-bayesian-model]]

[F2] A proper prior and probability model kernel define a joint probability law on their product, with the iterated-integral identity. [[lem-prior-and-model-kernel-define-joint-law]]

[F3] A finite product of standard-Borel spaces with its finite product sigma-algebra is standard Borel. [[lem-finite-products-of-standard-borel-spaces-are-standard-borel]]

[F4] Under AC, a random element with standard-Borel target has a regular conditional distribution given any sub-sigma-algebra, with probability sections at every sample point. [[thm-existence-of-regular-conditional-distributions-for-standard-borel-targets]]

[F5] Coordinate projections from a product measurable space are measurable. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[A1] AC holds; it is used to apply [F4]. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** construct the canonical joint law, then apply standard-Borel disintegration to the latent coordinate.

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Put $\Xi=H\times\Theta^m$ with its finite product sigma-algebra. The model definition supplies the proper latent prior $P_0$ on $\Xi$ and the probability kernel from $\Xi$ to the product observation space $\mathcal Y$; apply [F2] to obtain the joint probability law $P_0(d\xi)K(\xi,dy)$ on $\Xi\times\mathcal Y$. Let $\Omega=\Xi\times\mathcal Y$ with this law and let $\xi$ and $Y$ be the coordinate projections. By [F5], both projections are measurable. [F1, F2, F5, given]

2.1 The target $\Xi$ is standard Borel by [F3], since $H$ and $\Theta$ are standard Borel and there are finitely many factors. The coordinate map $\xi:\Omega\to\Xi$ is a measurable random element, and $\sigma(Y)$ is a sub-sigma-algebra of the canonical product sigma-algebra. By [F4] under [A1], $\xi$ has a regular conditional distribution given $\sigma(Y)$. The definition of a regular conditional distribution requires every section to be a probability measure, including at exceptional sample points. The theorem asserts no normalization formula on a fixed observation fiber. [A1, F3, F4, step 1.1] ∎
