## Statement

Let $n,q\ge1$, let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be IID with law $P$, let $f,h_1,\ldots,h_q$ be integrable, and let $\theta=E_P h$ be known. Write $\mathcal X_n$ for the sigma-algebra generated by the production sample, namely the smallest sigma-algebra containing each event $\{X_i\in B\}$ for measurable $B$. Let $\mathcal P$ be a sigma-algebra generated by pilot data and assume $\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal X_n$ are independent in the sense of [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]. Let the $\mathcal P$-measurable coefficient $\widehat\beta\in\mathbb R^q$ satisfy $E\|\widehat\beta\|<\infty$. Then the random-coefficient control estimator

$$\widehat\mu_{\widehat\beta}=\frac1n\sum_i\left[f(X_i)-\widehat\beta^{\mathsf T}(h(X_i)-\theta)\right]$$

is integrable and $E[\widehat\mu_{\widehat\beta}\mid\mathcal P]=E_Pf$ almost surely. The first-moment condition on $\widehat\beta$ ensures the displayed estimator is integrable; no claim is made for an arbitrary nonintegrable pilot coefficient.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The IID production sample, integrable target and controls, known control mean, independent pilot sigma-algebra, and integrable pilot coefficient in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-control-variate-estimator]]: With sample means, $\widehat\mu_\beta=\bar f-\beta^{\mathsf T}(\bar h-\theta)$.

[F2] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]: The production sample is mutually independent and identically distributed.

[F3] [[def-conditional-expectation-given-a-sigma-algebra]]: Conditional expectation is characterized by its integral equality on every event in the conditioning sigma-algebra.

[F4] [[thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables]]: Expectations of products of independent integrable real variables factor.

[F5] [[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]: Expectation is linear on integrable finite sums.

[F6] [[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]: Expectations of functions of a variable with law $P$ are integrals against $P$.

[F7] [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]: For events $A\in\mathcal P$ and $B\in\mathcal X_n$, independence means $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$.

## Proof

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 The production sample means are $\mathcal X_n$-measurable and satisfy $E\bar f=E_Pf$ and $E(\bar h-\theta)=0$ by [F2], [F5], and [F6]. Each $\widehat\beta_j$ is $\mathcal P$-measurable; for any Borel sets, its preimage event belongs to $\mathcal P$ while the corresponding production-mean event belongs to $\mathcal X_n$, so [F7] makes it independent of each production mean. Since $E\|\widehat\beta\|<\infty$, [F4] applied to the absolute product makes every $\widehat\beta_j(\bar h_j-\theta_j)$ integrable; the full estimator is therefore integrable. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7]

2.1 Let $A\in\mathcal P$. The random variable $\bar f$ is $\mathcal X_n$-measurable, so its Borel preimage events are independent of $A$ by [F7]; factorization [F4] gives $E[1_A\bar f]=P(A)E_Pf$. Also $1_A\widehat\beta_j$ is $\mathcal P$-measurable and independent of $\bar h_j-\theta_j$, which is $\mathcal X_n$-measurable, so [F4] gives $E[1_A\widehat\beta_j(\bar h_j-\theta_j)]=E[1_A\widehat\beta_j]E(\bar h_j-\theta_j)=0$. By [F1] and [F5], $E[1_A\widehat\mu_{\widehat\beta}]=P(A)E_Pf$. [F1, F4, F5, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 The constant $E_Pf$ is $\mathcal P$-measurable and its integral on every $A\in\mathcal P$ equals the integral of the estimator on $A$ by step 2.1. The defining characterization [F3] therefore gives $E[\widehat\mu_{\widehat\beta}\mid\mathcal P]=E_Pf$ almost surely. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
