## Statement

For every commutative ring $R$, every $q\in R^\times$, and every partition
$\lambda\vdash n$ with row symmetrizer $m_\lambda=x_\lambda$, put
$m_{st}:=T_{d(s)}^*m_\lambda T_{d(t)}$ for standard $\lambda$-tableaux $s,t$,
where $d(t)\in D_\lambda$ is the element with $t^\lambda\cdot d(t)=t$. Then:

1. $\{m_{st}:\lambda\vdash n,\ s,t\in\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)\}$ is an
$R$-basis of $H$ (the **Murphy basis**), and $m_{st}^*=m_{ts}$.

2. The $R$-spans $I^{\unrhd\lambda}$ of $\{m_{uv}:\mu\unrhd\lambda\}$ and
$I^{\rhd\lambda}$ of $\{m_{uv}:\mu\rhd\lambda\}$ are two-sided ideals of $H$
(dominance order), and $I^{\unrhd\lambda}/I^{\rhd\lambda}$ is free with basis
the classes of $\{m_{st}:s,t\in\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)\}$.

3. The **Murphy cell module**
$C_{\mathrm M}(\lambda):=M(\lambda)/(M(\lambda)\cap I^{\rhd\lambda})$ is free
with basis $\{m_t:=x_\lambda T_{d(t)}+I^{\rhd\lambda}:t\in\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)\}$
and satisfies the **cell law**: for $h\in H$ and standard $s,t$,
$$m_{st}h\equiv\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)}r_v\,m_{sv}\pmod{I^{\rhd\lambda}},\qquad r_v\in R,$$
with coefficients $r_v$ independent of $s$.

4. There is a unique $R$-bilinear **Murphy form** $G_\lambda$ on
$C_{\mathrm M}(\lambda)$ with
$m_{us}m_{tv}\equiv G_\lambda(m_s,m_t)m_{uv}\pmod{I^{\rhd\lambda}}$; it is
symmetric, associative in the sense
$G_\lambda(m_sh,m_t)=G_\lambda(m_s,m_th^*)$, and its radical is a submodule of
$C_{\mathrm M}(\lambda)$.

5. The basis, the ideals, the cell law and the form are compatible with every
base change.

The proof also supplies the auxiliary identities used by the comparison:
$\tau(T_w)=\delta_{w,1}$ defines a trace with
$\tau(T_u^*T_v)=\delta_{u,v}q^{\ell(u)}$ (step 1.3); for a composition
$\alpha$ and the partition $\beta$ obtained by sorting its parts,
$x_\alpha=T_dx_\beta T_d^{-1}$ with a block-reordering permutation $d$
(step 2.2); and for the Garnir composition $\alpha$ at a node of $\rho$,
$x_\rho\sum_{d\in S_\alpha\cap D_\rho}T_d
=\sum_{v\in S_\rho\cap D_\alpha}T_v^*x_\alpha$ (step 2.3).
The block-reordering and Garnir data are specified in those steps.

In particular $C_{\mathrm M}(\lambda)$ is finite free of rank
$f^\lambda=|\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)|$. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, $q\in R^\times$, $n\ge0$, the Hecke algebra $H=H_{R,q}(S_n)$ with standard basis $\{T_w\}$, star anti-automorphism $T_w^*=T_{w^{-1}}$ and sign automorphism $\theta(T_i)=(q-1)-T_i$; for every partition or composition $\rho$ of $n$ the row module $M(\rho)=x_\rho H$ with its distinguished basis, the three-case action rule and the identities $x_\rho T_w=T_wx_\rho=q^{\ell(w)}x_\rho$ for $w\in S_\rho$; and the partitions of $n$ ordered by dominance with conjugate $\rho'$.

[F1] Multiplication rules: $T_wT_i=T_{ws_i}$ if $\ell(ws_i)=\ell(w)+1$ and $T_wT_i=(q-1)T_w+qT_{ws_i}$ if $\ell(ws_i)=\ell(w)-1$; the mirror rule $T_iT_w=T_{s_iw}$ respectively $T_iT_w=(q-1)T_w+qT_{s_iw}$ holds on the left, and $\{T_w\}$ is an $R$-basis ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]], [[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] Row modules: for every composition $\alpha$, $x_\alpha H$ is free on $\{b_u=x_\alpha T_{d_\alpha(u)}:u\text{ row standard}\}$, with the three-case action and $x_\alpha T_w=T_wx_\alpha=q^{\ell(w)}x_\alpha$ for $w\in S_\alpha$ ([[lem-dj-bridge-row-permutation-module-standard-action]]).

[F3] Dominance and the RSK count: for partitions of $n$, $\unrhd$ is a partial order, dominance implies lexicographic order, and $\sum_{\rho\vdash n}(f^\rho)^2=n!$ with $f^\rho=|\operatorname{Std}(\rho)|$ ([[def-dominance-order-on-partitions]], [[cor-sum-of-squares-of-standard-tableau-numbers]]).

[F4] Opposite-dominance vanishing: if a partition $\kappa$ is not dominated by $\rho'$, then $x_\kappa Hy_\rho=0$ ([[lem-dj-bridge-parabolic-double-coset-annihilation]]); and $\theta(x_\rho)=q^{\ell(w_0(S_\rho))}y_\rho$ with $\theta$ an involutive automorphism commuting with $*$ ([[lem-dj-bridge-sign-automorphism-and-star-dual]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Compositions.** A composition of $n$ is a finite sequence $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_k)$ of nonnegative integers with $\sum\alpha_i=n$; its row blocks $B_r$, row group $S_\alpha$, row-filled tableau $t^\alpha$, row-standard tableaux, set $D_\alpha$, distinguished elements $d_\alpha(u)$, and elements $x_\alpha=\sum_{w\in S_\alpha}T_w$, $b_u:=x_\alpha T_{d_\alpha(u)}$ are defined exactly as for partitions in [[def-dj-bridge-row-column-permutation-modules-and-polytabloids]], and for row-standard $\alpha$-tableaux $s,t$ put $m^\alpha_{st}:=T_{d_\alpha(s)}^*x_\alpha T_{d_\alpha(t)}$, so that $b_t=m^\alpha_{t^\alpha t}$. Every statement of [F2] was proved for partitions but uses only that the row group is a product of symmetric groups on consecutive blocks, so it applies verbatim to compositions. [F2, algebra]

1.2 **Dominance of tableaux by counting.** For $\alpha$-tableaux $u,v$ and integers $m\ge0$, $k\ge1$ put $c_{m,k}(u):=\#\{x\in B_1\cup\cdots\cup B_k:u(x)\le m\}$; write $u\unrhd v$ when $c_{m,k}(u)\ge c_{m,k}(v)$ for all $m,k$, and $u\equiv v$ when all these numbers agree. Passing from $u$ to $u\cdot s_i$ exchanges the labels $i$ and $i+1$, so it changes only the counts at $m=i$, and $c_{i,k}(u\cdot s_i)=c_{i-1,k}(u)+[i+1\text{ lies in the first }k\text{ blocks of }u]$ because the label $i$ is replaced by $i+1$ and all other labels $\le i$ stay put. In particular $u\equiv v$ implies $u\cdot s_i\equiv v\cdot s_i$, and if $u,v$ are both row standard then $u\equiv v$ if and only if $u=v$, because the numbers $c_{m,k}$ determine the multiset of entries of each block and a row-standard tableau is its block multisets sorted increasingly. Suppose now that $s$ is row standard, that $s':=s\cdot s_i$ is row standard, and that the labels $i,i+1$ lie in rows $a<b$ of $s$, so that $s'$ is obtained from $s$ by moving $i$ down into row $b$ and $i+1$ up into row $a$. Then for every row-standard $r$ with $r\unrhd s$ one has $r\unrhd s'$ and $r\cdot s_i\unrhd s'$. Indeed for $m\ne i$ the counts are unchanged by both swaps, so $c_{m,k}(r)\ge c_{m,k}(s)=c_{m,k}(s')$ and the same for $r\cdot s_i$; for $m=i$ one has $c_{i,k}(s')=c_{i-1,k}(s)+[k\ge b]$, while $c_{i,k}(r)\ge c_{i,k}(s)\ge c_{i-1,k}(s)+[k\ge b]=c_{i,k}(s')$ for every $k$, and $c_{i,k}(r\cdot s_i)=c_{i-1,k}(r)+[i+1\text{ lies in the first }k\text{ blocks of }r]\ge c_{i-1,k}(r)\ge c_{i-1,k}(s)=c_{i,k}(s')$ for $k<b$, while for $k\ge b$ the sharper bound $c_{i,k}(r\cdot s_i)\ge c_{i+1,k}(r)-1\ge c_{i+1,k}(s)-1=c_{i-1,k}(s)+1=c_{i,k}(s')$ holds, the first inequality by $[i\text{ lies in the first }k\text{ blocks of }r]\le1$ and the last because both labels then lie in the first $k$ blocks of $s$. [F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 **The trace pairing.** Define $\tau:H\to R$ by $\tau(T_w)=\delta_{w,1}$. The multiplication rules give $\tau(T_wT_i)=\tau(T_iT_w)=q\delta_{w,s_i}$ for every $w$ and every generator $T_i$: in the two-case formulas the identity $T_1$ occurs only in the descent case $w=s_i$, with coefficient $q$. Moving generator letters across one another successively gives $\tau(ab)=\tau(ba)$ for all $a,b\in H$, and $\tau(a^*)=\tau(a)$ since $\tau$ is $R$-linear and $*$ permutes the standard basis. Let $B_0$ be the bilinear form defined on the standard basis by $B_0(T_u,T_v):=\delta_{u,v}q^{\ell(u)}$. Every right $s_i$-orbit $\{T_w,T_{ws_i}\}$ has two elements; writing $w$ for the element with $\ell(ws_i)=\ell(w)+1$, right multiplication by $T_i$ on the block spanned by the orbit has matrix $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&q\\1&q-1\end{pmatrix}$, and the restriction of $B_0$ to the block has matrix $D=\operatorname{diag}(q^{\ell(w)},q^{\ell(w)+1})$ with $A^{\mathsf T}D=DA$; distinct blocks are orthogonal, so $B_0(xT_i,y)=B_0(x,yT_i)$ for all $x,y\in H$. Iterating along a reduced word $T_u=T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_r}$ gives $B_0(T_u,T_v)=B_0(T_1,T_vT_{i_r}\cdots T_{i_1})=B_0(T_1,T_vT_u^*)$, and $B_0(T_1,y)=\tau(y)$ for every $y$ by the definitions; hence $B_0(T_u,T_v)=\tau(T_vT_u^*)=\tau(T_{u^{-1}}T_v)=\delta_{u,v}q^{\ell(u)}$. In particular the bilinear form $(a,b)\mapsto\tau(a^*b)$ equals $B_0$ and has diagonal Gram matrix with unit entries $q^{\ell(u)}$ in the standard basis, so it is unimodular. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 **The support lemma.** Let $g$ be a row-standard $\alpha$-tableau, let $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_r}$ be a reduced word, put $t:=g\cdot w$ and $g_k:=g\cdot(s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k})$, and assume $\ell(d_\alpha(t))=\ell(d_\alpha(g))+\ell(w)$. If $v$ is a row-standard $\alpha$-tableau with $v\rhd g$, then every row-standard tableau $u$ whose basis element $b_u$ is involved in $b_vT_w$ satisfies $u\rhd t$. The assumed length equality makes the concatenation of a reduced word for $d_\alpha(g)$ and the displayed word for $w$ reduced, since $d_\alpha(t)=d_\alpha(g)w$. Starting at the row-standard $t$ and removing its final letters backwards, each removed letter is a right descent. A right descent exchanges consecutive labels in reversed row-reading order; they cannot lie in one increasing row, so the swap preserves row standardness by [F2]. Thus every $g_k$ is row standard, $d_\alpha(g_k)=d_\alpha(g)s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$, and $\ell(d_\alpha(g_k))=\ell(d_\alpha(g))+k$. Hence each step replaces $g_k$ by the row-standard tableau $g_k\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}$ with $\ell(d_\alpha(g_k\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}))=\ell(d_\alpha(g_k))+1$, so the three-case rule of [F2] gives $b_{g_k}T_{i_{k+1}}=b_{g_{k+1}}$: the swapped tableau is row standard and the length increases. The labels $i,i+1$ therefore lie in different rows of $g_k$ (otherwise the swap would stay in one row and the rule would return a multiple of $b_{g_k}$), and since $g_{k+1}$ is row standard with $\ell(d_\alpha(g_k)s_{i_{k+1}})=\ell(d_\alpha(g_k))+1$, the row-reading position of $i$ precedes that of $i+1$, so $i$ lies in a strictly upper row of $g_k$; this is exactly the configuration of step 1.2. We now show by induction on $k$ that every row-standard $u$ with $b_u$ involved in $b_vT_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k}$ satisfies $u\unrhd g_k$ and $u\not\equiv g_k$. For $k=0$ we have $v\unrhd g$ and $v\ne g$, and $v\equiv g$ would give $g\unrhd v$, so $v\not\equiv g$. For the step, the action rule of [F2] shows that $b_uT_{i_{k+1}}$ is a combination of $b_u$ and, when $u\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}$ is row standard, $b_{u\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}}$; step 1.2 applied to $s:=g_k$, $s'=g_{k+1}$ gives $u\unrhd g_{k+1}$ and $u\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}\unrhd g_{k+1}$ whenever the swapped tableau is row standard. Neither output is $\equiv g_{k+1}$: if $u\equiv g_{k+1}$ then $u\unrhd g_k\unrhd g_{k+1}\equiv u$ forces $u\equiv g_k$, and if $u\cdot s_{i_{k+1}}\equiv g_{k+1}$ then applying the swap to the two equivalent tableaux gives $u\equiv g_k$, both contradicting the induction hypothesis. At the end $t$ is row standard, so $u\unrhd t$ together with $u\not\equiv t$ says exactly that $u\rhd t$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 **Compositions to partitions.** Let $\alpha$ be a composition and $\beta$ the partition obtained by rearranging its parts. Choose $d$ mapping each row block of $\beta$ increasingly onto a row block of $\alpha$ of the same size. Then $d^{-1}$ is increasing on each $\alpha$-block, $d$ is increasing on each $\beta$-block, $d\in D_\alpha$, $d^{-1}\in D_\beta$, and $d^{-1}S_\alpha d=S_\beta$. Hence $x_\alpha T_d=\sum_{w\in S_\alpha}T_{wd}=\sum_{u\in S_\alpha d}T_u$ and $T_dx_\beta=\sum_{v\in S_\beta}T_{dv}=\sum_{u\in dS_\beta}T_u$, and these agree because $S_\alpha d=d(d^{-1}S_\alpha d)=dS_\beta$. Thus $x_\alpha=T_dx_\beta T_d^{-1}$ and $T_d$ is invertible. Since $Hx_\beta$ is free with basis $\{T_{d_\beta(s)}^*x_\beta\}$ (the star of the basis of $x_\beta H$) and $x_\beta H$ is free with basis $\{x_\beta T_{d_\beta(u)}\}$ by [F2], expanding $T_{d_\alpha(s)}^*T_d$ and $T_d^{-1}T_{d_\alpha(t)}$ expresses $m^\alpha_{st}$ as an $R$-linear combination of $m^\beta_{uv}$ with $u,v$ row standard; all coefficients are Laurent polynomials in $q$, with no quantum factorial inverted. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

2.3 **The Garnir belt identity.** Let $\rho$ be a partition and $(i,j)$ a node with $j\le\rho_{i+1}$. Let $\alpha$ replace the two consecutive parts $\rho_i,\rho_{i+1}$ by $j-1,\rho_i+1,\rho_{i+1}-j$, deleting zeros; its blocks are those of $\rho$ except that the belt $a,\dots,b$, where $a=t^\rho(i,j)$ and $b=t^\rho(i+1,j)$, is cut into three consecutive blocks of sizes $j-1$, $\rho_i+1$ and $\rho_{i+1}-j$; the middle block is the interval $a,\dots,b$ itself, of size $\rho_i+1=b-a+1$. Put $D:=S_\alpha\cap D_\rho$ and $E:=S_\rho\cap D_\alpha$. Then $$x_\rho\sum_{d\in D}T_d=\sum_{v\in E}T_v^*x_\alpha.$$ Put $K=S_\rho\cap S_\alpha$. Sorting a permutation of $S_\alpha$ within each $\rho$-block preserves the $\alpha$-blocks, because every intersection of the two interval blocks is an interval. The unique sorted result is in $D$, so $D$ is a transversal for the right cosets $K\backslash S_\alpha$; likewise $E$ is a transversal for $K\backslash S_\rho$. Two products $ud$ and $u'd'$ coincide only if $d,d'$ lie in the same $K$-coset, which forces $d=d'$ and then $u=u'$. Inverting the representatives in $E$ gives the corresponding transversal for the cosets of $K$ on the other side. Thus the products $S_\rho\times D\to S_\rho S_\alpha$, $(u,d)\mapsto ud$, and $E\times S_\alpha\to S_\rho S_\alpha$, $(v,u)\mapsto v^{-1}u$, are bijections with length-additive products, because $D\subseteq D_\rho$, $E\subseteq D_\alpha$ and $\ell(u^{-1}v)=\ell(v^{-1}u)$ for all $u\in S_\alpha$, $v\in D_\alpha$; expanding both sides in the standard basis therefore gives $\sum_{w\in S_\rho S_\alpha}T_w$ for the left side and the same sum for the right side, since $T_v^*=T_{v^{-1}}$. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

3.1 **The Garnir tableau.** With $\rho,\alpha,(i,j)$ as in step 2.3, let $g$ be the row-standard tableau agreeing with $t^\rho$ outside the belt $a,\dots,b$, whose row $i+1$ carries the $j$ smallest belt entries increasingly in its first $j$ positions and whose row $i$ carries the remaining belt entries increasingly in its last $\rho_i-j+1$ positions. Among the tableaux $t^\rho\cdot d$, $d\in D$, exactly one fails to be standard, namely $g$, and every other one strictly dominates $g$: each of them agrees with $t^\rho$ outside the belt and is determined by the $j$-element subset $S$ of belt entries that it places increasingly in the first $j$ positions of row $i+1$, the complementary belt entries occupying increasingly the last $\rho_i-j+1$ positions of row $i$; column $j$ fails exactly when $\min(\mathrm{belt}\setminus S)>\max(S)$, that is, when $S$ consists of the $j$ smallest belt entries, which is $g$. For the dominance comparison, the cumulative counts $c_{m,k}$ of step 1.2 for $k\le i-1$ and $k\ge i+1$ do not depend on $S$; for $k=i$ row $i$ carries the belt complement of $S$, and $g$ carries the largest $\rho_i-j+1$ belt entries, whose count function is pointwise minimal, so every other belt tableau strictly dominates $g$. [step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 **The straightening identity.** Let $\nu$ be the partition obtained by sorting the parts of $\alpha$. Rewriting the left-hand side of the belt identity of step 2.3 as row-module basis elements through step 3.1, and applying step 2.2 to its right-hand side, the belt identity becomes $$x_\rho T_{d(g)}=h-\sum_{d\in D,\ d\ne d(g)}x_\rho T_{d},$$ where $h$ is an $R$-linear combination of elements $T_{d_\nu(s')}^*x_\nu T_{d_\nu(t')}$ with $s',t'$ row standard, and the tableaux $t^\rho\cdot d$ with $d\in D$, $d\ne d(g)$, are all standard and strictly more dominant than $g$; here $\nu$ is strictly greater than $\rho$ in lexicographic order because the part $\rho_i+1$ exceeds $\rho_i$ while all earlier parts are retained and sorting can only move such an increase earlier. [step 2.2, step 2.3, step 3.1]

4.2 **Length-additive reduction to a Garnir tableau.** Suppose the row-standard $\rho$-tableau $t$ violates column order at $(i,j)$, so $t(i,j)>t(i+1,j)$. Order the cells partially by their left-to-right order within each row and the extra relation $(i+1,j)<(i,j)$. The cells in row $i+1$ through column $j$ must then precede every cell in row $i$ from column $j$ onward. The label order of $t$ is a linear extension of this partial order. The label order of the Garnir tableau $g$ in step 3.1 is the row-reading order with precisely these two consecutive groups interchanged. Its inverted cell pairs relative to row reading are exactly the forced pairs between these groups. Sort the label-ordered cell list of $t$ into the label order of $g$ by swapping adjacent entries that occur in the opposite order in $g$. The swapped entries are incomparable in the partial order, because both lists are linear extensions; thus every intermediate list remains a linear extension and its tableau remains row standard with the chosen column violation. Each swapped pair is reversed relative to row reading, since the only reversals of $g$ are forced pairs which cannot be reversed in $t$. Consequently every swap exchanges consecutive labels at a right descent of the distinguished permutation and decreases its inversion length by one. The finite sorting ends at $g$. Reversing the $r$ swaps gives $t=g\cdot w$ and $d(t)=d(g)w$, with $\ell(d(t))=\ell(d(g))+r$. The triangle inequality gives $r\le\ell(w)$, while its $r$-letter expression gives $\ell(w)\le r$; hence $\ell(d(t))=\ell(d(g))+\ell(w)$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F1, F2, algebra]

5.1 **Two-index straightening.** Every $m^\rho_{st}$ with $s,t$ row standard and $t$ non-standard is an $R$-linear combination of elements $m^{\rho_0}_{uv}$ with either $\rho_0\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho$, or $\rho_0=\rho$ and $u\unrhd s$, $v\rhd t$. Indeed, writing $t=g\cdot w$ with $\ell(d(t))=\ell(d(g))+\ell(w)$ at an offending Garnir node by step 4.2, multiply the identity of step 4.1 on the left by $T_{d(s)}^*$ and on the right by $T_w$; step 2.1 pushes every same-shape term strictly above $t$ while the higher-shape terms are handled by descending lexicographic induction, in all same-shape terms this right-index reduction retains the first index $s$, because only the row-module identity $x_\rho T_{d(t)}$ is straightened. After the second index is standard, the left index is treated symmetrically using $m^\rho_{st}=m^\rho_{ts}{}^*$, and the finite dominance order on row-standard tableaux makes the inner induction terminate. Composing with step 2.2, every $m^\alpha_{st}$ with $\alpha$ a composition is a combination of partition-indexed $m^\nu_{uv}$ with $u,v$ standard. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 4.2]

6.1 **The Murphy basis.** For $\alpha=(1^n)$ one has $x_{(1^n)}=1$, so every standard basis element is a Murphy element and the standard Murphy family spans $H$ by step 5.1. Its cardinality is $\sum_\rho(f^\rho)^2=n!$ by [F3], equal to the rank of the free module $H$, so writing the family in the standard basis gives a square matrix that, having a right inverse, has unit determinant; hence the family including the empty shape is an $R$-basis of $H$, and $m_{st}^*=m_{ts}$ follows from $x_\rho^*=x_\rho$ and [F1]. This is clause (1). [step 5.1, F3]

7.1 **Lexicographic ideals and the first-index law.** Let $J^{\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho}$ be the span of all $m_{uv}$ whose shape is strictly lexicographically larger than $\rho$. Expand $x_\rho T_{d(t)}h$ in the row-module basis of [F2] and multiply on the left by $T_{d(s)}^*$. Step 5.1 straightens the resulting row-standard Murphy elements using only shapes $\unrhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho$; applying $*$ gives the analogous assertion for left multiplication. Since the family is a basis by step 6.1, the subspaces $J^{\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho}$ are two-sided ideals, and for standard $s,t$ and $h\in H$ one has $$m_{st}h\equiv\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)}r_{tv}(h)\,m_{sv}\pmod{J^{\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho}}$$ with coefficients independent of the first index $s$, because right straightening retains the first index and the same-shape coefficients of step 5.1 depend only on the column index. [step 5.1, step 6.1]

7.2 **The mixed pairing and its diagonal.** For partitions $\eta,\nu$ and standard tableaux index the matrix $Q_{(\eta,s,t),(\nu,a,b)}:=\tau(m^\eta_{st}\theta(m^{\nu'}_{ab}))$. By [F4] and step 1.3, $Q$ vanishes unless $\eta\unlhd\nu$: cyclically permuting the trace moves the factors until $x_\eta$ meets $H\theta(x_{\nu'})$, and $\theta(x_{\nu'})=q^{N}y_{\nu'}$ while $x_\eta Hy_{\nu'}=0$ unless $\eta$ is dominated by $(\nu')'=\nu$. Ordering shapes by lexicographic order, $Q$ is block upper triangular with square diagonal blocks, since transposition bijects $\operatorname{Std}(\nu)$ with $\operatorname{Std}(\nu')$ and $f^\nu=f^{\nu'}$; and $Q$ is invertible because by step 1.3 it is the matrix of the unimodular pairing $\tau(a^*b)$ between two bases of $H$, the standard Murphy basis and the $\theta$-image of the standard Murphy basis of conjugate shapes. Hence every diagonal block is perfect over $R$, and this remains true after every base change. [step 1.3, step 6.1, F4]

8.1 **Dominance ideals.** Let $\kappa$ be a partition and $h\in Hx_\kappa H$. If $\nu$ does not dominate $\kappa$, then $\tau(h\theta(m^{\nu'}_{ab}))=0$ for all $a,b$: cyclic permutation of the trace moves the factors of $h$ until $x_\kappa$ is followed by $H\theta(x_{\nu'})$, that is, by a multiple of $Hy_{\nu'}$, and $x_\kappa Hy_{\nu'}=0$ by [F4] because $\kappa$ is not dominated by $(\nu')'=\nu$. Expanding $h$ in the lex Murphy basis and inspecting shapes in increasing lexicographic order, only shapes $\eta\unlhd\nu$ in dominance pair nontrivially against the column labelled $\nu$ by step 7.2; for $\eta\ne\nu$ these shapes are lexicographically smaller and do not dominate $\kappa$, since $\kappa\unlhd\eta\unlhd\nu$ would contradict $\kappa\ntrianglelefteq\nu$, so their coefficients have already vanished; the perfect diagonal block at $\nu$ then forces all coefficients of shape $\nu$ to vanish. Therefore $Hx_\kappa H$ lies in the span of the Murphy elements of shapes $\unrhd\kappa$; since conversely $m^\eta_{ab}\in Hx_\eta H$ by definition, $I^{\unrhd\kappa}=\sum_{\eta\unrhd\kappa}Hx_\eta H$ and $I^{\rhd\kappa}=\sum_{\eta\rhd\kappa}Hx_\eta H$. Both are therefore two-sided ideals of $H$. [step 7.2, F4]

9.1 **The cell law and the cell module.** Let $s,t$ be standard $\rho$-tableaux and $h\in H$. Since $x_\rho T_{d(t)}h\in M(\rho)=\bigoplus_v R\,x_\rho T_{d(v)}$ with $v$ running over the row-standard $\rho$-tableaux, there are unique $c_{tv}(h)\in R$ with $x_\rho T_{d(t)}h=\sum_v c_{tv}(h)x_\rho T_{d(v)}$; multiplying on the left by $T_{d(s)}^*$ gives the exact identity $m_{st}h=\sum_v c_{tv}(h)m_{sv}$, whose coefficients do not involve $s$. For $v$ non-standard, step 5.1 writes $m_{sv}=\sum_{w\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)}b_{vw}m_{sw}+j_v$ with $b_{vw}\in R$ independent of the first index $s$ and $j_v\in J^{\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho}$; step 8.1 puts $m_{sv}$ and the $m_{sw}$ in $I^{\unrhd\rho}$, so $j_v\in J^{\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho}\cap I^{\unrhd\rho}$. Both spans are spanned by Murphy basis elements, of shapes $\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho$ and $\unrhd\rho$ respectively, and the Murphy basis is independent, so their intersection is spanned by the basis elements whose shapes satisfy both conditions; a shape $\mu\ne\rho$ satisfying $\mu\unrhd\rho$ and $\mu\rhd_{\mathrm{lex}}\rho$ is exactly a shape $\mu\rhd\rho$. Hence $j_v\in I^{\rhd\rho}$ and $$m_{st}h\equiv\sum_{v\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)}r_{tv}(h)\,m_{sv}\pmod{I^{\rhd\rho}}$$ with coefficients $r_{tv}(h)$ independent of the first index. By step 6.1 the quotient $I^{\unrhd\rho}/I^{\rhd\rho}$ is free with basis the classes of $\{m_{uv}:u,v\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)\}$, and $x_\rho T_{d(v)}=m^\rho_{t^\rho v}$; the congruence with $s=t^\rho$ shows that the image of $M(\rho)$ in this quotient is spanned by the $f^\rho$ independent classes $m_t=x_\rho T_{d(t)}+I^{\rhd\rho}$, with kernel $M(\rho)\cap I^{\rhd\rho}$. Therefore $C_{\mathrm M}(\rho)=M(\rho)/(M(\rho)\cap I^{\rhd\rho})$ is free with basis $\{m_t:t\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)\}$ and satisfies the displayed cell law. This is clauses (2) and (3). 10.1 **The cell form.** Let $u,v,s,t\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)$. Applying the cell law of step 9.1 to $m_{us}m_{tv}$ with $h=m_{tv}$ gives $m_{us}m_{tv}\equiv\sum_w r_{sw}(m_{tv})m_{uw}$, and applying it to $m_{vt}m_{su}=m_{tv}^*m_{us}^*$ with $h=m_{su}$ and starring gives $m_{us}m_{tv}\equiv\sum_w r_{tw}(m_{su})m_{wv}$. Comparing coefficients in the basis $\{m_{ab}:a,b\in\operatorname{Std}(\rho)\}$ of $I^{\unrhd\rho}/I^{\rhd\rho}$ yields $r_{sw}(m_{tv})=0$ for $w\ne v$ and $r_{sv}(m_{tv})=r_{tu}(m_{su})$, a scalar $G_\rho(m_s,m_t)\in R$ independent of $u$ and $v$; hence $m_{us}m_{tv}\equiv G_\rho(m_s,m_t)m_{uv}\pmod{I^{\rhd\rho}}$, and $G_\rho$ is the unique bilinear form with this property. Starring the defining congruence and exchanging $(s,t)$ with $(u,v)$ gives $G_\rho(m_t,m_s)=G_\rho(m_s,m_t)$. Comparing the two bracketings of $m_{us}hm_{tv}$ through step 9.1 gives $G_\rho(m_sh,m_t)=G_\rho(m_s,m_th^*)$, which shows that the radical of $G_\rho$ is a submodule of $C_{\mathrm M}(\rho)$. Every coefficient is a Laurent polynomial in $q$ obtained from the finite multiplication rules, so the form and all clauses survive every base change; the form may be degenerate and no determinant or quantum factorial is inverted, and for $n=0,1$ the unique basis vector has form $1$. This is clauses (4) and (5), and since only the presentation of $H$, inversion counting, the local double-coset vanishing, the trace computation and finite free duality were used, no choice principle is used.
