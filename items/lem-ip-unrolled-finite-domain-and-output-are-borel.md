## Statement

Let $n,m\ge1$, $X=\mathbb R^n$, and let $\Omega$ be a Borel subset of $\mathbb R^{m\times n}\times\mathbb R^m$ with its relative Borel sigma-algebra. Equip $\Omega\times X$ with the finite product sigma-algebra $\mathcal B(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal B(X)$. Fix an integer $N\ge0$, real parameters $\tau_0,\ldots,\tau_{N-1}$, a Borel initialization $I:\Omega\to X$, Borel sets $D_k\subseteq\Omega\times X$, and Borel maps $B_k:D_k\to X$ for $0\le k<N$, where each $D_k$ carries its relative product sigma-algebra. For $p=(A,y)\in\Omega$ and $x\in X$, define $c(p,x)=A^{\mathsf T}(y-Ax)$. Set $E_0=\Omega$ and $x_0=I$; recursively, for $0\le k<N$, set

$$E_{k+1}:=\{p\in E_k:(p,x_k(p)+\tau_k c(p,x_k(p)))\in D_k\},\qquad x_{k+1}(p):=B_k(p,x_k(p)+\tau_k c(p,x_k(p)))\quad(p\in E_{k+1}).$$

Then every $E_k$ is a Borel subset of $\Omega$ and every $x_k:E_k\to X$ is Borel. In particular, $E_N$ is exactly the set of inputs where all $N$ updates are defined, and the finite output map $x_N:E_N\to X$ is Borel.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional Borel spaces, Borel update domains and maps, Borel initialization, fixed finite layer parameters, and the recursive definitions in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: the product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles.

[F2] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]: a map is measurable when the preimage of each measurable codomain set is measurable.

[F3] [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]: a continuous map between topological spaces has Borel preimages of Borel sets.

[F4] [[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]: the composition of measurable maps between measurable spaces is measurable.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** induction.

1.1 The spaces $\Omega$ and $X$ are second-countable subspaces of finite-dimensional Euclidean spaces, so rectangles from countable bases form a countable base for $\Omega\times X$. Every open set is therefore a countable union of open rectangles and belongs to $\mathcal B(\Omega)\otimes\mathcal B(X)$ by [F1]. Conversely, for Borel $C\subseteq\Omega$ and $F\subseteq X$, $C\times F=\pi_1^{-1}(C)\cap\pi_2^{-1}(F)$ is Borel in the product topology because the coordinate projections are continuous and [F3] applies. Since such rectangles generate the product sigma-algebra by [F1], the product sigma-algebra is the Borel sigma-algebra of the product topology. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 The base domain $E_0=\Omega$ is Borel in itself, and the initial map $x_0=I$ is Borel by the Given clause. [given, base]

1.3 Fix $k<N$ and assume as the induction hypothesis that $E_k$ is Borel and $x_k:E_k\to X$ is Borel. Define $\iota_k:E_k\to\Omega\times X$ by $\iota_k(p)=(p,x_k(p))$. For every measurable rectangle $C\times F$, [F2] and the induction hypothesis give $\iota_k^{-1}(C\times F)=(E_k\cap C)\cap x_k^{-1}(F)$ Borel in $E_k$. The rectangles generate the target sigma-algebra by [F1], so preimages preserve the generated sigma-algebra and $\iota_k$ is Borel. [F1, F2, given, ih, assume-hyp, algebra]

2.1 Define $\Gamma_k(A,y,x)=(A,y,x+\tau_kA^{\mathsf T}(y-Ax))$. Each coordinate of its last component is a polynomial in the matrix, data, and state coordinates, so $\Gamma_k$ is continuous. By [F3] it is Borel; hence $\psi_k:=\Gamma_k\circ\iota_k$ is Borel by [F4]. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 The next domain is $E_{k+1}=\psi_k^{-1}(D_k)$. Since $D_k$ is Borel and $\psi_k$ is Borel, [F2] makes $E_{k+1}$ Borel in $E_k$; because $E_k$ is Borel in $\Omega$, $E_{k+1}$ is Borel in $\Omega$. [F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The restriction $\psi_k|_{E_{k+1}}:E_{k+1}\to D_k$ is Borel: the preimage of each relative-measurable set $D_k\cap H$ is $E_{k+1}\cap\psi_k^{-1}(H)$, which is Borel. Since $B_k:D_k\to X$ is Borel, [F4] makes $x_{k+1}=B_k\circ(\psi_k|_{E_{k+1}})$ Borel. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, given]

5.1 Step 1.2 supplies the base case, and steps 1.3, 2.1, 3.1, and 4.1 prove the implication from any Borel pair $(E_k,x_k)$ to $(E_{k+1},x_{k+1})$. Finite induction gives the claim for every $k\le N$. By its recursive definition, $E_N$ contains exactly the inputs at which each successive update is defined, and step 4.1 gives Borel measurability of the final output. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, discharge-induction: step 1.3] ∎
