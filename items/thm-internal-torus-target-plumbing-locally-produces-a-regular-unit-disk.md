## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $u_0$ be an actual integrable unit-domain disk for a PROPER planar attaching cut component $A$ in a closed surface $\Sigma$, satisfying the complete moving-slit kernel hypotheses. Suppose its actual scalar pole space is $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p_0\}$. In particular the disk is node-fixed regular of index two, every internal circle is active, inactive roots miss its active image, and its marked roots are not slit tips. Choose an interior root point $q$ outside all cover branch values and normalize the physical source by a positive affine map so $p_0(q)=i$. Fix a holomorphic target coordinate $\xi$ centered at $q$, and put $A_0=\partial_\xi p_0(q)\ne0$.

Take a flat complex torus $T$, a straight analytic nonseparating circle $\gamma\subset T$, a point $x\in\gamma$, and $a\in T\setminus\gamma$. Choose disjoint flat coordinates $\eta_a,\eta_x$ at $a,x$ by translations of ONE common flat torus coordinate, with $\gamma$ real near $x$. Form the actual complex target $\Sigma_\epsilon$ by plumbing $\xi\eta_a=\epsilon$. Retain all old attaching circles and add $\gamma$, with the fixed new marked tuple equal to the old tuple plus $x$. Choose a fixed phase for $\epsilon=\rho e^{i\theta}$ such that $\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}A_0)\ne0$.

For every sufficiently small $\rho>0$ there exists an actual smooth integrable unit-domain disk $u_\rho$ into $\operatorname{Sym}^{m+1}\Sigma_\epsilon$ for the corresponding enlarged planar cut component. Every internal circle is active, including the new one. Its new slit lies in the fixed $\eta_x$ chart and is described by the exact rational model with parameter
$$\lambda=-\epsilon A_0+O(|\epsilon|^2).$$
Its marked root $x$ is a regular slit-bank point. The actual node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ operator is onto of index two. Modulo positive affine source maps, this is the UNIQUE solution in the matched local response neighborhood of the split old component, constant torus sheet and rational disk sheet constructed in the proof. The family and every fixed-order local coefficient and incidence chart depend smoothly on $\rho>0$.

This is local existence, regularity and local uniqueness. It does not assert that every unit disk for $\Sigma_\epsilon$ enters this response neighborhood as $\epsilon\to0$, so it supplies no global count or target-degeneration exhaustion. No transverse split torus operator is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual old disk with verified two-dimensional scalar kernel, proper cut component, marked banks, flat torus and target plumbing data, and the fixed nondegenerate target phase in the Statement.

[F1] The moving-slit theorem identifies actual node-fixed kernels with the real simple-pole scalar space and derives regularity from a two-dimensional scalar kernel. Its nonlinear response theorem constructs the actual old localized response with moving endpoints and a fixed regular value at $q$, by solving the genuine disk section. ([[lem-unit-incidence-disk-linearization-is-the-moving-slit-pole-problem]], [[lem-regular-unit-disks-have-nonlinear-localized-incidence-responses-with-moving-slits]])

[F2] The actual flat-torus Fourier inverse solves $\bar\partial$ with zero integral, with bounded higher norms; it is twice the stated inverse of $2\bar\partial$. The round planar response supplies the disk real-boundary simple-pole inverse normalized at $i$. ([[lem-flat-complex-torus-cauchy-riemann-mean-inverse-and-normalized-dipoles]], [[lem-round-planar-real-boundary-pole-problem-has-a-bounded-normalized-response]])

[F3] The exact rational function $h_\lambda(z)=\lambda/(z-i)+\overline\lambda/(z+i)$ maps $\mathbb H$ biholomorphically onto the sphere minus its real slit, with $h_\lambda(\infty)=0$ and a regular marked bank if $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ne0$. Its slit endpoints are $-\operatorname{Im}\lambda\pm|\lambda|$, and the endpoint-coordinate Jacobian on each fixed bank is nonzero when $\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ne0$. ([[lem-real-boundary-simple-pole-model-parametrizes-a-constant-sheet-slit]])

[F4] Explicit Picard/Neumann and quantitative Newton constructions give parameter-smooth fixed points under Countable Choice. Coefficient contour integrals construct smooth symmetric fibers at branch points, and the actual unit-disk index is two. ([[lem-countable-choice-quantitative-newton-correction-and-smooth-zero-chart]], [[thm-cauchy-integral-formula-circle]], [[lem-integrable-planar-unit-domain-boundary-disks-have-index-two]])

## Proof

1.1 Choose a radius $r>0$ so the four coordinate disks at $q,a,x,i$ and their required buffered disks are disjoint where appropriate and have the actual charts just specified. On the disk sheet use the coordinate $$\tau_\lambda(z)=\lambda/h_\lambda(z)=\frac{z-i}{1+(\overline\lambda/\lambda)(z-i)/(2i+z-i)}.$$ It has derivative one at $i$ and is a holomorphic coordinate on a fixed disk there, uniformly for all phases of $\lambda\ne0$ after decreasing $r$. When $\lambda=\epsilon L$ with $L$ near the nonzero number $-A_0$, it is smooth with bounded derivatives in $L$ and $\rho$; its phase ratio is $\overline\epsilon\,\overline L/(\epsilon L)$ and the target phase is fixed. The second exact SOURCE plumbing is $\eta_x\tau_\lambda=\lambda$. Consequently the root maps on the torus and disk pieces agree EXACTLY there: $\eta_x=h_\lambda(z)$. The first source plumbing is the actual target relation $\xi\eta_a=\epsilon$. The old response in [F1] keeps the target-root coordinate $\xi$ near $q$ fixed while allowing its remote slit endpoints to move. These relations therefore define genuine complex source plumbings with smooth endpoint dependence; no approximate root-map matching is involved. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Construct all nonzero-mode responses explicitly. Fix a Hardy Sobolev order $s$ large enough to control the finitely many traces and derivatives below. For $G=\sum_{n\ge1}g_ne^{-in\vartheta}$ on a radius-$r$ circle put $E_G(w)=\sum_{n\ge1}g_n(r/w)^n$ outside that circle. Cut it off by $\chi=1$ near that circle and zero beyond radius $3r$, with derivative supported at radius greater than $2r$. Each fixed derivative of $f_G=\bar\partial(\chi E_G)$ is bounded by $C\|G\|_{H^s}$, since the differentiated $n$th term is a polynomial in $n$ times a factor at most $2^{-n}$ on that support. The old response [F1], with forcing $-f_G$, gives $p_{O,G}+\chi E_G$, whose regular value at $q$ is exactly $i$. Its regular correction $K_O(G)=p_{O,G}-p_0$ is a smooth nonlinear smoothing response, vanishes at $q$, is $O(\|G\|)$ and has bounded derivatives on a small ball. The disk response [F2] gives $\chi E_G-Q_D f_G$, with regular part vanishing at $i$, plus its free real affine function $az+b$. On the torus use TWO data and choose a fixed smooth density $\varrho$ supported away from BOTH puncture disks, with integral one. Put $f=f_{G_a}+f_{G_x}$ and $I=\int_T f$. The response is $$\chi_a E_{G_a}+\chi_xE_{G_x}-Q_T(f-I\varrho)+c_T.$$ It has $\bar\partial=I\varrho$ and is holomorphic near both punctures even when $I\ne0$. Every regular trace and coefficient is bounded linearly by the incoming data. Stokes on the punctured torus gives $I=-\pi r(g_{a,1}+g_{x,1})$. Subtracting a CONSTANT mean instead would not make the regular parts holomorphic at the punctures; the supported density is essential here. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Solve the four nonzero-mode equations. A positive trace $\sum_{n\ge1}h_n(w/r)^n$ transferred by $wv=t$ becomes the negative trace with coefficients $(t/r^2)^n h_n$. Thus its actual operator norm is at most $|t|/r^2$ for $|t|<r^2$. Collect the four incoming traces in a product Hilbert space. Matching their nonconstant Laurent terms is exactly $$G=P_{\epsilon,\lambda}\bigl(H(a,L)+K(G,L)\bigr),$$ where $H$ consists of the fixed old background and the positive traces of $az$ in the coordinate $\tau_\lambda$, and $K$ consists of the old nonlinear regular response and the disk/torus regular responses from step2.1. Constants contribute no positive modes. On fixed small balls about $a=1,L=-A_0,G=0$, all derivatives of $H,K$ needed at each finite order are bounded, whereas $\|P\|\le C\rho$. For small $\rho$ this is a contraction on $\|G\|\le C\rho$, with derivative norm at most one half, giving a unique actual $G(a,L,\rho)$. Differentiating its fixed point equation multiplies all unknown highest derivatives by the same invertible $I-PD_GK$. Each differentiated transfer still has a factor $\rho$. Hence $G=O(\rho)$, $\partial_aG=O(\rho)$ and $\partial_LG=O(\rho)$, with the corresponding fixed finite-order bounds. These conclusions follow from the convergent iterations and their norm remainder estimates, not a presumed formal series. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Match constants without discarding either disk affine freedom. Write $K_{Ta,0},K_{Tx,0}$ for the torus regular constants, and use that the old and disk response constants are normalized to zero. Constant matching is exactly $$c_T=i-K_{Ta,0}(G),\qquad b+ai=c_T+K_{Tx,0}(G).$$ Thus $a=1+\operatorname{Im}(K_{Tx,0}-K_{Ta,0})(G(a,L,\rho))$ is a scalar contraction with derivative $O(\rho)$. It has unique solution $a=1+O(\rho)>0$, with $\partial_La=O(\rho)$; its real part then gives $b$, and the first equation gives $c_T$. The remaining torus integral obstruction, by the exact first Laurent transfers and step2.1's Stokes identity, is a fixed nonzero factor times $$R=\epsilon\bigl(A_0+K_{O,1}(G)\bigr)+\lambda\bigl(a+K_{D,1}(G)\bigr).$$ Here the subscript $1$ denotes the derivative of the regular correction in its radius-independent puncture coordinate. Divide by $\epsilon$ and set $\lambda=\epsilon L$. This is $A_0+L+E(\rho,L)$ with $\|E\|_{C^1_L}\le C\rho$. The map $L\mapsto-A_0-E(\rho,L)$ is a contraction of a small closed disk about $-A_0$ into itself. It gives unique $L=-A_0+O(\rho)$, and the real two-dimensional derivative of $R/\epsilon$ is $I+O(\rho)$, hence invertible. Now $I=0$, so the matched torus function is genuinely holomorphic. This proves the stated expansion of $\lambda$. The chosen phase makes $\operatorname{Re}\lambda$ nonzero and bounded below in absolute value by a positive multiple of $\rho$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The matched functions define an actual disk. Glue the old moved incidence surface, the punctured torus and the disk sheet using step1.1. Their root maps are the old inclusion, the torus inclusion and $h_\lambda$ respectively. They agree on both plumbing annuli; their interiors jointly cover the enlarged cut component exactly once, except its old moved slits and the new small slit. No inactive constant value is met. The glued projection $p$ is meromorphic with ONLY its original simple boundary poles and the new disk pole at infinity, real on all other boundary points. Its positive boundary-pole coefficients stay positive: on the old component they change by $O(\rho)$ from positive values, and on the disk the affine coefficient is $a=1+O(\rho)$ with an $O(\rho)$ response correction. On small half-circles about these poles its imaginary part is positive, by the leading positive half-plane pole and its real smooth remainder. The maximum principle on the compact remainder gives $\operatorname{Im}p>0$ everywhere. It is therefore proper to $\mathbb H$. Each active boundary has exactly one simple pole, so its degree is one there; its derivative stays nonzero along these boundaries by the buffered higher response estimates and the original nonzero boundary derivatives. Counting preimages of a sufficiently large imaginary value gives total degree one larger than the old active degree. Add the unchanged inactive constant roots. The inverse fibers are actual holomorphic symmetric tuples, including at interior projection branches by the coefficient contour formula in [F4]. At physical boundary they have distinct labelled roots and hence are smooth, with one on each attaching circle. Their infinity tuple is exactly the old fixed node plus $x$, since $h_\lambda(\infty)=0$. Root-degree one gives precisely the enlarged unit domain. The new small slit is proper, and its marked bank is regular by step4.1 and [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Prove actual regularity, including the moving-tip directions. By [F1] a geometric kernel section of the new disk is exactly a real scalar function with simple poles at its marked roots and all old/new tips. At the two new tips, their real principal coefficients are equivalent to the two real endpoint displacements. The nonzero endpoint Jacobian in [F3] gives a UNIQUE complex $\delta\lambda$ realizing them. More explicitly on the disk sheet the corresponding fixed-target variation is $$\delta p_{\rm fixed\ target}=\delta p_D-p_D'\,\delta h_\lambda/h_\lambda'.$$ Subtract this actual $\delta\lambda$ contribution; the remaining disk variation has no tip pole, only the permitted simple boundary pole at infinity and incoming Laurent traces. Its pole-free difference from the actual disk response is therefore its two-dimensional real affine kernel. On the torus the same subtraction leaves its incoming Laurent traces and a complex constant, by [F2]'s actual zero-integral inverse. On the old component subtract the incoming negative Laurent extension with its cutoff. The result fills the puncture, has precisely the old moving-tip principal parts, and solves the linearization of the forced graph equation used in [F1]'s nonlinear response. The inverse-root formula there, and the coefficient contour construction through old cover branches, identify it with an actual section of the OLD disk linearization. Its regular constant at $q$ is its two-real phase. Thus after removing the global two-dimensional affine variation so that this old regular constant is zero, the old piece is EXACTLY the derivative of the actual nonlinear response, not an assumed scalar meromorphic inverse. This argument is reversible: the root coordinate is a real local diffeomorphism on the forcing patch, so its inverse variation and graph variation are related by the ordinary invertible real derivative there; off that patch they are the holomorphic formulas in [F1]. All pieces of any normalized kernel section are consequently the linearization of the four mode equations, the constant equations and the complex obstruction in steps3.1--4.1 at fixed $\epsilon$. Their derivatives are invertible by the proved contractions and $I+O(\rho)$ obstruction derivative. Hence the normalized kernel is zero. The unnormalized kernel is exactly the two global affine directions, so [F1] and the actual index two in [F4] make the node-fixed operator onto. This is the coupled geometric operator calculation; no transverse split operator was used. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The same argument proves local uniqueness in the stated response neighborhood. For any actual nearby disk with the same nondegenerate labelled slit banks, its root-degree-one geometry identifies its old punctured piece, torus piece and rational disk piece in the actual coordinates above. The old part minus its incoming cutoff Laurent extension fills the puncture and is an actual solution of the old forced map section. Normalize its regular constant to $i$ by the unique global positive affine source map; [F1]'s actual implicit uniqueness then identifies it with the old nonlinear response. The torus and disk pieces are their actual responses plus the constants already accounted for. They therefore satisfy precisely the equations in steps3.1--4.1, whose unique solution was proved there. Conversely step5.1 realizes that solution. Smooth dependence for $\rho>0$ follows from the norm-smooth response maps, their specified implicit inverses and the finite coefficient charts. This proves local uniqueness and regularity but does NOT put an arbitrary degenerating sequence into this neighborhood. In particular no fixed-target compactness statement has been substituted for the missing target-degeneration exhaustion, and no global count is inferred. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
