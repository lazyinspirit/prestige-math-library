## Statement

For every full-row-rank matrix $M\in\mathbb Z^{n\times m}$ with $m\ge n\ge1$, the algorithm of exact Euclidean column-Hermite basis extraction terminates and returns the unique matrix $H$ whose columns form a basis of the column lattice of $M$, with $H$ upper triangular, $h_{ii}>0$, and $0\le h_{ij}<h_{ii}$ for $i<j$.

## Statement references

[Milne, Lemma 2.44 and Remark 2.45(b)](https://www.jmilne.org/math/CourseNotes/ANTc.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The matrix and algorithm in the Statement.

[F1] For a positive integer divisor, integer division has a unique remainder in the interval from zero to one less than the divisor ([[thm-division-algorithm-in-z]]).

[F2] The integer Euclidean remainder descent terminates at the gcd ([[thm-euclidean-algorithm]]).

[F3] Absolute value is nonnegative, positive on nonzero integers, and each nonzero integer is a sign times its absolute value ([[def-int-abs]], [[lem-int-abs-properties]]).

[F4] The procedure and its output convention are those of the exact column-Hermite algorithm ([[alg-integer-column-hermite-normal-form]]).

## Proof



1.1 In the extended-Euclid loop of [F4], each current remainder is the same integer linear combination of $|a|,|b|$ as its tracked coefficient pair, because the remainder and coefficient pairs use the same update. If $b\ne0$, [F1] and [F2] terminate with the positive gcd $g$; restoring the signs from [F3] gives coefficients $s,t$ with $sa+tb=g$. If $b=0$, the procedure returns $g=|a|$ and the corresponding signed coefficient directly. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 For the active row pair $(a,b)$, the transformation matrix in [F4] has determinant $(sa+tb)/g=1$ and sends $(a,b)$ to $(g,0)$. Column swaps and sign changes are also unimodular, so every column operation preserves the generated lattice. [step 1.1, F4, algebra]

3.1 Each Euclidean call terminates by [F2]. At row $i$, the first $q_i=m-n+i$ active columns have zeros below row $i$ by the loop invariant. If every active entry in row $i$ were zero, then rows $i,i+1,\ldots,n$ would be supported only in the remaining $m-q_i=n-i$ columns, so those $n-i+1$ rows would be linearly dependent, contradicting full row rank. Thus an active nonzero entry exists; successive gcd transformations leave one positive gcd pivot and zero all earlier active entries in that row. Descending induction on $i$ makes the first $m-n$ columns zero and the last $n$ columns a nonsingular upper-triangular matrix. [F2, step 2.1, given, induction, algebra]

4.1 The final column reductions use [F1] with positive divisor $h_{ii}$. They preserve the lattice and upper-triangular shape, give $0\le h_{ij}<h_{ii}$, and finish after finitely many pairs $(i,j)$. Therefore the output is a basis of the input lattice satisfying the stated conditions. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, F4]

5.1 To prove uniqueness, compare two such bases for the same lattice. The last-row projection of the lattice is $h_{nn}\mathbb Z$, so its unique positive generator determines $h_{nn}$. The intersection of the lattice with the first $n-1$ coordinate hyperplane is generated by the first $n-1$ columns; induction determines the upper-left $(n-1)\times(n-1)$ block. The last columns of the two matrices differ by a vector in that intersection. Starting in row $n-1$ and descending to row $1$, their residues both lie in $[0,h_{ii})$, so each coordinate agrees. The two matrices are equal. [step 3.1, step 4.1, induction, algebra] ∎
