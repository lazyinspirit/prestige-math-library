## Statement

Assume full AC. Let $P\subset V=S^1\times D^2$ be an oriented smooth pattern of signed winding $w$, let $C\subset S^3$ be an oriented smooth companion, and form $K=P(C)$ by the specified zero-framed oriented tubular embedding. Let $P(U)$ denote its closure under the zero-framed unknotted embedding of the same product solid torus. Write $X_P=V\setminus\operatorname{int}N(P)$, with outer product marking $(\mu_V,\lambda_V)$. On its inner torus use $(\mu_P,\lambda_P)$, where $\lambda_P$ is the preferred longitude of $P(U)$. Then
$$H_1(X_P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_P]\oplus\mathbb Z[\lambda_V],\qquad [\mu_V]=w[\mu_P],\qquad [\lambda_P]=w[\lambda_V].$$
The inner marking becomes exactly the preferred marking $(\mu_K,\lambda_K)$ after satellite gluing. Compatible access paths are retained; arbitrary changes conjugate the corresponding group restrictions and do not change these homology or eigenvalue assertions.

Every irreducible representation of $\pi_1(E_{P(U)})$ extends, after restriction to $X_P$, to an irreducible representation of $\pi_1(E_K)$ with the identical inner peripheral eigenvalues. For $w\ne0$, every one-dimensional irreducible-character peripheral image curve of $C$ has a finite function-field cover carrying an actual irreducible satellite representation whose eigenvalues satisfy
$$M_K^w=M_C,\qquad L_K=L_C^w.$$
Here the common peripheral eigenline is part of the data. No pointwise simultaneous diagonalization at parabolic exceptional points is asserted.

In particular, suppose the enhanced polynomial of $K$ has support on a line of primitive direction $(q,p)$, where the exponent pair $(a,b)$ denotes $L^aM^b$. Then the enhanced polynomial of $P(U)$ has support on a line of the same direction. If $w\ne0$, the enhanced polynomial of $C$ has support on a line of direction $(q w^2,p)$, reduced to a primitive vector when necessary. Unit enhanced polynomials, with one-point support, are included. Thus for a finite slope $r=p/q$, $q\ne0$, pattern closure is $r$-thin and companion is $(r/w^2)$-thin. No assertion that $w\ne0$, that $P(U)$ is nontrivial, or that any knot is fibered or an instanton L-space knot is made here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual pattern, zero-framed embeddings, tubular and orientation data of the Statement, with compatible base paths across the gluing torus.

[A1] Full AC. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The pattern/companion construction has the stated product meridian and zero-framed longitude. Positive meridians freely generate the integral first homology of an oriented link exterior, and the preferred knot longitude is its degree-one zero-homology class. The degree-one Hurewicz map is abelianization. [[def-satellite-pattern-companion-and-winding-number]] [[lem-string-link-exterior-homology-and-meridian-maps]] [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] [[prop-the-first-hurewicz-map-in-degree-one-is-abelianization]]

[F2] The fundamental group of a union with connected collared overlap is the pushout; injectivity of the overlap maps is not required. The three-sphere is simply connected. [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]] [[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]]

[F3] Under Countable Choice, smooth maps have a null critical-value set. A regular fibre of an oriented circle map is finite, and its signed derivative count equals the map on integral $H_1$. [[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]] [[thm-regular-value-formula-for-degree]]

[F4] Finite generator-matrix and relator equations define actual representation varieties. Their two common-eigenline charts are finite type; a nonzero four-word determinant, with an inverse variable appended, certifies absolute irreducibility. A dominant incidence over an irreducible peripheral curve has a nonzero generic-fibre algebra; a maximal ideal gives a finite field extension carrying the actual matrices and common eigenvector. The finite-incidence construction does not require the originating character component to be one-dimensional. These are the actual constructions in the listed suppliers, not a presumed rational section of a character quotient. [[lem-sl2-representation-variety-is-affine]] [[lem-newton-edges-have-valued-finite-peripheral-representation-models]], Proof1.2 and2.2. Algebraic sets here have finitely many irreducible components, and affine-domain dimension is the transcendence degree of its function field. [[lem-classical-affine-algebraic-set-finite-irreducible-components]] [[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]]

[F5] The explicit rank proof for adjoint boundary homology, word-cocycle evaluation and moving-eigenbasis evaluation is supplied in [[lem-generic-adjoint-peripheral-kernel-is-the-logarithmic-conormal-line]], Proof2.2,3.1 and4.1. Those field-linear arguments use a nonparabolic peripheral matrix and finite first homology, not transcendence degree one; their needed extension to arbitrary finitely generated fields is proved in step2.3 below.

[F6] The enhanced curve is the union of one-dimensional peripheral eigenvalue-image components arising from irreducible characters, including images of higher-dimensional character components. Its reduced equation is square-free and a unit means the union is empty. [[def-enhanced-a-polynomial]]

## Proof

1.1 Embed $V$ as a zero-framed unknotted solid torus and let $A$ be the core of its complementary solid torus. Choose its orientation so that its positive meridian is $\lambda_V$ on the common torus. This is literal in the two standard complex-coordinate solid tori in $S^3$: the product longitude of one is the meridian of the other. Then $X_P$ is the exterior of the two-component link $P(U)\cup A$. By [F1], its homology has meridian basis $([\mu_P],[\mu_A])=([\mu_P],[\lambda_V])$. This uses the actual link exterior and not an asserted spine for an arbitrary pattern. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 The complement pieces are path-connected: cover a path in the connected ambient solid torus or three-sphere by finitely many straightening boxes for the embedded circles and boxes missing them. Choose its subdivision vertices off the circles in the successive open overlaps, and connect successive vertices in each box minus its straight coordinate axis; the latter is path-connected by radial and angular paths in the normal punctured plane. The tube radial retraction then gives a path in the exterior. Give each gluing torus a slightly enlarged product collar. Its two sides and overlap are path-connected, and collar retractions turn [F2]'s open-cover pushout into the pushout of the two actual exterior groups over the marked torus group. In particular, filling a knot exterior by its actual tubular solid torus identifies the solid-torus core generator with the longitude and kills the meridian. Eliminating that extra core generator in the pushout gives $\pi_1(S^3)=\pi_1(E_J)/\langle\!\langle\mu_J\rangle\!\rangle=1$ for every knot $J$. Thus its positive meridian normally generates its group. If its image under a representation is central, the whole representation has central image and is reducible. These facts require no incompressibility of the gluing torus. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Project $P$ to the product core circle. Its homological degree is the signed winding $w$ by definition. By [A1], Countable Choice holds, so [F3] supplies a regular angle $\theta$ with finitely many preimages. The disk $D=\{\theta\}\times D^2$ meets $P$ transversely at these points; its oriented local intersection signs are exactly the derivative signs of that projection. Remove disjoint small disks about those intersections. The remaining oriented punctured disk lies in $V\setminus P$ and has outer boundary $\mu_V$ and inner meridian boundaries with coefficients minus those signs. The small circles represent the positive $P$ meridian with the indicated signs, by the oriented normal-disk chart. Radial motion in the supplied tube, $z\mapsto((1-t)|z|+t\epsilon)z/|z|$, retracts the punctured tube to its boundary and fixes everything outside; thus $V\setminus P$ retracts to $X_P$. Transporting the punctured disk relation gives $[\mu_V]=(\sum\operatorname{sgn})[\mu_P]=w[\mu_P]$ by [F3]. No assertion about intersections of the entire supplied thick tube with $D$ is needed. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Write $[\lambda_P]=a[\mu_P]+b[\lambda_V]$ in step1.1's basis. Fill the axis tube to recover $E_{P(U)}$. This kills $[\lambda_V]=[\mu_A]$ and carries $[\mu_P]$ to its generator, while the preferred $\lambda_P$ maps to zero. Hence $a=0$. Instead fill the $P$ tube to recover $V$. This kills $[\mu_P]$, preserves $[\lambda_V]$, and carries the degree-one parallel $\lambda_P$ to $[P]=w[\lambda_V]$. Therefore $b=w$. This proves the inner homology relation without using an unproved symmetry of linking numbers. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 We record the needed global image bound, rather than assuming that the enhanced Definition captures a curve inside a two-dimensional image. On any finite irreducible common-eigenline incidence chart from [F4], take its actual generic matrices over its finitely generated function field $F$. If $M,L$ were algebraically independent, the meridian matrix would have distinct eigenvalues. The trace form makes its adjoint system self-dual. The actual torus homology has dimension two, and the cap/LES argument of [F5] applies over this $F$: restriction on cohomology is transpose to inclusion on homology, cap identifies it with the pair connector, and exactness gives $2=\dim\ker i_*+\operatorname{rank}i_*=2\operatorname{rank}i_*$. Thus the kernel is a line. For ANY $\mathbb C$-derivation $D$ of $F$, differentiating actual matrices gives $Z(g)=D\rho(g)\rho(g)^{-1}$. The actual word-chain pairing and the moving-eigenbasis computation in [F5] show that $(DL/L,DM/M)$, times a common nonzero scalar, annihilates this same line. Extend $M,L$ to a finite transcendence basis and take its two coordinate derivations. They extend through the finite algebraic extension to $F$: at a simple algebraic element $a$ with minimal polynomial $h$, put $Da=-(Dh)(a)/h'(a)$; characteristic zero makes the denominator nonzero, and differentiation descends through $(h)$ and then to quotients. The two resulting logarithmic pairs are independent, contradicting the annihilator line. Thus every image closure has dimension at most one. There are finitely many components by [F4]. The irreducible-character image is therefore contained in its selected enhanced curves together with finitely many isolated image points; in particular every nonconstant irreducible-representation eigenvalue family lies on the enhanced curve. No tangent-space identification, reducedness of a representation scheme, or exceptional specialized homology rank is used in this argument. [A1, F4, F5, F6, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 For a specified one-dimensional enhanced peripheral component of a knot $J$, [F4] gives an actual absolutely irreducible representation over a finite extension $F/\mathbb C(Y)$ with the generic eigenvalues $(M_J,L_J)$ of that component. Explicitly choose a dominant irreducible incidence chart over $Y$, localize its finite-type domain at the nonzero elements of $\mathbb C[Y]$, and take a maximal ideal of the resulting nonzero algebra. Its residue field is finite over $\mathbb C(Y)$ and retains the inverse of the nonzero irreducibility certificate. This also works when the character component has larger dimension. Conversely, an actual absolutely irreducible representation over such a field with nonconstant eigenvalue pair yields a dense family of complex irreducible representations: clear finitely many denominators in its generator matrices, eigenvector and certificate on an affine model of the field, and specialize on the nonempty certificate open. The finite-type incidence/image construction of [F4] identifies its closure; step2.3 makes its nonconstant image a selected enhanced curve. [A1, F4, F6, step 1.2, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

3.2 In $E_K=X_P\cup E_C$, the outer classes are identified with $\mu_C,\lambda_C$ by the specified zero-framed embedding. The inner $\mu_P$ is the positive satellite meridian because the embedding preserves normal orientation. Step2.2 gives $[\lambda_P]=w[\lambda_C]=0$ in $H_1(E_K)$, since the companion preferred longitude is zero there. The inner parallel still has degree one on $K$, so uniqueness in [F1] identifies it with the satellite preferred longitude. Equality of these torus homology classes is equality of their torus loop classes: the torus fundamental group is the two-coordinate winding group $\mathbb Z^2$, as follows by lifting the two circle coordinates to $\mathbb R^2$. Consequently no hidden meridian shift occurs in the group gluing or its eigenvalues. Basepoints may be placed on the common torus and the inner torus reached by one recorded path. Changing that path conjugates both inner peripheral elements together; independent basing choices are transported by their actual access connectors, never assumed to cancel. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 For the generic irreducible representation of any curve $Y$ in step3.1, one has $M_J^2\ne1$. If instead $M_J^2=1$, its meridian cannot be central by step1.2. Over the algebraic closure it is therefore $\epsilon(I+N)$ with $N\ne0$, $N^2=0$, and $\epsilon=\pm1$. A direct solution of $BN=NB$ in a basis with $N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ gives $B=aI+bN$; determinant one requires $a^2=1$. The commuting longitude consequently has eigenvalue $L_J=\pm1$. Both peripheral eigenvalues would be constant, contradicting that $Y$ is a curve. Thus the generic meridian has distinct eigenvalues and its two eigenspaces, defined over the field containing $M_J$, simultaneously diagonalize the longitude as well. The specified common eigenline determines the diagonal order. This proves only a generic assertion; it does not call a trace-$\pm2$ noncentral matrix central. [F4, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 For a representation $\sigma$ of $E_{P(U)}$, its restriction to $X_P$ kills $\lambda_V$. Indeed the complementary unknot exterior has group generated by $\mu_U$, with $\lambda_U=1$. The pushout eliminates $\mu_U$ by $\mu_U=\mu_V$ and gives $\pi_1(E_{P(U)})=\pi_1(X_P)/\langle\!\langle\lambda_V\rangle\!\rangle$. Define a companion representation by its abelianization, sending a class of meridian exponent $n$ to $\sigma(\mu_V)^n$. Its outer values are $\sigma(\mu_V)$ and $I=\sigma(\lambda_V)$, so it glues to the given pattern restriction. The image of the restriction to $X_P$ equals the whole image of $\sigma$ by the displayed quotient. Hence irreducible $\sigma$ gives an irreducible satellite representation, with exactly the same inner pair by step3.2. This works for every such representation, including unipotent, central-peripheral and other exceptional cases, and for every signed winding including zero. It also works over each finite field model of step3.1. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Suppose $w\ne0$ and take the actual companion model from steps3.1 and4.1. After a basis change its peripheral matrices are $\operatorname{diag}(M_C,M_C^{-1})$ and $\operatorname{diag}(L_C,L_C^{-1})$. Adjoin a root $z$ of $z^w=M_C$: for negative $w$ use the polynomial $T^{|w|}-M_C^{-1}$. This is a finite nonzero field extension and $z\ne0$. Define the pattern representation through its free abelianization from step1.1 by $$[\mu_P]\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1}),\qquad [\lambda_V]\longmapsto\operatorname{diag}(L_C,L_C^{-1}).$$ Steps2.1–2.2 make its outer values exactly the two companion matrices and its inner longitude $\operatorname{diag}(L_C^w,L_C^{-w})$. The actual pushout in step1.2 glues these representations. Its image contains the companion image, so it remains absolutely irreducible, with satellite eigenvalues $(z,L_C^w)$ by step3.2. These are nonconstant: if both were constant, so would be $M_C=z^w$ and $L_C$ (a root of a constant power), contrary to $Y$. Step3.1 therefore puts this family on an actual satellite enhanced curve. All operations take place over the finite extension just constructed, not over a presumed common field for all components. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Suppose first the satellite enhanced polynomial is nonconstant and thin of primitive support direction $(q,p)$. Removing a Laurent monomial writes it as a one-variable Laurent polynomial in $M^pL^q$, so its peripheral curves are the finite union $M^pL^q=c_j$ with $c_j\ne0$. For each companion curve, step5.1 gives a generic satellite pair satisfying one of these equations. Raising it to the signed integer power $w$ gives $$M_C^p L_C^{q w^2}=c_j^w.$$ Let $h=\gcd(|p|,|q w^2|)>0$. The binomial on the left factors into finitely many graphs of the primitive monomial $M_C^{p/h}L_C^{q w^2/h}$. Each such graph is irreducible: complete that primitive vector to a unimodular torus-coordinate change. The irreducible companion curve contained in their finite union must equal one graph, since both have dimension one. Thus all its factors have parallel Newton segments of direction $(q w^2,p)$; their finite product, the reduced enhanced equation, has support on that same line. If it has no factors it is a unit and is thin. If the satellite enhanced equation is a unit, any companion curve would instead contradict step5.1 and the nonconstant-image conclusion of step3.1; therefore the companion enhanced equation is also a unit. This handles that case without assuming nontriviality of any enhanced curve. [F6, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Step4.2 gives the identical reasoning for each pattern-closure peripheral curve, now with the very same pair $(M,L)$ rather than the power-transformed pair: it is contained in the satellite enhanced curve, so its binomial factors have support direction $(q,p)$. A unit satellite equation excludes any such curve and forces a unit pattern-closure equation. This proves both thinness transfers, with finite slope scaling $p/(q w^2)$ in the companion case. On a nonconstant companion curve the excluded locus $M_C^2=1$ is a proper closed subset and hence finite; step4.1 also rules out a component lying wholly in it. The algebraic identity of step6.1 holds on the entire curve by closure, including those exceptional points. It is not an assertion that each exceptional parabolic representation individually has a commuting $w$th root in $SL_2$ or extends over a chosen surgery. No filling equation, surgery representation, winding nonvanishing, genus bound, or instanton conclusion has been used. The zero-winding companion branch remains outside the asserted transfer; its exclusion in a thin-satellite classification requires its separate geometric/instanton hypothesis. [F6, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
