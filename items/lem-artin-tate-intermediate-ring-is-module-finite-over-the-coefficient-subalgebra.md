## Statement

Keep the data and the notation of [[lem-artin-tate-coefficient-subalgebra]]:
commutative rings $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$, each a subring of the next, with
$A$ Noetherian, $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, a $B$-module generating list
$y_1,\ldots,y_n$ of $C$ with $y_1=1_C$, coefficients $z_{ij},z_{ijk}\in B$ as
displayed there, and $A'$ the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ they generate.

Then $C$ is generated as an $A'$-module by $y_1,\ldots,y_n$, so $C$ is
module-finite over $A'$; and $B$ is module-finite over $A'$ as well
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data of [[lem-artin-tate-coefficient-subalgebra]], and the set $M:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A'y_j=\{\,\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_jy_j:a_j\in A'\,\}\subseteq C$.

[L1] In that setup $A\subseteq A'\subseteq B$, the algebra $A'$ is of finite type over $A$, and $A'$ is a Noetherian ring ([[lem-artin-tate-coefficient-subalgebra]]).

[L2] $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is the smallest subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_n$; an algebra is **module-finite** over $R$ when it is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] For a ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_i\in R$ and $s_i\in S$ ([[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

[L4] Let $A$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and $B$ a commutative $A$-algebra module-finite over $A$ with $A$ a subring of $B$; then every ring $C$ with $A\subseteq C\subseteq B$ is module-finite over $A$ and is a Noetherian ring ([[thm-module-finite-algebra-over-a-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[L5] A subring contains the identity of the ambient ring and is closed under sums, additive inverses and products ([[def-subring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The set $M=\sum_{j=1}^{n}A'y_j$ is the $A'$-submodule of $C$ generated by $y_1,\ldots,y_n$, by the finite-sum description of a generated submodule; it is in particular an additive subgroup of $C$ closed under multiplication by elements of $A'$. Since $y_1=1_C$ we have $A'=A'y_1\subseteq M$, and therefore $A\subseteq M$ and $1_C\in M$. [L1, L3, L5, given]

2.1 $M$ is closed under multiplication, hence is a subring of $C$. It suffices to multiply two generators with coefficients: for $a,a'\in A'$, $(ay_i)(a'y_j)=aa'(y_iy_j)=aa'\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{ijk}y_k=\sum_{k=1}^{n}(aa'z_{ijk})y_k$, and each $aa'z_{ijk}$ lies in $A'$ because $A'$ is a subring of $B$ containing every $z_{ijk}$. A product of two elements of $M$ expands by distributivity into a finite sum of such terms, so it lies in $M$; with the additive subgroup property and $1_C\in M$ from step 1.1, $M$ is a subring of $C$. [L1, L3, L5, step 1.1]

3.1 $M$ contains each $t_i$, since $t_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{ij}y_j$ and every $z_{ij}$ lies in $A'$. So $M$ is a subring of $C$ containing $A$ and $t_1,\ldots,t_r$. But $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ is the smallest such subring, so $C\subseteq M$; and $M\subseteq C$ by construction. Hence $C=M$, and $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ generate $C$ as an $A'$-module, so $C$ is module-finite over $A'$. [L2, L3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Now $A'$ is a Noetherian commutative ring, $A'$ is a subring of $C$, and $C$ is module-finite over $A'$; the intermediate-ring clause applied to $A'\subseteq B\subseteq C$ gives that $B$ is module-finite over $A'$. [L1, L4, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **No induction on total degree is needed.** Showing directly that $\sum_jA'y_j$ is a subring containing $A$ and the algebra generators, and then invoking minimality of $A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, replaces the degreewise reduction of a polynomial expression; the multiplication table $y_iy_j=\sum_kz_{ijk}y_k$ is exactly what makes the closure argument work in one line.

- **$y_1=1_C$ is used, and used here.** It is what puts $A'$ itself inside $M$, without which $M$ need not contain $A$ and the minimality argument would not apply.

- **Only $A'$ is known to be Noetherian.** The final step is applied with $A'$ as the Noetherian base, never with $B$, which is exactly the point of the Artin–Tate argument.
