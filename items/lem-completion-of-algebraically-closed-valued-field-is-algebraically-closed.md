## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. The completion of any algebraically closed nonarchimedean absolutely valued field $A$ is algebraically closed. More generally, every nonarchimedean absolutely valued field $K$ admits an isometric field embedding into a complete algebraically closed nonarchimedean valued field. No characteristic, separability, discreteness, local compactness, or nontriviality hypothesis is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and an algebraically closed nonarchimedean valued field $A$; for the second assertion, a nonarchimedean valued field $K$.

[F1] Under Countable Choice, the completion of an absolutely valued field is a complete valued field containing the original field densely and isometrically; nonarchimedeanness is preserved. ([[thm-completion-of-an-absolutely-valued-field]])

[F2] A finite extension of a complete nonarchimedean valued field has a unique extending nonarchimedean absolute value; its value at $x$ is $|N(x)|^{1/[L:K]}$. ([[thm-unique-extension-of-a-nonarchimedean-absolute-value]])

[F3] Under Choice, every field has an algebraic closure. An algebraically closed field contains a root of every nonconstant polynomial. ([[thm-existence-of-algebraic-closures]], [[def-algebraically-closed-field]])

[A1] Full Choice is assumed for algebraic closures, the countable sequences selected below, and their completions; it implies Countable Choice. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 Let $C$ be the completion of $A$, supplied by [F1] and [A1], and let $f(T)=T^n+\sum_{i<n}a_iT^i\in C[T]$ with $n\ge1$. By density and Choice, choose monic $f_j(T)=T^n+\sum_{i<n}a_{ij}T^i\in A[T]$ with $|a_{ij}-a_i|<1/j$ for all $i,j$, and choose a root $r_j\in A$ of each $f_j$ using [F3]. Put $R=\max(1,|a_0|,\ldots,|a_{n-1}|)$. Then $|a_{ij}|\le R$, and every $r_j$ satisfies $|r_j|\le R$: otherwise its leading term would strictly dominate all lower terms in $f_j(r_j)$. Consequently
$$|f(r_j)|=|f(r_j)-f_j(r_j)|\le R^{n-1}/j\longrightarrow0.$$
[F1, F3, A1, algebra]

1.2 By [F3] choose an algebraic closure of $C$ and let $E/C$ be the field generated by all roots of $f$. It is finite, since finitely many algebraic generators give a finite extension by successively adjoining them. Extend the absolute value from the complete field $C$ to $E$ by [F2]. Factor $f(T)=\prod_{k=1}^n(T-\rho_k)$ in $E[T]$, counting multiplicities. For each positive integer $m$, step 1.1 implies that for all sufficiently large $j$ the product $\prod_k|r_j-\rho_k|<m^{-n}$; therefore at least one factor is $<1/m$. Since there are only finitely many $k$, one fixed $k_0$ has $|r_j-\rho_{k_0}|$ arbitrarily small for arbitrarily large $j$. Otherwise each $k$ would have a positive eventual lower bound, contradicting the product tending to zero. Choose an increasing subsequence $j_m$ with $|r_{j_m}-\rho_{k_0}|<1/m$. [F2, F3, A1, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 The subsequence is Cauchy in $C$, because the ultrametric inequality gives $|r_{j_m}-r_{j_l}|\le\max(1/m,1/l)$. Completeness gives $r\in C$ with $r_{j_m}\to r$. Its isometric image in $E$ also tends to $r$, and the same sequence tends to $\rho_{k_0}$, so uniqueness of metric limits gives $r=\rho_{k_0}$. Hence $f$ has a root in $C$. Dividing an arbitrary nonconstant polynomial by its leading coefficient reduces to the monic case, proving that $C$ is algebraically closed. The proof treats multiplicities directly and uses no derivative or separability. [F1, F3, step 1.2, algebra]

2.1 For general $K$, first form its complete valued field $B$ using [F1], then an algebraic closure $A$ of $B$ using [F3]. Each finite intermediate extension $E/B$ has the extending absolute value of [F2]. These values agree on inclusions by uniqueness in [F2]; any two elements of $A$ lie in a common finite extension of $B$, so their union defines a nonarchimedean absolute value on all of $A$ extending that of $B$. Complete this $A$. By step 1.3 its completion is algebraically closed, and the composed embeddings of $K$ are isometric. For the trivial absolute value the same construction and argument apply; the completion of a trivially valued field is the field itself. [F1, F2, F3, A1, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
