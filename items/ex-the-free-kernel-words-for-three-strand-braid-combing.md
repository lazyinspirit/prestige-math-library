## Example

Assume AC for the free-kernel basis. For $n=3$ the combing words of
[[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]] are
$$x_2=\sigma_2^{2},\qquad x_1=\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2 .$$
With the standard pure braids $A_{23}=\sigma_2^{2}$ and
$A_{13}=\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2^{-1}$ of
[[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]], free cancellation gives
$$x_2=A_{23},\qquad x_1=A_{23}^{-1}A_{13}A_{23},$$
which is the combing identity of
[[lem-the-combed-geometric-decomposition-is-unique]] at $n=3$. Hence the free
kernel of the forgetting map $PB_3\to PB_2$, freely generated by
$A_{13},A_{23}$ through the batch-21 identification, is equally freely
generated by $x_1,x_2$; under the identification of that kernel with the
fundamental group of the twice-punctured disc fibre of the forgetting map, the
standard generators correspond to clockwise based meridians $\mu_1,\mu_2$
of the two punctures, the inverses of the positively oriented meridians
specified by [[lem-standard-pure-braids-generate-each-free-kernel]]. The
combing basis corresponds to $\mu_2^{-1}\mu_1\mu_2,\mu_2$: its first
element is a conjugate of the first standard meridian, rather than the same
based class for the original stem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The group $B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\rangle$ of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] with its defining Artin braid relation and permitted free insertions and deletions of adjacent inverse letters, the combing words $\alpha_j,x_j$ of [[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]] for $n=3$, the standard pure braids $A_{ij}$ of [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]] for $1\le i<j\le3$, and the surjection $\varphi_3\colon B_3\to G_3$ of [[prop-the-artin-presentation-surjects-onto-geometric-braids]].

[F1] For $n=3$ the combing words are $\alpha_3=1$, $\alpha_2=\sigma_2$, $\alpha_1=\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $x_2=\alpha_3^{-1}\sigma_2^{2}\alpha_3=\sigma_2^{2}$, $x_1=\alpha_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\alpha_2=\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2$ ([[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]]).

[F2] The standard pure braid generators of [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]] are the classes of the words $W_{ij}=\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i^{2}\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}$; for $n=3$ this gives $A_{23}=\sigma_2^{2}$ and $A_{13}=\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2^{-1}$, in the geometric group and, through the published isomorphism $\Psi_3$, in $PB_3$.

[F3] Assume AC. Then AC implies dependent choice and countable choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]), the forgetting map $PB_3\to PB_2$ has free kernel of rank $2$, and under the fiber-inclusion identification the elements $A_{13},A_{23}$ are a free basis of that kernel ([[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]], [[lem-standard-pure-braids-generate-each-free-kernel]]); this is the batch-21 identification referred to in the statement.

[F4] At $n=3$ the uniqueness lemma says: if $W_1$ is a word in $x_1^{\pm1},x_2^{\pm1}$ and $W_2$ a word in $\sigma_1^{\pm1}$ with $\varphi_3(W_1W_2)=1$, then (i) $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_1))=A_{23}^{-1}A_{13}A_{23}$ and $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_2))=A_{23}$, and $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_1)),\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_2))$ form a free basis of the kernel; (ii) $\varphi_3(W_1)=1$ with $W_1$ freely trivial; (iii) $\varphi_2(W_2)=1$ ([[lem-the-combed-geometric-decomposition-is-unique]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The combing words.** By [F1], $x_2=\sigma_2^{2}$ and $x_1=\alpha_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\alpha_2=\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2$; both displays are literal word computations from the definition, with $\alpha_3=1$ the empty word. [F1]

1.2 **The standard generators.** By [F2], $A_{23}=[W_{23}]$ with $W_{23}=\sigma_2^{2}$ (both outer blocks of the display empty) and $A_{13}=[W_{13}]$ with $W_{13}=\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2^{-1}$. [F2]

2.1 **The two identities, by free cancellation.** Substituting the words of steps 1.1 and 1.2, $$A_{23}^{-1}A_{13}A_{23}=\sigma_2^{-2}\bigl(\sigma_2\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2^{-1}\bigr)\sigma_2^{2}\equiv\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{2}\sigma_2=x_1$$ and $A_{23}=x_2$; the only moves are deletions of the adjacent inverse pairs $\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_2$, in the middle of the first display. This is the identity $x_1=P_1^{-1}A_{13}P_1$ with $P_1=A_{23}$ of the combing lemma at $n=3$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 **The kernel is freely generated by the two combing words.** Assume AC, so that the kernel $K$ of $PB_3\to PB_2$ is free with basis $A_{13},A_{23}$ by [F3]. Identify $K$ with the abstract free group $F(a_1,a_2)$ through $a_1\mapsto A_{13}$, $a_2\mapsto A_{23}$, and define the endomorphism $\theta\colon F\to F$ on that basis by $\theta(a_1):=a_2a_1a_2^{-1}$, $\theta(a_2):=a_2$. Let $\rho\colon F\to F$ be the homomorphism with $\rho(a_1):=a_2^{-1}a_1a_2$ and $\rho(a_2):=a_2$. Then $\theta(\rho(a_1))=\theta(a_2)^{-1}\theta(a_1)\theta(a_2)=a_2^{-1}(a_2a_1a_2^{-1})a_2=a_1$ and $\theta(\rho(a_2))=a_2$, so $\theta\circ\rho$ is the identity on the free basis and hence on $F$; therefore $\rho$ is injective and the elements $\rho(a_1)=A_{23}^{-1}A_{13}A_{23}$, $\rho(a_2)=A_{23}$ are a free basis of the subgroup they generate. By step 2.1 these are $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_1))$ and $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_2))$ under the identification of [F4], and they generate $K$: each of $A_{13}=A_{23}\rho(a_1)A_{23}^{-1}$ and $A_{23}=\rho(a_2)$ lies in $\langle\rho(a_1),\rho(a_2)\rangle$, while both $\rho(a_i)$ lie in $K$. Hence $\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_1)),\Psi_3(\varphi_3(x_2))$ are a free basis of $K$; the argument is a free-group computation and uses no choice principle beyond the freeness of $K$ supplied by [F3]. [F3, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 **Conclusion.** Combining steps 2.1 and 3.1: the two combing words satisfy $x_2=A_{23}$ and $x_1=A_{23}^{-1}A_{13}A_{23}$ by free cancellation, and the free kernel of $PB_3\to PB_2$, freely generated by $A_{13},A_{23}$, is equally freely generated by $x_1,x_2$. Under the fibre-inclusion identification, write $\mu_i$ for the clockwise meridian corresponding to $A_{i3}$ by [F3]. Step 2.1 gives the fibre classes $\mu_2^{-1}\mu_1\mu_2$ and $\mu_2$ for $x_1$ and $x_2$ respectively. In the free group these first-meridian classes differ: the word $\mu_2^{-1}\mu_1\mu_2$ is reduced and is not $\mu_1$; conjugation changes the based stem class. ∎ [F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

## Remarks

- The two free-cancellation displays of step 2.1 are choice-free; AC enters only through [F3], the batch-21 identification of the free kernel with basis $A_{13},A_{23}$, and through the uniqueness lemma [F4] that names the images of the combing words. The free-basis argument of step 3.1 is the $n=3$ instance of the left-inverse argument of [[lem-the-combed-geometric-decomposition-is-unique]]: an endomorphism fixing the conjugated basis shows that conjugation by $A_{23}$ is injective on the free group.
- The identification of the fibre with a twice-punctured disc and of $A_{13},A_{23}$ with the clockwise based meridians is asserted here only as the reading of the batch-21 supplier statement (the formula $\Psi([A_{in}])=(\kappa_*[\gamma_i])^{-1}$ with $\gamma_i$ counterclockwise), read during this dispatch; the suppliers [[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]] and [[lem-standard-pure-braids-generate-each-free-kernel]] are in-run drafts, and their certification, in particular the meridian clause, is flagged for the owner rather than proved locally.
