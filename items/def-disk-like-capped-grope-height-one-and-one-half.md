## Definition

Use the framed four-dimensional block and stage conventions for gropes in the cited definitions. A **capped grope** is a staged thickening whose bottom surface stage is joined along curves in a symplectic basis to higher surface stages, with the final tip circles capped by framed immersed disks. A **disk-like capped grope** has one connected bottom surface stage with exactly one attaching circle. Its **height** is the number of successive surface stages on each side of the symplectic basis, with caps terminating the stages. A disk-like capped grope has **height one and one-half**, written $1.5$, when its two sides have heights $(1,0)$ or $(0,1)$ in the asymmetric convention; equivalently, one half of the first-stage basis has one additional surface stage and the other half is capped immediately. The **attaching region** is the framed solid-torus neighborhood of the single attaching circle. For an immersed disk, a **double-point loop** is the image of a path in the domain disk joining the two preimages of a transverse self-intersection. In the disk-embedding property for an input capped grope, this loop is read as the corresponding element of the grope's fundamental group; choosing a basing path from the grope basepoint fixes that based element.
