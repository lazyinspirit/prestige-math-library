## Statement

Assume AC. Fix $n\ge1$, $p\ge0$, $C\ge1$, a sequence
$C^{-2}i^{-2p}\le\kappa_i^2\le C^2i^{-2p}$, a truth
$\theta_0\in\ell^2(\mathbb N_+,\mathbb R)$, where $\mathbb N_+=\{1,2,\ldots\}$,
and $0<\alpha<\infty$. Consider the prior
$\Pi_\alpha=\bigotimes_{i\ge1}N(0,i^{-1-2\alpha})$ and observations
$X_i=\kappa_i\theta_{0,i}+n^{-1/2}Z_i$, where the $Z_i$ are independent
$N(0,1)$. Denote their sampling law by $P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}$ and write
$\ell^2=\ell^2(\mathbb N_+,\mathbb R)$ throughout this lemma. For
$n\ge3$ and $0<\alpha\le A_n$, these are the model and prior in
[[def-adaptive-empirical-bayes-gaussian-sequence-procedure]]; the present
fixed-$\alpha$ calculation requires no maximization domain or selector. Put
$r=1+2\alpha$. Then the product prior is supported on $\ell^2$, and its
posterior kernel under the prior-predictive law $Q_{n,\alpha}$ is the product
of the coordinate laws
$$N\!\left( \frac{n\kappa_i x_i}{i^r+n\kappa_i^2}, \frac{1}{i^r+n\kappa_i^2} \right),\qquad i\ge1,$$
For $P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}$-almost every observation $x$, this same kernel is
supported on $\ell^2$, and its posterior mean belongs to $\ell^2$.

Let $Q_{n,\alpha}$ be the prior-predictive law of $X$ under this prior and
let $Q_{n,0}=\bigotimes_{i\ge1}N(0,n^{-1})$. Then $Q_{n,\alpha}\ll Q_{n,0}$
and
$$\log\frac{dQ_{n,\alpha}}{dQ_{n,0}}(x) =-\frac12\sum_{i=1}^{\infty} \left[ \log\!\left(1+\frac{n\kappa_i^2}{i^r}\right) -\frac{n^2\kappa_i^2x_i^2}{i^r+n\kappa_i^2} \right] =\ell_n(\alpha;x).$$
The density identity and absolute convergence hold $Q_{n,0}$-almost surely.
The same displayed score series also converges absolutely
$P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}$-almost surely; this latter assertion does not identify a
version of the Radon–Nikodym derivative under $P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the countable product Gaussian prior and sampling model in
the Statement, $0<\alpha<\infty$, and $\theta_0\in\ell^2$.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] AC supplies countable choice and dependent choice, the two choice
principles required by the countable-product probability construction.
[[lem-ac-supplies-sequential-choices-for-probability-constructions]]

[F2] Under countable choice and dependent choice, a countable product of
probability spaces exists and has the prescribed finite-coordinate
marginals. [[thm-countable-product-of-probability-spaces]]

[F3] The product sigma-algebra on a countable product is generated by
finite-coordinate cylinders. [[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]]

[F4] $N(m,\sigma^2)$ is the pushforward of the standard normal law under
$x\mapsto m+\sigma x$. [[def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws]]

[F5] Nonnegative coordinate sums can be integrated by Tonelli's theorem.
[[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F6] Probability measures that agree on a generating $\pi$-system agree on
its generated sigma-algebra. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F7] The sequence space $\ell^2$ consists of the families with finite sum of
squared coordinates. [[def-square-summable-family-on-an-arbitrary-index-set]]

[F8] In the procedure's range $n\ge3$, $0<\alpha\le A_n$, its model, prior
and coordinate update notation agree with those in the Statement.
[[def-adaptive-empirical-bayes-gaussian-sequence-procedure]]

[F9] Under Countable Choice, the $C^1$ diffeomorphism change-of-variables
formula applies to nonnegative measurable integrands.
[[thm-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-lebesgue-measurable-functions]]

[F10] A normal random variable with law $N(m,\sigma^2)$ has expectation $m$
and variance $\sigma^2$. [[lem-normal-location-scale-mean-and-variance]]

[F11] The standard normal density
$\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$ is positive, Borel measurable and integrates
to one. [[lem-normal-density-has-total-mass-one]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F1]–[F2], the independent prior coordinates and independent normal errors can be realized on countable product probability spaces. Fix one coordinate and put $s_i=i^{-r}$. By [F4], [F9] and [F11], the prior and sampling densities with respect to Lebesgue measure are $g_i(t)=(2\pi s_i)^{-1/2}e^{-t^2/(2s_i)},\qquad q_i(x\mid t)=\sqrt{\frac n{2\pi}}e^{-n(x-\kappa_i t)^2/2}.$ Their product has exponent $-\tfrac12[(s_i^{-1}+n\kappa_i^2)t^2-2n\kappa_ixt+nx^2]$. Completing the square in $t$ gives conditional variance $v_i=(s_i^{-1}+n\kappa_i^2)^{-1}$ and conditional mean $m_i(x)=v_i n\kappa_i x$. Integrating the same product over $t$ gives the prior-predictive density $\frac{\exp\{-x^2/[2(n^{-1}+\kappa_i^2s_i)]\}} {\sqrt{2\pi(n^{-1}+\kappa_i^2s_i)}}.$ This is the density of $N(0,n^{-1}+\kappa_i^2s_i)$ by [F4], [F9] and [F11]. In the procedure's parameter range, [F8] identifies the same coordinate update. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F8, F9, F11, given, algebra]

2.1 The scalar posterior densities in step 1.1 are probability densities by [F4], [F9] and [F11]. For each fixed data sequence, [F2] defines the product posterior measure $K(x,\cdot)$. On a finite-coordinate parameter cylinder $A$, $K(x,A)$ is a finite product of normal probabilities, hence is Borel in $x$. The scalar Bayes identity and independence of the coordinate pairs give the conditional-law identity for such $A$ and every finite-coordinate data cylinder. For fixed $A$, both sides, as functions of the data event, are finite measures; the scalar Bayes identity with the full data event shows that both have total mass equal to the prior probability of $A$. If that mass is positive, normalize both measures and apply [F6]; if it is zero, both measures are identically zero. The data cylinders generate the data sigma-algebra by [F3], so the identity holds for every data event. The class of parameter sets $A$ for which $K(\cdot,A)$ is Borel and the identity holds for every data event contains the parameter-cylinder $\pi$-system. It is a Dynkin system because complements use $1-K(x,A)$ and differences of finite measures, while countable disjoint unions use pointwise limits of measurable partial sums and countable additivity of the joint measures. A second use of [F3] and [F6] proves the identity for every product-measurable $A$. Therefore $K$ is a posterior probability kernel. [F2, F3, F4, F6, F9, F11, step 1.1]

2.2 Write $a_i=n\kappa_i^2s_i$. The density of the marginal $N(0,n^{-1}(1+a_i))$ coordinate relative to $N(0,n^{-1})$ is $L_i(x_i)=(1+a_i)^{-1/2}\exp\{n a_i x_i^2/[2(1+a_i)]\}$. Since $\sum_i a_i\le nC^2\sum_i i^{-1-2\alpha-2p}<\infty$, [F5] and [F10] give $\sum_i a_i Z_i^2<\infty$ almost surely under $Q_{n,0}$, where $Z_i=\sqrt nX_i$. Hence $\sum_i|\log L_i(X_i)|<\infty$ there. Choose $J$ so $a_i<1$ for $i>J$. Direct Gaussian integration gives $E_{Q_{n,0}}L_i^2=(1-a_i^2)^{-1/2}$ for $i>J$, and $\prod_{i>J}(1-a_i^2)^{-1/2}<\infty$ because $\sum_i a_i^2<\infty$. The finite tail products are mean-one martingales and are Cauchy in $L^2$: for $m>k>J$, independence gives $E|\prod_{i=J+1}^{m}L_i-\prod_{i=J+1}^{k}L_i|^2 =E(\prod_{i=J+1}^{k}L_i^2)\{\prod_{i=k+1}^{m}(1-a_i^2)^{-1/2}-1\}$, which tends uniformly to zero as $k\to\infty$. The finite initial product is independent of the tail and has mean one, so multiplication preserves $L^1$ convergence. By absolute convergence of the log series, the nonnegative limit is $\prod_iL_i=\exp(\sum_i\log L_i)$ and has expectation one. It agrees with the marginal product law on every finite cylinder, so [F6] identifies it as $dQ_{n,\alpha}/dQ_{n,0}$. Taking logarithms and substituting $s_i=i^{-r}$ gives the displayed $\ell_n(\alpha;x)$. [F2, F4, F5, F6, F10, F11, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Under $P_{\theta_0}^{(n)}$, [F10] and the independence in the model give $E X_i^2=\kappa_i^2\theta_{0,i}^2+n^{-1}$. The sum $\sum_i a_i E(nX_i^2)$ is finite: its noise part is $\sum_i a_i$, and its signal part is $n^2\sum_i\kappa_i^4i^{-r}\theta_{0,i}^2 \le n^2C^4\sum_i\theta_{0,i}^2<\infty$. Thus the same absolute-convergence bound for $\sum_i|\log L_i(X_i)|$ holds under the true sampling law. [F5, F10, given, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 By [F5] and [F10], the prior has expected squared norm $\sum_i i^{-r}<\infty$, hence belongs to $\ell^2$ almost surely. The posterior variances satisfy $\sum_i v_i\le\sum_i i^{-r}<\infty$. For its mean, write $m_i(X_i)=w_i\theta_{0,i}+ \sqrt n\kappa_i(i^r+n\kappa_i^2)^{-1}Z_i$, where $w_i=n\kappa_i^2/(i^r+n\kappa_i^2)\le1$. The cross term has mean zero because $E Z_i=0$ by [F10], so $E_{\theta_0}^{(n)}\sum_i m_i(X_i)^2 \le \sum_i\theta_{0,i}^2+ nC^2\sum_i i^{-2r-2p}<\infty.$ Therefore the posterior mean is in $\ell^2$ almost surely; for almost every such observation its posterior expected squared norm is $\sum_i(m_i(X_i)^2+v_i)<\infty$, so the posterior gives $\ell^2$ mass one. [F5, F7, F10, given, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
