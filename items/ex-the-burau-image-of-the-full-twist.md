## Example

Assume AC (inherited through the definition of the reduced representation and
the agreement theorem with the topological representation). For $n=3$ let
$\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ be the half twist and $\Delta^2$ the full
twist, which generates the center $Z(B_3)$
([[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]]).
Then the reduced Burau representation over $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$
sends the full twist to the scalar matrix
$$\bar\rho_3(\Delta^2)=t^3I_2,$$ so the image of the center is the infinite
cyclic subgroup $\langle t^3I_2\rangle$ of
$\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$, and $\bar\rho_3$ is injective on the center:
$\bar\rho_3(\Delta^{2k})=I_2$ holds if and only if $k=0$. In particular,
although the specialization $t\mapsto-1$ sends the scalar $t^3$ to $-1$ (so
that the image of $\Delta^2$ becomes $-I_2$ and $\Delta^2$ itself is not in the
kernel of $\bar\rho_3(-1)$), neither $\Delta^2$ nor any $\Delta^{2k}$ with
$k\ne0$ lies in the kernel of $\bar\rho_3$ over $\Lambda_1$.

## Verification

**Given:** the ring $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$; the half twist
$\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ of $B_3$ and the full twist $\Delta^2$; the
reduced representation $\bar\rho_3:B_3\to\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$ in
the basis $(g_1,g_2)$; the evaluation homomorphism
$\varepsilon:\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Z$, $t\mapsto-1$, applied entrywise.

[A1] In the basis $(g_1,g_2)$,
$\bar\rho_3(\sigma_1)=\begin{pmatrix}-t&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$,
$\bar\rho_3(\sigma_2)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\1&-t\end{pmatrix}$ and
$\bar\rho_3(\Delta^2)=t^3I_2$; $\bar\rho_3$ is a group homomorphism, so
$\bar\rho_3(\beta^m)=\bar\rho_3(\beta)^m$ for every $\beta\in B_3$ and every
$m\in\mathbb Z$
([[thm-topological-and-matrix-burau-representations-agree]],
[[def-reduced-burau-representation]]).

[A2] The center of $B_3$ is infinite cyclic and generated by the full twist:
$Z(B_3)=\langle\Delta^2\rangle=\{\Delta^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, with
$\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ the half twist
([[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]],
[[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

[A3] $t^m\ne1$ in $\Lambda_1$ for every integer $m\ne0$
([[lem-units-and-powers-of-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]], clause (b)); in
particular $t^{3k}\ne1$ for $k\ne0$, and $t^3I_2$ has infinite order in
$\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$.

[A4] The assignment $t\mapsto-1$ extends uniquely to a unital ring
homomorphism $\varepsilon:\Lambda_1\to\mathbb Z$ with
$\varepsilon(t^m)=(-1)^m$, and composition with $\varepsilon$ entrywise sends a
homomorphism into $\operatorname{GL}_2(\Lambda_1)$ to one into
$\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb Z)$
([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]], [[def-ring-homomorphism]]).

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *The full twist and its powers.* By [A1] and $\Delta^2=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1)^2$, the full twist satisfies $\bar\rho_3(\Delta^2)=t^3I_2$; hence for every $k\in\mathbb Z$ the homomorphism property gives $\bar\rho_3(\Delta^{2k})=(t^3I_2)^k=t^{3k}I_2$. [A1, A2, algebra]

2.1 *The image of the center.* Since $Z(B_3)=\langle\Delta^2\rangle$ by [A2], the image of the center is $\langle\bar\rho_3(\Delta^2)\rangle=\langle t^3I_2\rangle$. The matrix $t^3I_2$ has infinite order by [A3], so $\langle t^3I_2\rangle$ is infinite cyclic. Moreover $\bar\rho_3(\Delta^{2k})=t^{3k}I_2=I_2$ holds if and only if $t^{3k}=1$, which by [A3] happens if and only if $3k=0$, that is, if and only if $k=0$; hence $\bar\rho_3$ is injective on $Z(B_3)$. [A2, A3, step 1.1]

3.1 *The specialization at $t=-1$.* Apply the homomorphism $\varepsilon$ of [A4] entrywise to $\bar\rho_3(\Delta^2)=t^3I_2$: the result is $\bar\rho_3(-1)(\Delta^2)=(-1)^3I_2=-I_2\ne I_2$; so $\Delta^2\notin\ker\bar\rho_3(-1)$, even though its square $\Delta^4$ has image $(-I_2)^2=I_2$. Over $\Lambda_1$ the same conclusion is step 2.1: $\bar\rho_3(\Delta^{2k})\ne I_2$ for every $k\ne0$, so neither $\Delta^2$ nor any nonzero power $\Delta^{2k}$ lies in the kernel of $\bar\rho_3$. AC is inherited through the cited agreement theorem; the scalar and matrix computations are choice free. [A1, A4, step 2.1] ∎
