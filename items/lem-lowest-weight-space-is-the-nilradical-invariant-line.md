## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\mathfrak g$ be a
finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra
$\mathfrak h$, fixed positive system, negative nilradical $\mathfrak n^-$ and
Weyl vector $\rho$, and let $\mu$ be a dominant integral weight. Then the
space of $\mathfrak n^-$-invariants of the finite-dimensional irreducible
module $L(\mu)$ is one-dimensional and equals its lowest weight space:
$$\dim L(\mu)^{\mathfrak n^-}=1,\qquad L(\mu)^{\mathfrak n^-}=L(\mu)_{w_0\mu},$$
the lowest weight being $w_0\mu$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h,\mathfrak n^-$, a dominant integral weight $\mu$, the finite-dimensional irreducible module $L(\mu)$ and the longest Weyl element $w_0$.

[F1] If a representation $V$ is generated by a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$, then $V=U(\mathfrak n^-)v$, every weight of $V$ is of the form $\lambda-\sum_in_i\alpha_i$ with $n_i\ge0$ and hence lies below $\lambda$ in the root order, and $V_\lambda=\mathbb Cv$ ([[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]]).

[F2] A finite-dimensional irreducible highest weight module $V$ of highest weight $\lambda$ has one-dimensional $\lambda$-weight space, $\dim V_\lambda=1$ ([[prop-the-highest-weight-space-of-an-irreducible-module-is-one-dimensional]]).

[F3] For $\lambda$ dominant integral, the dual module $V(\lambda)^*$ is irreducible of highest weight $-w_0(\lambda)$; the dual action is $(x f)(v)=-f(x v)$ ([[prop-highest-weight-of-the-dual-representation]], [[prop-direct-sum-dual-hom-and-tensor-representations]]).

[F4] By the classification of finite-dimensional irreducible representations, $L(\mu)$ is the unique such module of highest weight $\mu$ up to isomorphism; it is a highest weight module in the sense of [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], so it contains a nonzero highest weight vector $v_\mu$ of weight $\mu$ with $\mathfrak n^+v_\mu=0$, and it is generated by $v_\mu$ because a finite-dimensional irreducible representation is generated by any of its highest weight vectors ([[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[prop-a-finite-dimensional-irreducible-module-is-generated-by-any-highest-weight-vector]]).

[F5] The negative nilradical is $\mathfrak n^-=\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$, a sum of weight spaces for the weights $-\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi^+$, so every element of $\mathfrak n^-$ is a sum of weight vectors of weights $-\gamma$ with $\gamma$ a nonnegative integer combination of positive roots, nonzero unless the element is zero ([[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]], [[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[F6] The evaluation pairing $V\times V^*\to\mathbb C$, $(v,f)\mapsto f(v)$, is nondegenerate and $\mathfrak g$-invariant for the dual action of [F3], and the weight spaces of $V$ and $V^*$ satisfy $\dim (V^*)_{-\nu}=\dim V_\nu$ for every weight $\nu$ of $V$ ([[prop-direct-sum-dual-hom-and-tensor-representations]], [[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $v_\mu$ be a highest weight vector of the finite-dimensional irreducible module $V=L(\mu)$, which exists and generates $V$ by [F4]. Apply [F1] with $\lambda=\mu$: $V=U(\mathfrak n^-)v_\mu$, all weights of $V$ lie below $\mu$, and $V_\mu=\mathbb Cv_\mu$. Applying [F3] and [F4] to $V$, its dual $V^*$ is finite-dimensional irreducible of highest weight $-w_0\mu$ with a highest weight vector $v^*$ generating $V^*$, and its weights lie below $-w_0\mu$ by [F1]. [F1, F3, F4, given]

2.1 Write $V=\mathbb Cv_\mu+\mathfrak n^-V$: every $y\in U(\mathfrak n^-)$ is a sum of a scalar and of nonempty products of elements of $\mathfrak n^-$, and a nonempty product $x_1\cdots x_k v_\mu=x_1(x_2\cdots x_kv_\mu)$ lies in $\mathfrak n^-V$; by step 1.1 this covers all of $V$. The sum is direct: an element of $\mathfrak n^-V$ is a sum of vectors $xv$ with $x\in\mathfrak n^-$ a weight vector of weight $-\gamma$, $\gamma\ne0$, by [F5], so its weight components are of weights $\nu-\gamma$ where $\nu$ is a weight of $V$; if such a component had weight $\mu$, then $\nu=\mu+\gamma$ would be a weight of $V$ strictly above $\mu$, contradicting step 1.1. Hence $\mathfrak n^-V\cap\mathbb Cv_\mu=0$ and $V/\mathfrak n^-V\cong V_\mu=\mathbb Cv_\mu$ is one-dimensional. [F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The same computation with $V$ replaced by $V^*$, using its highest weight $-w_0\mu$ and its generating highest weight vector from step 1.1, gives $V^*=\mathbb Cv^*\oplus\mathfrak n^-V^*$ and $V^*/\mathfrak n^-V^*\cong (V^*)_{-w_0\mu}$, which is one-dimensional by [F2]. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Identify $V$ with the double dual $(V^*)^*$ by $v\mapsto(v\mapsto f(v))$, using nondegeneracy of the evaluation pairing [F6]. For $u\in V$ and $x\in\mathfrak n^-$, $f\in V^*$ one has $(xf)(u)=-f(xu)$ by [F3], so the functional attached to $u$ vanishes on $\mathfrak n^-V^*$ exactly when $xu=0$ for all $x\in\mathfrak n^-$, that is exactly when $u\in V^{\mathfrak n^-}$. Therefore $V^{\mathfrak n^-}$ is identified with the annihilator of $\mathfrak n^-V^*$ in $(V^*)^*$, which is the dual space of $V^*/\mathfrak n^-V^*$; this identification is $\mathfrak h$-equivariant, since it is given by the canonical evaluations. By step 2.2 it follows that $\dim V^{\mathfrak n^-}=1$ and that the single weight of this line is $-(-w_0\mu)=w_0\mu$, the negative of the weight of $V^*/\mathfrak n^-V^*$. [F3, F6, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 By [F6] the pairing pairs the weight space $V_{w_0\mu}$ nondegenerately with $(V^*)_{-w_0\mu}$, so $\dim V_{w_0\mu}=\dim (V^*)_{-w_0\mu}=1$ by [F2] applied to the irreducible module $V^*$ of highest weight $-w_0\mu$. Every weight $\nu$ of $V$ satisfies $\nu\ge w_0\mu$: the pairing is nondegenerate, so $-\nu$ is a weight of $V^*$, and by [F1] applied to $V^*$ one has $-\nu$ below $-w_0\mu$, that is $\nu-w_0\mu\in Q^+$; hence $w_0\mu$ is the lowest weight of $V$ and $V_{w_0\mu}$ is its lowest weight space. Step 3.1 produces a one-dimensional $\mathfrak h$-stable line of weight $w_0\mu$ inside $V^{\mathfrak n^-}$, hence inside the one-dimensional space $V_{w_0\mu}$; therefore $V^{\mathfrak n^-}=V_{w_0\mu}$ and both are one-dimensional. [F1, F2, F6, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
