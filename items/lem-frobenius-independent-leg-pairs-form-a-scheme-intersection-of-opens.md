## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X/\mathbb F_q$ be a smooth projective geometrically connected curve and let $F_X$ be its $q$-power Frobenius. There is a scheme $\Lambda$ with an affine monomorphism $\Lambda\to Y=X\times X$ representing the following subfunctor of $Y$: a map $S\to Y$ factors through $\Lambda$ exactly when, for every geometric point $s$ of $S$ and every integer $i\ge0$, its legs satisfy $F_X^i(x_s)\ne y_s$ and $x_s\ne F_X^i(y_s)$. It is the inverse limit over $Y$ of the complements of the first finitely many Frobenius graphs. The morphisms $F_X\times\operatorname{id}$ and $\operatorname{id}\times F_X$ preserve $\Lambda$. In general $\Lambda\to Y$ is not an open immersion: for $X=\mathbb P^1$ its image contains the generic point of $Y$ but contains no nonempty open of $Y$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and $X$, $Y$, $F_X$ in the Statement.

[F1] A smooth relative curve has a locally free differential module of rank one and regular one-dimensional local fibre rings at rational points. ([[thm-differentials-smooth-locally-free]], [[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]])

[F2] Nakayama lifts a generator of an ideal modulo its square at a point of that ideal. For a local map with Noetherian target, multiplication by a fibrewise nonzerodivisor on the flat target is injective. ([[thm-nakayama-lemma]], [[lem-fibre-injective-map-flat-cokernel-noetherian-target]])

[F3] Affineness of a morphism is local on its target. For an affine morphism, restriction to a principal open of the target localizes its coordinate algebra. Affine schemes glue along open overlaps. ([[lem-affine-morphism-local-on-target]], [[lem-affine-morphism-structure-sheaf-pushforward-localizes]], [[thm-gluing-affine-schemes]])

[A1] AC is used through the smoothness, fibrewise-injectivity and affineness providers just named. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 Each graph is a section of one projection $Y\to X$, a smooth relative curve. Its ideal is locally principal and generated by a nonzerodivisor, as follows. On local affine charts for that section the ring augmentation $C\to A$ splits the base map. With kernel $I$, the map $I/I^2\to\Omega_{C/A}\otimes_C A$, $i\mapsto di$, is an isomorphism: the inverse derivation sends $c$ to $c-s(c)$ modulo $I^2$, where $s(c)$ denotes its image reinserted from $A$. Thus [F1] makes $I/I^2$ free of rank one near any section point. The ambient ring is Noetherian; [F2] lifts its basis to a local generator $f$ of $I$. In the fibre over the base point, the section is a rational point of a smooth curve, whose local ring is regular of dimension one by [F1]; the image of $f$ generates its nonzero maximal ideal and is a nonzerodivisor. The ambient local ring is flat over the base local ring by smoothness. Apply the fibrewise-injectivity assertion of [F2] to multiplication by $f$ on that ambient ring. It is injective. Off the graph the ideal is the unit ideal. This proves the effective Cartier assertion for each graph. [F1, F2, A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $U_n$ be the complement of the union of both orientations of the graphs of $F_X^i$ for $0\le i\le n$. A finite union has the same complement as the effective Cartier divisor defined by the product of their local equations. On an affine open trivializing that product the complement is a principal affine open. Therefore $U_n\to Y$ is affine by [F3]. The inclusions $U_{n+1}\subset U_n$ give, over any affine $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subset Y$, a direct system of coordinate algebras $A_n=\Gamma(U_n\cap V,\mathcal O)$. Put $L_V=\operatorname{Spec}(\varinjlim_n A_n)$. For every affine test scheme over $V$, the universal property of a ring colimit identifies maps into $L_V$ with compatible maps into all $U_n\cap V$. Hence $L_V$ represents their intersection as subfunctors over $V$. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Restricting $V$ to a principal open $D(a)$ replaces each $A_n$ by $(A_n)_a$ by [F3]. Localization commutes with the colimit: a ring map out of either construction is exactly a compatible family of maps from the $A_n$ sending $a$ to a unit. Thus $L_{D(a)}$ is the inverse image of $D(a)$ in $L_V$. These identifications on principal covers give open overlaps and satisfy the cocycle identities by uniqueness of the represented functor. Glue the $L_V$ by [F3], obtaining an affine $Y$-scheme $\Lambda$. Its functor is the intersection of the open subfunctors $U_n$, so it is a monomorphism. A map factors through every $U_n$ exactly when its image, equivalently all its geometric points, avoids every excluded graph; this is precisely the condition in the Statement. [F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 On algebraically closed field points Frobenius is bijective. If $(F_X x,y)$ were on a forbidden graph, then either $F_X^{i+1}x=y$, or $F_X x=F_X^i y$. For $i\ge1$ the second equality implies $x=F_X^{i-1}y$; for $i=0$ it says $F_X x=y$. All contradict independence of $(x,y)$. The other partial Frobenius is treated by exchanging the factors. The geometric-point factorization criterion in step 3.1 therefore gives both morphisms on $\Lambda$, including on arbitrary nonreduced test bases. [step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 Take $X=\mathbb P^1$ and the affine chart with coordinates $(x,y)$. Every forbidden graph is a proper closed curve, so the generic point avoids them all and is in the image of $\Lambda$. Their union is dense: for any nonzero $P\in\mathbb F_q[x,y]$, choose $i$ with $q^i>\deg_y P$. Distinct nonzero monomials $x^a y^b$ of $P$ then give distinct exponents $a q^i+b$ on substituting $x=y^{q^i}$, so $P(y^{q^i},y)\ne0$. Thus the generic point of that graph belongs to the nonempty principal open $D(P)$. Every nonempty open of $Y$ meets this affine chart and contains a nonempty such principal open. Consequently every nonempty open meets a forbidden graph, and none is contained in the image of $\Lambda$. Since that image contains the generic point, it cannot be open. [step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
