## Example

Let $\Gamma=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ with lexicographic order. There is a
valuation ring with value group $\Gamma$. It is not a discrete valuation ring,
and therefore it is not Noetherian.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and the lexicographically ordered abelian group $\Gamma=\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$.

[F1] A totally ordered abelian group has a translation-invariant total order
([[def-totally-ordered-abelian-group]]).

[F2] A valuation is a map to a totally ordered abelian group adjoined with
$\infty$ satisfying the exact-zero, multiplicative, and ultrametric laws
([[def-valuation-on-a-field]]).

[L1] A valuation ring is Noetherian exactly when it is a field or a DVR
([[thm-noetherian-valuation-ring-characterisation]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Form the group algebra $k[t^\Gamma]=\{\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma} a_\gamma t^\gamma : \text{only finitely many }a_\gamma\ne0\}$, and let $K$ be its fraction field. For a nonzero element $f=\sum a_\gamma t^\gamma$, define $v(f)$ to be the smallest $\gamma$ in its finite support. Because the order on $\Gamma$ is translation-invariant by [F1], the product of the two lowest terms is the unique lowest term of a product, so $v(fg)=v(f)+v(g)$. For sums, the minimum support can only stay the same or move upward, so $v(f+g)\ge\min\{v(f),v(g)\}$. Extending by $v(f/g)=v(f)-v(g)$ and $v(0)=\infty$ gives a valuation on $K$ in the sense of [F2], with value group all of $\Gamma$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $V:=\{x\in K:v(x)\ge0\}$. Then $V$ is a valuation ring and is not a field because $t^{(0,1)}$ has positive value. It is not a DVR, since its value group is $\Gamma$ rather than $\mathbb Z$: any cyclic subgroup of $\Gamma$ is generated by one pair, so it cannot contain both $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$. Hence [L1] implies that $V$ is not Noetherian. [L1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
