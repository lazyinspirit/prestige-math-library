## Statement

Let
$$ A:=\{z \in \mathbb C : z \text{ is algebraic over } \mathbb Q\}. $$
Then $A$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The subset $A \subseteq \mathbb C$ of elements algebraic over $\mathbb Q$.

[L1] The algebraic elements in an extension form a subfield ([[thm-algebraic-elements-form-a-subfield]]).

[L2] The field $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed ([[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]]).

[L3] A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements over the base field is finite over that base ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

[L4] An element is algebraic over a field if and only if its simple extension over that field is finite ([[cor-element-algebraic-iff-simple-extension-finite]]).

[L5] Degrees multiply in a finite tower ([[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]).

[L6] Every element of a finite extension is algebraic over the base field ([[thm-finite-field-extensions-are-algebraic]]).

[L7] An algebraic closure of a field is an algebraic extension whose top field is algebraically closed ([[def-algebraic-closure]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], the set $A$ is a subfield of $\mathbb C$ containing $\mathbb Q$. [L1]

1.2 Let $$ f(x)=a_nx^n+\cdots+a_0 \in A[x] $$ be nonconstant. Since $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed by [L2], the polynomial $f$ has a root $z \in \mathbb C$. [L2]

2.1 The coefficients $a_0,\dots,a_n$ are algebraic over $\mathbb Q$, so $$ F:=\mathbb Q(a_0,\dots,a_n) $$ is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ by [L3]. The element $z$ is a root of a nonzero polynomial over $F$, so it is algebraic over $F$; therefore [L4] makes $F(z)/F$ finite. By [L5], the extension $F(z)/\mathbb Q$ is finite, and then [L6] makes $z$ algebraic over $\mathbb Q$. Hence $z \in A$. [L3, L4, L5, L6, step 1.2]

3.1 Step 2.1 shows that every nonconstant polynomial in $A[x]$ has a root in $A$, so $A$ is algebraically closed. Since every element of $A$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$ by definition, [L7] makes $A$ an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q$. [L7, step 2.1] ∎
