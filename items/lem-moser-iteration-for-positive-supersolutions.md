## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice. Let $n\ge2$, let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, let $A$ and $L_0$ be as in [[thm-de-giorgi-local-boundedness-for-homogeneous-subsolutions]], and let $u\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R)$ with $u>0$ a.e. be a positive weak supersolution of $L_0u=0$.
Then the negative-power chain of the Moser iteration holds: for every $0<\rho<1$, every $p>0$ and every ball $B_R(x_0)\Subset\Omega$,
$$\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_R(x_0)|}\int_{B_R(x_0)}u^{-p}dx\Bigr)^{-1/p}\le C(n,\theta,M_a,\rho,p)\ \operatorname{ess\,inf}_{B_{\rho R}(x_0)}u .$$
Moreover there is an exponent $p_0=p_0(n,\theta,M_a)>0$ such that, for every $B_R(x_0)\Subset\Omega$,
$$\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_{3R/4}(x_0)|}\int_{B_{3R/4}(x_0)}u^{p_0}dx\Bigr)^{1/p_0}\le C(n,\theta,M_a)\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_{3R/4}(x_0)|}\int_{B_{3R/4}(x_0)}u^{-p_0}dx\Bigr)^{-1/p_0}.$$
The proof does not assume that $u$ is bounded away from zero: the negative-power tests and positive moments are handled after regularisation $u\mapsto u+\varepsilon$; monotone convergence passes the increasing negative moments, while dominated convergence passes the decreasing positive moments. The logarithmic estimate [[lem-logarithmic-caccioppoli-estimate-for-positive-supersolutions]] supplies the input for the comparison of opposite powers.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice; an open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge2$; measurable symmetric coefficients $A$ with $\theta|\xi|^2\le\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\le M_a^2|\xi|^2$; the principal operator $L_0u=-D_i(a^{ij}D_ju)$ and its form $a_0$; a class $u\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R)$ with $u>0$ a.e. and $a_0(u,v)\ge0$ for every nonnegative $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; a ball $B_R(x_0)\Subset\Omega$ and $0<\rho<1$.

[F1] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Composition, products and density: a globally Lipschitz scalar composition of an $H^1$ class obeys the Sobolev chain rule; a compactly supported smooth factor obeys the weak product rule; and a compactly supported $H^1$ class lies in $H^1_0$ by zero extension and smooth approximation. Thus the bounded truncation of $U^\beta$ and its cutoff test in step 1.1 are admissible ([[thm-sobolev-chain-rule-for-globally-lipschitz-scalar-functions]], [[lem-weak-leibniz-rule-with-a-smooth-factor]], [[lem-compact-support-zero-extension-in-wkp]], [[cor-compactly-supported-smooth-functions-are-dense-in-wkp-of-rn]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]], [[def-weak-subsolution-and-supersolution-of-a-divergence-form-equation]], [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[F2] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Ellipticity and boundedness of the coefficients: $\theta|\xi|^2\le a^{ij}\xi_i\xi_j\le M_a^2|\xi|^2$ for a.e. point and every $\xi$ ([[def-uniformly-elliptic-divergence-form-operator]], [[lem-elliptic-form-is-well-defined-and-bounded]]).

[F3] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Sobolev input: there is $\kappa>1$, namely $\kappa=n/(n-2)$ for $n\ge3$ and any fixed finite $\kappa>1$ for $n=2$, and a constant $S$ with $\|w\|_{L^{2\kappa}(B_1)}\le S\|Dw\|_{L^2(B_1)}$ for every $w\in H^1_0(B_1)$ ([[cor-sobolev-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]], [[thm-critical-sobolev-embedding-into-every-finite-lq]]). In dimension two the gradient-only form follows directly from the zero-boundary supplier: set $r=2\kappa/(\kappa+1)\in(1,2)$, so $r^*=2\kappa$. The same smooth approximants and finite measure put $w$ in $W^{1,r}_0(B_1)$, and Holder gives $\|w\|_{2\kappa}\le C(2,r)\|Dw\|_r\le C(2,r)|B_1|^{1/(2\kappa)}\|Dw\|_2$.

[F4] Assume the Axiom of Choice. If $g\in L^\infty(B_\rho)$ on a finite-measure ball, then $\|g\|_{L^q(B_\rho)}\to\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}|g|$ as $q\to\infty$ ([[thm-l-p-norms-converge-to-the-essential-supremum-for-essentially-bounded-l-r-functions]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[def-essential-supremum-with-respect-to-a-measure]]).

[F5] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Logarithmic Caccioppoli estimate: for every $\eta\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, $\int_\Omega\eta^2|D\log(u+\varepsilon)|^2dx\le\frac{4M_a^2}{\theta}\int_\Omega|D\eta|^2dx$ ([[lem-logarithmic-caccioppoli-estimate-for-positive-supersolutions]]).

[F6] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Poincare-Wirtinger inequality on balls, and the existence of smooth bumps between concentric balls with $|D\eta|\le C/(s-r)$ ([[thm-poincare-inequality-on-a-ball]], [[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]]).

[F7] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Monotone and dominated convergence for the integral, used to pass to the limit $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ in the regularised estimates ([[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]], [[thm-dominated-convergence]], [[def-essential-supremum-with-respect-to-a-measure]], [[def-ball-average-operator-on-r-n]]).

[F8] Dyadic differentiation and layer cake: for a locally integrable function, averages over shrinking dyadic subcubes containing $x$ converge to its Lebesgue value at almost every $x$. To use the whole-space supplier on a fixed covering cube, first zero-extend its integrable restriction. At a Lebesgue point $x$, enclose each containing cube of side $s$ in $B_{\sqrt n s}(x)$; the volume ratio is fixed, so the cube average of $|g-g(x)|$ tends to zero by [[thm-almost-every-point-is-a-lebesgue-point]]. For $j>0$, $\int |g|^j=j\int_0^\infty t^{j-1}|\{|g|>t\}|\,dt$, with extended nonnegative values ([[thm-lebesgue-differentiation-theorem-for-locally-integrable-functions-on-r-n]], [[thm-layer-cake-formula-for-l-p-powers]]).
## Proof

Here $\operatorname{avg} g$ denotes the normalized integral of $g$ over the ball in the surrounding estimate.

**Proof technique:** direct; regularise by $u+\varepsilon$, test with bounded truncations of negative powers to derive a positive-power Sobolev iteration for $1/(u+\varepsilon)$, and use the scale-invariant logarithmic Caccioppoli estimate to obtain local mean oscillation, a dyadic stopping estimate and exponential integrability of the logarithm.

1.1 Scaling, bounded truncation, density and the energy estimate. Under $y=(x-x_0)/R$, the principal divergence form and the weak supersolution inequality retain the same ellipticity bounds, while ball averages are invariant; it is enough to work on $B_1$. Fix $\varepsilon>0$, put $U=u+\varepsilon$, and for $p>0$ choose $\beta=-p-1<-1$. Then $U\ge\varepsilon$, so $U^\beta$ and its weak gradient are bounded by constants (depending on $p,\varepsilon$) times $1$ and $|Du|$, respectively. More explicitly, for $N>\varepsilon^\beta$ the globally Lipschitz bounded truncation $P_{\varepsilon,N}(t):=\min\{(\max\{t,0\}+\varepsilon)^\beta,N\}$ satisfies $P_{\varepsilon,N}(u)=U^\beta$ a.e. The cutoff product $\eta^2P_{\varepsilon,N}(u)$ lies in $H^1_0(B_1)$ by the chain and product rules and compact-support zero extension; approximate it in $H^1_0$ by nonnegative smooth tests and use continuity of the form to pass the supersolution inequality to this test. Testing with $\eta^2U^\beta$ gives $$(p+1)\int_{B_1}\eta^2U^{-p-2}\langle A DU,DU\rangle\,dx \le2\int_{B_1}|\eta|U^{-p-1}|\langle A DU,D\eta\rangle|\,dx.$$ By Cauchy--Schwarz in the $A$-energy, $|\langle A DU,D\eta\rangle|\le\langle A DU,DU\rangle^{1/2}\langle A D\eta,D\eta\rangle^{1/2}\le M_a|D\eta|\langle A DU,DU\rangle^{1/2}$. Absorbing the resulting energy square root yields $$\int_{B_1}\eta^2U^{-p-2}\langle A DU,DU\rangle\,dx \le\frac{4M_a^2}{(p+1)^2}\int_{B_1}U^{-p}|D\eta|^2\,dx.$$ Ellipticity and $D(U^{-p/2})=-(p/2)U^{-p/2-1}DU$ then give $\int\eta^2|D(U^{-p/2})|^2\le\frac{M_a^2p^2}{(p+1)^2\theta}\int U^{-p}|D\eta|^2\le\frac{M_a^2}{\theta}\int U^{-p}|D\eta|^2$. [given, F1, F2, F6, algebra]

1.2 Local logarithmic oscillation. Put $w=\log U$ and $\ell=\frac{1}{|B_{7/8}|}\int_{B_{7/8}}w$. The logarithmic Caccioppoli estimate [F5], with a smooth cutoff supported in $B_1$ and equal to one on $B_{7/8}$, gives $\int_{B_{7/8}}|Dw|^2\,dx\le C(n,\theta,M_a)$; Poincare [F6] therefore gives $\|w-\ell\|_{L^2(B_{7/8})}\le C$. Cover $B_{3/4}$ by finitely many axis-parallel cubes $Q_0$ of a fixed side $s_n>0$ so small that their closures lie in $B_{13/16}$ and every concentric ball below lies in $B_1$. For each dyadic subcube $Q$ of side $s$, let $B$ be the concentric ball of radius $\sqrt n s$, which contains $Q$. The logarithmic estimate with a smooth cutoff equal to one on $B$ and supported in the concentric ball of radius $2\sqrt n s$ gives $\int_B|Dw|^2\le C s^{n-2}$. The ball Poincare inequality [F6] on $B$, together with $|B|/|Q|=|B_{\sqrt n}|$, then yields $\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|w-w_Q|\le K$, with $K=K(n,\theta,M_a)\ge1$ independent of $Q$, $\varepsilon$ and $Q_0$. [given, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 The reverse-exponent iteration. Let $\kappa=n/(n-2)$ for $n\ge3$ and fix $\kappa=2$ for $n=2$, so the $L^{2\kappa}$ Sobolev inequality is available in both cases by [F3]. Combining step 1.1 with the product rule and a cutoff equal to one on $B_r$ and supported in $B_s$, $0<r<s\le1$, yields $$\|U^{-1}\|_{L^{p\kappa}(B_r)}\le\Bigl(\frac{C}{s-r}\Bigr)^{2/p}\|U^{-1}\|_{L^p(B_s)},\qquad C=C(n,\theta,M_a).$$ For $p_j=p\kappa^j$ and $r_j=\rho+(1-\rho)2^{-j}$, apply this with $(p_j,r_{j+1},r_j)$ and multiply. The logarithm of the product is bounded by a constant multiple of $\sum_{j\ge0}(1+j)\kappa^{-j}<\infty$, so $$\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}U^{-1}\le C_1\|U^{-1}\|_{L^p(B_1)},\qquad C_1=C_1(n,\theta,M_a,p,\rho).$$ Taking reciprocals and inserting the volume factor gives $\bigl(\frac1{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}U^{-p}\,dx\bigr)^{-1/p}\le C_2\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{B_\rho}U$. This is a positive-exponent iteration for $U^{-1}$; in particular the reverse-exponent range is $p_j=p\kappa^j>0$, with arbitrary starting $p>0$. [step 1.1, F3, F4, F6, algebra]

2.2 Bounded truncations, stopping cubes and factorial moments. For each $M>0$ set $X_M:=\min\{|w-\ell|,M\}$. It is a bounded $H^1$ truncation by the Lipschitz chain rule; its mean oscillation on every dyadic subcube of a covering cube $Q_0$ is at most $2K$. By [F8], dyadic averages differentiate $X_M$ almost everywhere, so the stopping cubes cover the relevant superlevel set up to a null set. For $g=X_M$ and every dyadic cube $Q$, $\operatorname{avg}_Q|g-g_Q|\le2K$: compare first with the constant $\min\{|w_Q-\ell|,M\}$ using the $1$-Lipschitz scalar map, then with $g_Q$. Set $A=2^{n+3}K$. In each cube $Q$ select the maximal proper dyadic subcubes $P$ with $\operatorname{avg}_P|g-g_Q|>A$. They are disjoint and their total measure is at most $q|Q|$, where $q=2K/A=2^{-(n+2)}$. Their immediate parents are not bad, so $|g_P-g_Q|\le\operatorname{avg}_P|g-g_Q|\le2^n A$. Outside their union, dyadic differentiation gives $|g-g_Q|\le A$ a.e. Repeat the same selection inside each selected cube, recentering at its own mean; its mean oscillation is still at most $2K$. The generation-$m$ union has measure at most $q^m|Q_0|$, while outside it the accumulated mean differences and final good-set bound give $|g-g_{Q_0}|\le m2^n A$ for $m\ge1$. Consequently there are dimensional constants $C_n,c_n>0$ such that $$\frac{|\{x\in Q_0:|X_M-(X_M)_{Q_0}|>t\}|}{|Q_0|}\le C_n e^{-c_nt/K}.$$ The layer-cake formula [F8] then yields $\frac{1}{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}|X_M-(X_M)_{Q_0}|^j\le C_n j!(K/c_n)^j$ for every integer $j\ge1$. The factorial cancels the denominator in the exponential series: its $j$th averaged term is at most $C_n(p_0K/c_n)^j$. Choose $p_0:=\min\{1,c_n/(2K)\}>0$. The geometric bound and monotone convergence of the nonnegative series give $$\frac{1}{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}e^{p_0|X_M-(X_M)_{Q_0}|}\,dx\le C'_n.$$ By step 1.2 and the fixed cube size, $(X_M)_{Q_0}\le\frac{1}{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}|w-\ell|\le C$ uniformly in $M$, hence $\frac{1}{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}e^{p_0X_M}\,dx\le e^{p_0C}C'_n$. Since $X_M\uparrow|w-\ell|$, monotone convergence gives $\frac{1}{|Q_0|}\int_{Q_0}e^{p_0|w-\ell|}\,dx\le C_3$. Summing over the finite cover and normalizing yields $\frac{1}{|B_{3/4}|}\int_{B_{3/4}}e^{p_0|w-\ell|}\,dx\le C_4$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$. The bounded negative-power test in step 1.1 was placed in $H^1_0$ by compact-support smooth density; here the bounded logarithm truncations ensure every oscillation estimate is finite before the monotone limit. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F4, F6, F7, F8, algebra]

3.1 The product constant and the limit $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. Since $e^{\pm p_0(w-\ell)}\le e^{p_0|w-\ell|}$, step 2.2 gives $$\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_{3/4}|}\int_{B_{3/4}}U^{p_0}\,dx\Bigr)\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_{3/4}|}\int_{B_{3/4}}U^{-p_0}\,dx\Bigr)\le C_4^2,$$ hence the second assertion with comparison constant $C_4^{2/p_0}$. For the fixed exponent $p_0$, $U^{-p_0}\uparrow u^{-p_0}$ and $U^{p_0}\downarrow u^{p_0}$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$; dominated convergence for the positive moment (using $(u+1)^{p_0}\le u+1$ since $p_0\le1$ on this bounded ball) and monotone convergence for the negative moment pass the product bound. Since $u>0$ a.e., the limiting positive moment is strictly positive, so the product bound also shows that this particular negative moment is finite. For an arbitrary exponent $p>0$ in the first assertion, monotone convergence passes $\operatorname{avg} U^{-p}$ with its extended value; interpret $(+\infty)^{-1/p}=0$, so the reciprocal negative-moment inequality remains valid without asserting finiteness. The same scaling as in step 1.1 restores arbitrary $R,x_0$; all constants depend only on the listed parameters, and no positive lower bound for $u$ is assumed. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, F7, algebra] ∎
