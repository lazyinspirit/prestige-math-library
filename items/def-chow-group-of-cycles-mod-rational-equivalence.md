## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), inherited from the order
function of [[lem-order-function-one-dimensional-local-domain]]; it is used
there for the finiteness of the lengths, and here only through that order
function. Let $k$ be a field and let $X$ be a scheme locally of finite type
over $k$. For $d\in\mathbb Z$ let $\operatorname{Rat}_d(X)\subseteq Z_d(X)$ be
the subgroup generated by all cycles of the form
$$(i_W)_*\operatorname{div}(r),\qquad \operatorname{div}(r)=\sum_{V\subseteq W}\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O_{W,V}}(r)\,[V],$$
where $W\subseteq X$ is an integral closed subscheme of dimension $d+1$
([[def-integral-scheme]]), $i_W:W\hookrightarrow X$ is the inclusion,
$r\in k(W)^*$ is a nonzero rational function on $W$
([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]], [[def-field-of-fractions]]), the sum runs
over the integral closed subschemes $V\subseteq W$ of dimension $d$, and
$\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O_{W,V}}(r)$ is the order function of
[[lem-order-function-one-dimensional-local-domain]] for the one-dimensional
Noetherian local domain $\mathcal O_{W,V}$ ([[def-local-ring]]). The sum is
locally finite, and finite when $W$ is of finite type, as proved below. The
elements of $\operatorname{Rat}_d(X)$ are the $d$-cycles **rationally
equivalent to zero**; two cycles are **rationally equivalent** if their
difference is, written $\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}\beta$. The **Chow group of
$d$-cycles** is
$$A_d(X):=Z_d(X)/\operatorname{Rat}_d(X),\qquad A_*(X)=\bigoplus_{d}A_d(X)\quad\text{for finite type }X.$$

**Basic facts.** (i) Rational equivalence is an equivalence relation compatible
with the group structure, by definition. (ii) If $W$ is a *normal* integral
closed subscheme, then $\operatorname{div}(r)$ agrees with the principal Weil
divisor of $r$ computed with the divisor theory of
[[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]],
[[def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group]]; in particular for $X$ integral
of dimension $n$ and normal (e.g. smooth), $A_n(X)\cong\mathbb Z$ generated by
$[X]$ and $A_d(X)=0$ for $d>\dim X$. (iii) For $X$ equidimensional of pure
dimension $n$, set $A^p(X):=A_{n-p}(X)$; this is the codimension grading used
throughout. (iv) Equivalent presentation: $\alpha\sim_{\mathrm{rat}}0$ if and
only if $\alpha=\sum_i (i_{W_i})_*\operatorname{div}(r_i)$ as above; this is the
"Fulton 1.6" definition with supports on closed subschemes of
$X\times\mathbb P^1$ specialized, and it is Stacks' Definition 42.19.1 (tag
02RW). For merely locally finite type $X$, use the locally finite cycle group of
[[def-algebraic-cycle-and-cycle-group]] and locally finite sums of principal
divisors supported on locally finite families of the subvarieties $W$. These
sums define the rational-equivalence subgroup; finite generation as written
above applies when $X$ is of finite type. In the general case the total group
$A_*(X)$ is the quotient of the total locally finite cycle group by locally
finite sums of these relations, allowing unbounded dimensions as in
[[def-algebraic-cycle-and-cycle-group]]. Thus pullback along a non-quasi-compact
flat map uses locally finite cycles, not a finite-support sum with infinitely
many components.

**Well-definedness of the divisor sum.** Let $W$ be integral of dimension $d+1$
and $0\ne r\in k(W)^*$. On an affine open chart $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq W$
with $W$ of finite type over $k$, write $r=a/b$ with $a,b\in A\setminus\{0\}$
under the identification $k(W)=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$. For an integral closed
subscheme $V\subseteq W$ of dimension $d$ meeting the chart in a height-one
prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$, the order $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathcal O_{W,V}}(r)$
of [[lem-order-function-one-dimensional-local-domain]] vanishes unless
$\mathfrak p$ contains $a$ or $b$, hence unless $\mathfrak p$ contains $ab$; then
$\mathfrak p$ is minimal over the principal ideal $(ab)$, hence has height one
by [[cor-minimal-prime-over-a-nonzerodivisor-has-height-one]]. A Noetherian ring
has only finitely many minimal primes over the radical of a principal ideal
([[cor-radical-ideal-has-finitely-many-minimal-primes-noetherian]]), so only
finitely many $V$ meet a fixed affine chart with nonzero coefficient; since $W$
of finite type over a field is quasi-compact, a finite affine cover exhibits
$\operatorname{div}(r)$ as a finite sum, and for merely locally finite type $W$
it is a locally finite sum in the sense of
[[def-algebraic-cycle-and-cycle-group]]. Thus the displayed sum defines an
element of $Z_d(W)$ in the finite-type case and of the locally finite cycle
group in general, and the subgroup $\operatorname{Rat}_d(X)$ is well defined.
