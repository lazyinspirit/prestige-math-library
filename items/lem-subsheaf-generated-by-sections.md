## Statement

Let $X$ be a topological space, let $\mathcal F$ be a sheaf of
abelian groups on $X$ ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]), and let
$(s_\alpha\in\mathcal F(U_\alpha))_{\alpha\in A}$ be a family of sections indexed
by a set $A$, each $U_\alpha\subseteq X$ open. For $x\in X$ let
$$G_x:=\langle\,(s_\alpha)_x:\alpha\in A\text{ with }x\in U_\alpha\,\rangle\ \le\ \mathcal F_x$$
be the subgroup of the stalk generated by the germs of the generators that are
defined at $x$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]], [[def-germ-of-section]]). A
**subsheaf of abelian groups** means a subsheaf of the underlying sheaf of sets
([[def-subsheaf]]) whose sections over every open set form a subgroup of the
ambient section group, with the inherited operations. Then:

1. the prescription
$$\langle s_\alpha\rangle(W):=\{\,s\in\mathcal F(W):s_x\in G_x\text{ for every }x\in W\,\},\qquad W\subseteq X\text{ open},$$
defines a subsheaf of abelian groups $\langle s_\alpha\rangle\subseteq\mathcal F$, called the
subsheaf generated by the family $(s_\alpha)$;
2. $s_\alpha\in\langle s_\alpha\rangle(U_\alpha)$ for every $\alpha\in A$;
3. $\langle s_\alpha\rangle_x=G_x$ for every $x\in X$;
4. if $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ is a subsheaf of abelian groups with
$s_\alpha\in\mathcal H(U_\alpha)$ for every $\alpha$, then
$\langle s_\alpha\rangle\subseteq\mathcal H$; conversely, if
$\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ is a subsheaf with $\mathcal H_x=G_x$ for every
$x\in X$, then $\mathcal H=\langle s_\alpha\rangle$;
5. if $(t_\beta\in\mathcal F(V_\beta))_{\beta\in B}$ is a further family of
sections with $t_\beta\in\langle s_\alpha\rangle(V_\beta)$ for every
$\beta\in B$, then the subsheaf generated by the combined family equals
$\langle s_\alpha\rangle$.

The construction is functorial in the family in the evident way and uses no
choice principle. In particular, when $A$ is finite and each $U_\alpha$ is a
compact open set, the subsheaf generated by the $s_\alpha$ is generated by
finitely many sections over compact opens.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A subsheaf $\mathcal H$ of a sheaf $\mathcal G$ may be identified with a choice of a subset $\mathcal H(U)\subseteq\mathcal G(U)$ for every open $U$, with restriction maps inherited from $\mathcal G$, such that these subsets define a sheaf ([[def-subsheaf]]).

[F2] A sheaf is precisely a presheaf whose compatible local sections glue uniquely ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]).

[F3] Elements of the stalk $\mathcal F_x$ are equivalence classes of pairs $(U,s)$ with $U$ an open neighbourhood of $x$ and $s\in\mathcal F(U)$, where $(U,s)\sim(V,t)$ when $s$ and $t$ agree on some smaller open neighbourhood of $x$; for a sheaf of abelian groups the stalk is the filtered colimit of the section groups over the neighbourhoods of $x$ ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]).

[F4] The germ of a section is its class in the stalk, and if $V\subseteq U$ is open and contains $x$ then $(s|_V)_x=s_x$ ([[def-germ-of-section]]).

[F5] For a subset $S$ of a group $G$, the subgroup $\langle S\rangle$ generated by $S$ contains $S$ and is contained in every subgroup of $G$ that contains $S$; it is the smallest such subgroup and $\langle\varnothing\rangle=\{e\}$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

## Proof

**Given:** A topological space $X$, a sheaf of abelian groups $\mathcal F$ on $X$, a family of sections $s_\alpha\in\mathcal F(U_\alpha)$ over open sets, the subgroups $G_x\le\mathcal F_x$ generated by the germs of the generators defined at $x$, and the prescription $H(W)=\{s\in\mathcal F(W):s_x\in G_x$ for all $x\in W\}$.

1.1 Define $H(W):=\{s\in\mathcal F(W):s_x\in G_x\text{ for every }x\in W\}$ for every open $W\subseteq X$, the prescription of assertion 1. Each $H(W)$ is a subgroup: germs preserve addition and zero (compute these on representatives), and each $G_x$ is a subgroup, so zero belongs to $H(W)$ and $s-t$ belongs whenever $s,t$ do. This is a subpresheaf of abelian groups of $\mathcal F$: for $W'\subseteq W$ and $s\in H(W)$ one has $(s|_{W'})_x=s_x$ for $x\in W'$ [F4], so $s|_{W'}\in H(W')$. [F4, given] [F4]

1.2 Let $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ be a subsheaf and let $s\in\mathcal F(W)$ be a section over an open $W$ such that for every $x\in W$ the germ $s_x$ lies in the image of the germ map $\mathcal H(W_x)\to\mathcal F_x$ for some open neighbourhood $W_x\subseteq W$ of $x$. Consider the family $\mathcal W$ of all pairs $(V,h)$ with $V\subseteq W$ open and $h\in\mathcal H(V)\subseteq\mathcal F(V)$ such that $h=s|_V$; this is a family of sections indexed by a set and defined by a condition, and for every $x\in W$ there is a member whose first component contains $x$: by hypothesis there are $h\in\mathcal H(W_x)$ and a smaller open neighbourhood $V\subseteq W_x$ of $x$ on which $h$ and $s$ agree [F3], and the restriction $h|_V\in\mathcal H(V)$ satisfies $h|_V=s|_V$, so $(V,h|_V)\in\mathcal W$. Any two members $(V,h)$ and $(V',h')$ of $\mathcal W$ are compatible on $V\cap V'$, because both agree there with $s$. By [F2] the family $(h)_{(V,h)\in\mathcal W}$ glues to a section $h_0\in\mathcal H(W)\subseteq\mathcal F(W)$, and $h_0|_V=h=s|_V$ for every member $(V,h)$; since the first components of the members cover $W$, locality in $\mathcal F$ [F2] gives $h_0=s$, hence $s\in\mathcal H(W)$. [F2, F3] [F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Let $W=\bigcup_{i\in I}W_i$ be an open cover and let $s_i\in H(W_i)$ be compatible on the overlaps in the sense of [F2]. Each $s_i$ is a section of $\mathcal F$ over $W_i$ and the $s_i$ are compatible sections of $\mathcal F$, so by [F2] there is $s\in\mathcal F(W)$ with $s|_{W_i}=s_i$ for all $i$. For $x\in W$ pick $i$ with $x\in W_i$; then $s_x=(s|_{W_i})_x=(s_i)_x\in G_x$ by [F4] and the definition of $H(W_i)$, hence $s\in H(W)$. Locality of $H$ is inherited from $\mathcal F$, so $H$ satisfies the sheaf condition and is a subsheaf of $\mathcal F$ by [F1]; this proves assertion 1. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, given] [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1]

2.2 Let $\alpha\in A$ and $x\in U_\alpha$. The germ $(s_\alpha)_x$ is one of the generators of the subgroup $G_x$, so $(s_\alpha)_x\in G_x$ by [F5]; hence $s_\alpha\in H(U_\alpha)$ by the definition of $H$, which proves assertion 2. [F5, step 1.1]

2.3 Fix $x\in X$. An element of $H_x$ is the class of a section $s\in H(W)$ over an open neighbourhood $W$ of $x$ [F3], and its image under the germ map into $\mathcal F_x$ is $s_x$, which lies in $G_x$ by the definition of $H(W)$; two sections of $H$ over neighbourhoods of $x$ with the same germ in $\mathcal F_x$ agree on a smaller neighbourhood [F3] and therefore define the same element of $H_x$, so this is well defined and $H_x\subseteq G_x$ inside $\mathcal F_x$. Conversely let $g\in G_x$. The finite integer combinations of the generating germs form a subgroup containing those germs, and every subgroup containing them contains all such combinations. Thus [F5] identifies this subgroup with $G_x$, and $g$ is a finite integer combination $g=\sum_{j}n_j(s_{\alpha_j})_x$ of germs of generators with $x\in U_{\alpha_j}$, $1\le j\le m$. Put $W:=\bigcap_{j}U_{\alpha_j}$ (with $W=X$ for the empty combination), an open neighbourhood of $x$, and $t:=\sum_{j}n_j\,s_{\alpha_j}|_W\in\mathcal F(W)$. The stalk is the filtered colimit of the section groups over neighbourhoods of $x$ [F3], so the germ of an integer combination of sections over a common open set is the corresponding integer combination of germs, and $t_x=g$. For every $y\in W$ one has $y\in U_{\alpha_j}$ for all $j$, so $t_y=\sum_jn_j(s_{\alpha_j})_y\in G_y$; hence $t\in H(W)$ and $g=t_x\in H_x$. Therefore $H_x=G_x$ inside $\mathcal F_x$, which proves assertion 3. [F3, F5, step 1.1]

2.4 Let $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ be a subsheaf of abelian groups with $s_\alpha\in\mathcal H(U_\alpha)$ for every $\alpha\in A$. For $x\in X$ and $\alpha$ with $x\in U_\alpha$ the germ $(s_\alpha)_x\in\mathcal F_x$ is the image of the section $s_\alpha\in\mathcal H(U_\alpha)$ under the germ map [F4], so $G_x\subseteq\mathcal H_x$ because $\mathcal H_x$ is a subgroup of $\mathcal F_x$ containing all these germs [F5]. Now let $s\in H(W)$. For every $x\in W$ the germ $s_x$ lies in $G_x\subseteq\mathcal H_x$, so the hypothesis of [step 1.2] holds for $\mathcal H$ and $s$, and [step 1.2] gives $s\in\mathcal H(W)$. Hence $H\subseteq\mathcal H$ as subsheaves of $\mathcal F$ by [F1], which is the first half of assertion 4. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.2, step 1.1]

2.5 Conversely let $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal F$ be a subsheaf with $\mathcal H_x=G_x$ for every $x\in X$. If $s\in\mathcal H(W)$ for an open $W$, then $s_x\in\mathcal H_x=G_x$ for every $x\in W$, so $s\in H(W)$ and $\mathcal H\subseteq H$ by [F1]. For the reverse inclusion, if $s\in H(W)$, then every germ $s_x$ belongs to $G_x=\mathcal H_x$, so [step 1.2] gives $s\in\mathcal H(W)$. Hence $H\subseteq\mathcal H$, and $\mathcal H=H$, proving the second half of assertion 4 even when $\mathcal H$ is initially only a subsheaf of sets. [F1, step 1.2, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $(t_\beta\in\mathcal F(V_\beta))_{\beta\in B}$ be a further family with $t_\beta\in H(V_\beta)$ for every $\beta$, and let $G'_x\le\mathcal F_x$ be the subgroup generated by the germs at $x$ of the combined family. For $x\in X$ one has $G_x\subseteq G'_x$, and every new generator $(t_\beta)_x$ with $x\in V_\beta$ lies in $G_x$ because $t_\beta\in H(V_\beta)$; hence $G'_x=G_x$ for every $x$. The subsheaf $\mathcal H:=H$ satisfies $\mathcal H_x=G_x=G'_x$ for every $x$ by [step 2.3], so [step 2.5] applied to the combined family gives that the subsheaf generated by the combined family equals $H$, which is assertion 5. ∎ [step 2.3, step 2.5, step 1.1]
