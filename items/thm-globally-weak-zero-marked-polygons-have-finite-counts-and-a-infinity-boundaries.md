## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the coherent all-index regular data of the named supplier on a finite globally weak triple-free pointed multidiagram of rank $m\ge1$, with a basepoint in every one-system cut component. Fix any finite maximum arity. Label a polygon with $d$ incoming corners by $x_i\in L_{i-1}\cap L_i$, $1\le i\le d$, and output $y\in L_0\cap L_d$. Its positive boundary-edge order is $L_0,L_d,L_{d-1},\ldots,L_1$. Thus at an incoming corner the boundary traversal is $L_i\to L_{i-1}$; the incoming strip has $t=0$ on $L_{i-1}$ and $t=1$ on $L_i$. All counts are over $\mathbb F_2$ and impose zero multiplicity at every basepoint.

For $d\ge2$ the actual polygon count dimension is $\mu+d-2$; there is no triangle translation quotient. Its zero-dimensional total moduli are finite across ALL literal realized classes. Its one-dimensional total moduli compactify to compact one-manifolds whose boundaries are exactly the rigid polygon–polygon conformal splittings and rigid strip–polygon external breakings. Every such pair with matching generator has its actual exhaustive collar. No sphere, one-torus disk, constant polygon or additional escape stratum occurs. The same assertions hold after restricting to any actual class, using its actual concatenation rule.

Let $m_1$ count unparametrized index-one strips and, for $d\ge2$, let $m_d$ count polygons of fixed-map index $2-d$ with their $d-2$ conformal parameters. These finite maps satisfy the actual A-infinity identities
$$\sum_{r+s=d+1}\ \sum_{j=0}^{r-1}m_r(x_1,\ldots,x_j,m_s(x_{j+1},\ldots,x_{j+s}),x_{j+s+1},\ldots,x_d)=0.$$
Here $m_1$ on an output is included, and all label-compatible intermediate generators are summed. In particular the triangle map is a chain map and the quadrilateral count is its actual associativity homotopy.

For a generic coherent one-parameter triangle family with the same regular end strips, the universal index-minus-one moduli are finite; the universal index-zero moduli have compact one-dimensional boundary consisting of parameter endpoints and strip–triangle breakings. Their count gives the chain homotopy between the endpoint triangle maps. For rank $m=0$, the same count conclusions hold directly with the unique constant triangle counted once, zero differential, and all higher rigid operations zero. The source conformal boundary gives the same identities; no free strip-translation quotient is assigned to the constant point map.

This assertion concerns the supplied analytic choices, and does not assert a top-generator cycle, a Heegaard-move inverse, a Spin-c extension classification or filling identification.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The chosen coherent all-index regular structures, the input/output ordering and the zero-mark condition in the statement. Fixed-map index means the actual operator index before conformal and auxiliary parameters.

[F1] Actual coherent data exist, including all-index regular autonomous end strips, all-index regular stable polygons, exact generator charts, flat face errors and the relative one-parameter construction. ([[thm-globally-weak-multidiagrams-admit-coherent-all-index-regular-polygon-data]])

Polygon map sections have the indicated Fredholm index when finite source parameters are included. Bounded-energy sequences have actual stable-source, sphere/disk and strip-tree limits with no loss and additive degrees. The zero-mark condition excludes all nonconstant spheres and one-torus disks. ([[lem-globally-weak-pointed-polygons-have-finite-all-index-domains]], [[thm-bounded-energy-polygons-have-stable-source-bubble-and-strip-limits]])

## Proof

1.1 Keep the source and operator conventions literal. An incoming holomorphic strip coordinate near a real boundary puncture is $z=\exp(\pi(s+it))$. Positive boundary traversal therefore passes from its $t=1$ edge to its $t=0$ edge. Reversing the incoming label order in the ordered real marked-point chart gives precisely the boundary-edge order in the Statement. A stable source with $d+1$ punctures has $d-2$ real parameters by [F2]. Its map operator therefore has count index $\mu+d-2$. A strip has its actual free translation and count dimension $\mu-1$; a stable polygon has no continuous conformal automorphism. All these dimensions refer to the actual Hilbert zero charts, not an assumed domain index formula. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F3] to any sequence of zero-mark polygons of a fixed count dimension zero or one. The domains and energies are bounded across all actual classes. Degree additivity forces every limit component to be zero-marked. A sphere has the same nonnegative coefficient on the connected surface, so that coefficient is zero and its energy vanishes. A boundary disk on one torus has coefficient constant on each one-system cut component; every such component has a basepoint, so all its coefficients and its energy vanish. Thus there are no nonconstant bubbles. Constant ghost bubble trees with no remaining nonconstant component are deleted: a finite tree of such ghosts has a terminal unstable vertex, because the source has no original interior markings and the only original boundary markings are the prescribed punctures on its stable polygon part. Constant stable polygon vertices cannot occur, since they would lie in three distinct tori. The remaining finite limit consists solely of nonconstant stable polygons and nonconstant strip levels. Every one is regular by the ALL-index choice in [F1]; no claim about regularity of a previously unexamined negative-index level has been made. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let the remaining tree have vertex count dimensions $a_v$ and $q$ nodes. Each $a_v$ is nonnegative because that component is an actual regular solution after its stated strip phase. The actual Fredholm node-additivity in [F4] and the source-dimension equality in [F2] give $$a_{\mathrm{total}}=\sum_v a_v+q.$$ One can also see the same equality from the actual local product chart in [F4], which includes one neck or relative strip position for each node and no triangle translation. Consequently a zero-dimensional limit has $q=0$ and a single rigid component. A one-dimensional non-interior limit has $q=1$ and two rigid components. This rules out all multi-level and multi-conformal degenerations in these dimensions by actual operator and source dimensions, not by assigning formal dimensions to singular spaces. If a sequence has only one component, the exact end equation and the whole-strip cutoff inverse promote core convergence to global $H^3$ convergence: for its difference $w_n$ from the limiting map, the tail satisfies $D_0w_n=0$, hence $\|\chi w_n\|_{H^3}\le C\|\chi'w_n\|_{H^2}\to0$ on each end. In a nodal sequence the pregluing residual adds the exponentially small term already accounted for in [F4]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The rigid total set is finite. It is discrete in its actual zero charts by regularity. By step3.1 every sequence in it has a convergent subsequence in that same total set, in the global chart topology and with its fixed corners. If it were infinite, Countable Choice supplies an ordered $n$-tuple of distinct members for each positive integer $n$. Flatten those tuples and retain first occurrences to obtain a sequence of distinct members. A convergent subsequence would eventually lie in a single isolated zero chart, a contradiction. This proves finiteness without assuming a finite or countable set of homotopy classes. The argument applies to all lower arities and all corner tuples as well, so there are only finitely many candidate broken rigid pairs for a one-dimensional space. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, construct, contradiction]

5.1 Construct its compactification rather than inferring a boundary manifold from a list of limits. For each of the finitely many broken pairs, [F4] gives an injective exhaustive half-interval collar. An interior point has its ordinary regular one-dimensional zero chart. Distinct collars are eventually disjoint: otherwise a sequence of common maps has both two-component limits; the actual source tree coordinates and smooth convergence on their positive-energy cores identify the stable vertices, while a diverging strip shift loses one positive-energy core and a bounded shift is fixed by its compact-source phase. Thus the two broken pairs are identical. The same argument separates a collar endpoint from every other endpoint and every interior point. Interior charts are Hausdorff as actual map/source charts. Give the enlarged set this chart and collar topology. It is second-countable because the interior configuration spaces are second-countable and only finitely many collars were added. Every sequence has an interior or nodal convergent subsequence by steps2.1–3.1, so it is compact under Countable Choice: a countable open cover with no finite subcover would allow a sequence outside its first $n$ members; a convergent subsequence contradicts its containing cover member, and any open cover has a countable subcover from the countable base. It is therefore a compact Hausdorff one-manifold with precisely those boundary points. Its boundary has even cardinality. Explicitly choose finitely many coordinate intervals and endpoint half-intervals with smaller closures covering it, subdivide them at finitely many regular interior cut points, and cancel paired interior interval ends; each remaining boundary germ is counted once, giving parity zero. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Every label-compatible pair of rigid components contributes exactly one boundary point: the actual source sewing and nonlinear gluing of [F4] give its collar, and exhaustion gives no additional branches. The external strip breaks give $m_1m_d$ and the terms inserting $m_1$ at inputs. The stable conformal face whose consecutive block has $s$ inputs has the lower operation $m_s$ inserted in the complementary $m_r$, where $r+s=d+1$. These are exactly the planar faces of [F2], with no other faces and no redundant triangle automorphisms. The two-dimensional case $d=1$ uses the same strip compactness and phase charts: dimension-zero strips have index one, dimension-one strips have index two, and their only boundaries are pairs of rigid strips, since zero-mark bubbles have already been excluded and all strips are regular at all indices. Step5.1's finite endpoint parity thus proves the stated identity including $m_1^2=0$. For $d=2$ it is the triangle chain-map identity. For $d=3$ it is the two triangle-composition terms plus the differential of and inputs to the quadrilateral count; its rigid fixed-map index is minus one because it has one conformal parameter. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Restricting to an actual Whitney class does not require a domain classification. In a sufficiently small map/source chart, nearby maps are joined by the actual source-dependent local addition with their boundary and corners fixed; in a collar the concatenated class is precisely the class obtained by the explicit generator-chart sewing. Thus this class label is locally constant on every interior chart and every added collar. Each prescribed total class is an open and closed subset of the compactification, and has the same finite counts and endpoint identity. The proof never quotients literal classes by a fundamental-group action or supposes that equal domains imply equal classes. Marked-degree weights, when retained in a parallel finite-count setting, multiply under these same sewings because degrees add; the present counts impose zero at every basepoint and need no such further setting as an assumption. [F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally include the one family parameter in the relative triangle zero charts of [F1]. Their universal index is $\mu+1$ because the triangle has no conformal parameter. The end strips are fixed regular data. Universal index zero corresponds to $\mu=-1$ and has no nodal limit: any broken triangle vertex has universal dimension at least zero and any strip has count dimension at least zero, while a node adds one. Universal index one corresponds to $\mu=0$; its only non-interior ends have one rigid universal triangle and one rigid fixed strip. All previous degree, compactness, exact-tail and gluing arguments are uniform on this compact family, with the family parameter retained as a component coordinate, so they prove finite universal index-zero sets and actual compact universal one-manifolds. Their remaining boundary points are the fixed-parameter endpoint triangles. Parity gives $F_1+F_0=m_1H+H\,m_1$, with the appropriate sum of input differentials, where $H$ counts the universal index-minus-one triangles. At a regular family parameter the ordinary slice counts and dimensions are the fixed-parameter ones; the proof has used the universal dimension everywhere else. This establishes the claimed analytic triangle homotopy and fixed-data A-infinity boundaries, without asserting a top-generator calculation or Heegaard-move independence. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 In rank zero the target and every product torus are one point. The map operator is the zero map between zero vector spaces and has index zero; the actual solution space is exactly its source parameter space. Thus the three-marked triangle has one regular isolated solution. A polygon with more than three marks has positive conformal dimension and contributes no rigid operation; a nonconstant strip does not exist. The four-marked space is the compact interval of [F2], whose two endpoints are its two triangle products, counted twice over $\mathbb F_2$. Higher source face identities are the same finite-tree boundary identities. A fixed-end one-parameter triangle family is one interval, with its identical endpoint count and no index-minus-one solution. This proves the stated rank-zero case directly, without applying a free translation argument to a constant strip. [F2, construct, algebra] ∎
