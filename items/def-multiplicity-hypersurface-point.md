## Definition

Let $k$ be any field, let $n\in\mathbb N$, put $P=k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$, and
let $a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\in k^n$. Suppose $0\ne f\in P$ and $f(a)=0$. Write
the unique finite homogeneous decomposition
$$
f(a_1+t_1,\ldots,a_n+t_n)=\sum_{j\ge0}f_j(t_1,\ldots,t_n),
$$
where $f_j$ is homogeneous of total degree $j$. The **multiplicity of the
hypersurface equation $f$ at $a$**, denoted $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)$, is
the least $j$ for which $f_j\ne0$.

Let $\mathfrak m_a=(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)$ and
$R=P_{\mathfrak m_a}=\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^n_k,a}$, with maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_aR$. For $g\in R$ of finite order, write
$\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak m}(g)=d$ when
$$g\in\mathfrak m^d\setminus\mathfrak m^{d+1}.$$
Then $\operatorname{mult}_a(f)=\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak m}(f)$.
In particular, replacing the local equation by $uf$ for a unit
$u\in R^\times$ leaves its $\mathfrak m$-adic order unchanged.

This is multiplicity of the equation, unchanged under a local unit. It is
not an invariant of the reduced support: for every integer $r\ge1$,
$\operatorname{mult}_a(f^r)=r\operatorname{mult}_a(f)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a finite $n\in\mathbb N$, a point $a\in k^n$, and a
nonzero polynomial $f\in k[X_1,\ldots,X_n]$ with $f(a)=0$.

[F1] [[def-monomials-multidegree-and-total-degree]]: a polynomial has a unique
finite monomial expansion; grouping its monomials by total degree gives a
unique finite sum of homogeneous parts.

[F2] [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]]: a polynomial is
homogeneous of degree $d$ when each occurring monomial has total degree $d$.

[F3] [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]: $P_{\mathfrak m_a}$ consists of
fractions $g/s$ with $s\notin\mathfrak m_a$.

[F4] [[thm-quotient-is-field-iff-ideal-maximal]]: $P/\mathfrak m_a$ is a
field if and only if $\mathfrak m_a$ is maximal.

[F5] [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]: $P_{\mathfrak m_a}$ is a
nonzero local ring with unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_aP_{\mathfrak m_a}$.

[F6] [[lem-tangent-cone-initial-ideal-presentation]]: at the rational origin
of $\operatorname{Spec}k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$, the canonical graded map from the
polynomial ring to the associated graded local ring is an isomorphism; it
sends each variable to its degree-one initial class.

[F7] [[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]]: multiplication in the
associated graded ring is induced by multiplication in the local ring.

[F8] [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: every field is an integral domain.

[F9] [[cor-multivariate-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]: a
polynomial ring in finitely many variables over an integral domain is an
integral domain.

[F10] [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]: on an affine scheme,
the structure-sheaf stalk at a prime is canonically the corresponding ring
localization.

[F11] [[cor-maximal-ideals-are-prime]]: every maximal ideal of a commutative
ring is prime.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Translate coordinates by $t_i=X_i-a_i$: the substitution $t_i\mapsto X_i-a_i$ is a polynomial-ring isomorphism with inverse $X_i\mapsto t_i+a_i$, and evaluation at $a$ becomes evaluation at the origin. By [F1], the latter has kernel generated by the variables, since every monomial with zero constant term is divisible by some $t_i$; hence its transported kernel is $\mathfrak m_a=(X_1-a_1,\ldots,X_n-a_n)$. Thus $P/\mathfrak m_a\cong k$, so [F4] makes $\mathfrak m_a$ maximal and [F11] makes it prime; [F3] and [F5] identify $R$ as a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$. Evaluation extends to $R$ because every denominator outside $\mathfrak m_a$ has nonzero value at $a$, and it identifies $R/\mathfrak m$ with $k$. The affine stalk identification [F10] gives $R=\mathcal O_{\mathbb A^n_k,a}$; translation identifies this filtered local ring with $k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]_{(t_1,\ldots,t_n)}$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F10, F11, given, algebra]

2.1 Write $f(a+t)=\sum_j f_j(t)$ as in [F1]–[F2], and let $d$ be the least index with $f_d\ne0$; it exists because translation is an isomorphism and $f\ne0$, and $d\ge1$ because $f(a)=0$. Apply [F6] with $I=0$ at the rational origin: it identifies the associated graded local ring with $k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$, taking the degree-$j$ symbol of a polynomial to its degree-$j$ homogeneous part. Hence $f\in\mathfrak m^d$ and its class in $\mathfrak m^d/\mathfrak m^{d+1}$ is the nonzero polynomial $f_d$, so $f\notin\mathfrak m^{d+1}$ and its $\mathfrak m$-adic order is exactly the least degree $d$. [F1, F2, F6, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Let $u\in R^\times$. Its degree-zero initial class is its nonzero residue in $R/\mathfrak m\cong k$, while the degree-$d$ initial class of $f$ is $f_d\ne0$ by step 2.1. By [F7], the initial class of $uf$ is the product of these classes; [F8]–[F9] make the identified graded ring $k[t_1,\ldots,t_n]$ a domain, so the product is nonzero. Thus $uf\in\mathfrak m^d\setminus\mathfrak m^{d+1}$ and $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak m}(uf)=d=\operatorname{mult}_a(f)$. For $r\ge1$, the initial class of $f^r$ is $f_d^r$, nonzero and homogeneous of degree $rd$ in the same domain, so $\operatorname{mult}_a(f^r)=rd$. [F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 In one variable, $f=(X-a)^3+(X-a)^4$ has multiplicity $3$, unchanged after multiplication by the local unit $1+(X-a)$ by step 3.1; $X-a$ has multiplicity $1$. Degree zero cannot occur for a nonzero polynomial vanishing at $a$, and the zero polynomial is excluded because it has no least nonzero homogeneous part. If $n=0$, every polynomial is constant, so no nonzero polynomial vanishes at the unique point of $k^0$. The proof uses only the fixed coordinate translation, the unique finite polynomial expansion, and a fixed local unit; it makes no family selection and uses neither AC nor DC. There is no interval or endpoint parameter and no iff assertion in this definition. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
