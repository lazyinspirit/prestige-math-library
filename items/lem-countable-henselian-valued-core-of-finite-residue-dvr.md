## Statement

Let $R$ be a Henselian DVR with finite residue field $k$ and fraction field $K$. Given finitely many elements of $K$, there is a countably enumerable subfield $K_0\subseteq K$ containing them such that $R_0=R\cap K_0$ is a Henselian DVR, has the same value group and residue field as $R$, and is dense in $R$ for the valuation topology. The ring $R_0$, its valuation, the inclusion of the given elements and the field operations have an explicit countable relational code in a single subset of $\mathbb N$. No choice axiom is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Henselian DVR, its finite residue field and the finitely many prescribed elements of its fraction field.

[F1] A Henselian local ring lifts each simple residue root of a monic polynomial uniquely. [[cor-henselian-local-simple-root-criterion]]

[F2] Unique lifting of simple residue roots for all monic polynomials implies the Henselian factor-lifting property. [[lem-simple-root-form-recovers-factor-lifting]]

[F3] A local ring is Henselian when its maximal-ideal pair has the coprime monic factor-lifting property. [[def-henselian-pair-and-henselian-local-ring]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** close an explicitly enumerated valued subfield under uniquely specified Hensel lifts.

1.1 Choose one uniformizer $\pi$, one representative in $R$ of each element of the finite field $k$, and the finitely many given elements. Let $K_0^{(0)}$ be the field they generate over the prime field, and put $R_0^{(0)}=K_0^{(0)}\cap R$. Enumerate the field by finite rational expressions in those generators; expressions with zero denominator are assigned zero. Mark the enumerated elements lying in $R$. These finite choices and the explicit enumeration use no choice axiom. [given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $K_0^{(m)}$ and its enumeration have been defined and put $R_0^{(m)}=K_0^{(m)}\cap R$. Enumerate monic polynomials with coefficients in $R_0^{(m)}$, padding invalid codes by a fixed polynomial. Enumerate the finitely many residue elements for each polynomial. Whenever a residue element is a simple root, [F1] gives its unique lift in $R$; otherwise use zero. By replacement these uniquely determined lifts form an enumerated family. Let $K_0^{(m+1)}$ be the field generated by $K_0^{(m)}$ and that family, enumerated by finite rational expressions. Ordinary recursion defines the sequence of fields without countable choice. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Put $K_0=\bigcup_{m\ge0}K_0^{(m)}$ and $R_0=K_0\cap R$. Pairing a stage number with an enumeration index explicitly enumerates $K_0$ and, by padding elements outside $R$ with zero, $R_0$. The inherited valuation has value group $\mathbb Z$ because $\pi\in K_0$; it makes $R_0$ a DVR and its residue field is $k$ because the chosen representatives lie in $R_0$. Any monic polynomial over $R_0$ and simple residue root already appear at one finite stage; step 2.1 puts the unique lift in $R_0$. By [F2] and [F3], $R_0$ is Henselian. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For any $x\in R$ and $n\ge1$, successive subtraction of the chosen residue representatives produces $a_0,\dots,a_{n-1}$ with $x-\sum_{j<n}a_j\pi^j\in\pi^nR$. This finite sum lies in $R_0$, proving density. Encode the explicit enumeration by indices, with equality, addition, multiplication, the valuation-ring predicate and the finitely many distinguished elements as subsets of finite powers of $\mathbb N$; standard pairing interleaves these relations into one subset of $\mathbb N$. The relations specify a structure isomorphic to $K_0$ with its valuation ring and distinguished coefficients. No new countable family of arbitrary choices is made. [step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
