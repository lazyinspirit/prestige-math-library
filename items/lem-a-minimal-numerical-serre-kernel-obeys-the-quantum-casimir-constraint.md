## Statement

Let $\nu=\sqrt{p^a}>1$, $K=\mathbb Q(\nu)$, $t\in\{\nu,\nu^{-1}\}$, and let $U_t$ be the actual integral-form specialization from [[lem-the-nondegenerate-integral-specialization-has-a-quantum-casimir]]. Let $T=K\langle F_i:i\in I\rangle$ be the free negative tensor algebra, let $J=\ker(T\to U_t^-)$, and let $S\subseteq J$ be the two-sided numerical quantum Serre ideal at parameter $t$. If $J/S\ne0$ and $\beta\in Q_+$ has least height with $(J/S)[-\beta]\ne0$, then
$$ (\beta,\beta)=2\operatorname{ht}\beta>0. $$
The quantum Casimir is used only on the already established actual specialization $U_t$, whose pairing is nondegenerate; it is not assumed to act on the numerical Serre-only algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The free half $T$, its map to the actual integral-specialized half $U_t^-$, the ideals $J$ and $S$ at parameter $t$, and a least-height residual degree $-\beta$.

[F1] The actual specialization $U_t$ has triangular decomposition, $M_{U_t}(\lambda)=U_t^-v_\lambda$, and on every subquotient of $\bigoplus_iM_{U_t}(-\alpha_i)$ a nonzero highest vector of label $-\beta$ has $(\beta,\beta)=2\operatorname{ht}\beta$ ([[lem-the-nondegenerate-integral-specialization-has-a-quantum-casimir]]).

[F2] The explicit Serre-free crossing calculation and opposite-Serre commutator calculation of [[lem-the-generic-borel-half-and-opposite-half-form-a-dj-crossed-double]] use only Laurent monomials, quantum Gaussian identities and the denominators $q-q^{-1}$. In its opposite-generator commutator computation, the opposite commutator of every Serre generator is zero by the identities $C_r[N-r]=C_{r+1}[r+1]$ and $\sum_r(-1)^r C_rq^{\pm(N-1)r}=0$. These identities remain valid at $q=t$, since $t>0$, $t\ne1$, and their sole denominator is nonzero. No field PBW dimension assertion is being specialized.

## Proof

1.1 Let $\widetilde U$ be the algebra generated by all $E_i,F_i,K_h$ with the toral, toral-action and mixed relations, and no relations within either root half. For every integral label $\lambda$, its universal highest module is freely based by $T v_\lambda$. To verify this without a presentation freeness assumption, define $F_i$ by left concatenation, $K_h$ on the word of degree $-\gamma$ by $t^{\langle\lambda-\gamma,h\rangle}$, and, for $w=F_{i_1}\cdots F_{i_n}$, define $E_jwv_\lambda$ to be the sum over $r$ with $i_r=j$ of $[\langle\lambda-\sum_{s>r}\alpha_{i_s},h_j\rangle]_t$ times the word with its $r$th letter deleted. The deletion formula satisfies $E_iF_jw=F_jE_iw+\delta_{ij}(K_i-K_i^{-1})w/(t-t^{-1})$; its labels verify both toral action relations. There are no further root-half relations to check. Thus these are $\widetilde U$-modules. Conversely the crossing relations move every positive and toral letter to the right in a highest module, so negative words span; the displayed representation makes them independent and proves the universal property. Denote this module by $\widetilde M(\lambda)$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The actual algebra $U_t$ is a quotient of $\widetilde U$: by [F1] its undivided roots and torus generate it and satisfy all the required mixed and toral relations. The canonical module map $\widetilde M(\lambda)\to M_{U_t}(\lambda)$ is, on negative coordinates, exactly $T\to U_t^-$. Its kernel is consequently $Jv_\lambda$ for every $\lambda$, so this subspace is a $\widetilde U$-submodule. At $\lambda=0$ the augmentation subspace $T_+v_0$ is also a submodule: the deletion formula cannot produce a scalar except from a single letter, whose coefficient is $[0]_t=0$. Each $F_iv_0$ is highest of label $-\alpha_i$. Therefore the maps $\widetilde M(-\alpha_i)\to T_+v_0$, $v_{-\alpha_i}\mapsto F_iv_0$, are module maps, and their direct sum is an isomorphism because $T_+=\bigoplus_iTF_i$ is the unique last-letter decomposition. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Induce that direct sum along $\widetilde U\to U_t$. The universal property, or the mutually inverse maps $u\otimes(av_\lambda)\mapsto ua v_\lambda$ and $a v_\lambda\mapsto a\otimes v_\lambda$, gives $V:=U_t\otimes_{\widetilde U}(T_+v_0)\cong\bigoplus_iM_{U_t}(-\alpha_i)$. Define $\ell:J\to V$ by $\ell(a)=1\otimes av_0$. Since $Jv_0$ is a submodule by step 2.1, its image $\ell(J)$ is a $U_t$-submodule of $V$. For $a=\sum_i u_iF_i$, the direct-sum coordinates of $\ell(a)$ are $(\pi(u_i)v_{-\alpha_i})_i$. By [F1], this is zero if and only if each $u_i\in J$. Consequently $\ker\ell=\sum_iJF_i=JT_+$; the last equality follows by splitting the last letter of a nonempty right factor and using that $J$ is a two-sided ideal. No flatness of tensor induction was assumed: its kernel is computed directly in the known Verma coordinates. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The subspace $Sv_0$ is a $\widetilde U$-submodule. Multiplication by a negative generator and toral action preserve it. For a positive generator, the specialized opposite-Serre commutator of [F2] vanishes on every Serre generator $s$. Expanding $[E_i,a s b]=[E_i,a]s b+a s[E_i,b]$ leaves a Serre factor in each term; moving its toral factors across this homogeneous factor multiplies it only by a scalar. The deletion formula therefore sends $Sv_0$ into itself. It follows that $\ell(S)$ is a $U_t$-submodule of $V$, contained in $\ell(J)$, and $\ell(J)/\ell(S)$ is a submodule of $V/\ell(S)$. By [F1], the Casimir acts by $1$ throughout this quotient. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Choose $a\in J[-\beta]$ with $a\notin S$. Its image $\ell(a)+\ell(S)$ is nonzero. Otherwise $a-s\in\ker\ell=JT_+$ for a homogeneous $s\in S[-\beta]$; writing $a-s=\sum_i u_iF_i$ gives $u_i\in J[-\beta+\alpha_i]$ of smaller height, hence $u_i\in S$ by minimality. This would give $a\in S$, a contradiction. Moreover $E_iav_0$ belongs to $Jv_0$ in lower degree by step 2.1, and therefore belongs to $Sv_0$. Thus the nonzero image of $a$ in $\ell(J)/\ell(S)$ is highest of label $-\beta$. Apply [F1] to the containing subquotient $V/\ell(S)$: $(\beta,\beta)=2\operatorname{ht}\beta$. Degree zero and every pure simple-root polynomial component survive $T\to U_t^-$ by the integral rank-one half, so in particular $\beta\ne0$; its positive height makes the right side strictly positive. This proves the claim. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
