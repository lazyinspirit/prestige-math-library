## Statement

Fix $m\ge1$ and let $A_m\text{-mod}$ be the abelian category of finite
generated graded left $A_m$-modules and degree-zero maps of
[[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]]. Let
$$0\to M'\xrightarrow{i}M\xrightarrow{q}M''\to0$$
be degreewise exact in $A_m\text{-mod}$, so that $i$ is injective, $q$ is
surjective and $\operatorname{im}i=\ker q$ degreewise, and suppose that $M'$
admits a finite graded projective resolution of length at most $a$ and $M''$
one of length at most $b$, in the sense that the resolutions
$$0\to P'_a\to\cdots\to P'_1\to P'_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon'}M'\to0,\qquad 0\to P''_b\to\cdots\to P''_1\to P''_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon''}M''\to0$$
consist of finitely generated graded projective modules with $P'_n=0$ for
$n>a$ and $P''_n=0$ for $n>b$. Then $M$ admits a finite graded projective
resolution of length at most $\max(a,b)$, that is, there is an exact sequence
$$0\to P_{\max(a,b)}\to\cdots\to P_1\to P_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}M\to0$$
of finitely generated graded projectives with $P_n=0$ for
$n>\max(a,b)$.

In particular, writing $\operatorname{pd}$ for the least such length (so that
$\operatorname{pd}X=0$ exactly when $X$ is finite graded projective, and the
zero module has $\operatorname{pd}0=0$), every degreewise extension of two
objects of finite projective dimension has finite projective dimension with
$\operatorname{pd}M\le\max(\operatorname{pd}M',\operatorname{pd}M'')$. The
argument is a horseshoe construction carried out on elements of graded modules
and selects only finitely many lifts; it uses no choice principle and no
arbitrary-abelian-category element chase.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the abelian category $A_m\text{-mod}$ over the algebra $A_m$, and a degreewise short exact sequence $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ in it.

[L1] $A_m\text{-mod}$ is the abelian category of finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules and degree-zero maps; kernels, cokernels and finite biproducts are computed degreewise and a sequence is exact precisely when it is exact degreewise; each vertex projective $P_i=A_me_i$ is a finite graded projective left $A_m$-module ([[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]]).

[L2] $A_m$ is free of rank $4m+1$ as a graded abelian group with basis the $4m+1$ displayed vertex, arrow and return classes, so its underlying $\mathbb Z$-module is free of finite rank $4m+1$ ([[lem-the-khovanov-seidel-algebra-has-the-four-m-plus-one-path-basis]]).

[F3] Every principal ideal domain is Noetherian; $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain ([[cor-principal-ideal-domains-are-noetherian]]).

[L4] Every finitely generated left module over a left Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]]).

[F5] A unital ring $R$ is left Noetherian when its left regular module ${}_RR$ is Noetherian, and unqualified "Noetherian ring" means left Noetherian ([[def-noetherian-ring]]).

[L6] A left $R$-module $M$ is Noetherian when every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-module]]).

[F7] A graded left $A_m$-module $P$ is graded projective when every degree-zero epimorphism $q:E\twoheadrightarrow M$ and every degree-zero $f:P\to M$ admit a degree-zero lift $\widetilde f:P\to E$ with $q\widetilde f=f$, and $P$ is finite graded projective when in addition it is generated by finitely many homogeneous elements; the zero module is finite graded projective ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[L8] A graded left $A$-module $P$ is finite graded projective if and only if it is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of internal shifts $A\{r_1\}\oplus\cdots\oplus A\{r_n\}$ ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[F9] A projective resolution of an object $X$ of an abelian category is an augmented chain complex $\cdots\to P_1\to P_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}X\to0$ with every $P_n$ projective that is exact at every displayed term ([[def-projective-resolution-in-an-abelian-category]]).

[F10] For an $A_m$-linear degree-zero map $f:X\to Y$ of graded left $A_m$-modules, the kernel and the image are graded submodules of $X$ and $Y$ and are computed degreewise; a map is a monomorphism exactly when it is injective and an epimorphism exactly when it is surjective ([[lem-graded-module-kernels-cokernels-and-biproducts-are-degreewise]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *$A_m$ is left Noetherian.* By [L2] the underlying $\mathbb Z$-module of $A_m$ is free of finite rank, hence finitely generated, and $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian by [F3]; so $A_m$ is a Noetherian $\mathbb Z$-module by [L4], which by [L6] means that every $\mathbb Z$-submodule of $A_m$ is finitely generated as a $\mathbb Z$-module. A left ideal of $A_m$ is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule, so every left ideal is finitely generated as a $\mathbb Z$-module and hence as a left ideal; since the submodules of the left regular module ${}_A A_m$ are exactly the left ideals, the regular module is Noetherian and [F5] makes $A_m$ left Noetherian. [L2, F3, L4, F5, L6]

1.2 *The lift and the comparison map $\varphi$.* For the augmentation $\varepsilon'':P''_0\to M''$ of [F9] and the epimorphism $q:M\to M''$, the module $P''_0$ is finite graded projective and $q$ is a degree-zero epimorphism, so the lifting property of [F7] produces a degree-zero $g:P''_0\to M$ with $qg=\varepsilon''$. Let $\varphi:P'_0\oplus P''_0\to M$ be the degree-zero map $\varphi(x',x''):=i\varepsilon'(x')+g(x'')$; both summands lie in $M$, and $\varphi$ is $A_m$-linear because $i$, $\varepsilon'$ and $g$ are. [F7, F9]

1.3 *Projectivity of the prepended term.* The direct sum $P'_0\oplus P''_0$ is a finite graded projective left $A_m$-module: by [L8] the modules $P'_0$ and $P''_0$ are degree-zero direct summands of finite direct sums of shifts of $A_m$, and the direct sum of two such summands, with the summand inclusions and projections added componentwise, is a degree-zero direct summand of the direct sum of the two finite direct sums of shifts, which is again a finite direct sum of shifts of $A_m$; so [L8] applies to $P'_0\oplus P''_0$. [L8, F7]

1.4 *The horseshoe recursion.* We prove by induction on $N\ge0$ the statement $\mathrm{P}(N)$: for every degreewise short exact sequence $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ in $A_m\text{-mod}$ and all integers $a,b\ge0$ with $\max(a,b)\le N$ such that $M'$ has a finite graded projective resolution of length at most $a$ and $M''$ one of length at most $b$, the module $M$ has a finite graded projective resolution of length at most $\max(a,b)$.

2.1 *Submodules of finitely generated graded modules are finitely generated.* Let $N$ be a finitely generated graded left $A_m$-module and $S\le N$ a submodule. Then $N$ is a finitely generated left module over the left Noetherian ring $A_m$ of step 1.1, so [L4] makes $N$ Noetherian, and by [L6] the submodule $S$ is finitely generated over $A_m$. Applied to $S$ a graded submodule, this gives a finite $A_m$-generating family of $S$, which by the homogeneous-component rewriting recorded in [F7] may be taken homogeneous; in particular the kernel of any degree-zero map of finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules is again an object of $A_m\text{-mod}$. [step 1.1, L4, L6, F7]

2.2 *$\varphi$ is an epimorphism.* Let $m\in M$. Since $\varepsilon'':P''_0\to M''$ is surjective by [F9], there is $x''\in P''_0$ with $q(m)=\varepsilon''(x'')=qg(x'')$; then $m-g(x'')\in\ker q=\operatorname{im}i$, so $m-g(x'')=i(m')$ for some $m'\in M'$, and surjectivity of $\varepsilon'$ gives $x'\in P'_0$ with $\varepsilon'(x')=m'$. Thus $\varphi(x',x'')=i\varepsilon'(x')+g(x'')=i(m')+g(x'')=m$, so $\varphi$ is surjective and hence an epimorphism by [F10]. [step 1.2, F9, F10]

3.1 *The kernel stays finitely generated.* Let $K:=\ker\varphi\le P'_0\oplus P''_0$. The module $P'_0$ is finite graded projective, in particular finitely generated, and so is $P''_0$; hence $P'_0\oplus P''_0$ is finitely generated as a graded left $A_m$-module, and by step 2.1 the submodule $K$ is finitely generated, so $K$ is an object of $A_m\text{-mod}$. [step 2.1, F7]

4.1 *The syzygy sequence is degreewise short exact.* Put $K':=\ker\varepsilon'\le P'_0$ and $K'':=\ker\varepsilon''\le P''_0$. The projection $\pi:K\to K''$, $(x',x'')\mapsto x''$, lands in $K''$: if $\varphi(x',x'')=i\varepsilon'(x')+g(x'')=0$, then $\varepsilon''(x'')=qg(x'')=-qi\varepsilon'(x')=0$. The inclusion $\jmath:K'\to K$, $x'\mapsto(x',0)$, has image exactly $\ker\pi$, since $(x',0)\in K$ means $i\varepsilon'(x')=0$, equivalently $\varepsilon'(x')=0$ because $i$ is injective. The map $\pi$ is surjective: given $x''\in K''$, one has $qg(x'')=\varepsilon''(x'')=0$, so $g(x'')=i(m')$ for some $m'\in M'$; choose $x'\in P'_0$ with $\varepsilon'(x')=-m'$, possible because $\varepsilon'$ is surjective. Then $\varphi(x',x'')=-i(m')+g(x'')=0$, and $(x',x'')\in K$ maps to $x''$. These are degree-zero module maps, and [L1] makes $0\to K'\xrightarrow{\jmath}K\xrightarrow{\pi}K''\to0$ degreewise exact in $A_m\text{-mod}$; $K,K',K''$ are finite graded modules by step 3.1 and step 2.1. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L1, F10]

*Base case $N=0$.* Here $a=b=0$, so the resolutions $0\to P'_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon'}M'\to0$ and $0\to P''_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon''}M''\to0$ are exact, so $\varepsilon'$ and $\varepsilon''$ are isomorphisms and $K'=K''=0$. By step 4.1 the sequence $0\to0\to K\to0\to0$ is degreewise exact, so $K=0$; by steps 1.2 and 2.2 the map $\varphi:P'_0\oplus P''_0\to M$ is an epimorphism with zero kernel, hence an isomorphism by [F10], and $M$ is finite graded projective by step 1.3. So $0\to P'_0\oplus P''_0\xrightarrow{\varphi}M\to0$ is a finite graded projective resolution of $M$ of length $0=\max(a,b)$, and $\mathrm{P}(0)$ holds.

*Inductive step.* Assume $N\ge1$ and $\mathrm{P}(N-1)$, and let $a,b$ with $c:=\max(a,b)\le N$ be given. If $c=0$, the base-case argument already proves the claim. Suppose $c\ge1$. If $a\ge1$, set $a^*:=a-1$; if $a=0$, set $a^*:=0$. Then $a^*\le N-1$ in both cases, because $a\ge1$ gives $a-1\le N-1$ and $a=0$ gives $a^*=0\le N-1$ as $N\ge1$. Define $b^*$ from $b$ in the same way, so $b^*\le N-1$ as well. First syzygies have resolutions of these lengths: if $a\ge1$, the exactness of the resolution of [F9] at $P'_1,\dots,P'_{a-1}$ and the vanishing $P'_{a+1}=0$ show that $0\to P'_a\to\cdots\to P'_1\to K'\to0$ is a finite graded projective resolution of $K'$ of length $a-1=a^*$; if $a=0$, then $K'=0$ and the zero complex $0\to0\to K'\to0$ is a finite graded projective resolution of $K'$ of length $0=a^*$, the zero module being finite graded projective by [F7]; the same alternative holds for $K''$ with $b^*$. By step 4.1 the sequence $0\to K'\to K\to K''\to0$ is degreewise short exact in $A_m\text{-mod}$, so $\mathrm{P}(N-1)$ applies to it with the bounds $a^*,b^*$ and yields a finite graded projective resolution of $K$ of length at most $\max(a^*,b^*)=c-1\le N-1$. Prepending the epimorphism $\varphi:P'_0\oplus P''_0\to M$ of step 1.2, with kernel $K$ the finitely generated module of step 3.1, produces $0\to\cdots\to P_0\xrightarrow{\varphi}M\to0$ with $P_0:=P'_0\oplus P''_0$ finite graded projective by step 1.3 and all higher terms those of the resolution of $K$, so $M$ has a finite graded projective resolution of length at most $\max(a^*,b^*)+1=c=\max(a,b)\le N$, and $\mathrm{P}(N)$ holds.

Every step of the recursion is a single application of the lifting property of [F7] and finitely many element choices inside a fixed module, and the induction hypothesis is applied to finitely many sequences; no family indexed by an infinite set is selected. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 1.3, F7, F9, F10]

5.1 *Conclusion.* Applying $\mathrm{P}(N)$ of step 1.4 with $N:=\max(a,b)$ proves the first claim: a degreewise short exact sequence $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ in $A_m\text{-mod}$ whose outer terms carry finite graded projective resolutions of lengths at most $a$ and $b$ makes $M$ carry one of length at most $\max(a,b)$; taking $a=\operatorname{pd}M'$ and $b=\operatorname{pd}M''$ gives $\operatorname{pd}M\le\max(\operatorname{pd}M',\operatorname{pd}M'')$, and the construction never selects more than finitely many lifts, so no choice principle is used. [step 1.4, F7] ∎
