## Statement

A rational prime $p$ ramifies in $K/\mathbb Q$ if and only if $p\mid d_K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A number field $K/\mathbb Q$ and a rational prime $p$.

[F1] The ring of integers $\mathcal O_K$ is a commutative ring and is a free $\mathbb Z$-module of rank $n=[K:\mathbb Q]$; any integral basis $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ is also a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $K$ ([[def-number-field]], [[def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]], [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]], [[thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree]]).

[F2] For prime $p$, $\mathbb F_p=\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z$ is a field with $p$ elements ([[def-prime]], [[thm-z-mod-p-is-a-field]], [[prop-integers-modulo-n-as-a-quotient-ring]], [[def-finite-field-and-its-order]], [[prop-canonical-quotient-ring-map]]).

[F3] For every finite field extension $L/F$, the field trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/F}(z)$ is the trace of the $F$-linear multiplication map $m_z:x\mapsto zx$; below this is applied to $K/\mathbb Q$ and to finite extensions of $\mathbb F_p$ ([[def-field-norm-and-trace]]).

[F4] For an integral basis, the discriminant $d_K$ is the determinant of its trace-pairing matrix, independent of the chosen integral basis ([[def-discriminant-of-a-number-field-basis-and-order]]).

[F5] Every nonzero integral ideal of $\mathcal O_K$ has a unique finite factorisation into powers of distinct nonzero prime ideals, constructed in ZF. For $p\mathcal O_K=\prod_i\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$, the $\mathfrak P_i$ are exactly the primes above $(p)$, $e_i=e(\mathfrak P_i/p)$, and $p$ is unramified exactly when every $e_i=1$ ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]], [[def-prime-above-and-residue-degree]], [[def-ramification-index]], [[def-split-inert-ramified-and-unramified-prime]]).

[F6] A nonzero ideal quotient of $\mathcal O_K$ is finite; quotienting by a prime ideal gives an integral domain, and a quotient is a field exactly when the ideal is maximal ([[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]], [[thm-subset-of-a-finite-set]], [[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]], [[thm-quotient-is-field-iff-ideal-maximal]], [[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]]).

[F7] Pairwise comaximal ideals satisfy the Chinese remainder theorem ([[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]).

[F8] If $E/\mathbb F_q$ is a finite extension of finite fields of degree $f$, then its Galois group is cyclic of order $f$, generated by the relative Frobenius $\sigma_q(x)=x^q$ ([[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]], [[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]]).

[F9] A finite family of distinct homomorphisms from a group into a field's multiplicative group is linearly independent over the field ([[thm-dedekind-linear-independence-of-characters]]).

[F10] A $f$-dimensional vector space over a field of $q$ elements has $q^f$ elements; a nonzero degree-$d$ polynomial over a field has at most $d$ roots ([[lem-cardinality-of-a-finite-dimensional-space-over-a-finite-field]], [[thm-root-bound-for-polynomials-over-a-domain]]).

[F11] Endomorphism trace is basis-independent and invariant under similarity ([[def-trace-of-an-endomorphism]], [[cor-trace-is-invariant-under-similarity]]).

[F12] In finite dimension, an independent set extends to a basis of any containing subspace without a choice principle; a bilinear form is nondegenerate exactly when its matrix is invertible, and a square matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is a unit ([[thm-dimension-of-a-linear-subspace]], [[def-matrix-radicals-rank-and-nondegeneracy-of-a-bilinear-form]], [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]]).

[F13] The field trace of every algebraic integer in a number field is an integer ([[cor-trace-and-norm-of-an-algebraic-integer]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix an integral basis $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ as in [F1], and put $A:=\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K$. The classes $\bar\alpha_1,\dots,\bar\alpha_n$ form an $\mathbb F_p$-basis of $A$, since $\mathcal O_K$ is free over $\mathbb Z$ with that basis. Thus $\dim_{\mathbb F_p}A=n\ge1$, so $p\mathcal O_K$ is proper; it is nonzero because $p\ne0$ in the characteristic zero field $K$. By [F5], write $p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{i=1}^r\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$ with distinct nonzero prime ideals and positive exponents. The list is nonempty because $p\mathcal O_K$ is proper. Define $\operatorname{Tr}_A(a):=\operatorname{tr}(m_a)$ for the $\mathbb F_p$-linear multiplication map on $A$, and $T_A(x,y):=\operatorname{Tr}_A(xy)$; matrix-trace linearity makes $T_A$ a bilinear form. [F1, F2, F5, F11, algebra]

1.2 Each $\mathfrak P_i$ is maximal. Indeed, [F6] makes $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak P_i$ finite, and [F6] also makes it an integral domain. For any nonzero class, multiplication by it is injective on this finite set, hence surjective, so the class has an inverse and the quotient is a field; [F6] then gives maximality. Therefore distinct $\mathfrak P_i,\mathfrak P_j$ are comaximal. Choose $u\in\mathfrak P_i$ and $v\in\mathfrak P_j$ with $u+v=1$. In the expansion of $(u+v)^{e_i+e_j-1}$, a term $u^k v^{e_i+e_j-1-k}$ lies in $\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$ if $k\ge e_i$, and otherwise its exponent of $v$ is at least $e_j$, so it lies in $\mathfrak P_j^{e_j}$. The sum is $1$; hence the powers $\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$ and $\mathfrak P_j^{e_j}$ are comaximal. [F6, algebra]

1.3 Put $A_i:=\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$. Applying [F7] to the pairwise comaximal powers gives $$A\cong\prod_{i=1}^r A_i.$$ If $e_i=1$, then $A_i=\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak P_i$ is a finite field. Since $\mathfrak P_i$ lies above $(p)$, the map $\mathbb Z/p\mathbb Z\to A_i$ induced by inclusion of $\mathbb Z$ is injective: its kernel is $(\mathfrak P_i\cap\mathbb Z)/(p)=0$. Thus $A_i/\mathbb F_p$ is a finite field extension, of degree $f_i=f(\mathfrak P_i/p)$ by definition. [F2, F5, F6, F7, algebra]

1.4 We first prove that the trace pairing of every finite extension $E/\mathbb F_p$ of finite fields is nondegenerate, without passing to an algebraic closure. Let $f=[E:\mathbb F_p]$ and $\sigma(x)=x^p$. By [F8], $\sigma^0,\dots,\sigma^{f-1}$ are the distinct automorphisms of $E$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Fix an $\mathbb F_p$-basis $b_1,\dots,b_f$ and form the square matrix $C=(\sigma^j(b_k))_{0\le j<f,\,1\le k\le f}$. If a row combination with coefficients $c_j\in E$ vanishes, then $\sum_jc_j\sigma^j(b_k)=0$ for each basis vector. Each $\sigma^j$ is $\mathbb F_p$-linear, so $\sum_jc_j\sigma^j(x)=0$ for every $x\in E$, and in particular for every $x\in E^\times$. The restrictions $\sigma^j|_{E^\times}$ are distinct group homomorphisms $E^\times\to E^\times$; [F9] makes them $E$-linearly independent, so every $c_j=0$. Hence $C$ is invertible. [F8, F9, algebra]

For $z\in E$, let $M_z$ be the matrix of multiplication by $z$ in this basis and let $D_z$ be diagonal with entries $\sigma^j(z)$. Since every entry of $M_z$ lies in $\mathbb F_p$, each $\sigma^j$ fixes those entries, and applying $\sigma^j$ to $zb_k=\sum_\ell(M_z)_{\ell k}b_\ell$ gives $CM_z=D_zC$. Thus $M_z=C^{-1}D_zC$. By [F11] and the definition of field trace, $$\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(z)=\operatorname{tr}(M_z)=\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}\sigma^j(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}z^{p^j}.$$ The polynomial $X+X^p+\cdots+X^{p^{f-1}}$ is nonzero of degree $p^{f-1}$. By [F10], $|E|=p^f$ and this polynomial has at most $p^{f-1}$ roots, so there is $w\in E$ with $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(w)\ne0$. For any $x\ne0$, taking $y=x^{-1}w$ gives $\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb F_p}(xy)\ne0$. This proves nondegeneracy of the trace pairing on $E$. The argument includes $f=1$ and $p=2$. [F3, F8, F9, F10, F11, algebra]

2.1 If every $e_i=1$, then [F7] identifies $A$ with the product of the finite fields $A_i$ from step 1.3. Multiplication by $(x_i y_i)_i$ on this product is block diagonal, so its trace is $\sum_i\operatorname{Tr}_{A_i/\mathbb F_p}(x_i y_i)$. The form on $A$ is therefore the orthogonal direct sum of the finite field trace pairings from step 1.4. Each is nondegenerate, so the product pairing is nondegenerate: for a nonzero vector, choose a nonzero component and pair it with a witness in that factor, taking zero in all other factors. [F7, step 1.3, step 1.4, algebra]

2.2 If some $e_i>1$, then $\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}\subsetneq\mathfrak P_i$. The containment is immediate; equality would contradict uniqueness in [F5], because the factorizations of $\mathfrak P_i$ and $\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$ have exponents $1$ and $e_i$. Choose $a\in\mathfrak P_i\setminus\mathfrak P_i^{e_i}$. Its class in $A_i$ is nonzero and nilpotent, since its $e_i$-th power is zero. Through the product isomorphism in step 1.3, it gives a nonzero nilpotent $\eta\in A$. For any $y\in A$, $u=\eta y$ is nilpotent, so multiplication $T=m_u$ on the finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$-space $A$ is nilpotent. [F5, step 1.3, algebra]

To compute its trace without eigenvalues, let $T^m=0$ and set $V_j=\ker(T^j)$ for $0\le j\le m$, with $V_0=0$ and $V_m=A$. Successively extend a basis of $V_{j-1}$ to a basis of $V_j$; these are finite-dimensional subspaces, so [F12] provides each extension without a choice principle. Order the resulting basis by these layers. Since $T(V_j)\subseteq V_{j-1}$, each new basis vector maps into the span of earlier vectors. The matrix of $T$ is strictly triangular and has trace zero. Therefore $\operatorname{tr}(m_{\eta y})=0$ for every $y\in A$, so the nonzero $\eta$ lies in the radical of the trace pairing on $A$. The pairing is degenerate. [F12, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 show that the trace pairing on $A$ is nondegenerate exactly when every $e_i=1$. By [F5], this is exactly when $p$ is unramified; equivalently, the pairing is degenerate exactly when $p$ ramifies. [F5, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 For $z\in\mathcal O_K$, multiplication by $z$ on $\mathcal O_K$ has an integer matrix in the integral basis $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$. Viewed over $\mathbb Q$, the same matrix represents multiplication by $z$ on $K$, so [F3] makes its matrix trace $\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(z)$. Reducing the integer matrix modulo $p$ gives multiplication by $\bar z$ on $A$; hence $$\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb F_p}(m_{\bar z})=\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(z)\pmod p.$$ Consequently the matrix of $T_A$ in the basis $\bar\alpha_1,\dots,\bar\alpha_n$ is the reduction modulo $p$ of $G=(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha_i\alpha_j))_{i,j}$. Since each $\alpha_i\alpha_j\in\mathcal O_K$ by [F1], [F13] makes every entry of $G$ an integer, and [F4] gives $\det G=d_K$. By [F12], the trace pairing $T_A$ is degenerate exactly when its matrix is not invertible, equivalently when $\det G\bmod p=0$; this is equivalent to $p\mid d_K$. Combining with step 3.1 proves that $p$ ramifies exactly when $p\mid d_K$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F11, F12, F13, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
