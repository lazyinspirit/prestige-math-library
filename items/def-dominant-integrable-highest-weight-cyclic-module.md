## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system with simple roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$, let
$\mathfrak n^+$ be the span of the positive root spaces
([[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]]),
let $\lambda$ be dominant integral with
$m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$
([[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]), and for each $i$
choose $e_i\in\mathfrak g_{\alpha_i}$, $f_i\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}$ with
$[e_i,f_i]=h_{\alpha_i}$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

In the universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak g)$
([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]]) write $\iota=\iota_{\mathfrak g}:\mathfrak g\to U(\mathfrak g)$ for the canonical linear map and $1_U$ for the unit. No injectivity of $\iota$ is required here. Let $I_\lambda$ be the left ideal
generated by the union of the three
sets
$$\iota(\mathfrak n^+),\qquad \{\iota(H)-\lambda(H)1_U:H\in\mathfrak h\},\qquad \{\iota(f_i)^{\,m_i+1}:i=1,\dots,r\}.$$
Define
$$M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda):=U(\mathfrak g)/I_\lambda ,$$
the quotient being taken as left $U(\mathfrak g)$-modules, and write
$$v_\lambda:=1+I_\lambda\in M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$$
for the class of the unit. Then $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ is called the
**dominant cyclic highest-weight module with simple-root integrability
relations** of highest weight $\lambda$, and $v_\lambda$ its **canonical
generator**. The subscript ``int'' records these defining relations; it does
not assert, at this stage, local finiteness of the whole module.

**Well-definedness and the defining relations.** For a subset $S$ of an
associative algebra, its generated left ideal is
$\{\sum_{j=1}^ku_js_j:k\ge0,\ u_j\in U(\mathfrak g),\ s_j\in S\}$; this is
the smallest left ideal containing $S$.  Thus $I_\lambda$ is well defined as a
linear subspace of $U(\mathfrak g)$, and the quotient carries a left
$U(\mathfrak g)$-module structure because $I_\lambda$ is a left ideal. The
notation $x\cdot v$ for $x\in\mathfrak g$ means $\iota(x)v$; the tensor
relations defining $U(\mathfrak g)$ ensure
$\iota([x,y])=\iota(x)\iota(y)-\iota(y)\iota(x)$, so this is a Lie-algebra
action. The class $v_\lambda$ therefore satisfies
$$x\cdot v_\lambda=0\ (x\in\mathfrak n^+),\qquad H\cdot v_\lambda=\lambda(H)v_\lambda\ (H\in\mathfrak h),\qquad f_i^{\,m_i+1}\cdot v_\lambda=0 ,$$
because the corresponding elements of $U(\mathfrak g)$ lie in $I_\lambda$.
The module is generated by $v_\lambda$, since the class of $1$ generates
$U(\mathfrak g)/I_\lambda$ under left multiplication.

The negative root spaces are one-dimensional by [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]], so the nonzero choices $f_i$ differ only by scalars: if $f_i$ is replaced by $cf_i$ with $c\in\mathbb C^\times$
and $e_i$ correspondingly by $c^{-1}e_i$, then the generator
$\iota(f_i)^{\,m_i+1}$ is replaced by the nonzero scalar multiple
$c^{m_i+1}\iota(f_i)^{\,m_i+1}$, which generates the same left ideal. Hence
$I_\lambda$, and with it $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$, does not depend on these
choices.

This names the cyclic presentation; nonvanishing, finite dimensionality and integrability of the resulting module require the subsequent highest-weight results. If a coefficient $m_i$ is zero, its defining power is the first power. If the index set of simple roots is empty, the third generating set is empty.
