## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ the ideal class
group is $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$,
generated by the class of the prime ideal
$\mathfrak p=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$. The concrete content is: $\mathcal O_K=
\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$, $d_K=-20$, the Minkowski constant satisfies
$M_K=\frac{2}{\pi}\sqrt{20}<3$, the ideal $\mathfrak p$ is the unique integral
ideal of norm $2$, it satisfies $\mathfrak p^2=(2)$, and it is not principal
because $a^2+5b^2=2$ has no integer solution.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-5})$ with
$\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ and discriminant $d_K=-20$, and the ideal
$\mathfrak p=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})\subseteq\mathcal O_K$.

[F1] For the squarefree integer $d=-5\equiv3\pmod4$, the quadratic-field
formulas give $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$ and $d_K=4d=-20$
([[thm-ring-of-integers-of-a-quadratic-field]],
[[cor-discriminant-of-a-quadratic-field]]).

[F2] $\pi>3$, from the Gregory-Leibniz partial sum through $N=7$
([[thm-gregory-leibniz-series-for-pi-from-a-finite-remainder]]).

[F3] Minkowski bound: every class of $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$
contains an integral ideal $\mathfrak b$ with $N\mathfrak b\le M_K$
([[thm-minkowski-bound-for-ideal-classes]],
[[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]]).

[F4] For a nonzero integral ideal, $N\mathfrak a=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|$;
for nonzero integral ideals $N(\mathfrak a\mathfrak b)=N\mathfrak a\,N\mathfrak b$;
and for $0\ne\beta\in\mathcal O_K$, $N((\beta))=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)|$
([[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]],
[[thm-ideal-norm-is-multiplicative]],
[[thm-principal-ideal-norm-is-absolute-field-norm]]).

[F5] Field norm as a determinant: for $\beta\in K$ the norm
$N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\beta)$ is the determinant of multiplication by $\beta$ on
the two-dimensional $\mathbb Q$-vector space $K$
([[def-field-norm-and-trace]]). In the basis $1,\sqrt{-5}$ the matrix of
multiplication by $a+b\sqrt{-5}$ is $\begin{pmatrix}a&-5b\\ b&a\end{pmatrix}$,
so $N_{K/\mathbb Q}(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a^2+5b^2$.

[F6] If $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak b$ are nonzero integral ideals with
$N\mathfrak a=N\mathfrak b$ finite, then $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak b$: by the
third isomorphism theorem for the additive groups, the group
$\mathfrak b/\mathfrak a$ has order $N\mathfrak a/N\mathfrak b=1$, hence is
trivial.

[F7] The rule $\varphi(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a+b\bmod2$ is a surjective ring
homomorphism $\mathcal O_K\to\mathbb F_2$, because $\varphi(\sqrt{-5})=1$
satisfies $1^2=-5\bmod2$; its kernel is $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})=\mathfrak p$, so
$\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p\cong\mathbb F_2$ and $N\mathfrak p=2$
([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-5}]$, $d_K=-20$, and the signature is $(r_1,r_2)=(0,1)$ with $n=2$. [F1, given]
1.2 The rule $\varphi(a+b\sqrt{-5})=a+b\pmod2$ is additive and multiplicative (the only nontrivial check is $(a+b\sqrt{-5})(a'+b'\sqrt{-5})=(aa'-5bb')+(ab'+a'b)\sqrt{-5}$ mapping to $aa'-5bb'+ab'+a'b\equiv aa'+bb'+ab'+a'b=(a+b)(a'+b')$ in $\mathbb F_2$), is surjective, and its kernel consists of the $a+b\sqrt{-5}$ with $a+b$ even, which are exactly the elements of the ideal $(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$: the kernel contains $2$ and $1+\sqrt{-5}$, and conversely $a+b\sqrt{-5}=b(1+\sqrt{-5})+(a-b)$ with $a-b$ even when $a+b$ is even. Hence $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p\cong\mathbb F_2$ and $N\mathfrak p=2$. [F4, F7, algebra]
2.1 Minkowski constant: $M_K=(4/\pi)^{r_2}(n!/n^n)\sqrt{|d_K|}=(4/\pi)\cdot(1/2)\cdot\sqrt{20}=\frac2\pi\sqrt{20}<\frac23\cdot\frac92=3$, since $\pi>3$ by [F2] and $\sqrt{20}<9/2$. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]
2.2 $p^2=(2)$: the products of the generators $2$ and $1+\sqrt{-5}$ are $4$, $2(1+\sqrt{-5})=2+2\sqrt{-5}$ and $(1+\sqrt{-5})^2=-4+2\sqrt{-5}$, all multiples of $2$, so $\mathfrak p^2\subseteq(2)$; by [F4] the norms are $N(\mathfrak p^2)=(N\mathfrak p)^2=4$ and $N((2))=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(2)|=4$, so [F6] gives $\mathfrak p^2=(2)$. [F4, F6, step 1.2, algebra]
2.3 Uniqueness of the norm-$2$ ideal: let $\mathfrak b$ be an integral ideal with $N\mathfrak b=2$. Then $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak b$ has two elements, so $2\in\mathfrak b$; the composite $\mathbb Z[X]\to\mathcal O_K\to\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak b$ sends $X^2+5$ to $0$ and $X$ to an element $u$ of the two-element ring $\mathbb F_2$ with $u^2=-5\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak b}$, so $u=1$ (as $0^2\ne1$); hence $X-1$ and $2$ lie in the kernel, the image of $(2,X-1)$ is $(2,\sqrt{-5}-1)=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})=\mathfrak p$, and $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak b$; with $N\mathfrak p=N\mathfrak b=2$, [F6] yields $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak p$. [F4, F6, F7, step 1.2, algebra]
2.4 $\mathfrak p$ is not principal: if $\mathfrak p=(\beta)$, then $\beta\in\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathcal O_K$ and by [F4] and [F5] the equation $a^2+5b^2=2$ would hold for $\beta=a+b\sqrt{-5}$. But $b=0$ gives $a^2=2$, impossible, and $b\ne0$ gives $a^2+5b^2\ge5>2$; so no such $\beta$ exists. [F4, F5, step 1.2, algebra]
3.1 In the class group, $[\mathfrak p]^2=[\mathfrak p^2]=[(2)]=1$ is the identity class, while $[\mathfrak p]\ne1$ by step 2.4; hence $[\mathfrak p]$ has order exactly $2$. [step 2.2, step 2.4, algebra]
3.2 Every class has an integral representative $\mathfrak b$ with $N\mathfrak b\le M_K<3$ by [F3] and step 2.1, so its norm is $1$ or $2$: norm $1$ forces $\mathfrak b=\mathcal O_K$, and norm $2$ forces $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak p$ by step 2.3. [F3, step 2.1, step 2.3]
4.1 Hence every class is either the principal class or $[\mathfrak p]$, so $\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)=\{1,[\mathfrak p]\}\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ is generated by the class of $\mathfrak p=(2,1+\sqrt{-5})$. [step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
