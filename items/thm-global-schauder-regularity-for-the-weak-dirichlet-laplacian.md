## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice together with Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$,
$0<\alpha<1$, let $\Omega$ be a bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain, let
$f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$. Then
the weak Dirichlet problem $-\Delta u=f$ in $\Omega$, $u=g$ on
$\partial\Omega$ (understood as $u-g\in H^1_0(\Omega)$) has exactly one
solution $u\in H^1(\Omega)$, and this solution belongs to
$C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$, satisfies $-\Delta u=f$ pointwise in $\Omega$
and $u=g$ on $\partial\Omega$, and obeys
$$\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le C\bigl(\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr)$$
with $C=C(n,\alpha,\Omega)$. This supplies the Laplace base point of the
continuity method rather than assuming it.
## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice, $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, the bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain $\Omega$, and $f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$, $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$.

[A1] The proof assumes the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Countable Choice is inherited by the measure and approximation interfaces; the Axiom of Choice is inherited by weak existence through its Poincaré supplier, and by the extension, trace, embedding, regularity and compactness interfaces. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] Weak formulation and solvability: the weak Dirichlet problem is the problem of $u\in H^1(\Omega)$ with $u-g\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ and $\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int_\Omega f\varphi$ for every $\varphi\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, where $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ belongs to $H^1(\Omega)$ and its trace $g|_{\partial\Omega}$ lies in $H^{1/2}(\partial\Omega)$ by [[thm-sharp-trace-theorem-for-w-one-p]] with $p=2$; [[thm-lp-trace-operator-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]] identifies the trace with the classical boundary restriction. By [[cor-inhomogeneous-weak-dirichlet-problem-by-a-trace-lifting]] the problem has exactly one solution $u\in H^1(\Omega)$; for the zero-boundary problem the solution is the one of [[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-for-the-weak-dirichlet-poisson-problem]]. The kernel of the trace is $H^1_0(\Omega)$ ([[thm-kernel-of-the-trace-is-w-one-p-zero]]), and for a continuous function on $\bar\Omega$ the Sobolev trace equals its boundary restriction ([[lem-sobolev-trace-agrees-with-continuous-boundary-values]]).

[F2] Extension of H\"older data: every $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ extends to a compactly supported $F^\sharp\in C_c^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb R^n)$ with $\|F^\sharp\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb R^n)}\le C(\Omega,\alpha)\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$: for real-valued $F$ take the McShane extension $F^\sharp(x)=\inf_{y\in\bar\Omega}(F(y)+[F]_{0,\alpha}|x-y|^\alpha)$ and multiply by a fixed cutoff equal to $1$ on a neighbourhood of $\bar\Omega$; complex-valued data are extended componentwise. The inequality $(a+b)^\alpha\le a^\alpha+b^\alpha$ gives $\big||x-y|^\alpha-|x'-y|^\alpha\big|\le|x-x'|^\alpha$; taking infima proves the extension Hölder bound, and the original Hölder inequality makes the infimum equal to $F(x)$ when $x\in\bar\Omega$. Multiplication by the fixed smooth cutoff preserves the bound up to its fixed constant.

[F3] Mollification smooths and controls: for a mollifier $\rho_\varepsilon$, the convolutions $F_\varepsilon:=F^\sharp*\rho_\varepsilon$ lie in $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ with $\sup|F_\varepsilon|\le\sup|F^\sharp|$, $[F_\varepsilon]_{0,\alpha}\le[F^\sharp]_{0,\alpha}$ and $F_\varepsilon\to F^\sharp$ uniformly on compact sets, in particular on $\bar\Omega$. ([[thm-convolution-with-a-mollifier-is-smooth-and-differentiates-under-the-integral-sign]])

[F4] Weak-to-strong global regularity ([[thm-weak-global-w-two-p-dirichlet-regularity-for-the-laplacian]]): if $p>n$, $\Omega$ is a bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain and $w\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ is a weak solution of $-\Delta w=h$ with $h\in L^p(\Omega)$, then $w\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$ and $\|w\|_{W^{2,p}}\le C(\|h\|_{L^p}+\|w\|_{L^p})$ with $C=C(n,p,\Omega)$.

[F5] Higher-order Sobolev embedding ([[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]]): on the bounded extension domain $\Omega$ ([[thm-extension-theorem-for-bounded-smooth-domains]]), for $k\ge1$ and $1\le q<\infty$, if $kq<n$ then $W^{k,q}\hookrightarrow L^r$ for $r\le nq/(n-kq)$, if $kq=n$ then $W^{k,q}\hookrightarrow L^r$ for every finite $r$, and if $kq>n$ then there are $C^{m,\beta}$ representatives for $m+\beta<k-n/q$. In particular $W^{2,q_i}\hookrightarrow L^{q_{i+1}}$ at each subcritical exponent in step 4.1, and for $p>n$, $W^{2,p}(\Omega)$ embeds in $C^{1,\gamma}(\bar\Omega)$ for every $0<\gamma<1-n/p$, with norm bounded by a constant times $\|w\|_{W^{2,p}}$.

[F6] Interior regularity for smooth forcing: if $h\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ then $Nh\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ and $-\Delta Nh=h$: after writing $Nh(x)=\int\Phi(z)h(x-z)dz$, differentiation falls on $h$, whose translated supports for $x$ in a compact set lie in one bounded set. Local integrability of $\Phi$ dominates every such differentiated integrand, so $D^\beta Nh=N(D^\beta h)$ for all $\beta$; and if $v$ is locally integrable and weakly harmonic on an open set, then $v$ is represented by a smooth function there. ([[def-newtonian-potential]], [[thm-newtonian-potential-solves-poisson-distributionally]], [[thm-newtonian-potential-for-holder-data-is-classical]], [[cor-locally-integrable-weakly-harmonic-functions-are-smooth]])

[F7] Maximum principle and barrier ([[thm-weak-maximum-principle-for-the-laplacian]]): if $v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\bar\Omega)$ and $\Delta v\ge0$ then $\max_{\bar\Omega}v=\max_{\partial\Omega}v$. If $\Omega\subseteq B_R(x_0)$ and $q(x):=M(R^2-|x-x_0|^2)/(2n)$, then $\Delta q=-M$ and $q\ge0$ on $\bar\Omega$.

[F8] Boundary Schauder estimate ([[thm-boundary-schauder-estimate-for-the-dirichlet-problem]]): if $v\in C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$ satisfies $\Delta v=h$ pointwise with $h\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $v=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, then $\|v\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le C(\|v\|_{C^0(\Omega)}+\|h\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)})$ with $C=C(n,\alpha,\Omega)$ (the coefficients are constant, so $\lambda=\Lambda=1$, $K=M=0$).

[F9] A pointwise bounded equicontinuous sequence of continuous maps from the compact metric space $\bar\Omega$ to a finite-dimensional Euclidean space has a uniformly convergent subsequence ([[cor-real-and-euclidean-vector-valued-ascoli-arzela]]). The identification of uniform limits of derivatives is proved locally in step 6.1, using the fundamental theorem of calculus on balls compactly contained in $\Omega$; the uniform Holder bound for the Hessians passes to the limit pointwise.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Reduction to zero boundary values. Put $F:=f+\Delta g$ on $\bar\Omega$. Since $g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $\Delta g\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$, one has $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with $\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+C(\Omega)\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}$. If $u$ is the weak solution of the problem of [F1], then $w:=u-g$ lies in $H^1_0(\Omega)$ and, for every $\varphi\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, $\int\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi-\int\nabla g\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int f\varphi+\int\Delta g\,\varphi=\int F\varphi$, the middle identity for $g$ being the weak form of $-\Delta g$ for $C^2$ functions (approximate $\varphi$ by $C_c^\infty$ functions and integrate by parts). So $w$ is a weak solution of the zero-boundary problem $-\Delta w=F$; conversely, if such a $w$ is shown to be $C^{2,\alpha}$ up to the boundary with $w=0$ on $\partial\Omega$, then $u=w+g$ is the required solution. It suffices to prove the zero-boundary statement for $F\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$: find $w\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ with $-\Delta w=F$ weakly, $w\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $\|w\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\le C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$. [F1, given, algebra, A1]

2.1 Extending and mollifying the data. By [F2] extend $F$ to $F^\sharp\in C_c^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb R^n)$ with $\|F^\sharp\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le C_1\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$, and put $F_\varepsilon:=F^\sharp*\rho_\varepsilon$ as in [F3]; then $M:=\sup_\varepsilon\|F_\varepsilon\|_\infty\le\|F^\sharp\|_\infty\le C_1\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$ and $\sup_\varepsilon[F_\varepsilon]_{0,\alpha}\le C_1\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$, while $F_\varepsilon\to F$ uniformly on $\bar\Omega$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The approximating weak solutions and the initial energy bound. Choose $p>n$, for instance $p=n+1$. For each $\varepsilon$, $F_\varepsilon|_\Omega\in L^p(\Omega)\subset H^{-1}(\Omega)$, so [F1] gives a unique weak solution $w_\varepsilon\in H^1_0(\Omega)$ of $-\Delta w_\varepsilon=F_\varepsilon$. Testing the weak equation with $w_\varepsilon$ (or its complex conjugate) and using Poincar\'e gives $\|w_\varepsilon\|_{H^1}\le C\|F_\varepsilon\|_{L^2}\le C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Uniform $W^{2,p}$ bound, including the $L^p$ term. Use the finite-exponent bootstrap in the proof of [F4], not just its final a priori estimate. For $n>2$ set $q_0=\min\{p,2n/(n-2)\}$; for $n=2$ set $q_0=p$. The energy estimate of step 3.1 and the first-order Sobolev embedding control $\|w_\varepsilon\|_{L^{q_0}}$. The supplier proof chooses a fixed shift $\lambda$ and a finite list $q_0\le q_1<\cdots<q_m=p$ (with no further step when $q_0=p$), where $q_{i+1}=\min\{p,nq_i/(n-2q_i)\}$ while $2q_i<n$, and $q_{i+1}=p$ once $2q_i\ge n$. At the first exponent, the shifted strong-solvability estimate for $(\lambda-\Delta)z=F_\varepsilon+\lambda w_\varepsilon$, together with energy uniqueness, identifies $z=w_\varepsilon$ and bounds $\|w_\varepsilon\|_{W^{2,q_0}}$ by $C(\|F_\varepsilon\|_{L^{q_0}}+\|w_\varepsilon\|_{L^{q_0}})$. At each later exponent $q_{i+1}$, the embedding in [F5] bounds $\|w_\varepsilon\|_{L^{q_{i+1}}}$ by the preceding $W^{2,q_i}$ norm; the next shifted estimate and energy uniqueness then give the $W^{2,q_{i+1}}$ bound. Every $\|F_\varepsilon\|_{L^{q_i}}$ is bounded by $C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$, and the list is finite, so induction gives $\|w_\varepsilon\|_{W^{2,p}}\le C(\|F_\varepsilon\|_{L^p}+\|w_\varepsilon\|_{H^1})\le C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$, uniformly in $\varepsilon$. This controls the $L^p(w_\varepsilon)$ term left explicit in the supplier's final a priori estimate. Applying [F5] with $k=2$, $p>n$, each $w_\varepsilon$ has a $C^{1,\gamma}(\bar\Omega)$ representative, for any fixed $0<\gamma<1-n/p$, with uniformly bounded norm. Its trace is zero because $w_\varepsilon\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; [F1] identifies this trace with the boundary values of the continuous representative. [F1, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Interior smoothness. Fix a point $x_0\in\Omega$ and a ball $B\Subset\Omega$ around it. The function $F_\varepsilon$ is smooth near $\bar B$; choose $\theta\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $\theta=1$ on a neighbourhood of $\bar B$ and set $H_\varepsilon:=\theta F_\varepsilon$, a compactly supported smooth function. By [F6], $NH_\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ and $-\Delta NH_\varepsilon=H_\varepsilon=F_\varepsilon$ on $B$. Hence $\Delta(w_\varepsilon-NH_\varepsilon)=0$ weakly on $B$: for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(B)$, $\int\nabla(w_\varepsilon-NH_\varepsilon)\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int F_\varepsilon\varphi-\int F_\varepsilon\varphi=0$. By the local smoothness of weakly harmonic functions in [F6], $w_\varepsilon-NH_\varepsilon$ agrees on $B$ with a smooth function; since $NH_\varepsilon$ is smooth, $w_\varepsilon$ agrees on $B$ with a smooth function. As $B$ and $x_0$ were arbitrary, $w_\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\Omega)$, and it satisfies $-\Delta w_\varepsilon=F_\varepsilon$ pointwise in $\Omega$. [F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 A uniform supremum bound with the weak maximum principle's sign. Choose $R>0$ and $x_0$ with $\Omega\subseteq B_R(x_0)$ and put $q(x):=M(R^2-|x-x_0|^2)/(2n)$, where $M:=\sup_\varepsilon\|F_\varepsilon\|_\infty$. Then $q\ge0$ on $\bar\Omega$ and $\Delta q=-M$. For a real-valued solution component $v_\varepsilon$ with datum $F_\varepsilon$ satisfying $|F_\varepsilon|\le M$, one has $\Delta(v_\varepsilon-q)=M-F_\varepsilon\ge0,\qquad \Delta(-v_\varepsilon-q)=M+F_\varepsilon\ge0.$ Both comparison functions are in $C^2(\Omega)\cap C(\bar\Omega)$ by steps 4.1 and 4.2, and their boundary values are $-q\le0$. The weak maximum principle [F7] therefore gives $v_\varepsilon-q\le0$ and $-v_\varepsilon-q\le0$, hence $|v_\varepsilon|\le q\le MR^2/(2n)$. If the data are complex, apply this argument to the real and imaginary parts separately; then $|w_\varepsilon|\le\sqrt2\,MR^2/(2n)$. By step 2.1, $M\le C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}$, so this is a uniform $C^0$ bound. [F7, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 Uniform $C^{2,\alpha}$ bounds and the limit. The functions $w_\varepsilon$ lie in $C^2(\Omega)\cap C^0(\bar\Omega)$ by steps 4.1 and 4.2, vanish on $\partial\Omega$, and satisfy $-\Delta w_\varepsilon=F_\varepsilon$ pointwise with $F_\varepsilon\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ and $\|F_\varepsilon\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}\le C_1\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}$. Apply [F8] to each real component of $w_\varepsilon$ with right-hand side the corresponding component of $-F_\varepsilon$ (the estimate is unchanged by this sign), and combine the component bounds if the data are complex. Using step 5.1, $$\|w_\varepsilon\|_{C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\le C_7\bigl(\|w_\varepsilon\|_{C^0(\Omega)}+\|F_\varepsilon\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)}\bigr)\le C_8\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)},$$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$. The boundary Schauder estimate gives uniform $C^{2,\alpha}$ control in each member of a finite cover of $\bar\Omega$ by interior balls and flattened boundary half-boxes. Thus the function, gradient and Hessian components are equicontinuous and pointwise bounded on $\bar\Omega$; if the functions are complex, list their real and imaginary components separately. By [F9], a subsequence of this finite-dimensional vector-valued family converges uniformly to limits $(w,G,H)$. On every ball $B\Subset\Omega$, the fundamental theorem of calculus along segments in $B$ and uniform convergence give $Dw=G$ and $DG=H$. The limits $G,H$ are continuous on $\bar\Omega$, so these derivatives extend continuously to the boundary; pointwise convergence of the Hessian difference quotients gives $[D^2w]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}\le\liminf_\varepsilon[D^2w_\varepsilon]_{0,\alpha;\Omega}$. Hence $w\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with the stated bound. Uniform convergence of $F_\varepsilon$ and of the second derivatives gives $-\Delta w=F$ pointwise in $\Omega$ and $w=0$ on $\partial\Omega$. [F8, F9, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Identification with the weak solution. The limit $w\in C^2(\bar\Omega)$ with $w=0$ on $\partial\Omega$ satisfies $\int\nabla w\cdot\nabla\varphi=\int F\varphi$ for every $\varphi\in H^1_0(\Omega)$: for $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ this is integration by parts, and $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ is dense in $H^1_0(\Omega)$. By [F1] the weak solution of the zero-boundary problem is unique, so $w$ is the unique weak solution $w=u-g$ of the original problem as identified in step 1.1. Therefore $u=w+g\in C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ solves $-\Delta u=f$ pointwise and $u=g$ on $\partial\Omega$, and $$\|u\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\le\|w\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\le C\|F\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+C\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\le C'\bigl(\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}+\|g\|_{C^{2,\alpha}}\bigr)$$ by step 1.1. This is the displayed estimate of the statement. [step 1.1, step 6.1, F1, algebra]

8.1 Conclusion. The weak Dirichlet problem has exactly one solution by [F1], and steps 2.1-7.1 show that this solution is the limit of the smooth approximating solutions, is of class $C^{2,\alpha}(\bar\Omega)$ with the stated bound, and solves the equation classically. In particular the Laplace operator with Dirichlet boundary values on a bounded $C^{2,\alpha}$ domain has the Schauder a priori estimate on weak solutions, a fact used as the base point of the method of continuity. The proof uses only the fixed exponent $p>n$ in the weak-to-strong step, and the finiteness of all constants is uniform in the mollification parameter. [step 2.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, F1, F8, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The proof is the classical approximation scheme: solve smooth approximating problems weakly, upgrade them with $W^{2,p}$ regularity, embed, use the maximum principle for a uniform supremum bound, apply the boundary Schauder estimate and pass to the limit by Arzela-Ascoli. Uniqueness of the weak solution identifies the limit, so no subsequence ambiguity remains.
- The uniform sup bound is what makes the boundary Schauder estimate applicable with constants independent of $\varepsilon$; the result is an a posteriori (regularity) statement, while the a priori estimate in the boundary theorem is applied after its quoted input establishes closure regularity.
- Only the fixed pair $(p,\gamma)$ with $p=n+1$, $0<\gamma<1-n/p$ is used in the embedding step; any $p>n$ gives the same conclusion.
