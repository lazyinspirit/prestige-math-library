## Statement

Let $F : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be nondecreasing and right-continuous, and let
$\mu_F$ be the associated Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure. Then for every Borel set
$E \subseteq \mathbb{R}$,

$$\mu_F(E) = \inf\{\, \mu_F(U) : E \subseteq U,\ U \text{ open} \,\} = \sup\{\, \mu_F(K) : K \subseteq E,\ K \text{ compact} \,\}.$$

If $\mu_F(E) < +\infty$, then equivalently: for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there
are an open set $U$ and a compact set $K$ with

$$K \subseteq E \subseteq U,\qquad \mu_F(U \setminus E) < \varepsilon,\qquad \mu_F(E \setminus K) < \varepsilon.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nondecreasing right-continuous function $F : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$, its Lebesgue-Stieltjes measure $\mu_F$, a Borel set $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}$, and $\varepsilon > 0$.

[L1] The half-open interval formulas hold for $\mu_F$, including the formulas for open and closed intervals. ([[thm-interval-formulas-and-atoms-for-lebesgue-stieltjes-measures]])

[L2] Measures are continuous from below and from above. ([[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]], [[thm-continuity-from-above-for-measures]])

[L3] The half-open intervals generate the Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb{R}$, and the monotone class generated by an algebra is the sigma-algebra it generates. ([[thm-seven-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-r]], [[thm-monotone-class]])

[L4] Every closed bounded interval is compact. ([[thm-heine-borel-r]])

[L5] The Borel sigma-algebra of a subspace is the trace of the ambient Borel sigma-algebra. ([[thm-borel-sigma-algebra-of-a-subspace-is-the-trace]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every bounded half-open interval is regular. Let $I = (a,b]$. [L1, L4, choose]

Choose $\eta > 0$ so small that
$F((b+\eta)^-) - F(b) < \varepsilon$; then the open interval
$U := (a,b+\eta)$ contains $I$ and [L1] gives

$$\mu_F(U \setminus I)=\mu_F((b,b+\eta))<\varepsilon.$$

Likewise choose $\delta > 0$ with $F((a+\delta)^-) - F(a) < \varepsilon$; then the closed interval $K := [a+\delta,b]$ is compact by [L4], satisfies $K \subseteq I$, and [L1] gives

$$\mu_F(I \setminus K) = \mu_F((a,a+\delta)) = F((a+\delta)^-) - F(a) < \varepsilon.$$

[L1, L4, choose]

2.1 Fix $m \ge 1$ and write $I_m := [-m,m]$. Let $\mathcal{O}_m$ be the family of Borel subsets $A \subseteq I_m$ such that for every $\varepsilon > 0$ there is an open set $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}$ with [step 1.1, L3, L5]

$$A \subseteq U,\qquad \mu_F((U \cap I_m) \setminus A) < \varepsilon.$$

By step 1.1, every trace $H \cap I_m$ with $H \in \mathcal{H}$ lies in $\mathcal{O}_m$. Those traces form an algebra on $I_m$, because $\mathcal{H}$ is an algebra on $\mathbb{R}$ and traces preserve complements and finite unions. By [L5] together with the generator statement of [L3], they generate the Borel sigma-algebra of the subspace $I_m$. [step 1.1, L3, L5]

3.1 The family $\mathcal{O}_m$ is a monotone class. If $A_n \uparrow A$ and $A_n \in \mathcal{O}_m$, choose open $U_n \supseteq A_n$ with $\mu_F((U_n \cap I_m) \setminus A_n) < \varepsilon 2^{-n-1}$. [step 2.1, L1, L2]

Then $U := \bigcup_n U_n$ is open, contains $A$, and

$$(U \cap I_m) \setminus A \subseteq \bigcup_n \big((U_n \cap I_m) \setminus A_n\big),$$

so $\mu_F((U \cap I_m) \setminus A) < \varepsilon$. If instead $A_n \downarrow A$, then $A_0 \subseteq I_m$ has finite measure by [L1], so [L2] gives $\mu_F(A_n \setminus A) \to 0$; choose $n$ with $\mu_F(A_n \setminus A) < \varepsilon/2$, then choose open $U_n \supseteq A_n$ with $\mu_F((U_n \cap I_m) \setminus A_n) < \varepsilon/2$. The open set $U_n$ contains $A$, and

$$\mu_F((U_n \cap I_m) \setminus A) \le \mu_F((U_n \cap I_m) \setminus A_n) + \mu_F(A_n \setminus A) < \varepsilon.$$

[step 2.1, L1, L2]

4.1 Step 3.1 makes $\mathcal{O}_m$ a monotone class containing the algebra from step 2.1. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L3]

Therefore [L3] gives that every Borel subset of $I_m$ lies in $\mathcal{O}_m$: every bounded Borel set is relatively outer regular inside a compact interval. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L3]

5.1 Let $A \subseteq I_m$ be Borel. Apply step 4.1 to the Borel complement $I_m \setminus A$ in the subspace $I_m$. [step 4.1, L4]

Choose open $U \supseteq I_m \setminus A$ with $\mu_F((U \cap I_m) \setminus (I_m \setminus A)) < \varepsilon$. Put $K := I_m \setminus U$. Then $K$ is closed in the compact interval $I_m$, hence compact by [L4], and $K \subseteq A$. Moreover

$$A \setminus K = A \cap U = (U \cap I_m) \setminus (I_m \setminus A),$$

so $\mu_F(A \setminus K) < \varepsilon$. Thus every bounded Borel set is also inner regular by compact sets. [step 4.1, L4]

6.1 The global inner regularity follows by truncation. Put $E_m := E \cap I_m$. [step 5.1, L2, algebra]

Then $E_m \uparrow E$, so [L2] gives $\mu_F(E) = \sup_m \mu_F(E_m)$. For each $m$, step 5.1 gives a compact $K_m \subseteq E_m \subseteq E$ with $\mu_F(E_m \setminus K_m) < 1/(m+1)$. Hence

$$\sup\{\, \mu_F(K) : K \subseteq E,\ K \text{ compact} \,\} \ge \sup_m \mu_F(K_m) = \sup_m \mu_F(E_m) = \mu_F(E),$$

while the reverse inequality is monotonicity. [step 5.1, L2, algebra]

7.1 For global outer regularity, first suppose $\mu_F(E) < +\infty$. Choose $m$ so large that $\mu_F(E \setminus I_m) < \varepsilon/3$. [step 4.1, step 6.1, L1, L2]

This is possible directly from [L2] on $E \cap I_m \uparrow E$. Step 4.1 gives an open $V \supseteq E \cap I_m$ with $\mu_F((V \cap I_m) \setminus E) < \varepsilon/3$. For each of the two tails $E \cap (m,\infty)$ and $E \cap (-\infty,-m)$, decompose into unit half-open strips and apply step 4.1 on each compact strip, choosing the errors summably below $\varepsilon/3$; enlarging each strip by a thin open shell whose $\mu_F$-measure is also chosen summably below $\varepsilon/3$ via [L1], the union of those stripwise open sets is open and contributes total excess below $2\varepsilon/3$. Taking the union with $V$ gives an open set $U \supseteq E$ with $\mu_F(U \setminus E) < \varepsilon$. [step 4.1, step 6.1, L1, L2]

8.1 If $\mu_F(E) = +\infty$, then every open $U \supseteq E$ also has $\mu_F(U) = +\infty$ by monotonicity. [step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

So $\inf\{\, \mu_F(U) : E \subseteq U,\ U \text{ open} \,\} = +\infty = \mu_F(E)$. Combining this with step 7.1 gives the outer regularity equality in all cases. The inner regularity equality is step 6.1, and the finite-measure $\varepsilon$-approximation statement is exactly the combination of steps 6.1 and 7.1. [step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
