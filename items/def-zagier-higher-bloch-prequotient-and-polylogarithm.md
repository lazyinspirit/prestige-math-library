## Definition

Let $F$ be a number field, $m\ge2$, and let $[x]$ denote the generator associated to $x\in\mathbb P^1(F)$. Put

$$\mathcal F_F:=\mathbb Z[\mathbb P^1(F)]/\langle[0],[\infty]\rangle.$$

Using multiplicative notation for $F^\times$, define

$$\beta_m:\mathcal F_F\longrightarrow \left(\operatorname{Sym}^{m-2}_{\mathbb Z}(F^\times)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\Lambda^2_{\mathbb Z}(F^\times)\right)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q,\qquad \beta_m([x])=x^{\odot(m-2)}\otimes\bigl(x\wedge(1-x)\bigr)$$

for $x\notin\{0,1,\infty\}$, and set $\beta_m([x])=0$ at $x=0,1,\infty$. Define $\mathcal A_m(F):=\ker\beta_m$. For $a\in\mathbb P^1(F)$, projective evaluation induces $\phi_a:\mathcal F_{\mathbb Q(t)}\to\mathcal F_F$ by $[x(t)]\mapsto[x(a)]$. Define $\mathcal C_m(F)$ to be the subgroup generated by all $\phi_a(u)-\phi_b(u)$ with $u\in\mathcal A_m(\mathbb Q(t))$ and $a,b\in\mathbb P^1(F)$.

For $j\ge1$, $\operatorname{Li}_j(z)$ is the analytic continuation of $\sum_{n\ge1}z^n/n^j$ from $|z|<1$, with the branches in the displayed finite expression continued simultaneously from those series along one common path; on a simply connected domain avoiding $0$ and $1$ this means their compatible local branches. Define the branchwise expression

$$\mathcal L_m(z):=\operatorname{Re}_m\!\left(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\frac{2^jB_j}{j!}\operatorname{Li}_{m-j}(z)\log^j|z|\right),$$

where $\operatorname{Re}_m$ means real part for odd $m$ and imaginary part for even $m$, and $B_j$ are defined by $t/(e^t-1)=\sum_{j\ge0}B_jt^j/j!$. This is the source normalization; the next theorem's claim is its branch independence, continuous extension, and compatibility with $\mathcal C_m(F)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A number field $F$ with its fixed complex embeddings, an integer $m\ge2$, and the symbols and functions specified in the Definition.

[F1] [[def-number-field]]: a number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$ and admits complex embeddings.

[F2] [[def-bernoulli-numbers-by-their-generating-function]]: Bernoulli numbers are the coefficients in $t/(e^t-1)=\sum_{j\ge0}B_jt^j/j!$.

[F3] [[def-natural-logarithm]]: $\log|z|$ is the real logarithm of the positive real number $|z|$ when $z\ne0$.

## Proof

1.1 The free abelian group on $\mathbb P^1(F)$ modulo the two stated generators is a group with the displayed generators. For generic $x$, both $x$ and $1-x$ lie in $F^\times$, so the formula for $\beta_m([x])$ is in its stated symmetric/exterior tensor target; at $x=0,1,\infty$ the prescribed value is zero. Thus the generator assignment extends linearly to $\mathcal F_F$ and defines $\mathcal A_m(F)$ as its kernel. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Each rational function $x(t)$ extends as a map $\mathbb P^1_F\to\mathbb P^1_F$, so projective evaluation sends $[0]$ and $[\infty]$ to zero symbols and defines $\phi_a$ on the quotient group. To check the kernel claim, at a finite $a$ use the leading-unit homomorphism $s_a(f)=((t-a)^{-\nu_a(f)}f)(a)$ from $\mathbb Q(t)^\times$ to $F^\times$, and at infinity use $1/t$. For a symbol whose value is neither0,1 nor infinity this gives its evaluated tensor symbol. At a zero, the factor $1-x$ has leading unit1; at a value1, $x$ has leading unit1; both exterior symbols vanish. At a pole, the two leading units are $c,-c$, so the exterior symbol is $c\wedge(-1)$, killed by rationalization since $(-1)^2=1$. Applying the induced homomorphism on the rationalized tensor target to $\beta_m(u)=0$ therefore gives $\beta_m(\phi_a(u))=0$. The same holds at $b$, so each specialization difference lies in $\mathcal A_m(F)$. Hence $\mathcal C_m(F)$ is a subgroup of $\mathcal A_m(F)$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 The common continuation exists along each path avoiding0 and1: cover its compact image by finitely many disks avoiding the punctures. Start from $\operatorname{Li}_1=-\log(1-z)$ and its continued logarithm. On each disk define each next $\operatorname{Li}_r$ by a primitive of $\operatorname{Li}_{r-1}/z$, with its integration constant fixed by the preceding overlap germ; a primitive on a disk is obtained by integrating the local power series termwise. Finitely many such operations continue the entire hierarchy up to weight $m$ coherently. Uniqueness of a primitive with its overlap germ makes the disk refinements compatible. The power-series continuation and branch convention therefore define the displayed $\mathcal L_m$ expression on each chosen simply connected domain; the Bernoulli coefficients and real logarithms are fixed by [F2,F3]. Its global single-valuedness and its vanishing on specialization relations are deliberately left to the following theorem rather than inferred from the formula. [F2, F3, given] ∎
