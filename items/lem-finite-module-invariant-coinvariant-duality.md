## Statement

Let $A=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ with $n\geq1$, let $M$ be a finite $A$-module and let $D(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A)$. If $\tau$ is an automorphism of $M$, give $D(M)$ the contragredient automorphism $\tau_D(\phi)=\phi\circ\tau^{-1}$. Evaluation induces perfect pairings
$$\ker(\tau-1)\times\operatorname{coker}(\tau_D-1)\longrightarrow A,\qquad \operatorname{coker}(\tau-1)\times\ker(\tau_D-1)\longrightarrow A.$$
More generally, for a group $Q$ acting on $M$, write $M^Q$ for its invariants and $M_Q=M/\langle qm-m:q\in Q,m\in M\rangle$ for its coinvariants, and give $D(M)$ its contragredient action. There are natural identifications $D(M_Q)=D(M)^Q$ and $D(M^Q)=D(M)_Q$. If $Q$ is finite with order coprime to $n$, the map $M^Q\to M_Q$ is an isomorphism and taking $Q$-invariants is exact on finite $A[Q]$-modules.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring, module, automorphism or group action in the statement.

[F1] Finite-module duality into $A$ is exact and evaluation gives natural biduality. [[lem-finite-cyclic-coefficient-duality-is-exact]]

## Proof

1.1 A functional on $M$ descends to $M_Q$ precisely when it annihilates all $qm-m$. This is equivalent to $Q$-invariance under the contragredient action, so $D(M_Q)=D(M)^Q$ naturally. Apply this identity to $D(M)$ and use the biduality in [F1]. Dualizing the resulting identity yields $D(M^Q)=D(M)_Q$. This does not require $Q$ to be finite, since its action factors through the finite set of automorphisms of $M$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 Suppose $Q$ is finite and its order is invertible in $A$. Set $e_Q=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{q\in Q}q$ as an endomorphism of $M$. Multiplying the sums gives $e_Q^2=e_Q$, and its image is exactly $M^Q$. Every $qm-m$ is in its kernel. Conversely $m-e_Qm=|Q|^{-1}\sum_{q\in Q}(m-qm)$ is in the subgroup of coinvariant relations, so that subgroup is precisely $\ker e_Q$. Hence $M=M^Q\oplus\ker e_Q$ and the natural map $M^Q\to M_Q$ is an isomorphism. [given, algebra]

2.1 Take $Q$ to be the cyclic group generated by $\tau$. Its invariants and coinvariants are respectively $\ker(\tau-1)$ and $\operatorname{coker}(\tau-1)$. The identifications in step 1.1 and biduality give both asserted perfect pairings. Explicitly, if $\tau m=m$, then $(\tau_D\phi-\phi)(m)=\phi(\tau^{-1}m-m)=0$, so the first evaluation pairing descends to the displayed cokernel; a functional annihilates $(\tau-1)M$ exactly when it is fixed by $\tau_D$, proving the second descent as well. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $0\to L\to M\to N\to0$ is an exact sequence of finite $A[Q]$-modules, the invariant sequence is left exact by its defining fixed-point equations. For $v\in N^Q$, choose one lift $m\in M$; the averaged element $e_Qm$ is invariant and maps to $e_Qv=v$. Thus invariants are also surjective at the last term and are exact. All maps are natural in equivariant morphisms. The zero ring case $n=1$, the trivial module and the trivial action satisfy the same formulas. This proves the claims. [step 1.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

These are finite coefficient calculations. Identifying étale cohomology on a strict trait with the invariant/coinvariant complexes requires separate ramification, cohomological comparison and cup-product arguments.
