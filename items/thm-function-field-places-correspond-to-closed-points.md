## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k=\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field, let $C$ be
a smooth proper geometrically integral curve over $k$, and let
$\varphi:F\to k(C)$ be a $k$-field isomorphism. For a closed point $x\in C$,
transport the normalized order $\operatorname{ord}_x$ along $\varphi$ and define
$|0|_x=0$ and
$$|a|_x=q^{-\operatorname{ord}_x(\varphi(a))[\kappa(x):k]}\qquad(a\in F^\times).$$
Then $x\mapsto[|\cdot|_x]$ is a bijection from the closed points of $C$ to
the equivalence classes of nontrivial absolute values on $F$. Every absolute
value on $F$ is nonarchimedean, and the absolute values $|\cdot|_x$ are the
normalized discrete representatives of their classes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$; a smooth
proper geometrically integral curve $C$ over $k$; a $k$-isomorphism
$\varphi:F\to k(C)$; and a nontrivial absolute value $|\cdot|$ on $F$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] A smooth proper geometrically integral curve is a nonempty separated
finite-type integral $k$-scheme of chain dimension one whose structure map to
$\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth and proper. Its function field is $k(C)$.
([[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]])

[F2] An absolute value satisfies $|a|=0$ iff $a=0$, $|ab|=|a||b|$, and
$|a+b|\le |a|+|b|$; two nontrivial absolute values are equivalent exactly when
one is a positive real power of the other. ([[def-multiplicative-absolute-value-on-a-field]], [[def-equivalent-field-absolute-values]])

[F3] Every element of $k^\times$ has finite multiplicative order, and the
degree of a closed point is $[\kappa(x):k]$, a positive integer.
([[def-finite-field-and-its-order]], [[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]])

[F4] For a finite-type quasi-separated morphism, properness is equivalent to
existence and uniqueness of lifts of all valuative diagrams over arbitrary
valuation rings. A valuative diagram consists of a valuation ring $R\subseteq
K$, a generic map $\operatorname{Spec}K\to X$, and a map $\operatorname{Spec}R\to
S$ making the square commute. ([[thm-valuative-criterion-properness]], [[def-valuative-diagram-separatedness]])

[F5] At a closed point $x$ of a smooth curve, $\mathcal O_{C,x}$ is a discrete
valuation ring, and its normalized order $\operatorname{ord}_x$ takes values in
$\mathbb Z$; every nonzero element is a uniformizer power times a unit.
([[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]], [[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]])

[F6] A valuation ring of a field has the property that for every nonzero
element of the field, either it or its inverse belongs to the ring. A discrete
valuation ring has a maximal ideal generated by a uniformizer, and a unit has
order zero. ([[def-valuation-ring]], [[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]])

## Proof



**Given:** The data and hypotheses above.

1.1 The absolute value is nonarchimedean. In characteristic $p>0$, for every $m\ge1$ the Frobenius identity and the triangle inequality give $|a+b|^{p^m}=|a^{p^m}+b^{p^m}|\le |a|^{p^m}+|b|^{p^m}.$ Taking $p^m$-th roots and letting $m\to\infty$ gives $|a+b|\le\max(|a|,|b|)$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Put $R=\{a\in F:|a|\le1\}$. By step 1.1 this is a subring of $F$. For each $a\ne0$, either $|a|\le1$ or $|a|>1$ and then $|a^{-1}|<1$, so [F6] makes $R$ a valuation ring. Its unique maximal ideal is $\mathfrak m_R=\{a\in R:|a|<1\}$: this set is an ideal by step 1.1, and every element of $R$ outside it has absolute value one and inverse in $R$, so is a unit. Since $|\cdot|$ is nontrivial, $\mathfrak m_R$ contains a nonzero element and $R$ is not a field. Every nonzero $a\in F$ or its inverse lies in $R$, so $\operatorname{Frac}(R)=F$. Every $c\in k^\times$ has $|c|=1$ because $c$ has finite order, hence $k\subseteq R$. [F2, F3, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The generic map $\operatorname{Spec}F\to C$ induced by $\varphi$ and the structure map $\operatorname{Spec}R\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ form a valuative diagram. Since $C\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is proper, [F4] supplies a unique lift $\operatorname{Spec}R\to C$. [A1, F1, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $x$ be the image of the closed point of $\operatorname{Spec}R$. It is not the generic point: that would give a local inclusion $k(C)=F\subseteq R$, contradicting that $R$ is not a field. Since $C$ is an integral curve, every non-generic point is closed, so $x$ is closed. The lift induces a local inclusion $\mathcal O_{C,x}\subseteq R$ inside their common fraction field $F$. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Under AC [A1], [F5] makes $\mathcal O_{C,x}$ a discrete valuation ring. If $z\in R\setminus\mathcal O_{C,x}$, then $z^{-1}$ belongs to the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{C,x}$; locality of the inclusion puts $z^{-1}$ in the maximal ideal of $R$, contradicting $zz^{-1}=1$. Hence $R=\mathcal O_{C,x}$. [A1, F5, F6, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Let $\pi_x$ be a uniformizer of this local ring. Every $a\in F^\times$ has the form $a=\pi_x^n u$ for $n=\operatorname{ord}_x(\varphi(a))\in\mathbb Z$ and a unit $u$; both $u$ and $u^{-1}$ lie in $R$, so $|u|=1$. Since $\pi_x\in\mathfrak m_R$, we have $0<|\pi_x|<1$. Put $c=-\log|\pi_x|/([\kappa(x):k]\log q)>0$. Then $|a|=|\pi_x|^n=(q^{-n[\kappa(x):k]})^c$, so the original absolute value is equivalent to the normalized $|\cdot|_x$ in the Statement. [F3, F5, F6, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Conversely, for every closed point $x$, the formula in the Statement is a nontrivial absolute value whose valuation ring is $\mathcal O_{C,x}$. If two closed points defined equivalent absolute values, positive powering would give the same valuation ring and hence two lifts of the same generic map in [F4]; uniqueness forces the closed points to agree. Thus surjectivity follows from the lift and valuation-ring identification in steps 3.1, 5.1 and 6.1, while injectivity follows from the unique-lift argument here; the normalized representatives are discrete by [F5]. AC is used through the curve-valuative and local-DVR suppliers, not in the Frobenius estimate or the valuation-ring comparison. [A1, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 6.1] ∎
