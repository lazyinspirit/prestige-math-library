## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $q$ be a prime power and put
$G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with diagonal torus $T$. Let
$\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ be compositions of $n$
with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$ and $J_1,\dots,J_s$, and let
$P_\alpha=L\ltimes U$ and $Q:=P_\beta=M\ltimes V$ be the corresponding standard
parabolics, so that $L=L_\alpha$, $U=U_\alpha$, $M=L_\beta$, $V=U_\beta$
([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]],
[[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]]). Let
$\mathcal R$ be any set of permutations of $S_n$, one for each
$(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset (in particular, one may take the
$(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing representatives)
([[lem-standard-parabolic-double-cosets-are-young-double-cosets]]), let $X$ be
a complex $M$-module, and for $\rho\in S_n$ put
$$w_\rho:=P_\rho,\qquad M_\rho:=w_\rho Mw_\rho^{-1},\qquad V_\rho:=w_\rho Vw_\rho^{-1},\qquad C_\rho:=L\cap M_\rho,\qquad A_\rho:=L\cap V_\rho,\qquad D_\rho:=U\cap M_\rho,$$
and let ${}^\rho X$ be the $M_\rho$-module with underlying vector space $X$
and action $m\cdot x:=(w_\rho^{-1}mw_\rho)\cdot x$
([[def-g-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]). Then:

1. **Indexing by double cosets.** The $(P_\alpha,Q)$-double cosets of $G$ are
   exactly the disjoint nonempty sets $P_\alpha w_\rho Q$ with
   $\rho\in\mathcal R$, and $G=\bigsqcup_{\rho\in\mathcal R}P_\alpha w_\rho Q$.
2. **Mackey formula.** There is an isomorphism of complex $L$-modules
   $${}^*\!R_L^G\bigl(R_M^GX\bigr)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal R}R_{C_\rho}^L\bigl(({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}\bigr),$$
   where $R_{C_\rho}^L$ is the Harish–Chandra induction from $C_\rho$ to $L$
   with respect to the parabolic subgroup $C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho$
   ([[def-harish-chandra-induction-and-restriction-for-finite-gl-n]]) and
   $({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$ is the space of $D_\rho$-invariants of ${}^\rho X$, a
   $C_\rho$-module. Moreover
   $$P_\alpha\cap M_\rho=C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho,\qquad L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho,$$
   so that the restriction occurring in the summand is the Harish–Chandra
   restriction from $M_\rho$ to $C_\rho$ with respect to $P_\alpha\cap M_\rho$
   and the induction is taken with respect to $L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$.
3. **Coordinate parabolics.** Let $\gamma=(S_1,\dots,S_r)$ and
   $\delta=(R_1,\dots,R_s)$ be ordered partitions of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ whose
   blocks have the sizes $a_1,\dots,a_r$ and $b_1,\dots,b_s$ of $\alpha$ and
   $\beta$, and let $P_\gamma=L_\gamma\ltimes U_\gamma$,
   $P_\delta=L_\delta\ltimes U_\delta$ be the corresponding coordinate
   parabolics ([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]]). Choose
   $\sigma,\tau\in S_n$ with $\sigma(I_i)=S_i$ and $\tau(J_j)=R_j$ for all
   $i,j$, so that $\gamma=\sigma(\alpha)$ and $\delta=\tau(\beta)$, put
   $u:=P_\sigma$, $v:=P_\tau$, regard $X$ as a complex $L_\delta$-module by
   $\ell\cdot x:=(v^{-1}\ell v)\cdot x$, and for $\rho\in\mathcal R$ put
   $\rho':=\sigma\rho\tau^{-1}$ and $w_{\rho'}:=P_{\rho'}=u\,w_\rho\,v^{-1}$.
   Then $P_\gamma=uP_\alpha u^{-1}$ and $P_\delta=vQv^{-1}$, the sets
   $P_\gamma w_{\rho'}P_\delta=u\,(P_\alpha w_\rho Q)\,v^{-1}$ are the
   $(P_\gamma,P_\delta)$-double cosets of $G$ as $\rho$ runs over $\mathcal R$,
   and claims 1 and 2 hold with $P_\alpha,Q,L,M,U,V$ replaced by
   $P_\gamma,P_\delta,L_\gamma,L_\delta,U_\gamma,U_\delta$ and with
   $\mathcal R,\rho$ replaced by the index set $\mathcal R$ with
   $\rho'=\sigma\rho\tau^{-1}$, the groups
   $$w_{\rho'}L_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}=uM_\rho u^{-1},\qquad w_{\rho'}U_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}=uV_\rho u^{-1},$$
   $$L_\gamma\cap w_{\rho'}L_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}=uC_\rho u^{-1},\qquad L_\gamma\cap w_{\rho'}U_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}=uA_\rho u^{-1},\qquad U_\gamma\cap w_{\rho'}L_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}=uD_\rho u^{-1},$$
   and the $uM_\rho u^{-1}$-module ${}^{\rho'}\!X$ with underlying vector space
   $X$ and action $m\cdot x:=(w_{\rho'}^{-1}mw_{\rho'})\cdot x$, the inner
   action being the $L_\delta$-action on $X$; that is,
   $${}^*\!R_{L_\gamma}^G\bigl(R_{L_\delta}^GX\bigr)\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal R}R_{uC_\rho u^{-1}}^{L_\gamma}\bigl(({}^{\rho'}\!X)^{uD_\rho u^{-1}}\bigr),$$
   the induction being taken with respect to the parabolic subgroup
   $u(C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho)u^{-1}$ of $L_\gamma$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, a prime power $q$, the group $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with diagonal torus $T$, compositions $\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ of $n$ with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$, $J_1,\dots,J_s$, the standard parabolics $P_\alpha=L\ltimes U$ and $Q=P_\beta=M\ltimes V$, a set $\mathcal R$ of permutation representatives of the $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double cosets, a complex $M$-module $X$, and for each $\rho\in S_n$ the permutation matrix $w_\rho=P_\rho$, the groups $M_\rho,V_\rho,C_\rho,A_\rho,D_\rho$ and the module ${}^\rho X$ displayed in the statement.

[L1] For every composition $\gamma$ of $n$ the standard parabolic $P_\gamma$, its Levi subgroup $L_\gamma$ and its unipotent radical $U_\gamma$ are given by the entry criteria $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$, respectively $\ne$, respectively $\ge$ together with $g_{kk}=1$; the multiplication map $L_\gamma\times U_\gamma\to P_\gamma$ is a bijection with $L_\gamma\cap U_\gamma=\{I_n\}$ and $U_\gamma\trianglelefteq P_\gamma$, so that $P_\gamma=L_\gamma\ltimes U_\gamma$ and each $g\in P_\gamma$ has a unique factorisation $g=l u$ with $l\in L_\gamma$, $u\in U_\gamma$; moreover $P_\gamma$ is the stabiliser in $G$ of the standard partial flag of type $\gamma$, and $P_{(n)}=G=L_{(n)}$ with $U_{(n)}=\{I_n\}$, while $P_{(1^n)}=B$, $L_{(1^n)}=T$ and $U_{(1^n)}=U$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]], [[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]]).

[L2] $W=N/T\cong S_n$ with $w_\tau=P_\tau T$; permutation matrices satisfy $(P_\sigma)_{ij}=1$ exactly when $i=\sigma(j)$, $P_\sigma P_\tau=P_{\sigma\circ\tau}$, $P_{\sigma^{-1}}=P_\sigma^{-1}$, $P_{\mathrm{id}}=I_n$, and $(P_\sigma AP_\sigma^{-1})_{kl}=A_{\sigma^{-1}(k),\sigma^{-1}(l)}$ for every matrix $A$ ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]], [[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

[L3] Every $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset contains exactly one $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing permutation, the assignment $W_\alpha w_\tau W_\beta\mapsto P_\alpha P_\tau P_\beta$ is a bijection onto the set of $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double cosets of $G$, and these double cosets are pairwise disjoint with union $G$ ([[lem-standard-parabolic-double-cosets-are-young-double-cosets]]).

[L4] For every $\rho\in S_n$ the sets $M_\rho,V_\rho$ are subgroups of $G$ with $M_\rho\cap V_\rho=\{I_n\}$ and $V_\rho\trianglelefteq w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}=M_\rho V_\rho$, the sets $C_\rho,A_\rho,D_\rho$ are subgroups with $C_\rho\le M_\rho$, $A_\rho\le V_\rho$, $D_\rho\le U$, $C_\rho$ normalising $A_\rho$ and $D_\rho$ and $A_\rho\cap C_\rho=\{I_n\}$; and with $(L/A_\rho)\times_{C_\rho}(D_\rho\backslash M_\rho)$ the balanced product of the classes $[lA_\rho,D_\rho m]$, $l\in L$, $m\in M_\rho$, under the relation $(lcA_\rho,D_\rho m)\sim(lA_\rho,D_\rho cm)$, $c\in C_\rho$, the map $\Phi_\rho([lA_\rho,D_\rho m]):=U\,l\,m\,w_\rho\,V$ is a bijection onto the set $S_\rho=\{UxV:x\in P_\alpha w_\rho Q\}$ of $(U,V)$-double cosets inside $P_\alpha w_\rho Q$, equivariant for the left actions $l'\cdot[lA_\rho,D_\rho m]=[l'lA_\rho,D_\rho m]$ and $l'\cdot(UxV)=Ul'xV$, and for the right actions $[lA_\rho,D_\rho m]\cdot b=[lA_\rho,D_\rho m\,w_\rho bw_\rho^{-1}]$ and $(UxV)\cdot b=UxbV$ with $b\in L_\beta=M$ ([[lem-parabolic-mackey-biset-splitting-in-gl-n]]).

[L5] Let $H$ be a finite group, $P=K\ltimes W$ an internal semidirect product of subgroups of $H$, and $\pi:P\to K$ the projection with kernel $W$; for a complex $K$-module $Y$ the Harish–Chandra induction is $R_K^H(Y)=\operatorname{Ind}_P^H(\operatorname{Inf}_K^PY)$, the set of functions $F:H\to Y$ with $F(h k w)=\pi(kw)^{-1}F(h)$ for all $h\in H$, $k\in K$, $w\in W$, with the action $(h_0\cdot F)(h)=F(h_0^{-1}h)$, and for a complex $H$-module $Z$ the Harish–Chandra restriction is ${}^*\!R_K^H(Z)=Z^W$ with the restricted $K$-action ([[def-harish-chandra-induction-and-restriction-for-finite-gl-n]], [[def-induced-r-linear-g-module-by-h-covariant-functions]]); applied to $H=G$ these are the functors of the statement for $(K,W)=(L,U)$ and $(K,W)=(M,V)$, and $\pi$ is a homomorphism, so that $W$ is its kernel and acts as the identity on inflated modules.

[L6] $L\le P_\alpha$, $U\trianglelefteq P_\alpha$, $P_\alpha=LU$ and $lUl^{-1}=U$ for every $l\in L$, so that $l\cdot(Ux):=Ulx$ is a well-defined left action of $L$ on $U\backslash G$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]], [[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]], [[def-subgroup]], [[def-normal-subgroup]]).

[L7] Coordinate parabolics satisfy $P_{\sigma(\gamma)}=P_\sigma P_\gamma P_\sigma^{-1}$, $L_{\sigma(\gamma)}=P_\sigma L_\gamma P_\sigma^{-1}$ and $U_{\sigma(\gamma)}=P_\sigma U_\gamma P_\sigma^{-1}$ for every permutation $\sigma$ and ordered partition $\gamma$, $L_\gamma$ depends only on the unordered collection of blocks of $\gamma$, and for ordered partitions $\gamma=(S_1,\dots,S_r)$ of the block sizes $|I_i|=a_i$ of a composition $\alpha$ there is $\sigma\in S_n$ with $\sigma(I_i)=S_i$, so that $\gamma=\sigma(\alpha)$ ([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]]).

[L8] $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,n\})$ is the group of bijections of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ under composition, every $\sigma$ is invertible with $(\sigma^{-1})^{-1}=\sigma$, and composition is associative ([[def-symmetric-group]], [[lem-symmetric-group-is-a-group]]).

[L9] For a subgroup $H\le G$ the right cosets $Hg=\{hg:h\in H\}$ partition $G$, the formula $(Hg)\cdot g_0:=Hgg_0$ defines a right action of $G$ on $H\backslash G$, and for a set $S$ with a right action of a group and a subgroup $K$ the orbits of $K$ on $S$ form a set $S/K$ of classes $sK$; a left action of a group on a set satisfies $e\cdot s=s$ and $(kk')\cdot s=k\cdot(k'\cdot s)$; and a complex $M$-module is a complex vector space carrying a linear action of $M$ ([[def-coset]], [[def-subgroup]], [[def-group-action]], [[def-g-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The groups attached to $\rho$.** Fix $\rho\in S_n$, put $w:=w_\rho$ and $\kappa(k):=\operatorname{blk}_\beta(\rho^{-1}(k))$ for $1\le k\le n$, so that $g\in M_\rho=wMw^{-1}$ holds exactly when $(w^{-1}gw)_{uv}=0$ for all $u,v$ with $\operatorname{blk}_\beta(u)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\beta(v)$; since $(w^{-1}gw)_{uv}=g_{\rho(u),\rho(v)}$ and $\operatorname{blk}_\beta(u)$ for $u=\rho^{-1}(k)$ is $\kappa(k)$ by [L2], the entry criteria of [L1] for the composition $\beta$ give $$M_\rho=\{\,g:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)\,\},\qquad V_\rho=\{\,g:g_{kk}=1\text{ for all }k,\ g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }k\ne l\text{ and }\kappa(k)\ge\kappa(l)\,\},$$ and intersecting with the criteria of [L1] for the composition $\alpha$ yields $$C_\rho=\{\,g\in G:g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)\text{ or }\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)\,\},$$ $$D_\rho=\{\,g:g_{kk}=1,\ g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }k\ne l\text{ and }\bigl(\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ge\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)\text{ or }\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)\bigr)\,\},$$ $$A_\rho=\{\,g:g_{kk}=1,\ g_{kl}=0\text{ whenever }k\ne l\text{ and }\bigl(\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)\text{ or }\kappa(k)\ge\kappa(l)\bigr)\,\}.$$ [L1, L2]

1.2 **Transport data for coordinate parabolics.** Let $\gamma,\delta,\sigma,\tau,u,v$ be as in claim 3; then $P_\gamma=uP_\alpha u^{-1}$, $L_\gamma=uLu^{-1}$, $U_\gamma=uUu^{-1}$, $P_\delta=vQv^{-1}$, $L_\delta=vMv^{-1}$, $U_\delta=vVv^{-1}$, $\gamma=\sigma(\alpha)$ and $\delta=\tau(\beta)$ by [L7], the permutation matrices satisfy $w_{\rho'}=P_{\sigma\rho\tau^{-1}}=P_\sigma P_\rho P_{\tau^{-1}}=u\,w_\rho\,v^{-1}$ and $w_{\rho'}^{-1}=v\,w_\rho^{-1}\,u^{-1}$ by [L2], and $\rho\mapsto\rho'=\sigma\rho\tau^{-1}$ is a bijection of $S_n$ with inverse $\rho'\mapsto\sigma^{-1}\rho'\tau$ by [L8]; since $W_\gamma=\sigma W_\alpha\sigma^{-1}$ and $W_\delta=\tau W_\beta\tau^{-1}$ by [L7], this bijection carries the $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset of $\rho$ onto the $(W_\gamma,W_\delta)$-double coset of $\rho'$, so the images of $\mathcal R$ are one per $(W_\gamma,W_\delta)$-double coset of $S_n$. [L2, L7, L8]

1.3 **The $U$-invariants as covariant functions.** Let $Y:=R_M^GX=\operatorname{Ind}_Q^G(\operatorname{Inf}_M^QX)$ and let $\pi:Q\to M$ be the projection with kernel $V$, so that $Y$ is the set of functions $f:G\to X$ with $f(gq)=\pi(q)^{-1}f(g)$ for all $g\in G$, $q\in Q$, the $U$-invariants are $Y^U=\{f\in Y:f(ug)=f(g)\text{ for all }u\in U\}$, and $Y^U$ carries the $L$-action $(l\cdot f)(g)=f(l^{-1}g)$ by [L5]; define $$\mathcal F:=\{\,\varphi:U\backslash G\to X:\varphi(Ug\cdot q)=\pi(q)^{-1}\varphi(Ug)\text{ for all }g\in G,\ q\in Q\,\},$$ the right action being $(Ug)\cdot q:=Ugq$ of [L9], with the left action $(l\cdot\varphi)(Ug):=\varphi(Ul^{-1}g)$ of [L6]. Then $\Psi(f)(Ug):=f(g)$ is a bijection $Y^U\to\mathcal F$: it is well defined and injective because $Ug=Ug'$ exactly when $g'=ug$ with $u\in U$ and $f(ug)=f(g)$; it is surjective because for $\varphi\in\mathcal F$ the formula $f_\varphi(g):=\varphi(Ug)$ defines an element of $Y^U$ with $\Psi(f_\varphi)=\varphi$, since $f_\varphi(gq)=\varphi(Ug\cdot q)=\pi(q)^{-1}f_\varphi(g)$ and $f_\varphi(ug)=\varphi(Uug)=\varphi(Ug)$; and it is $L$-equivariant because $\Psi(l\cdot f)(Ug)=(l\cdot f)(g)=f(l^{-1}g)=\Psi(f)(Ul^{-1}g)=(l\cdot\Psi(f))(Ug)$ for the well-defined left action of [L6]. [L5, L6, L9]

2.1 **Decomposition along the double cosets.** By [L3] the double cosets $P_\alpha w_\rho Q$ with $\rho\in\mathcal R$ are pairwise disjoint and cover $G$, hence the sets $Z_\rho:=\{Ux:x\in P_\alpha w_\rho Q\}=U\backslash(P_\alpha w_\rho Q)$ of right cosets are pairwise disjoint, nonempty ($Uw_\rho\in Z_\rho$) and cover $U\backslash G$; each $Z_\rho$ is stable under the left action of $L$, because $l\cdot(Ux)=Ulx$ with $lx\in P_\alpha w_\rho Q$ for $l\in L\le P_\alpha$ by [L6], and applying the same to $l^{-1}$ gives $l\cdot Z_\rho=Z_\rho$. Writing $\mathcal F_\rho:=\{\varphi\in\mathcal F:\varphi(z)=0\text{ for all }z\notin Z_\rho\}$, the support $\{z:\varphi(z)\ne0\}$ of $\varphi\in\mathcal F$ is a union of right $Q$-orbits, since $\varphi(z)\ne0$ implies $\varphi(zq)=\pi(q)^{-1}\varphi(z)\ne0$; because $Z_\rho\cdot Q=Z_\rho$ for every $\rho$, every $\varphi$ is the unique sum of its restrictions $\varphi_\rho\in\mathcal F_\rho$, and each $\mathcal F_\rho$ is $L$-stable because $Z_\rho$ is; hence $\mathcal F=\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal R}\mathcal F_\rho$ as $L$-modules. [L3, L6, step 1.3]

2.2 **The first intersected parabolic.** Let $\rho\in S_n$ and $g\in P_\alpha\cap M_\rho$. Write $g=lu$ with $l\in L=L_\alpha$ and $u\in U=U_\alpha$, the unique factorisation of [L1]; then $l$ is the $\alpha$-block diagonal part of $g$, because for $\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)=\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)$ the entry $(lu)_{kl}$ equals $l_{kl}u_{ll}=l_{kl}$ by the entry criteria of [L1] for $U_\alpha$, while $l_{kl}=0$ for $\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\alpha(l)$ by the entry criteria of [L1] for $L_\alpha$; by step 1.1 the matrix $g$ satisfies $g_{kl}=0$ whenever $\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(l)$, and $l$ inherits this because its entries are entries of $g$ inside $\alpha$-blocks, so $l\in L\cap M_\rho=C_\rho$; also $d:=l^{-1}g=u$ lies in $U$ and in $M_\rho$, because $l\in M_\rho$ and $g\in M_\rho$, so $d\in U\cap M_\rho=D_\rho$ and $g=ld\in C_\rho D_\rho$. Conversely $C_\rho\le L\le P_\alpha$ and $D_\rho\le U\le P_\alpha$ by [L1], and both lie in $M_\rho$, so $P_\alpha\cap M_\rho=C_\rho D_\rho$. Inside each coordinate block $\rho(J_j)$ of $M_\rho$, its matrices are upper block triangular on the nonempty intersections $I_i\cap\rho(J_j)$ ordered by $i$. Thus it is a coordinate parabolic of $M_\rho$ with Levi $C_\rho$ and radical $D_\rho$; this is the internal semidirect product $C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho$, because $C_\rho$ normalises $D_\rho$ by [L4] and $C_\rho\cap D_\rho\subseteq L\cap U=\{I_n\}$ by [L1]. [L1, L4, step 1.1]

2.3 **The second intersected parabolic.** Let $\rho\in S_n$ and $g\in L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$. By [L1] the element $g$ is $\alpha$-block diagonal, and since $w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}=w_\rho(MV)w_\rho^{-1}=M_\rho V_\rho$ there are $m\in M_\rho$ and $a\in V_\rho$ with $g=ma$; by step 1.1 the matrix $m$ is $\kappa$-block diagonal, so for $\kappa(k)=\kappa(l)$ the entry $(ma)_{kl}$ equals $m_{kl}a_{ll}=m_{kl}$, every other term $m_{ki}a_{il}$ of the sum vanishing because $m_{ki}=0$ for $\kappa(k)\ne\kappa(i)$ or because $a_{il}=0$ for $i\ne l$ with $\kappa(i)=\kappa(l)$; hence $m$ is the $\kappa$-block diagonal part of $g$ and, its entries being entries of the $\alpha$-block diagonal matrix $g$, it lies in $L\cap M_\rho=C_\rho$, while $a=m^{-1}g$ lies in $L$, as a product of elements of $L$, and in $V_\rho$, that is $a\in L\cap V_\rho=A_\rho$; so $g=ma\in C_\rho A_\rho$. Conversely $C_\rho\subseteq M_\rho\subseteq w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$ and $A_\rho\subseteq V_\rho\subseteq w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$, and both lie in $L$, so $L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho A_\rho$, which is the internal semidirect product $C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho$ by [L4]. Inside each block $I_i$ of $L$, the nonempty intersections $I_i\cap\rho(J_j)$ ordered by $j$ give precisely this upper block triangular subgroup, so it is a coordinate parabolic of $L$ with Levi $C_\rho$ and radical $A_\rho$. [L1, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 **The piece is a space of $M$-covariant functions on the biset.** Fix $\rho\in\mathcal R$; by step 2.1 the sets $Z_\rho$ and the spaces $\mathcal F_\rho$ are as defined there, and by [L1] and [L5] the projection $\pi:Q\to M$ has kernel $V$ and satisfies $\pi(m)=m$ for $m\in M$, so for $\varphi\in\mathcal F_\rho$ and $v\in V$ one has $\varphi(zv)=\pi(v)^{-1}\varphi(z)=\varphi(z)$: the function $\varphi$ is constant on the right $V$-orbits of $Z_\rho$, which are the sets $\{Uxv:v\in V\}=UxV$ with $x\in P_\alpha w_\rho Q$, so $Z_\rho/V$ is the set $S_\rho=\{UxV:x\in P_\alpha w_\rho Q\}$ of [L4] and $\Theta(UxV):=\varphi(Ux)$ is a well-defined function $\Theta:S_\rho\to X$. The right action $S_\rho\times M\to S_\rho$, $(UxV)\cdot m:=UxmV$, is well defined because $mVm^{-1}=V$ by [L4], and $\Theta((UxV)\cdot m)=\varphi(Uxm)=\pi(m)^{-1}\varphi(Ux)=m^{-1}\Theta(UxV)$; conversely, if $\Theta:S_\rho\to X$ satisfies $\Theta(s\cdot m)=m^{-1}\Theta(s)$ for all $s\in S_\rho$, $m\in M$, then $\varphi(Ux):=\Theta(UxV)$ is well defined on $Z_\rho$ (if $Ux=Ux'$ then $UxV=Ux'V$) and satisfies $\varphi(Uxq)=\Theta(UxmV)=\Theta((UxV)\cdot m)=m^{-1}\Theta(UxV)=\pi(q)^{-1}\varphi(Ux)$ for $q=mv\in Q$ written with $m\in M$, $v\in V$ as the unique factorisation of [L1], so $\varphi\in\mathcal F_\rho$. The two assignments are inverse to each other, and they are $L$-equivariant for the left actions $(l\cdot\Theta)(s):=\Theta(l^{-1}\cdot s)$ on $S_\rho$ and $(l\cdot\varphi)(z)=\varphi(l^{-1}\cdot z)$ on $Z_\rho$, because $\varphi(Ul^{-1}x)=\Theta(Ul^{-1}xV)$ for all $l\in L$ and $x$. [L1, L4, L5, step 2.1]

4.1 **Transport along the balanced product.** Fix $\rho\in\mathcal R$ and write $A:=A_\rho$, $C:=C_\rho$, $D:=D_\rho$. By [L4] the map $\Phi([lA,Dm]):=Ulmw_\rho V$ is a bijection from the balanced product $W_\rho:=(L/A)\times_C(D\backslash M_\rho)$ onto $S_\rho$, the left action $l'\cdot[lA,Dm]=[l'lA,Dm]$ corresponds to $l'\cdot(UxV)=Ul'xV$, and the right action of $b\in L_\beta=M$ given by $[lA,Dm]\cdot b=[lA,Dm\,w_\rho bw_\rho^{-1}]$ corresponds to $(UxV)\cdot b=UxbV$; since $b\mapsto w_\rho bw_\rho^{-1}$ is a bijection from $M$ onto $M_\rho$ with inverse $\mu\mapsto w_\rho^{-1}\mu w_\rho$, this right action is the right translation $[lA,Dm]\cdot\mu=[lA,Dm\mu]$ by the elements of $M_\rho$, so that $[lA,Dm]=[lA,DI_n]\cdot m$ for every $m\in M_\rho$. Transporting a function $\Theta:S_\rho\to X$ of step 3.1 along $\Phi$ gives the function $\chi:=\Theta\circ\Phi$ on $W_\rho$, and $\Theta\mapsto\chi$ is a bijection between the functions with the covariance of step 3.1 and the functions $\chi:W_\rho\to X$ with $\chi(y\cdot b)=b^{-1}\chi(y)$ for all $y\in W_\rho$, $b\in M$, under which the left $L$-actions become $(l'\cdot\chi)(y)=\chi(l'^{-1}\cdot y)$. [L4, step 3.1]

5.1 **Extraction of the coefficient function.** Fix $\rho\in\mathcal R$, keep $A,C,D$ of step 4.1, and let $\chi:W_\rho\to X$ satisfy $\chi(y\cdot b)=b^{-1}\chi(y)$ for all $y\in W_\rho$, $b\in M$; define $F:L/A\to X$ by $F(lA):=\chi([lA,DI_n])$, which is well defined because the class $[lA,DI_n]$ depends only on the coset $lA$. Then $F$ determines $\chi$, because $[lA,Dm]=[lA,DI_n]\cdot m$ by step 4.1 and hence $\chi([lA,Dm])=m^{-1}F(lA)$ for every $m\in M_\rho$, and $\chi$ determines $F$ by definition, so the correspondence is a bijection onto its image; furthermore $F(lA)\in({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$ for every $l$, because for $d\in D_\rho$ the element $m_0:=w_\rho^{-1}dw_\rho$ lies in $M$ and $[lA,DI_n]\cdot m_0=[lA,D\,w_\rho m_0w_\rho^{-1}]=[lA,Dd]=[lA,DI_n]$, so the covariance gives $F(lA)=\chi([lA,DI_n])=\chi([lA,DI_n]\cdot m_0)=m_0^{-1}F(lA)=(w_\rho^{-1}dw_\rho)^{-1}F(lA)$, that is, $d$ fixes $F(lA)$ under the $M_\rho$-action of ${}^\rho X$; and $F(lcA)=c^{-1}F(lA)$ for every $c\in C_\rho$, because the generating relation of [L4] gives $[lcA,DI_n]=[lA,Dc]$ and $[lA,Dc]=[lA,DI_n]\cdot(w_\rho^{-1}cw_\rho)$, so $F(lcA)=\chi([lA,Dc])=(w_\rho^{-1}cw_\rho)^{-1}F(lA)=c^{-1}F(lA)$ for the action of ${}^\rho X$. Conversely, if $F:L/A_\rho\to({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$ satisfies $F(lcA_\rho)=c^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$ for all $c\in C_\rho$, then $\chi_F([lA_\rho,D_\rho m]):=m^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$ is well defined: replacing $m$ by $dm$ with $d\in D_\rho$ replaces $m^{-1}$ by $m^{-1}(w_\rho^{-1}dw_\rho)^{-1}$, which acts trivially on the values of $F$ because $F$ takes values in $({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$, and replacing the pair $(lA_\rho,D_\rho m)$ by $(lcA_\rho,D_\rho c^{-1}m)$ in the same class replaces $m^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$ by $(c^{-1}m)^{-1}F(lcA_\rho)=m^{-1}c\,c^{-1}F(lA_\rho)=m^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$; the so-defined $\chi_F$ satisfies the covariance of step 4.1 and is $L$-equivariant, because $(l'\cdot\chi_F)(y)=\chi_F(l'^{-1}y)$ and $(l'\cdot F)(lA)=F(l'^{-1}lA)$; hence $\chi\mapsto F$ is a bijection from the covariant functions of step 4.1 onto the functions $F:L/A_\rho\to({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$ with $F(lcA_\rho)=c^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$. [L4, step 4.1]

6.1 **The summand is a Harish–Chandra induced module.** Fix $\rho\in\mathcal R$ and put $Y:=({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}$, a $C_\rho$-module by [L4]; by [L5] applied to the group $L$ and the internal semidirect product $C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho$ of step 2.3, the module $R_{C_\rho}^L(Y)$ is the set of functions $F:L\to Y$ with $F(l\cdot ca)=\pi'(ca)^{-1}F(l)$ for the projection $\pi':C_\rho A_\rho\to C_\rho$ with kernel $A_\rho$, and $\pi'(ca)=c$ because $C_\rho$ normalises $A_\rho$ and $C_\rho\cap A_\rho=\{I_n\}$ by [L4], so the condition is $F(lca)=c^{-1}F(l)$; the assignment $F\mapsto(lA_\rho\mapsto F(l))$ is a well-defined linear bijection from $R_{C_\rho}^L(Y)$ onto the set of functions of step 5.1, since a function on $L/A_\rho$ with $F(lcA_\rho)=c^{-1}F(lA_\rho)$ corresponds to a function on $L$ with $F(lca)=c^{-1}F(l)$ and conversely, elements of $A_\rho$ acting invisibly on the values; it commutes with the left translations that give the $L$-actions on both sides, and it is therefore an isomorphism of $L$-modules $\mathcal F_\rho\cong R_{C_\rho}^L(({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho})$ by step 3.1, step 4.1 and step 5.1. [L4, L5, step 3.1, step 5.1]

7.1 **Claims 1 and 2.** Claim 1 is [L3], the representatives in $\mathcal R$ being one per double coset; for claim 2, step 1.3 identifies $^*\!R_L^G(R_M^GX)=Y^U$ with $\mathcal F$ as an $L$-module, step 2.1 decomposes $\mathcal F=\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal R}\mathcal F_\rho$ as $L$-modules, and step 6.1 identifies each $\mathcal F_\rho$ with $R_{C_\rho}^L(({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho})$ as an $L$-module, so the two sides of the display of claim 2 are isomorphic as $L$-modules; the two structure statements are step 2.2 and step 2.3, which identify the parabolic subgroups occurring in the summand with those named in claim 2. [L3, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 6.1]

8.1 **Claim 3.** Assume the hypotheses of claim 3 and keep the notation $\gamma,\delta,\sigma,\tau,u,v,\rho'$ of step 1.2. Conjugation by $u$ maps $P_\alpha$ onto $P_\gamma$, $L$ onto $L_\gamma$, $U$ onto $U_\gamma$ and $M_\rho$ onto $uM_\rho u^{-1}=w_{\rho'}L_\delta w_{\rho'}^{-1}$ by step 1.2, so multiplying $P_\alpha w_\rho Q$ by $u$ on the left and $v^{-1}$ on the right gives $P_\gamma w_{\rho'}P_\delta=u\,(P_\alpha w_\rho Q)\,v^{-1}$, and claim 1 for $(\gamma,\delta)$ follows from claim 1 for $(\alpha,\beta)$, from the bijection $\rho\mapsto\rho'$ of step 1.2 and from $P_\delta=vQv^{-1}$. For claim 2, let $Z:=R_{L_\delta}^G(X)$ be the set of functions $f:G\to X$ with $f(gy)=\pi_\delta(y)^{-1}f(g)$ for all $y\in P_\delta$, where $\pi_\delta:P_\delta\to L_\delta$ is the projection with kernel $U_\delta$ and $L_\delta$ acts on $X$ by $\ell\cdot x=(v^{-1}\ell v)\cdot x$, and define $\Upsilon(f)(g):=f(ugv^{-1})$. For $q\in Q$, one has $vqv^{-1}\in P_\delta$ and $\pi_\delta(vqv^{-1})=v\pi(q)v^{-1}$, so $$\Upsilon(f)(gq)=f(ugqv^{-1})=f(ugv^{-1}\cdot vqv^{-1})=\pi_\delta(vqv^{-1})^{-1}\cdot f(ugv^{-1})=\pi(q)^{-1}\cdot\Upsilon(f)(g),$$ where the last equality uses the stated $L_\delta$-action on $X$. The inverse is $F\mapsto f$ with $f(h)=F(u^{-1}hv)$, so $\Upsilon$ is a bijection $Z\to R_M^G(X)$. For the left $G$-actions by translation, $\Upsilon(h\cdot f)=(u^{-1}hu)\cdot\Upsilon(f)$; thus $U_\gamma=uUu^{-1}$-invariants map to $U$-invariants, and the restricted map is $L_\gamma$-linear when $L_\gamma\to L$ is $p\mapsto u^{-1}pu$. Applying claim 2 on the standard side and transporting its summands back along this identification replaces $M_\rho,V_\rho,C_\rho,A_\rho,D_\rho$ by $uM_\rho u^{-1},uV_\rho u^{-1},uC_\rho u^{-1},uA_\rho u^{-1},uD_\rho u^{-1}$. The module on the transported $uM_\rho u^{-1}$ is the stated ${}^{\rho'}\!X$: if $m\in M_\rho$, then $v^{-1}w_{\rho'}^{-1}(umu^{-1})w_{\rho'}v=w_\rho^{-1}mw_\rho$, which gives the same action on $X$. Since $\rho\mapsto\sigma\rho\tau^{-1}$ bijects the Weyl double-coset sets, an arbitrary set of permutation representatives for the coordinate pair arises this way from an arbitrary standard set $\mathcal R$. This proves claim 3 for any such representatives. [L2, L4, L7, step 1.2, step 7.1]

8.2 **Boundary cases.** Use the block-increasing representatives for these boundary computations. For $n=1$ one has $\alpha=\beta=(1)$, $\mathcal R=\{\mathrm{id}\}$, $L=M=T=G=\mathbb F_q^\times$, $U=V=\{I_1\}$ and $C=G$, $A=D=\{I_1\}$, so claim 2 reads $^*\!R_G^GR_G^GX\cong X$ by [L5]; for $\alpha=\beta=(n)$ one has $L=M=G$, $U=V=\{I_n\}$, $\mathcal R=\{\mathrm{id}\}$, $C_\rho=G$ and $A_\rho=D_\rho=\{I_n\}$ by [L1], and claim 2 reads $X\cong R_G^G(X)\cong X$; for $\alpha=\beta=(1^n)$ one has $L=M=T$ and $U=V$ the standard maximal unipotent subgroup by [L1], and for every $\rho\in S_n$ the group $M_\rho=w_\rho Tw_\rho^{-1}=T$ is diagonal, so $C_\rho=T$, $D_\rho=U\cap T=\{I_n\}$ and $A_\rho=T\cap V_\rho=\{I_n\}$, a diagonal matrix lying in a conjugate of $U$ being unipotent as well as diagonal and hence equal to $I_n$; each summand is then $R_T^T(({}^\rho X)^{\{I_n\}})\cong{}^\rho X$, and since $\mathcal R=S_n$ claim 2 reads $$^*\!R_T^GR_T^GX\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\in S_n}{}^\rho X.$$ These twists need not be isomorphic to $X$: for $n=2$, $q=3$ and $X$ the one-dimensional character $\operatorname{diag}(a,d)\mapsto a$, the transposition twist is $\operatorname{diag}(a,d)\mapsto d$, which differs on $\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$. [L1, L3, L5, step 7.1]

9.1 **Conclusion.** Claim 1 and claim 2 are step 7.1, claim 3 is step 8.1, and the extreme and boundary configurations $n=1$, $\alpha=\beta=(n)$ and $\alpha=\beta=(1^n)$ are step 8.2; the proof is complete. ∎ [step 7.1, step 8.1, step 8.2]

## Remarks

- **Which parabolic subgroups the theorem covers.** For $q>2$ the coordinate parabolics are exactly the parabolic subgroups of $G$ containing the diagonal torus ([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]]), so claims 2 and 3 then apply to every pair of parabolic subgroups containing $T$; at $q=2$ the torus is trivial and not every parabolic subgroup is conjugate to a standard one by a permutation matrix, already for $n=2$ where the third Borel subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_2)$ is not a coordinate parabolic, which is why the theorem is stated for coordinate parabolics.
- **Dependence on the representatives.** The decomposition of claim 1 and the isomorphism of claim 2 depend on the chosen set $\mathcal R$ of permutation representatives, but only up to the transportation of the summands along the double cosets; no canonical intertwiner is asserted.
