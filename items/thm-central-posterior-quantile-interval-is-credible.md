## Statement

Let $\Pi_y$ be a posterior probability kernel on $\mathbb R$ and let $0<\alpha<1$. Define $C_\alpha(y)=[q_{\alpha/2}(y),q_{1-\alpha/2}(y)]$ using the quantiles from [[lem-measurable-posterior-quantile-and-cdf-bracketing]]. Then $C_\alpha$ is graph-measurable and $\Pi_y(C_\alpha(y))\ge1-\alpha$ for every $y$. Thus equal-tailed posterior quantile intervals are one kind of credible set.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The posterior probability kernel, the quantile convention, and $0<\alpha<1$ in the Statement.

[F1] A credible set is a graph-measurable set-valued rule whose posterior mass is at least its stated level. [[def-posterior-credible-set]]

[F2] By the quantile definition $q_u(y)=\inf\{r\in\mathbb Q:F_y(r)\ge u\}$, the defining sets are nested oppositely to $u$, so $u\le v$ implies $q_u(y)\le q_v(y)$. Each $q_u$ is measurable and satisfies $\Pi_y((-infty,q_u(y)))\le u\le\Pi_y((-infty,q_u(y)])$. [[lem-measurable-posterior-quantile-and-cdf-bracketing]]

[F3] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F4] There is a rational strictly between any two distinct real numbers. [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F5] If $A\subseteq B$ and $\Pi_y(A)<\infty$, then $\Pi_y(B\setminus A)=\Pi_y(B)-\Pi_y(A)$. [[prop-measure-of-a-set-difference]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Since $\alpha/2<1-\alpha/2$, the defining rational sets for the two quantiles are nested in the direction that gives $q_{\alpha/2}(y)\le q_{1-\alpha/2}(y)$. Both endpoint functions are measurable by [F2]. [given, F2]

1.2 Put $l=q_{\alpha/2}(y)$ and $u=q_{1-\alpha/2}(y)$. The disjoint difference $(-\infty,u]\setminus(-\infty,l)$ is $[l,u]$, so $\Pi_y([l,u])=\Pi_y((-\infty,u])-\Pi_y((-\infty,l)).$ By [F2], the first term is at least $1-\alpha/2$ and the second is at most $\alpha/2$, giving posterior mass at least $1-\alpha$. [given, F2, F5, algebra]

2.1 The complement of the interval membership graph consists of pairs with $x<q_{\alpha/2}(y)$ or $x>q_{1-\alpha/2}(y)$. By [F4], the first relation is the countable union over $r\in\mathbb Q$ of $\{y:q_{\alpha/2}(y)>r\}\times(-\infty,r),$ and the second is the countable union over $r\in\mathbb Q$ of $\{y:q_{1-\alpha/2}(y)<r\}\times(r,\infty).$ Each is a measurable rectangle by endpoint measurability, so [F3] makes the graph measurable. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 The graph measurability from step 2.1 and the mass bound from step 1.2 satisfy [F1], so $C_\alpha$ is a posterior credible set. [F1, step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎
