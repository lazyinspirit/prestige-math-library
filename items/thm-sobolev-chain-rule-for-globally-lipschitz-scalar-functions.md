  formula for $C^1$ maps with bounded derivative by smooth approximation

  design requires, and does not use the later density theorem.

## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, used for the published ACL characterisation
and the Countable-Choice interfaces cited in the proof. Let
$\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, $n\ge1$, let $1\le p\le\infty$, let
$u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb R)$, and let $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be
Lipschitz with constant $L\ge0$. Put
$$D_F:=\{\,t\in\mathbb R : F \text{ is differentiable at } t\,\},\qquad N_F:=\mathbb R\setminus D_F .$$

1. **Membership.** $F\circ u\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, and
   $F\circ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$ if and only if $F(u)\in L^p(\Omega)$.
   This holds automatically if $F(0)=0$, and also if $p<\infty$ and
   $\lambda_n(\Omega)<\infty$.
2. **Level-set clause.** $N_F$ has Lebesgue measure zero, and for every
   Lebesgue-null set $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ (no measurability of $N$ is
   assumed), every measurable representative $\hat u$ of $u$ and every
   measurable representative $\hat v_i$ of $D_iu$ satisfy: the set
   $Z_{i,N}:=\{x\in\Omega : \hat v_i(x)\neq0$ and $\hat u(x)\in N\}$ has
   Lebesgue outer measure zero. In particular $D_iu=0$ almost everywhere
   on the part of $\Omega$ where $u$ takes values in $N_F$.
3. **Chain rule with the level-set convention.** Let $\hat u$ and
   $\hat v_i$ be measurable representatives of $u$ and $D_iu$. Define
   $$\Phi_i(x):=\begin{cases}F'(\hat u(x))\,\hat v_i(x),&\hat u(x)\in D_F,\\ 0,&\hat u(x)\in N_F.\end{cases}$$
   Then $\Phi_i$ agrees almost everywhere with a measurable function,
   determines an element of $L^p(\Omega)$, and is a representative of the
   weak derivative:
   $$D_i(F\circ u)=\Phi_i\quad\text{almost everywhere on }\Omega,\qquad i=1,\ldots,n .$$
   That is, the classical product $F'(u)\,D_iu$ is used where $F$ is
   differentiable at $u$, and on the remaining preimage the product is
   taken as zero. Moreover, for every Borel function
   $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with $g=F'$ almost everywhere, the class of
   $g(u)D_iu$ is the same, and $D_i(F\circ u)=g(u)D_iu$ almost
   everywhere.

If $\Omega=\varnothing$ every assertion is vacuous. The constant case
$L=0$, the endpoint exponents $p=1$ and $p=\infty$ and the case $n=1$ are
included. No assertion is made about the pointwise derivative of an
arbitrary representative of $u$, and none about a pointwise derivative of
$F$ at a point where $F$ fails to be differentiable.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; an open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ with $n\ge1$; an exponent $1\le p\le\infty$; a class $u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb R)$; and an $L$-Lipschitz function $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ with $L\ge0$.

[F1] $W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ is the set of classes $u\in L^p(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ such that for every first-order multi-index there is an $L^p$ class with a locally integrable representative satisfying the signed test identity for every test function; each such derivative determines one class $D_iu$. For open $U$ with $\overline U$ compact in $\Omega$, the notation $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ means that the restriction of the class belongs to $W^{1,p}(U;\mathbb K)$ ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

[F2] $L^\infty(\mu)=\{f:X\to\mathbb R: f$ measurable and $\|f\|_\infty<\infty\}$ for a measure space $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ ([[def-l-infinity-on-a-measure-space]]).

[F3] For $1\le p<\infty$, $\mathcal L^p(\mu)$ consists of the measurable $f$ with $\int|f|^p\,d\mu<\infty$, and $L^p(\mu)$ denotes the quotient of $\mathcal L^p(\mu)$ by the almost-everywhere-zero functions ([[def-calligraphic-l-p-on-a-measure-space]]).

[F4] On a measure space, $L^p(\mu)$ for $0<p<\infty$ and for $p=\infty$ is the set of almost-everywhere classes of $\mathcal L^p(\mu)$ respectively $L^\infty(\mu)$, and for $1\le p\le\infty$ the displayed quotient agrees with the usual quotient-vector-space construction ([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]]).

[F5] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, $n\ge1$, $1\le p<\infty$ and $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$. A class $u$ on $\Omega$ lies in $W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ if and only if $u\in L^p(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ and $u$ has one measurable ACL representative $u^*$ whose classical coordinate derivatives $\partial_iu^*$ exist almost everywhere, are measurable, and belong to $L^p(\Omega;\mathbb K)$. In that case $\partial_iu^*$ is a representative of $D_iu$ for every $i$ ([[thm-acl-characterisation-of-w-one-p]]).

[F6] Assume Countable Choice for the completed-product convention. A measurable representative $u^*$ of a class on open $\Omega$ is absolutely continuous on almost every coordinate line (ACL) when its sections along almost every line in each coordinate direction are absolutely continuous on compact subintervals, with exceptional parameter sets allowed to depend on the direction and the box; the countable family of open rational boxes $Q$ with $\overline Q\subseteq\Omega$ covers $\Omega$, and the exceptional sets may be united into one null set per direction. For $n=1$ the condition is absolute continuity on every compact subinterval of $\Omega$; if $\Omega=\varnothing$ the condition is vacuous ([[def-absolute-continuity-on-almost-every-coordinate-line]]).

[F7] For $a\le b$, a function $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is absolutely continuous when each short finite family of disjoint subintervals with total length below $\delta$ has total endpoint oscillation below $\varepsilon$ ([[def-absolutely-continuous-function]]).

[F8] Assume Countable Choice. If $v=D^\alpha u$ weakly on $\Omega$ and $V\subseteq\Omega$ is open, then $v|_V=D^\alpha(u|_V)$ weakly on $V$ ([[lem-weak-derivative-linearity-locality-and-commutation]]).

[F9] If $F\in AC[a,b]$ and $h$ is Lipschitz on $F([a,b])$, then $h\circ F\in AC[a,b]$ ([[thm-a-lipschitz-function-after-an-absolutely-continuous-function-is-absolutely-continuous]]).

[F10] Let $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ be metric spaces. A function $f:X\to Y$ is Lipschitz with constant $L\ge0$ when $d_Y(f(x),f(x'))\le L\,d_X(x,x')$ for all $x,x'\in X$ ([[def-lipschitz-holder-contraction]]).

[F11] Every Lipschitz $f:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is absolutely continuous ([[thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy]]).

[F12] Assume Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. A real function $F$ on a compact interval $[a,b]$ is absolutely continuous if and only if $F'$ exists almost everywhere, $F'\in L^1[a,b]$, and $F(x)-F(a)=\int_a^xF'(t)\,dt$ for every $x\in[a,b]$ ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]]).

[F13] Assume Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. Let $a\le b$ and $c\le d$, let $f:[c,d]\to\mathbb R$ be a representative of an element of $L^1[c,d]$, let $F=I_f$ be its indefinite integral on $[c,d]$, and let $g:[a,b]\to[c,d]$ be absolutely continuous. If $F\circ g$ is absolutely continuous, then $(F\circ g)'=f(g)g'$ almost everywhere and $f(g)g'\in L^1[a,b]$; the conclusion holds for every such representative $f$ ([[lem-chain-rule-for-an-indefinite-integral-after-an-absolutely-continuous-composition]]).

[F14] Assume Countable Choice. Every box with $a_i\le b_i$ is Lebesgue measurable with measure the product of the side lengths, so a box with $a_i<b_i$ has finite positive measure ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]).

[F15] Assume Countable Choice. Every bounded Lebesgue measurable subset of $\mathbb R^n$ has finite measure ([[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).

[F16] If $1\le p,q,r\le\infty$ satisfy $1/r=1/p+1/q$ and $f,g$ lie in the corresponding spaces, then $fg$ lies in the space for $r$ and $\|fg\|_r\le\|f\|_p\|g\|_q$ ([[thm-generalized-holder-inequality-for-products]]).

[F17] If $\mu(X)<\infty$, $1\le p<r<\infty$ and $f\in\mathcal L^r(\mu)$, then $f\in\mathcal L^p(\mu)$; and if $1\le p<\infty$ and $f\in L^\infty(\mu)$, then $f\in\mathcal L^p(\mu)$ and $\|f\|_p\le\mu(X)^{1/p}\|f\|_\infty$ ([[thm-finite-measure-l-r-includes-into-l-p-for-p-less-r]]).

[F18] If $f$ is measurable with $\|f\|_\infty<\infty$, then $|f|\le\|f\|_\infty$ almost everywhere; and if $|f|\le M$ almost everywhere, then $\|f\|_\infty\le M$ ([[prop-essential-supremum-is-attained-as-the-least-essential-bound]]).

[F19] For $1\le p<\infty$ the class $\mathcal L^p(\mu)$ is a real vector space under pointwise addition and scalar multiplication, and so is $L^\infty(\mu)$ ([[thm-calligraphic-l-p-and-l-infinity-are-vector-spaces-for-p-at-least-one]]).

[F20] Assume Countable Choice. Every continuous map $\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^m$, $n,m\ge1$, is Borel measurable ([[cor-continuous-functions-are-borel-measurable]]).

[F21] If $f$ is measurable and $g$ is Borel measurable on its codomain, then $g\circ f$ is measurable ([[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]).

[F22] In ZF, every open cover of an open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ admits an at most countable locally finite smooth partition of unity with compact supports, each support contained in some member of the cover ([[lem-test-function-cutoffs-and-euclidean-localization]]).

[F23] The class $L^1(\mu)$ is a complex vector space and the Lebesgue integral is complex-linear on it ([[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]).

[F24] For nonnegative measurable $f\le g$ one has $\int f\le\int g$, and $\int cf=c\int f$ for $c\ge0$ ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]).

[F25] A measure $\mu$ on a space $X$ is finite if $\mu(X)<+\infty$ ([[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]).

[F26] In ZF the Axiom of Choice implies Countable Choice and the prescribed-start form of Dependent Choice ([[lem-ac-supplies-countable-and-dependent-choice-for-banach-integration]]).

[F27] The Axiom of Choice asserts a choice function for every family of nonempty sets ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F28] For $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, $f:A\to\mathbb R$ and a limit point $c$ of $A$, the difference quotient is $q_{f,c}(x)=(f(x)-f(c))/(x-c)$, and $f$ is differentiable at $c$ exactly when $\lim_{x\to c}q_{f,c}(x)$ exists, in which case $f'(c)$ is that limit ([[def-derivative]], [[def-function-limit]]).

[F29] For a sequence $(x_k)$ of reals, $\limsup_kx_k=\inf_n\sup_{k\ge n}x_k$ and $\liminf_kx_k=\sup_n\inf_{k\ge n}x_k$ in $\overline{\mathbb R}$, and $\lim_kx_k$ exists exactly when the two agree ([[def-limsup-liminf]], [[thm-convergence-iff-limsup-equals-liminf]]).

[F30] Let $(X,\mathcal A)$ be a measurable space and let $f_n:X\to\overline{\mathbb R}$ be measurable for every $n$. Then $\sup_nf_n$, $\inf_nf_n$, $\limsup_nf_n$ and $\liminf_nf_n$ are measurable, and the set where $(f_n)$ converges in $\overline{\mathbb R}$ is measurable ([[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]]).

[F31] Assume Countable Choice and $n\ge1$. The Lebesgue outer measure of $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is the infimum of $\sum_k\mu_0(A_k)$ over countable covers of $E$ by elementary sets $A_k$; in particular $\lambda_n^*(N)=0$ means that for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a countable family of boxes covering $N$ with total elementary volume below $\varepsilon$ ([[def-lebesgue-outer-measure]]).

[F32] Assume Countable Choice. Lebesgue outer measure is monotone and countably subadditive, and agrees with elementary volume on elementary sets, so $\lambda_1^*(E)\le b-a$ for $E\subseteq[a,b]$ and $\lambda_1^*$ of a countable union of null sets is zero ([[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-is-an-outer-measure-agreeing-with-volume]]).

[F33] Assume Countable Choice. For every subset $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is an open $U\supseteq E$ with $\lambda_n(U)\le\lambda_n^*(E)+\varepsilon$ ([[thm-lebesgue-outer-regularity-for-arbitrary-subsets]]).

[F34] Assume Countable Choice. Under the completed-product convention of the ACL definition, iterated integrals over $\mathbb R^{n-1}\times\mathbb R$ compute the integral against $\lambda_n$ for nonnegative measurable functions ([[thm-tonelli-and-fubini-for-completed-product-measures]], [[def-absolute-continuity-on-almost-every-coordinate-line]]).

[F35] The canonical naturals are cofinal in $\mathbb R$ ([[thm-of-archimedean]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F26] the Axiom of Choice [F27] yields Countable Choice and Dependent Choice in ZF. Only these are used below: Countable Choice in the ACL definition [F6], the locality lemma [F8], the box-measure formula [F14], finiteness of Lebesgue measure on bounded sets [F15], the Borel measurability of continuous maps [F20], outer regularity [F33], the completed-product convention [F34], and the countable selection of box representatives below; Dependent Choice in the one-dimensional chain rule [F13] and the fundamental theorem [F12]; and the ACL characterisation [F5] under the Axiom of Choice itself. [F5, F6, F8, F12, F13, F14, F15, F20, F26, F27, F33, F34, given]

1.2 Since $\mathbb R$ is a metric space and $F$ is $L$-Lipschitz, [F10] gives $|F(s)-F(t)|\le L|s-t|$ for all real $s,t$, hence $|F(t)|\le|F(0)|+L|t|$ for every $t$. For every compact interval $[c,d]$ the restriction $F|_{[c,d]}$ is Lipschitz, hence absolutely continuous in the sense of [F7] by [F11]. Applying [F12] on each $[c,d]$ gives: $F'$ exists almost everywhere on $[c,d]$, $F'\in L^1[c,d]$, and $F(x)-F(c)=\int_c^xf_0$ for every $x\in[c,d]$ and every measurable $f_0$ with $f_0=F'$ almost everywhere on $[c,d]$. Since $\mathbb R=\bigcup_{m\ge1}[-m,m]$, countable subadditivity of outer measure [F32] makes $N_F$ a Lebesgue-null set, and for every measurable $g$ with $g=F'$ almost everywhere on $\mathbb R$ the restriction $g|_{[c,d]}$ is a representative of the $L^1$ class of $(F|_{[c,d]})'$. [F7, F10, F11, F12, F32, given]

1.3 **Claim A (one-dimensional level sets).** Let $[a,b]$ be a compact interval, let $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ satisfy $\lambda_1^*(N)=0$, and let $g:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ be any function. Then the set $$Z:=\{\,x\in[a,b] : g(x)\in N \text{ and } g \text{ is differentiable at } x \text{ with } g'(x)\neq0\,\}$$ satisfies $\lambda_1^*(Z)=0$. **Proof of Claim A.** Fix $x\in Z$ and write $c:=g'(x)\neq0$. By the definition of the derivative as a limit of difference quotients [F28] there is $\delta>0$ with $|g(y)-g(x)-c(y-x)|\le\frac{|c|}{2}|y-x|$ for all $y\in[a,b]$ with $0<|y-x|<\delta$. Choose $k$ so large that $1/k<\delta$ and $1/k\le|c|/2$. Then for every such $y$ the reverse triangle inequality gives $|g(y)-g(x)|\ge\frac{|c|}{2}|y-x|\ge\frac{1}{k}|y-x|$. Consequently $Z\subseteq\bigcup_{k\ge1}Z_k$, where $Z_k$ is the set of $x\in Z$ such that $|g(y)-g(x)|\ge|x-y|/k$ for every $y\in(x-1/k,\,x+1/k)\cap[a,b]$. Fix $k$. By [F35] choose $M\ge1$ with $(b-a)/M<1/k$ and split $[a,b]$ into $M$ consecutive intervals $J$ of length $(b-a)/M<1/k$. It suffices to show $\lambda_1^*(Z_k\cap J)=0$ for one such $J$, since $Z_k$ is the union of the finitely many sets $Z_k\cap J$ and outer measure is subadditive [F32]. Let $\varepsilon>0$. Since $\lambda_1^*(N)=0$, the definition of outer measure [F31] provides countably many boxes, that is intervals $I_n\subseteq\mathbb R$, with $N\subseteq\bigcup_nI_n$ and $\sum_n\lambda_1(I_n)<\varepsilon$. Then $Z_k\cap J\subseteq\bigcup_ng^{-1}(I_n)$, so monotonicity and countable subadditivity [F32] give $$\lambda_1^*(Z_k\cap J)\le\sum_n\lambda_1^*\bigl(g^{-1}(I_n)\cap Z_k\cap J\bigr).$$ For each $n$, the set $E_n:=g^{-1}(I_n)\cap Z_k\cap J$ is contained in the interval $J$ of length below $1/k$, so any two points $u,v$ of $E_n$ satisfy $|u-v|<1/k$ and hence, by the defining property of $Z_k$, $|u-v|\le k|g(u)-g(v)|$; moreover $g(u),g(v)\in I_n$, so $|g(u)-g(v)|\le\lambda_1(I_n)$. If $E_n=\varnothing$, its outer measure is zero. Otherwise the pairwise estimate gives $\operatorname{diam}(E_n)\le k\lambda_1(I_n)$. For every $\delta>0$, the bounded set $E_n$ lies in an interval of length at most $\operatorname{diam}(E_n)+\delta$ (use its infimum and supremum), so $\lambda_1^*(E_n)\le\operatorname{diam}(E_n)\le k\lambda_1(I_n)$. Summing, $\lambda_1^*(Z_k\cap J)\le k\sum_n\lambda_1(I_n)<k\varepsilon$; since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, $\lambda_1^*(Z_k\cap J)=0$. Summing the finitely many $J$ gives $\lambda_1^*(Z_k)=0$, and countable subadditivity over $k$ gives $\lambda_1^*(Z)\le\sum_k\lambda_1^*(Z_k)=0$. This proves Claim A. [F28, F31, F32, F35, given]

2.1 We construct one measurable almost-everywhere version of $F'$ that vanishes off a controlled set. For $m\ge1$ and $t\in\mathbb R$ put $q_m(t):=m\bigl(F(t+1/m)-F(t)\bigr)$; each $q_m$ is continuous, hence Borel measurable by [F20], and $|q_m(t)|\le L$ for all $t$ by the Lipschitz bound of step 1.2. Let $\overline F:=\limsup_mq_m$ and $\underline F:=\liminf_mq_m$, extended-real-valued and measurable by [F30], and let $$C:=\{\,t\in\mathbb R : \overline F(t)=\underline F(t)\in\mathbb R\,\},\qquad f_0:=\overline F\cdot\mathbf 1_C ,$$ where $f_0$ is understood as $0$ outside $C$. The set $C$ is measurable by [F30], so $f_0$ is measurable, and $|f_0|\le L$ everywhere because on $C$ the value is $\lim_mq_m$, a limit of numbers bounded by $L$, and outside $C$ the value is $0$. If $F$ is differentiable at $t$, then $F'(t)$ is the limit of the difference quotients as the increment tends to $0$ [F28], so the sequence $q_m(t)=q_{F,t}(t+1/m)$ converges to $F'(t)$; the criterion [F29] then gives $\overline F(t)=\underline F(t)=F'(t)$ and hence $f_0(t)=F'(t)$. Thus $f_0=F'$ on $D_F$ pointwise, and $f_0=F'$ almost everywhere on $\mathbb R$ because $N_F$ is null by step 1.2. [F20, F28, F29, F30, step 1.2, given]

3.1 Fix one measurable representative $\hat u$ of the class $u$ and, for each $i$, one measurable representative $\hat v_i$ of the class $D_iu$; this is one representative plus finitely many others, so no infinite selection is made. By [F21] the function $f_0(\hat u)$ is measurable, and $|f_0(\hat u)|\le L$ everywhere by step 2.1, so [F2] and [F18] put $f_0(\hat u)$ in $L^\infty(\Omega;)$ with $\|f_0(\hat u)\|_\infty\le L$. Define the measurable function $h_i:=f_0(\hat u)\,\hat v_i$. For $1\le p<\infty$ the case $(p,q,r)=(\infty,p,p)$ of [F16] gives $h_i\in\mathcal L^p(\Omega)$ with $\|h_i\|_p\le L\|\hat v_i\|_p<\infty$, and for $p=\infty$ its $(\infty,\infty,\infty)$ case gives $h_i\in L^\infty(\Omega)$; in both cases [F3] and [F4] make $h_i$ an element of the quotient space $L^p(\Omega)$. If $\hat u'$ and $\hat v_i'$ are further measurable representatives of the same two classes, then $\hat u'=\hat u$ and $\hat v_i'=\hat v_i$ almost everywhere, hence $f_0(\hat u')\hat v_i'=f_0(\hat u)\hat v_i$ almost everywhere, and [F4] shows that the same class is obtained; we write $f_0(u)D_iu$ for it. [F2, F3, F4, F16, F18, F21, step 2.1, given]

4.1 Fix one coordinate direction $i$ and a rational box $Q=\prod_{j=1}^n(a_j,b_j)$ with $\overline Q\subseteq\Omega$, as in [F6], and write $Q=Q_{\widehat i}\times I_{i,Q}$. By [F14] $Q$ has finite measure, and the restriction $u|_Q$ of the class $u$ lies in $L^p(Q)$. Put $q:=p$ when $p<\infty$ and $q:=1$ when $p=\infty$. Then $u|_Q$ lies in $L^q(Q)$: for finite $p$ this is $q=p$, and for $p=\infty$ the class $u|_Q\in L^\infty(Q)$ is converted into $\mathcal L^1(Q)$ by the second clause of [F17]; and by [F8] each weak derivative restricts, $D_j(u|_Q)=(D_ju)|_Q$ on $Q$, with $(D_ju)|_Q\in L^q(Q)$ by the same two clauses. By [F1] this says $u|_Q\in W^{1,q}(Q;\mathbb R)$, and since $1\le q<\infty$ the ACL characterisation [F5] applies on the open box $Q$: it provides one measurable representative $u_Q^*$ of the class $u|_Q$, ACL in every direction, whose classical coordinate derivatives $\partial_ju_Q^*$ exist almost everywhere, are measurable, lie in $\mathcal L^q(Q)$, and represent $D_j(u|_Q)$ almost everywhere for every $j$. The assignment of one representative $u_Q^*$ to each rational box $Q$ is a selection from countably many nonempty sets, licensed by the Countable Choice of step 1.1. 5.1 **Level sets on a box.** Fix the rational box $Q$ and the ACL representative $u_Q^*$ of step 4.1, and recall the one-dimensional input Claim A of step 1.3. Let $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ be a Borel set with $\lambda_1(N)=0$. We claim that $w:=u_Q^*$ vanishes, as a classical partial derivative, almost everywhere on the measurable set $E_N:=\{x\in Q : u_Q^*(x)\in N\}$. Extend the measurable function $\partial_iu_Q^*$, initially defined almost everywhere on $Q$, by the value $0$ on the null set where it does not exist, and keep the notation $\partial_iu_Q^*$ for the extension; this changes the function only on a null set. Then the set $Z:=\{x\in Q : u_Q^*(x)\in N$ and $\partial_iu_Q^*(x)\neq0\}$ is measurable, because $u_Q^*$, the extension, and $N$ are measurable. By the completed-product convention and Tonelli [F34] applied to the indicator of $Z$, $$\lambda_n(Z)=\int_{Q_{\widehat i}}\lambda_1(Z_y)\,d\lambda_{n-1}(y),$$ where $Z_y=\{t\in I_{i,Q} : u_Q^*(\iota_i(y,t))\in N$ and $\partial_iu_Q^*(\iota_i(y,t))\neq0\}$ is the section. For every $y$ outside the exceptional set of the ACL definition [F6] the section $g_y(t):=u_Q^*(\iota_i(y,t))$ is absolutely continuous on every compact subinterval of $I_{i,Q}$, hence differentiable almost everywhere there by [F12]. For such $y$ and every compact subinterval $[a,b]\subseteq I_{i,Q}$, Claim A of step 1.3 applies to $g_y|_{[a,b]}$ and the null set $N$: the set of $t\in[a,b]$ with $g_y(t)\in N$ and $g_y$ differentiable at $t$ with $g_y'(t)\neq0$ has outer measure zero; and the set where $g_y'$ fails to exist is null by [F12], while $g_y'$ equals the partial derivative $\partial_iu_Q^*$ at every point of differentiability, by the definition of the partial derivative [F28] (the extension agrees with the classical partial derivative wherever the latter exists). Hence $\lambda_1(Z_y\cap[a,b])=0$. Exhausting $I_{i,Q}$ by countably many compact subintervals, countable subadditivity [F32] gives $\lambda_1(Z_y)=0$ for every such $y$; since the exceptional $y$-set is null, the integral above is $0$ and $\lambda_n(Z)=0$. Restoring the modification on the null set where the extension differs from the true partial derivative gives the claim: $\partial_iu_Q^*=0$ almost everywhere on $\{x\in Q : u_Q^*(x)\in N\}$, and consequently the weak derivative satisfies $D_iu=0$ almost everywhere on that set, by the representative comparison of step 4.1. [F6, F12, F28, F32, F34, step 1.3, step 4.1, given]

5.2 We verify the membership and representative clauses for the class $F\circ u|_Q$ on $Q$, whose exponent $q$ satisfies $1\le q<\infty$. First $F\circ u|_Q\in L^q(Q)$: by step 1.2 $|F(s)|\le|F(0)|+L|s|$ for every real $s$, so $|F(u)|\le|F(0)|+L|u|$ pointwise; the bound function lies in $\mathcal L^q(Q)$ because the constant belongs to $\mathcal L^q(Q)$ by the second clause of [F17] applied to the finite-measure box of [F14], the multiple $L|u|$ belongs to $\mathcal L^q(Q)$ by [F19], and sums of $\mathcal L^q$ functions belong to $\mathcal L^q(Q)$ by [F19]; monotonicity [F24] of the nonnegative integral then gives $\int_Q|F(u)|^q<\infty$, so [F3] and [F4] make $F\circ u|_Q$ an element of $L^q(Q)$. Second, $\Phi:=F\circ u_Q^*$ is a measurable representative of that class: it is measurable by [F21] applied to the Borel function $F$, which is continuous and hence Borel by [F20], and to the measurable $u_Q^*$ of step 4.1; and it agrees with $F\circ u$ almost everywhere on $Q$ because $u_Q^*=u$ almost everywhere there. Third, $\Phi$ is ACL: by [F6] the sections of $u_Q^*$ in direction $i$ are absolutely continuous in the sense of [F7] on every compact subinterval of $I_{i,Q}$, for every transverse parameter outside a null set depending on the direction, and $F$ is Lipschitz on the whole real line with constant $L$ by [F10], so [F9] makes each composite section $F\circ g_y$ absolutely continuous on every compact subinterval; the same null exceptional sets therefore serve for $\Phi$. 6.1 The classical derivative of $\Phi$ in direction $i$ exists almost everywhere on $Q$ and equals $G:=f_0(u_Q^*)\,\partial_iu_Q^*$ almost everywhere. Indeed, fix $y$ outside the exceptional set of [F6] and a compact subinterval $[\alpha,\beta]\subseteq I_{i,Q}$; the section $g:=g_y$ is absolutely continuous on $[\alpha,\beta]$ by [F7], its image $g([\alpha,\beta])=[c,d]$ is a compact interval, and by step 1.2 the restriction of $F$ to $[c,d]$ satisfies $F(x)=F(c)+\int_c^xf_0$ for all $x\in[c,d]$, so that $F|_{[c,d]}-F(c)=I_{f_0}$ for the function $f_0|_{[c,d]}$, which is a representative of the $L^1$ class of $(F|_{[c,d]})'$; the composite $F\circ g$ is absolutely continuous by [F9], so $(F-F(c))\circ g$ is absolutely continuous as well. Apply [F13] to this normalized indefinite integral to obtain $((F-F(c))\circ g)'=f_0(g)\,g'$ almost everywhere on $[\alpha,\beta]$, with $f_0(g)g'\in L^1[\alpha,\beta]$; since the derivative of a constant is zero, $(F\circ g)'=f_0(g)g'$ there; this holds for every compact subinterval, so $(F\circ g_y)'=f_0(g_y)g_y'$ almost everywhere on $I_{i,Q}$. At every point where the partial derivative $\partial_iu_Q^*$ exists, $g_y$ is differentiable there with $g_y'=\partial_iu_Q^*$, by the definition of the partial derivative [F28]. Since $\partial_iu_Q^*$ exists almost everywhere on $Q$ by step 4.1, and $(F\circ g_y)'$ exists almost everywhere on $I_{i,Q}$ for the good $y$, the partial derivative $\partial_i\Phi$ exists almost everywhere on $Q$ and agrees there with $G$: the set where either the partial derivative fails to exist or differs from $G$ is contained in the union of the null set of bad $y$ and, for each good $y$, a null set of $t$, which is null for the product measure by the completed-product convention and Tonelli [F34]. The function $G$ is measurable by [F21], since $f_0$ is measurable by step 2.1 and $u_Q^*$, $\partial_iu_Q^*$ are measurable, and $G$ lies in $\mathcal L^q(Q)$: $|f_0(u_Q^*)|\le L$ everywhere by step 2.1, so $f_0(u_Q^*)\in L^\infty(Q)$ by [F2] and [F18], and with $\partial_iu_Q^*\in\mathcal L^q(Q)$ from step 4.1 the case $(p,q,r)=(\infty,q,q)$ of [F16] gives $G\in\mathcal L^q(Q)$. All clauses of the characterisation [F5] hold for the class $F\circ u|_Q$ with representative $\Phi$: it lies in $L^q(Q)$ by step 5.2, $\Phi=F\circ u_Q^*$ is a measurable ACL representative, and its classical coordinate derivative in direction $i$ exists almost everywhere, is measurable and lies in $\mathcal L^q(Q)$. Hence $F\circ u|_Q\in W^{1,q}(Q;\mathbb R)$ and $D_i(F\circ u|_Q)$ is represented almost everywhere by $G$; by the comparison of step 4.1, $G=f_0(u_Q^*)\partial_iu_Q^*=h_i$ almost everywhere on $Q$, so $D_i(F\circ u|_Q)$ is represented almost everywhere by $h_i$. The same argument applies to every coordinate direction simultaneously, because the single representative $u_Q^*$ of step 4.1 is ACL in every direction and its classical coordinate derivatives represent all $D_j(u|_Q)$. 6.2 The level-set clause. Let first $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ be Borel with $\lambda_1(N)=0$. For every rational box $Q$, step 5.1 shows that $\partial_iu_Q^*=0$ almost everywhere on $\{x\in Q : u_Q^*(x)\in N\}$, while $u_Q^*=\hat u$ and $\partial_iu_Q^*=\hat v_i$ almost everywhere on $Q$ by step 4.1; hence the set $\{x\in Q : \hat v_i(x)\neq0$ and $\hat u(x)\in N\}$ is null. The rational boxes cover $\Omega$ and there are countably many of them, so countable subadditivity of Lebesgue measure (via [F32]) makes $Z_{i,N}=\{x\in\Omega : \hat v_i(x)\neq0$ and $\hat u(x)\in N\}$ a null set. Now let $N\subseteq\mathbb R$ be an arbitrary set with $\lambda_1^*(N)=0$. By outer regularity [F33], for every $m$ there is an open $G_m\supseteq N$ with $\lambda_1(G_m)\le1/m$; the intersection $N_0:=\bigcap_mG_m$ is a Borel set containing $N$, and it is null because it is contained in each $G_m$ and outer measure is monotone [F32]. Then $Z_{i,N}\subseteq Z_{i,N_0}$, which is null by the Borel case, so $\lambda_n^*(Z_{i,N})=0$ by monotonicity [F32]. This proves clause 2 in full; the case $N=N_F$ is available because $N_F$ is null by step 1.2. [F32, F33, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, given]

7.1 When $p=\infty$ the exponent used so far is $q=1$, and we upgrade the conclusion of step 6.1 to $W^{1,\infty}(Q)$. Each weak derivative class $D_j(F\circ u|_Q)$ lies in $L^\infty(Q)$: by step 6.1 it is represented by $f_0(u_Q^*)\partial_ju_Q^*$, whose absolute value is at most $L|\partial_ju_Q^*|\le L\|\partial_ju_Q^*\|_\infty$ almost everywhere by [F18] and the bound $|f_0|\le L$ of step 2.1, so the representative lies in $L^\infty(Q)$ by [F2]. 8.1 We patch the box conclusions into a global weak derivative identity. For the fixed direction $i$ and any test function $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, cover $\Omega$ by the rational boxes of [F6] and use [F22] to choose a locally finite smooth partition of unity $(\chi_k)$ subordinate to that cover, with compact supports. Only finitely many $\chi_k$ meet the compact support of $\varphi$, and $\varphi=\sum_k\chi_k\varphi$; each summand $\chi_k\varphi$ has compact support contained in some box $Q_k$ with $\overline{Q_k}\subseteq\Omega$, so it is a test function on $Q_k$. Step 6.1 gives the finite-exponent weak identity and step 7.1 supplies the $p=\infty$ case; together they give, for every $\psi\in C_c^\infty(Q_k)$, $\int_{Q_k}F(u)\,\partial_i\psi=-\int_{Q_k}h_i\psi$; applying this to the finitely many summands and summing with the linearity of the integral [F23] gives $\int_\Omega F(u)\,\partial_i\varphi=-\int_\Omega h_i\,\varphi$. Both sides are integrable: $u\in L^p(\Omega)$ is locally integrable, $F\circ u\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ because $|F(u)|\le|F(0)|+L|u|$ by step 1.2 with a constant function on finite-measure pieces ([F14] and [F19]), and $h_i\in L^p_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ — for finite $p$ because $[h_i]\in L^p(\Omega)$ by step 3.1 and for $p=\infty$ because $|h_i|\le L\|\hat v_i\|_\infty$ almost everywhere by [F18]. Since $\varphi$ was arbitrary and the argument applies to every direction, the definition [F1] gives $D_i(F\circ u)=[h_i]=f_0(u)D_iu$ weakly on $\Omega$ for every $i$, hence almost everywhere on $\Omega$. 9.1 We record the local membership. Let $U\subseteq\Omega$ be open with $\overline U$ compact in $\Omega$; then $U$ is bounded, so $\lambda_n(U)<\infty$ by [F15]. 9.2 The global membership criterion. If $F\circ u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)$, then $F\circ u\in L^p(\Omega)$ by the definition [F1], and $F\circ u$ is the class of $F(u)$. 11.1 The automatic cases and the closing discussion. If $F(0)=0$, then $|F(t)|=|F(t)-F(0)|\le L|t|$ for every $t$ by step 1.2, so $|F(u)|\le L|u|$ pointwise and $\int_\Omega|F(u)|^p\le L^p\int_\Omega|u|^p<\infty$ by monotonicity [F24] when $p<\infty$, while $\|F(u)\|_\infty\le L\|u\|_\infty$ by [F18] when $p=\infty$; in both cases $F(u)\in L^p(\Omega)$ by [F2], [F3] and [F4], and clause 1 follows from step 9.2. If $p<\infty$ and $\lambda_n(\Omega)<\infty$, so that the restricted Lebesgue measure is finite in the sense of [F25], the constant $|F(0)|$ lies in $L^\infty(\Omega)$ and hence in $\mathcal L^p(\Omega)$ by the second clause of [F17], while $L|u|\in\mathcal L^p(\Omega)$ by [F19]; the sum $|F(0)|+L|u|\in\mathcal L^p(\Omega)$ by [F19] dominates $|F(u)|$ pointwise by step 1.2, so $\int_\Omega|F(u)|^p<\infty$ by [F24] and $F(u)\in L^p(\Omega)$, and clause 1 follows from step 9.2. If $p=\infty$, then $|F(u)|\le|F(0)|+L\|u\|_\infty$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$ by [F18], so $F(u)\in L^\infty(\Omega)$ by [F2] and clause 1 follows from step 9.2. If $L=0$, then $F$ is the constant $F(0)$, so $F$ is differentiable everywhere with $F'=0$, $N_F=\varnothing$, and $f_0=0$; clauses 1 to 3 hold with $\Phi_i=0$ and $D_i(F\circ u)=0$, consistently with step 9.1. The case $n=1$ is included: the ACL definition [F6] then reads that one representative is absolutely continuous on every compact subinterval of the interval $\Omega$, and no transverse parameter occurs. If $\Omega=\varnothing$, the rational-box family of [F6] is empty, the only class is zero, the representative $\hat u$ may be taken to be $0$, and every displayed assertion holds vacuously with $Z_{i,N}=\varnothing$.
