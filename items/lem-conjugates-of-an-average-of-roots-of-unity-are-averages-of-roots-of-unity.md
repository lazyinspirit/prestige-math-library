## Statement

Let $n>0$, let $z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathbb C$ be roots of unity, and put $a=n^{-1}\sum_i z_i$. Then $a$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$. There is a finite cyclotomic splitting field $E\subset\mathbb C$ containing all the $z_i$ such that for every complex $\mathbb Q$-conjugate $b$ of $a$, some $\mathbb Q$-automorphism $\sigma:E\to E$ satisfies $b=\sigma(a)=n^{-1}\sum_i\sigma(z_i)$. Each $\sigma(z_i)$ has the same multiplicative order as $z_i$. No integrality hypothesis on $a$ is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n>0$, each $z_i$ a complex root of unity, and $a=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n z_i$.

[F1] A root of unity satisfies a positive power equation $z^m=1$; its order is the least positive such exponent ([[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]]).

[F2] An isomorphism of base fields extends to an isomorphism between splitting fields of a nonzero polynomial and its transported polynomial ([[thm-isomorphisms-extend-to-splitting-fields]]).

[F3] Conjugates over $\mathbb Q$ are roots of the same minimal polynomial ([[def-conjugate-elements-over-a-field]]).

[F4] A cyclotomic extension of order $N$ is the splitting field of $t^N-1$, generated by its roots ([[def-cyclotomic-extension]]).

[F5] Every nonconstant complex polynomial splits over $\mathbb C$ ([[cor-every-complex-polynomial-splits-into-linear-factors]]).

[F6] A field obtained by adjoining finitely many algebraic elements has finite degree ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

[F7] Every element of a finite field extension is algebraic ([[thm-finite-field-extensions-are-algebraic]]).

[F8] An algebraic splitting field of a nonzero polynomial is normal ([[prop-algebraic-splitting-extensions-are-normal]]).

[F9] If two elements are roots of corresponding monic irreducible polynomials, a base-field isomorphism extends to their simple fields sending one root to the other ([[lem-an-isomorphism-extends-across-a-simple-root-adjunction]]).

[F10] Finite sums use repeated addition with empty sum zero ([[def-finite-sum-in-a-commutative-monoid]]).

[F11] Base and successor steps prove a statement for every finite length ([[thm-induction-principle]]).

[F12] Normality says each element’s minimal polynomial over the base field splits in the extension ([[def-normal-algebraic-extension]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $m_i$ be the positive order of $z_i$, and set $N=m_1\cdots m_n>0$. Each $m_i$ divides $N$, hence $z_i^N=(z_i^{m_i})^{N/m_i}=1$. The nonconstant polynomial $q(t)=t^N-1$ splits in $\mathbb C$ by F5. Let $R$ be its set of roots there and $E=\mathbb Q(R)\subset\mathbb C$. The linear factorization has $N$ factors, so $R$ is finite. The polynomial splits over $E$, and $E$ is generated by these roots, so it is the cyclotomic splitting field of F4. All $z_i$ belong to $E$. [F1, F5, F4, algebra]

2.1 Each member of $R$ is algebraic over $\mathbb Q$, being a root of $q\in\mathbb Q[t]\setminus\{0\}$. F6 makes $E/\mathbb Q$ finite, and F7 makes it algebraic. As $1/n\in\mathbb Q$, repeated addition and multiplication in $E$ show that $a\in E$; F10 gives the same finite sum in $E$ and $\mathbb C$. In particular $a$ is algebraic by F7. [step 1.1, F6, F7, F10]

3.1 F8 applies to the algebraic splitting field $E/\mathbb Q$ of the nonzero polynomial $q$, giving normality. Let $p$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $a$ over $\mathbb Q$. By F12, $p$ splits over $E$. If $b\in\mathbb C$ is a conjugate of $a$, then $p(b)=0$ by F3. Write $p(t)=\prod_j(t-r_j)$ with $r_j\in E$. The field $\mathbb C$ has no zero divisors, so $\prod_j(b-r_j)=0$ forces $b=r_j$ for some $j$; thus $b\in E$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F8, F12, F3]

4.1 Apply F9 to the identity isomorphism of $\mathbb Q$ and the monic irreducible polynomial $p$, with roots $a$ and $b$. It gives a $\mathbb Q$-isomorphism $\tau:\mathbb Q(a)\to\mathbb Q(b)$ satisfying $\tau(a)=b$. Both fields lie in $E$. Since $\mathbb Q\subseteq\mathbb Q(a)\subseteq E$, one has $\mathbb Q(a)(R)=E$; the two inclusions follow respectively because $E$ contains $a,R$ and because $\mathbb Q(R)=E$. The same argument gives $\mathbb Q(b)(R)=E$. Thus $E$ is a splitting field of $q$ over each of these base fields. [F9, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The coefficients of $q$ are rational and $\tau$ fixes them, so $\tau_*q=q\ne0$. Apply F2 to the two splitting-field structures from step 4.1. It extends $\tau$ to a field isomorphism $\sigma:E\to E$. This is a $\mathbb Q$-automorphism and $\sigma(a)=b$. [F2, step 4.1]

6.1 A field homomorphism preserves finite sums: it sends the empty sum to $0$, and $\sigma(A+z)=\sigma(A)+\sigma(z)$ preserves the assertion when a term is appended; F11 proves this for every length in F10. Since $\sigma(1/n)=1/n$, it follows that $b=\sigma(a)=n^{-1}\sum_i\sigma(z_i)$. [step 5.1, F10, F11, algebra]

7.1 Multiplicativity gives $\sigma(z_i)^{m_i}=\sigma(z_i^{m_i})=1$. If $\sigma(z_i)^d=1$ for a positive $d<m_i$, applying $\sigma^{-1}$ gives $z_i^d=1$, contrary to the definition of $m_i$ in F1. Thus the order is exactly $m_i$. This covers $m_i=1$ and repeated roots. When $n=1$, the construction still applies with $a=z_1$. When $a=0$, its minimal polynomial is $t$, so $b=0$; steps 4.1–6.1 still apply. No step imposed integrality on $a$. [F1, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, F3] ∎
