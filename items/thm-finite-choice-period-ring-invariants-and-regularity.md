## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_{\omega,\mathrm{fin}}$. Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be a GIVEN valued algebraic closure with ordinary completion $C$ and a GIVEN compatible primitive cyclotomic system. For each finite $K\subset F$ with maximal unramified subfield $K_0$, the period rings satisfy $$B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{G_K}=K,\qquad B_{\mathrm{cris}}^{G_K}=B_{\mathrm{st}}^{G_K}=K_0.$$ Moreover each of $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$, $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and $B_{\mathrm{st}}$ is $(\mathbb Q_p,G_K)$-regular. This does not assert the choice-free parent claim.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable choice for finite sets, the stated valued closure, completion, primitive cyclotomic system and finite field $K$.

[A1] Countable choice for nonempty finite sets holds. [[def-choice-for-pairs-and-countable-finite-choice]]

[F1] Under [A1], restrictions to finite Galois subextensions are surjective. [[lem-countable-finite-choice-padic-galois-lifting-and-period-comparison]]

[F2] Given finite Galois lifting, the de Rham invariants are $K$, while the crystalline and semistable ring and fraction-field invariants are all $K_0$. [[lem-choice-free-period-invariants-from-finite-galois-lifting]]

[F3] Under [A1], every nonzero stable crystalline or semistable line is generated by a unit. [[lem-finite-choice-period-stable-lines-are-units]]

[F4] The de Rham ring is the fraction field of its de Rham plus DVR. [[lem-choice-free-de-rham-period-completion-is-a-discrete-valuation-ring]]

[F5] Regularity requires invariant ring a field, invariants of the fraction field equal to it, and units on all nonzero stable lines. [[def-regular-period-ring]]

## Proof

1.1 By [A1] and [F1], the finite-lifting hypothesis of [F2] holds. Therefore $B_{\mathrm{dR}}^{G_K}=K$ and the crystalline and semistable invariants, both for the rings and their fraction fields, are $K_0$. These invariant rings are fields. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F3], every nonzero stable line in the crystalline or semistable ring has a unit generator. The fraction-field equalities in step 1.1 and the regularity definition [F5] therefore establish regularity of both rings. By [F4], $B_{\mathrm{dR}}$ is a field, so its fraction field is itself and each nonzero element is a unit. Its invariant field is $K$ by step 1.1, so it is regular as well. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
