## Statement

Let $(U,y)$ be a finite population and let $p_d$ be a sampling design as in
[[def-finite-population-and-sampling-design]]. Suppose $\pi_i>0$ for every
$i\in U$. Give the real target space its Borel sigma-algebra. For an observed
record $(s,z)\in\mathcal X_d$, define
$$\widehat T_{HT}(s,z):=\sum_{i\in s}\frac{z_i}{\pi_i},\qquad \widehat{\bar y}_{HT}(s,z):=\frac{\widehat T_{HT}(s,z)}{N}.$$
Under the induced observed-data law $P_y^d$, these are estimators of the
finite-population total and mean, respectively, and
$$E_y^d[\widehat T_{HT}]=T(y),\qquad E_y^d[\widehat{\bar y}_{HT}]=\bar y_U.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite nonempty unit set $U$, fixed values $y_i\in\mathbb R$,
a finite sampling design $p_d$, and $\pi_i>0$ for every $i\in U$.

[F1] [[def-finite-population-and-sampling-design]]: The design has support
$\mathcal S_d$, weights $p_d$, inclusion indicators $I_i(s)$, probabilities
$\pi_i$, total $T(y)$, mean $\bar y_U$, and observed record $O_y(s)$.

[F2] [[lem-finite-population-design-law-is-a-probability-measure]]: The
induced $P_y^d$ is a probability measure on the common observed-record space.

[F3] [[def-statistic-and-estimator]]: A statistic is a measurable map from the
experiment's observation space; it is an estimator when its target is the
specified estimand.

[F4] [[def-indicator-random-variable]]: For an event $A$, its indicator is
$1$ on $A$ and $0$ outside $A$.

[F5] [[lem-indicator-expectation-and-products]]: On a finite probability
space, $E[\mathbf1_A]=P(A)$ and products of event indicators are indicators
of intersections.

[F6] [[def-expectation-on-a-finite-probability-space]]: Expectation is the
finite weighted sum over design outcomes.

[F7] [[thm-linearity-of-expectation]]: Expectation is linear for every finite
family of real random variables on one finite probability space, without an
independence assumption.

[F8] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: The Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb R$ is
the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets.

[F9] [[def-unbiased-estimator-and-umvu]]: An estimator is unbiased when it is
integrable under every model law and its expectation equals the estimand at
every parameter.

## Proof

1.1 For every $i$, $\pi_i>0$ makes the displayed denominator nonzero, so the formula defines a real-valued map on $\mathcal X_d$. Its domain has the full power-set sigma-algebra, so the preimage of every Borel set is measurable by [F8]; its target is the total estimand from [F1]. Thus [F2] and [F3] make $\widehat T_{HT}$ an estimator. For each fixed $y$, it has finite support and finite real values, hence is integrable under $P_y^d$; division by the positive integer $N$ gives the same properties for $\widehat{\bar y}_{HT}$. Thus both satisfy the integrability clause in [F9]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F8, F9, algebra]

1.2 For each $i\in U$, let $A_i=\{s\in\mathcal S_d:i\in s\}$. Then $I_i=\mathbf1_{A_i}$ by [F1] and [F4], so [F5] and [F6] give $E_d[I_i]=P_d(A_i)=\pi_i$. [F1, F4, F5, F6]

2.1 On a sample $s$, the observed values satisfy $z_i=y_i$ for $i\in s$; hence $\widehat T_{HT}(O_y(s))=\sum_{i\in U}I_i(s)y_i/\pi_i$. Applying [F7] to this finite sum and using step 1.2 gives $E_y^d[\widehat T_{HT}]=\sum_{i\in U}\frac{y_i}{\pi_i}E_d[I_i]=\sum_{i\in U}y_i=T(y)$. Together with the integrability in step 1.1, this is unbiasedness by [F9]. No independence identity is used. [F1, F6, F7, F9, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $N\ge1$ is fixed, linearity of the finite weighted expectation gives $E_y^d[\widehat{\bar y}_{HT}]=E_y^d[\widehat T_{HT}]/N=T(y)/N=\bar y_U$ by step 2.1. Together with step 1.1, [F9] makes this the required unbiasedness claim for the mean estimator as well. [given, F6, F7, F9, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
