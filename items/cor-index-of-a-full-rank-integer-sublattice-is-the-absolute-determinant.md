## Statement

Let $n\ge1$.

1. Let $A\in M_n(\mathbb Z)$ and let $L=A\mathbb Z^n\subseteq\mathbb Z^n$ be the subgroup generated by the columns of $A$. If $\det A\ne0$, then the quotient group $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is finite of order $\lvert\det A\rvert$; if $\det A=0$, then $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is infinite.
2. Every subgroup $L\subseteq\mathbb Z^n$ is $A\mathbb Z^n$ for some $A\in M_n(\mathbb Z)$.

So a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ has finite index exactly when it is generated by the columns of a square integer matrix of nonzero determinant, and its index is then the absolute determinant of every such matrix.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n\ge1$, and for clause 1 a matrix $A\in M_n(\mathbb Z)$ with $L=A\mathbb Z^n$.

[F1] Abelian groups and $\mathbb Z$-modules have the same objects and morphisms, so a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule and the quotient group is the quotient module ([[prop-abelian-groups-are-z-modules]]). The ring $\mathbb Z$ is a commutative ring with identity in which a product of nonzero elements is nonzero, so it is an integral domain, and every subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$ is cyclic, so every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is principal: $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain ([[thm-int-comm-ring]], [[lem-int-cancellation]], [[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]], [[def-principal-ideal-domain]]).

[F2] For every $A\in M_{m\times n}(R)$ over a PID there are invertible $P,Q$ with $PAQ=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0)$ and $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_r$, every $d_i\ne0$; equivalence means $B=PAQ$ with $P\in\operatorname{GL}_m(R)$ and $Q\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ ([[thm-smith-normal-form-existence-over-a-pid]], [[def-matrix-equivalence-and-smith-normal-form-over-a-pid]]).

[F3] Over a commutative ring $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$, a square matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is a unit, the determinant of a triangular matrix is the product of its diagonal entries, and the units of $\mathbb Z$ are $1$ and $-1$ ([[thm-determinant-multiplicative]], [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]], [[thm-determinant-of-a-triangular-matrix]], [[lem-units-of-z]]).

[F4] For every module homomorphism $f:M\to N$ there is an isomorphism $M/\ker f\cong\operatorname{im}f$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-modules]]).

[F5] For a submodule of a free module of finite rank $n$ over a PID there is a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of the ambient module and nonzero elements $a_1\mid\cdots\mid a_r$ with $r\le n$ such that $a_1e_1,\ldots,a_re_r$ is a basis of the submodule ([[thm-simultaneous-basis-theorem-for-pid-submodules]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] the quotient $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is a quotient of $\mathbb Z$-modules, and by [F2] applied over the PID $\mathbb Z$ there are $P,Q\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb Z)$ with $PAQ=D=\operatorname{diag}(d_1,\ldots,d_r,0,\ldots,0)$, every $d_i\ne0$. [given, F1, F2]

1.2 By [F3], $\det P$ and $\det Q$ are units of $\mathbb Z$, hence $\pm1$, so $\lvert\det D\rvert=\lvert\det P\rvert\lvert\det A\rvert\lvert\det Q\rvert=\lvert\det A\rvert$; and $D$ is diagonal, so $\det D=d_1\cdots d_r\cdot0^{\,n-r}$, which is $d_1\cdots d_n$ when $r=n$ and $0$ when $r<n$. [given, F3, algebra]

2.1 Since $Q$ is invertible over $\mathbb Z$, $Q\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z^n$, so $A\mathbb Z^n=AQ\mathbb Z^n=P^{-1}D\mathbb Z^n$. The map $x\mapsto Px$ is an automorphism of $\mathbb Z^n$ carrying $L=P^{-1}D\mathbb Z^n$ onto $D\mathbb Z^n$, so it induces an isomorphism $\mathbb Z^n/L\cong\mathbb Z^n/D\mathbb Z^n$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Write $d_i=0$ for $r<i\le n$, and let $\pi:\mathbb Z^n\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^n\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z$ send $x$ to the tuple of residues $x_i+d_i\mathbb Z$, a surjective homomorphism whose kernel is $D\mathbb Z^n$. By [F4], $\mathbb Z^n/D\mathbb Z^n\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^n\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z$, and with step 2.1 this group is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^n/L$. [step 2.1, F4, algebra]

4.1 If $\det A\ne0$ then $\det D\ne0$ by step 1.2, so $r=n$ and every $d_i$ is nonzero; each $\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z$ is then finite of order $\lvert d_i\rvert$, so by step 3.1 the quotient $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is finite of order $\lvert d_1\cdots d_n\rvert=\lvert\det D\rvert=\lvert\det A\rvert$. [step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 If $\det A=0$ then $\det D=0$ by step 1.2, so $r<n$ and $d_n=0$; the summand $\mathbb Z/d_n\mathbb Z=\mathbb Z$ is infinite, so by step 3.1 the quotient $\mathbb Z^n/L$ is infinite. This proves clause 1. [step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For clause 2, let $L$ be any subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$. By [F1] it is a $\mathbb Z$-submodule of the free module $\mathbb Z^n$ of rank $n$ over the PID $\mathbb Z$, so [F5] gives a basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $\mathbb Z^n$ and nonzero $a_1\mid\cdots\mid a_r$, $r\le n$, with $a_1e_1,\ldots,a_re_r$ a basis of $L$. Let $A$ be the matrix whose first $r$ columns are $a_1e_1,\ldots,a_re_r$ and whose remaining $n-r$ columns are zero; its entries are integers because the $e_i$ and the $a_i$ are. Then $A\mathbb Z^n$ is the set of integer combinations of those columns, which is exactly $L$, so clause 2 holds; combining it with clause 1 gives the final sentence of the Statement. [given, F1, F5, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct] ∎

## Remark

The corollary is what makes the determinant a counting invariant: clause 2 says every subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ is a column lattice, and for $A$ with nonzero determinant the columns of $A$ generate a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^n$ of index exactly $\lvert\det A\rvert$, and the Smith invariant factors $d_1\mid\cdots\mid d_n$ refine that single number into the isomorphism type $\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z/d_i\mathbb Z$ of the quotient. Nothing here needs a Euclidean volume: the count comes from the invariant factors, and the determinant enters only because it is unchanged up to sign by multiplication with matrices invertible over $\mathbb Z$.
