## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, let $\lambda$ be dominant integral, and let
$M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)/I_\lambda$ be the cyclic module with
canonical generator $v_\lambda$ of
[[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]]. Then
$$v_\lambda\ne0 ,$$
$v_\lambda$ is a weight vector of weight $\lambda$, and
$\mathfrak n^+\cdot v_\lambda=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system, a dominant integral $\lambda$ with $m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$, lowering vectors $f_i\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i}$ and the module $M=M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ with generator $v_\lambda$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory supplying [L1], [L2] and [L4] and through the Serre theorem [L5] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The chosen positive system gives the direct sum $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak n^-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^+$, and the PBW monomials in an ordered basis of $\mathfrak g$ listing a basis of $\mathfrak n^-$ first, then one of $\mathfrak h$, then one of $\mathfrak n^+$, form a basis of $U(\mathfrak g)$; in particular $U(\mathfrak g)=U(\mathfrak n^-)U(\mathfrak h)U(\mathfrak n^+)$ as a linear span and $U(\mathfrak n^-)$ is spanned by monomials in a basis of $\mathfrak n^-$ ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L2] In the universal enveloping algebra, the product $u_1u_2\cdots u_k$ of elements of $\mathfrak g$ has the bracket rule $XY-YX=[X,Y]$; in particular for the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-triple $(e_i,f_i,h_{\alpha_i})$ one has $e_if_i-f_ie_i=h_{\alpha_i}$, $h_{\alpha_i}f_i-f_ih_{\alpha_i}=-2f_i$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]], [[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]]).

[L3] The modules $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ are defined by the relations $x\cdot v_\lambda=0$ for $x\in\mathfrak n^+$, $H\cdot v_\lambda=\lambda(H)v_\lambda$ for $H\in\mathfrak h$, and $f_i^{\,m_i+1}\cdot v_\lambda=0$ ([[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]]).

[L4] Any module generated by a highest weight vector $w$ of weight $\mu$ satisfies $U(\mathfrak g)w=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cw$ and has all weights $\le\mu$, and it has $w$ as the only vector of weight $\mu$ up to scalars ([[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]], [[def-partial-order-on-weights]]).

[L5] For $i\ne j$ the simple-root vectors satisfy $[e_j,f_i]=0$, and $[e_i,f_i]=h_{\alpha_i}$, in the Serre presentation of $\mathfrak g$ by the triples $(e_i,f_i,h_{\alpha_i})$ ([[thm-serre-presentation-theorem]]).

[L6] The elements $f_i^{\,m_i+1}$ are nonzero, and the positive roots are exactly the nonzero nonnegative integral combinations of the simple roots, which are linearly independent ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $J\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)$ be the left ideal generated by $\mathfrak n^+$ and the elements $H-\lambda(H)$ with $H\in\mathfrak h$, and let $N=U(\mathfrak g)/J$ with $v_0=1+J$; then $\mathfrak n^+\cdot v_0=0$ and $H\cdot v_0=\lambda(H)v_0$ for $H\in\mathfrak h$, and by [L1] every element of $N$ is a linear combination of vectors $u\cdot v_0$ with $u\in U(\mathfrak n^-)$, so the linear map $\varphi:U(\mathfrak n^-)\to N$, $\varphi(u)=u\cdot v_0$, is onto. [A1, L1, L3]

2.1 The map $\varphi$ is also injective.  Let $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^+$.  The linear functional $\chi_\lambda:\mathfrak b\to\mathbb C$ defined by $\chi_\lambda(H+x)=\lambda(H)$ is a Lie-algebra homomorphism to the abelian Lie algebra $\mathbb C$, because $[\mathfrak b,\mathfrak b]\subseteq\mathfrak n^+$. Its multiplicative extension to the tensor algebra kills every relation $XY-YX-[X,Y]$, so the quotient definition in [L2] makes it an algebra homomorphism $\chi_\lambda:U(\mathfrak b)\to\mathbb C$.  By the PBW basis of [L1] every $u\in U(\mathfrak g)$ has a unique finite expansion $u=\sum_Af^Au_A$, with $u_A\in U(\mathfrak b)$ and $f^A$ ranging over the monomials in a basis of $\mathfrak n^-$. Define $\rho(u)=\sum_A\chi_\lambda(u_A)f^A$. Then $\rho$ is the identity on $U(\mathfrak n^-)$ and vanishes on $J$: for $x\in\mathfrak n^+$ and $H\in\mathfrak h$, one has $\chi_\lambda(x)=0$ and $\chi_\lambda(H-\lambda(H)1)=0$, so $\rho(u x)=\rho(u(H-\lambda(H)1))=0$ for every $u\in U(\mathfrak g)$. Thus $U(\mathfrak n^-)\cap J=0$, and $\varphi$ is a linear isomorphism by step 1.1. In particular $v_0\ne0$, and the action of $U(\mathfrak n^-)$ on $N$ corresponds under $\varphi$ to left multiplication. [A1, L1, L2, step 1.1]

3.1 For each $i$ set $w_i:=f_i^{\,m_i+1}\cdot v_0=\varphi(f_i^{\,m_i+1})$; this is nonzero by [L6] and step 2.1, and it has weight $\lambda-(m_i+1)\alpha_i$. [L6, step 2.1]

4.1 For every $i$ the vector $w_i$ satisfies $\mathfrak n^+\cdot w_i=0$: for $j=i$ one has $e_iw_i=[e_i,f_i^{\,m_i+1}]\cdot v_0$ by the product rule [L2] and $e_i\cdot v_0=0$, and the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-commutation identity $[e_i,f_i^{n}]=nf_i^{n-1}(h_{\alpha_i}-(n-1))$ gives $[e_i,f_i^{\,m_i+1}]v_0=(m_i+1)f_i^{\,m_i}(h_{\alpha_i}-m_i)\cdot v_0=0$ because $h_{\alpha_i}\cdot v_0=m_iv_0$; for $j\ne i$ one has $[e_j,f_i]=0$ by [L5], hence $[e_j,f_i^{\,m_i+1}]=0$ and $e_jw_i=f_i^{\,m_i+1}\cdot(e_j\cdot v_0)+[e_j,f_i^{\,m_i+1}]\cdot v_0=0$. [L2, L3, L5, step 3.1]

5.1 By steps 3.1 and 4.1 each $w_i$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda-(m_i+1)\alpha_i$, so by [L4] its submodule $K_i=U(\mathfrak g)w_i$ has all weights $\le\lambda-(m_i+1)\alpha_i$; since $(m_i+1)\alpha_i$ is a nonzero element of $Q_+$ by [L6] and $\lambda-(\lambda-(m_i+1)\alpha_i)=(m_i+1)\alpha_i\notin -Q_+$, no weight of $K_i$ equals $\lambda$, and $w_i\ne0$. [L4, L6, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Let $K=\sum_iK_i$. A vector of weight $\lambda$ in a sum of submodules lies in the sum of their $\lambda$-weight spaces, each of which is zero by step 5.1, so $K$ has no weight $\lambda$; in particular $v_0\notin K$. [step 5.1]

6.2 The left ideal $I_\lambda$ defining $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ equals $J+\sum_iU(\mathfrak g)f_i^{\,m_i+1}$, because it is the left ideal generated by the generators of $J$ together with the elements $f_i^{\,m_i+1}$; passing to the quotient by $J$ and using the isomorphism of step 2.1 gives $I_\lambda/J=\sum_i(U(\mathfrak g)f_i^{\,m_i+1}+J)/J=\sum_iK_i=K$. [A1, step 5.1]

7.1 Therefore $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)/I_\lambda=N/K$, and the canonical generator $v_\lambda=v_0+K$ is nonzero by step 6.1. [step 6.1, step 6.2]

8.1 Finally $v_\lambda$ has weight $\lambda$, because $H\cdot v_\lambda=\lambda(H)v_\lambda$ holds in $N$ and passes to the quotient, and $\mathfrak n^+\cdot v_\lambda=0$ for the same reason; hence $v_\lambda$ is a nonzero weight vector of weight $\lambda$ killed by $\mathfrak n^+$. [L3, step 7.1]

9.1 The class of the unit in $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ is nonzero, has weight $\lambda$, and is killed by $\mathfrak n^+$, as asserted. [step 8.1] ∎
