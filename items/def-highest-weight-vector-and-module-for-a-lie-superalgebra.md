## Definition

Fix a triangular decomposition datum $(\mathfrak n^-,\mathfrak h,\mathfrak n^+)$ as in [[def-triangular-decomposition-of-a-lie-superalgebra]] and a left $\mathfrak g$-supermodule $V$. Here $\mathfrak h^*=\mathcal L(\mathfrak h,\mathbb C)$ is the algebraic dual from [[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]]. A **highest-weight vector of weight** $\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$ is a nonzero homogeneous $v\in V$ such that
$$ h\cdot v=\lambda(h)v\quad(h\in\mathfrak h),\qquad n\cdot v=0\quad(n\in\mathfrak n^+). $$
The **highest-weight module generated by** $v$ is the smallest graded $\mathfrak g$-invariant subspace containing $v$, equivalently the span of $v$ and all vectors $x_1\cdots x_r\cdot v$ with $r\ge1$ and homogeneous $x_i\in\mathfrak g$. If this span is all of $V$, then $V$ is a highest-weight module of weight $\lambda$.

This definition makes no finite-dimensionality, integrability, semisimplicity, root-system, or classification assertion. In particular, the ordinary highest-weight classification for finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebras does not apply to a general Lie superalgebra.
