## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$, **assume the Axiom of Countable Choice**
([[def-countable-choice]]) and let $T : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be an
invertible linear map ([[def-linear-map]]). Then:

1. $T[E]$ is a Borel set for every Borel set $E$, and $T$ carries open sets to
   open sets;
2. there is a strictly positive real $c(T)$, namely
   $c(T) = \lambda_n\big(T[(0,1]^n]\big)$, with
   $$\lambda_n\big(T[E]\big) \;=\; c(T)\,\lambda_n(E) \qquad \text{for every } E \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n);$$
3. $c(S \circ T) = c(S)\,c(T)$ for invertible linear $S$ and $T$, and
   $c(\mathrm{id}) = 1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n \ge 1$, the Axiom of Countable Choice, and an invertible linear map $T$ of $\mathbb{R}^n$.

[L1] Assuming countable choice, a measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\mu(E+h)=\mu(E)$ for every Borel $E$ and every $h$, and with $\mu\big((0,1]^n\big)=1$, equals $\lambda_n$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$; in particular the theorem notes that the restriction of $\lambda_n$ to $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ satisfies these hypotheses ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-is-the-unique-normalised-translation-invariant-borel-measure]]).

[L2] Assuming countable choice, every Borel subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is Lebesgue measurable ([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]), and $\lambda_n$ is a measure on $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-is-a-complete-measure]]).

[L3] Every set $R$ with $R^{\circ} \subseteq R \subseteq \overline{R}$ is Lebesgue measurable with $\lambda_n(R) = \prod_{i<n}(b_i-a_i)$ ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]).

[L4] Every bounded subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ has finite outer measure ([[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).

[F1] For every linear $L:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^n$ there is a unique matrix $A$ such that $(Lh)_i=\sum_{j<m}a_{ij}h_j$, and there is $K\ge0$ with $\|Lh\|_2\le K\|h\|_2$ for every $h$ ([[lem-euclidean-linear-maps-have-matrices-and-are-bounded]], [[def-linear-map]]).

[F2] The **translate** of $E\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ by $a$ is $E+a:=\{x+a:x\in E\}$ ([[def-translation-of-a-set-in-rn]]).

[F3] A **measure** on $(X,\mathcal A)$ is a function $\mu:\mathcal A\to[0,+\infty]$ with $\mu(\varnothing)=0$ that is countably additive on pairwise disjoint sequences ([[def-measure]]), and a scalar multiple $c\mu$ is again a measure ([[thm-nonnegative-weighted-sums-of-measures]], [[def-nonnegative-weighted-sum-of-measures]]).

[F4] The **Borel sigma-algebra** is the sigma-algebra generated by the open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), $\sigma_X(\mathcal E)$ is the smallest sigma-algebra containing $\mathcal E$ ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]), and a sigma-algebra is closed under complements and countable unions ([[def-sigma-algebra]]).

[F5] A subset $U$ is open in $(X,d)$ when every $x \in U$ has a ball $B(x,r) \subseteq U$ ([[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]]), and $A$ is **bounded** when it is empty or lies in some ball ([[def-metric-bounded-diameter]]).

[F6] For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$, $\lVert x\rVert_\infty \le \lVert x\rVert_2 \le \lVert x\rVert_1 \le n\,\lVert x\rVert_\infty$, and $\lVert x-y\rVert_2 = d_2(x,y)$, $\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty = d_\infty(x,y)$ ([[lem-every-norm-on-rn-is-continuous-for-the-euclidean-metric]], claim 3; [[lem-p-norms-are-norms-and-induce-the-published-metrics]], claim 3; [[def-p-norms-on-rn]]; [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The inverse $T^{-1}$ is linear, so there are reals $K \ge 0$ and $K_T \ge 0$ with $\lVert T^{-1}y\rVert_2 \le K\lVert y\rVert_2$ and $\lVert Tx\rVert_2 \le K_T\lVert x\rVert_2$ for all $x,y$; put $K' := K+1 > 0$. [F1, F6]

2.1 $T$ carries open sets to open sets: if $U$ is open, $y = Tx \in T[U]$ and $B(x,r) \subseteq U$, then $d_2(y,z)<r/K'$ gives $d_2\big(x,T^{-1}z\big) = \lVert T^{-1}(y-z)\rVert_2 \le K\,d_2(y,z) < r$, so $T^{-1}z \in U$ and $z \in T[U]$. [step 1.1, F1, F5, F6]

3.1 The family of $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ with $T[E]$ Borel is a sigma-algebra, because $T$ is a bijection and so $T[\cdot]$ commutes with complements and with countable unions, and it contains every open set by step 2.1; minimality of $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ over the open sets gives claim 1. [step 2.1, F4]

3.2 $T[(0,1]^n]$ is bounded, being contained in the ball about the origin of radius $K_T\sqrt{n}+1$, so it has finite measure; and it contains $T[V]$ for the nonempty open box $V := \{x : 0<x_i<1\ (i<n)\}$, which is open and nonempty by step 2.1, hence contains a ball $B(y,r)$ and with it the open box $\{x : |x_i-y_i|<r/n\}$, whose measure $(2r/n)^n$ is a strictly positive real. So $c(T) := \lambda_n\big(T[(0,1]^n]\big)$ is a strictly positive real. [step 2.1, L2, L3, L4, F1, F5, F6]

4.1 The assignment $\nu(E) := \lambda_n(T[E])$ is well defined on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ by claim 1, and it is a measure: $\nu(\varnothing)=0$, and $T$ being injective carries a pairwise disjoint sequence to a pairwise disjoint sequence with $T\big[\bigcup_kE_k\big] = \bigcup_kT[E_k]$, so countable additivity of $\lambda_n$ transfers. It is translation invariant, since $T[E+h] = T[E]+T(h)$ by linearity and $\lambda_n$ is translation invariant. [step 3.1, L1, L2, F2, F3]

5.1 By step 3.2 the scalar multiple $c(T)^{-1}\nu$ is a measure on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, it is translation invariant, and it gives the unit cube the value $1$, so the uniqueness theorem identifies it with $\lambda_n$ on the Borel sets; that is claim 2. Claim 3 follows by evaluating at the unit cube: $c(S\circ T) = \lambda_n\big(S[T[(0,1]^n]]\big) = c(S)\lambda_n\big(T[(0,1]^n]\big) = c(S)c(T)$, and the identity map gives $c(\mathrm{id}) = \lambda_n\big((0,1]^n\big) = 1$. [step 3.2, step 4.1, L1, L3, F3] ∎
