## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $k$ be a field, $T_2$ the diagonal torus of $\mathrm{SL}_2$, $U^+=\{\binom{1\ a}{0\ 1}\}$ and $U^-=\{\binom{1\ 0}{a\ 1}\}$ ([[lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]], [[def-morphism-and-closed-subgroup-scheme]]). Then: (a) $\mathrm{SL}_2$ is generated by $U^+$ and $U^-$, and $\mathrm{SL}_2$ and $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ are perfect; (b) the isomorphisms $u_\alpha(a)=\binom{1\ a}{0\ 1}$ and $u_{-\alpha}(a)=\binom{1\ 0}{a\ 1}$ satisfy $tu_\alpha(a)t^{-1}=u_\alpha(\alpha(t)a)$ and $tu_{-\alpha}(a)t^{-1}=u_{-\alpha}(\alpha(t)^{-1}a)$ for $t=\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1})$ and the root $\alpha(t)=x^2$; (c) $n_\alpha=u_\alpha(1)u_{-\alpha}(-1)u_\alpha(1)=\binom{0\ 1}{-1\ 0}$ represents the nontrivial element $s_\alpha$ of $W(\mathrm{SL}_2,T_2)$, $n_\alpha^2=\alpha^\vee(-1)$, and $s_\alpha$ acts on $X(T_2)$ by $\chi\mapsto\chi-\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ with $\alpha^\vee=\operatorname{id}$; (d) the natural surjection $\mathrm{SL}_2\to\mathrm{PGL}_2$ is a central isogeny with kernel $\mu_2$, $\mathrm{SL}_2$ is simply connected, and every automorphism of $(\mathrm{SL}_2,T_2)$ maps $U^+$ to $U^+$ or $U^-$ and is determined by its restrictions to $T_2$ and $U^+$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a field $k$, the group $\mathrm{SL}_2$ with its diagonal torus $T_2$ and the subgroups $U^\pm$ of unipotent triangular matrices.

[F1] $\mathrm{SL}_2$ and $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ are affine group schemes of finite type with their standard matrix coordinates ([[lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]], [[def-morphism-and-closed-subgroup-scheme]]); the diagonal torus is split with $X(T_2)=\mathbb Z\chi$ and cocharacters $\mathbb Z\chi^\vee$, $\chi(\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1}))=x$, and $\alpha=2\chi$ is the root with coroot $\alpha^\vee=\chi^\vee$ ([[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]]).

[F2] Limit subgroups of cocharacters and the derived subgroup are available ([[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]], [[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]], [[lem-derived-subgroup-properties]]); the Lie functor detects generation for smooth connected groups ([[lem-lie-functor-exactness-fixed-points-and-generation]]).

[F3] Homogeneous spaces of smooth affine groups are representable, so quotients such as $\mathrm{SL}_2/\mu_2$ and $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ are available as group schemes ([[thm-homogeneous-space-for-smooth-affine-group]]).


[F4] A central isogeny from a smooth connected group onto a reductive group has reductive source: its smooth connected unipotent radical has trivial image and is then a subgroup of the finite kernel, hence is a smooth connected zero-dimensional group and is trivial. The finite centre lies in each maximal torus, and maximal tori map onto maximal tori. ([[lem-reductive-center-radical-and-semisimple-quotient]], [[lem-maximal-tori-extension-conjugacy-and-derived-group]], [[lem-unipotent-and-diagonalizable-intersection-is-trivial]]) The split maximal tori of $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ are conjugates of its diagonal torus; its normalizer acts on that torus by inversion. This is the direct matrix input in Milne20.31.

## Proof

1.1 For (a), work first over an algebraic closure and write $g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ with $ad-bc=1$. If $a\ne0$, direct multiplication gives $g=u_{-\alpha}(c/a)\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})u_\alpha(b/a)$. Put $w(a)=u_\alpha(a)u_{-\alpha}(-a^{-1})u_\alpha(a)=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\-a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}$; then $w(a)w(-1)=\operatorname{diag}(a,a^{-1})$. If $a=0$, then $c\ne0$, and $u_\alpha(1)g$ has nonzero top-left entry, reducing to the previous case. Thus $U^\pm$ generate the smooth group $\mathrm{SL}_2$: a closed subgroup containing them contains every geometric point, hence the whole reduced group. For perfectness, choose $x$ in the algebraic closure with $x^2\ne1$. The identity $[\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1}),u_\alpha(t)]=u_\alpha((x^2-1)t)$ puts $U^+$ in the derived subgroup; conjugation by $n_\alpha$ does the same for $U^-$. They generate $\mathrm{SL}_2$, and its quotient $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ is therefore perfect. These equalities of algebraic groups descend to $k$; no assertion of perfectness of the abstract groups of $k$-points is needed. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For (b), matrix multiplication gives $\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1})u_\alpha(a)\operatorname{diag}(x^{-1},x)=u_\alpha(x^2a)$ and $\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1})u_{-\alpha}(a)\operatorname{diag}(x^{-1},x)=u_{-\alpha}(x^{-2}a)$. These identities hold over every $k$-algebra and are exactly the stated conjugation formulas for $\alpha(t)=x^2$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For (c), direct multiplication gives $u_\alpha(1)u_{-\alpha}(-1)u_\alpha(1)=\binom{0\ 1}{-1\ 0}=n_\alpha$, and $n_\alpha^2=-I=\alpha^\vee(-1)$ where $\alpha^\vee:\mathbf G_m\to T_2$ is the cocharacter $t\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(t,t^{-1})$ with $\chi\circ\alpha^\vee=\operatorname{id}$. Since $n_\alpha\notin T_2$ but $n_\alpha$ normalizes $T_2$ (it conjugates $\operatorname{diag}(x,x^{-1})$ to $\operatorname{diag}(x^{-1},x)$), it represents the nontrivial element $s_\alpha$ of $W(\mathrm{SL}_2,T_2)$; the induced action on characters is $s_\alpha(\chi)=\chi-\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha=\chi-2\chi=-\chi$, the reflection in the root $\alpha=2\chi$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For (d), fppf locally every class in $\mathrm{PGL}_2$ has a matrix representative whose determinant can be made $1$ by adjoining a square root and rescaling. Thus $\mathrm{SL}_2\to\mathrm{PGL}_2$ is surjective as a group scheme, with kernel the scalar matrices of determinant $1$, namely $\mu_2$. This is a central isogeny, including characteristic $2$. For the universal-cover assertion, work over an algebraic closure and let $H\to\mathrm{PGL}_2$ be a central isogeny of smooth connected groups with kernel $N$. By [F4], $H$ is reductive and a maximal torus $S$ contains $N$ and maps onto the one-dimensional diagonal torus. Thus $S\cong\mathbf G_m$ and $N\cong\mu_m$ for some $m$. Lift the nontrivial Weyl normalizer point to $H$; it normalizes $S$ and acts by inversion, because this is its induced action on $S/N$ and the character lattice map is injective of finite index. Centrality makes that inversion trivial on $\mu_m$, so its character group $\mathbb Z/m$ is killed by $2$, forcing $m$ to divide $2$. Any central cover of $\mathrm{SL}_2$, when composed with $\mathrm{SL}_2\to\mathrm{PGL}_2$, therefore has degree at most two; the latter map already has degree two, so the former has degree one and is an isomorphism. This proves that $\mathrm{SL}_2$ is simply connected and is the universal central cover of $\mathrm{PGL}_2$, exactly the argument of Milne20.31, including characteristic two. Finally, the pair-automorphism theorem cited in the sources identifies automorphisms of $(\mathrm{SL}_2,T_2)$ with conjugations by $(N_{\mathrm{SL}_2}(T_2)/\mu_2)(k)$. Such a conjugation either preserves the two root subgroups or interchanges them. After composing with conjugation by $n_\alpha$ if necessary, it preserves $U^+$ and is a diagonal conjugation, whose parameter is determined by its action on $U^+$. Hence its restrictions to $T_2$ and $U^+$ determine it. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
