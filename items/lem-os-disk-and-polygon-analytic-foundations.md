## Statement

Assume ACω. Let a finite pointed Heegaard multidiagram have actual complete handlebody disk systems for closed oriented three-manifolds, with a basepoint in every one-system cut component. Ordered polygons use distinct labeled, pairwise transverse attaching systems; when an operation repeats a geometric system, use its separately labeled small transverse auxiliary copies. For the full hat operations impose global weak multidiagram admissibility: every nonzero full-curve multiperiodic domain vanishing at all basepoints has both signs. For operations in a specified total Spin-c family, ordinary weak admissibility is also allowed: every nonzero formal periodic domain difference in the capped Spin-c kernel, normalized at every mark and with Chern evaluation zero, has both signs. The same formal sector condition is imposed on each relevant boundary pair, including intended sectors with no generators. In this ordinary weak branch all basepoint multiplicities are fixed to zero, the required arities and compact auxiliary parameter families are finite, and counts use the corresponding fixed actual index bands. Strict all-mark normalized strong admissibility is a further allowed branch; it is not substituted for ordinary weakness. For a single diagram the full-module admissibility convention tests all intended Spin-c sectors, including empty sectors, and implies the required global weak domain condition.

In positive symmetric rank choose, among the auxiliary data, an arbitrarily small pointed Hamiltonian representative with no triple product-torus intersection, following its pair generators and transporting its domains, actual Spin-c labels and admissibility. Fix that representative. Compatible generic source-dependent perturbations, coherent over the required conformal faces and auxiliary parameter families, then make the disk and polygon spaces entering the hat differential, continuation, handleslide, stabilization and surgery operations regular with their actual expected dimensions. The common protected tensor and arbitrary constant product-angle end charts can be prescribed, and coherent perturbations can be arbitrarily small about a supplied compatible reference model. Auxiliary polygon and anchored-continuation parameter families here keep the autonomous end paths fixed and regular on the required energy budget; regular compatible parameter-boundary data may be retained. No slice-regularity assertion is made for arbitrary parameter-varying autonomous end paths. This does not assert regularity by almost-complex variations alone on an arbitrary fixed triple, or invariance under the preliminary curve choice.

Dimensions include every allowed conformal and auxiliary family parameter. Quotient only actual source automorphisms: longitudinal translation acts freely on autonomous nonconstant two-ended strips; an anchored source-dependent continuation strip and a three-marked triangle have no translation quotient. Every total zero-dimensional space used in a mod-two count is finite, across all its literal realized classes and with its specified labels. The one-dimensional spaces entering the chain-map, composition and homotopy identities compactify to actual compact one-manifolds. At fixed data their boundaries are precisely the matching rigid polygon–polygon conformal splittings and strip–polygon breakings. In an auxiliary family the broken configurations are the rigid joint fiber configurations at one common parameter, together with the prescribed parameter-boundary strata; they need not be products of individually rigid parameterized polygon spaces. Every such broken end has an exhaustive gluing collar. For the zero-basepoint operations no nonconstant sphere or one-torus boundary disk survives; thus the specified boundary-degeneration terms vanish, and there is no further escape or uncontrolled periodic-domain end. Boundary parity gives the differential square-zero identity and the polygon chain-map, composition and homotopy identities.

At symmetric rank zero retain the original empty systems and use the point target directly: the differential is zero, the matching constant triangle is counted once, higher rigid operations vanish, and the conformal and parameter boundary identities are the actual source-space identities. No triple-torus exclusion or free translation quotient is imposed on that point map.

This is the analytic count theorem. It does not construct a subordinate surgery diagram, prove that a named top generator is a cycle, identify the homology of a surgery total complex with a filling, or prove Heegaard-move invariance. Those remain duties of the respective operation constructions.

1.1 The admissibility inputs are actual domain bounds, not a formal assumption that the desired moduli are compact. For several systems the multiperiodic condition in the Statement is imposed on the actual chosen auxiliary multidiagram; pairwise conditions alone are not substituted for it. Its nonnegative zero-mark domains are finite before any index or regularity choice, as proved in [F3]. For the ordinary weak specified-sector branch, fix the finitely many required corner tuples, arities and count dimensions zero and one. If a source has $d$ inputs and $p$ allowed family parameters, its fixed-map index is its count dimension minus $d-2+p$; the finite strata of a compact parameter family therefore require only a finite list of actual indices. Strips and anchored profiles have their stated respective index shifts. For each nonempty same-corner, same-Spin-c slice choose one actual smooth reference and identical transverse real-plane caps for it and every comparison map. The protected nearly symmetric comparison homotopy in [F7] preserves those boundary planes and caps. Consequently the actual fixed-map index difference is $\nu(D(u)-D(u_0))=\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(D(u)-D(u_0))\rangle$ at zero entire mark vector. Thus each fixed actual index gives a fixed relative-index slice after subtracting the reference index. The common conformal and external parameter columns cancel; their finite dimension shifts have already been included. Apply [F2] to these relative-index slices to obtain finitely many nonnegative domains, independently of coefficients. Include the actual continuous concatenations representing the total classes of the required boundary identities: their total index is the same prescribed finite list by actual cap additivity, their marks are zero, and their total Spin-c extensions are the specified ones. Thus their domains belong to those same affine slices, not to a hypothetical finite class list. There are finitely many slices, so selecting their references uses only finite choice, and their union is finite. The domain-energy estimate of [F6] supplies a uniform energy bound $E_0$ before the coefficient choices, on a small family with uniformly bounded geometric constants. Enlarge the finite list for the finitely many separated compositions and relative homotopies required, and choose a strict energy budget $E>E_0$. In the strict branch [F2] separately supplies its stronger finite upper-index domains and lower strip index. No lower index bound is claimed for ordinary weak data. For a finite-ended polygon or anchored strip truncate every end in its common exact generator chart inside $U_1$, multiply its coordinate map by a real cutoff and cap at the generator.
