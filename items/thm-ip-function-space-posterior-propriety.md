## Statement

Let $X$ be a separable real Banach space with norm $\|\cdot\|_X$, let $D\subseteq X$ be Borel, and let $\mu_0$ be a Borel probability on $X$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$. Here $\mathcal B(X)$ is generated by the norm-open sets. Give $D$ the relative topology and Borel sigma-algebra $\mathcal B(D)=\{D\cap B:B\in\mathcal B(X)\}$, and let $\mu_{0,D}$ be the restriction of $\mu_0$ to $D$, so $\mu_{0,D}(A)=\mu_0(A)$ for $A\in\mathcal B(D)$. Let $(\mathsf Y,\mathcal Y)$ be a measurable data space. Assume $\Phi:D\times\mathsf Y\to(-\infty,+\infty]$ is measurable from $\bigl(D\times\mathsf Y,\mathcal B(D)\otimes\mathcal Y\bigr)$ to $\bigl((-\infty,+\infty],\mathcal B((-\infty,+\infty])\bigr)$, where $\mathcal B(\overline{\mathbb R})$ is generated by the order topology on the extended real line and $\mathcal B((-\infty,+\infty])$ is its trace on $(-\infty,+\infty]$; thus $+\infty$ is allowed and $-\infty$ is excluded. Let $\mathsf Y_0\subseteq\mathsf Y$. Suppose that for every $y\in\mathsf Y_0$, the section $u\mapsto\Phi(u;y)$ is finite and continuous as a real-valued function on $D$ with its relative topology inherited from the norm topology. Suppose there is one Borel function $M_1:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$, independent of $y\in\mathsf Y_0$, such that
$$\Phi(u;y)\ge -M_1(\|u\|_X)\quad(u\in D,\ y\in\mathsf Y_0),\qquad \int_D e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)<\infty.$$
Define $\operatorname{supp}\mu_0:=\{x\in X:\mu_0(U)>0\text{ for every open neighborhood }U\text{ of }x\}$. Then for every $y\in\mathsf Y_0$, the evidence $Z_\Phi(y)$ is finite and strictly positive, and the normalized fiberwise update $\Pi^y$ is a probability measure on $D$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A separable real Banach space $X$, a Borel set $D\subseteq X$, a Borel probability $\mu_0$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$, a jointly measurable potential $\Phi$ with finite continuous sections on $D$ for $y\in\mathsf Y_0$, and one common envelope $M_1$ satisfying the stated integrability condition.

[F1] A prior on a forward domain is a Borel probability $\mu_0$ on $X$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$, and its restriction $\mu_{0,D}$ is a probability on $D$. [[def-ip-prior-on-a-forward-domain]]

[F2] For a fixed $y$, $Z_\Phi(y)=\int_D e^{-\Phi(u;y)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)$ and, only for positive finite evidence, $\Pi^y(A)=Z_\Phi(y)^{-1}\int_Ae^{-\Phi(u;y)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)$. [[def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law]]

[F3] Separability means that some at most countable subset is dense. [[def-separable-space]]

[F4] A product of two at most countable sets is at most countable. [[thm-product-of-countable]]

[F5] The rational numbers are countably infinite. [[thm-rationals-countable]]

[F6] Between any two distinct reals lies a rational. [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F7] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F8] A measure of a countable union is at most the sum of the measures of its members. [[thm-finite-and-countable-subadditivity-of-measures]]

[F9] If measurable sets satisfy $A\subseteq B$, then $\mu(A)\le\mu(B)$. [[prop-measure-monotonicity]]

[F10] For nonnegative measurable functions, pointwise domination implies domination of their nonnegative integrals. [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]

[F11] For a measure $\mu$ and nonnegative measurable likelihood $L_y$, the normalized update $A\mapsto Z^{-1}\int_A L_y\,d\mu$ is a probability measure exactly when $0<Z<\infty$. [[prop-fiberwise-posterior-propriety-iff-finite-positive-evidence]]

[F12] A measure assigns zero mass to the empty set. [[def-measure]]

[F13] A measure is countably additive on pairwise-disjoint measurable sequences. [[def-measure]]

[F14] A real Banach space is a real normed space with induced metric $d_X(x,x')=\|x-x'\|_X$, complete for that metric. [[def-banach-space]]

[F15] The norm satisfies $\|u+v\|_X\le\|u\|_X+\|v\|_X$. [[def-norm-and-normed-space]]

[F16] The open metric ball is $B(x,r)=\{z:d_X(x,z)<r\}$ for $r>0$. [[def-metric-ball]]

[F17] Every positive-radius open metric ball is open in the metric topology. [[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]]

[F18] The open sets of $D$ with the relative topology are precisely traces $U\cap D$ of open sets $U$ in $X$. [[def-subspace-topology-top]]

[F19] The metric topology is the collection of open sets for the metric induced by the norm. [[def-metric-topology]]

[F20] Continuity at a point means that the preimage of each target neighborhood is a neighborhood of that point. [[def-continuous-map-top]]

[F21] The real exponential is continuous, positive and increasing. [[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]], [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]]

[F22] A nonempty at most countable set is the surjective image of $\mathbb N$. [[lem-countable-iff-surjection-from-n]]

[F23] Every subset of an at most countable set is at most countable. [[lem-subset-of-countable]]

[F24] The norm triangle inequality gives $\|u\|_X-\|v\|_X\le\|u-v\|_X$ and the reverse inequality after swapping $u,v$, so $u\mapsto\|u\|_X$ is continuous; composing measurable maps with Borel maps preserves measurability. ## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Choose a countable dense set $C\subseteq X$ using separability [F3]. The positive rationals $\mathbb Q_{>0}$ are at most countable by [F5] and [F23], so $C\times\mathbb Q_{>0}$ is at most countable by [F4]. It is nonempty because $X$ is a nonempty vector space, $C$ is dense, and $1\in\mathbb Q_{>0}$. By [F22], enumerate $C\times\mathbb Q_{>0}$ by a surjection from $\mathbb N$; composing that map with $(c,q)\mapsto B(c,q)$ shows that $\mathcal B_0:=\{B(c,q):c\in C,\ q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}\}$ is at most countable. It consists of open sets by [F17], and it is a base: if $x\in U$ with $U$ open, choose $r>0$ with $B(x,r)\subseteq U$, choose $c\in C$ with $d:=\|x-c\|_X<r/3$, so $d<r-d$, and then choose a rational $q$ with $d<q<r-d$ by [F6]. The norm triangle inequality [F15] gives $x\in B(c,q)\subseteq B(x,r)\subseteq U$. [given, F3, F4, F5, F6, F14, F15, F16, F17, F19, F22, F23, choose, construct]

1.2 Fix any $y\in\mathsf Y_0$. The section $\Phi(\cdot;y)$ is continuous and real-valued on $D$, so its preimages of open subsets of $\mathbb R$ are relatively open by [F18, F20] and hence Borel by [F7]. Since the exponential is continuous [F21], $e^{-\Phi(\cdot;y)}$ is Borel measurable. By [F24], $u\mapsto e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}$ is also measurable. The common lower envelope and monotonicity of the exponential [F21] give $0\le e^{-\Phi(u;y)}\le e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}$ for every $u\in D$. Thus [F10] and the assumed envelope integrability give $Z_\Phi(y)\le\int_D e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)<\infty$. Since $y$ was arbitrary, this holds for every $y\in\mathsf Y_0$. [F2, F7, F10, F18, F20, F21, F24, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $\mathcal N:=\{B\in\mathcal B_0:\mu_0(B)=0\}$ and $N:=\bigcup\mathcal N$. By [F23], $\mathcal N$ is at most countable. If $\mathcal N=\varnothing$, then $N=\varnothing$ and $\mu_0(N)=0$ by [F12]. Otherwise [F22] gives a surjection $b:\mathbb N\to\mathcal N$; each $b_n$ is Borel by [F7] and [F17] and has measure zero, so [F8] gives $\mu_0(N)=\mu_0(\bigcup_n b_n)\le\sum_n\mu_0(b_n)=0$. If $x\notin N$ and $U$ is an open neighborhood of $x$, the base property supplies $B\in\mathcal B_0$ with $x\in B\subseteq U$; then $\mu_0(B)>0$, and [F9] gives $\mu_0(U)\ge\mu_0(B)>0$. A point in $N$ lies in a zero-mass open neighborhood. Therefore $X\setminus N=\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$ and $\mu_0(\operatorname{supp}\mu_0)=1$. [F7, F8, F9, F12, F17, F22, F23, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Since $\mu_0$ is a probability and $\mu_0(D)=1$, countable additivity [F13] on $X=D\sqcup(X\setminus D)$ gives $\mu_0(X\setminus D)=0$. Likewise, $\mu_0(\operatorname{supp}\mu_0)=1$ by step 2.1 and $\mu_0(X)=1$, so [F13] on the support and its complement gives $\mu_0(X\setminus\operatorname{supp}\mu_0)=0$. By [F8] their union is null, so $\mu_0(D\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu_0)=1$. This intersection is nonempty because [F12] gives $\mu_0(\varnothing)=0$. Choose $x\in D\cap\operatorname{supp}\mu_0$. [F1, F8, F12, F13, step 2.1, choose]

4.1 Fix $y\in\mathsf Y_0$. The open interval $(-\infty,\Phi(x;y)+1)$ contains $\Phi(x;y)$; continuity at $x$ [F20] gives a relative-open neighborhood $W$ mapped into this interval. By [F18], write $W=U\cap D$ for an open $U\subseteq X$ containing $x$. Thus $\Phi(u;y)\le\Phi(x;y)+1$ on $W$. By the support property $\mu_0(U)>0$. Since $U\setminus D\subseteq X\setminus D$ and step 3.1 gives $\mu_0(X\setminus D)=0$, [F9] gives $\mu_0(U\setminus D)=0$; countable additivity [F13] on $U=W\sqcup(U\setminus D)$ yields $\mu_{0,D}(W)=\mu_0(U)>0$. On $W$, $e^{-\Phi(u;y)}\ge e^{-\Phi(x;y)-1}$. Therefore monotonicity and positive scalar homogeneity of the nonnegative integral [F10] give $Z_\Phi(y)=\int_D e^{-\Phi(u;y)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)\ge\int_W e^{-\Phi(u;y)}\,\mu_{0,D}(du)\ge e^{-\Phi(x;y)-1}\mu_{0,D}(W)>0$. [F1, F2, F10, F13, F18, F20, step 3.1, given, choose, algebra]

5.1 For the arbitrary $y$ fixed in step 4.1, steps 1.2 and 4.1 give $0<Z_\Phi(y)<\infty$. Applying [F11] to the measurable likelihood section $e^{-\Phi(\cdot;y)}$ shows that the normalized update $\Pi^y$ is a probability measure on $D$. Since $y\in\mathsf Y_0$ was arbitrary, this holds for every such datum. [F2, F11, step 1.2, step 4.1, given] ∎
