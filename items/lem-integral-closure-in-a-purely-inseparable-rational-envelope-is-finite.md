## Statement

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, let $K'/K$ be finite purely
inseparable, let $q=p^{e}$, and let $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ be algebraically
independent over $K$. Put
$E=K'(x_1^{1/q},\ldots,x_d^{1/q})$ and $R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$. Then the integral
closure of $R$ in $E$ is exactly

$$ K'[x_1^{1/q},\ldots,x_d^{1/q}], $$

a polynomial ring over the field $K'$; and for every intermediate field
$K(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\subseteq L\subseteq E$, the integral closure of $R$ in $L$
is a finite $R$-module.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $K$ of characteristic $p>0$, a finite purely inseparable extension $K'/K$, an exponent $e$ with $q=p^{e}$, and algebraically independent $x_1,\ldots,x_d$ over $K$; $E=K'(x_1^{1/q},\ldots,x_d^{1/q})$ with $x_i^{1/q}\in E$ the $q$-th root of $x_i$, and $R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$.

[L1] A field extension $L/K$ of characteristic $p>0$ is purely inseparable when every $\alpha\in L$ has $\alpha^{p^{n}}\in K$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$, the exponent $0$ permitted; a finite extension has finite degree equal to its dimension as a vector space over the base ([[def-purely-inseparable-extension]], [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]).

[L2] In a field of characteristic $p>0$ the map $z\mapsto z^{p}$ is an injective field endomorphism, with $n$-fold iterate $z\mapsto z^{p^{n}}$ ([[thm-frobenius-endomorphism-and-finite-field-automorphism]]).

[L3] The evaluation homomorphism $K'[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]\to E$ with $Y_i\mapsto y_i$ has zero kernel exactly when $y_1,\ldots,y_d$ are algebraically independent over $K'$; and nonzero polynomial functions of algebraically independent elements are nonzero ([[def-algebraic-and-transcendental-elements]], [[def-polynomial-evaluation-and-root]], [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L4] $\operatorname{Frac}(D)=(D\setminus\{0\})^{-1}D$ for a domain $D$, and $F(S)$ denotes the smallest subfield containing $F\cup S$; a $K'$-subalgebra generated by finitely many elements is written $K'[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$ ([[def-field-of-fractions]], [[def-field-extension-generated-subfields-and-simple-extension]], [[def-finitely-generated-field-extension]], [[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L5] For every field $F$ and finite $d\ge0$ the ring $F[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]$ is an integrally closed domain ([[lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed]]).

[L6] An element is integral over a subring when it is a root of a monic polynomial over that subring; the integral closure of $A$ in an extension field is the set of integral elements, and it is a subring of that field; $A$ is integrally closed when it equals its closure in $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]], [[def-integral-ring-extension]], [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]], [[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[L7] $K'/K$ finite implies that $K'$ has a finite $K$-basis, so a finite-dimensional vector space has a finite basis ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]], [[def-dimension]]).

[L8] $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is Noetherian ([[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct]]) and every submodule of a finitely generated module over a commutative Noetherian ring is finitely generated ([[lem-submodules-of-finite-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-finite-direct]], [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[L9] A ring of fractions $\operatorname{Frac}(D)$ of a domain $D$ is a field and $D$ is a nonzero commutative ring without zero divisors ([[def-field-of-fractions]], [[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The elements $y_i:=x_i^{1/q}$ are algebraically independent over $K'$. First there is an exponent $N\ge0$ with $K'^{p^{N}}\subseteq K$: by [L1] each element of $K'$ has a $p$-power in $K$, and by [L7] the finite extension $K'$ is spanned over $K$ by finitely many elements $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_r$, so choosing $N$ as the maximum of the finitely many exponents $n_j$ with $\gamma_j^{p^{n_j}}\in K$ gives $\gamma_j^{p^{N}}\in K$ for every $j$; since the $p^{N}$-th power map is additive and multiplicative by [L2] and $K^{p^{N}}\subseteq K$, every element of $K'^{p^{N}}=K^{p^{N}}(\gamma_1^{p^{N}},\ldots,\gamma_r^{p^{N}})$ lies in $K$. Now let $P=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}Y^{\alpha}\in K'[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]$ satisfy $P(y_1,\ldots,y_d)=0$; raising this identity to the $p^{N}q$-th power and using additivity and multiplicativity of the Frobenius iterates [L2] together with $y_i^{p^{N}q}=(y_i^{q})^{p^{N}}=x_i^{p^{N}}$ gives $0=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}^{p^{N}q}(x_1^{p^{N}})^{\alpha_1}\cdots(x_d^{p^{N}})^{\alpha_d}$ with every coefficient $c_{\alpha}^{p^{N}q}$ in $K$. If a nonzero $R\in K[T_1,\ldots,T_d]$ satisfied $R(x_1^{p^{N}},\ldots,x_d^{p^{N}})=0$, then the nonzero polynomial $R(T_1^{p^{N}},\ldots,T_d^{p^{N}})$ would vanish at $x_1,\ldots,x_d$, contradicting algebraic independence of the $x_i$ over $K$; so the displayed relation forces $c_{\alpha}^{p^{N}q}=0$ for every $\alpha$, and then $c_{\alpha}=0$ because Frobenius is injective by [L2]. Hence $P=0$ and the evaluation map is injective by [L3]; it is surjective onto the $K'$-subalgebra $T:=K'[y_1,\ldots,y_d]\subseteq E$ generated by the $y_i$. Hence $T$ is isomorphic to the polynomial ring $K'[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]$ over the field $K'$, so $T$ is an integrally closed domain by [L5], and $\operatorname{Frac}(T)=E$, because $E$ is the smallest field containing $K'$ and the $y_i$ by [L4] while $\operatorname{Frac}(T)$ is the smallest field containing $T$ and equals the field of fractions of $K'[Y_1,\ldots,Y_d]$ under the isomorphism. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L7, L9, given, algebra]

2.1 $T$ is integral over $R$ and contains it: $R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]=K[y_1^{q},\ldots,y_d^{q}]\subseteq K'[y_1,\ldots,y_d]=T$, every $y_i$ is a root of the monic polynomial $Z^{q}-x_i\in R[Z]$, and every element of the field $K'$ is algebraic, hence integral, over the field $K\subseteq R$. By [L6] the elements of $E$ integral over $R$ form a subring of $E$ containing $R$, containing $K'$ and containing every $y_i$; being a subring, it contains the subring $T$ these elements generate, so $T\subseteq\overline R$ for the integral closure $\overline R$ of $R$ in $E$. Conversely every $z\in E$ integral over $R$ is integral over $T$, since the monic equation for $z$ over $R$ has coefficients in $R\subseteq T$; as $T$ is integrally closed with fraction field $E$ by step 1.1, such $z$ lies in $T$. Hence $\overline R=T$. [L6, step 1.1, given]

2.2 The closure $T$ is a finite $R$-module. By [L7] fix a finite $K$-basis $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m$ of $K'$. Every element of $T=K'[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$ is a finite sum of terms $c\,y_1^{a_1}\cdots y_d^{a_d}$ with $c\in K'$ and $a_i\in\mathbb N$, and writing $c=\sum_j\lambda_j\beta_j$ with $\lambda_j\in K$ and reducing exponents modulo $q$ via $y_i^{a_i}=y_i^{q\lfloor a_i/q\rfloor}y_i^{a_i\bmod q}=x_i^{\lfloor a_i/q\rfloor}y_i^{a_i\bmod q}$ shows that the finite list $\beta_jy_1^{a_1}\cdots y_d^{a_d}$ with $1\le j\le m$ and $0\le a_i<q$ generates $T$ as an $R$-module, using [L3] for the uniqueness of the expressions of elements of the polynomial ring $T$. Hence $T$ is a finitely generated $R$-module, and $R$ is Noetherian by [L8]. [L3, L7, L8, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Let $K(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\subseteq L\subseteq E$ be an intermediate field. For $z\in L$ the monic polynomial equations over $R$ satisfied by $z$ are the same whether $z$ is regarded in $L$ or in $E$, so the integral closure of $R$ in $L$ is $\overline R\cap L=T\cap L$ by step 2.1. Now $T\cap L$ is an $R$-submodule of the finitely generated $R$-module $T$, and $R$ is Noetherian by [L8], so $T\cap L$ is a finitely generated, hence finite, $R$-module by [L8]. In particular, taking $L=E$ recovers the assertion that the closure of $R$ in $E$ is $T=K'[x_1^{1/q},\ldots,x_d^{1/q}]$, a polynomial ring over $K'$. [L8, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
