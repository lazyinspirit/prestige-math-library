## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $K$ be a global field,
let $S$ be a finite set of distinct places of $K$, and for each $v\in S$ let
$c_v\in K_v$ and $\epsilon_v>0$. Then there exists $a\in K$ such that
$$|a-c_v|_v<\epsilon_v\qquad(v\in S).$$
The conclusion imposes conditions only at the listed places.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and CC; a global field $K$; a finite set $S$ of distinct
places; targets $c_v\in K_v$; and positive errors $\epsilon_v$.

[A1] AC implies DC and CC; the completion construction requires CC, while the
place, curve, Riemann--Roch and principal-divisor interfaces below retain their
stated AC or DC hypotheses. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]],
[[def-dependent-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]],
[[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]])

[F1] Places are equivalence classes of nontrivial absolute values. The
completion $K_v$ has a dense isometric embedding of $K$ and a compatible
absolute value; distinct places are distinct equivalence classes.
([[def-place-of-a-global-field]],
[[def-completion-of-a-global-field-at-a-place]])

[F2] For finitely many pairwise inequivalent nontrivial absolute values on a
field, arbitrary targets in the field and positive errors admit a simultaneous
approximation by one field element. ([[thm-weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values]])

[F3] In a function field over $k=\mathbb F_q$, places correspond to closed
points $v$ on a smooth proper curve $C/k$, with
$|x|_v=q^{-\operatorname{ord}_v(x)[\kappa(v):k]}$.
([[def-place-of-a-global-field]])

[F4] At each closed point $v$ of a smooth curve, $\mathcal O_{C,v}$ is a DVR
with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_v$ generated by a uniformizer, and its residue
field has degree $d_v=[\kappa(v):k]$.
([[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]],
[[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]])

[F5] For every divisor $T$, full Riemann--Roch gives
$l(T)-l(K_C-T)=\deg_k(T)+1-g$, where $l(T)=\dim_kL(T)$ and
$L(T)=\{f\in k(C)^\times:\operatorname{div}(f)+T\ge0\}\cup\{0\}$.
([[thm-full-riemann-roch-divisor]], [[def-little-l-divisor]],
[[def-riemann-roch-space-of-divisor]])

[F6] The genus $g$ is a nonnegative integer, and every canonical divisor has
degree $2g-2$. ([[def-genus-euler-characteristic-curve]],
[[cor-canonical-degree-two-g-minus-two]])

[F7] Principal divisors have degree zero; under DC they have finite support.
([[thm-principal-divisor-degree-zero-proper-curve]],
[[def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group]],
[[def-divisor-smooth-proper-curve]])

[F8] A smooth proper geometrically integral curve over a finite field has
infinitely many closed points. ([[lem-smooth-proper-curve-over-finite-field-has-infinitely-many-closed-points]])

## Proof



**Given:** The data and hypotheses above.

1.1 If $S=\varnothing$, take $a=0$. Suppose $S\ne\varnothing$ and $K$ is a number field. For each $v\in S$, density in [F1] gives $b_v\in K$ with $|b_v-c_v|_v<\epsilon_v/2$. The absolute values representing the distinct places are pairwise inequivalent, so [F2], applied to targets $b_v$ and errors $\epsilon_v/2$, gives $a\in K$ with $|a-b_v|_v<\epsilon_v/2$ for all $v\in S$. The triangle inequality yields $|a-c_v|_v\le|a-b_v|_v+|b_v-c_v|_v<\epsilon_v$. [F1, F2, given, choose]

1.2 Suppose $S\ne\varnothing$ and $K=k(C)$ is a function field, with $k=\mathbb F_q$. By density in [F1], for each $v\in S$ choose $b_v\in K$ such that $|b_v-c_v|_v<\epsilon_v/2$. For each $v$, let $\lambda_v>0$ be such that the representative defining $K_v$ is the $\lambda_v$-th power of the normalized absolute value in [F3]; such a number exists by the equivalence defining a place. To clear any poles of these targets at the listed places, define $r_v=0$ if $b_v=0$, and otherwise $r_v=\max\{0,-\operatorname{ord}_v(b_v)\}$. Put $D_0=\sum_{v\in S}r_v[v]$. By [F8], choose a closed point $Q\notin S$. Choose $m_0\ge1$ sufficiently large that the effective divisor $E_0=m_0[Q]$ satisfies $\deg_k(E_0-D_0)\ge g$. Riemann--Roch [F5] gives $l(E_0-D_0)\ge\deg_k(E_0-D_0)+1-g\ge1$, so choose a nonzero $h\in L(E_0-D_0)$. Since $Q\notin S$, the defining inequalities for this Riemann--Roch space give $e_v:=\operatorname{ord}_v(h)\ge r_v$ for every $v\in S$; hence $hb_v\in\mathcal O_{C,v}$. For each $v$ choose an integer $N_v>e_v$ such that $q^{-\lambda_v(N_v-e_v)d_v}<\epsilon_v/2$, where $d_v=[\kappa(v):k]$, and put $D_1=\sum_{v\in S}N_v[v]$. Choose $m_1\ge1$ sufficiently large that $E_1=m_1[Q]$ satisfies $\deg_k(E_1)>2g-2$ and $\deg_k(E_1-D_1)>2g-2$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F8, given, choose, construct]

2.1 Define the $k$-linear evaluation map $\operatorname{ev}:L(E_1)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{v\in S}\mathcal O_{C,v}/\mathfrak m_v^{N_v},\qquad f\longmapsto(f\bmod\mathfrak m_v^{N_v})_{v\in S}.$ It is defined because $Q\notin S$, so every $f\in L(E_1)$ is regular at each $v\in S$. Its kernel is exactly $L(E_1-D_1)$: at each $v\in S$, vanishing of the residue class means $\operatorname{ord}_v(f)\ge N_v$, while outside $S$ the divisors $E_1$ and $E_1-D_1$ agree. Each quotient $\mathcal O_{C,v}/\mathfrak m_v^{N_v}$ has $k$-dimension $N_vd_v$, since its filtration has $N_v$ successive quotients isomorphic to $\kappa(v)$ by the DVR property [F4]. Thus the target dimension is $\deg_k(D_1)$. [F4, F5, step 1.2, given, construct]

3.1 If $\deg_k(T)>2g-2$, then [F6] gives $\deg_k(K_C-T)<0$. Hence $l(K_C-T)=0$: a nonzero member would make $\operatorname{div}(f)+K_C-T$ effective by [F5], whose degree would be $\deg_k(K_C-T)<0$ by [F7], impossible for an effective divisor. Applying Riemann--Roch [F5] to $T=E_1$ and $T=E_1-D_1$ now gives $l(E_1)-l(E_1-D_1)=\deg_k(D_1)$. By step 2.1 this is the rank of $\operatorname{ev}$, equal to the dimension of its target, so $\operatorname{ev}$ is surjective. [F5, F6, F7, step 1.2, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 Surjectivity gives $u\in L(E_1)$ whose residues at every $v\in S$ agree with those of $hb_v$. Set $a=u/h\in K$. Then $u-hb_v\in\mathfrak m_v^{N_v}$. If $a\ne b_v$, the valuation property and $\operatorname{ord}_v(h)=e_v$ give $\operatorname{ord}_v(a-b_v)\ge N_v-e_v$, hence $|a-b_v|_v\le q^{-\lambda_v(N_v-e_v)d_v}<\epsilon_v/2$ by the choice of $N_v$; if $a=b_v$, the same absolute-value bound is immediate. The nonarchimedean triangle inequality and the choice of $b_v$ give $|a-c_v|_v\le\max\{|a-b_v|_v,|b_v-c_v|_v\}<\epsilon_v$ for every $v\in S$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 The empty set case is step 1.1, the number-field case is step 1.1, and the function-field case is steps 1.2--4.1. The two cases in [F1] exhaust all global fields, proving weak approximation. AC is used through the place/model and curve-theoretic suppliers; CC is used for the general metric completions; the function-field jet interpolation uses the full Riemann--Roch theorem and clears the finitely many target poles before taking residues. [A1, F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
