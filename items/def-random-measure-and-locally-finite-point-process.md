## Definition

Fix a locally compact, second-countable Hausdorff topological space $S$
([[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-second-countable-space]],
[[def-hausdorff-space]]). Here locally compact means that every point has a
compact neighbourhood; second countable means that the topology has an at most
countable basis; and Hausdorff means that distinct points have disjoint open
neighbourhoods. Let $\mathcal B(S)$ be the Borel sigma-algebra generated by the
open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

A **counting measure** on $S$ is a Borel measure of the form
$\mu=\sum_{j\in J}\delta_{x_j}$ for a finite or countable index set $J$ and
points $x_j\in S$, where repetitions are allowed and the empty sum is zero.
Each $\delta_x$ is the unit point mass at $x$
([[def-dirac-measure]], [[prop-dirac-measure-is-a-probability-measure]]), and
the countable sum is a measure by
[[thm-nonnegative-weighted-sums-of-measures]]. The **multiplicity** at $x$ is
$\mu(\{x\})$. The counting measure is **locally finite** if
$\mu(K)<\infty$ for every compact subset $K\subseteq S$
([[def-compact-space]]).

Let $\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$ be the set of locally finite counting
measures, equipped with the sigma-algebra generated by the **evaluation maps**
$\mu\mapsto\mu(B)$ for $B\in\mathcal B(S)$. A **point process** on $S$ is a
measurable map $\eta:(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)\to
(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S),\mathcal E)$ from a probability space
([[def-probability-measure]]), with measurability in the sense of
[[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]. Equivalently, every
count $\eta(B)$ is a measurable random variable, because the evaluation maps
generate $\mathcal E$. Its **law** is the probability measure
$\mathbb P_\eta(A)=\mathbb P(\eta^{-1}(A))$ on $(\mathcal N_{\mathrm{lf}}(S),\mathcal E)$
([[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]],
[[lem-law-of-a-random-element-is-a-probability-measure]]). The process is
classified by its realized configurations: a configuration $\mu$ is
**simple** if $\mu(\{x\})\le1$ for every $x$, and is **multiplicity-bearing**
if some singleton has mass greater than one. This is a pointwise distinction;
no almost-sure simplicity assertion is part of this definition.

Let $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{lf}}(S)$ be the set of all locally finite Borel
measures on $S$, with the sigma-algebra generated by their evaluation maps. A
**random locally finite measure** $\xi$ is a measurable map into this space.

For a nonnegative Borel function $f$, write
$\eta(f):=\int_S f\,d\eta$. Define the expected measure, or **intensity
measure**, by $\nu(B):=\mathbb E[\eta(B)]$. The expected-measure and
random-integral measurability assertions are justified by
[[lem-mean-measure-and-integrals-of-a-random-point-process]]. The measure
$\nu$ need not be locally finite; it is locally finite exactly when
$\mathbb E[\eta(K)]<\infty$ for each compact $K$.

The mean measure of a random locally finite measure is likewise
$\nu(B)=\mathbb E[\xi(B)]$, covered by the same expected-measure lemma. Local
finiteness of each realization does not by itself assert local finiteness of
$\nu$.
