## Statement

Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be a GIVEN valued algebraic closure, $C=\widehat F$ its ordinary completion, and a compatible primitive cyclotomic system be GIVEN. Let $W=W(\mathcal O_C^\flat)$, $A=\mathcal O_C$, and $R=B_{\mathrm{dR}}^+$. Suppose a tilt element $y$ with $y^\sharp=p$ is GIVEN and that $\xi=[y]-p$ generates $\ker\theta$ in $W$. Put $\beta=\xi/p$ and
$$S=\left\{\sum_{n\ge0}a_n\beta^n:(a_n)_{n\ge0}\in W^{\mathbb N}\right\}\subset R,\qquad u=\log(1+\beta).$$
Then $S$ is a subring, $\bigcap_{r\ge0}p^rS=0$, $A_{\mathrm{cris}}\subseteq S$, and $u\notin\operatorname{Frac}(S)$. Consequently $u\notin\operatorname{Frac}(B_{\mathrm{cris}})$. The statement does not assert existence of $y$ or transcendence of $u$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The valued closure, compatible primitive cyclotomic system and one element $y$ satisfying the displayed assumptions.

[F1] The Fontaine map $\theta:W\to A$ is surjective and has a specified set-theoretic section $\sigma:A\to W$. [[lem-choice-free-fontaine-map-surjectivity-for-the-padic-completion]]

[F2] $R$ is a complete separated DVR containing $W[1/p]$, its residue field is $C$, and the generator of $\ker\theta$ is a uniformizer. [[lem-choice-free-de-rham-period-completion-is-a-discrete-valuation-ring]]

[F3] The cyclotomic kernel generator is regular and the tilt is a valuation domain. [[lem-cyclotomic-tilt-valuation-and-fontaine-principal-kernel]]

[F4] The actual completed crystalline ring is the $p$-adic completion of the divided-power algebra generated by $\xi_0^m/m!$ for the cyclotomic kernel generator $\xi_0$, embeds in $R$, and has the finite-quotient bound stated in steps 3.2–5.1 of the completed embedding proof. [[lem-choice-free-crystalline-period-completion-and-de-rham-map]] [[lem-choice-free-completed-crystalline-embedding-in-the-de-rham-ring]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** compare the finite de Rham leading coefficients of an auxiliary power-series ring and use one exceptional logarithm denominator.

1.1 **Ring and filtration.** Finite coefficient convolutions show that sums and products of two displayed series are again displayed series. If $z=\sum a_n\beta^n\in S$ has zero residue, then $a_0=\xi b$, and moving $a_0=p\beta b$ into the next term shows $z\in\beta S$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. Iterating only finitely many times gives $S\cap\beta^iR=\beta^iS$. The leading-coefficient map $\theta_i:\beta^iS\to A$, $\beta^iv\mapsto\theta(v)$, is well defined because $R$ is a domain, and is surjective because $\theta$ is. [F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra]

1.2 **A leading coefficient modulo $p$, without countable choice.** Fix one explicit series $a=\sum b_n\beta^n\in S$. If every $\theta(b_n)\in pA$, define, for all $n$, $c_n=\sigma(\theta(b_n)/p)$ and $d_n=(b_n-pc_n)/\xi$. Division is unique because $\xi$ is regular. Then $$ a=p\left(\sum_{n\ge0}c_n\beta^n+\sum_{n\ge0}d_n\beta^{n+1}\right)\in pS. $$ The specified function $\sigma$ supplies these coefficients simultaneously. Hence if $a\notin pS$, its selected representation has a least index $i$ for which $\theta(b_i)\notin pA$. For each of the finitely many indices below $i$, the same division gives $b_n=p(c_n+\beta d_n)$. Thus $a=p a'+\beta^i v$ for $a',v\in S$ with $\theta(v)=\theta(b_i)\notin pA$. [F1, F3, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Algebraic divided powers.** Both $\xi_0$ and $\xi$ generate $\ker\theta$, so $\xi_0=v\xi$ for a unit $v\in W$. For every $m$, $v_p(m!)\le m$, hence $\xi_0^m/m!=v^m(p^m/m!)\beta^m\in S$. The finite algebra $D$ generated by these divided powers over $W$ is therefore contained in $S$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Separation.** If $z\in\bigcap_{r\ge0}p^rS$, its residue in $A$ lies in $\bigcap_r p^rA=0$. Suppose inductively that $z\in\beta^iR$. For every $r$, write $z=p^rz_r$ with $z_r\in S$, one choice for that fixed $r$. Since $p$ is a unit in $R$, $z_r\in\beta^iR$, hence $z_r\in\beta^iS$ by step 1.1. Thus the $i$-th de Rham leading coefficient of $z$ lies in $p^rA$ for every $r$, and is zero. Consequently $z\in\beta^{i+1}R$. The induction is pointwise and chooses no sequence of $z_r$. As $R$ is separated, $z=0$. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Finite quotient closure.** For $n\ge1$ put $T_n=W/\xi^nW$, $M_n=p^{-(n-1)}T_n$ and let $S_n$ be the image of $S$ in $R/\beta^nR$. Truncating a series gives $S_n\subseteq M_n$, while constant series give $T_n\subseteq S_n$. In the p-adic topology of $M_n$, $T_n=p^{n-1}M_n$ is open; hence its containing subgroup $S_n$ is both open and closed. To bind the required crystalline finite quotient without importing a proof-only statement, put $F_n=D\cap\xi_0^nW[1/p]$. Finite elimination of coefficients by the principal kernel gives $F_n=\sum_{m\ge n}W(\xi_0^m/m!)$: at each index below $n$, its coefficient has zero theta image and can be divided by $\xi_0$ and absorbed into the next divided power. The quotient $E_n=D/F_n$ embeds in $W[1/p]/\xi_0^nW[1/p]$ and is spanned by the first $n$ divided powers. There is a canonical short exact sequence $0\to A\to E_n\to E_{n-1}\to0$ whose kernel sends a lift $w$ of $a\in A$ to $w\xi_0^{n-1}/(n-1)!$; changing the lift by $\xi_0$ changes this by an element of $F_n$. It is injective by its leading coefficient. Since $p$ is regular on $E_{n-1}$, reductions preserve left exactness; finite induction from the p-complete separated ring $E_1=A$ proves each $E_n$ p-complete and separated, lifting only one fixed residue at a time. Hence the quotient map $D\to E_n$ uniquely extends to $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$, and the recorded finite-quotient construction identifies its composite with the de Rham component. Because $\xi_0$ is a unit times $\xi$, this image lies in $p^{-h_n}T_n\subseteq M_n$, where $h_n=v_p((n-1)!)\le n-1$. For one fixed $r$, choose a finite $d_r\in D$ representing $x\bmod p^r$; their images differ by an element of $p^rM_n$. By step 2.1 the image of $d_r$ is in $S_n$, so closedness gives $x\bmod\beta^n\in S_n$. This is a separate finite choice for each assertion, not a selected sequence. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **The logarithm is not rational over $S$.** Let $a\in S\setminus pS$ and suppose $p^r a u\in S$. Choose an integer $j>r$ with $p^j>i$, where $i$ is from step 1.2. Modulo $\beta^{2p^j}R$, every coefficient $p^{j-1}/n$ of $p^{j-1}u$ is $p$-integral except the term $n=p^j$: among positive $n<2p^j$, this is the unique multiple of $p^j$. Thus $$ p^{j-1}u\equiv (-1)^{p^j+1}\beta^{p^j}/p\pmod{S+\beta^{2p^j}R}. $$ The assumed $p^r a u\in S$ gives $p^{j-1}a u\in S$. Substituting $a=pa'+\beta^iv$ and using $i<p^j$, we obtain $\beta^{i+p^j}v/p\in S+\beta^{2p^j}R$. The left side has order at least $i+p^j$, so step 1.1 forces its leading coefficient $\theta(v)/p$ to lie in $A$, a contradiction. Finally if $u=s_1/s_2$ with $s_1,s_2\in S$, $s_2\ne0$, step 2.2 provides a largest $r$ such that $s_2\in p^rS$. Write $s_2=p^ra$ with $a\notin pS$. Then $p^rau=s_1\in S$, contradicting the preceding argument. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 **Coherent coefficients.** More generally let $z\in R$ have image in every $S_n$. Define $a_0=\sigma(\theta(z))\in W$ and $z_1=(z-a_0)/\beta$. For each fixed $n$, one finite representative of $z\bmod\beta^{n+1}$ in $S_{n+1}$ differs from $a_0$ by a residue-zero series; the finite shift argument in step 1.1 divides it by $\beta$ in $S_n$. Thus every quotient of $z_1$ also lies in the corresponding $S_n$. Iterate the specified function $z_i\mapsto(a_i,z_{i+1})$ with $a_i=\sigma(\theta(z_i))$. For every $N$, $z=\sum_{i<N}a_i\beta^i+\beta^Nz_N$, so separatedness and completeness of $R$ give $z=\sum_{i\ge0}a_i\beta^i\in S$. Applying this to $z$ the image of $x\in A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ and step 3.1 gives $A_{\mathrm{cris}}\subseteq S$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Crystalline consequence.** Since $B_{\mathrm{cris}}$ is obtained from the domain $A_{\mathrm{cris}}$ by inverting the nonzero elements $p$ and $t$, both have the same fraction field. Step 4.1 embeds this field in $\operatorname{Frac}(S)$, so step 3.2 proves $u\notin\operatorname{Frac}(B_{\mathrm{cris}})$. No transcendence or existence of $y$ is inferred. [F4, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
