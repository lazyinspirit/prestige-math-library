## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex vector space, let $n\ge0$, and let
$E:=V^{\otimes n}$ carry the left place action of $S_n$
([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]). Write
$W:=\operatorname{End}(V)$ and let
$\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E):=\{F\in\operatorname{End}(E):\sigma F=F\sigma
\text{ for all }\sigma\in S_n\}$ be the centralizer of the action. Then:

1. **(Canonical identification.)** The linear map
   $\Psi:W^{\otimes n}\to\operatorname{End}(E)$ with
   $\Psi(T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_n)(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)
   =T_1v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_nv_n$ on elementary tensors is a linear
   isomorphism, and it is equivariant for the place action of $S_n$ on
   $W^{\otimes n}$ and conjugation $F\mapsto\sigma F\sigma^{-1}$ on
   $\operatorname{End}(E)$. Hence $\Psi$ restricts to an isomorphism from the
   invariant tensors, that is from the image of the symmetrization operator
   $\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\sigma$ on $W^{\otimes n}$ (the $n$-th
   symmetric tensor power of $W$), onto $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$.
2. **(Spans.)** The subspaces $\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}:T\in W\}$
   and $\operatorname{span}\{g^{\otimes n}:g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\}$ of
   $W^{\otimes n}$ are both equal to the invariant tensors; equivalently, with
   $\Delta(T)=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes T\otimes\mathbf 1^{\otimes(n-i)}$
   and $A_n$ the unital $\mathbb C$-subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}(E)$
   generated by all $\Delta(T)$ (the image of the diagonal action of
   $U(\mathfrak{gl}(V))$ as defined there,
   [[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]),
   $$\textstyle\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)=\Psi(\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}:T\in W\})=\Psi(\operatorname{span}\{g^{\otimes n}:g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\})=A_n.$$

All statements include $n=0$, where $W^{\otimes0}=\mathbb C$ and $E=\mathbb C$,
and the case $V=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$, an integer $n\ge0$, $E=V^{\otimes n}$ with its left $S_n$-action, and $W=\operatorname{End}(V)$.

[F1] $\sigma\cdot(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$ defines a left $S_n$-action on $E$ by linear maps, $E=\mathbb C$ for $n=0$; the diagonal operators $\Delta(T)$ and the algebra $A_n$ are as displayed, with $\Delta$ linear in $T$ ([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]).

[F2] If $e_1,\dots,e_d$ is a basis of $V$, the elementary tensors $e_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_n}$ form a basis of $E$, and for $n=0$ the single element $1$ is a basis of $E=\mathbb C$ ([[thm-tensor-product-basis-from-bases]], [[def-linear-basis]]).

[F3] The assignment $(v_1,\dots,v_n)\mapsto v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n$ is multilinear, so linear maps out of $E$ are the same as multilinear maps on $V^n$ ([[cor-finite-iterated-tensor-products-represent-multilinear-maps]]).

[F4] For finite-dimensional $V$ one has $\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{End}(V)=d^2$ with $d=\dim_{\mathbb C}V$, and matrix representation relative to bases is a vector-space isomorphism ([[cor-dimensions-of-matrix-and-linear-map-spaces]], [[thm-matrix-representation-is-a-vector-space-isomorphism]]).

[F5] Every nonzero polynomial over an integral domain of degree $m$ has at most $m$ distinct roots. The characteristic polynomial of $-T$ is $\chi_{-T}(t)=\det(t\,\mathbf 1_V+T)$, a monic polynomial of degree $d=\dim_{\mathbb C}V$; an operator is invertible if and only if its determinant is nonzero ([[thm-root-bound-for-polynomials-over-a-domain]], [[def-characteristic-polynomial-of-an-operator]], [[thm-operator-invertible-iff-determinant-nonzero]]).

[F6] $\mathbb C$ is an infinite field.

[F7] In $n$ variables the elementary symmetric polynomial $e_n$ is $x_1\cdots x_n$, with $e_0=1$ for $n=0$; if $n!\,1_R$ is a unit in a commutative ring $R$, then $e_n$ is a polynomial in the power sums $\sum_i x_i^k$ with coefficients in $R$ ([[def-elementary-symmetric-polynomials]], [[cor-power-sums-generate-when-factorial-is-invertible]]).

[F8] A unital ring homomorphism from a polynomial ring on generators $x_1,\dots,x_m$ over a commutative ring $R$ into a commutative $R$-algebra $S$ exists uniquely with prescribed images of the $x_i$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 [construct] Fix $T_1,\dots,T_n\in W$. For fixed $(v_1,\dots,v_n)\in V^n$ the assignment $T_i\mapsto T_iv_i$ is linear in each variable, so $(T_1,\dots,T_n)\mapsto T_1v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_nv_n$ is multilinear in the $T_i$ and its values in $E$ are linear in $(v_1,\dots,v_n)$ separately in each $v_i$; hence there is a unique linear map $\Phi(T_1,\dots,T_n)\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ with the displayed value on every elementary tensor by [F3], and $\Phi$ is multilinear in the $T_i$ because both sides of every identity are checked on the spanning elementary tensors by [F3]. By the representing property of the tensor product [F3] there is a unique linear $\Psi:W^{\otimes n}\to\operatorname{End}(E)$ with $\Psi(T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_n)=\Phi(T_1,\dots,T_n)$, which is the map of the Statement; in particular $\Psi$ is linear and is given on elementary tensors as displayed. For $n=0$ one has $W^{\otimes0}=\mathbb C$ and $\Psi(1)=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}$ by the same universal property. [F1, F3, construct, algebra]

1.2 Let $p:=\frac1{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\sigma\in\operatorname{End}(W^{\otimes n})$, a well-defined operator because $n!\ne0$ in $\mathbb C$; then $p^2=p$, since each group element occurs $n!$ times in the product sum, and $p$ commutes with the action, since left multiplication permutes the summands. Its image is exactly the invariant subspace $(W^{\otimes n})^{S_n}$: $pX$ is invariant for every $X$, and $pX=X$ whenever $X$ is invariant. Thus the invariant tensors are the image of the symmetrization operator. Pure tensors span $W^{\otimes n}$, so their symmetrizations span the image of $p$. For $T_1,\dots,T_n\in W$, inclusion-exclusion gives $$\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}T_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{\sigma(n)} =\sum_{J\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}}(-1)^{n-|J|}\Bigl(\sum_{j\in J}T_j\Bigr)^{\otimes n}.$$ Indeed, after expanding the right side, an ordered tensor survives exactly when every index $1,\dots,n$ occurs, which in a tensor with $n$ factors means each occurs once. Hence each symmetrized pure tensor is a linear combination of powers $T^{\otimes n}$, and $(W^{\otimes n})^{S_n}=\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}:T\in W\}$. For $n=0$ this equality is immediate in $W^{\otimes0}=\mathbb C$. [F1, algebra, construct]

1.3 The two spans of the Statement are equal. One inclusion is clear, since $\operatorname{GL}(V)\subseteq W$. For the reverse let $T\in W$ and consider the $W^{\otimes n}$-valued polynomial $t\mapsto(T+t\,\mathbf 1_V)^{\otimes n}$; expanding the tensor power of a sum gives $(T+t\,\mathbf 1_V)^{\otimes n}=\sum_{k=0}^nt^kc_k$ with $c_0=T^{\otimes n}$, so it is a polynomial of degree at most $n$ in $t$. The function $t\mapsto\det(t\,\mathbf 1_V+T)$ is a monic polynomial of degree $d=\dim_{\mathbb C}V$ by [F5], hence nonzero with at most $d$ roots by [F5], so by [F6] there exist $n+1$ distinct scalars $t_1,\dots,t_{n+1}$ with $T+t_i\mathbf 1_V$ invertible for all $i$ by [F5] (if $d=0$ then $W=\{0\}$ and $T=0$ is invertible, so any distinct scalars work). For the Lagrange polynomials $L_i(t):=\prod_{j\ne i}(t-t_j)/(t_i-t_j)$ one has $t^k=\sum_i t_i^kL_i(t)$ for every $t$ and every $0\le k\le n$, because it holds at the $n+1$ distinct points and both sides have degree at most $n$; multiplying by $c_k$ and summing gives $T^{\otimes n}=c_0=\sum_iL_i(0)\,(T+t_i\mathbf 1_V)^{\otimes n}\in\operatorname{span}\{g^{\otimes n}:g\in\operatorname{GL}(V)\}$. Hence the two spans are equal. [F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 The matrix units of $W$ with respect to a basis $e_1,\dots,e_d$ of $V$ form a basis of $W$: define $E_{ab}\in W$ by $E_{ab}e_c:=\delta_{bc}e_a$; every $S\in W$ satisfies $S=\sum_{a,b}s_{ab}E_{ab}$ with $s_{ab}$ the coefficients in $S(e_b)=\sum_as_{ab}e_a$, because the two sides agree on each basis vector of $V$, and a relation $\sum_{a,b}\lambda_{ab}E_{ab}=0$ evaluated at $e_b$ gives $\sum_a\lambda_{ab}e_a=0$, so all $\lambda_{ab}=0$. Likewise the endomorphisms $f_{ab}$ of $E$ defined by $f_{ab}(e_{c_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{c_n}):=\delta_{b_1c_1}\cdots\delta_{b_nc_n}\,e_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_n}$ for words $a,b\in[d]^n$ form a basis of $\operatorname{End}(E)$, indexed by the finite set $[d]^n\times[d]^n$; for $n=0$ this is the single endomorphism $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}$ of the one-dimensional space $\mathbb C$, and for $d=0$ and $n\ge1$ all these sets of words are empty and both spaces are zero. By [F2] and [F3] the $d^{2n}$ tensors $E_{a_1b_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes E_{a_nb_n}$ form a basis of $W^{\otimes n}$, and $\Psi$ sends such a tensor to $f_{ab}$ by the formula of step 1.1; a linear map that carries a basis bijectively onto a basis is an isomorphism, so $\Psi$ is a linear isomorphism. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 $\Psi$ is $S_n$-equivariant for the place action on $W^{\otimes n}$ and conjugation on $\operatorname{End}(E)$: for $\sigma\in S_n$ and $T_1,\dots,T_n\in W$ the place action gives $\sigma\cdot(T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_n)=T_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$ by [F1], so by step 1.1 both $\Psi(\sigma\cdot(T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_n))$ and $\sigma\Psi(T_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_n)\sigma^{-1}$ map $v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n$ to $T_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes T_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}v_n$; two linear maps agreeing on the spanning elementary tensors agree, so the identity $\Psi(\sigma\cdot X)=\sigma\Psi(X)\sigma^{-1}$ holds for all $X\in W^{\otimes n}$ by linearity. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 For $T\in W$ one has $\Psi(T^{\otimes n})\in A_n$. For $n=0$ this is $\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}\in A_0$, since $A_0$ is unital. Assume $n\ge1$ and put $T_i:=\mathbf 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes T\otimes\mathbf 1^{\otimes(n-i)}\in\operatorname{End}(E)$ for $1\le i\le n$; these operators commute pairwise and $\Psi(T^{\otimes n})=T_1T_2\cdots T_n$, while $\sum_{i=1}^nT_i^k=\Delta(T^k)$ for every $k\ge1$, all by the tensor formula of step 1.1 and [F1]. By [F7] with $R=\mathbb C$ (where $n!$ is a unit) there is a polynomial $Q$ in $n$ variables over $\mathbb C$ with $e_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)=Q(p_1,\dots,p_n)$ in $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, where $p_k=\sum_i x_i^k$. The operators $T_1,\dots,T_n$ commute, so the $\mathbb C$-subalgebra they generate is commutative, and the universal property of the polynomial ring [F8] gives a $\mathbb C$-algebra homomorphism sending $x_i\mapsto T_i$; it sends $e_n(x_1,\dots,x_n)$ to $T_1\cdots T_n$ by [F7] and $p_k$ to $\Delta(T^k)$, so $T_1\cdots T_n=Q(\Delta(T),\dots,\Delta(T^n))\in A_n$, since each $\Delta(T^k)$ lies in the generating algebra $A_n$ and $Q$ has coefficients in $\mathbb C$. [F1, F7, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $T\in W$ put $d_T:=\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes T\otimes\mathbf 1^{\otimes(n-i)}\in W^{\otimes n}$ (the empty sum for $n=0$, giving $d_T=0\in\mathbb C$). Then $\Psi(d_T)=\Delta(T)$ by step 1.1, and $d_T$ is invariant under the place action: a permutation sends the $i$-th summand, which has $T$ in position $i$, to the same kind of summand with $T$ in position $\sigma(i)$ by [F1], and $\sigma$ permutes the index set, so $\sigma\cdot d_T=d_T$. By the equivariance of step 2.2, $\Delta(T)=\Psi(d_T)$ is fixed by conjugation by every $\sigma\in S_n$, that is $\Delta(T)\in\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$; since $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$ is closed under addition and composition and $A_n$ is generated by the $\Delta(T)$, this gives $A_n\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Combining steps: $\Psi$ induces a bijection from $(W^{\otimes n})^{S_n}$ onto $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$ by step 2.2 and step 2.1, the invariant subspace is $\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}\}=\operatorname{span}\{g^{\otimes n}\}$ by steps 1.2 and 1.3, and $\Psi(\operatorname{span}\{T^{\otimes n}\})\subseteq A_n\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)$ by steps 3.1 and 2.3, so all four subspaces of the Statement coincide. For $n=0$: $W^{\otimes0}=E=\mathbb C$, $\Psi$ is the identity, $S_0$ is trivial, $T^{\otimes0}=1$ for every $T$, $\Delta(T)=0$ and $A_0=\mathbb C\,\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}$, and the displayed chain reads $\mathbb C=\mathbb C=\mathbb C=\mathbb C$. For $V=0$ and $n\ge1$: $W=0$, $E=0$, both sides of the identity are the zero space, and $A_n=0$. This proves both claims. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 1.3, step 2.3, discharge-construct] ∎

## Remarks

- **What is used where.** The basis argument of step 2.1 identifies $\operatorname{End}(V^{\otimes n})$ with $W^{\otimes n}$; steps 1.2 and 1.3 are the polarization step, expressing an arbitrary invariant tensor through the powers $T^{\otimes n}$ and then through powers of invertible operators; step 2.3 is the Newton-identity argument identifying the algebra generated by the place operators $\Delta(T)$ with those powers. The interpolation in step 1.3 is where the field $\mathbb C$ is used as an infinite field of characteristic zero; the statement is false in characteristic $p\le n$, where $n!$ is not invertible.

- **The zero and empty cases.** For $n=0$ the symmetric tensor power is the ground field and the whole claim degenerates to $\mathbb C=\mathbb C$; for $V=0$ and $n\ge1$ both $E$ and $W^{\otimes n}$ are the zero space, so $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)=A_n=0$. These are the only cases in which the basis argument of step 2.1 has no words $a,b$.

- **Relation to the double centralizer.** The equality $\operatorname{End}_{S_n}(E)=A_n$ identifies the commutant of the $\mathbb C[S_n]$-image with the image of the diagonal $\mathfrak{gl}(V)$-action. The converse commutant equality is the other half of the double-centralizer statement proved on this page ([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]).

- **No choice.** All bases, matrix units and the finitely many scalars $t_1,\dots,t_{n+1}$ are chosen from explicit finite or countable ranges; the multilinear universal property of [F3] produces the maps without any selection, and no selection principle is used.
