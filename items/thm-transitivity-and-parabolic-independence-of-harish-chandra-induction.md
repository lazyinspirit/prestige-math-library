## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $q$ be a prime power and put $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with
diagonal torus $T$ and standard basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$. Recall that an ordered
partition $\gamma=(S_1,\dots,S_r)$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ defines the coordinate
parabolic $P_\gamma=L_\gamma\ltimes U_\gamma$ of $G$, and that $L_\gamma$
depends only on the unordered collection of blocks, while $P_\gamma$ and
$U_\gamma$ depend on their order
([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]]); for a composition
$\alpha$ this is the standard parabolic $P_\alpha=L_\alpha\ltimes U_\alpha$
([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]],
[[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]]).

1. **Transitivity.** Let $\delta$ and $\gamma$ be ordered partitions of
   $\{1,\dots,n\}$ such that every block of $\gamma$ is a union of blocks of
   $\delta$ and
   $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\le\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$ whenever
   $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)<\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$ (that is, the
   order of the blocks of $\delta$ refines the order of the blocks of
   $\gamma$), so that
   $K:=L_\delta\le L:=L_\gamma$, $P_\delta=K\ltimes U_\delta\le P_\gamma=L\ltimes U_\gamma$
   and $U_\gamma\le U_\delta$. Then $L\cap P_\delta=K\ltimes(L\cap U_\delta)$ is a
   coordinate parabolic of $L$, and there are isomorphisms of functors
   $$R_K^{G,P_\delta}\;\cong\;R_L^{G,P_\gamma}\circ R_K^{L,L\cap P_\delta}, \qquad {}^*\!R_K^{G,P_\delta}\;\cong\;{}^*\!R_K^{L,L\cap P_\delta}\circ{}^*\!R_L^{G,P_\gamma},$$
   where each $R$ and ${}^*\!R$ is taken with respect to the displayed parabolic
   ([[def-harish-chandra-induction-and-restriction-for-finite-gl-n]]).
2. **Independence of the parabolic.** Let $\gamma=(S_1,\dots,S_r)$ be an ordered
   partition with blocks $S_1,\dots,S_r$ and let $\gamma'$ be the ordered
   partition obtained by listing the same blocks in another order, so that
   $L_{\gamma'}=L_\gamma=:L$ but $P_{\gamma'}$ need not equal $P_\gamma$. Then
   over $\mathbb C$ there are isomorphisms of functors on complex $L$-modules
   $$R_L^{G,P_\gamma}\;\cong\;R_L^{G,P_{\gamma'}}, \qquad {}^*\!R_L^{G,P_\gamma}\;\cong\;{}^*\!R_L^{G,P_{\gamma'}};$$
   equivalently, $R_L^{G,P_\gamma}W\cong R_L^{G,P_{\gamma'}}W$ for every complex
   $L$-module $W$, and $X^{U_\gamma}\cong X^{U_{\gamma'}}$ as $L$-modules for
   every complex $G$-module $X$. The isomorphisms are built from a choice of
   double-coset representatives and of isomorphisms on simple $L$-modules and
   need not be canonical; all the choices are finite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, a prime power $q$, the group $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with diagonal torus $T$, ordered partitions $\gamma=(S_1,\dots,S_r)$ and $\gamma'$ of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ with the same blocks, a refinement $\delta$ of $\gamma$ as in claim 1, and complex modules $W$ over $L_\gamma$ and $X$ over $G$.

[L1] For a coordinate parabolic $P=L_P\ltimes U_P$ of $G$ and a complex $L_P$-module $W$ one has $R_{L_P}^{G,P}(W)=\operatorname{Ind}_{P}^G(\operatorname{Inf}_{L_P}^PW)=\mathbb C[G/U_P]\otimes_{\mathbb C L_P}W$, the induced module being realised as the set of functions $f:G\to W$ with $f(g\,p)=\pi(p)^{-1}f(g)$ for all $g\in G$, $p\in P$, where $\pi:P\to L_P$ is the projection with kernel $U_P$; for a complex $G$-module $X$ one has ${}^*\!R_{L_P}^{G,P}(X)=X^{U_P}$ with $L_P$ acting through $P/U_P\cong L_P$; both constructions are functorial, $R$ commutes with finite direct sums in $W$, and for $L_P=G$ and the trivial parabolic $P=G$ both functors are the identity. The tensor model follows from [[rem-induced-representation-agrees-with-the-tensor-product-model]]: imposing $gu\otimes w=g\otimes w$ for $u\in U_P$, which acts trivially on the inflation, identifies $\mathbb C[G]\otimes_{\mathbb CP}W$ with $\mathbb C[G/U_P]\otimes_{\mathbb CL_P}W$ ([[def-harish-chandra-induction-and-restriction-for-finite-gl-n]], [[def-induced-r-linear-g-module-by-h-covariant-functions]], [[def-g-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L2] For an ordered partition $\eta=(T_1,\dots,T_s)$ the subgroups $P_\eta,L_\eta,U_\eta$ are given by the entry conditions $g_{kl}=0$ when $\operatorname{blk}_\eta(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\eta(l)$ for $P_\eta$, when $\operatorname{blk}_\eta(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\eta(l)$ for $L_\eta$, and when $k\ne l$ and $\operatorname{blk}_\eta(k)\ge\operatorname{blk}_\eta(l)$, with $g_{kk}=1$, for $U_\eta$; $P_\eta=L_\eta\ltimes U_\eta$, $U_\eta\trianglelefteq P_\eta$, $L_\eta\cap U_\eta=\{I_n\}$, $L_\eta$ is the group of block diagonal, equivalently preserving each coordinate block subspace, invertible matrices of that type, $N\cap P_\eta=T\{\,P_\rho:\rho\in W_\eta\,\}$ for the monomial subgroup $N$ and the parabolic Weyl group $W_\eta$, and $P_\eta$ is the stabiliser of the coordinate flag with successive spans of the blocks $T_1,\dots,T_s$ ([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]], [[thm-levi-decomposition-of-standard-parabolics-in-gl-n-fq]], [[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[L3] Two indices lying in a common block of $\delta$ lie in a common block of $\gamma$ (each block of $\gamma$ is a union of blocks of $\delta$), so $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)=\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$ implies $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)=\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$; and the order hypothesis of claim 1 says that $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)<\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$ implies $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\le\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$, which applied with the roles of $k$ and $l$ exchanged gives $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$ implies $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\ge\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$. Hence $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$ forces $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$ and then $\operatorname{blk}_\delta(k)>\operatorname{blk}_\delta(l)$; consequently $P_\delta\le P_\gamma$, $L_\delta\le L_\gamma$ and $U_\gamma\le U_\delta$, and a matrix that is block diagonal for $\delta$ is block diagonal for $\gamma$ ([[def-coordinate-parabolics-for-ordered-partitions]]).

[L4] For complex $L_P$-modules $V$ and complex $G$-modules $Y$ there is a natural isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_{L_P}^{G,P}V,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{L_P}(V,{}^*\!R_{L_P}^{G,P}Y)$ ([[thm-harish-chandra-adjunction-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[L5] Let $P=L\ltimes U$ and $Q=L\ltimes V$ be coordinate parabolics of $G$ with the same coordinate Levi $L$, let $\mathcal D$ be any fixed set of permutation representatives $\rho$ of the $(W_L,W_L)$-double cosets of $S_n$, put $w_\rho=P_\rho$, $C_\rho=L\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$, $D_\rho=U\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$ and $A_\rho=L\cap w_\rho Vw_\rho^{-1}$, and let $X$ be a complex $L$-module. Then $C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho=P\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$, $C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho=L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$, and there is an isomorphism of complex $L$-modules ${}^*\!R_L^{G,P}(R_L^{G,Q}X)\cong\bigoplus_{\rho\in\mathcal D}R_{C_\rho}^{L,L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}}\bigl(({}^\rho X)^{D_\rho}\bigr)$, where ${}^\rho X$ carries the $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$-action $m\cdot x=(w_\rho^{-1}mw_\rho)\cdot x$ ([[thm-parabolic-mackey-formula-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[L6] Over $\mathbb C$, every finite-dimensional module of a finite group is completely reducible, its isotypic decomposition is unique, and for a simple module $S$ the multiplicity of $S$ in a completely reducible module $V$ is the inner product $\langle\chi_V,\chi_S\rangle$; consequently two finite-dimensional completely reducible complex modules with equal characters are isomorphic. Also $\operatorname{End}_L(S)=\mathbb C$ for every simple finite-dimensional complex $L$-module $S$ ([[cor-endomorphisms-of-an-irreducible-over-an-algebraically-closed-field-are-scalars]]) ([[thm-maschkes-theorem-for-finite-groups-over-fields-whose-characteristic-does-not-divide-the-group-order]], [[thm-isotypic-decomposition-of-a-completely-reducible-representation-is-unique]], [[cor-multiplicity-of-an-irreducible-summand-is-a-character-inner-product]]).

[L7] The standard inner product $\langle\chi,\psi\rangle_G=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g)\overline{\psi(g)}$ on complex class functions of a finite group is a positive definite Hermitian form, $\langle\chi_V,\chi_W\rangle_G=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(W,V)$ for finite-dimensional complex modules, and $\langle\chi,\chi\rangle_G=0$ forces $\chi=0$ ([[def-standard-inner-product-on-complex-class-functions]], [[thm-character-inner-product-computes-intertwiner-dimension]]).

[L8] For subgroups $H,K\le G$ the cosets $gU=\{gu:u\in U\}$ partition $G$, intersections of subgroups and conjugation of subgroups are subgroups, normality of $U$ in $P$ means $pUp^{-1}=U$ for all $p\in P$, a left action satisfies $e\cdot x=x$ and $(gh)\cdot x=g\cdot(h\cdot x)$, a complex $G$-module is a complex vector space with a linear action of $G$, and a balanced product of a right $L$-set and a left $L$-set is the quotient by $(x\cdot l,y)\sim(x,l\cdot y)$ ([[def-coset]], [[def-subgroup]], [[def-normal-subgroup]], [[def-group-action]], [[def-g-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]).


[L9] Ordinary induction is transitive for subgroups of a finite group ([[thm-transitivity-of-induction-for-finite-groups]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The refinement factorisation.** Let $v\in U_\delta$. Its blocks of rows and columns for $\gamma$ are unions of blocks for $\delta$ by [L3], so we may write $v=A\,D$, where $D$ is the $\gamma$-block diagonal matrix obtained from $v$ by replacing every entry lying in an off-diagonal $\gamma$-block $(k,l)$ (that is $\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(k)\ne\operatorname{blk}_\gamma(l)$) by $0$, and $A:=vD^{-1}$. Each diagonal $\gamma$-block of $v$ is itself a $\delta$-block upper unitriangular matrix (after ordering coordinates by their $\delta$-blocks, its diagonal blocks are identities and its blocks below them vanish), so it is invertible and $D$ is invertible; $D$ is block diagonal for $\gamma$ and lies in $U_\delta$, hence $D\in L_\gamma\cap U_\delta$, while $A=vD^{-1}$ is the product of two $\gamma$-block upper triangular matrices (by [L2] applied to $v$ and to the block diagonal matrix $D^{-1}$), so $A$ is $\gamma$-block upper triangular with identity diagonal blocks, that is $A\in U_\gamma$. Every $v\in U_\delta$ therefore lies in $U_\gamma\cdot(L_\gamma\cap U_\delta)$, and since $U_\gamma\le U_\delta$ and $L_\gamma\cap U_\delta\le U_\delta$ the product is contained in $U_\delta$, so $U_\delta=U_\gamma\,(L_\gamma\cap U_\delta)$; the intersection $U_\gamma\cap(L_\gamma\cap U_\delta)=L_\gamma\cap U_\gamma$ is trivial by [L2], the factorisation is unique because $D$ is the diagonal part of $v$, and $U_\gamma\trianglelefteq U_\delta$ because $U_\delta\le P_\gamma$ by [L3] and $U_\gamma\trianglelefteq P_\gamma$ by [L2]; hence $U_\delta=U_\gamma\rtimes(L_\gamma\cap U_\delta)$. [L2, L3, algebra]

1.2 **The induction hypothesis and products.** Prove claim 2 for $\operatorname{GL}_m(\mathbb F_q)$ by strong induction on $m$. For $m=1$, and also whenever $L=G$, the only coordinate parabolic with Levi $L$ is $G$, so both functors are identities. Assume the result for degrees less than $m$. For $H=H_1\times H_2$ and $P_i=L_i\ltimes U_i\le H_i$, the bijection $H/(U_1\times U_2)\to(H_1/U_1)\times(H_2/U_2)$ is equivariant for left $H$ and right $L_1\times L_2$. Linearising gives the exterior tensor product of the two induction bimodules of [L1]. A natural isomorphism between induction functors for two parabolics in $H_i$, evaluated on the regular $\mathbb C L_i$-module, is an isomorphism of $(H_i,L_i)$-bimodules: naturality for right multiplication by each element of $L_i$ proves right equivariance. Tensoring these bimodule isomorphisms and then tensoring over $\mathbb C[L_1\times L_2]$ with any module proves independence of induction in $H$ for arbitrary modules, not only exterior products. Adjunction [L4], applied factorwise, gives natural isomorphisms between the represented Hom functors for the two restrictions in $H$. Evaluating these isomorphisms and their inverses at identity maps gives inverse module maps, proving independence of restriction in $H$. Iterating this construction shows that the induction hypothesis applies to a proper coordinate Levi of $\operatorname{GL}_m$, since each of its factors has degree strictly smaller than $m$, although the sum of their degrees remains $m$. [L1, L4, L8, given, induction, algebra]

1.3 **The intersected parabolics and the pairing formula.** Let $P=L\ltimes U=P_\gamma$ and $Q=L\ltimes V=P_{\gamma'}$ be as in claim 2, let $X=\lambda$ be an irreducible complex $L$-module, and let $\mathcal D$, $w_\rho$, $C_\rho$, $D_\rho$, $A_\rho$ be as in [L5]. Then $\operatorname{Hom}_G\bigl(R_L^{G,P}\lambda,R_L^{G,Q}\lambda\bigr)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_L\bigl(\lambda,{}^*\!R_L^{G,P}(R_L^{G,Q}\lambda)\bigr)$ by adjunction [L4], and expanding the restriction by the Mackey formula [L5] turns this into a direct sum, so that $$\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G\bigl(R_L^{G,P}\lambda,R_L^{G,Q}\lambda\bigr)=\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal D}T_\rho(P,Q),\qquad T_\rho(P,Q):=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_L\bigl(\lambda,\;R_{C_\rho}^{L,L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}}\bigl(({}^\rho\lambda)^{D_\rho}\bigr)\bigr),$$ where $({}^\rho\lambda)^{D_\rho}={}^*\!R_{C_\rho}^{w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1},P\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}}({}^\rho\lambda)$ is the $C_\rho$-module obtained by restricting from $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$ along the parabolic $P\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho$ of [L5], and the outer induction is taken along the parabolic $L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho\ltimes A_\rho$; they are parabolics of $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$ and $L$, respectively, with common Levi $C_\rho$ by [L5]. The same formula with $Q$ replaced by $P$ computes $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,P}\lambda,R_L^{G,P}\lambda)$. [L4, L5, algebra]

1.4 **The terms with $C_\rho=L$.** Suppose $C_\rho=L$, that is $w_\rho\in N_G(L)$, equivalently $\rho$ lies in the normaliser $N_{S_n}(W_L)$ of the parabolic Weyl group. Then $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}=L$ and [L5] gives $D_\rho=U\cap L=\{I_n\}$ and $A_\rho=L\cap w_\rho Vw_\rho^{-1}=w_\rho(L\cap V)w_\rho^{-1}=\{I_n\}$; the parabolic $L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}$ is $L$ itself, so $R_{C_\rho}^{L,L}=R_L^{L,L}$ is the identity functor and $({}^\rho\lambda)^{D_\rho}$ is the module ${}^\rho\lambda$ with underlying space $\lambda$ and $L$-action $l\cdot x=(w_\rho^{-1}lw_\rho)\cdot x$. Hence $T_\rho(P,Q)=\dim\operatorname{Hom}_L(\lambda,{}^\rho\lambda)$, which is $1$ if $\lambda\cong{}^\rho\lambda$ and $0$ otherwise; in particular this term depends only on $L$, $\lambda$ and $\rho$, and not on the parabolics $P$ and $Q$. [L1, L2, L5, L6, algebra]

2.1 **The bijection between coset sets.** Let $L=L_\gamma$, $K=L_\delta$ and $H:=L\cap U_\delta$. By step 1.1, $U_\delta=U_\gamma\rtimes H$, and $K$ normalises $H$ because $K\ltimes H=L\cap P_\delta$ is a parabolic of $L$ by [L2] and [L3]. Form the balanced product $G/U_\gamma\times_L L/H$ of the right $L$-set $G/U_\gamma$ and the left $L$-set $L/H$ of [L8]. The map $$\Phi:[gU_\gamma,lH]\longmapsto glU_\delta$$ is well defined: changing $g$ to $gu$ for $u\in U_\gamma$ does not change the image since $l^{-1}ul\in U_\gamma\subseteq U_\delta$ by [L2]; changing $l$ to $lh$ for $h\in H$ does not change it since $h\in U_\delta$; and the balancing relation $(gl_0U_\gamma,lH)\sim(gU_\gamma,l_0lH)$ has the same image by associativity. It is surjective since $gU_\delta=\Phi([gU_\gamma,H])$. To prove injectivity, suppose $glU_\delta=g'l'U_\delta$, so $gl=g'l'u_\delta$ for some $u_\delta\in U_\delta$. Write $u_\delta=uh$ with $u\in U_\gamma$, $h\in H$ by step 1.1. Then $g=g'l'u h l^{-1}$, and $l'u(l')^{-1}\in U_\gamma$ by [L2], so $gU_\gamma=g'l'h l^{-1}U_\gamma$. Balancing by $l_0=l'h l^{-1}\in L$ gives $$[gU_\gamma,lH]=[g'U_\gamma,l'hH]=[g'U_\gamma,l'H],$$ proving injectivity. The map is equivariant for left multiplication by $G$. It is also equivariant for the right $K$-actions $[gU_\gamma,lH]\cdot k:=[gU_\gamma,lkH]$ and $(glU_\delta)\cdot k:=glkU_\delta$: the first is well defined since $K$ normalises $H$, and both images are $glkU_\delta$. [L2, L3, L8, step 1.1]

2.2 **The remaining terms.** Suppose $C_\rho\ne L$. Then $C_\rho$ is a proper Levi subgroup of $L$, and $L$ is a product of general linear groups over $\mathbb F_q$; by step 1.2 the induction hypothesis applies in $L$, so the functors $R_{C_\rho}^{L,L\cap w_\rho Qw_\rho^{-1}}$ and $R_{C_\rho}^{L,L\cap w_\rho Pw_\rho^{-1}}$ are isomorphic, both parabolics having Levi $C_\rho$ by [L5]. Applying the isomorphism to the module $({}^\rho\lambda)^{D_\rho}$ shows that the term $T_\rho(P,Q)$ of step 1.3 equals the corresponding term $T_\rho(P,P)$ in which $R_L^{G,Q}$ is replaced by $R_L^{G,P}$; the module $({}^\rho\lambda)^{D_\rho}$ is unchanged in this replacement because $D_\rho$ refers to the first parabolic $P$ in both cases. Likewise, the induction hypothesis applied in the group $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$, whose factors, like those of the proper Levi $L$, each have degree smaller than $n$, shows that the functors ${}^*\!R_{C_\rho}^{w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1},P\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}}$ and ${}^*\!R_{C_\rho}^{w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1},Q\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}}$ from $w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}$ to $C_\rho$ are isomorphic, both parabolics $P\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho$ and $Q\cap w_\rho Lw_\rho^{-1}=C_\rho\ltimes D_\rho'$ having Levi $C_\rho$ by [L5]; hence $T_\rho(P,P)=T_\rho(Q,Q)$, and the two applications together give $T_\rho(P,Q)=T_\rho(P,P)=T_\rho(Q,Q)$ for every $\rho\in\mathcal D$. [L1, L5, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 **Transitivity of Harish-Chandra induction.** Let $W$ be a complex $K$-module. Inflating through the surjection $P_\delta\to K$ and inducing in two steps gives $\operatorname{Ind}_{P_\delta}^{G}(\operatorname{Inf}_K^{P_\delta}W)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{P_\gamma}^{G}\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{P_\delta}^{P_\gamma}(\operatorname{Inf}_K^{P_\delta}W)\bigr)$ by transitivity of ordinary induction [L9], and $\operatorname{Ind}_{P_\delta}^{P_\gamma}(\operatorname{Inf}_K^{P_\delta}W)\cong \mathbb C[P_\gamma/U_\delta]\otimes_{\mathbb C K}W$ as $P_\gamma$-modules. Restricting the bijection $\Phi$ of step 2.1 to $P_\gamma/U_\delta\subseteq G/U_\delta$ gives a $(P_\gamma,K)$-equivariant bijection $P_\gamma/U_\delta\cong P_\gamma/U_\gamma\times_L L/(L\cap U_\delta)$: well-definedness and equivariance are inherited, and every $(pU_\gamma,l(L\cap U_\delta))$ with $p\in P_\gamma$, $l\in L$ has $\Phi(pU_\gamma,l(L\cap U_\delta))=plU_\delta$ with $pl\in P_\gamma L=P_\gamma$, since $P_\gamma=LU_\gamma$ by [L2]. Therefore $\mathbb C[P_\gamma/U_\delta]\otimes_{\mathbb C K}W\cong\mathbb C[P_\gamma/U_\gamma]\otimes_{\mathbb C L}\bigl(\mathbb C[L/(L\cap U_\delta)]\otimes_{\mathbb C K}W\bigr)$, which is the inflation to $P_\gamma$ of $R_K^{L,L\cap P_\delta}(W)=R_K^L(W)$ taken with respect to the parabolic $L\cap P_\delta=K(L\cap U_\delta)$ of $L$; the latter is a parabolic with radical $L\cap U_\delta$ by step 1.1 and [L2], and its Levi subgroup is $K$. Applying $\operatorname{Ind}_{P_\gamma}^G$ gives the first displayed isomorphism of claim 1, namely $R_K^{G,P_\delta}\cong R_L^{G,P_\gamma}\circ R_K^{L,L\cap P_\delta}$, with the identification $\mathbb C[G/U_\gamma]\otimes_{\mathbb C L}(\mathbb C[L/(L\cap U_\delta)]\otimes_{\mathbb C K}W)$ of the two sides. [L1, L2, L3, L9, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 **Equal pairings and equal characters.** Fix an irreducible $\lambda$. By steps 1.3, 1.4 and 2.2 every summand of the three sums for $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,P}\lambda,R_L^{G,Q}\lambda)$, $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,P}\lambda,R_L^{G,P}\lambda)$ and $\dim\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,Q}\lambda,R_L^{G,Q}\lambda)$ coincides, so the three numbers are equal. Writing $\chi_\lambda^P,\chi_\lambda^Q$ for the characters of $R_L^{G,P}\lambda$ and $R_L^{G,Q}\lambda$, positivity of the inner product [L7] gives $0\le\langle\chi_\lambda^P-\chi_\lambda^Q,\chi_\lambda^P-\chi_\lambda^Q\rangle_G=\langle\chi_\lambda^P,\chi_\lambda^P\rangle_G-2\langle\chi_\lambda^P,\chi_\lambda^Q\rangle_G+\langle\chi_\lambda^Q,\chi_\lambda^Q\rangle_G=0$, where the inner products are the dimensions computed above by [L7]; therefore $\chi_\lambda^P=\chi_\lambda^Q$ by [L7]. Both modules are completely reducible by [L6], and a completely reducible complex $G$-module is determined up to isomorphism by its character because its multiplicities are inner products with irreducible characters by [L6]; hence $R_L^{G,P}\lambda\cong R_L^{G,Q}\lambda$ for every irreducible $\lambda$. [L6, L7, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 **Transitivity of Harish-Chandra restriction.** For a complex $G$-module $X$ one has $X^{U_\delta}=X^{U_\gamma\rtimes(L\cap U_\delta)}=(X^{U_\gamma})^{L\cap U_\delta}$ by step 1.1, since a vector is fixed by every element of $U_\delta$ exactly when it is fixed by $U_\gamma$ and by $L\cap U_\delta$. 4.2 **From simple modules to natural functors.** Choose representatives $S_1,\dots,S_t$ for the finitely many simple complex $L$-modules, and choose isomorphisms $\varphi_i:R_L^{G,P}S_i\to R_L^{G,Q}S_i$ using step 3.2. There are finitely many simple types because each is a quotient of the finite-dimensional regular module, which is completely reducible by [L6]. For every complex $L$-module $W$, the evaluation map $\bigoplus_{i=1}^t S_i\otimes_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Hom}_L(S_i,W)\to W$, $s\otimes f\mapsto f(s)$, is a natural isomorphism. For finite-dimensional $W$ this follows from complete reducibility and $\operatorname{End}_L(S_i)=\mathbb C$ in [L6]. For arbitrary $W$, each vector generates the finite-dimensional submodule $\mathbb C Lw$, giving surjectivity; any element of the kernel involves only finitely many maps from the finite-dimensional $S_i$, whose images lie in a finite-dimensional submodule, giving injectivity by the finite-dimensional case. The tensor model [L1] therefore identifies each induced module naturally with $\bigoplus_i R_L^{G,P}S_i\otimes_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{Hom}_L(S_i,W)$, or its $Q$ version. The maps $\bigoplus_i(\varphi_i\otimes\mathrm{id})$ define a natural isomorphism between them, since a map $W\to W'$ acts on the Hom factors by postcomposition. Only the finitely many $\varphi_i$ are chosen; no bases or decompositions of arbitrary $W$ are chosen. [L1, L6, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 **Independence of the restriction.** Let $X$ be a complex $G$-module. Composing the natural isomorphisms $\operatorname{Hom}_L(W,X^{U})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,P}W,X)$ of [L4] with the natural isomorphism $R_L^{G,P}\cong R_L^{G,Q}$ of step 4.2 and with the second adjunction $\operatorname{Hom}_G(R_L^{G,Q}W,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_L(W,X^{V})$ yields a natural isomorphism of functors of $W$ from $\operatorname{Hom}_L(-,X^{U})$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_L(-,X^{V})$, hence an $L$-linear isomorphism $\phi_X:X^{U}\to X^{V}$: evaluate the natural Hom-space map at $W=X^U$ on $\mathrm{id}_{X^U}$, and evaluate its inverse at $W=X^V$ on $\mathrm{id}_{X^V}$. Naturality with respect to these maps makes their two composites the respective identities, and the construction is natural in $X$ because the adjunction isomorphisms are natural in both variables. Thus ${}^*\!R_L^{G,P}\cong{}^*\!R_L^{G,Q}$ as functors, which completes both displayed isomorphisms of claim 2. [L1, L4, step 4.2, algebra]
