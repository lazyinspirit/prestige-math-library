## Statement

Consider the finite sparse input and normalized SDP promises of [[alg-qa-30-quantum-sdp-solver]], with dimension $n\ge1$, $m\ge1$, sparsity capacity $s\ge1$, $R>0$, $r\ge1$, and nontrivial accuracy $0<\varepsilon<2R$. Put $Q=Rr/\varepsilon$. Supply successful certified implementations of the sparse encoding, controlled evolution, finite rotations and arithmetic, with their error budgets as in that algorithm. Its matrix input-query bound, including the derived sparse sums, is
$$\widetilde O\bigl(\sqrt{nm}\,s^2Q^8\bigr).$$
For diagonal input matrices there is a scalar flagged-trace implementation with matrix input-query bound
$$\widetilde O\bigl(\sqrt{nm}\,Q^5\bigr).$$
The tilde suppresses logarithms in dimension, sparsity, accuracy and the declared failure budgets. The zero-dimension and coarse-accuracy boundary branches have the direct costs stated by the algorithm. The diagonal improvement uses scalar entry evaluation and approximate minimum finding, rather than substituting $s=1$ in the general bound.

These are matrix-query bounds. Let $N_{f,t}=\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}/\theta)$ count complete flag-circuit accesses in threshold round $t$, and let $N_{s,t}=\widetilde O(\sqrt n)$ count its spectral-shift sampler accesses. Every access includes its actual forward, inverse or controlled implementation. If $C_\Phi$ prices each reference preparation or reversal, $F_t$ is the once-per-round factory cost, $G_{f,t},G_{s,t}$ price the remaining gates of those complete primitives, and $A_t$ prices the remaining estimator/minimum/score arithmetic and classical control, the corresponding charged gate/time record is
$$\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl[F_t+N_{f,t}(C_\Phi+G_{f,t})+N_{s,t}(C_\Phi+G_{s,t})+A_t\bigr]+G_{\rm QRAM}+W,$$
times the declared number of threshold calls. Here $G_{\rm QRAM}$ is the actual memory construction, update and access cost if QRAM is used, and $W$ is output and record writing. Omit a separately written cost if it is already included in a primitive price. In particular reference preparation is repeatedly charged, totaling $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}Q^3C_\Phi)$ per threshold run. Phase-list generation, coefficient bits, finite value arithmetic, preparation, precision synthesis, work and descriptions are included at their actual prices. The source's same-order gate conclusion holds when these explicit costs fit its stated model and the respective query envelope; no such fit follows from matrix-query counts alone.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite encoded Hermitian input matrices, norm and sparsity promises, declared successful implementations and precision budgets, and the complete sampler/inverse/control/reflection interfaces in the Statement.

[F1] [[alg-qa-30-quantum-sdp-solver]], [[lem-qa-30-mmw-to-sdp-feasibility]] and [[thm-qa-30-sdp-binary-search-correctness]] supply at most two new matrix indices per round, $T=\widetilde O(Q^2)$, $\eta=\widetilde O(Q^{-1})$, $\theta^{-1}=O(Q)$, and logarithmically many threshold calls. The exact target Gibbs exponent $L_t$ has norm at most $\eta t$.

[F2] [[def-qa-30-sdp-sparse-oracle-model]] supplies total finite XOR location/value access. [[lem-qa-25-sorted-xor-sparse-access-isometries]] permits distinct sorted support unions containing zero-valued columns, gives normalization $dh$ for the exact decoded matrix with entry bound $h$, and costs $O(\log(d+1))$ location calls and $O(1)$ value calls, including controls and adjoints.

[F3] [[thm-qa-25-qubitized-simulation-resource-bound]] and [[thm-qa-25-small-time-aware-simulator-correctness]] give successful certified simulation with clean-input error $\zeta$ using $O(\alpha|u|+\log(1/\zeta))$ signal calls at normalization $\alpha$; this loose bound retains their sharper degree estimate. [[lem-qa-25-clean-subspace-unitary-hybrid]] extends clean-input errors to actual inverses, controls, references and products.

[F4] [[thm-qa-30-smooth-fourier-approximation]] gives finite Fourier coefficients, norm at most $1+e/8$ after declared rounding/dilution, largest time $O(\Delta^{-1}\log(1/e))$, frequency count $\widetilde O(K)$ for Gibbs spectral width $O(K)$ and fixed margin, and coefficient generation in $\widetilde O(K^5)$ arithmetic operations.

[F5] [[lem-qa-30-spectral-minimum-shift]] gives a measured rational approximate minimum frozen once per round, using $\widetilde O(\sqrt n)$ complete spectral samplers whose QPE power times sum to $O(1)$ per trial. [[def-qa-30-sdp-reference-state-access]] supplies the original-label reference state at price $C_\Phi$; for $n=2^q$ its explicit circuit costs $q$ Hadamards and $q$ CNOTs.

[F6] [[thm-qa-30-flagged-trace-error-and-cost]] gives common-normalization probability floors and trace-label cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt n/\theta)$ complete flag accesses when the two successful blocks have error at most $e\le\theta/(100000n)$. [[thm-qa-30-generalized-minimum-guarantee]] and [[lem-qa-30-two-sparse-estimated-oracle]] give two robust minima costing $\widetilde O(\sqrt m)$ trace-label accesses per round, with their cleaning and confidence budgets.

[F7] [[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]] gives finite Hermitian functional calculus. [[thm-qa-19-matrix-exponential-unitary-properties]] gives unitary evolution and its derivative. [[cor-operator-norm-submultiplicative-and-t-star-t-identity]] bounds products. [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]] and [[cor-taylor-remainder-bound]] give the bounded-argument scalar Taylor estimates used below.

## Proof


1.1 At round $t\ge1$, retain a list of at most $k_t=2t+1$ original constituent matrices; repetitions may remain. An identity shift adds at most one column per row, so use capacity $D_t=sk_t+1=O(st)$. A derived sorted row query scans all $k_ts$ raw slots, masks missing entries, sorts column records, merges duplicate columns and sums their coefficient-weighted values. Retain every distinct union column, including summed value zero, and pad missing slots by the supplied sentinel. No real value zero test occurs. The input Hermitian supports and the diagonal are transposition-compatible, so their union is also. A fixed sorting network and finite scans compute the requested answer, XOR-copy it, then reverse the retained history. This is a total reversible oracle with $O(st)$ original location/value calls, including reversal and masked invalid-input handling. A derived entry query sums $k_t$ entries with $O(t)$ value calls. Sorting an individual observable row similarly costs $O(s)$ raw queries. These operations work coherently in row and observable indices. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

1.2 For a decoded Hermitian $F$ of capacity $d_F$ and entry bound $h_F>0$, [F2] and [F3] simulate signed time $u$ with error $\zeta$ in $O((d_Fh_F|u|+\log(1/\zeta))\log(d_F+1))$ derived location calls and $O(d_Fh_F|u|+\log(1/\zeta))$ value calls. A supplied upper bound suffices; no global maximum-entry computation is needed. The actual inverse and controlled implementations retain this order. Arithmetic, slot preparation, phases, precision and work remain priced separately. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The coefficient $\eta$ and hence the target entries need not be rational. Write $L_t=\sum_{\ell\le k_t}c_\ell B_\ell$, with $\|B_\ell\|\le1$. Freeze real rational $\widehat c_\ell$ with $|\widehat c_\ell-c_\ell|\le\delta_t/k_t$, and decode the exact finite matrix $\widehat L_t=\sum_\ell\widehat c_\ell B_\ell$. Then $\widehat L_t$ is exactly Hermitian and $\|\widehat L_t-L_t\|\le\delta_t$. Common real coefficient rounding, rather than independent conjugate-entry rounding, preserves Hermiticity. The row union is independent of cancellation and its value oracle agrees exactly with this decoded matrix. Certified coefficient enclosures and exact rational sum arithmetic are factory/bit costs, without matrix queries. If an input oracle itself approximates a different target, add its certified operator error; entrywise error $u$ gives operator error at most $nu$ by the Frobenius bound. Absent such a certificate, the exact decoded input is the input matrix. [given, F1, F2, F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Write the Fourier times as $q\omega$, $|q|\le J$, $\omega=\pi/(2(r_F+\Delta))$. Implement SELECT by signed binary digits of $q$: at place $\ell$, apply the evolution at $2^\ell\omega$ controlled on the digit and sign. The ideal factors commute and multiply to $e^{iq\omega F}$. There are $O(\log(J+2))$ slots and their absolute times sum to less than $2(J+1)|\omega|$ per sign branch. Assign each slot error $\zeta/O(\log(J+2))$. The clean-subspace hybrid proves total SELECT error at most $\zeta$, including shared retained simulator work, inverses and references, without asserting actual work is clean. To use rational times, replace $\omega$ by rational $\widehat\omega$ with error at most $\zeta/[4(J+1)\max(1,\|F\|)]$; the spectral scalar inequality $|e^{ia}-e^{ib}|\le|a-b|$ bounds its additional error, and split the slot budget accordingly. Thus SELECT costs $\widetilde O(d_Fh_F(J+1)|\omega|+1)$ derived queries. For fixed $\Delta=1/2$, [F4] makes $(J+1)|\omega|=O(\log(1/e))$, giving $\widetilde O(d_Fh_F+1)$, without multiplying by the frequency count. [F3, F4, F7, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 For Hermitian $F,\widehat F$, differentiating $e^{i(u-v)F}e^{iv\widehat F}$ and integrating its finite matrix entries gives $\|e^{iuF}-e^{iu\widehat F}\|\le|u|\|F-\widehat F\|$, for either sign of $u$. For positive $G,\widehat G$, differentiating $e^{-(a-v)G}e^{-v\widehat G}$ on $[0,a]$ similarly gives $\|e^{-aG}-e^{-a\widehat G}\|\le a\|G-\widehat G\|$, since spectral calculus makes both factors contractions. No commutation is used. Choose $\delta_t\le\min(1/8,e/100)$; target Gibbs-amplitude error at $a=1/2$ is at most $e/200$. If comparing individual target evolutions, also impose $\delta_t\le\zeta/(4(U_t+1))$ for total absolute time bound $U_t$ and SELECT budget $\zeta$. This increases certified coefficient precision logarithmically, without adding matrix queries. Allocate finite signal and rotation synthesis within the remaining block and hybrid budgets. [F3, F7, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

3.2 The sparse bridge encodes a padded $F\oplus0$. Prepare $\Phi_n$ on the original labels, rather than a uniform reference on the enlarged register. Ideal evolutions preserve that original subspace, so Fourier accuracy is needed only on the original spectrum; padded zeros outside the Gibbs approximation interval cause no change. Include padded-system and simulator-work leakage in the clean-input isometry error. The hybrid in [F3] then controls it throughout repeated calls. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Apply [F5] once to $\widehat L_t$. Its rational lower shift $\widetilde\lambda$ lies between $\lambda_{\min}(\widehat L_t)-1/4$ and $\lambda_{\min}(\widehat L_t)$ on the good event. Put $\widehat G_t=\widehat L_t-\widetilde\lambda I+3I/2$ and $G_t=L_t-\widetilde\lambda I+3I/2$. The variational quadratic-form characterization gives $|\lambda_{\min}(\widehat L_t)-\lambda_{\min}(L_t)|\le\delta_t$. Hence both minima lie in $[1,2]$, and $\|G_t\|,\|\widehat G_t\|\le2\eta t+2=2K_t$ for $K_t=1+\eta t$: the decoded spectral diameter is at most $2\eta t+2\delta_t$ with minimum at most $7/4$, while the target diameter is at most $2\eta t$ with minimum at most $7/4+\delta_t$. A common scalar shift changes no target normalized Gibbs density. Freeze it and all coefficient, norm, cutoff and synthesis data before constructing indexed trace labels. Constant-total-time QPE and [F5] cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt nD_tK_t)$ derived queries, or $\widetilde O(\sqrt n s^2t^2K_t)$ raw queries by step 1.1. [F1, F5, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2, algebra, construct]

4.2 For diagonal inputs, evaluate the finite scalar $\widehat L_t(i)$ using $O(t)$ original diagonal-value queries and no sparse locations. Round its value to error at most $1/8$, preserve the uniform index, and apply the robust minimum from [F6]. Its good selected label $s$ is at least $\lambda_{\min}(\widehat L_t)-1/8$ by goodness and at most that minimum plus $1/8$ by nondomination at a minimizing index. It costs $\widetilde O(\sqrt n)$ scalar evaluations; comparisons are finite rational comparisons, not exact irrational-energy comparisons. Measure once and freeze this shift. With $\widehat g_i=\widehat L_t(i)-s+3/2$, $g_i=L_t(i)-s+3/2$, and $\delta_t\le\min(1/8,\mu/100)$, both minima lie in $[1,2]$ and $|g_i-\widehat g_i|\le\delta_t$. Thus $Z=\sum_i e^{-g_i}\ge e^{-2}$ and the target Gibbs ratio is unchanged. [F5, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Use [F4] for $\widehat G_t$ with analytic error parameter $e/10$, fixed extension margin $1/2$, and interval parameters $x_0=K_t^++1/2,r_F=K_t^+$, where a certified rational enclosure chooses $K_t\le K_t^+\le K_t+1$. Its coefficient budget is one, and $K_t^+=O(K_t)$ preserves every count. The natural normalization $1+e/80$ is further diluted by the known scalar $(1+e/80)/(1+e/8)$ to the trace-common $B_G^+=1+e/8$. The analytic approximation, coefficient rounding, finite SELECT, PREPARE and dilution errors are allocated to less than $e/2$; step 3.1 contributes at most $e/200$ against the target block. Thus [F6]'s final error at most $e$ holds. The observable $F_A=\frac12\sqrt{I+A/2}$ uses the fixed interval $[-1,1]$, margin $1/2$, coefficient budget one and the same final $B_A^+=1+e/8$. Its baseline is $I/(2B_A^+)$, with the same frozen Gibbs block and normalization. Rational upper enclosures $h_G\ge2K_t$ with $h_G=O(K_t)$ suffice. [F4, F6, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

5.2 Given diagonal $a_i\in[-1,1]$, compute an approximation to $f_i=(1+a_i/2)e^{-g_i}/4$ from the finite values $\widehat g_i,a_i$, then rotate a flag to that probability with absolute error at most $\mu$. Allocate $\mu/2$ to finite arithmetic/rotation; the energy perturbation adds at most $(3/8)\delta_t<\mu/2$ by step 3.1. Prepare the original reference, retain arithmetic work, and reverse the entire circuit for its inverse. Orthogonal index labels make its success probability an additive-$\mu$ approximation of $p_A=(4n)^{-1}\sum_i(1+a_i/2)e^{-g_i}$; use $a_i=0$ and the same shift for $p_0=Z/(4n)$. Then $p_0>1/(32n)$, $p_A\ge p_0/2$, and $p_A/p_0=1+\operatorname{tr}(A\rho)/2$. With $\mu\le\theta/(100000n)$ the same elementary ratio-stability and relative-estimation argument of [F6] applies, with floor $1/(1024n)$ and $\widetilde O(\sqrt n/\theta)$ complete scalar flag calls. [F6, step 3.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Step 2.2 and step 5.1 give $\widetilde O(D_tK_t)$ derived Gibbs queries and $\widetilde O(s)$ observable queries per complete flag circuit. Their raw costs are respectively $\widetilde O(D_tK_tst)$ and $\widetilde O(s^2)$ by step 1.1, so $Q_{f,t}=\widetilde O(s^2t^2K_t)$. Inverses, coherent controls and the baseline have the same order. By [F6], one trace label costs $\widetilde O(\sqrt n s^2t^2K_t/\theta)$ raw queries, and two sparse replies cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}s^2t^2(1+\eta t)/\theta)$ per round. The separate scalar $C$ trace and once-only shift are dominated. Every Gibbs/reference preparation inside relative estimation is counted, using the inverse of its whole circuit, without a normalized postselected-state inverse. [F6, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

6.2 Generate Gibbs Fourier coefficients once per round: their arithmetic count is $\widetilde O(K_t^5)$, summing to $\widetilde O(T+\eta^5T^6)=\widetilde O(Q^7)$. Their actual bit costs and simulator phase generation remain in $F_t$. The observable coefficient pattern is common to every index and is also generated once; only its evolution SELECT depends on $j$. A length-$J_t=\widetilde O(K_t)$ coefficient table has a binary-tree PREPARE with $O(J_t)$ conditional rotations: each node splits its incoming amplitude in the ratio of its two subtree weights, giving square-root leaf weights by induction, with fixed completion at zero-weight nodes. Its phases are in SELECT and its norm slack is diluted. This is an explicit rotation/table-use count, with weight arithmetic, precision and synthesis separately charged; its arithmetic order is dominated by $D_tK_t$. Each round adds only a constant number of new coefficient records, and no dense list of all matrix rows is materialized. [F1, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.3 The scalar exponential requires no factor $\max_i g_i$. Put $B=\lceil\log_2(32/\mu)\rceil$, found by rational doubling comparisons. When $\widehat g_i\ge B$, use zero, with error $e^{-B}\le2^{-B}\le\mu/32$. Otherwise $1\le\widehat g_i\le B$. Starting with $n_0=2$, double $n_0$ until the exact rational test $3^BB^{n_0}/n_0!\le\mu/64$ passes, then evaluate the Taylor polynomial through degree $n_0-1$. Its remainder is at most $e^BB^{n_0}/n_0!\le\mu/64$; the elementary exponential series gives $2\le e<3$. For even $n_0\ge4B^2$, the final half of the factorial gives $B^{n_0}/n_0!\le(2B^2/n_0)^{n_0/2}\le2^{-n_0/2}$. Thus any even $n_0\ge\max(4B^2,2\lceil\log_2(64\cdot3^B/\mu)\rceil)$ passes, and doubling selects $O(\log^2(1/\mu))$ terms. Exact rational evaluation followed by one rounding controls cancellation without assuming unit-cost long integers; bit lengths and work are polynomial in this term count and the actual energy encoding length. Clip the probability to $[0,1]$. [F7, step 4.2, step 5.2, construct, algebra]

7.1 Summing step 6.1 over at most $T$ rounds gives $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}s^2\theta^{-1}(T^3+\eta T^4))$. Insert [F1]'s $T=\widetilde O(Q^2)$, $\eta=\widetilde O(Q^{-1})$, and $\theta^{-1}=O(Q)$ to obtain $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}s^2Q^8)$. Since the present nontrivial regime has $Q>1/2$, the $Q^7$ term is also $O(Q^8)$; equivalently use $\max(1,Q)$ without changing the order. The outer threshold count is logarithmic. The early-time $1$ in $K_t$ therefore creates no missing query factor. [F1, step 6.1, algebra]

7.2 To implement a finite probability rotation, bisect $[0,\pi/2]$, approximate its midpoint $\sin^2$ to fixed error $\mu/32$ by bounded-argument Taylor arithmetic, and compare the resulting interval with the target rational probability. If they overlap, that midpoint already has probability error at most $\mu/16$ after target rounding; otherwise the comparison identifies the correct half. After $O(\log(1/\mu))$ iterations its width also suffices, since $|\sin^2x-\sin^2y|\le2|x-y|$. Certified rational enclosures for $\pi$ and finite rotations have their actual arithmetic/synthesis price. Reversibly compute the angle, apply its binary-controlled rotations, and retain or uncompute scratch as declared. There is a fixed finite cap and no equality-dependent refinement. This supplies the scalar flag with $O(t)$ matrix queries, including its actual inverse/control forms; large energies affect encoding/comparison costs rather than matrix-query count. [F7, step 5.2, step 6.3, construct, algebra]

8.1 The primitive counts are $N_{f,t}=\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}/\theta)$ and $N_{s,t}=\widetilde O(\sqrt n)$. Multiplying them by their actual complete-circuit prices and adding factory, estimator, score-comparison, memory, output, arithmetic and history costs gives exactly the additive record in the Statement. In particular reference preparation contributes $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}\theta^{-1}T C_\Phi)=\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}Q^3C_\Phi)$ per threshold run, rather than one $C_\Phi$. For power-of-two $n$, [F5] supplies $C_\Phi=O(\log n)$; otherwise retain its supplied repeated price and separately budget any reference error. The scan/sort implementation uses $O(st\log^2(st+2))$ compare/exchanges per derived row call by padded bitonic sorting, whose recursive half-sorts and logarithmic merge give that recurrence. Each comparison, rational multiplication/addition and reversible history has its actual bit/gate price. A chosen QRAM implementation instead includes its actual construction/update/address-unitary prices. Therefore the stated conditional gate boundary follows, without deriving bit efficiency from an oracle count. [F2, F3, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 7.1, step 6.2, algebra]

9.1 The diagonal trace label consequently costs $\widetilde O(\sqrt n\,t/\theta)$ raw queries. The once-per-round approximate minimum costs $\widetilde O(\sqrt n\,t)$ and is dominated. Two robust minima give round cost $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}\,t/\theta)$, so summation yields $\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}\theta^{-1}T^2)=\widetilde O(\sqrt{nm}Q^5)$. This scalar construction removes both sparse-row growth and the $K_t$ evolution factor; it is not a consequence of setting $s=1$ in step 7.1. All repeated reference, scalar arithmetic, compilation, comparison, classical control, output and optional memory prices remain in step 8.1's additive record. The source-like same-order diagonal gate conclusion is therefore conditional on these prices fitting that model and envelope. This proves both query bounds and the stated gate-cost boundary. [F1, F6, step 8.1, step 4.2, step 5.2, step 7.2, algebra] ∎
