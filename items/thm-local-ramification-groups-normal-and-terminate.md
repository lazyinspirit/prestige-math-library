## Statement

Let $L/K$ be a finite Galois extension of nonarchimedean local fields with
finite residue fields, and let $G_i$ be its lower ramification subsets for
$i\ge0$. Then each $G_i$ is a normal subgroup of $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$,
the sequence is decreasing, and $G_i=\{1\}$ for all sufficiently large $i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite Galois extension $L/K$ and the complete normalized discrete
valuation on $L$ with finite residue field. No compatibility between the two
given local valuations is silently assumed: the proof establishes the needed
automorphism invariance intrinsically on $L$.

[F1] The literal lower-subset condition is $\sigma\in G_i$ exactly when
$v_L(\sigma x-x)\ge i+1$ for every $x\in\mathcal O_L$.
[[def-lower-ramification-groups-of-a-local-galois-extension]]

[F2] The field $L$ is complete with normalized surjective integer valuation,
valuation ring $\mathcal O_L$, maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_L$ generated by a
uniformizer, and finite residue field $k_L$ of cardinality $q\ge2$.
[[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]

[F3] A finite field extension has a finite basis over its base field.
[[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]

[F4] The nonzero elements of a finite field form a cyclic group. In cardinality
$q$ its order is $q-1$, so every residue element $t$ satisfies $t^q=t$.
[[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

[F5] Over a complete nonarchimedean field, a simple residue root of a monic
polynomial with integral coefficients has a unique integral root lifting it.
The actual proof uses initial finite lifts followed by deterministic polynomial
corrections; it has no AC or CC assumption.
[[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]

[F6] For a finite Galois extension with its full Galois group $G$, the fixed
field $L^G$ equals $K$.
[[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** first recover the valuation ring from an automorphism-invariant
ring using a fixed polynomial, then prove the subgroup and finite termination
properties with integral witnesses.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Put $f(T)=T^q-T-1$ and $V=\{f(x)^{-1}:x\in L\}$. The polynomial has no zero in $L$. If $x\in\mathcal O_L$, its reduction is $-1$ by [F4], so $f(x)$ is a unit. If $v_L(x)<0$, the term $x^q$ has strictly smaller valuation than $x$ and $1$, so $v_L(f(x))=qv_L(x)<0$. Hence in both cases $f(x)\ne0$ and $f(x)^{-1}\in\mathcal O_L$. Therefore $U:=V-V\subseteq\mathcal O_L$. Every field automorphism of $L$ preserves $V$ and $U$, because $f$ has integer coefficients and the automorphism permutes $L$. This assertion does not use continuity or valuation preservation. [F2, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We have $\mathfrak m_L\subseteq U$. Fix $y\in\mathfrak m_L$; then $y-1$ is a unit. The monic polynomial $h(T)=f(T)-(y-1)^{-1}$ has integral coefficients and reduces to $T^q-T$, with simple root $0$. By [F5], there is $x\in\mathfrak m_L$ with $f(x)=(y-1)^{-1}$. Since $f(0)=-1$, it follows that $f(x)^{-1}-f(0)^{-1}=(y-1)-(-1)=y$, proving the inclusion. This selects a root only for the fixed $y$; its existence and uniqueness are given by the deterministic Hensel construction. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $R$ be the unital subring of $L$ generated by $U$. Steps 1.1–2.1 give $\mathfrak m_L\subseteq R\subseteq\mathcal O_L$ and show that every field automorphism preserves $R$. The integral closure of $R$ inside $L$ is exactly $\mathcal O_L$. Indeed, for $z\in\mathcal O_L$, [F4] gives $c=z^q-z\in\mathfrak m_L\subseteq R$, so $z$ satisfies the monic equation $T^q-T-c=0$ over $R$. Conversely, if $v_L(z)<0$, no monic equation $z^d+\sum_{j<d}r_jz^j=0$ with $r_j\in R\subseteq\mathcal O_L$ is possible: $dv_L(z)$ is strictly smaller than every $v_L(r_j)+jv_L(z)$, preventing cancellation. Automorphisms preserving $R$ therefore preserve $\mathcal O_L$. They preserve its units, hence its maximal ideal and every power of that ideal. For integral nonzero $z$, the condition $z\in\mathfrak m_L^j\setminus\mathfrak m_L^{j+1}$ determines $v_L(z)=j$; negative values are determined by inversion. Thus every field automorphism of $L$, in particular every element of $G$, preserves the normalized valuation and maps $\mathcal O_L$ onto itself. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The identity satisfies [F1]. If $\sigma,\tau\in G_i$ and $x\in\mathcal O_L$, then $\sigma\tau x-x=\sigma(\tau x-x)+(\sigma x-x)$ has valuation at least $i+1$ by step 3.1 and [F1]. Also $\sigma^{-1}x-x=\sigma^{-1}(x-\sigma x)$ has valuation at least $i+1$. Hence $G_i$ is a subgroup. Increasing the defining threshold shows that the sequence is decreasing. For $\rho\in G$, step 3.1 and the fact that $\rho$ permutes $\mathcal O_L$ show that the displacement inequalities for $\rho^{-1}\sigma\rho$ are exactly those for $\sigma$, so $\rho^{-1}G_i\rho=G_i$. This proves normality. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Fix a uniformizer $\pi_L$. The finite product $a=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(\pi_L)$ is fixed by $G$, so belongs to $K^\times$ by [F6]. Step 3.1 gives $v_L(a)=|G|>0$. Choose a finite $K$-basis $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ by [F3]. For each $j$, choose an integer $t_j\ge0$ with $t_jv_L(a)+v_L(b_j)\ge0$ and put $e_j=a^{t_j}b_j$. These are still a $K$-basis and now lie in $\mathcal O_L$. Only finitely many scalings are chosen; this argument does not require any comparison with the separately given valuation on $K$. [F2, F3, F6, step 3.1, choose, construct, algebra]

5.1 If $G=\{1\}$, all $G_i$ are already trivial and take $N=0$. Otherwise, for each nonidentity $\sigma$, choose the least index $j(\sigma)$ for which $\sigma(e_j)\ne e_j$. It exists because a $K$-linear automorphism fixing a basis is the identity. The finitely many integers $r_\sigma=v_L(\sigma(e_{j(\sigma)})-e_{j(\sigma)})$ are finite. Take $N=\max(0,\max_{\sigma\ne1}r_\sigma)$. Every chosen witness is integral by step 4.2, so [F1] would require its displacement valuation to be at least $N+1$ if $\sigma$ belonged to $G_N$. Since $r_\sigma\le N$, no nonidentity element belongs to $G_N$. By step 4.1, $G_i=\{1\}$ for every $i\ge N$. This proves the full unqualified Statement. [F1, F3, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
