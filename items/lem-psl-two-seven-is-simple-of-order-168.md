## Statement

The explicitly constructed group $G=\operatorname{SL}_2(\mathbb F_7)/\{I,-I\}=\operatorname{PSL}(2,7)$ is nonabelian, simple, and has order $168$. This proof uses no classification theorem and no choice principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Arithmetic modulo $7$ is a commutative ring by [[thm-integers-modulo-n-basic-algebra]]. Its nonzero elements $1,2,3,4,5,6$ have respective inverses $1,4,5,2,3,6$, so it is the field $\mathbb F_7$. Matrix multiplication is associative and unital by [[thm-matrix-multiplication-laws]].

[F2] Quotients by normal subgroups have the coset multiplication and group laws ([[def-quotient-group]], [[thm-quotient-group-laws]]); simplicity means nontriviality and absence of nontrivial proper normal subgroups ([[def-normal-subgroup]], [[def-simple-group]]).

[F3] Commutators are $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ and generate the normal subgroup $[G,G]$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]], [[lem-commutator-subgroup-is-normal]]). An abelian quotient $G/N$ forces $[G,G]\subseteq N$ ([[thm-quotient-abelian-iff-contains-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F4] A group action assigns compatible permutations to its elements ([[def-group-action]]).



**Given:** The field and matrix arithmetic of F1. Define $S$ to be the two-by-two matrices $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$ over $\mathbb F_7$ with $ad-bc=1$.

## Proof

1.1 Direct expansion of a product shows that the determinant of the product of two such matrices is the product of their determinants. Their inverses are $\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{pmatrix}$; together with F1 this makes $S$ a group. Its first column can be any of the $7^2-1=48$ nonzero vectors. For first column $(a,c)$ with $a\ne0$, there are exactly seven second columns: $b$ is arbitrary and $d=(1+bc)/a$; for $a=0$ one has $c\ne0$, $b=-1/c$, and $d$ arbitrary. Thus $|S|=48\cdot7=336$. The central subgroup $Z=\{I,-I\}$ has two elements; its cosets pair each matrix with its negative, so $G=S/Z$ has order $168$. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Write $U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$, $L(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\t&1\end{pmatrix}$, $D(a)=\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$, and $W(a)=\begin{pmatrix}0&a\\-a^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}$ for $a\ne0$. Multiplication gives $W(a)=U(a)L(-a^{-1})U(a)$ and $D(a)=W(a)W(1)^{-1}$. These upper and lower matrices generate $S$: if the top left entry $a$ of $M=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in S$ is nonzero, then $M=L(c/a)D(a)U(b/a)$; if it is zero, left multiplication by $W(1)$ makes it nonzero, since $c\ne0$. Every displayed factor is generated by the $U$ and $L$, proving the assertion. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In $S$, $[D(2),U(t)]=U(3t)$ since $2^2-1=3$ in $\mathbb F_7$. As multiplication by $3$ permutes the field, every $U(t)$ is a commutator. Also $W(1)U(t)W(1)^{-1}=L(-t)$, so normality of the commutator subgroup puts every $L(t)$ in it. Step 2.1 gives $[S,S]=S$. The same calculations after quotienting, and the images of the same generators, give $[G,G]=G$. Since $|G|=168>1$, $G$ cannot be abelian: all commutators in an abelian group are the identity. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Let $\Omega$ be the one-dimensional subspaces of $\mathbb F_7^2$, written $\infty=[1:0]$ and $x=[x:1]$ for $x\in\mathbb F_7$. The matrices in $S$ permute these lines, and $Z$ fixes them, giving an action of $G$ on these eight points. It is faithful: a matrix fixing all lines fixes $[1:0]$ and $[0:1]$, hence is diagonal; fixing $[1:1]$ makes its two diagonal entries equal, and determinant one then makes the matrix $I$ or $-I$. The action is transitive because $W(1)$ sends $\infty$ to $0$ and $U(t)$ translates $x$ to $x+t$. The stabilizer $H$ of $\infty$ contains all these translations and is transitive on the other seven points. Thus the action is two-transitive: first move one entry of an ordered pair to its target, then use the target stabilizer to move the other entry. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 If $N\trianglelefteq G$, its orbits form a $G$-invariant equivalence relation on $\Omega$, since $g(Nx)=N(gx)$. Under two-transitivity any equivalent pair of distinct points makes every pair of distinct points equivalent, by transporting that ordered pair. Thus either all $N$-orbits are singletons, or $N$ is transitive. In the singleton case faithfulness forces $N=1$. If $N\ne1$, then $N$ is transitive and $G=NH$: for any $g\in G$ take $n\in N$ with $n\infty=g\infty$ and then $n^{-1}g\in H$. [F2, step 3.2]

5.1 Let $A$ be the image of $\{U(t):t\in\mathbb F_7\}$ in $G$. It is abelian, since $U(s)U(t)=U(s+t)$. Every matrix representing a member of $H$ is upper triangular, of the form $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\0&a^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$; it conjugates $U(t)$ to $U(a^2t)$, so $A\trianglelefteq H$. The conjugates of $A$ generate $G$, since they include the images of all $U(t)$ and $L(t)$ by step 3.1 and these generate by step 2.1. For nontrivial normal $N$, let $\pi:G\to G/N$ be the quotient map. By step 4.1 any $g$ is $nh$ with $n\in N$, $h\in H$, and therefore $\pi(gAg^{-1})=\pi(hAh^{-1})=\pi(A)$. Hence $G/N$, generated by the images of these conjugates, equals the abelian subgroup $\pi(A)$. F3 and step 3.1 imply $G=[G,G]\subseteq N$, so $N=G$. With step 1.1 this proves simplicity, order $168$, and nonabelianness. All sets and selections used are finite. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
