## Example

Let $R$ be a commutative integral domain with fraction field $K$, and let $0\ne a\in R$. For the cochain complex $C^\bullet=(R\xrightarrow{\,a\,}R)$ in degrees $-1,0$, scalar extension to $K$ is acyclic, its relative determinant ideal is $aR$, and the cokernel of its differential has zeroth Fitting ideal $aR$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative domain $R$, its fraction field $K$, and a nonzero element $a\in R$.

[F1] [[def-determinant-line-of-a-perfect-complex]]: for a chosen bounded finite-projective complex, its determinant line is the alternating tensor product of its term determinants; if its scalar extension to $K$ is acyclic, split cycle sequences give its acyclicity trivialization and relative determinant ideal.

[F2] [[def-fitting-ideal-of-a-finitely-presented-module]]: the zeroth Fitting ideal of a module with presentation matrix $A:R^m\to R^n$ is generated by the $n\times n$ minors of $A$.

[F3] [[thm-fitting-ideal-from-relative-determinant]]: for a square two-term complex $R^n\xrightarrow{A}R^n$ with $\det A\ne0$, its relative determinant ideal equals the zeroth Fitting ideal of its cokernel.

[F4] [[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]] and [[def-field-of-fractions]]: $K$ is a field containing $R$, and every nonzero element of $R$ is invertible in $K$.

## Verification

1.1 The differential of $C^\bullet\otimes_RK$ is multiplication by $a$, which is invertible by [F4]. Thus the extended two-term complex is acyclic. With the standard basis $1$ in each degree, its determinant line is $R$, and the acyclicity trivialization sends its basis to $a^{-1}$; the image lattice is $a^{-1}R$, whose inverse fractional ideal is $aR$. [F1, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The cokernel has presentation $R\xrightarrow{a}R\to R/aR\to0$. Its only $1\times1$ minor is $a$, so [F2] gives $\operatorname{Fit}_0(R/aR)=aR$. This agrees with the relative determinant ideal from step 1.1, as asserted also by [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
