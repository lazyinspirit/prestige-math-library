## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $K/k$ be finitely generated, with $k$ algebraically closed in $K$, and suppose $K$ is finite separable over a purely transcendental field $k(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$. Let $A/K$ be an abelian variety. There exist a smooth geometrically integral affine $k$-scheme $S$ with function field $K$ and a projective abelian scheme $\mathcal A\to S$ whose generic fibre is $A$. Its total space $\mathcal A$ is smooth and geometrically integral over $k$.

## Statement references

[Conrad, §2 p. 3 and §5 p. 18](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/papers/Kktrace.pdf): the smooth-base spreading and projective shrinking; the affine base, geometric-integrality conclusions, and explicit construction are proved below.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, DC, the stated field extension and $A/K$.

[F1] A finite separable extension is generated by one element. Under the stated hypotheses, $E\otimes_k K$ is a domain for every field extension $E/k$. [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]] [[lem-regular-field-extension-tensor-products-are-domains]]

[F2] The invertible-Jacobian criterion gives smoothness, and smooth morphisms are stable under base change and composition. [[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]] [[thm-smooth-morphisms-stable-base-change-composition]]

[F3] Every abelian variety over a field is projective. Smoothness and geometric connectedness of an abelian variety imply geometric integrality. [[thm-abelian-variety-is-projective]] [[def-abelian-variety-over-a-field]]

[F4] Finite-presentation schemes and morphisms descend through filtered colimits of rings, and equalities of such morphisms descend after passing to a later stage. A projective morphism is proper. [[lem-filtered-colimit-fp-scheme-stage]] [[thm-projective-morphism-proper]]

[F5] The smooth locus of a finite-presentation morphism is open, and a proper morphism is closed. [[thm-smooth-locus-open]] [[thm-proper-morphism-closed-image]]

[F6] A proper geometrically integral variety over a field has global functions equal to the field. For a proper finite-presentation morphism and a coherent flat sheaf, surjectivity of degree-zero cohomology base change at a point gives a neighbourhood on which the direct image is finite locally free and commutes with arbitrary base change. [[thm-global-functions-proper-integral-variety]] [[thm-cohomology-and-base-change]]

[F7] A smooth proper finitely presented group scheme with connected geometric fibres and constant relative dimension is an abelian scheme. [[def-abelian-scheme]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** construct a smooth affine field model, spread a projective presentation and the group maps by finite presentation, and shrink to obtain smooth connected fibres.

1.1 Put $F=k(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$. By [F1] choose $\alpha$ with $K=F(\alpha)$, including $\alpha=0$ if $K=F$. After inverting a nonzero polynomial $d\in k[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, the monic minimal polynomial $P$ of $\alpha$ has coefficients in $B=k[t_1,\ldots,t_r,1/d]$ and its discriminant is a unit in $B$. Set $R=B[T]/(P)$. As $P$ is monic, $R$ is free over $B$ with basis $1,T,\ldots,T^{\deg P-1}$; its localization to $F$ is $K$, so $R$ embeds in $K$ and has fraction field $K$. The discriminant identity makes $P'(T)$ a unit in $R$; the Jacobian criterion [F2] applied to $P$ and the polynomial coordinates makes $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}R$ smooth over $k$. For any field extension $E/k$, the injection $R\hookrightarrow K$ remains injective after tensoring with $E$, and [F1] makes $E\otimes_k K$ a domain. Hence $E\otimes_k R$ is a domain and $S_0$ is geometrically integral. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 By [F3], choose a closed immersion $A\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_K$ cut out by finitely many homogeneous polynomials. Lift their coefficients to a common localization $R[1/s]$ with $s\ne0$, and let $X$ be their closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^N_{R[1/s]}$. Its generic fibre is the given embedded $A$, and $X$ is projective and finitely presented over the noetherian base $R[1/s]$. Since $K$ is the filtered colimit of all its nonzero localizations, [F4] descends the multiplication, inverse and identity maps and, after a further common localization, all finitely many group and commutativity identities. Thus $X$ becomes a projective group scheme with generic fibre $A$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 By [F4] $X$ is proper. Its nonsmooth locus is closed by [F5] and avoids the generic fibre; its image in the base is closed by properness and omits the generic point. Removing that image and then taking a nonempty principal open gives a smooth proper group scheme over an affine localization $S_1$ of $S_0$. [F4, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 The generic fibre is geometrically integral by [F3], so its global functions are $K$ by [F6]. The generic degree-zero base-change map is an isomorphism: a finite affine cover of the separated scheme $X$ computes global sections as a finite kernel of restriction maps, and localization commutes with that kernel. Smoothness makes $\mathcal O_X$ flat over $S_1$, and the noetherian $X$ makes it coherent. Apply [F6] at the generic point and shrink to an affine neighbourhood on which $f_*\mathcal O_X$ is finite locally free and commutes with arbitrary base change. The unit map $\mathcal O_{S_1}\to f_*\mathcal O_X$ is an isomorphism at the generic point; its kernel and cokernel vanish after another localization. Thus, over the resulting nonempty principal open $S$, the global function algebra of every geometric fibre is its base field. [F3, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Each geometric fibre has an identity point. If it were disconnected, a nontrivial open-and-closed decomposition would give a nontrivial idempotent in its global functions, contrary to step 4.1. The relative dimension is locally constant for a smooth group scheme: translation identifies every tangent fibre with that at the identity, whose rank is locally constant on $S$. Since $S$ is integral, the rank is constant. Hence [F7] makes $\mathcal A=X_S$ an abelian scheme. It remains projective and has generic fibre $A$. [F7, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 The composite $\mathcal A\to S\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is smooth by [F2]. After any algebraically closed extension $E/k$, its base $S_E$ is integral by step 1.1, and the smooth map $\mathcal A_E\to S_E$ is open. The noetherian smooth scheme $\mathcal A_E$ is regular, so its irreducible components are disjoint open-and-closed subsets; each component has nonempty open image in $S_E$ and therefore meets its generic fibre. That generic fibre is an abelian variety by [F7], hence integral by [F3], so there can be only one component. Thus $\mathcal A_E$ is integral, proving geometric integrality of $\mathcal A$. [F2, F3, F7, step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎

AC and DC are inherited from the finite-presentation, geometric-integrality and cohomology-and-base-change suppliers. The construction uses only finitely many coefficients and group identities after the selected presentations.
