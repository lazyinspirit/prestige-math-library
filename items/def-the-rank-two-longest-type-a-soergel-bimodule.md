## Definition

**The parabolic.** Keep $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $\deg x_i=2$, the
simple reflections $s_i=(i\ i+1)$ and the invariants $R^{s_i}$ of
[[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]], and let
$1\le i\le n-2$. Put
$$W_{i,i+1}:=\langle s_i,s_{i+1}\rangle\le S_n,\qquad R^{W_{i,i+1}}:=R^{s_i}\cap R^{s_{i+1}},$$
the parabolic subgroup generated by the two adjacent simple reflections and its
invariant ring. The two generators act as adjacent transpositions on the three indicated
coordinates and fix every other coordinate, so their restriction is an
isomorphism $W_{i,i+1}\cong S_3$ by the generation clause of
[[lem-type-a-reduced-words-are-connected-by-braid-moves]]; the subgroup permutes the three coordinates
$x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}$ and fixes every other coordinate, so $R^{W_{i,i+1}}$ is
the ring of polynomials in $x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}$ that are invariant under all
permutations of the three, with all other $x_j$ adjoined as invariants. The
element
$$w_{i,i+1}:=s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}\in W_{i,i+1}$$
is the longest element of $W_{i,i+1}$, of length $3$.

**The bimodule.** Define
$$B_{i,i+1,i}:=R\otimes_{R^{W_{i,i+1}}}R(3),$$
the balanced tensor product of the $(R,R^{W_{i,i+1}})$-bimodule $R$ with the
$(R^{W_{i,i+1}},R)$-bimodule $R$, with the total grading of the tensor product
shifted by the external shift $(3)$ of
[[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]], that is
$$(3)=\{-3\},\qquad (B_{i,i+1,i})_d=\bigl(R\otimes_{R^{W_{i,i+1}}}R\bigr)_{d+3} \ \ \text{in the convention } M(r)_d=M_{d+r}.$$
It is the *rank-two longest type-A Soergel bimodule* attached to $i$. Equivalently
$B_{i,i+1,i}=R\otimes_{R^{W_{i,i+1}}}R\{-3\}$ in the internal notation
$M\{r\}_d=M_{d-r}$ of the library, and when $n=3$ and $i=1$ it is
$B_{1,2,1}=R\otimes_{R^{S_3}}R(3)$. The Bott–Samelson bimodule of the word
$(i,i+1,i)$ is
$$B_{\underline{i,i+1,i}}=B_i\otimes_RB_{i+1}\otimes_RB_i =R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R\otimes_{R^{s_{i+1}}}R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R(3)$$
in the notation of [[def-bott-samelson-bimodule-of-a-word]].

## Remark

**(a) Why "longest".** For adjacent $i,i+1$ the rank-two parabolic is finite of
type $A_2$, so by the description of the $2m_{st}$-valent vertex of Elias–Williamson
(p. 6) the indecomposable bimodule $B_{w_{i,i+1}}$ indexed by the longest
element of the parabolic occurs as a direct summand with multiplicity one in
each of the two Bott–Samelson bimodules $B_iB_{i+1}B_i$ and
$B_{i+1}B_iB_{i+1}$; the rank-two decomposition theorem of this page
identifies that summand explicitly as $B_{i,i+1,i}$, so that
$B_iB_{i+1}B_i\cong B_{i,i+1,i}\oplus B_i$. The reader should not read this
remark as a proof: the summand identification is the content of the next item on
this page.

**(b) The underlying rank.** The ring $R$ is free as an $R^{W_{i,i+1}}$-module of
rank $|W_{i,i+1}|=6$; this is the classical Chevalley theorem for the rank-two
parabolic, and in the case $n=3$ it is the isomorphism
$R\cong\bigoplus_{w\in S_3}R^{S_3}(-2\ell(w))$ of $R^{S_3}$-modules used in the
source. Consequently $B_{i,i+1,i}$, whose grading is shifted by $(3)$, is a free
graded $R$-module of rank six on each side, with homogeneous basis degrees
$2\ell(w)-3$ in the convention $(r)$, $w\in S_3$; as a graded left $R$-module
$$B_{i,i+1,i}\cong R(-3)\oplus R(-1)^{\oplus2}\oplus R(1)^{\oplus2}\oplus R(3).$$

**(c) The rank-one analogue.** For $n=2$ the parabolic of the single simple
reflection is $W_1=\langle s_1\rangle$ with longest element $s_1$ of length
$1$. Its analogous invariant-ring formula is
$B_{W_1}:=R\otimes_{R^{s_1}}R(1)=B_1$ of
[[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]]. This is the rank-one
generator, not an instance of the rank-two notation $B_{i,i+1,i}$, since two
adjacent simple reflections do not exist for $n=2$.

**(d) Degenerate indices.** For $n\le2$ there is no pair of adjacent simple
reflections, $W_{i,i+1}$ is undefined, and the notation $B_{i,i+1,i}$ is not
used; the objects of the type-A Soergel categories of this page are then
generated by the single $B_1$ (or by $R$ alone when $n\le1$).

**(e) Imported facts of Libedinsky used by the decomposition theorem.** The
rank-two decomposition theorem of this page imports the computation of
Libedinsky's §4.4 for the triple product $B_sB_tB_s$ of the rank-two parabolic
generated by $s\ne t$; in the type-A notation of this item, with $s=s_i$,
$t=s_{i+1}$ and the three coordinates $x_i,x_{i+1},x_{i+2}$, its content is the
following. For the idempotent $e$ constructed from that computation, the image
$\operatorname{im}(1-e)$ is generated as an $R$-bimodule by the $1$-tensor
$1^{\otimes}:=1\otimes1\otimes1\otimes1\in B_{\underline{i,i+1,i}}$, and
$B_iB_{i+1}B_i$ is generated as an $R$-bimodule by $1^{\otimes}$ together with
$1\otimes x_i\otimes1\otimes1$. Moreover there is a graded homomorphism of
$R$-bimodules
$$R\otimes_{R^{W_{i,i+1}}}R(3)\longrightarrow B_{\underline{i,i+1,i}},\qquad p\otimes q\longmapsto p\otimes1\otimes1\otimes q,$$
whose image is the sub-bimodule $\langle1^{\otimes}\rangle$ generated by the
$1$-tensor; since both sides are isomorphic to
$R(-3)\oplus R(-1)^{\oplus2}\oplus R(1)^{\oplus2}\oplus R(3)$ as graded left
$R$-modules, this homomorphism is an isomorphism onto $\operatorname{im}(1-e)$.
These four statements are quoted from Libedinsky, §4.4.1 and §4.4.2, and are used
in the decomposition theorem of this page, not proved here.
