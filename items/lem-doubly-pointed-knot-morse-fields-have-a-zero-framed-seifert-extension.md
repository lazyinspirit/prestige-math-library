## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Supply an embedded admissible DOUBLY pointed diagram for an oriented knot $K$ in the standard oriented three-sphere, its compression disks and compatible Morse trajectory data, and a compact oriented Seifert surface $F$. Use the knot orientation from $w$ to $z$ in the alpha handlebody and from $z$ to $w$ in the beta handlebody. In positive genus let $x$ be a generator. In genus zero use the empty generator. There is an actual relative knot Spin-c lift $t_{w,z}(x)$ on the zero surgery $S^3_0(K)$, constructed with the following convention: on the knot exterior use the NEGATIVE of the compatible gradient after cancelling the index-one/index-two trajectories selected by $x$; across the zero-surgery solid torus use the zero-relative-Euler extension specified in the proof. Reversing this vector field convention would reverse its Alexander evaluation and is not silently permitted.

The integer
$$A_F(x)=\frac12\langle c_1(t_{w,z}(x)),[\widehat F]\rangle$$
is well defined, independent of the local extension and framing choices in that construction and of the choice of Seifert surface for the fixed oriented knot. For every actual Whitney class from $x$ to $y$,
$$A_F(x)-A_F(y)=n_z(\phi)-n_w(\phi).$$
The standard genus-zero unknot generator has value zero. The construction is carried by orientation-preserving isomorphisms of the supplied knot, surface and Morse data, and by a smooth mark-free attaching-curve isotopy with its followed generator. Swapping $w,z$ with the same underlying attaching systems gives $A_{z,w}(x)=-A_{w,z}(x)$; this is a statement about the actual Chern pairing, not a homological symmetry theorem. Its value can be computed as half the relative Euler number of the exterior perpendicular plane on $F\setminus N(K)$ with the explicit boundary frame in the proof; the solid-torus cap contributes zero.

Consequently this construction fixes the additive constant of the relative Alexander grading on every connected generator set. In an actual full filtered minus differential, including positive multiplicities at both basepoints, the term $U^{n_w(\phi)}y$ has Alexander level $A_F(x)-n_z(\phi)$. An exact finite matrix model of that diagram acquires its absolute grading by evaluating this Euler number at one generator and then using the relative differences. This last assertion does not claim that an uncalibrated numerical matrix or the relative grading alone already supplies that Euler evaluation. In particular, if an actual orientation-preserving diagram isomorphism exchanges the two marks, followed by a mark-free attaching-curve isotopy returning its generator to $y$ in the original diagram, then $A_F(x)=-A_F(y)$. If the relative calculation also gives $A_F(x)=A_F(y)$, both are zero. Arbitrary Heegaard-move comparison and a grid closed-form centering calculation are separate operations.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The embedded doubly pointed diagram, its actual generator trajectories, the orientation convention in the Statement, the oriented Seifert surface, and the zero-framed surgery defined using its longitude.

[F1] Actual Morse generator lifts are constructed by replacing the gradient on disjoint trajectory balls. On a positive transverse disk to an index-one trajectory the replacement has degree $+1$ and changes the perpendicular-plane Euler number by $+2$. Difference lines of two lifts are classified by their finite integral two-cell winding cochain, and the determinant changes by its square. These are finite constructions, not the full-AC classification on arbitrary spaces. ([[lem-heegaard-polygon-spin-c-difference-is-the-capped-periodic-domain]])

[F2] Closed oriented three-manifolds admit actual complete pointed Heegaard data under ACω. Their finite Heegaard Spin construction gives an actual Spin-c lift with trivial determinant. ([[lem-countable-choice-closed-three-manifolds-admit-complete-pointed-heegaard-data]], [[lem-complete-heegaard-data-have-a-zero-chern-spin-c-sector]])

[F3] Actual divisor degrees give the signed region domain of a smooth Whitney representative, with its labelled alpha and beta boundary paths. They are invariant under relative homotopies. ([[lem-symmetric-product-divisor-intersections-and-point-movement]], [[lem-heegaard-polygon-spin-c-difference-is-the-capped-periodic-domain]])

[F4] The allowed fixed-splitting arc normalization makes tangential marked crossings transverse, supplies neat bridge witnesses and carries the oriented knot and its Seifert surface by an actual ambient isotopy. The surface diagram and its generator set remain fixed. ([[lem-smooth-bridge-witnesses-admit-neat-corner-parametrizations-after-normalization]])

## Proof

1.1 If the marked arcs are tangential, first use [F4] and carry $F$ by its ambient extension. The cut-ball unknotted-arc witnesses may be chosen in those balls and hence disjoint from the compression faces. This is the explicit arc-normalization comparison permitted by the pointed-data definition, not a surface-preserving diffeomorphism turning a tangency into a transverse crossing. Choose the min and max inside the supplied trivial bridge arcs in the two cut balls. Their two basepoint trajectories then form the given knot after rounding at those critical points. This is possible in the fixed balls because the supplied arc has a boundary-parallel disk: a product neighborhood of that disk straightens it to a diameter; use radial Morse coordinates there and extend across the remaining ball collar. The compression-disk trajectory pieces are unchanged and are disjoint from a sufficiently small knot tube. On that tube take coordinates $(t,r,\theta)$, oriented by $dt\wedge dr\wedge d\theta$, with $t$ following $K$, $z$ at $t=0$ and $w$ at $t=\pi$. The alpha arc is $\pi<t<2\pi$ and the beta arc is $0<t<\pi$. The Seifert framing makes $\theta=0$ the inward surface direction. On a rescaled tube $r\le1$ take $f=\sin t(1-r^2/2)$ and a metric whose $tt$ coefficient is $1-r^2/2$ and whose normal part is Euclidean. Its gradient is $v=\cos t\,\partial_t-\sin t\,r\partial_r$, with precisely the required min at $3\pi/2$ and max at $\pi/2$. Near the two regular bridge pieces its nonzero longitudinal component permits interpolation with the compatible flow coordinates without a zero; near the extrema use these exact Morse coordinates. Cancel the selected generator trajectories outside this tube by [F1], leaving this prescribed tube model fixed. All auxiliary changes are either carried trajectory neighborhoods or choices inside balls; the latter do not change a lift class by the finite obstruction construction. On the exterior use $-v_x$, the negative modified gradient. [F1, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Write the new solid torus as $D^2_{R,\varphi}\times S^1_\lambda$. Its boundary is attached by $\varphi=t$, $\lambda=-\theta$; its radial collar vector agrees with the exterior continuation of $\partial_r$. The orientation $dR\wedge d\varphi\wedge d\lambda$ is then the original orientation $dt\wedge dr\wedge d\theta$. At $R=1$, the negative-gradient boundary field is $\sin\varphi\,\partial_R-\cos\varphi\,\partial_\varphi$. In disk Cartesian coordinates its planar complex component is $-i e^{2i\varphi}$. Thus a constant planar extension would be incorrect. Fix $0<a<r_0<1$ and a radial cutoff $\chi$ equal to one for $R\le r_0$ and zero near one. Put $q(\zeta)=-i(\zeta^2-a^2\chi(|\zeta|))$. Its only zeros are $\zeta=\pm a$: outside $R\le r_0$ the inequality $R^2>a^2\chi$ excludes a zero. Choose a smooth real function $h$ supported away from the boundary, positive at $a$ and negative at $-a$. Then $V=(\operatorname{Re}q,\operatorname{Im}q,h)$ is nowhere zero and has exactly the required boundary value. Interpolate its collar with the actual exterior collar, preserving the boundary value: the fields are uniformly close on a sufficiently thin collar and a convex interpolation stays nonzero. Normalize to unit length. This is an explicit extension on the actual oriented zero-surgery solid torus. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 On a meridian cap disk the projection of $\partial_\lambda$ to $V^\perp$ gives a section nonzero on the boundary, with zeros precisely at $a,-a$. Near either zero the planar component $q$ is a complex-linear local coordinate of degree one. At $V=+\partial_\lambda$ the oriented perpendicular plane has the disk orientation and this projected section has index $+1$; at $V=-\partial_\lambda$ its plane orientation is reversed and the index is $-1$. Multiplication by a nonzero real scalar in the local projection changes neither index. The relative Euler number on the cap with boundary frame $\partial_\lambda$ is therefore zero. Any two extensions with this boundary value and zero relative Euler number give the same relative Spin-c lift. Indeed their difference line has finite obstruction group $H^2(D^2\times S^1,\partial;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, evaluated on the meridian disk, as seen by its one relative two-cell and one relative three-cell. The determinant difference is twice that integer by [F1]. Zero relative Euler difference makes the integer zero. Varying $a,\chi,h$ therefore changes no lift, and the same reasoning covers arbitrary local extension choices with the stated zero-Euler condition. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The framed knot tube is fixed up to isotopy by the zero framing from $F$. Two such framings differ by a map of the knot circle to $SO(2)$ of winding zero, which has a periodic real angle lift; multiply that angle by the isotopy parameter to identify them. Reparametrizations keeping the ordered marks and the orientation are similarly isotopic through such tube coordinates. Carrying the exterior field and the zero-Euler cap during these identifications preserves its relative lift. On the exterior the selected generator trajectories lie in the specified compression disks; changing their auxiliary parametrizations or small cancellations is a homotopy of those tracks disjoint from the knot, followed by changes inside balls. Their finite difference cochain is zero by [F1]. Thus these choices do not change the resulting lift. The elementary Mayer–Vietoris calculation for the exterior $E$ and its original solid torus gives $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, generated by the positive meridian, and $H_2(E;\mathbb Z)=0$: the boundary fundamental class is the image of the oriented fundamental class of $S^3$, and the remaining boundary map identifies the longitude with zero and the meridian with the exterior generator. Filling the longitude instead gives $H_2(S^3_0(K);\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, generated by the Seifert cap. Two Seifert surfaces differ by a cycle in $E$ and hence give the same capped class and framing. This proves the asserted surface independence. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The perpendicular plane defines an actual Spin-c lift, by the explicit oriented-two-plane lift in [F1]. Apply [F2] to the closed zero surgery to obtain a determinant-trivial Spin-c lift there. The difference between this lift and the constructed one is the actual finite difference line of [F1]; its determinant square gives $c_1(t_{w,z}(x))=2c_1(L)$. Its pairing with the integral Seifert cap is therefore even, proving that $A_F(x)$ is integral without importing a full-choice Spin-c torsor theorem. Along the boundary of $F\setminus N(K)$ the field is tangent to its collar, so $\partial_\lambda=-\partial_\theta$ is a nonzero perpendicular section. The determinant pairing is the sum of its relative Euler obstructions on the exterior surface and the cap. Step3.1 makes the latter zero. Thus half the exterior relative Euler obstruction is the stated practical evaluation. One can compute that obstruction by any finite subdivision and line frames: sum the integer boundary windings of the section on its oriented two-cells; internal edges cancel. This is a finite Euler computation, not a presumed absolute grading. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Check the relative sign using actual trajectory cochains. Let $a$ be the sum of alpha paths from $x$ to $y$ and $b$ the sum of beta paths from $x$ to $y$, supplied by the smoothed Whitney representative. The closed cycle $C=a-b$ is homologous in the knot exterior to $\gamma_y-\gamma_x$, where $\gamma_x,\gamma_y$ are the selected positive-gradient index-one/index-two trajectories: the lower compression disks join $y$ back to $x$ in $\gamma_x-\gamma_y$, and the upper ones join $x$ to $y$. For the POSITIVE-gradient fields [F1]'s transverse degree calculation gives difference line $\operatorname{PD}(\gamma_x-\gamma_y)$. Negating the fields reverses the oriented perpendicular planes and conjugates their determinant and difference lines. Hence for the convention of step1.1 the difference is $t_{w,z}(x)-t_{w,z}(y)=\operatorname{PD}[C]$ on zero surgery. This equality is a finite cochain equality: a positive crossing of a transverse disk changes the generator cancellation degree by one, and the difference line has winding one; the identical cap fields add no further cochain. To evaluate $[C]$, use [F3]'s incidence domain. Its oriented lower alpha boundary goes from $x$ to $y$ and its upper beta boundary from $y$ to $x$. The possible full-circle corrections cap in the given compression disks, which miss $K$. The resulting spanning chain intersects $K$ positively at $z$, where $K$ exits the alpha handlebody, and negatively at $w$, where it enters. Thus $\operatorname{lk}(C,K)=n_z(\phi)-n_w(\phi)$. The meridian is oriented to pair positively with $F$. Taking determinant Chern classes doubles the difference line, so evaluation on $\widehat F$ proves $A_F(x)-A_F(y)=n_z(\phi)-n_w(\phi)$. This also fixes the sign rather than inferring it from an unsigned intersection count. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.2 Check the basepoint-swap sign at the field level. The swapped diagram represents the oppositely oriented knot, with Seifert surface $-F$. Use $t'=\pi-t$ and $\theta'=-\theta$ on its knot tube. Its model function satisfies $\sin t'(1-r^2/2)=\sin t(1-r^2/2)$, so the exterior modified gradient and its negative are the SAME physical fields, with the same generator trajectory cancellations. On the two filled solid tori the coordinate identification is $(R,\varphi,\lambda)\mapsto(R,\pi-\varphi,-\lambda)$. It preserves three-dimensional orientation and agrees with the identity on the physical exterior. It takes the meridian cap to the corresponding cap and its boundary normal frame to the negative of the other one. Multiplication of a boundary frame by the constant $-1$ changes no relative Euler number. Thus the transported cap has relative Euler zero, and step3.1 identifies its relative lift with the canonical swapped cap. The constructed Spin-c field is therefore unchanged on the identified zero surgery, while its capped surface is $-\widehat F$. This proves $A_{z,w}(x)=-A_{w,z}(x)$ without using a symmetry of knot Floer homology. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.3 For a mark-free attaching-curve isotopy, extend its surface velocity separately over the relevant handlebody collar, keeping the marked neighborhoods fixed, and carry its compression disks and their Morse data. In collar coordinates the extension is $(p,r)\mapsto(\phi_{t\chi(r)}(p),r)$, with the inverse obtained by inverting the same surface map at fixed $r$. The other handlebody is held fixed as an embedded piece. The moved bridge arc has fixed jets near the marks; together with the unchanged opposite arc it is one smooth knot isotopy. Its velocity has an ambient extension: use finitely many graph charts on its compact moving track, extend the velocity constantly in the transverse directions, cut it off and patch with weights summing to one near the track. Its compactly supported flow carries the knot and $F$. A followed transverse generator gives continuously moving disjoint index-one/index-two trajectory tubes, with matching corner coordinates. On the common exterior the modified negative gradients can be chosen continuously; the knot tube model is carried with them and the canonical filling stays in its zero-Euler relative class by step3.1. The determinant winding on each cell of the carried Seifert surface is an integer continuous in the parameter, hence constant. This proves that its Chern evaluation, and therefore $A$, is unchanged under this actual followed-generator operation. No holomorphic count comparison or invariance of a non-followed generator has been inferred. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 For the standard unknot use the genus-zero Morse model and the standard spanning disk. On its exterior part the negative gradient lies tangent to that disk. Its normal direction, which equals the boundary frame $\partial_\lambda$ up to a fixed nonzero scalar, is a nowhere-zero section of the perpendicular plane. Its relative Euler obstruction is zero, as is the cap obstruction by step3.1. Hence its unique generator has $A=0$. All constructions commute with orientation-preserving pushforward of the supplied data, so their Euler and Chern evaluations are preserved by such diagram isomorphisms. Finally step6.1 and the assigned filtration degree $-1$ of $U$ give $A(U^{n_w}y)=A(y)-n_w=A(x)-n_z$ for every actual differential term, including positive values of both multiplicities. Any two functions with these differences on a connected generator set differ by a common constant; step5.1 evaluates that constant at one generator. An exact finite disk-count matrix with that evaluated anchor therefore has the actual absolute filtration. No assertion about invariance under an unproved move, or an unevaluated finite-model centering, was used. [step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally combine the actual isomorphism and followed isotopy in the Statement. Pushforward preserves the Chern evaluation, and the isomorphism interchanges the mark labels. The subsequent isotopy preserves that swapped evaluation by step6.3. Step6.2 expresses the latter at its final generator as the NEGATIVE of its original-order evaluation. Hence $A_F(x)=-A_F(y)$. If the independently computed relative difference is zero, integrality from step5.1 implies $2A_F(x)=0$ and both integers vanish. This is a finite geometric centering certificate. It uses neither symmetry of an Alexander polynomial nor genus detection nor a homology rank to select the center. Together with steps6.1–7.1 it proves every stated normalization and full filtered-degree assertion. [step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 6.2, step 6.3, algebra] ∎
