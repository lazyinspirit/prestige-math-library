## Definition

Let $(E,\mathcal E)$ be a standard Borel space and let $\nu$ be a sigma-finite
measure on it. A **counting measure** on $(E,\mathcal E)$ is a measure of the
form $\eta=\sum_{j\in J}\delta_{z_j}$ for a finite or countable index set $J$;
repetitions among the points $z_j$ are allowed. Equip the set
$\mathcal N(E)$ of these measures with the **evaluation sigma-algebra**
generated by the maps $\eta\mapsto\eta(B)$, $B\in\mathcal E$. A random
counting measure is a measurable map from a probability space into
$\mathcal N(E)$.

A **Poisson random measure** with intensity $\nu$ is a random counting
measure $N$ such that for every finite pairwise-disjoint family
$B_1,\ldots,B_m\in\mathcal E$ with $\nu(B_j)<\infty$, the counts
$N(B_1),\ldots,N(B_m)$ are mutually independent and
$N(B_j)\sim\operatorname{Pois}(\nu(B_j))$. Here
$\operatorname{Pois}(c)$ is the law in [[def-poisson-law]] with exposure
$e=c$ and rate $\lambda=1$, so $c=0$ means the point mass at zero. This
definition imposes no topology, local compactness, or local-finiteness
condition on $E$.
