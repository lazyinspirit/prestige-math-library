## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be an LCH group with a fixed left Haar
measure, let $\lambda_G$ be its left regular representation on $L^2(G)$
([[def-left-and-right-regular-unitary-representations]],
[[thm-regular-representations-are-unitary-and-strongly-continuous]],
[[def-hilbert-space]]), and for $f\in L^1(G)$ let $\lambda_G(f)\in\mathcal B(L^2(G))$
be the integrated form of $\lambda_G$ at $f$
([[def-integrated-form-of-a-unitary-representation]],
[[def-bounded-linear-operator]]). The **reduced group C\*-algebra**
$C^*_r(G)$ is the norm closure in $\mathcal B(L^2(G))$ of
$$\{\lambda_G(f):f\in L^1(G)\};$$
it is the C\*-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(L^2(G))$ generated by the integrated
left regular representation.

## Remarks

- **Well-definedness.** By
  [[lem-integrated-forms-are-nondegenerate-star-representations]] the map
  $f\mapsto\lambda_G(f)$ is multiplicative and star-preserving, so its image
  is a complex $\ast$-subalgebra of the C\*-algebra $\mathcal B(L^2(G))$. The
  norm closure of a $\ast$-subalgebra of a C\*-algebra is again a
  $\ast$-subalgebra — sums, products and adjoints of limits are the limits of
  the corresponding sums, products and adjoints by continuity of the algebra
  operations and of the adjoint — and it is complete as a closed subset of
  the complete space $\mathcal B(L^2(G))$
  ([[def-c-star-algebra]], [[def-c-star-algebra-generated-by-a-normal-operator]]).
  Hence $C^*_r(G)$ with the inherited operations is a C\*-algebra, a
  C\*-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(L^2(G))$.
- **Quotient description.** With $N:=\{f\in L^1(G):\lambda_G(f)=0\}$ the null
  ideal of the norm $\|f\|_r:=\|\lambda_G(f)\|$, the integrated form
  identifies $C^*_r(G)$ with the completion of the quotient normed algebra
  $L^1(G)/N$; the identity map on the dense subspace shows that this
  completion is the same C\*-algebra as the norm closure above
  ([BeHV–08, F.4.6]). In particular $\|f\|_r\le\|f\|_1$ for every
  $f\in L^1(G)$, since the integrated form is contractive.
- **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the Haar measure, the
  regular representation and the integrated form; the closure argument adds
  no further choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).
