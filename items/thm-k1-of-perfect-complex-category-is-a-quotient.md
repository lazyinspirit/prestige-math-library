## Statement

Let $R$ be any unital associative ring. The canonical homomorphism from $K_1(R)$ to $K_1^{\det}(D_{\mathrm{perf}}(R))$, the fundamental group of the Picard category of a universal determinant functor on perfect complexes, is surjective. No regularity hypothesis on $R$ is required for surjectivity.

## Statement references

[Breuning, Theorem 1.1 and Proposition 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0812.1556)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring, small perfect category and universal determinant functor in the Statement. A loop is always a finite morphism expression in the concrete universal category; no selection over an infinite family of objects is made.

[F1] $K_1(R)$ is the abelianization of the stable general linear group. [[def-algebraic-k0-and-k1-of-a-ring]]

[F2] A determinant functor has isomorphism, triangle, naturality, octahedral-filtration and split-commutativity maps. Its target is a Picard category and its determinant $K_1$ is the automorphism group of the unit. [[def-universal-determinant-functor-on-a-perfect-derived-category]]

[F3] The universal category exists. We use its concrete typed finite-word construction: object symbols, tensor, structural symmetries, isomorphism arrows and triangle arrows, divided by their exact determinant relations. [[thm-universal-determinant-functor-exists-on-small-triangulated-categories]]

[F4] A finite projective is a summand of a finite free module, and a short exact sequence with projective quotient splits. [[def-projective-module]] [[thm-projective-module-characterizations]]

[F5] Bounded acyclic projective complexes split and are contractible. Their cycle modules are finite projective when the terms are finite projective. [[lem-bounded-acyclic-projective-complex-is-split-contractible]]

[F6] A derived morphism between bounded finite-projective representatives is a chain map uniquely up to homotopy; cones are again bounded finite projective. [[lem-perfect-complexes-form-a-triangulated-subcategory]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite-word stabilization, explicit bounded-complex determinant extension, and reduction of triangle words.

1.1 Write $g$ for the universal determinant and $d(P)=g(P[0])$ for a finite projective. Split triangles give maps $e(A,B,C):d(A)\otimes d(C)\to d(B)$ for short exact projective sequences. They have naturality, filtration associativity and split commutativity by [F2]. Transporting an automorphism of $d(P)$ to the unit by an inverse object is unambiguous: two evaluations conjugate the resulting automorphism, and unit automorphisms are abelian by the two unit-compatible operations, composition and tensor (the interchange law). Appending an identity summand does not change this transported automorphism. Thus free-module automorphisms define the canonical stable map of [F1], and it factors through abelianization. For left modules use row vectors and right matrices; the reversal between matrix and function composition disappears in this abelian target. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We first prove a finite-word fact for the split projective data $d,e$. Every unit loop generated by module isomorphisms, $e$, their inverses, tensor structural arrows and the corresponding inverse-object evaluations is represented by a projective-module automorphism. Here is the normalization algorithm. In the finitely many vertices of the word, expand tensor parentheses and mark every occurrence of an inverse projective factor. Choose a positive direct sum $C$ containing one labelled copy for EVERY such occurrence. Tensor the whole path by $d(C)$, with identities, and at each vertex cancel each inverse factor against its labelled positive copy in $C$. The remaining factors are positive projective factors; identify their tensor with the determinant of their ordered direct sum using the split $e$ maps. All choices and cancellations in this operation are finite. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Verify the algorithm letter by letter. A positive module isomorphism becomes that isomorphism on its summand and identities on the others. For a negative occurrence of $a:P\to Q$, keep both labelled copies $P,Q$ in $C$; after cancellation its map on the uncancelled summand is $a^{-1}:Q\to P$, with the displayed block interchanges. A split comparison becomes the canonical identification of $P\oplus Q$ with its two labelled summands, or the actual chosen split-sequence isomorphism; inverse comparisons give its inverse. Associativity only changes parentheses. Symmetry permutes the labelled blocks. Inserting or deleting a positive/inverse pair becomes identity after its evaluation, with any intervening interchanges retained. Thus these transformed arrows are determinants of actual projective isomorphisms. To check the only possible inverse-cancellation self-interchange, compare $0\to P\to P\oplus P\to P\to0$ with itself: a block unipotent acts trivially by naturality, since it acts identically on the ends. The exact block identity $E_{12}(1)E_{21}(-1)E_{12}(1)=S\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$, where $S$ swaps the two copies of $P$, therefore identifies the self-symmetry with $d(-1_P)$ under cancellation. Hence it too is an actual module automorphism, not an extra generator. Evaluation identities prove the negative-letter assertion by tensoring against its cancelled positive factors and cancelling that faithful tensor functor. These checks also apply inside tensor products of letters, so the procedure covers every finite expression, including inverse arrows. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 A loop starts and ends, after this operation, at the same $d(C)$. The composite of the actual intervening projective isomorphisms is therefore an automorphism $u$ of $C$ whose determinant is the given unit loop after cancelling $d(C)$. The assertion is independent of the chosen finite splittings: changing a splitting is a block unipotent, killed by the end-identity naturality just proved. Choose one finite projective complement $C'$ with $C\oplus C'\cong R^n$ by [F4]. Then $u\oplus1_{C'}$ is a free-module automorphism with the same cancelled determinant. If $n=0$, add $R$ with its identity to use the positive-size general linear groups of [F1]. This proves the finite-word fact and its stable-GL conclusion. Additional relations in the target can identify these loops but cannot create a new word, so the assertion remains valid in a quotient target. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 We must show that ALL universal loops reduce to the preceding words, including complex isomorphisms and triangles. We give the needed bounded-complex construction explicitly. For each $d(P)$ use the inverse $g(P[1])$ and evaluation supplied by the triangle $P\to0\to P[1]$; inverse objects of finite tensor words are obtained recursively. Thus no family of inverse choices is required. For an isomorphism $a:L\to M$, define $a^v:L^v\to M^v$ by $\delta_M(a\otimes a^v)=\delta_L$. Define $\vartheta:(L\otimes M)^v\to L^v\otimes M^v$ by evaluation after shuffling the labelled factors beside their duals. Tensoring by an invertible object is fully faithful, so these conditions uniquely determine the arrows. They prove their composition, associativity and symmetry compatibilities by evaluation: both proposed sides shuffle the same labelled factors and then apply the same evaluations. Adjacent shuffles obey involution, disjoint commutation and the three-factor braid relation by symmetry and the hexagon, retaining self-symmetries. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For a bounded projective complex $P$, put $D(P)=\bigotimes_{n\ \mathrm{decreasing}}d(P^n)^{(-1)^n}$. Define $I(A,B,C):D(A)\otimes D(C)\to D(B)$ for a termwise exact sequence by shuffling into decreasing degree, then applying $e$ in even degree and $e^v\vartheta^{-1}$ in odd degree. It is natural and has the three-by-three filtration and split-symmetry identities. Indeed in even degrees these are the corresponding identities of $e$; in odd degrees evaluate against the positive factors and use step 4.2 to reduce to the identical equation for $e$. The shuffles on the two sides are the same permutation of the labelled degree-and-row factors and agree by the elementary adjacent-shuffle relations there. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 For an acyclic identity disk $A\xrightarrow u B$ in degrees $m,m+1$, define its map $t:D\to1$ as follows. For odd $m$, use $\delta_{d(B)}(1\otimes d(u)^v)$ on $d(B)\otimes d(A)^v$. For even $m$, use $1\otimes d(u)$ on $d(B)^v\otimes d(A)$, interchange and evaluate. For a general acyclic complex $P$ with bottom degree $m$, its canonical two-term subcomplex $K_m(P)=(P^m\to dP^m)$ is such a disk; the quotient $L_m(P)$ is acyclic with finite-projective terms by [F5]. Define $t_P I(K_m(P),P,L_m(P))=t_{K_m(P)}\otimes t_{L_m(P)}$ recursively. This is natural under chain isomorphisms and multiplicative in an exact sequence of acyclic complexes. For disks multiplicativity is the evaluation identity of $\vartheta$ in its two parities. For longer complexes their bottom disks and quotients form a three-by-three exact diagram; substitute disk multiplicativity and induction into the filtration identity of step 5.1. This gives $t_B I(A,B,C)=t_A\otimes t_C$. Zero padding has unit comparisons, so it changes none of these maps. [F2, F4, F5, step 4.2, step 5.1, construct, induction, algebra]

7.1 For a degreewise injection $u:A\to B$ with acyclic finite-projective quotient $H$, put $D'(u)=I(A,B,H)(1\otimes t_H^{-1})$. For composable such injections the exact sequence of their three quotients and step 6.1 prove composition. The same three-by-three calculation gives naturality for an exact diagram of these injections. For a quasi-isomorphism $f:P\to Q$, use $Z_f^n=P^n\oplus Q^n\oplus P^{n+1}$, $d(p,q,v)=(dp-v,dq+fv,-dv)$. Its two inclusions $\alpha:P\to Z_f$, $\beta:Q\to Z_f$ have quotients $\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ and $\operatorname{Cone}(-1_P)$, so are such injections; put $D(f)=D'(\beta)^{-1}D'(\alpha)$. For composable $f:P\to Q$, $h:Q\to H$, the common complex with coordinates $(p,q,z,u,v)$ and differential $(dp-u,dq+fu-v,dz+hv,-du,-dv)$ receives $Z_f,Z_h,Z_{hf}$ by $(p,q,u)\mapsto(p,q,0,u,0)$, $(q,z,v)\mapsto(0,q,z,0,v)$, $(p,z,u)\mapsto(p,0,z,u,fu)$. Its three quotients are the corresponding acyclic cones. The three equalities on the original $P,Q,H$ inclusions, followed by cancellation of $D'$ maps, give $D(hf)=D(h)D(f)$. Thus $D(1)$, an invertible idempotent, is identity. Cylinders of an exact diagram are termwise exact; applying injection naturality twice proves naturality of $I$ for arbitrary quasi-isomorphisms. [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

7.2 We record why this construction agrees with any complex determinant extending $d$, rather than merely being a candidate. Write $P_n$ for a projective in degree $n$, and $B_n(P)$ for its identity disk in degrees $n,n+1$. For any such extension $G$, the zero quasi-isomorphism of the disk and $0\to P_{n+1}\to B_n(P)\to P_n\to0$ give a pairing $G(P_{n+1})\otimes G(P_n)\to1$. Starting with identity in degree zero, the pairings uniquely determine the comparison between $G(P_n)$ and $D(P_n)$ successively in both directions on the integer line. The disk sequence for an exact module sequence is a three-by-three diagram. Its filtration identity says the pairings are multiplicative with the prescribed middle shuffle. Evaluating against the degree-$n$ factors and cancelling them therefore proves that the determined comparison also preserves exact module sequences in degree $n+1$ and degree $n-1$. This finite induction is natural for module isomorphisms. [F2, F4, F5, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, induction, algebra]

8.1 The stupid filtration $0\to P_{\ge n+1}\to P_{\ge n}\to(P^n)_n\to0$ now determines a comparison $G(P)\to D(P)$ by finite descending induction. A three-by-three diagram of these filtrations for $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ proves compatibility with EVERY exact-sequence map: the higher truncated row is compatible by induction, the single-module quotient row by step 7.2, and substitution into the common filtration identity gives the desired middle-row equality. The comparison preserves acyclic $t$ by the bottom-disk recursion. Naturality for a good injection is then forced by its exact sequence and its quotient's zero map. For a general quasi-isomorphism use its cylinder: $Z_f\to Q$, $(p,q,v)\mapsto fp+q$, is a quasi-isomorphism and has composites $f,1_Q$ with the two inclusions, so both $G$ and $D$ evaluate $f$ by the same ratio of those inclusions. Thus the comparison respects every quasi-isomorphism. This also proves uniqueness: disk pairings force the shifted module comparisons, and stupid filtrations force the rest. All comparisons for a given finite diagram use finitely many steps. [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 7.2, construct, induction, algebra]

9.1 Apply steps 7.2–8.1 to the restriction of $g$ to bounded projective complexes. The exact-sequence maps come from their distinguished triangles; their filtration diagrams are octahedral diagrams in [F2]. A quasi-isomorphism is a derived isomorphism. Hence complex-isomorphism arrows, after these object comparisons, are expressions in the projective letters of step 2.1: all $D,I,t$ formulas in steps 4.2–7.1 use only those letters and their finite inverse/evaluation operations. Chain homotopic quasi-isomorphisms give the same $D$ arrow: the cylinder $P\oplus P\oplus P[1]$ with differential $(dp_0+v,dp_1-v,-dv)$ projects by $p_0+p_1$ to $P$ with contractible kernel, while a homotopy $f-h=ds+sd$ gives the chain map $(p_0,p_1,v)\mapsto fp_0+hp_1+sv$ to the common target. Functoriality makes the two inclusion images equal, so $D(f)=D(h)$. This matches exactly the derived-isomorphism interpretation of [F6], and covers roofs after replacing their finitely many perfect objects by bounded projective representatives. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 7.2, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Every distinguished triangle between these representatives is isomorphic to a degreewise split exact sequence triangle of bounded projectives. For its cone model $P\xrightarrow f Q\to\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ use $0\to P\to Z_f\to\operatorname{Cone}(f)\to0$ and the quasi-isomorphism $Z_f\to Q$ of step 8.1; the sign is checked on $\operatorname{Cone}(\alpha)$: its quotient to $\operatorname{Cone}(f)$ sends $((p,q,u),v)$ to $(q,u)$ and has contractible kernel. Its projection $v$ to $P[1]$ differs from $u$ by the homotopy given by the first coordinate $p$, since $-dp+(dp-u+v)=v-u$. Thus the last map is the usual positive cone projection in the derived category, not its negative. For a general triangle use its isomorphism to that cone triangle from [F6]. Naturality of $g_2$ therefore expresses its triangle arrow in the exact-sequence comparison just normalized and the derived-isomorphism arrows of step 9.1. This step does not assume regularity or that cohomology modules are projective. [F2, F4, F6, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, construct, algebra]

11.1 Finally use the actual finite-word universal model of [F3]. A unit automorphism is represented by one finite arrow word. Only finitely many perfect objects, isomorphisms and triangles occur in it. Choose bounded projective representatives for those finitely many objects by [F6], and insert their isomorphisms on the word's edges; these internal object comparisons cancel in compositions and tensors. Apply steps 9.1–10.1. The loop is now a finite word of exactly the split projective type in step 2.1; the comparisons at zero are the unit comparisons. Steps 2.1–4.1 produce an automorphism of a finite free module with that same unit automorphism. Thus every element of $K_1^{\det}(D_{\mathrm{perf}}(R))$ is in the canonical map's image. The zero ring, empty term supports and identity loops are included by the zero module and unit maps. Only finite choices were used for each loop; no global basis, regularity, maximal-order or Smith-normal-form hypothesis was introduced. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, construct, algebra] ∎
