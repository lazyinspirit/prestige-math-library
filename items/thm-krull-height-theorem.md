## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring, let
$$ I=(x_1,\ldots,x_n) $$
be an ideal generated by $n\ge1$ elements, and let $\mathfrak p$ be a prime ideal minimal over $I$. Then $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\le n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Noetherian commutative ring $R$, an $n$-generated ideal $I=(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ with $n\ge1$, and a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ minimal over $I$.

[L1] The case $n=1$ is Krull's principal ideal theorem ([[thm-krull-principal-ideal-theorem]]).

[L2] Under the assumed Axiom of Choice, for $n\ge2$ and a positive-length chain, one may replace the first generator by an element $b$ from $\mathfrak p_1$ of a same-length chain so that $\mathfrak p$ is minimal over $(b,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$ ([[lem-height-theorem-first-generator-reduction]]).

[L3] Under the same Choice assumption, after quotienting by that chosen $b$, the image $\mathfrak p/(b)$ is minimal over $n-1$ generators and a chain ending at $\mathfrak p$ loses one step ([[lem-height-theorem-quotient-induction]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction on the number of generators.

1.1 If $n=1$, [L1] gives $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\le1$. [L1, base, given]

1.2 Assume $n\ge2$ and that the theorem is known for $(n-1)$-generated ideals. Suppose for contradiction that $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\ge n+1$. Then there exists a strict prime chain $$ \mathfrak p=\mathfrak p_d\supsetneq\mathfrak p_{d-1}\supsetneq\cdots\supsetneq\mathfrak p_0 $$ with $d\ge n+1\ge3$. Under the assumed AC, [L2] replaces $x_1$ by a suitable element $b\in\mathfrak p_1$ of a same-length chain for which $\mathfrak p$ is minimal over $(b,x_2,\ldots,x_n)$. [L2, ih, given]

2.1 By [L3], the quotient prime $\mathfrak p/(b)$ is minimal over the $(n-1)$-generated ideal generated by the images of $x_2,\ldots,x_n$, and the above chain descends to a strict chain of length $d-1\ge n$ ending at $\mathfrak p/(b)$ in $R/(b)$. This contradicts the induction hypothesis for $(n-1)$ generators. [L3, step 1.2, ih]

3.1 Therefore the assumption $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\ge n+1$ is impossible, and $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)\le n$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, discharge-induction] ∎
