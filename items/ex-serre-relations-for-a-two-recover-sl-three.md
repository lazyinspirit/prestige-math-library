## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice; it is inherited from the Serre presentation theorem used below.

For $A=\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$ the Serre generators map to
$e_1\mapsto E_{12}$, $e_2\mapsto E_{23}$, $f_1\mapsto E_{21}$,
$f_2\mapsto E_{32}$, $h_1\mapsto E_{11}-E_{22}$,
$h_2\mapsto E_{22}-E_{33}$ in $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$, and this
assignment is an isomorphism $\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; the Cartan matrix $A$ of $A_2$, the Serre algebra $\mathfrak g(A)$ and the matrices in $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$.

[A1] The standing AC assumption is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is inherited through the Serre triangular-decomposition theorem in [L1].

[L1] $\mathfrak g(A)$ is presented by the Serre generators and relations, and has the triangular decomposition $\mathfrak n_-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n_+$, where $\mathfrak h$ is spanned by $h_1,h_2$ and $\mathfrak n_-$ is generated by $f_1,f_2$ while $\mathfrak n_+$ is generated by $e_1,e_2$ ([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]], [[thm-serre-presentation-theorem]]).

[L2] $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$ is the Lie algebra of traceless $3\times3$ matrices with the commutator ([[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]]); direct multiplication of matrix units gives $E_{ij}E_{kl}=\delta_{jk}E_{il}$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The images satisfy the Cartan and generator relations: $[h_i,h_j]=0$; $[h_1,E_{12}]=2E_{12}$, $[h_1,E_{23}]=-E_{23}$, $[h_2,E_{12}]=-E_{12}$, $[h_2,E_{23}]=2E_{23}$ and the negatives on the $f$'s; $[E_{12},E_{21}]=h_1$, $[E_{23},E_{32}]=h_2$, and the cross brackets $[E_{12},E_{32}]$ and $[E_{23},E_{21}]$ vanish. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 Direct multiplication also gives $(\operatorname{ad}E_{12})^2E_{23}=(\operatorname{ad}E_{23})^2E_{12}=(\operatorname{ad}E_{21})^2E_{32}=(\operatorname{ad}E_{32})^2E_{21}=0$, so all four Serre relations hold. Hence the assignment induces a Lie-algebra homomorphism $\varphi:\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$. [L1, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The images generate $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$: $[E_{12},E_{23}]=E_{13}$, $[E_{23},E_{12}]=-E_{13}$, and $[E_{21},E_{32}]=-E_{31}$. Thus the image contains all six off-diagonal matrix units and the independent diagonal matrices $h_1,h_2$, which form a basis of the eight-dimensional space of traceless $3\times3$ matrices. Hence $\varphi$ is surjective. [L2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Put $z=[e_1,e_2]$. The positive Serre relations give $[e_1,z]=[e_2,z]=0$, so $\operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,z)$ is a Lie subalgebra containing the positive generators and contained in the subalgebra they generate; hence it equals $\mathfrak n_+$. With $w=[f_1,f_2]$, the negative Serre relations likewise give $[f_1,w]=[f_2,w]=0$, so $\mathfrak n_-=\operatorname{span}(f_1,f_2,w)$ and each half has dimension at most three. Since $\mathfrak h$ is spanned by $h_1,h_2$, the triangular decomposition in [L1] gives $\dim\mathfrak g(A)\le8$. Surjectivity from step 3.1 onto the eight-dimensional algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$ gives the reverse inequality, so $\dim\mathfrak g(A)=8$ and $\ker\varphi=0$. Thus $\varphi$ is the asserted isomorphism. [A1, L1, L2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
