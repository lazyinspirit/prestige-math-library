## Statement

Let $X$ be a locally Noetherian scheme and $x\in X$. Write
$R=\mathcal O_{X,x}$, let $\mathfrak m_x$ be its maximal ideal, and set
$\kappa(x)=R/\mathfrak m_x$. The point $x$ is **regular** when $R$ is a
regular local ring, with regularity defined by
$\operatorname{edim}R=\dim R$. Then the intrinsic tangent space $T_xX$ is
finite-dimensional over $\kappa(x)$, and
$$x\text{ is regular}\Longleftrightarrow\dim_{\kappa(x)}T_xX=\dim\mathcal O_{X,x}.$$
This is absolute regularity of the local ring; it asserts no smoothness over a
base field.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A locally Noetherian scheme $X$ and a point $x\in X$.

[F1] [[def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]]: a locally Noetherian scheme has an affine open cover by spectra of Noetherian rings.

[F2] [[def-affine-open-subscheme]]: an open subscheme carries the restricted structure sheaf, and it is affine when that restricted locally ringed space is affine.

[F3] [[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]: the stalk $\mathcal F_x$ is the filtered colimit of $\mathcal F(U)$ over open neighborhoods $U$ of $x$.

[F4] [[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]: for a point $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}B$, the affine structure-sheaf stalk is canonically $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}B,\mathfrak p}\cong B_{\mathfrak p}$.

[F5] [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]: $B_{\mathfrak p}$ consists of fractions $b/s$ with $s\notin\mathfrak p$.

[F6] [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]: $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is a nonzero local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$.

[F7] [[def-noetherian-ring]]: a ring is left Noetherian when its left regular module is Noetherian; here $B$ is commutative, so its ideals are submodules of that regular module.

[F8] [[def-noetherian-module]]: every submodule of a Noetherian module is finitely generated.

[F9] [[def-zariski-tangent-space-point]]: $T_xX=\operatorname{Hom}_{\kappa(x)}(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2,\kappa(x))$.

[F10] [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]: for a nonzero Noetherian local ring, $\operatorname{edim}R=\dim_{\kappa(x)}(\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2)$, and $R$ is regular local exactly when $\operatorname{edim}R=\dim R$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Noetherian local stalk. Fix $x$. By [F1], there is an affine open neighborhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ of $x$ with $B$ Noetherian. Because the sheaf on $U$ is the restriction from $X$ [F2], neighborhoods of $x$ contained in $U$ are cofinal among its neighborhoods in $X$; the stalk-colimit description [F3] therefore identifies $\mathcal O_{X,x}$ with $\mathcal O_{U,x}$. Let $\mathfrak p\subset B$ be the prime corresponding to $x$. By [F4], $R\cong B_{\mathfrak p}$, and [F6] makes this a nonzero local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$. We verify Noetherianity directly. Let $J$ be any ideal of $B_{\mathfrak p}$ and contract it to $I=\{b\in B:b/1\in J\}$. This is an ideal of $B$, hence a submodule of its regular module [F7]; by [F8], take a finite generating list $b_1,\ldots,b_q$ of $I$. If $a/s\in J$, then [F5] and the ideal property give $a/1=(s/1)(a/s)\in J$, so $a\in I$ and $a=\sum_i c_i b_i$. It follows that $a/s=\sum_i(c_i/s)(b_i/1)$. Conversely every $b_i/1$ lies in $J$, so these images generate $J$. Thus every ideal of $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is finitely generated and $R$ is Noetherian. This uses a chart for the fixed point and a finite list for the fixed ideal, not a simultaneous choice over all points or ideals. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, given, algebra]

2.1 Intrinsic tangent dimension and regularity. By step 1.1, $R$ is Noetherian local, so its maximal ideal is finitely generated by [F7, F8]. The images of a finite generating list span $V=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2$ over $\kappa(x)$; hence $V$ is finite-dimensional. A finite basis of $V$ gives the same number of dual basis vectors, so [F9] yields $\dim_{\kappa(x)}T_xX=\dim_{\kappa(x)}V=\operatorname{edim}R$ by [F10]. Therefore $R$ is regular local if and only if $\dim_{\kappa(x)}T_xX=\dim R$, proving both directions. If $\dim R=0$ or $1$, this is respectively the equality $\operatorname{edim}R=0$ or $1$; if $\mathfrak m_x=0$, both criteria reduce to $0=\dim R$. Since $T_xX$ is finite-dimensional, an infinite value of $\dim R$ cannot satisfy either criterion. If $X$ is empty, there is no point to test. The argument uses only finite generation and finite-dimensional linear algebra, so neither AC nor DC is used. [step 1.1, F7, F8, F9, F10, given, algebra] ∎
