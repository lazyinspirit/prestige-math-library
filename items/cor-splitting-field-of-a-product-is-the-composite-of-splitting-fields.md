## Statement

Let $f,g\in F[x]$ be nonzero. Inside a common field extension $\Omega/F$, let $E_f$ and $E_g$ be their respective splitting fields. Then the composite $E_fE_g$ inside $\Omega$ is a splitting field of $fg$ over $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Nonzero $f,g\in F[x]$ and splitting fields $E_f,E_g\subseteq\Omega$.

[F1] A splitting field is the subfield generated over $F$ by all roots of the polynomial, over which that polynomial splits ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]).

[F2] The composite $E_fE_g$ is the smallest subfield of $\Omega$ containing both $E_f$ and $E_g$ ([[cor-composite-of-two-subfields]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Both $f$ and $g$ split over $E_fE_g$ because the composite contains their splitting fields. Hence their product $fg$ splits there. [F1, F2]

2.1 In the field $\Omega$, an element $a$ is a root of $fg$ exactly when $f(a)g(a)=0$, hence exactly when it is a root of $f$ or of $g$. Therefore the field generated by the roots of $fg$ is the field generated jointly by $E_f$ and $E_g$, which is $E_fE_g$ by [F2]. [F1, F2, algebra] ∎
