## Example

**(i) Two conjugate reflections generating a parabolic subgroup that is not
standard.** Let $W=S_4$ with simple reflections $s_1=(1\,2)$, $s_2=(2\,3)$,
$s_3=(3\,4)$, in the type-$A_3$ identification of
[[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (4), and let

$$H:=\langle (1\,3),\,(2\,4)\rangle\le S_4.$$

Writing one-line notation, $(1\,3)=3214$ and $(2\,4)=1432$, and

$$H=\{1234,\ 1432,\ 3214,\ 3412\}$$

has order $4$; moreover $(1\,3)=s_2s_1s_2$, $(2\,4)=s_2s_3s_2$, so

$$H=s_2\,W_{\{s_1,s_3\}}\,s_2,$$

i.e. $H$ is the conjugate of a standard parabolic subgroup. The two generators
are conjugate in $S_4$: indeed $(2\,4)=w(1\,3)w^{-1}$ for $w=(1\,2)(3\,4)=2143$.
Finally $H$ is not equal to any of the eight standard parabolic subgroups $W_I$
($I\subseteq\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$) of $S_4$. Thus $H$ is a parabolic subgroup (a
conjugate of a standard one) which is not standard, generated by two conjugate
reflections: "standard parabolic" is strictly stronger than "parabolic".

**(ii) A reflection subgroup that is not parabolic.** Let $W=\langle s,t\rangle$
be the infinite dihedral group with $m(s,t)=\infty$ and put $u:=st$
([[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]] (3)(c), (4)). The element
$tst$ is a reflection (a conjugate of $s$), and

$$H':=\langle s,\ tst\rangle=\langle s,\ u^2\rangle=\{u^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{u^{2k}s:k\in\mathbb Z\}\le W$$

is a subgroup generated by the two reflections $s$ and $tst$, i.e. a reflection
subgroup. It contains $u^2$ and $u^{-2}$, has index $2$ in $W$, and is infinite;
consequently it is not equal to $W$ and not one of the order-two subgroups
$\{1,r\}$, and the parabolic subgroups of $W$ are exactly $\{1\}$, the order-two
subgroups $\{1,r\}$ generated by a reflection $r\in T$, and $W$ itself, so that
$H'$ is a reflection subgroup which is *not* a parabolic subgroup. Together with
(i) this shows that the three classes

$$\text{standard parabolic}\ \subsetneq\ \text{parabolic}\ \subsetneq\ \text{reflection subgroup}$$

are pairwise distinct, where the first inclusion is the definition and the
others are the two computations above.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Coxeter group $W$ of type $A_3$ with $S=\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$, identified with $S_4$ by $s_i\mapsto(i\ i+1)$, and the infinite dihedral group $W=\langle s,t\rangle$ with $m(s,t)=\infty$.

[F1] For the type-$A_{n-1}$ matrix the assignment $s_i\mapsto(i\ i+1)$ extends to an isomorphism $W\to S_n$ with $\ell(w)=\operatorname{inv}(\varphi(w))$; for every $J\subseteq S$ one has $W_J=\{w\in W:S(w)\subseteq J\}$, $(W_J,J)$ is a Coxeter system and $W_J\cap S=J$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]).

[F2] $W_I=\langle s:s\in I\rangle$ is the standard parabolic subgroup of type $I$; a parabolic subgroup is a conjugate $wW_Iw^{-1}$, a reflection subgroup is one generated by the reflections it contains, and every parabolic subgroup is a reflection subgroup ([[def-cg-parabolic-quotient-and-two-sided-minima]]).

[F3] If $m(s,t)=\infty$ then $st$ has infinite order in $W$ and $s\ne t$, and the alternating words in $s,t$ are reduced in the ambient group: the value of an alternating word of length $q\ge1$ has length exactly $q$ ([[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]]).

[F4] $T=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$ is the set of reflections of $W$; it contains every conjugate of every simple reflection, and $wsw^{-1}$ is a reflection for all $w\in W$, $s\in S$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]).

[F5] The presented group $W$ of [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]] has the universal property: every assignment of the generators to elements of a group satisfying the defining relators extends uniquely to a homomorphism.

[F6] A group homomorphism $\varphi:G\to G'$ satisfies $\varphi(xy)=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)$ and $\varphi(x^{-1})=\varphi(x)^{-1}$ for all $x,y\in G$, hence $\varphi(ghg^{-1})=\varphi(g)\varphi(h)\varphi(g)^{-1}$ for all $g,h\in G$ ([[def-group-homomorphism]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct finite computations in $S_4$ and reduced-word arithmetic in the infinite dihedral group.

1.1 *The subgroup $H$.* The one-line displays $(1\,3)=3214$ and $(2\,4)=1432$ record that these permutations swap $1\leftrightarrow3$ and $2\leftrightarrow4$ respectively and fix the remaining points. Both are involutions with disjoint supports, so they commute and $H=\{1,(1\,3),(2\,4),(1\,3)(2\,4)\}$, of order $4$; the product is computed by applying the right factor first, $(1\,3)(2\,4)\colon1\mapsto3$, $2\mapsto4$, $3\mapsto1$, $4\mapsto2$, hence $(1\,3)(2\,4)=3412$ and $H=\{1234,1432,3214,3412\}$. Further $s_2s_1s_2=(2\,3)(1\,2)(2\,3)=(1\,3)$ and $s_2s_3s_2=(2\,4)$, as direct computations in cycle notation; the two generators $s_1,s_3$ commute, so $W_{\{s_1,s_3\}}=\{1,s_1,s_3,s_1s_3\}=\{1234,2134,1243,2143\}$, and conjugating by $s_2$ gives $H=\langle s_2s_1s_2,\,s_2s_3s_2\rangle=s_2W_{\{s_1,s_3\}}s_2$, so $H$ is a parabolic subgroup by [F2]. Finally $w=(1\,2)(3\,4)=2143$ satisfies $w(1)=2$, $w(3)=4$ and $w^{-1}(2)=1$, $w^{-1}(4)=3$, so $w(1\,3)w^{-1}$ swaps $2$ and $4$ and fixes $1,3$, that is, $w(1\,3)w^{-1}=(2\,4)$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 *The subgroup $H'$.* Put $u:=st$. Every word in $s,t$ can be shortened by cancelling adjacent equal letters $ss$ or $tt$ until it alternates, so an alternating word is $(st)^k$, $(ts)^k$, $(st)^ks$ or $(ts)^ks$ for some $k\ge0$; since $(ts)^k=u^{-k}$ and $(ts)^ks=u^{-k}s$, we get $W=\{u^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{u^ks:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, with distinct powers because $u$ has infinite order. The two families are disjoint: the separate homomorphism $\chi:W\to\mathbb Z/2$ defined by $s\mapsto1$, $t\mapsto1$ exists by [F5] and takes $u^k$ to $0$ and $u^ks$ to $1$. Since $s=s^{-1}$ and $t=t^{-1}$ we have $sus^{-1}=s(st)s=ts=u^{-1}$ and hence $su^{2m}s=u^{-2m}$ for all $m$. In particular $tst=t(st)t\cdot t=(ts)^2s=u^{-2}s$, so $tst$ is a reflection by [F4], and $tst=u^{-2}s\in\langle s,u^2\rangle$; conversely $s\cdot(u^{-2}s)=su^{-2}s=u^{2}$ and $(u^{-2}s)\cdot s=u^{-2}$, so $\langle s,tst\rangle=\langle s,u^2\rangle$. The set $S_0:=\{u^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{u^{2k}s:k\in\mathbb Z\}$ is closed under multiplication: the four product types use $su^{2m}s=u^{-2m}$ and give $u^{2k}u^{2m}=u^{2(k+m)}$, $u^{2k}s\cdot u^{2m}s=u^{2(k-m)}$, $u^{2k}\cdot u^{2m}s=u^{2(k+m)}s$ and $u^{2k}s\cdot u^{2m}=u^{2(k-m)}s$; it contains $s=u^0s$ and $u^2$, and each of its elements is a product of $s$ and $u^2$; hence $S_0=\langle s,u^2\rangle=H'$. The assignment $s\mapsto0$, $t\mapsto1$ sends the relators $s^2$ and $t^2$ to $0$, so by [F5] it defines a homomorphism $\varphi\colon W\to\mathbb Z/2$; by [F6], $\varphi(u^k)=k\bmod2$ and $\varphi(u^ks)=k\bmod2$, so the set $H_0:=\{w:\varphi(w)=0\}$ equals $\{u^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{u^{2k}s:k\in\mathbb Z\}=S_0=H'$, and since $\varphi(t)=1$ the complement of $H_0$ is the single coset $H_0t$; hence $H'$ has exactly two cosets in $W$, of which it is one, so it has index $2$. Since $u$ has infinite order by [F3], $W$ is infinite and $H'$, of index $2$, is infinite as well. [F3, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 *$H$ is not standard.* By [F1] the standard parabolic subgroups of $S_4$ are $W_J=\{w:S(w)\subseteq J\}$ for the eight subsets $J\subseteq\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$, and $W_J\cap S=J$ makes distinct subsets give distinct subgroups. Listing by the defining relations: $W_\emptyset=\{1234\}$, $W_{\{s_1\}}=\{1234,2134\}$, $W_{\{s_2\}}=\{1234,1324\}$, $W_{\{s_3\}}=\{1234,1243\}$, $W_{\{s_1,s_2\}}=\{1234,2134,1324,2314,3124,3214\}$, $W_{\{s_2,s_3\}}=\{1234,1324,1243,1342,1423,1432\}$, $W_{\{s_1,s_3\}}=\{1234,2134,1243,2143\}$ and $W_S=S_4$, of orders $1,2,2,2,6,6,4,24$. By step 1.1, $H$ has order $4$ and contains $3214$; the only standard parabolic of order $4$ is $W_{\{s_1,s_3\}}$, whose displayed list does not contain $3214$, and no other $W_J$ has order $4$. Hence $H\neq W_J$ for every $J$, while $H$ is parabolic by step 1.1: the inclusion "standard parabolic $\subsetneq$ parabolic" is strict. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 *$H'$ is not parabolic.* By [F2] the parabolic subgroups of $W$ are the conjugates of $W_\emptyset=\{1\}$, $W_{\{s\}}=\{1,s\}$, $W_{\{t\}}=\{1,t\}$ and $W_{\{s,t\}}=W$. The subgroups $\{1\}$ and $W$ are their own conjugates; the conjugates of $\{1,s\}$ or of $\{1,t\}$ are the subgroups $\{1,r\}$ with $r\in T$, and conversely every $r\in T$ is a conjugate of the simple reflection $s$ or of the simple reflection $t$ by [F4], so the conjugates of these two standard parabolics are exactly the subgroups $\{1,r\}$, $r\in T$. By step 1.2 the group $H'$ contains $s$, and $s\neq1$ because $s=1$ would force $st=t$ to have order $2$, against [F3]; further $H'$ is infinite and has index $2$ in $W$, so $t\notin H'$ (indeed $\varphi(t)=1$) and $H'\neq W$. Hence $H'$ is none of $\{1\}$, of the two-element subgroups $\{1,r\}$, or of $W$: $H'$ is a reflection subgroup that is not parabolic, and the inclusion "parabolic $\subsetneq$ reflection subgroup" is strict. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 *The three classes.* By definition every standard parabolic subgroup is parabolic, and by [F2] every parabolic subgroup is a reflection subgroup, so the two inclusions of the statement hold; by step 2.1 the first is strict and by step 2.2 the second is strict, and the examples $H$ (parabolic, not standard) and $H'$ (reflection subgroup, not parabolic) exhibit the strictness. This completes the example. [F2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
