## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $n\ge1$, assume $\operatorname{char}F\nmid n$, and
assume $\mu_n\subseteq F$. Let

$$(F^\times)^n\subseteq B\subseteq F^\times$$

have finite quotient, and let $K=F(B^{1/n})$ be the associated finite Kummer
extension ([[def-kummer-extension]]). Then the rule

$$\langle \sigma,\; b(F^\times)^n\rangle:=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta},\qquad \beta^n=b,$$

defines a well-defined bilinear pairing

$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\times B/(F^\times)^n\to\mu_n,$$

and it is nondegenerate in both variables. In this sense the Kummer pairing is
perfect.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The field $F$, the integer $n$, the subgroup $B$, the Kummer extension $K=F(B^{1/n})$, an automorphism $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$, and an element $b\in B$ with chosen $n$-th root $\beta\in K$.

[F1] A Kummer extension is a finite Galois extension generated by $n$-th roots of elements of $B$, with $\mu_n\subseteq F$ ([[def-kummer-extension]]).

[L1] For a finite Galois extension, the fixed field of the full Galois group is the base field ([[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Because $\sigma(\beta)^n=\sigma(b)=b=\beta^n$, the quotient $\sigma(\beta)/\beta$ is an $n$-th root of unity, so it lies in $\mu_n\subseteq F$. If $\beta'=\zeta\beta$ is another chosen $n$-th root of $b$, then $$\frac{\sigma(\beta')}{\beta'}=\frac{\sigma(\zeta)\sigma(\beta)}{\zeta\beta}=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta},$$ since $\sigma$ fixes $F$ and hence $\zeta$. If $b'=bc^n$ represents the same class in $B/(F^\times)^n$, then choosing $\beta'=\beta c$ gives the same quotient. Thus the pairing is well defined. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Bilinearity is immediate: $$\left\langle \sigma\tau,\bar b\right\rangle=\frac{\sigma\tau(\beta)}{\beta}=\frac{\sigma(\tau(\beta))}{\tau(\beta)}\frac{\tau(\beta)}{\beta}=\langle \sigma,\bar b\rangle\langle \tau,\bar b\rangle,$$ and for classes $\bar b,\bar c$ represented by roots $\beta,\gamma$, $$\langle \sigma,\bar b\bar c\rangle=\frac{\sigma(\beta\gamma)}{\beta\gamma}=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\frac{\sigma(\gamma)}{\gamma}=\langle \sigma,\bar b\rangle\langle \sigma,\bar c\rangle.$$ [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For nondegeneracy on the Galois side, let $\sigma\ne1$. Since $K$ is generated over $F$ by the chosen $n$-th roots of elements of $B$, some such root $\beta$ satisfies $\sigma(\beta)\ne\beta$. For the class $\bar b$ of $b=\beta^n$, one then has $$\langle \sigma,\bar b\rangle=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\ne1.$$ So no nontrivial automorphism lies in the left kernel. [F1, step 1.1]

2.3 For nondegeneracy on the $B/(F^\times)^n$ side, let $\bar b$ be nontrivial. Then $\beta\notin F$, for otherwise $b=\beta^n$ would lie in $(F^\times)^n$. By [F1] and [L1], some $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ satisfies $\sigma(\beta)\ne\beta$. Hence $$\langle \sigma,\bar b\rangle=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\ne1,$$ so no nontrivial class lies in the right kernel. [F1, L1, step 1.1]

3.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1, 2.2, and 2.3 prove the stated well-defined bilinear pairing and its nondegeneracy in both variables. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3] ∎
