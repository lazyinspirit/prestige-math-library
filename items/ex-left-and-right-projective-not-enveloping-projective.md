## Example

Let $k$ be a field and let $A=k[x]$, with $x$ in internal degree zero. Put
$F=A$ in cochain degree zero and zero in every other cochain degree. Then $F$
is finite graded projective as a left $A$-module and projective as an underlying
right $A$-module, and $F\otimes_A-$ is naturally the identity on bounded left
$A$-complexes. However, the diagonal bimodule $A$ is not projective as a left
module over its enveloping algebra $A^e=A\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{op}}$.

## Verification

**Given:** A field $k$, the polynomial algebra $A=k[x]$ graded entirely in
internal degree zero, and the one-term cochain complex $F=A$.

[L1] Field multiplication on all of $k$ is associative and commutative with
identity $1$ ([[def-field]]).

[L2] Every field is a commutative ring with $1\ne0$ and an integral domain
([[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L3] An integral domain is a commutative ring with $1\ne0$ and no zero divisors
([[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]]).

[L4] A polynomial ring on two indeterminates has finite-support coefficients
indexed by monomials ([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]]).

[L5] The notation $k[x,y]$ can be taken as the iterated ring $k[x][y]$
([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L6] A polynomial ring in finitely many indeterminates over a domain is a
domain, including the case of two indeterminates
([[cor-multivariate-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]).

[L7] Every tensor is a finite sum of elementary tensors
([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]).

[L8] The tensor relations include $(mr)\otimes n=m\otimes(rn)$ for a right
$R$-module $M$ and left $R$-module $N$
([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]).

[L9] A projective module lifts every module map across every surjective module
map ([[def-projective-module]]).

[L10] The regular graded module $A\{0\}$ is one of the finite shifted-free
modules that are finite graded projective
([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[L11] A bounded bimodule complex with finite graded projective left terms and
projective underlying right terms computes its exact derived tensor functor by
ordinary signed totalization
([[thm-a-bounded-projective-bimodule-complex-defines-a-derived-tensor-functor]]).

1.1 The regular left $A$-module is $A\{0\}$, generated by its degree-zero unit; [L10] makes it finite graded projective. Thus the sole nonzero term of $F$ meets the left projectivity hypothesis. [L10, given, algebra]

1.2 By [L2, L6] with one and two indeterminates, $A=k[x]$ and $R:=k[x,y]$ are domains, hence commutative rings by [L3]. Define the enveloping action by $(a\otimes b^{\mathrm{op}})\cdot m=amb$. The map $R\to A\otimes_k A^{\mathrm{op}}$ sending $x^iy^j$ to $x^i\otimes(x^j)^{\mathrm{op}}$ is multiplicative because $A$ is commutative; its inverse sends $f(x)\otimes g(x)^{\mathrm{op}}$ to $f(x)g(y)$. This inverse is balanced over $k$, and the maps are inverse on the monomials and elementary tensors that span their modules by [L4, L5, L7, L8]. Thus $A^e$ is isomorphic to the commutative ring $R$. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, L7, L8, algebra]

2.1 The underlying right regular module is projective: given a surjection $q:E\twoheadrightarrow M$ of right $A$-modules and a map $g:A\to M$, choose $e\in E$ with $q(e)=g(1)$ and define $\widetilde g(a)=ea$; then $q\widetilde g=g$. Since the complex $F$ is concentrated in degree zero, $A\otimes_A X\to X$, $a\otimes z\mapsto az$, is a natural chain isomorphism for every bounded left $A$-complex $X$, with inverse $z\mapsto1\otimes z$. Under the standing size convention in [L11], its derived tensor functor is represented by this ordinary tensor operation as well. [L9, L11, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 In these coordinates the action on $A$ is induced by the surjective ring map $\mu:R\to A$, $f(x,y)\mapsto f(x,x)$; it is surjective since every $g(x)\in A$ is $\mu(g(x))$. For a monomial $x^iy^j$ with $j\ge1$, $x^iy^j-x^{i+j}=x^i(y-x)\sum_{r=0}^{j-1}y^{j-1-r}x^r$, while the difference is zero for $j=0$; by finite support [L4], $f-f(x,x)\in(x-y)$ for every $f$. Hence $\ker\mu=(x-y)$. This ideal is nonzero because the distinct monomials $x$ and $y$ have nonzero coefficients, and proper because $\mu(1)=1\ne0$. [L2, L3, L4, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Suppose for contradiction that $A$ is projective as a left $R$-module. Since $\mu$ is a surjection, [L9] lifts $\operatorname{id}_A$ to an $R$-linear map $s:A\to R$ with $\mu s=\operatorname{id}_A$. Then $p=\operatorname{id}_R-s\mu$ is an $R$-linear projection onto $I:=\ker\mu$: $p(R)\subseteq I$ and $p(i)=i$ for $i\in I$. Every $R$-linear map $p:R\to R$ is multiplication by $e:=p(1)$, so $I=Re$; also $p^2=p$ gives $e^2=e$. [L9, step 2.2, given, assume-contra]

4.1 By [L2, L3, and L6], $R$ is a commutative domain. Thus $e^2=e$ implies $e(e-1)=0$, so $e=0$ or $e=1$. Then $I=Re$ is either zero or all of $R$, contradicting Step 2.2, where $I$ was shown nonzero and proper. Therefore $A$ is not projective over $A^e$. [L2, L3, L6, step 2.2, step 3.1, discharge-contradiction: the kernel is nonzero and proper]

5.1 The zero polynomial has empty support and lies in $\ker\mu$; the kernel calculation includes empty finite sums. By [L2], $1\ne0$ in $k$, so the zero algebra is excluded. In characteristic two, $x-y=x+y$ still has two distinct monomials, so $I$ remains nonzero and proper. The complex $F$ has exactly one nonzero cochain term in degree zero, with zero differential, so both endpoints are covered by the unit calculation. The only lift used is the single lift of $\operatorname{id}_A$ supplied by projectivity; no family of choices or AC is used. This example proves no iff statement. [L2, L4, L9, L10, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, given, algebra] $\square$
