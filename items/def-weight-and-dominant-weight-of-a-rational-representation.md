## Definition

Let $k$ be a field, let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group over $k$ with Weyl
group $W$ and root datum $(X,\Phi,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$, $X=X(T)$, and let
$B\supseteq T$ be a Borel subgroup with system of positive roots $\Phi^+$ and
base $\Delta$
([[def-split-reductive-algebraic-group]],
[[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]],
[[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]],
[[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]]). For a rational
representation $(V,r)$ of $G$
([[def-rational-representation-and-comodule-of-an-affine-group-scheme]]) the
$T$-weight decomposition is $V=\bigoplus_{\chi\in X}V_\chi$ with
$V_\chi=\{v\in V:\rho(v)=v\otimes e_\chi\}$
([[lem-representations-of-diagonalizable-groups-are-sums-of-eigenspaces]]); the
**weights** of $(V,r)$ are the characters $\chi$ with $V_\chi\ne0$, and
$V_\chi$ is the **weight space** of $\chi$. An element $\lambda\in X$ is
**dominant** if $\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\ge0$ for every
$\alpha\in\Phi^+$ (equivalently, for every $\alpha\in\Delta$); write $X^+$ for
the set of dominant weights. Define a partial order on $X$ by $\lambda\ge\mu$ if
and only if
$\lambda-\mu=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with
$m_\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$. The **fundamental weights** $\omega_i$
($i\in\Delta$) are the unique elements of $\mathbb Q\Phi\subseteq
X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$ dual to the simple coroots,
$\langle\omega_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=\delta_{ij}$; they form a
basis of $\mathbb Q\Phi$ and every $\lambda\in X\otimes\mathbb Q$
decomposes as
$\lambda=\sum_i\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\omega_i+\lambda_0$ with
$\lambda_0$ pairing to $0$ with every coroot
([[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

## Remarks

- **Conventions.** Dominance is tested on the positive system $\Phi^+$, which
  is the system of roots of $B$; the equivalence of testing on $\Phi^+$ or on
  the base $\Delta$ holds for coroot pairings as follows. Average an inner
  product on $X\otimes\mathbb R$ over the finite Weyl group of
  [[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]. Each root reflection is then orthogonal,
  so this inner product identifies
  $\alpha^\vee$ with $2\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$. If
  $\alpha=\sum_i n_i\alpha_i$ is positive, then
  $\alpha^\vee=\sum_i n_i(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)/(\alpha,\alpha)\,
  \alpha_i^\vee$, a nonnegative combination of simple coroots.
  Thus testing the simple coroots suffices. The order is generated by the positive simple
  roots, so $\lambda\ge\mu$ is the relation used for highest weights.
- **Existence and uniqueness.** In that inner product the simple-coroot
  functionals are independent on the root span, since the simple roots form
  a basis. Their pairing matrix is rational and invertible, so their dual
  vectors $\omega_i$ lie uniquely in $\mathbb Q\Phi$. Subtracting
  $\sum_i\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\omega_i$ from $\lambda$
  gives the displayed $\lambda_0$, which annihilates all coroots by the
  preceding coroot expansion. Specifying the root span is essential when
  the central torus is nontrivial.
- **Fundamental weights need not lie in $X$.** The $\omega_i$ are elements of
  the rational vector space $X\otimes\mathbb Q$; the correction term
  $\lambda_0$ in the displayed decomposition lies in the annihilator of the
  coroot lattice, and it need not belong to $X$, nor need
  $\sum_i\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\omega_i$ lie in $X$ for an
  arbitrary reductive root datum. This is why the classification below is proved
  through the product-with-a-torus and central-isogeny route rather than by
  adding a lattice correction term directly.
- **No Choice.** The definition only records eigenspace decompositions and
  lattice dualities of the cited suppliers; no choice principle is used.
