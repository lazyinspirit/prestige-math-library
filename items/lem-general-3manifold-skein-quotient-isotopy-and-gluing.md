## Statement

Let $M$ and $N$ be smooth oriented $3$-manifolds, and let
$f:M\to N$ be an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. Then
$[L]\mapsto[f(L)]$ induces an $R$-module isomorphism
$f_*:\mathcal K_A(M)\to\mathcal K_A(N)$. These maps respect identity
diffeomorphisms and composition.

Let $\Sigma$ be a compact oriented surface with a specified product collar
$\iota:\Sigma\times[0,1]\hookrightarrow M$ whose lower face lies in
$\partial M$. Give $\Sigma\times[0,1]$ the orientation pulled back from $M$
by $\iota$; the skein module and stacking product below use this orientation.
The stacking product on
$\mathcal K_A(\Sigma\times[0,1])$ and collar gluing define a unital left
action on $\mathcal K_A(M)$: a closed framed link in the surface product is
placed in a sufficiently thin outer subcollar and united with a framed link
in the interior of $M$. The action is independent of the thin-collar width,
associative, and natural under orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms that
respect the specified collars. Only closed framed links are glued; no relative
boundary endpoints are part of this action.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Smooth oriented $3$-manifolds $M,N$, an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism $f:M\to N$, an oriented surface collar in $\partial M$, and the Kauffman bracket skein quotients defined above.

[F1] The free module is generated by framed unoriented link isotopy classes; its relation submodule is generated by the local crossing relation and the zero-framed trivial-circle relation with $R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$ and $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$ ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]], [[def-kauffman-bracket-smoothing-and-loop-value]]).

[F2] The collar product is oriented by pullback under $\iota$, so its embedding carries each defining crossing and trivial-circle relation to the identical relation in $M$ ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]]).

## Proof


1.1 Define $\widetilde f:\mathcal F_R(M)\to\mathcal F_R(N)$ on basis elements by $\widetilde f([L])=[f(L)]$ and extend $R$-linearly. A diffeomorphism sends ambient-isotopic framed annuli to ambient-isotopic framed annuli and sends the framing annuli by its derivative, so this is well-defined on the free modules. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 Fix framed-link representatives $P$ in $\Sigma\times(0,1)$ and $L$ in the interior of $M$. To check independence of isotopy representatives, take any isotopy track of either framed link. Its framing annuli have compact trace in the interior. Place the trace of the product-factor link in a sufficiently thin outer collar layer disjoint from the trace of the $M$-factor link. The disjoint-union family is a smooth isotopy of framed annuli in $M$. In finitely many submanifold charts of its compact trace, extend the annulus velocity to ambient charts with cutoffs and combine these extensions with a partition of unity; the resulting compactly supported time-dependent vector field has a flow that realizes this isotopy by ambient diffeomorphisms. Thus the union class is independent of both representatives. For the quotient relations, rescale $P$ into a boundary layer disjoint from $L$. A change of $P$ by either defining relation becomes the identical local relation after the collar embedding by [F2]; a change of $L$ by either relation remains the identical relation with the disjoint collar link outside its ball. The same local check in two successive collar layers shows that stacking descends to the skein quotients. Hence collar gluing gives a bilinear action. Decreasing the collar width uses an orientation-preserving product rescaling isotopy supported in a thin boundary layer and disjoint from $L$, so the resulting skein class is unchanged. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 The image of an oriented crossing ball is an oriented ball. Its transported chart preserves the over/under crossing and both endpoint pairings, so $\widetilde f([L_\times]-A[L_A]-A^{-1}[L_B])$ is the corresponding defining relation in $N$. A zero-framed trivial circle bounding a disk in the ball maps to another zero-framed trivial circle bounding the image disk. Thus $\widetilde f(\mathcal R_A(M))\subseteq\mathcal R_A(N)$ and $\widetilde f$ descends to $f_*$. Applying the same construction to $f^{-1}$ gives the inverse; applying it to a composite gives the composite map on every link generator. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Place two algebra links in successive subcollars and the $M$-link deeper inside. Both $(P*Q)\cdot L$ and $P\cdot(Q\cdot L)$ are represented by the same ordered union, up to an orientation-preserving product rescaling, and the empty link acts as the identity. Thus the action is associative and unital. If an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism carries one specified collar productwise to another, it sends each stacked or glued link union to the corresponding union, so its module maps intertwine the actions. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
