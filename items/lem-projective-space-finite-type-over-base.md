## Statement

Let $S$ be a scheme and let $n\ge0$. The structure morphism
$$\pi:\mathbb P^n_S\longrightarrow S$$
is of finite type. No Noetherian, field or nonemptiness hypothesis is used,
and the case $S=\varnothing$ is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $S$, an integer $n\ge0$, and the projection $\pi:\mathbb P^n_S\to S$ of the relative projective space of [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]].

[F1] For every scheme $S$ the standard charts $U^S_i=S\times_{\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb Z}U_i$ for $i=0,\dots,n$ are open subschemes forming an open cover of $\mathbb P^n_S$ and are affine over $S$; over an affine base $S=\operatorname{Spec}A$ the $i$-th chart is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}A[x^{(i)}_\ell:\ell\ne i]$, and charts, overlaps and comparisons commute with base change. ([[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]])

[F2] A commutative $R$-algebra $A$ is of finite type over $R$ when $A=R[a_1,\dots,a_m]$ for some $m\ge0$ and elements $a_i\in A$; in particular a polynomial algebra $R[x_1,\dots,x_m]$ is of finite type over $R$, being generated by its $m$ indeterminates, and at $m=0$ the condition is that $A$ equals the image of the structure map $R\to A$. ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]])

[F3] Being locally of finite type is affine-local on source and target, and a quasi-compact morphism locally of finite type is of finite type; equivalently, over each affine target open it may be tested on a finite affine source cover. ([[lem-finite-type-local-on-source-and-target]])

[F4] A morphism $f:X\to S$ is quasi-compact if $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subseteq S$. ([[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]])

[F5] For $f:X\to S$, $f$ is quasi-compact if and only if the inverse image of every affine open of $S$ is quasi-compact. ([[lem-base-change-quasi-compact-morphisms]])

[F6] Every affine scheme is quasi-compact. ([[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]])

[F7] A topological space is compact when every open cover of it has a finite subcover. ([[def-compact-space]])

[F8] A scheme is a locally ringed space in which every point has an open neighbourhood which, with the restricted structure sheaf, is an affine scheme. ([[def-scheme]])



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: check local finite type on the standard affine charts over affine opens of the base, then quasi-compactness from the finite chart cover and the affine-open criterion.

1.1 Let $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ be an affine open, which exists around every point of $S$ by [F8]. By [F1] the charts $U^A_0,\dots,U^A_n$ are open subschemes forming an open cover of $\pi^{-1}(V)=\mathbb P^n_A$, and $U^A_i=\operatorname{Spec}A[x^{(i)}_\ell:\ell\ne i]$ is an affine scheme over $V$. [F1, F8]

2.1 For each $i$ the ring map $A\to A[x^{(i)}_\ell:\ell\ne i]$ is of finite type: when $n\ge1$ the target polynomial algebra is generated as an $A$-algebra by its finitely many indeterminates, and when $n=0$ there is one chart with coordinate ring $A$, the identity map, which is of finite type by the empty generating list in [F2]. [F2, step 1.1]

2.2 For every affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the inverse image $\pi^{-1}(V)=\mathbb P^n_A$ is the union of the finitely many affine charts $U^A_0,\dots,U^A_n$, and each $U^A_i$ is quasi-compact by [F6]. A finite union of quasi-compact subspaces is quasi-compact: an open cover of the union restricts to an open cover of each $U^A_i$, from which finitely many members can be chosen by [F7], and the finitely many resulting subfamilies cover the union. Hence $\pi^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact. [F1, F6, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 By the affine-local criterion of [F3], steps 1.1 and 2.1 show that $\pi$ is locally of finite type: over each affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the source is covered by the affine charts $U^A_i$ on which the ring map is of finite type. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 By [F5] the morphism $\pi$ is quasi-compact, and by [F3] a quasi-compact morphism that is locally of finite type is of finite type; with step 3.1 this proves that $\pi:\mathbb P^n_S\to S$ is of finite type in the sense of [F4]. The empty base gives $\mathbb P^n_\varnothing=\varnothing$ and the empty morphism, which is of finite type vacuously, and for $n=0$ the unique chart has $\mathbb P^0_S\cong S$ with $\pi$ an isomorphism, included in the computations above. [F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 2.2] ∎
