## Statement refuted

That the rational intersection theorem
[[thm-intersections-of-rational-cyclotomic-fields]] holds over every base field:
that for every field $K$ and all positive integers $m,n$ with the characteristic
of $K$ dividing neither,

$$K(\mu_m)\cap K(\mu_n)=K(\mu_{\gcd(m,n)}).$$

The witness below takes $K=\mathbb F_3$, $m=5$ and $n=7$, and realizes both
splitting fields inside one fixed field $\Omega$ of order $3^{12}$. Since
$\gcd(5,7)=1$, the right-hand side is $K(\mu_1)=\mathbb F_3$, while the
intersection on the left is the common subfield $\mathbb F_9\subseteq\Omega$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The base field $K=\mathbb F_3$ and a field $\Omega$ of order $3^{12}$, which exists by [[thm-existence-of-finite-fields]]. The two cyclotomic splitting fields will be identified with their base-field-isomorphic copies inside $\Omega$.

[L1] For $\gcd(n,q)=1$, the image of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_q(\mu_n)/\mathbb F_q)$ in $(\mathbb Z/n)^\times$ is generated by $[q]$, so the degree of $\mathbb F_q(\mu_n)/\mathbb F_q$ is the order of $[q]$ modulo $n$ ([[thm-the-cyclotomic-galois-group-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L2] The intermediate fields of $\mathbb F_{q^{N}}/\mathbb F_q$ are exactly the $\mathbb F_{q^{d}}$ for the positive divisors $d$ of $N$, one for each divisor, with $\mathbb F_{q^{d}}\subseteq\mathbb F_{q^{e}}$ exactly when $d\mid e$ ([[thm-intermediate-fields-of-an-extension-of-finite-fields]]).

[L3] Over $\mathbb Q$ one has $\mathbb Q(\mu_m)\cap\mathbb Q(\mu_n)=\mathbb Q(\mu_{\gcd(m,n)})$ ([[thm-intersections-of-rational-cyclotomic-fields]]).

[L4] $K(\mu_r)$ is the splitting field of $t^{r}-1$ over $K$ ([[def-cyclotomic-extension]]).

[L5] Finite fields of the same order are isomorphic by an isomorphism fixing their common prime field ([[thm-uniqueness-of-finite-fields]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $(\mathbb Z/5)^\times$, the class $[3]$ has order $4$, since $3^{4}=81\equiv1\pmod 5$ and no smaller positive power of $3$ is congruent to $1$ modulo $5$. So [L1] gives $[\mathbb F_3(\mu_5):\mathbb F_3]=4$, hence this splitting field has order $3^4$ and [L5] lets us identify it over $\mathbb F_3$ with the unique subfield $\mathbb F_{3^4}$ of $\Omega$. [L1, L2, L4, L5, algebra]

1.2 In $(\mathbb Z/7)^\times$, the powers of $[3]$ are $[3],[2],[6],[4],[5],[1]$, so $[3]$ has order $6$. Thus [L1] gives $[\mathbb F_3(\mu_7):\mathbb F_3]=6$, hence this splitting field has order $3^6$ and [L5] lets us identify it over $\mathbb F_3$ with the unique subfield $\mathbb F_{3^6}$ of $\Omega$. [L1, L2, L4, L5, algebra]

2.1 Under the fixed identifications of steps 1.1 and 1.2, both $\mathbb F_{3^{4}}$ and $\mathbb F_{3^{6}}$ are subfields of $\Omega=\mathbb F_{3^{12}}$ by [L2], since $4\mid12$ and $6\mid12$. Their intersection is then an intermediate field of $\Omega/\mathbb F_3$, so by [L2] it is $\mathbb F_{3^{d}}$ for some divisor $d$ of $12$. Because the intersection lies in both fields, [L2] gives $d\mid4$ and $d\mid6$, hence $d\mid2$; and since $\mathbb F_{3^{2}}$ lies in both fields, [L2] gives $2\mid d$. Therefore $d=2$ and $$ \mathbb F_3(\mu_5)\cap\mathbb F_3(\mu_7)=\mathbb F_{3^{2}}=\mathbb F_9. $$ [step 1.1, step 1.2, L2]

3.1 Since $\gcd(5,7)=1$, the right-hand side of the refuted identity is $K(\mu_1)$, which is just $K=\mathbb F_3$ because $t-1$ already splits over $K$. So the claimed equality would read $\mathbb F_9=\mathbb F_3$, which is false. [step 2.1, L4, algebra]

4.1 The refuted statement therefore fails over the base field $\mathbb F_3$, even though the rational theorem [L3] is true. [step 3.1, L3] ∎

## Remarks

- **Why the rational hypothesis matters.** Over finite fields the intersection is controlled by the gcd of the extension degrees, not by the gcd of the orders of the roots of unity.
