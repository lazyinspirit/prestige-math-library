## Example

Let $\zeta:=\zeta_7$. Then the fixed field of the unique order-two subgroup of
$\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q)$ is

$$\mathbb Q(\zeta+\zeta^{-1}),$$

a degree-three Galois extension of $\mathbb Q$ with cyclic Galois group, and the
element $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$ has minimal polynomial

$$t^{3}+t^{2}-2t-1.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A primitive seventh root of unity $\zeta=\zeta_7$.

[L1] $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/7)^\times$ and has order $\varphi(7)=6$ ([[cor-the-galois-group-of-a-rational-cyclotomic-field]]).

[L2] A finite cyclic group has exactly one subgroup of each order dividing its own ([[lem-subgroup-lattice-of-a-finite-cyclic-group]]).

[L3] For a finite Galois extension, subgroups correspond to intermediate fields, and the fixed field of a subgroup $H$ has degree equal to the subgroup index ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]).

[L4] Every finite subgroup of the unit group of an integral domain is cyclic ([[cor-finite-subgroups-of-units-in-a-domain-are-cyclic]]).

[L5] Under the finite Galois correspondence, a normal subgroup $H$ has a Galois fixed field and restriction gives $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)\cong G/H$ ([[thm-normal-subgroups-and-quotients-in-the-galois-correspondence]]).

[L6] Every finite group of prime order is cyclic ([[cor-prime-order-group-is-cyclic]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the Galois group of $\mathbb Q(\zeta)/\mathbb Q$ is isomorphic to the finite subgroup $(\mathbb Z/7)^\times$ of the unit group of the field $\mathbb Z/7$, so [L4] makes it cyclic; its order is $6$. Thus [L2] gives a unique subgroup $H$ of order $2$, and [L3] makes its fixed field $F$ have degree $[F:\mathbb Q]=6/2=3$. [L1, L2, L3, L4]

2.1 The subgroup $H$ is generated by the class $[-1]$, so it acts by complex conjugation. Therefore $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$ is fixed by $H$ and lies in $F$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $x:=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$. Then $$ \zeta^{2}+\zeta^{-2}=x^{2}-2,\qquad \zeta^{3}+\zeta^{-3}=x^{3}-3x. $$ Since $1+\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{3}+\zeta^{4}+\zeta^{5}+\zeta^{6}=0$, dividing by $\zeta^{3}$ gives $$ 1+(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})+(\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{-2})+(\zeta^{3}+\zeta^{-3})=0. $$ Substituting the expressions above yields $$ x^{3}+x^{2}-2x-1=0. $$ [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The element $x$ is not rational: if it were, then $\zeta$ would satisfy the quadratic polynomial $t^{2}-xt+1\in\mathbb Q[t]$, which would force $[\mathbb Q(\zeta):\mathbb Q]\le2$, contradicting [L1]. Since $x\in F$, the prime degree $[F:\mathbb Q]=3$ from step 1.1 leaves only the subfields $\mathbb Q$ and $F$, so $\mathbb Q(x)=F$. Therefore the minimal polynomial of $x$ has degree $3$, and the cubic from step 3.1 is that minimal polynomial. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L1]

5.1 The ambient Galois group is cyclic and hence abelian, so $H$ is normal. By [L5], the fixed field $F/\mathbb Q$ is Galois and $\operatorname{Gal}(F/\mathbb Q)$ is isomorphic to the quotient by $H$, which has order $3$. The group is cyclic by [L6], so $F/\mathbb Q$ is a cyclic cubic Galois extension. [step 1.1, L5, L6, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **This is the smallest nontrivial case of the subfield theorem.** The subgroup lattice of $(\mathbb Z/7)^\times$ has one index-two subgroup, and the fixed field is already visible through the real element $\zeta+\zeta^{-1}$.
