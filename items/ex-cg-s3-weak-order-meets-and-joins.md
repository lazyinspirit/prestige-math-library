## Example

Let $(S,m)$ be the Coxeter matrix of type $A_2$: $S=\{s,t\}$, $m(s,t)=3$, let
$W$ be the presented group with length $\ell$, identified with the symmetric
group $S_3$ by [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]
(4), and let $\le_R,\le_L$ be the weak orders of
[[def-cg-left-right-weak-order-and-descents]]. Then
$W=\{1,s,t,st,ts,w_0\}$ with $w_0=sts=tst$ and $\ell(1)=0$,
$\ell(s)=\ell(t)=1$, $\ell(st)=\ell(ts)=2$, $\ell(w_0)=3$, and:

**(1) Covers and the lattice table.** The right weak order has exactly the
cover relations $1\lessdot_R s$, $1\lessdot_R t$, $s\lessdot_R st$,
$t\lessdot_R ts$, $st\lessdot_R w_0$, $ts\lessdot_R w_0$, so its Hasse
diagram is the hexagon formed by the two chains $1<s<st<w_0$ and
$1<t<ts<w_0$. Meets and joins are the following complete table: on elements
of one chain, meet and join are the smaller and the larger; across the two
chains one has

$$u\wedge v=1\ \text{ for }\ (u,v)\in\{(s,t),(s,ts),(st,t),(st,ts)\},\qquad w_0\wedge v=v\ \text{ for }\ v\in\{s,t,st,ts\},$$

and $u\vee v=w_0$ for the four pairs
$(u,v)\in\{(s,t),(s,ts),(st,t),(st,ts)\}$ and their reversals, as well as
whenever one of $u,v$ equals $w_0$. In particular
$\bigwedge\varnothing=w_0$ and $\bigvee\varnothing=1$, and every meet and
join in the table is the unique element with the corresponding universal
bound property.

**(2) Left order and inversion.** The left weak order is the image of the
right order under $w\mapsto w^{-1}$: for example $s\le_R st$ but
$s\not\le_L st$, while $t\le_L st$ but $t\not\le_R st$. With the simple
roots $\alpha_s,\alpha_t$ and the positive root $\alpha_s+\alpha_t$ of $A_2$,
the six inversion sets $N(w^{-1})$ are

$$\varnothing,\ \{\alpha_s\},\ \{\alpha_t\},\ \{\alpha_s,\alpha_s+\alpha_t\},\ \{\alpha_t,\alpha_s+\alpha_t\},\ \Phi_+$$

for $w=1,s,t,st,ts,w_0$ respectively, and the criterion
$u\le_R v\iff N(u^{-1})\subseteq N(v^{-1})$ of
[[lem-cg-weak-order-is-a-graded-partial-order]] (4) is verified on all $36$
pairs.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Coxeter matrix of type $A_2$: $S=\{s,t\}$, $m(s,t)=3$; the presented group $W$ with length $\ell$, weak orders $\le_R,\le_L$, canonical reflection representation $\rho$ on $V=\mathbb R^S$ with basis $e_s,e_t$, Coxeter form $B$, and signed root system $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-.$

[F1] [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]: the Coxeter presentation has relators $u^2=1$ for $u\in S$ and $(uv)^{m(u,v)}=1$ for distinct generators when $m(u,v)<\infty$; in type $A_2$, $s^2=t^2=1$ and $(st)^3=1$.

[F2] [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]: $W$ is generated by $S$ and $\ell(w)$ is the minimum length of a word in $S$ representing $w$, with $\ell(1)=0$; reduced expressions realize this minimum.

[F3] [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (4): for the type-$A_{n-1}$ matrix, $s_i\mapsto(i\ i+1)$ extends to an isomorphism $W\to S_n$.

[F4] [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (4): under that isomorphism, $\ell(w)=\operatorname{inv}(\varphi(w))$.

[F5] [[def-cg-left-right-weak-order-and-descents]] (1): $u\le_R v$ iff $v=ux$ with $\ell(v)=\ell(u)+\ell(x)$.

[F6] [[def-cg-left-right-weak-order-and-descents]] (1): $u\le_L v$ iff $v=xu$ with $\ell(v)=\ell(u)+\ell(x)$.

[F7] [[lem-cg-weak-order-is-a-graded-partial-order]] (2): $u\lessdot_R v$ iff $v=us$ for some $s\in S$ with $\ell(v)=\ell(u)+1$, and every comparison is a chain of such covers.

[F8] [[lem-cg-weak-order-is-a-graded-partial-order]] (4): $u\le_R v$ iff $N(u^{-1})\subseteq N(v^{-1})$.

[F9] [[lem-cg-weak-order-is-a-graded-partial-order]] (1): both weak orders are partial orders with minimum $1$, and inversion is an order isomorphism $(W,\le_R)\to(W,\le_L)$.

[F10] [[thm-cg-weak-order-meet-semilattice-and-finite-lattice]] (2): if $W$ is finite, both weak orders have minimum $1$ and maximum $w_0$, with $\bigwedge\varnothing=w_0$ and $\bigvee\varnothing=1$.

[F11] [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (2): $B$ is symmetric, $B(e_s,e_s)=B(e_t,e_t)=1$, and $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/3)$.

[F12] [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (3): if $B(a,a)\ne0$, then $r_a(v)=v-\frac{2B(v,a)}{B(a,a)}a$.

[F13] [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]] (1): $\rho$ is a homomorphism with $\rho(s)=r_{e_s}$ for every $s\in S$.

[F14] [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]] (2): $\Phi=\{\rho(w)e_u:w\in W,\ u\in S\}$ is the orbit of the simple roots.

[F15] [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]] (3), [[thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]] (2): $V_+=\{\sum_{u\in S}\lambda_u e_u:\lambda_u\ge0\}$, $\Phi_+=\Phi\cap V_+$, $\Phi_-=-\Phi_+$, and $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-.$

[F16] [[def-cg-geometric-inversion-set]] (1): $N(w)=\{\alpha\in\Phi_+:\rho(w)\alpha\in\Phi_-\}$.

[F17] [[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]] (2): for every reduced expression $w=s_1\cdots s_n$, $N(w^{-1})=\{\rho(s_1\cdots s_{i-1})e_{s_i}:1\le i\le n\}$, with the displayed elements pairwise distinct positive roots.

[F18] [[thm-double-angle-and-power-reduction-identities]]: $\cos(2x)=2\cos^2x-1$ for every real $x$.

[F19] [[thm-cofunction-supplementary-and-reflection-identities]]: $\cos(\pi-x)=-\cos x$ for every real $x$.

[F20] [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]: cosine is strictly decreasing on $[2m\pi,(2m+1)\pi]$ for every integer $m$.

[F21] [[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]]: $\cos(\pi/2)=0$.

[F22] [[def-cg-left-right-weak-order-and-descents]] (3): a meet is a greatest lower bound and a join is a least upper bound, with their universal lower- and upper-bound properties.

[A1] In a poset, common lower bounds form the intersection of the down-sets, and common upper bounds form the intersection of the up-sets. Their greatest and least elements, respectively, are the meet and join.

## Proof

1.1 The six elements: the type-$A$ isomorphism and inversion-length formula [F3,F4] give $|W|=6$, $\ell(s)=\ell(t)=1$, $\ell(st)=\ell(ts)=2$ and $\ell(sts)=3$. The values $1,s,t,st,ts,sts$ are distinct: $1$ has length $0$ by [F2], the isomorphism separates $s$ and $t$, elements of different lengths are distinct, and $st=ts$ would imply $sts=t$ after right multiplication by $s$, contradicting their lengths. From the presentation [F1], $(st)^3=1$ and $s^2=t^2=1$; hence $(sts)(tst)=1$, while $tst$ is an involution. Therefore $sts=tst=:w_0$, and the six displayed elements exhaust $W$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 The $A_2$ root set and reflection actions: put $c:=\cos(\pi/3)$. By [F18,F19], $2c^2-1=\cos(2\pi/3)=-c$, so $(2c-1)(c+1)=0$. Since $\pi/3<\pi/2$, [F20] and [F21] give $c=\cos(\pi/3)>\cos(\pi/2)=0$, hence $c=1/2$. From the Coxeter form and reflection formula [F11,F12], $\rho(s)e_s=-e_s$, $\rho(s)e_t=e_t+e_s$, $\rho(t)e_t=-e_t$, and $\rho(t)e_s=e_s+e_t$; thus $\rho(s)(e_s+e_t)=e_t$ and $\rho(t)(e_s+e_t)=e_s$. These actions and linearity show that $\mathcal R:=\{\pm e_s,\pm e_t,\pm(e_s+e_t)\}$ is invariant under both generators. Since $W$ is generated by $S$ [F2] and $\rho$ is a homomorphism [F13], every $\rho(w)$ preserves $\mathcal R$. The root-orbit definition [F14] gives $\Phi\subseteq\mathcal R$; conversely $e_s,e_t$ are roots, $-e_s=\rho(s)e_s$, $-e_t=\rho(t)e_t$, $e_s+e_t=\rho(s)e_t$, and $-(e_s+e_t)=\rho(s)\rho(t)e_t$, so $\Phi=\mathcal R$. The positive cone and sign theorem [F15] then give $\Phi_+=\{\alpha_s,\alpha_t,\alpha_s+\alpha_t\}$, where $\alpha_s=e_s$ and $\alpha_t=e_t$. [F2, F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, F18, F19, F20, F21, given, algebra]

2.1 Covers: the cover characterization [F7] says a right cover is right multiplication by a simple generator with length rise one. Multiplying the six values of step 1.1 by $s,t$ gives the rises $1s=s$, $1t=t$, $st=s\cdot t$, $ts=t\cdot s$, $w_0=st\cdot s$, and $w_0=ts\cdot t$; each raises length by one by [F4]. The other six products are $ss=1$, $tt=1$, $(st)t=s$, $(ts)s=t$, $w_0s=st$, and $w_0t=ts$, none of which raises length. Hence the right covers are exactly the six displayed in the statement. [F1, F4, F7, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 The asymmetry: $st=s\cdot t$ and $\ell(st)=\ell(s)+\ell(t)$, so $s\le_R st$ by [F5]. If $s\le_L st$, [F6] gives $st=xs$ and $\ell(st)=\ell(x)+\ell(s)$; right multiplication by $s$ gives $x=sts=w_0$, contradicting $2=\ell(st)=\ell(x)+1=4$. Likewise $st=s\cdot t$ with $\ell(st)=\ell(s)+\ell(t)$ gives $t\le_L st$, while $t\le_R st$ would force $x=t^{-1}st=tst=w_0$ and $2=\ell(st)=1+3$, impossible. Inversion is an order isomorphism by [F9], consistent with these comparisons. [F1, F4, F5, F6, F9, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.3 The six inversion sets: by the definition [F16] and prefix-root formula [F17], the reduced expressions from step 1.1 and the roots from step 1.2 give $N(1)=\varnothing$, $N(s^{-1})=N(s)=\{\alpha_s\}$, $N(t^{-1})=N(t)=\{\alpha_t\}$, $N((st)^{-1})=N(ts)=\{\alpha_s,\rho(s)\alpha_t\}=\{\alpha_s,\alpha_s+\alpha_t\}$, and $N((ts)^{-1})=N(st)=\{\alpha_t,\rho(t)\alpha_s\}=\{\alpha_t,\alpha_s+\alpha_t\}$. For $w_0=sts=tst$, the same formula gives $N(w_0^{-1})=\{\alpha_s,\rho(s)\alpha_t,\rho(st)\alpha_s\}=\{\alpha_s,\alpha_s+\alpha_t,\alpha_t\}=\Phi_+$, since $\rho(st)\alpha_s=\rho(s)(\alpha_s+\alpha_t)=\alpha_t$. [F1, F13, F16, F17, step 1.1, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 The right order: the cover chains of step 2.1 and the chain characterization in [F7] give exactly the $17$ relations: $1\le_R v$ for all six $v$; $s\le_R s,st,w_0$; $t\le_R t,ts,w_0$; $st\le_R st,w_0$; $ts\le_R ts,w_0$; and $w_0\le_R w_0$. The remaining $19$ of the $36$ ordered pairs do not satisfy $u\le_R v$; eight have incomparable entries, and eleven are reversals of strict comparisons. The down-sets are $\downarrow1=\{1\}$, $\downarrow s=\{1,s\}$, $\downarrow t=\{1,t\}$, $\downarrow st=\{1,s,st\}$, $\downarrow ts=\{1,t,ts\}$ and $\downarrow w_0=W$; the up-sets are $\uparrow1=W$, $\uparrow s=\{s,st,w_0\}$, $\uparrow t=\{t,ts,w_0\}$, $\uparrow st=\{st,w_0\}$, $\uparrow ts=\{ts,w_0\}$ and $\uparrow w_0=\{w_0\}$. The minimum $1$ and poset properties used here are in [F9]. [F7, F9, step 2.1, given, algebra]

3.2 The order isomorphism: inversion is an order isomorphism by [F9], so left weak order is the image of the right order under $w\mapsto w^{-1}$. In particular, $s\le_R st$ but $s\not\le_L st$, while $t\le_L st$ but $t\not\le_R st$, as shown in step 2.2. [F9, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 Meets: intersections of down-sets from step 3.1 are $\{1\}$ for the four cross pairs $(s,t),(s,ts),(st,t),(st,ts)$; they are the down-set of the smaller element for pairs in one chain, and the down-set of the other element when one is $w_0$. By [A1] their greatest elements are the greatest common lower bounds, so the cross-pair meets are $1$, the meet along a chain is its smaller element, and $w_0\wedge v=v$ for $v\in\{s,t,st,ts\}$. Each listed value lies below both elements and dominates every common lower bound, the universal property in [F22]. For the empty meet every element is a lower bound, and the maximum $w_0$ gives $\bigwedge\varnothing=w_0$, in agreement with [F10]. [A1, F10, F22, step 3.1, given, algebra]

4.2 Joins: intersections of up-sets from step 3.1 are $\{w_0\}$ for the four cross pairs $(s,t),(s,ts),(st,t),(st,ts)$ and for every pair containing $w_0$; on a chain, the intersection is the up-set of the larger element. By [A1] their least elements are the least common upper bounds, so every cross-pair join and every join involving $w_0$ is $w_0$, and joins along a chain are its larger element. Each listed value is an upper bound and lies below every common upper bound, the universal property in [F22]. For the empty join every element is an upper bound, and the minimum $1$ gives $\bigvee\varnothing=1$, in agreement with [F10]. [A1, F10, F22, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 The criterion on all $36$ pairs: write $\gamma=\alpha_s+\alpha_t$. The six sets of step 2.3 are $\varnothing$, $\{\alpha_s\}$, $\{\alpha_t\}$, $\{\alpha_s,\gamma\}$, $\{\alpha_t,\gamma\}$, and $\{\alpha_s,\alpha_t,\gamma\}$. Their inclusion relations consist of the six reflexive pairs; the five strict inclusions from $\varnothing$ to every nonempty set; the four inclusions from each singleton to its containing intermediate set and to the full set; and the two inclusions from the intermediate sets to the full set, for $17$ relations total. The remaining $19$ ordered pairs do not satisfy the directed inclusion $N(u^{-1})\subseteq N(v^{-1})$. Comparing these $17$ inclusions and $19$ failures with the corresponding right-order relations and failures from step 3.1 proves, in both directions, $u\le_R v\iff N(u^{-1})\subseteq N(v^{-1})$ on all $36$ pairs by [F8]. No Choice is used. [F8, step 2.3, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
