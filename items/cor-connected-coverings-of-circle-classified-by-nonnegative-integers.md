## Statement

For each $n\in\mathbb N$, let $n\mathbb Z$ be the subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$ generated by $n$. Connected coverings of $\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, up to based isomorphism or up to unbased isomorphism, are in bijection with the nonnegative integers through the subgroup

$$n\mathbb Z\le\pi_1(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,[0])\cong\mathbb Z.$$

For $n\ge1$ the corresponding covering has $n$ sheets. The case $n=0$ is the infinite-sheeted universal covering $\mathbb R\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, and $n=1$ is the one-sheeted covering.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The quotient circle based at $[0]$.

[L1] Based connected coverings correspond to subgroups of the base fundamental group, while unbased connected coverings correspond to conjugacy classes of subgroups ([[thm-classification-of-connected-covering-spaces]]).

[L2] The quotient projection $\mathbb R\to\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ is a universal covering ([[cor-real-line-is-universal-cover-of-circle]]).

[F1] Degree gives an isomorphism $\pi_1(\mathbb R/\mathbb Z,[0])\cong(\mathbb Z,+)$ ([[thm-fundamental-group-of-the-circle]]).

[F2] Every subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$ is $n\mathbb Z$ for exactly one natural number $n$ ([[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]]).

[F3] The number of sheets of a connected covering is the index of its induced subgroup, with both finite or both infinite ([[thm-sheets-equal-fundamental-group-index]]).

[F4] The additive group of $\mathbb Z$ is abelian ([[thm-int-comm-ring]]).

[F5] The quotient group $(\mathbb Z,+)/n\mathbb Z$ has the same coset set as $\mathbb Z/n$ ([[prop-integers-modulo-n-as-a-quotient-group]]).

[F6] For $n\ge1$, the set $\mathbb Z/n$ has exactly $n$ elements; for $n=0$, it is in bijection with $\mathbb Z$ ([[thm-standard-representatives-modulo-n]]).

[F7] The quotient circle is nonempty and path-connected ([[prop-real-line-mod-integers-is-compact-and-path-connected]]).

[F8] The quotient map is open, and every real interval of length below one maps homeomorphically to its image in the quotient circle ([[lem-open-quotient-arcs-in-real-line-mod-integers]]).

[F9] Every nonempty convex interval is simply connected ([[thm-convex-subsets-have-trivial-fundamental-group]]).

[F10] Local path-connectedness requires arbitrarily small open path-connected neighbourhoods, while semilocal simple connectedness requires a neighbourhood whose inclusion induces the trivial fundamental-group map ([[def-locally-connected]], [[def-semilocally-simply-connected-space]]).

[F11] A pointed homeomorphism induces a fundamental-group isomorphism ([[thm-induced-fundamental-group-map-functoriality]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $[a]$ be a circle point and let $N$ be an open neighbourhood of it. The inverse image of $N$ is open and contains $a$, so it contains an interval $J$ about $a$ of length below one. By [F8], $p[J]$ is an open neighbourhood of $[a]$ inside $N$ and is homeomorphic to the convex interval $J$. Thus [F9], [F10], and [F11] show that the circle is locally path-connected and semilocally simply connected; [F7] supplies nonemptiness and path-connectedness. The classification theorem [L1] therefore applies. Transporting its subgroups through [F1], [F2] says that every induced subgroup is uniquely $n\mathbb Z$ for one $n\in\mathbb N$. [L1, F1, F2, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11]

2.1 By [L1], this gives one based-isomorphism class for each $n$. Since [F4] makes every conjugate of $n\mathbb Z$ equal to itself, the same parameter gives the unbased-isomorphism classes. Conversely, [L1] realizes every $n\mathbb Z$, so both correspondences are bijections. [step 1.1, L1, F4]

3.1 For $n\ge1$, [F5] and [F6] give $[\mathbb Z:n\mathbb Z]=n$, so [F3] gives $n$ sheets. For $n=0$, the subgroup is trivial, [F5] and [F6] give infinite index, and [L2] realizes this class by the real-line universal cover. At $n=1$ the index is one. [step 2.1, L2, F3, F5, F6] ∎
