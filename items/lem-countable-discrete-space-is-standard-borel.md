## Statement

If $S$ is a nonempty at-most-countable set, then $(S,\mathcal P(S))$ is a
standard Borel space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonempty at-most-countable set $S$ and its power-set
$\mathcal P(S)$.

[F1] [[def-countable]]: an at-most-countable set is finite or equinumerous
with $\mathbb N$.

[F2] [[def-sigma-algebra]]: a sigma-algebra contains the whole space and is
closed under complements and countable unions.

[F3] [[def-metric-space]]: a metric is a real-valued function that separates
distinct points, is symmetric, and satisfies the triangle inequality. The
definition does not assume nonnegativity; for the metric constructed below,
nonnegativity follows directly from its two cases.

[F4] [[def-metric-ball]]: an open ball of radius $r>0$ about $s$ is
$B(s,r)=\{t\in S:d(s,t)<r\}$.

[F5] [[def-metric-topology]]: a set is open when every one of its points has
a ball around it contained in the set.

[F6] [[def-cauchy-in-metric]]: a sequence is Cauchy if for every rational
$\epsilon>0$ its terms are pairwise within $\epsilon$ after some index.

[F7] [[def-complete-metric-space]]: a metric space is complete if every
Cauchy sequence converges to a point of the space.

[F8] [[def-separable-space]]: a space is separable if it has a countable dense
subset.

[F9] [[def-polish-space]]: a topological space is Polish if it is separable
and its topology is induced by a complete metric.

[F10] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by
the open sets of a topological space.

[F11] [[def-standard-borel-space]]: a measurable space is standard Borel if
it is measurably isomorphic to a Polish space with its Borel sigma-algebra.

## Proof

1.1 Define $d(s,t)=0$ when $s=t$ and $d(s,t)=1$ when $s\ne t$. These values are nonnegative, the definition is symmetric, and $d(s,t)=0$ exactly when $s=t$. If $s\ne u$, then at least one of $s\ne t$ or $t\ne u$ holds, so $d(s,u)=1\le d(s,t)+d(t,u)$; if $s=u$, the triangle inequality follows from nonnegativity. Thus [F3] makes $d$ a metric. For every $s\in S$, [F4] gives $B(s,1/2)=\{s\}$, so [F5] shows that every subset of $S$ is open. [F3, F4, F5, given]

2.1 If $(s_n)$ is Cauchy, [F6] with the rational $\epsilon=1/2$ gives an index $N$ such that $d(s_m,s_n)<1/2$ for all $m,n\ge N$. Hence all $s_n$ for $n\ge N$ are equal, so the sequence converges in $S$; by [F7], $d$ is complete. Since $S$ is at-most-countable and dense in itself, [F8] makes $(S,d)$ separable. [F6, F7, F8, F1, step 1.1, given]

3.1 The power set is closed under relative complements and countable unions, so it is a sigma-algebra by [F2]. By [F10] and step 1.1, every subset of $S$ belongs to the Borel sigma-algebra. Conversely, every Borel set is a subset of $S$, so this sigma-algebra is exactly $\mathcal P(S)$. Therefore $(S,d)$ is Polish by [F9], and the identity map from $(S,\mathcal P(S))$ to $(S,\mathcal B(S,d))$ is a measurable bijection with measurable inverse. [F2, F10, step 1.1, F9, step 2.1, given]

4.1 The identity map in step 3.1 is a measurable isomorphism to a Polish space, so [F11] gives that $(S,\mathcal P(S))$ is standard Borel. [step 3.1, F11] ∎
