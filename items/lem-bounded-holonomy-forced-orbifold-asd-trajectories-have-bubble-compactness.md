## Statement

Assume full AC. Use the fixed earring orbifold data, metric and an allowed holonomy perturbation in a bounded set of the constructed perturbation space. On every compact subcylinder, a sequence of smooth perturbed ASD trajectories with a common upper bound on flow energy has, after gauges and a subsequence, a limit away from finitely many points. On that punctured subcylinder the convergence is strong $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for every finite $p$ in local gauges. The limiting connection extends across those points as a smooth perturbed ASD connection on an actual smooth orbifold bundle. The lost energy at each point is a positive multiple of $8\pi^2$ at an ordinary point and a positive multiple of $4\pi^2$ on the singular surface, in the fundamental trace convention.

All concentrated bundles are accounted for by finitely many nonflat ordinary or order-two invariant ASD sphere bubbles with flat matching collars and no additional clutching contribution on the intervening necks. Thus the cylindrical index difference is the sum of their actual relative indices, at least eight per ordinary bubble and at least four per invariant bubble. In particular every concentration point has index loss at least four. If there are no concentration points, convergence on smaller closed subcylinders is smooth in orbifold gauges. The statement is local in the cylinder direction and does not assert convergence at its infinite ends or any nonlinear gluing collar.

## Proof

**Given:** A bounded holonomy perturbation set and a uniform flow-energy bound on a slightly larger compact cylinder than the one under consideration. Write $M$ for the pointwise bound on its forcing term.

1.1 **Curvature energy and the actual inhomogeneous local estimate.** If perturbations vary in the bounded set, first extract their coefficients diagonally. Fatou gives a coefficientwise limit in the same weighted $\ell^1$ ball. The choice $C_i\ge2^i$ times each of the first $i$ defining constants makes the tail of every fixed field seminorm at most $C2^{-N}$ on bounded connection sets, uniformly over this ball. Thus the actual fields converge in every such seminorm to the field of the coefficientwise limit, even though convergence in the perturbation norm was not assumed. In temporal gauge the equation is $\partial_tB+*F_B+V(B)=0$. Hence $|F_A|^2=|\partial_tB|^2+|F_B|^2\le3|\partial_tB|^2+2M^2$. This bounds curvature energy on a finite cylinder, including each lifted orbifold chart with factor at most two. On a normal four-ball of radius $r$, rescale and use the actual critical gauge [F1] whenever its curvature energy is below the fixed threshold. Its equation is $Da=E_j\partial_ja+B_g(a)a+f$, with $\|f\|_\infty\le Cr^2M$. For a cutoff, the forcing in the simultaneous $Y_2,Y_3$ construction is the sum of the old cutoff derivative term and this bounded compact source; it is in both $L^2$ and $L^3$. The same contraction, with its principal coefficient and small $\|a\|_4$ absorbed, gives $\|a\|_{12}\le C(\|F_A\|_2+r^2M)$. The first-order estimate then gives $\|a\|_{W^{1,6}}\le C(\|F_A\|_2+r^2M+(r^2M)^2)$ on a smaller ball. Shrink the fixed coordinate radius so $r^2M\le1$. In particular $r\|a\|_\infty\le C(\|F_A\|_2+r^2M)$ in physical coordinates. Only bounded forcing, not bounds on its spatial derivatives near other concentration points, was used. [F1, F2, F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Finite point extraction and the correct preliminary convergence.** Apply [F3]'s rational-ball diagonal extraction to the curvature bound of step 1.1, on finitely many ordinary and lifted invariant charts. It produces a finite bad set and small-energy balls covering every compact subset of its complement. The local potentials are bounded in $W^{1,6}$ by step 1.1, hence have uniformly convergent subsequences and weak $W^{1,6}$ limits. Their transition equation $dh=h a_j-a_i h$ and compact group values give convergent $C^1$ subsequences on smaller overlaps; cocycles and isotropy pass to the limits. This yields a $W^{1,6}$ connection on the limiting punctured bundle. The holonomy forcing passes strongly in each finite $L^p$ space. Indeed at a time different from the finitely many times containing a concentration point, every fixed cylinder loop misses all the bad points; its holonomy and transported gradient converge from the local uniform connection and transition convergence. The excluded times have measure zero. The uniform bound $M$, dominated convergence for each cylinder function, and the uniform weighted-sum tail bound from [F2] prove the assertion for the full perturbation. The local weak equation now passes, and the inhomogeneous first-order estimate applied to differences makes the potential convergence strong in $W^{1,p}$ on smaller good balls, first at $p=6$ and then every finite $p$, because the potentials converge uniformly and the forcing converges strongly in that $L^p$. For each fixed $p$, shrink the good coordinate balls further so the smooth variable-principal coefficient has norm small enough for that $L^p$ estimate; a finite cover of a chosen compact good subset suffices. No one absorption radius uniform in all $p$ is assumed. We do not assert derivative convergence of the nonlocal forcing away from a point before global removal. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Annular frames from small potentials, with the finite symmetry retained.** The bounded-forcing estimate replaces the curvature-supremum input in the puncture construction. On a fixed rescaled annulus cover it by finitely many overlapping small balls with connected good overlaps. Their local gauges from step 1.1 have potentials at most $C\delta$, where $\delta=\sqrt{E_j}+r_j^2M$ and $E_j$ is the energy on a wider dyadic annulus. On an overlap $dh_{ij}=h_{ij}a_j-a_i h_{ij}$ gives oscillation at most $C\delta$. Approximate it by its value at a reference point. Normalize those constants along a spanning tree of the finite nerve. Every remaining edge cycle contracts in the annulus, since it retracts to $S^3$; its chosen finite triangular filling in the nerve exists by the actual good-cover equivalence [F5]. Each face's transition product differs from one by $O(\delta)$, because the true cocycles have product one. Thus after the tree normalization every transition is $O(\delta)$ from one, with a fixed constant for this finite cover. A partition average of the local quaternionic frames and scalar normalization gives one annular frame, with potential at most $C\delta/r_j$. On a lifted singular chart this can be made equivariant. For the one nontrivial deck element the relative action in that frame has derivative bounded by $C\delta/r_j$, hence is $O(\delta)$ from its value at a fixed point of the annulus; that value is a conjugate of $i$. Rotate the frame by a constant to make it $i$, then average the frame with its transform $q(x)\mapsto i q(\gamma x)(-i)$ and quaternionically normalize. They differ by $O(\delta)$, so normalization is nonsingular and retains the same potential bound. Neighboring annular equivariant frames may be aligned at a common fixed point by constants commuting with $i$. Thus the dyadic alignment and two-overlap partition construction in [F3] supplies an equivariant punctured-ball gauge with $\int|a|^4\le C\sum_j(E_j^2+r_j^8M^4)<\infty$, since the wider annuli have finite overlap. No curvature derivative or false endpoint radial estimate has been substituted for this gauge construction. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Removal and the finite group action across a point.** In this punctured $L^4$ frame use the exact cutoffs of [F3]: $F_{\chi a}=\chi F_a+d\chi\wedge a+(\chi^2-\chi)a^2$. The last two terms tend to zero in $L^2$ by absolute continuity and Holder, with bounds $C\|a\|_{4,\mathrm{ann}}$ and its square. The bounded right side also converges in every finite $L^p$ after zero extension. Full-ball critical Coulomb gauges therefore have bounded $W^{1,2}$ and small $L^4$ potentials. As in [F3], weak extraction and the gauge equations give a limiting unitary comparison on every punctured compact set; bounded matrices converge almost everywhere and in finite $L^p$, so the transformed bounded source passes strongly in $L^2$. The limiting whole-ball equation is the actual inhomogeneous weak equation, not the unperturbed one. Step 1.1's simultaneous weak bootstrap puts its potential in $W^{1,6}$. The comparison equation makes the punctured gauge $W^{2,6}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. These charts glue to a $C^1$ bundle across the point. A finite partition embeds that bundle by its local frames into a trivial finite-rank bundle, giving a $C^1$ orthogonal projector. Smoothly approximate the projector matrix and project its spectrum across the gap at $1/2$; the convergent local resolvent power series gives a smooth nearby projector of the same rank. The restriction between the two images and its polar normalization is an actual bundle isomorphism. Quaternionic or orthogonal structure and the finite action are retained by averaging before this projection. Thus use a smooth bundle and the transferred $W^{1,6}$ connection. Once all points have been filled, the actual holonomy field has the full product-cylinder Sobolev mapping bounds of [F2]; its first weak derivatives are integrals of curvature under the transport formula, so $V\in W^{1,6}$. The equation improves the connection to $W^{2,6}$ in its finite gauges, and induction gives every order, hence smoothness. The original deck transformations also extend: in the removed smooth connection frame they satisfy $dg_\gamma=g_\gamma A-\gamma^*A g_\gamma$, so are Lipschitz across the point and then smooth by this first-order equation. Their group identities persist. On a fixed surface their isotropy trace is constant by continuity, hence is the same nontrivial rotation as on the punctured fixed surface. This is an actual smooth orbifold extension. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Comparison, atoms and their positive energy quantum.** The finite transition-average construction in [F3] now identifies the original bundles with the limiting bundle off the bad points; its proof needs only uniform closeness of the transition matrices for topological identification. Individual gauges may then be smoothed, without changing their clutching class. Strong local $W^{1,2}$ convergence passes the curvature polynomial in $L^1$. The same disjoint-ball and continuous-test-function argument as [F3] gives the limiting energy measure and positive atoms. On a shrinking four-ball the difference between curvature energy and twice the temporal derivative energy is bounded by $C M\operatorname{vol}(B_r)^{1/2}\|\partial_tB\|_2+C M^2\operatorname{vol}(B_r)$, which tends to zero. Thus these are exactly the flow-energy atoms as well. Glue an original ball and the oppositely oriented limiting ball using the comparison, flattening only their common tangential collar connection as in [F3]. On an ordinary chart its integer Chern number gives atom $8\pi^2m$; the error $2\|F_A^+\|_2^2\le C M^2r^4$ vanishes. On a singular chart lift the same construction and integral to the double cover and divide by two, giving $4\pi^2m$. Each $m$ is a positive integer. This proves a uniform positive energy quantum and a finite bound on the number of concentration points, without identifying an atom with a BPST profile. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **All bubble bundles and no hidden neck clutch.** Extract bubbles by selecting the least local radius carrying a fixed energy threshold below the quantum in step 5.1. After rescaling, the right side is bounded by $r^2M\to0$; step 1.1 and the strong-difference argument of step 2.1 give an unperturbed nonflat ASD limit on bounded regions with any remaining finite bad points removed by step 4.1. Its finite-energy point removal at infinity gives a smooth ASD sphere. A center at distance much greater than its scale from the singular plane gives ordinary bubbles and their deck partners; at bounded relative distance, center at the nearest fixed plane and obtain an invariant bubble. If a neck fails to have small energy on every dyadic annulus, repeat this rescaling at that annulus. Each new nonflat bubble consumes at least the positive quantum of step 5.1, so only finitely many repetitions are possible. On every remaining neck the maximum dyadic energy tends to zero. Its frames from step 3.1 are uniformly small after rescaling and have transition maps close to constants; the aligned transitions are homotopic to the identity, equivariantly in the singular case. Choose boundary spheres by radial Fubini so their tangential curvature $L^2$ norms are bounded by the neighboring annular energy. On such a sphere $|CS(a)|\le C\|a\|_2\|F_a\|_2+C\|a\|_\infty\|a\|_2^2\le C\delta^2$, with $\delta$ from step 3.1. The aligned frames give the same real lift of this transgression along the neck, because each transition is close to a constant and has zero degree. Stokes therefore bounds total neck energy by its two endpoint $C\delta^2$ terms plus $2\int|F_A^+|^2$. The latter is at most $C M^2r_{\mathrm{outer}}^4$ in physical coordinates. Taking the outer radius to zero after the sequence limit makes all these terms vanish. Thus the remaining necks carry neither extra energy nor a nonzero equivariant clutching class. The finite bubble bundles account for the whole local topological change; this has been proved rather than inferred from weak convergence alone. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 **Index accounting and the no-bubble convergence used later.** Apply the exact finite equivariant block rotation [F4] at every bubble and ordinary deck orbit. The neck identifications of step 6.1 have trivial clutch and contribute zero, so subtraction of the limiting cylindrical index is exactly the sum of the bubble relative indices. They are at least four for invariant bubbles and eight for ordinary ones, proving the asserted lower loss. Finally, if there are no bad points, the preliminary strong $W^{1,6}$ convergence is on a finite cover of the whole smaller cylinder. The holonomy fields then converge in $W^{1,6}$ by their actual Sobolev mapping formulas, so the first-order equation improves convergence to $W^{2,6}$. Repeating proves convergence in every order and hence smoothly. The same statement applies to any bounded window after an arbitrary time translation. Infinite-end convergence, broken-level count, generic regularity and nonlinear gluing still require their separate arguments. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

The complete finite-cylinder/perturbation energy discussion on PDF69–70 and compactness/index/energy discussion on PDF74–76 of the118-page instanton primary were read. The proof deliberately does not import the cited singular compactness theorem. The ordinary curved argument is reused at its exact local operations, with the bounded forcing, nonlocal-field convergence and finite isotropy supplied explicitly. In particular derivative convergence away from isolated points is not asserted before the nonlocal holonomy field can be bootstrapped on the filled cylinder; this avoids assuming that a loop-supported perturbation has purely local higher-derivative bounds near another bubbling point.
