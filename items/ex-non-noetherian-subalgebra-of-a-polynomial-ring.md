## Example

Let $k$ be a field and work inside $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$
([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]). Put

$$A\;:=\;k+x\,k[x,y]\;=\;\{\,c+xf\;:\;c\in k,\ f\in k[x,y]\,\}.$$

Then $A$ is a subring of $k[x,y]$ containing $k$, and it is the subalgebra
$k[x,xy,xy^2,\ldots]$ generated over $k$ by the elements $xy^{i}$ for
$i\in\mathbb N$. The ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $A$ generated by those elements is
$x\,k[x,y]$, and it is **not** finitely generated, so $A$ is not Noetherian —
even though $k[x,y]$ is.

Two consequences follow: a subring of a Noetherian ring need not be Noetherian,
and a subalgebra of an algebra of finite type over a field need not itself be of
finite type.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the ring $k[x,y]=k[x][y]$, the subset $A=k+xk[x,y]$ of it, and the elements $xy^{i}$ for $i\in\mathbb N$. Coefficients are read in the iterated form: an element of $k[x][y]$ is a finitely supported family of elements of $k[x]$, indexed by the exponent of $y$.

[L1] A subset $S$ of a ring is a **subring** when $1\in S$ and $S$ is closed under addition, additive inverses and multiplication; it is then a ring with the same zero and identity ([[def-subring]]).

[L2] $R[x]$ is the set of finitely supported functions $\mathbb N\to R$ with $(a+b)_i=a_i+b_i$ and $(ab)_i=\sum_{j+k=i}a_jb_k$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L3] Polynomial rings in finitely many indeterminates are defined by $R[x_1,\ldots,x_0]:=R$ and $R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}]:=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n][x_{n+1}]$ ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L4] For $S\subseteq R$, $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, so $S\subseteq(S)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[L5] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$; the empty sum is included and equals $0$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L6] For a commutative ring, being Noetherian is equivalent to every ideal being finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[L7] Every field is a Noetherian ring ([[ex-noetherian-integers-and-fields]]).

[L8] If $R$ is a Noetherian commutative ring then $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ is Noetherian for every $n\in\mathbb N$ ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]).

[L9] Every commutative algebra of finite type over a Noetherian commutative ring is a Noetherian ring ([[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[L10] $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is the smallest subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, and $A$ is of finite type over $R$ when it equals such a subring for a finite list ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $A$ is a subring of $k[x,y]$. It contains $1=1+x\cdot0$; it is closed under addition and additive inverses because $k$ and $xk[x,y]$ both are; and $(c+xf)(c'+xf')=cc'+x\big(cf'+c'f+xff'\big)$ lies in $A$. [L1, L2, L3, given]

1.2 $A=k[x,xy,xy^2,\ldots]$, the smallest subring of $k[x,y]$ containing $k$ and every $xy^{i}$. That subring is contained in $A$ because $A$ is a subring containing $k$ and each $xy^{i}=0+x\cdot y^{i}$. Conversely every $c+xf$ lies in it: expanding $f$ as a $k$-linear combination of monomials $x^{a}y^{b}$ gives $xf$ as a $k$-linear combination of $x^{a+1}y^{b}=x^{a}\cdot(xy^{b})$, and each of $x=xy^{0}$ and $xy^{b}$ is one of the listed generators. [L1, L3, L10, given]

2.1 The ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $A$ generated by $\{xy^{i}:i\in\mathbb N\}$ equals $x\,k[x,y]$. An element of $\mathfrak a$ is a finite sum $\sum_j a_j\,xy^{n_j}$ with $a_j\in A$, which lies in $x\,k[x,y]$; conversely, for $f\in k[x,y]$ expanded as a $k$-linear combination of monomials $x^{a}y^{b}$, the element $xf$ is the corresponding combination of $x^{a}\cdot(xy^{b})$ with $x^{a}\in A$, hence lies in $\mathfrak a$. [L4, L5, step 1.1]

3.1 Suppose $\mathfrak a=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$ as an ideal of $A$, with $m\in\mathbb N$ and $f_j\in\mathfrak a$. Since $x=xy^{0}\in\mathfrak a$ and $x\ne0$, the ideal $\mathfrak a$ is nonzero, so at least one $f_j$ is nonzero; let $N\in\mathbb N$ be an index beyond which every $f_j$ has vanishing $y$-coefficients, that is, the $y^{b}$-coefficient of every $f_j$ is $0$ for $b>N$. Any element of $(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$ is $\sum_j a_jf_j$ with $a_j\in A$; writing $a_j=c_j+xh_j$ with $c_j\in k$ and $h_j\in k[x,y]$, and using $f_j\in x\,k[x,y]$ from step 2.1, this element equals $\sum_j c_jf_j+x^{2}\,g$ for some $g\in k[x,y]$. Its $y^{N+1}$-coefficient is therefore the sum of $\sum_j c_j\cdot0=0$ and an element of $x^{2}k[x]$, hence lies in $x^{2}k[x]$. [L2, L4, L5, step 2.1]

4.1 But $xy^{N+1}$ lies in $\mathfrak a$ and its $y^{N+1}$-coefficient is $x$, which is not in $x^{2}k[x]$: every element of $x^{2}k[x]$ has vanishing coefficient at $x^{1}$, whereas $x$ has coefficient $1\ne0$ there. So $xy^{N+1}\notin(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$, contradicting $\mathfrak a=(f_1,\ldots,f_m)$. Hence $\mathfrak a$ is not finitely generated and $A$ is not Noetherian. [L2, L6, step 3.1]

5.1 The ambient ring $k[x,y]$ is Noetherian, since $k$ is a field and a polynomial ring in finitely many variables over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian. So the Noetherian ring $k[x,y]$ has the non-Noetherian subring $A$. And $A$ is not of finite type over $k$: an algebra of finite type over the Noetherian ring $k$ would be Noetherian, which $A$ is not, while $k[x,y]$ itself is of finite type over $k$. [L7, L8, L9, L10, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where finite generation actually fails.** The ideal $\mathfrak a$ needs the elements $xy^{i}$ for arbitrarily large $i$: multiplying by an element of $A$ either scales by a constant, which cannot raise the $y$-exponent, or introduces a factor $x$, which pushes the term into $x^{2}k[x,y]$ and out of reach of the coefficient examined in step 3.1.

- **The failure is not a failure of the ambient ring.** $k[x,y]$ satisfies every chain condition the Hilbert basis theorem gives it. What the subring lacks is any map back to it, which is exactly the hypothesis [[lem-retract-of-a-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]] adds in order to make the conclusion descend.
