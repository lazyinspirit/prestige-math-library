## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime and let
$$1\longrightarrow H\longrightarrow G\xrightarrow{\pi}\Gamma\longrightarrow1$$
be an exact sequence of profinite groups with $H$ finite and $\Gamma$ topologically isomorphic to the additive group $\mathbb Z_p$. Then $\pi$ admits a continuous group section. Consequently $G=H\rtimes\Gamma'$ for a closed subgroup $\Gamma'\cong\mathbb Z_p$. Moreover, $\Gamma'$ contains an open subgroup $\Gamma_0\cong\mathbb Z_p$ that is central in $G$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact sequence and the prime $p$ in the Statement.

[F1] A profinite group is an inverse limit of finite discrete groups. [[def-profinite-group-by-inverse-limit]]

[F2] The additive group $\mathbb Z_p$ is pro-$p$. [[def-p-adic-integers-as-compatible-residue-classes]] [[def-pro-p-group]]

[F3] For coprime positive integers $a,b$, the residue map $\mathbb Z/(ab)\to\mathbb Z/a\times\mathbb Z/b$ is an isomorphism. [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem]]

## Proof

**Given:** The exact sequence in the Statement.

1.1 Choose $\bar\gamma\in\Gamma$ whose integer powers are dense (a topological generator), and a lift $\gamma\in G$. The closed subgroup $D=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}$ is procyclic, meaning that the powers of $\gamma$ are dense in it. Every finite quotient of $D$ is cyclic. If its order is $p^a m$ with $p\nmid m$, [F3] identifies it with the product of its cyclic quotients of orders $p^a$ and $m$. Under a quotient map between finite cyclic quotients, the maps on these coordinates are reductions: both the CRT coordinates and the quotient maps send a representative to its residue modulo the indicated divisor. Thus these finite decompositions are compatible, and their inverse limit is $D=D_p\times D_{p'}$, where $D_p$ is procyclic pro-$p$ and all finite quotients of $D_{p'}$ have order prime to $p$. [F1, F3, given, choose, construct]

1.2 For every $n\ge1$, the image of $D$ in $\Gamma/p^n\Gamma$ is the whole cyclic group because it contains the generator $\bar\gamma$. The image of $D_{p'}$ in this finite $p$-group is trivial, so the image of $D_p$ is also the whole group. Thus $D_p$ has quotients of order $p^n$ for every $n$. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 Let $d$ be the projection of $\gamma$ to $D_p$. Every finite quotient of $D_p$ is cyclic of order $p^a$ and is generated by the image of $d$. By 1.2 these orders are unbounded, so for each $n$ the canonical quotient of order $p^n$ occurs; every other finite quotient of order $p^a$ factors through this one at $n=a$. Therefore the finite quotients of $D_p$, with their transition maps, have the same inverse limit as the system $\mathbb Z/p^n\mathbb Z$ under reduction. By [F2], this inverse limit is $\mathbb Z_p$, and the isomorphism sends $d$ to $1$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

2.1 The restriction $\pi|_{D_p}:D_p\to\Gamma$ induces a surjection modulo $p^n$ for every $n$, by 1.2. Under the isomorphism $D_p\cong\mathbb Z_p$ from 1.3 and a fixed identification $\Gamma\cong\mathbb Z_p$, a continuous homomorphism is multiplication by its value at $1$: this holds on the dense integers and extends to $\mathbb Z_p$ by continuity. Surjectivity modulo $p$ makes that value a unit. Therefore $\pi|_{D_p}$ is an isomorphism, and its inverse is a continuous section. Its image $\Gamma'$ gives $G=H\rtimes\Gamma'$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

2.2 Conjugation gives a continuous homomorphism $\Gamma'\to\operatorname{Aut}(H)$. The target is finite, so the kernel $\Gamma_0$ is open in $\Gamma'$ and is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. It centralizes $H$ by definition and centralizes the abelian group $\Gamma'$, hence $\Gamma_0\le Z(G)$. [given, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
