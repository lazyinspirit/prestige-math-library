## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $G$ be profinite, $M$ a finite discrete continuous $G$-module, and $C$ a discrete continuous $G$-module. Over the open normal subgroups $N$ acting trivially on $M$, the actual inflation maps induce natural isomorphisms
$$\operatorname*{colim}_N\operatorname{Ext}^r_{G/N}(M,C^N)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Ext}^r_G(M,C)\qquad(r\ge0).$$
If $G$ is finite and $(C_i)_{i\in I}$ is any family of $G$-modules, the natural map
$$\operatorname{Ext}^r_G(M,\prod_i C_i)\xrightarrow{\sim}\prod_i\operatorname{Ext}^r_G(M,C_i)$$
is an isomorphism. If $D\le G$ and $A$ is a $D$-module, finite coinduction also gives the actual natural isomorphism
$$\operatorname{Ext}^r_G(M,\operatorname{Coind}_D^G A)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Ext}^r_D(M|_D,A).$$
The products are taken in the ordinary finite-group module category; no arbitrary product of discrete profinite modules is asserted to be discrete.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The profinite group, finite module, coefficient, product family, finite subgroup and explicit AC/DC in the Statement.

[A1] AC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] Discrete profinite modules have injective Ext and its finite-free-lattice cohomology comparison; the comparison is natural. [[lem-discrete-profinite-modules-injective-ext-and-cochain-comparison]]

[F2] Continuous cohomology of discrete modules is the filtered limit of finite-quotient cohomology with fixed coefficients. [[thm-continuous-profinite-cohomology-as-direct-limit-of-finite-quotients]]

[F3] Every subgroup of a free abelian group is free. [[lem-subgroups-of-free-abelian-groups-are-free]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use one finite-rank integral lattice resolution, compare its first-quadrant Ext double complexes, and use finite group-ring resolutions for products.

1.1 Choose an open normal subgroup $U$ acting trivially on $M$, and write $Q=G/U$. A finite sum of $\mathbb Z[Q]$ maps onto $M$. Its kernel is a subgroup of a finite-rank free abelian group, hence free of finite rank and finitely generated by [F3]. Repeat with finite sums of $\mathbb Z[Q]$ to obtain an exact resolution $P_\bullet\to M$ whose every term is a finite free $\mathbb Z[Q]$-module, in particular a finite free integer $G$-lattice. AC/DC supplies the recursion. [A1, F3, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 For an injective resolution $C\to I^\bullet$ in the continuous $G$-category, the first-quadrant double complex $\operatorname{Hom}_G(P_q,I^p)$ has finite direct sums in each total degree. Exactness of $P_\bullet$ and injectivity of each $I^p$ make one filtration compute $\operatorname{Ext}^r_G(M,C)$; the other has first page $\operatorname{Ext}^p_G(P_q,C)$. The same construction applies to each finite quotient $G/N$ with coefficient $C^N$. By [F1], each first-page term is ordinary continuous cohomology of $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(P_q,C)$. Because $N\subseteq U$ acts trivially on $P_q$, its $N$-fixed coefficient is $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(P_q,C^N)$. Thus [F2] identifies the filtered limit of every finite-quotient first-page term with the corresponding profinite term. Filtered colimits are exact, and finite-total-degree spectral-sequence comparison gives the asserted isomorphism on abutments. All maps are the natural inflation maps. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Now let $G$ be finite and choose the finite-rank free $\mathbb Z[G]$-resolution in step 1.1 with $Q=G$. For every term $P_q$, the functor $\operatorname{Hom}_G(P_q,-)$ commutes with arbitrary products. Under AC, products of surjections are surjective, so products of cochain complexes have cohomology equal to the product of their cohomologies. The natural product map on Ext is therefore an isomorphism in every degree. [A1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $D\le G$ finite, $\operatorname{Coind}_D^G$ is exact and right adjoint to restriction, because it is a finite product indexed by cosets. The restriction functor is exact, so its right adjoint takes injectives to injectives. Coinducing a $D$-injective resolution of $A$ and using the Hom adjunction identifies the actual Ext complexes, proving the displayed isomorphism. Empty product families, the trivial quotient and $r=0$ are included. [F1, step 2.2, construct, algebra] ∎
