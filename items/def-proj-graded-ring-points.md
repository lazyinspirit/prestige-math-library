## Definition

Let $S=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}S_d$ be a commutative nonnegatively graded ring
([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]) and let
$$S_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}S_d$$
be its **irrelevant ideal**. Indeed $S_+$ is an ideal: it is an additive
subgroup, and for $s_i\in S_i$ with $i>0$ and $s_j\in S_j$ with $j\ge0$ one has
$s_is_j\in S_{i+j}$ with $i+j>0$.

An ideal $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ is **homogeneous** when it is generated by
homogeneous elements. Equivalently, $\mathfrak p$ is homogeneous when for every
$a\in\mathfrak p$ each homogeneous component of $a$ lies in $\mathfrak p$: if
$\mathfrak p=(h_\lambda)$ with $h_\lambda$ homogeneous and
$a=\sum_d a_d\in\mathfrak p$, then each $a_d$ is an $S$-linear combination of
the generators and so lies in $\mathfrak p$. A homogeneous ideal is a
**homogeneous prime** when it is prime as an ideal.

The **projective spectrum** $\operatorname{Proj}S$ is the set of homogeneous
prime ideals $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ with
$$S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p .$$
For a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq S$ put
$$V_+(I)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}S:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\},$$
and declare these the **closed sets** of the Zariski topology on
$\operatorname{Proj}S$; a subset is **open** when its complement is closed. The
family $\{V_+(I)\}$ is closed under the topological operations (see the
Remarks), so the declaration defines a topology, which is the trace on
$\operatorname{Proj}S$ of the Zariski topology of $\operatorname{Spec}S$
([[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]]) restricted to the homogeneous primes that do
not contain $S_+$, presented by homogeneous generators.

The **zero ring is allowed**: for $S=0$ the ring has no prime ideals at all, so
$\operatorname{Proj}0=\varnothing$ with its unique topology and
$V_+(I)=\varnothing$ for every homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq 0$. Consequently
$\operatorname{Proj}S$ may be empty for nonzero $S$ as well, and all statements
below are meant to include the empty case.

## Remarks

- **The closed sets are the topological ones.** $V_+(0)=\operatorname{Proj}S$,
  since every $\mathfrak p$ contains $0$, and $V_+(S)=\varnothing$, since no
  prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ contains the unit ideal $S$. For a
  family $(I_\lambda)$ of homogeneous ideals, $\bigcap_\lambda V_+(I_\lambda)
  =V_+(\sum_\lambda I_\lambda)$ because $\mathfrak p$ contains every
  $I_\lambda$ if and only if it contains their sum, and $\sum_\lambda
  I_\lambda$ is homogeneous; the union of $V_+(I)$ and $V_+(J)$ is $V_+(IJ)$
  because a prime containing the product $IJ$ contains $I$ or $J$, while a
  prime containing $I$ contains $IJ$; the product of homogeneous ideals is
  homogeneous. Intersections of the form $\bigcap_\lambda V_+(I_\lambda)$
  include arbitrary, possibly infinite, index families; for the empty family
  the intersection is $\operatorname{Proj}S=V_+(0)$.
- **Uniqueness of the presentation is not claimed.** Many homogeneous ideals
  present the same closed set; the relevant saturation statement for
  polynomial rings is proved later.
- The condition $S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$ excludes exactly the homogeneous
  primes above the irrelevant ideal; a homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p$ with
  $S_+\subseteq\mathfrak p$ is not a point of $\operatorname{Proj}S$.
