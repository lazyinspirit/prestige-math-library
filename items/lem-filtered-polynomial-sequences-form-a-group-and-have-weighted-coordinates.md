## Statement

Let $G_0\supseteq G_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq G_{d+1}=\{e\}$ be subgroups of a group, with $[G_i,G_j]\subseteq G_{i+j}$, and set $G_i=\{e\}$ for $i>d$. Call $f:\mathbb Z\to G_0$ polynomial for this prefiltration when every $k$-fold right difference lies in $G_k$. Such maps form a group under pointwise multiplication and inversion, and integer affine substitution $n\mapsto a+bn$ preserves polynomiality.

Suppose additionally that $G_0=G_1=G$ has a supplied adapted Mal’cev basis $X_1,\ldots,X_m$, with $m_i=\dim G_i$. A map $g:\mathbb Z\to G$ is polynomial if and only if

$$\psi_X(g(n))=\sum_{i=0}^d t_i\binom ni,\qquad (t_i)_j=0\ \text{whenever}\ j\leq m-m_i.$$

Equivalently, its $j$th coordinate is a real polynomial of degree at most $w_j=\max\{i:X_j\in\operatorname{Lie}(G_i)\}$. Every such sequence also has an ordered group-valued expansion

$$g(n)=u_0u_1^{\binom n1}\cdots u_d^{\binom nd},\qquad u_i\in G_i,$$

for every integer $n$, including negative integers. In this expansion, $g(0)=g(1)=e$ implies $u_0=u_1=e$. The zero-dimensional case has the empty coordinate list and the identity sequence.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite prefiltration as stated; for the coordinate assertions, a supplied adapted Mal’cev basis for $G_\bullet$.

[F1] Right differences are $\partial_hf(n)=f(n+h)f(n)^{-1}$; polynomiality quantifies over every integer increment and base point. [[def-polynomial-sequence-on-a-filtered-group]]

[F2] The adapted basis gives global ordered exponential coordinates, normal exponential tails $H_j=\exp\operatorname{span}(X_{j+1},\ldots,X_m)$, and $G_i=H_{m-m_i}$. [[def-malcev-basis-and-rationality]]

[F3] Under this supplied chart, exponential images of Lie subalgebras are closed connected subgroups; the quotient of two successive tails is the additive real line, with the intervening basis coordinate. These facts follow from the finite exponential and BCH construction without a choice assumption. [[lem-finite-nilpotent-exponential-and-bch-calculus]]

## Proof

1.1 For $S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}$ let $F_S=\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^k:\omega_i=1\text{ for }i\in S\}$. Write $U_S(a)$ for the tuple equal to $a$ on $F_S$ and $e$ elsewhere, where $a\in G_{|S|}$. Define $K_k(G_\bullet)$ as the subgroup of $G_0^{\{0,1\}^k}$ generated by these tuples, and put $K_0(G_\bullet)=G_0$. For the shifted prefiltration $G^+_i=G_{i+1}$, the subgroup $K_k(G^+_\bullet)$ is contained in $K_k(G_\bullet)$. It is normal there: the commutator of its generator on $F_S$ with an unshifted generator on $F_T$ is supported on $F_{S\cup T}$, with value in $G_{|S|+1+|T|}\subseteq G_{|S\cup T|+1}$. The same holds for inverse generators, so their conjugations preserve the shifted subgroup. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Split a $(k+1)$-cube by its last coordinate. The preceding normality makes $\{(A,DA):A\in K_k(G_\bullet),\ D\in K_k(G^+_\bullet)\}$ a subgroup. Faces not requiring the last coordinate are diagonal generators $(U_S(a),U_S(a))$; those requiring it are $(e,U_S(a))$ with $a\in G_{|S|+1}$. These are precisely generators of that subgroup, proving
$$K_{k+1}(G_\bullet)=\{(A,DA):A\in K_k(G_\bullet),\ D\in K_k(G^+_\bullet)\}.$$
Its decomposition is unique: $A$ is the first slice and $D$ is the second slice times $A^{-1}$. This also holds for every shifted prefiltration. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 In the adapted chart the tails in [F2] have precisely their ordered tail coordinates, and $H_{j-1}/H_j$ has additive coordinate along $X_j$ by [F3]. Thus, for a polynomial sequence valued in $H_{j-1}$, its quotient coordinate $p_j(n)$ has every $(w_j+1)$-fold difference zero: derivatives of that order lie in $G_{w_j+1}$, whose image in this quotient is zero. In particular $\Delta^{w_j+1}p_j=0$. Subtract $\sum_{i=0}^{w_j}\Delta^ip_j(0)\binom ni$. The difference has zero values at $0,\ldots,w_j$ and satisfies this same recurrence, whose leading and trailing coefficients are nonzero. The recurrence propagates the zero values forward and backward, proving that $p_j$ is exactly that real polynomial on all of $\mathbb Z$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 For $x,h_1,\ldots,h_k\in\mathbb Z$, set $C_k(f;x,h)_\omega=f(x+\sum_i\omega_ih_i)$. If $f$ is polynomial, induction on $k$, simultaneously for every prefiltration, puts all these cubes in $K_k(G_\bullet)$: the first slice of its $(k+1)$-cube is a cube of $f$, and the second times the inverse of the first is a cube of $\partial_{h_{k+1}}f$, polynomial for the shifted prefiltration. The induction starts with the order-zero assertion $f(x)\in G_0$. Conversely, if every cube belongs to the corresponding $K_k$, uniqueness in step 1.2 puts every cube of $\partial_hf$ in $K_k(G^+_\bullet)$. Repeat this finite argument for each specified finite list of increments; its order-zero conclusion is $\partial_{h_j}\cdots\partial_{h_1}f(x)\in G_j$. Thus the cube condition is equivalent to polynomiality. [F1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Cubes of $fg$ are pointwise products of cubes of $f$ and $g$, and cubes of $f^{-1}$ are their inverses. Since every $K_k$ is a group, step 2.1 proves closure under pointwise products and inverses. For $\widetilde f(n)=f(a+bn)$, each successive derivative is the corresponding derivative of $f$ with increments $bh_1,\ldots,bh_j$, evaluated at $a+bn$; hence integer affine substitution preserves polynomiality. This includes $b=0$ and all negative increments. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $p$ is a real polynomial of degree at most $w_j$, the sequence $\exp(p(n)X_j)$ is polynomial for $G_\bullet$: its $k$th right difference is $\exp((\Delta_{h_k}\cdots\Delta_{h_1}p)(n)X_j)$, in $G_{w_j}\subseteq G_k$ for $k\leq w_j$, and equal to $e$ for $k>w_j$. Apply step 1.3 first with $j=1$, and remove this exponential factor on the left. Step 3.1 preserves polynomiality; ordered coordinates show the residual is valued in $H_1$. Repeat for $j=2,\ldots,m$. This proves that every polynomial sequence has coordinate polynomials of the stated degrees. Conversely, each exponential factor with the stated degree is polynomial by the calculation above, and their product is polynomial by step 3.1. Expanding the real coordinate polynomials in the binomial basis gives precisely the displayed support condition, since $w_j\geq i$ exactly when $j>m-m_i$. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, step 1.3, algebra]

4.2 For the group-valued expansion, put $u_0=g(0)$ and $r_1(n)=u_0^{-1}g(n)$. This is polynomial, valued in $G_1=G$, and equal to $e$ at $0$. Suppose $r_i$ is polynomial, valued in $G_i$, and equal to $e$ at $0,\ldots,i-1$. Choose $u_i=r_i(i)\in G_i$. The map $u_i^{\binom ni}$ is polynomial: its $k$th difference remains a power of $u_i$ in $G_i\subseteq G_k$ for $k\leq i$, and is identity for $k>i$. Hence $r_{i+1}(n)=u_i^{-\binom ni}r_i(n)$ is polynomial by step 3.1, still valued in $G_i$, and vanishes at $0,\ldots,i$. [F1, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 Since $[G_i,G_i]\subseteq G_{2i}\subseteq G_{i+1}$ for $i\geq1$, project $r_{i+1}$ to the abelian group $G_i/G_{i+1}$. Its $(i+1)$-fold unit difference is zero by polynomiality, and its first $i+1$ values are zero. The recurrence $\Delta^{i+1}=0$ has leading and trailing coefficients $1$ or $-1$; these are invertible operations in any abelian group, so the zero values propagate in both directions. Thus $r_{i+1}$ is valued in $G_{i+1}$. After $d$ such removals the residual is valued in $G_{d+1}=\{e\}$, yielding $g(n)=u_0u_1^{\binom n1}\cdots u_d^{\binom nd}$ in exactly that order. The construction gives $u_0=g(0)$ and $u_1=u_0^{-1}g(1)$, so $g(0)=g(1)=e$ forces both to be identity. Dimension zero has the unique empty product; all recurrences and binomial identities include negative integers. [given, F1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
