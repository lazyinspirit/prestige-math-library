## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group over $k$, $B\supseteq T$ a Borel subgroup, $\Delta$ the corresponding base and $W=N_G(T)/T$ ([[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). For a simple root $\alpha\in\Delta$ with image $s_\alpha\in W$ and any $w\in W$ one has the Tits inclusions $s_\alpha B(k)w\subseteq B(k)wB(k)\cup B(k)s_\alpha wB(k)$. Equivalently, the quadruple $(G(k),B(k),N_G(T)(k),S)$ with $S=\{s_\alpha:\alpha\in\Delta\}$ is a Tits system (BN-pair) in the abstract group $G(k)$: (T1) $G(k)$ is generated by $B(k)$ and $N(k)$ and $B(k)\cap N(k)=T(k)$ is normal in $N(k)$; (T2) the elements of $S$ are involutions generating $W(k)=N(k)/T(k)$; (T3) $sB(k)w\subseteq B(k)wB(k)\cup B(k)swB(k)$ for all $s\in S$, $w\in W$; (T4) $sB(k)s\not\subseteq B(k)$ for $s\in S$. In particular $B(k)s_\alpha B(k)\cup B(k)=B(k)\cup B(k)s_\alpha B(k)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, base $\Delta$, $S=\{s_\alpha:\alpha\in\Delta\}$ and $W=N_G(T)/T$.

[F1] The independent geometric cellular input is Milne21.70–21.73 and21.79: $G/B$ is the disjoint union of $U$-orbits at the Weyl fixed points, and their root-coordinate sections give $G=\bigsqcup_w U^wn_wB$ with each multiplication $U^w\times B$ a $k$-scheme isomorphism onto its cell. These source results are proved from Bialynicki-Birula cells and root coordinates before the Tits-system proposition21.75, and give the full arbitrary-field point decomposition. Weyl representatives and conjugation of root groups are supplied by [[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]], and all ordered positive root-coordinate products by [[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]] and [[lem-root-coordinate-cells-and-generation]].

[F2] The two Borel subgroups of $G_\alpha$ containing $T$ are $U_\alpha T$ and $U_{-\alpha}T$, and the rank-one group satisfies $G_\alpha=B_\alpha\sqcup B_\alpha n_\alpha B_\alpha$ with $B_\alpha=U_\alpha T$; hence every element of $U_{-\alpha}(k)\smallsetminus\{1\}$ lies in $B_\alpha(k)n_\alpha B_\alpha(k)$ ([[lem-borel-root-group-opposition]], [[lem-sl2-structure-and-root-coordinates]]).

[F3] The Weyl group acts simply transitively on the Borels containing $T$, and $W$ is a finite group generated by the involutions $s_\alpha$, $\alpha\in\Delta$ ([[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]], [[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

## Proof

1.1 The independent source-cell isomorphisms of [F1] give $G(k)=\bigsqcup_w U^w(k)n_wB(k)$, so $G(k)$ is generated by $B(k)$ and $N(k)$. This is a point statement obtained from scheme isomorphisms defined over $k$, not from algebraic generation alone. The Weyl action on Borels is free by [F3], so $B(k)\cap N(k)=T(k)$; this subgroup is normal in $N(k)$ by the normalizer definition. Thus(T1) holds. The field-point quotient description of [F3] identifies $N(k)/T(k)$ with $W$, generated by its simple reflections of order two, proving(T2). [F1, F3]

1.2 Fix a simple root $\alpha$, write $s=s_\alpha$ and choose $n_s$. A simple reflection permutes $\Phi^+\smallsetminus\{\alpha\}$. Order those root groups first and $U_\alpha$ last in the coordinates of $B$ from [F1]; conjugating their first factors by $n_s$ keeps them in $B$, while $U_\alpha$ becomes $U_{-\alpha}$. Consequently it suffices for(T3) to show $U_{-\alpha}(k)n_sn_w\subseteq B(k)n_wB(k)\cup B(k)n_sn_wB(k).$ If $w^{-1}\alpha$ is positive, conjugating root groups gives $U_{-\alpha}n_sn_w=n_sn_wU_{w^{-1}\alpha}$, so this set lies in the second double coset. [F1, F3]

2.1 If $w^{-1}\alpha$ is negative, the identity element of $U_{-\alpha}$ gives the second double coset. For a nonidentity element $u$, the explicit rank-one factorization [F2] gives $u\in B_\alpha(k)n_sB_\alpha(k)$, where $B_\alpha=T U_\alpha$. Hence $u n_s n_w\in B(k)n_sB_\alpha(k)n_sn_w\subseteq B(k)U_{-\alpha}(k)n_w=B(k)n_wU_{-w^{-1}\alpha}(k)\subseteq B(k)n_wB(k).$ Here $n_sB_\alpha n_s^{-1}=T U_{-\alpha}$ and $n_s^2\in T$, and the final root is positive. These algebraic formulas hold over every field, including small finite fields and characteristic two. This proves(T3) in both sign cases. [F1, F2, step 1.2]

3.1 The negative root parameter $u_{-\alpha}(1)$ is not in $B$: for a regular dominant cocharacter with $B=P_G(\lambda)$, its orbit parameter is $t^{-\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle}$ and has no limit at zero. Thus $n_sBn_s^{-1}$ is not contained in $B$, proving(T4). The four axioms yield the full Tits system with the claimed inclusion, and the displayed union with $B(k)$ at the end of the Statement is the same union with its two summands written in the opposite order. No substantive assertion is derived from that tautological equality. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
