## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. Let $K$ be a global field, let $S$ be a nonempty set of its places containing all archimedean places, and let $K_S$ be the maximal Galois extension unramified outside $S$. For each finite intermediate field $F/K$ in $K_S$, write $S_F$ for the places above $S$ and $\operatorname{Cl}_{S_F}(F)$ for the ideal class group of the ring of elements integral at every finite place outside $S_F$. Every class of $\operatorname{Cl}_{S_F}(F)$ becomes principal after extension to $\operatorname{Cl}_{S_L}(L)$ for some finite intermediate extension $L/F$ inside $K_S$. Consequently
$$\operatorname*{colim}_{F\subset K_S}\operatorname{Cl}_{S_F}(F)=0,$$
where the limit uses ideal extension. This claim concerns actual ideals and does not assume a principal ideal theorem or Poitou–Tate duality.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The global field, nonempty $S$, tower and explicit AC, CC and DC of the Statement.

[A1] AC, CC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] Number-field ideal class groups are finite. A one-point coefficient ring of a smooth proper curve over a finite field has finite ideal class group, and the curve/function-field equivalence supplies such a model under AC. [[thm-number-field-ideal-class-group-finite-in-zf]] [[lem-function-field-coefficient-ring-has-finite-ideal-class-group]] [[thm-curves-function-fields-equivalence]]

[F2] Every open finite-index idèle-class subgroup of a number field or global function field is the norm group of a unique finite abelian extension; its actual global Artin map has exactly that kernel. [[thm-global-class-field-existence-number-field]] [[thm-global-function-field-class-field-existence]] [[thm-global-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]] [[thm-global-function-field-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]]

[F3] For finite local fields of the stated type, local reciprocity identifies norm groups of finite abelian extensions uniquely; unramified local units are norms. [[thm-local-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]] [[lem-unramified-local-unit-norms-and-cohomology]]

[F4] For every finite group, transfer from its abelianization to the abelianization of its commutator subgroup is zero. [[lem-finite-commutator-transfer-vanishes]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** construct two unramified class-field stages and identify ideal extension with finite transfer.

1.1 Fix a finite Galois $F/K$ in $K_S$. In the number-field case let $T$ be all archimedean places of $F$. In the function-field case choose one $v_0\in S_F$ and let $T=\{v_0\}$. The $T$-ideal class group is finite by [F1]: in the second case use the smooth proper curve over the finite exact constant field with $v_0$ as the omitted point. For a larger set of finite places, localization makes its ideal class group the quotient by the subgroup generated by the newly inverted primes. This follows directly from finite prime-ideal factorization and remains true for infinitely many inverted primes because each fractional ideal has finite support. [A1, F1, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $N_T\le C_F$ be the image of the idèle subgroup whose finite coordinates lie in $\mathcal O_v^\times$ outside $T$, whose coordinates at $T$ are arbitrary, and whose archimedean coordinates are arbitrary. It is open because the idèle quotient map is open. The valuation map identifies $C_F/N_T$ with the $T$-ideal class group: its kernel consists exactly of idèles differing from a principal idèle by units at the noninverted finite places. Thus $N_T$ has finite index by step 1.1. By [F2], it is the norm group of a unique finite abelian extension $H/F$, and its actual Artin map identifies $\operatorname{Gal}(H/F)$ with that class group. [A1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every place of $T$ splits completely in $H$, since its full local multiplicative group maps trivially under the local factor of the global Artin map. At a finite place outside $T$, the local units map trivially. The local Artin quotient is consequently generated by a uniformizer. If its order is $n$, its kernel is $\mathcal O_v^\times\pi^{n\mathbb Z}$; the degree-$n$ unramified extension has this same norm subgroup by [F3] and the valuation formula, so the uniqueness clause in [F3] makes the actual local extension unramified. Conversely any finite abelian extension unramified at all finite places and split at $T$ has norm kernel containing $N_T$: [F3] supplies the unit norms and a split factor has all local elements as norms. By [F2]'s inclusion-reversing uniqueness, such an extension lies in $H$. Hence $H$ is maximal for these local conditions. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Apply steps 1.1–3.1 to $H$ with $T_H$ the places over $T$. In the function-field case $T_H$ is finite and nonempty; its ideal class group is a quotient of the finite class group obtained by omitting any one of its places. Obtain a finite abelian $H_2/H$ unramified at all finite places and split at $T_H$, with Artin group the $T_H$-ideal class group. Every $F$-automorphism of $H$ carries this extension to one with the same local conditions, so its uniqueness makes $H_2/F$ Galois. The composite is unramified at finite places and split at $T$. Its maximal abelian subextension over $F$ is $H$ by step 3.1. Thus, for $G=\operatorname{Gal}(H_2/F)$ and $D=\operatorname{Gal}(H_2/H)$, one has $D=G'$ and $G''=1$. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, choose, construct, algebra]

5.1 For a finite place $v\notin T$, choose a place of $H_2$ above it and let $g\in G$ be its arithmetic Frobenius. Left cosets of $D$ correspond to places of $H$ above $v$. On a $g$-orbit of length $f$, choose successive coset representatives along that orbit. In the transfer product every interior factor cancels; the remaining factor is a conjugate in $D$ of $g^f$, namely the arithmetic Frobenius of the corresponding place of $H$ in $H_2/H$. Multiplication over the finitely many orbits gives exactly the Artin image of the extended ideal $v\mathcal O_H$. Thus ideal extension $\operatorname{Cl}_T(F)\to\operatorname{Cl}_{T_H}(H)$ corresponds to transfer $G/G'\to G'/G''$. By [F4] that transfer is zero, and the target Artin isomorphism from step 4.1 implies that every extended class is principal. [A1, F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, choose, construct, algebra]

6.1 Since $S_F$ contains $T$ in the function-field case and contains every archimedean place in the number-field case, step 1.1 makes $\operatorname{Cl}_{S_F}(F)$ a quotient of $\operatorname{Cl}_T(F)$, and likewise after passing to $H$. The extension map on these quotients is therefore zero by step 5.1. The field $H/F$ is unramified at every finite place. Its finite normal closure over $K$ is unramified outside $S$, since $F/K$ is; hence $H\subseteq K_S$. Every class represented at a finite stage of the directed system therefore dies at a finite later stage. The finite Galois stages are cofinal, so the direct limit is zero. This also includes the empty class and the case where all finite places lie in $S$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
