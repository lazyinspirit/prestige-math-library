## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Fix $n\ge0$ and
$p\in\mathbb C^n$. For $n=0$ set $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^0,p}:=\mathbb C$; for
$n\ge1$ read $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ through the translation convention
recorded on this page. Then the Krull dimension of the germ ring is

$$\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}=n .$$

For $n\ge1$ the maximal ideal of $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ is generated by
the $n$ coordinate differences:

$$\mathfrak m_{\mathbb C^n,p}=(z_1-p_1,\ \dots,\ z_n-p_n).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge0$ and the germ ring $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$
of [[def-holomorphic-germ-ring-and-its-maximal-ideal]], transported from the
published origin case by the page's translation convention; for $n=0$ the ring
is $\mathbb C$.

[F1] The germ ring $\mathcal O_{n,0}$ is a commutative ring with identity; its
distinguished ideal is $\mathfrak m_{n,0}=\{f:f(0)=0\}$, and
$\mathcal O_{0,0}=\mathbb C$, $\mathfrak m_{0,0}=\{0\}$
([[def-holomorphic-germ-ring-and-its-maximal-ideal]]).

[F2] Units of $\mathcal O_{n,0}$ are exactly the germs with nonzero value at
$0$, and $\mathcal O_{n,0}$ is a local ring with maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m_{n,0}$; every proper ideal of a local ring is contained in its
unique maximal ideal ([[prop-units-in-the-holomorphic-germ-ring]],
[[def-local-ring]]).

[F3] If $f$ is holomorphic on a polydisc about $0$, then on a smaller polydisc
$f(z)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha$ with $|c_\alpha|\le M\prod_k r_k^{-\alpha_k}$
for some $M$ ([[thm-power-series-expansion-in-several-complex-variables]]).

[F4] Coefficients obeying such a bound define a holomorphic function by their
power series, and a function has at most one such representation
([[thm-power-series-define-holomorphic-functions-in-several-variables]],
[[cor-uniqueness-of-multivariable-power-series-coefficients]]).

[F5] For $m\ge1$ the ring $\mathcal O_{m,0}$ is a unique factorisation domain,
hence an integral domain, and it is Noetherian
([[thm-holomorphic-germ-ring-is-a-ufd]], [[def-unique-factorisation-domain]],
[[thm-holomorphic-germ-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[F6] A quotient $R/P$ is an integral domain exactly when $P$ is prime, and
every maximal ideal is prime ([[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]],
[[cor-maximal-ideals-are-prime]]).

[F7] The Krull dimension of a nonzero commutative ring is the supremum of the
lengths of strict chains of prime ideals; $\mathbb C$ is a field
([[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]], [[thm-complex-numbers-form-a-field]]).

[F8] Assume AC. Let $R$ be Noetherian and let $I=(x_1,\dots,x_k)$ with $k\ge1$;
every prime ideal minimal over $I$ has height at most $k$
([[thm-krull-height-theorem]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F9] The height of a prime is the dimension of the localisation,
$\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=\dim R_{\mathfrak p}$, and contraction along
$R\to S^{-1}R$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from
$\operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}R)$ onto the primes of $R$ disjoint from $S$
([[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]],
[[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).




**Proof technique:** direct — compute the maximal ideal from power-series grouping, identify the coordinate quotients, and bound every prime chain by the height of the maximal ideal.

## Proof

1.1 Suppose first that $n=0$. Then $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^0,p}=\mathbb C$ is a field by [F1] and [F7]. A nonzero ideal of a field contains a nonzero element, which is a unit, so it is the whole ring; hence $(0)$ is the only prime ideal, there is no strict chain of prime ideals of length $\ge1$, and $\dim\mathbb C=0$ by [F7]. [given, F1, F7]

1.2 Now let $n\ge1$, write $\mathcal O:=\mathcal O_{n,0}$ and $\mathfrak m:=\mathfrak m_{n,0}$ for the origin case; the general-centre case is transported at the end. Choose a representative of $f\in\mathcal O$ on a polydisc and let $f(z)=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha z^\alpha$ be its expansion with the bound of [F3]. Group the nonzero multi-indices by their first positive coordinate and set $$h_i(z):=\sum_{\alpha_i\ge1,\ \alpha_j=0\ (j<i)}c_\alpha z^{\alpha-e_i}\qquad(1\le i\le n).$$ The coefficient of $z^\beta$ in $h_i$ is $c_{\beta+e_i}$ for the indices with $\beta_j=0$ for $j<i$, so it obeys the bound $|c_{\beta+e_i}|\le M r_i^{-1}\prod_k r_k^{-\beta_k}$; by [F4] each $h_i$ is holomorphic on the polydisc. Every nonzero multi-index has a first positive coordinate, so it contributes to exactly one $h_i$, and the subseries of an absolutely convergent series converge to the corresponding partial sums; hence $$f=f(0)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_i h_i$$ as germs on the polydisc. [given, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 Consequently $f\in\mathfrak m$ (that is, $f(0)=0$) if and only if $f$ lies in the ideal $(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ of $\mathcal O$. Conversely each coordinate germ $z_i$ vanishes at $0$, so $(z_1,\dots,z_n)\subseteq\mathfrak m$. Therefore $\mathfrak m=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$, an ideal generated by $n$ elements. [step 1.2, F1, F2]

2.2 Fix $0\le k\le n$ and define $\varphi:\mathcal O_{n-k,0}\to\mathcal O/(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ on the germ of $g(z_{k+1},\dots,z_n)$ as the class of its inclusion in $\mathcal O$. This is a well-defined unital ring homomorphism, because addition and multiplication of germs are represented pointwise and the inclusion respects them. It is surjective: expanding any $F\in\mathcal O$ as in step 1.2 and grouping the multi-indices with $\alpha_1=\dots=\alpha_k=0$ into a germ $G$ of the remaining variables, [F4] makes $G$ holomorphic while the complementary subseries is divisible by one of $z_1,\dots,z_k$, so $F-G\in(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ and $\varphi(G)=F+(z_1,\dots,z_k)$. [step 1.2, F1, F4, construct]

3.1 The map $\varphi$ of step 2.2 is injective: if $\varphi(g)=0$, then $g\in(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ as a germ in $\mathcal O$, so representatives on a common polydisc satisfy $g(z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}z_iH_i(z)$ there; setting $z_1=\dots=z_k=0$ kills the right-hand side, so the representative of $g$, which does not involve the first $k$ variables, vanishes on a polydisc in $\mathbb C^{n-k}$, which is exactly the zero germ. Hence $\mathcal O/(z_1,\dots,z_k)\cong\mathcal O_{n-k,0}$. [step 2.2, F1]

4.1 By step 3.1 each ideal $(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ has quotient isomorphic to $\mathcal O_{n-k,0}$. If $k<n$, then $n-k\ge1$, so this quotient is an integral domain by [F5]; if $k=n$, it is $\mathcal O_{0,0}=\mathbb C$, a field by [F7] and hence an integral domain. Thus every $(z_1,\dots,z_k)$ with $0\le k\le n$ is prime by [F6]. In particular $\mathfrak m=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ is prime, and it is maximal by [F2]. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F5, F6, F7]

5.1 The chain of prime ideals $$(0)\subsetneq(z_1)\subsetneq(z_1,z_2)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(z_1,\dots,z_n)=\mathfrak m$$ is strict: $(0)$ is prime because $\mathcal O$ is a domain by [F5], each later term is prime by step 4.1, and $z_k\notin(z_1,\dots,z_{k-1})$, since otherwise its class in $\mathcal O/(z_1,\dots,z_{k-1})\cong\mathcal O_{n-k+1,0}$ would be zero by step 3.1, whereas that class corresponds to the first coordinate germ of $\mathcal O_{n-k+1,0}$, which is nonzero. This chain has length $n$, so $\dim\mathcal O\ge n$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F5, F7]

5.2 For the reverse inequality, [F5] makes $\mathcal O$ Noetherian, and by step 2.1 the prime ideal $\mathfrak m$ is generated by $n$ elements, hence is minimal over that ideal. Under AC, [F8] gives $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\le n$. [step 2.1, step 4.1, F5, F8, given]

6.1 Every proper ideal of $\mathcal O$ is contained in $\mathfrak m$: if $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak m$, then $I$ contains an element outside the maximal ideal, that element is a unit by [F2], and $I=\mathcal O$. Hence the primes disjoint from $S=\mathcal O\setminus\mathfrak m$ are exactly the primes of $\mathcal O$. By [F9], contraction is an inclusion-preserving bijection $\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{\mathfrak m})\to\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O)$ whose inverse is the inclusion-preserving prime extension $P\mapsto S^{-1}P$. Thus a strict chain of primes in $\mathcal O$ extends to a strict chain of the same length in $\mathcal O_{\mathfrak m}$, so $\dim\mathcal O\le\dim\mathcal O_{\mathfrak m}=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\le n$ by step 5.2. [step 5.2, F2, F9]

7.1 Steps 5.1 and 6.1 give $\dim\mathcal O_{n,0}=n$ for $n\ge1$, step 1.1 gives it for $n=0$, and step 2.1 identifies the maximal ideal with $(z_1,\dots,z_n)$. Transporting along the translation convention replaces each coordinate function $z_i$ by the coordinate difference $z_i-p_i$ and does not change dimensions, so $\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}=n$ and $\mathfrak m_{\mathbb C^n,p}=(z_1-p_1,\dots,z_n-p_n)$ for every $n\ge1$ and every $p\in\mathbb C^n$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, given] ∎
