## Statement

Assume AC and fix a finite-horizon linear-Gaussian model
([[def-linear-gaussian-state-space-model-with-independent-noises]]) with
$P_0,Q_j,R_j\succeq0$. Run the forward Kalman filter on data $y_{1:T}$,
with every innovation covariance $S_j$ positive definite. Also assume that
every one-step forecast covariance $P_{k+1|k}$ is positive definite for
$0\le k<T$. Let $(m_{k|T},P_{k|T})_{k=0}^T$ be the output of
[[alg-rauch-tung-striebel-smoother]] applied to that forward output. Then,
for every $0\le k\le T$,
$$N_{d_k}(m_{k|T}(y_{1:T}),P_{k|T})$$
is an everywhere-defined fixed-interval smoothing kernel for $X_k$ in the
sense of [[def-fixed-interval-gaussian-smoothing-law]]. In particular,
$P_{k|T}\succeq0$. Singular initial, process, measurement, filtered, and
smoothed covariances are allowed; only the innovation matrices solved by the
filter and the forecast matrices inverted by this smoother must be positive
definite. The formulas select a conditional-law version at every data value;
other versions may differ on a set of observation data of probability zero.
No pseudoinverse result for a singular forecast is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the finite linear-Gaussian model, positive-definite innovation covariances, and positive-definite forecast covariances as in the Statement.

[A1] AC is the Axiom of Choice assumed by the finite-dimensional Gaussian and conditional-law suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F1] The model uses affine state and observation equations and permits positive-semidefinite, possibly singular, Gaussian covariance matrices. [[def-linear-gaussian-state-space-model-with-independent-noises]].

[F2] The forward procedure returns the filtered and predicted means and covariances used in the statement, and its linear solves are defined when the innovation covariances are positive definite. [[alg-kalman-filter]].

[F3] For every observation history, $N(m_{k|k},P_{k|k})$ is an exact filtering-kernel version, with $P_{k|k}\succeq0$. [[thm-kalman-filter-is-exact-posterior]].

[F4] A fixed-interval smoothing kernel is a probability kernel whose composition with the complete observation vector is an RCD of $X_k$ given that vector. [[def-fixed-interval-gaussian-smoothing-law]].

[F5] The Gaussian backward kernel $Q_k$ is an RCD of $X_k$ given $(Y_{1:T},X_{k+1})$, with mean affine in $X_{k+1}$ and fixed positive-semidefinite conditional covariance $V_k$. [[lem-backward-gaussian-kernel-is-valid-under-full-smoothing-data]].

[F6] Integrating a nonnegative product-measurable function against a probability kernel gives a measurable function of the conditioning data. [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]].

[F7] Increasing limits commute with nonnegative integrals; this yields countable additivity for measures defined by integrating a probability kernel. [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]].

[F8] Product Borel sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles, and finite measures agreeing on a generating pi-system agree everywhere. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]], [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]].

[F9] Finite products of probability measures exist and satisfy the iterated integral formula on rectangles. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]].

[F10] A finite independent stack of Gaussian vectors is jointly Gaussian, including when its covariance blocks are singular. [[lem-finite-independent-gaussian-vectors-form-joint-multivariate-normal]].

[F11] Under AC, $N_d(m,P)$ exists for every finite-dimensional symmetric positive-semidefinite matrix $P$. [[def-multivariate-normal-law]].

[F12] Coordinate projections under a product probability measure are independent random elements. [[def-independent-random-elements]].

[F13] The RTS algorithm computes its gain by the increasing-row and decreasing-row recurrences in its Definition. [[alg-rauch-tung-striebel-smoother]].

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 At time $T$, the conditioning data for the forward filter are exactly $y_{1:T}$. By [F3], the terminal filter kernel $N(m_{T|T},P_{T|T})$ is an everywhere conditional-law version of $X_T$ given these data, with $P_{T|T}\succeq0$. It is therefore the base case for the fixed-interval smoothing kernels in [F4]. [A1, F1, F3, F4, given, base]

2.1 Suppose the Gaussian kernel $\psi_{k+1|T}(y,\cdot)$ is an RCD of $X_{k+1}$ given $Y_{1:T}=y$. Define $\psi_{k|T}(y,A)=\int Q_k(y_{1:k},x,A)\,\psi_{k+1|T}(y,dx)$ for each Borel state set $A$. This is a probability kernel by [F5]–[F6]. To verify its RCD identity, define $\nu(D)=\int\!\int 1_D(y,x)\,\psi_{k+1|T}(y,dx)\,\mathbb P_Y(dy)$ for Borel $D$ in the product of data and state spaces. Measurability follows from [F6]; for pairwise disjoint $D_n$, monotone convergence gives countable additivity, and $\nu$ has total mass one. Its rectangle values equal those of the actual joint law of $(Y_{1:T},X_{k+1})$ by the RCD identity for $\psi_{k+1|T}$. By [F8], the measures agree on every Borel set. Thus for any Borel data set $B$ and state set $A$, $\int_B\psi_{k|T}(y,A)\,\mathbb P_Y(dy)=\int_{B\times\mathbb R^{d_{k+1}}}Q_k(y_{1:k},x,A)\,\mathbb P_{(Y_{1:T},X_{k+1})}(dy,dx)=\mathbb P(X_k\in A,Y_{1:T}\in B)$, where the last equality is [F5] applied to the conditioning event $B\times\mathbb R^{d_{k+1}}$. Hence $\psi_{k|T}$ is the required RCD. [F5, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, ih, construct]

3.1 Write $P^-=P_{k+1|k}$ and $V_k=P_{k|k}-C_kP^-C_k^T$ as in [F5]. By [F13], row substitution gives $L_kU_k=F_{k+1}P_{k|k}$ and $L_k^TC_k^T=U_k$. Since $P^-=L_kL_k^T$, these equations yield $P^-C_k^T=F_{k+1}P_{k|k}$ and hence $C_k=P_{k|k}F_{k+1}^T(P^-)^{-1}$, exactly the gain in [F5]. On the product probability space carrying independent $U\sim N(0,V_k)$ and $W\sim N(m_{k+1|T},P_{k+1|T})$, the integral in step 2.1 is the law of $m_{k|k}+C_k(W-m_{k+1|k})+U$ by [F9]. The covariance matrices of $U$ and $W$ are positive semidefinite by [F5] and the induction hypothesis, so both Gaussian laws exist by [A1, F11]. The product coordinates are independent by [F12]; [F10] makes their stack jointly Gaussian, and its affine image is Gaussian by the projection characterization in [F11]. Independence makes the cross-covariance terms vanish, so its mean is $m_{k|k}+C_k(m_{k+1|T}-m_{k+1|k})$ and its covariance is $V_k+C_kP_{k+1|T}C_k^T=P_{k|k}+C_k(P_{k+1|T}-P^-)C_k^T$. This is exactly the smoother update in the statement and algorithm. The first covariance expression is positive semidefinite, so the updated covariance is positive semidefinite as well. [A1, F5, F9, F10, F11, F12, F13, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Starting from step 1.1, apply steps 2.1–3.1 for $k=T-1,T-2,\ldots,0$. The induction constructs an everywhere smoothing kernel at each time and identifies its mean and covariance with the algorithm output. When $T=0$ only the base case is needed. Positive definiteness is used only for the forecast inverses in [F5] and the forward innovation solves in [F2]; no singular forecast is inverted. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, discharge-induction] ∎
