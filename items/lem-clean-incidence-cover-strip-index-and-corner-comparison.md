## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the following clean incidence data, including the stated finite presentations. Let $u$ be an actual smooth fixed-end strip in $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$, $m\ge1$, with product torus boundary and product-integrable transverse ends, and let $P$ be its actual real Cauchy–Riemann linearization. Its compactification is a Whitney map from an oriented disk $B$ with two distinguished boundary points $p_-,p_+$ and complementary alpha and beta arcs. The end charts take the product tori to $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$ and the chosen almost complex structure to $i$. The almost complex structure agrees with the integrable symmetric-product structure near the diagonal and tames the descended product area form elsewhere.

Suppose this compactified map is presented by an embedded incidence surface $(f,g):S\to B\times\Sigma$, where $S$ is a compact oriented surface with boundary and $f$ is a proper degree-$m$ oriented branched cover of $B$, unramified in the topological boundary sense. Thus $u(z)$ is the unordered multiset $g(f^{-1}(z))$ with the cover multiplicities. There are $m$ distinct boundary corner points over each $p_\pm$ and no other corners; their image sectors under $g$ have angles $\pi/2$ or $3\pi/2$ in the corresponding positive generator surface charts. Every branch of $f$ is interior, has positive local power model, and is a regular point of $g$. Away from the corners, $g$ is an oriented local diffeomorphism except at finitely many positive interior power-model branch points; it has no boundary critical points. These branches are regular points of $f$. The cuts of $S$ along $g^{-1}(\boldsymbol\alpha\cup\boldsymbol\beta)$ are supplied as a finite cut-cover presentation with the adapted lifted cellulations and angle data of the finite sector-cover theorem. The image multiplicities of this presentation are exactly the domain $D$ of $u$. Also supply an adapted finite target triangulation for the degree-$m$ cover $f$, as in that theorem.

Require the following explicit rounding and field data. Round the $2m$ corners to give $S'$ and a degree-$m$ boundary-unramified cover $f':S'\to B'$ of a rounded and truncated base disk, with $g'$ equal to $g$ outside corner neighborhoods. In the generator charts its new boundary arcs round the corresponding image sectors with tangent angle change $\pi-\alpha$ for sector angle $\alpha$. The rounded incidence $(f',g')$ remains embedded, and its end sheets stay in the corresponding disjoint generator-factor neighborhoods. The induced symmetric-product map $u'$ equals $u$ outside the generator neighborhoods, and the change is supported in those product charts and preserves the alpha/beta boundary arcs. The rounding has a supplied finite cell refinement verifying $\chi(S')=\chi(S)$ and changes none of the interior branch defects. Supply a finite smooth triangle parametrization of $S'$ and a smooth field $v$ positive tangent on its boundary, with finitely many isolated interior zeros away from the triangle edges and all branch points. The images under $f'$ of all branch points of either projection and of all zeros of $v$ are distinct. No branch points or zeros occur on the new boundary arcs. These are hypotheses about actual maps and finite data, not an assertion that all Whitney classes admit them.

Then the actual real Fredholm index, before translation quotient, is
$$\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P=m-\chi(S)+2e(D).$$
It is the same on all integer fixed-end Hilbert Sobolev realizations and with smooth two-sided weights below the end gap. More precisely, if $L'$ is the boundary plane loop obtained by pushing the positive tangent lines of $\partial S'$ through $dg'$ into the symmetric product, and $c_{270}$ is the number of reflex corners before rounding, then
$$M(L')=R_f+2R_g+2\chi(S),\qquad M(L)-M(L')=-m+c_{270},$$
where $R_f,R_g$ are the sums of interior branch defects of the two projections, and $L$ is the actual boundary loop with the specified end caps. In particular this proves the cap/corner comparison under the listed data. It does not prove that an arbitrary Whitney class has such a presentation, the equality $\chi(S)=m-n_{\mathbf x}(D)-n_{\mathbf y}(D)+e(D)$, treatment of nodes or collapsed tubes, or any multipointed sphere shift.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and all the actual geometric, finite cover, sector, triangulation, rounding and field data in the Statement. In particular no existence theorem for such presentations is being assumed without its listed data.

[F1] For the actual product-integrable-end operator its real index equals the squared determinant winding with vertical caps $e^{i\pi\rho(t)/2}\mathbb R^m$. Finite planar excision adds local windings; a positive source-cover branch contributes $r-1$, target ramification of defect $r$ contributes $2r$, and an ordinary field zero of index $k$ contributes $2k$. The same actual index holds on higher integer Hilbert Sobolev and smooth weighted realizations ([[lem-relative-strip-maslov-determinant-excision-and-local-models]]).

[F2] An adapted finite cover of a disk has $\chi(S)=m-R_f$. The finite cut-cover sector identity gives $e(D)=\chi(S)-c_{90}/4+c_{270}/4+R_g$ when the only boundary exceptions are the given right/reflex corners and there is no other boundary ramification ([[lem-finite-sector-cover-euler-measure-and-boundary-ramification]]).

[F3] A supplied boundary-positive tangent field on a supplied finite smooth triangulated surface has zero index sum $\chi(S')$ ([[lem-finite-triangulated-surface-tangent-field-index-sum]]).

[F4] For a compatible $J_0$ and a tame $J$ the pointwise Cayley coordinates $A=-J_0J$, $C=(A-I)(A+I)^{-1}$ satisfy $CJ_0=-J_0C$, $\|C\|<1$, and $J_r=J_0(I+rC)(I-rC)^{-1}$ is a smooth tame interpolation. Product generator chart planes are Lagrangian for the descended product area form ([[lem-product-integrable-generator-neighborhoods-and-relative-tame-extension]], explicit pointwise interpolation and chart proofs).

## Proof

1.1 **Compare complex structures without assuming a new Fredholm theorem.** Put $J_0=\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$. Off the diagonal it is compatible with the descended product form. Use [F4] there to contract the given tame $J$ to $J_0$; on the protected neighborhood where $J=J_0$ the Cayley coefficient is zero, so the interpolation extends smoothly across it. Every product torus tangent plane remains totally real because it is Lagrangian and every interpolated structure is tame: $a,J_ra$ cannot both lie in a Lagrangian plane for $a\ne0$, since that would give $\omega(a,J_ra)=0$. The end caps are fixed products of real lines in the specified surface charts, hence also Lagrangian and totally real throughout. The squared determinant winding is constant in this interpolation. To justify a common continuous frame rather than assume it, take a finite complex-chart cover of the pullback complex bundle on the disk times the parameter interval and embed its fibers in a fixed $\mathbb C^N$ by a finite partition of unity. In local image columns $V$ the projection is $Q=V(V^*V)^{-1}V^*$, so it is continuous. Choose a star-shaped parametrization of this compact base and use uniform continuity to select an integer $N_0$ with $\|Q(x_{j+1})-Q(x_j)\|<1/2$ for the radial points $x_j=x_0+j(x-x_0)/N_0$, for every $x$. The restriction of $Q(x_{j+1})$ from the image of $Q(x_j)$ is injective, hence bijective onto its equal-dimensional image: its distance from the input vector is less than half that vector's norm. The finite product $Q(x_{N_0})\cdots Q(x_1)$ applied to a fixed basis of the image at $x_0$ therefore gives a continuous complex frame. No differentiability of the compactified map at the two punctures is needed. In that frame the squared determinant boundary phase is a continuous homotopy, so its winding is constant by finite argument lifts. Thus [F1] computes the actual index using the standard symmetric-product complex structure as well. No Fredholm assertion for an interpolated end operator is required. [F1, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Rounding compares boundary loops in actual bundles.** The modification from $u$ to $u'$ is confined to the stated generator product charts. Compare the maps there by straight interpolation in small convex coordinate neighborhoods, keeping their common outside values fixed. On the retained alpha/beta boundary arcs these coordinates are real/imaginary axes, so this interpolation preserves the required torus boundary. Use the fixed coordinate product-line caps during this comparison. Those planes are Lagrangian for the product area form at every point of the chart, and hence remain totally real for the structures used in step 1.1. The pullback complex bundle over this map homotopy has a continuous frame by the finite projection construction in step 1.1. Consequently its cap-completed squared determinant loops form a continuous homotopy and retain the winding of the original actual operator. This argument also permits shortening the far ends within the chart before making the corner comparison. For $u'$ the branch points are interior, so over its boundary $f'$ is unramified and all sheet values are distinct, by the embedded incidence hypothesis and the small supported rounding. The product of the images under $dg'$ of the nonzero positive tangent lines on the source boundary is therefore a well-defined totally real plane loop $L'$: in the off-diagonal product coordinates it is a product of nonzero real lines. Ordering sheets only changes a real determinant sign and disappears after squaring. [F1, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Extend the pushed field and compute its only defects.** Away from the finitely many distinct images of the branches and field zeros, push the lines spanned by $v$ through $dg'$ on the sheets of $f'$. Distinct sheets have distinct target points by embeddedness, and every pushed vector is nonzero there, so they define a continuous totally real plane field over the punctured disk. Apply finite excision from [F1]. Near a source-cover branch, $g'$ is regular and orientation preserving; its first derivative is a positive real matrix. Shrinking the source disk makes its higher-order remainder uniformly small compared with that derivative on its boundary. A homotopy of positive invertible real matrices to the identity, together with this small-remainder homotopy, preserves sheet separation and nonzero tangent vectors on that boundary. Hence the branch winding is the source-cover value $r-1$ in [F1]. Near a target branch the base projection is regular, and the positive local power model sends a nonzero source vector to a direction of winding equal to its ramification defect, so its squared contribution is twice that defect. At a zero of $v$ both projections are orientation-preserving local diffeomorphisms; they preserve its local vector winding, giving twice its index. A field nonzero near a branch can be deformed to a constant in a small oriented chart because its directions there lie in a single open semicircle; this introduces no extra defect. No other punctures are present by hypothesis. Thus $M(L')=R_f+2R_g+2\sum\operatorname{ind}(v)$. Apply [F3] and the supplied rounding cell equality to get $M(L')=R_f+2R_g+2\chi(S)$. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 **Compute each end-corner correction with the actual orientation.** Use the positive generator surface coordinates, alpha the real axis and beta the imaginary axis. In this local comparison each factor is a real line in an oriented surface tangent plane. One may interpolate its orientation-compatible complex structure to the constant coordinate $i$, keeping all these lines totally real. The finite-frame homotopy argument of step 1.1 shows the closed comparison winding is unchanged. Thus actual line-angle changes may be computed in these coordinates even if the global background complex structure is not constant there. The standard cap on the left is traversed from beta to alpha and has lifted line angle change $-\pi/2$; the right cap is traversed from alpha to beta and has change $+\pi/2$. By the explicit rounding hypothesis, the pushed source tangent on a rounded acute corner turns $\pi-\pi/2=+\pi/2$, and on a rounded reflex corner turns $\pi-3\pi/2=-\pi/2$. For clarity these are actual lifted changes, not just endpoint line angles modulo $\pi$; the stipulated rounding follows the boundary orientation and takes the short exterior turn. The difference of squared-phase windings is the difference of these line-angle changes divided by $\pi$. It is therefore $-1$ at an acute left corner, zero at a reflex left or acute right corner, and $+1$ at a reflex right corner. There are exactly $m$ left corners. Consequently the sum is $-m+c_{270}$, proving $M(L)-M(L')=-m+c_{270}$. This scalar computation is unchanged by changing the orientation-positive complex coordinate, since its derivative over the local disk acts through a homotopy of positive real matrices; winding of the closed comparison loop is preserved. The product generator factors add these comparisons, while all other boundary portions of $L$ and $L'$ coincide. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **Eliminate branch counts and retain the full remaining obligation.** Since $c_{90}+c_{270}=2m$, [F2] gives $R_f=m-\chi(S)$ and $R_g=e(D)-\chi(S)+m/2-c_{270}/2$. Substitute them in step 3.1 and add the correction of step 3.2: $M(L)=(m-\chi(S))+2(e(D)-\chi(S)+m/2-c_{270}/2)+2\chi(S)-m+c_{270}=m-\chi(S)+2e(D)$. By the complex-structure and rounding comparisons and [F1] this is precisely the actual real Fredholm index, with its stated higher Sobolev and weighted versions before translation. The argument has used each supplied incidence, cell, field and rounding hypothesis explicitly. In particular the further equality involving $n_{\mathbf x},n_{\mathbf y}$ and the existence of the clean presentation for arbitrary relevant classes are not conclusions of this lemma. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
