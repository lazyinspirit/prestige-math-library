## Example

Let $K:=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^{4}+t+1)$ and let $\alpha$ be the class of $t$, so
$\alpha^{4}=\alpha+1$. Then $K$ is a field of order $16$, and the four
conjugates of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb F_2$,

$$\alpha,\quad \alpha^{2},\quad \alpha^{4}=\alpha+1,\quad \alpha^{8}=\alpha^{2}+1,$$

are pairwise distinct, are exactly the roots of $t^{4}+t+1$ in $K$, and satisfy
$\alpha^{16}=\alpha$, so the Frobenius orbit closes at length four.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial $\pi:=t^{4}+t+1\in\mathbb F_2[t]$, the ring $K=\mathbb F_2[t]/(\pi)$, and the class $\alpha$ of $t$, so $\alpha^{4}=\alpha+1$ since $-1=1$ in characteristic two; squaring is additive there.

[L1] A polynomial of degree $2$ or $3$ over a field is irreducible if and only if it has no root in that field ([[thm-quadratic-and-cubic-irreducibility-test]]).

[L2] $f(a)=0$ if and only if $x-a$ divides $f$ ([[cor-factor-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]]); and over an integral domain $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$ for nonzero $f,g$ ([[thm-polynomial-degree-of-a-product-over-a-domain]], [[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]).

[L3] For a field $F$ and nonconstant $p\in F[x]$, $p$ is irreducible if and only if $F[x]/(p)$ is a field ([[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]).

[L4] A monic irreducible vanishing at $a$ is the minimal polynomial of $a$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]), and then $F(a)$ has power basis $1,a,\dots,a^{n-1}$ with $[F(a):F]=n$ its degree ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]], [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]).

[L5] An extension of finite fields of degree $n$ over $\mathbb F_q$ is Galois with cyclic Galois group generated by $\sigma_q(x)=x^{q}$, and has $q^{n}$ elements ([[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]], [[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]], [[lem-order-of-the-relative-frobenius]]).

[L6] A monic irreducible $\pi$ of degree $d$ over $\mathbb F_q$ with a root $\alpha$ has the $d$ distinct roots $\alpha,\alpha^{q},\dots,\alpha^{q^{d-1}}$ and $\pi=\prod_{i<d}(t-\alpha^{q^{i}})$ ([[thm-conjugates-over-a-finite-field-are-the-frobenius-powers]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $\pi$ has no root in $\mathbb F_2$, since $\pi(0)=1$ and $\pi(1)=1+1+1=1$; so by [L2] it has no factor of degree one. [L2, given]

1.2 The only monic irreducible quadratic in $\mathbb F_2[t]$ is $t^{2}+t+1$: the four monic quadratics are $t^{2}$, $t^{2}+1$, $t^{2}+t$ and $t^{2}+t+1$, and the first three have the root $0$, $1$ and $0$ respectively, so [L1] leaves only the last. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 $\pi$ is irreducible. A factorisation of $\pi$ into two nonconstant factors has degrees summing to four by [L2], so it is either $1+3$, excluded by step 1.1, or $2+2$; and every monic quadratic factor would have to be irreducible, hence equal to $t^{2}+t+1$ by step 1.2, giving $\pi=(t^{2}+t+1)^{2}=t^{4}+t^{2}+1$, which is not $\pi$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L2, given]

3.1 By [L3] the ring $K$ is a field; $\pi$ is monic irreducible with $\pi(\alpha)=0$, so $[K:\mathbb F_2]=4$ with power basis $1,\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{3}$ by [L4], and $|K|=2^{4}=16$ by [L5]. [step 2.1, L3, L4, L5]

4.1 Compute the conjugates in that basis: $\alpha^{4}=\alpha+1$ by hypothesis, and $\alpha^{8}=(\alpha^{4})^{2}=(\alpha+1)^{2}=\alpha^{2}+1$. So the four elements $\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{4},\alpha^{8}$ have coordinate lists $(0,1,0,0)$, $(0,0,1,0)$, $(1,1,0,0)$ and $(1,0,1,0)$, which are pairwise different, so the four elements are pairwise distinct. [step 3.1, given]

5.1 $\alpha^{16}=(\alpha^{8})^{2}=(\alpha^{2}+1)^{2}=\alpha^{4}+1=(\alpha+1)+1=\alpha$, so the orbit closes after four steps. [step 4.1, given]

6.1 By [L6] applied to $\pi$, of degree four, the elements $\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{4},\alpha^{8}$ are exactly the roots of $\pi$ and $\pi=(t-\alpha)(t-\alpha^{2})(t-\alpha^{4})(t-\alpha^{8})$ in $K[t]$; steps 4.1 and 5.1 verify the distinctness and the closing of the orbit directly. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, L6] ∎
