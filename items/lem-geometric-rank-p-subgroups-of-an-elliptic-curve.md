## Statement

Assume AC. Let E be an elliptic curve over an algebraically closed field k of characteristic p>0. Choose a formal parameter T at its identity, and write `[p]^*T=aT^p+O(T^(p+1))`. If a≠0, the finite group scheme E[p] has exactly two subgroup schemes of rank p: the connected kernel of relative Frobenius and one étale subgroup. If a=0, E[p] has exactly one subgroup scheme of rank p, the kernel of relative Frobenius. The dichotomy is independent of T. This is a classification of geometric subgroup schemes, not their moduli-scheme multiplicities.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, k, E, p and T as in the Statement.

[F1] Relative Frobenius F and Verschiebung V have degree p, are finite flat, and satisfy `VF=FV=[p]`. [[lem-relative-frobenius-and-verschiebung-of-an-elliptic-family]]

[F2] Multiplication by p on E is finite flat of degree p², so E[p] has rank p². [[lem-multiplication-by-n-on-abelian-scheme]]

[F3] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** the tangent of Verschiebung and the local length of p-torsion.

1.1 In formal parameters compatible with relative Frobenius, `F^*T'=T^p`. The identity `VF=[p]` gives `V^*T=aT'+O(T'^2)`, so the differential of V at the identity is multiplication by a. Changing formal parameters multiplies this coefficient by a unit, so the condition a=0 is intrinsic. The kernel of F has coordinate ring `k[[T]]/(T^p)` and is connected of rank p by [F1]. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If a≠0, V has nonzero differential at the identity and hence everywhere by translation; it is finite étale of degree p. Thus ker V is a constant group of p elements over algebraically closed k. The fppf surjection `F:E[p]→ker V` has connected kernel ker F, and F is radicial, so E[p] has exactly p geometric points. Their reduced union is a constant étale subgroup `C_et` of rank p. A rank-p subgroup H has connected identity component of rank either 1 or p, because the components are translates of equal length. If H is connected, its image in the étale group ker V is trivial, so H⊂ker F and equality follows from the equal ranks. If H is not connected, it is étale and consists of p distinct points, so H=C_et. These two subgroup schemes are distinct. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 If a=0, V has zero differential everywhere. Its finite degree is the prime p by [F1], so its function-field extension has inseparable degree p and separable degree one. Thus V, and consequently `[p]=VF`, is purely inseparable and has only the identity as a geometric kernel point. By [F2], E[p] is therefore a local finite scheme of length p². Its local ring is `k[[T]]/(T^(p²))`: the defining power series of the finite kernel has order p² and hence equals T^(p²) times a unit. Every ideal in this principal local ring is generated by a power of T; the only quotient of length p is by `(T^p)`. Thus every rank-p subgroup has the same underlying closed subscheme as ker F, and is ker F. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎

## Source and authoring record

The proof classifies subgroup schemes over an algebraically closed field. It does not determine their deformation spaces, multiplicities, or a correspondence identity.
