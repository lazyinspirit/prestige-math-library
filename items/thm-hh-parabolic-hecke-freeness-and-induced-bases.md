## Statement

Let $(S,m)$, $W$, $\ell$, $R$, and $H$ be as in [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]] and [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]], and let $J\subseteq S$. Let $H_J$ be the candidate submodule and let induction and restriction be as in [[def-hh-parabolic-hecke-subalgebra-and-induced-module]]. By [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]], $W_J$ is the Coxeter group on $J$ with the restricted matrix. The inherited parameters define its Hecke algebra over $R$ by the same presentation.

Use the coset convention of [[def-coset]]: the right cosets are $W_Jd$ and the left cosets are $dW_J$. Define
$$
D_R(J):=\{d\in W:\ell(sd)>\ell(d)\text{ for every }s\in J\},\qquad D_L(J):=\{d\in W:\ell(ds)>\ell(d)\text{ for every }s\in J\}.
$$
These are respectively the unique minimal representatives of $W_J\backslash W$ and $W/W_J$.

1. The candidate $H_J=\bigoplus_{u\in W_J}RT_u$ is the subalgebra of $H$ generated by $T_j$ for $j\in J$. The homomorphism from the restricted-system Hecke algebra that sends each generator $T_j$ to the same element of $H$ is injective, with image $H_J$.

2. There are direct-sum decompositions
$$
H=\bigoplus_{d\in D_R(J)}H_JT_d\quad\text{as a left }H_J\text{-module},\qquad H=\bigoplus_{d\in D_L(J)}T_dH_J\quad\text{as a right }H_J\text{-module}.
$$
Thus the displayed $T_d$ are free module bases on the indicated sides. These assertions hold for arbitrary, possibly infinite, $W$.

3. If $M$ is a left $H_J$-module that is free over $R$ with basis $B$, then $H\otimes_{H_J}M$ is free over $R$ with basis
$$
\{T_d\otimes b:d\in D_L(J),\ b\in B\}.
$$
For every left $H$-module $N$, the maps
$$
\operatorname{Hom}_H(H\otimes_{H_J}M,N)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{H_J}(M,\operatorname{Res}_J^W N),\qquad F\longmapsto[m\mapsto F(1\otimes m)],
$$
and
$$
g\longmapsto[h\otimes m\mapsto h\,g(m)]
$$
are inverse bijections. In particular, induction is left adjoint to restriction. If $W$ is finite, the two bases of $H$ over $H_J$ have cardinality $[W:W_J]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, its group $W$ and length $\ell$, the generic Hecke algebra $H$ over $R$, a subset $J\subseteq S$, a left $H_J$-module $M$, and a left $H$-module $N$.

[F1] The candidate parabolic module is $H_J=\bigoplus_{u\in W_J}RT_u$; induction is the balanced tensor product $H\otimes_{H_J}M$, with $(hk)\otimes m=h\otimes(km)$, and restriction uses the inclusion into $H$. ([[def-hh-parabolic-hecke-subalgebra-and-induced-module]])

[F2] For any finite Coxeter system and any commutative specialization of its universal parameter ring, the standard elements $T_w$ form a basis of the Hecke algebra. ([[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]])

[F3] The reduced product $T_w$ is well defined; the left and right length-multiplication rules hold for every $s\in S,w\in W$. ([[lem-hh-reduced-word-independence-and-length-multiplication]])

[F4] Every right coset $W_Jw$ has a unique minimal element $d$ and a unique factorization $w=ud$ with $u\in W_J$; then $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$. Every left coset $wW_J$ has a unique minimal element $d$ and a unique factorization $w=du$ with the same length additivity. ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]])

[F5] Under the library's coset convention, $gH$ is a left coset and $Hg$ is a right coset. ([[def-coset]])

[F6] The restricted system on $W_J$ has the same braid labels on $J$, and a Hecke algebra is the presented algebra with the quadratic and finite braid relations. Parameters constant on odd-edge components of $S$ remain constant along odd-edge paths in $J$, so they specialize the restricted-system parameter ring to $R$. ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]], [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data and hypotheses of the Statement.

1.1 Let $K_J:=\bigoplus_{u\in W_J}RT_u$. If $u\in W_J$ and $j\in J$, the left and right length-multiplication rules in [F3] express $T_jT_u$ and $T_uT_j$ as linear combinations of $T_{ju},T_u$ and $T_{uj},T_u$ respectively; since $ju,uj\in W_J$, both products lie in $K_J$. Each $T_u$ is a product of the generators indexed by $J$, so $K_J$ is contained in the $R$-subalgebra they generate; conversely it is closed under multiplication by every such generator and contains $1$, so every word in those generators lies in $K_J$. Hence $K_J$ is exactly that generated subalgebra. [F1, F3, algebra]

1.2 For each $w\in W$, [F4] gives a unique factorization $w=ud$ with $u\in W_J$ and $d\in D_R(J)$ and $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$. Thus $W_Jd$ is the right coset represented by $d$ under [F5]. Choose reduced expressions for $u$ and $d$; their concatenation has length $\ell(u)+\ell(d)=\ell(ud)$ and so is reduced, giving $T_w=T_uT_d$ by [F3]. Thus the elements of $H_JT_d$ span $H$. If a finite sum $\sum_d h_dT_d=0$ with $h_d\in H_J$ is given, expand each $h_d$ as a finite sum $\sum_{u\in W_J}r_{u,d}T_u$ using [F1]. The resulting sum is $\sum_{u,d}r_{u,d}T_{ud}=0$. Unique factorization makes the elements $ud$ distinct for distinct pairs $(u,d)$; linear independence of the standard basis [F2] forces every $r_{u,d}=0$. This proves the left-module direct sum and its basis. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]

1.3 For each $w\in W$, the left-coset part of [F4] gives a unique factorization $w=du$ with $d\in D_L(J)$, $u\in W_J$, and $\ell(w)=\ell(d)+\ell(u)$. By [F5], $dW_J$ is the left coset represented by $d$. Reduced expressions for $d$ followed by $u$ concatenate to a reduced expression of $du$, so $T_w=T_dT_u$ by [F3]. Hence the elements of $T_dH_J$ span $H$. If $\sum_dT_dh_d=0$, expand $h_d=\sum_{u\in W_J}r_{d,u}T_u$; unique left-coset factorization makes all $du$ distinct, and [F2] then forces every coefficient to vanish. This proves the right-module direct sum and its basis. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]

2.1 By [F4], $(W_J,J)$ is a Coxeter system. Any odd-edge path in $J$ is also an odd-edge path in $S$, so the inherited parameters agree along every odd component of $J$ and give a ring map from the restricted system's universal parameter ring to $R$. Thus [F6] supplies a homomorphism from the restricted Hecke presentation to $H$ sending each $T_j$ to $T_j$. Apply the standard-basis theorem [F2] to the restricted system and this parameter map: its standard elements $T_u$ form an $R$-basis. Their images are the correspondingly named elements of the ambient $H$-basis, so the map is injective; its image is $K_J$ by step 1.1 and because every $u\in W_J$ has a word in $J$. Thus the candidate in [F1] is an embedded subalgebra, proving part 1. [F1, F2, F4, F6, step 1.1]

2.2 By step 1.3, every $h\in H$ has a unique finite expansion $h=\sum_{d\in D_L(J)}T_dk_d$ with $k_d\in H_J$. Define $\Phi:\bigoplus_{d\in D_L(J)}M\to H\otimes_{H_J}M$ by $(m_d)_d\mapsto\sum_dT_d\otimes m_d$, and define $\Psi(h\otimes m):=(k_dm)_d$. The latter respects the balancing relation: the coefficients of $hk$ are $k_dk$, so $\Psi(hk\otimes m)=(k_d(km))_d=\Psi(h\otimes km)$. Uniqueness of the expansion shows $\Psi\Phi$ and $\Phi\Psi$ are identities. Thus $H\otimes_{H_J}M\cong\bigoplus_{d\in D_L(J)}M$; if $B$ is an $R$-basis of $M$, the displayed tensors form an $R$-basis. [F1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 For an $H_J$-linear map $g:M\to\operatorname{Res}_J^W N$, the formula $\widetilde g(h\otimes m)=h g(m)$ is balanced because $hk g(m)=h g(km)$, and it is $H$-linear. Conversely, if $F:H\otimes_{H_J}M\to N$ is $H$-linear, $m\mapsto F(1\otimes m)$ is $H_J$-linear since $1\otimes km=k\otimes m$. The two constructions are inverse: evaluation at $1\otimes m$ recovers $g(m)$, and $H$-linearity gives $F(h\otimes m)=hF(1\otimes m)$. This proves the adjunction. [F1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The coset decompositions, their independence arguments, and the adjunction used no finiteness of $W$ and no choice of coset representatives: [F4] supplies each unique minimal representative. If $W$ is finite, each coset has $|W_J|$ elements, the cosets partition $W$, and the number of representatives is $|W|/|W_J|=[W:W_J]$. All coefficient arguments use standard bases and remain valid after any commutative base change of $R$ because the standard basis in [F2] does. [F2, F4, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 3.1] ∎
