## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $p$ be a prime, let $R=\mathbb Z_p[\![T]\!]$, and let $M$ be a finitely generated torsion $R$-module. Choose a finite presentation $R^a\to R^b\to M\to0$ and write $F=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(M)$ for the ideal generated by its $b\times b$ minors, using the empty-minor convention $F=R$ when $b=0$. The proof shows the stated conclusions are independent of the chosen finite presentation. Define its divisorial hull by
$$F^{\mathrm{div}}=\bigcap_{\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)=1} F R_{\mathfrak q}\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(R).$$
Then $\operatorname{char}_R(M)=F^{\mathrm{div}}$. For every nonzero $g\in R$, if $g\in F$ then $(g)\subseteq\operatorname{char}_R(M)$, and if $F\subseteq(g)$ then $\operatorname{char}_R(M)\subseteq(g)$. Consequently $F=(g)$ implies $\operatorname{char}_R(M)=(g)$. No arithmetic Fitting identity or main-conjecture equality is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a prime p, R and M as in the Statement.

[F1] R is a Noetherian normal domain and the characteristic ideal is the finite product of height-one primes with exponents the localized module lengths. [[def-iwasawa-characteristic-ideal-over-zp-power-series]]

[F3] A height-one localization of a Noetherian normal domain is a DVR. [[thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr]]

[F4] Localization and polynomial extension preserve regularity, completion preserves regular local rings, and a regular local ring is a UFD, under AC. [[thm-localisation-and-polynomial-extension-of-regular-rings]] [[thm-completion-preserves-regular-local-rings]] [[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]]

[A1] AC is assumed for the characteristic-ideal and regular-ring suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Localize a presentation at each height-one prime and diagonalize it over the resulting DVR.

1.1 Since R is Noetherian and M is finitely generated, choose a finite presentation $R^a\to R^b\to M\to0$. Fix a height-one prime q. Its localization is a DVR D by [F1] and [F3], and the localized module is finitely generated and torsion over D, hence has finite length. Localization of the finite presentation shows that $F D$ is generated by the maximal minors of the localized presentation matrix. [F1, F3, A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Elementary row and column operations over D reduce this matrix to diagonal entries $\pi^{n_1},\ldots,\pi^{n_b}$ and zero remaining columns, where $\pi$ is a uniformizer and $n_i\ge0$. The matrix has full row rank because its cokernel is torsion. Invertible operations preserve the ideal of maximal minors. The cokernel is $\bigoplus_iD/(\pi^{n_i})$ and has length $\sum_i n_i$; the maximal-minor ideal is $(\pi^{\sum_i n_i})$. The case b=0 gives D and length zero. Thus $F D=(\pi^{\ell_D(M_q)})$ at every height-one q. [step 1.1, algebra, cases]

3.1 The coefficient ring $\mathbb Z_p$ is a DVR, hence regular local. Its polynomial ring localized at $(p,T)$ is regular local by [F4], and its completion is R, so [F4] makes R a UFD. In a UFD every height-one prime is generated by an irreducible: a nonzero element of the prime has an irreducible factor in it, and the principal prime it generates must equal the height-one prime. For a nonzero element x of R, factor x into irreducibles; the intersection of the local ideals $xR_q$ over all height-one q is precisely $(x)$, by comparing the finitely many exponents in the factorization. Likewise the finite product in [F1] is exactly the intersection of its height-one localizations. Combining this with step 2.1 gives $\operatorname{char}_R(M)=\bigcap_q F R_q=F^{\mathrm{div}}$. [F1, F4, A1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 If $g\in F$, then $(g)\subseteq F\subseteq F^{\mathrm{div}}$. If $F\subseteq(g)$, localizing and intersecting gives $F^{\mathrm{div}}\subseteq\bigcap_q(g)R_q=(g)$ by step 3.1. If $F=(g)$ both inclusions give equality. This uses no arithmetic Fitting identity. [step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
