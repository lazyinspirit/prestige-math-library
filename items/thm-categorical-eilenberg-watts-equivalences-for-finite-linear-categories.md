## Statement

Let $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$ be finite $k$-linear abelian categories with supplied small module models $R\text{-}\mathrm{mod},S\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ ([[def-abelian-category]]), and identify $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B$ with the finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodules through [[lem-opposite-deligne-product-identifies-with-finite-bimodules]]. For a finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodule $M$, with dual $M^*$ the $(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$-bimodule $\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k)$ ([[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]], [[def-bimodule]]), define the Eilenberg–Watts functors
$$\Phi^{l}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(M^{*},-):\mathcal A\to\mathcal B,\qquad \Phi^{r}(M)=M\otimes_{\mathcal A}-:\mathcal A\to\mathcal B.$$
Then $\Phi^{l}(M)$ is $k$-linear and left exact and $\Phi^{r}(M)$ is $k$-linear and right exact ([[def-left-exact-and-right-exact-functor]], [[def-k-linear-category-and-k-linear-functor]]), and the induced functors
$$\Phi^{l}:\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B\longrightarrow\operatorname{Lex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B),\qquad \Phi^{r}:\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B\longrightarrow\operatorname{Rex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$$
are equivalences of categories; equivalently the triangle $\operatorname{Lex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)\simeq\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B\simeq\operatorname{Rex}(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ of categorical Eilenberg–Watts holds, with quasi-inverses $\Psi^{l},\Psi^{r}$ given by the (co)end formulas constructed on this page. On an external object $\bar a\boxtimes b$ these compute, naturally in an object $X$ of $\mathcal A$, as
$$\Phi^{l}(\bar a\boxtimes b)(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(a,X)\otimes_kb,\qquad \Phi^{r}(\bar a\boxtimes b)(X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(X,a)^{*}\otimes_kb,$$
both identifications respecting the left $\mathcal B$-actions. Algebraic subscripts and bimodules refer to those model algebras and the displayed functors are transported along the supplied equivalences. The Deligne product uses the AC-dependent existence theorem; no additional choice is needed for the tensor and Hom equivalences.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite $k$-linear abelian categories $\mathcal A,\mathcal B$, the identification of $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B$ with finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodules, and for a finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodule $M$ its dual $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(M,k)$, an $(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$-bimodule.

[F1] For finite-dimensional $k$-algebras $A,B$ the assignment $M\mapsto T_M=M\otimes_A-$ is an equivalence of categories from finite-dimensional $(B,A)$-bimodules with bimodule maps to $k$-linear right exact functors $A\text{-}\mathrm{mod}\to B\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ with all natural transformations; each $T_M$ is well defined, $k$-linear and right exact, and every such functor is naturally isomorphic to $T_{F(A)}$ ([[thm-finite-eilenberg-watts-for-right-exact-linear-functors]]).

[F2] For finite-dimensional $k$-algebras $A,B$ the assignment $M\mapsto\operatorname{Hom}_A(M^{*},-)$ is an equivalence of categories from finite-dimensional $(B,A)$-bimodules with bimodule maps to $k$-linear left exact functors $A\text{-}\mathrm{mod}\to B\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ with all natural transformations, with quasi-inverse $F\mapsto F(A^{*})$; the left $B$-action on the Hom is $(b\varphi)(u)=\varphi(u\cdot b)$ ([[thm-finite-left-exact-functors-are-hom-functors-with-dual-bimodule-kernels]]).

[F3] There is an equivalence of $k$-linear categories $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B\simeq(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)\text{-}\mathrm{bimod}$ carrying an external object $\bar a\boxtimes b$ to $b\otimes_ka^*$, and the construction is well defined up to an equivalence respecting the universal bifunctors ([[lem-opposite-deligne-product-identifies-with-finite-bimodules]]).

[F4] The chosen small module models and equivalences $\mathcal A\simeq R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ and $\mathcal B\simeq S\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ are part of the statement data. The module-model theorem supplies an equivalence from its fully faithful and essentially surjective functor when splitting data are supplied ([[thm-finite-abelian-categories-are-finite-dimensional-module-categories]]).

[F5] The $k$-dual $X^*=\operatorname{Hom}_k(X,k)$ of a finite-dimensional module is an exact contravariant equivalence and the evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_X:X\to X^{**}$ is a natural isomorphism ([[lem-finite-module-duality-is-exact-with-commuting-bimodule-actions]], [[def-algebraic-dual-and-linear-functional]]).

[F6] The tensor product is functorial and universal for balanced maps, so an isomorphism between two k-bilinear constructions follows from a bijection of the balanced maps they classify ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]], [[prop-functoriality-of-module-tensor-products]]); the two actions of an $(S,R)$-bimodule commute ([[def-bimodule]]).

[F7] An end is a universal dinatural family of maps into the diagonal values of a bifunctor, and a coend is a universal dinatural family out of them; their existence is not automatic ([[def-end-and-coend]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Use the supplied finite-dimensional unital $k$-algebras $R,S$ and $k$-linear equivalences of [F4] $\mathcal A\simeq R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$, $\mathcal B\simeq S\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$, under which finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodules correspond to finite $(S,R)$-bimodules; the two classification theorems [F1] and [F2] are stated for such algebras and are equivalences of categories including all natural transformations, hence after transport they identify finite $(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)$-bimodules with $\operatorname{Rex}_k(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ and with $\operatorname{Lex}_k(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ ([[def-equivalence-and-adjoint-equivalence-of-categories]], [[def-natural-transformation]], [[def-opposite-ring]]). Composing with the equivalence $\mathcal A^{\mathrm{op}}\boxtimes\mathcal B\simeq(\mathcal B,\mathcal A)\text{-}\mathrm{bimod}$ of [F3] exhibits $\Phi^{r}$ and $\Phi^{l}$ as equivalences onto $\operatorname{Rex}_k(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$ and $\operatorname{Lex}_k(\mathcal A,\mathcal B)$, with quasi-inverses the transported inverse constructions of [F1] and [F2]. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 Work in the supplied small module models, with the scalar and size conventions of [F1] and [F2]. For a $k$-linear functor $G:R\text{-}\mathrm{mod}\to S\text{-}\mathrm{mod}$ put $H_G(a,b)=G(b)\otimes_ka^*$, an $(S,R)$-bimodule with right action $(v\otimes\lambda)r=v\otimes(\lambda\cdot r)$. This models $\bar a\boxtimes G(b)$ by [F3]. Give $G(R)$ the right action $vr=G(r_r)(v)$, where $r_r(t)=tr$, and give $G(R^*)$ the right action $vr=G(t_r)(v)$, where $t_r(\lambda)=\lambda\cdot r$. Here $(r\lambda)(t)=\lambda(tr)$ and $(\lambda\cdot r)(t)=\lambda(rt)$ on $R^*$. These maps are left $R$-linear, and functoriality and $k$-linearity give commuting, scalar-compatible bimodule actions. The finite-dual-basis map $v\otimes\lambda\mapsto[x\mapsto\lambda(x)v]$ identifies $H_G(a,a)$ with $\operatorname{Hom}_k(a,G(a))$; it is independent of the basis, with right action $(pr)(x)=p(rx)$. We construct the universal families of [F7] in these finite bimodules. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7]

2.1 Under the correspondence of step 1.1 the functor $\Phi^{r}(M)=M\otimes_{\mathcal A}-$ is well defined, $k$-linear and right exact, and $\Phi^{l}(M)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal A}(M^{*},-)$ is well defined, $k$-linear and left exact, because these are the transported statements of [F1](i) and [F2] for the model algebras ([[def-k-linear-category-and-k-linear-functor]], [[def-left-exact-and-right-exact-functor]], [[def-bimodule]]). [step 1.1, F1, F2]

2.2 For $x\in a$ let $\ell_x:R\to a$ be $\ell_x(t)=tx$. Define $i_a:G(R)\to H_G(a,a)$ by $i_a(v)(x)=G(\ell_x)(v)$ in the preceding Hom identification. It is $S$-linear and right $R$-linear because $\ell_{rx}=\ell_xr_r$. For an $R$-linear $u:a\to b$, $u\ell_x=\ell_{u(x)}$, so $G(u)i_a(v)(x)=i_b(v)(u(x))$; this is dinaturality. For any dinatural bimodule maps $j_a:Z\to H_G(a,a)$, set $h(z)=j_R(z)(1)$. Dinaturality at $\ell_x$ gives $j_a(z)(x)=G(\ell_x)(h(z))$, so $j_a=i_ah$. The map $h$ is $S$-linear, and it is right $R$-linear since $h(zr)=j_R(zr)(1)=j_R(z)(r)=G(r_r)(h(z))$. Evaluation at $1$ gives $i_R(v)(1)=v$, proving uniqueness of $h$. Thus $G(R)$ with $i_a$ is $\int_aG(a)\otimes_ka^*$ in the finite bimodule category. [step 1.2, F7]

2.3 For $\lambda\in a^*$ define the left $R$-linear map $t_\lambda:a\to R^*$ by $t_\lambda(x)(r)=\lambda(rx)$, and put $q_a(v\otimes\lambda)=G(t_\lambda)(v)$. Bilinearity gives a map $H_G(a,a)\to G(R^*)$; it is $S$-linear and right $R$-linear because $t_{\lambda\cdot r}=t_rt_\lambda$. For $u:a\to b$ and $\mu\in b^*$, $t_\mu u=t_{\mu u}$, so $q_b(G(u)v\otimes\mu)=q_a(v\otimes\mu u)$, proving dinaturality. Given any dinatural bimodule maps $c_a:H_G(a,a)\to Z$, let $\varepsilon\in(R^*)^*$ be evaluation at $1$, and define $h(v)=c_{R^*}(v\otimes\varepsilon)$. Since $\varepsilon t_\lambda=\lambda$, dinaturality at $t_\lambda$ yields $hq_a=c_a$. This $h$ is $S$-linear; moreover $\varepsilon t_r=\varepsilon\cdot r$ (both evaluate $\lambda$ at $r$), so dinaturality at $t_r$ and right $R$-linearity of $c_{R^*}$ give $h(vr)=c_{R^*}(G(t_r)v\otimes\varepsilon)=c_{R^*}(v\otimes\varepsilon\cdot r)=h(v)r$. Finally $t_\varepsilon:R^*\to R^*$ is the identity, hence $q_{R^*}(v\otimes\varepsilon)=v$, proving uniqueness of $h$. Thus $G(R^*)$ with $q_a$ is $\int^aG(a)\otimes_ka^*$ in the finite bimodule category. [step 1.2, F6, F7]

3.1 In the module models, $M=b\otimes_ka^*$ has dual $M^*\cong b^*\otimes_ka$, by the evaluation pairing and finite dual bases. Put $Q=a^*\otimes_RX$. For $f\in\operatorname{Hom}_R(X,a)$ define $J(f)\in Q^*$ by $J(f)(\lambda\otimes x)=\lambda(f(x))$. Balancedness follows from $R$-linearity of $f$. Conversely, for $g\in Q^*$ set $f_g(x)=\operatorname{ev}_a^{-1}(\lambda\mapsto g(\lambda\otimes x))$. Then $f_g(rx)=rf_g(x)$ because $g(\lambda\otimes rx)=g((\lambda\cdot r)\otimes x)$ and $(\lambda\cdot r)(y)=\lambda(ry)$. The two constructions are inverse, so $J:\operatorname{Hom}_R(X,a)\to Q^*$ is a natural isomorphism. Dualizing and using $Q\cong Q^{**}$ gives $Q\cong\operatorname{Hom}_R(X,a)^*$. The tensor universal property gives $(b\otimes_ka^*)\otimes_RX\cong b\otimes_kQ$. Likewise currying gives $\operatorname{Hom}_R(b^*\otimes_ka,X)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(b^*,\operatorname{Hom}_R(a,X))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_R(a,X)\otimes_kb$; the last map and its inverse are finite-dual-basis evaluation maps. Thus the two external formulas hold naturally in $a,b,X$, with the $S$-action on the factor $b$ corresponding on Hom to precomposition by the right $S$-action of $b^*$. [step 2.1, F2, F3, F5, F6]

4.1 Transporting these universal families through [F3] and the supplied module equivalences gives $\Psi^l(F)=\int^{a\in\mathcal A}\bar a\boxtimes F(a)$ for left exact $F$ and $\Psi^r(G)=\int_{a\in\mathcal A}\bar a\boxtimes G(a)$ for right exact $G$. Their model kernels are respectively $F(R^*)$ and $G(R)$ by steps 2.2–2.3. For a natural transformation $\eta$, naturality at $\ell_x$ and $t_\lambda$ identifies the induced maps of these universal objects with $\eta_R$ and $\eta_{R^*}$, respectively. Hence these are precisely the quasi-inverse functors of [F1] and [F2], including their natural comparison isomorphisms in both composites. Together with steps 1.1 and 3.1 this proves the claimed triangle and external formulas. No commutativity or algebraic closure is needed. The Deligne-product data carry the stated Axiom of Choice assumption; the displayed maps are canonical and use no additional choice or infinite-dimensional (co)limits. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, F1, F2, F3] ∎
