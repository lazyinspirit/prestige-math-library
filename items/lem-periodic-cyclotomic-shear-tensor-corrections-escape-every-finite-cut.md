## Statement

Use the actual periodic modules, basis $b_{j,a}$, finite cuts $M^{(J)}$, denominators $p=q+q^{-1}$ and $h=q^2+q^{-2}=p^2-2$ of [[lem-two-cyclotomic-shears-give-a-periodic-integrable-based-sl-two-chain]]. On $M^{(J)}\otimes M^{(J)}$ use the rank-one tensor bar $\Psi=\Theta(\bar{\phantom{x}}\otimes\bar{\phantom{x}})$, with

$$\Theta=\sum_{r\ge0}a_r E^{(r)}\otimes F^{(r)},\qquad a_r=q^{-r}\prod_{s=1}^r(q^{2s}-1).$$

There is a unique normalized bar-fixed lift $G^{(J)}$ of the raw seed $b_{0,0}\otimes b_{0,2}$: its seed coefficient is $1$ and every other coefficient belongs to $q\mathbb Z[q]$, with corrections strictly increasing the first factor weight. Let $f_j^{(J)}$ be its coefficient at $b_{j,0}\otimes b_{j,n_j}$, for $j\le J$. These coefficients agree as the cut increases. In the Laurent polynomial quotient over $\mathbb Q$ they satisfy

$$f_{2k+1}^{(J)}\equiv(-1)^k(2k+1)!(q^3-q)\pmod{q^4+1},\qquad f_{2k}^{(J)}\equiv(-1)^k2(2k+1)!\pmod{q^2+1}\quad(k\ge1).$$

In particular $f_J^{(J)}\ne0$ for every $J\ge1$. Thus the normalized corrections escape every finite constituent cut; this is a claim about the corrected lifts, not merely about repeated raw-bar closure. All constructions and proofs are choice free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual finite periodic chain and its integral based lattice in [[lem-two-cyclotomic-shears-give-a-periodic-integrable-based-sl-two-chain]], rank-one divided-power actions in [[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]] and [[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]], the exact rank-one coefficient formula in [[thm-the-weightwise-quasi-r-intertwiner-exists-and-is-unique]], and tensor-bar involutivity and compatibility in [[lem-quasi-r-intertwining-determines-the-inverse-and-coproduct-factorizations]].

[F1] The chain has $n_{2k}=8k+2$, $n_{2k+1}=8k+4$. Its coordinate map is $C=1+S$, $C^{-1}=1-S$, and link $j$ maps position $t\ell_j$ to $t\ell_j+\ell_j-1$ with coefficient $(-1)^t/p_j$, where $(\ell_j,p_j)=(2,p)$ for even $j$ and $(4,h)$ for odd $j$. The integral actions and the zero chart are those proved by [[lem-two-cyclotomic-shears-give-a-periodic-integrable-based-sl-two-chain]].

[F2] In a divided string of length $m$, $E^{(r)}u_a=\binom{m-a+r}{r}_q u_{a-r}$ and $F^{(r)}u_a=\binom{a+r}{r}_q u_{a+r}$, with vectors outside the string zero. The tensor bar is involutive and compatible with divided $E,F$; its rank-one coefficients are the displayed $a_r$ ([[thm-the-weightwise-quasi-r-intertwiner-exists-and-is-unique]], [[thm-quantized-sl-two-string-formulas]], [[lem-divided-power-and-toral-quantum-binomial-reordering-identities]], [[lem-quasi-r-intertwining-determines-the-inverse-and-coproduct-factorizations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Finite normalized correction.** In raw coordinates the bar is $(C^{-1}\otimes C^{-1})\Theta(C\otimes C)$ followed by scalar bar. Its coefficients are Laurent integral: each term $a_rE^{(r)}\otimes F^{(r)}$ acts integrally by [F1], and $a_r\in\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$. Its diagonal is $1$ and every other term strictly raises the first weight, since $C$ preserves weights and every $r>0$ term does so. In a finite cut there are finitely many raw weight-zero vectors. Successively remove the lowest remaining first-weight error: involutivity makes its scalar coefficient $x$ satisfy $x+\bar x=0$, so its positive Laurent part is the unique coefficient in $q\mathbb Z[q]$ whose difference from its bar is $x$. This finite induction gives existence and uniqueness of $G^{(J)}$. All its nonseed weights have first weight at least $2$. Every map in the raw bar raises constituent indices; projecting away indices exceeding $J$ commutes with the bar computed in a larger cut. Uniqueness therefore proves agreement of the projected normalized lifts and of $f_j^{(J)}$. [F1, F2, algebra, construct]

2.1 **A uniformly bounded set of positions.** Write an ordinary weight-zero tensor vector as $u_{j,a}\otimes u_{l,n_l-d}$; then $n_j-n_l=2(a-d)$. The seed has $a=d=0$. Both $C$ and $C^{-1}$ preserve $a\le3$ and $d\le3$: on an even link the only eligible positions or endpoint distances in this range are $0,2$, which become $1,3$; on an odd link only $0$ is eligible and it becomes $3$. In endpoint distances the same assertion follows from $n_j\equiv0\pmod{\ell_j}$ and $n_{j+1}=n_j+2(\ell_j-1)$. Every term of $\Theta$ lowers both $a$ and $d$. Thus the raw bar and the correction induction preserve these bounds. Nonadjacent string lengths differ by at least $8$, whereas $|2(a-d)|\le6$, so only equal or adjacent constituent indices occur. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Ordinary normalized invariant coordinates.** In a constituent pair of lengths $m,n$, put $D_{a,d}=\Delta(F^{(a)})(u_0\otimes u_{n-d})$ if $a\ge d$, and $D_{a,d}=\Delta(E^{(d)})(u_a\otimes u_n)$ if $a<d$. Their leading ordinary vector is $u_a\otimes u_{n-d}$. Their term with first position $a-t$ has respectively coefficient $q^{t(m-a+t)}\binom{n-d+t}{t}_q$ or $q^{t(n-d+t)}\binom{m-a+t}{t}_q$, and is omitted if an endpoint is exceeded. These vectors are bar fixed by [F2], because their starting tensors have highest first factor or lowest second factor. The lowest exponent of either nonleading coefficient is respectively $t(a-d+t)$ or $t(d-a+t)$, strictly positive. Hence their transition and inverse are unitriangular matrices over $\mathbb Z[q]$. Expand the ordinary-coordinate vector $(C\otimes C)G^{(J)}$ in these $D$ vectors, with coefficients $z_{j,l,a}$. They are bar fixed and regular at $q=0$; except for the original seed coefficient their residues are zero. In pair $(j,l)$ their denominator divides $p_{j-1}p_{l-1}$, with missing index $-1$ interpreted as denominator $1$, because these are the only incoming shear denominators and the inverse $D$ matrix is Laurent integral. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 **Parity and the bounded rational states.** Put $\delta_j=\deg_q p_j\pmod2$ and define $\tau_0=0$, $\tau_{j+1}=\tau_j+j+\delta_j\pmod2$. Assign tuple $(j,a,l,n_l-d)$ the parity $\epsilon=a(1+j+l)+\tau_j+\tau_l+jl\pmod2$. Its coefficient in every vector under consideration contains only powers of $q$ of that parity. Indeed $n_j/2\equiv j+1\pmod2$; a first shear has even source position and a second shear has even source position $n_l-d$, hence $a\equiv j+l\pmod2$ in the latter case. In both cases the change of $\epsilon$ is $\delta_j$ or $\delta_l$, exactly the parity of the shear coefficient. A term of $\Theta$ with index $r$ has coefficient parity $r(1+j+l)$, exactly the change from $a$ to $a-r$. The seed has parity zero, positive Laurent extraction preserves parity, and the $D$ coefficients have parity $t(1+j+l)$. In particular diagonal $z_{j,j,a}$ has parity $j+a$. A bar-fixed Laurent polynomial is a polynomial in $p=q+q^{-1}$, by pairing its opposite exponents and the recurrence $q^s+q^{-s}=p(q^{s-1}+q^{1-s})-(q^{s-2}+q^{2-s})$. Multiplying $z$ by its bar-fixed denominator therefore gives a polynomial in $p$; zero residue at both ends bounds its degree strictly below that of the denominator. Thus on an odd diagonal $z_3=c_3/p^2,z_2=A_2/p,z_1=c_1/p^2,z_0=A_0/p$; on an even diagonal $z_3=up/h^2+vp/h$, and initially $z_2,z_0$ have the form $A/h+B/h^2$, while $z_1$ has the form $Ap/h+Bp/h^2$. The constants may be treated over $\mathbb Q$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **The scalar calculations used below.** On a diagonal of length $m$ write $H_{a,t}=q^{t(m-a+t)}\binom{m-a+t}{t}_q$; this is the coefficient from $D_a$ to first position $a-t$. All following evaluations follow by substituting in $[s]=(q^s-q^{-s})/(q-q^{-1})$ and $\binom Nt_q=\prod_{v=0}^{t-1}[N-v]/[t-v]$. Modulo $h$, one has $p^2=2$, $q^4=-1$, $[8s]/h=-2sp$. For $m=8k+10$ these give $H_{3,1}/h=-2(k+1)p$, $H_{2,2}=-1$, $H_{1,1}=q^2p$, $H_{3,3}/h=2(k+1)q^2p$, and $H_{3,2}$ is divisible by $h$. For $m=8k+4$, all endpoint coefficients $H_{a,a}$ with $1\le a\le3$ vanish modulo $h$. Modulo $p$, one has $q^2=-1$; for $m=8k+4$ the evaluations are $H_{3,1}/p=-(4k+1)$, $H_{3,2}=4k+1$, $H_{1,1}/p=-(4k+2)$, $H_{2,2}=4k+2$, and $H_{3,3}/p=-(4k+2)(4k+1)$. For $m=8k+2$ one has $H_{2,2}=4k+1$. Quotients here are taken only when the numerator is divisible by the indicated denominator, and evaluations are in $\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]/(q^4+1)$ or $\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]/(q^2+1)$ understood as the corresponding residue fields. [F2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 **Base odd diagonal.** For $j=1$, $m=4$, the first-weight bound excludes positions $2,3$, so only $z_1=c/p^2,z_0=A/p$ occur. The double pole in the ordinary component at position $1$ comes only from shearing both seed factors; its sign is negative, hence $c=-1$. The ordinary endpoint has no incoming first shear and no admissible backward mixed tensor with first position $0$, so it equals its Laurent integral raw coefficient. Since $H_{1,1}/p=-2$ modulo $p$, cancellation of its simple pole forces $A=2c=-2$. Thus the odd scalar starts at $c_0=-1$, with endpoint residue $-2/p=-p=q^3-q$ modulo $h$. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 **Odd to even: the double pole.** Suppose the odd diagonal $j=2k+1$, $m=8k+4$, has $z_3=z_1=c/p^2$, $z_2=(4k+1)c/p$ and $z_0=(4k+2)c/p$; for $k=0$ use the base state with positions $2,3$ absent. Its endpoint residue modulo $h$ is $(2k+1)cp$, because all the other endpoint coefficients vanish there. On the next even diagonal, of length $8k+10$, the only double $h$ pole at first position $3$ is the product of the two incoming shears of the previous endpoint. The first sign is positive and the second is $(-1)^{(8k+4)/4}=-1$. Mixed incoming terms have only a single pole. Therefore the numerator $up$ of $z_3=up/h^2+vp/h$ is the negative of that endpoint residue, and $u=-(2k+1)c$. [F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 **Odd to even: the simple poles.** On this even diagonal, the ordinary components at first positions $0,1,2$ equal their integral raw components: an incoming first shear lands only at position $3$, and a backward adjacent pair has first position at least $3$ by the weight equation. In the component at position $2$, $H_{3,1}$ is divisible by $h$, so there is no double pole; the rational-state bounds force $z_2=C/h$. Cancellation of its simple pole gives $C=4(k+1)u$. Similarly $H_{3,2}$ and $H_{3,3}$ are divisible by $h$, forcing $z_1=Bp/h$ and $z_0=A/h$. The endpoint pole equation modulo $h$ is $A-C+(2B+C)q^2=0$, by step 5.1. The elements $1,q^2$ are rationally independent in that residue field, so $A=C$ and $B=-C/2$. This proves $z_2=z_0=C/h$, $z_1=-Cp/(2h)$, with $C=-4(k+1)(2k+1)c$; no value of $v$ is needed. Its endpoint modulo $p$ is $-(2k+3)C$, since the odd-position states vanish there and $H_{2,2}=4k+5$ for length $8k+10$. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 **Even to odd: the two double poles.** Let the preceding even diagonal have length $8k+2$, with the state just proved and scalar $C$, and consider the next odd diagonal of length $m=8k+4$, with $k\ge1$. At first position $3$, the double $p$ pole comes only from both shears of the preceding raw vector at position $2$. Its two signs are negative and positive. That raw coefficient evaluated modulo $p$ is $C/h=-C/2$: its ordinary component is integral by step 8.1, and the $z_3$ contribution vanishes because it has a factor $p$. Thus $c_3=C/2=:c'$. At first position $1$, the double pole comes from both shears of the preceding endpoint, again with negative total sign. That endpoint equals $-(2k+1)C$ modulo $p$. On the current diagonal its double-pole numerator is $c_1+(4k+1)c'$, using $H_{3,2}=4k+1$. Equating it to $(2k+1)C=(4k+2)c'$ forces $c_1=c'$. Single-pole mixed terms cannot alter either double-pole equation. [F1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 **Even to odd: the simple poles.** The ordinary component at first position $2$ is integral. An incoming first shear lands at positions $1,3$; an incoming second shear would have to start in the backward mixed pair at second position $m-3$, which is odd and is therefore killed by the even link. Hence $z_2+c'H_{3,1}/p^2$ has no pole, giving $A_2=(4k+1)c'$. The ordinary endpoint is also integral: no first shear lands at position $0$, and the backward mixed pair requires first position at least $1$. Its pole equation is $A_0-(4k+2)c'+(4k+2)A_2-(4k+2)(4k+1)c'=0$, so $A_0=(4k+2)c'$. Consequently the odd state has exactly the form used in step 7.1. Combining steps 8.1 and 9.1 gives the recurrence $c_{k+1}=-(2k+2)(2k+1)c_k$, and therefore $c_k=(-1)^{k+1}(2k)!$. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 **Nonzero endpoint residues in every cut.** The odd endpoint residue is $(2k+1)c_kp=(-1)^k(2k+1)!(q^3-q)$ modulo $h$. The following even scalar is $C=(-1)^k2(2k+2)!$, and its endpoint residue is $-(2k+3)C=(-1)^{k+1}2(2k+3)!$ modulo $p$. Multiplication by powers of $q$ identifies these ideals with $(q^4+1)$ and $(q^2+1)$ respectively. These residues are nonzero in their characteristic-zero residue fields, so every asserted endpoint coefficient is nonzero. The computation used only incoming shears and thus applies to every containing finite cut; step 1.1 also gives their coherence. Finite arithmetic, explicit projections and finite induction used no choice. This proves the stated escape of normalized corrected lifts. [step 1.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, step 10.1, algebra] ∎
