## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice as in
[[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]]. Fix a finite
horizon $T>0$, a probability space with an increasing filtration
$(\mathcal F_t)$ in the all-pairs convention of
[[def-continuous-time-filtration-and-all-pairs-martingale]], and an adapted
càdlàg (right-continuous with left limits), nondecreasing, integer-valued
process $N$ with $N_0=0$, $N_T<\infty$ almost surely, and jumps
$\Delta N_t\in\{0,1\}$ outside one null event. Such a process is **simple**:
its event times are strictly ordered,
$0<T_1<\cdots<T_{N_T}\le T$. It is **locally finite** when it has finitely
many events on every finite interval.

For conditional-law calculations, the model supplies a measurable history
space $(\mathsf H,\mathcal G)$, an adapted measurable history random element
$H_t$ recording the initial information and events observed through time $t$,
and a measurable update map $h\mapsto h\oplus u$ that appends an event at
time $u$. Write $\mathcal H_t:=\sigma(H_t)$ and let $H_{t-}$ record only
events strictly before $t$. Relevant exogenous covariates are included in
the supplied history. Measurability has the meaning of
[[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]. Predictability is
measurability for the predictable sigma-algebra generated by
$(s,u]\times A$ with $A\in\mathcal F_s$ and the time-zero generators, as in
[[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]].

The model supplies a probability kernel $K_{t,h}$ on $(t,\infty]$ for the
next event time given history $h$ at time $t$
([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]],
[[def-conditional-law-given-a-random-element]]); its finite-time density
versions are jointly measurable in $(t,h,u)$. The value $+\infty$ means that
no later event occurs. If the finite-time measure is absolutely continuous
with respect to Lebesgue measure
([[def-absolutely-continuous-with-respect-to-a-positive-measure]],
[[def-lebesgue-measure-and-the-lebesgue-sigma-algebra]],
[[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]), let $f_{t,h}$ be its supplied
nonnegative Radon--Nikodym density
([[def-radon-nikodym-derivative]]). Write
$S_{t,h}(u):=K_{t,h}((u,\infty])$ for its survivor and define its
**no-event conditional hazard** by
$$\lambda_{t,h}^*(u):=\frac{f_{t,h}(u)}{S_{t,h}(u)}\qquad\text{where }S_{t,h}(u)>0.$$
Assume these hazards are locally integrable on every finite interval. The
model also supplies a nonnegative predictable effective-intensity process
$\lambda^*$ compatible with these waiting-time hazards: for each history
$h$ at time $t$, on its supplied measurable no-event continuation up to
$u>t$, the pre-event intensity $\lambda^*(u\mid h_{u-})$ agrees with
$\lambda_{t,h}^*(u)$ for Lebesgue-almost every $u$. The jointly measurable
hazard representatives used in the event-density factors are these same
versions on their proper domains $u>t$. Compatibility with the predictable
process is a model requirement, not an evaluation of a waiting-time density
at its excluded left endpoint. This pre-event convention means the event
at $u$ does not change its own intensity.

The **temporal conditional-intensity model** requires both this
hazard-compatible conditional law and the separate counting-process
compensator contract of
[[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]]: if an at-risk
indicator $Y$ and intensity density $\lambda$ are separately specified, the
effective intensity is $\lambda^*=Y\lambda$; without an at-risk indicator,
it is $\lambda^*$. Then $A_t=\int_0^t\lambda^*(u)\,du$ is finite pathwise,
continuous, adapted and predictable, $\mathbb E[A_T]<\infty$, and $N-A$ is
a square-integrable martingale in the all-pairs convention of
[[def-continuous-time-adapted-process-and-martingale]]. At-risk exposure, if
present, is part of the effective intensity. The compensator clause is a
model assumption: a displayed hazard formula alone proves neither that a
process with this law exists nor that the martingale contract holds. No
stationarity or non-explosion claim follows from writing an intensity
specification.

## Source notes

Rasmussen defines conditional intensity through the conditional waiting-time
hazard and makes the terminating-process case explicit. Kass, Eden, and Brown
use the pre-event history convention and distinguish an intensity formula
from the assumptions needed for a point-process law. The local compensator
contract is inherited exactly from the counting-process page.
