## Statement

Let $K\subset S^3$ be a smooth oriented knot with a chosen closed product
tubular neighborhood $N(K)$, exterior $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$,
and positive meridian $\mu_K$. Then
$$H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_K].$$
The assertion uses integral singular homology and the stated tube convention.

## Statement references

[Hatcher, §2.2, Example 2.36](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATch2.pdf)

[Cattabriga–Francaviglia, §4.1](https://poisson.phc.dm.unipi.it/~camponovo/libri/knot.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The chosen product tube, exterior, and positive-meridian convention.

[F1] [[def-knot-exterior]] fixes $N(K)$ and $E_K$; [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]] orients $\mu_K$ as the boundary of a positive normal disk.

[F2] [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]] and [[thm-cellular-boundary-is-the-incidence-degree-matrix]]: the product torus has the CW structure with one vertex, two one-cells and one two-cell attached by the commutator; its cellular boundary map in degree two is zero, so $H_1(\partial N(K);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ on the meridian and product section.

[F3] [[thm-excision-for-singular-homology]] and [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]]: relative homology is unchanged by excision and each pair has its natural long exact sequence.

[F4] [[cor-homology-of-spheres]]: $H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z)=H_2(S^3;\mathbb Z)=0$, and $H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$, $H_2(S^1;\mathbb Z)=0$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The solid torus $N(K)=S^1\times D^2$ deformation retracts to its core circle, so by [F4] it has $H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ and $H_2(N(K);\mathbb Z)=0$. The product torus has $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^2$ by [F2]; inclusion sends the product section to the core generator and the meridian to zero because the meridian bounds a fiber disk. Hence $H_1(\partial N(K))\to H_1(N(K))$ is surjective with kernel exactly $\mathbb Z[\mu_K]$. Exactness in [F3] identifies $H_2(N(K),\partial N(K);\mathbb Z)$ with $\mathbb Z$, and its boundary map sends a relative normal disk generator to $[\mu_K]$. [F2, F3, F4, given, construct]

2.1 Enlarge $N(K)$ by a product collar and apply excision [F3] to the pair $(S^3,E_K)$. It identifies $H_2(S^3,E_K;\mathbb Z)$ with $H_2(N(K),\partial N(K);\mathbb Z)$, with the normal disk generator preserved. By the long exact sequence of $(S^3,E_K)$ and [F4], the connecting map $H_2(S^3,E_K)\to H_1(E_K)$ is an isomorphism, because the adjacent groups $H_2(S^3)$ and $H_1(S^3)$ vanish. Naturality sends the relative normal disk to its boundary positive meridian. Thus $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is infinite cyclic on $[\mu_K]$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct] ∎
