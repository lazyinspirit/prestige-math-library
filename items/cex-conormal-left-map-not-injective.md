## Statement refuted

“For an ideal $I$ of a ring $P$, the conormal map
$I/I^{2}\to B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/A}$ is injective.”

## Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field, $P=k[x]$, and let $I=(x^{2})\subseteq P$ with quotient
$B=P/I=k[x]/(x^{2})$. In the conormal sequence
$$I/I^{2}\longrightarrow B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}\longrightarrow\Omega_{B/k}\longrightarrow0$$
the class $[x^{3}]\in I/I^{2}=(x^{2})/(x^{4})$ is nonzero, but its image
$1\otimes\mathrm d(x^{3})=3x^{2}\,\mathrm dx$ is $0$ in
$B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}\cong B\,\mathrm dx$, because $x^{2}=0$ in $B$. Hence
the left map has a nonzero kernel, in every characteristic: for $p=3$ the
element $3x^{2}$ is already $0$ in $P$, and for $p\neq3$ it becomes $0$ after
the identification. In particular the sequence is only right exact.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the polynomial algebra $P=k[x]$, the principal ideal $I=(x^{2})=x^{2}P$, the quotient $B=P/I$ and the class $x^{3}\in I$.

[F1] [[thm-conormal-exact-sequence-algebra]]: for a ring map $A\to P$, an ideal $I\subseteq P$ and $B=P/I$, the sequence $I/I^{2}\xrightarrow{\alpha}B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/A}\to\Omega_{B/A}\to0$ is exact with $\alpha$ sending the class of $i\in I$ to $1\otimes\mathrm di$; no injectivity of $\alpha$ is asserted.

[F2] [[lem-differentials-polynomial-algebra-free]] with $n=1$: $\Omega_{P/k}$ is free with basis $\mathrm dx$, and $\mathrm dg=(\partial g/\partial x)\mathrm dx$ for every $g\in P$, where $\partial/\partial x$ is the $k$-derivation with $\partial x/\partial x=1$; for the powers this gives $\partial(x^{n})/\partial x=n\,x^{n-1}$.

[F3] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: the map $\mathrm d$ is a $k$-derivation, so it is additive, kills $k$, and satisfies the Leibniz rule.

[F4] [[thm-polynomial-degree-of-a-product-over-a-domain]]: in an integral domain, nonzero polynomials satisfy $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$ and $fg\neq0$.

## Verification

1.1 Apply [F1] with $A=k$: the sequence $I/I^{2}\xrightarrow{\alpha}B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}\to\Omega_{B/k}\to0$ is exact, $\alpha$ sends the class of $i\in I$ to $1\otimes\mathrm di$, and the statement of [F1] explicitly leaves injectivity of $\alpha$ open. [F1, given]

1.2 The powers of the ideal: $I=x^{2}P$ and $I^{2}=x^{4}P$, since $I^{2}$ is generated by the products of two elements of $I$, and $x^{2}f\cdot x^{2}g=x^{4}fg$. Hence $I/I^{2}=(x^{2})/(x^{4})$. The class $[x^{3}]$ is nonzero: if $x^{3}\in(x^{4})$, there would be $g\in P$ with $x^{3}=x^{4}g$, so $\deg(x^{3})=3$ equals $\deg(x^{4}g)=4+\deg g$ for $g\neq0$ and is impossible for $g=0$, by the degree rule of [F4] applied in the integral domain $P=k[x]$. [F4, given]

1.3 The target: by [F2] the module $\Omega_{P/k}$ is free with basis $\mathrm dx$, so $B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}\cong B\,\mathrm dx$ as $B$-modules, via $b\otimes\mathrm dx\mapsto b\,\mathrm dx$; here $B=B$ is written as usual and $x^{2}=0$ in $B$ because $I=(x^{2})$. [F2, given]

2.1 The image of the class: by step 1.1, $\alpha([x^{3}])=1\otimes\mathrm d(x^{3})$. By [F2] the derivation $\mathrm d$ satisfies $\mathrm d(x^{3})=(\partial(x^{3})/\partial x)\,\mathrm dx=3x^{2}\,\mathrm dx$, the coefficient $3$ being read in $k$; by the Leibniz rule [F3] this is the same as $\partial(x^{3})/\partial x=3x^{2}$ obtained from $\partial(x^{n})/\partial x=n x^{n-1}$. [F2, F3]

3.1 Under the identification of step 1.3 the element $1\otimes 3x^{2}\mathrm dx$ of step 2.1 is $3x^{2}\cdot\mathrm dx\in B\,\mathrm dx$, and this is $0$ since $x^{2}=0$ in $B$. So $\alpha([x^{3}])=0$, while $[x^{3}]\neq0$ by step 1.2; the class $[x^{3}]$ therefore lies in the nonzero kernel of $\alpha$, in every characteristic: for $p=3$ the coefficient $3x^{2}$ is already zero in $P$, and for $p\neq3$ it is nonzero in $P\,\mathrm dx$ but its image in $B\,\mathrm dx$ vanishes. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 Consequently the left map $\alpha\colon I/I^{2}\to B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}$ of the conormal sequence is not injective, so the sequence is exact at $B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/k}$ and at $\Omega_{B/k}$ but not at $I/I^{2}$; the sequence is only right exact. The witness is the single element $[x^{3}]$, whose image is $3x^{2}\,\mathrm dx=0$. This refutes the displayed statement and shows why injectivity of $\alpha$ is deliberately excluded from [F1]. [step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
