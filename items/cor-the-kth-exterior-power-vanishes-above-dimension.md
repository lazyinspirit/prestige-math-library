## Statement

If $V$ is finite-dimensional with $\dim V=n$ and $k>n$, then $\Lambda^kV=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional vector space $V$ with $\dim V=n$ and a degree $k>n$.

[L1] The equality $\dim V=n$ means that $V$ has a basis with $n$ elements ([[def-dimension]]).

[L2] In a space with a spanning set of $n$ elements, every linearly independent subset has at most $n$ elements ([[cor-independent-set-is-no-larger-than-a-finite-spanning-set]]).

[L3] In a finite-dimensional vector space, a decomposable wedge is nonzero exactly when its vectors are linearly independent ([[thm-a-decomposable-wedge-is-nonzero-exactly-for-independent-vectors]]).

[L4] The exterior power $\Lambda^kV$ is the quotient of $V^{\otimes k}$ generated by decomposable wedges $v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k$ ([[def-kth-exterior-power-by-quotient]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.


1.1 If some $k$-tuple $(v_1,\ldots,v_k)$ in $V$ were linearly independent, then its image set would be a linearly independent subset of $V$ with $k$ elements. That contradicts [L1] and [L2] because $k>n$. So every $k$-tuple in $V$ is linearly dependent. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 By [L3], step 1.1 forces every decomposable wedge $v_1\wedge\cdots\wedge v_k$ to vanish. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 By [L4], every element of $\Lambda^kV$ is a linear combination of decomposable wedges, so step 2.1 forces every element of $\Lambda^kV$ to be zero. [L4, step 2.1]

4.1 Hence $\Lambda^kV=0$ whenever $k>n$. [step 3.1] ∎
