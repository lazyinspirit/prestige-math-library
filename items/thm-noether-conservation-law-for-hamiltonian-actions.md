## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $(M,\omega,G,\mu)$ be a Hamiltonian
$G$-space, and let $H\in C^\infty(M)$ be $G$-invariant: $H(g\cdot p)=H(p)$ for
all $g\in G$ and $p\in M$. Then

$$\{\mu^\xi,H\}=0\qquad\text{for every }\xi\in\mathfrak g,$$

and the moment map $\mu$ is constant along the Hamiltonian flow of $H$. In the
language of mechanics, every component of the moment map is a conserved
quantity for the dynamics generated by the invariant Hamiltonian $H$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, a Hamiltonian $G$-space $(M,\omega,G,\mu)$ and a $G$-invariant smooth function $H$.

[A1] $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is [[def-countable-choice|countable choice]]; it is used only through the fundamental-field interface cited in [F2].

[F1] $H(g\cdot p)=H(p)$ for all $g\in G$ and $p\in M$. [given]

[F2] $\xi_M(p)=\left.\frac d{dt}\right|_0\exp_G(-t\xi)\cdot p$ and $\xi_M$ is smooth. [[def-fundamental-vector-field-of-a-left-action]].

[F3] $X_{\mu^\xi}=-\xi_M$ for every $\xi$. [[prop-moment-map-components-generate-the-negative-infinitesimal-action]].

[F4] $\{F,G\}=\omega(X_F,X_G)$ and $X_G(F)=\{F,G\}$. [[def-poisson-bracket-on-a-symplectic-manifold]].

[F5] $F$ is a first integral of $H$, meaning constant along every integral curve of $X_H$, if and only if $\{F,H\}=0$ on $M$. [[prop-f-is-a-first-integral-of-h-iff-f-and-h-poisson-commute]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $\xi\in\mathfrak g$ and $p\in M$. The curve $t\mapsto H(\exp_G(-t\xi)\cdot p)$ is constant by [F1], so its derivative at $t=0$ vanishes. By [F2] that derivative is $dH_p(\xi_M(p))$, hence $dH(\xi_M)=0$ on $M$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 Noether's identity follows: $0=dH(\xi_M)=dH(-X_{\mu^\xi})=-X_{\mu^\xi}(H)=-\{H,\mu^\xi\}$ by [F3] and the Poisson convention [F4]; hence $\{\mu^\xi,H\}=\{H,\mu^\xi\}=0$ by skew-symmetry. [step 1.1, F3, F4]

3.1 By [F5] and step 2.1, every component $\mu^\xi$ is a first integral of $H$, that is constant along each integral curve of $X_H$. Since the covector $\mu(p)$ is determined by its finitely many components $\mu^\xi(p)$, the map $\mu$ is constant along the Hamiltonian flow of $H$. [step 2.1, F5, A1] ∎
