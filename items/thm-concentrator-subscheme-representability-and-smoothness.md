## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $X$ be a separated $k$-scheme of finite type with a locally affine action of $\mathbf G_m$ and let $Z\subseteq X$ be a $\mathbf G_m$-stable closed subscheme ([[def-limit-of-a-gm-orbit-and-concentrator-subscheme]]). Then the concentrator functor $R\mapsto\{x\in X(R): \lim_{t\to0}tx\text{ exists and lies in }Z(R)\}$ is representable by a scheme $X(Z)$; its realization morphism $i:X(Z)\to X$ is a local immersion and the limit morphism $p:X(Z)\to Z$ is affine. If $X$ and $Z$ are smooth, then $X(Z)$ is smooth and $i$ identifies its geometric points with the indicated limit locus. If $X$ is affine, $i$ is a closed immersion; if $X$ and $Z$ are also smooth, $X(Z)$ is the unique smooth closed subscheme with that locus. The construction and the pair $(X(Z),p)$ commute with extension of the base field and with $\mathbf G_m$-equivariant morphisms, and for the conjugation action of $\mathbf G_m$ on a smooth affine group $G$ the scheme $G(\{g:\lim_{t\to0}t\cdot g=e\})$ is a normal algebraic subgroup of $P_G(\lambda)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a separated finite-type $k$-scheme $X$ with a locally affine $\mathbf G_m$-action, and a $\mathbf G_m$-stable closed subscheme $Z\subseteq X$.

[F1] Limits, the affine gradation and the concentrator subscheme $X(Z)$ are as in [[def-limit-of-a-gm-orbit-and-concentrator-subscheme]]; for $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ affine with gradation $A=\bigoplus_nA_n$ and $\mathfrak a=\ker(A\to A(Z))$, the concentrator is cut out by the ideal generated by $\mathfrak a\cap A_0+\sum_{n<0}A_n$ ([[def-affine-scheme]]).

[F2] Morphisms $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ correspond to $k$-algebra homomorphisms $A\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$, so a closed subscheme $\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak b)$ represents the points of $X$ killing $\mathfrak b$ ([[thm-global-sections-affine-scheme]], [[thm-morphisms-into-affine-scheme-global-sections]]).

[F3] Graded Nakayama and the Hesselink regularity comparison ([[lem-graded-nakayama]]): $M=\mathfrak mM$, $M_{\mathfrak m}=0$ and $M=0$ are equivalent for finitely generated graded modules, and if $A_{\mathfrak m}$ and $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak m}$ are regular then so is $(A/\mathfrak b)_{\mathfrak m}$ for $\mathfrak b$ generated by $\mathfrak a\cap A_0+\sum_{n<0}A_n$.

[F4] A finite-type scheme is smooth if and only if all its geometric points are regular (Milne, A.54). Over an algebraically closed field, its nonregular locus is closed: the smooth locus is the union of the open invertible-Jacobian-minor loci of [[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]]. Two reduced closed subschemes with the same geometric points are equal, because their radical ideals are determined by those points (Milne, A.30; reduction is [[def-reduction-of-scheme]]).

## Proof

1.1 Suppose first that $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is affine, with gradation $A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_n$ induced by the action, and let $\mathfrak b$ be the ideal generated by $\mathfrak a\cap A_0+\sum_{n<0}A_n$, so that $X(Z)=\operatorname{Spec}(A/\mathfrak b)$ in the sense of [F1]. For a $k$-algebra $R$ and a point $x\in X(R)$, identified with a homomorphism $x:A\to R$ by [F2], the orbit map corresponds to $A\to R[T,T^{-1}]$, $\sum_nf_n\mapsto\sum_nx(f_n)T^n$; the limit exists exactly when $x(A_n)=0$ for all $n<0$, and then the limit point is the composite $f\mapsto x(f_0)$ together with the point of $Z$. The limit lies in $Z(R)$ exactly when this composite kills $\mathfrak a$, that is, when $x(\mathfrak a\cap A_0)=0$; combining, $x$ satisfies the defining property of the concentrator functor exactly when $x$ kills $\mathfrak b$, i.e. exactly when $x$ factors through $A/\mathfrak b$. Hence the affine concentrator represents the functor and the inclusion is a closed immersion. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Keep $X$ affine and suppose $X$ and $Z$ smooth. Work over an algebraic closure and let $x$ be a geometric point of $X(Z)$, with fixed limit $y$. Localize $A$ by $A_0\setminus\mathfrak m_0$, where $\mathfrak m_0$ is the ideal of $y$ in $A_0$. Evaluation at the fixed point $y$ kills every nonzero-degree piece, so $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\oplus\bigoplus_{n\ne0}A_n$ is now a homogeneous maximal ideal. The local rings of $X$ and $Z$ at $y$ are regular, so [F3] shows that the local ring of $X(Z)$ at $y$ is regular. The quotient $A/\mathfrak b$ has only nonnegative degrees, giving an $\mathbf A^1$-action that contracts $x$ to $y$. The nonregular locus of this finite-type scheme is closed and $\mathbf G_m$-stable; if it contained $x$, its closedness would also put $y$ in it, a contradiction. Hence every geometric point $x$ is regular and $X(Z)$ is smooth. In the affine case uniqueness as a smooth closed subscheme follows from reducedness and equality of geometric point sets. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Choose a finite cover by invariant affine opens $X_i$ and put $Z_i=Z\cap X_i$. The limit functor is local on the test scheme, because orbit extensions glue uniquely by separatedness. Its subfunctor consisting of extensions landing in $X_i$ is open: it is the condition that the limit morphism to $Z$ lands in $Z_i$. Indeed, an invariant closed complement $X\setminus X_i$ contains the limit of any orbit starting in it; hence a limit in $X_i$ forces the entire orbit extension into $X_i$, checked on geometric fibers. The affine schemes $X_i(Z_i)$ of step 1.1 therefore glue along these open subfunctors and cover the representing scheme $X(Z)$. On each such open the realization is a closed immersion into $X_i$, making it a local immersion. Also $p^{-1}(Z_i)=X_i(Z_i)$ is affine, so $p$ is affine. Smoothness follows locally from step 2.1, and representability supplies uniqueness of the scheme and both realization morphisms. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Base change: for a field extension $k'/k$ the graded description of step 1.1 is stable under $A\mapsto A\otimes_kk'$ and limits of $R\otimes_kk'$-points are computed after this base change, so $X(Z)_{k'}=X_{k'}(Z_{k'})$ compatibly with $i$ and $p$; for a $\mathbf G_m$-equivariant morphism $f:X\to X'$ carrying $Z$ into $Z'$ the same functorial description shows $f(X(Z))\subseteq X'(Z')$, giving the morphism of pairs. For the conjugation action of $\mathbf G_m$ on a smooth affine group $G$ with cocharacter $\lambda$, let $H=G(\{g:\lim_{t\to0}t\cdot g=e\})$. The defining condition is closed and its points form a subgroup of $G(R)$ for every $R$, because conjugation is a group homomorphism, $(gh)_t=tgt^{-1}\,tht^{-1}$, and the limit of a product is the product of the limits; hence $H$ is an algebraic subgroup. It is normal in $P_G(\lambda)$: for $g\in P_G(\lambda)(R)$ the limit $q=\lim_{t\to0}t\cdot g$ is defined, and for $h\in H(R)$ the conjugates $ghg^{-1}$ have limit $qeq^{-1}=e$, so $gHg^{-1}\subseteq H$, and symmetry gives $gHg^{-1}=H$. AC is used only through the geometric suppliers named in the deps. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
