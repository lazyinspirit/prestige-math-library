## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with $W=\{1,s\}$, positive root $\alpha$, $\rho=\alpha/2$ and $\lambda=m\omega$ with $m\ge0$ dominant integral. Then $s\circ\lambda=-\lambda-2\rho=-(m+2)\omega$ and the BGG resolution of [[thm-bgg-resolution-of-a-finite-dimensional-simple-module]] reads

$$0\to M\bigl(-(m+2)\omega\bigr)\to M(m\omega)\to L(m\omega)\to0,$$

with the injection the canonical embedding of the Verma submodule generated by the singular vector $f^{m+1}v_\lambda$ and the surjection the canonical projection. Both end degrees are computed: $|\Phi^+|=1$, $w_0=s$, $C_0=M(\lambda)$, $C_1=M(s\circ\lambda)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with its standard positive root $\alpha$ and $\rho=\alpha/2=\omega$, the Weyl group $W=\{1,s\}$, a dominant integral weight $\lambda=m\omega$ with $m\ge0$, and the BGG complex $C_\bullet(\lambda)$.

[F1] $|\Phi^+|=1$, $\alpha=2\omega$, $s(\omega)=-\omega$ and $w_0=s$; for $\lambda=m\omega$ one has $\lambda+\rho=(m+1)\omega$ and $s\circ\lambda=s(\lambda+\rho)-\rho=-(m+1)\omega-\omega=-(m+2)\omega=-\lambda-2\rho$ ([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]], [[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]]).

[F2] $C_0(\lambda)=M(\lambda)$, $C_1(\lambda)=M(s\circ\lambda)$, and $C_k(\lambda)=0$ for $k\ge2$; the only arrow is the cover $s\rhd e$, the first differential is $d_1=\varepsilon(s,e)\iota_{s\to e}$ with $\varepsilon(s,e)=\pm1$, and $d_0=\pi\colon M(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L(\lambda)$ ([[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]], [[def-bgg-differential-from-signed-verma-maps]]).

[F3] The canonical embedding $\iota_{s\to e}\colon M(s\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$ is a nonzero $\mathfrak g$-homomorphism, injective with image the submodule generated by the singular vector $f^{\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle}v_\lambda=f^{m+1}v_\lambda$ of weight $s\circ\lambda$; it is the canonical submodule $M(s\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(\lambda)$ and every element of the one-dimensional space $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathfrak g}(M(s\circ\lambda),M(\lambda))$ is a scalar multiple of it ([[lem-simple-root-singular-vector-in-a-verma-module]], [[lem-dominant-integral-dot-translates-embed-in-the-verma-module]], [[lem-bruhat-covers-give-unique-verma-embeddings]], [[prop-simple-reflection-embedding-of-verma-modules]]).

[F4] For $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ the full augmented sequence is exact: $\operatorname{im}d_1=\ker d_0=\ker\pi$, $\operatorname{coker}d_1=L(\lambda)$, and here $\ker d_1=0$ because $C_2(\lambda)=0$ and $d_1$ is injective ([[thm-bgg-resolution-of-a-finite-dimensional-simple-module]], [[lem-the-bgg-augmentation-has-image-the-simple-module]]).

[F5] The irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module with highest weight $m\omega$ has dimension $m+1$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]); the weight $-(m+2)\omega$ satisfies $\langle-(m+2)\omega+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle=-(m+1)<0$, so $M(-(m+2)\omega)$ is simple ([[cor-antidominant-verma-modules-are-simple]]).

## Verification

1.1 The terms: by [F1] and [F2], $C_0=M(m\omega)$ and $C_1=M(s\circ m\omega)=M(-(m+2)\omega)$, and $C_k=0$ for $k\ge2$; hence the complex is concentrated in degrees $0$ and $1$, and the sequence displayed in the Example is the BGG complex with $d_0=\pi$. [F1, F2]

1.2 The injection: by [F3] the map $d_1$ is, up to the sign $\pm1$, the canonical embedding $M(-(m+2)\omega)\hookrightarrow M(m\omega)$ with image the Verma submodule generated by $f^{m+1}v_\lambda$, and it is injective; its source is even a simple Verma module by [F5], but simplicity is not needed for the injection. [F3, F5]

1.3 Exactness at $C_0$: by [F4], $\operatorname{im}d_1=\ker\pi=\ker d_0$ and $\operatorname{coker}d_1=L(m\omega)$; equivalently the middle quotient $M(m\omega)/M(-(m+2)\omega)\cong L(m\omega)$ is the standard finite-dimensional quotient of dimension $m+1$ by [F5]. [F4, F5]

2.1 Exactness at $C_1$: $\ker d_1=0=\operatorname{im}d_2$, since $C_2=0$ and $d_1$ is injective by step 1.2; this is the injectivity of a nonzero Verma map. [step 1.2]

3.1 Combining the computed end degrees of step 1.1, the identification of the injection in step 1.2, and the exactness in steps 1.3 and 2.1, the BGG resolution of $L(m\omega)$ is exactly $0\to M(-(m+2)\omega)\to M(m\omega)\to L(m\omega)\to0$ with the stated maps, and $|\Phi^+|=1$ is the length of the resolution. [step 1.1, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
