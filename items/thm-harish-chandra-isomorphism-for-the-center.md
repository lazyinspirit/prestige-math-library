## Statement

The shifted Harish-Chandra map

$$ \operatorname{HC}_\rho\colon Z(U(\mathfrak g))\to S(\mathfrak h)^W, \qquad \operatorname{HC}_\rho(z)(\lambda)=\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda-\rho), $$

is an algebra isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The shifted Harish-Chandra map $$ \operatorname{HC}_\rho(z)(\lambda):=\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda-\rho) $$ on the center of $U(\mathfrak g)$.

[F1] The Harish-Chandra projection is multiplicative on the center ([[prop-harish-chandra-projection-is-multiplicative-on-the-center]]).

[F2] The leading PBW symbol of a central element is invariant, and Chevalley restriction identifies symmetric invariants on $\mathfrak g$ with Weyl invariants on $\mathfrak h$ ([[lem-associated-graded-symbol-of-a-central-element-is-invariant]], [[thm-symmetric-invariants-restrict-to-weyl-invariants]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $f_z(\mu):=\operatorname{pr}(z)(\mu)$ for $z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$. Multiplicativity from [F1] shows that $\operatorname{HC}_\rho(z)(\lambda)=f_z(\lambda-\rho)$ defines an algebra homomorphism. To identify its image, fix a simple reflection $s_i$ and a weight $\mu$ with $\langle \mu+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=m\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. In the cyclic highest-weight quotient generated by a highest vector $v_\mu$, the standard rank-one $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ calculation gives $e_if_i^mv_\mu=0$, so $f_i^mv_\mu$ is a highest-weight vector of weight $s_i\cdot\mu$. Because $z$ is central, it acts by one scalar on the whole module, and [[lem-harish-chandra-projection-computes-highest-weight-scalars]] therefore gives $f_z(\mu)=f_z(s_i\cdot\mu)$. These weights are Zariski dense, so $f_z$ is invariant under the dot action of every simple reflection, hence under $W$. By [[lem-rho-shift-intertwines-the-dot-and-ordinary-weyl-actions]], $\operatorname{HC}_\rho(z)$ lies in $S(\mathfrak h)^W$. [F1, given]

2.1 The PBW filtration on $U(\mathfrak g)$ induces a filtration on the center, and the Harish-Chandra projection does not increase PBW degree because it is defined by projecting the PBW decomposition onto the $U(\mathfrak h)$ summand. Translation by $\rho$ preserves polynomial degree on $S(\mathfrak h)$, so $\operatorname{HC}_\rho$ is a filtered algebra homomorphism. [step 1.1]

3.1 The PBW theorem gives the symmetrization map $$\operatorname{sym}:S(\mathfrak g)\to U(\mathfrak g),\qquad x_1\cdots x_m\mapsto\frac1{m!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}x_{\sigma(1)}\cdots x_{\sigma(m)},$$ as a filtration-preserving vector-space isomorphism whose associated graded map is the identity. The adjoint action is a derivation on both sides, so $\operatorname{sym}$ is $\mathfrak g$-equivariant. Since an element of $U(\mathfrak g)$ is central exactly when it is killed by the adjoint action of the generators $\mathfrak g$, symmetrization restricts to invariant lifts and gives $$\operatorname{gr}Z(U(\mathfrak g))=S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}.$$ [[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]] [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 In the PBW decomposition, the top-degree part of $\operatorname{pr}(z)$ is obtained by discarding every monomial whose symbol contains a factor from $\mathfrak n^-$ or $\mathfrak n^+$. Translation by $\rho$ changes only lower-degree terms. Thus the associated-graded Harish-Chandra map is exactly restriction $$S(\mathfrak g)^{\mathfrak g}\longrightarrow S(\mathfrak h)^W,$$ which is an isomorphism by [F2]. [F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 A filtered algebra homomorphism whose associated graded map is an isomorphism is itself an isomorphism: lift target elements degree by degree for surjectivity, and take the leading symbol of a kernel element for injectivity. Applying this to steps 2.1 and 4.1 shows that $\operatorname{HC}_\rho$ is an algebra isomorphism $Z(U(\mathfrak g))\xrightarrow{\sim} S(\mathfrak h)^W$. [step 2.1, step 4.1] ∎
