## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(V,r)$ be a
simple rational representation of a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel
subgroup $B\supseteq T$ ([[def-borel-subgroup-and-maximal-torus]], [[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Then: (a)
$V$ contains a primitive vector $v$, and it is unique up to multiplication by a
nonzero scalar; (b) its weight $\lambda$ is dominant and the weight space
$V_\lambda=kv$ is one-dimensional; (c) every weight $\mu$ of $V$ satisfies
$\mu\le\lambda$, i.e. $\mu=\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with
$m_\alpha\ge0$; (d) any two primitive vectors of $V$ have the same weight; this
weight is called the **highest weight** of $V$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A simple rational representation $(V,r)$ of the split reductive group
$(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, unipotent radical $U=B_u$ and
$B/U\cong T$.

[F1] *$B$ is trigonalizable.* The split-Borel root-subgroup theorem gives the
multiplication map $\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}U_\alpha\to U=B_u$ as a
$T$-equivariant isomorphism of varieties for any ordering
([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Since $B$ is split connected solvable with unipotent
radical $U$ and $B/U\cong T$ diagonalizable, $B$ is trigonalizable
([[def-trigonalizable-algebraic-group]],
[[def-unipotent-algebraic-group]],
[[def-borel-subgroup-and-maximal-torus]]).

[F2] *Invariant flags.* Every finite-dimensional rational representation of a
trigonalizable group has a complete flag of subrepresentations
([[lem-trigonalizable-iff-invariant-flags]]).

[F3] *Finite dimensionality.* Simple rational representations of an affine
group scheme of finite type are finite-dimensional
([[lem-simple-rational-representations-are-finite-dimensional]]).

[F4] *Modules generated by a primitive vector.* If a rational representation
$W$ of $(G,T)$ is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector of weight
$\lambda$, then $\lambda$ is dominant, $W_\lambda$ is one-dimensional, and every
weight of $W$ is of the form
$\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$, $m_\alpha\ge0$
([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]],
[[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F5] The simple roots form a basis of the root lattice, so an expansion in $\Delta$ has unique coefficients, and a sum of nonnegative multiples of simple roots equalling zero has all coefficients zero. ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], and the finite-dimensionality of $V$ from [F3], the $B$-module $V$ has a complete flag of subrepresentations; its one-dimensional member is a $B$-stable line $kv$ with $v\ne0$, so $v$ is primitive. [F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Since $V$ is simple and $v\ne0$, the $G$-submodule generated by $v$ is nonzero, hence equal to $V$; so $V$ is generated by the primitive vector $v$. Let $\lambda$ be its weight. By [F4] the weight $\lambda$ is dominant, $V_\lambda=kv$ is one-dimensional, and every weight of $V$ has the form $\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$. This proves (b) and (c). [F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $v'$ be another primitive vector of $V$, of weight $\lambda'$. Since $V$ is simple, $v'$ also generates $V$ as a $G$-module, so [F4] applied to $v'$ gives $\lambda=\lambda'-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m'_\alpha\alpha$ with $m'_\alpha\ge0$, while [F4] applied to $v$ gives $\lambda'=\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$. Adding gives $0=\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}(m_\alpha+m'_\alpha)\alpha$, so by [F5] all $m_\alpha=m'_\alpha=0$ and $\lambda=\lambda'$. [F4, F5, step 2.1]

4.1 Any two primitive vectors $v,v'$ have the same weight $\lambda$ by step 3.1, and since $V_\lambda=kv$ is one-dimensional by step 2.1, $v'$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $v$; this proves (a) and (d). Together with step 2.1 the four assertions hold, and the common weight is the highest weight of $V$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The primitive vector exists because a split connected solvable group is
  trigonalizable, so the invariant-flag theorem applies to the finite-dimensional
  module $V$.
- Uniqueness up to scalar follows from the two inclusions
  $\lambda'\le\lambda$ and $\lambda\le\lambda'$ together with the
  one-dimensionality of the top weight space.
