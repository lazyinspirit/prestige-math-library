## Statement

Let $G$ be a group ([[def-group]]) whose derived subgroup $[G,G]$ is contained
in the centre $Z(G)$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]],
[[def-center-of-a-group]]). If $[G,G]\le Z(G)$ then $[xy,w]=[x,w][y,w]$,
$[x,yw]=[x,y][x,w]$, and $[x^n,y]=[x,y]^n=[x,y^n]$ for every integer $n$ and all
$x,y,w\in G$, integer powers being those of [[def-group-power]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group $G$ with $[G,G]\le Z(G)$, and elements $x,y,w\in G$.

[F1] For $g,h\in G$ the commutator is $[g,h]:=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $[G,G]:=\langle\{[g,h]:g,h\in G\}\rangle$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F2] $Z(G):=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$ ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[L1] For all $m,n\in\mathbb Z$ one has $g^{m+n}=g^mg^n$ and $g^{-m}=(g^m)^{-1}$ ([[lem-group-power-laws]]).

[L2] $(gh)^{-1}=h^{-1}g^{-1}$ ([[lem-group-inverse-laws]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every commutator $[g,h]$ lies in $[G,G]$, hence in $Z(G)$, so it commutes with every element of $G$ and may be moved to any position in a product without changing that product. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 Expanding, $[xy,w]=(xy)w(xy)^{-1}w^{-1}=xywy^{-1}x^{-1}w^{-1}=x\,(ywy^{-1}w^{-1})\,wx^{-1}w^{-1}=x[y,w]wx^{-1}w^{-1}=[y,w]\,xwx^{-1}w^{-1}=[y,w][x,w]=[x,w][y,w]$, the last two equalities moving the central factor $[y,w]$ past $x$ and then past $[x,w]$. [F1, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Expanding in the second variable, $[x,yw]=x(yw)x^{-1}(yw)^{-1}=xywx^{-1}w^{-1}y^{-1}=(xyx^{-1})(xwx^{-1}w^{-1})y^{-1}=(xyx^{-1})[x,w]y^{-1}=[x,w]\,xyx^{-1}y^{-1}=[x,y][x,w]$, where the central factor $[x,w]$ is moved past $y^{-1}$ and then past $[x,y]$. [F1, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $n\in\mathbb N$ the identity $[x^n,y]=[x,y]^n$ follows by induction: at $n=0$ both sides are the identity, since $x^0$ is the identity and $[e,y]=eye^{-1}y^{-1}=e$, and $[x^{n+1},y]=[x^nx,y]=[x^n,y][x,y]=[x,y]^n[x,y]=[x,y]^{n+1}$. [F1, L1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For a negative integer $n$ put $k=-n$; then $e=[x^0,y]=[x^kx^{-k},y]=[x^k,y][x^{-k},y]$ by step 2.1, so $[x^n,y]=[x^{-k},y]=([x,y]^k)^{-1}=[x,y]^{-k}=[x,y]^n$, and the identity $[x,y^n]=[x,y]^n$ is obtained the same way from step 2.2. [L1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The hypothesis $[G,G]\le Z(G)$ is exactly the vanishing of the third term of the lower central series: $\gamma_3(G)=[G,[G,G]]$ is trivial precisely when every commutator commutes with every element of $G$ ([[def-subgroup-commutator-and-lower-central-series]]).

The first two identities are additivity in each variable separately, and they fail without the hypothesis: in general $[xy,w]=x[y,w]x^{-1}\cdot[x,w]$, and the conjugating factor is what the hypothesis removes.
