## Statement

Let $\Lambda=\Lambda_j$ be a level-one fundamental weight and keep the
conventions of
[[def-type-a-affine-presentations-and-charged-cyclotomic-quotients]]. Then the
assignments $T_r\mapsto T_r$ ($1\le r<d$) in the nondegenerate case and
$s_r\mapsto s_r$ in the degenerate case extend to $F$-algebra isomorphisms
$$H_{F,q}(S_d)\longrightarrow H^\Lambda_d,\qquad FS_d\longrightarrow H^\Lambda_d$$
respectively ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]],
[[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$, the level-one fundamental weight $\Lambda=\Lambda_j$,
the cyclotomic quotient $H^\Lambda_d$ of
[[def-type-a-affine-presentations-and-charged-cyclotomic-quotients]], and the
finite type-A Hecke algebra $H_{F,q}(S_d)$ with quadratic relation
$T_r^2=(q-1)T_r+q$, $q=q_H$, respectively the group algebra $FS_d$.

[F1] At level one the cyclotomic relation reads $X_1=q^j$ in the nondegenerate
branch and $x_1=j\cdot1_F$ in the degenerate branch; the mixed relations give
$X_{r+1}=q^{-1}T_rX_rT_r$ and $x_{r+1}=s_rx_rs_r+s_r$, so every polynomial
generator is expressible in the first one and the finite generators
([[def-type-a-affine-presentations-and-charged-cyclotomic-quotients]]).

[F2] The finite Hecke algebra has standard basis $\{T_w:w\in S_d\}$; the
Jucys–Murphy elements $L_r=\sum_{a<r}(a\ r)$ in $FS_d$ commute and satisfy
$s_rL_{r+1}=L_rs_r+1$ and $s_rL_t=L_ts_r$ for $t\notin\{r,r+1\}$, with the
analogous multiplicative identities in $H_{F,q}(S_d)$
([[thm-hecke-jucys-murphy-action-is-dominance-triangular]],
[[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The level-one quotient $H^\Lambda_d$ and the finite generators

$T_r$ (respectively $s_r$).

1.1 Surjectivity of the natural map. By [F1] the first polynomial generator is the scalar $q^j$ (respectively $j$), and the recursion expressing every $X_{r+1}$ (respectively $x_{r+1}$) in $X_r$ and $T_r$ (respectively $s_r$) shows that the algebra generated by the finite generators and $X_1$ is already generated by the finite generators alone. Hence the assignment of the Statement is a surjective algebra homomorphism. [F1, algebra]

1.2 The inverse in the nondegenerate branch. Let $L_r$ be the additive Jucys–Murphy element and put $M_r:=1+(q-1)L_r=q^{1-r}T_{r-1}\cdots T_1^{\,2}\cdots T_{r-1},$ a product of units, so every $M_r$ is invertible; the $M_r$ commute because the $L_r$ do, and $T_rM_rT_r=qM_{r+1}$ while $T_rM_t=M_tT_r$ for $t\notin\{r,r+1\}$ follow from [F2] by separating the term $a=r$ in $L_{r+1}$ and using the finite braid relation on the three affected transpositions. Setting $X_r:=q^jM_r$ therefore produces commuting units with $T_rX_rT_r=qX_{r+1}$, distant mixed commutation, the finite Hecke relations and $X_1=q^j$. These elements satisfy the complete affine presentation, so the assignment defines a homomorphism $H^\Lambda_d\to H_{F,q}(S_d)$ fixing the $T_r$. [F1, F2, construct]

1.3 The inverse in the degenerate branch. Put $x_r:=j+L_r$ with $L_r=\sum_{a<r}(a\ r)\in FS_d$. Direct transposition multiplication gives $s_rL_{r+1}s_r=L_r+s_r$, hence $s_rx_{r+1}=x_rs_r+1$, and the $x_r$ commute and commute with distant generators by [F2]; at $r=1$ this gives $x_1=j$. These elements satisfy the degenerate affine relations, so the assignment defines a homomorphism $H^\Lambda_d\to FS_d$ fixing the $s_r$. [F1, F2, construct]

2.1 Mutual inverses. The two constructions of steps 1.2 and 1.3 fix every finite generator, and the composites with the surjection of step 1.1 fix every presentation generator: the finite generators by construction, and the polynomial generators because both sides satisfy the same recursion from $X_1=q^j$ (respectively $x_1=j$) and the mixed relations. Hence both composites are the identity on a generating set, so the maps are mutually inverse isomorphisms; rank zero gives $F$ on both sides. This removes any late-basis or dimension dependency and introduces no BK or primitive-block input. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The identification is proved by an explicit inverse rather than by comparing
dimensions, so it does not depend on the later cyclotomic basis theorem, the
BK isomorphism or any block-primitivity statement. No choice principle is
used.
