## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), as required by the
affine structure sheaves in [[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]].
Let $k$ be a field, let $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/I$ be a standard graded quotient in
which the images of the variables have degree one, and let $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$
have standard charts $D_+(x_i)=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ with
$A_i=(S_{x_i})_0$, as in
[[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]]. Fix $i$ and let
$\lambda_i:S\to S_{x_i}$ be the localization map.

1. **Chart primes.** The maps
   $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$ and
   $\mathfrak p_0\mapsto\lambda_i^{-1}(\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i})$ are inverse,
   inclusion-preserving bijections between the homogeneous primes
   $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak p$ (these are exactly the
   points of $\operatorname{Proj}S$ lying in $D_+(x_i)$) and the points
   $\mathfrak p_0$ of the chart $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$.
2. **Local rings.** If $\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p_0$ correspond as in 1,
   then $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\cong(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_0}$.
3. **Overlaps.** If $x_i,x_j\notin\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p_{0,i}$,
   $\mathfrak p_{0,j}$ are the corresponding primes of $A_i$ and $A_j$, then in
   $A_{ij}=(S_{x_ix_j})_0$ one has
   $\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,j}A_{ij}$, and the two charts compute
   the same local ring at the point:
   $(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_{0,i}}\cong(A_j)_{\mathfrak p_{0,j}}$.
4. **Field extension.** Let $k\subseteq K$ be a field extension and let
   $S_K=S\otimes_kK$ be graded with $\deg(s\otimes\mu)=\deg s$. Then
   $(S_K)_{x_i}\cong S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$ as graded $K$-algebras and
   $((S_K)_{x_i})_0\cong A_i\otimes_kK$. Contraction along $S\to S_K$ carries
   homogeneous primes of $S_K$ avoiding $x_i$ to homogeneous primes of $S$
   avoiding $x_i$, and for $\mathfrak q\mapsto\mathfrak p:=\mathfrak q\cap S$
   with chart primes $\mathfrak q_0\in\operatorname{Spec}(A_i\otimes_kK)$ and
   $\mathfrak p_0\in\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ one has
   $\mathfrak q_0\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_0$. Moreover
   $\mathcal O_{X_K,\mathfrak q}$ is the localization of
   $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\otimes_kK$ at the prime induced by $\mathfrak q_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a field $k$, a standard graded quotient $S=k[x_0,\ldots,x_n]/I$ of the polynomial ring, the projective scheme $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$ with its standard charts, a fixed index $i$, and a field extension $k\subseteq K$.

[L1] Under AC, $\operatorname{Proj}S$ has as points the homogeneous primes $\mathfrak p$ with $S_+\nsubseteq\mathfrak p$; its standard chart $D_+(x_i)$ is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ with $A_i=(S_{x_i})_0$, where $S_{x_i}$ is a graded ring in which $x_i$, homogeneous of degree one, is inverted; the overlaps are $A_{ij}=(S_{x_ix_j})_0$, obtained from $A_i$ by inverting the degree-zero element $x_j/x_i$ and from $A_j$ by inverting $x_i/x_j$; the standard charts cover $X$ ([[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]]). The AC use is inherited from the affine chart sheaf construction; the extension-contraction calculations below need no further choice.

[L2] In a graded ring every element has a unique expression as a sum of homogeneous elements, its homogeneous components; an ideal is homogeneous when it contains the homogeneous component of each of its elements, and such an ideal is generated by its homogeneous elements ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]], [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]]).

[L3] Contraction along a localization map $R\to T^{-1}R$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(T^{-1}R)$ onto the primes of $R$ avoiding $T$, with inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pT^{-1}R$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[L4] For a prime $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}A$ the stalk of the affine structure sheaf is $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}A,\mathfrak p}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}$ ([[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]]); the stalk at a point of a scheme is computed as the colimit over the open neighbourhoods of that point, so it may be computed in any affine chart containing it ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]).

[L5] If $S,T\subseteq R$ are multiplicative with generated multiplicative set $U$ and $\overline T$ is the image of $T$ in $S^{-1}R$, then $\overline T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)\cong U^{-1}R$; in particular $(R_f)_g\cong R_{fg}$ for $f,g\in R$ ([[prop-iterated-localisation]]).

[L6] A proper ideal $P$ is prime exactly when $R/P$ is an integral domain; in particular a quotient of a domain by a prime ideal is a domain, and the polynomial ring over a domain is a domain ([[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]], [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]).

[L7] A homomorphism out of a localization is the same thing as a homomorphism out of the base ring inverting the denominator set, and it is uniquely determined by its values on the base ring ([[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]]); two localizations with the same universal property are canonically isomorphic ([[cor-localisation-is-unique-up-to-unique-isomorphism]]).

[L8] Maps from a tensor product of commutative $k$-algebras correspond to pairs of maps from the factors ([[thm-coproduct-property-of-tensor-products-of-commutative-algebras]]), and contraction of a prime ideal along a ring homomorphism is prime ([[thm-prime-spectrum-map-by-contraction]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $z\in S_{x_i}$ be homogeneous of degree $m$ and write $z=s/x_i^e$ with $s\in S$ and $e\ge0$; writing $s=\sum_ds_d$ for the homogeneous components of $s$, the elements $s_d/x_i^e$ are homogeneous of degree $d-e$ in $S_{x_i}$ and sum to $z$. Since $z$ is homogeneous of degree $m$, only the summand with $d-e=m$ can be nonzero, so $z=s_{m+e}/x_i^e=(s_{m+e}/x_i^{m+e})x_i^m$ with $a:=s_{m+e}/x_i^{m+e}\in(S_{x_i})_0=A_i$. Because the direct-sum expression in a graded ring is unique [L2], it follows that $S_{x_i}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}A_ix_i^m$, that $a_mx_i^m$ is the degree-$m$ component of $\sum_ma_mx_i^m$, and that $x_i$ is a unit; in particular the degree-$m$ part of $S_{x_i}$ is exactly $A_ix_i^m$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 Let $k\subseteq K$ be a field extension and $S_K=S\otimes_kK$, graded by $\deg(s\otimes\mu)=\deg s$. The $k$-algebra maps $S_{x_i}\to(S_K)_{x_i}$, $s/x_i^e\mapsto(s\otimes1)(x_i\otimes1)^{-e}$, and $K\to(S_K)_{x_i}$, $\mu\mapsto1\otimes\mu$, induce by the coproduct property [L8] a $k$-algebra map $S_{x_i}\otimes_kK\to(S_K)_{x_i}$; conversely the map $S_K\to S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$ built from $S\to S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$ and $K\to S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$ inverts $x_i\otimes1$, whose inverse is $x_i^{-1}\otimes1$, so by [L7] it induces $(S_K)_{x_i}\to S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$. Each composite is a map fixing the base ring and the inverted element, hence is the identity by the uniqueness in [L7] and [L8]; thus $(S_K)_{x_i}\cong S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$, compatibly with the gradings, since the maps send homogeneous elements to homogeneous elements of the same degree. As $K$ lies in degree zero, the degree-$m$ part of $S_{x_i}\otimes_kK$ is $(S_{x_i})_m\otimes_kK=A_ix_i^m\otimes_kK$ by 1.1, so $((S_K)_{x_i})_0=A_i\otimes_kK$ and $S_K$ is standard graded over $K$ with the images of the $x_i$ as degree-one generators. [L7, L8, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 If $\mathfrak a\subseteq S$ is homogeneous, then $\mathfrak aS_{x_i}$ is a homogeneous ideal of $S_{x_i}$: writing an element as a finite sum $\sum_\ell a^{(\ell)}/x_i^{e_\ell}$ with $a^{(\ell)}\in\mathfrak a$ and decomposing each $a^{(\ell)}=\sum_da^{(\ell)}_d$ into components, which lie in $\mathfrak a$ by [L2], exhibits the element as a sum of homogeneous elements of $S_{x_i}$, so each of its homogeneous components lies in $\mathfrak aS_{x_i}$. If $\mathfrak b\subseteq S_{x_i}$ is a homogeneous ideal and $s=\sum_ds_d\in\lambda_i^{-1}(\mathfrak b)$, then $\lambda_i(s)=\sum_d\lambda_i(s_d)$ with $\lambda_i(s_d)$ homogeneous of degree $d$ by [L1], so each $\lambda_i(s_d)\in\mathfrak b$ and hence each $s_d\in\lambda_i^{-1}(\mathfrak b)$. Thus extension and contraction along $\lambda_i$ preserve homogeneity of ideals. [L1, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 Let $\mathfrak p_0\subseteq A_i$ be a prime. By 1.1 the ideal it generates in $S_{x_i}$ is $\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i}=\bigoplus_{m\in\mathbb Z}\mathfrak p_0x_i^m$, which is homogeneous and satisfies $\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i}\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_0$; its quotient is $\bigoplus_m(A_i/\mathfrak p_0)x_i^m$, a ring in which $x_i$ is a unit, and a product of two nonzero elements there has nonzero coefficient at the lowest occurring power of $x_i$ because $A_i/\mathfrak p_0$ is a domain by [L6]. Hence $\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i}$ is a homogeneous prime. Conversely, if $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{x_i}$ is a homogeneous prime with $\mathfrak q_0:=\mathfrak q\cap A_i$, then every homogeneous $z\in\mathfrak q$ of degree $m$ equals $ax_i^m$ with $a\in A_i$ by 1.1, and $a=x_i^{-m}z\in\mathfrak q$ because $x_i$ is a unit, so $z\in\mathfrak q_0S_{x_i}$; since homogeneous ideals are generated by their homogeneous elements [L2] we get $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak q_0S_{x_i}$. Therefore $\mathfrak p_0\mapsto\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i}$ and $\mathfrak q\mapsto\mathfrak q\cap A_i$ are inverse bijections between $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ and the homogeneous primes of $S_{x_i}$, both given by extension respectively contraction of ideals and therefore inclusion-preserving. [L2, L6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Applying [L3] to $S$ and the multiplicative set of powers of $x_i$: contraction along $\lambda_i$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(S_{x_i})$ onto the primes of $S$ avoiding $x_i$, with inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$. By 2.2 this bijection and its inverse carry homogeneous primes to homogeneous primes, and since the two maps are given by contraction and extension of ideals, they restrict to inverse inclusion-preserving bijections between the homogeneous primes of $S$ avoiding $x_i$ and the homogeneous primes of $S_{x_i}$. [L3, step 2.2]

4.1 Composing the bijections of 3.1 and 2.3 gives inverse bijections between the homogeneous primes $\mathfrak p$ of $S$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak p$ and the points $\mathfrak p_0$ of $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$: the composite sends $\mathfrak p$ to $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$ and $\mathfrak p_0$ to $\lambda_i^{-1}(\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i})$, both maps are inclusion-preserving, and each composite is the identity because $\mathfrak p_0S_{x_i}\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_0$ by 2.3 and because for a homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p$ of $S$ avoiding $x_i$, the ideal $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ is a homogeneous prime of $S_{x_i}$ by 3.1 to which $\mathfrak q=(\mathfrak q\cap A_i)S_{x_i}$ applies, while its contraction to $S$ is $\mathfrak p$ by the inverse property in [L3]. A homogeneous prime with $x_i\notin\mathfrak p$ satisfies $S_+\nsubseteq\mathfrak p$ since $x_i\in S_+$, and it lies in $D_+(x_i)$ by [L1]; these are exactly the points of the chart. This is claim 1. [L1, L3, step 2.3, step 3.1]

5.1 By [L1] the chart $D_+(x_i)$ is the affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ and, by 4.1, the point of $D_+(x_i)$ corresponding to $\mathfrak p$ is the prime $\mathfrak p_0=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$ of $A_i$. Since the stalk of a scheme at a point may be computed in any open chart containing it and the stalk of an affine scheme at a prime is the localization at that prime [L4], we get $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\cong(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_0}$. This is claim 2. [L1, L4, step 4.1]

5.2 Assume now $x_i,x_j\notin\mathfrak p$ and set $\mathfrak p_{0,i}=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$, $\mathfrak p_{0,j}=\mathfrak pS_{x_j}\cap A_j$. By [L3] applied to $S$ and the multiplicative set generated by $x_i$ and $x_j$, which $\mathfrak p$ avoids, the ideal $\mathfrak pS_{x_ix_j}$ is a prime of $S_{x_ix_j}$, so $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}:=\mathfrak pS_{x_ix_j}\cap A_{ij}$ is a prime of $A_{ij}$ by [L8]. Its contraction $Q:=\mathfrak pS_{x_ix_j}\cap S_{x_i}$ is a prime of $S_{x_i}$ containing $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$, and $Q\cap S=\mathfrak p$: an element $z\in Q$ satisfies $x_j^Nz\in\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ for some $N\ge0$, because $\mathfrak pS_{x_ix_j}$ is the localization of $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ at $x_j$ and $\frac z1$ is a fraction with numerator in $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$; so for $s\in S\cap Q$ we get $x_j^Ns\in\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap S=\mathfrak p$ by the inverse property in [L3], whence $s\in\mathfrak p$ as $x_j\notin\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p$ is prime. Contraction is injective on primes of $S_{x_i}$ by [L3], so $Q=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$, and intersecting with $A_i\subseteq S_{x_i}$ gives $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_{0,i}$. By [L1] the ring $A_{ij}$ is the localization of $A_i$ at the element $x_j/x_i$, which does not lie in $\mathfrak p_{0,i}$: otherwise $x_j=x_i\cdot(x_j/x_i)\in\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap S=\mathfrak p$, since the contraction of $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ to $S$ is $\mathfrak p$. So [L3] applied to $A_i$ and the powers of $x_j/x_i$ shows that $\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}$ is the unique prime of $A_{ij}$ contracting to $\mathfrak p_{0,i}$; since $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}$ is such a prime, $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}$, and by the same argument with $i,j$ exchanged, $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,j}A_{ij}$. Hence $\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,j}A_{ij}$. [L1, L3, L8, step 4.1]

5.3 Let $\mathfrak q$ be a homogeneous prime of $S_K$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak q$ and $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap S$. Since $S\to S_K$, $s\mapsto s\otimes1$, is degree-preserving, the degree-$d$ component of $\mathfrak p$ is the contraction of the degree-$d$ component of $\mathfrak q$; hence $\mathfrak p$ is homogeneous [L2], and $x_i\notin\mathfrak p$ is clear. Let $\mathfrak q_0=\mathfrak q(S_K)_{x_i}\cap(A_i\otimes_kK)$ and $\mathfrak p_0=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$ be the chart primes of $\mathfrak q$ for $X_K=\operatorname{Proj}S_K$ and of $\mathfrak p$, both given by claim 1 (4.1) applied to the standard graded $K$-algebra $S_K$ of 2.1 and to $S$. Under the identifications of 2.1 we have $A_i\subseteq A_i\otimes_kK\subseteq(S_K)_{x_i}$ and $S_{x_i}\subseteq(S_K)_{x_i}$. The contraction $Q:=\mathfrak q(S_K)_{x_i}\cap S_{x_i}$ is a prime of $S_{x_i}$ containing $\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ whose contraction to $S$ is $\mathfrak p$: if $s\in S\cap Q$ then $x_i^Ns\in\mathfrak q$ for some $N\ge0$, so $x_i^Ns\in\mathfrak q\cap S=\mathfrak p$ and hence $s\in\mathfrak p$. Contraction is injective on primes of $S_{x_i}$ by [L3], so $Q=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}$ and therefore $\mathfrak q_0\cap A_i=Q\cap A_i=\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_0$. [L2, L3, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 With the notation of 5.2, the point $\mathfrak p$ lies in both charts, so by 5.1 its stalk is $(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_{0,i}}$ when computed in chart $i$ and $(A_j)_{\mathfrak p_{0,j}}$ when computed in chart $j$. Since $A_{ij}$ is the localization of $A_i$ at $x_j/x_i$, the general form of [L5] applied to the multiplicative set $A_i\setminus\mathfrak p_{0,i}$ gives $(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_{0,i}}\cong(A_{ij})_{\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}}$; indeed, put $f=x_j/x_i\notin\mathfrak p_{0,i}$. Every element outside $\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}$ is a fraction $a/f^n$ with $a\notin\mathfrak p_{0,i}$. After inverting the images of $A_i\setminus\mathfrak p_{0,i}$, such a fraction is a unit, with inverse $f^n/a$. Conversely every image of an element of $A_i\setminus\mathfrak p_{0,i}$ is outside the extended prime. Thus inverting these images and inverting the entire prime complement have the same universal property [L7]. By 5.2, $\mathfrak p_{0,i}A_{ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,ij}=\mathfrak p_{0,j}A_{ij}$, so both charts give the localization of $A_{ij}$ at $\mathfrak p_{0,ij}$; in particular $(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_{0,i}}\cong(A_j)_{\mathfrak p_{0,j}}$, which completes claim 3. [L5, L7, step 5.1, step 5.2]

6.2 With the notation of 5.3 and 2.1, 5.1 applied to the chart $(A_i\otimes_kK)_{\mathfrak q_0}$ of $X_K$ gives $\mathcal O_{X_K,\mathfrak q}\cong(A_i\otimes_kK)_{\mathfrak q_0}$, while $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\otimes_kK\cong(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_0}\otimes_kK$. Put $B=A_i\otimes_kK$ and $S_0=A_i\setminus\mathfrak p_0\subseteq B$. The $k$-algebras $S_0^{-1}B$ and $(A_i)_{\mathfrak p_0}\otimes_kK$ are canonically isomorphic: by [L7] and [L8], homomorphisms from either of them into a commutative $k$-algebra $C$ correspond naturally to a pair consisting of a $k$-algebra map $A_i\to C$ inverting $S_0$ and a $k$-algebra map $K\to C$, so both are localizations of $B$ at $S_0$ and [L7] identifies them. By 5.3 we have $\mathfrak q_0\cap A_i=\mathfrak p_0$, so $S_0\subseteq B\setminus\mathfrak q_0$; the general form of [L5] applied to the multiplicative sets $S_0$ and $B\setminus\mathfrak q_0$ in $B$ therefore gives $$\mathcal O_{X_K,\mathfrak q}\cong B_{\mathfrak q_0}\cong\overline{(B\setminus\mathfrak q_0)}^{-1}\bigl(S_0^{-1}B\bigr),$$ the localization of $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\otimes_kK\cong S_0^{-1}B$ at the multiplicative set generated by the image of $B\setminus\mathfrak q_0$. Every element outside the prime $\mathfrak q_0S_0^{-1}B$ is a fraction $b/s$ with $b\notin\mathfrak q_0$ and $s\in S_0$. Inverting the image of $B\setminus\mathfrak q_0$ makes $b/s$ a unit, with inverse $s/b$. Conversely those images lie outside the extended prime, so the two localizations have the same universal property [L7]. Hence $\mathcal O_{X_K,\mathfrak q}$ is the localization of $\mathcal O_{X,\mathfrak p}\otimes_kK$ at the prime induced by $\mathfrak q_0$, which completes claim 4. [L5, L7, L8, step 2.1, step 5.1, step 5.3]

7.1 Claim 1 is 4.1 and claim 2 is 5.1; claim 3 is 5.2 together with 6.1; claim 4 is 2.1, 5.3 and 6.2. The degenerate case is covered: if $x_i$ is nilpotent in $S$, then $1=0$ in $A_i$, so $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ is empty, and no prime of $S$ avoids $x_i$, so both sides of the bijection in claim 1 are empty, consistently with $D_+(x_i)=\operatorname{Spec}0$. AC is inherited through the affine chart sheaves in [L1]; the algebraic correspondences use no additional choice, and no hypothesis on $I$ beyond homogeneity is needed. The arguments are valid over an arbitrary field $k$. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 5.3, step 6.1, step 6.2, L1] ∎
