## Statement

Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field, let $n\ge1$, and let $\psi:K\to\mathbb C^\times$ be a nontrivial continuous additive character. Put $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$, $N=\{u\in G:u_{ii}=1,\ u_{ij}=0\text{ for }i>j\}$, and $\psi_N(u)=\psi(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i,i+1})$. Let $V$ be a nonzero complex vector space with a $G$-action $\pi$. The action is **smooth** if every vector is fixed by a compact open subgroup of $G$, **admissible** if $V^J=\{v:\pi(j)v=v\text{ for all }j\in J\}$ is finite-dimensional for every compact open subgroup $J$, and **irreducible** if it has no nonzero proper $G$-invariant subspace. A $\psi_N$-equivariant linear form is a complex-linear map $\ell:V\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $\ell(\pi(u)v)=\psi_N(u)\ell(v)$ for all $u\in N$ and $v\in V$. For every irreducible smooth admissible $\pi$, the vector space of such linear forms has dimension at most one. If it is nonzero (the meaning of locally generic here), its dimension is exactly one.

## Proof

**Given:** The stated data and the listed facts.

1.1 A finite continuous invariant-factor stratification. For x in GL_n(K) consider tI-x over K[t]. For each k=1,...,n list its k-minors p_i(t), and designate the leading principal k-minor p_0, which is monic of degree k. In the k-dimensional algebra A_0=K[t]/p_0 form the matrix B_k(x) whose columns are the coefficient vectors of t^j p_i mod p_0,   0<=j<k, for all k-minors p_i. Its entries are POLYNOMIAL in the entries of x: monic polynomial division uses no parameter-dependent denominator. The column span is the ideal generated by the p_i in A_0. If Delta_k is their monic gcd, the quotient by that ideal is K[t]/Delta_k. Thus rank B_k = k-deg Delta_k. This is exactly the actual determinantal-divisor interface. Delta_0=1 and the invariant factors, including unit factors, are Delta_k/Delta_(k-1). Their ordered degrees therefore depend only on this finite rank vector. Conversely the degrees determine that vector. These ranks are invariant under conjugation and transposition, since the ideals of minors are. On each constant-rank stratum Delta_k varies continuously. Here is an explicit verification rather than an assumed continuity of gcd. Put g=deg Delta_k. If g=k, its column ideal is zero and Delta_k=p_0. If g<k, select any independent set of k-g columns of B_k on the open chart where they remain independent. In its column ideal every polynomial of degree<k is a multiple of Delta_k. Projection to coefficients in degrees g,...,k-1 is an isomorphism on this ideal: the multiples Delta_k,t Delta_k,...,t^(k-g-1) Delta_k form a triangular basis with leading coefficient one. Consequently the unique vector of the ideal with coefficient 1 in degree g and 0 in higher degrees is Delta_k. Solve this square linear system in the selected column basis. Its determinant is nonzero; the solution coefficients are rational, hence continuous, on the chart. The finitely many independent-column charts cover the stratum and give the same monic polynomial. Monic division then makes every invariant-factor coefficient continuous. Let R(x)=sum_k rank B_k(x). The sets Z_c={R<=c} are closed: each is a finite union of simultaneous closed rank bounds. They are conjugation- and transpose-stable. There are finitely many c. In a layer Z_c\Z_(c-1), each fixed rank vector is closed and open: intersect its individual closed upper rank bounds with the layer; equality of the sum forces equality of all ranks. These finitely many locally closed pieces are exactly the invariant-factor DEGREE strata X_d. For such a stratum let B_d be the space of tuples of monic polynomials of those degrees, in divisibility order, with total degree n and with the final polynomial having nonzero constant term. It is a closed subspace of an open coefficient affine space: divisibility is the vanishing of the remainder on monic division. In particular it is locally compact Hausdorff and zero-dimensional. The invariant-factor map p:X_d→B_d is continuous by the explicit calculation above. Its fibers are SINGLE K-rational conjugacy orbits by the actual rational canonical form theorem. No separability of the invariant polynomials is assumed, so this includes inseparable polynomials in positive characteristic. The matrix classifications used in this step are [F2, F3]. 2.1 Local sections of the conjugacy orbit map. Fix x and its orbit O_x. For y in O_x solve the finite linear system M x = y M in the n^2 entries of M. Its kernel dimension is constant along the orbit, because y is conjugate to x. Choose a nonzero maximal pivot minor at y=x. Fix the remaining free coordinates of M to their values in I and solve for the pivot coordinates. The remaining equations are redundant since the rank is constant on the orbit. This produces a rational continuous solution M(y) with M(x)=I on a relative neighborhood of x in O_x. After shrinking, det M(y) remains nonzero. Hence y=M(y)xM(y)^-1. Translation gives such sections at every point. Thus G/C_x→O_x is a homeomorphism with local continuous sections, where C_x is the unit group in the finite-dimensional algebra A_x={a:ax=xa}. This also verifies the quotient topology needed for tests. The orbit is locally closed by step 1.1, hence locally compact and zero-dimensional. No inverse-function theorem or global choice of conjugators is used. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Centralizers are unimodular, including positive characteristic. There is a short linear proof avoiding a reductive-centralizer assertion. On gl_n(K) the pairing (u,v)↦tr(uv) is nondegenerate in every characteristic. The tangent quotient T=gl_n/A_x carries the alternating form omega_x(u mod A_x,v mod A_x)=tr(x[u,v]). It is well-defined and nondegenerate: its kernel is exactly the set of u commuting with x, by cyclicity of the finite matrix trace and nondegeneracy of the full trace pairing. Every a in C_x preserves it under conjugation. A linear map preserving a nondegenerate alternating form has determinant one in every characteristic. For completeness, symplectic elimination gives an even-dimensional symplectic basis, and the polynomial identity Pf(P^t Omega P)=det(P)Pf(Omega) forces det(P)=1; this identity has integral coefficients and remains valid in characteristic two. Pf(Omega) is nonzero because det(Omega)=Pf(Omega)^2. Conjugation by a has determinant one on the whole matrix space, since its left and right determinants are (det a)^n and (det a)^(-n). The exact sequence A_x→gl_n→T therefore gives det(Ad(a)|A_x)=1. Here is the corresponding test integration without a Lie-group modular theorem. Choose a finite K-basis of A_x and its additive product integration. For a in the open unit group C_x, put d(a)=det(L_a:A_x→A_x). The density |d(a)|^-1 da defines left-invariant integration on C_x, by the elementary linear change-of-variables formula. Right translation has multiplier |det R_a|/|det L_a|. Their ratio is the absolute determinant of Ad(a), which is one. Hence this integration is also right-invariant. This proves unimodularity. Exactly the same construction for A=M_n(K) gives the usual unimodular test integration on G. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Invariant orbital integration and transposition. For a closed unimodular $C_x$ with the local sections of step 2.1, the map $P:C_c^\infty(G)\to C_c^\infty(G/C_x)$, $P(\phi)(gC_x)=\int_{C_x}\phi(gc)\,dc$, is onto. On any section chart, lift a quotient test by multiplying it with a compact $C_x$-test of integral one; a finite compact-open partition handles its support. Its kernel is generated by right $C_x$-translation differences, as follows. Use finitely many section charts over the compact support and refine to a finite compact-open partition; in each product chart $U\times C_x$, a test is a finite sum of rectangles $1_A(y)\phi(c)$. If its orbit integral is zero, each fiber function has integral zero. Choose a compact-open subgroup $J\subset C_x$ that left-stabilizes the locally constant compactly supported function $\phi$; write $\phi=\sum_c a_c1_{Jc}$. Since the integral is zero, $\sum_c a_c=0$, so after fixing one coset $Jc_0$ we have $\phi=\sum_{c\ne c_0}a_c(1_{Jc}-1_{Jc_0})$, a finite sum of right-translation differences of $1_J$. The same identity in each rectangle proves the kernel claim. Such differences have zero $G$-integral because $G$ is unimodular. Therefore $G$-integration factors through $P$ to a positive nonzero invariant integration $\nu_x$ on the orbit. Every invariant linear functional on the orbit is a scalar multiple of $\nu_x$: its pullback by $P$ is left-invariant integration on $G$, and the latter space is one-dimensional by the finite-coset test argument. This uniqueness proof does not invoke a general invariant-measure existence theorem. Every matrix is similar to its transpose. This follows explicitly from rational canonical form: on $K[t]/f$ of degree $d$, the bilinear form extracting the coefficient of $t^{d-1}$ from $ab\bmod f$ is symmetric and nondegenerate, and multiplication by $t$ is self-adjoint. Nondegeneracy follows by multiplying a nonzero polynomial of degree $j$ by $t^{d-1-j}$. Direct sum over companion blocks gives an invertible symmetric intertwining matrix. Hence transposition preserves each orbit. It conjugates the $G$-action by the automorphism $g\mapsto g^{-\mathsf T}$, so the pushforward of $\nu_x$ is again positive invariant integration. Its scalar multiplier is positive and squares to one, since transposition is an involution; hence it equals one. Thus transposition acts as the identity on the conjugation-coinvariant test space of each orbit. Here is the fiberwise localization argument needed for $X_d\to B_d$. More generally, let $p:X\to B$ be a continuous map of locally compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional spaces, and suppose a group $H$ acts on $X$ with $p(hx)=p(x)$. Restriction $C_c^\infty(X)\to C_c^\infty(Y)$ is onto for closed $Y$: write a test on $Y$ as a finite linear combination of indicators of compact-open value sets; cover each such compact set by finitely many compact-open neighborhoods in $X$ disjoint from the closed complement in $Y$, and disjointify the resulting finite cover. Now let a test $f$ restrict to zero in the twisted coinvariants on each fiber. For fixed $b$, express $f|_{X_b}$ as a finite sum of twisted relations. Extend the finitely many fiber tests to $X$ by the restriction statement. The error $e$ is compactly supported and zero on $X_b$; since it is locally constant, its support misses $X_b$. Thus the compact set $p(\operatorname{supp}e)$ is closed and misses $b$. Choose a compact-open neighborhood $W_b$ disjoint from it. Multiplication by $1_{p^{-1}(W_b)}$ preserves twisted relations because $p$ is $H$-invariant, so the class of $f$ vanishes over $W_b$. Finitely many such neighborhoods cover the compact set $p(\operatorname{supp}f)$; refine them into a finite disjoint compact-open partition there and add the corresponding localized relations. This proves that $f$ has zero global twisted-coinvariant class, with no exactness assumption on the infinite coinvariant functor. Apply this argument to $f-f^{\mathsf T}$: its class vanishes on each orbit fiber by the preceding orbital calculation, hence it vanishes on $X_d$. To globalize, take a conjugation-invariant linear functional $D$ on $C_c^\infty(G)$ and form its transpose-anti-invariant part $A=D-D^{\mathsf T}$. On the top open rank layer, every component $X_d$ has zero restriction of $A$. Therefore $A$ is supported on the remaining closed $Z_c$ and factors through its test space, by the closed-set extension argument just given. Repeat down the finite closed rank filtration. All restrictions vanish, so $A=0$. We have proved that every conjugation-invariant linear functional on $C_c^\infty(\mathrm{GL}_n(K))$ is transpose-invariant. No distribution regularity on regular semisimple elements, density argument about singular support, or characteristic-zero field assumption is used. [step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Elementary admissible smooth duality. Let (pi,V) be irreducible smooth admissible. Define V^vee as the smooth vectors in the algebraic dual, with pi^vee(g)ell(v)=ell(pi(g^-1)v). For any compact-open J, normalized finite averaging e_J gives (V^vee)^J = (V^J)^*. Every functional on the finite-dimensional V^J extends by composition with e_J; conversely a J-fixed functional equals its composition with e_J. Thus the pairing separates V and the smooth double-dual is V: check the finite bidual on each J-fixed space and use smoothness. These are explicit finite-dimensional statements, not arbitrary Hahn–Banach extensions. The smooth dual is irreducible. A nonzero submodule W of V^vee has zero annihilator in V, by irreducibility of V. For each J, W^J separates V^J: if v in V^J is annihilated by W^J, averaging any ell in W over J shows ell(v)=0. Since V^J is finite-dimensional, W^J=(V^J)^*. Hence W=V^vee. This also proves admissibility of V^vee. For a compact test f, pi(f) has finite rank: choose J making f bi-J-invariant, so pi(f)=e_J pi(f)e_J has image in finite-dimensional V^J. Its algebraic trace defines a conjugation-invariant functional Theta_pi(f). Finite-rank trace is invariant under similarity, which proves the asserted invariance directly. Smooth duality and finite J-biduality give Theta_(pi^vee)(f)=Theta_pi(f composed with inversion). Both inversion and transposition preserve G-integration. Indeed they interchange left/right integration, and normalization on a fixed compact-open subgroup identifies their possible scalar with one. Set iota(g)=g^(-t). step 4.1 therefore gives Theta_(pi composed with iota) = Theta_(pi^vee). [step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 These traces really determine the admissible irreducible representation. Fix J with V^J nonzero and let A_J be the unital convolution algebra of compact bi-J tests, normalized by vol(J)=1. The A_J-module V^J is irreducible. A nonzero stable subspace generates V under G; averaging the finite linear combinations of these translates back to V^J shows it was all V^J. If two admissible irreducibles have identical trace functionals, the traces of e_J show their J-fixed dimensions agree and are nonzero. Their finite-dimensional simple A_J-modules have equal traces on A_J. Put B equal to the finite-dimensional image of A_J on their direct sum. The faithful module M=V^J⊕W^J is a finite direct sum of simple B-modules. Every submodule of a finite-dimensional semisimple module is semisimple: choose a nonzero submodule of smallest positive dimension, project it nontrivially onto a simple summand, and split it by that projection; finite induction finishes. If m_1,...,m_d is a complex basis of M, the map b↦(bm_1,...,bm_d) embeds the left regular module B in M^d. Thus B is a semisimple ring. The actual Wedderburn–Artin theorem gives a finite product of matrix algebras over division algebras. Each finite-dimensional complex division algebra is C: every element has a complex eigenvalue, and the resulting noninvertible difference from that scalar must vanish. The two simple B-modules V^J and W^J correspond to factors. Equality of traces for all b∈B holds because B is the image of A_J. In particular the central identity of each factor has equal trace on both modules; its trace is the positive dimension of the module on that factor and zero on the others. Hence the two modules belong to the same factor and are isomorphic as A_J-modules. This gives the finite character-comparison argument locally, without depending on the later Faltings page. Finally such a nonzero simple A_J-module U determines the irreducible G-representation uniquely. Form M=C_c^infinity(G)e_J tensor_(A_J) U. It is generated by its J-fixed part, and M^J=A_J tensor U=U. Every proper submodule has zero J-fixed part; if it had the whole U it would contain the generators. The sum of ALL proper submodules still has zero J-fixed part, since each vector in that sum is a finite sum and e_J preserves every submodule. It is therefore proper and is the unique maximal proper submodule. Consequently M has a unique irreducible quotient, and every irreducible with J-fixed module U is that quotient. No Zorn lemma is used. Thus identical trace functionals imply isomorphism. In particular pi^vee is isomorphic to pi composed with iota. Conjugating by w0 also gives an isomorphism I:V^vee→V with I pi^vee(g)=pi(alpha(g)) I, alpha(g)=w0 g^(-t) w0=sigma(g)^-1. The semisimple-ring decomposition used here is [F4]. 7.1 Finite Hecke density and generalized coefficients. We record the exact finite-dimensional algebra fact needed next. The image B of A_J on V^J is all End_C(V^J). Its action is faithful simple. Embed its left regular module in a finite direct sum of copies of V^J by evaluating on a finite basis. The finite-dimensional semisimple-submodule argument proved in the preceding step makes that regular module semisimple. Apply the actual published Wedderburn–Artin theorem. Faithful simplicity leaves one matrix factor; its division algebra is finite-dimensional over C and equals C, since every element has a complex eigenvalue and a nonzero nonunit in a division ring is impossible. Hence the image is the full matrix algebra. This is finite algebra, not an unproved topological density theorem. Let C=I^-1 and define b(v,w)=(Cv)(w). It is nondegenerate and satisfies b(pi(g)v,w)=b(v,pi(sigma(g))w). The transpose form satisfies the same relation. Schur's scalar conclusion holds for V: restrict a G-endomorphism to a nonzero finite-dimensional simple V^J, where an eigenvalue proves scalarity, and use that V^J generates V. Therefore b^t=epsilon b with epsilon=+1 or -1. Take the cofinal principal-congruence subgroups J of GL_n(O); they are stable under alpha and sigma. The restriction of b to V^J is nondegenerate. For arbitrary linear functionals lambda,lambda' on V, let v_(lambda',J) in V^J be defined by b(z,v_(lambda',J))=lambda'(z), z in V^J. For a bi-J test f set D_(lambda,lambda')(f)=lambda(pi(f)v_(lambda',J)). This is independent of sufficiently small such J. Averaging e_J is self-adjoint for b because sigma preserves J and its normalized integration; hence e_J v_(lambda',J')=v_(lambda',J) when J' is smaller. A common smaller congruence subgroup handles sums of tests, so D is linear. The tensor map lambda tensor lambda'↦D_(lambda,lambda') is injective on every finite-dimensional tensor subspace of V* tensor V*. Indeed choose J small enough that both finite sets of independent functionals remain independent on V^J, using finitely many testing vectors. The vectors v_(lambda',J) are then independent. the full matrix image just established lets pi(f), for bi-J f, be any endomorphism of V^J. Rank-one matrix evaluation separates every nonzero tensor. This proves injectivity without an infinite-dimensional kernel theorem. For clarity about transformations, lambda' composed with I is a functional mu on V^vee. Its restriction to (V^vee)^J is represented by v_(lambda',J) under finite biduality. Thus D is the well-defined generalized coefficient lambda(pi(f)mu); this notation means exactly the preceding finite-rank definition, not an integral of an undefined distribution vector. 8.1 The bi-Whittaker theorem now gives actual uniqueness. Suppose lambda and lambda' are psi_N-Whittaker functionals on V. Since alpha preserves N and sends its pinned character to its inverse, mu=lambda' composed with I is a psi_N^-1 functional on V^vee. Direct left/right translation of the finite generalized coefficient gives D(L_(u,v) f)=psi_N(u)psi_N(v)^-1 D(f). For example left translation inserts pi(u), giving psi_N(u); right test translation by v inserts pi(v^-1) on the generalized vector mu, giving psi_N(v)^-1. These identities can equally be checked after choosing a common smaller congruence subgroup and using finite biduality. The already complete p-adic bi-Whittaker distribution theorem applies. The b-adjoint of pi(f) is pi(f composed with sigma), since this holds on each group operator and sigma preserves integration. On each finite J-fixed space, writing b with matrix S, one has S^t=epsilon S and D_(lambda,lambda')(f composed with sigma) = epsilon D_(lambda',lambda)(f). This is the finite matrix identity lambda S^-1 A^t (lambda')^t = epsilon lambda' A S^-1 lambda^t. The distribution theorem therefore says D_(lambda,lambda')=epsilon D_(lambda',lambda). If lambda is any nonzero Whittaker functional, tensor injectivity makes D_(lambda,lambda) nonzero, forcing epsilon=1. Tensor injectivity then yields lambda tensor lambda' = lambda' tensor lambda for every pair of Whittaker functionals. Two independent functionals have a nonzero alternating tensor, as tested on two vectors. They therefore cannot be independent. The Whittaker space has dimension at most one, and under the original genericity premise it has dimension EXACTLY one. This proves the entire NONARCHIMEDEAN clause of the original local theorem, in both characteristics and at its unqualified choice scope. It does not assert the real/complex clause. Every smoothing and separation operation in this branch was a finite compact average or finite-dimensional Hecke operation; none uses an archimedean Casselman–Wallach duality theorem.
