## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a complete globally weak pointed Heegaard diagram, its surface complex structure j and positive area form, and its smooth symmetric-product target. Let $J^-_t,J^+_t$ be two allowed generic hat paths for that fixed background, tame for this same form off their protected symmetric neighborhoods, with actual time-constant endpoint collars. Their nonconstant zero-basepoint strips are regular at all actual indices. Their product-integrable generator normal forms, if supplied, need not agree; no such normal form is required at either endpoint. Full tensor values on the two boundary tori may also differ.

There are actual finite zero-basepoint anchored continuation chain maps between their hat complexes and actual finite inverse chain homotopies. The coefficient profile is compactly source-dependent and stationary at its two source ends. Generic relative one-parameter profiles give the actual chain homotopies, and separated-transition gluing gives actual composition. No quotient by translation is taken on an anchored central solution. The constant-profile continuation counts the identity.

This removes the shared-generator prescription from the fixed-background comparison, including optional generator charts and arbitrary transverse complex angles. It is not an independence assertion outside the stated common-background and generic endpoint hypotheses. Rank zero has the direct point-target comparison.

## Proof

1.1 Keep a common smaller neighborhood of all protected divisors on which both paths equal $\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$. Interpolate elsewhere using tame Cayley matrices relative to one compatible reference; their norm-less-than-one condition is convex. Take a smooth compact source transition stationary at its ends, and choose a common smaller time collar from the two endpoint paths. This gives actual profiles $J_{s,t}$ preserving the protected geometry, the two physical boundary tori and the common taming form. They may have different full tensors along those tori and different generator jets. Compact families of such transitions have uniform finite derivative and tameness bounds. The allowed generic perturbations below are away from the protected divisors and boundary tori, retaining each chosen boundary coefficient family. [F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Prove the required generator inverse independently of constant complex coordinates. Fix transverse Lagrangian planes $L_0,L_1$ in a symplectic real vector space $(V,\omega_0)$. Let $K(s,t)$ be ANY uniformly tame smooth complex-matrix field with the bounds of step 1.1. On the actual $H^1$ real-boundary space put $D_Kv=v_s+Kv_t$. For compactly supported smooth boundary-compatible v, Stokes gives $\int\omega_0(v_s,v_t)=0$: on each horizontal edge both v and its s derivative lie in the same Lagrangian plane, and the vertical ends vanish. The identity $v_s=D_Kv-Kv_t$ then implies $$c\|v_t\|_2^2\le C\|D_Kv\|_2\|v_t\|_2.$$ Thus $\|v_t\|_2+\|v_s\|_2\le C\|D_Kv\|_2$. Transversality also gives $\|v\|_2\le C\|v_t\|_2$: for each t path, $v(0)\in L_0,v(1)\in L_1$, and its endpoint difference bounds both endpoints by the inverse of $L_0\oplus L_1\to V$. Integration of its derivative proves the assertion. Density extends the full $H^1$ coercivity estimate to the actual domain; real-trace approximation follows by finite boundary flattenings and reflection as in [F3]. In particular the kernel is zero. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Justify the boundary estimates for the ACTUAL source-varying torus tensors. On any bounded source boundary patch let a denote its finite profile parameters and z its source coordinate. Introduce complex coordinates for a, restricting their imaginary parts to zero, and retain z as one additional complex coordinate. The block tensor $\widehat J=i\oplus J_{a,z}(u)$ is a SINGLE smooth tensor on this enlarged local target. The map $z\mapsto(a,z,u(z))$ is holomorphic exactly when $u_s+J_{a,z}(u)u_t=0$. Its boundary lies on $\mathbb R^p\times\mathbb R\times L_e$ in that patch. This submanifold is totally real: if the image under $\widehat J$ of a tangent vector is again tangent, its parameter and source components vanish first, then $Jv\in TL_e$ forces v zero by tameness and the Lagrangian property. The product area form on the base plus the actual target form tames the block tensor uniformly on a smaller compact patch. Use finitely many charts; coefficients extend to slightly larger compact boxes using the same Cayley cutoff, so estimates use genuine open neighborhoods. The fixed-target real-boundary estimates of [F3] therefore apply with uniform constants, even though the original tensor varies along the source boundary. Equivalently [F2]'s ONE-torus frame, with source coordinates as parameters, fixes that boundary value; disjoint source collars treat the two edges, without trying to normalize both planes by one derivative at their intersection. Base derivatives on a source disk of radius r have energy $O(r^2)$ and vanish after bubbling rescale. Thus every bubble is vertical and has precisely the original frozen target tensor and physical boundary condition. For a fixed small window the graph estimate first puts a sufficiently small-energy map in one small physical target chart. Subtract a constant in that chart (on its real plane in a boundary chart). The physical equation has no additive forcing at a constant. Real boundary Poincare after subtracting the tangential mean, followed by the local linear estimate with its bounded source-dependent real frame, gives an interior derivative bound $\|du\|_{C^k}\le C_k\|du\|_2$ on a smaller window; absorb its small spatial coefficient difference and bootstrap. Thus physical derivatives tend to zero when physical window energy does. No graph base-area term is used on long strips. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Homotope K through uniformly tame matrix fields to a constant compatible tensor making these two planes transverse standard real planes. The finite Cayley contraction has common bounds. The known operator [F1] is onto. Onto-ness is open by Neumann and closed by the estimate of step 2.1: if $D_nu_n=f$ and its coefficients converge uniformly, then $\|u_n-u_l\|_{H^1}\le C\|(D_l-D_n)u_l\|_2\to0$, so its actual $H^1$ limit solves the limiting equation. This proves a bounded inverse for EVERY such K, uniformly on compact profile families. No zero-order term occurs at a constant generator x in the physical equation: $dF_x\xi=\xi_s+J_{s,t}(x)\xi_t$ because $u_t=0$. A smooth weight $e^{\delta\rho(s)}$, $|\rho'|\le1$, changes this operator by multiplication by $-\delta\rho'I$. Choose $\delta$ smaller than half the uniform inverse bound reciprocal; Neumann gives its weighted inverse. Tangential s differences and the equation for t derivatives give all integer higher realizations on nested boundary charts, with only the zeroth real trace condition. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The same argument covers sequences of convergent smooth coefficients, not just an initially supplied finite family. Pass to a subsequence whose first n seminorm errors are below $2^{-n^2}$. Such a subsequence is specified by the first integer satisfying each finite bound. Put its parameters at $2^{-n}$ and interpolate the coefficient differences with disjoint smooth bumps of width comparable to $2^{-n}$. Their fixed-order derivatives tend to zero at zero. This makes a smooth finite-parameter Cayley family through the subsequence and its limit, with uniform tameness and the same boundary/protected conditions. Applying step 2.2 to that family supplies the local estimates and bubble extraction used for all compactness sequences below. This is an explicit recursion on integers with definable least choices, not dependent choice. [F2, F3, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Supply the nonlinear tail and neck estimate, rather than asserting a Fourier conclusion for nonconstant coefficients. In a fixed smooth generator chart straighten the two physical tori to $L_0,L_1$, and freeze the actual symplectic form at x. Both planes are Lagrangian for this constant form. Shrinking the chart makes all the actual tensors uniformly tame for it. Write the map as v with $|v|\le r$. Its equation is $$D_{J(x)}v=-\bigl(J(s,t,v)-J(s,t,x)\bigr)v_t.$$ The coefficient difference is at most Cr. The t-path Poincare estimate in step 2.1 makes v actually $L^2$ on any tail or neck of finite physical energy; its weak derivatives have the same property. Apply the whole-strip inverse to a cutoff $\chi v$. For a tail multiply first by the bounded truncated weight $w_N=\exp(\delta\min(\rho,N))$, smoothed with derivative at most $\delta w_N$. The resulting inequality bounds its $H^1$ norm by the first cutoff-window norm plus $C(r+\delta)$ times itself. Choose r and then delta so the latter is absorbed, uniformly in N. Monotone exhaustion gives a genuine exponentially weighted tail bound. The boundary estimates of step 2.2 and the equation then bootstrap every finite derivative with the same smaller exponential rate. On a long neck use cutoffs at its two ends; the two weighted estimates give exponential decay into its middle. The linearization differs from the frozen inverse by a first-order coefficient $O(|v|)$ and a zero-order coefficient $O(|v_t|)$. Their $H^3\to H^2$ norms tend to zero on the middle neck by these actual higher bounds. This accounts for full boundary jets and nonlinear spatial variation; it does not assume $J(u)=J(x)$ on a neighborhood. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 The actual Hilbert section is the same $H^3$ map section with zeroth real boundary trace in [F3]; its nonlinear coefficient map is smooth by its proved product estimates. On source ends use the frozen inverse in step 3.1 and absorb the small asymptotic coefficients as in step 4.1. On the remaining compact source use the local interior and real-boundary parametrices from [F3]. Finite patching has compact error by its finite-cell Sobolev approximation, giving the actual Fredholm section. Its class index is retained under these tame generator homotopies: the asymptotic models stay invertible by step 3.1, and the compact-core determinant caps change continuously without a zero or a winding jump. Constant generators are regular of index zero by step 3.1. For nonconstant central solutions, the positive-degree free-image argument in [F3] supplies an open free interior site. Source-local anti-J bumps kill every nonzero smooth adjoint obstruction there; their supports avoid both physical boundary tori, so change none of the chosen boundary family. Its argument uses neither a source-independent boundary tensor nor a fixed generator jet. Countably many map charts and index strata give genuine all-index regular compact-transition profiles and relative finite-parameter families. At the source ends use the stipulated actual all-index regular endpoint data. [F1, F3, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Compactness remains a physical energy assertion. For the original unforced equation use [F4]'s physical degree-area energy estimate. Away from the protected set it is the tameness estimate for the descended product form; on that set its actual ordered-root proof applies because the structure is the unchanged symmetric one. Thus energy is uniformly bounded on ALL zero-mark domains, unchanged by source dependence elsewhere. Apply its interior concentration proof and step 2.2 on each boundary patch. Rescaled bubbles are vertical; degree additivity and the zero-mark one-system condition make every compact bubble constant, including contained-divisor bubbles. On a long gap without an energy concentration, step 2.2 makes the physical derivatives small on each inner unit window. Its two boundary values are correspondingly close. Off any fixed neighborhoods of the finite transverse intersection set the two compact tori have positive separation, so each whole window lies near one generator. Overlapping windows and their connected image select the SAME generator throughout the gap. This proves the required uniform image collapse; otherwise there is a positive-energy window. Apply the same argument to the escaping tails. Step 4.1 then gives the actual higher neck and tail collapse. Consequently the complete limit is the anchored central solution with autonomous end strips, or the two anchored solutions for a separated-transition limit. The given end strips are all-index regular and have nonzero translation kernel, so every nonconstant one has index at least one. Homotope each matching frozen generator operator through the invertible fields of step 3.1 to its compatible constant model. This preserves Fredholm index on both sides and introduces no seam index. The actual common-cap excision in [F1] therefore gives unconditional index addition BEFORE component regularity is used. Hence dimension zero has one rigid anchored solution, and dimension one has precisely the rigid central/end pair or prescribed source-parameter endpoint. No unexamined negative-index strip or varying-boundary bubble is omitted. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Give those limits actual collars and exhaustion. Use [F5]'s retained compact cores and squared cutoffs; on every middle neck its exact transverse inverse is now the actual inverse of step 3.1. The cutoff commutator is $O(1/T)$, while the coefficient and zero-order errors tend to zero by step 4.1. Thus the patched right inverse has error tending to zero, and its Neumann inverse is genuine. The preglue residual is exponentially small by step 4.1; the same actual $H^3\to H^2$ quadratic estimates give a unique Newton correction on its complement. Cut a convergent nodal sequence into the retained cores. The cutoff inverse of step 4.1 upgrades its local convergence and collapsing neck to global $H^3$ distance from that preglue; the compact-core phase conditions and the actual kernel matching in [F5] then put it in that unique zero chart. Distinct positive-energy cores and the anchored source profiles distinguish collar endpoints exactly as in [F5]. Thus these are injective exhaustive collars, with no assumed count bijection. This replaces EACH exact-generator use in the existing gluing and exhaustion proof. [F1, F3, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 The actual zero-dimensional total sets are finite by discreteness and the compactness in step 6.1; use ordered finite tuples and flatten them for the Countable-Choice sequence argument in [F5]. Add their finitely many broken collars from step 7.1. The same actual core and source tests separate points, so the enlarged one-dimensional set is Hausdorff and second-countable; sequential compactness makes it compact. Its finite interval boundary parity gives the chain-map and relative one-parameter homotopy identities, with every central solution anchored. For two separated transitions the actual long-neck collar in step 7.1 gives one solution for each rigid pair; compactness excludes all others. This proves composition for the finite actual matrices, not a formal continuation axiom. [F4, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Concatenate a profile and its reverse. Its literal fixed-background Cayley contraction to the constant profile preserves the protected geometry, common form and actual source ends; no target holonomy is introduced. Apply step 8.1's relative family to obtain the inverse chain homotopy. Each constant generator has exactly the invertible index-zero operator of step 3.1 and contributes one. Thus this profile counts the identity, and the two continuation maps have actual inverse homotopies. All given generator-chart prescriptions have been left free during the comparison. This proves the stated operation for independently chosen generator data, rather than extending a fixed-chart Statement by omission. [F1, F3, F5, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra] ∎
