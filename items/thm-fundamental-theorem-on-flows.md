## Statement

Let $X$ be a smooth vector field on $M$. For each $p\in M$, let
$\gamma_p:I_p\to M$ be the maximal integral curve through $p$, and set

$$ \mathcal D:=\{(t,p)\in\mathbb R\times M:t\in I_p\}, \qquad \Phi(t,p):=\gamma_p(t). $$

Then $\mathcal D$ is open in $\mathbb R\times M$, each fibre $\mathcal D_p$ is
an interval containing $0$, the map $\Phi:\mathcal D\to M$ is smooth, and
$\Phi$ is the unique maximal local flow generated by $X$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth vector field $X$ on $M$.

[L1] Every point lies on a unique maximal integral curve ([[thm-unique-maximal-integral-curve-through-each-point]]).

[L2] Integral curves exist uniquely on uniform local time intervals and depend smoothly on the initial point ([[thm-local-existence-uniqueness-and-smooth-dependence-for-manifold-integral-curves]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], for each $p\in M$ there is a unique maximal integral curve $\gamma_p:I_p\to M$ through $p$. Therefore the set $\mathcal D$ and the map $\Phi(t,p)=\gamma_p(t)$ are well defined, each fibre $\mathcal D_p=I_p$ is an open interval containing $0$, and $\Phi(0,p)=p$. [L1, given]

1.2 Fix $p\in M$ and $s\in I_p$, and put $q:=\Phi(s,p)=\gamma_p(s)$. Then the translated curve $t\mapsto \gamma_p(t+s)$ is an integral curve through $q$ on the interval $I_p-s:=\{t:t+s\in I_p\}$. By uniqueness of maximal integral curves, it agrees with $\gamma_q$ on their common domain, so $$\Phi(t,\Phi(s,p))=\Phi(t+s,p)$$ whenever both sides are defined. The same translation argument applied to $\gamma_q$ shows $I_q=I_p-s$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 Let $W\subseteq\mathcal D$ be the set of all $(t,p)\in\mathcal D$ such that $\Phi$ is defined and smooth on some product neighbourhood $J\times U\subseteq\mathcal D$ of $(t,p)$. By [L2], every $(0,p)$ lies in $W$. Suppose $W\ne\mathcal D$. Choose $(\tau,p_0)\in\mathcal D\setminus W$; replacing $\tau$ by $-\tau$ if needed, we may assume $\tau>0$. Let $$t_0:=\inf\{t\in\mathbb R:(t,p_0)\notin W\}.$$ Then $t_0\in I_{p_0}$, because $(0,p_0)\in W$ and $I_{p_0}$ is an open interval containing $\tau$. Put $q_0:=\Phi(t_0,p_0)$. Applying [L2] at $q_0$ gives $\varepsilon>0$ and an open neighbourhood $U_0$ of $q_0$ such that the local flow is smooth on $(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)\times U_0$. Choose $t_1<t_0$ with $t_1+\varepsilon>t_0$ and $\Phi(t_1,p_0)\in U_0$. Because $(t_1,p_0)\in W$, there is a product neighbourhood $(t_1-\delta,t_1+\delta)\times U_1\subseteq W$; shrinking $U_1$ if necessary, we may assume $\Phi(\{t_1\}\times U_1)\subseteq U_0$. [L2, step 1.1, step 1.2, choose, assume-contra]

3.1 Define $$\widetilde\Phi(t,p):=\begin{cases}\Phi(t,p),&0\le t<t_1,\\ \Psi(t-t_1,\Phi(t_1,p)),&t_1-\varepsilon<t<t_1+\varepsilon,\end{cases}$$ where $\Psi$ is the local flow from step 2.1. By step 1.2, the two formulas agree on the overlap, so $\widetilde\Phi$ is a smooth extension of $\Phi$ to a product neighbourhood of $(t_0,p_0)$. This contradicts the choice of $t_0$. Therefore $W=\mathcal D$, so $\mathcal D$ is open and $\Phi$ is smooth. [step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, discharge-contradiction]

4.1 Step 3.1 makes each slice $\mathcal D_t:=\{p:(t,p)\in\mathcal D\}$ open in $M$. For $p\in\mathcal D_t$, step 1.2 gives $I_{\Phi(t,p)}=I_p-t$, so $-t\in I_{\Phi(t,p)}$ and hence $\Phi(t,p)\in\mathcal D_{-t}$. The same step also yields $$\Phi(-t,\Phi(t,p))=\Phi(0,p)=p,$$ and symmetrically $\Phi(t,\Phi(-t,q))=q$ for $q\in\mathcal D_{-t}$. Thus $\Phi_t:\mathcal D_t\to \mathcal D_{-t}$ is a diffeomorphism with inverse $\Phi_{-t}$. [step 1.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 1.1-4.1 show that $\Phi:\mathcal D\to M$ is a smooth local flow whose time slices are exactly the maximal integral curves of $X$. Any other local flow of $X$ has the same time slices by uniqueness of integral curves, so its domain is contained in $\mathcal D$ and its map agrees with $\Phi$. Therefore $\Phi$ is the unique maximal local flow generated by $X$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
