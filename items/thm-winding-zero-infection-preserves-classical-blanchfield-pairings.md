## Statement

Let $P\cup\eta\subset S^3$ be a smooth oriented two-component link with unknotted axis and $\operatorname{lk}(P,\eta)=0$. Supply the disjoint closed product tubes, positive meridians and preferred zero-linking longitudes. For a smooth oriented companion knot $K$, form the untwisted infection $P(K)$ and the standard infection cobordism $E$ with oriented boundary $M(P)\sqcup M(K)\sqcup(-M(P(K)))$, where $M$ denotes the actual preferred zero-longitude surgery.

Let $k$ be any commutative field, $\Lambda=k[t,t^{-1}]$ with involution $\bar t=t^{-1}$, and $F=k(t)$. Use the coefficient system on $E$ sending the positive meridian of $P$ to $t$, and its restrictions to the boundary, with the standard based Hermitian form. On $M(P)$ and $M(P(K))$ these are their positive classical infinite-cyclic systems. The two inclusions give isomorphisms
$$H_1(M(P);\Lambda)\xrightarrow{i_P}H_1(E;\Lambda)\xleftarrow{i_{P(K)}}H_1(M(P(K));\Lambda).$$
Consequently $T=i_{P(K)}^{-1}i_P$ is an isometry of their classical Blanchfield pairings:
$$B_{P(K)}(Tx,Ty)=B_P(x,y).$$
With base paths through the common pattern exterior fixed as part of the peripheral data, $T[\eta]=[\mu_K]$, where the companion meridian on the infected side is oriented by the prescribed longitude-to-meridian identification. Thus a nonzero axis self-pairing remains nonzero on this companion-meridian class. For another base path the corresponding equality is conjugated by the induced power of $t$, which preserves its self-pairing. No choice axiom or finite CW model for the surgery manifolds is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite smooth infection data, zero winding number, field and based coefficient systems in the Statement.

[F1] The actual collared standard infection cobordism has ordinary $H_1(E;k)=k$, with meridians of $P,K,P(K)$ mapping to $1,0,1$ in the winding-zero case. Its incoming components generate $H_2(E;k)$, and every knot zero surgery has first Betti number one over $k$. Its product construction retracts onto $M(P)\cup_{N(\eta)=V_K}M(K)$, where $V_K$ is the filling solid torus. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]]

[F2] For a compact oriented topological four-manifold with Betti-one boundary components, PTFA fraction-field boundary-intersection rank is at most ordinary rank. A nontrivial PTFA coefficient system on a compact Betti-one manifold has zero fraction-field first homology. [[thm-ptfa-localized-boundary-intersection-rank-is-no-larger-than-ordinary-rank]], [[lem-ptfa-localization-kills-first-homology-of-betti-one-manifolds]]

[F3] If localized second homology of a compact collared four-manifold is generated by its entire boundary, the linking-form kernel on any finite union of localized-first-acyclic boundary components is isotropic. The form uses inverse Bockstein, oriented cap duality and evaluation. [[lem-zero-localized-intersection-rank-forces-finite-boundary-union-kernel-isotropy]]

[F4] Product-collared gluing has the local-coefficient Mayer–Vietoris and pair exact sequences, including excision. [[thm-excision-and-mayer-vietoris-with-local-coefficients]], [[thm-pair-long-exact-sequences-with-local-coefficients]]

## Proof

1.1 Use the actual product gluing from [F1]. Put $S=N(\eta)=V_K$, $T_0=\partial S$, $A=M(P)\setminus\operatorname{int}N(\eta)$ and $X_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$. Its spine is simultaneously $Z=M(P)\cup_S M(K)$ and $Z=(A\cup_{T_0}X_K)\cup_{T_0}S=M(P(K))\cup_{T_0}S$. The product-coordinate deformation to this spine fixes the remaining bottom faces $A$ and $X_K$; the rounded torus collars identify their union with the outgoing boundary. Hence it gives an equivalence of pairs $(E,M(P(K)))\simeq(Z,M(P(K)))$. Excision in these collars identifies its relative homology with $H_*(S,T_0)$. These are actual subspaces and product homotopies, rather than an inference from ordinary homology. [F1, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ordinary generator of $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ defines the specified infinite-cyclic system; the same integral peripheral computation in [F1] shows that it sends $\mu_P$ and $\mu_{P(K)}$ to $t$ and $\mu_K$ to $1$. Its restriction to $S$ is trivial, since the core represents the zero-winding axis. It is also trivial on $M(K)$ and $X_K$: their ordinary first homology is generated by $\mu_K$, so their map to $\mathbb Z$ is zero. Trivial coefficient groups on these pieces are their ordinary $k$ homology tensored with $\Lambda$. This assertion does not require an infinite vector-space splitting: the singular chain complex with trivial coefficients is its $k$ complex tensored with the free $k$ module $\Lambda=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}kt^n$; cycles, boundaries and exactness commute with this explicit direct sum. In particular $H_1(S;\Lambda)=\Lambda$, $H_1(M(K);\Lambda)=\Lambda[\mu_K]$, and the core inclusion into $M(K)$ is this generator. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Mayer–Vietoris for the first description of $Z$ gives the degree-one difference map $H_1(S;\Lambda)\to H_1(M(P);\Lambda)\oplus H_1(M(K);\Lambda)$ as $a\mapsto([\eta]a,-[\mu_K]a)$. It is injective because its second coordinate is minus the identity of $\Lambda$. The following degree-zero difference map is also injective: it is $\Lambda\to\Lambda/(t-1)\oplus\Lambda$, $a\mapsto(a\bmod(t-1),-a)$. Exactness therefore identifies $H_1(E;\Lambda)$ with the quotient of $H_1(M(P);\Lambda)\oplus\Lambda$ by the displayed graph. The first summand maps isomorphically onto that quotient; explicitly $(x,a)$ maps to $x+[\eta]a$. In particular the core of $S$, equivalently the companion meridian, maps to the image of $[\eta]$ under this isomorphism. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The group $\mathbb Z$ is PTFA, its $k$ group ring is the stated $\Lambda$, and its fraction division ring is $F$. All three boundary components of $E$ have ordinary first Betti number one. The ordinary boundary-cokernel rank is zero by the incoming second-homology generation in [F1]. Applying the full rank comparison in [F2] therefore gives $H_2(E;F)=\operatorname{im}H_2(\partial E;F)$. On $M(P)$ and $M(P(K))$ the restricted infinite-cyclic maps are nontrivial and their first Betti numbers are one, so the other assertion in [F2] gives $H_1(-;F)=0$ there. No vanishing on $M(K)$ is claimed or needed. Consequently [F3] applies to the selected two-component oriented boundary $N=M(P)\sqcup(-M(P(K)))$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 In the pair from step 1.1, the trivial coefficient relative solid-torus calculation gives $H_1(S,T_0;\Lambda)=0$ and $H_2(S,T_0;\Lambda)=\Lambda$, generated by a meridian disk. Indeed $H_1(T_0;\Lambda)\to H_1(S;\Lambda)$ is onto with kernel the meridian summand, and the degree-zero inclusion is an isomorphism. Under the gluing, the meridian boundary of that disk is $\mu_\eta=\lambda_K$. This longitude represents zero in $H_1(X_K;\Lambda)$: the coefficients on $X_K$ are trivial, and the preferred longitude has zero ordinary class in the knot exterior by its zero-linking property, as explicitly computed in [F1]. Its class is therefore zero in $H_1(M(P(K));\Lambda)$. The relative connector $H_2(E,M(P(K));\Lambda)\to H_1(M(P(K));\Lambda)$ is zero on the generator and hence zero. Pair exactness now makes $i_{P(K)}$ an isomorphism, since the following relative group is zero. Together with step 3.1 this proves both asserted isomorphisms and identifies $T[\eta]$ with $[\mu_K]$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For $x,y\in H_1(M(P);\Lambda)$, the classes $(x,-Tx)$ and $(y,-Ty)$ lie in the kernel of $H_1(N;\Lambda)\to H_1(E;\Lambda)$ by the definition of $T$. The orthogonal sum of the boundary linking forms on this kernel is zero by step 3.2. Reversing the orientation of a closed component negates its cap fundamental class, hence negates inverse-cap duality and the resulting Blanchfield form; its Bockstein and evaluation are unchanged. Bilinearity gives $B_{-M(P(K))}(-Tx,-Ty)=-B_{P(K)}(Tx,Ty)$. Thus kernel isotropy says precisely $B_P(x,y)-B_{P(K)}(Tx,Ty)=0$, proving the claimed isometry. This uses the actual Bockstein linking forms and does not assume any general nonsingularity theorem. [F3, step 4.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Apply step 5.1 to $x=y=[\eta]$ and use the class identification in step 4.1. It gives $B_{P(K)}([\mu_K],[\mu_K])=B_P([\eta],[\eta])$, so nonzero self-value is preserved. Altering a base path multiplies the based class by some $t^a$; conjugate linearity in the first variable and right linearity in the second multiply its self-value by $t^{-a}$ and $t^a$, whose product is one in the commutative quotient module $F/\Lambda$. All constructions used supplied smooth peripheral products, finite product gluings and the choice-free local homological suppliers. They introduce neither a locally flat annulus-tube assertion nor an unproved arbitrary twisted boundary-generation claim. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
