## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $C$ be an integral smooth projective $k$-curve. Let $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(C)$ be the subgroup generated by differences of fibres in connected finite-type families of line bundles on $C$. Then degree induces an isomorphism
$$\operatorname{Pic}(C)/\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(C)\cong\mathbb Z.$$
In particular the Néron–Severi group of $C$, with the family-equivalence convention in the Statement, is finitely generated. This is the one-dimensional case only.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field, smooth projective integral curve and family-equivalence subgroup in the Statement.

[A1] AC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The Picard sheaf of a smooth projective connected curve is represented by a group scheme whose degree components are open and closed; its degree-zero component is a connected Jacobian with a universal rigidified line bundle after a rational point is chosen. [[thm-picard-zero-represented-by-jacobian]]

[F2] Degree is a well-defined homomorphism from the Picard group of a normal proper curve to $\mathbb Z$. [[cor-degree-descends-picard-curve]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** compare the connected-family subgroup with the degree-zero Jacobian.

1.1 Every connected finite-type family of line bundles on $C$ determines a morphism from its parameter scheme to the Picard scheme in [F1]. Since the degree components are open and closed, a connected parameter scheme maps into a single degree component. The two fibre classes of the family therefore have the same degree. By [F2], every generator of $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(C)$ lies in the kernel of degree. [A1, F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Choose $p\in C(k)$; such a point exists because a nonempty finite-type scheme over an algebraically closed field has a closed rational point. By [F1], the degree-zero Picard component is the connected Jacobian $J$ and has a universal line bundle on $C\times J$, rigidified along $p$. Every degree-zero line bundle determines a $k$-point of $J$, and the difference between the universal fibres at that point and at the identity is its class. Thus the kernel of degree lies in the subgroup generated by connected families. Together with step 1.1 this identifies $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(C)$ with the degree kernel. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 The line bundle $\mathcal O_C(p)$ has degree one, so degree is surjective onto $\mathbb Z$. The first isomorphism theorem and step 2.1 give the displayed isomorphism. In dimension zero the corresponding smooth integral projective scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}k$ and its Picard group is zero. [F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
