## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\zeta\in\mathfrak h^*$ be a **dominant integral weight**, that is,
$\langle\zeta,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ for every simple root
$\alpha_i$ (equivalently, when $\zeta$ lies in the real span of the roots,
$\langle\zeta,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ for every positive root
$\alpha$). Then
$$\zeta-w\zeta\in Q^+\qquad\text{for every }w\in W,$$
where $W$ is the Weyl group of
[[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]] and $Q^+$ is the cone of
[[def-partial-order-on-weights]]. In particular $\zeta$ is the maximum of its
Weyl orbit for the order $\mu\le\nu$ defined by $\nu-\mu\in Q^+$, and if
$w\zeta\ge\zeta$ then $w\zeta=\zeta$.

Consequently, if $\lambda$ is a weight with $\lambda+\rho$ dominant integral
($\rho$ the Weyl vector of [[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]]),
then $\lambda-w\mathbin\cdot\lambda\in Q^+$ for every $w\in W$, and $\lambda$
is the maximum of its linkage class $W_\lambda\mathbin\cdot\lambda$ of
[[def-integral-weyl-group-of-a-weight]]; here
$W_\lambda=W$ because every simple reflection pairs integrally with
$\lambda+\rho$.

Integrality is used with its full strength: for a dominant $\zeta$ that is not
integral the conclusion $\zeta-w\zeta\in Q^+$ fails for reflections pairing
non-integrally with $\zeta$, and only the reflections integral at $\zeta$ can
be used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a dominant integral weight $\zeta$ in $\mathfrak h^*$, and the Weyl group $W$ generated by the root reflections $s_\alpha$ of [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]].

[F1] The reflection is $s_\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$, and under the identification of [[prop-the-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]] (parts (iii) and (iv)) these reflections are the reflections of the reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi$ with $W$-invariant positive definite form on the real span $E$ of the roots ([[thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]], [[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]]).

[F2] Simple reflections generate $W$; word length satisfies $\ell(ws_i)=\ell(w)-1$ exactly when $w\alpha_i<0$, and in a reduced word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$ every prefix is reduced ([[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]]).

[F3] The relation $\mu\le\lambda$ defined by $\lambda-\mu\in Q^+$ is a partial order on $\mathfrak h^*$ ([[def-partial-order-on-weights]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct, through a reduced-word telescoping identity whose terms are nonnegative integer multiples of positive roots.

1.1 By [F2] the simple reflections generate $W$, so choose a reduced expression $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$ with $k=\ell(w)$ and put $w_j=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_j}$. Since $w_j=w_{j-1}s_{i_j}$, the telescoping sum gives $\zeta-w\zeta=\sum_{j=1}^k(w_{j-1}\zeta-w_j\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^k w_{j-1}(\zeta-s_{i_j}\zeta)=\sum_{j=1}^k\langle\zeta,\alpha_{i_j}^\vee\rangle\,w_{j-1}\alpha_{i_j}$, using [F1] for the last equality. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Each coefficient is $\langle\zeta,\alpha_{i_j}^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ because $\alpha_{i_j}$ is simple and $\zeta$ is dominant integral, and each vector $w_{j-1}\alpha_{i_j}$ is a positive root: otherwise $\ell(w_{j-1}s_{i_j})=\ell(w_{j-1})-1$ by [F2], contradicting that the prefix $w_j$ of the reduced word $w$ is reduced and that $w=w_js_{i_{j+1}}\cdots s_{i_k}$ has length $k$. Hence every term of the sum of step 1.1 lies in $\mathbb Z_{\ge0}\Phi^+\subseteq Q^+$, and $\zeta-w\zeta\in Q^+$ for every $w\in W$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $\zeta-w\zeta\in Q^+$ means $w\zeta\le\zeta$, every Weyl conjugate of $\zeta$ lies below $\zeta$ in the root order, so $\zeta$ is the maximum of its Weyl orbit; and if in addition $\zeta\le w\zeta$, then $\zeta=w\zeta$ by antisymmetry of the partial order [F3]. [F3, step 2.1]

3.2 For the dot-action statement let $\lambda$ be a weight with $\lambda+\rho$ dominant integral, and apply step 2.1 to $\zeta=\lambda+\rho$: then $(\lambda+\rho)-w(\lambda+\rho)\in Q^+$, that is, $\lambda-w\mathbin\cdot\lambda\in Q^+$ for every $w\in W$; moreover $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ for every simple root $\alpha_i$, so every simple reflection lies in $W_\lambda$ and $W_\lambda=W$ by [F2]; hence every element of the linkage class $W_\lambda\mathbin\cdot\lambda$ is a Weyl conjugate of $\lambda$ and lies below $\lambda$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Combining steps 3.1 and 3.2: for every $w\in W$ one has $\zeta-w\zeta\in Q^+$ and $\lambda-w\mathbin\cdot\lambda\in Q^+$ in the dot setting, so $\zeta$ and $\lambda$ are the maxima of their orbits and linkage classes respectively, and $w\zeta\ge\zeta$ forces $w\zeta=\zeta$. [step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
