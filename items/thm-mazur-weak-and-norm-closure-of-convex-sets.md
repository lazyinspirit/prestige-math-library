## Statement

For a convex subset $C$ of a normed space $X$, its norm closure equals its weak
closure. Here the weak topology is generated by the basic neighbourhoods of $x$


$$\{y:|f_j(y-x)|<\varepsilon\ (1\le j\le n)\}$$

for finitely many $f_j\in X^*$ and some $\varepsilon>0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A convex set $C\subseteq X$ and $D=\overline C^{\|\cdot\|}$.

[F1] Disjoint convex sets, one open, are strictly separated by a nonzero continuous functional ([[thm-separation-of-disjoint-convex-sets-one-open]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every $f\in X^*$ is norm-continuous, so every displayed basic weak neighbourhood is norm-open. Thus every weak-open set is norm-open, and $D\subseteq\overline C^{\mathrm{weak}}$. [given]

1.2 If $C=\varnothing$, both closures are empty. Otherwise $D$ is nonempty; if $x\notin D$, choose $r>0$ with $x\notin D+B(0,r)$. This latter set is open and convex, so [F1] yields $f\ne0$ with $\operatorname{Re}f(d+b)<\operatorname{Re}f(x)$ for $d\in D$, $\|b\|<r$. [given, F1, choose]

2.1 Taking suprema over $b\in B(0,r)$ gives $\sup_{d\in D}\operatorname{Re}f(d)+r\|f\|\le\operatorname{Re}f(x)$. The weak neighbourhood $\{y:|f(y-x)|<r\|f\|/2\}$ therefore misses $D$, hence misses $C$. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Thus $x$ is not in the weak closure whenever it is not in $D$, proving the reverse inclusion and equality. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
