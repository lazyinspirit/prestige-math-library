## Statement

Let $K\subseteq L$ be fields. Let $(\delta_j)_{j\in J}$ be any family of
derivations of $K$ extending to derivations $D_j$ of $L$, and put
$k_0=\bigcap_j\ker\delta_j$, with $k_0=K$ when $J$ is empty. If
$h_1,\ldots,h_r\in L$ satisfy $D_jh_i=0$ for all $i,j$, their relation
ideal
$$I=\{F\in K[X_1,\ldots,X_r]:F(h_1,\ldots,h_r)=0\}$$
is generated over $K$ by $I_0=I\cap k_0[X_1,\ldots,X_r]$. Equivalently,
the multiplication map
$$K\otimes_{k_0} k_0[h_1,\ldots,h_r]\longrightarrow K[h_1,\ldots,h_r]$$
is an isomorphism. More generally, every $K$-linear subspace of $K^n$
stable under applying all the $\delta_j$ coordinatewise has a basis with
entries in $k_0$. No characteristic, perfection or separability hypothesis is
needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields, derivations, common constant field, tuple and relation
ideal of the Statement.

[F1] A derivation is additive and obeys the Leibniz rule, hence kills zero
and one. [[def-derivation-algebra]]

[F2] Every finite matrix over a field can be reduced to reduced row echelon
form by invertible row operations; each nonzero row has a pivot equal to one
and its pivot column is zero in every other row.
[[thm-gauss-jordan-elimination-produces-reduced-row-echelon-form]]

[F3] Tensoring preserves cokernels and surjections.
[[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; differentiate a row-reduced basis and use its
pivot columns to force every derivative to vanish.

1.1 The kernel of each $\delta_j$ is a subfield, since the inverse rule from Leibniz is $\delta_j(a^{-1})=-a^{-2}\delta_j(a)$. Hence $k_0$ is a field, including the stated empty intersection. Let $W\subseteq K^n$ be stable as in the last assertion. Choose a finite basis and reduce its row matrix by [F2]. Its nonzero rows $w_1,\ldots,w_s$ remain a basis of $W$. For each $j$, the row $\delta_j(w_i)$ lies in $W$ and has zero entries in all pivot columns, since every pivot-column entry of the reduced matrix is zero or one. In a linear combination of its basis rows the coefficients are precisely the entries in those pivot columns. Thus $\delta_j(w_i)=0$. Every entry of every $w_i$ lies in $k_0$, proving the subspace assertion, also for $W=0$ or $n=0$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Order the finitely many monomials of total degree at most $m$ and identify their coefficient vectors with a space $K^{n_m}$. Let $I_{\le m}$ be the relations of degree at most $m$. For any $F\in I_{\le m}$, differentiating $F(h)=0$ and using $D_jh_i=0$ shows $(\delta_j F)(h)=0$, where $\delta_j$ acts on coefficients alone. It does not increase the degree. Thus $I_{\le m}$ is stable under every coefficient derivation. Step 1.1 supplies it with a basis of polynomials in $k_0[X_1,\ldots,X_r]$, necessarily in $I_0$. Every polynomial of $I$ lies in some $I_{\le m}$, so it is a $K$-linear combination of elements of $I_0$. This proves $I=I_0 K[X_1,\ldots,X_r]$; the reverse containment also follows directly because $I$ is an ideal. [F1, given, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 To verify the tensor assertion explicitly, identify $k_0[h]$ with $k_0[X]/I_0$ and $K[h]$ with $K[X]/I$. The monomials give the explicit coefficientwise identification $K\otimes_{k_0}k_0[X]=K[X]$. Applying [F3] to $I_0\to k_0[X]\to k_0[X]/I_0\to0$ identifies extension of coefficients of the quotient with $K[X]/I_0K[X]$. By step 2.1 this is $K[X]/I$, and multiplication of pure tensors gives exactly the map in the Statement. Surjectivity follows because the target is generated by the $h_i$ over $K$; injectivity is the equality of the relation ideals. For $r=0$, both polynomial rings are their coefficient fields, $I=I_0=0$, and the map is the identity identification $K\otimes_{k_0}k_0=K$. For empty $J$ the claim is ordinary coefficient extension from $K$ to itself. [F3, given, algebra, step 2.1] ∎
