## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $q=p^f$, $f\ge1$, and let $A/\mathbb F_q$ be an abelian variety of dimension $g\ge0$. Its $q$-power Frobenius $F_q:A\to A$ is a finite surjective group homomorphism, hence an isogeny, and has degree
$$\deg(F_q)=[\mathbb F_q(A):F_q^*\mathbb F_q(A)]=q^g.$$
Degree here means the extension degree of the function fields, without replacing an inseparable kernel by its set of geometric points.

More generally let $u:B\to C$ be a finite surjective homomorphism of abelian varieties of dimension $g$ over any field $k$ of characteristic $p>0$. Put $L=k(B)$, $K=u^*k(C)\subseteq L$, and let $d=\dim_k\ker(du_0:T_0B\to T_0C)$. If $L^{p^m}\subseteq K$ for some $m\ge0$, then
$$\deg(u)=[L:K]\le p^{md}.$$
The case $m=0$ says $K=L$ and degree one. For $m\ge1$ the bound retains the full nonreduced, purely inseparable degree. No separability, étaleness or CM condition on $u$ is imposed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the finite-field Frobenius or finite surjective homomorphism in the Statement.

[A1] AC is the explicitly adopted axiom for the algebraic-scheme and finite transcendence-basis suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] Abelian varieties are smooth proper geometrically integral group varieties under AC and are commutative; an isogeny is a surjective group homomorphism with finite kernel ([[def-abelian-variety-over-a-field]], [[prop-abelian-variety-commutativity-from-rigidity]], [[def-k-isogeny-of-abelian-varieties]]).

[F2] The binomial theorem and divisibility of the intermediate $p$th binomial coefficients imply $(x+y)^p=x^p+y^p$ in every commutative ring of characteristic $p$ ([[thm-binomial-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[lem-prime-divides-intermediate-binomial-coefficients]]). Frobenius in characteristic $p$ is an injective field homomorphism, and is an automorphism on a finite field ([[thm-frobenius-endomorphism-and-finite-field-automorphism]]). In a finite group the order of an element divides the group order ([[thm-lagrange]]).

[F3] A finite-type integral affine variety has dimension equal to the transcendence degree of its function field. A maximal algebraically independent subset of a finite generating family is a transcendence basis, and adjoining finitely many algebraic elements gives a finite extension ([[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]], [[lem-maximal-algebraically-independent-subset-is-a-transcendence-basis]], [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]). Degrees multiply in a finite field tower ([[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]).

[F4] A purely inseparable element in characteristic $p$ has minimal polynomial $X^{p^e}-a$. In particular, if it is not in its ground field, then $e\ge1$ and that derivative is zero ([[thm-purely-inseparable-extension-characterizations]]).

[F5] Kähler differentials represent derivations into any module, in particular $L$-valued derivations of a field extension; they are generated by the differentials of algebra generators ([[cor-derivations-represented-by-differentials]], [[thm-kahler-differentials-existence-presentation]]).

[F6] The transitivity sequence $L\otimes_K\Omega_{K/k}\to\Omega_{L/k}\to\Omega_{L/K}\to0$ is exact. On a smooth $g$-dimensional variety the differential sheaf is locally free of rank $g$, and localization computes the differential module of its function field ([[thm-transitivity-exact-sequence-differentials]], [[thm-differentials-smooth-locally-free]], [[lem-ag-differentials-localization-base-change]]).

## Proof

1.1 A nonzero element of $\mathbb F_q$ has order dividing $q-1$ by [F2], so $c^q=c$ for every $c\in\mathbb F_q$. On any affine finite-type chart with coordinate algebra $D=\mathbb F_q[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$, the iterated binomial identity in [F2] makes the $q$-power map an algebra endomorphism. Every polynomial is a $D^q$-linear combination of the finitely many monomials $\prod_i x_i^{e_i}$ with $0\le e_i<q$: split every exponent into its residue and a multiple of $q$, and use that coefficients are $q$th powers in the finite field. The same statement passes to any quotient presentation of $D$. Thus this ring map is module-finite. The induced map on prime ideals is the identity because $a^q\in\mathfrak p$ if and only if $a\in\mathfrak p$. Such affine charts therefore glue to the finite, surjective $\mathbb F_q$-morphism $F_q:A\to A$. Its naturality for multiplication and the identity makes it a group homomorphism; its kernel is finite as a fibre of a finite morphism, hence [F1] makes it an isogeny. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We prove the field bound needed for the second assertion. Let $K\subseteq L$ be finite of characteristic $p$ with $L^{p^m}\subseteq K$ and $m\ge1$, and put $Q=p^m$. Choose a generating tuple $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ for $L/K$ of least possible finite length. For each $i$, $x_i$ is not in $K_i=K(x_j:j\ne i)$, while $x_i^Q\in K$. It is purely inseparable over $K_i$, so by [F4] its minimal polynomial over $K_i$ is $X^{p^{e_i}}-a_i$ with $e_i\ge1$. Formal differentiation with respect to $X$ therefore passes to the quotient field $K_i[X]/(X^{p^{e_i}}-a_i)=L$, giving a $K$-derivation $D_i:L\to L$ with $D_i(x_i)=1$ and $D_i(x_j)=0$ for $j\ne i$. By [F5] these are $L$-linear functionals on $\Omega_{L/K}$. Evaluation on $dx_j$ shows that $dx_1,\ldots,dx_r$ are linearly independent. They also span by the derivation rules for sums, products and inverses. Hence $r=\dim_L\Omega_{L/K}$. Each successive adjunction has degree at most $Q$, because $x_i$ satisfies $T^Q-x_i^Q$ over $K$. Thus $[L:K]\le Q^r=Q^{\dim_L\Omega_{L/K}}$. If $L=K$, the empty generating tuple gives the same result. [A1, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 Put $L=\mathbb F_q(A)$. By [F3], choose a transcendence basis $t_1,\ldots,t_g$ such that $L$ is a finite extension of $F=\mathbb F_q(t_1,\ldots,t_g)$, say of degree $s$. The restricted monomials $\prod_i t_i^{e_i}$, $0\le e_i<q$, form a basis of $F$ over $F^q=\mathbb F_q(t_1^q,\ldots,t_g^q)$. For independence clear denominators in a putative relation; algebraic independence makes terms with different exponent residues modulo $q$ distinct polynomial monomials. For spanning first reduce polynomial exponents modulo $q$, and then note that any nonzero denominator $h$ has inverse $h^{q-1}/h^q$ with $h^q\in F^q$. Therefore $[F:F^q]=q^g$. Frobenius is a field isomorphism from $L/F$ onto $L^q/F^q$, so $[L^q:F^q]=s$. By the tower law, $sq^g=[L:F^q]=[L:L^q]s$. The pullback of $F_q$ has image exactly $L^q$, giving $\deg(F_q)=q^g$. The empty transcendence basis at $g=0$ gives the same computation with $q^0=1$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

3.1 Apply step 1.2 to $L=k(B)$ and $K=u^*k(C)$. Both varieties are smooth of dimension $g$ by [F1], so [F6] gives $g$-dimensional generic differential spaces over $k$. The map between them has rank $g-d$: the group identity $u\circ t_b=t_{u(b)}\circ u$ identifies its tangent map at any field-valued point $b$ with its tangent map at the identity, after scalar extension. This applies also at the generic point with value field $L$. Dualizing between these finite-dimensional tangent/cotangent spaces preserves rank. The exact sequence in [F6] therefore gives $\dim_L\Omega_{L/K}=d$. For $m\ge1$, step 1.2 now gives $[L:K]\le p^{md}$. If $m=0$, the hypothesis $L\subseteq K$ forces equality $K=L$, giving degree one directly. This proves the full degree inequality, including inseparable maps and nonreduced kernels, without counting their geometric points. [F1, F6, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
