## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathcal A$ be a planar annulus
and identify the solid torus with $\mathcal A\times[-1,1]$. Let
$R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$, $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$, and let $z$ be a formal
polynomial variable. For a regular annular diagram $D$, a state $S$ smooths
every crossing. Let $a(S)$ and $b(S)$ count its A- and B-smoothings, let
$u(S)$ count the resulting circles that bound disks in $\mathcal A$, and let
$e(S)$ count the resulting circles that do not bound disks in $\mathcal A$;
call a circle that does not bound a disk essential.
If $m$ is the total full-twist correction from the blackboard framing to the
specified framing, counting an A-type twist positively and its inverse
negatively as in the framed diagram calculus, set
$$
\Psi(D,m)=(-A^3)^m\sum_S A^{a(S)-b(S)}\delta^{u(S)}z^{e(S)}.
$$
This value is independent of the regular annular diagram and twist correction
of the framed link and induces an $R$-linear map
$$
\Psi:\mathcal K_A(\mathcal A\times[-1,1])\longrightarrow R[z].
$$
For the empty link, the empty diagram has its unique empty state and the
formula gives $\Psi(\varnothing)=1$.

in an annular state; every smoothed component is classified by these two
types.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a framed link in the solid torus $\mathcal A\times[-1,1]$, and a regular annular diagram with its full-twist correction.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice, written $\mathrm{AC}$ ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[A2] Countable choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, means every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[A3] In ZF, $\mathrm{AC}$ implies $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F1] At a crossing, the Kauffman bracket state sum uses the A-smoothing with weight $A$, the B-smoothing with weight $A^{-1}$, and the loop value $\delta=-A^2-A^{-2}$ ([[def-kauffman-bracket-smoothing-and-loop-value]]).

[F2] The module is generated by framed-link classes modulo the crossing and trivial-circle relations ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]]).

[F3] Every framed link in the solid-torus product admits a regular annular diagram with signed full-twist correction marks, and framed isotopies of links are represented by annular planar isotopies and finitely many R1, R2, and R3 moves; R1 changes the blackboard twist correction by the opposite unit ([[lem-framed-link-diagram-calculus]]).

[F4] In the fixed source-chart convention, the crossing operators cancel under R2 and satisfy the three-strand braid relation under R3; these are equalities of local Temperley–Lieb tangles ([[lem-kauffman-bracket-r2-r3-identities]]).

[F5] For two disjoint smoothly embedded circles in $S^2$, the complement consists of two open disks and a unique open annulus bounded by the circles ([[lem-two-disjoint-circles-in-s-two-cobound-an-annulus]]).

## Proof


1.1 Let $B_i$ and $B_o$ be the inner and outer boundary circles of the planar annulus. For any embedded state circle $C$, apply [F5] to $C$ and $B_i$. Their complement has a disk component on the $B_i$ side, a disk component on the $C$ side, and an annulus component. Since $B_o$ lies outside the disk bounded by $B_i$, it lies either in the annulus component or in the disk component on the $C$ side. In the first case the disk component on the $C$ side lies inside the disk bounded by $B_o$ and outside the disk bounded by $B_i$, so $C$ bounds a disk in $\mathcal A$. In the second case $C$ separates $B_i$ from $B_o$, and the annulus component between $C$ and $B_i$ lies inside the disk bounded by $B_o$ and outside the disk bounded by $B_i$. The two disk sides of $C$ then contain $B_i$ and $B_o$, respectively, so neither is contained in $\mathcal A$; hence $C$ is essential exactly in this second case. Thus an essential $C$ has $B_i$ and $B_o$ on opposite disk sides. For two disjoint essential state circles, apply [F5] to the pair. Each disk side containing $B_i$ contains the disk bounded by $B_i$, while $B_o$ lies on the opposite side of both; therefore the curves are nested and the complementary annulus between them lies outside the disk bounded by $B_i$ and inside the disk bounded by $B_o$, hence in $\mathcal A$. Ordering the finitely many essential circles by disk inclusion shows every consecutive pair bounds such an annular band, so they are parallel. Thus $u(S)$ and $e(S)$ are unambiguous finite counts, and each state contributes a Laurent-polynomial multiple of $z^{e(S)}$. [A1, F1, F5, given, cases, construct]

2.1 The state set is finite. At a chosen crossing, partition it into states using the A-smoothing and states using the B-smoothing. In a framed skein triple the exterior annular strips agree; place the same twist marks on those common arcs and none on the blackboard-framed local crossing or smoothings. Their total correction $m$ is common to all three, so the partition gives $\Psi(D_\times,m)=A\Psi(D_A,m)+A^{-1}\Psi(D_B,m)$. Adding a zero-framed trivial circle increases $u(S)$ by one in every state, so it multiplies $\Psi$ by $\delta$. Hence the state expansion annihilates both relation generators in [F2]. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Annular planar isotopy preserves which state circles bound disks. Under R2 and R3, the local tangle identities in [F4] remain equal after attaching the same exterior annular tangle; evaluating each resulting contractible loop by $\delta$ and each essential circle by $z$ is an $R$-linear operation, so the state expansions agree. For an A-type R1 kink the local state factor is $A\delta+A^{-1}=-A^3$; for the opposite B-type kink it is $A+A^{-1}\delta=-A^{-3}$. By [F3] the correction decreases by one for the A-type kink and increases by one for the B-type kink, so the factor $(-A^3)^m$ cancels the raw state-sum change. The value is therefore independent of diagram and framing correction. [A2, A3, F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F3], every framed-link generator has a regular annular diagram and framing correction. Steps 2.1 and 3.1 show that the state expansion is independent of that choice and vanishes on both generators of the defining submodule [F2]. Its $R$-linear extension therefore factors through the quotient and gives the stated map $\Psi$. [A3, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
