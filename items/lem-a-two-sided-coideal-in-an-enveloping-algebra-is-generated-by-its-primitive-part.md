## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field of
characteristic $0$ ([[def-field]], [[def-ring-characteristic]]) and let
$\mathfrak l$ be a Lie algebra over $k$ ([[def-lie-algebra-over-a-field]]).
Equip its universal enveloping algebra $U(\mathfrak l)$ with the standard
cocommutative Hopf structure
([[rem-hopf-algebra-structure-on-the-enveloping-algebra]]), with coproduct
$\Delta(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x$ for $x\in\mathfrak l$ and counit
$\varepsilon(x)=0$. If $J\trianglelefteq U(\mathfrak l)$ is a two-sided ideal
such that

$$\Delta(J)\subseteq J\otimes U(\mathfrak l)+U(\mathfrak l)\otimes J,\qquad J\subseteq\ker\varepsilon,$$

then $\mathfrak j:=J\cap\mathfrak l$ is a Lie ideal and
$J=U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j=\mathfrak jU(\mathfrak l)$. Thus $J$ is uniquely
determined by $\mathfrak j$ among two-sided ideals satisfying both displayed
conditions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a characteristic-zero field $k$, a Lie algebra $\mathfrak l$, and a two-sided ideal $J$ satisfying the coproduct and augmentation conditions.

[A1] AC is the assertion that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] Under AC, every vector space has a basis ([[cor-every-vector-space-has-a-basis]]), and every independent set can be extended to a basis ([[thm-every-independent-set-extends-to-a-basis]]).

[L2] Under AC, every set, hence any chosen basis, can be well ordered ([[thm-well-ordering-theorem]]).

[L3] For a supplied totally ordered basis of $\mathfrak l$, ordered monomials form a basis of $U(\mathfrak l)$ and the PBW symbol map identifies $\operatorname{gr}U(\mathfrak l)$ with $S(\mathfrak l)$ ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L4] The standard coproduct and counit are algebra maps, and are primitive on $\mathfrak l$ ([[rem-hopf-algebra-structure-on-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-bialgebra-counit-and-antipode]]).

[L5] The PBW filtration $F_nU(\mathfrak l)$ is exhaustive and is spanned by products of at most $n$ elements of $\mathfrak l$ ([[def-pbw-filtration-on-the-universal-enveloping-algebra]]).

[L6] Linear maps $V\to A$ into commutative unital algebras extend uniquely to $S(V)$, and ring maps killing an ideal factor through the quotient ([[def-symmetric-algebra-of-a-vector-space]], [[def-quotient-vector-space-and-canonical-projection]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[thm-universal-property-of-the-symmetric-algebra]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[L7] In a characteristic-zero field, every positive integer is nonzero and invertible ([[def-field]], [[def-ring-characteristic]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** PBW filtration and induction on degree.

1.1 By [A1] and [L1], choose a basis of $\mathfrak l$; by [L2], give it a total well-order. Apply [L3]. In particular $\mathfrak l$ embeds in $U(\mathfrak l)$, and the associated graded algebra of the filtration [L5] is $S(\mathfrak l)$. [A1, L1, L2, L3, L5, construct]

1.2 By [L4], $\Delta$ is filtered for the total-degree filtration on $U(\mathfrak l)\otimes U(\mathfrak l)$: this holds on each degree-one generator because $\Delta(x)=x\otimes1+1\otimes x$, and hence on products because $\Delta$ is an algebra map. Its associated graded coproduct on $S(\mathfrak l)$ is the algebra map making every $x\in\mathfrak l$ primitive, since the two maps agree on the algebra generators. [L3, L4, L5, algebra]

1.3 Put $J_n:=J\cap F_nU(\mathfrak l)$ and $K:=\operatorname{gr}J$. The ideal property makes $K$ a graded ideal of $S(\mathfrak l)$. To see its coideal property, use AC and [L1] to choose a complement $W_n$ of $J_{n-1}$ in $J_n$; its image in $F_n/F_{n-1}$ is a subspace, so choose a complement there and lift it to $C_n\subset F_n$. Thus $F_n=F_{n-1}\oplus W_n\oplus C_n$ and $J_n=J_{n-1}\oplus W_n$. The total-degree filtration then has associated graded pieces $W_p\otimes(W_q\oplus C_q)$ and $(W_p\oplus C_p)\otimes W_q$ for $J\otimes U+U\otimes J$. Taking highest-degree symbols in the assumed containment gives $\Delta_{S(\mathfrak l)}(K)\subseteq K\otimes S(\mathfrak l)+S(\mathfrak l)\otimes K$. [A1, L1, L3, L4, L5, construct]

1.4 Since $\varepsilon(J)=0$ and $F_0U(\mathfrak l)=k1$, we have $J_0=0$. PBW gives $F_1U(\mathfrak l)=k1\oplus\mathfrak l$, so $K_0=0$ and $K_1=\mathfrak j$. Also $\mathfrak j$ is a Lie ideal: for $x\in\mathfrak l$ and $a\in\mathfrak j$, the commutator $xa-ax=[x,a]$ lies in $J$ because $J$ is two-sided, and lies in $\mathfrak l$ by the enveloping relation. [L3, L4, L5, given, base, algebra]

1.5 Let $I_{\mathfrak j}$ be the ideal of $S(\mathfrak l)$ generated by $\mathfrak j$, and let $\pi:S(\mathfrak l)\to S(\mathfrak l/\mathfrak j)$ be the map induced by the vector-space quotient. The universal properties in [L6] construct inverse algebra maps between $S(\mathfrak l)/I_{\mathfrak j}$ and $S(\mathfrak l/\mathfrak j)$: maps from either algebra to a commutative unital $k$-algebra $A$ correspond exactly to linear maps $\mathfrak l\to A$ vanishing on $\mathfrak j$. The maps are inverse because they agree with the identity on the algebra generators. Hence $\ker\pi=I_{\mathfrak j}$. [L6, given, algebra]

2.1 We prove by induction on $n$ that $K_n\subseteq I_{\mathfrak j}\cap S^n(\mathfrak l)$. The claim holds in degrees $0$ and $1$ by step 1.4. For $n\ge2$, take $z\in K_n$. Its reduced coproduct has, in bidegree $(p,q)$ with $p,q>0$ and $p+q=n$, a component in $K_p\otimes S^q(\mathfrak l)+S^p(\mathfrak l)\otimes K_q$ by step 1.3; both $p,q<n$, so the induction hypothesis makes its image under $\pi\otimes\pi$ zero. Thus $\pi(z)$ has zero reduced coproduct and is primitive in $S(\mathfrak l/\mathfrak j)$. The $(n-1,1)$ component of the coproduct of a homogeneous degree-$n$ element $f$ is the sum obtained by placing each of its $n$ factors in the second tensor slot; multiplying the two slots gives $nf$. If $f$ is primitive this component is zero, so [L7] forces $f=0$. Consequently $\pi(z)=0$ and $z\in I_{\mathfrak j}$. [L6, L7, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 1.5, induction, ih, algebra]

3.1 The reverse inclusion $I_{\mathfrak j}\subseteq K$ follows because $K$ is an ideal and contains $K_1=\mathfrak j$. Hence $\operatorname{gr}J=K=I_{\mathfrak j}$. [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Since $J$ is two-sided, the left and right ideals $U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j$ and $\mathfrak jU(\mathfrak l)$ lie in $J$, so their associated graded spaces lie in $\operatorname{gr}J=I_{\mathfrak j}$ by step 3.1. Conversely, each degree-$n$ element of $I_{\mathfrak j}$ is a finite sum of products in $S^{n-1}(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j$, and PBW lifts that sum to an element of $U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j\cap F_n$ with the same symbol. The right-handed products lift identically. Hence both associated graded spaces equal $I_{\mathfrak j}$. [L3, L5, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For $x\in J\cap F_n$, step 4.1 supplies $y\in U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j\cap F_n$ with the same degree-$n$ symbol as $x$. Then $x-y\in J\cap F_{n-1}$; descending induction, starting with $J\cap F_0=0$ from step 1.4, gives $x\in U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j$. The identical argument with the right-generated ideal gives $x\in\mathfrak jU(\mathfrak l)$. Thus $J=U(\mathfrak l)\mathfrak j=\mathfrak jU(\mathfrak l)$, and applying this equality to any other ideal satisfying the same two conditions and the same intersection proves the stated uniqueness. [step 1.4, step 2.1, step 4.1, induction, discharge-induction: step 2.1, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The zero Lie algebra is included: then $U(0)=k$, $\ker\varepsilon=0$, and the only admissible ideal is $J=0$.
- The augmentation hypothesis is necessary. For nonzero $\mathfrak l$, the ideal $J=U(\mathfrak l)$ satisfies the coproduct containment, but $J\cap\mathfrak l=\mathfrak l$ while $U(\mathfrak l)(J\cap\mathfrak l)=\ker\varepsilon\ne U(\mathfrak l)$.
- The unrestricted “largest ideal with fixed primitive part” claim is false: for $\mathfrak l=kx$, $U(\mathfrak l)=k[x]$, the ideals $0$ and $(x^2)$ have the same intersection $0$ with $\mathfrak l$, and $0\subsetneq(x^2)$.
- AC is used to choose and well-order a basis of arbitrary $\mathfrak l$, as required by the supplied general PBW theorem, and to split the induced filtration of $J$ in step 1.3. These are the only nonconstructive choices in this proof; the characteristic-zero use is exactly the invertibility of $n$ in step 2.1. No assertion is made in positive characteristic.
