## Statement

For every essentially small abelian category $\mathcal C$, under the standing
bounded-derived localization size convention of
[[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]], degree-zero inclusion induces
an isomorphism $G_0(\mathcal C)\to K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$. Its
inverse is $[X]\mapsto\sum_n(-1)^n[H^n(X)]$. It does not require enough
projectives, enough injectives, Noetherianity or finite global dimension.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An essentially small abelian category $\mathcal C$, its stalk
complexes, and the bounded derived category $D^b(\mathcal C)$ under the
standing size convention of [[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]].

[F1] $G_0(\mathcal C)$ is the free abelian group on
$\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$ modulo the relations
$[Y]=[X]+[Z]$ from short exact sequences, and a class function on
$\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$ additive on short exact sequences factors
uniquely through $G_0(\mathcal C)$
([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]],
[[thm-grothendieck-group-universal-properties-and-functoriality]]).

[F2] $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(\mathcal T)$ is the free abelian group on
$\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal T)$ modulo the subgroup generated by the
triangle relations $[Y]-[X]-[Z]$, and $[0]=0$,
$[X[n]]=(-1)^n[X]$ for every integer $n$
([[def-triangulated-grothendieck-group]],
[[lem-triangulated-k-zero-shifts-and-exact-functors]]).

[F3] A function from a set to an abelian group extends uniquely to a
homomorphism on the free abelian group on that set, and a homomorphism killing
a subgroup factors uniquely through the quotient group
([[def-free-abelian-group]], [[thm-quotient-group-universal-property]]).

[F4] $D(\mathcal C)$ is triangulated with distinguished triangles the
isomorphic images of cone triangles, the localization is exact, and every
distinguished triangle carries a long exact cohomology sequence
$\cdots\to H^n(X)\to H^n(Y)\to H^n(Z)\to H^{n+1}(X)\to\cdots$ whose maps are
the images of the triangle's maps; the functors $H^n$ factor through the
localization ([[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]],
[[thm-the-derived-category-inherits-a-triangulated-structure]],
[[prop-cohomology-factors-through-the-derived-category]]).

[F5] Under the standing size convention, $D^b(\mathcal C)\to D(\mathcal C)$ is
a fully faithful exact inclusion of a full triangulated subcategory, and its
essential image is exactly the objects with bounded cohomology
([[prop-bounded-derived-localizations-embed-fully-faithfully]]).

[F6] Every short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ of cochain complexes
gives a distinguished triangle $A\to B\to C\to A[1]$ in $D(\mathcal C)$, and
for every integer $n$ there are canonical distinguished triangles
$\tau^{\leq n}X\to\tau^{\leq n+1}X\to H^{n+1}(X)[-n-1]\to(\tau^{\leq n}X)[1]$;
canonical truncations are functorial on the derived category with
$H^i(\tau^{\leq n}X)=H^i(X)$ for $i\leq n$ and zero for $i>n$
([[thm-canonical-truncations-fit-a-distinguished-triangle]],
[[lem-canonical-truncation-is-a-complex-and-has-the-claimed-cohomology]],
[[def-canonical-truncation-of-a-complex]]).

[F7] The stalk complex $S^0(A)$ has $S^0(A)^0=A$, vanishes in every other
degree and has zero differentials; its cohomology is $H^0(S^0(A))\cong A$ and
$H^n(S^0(A))=0$ for $n\neq0$ ([[def-zero-and-stalk-complex]],
[[def-cohomology-object-of-a-cochain-complex]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The assignment $A\mapsto S^0(A)$, functorial by degreewise application, sends isomorphic objects of $\mathcal C$ to isomorphic degree-zero stalk complexes, so $[A]\mapsto[S^0(A)]$ is a well-defined class function on $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$ with values in $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$: the stalk complex has cohomology supported in degree $0$ by [F7], hence lies in $D^b(\mathcal C)$ by [F5]. A short exact sequence $0\to A\to B\to C\to0$ of $\mathcal C$, viewed in degree zero, is degreewise short exact as a sequence of complexes (in every other degree it reads $0\to0\to0$ with zero differentials), so [F6] gives a distinguished triangle $S^0(A)\to S^0(B)\to S^0(C)\to S^0(A)[1]$ in $D(\mathcal C)$ whose objects all have bounded cohomology and therefore lie in $D^b(\mathcal C)$ by [F5]; its relation $[S^0(B)]=[S^0(A)]+[S^0(C)]$ holds in $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$ by [F2]. Thus the class function is additive on short exact sequences, and [F1] gives a unique homomorphism $\iota_*:G_0(\mathcal C)\to K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$ with $\iota_*([A])=[S^0(A)]$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, construct, algebra]

1.2 For $X$ in $D^b(\mathcal C)$ the cohomology objects $H^n(X)$ are defined for all $n$ by [F4] and vanish outside a finite interval by [F5]; set $\chi(X):=\sum_n(-1)^n[H^n(X)]\in G_0(\mathcal C)$, a finite alternating sum. If $X\cong X'$ in $D^b(\mathcal C)$, then $H^n(X)\cong H^n(X')$ for every $n$ because the cohomology functors factor through the derived category [F4], so $\chi$ is a class function on $\operatorname{Iso}(D^b(\mathcal C))$. The zero object has $\chi(0)=0$, since its cohomology vanishes in every degree. [F4, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 The class function $\chi$ of step 1.2 is additive on distinguished triangles. Let $X\to Y\to Z\to X[1]$ be a distinguished triangle of $D^b(\mathcal C)$; its image under the exact inclusion is distinguished in $D(\mathcal C)$ by [F5], so [F4] gives the long exact cohomology sequence $\cdots\to H^n(X)\to H^n(Y)\to H^n(Z)\to H^{n+1}(X)\to\cdots$. Choose integers $a\leq b$ with $H^n(X)=H^n(Y)=H^n(Z)=0$ for $n<a$ and $n>b$; such bounds exist since all three objects lie in the essential image described in [F5], and the sequence vanishes outside $[a,b]$. For each $n$ put $J^n:=\ker(H^n(X)\to H^n(Y))$, $K^n:=\operatorname{im}(H^n(X)\to H^n(Y))$ and $I^n:=\operatorname{im}(H^n(Y)\to H^n(Z))$, objects of $\mathcal C$ that are subobjects or quotients of $H^n(X),H^n(Y),H^n(Z)$ and hence vanish for $n<a$ and $n>b$. Exactness at $H^n(X)$, $H^n(Y)$ and $H^n(Z)$, together with $\operatorname{im}(H^n(Z)\to H^{n+1}(X))=\ker(H^{n+1}(X)\to H^{n+1}(Y))=J^{n+1}$, gives the short exact sequences $0\to J^n\to H^n(X)\to K^n\to0$, $0\to K^n\to H^n(Y)\to I^n\to0$ and $0\to I^n\to H^n(Z)\to J^{n+1}\to0$ in $\mathcal C$, with $J^{b+1}\subseteq H^{b+1}(X)=0$. Multiplying the three resulting $G_0$-relations by $(-1)^n$ and summing over the finitely many nonzero degrees gives $\chi(Y)=\chi(X)+\chi(Z)$: the $J$-terms telescope, since $\sum_n(-1)^n[J^{n+1}]=-\sum_n(-1)^n[J^n]$, while the $K$- and $I$-terms cancel between $[H^n(X)]+[H^n(Z)]$ and $[H^n(Y)]$. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $\chi$ is a class function additive on distinguished triangles by step 2.1, extending it over the free abelian group on $\operatorname{Iso}(D^b(\mathcal C))$ and applying the quotient universal property, exactly as the functor-induced class functions of [F2] are factored, produces a unique homomorphism $\overline\chi:K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))\to G_0(\mathcal C)$ with $\overline\chi([X])=\chi(X)$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The composite $\overline\chi\circ\iota_*$ is the identity of $G_0(\mathcal C)$: for an object $A$ of $\mathcal C$, $\overline\chi(\iota_*([A]))=\chi(S^0(A))=\sum_n(-1)^n[H^n(S^0(A))]=[A]$ by [F7], so the two homomorphisms agree on every generator of $G_0(\mathcal C)$ [F1], where $\iota_*$ is the homomorphism of step 1.1 and $\overline\chi$ is that of step 3.1. [F1, F7, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 The composite $\iota_*\circ\overline\chi$ is the identity of $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$. Let $X$ have $H^n(X)=0$ for $n<a$ and $n>b$. The canonical truncations are objects of $D^b(\mathcal C)$ and the triangles $\tau^{\leq n}X\to\tau^{\leq n+1}X\to H^{n+1}(X)[-n-1]\to(\tau^{\leq n}X)[1]$ of [F6] are distinguished in $D^b(\mathcal C)$ by [F5]. The complex $\tau^{\leq a-1}X$ has zero cohomology in every degree by [F6], so the zero map $\tau^{\leq a-1}X\to0$ is a quasi-isomorphism and $[\tau^{\leq a-1}X]=0$ in $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$. For each $n=a-1,\dots,b-1$ the triangle relation and $[H^{n+1}(X)[-n-1]]=(-1)^{n+1}[H^{n+1}(X)[0]]$ from [F2] give $[\tau^{\leq n+1}X]=[\tau^{\leq n}X]+(-1)^{n+1}[H^{n+1}(X)[0]]$, and summing these relations telescopes to $[\tau^{\leq b}X]=\sum_{n=a}^{b}(-1)^n[H^n(X)[0]]$. The truncation map $\tau^{\leq b}X\to X$ is a quasi-isomorphism by the cohomology formula of [F6], so $[\tau^{\leq b}X]=[X]$ and $[X]=\sum_{n=a}^{b}(-1)^n[H^n(X)[0]]=\sum_{n=a}^{b}(-1)^n\iota_*([H^n(X)])=\iota_*(\overline\chi([X]))$, using the identification $\iota_*([H^n(X)])=[S^0(H^n(X))]=[H^n(X)[0]]$ of step 1.1. The classes $[X]$ generate $K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$ [F2], so $\iota_*\circ\overline\chi$ is the identity. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 3.1, induction, algebra]

5.1 Steps 4.1 and 4.2 exhibit $\overline\chi$ as a two-sided inverse of $\iota_*$, so degree-zero inclusion induces the asserted isomorphism $G_0(\mathcal C)\to K_0^{\mathrm{tri}}(D^b(\mathcal C))$ whose inverse sends $[X]$ to $\sum_n(-1)^n[H^n(X)]$. The argument used only the free abelian group presentations, their universal properties, the long exact cohomology sequence and the finite canonical-truncation induction: no enough-projectives or enough-injectives hypothesis, no Noetherianity and no finite global dimension was used, and no choice principle occurs. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
