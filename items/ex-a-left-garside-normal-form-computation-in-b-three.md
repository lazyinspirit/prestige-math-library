## Example

Take $n=3$, so that $B_3$ has the two generators $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, the
positive monoid $B_3^{+}$ has the two atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, and the half
twist is

$$\Delta=T_1T_2=\sigma_1\,(\sigma_2\sigma_1)=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1,$$

of length $N=1+2=3$. Let $x:=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2\in B_3$. The example
computes the left Garside normal form of $x$ of
[[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]] and checks:

1. $\sigma_1^{-1}=\Delta^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2$, hence
   $x=\Delta^{-1}\,\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$ with $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}\in B_3^{+}$;
2. $\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, so in particular
   $\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$;
3. the left normal form of $x$ is
   $$x=\Delta^{-1}\cdot(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\cdot\sigma_2,$$
   that is $p(x)=-1$, $A(x)=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$ and the two factors
   $a_1=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, $a_2=\sigma_2$ are proper simple braids with
   $\pi(a_1)=s_1s_2$, $\pi(a_2)=s_2$;
4. the factor pair is left weighted: $(a_1a_2)\wedge_L\Delta=a_1$;
5. consequently $x\notin B_3^{+}$, in agreement with $p(x)=-1<0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The natural number $3$; the braid group
$B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle$
of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]; its positive monoid
$B_3^{+}$ with atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ and length $\ell$; the half twist
$\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\in B_3^{+}$ of length $3$; and the element
$x:=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2\in B_3$.

[F1] $B_3^{+}$ is generated by the atoms, $\ell([w])=|w|$ is additive,
$\ell(z)=0$ forces $z=1$, and the braid relation gives
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$, so both triangular words
for $\Delta$ agree: $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$
([[def-positive-braid-monoid]],
[[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]],
[[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

[F2] $a\preccurlyeq_Lb$ means $b=ac$ for some $c\in B_3^{+}$, and then
$\ell(a)\le\ell(b)$; a left divisor of an atom has length $0$ or $1$, so the
left divisors of $\sigma_1$ are among $1,\sigma_1$ and those of $\sigma_2$
among $1,\sigma_2$ ([[def-left-and-right-divisibility-for-positive-braids]],
[[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]]).

[F3] $\pi\colon B_3^{+}\to S_3$ is a monoid homomorphism with
$\pi(\sigma_i)=s_i$; in the composition convention of [[def-symmetric-group]]
the adjacent transpositions $s_1=(1\,2)$ and $s_2=(2\,3)$ are distinct, so
$\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$ ([[lem-reduced-adjacent-transposition-words-have-well-defined-positive-lifts]],
[[def-symmetric-group]]).

[F4] $B_3^{+}$ is a submonoid of $B_3$, and for $x\in B_3$ one has
$1\preccurlyeq_Lx$ if and only if $x\in B_3^{+}$
([[thm-the-ore-fraction-group-of-positive-braids-is-the-artin-braid-group]],
[[thm-left-and-right-divisibility-extend-to-lattice-orders-on-the-braid-group]]).

[F5] **Scaling identity.** For $D,A,B\in B_3^{+}$ one has
$D(A\wedge_LB)=(DA)\wedge_L(DB)$, where the meet is the positive left-gcd
([[thm-left-and-right-divisibility-extend-to-lattice-orders-on-the-braid-group]]).

[F6] Every nonempty finite family in $B_3^{+}$ has a unique left-gcd and a
unique left-lcm; in particular $\Delta\wedge_LA$ exists for every
$A\in B_3^{+}$, and every common left divisor of $\Delta$ and $A$ left-divides
$\Delta\wedge_LA$ ([[thm-positive-braids-have-left-and-right-gcds-and-lcms]]).

[F7] **Normal form.** Every $x\in B_3$ has a unique expression
$x=\Delta^{p}a_1\cdots a_r$ with $p\in\mathbb Z$, $r\in\mathbb N$, every $a_i$
a proper simple braid and $a_i=\Delta\wedge_L(a_i\cdots a_r)$; in such an
expression $p=p(x)$ is the largest $p$ with $\Delta^{p}\preccurlyeq_Lx$, the
product $a_1\cdots a_r$ equals $A(x)=\Delta^{-p(x)}x$, and $\bigl(p(x),A(x)\bigr)$
is the unique pair with $x=\Delta^{p}A$, $A\in B_3^{+}$ and
$\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_LA$. Moreover $p(x)\ge0$ if and only if
$x\in B_3^{+}$, and if $r\ge2$ then $(a_ia_{i+1})\wedge_L\Delta=a_i$ for
$1\le i<r$ ([[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The half twist and the rewriting of $\sigma_1^{-1}$.** By [F1], $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ has length $3$. In the group $B_3$, in which $B_3^{+}$ sits as a submonoid by [F4], multiplying out gives $\Delta^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1)^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2^{-1}\sigma_2=\sigma_1^{-1}$, so $\sigma_1^{-1}=\Delta^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2$; substituting this identity into the given element $x=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2$ gives $x=\Delta^{-1}\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, and $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}\in B_3^{+}$ because it is a product of atoms. [F1, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 **The atoms are coprime.** Let $d\in B_3^{+}$ be a common left divisor of $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$. By [F2] write $\sigma_1=dc$ with $c\in B_3^{+}$; additivity of $\ell$ [F1] gives $\ell(d)+\ell(c)=1$. If $\ell(d)=0$ then $d=1$ [F1]; if $\ell(d)=1$ then $\ell(c)=0$, so $c=1$ and $d=\sigma_1$. Since $d$ also left-divides $\sigma_2$, write $\sigma_2=dc'$ with a possibly different suffix $c'$. Length gives $\ell(c')=0$, hence $c'=1$ and $\sigma_2=d=\sigma_1$, contradicting $\pi(\sigma_2)=s_2\ne s_1=\pi(\sigma_1)$ [F3]. So the only common left divisor of the two atoms is $1$, and by [F6] their left-gcd is $\sigma_1\wedge_L\sigma_2=1$. [F1, F2, F3, F6]

2.1 **The meet with $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$.** Apply the scaling identity [F5] to $D:=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, $A:=\sigma_1$ and $B:=\sigma_2$; by [F1] the two products are $DA=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\Delta$ and $DB=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_2=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, so $\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1)\wedge_L(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_2)=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)(\sigma_1\wedge_L\sigma_2)=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\cdot1=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, the third equality by step 1.2. Hence $\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$: if $\Delta$ were a common left divisor of $\Delta$ and $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, then by [F6] it would satisfy $\Delta\preccurlyeq_L\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}=\sigma_1\sigma_2$, and monotonicity of $\ell$ [F2] would force $3=\ell(\Delta)\le\ell(\sigma_1\sigma_2)=2$, a contradiction. [F1, F2, F5, F6, step 1.2]

3.1 **The maximal $\Delta$-exponent is $-1$.** The pair $(p,A):=(-1,\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2})$ satisfies $x=\Delta^{p}A$ by step 1.1, $A\in B_3^{+}$ by step 1.1 and $\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_LA$ by step 2.1; by the uniqueness in [F7] of the pair $(p,A)$ it coincides with $\bigl(p(x),A(x)\bigr)$, hence $p(x)=-1$ and $A(x)=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$. [F7, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 **The permutation images and left weighting.** Since $\pi$ is a homomorphism with $\pi(\sigma_i)=s_i$ [F3], the two factors have images $\pi(a_1)=s_1s_2$ and $\pi(a_2)=s_2$, which are distinct; in particular $a_1\ne a_2$, so the two-factor form is not a repetition of one simple braid. Left weighting is the instance $i=1<r=2$ of the last assertion of [F7]: $(a_1a_2)\wedge_L\Delta=a_1$, which is literally the computation of step 2.1. [F3, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 **The two greedy factors.** Put $a_1:=\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $a_2:=\sigma_2$, so that $A(x)=a_1a_2$ by step 3.1. Then $\Delta\wedge_LA(x)=\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}=a_1$ by step 2.1, and the unique $A_1$ with $A(x)=a_1A_1$ is $A_1=\sigma_2=a_2$, since $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\sigma_2=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$ [F1]; further $a_2\preccurlyeq_L\Delta$ because $\Delta=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2=\sigma_2(\sigma_1\sigma_2)$ [F1], hence $\Delta\wedge_LA_1=\Delta\wedge_La_2=a_2$ [F6], and the unique $A_2$ with $A_1=a_2A_2$ is $A_2=1$. So the recursion of [F7] produces $r=2$ with factors $a_1,a_2$. Both are proper simple braids: $\Delta=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\sigma_1$ exhibits $a_1\preccurlyeq_L\Delta$ and $\Delta=\sigma_2(\sigma_1\sigma_2)$ exhibits $a_2\preccurlyeq_L\Delta$ [F1], while $a_1\ne1,\Delta$ and $a_2\ne1,\Delta$ because $\ell(a_1)=2$ and $\ell(a_2)=1$ lie in $\{1,2\}$ and not in $\{0,3\}$ [F1]. [F1, F6, F7, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 **The normal form conditions hold.** For $i=2$, step 4.1 gives $\Delta\wedge_La_2=a_2=\Delta\wedge_L(a_2)$; for $i=1$, step 2.1 gives $\Delta\wedge_L(a_1a_2)=\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}=a_1$. So $x=\Delta^{-1}a_1a_2$ with both $a_i$ proper simple and $a_i=\Delta\wedge_L(a_i\cdots a_r)$ for $i=1,2$; by the uniqueness in [F7] this is the left normal form of $x$, and [F7] identifies its data as $p(x)=-1$ and $A(x)=a_1a_2=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, in agreement with step 3.1. [F7, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 **Conclusion.** The element $x=\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2$ of $B_3$ has left Garside normal form $x=\Delta^{-1}\,(\sigma_1\sigma_2)\,\sigma_2$ with $p(x)=-1$ and $A(x)=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, with proper simple factors $a_1=\sigma_1\sigma_2$ and $a_2=\sigma_2$ whose images in $S_3$ are $s_1s_2$ and $s_2$, and with the factor pair left weighted by step 3.2. Moreover $x\notin B_3^{+}$, because $p(x)=-1<0$ while $p\ge0$ characterises positivity by [F7]; this is the qualitative content of the computation, since $x$ is visibly written with an inverse letter. No choice principle is used, the only selections being the explicit words displayed above. ∎ [F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 3.2]

## Remarks

- **Reading off the algorithm.** In the notation of
  [[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]] the recursion runs
  $A_0=\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$, $a_1=\Delta\wedge_LA_0=\sigma_1\sigma_2$,
  $A_1=\sigma_2$, $a_2=\Delta\wedge_LA_1=\sigma_2$, $A_2=1$; the factor
  $\sigma_1\sigma_2$ is the maximal simple prefix of $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$
  because $\Delta$ does not divide $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$ on the left, and the
  remainder $\sigma_2$ is already simple.
- **Why the $\Delta$-exponent is negative.** The computation above gives $p(x)=-1$; by [[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]]
  (d)(ii), this proves $\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_2$ is not positive. The calculation
  exhibits the witness pair $(-1,\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2})$ of that theorem's
  uniqueness clause rather than merely asserting it. Note that
  $\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$ has the same length $3$ as $\Delta$; length alone
  therefore decides neither left divisibility nor the meet, and it is the
  scaling computation of step 2.1 that shows
  $\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_1\sigma_2^{2}$.
- **Conventions.** All products are read left to right as words in the
  generators, and the permutation images are those of the positive monoid map
  $\pi$; the cycle notation is that of [[def-symmetric-group]], so
  $s_1s_2=(1\,2\,3)$. No choice principle is used: every object in the
  computation is an explicitly displayed finite word.
