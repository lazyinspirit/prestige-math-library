## Statement

Let $n\in\mathbb N$, let $B_n^{+}$ be the positive braid monoid of
[[def-positive-braid-monoid]] with its atoms $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$, its
length $\ell$, its half twist $\Delta$
([[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]) and its cancellation
laws ([[lem-the-positive-braid-monoid-is-left-and-right-cancellative]]), and
let $B_n$ be the Artin braid group of
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]], with the same generators
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ and the same defining relations. Then:

**(a) Ore condition.** For all $a,b\in B_n^{+}$ there exist $c,d\in B_n^{+}$
with $ac=bd$; indeed one may take $c,d$ with $ac=bd=\Delta^{2r}$ for some
$r\in\mathbb N$. Consequently $B_n^{+}$ is a cancellative Ore monoid.

**(b) The group of fractions.** There is a group $G$ together with an injective
monoid homomorphism $\eta\colon B_n^{+}\to G$ such that

  (i) every element of $G$ has the form $\eta(a)\eta(b)^{-1}$ with
      $a,b\in B_n^{+}$, and in fact the stronger description
      $\eta(a)\eta(\Delta)^{-2k}$ with $a\in B_n^{+}$, $k\in\mathbb N$ holds;
  (ii) **universal property.** for every group $H$ and every monoid
      homomorphism $f\colon B_n^{+}\to H$ there is a unique group
      homomorphism $\bar f\colon G\to H$ with $\bar f\circ\eta=f$.

We call $G$ *the group of fractions* of $B_n^{+}$; it is determined up to a
unique isomorphism compatible with $\eta$.

**(c) Identification with the braid group.** The assignment
$\sigma_i\mapsto\eta(\sigma_i)$ extends to an isomorphism
$B_n\to G$. Consequently the canonical map $\kappa\colon B_n^{+}\to B_n$,
$\sigma_i\mapsto\sigma_i$, is an injective monoid homomorphism: $B_n^{+}$ is
isomorphic to the submonoid of $B_n$ consisting of the elements that can be
written as positive words, so the two meanings of "positive braid" agree.

No choice principle is used; the group $G$ is an explicit quotient of
$B_n^{+}\times\mathbb N$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n$, the monoid $B_n^{+}$ with generators $\sigma_i$, length $\ell$, half twist $\Delta$ and cancellation laws, and the Artin group $B_n$ with its presentation.

[F1] $B_n^{+}$ is generated as a monoid by the $\sigma_i$, with product $[u][v]=[uv]$; $\ell(x)=0$ only for $x=1$, so $xy=1$ forces $x=y=1$ ([[def-positive-braid-monoid]], [[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]]).

[F2] **Cancellation and $\Delta$-powers.** $xa=xb\Rightarrow a=b$ and $ax=bx\Rightarrow a=b$; every $a\in B_n^{+}$ satisfies $a\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{m}$ and $a\preccurlyeq_R\Delta^{m}$ for some $m$, and if $a\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{m}$ then also $a\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{m+1}$, since $\Delta^{m+1}=\Delta^{m}\Delta=ac\Delta=a(c\Delta)$ when $\Delta^{m}=ac$ ([[lem-the-positive-braid-monoid-is-left-and-right-cancellative]], [[lem-every-positive-braid-divides-a-power-of-delta-on-both-sides]]).

[F3] **Centrality of $\Delta^{2}$.** $w\Delta^{2}=\Delta^{2}w$ for every positive word $w$; hence $\Delta^{2}$ commutes with every element of $B_n^{+}$ ([[lem-conjugation-by-delta-reverses-artin-generators]]).

[F4] **The Artin group.** $B_n$ is the quotient of the free group on $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ by the normal closure of the words $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i(\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1})^{-1}$ and $\sigma_i\sigma_j(\sigma_j\sigma_i)^{-1}$; consequently, for any group $H$ and any elements $x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}\in H$ satisfying $x_ix_{i+1}x_i=x_{i+1}x_ix_{i+1}$ and $x_ix_j=x_jx_i$ for $|i-j|>1$, there is a unique group homomorphism $B_n\to H$ with $\sigma_i\mapsto x_i$, and $B_n$ is generated by the $\sigma_i$ ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]).

[F5] **The monoid universal property.** For any monoid $M$ and elements $a_i\in M$ satisfying the same relations, there is a unique monoid homomorphism $B_n^{+}\to M$ with $\sigma_i\mapsto a_i$ ([[def-positive-braid-monoid]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The relation $\approx$.** On the set $P:=B_n^{+}\times\mathbb N$ define $(a,k)\approx(b,l)$ to mean $a\Delta^{2l}=b\Delta^{2k}$ in $B_n^{+}$. This is an equivalence relation: reflexivity and symmetry are immediate from the symmetry of the defining equation, and if $a\Delta^{2l}=b\Delta^{2k}$ and $b\Delta^{2m}=c\Delta^{2l}$, then $a\Delta^{2l}\Delta^{2m}=b\Delta^{2k}\Delta^{2m}=\Delta^{2k}b\Delta^{2m}$ by [F3], and $b\Delta^{2m}=c\Delta^{2l}$ gives $\Delta^{2k}b\Delta^{2m}=\Delta^{2k}c\Delta^{2l}=c\Delta^{2k}\Delta^{2l}$; hence $a\Delta^{2m}\Delta^{2l}=c\Delta^{2k}\Delta^{2l}$ and right cancellation [F2] yields $a\Delta^{2m}=c\Delta^{2k}$, that is, $(a,k)\approx(c,m)$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 **The product is well defined.** Put $[a,k]\cdot[b,l]:=[ab,k+l]$, where $[a,k]$ denotes the $\approx$-class. If $(a,k)\approx(a',k')$, that is $a\Delta^{2k'}=a'\Delta^{2k}$, then $ab\Delta^{2(k'+l)}=a\Delta^{2k'}b\Delta^{2l}=(a\Delta^{2k'})b\Delta^{2l}=(a'\Delta^{2k})b\Delta^{2l}=a'b\Delta^{2(k+l)}$ using [F3] twice, so $(ab,k+l)\approx(a'b,k'+l)$; the verification in the second argument is the same computation with the factors interchanged, $ab\Delta^{2(k+l')}=a\Delta^{2k}b\Delta^{2l'}=a\Delta^{2k}b'\Delta^{2l}=ab'\Delta^{2(k+l)}$. Hence the product is independent of the chosen representatives, and it is associative with two-sided identity $[1,0]$ because these hold for the product and for addition in $\mathbb N$; so the quotient $P/\!\approx$ is a monoid, denoted $G$. [F1, F3]

1.3 **Every class has a right inverse.** Given $(a,k)$, choose $r\ge k$ with $a\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{2r}$, which is possible by [F2] because $\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{m}$ implies $\preccurlyeq_L\Delta^{m'}$ for every $m'\ge m$. Write $\Delta^{2r}=ac$ with $c\in B_n^{+}$. Then $(a,k)\cdot(c,r-k)=(ac,r)=(\Delta^{2r},r)$, and $(\Delta^{2r},r)\approx(1,0)$ because $\Delta^{2r}\Delta^{0}=1\cdot\Delta^{2r}$. [F1, F2]

2.1 **$G$ is a group.** By step 1.3 every element $x$ of the monoid $G$ has a right inverse $y$: $xy=1$. Applying the same to $y$ gives $z$ with $yz=1$. Then $x=x\cdot1=x(yz)=(xy)z=1\cdot z=z$, so $yx=yz=1$ as well: $y$ is a two-sided inverse of $x$. Hence every element of $G$ is invertible and $G$ is a group. [F1, step 1.2, step 1.3]

2.2 **$\eta$ is injective.** Define $\eta(a):=[a,0]$. It is a monoid homomorphism by step 1.2: $\eta(ab)=[ab,0]=[a,0][b,0]=\eta(a)\eta(b)$ because $0+0=0$. If $\eta(a)=\eta(b)$, then $(a,0)\approx(b,0)$, that is $a\Delta^{0}=b\Delta^{0}$, so $a=b$; hence $\eta$ is injective. [F1, step 1.2]

3.1 **The shape of the elements of $G$.** By step 1.3, applied to $(\Delta^{2k},0)$, the class $[\Delta^{2k},0]$ is invertible with $[\Delta^{2k},0]^{-1}=[1,k]$; and $[a,k]=[a,0]\cdot[1,k]$ because $(a\cdot1,0+k)=(a,k)$. Hence every element of $G$ has the form $\eta(a)\eta(\Delta)^{-2k}$; taking $b:=\Delta^{2k}$ and using $[\Delta^{2k},0]=\eta(b)$ this is $\eta(a)\eta(b)^{-1}$, which is (b)(i). [F1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 **The universal property.** Let $f\colon B_n^{+}\to H$ be a monoid homomorphism into a group. Define $\bar f([a,k]):=f(a)f(\Delta)^{-2k}$. This is well defined: if $a\Delta^{2l}=b\Delta^{2k}$ then applying $f$ and multiplying by $f(\Delta)^{-2k-2l}$ gives $f(a)f(\Delta)^{-2k}=f(b)f(\Delta)^{-2l}$. It is a homomorphism: $\bar f([a,k][b,l])=\bar f([ab,k+l])=f(a)f(b)f(\Delta)^{-2k-2l}$, while $\bar f([a,k])\bar f([b,l])=f(a)f(\Delta)^{-2k}f(b)f(\Delta)^{-2l}$, and these agree because $f(\Delta)^{2}=f(\Delta^{2})$ lies in the centre of the image of $f$ by [F3], so that $f(\Delta)^{-2k}f(b)=f(b)f(\Delta)^{-2k}$. Finally $\bar f(\eta(a))=\bar f([a,0])=f(a)$, and $\bar f$ is unique with this property because every element of $G$ is a product of elements $\eta(a)$ and inverses $\eta(\Delta)^{-1}$, as shown in step 3.1, so a group homomorphism out of $G$ is determined by its values on the $\eta(a)$. [F1, F3, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 **Identification with $B_n$.** The assignment $\sigma_i\mapsto\eta(\sigma_i)$ satisfies the defining relations of [F4] because they hold in $B_n^{+}$ and $\eta$ is a homomorphism, so [F4] gives a group homomorphism $\psi\colon B_n\to G$ with $\psi(\sigma_i)=\eta(\sigma_i)$. In the other direction, the relations hold in $B_n$ itself, so [F5] gives a monoid homomorphism $\kappa\colon B_n^{+}\to B_n$ with $\kappa(\sigma_i)=\sigma_i$, and step 4.1 applied to $f:=\kappa$ gives a group homomorphism $\varphi\colon G\to B_n$ with $\varphi\circ\eta=\kappa$ and hence $\varphi(\eta(\sigma_i))=\sigma_i$. Then $\varphi\circ\psi$ and $\mathrm{id}_{B_n}$ are group endomorphisms of $B_n$ agreeing on the generators $\sigma_i$, which generate $B_n$ by [F4], so $\varphi\circ\psi=\mathrm{id}_{B_n}$; similarly $\psi\circ\varphi$ and $\mathrm{id}_{G}$ are group endomorphisms of $G$ agreeing on the $\eta(\sigma_i)$, and these generate $G$ as a group because the $\sigma_i$ generate $B_n^{+}$ by [F1] so every element of $G$ is a product of elements $\eta(\sigma_i)^{\pm1}$ by step 3.1; hence $\psi\circ\varphi=\mathrm{id}_G$. Thus $\psi$ is an isomorphism with inverse $\varphi$, and $\kappa=\varphi\circ\eta$ is injective with image $\varphi(\eta(B_n^{+}))$, the set of classes of positive words. This is (c). [F1, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 **Assembly.** Part (a) is the existence statement of [F2] together with the common multiple $\Delta^{2r}$ of step 1.3 (take $c,d$ as there, for $a$ and $b$, with a common even power). Part (b) comprises the construction of $G$ in steps 1.1--1.3, the group axioms in step 2.1, the injectivity of $\eta$ in step 2.2, the shape of the elements in step 3.1, and the universal property in step 4.1. Part (c) is step 5.1. No inverse is assumed in $B_n^{+}$ anywhere: the inverses live in the constructed quotient, and the only inputs about $\Delta$ are the $\Delta$-power divisibility and the centrality of $\Delta^{2}$. All constructions are explicit, all exponents are natural numbers, and no choice principle is used. ∎ [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1]

## Remarks

- **Why the construction uses $\Delta^{2}$ and not $\Delta$.** The relation
  defining $\approx$ compares $a\Delta^{2l}$ with $b\Delta^{2k}$; centrality of
  $\Delta^{2}$ is what makes the product well defined and makes
  $f(\Delta)^{-2k}$ commute with the image of $B_n^{+}$ in the universal
  property. Odd powers would only be central in the cases $n\le2$: for $n\ge3$
  the element $\Delta$ conjugates $\sigma_i$ to $\sigma_{n-i}$ rather than
  centralising it ([[lem-conjugation-by-delta-reverses-artin-generators]]), and
  the same construction with $2$ replaced by $1$ would fail to be well defined.
- **Comparison with GM.** GM argue that "as every two elements have a common
  multiple (some power of $\Delta$), and $B_n^{+}$ is cancellative, Ore's
  condition says that $B_n^{+}$ embeds in its group of fractions. This group of
  fractions, due to presentation (3.1), is precisely $B_n$." Steps 1.1--4.1
  spell out the standard construction behind that sentence: the Ore condition
  is used only to find $r$ in step 1.3, cancellation only in steps 1.1 and 1.2
  and in step 2.2, and the presentation comparison is step 4.1.
- **What the injectivity of $\kappa$ says.** Since every element of $B_n$ is
  $\kappa(a)\kappa(b)^{-1}$, and $\kappa$ is injective, the usual abuse of
  notation is justified: from this point on a positive braid may be regarded as
  an element of $B_n$, and the monoid orders $\preccurlyeq_L,\preccurlyeq_R$
  extend to $B_n$
  ([[thm-left-and-right-divisibility-extend-to-lattice-orders-on-the-braid-group]]).
  The statement that $B_n^{+}$ is not itself a group is
  [[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]] (no nontrivial
  invertible element).
- The group $G$ is presented by the same generators and the same relations as
  $B_n$: this is what step 4.1 verifies, and it is the sense in which "the
  group of fractions is the Artin braid group" rather than merely a group
  containing $B_n^{+}$.
- Nothing here uses a choice principle: $G$ is a quotient of an explicit set,
  and the exponent $r$ of step 1.3 is bounded by a natural number read off from
  a word for $a$.
