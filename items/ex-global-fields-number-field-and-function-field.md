## Example

The field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ is a number field. For every finite field
$k=\mathbb F_q$, the rational function field $k(t)$ is a global function
field whose exact constant field is $k$. Thus both are global fields.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ and a finite field $k=\mathbb F_q$.

[F1] A number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$.
([[def-number-field]])

[F2] A global field is a number field or a finitely generated
one-variable function field over a finite exact constant field.
([[def-global-field]])

[F3] A finite field is a field with finite underlying set.
([[def-finite-field-and-its-order]])

[F4] $F(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ denotes the smallest subfield containing the base
field and the listed generators; this is a finitely generated extension.
([[def-finitely-generated-field-extension]])

[F5] An element is algebraic if it is a root of a nonzero polynomial over the
base field, and algebraicity is transitive through towers of algebraic field
extensions. ([[def-algebraic-and-transcendental-elements]],
[[thm-transitivity-of-algebraicity]])

[F6] A simple algebraic extension generated by an element with minimal
polynomial of degree $n$ has degree $n$ and the corresponding power basis.
([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]])

[F7] The rational function field $k(t)$ is the fraction field of $k[t]$;
each element is $f(t)/g(t)$ with $f,g\in k[t]$ and $g\ne0$.
([[def-field-of-fractions]], [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]])

[F8] The relative algebraic closure of $k$ in $F$ is the set of elements of
$F$ algebraic over $k$. ([[def-relative-algebraic-closure]])

## Verification



**Given:** The fields above.

1.1 Suppose $\sqrt2=a/b$ for relatively prime integers $a,b$ with $b\ne0$. Then $a^2=2b^2$, so $a$ is even; writing $a=2c$ gives $b^2=2c^2$, so $b$ is even as well, contradicting relative primality. Thus $X^2-2$ has no rational root and, being quadratic, is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$. It is the minimal polynomial of $\sqrt2$, so [F6] gives $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2$. By [F1], $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)$ is a number field and hence a global field by [F2]. [F1, F2, F6, given, algebra]

1.2 The indeterminate $t$ is transcendental over $k$, and [F7] identifies $k(t)$ with the fraction field of $k[t]$. It is generated over $k$ by $t$, while $k(t)/k(t)$ is algebraic, so it is a one-variable function field as in [F2]. [F2, F3, F4, F7, given]

2.1 Let $x\in k(t)$ be algebraic over $k$ and write $x=f(t)/g(t)$ using [F7]. Form $P(X)=f(X)-xg(X)\in k(x)[X]$. Then $P(t)=0$. If $P$ is the zero polynomial, choose a nonzero coefficient $g_j$ of $g$; coefficientwise equality gives $f_j=xg_j$, hence $x=f_j/g_j\in k$. If $P$ is nonzero, its root $t$ is algebraic over $k(x)$. Since $x$ is algebraic over $k$, [F5] implies $t$ is algebraic over $k$, contradicting the transcendence of $t$. Thus every element of $k(t)$ algebraic over $k$ lies in $k$; the reverse inclusion is immediate, so [F8] gives exact constant field $k$. [F5, F7, F8, step 1.2, given, cases, algebra]

3.1 Steps 1.1, 1.2, and 2.1 prove the two claims in the Example: one number field and one function field with the stated full constant field are global fields. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
