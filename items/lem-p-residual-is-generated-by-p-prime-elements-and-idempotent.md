## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group and let $p$ be a prime. Then:

1. $O^{p}(G)$ is generated by the set of $p'$-elements of $G$;
2. $G=P\,O^{p}(G)$ for every Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $G$;
3. $O^{p}\bigl(O^{p}(G)\bigr)=O^{p}(G)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$, a prime $p$, and the $p$-residual $O^{p}(G)$ of [[def-p-residual-of-a-finite-group]]; $p'$-elements are as in [[def-p-prime-core-of-a-finite-group]].

[F1] $O^{p}(G)\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$, $G/O^{p}(G)$ is a finite $p$-group, and $O^{p}(G)$ is the least normal subgroup of $G$ with $p$-group quotient ([[def-p-residual-of-a-finite-group]], [[def-normal-subgroup]]).

[F2] If $\theta:G\to A$ is a homomorphism into a finite $p$-group $A$ and $x\in G$ is a $p'$-element, then $\theta(x)=e$: $\operatorname{ord}(\theta(x))$ divides $\operatorname{ord}(x)$ and divides $|A|$, hence is both prime to $p$ and a power of $p$, so equals $1$ ([[lem-order-characterisation]], [[lem-group-homomorphism-basic-properties]], [[cor-order-of-element-divides-group-order]], [[def-order-in-a-group]], [[def-p-adic-valuation]]).

[F3] By [[lem-sylow-subgroups-of-a-normal-subgroup-are-intersections]] applied to the normal subgroup $O^{p}(G)$ and a Sylow $P$ of $G$: $P\cap O^{p}(G)\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(O^{p}(G))$ and $G=P\,O^{p}(G)$, since $[G:O^{p}(G)]$ is a power of $p$ by [F1] ([[thm-sylow-first-theorem]], [[def-sylow-p-subgroup]]).

[F4] If a prime $\ell$ divides the order of a finite group $H$, then $H$ has an element of order $\ell$ ([[thm-cauchys-theorem-for-finite-groups]]).

[F5] The subgroup generated by a set $Y$ consists of finite products of elements of $Y$ and their inverses; conjugation is an automorphism, so $g\langle Y\rangle g^{-1}=\langle gYg^{-1}\rangle$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]], [[thm-conjugation-is-an-automorphism]], [[lem-group-homomorphism-basic-properties]]).

[F6] $G/R$ is a finite $p$-group if and only if $[G:R]$ is a power of $p$; if $[G:R]$ has a prime divisor $\ell$, then $\ell\ne p$ means $G/R$ is not a $p$-group; and $x^{k}=e$ implies $(x^{k})^{j}=e$ for every $j$ ([[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]], [[def-finite-p-group]], [[lem-every-integer-above-one-has-a-prime-divisor]], [[lem-divisibility-basic]], [[def-order-in-a-group]]).

[F7] $\operatorname{ord}(x^{-1})=\operatorname{ord}(x)$, and conjugation preserves orders ([[lem-order-characterisation]], [[thm-conjugation-is-an-automorphism]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $Y$ be the set of $p'$-elements of $G$ and let $R:=\langle Y\rangle$. Then $R\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$: if $y\in Y$ then every conjugate $gyg^{-1}$ is again a $p'$-element by [F7], so $gYg^{-1}=Y$ and hence $gRg^{-1}=R$ by [F5]. [F5, F7, algebra]

1.2 $G/R$ is a $p$-group. Suppose not; then by [F6] some prime $q\ne p$ divides $[G:R]$, so $G/R$ has an element $yR$ of order $q$ by [F4]. Let $\operatorname{ord}(y)=p^{s}e$ with $p\nmid e$ ([F1] of [[def-p-adic-valuation]]); then $(y^{p^{s}})^{e}=y^{\operatorname{ord}(y)}=e$, so $y^{p^{s}}$ has order dividing $e$ and is therefore a $p'$-element, that is, $y^{p^{s}}\in Y\subseteq R$ by [F6]. Hence $(yR)^{p^{s}}=R$, so the order of $yR$ divides $p^{s}$; but that order is $q$, a prime different from $p$, a contradiction. [F4, F5, F6, algebra]

1.3 Claim 2 is exactly the second assertion of [F3]. [F3]

2.1 Therefore $O^{p}(G)\le R$: $R$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ with $p$-group quotient by steps 1.1 and 1.2, and $O^{p}(G)$ is the least such by [F1]. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Conversely $Y\subseteq O^{p}(G)$: the natural map $G\to G/O^{p}(G)$ is a homomorphism onto a finite $p$-group by [F1], so it kills every $p'$-element by [F2]. Hence $R=\langle Y\rangle\subseteq O^{p}(G)$, and with step 2.1, $R=O^{p}(G)$; this is claim 1. [F1, F2, step 2.1]

4.1 Claim 3: put $K:=O^{p}(G)$. By claim 1 applied to the finite group $K$, $O^{p}(K)$ is generated by the $p'$-elements of $K$; every such element is a $p'$-element of $G$ and so lies in $K$ trivially, while conversely claim 1 gives $K=\langle Y\rangle$ with $Y$ the $p'$-elements of $G$, and every $y\in Y$ is an element of $K$ and hence a $p'$-element of $K$. The two generating sets agree, so $O^{p}(K)=K$. ∎ [step 3.1, algebra]
