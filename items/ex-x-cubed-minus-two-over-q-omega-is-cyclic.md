## Example

Let $\omega$ be a primitive cube root of unity and put $F=\mathbb Q(\omega)$.
Then $x^3-2$ is irreducible over $F$, and its splitting field is

$$F(\sqrt[3]{2}),$$

which is a cyclic extension of degree $3$ over $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The field $F=\mathbb Q(\omega)$ and the polynomial $x^3-2$.

[L1] Norm and trace are given by the embedding formulas ([[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]]).

[L2] Over a base containing $\mu_3$, a degree-three extension is cyclic exactly when it is generated by a root of an irreducible cubic $x^3-a$ ([[thm-cyclic-degree-n-extensions-with-roots-of-unity-are-kummer]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The field $F/\mathbb Q$ has degree $2$. If $2=\beta^3$ for some $\beta\in F$, then taking norms from $F$ to $\mathbb Q$ gives $$4=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(2)=N_{F/\mathbb Q}(\beta)^3,$$ which is impossible because no rational cube equals $4$. So $2$ is not a cube in $F$. Hence $x^3-2$ has no root in $F$ and is irreducible there. [L1, algebra]

2.1 Since $\omega\in F$, all roots of $x^3-2$ are $$\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega\sqrt[3]{2},\ \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2},$$ so they all lie in $F(\sqrt[3]{2})$. The irreducibility from step 1.1 and [L2] therefore make $F(\sqrt[3]{2})/F$ cyclic of degree $3$. [L2, step 1.1] ∎
