## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. Let $p,q$ be coprime nonzero integers, let $K=T(p,q)$ be the oriented primitive slope curve in the standard Heegaard torus, and let $\rho_t(z_1,z_2)=(e^{2\pi i p t}z_1,e^{2\pi i q t}z_2)$ be the weighted circle action on the unit $S^3\subset\mathbb C^2$. Choose $N(K)$ to be a sufficiently small closed metric tube around the orbit $K$ for the round metric; this tube is invariant because $\rho_t$ is isometric and preserves $K$. Set $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$. Let $x$ and $y$ be based generators of the fundamental groups of the two solid-torus pieces obtained by cutting $E_K$ along the annulus left on the Heegaard torus, oriented so that the annulus core maps to $x^p$ and $y^q$. Then

$$\pi_1(E_K)=\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle.$$

The common element $h=x^p=y^q$ is central and represents a regular fiber of a Seifert fibration of $E_K$ over a disk. The fibration has two distinguished core fibers of multiplicities $|p|$ and $|q|$; a fiber of multiplicity one is regular, and a fiber of multiplicity greater than one is exceptional. Thus there are exactly two exceptional fibers when $|p|,|q|>1$, and one is regular when one absolute parameter is $1$.

For any integers $\alpha,\beta$ with $p\alpha+q\beta=1$, compatible based paths identify the positive normal-disk meridian and preferred longitude in the peripheral system as

$$\mu=y^\alpha x^\beta,\qquad \lambda=h\mu^{-pq}.$$

Here the positive meridian is the oriented boundary of a normal disk, and the preferred longitude has degree one around $K$ and maps to zero in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Their homology classes form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$, so both classes are primitive.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, coprime nonzero integers $p,q$, the chosen knot exterior and based paths, and the specified Heegaard torus.

[A1] Full AC is the axiom that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] Full AC implies the Axiom of Countable Choice, which is the hypothesis of the exterior and peripheral-system definitions ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-coprime-torus-knot]] identifies $K$ with the oriented primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$ on the standard unknotted Heegaard torus.

[F2] [[def-knot-exterior]] sets $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ with its boundary torus, and [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]] defines the positive meridian by the normal-disk boundary and the preferred longitude by degree one and zero exterior homology.

[F3] Under full AC, [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] proves $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ generated by the positive meridian, proves existence and uniqueness of the preferred-longitude class, and proves that their boundary classes form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$.

[F4] [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]] identifies the fundamental group of a union from a path-connected open cover with the pushout of the two inclusion-induced maps from the intersection.

[F5] The smooth inverse function theorem says that a smooth map with invertible differential at a point is a diffeomorphism between suitable open neighbourhoods of that point and its image ([[thm-smooth-inverse-function-theorem-on-manifolds]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The knot, exterior, Heegaard splitting and AC hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 **The round-metric tube is an admissible invariant tube.** Full AC supplies the countable-choice hypothesis for the smooth normal-bundle structure by [L1]. Regard $S^3$ as the unit sphere in $\mathbb R^4$ with its round metric, and identify each quotient normal plane $T_xS^3/T_xK$ with its orthogonal complement to $T_xK$ in $T_xS^3$. For $v\in\nu_x(K)$ let $\widehat v$ be that representative and define
$$\Phi(x,v)=\cos(\|v\|)x+\frac{\sin(\|v\|)}{\|v\|}\widehat v,$$
with $\Phi(x,0)=x$. This is the round normal exponential map. Its differential along the zero section sends a tangent-normal pair $(u,w)$ to $u+w$, an isomorphism. By [F5] it is a local diffeomorphism at every zero vector. Compactness of $K$ gives a uniform disk neighbourhood on which its differential stays invertible. After shrinking that radius, $\Phi$ is injective there: otherwise there would be distinct $(x_n,v_n),(y_n,w_n)$ with $\|v_n\|,\|w_n\|\to0$ and equal images; compactness gives subsequences converging to $(x,0),(y,0)$, equality gives $x=y$, and a local inverse near $(x,0)$ contradicts distinctness. Thus, for some $R>0$, $\Phi$ is a diffeomorphism from the open normal disk bundle of radius $R$ onto an open neighbourhood of $K$. Choose $0<\epsilon<R/2$ also smaller than $\pi$ and than the distance from $K$ to either coordinate circle. The closed metric $\epsilon$-neighbourhood of $K$ equals $\Phi(\{v:\|v\|\le\epsilon\})$: a nearest point of $K$ exists for every point in that neighbourhood, and minimizing round distance is equivalent to maximizing the Euclidean dot product with that point; differentiating along $K$ shows the corresponding short great-circle segment is normal to $K$. Conversely, each normal vector of length at most $\epsilon$ gives a geodesic of that length, and injectivity on the radius-$R$ disk bundle rules out a closer nearest point. Hence this $N(K)$ is the closed tubular neighbourhood required by [F2], is disjoint from the two coordinate circles, and is invariant under $\rho_t$, since these maps are orthogonal and preserve $K$. Finally, the local product charts of $\Phi$ and compactness of $K$ allow $\epsilon$ to be decreased so that $N(K)\cap T_r$ is an annular neighbourhood of $K$ in the Heegaard torus; its two halves are boundary collars on the two solid-torus sides. [A1, L1, F2, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The primitive slope $p\alpha+q\beta$ is essential on the Heegaard torus. Removing a small annular neighborhood of $K$ from that torus leaves a closed annulus $A$. On either side, removing the corresponding half of the tube from a solid torus only removes a boundary collar, so the two pieces retract to solid tori $W_x,W_y$ and their overlap retracts to $A$. Thicken the pieces by half-collars to obtain path-connected open sets covering $E_K$ whose intersection retracts to $A$. The core of $A$ winds $p$ times around the core generator $x$ on one side and $q$ times around $y$ on the other. Full AC [A1] supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of [F2] by [L1]. [given, A1, F1, F2, L1, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 The generic orbits of $\rho_t$ have slope $(p,q)$ and trivial stabilizer: if both coordinates are nonzero, a stabilizing parameter $t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ satisfies $pt,qt\in\mathbb Z$, hence $t=0$ because $\gcd(p,q)=1$. The stabilizer orders on the coordinate circles $z_1=0$ and $z_2=0$ are $|q|$ and $|p|$, respectively. The orbit space is a sphere: on each intermediate torus $|z_1|=r$ with $0<r<1$, quotienting by the primitive circle orbit leaves a circle, and at $r=0,1$ that quotient circle collapses to the corresponding coordinate-orbit point. The invariant tube from step 1.1 lies in the free-orbit region, and its quotient is a disk: every orbit in its normal disk bundle meets the fiber over one chosen point of $K$ exactly once. Removing its interior therefore removes a disk from the orbit sphere, leaving a disk. The quotient map is a Seifert fibration of $E_K$ with the two stated core multiplicities. Multiplicity one is regular; multiplicity greater than one is exceptional. [F1, given, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The three fundamental groups in this cover are $\pi_1(W_x)=\langle x\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, $\pi_1(W_y)=\langle y\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, and $\pi_1(A)=\langle a\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$, with inclusion maps $a\mapsto x^p$ and $a\mapsto y^q$. By [F4], $\pi_1(E_K)$ is their pushout. A group receiving maps from the two cyclic factors is determined by images of $x,y$; the maps agree on the intersection exactly when $x^p=y^q$. Hence the pushout has presentation $\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle$. [F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 A regular orbit parallel to $K$ represents the annulus core $x^p=y^q=h$. This element commutes with $x$ because it is $x^p$, and commutes with $y$ because it is $y^q$; as $x,y$ generate the group from step 3.1, $h$ is central. [step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.2 Choose integers $c,d$ with $pd-qc=1$ and let $L=c\alpha+d\beta$. With the coordinate orientation determined by $\alpha$ followed by $\beta$, the ordered intersection number $K\cdot L=pd-qc=1$, so $L$ is a simple curve meeting $K$ once. At the intersection, the boundary $m$ of a small normal disk splits into arcs $\gamma_x,\gamma_y$ on the two solid-torus sides, while $L$ splits into its crossing arc and complementary arc $L_1$. Inserting $L_1L_1^{-1}$ into $m=\gamma_x\gamma_y$ gives $m=(\gamma_xL_1)(L_1^{-1}\gamma_y)$. The first factor winds $c$ times around the $x$ core and the second winds $-d$ times around the $y$ core, hence $m=x^c y^{-d}$. To calibrate signs geometrically, the positively oriented coordinate cores representing $x$ and $y$ bound the hemispherical disks $D_x=\{\operatorname{Im}z_2=0,\ \operatorname{Re}z_2\ge0\}\cap S^3$ and $D_y=\{\operatorname{Im}z_1=0,\ \operatorname{Re}z_1\ge0\}\cap S^3$, oriented by their respective core boundaries. The knot meets $D_x$ transversely in $|q|$ points, each with intersection sign $\operatorname{sgn}(q)$, and meets $D_y$ in $|p|$ points, each with sign $\operatorname{sgn}(p)$. For the first sign, at a point where $z_2$ is positive real, the ambient orientation is the complex $z_1$ orientation followed by increasing $\operatorname{Im}z_2$; the component of the knot tangent transverse to $D_x$ has sign $q$. For the second, at positive real $z_1$, the ambient orientation is increasing $\operatorname{Im}z_1$ followed by the complex $z_2$ orientation, and the transverse tangent component has sign $p$. Deleting small disks about these intersections gives oriented surfaces in the knot complement with boundary relations $[x]=q[\mu]$ and $[y]=p[\mu]$, where $\mu$ is the positive normal-disk meridian. Thus the abelianization $x\mapsto q$, $y\mapsto p$ sends this geometric meridian to $1$: [F3] makes $[\mu]$ a generator, and the displayed relations and $\gcd(p,q)=1$ fix its sign. The disk-boundary curve $m$ has abelianization $cq-dp=-1$, so its orientation is opposite to that positive meridian. Thus $\mu=m^{-1}=y^d x^{-c}$. Setting $\alpha=d$ and $\beta=-c$ gives $p\alpha+q\beta=1$ and the stated word; another Bezout solution changes the exponents by $(qk,-pk)$, so the word is unchanged because $y^{qk}=h^k=x^{pk}$ and $h$ is central. [A1, L1, F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

5.1 Abelianizing the presentation in step 3.1 gives the relation $p[x]=q[y]$. The map $x\mapsto q$, $y\mapsto p$ is surjective because $\gcd(p,q)=1$, and its kernel in $\mathbb Z^2$ is generated by $(p,-q)$, exactly the relation vector; hence it identifies this quotient with $\mathbb Z$. Step 4.2 sends the positive meridian to $1$, so by [F3] it is the positive generator of $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Thus $[h]=p[x]=pq[\mu]$. The regular orbit $h$ is a degree-one parallel of $K$ under the equivariant normal-bundle projection from step 1.1, and $\mu$ has degree zero there, so $\lambda=h\mu^{-pq}$ has degree one and zero image in $H_1(E_K)$. Uniqueness in [F3] identifies it with the preferred longitude; [F3] also proves that $[\mu],[\lambda]$ form a basis of $H_1(\partial E_K;\mathbb Z)$, so each is primitive. [L1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
