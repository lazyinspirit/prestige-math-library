## Statement

Let $D\subset B^4$ be a smooth properly embedded disk with boundary knot
$K\subset S^3=\partial B^4$. Choose a product tubular neighborhood
$\nu(D)\cong D^2\times D^2$ whose intersection with $S^3$ is the chosen tube
$N(K)$, and let $X=\overline{B^4\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(D)}$. Then the
inclusion $E_K\hookrightarrow X$ induces an isomorphism
$$H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\cong}H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$$
carrying the positive meridian of $K$ to the positive normal-circle generator.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth proper disk $D$ in the standard $B^4$, its product tubular neighborhood, and the induced knot exterior on the boundary.

[F1] [[lem-knot-exterior-h1-is-generated-by-meridian]]: $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_K]$.

[F2] [[thm-excision-for-singular-homology]] and [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-a-pair-in-singular-homology]]: relative homology excision and the natural pair sequence apply to the compact manifold pairs below.

[F3] [[cor-contractible-nonempty-spaces-have-the-homology-of-a-point]] gives the homology of any nonempty contractible space. The contractions $x\mapsto(1-t)x$ of $B^4$ and $(u,v)\mapsto((1-t)u,(1-t)v)$ of $D^2\times D^2$ therefore make their positive-degree homology zero. By [[thm-homotopy-equivalences-induce-isomorphisms-on-singular-homology]], the retraction $(u,z)\mapsto(0,z)$ identifies the homology of $D^2\times S^1$ with that of $S^1$; [[cor-homology-of-spheres]] gives $H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and $H_2(S^1;\mathbb Z)=0$.

[F4] [[def-knot-exterior]] and [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]] fix the boundary exterior and positive-meridian orientation.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The portion of $\partial X$ added by deleting $\nu(D)$ is the normal-circle bundle $D^2\times S^1$, and it meets $E_K$ in $S^1\times S^1$. By excision [F2], the relative pair $(B^4,X)$ has the homology of $(D^2\times D^2,D^2\times S^1)$. The latter solid-torus subspace has $H_1\cong\mathbb Z$ generated by a normal circle and $H_2=0$, while $D^2\times D^2$ is contractible by [F3]. Its pair sequence [F2] therefore identifies $H_2(B^4,X;\mathbb Z)$ with $\mathbb Z$, generated by a normal disk whose boundary is that normal circle. [F2, F3, given, construct]

2.1 In the pair sequence of $(B^4,X)$, the segment $$H_2(B^4)\longrightarrow H_2(B^4,X)\xrightarrow{\partial}H_1(X)\longrightarrow H_1(B^4)$$ has zero end groups by [F3]. Hence $\partial$ is an isomorphism. The generator from step 1.1 maps to the normal circle on $D^2\times S^1$. A path in the base disk $D^2$ from an interior point to a boundary point gives a homotopy of these normal circles to the boundary meridian of $K$, with orientation fixed by [F4]. Thus $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[\mu_X]$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 The inclusion $E_K\hookrightarrow X$ carries the boundary meridian $\mu_K$ to the same normal circle $\mu_X$. It is therefore an isomorphism between the infinite cyclic groups in [F1] and step 2.1. [F1, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
