## Statement refuted

Write $G(K):=\pi_1(S^3\setminus K)$ for the group of a smooth oriented knot $K$. The claim is that for every pair of such knots $K_1,K_2$, an isomorphism $G(K_1)^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong G(K_2)^{\mathrm{ab}}$ implies that $K_1$ and $K_2$ are ambient isotopic.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The crossing-free unknot code and the standard three-crossing trefoil diagram, with their Wirtinger presentations.

[F1] The constructor returns one meridian generator per under-arc and one relator per crossing. [[alg-knot-wirtinger-presentation-from-code]]

[F2] The over- and under-meridians at an oriented crossing satisfy the conjugacy relation determined by its chart. [[lem-wirtinger-local-meridian-conjugacy]]

[F3] The constructor's presentation group is isomorphic to the knot complement group. [[thm-knot-wirtinger-presentation-correctness]]

[F4] A group presentation is a quotient of the free group on the generators by the relator normal closure. [[def-group-presentation]]

[F5] The free group on one generator is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$ by exponent sum. [[def-free-group]]

[F6] Quotient maps from free groups factor uniquely through their relator normal closures. [[thm-quotient-group-universal-property]]

[F7] The commutator subgroup is generated by $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$. [[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]

[F8] The quotient $G^{\mathrm{ab}}=G/[G,G]$ is abelian and universal among homomorphisms to abelian groups. [[thm-derived-subgroup-is-characteristic-and-abelianization-is-universal]]

[F9] An ambient isotopy of knots induces a homeomorphism of their complements. [[def-oriented-link-in-s-three-and-ambient-isotopy]]

[F10] A pointed homeomorphism induces a fundamental-group isomorphism. [[def-induced-homomorphism-on-fundamental-groups]]

[F11] Induced fundamental-group maps are functorial under composition. [[thm-induced-fundamental-group-map-functoriality]]

## Counterexample

Let $G_J:=\pi_1(S^3\setminus J)$ for a knot $J$. Let $U$ be the crossing-free unknot and let $T$ be the closed three-crossing diagram displayed above, formed from two oriented strands and the two exterior closure arcs. With the shown orientation and under-arc labels $a,b,c$, its crossing triples $(o,L,R)$ are $(b,c,a)$, $(c,a,b)$, and $(a,b,c)$, so its crossing equations are $b=cac^{-1}$, $a=bcb^{-1}$, and $c=aba^{-1}$. Eliminating $c$ gives $G_T\cong\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle$. The map $a\mapsto(12)$, $b\mapsto(23)$ is a surjection of $G_T$ onto the nonabelian permutation group $S_3$. Consequently $G_U\cong\mathbb Z$ and $G_T$ is noncyclic. Their abelianizations are both $\mathbb Z$, so they are not distinguished by abelianization. Ambient-isotopic knots have isomorphic complement groups, hence $U$ and $T$ are not ambient isotopic. Thus abelianization alone does not classify knots.

1.1 By [F1] and [F3], the crossing-free unknot group is $\langle x\mid\ \rangle$, the free group on one generator. By [F5] it is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z$. All commutators in $\mathbb Z$ are trivial by [F7], so $[\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z]=1$ and [F8] identifies its abelianization with $\mathbb Z$. [given, F1, F3, F4, F5, F7, F8, algebra]

1.2 A valid rational code for the displayed trefoil uses the two diagonals from $(\pm1,3-j)$ to $(\mp1,2-j)$ for $j=0,1,2$, subdivided at their crossing $(0,5/2-j)$, with the right-starting branch over and both diagonals oriented downward. Close the left endpoints by $(-1,0)\to(-2,0)\to(-2,3)\to(-1,3)$ and the right endpoints by the reflected route. Each strip has just its one transverse crossing, distinct strip interiors and the two exterior closure routes are disjoint, and the three swaps trace one circle. Thus these integer and half-integer vertices give admissible input for [F1]; rounding the bends gives the displayed diagram. The trefoil's displayed crossing triples and [F2] give $b=cac^{-1}$, $a=bcb^{-1}$, and $c=aba^{-1}$. Substituting the last into the second yields $a=bab a^{-1}b^{-1}$; right multiplication by $ba$ gives $aba=bab$. Under this relation, $cac^{-1}=a b a b^{-1}a^{-1}=b$, so the first equation follows. Thus eliminating $c$ gives $\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle$, and [F3] identifies it with $G_T$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 In the abelian quotient of [F8], written additively, $aba=bab$ gives $2[a]+[b]=[a]+2[b]$, hence $[a]=[b]$ and the abelianization is generated by $[a]$. The map $G_T\to\mathbb Z$ sending $a,b$ to $1$ respects the relator and is surjective; by [F8] it factors through the abelianization. The map $\mathbb Z\to G_T^{\mathrm{ab}}$ sending $1$ to $[a]$ is inverse, so $G_T^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong\mathbb Z$. [F8, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 The map $a\mapsto(12)$, $b\mapsto(23)$ respects $aba=bab$, since both sides equal $(13)$. Its image contains two adjacent transpositions and hence all six elements of $S_3$, so it is surjective by [F4] and [F6]. Since $S_3$ is nonabelian, $G_T$ is nonabelian; every quotient of the cyclic group $\mathbb Z$ is cyclic, so $G_T\not\cong\mathbb Z\cong G_U$. [F4, F5, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 If $U$ and $T$ were ambient isotopic, [F9] would give a homeomorphism of their complements; [F10] and [F11] would induce a fundamental-group isomorphism. Step 2.2 rules this out. Their abelianizations nevertheless agree by steps 1.1 and 2.1, proving the stated counterexample. [F9, F10, F11, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
