## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $\mathfrak a_1,\ldots,\mathfrak a_r$ be
ideals of $R$, with $r\in\mathbb N$ and $r\ge1$, such that

$$\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak a_i=0$$

and every quotient ring $R/\mathfrak a_i$ ([[def-quotient-ring]]) is Noetherian.
Then $R$ is Noetherian.

The restriction $r\ge1$ avoids the vacuous endpoint. If $r=0$, the empty
intersection is $R$ itself ([[lem-ideal-criterion-and-intersections]]), so the
displayed condition forces $R=0$; the conclusion is then still true because the
zero ring is Noetherian.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, ideals $\mathfrak a_1,\ldots,\mathfrak a_r$ with $r\ge1$ and zero intersection, and Noetherian quotient rings $R/\mathfrak a_i$ with canonical projections $\pi_i\colon R\to R/\mathfrak a_i$.

[L1] A finite direct sum is Noetherian if and only if every summand is Noetherian, and it is Artinian if and only if every summand is Artinian. The empty direct sum is included ([[cor-finite-direct-sums-preserve-chain-conditions]]).

[L2] For a unital ring $R$ and a family $(M_i)_{i\in I}$ of left $R$-modules, the direct sum $\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i$ is the submodule of the coordinatewise product consisting of the families of finite support ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]).

[L3] A left $R$-module $M$ is **Noetherian** when every submodule of $M$ is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-module]]).

[L4] The canonical projection $R\to R/I$ is a surjective ring homomorphism with kernel $I$ ([[prop-canonical-quotient-ring-map]]).

[L5] An **$R$-algebra** is a unital ring $A$ with a unital ring homomorphism $\eta_A:R\to A$ of central image; the induced scalar action $ra:=\eta_A(r)a$ makes $A$ an $R$-module ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L6] A function $f:M\to N$ between left $R$-modules is an **$R$-module homomorphism** if $f(m+m')=f(m)+f(m')$ and $f(rm)=rf(m)$ for all $m,m'\in M$ and $r\in R$ ([[def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]]).

[L7] For a commutative ring, being Noetherian is equivalent to every ideal being finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Each $R/\mathfrak a_i$ is a Noetherian $R$-module for the action $r\cdot\bar x:=\pi_i(r)\bar x$, which is the algebra action along the surjective projection $\pi_i$. A subset of $R/\mathfrak a_i$ closed under that action is closed under multiplication by every element of $R/\mathfrak a_i$, because $\pi_i$ is onto, so the $R$-submodules of $R/\mathfrak a_i$ are exactly its ideals; each such ideal is generated over $R/\mathfrak a_i$ by a finite list, since $R/\mathfrak a_i$ is a Noetherian ring, and the same list generates it over $R$ because every coefficient in $R/\mathfrak a_i$ is $\pi_i$ of a coefficient in $R$. [L3, L4, L5, L7, given]

1.2 The map $\phi\colon R\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}R/\mathfrak a_i$, $\phi(x)=(\pi_1(x),\ldots,\pi_r(x))$, is $R$-linear, and $\phi(x)=0$ says $x\in\mathfrak a_i$ for every $i$, so $\ker\phi=\bigcap_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak a_i=0$ and $\phi$ is injective. [L2, L6, given]

2.1 The direct sum $\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}R/\mathfrak a_i$ has finitely many summands, each Noetherian as an $R$-module, so it is a Noetherian $R$-module. [L1, L2, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $\mathfrak b$ be an ideal of $R$. Its image $\phi(\mathfrak b)$ is an $R$-submodule of the direct sum, being the image of a submodule under an $R$-linear map, so it is generated by finitely many of its own elements $\phi(b_1),\ldots,\phi(b_k)$ with $b_1,\ldots,b_k\in\mathfrak b$ and $k\in\mathbb N$. For $b\in\mathfrak b$ write $\phi(b)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_j\phi(b_j)=\phi\big(\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jb_j\big)$ with $c_j\in R$; injectivity of $\phi$ gives $b=\sum_{j=1}^{k}c_jb_j$, so $\mathfrak b=(b_1,\ldots,b_k)$. [L3, L6, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Every ideal of $R$ is therefore finitely generated, and a commutative ring with that property is Noetherian. [L7, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Neither hypothesis can be dropped.** Without the zero intersection the map $\phi$ of step 1.2 has a kernel and step 3.1 cannot pull generators back; taking $r=1$ and $\mathfrak a_1=R$ shows what goes wrong, since the zero ring is Noetherian while $R$ need not be. Without finiteness of the list, the direct sum in step 2.1 need not be Noetherian.

- **The ideals are not assumed distinct, comparable or proper.** Repetitions and the values $0$ and $R$ are all admitted; only the intersection and the Noetherian quotients are used.

- **The intersection condition says $R$ embeds in the product of its quotients.** That is the entire content of step 1.2, and it is why the corollary is about an embedding rather than about a decomposition: no claim is made that $\phi$ is surjective.
