## Statement

Let $(\Pi_i)_{i\in I}$ be a family of pi-systems in a probability space
$(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P)$, and assume $\Omega\in\Pi_i$ for every $i$.
If the family $(\Pi_i)_{i\in I}$ is independent, then the sigma-algebras
$(\sigma(\Pi_i))_{i\in I}$ are independent.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Pi-systems $(\Pi_i)_{i\in I}$ with $\Omega\in\Pi_i$ for every $i$,
and assume the family $(\Pi_i)_{i\in I}$ is independent.

[L1] Independence of sigma-algebras and event classes is checked on finite
subfamilies. ([[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]])

[L2] If a lambda-system contains a pi-system, then it contains the sigma-algebra
generated by that pi-system. ([[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], it suffices to fix a finite list of distinct indices $i_0,\dots,i_{n-1}$ and prove that $\sigma(\Pi_{i_0}),\dots,\sigma(\Pi_{i_{n-1}})$ are independent. [given, L1]

1.2 Fix $B_k\in\Pi_{i_k}$ for $k<n-1$ and define $$\Lambda_{n-1}:=\left\{A\in\sigma(\Pi_{i_{n-1}}):\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_{k<n-1}B_k\cap A\right)=\left(\prod_{k<n-1}\mathbb P(B_k)\right)\mathbb P(A)\right\}.$$ Because $\Omega\in\Pi_{i_{n-1}}$, the class $\Lambda_{n-1}$ contains $\Omega$. It is closed under relative differences of nested sets and under increasing countable unions because both sides of the defining identity are countably additive in $A$. Since the original pi-systems are independent, every $A\in\Pi_{i_{n-1}}$ lies in $\Lambda_{n-1}$. Therefore [L2] gives $\sigma(\Pi_{i_{n-1}})\subseteq\Lambda_{n-1}$. [given, L2]

2.1 Repeat the construction of step 1.2 for the coordinates $n-2,n-3,\dots,0$, each time freezing already-promoted later coordinates in $\sigma(\Pi_{i_j})$ and keeping the earlier coordinates inside the original pi-systems. Each stage produces a lambda-system containing the relevant pi-system, so [L2] successively replaces every $\Pi_{i_j}$ by $\sigma(\Pi_{i_j})$. Hence $$\mathbb P\left(\bigcap_{k<n}A_k\right)=\prod_{k<n}\mathbb P(A_k)\qquad(A_k\in\sigma(\Pi_{i_k})).$$ [step 1.2, L2]

3.1 Since the finite choice of indices was arbitrary, the full family $(\sigma(\Pi_i))_{i\in I}$ is independent. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
