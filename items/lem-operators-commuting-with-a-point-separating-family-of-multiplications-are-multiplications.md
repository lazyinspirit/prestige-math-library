## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$ be a $\sigma$-finite measure
space and let $H=L^2(X,\Sigma,\mu;\mathbb C)$ with the integral pairing
([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]],
[[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]],
[[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]). For a bounded measurable
$h$ write $M_hf=h\cdot f$ for the multiplication operator. Let $\mathcal F$ be
a family of bounded real measurable functions generating $\Sigma$ modulo null
sets, in the sense that $\Sigma$ is the completion of the $\sigma$-algebra
$\sigma(\mathcal F)$ generated by the sets
$h^{-1}(B)$ $(h\in\mathcal F,\ B$ Borel$)$. If $T\in\mathcal B(H)$
([[def-bounded-linear-operator]]) commutes with $M_h$ for every
$h\in\mathcal F$, then $T=M_m$ for some bounded measurable $m$. If moreover
$T$ commutes with the unitary operators $U_Sf=f\circ S^{-1}$ induced by an
ergodic family of invertible measure-preserving transformations $S$ with measurable inverses of $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$
([[def-measure-preserving-transformation-and-system]],
[[def-ergodic-measure-preserving-system]]), then $m$ is constant almost
everywhere. Here ergodicity of the family means that every measurable set
invariant modulo null sets under every member is null or conull.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a $\sigma$-finite measure space $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$; the complex Hilbert space $H=L^2(X,\Sigma,\mu;\mathbb C)$; a family $\mathcal F$ of bounded real measurable functions whose generated $\sigma$-algebra completes to $\Sigma$; $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ commuting with every $M_h$, $h\in\mathcal F$.

[F1] $H$ is a Hilbert space, its elements are a.e. classes, and $\|f\|_2^2=\int|f|^2\,d\mu$, $\langle f,g\rangle=\int f\overline g\,d\mu$ ([[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[def-hilbert-space]]).

[F2] For bounded measurable $\varphi$ the operator $M_\varphi$ is bounded with $\|M_\varphi\|\le\|\varphi\|_\infty$, $M_\varphi^*=M_{\overline\varphi}$, $M_{\varphi\psi}=M_\varphi M_\psi$, $M_1=I$, and for real $\varphi$ the operator $M_\varphi$ is self-adjoint; the essential supremum $\|\varphi\|_\infty$ is the least essential bound, i.e. $|\varphi|\le\|\varphi\|_\infty$ a.e. ([[def-essential-supremum-with-respect-to-a-measure]], [[prop-essential-supremum-is-attained-as-the-least-essential-bound]], [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]).

[F3] The spectral theorem in PVM form: every bounded normal operator $N$ has a unique regular projection valued measure $E$ on the Borel $\sigma$-algebra of the compact set $\sigma(N)$ with $\Phi_E(f)=f(N)$ for every continuous $f$, where $\Phi_E$ is the bounded PVM integral and $f\mapsto f(N)$ the continuous calculus; the bounded Borel calculus satisfies $\mathbf 1_B(N)=E(B)$, and every $S\in\mathcal B(H)$ commuting with $N$ and $N^*$ commutes with $f(N)$ for every bounded Borel $f$ ([[thm-spectral-theorem-for-bounded-normal-operators-pvm-form]], [[thm-bounded-borel-pvm-integral]], [[thm-continuous-functional-calculus-for-bounded-normal-operators]], [[thm-borel-functional-calculus-for-bounded-normal-operators]], [[def-projection-valued-measure]]).

[F4] Bounded pointwise convergence on a finite measure space implies $L^2$ convergence, and scalar products and sums of bounded functions converge likewise ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F5] Every finite Borel measure on a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space is regular ([[cor-second-countable-lch-locally-finite-borel-measures-are-regular]]).

[F6] If an algebra of subsets has monotone closure $m_X$ and generated $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma_X$, then $m_X(\mathcal A)=\sigma_X(\mathcal A)$ ([[thm-monotone-class]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** AC, a $\sigma$-finite measure space $(X,\Sigma,\mu)$, the Hilbert space $H=L^2(X,\Sigma,\mu;\mathbb C)$, a generating family $\mathcal F$ of bounded real measurable functions, and $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ commuting with every $M_h$ for $h\in\mathcal F$.

1.1 If $\mu(X)=0$, then $H=0$, $T=M_0$, and $m=0$ is constant, so both conclusions hold. Hence assume $\mu(X)>0$, which by $\sigma$-finiteness implies $H\ne0$. For bounded real $h$, let $R(h)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb R:\mu(\{|h-\lambda|<\epsilon\})>0\text{ for every }\epsilon>0\}$. Its complement is a union of countably many rational intervals with null preimages, so $h\in R(h)$ a.e.; consequently $R(h)$ is nonempty, closed and bounded. The operator $M_h$ is bounded self-adjoint. If $\lambda\notin R(h)$ is real, then $|h-\lambda|\ge\epsilon$ a.e. for some $\epsilon>0$, giving the bounded inverse $M_{(h-\lambda)^{-1}}$ of $M_h-\lambda I$; for nonreal $\lambda$, use $|h-\lambda|\ge|\operatorname{Im}\lambda|$. If $\lambda\in R(h)$, $\sigma$-finiteness supplies a measurable $E\subseteq\{|h-\lambda|<\epsilon\}$ with $0<\mu(E)<\infty$. The unit vector $\mathbf1_E/\sqrt{\mu(E)}$ has image under $M_h-\lambda I$ of norm at most $\epsilon$, ruling out a bounded inverse. Thus $\sigma(M_h)=R(h)$. On Borel subsets of $R(h)$ define $F(B)=M_{\mathbf1_{h^{-1}(B)}}$. These are orthogonal projections with $F(R(h))=I$, and disjoint unions give strong countable additivity by dominated convergence applied to $|v|^2$ for each $v\in H$. The scalar measures $\langle F(B)v,v\rangle=\int_{h^{-1}(B)}|v|^2\,d\mu$ are finite, hence regular on the compact subset $R(h)$ of $\mathbb R$ by [F5]. Integrating simple functions and then uniform approximants gives $\Phi_F(f)=M_{f\circ h}$ for continuous $f$, in particular $\Phi_F(z)=M_h$. Uniqueness in [F3] identifies $F$ as the spectral PVM of $M_h$, so $\mathbf1_B(M_h)=M_{\mathbf1_{h^{-1}(B)}}$ for every Borel $B$; for $B\subseteq\mathbb R$ use $B\cap R(h)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]

1.2 If $N$ is bounded normal and $S\in\mathcal B(H)$ commutes with $N$ and $N^*$, then $S$ commutes with $f(N)$ for every bounded Borel function $f$ on $\sigma(N)$; in particular $S$ commutes with every spectral projection $\mathbf 1_B(N)=E(B)$. This is the commutation clause of the bounded Borel calculus [F3]. [F3]

1.3 Let $W:=\{g\in L^\infty(\mu):TM_g=M_gT\}$ be the set of bounded measurable functions whose multiplication commutes with $T$. Then $W$ contains the constants, is a complex vector space, is closed under products by [F2], and is closed under bounded pointwise almost-everywhere convergence: if $g_n\in W$, $\|g_n\|_\infty\le C$ and $g_n\to g$ pointwise a.e., then for $\varphi\in H$ the dominated convergence theorem [F4] gives $g_n\varphi\to g\varphi$ and $g_n(T\varphi)\to g(T\varphi)$ in $L^2$, while $T(g_n\varphi)=g_nT\varphi$; passing to the limit gives $T(g\varphi)=g(T\varphi)$, that is, $g\in W$. [F2, F4]

2.1 If $T$ commutes with $M_h$ for a bounded real measurable $h$, then $T$ commutes with $M_{\mathbf 1_{h^{-1}(B)}}$ for every Borel $B\subseteq\sigma(M_h)$: since $M_h$ is self-adjoint, $T$ commutes with $M_h$ and $M_h^*=M_h$, so step 1.2 makes $T$ commute with $\mathbf 1_B(M_h)$, which equals $M_{\mathbf 1_{h^{-1}(B)}}$ by step 1.1. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The set $\mathcal D:=\{B\in\Sigma:\mathbf 1_B\in W\}$ contains the algebra generated by the cylinder sets $h^{-1}(B')$ with $h\in\mathcal F$ and $B'$ Borel: each such indicator lies in $W$ by step 2.1 (completing $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ to $\Sigma$ only affects null sets, on which indicators differ by $W$-elements), and $W$ is closed under finite linear combinations and products by step 1.3, so finite unions and intersections of cylinders have indicators in $W$. Moreover $\mathcal D$ is closed under complements (as $1-\mathbf 1_B\in W$) and under increasing countable unions (as indicators converge boundedly pointwise), so it is a monotone class; by [F6] it contains $\sigma(\mathcal F)$ and hence, after completing by null sets, all of $\Sigma$. Since every bounded $\Sigma$-measurable function is a bounded pointwise limit of simple functions, step 1.3 shows $W=L^\infty(\mu)$: $T$ commutes with $M_g$ for every bounded measurable $g$. [F6, step 2.1, step 1.3]

4.1 Consequently $T=M_m$ for a bounded measurable $m$. Choose measurable sets $E_n$ of finite measure with $E_n\uparrow X$ up to a null set (possible by $\sigma$-finiteness), and put $u_n:=T\mathbf 1_{E_n}\in H$, $m_n:=u_n|_{E_n}$. For every bounded measurable $f$ supported in $E_n$ one has $Tf=T(M_f\mathbf 1_{E_n})=M_fT\mathbf 1_{E_n}=m_nf$ by step 3.1, so in particular $m_n\mathbf 1_B=T\mathbf 1_B$ for every measurable $B\subseteq E_n$ and $\|m_n\mathbf 1_B\|_2\le\|T\|\,\|\mathbf 1_B\|_2=\|T\|\mu(B)^{1/2}$. Applying this to $B=\{|m_n|>\|T\|+\epsilon\}\cap E_n$ gives $(\|T\|+\epsilon)\mu(B)^{1/2}\le\|T\|\mu(B)^{1/2}$, hence $\mu(B)=0$ and $\|m_n\|_\infty\le\|T\|$ on $E_n$; the functions $m_n$ therefore glue (they agree a.e. on overlaps by the same computation with $B\subseteq E_n\cap E_k$) to a bounded measurable $m$. For bounded measurable $f$ supported in one $E_n$, the already established identity gives $Tf=m_nf=M_mf$. These functions are dense in $H$: for arbitrary $f\in H$, the bounded functions $f\mathbf 1_{E_n}\mathbf 1_{\{|f|\le n\}}$ converge to $f$ in $L^2$ by [F4]. Since $T$ and $M_m$ are bounded, $T=M_m$. [F1, F4, step 3.1]

5.1 For the ergodic clause, observe first that if $S$ is measure preserving with induced unitary $U_Sf=f\circ S^{-1}$ and $T=M_m$ commutes with $U_S$, then $m=m\circ S^{-1}$ a.e.: $U_SM_mU_S^{-1}=M_{m\circ S^{-1}}$ because $(U_SM_mU_S^{-1})f=(m\cdot(f\circ S))\circ S^{-1}=(m\circ S^{-1})f$, so $M_m=U_SM_mU_S^{-1}$ and multiplication by two functions agrees only if the functions agree a.e. [F2]. Hence every rational level set $A_q:=\{\operatorname{Re}m>q\}$ satisfies $\mu(A_q\triangle S^{-1}A_q)=0$ for every $S$ in the family, so each $A_q$ is invariant modulo null sets; ergodicity gives $\mu(A_q)=0$ or $\mu(X\setminus A_q)=0$. The set $\{q\in\mathbb Q:\mu(A_q)=0\}$ is a final segment with finite infimum $a$, because $m$ is essentially bounded. On the conull set where all rational level sets are decided, every rational $q<a$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}m>q$ and every rational $q>a$ satisfies $\operatorname{Re}m\le q$, so $\operatorname{Re}m=a$, hence constant a.e.; the same argument applied to $\operatorname{Im}m$ makes $m$ constant a.e. [step 4.1, def. measure-preserving transformation, def. ergodic system]

6.1 The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the spectral, PVM-integral and measure-regularity suppliers of [F1]–[F6]; the commutant, exhaustion and ergodicity computations add no further choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). [given, F3, F5] ∎
