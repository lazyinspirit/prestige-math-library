## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice and let $n\ge1$. The diagonal unitary matrices
$T_{U(n)}=\{\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\dots,z_n):|z_j|=1\}$ form a maximal torus of
$U(n)$, its determinant-one part
$T_{SU(n)}=\{\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\dots,z_n):|z_j|=1,\ z_1\cdots z_n=1\}$ is a
maximal torus of $SU(n)$, and in both cases the Weyl group is the symmetric
group $S_n$ acting by permuting the coordinates.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, the groups $U(n)$ and $SU(n)$ with their standard maximal tori and the permutation matrices.

[L1] A torus is a compact connected abelian Lie group and a maximal torus is maximal under inclusion of torus subgroups; for a compact connected group with maximal torus $T$ the Weyl group is $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ and agrees with the root-system Weyl group ([[def-torus-and-maximal-torus-in-a-compact-lie-group]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]], [[thm-analytic-and-root-system-weyl-groups-agree]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The diagonal unitary matrices form a compact connected abelian subgroup of $U(n)$. A matrix commuting with every diagonal unitary matrix has zero $(i,j)$-entry for $i\ne j$, by choosing diagonal phases whose $i$th and $j$th entries differ; hence the centralizer of $T_{U(n)}$ is itself, so it is maximal. For $n\ge2$ the same entrywise argument uses determinant-one diagonal phases and shows that the centralizer of $T_{SU(n)}$ in $SU(n)$ is $T_{SU(n)}$; for $n=1$, $SU(1)$ is trivial. Thus both displayed tori are maximal. [L1, algebra]

2.1 The permutation matrices $\pi_\sigma$ are unitary and satisfy $\pi_\sigma\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\dots,z_n)\pi_\sigma^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(z_{\sigma(1)},\dots,z_{\sigma(n)})$, so they lie in the normalizer and induce $S_n$ in the Weyl group. For $SU(n)$ choose a diagonal unitary $d_\sigma$ with $\det d_\sigma=(\det\pi_\sigma)^{-1}$; then $d_\sigma\pi_\sigma\in SU(n)$ and, because $d_\sigma$ commutes with the diagonal torus, it induces the same coordinate permutation. [L1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Conversely, let $g$ normalize the diagonal torus. Choose a regular element $t=\operatorname{diag}(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ of the torus with pairwise distinct $z_j$. Then $gtg^{-1}\in T$, and the $z_j$ are the eigenvalues of $t$; since $gtg^{-1}$ has the same eigenvalues, and the eigenspaces of $t$ are the coordinate lines, $g$ permutes those lines up to scalars, hence equals a permutation matrix times a diagonal matrix. Therefore the normalizer is generated by the torus and the permutation matrices and $W\cong S_n$. [L1, step 2.1]

4.1 Consequently the Weyl groups of $U(n)$ and $SU(n)$ are $S_n$ acting by coordinate permutation, in agreement with the root-system computation for types $A_{n-1}$. [L1, step 3.1] ∎
