## Statement

Let $G$ act faithfully and primitively on $\Omega$, and fix $\alpha \in \Omega$.
Write
$$G_\alpha := \{\, g \in G : g \cdot \alpha = \alpha \,\}.$$
Assume $A \trianglelefteq G_\alpha$ is nontrivial and abelian, and that the
conjugates $\{\, gAg^{-1} : g \in G \,\}$ generate $G$.

Then every nontrivial normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$ contains the
commutator subgroup $[G,G]$. In particular, if $G = [G,G]$, then $G$ is simple.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A faithful primitive action of $G$ on $\Omega$, a point $\alpha \in \Omega$, a nontrivial abelian normal subgroup $A \trianglelefteq G_\alpha$, and the conjugates of $A$ generate $G$.

[L1] In a faithful primitive action, every nontrivial normal subgroup is transitive ([[thm-normal-subgroups-of-a-primitive-action-are-transitive-or-kernel-contained]]).

[L2] The commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators $[g,h] = ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[L3] A normal subgroup satisfies $gNg^{-1} = N$ for every $g \in G$ ([[def-normal-subgroup]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $N \trianglelefteq G$ be nontrivial. By [L1], $N$ is transitive on $\Omega$. Hence for every $g \in G$ there is $n \in N$ with $n \cdot \alpha = g \cdot \alpha$, and then $g^{-1}n \in G_\alpha$. So $G = N G_\alpha$. [L1, choose]

2.1 Fix $g \in G$, and write $g = nh$ with $n \in N$ and $h \in G_\alpha$ as in step 1.1. Because $A \trianglelefteq G_\alpha$, one has $hAh^{-1} = A$. For $a \in A$, the element $nan^{-1}a^{-1}$ lies in $N$ by [L3], so $nan^{-1} = (nan^{-1}a^{-1})a \in NA$. Therefore $$gAg^{-1} = n(hAh^{-1})n^{-1} = nAn^{-1} \subseteq NA.$$ [step 1.1, L3, algebra]

3.1 The conjugates of $A$ generate $G$ by hypothesis, and step 2.1 puts each of them inside $NA$. Hence $G = NA$. Modulo $N$, this says $G/N$ is generated by the image of $A$; since $A$ is abelian, $G/N$ is abelian. [step 2.1]

4.1 Because $G/N$ is abelian, every commutator of $G$ lies in $N$. By [L2], the subgroup they generate is $[G,G]$, so $[G,G] \le N$. [L2, step 3.1]

5.1 Step 4.1 holds for every nontrivial normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$. Therefore if $G = [G,G]$, every nontrivial normal subgroup contains all of $G$ and is equal to $G$. So $G$ is simple. [step 4.1] ∎
