## Statement

Let $f:X\to S$ be a separated quasi-finite morphism of finite type between Noetherian schemes. Then $f$ is quasi-affine: for every affine open $S_0\subseteq S$, the scheme $f^{-1}(S_0)$ is isomorphic over $S_0$ to an open subscheme of an affine $S_0$-scheme. No choice hypothesis is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The separated quasi-finite morphism of finite type $f:X\to S$ between Noetherian schemes in the Statement.

[F1] A Noetherian scheme has a finite affine cover by Noetherian rings. A separated morphism has closed-immersion diagonal. A quasi-finite morphism is of finite type and its affine local algebra is quasi-finite at the specified prime; a finite morphism has affine inverse images of affine opens with module-finite coordinate rings ([[def-locally-noetherian-and-noetherian-scheme]], [[def-separated-morphism-schemes]], [[def-quasi-finite-morphism-schemes]], [[def-finite-morphism-schemes]]).

[F2] For a finite-type ring map $A\to C$ quasi-finite at a supplied prime $\mathfrak q$, put $C'=\operatorname{Int}_A(C)\subseteq C$. There is $g\in C'\setminus\mathfrak q$ such that $C'_g=C_g$. A subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements is module-finite ([[lem-nagata-algebraic-zariski-main-localization]], [[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

[F4] A single flat surjective quasi-compact morphism is fpqc. We use only covers which are affine flat surjective and locally finitely presented, together with their restrictions to base opens; the descent property needed for them is proved in the proof below ([[def-fpqc-morphism-schemes]]).

[F5] For an fpqc morphism $Y\to T$ and any $\mathcal O_T$-module $\mathcal M$, vanishing of its pullback implies $\mathcal M=0$ ([[thm-faithfully-flat-descent-vanishing]]).

[F6] The canonical map $R\to\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}R,\mathcal O)$ is an isomorphism, including the zero ring. For every scheme $Y$, maps $Y\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ correspond naturally to ring maps $R\to\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y)$ ([[thm-global-sections-affine-scheme]], [[thm-morphisms-into-affine-scheme-global-sections]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We first record elementary finite-dimensional arguments used below. A nonzero finite-dimensional algebra $E$ over a field has a maximal ideal: among dimensions of proper ideals choose the largest and take an ideal of that dimension. A prime of $E$ is maximal, since a finite-dimensional domain is a field (injective multiplication by a nonzero element is surjective). For $a\in E$, the increasing kernels and decreasing images of multiplication by powers of $a$ stabilize; for a sufficiently large exponent their direct-sum decomposition shows that $E_a$ is the stable image, hence finite-dimensional, and is nonzero exactly when $a$ is not nilpotent. These are finite vector-space arguments, including when $E=0$. [algebra]

1.2 We prove the universal openness of the explicit factorization charts used below. Let $F(T)$ be monic of degree $n=r+s$ over a ring $R$. Write $C_0$ for its universal monic factorization algebra, with universal factors $G,H$ of degrees $r,s$, and $\Delta=\operatorname{Res}(G,H)$. Form the ordered splitting algebra $D$ of $F$ by adjoining a root of the current monic polynomial and dividing by the corresponding monic linear factor, successively. The monomial bases at these adjunctions give an $R$-basis of size $n!$, containing $1$. Over $C_0$, independently split $G$ and $H$ in this same way; the resulting algebra is finite free of rank $r!s!$, with basis containing $1$. It is canonically $D$: send the first $r$ ordered roots to the roots of $G$ and the last $s$ to those of $H$; conversely define the coefficients of $G,H$ as the elementary symmetric polynomials of those two blocks. The coefficient identities and monic division show these substitutions are inverse. This argument holds before inverting $\Delta$, including zero-degree factors. [construct, algebra]

1.3 It suffices to work over an affine open $S_0=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $S$; replace $X$ by its inverse image. This inverse image is quasi-compact because $f$ is of finite type, and is separated over $A$. Choose a finite affine cover $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^mX_i$. Each $X_i\cap X_j$ is affine: it is the inverse image of the closed diagonal under the map from the affine scheme $X_i\times_A X_j$, and hence a closed subscheme of that affine scheme. Put $B=\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$ and $Z=\operatorname{Spec}B$, and let $j:X\to Z$ be the canonical $A$-morphism furnished by [F6]. If $X$ is empty this is already the required open immersion. [given, F1, F6, construct]

1.4 We establish the descent assertion that will be used, without a general family-refinement theorem. For an affine flat surjective map $\operatorname{Spec}T\to\operatorname{Spec}R$, the complex $0\to R\to T\xrightarrow{d}T\otimes_RT$, $d(t)=t\otimes1-1\otimes t$, is exact. After tensoring with $T$ it is split exact at its first two terms: multiplication retracts $T\to T\otimes_RT$, and if $z\in T\otimes_RT$ satisfies $\sum t_0\otimes t_1\otimes1=\sum t_0\otimes1\otimes t_1$, multiplication of the first two factors gives $z=(\sum t_0t_1)\otimes1$. Flatness commutes with kernels and quotients, so the kernel and homology of the original complex pull back to zero. They vanish by [F5], since an affine surjective flat map is fpqc. Hence a compatible ring map $A\to T$ descends uniquely to $A\to R$, with its ring identities checked by injectivity. For an affine flat surjection $Y'\to Y$ with possibly non-affine $Y$, apply this argument over each affine open of $Y$, whose inverse image is affine. Naturality and uniqueness glue the descended maps to every affine target and show they pull back to the original map. The same holds over any open of $Y$. This proves exactly the affine-target descent used below. [F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 Every supplied prime $\mathfrak m$ of a finite-dimensional algebra $E$ is isolated in its spectrum. Indeed choose $N$ with $\mathfrak m^N=\mathfrak m^{N+1}$ and put $J=\mathfrak m^N$; then $J^2=J$. For finite generators $y_i$ of $J$, write $y_i=\sum_j a_{ij}y_j$ with $a_{ij}\in J$. The adjugate identity gives $dJ=0$ for $d=\det(I-(a_{ij}))=1-e$, where $e\in J$. Thus $e^2=e$ and $J=Ee$. Localizing at $1-e$ gives $E/J$, whose ideal $\mathfrak m/J$ is nilpotent and whose quotient is the field $E/\mathfrak m$. Consequently $D(1-e)$ consists of the single point $\mathfrak m$. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Finite morphisms are closed by a finite-module argument requiring no general prime-ideal existence principle. For a finite $R$-algebra $C$, a prime $\mathfrak p$ contains $\operatorname{Ann}_R C$ exactly when $C_{\mathfrak p}\ne0$: if the latter vanishes, a product of annihilators of finitely many generators lies outside $\mathfrak p$ and annihilates $C$. The finite determinant form of Nakayama's lemma then gives $C\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\ne0$; explicitly, a finite generating system satisfying $M=\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}M$ is killed by a determinant congruent to $1$ modulo the maximal ideal, hence vanishes. Step 1.1 supplies a prime of this nonzero finite-dimensional fibre. Thus the image is $V(\operatorname{Ann}_R C)$. Apply the same argument to every quotient $C/I$ to obtain closedness on affine opens, and hence for finite morphisms of schemes. Tensoring a finite generating system also proves stability of finiteness under base change; composites are finite by multiplying finite generating systems. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 Put $C=(C_0)_\Delta$. The map $\operatorname{Spec}(D[1/\Delta])\to\operatorname{Spec}C$ is surjective: at each supplied prime its fibre is a nonzero finite free algebra of positive rank and step 1.1 applies. For $a\in C_0$, the image of $D(a/\Delta^m)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}C$ in $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is therefore the image of $D(a\Delta)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}D$. If multiplication by $a\Delta$ on the finite free $R$-module $D$ has characteristic polynomial $T^{n!}+c_1T^{n!-1}+\cdots+c_{n!}$, this image is $\bigcup_iD(c_i)$. To see this at a supplied base prime, pass to its residue field. Step 1.1 says that the localized fibre is nonzero exactly when $a\Delta$ is not nilpotent. The characteristic polynomial is $T^{n!}$ exactly when that endomorphism is nilpotent: one direction follows from Cayley--Hamilton (the adjugate identity for the matrix $TI-M$), and the other from a basis adapted to the finite filtration by kernels of powers. This proves the asserted image formula. Matrices, coefficients and monic root constructions commute with every base change, so the chart is universally open. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.4 For any flat $A$-algebra $A'$, the sheaf equalizer sequence for this finite affine cover is $0\to B\to\prod_i\Gamma(X_i,\mathcal O)\to\prod_{i,j}\Gamma(X_i\cap X_j,\mathcal O)$, where the last arrow is the difference of the restrictions. Flat tensor product preserves its kernel and finite products. The base changed opens and intersections are affine, whose coordinate rings are the corresponding tensor products by [F6]. Their equalizer is $\Gamma(X_{A'},\mathcal O)$. We therefore have a natural isomorphism $B\otimes_AA'\cong\Gamma(X_{A'},\mathcal O)$, identifying $j_{A'}$ with the canonical affine-hull map. No infinite cover or product is involved. [F6, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 These charts also have finite-dimensional fibres and are flat and finitely presented. Indeed $D[1/\Delta]$ is flat over $R$, since $D$ is free and localization is an exact filtered colimit of repeated multiplication on that free module. It is finite free of positive rank over $C$, with basis containing $1$. For an injection of $R$-modules, the kernel after tensoring with $C$ becomes zero after tensoring with this free faithful $C$-module; thus the kernel is zero, proving flatness of $C$ over $R$. Its displayed equations and localization prove finite presentation. On any field fibre, $D[1/\Delta]$ is finite-dimensional by step 1.1, and $C$ injects into it by the free basis containing $1$, so the chart fibre is finite-dimensional. Principal localizations preserve these assertions and openness. Compositions of two charts do also: after the first field fibre the intermediate algebra is finite-dimensional, and the next root algebra is finite free over it, with localizations still finite-dimensional by step 1.1. Consequently these explicit charts, and their base changes and open restrictions, are open and flat locally finitely presented, with finite-dimensional fibres. [ step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.3, algebra]

3.2 Fix $x\in X$ over $s\in\operatorname{Spec}A$, and an affine neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec}C$ of $x$ with prime $\mathfrak q$. Apply [F2] to get $g\in C'\setminus\mathfrak q$ and $C_g=C'_g$. For finitely many $A$-algebra generators $c_i$ of $C$, write $c_i=a_i/g^{n_i}$ in $C_g$ with $a_i\in C'$. The subalgebra $D_0=A[g,a_1,\ldots,a_t]\subseteq C$ is finite over $A$ by [F2], and $(D_0)_g=C_g$. Its fibre at $s$ is a localization of a finite-dimensional algebra, hence finite-dimensional by step 1.1. By step 2.1 the point corresponding to $x$ is isolated in this fibre, and therefore in $X_s$, since $\operatorname{Spec}C_g$ is an open neighbourhood of $x$. In particular the given point has an isolated finite fibre neighbourhood. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 We now construct the finite open neighbourhood directly from step 3.2. Write $D_0\subseteq C$, $g\in D_0\setminus\mathfrak q$ with $D_0$ finite over $A$ and $(D_0)_g=C_g$. Choose a monic polynomial $P(T)\in A[T]$ annihilating $g$. In $k[T]$, $k=\kappa(s)$, write $\overline P=T^e\overline H$ with $\overline H(0)\ne0$, multiplying $P$ by $T$ first to arrange $e\ge1$. Since $g$ has nonzero image in $\kappa(x)$ and $P(g)=0$, $\overline H$ has positive degree. Form the chart of steps 1.2 and 2.3 for the two coprime factors $T^e,\overline H$; invert also the constant coefficient of its universal factor $H$. Thus over its coordinate ring $A'$ we have $P=IH$, a Bezout identity between $I,H$, and $H(0)$ a unit. Coefficient substitution defines a map $A'\to k$ extending $A\to k$. Its kernel is a specified point $u$ over $s$ with residue field $k$: the fraction field of the image is $k$, since the image contains $A/\mathfrak p$ and lies in its fraction field. By step 3.1 this affine map $U=\operatorname{Spec}A'\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is flat, open and finitely presented, with finite-dimensional fibres. [F2, step 1.2, step 2.3, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 In $D_0\otimes_AA'$, the two elements $I(g),H(g)$ generate the unit ideal and have product zero. Thus the algebra splits into the product of its localizations at those elements. The factor $E=(D_0\otimes_AA')_{I(g)}$ is a direct summand of a finite $A'$-module, hence is finite over $A'$ (project its finite generating list). In $E$ we have $H(g)=0$, and the unit constant term of $H$ expresses $g^{-1}$ as a polynomial in $g$. Therefore, localizing $(D_0)_g=C_g$ after the flat base change gives $E=(C\otimes_AA')_{gI(g)}$. Consequently $V=\operatorname{Spec}E$ is an affine open of $X_U$, finite over $U$. The map $C\otimes_AA'\to\kappa(x)$ induced by $A'\to k\to\kappa(x)$ sends $g$ to a nonzero element and $I(g)$ to $g^e$, so defines a specified point of $V$ mapping to $x$. This is the complete finite-open construction needed here. It uses a single explicit factorization chart and principal localization; no uniqueness of its fibre point or external finite-localization theorem is required. [F2, step 3.2, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Use the affine flat neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}A'$ and affine finite open $V\subseteq X_U$ constructed in steps 4.1 and 5.1. The open immersion $V\to X_U$ is also closed. Indeed its graph into $V\times_UX_U$ is a closed immersion by separatedness of $X_U/U$, and the projection to $X_U$ is finite as a base change of $V\to U$. Closed immersions are finite on affine coordinate rings, so their composite is finite; step 2.2 makes its image closed. Hence $X_U=V\amalg W$ with both parts open and closed. [given, F1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 The product decomposition $\Gamma(X_U,\mathcal O)=\Gamma(V,\mathcal O)\times\Gamma(W,\mathcal O)$ has idempotent $e=(1,0)$. By step 2.4 it is $B\otimes_A A'$, so $O'=D(e)\subseteq Z_U$ is canonically the affine scheme $V$. Naturality in [F6] shows that $j_U^{-1}(O')=V$ and $j_U|_V:V\to O'$ is its affine canonical isomorphism. Equivalently, $X\times_Z O'\to O'$ is an isomorphism. The map $O'\to Z$ is a principal-open restriction of the base change of $U\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, hence is affine, flat locally finitely presented and open with finite-dimensional fibres by step 3.1. Its image $O\subseteq Z$ is open and contains $j(x)$. The induced $p:O'\to O$ is surjective, affine and flat locally finitely presented; in particular it is fpqc and has the affine-target descent property of step 1.4. [F4, F6, step 3.1, step 2.4, step 1.4, step 6.1, construct]

8.1 We explicitly descend the inverse over $O'$. Let $h':O'\to X$ be the inverse supplied by step 7.1, so $jh'$ is the structural map to $Z$. Over $T=O'\times_O O'$, the two maps $h'\operatorname{pr}_1$ and $h'\operatorname{pr}_2$ agree. Pair either with $\operatorname{pr}_1:T\to O'$: they give maps to $X\times_ZO'$, whose projection to $O'$ is an isomorphism by step 7.1, and both have the same projection. This proves the cocycle equality as scheme maps. [step 7.1, construct]

9.1 For the finite affine cover $X_i$ in step 1.3 put $T_i=(h')^{-1}(X_i)\subseteq O'$. This open is saturated for $p$. To prove it, let $y,y'\in O'$ lie over the same $o\in O$. Their residue fields are finite extensions of $\kappa(o)$ by the finite-dimensional fibre assertion of step 7.1. The nonzero finite-dimensional algebra $\kappa(y)\otimes_{\kappa(o)}\kappa(y')$ has a prime by step 1.1, giving a point of $T$ above both $y,y'$. Step 8.1 then says that $h'(y)$ belongs to the open $X_i$ exactly when $h'(y')$ does. Since $p$ is open, $O_i=p(T_i)$ is open in $O$, $p^{-1}(O_i)=T_i$, and the $O_i$ cover $O$. [step 1.1, step 1.3, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct]

10.1 The map $T_i\to O_i$ is the restriction of the affine flat surjection $p$. By step 1.4, the cocycle-compatible map $T_i\to X_i$ descends uniquely to $h_i:O_i\to X_i$. The structural map to the affine base $\operatorname{Spec}A$ also descends uniquely, so $h_i$ is an $A$-map. On $O_i\cap O_j$ the two maps land in $X_i\cap X_j$: this follows after the surjective cover by the inverse-image characterization of an open subset. That intersection is affine by step 1.3, so step 1.4 makes the maps equal there. They glue to an $A$-map $h:O\to X$. Applying step 1.4 to the affine target $Z$ gives $jh=\operatorname{id}_O$ as maps to $Z$, since this equality holds after $p$. [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 8.1, step 9.1, construct]

11.1 Regard $h$ as a map to $X_O=X\times_ZO$. The two $A$-maps $hj$ and $\operatorname{id}_{X_O}$ have closed equalizer because $X$ is separated over $A$. After the fpqc base change $X_O\times_O O'\to X_O$, they are equal by step 7.1 and the construction of $h$. If $\mathcal I$ is the ideal of their closed equalizer, flatness makes its pullback inject into the structure sheaf of that base change, with image the zero ideal. Thus its pullback is zero; [F5] gives $\mathcal I=0$. The two maps are equal on $X_O$, and step 10.1 already gives $jh=\operatorname{id}_O$. Consequently $j^{-1}(O)\to O$ is an isomorphism. [given, F1, F5, step 7.1, step 10.1]

12.1 For every $x\in X$, steps 3.2 to 11.1 exhibit an open $O\subseteq Z$ containing $j(x)$ over which $j$ is an isomorphism. Take the family of all opens with this property, without selecting a witness for each point, and let $\Omega$ be their union. It contains $j(X)$; conversely each such open is in $j(X)$, so $\Omega=j(X)$. The inverses over these opens agree by their inverse identities and hence glue. Thus $j:X\to Z$ identifies $X$ with the open subscheme $\Omega$ of the affine $A$-scheme $Z$. Step 1.3 applies over every affine open of the original $S$, proving exactly the quasi-affineness in the Statement, including empty source or base. All selections in the construction were finite or concerned a single supplied point; no choice axiom, normalization base-change theorem or unproved general descent assertion was used. [given, step 1.3, step 11.1] ∎
