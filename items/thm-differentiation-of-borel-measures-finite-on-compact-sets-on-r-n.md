## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), hence also countable choice ([[def-countable-choice]]).

Let $\nu$ be a sigma-finite Borel measure on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is finite on
compact sets. Write
$$\nu=\nu_a+\nu_s,\qquad \nu_a\ll\lambda,\qquad \nu_s\perp\lambda,$$
for its Lebesgue decomposition on the Borel sigma-algebra relative to the restriction of Lebesgue measure, and choose a Borel
measurable representative $f$ of the Radon-Nikodym class
$d\nu_a/d\lambda$. Then for Lebesgue-almost every $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$,
$$\lim_{r\to0^+}\frac{\nu(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}=f(x).$$
More generally, let $A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$, and suppose that for each $x\in A$
a family $(E_r(x))_{r>0}$ of Borel sets shrinking nicely to $x$ is specified.
Then for Lebesgue-almost every $x\in A$,
$$\lim_{r\to0^+}\frac{\nu(E_r(x))}{\lambda(E_r(x))}=f(x).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a sigma-finite Borel measure $\nu$ on $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is finite on compact sets. All measure decompositions are on the common Borel sigma-algebra; the resulting Borel density is also Lebesgue measurable.

[L1] Such a measure admits a Lebesgue decomposition $$\nu=\nu_a+\nu_s,\qquad \nu_a\ll\lambda,\qquad \nu_s\perp\lambda,$$ and the Radon-Nikodym class $d\nu_a/d\lambda$ has a measurable representative $f$. ([[thm-lebesgue-decomposition-exists-for-sigma-finite-signed-measures]], [[thm-radon-nikodym-density-exists-and-is-unique-up-to-almost-everywhere-equality]], [[def-radon-nikodym-derivative]])

[L2] If $(E_r)$ shrinks nicely to $x$, then $$\frac{1}{\lambda(E_r)}\int_{E_r}f\,d\lambda\to f(x)$$ for almost every $x$. ([[thm-differentiation-along-families-shrinking-nicely]])

[L3] Borel sets are Lebesgue measurable; bounded Borel sets have finite Lebesgue measure. Closed bounded Euclidean sets are compact. ([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]], [[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]])

[L4] Assuming the Axiom of Countable Choice, Lebesgue measure is inner regular by compact subsets on measurable sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$. ([[thm-lebesgue-inner-regularity-by-closed-and-compact-sets]])

[L5] Increasing measurable unions pass through positive measures. ([[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]])

[L6] Lebesgue measure is translation invariant and scales by $c^n$ under positive dilations. Every open ball is Borel, contains an open cube of positive volume, and is bounded; thus its measure is positive and finite. ([[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-and-measurability-are-translation-invariant]], [[thm-lebesgue-measure-under-dilations-and-reflections]], [[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]], [L3])

[L7] Borel sets form the sigma-algebra generated by open sets. ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The closed balls $X_j=\overline{B(0,j)}$, $j\ge1$, give a common finite-measure Borel exhaustion for $\nu$ and $\lambda$ by [L3]. Under the given AC, [L1] therefore gives $\nu=\nu_a+\nu_s$ and a finite real Borel density $f$ of $\nu_a$. Choose a Borel null set $N$ carrying $\nu_s$. For every Borel $E$, absolute continuity and concentration give $$\nu_s(E)=\nu(E\cap N)\ge0,\qquad\nu_a(E)=\nu(E\setminus N)\ge0.$$ Both components are positive and dominated by $\nu$. On each finite exhaustion piece, the set where $f\le-1/k$ has measure zero: its density integral is at most $-\lambda(E)/k$ but equals $\nu_a(E)\ge0$. Taking the countable union over $j,k$ and replacing negative values by zero yields $f\ge0$ without changing its class. For every ball, $$\int_{B(x,R)}|f|\,d\lambda=\nu_a(B(x,R))\le\nu(\overline{B(x,R)})<\infty.$$ Thus $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$. [L1, L3, given, choose, algebra]

1.2 We prove locally the outer approximation needed below. Let $\eta$ be any Borel measure finite on compact sets and $V=B(0,j)$ for $j\ge1$; then $\eta(V)<\infty$. Every open $W\subseteq V$ is an increasing union of compact sets: use the sets of points of $W$ with norm at most $m$ and distance at least $1/m$ from its complement, $m\ge1$. These are closed and bounded, so [L3] and [L5] approximate $W$ from inside by compact sets in $\eta$-measure. Let $\mathcal C$ be the Borel $E\subseteq V$ such that for every $\delta>0$ there are relatively closed $F$ and relatively open $O$ with $F\subseteq E\subseteq O\subseteq V$ and $\eta(O\setminus F)<\delta$. All relatively open sets are in $\mathcal C$ by the compact approximation just proved. Relative complements preserve $\mathcal C$ by interchanging $F$ and $O$. Countable unions preserve outer approximation by choosing open supersets with summable errors; they preserve inner approximation by first taking a finite partial union with small remaining measure (using $\eta(V)<\infty$ and [L5]) and then taking a finite union of relatively closed approximants. Thus $\mathcal C$ is a sigma-algebra, and [L7] implies that it contains every Borel subset of $V$. In particular each such set has arbitrarily accurate relatively open outer approximations. The countable selections are covered by AC. [L3, L5, L7, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If $H$ is a Borel $\eta$-null set, put $V_0=\varnothing$, $V_j=B(0,j)$ and $H_j=H\cap(V_j\setminus V_{j-1})$ for $j\ge1$. Step 1.2 gives relatively open $O_j\subseteq V_j$ containing $H_j$ with $\eta(O_j)<\varepsilon2^{-j}$. As $V_j$ is open, each $O_j$ is open in $\mathbb R^n$. Their union is an open set containing $H$ with measure less than $\varepsilon$. This proves the required null-set outer approximation without importing a later regularity theorem. [step 1.2, given, choose, algebra]

2.2 Let $(E_r(x))_{r>0}$ be a family of Borel sets shrinking nicely to $x$ with constant $\alpha_x>0$. Then $$\frac{\nu(E_r(x))}{\lambda(E_r(x))}=\frac{1}{\lambda(E_r(x))}\int_{E_r(x)}f\,d\lambda+\frac{\nu_s(E_r(x))}{\lambda(E_r(x))},$$ while positivity and the defining comparison give $$0\le\frac{\nu_s(E_r(x))}{\lambda(E_r(x))}\le\frac{1}{\alpha_x}\frac{\nu_s(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}.$$ Consequently [L2] and step 1.1 reduce both conclusions to proving $$\frac{\nu_s(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}\longrightarrow0$$ for almost every $x$. [L1, L2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.3 Put $G:=\mathbb{R}^n\setminus N$, so $G$ is Borel, $\lambda(\mathbb{R}^n\setminus G)=0$, and $\nu_s(G)=0$. For each $m\ge1$, define $$D_m(x):=\sup_{0<r<1/m}\frac{\nu_s(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}.$$ For fixed $r>0$, if $0<\delta<r$ and $\|x-y\|_2<\delta$, then $$B(x,r-\delta)\subseteq B(y,r).$$ By [L5], $\nu_s(B(x,r-\delta))\uparrow\nu_s(B(x,r))$ as $\delta\downarrow0$, so $x\mapsto\nu_s(B(x,r))$ is lower semicontinuous. Translation invariance gives the fixed positive denominator $\lambda(B(x,r))=\lambda(B(0,r))$, so each $D_m$ is lower semicontinuous and each set $$\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:D_m(x)>\frac1k\right\}$$ is open. For $k\ge1$, put $$F_k:=G\cap\bigcap_{m\ge1}\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^n:D_m(x)>\frac1k\right\}.$$ Then $F_k$ is Borel. If $x\in G$ and $$L(x):=\limsup_{r\to0^+}\frac{\nu_s(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}>0,$$ choose $k$ with $1/k<L(x)$. Since $D_m(x)\ge L(x)>1/k$ for every $m$, one has $x\in F_k$. Thus $$\{x\in G:L(x)>0\}\subseteq\bigcup_{k\ge1}F_k,$$ and it is enough to prove $\lambda(F_k)=0$ for every $k$. [step 1.1, L5, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Fix $k\ge1$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By steps 1.1 and 2.1, there is an open $U_\varepsilon\supseteq G$ with $\nu_s(U_\varepsilon)<\varepsilon$. Let $K\subseteq F_k$ be nonempty and compact (the empty case has measure zero). Consider all balls $B(x,r)\subseteq U_\varepsilon$ with $x\in K$ and $\nu_s(B(x,r))>\lambda(B(x,r))/k$. They cover $K$: for each $x$, choose $m$ with $B(x,1/m)\subseteq U_\varepsilon$, and use $D_m(x)>1/k$. Compactness gives a finite subcover. Greedily retain a largest-radius ball and discard all balls meeting it, repeating on the remaining finite family. The retained balls $B_1,\ldots,B_q$ are disjoint. Any discarded ball of radius $r$ meets a retained one of radius $R\ge r$; their centres are less than $r+R$ apart, so every point of the discarded ball is less than $2r+R\le3R<5R$ from the retained centre. Hence $K\subseteq\bigcup_j5B_j$. By [L6] and disjointness, $$\lambda(K)\le5^n\sum_j\lambda(B_j)<5^nk\sum_j\nu_s(B_j)\le5^nk\nu_s(U_\varepsilon)<5^nk\varepsilon.$$ [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.3, L6, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Since $\varepsilon>0$ was arbitrary, step 3.1 gives $\lambda(K)=0$ for every compact $K\subseteq F_k$. Because $F_k$ is Borel by step 2.3, [L4] implies $$\lambda(F_k)=\sup\{\lambda(K):K\subseteq F_k,\ K\text{ compact}\}=0.$$ Step 2.3 now shows that the set where $L(x)>0$ is contained in the null set $$(\mathbb{R}^n\setminus G)\cup\bigcup_{k\ge1}F_k.$$ The ratios defining $L$ are nonnegative, so $$\lim_{r\to0^+}\frac{\nu_s(B(x,r))}{\lambda(B(x,r))}=0$$ for almost every $x$. [step 2.3, step 3.1, L4, algebra]

5.1 Combine step 4.1 with the comparison in step 2.2 and the differentiation theorem [L2] for the locally integrable representative $f$. For any specified Borel families $(E_r(x))_{r>0}$ shrinking nicely to the points of $A$, this gives $$\frac{\nu(E_r(x))}{\lambda(E_r(x))}\to f(x)$$ for almost every $x\in A$. Taking $A=\mathbb{R}^n$ and $E_r(x)=B(x,r)$ gives the ball conclusion. [L2, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 4.1] ∎
