## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $C$ be a finite union of distinct irreducible one-dimensional closed algebraic subsets of $(\mathbb C^*)^2$. Suppose its radical vanishing ideal in $\mathbb C[M^{\pm1},L^{\pm1}]$ is the scalar extension of a radical ideal in $\mathbb Q[M^{\pm1},L^{\pm1}]$; this is what it means here for the reduced union to be defined over $\mathbb Q$. Then its complex vanishing ideal is generated by a square-free Laurent polynomial $F$ with primitive integer coefficients. Any two such primitive integer-coefficient generators differ by multiplication by $\pm M^aL^b$, where $a,b\in\mathbb Z$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, the two-dimensional algebraic torus $(\mathbb C^*)^2$, and the reduced finite union $C$ of distinct irreducible one-dimensional closed subvarieties, with a rational structure on the union.

[F1] Every prime ideal of height one in a finite-variable polynomial ring over a field is principal and generated by an irreducible element ([[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-rings-over-fields-are-ufds]]).

[F2] For an affine domain of finite type over a field, its Krull dimension is the transcendence degree of its fraction field ([[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]]).

[F3] Geometric dimension of an affine algebraic set over an algebraically closed field equals Krull dimension of its coordinate ring, under full AC ([[thm-affine-variety-dimension-coordinate-ring]]; [[def-axiom-of-choice]]). The torus is affine: $(M,L)\mapsto(M,L,M^{-1},L^{-1})$ identifies it with $\{(M,L,U,V):MU=LV=1\}\subset\mathbb C^4$.

[F4] For a finite-type domain $A$ and prime $P$, full AC gives $\operatorname{ht}(P)+\dim(A/P)=\dim A$ ([[cor-height-plus-quotient-dimension-affine-domain]]; [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F5] Prime ideals disjoint from a localization set correspond by extension and contraction, preserving inclusions ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[F6] The Laurent polynomial ring is the localization of $\mathbb C[M,L]$ obtained by inverting $M$ and $L$; its units are nonzero scalars times monomials, as follows from polynomial factorization in [F1].

[F7] The fixed field of all automorphisms of $\mathbb C$ fixing $\mathbb Q$ is $\mathbb Q$ ([[lem-fixed-field-of-automorphisms-of-c-over-q-is-q]]).

## Proof

1.1 Put $R=\mathbb C[M,L]$ and $S=\{M^aL^b:a,b\ge0\}$. The coordinate ring of $(\mathbb C^*)^2$ is $A=S^{-1}R$, a finite-type domain with fraction field $\mathbb C(M,L)$ of transcendence degree two. Each irreducible curve $Y$ in $C$ has prime ideal $P_Y\subset A$, and its quotient coordinate ring has dimension one by [F3]. Thus [F2] gives $\dim A=2$ and $\dim(A/P_Y)=1$; [F4] yields $\operatorname{ht}(P_Y)=1$. [F2, F3, F4, given]

2.1 Contract $P_Y$ to $p_Y=P_Y\cap R$. It avoids $S$ because every element of $S$ is a unit in $A$. By [F5], contraction and extension identify all primes below $P_Y$ with primes below $p_Y$, so $p_Y$ has height one. By [F1], $p_Y=(f_Y)$ for an irreducible polynomial $f_Y\in R$. Since $p_Y$ avoids $M$ and $L$, neither variable divides $f_Y$. In the localization of the UFD $R$, an irreducible remains prime unless it divides an inverted element; therefore $f_Y/1$ is a nonunit prime and $P_Y=(f_Y/1)$. [F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Distinct components have distinct prime ideals, hence their irreducible generators $f_Y$ are pairwise nonassociate in the Laurent ring. A Laurent polynomial belongs to every $(f_Y)$ exactly when each pairwise nonassociate prime factor divides it; unique factorization then makes it divisible by their product. If $C$ is empty, the vanishing ideal is the unit ideal and the empty product is $F_0=1$; otherwise the same product over the components generates the vanishing ideal. In either case the generator is square-free, and any other generator differs by a Laurent unit. [F1, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By the stated rational structure, every automorphism of $\mathbb C$ fixing $\mathbb Q$ preserves the vanishing ideal. Shift the finite support of $F_0$ so its lexicographically least exponent pair is $(0,0)$, then scale its coefficient there to $1$. This gives a unique normalized generator: by [F6] any two generators differ by a Laurent unit, and the support shift and specified coefficient remove that unit. Every such field automorphism preserves the normalization, hence fixes each coefficient. By [F7] the coefficients are rational. Clearing denominators and dividing by their integer gcd gives a primitive integer Laurent polynomial. Any two primitive integer-coefficient generators differ by a Laurent unit with rational scalar part; primitivity forces that scalar to be $\pm1$. [F6, F7, given, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1, 3.1, 4.1 prove existence of the square-free primitive integer equation and show that its only ambiguity is $\pm M^aL^b$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
