## Statement

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space, let $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$, let $X$ be a random element of $(S,\Sigma)$, and let $K$ be a regular conditional distribution of $X$ given $\mathcal G$. If $T$ is a measurable space and $g:(\Omega,\mathcal G)\times(S,\Sigma)\to(T,\Tau)$ is measurable, then
$$K_g(\omega,B):=K\bigl(\omega,\{x:g(\omega,x)\in B\}\bigr),\qquad B\in\Tau,$$
is a regular conditional distribution of $g(\omega,X(\omega))$ given $\mathcal G$.

If, in addition, $U$ is a random element of a measurable space $(V,\mathcal V)$, is independent of $\mathcal G\vee\sigma(X)$, and has law $\lambda$, then $K(\omega,\cdot)\otimes\lambda$ is a regular conditional distribution of $(X,U)$ given $\mathcal G$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The probability space, sub-sigma-algebra, random element, regular conditional distribution, and measurable map in the statement; for the second assertion, the independent random element $U$ and its law $\lambda$.

[F1] A regular conditional distribution is an everywhere probability kernel whose evaluations satisfy $\int_H K(\omega,A)\,P(d\omega)=P(H\cap\{X\in A\})$ for every $H\in\mathcal G$ and $A\in\Sigma$. [[def-regular-conditional-distribution]]

[F2] Product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F3] Every section of a probability kernel is a probability measure, and each event evaluation is measurable in the conditioning point. [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F4] The product of two probability measures exists, is a probability measure, and has the rectangle formula. [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F5] Independence of sigma-algebras gives $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$ for events from the respective sigma-algebras. [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]

[F6] Increasing limits pass through nonnegative integrals. [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]

[F7] A lambda-system containing a generating pi-system contains its generated sigma-algebra. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F8] Increasing pointwise limits of measurable extended-real functions are measurable. [[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]]

[F9] A finite tuple of measurable coordinate maps is measurable into the finite product sigma-algebra. [[lem-coordinate-maps-and-permutations-are-measurable-on-finite-products]]

[F10] Every section of a product-measurable set is measurable in the corresponding factor. [[thm-sections-of-product-measurable-sets-are-measurable]]

[A1] The map $g$ is measurable for $\mathcal G\otimes\Sigma$ and $\Tau$.

[A2] In the second assertion, $U$ is independent of $\mathcal G\vee\sigma(X)$ and has law $\lambda$.

## Proof

1.1 Let $\mathcal D$ consist of product-measurable sets $E\subseteq\Omega\times S$ for which $k_E(\omega):=K(\omega,E_\omega)$ is $\mathcal G$-measurable and $\int_H k_E\,dP=P(H\cap\{(\omega,X(\omega))\in E\})$ for every $H\in\mathcal G$. For a rectangle $E=A\times B$, $k_E=\mathbf1_AK(\cdot,B)$ is measurable by [F1], and its integral identity follows by applying [F1] to $H\cap A$. Thus all rectangles lie in $\mathcal D$. [F1, F2]

1.2 For each $\omega$, define $\bar K(\omega,\cdot)=K(\omega,\cdot)\otimes\lambda$, which is a probability measure by [F4]. On a rectangle $B\times C$, its evaluation is $K(\omega,B)\lambda(C)$, a $\mathcal G$-measurable function. For $H\in\mathcal G$, the integral of this evaluation over $H$ equals $\lambda(C)P(H\cap\{X\in B\})$ by [F1]. Since $H\cap\{X\in B\}\in\mathcal G\vee\sigma(X)$, independence [A2, F5] makes this $P(H\cap\{X\in B\}\cap\{U\in C\})$. [A2, F1, F4, F5]

2.1 The whole space is in $\mathcal D$. If $E\in\mathcal D$, then $k_{E^c}=1-k_E$ and the integral identity for $E^c$ follows by subtracting the identity for $E$ from $P(H)$. If $E_j$ are disjoint members of $\mathcal D$, then their sections are disjoint, so $k_{\cup_jE_j}=\sum_j k_{E_j}$ pointwise; [F8] makes the increasing partial sums' limit measurable, and [F6] gives the integral identity for the union. Hence $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system. Measurable rectangles contain $\Omega\times S$ and are closed under finite intersections, so they form a pi-system generating $\mathcal G\otimes\Sigma$ by [F2]; [F7] therefore gives $\mathcal D=\mathcal G\otimes\Sigma$. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 For $B\in\Tau$, [A1] makes $E=g^{-1}(B)$ product-measurable and [F10] makes its sections measurable, so $K_g(\omega,B)$ is defined. The map $\omega\mapsto(\omega,X(\omega))$ is measurable from $(\Omega,\mathcal F)$ into $(\Omega\times S,\mathcal G\otimes\Sigma)$: the identity map into $(\Omega,\mathcal G)$ is measurable because $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal F$, and $X$ is measurable, so [F9] applies. Therefore $\{g(\omega,X(\omega))\in B\}$ is the measurable preimage of $E$ under this map. Step 2.1 gives measurability and the conditional-event identity for $K_g(\cdot,B)$. For every $\omega$, $K_g(\omega,\cdot)$ is the pushforward of the probability measure $K(\omega,\cdot)$, hence is a probability measure. These are exactly the clauses in [F1], so $K_g$ is a regular conditional distribution of $g(\omega,X(\omega))$. [A1, F1, F9, F10, step 2.1]

4.1 The tuple $(X,U)$ is measurable into the product space by [F9]. The class of sets in $\Sigma\otimes\mathcal V$ for which the evaluation of $\bar K$ is measurable and the conditional-event identity holds is a lambda-system: complements subtract from the whole-space identity, and disjoint unions follow from countable additivity, measurable increasing limits [F8], and [F6]. It contains all rectangles by step 1.2, so [F2, F7] show that it contains $\Sigma\otimes\mathcal V$. Every section is the product probability measure from [F4]; consequently $\bar K$ is a regular conditional distribution of $(X,U)$ given $\mathcal G$. [F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.2] ∎
