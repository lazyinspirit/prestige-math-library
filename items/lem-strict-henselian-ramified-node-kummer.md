## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, let $e,n\ge1$ with $n$ invertible in $k$, and set
$$B_0=(k\llbracket s\rrbracket[u,v]/(uv-s^e))_{(s,u,v)}.$$
Let $H$ be the henselization of $B_0$ at its closed point and put $R=H[s^{-1}]$. Then $H$ is strictly henselian and Noetherian, its completion is $k\llbracket u,v,s\rrbracket/(uv-s^e)$, and its completion map is faithfully flat. Moreover $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$ and
$$R^\times/(R^\times)^n\cong(\mathbb Z/n)^2,\qquad
R^\times/\bigl((R^\times)^n\langle s\rangle\bigr)\cong\mathbb Z/n,$$
the last group generated by $u$. The group of étale $\mu_n$-torsor classes on $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is canonically the first unit quotient. These assertions include characteristic-divisible $e$ and use no Axiom of Choice. They do not assert a geometric nearby-cycles comparison.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the positive integers $e,n$, the specified local ring and its henselization.

## Proof

1.1 Construct $H$ as the filtered colimit of pointed étale neighbourhoods of $B_0$ with residue field $k$, localized at the indicated point. Each stage $E$ is Noetherian local with residue $k$. For every $r$, the map $B_0/\mathfrak m^r\to E/\mathfrak m_E^r$ is an isomorphism: a standard étale presentation has invertible Jacobian at the specified residue point, and the unique finite nilpotent Newton corrections lift that point through each Artinian quotient. Thus all stage completions are the same Noetherian ring $C=k\llbracket u,v,s\rrbracket/(uv-s^e)$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For any Noetherian local $k$-algebra $E$ with residue field $k$, completion is exact on finite modules using only finite choices. Artin–Rees gives injectivity: its Rees ring is generated by finitely many elements, and finite generation of $\bigoplus_r(M'\cap\mathfrak m^rM)T^r$ yields $M'\cap\mathfrak m^rM\subseteq\mathfrak m^{r-c}M'$ for large $r$. For surjectivity, present the target by a finite free module $F$. Given compatible target residues, at level $r$ take the images of all lifts from level $N\ge r$; these nonempty affine subspaces of the finite-dimensional $k$-space $F/\mathfrak m^rF$ descend and stabilize. Their stable images map surjectively from level $r+1$ to level $r$. Fix finite generators of $\mathfrak m$ and use their ordered monomials and reduced row echelon form to choose each lift canonically from the appropriate affine fibre. Deterministic recursion gives a compatible lift, without selecting an arbitrary countable family. [step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra] [step 1.1]

3.1 Exact completion and a finite presentation identify $\widehat E\otimes_E M$ with $\widehat M$ for each finite module. Applying this to finitely generated ideals proves that $E\to\widehat E$ is flat, and it is faithfully flat because it is local with the same residue field. Krull intersection, using Artin–Rees and finite Nakayama, makes it injective. Thus all pointed stages embed into $C$. Their colimit $H$ is a local subring of $C$ with the same maximal-adic quotients and completion. Every ideal $I\subset H$ is finitely generated: choose finitely many elements of $I$ generating $IC$; for each $h\in I$, one common stage contains $h$ and those generators, and faithful flatness of that stage's completion contracts the generated ideal back to that stage. The same finite-module completion argument applied to the resulting Noetherian local ring $H$ makes $H\to C$ faithfully flat. [step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, algebra] [step 2.1]

4.1 Put $A_0=(k\llbracket s\rrbracket[a,b]/(ab-s))_{(s,a,b)}$ and $A=A_0\otimes_{B_0}H$, using $u=a^e$, $v=b^e$. The finite congruence grading with weights $1,-1$ has degree-zero subring $H$: before localization this follows termwise from the normal forms $a^r s^j$ and $b^r s^j$, and finite base change preserves the direct sum. The finite cover has a unique closed point above the closed point of $H$, so $A$ is local with maximal ideal $(a,b)$. Its completion is $k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$. Faithful-flat injection into that domain makes $A$ a domain; applying the same argument after quotienting by $a$ embeds $A/(a)$ in $k\llbracket b\rrbracket$, so $b$ is a nonzerodivisor there. [step 3.1, algebra] [step 3.1]

5.1 The degree-zero localization $R=H[s^{-1}]$ has trivial Picard group. Indeed represent a line bundle by an invertible fractional ideal $I$, clear finitely many denominators in $H$, and extend its generators to a graded ideal $J$ of $A$. The inverse ideal and its inverse $N=J^{\vee\vee}$ are finite graded fractional $A$-modules, and $N$ is reflexive. It is $(a,b)$-regular: if $bz\in aN$, then every dual functional sends $z$ into $aA$ because $b$ is a nonzerodivisor modulo $a$; reflexivity gives $z/a\in N$. The Koszul resolution of $k=A/(a,b)$ then yields $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,k)=0$. A finite minimal presentation and Nakayama make $N$ free of rank one. Decomposing a generator into the finitely many weights gives a homogeneous generator, because one of its coefficients is a unit in the local ring. After inverting $s=ab$, the unit $a$ shifts that generator to weight zero; taking degree zero descends it to a generator of $I$. This proves $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$ without using factoriality or assuming $e$ invertible. [step 4.1, choose, algebra] [step 4.1]

6.1 In the completed model $C$, the substitution $u=a^e$, $v=b^e$, $s=ab$ embeds it into $k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$ as the subring of monomials whose exponents differ by a multiple of $e$. If a localized element is a unit, multiplying it by a power of $s$ and cancelling the prime elements $a,b$ in the two-variable power-series ring shows it is uniquely $u^q s^r$ times a unit of $C$. For a unit $f\in R^\times$, divide it by that monomial. The quotient and its inverse lie in $\operatorname{Frac}(H)$ and in $C$. Faithful flatness gives $H=C\cap\operatorname{Frac}(H)$: for $x/y$ in the intersection, contraction of $(y)C$ gives $x\in(y)H$. Hence the quotient is a unit of $H$. Hensel's simple-root construction and algebraic closedness of $k$ make every unit of $H$ an $n$th power. The two unit quotients in the Statement follow from the independent exponents of $u,s$. [step 3.1, construct, algebra] [step 5.1]

7.1 Because $n$ is invertible and all $n$th roots of unity lie in $k$, $\mu_n$ is finite étale and split. For a $\mu_n$-torsor, its finite locally free coordinate algebra decomposes by the character idempotents into invertible eigenspaces whose multiplication is compatible. Step 5.1 trivializes the weight-one line; a chosen generator has $n$th power equal to a unit $f$, and the torsor is $z^n=f$. Conversely that equation defines an étale $\mu_n$-torsor, and two such equations define isomorphic torsors exactly when their units differ by an $n$th power. Thus torsor classes are precisely the first quotient of step 6.1. Every selection in the proof is finite for the given object, and all infinite lifts use deterministic recursions. [step 5.1, step 6.1, choose, algebra] [step 6.1] ∎
