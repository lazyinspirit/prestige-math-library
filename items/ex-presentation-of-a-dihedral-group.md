## Example

Let $n\geq3$ and let $\rho,\sigma\in\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb Z/n)$ be the
permutations $\rho(k)=k+1$ and $\sigma(k)=-k$. The **dihedral group** is the
generated subgroup

$$D_n:=\langle\{\rho,\sigma\}\rangle\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb Z/n).$$

Then $D_n$ has exactly $2n$ elements and

$$D_n\cong\langle r,s\mid r^n,\ s^2,\ srs^{-1}r\rangle,$$

where $r$ corresponds to $\rho$ and $s$ to $\sigma$. Reading $\mathbb Z/n$ as
the vertices of a regular $n$-gon in cyclic order, $\rho$ is the rotation by
one vertex and $\sigma$ the reflection fixing $0$. That reading motivates the
name and is not used below: every step argues about permutations of
$\mathbb Z/n$. At $n=2$ the map $\sigma$ is the identity, so the
construction degenerates and $|D_2|=2$; the Klein four-group is treated
separately.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n\geq3$, the set $\mathbb Z/n$, the permutations $\rho(k)=k+1$ and $\sigma(k)=-k$, and $D_n=\langle\{\rho,\sigma\}\rangle\leq\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb Z/n)$.

[L1] The set $\mathbb Z/n$ has $n$ elements, represented uniquely by $0,\ldots,n-1$ ([[thm-standard-representatives-modulo-n]]).

[L2] A map of generators that sends every relator to the identity extends uniquely to a homomorphism from the presented group ([[thm-von-dyck]]).

[L3] Two disjoint finite sets have union of cardinality equal to the sum of their cardinalities ([[thm-sum-rule]]).

[F1] The subgroup $\langle S\rangle$ generated by $S$ is the smallest subgroup containing $S$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 Direct substitution on $\mathbb Z/n$ gives $\rho^n=\operatorname{id}$, $\sigma^2=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sigma\rho\sigma^{-1}\rho=\operatorname{id}$ because $\sigma\rho\sigma(k)=k-1$; thus the displayed relators hold. [given, construct]

1.2 In $P$, the relators give $s^{-1}=s$, $sr=r^{-1}s$, $r^n=e$, and $s^2=e$; moving every $s$ to the right and reducing exponents therefore writes every element as $r^is^\varepsilon$ with $0\leq i<n$ and $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$. [given]

2.1 The permutations $\rho^i$ are distinct by their values $\rho^i(0)=i$, and the permutations $\rho^i\sigma$ are likewise distinct; the two families are disjoint because equality would first force the same $i$ from the value at $0$ and then force $i+1=i-1$ in $\mathbb Z/n$ from the value at $1$, impossible for $n\geq3$. Step 1.1 gives the same relations among $\rho$ and $\sigma$, so the set $\{\rho^i\sigma^\varepsilon:0\leq i<n,\ \varepsilon\in\{0,1\}\}$ is closed under products and inverses and contains $\rho$ and $\sigma$; being a subgroup containing $\{\rho,\sigma\}$, it equals $D_n$ by the minimality in [F1]. Hence [L1] and [L3] give exactly $2n$ elements of $D_n$. [F1, L1, L3, step 1.1, given]

2.2 By [L2], construct a homomorphism from the displayed presentation $P$ to $D_n$, sending $r$ to $\rho$ and $s$ to $\sigma$; its image is a subgroup containing $\rho$ and $\sigma$, so [F1] makes it all of $D_n$ and the homomorphism surjective. [F1, L2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Step 1.2 gives at most the same $2n$ normal forms in $P$, and step 2.1 shows that their images under the surjection of step 2.2 are all distinct; therefore that homomorphism is bijective and hence an isomorphism. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The target is the subgroup $D_n=\langle\{\rho,\sigma\}\rangle$ of $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathbb Z/n)$ specified in the Example, which step 2.1 shows has order $2n$; so the displayed isomorphism holds with the stated conventions and boundary $n\geq3$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, discharge-construct] ∎
