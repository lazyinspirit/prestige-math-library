## Example

Let $n\geq3$. Here $D_n$ means the symmetry group of a regular $n$-gon, of
order $2n$, acting on the vertex set $\mathbb Z/n$. With
$\rho(k)=k+1$ and $\sigma(k)=-k$, it is generated by the rotation $\rho$ and
reflection $\sigma$, and

$$D_n\cong\langle r,s\mid r^n,\ s^2,\ srs^{-1}r\rangle,$$

where $r$ corresponds to $\rho$ and $s$ to $\sigma$. The case $n=2$ is not
included in this geometric convention; the Klein four-group is handled
separately.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n\geq3$, the vertex set $\mathbb Z/n$, and the permutations $\rho(k)=k+1$ and $\sigma(k)=-k$.

[L1] The set $\mathbb Z/n$ has $n$ elements, represented uniquely by $0,\ldots,n-1$ ([[thm-standard-representatives-modulo-n]]).

[L2] A map of generators that sends every relator to the identity extends uniquely to a homomorphism from the presented group ([[thm-von-dyck]]).

[L3] Two disjoint finite sets have union of cardinality equal to the sum of their cardinalities ([[thm-sum-rule]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 Direct substitution on $\mathbb Z/n$ gives $\rho^n=\operatorname{id}$, $\sigma^2=\operatorname{id}$, and $\sigma\rho\sigma^{-1}\rho=\operatorname{id}$ because $\sigma\rho\sigma(k)=k-1$; thus the displayed relators hold. [given, construct]

1.2 In $P$, the relators give $s^{-1}=s$, $sr=r^{-1}s$, $r^n=e$, and $s^2=e$; moving every $s$ to the right and reducing exponents therefore writes every element as $r^is^\varepsilon$ with $0\leq i<n$ and $\varepsilon\in\{0,1\}$. [given]

1.3 The permutations $\rho^i$ are distinct by their values $\rho^i(0)=i$, and the permutations $\rho^i\sigma$ are likewise distinct; the two families are disjoint because equality would first force the same $i$ from the value at $0$ and then force $i+1=i-1$ in $\mathbb Z/n$ from the value at $1$, impossible for $n\geq3$. The same rewriting applies to the displayed relations in $D_n=\langle\rho,\sigma\rangle$, so these families exhaust $D_n$. Hence [L1] and [L3] give exactly $2n$ elements of $D_n$. [L1, L3, given]

2.1 By [L2], construct a homomorphism from the displayed presentation $P$ to $D_n=\langle\rho,\sigma\rangle$, sending $r$ to $\rho$ and $s$ to $\sigma$; it is surjective by the definition of $D_n$. [L2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Step 1.2 gives at most the same $2n$ normal forms in $P$, and step 1.3 shows that their images under the surjection of step 2.1 are all distinct; therefore that homomorphism is bijective and hence an isomorphism. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 The target is the order-$2n$ symmetry group of the regular $n$-gon specified in the Example, so the isomorphism has the stated convention and boundary $n\geq3$. [step 1.3, step 3.1, discharge-construct] ∎
