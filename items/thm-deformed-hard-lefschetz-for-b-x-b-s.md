## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A simple reflection $s$ and $x\in S_n$ with $x\ne xs$, the tensor product $M=B_x^RB_s^R$ with its induced invariant form and the deformed operator $L_\zeta$ ([[def-the-deformed-lefschetz-operator-on-b-x-b-s]]); a reduced expression $\underline x=s_1\cdots s_m$; the centered statements $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)_\zeta$, $\mathrm{HR}(x,s)_\zeta$ and their strong embedded forms with lowest degree $-\ell(x)-1$ ([[def-primitive-subspaces-hard-lefschetz-and-hodge-riemann-signs]]); the hypothesis $\mathrm S(\le x)$ and, where stated, $\mathrm{HR}(z,t)$ for $z<x$ and simple $t$ with $zt>z$, $\mathrm{HRbar}(<x,s)_\zeta$, $\mathrm{HR}(x)$ and $\mathrm{hL}(z)$ for $z<xs$.

[F1] Descent lemma: if $xs<x$ and $\mathrm S(x)$, then $M\cong N\otimes_{S^s}S$ for a graded $(S,S^s)$-bimodule $N$; with respect to the resulting identification $M=B_x(1)\oplus B_x(-1)$ the reduced operator is the matrix $L_\zeta=\begin{pmatrix}\rho\cdot(-)&0\\ \zeta\rho(\alpha_s^\vee)&\rho\cdot(-)\end{pmatrix}$; if $\mathrm{hL}(x)$ holds and $\zeta>0$ then this matrix satisfies centered hard Lefschetz, while at $\zeta=0$ it fails centered hard Lefschetz ([[lem-right-descent-soergel-bimodules-descend-to-simple-invariants-under-the-character-hypothesis]]).

[F2] Factorization identities: with $\gamma_i:=(s_{i-1}\cdots s_1\rho)(\alpha_{s_i}^\vee)$ for $1\le i\le m$ and $\gamma_{m+1}:=(x^{-1}\rho)(\alpha_s^\vee)+\zeta\rho(\alpha_s^\vee)$, the rescaled intersection forms $\langle-,-\rangle_\gamma$ on $\bigoplus_i\mathrm{BS}(\widehat{\underline{xs}}_i)$ satisfy $\langle b,L_\zeta b'\rangle_{\overline M}=\langle\varphi(b),\varphi(b')\rangle_\gamma^{\overline{\ }}$ for all $b,b'$, where $\varphi:M\to{}^1F_xB_s\oplus B_x(1)$ is the first differential of $F_xF_s$; for a reduced $\underline x$ the scalars $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$ are strictly positive and $\gamma_{m+1}>0$ whenever $xs>x$ ([[lem-the-lefschetz-operator-factors-through-deleted-bott-samelsons]], [[lem-rational-and-real-type-a-soergel-characters-and-indecomposable-labels-agree]]).

[F3] Injectivity of the minimal differential: under $\mathrm S(\le x)$ the reduced first differential $\overline\varphi$ of $F_xF_s$ is injective in internal degrees $\le\ell(x)$, for the nonnegative representative $F_xF_s$ and for homotopy-equivalent representatives with no term in degree $-1$ obtained by cancelling contractible summands whose source lies in a positive cohomological degree ([[lem-the-minimal-rouquier-differential-supplies-weak-lefschetz-injectivity]]).

[F4] Weak-Lefschetz substitute: if $\varphi:V\to W(1)$ is a map of graded $R[L]$-modules with $\varphi$ injective in degrees $\le-1$, $\langle\varphi(a),\varphi(b)\rangle_W=\langle a,Lb\rangle_V$ and $W$ satisfying HR with the standard sign, then $L^i:V^{-i}\to V^i$ is injective for all $i\ge0$, and if $\dim V^i=\dim V^{-i}$ for all $i$ then $L$ satisfies hL on $V$; an $L$-stable graded subspace with symmetric Betti numbers of a space satisfying hL and HR inherits both with the ambient signs ([[lem-family-signature-constancy-and-the-weak-lefschetz-substitute]]).

[F5] Rouquier HR: under $\mathrm S(\le x)$ and $\mathrm{HR}(z,t)$ for all $z<x$ and simple $t$ with $zt>z$, each ${}^jF_x(-j)$ embedded in $\bigoplus_{x'\in\pi(x,j)}\mathrm{BS}(x')$ satisfies HR with the induced rescaled forms and the global sign of the Rouquier theorem; in particular ${}^1F_x(-1)$ satisfies HR and ${}^1F_x(-1)=\bigoplus_{z<x}B_z^{m_{z,1}}$ componentwise ([[thm-minimal-positive-rouquier-complexes-carry-the-induced-hodge-riemann-signs]], [[thm-minimal-positive-rouquier-complexes-are-linear-under-the-lower-soergel-induction]]).

[F6] Tensoring lemma: if $B=\bigoplus_zB_z^{R,m_z}$ is self-dual with $m_z\ne0\Rightarrow\mathrm S(z)$ and $\mathrm{HR}(z,s)_\zeta$, and $B$ carries an invariant non-degenerate form making it even or odd and satisfying HR with the standard sign for $\rho$, then $BB_s$ satisfies HR with the standard sign for $L_\zeta$ and the induced form ([[lem-tensoring-with-b-s-preserves-hodge-riemann-signs|Tensoring a direct sum of Hodge-Riemann bimodules with $B_s$ preserves the signs]]).

[F7] Linearity and perverse bounds: under $\mathrm S(\le x)$ one has ${}^1F_x=\bigoplus_{z<x}B_z(1)^{m_{z,1}}$ with $m_{z,1}\ge0$ and $m_{z,1}=0$ unless $1\equiv\ell(x)-\ell(z)\pmod2$. The linearity proof also establishes $B_zF_s\simeq B_z(-1)$ for $zs<z$ under $S(z)$. The tensor bound $F_xF_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$ is derived in step 4.2 below, rather than assumed from the bound for $F_x$. The proof below derives the needed forward split injection and Gaussian cancellation from these bounds, keeping the degree-zero term $B_xB_s$ ([[thm-minimal-positive-rouquier-complexes-are-linear-under-the-lower-soergel-induction]], [[lem-radical-minimal-complexes-and-perverse-filtrations-in-the-soergel-category]], [[def-minimal-complexes-and-perverse-truncations-in-the-type-a-soergel-category]]).

[F8] Characters are additive and multiplicative, and their standard coefficients record nonnegative graded support multiplicities. Under $S(z)$, the coefficients $p_{u,z}$ of $\underline H_z$ therefore have nonnegative coefficients; in particular $\mu(u,z)=[v]p_{u,z}\ge0$. The ascent multiplication formula is $\underline H_z\underline H_s=\underline H_{zs}+\sum_{us<u<z}\mu(u,z)\underline H_u$ ([[lem-the-type-a-standard-character-is-multiplicative]], [[thm-kazhdan-lusztig-basis-multiplication-formula]]).

[F9] Without any character hypothesis, $B_x$ is an unshifted direct summand of every reduced word for $x$, and its right reduction has one-dimensional extremal degrees $-\ell(x)$ and $\ell(x)$. Each real generator has right-basis degrees $-1,1$, so iterating these bases gives a word of length $m$ right-basis degrees in $[-m,m]$, and tensoring a right-free module with $B_s$ adds $-1,1$ to its basis degrees. The rank-one splitting $B_sB_s=B_s(1)\oplus B_s(-1)$ holds independently of characters and remains valid over $\mathbb R$ ([[lem-indecomposable-type-a-soergel-bimodules-are-classified-by-top-support]], [[lem-normalized-type-a-soergel-bimodules-are-self-dual-and-have-length-degree-bounds]], [[def-real-type-a-soergel-category-and-scalar-extension]], [[lem-the-rank-one-soergel-bimodule-square-splits]]).

## Statement

The hard Lefschetz theorem for $L_\zeta$ on $M=B_x^RB_s^R$ holds in the following three cases.

(1) $\zeta>0$ and $xs<x$: under $\mathrm S(x)$, $\mathrm{hL}(x)$ implies $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)_\zeta$. The $\mathrm{hL}(x)$-only descent implication, with the same matrix and without an assumed $\mathrm S(x)$, is retained as an after-induction consequence in the character theorem, where $\mathrm S(x)$ has already been proved.

(2) $\zeta>0$ and $xs>x$: if $\mathrm S(\le x)$, $\mathrm{HR}(z,t)$ for all $z<x$ and simple $t$ with $zt>z$, $\mathrm{HRbar}(<x,s)_\zeta$ and $\mathrm{HR}(x)$ hold, then $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)_\zeta$ holds.

(3) $\zeta=0$ and $xs>x$: if $\mathrm S(\le x)$, $\mathrm{HR}(z,t)$ for all $z<x$ and simple $t$ with $zt>z$, $\mathrm{HR}(x)$ and $\mathrm{hL}(z)$ for all $z<xs$ hold, then $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)$ holds.

In the remaining case $\zeta=0$ and $xs<x$, centered hard Lefschetz fails without any character or hard-Lefschetz hypothesis on $B_x$: its nonzero bottom class in degree $-\ell(x)-1$ is killed by $L_0^{\ell(x)+1}$, although the opposite degree $\ell(x)+1$ is nonzero.

## Proof
**Proof technique:** direct; case (1) is the explicit descent matrix, case (2) uses the factorization identity and the weak-Lefschetz substitute, and case (3) separates the kernel of the reduced third component. Bimodule differentials are unreduced; their actions on reduced vectors below always mean the induced reduced differentials. All hL/HR spaces are finite right reductions.

1.1 **Case (1), positive parameter.** Assume $xs<x$, $\mathrm S(x)$ and $\mathrm{hL}(x)$. By [F1] the reduced operator is the matrix with diagonal blocks $\rho\cdot(-)$ on $B_x(1)$ and $B_x(-1)$ and lower off-diagonal block $\zeta\rho(\alpha_s^\vee)$, a scalar multiple of the identity; since $\rho\cdot(-)$ satisfies hL on $B_x$, the $\rho$-action completes to an $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R)$-action and, after rescaling by $\zeta\ne0$, the matrix describes the action of the raising operator on the tensor product of $B_x$ with the two-dimensional standard representation; hence its powers are isomorphisms in the centered degrees and $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)_\zeta$ holds, which is case (1). [given, F1]

1.2 **Case (2), setting and form identity.** Assume $\zeta>0$, $xs>x$, $\mathrm S(\le x)$, $\mathrm{HR}(z,t)$ for all $z<x$ with $zt>z$, $\mathrm{HRbar}(<x,s)_\zeta$ and $\mathrm{HR}(x)$. By [F2] the scalars $\gamma_i$ for $1\le i\le m$ are strictly positive and $\gamma_{m+1}=(x^{-1}\rho)(\alpha_s^\vee)+\zeta\rho(\alpha_s^\vee)>0$, and with $\varphi:M\to{}^1F_xB_s\oplus B_x(1)$ the first differential of $F_xF_s$ one has $\langle b,L_\zeta b'\rangle_{\overline M}=\langle\overline\varphi(b),\overline\varphi(b')\rangle_\gamma$ for all $b,b'\in\overline M$; by [F3] the reduced $\varphi$ is injective in internal degrees $\le\ell(x)$. [given, F2, F3]

1.3 **Failure at $\zeta=0$ for a descent, without $\mathrm S(x)$.** Put $m=\ell(x)$ and choose a reduced expression $\underline x=\underline y s$, where $y=xs$. By [F9], tensoring the split inclusion $B_x\subseteq\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)$ with $B_s$ and then reducing on the right gives an $L_0$-equivariant split inclusion $\overline M\subseteq\overline{\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)B_s}$. The rank-one splitting gives the ambient bimodule decomposition $\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)B_s=\mathrm{BS}(\underline y)B_sB_s\cong\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)(1)\oplus\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)(-1)$, which commutes with left multiplication by $\rho$. The reduction of $\mathrm{BS}(\underline x)$ is supported in $[-m,m]$, so $L_0^{m+1}$ vanishes on both shifted copies, and therefore on $\overline M$. A homogeneous right basis of $B_x$ has extremal degrees $-m,m$; tensoring with the right basis of $B_s$ adds $-1,1$ to these degrees. Hence $\overline M^{-m-1}$ and $\overline M^{m+1}$ are both nonzero. The centered map $L_0^{m+1}:\overline M^{-m-1}\to\overline M^{m+1}$ is thus zero with nonzero source and target, proving the unconditional failure statement. [given, F9, algebra]

2.1 **The operator commutes with $\varphi$.** Writing $\varphi=(d_1,d_2)$ with $d_1$ into ${}^1F_xB_s$ and $d_2$ into $B_x(1)$, the deleted-word factorization of [F2] shows that $d_1$ commutes with $L_\zeta$ and that $d_2(L_\zeta b)=\rho\cdot d_2(b)+d_2(b)\cdot\zeta\rho$. After right reduction the last right multiple vanishes. Thus, with $L$ equal to $L_\zeta$ on the reduced first summand and $\rho\cdot(-)$ on the reduced second summand, $\overline\varphi(L_\zeta b)=L\overline\varphi(b)$ for every $b\in\overline M$. No such equality with this $L$ is asserted on unreduced $M$. [step 1.2, F2, algebra]

3.1 **The target satisfies HR.** Consider the graded space $W:=\overline{{}^1F_x(-1)B_s}\oplus\overline{B_x}$ with the operator $L$ of step 2.1 and the form $\langle-,-\rangle_\gamma$. The two summands are orthogonal for the induced form, $B_x$ satisfies hL and HR by $\mathrm{HR}(x)$, and ${}^1F_x(-1)B_s$ satisfies hL and HR with the standard sign: its summands $B_z$ with $m_{z,1}>0$ and $z<x$ satisfy $\mathrm S(z)$ and, for every such $z$, the hypothesis $\mathrm{HR}(z,s)_\zeta$ (which $\mathrm{HRbar}(<x,s)_\zeta$ supplies, including descents since $\zeta>0$), so the tensoring lemma [F6] applies to ${}^1F_x(-1)=\bigoplus_{z<x}B_z^{m_{z,1}}$ with the positivity of the rescalings; the induced signs of ${}^1F_x(-1)$ furnished by [F5] match the standard sign. Hence $W$ satisfies HR with the standard sign, and the reduced map $\overline\varphi$ sends $\overline M$ into $W(1)$, since right reduction of the first differential target is $\overline{{}^1F_xB_s}\oplus\overline{B_x}(1)=W(1)$. [step 2.1, F5, F6]

4.1 **Case (2) concluded.** Apply the weak-Lefschetz substitute [F4] to $\overline\varphi:\overline M\to W(1)$: its three hypotheses hold by steps 1.2, 2.1 and 3.1 (injectivity in degrees $\le\ell(x)$ contains injectivity in degrees $\le-1$, the form identity is the one of [F2], and $W$ satisfies HR by step 3.1). Hence $L_\zeta^k:\overline M^{-k}\to\overline M^k$ is injective for all $k\ge0$; since $\overline M$ has symmetric dimensions by self-duality, equal dimensions give isomorphisms, which is $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)_\zeta$ and proves case (2). [step 1.2, step 3.1, F4]

4.2 **Case (3), the separated differential.** Assume $\zeta=0$, $xs>x$, $\mathrm S(\le x)$, the ascent HR hypothesis for lower elements, $\mathrm{HR}(x)$ and $\mathrm{hL}(z)$ for all $z<xs$. Use the same positive scalars $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_{m+1}$ with $\zeta=0$ and the same form identity as in [F2] with $\varphi$ the first differential of $F_xF_s$. Decompose ${}^1F_x(-1)=B^\uparrow\oplus B^\downarrow$ orthogonally according to $zs>z$ and $zs<z$ as in step 3.1, retaining the multiplicities $m_{z,1}$. To prove the required tensor bound, take any $z\le x$. If $zs>z$, [F8] makes $B_zB_s$ perverse without assuming $S(zs)$, so $B_zF_s=[B_zB_s\to B_z(1)]$ satisfies the perverse lower bound. If $zs<z$, the descent contraction in [F7] gives $B_zF_s\simeq B_z(-1)$, which also satisfies that bound. By linearity, the $k$-th term of $F_x$ is a sum of $B_z(k)$ with $z\le x$. Tensor its stupid-filtration triangles with $F_s$: each quotient is a sum of $(B_zF_s)(k)[-k]$, whose internal shifts and cohomological indices both increase by $k$, preserving the lower bound. Finite descending induction and extension closure in the radical/perverse lemma give $F_xF_s\in pK^b_{\ge0}$. The offending degree-one block is $\tau_{\le-2}(B^\downarrow B_s(1))=B^\downarrow(2)$. In the radical quotient it cannot receive a differential from the perverse degree-zero source: its shifted simple classes differ. The minimal model has no such shift in degree one, so the quotient differential out of this block is injective (otherwise that quotient simple would survive in degree one). Lift its quotient left inverse and invert the resulting identity-plus-radical composite, as in the radical lemma, to obtain a genuine split injection. Gaussian elimination cancels this forward contractible block without changing the degree-zero term. The resulting homotopy-equivalent complex has first differential $d=(d_1,d_2,d_3):B_xB_s\to B_x(1)\oplus B^\uparrow B_s(1)\oplus B^\downarrow$, all components commuting with left multiplication by $\rho$, and $\overline d$ injective in degrees $\le\ell(x)$ by [F3]. [step 1.2, F7, F8]

5.1 **Case (3), the two claims.** Fix $0\ne b\in\overline M^{-k}$ with $k\ge0$, so that $\overline d(b)\ne0$ by the injectivity just recalled. If $\overline d_3(b)\ne0$, then $\rho^k(d_3(b))=d_3(\rho^kb)$ by commutation, and $\rho^k(d_3(b))\ne0$ because $\overline d_3(b)$ lies in $B^\downarrow$, each of whose indecomposable summands $B_z$ has $z<xs$ and satisfies $\mathrm{hL}(z)$ by hypothesis, so left multiplication by $\rho$ satisfies hL on $B^\downarrow$; hence $\rho^kb\ne0$. If $\overline d_3(b)=0$, put $V=\ker(\overline d_3)\subseteq\overline M$ and $W=\overline{B_x}\oplus\overline{B^\uparrow B_s}$ with the restricted forms and the operator $\rho\cdot(-)$, write $\varphi_V:V\to W(1)$ for the restriction of $(\overline d_1,\overline d_2)$: it commutes with $\rho$, it is injective in degrees $\le-1$ (indeed in degrees $\le\ell(x)$), and it is form-compatible as follows: in the original degree-one target the descent block is $B^\downarrow(2)\oplus B^\downarrow$, pairing the two shifted pieces. The first piece has zero self-pairing: its invariant form map is $B^\downarrow(2)\to D(B^\downarrow(2))\cong B^\downarrow(-2)$, a negative-degree map between unshifted perverse summands, so it vanishes by the proved perverse Hom bound, while on $V$ the surviving $B^\downarrow$ coordinate $d_3$ is zero. Consequently both its cross-pairings and its surviving self-pairing contribute zero on $V$, so $\langle b,\rho b'\rangle_V=\langle\varphi_V(b),\varphi_V(b')\rangle_W$; and $W$ satisfies HR with the standard sign, since $B_x$ does by $\mathrm{HR}(x)$ and $B^\uparrow B_s$ does by the tensoring lemma [F6] applied with $\zeta=0$ to the ascent summands. The weak-Lefschetz substitute [F4] gives $\rho^k$ injective on $V^{-k}$, whence $\rho^kb\ne0$. Both claims show that $\rho^k$ has trivial kernel on $\overline M^{-k}$; by symmetric dimensions of $\overline M$ it is an isomorphism, which is $\mathrm{hL}(x,s)$. [step 4.2, F4, F6]

6.1 **Conclusion and caveats.** Steps 1.1 and 1.3 treat the descent cases, step 4.1 case (2) and step 5.1 case (3); no step assumes HR for the unseparated zero-parameter descent target, and no step invokes any singular-category descent theorem or any statement beyond the suppliers listed. This proves the three-case theorem and the failure statement. [step 1.3, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The hypothesis $\mathrm{HRbar}(<x,s)_\zeta$ in case (2) is used only through the summandwise statements $\mathrm{HR}(z,s)_\zeta$ for $z<x$, which is exactly the input of the tensoring lemma; the strong embedded form is not otherwise needed.
- In case (3) the shifted descent piece is cancelled before the substitute is applied, and the vanishing descent contribution on $\ker d_3$ is what makes the smaller target $W=B_x\oplus B^\uparrow B_s$ form-compatible; this is the precise sense in which the zero-parameter descent failure does not obstruct the ascent case.
