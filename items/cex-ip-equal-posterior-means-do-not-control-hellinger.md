## Statement refuted

For any proper prior probability measure on a measurable real-valued parameter space and any two finite likelihood potentials with positive finite evidence and finite posterior means, equality of the two posterior means implies equality of the posterior laws and hence zero Hellinger distance.

## Statement references

[[def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law|A finite positive evidence normalizes a likelihood potential against the prior to give a fixed-data posterior update.]]

[[def-hellinger-distance-for-dominated-laws|For supplied densities, the library Hellinger distance is the root-L2 norm of the difference of their square roots.]]

[[def-measurable-space|A measurable space is a set equipped with a sigma-algebra.]]

[[def-probability-measure|A probability measure has total mass one.]]

[[def-borel-sigma-algebra|The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets.]]

[[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable|The mean of an integrable real random variable is its probability integral.]]

[[def-expectation-on-a-finite-probability-space|On a finite probability space, expectation is the finite weighted sum.]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The false statement above, the fixed-data potential update convention, and the library root-L2 Hellinger convention.

[F1] For a finite potential $\Phi$ on a prior space, the evidence is $Z_\Phi=\int e^{-\Phi}\,d\mu_0$ and the posterior density relative to the prior is $e^{-\Phi}/Z_\Phi$ whenever $0<Z_\Phi<\infty$. [[def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law]]

[F2] If $P,Q$ have supplied densities $p,q$ with respect to a common measure $\nu$, then $h(P,Q)^2=\int(\sqrt p-\sqrt q)^2\,d\nu$. [[def-hellinger-distance-for-dominated-laws]]

[F3] The natural logarithm is the inverse of the real exponential on $(0,\infty)$. [[def-natural-logarithm]]

[F4] A measurable space is a set equipped with a sigma-algebra, and a probability measure is a countably additive measure with total mass one. [[def-measurable-space]], [[def-probability-measure]]

[F5] A posterior mean of an integrable real parameter is its expectation; on this finite space it is the finite probability-weighted sum. [[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]], [[def-expectation-on-a-finite-probability-space]]

[F6] For any $a\in\mathbb R$, the intervals $(x-|x-a|/2,x+|x-a|/2)$, indexed by $x\ne a$, cover $\mathbb R\setminus\{a\}$ and omit $a$; hence $\mathbb R\setminus\{a\}$ is open and $\{a\}$ is closed. Thus $D$ is Borel, every subset of the finite set $D$ is Borel, and its trace Borel sigma-algebra is the power set; every map from $D$ to a measurable target is consequently measurable. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

## Counterexample

Take $X=\mathbb R$, $D=\{-1,0,1\}$ with its Borel subspace structure, and the prior $\mu_0$ that assigns mass $1/3$ to each point. Put $\varepsilon=1/100$ and define two probability mass functions on $D$ by
$$p(-1)=p(1)=\varepsilon/2,\qquad p(0)=1-\varepsilon,$$
$$q(-1)=q(1)=(1-\varepsilon)/2,\qquad q(0)=\varepsilon.$$
Define finite potentials $\Phi_P(u)=-\log(3p(u))$ and $\Phi_Q(u)=-\log(3q(u))$. Their evidences are both one, so their posterior updates are precisely the laws with mass functions $p$ and $q$.

1.1 The uniform prior is the finite atomic measure $\mu_0(A):=|A|/3$ for $A\subseteq D$; it is countably additive on this finite space and has $\mu_0(D)=1$, so [F4] makes it a probability measure. The masses of $p$ and $q$ are positive and each sums to one. By [F6], $D$ is Borel and both finite potentials are measurable. By [F3], $e^{-\Phi_P(u)}=3p(u)$ and $e^{-\Phi_Q(u)}=3q(u)$ for every $u\in D$. Thus $Z_{\Phi_P}=\frac13\sum_{u\in D}3p(u)=1$ and $Z_{\Phi_Q}=\frac13\sum_{u\in D}3q(u)=1$. By [F1], the two posterior updates have masses $p$ and $q$; they are proper and have finite means. [F1, F3, F4, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F5], their means are $(-1)(\varepsilon/2)+(1)(\varepsilon/2)=0$ and $(-1)((1-\varepsilon)/2)+(1)((1-\varepsilon)/2)=0$, respectively. Hence their posterior means agree. [F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Using $\mu_0$ as the common dominating measure, the corresponding densities are $3p$ and $3q$. By [F2], $h(P,Q)^2=2\left(\sqrt{1/200}-\sqrt{99/200}\right)^2+\left(\sqrt{99/100}-1/10\right)^2.$ Since $99/100>(9/10)^2$, the central term is greater than $(4/5)^2=16/25$. Since $99/200>(7/10)^2$ and $1/200<(1/14)^2$, each endpoint squared difference is greater than $(7/10-1/14)^2=(22/35)^2$. Therefore $h(P,Q)^2>16/25+2(22/35)^2=1752/1225>1$. Equal posterior means thus coexist with Hellinger distance greater than one. [F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

This finite example only refutes a mean-only conclusion. It makes no claim that the two potentials satisfy the Hellinger stability or discretization hypotheses on the A page.
