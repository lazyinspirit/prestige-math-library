## Example

Take $k=3$ with factors $A,B,C$ and the positive generator equation $x_Ax_Bx_C=+1$, conventionally written $I=ABC$. The four runs, in $(A,B,C)$ order, are $(-1,-1,+1)$, $(-1,+1,-1)$, $(+1,-1,-1)$, and $(+1,+1,+1)$. The complete equal-column alias classes are

$$\{I,ABC\},\qquad \{A,BC\},\qquad \{B,AC\},\qquad \{C,AB\}.$$

In the unrestricted model with all eight factorial terms, each pair contributes through its coefficient sum. Separating a main effect from its aliased interaction therefore requires additional runs or an explicit sparsity or hierarchy assumption.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $k=3$, the generator equation $x_Ax_Bx_C=+1$, the four runs displayed in the Example, and the unrestricted fixed-design model with columns for all subsets of $\{A,B,C\}$.

[F1] [[def-regular-fractional-factorial-defining-relation-and-resolution]]: a regular fraction is specified by independent generator equations; $H$ is generated by their signed words, and equal-column aliases are columns that agree on every run.

[F2] [[thm-regular-fractional-factorial-aliasing-criterion]]: $A$ and $B$ have equal columns exactly when $(+1,A\triangle B)\in H$.

[F3] [[thm-factorial-column-aliases-prevent-separate-identification]]: if two distinct columns satisfy $c_A=\sigma c_B$, their contribution depends only on $\sigma\beta_A+\beta_B$, so the coefficients are not separately identifiable in the unrestricted model.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** enumerate the fraction and multiply its one defining word by every effect word.

1.1 Choose $x_A,x_B\in\{-1,+1\}$ freely and set $x_C=x_Ax_B$. This gives exactly the four runs listed in the Example, one for each of the four choices of $(x_A,x_B)$, and every run satisfies $x_Ax_Bx_C=+1$. [given, F1, construct, choose]

2.1 Here $H=\{I,ABC\}$. By [F2], effects have equal columns exactly when their symmetric difference is either $\varnothing$ or $\{A,B,C\}$. Pairing each subset with its complement gives precisely $\{I,ABC\}$, $\{A,BC\}$, $\{B,AC\}$, and $\{C,AB\}$; these four pairs exhaust the eight subsets. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 In each listed pair the columns are equal, so [F3] shows that the corresponding terms enter the fitted vector through $\beta_I+\beta_{ABC}$, $\beta_A+\beta_{BC}$, $\beta_B+\beta_{AC}$, or $\beta_C+\beta_{AB}$, respectively. The design by itself does not identify the two coefficients within any pair separately. [given, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
