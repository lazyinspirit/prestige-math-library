## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $(X,\mathcal B,\mu)$ be a sigma-finite standard-Borel measure space ([[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]) and let $(H_x,e_n(x))_{x\in X}$ be a measurable complex Hilbert field with a countable fundamental family ([[def-measurable-hilbert-field-from-a-countable-fundamental-family]]). Write $\mathcal H=\int_X^\oplus H_x\,d\mu(x)$ for its direct-integral Hilbert space ([[def-direct-integral-of-a-measurable-hilbert-field]], [[thm-direct-integrals-of-measurable-hilbert-fields-are-hilbert-spaces]]). For every $x$, let $M_x\subseteq\mathcal B(H_x)$ be a unital concrete von Neumann algebra ([[def-von-neumann-algebra-and-commutant]]). The field $x\mapsto M_x$ is **measurable** if there is a sequence $(T^{(k)})_{k\ge1}$ of weakly measurable operator fields ([[def-measurable-and-decomposable-operator-fields]]) such that

$$M_x=W^*\bigl(T_x^{(1)},T_x^{(2)},\ldots\bigr)$$

for $\mu$-almost every $x$, where $W^*$ denotes the weak-operator closure of the unital $*$-algebra generated by the listed operators.

For a measurable field $x\mapsto M_x$, define its **direct integral** to be the set

$$\int_X^\oplus M_x\,d\mu(x):=\left\{\int_X^\oplus T_x\,d\mu(x):\ (T_x)_{x\in X}\text{ is weakly measurable and essentially bounded, and }T_x\in M_x\text{ for }\mu\text{-almost every }x\right\}\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathcal H),$$

where each induced operator is supplied by [[thm-measurable-essentially-bounded-operator-fields-act-decomposably]]. Define the **diagonal algebra** by

$$\mathcal D:=\{M_f:f\in L^\infty(X,\mu),\ (M_f\xi)(x)=f(x)\xi(x)\},$$

using the complex $L^\infty$ convention of [[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]]. Then

$$\mathcal D\subseteq\int_X^\oplus M_x\,d\mu(x).$$

The fibres may be zero-dimensional: when $H_x=\{0\}$, unitality means $M_x=\{0\}$ and $I_{H_x}=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] AC is an explicit hypothesis and dependency. The von Neumann algebra setup, direct-integral Hilbert space, and decomposable operator action inherit the exact AC uses stated by their suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] Under AC the direct-integral space $\mathcal H$ is a complete Hilbert space ([[thm-direct-integrals-of-measurable-hilbert-fields-are-hilbert-spaces]]).

[F2] A concrete von Neumann algebra is a unital weak-operator-closed $*$-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(H_x)$; the zero Hilbert space is allowed with sole unital algebra $\{0\}$ ([[def-von-neumann-algebra-and-commutant]]).

[F3] An operator field is weakly measurable when its fundamental matrix coefficients are measurable; an operator field is essentially bounded when $\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_x\|T_x\|<\infty$ ([[def-measurable-and-decomposable-operator-fields]]).

[F4] Every weakly measurable essentially bounded field induces a well-defined bounded decomposable operator on $\mathcal H$, acting on classes by $[\xi]\mapsto[x\mapsto T_x\xi(x)]$ ([[thm-measurable-essentially-bounded-operator-fields-act-decomposably]]).

[F5] Every $f\in L^\infty(X,\mu)$ is an almost-everywhere class of measurable complex functions with finite essential bound ([[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]]).

[F6] The fundamental vectors $e_n$ are measurable sections, and $x\mapsto\langle e_n(x),e_m(x)\rangle$ is Borel measurable ([[def-measurable-hilbert-field-from-a-countable-fundamental-family]]).

[F7] Products of measurable complex scalar functions are measurable ([[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]], [[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]]).

[F8] The direct integral identifies sections equal outside a measurable null set ([[def-direct-integral-of-a-measurable-hilbert-field]]).

[F9] The direct-integral inner product is the integral of the fibre inner products, with the fibre pairing linear in the first variable ([[def-direct-integral-of-a-measurable-hilbert-field]], [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]).

[F10] $L^\infty(X,\mu)$ is closed under sums, scalar multiples, products, and complex conjugation ([[def-complex-lp-and-euclidean-test-function-conventions]]).

[F11] The base is a standard-Borel space with a sigma-finite measure, as assumed in the definition ([[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-finite-sigma-finite-and-semifinite-measures]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The AC-qualified measurable Hilbert field, its direct-integral Hilbert space, the field $x\mapsto M_x$, and its countable weakly measurable generating family.

1.1 By [F1], $\mathcal H$ is a Hilbert space. For any weakly measurable essentially bounded operator field $(T_x)$ with $T_x\in M_x$ almost everywhere, [F4] supplies a bounded operator $\int_X^\oplus T_x\,d\mu(x)\in\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$. Thus the displayed direct integral is a well-defined subset of $\mathcal B(\mathcal H)$; this definition does not assert that the set is weak-operator closed. [A1, F1, F4, F11]

1.2 Fix $f\in L^\infty(X,\mu)$ and choose a measurable representative. By [F5], some finite $C$ and measurable null set $N$ satisfy $|f(x)|\le C$ for every $x\notin N$. Define $T_x:=f(x)I_{H_x}$. Its fundamental matrix coefficients are $\langle T_xe_n(x),e_m(x)\rangle=f(x)\langle e_n(x),e_m(x)\rangle$, measurable by [F6, F7]; its operator norm is at most $|f(x)|$, including when $H_x=\{0\}$, so it is essentially bounded. Since every $M_x$ is unital, $f(x)I_{H_x}\in M_x$ for every $x$; at a zero fibre this is $0\in\{0\}$. Hence this field satisfies the direct-integral membership conditions. [F2, F3, F5, F6, F7]

2.1 By [F4], the field of step 1.2 induces a decomposable operator acting on square-integrable classes by $[\xi]\mapsto[x\mapsto f(x)\xi(x)]$, which is exactly $M_f$. If $f$ is changed on a measurable null set, [F4] gives the same induced operator, so $M_f$ depends only on its $L^\infty$ class. Pointwise action gives $M_f+M_g=M_{f+g}$, $cM_f=M_{cf}$, and $M_fM_g=M_{fg}$; the first-variable-linear integral pairing gives $\langle M_f[\xi],[\eta]\rangle=\langle[\xi],M_{\overline f}[\eta]\rangle$, so $M_f^*=M_{\overline f}$. Since $1\in L^\infty$ and $M_1=I_{\mathcal H}$ (including $I_{\mathcal H}=0$ when $\mathcal H=\{0\}$), [F8] makes $\mathcal D$ a unital $*$-algebra. Each $M_f$ belongs to the displayed direct-integral set by step 1.2, proving $\mathcal D\subseteq\int_X^\oplus M_x\,d\mu(x)$. [F4, F8, F9, F10, step 1.2] ∎

## Remarks

The definition specifies a set of decomposable operators. Weak-operator closure of this set is a separate theorem for measurable fields; no closure assertion is built into the definition. The diagonal inclusion is proved locally above.
