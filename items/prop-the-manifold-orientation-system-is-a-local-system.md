## Statement

Let $M$ be a boundaryless $n$-manifold. The published orientation system $\mathcal O_M$ is a functor $\Pi_1(M)\to\mathbb Z\text{-}\mathsf{Mod}$. For a commutative unital ring $R$, the stalkwise extension
$$\mathcal O_M^R=\mathcal O_M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$$
is a rank-one $R$-module local system. At a chosen basepoint of a component, a loop acts on a chosen generator by its orientation character $w_M:\pi_1(M)\to\{\pm1\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A boundaryless $n$-manifold $M$ and a commutative unital ring $R$.

[F1] [[def-orientation-local-system-and-orientation-cover]] constructs the stalks $\mathcal O_x=H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)$ and transport $T_\gamma$ depending only on the endpoint-fixed homotopy class of $\gamma$; it proves identity, reversal, and concatenation laws.

[F2] [[def-local-system-of-r-modules-and-its-pullback]] defines a local system as a covariant fundamental-groupoid functor.

[F3] [[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]] constructs the tensor product as an abelian group generated by elementary tensors, modulo additivity and balancing relations. Its arbitrary-ring definition supplies no further module structure or functoriality.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Assign $x\mapsto\mathcal O_x$ and $[\gamma:x\to y]\mapsto T_\gamma$. Homotopy invariance in [F1] makes the arrow map well defined. The constant-path and concatenation identities say respectively that identities and composition in $\Pi_1(M)$ are preserved, while reversal says every transport is an isomorphism. This is precisely the functor required by [F2]. [F1, F2]

1.2 Put $(\mathcal O_M^R)_x=\mathcal O_x\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R$. On elementary tensors define
$$q\cdot(o\otimes r):=o\otimes(qr).$$
The additive and balancing relations in [F3] are preserved by this formula;
distributivity, associativity, and the unit law in $R$ therefore make the
tensor product a left $R$-module. For a path $\gamma:x\to y$, the formula
$$o\otimes r\longmapsto T_\gamma(o)\otimes r$$
also preserves every relation in [F3], because $T_\gamma$ is an additive,
hence $\mathbb Z$-linear, map. It descends to an $R$-linear map denoted
$T_\gamma\otimes1_R$. Identity and composition agree on elementary tensors,
which generate the tensor product, and $T_{\bar\gamma}\otimes1_R$ is the
inverse. Thus these maps define an $R$-module local system. Finally, if
$o_x$ generates $\mathcal O_x$, the maps
$$R\longrightarrow(\mathcal O_M^R)_x,\quad r\longmapsto o_x\otimes r,\qquad (m o_x)\otimes r\longmapsto mr$$
are well defined inverse $R$-linear maps by the same relations. Hence every
stalk is free of rank one, including the zero ring under the usual rank-one
convention $R\cong R$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

2.1 Fix $x$ and a generator $o_x$. A loop transport is an automorphism of the infinite cyclic group $\mathcal O_x$, so $T_\gamma(o_x)=w_M(\gamma)o_x$ for a unique sign. Composition in step 1.1 makes $w_M$ a homomorphism. It is $+1$ exactly when the lifted path in the orientation cover returns to the chosen generator sheet, and $-1$ exactly when it returns to the other sheet, so it is the orientation character. Scalar extension gives the same multiplication by $w_M(\gamma)$ on $\mathcal O_x^R$. Changing $o_x$ to $-o_x$ does not change this sign. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For an orientable component, a continuous choice of generator identifies the system with the constant rank-one system; conversely such a trivialization selects one sheet of the orientation cover and orients the component. The empty manifold gives the empty functor, dimension zero gives constant stalks on a discrete space, and disconnected manifolds are handled componentwise. No global generator or path family is selected in the construction, so no AC is used. [F1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
