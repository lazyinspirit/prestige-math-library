## Definition

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},v)$ be a finite-type symmetrizable Cartan datum, with
$v_i=v^{d_i}$, and let $U_v(\mathfrak g)$ be the scalar extension of
[[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]] from $\mathbb Q(v)$ to
$\mathbb C(v)$. The finite-type Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ is the Serre Lie
algebra of the matrix $A$
([[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]). Write
$A_L=\mathbb C[v,v^{-1}]$ and
$A_{DK}=\mathbb C[v,v^{-1},(v_i-v_i^{-1})^{-1}\ (i\in I)]$.

The **Lusztig divided-power form** $U^L_{A_L}(\mathfrak g)$ is the scalar
extension to $A_L$ of the subalgebra $U_A$ defined in
[[def-lusztig-divided-power-integral-form]]. Thus it is generated by the
divided powers $E_i^{(r)},F_i^{(r)}$, the toral elements $K_h^{\pm1}$, and the
toral quantum binomials $T_{i,c,t}$.

The **De Concini–Kac form** $U^{DK}_{A_{DK}}(\mathfrak g)$ is the
$A_{DK}$-subalgebra of $U_v(\mathfrak g)$ generated by $E_i,F_i$ and
$K_h^{\pm1}$. It uses ordinary powers of the root generators. This definition
does not assert that it is free over $A_{DK}$ or that its later specialization
embeds into the Lusztig specialization.

Let $\zeta\in\mathbb C^\times$ be a root of unity. Require
$\zeta_i-\zeta_i^{-1}\ne0$ for each $i$ and put
$\ell_i=\operatorname{ord}(\zeta_i^2)>1$. The specialization map sends
$v\mapsto\zeta$ and has target $\mathbb C$. Define
$$U^{DK}_\zeta(\mathfrak g):=U^{DK}_{A_{DK}}(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{A_{DK},\,v\mapsto\zeta}\mathbb C,\qquad U^L_\zeta(\mathfrak g):=U^L_{A_L}(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{A_L,\,v\mapsto\zeta}\mathbb C.$$
In the standard good odd-order branch, $\zeta$ has odd order $\ell$ and
$\ell$ is relatively prime to every $d_i$; then every $\ell_i=\ell$.

A root-power quotient depends on the chosen toral lattice. In the
convention where the torus is generated by the $K_i:=K_{d_i h_i}$, define
the **simple-root-power candidate** $u_\zeta^{\mathrm{cand}}(\mathfrak g)$ as the quotient of that ordinary-generator De Concini–Kac algebra by the
two-sided ideal generated by
$E_i^{\ell_i},F_i^{\ell_i},K_i^{\ell_i}-1$ for $i\in I$. This names the quotient construction only. In higher rank it is not the definition of the standard small quantum group: nonsimple positive-root powers need not be killed by imposing only the displayed simple-root powers. No finite dimension, embedding, or higher-rank identification is asserted for this candidate. The rank-one results below use this quotient, where there are no nonsimple positive roots, and denote it by $u_\zeta(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$.

For a standard **small image form**, let $D_\zeta^{(i)}$ be the specialization of the ordinary-generator form with torus generated by the $K_i^{\pm1}$. Define
$$\overline U^L_\zeta=U^L_\zeta/(K_h^\ell-1:h\in P^\vee),\qquad \overline u_\zeta=\operatorname{im}\bigl(D_\zeta^{(i)}\longrightarrow\overline U^L_\zeta\bigr)$$
in the good odd-order branch. The map is the specialization of the generic inclusion into the divided-power form localized by the invertible $v_i-v_i^{-1}$, followed by the toral quotient; it is well defined because those denominators are nonzero at the chosen root. This image definition fixes the actual ordinary-generator form without claiming a presentation by simple root-power relations. The general dimension and root-vector PBW description require separate proofs; no such assertion is implicit here.

The generic inclusion of the ordinary-generator form into the divided-power
form does not by itself assert an inclusion after specializing $v$. The
rank-one items on this page prove a specific root-power quotient and exhibit
the failure of injectivity after specialization. This definition does not
assert a quantum Frobenius theorem or any generic-$v$ semisimplicity at a root
of unity.
