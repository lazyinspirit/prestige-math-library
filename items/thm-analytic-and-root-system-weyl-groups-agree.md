## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a compact connected Lie group with
maximal torus $T$. Then the faithful action of $W(G,T)$ on the semisimple part
of $\mathfrak t$ identifies it with the Weyl group generated by the root
reflections: for every root $\alpha$ the associated compact root $SU(2)$
supplies a cocharacter $\alpha^\vee:S^1\to T$ with
$\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$, and conjugation by its standard normalizer
element acts on $\mathfrak t$ by the reflection $s_\alpha$; the resulting
reflections generate $W(G,T)$.

Here $S^1=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ is identified with the multiplicative unit circle by $[r]\mapsto e^{2\pi ir}$. Root differentials are imaginary on $\mathfrak t$ and real on $i\mathfrak t$. Reflections act on both spaces by complex-linear extension and act trivially on the central summand.

Moreover, if $\sigma$ is conjugation of the complexified semisimple algebra with
respect to its compact real form, the root vectors in each root triple may be
normalized so that $\sigma(e_\alpha)=-f_\alpha$ and
$\sigma(f_\alpha)=-e_\alpha$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected Lie group $G$ with maximal torus $T$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and root system $\Phi=\Phi(G,T)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the metric/structure theory of [L1] and [L4] and through the integration theorem [L3].

[L1] The roots of $(G,T)$, with their differentials, form a reduced crystallographic root system on the dual of $\mathfrak t\cap[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$, vanish on the central directions, and have the finite adjoint weight-space decomposition in [[def-roots-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]]. The reflection $s_\alpha$ exists for every root, and $W(\Phi)$ is generated by these reflections ([[thm-compact-group-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system-on-the-semisimple-part]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).

[L2] For every root $\alpha$ of a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra there are $e_\alpha\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and $f_\alpha\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ with $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=h_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,e_\alpha]=2e_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,f_\alpha]=-2f_\alpha$, where $h_\alpha$ is the coroot with $\alpha(h_\alpha)=2$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

[L3] Assuming countable choice, for a connected simply connected real Lie group $G_1$, a real Lie group $H$ and a Lie-algebra homomorphism $\phi:\operatorname{Lie}(G_1)\to\operatorname{Lie}(H)$ there is a unique Lie-group homomorphism $F:G_1\to H$ with $dF_e=\phi$ ([[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]]).

[L4] $C_G(S)$ is connected for every torus $S\le G$, $C_G(T)=T$, and $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ is finite; every element of $G$ lies in a maximal torus and any two maximal tori are conjugate; every torus lies in a maximal torus ([[thm-compact-group-weyl-group-is-finite]], [[thm-every-element-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group-lies-in-a-maximal-torus]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]], [[cor-every-compact-connected-abelian-subgroup-is-contained-in-a-maximal-torus]]).

[L5] The root Weyl group acts simply transitively on the chambers, equivalently on the positive systems of $\Phi$ ([[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]]).

[L6] The Weyl vector $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha$ satisfies $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$ for the simple roots ([[prop-weyl-vector-is-the-sum-of-fundamental-weights]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho]]). Positive roots have nonnegative integral simple-root coordinates ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

[L7] Root spaces in a complex semisimple Lie algebra are one-dimensional ([[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]]). For the complexification $\mathfrak s_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak s\oplus i\mathfrak s$ in [L1], the map $\sigma(U+iV)=U-iV$ is a conjugate-linear bracket-preserving involution with fixed algebra $\mathfrak s$, directly from the complex-bilinear extension of the real bracket and uniqueness of real and imaginary parts.

[L8] $SU(2)$ is a real Lie group with algebra the traceless skew-Hermitian matrices, and $S^3$ is simply connected ([[ex-unitary-and-special-unitary-lie-groups]], [[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]]).

[L9] Exponentials are natural under Lie homomorphisms and $d\operatorname{Ad}=\operatorname{ad}$; closed subgroups are embedded Lie subgroups, and exponentials are local diffeomorphisms at zero ([[prop-exponential-map-is-natural-for-lie-group-homomorphisms]], [[thm-the-differential-of-adjoint-is-ad]], [[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]], [[cor-the-exponential-map-is-a-local-diffeomorphism-at-zero]]).

[L10] A compact Lie group has an $\operatorname{Ad}$-invariant positive-definite inner product on its Lie algebra. Complete reducibility of the adjoint module gives the reductive splitting $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)\oplus[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$ with semisimple derived algebra, and a semisimple Lie algebra is centerless ([[prop-compact-lie-groups-admit-bi-invariant-riemannian-metrics]], [[thm-equivalent-characterizations-of-reductive-lie-algebras]], [[cor-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-centerless-and-perfect]]).

[L11] In a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, the Cartan subalgebras are exactly the maximal toral subalgebras ([[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-exactly-maximal-toral-subalgebras]]). For $X$ in a real Lie algebra, $\operatorname{Ad}(\exp X)=\exp(\operatorname{ad}X)$ ([[prop-adjoint-exponential-identity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $\mathfrak s=[\mathfrak g,\mathfrak g]$, $\mathfrak z=\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)$ and $\mathfrak t_s=\mathfrak t\cap\mathfrak s$. The invariant inner product in [L10] makes the orthogonal complement of every ideal an ideal, so finite-dimensional induction makes the adjoint module completely reducible; [L10] therefore gives $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak z\oplus\mathfrak s$ with $\mathfrak s$ semisimple. We next identify the exact Lie-algebra root interface rather than assuming it from [L1]. If $X$ centralizes $\mathfrak t$, then [L11] gives $\operatorname{Ad}(\exp(sX))H=H$ for every $H\in\mathfrak t$; naturality and an exponential identity neighborhood show that $\exp(sX)$ centralizes $T$, so [L4] puts it in $T$ and differentiation gives $X\in\mathfrak t$. Thus $\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak t)=\mathfrak t$, whence $\mathfrak z\subseteq\mathfrak t$ and $\mathfrak t=\mathfrak z\oplus\mathfrak t_s$. The operators $\operatorname{ad}_H$ for $H\in\mathfrak t_s$ are commuting skew-adjoint operators, so their complexifications are simultaneously diagonalizable. If $U+iV\in\mathfrak s_{\mathbb C}$ centralizes $(\mathfrak t_s)_{\mathbb C}$, then $U,V\in\mathfrak s$ centralize $\mathfrak t_s$ and also $\mathfrak z$, hence all of $\mathfrak t$; the centralizer equality puts $U,V$ in $\mathfrak t_s$. Therefore $(\mathfrak t_s)_{\mathbb C}$ is maximal toral, hence Cartan by [L11]. Finally the adjoint $T$-weight decomposition in [L1], after differentiation, has zero space $\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}$ and nonzero weights trivial on $\mathfrak z$; restricting it to $\mathfrak s_{\mathbb C}$ gives exactly the root-space decomposition relative to this Cartan algebra. Thus every root used below is a Lie-algebra root to which [L2] and [L7] apply. The invariant inner product makes every $\operatorname{ad}_X$, $X\in\mathfrak s$, skew-adjoint; in an orthonormal real basis,
$$B(X,X)=\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}_X^2)=-\sum_{j,k}(\operatorname{ad}_X)_{jk}^{2}\le0.$$
Equality forces $\operatorname{ad}_X=0$, hence $X=0$ because $\mathfrak s$ is centerless by [L10]. Thus $B$ is negative definite on $\mathfrak s$ and its complex-bilinear extension is positive definite on $i\mathfrak t_s$. For a root use [L2] to choose $e,f,h$. Conjugation $\sigma$ sends its root space to the opposite one because the root is imaginary on $\mathfrak t$, and sends $h\in i\mathfrak t_s$ to $-h$. By [L7], $\sigma(e)=cf$ for a nonzero scalar. Invariance of $B$ gives $B(h,h)=B([e,f],h)=2B(e,f)>0$. Writing $e=U+iV$ with $U,V\in\mathfrak s$, we have $B(e,\sigma e)=B(U,U)+B(V,V)<0$. Therefore $c=B(e,\sigma e)/B(e,f)$ is real and negative. Replacing $(e,f)$ by $(ae,a^{-1}f)$ with $|a|^2=-1/c$ gives $\sigma(e)=-f$, and involutivity gives $\sigma(f)=-e$. Thus $X=e-f$, $Y=i(e+f)$ and $H=ih$ belong to $\mathfrak s$ and satisfy $[H,X]=2Y$, $[H,Y]=-2X$, $[X,Y]=2H$. They are the images of the standard traceless skew-Hermitian basis of $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. [L1, L2, L4, L7, L8, L10, L11]

1.2 The map $(z,w)\mapsto\begin{pmatrix}z&-\overline w\\w&\overline z\end{pmatrix}$ identifies the unit sphere $|z|^2+|w|^2=1$ in $\mathbb C^2$ homeomorphically with $SU(2)$: orthonormality of the columns and determinant one force the displayed second column, and the inverse reads the first column. Thus $SU(2)$ is connected and simply connected by [L8]. A connected Lie group is generated by any exponential neighborhood of the identity: the generated subgroup is open and all its cosets are open, so connectedness forces it to be the whole group. [L8, L9]

2.1 By [L3] the inclusion $\mathfrak{su}(2)\cong\mathfrak k\hookrightarrow\mathfrak g$ integrates to a Lie-group homomorphism $\Phi_\alpha:SU(2)\to G$. Define $\alpha^\vee:S^1\to G$ by $\alpha^\vee(z):=\Phi_\alpha(\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1}))$. Since $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta})=\exp(\theta\cdot\operatorname{diag}(i,-i))$ and $\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ corresponds to $H\in\mathfrak t$, the image of $\alpha^\vee$ is $\exp(\mathbb RH)\subseteq T$, so $\alpha^\vee$ is a cocharacter of $T$. [L3, L9, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 On the root space $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ the element $H$ acts by the scalar $\alpha(H)=i\alpha(h_\alpha)=2i$, so $\exp(\theta H)$ acts on $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ by $e^{2i\theta}$, and $\Phi_\alpha(\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1}))$ acts on $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ by $z^2$; therefore $\alpha(\alpha^\vee(z))=z^2$ for all $z$, that is, $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$. [L2, L9, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 The standard matrix $n_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ equals $\exp(-\pi X_0/2)$, where $X_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. It conjugates $\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ to its negative. If $Z\in\mathfrak t$ satisfies $\alpha(Z)=0$, the root relations give $[Z,e]=[Z,f]=0$, so $[Z,X]=0$ and [L9] gives $\operatorname{Ad}(\Phi_\alpha(n_0))Z=Z$. Hence $n_\alpha=\Phi_\alpha(n_0)$ fixes $\ker\alpha$ pointwise and negates $H$. Since $\alpha(H)=2i$, these subspaces give all of $\mathfrak t$, and the action is the coroot reflection. Naturality and generation by exponential neighborhoods (step 1.2) give $n_\alpha Tn_\alpha^{-1}=T$. Every element of connected $G$ fixes $\mathfrak z(\mathfrak g)$ under the adjoint action: this holds on exponentials since $\operatorname{ad}_Z$ kills the center, and hence on their generated group. Thus the faithful action in [L4] stays faithful on $\mathfrak t_s$, and the realized reflections give $W(\Phi)\subseteq W(G,T)$. [L1, L2, L4, L9, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Conversely let $g\in N_G(T)$ with class $w\in W(G,T)$. Conjugation sends a root vector of weight $\alpha$ to one of weight $\alpha\circ\operatorname{Ad}(g)^{-1}$, so it permutes the roots. It preserves $B$ because it conjugates adjoint matrices and trace is invariant under conjugation. Therefore it takes positive systems to positive systems. By [L5] there is $w'\in W(\Phi)$ with $w'w(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$; by step 3.2 the element $w'$ is realised by some $n'\in N_G(T)$, so $a:=n'g$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ad}(a)(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$. [L1, L5, step 3.2]

5.1 Because $a$ permutes the positive roots, it fixes their half-sum $\rho$. Let $H_\rho\in i\mathfrak t_s$ be the $B$-dual of $\rho$; invariance of $B$ gives $\operatorname{Ad}(a)H_\rho=H_\rho$. Set $V_\rho=iH_\rho\in\mathfrak t_s$. For each simple root, [L6] gives $(\rho,\alpha_i)=|\alpha_i|^2/2>0$. Every positive root is a nonzero nonnegative combination of simple roots, so $(\rho,\beta)>0$ for positive $\beta$, and the pairing is nonzero for every root. In particular $\beta(V_\rho)=i\beta(H_\rho)=i(\beta,\rho)\ne0$. [L1, L6, step 4.1]

6.1 Let $S=\overline{\exp(\mathbb RV_\rho)}\subseteq T$. Continuity of multiplication and inversion makes this a closed subgroup; it is abelian, compact and connected as the closure of a connected subgroup, hence a torus by [L9]. Naturality implies that $a$ centralizes its dense one-parameter subgroup, and therefore $S$. The closed subgroup $C_G(S)$ has Lie algebra consisting of the vectors fixed by every $\operatorname{Ad}(s)$: necessity follows by differentiating conjugation, and sufficiency by naturality of the exponential and its local charts. The root decomposition in [L1] and $\beta(V_\rho)\ne0$ show that this fixed algebra is exactly $\mathfrak t$. By [L4] the centralizer is connected. It contains $T$, and the exponential charts of these two groups with the same Lie algebra show $T$ is open in it; connectedness gives $C_G(S)=T$. Thus $a\in T$. If the root set is empty, [L1] gives $\mathfrak t=\mathfrak g$, $\rho=V_\rho=0$ and $S=\{e\}$; the same open-subgroup argument gives $G=T$ and both Weyl groups are trivial. [L1, L4, L9, step 5.1]

7.1 Hence $g\in n'^{-1}T$, so $w\in w'^{-1}W(\Phi)\subseteq W(\Phi)$; combined with step 3.2, the analytic Weyl group $W(G,T)$ equals the root-system Weyl group $W(\Phi)$, and the cocharacters $\alpha^\vee$ of step 2.1 supply the claimed reflections with pairing $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$. The Axiom of Choice entered only through the cited metric, structure and integration theory. [A1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 6.1] ∎
