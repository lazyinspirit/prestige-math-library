## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a compact connected Lie group with
maximal torus $T$. Then the faithful action of $W(G,T)$ on the semisimple part
of $\mathfrak t$ identifies it with the Weyl group generated by the root
reflections: for every root $\alpha$ the associated compact root $SU(2)$
supplies a cocharacter $\alpha^\vee:S^1\to T$ with
$\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$, and conjugation by its standard normalizer
element acts on $\mathfrak t$ by the reflection $s_\alpha$; the resulting
reflections generate $W(G,T)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected Lie group $G$ with maximal torus $T$, Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and root system $\Phi=\Phi(G,T)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the metric/structure theory of [L1] and [L4] and through the integration theorem [L3].

[L1] The roots of $(G,T)$, with their differentials, form a reduced crystallographic root system on the dual of the semisimple part of $\mathfrak t$, with reflection $s_\alpha$ on that dual for every root $\alpha$; the Weyl group $W(\Phi)$ of this root system is generated by the $s_\alpha$ ([[thm-compact-group-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system-on-the-semisimple-part]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]], [[def-roots-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]]).

[L2] For every root $\alpha$ of a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra there are $e_\alpha\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and $f_\alpha\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ with $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=h_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,e_\alpha]=2e_\alpha$, $[h_\alpha,f_\alpha]=-2f_\alpha$, where $h_\alpha$ is the coroot with $\alpha(h_\alpha)=2$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

[L3] For a connected simply connected real Lie group $G_1$, a real Lie group $H$ and a Lie-algebra homomorphism $\phi:\operatorname{Lie}(G_1)\to\operatorname{Lie}(H)$ there is a unique Lie-group homomorphism $F:G_1\to H$ with $dF_e=\phi$ ([[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]]).

[L4] $C_G(S)$ is connected for every torus $S\le G$, $C_G(T)=T$, and $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ is finite; every element of $G$ lies in a maximal torus and any two maximal tori are conjugate; every torus lies in a maximal torus ([[thm-compact-group-weyl-group-is-finite]], [[thm-every-element-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group-lies-in-a-maximal-torus]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]], [[cor-every-compact-connected-abelian-subgroup-is-contained-in-a-maximal-torus]]).

[L5] The root Weyl group acts simply transitively on the chambers, equivalently on the positive systems of $\Phi$ ([[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]]).

[L6] The Weyl vector $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha$ satisfies $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$ for the simple roots, so $\langle\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle>0$ for every positive root and $\rho$ is regular ([[prop-weyl-vector-is-the-sum-of-fundamental-weights]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\alpha$ be a root. By [L2] there are $e_\alpha,f_\alpha,h_\alpha$ spanning a copy of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ in $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$, and the compact conjugation $\sigma$ of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ over $\mathfrak g$ interchanges $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$; replacing $e_\alpha$ by a nonzero scalar multiple we may assume $\sigma(e_\alpha)=f_\alpha$. Then $X:=e_\alpha-f_\alpha$, $Y:=i(e_\alpha+f_\alpha)$, $H:=ih_\alpha$ lie in $\mathfrak g$ and satisfy $[H,X]=2Y$, $[H,Y]=-2X$, $[X,Y]=2H$, so they span a real subalgebra $\mathfrak k$ isomorphic to $\mathfrak{su}(2)$. [L2]

2.1 By [L3] the inclusion $\mathfrak{su}(2)\cong\mathfrak k\hookrightarrow\mathfrak g$ integrates to a Lie-group homomorphism $\Phi_\alpha:SU(2)\to G$. Define $\alpha^\vee:S^1\to G$ by $\alpha^\vee(z):=\Phi_\alpha(\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1}))$. Since $\operatorname{diag}(e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta})=\exp(\theta\cdot\operatorname{diag}(i,-i))$ and $\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ corresponds to $H\in\mathfrak t$, the image of $\alpha^\vee$ is $\exp(\mathbb RH)\subseteq T$, so $\alpha^\vee$ is a cocharacter of $T$. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 On the root space $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ the element $H$ acts by the scalar $\alpha(H)=i\alpha(h_\alpha)=2i$, so $\exp(\theta H)$ acts on $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ by $e^{2i\theta}$, and $\Phi_\alpha(\operatorname{diag}(z,z^{-1}))$ acts on $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ by $z^2$; therefore $\alpha(\alpha^\vee(z))=z^2$ for all $z$, that is, $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$. [L2, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 The element $n_\alpha:=\Phi_\alpha\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ lies in $N_G(T)$: it conjugates $H$ to $-H$ and, by the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ commutation relations, exchanges the $\pm\alpha$ weight spaces while fixing pointwise the kernel of $\alpha$ in $\mathfrak t$, so its action on $\mathfrak t$ is exactly the reflection $s_\alpha$; hence $W(\Phi)\subseteq W(G,T)$ under the action of [L1]. [L1, L2, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Conversely let $g\in N_G(T)$ with class $w\in W(G,T)$. By [L5] there is $w'\in W(\Phi)$ with $w'w(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$; by step 3.2 the element $w'$ is realised by some $n'\in N_G(T)$, so $a:=n'g$ satisfies $\operatorname{Ad}(a)(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$. [L1, L5, step 3.2]

5.1 An automorphism of the root system preserving the positive system permutes the simple roots, hence permutes the fundamental weights; therefore $\operatorname{Ad}(a)^*\rho=\rho$ for the induced action on the dual of $\mathfrak t$, and if $H_\rho$ denotes the element of $\mathfrak t$ dual to $\rho$ under the invariant form, then $\operatorname{Ad}(a)H_\rho=H_\rho$. [L1, L6, step 4.1]

6.1 The closure $S:=\overline{\exp(\mathbb RH_\rho)}$ is a torus containing $\exp(\mathbb RH_\rho)$, and $a$ centralizes $S$ because it fixes the dense one-parameter subgroup pointwise. Since $\rho$ is regular by [L6], every root $\beta$ satisfies $\beta(H_\rho)=\langle\beta,\rho\rangle\ne0$, so no root space of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ is fixed by the adjoint action of the nontrivial character $\beta|_S$; hence $\operatorname{Lie}(C_G(S))=\mathfrak c_{\mathfrak g}(S)=\mathfrak t$. By [L4] the group $C_G(S)$ is connected and contains $T$, and a connected subgroup with Lie algebra $\mathfrak t$ containing the connected subgroup $T$ with the same Lie algebra coincides with $T$; therefore $a\in C_G(S)=T$. [L4, L6, step 5.1]

7.1 Hence $g\in n'^{-1}T$, so $w\in w'^{-1}W(\Phi)\subseteq W(\Phi)$; combined with step 3.2, the analytic Weyl group $W(G,T)$ equals the root-system Weyl group $W(\Phi)$, and the cocharacters $\alpha^\vee$ of step 2.1 supply the claimed reflections with pairing $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$. The Axiom of Choice entered only through the cited metric, structure and integration theory. [A1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 6.1] ∎
