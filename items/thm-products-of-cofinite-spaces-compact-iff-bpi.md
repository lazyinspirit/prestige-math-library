## Statement

Over $\mathrm{ZF}$, BPI ([[def-boolean-prime-ideal-principle]]) is equivalent to
the compactness in the product topology ([[def-product-topology]],
[[def-compact-space]]) of every product of spaces each carrying the cofinite
topology ([[def-standard-topologies]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A family $\{(X_i, \mathcal{T}_i)\}_{i \in I}$ of spaces with $\mathcal{T}_i$ the cofinite topology on $X_i$, and its product $X$ with projections $\pi_i$.

[F1] The closed sets of the cofinite topology on a set are the set itself and its finite subsets; the cofinite space is $T_1$ ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-t0-and-t1-spaces]]).

[F2] A space is compact if and only if every family of closed sets with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection ([[thm-compact-iff-fip]], [[def-finite-intersection-property]]).

[F3] The set ultrafilter lemma is equivalent over ZF to BPI: every filter on a set extends to an ultrafilter, and an ultrafilter contains a set exactly when it contains no member of the complement ([[thm-bpi-equivalent-to-set-ultrafilter-lemma]], [[def-ultrafilter]], [[thm-ultrafilter-characterisation]], [[def-boolean-prime-ideal-principle]]).

[F4] Under BPI, every product of compact Hausdorff spaces is compact, and the two-point discrete space is compact Hausdorff ([[thm-bpi-equivalent-to-compact-hausdorff-tychonoff]], [[def-standard-topologies]]).

[L1] The complements of basic product-open sets form the following closed basis: $$\mathcal C(X)=\left\{\bigcup_{q\in Q}\pi_q^{-1}[C_q]: Q\subseteq I\text{ finite and every }C_q\subseteq X_q\text{ is closed} \right\}.$$ Consequently $X$ is compact if every subfamily of $\mathcal C(X)$ with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection: the complementary basic open sets then satisfy the finite-subcover test ([[def-product-topology]], [[def-compact-space]], [[def-finite-intersection-property]]).

[L2] Every cofinite space is compact in ZF. Indeed, a family of its closed sets with the finite intersection property either contains only the whole space, or contains a finite member $C$; in the latter case the intersections with $C$ have nonempty total intersection, since otherwise finitely many of them already exclude the finitely many points of $C$ ([[thm-compact-iff-fip]], [[def-natural-numbers]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In the empty product case, if some $X_i = \varnothing$ then $X = \varnothing$ and $X$ is compact, and if $I = \varnothing$ then $X$ is a one-point space and compact; assume in the forward direction that $X \ne \varnothing$ and fix $\bar{x} \in X$. [given, F2]

2.1 By [L1], it is enough to let $\mathcal H\subseteq\mathcal C(X)$ have the finite intersection property. By [F3], extend the filter generated by $\mathcal H$ to an ultrafilter $\mathcal F$ of subsets of $X$. [step 1.1, F3, L1]

3.1 For each $i\in I$, push $\mathcal F$ forward through the projection by putting$$ \mathcal G_i:=\{B\subseteq X_i:\pi_i^{-1}[B]\in\mathcal F\}. $$ Preimages preserve complements and finite intersections, so $\mathcal G_i$ is an ultrafilter on $X_i$. Its closed members have the finite intersection property. By compactness of the cofinite factor [L2], their intersection $$A_i:=\bigcap\{C\in\mathcal G_i:C\text{ is closed in }X_i\}$$ is nonempty. [step 2.1, F2, F3, L2]


4.1 By [F1], each $A_i$, being an intersection of closed sets, is either all of $X_i$ or finite. If it is finite, $\mathcal G_i$ contains a finite set: this is $X_i$ itself when $X_i$ is finite, while if $X_i$ is infinite and $A_i\ne X_i$, some closed member occurring in the intersection defining $A_i$ is proper and hence finite. An ultrafilter containing a nonempty finite set contains exactly one of its singleton subsets. If that singleton is $\{a_i\}$, then every member of $\mathcal G_i$ contains $a_i$, and $\{a_i\}$ is itself a closed member, so $A_i=\{a_i\}$. Define $x_i:=a_i$ in this case and $x_i:=\bar x_i$ when $A_i=X_i$. This defines $x=(x_i)_{i\in I}\in X$ without making a new choice. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F1, F3]

5.1 Fix $H\in\mathcal H$. By [L1], write $H=\bigcup_{q\in Q}\pi_q^{-1}[C_q]$ with $Q$ finite and each $C_q$ closed. Since $H\in\mathcal F$, finite primeness of the ultrafilter gives some $q\in Q$ with $\pi_q^{-1}[C_q]\in\mathcal F$. Thus $C_q\in\mathcal G_q$, so $x_q\in A_q\subseteq C_q$ by step 4.1. Hence $x\in H$. Therefore $x\in\bigcap\mathcal H$, and [L1] proves that $X$ is compact. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F3, L1]

6.1 Conversely, assume every product of cofinite spaces is compact and let $S$ be a set; the two-point space $2=\{0,1\}$ with the cofinite topology is the discrete two-point space, so $2^S$ is compact by hypothesis. Compactness of $2^S$ for every $S$ is the product form of the set-ultrafilter lemma, which is BPI by [F3] and [F4]. [F3, F4] ∎
