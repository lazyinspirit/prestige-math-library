## Statement

Over $\mathrm{ZF}$, BPI ([[def-boolean-prime-ideal-principle]]) is equivalent to
the compactness in the product topology ([[def-product-topology]],
[[def-compact-space]]) of every product of spaces each carrying the cofinite
topology ([[def-standard-topologies]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A family $\{(X_i, \mathcal{T}_i)\}_{i \in I}$ of spaces with $\mathcal{T}_i$ the cofinite topology on $X_i$, and its product $X$ with projections $\pi_i$.

[F1] The closed sets of the cofinite topology on a set are the set itself and its finite subsets; the cofinite space is $T_1$ ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-t0-and-t1-spaces]]).

[F2] A space is compact if and only if every family of closed sets with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection ([[thm-compact-iff-fip]], [[def-finite-intersection-property]]).

[F3] The set ultrafilter lemma is equivalent over ZF to BPI: every filter on a set extends to an ultrafilter, and an ultrafilter contains a set exactly when it contains no member of the complement ([[thm-bpi-equivalent-to-set-ultrafilter-lemma]], [[def-ultrafilter]], [[thm-ultrafilter-characterisation]], [[def-boolean-prime-ideal-principle]]).

[F4] Under BPI, every product of compact Hausdorff spaces is compact, and the two-point discrete space is compact Hausdorff ([[thm-bpi-equivalent-to-compact-hausdorff-tychonoff]], [[def-standard-topologies]]).

[L1] The projection of a closed set of the product need not be closed, but for the cofinite factor the intersection of a descending family of closed sets containing the whole set once is either the whole set or a finite set, and maximality of an ultrafilter turns a finite member into a singleton ([[def-subspace-topology-top]], [[def-topological-space]], [[def-natural-numbers]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In the empty product case, if some $X_i = \varnothing$ then $X = \varnothing$ and $X$ is compact, and if $I = \varnothing$ then $X$ is a one-point space and compact; assume in the forward direction that $X \ne \varnothing$ and fix $\bar{x} \in X$. [given, F2]

2.1 Let $\mathcal{H}$ be a family of closed subsets of $X$ with the finite intersection property; by [F3] extend the filter generated by $\mathcal{H}$ to an ultrafilter $\mathcal{F}$ of subsets of $X$. [step 1.1, F3]

3.1 For each $i \in I$ let $\mathcal{F}_i$ be the family of projections $\pi_i[F]$, $F \in \mathcal{F}$; it is a filter base on $X_i$, and by [F3] it is contained in an ultrafilter $\mathcal{G}_i$ of $X_i$, whose closed members have an intersection $A_i$ that is nonempty because $X_i$ is compact by [F2] and the closed members of an ultrafilter have the finite intersection property. [step 2.1, F2, F3]

4.1 By [F1] and [L1], each $A_i$ is either all of $X_i$ or finite; in the finite case maximality of $\mathcal{G}_i$ forces $A_i$ to be a singleton, so define $x_i$ to be its unique point when $A_i$ is finite and $x_i := \bar{x}_i$ otherwise; then the point $x = (x_i)_{i \in I}$ lies in every member of $\mathcal{H}$, because membership of a closed set is decided coordinatewise through the ultrafilter, so $\bigcap \mathcal{H} \ne \varnothing$ and $X$ is compact by [F2]. [step 3.1, F1, L1]

5.1 Conversely, assume every product of cofinite spaces is compact and let $S$ be a set; the two-point space $2 = \{0,1\}$ with the cofinite topology is the discrete two-point space, so $2^{S}$ is a product of cofinite compact spaces and is compact by hypothesis, and compactness of $2^{S}$ for every $S$ is the product form of the set ultrafilter lemma, which is BPI by [F3] and [F4]. [step 4.1, F3, F4] ∎
