**What is proved above.** Starting from a Noetherian commutative ring $R$, each
of the following is again Noetherian:

- every quotient $R/I$ and every localisation $S^{-1}R$
  ([[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]);
- the polynomial ring $R[x]$ ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]) and, by induction,
  $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ for each $n\in\mathbb N$
  ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]);
- every commutative $R$-algebra of finite type
  ([[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]);
- every module-finite commutative $R$-algebra, and every ring between $R$ and
  such an algebra
  ([[thm-module-finite-algebra-over-a-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]);
- the product $R\times S$ with $S$ Noetherian
  ([[cor-product-of-two-noetherian-rings-is-noetherian]]);
- a subring $R_0\subseteq R$ that admits an $R_0$-linear retraction $R\to R_0$
  fixing $R_0$ pointwise ([[lem-retract-of-a-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

**What fails, and why it is worth saying.** Every entry above supplies some map
or some finiteness relating the new ring to $R$. Two natural-looking weakenings
supply neither, and both fail.

*An arbitrary subring.* Being an additive subgroup closed under multiplication
gives no way to pull a generating set of an ideal of the subring back from the
larger ring, and the conclusion is false: a subring of a Noetherian ring need
not be Noetherian. The companion examples page of this pair works a witness
inside a polynomial ring in two variables, where the failing ideal is visibly
not finitely generated. The retraction hypothesis is exactly what is missing:
it is a map back, and with it the argument runs.

*Infinitely many indeterminates.* The polynomial-ring statement is proved by an induction on the number of
indeterminates, and that induction has no limit stage: it covers a finite list and
no more. The companion examples page of this pair carries the witness, a ring of
polynomials in countably many indeterminates in which the ideals generated by
initial segments of the variables form a strictly ascending chain. The same
remark applies to the product statement, which is proved for two factors and
extends by iteration to a finite list; a product indexed by an infinite set is
not among the constructions [[cor-product-of-two-noetherian-rings-is-noetherian]]
speaks about.

**A hypothesis that is not needed anywhere above.** No result above assumes that $R$ is an integral domain, that $R$ is nonzero, or that its ideals
are principal. The zero ring is Noetherian and is admitted throughout, and rings
with zero divisors are admitted in the Hilbert basis argument in particular,
which never multiplies two leading coefficients together.
