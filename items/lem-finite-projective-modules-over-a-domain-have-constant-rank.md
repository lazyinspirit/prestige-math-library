## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative integral domain with fraction field $K$, and let $P$ be a finitely generated projective $R$-module. For every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$, the localization $P_{\mathfrak p}=(R\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}P$ is free of finite rank, and that rank is independent of $\mathfrak p$ and equals $\dim_K(P\otimes_RK)$. No choice principle is required.

## Statement references

[The Stacks Project, More on Algebra, Section 10.78](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00NV)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative domain $R$, its fraction field $K$, a finitely generated projective module $P$, and a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$.

[F1] [[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]]: $R$ is commutative, nonzero, and has no zero divisors.

[F2] [[def-field-of-fractions]]: $K$ is the localization of $R$ at $R\setminus\{0\}$.

[F3] [[def-projective-module]] and [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]: $P$ is projective and generated by a finite list.

[F4] [[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]] and [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]: $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}$; a fraction is a unit exactly when its numerator is outside $\mathfrak p$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct local freeness and connectedness.

1.1 Put $A=R_{\mathfrak p}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak pA$, and $k=A/\mathfrak m$. Localization preserves the finite generating list of $P$, and localizing a split surjection onto $P$ shows $P_{\mathfrak p}$ is projective. Let $d=\dim_k(P_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak mP_{\mathfrak p})$, choose a basis of this finite-dimensional vector space, and lift its $d$ elements to $P_{\mathfrak p}$. The resulting map $\phi:A^d\to P_{\mathfrak p}$ has finitely generated cokernel $C$ with $C=\mathfrak mC$. To prove $C=0$ without choice, take finite generators $c_1,\ldots,c_q$ and write $c_i=\sum_j a_{ij}c_j$ with $a_{ij}\in\mathfrak m$. Then $(I-(a_{ij}))(c_j)=0$; its determinant is $1$ modulo $\mathfrak m$, hence is a unit in the local ring $A$, so the adjugate identity gives every $c_j=0$. Thus $\phi$ is surjective and splits because $P_{\mathfrak p}$ is projective. Its kernel $N$ is a finite direct summand of $A^d$, and reduction modulo $\mathfrak m$ gives $N/\mathfrak mN=0$ because the chosen basis makes $\phi\otimes_Ak$ an isomorphism. Applying the same finite-generator determinant argument to $N$ gives $N=0$. Hence $P_{\mathfrak p}\cong A^d$. [F1, F3, F4, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 The rank is locally constant on the prime spectrum. Indeed, $P$ is finitely presented: a finite generating set gives a surjection $R^n\twoheadrightarrow P$; projectivity splits it, and its kernel is a direct summand of $R^n$, hence finitely generated. At $\mathfrak p$, the isomorphism $P_{\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}^d$ and its inverse are represented by finitely many fractions on generators and relations of $P$; clearing their finitely many denominators and then the finitely many identity discrepancies gives some $s\notin\mathfrak p$ with $P_s\cong R_s^d$. Thus the rank equals $d$ at every prime not containing $s$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The zero ideal is prime by [F1], and lies in every nonempty basic open set $D(s)$ because such a set has $s\ne0$. The locally constant rank function has open fibers with open complements; any nonempty fiber and its complement would both contain the dense generic point $(0)$, which is impossible. Therefore the rank is constant, and its value at $(0)$ is $\dim_K(P\otimes_RK)$ by [F2]. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
