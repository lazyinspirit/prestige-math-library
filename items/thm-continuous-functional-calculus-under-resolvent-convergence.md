## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A_n,A$ be self-adjoint operators on $H$ and
suppose $A_n\to A$ in the strong resolvent sense. Then
$f(A_n)x\to f(A)x$ for every bounded continuous $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb C$ and
every $x\in H$. If $A_n\to A$ in the norm resolvent sense, then
$\|f(A_n)-f(A)\|\to0$ for every bounded continuous $f$ with
$\lim_{t\to+\infty}f(t)=\lim_{t\to-\infty}f(t)$. In both cases the conclusion
does not depend on the nonreal parameter used in the definition of convergence.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] For self-adjoint $S$ and bounded continuous $f$ the operator $f(S)$ is bounded with $\|f(S)\|\le\|f\|_\infty$, and for bounded Borel $h$ one has $\|h(S)x\|^2=\int|h|^2dE^S_x$; moreover $\|(1-\chi(S))x\|^2=\int(1-\chi)^2dE^S_x$ ([[thm-unbounded-borel-functional-calculus]], [[thm-spectral-theorem-for-unbounded-self-adjoint-operators]]).

[A2] The set of bounded continuous functions for which strong convergence $f(A_n)\to f(A)$ holds is closed under linear combinations, products, conjugation and uniform limits, and contains $1$ and the functions $r_m(\lambda)=(\lambda-z_m)^{-1}$ for every nonreal $z_m$, because resolvent convergence transfers to every nonreal parameter and to products by the uniform resolvent bound ([[def-norm-and-strong-resolvent-convergence]], [[lem-resolvent-star-algebra-is-dense-in-c-zero]]).

[A3] $\mathcal A=C_0(\mathbb R)$ for the algebra generated by $r(\lambda)=(\lambda-z)^{-1}$, and its closure under uniform limits together with the constants is the algebra of bounded continuous functions having a common limit at $\pm\infty$ ([[lem-resolvent-star-algebra-is-dense-in-c-zero]]).

[A4] Scalar dominated convergence applies to the finite measures $E^S_x$: if $0\le\chi_m\le1$ and $\chi_m\to0$ pointwise then $\int(1-\chi_m)^2dE^S_x\to0$ ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** Self-adjoint $A_n,A$ with $A_n\to A$ in the strong (norm) resolvent sense.

1.1 Strong convergence holds for every $f\in C_0(\mathbb R)$: by [A3] the algebra generated by $r$ is dense in $C_0(\mathbb R)$, [A2] shows that the good set is a uniformly closed $*$-algebra containing $r$, hence contains its uniform closure; the same argument with norms gives the norm case for $C_0(\mathbb R)$. [A2, A3]

2.1 Hence the norm case holds for every bounded continuous $f$ with a common limit $L$ at $\pm\infty$: write $f=L+g$ with $g\in C_0(\mathbb R)$, so $f(A_n)-f(A)=g(A_n)-g(A)$ and step 1.1 applies. [A3, step 1.1]

2.2 Strong case for bounded continuous $f$: choose continuous $\chi_m$ with $0\le\chi_m\le1$, compact support and $\chi_m=1$ on $[-m,m]$, and decompose $f(A_n)-f(A)=f(A_n)(1-\chi_m(A))+f(A_n)(\chi_m(A)-\chi_m(A_n))+(f\chi_m)(A_n)-(f\chi_m)(A)+f(A)(\chi_m(A)-1)$. All four terms are controlled: $\|f(A_n)\|,\|f(A)\|\le\|f\|_\infty$ by [A1]; $\|(1-\chi_m(A))x\|\to0$ by [A4]; $\|(\chi_m(A)-\chi_m(A_n))x\|\to0$ and $\|(f\chi_m)(A_n)-(f\chi_m)(A)x\|\to0$ for fixed $m$ by step 1.1, since $\chi_m$ and $f\chi_m$ lie in $C_0(\mathbb R)$. Hence $\|(f(A_n)-f(A))x\|$ is arbitrarily small: an $\varepsilon/3$ argument over $m$ and then $n$. [A1, A4, step 1.1]

3.1 Parameter independence is the consequence in [A2] and [A3]; the two convergence statements are step 2.1 (norm, common limits) and step 2.2 (strong, bounded continuous). ∎
