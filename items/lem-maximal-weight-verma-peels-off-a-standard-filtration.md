## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $X\in\mathcal O$ be
Verma-filtered ([[def-verma-flag-and-its-multiplicities]]) with a fixed finite
flag of length $m$, and let $v\ne0$ be a vector of weight $\nu$ in $X$ such
that $\nu$ is maximal among the weights of $X$, that is,
there is no weight $\gamma$ of $X$ with $\gamma-\nu\in Q^+\setminus\{0\}$. Then $v$ is a
highest-weight vector, the induced homomorphism
$\Delta(\nu)\to X$, $v_\nu\mapsto v$, is injective, and the cokernel
$X/\Delta(\nu)$ admits a finite Verma flag of length $m-1$: the given flag of
$X$ induces a flag of the cokernel after removing exactly one factor
$\Delta(\nu)$.

The hypothesis that $\nu$ is maximal in the support of $X$ is essential; it is
used below to force the first flag factor met by the image to be $\Delta(\nu)$,
and it cannot be dropped.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Verma-filtered object $X$ with a fixed flag $0=F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_m=X$ whose factors $F_i/F_{i-1}\cong\Delta(\mu_i)$ are Verma modules, and a nonzero vector $v\in X$ of weight $\nu$ maximal among the weights of $X$.

[F1] Flags are chains of subobjects with Verma quotients, and quotients and subobjects of objects of $\mathcal O$ lie in $\mathcal O$ ([[def-verma-flag-and-its-multiplicities]]).

[F2] The weights of $\Delta(\lambda)=M(\lambda)$ are exactly $\lambda-Q^+$ and $M(\lambda)_\lambda=\mathbb Cv_\lambda$; a $\mathfrak g$-homomorphism $M(\lambda)\to V$ into a $\mathfrak g$-module $V$ is determined by, and exists for, any $\mathfrak n^+$-fixed vector of weight $\lambda$ in $V$ ([[prop-weights-of-a-verma-module-lie-below-lambda]], [[thm-universal-property-of-verma-modules]], [[prop-verma-homomorphisms-are-singular-vectors]]).

[F3] Every nonzero homomorphism between Verma modules is injective, and every nonzero submodule of a Verma module contains a nonzero $\mathfrak n^+$-fixed vector ([[lem-a-nonzero-verma-homomorphism-is-injective]], [[lem-every-nonzero-verma-submodule-contains-a-singular-vector]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: let the image of the induced Verma map meet the flag and compare weights at the first factor it reaches.

1.1 Since $\nu$ is maximal among the weights of $X$, the vector $v$ is $\mathfrak n^+$-fixed: for $x\in\mathfrak n^+$ of weight $\alpha\in Q^+\setminus\{0\}$ the vector $xv$, if nonzero, would have weight $\nu+\alpha$, contradicting maximality. By [F2] there is a homomorphism $f:\Delta(\nu)\to X$ with $f(v_\nu)=v\ne0$; let $i$ be the smallest index with $f(\Delta(\nu))\subseteq F_i$, which exists because $F_m=X$. By minimality $f(\Delta(\nu))$ is not contained in $F_{i-1}$, so the composite $\bar f:\Delta(\nu)\xrightarrow{f}F_i\twoheadrightarrow F_i/F_{i-1}=\Delta(\mu_i)$ is nonzero. [F2, given]

2.1 Since $\bar f\ne0$, the weight $\nu$ of $v_\nu$ maps to a nonzero vector in $\Delta(\mu_i)$, so $\nu$ is a weight of $\Delta(\mu_i)$ and therefore $\nu\le\mu_i$ by [F2]. On the other hand $\mu_i$ is a weight of the subquotient $F_i/F_{i-1}$ of $X$, hence a weight of $X$, and the relation $\nu\le\mu_i$ and maximality of $\nu$ force $\mu_i=\nu$, so $\bar f$ is a nonzero endomorphism of the Verma module $\Delta(\nu)$. Since $\Delta(\nu)$ is generated by $v_\nu$ and $\bar f(v_\nu)\in\Delta(\nu)_\nu=\mathbb Cv_\nu$ is nonzero by [F2], the image of $\bar f$ contains $v_\nu$, so $\bar f$ is surjective, and it is injective by [F3]; hence $\bar f$ is an isomorphism. Now $f$ itself is injective: if $\ker f\ne0$, then by [F3] it contains a nonzero $\mathfrak n^+$-fixed vector of some weight $\eta$, which by [F2] provides a nonzero homomorphism $g:\Delta(\eta)\to\Delta(\nu)$ with image in $\ker f$; then $\bar fg=0$, while $\bar f$ is injective and $g\ne0$, so $\bar fg\ne0$, a contradiction. Hence $f:\Delta(\nu)\hookrightarrow X$ is injective. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Identify $\Delta(\nu)$ with its image $f(\Delta(\nu))\subseteq F_i$. Since $\bar f$ is an isomorphism onto $F_i/F_{i-1}$, one has $F_i=f(\Delta(\nu))+F_{i-1}$ and $f(\Delta(\nu))\cap F_{i-1}=0$, so $F_i/f(\Delta(\nu))\cong F_{i-1}$. In the quotient $X/f(\Delta(\nu))$ the images of the flag pieces form the chain $0\subseteq F_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq F_{i-1}=F_i/f(\Delta(\nu))\subseteq F_{i+1}/f(\Delta(\nu))\subseteq\cdots\subseteq X/f(\Delta(\nu))$, whose successive quotients are $\Delta(\mu_j)$ for $j\ne i$ and zero at the repeated step, and $F_j/f(\Delta(\nu))\big/F_{j-1}/f(\Delta(\nu))\cong F_j/F_{j-1}=\Delta(\mu_j)$ for $j>i$; hence $X/f(\Delta(\nu))$ has a Verma flag whose factors are exactly the $\Delta(\mu_j)$ with $j\ne i$, of length $m-1$, and the removed factor is $\Delta(\mu_i)=\Delta(\nu)$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 2.1 and 3.1 prove that $v$ is a highest-weight vector, that $\Delta(\nu)\to X$ is injective, and that the cokernel has a Verma flag induced from the given flag by deleting exactly one factor, of length $m-1$. [step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
