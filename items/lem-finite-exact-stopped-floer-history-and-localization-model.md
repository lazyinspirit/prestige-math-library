2.1 For completeness weak unique continuation needed for smooth solution/cokernel sections has a local direct proof. If $\partial_{\bar z}v=Cv$ with a bounded real-linear zero-order coefficient, replace that coefficient by the bounded complex-linear matrix $A(z)=(C(z)v(z))v(z)^*/|v(z)|^2$ where $v\ne0$, and zero where $v=0$. Then $Av=Cv$. The Cauchy transform $Tf(z)=\pi^{-1}\int f(w)/(z-w)\,dA(w)$ satisfies $\partial_{\bar z}Tf=f$ distributionally and $\|Tf\|_\infty\le4r\|f\|_\infty$ on a radius-$r$ disk. The identity follows by polar integration against a test function; continuity follows by deleting a small kernel disk and uniformly translating the remaining integrable kernel. Thus $P=I+T(AP)$ has a continuous solution with $\|P-I\|<1/2$ on a sufficiently small disk, by its geometric iteration, and $P$ is invertible. Its weak derivatives are locally $L^2$: for smooth compact approximants $f_j$ the identity $\|\partial Tf_j\|_2=\|\partial_{\bar z}Tf_j\|_2=\|f_j\|_2$ follows from the preceding integration by parts after a large-radius cutoff (the transform is $O(1/|z|)$ and its derivative $O(1/|z|^2)$); approximate bounded compact $f$ in both $L^2$ and $L^q$, $q>2$. The kernel is locally in $L^{q'}$, so the transforms converge uniformly on compact sets, and their derivatives converge in $L^2$. Consequently $P,P^{-1}$ are continuous $W^{1,2}$ matrices and $P^{-1}v$ is weakly holomorphic, by the product rule, first for smooth approximants and then in $W^{1,2}$. Mollifying this equation on smaller disks and using the mean-value identity shows its continuous representative is holomorphic. It is either zero or has a first nonzero Taylor coefficient and isolated zeros. Vanishing on an open set therefore propagates across each overlapping disk. A smooth variable principal complex structure is reduced to this one by $\Phi=(I-Ji)/2$, since $J\Phi=\Phi i$ and $\Phi$ is invertible near a point with $J=i$.
