## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $F$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field of characteristic $p$. Then
$$K_2^M(F)/pK_2^M(F)= \begin{cases} 0,&\text{if }F\text{ has no nontrivial }p\text{th root of unity},\\ \text{a cyclic group of order }p,&\text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$
In equal characteristic $\operatorname{char}F=p$ there is the further literal conclusion: for every $n\ge2$, multiplication by $p$ on $K_n^M(F)$ is bijective, with inverse induced on symbols by
$$\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}\longmapsto N_{F^{1/p}/F}\{a_1^{1/p},a_2^{1/p},a_3,\ldots,a_n\}.$$
The second clause does not assert mixed-characteristic $p$-torsion classification or unique divisibility at all primes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the data in the Statement.

[F1] Finite multiplicative representatives exist; in equal characteristic $F^{1/p}/F$ is explicitly a degree-$p$ local extension containing all the unique $p$th roots. [[lem-finite-residue-teichmuller-lifts-and-equal-characteristic-laurent-coordinates]]

[F2] In mixed characteristic the complete finite unit-filtration calculation gives generators of $U_1/U_1^p$ at noncritical depths and at most one critical class, present exactly when $\mu_p\subset F$, for all $p$ including $2$. [[lem-mixed-characteristic-principal-unit-p-power-filtration]]

[F3] Under the actual canonical AC/DC Artin hypotheses, the Steinberg Hilbert map $H_p$ is onto $\mu_p$ whenever the characteristic is not $p$ and $\mu_p\subset F$. [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F4] Milnor groups have the multilinear Steinberg presentation, products concatenate entries, and symbols anticommute with $\{a,a\}=\{a,-1\}$. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]] [[lem-milnor-symbol-anticommutativity-and-repeated-entry-identities]]

[F5] Canonical finite Milnor transfer under AC has the projection formula and its $K_1$ component is the determinant field norm, including purely inseparable extensions. [[thm-bass-tate-kato-norm-transfer-for-milnor-k-groups]]

[F6] A principal unit has a principal $d$th root when $p\nmid d$, by simple-root Hensel. [[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]

[A1] AC and DC are explicit for the transfer and canonical Hilbert branches. The mixed-characteristic upper bound below uses only the intrinsic algebraic unit calculations. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Put $h=q-1$. If $\theta\in\mu_h$ and $\epsilon\in U_1$, [F6] supplies $\eta\in U_1$ with $\eta^h=\epsilon$. Hence $\{\theta,\epsilon\}=\{\theta,\eta^h\}=\{\theta^h,\eta\}=0$ exactly. In a quotient modulo $p$, every $\theta\in\mu_h$ is itself a $p$th power since $p$ is prime to $h$. Writing each nonzero field element as a power of $\pi$ times its multiplicative residue lift times a principal unit, multilinearity and $\{\pi,\pi\}=\{\pi,-1\}$ show that $K_2^M(F)/p$ is generated by $\{\epsilon,\pi\}$ and $\{\epsilon_1,\epsilon_2\}$ with principal units. This includes $h=1$ and $p=2$, where $-1$ is allowed to have a principal-unit factor. [F1, F4, F6, given, algebra]

1.2 In characteristic $p$, take the explicit degree-$p$ extension $L=F^{1/p}$ from [F1]. For every $n\ge2$, define the displayed expression $f_n$ on entries using the transfer [F5]. It is multilinear since the unique-root map is multiplicative. Check every Steinberg pair in the tensor presentation [F4]. If both entries are among the first two, their roots sum to $1$ by Frobenius injectivity. If neither is, the original pair still sums to $1$. If exactly one is, write its root as $A$, so the other entry is $1-A^p=(1-A)^p$; their symbol is $\{A,(1-A)^p\}=p\{A,1-A\}=0$. Reordering covers any nonadjacent positions, and multiplying by the other entries preserves zero. Thus $f_n$ descends to a group homomorphism, not just a function on generating symbols. [A1, F1, F4, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 Assume characteristic zero and use $E,C$ from [F2]. Write $\epsilon_1=1+x$, $\epsilon_2=1+y$ with $i=v(x)>0$, $j=v(y)>0$, omitting a symbol with an entry $1$. Exact Steinberg expansions give $$\{1+x,1+y\}=\{1+x,-x(1+y)\} =-\{1+xy/(1+x),-x(1+y)\}.$$ Indeed the first equality adds $\{1+x,-x\}=0$, and the second uses $1+x+xy=(1+x)(1+xy/(1+x))$ paired with $-x(1+y)$. Put $x=\pi^i\gamma$. The last expression separates into $-i\{1+xy/(1+x),\pi\}$ and $-\{1+xy/(1+x),-\gamma(1+y)\}$. Split that last unit into its multiplicative lift and a principal unit; the lift term is zero by step 1.1. The first entry of the remaining pair is now a principal unit of exact depth $i+j>i$. If the other entry is $1$, stop; otherwise repeat. Its depth is at least one, so every repetition strictly increases the first depth. After finitely many repetitions it exceeds $C$, and [F2] makes that entry a $p$th power. Thus every unit-unit symbol is a finite sum of unit/uniformizer symbols modulo $p$. No infinite sum or continuity of the abstract Milnor group is used. [F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For the unit/uniformizer symbols use [F2]. At each noncritical generator $g=(1-\theta\pi^i)^i$, with $p\nmid i$, one has $$\{\pi,g\}=\{\pi^i,1-\theta\pi^i\} =\{\theta\pi^i,1-\theta\pi^i\}=0,$$ because the added $\theta$ symbol is zero by step 1.1 and the last pair is Steinberg. If no nontrivial $p$th root belongs to $F$, [F2] has no critical class, so the quotient is zero. Otherwise it is generated by one symbol $\{\omega,\pi\}$ and has order at most $p$. By [F3] there is a surjection to $\mu_p$ of order $p$, killed by $p$, so its order is exactly $p$. This proves the mixed-characteristic clause, including $p=2$. [A1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For $B\in L$, its field norm is $B^p$. If $B\in F$ this is the scalar determinant of degree $p$; otherwise $L=F(B)$ has minimal polynomial $T^p-B^p$, and the companion determinant gives the same result (also for $p=2$ in characteristic $2$). By projection in [F5], $$p f_n\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\} =N_{L/F}\{a_1,a_2^{1/p},a_3,\ldots,a_n\} =\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}.$$ For the last equality move all constant entries past the lone root entry, apply the left module formula, then reorder back; the two signs agree and cancel. Since a homomorphism commutes with integer multiplication, also $f_n p=p f_n=\mathrm{id}$. Thus $p$ is bijective in every degree $n\ge2$, and in particular the equal-characteristic $K_2/p$ quotient is zero. There is no nontrivial $p$th root of unity in characteristic $p$, so this completes both cases of the Statement. No mixed-characteristic torsion claim follows from the quotient calculation. [A1, F1, F4, F5, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
