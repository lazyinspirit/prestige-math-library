## Statement

Fix an integer $n \ge 1$. Let $\mathcal R_n$ be the Riemann surface of the
complete analytic function generated by the principal $n$th-root germ at $1$
over $\mathbb C^\times$. Define

$$\Lambda_n:\mathcal R_n\to\mathbb C^\times,\qquad \Lambda_n([\rho]_z):=\rho(z).$$

Then $\Lambda_n$ is a biholomorphism, and if $p_n:\mathcal R_n\to\mathbb C^\times$
is the germ projection, then

$$p_n([\rho]_z)=\Lambda_n([\rho]_z)^n.$$

Thus $\mathcal R_n$ is the standard $n$-sheeted covering
$w \mapsto w^n$ of $\mathbb C^\times$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The $n$th-root germ surface $\mathcal R_n$ and its projection $p_n$.

[L1] The principal root branch on the slit plane is a biholomorphism onto the
sector $V_n$, with inverse $w \mapsto w^n$
([[thm-slit-plane-root-branch-biholomorphism-to-a-sector]]).

[L2] The logarithm surface is biholomorphic to $\mathbb C$ over
$\mathbb C^\times$ via the exponential map
([[thm-riemann-surface-of-the-logarithm]]).

[L3] The germ projection on a complete analytic function is a local
biholomorphism ([[thm-germ-projection-is-a-local-biholomorphism]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For each $w \in \mathbb C^\times$, let $V_w:=w^n B(1,1/2)$ and define $\rho_w(u):=w\exp\!\left(\operatorname{Log}(u/w^n)/n\right)$ for $u\in V_w$. Because $B(1,1/2)\subseteq \mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$, the principal logarithm is defined there, and $\rho_w(u)^n=w^n\exp(\operatorname{Log}(u/w^n))=u$ while $\rho_w(w^n)=w$. So $\Psi(w):=[\rho_w]_{w^n}$ is an $n$th-root germ over $w^n$. [L1, algebra]

1.2 The germ $\Psi(w)$ lies on $\mathcal R_n$. By [L2], choose $\lambda \in \mathbb C$ with $e^\lambda=w$. Along the path $\gamma(t)=e^{nt\lambda}$ from $1$ to $w^n$, refine $[0,1]$ so that successive values of $e^{t\lambda}$ are close enough for the neighboring branches $\rho_{e^{t_j\lambda}}$ to agree on overlaps, exactly as in the logarithm-surface construction. This continues the principal root germ at $1$ to $\Psi(w)$. [L2, choose]

2.1 If $\Psi(w)=\Psi(v)$, then the two germs have the same base point and the same value there, so $w=v$. Hence $\Psi$ is injective. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Let $\xi=[\rho]_z \in \mathcal R_n$ and put $w:=\rho(z)\in\mathbb C^\times$. Shrink the representative domain to a connected disc $U \subseteq V_w$. On $U$ the quotient $h:=\rho/\rho_w$ is holomorphic and satisfies $h(u)^n=1$ for every $u\in U$. So $h(U)$ lies in the finite set of $n$th roots of unity. Since $U$ is connected and $h(z)=1$, the function $h$ is constantly $1$. Thus $\xi=\Psi(w)$, and $\Psi$ is surjective. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The inverse of $\Psi$ is $\Lambda_n$. In a chart $N(\rho,U)$ one has $\Lambda_n\circ\phi_{\rho,U}^{-1}(u)=\rho(u)$, so [L3] makes $\Lambda_n$ holomorphic. Near any fixed $w_0 \in \mathbb C^\times$, the chart $N(\rho_{w_0},V_{w_0})$ satisfies $\phi_{\rho_{w_0},V_{w_0}}(\Psi(w))=w^n$, which is holomorphic in $w$, so $\Psi$ is holomorphic. Therefore $\Lambda_n$ is a biholomorphism. [L3, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 For every $[\rho]_z \in \mathcal R_n$ one has $p_n([\rho]_z)=z=\rho(z)^n=\Lambda_n([\rho]_z)^n$, so under $\Lambda_n$ the projection is the power map $w \mapsto w^n$. Its fibre over a nonzero point consists of the $n$ distinct roots $w\zeta_n^k$ for $0 \le k < n$, so this is exactly the standard $n$-sheeted covering of $\mathbb C^\times$. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
