## Statement

Assume AC and Countable Choice. Let $K$ be a number field, let $\mathfrak m=(\mathfrak m_0,S_\infty)$ be a modulus, and let $\chi:\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\to\mathbb C^\times$ be a finite-order ray-class character. Put
$$R_{\mathfrak m}=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m_0}(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}})^\times\right)\times\{\pm1\}^{S_\infty}.$$
There is a unique character $\chi_f:(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m_0)^\times\to\mathbb C^\times$ and a unique parity vector $(\epsilon_\sigma)_{\sigma\in S_\infty}\in\{0,1\}^{S_\infty}$ such that, for every $\alpha\in K^\times$ prime to $\mathfrak m_0$,
$$\chi((\alpha))=\chi_f(\alpha\bmod\mathfrak m_0)\prod_{\sigma\in S_\infty}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma(\alpha))^{\epsilon_\sigma}.$$
If $\chi$ is primitive as a ray-class character, then $\chi_f$ does not factor through the unit group modulo any proper finite divisor of $\mathfrak m_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and Countable Choice; a number field $K$, a modulus $\mathfrak m$, and a character $\chi$ of its ray class group.

[F1] The residue-and-sign exact sequence is $\mathcal O_K^\times\xrightarrow{\rho}R_{\mathfrak m}\xrightarrow{\phi}\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\to\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)\to1$; $\ker\phi=\operatorname{im}\rho$, and $\phi(r)$ is the ray class of an ideal generated by any element with residue/sign data $r$. [[thm-ray-class-exact-sequence]]

[F2] The finite group $R_{\mathfrak m}$ is the direct product of the residue-unit groups at the finite prime powers of $\mathfrak m_0$ and one two-element sign group for each $\sigma\in S_\infty$. [[def-global-field-modulus]] [[thm-ray-class-exact-sequence]]

[F3] Unique prime factorization of $\mathfrak m_0$ and the Chinese remainder theorem identify the product of the finite residue rings with $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m_0$, and therefore identify their unit groups. [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]] [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]

[F4] For a divisor $\mathfrak f=(\mathfrak f_0,S'_\infty)$ of $\mathfrak m$, the natural map $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\to\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak f}(K)$ sends an ideal class to its smaller-modulus class; it is well-defined because the ray-principal subgroup for $\mathfrak m$ is contained in the one for $\mathfrak f$. [[def-ray-class-group]] [[def-global-field-modulus]] [[thm-weak-approximation-for-global-fields]]

[A1] AC and Countable Choice are the assumptions of the exact-sequence supplier used in [F1]. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and assumptions in the Statement.

1.1 Compose $\chi$ with $\phi$ from [F1] to obtain a character $\eta=\chi\circ\phi$ of $R_{\mathfrak m}$. Because $R_{\mathfrak m}$ is a direct product by [F2], $\eta$ is the product of its restrictions to the finite residue-unit factors and the sign factors. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 The product of the finite restrictions is a unique character $\chi_f$ of $(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak m_0)^\times$ by [F3]. A character of a sign factor $\{\pm1\}$ sends $-1$ to either $1$ or $-1$, so it is uniquely $x\mapsto x^{\epsilon_\sigma}$ for $\epsilon_\sigma\in\{0,1\}$. Thus $\eta(r)=\chi_f(r_f)\prod_{\sigma\in S_\infty}r_\sigma^{\epsilon_\sigma}$ for every $r\in R_{\mathfrak m}$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $\alpha$ is prime to $\mathfrak m_0$, its residue and signs define $r_\alpha\in R_{\mathfrak m}$, and [F1] gives $\phi(r_\alpha)=[(\alpha)]_{\mathfrak m}$. Hence $\chi((\alpha))=\eta(r_\alpha)=\chi_f(\alpha\bmod\mathfrak m_0)\prod_{\sigma\in S_\infty}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma(\alpha))^{\epsilon_\sigma}$, as asserted. Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the restrictions to each direct factor in step 1.1. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Suppose $\chi_f$ factors through a proper finite divisor $\mathfrak m_0'\mid\mathfrak m_0$. By [F4], the natural map to $\operatorname{Cl}_{(\mathfrak m_0',S_\infty)}(K)$ is well-defined and is surjective: for an ideal prime to $\mathfrak m_0'$, weak approximation chooses a scalar congruent to $1$ at $\mathfrak m_0'$, positive at $S_\infty$, and with valuations cancelling that ideal at the additional primes of $\mathfrak m_0$. Multiplication by that principal ideal produces a representative prime to $\mathfrak m_0$ without changing its smaller ray class. The valuation and sign conditions are finitely many open conditions as in the exact-sequence proof. If a ray class lies in its kernel, an ideal representative is principal, $(\alpha)$, with $\alpha\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak m_0'}$ and positive at every place of $S_\infty$, by the definition of the smaller ray class group. Then $\chi((\alpha))=\chi_f(\alpha\bmod\mathfrak m_0)=1$, since $\chi_f$ factors modulo $\mathfrak m_0'$ and all signs are positive. Thus $\chi$ is trivial on the kernel and factors through the proper modulus $(\mathfrak m_0',S_\infty)$, contradicting primitivity. [F1, F4, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
