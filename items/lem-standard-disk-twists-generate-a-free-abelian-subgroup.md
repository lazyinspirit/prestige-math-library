## Statement

The subgroup $\langle[\tau_0],\dots,[\tau_{m-1}]\rangle$ of
$G=\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(D,\partial D;\Delta)$ generated by the classes of
the standard nested twists is free abelian of rank $m$; equivalently, if
$\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}\tau_j^{e_j}$ is isotopic to the identity in $G$, then
$e_0=\dots=e_{m-1}=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The fixed nested picture of [[def-basic-arcs-admissible-curves-and-normal-form]], the twists $\tau_j$ about $l_j$ and their classes in $G$, and an exponent vector $(e_0,\dots,e_{m-1})\in\mathbb Z^m$.

[L1] The twists commute, $[\tau_i,\tau_j]=1$, and $\tau_j$ fixes every basic arc $b_k$ with $k\ne j$ up to isotopy ([[lem-standard-twists-fix-the-complementary-basic-arcs-and-commute]]).

[L2] In the standard picture, $l_j$ encloses the suffix $\{q_j,\ldots,q_m\}$ and a positive Dehn twist can be represented by the endpoint of an unmarked disk isotopy rotating the enclosed disk through one full turn, interpolated to the identity across its supporting annulus ([[def-basic-arcs-admissible-curves-and-normal-form]]). These endpoints fix every marked point.

[L3] A closed nonzero-vector path has an integer winding, obtained by normalizing it to the unit circle. This integer is invariant under homotopy and additive under concatenation; one positive turn has winding $1$ ([[cor-geometric-unit-circle-has-fundamental-group-z]]). Reversing the twist convention changes all computed signs together and does not affect the argument.


## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Pair windings detect the twist exponents.* For $0\le k<m$, follow the pair $(q_k,q_m)$ during an unmarked isotopy from the identity to $\tau_j$, and take the winding of their nonzero difference. In the standard circular model of [L2], if $j\le k$ both points are inside the rotating disk and their difference makes one full turn, giving $1$. If $j>k$, only $q_m$ moves, and its path lies in a disk not containing $q_k$, so the difference loop has winding zero. Concatenation gives pair winding $\sum_{j=0}^{k}e_j$ for the standard unmarked isotopy to $\prod_j\tau_j^{e_j}$; negative exponents reverse the corresponding paths. [L2, L3]

2.1 *A relation has zero pair windings.* Suppose that product is isotopic to the identity relative to the boundary and the marked set. Append that marked isotopy to the unmarked isotopy in step 1.1; the marks are constant on the appended part, so it does not change the pair windings. This produces a loop $f_t$ in the group of boundary-fixed homeomorphisms of the unmarked unit disk. It contracts by the explicit Alexander formula $A_s(f)(x)=s f(x/s)$ for $|x|\le s$, $A_s(f)(x)=x$ for $|x|\ge s$, and $A_0(f)=\mathrm{id}$. The two formulas agree on $|x|=s$ because $f$ fixes the boundary; each $A_s(f)$ is a homeomorphism, and continuity at $s=0$ follows from $|A_s(f)(x)-x|\le2s$. Applying this contraction to the loop $f_t$ gives a homotopy of each pair's nonzero difference loop to the constant loop. Thus every winding from step 1.1 is zero. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 *The triangular equations force all exponents to vanish.* For $k=0$ the zero-winding equation is $e_0=0$. For each $1\le k<m$, subtract the equation for $k-1$ from that for $k$ to obtain $e_k=0$. Hence the only relation among the commuting twists is the trivial exponent vector. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 *Conclusion.* Commutation [L1] defines a surjective homomorphism $\mathbb Z^m$ onto the generated subgroup, and step 3.1 proves it is injective. Thus that subgroup is free abelian of rank $m$, including $m=1$, when the single pair winding detects $\tau_0$. The proof uses explicit isotopies and finitely many windings and requires no choice axiom. [L1, step 3.1] ∎
