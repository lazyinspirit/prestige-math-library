## Statement

Let $n\ge2$, let $\Delta=\Delta_n=T_1T_2\cdots T_{n-1}$ be the half twist of
[[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]], where
$T_k=\sigma_k\sigma_{k-1}\cdots\sigma_1$ and $U_k=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k$, and
let $\tau$ be the index-reversal automorphism of $B_n^{+}$ induced by
$\sigma_j\mapsto\sigma_{n-j}$ (it is well defined because it permutes the
defining relations of [[def-positive-braid-monoid]]). Then:

**(a) The conjugation identity.** $\sigma_i\,\Delta\equiv^{+}\Delta\,\sigma_{n-i}$
for every $1\le i\le n-1$.

**(b) The mirror identity.** $\Delta\,\sigma_i\equiv^{+}\sigma_{n-i}\,\Delta$
for every $i$; more generally $\Delta\,w\equiv^{+}\tau(w)\,\Delta$ and
$w\,\Delta\equiv^{+}\Delta\,\tau(w)$ for every positive word $w$.

**(c) Index reversal fixes the half twist.** $\tau(\Delta)=\Delta$, and the
class of the reversed word $\Delta^{\mathrm{rev}}=U_{n-1}U_{n-2}\cdots U_1$ is
$\Delta$ as well; equivalently $\rho(\Delta)=\Delta$ for the reversal
anti-automorphism $\rho$ of
[[lem-the-positive-braid-monoid-is-left-and-right-cancellative]].

**(d) The square of the half twist is central.** $w\,\Delta^2\equiv^{+}\Delta^2\,w$
for every positive word $w$; in particular $\sigma_i\Delta^2\equiv^{+}\Delta^2\sigma_i$
for every $i$.

**(e) Sliding a generator through a triangular block.**
$\sigma_j\,T_k\equiv^{+}T_k\,\sigma_{j+1}$ whenever $1\le j\le k-1$. The
restriction $j\le k-1$ is essential: when $k\le n-2$, the analogous words
$\sigma_kT_k$ and $T_k\sigma_{k+1}$ have different supports, hence are
distinct in $B_n^{+}$.

For $n=0,1$ the alphabet is empty, $\Delta=1$ and all statements are trivial.
No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n\ge2$, the monoid $B_n^{+}$ with its generators $\sigma_i$, the blocks $T_k=\sigma_k\cdots\sigma_1$ and $U_k=\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_k$, the half twist $\Delta=T_1\cdots T_{n-1}$ of length $N=n(n-1)/2$, and the index-reversal map $\tau$.

[F1] $B_n^{+}=\Sigma_n^{*}/\!\equiv^{+}$ with $[uv]=[u][v]$, generated by the braid pairs $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ ($1\le i\le n-2$) and the far-commutation pairs $\sigma_i\sigma_j=\sigma_j\sigma_i$ ($|i-j|\ge2$); $\ell([w])=|w|$ with $\ell(x)=0$ only for $x=1$. Every defining pair preserves the set of generators occurring in a word (its support), so an equivalence derivation beginning in a sub-alphabet stays in that sub-alphabet ([[def-positive-braid-monoid]], [[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]]).

[F2] **Cancellation** ([[lem-the-positive-braid-monoid-is-left-and-right-cancellative]]): $xa=xb\Rightarrow a=b$ and $ax=bx\Rightarrow a=b$ in $B_n^{+}$; reversal $\rho$ is an anti-automorphism with $\rho(\sigma_j)=\sigma_j$, so $\rho[(T_1\cdots T_{n-1})]=[U_{n-1}\cdots U_1]$.

[F3] The blocks: $T_1=\sigma_1$, $\Delta=T_1\cdots T_{n-1}$, and $\Delta_n=\Delta_{n-1}T_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$, where $\Delta_{n-1}=T_1\cdots T_{n-2}$ is the half twist of the sub-alphabet $\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-2}\}$ ([[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The sliding lemma (e).** Let $1\le j\le k-1$ and consider $\sigma_jT_k=\sigma_j\sigma_k\sigma_{k-1}\cdots\sigma_1$. First move the leading $\sigma_j$ rightwards across $\sigma_k,\sigma_{k-1},\dots,\sigma_{j+2}$: each of these letters is at distance $\ge2$ from $j$, so far commutation [F1] applies, giving $\sigma_k\cdots\sigma_{j+2}\,\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j\,\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_1$ (the indices $k,\dots,j+2$ are present by $j\le k-1$, and the block $\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j$ is contiguous because the remaining letters of $T_k$ after $\sigma_{j+2}$ are $\sigma_{j+1},\sigma_j$). Second, apply the three-term relation to that contiguous block: $\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j\equiv^{+}\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}$, giving $\sigma_k\cdots\sigma_{j+2}\,\sigma_{j+1}\,\sigma_j\sigma_{j+1}\,\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_1$. Third, move the rightmost $\sigma_{j+1}$ rightwards across $\sigma_{j-1},\sigma_{j-2},\dots,\sigma_1$: each is at distance $\ge2$ from $j+1$, so far commutation gives $\sigma_k\cdots\sigma_{j+2}\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_j\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_1\sigma_{j+1}=T_k\sigma_{j+1}$. Hence $\sigma_jT_k\equiv^{+}T_k\sigma_{j+1}$. For $j=k\le n-2$, the two words $\sigma_kT_k$ and $T_k\sigma_{k+1}$ have different supports: the first uses exactly $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_k$, while the second also uses $\sigma_{k+1}$. By [F1] they are not equivalent; when $k=n-1$, $\sigma_{k+1}$ is not a generator, so the comparison is not stated. [F1]

1.2 **Word reversal fixes the half twist (c), first half.** For $m\ge2$ let $R_m:=U_{m-1}U_{m-2}\cdots U_1$, the reversal of the word $T_1T_2\cdots T_{m-1}$ that defines $\Delta_m$. We prove by induction on $m$ that
$$R_m=\Delta_m,$$
together with the auxiliary identity $C(m):\ U_{m-1}\Delta_{m-1}=\Delta_m$. All these identities take place in the sub-monoid generated by $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{m-1}$ inside $B_n^{+}$; by [F1] every class containing a word over that sub-alphabet has a representative over it, so the computation may be performed in the sub-alphabet and read in $B_n^{+}$. For $m=2$ one has $R_2=U_1=\sigma_1=T_1=\Delta_2$, and $C(2)$ reads $U_1\Delta_1=\sigma_1\cdot1=\sigma_1=\Delta_2$, both by [F3]. For the step $m\ge3$ assume $R_{m-1}=\Delta_{m-1}$ and $C(m-1)$. By the definitions of the blocks in the Given, $U_{m-1}=U_{m-2}\sigma_{m-1}$, $T_{m-1}=\sigma_{m-1}T_{m-2}$, while $\Delta_{m-1}=\Delta_{m-2}T_{m-2}$ is the recursion of [F3], so
$$U_{m-1}\Delta_{m-1}=U_{m-2}\,\sigma_{m-1}\Delta_{m-2}\,T_{m-2}=U_{m-2}\,\Delta_{m-2}\,\sigma_{m-1}T_{m-2}=\Delta_{m-1}\,\sigma_{m-1}T_{m-2}=\Delta_{m-1}T_{m-1}=\Delta_m .$$
The middle equality holds because every letter of the word $\Delta_{m-2}$ lies in $\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{m-3}\}$ and hence is at distance $\ge2$ from $\sigma_{m-1}$, so finitely many far-commutation relations of [F1] interchange $\sigma_{m-1}$ with $\Delta_{m-2}$; the fourth equality is $C(m-1)$; the last is the recursion of [F3]. This proves $C(m)$, and then $R_m=U_{m-1}R_{m-1}=U_{m-1}\Delta_{m-1}=\Delta_m$ by the induction hypothesis and $C(m)$. Since $R_m$ is the reversed word of $\Delta_m$ and $\rho$ is the reversal anti-automorphism of [F2], this is the assertion $\rho(\Delta_m)=\Delta_m$ of (c). [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 **Interior case of a simultaneous induction for (a).** The interior and boundary cases below together prove the full assertion (a) by induction on $m$: the induction hypothesis at level $m-1$ includes its boundary case, the interior argument proves every lower index at level $m$, and the boundary argument then proves the final index. For every $m\ge2$ and every $1\le i\le m-2$, we prove $\sigma_i\Delta_m\equiv^{+}\Delta_m\sigma_{m-i}$. For $m=2$ the range is empty, so the assertion is vacuous. Assume the full assertion (a), including its boundary case, has been proved at level $m-1$ by the preceding induction stage; by [F3] write $\Delta_m=\Delta_{m-1}T_{m-1}$. The induction hypothesis applied to the sub-alphabet $\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{m-2}\}$ states $\sigma_i\Delta_{m-1}\equiv^{+}\Delta_{m-1}\sigma_{m-1-i}$ for every $1\le i\le m-2$, and every word in that equivalence is a word over $\{\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{m-2}\}$; since the displayed derivation is valid in that sub-alphabet (support is preserved by [F1]), the same equivalence holds in $B_n^{+}$. Multiplying by $T_{m-1}$ on the right and using multiplicativity gives $\sigma_i\Delta_{m-1}T_{m-1}\equiv^{+}\Delta_{m-1}\sigma_{m-1-i}T_{m-1}$. Now apply the sliding lemma 1.1 with $j=m-1-i$ and $k=m-1$: the constraint $1\le j\le k-1$ is exactly $1\le m-1-i\le m-2$, that is $1\le i\le m-2$, which holds; we obtain $\sigma_{m-1-i}T_{m-1}\equiv^{+}T_{m-1}\sigma_{m-i}$. Hence $\sigma_i\Delta_m\equiv^{+}\Delta_{m-1}T_{m-1}\sigma_{m-i}=\Delta_m\sigma_{m-i}$, as required. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 **The remaining case $i=m-1$ of (a).** For $m=2$ the unique index is $i=1=m-1$, and $\sigma_1\Delta_2\equiv^{+}\sigma_1\sigma_1\equiv^{+}\Delta_2\sigma_1$ holds because both sides are the very same word. For $m\ge3$, apply the anti-automorphism $\rho$ of [F2] to the case $i=1$ of step 2.1, which is in range because $1\le m-2$; recall $\rho(\sigma_j)=\sigma_j$ and $\rho(\Delta_m)=\Delta_m$ by step 1.2, and $\rho(xy)=\rho(y)\rho(x)$. The equivalence $\sigma_1\Delta_m\equiv^{+}\Delta_m\sigma_{m-1}$ gives $\Delta_m\sigma_1=\rho(\Delta_m)\rho(\sigma_1)=\rho(\sigma_1\Delta_m)\equiv^{+}\rho(\Delta_m\sigma_{m-1})=\rho(\sigma_{m-1})\rho(\Delta_m)=\sigma_{m-1}\Delta_m$, and since $m-(m-1)=1$ this is case $i=m-1$ of (a). Together with step 2.1 this proves (a) for every $i\in\{1,\dots,m-1\}$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 **The mirror identity (b).** Applying (a) with the index $m-i$ (which lies in $\{1,\dots,m-1\}$) gives $\sigma_{m-i}\Delta_m\equiv^{+}\Delta_m\sigma_{m-(m-i)}=\Delta_m\sigma_i$, which is the first assertion. For the second, argue by induction on the length of a positive word $w$ ([[thm-induction-principle]]): $\varepsilon$ gives $\Delta\varepsilon=\varepsilon\Delta$; if $w=w'\sigma_i$ and $\Delta w'\equiv^{+}\tau(w')\Delta$, then $\Delta w=\Delta w'\sigma_i\equiv^{+}\tau(w')\Delta\sigma_i\equiv^{+}\tau(w')\sigma_{m-i}\Delta=\tau(w)\Delta$, using the first assertion. Applying the same argument to $\tau(w)$ and using $\tau\circ\tau=\mathrm{id}$ gives $\Delta\tau(w)\equiv^{+}w\Delta$, that is, $w\Delta\equiv^{+}\Delta\tau(w)$. [F1, step 3.1]

5.1 **$\tau$ fixes the half twist (c), second half.** Put $w:=\Delta_m$ in the identity $\Delta_m w\equiv^{+}\tau(w)\Delta_m$ of step 4.1: $\Delta_m\Delta_m\equiv^{+}\tau(\Delta_m)\Delta_m$. Right cancellation [F2] gives $\tau(\Delta_m)=\Delta_m$. Together with the equality $\rho(\Delta_m)=\Delta_m$ of step 1.2 this establishes (c). [F2, step 1.2, step 4.1]

5.2 **Centrality of $\Delta^2$ (d).** For a positive word $w$, by step 4.1 applied to $w$ and to $\tau(w)$, and using $\tau\circ\tau=\mathrm{id}$, we get $\Delta^2w=\Delta(\Delta w)\equiv^{+}\Delta(\tau(w)\Delta)=(\Delta\tau(w))\Delta\equiv^{+}(w\Delta)\Delta=w\Delta^2$, which is (d). For $w=\sigma_i$ this is the stated generator case. [F1, step 4.1]

6.1 **Assembly.** Part (a) is steps 2.1 and 3.1, part (b) is step 4.1, part (c) is steps 1.2 and 5.1, part (d) is step 5.2 and part (e) is step 1.1. Every induction above is over $\mathbb N$ (length of a word, or the level $m$), all computations are finite, and no choice principle is used; the case $n\le1$ of the statement is the trivial empty-alphabet case. ∎ [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2]

## Remarks

- **No source fact is assumed.** GM Section 4, printed p. 27, reports all of (a) and the reversed-word equality as Garside's "elementary arguments" without reproducing the slides; here every move is reconstructed. The reversed-word equality $\rho(\Delta)=\Delta$ is proved in step 1.2 by the auxiliary recursion $U_{m-1}\Delta_{m-1}=\Delta_m$, the case $i\le m-2$ of (a) is derived in step 2.1 from the sliding lemma 1.1, the case $i=m-1$ is obtained in step 3.1 by transporting the case $i=1$ through the reversal anti-automorphism $\rho$, and the mirror identity, $\tau(\Delta)=\Delta$ and the centrality of $\Delta^2$ are proved in steps 4.1--5.2 from (a) and cancellation.
- Note how the two triangular blocks are used: the *recursion* $\Delta_n=\Delta_{n-1}T_{n-1}$ couples a letter $\sigma_i$ with the index $n-i$, and the sliding lemma 1.1 realises exactly that shift inside a block. Reversal $\rho$ is not the same operation as $\tau$: $\rho$ fixes each letter but reverses products, $\tau$ permutes letters without reversing products.
- Conventions: all identities are in the monoid $B_n^{+}$, so no inverse of $\Delta$ and no conjugation in a group are used; the phrase "conjugation by $\Delta$" in the title refers to the two-sided sliding $\sigma_i\Delta=\Delta\sigma_{n-i}$, which is a conjugation identity only after the Ore embedding of [[thm-the-ore-fraction-group-of-positive-braids-is-the-artin-braid-group]]. No choice principle is used.
