## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), as used by the
associated-module-sheaf existence theorem below. Let $X$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-coherent
$\mathcal O_X$-module of finite type
([[def-finite-type-finite-presentation-module-sheaf]]). Let $k\ge0$ be an
integer. The **$k$-th Fitting ideal sheaf** $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)$
is defined affine locally as follows.

Let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be affine and let
$\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ for a finitely generated $A$-module $M$; choose
a finite generating set and the resulting presentation
$$A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\ \varphi\ }A^n\xrightarrow{\ \ }M\longrightarrow0$$
with $n$ finite and $J$ an arbitrary index set
([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]). Let $I_{n-k}(\varphi)$ be the
ideal of $A$ generated by the $(n-k)\times(n-k)$ minors of a matrix of
$\varphi$, with the conventions $I_r(\varphi)=A$ for $r\le0$ and
$I_r(\varphi)=0$ once $r$ exceeds the number of rows or the number of columns
([[lem-fitting-ideals-presentation-independent]]). Then
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U\;:=\;\widetilde{I_{n-k}(\varphi)},$$
the associated sheaf of that ideal, viewed as a subsheaf of
$\mathcal O_U=\widetilde A$
([[def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]]). By
[[lem-fitting-ideals-presentation-independent]] the ideal $I_{n-k}(\varphi)$
depends only on $M$ and $k$, not on the chosen generating set, presentation or
integer $n$, and it commutes with localisation; hence the local data are
canonical and compatible on overlaps $D(f)$, where they are given by
$I_{n-k}(\varphi)\cdot A_f$
([[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]],
[[thm-associated-module-sheaf-exists]]). Gluing over an affine cover of $X$
produces an ideal sheaf
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)\subseteq\mathcal O_X.$$
It is quasi-coherent as an ideal sheaf
([[def-quasi-coherent-ideal-sheaf]]), since on each affine chart it is the ideal
sheaf associated to the $A$-module $I_{n-k}(\varphi)$.
The associated-sheaf existence and identification on distinguished opens use
the stated Axiom of Choice through [[thm-associated-module-sheaf-exists]];
presentation independence and localisation of minors use only the cited
algebraic supplier.

*Boundary values.* The convention $I_r=A$ for $r\le0$ records the empty
product, a $0\times0$ minor being $1$. For $k=-1$ the convention $I_r=0$ for
$r$ larger than the number of rows gives $\operatorname{Fitt}_{-1}(\mathcal F)=0$.
For $\mathcal F=0$ the presentation with $n=0$ gives
$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(0)=\mathcal O_X$ for every $k\ge0$. In general
$\operatorname{Fitt}_0$ is generated by the $n\times n$ minors of a relation
matrix, so it need not be $\mathcal O_X$: if $\mathcal F$ is free of rank $r$
on $U$, presented there with $n=r$ generators and no relations, then
$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U=0$ for $k<r$ and
$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U=\mathcal O_U$ for $k\ge r$; in
particular $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal O_U)=0$.
For $0\le k<n$, the ideal $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U$ is zero
precisely when all $(n-k)\times(n-k)$ minors of the chosen relation matrix
vanish. For $k\ge n$ it is $\mathcal O_U$ by convention, so it is zero only
when $A$ is the zero ring.

*Basic properties.* $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)$ is defined for every
$k\ge0$ and depends only on $\mathcal F$; it is compatible with base change and
with restriction to open subschemes, and for $k=0$ it cuts out the support in
the finite type case (the rank-locus theorem on this page). The ideals are
nested in the sense $I_r\subseteq I_s$ for $s\le r$, since a larger minor is an
$A$-linear combination of smaller ones by Laplace expansion; hence
$\operatorname{Fitt}_{k-1}\subseteq\operatorname{Fitt}_k$ for all $k\ge1$, so
the Fitting ideals increase with $k$ and reach
$\operatorname{Fitt}_n(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$ on a chart with $n$
generators.
