## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$. Then $g$ is
$\sigma(f)$-measurable if and only if there is a Borel measurable function
$h : \mathbb{R} \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$ such that

$$g = h \circ f.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ and functions $f : X \to \mathbb{R}$ and $g : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$.

[L1] The sigma-algebra generated by $f$ is $\sigma(f)=\{f^{-1}(B):B \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})\}$. ([[def-sigma-algebra-generated-by-a-function]])

[L2] Threshold measurability characterizes $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$-valued measurability. ([[thm-threshold-characterisations-of-real-and-extended-real-measurability]])

[L3] $\mathbb Q$ is countable ([[thm-rationals-countable]]), so [[def-countable-choice]] selects one Borel lift for each rational threshold in step 1.2.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $g=h\circ f$ for Borel $h$, then for each Borel $C\subseteq\overline{\mathbb R}$, $g^{-1}(C)=f^{-1}(h^{-1}(C))\in\sigma(f)$ by [L1]. [L1, given]

1.2 Conversely suppose $g$ is $\sigma(f)$-measurable. For every rational $q$, [L1] and [L2] say that the family of Borel $A\subseteq\mathbb R$ with $f^{-1}(A)=\{g\le q\}$ is nonempty. By [L3], $\mathbb Q$ is countable; apply the stated $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ to choose one $A_q$ for every $q$. Define $B_q=\bigcap_{r\in\mathbb Q,\ r>q}A_r$. These sets are Borel and increasing in $q$, and $f^{-1}(B_q)=\bigcap_{r>q}\{g\le r\}=\{g\le q\}$, including when $g$ takes an infinite value. [L1, L2, L3, choose, algebra]

2.1 The family also satisfies $B_q=\bigcap_{r\in\mathbb Q,\ r>q}B_r$. Indeed the right side expands to the intersection of all $A_s$ with rational $s>r>q$; every rational $s>q$ has a rational $r$ strictly between $q$ and $s$, and every $s>r>q$ also has $s>q$. Thus the two collections of $A_s$ have the same intersection. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 For $y\in\mathbb R$ put $h(y)=\inf\{q\in\mathbb Q:y\in B_q\}$ in $\overline{\mathbb R}$, with empty infimum $+\infty$. For rational $q$, monotonicity and step 2.1 give $\{h\le q\}=\bigcap_{r\in\mathbb Q,\ r>q}B_r=B_q$: if the infimum is at most $q$, some index below each $r>q$ belongs to the defining set, and conversely membership in every $B_r$ forces the infimum at most $q$. The threshold criterion [L2] therefore makes $h$ Borel measurable. [L2, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 For every $x\in X$ and rational $q$, steps 1.2 and 3.1 give $h(f(x))\le q$ exactly when $g(x)\le q$. Rational thresholds distinguish all points of $\overline{\mathbb R}$, including both infinities, hence $h(f(x))=g(x)$. Together with step 1.1 this proves the equivalence. [L2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1] ∎
