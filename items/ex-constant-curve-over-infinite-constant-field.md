## Example

Let $k=\mathbb C$, $f(x)=x^5-x$, and let $a_1,\ldots,a_5$ be its five
distinct roots. Glue
$$U=\operatorname{Spec}k[x,y]/(y^2-f(x))$$
to
$$V=\operatorname{Spec}k[u,w]/(w^2-u\prod_{i=1}^5(1-a_i u))$$
on $x,u\ne0$ by $u=x^{-1}$ and $w=u^3y$, obtaining $C_0$. Put
$K=\mathbb C(t)$ and $X=C_0\times_{\operatorname{Spec}k}\operatorname{Spec}K$.
Then $(K,k)$ is a function field with exact constant field $k$, $X$ is a
constant smooth proper geometrically integral curve of genus two over $K$,
and $X(K)$ is infinite, where $X(K)=\operatorname{Hom}_K(\operatorname{Spec}K,X)$:
its points include the images of all affine
$k$-points $(a,y)$ with $a\notin\{a_1,\ldots,a_5\}$ and $y^2=f(a)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $k=\mathbb C$, the curve $C_0$ and field $K=\mathbb C(t)$
specified in the Example, and $X=C_{0,K}$.

[A1] AC: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The displayed squarefree-quintic double cover is smooth, proper,
geometrically integral and of genus two. It remains so after field extension.
[[lem-squarefree-quintic-double-cover-is-genus-two]]

[F2] The function field $\mathbb C(t)$ is finitely generated of
transcendence degree one over $\mathbb C$; since $\mathbb C$ is algebraically
closed, it is the exact constant field.
[[def-function-field-with-exact-constant-field]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited through the double-cover supplier [F1]. The proof's set of
points is shown infinite by a surjection onto an infinite set and uses no
choice function.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; verify the field constants and exhibit infinitely
many points by their distinct first coordinates.

1.1 The five roots of $f(x)=x^5-x$ are distinct in characteristic zero. By [F1], $C_0$ is smooth, proper and geometrically integral of genus two. The field $K=\mathbb C(t)$ is generated by one transcendental element, so it has transcendence degree one; every element of $K$ algebraic over the algebraically closed field $\mathbb C$ is in $\mathbb C$. Thus $(K,k)$ has exact constant field $k$, and the product defining $X$ makes it constant. [A1, F1, F2, given]

2.1 For each $a\in\mathbb C\setminus\{a_1,\ldots,a_5\}$ the value $f(a)$ is nonzero and has a square root in $\mathbb C$. Hence the set of affine $k$-points displayed in the Example maps onto the infinite set $\mathbb C\setminus\{a_1,\ldots,a_5\}$ by its first coordinate, so it is infinite. Base change maps these distinct points to distinct elements of $X(K)$. [F1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
