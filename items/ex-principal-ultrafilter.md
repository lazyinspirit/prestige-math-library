## Example

Let $x\in X$. The family

$$\mathcal U_x:=\{A\subseteq X:x\in A\}$$

is the **principal ultrafilter at $x$**. It is the filter generated by the
singleton $\{x\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A set $X$, a point $x\in X$, and the family $\mathcal U_x$ displayed above.

[L1] If $\emptyset\neq C\subseteq X$, then $\mathcal F_C=\{A\subseteq X:C\subseteq A\}$ is the filter generated by $\{C\}$, and it is an ultrafilter exactly when $C$ is a singleton ([[ex-principal-filter-generated-by-a-set]]).

[F1] A principal ultrafilter on $X$ is an ultrafilter of the form $\{A\subseteq X:y\in A\}$ for some $y\in X$ ([[def-ultrafilter]]).

[L2] A filter is an ultrafilter exactly when it contains exactly one of $A$ and $X\setminus A$ for every $A\subseteq X$ ([[thm-ultrafilter-characterisation]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The set $\{x\}$ is a nonempty singleton, and $\{x\}\subseteq A$ holds exactly when $x\in A$. [given]

1.2 For every $A\subseteq X$, exactly one of $x\in A$ and $x\in X\setminus A$ holds. [given]

2.1 Applying [L1] to $C=\{x\}$ shows that $\mathcal U_x$ is the filter generated by $\{\{x\}\}$ and is an ultrafilter. [step 1.1, L1]

3.1 Equivalently, step 1.2 verifies the complementary-pair condition of [L2] directly. [step 1.2, step 2.1, L2]

4.1 By [F1], this ultrafilter is principal, and the displayed formula identifies it as the principal ultrafilter at $x$. [step 2.1, F1] ∎
