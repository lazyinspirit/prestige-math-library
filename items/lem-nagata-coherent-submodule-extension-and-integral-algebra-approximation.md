## Statement

Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme, $U\subseteq X$ open, and $\mathcal F$ a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module. Every coherent submodule $\mathcal E_U\subseteq\mathcal F|_U$ extends to a coherent submodule $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal F$ with exact restriction $\mathcal E|_U=\mathcal E_U$.

Consequently $\mathcal F$ is the filtered union of all its coherent submodules. If $\mathcal A$ is a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-algebra integral over $\mathcal O_X$, it is the filtered union of its quasi-coherent finite $\mathcal O_X$-subalgebras; these are coherent as modules. In particular any finite set of local sections on an affine open is contained in the restriction of such a finite subalgebra. No separatedness, reducedness, density or choice hypothesis is required, and empty opens and zero modules and algebras are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian scheme, open, quasi-coherent sheaf and coherent submodule; for the algebra consequence the actual integral algebra structure.

[F1] On an affine open, a given quasi-coherent module sheaf is described by a module and its localizations. Coherence means finite local generation with finite relation kernels. [[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]] [[def-coherent-module-scheme]]

[F2] Exact localization identifies a localized quotient with the quotient of the localized modules. An element which becomes zero after inverting $f$ is killed by some power of $f$. [[cor-localisation-commutes-with-kernels-images-and-cokernels]]

[F3] Finitely many integral algebra elements generate a module-finite subalgebra. [[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]

## Proof

1.1 First solve extension on one affine $W=\operatorname{Spec}R$. Put $O=U\cap W$ and $M=\mathcal F(W)$, so $\mathcal F|_W$ has localizations $M_f$ by [F1]. The Noetherian open $O$ has a finite cover by principal opens $D(f_i)\subseteq W$ on which $\mathcal E_U$ has finite module generators $e_{i\ell}$. Express each generator in $M_{f_i}$ as a fraction $m_{i\ell}/f_i^{a_{i\ell}}$ with $m_{i\ell}\in M$. On an overlap $D(f_if_j)$ this numerator belongs to the restriction of $\mathcal E_U$. Hence its class in $M_{f_j}/\mathcal E_U(D(f_j))$ becomes zero after inverting $f_i$. By [F2], a power of $f_i$ kills that class. For each finite generator choose an exponent $b_{i\ell}$ at least as large as the exponents required for all the finitely many $j$. Then $n_{i\ell}=f_i^{b_{i\ell}}m_{i\ell}$ belongs to $\mathcal E_U(D(f_j))$ for every $j$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $N\subseteq M$ be the submodule generated by these finitely many $n_{i\ell}$. The finite-type quasi-coherent subsheaf of $\mathcal F|_W$ defined by $N$ restricts into $\mathcal E_U$ on every $D(f_j)$ by step 1.1. On $D(f_i)$ its own generators satisfy $n_{i\ell}=f_i^{a_{i\ell}+b_{i\ell}}e_{i\ell}$, with $f_i$ invertible there, so it contains all generators of $\mathcal E_U(D(f_i))$. Thus its restriction is exactly $\mathcal E_U|_O$. The submodule is coherent directly: over the Noetherian ring $R$, $N$ is finite, and kernels of maps from finite free modules into $N$ are finite submodules of finite free modules; exact localization gives the same relation kernels on principal opens. The latter finiteness follows by finite induction on the free rank, projecting a submodule onto the last coordinate ideal and lifting a finite generating family of that ideal. This verifies coherence from its defining finite-kernel condition rather than invoking a choice-qualified associated-sheaf existence theorem. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a finite affine cover $W_1,\ldots,W_r$ of $X$. Starting with the supplied submodule on $U$, apply steps 1.1–2.1 to its restriction on $W_i\cap(U\cup W_1\cup\cdots\cup W_{i-1})$. Each overlap is a Noetherian open and therefore has the finite principal covers used there. The resulting coherent submodule on $W_i$ agrees EXACTLY with the existing one on the overlap, as subsheaves of the same $\mathcal F$. They glue to a coherent submodule on the union. Finite induction produces the claimed global $\mathcal E$, retaining its original restriction on $U$. Empty overlaps give $N=0$ and glue without a restriction. If $U$ is empty one may start with the zero submodule; if $X$ is empty all constructions are empty. No separation of intersections into affine schemes was required. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 For the filtered-union claim, a finite list of sections on an affine open generates a finite submodule of its affine module. Over its Noetherian coordinate ring that submodule is coherent by the argument in step 2.1, so step 3.1 extends it to a coherent submodule of $\mathcal F$. Every local section is therefore locally contained in a global coherent submodule. Sums of two coherent submodules are again coherent, since their local modules are finite and the same finite-kernel argument applies. Taking ALL coherent submodules gives a directed system whose sheaf union is $\mathcal F$; no submodule is chosen for an arbitrary family of sections. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Apply this to the underlying module of the integral algebra $\mathcal A$. For a coherent submodule $\mathcal E\subseteq\mathcal A$, let $\mathcal B$ be the subalgebra generated by $\mathcal E$ and the unit. On each affine open, a finite list of module generators of $\mathcal E$ generates this algebra. All are integral over the base ring by the given integrality, so [F3] makes it module-finite. These subalgebras localize and glue by their finite polynomial descriptions, so $\mathcal B$ is quasi-coherent and coherent as a module by Noetherianity. They contain every finite local list by step 4.1. Two finite quasi-coherent subalgebras lie in the algebra generated by their sum, which is finite by the same argument. Thus the system of ALL finite quasi-coherent subalgebras is directed and has union $\mathcal A$. Empty section lists yield the image of the unit algebra, and a zero algebra simply yields the zero sheaf. This proves every assertion without extending the scope beyond Noetherian bases. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
