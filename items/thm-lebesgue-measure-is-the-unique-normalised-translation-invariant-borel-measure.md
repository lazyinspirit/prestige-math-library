## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$ and **assume the Axiom of Countable Choice**
([[def-countable-choice]]). Let $\mu$ be a measure on
$\big(\mathbb{R}^n,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)\big)$ ([[def-measure]]) such that
$\mu(E+h) = \mu(E)$ for every Borel set $E$ and every $h \in \mathbb{R}^n$, and
$\mu\big((0,1]^n\big) = 1$. Then

$$\mu(E) \;=\; \lambda_n(E) \qquad \text{for every } E \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n).$$

The hypothesis is meaningful because a translate of a Borel set is Borel, and it
is satisfied by the restriction of $\lambda_n$ to $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, so
the theorem says that measure is the only one satisfying it. Finiteness on
bounded sets is a consequence of the normalisation, not a further hypothesis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n \ge 1$, the Axiom of Countable Choice, and a translation-invariant measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\mu\big((0,1]^n\big)=1$.

[L1] If $\mu$ is a measure on the Borel sets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ that is translation invariant and gives the unit cube measure $1$, then $\mu(Q) = 2^{-kn}$ for every dyadic cube $Q$ of generation $k$ ([[lem-dyadic-cube-values-are-forced-by-translation-invariance-and-normalisation]], [[def-dyadic-cube-in-rn]]).

[L2] Every open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is the union of an at most countable family of pairwise disjoint dyadic cubes ([[thm-open-subsets-of-rn-are-countable-disjoint-unions-of-dyadic-cubes]]).

[L3] $\operatorname{vol}(Q_{k,m}) = 2^{-kn}$ ([[lem-dyadic-cubes-of-one-generation-partition-rn]]).

[L4] Assuming countable choice, every Borel subset of $\mathbb{R}^n$ is Lebesgue measurable ([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]) and $\lambda_n$ is a measure on $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with $\lambda_n(B)=\operatorname{vol}(B)$ for every half-open box ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-is-a-complete-measure]]).

[L5] Every set $R$ with $R^{\circ} \subseteq R \subseteq \overline{R}$ is Lebesgue measurable with $\lambda_n(R) = \prod_{i<n}(b_i-a_i)$ ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]).

[L6] $\lambda_n^*(E+h) = \lambda_n^*(E)$ for every subset $E$, $E$ is Lebesgue measurable if and only if $E+h$ is, and $\lambda_n(E+h)=\lambda_n(E)$ for measurable $E$ ([[thm-lebesgue-outer-measure-and-measurability-are-translation-invariant]], [[def-translation-of-a-set-in-rn]]).

[F1] Let $\mathcal P$ be a pi-system on $X$ generating $\mathcal A$, and let $\mu,\nu$ be measures on $(X,\mathcal A)$ that agree on $\mathcal P$; suppose there is an increasing sequence $(P_n)$ in $\mathcal P$ with $X=\bigcup_nP_n$ and $\mu(P_n)=\nu(P_n)<+\infty$ for every $n$; then $\mu=\nu$ on $\mathcal A$ ([[thm-measure-uniqueness-on-a-sigma-finite-pi-system]]).

[F2] A **pi-system on $X$** is a nonempty family $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal P(X)$ closed under binary intersections ([[def-pi-system]]).

[F3] The **Borel sigma-algebra of $X$** is the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), and $\sigma_X(\mathcal E)$ is the unique smallest sigma-algebra on $X$ containing $\mathcal E$ ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]); a sigma-algebra is closed under complements and countable unions ([[def-sigma-algebra]]).

[F4] A subset $U$ is open in $(X,d)$ when every $x \in U$ has a ball $B(x,r) \subseteq U$ ([[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]]); a finite intersection of open sets is open ([[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]], claim 3); and $d_2(x,y) := \sqrt{\sum_{k<n}(x_k-y_k)^2}$ is a metric on $\mathbb{R}^n$ ([[lem-metrics-on-rn]]).

[F5] A **measure** is countably additive on pairwise disjoint measurable sequences ([[def-measure]]), and an at most countable family may be presented as a sequence ([[def-countable]]).

[F6] Every complete ordered field $F$ is Archimedean: for every $x \in F$ there is a natural number $n \ge 1$ with $x < n \cdot 1_F$ ([[thm-of-archimedean]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 A translate of a Borel set is Borel: the family of $E \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ whose translate $E+h$ is Borel contains every open set, since $d_2(x+h,y+h)=d_2(x,y)$ makes $B(x,r)+h = B(x+h,r)$ and hence $U+h$ open for open $U$, and it is a sigma-algebra because translation commutes with complements and with countable unions; minimality of $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ over the open sets finishes it. [F3, F4]

1.2 The open subsets of $\mathbb{R}^n$ form a pi-system generating $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$: the family is nonempty and closed under binary intersections, and the Borel sigma-algebra is by definition the one it generates. [F2, F3, F4]

1.3 The restriction of $\lambda_n$ to the Borel sets is a measure satisfying the two hypotheses, by translation invariance and by $\lambda_n\big((0,1]^n\big) = 1$. [L4, L5, L6]

2.1 By the dyadic lemma both $\mu$ and $\lambda_n$ give a generation-$k$ dyadic cube the value $2^{-kn}$, the latter because a dyadic cube is a half-open box of that volume. [step 1.3, L1, L3, L4]

3.1 Both measures therefore agree on every open set: such a set is the union of an at most countable pairwise disjoint family of dyadic cubes, which may be presented as a sequence, and countable additivity gives the same value for the two measures. [step 2.1, L2, F5]

4.1 The open cubes $P_k := \{\, x : |x_i| < k+1 \text{ for every } i<n \,\}$ form an increasing sequence of open sets with union $\mathbb{R}^n$, by the Archimedean property, and $\mu(P_k) = \lambda_n(P_k) = (2k+2)^n < +\infty$ by step 3.1 and the box theorem; the uniqueness theorem for a sigma-finite generating pi-system therefore gives $\mu = \lambda_n$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and step 1.1 makes the invariance hypothesis meaningful throughout. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, L5, F1, F6] ∎
