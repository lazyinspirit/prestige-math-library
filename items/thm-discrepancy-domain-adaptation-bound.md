## Statement

Let $P,Q$ be the source and target input marginals on a common measurable space $\mathsf X$. Let $\mathsf Y$ be a measurable output space, $\mathcal H$ a nonempty class of measurable functions $h:\mathsf X\to\mathsf Y$, and $f_P,f_Q:\mathsf X\to\mathsf Y$ measurable labeling functions. Let $L:\mathsf Y\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$ be product-measurable, symmetric, and satisfy the triangle inequality. Define
$$L_D(g_1,g_2):=\int_{\mathsf X}L(g_1(x),g_2(x))\,D(dx)\quad(D\in\{P,Q\}),\qquad \operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q):=\sup_{g_1,g_2\in\mathcal H}|L_P(g_1,g_2)-L_Q(g_1,g_2)|.$$
Assume that every pairwise integral in this discrepancy definition is finite and that source and target minimizers $h_P^*\in\arg\min_{g\in\mathcal H}L_P(g,f_P)$ and $h_Q^*\in\arg\min_{g\in\mathcal H}L_Q(g,f_Q)$ exist. Then every $h\in\mathcal H$ satisfies
$$L_P(h,f_P)\le L_P(h_P^*,f_P)+L_Q(h,h_Q^*)+\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q)+\min\{L_Q(h_Q^*,h_P^*),L_P(h_Q^*,h_P^*)\}.$$
The supremum defining $\operatorname{disc}_L$ may be infinite, in which case the inequality is interpreted in the extended nonnegative reals.

## Statement references

[Mansour, Mohri, and Rostamizadeh, Definition 4 and Theorem 8, arXiv version 3, §§3–4.1, pp. 3–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3430v3): the discrepancy definition and the bound with the minimum of both cross-domain terms (Theorem 8, Eq. (11)); its proof gives both triangle-inequality routes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Probability input marginals $P,Q$; a nonempty measurable hypothesis class $\mathcal H$; measurable labeling functions $f_P,f_Q$; a measurable symmetric loss satisfying the triangle inequality; finite pairwise risks for the discrepancy; and attained source and target minima. Labeling risks may be extended nonnegative values.

[F1] [[def-source-target-distributions-and-shift]]: the input marginals $P,Q$ are probability measures on the common measurable input space.

[F2] [[def-loss-discrepancy-between-domains]]: every pair $g_1,g_2\in\mathcal H$ has finite $L_P(g_1,g_2),L_Q(g_1,g_2)$, and $|L_P(g_1,g_2)-L_Q(g_1,g_2)|\le\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q)$.

[F3] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]: a map is measurable when preimages of measurable sets are measurable.

[F4] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: the product sigma-algebra on $\mathsf Y\times\mathsf Y$ is generated by measurable rectangles.

[F5] [[cor-additivity-of-the-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]]: the nonnegative integral is additive for sums of nonnegative measurable functions.

[F6] [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]: the nonnegative integral is monotone.




## Proof

1.1 For measurable $g_1,g_2:\mathsf X\to\mathsf Y$, let $G(x)=(g_1(x),g_2(x))$. For each measurable rectangle $A\times B$, $G^{-1}(A\times B)=g_1^{-1}(A)\cap g_2^{-1}(B)\in\Sigma_X$, so $G$ is measurable into $\Sigma_Y\otimes\Sigma_Y$ by [F3] and [F4]. For each Borel $C\subseteq[0,+\infty]$, $(L\circ G)^{-1}(C)=G^{-1}(L^{-1}(C))\in\Sigma_X$; hence all displayed loss integrands are measurable. Pairwise integrals with both arguments in $\mathcal H$ are finite by [F2], while integrals involving $f_P$ or $f_Q$ are defined extended nonnegative values. [F2, F3, F4, given]

1.2 Fix $h\in\mathcal H$. Applying the triangle inequality twice pointwise and then using monotonicity and additivity of the nonnegative integral [F5, F6] under $P$ gives $L_P(h,f_P)\le L_P(h,h_Q^*)+L_P(h_Q^*,h_P^*)+L_P(h_P^*,f_P)$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, given, algebra]

1.3 A second triangle inequality, integrated under $P$ using monotonicity and additivity of the nonnegative integral [F5, F6], gives $L_P(h,f_P)\le L_P(h,h_P^*)+L_P(h_P^*,f_P)$. Apply [F2] to the first term, then integrate the triangle inequality under $Q$ using [F5, F6] to obtain $L_Q(h,h_P^*)\le L_Q(h,h_Q^*)+L_Q(h_Q^*,h_P^*)$. Hence $L_P(h,f_P)\le L_P(h_P^*,f_P)+L_Q(h,h_Q^*)+\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q)+L_Q(h_Q^*,h_P^*)$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F2], $L_P(h,h_Q^*)\le L_Q(h,h_Q^*)+\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q)$. Substitution in step 1.2 yields $L_P(h,f_P)\le L_P(h_P^*,f_P)+L_Q(h,h_Q^*)+\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q)+L_P(h_Q^*,h_P^*)$. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The bounds in steps 2.1 and 1.3 have the same terms except for the final cross-domain term. Taking the smaller of those terms proves the stated minimum bound. [step 2.1, step 1.3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The discrepancy in this theorem is a population quantity. Estimating it from finite samples, or replacing the input laws by empirical laws, requires additional bounded-loss and complexity assumptions and is a separate result.

[Mansour, Mohri, and Rostamizadeh, §§3–4.1, arXiv version 3, pp. 3–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0902.3430v3): finite-sample discrepancy estimation and the empirical target-risk bound are stated separately with Rademacher and bounded-loss hypotheses.
