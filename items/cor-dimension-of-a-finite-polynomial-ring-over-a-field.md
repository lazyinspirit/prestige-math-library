## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $n\ge0$. Then
$$ \dim k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=n. $$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and an integer $n\ge0$.

[L1] Every nonzero finite-type $k$-domain $D$ is module-finite and hence integral over a polynomial subring $B=k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$ ([[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]]). Comparable primes in an integral extension with equal contraction are equal ([[thm-incomparability-for-integral-extensions]]).

[L2] Finite transcendence degree is additive in field towers, so algebraic extensions preserve transcendence degree ([[cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $D$ be any finite-type $k$-domain and $Q\subsetneq Q'$ primes of $D$. Set $E=D/Q$, a nonzero finite-type $k$-domain, and $J=Q'/Q$, a nonzero prime of $E$. By [L1], fix a module-finite normalization $B=k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]\subset E$. Its fraction field extension is algebraic, so [L2] gives $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(E)=d$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 By incomparability in [L1], $q:=J\cap B$ is nonzero: otherwise $(0)\subsetneq J$ would be comparable primes of $E$ with the same contraction $(0)$ in $B$. If $d=0$, then $B=k$ is a field and has no nonzero proper prime, so the assumed strict pair cannot exist. For a strict pair one therefore has $d\ge1$. A nonzero polynomial $f\in q$ gives a nontrivial algebraic relation among the images of $z_1,\ldots,z_d$ in the domain $B/q$. Among this finite set of images, take a maximal algebraically independent subset; because the full set is dependent, it has at most $d-1$ members. Maximality makes each omitted image algebraic over the field generated by the subset. Since all images generate $\operatorname{Frac}(B/q)$, this proves $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(B/q)\le d-1$. [L1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The quotient $E/J$ is integral over $B/q$, hence its fraction field is algebraic over $\operatorname{Frac}(B/q)$. By [L2] and step 2.1, $\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(D/Q')\le d-1<d=\operatorname{trdeg}_k\operatorname{Frac}(D/Q)$. Thus transcendence degree drops at least one along every strict prime inclusion in a finite-type domain. [L2, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Apply this drop to any strict chain $P_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_r$ in $k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. The fraction field of the initial quotient is generated by at most $n$ images of variables, so its transcendence degree is at most $n$; the final transcendence degree is nonnegative. Hence $r\le n$. For $n\ge1$, the coordinate ideals $(0)\subsetneq(x_1)\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$ are prime and form a chain of length $n$. When $n=0$, the sole prime is $(0)$ and both bounds are zero. Therefore $\dim k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=n$ for every $n\ge0$. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
