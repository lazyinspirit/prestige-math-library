## Statement

For every associative unital ring $R$, the stable elementary subgroup $\mathrm E(R)$ is a normal subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ and $$\mathrm E(R)=[\mathrm{GL}(R),\mathrm{GL}(R)].$$ Consequently $\mathrm{GL}(R)/\mathrm E(R)$ is abelian. Moreover, for every $n$, an upper unitriangular matrix $I_n+N$ in specified ordered coordinates, with $N_{ij}=0$ unless $i<j$, lies in $\mathrm E_n(R)$ in those coordinates. The matrix of the same automorphism in any other ordered basis lies in the stable subgroup $\mathrm E(R)$ by normality, and hence lies in $\mathrm E_N(R)$ after some finite stabilization; this need not hold at the original matrix size.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An associative unital ring $R$ and its stable groups $\mathrm{GL}(R)\supseteq\mathrm E(R)$ and elementary matrices $e_{ij}(r)=I+rE_{ij}$ ([[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]]).

[F1] $\mathrm E(R)$ is by definition the subgroup generated by all elementary matrices, $e_{ij}(r)^{-1}=e_{ij}(-r)$, and matrix multiplication is the group operation, so $[g,h]$ denotes $ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ ([[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]]).

[F2] A normal subgroup is a subgroup closed under conjugation, and the subgroup generated by a family is the smallest subgroup containing it ([[def-normal-subgroup]], [[def-generated-subgroup]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $i,j,k$ be pairwise distinct and $r,s\in R$. With $X=rE_{ij}$ and $Y=sE_{jk}$ one has $X^2=Y^2=0$, $XY=rsE_{ik}$, and $E_{jk}E_{ij}=E_{ij}E_{ik}=E_{ik}E_{jk}=0$, so expanding $(I+X+Y+XY)(I-X-Y+XY)$ gives $e_{ij}(r)e_{jk}(s)e_{ij}(-r)e_{jk}(-s)=I+rsE_{ik}=e_{ik}(rs)$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 For $Z\in\mathrm{GL}_m(R)$ the matrix $\operatorname{diag}(Z,Z^{-1})$ lies in $\mathrm E_{2m}(R)$: with $u(C)=I+\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}E_{i,m+j}$ and $l(C)=I+\sum_{i,j}c_{ij}E_{m+i,j}$ one has $u(Z)l(-Z^{-1})u(Z)=\begin{pmatrix}0&Z\\-Z^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}$, and each of $u(C),l(C)$ is a product of elementary matrices with distinct indices, while $u(1)l(-1)u(1)=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and multiplying the first displayed block swap by the inverse of the second gives $\begin{pmatrix}0&Z\\-Z^{-1}&0\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}=\operatorname{diag}(Z,Z^{-1})$. The inverse of the second block swap is elementary because it is the inverse of a product of elementary matrices. [given, F1, algebra]

1.3 For the upper unitriangular claim, use the specified ordered basis as the coordinates in which the matrix is given; it has the standard form $A=I_n+N$ with $N_{ij}=0$ unless $i<j$, so it remains to prove directly that this coordinate matrix belongs to $\mathrm E_n(R)$. [given]

2.1 Every elementary matrix is a commutator: for $i\ne k$ and $t\in R$, stabilize if necessary so that some index $j$ distinct from $i$ and $k$ exists, and apply step 1.1 with $r=t$ and $s=1$ to get $e_{ik}(t)=[e_{ij}(t),e_{jk}(1)]$; hence every generator of $\mathrm E(R)$ lies in $[\mathrm{GL}(R),\mathrm{GL}(R)]$, so $\mathrm E(R)\subseteq[\mathrm{GL}(R),\mathrm{GL}(R)]$. [given, F1, step 1.1]

2.2 For $X,Y\in\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ one has $\operatorname{diag}(X,X^{-1})\operatorname{diag}(Y,Y^{-1})\operatorname{diag}((YX)^{-1},YX)=\operatorname{diag}(XY(YX)^{-1},X^{-1}Y^{-1}YX)=\operatorname{diag}([X,Y],1_n)$, which represents $[X,Y]$ in $\mathrm{GL}(R)$; each factor on the left lies in $\mathrm E(R)$ by step 1.2, so $[\mathrm{GL}(R),\mathrm{GL}(R)]\subseteq\mathrm E(R)$. [given, F1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.3 Argue by induction on $n$: for $n=1$ the matrix $A=I_1$ is the empty product of elementary matrices, while for $n\ge2$ one writes $A=\begin{pmatrix}A'&v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ with $A'$ upper unitriangular of size $n-1$ and has $\operatorname{diag}(A',1)^{-1}A=\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&A'^{-1}v\\0&1\end{pmatrix}=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\bigl(I+(A'^{-1}v)_iE_{in}\bigr)$, a product of elementary matrices because $E_{in}E_{jn}=0$ for $i\ne j$; the induction hypothesis gives $A'\in\mathrm E_{n-1}(R)$, hence $A\in\mathrm E_n(R)$ in the specified coordinates. [given, F1, step 1.3, induction, algebra]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 give $\mathrm E(R)=[\mathrm{GL}(R),\mathrm{GL}(R)]$, and a commutator subgroup is normal, so $\mathrm E(R)$ is normal in $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ by [F2] and $\mathrm{GL}(R)/\mathrm E(R)$ is abelian because every commutator lies in the kernel of the quotient map. [F2, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 If another ordered basis is used, let $P\in\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ be the matrix whose columns are that basis in the specified coordinates. The new matrix is $P^{-1}AP$. By step 2.3, $A\in\mathrm E_n(R)\subseteq\mathrm E(R)$, and by step 3.1 the stable subgroup is normal, so $P^{-1}AP\in\mathrm E(R)$. By the definition of the stable elementary subgroup as the union under stabilization, this matrix belongs to $\mathrm E_N(R)$ for some finite $N$ after stabilization. [F1, step 3.1, step 2.3]

∎
