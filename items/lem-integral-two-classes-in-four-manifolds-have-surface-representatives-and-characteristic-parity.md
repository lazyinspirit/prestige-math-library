## Statement

Assume AC. Let $B$ be a compact oriented smooth four-manifold, possibly with boundary. Every $x\in H_2(B;\mathbb Z)$ is represented by a closed oriented smooth embedded surface $i:F\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Int}B$, with disconnected or empty $F$ allowed. For its oriented normal plane bundle $\nu_F$,
$$\langle w_2(TB),\rho_2x\rangle=\langle e(\nu_F),[F]\rangle\pmod2.$$
If $x$ is torsion, this integer normal Euler number is zero. In particular $w_2(TB)$ evaluates to zero on the reductions of all integral torsion degree-two classes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the compact oriented smooth four-manifold $B$, and an integral class $x$.

[F1] Smooth collars exist; compact surfaces have finite polygon normal forms, and an oriented closed component is a sphere or a handle surface with Euler characteristic $2-2g$. Continuous maps between smooth manifolds admit homotopic smooth approximations, and homotopic maps induce equal homology maps ([[thm-collar-neighborhood-theorem]], [[thm-polygonal-normal-form-for-compact-connected-surfaces]], [[thm-whitney-approximation-for-manifold-valued-maps]], [[cor-homotopic-maps-induce-the-same-map-on-singular-homology]]).

[F2] A smooth target embeds in a finite Euclidean space and has a smooth tubular retraction there. Smooth partitions and bumps exist. A smooth family transverse to a submanifold has transverse slices outside a null parameter set; a transverse inverse image has the expected codimension ([[thm-every-smooth-manifold-embeds-in-some-finite-dimensional-euclidean-space]], [[thm-euclidean-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem]], [[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]], [[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[thm-transverse-preimage-theorem]]).

[F3] Tubular neighborhoods and oriented normal Thom classes exist. Excision transports the normal class to relative ambient cohomology, and its zero-section restriction is the normal Euler class ([[thm-tubular-neighbourhood-theorem-in-a-smooth-ambient-manifold]], [[thm-thom-isomorphism-for-oriented-vector-bundles]], [[thm-excision-for-singular-cohomology]], [[def-euler-class-by-zero-section-pullback-of-the-thom-class]]).

[F4] Stiefel–Whitney classes are natural and satisfy the Whitney formula; an oriented plane has $w_2=\rho_2e$. On a closed oriented surface, $\langle e(TF),[F]\rangle=\chi(F)$ ([[thm-naturality-of-stiefel-whitney-classes]], [[thm-whitney-sum-formula-for-stiefel-whitney-classes]], [[thm-mod-two-euler-class-is-the-top-stiefel-whitney-class]], [[cor-euler-number-of-the-tangent-bundle-is-the-euler-characteristic]]).

[A1] Full AC is inherited from these suppliers; all additional chain gluings, perturbation families, and local resolutions below are finite ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Represent $x$ by a finite singular integral 2-cycle. Replace a coefficient by that many copies of its triangle, oriented according to its sign. In the boundary chain, occurrences of each identical singular edge have total coefficient zero, so pair its positively and negatively oriented occurrences. Glue each paired pair by its affine edge identification. The triangle orientations agree across the gluing. Every edge now has two incident half-disks. Each vertex link is a finite graph all of whose vertices have degree two, hence a disjoint union of circles; split a vertex once for each link circle. The resulting finite quotient is a closed oriented topological surface $F_0$, with a continuous map to $B$ defined by the singular triangles. The image of its oriented fundamental cycle is exactly the original cycle. Sphere and handle models from [F1] give $F_0$ a smooth structure, transported by the polygon homeomorphism; their smooth structures can be constructed by gluing finitely many smooth handles to a punctured sphere. Thus no representability theorem is being assumed. [F1, A1, construct]

2.1 Use a collar to push this map into the interior: choose a smooth strictly increasing collar function $a:[0,\epsilon)\to[0,\epsilon)$ with $a(0)>0$ and $a(t)=t$ for $t\ge\epsilon/2$. Such a function is $t+\delta\eta(t)$, where $\eta=1$ near zero, vanishes for $t\ge\epsilon/2$, and $\delta>0$ is small enough that $1+\delta\eta'(t)>0$. Interpolation with $t$ is a homotopy of maps into $B$. Apply smooth approximation [F1] to the resulting map $F_0\to\operatorname{Int}B$. It still represents $x$. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 We give the general-position details needed for this particular compact source. Embed the target $N=\operatorname{Int}B$ in $\mathbb R^d$ and choose a smooth tubular retraction $R$ as in [F2]. Cover $F_0$ by finitely many coordinate cores, with bumps equal to one near each core. In a source chart with coordinates $u_1,u_2$, vary each ambient coordinate of the map by the bump times an arbitrary affine function of $u_1,u_2$, and compose with $R$. The resulting finite-dimensional family has independently variable value and first derivative at every source point at parameter zero. In target coordinates this says that the parameter differential of its first-jet map surjects onto the value and derivative coordinates; it remains surjective on the compact source for sufficiently small parameters. The first-jet bundle here has coordinates $(u,y,A)$, with $A$ a $4\times2$ matrix; changes of charts act by the chain rule and preserve rank. Matrices of rank one form a smooth stratum of codimension $(4-1)(2-1)=3$ (solve one nonzero entry for the remaining dependent rows and columns), and rank zero has codimension eight. Apply parametric transversality to these two strata. A transverse slice from a two-dimensional source cannot meet either stratum, so a sufficiently small parameter gives an immersion, homotopic to the starting map through the same tubular family. [F2, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 For an immersion of a compact source, there is a $\delta>0$ and a $C^1$ neighborhood in which every map is injective on each source pair of distance less than $3\delta$. To verify this, in a small chart project to an invertible two-coordinate minor of its derivative; on a smaller convex chart the derivative stays uniformly close to its central invertible matrix, giving a uniform lower Lipschitz bound by integrating along line segments. Finitely many smaller charts cover the source, and a Lebesgue number and the minimum of these bounds give the assertion. Thus possible double or triple points have pairwise source distances at least $\delta$. On the compact spaces of such pairs and triples choose finite covers by product neighborhoods whose source neighborhoods are disjoint. Add finitely many bump perturbations supported in those disjoint neighborhoods, independently in every ambient target coordinate. At parameter zero their pair and triple evaluations surject onto all target value coordinates, and this persists near the compact configuration sets for sufficiently small parameters. Apply parametric transversality to the diagonal in $N^2$ and the small diagonal in $N^3$, of codimensions four and eight. A good slice has transverse isolated double points and no triple points, since $\dim F_0^3=6<8$. Double points form a closed discrete subset of a compact pair configuration set, hence are finite. Restrict the parameters to preserve the preceding immersion and short-pair injectivity. The union of the two null exceptional parameter sets cannot fill a parameter ball, so such a slice exists. [F2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 At each of the finitely many image double points take mutually disjoint small coordinate balls meeting no other sheets. The two transverse disks can be straightened simultaneously: parametrize each sheet by its tangent coordinates and use the inverse function theorem on the sum of their local parametrizations. In an orientation-preserving chart write them as the coordinate disks in $\mathbb C^2$. Orient the first by the complex orientation and the second by $\varepsilon$ times its complex orientation, where $\varepsilon=\pm1$ is the intersection sign. Inside the coordinate ball replace them by the annulus $(\theta,s)\longmapsto\bigl(f(s)e^{i\theta},g(s)e^{-i\varepsilon\theta}\bigr)$, where $(f,g)$ is a smooth embedded quadrant arc from $(r,0)$ to $(0,r)$, lies inside the quarter-disk except at its endpoints, starts along the decreasing horizontal axis and ends along the increasing vertical axis. Such an arc is obtained by rounding the corners of a simple polygonal route in the quarter-disk, keeping its initial and final axis intervals. Give the annulus orientation $(\partial_\theta,\partial_s)$. Its boundary at the first end is the positive first circle; at the second it is $-\partial_\theta$, whose image is $\varepsilon$ times the positive second circle. Thus the oriented boundary matches both original disks. The axis intervals match the original disk collars; after deleting those disks the replacement is a smooth oriented embedded surface. The parametrization is embedded because $(f,g)$ is embedded, never simultaneously zero, and the nonzero complex coordinate determines $\theta$. [step 4.1, construct]

6.1 The oriented difference between this annulus and the original pair of disks is a 2-cycle in its coordinate ball, since their boundary circles and collars agree. It bounds a singular 3-chain there: the usual straight cone to the ball center satisfies $\partial C(z)=z$ for a cycle of positive degree, by cancellation of its side faces. Therefore the replacement preserves the integral homology class. Perform the finitely many replacements in disjoint balls. The resulting surface $F$ is closed, oriented, smooth, embedded in the interior, and represents $x$. For the zero class one may instead take the empty surface. [step 2.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Orient $\nu_F$ by $TB|_F\cong TF\oplus\nu_F$. Both summands are oriented, so their first Stiefel–Whitney classes vanish. The Whitney formula and [F4] give $i^*w_2(TB)=w_2(TF)+w_2(\nu_F)=\rho_2e(TF)+\rho_2e(\nu_F)$. Pairing with $[F]$ gives $\chi(F)+\langle e(\nu_F),[F]\rangle$ modulo two. Each oriented closed component has Euler characteristic $2-2g$ by [F1], so $\chi(F)$ is even. Naturality of evaluation proves the asserted parity identity, including disconnected $F$. [F1, F4, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Finally choose an open normal tube $U\subset\operatorname{Int}B$. The normal Thom class defines a class in $H^2(U,U\setminus F;\mathbb Z)$; the punctured disk bundle retracts radially to its sphere bundle, so this is the usual disk/sphere Thom class. Excision [F3] transports it to $H^2(B,B\setminus F;\mathbb Z)$; let $a\in H^2(B;\mathbb Z)$ be its absolute image. By the Euler definition, $i^*a=e(\nu_F)$. Hence $\langle e(\nu_F),[F]\rangle=\langle a,x\rangle$. If $nx=0$ for an integer $n>0$, multiplication by $n$ annihilates this integer pairing; since $\mathbb Z$ has no torsion, it is zero. Step 7.1 now proves the final assertion. No nonsingular intersection form, closed-ambient duality, or Whitney cancellation theorem is used. All choice assumptions are those of [A1]. [F3, A1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
