## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a globally weakly admissible complete pointed Heegaard diagram of ANY closed connected oriented smooth three-manifold, one integrable surface background $j$, area form and protected small Cayley coefficient family with common exact product generator charts. Let $J^0,J^1$ be two genuine time-collar paths with the same background endpoint structures, each regular at EVERY nonconstant zero-basepoint strip. Their actual index-one counts define the finite hat complexes $C^0,C^1$.

For a compact-transition source-dependent choice $K$ from $J^0$ to $J^1$ regular at every zero-basepoint solution, count its actual index-zero solutions WITHOUT translation quotient to obtain $\Phi_K:C^0\to C^1$. Then:

1. $d^1\Phi_K=\Phi_Kd^0$.
2. Two such compact-transition choices $K_0,K_1$ are joined by an ACTUAL regular relative parameter family. Its finite zero-basepoint index-minus-one solutions define a finite map $H$ with $\Phi_{K_1}+\Phi_{K_0}=d^1H+Hd^0$.
3. Continuation matrices compose up to this actual chain homotopy; their identity choice is the constant longitudinal path, whose matrix is the identity. Reversing the endpoints gives a continuation map which is a chain-homotopy inverse.

The one-dimensional moduli used for the first two identities have actual compactifications as compact topological one-manifolds with exactly the corresponding endpoint and broken boundary sets, with the actual exhaustive collars. Consequently all paths in this fixed-background protected family satisfying the stated regularity have chain-homotopy equivalent chosen hat complexes.

This compares AUXILIARY PATHS on the same globally weak diagram and the same integrable surface background. It does not compare different $j$, every merely sector-admissible diagram, attaching-circle moves, integral orientations, absolute gradings, Spin-c decompositions or plus/infinity/cobordism packages. No polygon counts are asserted. Genus zero gives the identity of the one-generator zero complex.

## Proof

1.1 The common energy bound and regular end paths give finite autonomous rigid count sets by [F2]. Their differentials square to zero by the same actual fixed-class argument in [F1]: only finitely many actual rigid pairs occur, each concatenated zero-marked index-two class has nonnegative domain in the original finite set, and its fixed-class energy bound is below the chosen all-zero-mark regularity range. In fact all zero-marked solutions are regular here, so that range contains every required bound. Apply [F2] to each of those finitely many actual classes and sum the even pair counts. Thus $C^0,C^1$ are actual finite complexes for the given paths, not merely for one specially selected path. The continuation matrices are finite by [F3]. Constant generator maps for any continuation profile have the exact invertible index-zero $D_0$ operator. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 First construct a regular relative family between two continuation profiles. Write their small Cayley coefficients relative to the common background and interpolate them using a smooth parameter function constant near $a=0,1$. This gives a background $K^b_a(s,t)$ equal to $K_0,K_1$ at its parameter endpoints and to the fixed $J^0,J^1$ outside one common compact source interval. It has a common positive time collar. To make an auxiliary universal family enumerate anti-$K^b$ matrix bumps with compact support in $(0,1)_a\times\mathbb R_s\times(0,1)_t\times(X\setminus P_X)$, where $P_X$ is the separated protected spatial set of [F4]. Give their coefficients the rapid Hilbert weights of that theorem, including every source derivative through order $n$ and the weight $e^{n\sqrt{1+s^2}}$. Uniform convergence of all derivatives makes the sum flat to every order at both parameter endpoints AND both time endpoints and rapidly decreasing in $s$. Its small Cayley transform is a genuine smooth family, with the exact endpoint profiles and fixed autonomous limits. At an interior parameter and a nonconstant solution, [F4]'s source-dependent adjoint proof applies with the additional compact parameter coordinate: any nonzero cokernel has a smooth nonzero value at a free source point, and a parameter/source/target bump makes its pairing positive. The family is therefore universally onto there. It is already onto at parameter endpoints since $K_0,K_1$ are regular at all zero-marked solutions, and at every constant map by its exact inverse. The same actual second-countable configuration construction and the augmented Fredholm derivative give parameter projection index $\mu+1$. Apply Countable-Choice Sard to the OPEN INTERIOR-parameter zero manifold on each integer-index stratum and the finite endpoint pairs; the parameter boundary is already regular and stays fixed. This chooses an auxiliary parameter for which ALL augmented zero-basepoint derivatives are onto. Its parameter-boundary zeros are neat: the fixed-profile operator is onto there, so the parameter coordinate restricts to a submersion on the zero chart. No target injectivity assumption or genericity claim for each individual unaugmented slice was used. [F1, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Replace that auxiliary family by genuine compact-transition, time-collar families, preserving its exact parameter endpoints. Multiply its perturbation coefficient by the longitudinal and time cutoffs in [F4]. Its flat time jets and exponential source estimates give uniform all-order convergence including parameter derivatives. The endpoint coefficients remain exactly $K_0,K_1$ because the perturbation and all its parameter-end jets vanish there. The asymptotically autonomous tail bounds in [F1] are uniform during approximation. If augmented regularity failed along a subsequence, fix a limiting parameter and apply [F1] to its bounded-energy solutions. The limiting central AUGMENTED operator is onto by step 1.2, or its unaugmented fixed endpoint operator is onto when the limiting parameter is $0$ or $1$. All autonomous end operators are onto. Patch their bounded right inverses with squared longitudinal cutoffs as in [F4], retaining the one central parameter output. Its forcing outside the central core causes only a vanishing additional error: it is zero on all exact generator necks, while on escaping autonomous cores its coefficient is uniformly small by the exponential source derivative bound, so its $L^2$ norm is at most that coefficient bound times the common finite $\|u_t\|_2$. Fixed-core coefficient errors tend to zero and cutoff commutators are $O(R^{-1})$. Hence the actual augmented right-inverse error tends to zero, and its Neumann correction contradicts nononto-ness. Choose a late genuine family $K_a$ regular in the augmented sense at EVERY zero-basepoint solution. It has one common finite transition interval, fixed autonomous ends, positive time collars and exact endpoint profiles. This is a compactness-and-operator proof of the approximation step, not an inference from genericity of the limit alone. [F1, F4, step 1.2, construct, contradiction]

3.1 Its parameterized index-minus-one solution set is finite. Every such zero has augmented index zero. A sequence has an anchored limit by [F1], with converging parameter. The central augmented operator is onto, so its unaugmented index is at least minus one. Each nonconstant autonomous end level has index at least one, since its regular operator has a nonzero translation kernel. Index additivity with total unaugmented index minus one therefore leaves no end level and makes the central augmented index zero. A limit at a parameter endpoint would have an onto unaugmented index-minus-one operator, which is impossible. Thus every limit is interior. Smooth core convergence and the exact transverse-chart tail inverse give GLOBAL $H^3$ convergence, as in [F4], with the parameter converging too. Its actual zero chart has dimension zero, so the sequence is eventually that solution. The Countable-Choice ordered-finite-tuples argument in [F3] rules out infinitely many distinct solutions. Count these finite pairs $(a,u)$ without translation to define $H$; the underlying actual strip index is minus one. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 We now supply the compact one-manifolds for both identities. For a FIXED continuation profile use its index-one zero space; each actual unaugmented operator is onto, so this is a smooth one-manifold in the actual $H^3$ map topology. For the family in step 2.1 use its parameterized index-zero zero space; its augmented operators are onto, again giving a one-manifold, with parameter-end boundary charts transverse as shown in step 1.2. These spaces are Hausdorff and second-countable as actual zero subspaces of the map/parameter manifolds; there is NO translation quotient. In the first case [F1]'s index sum permits either one central index-one strip, or an index-zero central solution and exactly one autonomous index-one level at the negative or positive end. In the second case it permits one central parameterized index-zero solution, parameter-end index-zero solutions, or an interior parameterized index-minus-one central solution and exactly one autonomous index-one level. Indeed central regularity gives index at least zero in the fixed case and at least minus one in the one-parameter case; all end indices are positive. No negative nonregular limiting central index has been left unchecked. Zero marks exclude every compact bubble before this index classification. Global $H^3$ convergence in the unbroken case follows from the fixed tail-cutoff inverse, so those limits remain interior or actual parameter-boundary points. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Every listed broken point is ACTUALLY present and has the required exhaustive collar: apply [F3]'s anchored gluing with $q=0$ to the fixed-profile index-zero central solution, or with $q=1$ to the parameterized index-minus-one central solution. The latter augmented derivative is an isomorphism and its parameter lies in the interior by step 3.1, exactly as required by that gluing theorem. The actual glued class and zero mark degree are preserved, and its full local zero chart is the actual one-dimensional space just described. The finite candidate boundary sets consist, respectively, of finite continuation zero-count sets times finite autonomous rigid sets, or of finite $H$ sets times finite rigid sets, together with the two finite endpoint count sets. There are no additional boundary points by the actual compactness classification in step 4.1. In particular a finite juxtaposition count has not been assumed equal to a product; that assertion is supplied separately by the actual two-transition gluing in [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Verify that adjoining these finite sets really gives compact Hausdorff one-manifolds. For a sequence converging to two proposed boundary points, the anchored central component is unique, because both convergence descriptions give the same unshifted smooth limit on each finite rectangle, and in the parameterized case also the same parameter limit. Two different proposed escaping end levels cannot persist: one of their positive-energy cores would lie in the other decomposition's energy-collapsing gap or tail if their centers separated by an unbounded amount. Bounded center differences identify the same autonomous orbit by smooth convergence. A left and a right escaping component cannot represent one limit of the stipulated one-end-level type, since their center difference diverges. Strong global $H^3$ interior convergence precludes any positive energy escaping to either tail, and excludes simultaneous interior and broken limits. Thus the boundary limits are unique. Distinct exhaustive collar tails may consequently be shortened to disjoint ones, separated from each fixed interior point; failure would select a sequence with two distinct limits. Parameter-boundary charts are already ordinary half-interval charts and cannot also be broken limits: a broken family limit has its central minus-one parameter strictly interior, whereas fixed endpoint profiles have no such solutions. Adding the finite broken points with their actual half-interval collars therefore gives a Hausdorff second-countable topological one-manifold. Every sequence has a convergent subsequence by step 4.1 and the exhaustive collars. Under Countable Choice second-countability plus sequential compactness implies compactness: a countable subcover with no finite subcover would supply points outside its first $n$ members and a convergent subsequence contradicting a member containing its limit. This is the actual compactification, not an assumed abstract stable-map space. [F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The boundary of either compact topological one-manifold is even. Here is the elementary argument, so no boundary smoothness of an ambient compactifying map is needed. Cover it by finitely many small interval or half-interval charts with closures in larger such charts. Cut at their finitely many frontier points and at the actual boundary. Membership in a small chart is constant on each complementary component, since its frontier has been removed; hence each such component lies in one chart and is an interval. Its ends are in the finite cut set; an endpoint-free component contained in one chart would be a compact open interval, impossible. Finitely many germs at the cut points give finitely many interval pieces. Interior cut points have two incident germs and actual boundary points one, so $2e=2i+b$ and $b$ is even. For the fixed profile, its boundary sets in step 5.1 are exactly the products counting $d^1\Phi_K$ and $\Phi_Kd^0$ with each possible intermediate generator; their equal mod-two totals give the chain-map identity. For the parameterized family its other boundary points are exactly the $a=0,1$ zero-count sets. Its broken sets count $d^1H$ and $Hd^0$. Their even total gives $\Phi_{K_1}+\Phi_{K_0}=d^1H+Hd^0$. All sums are finite by steps 1.1 and 3.1. No formal domain-class identification or unspecified paired ends were used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Finally compare compositions and obtain inverses. The actual separated-transition theorem in [F3] gives, for sufficiently large $R$, a compact-transition profile from $J^0$ to $J^2$ whose matrix is EXACTLY $\Phi_{12}\Phi_{01}$ and whose zero-basepoint operators are all regular. Any other regular direct profile with those endpoints is joined to it by the actual relative family in steps 1.2 and 2.1. Step 7.1 gives the chain homotopy between the two matrices. If both endpoints equal $J^i$, the constant longitudinal choice $K(s,t)=J^i_t$ has precisely the constant generators as its index-zero solutions: every nonconstant autonomous solution is regular and has a nonzero translation kernel, hence index at least one. The constants are regular of index zero, each is one actual map with no translation quotient, and therefore this matrix is the identity. Choose a reverse regular compact-transition profile using [F3]'s finite continuation theorem. The two separated compositions are chain homotopic by step 7.1 to these two identity profiles. Thus the forward and reverse matrices are actual chain-homotopy inverses. This proves fixed-background auxiliary-path independence only. In genus zero all structures act on a point, the unique zero-index map is the identity and all minus-one and index-one sets are empty, giving the same identities directly. No change of surface complex structure, Heegaard move, absolute normalization or larger Floer package was inferred. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra] ∎
