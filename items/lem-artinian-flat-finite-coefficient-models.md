## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let k be a field of characteristic p, let (T,m,K) be an Artinian local k-algebra, and suppose V(K/k)=ker(K tensor_k Omega_(k/F_p) -> Omega_(K/F_p)) is finite-dimensional over K. For every finite subset E of T, there is an Artinian essentially finite-type k-subalgebra A⊂T containing E, with A→T flat and m_A T=m_T. There is a subfield k⊂F⊂K finitely generated over k and a coefficient section σ:K→T whose image of F lies in A, such that the natural map K⊗_F A→T is an isomorphism. The section σ is not assumed k-linear.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite equations, the specified local constructions and the explicit supplier interfaces.

1.1 Choose finitely many generators `lambda_1,...,lambda_d` of `m`, and let `N` be its nilpotence exponent. The prime-field p-basis lifting theorem already proved supplies a section `sigma:K -> T` of its residue map. The images of `sigma(K)` and the `lambda_i` generate `T`: subtract a residue lift, write the remaining element in the ideal generated by the `lambda_i`, repeat on its finitely many coefficients, and terminate at `m^N=0`. Thus `K[X]/(X)^N -> T` is surjective for this coefficient section. The section is generally **not** k-linear. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We prove by induction on `N` that `sigma` can be chosen so there is a subfield `k subset F subset K`, finitely generated over the residue image of `k`, with the given image of `k` in `T` contained in `sigma(F)[lambda]`. For `N=1`, use the identity coefficient section and `F=k`. For the induction step put `J=m^(N-1)` and `T'=T/J`; then `J^2=mJ=0`. The induction hypothesis gives a coefficient section `sigma':K -> T'` and a finitely generated `F'` for which the given `k`-image lies in `A'=sigma'(F')[lambda mod J]`. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Lift `sigma'` to `sigma:K -> T` by the explicit prime-field p-basis lifting formula across the nilpotent quotient, not by an unspecified relative Cohen theorem. Let `A_0=F'[X_1,...,X_d]/(X)^N`. It maps to `T` using `sigma` and `lambda`, and maps surjectively to `A'`. The latter kernel is nilpotent because it is contained in the maximal ideal `(X)` and the residue map is the identity on `F'`. The given map `tau':k -> A'` therefore lifts to a map `tau:k -> A_0`, again by the prime-field p-basis lifting theorem for arbitrary nilpotent quotient rings. Denote the map `A_0 -> T` by `Psi_sigma`. The difference `theta=i-Psi_sigma tau:k -> J` is a derivation, where `i` is the given k-embedding and `J` is a K-vector space via its residue action. [F1, F2, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Choose a maximal p-independent subset `{y_j}` of `k` over `k^p` by Zorn under AC. Maximality makes it generate `k` over `k^p`, and its restricted monomials form a basis over `k^p`. Formal differentiation of those unique finite monomial expressions shows that `Omega_(k/F_p)` is free on the `dy_j`. The finite-dimensional subspace `V(K/k)` lies in the span of a finite sublist `J_0` of these basis vectors, since each of finitely many vector generators has finite support. Thus the images of `dy_j`, `j notin J_0`, are linearly independent in `Omega_(K/F_p)`. Extend them to a K-basis using AC and define a K-linear map to `J` sending them to `theta(y_j)`. By the universal property this is a derivation `D:K -> J`. Replace the coefficient section by `sigma+D`; it remains a ring section because `J^2=0`. [F1, F2, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Under this replacement the discrepancy changes to `theta-D|k`. To verify this rather than assume it, write `tau(a)` as its polynomial in the `X_i`. Changing its coefficient lifts contributes only the derivative of the constant coefficient: every positive-degree term multiplies `J` by `m` and is zero. Its constant coefficient is the residue `a in k subset F'`. Thus the change is exactly `D(a)`. The new discrepancy vanishes on every `dy_j` outside the finite list `J_0`. [F1, F2, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 Its finitely many remaining values `c_j in J`, `j in J_0`, have expressions `c_j=sum_(|e|=N-1) c_(j,e) lambda^e`, with `c_(j,e) in K`. There are finitely many coefficients. Enlarge `F'` by them to `F`. For every `a in k`, write `da=sum_j a_j dy_j` with finite support and `a_j in k`. The remaining discrepancy is `sum_(j in J_0) a_j c_j`, which lies in `sigma(F)[lambda]`. The other term `Psi_sigma tau(a)` is already in `sigma(F')[lambda]`. Hence the entire given image `i(k)` lies in `sigma(F)[lambda]`. This proves the claim without pretending that `sigma|k=i`. [F1, F2, step 5.1, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 Enlarge `F` by the finitely many coefficients expressing the prescribed elements of `E` as polynomials in `lambda` over `sigma(K)`. The kernel of `K[X] -> T` is a finitely generated ideal. Choose its finitely many polynomial generators and enlarge `F` by their coefficients. Include all degree-`N` monomials explicitly among those generators; they already vanish. All these relations have zero constant coefficient, since their image in the residue field is that constant. Define `A=F[X_1,...,X_d]/(g_1,...,g_s)` with the same finite list. Then `K tensor_F A = T`. Faithful flatness of the field extension shows `A -> T` is injective and flat. The explicit degree-`N` monomial relations make `A` finite dimensional over `F`, its nilpotent maximal ideal is generated by `X`, and its residue field is `F`. Thus it is local Artinian and `m_A T=m`. The coefficient-direction claim gives the actual k-embedding into `A`, and the coefficient expressions put `E` inside it. [F1, F2, step 6.1, given, construct, algebra]

8.1 We check essential finite type for this **given** k-embedding, which need not be the coefficient embedding. Choose finitely many elements `u_i` generating `F` as a field over `k`, and form the actual subalgebra `B_0=i(k)[sigma(u_i),lambda_j] subset A`. Localize it at its intersection with `m_A`; call the resulting subring `B`. Its residue field is `k(u_i)=F`, and every inverted element is a unit in `A`. For any `a in A`, choose a residue lift in `B`; the remainder is a sum of `lambda_j` times elements of `A`. Replace their residues by elements of `B` and repeat through the finite nilpotence exponent. This expresses `a` in `B`, so `A=B`. Therefore `A` is a localization of a finitely generated k-algebra for the actual k-structure. The argument did not infer this from the unrelated coefficient field `sigma(F)` alone. [F1, F2, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 These subalgebras are directed. Each is essentially of finite type over the given `k`; a finite set of algebra generators before its localization determines it inside `T`, since all its denominators have nonzero residue and are units in any larger local Artinian subalgebra containing those generators. Apply the construction to the generators of two such subalgebras to get a third containing both. Applying it to an arbitrary finite subset of `T` proves that their union is `T`. This completes every assertion. [F1, F2, step 8.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
