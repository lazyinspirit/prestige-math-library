## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC). Let $L/K$ be a finite separable extension of number fields, let $\mathfrak p$ be a finite prime of $K$, and for each prime $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$ let $f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}$ be its residue degree and $d_{\mathfrak P}$ the exponent of the different of the local extension $L_{\mathfrak P}/K_{\mathfrak p}$. Then the relative discriminant ideal satisfies

$$v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak d_{L/K})=\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}d_{\mathfrak P}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice (AC); a finite separable extension $L/K$ of number fields and a finite prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$.

[F1] The relative discriminant ideal is generated by determinants of relative trace Gram matrices of integral $K$-bases. [[def-relative-discriminant-ideal-of-a-number-field-extension]]

[F2] The field tensor product decomposes as $L\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak p}\cong\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}L_{\mathfrak P}$, and the field trace is the sum of the local traces. [[lem-number-field-completions-as-local-polynomial-factors]]

[F3] The local codifferent is the trace-dual set and the different is its inverse once that set is a nonzero fractional ideal. The finite Gram/index proof in steps 2.1 and 3.1 below establishes that definedness and the substantive identity $\delta=f d$; neither is assumed merely from the definition. [[def-different-ideal-of-a-finite-local-extension]] [[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]]

[F4] Rings of integers are finite torsion-free modules over Dedekind domains, and nonzero ideals factor into finitely many prime powers; the Chinese remainder theorem applies to these pairwise comaximal powers. [[thm-finite-torsionfree-modules-over-dedekind-domains]] [[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]] [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]] [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]

[F5] The ring of integers is the integral closure, and completion of the finite module is obtained by the inverse limit of its quotients by powers of $\mathfrak p$. [[thm-finite-integral-closure-in-a-finite-separable-extension]] [[def-completion-of-a-number-field-at-a-prime]]


[A1] The Axiom of Choice is an explicit hypothesis of the completion decomposition [F2], which is used in step 1.1; this proof uses no Countable Choice. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[lem-number-field-completions-as-local-polynomial-factors]]

[F6] The local integer rings are DVRs with finite residue fields, and separable field extensions have nondegenerate trace pairing. [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]] [[thm-trace-form-is-nondegenerate-iff-separable]]

[F7] The adjugate identity holds for integral matrices. [[thm-adjugate-identity-over-a-commutative-ring]]

[F8] Traces are finite sums of conjugates, integral elements form a subring, and a valuation ring is integrally closed. [[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]] [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]] [[thm-valuation-ring-is-integrally-closed]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Factor $\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L=\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}\mathfrak P^{e_{\mathfrak P}}$ by [F4]. The powers $\mathfrak p^r\mathcal O_L$ equal $\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}\mathfrak P^{re_{\mathfrak P}}$, and these exponents are cofinal with the separate $\mathfrak P$-adic powers. Finite CRT identifies the quotients with the corresponding finite product of local quotients. Localizing the finite projective $\mathcal O_K$-module $\mathcal O_L$ at $\mathfrak p$ makes it free: the Dedekind module decomposition [F4] becomes a finite sum of principal fractional ideals over a DVR. Its completion is its tensor product with $A=\mathcal O_{K_{\mathfrak p}}$, by taking coordinates in that finite basis. Passing to the inverse limits therefore gives $$ \mathcal O_L\otimes_{\mathcal O_K}A\cong\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}B_{\mathfrak P},\qquad B_{\mathfrak P}=\mathcal O_{L_{\mathfrak P}}. $$ Each factor is a finite projective $A$-summand of the finite free left-hand side, hence is free over the DVR $A$: embed it in a finite free module and use induction on coordinates and least-valuation generators of image ideals. Its rank is $[L_{\mathfrak P}:K_{\mathfrak p}]$ after tensoring with the fraction field. Thus the required local integral bases actually exist. [A1, F2, F4, F5, F6, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix one factor $B=B_{\mathfrak P}$ with fraction field $E$ over $F=K_{\mathfrak p}$, and choose its $A$-basis $b_i$. Its trace Gram matrix $G$ is integral: products are integral and their traces, sums of integral conjugates lying in $F$, lie in the integrally closed DVR $A$. It is nonsingular by [F6]. In these coordinates the trace-dual set is exactly $B^\vee=G^{-1}A^r$. It is a $B$-module, since for $x\in B^\vee$, $b,c\in B$, one has $\operatorname{Tr}((bx)c)=\operatorname{Tr}(x(bc))\in A$. The adjugate identity bounds it inside $(\det G)^{-1}B$, and it contains $B$. Hence it is a nonzero fractional ideal of the DVR $B$. Write $B^\vee=\pi_B^{-d}B$ with $d\ge0$; its inverse is exactly the different $\pi_B^dB$ of [F3], proving the required well-definedness here. [F3, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Multiplication by $G$ identifies $B^\vee/B$ with $A^r/GA^r$. A finite DVR matrix argument computes its size: choose a nonzero entry of least valuation, use row/column swaps to put it first, and clear its row and column since it divides every entry; repeat on the remaining nonsingular block. Invertible operations do not change the cokernel. The resulting diagonal powers $\pi_A^{a_i}$ give cardinality $\#k_F^{\sum a_i}=\#k_F^{v_F(\det G)}$. But $B^\vee/B=\pi_B^{-d}B/B$ has $d$ successive quotients isomorphic to $k_E$, so its cardinality is $\#k_E^d=\#k_F^{f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}d}$. Thus the local trace-discriminant exponent is precisely $f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}d_{\mathfrak P}$. This is the explicit Gram/index bridge, including $d=0$, rather than a citation to a different definition or an unproved norm identity. [F3, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 After the integral completion in step 1.1, [F2] makes the relative trace pairing an orthogonal direct sum of the local pairings. The local determinant ideal of the relative discriminant is consequently the product of their determinant ideals. Indeed a local free basis computes that ideal: any integral tuple changes its determinant by the square of an integral coordinate determinant, and a local basis can be obtained from integral tuples after clearing denominators outside $\mathfrak p$. Completion preserves its valuation. The Gram matrix of the product basis is block diagonal, so its valuation is the sum of the local valuations. Substituting step 3.1 gives $$ v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak d_{L/K})=\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}d_{\mathfrak P}. $$ No local monogeneity, classification or backward dependency on the later Faltings page is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
