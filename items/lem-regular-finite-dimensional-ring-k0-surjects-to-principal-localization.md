## Statement

Assume AC. Let $A$ be a commutative Noetherian ring of finite Krull dimension such that every localization at a maximal ideal is regular, and let $g\in A$. Then the map
$$K_0(A)\longrightarrow K_0(A_g)$$
induced by localization is surjective. The zero localization is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian finite-dimensional regular ring and element in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] $K_0$ is generated by finite projective modules with direct-sum relations. [[def-algebraic-k0-and-k1-of-a-ring]]

[F2] A regular local ring of dimension at most $d$ has global dimension at most $d$. [[thm-auslander-buchsbaum-serre-regularity-criterion]]

[F3] Kernels of two projective presentations differ by adding the other presentation's projective module. [[thm-schanuel-lemma-in-an-abelian-category]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** descend a finite presentation and resolve its module by finite projectives over the regular ring.

1.1 If $A=0$ then $A_g=0$ and the target is zero, so assume $A\ne0$. Let $P$ be a finite projective $A_g$-module. As $A_g$ is Noetherian, choose a finite presentation $A_g^a\to A_g^b\to P\to0$. Clear the finitely many denominators in its matrix to obtain a finite $A$-module $M$ with $M_g\cong P$. This uses no claim that $P$ itself extends as a projective module. [A1, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put $d=\dim A$. Construct a finite free resolution of $M$ through $d$ steps, with finitely generated $d$th syzygy $N$; Noetherianity makes each kernel finite. For each maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, [F2] gives projective dimension at most $d$ over $A_\mathfrak m$. Repeated [F3] comparison of the localized free resolution with a length-$d$ projective resolution shows $N_\mathfrak m$ is projective. Thus $N$ is locally projective at every maximal ideal. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 A finite presented module which is projective at every maximal localization is projective. Indeed take a finite free surjection $A^r\to N$. At each maximal ideal a local section exists; its finite matrix entries and the equation that it is a section descend to some principal neighborhood $D(f_i)$. Quasi-compactness gives finitely many such neighborhoods covering $\operatorname{Spec}A$. After clearing denominators, suitable positive powers of the $f_i$ still generate the unit ideal, so a finite linear combination of the cleared local sections gives a global section of $A^r\to N$. Therefore $N$ is projective, and step 2.1 is a finite projective resolution of $M$. [step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra]

4.1 Localize the resolution at $g$. Its exact sequences split successively because its terminal module is the projective module $P$, so [F1] gives $[P]=\sum_{i=0}^d(-1)^i[Q_i\otimes_AA_g]$, where $Q_d=N$ and the other $Q_i$ are the chosen finite free modules. Thus every generator of $K_0(A_g)$ lies in the image, proving surjectivity. If $A_g=0$, its $K_0$ is zero and the conclusion is immediate. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
