## Statement

Let $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+$, $\mathcal V$, $\langle V\rangle$, and $p_\hbar:U_\hbar\mathfrak b^+\to\mathcal V$ be as in [[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]], with $R=\mathbb C\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ and $q=e^\hbar$, and let $U\mathfrak n^+$ be the positive half of the Kac–Moody algebra. Assume AC; it is used only to apply the two-sided coideal lemma in step 3.1. For an indeterminate $q'$, let $U_{q'}\mathfrak n^+$ be the algebra over $\mathbb C(q')$ with the same generators and positive quantum Serre relations, with $q_i'=q'^{d_i}$. Then:

(i) The composite $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+\to U_\hbar\mathfrak b^+\xrightarrow{p_\hbar}\mathcal V$ is an isomorphism of $R$-algebras onto $\langle V\rangle$. In particular, $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+$ is a free $R$-module and each root-graded component $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]$ is a finite-rank free $R$-module.

(ii) The classical limit is the classical half: $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+/\hbar U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+\cong U\mathfrak n^+$ as $\mathbb N^I$-graded algebras, by $e_i\mapsto\bar e_i$.

(iii) For every $\alpha\in Q_+$,
$$\operatorname{rank}_{R}U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]=\dim_{\mathbb C}U\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]=\dim_{\mathbb C(q')}U_{q'}\mathfrak n^+[\alpha].$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite symmetrizable Cartan datum and the formal Borel and quantum half of [[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]].

[F1] The formal Borel defines the conditional algebra map $p_\hbar$, gives $\mathcal V=\langle V\rangle\otimes_R A_C$ as an $R$-module, and gives the coproduct on Cartan symbols and words ([[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]).

[F2] For each $i\ne j$, the positive quantum Serre sum vanishes in $\langle V\rangle$; this is part (i) only of [[lem-quantum-serre-sums-vanish-in-the-formal-quantum-shuffle-algebra]]. The supplier's annihilation statement (ii) is not needed for this embedding proof.

[F3] At $q_i=1$, the positive Serre relations present $U\mathfrak n^+$, and the classical Borel is generated by its Cartan and positive simple generators ([[thm-serre-presentation-of-a-kac-moody-algebra]]).

[F4] Under AC, an augmented two-sided coideal ideal $J$ in $U\mathfrak l$ is generated by its primitive intersection $J\cap\mathfrak l$ ([[lem-a-two-sided-coideal-in-an-enveloping-algebra-is-generated-by-its-primitive-part]]).

[F5] The opposite classical Borels are degreewise perfectly paired Lie bialgebras ([[lem-opposite-symmetrizable-kac-moody-borels-are-root-degreewise-dual-lie-bialgebras]]).

[F6] The formal order on $R$ is additive on nonzero products, and $R$ is a domain ([[lem-formal-order-laws]]).

[F7] A finitely generated module over a PID is its torsion submodule plus a finite free summand ([[cor-torsion-splits-from-the-free-part-over-a-pid]]).

[F8] The symmetric Gaussian binomial is defined by the quantum-factorial quotient ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F9] AC is the assertion that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F10] A positive-sized square matrix over a commutative ring is invertible exactly when its determinant is a unit ([[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]], [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]]).

[F11] For a finite-dimensional vector space, the dimension of a quotient by a finite-dimensional subspace is the ambient dimension minus the subspace dimension ([[thm-rank-nullity]]).

[F12] The fraction field of a domain is a field, and every injective map from the domain to a field extends uniquely and injectively to its fraction field ([[def-field-of-fractions]], [[thm-field-of-fractions-is-a-field-and-the-domain-embeds]], [[thm-universal-property-of-the-field-of-fractions]]).

[F13] A PID is a domain in which every ideal is principal ([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]).

[F14] Every finitely generated module over a PID is a finite direct sum of cyclic modules ([[thm-invariant-factor-decomposition-over-a-pid]]).

[F15] The enveloping algebra is the tensor algebra quotient by the Lie-relator ideal, and Lie algebra maps into associative algebras extend uniquely to it ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]], [[thm-universal-property-of-the-universal-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F16] Tensoring is right exact, so a quotient presentation remains a quotient by the scalar-extended relation subspace ([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).

[F17] The standard coproduct on $U\mathfrak b^+$ makes its Lie generators primitive and is cocommutative ([[rem-hopf-algebra-structure-on-the-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F18] A generalized Cartan matrix has a finite nonempty index set $I=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ with $n\ge1$ ([[def-generalized-cartan-matrix]]).

[F19] The symmetrizer entries $d_i$ are positive integers and $q_i=q^{d_i}$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F20] $R=\mathbb C\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ is the formal power-series ring with coefficientwise operations ([[def-formal-power-series-and-coefficient-extraction]]).

[F21] $e^\hbar$ is the formal exponential $\sum_{m\ge0}\hbar^m/m!$ ([[def-formal-exponential-logarithm-and-powers]]).

[F22] A formal power series is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is a unit ([[thm-formal-power-series-unit-criterion]]).

[F23] A submodule of a finite-rank free PID module is finite-rank free ([[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]]).

[F24] Every symmetric Gaussian coefficient is a Laurent polynomial in $q_i$ ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Reduce the conditional shuffle map to the classical Borel, prove that its kernel has no primitive part by graded Lie-bialgebra duality, and then compare finite presentations over $R$ and over the generic field.

1.1 By [F2], the generator assignment of [F1] defines $p_\hbar:U_\hbar\mathfrak b^+\to\mathcal V$. Its restriction to the Cartan coefficient algebra $A_C$ is the identity, and its image contains every one-letter word $[v_i]$. Every element of $\mathcal V=\langle V\rangle\otimes_R A_C$ is a finite sum of products of a letter-generated element and an element of $A_C$, so $p_\hbar$ is surjective. In the symmetric formula, $[m]_i=q_i^{-(m-1)}(1+q_i^2+\cdots+q_i^{2m-2})$ specializes to $m$ at $q_i=1$; therefore $[m]_i!$ specializes to $m!$ and each Gaussian coefficient specializes to $\binom mr$. By [F3], the reduced source is $U\mathfrak b^+$ and reduction defines a surjective graded algebra map $p:U\mathfrak b^+\to\mathcal V_0:=\mathcal V/\hbar\mathcal V$. [F1, F2, F3, F8, F18, algebra]

1.2 The ring $R$ is a PID: if $I\ne0$ is an ideal, the orders of its nonzero elements have a least value $m\in\mathbb N$; choose $f\in I$ of order $m$. It has the form $f=\hbar^m u$ with $u$ a unit by [F22], so $\hbar^m\in I$, while every $g\in I$ has order at least $m$ and is divisible by $\hbar^m$. Thus $I=(\hbar^m)$; the zero ideal is principal as well, and [F6] says $R$ is a domain, so [F13] applies. [F6, F13, F20, F22, given, algebra]

2.1 Each generator $h_i,D_j,e_i$ of the classical Borel has primitive coproduct by [F17], and its image under $p$ is primitive in $\mathcal V_0$: this follows for Cartan symbols from [F1] and for $[v_i]$ by reducing its displayed coproduct modulo $\hbar$. Hence $p$ commutes with coproduct and counit on generators, and therefore on the generated algebra. Thus $p$ is a surjective Hopf map; since $U\mathfrak b^+$ is cocommutative by [F17], so is $\mathcal V_0$. This argument establishes Hopf compatibility only after reduction and makes no Hopf claim about $p_\hbar$. [F1, F3, F17, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $J=\ker p$ and $\mathfrak j=J\cap\mathfrak b^+$. As the kernel of a Hopf map, $J$ is a two-sided ideal and has zero counit. For $x\in J$, $(p\otimes p)\Delta(x)=0$; right exactness of tensor products and surjectivity of $p$ give $\ker(p\otimes p)=J\otimes U\mathfrak b^++U\mathfrak b^+\otimes J$, so $J$ is a coideal. Under the stated AC hypothesis, apply [F4] to obtain $J=U\mathfrak b^+\mathfrak j=\mathfrak j U\mathfrak b^+$ with $\mathfrak j$ a Lie ideal. The universal property [F15] identifies the quotient $U\mathfrak b^+/J$ with $U\mathfrak a$, where $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak b^+/\mathfrak j$, and $p$ with the quotient map. AC is used here only through [F4]; all other choices below are finite-dimensional or explicitly specified. [F4, F9, F15, F16, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 The first-order skew part $\delta(\bar x)=((\Delta(x)-\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(x))/\hbar)\bmod\hbar$ of the formal Borel coproduct is well-defined on $\mathcal V_0$: its numerator reduces to zero by step 2.1, and changing a lift by $\hbar y$ changes the quotient by $\Delta(y)-\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}(y)$, which is zero modulo $\hbar$ because $\mathcal V_0$ is cocommutative. The quotient is unique because $R$ is a domain and the coefficientwise word–Cartan module is $\hbar$-torsion-free. To see this, each $\langle V\rangle[\gamma]$ is a submodule of a finite free word module and hence finite free by [F23] and step 1.2; tensoring each such component with the coefficientwise torsion-free $A_C$ in [F1] preserves $\hbar$-torsion-freeness, as do its coefficientwise completed tensor powers. Put $D=\Delta-\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$ and let $c$ cyclically permute three tensor factors. Coassociativity gives $(1+c+c^2)(D\otimes\operatorname{id})D=0$: expanding $D$ gives four permutations of $(\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta=(\operatorname{id}\otimes\Delta)\Delta$, whose cyclic sums cancel in pairs. Since $D$ is divisible by $\hbar$, division by $\hbar^2$ and reduction prove co-Jacobi for $\delta$, and flip gives antisymmetry. For primitive $a,b\in\mathcal V_0$, subtracting the two algebra-map commutator identities for $\Delta$ and $\Delta^{\mathrm{op}}$, dividing by $\hbar$, and reducing gives $\delta([a,b])=[a\otimes1+1\otimes a,\delta(b)]-[b\otimes1+1\otimes b,\delta(a)]$, the required $1$-cocycle rule. Directly from [F1], $\delta(\bar h_i)=\delta(\bar D_j)=0$ and $\delta(\overline{[v_i]})=d_i\overline{[v_i]}\wedge\bar h_i$. These values lie in $\bigwedge^2\mathfrak a$; since $\mathfrak a$ is generated by their images and the cobracket is a $1$-cocycle, $\delta$ restricts to a Lie-bialgebra cobracket on $\mathfrak a$. The transposed bracket in [F5] gives the same formulas on $\mathfrak b^+$: its Cartan cobracket is zero and its explicit rescaled-Manin normalization gives $\delta_{\mathfrak b^+}(e_i)=d_i e_i\wedge h_i$. Thus $p|_{\mathfrak b^+}:\mathfrak b^+\to\mathfrak a$ is a Lie-bialgebra map, because both cobrackets obey the $1$-cocycle rule and agree on the Cartan and simple generators. [F1, F5, F23, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The graded dual $p^*:\mathfrak a^{\mathrm{gr}\prime}\hookrightarrow(\mathfrak b^+)^{\mathrm{gr}\prime}\cong\mathfrak b^-$ is injective and is a Lie-algebra map by step 3.2 and [F5]; every root component is finite-dimensional. Its image contains the full Cartan subalgebra because $p$ maps the classical Cartan basis to the independent polynomial Cartan variables, and it contains each $f_i$ because $p(e_i)=\overline{[v_i]}\ne0$ in the one-dimensional simple-root component. Since $\mathfrak b^-$ is generated by its Cartan and the $f_i$ by the separate Serre presentation [F3], the image is all of $\mathfrak b^-$. Dualizing each finite-dimensional root component shows $p|_{\mathfrak b^+}$ is an isomorphism, so $\mathfrak j=0$ and $p:U\mathfrak b^+\to\mathcal V_0$ is an isomorphism. [F1, F3, F5, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 The map $p$ preserves the $\mathbb N^I$ grading and sends $e_i$ to $\overline{[v_i]}$. The module decomposition in [F1] identifies the image of $\langle V\rangle$ in $\mathcal V_0$ with $\langle V\rangle/\hbar\langle V\rangle$. Restricting the isomorphism of step 4.1 to the positive subalgebra proves $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+/\hbar U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+\cong U\mathfrak n^+$ and identifies that classical half with $\langle V\rangle/\hbar\langle V\rangle$. [F1, F3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Fix $\alpha\in Q_+$. Since $I$ is finite by [F18], the word space $\mathrm{Sh}(V)[\alpha]$ has finitely many words and is finite free over $R$, so $N_\alpha:=\langle V\rangle[\alpha]$ is finite-rank free by [F23]. The presented component $M_\alpha:=U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]$ is finitely generated, since it is a quotient of the finite free span of words of degree $\alpha$. By [F7] and [F14] over the PID of step 1.2, write $M_\alpha\cong R^p\oplus T$, with $T\cong\bigoplus_{j=1}^tR/(\hbar^{m_j})$ for positive integers $m_j$: every nonzero nonunit of $R$ is a unit times a power of $\hbar$. Since $M_\alpha/\hbar M_\alpha\cong U\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]$ by step 5.1, if $d=\dim_\mathbb C U\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]$, then $d=p+t$. [F3, F7, F14, F18, F23, step 1.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The surjection $p_\hbar$ restricts to $M_\alpha\twoheadrightarrow N_\alpha$, and $N_\alpha$ has rank $d$ because its reduction is the classical component in step 5.1. The target is torsion-free, so this map kills $T$; reducing the induced surjection $R^p\twoheadrightarrow N_\alpha$ modulo $\hbar$ gives $p\ge d$. Together with $d=p+t$, this forces $t=0$ and $p=d$. At $\alpha=0$, both components are $R\cdot1$ and the map sends unit to unit. If $d=0$, the decomposition gives $M_\alpha=N_\alpha=0$. For $d>0$, choose bases: the surjection is a square matrix whose determinant reduces to a nonzero determinant over $\mathbb C$, hence is a unit by [F22]; [F10] makes it invertible. Therefore $M_\alpha\to N_\alpha$ is an isomorphism for every $\alpha$, proving (i) and the first equality in (iii). [F7, F10, F22, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Evaluation $q'\mapsto e^\hbar$ embeds $\mathbb C[q']$ into $R$: for a nonzero polynomial, factor out its maximal power of $(q'-1)$; the remaining factor has nonzero value at $1$, while $e^\hbar-1\ne0$, so its evaluation is nonzero in the domain $R$. By [F19] and [F21], this substitution sends $q_i'=q'^{d_i}$ to $e^{d_i\hbar}$; [F12] extends the injection to $\mathbb C(q')\hookrightarrow K$. For fixed $\alpha$, the relations in that degree are the columns of a finite matrix over $\mathbb C[q'^{\pm1}]$; finiteness follows because there are finitely many words of degree $\alpha$, and [F24] makes every Gaussian entry Laurent polynomial. Substituting $q'=e^\hbar$ gives the formal presentation matrix for $M_\alpha$. By [F16], its quotient after extension to $K$ is the quotient by the same specialized columns. A field embedding preserves which matrix minors vanish, so the generic and formal matrices have the same rank; [F11] and step 7.1 give $\dim_{\mathbb C(q')}U_{q'}\mathfrak n^+[\alpha]=\dim_K(M_\alpha\otimes_RK)=d$. This proves the remaining equality in (iii) and completes all assertions. The theorem is not an iff statement, so there are no reverse implications to prove. [F6, F11, F12, F16, F19, F20, F21, F24, step 1.2, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
