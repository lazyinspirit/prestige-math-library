## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a number field and let $S$ be a finite set of finite primes of $K$. Write $R=\mathcal O_{K,S}$ and let $S_L$ denote the primes of a finite extension $L/K$ above $S$. There is a finite extension $L/K$ such that the extension map $\operatorname{Pic}(R)\to\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_{L,S_L})$ sends every class to zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a number field $K$, and a finite set $S$ of finite primes.

[A1] AC states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The Picard group of the ring of $S$-integers of a number field is finite, and that ring is Dedekind. [[lem-finiteness-of-picard-group-of-s-integers]]

[F2] For a Dedekind domain, the Picard group is the group of nonzero fractional ideals modulo principal fractional ideals. [[thm-ideal-class-group-is-the-picard-group]] [[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]]

[F3] Every nonzero fractional ideal of a Dedekind domain has a unique finite prime-ideal factorization. [[thm-unique-factorisation-of-ideals-in-dedekind-domains]]

[F4] Every element of a finite group has finite order. [[thm-lagrange]]

## Proof
1.1 By [F1], $\operatorname{Pic}(R)$ is a finite group. For each of its finitely many classes choose a nonzero fractional ideal $I_j$ representing it, using [F2]. By [F4], the class of $I_j$ has some finite order $m_j\ge1$, so there is $a_j\in K^\times$ with $I_j^{m_j}=(a_j)$ as fractional ideals of $R$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given, choose]

2.1 In an algebraic closure of $K$, choose $\beta_j$ with $\beta_j^{m_j}=a_j$ for each of the finitely many indices $j$, and let $L=K(\beta_j)_j$. This is a finite extension because it is generated by finitely many algebraic elements. [A1, step 1.1, choose, construct]

3.1 Let $\mathfrak Q$ be any nonzero prime ideal of $\mathcal O_{L,S_L}$. Extending $I_j^{m_j}=(a_j)$ to this Dedekind domain and taking $\mathfrak Q$-valuations gives $m_jv_{\mathfrak Q}(I_j\mathcal O_{L,S_L})=v_{\mathfrak Q}(a_j)=v_{\mathfrak Q}(\beta_j^{m_j})=m_jv_{\mathfrak Q}(\beta_j)$. Dividing by $m_j$ shows that $I_j\mathcal O_{L,S_L}$ and $(\beta_j)$ have equal valuation at every prime. By unique factorization [F3], they are equal fractional ideals. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Step 3.1 makes every representative class from step 1.1 principal after extension to $\mathcal O_{L,S_L}$. These representatives exhaust $\operatorname{Pic}(R)$, so the extension map on Picard groups is zero. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
