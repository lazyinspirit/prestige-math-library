## Statement

Let $F$ be a complete discretely valued field of characteristic zero with finite residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$ and cardinality $q$. Fix $\pi$, put $e=v_F(p)$, $E=e/(p-1)$, $C=pE$, $u=p/\pi^e$, and $U_i=1+\pi^i\mathcal O_F$. The graded $p$th-power maps on the additive residue coordinates are
$$a\mapsto a^p\quad(i<E),\qquad a\mapsto\bar u\,a\quad(i>E),\qquad \Psi(a)=a^p+\bar u\,a\quad(i=E\in\mathbb Z),$$
with respective target depths $pi,i+e,C$. For every integer $N>C$, one has $U_N\subseteq(U_{N-e})^p$. If $E$ is integral, $\Psi$ has kernel and cokernel of order $p$ exactly when $F$ contains a nontrivial $p$th root of unity, and otherwise is bijective. Such a root cannot occur if $E$ is not integral.

Let $\Theta$ be the finite multiplicative representatives of $k^\times$. The quotient $U_1/U_1^p$ is generated by the classes of $1-\theta\pi^i$ for $\theta\in\Theta$, $1\le i<C$, $p\nmid i$, and additionally a single class $\omega=1-\theta_*\pi^C$ only when $E\in\mathbb Z$ and $\mu_p\subset F$, where $-\bar\theta_*$ represents a nonzero element of $k/\Psi(k)$. Each noncritical generator can equivalently be replaced by $(1-\theta\pi^i)^i$. These assertions include $p=2$, make no uniqueness claim for the generator coordinates, and use no choice axiom.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the data in the Statement.

[F1] The field is complete with its supplied discrete valuation, finite residue field and uniformizer. [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]

[F2] The finite multiplicative representatives exist and are unique residue lifts; they need not be an additive coefficient field in mixed characteristic. [[lem-finite-residue-teichmuller-lifts-and-equal-characteristic-laurent-coordinates]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Since $k$ has characteristic $p$, $e=v_F(p)$ is positive; integers prime to $p$ are units. The maps $1+a\pi^i\mapsto\bar a$ identify $U_i/U_{i+1}$ with the additive group of $k$, because products add leading coefficients. For $1\le j<p$, $\binom pj$ has valuation $e$. In the expansion of $(1+a\pi^i)^p$, the possible least terms are $pa\pi^i$ and $a^p\pi^{pi}$; all intermediate terms have larger valuation. This proves the three displayed graded maps, including when a leading coefficient vanishes. Changing an input by $U_{i+1}$ changes the output only beyond the target depth, so the maps are well-defined on the indicated quotients. The intermediate sum is empty when $p=2$, and the same calculation applies. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix an integer $N>C$ and $\epsilon\in U_N$. Starting with $y_N=1$, maintain $\epsilon y_n^{-p}\in U_n$ for $n\ge N$. If its residue coefficient at depth $n$ is $a_n$, choose the unique representative $c_n$ of $\bar u^{-1}a_n$ in the fixed finite set from [F2], and set $y_{n+1}=y_n(1+c_n\pi^{n-e})$. Since $n-e>E$, the second graded map of step 1.1 makes the new error lie in $U_{n+1}$. A zero coefficient gives the factor $1$. These factors have depths tending to infinity, so their partial products converge to $y\in U_{N-e}$ by completeness. Taking limits of the errors gives $y^p=\epsilon$. This is a specified recursion using finite representatives and unique limits, not a new choice at every depth. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $\zeta\ne1$ and $\zeta^p=1$. Its residue is $1$, since in characteristic $p$ the only $p$th root of $1$ is $1$. Put $x=\zeta-1$ and $r=v_F(x)>0$. In $$p+\binom p2x+\cdots+x^{p-1}=0$$ the intermediate terms have values strictly above $e$, whereas the last term has value $(p-1)r$. The least value can occur twice only when $r=E$, so $E$ is integral. The leading coefficient of $x$ is a nonzero element of $\ker\Psi$. Conversely a nonzero $a\in\ker\Psi$ makes $z=1+[a]\pi^E$ satisfy $z^p\in U_{C+1}$. Step 2.1 supplies $y\in U_{E+1}$ with $y^p=z^p$. Then $z/y$ is a nontrivial $p$th root, since its first term at depth $E$ is nonzero. This proves the root criterion for every $p$, including $2$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The additive map $\Psi$ is $\mathbb F_p$-linear. If it has one nonzero kernel element $a$, then $\Psi(ab)=a^p(b^p-b)$, so its kernel is $a\mathbb F_p$ of order $p$. Otherwise its kernel has order one. For an endomorphism of the finite additive group of $k$, kernel and cokernel have the same order. Thus its cokernel has order $p$ exactly in the root case of step 3.1, and it is bijective otherwise. In the root case any fixed $-\bar\theta_*$ outside the image generates its one-dimensional $\mathbb F_p$ cokernel. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Reduce any principal unit successively through the finitely many integer depths up to $C$. At a depth $i<C$ divisible by $p$, one has $i/p<E$ and the first graded map is the bijective Frobenius on the finite field; an actual $p$th power of a unit of depth $i/p$ removes that coefficient. At a depth $i<C$ not divisible by $p$, remove the coefficient using a unit $1-\theta\pi^i$ from [F2]. At an integral critical depth $C$, remove its image component by the $p$th power of a depth-$E$ unit; step 4.1 leaves only a power $\omega^j$, $0\le j<p$, in the root case, and no component otherwise. If $C$ is not integral there is no critical step; $C$ is integral exactly when $E$ is, since $p$ and $p-1$ are coprime. The final remainder has integer depth greater than $C$ and is a $p$th power by step 2.1. This proves the claimed finite generation modulo $U_1^p$. Finally $U_1/U_1^p$ has exponent $p$, so raising a generator to an integer $i$ prime to $p$ does not change the subgroup it generates. No basis or uniqueness assertion follows or is used. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
