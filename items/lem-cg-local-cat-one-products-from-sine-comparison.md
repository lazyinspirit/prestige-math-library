## Statement

**(i) The $l^2$ product.** For metric spaces $(X,d_X)$ and $(Y,d_Y)$ let $X\times Y$ carry the $l^2$ product metric $d((x,y),(x',y')):=(d_X(x,x')^2+d_Y(y,y')^2)^{1/2}$ ([[def-metric-space]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]). If $X$ and $Y$ are locally CAT(1), then $X\times Y$ is locally CAT(1) ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]). More precisely, if $B(x,r)\subseteq X$ and $B(y,r)\subseteq Y$ are CAT(1) and convex for some $r<\pi/2$, then every triangle in the product chart $B(x,r)\times B(y,r)$ whose perimeter is $<2\pi$ satisfies the CAT(1) inequality; the same holds with either factor replaced by a Euclidean space.

**(ii) Model vertex-to-side comparison in $S^2\times S^2$.** Let $v\colon[0,1]\to S^2\times S^2$ be a constant-speed geodesic segment, let $p\in S^2\times S^2$, put $a=d(p,v(0))$, $b=d(p,v(1))$, $V=d(v(0),v(1))$ and assume the closed curve formed by $v$ and geodesic segments from $p$ has perimeter $<2\pi$. Then for all $t\in[0,1]$,
$$\cos d(p,v(t))\ \ge\ \frac{\sin((1-t)V)\cos a+\sin(tV)\cos b}{\sin V},\qquad V>0,$$
the right-hand side being the cosine of the vertex-to-side distance of the spherical comparison triangle with side lengths $a,b,V$; for $V=0$ the inequality reduces to $d(p,v(t))\le a$.

**(iii) Reduction to the model.** For a triangle in a product of CAT(1) spaces whose projections to the two factors are geodesic segments, comparison with the paired factor-model triangles and the model inequality (ii) yields the CAT(1) vertex-to-side inequality for the product triangle; the vertex-to-side comparisons together with the spherical law of cosines ([[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]]) give all side-point comparisons, and hence the CAT(1) inequality on sufficiently small product charts. No smooth-manifold comparison, curvature tensor or CAT(0) squared-distance surrogate is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Locally CAT(1) spaces $X,Y$ with CAT(1) convex balls $B(x,r),B(y,r)$, $r<\pi/2$; for clause (ii) a constant-speed geodesic $v$ in $S^2\times S^2$, a point $p$, and the numbers $a,b,V$.

[F1] [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]: CAT(1) means that every pair of points at distance $<\pi$ is joined by a geodesic segment and that every geodesic triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ satisfies $d(x,y)\le d_S(\bar x,\bar y)$ for all points $x,y$ of the triangle; locally CAT(1) means that every point has a CAT(1) closed ball $\bar B(x,r)$, with the induced metric; $d_S(x,y)=\arccos(x\cdot y)$ on $S^2$.

[F2] [[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]]: for side lengths satisfying the triangle inequalities with perimeter $<2\pi$ a comparison triangle in $S^2$ exists and is unique up to isometry; the spherical cosine rule $\cos c=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b\cos\gamma$ holds for a triangle of $S^2$ with sides $a,b,c<\pi$ and angle $\gamma$ opposite $c$; balls of radius $<\pi/2$ in a CAT(1) space are convex with unique geodesics.

[F3] [[def-metric-space]]: the metric axioms, the triangle inequality, and the product metric $d^2=d_X^2+d_Y^2$ on $X\times Y$.

[F4] [[def-metric-ball]]: $B(x,r)=\{y:d(x,y)<r\}$, and a subset of a geodesic space is convex when it contains a geodesic segment between any two of its points.

[F5] [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]: the Euclidean metric on $\mathbb R^n$ and its $l^2$ form.

[F6] [[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]]: $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$ and, more generally, the spheres $S^{n-1}$ as subsets of Euclidean space.

[F7] [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]: the Euclidean inner product induces the norm; $|\langle u,v\rangle|\le\|u\|\,\|v\|$; consequently $\sum_j\sqrt{a_j^2+b_j^2}\ge\sqrt{(\sum_ja_j)^2+(\sum_jb_j)^2}$ for nonnegative reals, and $(R')^2\le\sum_i(r_i')^2$ when $R^2=\sum_ir_i^2$.

[F8] [[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]], [[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]]: sine and cosine are continuous and differentiable with the addition formulas, $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos0=1$ and $\cos\pi=-1$, $\sin>0$ on $(0,\pi)$, and $\pi>0$ is the first positive zero of sine.

[F9] [[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]], [[def-derivative]], [[thm-chain-rule]]: $\sin'=\cos$, $\cos'=-\sin$, and the chain rule for composites of differentiable functions.

[F10] [[thm-extreme-value-metric]]: a continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact metric space attains its minimum.

[F11] $C^2$ has the meaning of [[def-higher-derivatives-and-smoothness]]. At an interior minimum of a $C^2$ function its first derivative is zero by [[thm-fermat-interior-extremum]], and its second derivative is nonnegative: a negative value would make it a strict local maximum by [[thm-second-derivative-test]]. Differentiation of sums, products and quotients is supplied by [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]; the mean-value theorem is [[cor-mean-value-theorem]]. The inverse trigonometric derivatives are [[thm-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]; applying [[thm-derivative-of-an-inverse]] to $u\mapsto u^2$ on $(0,\infty)$ gives $(\sqrt z)'=1/(2\sqrt z)$, which can be differentiated again there. Thus the positive nonantipodal radial functions below are $C^2$.



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Product geodesics.** Let $\gamma=(\gamma_1,\gamma_2)$ be a constant-speed geodesic segment in $X_1\times X_2$ with endpoints $P,Q$, so that $d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=(t-s)L$ for $0\le s\le t\le1$ with $L=d(P,Q)$; let $\ell_i$ be the length of $\gamma_i$ and put $L_i:=d_i(P_i,Q_i)$. For every partition $0=t_0<\dots<t_m=1$, the Minkowski inequality of [F7] gives $\sum_jd(\gamma(t_{j-1}),\gamma(t_j))\ge\sqrt{(\sum_jd_1(\gamma_1(t_{j-1}),\gamma_1(t_j)))^2+(\sum_jd_2(\gamma_2(t_{j-1}),\gamma_2(t_j)))^2}$, taking common refinements of partitions approximating both component lengths gives $L\ge\sqrt{\ell_1^2+\ell_2^2}$; since $\ell_i\ge L_i$ and $L=\sqrt{L_1^2+L_2^2}$, it follows that $\ell_i=L_i$ for both $i$. Hence each $\gamma_i$ has length equal to the distance between its endpoints, so $\gamma_i$ minimizes between its endpoints. For the partition $0,s,t,1$, equality in the Euclidean triangle inequality for the nonnegative component-distance vectors is forced by the product-geodesic equality. Its equality case, from [F7], makes each vector proportional to $(L_1,L_2)$; the middle vector has norm $(t-s)L$, hence $d_i(\gamma_i(s),\gamma_i(t))=(t-s)L_i$: the projections are geodesics with constant speeds $L_i$ and $L^2=L_1^2+L_2^2$. Conversely, if the $\gamma_i$ are constant-speed geodesics with proportional parametrizations and speeds $L_i$, then $d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))^2=\sum_i(t-s)^2L_i^2=(t-s)^2L^2$, so $\gamma$ is a constant-speed geodesic. [F3, F5, F7, algebra]

1.2 **Sine comparison on a short interval.** Let $0<V<\pi$ and let $F\in C^2([0,1])$ satisfy $F''+V^2F\le0$. Put $H(t)=(\sin((1-t)V)F(0)+\sin(tV)F(1))/\sin V$ and $G=F-H$; then $G(0)=G(1)=0$ and $G''+V^2G\le0$. Choose $K=(V+\pi)/2$ and the positive function $w(t)=\cos(K(t-1/2))$. If $G$ were negative somewhere, $G/w$ would attain a negative minimum at an interior $t_0$; there $(G/w)'=0$ and $(G/w)''\ge0$ by [F11]. Writing $G=w(G/w)$ gives $G''+V^2G=w(G/w)''+2w'(G/w)'+(V^2-K^2)w(G/w)>0$ at $t_0$, because $K>V$, $w>0$, and $G/w<0$, a contradiction. Hence $F\ge H$. The required $V<\pi$ in the model follows from the triangle inequality $V\le a+b$ and $a+b+V<2\pi$. [F8, F9, F10, F11, algebra]

2.1 **Radial identities in the model.** Let $M=M_1\times\dots\times M_m$ where each factor $M_i$ is either the round sphere $S^2$ with metric $d_S$ or a Euclidean space $\mathbb R^{k_i}$ with its metric ([F5], [F6]), and let $v\colon[0,1]\to M$ be a constant-speed geodesic segment with factor curves $v_i$ of speeds $V_i$ and $V^2=\sum_iV_i^2$, as in step 1.1. Fix $p\in M$, write $r_i(t):=d_{M_i}(p_i,v_i(t))$ and $R(t):=(\sum_ir_i(t)^2)^{1/2}=d_M(p,v(t))$, and assume first that $r_i(t)>0$ for all $i$ and $t$. Then $R$ is $V$-Lipschitz, so $R(t)\le\min\{a+tV,b+(1-t)V\}$ where $a=R(0)$ and $b=R(1)$, and under the perimeter hypothesis $a+b+V<2\pi$ one has $R(t)\le\frac12(a+b+V)<\pi$; also $|r_i'|\le V_i$. If $M_i=S^2$ put $f_i(u):=u\cot u$ and $A_i:=f_i(r_i)$, while for a Euclidean factor put $f_i\equiv1$ and $A_i:=1$. Differentiating the relation $\cos r_i(t)=p_i\cdot v_i(t)$ twice along the great circle $v_i$ gives $r_i''=\cot r_i\,(V_i^2-(r_i')^2)$, that is $r_ir_i''=A_i(V_i^2-(r_i')^2)$, by [F9], [F8] and [F1]; in the Euclidean factor the same identity $r_ir_i''=V_i^2-(r_i')^2$ follows by differentiating $r_i^2=|p_i-v_i(t)|^2$ twice for an affine $v_i$ ([F5], [F9]). Summing the identities over $i$ and using $R^2=\sum_ir_i^2$, so that $RR''=\sum_i(r_i')^2+\sum_ir_ir_i''-(R')^2$, one gets $RR''=\sum_iA_iV_i^2+\sum_i(1-A_i)(r_i')^2-(R')^2$. [F1, F5, F6, F8, F9, F11, algebra]

3.1 **A differential inequality for $R$.** In the situation of step 2.1 put $f(R):=R\cot R$. Subtracting $f(R)(V^2-(R')^2)$ from the identity of step 2.1 gives $RR''-f(R)(V^2-(R')^2)=\sum_i(A_i-f(R))(V_i^2-(r_i')^2)+(1-f(R))(\sum_i(r_i')^2-(R')^2)$, as expanding the right-hand side and using $\sum_iV_i^2=V^2$ reproduces the left-hand side. Each factor is nonnegative: $V_i^2-(r_i')^2\ge0$ because $r_i$ is $V_i$-Lipschitz; $A_i\ge f(R)$ because either $A_i=f_i(r_i)$ with $r_i\le R$ and $f_i(u)=u\cot u$ decreasing on $(0,\pi)$ — indeed $f_i'(u)=(\sin u\cos u-u)/\sin^2u<0$: the function $u-\sin u\cos u$ vanishes at zero and has derivative $2\sin^2u>0$ on $(0,\pi)$, so [F11]'s mean-value theorem makes it positive — or $A_i=1\ge f(R)$, valid because $\sin u-u\cos u$ vanishes at zero and has derivative $u\sin u>0$ on $(0,\pi)$, so the same theorem gives $u\cot u<1$; and $1-f(R)\ge0$ together with $\sum_i(r_i')^2\ge(R')^2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz [F7]. Hence $RR''-f(R)(V^2-(R')^2)\ge0$. [step 2.1, F7, F8, F9, F11, algebra]

4.1 **The comparison function satisfies the differential inequality.** With $F:=\cos R$ one computes $F'=-\sin R\,R'$ and $F''=-\cos R\,(R')^2-\sin R\,R''$, so $(\sin R/R)(RR''-f(R)(V^2-(R')^2))=\sin R\,R''-\cos R\,(V^2-(R')^2)=-(F''+V^2F)$, because $Rf(R)=R^2\cot R$ and $\sin R>0$; since $0<R<\pi$, step 3.1 gives $F''+V^2F\le0$ wherever $R>0$. [step 3.1, F8, F9, F11, algebra]

5.1 **The model inequality (ii).** In the situation of steps 2.1–3.1 with $V>0$ and $r_i>0$ everywhere, step 1.2 applied to $F=\cos R$ with $F(0)=\cos a$, $F(1)=\cos b$ gives $\cos R(t)\ge(\sin((1-t)V)\cos a+\sin(tV)\cos b)/\sin V$, whose right-hand side is $\cos d_{S^2}(\bar p,\bar v(t))$ for the spherical comparison triangle with side lengths $a,b,V$ and the comparison point $\bar v(t)$ at parameter $t$ on the side of length $V$, by the cosine rule [F2]; since $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ and both arguments lie in $[0,\pi]$ ([F8]), $d(p,v(t))\le d_{S^2}(\bar p,\bar v(t))$. If some factor distance vanishes at some time, choose approximating data $p^{\epsilon}$ with every $r_i^{\epsilon}>0$ throughout: for a spherical factor the trace of $v_i$ has empty interior in $S^2$, so $p_i^{\epsilon}$ can be chosen arbitrarily close to $p_i$ outside it; for a Euclidean factor, first embed $\mathbb R^{k_i}$ isometrically in $\mathbb R^{k_i+1}$ and displace $p_i$ by $\epsilon$ in the new orthogonal direction, making its distance to the entire trace positive; the inequality proved for the approximants passes to the limit because $R$, $a$, $b$ and $V$ depend continuously on $(p,v(0),v(1))$ and on $t\mapsto v(t)$ ([F3]). For $V=0$ the geodesic $v$ is constant and $d(p,v(t))=a$ for all $t$, which is the asserted inequality. [step 1.2, step 4.1, F2, F3, F8, algebra]

6.1 **Vertex-to-side inequality in a product chart.** Let $B(x,r)\subseteq X$, $B(y,r)\subseteq Y$ be CAT(1) and convex, $r<\pi/2$, let $(P,Q,R)$ be a geodesic triangle in the chart $B(x,r)\times B(y,r)$ with perimeter $<2\pi$, and let $U$ be a point of the side $[Q,R]$, say at parameter $t$ from $Q$. By step 1.1 the factor projections of the sides are geodesic segments in $X$ and $Y$ lying in the convex balls $B(x,r),B(y,r)$ ([F4]), and $U_i$ lies on $[Q_i,R_i]$ at the same parameter $t$. The factor triangles $(P_i,Q_i,R_i)$ have perimeter at most the perimeter of $(P,Q,R)$, hence $<2\pi$, so the CAT(1) inequality of [F1] applies to the pair $(P_i,U_i)$: $d_i(P_i,U_i)\le d_{S^2}(P_i',U_i')$, where $P_i',U_i'$ are the corresponding points of the spherical comparison triangle of $(P_i,Q_i,R_i)$. Therefore $d(P,U)^2=\sum_id_i(P_i,U_i)^2\le\sum_id_{S^2}(P_i',U_i')^2$. By step 1.1 the pairing $t\mapsto(U_1',U_2')$ is a constant-speed geodesic in $S^2\times S^2$ from $(Q_1',Q_2')$ to $(R_1',R_2')$ of length $\sqrt{\sum_id_i(Q_i,R_i)^2}=d(Q,R)$, while $\sqrt{\sum_id_{S^2}(P_i',Q_i')^2}=d(P,Q)$ and likewise for $R$. Since the perimeter hypothesis is unchanged, the model inequality of step 5.1 applies with $p:=(P_1',P_2')$, $a=d(P,Q)$, $b=d(P,R)$ and $V=d(Q,R)$: $d(P,U)\le d_{S^2\times S^2}((P_1',P_2'),(U_1',U_2'))\le d_{S^2}(\bar P,\bar U)$, where $(\bar P,\bar Q,\bar R)$ is the spherical comparison triangle of $(P,Q,R)$ and $\bar U$ is the comparison point of $U$. [step 1.1, step 5.1, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

7.1 **All side-point comparisons.** With the notation of step 6.1 let now $U$ lie on $[P,Q]$ and $V$ on $[P,R]$. The triangle $(P,U,R)$ has perimeter at most that of $(P,Q,R)$, so step 6.1 applies to it at the vertex $U$ and the point $V$ of the side $[P,R]$: $d(U,V)\le d_{S^2}(U'',V'')$, where $U'',V''$ are the corresponding points of the comparison triangle of $(P,U,R)$. That comparison triangle and the configuration $(\bar P,\bar U,\bar V)$ of the big comparison triangle have equal legs $|PU|$ and $|PV|$ from the vertex $P$, while their opposite sides satisfy $|U''R''|=d(U,R)\le d_{S^2}(\bar U,\bar R)$, the latter by step 6.1 applied to the big triangle at the vertex $R$ with the point $U$ of the side $[P,Q]$. For fixed legs the angle at the vertex of a spherical triangle is an increasing function of the opposite side, because the cosine rule of [F2] gives $\cos\gamma=(\cos c-\cos a\cos b)/(\sin a\sin b)$ and $\cos$ is decreasing ([F8]); hence the angle at $P''$ is at most the angle at $\bar P$, and applying the cosine rule to both configurations gives $\cos d_{S^2}(U'',V'')\ge\cos d_{S^2}(\bar U,\bar V)$, that is $d(U,V)\le d_{S^2}(\bar U,\bar V)$. Pairs on the same side satisfy equality because the comparison map is an isometry on each side, and pairs on two sides sharing a vertex reduce to the treated case after relabelling the triangle; thus every pair of points of $(P,Q,R)$ satisfies the CAT(1) inequality. [step 6.1, F2, F8, algebra]

8.1 **Local CAT(1) of products.** Let $B(x,r)\subseteq X$, $B(y,r)\subseteq Y$ be CAT(1) and convex with $r<\pi/2$: the chart $B(x,r)\times B(y,r)$ is convex in $X\times Y$, because a product geodesic between two of its points has factor geodesics lying in the convex factor balls by step 1.1, so the induced metric on the chart agrees with the restricted product metric. Every pair of points of the chart at distance $<\pi$ has product distance with factor components $<\pi$ and is joined by the product of the factor geodesics inside the chart, and every geodesic triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ in the chart satisfies the CAT(1) inequality by steps 6.1 and 7.1; hence the chart is a CAT(1) space. Choose $0<\rho<\min\{r,\pi/2\}$; the closed product ball of radius $\rho$ about $(x,y)$ lies in this CAT(1) chart and is convex by [F2], hence is itself CAT(1). Therefore $X\times Y$ is locally CAT(1) whenever $X$ and $Y$ are, and if $B(x,r),B(y,r)$ are CAT(1) and convex then every triangle in the chart of perimeter $<2\pi$ satisfies the CAT(1) inequality, the same argument applying verbatim when a factor is Euclidean (steps 2.1 and 5.1 treat the flat case). This proves (i), (ii) and (iii). [step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, F1, F4] ∎
