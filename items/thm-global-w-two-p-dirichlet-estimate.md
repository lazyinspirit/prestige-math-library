## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$, $1<p<\infty$, let $\Omega$ be a bounded $C^{1,1}$ domain, and let $L=a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+b^i\partial_i+c$ be uniformly elliptic on $\Omega$ with $a^{ij}\in C^0(\bar\Omega)$, $\|b\|_\infty+\|c\|_\infty\le M$, and ellipticity constants $\lambda,\Lambda$. Then there is $C<\infty$, depending on $n,p,\lambda,\Lambda,M,\Omega$ and the modulus of continuity of $A$, such that every $u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$ satisfies
$$\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\le C\bigl(\|Lu\|_{L^p(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\bigr).$$
The estimate is a priori and asserts neither solvability nor weak-to-strong regularity: the function is assumed to lie in $W^{2,p}$ with zero trace.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice and $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $n\ge2$, $1<p<\infty$, the bounded $C^{1,1}$ domain $\Omega$, the operator $L$ with the stated bounds, and $u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is inherited by the half-space trace, extension and Lipschitz/Sobolev interfaces; Countable Choice is inherited by the estimate and measure interfaces. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) All covers and bump families are finite and explicitly exhibited. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] The spaces $W^{2,p}$ and $W^{1,p}_0$ are those of [[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]] and [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]; in particular, $W^{1,p}_0(\Omega)$ is the $W^{1,p}$ closure of $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, and multiplication by a smooth compactly supported cutoff preserves that closure, by multiplying the approximating test functions. ([[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]])

[F2] Interior $W^{2,p}$ estimate ([[thm-interior-w-two-p-estimate-for-uniformly-elliptic-equations]]): under its stated hypotheses, every $w\in W^{2,p}(B_R(x_0))$ with $Lw\in L^p$ satisfies the scale-invariant estimate $$\sum_{j=0}^{2}R^{j-2}\max_{|\beta|=j}\|D^\beta w\|_{L^p(B_{R/2}(x_0))}\le C\bigl(R^{-2}\|w\|_{L^p(B_R(x_0))}+\|Lw\|_{L^p(B_R(x_0))}\bigr).$$ On any fixed patch radius this implies the corresponding unweighted $W^{2,p}$ estimate with a constant also depending on that radius, which is the form used below.

[F3] Whole-space Laplace estimate ([[thm-global-w-two-p-estimate-for-the-laplacian-on-rn]]): $\|D^2w\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}\le C_{n,p}\|\Delta w\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}$ for every $w\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^n)$ and $1<p<\infty$, with the max-form convention for $\|D^2w\|_{L^p}$ up to dimensional constants.

[F4] Half-space Dirichlet estimate for constant coefficients. Let $A_0$ be symmetric positive definite with $\lambda|\xi|^2\le A_0^{ij}\xi_i\xi_j\le\Lambda|\xi|^2$, let $H=\{x_n>0\}$ and let $v\in W^{2,p}(H)\cap W^{1,p}_0(H)$. Then $$\|\partial_{ij}v\|_{L^p(H)}\le C_{n,p,\lambda,\Lambda}\|A_0^{ij}\partial_i\partial_jv\|_{L^p(H)}.$$ Proof: put $y:=A_0^{-1/2}x$, so $x=A_0^{1/2}y$ maps $H$ onto the half-space $H'=\{y\cdot\nu>0\}$ with $\nu:=A_0^{1/2}e_n$, and define $\widetilde v(y):=v(A_0^{1/2}y)$. The chain rule gives $\Delta_y\widetilde v(y)=(A_0^{ij}\partial_i\partial_jv)(A_0^{1/2}y)$; after a rotation $H'$ is $\{y_n>0\}$ and the Laplacian is invariant. Let $V$ be the odd extension of $\widetilde v$ in the normal variable $y_n$. Its trace is zero; The trace operator of [[thm-trace-estimate-on-the-half-space]] applies to $\widetilde v$ and its first derivatives. Approximate $\widetilde v$ by functions smooth up to the face by applying [[thm-wkp-extension-from-a-half-space]] and whole-space smooth density. Trace continuity and tangential integration by parts against a compact boundary test give $T(\partial_a\widetilde v)=\partial_a(T\widetilde v)=0$ for $a<n$. The odd extension has zero function trace; its normal derivative is even, so its two traces agree, while each tangential derivative is odd with zero trace. Integration by parts on the two half-spaces therefore produces no interface distributions through order two, so $V\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^n)$ and $\Delta V$ is the odd extension of $\Delta\widetilde v$. Thus $\|\Delta V\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}=2^{1/p}\|\Delta\widetilde v\|_{L^p(H')}$, with the same factor for each second derivative. Applying [F3] to $V$ and changing variables back through $A_0^{\pm1/2}$ (whose operator norms are bounded by $\max\{\lambda^{-1/2},\Lambda^{1/2}\}$, with Jacobian factors likewise controlled by $\lambda,\Lambda$) gives the claim. The reflection and weak derivative compatibility are established here; Wang's Schauder Theorem 1' is not an Lp supplier.

[F5] Cutoff commutator ([[lem-cutoff-commutator-for-local-w-two-p-estimates]]): for $\eta\in C_c^\infty(B_R(x_0))$ and $w\in W^{2,p}(B_R(x_0))$, $$L(\eta w)=\eta Lw+(a^{ij}+a^{ji})(\partial_i\eta)\partial_jw+\bigl(a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j\eta+b^i\partial_i\eta\bigr)w\quad\text{a.e.}$$ with the corresponding $L^p$ commutator bound. In this theorem's symmetric principal-matrix convention, $a^{ij}=a^{ji}$, so the cross term specializes to $2a^{ij}(\partial_i\eta)\partial_jw$ and the previous bound with $2\Lambda$ is valid. The identity applies on each flattened half-box with its transformed symmetric principal matrix.

[F6] Absorption ([[lem-lp-interpolation-absorbs-lower-order-derivatives]]): for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $C(n,p,\varepsilon)$ with $\|Dw\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}\le\varepsilon\|D^2w\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}+C\|w\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}$ for $w\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^n)$, and on balls $\|Dw\|_{L^p(B_R(x_0))}\le\varepsilon R\|D^2w\|_{L^p(B_{2R}(x_0))}+CR^{-1}\|w\|_{L^p(B_{2R}(x_0))}$.

[F7] Local $C^{1,1}$ flattening. Here $C^{1,1}$ means that each local boundary graph is $C^1$ and its first derivatives are Lipschitz on compact patches. The flattening map is a shear with determinant one; its first derivative and inverse are bounded, and its second weak derivatives are essentially bounded: apply the real-valued converse of [[thm-w-one-infinity-functions-have-lipschitz-representatives]] to each Lipschitz graph-gradient component on a smaller convex base box. For smooth $w$, mollify the graph on a slightly larger base box. The graph functions and gradients converge uniformly; their uniformly bounded second derivatives converge locally in each finite $L^p$ to the weak second derivatives. Apply the ordinary chain rule to the smooth shears, then pass to the weak derivative identities using change of variables and these convergences. This gives $D(w\circ\Phi)=(Dw\circ\Phi)D\Phi$ and $D^2(w\circ\Phi)=(D^2w\circ\Phi)[D\Phi,D\Phi]+(Dw\circ\Phi)D^2\Phi$. The change-of-variables formula and the bound $\|gh\|_{L^p}\le\|g\|_{L^\infty}\|h\|_{L^p}$ therefore bound the local $W^{1,p}$ and $W^{2,p}$ norms of the pullback by the corresponding original norms, with constants controlled by the chart bounds. For general $w\in W^{2,p}$, smooth approximation on the open chart patch ([[thm-meyers-serrin-density-on-an-arbitrary-open-set]]) and weak stability of derivatives ([[lem-weak-stability-of-sobolev-derivatives]]) give the same weak chain rule and estimate. The Jacobian and chart derivatives are controlled on the finite atlas of the bounded $C^{1,1}$ domain ([[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]], [[thm-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-lebesgue-measurable-functions]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[lem-classical-derivatives-are-weak-derivatives]], [[thm-holder-inequality-for-integrals]]). The transformed lower-order coefficients are bounded by the original bounds and the chart's $C^{1,1}$ bounds. ([[def-uniformly-elliptic-nondivergence-operator]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Uniform charts, transformed-coefficient modulus, and bounded overlap. Start with a finite $C^{1,1}$ boundary atlas and a finite interior atlas. In a boundary chart $x=\Phi(y)$ the transformed principal matrix is $\widetilde A(y)=D\Phi(y)^{-1}A(\Phi(y))D\Phi(y)^{-T}$. The chart maps and their first derivatives are uniformly bounded on the finite atlas, and $D\Phi$ is Lipschitz; therefore, for a modulus $\omega_A$ of $A$ on $\bar\Omega$, $$\omega_{\widetilde A}(s)\le C_0\bigl(\omega_A(C_0s)+s\bigr)$$ on every chart, with one $C_0$ for the finite atlas. Choose a small scale $r$ so the half-space estimate constant times $\omega_{\widetilde A}(C_0r)$ is as small as required below. A grid in each chart and in the interior gives inner patches covering $\Omega$: boundary half-patches cover a collar of width comparable to $r$, and the remaining interior balls have doubled balls contained in $\Omega$. Choose fixed larger patches for cutoffs and the doubled-ball interpolation inequality, still inside the chart or $\Omega$. The expanded patches have overlap at most $N_0$, independent of $r$; smooth cutoffs equal to one on inner patches and supported on the expanded patches have first and second derivatives bounded by $Cr^{-1}$ and $Cr^{-2}$. The grid overlap and finite atlas fix $N_0$ before we choose the local Hessian error. [F1, F7, given, construct, A1]

2.1 Interior patches with doubled balls inside $\Omega$. For an interior member $B_r(x_\nu)$, step 1.1 ensures $B_{2r}(x_\nu)\subset\Omega$. Apply [F2] to $u$ on $B_{2r}(x_\nu)$ to obtain $$\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(B_r(x_\nu))}\le C\bigl(r^{-2}\|u\|_{L^p(B_{2r}(x_\nu))}+\|Lu\|_{L^p(B_{2r}(x_\nu))}\bigr).$$ These doubled balls are all contained in $\Omega$, and their constants are uniform because the coefficient modulus and the dimensionless lower-order bounds are controlled at the fixed scale. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Boundary patches and a prescribed local error. In a boundary chart flatten the graph, write $\widetilde u=u\circ\Phi$, let $\widetilde L$ be the transformed operator, and take a cutoff $\chi$ equal to one on an inner half-patch and supported on a larger half-patch. Set $v=\chi\widetilde u$. To see $v\in W^{1,p}_0(\mathbb R^n_+)$, choose $u_k\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ converging to $u$ in $W^{1,p}$ by [F1]. Their pullbacks, multiplied by $\chi$, are compactly supported in the open half-space and lie in $W^{1,p}_0(\mathbb R^n_+)$ by mollification. The smooth W^{1,p} chart estimate [F7] makes these pullbacks Cauchy in $W^{1,p}$; the change-of-variables $L^p$ estimate identifies their limit with $v$, so closedness of $W^{1,p}_0$ gives $v\in W^{1,p}_0(\mathbb R^n_+)$. Since $u\in W^{2,p}$, the same chart rule gives $v\in W^{2,p}(\mathbb R^n_+)$; extend it by zero away from the patch. Freeze the transformed principal matrix at the chart centre to get $\widetilde L_0$. The half-space estimate [F4], whose odd-reflection proof applies the whole-space estimate [F3], and the product identity [F5] bound $\|D^2v\|_{L^p}$ by the transformed $\|Lu\|_{L^p}$, the principal error $\|\chi(\widetilde A_0-\widetilde A):D^2\widetilde u\|_{L^p}$, lower-order products, and cutoff commutators bounded by $C(r^{-1}\|D\widetilde u\|_{L^p}+r^{-2}\|\widetilde u\|_{L^p})$ on the expanded patch. The identity $$\chi D^2\widetilde u=D^2v-D\chi\otimes D\widetilde u-D\widetilde u\otimes D\chi-(D^2\chi)\widetilde u$$ puts the principal error on the left with coefficient at most $C\omega_{\widetilde A}(C_0r)$, which is made small in step 1.1. For the term $\chi D\widetilde u$, write it as $Dv-(D\chi)\widetilde u$ and apply the whole-space interpolation inequality [F6] to the odd extension of $v$; choose its parameter small enough to absorb the resulting $\|D^2v\|_{L^p}$ term. For the cutoff commutator, odd-extend $\widetilde u$ across the flat face on the larger half-ball and apply the doubled-ball form of [F6], giving for every $\varepsilon>0$ $$r^{-1}\|D\widetilde u\|_{L^p(P^+)}\le\varepsilon\|D^2\widetilde u\|_{L^p(P^{++})}+C_\varepsilon r^{-2}\|\widetilde u\|_{L^p(P^{++})}.$$ The chart chain rule [F7] also contributes bounded first-order terms when comparing second derivatives; the same interpolation absorbs them into an arbitrarily small multiple of the outer Hessian norm. Thus, after first fixing the transformed-coefficient oscillation and then choosing the interpolation parameters, for any prescribed $\eta>0$ the boundary patch satisfies $$\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(P)}\le C_1\bigl(\|Lu\|_{L^p(P^{++})}+r^{-2}\|u\|_{L^p(P^{++})}\bigr)+\eta\|D^2u\|_{L^p(P^{++})},$$ where $P$ is the inner patch and $P^+$, $P^{++}$ are fixed expanded patches from step 1.1. All chart Jacobians and lower-order coefficient bounds enter $C_1$, which is independent of $u$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Sum with bounded overlap and absorb quantitatively. The inner patches cover $\Omega$ and the expanded patches have overlap at most $N_0$. Taking the $\ell^p$ sum of the local estimates in steps 2.1 and 2.2 therefore gives $$\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\le C_2\bigl(\|Lu\|_{L^p(\Omega)}+r^{-2}\|u\|_{L^p(\Omega)}\bigr)+C_{\mathrm{ov}}\eta\|D^2u\|_{L^p(\Omega)},$$ where $C_{\mathrm{ov}}$ depends only on the fixed overlap and chart constants. Now choose the local error from step 2.2 after this overlap constant is fixed so that $C_{\mathrm{ov}}\eta\le\tfrac12$. Since $\|D^2u\|_{L^p}\le\|u\|_{W^{2,p}}$, the last term is absorbed into the left side. This yields the stated estimate; the finite-overlap factor is accounted for explicitly rather than assumed small. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion. Step 3.1 yields the displayed a priori estimate with a constant depending on $n,p,\lambda,\Lambda,M,\Omega$, the finite $C^{1,1}$ atlas and the modulus $\omega_A$ through $\omega_{\widetilde A}(s)\le C_0(\omega_A(C_0s)+s)$. The zero trace is used for the odd extension in the half-space estimate, and every interior doubled ball is contained in $\Omega$. The proof asserts neither solvability nor weak-to-strong regularity. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F4, given] ∎
## Remarks

- The globalization has two ingredients: the interior estimate [F2] and the half-space Dirichlet estimate [F4], the latter used after flattening and freezing. The freezing radius is chosen once, uniformly over the finite atlas, using continuity of the principal coefficients on the compact set $\bar\Omega$; this is where the modulus of continuity enters the constant.
- The estimate is genuinely a priori: the odd reflection used in [F4] requires a function already in $W^{2,p}$ with zero trace. Obtaining that membership from a weak formulation is the content of the weak-to-strong regularity theorem of this page, not of this a priori estimate.
