## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $\lambda\vdash n$, let $F$ be any field, and let
$r_1<r_2<\cdots<r_m$ be the rows of the removable corners of $\lambda$, as in
[[def-corner-order-and-specht-deletion-map]]. For $1\le i\le m$ let
$$V_i:=\operatorname{span}_F\bigl\{\,e_t:\ t\ \text{a standard }\lambda\text{-tableau whose entry }n\text{ lies in one of the rows }r_1,\dots,r_i\,\bigr\}$$
inside $S^\lambda_F$, and put $V_0:=0$. Then each $V_i$ is stable under the
action of $S_{n-1}$ on $S^\lambda_F$ obtained by restriction from $S_n$, that
is $\sigma\cdot V_i\subseteq V_i$ for every $\sigma\in S_{n-1}$; moreover
$V_0\subseteq V_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_m=S^\lambda_F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** an integer $n\ge1$, a partition $\lambda\vdash n$, a field $F$, the removable rows $r_1<\cdots<r_m$ of $\lambda$, and the subspaces $V_i$ of the Statement.

[F1] $M^\lambda_F$ is free with the $\lambda$-tabloids as basis, $e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}$ for a $\lambda$-tableau $t$, and $S^\lambda_F$ is the $F$-span of all $e_t$; moreover $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t$ and $\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t$ for $\gamma$ in the column stabilizer $C_t$, so $S^\lambda_F$ is an $S_n$-submodule and is generated by any one $e_t$ ([[def-polytabloid-specht-module-over-an-arbitrary-field]], [[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F2] The standard polytabloids $\{e_t:t\text{ standard}\}$ are an $F$-basis of $S^\lambda_F$, and every polytabloid is an $F$-linear (indeed $\mathbb Z$-linear) combination of standard polytabloids ([[lem-integral-specht-garnir-straightening-and-field-basis]], claims 2 and 3).

[F3] A $\lambda$-tableau is column-standard when its entries strictly increase down every column; for a column-standard tableau $u$, writing $c_u(a)$ for the column of the label $a$, the largest label on which two distinct column-standard tableaux differ has comparable column numbers, and the resulting relation $s\prec u\iff c_s(x)<c_u(x)$ for the largest label $x$ with $c_s(x)\ne c_u(x)$ is a finite strict total order on the column-standard $\lambda$-tableaux ([[def-tabloid-and-column-orders-for-specht-straightening]]).

[F4] The removable nodes of $\lambda$ are exactly the nodes $(i,\lambda_i)$ with $\lambda_i>\lambda_{i+1}$ (with $\lambda_{k+1}:=0$), and deleting a removable node leaves the diagram of a partition of $n-1$; for a removable node $(r,\lambda_r)$ the column $\lambda_r$ has boxes in exactly the rows $1,\dots,r$, so $(r,\lambda_r)$ is the bottom box of that column and column $j$ of $[\lambda]$ has height $\lambda'_j$ with $\lambda'_1\ge\lambda'_2\ge\cdots$ ([[def-removable-and-addable-nodes-of-a-partition]], [[def-partition-young-diagram-and-conjugate-partition]]).

[F5] $C_u$ is the subgroup of permutations preserving each column set of $u$; sorting the entries of every column of any tableau $u$ increasingly gives a column-standard tableau $u^{\mathrm{col}}$, and $e_{u^{\mathrm{col}}}= \operatorname{sgn}(\pi)e_u$ for the permutation $\pi\in C_u$ with $\pi\cdot u=u^{\mathrm{col}}$ ([[def-row-and-column-stabilizers-of-a-tableau]], [F1]).

[F6] If $t$ is a standard $\lambda$-tableau, then the box of $t$ containing $n$ is removable, and deleting it leaves a standard tableau of size $n-1$ ([[lem-largest-entry-of-a-standard-tableau-is-removable]]).

[F7] $\operatorname{sgn}$ is a homomorphism and $\operatorname{sgn}(1)=1$ ([[thm-sign-is-a-homomorphism]]).

[F8] **(Garnir.)** Let $t$ be a $\lambda$-tableau, $j,j+1$ adjacent columns, $X$ a set of entries of column $j$ of $t$ and $Y$ a set of entries of column $j+1$ of $t$ with $|X|+|Y|>\lambda'_j$, and let $T$ be any left-coset transversal containing $1$ for $S_{X\cup Y}/(S_X\times S_Y)$, where the transpositions in $S_X$ and $S_Y$ act on the corresponding labels and fix all other labels. Then $\sum_{g\in T}\operatorname{sgn}(g)\,g\cdot e_t=0$ in $M^\lambda_F$ ([[lem-integral-specht-garnir-straightening-and-field-basis]], claim 1).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 [construct] We fix notation for the invariance argument. For a tableau $u$ let $c(u)$ be the column containing the label $n$; since $n$ is the largest label, $n$ is the bottom entry of its column in every column-standard tableau, and if $n$ already lies at the bottom of its column in $u$ then the sorting permutation $\pi\in C_u$ with $\pi\cdot u=u^{\mathrm{col}}$ fixes the column of $n$ and leaves $n$ in its box, so $n$ occupies the same box in $u$ and in $u^{\mathrm{col}}$; in this situation we write $\rho(u)$ for the row of the box containing $n$, and then $\rho(u^{\mathrm{col}})=\rho(u)$ and $e_u=\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)^{-1}e_{u^{\mathrm{col}}}=\pm e_{u^{\mathrm{col}}}$ by [F5] and [F7]. [F1, F5, F7, construct, algebra]

1.2 For $\sigma\in S_{n-1}$ and any tableau $u$ the tableau $\sigma\cdot u$ has the same entry $n$ in the same box as $u$, because $\sigma$ fixes the label $n$; so if $u$ has $n$ at the bottom of its column, then so does $\sigma\cdot u$, with $\rho(\sigma\cdot u)=\rho(u)$ and $c(\sigma\cdot u)=c(u)$. [construct, algebra]

1.3 Thus it suffices to prove the straightening statement (S): if $u$ is a tableau in which $n$ lies at the bottom of its column, then $e_u$ is an $F$-linear combination of standard polytabloids $e_{t'}$ with $\rho(t')\le\rho(u)$. Indeed, for a standard $\lambda$-tableau $t$ with $n$ in row $r_l$ and $\sigma\in S_{n-1}$, the tableau $u:=\sigma\cdot t$ has $n$ in the same box as $t$, and that box is removable and hence the bottom box of its column by [F6] and [F4]; so $u$ satisfies the hypothesis of (S), and $e_{\sigma\cdot t}\in V_l$ follows from (S) together with the fact that a standard $t'$ with $\rho(t')\le r_l$ has $\rho(t')$ equal to some removable row $r_{l'}$ with $l'\le l$, by [F6] and [F4]. [F1, F4, F5, F6, construct, algebra]

1.4 **(Garnir setup.)** Let $s$ be column-standard and not standard. Since the entries strictly increase down each column but some row fails to weakly increase, there are a row $q$ and a column index $j$ with $s(q,j)>s(q,j+1)$. Put $h_j:=\lambda'_j$ and let $X$ be the set of labels in the boxes $(r,j)$ for $q\le r\le h_j$ and $Y$ the set of labels in the boxes $(r,j+1)$ for $1\le r\le q$; these are label sets of the two adjacent columns $j,j+1$ of $s$, and $|X|+|Y|=(h_j-q+1)+q=h_j+1>\lambda'_j$. Column-standardness gives $s(q,j)<s(q+1,j)<\cdots<s(h_j,j)$, so every label in $X$ is $\ge s(q,j)$, and gives $s(1,j+1)<\cdots<s(q,j+1)$, so every label in $Y$ is $\le s(q,j+1)$; since $s(q,j)>s(q,j+1)$, every label of $X$ is strictly larger than every label of $Y$. [F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 **(The transversal.)** Put $Z:=X\cup Y$, $p:=|X|$, and for every $p$-element subset $A\subseteq Z$ write $X\setminus A=\{a_1<\cdots<a_r\}$ and $A\setminus X=\{b_1<\cdots<b_r\}$ and put $g_A:=(a_1\,b_1)\cdots(a_r\,b_r)$, the empty product for $A=X$ giving $g_X=1$. Then $g_A(X)=A$, the $g_A$ are pairwise distinct, and they form a left-coset transversal for $H:=S_X\times S_Y$ in $S_Z$ containing $1$: indeed $H$ is exactly the setwise stabilizer of $X$ in $S_Z$, so the left coset $gH$ is determined by $g(X)$, and $g_A(X)=A$ realizes every possible value $A$. Hence the Garnir relation [F8] applies to $s$, $X$, $Y$ and this transversal and gives $\sum_A\operatorname{sgn}(g_A)\,g_A\cdot e_s=0$ in $M^\lambda_F$, so $e_s=-\sum_{A\ne X}\operatorname{sgn}(g_A)\,e_{g_A\cdot s}$ by [F1] and [F7]; here $g_A$ acts on $e_s$ through the action on tabloids, that is $g_A\cdot e_s=e_{g_A\cdot s}$ by [F1]. [F1, F7, F8, step 1.4, construct, algebra]

3.1 **(The move and the order.)** Let $s$ and $X,Y$ be as in step 1.4 and let $A\ne X$, and let $u_A:=(g_A\cdot s)^{\mathrm{col}}$ be the column-sorted tableau of $g_A\cdot s$. Then $s\prec u_A$ in the order of [F3]. Indeed, $g_A$ fixes every label outside $Z$ and moves the labels $a_i\in X\setminus A$ into the boxes previously holding $b_i\in A\setminus X\subseteq Y$ and conversely, so the labels that change column are exactly the elements of $(X\setminus A)\cup(A\setminus X)$, those in $X\setminus A$ moving from column $j$ to column $j+1$ and those in $A\setminus X$ moving from column $j+1$ to column $j$; the largest of them is $x_A:=\max(X\setminus A)$, since every element of $X\setminus A$ exceeds every element of $Y$ by step 1.4; every label larger than $x_A$ therefore stays in its column; column sorting preserves the column of each label, so in $u_A$ the label $x_A$ lies in column $j+1$ while in $s$ it lies in column $j$, and $x_A$ is the largest label on which $s$ and $u_A$ differ, whence $s\prec u_A$. [F3, F5, step 1.4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **(The move does not raise $\rho$.)** With $s$, $X$, $Y$ and $A\ne X$ as in step 3.1 one has $\rho(u_A)\le\rho(s)$. Let $c:=c(s)$ be the column containing $n$ in $s$; since $s$ is column-standard, $n$ is the bottom entry of column $c$, so $\rho(s)=\lambda'_c$ by [F4]. If $c\notin\{j,j+1\}$, then the labels moved by $g_A$ lie in columns $j$ and $j+1$, so $n$ is fixed and stays at the bottom of column $c$, giving $\rho(u_A)=\rho(s)$. If $c=j$, then $n\in X$ because $n$ is the bottom entry of column $j$ with row $h_j\ge q$; if $n\notin A$, then $n=\max(X\setminus A)=x_A$ is moved to column $j+1$ and after column sorting lies at the bottom of column $j+1$, so $\rho(u_A)=\lambda'_{j+1}\le\lambda'_j=\rho(s)$ by [F4]; while if $n\in A$, then $n$ is neither among the $a_i$ nor among the $b_i$, hence is fixed and stays at the bottom of column $j$, so $\rho(u_A)=\rho(s)$. Finally, if $c=j+1$, then $n\notin Y$: otherwise $n$ would be smaller than every element of $X$ by step 1.4, contradicting the maximality of $n$ because $X\ne\varnothing$; so $n\notin X\cup Y$, $n$ is fixed, and again $\rho(u_A)=\rho(s)$. [F4, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **(Row-controlled straightening.)** Every column-standard $s$ satisfies: $e_s$ is an $F$-linear combination of standard polytabloids $e_{t'}$ with $\rho(t')\le\rho(s)$. List the finitely many column-standard tableaux as $s_1\prec s_2\prec\cdots\prec s_N$ using the finite strict total order of [F3] and prove the assertion for $s_k$ by downward induction on $k$. If $s_k$ is standard there is nothing to prove. If $s_k$ is not standard, then steps 1.4, 2.1 and 3.1 produce, for each $A\ne X$, a column-standard tableau $u_A\succ s_k$, so $u_A=s_l$ with $l>k$, and $e_{s_k}=\sum_{A\ne X}\pm e_{u_A}$ by steps 2.1 and 1.1; by the induction hypothesis each $e_{u_A}$ is a combination of standard polytabloids with $\rho\le\rho(u_A)$, and $\rho(u_A)\le\rho(s_k)$ by step 4.1, so $e_{s_k}$ is such a combination as well. This also shows that $s_N$ is standard, since otherwise it would satisfy $s_N\prec u_A$ for some $A$, contradicting maximality; hence the induction covers $k=N$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 This proves (S) of step 1.3: if $u$ has $n$ at the bottom of its column, then $e_u=\pm e_{u^{\mathrm{col}}}$ with $\rho(u^{\mathrm{col}})=\rho(u)$ and $u^{\mathrm{col}}$ column-standard by step 1.1, and step 5.1 expands $e_{u^{\mathrm{col}}}$ in standard polytabloids with $\rho\le\rho(u)$, the sign being absorbed into the coefficients over $F$. [F5, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 **(Invariance.)** Let $\sigma\in S_{n-1}$ and let $t$ be a standard tableau whose entry $n$ lies in row $r_l$. The box of $n$ in $t$ is removable by [F6] and is the bottom box of its column by [F4]; $\sigma$ fixes the label $n$, so $u:=\sigma\cdot t$ has $n$ in the same box by step 1.2, and by step 6.1 $e_u$ is an $F$-linear combination of standard polytabloids $e_{t'}$ with $\rho(t')\le r_l$. Each such $t'$ is standard, so by [F6] and [F4] the row $\rho(t')$ of $n$ in $t'$ is one of the removable rows $r_1<\cdots<r_m$, and $\rho(t')\le r_l$ then gives $\rho(t')=r_{l'}$ with $l'\le l$; hence $e_{t'}\in V_l$. Since $e_u=e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t$ by [F1], we get $\sigma\cdot e_t\in V_l\subseteq V_i$ whenever $l\le i$. As the polytabloids $e_t$ with $n$ in rows $r_1,\dots,r_i$ span $V_i$, this proves $\sigma\cdot V_i\subseteq V_i$ for every $\sigma\in S_{n-1}$, that is, $V_i$ is $S_{n-1}$-stable. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.2, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 **(The chain and the top term.)** $V_0=0\subseteq V_1$ is the definition, and $V_i\subseteq V_{i+1}$ for $1\le i<m$ holds because the set of tableaux whose entry $n$ lies in rows $r_1,\dots,r_i$ is contained in the corresponding set for $i+1$. For $V_m=S^\lambda_F$: the inclusion $V_m\subseteq S^\lambda_F$ is clear, and conversely the standard polytabloids span $S^\lambda_F$ by [F2]; if $t$ is standard then the box of $n$ is removable by [F6], so its row is one of $r_1,\dots,r_m$ by [F4], whence $e_t\in V_m$. This proves the chain $0=V_0\subseteq V_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_m=S^\lambda_F$. [F1, F2, F4, F6, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For $n=1$ we have $\lambda=(1)$, $m=1$, $r_1=1$, the only standard tableau is the single box with entry $1$, the group $S_{n-1}=S_0$ is trivial, and $V_1=S^\lambda_F$ is stable; the argument above covers this case, as it does every $n\ge1$. The field $F$ is arbitrary: no division and no characteristic hypothesis is used, the straightening being the integral algorithm of [F2]. The only choices are the finite descent $(q,j)$ of step 1.4 and the finitely many transversal elements $g_A$ of step 2.1, both given by explicit rules on finite data, so no choice principle is invoked. This proves the $S_{n-1}$-stability of every $V_i$, the chain $0=V_0\subseteq\cdots\subseteq V_m=S^\lambda_F$, and completes the proof. [F2, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, discharge-construct] ∎

## Remarks

- **What is proved where.** Steps 1.3--6.1 give the straightening argument with a controlled label: $n$ starts at the bottom of its column and never moves down. Step 3.1 shows that each Garnir move advances in the column order, so the finite induction in step 5.1 terminates. Step 4.1 controls the row: a move either fixes $n$ or carries it to the bottom of the adjacent column, whose height $\lambda'_{j+1}$ is at most the height $\lambda'_j$ it came from.

- **Why the deleted corner stays put.** After stripping the labels $1,\dots,n-1$ the surviving entries of each standard term lie in rows $r_1,\dots,r_l$ with $l\le i$, which is exactly the input to the successive quotient computation ([[lem-specht-branching-successive-quotients]]).

- **No splitting is claimed here.** The subspaces $V_i$ are only shown to be nested $S_{n-1}$-stable subspaces; that the successive quotients are Specht modules, and in characteristic zero that the filtration splits, are separate statements of this page.
