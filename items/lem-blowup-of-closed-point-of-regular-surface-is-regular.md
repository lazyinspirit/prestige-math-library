## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $S$ be a **regular
surface**: a locally Noetherian scheme of pure dimension two all of whose local
rings are regular ([[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]). Let
$p\in S$ be a closed point and let $\pi:S'\to S$ be the blowup of $S$ in $p$
([[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]]), with exceptional curve
$E=\pi^{-1}(p)$ ([[def-exceptional-divisor-blowup]]). Then $S'$ is regular of
pure dimension two, and $E$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $S'$. Moreover:

1. if $\dim\mathcal O_{S,p}=2$, there is an isomorphism
   $E\cong\mathbb P^1_{\kappa(p)}$ over $\kappa(p)$;
2. if $\dim\mathcal O_{S,p}=1$, the morphism $\pi$ is an isomorphism and $E$ is
   the reduced point $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(p)$;

and in either case $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism
$S'\setminus E\to S\setminus\{p\}$. In particular the blowup creates no new
singular one-dimensional component: the only new one-dimensional component is
$E$, which is a regular curve in case 1.

No smoothness over any ground field is asserted: over a non-perfect residue
field extension the blowup can be regular without being smooth over the ground
field ([[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]]), and this lemma claims regularity only.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Local algebra of the regular local ring $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$ in the case
$\dim A=2$: $A$ is a Noetherian regular local ring of dimension two, hence an
integral domain and a unique factorization domain; its maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m=(x,y)$ is generated by a regular system of parameters. The
polynomial ring $A[T]$ is regular and $\dim A[T]=3$, localizations of a regular
ring are regular, and for $z\in\mathfrak m\setminus\mathfrak m^2$ the quotient
$A/(z)$ is regular local of dimension one less; in a regular local ring a
generating list of the maximal ideal with as many entries as the dimension is a
regular system of parameters, whose classes form a basis of the cotangent space
([[lem-regular-local-domain-induction]],
[[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]],
[[thm-localisation-and-polynomial-extension-of-regular-rings]],
[[cor-localisations-of-regular-local-rings-are-regular]],
[[lem-regular-local-quotient-by-parameter-is-regular]],
[[thm-dimension-of-a-polynomial-ring-over-a-noetherian-ring]],
[[lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis]]).

[F2] If $A$ is a regular local ring of dimension two with residue field
$\kappa$, then any cotangent basis induces a graded $\kappa$-algebra
isomorphism $\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A\cong\kappa[X,Y]$ with standard
grading ([[thm-associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring]]).

[F3] A one-dimensional regular local ring is a discrete valuation ring: its
maximal ideal is principal, generated by a uniformizer
([[thm-one-dimensional-regular-local-rings-are-dvrs]],
[[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]).

[F4] The Axiom of Choice is assumed for the cited local-algebra theorems
([F1], [F2]) as stated there; no further selection is made in this proof
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Given:** AC, a regular surface $S$, a closed point $p\in S$, the blowup $\pi:S'=\operatorname{Bl}_pS\to S$ and the exceptional curve $E=\pi^{-1}(p)$.

1.1 Take a Noetherian affine neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}R$ of $p$, with center ideal $\mathfrak n$, and put $A=R_{\mathfrak n}=\mathcal O_{S,p}$. The blowup restricts to the blowup on $U$ ([[lem-blowup-local-on-base-scheme]]). Flat base change along $R\to A$ identifies its further pullback with $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathfrak m}\operatorname{Spec}A$, where $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak nA$ ([[thm-blowup-base-change-flat]]). The local ring of any point above $p$ is unchanged by this localization, because every element of $R\setminus\mathfrak n$ is already a unit there. Thus regularity above $p$ can be checked over $A$. Its dimension is one or two: $p$ cannot be a zero-dimensional irreducible component of the pure two-dimensional regular scheme, and the dimension is at most two. The two dimensions are treated separately below. [F1, F3, given]

2.1 Suppose $\dim A=2$ and choose regular parameters $x,y$ generating $\mathfrak m$. The standard charts are $A[\mathfrak m/x]$ and $A[\mathfrak m/y]$ ([[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]]). By the power-torsion presentation of [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]], the first is the quotient of $B'=A[T]/(xT-y)$ by its $x$-power torsion. This torsion is zero. Indeed $A/(x)$ is a regular one-dimensional local domain and $y$ is nonzero modulo $x$ by [F1]. If $xg=(xT-y)h$ in $A[T]$, reduction modulo $x$ gives $\overline y\,\overline h=0$ in the domain $(A/(x))[T]$, so $h=xh_1$; cancelling $x$ in the domain $A[T]$ gives $g=(xT-y)h_1$. Thus multiplication by $x$ is injective on $B'$, and $A[\mathfrak m/x]=B'$. It is a domain because it embeds into $A[1/x]$. The other chart is symmetrically $A[U]/(yU-x)$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 In the case $\dim A=1$, the one-dimensional regular local ring $A$ is a discrete valuation ring [F3], so $\mathfrak m$ is principal generated by a uniformizer, the center ideal is invertible on a neighbourhood of $p$, and the blowup of an invertible ideal is an isomorphism: $\pi$ is an isomorphism ([[thm-blowup-effective-cartier-divisor-isomorphism]]). Then $E=\pi^{-1}(p)$ is the closed point $p$ with its reduced structure $\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(p)$, and it is an effective Cartier divisor. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Consider a prime $Q$ of $B=A[T]/(xT-y)$ whose contraction $\mathfrak q$ in $A$ is not $\mathfrak m$. If $x\notin\mathfrak q$, the localized chart is $B\otimes_AA_{\mathfrak q}=A_{\mathfrak q}$ by solving $T=y/x$. If $x\in\mathfrak q$, then $y\notin\mathfrak q$; in the localized chart $y=xT$ is a unit, so $x$ is a unit and the chart is $A_{\mathfrak q}[1/x]$. In either case $B_Q$ is a localization of the regular ring $A_{\mathfrak q}$ and is regular. This includes contraction $\mathfrak q=(0)$, where the local ring is a field. [F1, step 2.1, cases, algebra]

3.2 For a prime $Q$ of $B$ over $\mathfrak m$, its preimage $P$ in $C=A[T]$ is either $\mathfrak mC$ or $(x,y,h(T))$, where $h$ lifts a monic irreducible polynomial of $\kappa[T]$. The regular local ring $C_P$ has dimension two in the first case and three in the second: the prime chains $(0)\subsetneq(x)\subsetneq\mathfrak mC$ and, in the second case, $\mathfrak mC\subsetneq P$ give the lower bounds, while the displayed two or three maximal-ideal generators give the upper bounds on dimension. Consequently $x,y$, or $x,y,h$, form a cotangent basis by [F1]. The class of $xT-y$ in $PC_P/(PC_P)^2$ is the residue of $T$ times the class of $x$ minus the class of $y$, and is nonzero because the coefficient of $y$ is $-1$. The parameter-quotient lemma therefore makes $B_Q=C_P/(xT-y)$ regular. Its dimension is one for $P=\mathfrak mC$ (the generic point of the exceptional curve), and two for $P=(x,y,h)$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 3.1 and 3.2 prove regularity on the $x$-chart, and the symmetric proof proves it on the $y$-chart; step 1.1 transfers these local rings to all points above $p$. Away from $p$ the blowup is an isomorphism ([[lem-blowup-isomorphism-off-center]]), so $S'$ is regular everywhere. It is pure of dimension two as well. In the dimension-one case this follows from the identity in step 2.2. In the dimension-two case the local charts above $p$ are domains of dimension two: all their prime-local dimensions are at most two, and step 3.2 exhibits dimension-two closed local rings. The exceptional divisor is not an irreducible component, since its chart equation $x$ (or $y$) is a nonzerodivisor. Thus every component meeting the exceptional divisor is the strict transform of the corresponding component through $p$ and has dimension two; every other component is unchanged. The components of the original regular surface all have dimension two, so the same holds for $S'$. The dimension-one local ring at the generic point of $E$ does not contradict pure dimension two. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2]

5.1 The exceptional curve is effective Cartier: the pulled-back center ideal $\mathcal I_p\mathcal O_{S'}$ is invertible with nonzerodivisor local generators, and $E=V(\mathcal I_p\mathcal O_{S'})$ is the associated effective Cartier divisor ([[thm-pullback-center-ideal-invertible]]). In the case $\dim A=2$, the normal cone theorem identifies $E$ with $\operatorname{Proj}_{Z}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathcal I_p}\mathcal O_S)$ over $Z=\{p\}$ ([[thm-exceptional-divisor-normal-cone-proj]]); on the one-point scheme $Z=\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(p)$ its graded pieces are $\mathfrak m^n/\mathfrak m^{n+1}$, so $E\cong\operatorname{Proj}(\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}A)$ and [F2] gives $E\cong\operatorname{Proj}\kappa(p)[X,Y]=\mathbb P^1_{\kappa(p)}$ over $\kappa(p)$. In particular $E$ is a regular curve, so the blowup creates no new singular one-dimensional component. [F2, step 4.1]

6.1 Collecting: $S'$ is regular of pure dimension two and $E$ is effective Cartier by steps 4.1 and 5.1; in the two-dimensional case $E\cong\mathbb P^1_{\kappa(p)}$ by step 5.1; in the one-dimensional case $\pi$ is an isomorphism and $E=\operatorname{Spec}\kappa(p)$ by step 2.2; and $\pi$ restricts to an isomorphism off $E$ by step 4.1. Only regularity of $S$ and of the local charts was used, never smoothness over a field, so no smoothness claim is made. [F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 2.2] ∎
