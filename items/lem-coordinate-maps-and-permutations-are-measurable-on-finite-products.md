## Statement

Let $(S_i,\Sigma_i)$, $1\le i\le n$, be finitely many measurable spaces, with
$n\ge1$, and equip $\prod_{i=1}^nS_i$ with its finite product sigma-algebra.
If $f_i:(\Omega,\mathcal F)\to(S_i,\Sigma_i)$ are measurable, then the tuple
$f=(f_1,\ldots,f_n)$ is measurable. If all factors equal one measurable space
$(S,\Sigma)$, every coordinate permutation $\tau_\pi:S^n\to S^n$ is
measurable, where $(\tau_\pi z)_i=z_{\pi(i)}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite product sigma-algebra convention, measurable coordinate maps, and the stated coordinate-permutation convention.

[F1] The finite product sigma-algebra is generated by the finite measurable rectangles. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F2] A measurable rectangle is a product of measurable sets in the coordinate spaces. [[def-measurable-rectangle]]

[F3] A map is measurable when the preimage of each measurable target set is measurable. [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

[F4] A sigma-algebra is closed under complements and countable unions, hence under finite intersections. [[def-sigma-algebra]]

[A1] Each coordinate map $f_i$ is $\mathcal F/\Sigma_i$-measurable.

## Proof

1.1 By induction on $n$, the finite product sigma-algebra is generated by the $n$-coordinate rectangles. The base case is $n=1$. At the induction step, let $\mathcal S$ be the sigma-algebra generated by $(n+1)$-coordinate rectangles. For each measurable last-coordinate set $B$, the class of first-coordinate sets $A$ with $A\times B\in\mathcal S$ is a sigma-algebra: it contains the whole first product since the whole-first-product times $B$ is a coordinate rectangle, and complements and countable unions follow by relative difference inside that set and by countable unions. It contains the $n$-coordinate rectangles by construction, so it contains the recursively defined $n$-fold product sigma-algebra. Thus $\mathcal S$ contains every rectangle used in the recursive binary product. Conversely, each $(n+1)$-coordinate rectangle belongs to that recursive product sigma-algebra by its defining binary rectangle clause, so the two generated sigma-algebras agree. [F1, F2, F4]

2.1 For a generating rectangle $E_1\times\cdots\times E_n$, its preimage under $f$ is $\bigcap_{i=1}^n f_i^{-1}(E_i)$. Each set in this finite intersection belongs to $\mathcal F$ by [A1], so the preimage belongs to $\mathcal F$ by [F4]. The class of target sets whose preimages under $f$ lie in $\mathcal F$ is a sigma-algebra because preimages preserve complements and countable unions; by step 1.1 it contains the generators, so it contains the product sigma-algebra and [F3] makes $f$ measurable. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 For $\pi$ a permutation, $\tau_\pi^{-1}(E_1\times\cdots\times E_n)=E_{\pi^{-1}(1)}\times\cdots\times E_{\pi^{-1}(n)}$, which is a generating rectangle by [F2]. The same generated-sigma-algebra argument of step 2.1 proves that $\tau_\pi$ is measurable. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
