## Statement

Let $G\leq\operatorname{GL}(V)$ be a finite group generated by complex reflections, where a complex reflection is a nonidentity element fixing a hyperplane pointwise. Put $S=\mathbb C[V]$, $R=S^G$ and $I=SR_+$. A finite minimal set of homogeneous positive-degree invariants $f_1,\ldots,f_r$ generating $I$ exists. Every such set generates $R$ as an algebra and is algebraically independent. Here minimal means that no $f_j$ belongs to the $S$-ideal generated by the others. No finiteness or freeness assertion for $S/I$ is used in this proof.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stated finite complex reflection group and its polynomial algebra.

[F1] The grading, invariant ideal and graded $R$-linear Reynolds projection are defined in [[def-finite-linear-invariant-and-coinvariant-polynomial-algebras]].

[F2] Polynomial rings over Noetherian commutative rings are Noetherian ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

[F3] Noetherianity is equivalent to finite generation of every ideal, in the choice-free $1\Leftrightarrow2$ branch of [[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]. Its separate DC-dependent maximal-condition implication is not used.

## Proof

1.1 A field has only ideals $0$ and itself: an ideal containing nonzero $a$ contains $a^{-1}a=1$. They are generated by the empty set and by $1$. Thus $\mathbb C$ is Noetherian by F3, and finitely many applications of F2 make $S$ Noetherian. F3 supplies finite generators for $I$. Each is a finite sum of multiples of positive-degree invariants by F1; replace each invariant by its finitely many homogeneous components. The resulting finite family of homogeneous positive-degree invariants still generates $I$. Successively discard a redundant member until none remains, producing the required minimal family. If an invariant homogeneous $f$ has degree $d>0$, write $f=\sum_j s_j f_j$ with $s_j$ homogeneous of degree $d-\deg f_j<d$, discarding negative-degree terms. Reynolds gives $f=\sum_j\mathcal R(s_j)f_j$. Induction on $d$, starting with constants at degree zero, proves that all invariants are polynomials in the $f_j$. [F1, F2, F3, given]

1.2 We prove a relation fact. Suppose homogeneous invariants $F_1,\ldots,F_m$ satisfy $F_1\notin(F_2,\ldots,F_m)S$, and homogeneous coefficients obey $\sum_j g_jF_j=0$. Then $g_1\in I$. Induct on $D=\deg g_1$ when $g_1\ne0$; the zero case is immediate. At $D=0$, a nonzero scalar $g_1$ would solve for $F_1$ in the other ideal, impossible. For $D>0$ and a generating reflection $\sigma$, choose a nonzero linear equation $\alpha$ for its fixed hyperplane. Each $\sigma g_j-g_j$ vanishes there, hence equals $\alpha h_j$: take $\alpha$ as a coordinate and set that coordinate to zero in the polynomial, so the constant term in it is zero. Since $\sigma F_j=F_j$, subtracting the original relation from its transform and canceling the nonzero factor $\alpha$ gives $\sum_j h_jF_j=0$. Cancellation is valid since a polynomial ring over a field is a domain, as its leading monomial products have nonzero leading coefficient. Now $\deg h_1=D-1$ if it is nonzero, so induction yields $h_1\in I$, hence $\sigma g_1-g_1\in I$. The ideal $I$ is $G$-stable by F1. Writing any $g\in G$ as a product of generating reflections and telescoping therefore gives $gg_1-g_1\in I$. Averaging gives $\mathcal R(g_1)-g_1\in I$, while $\mathcal R(g_1)$ is either zero or a positive-degree invariant, hence in $I$. Therefore $g_1\in I$. [F1, given]

2.1 Suppose a nonzero polynomial relation $H(f_1,\ldots,f_r)=0$ exists. Give its variables weights $d_j=\deg f_j>0$. Separating weighted homogeneous parts and choosing least positive weighted degree gives such an $H$ of minimal degree $D$. Some partial derivative $H_j=\partial H/\partial y_j$ is nonzero in characteristic zero. Its weighted degree is $D-d_j<D$, so $H_j(f)\ne0$ by minimality (a nonzero constant also cannot evaluate to zero). From the finite family $H_j(f)$ choose a minimal subfamily generating their $S$-ideal and relabel it $H_1(f),\ldots,H_m(f)$, with $m\geq1$. For $i>m$ write $H_i(f)=\sum_{j=1}^m a_{ij}H_j(f)$, taking homogeneous coefficients of degrees $d_j-d_i$ and zero coefficients when this number is negative. [step 1.1, given]

3.1 Differentiate $H(f)=0$ with respect to each coordinate $x_k$. The chain rule, verified on monomials, gives $\sum_j H_j(f)\partial_k f_j=0$. Substituting 2.1 yields $\sum_{j=1}^m H_j(f)(\partial_k f_j+\sum_{i>m}a_{ij}\partial_k f_i)=0$. Minimality of the derivative generating subfamily gives $H_1(f)\notin(H_2(f),\ldots,H_m(f))S$. Apply 1.2 to obtain $\partial_k f_1+\sum_{i>m}a_{i1}\partial_k f_i\in I$. It is homogeneous of degree $d_1-1$, so by 1.1 write it $\sum_j q_{jk}f_j$ with $\deg q_{jk}=d_1-d_j-1$, again zero in negative degrees. In particular $q_{1k}=0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Multiply these identities by $x_k$ and sum over $k$. The monomial identity $\sum_k x_k\partial_k f_j=d_j f_j$ gives $d_1f_1+\sum_{i>m}a_{i1}d_i f_i=\sum_{j\ne1}(\sum_k x_kq_{jk})f_j$. Since $d_1$ is a nonzero integer, this puts $f_1$ in the ideal of the other basic generators, contrary to their minimality. Thus no polynomial relation exists, proving algebraic independence. The precise exclusion of the self-coefficient is $\deg q_{1k}=-1$, not a positivity claim about arbitrary invariant forms. If $I=0$, step 1.1 gives $R=\mathbb C$ and the empty generating set is algebraically independent. The argument allows degree-one invariants and the trivial group; for $V=0$ it gives that empty set. All selections are finite or least positive degrees. F2 uses only the finite-generation and ascending-chain consequences in its proof, and F3 is invoked only in its explicitly choice-free branch; no AC is needed. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
