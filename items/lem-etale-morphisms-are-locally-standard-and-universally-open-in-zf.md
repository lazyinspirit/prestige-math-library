## Statement

In ZF, let $f:X\to S$ be étale and let $x\in X$. There are affine neighbourhoods of $x$ and $f(x)$, and a further principal neighbourhood of $x$, on which the map is standard étale: its algebra has the form $(A[T]/(P))_g$ with $P$ monic and $P'$ invertible. Every standard étale map is étale. Every étale morphism is universally open. In particular, on a quasi-compact scheme any jointly surjective étale family admits a finite subfamily after refining by affine étale charts. No ambient choice principle, Noetherian hypothesis on the base, or chosen algebraic closure is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The étale map and the one specified source point in the local-chart assertion; a given base change or covering in the remaining assertions.

[F1] Étale at a point means finite presentation, flatness, and geometric regularity of the fibre with relative dimension zero. Local fibre dimension is the infimum of dimensions of its open neighbourhoods. [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]] [[def-relative-dimension-smooth-morphism]]

[F2] For a finite-type algebra quasi-finite at a specified prime, its relative integral closure becomes the whole algebra after inverting one specified integral element outside that prime. No choice hypothesis is assumed in this theorem. [[lem-nagata-algebraic-zariski-main-localization]]

[F3] Every finite separable extension of fields has one primitive element. [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]

[F4] Kähler differentials of a polynomial quotient are generated by differentials of its variables with the derivatives of its defining relations; standard étale algebras have monic equation and invertible derivative. [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]] [[def-standard-etale-algebra]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** a finite-dimensional fibre, choice-free algebraic Zariski main, and characteristic-polynomial image witnesses.

1.1 Work on affine charts $A\to B$ at the specified point $\mathfrak q$, with $B$ finitely presented and $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A$. The fibre $B\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is of finite type over a field. By relative dimension zero in [F1], a principal neighbourhood of the selected fibre point has dimension zero. A finite-type zero-dimensional algebra over a field is finite-dimensional: prove finite Noether normalization by induction on a finite generator list, using a triangular substitution with distinct top degrees to make one nonzero relation monic. If the resulting polynomial subalgebra had a variable, take a prime of the finite algebra over its zero prime and then one above the strictly larger prime generated by that variable. Both are found by maximal ideals in finite-dimensional residue algebras and determinant Nakayama; their contractions form a strict chain, contradicting dimension zero. Thus the normalization has no variable. No algebraic closure or arbitrary maximal-ideal selection is used. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The selected local fibre is a regular local ring of dimension zero by [F1], hence is a field $K=\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ finite over $k=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ by step 1.1. It is separable over $k$: if a finite field extension were inseparable, base change along the specified finite extension $K/k$ would make the diagonal local factor of $K\otimes_k K$ nonreduced, by the repeated minimal polynomial; that zero-dimensional local factor could not be regular. This contradicts the geometric regularity asserted for that particular field extension in [F1]. Hence the map is quasi-finite at the selected prime. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 By [F2], an element $g$ of the integral closure of $A$ in $B$, outside $\mathfrak q$, satisfies $B_g=A'_g$ for that closure $A'$. For the finitely many generators of $B$, choose finitely many representatives in $A'_g$, clear powers of $g$, and let $T$ be the $A$-subalgebra generated by $g$ and those integral numerators. It is finite over $A$ by the determinant/module argument for finitely many integral elements. The inclusion $T\subseteq B$ induces $T_g\simeq B_g$. Thus, after shrinking around $\mathfrak q$, the original chart is a principal open of a finite $A$-algebra. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The fibre $T\otimes_A k$ is a finite-dimensional commutative $k$-algebra and therefore a finite product of its local Artinian factors: factor characteristic/minimal polynomials into finitely many coprime powers and lift the resulting idempotents by finite nilpotent algebra. The selected factor is $K$ by step 2.1. By [F3], choose one nonzero primitive element of $K/k$ and use zero on the other factors. Its coordinates have finitely many denominators from $A\setminus\mathfrak p$; multiply by their common nonzero image in $k$ so the tuple lifts to an element $t\in T$. Multiplication by that nonzero scalar preserves primitive generation and keeps the selected coordinate nonzero. Put $C=A[t]\subseteq T$ and $\mathfrak q'=\mathfrak q\cap C$. [F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The selected finite fibre factor is the only factor whose contraction to $C$ is $\mathfrak q'$: on every other factor $t=0$, while its value at the selected point is nonzero. The finite $C_{\mathfrak q'}$-algebra $T_{\mathfrak q'}$ is local with residue $K$. Indeed, an element outside the selected maximal ideal is invertible modulo that factor; its quotient is a finite module with zero closed fibre, so determinant Nakayama makes the quotient zero. The residue field of $C_{\mathfrak q'}$ is also $K$, since $t$ is primitive. The finite cokernel of $C_{\mathfrak q'}\to T_{\mathfrak q'}$ has zero closed fibre and vanishes by the same determinant argument. The map was injective, so it is an isomorphism. Finite generators let us shrink once more to identify a neighbourhood of $\mathfrak q$ with a localization of the monogenic finite algebra $C=A[t]$. [step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Let $I=\ker(A[T]\to C)$, and let $Q$ be the inverse image of the selected prime. The selected fibre local ring is the separable field $K$, so [F4] gives $\Omega_{C/A}\otimes_CK\cong\Omega_{K/k}=0$. Since the original algebra is finitely presented, $\Omega_{C/A}$ is finite near the point; determinant Nakayama gives $\Omega_{C/A,\mathfrak q}=0$. The polynomial-quotient formula [F4] then gives a finite combination of derivatives of elements of $I$ which is a unit at $Q$. Combining the relations and clearing one denominator outside $Q$ produces $P_1\in I$ with $P_1'(t)$ a unit at the point. [F1, F4, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The element $t$ is integral over $A$, so take a monic $P_0\in I$. For $N\ge2$ with $N\deg P_0>\deg P_1$, the polynomial $P=P_0^N+P_1$ is monic, belongs to $I$, and has $P'(t)=P_1'(t)$, a unit at the point. Put $C'=A[T]/(P)$. The map $C'\to C$ is surjective. Its localized source and target are flat over $A_{\mathfrak p}$: the source is a localization of the monic finite-free algebra, and the target is the original flat étale local algebra. Their selected closed fibres are the same field $K$, because the selected irreducible factor of $P$ occurs once. The localized kernel is finite: the chosen one-generator presentation of the locally finitely presented algebra has finitely many relations. Tensoring the kernel sequence with $k$ is exact by target flatness; the kernel has zero fibre and vanishes by determinant Nakayama. Clearing the finitely many relations gives one principal neighbourhood on which $C'\to C$ is an isomorphism; invert $P'$ there. This is the required standard étale chart. [F1, F4, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Conversely, if $P$ is monic and $P'$ is invertible, the Jacobian calculation for one equation gives a flat finitely presented map with geometrically regular zero-dimensional fibres: $A[T]/P$ is finite free, and after any field extension the derivative is coprime to the equation on the chosen open, so all local fibre factors are separable fields. Thus a standard étale map is étale. [F1, F4, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Let $D=A[T]/(P)$ with $P$ monic of degree $d$, and let $h\in D$. In the finite free basis $1,T,\ldots,T^{d-1}$, multiplication by $h$ has characteristic polynomial $Z^d+c_1Z^{d-1}+\cdots+c_d$. For a prime $\mathfrak p\subset A$, the fibre of $D_h$ is nonempty exactly when $h$ is not nilpotent in the finite-dimensional fibre algebra. Cayley–Hamilton says this is equivalent to at least one $c_i\notin\mathfrak p$. If the localized fibre is nonzero, its finite-dimensional algebra has a maximal ideal by maximizing the dimension of a proper ideal; this is a finite argument. Hence the image of $\operatorname{Spec}(D_h)\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is exactly $\bigcup_iD(c_i)$, an open subset. The same matrices and coefficients after any base change prove universal openness. [step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 A principal open in a standard étale chart is again $D_h$ for a suitable element of the finite monic algebra after clearing a power of the original denominator. Thus its image is open by step 9.1. Arbitrary opens are unions of such principal opens, and the local chart in step 7.1 covers every source point. Therefore every étale map is universally open. For a jointly surjective étale family over a quasi-compact target, images of affine étale chart refinements are an open cover, so finitely many cover the target; the corresponding finitely many charts give the required finite refinement. This concludes without an indexed family of choices. [F1, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, given, construct] ∎
