## Definition

Fix $n\ge1$ and a point $p\in\mathbb C^n$, and write
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ for the ring of holomorphic germs at $p$, with
addition and multiplication of germs defined by representatives on a common
neighbourhood ([[def-holomorphic-germ-ring-and-its-maximal-ideal]]). Recall
that a germ is a unit exactly when its value at $p$ is nonzero
([[prop-units-in-the-holomorphic-germ-ring]]).

A germ $f\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ is a **hypersurface equation germ** at
$p$ when $f$ is nonzero and not a unit.

Such an equation germ $f$ is **reduced** when no irreducible element of
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ divides $f$ twice: there is no irreducible
$q\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ with $q^2\mid f$ in
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$. In other words, a reduced equation germ is a
nonzero nonunit that is not divisible by the square of an irreducible germ.

The zero germ and the unit germs are excluded from hypersurface equations, so
reducedness is only defined for nonzero nonunits.

**Convention for a general centre.** The published germ ring is defined at the
origin, and on this page $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ is read through the
translation convention: the biholomorphism $z\mapsto z+p$ of $\mathbb C^n$
pulls germs at $p$ back to germs at $0$, so $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ denotes
the isomorphic ring of holomorphic germs at $p$, a germ at $p$ corresponds to
its translate $z\mapsto f(z+p)$ at $0$, and the maximal ideal is the translate
of $\mathfrak m_{n,0}$; the dimension lemma proved below identifies it with the
ideal generated by the coordinate differences $z_i-p_i$. Every definition and
result on this page is transported along this identification, which only
relabels the base point. At $p=0$ the convention is the identity.

By the unique factorisation property of the holomorphic germ ring
([[thm-holomorphic-germ-ring-is-a-ufd]]) a nonzero nonunit $f$ has a
factorisation

$$f=u\,q_1^{e_1}\cdots q_r^{e_r}$$

with $u$ a unit, $r\ge1$, the $q_i$ pairwise nonassociate irreducible germs,
and exponents $e_i\ge1$; the $r$-tuple of associate classes of the $q_i$ and
the exponents $e_i$ are determined by $f$. Comparing two such factorisations
shows that $f$ is reduced exactly when every $e_i=1$: if some $e_i\ge2$ then
$q_i^2\mid f$, and conversely a divisor $q^2\mid f$ with $q$ irreducible makes
$q$ associate to one of the $q_i$ with $e_i\ge2$, by uniqueness of the
factorisation applied to a factorisation of the quotient.

Reducedness is a property of the equation germ, not of its zero set. The germs
$z_1$ and $z_1^2$ at the origin of $\mathbb C^2$ are both nonzero nonunits and
have the same zero set near the origin, but only $z_1$ is reduced. The
geometric identification of equations that cut out the same zero set germ is
the subject of the later definition of a hypersurface germ on this page.
