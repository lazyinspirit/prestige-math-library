## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let $K:S^1\to S^3$ be a smooth oriented embedding and write its quotient normal bundle as $\nu_K=K^*TS^3/dK(TS^1)$. Choose an orientation-preserving trivialization $\nu_K\cong S^1\times\mathbb R^2$ and a tubular map $\Phi:\Omega\to S^3$, meaning a smooth embedding from an open neighbourhood $\Omega\subseteq\nu_K$ of the zero section onto an open neighbourhood of $K(S^1)$ with $\Phi(s,0)=K(s)$, such that in these product coordinates $\Omega$ contains $S^1\times D^2_{2\epsilon}$ for some $\epsilon>0$. Here $D^2_r=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:\|z\|\le r\}$ uses the product Euclidean norm induced by the chosen trivialization. Set $N(K)=\Phi(S^1\times D^2_\epsilon)$, $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$, $T_K=\partial E_K$, and let $i:T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$ be inclusion, $p:T_K\to S^1$ the product projection, and $\mu$ the positive boundary of a normal disk, using the normal-plane orientation determined by the orientations of $K$ and $S^3$. A class is primitive if it is not $m$ times another class for any integer $|m|>1$. Then $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$ is generated by $[\mu]$. The kernel of $i_*:H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is a primitive rank-one direct summand generated by a unique class $[\lambda]$ with $p_*[\lambda]=1\in H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)$. It has a simple closed representative, is the preferred longitude, and $([\mu],[\lambda])$ is a basis of $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Under countable choice [A1], [F5] supplies a product tubular map and a closed product disk tube with the stated norm and radius data for every smooth oriented knot. [A1, F5, given, choose]

1.2 Write $D_r^2=\{z\in\mathbb R^2:|z|\le r\}$ in the product coordinates and set $U=\Phi(S^1\times\{|z|<3\epsilon/2\})$ and $V=S^3\setminus\Phi(S^1\times D^2_{\epsilon/2})$. The set $U$ is open because its source is open in the tubular domain and $\Phi$ maps that domain diffeomorphically onto an open set. The removed disk image is compact, hence closed in Hausdorff $S^3$, so $V$ is open. They cover $S^3$, and $U\cap V=\Phi(S^1\times\{\epsilon/2<|z|<3\epsilon/2\})$. [F1, F5, construct]

2.1 Radial homotopies retract $U$ onto $N(K)$ by replacing each fibre radius $r$ with $(1-t)r+t\min(r,\epsilon)$, fixing the zero vector; retract $V$ onto $E_K$ by replacing $r$ with $(1-t)r+t\max(r,\epsilon)$ for $\epsilon/2<r\le2\epsilon$ and fixing points outside the closed radius-$2\epsilon$ product tube; and retract $U\cap V$ onto $T_K$ by replacing $r$ with $(1-t)r+t\epsilon$. Each formula remains within its domain, agrees with the identity at the relevant boundary, and fixes its target. Thus these are deformation retractions. [F3, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 On the overlap, composing its inclusion into $U$ with the first retraction gives radius $\min(r,\epsilon)$, while retracting first to $T_K$ and including into $N(K)$ gives radius $\epsilon$; linear interpolation stays in $N(K)$. For the inclusion into $V$, the corresponding radii are $\max(r,\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon$, and their interpolation stays in $E_K$. Hence, after the deformation-retraction identifications, the two maps in Mayer–Vietoris are the maps induced by $T_K\hookrightarrow N(K)$ and $T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$. By [F7], homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism on homology. [F7, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 By [F9], the Mayer–Vietoris sequence for the open cover of step 1.2 has the segment $$H_2(S^3;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(U\cap V;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(U;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(V;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z).$$ The outer groups vanish by [F6], so the middle map is an isomorphism. Under the identifications of step 3.1, it is induced by the two boundary inclusions. [F6, F7, F9, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 By [F8], $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$ with basis $([\mu],[\sigma])$. The product tube $N(K)\cong S^1\times D^2$ retracts to its core $S^1$ by contraction in each disk fibre, so homotopy invariance [F7] and [F6] give $H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. Put $H=H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. Steps 2.1, 3.1, 4.1 give $\mathbb Z^2\cong\mathbb Z\oplus H$. The isomorphism embeds $H$ as a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^2$. Every subgroup of $\mathbb Z^2$ is free of rank at most two: projection to one coordinate has cyclic image; if nonzero, choose a preimage of its positive generator to split it off, and the kernel is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z$; if zero, the subgroup already lies in $\mathbb Z$. Since the complementary $\mathbb Z$ has rank one and the total rank is two, $H$ has rank one. Therefore $H\cong\mathbb Z$. [F5, F6, F7, F8, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Choose $c$ to be the first component of the Mayer–Vietoris image of $[\sigma]$; it is a generator of $H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)$ because the product section projects with degree one to the core of the product tube [F5, F8]. Choose a generator $h$ of $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$, which exists by step 5.1. The meridian maps to zero in the $N(K)$ component because it bounds its fibre disk. In the bases $([\mu],[\sigma])$ and $(c,h)$, the Mayer–Vietoris isomorphism of step 4.1 has first column $(0,a)$ for some integer $a$ and second column whose first coordinate is $1$. Its determinant is therefore $\pm a$; since the map is an isomorphism over $\mathbb Z$, $a=\pm1$. Thus $[\mu]$ generates $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. [F4, F5, F8, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Let $k\in\mathbb Z$ be the unique integer with $i_*[\sigma]=k[\mu]$, and put $[\lambda]=[\sigma]-k[\mu]$. Then $i_*[\lambda]=0$, $p_*[\lambda]=1$, and the change from the basis $([\mu],[\sigma])$ to $([\mu],[\lambda])$ has determinant $1$. In the new basis, $i_*(a[\mu]+b[\lambda])=a[\mu]$, so the kernel is exactly $\mathbb Z[\lambda]$, a primitive rank-one direct summand. Every degree-one class is $[\sigma]+a[\mu]$ and maps to zero exactly when $a=-k$, proving uniqueness. The curve $t\mapsto(e^{2\pi it},e^{-2\pi ikt})$ is embedded in $S^1\times S^1$ because its first coordinate is injective on $S^1$; it represents $[\lambda]$ and is a preferred longitude. [F4, F8, step 6.1, algebra, construct] ∎
