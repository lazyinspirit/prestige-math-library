## Definition

Work over $\mathbb C$ and fix $n\ge1$ together with a shift matrix
$\sigma=(s_{i,j})_{1\le i,j\le n}$ of nonnegative integers satisfying
$s_{i,j}+s_{j,k}=s_{i,k}$ whenever $i\le j\le k$ or $i\ge j\ge k$, and
$s_{i,i}=0$. The **shifted Yangian** $Y_n(\sigma)$ is the associative unital
$\mathbb C$-algebra ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]) generated by the
shifted Drinfeld generators $D_i^{(r)}$ ($1\le i\le n$, $r\ge1$),
$E_i^{(r)}$ ($1\le i<n$, $r>s_{i,i+1}$), and
$F_i^{(r)}$ ($1\le i<n$, $r>s_{i+1,i}$). For $i<j$, the higher-root
coefficients $E_{i,j}^{(r)}$ have range $r>s_{i,j}$ and
$F_{j,i}^{(r)}$ have range $r>s_{j,i}$; they are defined recursively by
adjacent-root commutators, not declared as a duplicate independent family.
Put $D_i^{(0)}=1$, $D_i(u)=\sum_{r\ge0}D_i^{(r)}u^{-r}$ and $\widetilde D_i(u)=-D_i(u)^{-1}$. The defining relations are the following, for every index for which all displayed generators exist (in particular the $E$ and $F$ indices exceed their respective shifts):

$$[D_i^{(r)},D_j^{(s)}]=0,\qquad [E_i^{(r)},F_j^{(s)}]=\delta_{ij}\sum_{t=0}^{r+s-1}\widetilde D_i^{(t)}D_{i+1}^{(r+s-1-t)}.$$

$$[D_i^{(r)},E_j^{(s)}]=(\delta_{ij}-\delta_{i,j+1})\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}D_i^{(t)}E_j^{(r+s-1-t)}.$$

$$[D_i^{(r)},F_j^{(s)}]=(\delta_{i,j+1}-\delta_{ij})\sum_{t=0}^{r-1}F_j^{(r+s-1-t)}D_i^{(t)}.$$

$$[E_i^{(r)},E_i^{(s+1)}]-[E_i^{(r+1)},E_i^{(s)}]=E_i^{(r)}E_i^{(s)}+E_i^{(s)}E_i^{(r)}.$$

$$[F_i^{(r+1)},F_i^{(s)}]-[F_i^{(r)},F_i^{(s+1)}]=F_i^{(r)}F_i^{(s)}+F_i^{(s)}F_i^{(r)}.$$

$$[E_i^{(r)},E_{i+1}^{(s+1)}]-[E_i^{(r+1)},E_{i+1}^{(s)}]=-E_i^{(r)}E_{i+1}^{(s)}.$$

$$[F_i^{(r+1)},F_{i+1}^{(s)}]-[F_i^{(r)},F_{i+1}^{(s+1)}]=-F_{i+1}^{(s)}F_i^{(r)}.$$

For $|i-j|>1$, $[E_i^{(r)},E_j^{(s)}]=[F_i^{(r)},F_j^{(s)}]=0$. For $|i-j|=1$ impose

$$[E_i^{(r)},[E_i^{(s)},E_j^{(t)}]]+[E_i^{(s)},[E_i^{(r)},E_j^{(t)}]]=0,$$

$$[F_i^{(r)},[F_i^{(s)},F_j^{(t)}]]+[F_i^{(s)},[F_i^{(r)},F_j^{(t)}]]=0.$$

The higher roots are specified without new independent generators by $E_{i,i+1}^{(r)}=E_i^{(r)}$, $F_{i+1,i}^{(r)}=F_i^{(r)}$, and, for $j>i+1$,

$$E_{i,j}^{(r)}=[E_{i,j-1}^{(r-s_{j-1,j})},E_{j-1}^{(s_{j-1,j}+1)}],\qquad F_{j,i}^{(r)}=[F_{j-1}^{(s_{j,j-1}+1)},F_{j-1,i}^{(r-s_{j,j-1})}].$$

These are [BK, Section 2.1, (2.7)–(2.18), and Section 2.2, (2.21)–(2.22)], with the negative root written in matrix-row order here. Two filtrations are fixed on it: the **canonical
filtration**, in which each generator of upper index $r$ has degree $r$, and
the **loop filtration**, in which it has degree $r-1$; their associated
graded algebras are different (the canonical one is commutative polynomial,
the loop one is a current algebra).

For the pyramid $\lambda=(0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n)$ with conjugate
$q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$ ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]])
the relevant shift matrix is $s_{i,j}=p_j-p_i$ for $i\le j$ and $s_{i,j}=0$
for $i\ge j$, and the **type-A finite W-algebra** is the quotient
$$W(\lambda)=Y_n(\sigma)/\bigl(D_1^{(r)},\ r>p_1\bigr)$$
of $Y_n(\sigma)$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the higher modes of
$D_1$ beyond $p_1$.

Write the Gauss decomposition $T(u)=F(u)D(u)E(u)$ of the generating matrix,
with $T(u)=\sum_{r\ge0}T_{i,j}^{(r)}u^{-r}$, $T_{i,j}^{(0)}=\delta_{i,j}$, and
**no** additional factor $u^{s_{i,j}}$: the coefficient of $u^{-r}$ is the
mode $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$. Its modes satisfy $T_{i,j}^{(r)}=0$ for
$0<r\le s_{i,j}$, and in the finite quotient also $T_{i,j}^{(r)}=0$ for
$r>S_{i,j}:=s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$, so the finite quotient has the PBW basis
of ordered monomials
$$T_{i,j}^{(r)},\qquad s_{i,j}<r\le S_{i,j}.$$
The auxiliary signed path polynomials used in the source to construct the
Gauss generators are not all identified with these shifted $T$-modes; the root
of $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ is the weight $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$, and the
surviving modes have loop leading term $(-1)^{r-1}e_{i,j;r-1}$ in the
centralizer basis of the pyramid.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, a shift matrix $\sigma$ as above, the pyramid $\lambda$ with row lengths $0\le p_1\le\cdots\le p_n$ and columns $q_1\ge\cdots\ge q_l$, and the generators and filtrations of the Definition.

[F1] The pyramid data: $e\in\mathfrak g_1$ of Jordan type $\lambda$, the centralizer basis $e_{i,j;r}$ with $s_{i,j}\le r<p_j$, and the character $\chi$; the finite W-algebra $W(\lambda)$ is generated by $\mathfrak p$-matrix coefficients subject to the loop filtration ([[def-a-type-a-nilpotent-pyramid-good-grading-and-whittaker-character]], [[def-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-and-whittaker-category]]).

[F2] The RTT presentation: the ordinary Yangian RTT algebra is generated by the matrix coefficients $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$ with the RTT relation $[T_{i,j}^{(r)},T_{h,k}^{(s)}]=\sum_{t=0}^{\min(r,s)-1}(T_{i,k}^{(r+s-1-t)}T_{h,j}^{(t)}-T_{i,k}^{(t)}T_{h,j}^{(r+s-1-t)})$ and $T^{(0)}=I$; the Gauss factorization is formal matrix multiplication and inversion, and the structure constants of the D/E/F presentation are the coefficients of the products of the RTT blocks ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]]).

[F3] The local well-definedness suppliers are [[thm-ordered-monomial-pbw-basis-for-the-shifted-yangian]] and [[thm-the-loop-filtration-on-the-type-a-finite-w-algebra-has-associated-graded-u-of-g-e]], declared in `justified_by`. Their proofs start from the abstract presentation and the independently defined quantum Hamiltonian reduction: invariant original/reflected path coefficients, finite Levi symbol independence and an exact weighted slice-dimension comparison establish the finite reduction basis; index-complete coefficient reduction establishes the truncated quotient and finite $T$ range. Ambient enveloping PBW establishes loop-symbol independence. These results justify the Definition, rather than taking its asserted finite PBW basis as a premise.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The abstract RTT/shifted Drinfeld data, the original pyramid shift, and the local well-definedness constructions of [F3].

1.1 **Ranges and shift arithmetic.** The monotone shift identities hold by telescoping $p_k-p_i=(p_j-p_i)+(p_k-p_j)$ on increasing indices and by zero lower shifts on decreasing indices. The adjacent positive generators have threshold $s_{i,i+1}$, the adjacent negative generators threshold $s_{i+1,i}$, and their higher-root commutator recursions give respectively $s_{i,j}$ and $s_{j,i}$. Thus for the asymmetric original pyramid an upper coefficient begins after $p_j-p_i$ whereas its lower counterpart begins at mode $1$; these are distinct ranges. The formal Gauss coefficients are computed by triangular inversion with diagonal constant term $1$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 **Infinite filtrations.** The RTT relation lowers canonical degree under a commutator, and its degree-$r+s-2$ loop part is the current Lie bracket. The actual ordered-product evaluation and Gauss Jacobian in [F3] prove independence of the ordered root-mode words, while the defining relations give spanning with both degree assignments. Therefore the canonical associated graded is polynomial and the loop associated graded is the enveloping algebra of the shifted current Lie subalgebra. Their distinction follows from their respective multiplication, rather than filtered-element independence alone. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Finite quotient and actual reduction.** By [F3], the actual signed path images satisfy the shifted coefficient relations and $D_1^{(r)}=0$ for $r>p_1$. The coefficient reductions give finite spanning words in the abstract quotient. Their images are independent invariant monomials, whose weighted count equals every filtered dimension of the independently constructed whole reduction. Hence the quotient maps isomorphically to that reduction. The finite $T$ coefficient induction gives zero below the shift and above $S_{i,j}=s_{i,j}+p_{\min(i,j)}$, and the finite Gauss-to-$T$ coordinate change gives the ordered surviving $T$-monomial basis. This uses finite spanning and independent reduction images; restriction of an infinite PBW basis to a quotient would not prove independence. Only $D_1$ is directly truncated: higher diagonal Gauss coefficients remain polynomial expressions. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Weights and actual loop symbols.** The path of canonical degree $r$ with $k$ matrix factors has good degree at most $r-k$, so only its single-factor part contributes in loop degree $r-1$. It is $(-1)^{r-1}e_{i,j;r-1}$, of row weight $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$. The ambient homogeneous centralizer basis and enveloping PBW in [F3] make these symbols and their ordered products independent, establishing the finite loop filtration. Auxiliary paths use their declared cut and sign conventions and are not automatically all the shifted $T$ coefficients. For $p=(1,2)$ the upper threshold is $s_{1,2}=1$, so the first upper coefficient is $T_{1,2}^{(2)}$ with symbol $-e_{1,2;1}$; the corresponding lower threshold is $s_{2,1}=0$. This verifies the unrescaled coefficient convention and the asymmetric range. [F1, F3, step 2.2, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The index convention is deliberately unshifted: the coefficient of $u^{-r}$ is $T_{i,j}^{(r)}$, no power of $u$ is inserted, and the filtrations only change the assignation of degrees. Rescaling the series by $u^{s_{i,j}}$ would shift the ranges and is not used anywhere on this page.

- The finite quotient keeps the higher diagonal modes $D_i^{(r)}$ as polynomial expressions rather than setting them to zero; only the modes $D_1^{(r)}$ with $r>p_1$ are truncated.
