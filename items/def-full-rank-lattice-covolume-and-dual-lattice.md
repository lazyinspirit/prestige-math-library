## Definition

Let $n\ge1$. A **full-rank lattice** in $\mathbb R^n$ is a subgroup
$\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ of the form

$$\Lambda=A\mathbb Z^n=\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^n k_ia_i:k=(k_1,\dots,k_n)\in\mathbb Z^n\Bigr\},$$

where $A$ is an invertible real $n\times n$ matrix with columns $a_1,\dots,a_n$;
here $Ak$ is the matrix product ([[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]) and
the column matrix $Ak$ is identified with the vector it represents. A
full-rank lattice is exactly a full Euclidean lattice in the sense of
[[def-full-euclidean-lattice-and-covolume]]: invertibility of $A$ makes its
columns linearly independent over $\mathbb R$
([[thm-real-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-nonzero]]), so they form a
real basis of $\mathbb R^n$ and $\Lambda$ is their $\mathbb Z$-span, and
conversely the basis matrix of every full Euclidean lattice is invertible. The
**covolume** of $\Lambda$ is

$$\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda):=|\det A|>0,$$

with the determinant and the real absolute value of
[[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]], and the **dual lattice** is

$$\Lambda^*:=\{\xi\in\mathbb R^n:\xi\cdot\lambda\in\mathbb Z\text{ for every }\lambda\in\Lambda\}=A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n,$$

where $\xi\cdot\lambda$ is the Euclidean inner product
([[def-euclidean-inner-product]]), $A^{-T}:=(A^{-1})^{T}=(A^{T})^{-1}$ is formed
with the transpose of [[def-transpose-of-a-matrix]] and the inverse of
[[def-invertible-matrix-and-general-linear-group]]
([[prop-transpose-laws]] for the identity $(A^{-1})^{T}=(A^{T})^{-1}$, and
[[thm-real-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-nonzero]] for the existence
of $A^{-1}$).

**Well-definedness: the presentation does not matter.** Suppose
$B\mathbb Z^n=A\mathbb Z^n$ for a second invertible real matrix $B$. Then
$U:=A^{-1}B$ has integer entries: each column $b_j$ of $B$ lies in
$A\mathbb Z^n$, say $b_j=Au_j$ with $u_j\in\mathbb Z^n$, so
$U=(u_1\ \cdots\ u_n)\in M_n(\mathbb Z)$
([[def-matrices-over-a-commutative-ring]],
[[def-ring-matrix-product-identity-and-transpose]]). Exchanging the roles of
$A$ and $B$ shows likewise that $U^{-1}=B^{-1}A$ has integer entries. Hence
$\det U\in\mathbb Z$ and $\det U^{-1}\in\mathbb Z$, and
$\det U\cdot\det U^{-1}=\det(UU^{-1})=\det I_n=1$
([[thm-determinant-multiplicative]], [[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]);
by [[lem-units-of-z]] the only way two integers can multiply to $1$ is
$\det U=\det U^{-1}=\pm1$ (with the same sign). Consequently
$|\det B|=|\det A|\,|\det U|=|\det A|$, so the covolume is independent of the
presentation. For the dual, $B^{-T}\mathbb Z^n=A^{-T}U^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$ by
[[prop-transpose-laws]], and $U^{-T}=(U^{-1})^{T}$ as well as $U^{T}$ have
integer entries ([[def-transpose-of-a-matrix]]); matrices with integer entries
send $\mathbb Z^n$ into $\mathbb Z^n$ under matrix multiplication
([[def-ring-matrix-product-identity-and-transpose]]), so
$U^{-T}\mathbb Z^n\subseteq\mathbb Z^n$ and, applying the same statement to the
integer matrix $U^{T}=(U^{-T})^{-1}$, also
$\mathbb Z^n\subseteq U^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$. Hence $U^{-T}\mathbb Z^n=\mathbb Z^n$
and $B^{-T}\mathbb Z^n=A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$.

**The dual in closed form.** For $\xi=A^{-T}m$ with $m\in\mathbb Z^n$ and
$\lambda=Ak$ with $k\in\mathbb Z^n$ one has
$\xi\cdot\lambda=(A^{-T}m)\cdot(Ak)=m\cdot k\in\mathbb Z$, because
$(A^{-T}m)\cdot(Ak)=m\cdot(A^{-1}Ak)$; so $A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$ is contained in
the defining set. Conversely, if $\xi\cdot\lambda\in\mathbb Z$ for every
$\lambda\in\Lambda$, then testing $\lambda=a_j=Ae_j$ gives
$\xi\cdot a_j=(A^{T}\xi)_j\in\mathbb Z$ for every $j$, so
$A^{T}\xi\in\mathbb Z^n$ and $\xi=A^{-T}(A^{T}\xi)\in A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$. This
also shows that the second description of $\Lambda^*$ depends only on
$\Lambda$. The dual is itself a full-rank lattice: $A^{-T}$ is invertible, its
columns being a real basis, and
$\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda^*)=|\det A^{-T}|=|\det A^{-1}|=|\det A|^{-1}=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)^{-1}$,
using $\det A^{-T}=\det A^{-1}$ ([[thm-determinant-of-transpose]]) and
$\det A^{-1}=(\det A)^{-1}$ ([[cor-determinant-of-an-inverse]]). For
$\Lambda=\mathbb Z^n$, that is $A=I_n$, one gets $\Lambda^*=\mathbb Z^n$
because $I_n^{-T}=I_n$ ([[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]]).

**Characters.** For $\lambda^*\in\mathbb R^n$ put
$\chi_{\lambda^*}(x):=e^{2\pi i\,\lambda^*\cdot x}$, with the complex
exponential of [[def-complex-exponential]]. Then
$\chi_{\lambda^*}(x+\lambda)=\chi_{\lambda^*}(x)e^{2\pi i\,\lambda^*\cdot\lambda}$
for every $\lambda\in\Lambda$, so $\chi_{\lambda^*}$ is $\Lambda$-periodic
exactly when $e^{2\pi i\,\lambda^*\cdot\lambda}=1$ for every
$\lambda\in\Lambda$, that is ([[thm-kernel-and-fibres-of-complex-exponential]])
exactly when $\lambda^*\cdot\lambda\in\mathbb Z$ for every
$\lambda\in\Lambda$ --- precisely the condition $\lambda^*\in\Lambda^*$. Thus
$\lambda^*\mapsto\chi_{\lambda^*}$ is a bijection from $\Lambda^*$ onto the set
of exponential characters $\chi_{\lambda^*}$ of the torus
$\mathbb R^n/\Lambda$, and it is a group isomorphism for pointwise
multiplication because $\chi_{\lambda^*+\eta^*}=\chi_{\lambda^*}\chi_{\eta^*}$;
this is the lattice case of the characters of
[[def-pontryagin-dual-and-compact-open-topology]]. The assertion is about the
characters of the displayed exponential form; no claim is made here that every
continuous character of $\mathbb R^n/\Lambda$ is of that form.

No choice principle is used anywhere in this item.
