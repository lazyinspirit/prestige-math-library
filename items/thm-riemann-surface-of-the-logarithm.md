## Statement

Let $\mathcal R_{\log}$ be the Riemann surface of the complete analytic
function generated by the principal logarithm germ at $1$ over
$\mathbb C^\times$. Define

$$\Lambda:\mathcal R_{\log}\to\mathbb C,\qquad \Lambda([\ell]_z):=\ell(z).$$

Then $\Lambda$ is a biholomorphism, and if
$p:\mathcal R_{\log}\to\mathbb C^\times$ is the germ projection, then

$$p=\exp\circ\Lambda.$$

So, after identifying $\mathcal R_{\log}$ with $\mathbb C$ through $\Lambda$,
the projection is the exponential covering
$\exp:\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^\times$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The logarithm germ surface $\mathcal R_{\log}$ and its projection $p$.

[L1] On the principal strip $P=\{w:-\pi<\operatorname{Im}w<\pi\}$, the
exponential is a biholomorphism onto the slit plane
$S=\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$, with inverse the principal logarithm
([[thm-principal-exponential-biholomorphism-from-principal-strip]]).

[L2] $\exp u=\exp v$ exactly when $u-v\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$
([[thm-kernel-and-fibres-of-complex-exponential]]).

[L3] The germ projection on a complete analytic function is a local
biholomorphism ([[thm-germ-projection-is-a-local-biholomorphism]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For each $w \in \mathbb C$, let $V_w:=e^w B(1,1/2)$ and define $\ell_w(u):=w+\operatorname{Log}(e^{-w}u)$ for $u\in V_w$. Because $B(1,1/2)\subseteq S$, fact [L1] makes $\ell_w$ holomorphic on $V_w$, with $\exp(\ell_w(u))=e^w e^{\operatorname{Log}(e^{-w}u)}=u$ and $\ell_w(e^w)=w$. Thus $\Phi(w):=[\ell_w]_{e^w}$ is a well-defined logarithm germ over $e^w$. [L1, algebra]

1.2 The germ $\Phi(w)$ lies on $\mathcal R_{\log}$. Indeed, along the path $\gamma_w(t)=e^{tw}$ from $1$ to $e^w$, choose a subdivision fine enough that for every adjacent pair $t_j,t_{j+1}$ and every $t\in[t_j,t_{j+1}]$ one has $$|e^{(t-t_j)w}-1|<\frac12,$$ and also $|\operatorname{Im}((t_{j+1}-t_j)w)|<\pi$. The first condition puts the entire subpath $\gamma_w([t_j,t_{j+1}])$ inside $V_{t_jw}$. At the joining point, the second condition gives $$\ell_{t_jw}(e^{t_{j+1}w})=t_jw+\operatorname{Log}(e^{(t_{j+1}-t_j)w})=t_{j+1}w,$$ so consecutive branches agree there as germs. Starting at $t_0=0$ gives the principal logarithm germ at $1$, and the final germ is $\Phi(w)$. [L1, choose]

2.1 If $\Phi(w)=\Phi(v)$, then the two germs have the same base point and the same value at that base point. Therefore $e^w=e^v$ and $w=v$. So $\Phi$ is injective. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Let $\xi=[\ell]_z$ be any point of $\mathcal R_{\log}$ and put $w:=\ell(z)$. Shrink the representative domain of $\ell$ to a disc $U \subseteq V_w$ on which $\ell(U)\subseteq B(w,\pi)$. On $U$ one has $\exp(\ell(u))=u=\exp(\ell_w(u))$, so [L2] gives $\ell(u)-\ell_w(u)\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ for every $u\in U$. The difference is continuous, takes the value $0$ at $z$, and its image lies in the discrete set $2\pi i\mathbb Z$; because $U$ is connected, the difference is identically $0$. Hence $\xi=\Phi(w)$, and $\Phi$ is surjective. [L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The map $\Lambda$ is the inverse of $\Phi$ by steps 2.1 and 2.2. In a chart $N(\ell,U)$ on the germ surface one has $\Lambda\circ\phi_{\ell,U}^{-1}(u)=\ell(u)$, so [L3] makes $\Lambda$ holomorphic. Near any $w_0 \in \mathbb C$, if $|w-w_0|<1/4$ then $\Phi(w)\in N(\ell_{w_0},V_{w_0})$ and $\phi_{\ell_{w_0},V_{w_0}}(\Phi(w))=e^w$, which is holomorphic in $w$; so $\Phi$ is holomorphic as well. Therefore $\Lambda$ is a biholomorphism. [L3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 For every $[\ell]_z \in \mathcal R_{\log}$ one has $p([\ell]_z)=z=\exp(\ell(z))=\exp(\Lambda([\ell]_z))$, so $p=\exp\circ\Lambda$. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
