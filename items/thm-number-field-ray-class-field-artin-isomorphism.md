## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC), Countable Choice (CC), and Dependent Choice (DC). Let $K$ be a number field and let $\mathfrak m=(\mathfrak m_0,S_\infty)$ be a modulus, consisting of a nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak m_0$ and a finite set $S_\infty$ of real places. Let $C_{\mathfrak m}\le C_K$ be the open subgroup of idèle classes whose finite components are units congruent to $1$ modulo $\mathfrak m_0$ and whose real components are positive at every place in $S_\infty$. Then the associated ray class group is $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)=C_K/C_{\mathfrak m}$. There is a unique finite abelian extension $K_{\mathfrak m}/K$ with norm subgroup $C_{\mathfrak m}$, and global Artin reciprocity gives

$$\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\xrightarrow{\sim}\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak m}/K).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice (AC), Countable Choice (CC), and Dependent Choice (DC); a number field $K$ and modulus $\mathfrak m$.

[F1] The ray class group is the group of fractional ideals prime to the finite modulus modulo the principal ideals generated by elements congruent to $1$ at its finite primes and positive at its selected real places. [[def-ray-class-group]] [[lem-ray-class-congruence-subgroup-well-defined]]

[F2] Ideles have the restricted-product topology, and $C_K$ has the quotient topology. Finite-place valuations are normalized to have value group $\mathbb Z$, with finite residue fields and uniformizers; principal fractional ideals have the corresponding valuations. [[def-idele-group-of-a-global-field]] [[def-idele-class-group-of-a-global-field]] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]] [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]]

[F3] Every open finite-index subgroup of $C_K$ is the norm subgroup of a unique finite abelian extension. [[thm-global-class-field-existence-number-field]]

[F4] The Artin map identifies the quotient by a finite abelian norm subgroup with the Galois group. [[thm-global-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]]

[F5] Weak approximation prescribes finitely many open conditions at finite and real places. The ray exact sequence has residue/sign group mapping onto the kernel of the map to the ordinary ideal class group; the ordinary ideal class group is finite. [[thm-weak-approximation-for-global-fields]] [[thm-ray-class-exact-sequence]] [[thm-finiteness-of-the-number-field-class-group]]


[A1] The approximation, ray exact sequence and class-group finiteness inputs [F5] are covered by AC and Countable Choice; global existence and reciprocity [F3]–[F4] also assume DC. These are the unchanged public hypotheses, used in steps 1.1–4.1. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]
## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Write $U_{\mathfrak m}$ for the idelic subgroup with factor $1+\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}}\mathcal O_{K_v}$ at a finite prime dividing $\mathfrak m_0$, factor $\mathcal O_{K_v}^\times$ at every other finite prime, positive real factor at each selected real place, and full multiplicative factor at every other infinite place. The subgroup $C_{\mathfrak m}$ in the Statement is its image in $C_K$: the conditions mean that a class has such a representative. These factors are subgroups (the valuation identity $u^{-1}-1=-(u-1)/u$ verifies inverse closure for the principal units). They are open, and differ from the reference unit factors at only finitely many finite places. Thus $U_{\mathfrak m}$ is open. Its saturated inverse image in the ideles is $K^\times U_{\mathfrak m}$, a union of open translates, so $C_{\mathfrak m}$ is open by the quotient topology. [F2, given, construct]

1.2 Let $J_{\mathfrak m}$ be the subgroup of ideles satisfying just the selected finite congruences and real positivity, with no unit restriction at other finite places. For an arbitrary idele $x$, weak approximation [F5] supplies $a\in K^\times$ with $x_v/a\in1+\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}}\mathcal O_{K_v}$ at each selected finite place and $x_v/a>0$ at each selected real place. These are open conditions on $a$ about the nonzero $x_v$; if none are prescribed take $a=1$. Hence $x/a\in J_{\mathfrak m}$, and $J_{\mathfrak m}$ surjects onto $C_K$. Its intersection with the principal ideles is $K_{\mathfrak m,1}$ of [F1]. [F1, F2, F5, choose, construct]

2.1 The valuation map $J_{\mathfrak m}\to I_K(\mathfrak m_0)$ sends $x$ to $\prod_{\mathfrak p\nmid\mathfrak m_0}\mathfrak p^{v_{\mathfrak p}(x_{\mathfrak p})}$. This product has finite support by the idele condition. The map is onto: realize each of the finitely many ideal exponents by a local uniformizer power and put $1$ at all other components. Its kernel is $U_{\mathfrak m}$, and its image on $K_{\mathfrak m,1}$ is exactly the ray-principal subgroup. Step 1.2 and the quotient homomorphisms therefore give $C_K/C_{\mathfrak m}\cong J_{\mathfrak m}/(K_{\mathfrak m,1}U_{\mathfrak m})\cong\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)$. More explicitly, the kernel of $J_{\mathfrak m}\to C_K/C_{\mathfrak m}$ is $J_{\mathfrak m}\cap K^\times U_{\mathfrak m}=K_{\mathfrak m,1}U_{\mathfrak m}$, since $U_{\mathfrak m}\subseteq J_{\mathfrak m}$. This supplies the quotient identification rather than assuming it from the principal-ideal subgroup lemma. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra, construct]

3.1 The residue/sign group of [F5] is finite: each $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p^n$ injects into $\mathcal O_{K_v}/\mathfrak p^n\mathcal O_{K_v}$ by the valuation criterion, and this latter ring is finite by its $n$ successive residue-field quotients from [F2]; there are only finitely many sign factors. The exact sequence [F5] and finiteness of the ordinary class group now imply finiteness of $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)$. By step 2.1, $C_{\mathfrak m}$ has finite index. Together with step 1.1 this verifies all the hypotheses of [F3], which supplies a unique finite abelian extension $K_{\mathfrak m}/K$ with $N_{K_{\mathfrak m}/K}C_{K_{\mathfrak m}}=C_{\mathfrak m}$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, A1, algebra]

4.1 Applying [F4] to the extension in step 3.1 identifies $C_K/C_{\mathfrak m}$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathfrak m}/K)$. Transporting this map through step 2.1 gives the asserted Artin isomorphism from $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)$. The constructions also cover the unit ideal modulus and an empty selected real set. [F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra, A1] ∎
