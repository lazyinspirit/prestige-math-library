## Statement

Let
$$Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])=\{z\in\mathbb C[S_n]: zh=hz\text{ for every }h\in S_{n-1}\}$$
be the centralizer of the subalgebra $\mathbb C[S_{n-1}]$ in
$\mathbb C[S_n]$. Then
$Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ is a commutative subalgebra of
$\mathbb C[S_n]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, the group $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,n\})$ with its subgroup $S_{n-1}=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,n-1\})$ of permutations fixing $n$, and the group algebra $\mathbb C[S_n]$ with basis $\{[g]:g\in S_n\}$ and multiplication $[g][h]=[gh]$ ([[def-partition-young-diagram-and-conjugate-partition]], [[def-symmetric-group]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]).

[F1] For every $g\in S_n$ there is an involution $h_g\in S_{n-1}$ with $h_ggh_g^{-1}=g^{-1}$; the proof of the cited lemma exhibits $h_g$ by explicit formulas on the cycles of $g$ ([[lem-symmetric-group-conjugation-to-inverse-within-the-preceding-group]]).

[F2] $\mathbb C[S_n]$ has the group elements as a $\mathbb C$-basis, every element has a unique expansion $\sum_{g}c_g[g]$ with finitely many nonzero coefficients, and $[g][h]=[gh]$; coefficients of equal basis elements are equal. The inverse in $S_n$ reverses products: $(gh)^{-1}=h^{-1}g^{-1}$ ([[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]], [[def-symmetric-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** inversion anti-automorphism.

1.1 The centralizer $C:=Z(\mathbb C[S_n],\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ is a $\mathbb C$-subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$: it contains $1$, is closed under addition and scalar multiplication because equality with each $h\in S_{n-1}$ is preserved by these operations, and is closed under multiplication because $yz\,h=y\,hz=h\,yz$ for all $h\in S_{n-1}$ whenever $y,z\in C$; closure under multiplication is also checked on the basis expansions using [F2]. [F2, given, algebra]

1.2 Define $\sigma:\mathbb C[S_n]\to\mathbb C[S_n]$ on the basis by $\sigma([g]):=[g^{-1}]$ and extend $\mathbb C$-linearly: $\sigma(\sum_gc_g[g])=\sum_gc_g[g^{-1}]$. Then $\sigma$ is an involution, since $(g^{-1})^{-1}=g$, and it is an anti-automorphism: $\sigma([g][h])=\sigma([gh])=[(gh)^{-1}]=[h^{-1}g^{-1}]=[h^{-1}][g^{-1}]=\sigma([h])\sigma([g])$ by [F2], and the identity extends to all elements by bilinearity. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 The anti-automorphism $\sigma$ fixes every element of $C$. Let $z=\sum_gc_g[g]\in C$, and fix one $g\in S_n$. By [F1] there is $h_g\in S_{n-1}$ with $h_ggh_g^{-1}=g^{-1}$. Since $z=h_gzh_g^{-1}$, comparison of the coefficient of $[g^{-1}]$ on the two sides gives $c_{g^{-1}}=c_g$; conjugation is a bijection, so exactly the summand indexed by $g$ contributes on the right. This equality holds for every $g$, and therefore $\sigma(z)=z$ by [F2]. The argument compares each coefficient separately and requires no common conjugator. [step 1.2, F1, F2, algebra]

3.1 For $y,z\in C$ we have $yz=\sigma(yz)=\sigma(z)\sigma(y)=zy$: the first equality is step 2.1, the second holds because $\sigma$ is an anti-automorphism by step 1.2, and the third is step 2.1 applied to $y$ and to $z$. Hence $C$ is a commutative subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ by step 1.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Choice-free.** The element $h_g$ is constructed in [[lem-symmetric-group-conjugation-to-inverse-within-the-preceding-group]] by reversing the cycles of $g$, so no selection principle is used; the argument also avoids Maschke's theorem and any semisimplicity input, and works verbatim with $\mathbb C$ replaced by any commutative ring.

- **What the centralizer contains.** The centre $Z(\mathbb C[S_{n-1}])$ and the element $X_n=\sum_{j<n}(j\ n)$ both lie in $C$: elements of the centre commute with $S_{n-1}$, and conjugating $(j\ n)$ by $h\in S_{n-1}$ gives $(h(j)\ n)$, so conjugation permutes the summands of $X_n$. The opposite inclusion, that $C$ is generated by these two pieces, is [[thm-relative-centralizer-is-generated-by-the-previous-center-and-last-jucys-murphy-element]].
