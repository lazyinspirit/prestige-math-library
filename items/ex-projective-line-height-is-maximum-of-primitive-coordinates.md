## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For every $P\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$, choose coprime integers $a,b$, not both zero, with $P=[a:b]$. Then the absolute logarithmic height satisfies
$$
h(P)=\log\max\{|a|,|b|\},
$$
or equivalently $H(P)=\max\{|a|,|b|\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a rational projective point $P\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)$ and a primitive integer pair $(a,b)$ representing it.

[A1] AC is the premise required by the absolute-height definition. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] For $K=\mathbb Q$, the degree is one, the only archimedean factor is the ordinary maximum, and the finite factor is the inverse norm of the ideal generated by the coordinates. ([[def-absolute-logarithmic-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]], [[def-absolute-multiplicative-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]])

## Verification

1.1 Clear denominators in any rational representative of $P$ and divide the resulting integer pair by its greatest common divisor; this gives a primitive pair $(a,b)$. If another primitive integer pair represents the same point, it is $q(a,b)$ for $q=m/n$ in lowest terms; integrality forces $n$ to divide both $a$ and $b$, so $n=1$, and primitivity of the second pair forces $|m|=1$. Thus the displayed maximum is independent of the choice. [given, A1, choose, algebra]

2.1 Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$, Bezout's identity gives integers $u,v$ with $ua+vb=1$, so $(a,b)=\mathbb Z$ and its ideal norm is $1$. The relative height formula in [F1] therefore has finite factor $1$ and archimedean factor $\max\{|a|,|b|\}$. Since $[\mathbb Q:\mathbb Q]=1$, this equals the absolute multiplicative height; taking its logarithm gives the displayed formula. [given, A1, F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
