## Statement

Let $A \to B$ and $B \to C$ be integral ring maps. Then the composite map $A \to C$ is integral.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Integral ring maps $A \to B$ and $B \to C$.

[L1] A ring map is integral exactly when every element of the target ring is integral over the source ring ([[def-integral-ring-extension]]).

[L2] A subalgebra generated by finitely many integral elements is module-finite over the base ring ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

[L3] If $B$ is module-finite over $A$ and $M$ is module-finite over $B$, then $M$ is module-finite over $A$ ([[lem-transitivity-of-module-finiteness]]).

[L4] For a nonzero commutative ring $R$ and $c$ in an $R$-algebra, the following are equivalent: $c$ is integral over $R$; $R[c]$ is a finitely generated $R$-module; and there exists a faithful $R[c]$-module finitely generated over $R$ ([[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $A=0$, then $1_A=0_A$, so every unital image of $A$ is the zero ring; hence $B=0$ and then $C=0$, making the composite integral trivially. For the rest of the proof assume $A\ne0$. [L1, given, algebra]

1.2 Let $c\in C$. By [L1], the element $c$ is integral over $B$, so there is a monic equation $c^n+b_{n-1}c^{n-1}+\cdots+b_0=0$ with $b_i\in B$. Again by [L1], each coefficient $b_i$ is integral over $A$, so [L2] makes the $A$-subalgebra $D:=A[b_0,\ldots,b_{n-1}]$ module-finite over $A$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 The same equation for $c$ has coefficients in $D$, so $c$ is integral over $D$. Because $D$ is an $A$-subalgebra of $B$, it contains the image of $1_A=1_B$, so it is nonzero. Therefore [L4] makes $D[c]$ a finitely generated $D$-module, and then [L3] gives that $D[c]$ is a finitely generated $A$-module. [L3, L4, step 1.2, given]

3.1 The ring $D[c]$ is a faithful module over the subring $A[c]$, and step 2.1 shows that this faithful $A[c]$-module is finitely generated over $A$. By [L4], $c$ is integral over $A$. Since $c\in C$ was arbitrary, the map $A\to C$ is integral. [L4, step 2.1, given] ∎
