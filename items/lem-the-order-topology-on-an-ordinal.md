## Statement

Let $\gamma$ be an ordinal ([[def-ordinal]]), regarded as the set of ordinals
below it, linearly ordered by membership ([[lem-ordinal-trichotomy]]), and give it
the order topology ([[def-order-topology-on-a-linearly-ordered-set]]). For
$\alpha, \beta \in \gamma$ write

$$[0,\beta] := \{\, \xi \in \gamma : \xi \le \beta \,\}, \qquad (\alpha,\beta] := \{\, \xi \in \gamma : \alpha < \xi \le \beta \,\} .$$

Then:

1. Every set of either form is **clopen** ([[def-topological-space]]), and
   $$\mathcal{B}_\gamma \;:=\; \{\, [0,\beta] : \beta \in \gamma \,\} \;\cup\; \{\, (\alpha,\beta] : \alpha, \beta \in \gamma \,\}$$
   is a basis for the order topology of $\gamma$
   ([[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).
2. The isolated points of $\gamma$ ([[def-interior-closure-boundary-top]]) are
   exactly the ordinals $\xi \in \gamma$ that are $0$ or a successor; a limit
   ordinal $\xi \in \gamma$ ([[def-limit-ordinal]]) is not isolated.
3. $\gamma$ with its order topology is Hausdorff ([[def-hausdorff-space]]).

**Regularity is not claimed here**, and nothing below asserts any separation
property beyond claim 3; the finer separation axioms are not available at this
point in the reading order.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An ordinal $\gamma$ with the order topology of the membership order on it.

[L1] $\gamma$ is the set of the ordinals below it; membership is a strict linear order on it, $\alpha \le \beta$ abbreviates "$\alpha \in \beta$ or $\alpha = \beta$", and $\alpha \subseteq \beta$ holds exactly when $\alpha \le \beta$ ([[def-ordinal]], [[lem-ordinal-basics]], [[lem-ordinal-trichotomy]], [[def-partial-order]]).

[L2] The order topology of a linearly ordered set $L$ is generated by the open rays $L_{<a}$ and $L_{>a}$, and the family consisting of $L$, the open rays and the open intervals $(a,b)$ is a basis for it ([[def-order-topology-on-a-linearly-ordered-set]], [[thm-basis-criterion]], [[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

[L3] A family of open sets is a basis for a topology exactly when every open $U$ and every $x \in U$ admit a member $B$ of the family with $x \in B \subseteq U$ ([[def-topology-basis-subbasis]]).

[L4] Arbitrary unions and finite intersections of open sets are open, and a set is closed exactly when its complement is open ([[def-topological-space]]).

[L5] $\beta^{+} = \beta \cup \{\beta\}$ is an ordinal, and for ordinals $\beta < \alpha$ holds exactly when $\beta^{+} \le \alpha$: from $\beta \in \alpha$ one gets $\beta \subseteq \alpha$ and $\{\beta\} \subseteq \alpha$, hence $\beta^{+} \subseteq \alpha$, and conversely $\beta \in \beta^{+} \subseteq \alpha$. Every ordinal is $0$, a successor or a limit ordinal ([[lem-ordinal-basics]], [[def-limit-ordinal]], [[def-ordinal]]).

[L6] A point $x$ of a space $X$ is isolated exactly when $\{x\}$ is open, since a neighbourhood $N$ of $x$ with $N \cap X = \{x\}$ contains an open $U$ with $x \in U \subseteq \{x\}$, and conversely ([[def-interior-closure-boundary-top]], [[def-neighbourhood-top]]).

[L7] A space is Hausdorff when distinct points lie in disjoint open sets ([[def-hausdorff-space]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $\beta \in \gamma$ the set $[0,\beta]$ is open: if $\beta^{+} \in \gamma$ then $\xi \le \beta$ is equivalent to $\xi < \beta^{+}$ by [L5], so $[0,\beta] = \gamma_{<\beta^{+}}$, an open ray; and if $\beta^{+} \notin \gamma$ then $\beta^{+} \subseteq \gamma$, since every ordinal below $\beta^{+}$ is $\le \beta$ and so lies in $\gamma$, whence $\beta^{+} = \gamma$ by [L1] and $[0,\beta] = \gamma$, which is open. [L1, L2, L4, L5]

1.2 For $\xi \ne \eta$ in $\gamma$ the trichotomy of [L1] gives $\xi < \eta$ after renaming. By [L5] the inequality $\xi < \eta$ gives $\xi^{+} \le \eta$, and since $\eta \in \gamma$, [L1] puts $\xi^{+}$ in $\gamma$ as well; so $\gamma_{<\xi^{+}}$ and $\gamma_{>\xi}$ are open rays, and they contain $\xi$ and $\eta$ respectively, since $\xi < \xi^{+}$ and $\xi < \eta$. They are disjoint: a common point $\zeta$ would satisfy $\zeta < \xi^{+}$, hence $\zeta \le \xi$ by [L5] and trichotomy, and $\xi < \zeta$ at once, which [L1] forbids. So claim 3 holds by [L7]. [L1, L2, L5, L7]

2.1 For $\alpha, \beta \in \gamma$ the set $(\alpha,\beta] = \gamma_{>\alpha} \cap [0,\beta]$ is open by [L4], being an intersection of two open sets. [L2, L4, step 1.1]

2.2 Every set $[0,\beta]$ is closed, its complement being the open ray $\gamma_{>\beta}$. [L2, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 Every set $(\alpha,\beta]$ is closed: its complement in $\gamma$ is $[0,\alpha] \cup \gamma_{>\beta}$, a union of an open set by step 1.1 and an open ray, hence open by [L4]. So every member of $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$ is clopen. [L2, L4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 Claim 2, the isolated points. The point $0$ is isolated when $0 \in \gamma$, since $\{0\} = [0,0]$ is open by step 1.1; and a successor $\xi = \eta^{+} \in \gamma$ is isolated, since $\eta < \xi$ puts $\eta$ in $\gamma$ and $\{\xi\} = (\eta,\xi]$ is open by step 2.1. [L5, L6, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$ is a basis. Let $U$ be open and $\xi \in U$; by [L2] and [L3] there is a set $B$ among $\gamma$, the open rays and the open intervals with $\xi \in B \subseteq U$. If $B = \gamma$ or $B = \gamma_{<b}$, then $[0,\xi]$ contains $\xi$ and lies inside $B$, since $\eta \le \xi$ gives $\eta < b$ in the second case. If $B = \gamma_{>a}$ or $B = (a,b)$, then $a < \xi$ and $(a,\xi]$ contains $\xi$ and lies inside $B$. In each case a member of $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$ sits between $\xi$ and $U$, and its members are open by step 1.1 and step 2.1, so [L3] applies and claim 1 is proved. [L2, L3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Conversely let $\xi \in \gamma$ be a limit ordinal. Were $\{\xi\}$ open, step 4.1 would supply $B \in \mathcal{B}_\gamma$ with $\xi \in B \subseteq \{\xi\}$, so $B = \{\xi\}$. If $B = [0,\beta]$ then $\beta = \xi$ and $0 \in B$, forcing $\xi = 0$, which no limit ordinal is. If $B = (\alpha,\xi]$ with $\alpha < \xi$ then $\alpha^{+} \le \xi$ by [L5], and $\alpha^{+} \ne \xi$ because $\xi$ is not a successor, so $\alpha < \alpha^{+} < \xi$ puts $\alpha^{+}$ in $B$ alongside $\xi$. Both cases are impossible, so $\{\xi\}$ is not open and $\xi$ is not isolated by [L6]; with step 3.2 this is claim 2. [L5, L6, step 3.2, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

**Why the half-open sets and not the open intervals.** In an ordinal every point other than a limit is isolated, and the sets $(\alpha,\beta]$ are exactly the basic sets that stay clopen: an open interval $(\alpha,\beta)$ need not be closed, while $(\alpha,\beta]$ always is, because its complement is again a union of sets of the two admissible forms. That every basic set is clopen is what makes an ordinal space totally disconnected in the naive sense and is used repeatedly in [[thm-ordinal-spaces-and-compactness]].

**The topology defined here is the general order topology and not a second notion.** It is [[def-order-topology-on-a-linearly-ordered-set]] applied to the linearly ordered set $\gamma$, and claim 1 says only that the general basis of rays and intervals may be replaced by the more convenient $\mathcal{B}_\gamma$. A published item elsewhere in the library states the same topology on an ordinal directly, as `def-order-topology-on-an-ordinal`; it is named here in plain text because its page comes later in the reading order, and the agreement between the two descriptions is exactly claim 1.

**The greatest-element case is not an edge case to be waved through.** When $\gamma$ is a successor $\delta^{+}$ its greatest element is $\delta$ and $[0,\delta] = \gamma$; step 1.1 treats that case explicitly, and it is the case that makes a successor ordinal compact ([[thm-ordinal-spaces-and-compactness]]).
