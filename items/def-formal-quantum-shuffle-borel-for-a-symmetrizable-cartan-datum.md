## Definition

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]), $n=|I|$, and $r=\operatorname{rank}A$. Choose $J\subseteq I$ with $|J|=r$ and $\det(A_J)\ne0$, as supplied by [[lem-nonsingular-principal-minor-of-the-symmetrized-cartan-matrix]]. Work over $R=\mathbb C\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ with $q=e^{\hbar}$ and $q_i=e^{d_i\hbar}$.

(a) **Word space and shuffle product.** Let $V=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb C v_i$ with $\deg v_i=\epsilon_i\in\mathbb N^I$. Set $\mathrm{Sh}(V)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V^{\otimes k}\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ and denote $z_1\otimes\cdots\otimes z_k$ by $[z_1|\cdots|z_k]$. For homogeneous letters, let $\Sigma_{k,l}$ consist of permutations $\sigma$ mapping original positions to output positions, with $\sigma(1)<\cdots<\sigma(k)$ and $\sigma(k+1)<\cdots<\sigma(k+l)$. Define

$$[z_1|\cdots|z_k]\,[z_{k+1}|\cdots|z_{k+l}]=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{k,l}}q^{-\sum_{i<j,\,\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)}\langle\deg z_i,\deg z_j\rangle}[z_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}|\cdots|z_{\sigma^{-1}(k+l)}],\qquad \langle\epsilon_i,\epsilon_j\rangle=d_i a_{ij}.$$

This product is associative and makes $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ an $\mathbb N^I$-graded algebra; every color-degree component is a finite free $R$-module. Write $\langle V\rangle$ for the subalgebra generated by the one-letter words $[v_i]$.

(b) **Cartan derivations and crossed products.** Define $R$-linear derivations on letters by $h_i^V(v_j)=a_{ij}v_j$ and $D_j^V(v_i)=\delta_{ij}v_i$ for $j\in I\setminus J$, and extend them to words by summing their action over positions. They extend as commuting derivations of $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ and preserve $\langle V\rangle$. Let $\mathcal C$ consist of $n$ commuting symbols $H_i$ corresponding to $h_i^V$ and $n-r$ commuting symbols $D_j$ corresponding to $D_j^V$. Put $A_C:=\varprojlim_N R[\mathcal C]/\hbar^N R[\mathcal C]\cong\mathbb C[\mathcal C]\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$, so each coefficient of $\hbar^m$ is a finite Cartan polynomial. The Cartan crossed-product modules are

$$\mathcal V:=\langle V\rangle\otimes_R A_C,\qquad \mathcal S:=\mathrm{Sh}(V)\otimes_R A_C.$$

The Cartan symbols are independent polynomial generators; the operators $h_i^V,D_j^V$ describe their commutators and are not their images under a Cartan inclusion. The Cartan coefficient algebra $A_C$ is $\hbar$-adically complete; no algebraic-freeness claim is made for the crossed products. Their products extend the products of the two factors and obey $X_s x-xX_s=\delta_s(x)$ for the corresponding derivation $\delta_s$. Equivalently, for multi-indices $\alpha,\beta$,

$$ (xX^\alpha)(yX^\beta)=\sum_{0\le t\le\alpha}\binom{\alpha}{t}x\,\delta^t(y)X^{\alpha+\beta-t},$$

where $\binom{\alpha}{t}=\prod_s\binom{\alpha_s}{t_s}$ and $\delta^t=\prod_s\delta_s^{t_s}$. The $r$ coroot directions indexed by $J$ are linearly independent because $A_J$ is nonsingular, and the remaining $n-r$ directions supplement the minimal realization of dimension $2n-r$ ([[def-realization-of-a-generalized-cartan-matrix]]); the number of Cartan symbols is $n+(n-r)=2n-r$.

(c) **$\hbar$-adic Hopf structure and conditional Serre map.** In the commutative formal Cartan factor, define $\exp(\hbar Z)=\sum_{m\ge0}\hbar^m Z^m/m!$ for each Cartan polynomial $Z$; every coefficient is a finite polynomial, so this is an element of $A_C$. Write $\widehat\otimes_R$ for the **color-degreewise $\hbar$-adically completed tensor product**: for each total color degree $\gamma$, complete $\bigoplus_{\alpha+\beta=\gamma}\mathcal S[\alpha]\otimes_R\mathcal S[\beta]$ in the $\hbar$-adic topology, then take the direct sum over $\gamma$; use the same convention for $\mathcal V$ and for iterated tensor products. There are finitely many splittings of each $\gamma\in\mathbb N^I$. The algebras are complete in each color degree, and multiplication extends on these degreewise completed tensors. Their direct sums are not asserted to be complete for limits with unbounded color support. These formulas make $\mathcal S$ a topological Hopf $R$-algebra: $\Delta:\mathcal S\to\mathcal S\widehat\otimes_R\mathcal S$ and $\varepsilon:\mathcal S\to R$ are continuous algebra maps, $\Delta$ is coassociative with both counit identities, and the continuous antipode satisfies both convolution-inverse equations.

$$\Delta_{\mathcal S}(X_s)=X_s\otimes1+1\otimes X_s,\qquad \Delta_{\mathcal S}([v_{i_1}|\cdots|v_{i_m}])=\sum_{k=0}^{m}[v_{i_1}|\cdots|v_{i_k}]\otimes\exp\!\Bigl(\hbar\sum_{t\le k}d_{i_t}H_{i_t}\Bigr)[v_{i_{k+1}}|\cdots|v_{i_m}].$$

The counit sends every nonempty word and every $X_s$ to $0$ and sends $1$ to $1$. The antipode is the common convolution inverse constructed recursively in word length and Cartan-polynomial degree. The same structure maps make $\mathcal V$ a topological Hopf subalgebra.

Let $E_R:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}Re_i$ and $T_R(E_R):=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}E_R^{\otimes_R k}$ be its tensor algebra with concatenation; every assignment of the $e_i$ to a unital $R$-algebra extends uniquely by wordwise multiplication. Let $U_{\hbar}\mathfrak n^+$ be the quotient of $T_R(E_R)$ by the two-sided ideal generated by

$$\sum_{s=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^s\binom{1-a_{ij}}{s}_{q_i}e_i^{1-a_{ij}-s}e_je_i^s=0\qquad(i\ne j),$$

where the symmetric Gaussian coefficients are evaluated at $q_i=e^{d_i\hbar}$. Let $U_{\hbar}\mathfrak b^+$ be the crossed product of this quotient with the commuting Cartan symbols $H_i,D_j$, using the multiplication rule of part (b) for the induced derivations and acting by $[H_i,e_j]=a_{ij}e_j$ and $[D_j,e_i]=\delta_{ij}e_i$. If the quantum Serre sums vanish in $\langle V\rangle$, then $H_i\mapsto H_i$, $D_j\mapsto D_j$, and $e_i\mapsto[v_i]$, with the first two images the independent target Cartan symbols in $A_C$ define an algebra homomorphism $p_{\hbar}:U_{\hbar}\mathfrak b^+\to\mathcal V$. This definition makes no assertion that the Serre-vanishing condition holds or that $p_{\hbar}$ is injective. All formal exponentials are interpreted $\hbar$-adically, and tensor completions are taken color-degreewise as specified above; no limit with unbounded color support is placed in either direct-sum algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite symmetrizable Cartan datum, its minimal realization, and the formal parameter $\hbar$.

[F1] $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$, so $\langle\epsilon_i,\epsilon_j\rangle=d_i a_{ij}$ is a symmetric bilinear form ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F2] There is a set $J$ with $|J|=r$ and $\det(A_J)\ne0$ ([[lem-nonsingular-principal-minor-of-the-symmetrized-cartan-matrix]]).

[F3] A minimal realization has dimension $2n-r$ and its simple coroots are linearly independent ([[def-realization-of-a-generalized-cartan-matrix]]).

[F4] Formal power series use coefficientwise addition and finite Cauchy products; $\mathbb C\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ is complete and separated in its $\hbar$-adic topology ([[def-formal-power-series-and-coefficient-extraction]]).

[F5] For a commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra and $u$ divisible by the formal variable, $\exp(u)=\sum_{m\ge0}u^m/m!$ ([[def-formal-exponential-logarithm-and-powers]]).

[F6] The symmetric Gaussian coefficients specialize to Laurent-polynomial coefficients in $q_i$, so their substitution $q_i=e^{d_i\hbar}$ lies in $R$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]], [[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

[F7] Tensor powers form the direct-sum word space and concatenation is associative ([[def-tensor-algebra-of-a-vector-space]]).

[F8] The bialgebra and Hopf axioms are algebra-map coproduct and counit, coassociativity, both counit identities, and both antipode convolution-inverse equations; here their tensor products are $\hbar$-adically completed as specified in (c) ([[def-bialgebra-counit-and-antipode]]).

[F9] A map killing a two-sided ideal factors through its quotient ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[def-quotient-ring]], [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]).

[F10] Tensor products of algebras have multiplication $(a\otimes b)(a'\otimes b')=aa'\otimes bb'$ ([[thm-tensor-product-of-algebras-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F11] No axiom of choice is used: $J$ is a single subset of the finite set $I$ supplied by [F2]; all sums are finite and the recursion terminates on finite word length and Cartan degree.

[F12] A polynomial ring on the finite family of Cartan symbols consists of finite polynomials in those commuting symbols ([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]]).

[F13] A formal series over $\mathbb C$ is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is nonzero; a submodule of a finite free module over a PID is finite free ([[thm-formal-power-series-unit-criterion]], [[def-principal-ideal-domain]], [[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Verify the shuffle and Ore products by finite combinatorics, check the Hopf maps on word cuts and crossed relations, then construct the antipode by a terminating recursion on word length and Cartan-polynomial degree.

1.1 For three word blocks, a shuffle of all letters is uniquely a shuffle of the first two blocks followed by a shuffle with the third, and also uniquely a shuffle of the last two followed by one with the first. In either description every inverted pair of letters occurs exactly once and contributes $q^{-\langle\deg z_i,\deg z_j\rangle}$; scalar weights therefore agree, proving associativity. The color degree is additive, and a fixed color multidegree has only finitely many words, each an $R$-basis vector in the tensor-word space. Thus every graded component of $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ is finite free. Also $R$ is a PID: a nonzero ideal has a least order $m$, an element of that order is $\hbar^m$ times a unit by [F13], and every other element is divisible by $\hbar^m$, so the ideal is $(\hbar^m)$. Each $\langle V\rangle[\alpha]$ is a submodule of the finite free word module and hence finite free by [F13]. Tensoring these finite bases with $A_C$ shows that each color-degree component of $\mathcal S$ and $\mathcal V$ is a finite direct sum of copies of the complete coefficient algebra $A_C$. Consequently each is $\hbar$-adically complete, and continuous multiplication on a fixed degree split extends to its completion; the finitely many splits of a fixed total degree give the stated completed multiplication. [F4, F7, F13, algebra]

1.2 On a word, $h_i^V$ acts by the sum of the $a_{ij}$ weights of its letters and $D_j^V$ by the number of color-$j$ letters. Every shuffle term preserves these color counts, so each operator satisfies Leibniz for the shuffle product; their diagonal actions commute. Since each sends $v_i$ to a scalar multiple of itself, it preserves the subalgebra $\langle V\rangle$. [algebra]

1.3 Repeatedly commute each $X_s$ past a coefficient using $X_sx=xX_s+\delta_s(x)$; the commuting derivations and Leibniz rule give the finite multi-binomial formula in (b). Associativity follows from the two possible reductions of $X_sX_t x$, which agree because $\delta_s\delta_t=\delta_t\delta_s$, and the two reductions of $X_sxy$, which agree because $\delta_s(xy)=\delta_s(x)y+x\delta_s(y)$. Products of formal $\hbar$-series have finite coefficient sums and each multi-index sum is finite, so multiplication is well-defined coefficientwise. [F4, F7, F12, algebra]

1.4 The principal minor gives $|I\setminus J|=n-r$; the minimal realization has $n$ independent simple coroots and dimension $2n-r$, so the $n$ coroot symbols and $n-r$ supplementary symbols give the required count. This uses no claim that $J$ alone spans the Cartan. [F2, F3, given]

1.5 The exponent $Z=\sum_{t\le k}d_{i_t}H_{i_t}$ is a polynomial in commuting Cartan variables. Apply [F5] in the commutative coefficient algebra with formal variable $\hbar$: at order $m$ the coefficient is the finite polynomial $Z^m/m!$. The coefficient of $\hbar^m$ in $\exp(\hbar Z)\exp(-\hbar Z)$ is $Z^m\sum_{j=0}^m(-1)^{m-j}\binom mj/m!$, equal to $0$ for $m>0$ and $1$ for $m=0$, so $G_k$ is invertible with inverse $\exp(-\hbar Z)$. Since each Cartan variable is primitive, coefficientwise binomial expansion gives $\Delta(G_k)=\exp(\hbar(Z\otimes1+1\otimes Z))=G_k\otimes G_k$. [F4, F5, F12, algebra]

2.1 Fix cuts $u=u_+u_-$ and $v=v_+v_-$, and write $G_\alpha=\exp(\hbar\sum_i\alpha_i d_iH_i)$ for a prefix of color degree $\alpha$. If $w_\beta$ has color degree $\beta$, then $H_iw_\beta=w_\beta(H_i+\sum_j\beta_j a_{ij})$, so $G_\alpha w_\beta=q^{\sum_{i,j}\alpha_i d_i\beta_j a_{ij}}w_\beta G_\alpha=q^{\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle}w_\beta G_\alpha$. A global shuffle with this cut consists of a shuffle of the prefixes and a shuffle of the suffixes; its cross-cut inversions contribute $q^{-\langle\deg u_-,\deg v_+\rangle}=q^{-\langle\deg v_+,\deg u_-\rangle}$ by [F1]. On the right, multiplying the second tensor factors gives $(G_{\deg u_+}u_-)(G_{\deg v_+}v_-)$; moving $u_-$ past $G_{\deg v_+}$ contributes the same cross-cut factor. Rewriting the resulting combined prefix exponential in word-times-Cartan normal form contributes $q^{\langle\deg u_++\deg v_+,\deg u_-+\deg v_-\rangle}$. Rewriting the corresponding Cartan-left factor on the left-hand coproduct gives this identical normal-form factor. The prefix and suffix shuffle weights also agree, so every cut and pair of shuffles has the same coefficient on both sides. Thus $\Delta$ preserves the shuffle product. [F1, F7, F10, step 1.5, algebra]

2.2 The operators $h_i^V,D_j^V$ are additive over a word cut and annihilate the Cartan symbols, so they fix each Cartan exponential. Hence $\Delta(\delta_s(w))=(\delta_s\otimes\mathrm{id}+\mathrm{id}\otimes\delta_s)\Delta(w)$, which is exactly the commutator of $\Delta(X_s)=X_s\otimes1+1\otimes X_s$ with $\Delta(w)$. Thus the coproduct preserves every crossed relation and extends as an algebra map to $\mathcal S$. [step 1.2, F10, algebra]

2.3 Both iterated coproducts of a word sum over two cuts; the factors agree because each prefix exponential is group-like, so $\Delta$ is coassociative. Define $\varepsilon$ as projection to the empty-word, constant-Cartan coefficient. It is an algebra map: positive color degree maps to zero, and every crossed commutator $\delta_s(x)$ with a positive-degree word also maps to zero. The counit identities hold because only the empty-prefix term survives after applying $\varepsilon$ to the first factor, only the all-letter prefix term survives after applying it to the second, and every Cartan exponential has counit $1$. [step 1.5, F4, F8, algebra]

3.1 Let $w$ be a word and $P$ a Cartan monomial, and induct lexicographically on (word length, Cartan degree). On Cartan polynomials set $S(P(X))=P(-X)$; these are the antipode equations for the primitive commuting Cartan generators. For positive word length, in $\Delta(wP)$ the unique term with the full word and full Cartan degree in the first tensor factor is $wP\otimes G_w$. Every other term has a shorter first word or smaller first Cartan degree. Since $G_w$ is invertible, the left convolution equation recursively determines $S(wP)$. The unique term with full word and full Cartan degree in the second tensor factor is $1\otimes wP$; all other terms have a shorter second word or smaller second Cartan degree, so the right convolution equation recursively determines a right inverse as well. The recursions terminate at every pair of finite word length and Cartan degree; each coefficient of every $G_w^{\pm1}$ is a finite Cartan polynomial, so they extend coefficientwise to the formal $\hbar$-series in the stated modules. The left and right convolution inverses coincide by associativity. For a letter $v_i$, $\Delta(v_i)=1\otimes v_i+v_i\otimes G_i$, so the equations give $S(v_i)=-v_iG_i^{-1}=-[v_i]\exp(-\hbar d_iH_i)$. [step 1.5, step 2.3, F4, F8, algebra]

4.1 In $\operatorname{Hom}_R(\mathcal S\widehat\otimes_R\mathcal S,\mathcal S)$ with convolution from the completed tensor coalgebra, $S\circ m$ is a two-sided convolution inverse of $m$: since $\Delta$ and $\varepsilon$ are algebra maps, applying the two antipode equations to $ab$ gives both inverse equations. The map $T=m(S\otimes S)\circ\mathrm{flip}$ is also a two-sided inverse, since $(T*m)(a\otimes b)=\sum S(b_{(1)})S(a_{(1)})a_{(2)}b_{(2)}=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b)1$ and $(m*T)(a\otimes b)=\sum a_{(1)}b_{(1)}S(b_{(2)})S(a_{(2)})=\varepsilon(a)\varepsilon(b)1$. These equalities follow by first applying the left and right antipode equations to $a$ and $b$, respectively. Uniqueness of convolution inverses gives $S\circ m=T$, so $S$ is anti-multiplicative. Hence $S(X_s)=-X_s$ and $S([v_i])=-[v_i]\exp(-\hbar d_iH_i)$ show $S(\mathcal V)\subseteq\mathcal V$. The coproduct and counit also restrict, so $\mathcal V$ is a topological Hopf subalgebra. [step 2.2, step 3.1, F8, algebra]

5.1 The Cartan derivations preserve the two-sided ideal generated by the quantum Serre sums because each sum is homogeneous in the color grading. Under the stated vanishing condition, the free-algebra assignment $e_i\mapsto[v_i]$ kills every defining Serre generator; its Cartan commutators match the crossed-product derivations. By [F9] it therefore factors uniquely through $U_{\hbar}\mathfrak b^+$ to the stated algebra map. [F6, F9, given, algebra] ∎
