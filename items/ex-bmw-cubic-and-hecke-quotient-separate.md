## Example

In $BMW_2$ take $q=2$, $\rho=3$, $z=q-q^{-1}=3/2$, and $\delta=25/9$, which satisfies $z(\delta-1)=\rho-\rho^{-1}$. There are three one-dimensional representations with $(g_1,e_1)=(2,0)$, $(-1/2,0)$, and $(1/3,25/9)$. Thus the generator takes all three distinct roots of its cubic across these representations. The third representation shows $e_1\ne0$ in the full algebra, so the quotient by $e_1$ is proper; the Hecke quotient imposes the quadratic that fails in the third representation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The BMW$_2$ presentation from [[def-bmw-algebra-with-fixed-kauffman-skein-convention]] and the cubic/quotient relation from [[lem-bmw-cubic-relation-and-hecke-quotient]].

[F1] [[def-bmw-algebra-with-fixed-kauffman-skein-convention]]: for $n=2$, the defining relations are the loop, skein, untwisting, and inverse relations for $g_1,e_1$.

[F2] [[lem-bmw-cubic-relation-and-hecke-quotient]]: the full generator satisfies the cubic, while setting $e_1=0$ gives the Hecke quadratic.

## Verification

1.1 For $(g,e)=(2,0)$ and $(-1/2,0)$, $g-g^{-1}=3/2=z(1-e)$ and the loop and untwisting relations hold because $e=0$. For $(g,e)=(1/3,25/9)$, $g-g^{-1}=-8/3=z(1-e)$, $ge=e g=25/27=\rho^{-1}e$, and $e^2=625/81=\delta e$. Every $g$ is invertible, and BMW$_2$ has no adjacent-index relations, so these are valid one-dimensional representations. [F1, algebra]

2.1 The three representations evaluate $g_1$ at the distinct numbers $2,-1/2,1/3$. Since the third sends $e_1$ to $25/9\ne0$, the ideal generated by $e_1$ is nonzero, so the canonical map to the quotient by that ideal is not injective. The quadratic $(g_1-2)(g_1+1/2)$ evaluates to $-25/18\ne0$ in the third representation, whereas setting $e_1=0$ forces that quadratic by [F2]. [F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
