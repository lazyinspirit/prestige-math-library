## Statement

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2$ be a full complex lattice with
oriented basis, let $\wp=\wp_\Lambda$ and $\wp'$ be its Weierstrass function and
its derivative ([[def-weierstrass-elliptic-p-function]]), and let $K(T_\Lambda)$
be the field of meromorphic functions on the torus $T_\Lambda$, identified with
the field of $\Lambda$-elliptic functions through pullback along
$\pi:\mathbb C\to T_\Lambda$ ([[def-elliptic-function-for-a-lattice]]). Then:

1. every even $\Lambda$-elliptic function $h$ is $h=R(\wp)$ for a rational
   function $R\in\mathbb C(x)$;
2. every odd $\Lambda$-elliptic function $k$ is $k=\wp'\cdot S(\wp)$ for a
   rational function $S\in\mathbb C(x)$;
3. consequently every $\Lambda$-elliptic function $f$ is
   $$f=R(\wp)+\wp'S(\wp)\qquad(R,S\in\mathbb C(x)),$$
   so that $K(T_\Lambda)=\mathbb C(\wp,\wp')$; the two generators satisfy the
   cubic relation
   $$(\wp')^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3 .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A full complex lattice $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2$ with oriented basis, the Weierstrass function $\wp=\wp_\Lambda$ and its derivative $\wp'$, and the torus $T_\Lambda=\mathbb C/\Lambda$ with class map $\pi$; the half-periods $h_1=\omega_1/2$, $h_2=\omega_2/2$, $h_3=(\omega_1+\omega_2)/2$, the values $e_j=\wp(h_j)$, and an arbitrary $\Lambda$-elliptic meromorphic function $f$.

[F1] A meromorphic $f:\mathbb C\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ is $\Lambda$-elliptic exactly when it is the pullback $f=g\circ\pi$ of a meromorphic function $g$ on $T_\Lambda$, uniquely determined by $f$; the periodicity is $f(z+\lambda)=f(z)$ with poles matched, and a meromorphic function is by convention never the constant map $\infty$ ([[def-elliptic-function-for-a-lattice]], [[def-holomorphic-and-meromorphic-map-of-riemann-surfaces]]). The meromorphic functions on a connected plane domain form a field, so sums, products and quotients with nonzero denominator of meromorphic functions on $\mathbb C$ are meromorphic again ([[cor-meromorphic-functions-on-a-domain-form-a-field]], [[def-meromorphic-function-complex-domain]]). Composites of holomorphic maps of Riemann surfaces are holomorphic, and a holomorphic map of Riemann surfaces is continuous.

[F2] $\wp$ is even and $\Lambda$-periodic, $\wp'$ is odd and $\Lambda$-periodic and $\wp'\not\equiv0$; at each lattice point $\wp$ has a double pole with principal part $(z-\lambda)^{-2}$, so $\wp(z)-z^{-2}$ extends holomorphically to $0$ and is even there; $\wp'(z)=-2\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda}(z-\omega)^{-3}$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ with that series normally convergent, so $\wp'$ has a pole of order $3$ at each lattice point and no other poles ([[thm-weierstrass-p-normal-convergence-and-periodicity]]).

[F3] The torus form $\bar\wp:T_\Lambda\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ of $\wp$ has degree $2$: $\sum_{x\in\bar\wp^{-1}(a)}e_x(\bar\wp)=2$ for every $a\in\widehat{\mathbb C}$, so every fibre is nonempty, and $\wp(z)=\wp(w)$ if and only if $w\equiv\pm z$ modulo $\Lambda$; its critical points are exactly the four classes $[0],[h_1],[h_2],[h_3]$, with $\bar\wp([0])=\infty$ and $\bar\wp([h_j])=e_j$ for the three distinct values $e_1,e_2,e_3\in\mathbb C$, and the fibre over each $e_j$ is the single class $[h_j]$ with $e_{[h_j]}(\bar\wp)=2$. Moreover the zeros of $\wp'$ are exactly the $\Lambda$-translates of $h_1,h_2,h_3$, each of order one ([[lem-weierstrass-p-degree-two-and-half-periods]]).

[F4] The Weierstrass functions satisfy $(\wp')^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, with $g_2=60G_4$ and $g_3=140G_6$ ([[thm-weierstrass-p-differential-equation]]).

[F5] A map $\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ is meromorphic on the Riemann sphere exactly when it is not identically $\infty$ and there are coprime polynomials $P,Q$, not both zero, with $f(z)=P(z)/Q(z)$ on the finite chart ([[thm-meromorphic-functions-riemann-sphere-are-rational]]).

[F6] For a nonconstant holomorphic map $f$ of Riemann surfaces and a point $x$ there are centred charts with chart expression $z\mapsto z^{e_x(f)}$, and $e_x(f)=\deg_x(f)=\operatorname{ord}_x(f-f(x))$; $e_x(f)=1$ exactly when $f$ is a local biholomorphism at $x$ ([[thm-local-normal-form-holomorphic-map-riemann-surfaces]], [[def-ramification-index-and-branch-value]], [[def-local-degree-holomorphic-map]]). For a holomorphic function on a plane domain, $f'(a)\ne0$ is equivalent to $f$ being biholomorphic between neighbourhoods of $a$ and $f(a)$, with a holomorphic local inverse ([[thm-holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem]]).

[F7] A holomorphic function has a zero of order $m$ at $a$ exactly when it equals $(z-a)^mg(z)$ near $a$ with $g$ holomorphic and $g(a)\ne0$ ([[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]]).

[F8] The pole set of a meromorphic function on a plane domain is discrete, and a pole has a finite order $m$ with $(z-a)^mf$ extending holomorphically and nonvanishingly at $a$ ([[thm-poles-meromorphic-function-are-discrete-and-countable]], [[def-isolated-singularity-types]]); a function holomorphic on a disc equals its Taylor series there ([[thm-taylor-expansion-holomorphic-function]]); a function holomorphic on an annulus $A(a;r,R)$, $0\le r<R\le\infty$, has a locally uniformly convergent Laurent expansion there, with uniquely determined coefficients ([[thm-laurent-expansion-annulus]], [[thm-laurent-coefficient-formula-and-uniqueness]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Even and odd parts.) Let $\sigma(z):=-z$. In the field of meromorphic functions on $\mathbb C$ put $h:=\tfrac12(f+f\circ\sigma)$ and $k:=\tfrac12(f-f\circ\sigma)$: since $\sigma$ is a biholomorphism of $\mathbb C$, the composite $f\circ\sigma$ is meromorphic by [F1], so $h$ and $k$ are meromorphic by the field property in [F1], and $f=h+k$. Both are $\Lambda$-periodic because $f$ is: for $\lambda\in\Lambda$ one has $h(z+\lambda)=\tfrac12(f(z+\lambda)+f(-z-\lambda))=\tfrac12(f(z)+f(-z))=h(z)$ and likewise for $k$. Finally $h\circ\sigma=h$ and $k\circ\sigma=-k$, that is, $h$ is even and $k$ is odd. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 (Even meromorphic functions near $0$ are functions of the square.) Let $G$ be meromorphic on a disc $|u|<\rho$ and even, $G(-u)=G(u)$ for all $u\ne0$ in that disc. Then there is a meromorphic $\Gamma$ near $0$ with $G(u)=\Gamma(u^2)$ for all small $u\ne0$. Indeed, by [F8] the pole set of $G$ is discrete and a pole at $0$ has a finite order, so there are $0<\rho'\le\rho$ and an integer $m\ge0$ such that $G$ has no poles in $0<|u|<\rho'$ and $u^mG(u)$ is holomorphic on $|u|<\rho'$; by the Taylor expansion in [F8] there are $b_n\in\mathbb C$ with $u^mG(u)=\sum_{n\ge0}b_nu^n$ for $|u|<\rho'$, and dividing by $u^m$ exhibits $G(u)=\sum_{n\ge-m}a_nu^n$ on the annulus $0<|u|<\rho'$, with $a_n:=b_{m+n}$ for $n\ge-m$ and $a_n:=0$ for $n<-m$; this is the locally uniformly convergent Laurent expansion of $G$ on that annulus by [F8]. Evenness says $\sum_na_n(-u)^n=\sum_na_nu^n$ for $0<|u|<\rho'$, so $a_n=(-1)^na_n$ for every $n$ by uniqueness of Laurent coefficients [F8], and $a_n=0$ for every odd $n$. Hence $G(u)=\sum_ia_{2i}u^{2i}=\Gamma(u^2)$ for $0<|u|<\rho'$, where $\Gamma(w):=\sum_{i\ge\lceil-m/2\rceil}a_{2i}w^i$ is a Laurent series with finitely many negative powers converging for $0<|w|<\rho'^2$, thus a meromorphic function of $w$ near $0$. [F8, given, algebra]

2.1 (Local structure of $\wp$ at $0$ and at the half-periods, and at regular points.) (a) By the principal-part clause of [F2], $\wp(z)=z^{-2}+E_0(z)$ with $E_0$ holomorphic near $0$; $E_0$ is even because $\wp$ is, so by step 1.2 there is a holomorphic $\widetilde E_0$ near $0$ with $E_0(z)=\widetilde E_0(z^2)$, and hence $\tfrac1{\wp(z)}=\tfrac{z^2}{1+z^2E_0(z)}=z^2\,r_0(z^2)$ with $r_0(w):=1/(1+w\widetilde E_0(w))$ holomorphic near $0$ and $r_0(0)=1\ne0$. (b) For each $j$ the function $u\mapsto\wp(h_j+u)$ is even in $u$, because $\wp$ is even and $2h_j\in\Lambda$: $\wp(h_j+u)=\wp(-h_j-u)=\wp(h_j-u)$; by [F6] and [F3] the order of the zero of $\wp(h_j+u)-e_j$ at $u=0$ equals $e_{[h_j]}(\bar\wp)=2$, so by [F7] $\wp(h_j+u)-e_j=u^2g(u)$ with $g$ holomorphic near $0$ and $g(0)\ne0$, and evenness in $u$ forces $g(u)=\widetilde g(u^2)$ with $\widetilde g(0)=g(0)\ne0$ by step 1.2; thus $\wp(h_j+u)-e_j=u^2r_j(u^2)$ with $r_j$ holomorphic near $0$ and $r_j(0)\ne0$. (c) If $z_0\notin\Lambda$ and $\wp'(z_0)\ne0$, then $\wp$ is holomorphic near $z_0$ with $\wp'(z_0)\ne0$, so by [F6] it is biholomorphic between a neighbourhood of $z_0$ and a neighbourhood of $\wp(z_0)$, with a holomorphic local inverse. [F2, F3, F6, F7, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 (An even elliptic function descends through $\wp$.) Let $h$ be even and $\Lambda$-elliptic. Define $F:\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ by $F(\wp(z)):=h(z)$: this is well defined because every $a\in\widehat{\mathbb C}$ is a value of $\wp$ by [F3], and if $\wp(z)=\wp(w)$ then $w\equiv\pm z$ modulo $\Lambda$ by [F3], so $h(w)=h(z)$ by evenness and periodicity. $F$ is not identically $\infty$, since $h$ is meromorphic and hence not the constant map $\infty$ by [F1]. $F$ is meromorphic: (a) if $a\notin\{\infty,e_1,e_2,e_3\}$, choose $z_0$ with $\wp(z_0)=a$; then $z_0\notin\Lambda$ and $\wp'(z_0)\ne0$, because otherwise the zero set description in [F3] gives $z_0\equiv h_j$ and $a=e_j$, contrary to the choice of $a$; by step 2.1(c) let $\varphi$ be a holomorphic local inverse of $\wp$ near $a$; then $F=h\circ\varphi$ near $a$, a composite of holomorphic maps to $\widehat{\mathbb C}$, which is meromorphic because $h$ is meromorphic and not constant $\infty$; (b) if $a=e_j$, put $u=z-h_j$; by step 2.1(b) $\wp(h_j+u)-e_j=\eta_j(u^2)$ for $\eta_j(w):=w\,r_j(w)$ holomorphic near $0$ with $\eta_j(0)=0$ and $\eta_j'(0)=r_j(0)\ne0$, hence biholomorphic near $0$ by [F6], and by step 1.2 applied to the even meromorphic function $H_j(u):=h(h_j+u)$ there is a meromorphic $G_j$ with $H_j(u)=G_j(u^2)$ near $0$; for small $s$ the equation $s=\wp(h_j+u)-e_j=\eta_j(u^2)$ is solved by $u^2=\eta_j^{-1}(s)$, so $F(e_j+s)=h(h_j+u)=G_j(u^2)=G_j(\eta_j^{-1}(s))$ is meromorphic in $s$; (c) if $a=\infty$, then $\wp(z)=\infty$ exactly for $z\in\Lambda$. By step 2.1(a), $w:=1/\wp(z)=\eta_0(z^2)$ with $\eta_0(t):=t\,r_0(t)$ holomorphic near $0$ and $\eta_0'(0)=1\ne0$, so $\eta_0$ has a holomorphic local inverse by [F6]. The even meromorphic function $h$ has, by step 1.2, a meromorphic germ $\Gamma$ with $h(z)=\Gamma(z^2)$ near $0$ (including the case $h\equiv0$). Hence in the source chart $w=1/a$ at $a=\infty$, the descended function is $F(1/w)=\Gamma(\eta_0^{-1}(w))$, meromorphic near $w=0$. Thus $F$ is meromorphic at $\infty$ too. [F1, F2, F3, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 (The even part is rational in $\wp$.) By step 3.1 the map $F$ is meromorphic on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$ and not identically $\infty$, so by [F5] there are coprime polynomials $P,Q$, not both zero, with $F=P/Q$ on the finite chart. Let $R:=P/Q\in\mathbb C(x)$, viewed as the meromorphic map $\widehat{\mathbb C}\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ it defines; $F$ and $R$ are continuous by [F1] and agree on the dense open set $\mathbb C\setminus\{s:Q(s)=0\}$, so $F=R$ on $\widehat{\mathbb C}$. Hence $h(z)=F(\wp(z))=R(\wp(z))$ for every $z\in\mathbb C$: every even $\Lambda$-elliptic function is rational in $\wp$. [F1, F5, given, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 (The odd part.) Let $k$ be odd and $\Lambda$-elliptic. The quotient $k/\wp'$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb C$ by [F1], because $k$ and $\wp'$ are meromorphic and $\wp'\not\equiv0$ by [F2]; it is even, $(k/\wp')(-z)=k(-z)/\wp'(-z)=(-k(z))/(-\wp'(z))=(k/\wp')(z)$, using that $\wp'$ is odd by [F2], and it is $\Lambda$-periodic because $k$ is $\Lambda$-periodic and $\wp'$ is by [F2]. Being even and $\Lambda$-elliptic, $k/\wp'=S(\wp)$ for some $S\in\mathbb C(x)$ by step 4.1, hence $k=\wp'\cdot S(\wp)$: every odd $\Lambda$-elliptic function is of this form. [F1, F2, given, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 (Assembly.) Let $f$ be any $\Lambda$-elliptic function and write $f=h+k$ as in step 1.1 with $h$ even and $k$ odd. By step 4.1 $h=R(\wp)$ for some $R\in\mathbb C(x)$ and by step 5.1 $k=\wp'S(\wp)$ for some $S\in\mathbb C(x)$, so $f=R(\wp)+\wp'S(\wp)$. Conversely, if $R,S\in\mathbb C(x)$, then $R\circ\wp$ and $S\circ\wp$ are meromorphic as composites of holomorphic maps $\mathbb C\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ read in the extended sense at the poles of $\wp$, $\wp'$ is meromorphic by [F2], and sums and products of meromorphic functions are meromorphic by [F1]; the sum is $\Lambda$-periodic because $\wp$ and $\wp'$ are, so it is $\Lambda$-elliptic. Therefore the field of $\Lambda$-elliptic functions is exactly $\mathbb C(\wp,\wp')$, which by [F1] is $K(T_\Lambda)$ under pullback, and [F4] gives the cubic relation $(\wp')^2=4\wp^3-g_2\wp-g_3$. ∎

## Remarks

The only geometric input beyond the differential equation is the fibre description of the degree-two map $\wp:T_\Lambda\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$: an even function is constant on the two-point fibres (and on the four ramified fibres), which is what lets it descend to a meromorphic function of $\wp$, and the descended map is meromorphic at the branch values because a meromorphic even function of a local coordinate is a meromorphic function of its square and $\wp-e_j$ (respectively $1/\wp$ at the pole) is a local coordinate vanishing to order two. The odd part needs no separate fibre analysis: $\wp'$ is odd and not identically zero, so the quotient of an odd elliptic function by $\wp'$ is an even elliptic function, to which the even case applies. No Riemann-Roch theorem and no group law are used; the cubic relation comes from [[thm-weierstrass-p-differential-equation]].
