## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and $n\ge2$. Every nonzero Cartesian second derivative $\partial_{ij}\Phi$ has $|\partial_{ij}\Phi|\notin L^1(B_\varepsilon(0))$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, although the signed kernel has annular cancellation. Thus the formal absolutely convergent integral $\int\partial_{ij}\Phi(x-y)f(y)dy$ can fail at a point with $f(x)\ne0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $n\ge2$, the normalized kernel $\Phi$, and indices $1\le i,j\le n$.

[A1] Countable Choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, is assumed ([[def-countable-choice]]). It is used only through the named sphere, polar-coordinate, ball-measure, interval-measure and null-set interfaces below; no full Axiom of Choice is used.

[F1] For $n\ge3$, $\Phi(x)=|x|^{2-n}/((n-2)\omega_{n-1})$; for $n=2$, $\Phi(x)=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|x|$, with $\omega_{n-1}>0$ ([[def-laplace-fundamental-solution-with-positive-minus-laplacian-sign]]).

[F2] For Borel $E\subseteq S^{n-1}$, the polar surface measure is $\sigma(E)=n\lambda_n(\{r\omega:\omega\in E,\ 0<r\le1\})$ ([[def-polar-surface-measure-on-the-unit-sphere]]).

[F3] Orthogonal transformations preserve $\sigma$ ([[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]]).

[F4] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, nonnegative Borel functions satisfy the polar integration formula ([[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]]).

[F25] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $\sigma$ is a finite Borel measure on $S^{n-1}$ ([[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]]).

[F5] Under Countable Choice, every Euclidean ball of positive radius has positive finite Lebesgue measure ([[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]]).

[F6] $B(x,r)=\{y:d(x,y)<r\}$ is the open metric ball for $r>0$ ([[def-metric-ball]]).

[F7] Borel sets form the sigma-algebra generated by open sets ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[F8] A scalar function is $C^k$ when its iterated coordinate derivatives through order $k$ exist and are continuous ([[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]]).

[F9] A Euclidean map is $C^k$ when each component is $C^k$ ([[def-ck-euclidean-maps-and-diffeomorphisms]]).

[F10] Finite sums and products and compositions of $C^k$ Euclidean maps are $C^k$ ([[thm-ck-euclidean-maps-closed-under-algebra-and-composition]]).

[F11] The total chain rule gives $D(g\circ f)(a)=Dg(f(a))\circ Df(a)$ ([[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]).

[F12] The matrix of a total derivative gives the coordinate partial derivatives ([[thm-total-derivative-computes-directional-and-partial-derivatives]]).

[F13] For $t>0$, $(t^\alpha)'=\alpha t^{\alpha-1}$ for every real $\alpha$ ([[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]]).

[F14] For $t>0$, $\log'(t)=1/t$ ([[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]]).

[F15] Products of differentiable real functions obey the product rule ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]).

[F16] A measurable real or complex function is integrable exactly when its absolute value has finite integral ([[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]).

[F26] For a real measurable function $u$, the integral is defined only when at most one of $\int u^+$ and $\int u^-$ is infinite, where $u^+=\max(u,0)$ and $u^-=\max(-u,0)$ ([[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]).

[F17] A measurable function is locally integrable on $\mathbb R^n$ when its absolute integral is finite on every positive-radius ball ([[def-locally-integrable-function-on-r-n]]).

[F18] The nonnegative integral is monotone: $0\le g\le h$ implies $\int g\le\int h$ ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]).

[F19] For nonnegative measurable $g$ and measurable $E$, $\int_Eg\,d\mu=\int g\mathbf1_E\,d\mu$ ([[def-integral-over-a-measurable-set]]).

[F20] If $g$ is nonnegative measurable, $E\mapsto\int_Eg\,d\mu$ is a measure ([[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]).

[F21] Every one-dimensional interval with finite endpoints is measurable and has measure equal to its length, for all endpoint conventions ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]).

[F22] The Lebesgue integral is linear on $L^1$ ([[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]).

[F23] Integrals are invariant under measure-preserving maps ([[thm-integrals-are-invariant-under-measure-preserving-maps]]).

[F24] Every countable subset of $\mathbb R^n$, in particular $\{0\}$, is Lebesgue null ([[prop-countable-subsets-of-rn-are-lebesgue-null]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $r=|x|$ and $\omega=x/r$ for $x\ne0$. On $(0,\infty)$ the power and logarithm profiles in [F1] are $C^2$ by [F13]–[F15]; $r=(\sum_kx_k^2)^{1/2}$ is $C^2$ on the punctured space by [F8]–[F10]. The chain and product rules [F11]–[F15] give $\partial_iq(r)=q'(r)x_i/r$ and $\partial_j\partial_iq(r)=q''(r)x_ix_j/r^2+q'(r)(\delta_{ij}/r-x_ix_j/r^3)$. For $n\ge3$ set $c_n=1/\omega_{n-1}$; for $n=2$ set $c_2=1/(2\pi)$. In both cases $q'=-c_nr^{1-n}$ and $q''=(n-1)c_nr^{-n}$, hence $\partial_{ij}\Phi(x)=c_n|x|^{-n}A_{ij}(\omega)$ where $A_{ij}(\omega)=n\omega_i\omega_j-\delta_{ij}$. In particular $c_n>0$ and this derivative is even under $x\mapsto-x$. [F1, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, F13, F14, F15, cases, algebra]

1.2 Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and let $h(r)=1/r$ on $(0,\varepsilon)$. For $k\ge1$, put $I_k=(\varepsilon2^{-k},\varepsilon2^{1-k})$. Its length is $\varepsilon2^{-k}$ by [F21], and $h(r)\ge2^{k-1}/\varepsilon$ on $I_k$, so [F18] gives $\int_{I_k}h(r)\,dr\ge1/2$. The measure $E\mapsto\int_Eh(r)\,dr$ is countably additive by [F19]–[F20]; the intervals are disjoint, so the integral over $(0,\varepsilon)$ is at least $m/2$ for every finite union of the first $m$ intervals. Letting $m$ increase proves $\int_0^\varepsilon dr/r=+\infty$. The interval-measure supplier uses the stated $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ assumption [A1]. [A1, F18, F19, F20, F21, algebra]

2.1 On $S^{n-1}$, $A_{ij}$ is bounded and continuous. If $i\ne j$, reflection of coordinate $i$ preserves $\sigma$ and sends $A_{ij}$ to $-A_{ij}$, so [F23] gives $\int A_{ij}\,d\sigma=0$. If $i=j$, coordinate permutations make all $\int\omega_i^2d\sigma$ equal; since $\sum_i\omega_i^2=1$, linearity [F22] gives each value $\sigma(S^{n-1})/n$ and again $\int A_{ii}\,d\sigma=0$. For $i=j$, $A_{ii}(e_i)=n-1>0$ and $A_{ii}(e_k)=-1<0$ for $k\ne i$; for $i\ne j$, $A_{ij}((e_i\pm e_j)/\sqrt2)=\pm n/2$. Thus each sign occurs on a nonempty relative-open cap. Choose such a cap $E=\{\omega\in S^{n-1}:|\omega-\omega_0|<\eta\}$ small enough that one sign of $A_{ij}$ has magnitude greater than some $c>0$ throughout $E$. For $0<\rho<\min(1/8,\eta/8)$ and $z\in B(\omega_0/2,\rho)$, one has $0<|z|<1$ and $|z/|z|-\omega_0|\le |1-2|z||+2|z-\omega_0/2|\le4\rho<\eta$. Hence this Euclidean ball is contained in the cone $\{r\omega:\omega\in E,\ 0<r\le1\}$. The positive ball measure [F5] and monotonicity applied to indicators [F18] give $\sigma(E)>0$ by [F2]. Since $\sigma$ is finite by [F25], $a_{ij}:=\int_{S^{n-1}}|A_{ij}|\,d\sigma$ is finite and strictly positive, and the positive and negative angular parts each have positive integral. [A1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F18, F22, F23, F25, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Extend the formula in step 1.1 by setting its value at $0$ to $0$; [F24] makes this choice immaterial to its integral. The punctured space is open because it is the union of the open balls $B(x,|x|/2)$ over $x\ne0$ [F6], so $\{0\}$ is closed. The extension is continuous on that open set. For every open $V\subseteq\mathbb R$, its preimage is the open preimage under the restriction to $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$, together with $\{0\}$ exactly when $0\in V$; thus it is Borel. Since open sets generate the Borel sigma-algebra [F7] and inverse images preserve sigma-algebra operations, the extension is Borel measurable. For $B_\varepsilon(0)$, [F4] and step 2.1 give $$\int_{B_\varepsilon(0)}|\partial_{ij}\Phi(x)|\,dx=c_na_{ij}\int_0^\varepsilon\frac{dr}{r}=+\infty$$ by step 1.2. Since $c_n>0$ and $a_{ij}>0$, [F16] proves $\partial_{ij}\Phi\notin L^1(B_\varepsilon(0))$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, and [F17] says it is not locally integrable at the pole. This uses $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ [A1] through the polar formula [F4]. [A1, F4, F6, F7, F16, F17, F24, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 For $0<a<b<\infty$, the derivative in step 1.1 is integrable on the annulus $a<|x|<b$, since it is bounded there and the annulus has finite measure. Applying [F4] separately to its positive and negative parts and using [F19] and [F22], the signed integral equals $c_n\int_a^b r^{-1}dr\int_{S^{n-1}}A_{ij}(\omega)d\sigma(\omega)=0$ by step 2.1. The radial integral is finite because $1/r$ is bounded on $[a,b]$ and that interval has finite measure [F21]. Thus every concentric annular truncation cancels, although absolute integrability fails at the removed pole. [A1, F4, F5, F16, F19, F21, F22, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $f(y)=\mathbf1_{B_1(0)}(y)$, a Borel bounded function with $f(0)=1$ by [F6]–[F7]. At $x=0$, evenness from step 1.1 and step 3.1 give $\int|\partial_{ij}\Phi(-y)f(y)|dy=+\infty$. Write $A_{ij}^+=\max(A_{ij},0)$ and $A_{ij}^- =\max(-A_{ij},0)$. More specifically, [F4] on the positive and negative parts gives respectively $$c_n\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}A_{ij}^+\,d\sigma\right)\int_0^1\frac{dr}{r}=+\infty,\qquad c_n\left(\int_{S^{n-1}}A_{ij}^-\,d\sigma\right)\int_0^1\frac{dr}{r}=+\infty,$$ since both angular factors are positive by the sign caps in step 2.1 and the radial factor diverges by step 1.2. Each concentric annular truncation inside $B_1$ has signed integral zero by step 3.2. Thus the ordinary Lebesgue integral is undefined there by [F26], and the formal absolute-convergence claim fails at a point where $f$ is nonzero. Countable Choice [A1] is used only through the named measure suppliers [F2]–[F5], [F21], and [F24]; no full AC is invoked. [A1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F26, F21, F24, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct] ∎

## Source notes

Hunter §2.6.1, equation (2.14), prints the diagonal second-derivative formula; §2.7.1, Theorem 2.26, equations (2.25)–(2.28), subtracts $f(x)$ and includes a boundary correction in the classical second-derivative integral formula. The present item derives a positive angular lower bound and a concrete bounded-data witness; the source's order estimate alone is not used as the lower-bound proof.
