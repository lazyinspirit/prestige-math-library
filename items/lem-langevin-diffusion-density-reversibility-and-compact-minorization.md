## Statement

Assume AC. Let $d\ge1$, $U\in C^2(\mathbb R^d)$, $\nabla U$ be $L$-Lipschitz,
and let $x_\star$ be a global minimizer with $U(x_\star)=0$.
The solutions of
$$dX_t=-\nabla U(X_t)\,dt+\sqrt2\,dB_t,\qquad X_0=x,$$
define a Borel Markov semigroup $P_t$. For every $t>0$ it has a positive
jointly Borel density $p_t(x,y)$ relative to Lebesgue measure, and
$$e^{-U(x)}p_t(x,y)=e^{-U(y)}p_t(y,x).$$
If $Z=\int e^{-U}\,dx\in(0,\infty)$, then $Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}dx$ is invariant.
For every $R>0$ and $t>0$, there are $\epsilon>0$ and a probability $\nu$ such
that $P_t(x,A)\ge\epsilon\nu(A)$ for every Borel $A$ and
$\|x-x_\star\|\le R$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the potential, minimizer, and Brownian equation in the Statement.

[A1] AC is inherited through Brownian, Gaussian, Ito and change-of-measure constructions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Globally Lipschitz coefficients with linear growth have a unique strong solution from each square-integrable initial state on every finite horizon. [[thm-global-strong-well-posedness-for-lipschitz-ito-sdes]]

[F2] A true stochastic exponential changes a Brownian drift by its integrand. [[lem-finite-horizon-girsanov-and-relative-entropy-for-lipschitz-drifts]]

[F3] Multidimensional Ito's formula applies to $U(x+\sqrt2 B_t)$ and to its exponential compositions. [[thm-multidimensional-ito-formula-for-brownian-driven-processes]]

[F4] Brownian finite-dimensional laws are Gaussian with covariance $\min(s,u)I_d$, independent future increments, and continuous paths; uncorrelated jointly Gaussian finite blocks are independent. [[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]] [[lem-uncorrelated-jointly-gaussian-blocks-are-independent]]

[F5] Continuous-path Borel sets are generated by rational evaluations; equality on a generating pi-system extends by the pi-lambda theorem. The same assertion on a finite interval and in finitely many coordinates follows by restriction, constant extension, and finite products. [[lem-borel-sigma-algebra-of-continuous-path-space-is-generated-by-coordinates]] [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F6] The Gaussian increment $\sqrt2 B_t$ has density $h_t(z)=(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-\|z\|^2/(4t)}$. Each coordinate Brownian path obeys $\mathbb E\sup_{s\le t}|B_s^i|^2\le4t$. [[thm-nondegenerate-multivariate-normal-law-has-density]] [[thm-doob-maximal-bound-for-the-ito-integral]]

[F7] Tonelli interchanges nonnegative integrals, and dominated convergence applies under a fixed integrable majorant. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]] [[thm-dominated-convergence]]

[F8] A differentiable global minimizer has zero gradient. [[thm-fermat-for-euclidean-local-extrema]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Put $g=\nabla U$. By [F8], $g(x_\star)=0$, so $\|g(x)\|\le L\|x-x_\star\|$; the equation meets [F1]. Its additive-noise integral equation is $X_s=x-\int_0^s g(X_u)du+\sqrt2 B_s$. For each continuous input path the deterministic Picard iteration converges uniformly on finite intervals: successive differences are bounded by a fixed finite constant times $L^ns^n/n!$, by repeated integration. The limit is the unique solution; subtraction and the same iterative integral bound give $\sup_{s\le t}\|X_s^{x,w}-X_s^{z,v}\|\le e^{Lt}(\|x-z\|+\sqrt2\sup_{s\le t}\|w_s-v_s\|)$. Thus the solution is a continuous function of its initial point and driving path. Its flow composition identity follows from uniqueness of the integral equation. Independent Brownian future increments [F4] now give the Markov and semigroup identities; integration of its Borel solution map against the Brownian law gives a Borel transition kernel. Solutions on integer horizons agree by uniqueness and hence define all $t\ge0$. [A1, F1, F4, F8, construct]

1.2 Define $\beta_s=\sqrt2(B_s-sB_t/t)$ for $0\le s\le t$. For any finite list of times its coordinates together with $B_t$ are jointly Gaussian and $\operatorname{Cov}(B_s-sB_t/t,B_t)=0$. By [F4] that finite bridge block is independent of $B_t$. Apply [F5] to rational-time cylinders to obtain independence of the entire bridge path and $B_t$. Thus Brownian paths disintegrate explicitly as $$Y_s=x+(s/t)(y-x)+\beta_s$$ when their endpoint has value $y$, with endpoint density $h_t(y-x)$ and bridge law independent of $y$. This identity holds first for cylinder functions by independence and the Gaussian endpoint density, then for all bounded Borel path functions by [F5], and for nonnegative ones by monotone approximation. The bridge has covariance $2(\min(s,u)-su/t)I_d$, which is unchanged when $(s,u)$ is replaced by $(t-s,t-u)$; its mean is zero. Gaussian finite-dimensional laws and [F5] therefore also give equality in law of $\beta_s$ and $\beta_{t-s}$. [F4, F5, F6, construct]

2.1 Under Brownian law put $Y_s=x+\sqrt2 B_s$. The stochastic exponential with integrand $-g(Y_s)/\sqrt2$ is, by [F3], $$Z_s=\exp\!\left(\frac{U(x)-U(Y_s)}2+\int_0^s\left\{\frac{\Delta U(Y_u)}2-\frac{\|g(Y_u)\|^2}4\right\}du\right).$$ Lipschitz continuity gives $|\partial_{ii}U|\le L$ by taking coordinate difference quotients. Since $U\ge0$, for $s\le t$ this positive local martingale is bounded above by $e^{U(x)/2+tdL/2}$. Stop at increasing energy/local-martingale levels. Dominated convergence [F7] in the stopped martingale identities makes $Z$ a true martingale of mean one. By [F2], under $Z_t$ times Brownian law the process $B_s+\int_0^s g(Y_u)/\sqrt2\,du$ is Brownian, so $Y$ solves the Langevin equation. The explicit unique solution map of step 1.1 identifies its law with $P_t(x,\cdot)$. [F2, F3, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $H(z)=\Delta U(z)/2-\|g(z)\|^2/4$ and $b_s^{x,y}=x+(s/t)(y-x)+\beta_s$. Combining steps 2.1 and 1.2 gives the density $$p_t(x,y)=h_t(y-x)e^{(U(x)-U(y))/2}\,\mathbb E\exp\!\left(\int_0^t H(b_s^{x,y})ds\right).$$ The integral is a continuous function of $(x,y,\beta)$ in the uniform path topology, since $H$ is continuous and uniformly continuous on each compact set containing the convergent paths. Its exponential is strictly positive and bounded above by $e^{tdL/2}$. Consequently its expectation is positive, finite, and continuous in $(x,y)$ by dominated convergence; the density is jointly Borel. Its integral in $y$ is one by step 2.1. Bridge reversal from step 1.2 interchanges $x,y$ without changing the integral of $H$, and $h_t(y-x)=h_t(x-y)$. Multiplication by $e^{-U(x)}$ proves the asserted symmetry. If $Z<\infty$, Tonelli and the mass-one identity give $$\int Z^{-1}e^{-U(x)}p_t(x,y)dx=Z^{-1}e^{-U(y)}\int p_t(y,x)dx=Z^{-1}e^{-U(y)},$$ proving invariance. [F5, F7, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 By [F6], $\mathbb E\sup_{s\le t}\|B_s\|^2\le4dt$, and $\sup\|\beta_s\|^2\le8\sup\|B_s\|^2$. With $a_0=\sqrt{64dt}$, Markov's inequality (integrate $1_{\{Y>a_0^2\}}\le Y/a_0^2$ for $Y=\sup\|\beta\|^2$) gives $\Pr(\sup\|\beta\|\le a_0)\ge1/2$. If both endpoints lie in the ball $D=\{\|z-x_\star\|\le R\}$, on this event the bridge lies in the radius $R+a_0$ ball. Therefore $H(b_s^{x,y})\ge-dL/2-L^2(R+a_0)^2/4$ and the bridge expectation in step 3.1 is at least one half times the exponential of $-t[dL/2+L^2(R+a_0)^2/4]$. The heat factor is at least $(4\pi t)^{-d/2}e^{-R^2/t}$ on $D^2$, and $e^{(U(x)-U(y))/2}\ge e^{-\sup_D U/2}>0$. Thus a constant $c>0$ bounds $p_t$ below on $D^2$. Let $\nu$ be normalized Lebesgue measure on $D$ and $\epsilon=\min\{c\operatorname{Leb}(D),1/2\}>0$. Integrating the density over $A\cap D$ proves the stated minorization. The radius is positive, so the normalizing volume is finite and nonzero. [F6, F7, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
