## Example

Let $S=\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$ with $m(s_1,s_2)=m(s_2,s_3)=3$ and $m(s_1,s_3)=2$, the Coxeter matrix of type $A_3$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), let $W$ be the presented group and let $w:=s_1s_3s_2\in W$. Then:

**(1)** The heap $P$ of the word $(s_1,s_3,s_2)$ has elements $1,2,3$ with $1\prec3$, $2\prec3$ and no relation between $1$ and $2$; its covering pairs are $1\lessdot3$ and $2\lessdot3$, with labels $s_1,s_2$ and $s_3,s_2$.

**(2)** $P$ satisfies both conditions of [[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]] (2): its chains have at most two elements, so it has no convex alternating chain of length $m(s_1,s_2)=m(s_2,s_3)=3$, and its covering pairs have distinct labels. Hence the word is reduced, $w$ is fully commutative and $P$ is the heap $P_w$ of $w$; in particular $\ell(w)=3$.

**(3)** The linear extensions of $P$ are exactly $(1,2,3)$ and $(2,1,3)$, and the corresponding labeled linear extensions are the words $s_1s_3s_2$ and $s_3s_1s_2$. By [[thm-cg-heaps-classify-commutation-classes]] (1) these are exactly the members of the commutativity class $C((s_1,s_3,s_2))=\mathcal R(w)$: the two reduced words of $w$ differ by the commute $s_1s_3=s_3s_1$.

**(4)** The order ideals of $P$ are $\varnothing$, $\{1\}$, $\{2\}$, $\{1,2\}$ and $\{1,2,3\}$; they form a five-element distributive lattice under inclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Coxeter matrix of type $A_3$ on $S=\{s_1,s_2,s_3\}$, the presented group $W$, and the word $s=(s_1,s_3,s_2)$ with heap $P=P_s$ and product $w=s_1s_3s_2$.

[F1] The heap of a word is the labeled poset whose relations are generated by $i\prec_s j$ for $i<j$ with $s_i=s_j$ or $m(s_i,s_j)\ge3$; labeled linear extensions $L(P_s,s)$ are read from linear extensions; $C(s)$ is the commutativity class of $s$; and $w$ is fully commutative when $\mathcal R(w)=C(s)$ ([[def-cg-labeled-word-heap-and-fully-commutative-element]], clauses (2), (4), (5), (6)).

[F2] For the Coxeter matrix, $m(s,t)$ is the order of $st$ in $W$; in particular $m(s_1,s_3)=2$ means that $s_1$ and $s_3$ commute in $W$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F3] For a word $s$ with heap $P_s$ and product $w$, conditions (a) and (b) of [[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]] (2) together are equivalent to: $s$ is reduced and $w$ is fully commutative; when they hold, $P_s$ is the heap of $w$ ([[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]], clause (2)).

[F4] For every word $q$ one has $L(P_q,q)=C(q)$ ([[thm-cg-heaps-classify-commutation-classes]], clause (1)).

[F5] An element $y$ covers $x$ when $x\prec y$ and no $z$ satisfies $x\prec z\prec y$ ([[def-graded-poset-and-rank]]).

[F6] For a finite poset $P$, the order ideals $J(P)$ form a finite distributive lattice under inclusion, with meet intersection and join union ([[lem-order-ideals-form-a-distributive-lattice]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The type $A_3$ Coxeter matrix and the word $s=(s_1,s_3,s_2)$ with product $w$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The heap relations are computed pair by pair from [F1]: positions $1,2$ carry the distinct commuting letters $s_1,s_3$ with $m(s_1,s_3)=2$, so there is no relation between them; position $3$ is above position $1$ because $1<3$ and $m(s_1,s_2)=3\ge3$; and position $3$ is above position $2$ because $2<3$ and $m(s_3,s_2)=m(s_2,s_3)=3$. These are therefore all generating relations, and the transitive closure adds nothing, so $P$ is the three-element poset with exactly $1\prec3$ and $2\prec3$. By [F5] its covering pairs are $1\lessdot3$ and $2\lessdot3$: the only elements are $1,2,3$, and no $z$ satisfies $1\prec z\prec3$ or $2\prec z\prec3$, since $1$ and $2$ are incomparable. The covering labels are $(s_1,s_2)$ and $(s_3,s_2)$. [given, F1, F5]

1.2 The order ideals are the subsets $I\subseteq\{1,2,3\}$ that contain $1$ and $2$ whenever they contain $3$, that is, $\varnothing,\{1\},\{2\},\{1,2\},\{1,2,3\}$: downward closure of a subset of the three-element poset is a condition only on the predecessors of $3$. These five sets are exactly $J(P)$, and by [F6] they form a finite distributive lattice under inclusion, with meet intersection and join union. [given, F1, F6]

2.1 The criterion of [F3] applies: every chain of $P$ has at most two elements, since the only relations are $1\prec3$ and $2\prec3$, so there is no convex chain of any length $m\ge3$, and in particular none of length $m(s_1,s_2)=m(s_2,s_3)=3$; hence condition (a) of [[thm-cg-fully-commutative-forbidden-chain-criterion]] (2) holds. The covering pairs $1\lessdot3$ and $2\lessdot3$ have labels $(s_1,s_2)$ and $(s_3,s_2)$, and $s_1,s_2,s_3$ are pairwise distinct, so condition (b) holds. By [F3] the word $(s_1,s_3,s_2)$ is reduced, $w$ is fully commutative and $P=P_w$; since a reduced word of $w$ has length $\ell(w)$, this gives $\ell(w)=3$. [given, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 A listing of $\{1,2,3\}$ is a linear extension of $P$ exactly when $3$ is last, because both relations point to $3$ and $1,2$ are incomparable; the two linear extensions are therefore $(1,2,3)$ and $(2,1,3)$, with labeled words $s_1s_3s_2$ and $s_3s_1s_2$. By [F4], $L(P,s)=C(s)$, and by 2.1 the word is reduced with $w$ fully commutative, so $C(s)=\mathcal R(w)$; hence the reduced words of $w$ are exactly these two words, which differ by interchanging the adjacent commuting letters $s_1,s_3$. [given, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
