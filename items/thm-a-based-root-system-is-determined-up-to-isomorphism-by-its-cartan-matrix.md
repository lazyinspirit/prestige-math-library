## Statement

Let $\Phi\subseteq E$ and $\Phi'\subseteq E'$ be reduced crystallographic root
systems with bases $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ and
$\Delta'=\{\alpha_1',\dots,\alpha_r'\}$ and Cartan matrices $A=(a_{ij})$ and
$A'=(a'_{ij})$ ([[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]]). If $A=A'$ then
the linear map $\varphi:E\to E'$ with $\varphi(\alpha_i)=\alpha_i'$ for all $i$
is an isomorphism of root systems; in particular $\varphi(\Phi)=\Phi'$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Based reduced crystallographic root systems $(\Phi,\Delta)$ and $(\Phi',\Delta')$ with the same Cartan matrix $A=A'$, and the linear map $\varphi$ sending $\alpha_i$ to $\alpha_i'$.

[L1] $\Delta$ and $\Delta'$ are bases of $E$ and $E'$, and every root is a unique integral combination of its base with coefficients of one sign ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]]).

[L2] $s_{\alpha_i}(\alpha_j)=\alpha_j-a_{ij}\alpha_i$, and the same formula with primes holds in $\Phi'$ because $A=A'$ ([[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]]).

[L3] If $\gamma,\delta\in\Phi$ are nonproportional with $(\gamma,\delta)>0$ then $\gamma-\delta\in\Phi$ ([[thm-rank-two-root-system-classification]]).

[L4] The Gram matrix $G=((\alpha_i,\alpha_j))$ of the simple roots satisfies $a_{ij}=2G_{ij}/G_{ii}$, and $G$ is positive definite; the numbers $a_{ij}$ determine $G$ up to one positive scalar on each connected component of the graph with edges $a_{ij}\ne0$ ([[prop-finite-type-cartan-matrix-properties]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every root of $\Phi$ is a Weyl conjugate of a simple root. Indeed, if a positive root $\gamma=\sum_in_i\alpha_i$ is not simple, then $0<(\gamma,\gamma)=\sum_in_i(\gamma,\alpha_i)$ gives an index $i$ with $(\gamma,\alpha_i)>0$, so $\gamma-\alpha_i\in\Phi$ by [L3]; its coordinates are those of $\gamma$ except in position $i$, and since its nonzero coordinates have one sign, its $i$-th coordinate is at least $1$ and $\gamma-\alpha_i$ is a positive root of smaller height. Iterating reaches a simple root, and inverting the reflections exhibits $\gamma$ as $w(\delta)$ for some $w$ in the group generated by the simple reflections and some simple root $\delta$. [L1, L3, algebra]

2.1 The matrix of $s_{\alpha_i}$ in the basis $\Delta$ has entries $(s_{\alpha_i})_{jk}=\delta_{jk}-a_{ij}\delta_{ik}$ by [L2]; it is therefore determined by $A$, and the corresponding matrix for $s_{\alpha_i'}$ in the basis $\Delta'$ is the same. Hence $\varphi\,s_{\alpha_i}\,\varphi^{-1}=s_{\alpha_i'}$ for all $i$, because both sides are linear and agree on the basis $\Delta'$: $\varphi s_{\alpha_i}\varphi^{-1}(\alpha_j')=\varphi s_{\alpha_i}(\alpha_j)=\varphi(\alpha_j-a_{ij}\alpha_i)=\alpha_j'-a_{ij}\alpha_i'=s_{\alpha_i'}(\alpha_j')$. [L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Consequently $\varphi$ carries the Weyl orbit of $\Delta$ onto the Weyl orbit of $\Delta'$, and by step 1.1 (applied to $\Phi$ and to $\Phi'$) it carries $\Phi$ onto $\Phi'$; in particular $\varphi$ is a linear isomorphism, since it maps the basis $\Delta$ onto the basis $\Delta'$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 It remains to check that $\varphi$ preserves Cartan integers. By [L4] the Gram matrices $G$ and $G'$ satisfy $a_{ij}=2G_{ij}/G_{ii}=2G'_{ij}/G'_{ii}$ and are positive definite; for indices $i,j$ joined by an edge one has $a_{ij},a_{ji}<0$ and $a_{ij}G_{ii}=2G_{ij}=2G_{ji}=a_{ji}G_{jj}$, so $G'_{ii}/G_{ii}=G'_{jj}/G_{jj}$; by connectivity along edges the ratios $G'_{ii}/G_{ii}$ are constant on each connected component of the graph on $\Delta$ with edges $a_{ij}\ne0$. Define a second inner product on $E$ by $(x,y)_1=(\varphi x,\varphi y)_{E'}$. Its Gram matrix in the basis $\Delta$ is $G'$, which differs from $G$ by a positive scalar on each connected component; therefore for roots $\gamma,\delta$ of $\Phi$ the Cartan integers computed with $(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)$ and with $(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)_1$ agree, because scaling an inner product on a component by $\lambda>0$ multiplies both $(\gamma,\delta)$ and $(\delta,\delta)$ by $\lambda^{2}$ when $\gamma,\delta$ lie in that component and gives $0$ otherwise. Since $\varphi(\Phi)=\Phi'$ by step 3.1, $\varphi$ is an isomorphism of root systems. [step 3.1, L4, algebra] ∎
