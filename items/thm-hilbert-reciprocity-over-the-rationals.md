## Statement

For all $a,b\in\mathbb Q^\times$,

$$
\prod_{v\le\infty}(a,b)_v=1,
$$

and all but finitely many factors are $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Two nonzero rational numbers $a$ and $b$.

[L1] The Hilbert symbol is bilinear on the square-class group ([[thm-hilbert-symbol-is-symmetric-bilinear-and-nondegenerate]]).

[L2] The explicit local formulas are known at $\infty$, odd $p$, and $2$ ([[thm-real-hilbert-symbol-formula]], [[thm-odd-p-hilbert-symbol-formula]], [[thm-two-adic-hilbert-symbol-formula]]).

[L3] Quadratic reciprocity and its two supplements are already proved ([[thm-quadratic-reciprocity]], [[thm-first-supplement-to-quadratic-reciprocity]], [[thm-second-supplement-to-quadratic-reciprocity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], the map $(a,b)\mapsto\prod_v(a,b)_v$ is bilinear on $\mathbb Q^\times/(\mathbb Q^\times)^2$. This square-class group is generated by the classes of $-1$, $2$, and the odd primes. Hence it is enough to check the product formula on pairs of generators. [L1, given, algebra]

1.2 For the pair $(-1,-1)$, [L2] gives $(-1,-1)_\infty=-1$ and $(-1,-1)_2=-1$, while $(-1,-1)_p=1$ for every odd prime $p$, so the global product is $1$. For the pair $(-1,2)$, the identity $-1\cdot1^2+2\cdot1^2=1$ shows $(-1,2)_v=1$ at every place $v$, so the global product is again $1$. [L2, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $p$ be an odd prime. Then [L2] gives $(-1,p)_v=1$ for $v\notin\{2,p\}$ and $$ (-1,p)_2=(-1)^{(p-1)/2},\qquad (-1,p)_p=\left(\frac{-1}p\right)=(-1)^{(p-1)/2} $$ by the first supplement from [L3], so the product for $(-1,p)$ is $1$. The same local formulas imply $(p,p)_v=(-1,p)_v$ for every place $v$, so the pair $(p,p)$ also has global product $1$. [L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 If $p$ is an odd prime, then $(2,p)_v=1$ for $v\notin\{2,p\}$ and $$ (2,p)_2=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8},\qquad (2,p)_p=\left(\frac2p\right)=(-1)^{(p^2-1)/8} $$ by the second supplement from [L3], so the pair $(2,p)$ has global product $1$. [L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $p$ and $q$ are distinct odd primes, then $(p,q)_v=1$ for $v\notin\{2,p,q\}$, while $$ (p,q)_2=(-1)^{(p-1)(q-1)/4},\qquad (p,q)_p=\left(\frac qp\right),\qquad (p,q)_q=\left(\frac pq\right). $$ Quadratic reciprocity [L3] says that the product of these three terms is $1$. Every generator pair therefore has global product $1$, and bilinearity from step 1.1 gives the reciprocity law for all $a,b\in\mathbb Q^\times$. [L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
