## Statement

Let $E/F$ be algebraic and suppose $E=F(T)$ for a subset $T\subseteq E$. If the minimal polynomial over $F$ of every $t\in T$ splits over $E$, then $E/F$ is normal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An algebraic extension $E/F$, a generating set $T$, and the splitting hypothesis in the Statement.

[F1] The field $F(T)$ is the smallest subfield containing $F\cup T$ ([[def-field-extension-generated-subfields-and-simple-extension]]).

[F2] Every element algebraic over $F$ has a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[F3] An algebraic splitting field of a nonzero polynomial is normal ([[prop-algebraic-splitting-extensions-are-normal]]).

[F4] Normality means that the minimal polynomial of every element of the extension splits there ([[def-normal-algebraic-extension]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite-support reduction.

1.1 The union $U=\bigcup_{T_0\subseteq T,\ T_0\text{ finite}}F(T_0)$ is a subfield of $E$: sums, products, inverses, and pairs of elements lie in the field generated by the union of their two finite supports. It contains $F\cup T$, so [F1] gives $E=F(T)\subseteq U$, while the reverse inclusion is immediate. [F1]

2.1 Fix $\beta\in E$. By step 1.1, choose a finite set $T_0=\{t_1,\ldots,t_m\}\subseteq T$ with $\beta\in F(T_0)$. Let $p_j$ be the minimal polynomial of $t_j$ over $F$, and let $K\subseteq E$ be the field generated by all roots in $E$ of the product $p_1\cdots p_m$. If $m=0$, take the product to be $1$ and $K=F$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Each $p_j$ splits over $E$ by hypothesis. Their product is monic and hence nonzero, so $K$ is its splitting field and contains every $t_j$; hence $\beta\in F(T_0)\subseteq K$. Also $K/F$ is algebraic because $K\subseteq E$ and $E/F$ is algebraic. By [F3], $K/F$ is normal. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $m_\beta$ be the minimal polynomial of $\beta$ over $F$. Since $\beta\in K$ and $K/F$ is normal, $m_\beta$ splits over $K$, hence over $E$. This holds for every $\beta\in E$, so [F4] proves that $E/F$ is normal. [F4, step 3.1] ∎
