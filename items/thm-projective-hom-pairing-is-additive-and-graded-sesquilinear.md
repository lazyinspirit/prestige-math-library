## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $A$ be a finite-dimensional unital associative $k$-algebra. For finite-dimensional projective left $A$-modules $P$ and finite-dimensional left $A$-modules $M$, the generator value $h(P,M):=\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)$ from [[def-projective-simple-hom-pairing-on-grothendieck-groups]] extends uniquely to a $\mathbb Z$-bilinear pairing $\langle-,-\rangle:K_0(A)\times G_0(A)\to\mathbb Z$.

If $A$ is also $\mathbb Z$-graded and $P,M$ are finite-dimensional graded left $A$-modules with $P$ projective in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$, the generator value $h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M):=\sum_{d\in\mathbb Z}v^d\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)$ extends uniquely to a $\mathbb Z$-bilinear pairing $\langle-,-\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}:K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)\times G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)\to R$, where $R:=\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$.

For all $r,s\in\mathbb Z$ and generator classes $[P],[M]$, $\langle v^r[P],v^s[M]\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}=v^{s-r}\langle[P],[M]\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}$. Consequently $\langle fx,gy\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}=\overline f\,g\,\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}$ for $f,g\in R$, $x\in K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ and $y\in G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$, with $\overline f(v):=f(v^{-1})$. Thus the first variable is conjugate-linear for the involution $v\mapsto v^{-1}$ and the second is linear. No axiom of choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a finite-dimensional unital associative $k$-algebra
$A$, and the finite-dimensional module objects specified in the Statement. In
the graded case $A$ is a $\mathbb Z$-graded $k$-algebra and all morphisms in
$\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ preserve degree. The groups and generator values
have the conventions in the Statement and cited definitions. No axiom of
choice is used.

[F1] The ungraded generator value is
$h(P,M)=\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)$
([[def-projective-simple-hom-pairing-on-grothendieck-groups]]).

[F2] The category of all left $A$-modules is abelian
([[thm-modules-over-a-ring-form-an-abelian-category]]).

[F3] A projective left $A$-module has the lifting property for every
surjective module homomorphism ([[def-projective-module]]).

[F4] In an abelian category, $\operatorname{Hom}(P,-)$ is exact when $P$ is
projective ([[thm-an-object-is-projective-exactly-when-hom-out-of-it-is-exact]]).

[F5] The category $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ is abelian and its exact
sequences, kernels, cokernels and finite biproducts are computed degreewise
([[lem-graded-module-kernels-cokernels-and-biproducts-are-degreewise]]).

[F6] A finite graded projective is projective in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$,
so it has the degree-zero lifting property against degree-zero epimorphisms
([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[F7] The internal shift is $M\{r\}_i=M_{i-r}$, is invertible with inverse
$\{-r\}$, and preserves the underlying vector space
([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

[F8] A degree-$d$ homogeneous $A$-linear map $P\to M$ sends $P_i$ into
$M_{i+d}$ ([[def-graded-balanced-tensor-product-and-homogeneous-hom]]).

[F9] $G_0$ is generated by object classes and imposes the relation
$[M]=[M']+[M'']$ for every short exact sequence
$0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$
([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]]).

[F10] Split $K_0$ imposes the direct-sum relation
$[P\oplus Q]=[P]+[Q]$ ([[def-split-grothendieck-group-of-an-additive-category]]).

[F11] Exact-sequence-additive class functions factor uniquely through $G_0$,
and direct-sum-additive class functions factor uniquely through split $K_0$
([[thm-grothendieck-group-universal-properties-and-functoriality]]).

[F12] The graded groups are $R$-modules with
$v^r[M]=[M\{r\}]$ and $v^r[P]=[P\{r\}]$
([[def-graded-grothendieck-group-shift-module-and-cartan-map]]).

[F13] Maps out of a finite direct sum are uniquely determined by their
restrictions to its summands ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-direct-sums]]).

[F14] For a linear map with finite-dimensional domain,
$\dim V=\dim\ker T+\dim\operatorname{im}T$
([[thm-rank-nullity]]).

[F15] Scalars act centrally on a $k$-algebra, so an $A$-linear map between left
$A$-modules is $k$-linear ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F16] The space of linear maps is a $k$-vector space under pointwise addition
and scalar multiplication ([[prop-linear-maps-form-a-vector-space]]).

[F17] For finite-dimensional $k$-vector spaces $V,W$, the space of linear
maps $V\to W$ is finite-dimensional ([[cor-dimensions-of-matrix-and-linear-map-spaces]]).

[F18] The graded generator value is
$h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M)=\sum_d v^d\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)$
([[def-projective-simple-hom-pairing-on-grothendieck-groups]]).

[F19] An abelian category is additive, has kernels and cokernels, and the
canonical comparison from coimage to image is an isomorphism
([[def-abelian-category]]).

[F20] An additive category is preadditive and has all finite biproducts
([[def-additive-category]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ and $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ for the full subcategories of finite-dimensional modules in $A\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ and $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$. The ambient categories are abelian by [F2] and [F5]. The finite full subcategories inherit preadditive Hom groups and composition from their ambient module categories. They are closed under kernels and cokernels: in the ungraded case these are a subspace of a finite-dimensional domain and a quotient of a finite-dimensional codomain; in the graded case [F5] computes them degreewise and their underlying spaces remain finite-dimensional. They also inherit finite biproducts, so they are additive by [F20]. For each map, its coimage and image remain in the finite subcategory because they are built from these kernels and cokernels; the ambient coimage-to-image isomorphism and its inverse are therefore morphisms in the full subcategory. By [F19], both finite subcategories are abelian. They are essentially small: for each ungraded dimension $n$, structures on the carrier $k^n$ form a subset of the set of functions $A\times k^n\to k^n$ satisfying the module identities; for graded modules, finite-support dimension vectors $\mathbf n:\mathbb Z\to\mathbb N$ with finite sum form a set, and the possible graded actions on $\bigoplus_d k^{\mathbf n(d)}$ form a set of families of maps satisfying the action identities. Every object is isomorphic to one of these models by a finite (homogeneous) basis. These objectwise coordinate models prove essential smallness without simultaneous basis choices. The full projective subcategories inherit essential smallness. [F2, F5, F19, F20, given, choose, construct, algebra]

1.2 Let $0\to M'\to M\to M''\to0$ be short exact in $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$. Centrality [F15] makes each $A$-linear map $k$-linear; sums and scalar multiples preserve $A$-linearity, so $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)$ is a $k$-subspace of the full linear-map space [F16], hence finite-dimensional by [F17]. Since $P$ is projective by [F3], exactness of $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,-)$ in the ambient abelian category [F4] gives $0\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M')\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M'')\to0$. Its arrows are $k$-linear by [F15]. The last arrow is surjective and its kernel is the image of the first, isomorphic to $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M')$; rank-nullity [F14] gives $h(P,M)=h(P,M')+h(P,M'')$, also when any of these spaces is zero. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F14, F15, F16, F17, given, algebra]

1.3 For every integer $d$, shift by $d$ is an exact equivalence of $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$: [F7] reindexes each homogeneous piece, so [F5] shows it preserves exact sequences, and its inverse is shift by $-d$. Thus if $q:E\twoheadrightarrow N$ is a degree-zero epimorphism, $q\{-d\}$ is an epimorphism. Given a degree-zero map $f:P\{d\}\to N$, shift it by $\{-d\}$ and lift the resulting map $P\to N\{-d\}$ through $q\{-d\}$ using the projectivity [F6]; shifting the lift back shows $P\{d\}$ is graded projective. It remains finite-dimensional because its underlying vector space is unchanged [F7]. [F5, F6, F7, given, construct, algebra]

1.4 The supports of finite-dimensional graded vector spaces $P$ and $M$ are finite: if a space of dimension $n$ had more than $n$ nonzero homogeneous components, choosing $n+1$ such components and one nonzero vector in each would contradict linear independence. If $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)\ne0$, a nonzero map has some element with nonzero image; decomposing it into homogeneous components shows some $P_i$ maps nontrivially into $M_{i+d}$. Hence $d\in\operatorname{supp}(M)-\operatorname{supp}(P)$, a finite set, and the sum defining $h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M)$ in [F18] has finite support. If either module is zero, the sum is empty and equals zero. The argument makes only finite selections. [F8, F18, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 A function on the underlying modules is degree zero from $P\{d\}$ to $M$ exactly when it sends $P\{d\}_i=P_{i-d}$ into $M_i$ for every $i$; setting $j=i-d$ makes this precisely the degree-$d$ condition $P_j\to M_{j+d}$ [F8]. Therefore $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A,0}(P\{d\},M)$. The degree-$d$ Hom space is a $k$-subspace of the full linear-map space, since its degree and $A$-linearity conditions are preserved by addition and scalar multiplication [F15, F16]; it is finite-dimensional by [F17]. [F8, F15, F16, F17, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 A degree-$d$ map $P\{r\}\to M\{s\}$ is the same underlying $A$-linear function as a map $P\to M$ of degree $e=d+r-s$: from $P\{r\}_i=P_{i-r}$ its image lies in $M\{s\}_{i+d}=M_{i+d-s}$, which after $j=i-r$ is $M_{j+e}$. Thus $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P\{r\},M\{s\})\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d+r-s}(P,M)$. Reindexing the finite sum from step 1.4 gives $h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P\{r\},M\{s\})=\sum_e v^{e-r+s}\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,e}(P,M)=v^{s-r}h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M)$. The group actions [F12] identify these shifts with multiplication by $v^r$ and $v^s$, proving the displayed formula on generators. [F7, F8, F12, F18, step 1.4, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply [F4] in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ to the projective object $P\{d\}$ from step 1.3 and any short exact sequence of finite-dimensional graded modules. Step 2.1 identifies the resulting exact Hom sequence with the degree-$d$ Hom sequence, whose maps are $k$-linear by [F15] and whose spaces are finite-dimensional by step 2.1. Rank-nullity [F14] gives $\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M)=\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M')+\dim_k\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,M'')$. The three sums have finite support by step 1.4, so summing the coefficient identities gives $h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M)=h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M')+h_{\mathrm{gr}}(P,M'')$, including sequences with zero terms. [F18, F4, F5, F14, F15, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 1.4, given, algebra]

4.1 For fixed $P$, postcomposition by a module isomorphism $M\to M'$ identifies the ungraded Hom spaces; in the graded case a degree-zero isomorphism identifies every degree-$d$ Hom space [F8]. The generator values are therefore class functions. By steps 1.2 and 3.1 they are exact-sequence-additive. The $G_0$ presentation [F9] and universal property [F11] give unique homomorphisms $\overline h_P:G_0(A)\to\mathbb Z$ and $\overline h^{\mathrm{gr}}_P:G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)\to R$ with the prescribed values on object classes [F1, F18]. [F1, F8, F9, F11, F18, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 If $P\cong P'$, precomposition with an isomorphism identifies their Hom spaces, preserving each degree in the graded case; hence the functions $[P]\mapsto\overline h_P$ and $[P]\mapsto\overline h^{\mathrm{gr}}_P$ are class functions. The zero module is projective, and finite direct sums of projectives remain projective because lifts on the summands combine to a lift on the sum [F3, F6]. The direct-sum universal property [F13] gives $\operatorname{Hom}_A(P\oplus Q,M)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_A(P,M)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_A(Q,M)$; in the graded case this decomposition preserves each degree because finite biproducts are computed degreewise [F5]. Thus the class functions are additive in the projective variable. The finite projective subcategories are essentially small by step 1.1; they are full preadditive subcategories and have a zero object and finite biproducts, so they are additive by [F20]. The split relation [F10] and universal property [F11], with target the abelian group of homomorphisms out of the corresponding $G_0$, factor these class functions uniquely through $K_0(A)$ and $K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$. Evaluation defines the claimed pairings, which are $\mathbb Z$-bilinear and unique because object classes generate both groups; zero Hom spaces give zero on zero objects. [F1, F3, F5, F6, F10, F11, F13, F18, F20, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Bilinearity extends the generator shift identity to finite Laurent combinations. For $f=\sum_r a_rv^r$ and $g=\sum_s b_sv^s$, $\langle fx,gy\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}=\sum_{r,s}a_rb_sv^{s-r}\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}=\overline f\,g\,\langle x,y\rangle_{\mathrm{gr}}$, where $\overline f(v)=f(v^{-1})$. Thus the graded pairing is sesquilinear, with no additional sign convention. [F12, step 5.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra] ∎
