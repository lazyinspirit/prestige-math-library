## Statement

Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field
with finite residue field $k$ of order $q$. For any finite extension $M/K$,
let $w_M=-\log_q|\cdot|_M$ for the unique extension of the base absolute value,
let $e(M/K)=[w_M(M^\times):\mathbb Z]$, and let $k_M$ be its residue field. Call
$M/K$ **unramified** when $e(M/K)=1$ and $k_M/k$ is separable. For every
finite extension $\ell/k$, there is, up to $K$-isomorphism, a unique finite
unramified extension $L/K$ whose residue field is $k$-isomorphic to $\ell$.
For every such $L$, reduction induces an isomorphism
$\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\to\operatorname{Gal}(k_L/k)$. Hence there is a
unique $K$-automorphism of $L$ whose reduction is $x\mapsto x^q$; call it the
arithmetic Frobenius of $L/K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $K$, $k=\mathbb F_q$, and a finite extension
$\ell/k$ of degree $d$.

[F1] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]: $K$ is
complete with discrete valuation, valuation ring $\mathcal O_K$, and finite
residue field $k$.

[F2] [[thm-finite-extension-local-degree-equals-ramification-index-times-residue-degree]]:
Every finite extension $M/K$ satisfies
$[M:K]=e(M/K)[k_M:k]$, with both factors finite.

[F3] [[cor-finite-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-simple]]: There is
$\bar a\in\ell$ with $\ell=k(\bar a)$.

[F4] [[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]]:
The extension $\ell/k$ is Galois and its group is cyclic of order $d$,
generated by the relative Frobenius.

[F5] [[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]]: The relative
Frobenius is $x\mapsto x^q$.

[F6] [[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]: Over a
complete nonarchimedean valued field, a monic factorization into coprime
residue polynomials lifts to a monic factorization with those reductions; a
simple residue root lifts uniquely to a root.

[F7] [[thm-valuation-ring-is-integrally-closed]]: A valuation ring is an
integrally closed domain.

[F8] [[thm-finite-extension-local-degree-equals-ramification-index-times-residue-degree]]
and [[lem-choice-free-norm-equivalence-over-finite-residue-local-field]]:
The degree theorem constructs the unique extending absolute value on every
finite extension of $K$ in its proof, steps 1.1, 2.1, and 3.1. It is a vector-space
norm, so the choice-free norm-equivalence lemma proves completeness.
If $\iota:M\to M'$ is a $K$-embedding between finite extensions,
$x\mapsto|\iota(x)|_{M'}$ is an extending absolute value on $M$, so
uniqueness makes the embedding isometric. No Countable Choice is used
in this finite-residue local-field argument.

[F9] [[thm-uniqueness-of-finite-fields]]: Finite fields of the same order are
isomorphic, so the base $k$ can be identified with $\mathbb F_q$ when applying
the finite-field Frobenius theorem [F4].

[F10] [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]: For a finite field
extension, $[L:K]$ is its dimension as a $K$-vector space.

[F11] [[def-finite-galois-extension-and-galois-group]]: A finite extension is
Galois when it is separable and normal; $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$ is its group
of $K$-automorphisms.

[F12] [[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]: The degree is
the largest exponent with nonzero coefficient, and a polynomial is monic when
its leading coefficient is $1$.

[F13] [[def-irreducible-and-prime-elements-in-a-domain]]: An irreducible
polynomial is a nonzero nonunit whose factorizations have a unit factor.

[F14] [[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]: For a nonconstant
polynomial over a field, its quotient ring is a field exactly when the
polynomial is irreducible.

[F15] [[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]]: If
$a$ has minimal polynomial of degree $d$ over a field $F$, then $F(a)/F$ has
degree $d$ and basis $1,a,\ldots,a^{d-1}$.

[F16] [[thm-splitting-fields-exist-for-nonzero-polynomials]]: Every nonzero
polynomial over a field has a splitting field.

[F17] [[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]]: An element is integral
over a ring when it is a root of a monic polynomial over that ring.

[F18] [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]]: The elements integral over a
nonzero ring form a subring in any containing commutative ring.

[F19] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]: An algebraic element
has a unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial generating the kernel of
evaluation.

[F20] [[def-formal-derivative-of-a-polynomial]]: For
$f=\sum_i a_iT^i$, the formal derivative is $f'=\sum_{i\ge1}i a_iT^{i-1}$.

[F21] [[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]: A
nonzero polynomial over a field is separable exactly when its gcd with its
formal derivative is $1$.

[F22] [[def-repeated-root-and-separable-polynomial]]: A polynomial is
separable when it has no repeated root in any extension field.

[F23] [[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]: A finite extension
is Galois exactly when it is the splitting field of a separable polynomial.

[F24] [[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]: A splitting field
is generated over its base by all roots of the polynomial.

## Proof

**Given:** $K$, $k$, and $\ell/k$ as above.


1.1 Put $d=[\ell:k]$, the extension degree by [F10], and choose a primitive generator $\bar a$ as in [F3]. Let $\bar g\in k[T]$ be its monic minimal polynomial, of degree $d$, by [F19]. Identify $k$ with $\mathbb F_q$ by [F9]. The finite-field Frobenius theorem [F4] and the relative Frobenius definition [F5] give $\bar a^{q^d}=\bar a$, so $\bar g$ divides $P(T)=T^{q^d}-T$. By [F20], $P'=-1$ in characteristic $p$; hence $\gcd(P,P')=1$ and [F21] makes $P$ separable in the sense of [F22]. Since $\bar g$ is irreducible, a common factor of $\bar g$ and $\bar H=P/\bar g$ would imply $\bar g^2\mid P$; a root of $\bar g$ in a splitting field [F16] would then be repeated in $P$, contradicting separability. Thus $\bar g$ and $\bar H$ are coprime. [F3, F4, F5, F9, F10, F16, F19, F20, F21, F22, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 Lift $\bar g$ and $\bar H$ coefficientwise to monic polynomials in $\mathcal O_K[T]$. By [F1] and [F6], the complete-DVR Hensel factor lift gives monic $G,H\in\mathcal O_K[T]$ with $P=GH$, $\bar G=\bar g$, and $\bar H=P/\bar g$. [F1, F6, F12, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The polynomial $G$ is irreducible over $K$. If it were reducible, since $G$ is monic it would factor as $G=UV$ with monic positive-degree $U,V\in K[T]$. Take a splitting field of $G$ using [F16]. Every root of $U$ or $V$ is a root of the monic $G$, hence is integral over $\mathcal O_K$ by [F12, F17]. The coefficients of $U$ and $V$ are elementary symmetric polynomials in their roots, so [F18] makes them integral over $\mathcal O_K$. They lie in $K$, and the valuation ring $\mathcal O_K$ is integrally closed by [F7], so all coefficients lie in $\mathcal O_K$. Their reductions remain monic of positive degree and multiply to $\bar G=\bar g$, contradicting the irreducibility of $\bar g$ [F13]. Thus $G$ is irreducible. By [F14], $L_0=K[T]/(G)$ is a field. If $\alpha$ is the class of $T$, its minimal polynomial is $G$ by [F19], so [F15] gives $[L_0:K]=d$. [F7, F12, F13, F14, F15, F16, F17, F18, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Let $L/K$ be any finite unramified extension with residue field $k$-isomorphic to $\ell$. Then $[L:K]=d$ by [F2]. Choose in $k_L$ the image $\bar b$ corresponding to $\bar a$ under the residue-field isomorphism. The polynomial $P(T)=T^{q^d}-T$ has $\bar b$ as a simple residue root because its reduction has derivative $-1$, so [F6] gives a unique lift $b\in\mathcal O_L$ with residue $\bar b$ and $P(b)=0$; $L$ is complete by [F8]. The residue $\bar H(\bar b)$ is nonzero since $\bar g$ and $\bar H$ are coprime, so $H(b)$ is a unit. From $0=P(b)=G(b)H(b)$ it follows that $G(b)=0$. [F2, F6, F8, F10, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, choose]

4.1 Let $\alpha$ be the image of $T$ in $L_0$. It is integral over $\mathcal O_K$ because $G$ is monic [F12, F17]; if $|\alpha|_{L_0}>1$, its leading term in $G(\alpha)$ would have strictly larger absolute value than every lower term, contradicting $G(\alpha)=0$. Thus $\alpha\in\mathcal O_{L_0}$ and its residue has minimal polynomial $\bar g$, so [F15] gives $[k(\bar\alpha):k]=d$ and the residue degree $f_0=[k_{L_0}:k]$ is at least $d$. By [F2], $d=[L_0:K]=e(L_0/K)f_0$. Since $e(L_0/K)\ge1$ and $f_0\ge d$, equality forces $e(L_0/K)=1$ and $f_0=d$. Hence $L_0/K$ is unramified and its residue field is $k$-isomorphic to $\ell$. [F2, F8, F10, F12, F15, F17, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Since $G(b)=0$ and $G$ is irreducible by step 3.1, [F19] makes $G$ the minimal polynomial of $b$; [F15] gives $[K(b):K]=d$. The residue isomorphism used in step 3.2 sends $\bar a$ to $\bar b$, so $\bar b$ has minimal polynomial $\bar g$ and [F15] gives $[k(\bar b):k]=d$. Thus the residue field of $K(b)$ has degree at least $d$. Applying [F2] to $K(b)/K$ forces its ramification index to be $1$ and residue degree $d$. Applying [F2] to $L/K$ gives $[L:K]=d$; since $K(b)\subseteq L$ and both degrees are $d$, $K(b)=L$. Evaluation at $b$ and at the class $\alpha$ in step 3.1 has kernel $(G)$ by [F19], so both fields are isomorphic over $K$ to $K[T]/(G)$. This proves uniqueness up to $K$-isomorphism. [F2, F15, F19, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

4.3 The field $L_0$ from step 3.1 is complete by [F8]. Since $\bar a$ generates $\ell/k$, its minimal polynomial $\bar g$ has degree $d$. By [F4] the relative Frobenius has order $d$ and generates the residue Galois group, so its $d$ conjugates $\bar a^{q^j}$ are distinct roots of $\bar g$; [F5] shows that each is a root of $P$. Separability of $P$ [F21, F22] makes each a simple root. By [F6], each lifts uniquely to a root $b_j$ of $P$ in $\mathcal O_{L_0}$. Since $\bar H(\bar a^{q^j})\ne0$, each $H(b_j)$ is a unit, so $G(b_j)=0$. These $d$ roots have distinct residues and exhaust the degree-$d$ polynomial $G$. Thus $G$ splits over $L_0$ into distinct linear factors; the roots remain distinct in every extension, so [F22] makes $G$ separable. Since $L_0=K(\alpha)$ is generated by a root of $G$ and all roots of $G$ lie in $L_0$, [F24] makes it a splitting field of $G$. The extension has degree $d$ by [F15], so [F23] makes $L_0/K$ Galois. [F4, F5, F6, F8, F10, F12, F15, F21, F22, F23, F24, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By [F8], each $K$-automorphism of $L_0$ preserves its valuation ring and induces a $k$-automorphism of $k_{L_0}$. Conversely, every element of $\operatorname{Gal}(k_{L_0}/k)$ sends $\bar a$ to one of the roots $\bar a^{q^j}$ by [F4]; the unique Hensel lift $b_j$ gives a $K$-embedding sending $\alpha$ to $b_j$. This embedding is surjective because it is injective between $K$-vector spaces of the same finite dimension $d$, so it is a $K$-automorphism reducing to that residue automorphism. If two $K$-automorphisms induce the same residue map, they send $\alpha$ to roots of $G$ with the same residue; uniqueness in Hensel's lemma makes those roots equal, and $L_0=K(\alpha)$ makes the automorphisms equal. Thus reduction is an isomorphism and the unique lift of $x\mapsto x^q$ is the arithmetic Frobenius of $L_0/K$. [F4, F5, F6, F8, F10, F11, F15, F19, step 3.1, step 4.3, algebra]

6.1 Let $L/K$ be any finite unramified extension with residue field $k$-isomorphic to $\ell$. Step 4.2 gives a $K$-isomorphism $L_0\to L$. By uniqueness of the extended absolute value [F8], this isomorphism preserves the valuations, valuation rings, and residue maps. Conjugation therefore identifies the reduction map for $L/K$ with the isomorphism in step 5.1 and transports the unique arithmetic Frobenius lift. This proves the remaining claims for every such $L$. [F8, step 4.2, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
