## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Dependent Choice. Let $k\subseteq K$ satisfy $K=k(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ for a possibly empty finite tuple $a_i\in K$. Assume every element of $K$ algebraic over $k$ belongs to $k$. Assume also there is a possibly empty finite tuple $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in K$ such that no nonzero polynomial in $k[T_1,\ldots,T_r]$ vanishes at the tuple, and the subfield $F=k(t_1,\ldots,t_r)$ generated by $k$ and these elements satisfies $[K:F]<\infty$ and every element of $K$ has a minimal polynomial over $F$ with no repeated root. These conditions define a finitely generated regular extension $K/k$. Let $A/K$ be an abelian variety, that is, a proper, geometrically connected, smooth separated group variety of finite type. A $K/k$-trace pair $(T,\tau)$ exists, is unique up to a unique isomorphism compatible with $\tau$, and its universal homomorphism $\tau:T_K\to A$ has finite kernel.

## Proof

1.1 We construct first a left adjoint, the $K/k$-image, for any abelian variety $G/K$. Consider all homomorphisms $a:G\to B_K$ with $B/k$ abelian. By the quotient/image and Chow subvariety-descent lemma, the image has the form $C_K$ for a unique abelian subvariety $C\subset B$. Thus $a$ factors through a surjection $G\to C_K$. The collection of kernels of these surjections is a nonempty set of closed subgroup schemes of $G$: it includes the zero homomorphism to the point group, and is a subcollection of the set of closed subschemes of the fixed noetherian scheme $G$. It is closed under finite intersection. Indeed the image of $(a,a'):G\to(B\times B')_K$ is the base change of an abelian subvariety over $k$, and its kernel is the scheme intersection of the two kernels. [F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 A noetherian scheme satisfies the descending chain condition on closed subschemes, by the ascending chain condition on their ideal sheaves on a finite affine cover. Choose a minimal member $H$ of this collection and a corresponding surjection $q:G\to I_K$, where $I/k$ is abelian. For every other $a:G\to B_K$, minimality applied to $H\cap\ker a$ gives $H\subset\ker a$. The quotient universal property supplies a unique homomorphism $a':I_K\to B_K$ with $a=a'q$. Hom descent supplies a unique homomorphism $b:I\to B$ over $k$ with $b_K=a'$. Hence composition with $q$ gives a natural bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_k(I,B)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_K(G,B_K)$. Minimality uses the stated AC/DC; the argument makes no assumption that the kernel is reduced or connected. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Apply this construction to $G=A^\vee$ and write $I$ for its image. Set $T=I^\vee$. Under biduality, the dual $q^\vee:T_K\to A^{\vee\vee}\cong A$ is a homomorphism $\tau:T_K\to A$. For every abelian variety $C/k$, duality, step 2.1, and duality again give natural bijections $\operatorname{Hom}_k(C,T)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_k(I,C^\vee)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_K(A^\vee,C_K^\vee)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_K(C_K,A).$ The composite sends $h$ to $\tau h_K$, by functoriality of duality and the definition of $q$. Thus $(T,\tau)$ has the full trace universal property, for every homomorphism and without a finite-kernel restriction on that homomorphism. The surjectivity of $q$ and part (c) of the projector/descent lemma show that $\ker\tau$ is finite as a group scheme, including its possible infinitesimal part. [F2, F4, F5, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 If $(T',\tau')$ is another trace pair, its universal property gives unique $u:T\to T'$ and $v:T'\to T$ compatible with $\tau,\tau'$. The same uniqueness applied to the composites yields $vu=\operatorname{id}_T$ and $uv=\operatorname{id}_{T'}$. Hence the pairs are uniquely compatibly isomorphic. If $K=k$, the identity pair $(A,\operatorname{id})$ already has the universal property, so the empty-tuple case is included. If $A=0$, the construction gives the point trace with trivial kernel. [F5, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
