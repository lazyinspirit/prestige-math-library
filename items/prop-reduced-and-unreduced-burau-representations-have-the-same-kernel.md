## Statement

Assume AC (inherited through the topological definition of the reduced
representation and the agreement theorem). Let $n\ge2$, let
$\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ and $\bar\rho_n$ be the unreduced and reduced Burau
representations over $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$, and let
$K=\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda_1)\cong\mathbb Q(t)$ be the fraction field of the
integral domain $\Lambda_1$, so that $\Lambda_1\subseteq K$ canonically. Then
$$\ker\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n=\ker\bar\rho_n,$$ that is, a braid acts trivially in
the unreduced representation if and only if it acts trivially in the reduced
one. The proof uses the field splitting: over $K$,
$$K^n=\ker\sigma\oplus Kv$$ with $\sigma=(1,t,\dots,t^{n-1})$ and
$v=(1,\dots,1)^T$, both summands $B_n$-invariant, the line $Kv$ trivial, and
$\ker\sigma$ the reduced module after extension of scalars; hence trivial action
is equivalent on the two summands. **Caveat:** this splitting is a field
statement, obtained by inverting the nonzero elements of $\Lambda_1$. It is
not asserted over $\Lambda_1$, and no integral complement or integral splitting
of the exact sequence of
[[prop-unreduced-burau-fits-an-exact-sequence-with-the-reduced-module]] is
claimed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; $n\ge2$; the ring $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ and its fraction field $K$; the free module $W=\Lambda_1^n$ with its standard basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$; the unreduced representation $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ on $W$ and the reduced representation $\bar\rho_n$ on $M_{\mathrm{red}}$; the column vector $v=(1,\dots,1)^T$ and the row vector $\sigma=(1,t,\dots,t^{n-1})$.

[F1] $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n:B_n\to\operatorname{GL}_n(\Lambda_1)$ and $\bar\rho_n:B_n\to\operatorname{GL}_{n-1}(\Lambda_1)$ are group homomorphisms: the first acts on $W$ by the matrices $B_i$ of [[def-unreduced-burau-matrices]] extended multiplicatively, and the second is the matrix of the action on $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ in its fixed basis ([[lem-unreduced-burau-matrices-satisfy-the-artin-relations]], [[def-reduced-burau-representation]]); the braid group is generated by $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]).

[F2] $U=H_1(\tilde X,p^{-1}d;\mathbb Z)$ is free with basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$; the action of $B_n$ on $U$ has matrix $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)$ in this basis; the inclusion $M_{\mathrm{red}}\to U$ is $B_n$-equivariant with image $\ker\partial_*$, and under the basis identification $U\cong W$ the submodule $\ker\partial_*$ is carried onto $\ker\sigma=\{x\in W:\sigma(x)=0\}$, the connecting map being $\partial_*(e_i)=t^{i-1}(t-1)$. Hence the action on $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ corresponds to the restriction of $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ to $\ker\sigma$ ([[thm-topological-and-matrix-burau-representations-agree]], clause (1); [[prop-unreduced-burau-fits-an-exact-sequence-with-the-reduced-module]]; [[def-reduced-burau-representation]]).

[F3] $B_iv=v$ and $\sigma B_i=\sigma$ for every $i$, hence $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)v=v$ and $\sigma\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)=\sigma$ for every $\beta\in B_n$ by [F1]; and $\sigma(v)=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1}$ is a nonzero element of $\Lambda_1$ ([[lem-the-invariant-vector-and-covector-of-the-unreduced-burau]] (a), (b), (c)).

[F4] $\Lambda_1$ is an integral domain, its fraction field $K=\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda_1)$ is a field containing $\Lambda_1$, and the structure map $\Lambda_1\to K$ is injective: a nonzero element of a domain is not equivalent to $0$ in its localisation at the nonzero elements ([[lem-units-and-powers-of-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]] (a), [[def-field-of-fractions]], [[def-restriction-and-extension-of-scalars]]).

[F5] $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ is free of rank $n-1$ and, under the identification of [F2], $\ker\sigma$ is a direct summand of $W$: since $\sigma(e_1)=1$, every $x\in W$ is $x=(x-\sigma(x)e_1)+\sigma(x)e_1$ with $x-\sigma(x)e_1\in\ker\sigma$ ([[lem-the-reduced-burau-module-is-free-of-rank-n-minus-one]], [[prop-unreduced-burau-fits-an-exact-sequence-with-the-reduced-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Extension of scalars is injective on endomorphism rings.* Let $V$ be a free $\Lambda_1$-module of finite rank with basis $x_1,\dots,x_m$ and let $T:V\to V$ be $\Lambda_1$-linear. The coordinate isomorphism $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}V\to K^m$ sends $a\otimes\sum_j\lambda_jx_j$ to $(a\lambda_j)_j$, with inverse $(c_j)_j\mapsto\sum_jc_j\otimes x_j$. Then $T=\mathrm{id}_V$ if and only if $T_K:=\mathrm{id}_K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}T$ is the identity of $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}V$: the forward direction is immediate, and if $T_K=\mathrm{id}$ then $1\otimes(T-\mathrm{id})(x_j)=0$ in $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}V\cong K^m$ for each $j$, and reading this in the coordinates of $V$ and using the injectivity of $\Lambda_1\to K$ of [F4] gives $(T-\mathrm{id})(x_j)=0$ for all $j$, so $T=\mathrm{id}_V$. Applying this to $W$ (free of rank $n$ by [F1]) and to $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ (free of rank $n-1$ by [F5]), for every $\beta\in B_n$ we have $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)=I_n$ if and only if its extension acts as the identity on $K^n=K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}W$, and $\bar\rho_n(\beta)=I_{n-1}$ if and only if $\beta$ acts as the identity on $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}M_{\mathrm{red}}$. [F1, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 *The field splitting and its invariance.* Extend $\sigma$ to the $K$-linear functional $\sigma_K$ on $K^n$ with the same coordinates. By [F3], $\sigma(v)=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1}$ is a nonzero element of $\Lambda_1$, hence a nonzero element of $K$, so $\sigma_K(v)\ne0$ and $\ker\sigma_K\cap Kv=0$; every $x\in K^n$ has the decomposition $x=(x-\sigma_K(x)\sigma_K(v)^{-1}v)+\sigma_K(x)\sigma_K(v)^{-1}v$, whose first term lies in $\ker\sigma_K$. Thus $K^n=\ker\sigma_K\oplus Kv$. Both summands are $B_n$-invariant: $Kv$ because $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)v=v$ for every $\beta$ by [F3], and $\ker\sigma_K$ because the row-vector identity $\sigma\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)=\sigma$ extends to $K$ and gives $\sigma_K(\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)x)=\sigma_K(x)$ for every $x\in K^n$; on $Kv$ the action is the identity. Moreover $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}\ker\sigma=\ker\sigma_K$ inside $K^n$: the split decomposition $W=\ker\sigma\oplus\Lambda_1e_1$ of [F5] extends to $K^n=(K\otimes\ker\sigma)\oplus Ke_1$, with the extended inclusion injective because it still has a left inverse. The functional $\sigma_K$ vanishes on the first summand and sends $ae_1$ to $a$, so its kernel is exactly that first summand. [F1, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 *Transport to the reduced action.* The identification $M_{\mathrm{red}}\cong\ker\sigma$ of [F2] is $\Lambda_1$-linear and $B_n$-equivariant, so after extension of scalars it gives $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}M_{\mathrm{red}}\cong K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}\ker\sigma=\ker\sigma_K$, and the action of every $\beta\in B_n$ on $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}M_{\mathrm{red}}$ corresponds to the restriction of $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K$ to $\ker\sigma_K$. Combining with step 1.1 applied to the free module $M_{\mathrm{red}}$, for every $\beta\in B_n$: $\bar\rho_n(\beta)=I_{n-1}$ if and only if the action of $\beta$ on $K\otimes_{\Lambda_1}M_{\mathrm{red}}$ is the identity, if and only if $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K$ is the identity on $\ker\sigma_K$. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 *Equality of kernels.* Let $\beta\in B_n$. Since $K^n=\ker\sigma_K\oplus Kv$ by step 1.2 and $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K$ is the identity on $Kv$, the map $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K$ is the identity on $K^n$ if and only if it is the identity on $\ker\sigma_K$. Therefore step 1.1 for $W$ and step 2.1 give $\beta\in\ker\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ if and only if $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)=I_n$ if and only if $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K=\mathrm{id}_{K^n}$ if and only if $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)_K$ is the identity on $\ker\sigma_K$ if and only if $\bar\rho_n(\beta)=I_{n-1}$ if and only if $\beta\in\ker\bar\rho_n$. Hence $\ker\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n=\ker\bar\rho_n$ as subgroups of $B_n$. The argument used the field splitting of step 1.2; no integral complement or splitting over $\Lambda_1$ is asserted. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
