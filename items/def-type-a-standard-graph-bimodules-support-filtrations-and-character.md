## Definition

**Graphs and supports.** Keep $k=\mathbb Q$, $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with
$\deg x_i=2$, and the affine scheme $V=\mathbb A^n_k=\operatorname{Spec}R$,
whose $k$-points are $V(k)=k^n$. Use the place-permuting action of $S_n$
from [[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]].
For $w\in S_n$ let
$$Gr(w):=\operatorname{Graph}(w:V\to V)\subseteq V\times_kV,\quad Gr(w)(k)=\{(w\lambda,\lambda):\lambda\in k^n\},\qquad Gr(A):=\bigcup_{w\in A}Gr(w)\ \ (A\subseteq S_n),$$
so that $V\times_kV=\operatorname{Spec}(R\otimes_kR)$ and $Gr(w)$ is the graph
of the map $\lambda\mapsto w\lambda$. A graded $(R,R)$-bimodule $M$ is the
same thing as a graded module over $R\otimes_kR$, and the support of $m\in M$ is
the zero set $\operatorname{supp}(m):=V(\operatorname{Ann}(m))\subseteq V\times V$
of its annihilator ideal in $R\otimes_kR$. For $A\subseteq S_n$ put
$$\Gamma_AM:=\{m\in M:\operatorname{supp}(m)\subseteq Gr(A)\},$$
a graded sub-bimodule of $M$. Since $Gr(A)$ is closed with radical ideal
$I(Gr(A))=\bigcap_{w\in A}I(Gr(w))$ (an intersection of prime ideals, since each
$Gr(w)$ is a linear subspace of $V\times V$ complementing the second factor), an
element $m$ lies in $\Gamma_AM$ exactly when $I(Gr(A))^N m=0$ for some $N\ge0$:
this is the Nullstellensatz together with the Noetherianity of
$R\otimes_kR$. The ideal itself need not annihilate such an element — in
$(R\otimes_kR)/I(Gr(e))^2$ the class of $1$ is supported on $Gr(e)$ but is not
killed by $I(Gr(e))$ — which is why the criterion is stated with a power of the
ideal. For $i\in\mathbb Z$ we write
$$\Gamma_{\ge i}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\ell(x)\ge i\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{\le i}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\ell(x)\le i\}}M,$$
using the length function of [[lem-type-a-reduced-words-are-connected-by-braid-moves]],
while for $y\in S_n$ the $y$-indexed symbols denote the *Bruhat
principal-set* cutoffs of Soergel's Notation 6.1,
$$\Gamma_{\le y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x\le y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{<y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x<y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{\ge y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x\ge y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{>y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x>y\}}M,$$
with $\Gamma_yM:=\Gamma_{\{y\}}M$ the elements concentrated on the single graph
$Gr(y)$ and $\Gamma_{\ne y}M:=\Gamma_{S_n\setminus\{y\}}M$ the elements
concentrated off $Gr(y)$. The integer-indexed symbols of the previous display are
the *length* cutoffs used to define the flags below, and they must not be
confused with the element-indexed Bruhat cutoffs: for $y=s_1$ in $S_4$ the
submodule $\Gamma_{\le1}M$ contains $Gr(s_3)$ while
$\Gamma_{\le y}M=\Gamma_{\{e,s_1\}}M$ does not, and the two cutoffs agree at
the extremes, $\Gamma_{\le w_0}=\Gamma_{\le\ell(w_0)}=M$ and
$\Gamma_{<e}=\Gamma_{\le-1}=0$. So
$\Gamma^yM:=M/\Gamma_{\ne y}M$ is Soergel's $y$-layer
*quotient*; following Soergel's
Notation 6.1 we write the two $y$-layer quotients with an underline,
$$\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M:=\Gamma_{\le y}M/\Gamma_{<y}M,\qquad \underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M:=\Gamma_{\ge y}M/\Gamma_{>y}M,$$
so that $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ is the quotient of the lower support
submodule by $\Gamma_{<y}M$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M$ the quotient of
the upper support submodule by the part supported strictly above $Gr(y)$.

**Standard bimodules.** For $w\in S_n$ let $R_w$ be the graded $(R,R)$-bimodule
which equals $R$ as a graded $k$-vector space and has
$$f\cdot x=fx,\qquad x\cdot g=w(g)\,x\qquad(f,g,x\in R),$$
where $w(g)(\lambda):=g(w^{-1}\lambda)$; $R_w$ is generated by the element $1$ of
degree $0$. Write $(k)$ for the *external* shift $M(k):=M\{-k\}$ of
[[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]], so that
$M(k)_d=M_{d+k}$; this is the shift convention of the type-A sources, and it is
the one in which $B_i=R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R(1)$ of
[[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]] has $1\otimes1$ of
degree $-1$. Put
$$\Delta_w(d):=R_w(d-\ell(w)),\qquad \nabla_w(d):=R_w(d+\ell(w))\qquad(d\in\mathbb Z),$$
so that $\Delta_w(d)$ is the standard bimodule $R_w$ with its generator in
degree $\ell(w)-d$, and $\nabla_w(d)$ is $R_w$ with its generator in degree
$-\ell(w)-d$. Thus $\Delta_w(0)=R_w\{\ell(w)\}$,
$\nabla_w(0)=R_w\{-\ell(w)\}$ and $\Delta_e(d)=\nabla_e(d)=R(d)$ is generated
in degree $-d$; in particular $\Delta_e(0)=\nabla_e(0)=R_e=R$.

For graded free $R$-modules in this type-A page, a homogeneous free generator of degree $a$ contributes $v^a$ to graded rank. This is the $v\mapsto v^{-1}$ transform of Soergel's rank convention in his Notation 5.2; all rank formulas below use this convention.

**Support filtrations.** A $\Delta$-flag of a graded $R$-bimodule $M$ is a
finite chain $0=M_0\subseteq M_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_k=M$ of graded
sub-bimodules which refines the length filtration
$$M=\Gamma_{\ge0}M\supseteq\Gamma_{\ge1}M\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\Gamma_{\ge\ell(w_0)+1}M=0$$
of $M$ (here $\Gamma_{\ge0}M=M$ for every bimodule supported on
$Gr(S_n)=\bigcup_{w}Gr(w)$, which all bimodules of this page are, and
$\Gamma_{\ge i}M=0$ for $i>\ell(w_0)$ because no graph $Gr(x)$ with
$\ell(x)>i$ remains) and whose successive quotients are
standard bimodules $M_j/M_{j-1}\cong R_{x_j}\{a_j\}$. We require each
length layer to be a finite direct sum of shifted graph bimodules of that
length, as in Soergel's Definition 5.4. Thus the
quotients carry a numbering with $\ell(x_1)\ge\cdots\ge\ell(x_k)$ and each
$R_{x_j}\{a_j\}$ is one graph, so that $M$ has a "support flag" in the sense of
Soergel's $F_\Delta$. A $\nabla$-flag has the same direct-sum condition on its length layers, with the reverse length
filtration $\Gamma_{\le i}M$ and $\ell(x_1)\le\cdots\le\ell(x_k)$. The
refinement condition is a condition on supports, not an ordering of incomparable
quotients of equal length; only the lengths are ordered. For $i=\ell(x)$ the
graded multiplicity is
$$(M:\Delta_x(d)):=\text{multiplicity of }\Delta_x(d)\text{ in } \Gamma_{\ge i}M/\Gamma_{\ge i+1}M,$$
and, for a $\nabla$-flag, $(M:\nabla_x(d))$ is the multiplicity of
$\nabla_x(d)$ in $\Gamma_{\le i}M/\Gamma_{\le i-1}M$; a priori both symbols
refer to a chosen compatible enumeration (the next items on this page prove that
they do not depend on it). With $T_x$ the Hecke basis of
[[lem-type-a-hecke-standard-basis-for-soergel-comparison]] and
$\widetilde T_x:=v^{\ell(x)}T_x$, the character sums attached to a flag are
$$h_\Delta(M):=\sum_{x,d}(M:\Delta_x(d))\,v^d\,\widetilde T_x,\qquad h_\nabla(M):=\sum_{x,d}(M:\nabla_x(d))\,v^{-d}\,\widetilde T_x,$$
and they are used only through the items that prove these sums intrinsic.

**Small $n$.** For $n\le1$ there is a single element $e$ and every object is a finite direct sum of shifts $\bigoplus_j R(d_j)$. Its one-graph flags have these summands as quotients, so $h_\Delta=\sum_jv^{d_j}\widetilde T_e$ and $h_\nabla=\sum_jv^{-d_j}\widetilde T_e$; in particular both characters send the unit $R$ to $1$.

## Remark

**(a) Maps between standard bimodules.** For $v,w\in S_n$ and $a,b\in\mathbb Z$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}\bigl(R_v(a),R_w(b)\bigr)\cong \begin{cases}R(b-a),&v=w,\\ 0,&v\ne w,\end{cases}$$
where $R(c)$ denotes the free graded $R$-module on one generator of degree $-c$.
Indeed a bimodule map is determined by $\varphi(1)=h\in R$, and $f\cdot1\cdot g\mapsto
f\,h\,w(g)$ must equal $v(g)\,f\,h$; since $R$ is a domain this forces
$v(g)h=w(g)h$ for all $g$, hence $h=0$ or $v=w$. The generator $1$ of $R_v(a)$
has degree $-a$ and maps to $h$, which has degree $\deg h-b$ as an element of
$R_w(b)$, so the homogeneous map $\varphi_h$ has degree $\deg h+a-b$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}(R_v(a),R_w(b))\cong R(b-a)$ in the case $v=w$.

**(b) Composition.** The $R$-balanced assignment
$$R_w\otimes_RR_v\longrightarrow R_{wv},\qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto a\,w(b),$$
where $w(b)(\lambda)=b(w^{-1}\lambda)$ and the product is taken in $R$, is a
well-defined degree-zero isomorphism of graded $(R,R)$-bimodules. It is well
defined because for $f\in R$ the relation $(a\cdot f)\otimes b=a\otimes(fb)$
reads $w(f)a\otimes b=a\otimes fb$ and maps to $w(f)\,a\,w(b)=a\,w(f)w(b)$ on
both sides; it is left $R$-linear, and it respects the right actions because
$w(bh)=w(b)w(h)$ for $h\in R$. It sends the $1$-tensor $1\otimes1$ of degree $0$
to $1$ and is therefore surjective, and both sides are free of rank one as left
$R$-modules, so it is an isomorphism. In particular
$R_w\otimes_RR_v\cong R_{wv}$ (and $R_w\otimes_RR_v\cong R_w$ only when $v=e$),
while tensoring with $w=e$ shows that the assignment is compatible with the
unit. The single assignment $1\otimes1\mapsto1$ does extend uniquely to a bimodule
map, and the map it extends to is exactly the twisted one above; what fails is
the untwisted formula $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, which is not balanced when $w\ne e$: the equality $(1\cdot f)\otimes1=1\otimes f$ in $R_w\otimes_RR_v$ would map to $w(f)$ on the left and $f$ on the right, and these differ for a polynomial moved by $w$.

**(c) The rank-one calculus.** Let $s=s_i$, $R^s=R^{s_i}$, $\alpha=\alpha_i$,
$\delta:=\alpha/2$, and $B_s=R\otimes_{R^s}R(1)$ with the images
$u:=1\otimes1$ of degree $-1$ and $w_0:=1\otimes\delta$ of degree $1$
([[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]]); these two elements
form a graded left $R$-basis of $B_s$, and every $g\in R$ has a unique
decomposition $g=g^++\delta g^-$ with $g^\pm\in R^s$, given by
$g^+=\tfrac12(g+s(g))$. The right action on the basis is
$$u\cdot g=g^+u+g^-w_0,\qquad w_0\cdot g=\delta^2g^-u+g^+w_0,$$
as follows from $1\otimes g=1\otimes(g^++\delta g^-)=g^+u+g^-w_0$ and
$\delta g=\delta g^++\delta^2g^-$ with $\delta^2=\alpha^2/4\in R^s$. Hence
$$(\delta u+w_0)\cdot g=g\,(\delta u+w_0),\qquad (\delta u-w_0)\cdot g=s(g)(\delta u-w_0),$$
so $R(\delta u+w_0)\cong R(-1)$ and $R(\delta u-w_0)\cong R_s(-1)$ are
sub-bimodules of $B_s$, generated in degree $1$. Moreover for
$f,h\in R$ one has $(fu+hw_0)\mapsto f+h\delta$ under $f\otimes g\mapsto fg$
and $(fu+hw_0)\mapsto f-h\delta$ under $f\otimes g\mapsto f s(g)$, because
$1\otimes g=g^+u+g^-w_0$ and $s(g)=g^+-\delta g^-$; both maps are surjective,
their kernels are the two displayed sub-bimodules, and all four maps of
$$0\to R(-1)\xrightarrow{\;1\mapsto\delta u+w_0\;}B_s \xrightarrow{\;f\otimes g\mapsto f s(g)\;}R_s(1)\to0,$$
$$0\to R_s(-1)\xrightarrow{\;1\mapsto\delta u-w_0\;}B_s \xrightarrow{\;f\otimes g\mapsto fg\;}R(1)\to0$$
are degree zero: sources and targets are generated in degree whose shifts match
the degree-$1$ elements $\delta u\pm w_0\in B_s$. Finally the two quotients are
the image of $u$, a free rank-one $R$-module with right action
$u\cdot g\equiv gu$ modulo $\delta u-w_0$ and $u\cdot g\equiv s(g)u$ modulo
$\delta u+w_0$ respectively, that is $R(1)$ and $R_s(1)$.

**(d) Imported statements of Soergel, recorded for the later items.** The later
items of this page cite the following results of Soergel's
*Kazhdan–Lusztig-Polynome und unzerlegbare Bimoduln* through this definition;
we record their content here, in the notation fixed above, so that every later
citation has a textual anchor in this item. Let $\mathcal B$ be Soergel's category of special bimodules (Definition 5.11).
The concrete criterion of Lemma 5.13 is that $B\in\mathcal B$ precisely when
$B\oplus C\cong D$ for finite sums $C,D$ of shifted Bott–Samelson products.
It does not say that $B$ itself is such a sum. Let $\operatorname{add}\mathcal B$
be the category of direct summands of special bimodules (Bemerkung 6.12).
Since every special bimodule is a summand of such a $D$, and every word is
special, this is exactly the idempotent completion defining $\mathrm{SBim}_n$.

1. *Closure under tensoring with a generator.* (Proposition 5.7(1) and
   Proposition 5.9(1).) If $M\in F_\Delta$ then $R\otimes_{R^s}M\in F_\Delta$,
   and if $M\in F_\nabla$ then $R\otimes_{R^s}M\in F_\nabla$; in both cases the
   quotients of the resulting flag are again standard bimodules, indexed by the
   quotients of the flag of $M$. Moreover (the proofs of the same two
   propositions) the multiplicities of the new flag are given, for
   $\ell(x)>\ell(sx)$, by the recursions
   $$(B_s\otimes_RM:\Delta_x(d))=(M:\Delta_x(d+1))+(M:\Delta_{sx}(d)),\qquad (B_s\otimes_RM:\Delta_{sx}(d))=(M:\Delta_x(d))+(M:\Delta_{sx}(d-1)),$$
   and dually
   $$(B_s\otimes_RM:\nabla_x(d))=(M:\nabla_x(d-1))+(M:\nabla_{sx}(d)),\qquad (B_s\otimes_RM:\nabla_{sx}(d))=(M:\nabla_x(d))+(M:\nabla_{sx}(d+1)).$$
   In Notation 5.6 the source uses the shifted reflection functor
   $\theta_s=R[1]\otimes_{R^s}(-)=B_s\otimes_R-$, with $[1]=(1)=\{-1\}$.
   Read in the other direction, the two
   recursions say that a flag quotient of $M$ with graph $y$ and index $e$
   produces the two quotients with graphs $sy$ and $y$ of $B_s\otimes_RM$: the
   quotient with graph $sy$ carries the same index $e$, while the index of the
   quotient with graph $y$ changes by $-1$ in the $\Delta$-chart when
   $\ell(y)>\ell(sy)$ and by $+1$ when $\ell(sy)>\ell(y)$, the two signs being
   exchanged in the $\nabla$-chart. The two pairs match the two Hecke expansions
   of $(\widetilde T_s+v)h_\Delta(M)$ and $(\widetilde T_s+v)h_\nabla(M)$ in
   the basis $\{\widetilde T_x\}$, which is the source's own interpretation of
   the recursions.
2. *Stability of the flag categories.* (Bemerkung 5.5.) The layers
   $\Gamma_{\ge i}M/\Gamma_{\ge i+1}M$ are additive in $M$, so $F_\Delta$ is
   stable under finite direct sums and, by the graded Krull–Schmidt theorem,
   under direct summands as well; the same holds for $F_\nabla$ with the
   filtration $\Gamma_{\le i}$.
3. *Localization at a reflection.* (Notation 6.9 and Lemma 6.10.) For a
   reflection $t$ let $R_{(t)}$ be the localization of $R$ at the homogeneous
   functions that do not vanish identically on the reflection hyperplane
   $V^t$. For every special bimodule $B$ its localization
   $B\otimes_RR_{(t)}$ is a direct summand of a finite direct sum of
   graded shifts of $R_y\otimes_RR_{(t)}$ and $R_{y,yt}\otimes_RR_{(t)}$,
   for $y<yt$. Here
   $R_{y,yt}:=(R\otimes_kR)/(I(Gr(y))\cap I(Gr(yt)))$ is the coordinate
   bimodule of the union of the two graphs. The two-graph module generally
   does not split into its two graph modules over $R_{(t)}$: their common
   reflection equation has not been inverted. After additionally inverting
   that equation, the two graphs separate and the two-graph piece splits.

4. *Sections have graph support, and the four layers.* (Bemerkung 6.2.) Let a
   graded bimodule carry a finite filtration by sub-bimodules whose successive
   quotients are single graphs $Gr(x)$. For any section $m$, filter its cyclic
   $(R\otimes_kR)$-submodule $C=(R\otimes_kR)m$ by intersecting with the given
   filtration. Each successive quotient of this induced filtration embeds in a
   single-graph module. Every nonzero submodule of a single-graph module has
   that same support, since its coordinate ring is the domain $R$; a zero
   quotient has empty support. Support is the union of the supports of the
   successive quotients in a finite filtration, so
   $\operatorname{supp}(m)=\operatorname{supp}(C)$ is a union of graphs
   $Gr(x)$ with $x\in S_n$. Consequently
   $\Gamma_yB\cap\Gamma_{\ne y}B=0$, so the evident maps
   $\Gamma_yB\to\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}B$ and
   $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}B\to\Gamma^yB$ (the second is the composite of the
   quotient map onto $\Gamma_{\ge y}B/\Gamma_{>y}B$ with the inclusion of that
   quotient in $B/\Gamma_{\ne y}B$, which is defined because
   $\Gamma_{>y}B\subseteq\Gamma_{\ne y}B$) are injective, and
   $\Gamma_yB=\Gamma_{\le y}B\cap\Gamma_{\ge y}B$. Moreover right multiplication
   by a nonzero $p\in R$ does not change supports, $(\Gamma_AB)p=\Gamma_A(Bp)$,
   so the localizations below may be formed layer by layer.
5. *Top-layer localization.* (Notation 6.5 and Satz 6.6, extended in
   Lemma 6.13.) For $y\in S_n$ put
   $p_y:=\prod_{t:\,yt<y}c_t$, the product over the finitely many
   reflections $t=(a\,b)$ with $a<b$, using the fixed hyperplane equations
   $c_t=x_a-x_b$ of [[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]]. For $B\in\mathcal B$ the natural morphisms
   induce isomorphisms $\Gamma_yB\to(\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}B)p_y$ and
   $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}B\to(\Gamma^yB)p_y$, where
   $\Gamma^yB=B/\Gamma_{\ne y}B$ is the $y$-layer quotient of Notation 6.1; the
   first paragraph of the proof of Lemma 6.13 observes that the isomorphism
   $\Gamma_yN\cong(\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}N)p_y$ of Satz 6.6 holds for all
   $N\in\operatorname{add}\mathcal B$ as well, and the same holds for the second
   isomorphism, so both are available for every $N\in\operatorname{add}
   \mathcal B$.
6. *Hom formula for the additive closure.* (Theorem 5.15, Lemma 6.13 and
   Satz 6.14(4).) For $M\in F_\Delta$ and $N\in\operatorname{add}\mathcal B$,
   and likewise for $M\in\operatorname{add}\mathcal B$ and $N\in F_\nabla$, the
   graded module $\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}(M,N)$ is free with
   $$\operatorname{rk}\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}(M,N) =\sum_{x,d,e}(M:\Delta_x(d))(N:\nabla_x(e))v^{d-e},$$
   and $\operatorname{add}\mathcal B=\mathcal B$ by Satz 6.14(4), where
   $\mathcal B$ has the stable-sum meaning just specified. For the first
   step of Lemma 6.13, use item 5 and item 7: the canonical lower layer of
   $N$ is $\bigoplus_\mu\nabla_y(\mu)^{\oplus(N:\nabla_y(\mu))}$,
   and its submodule obtained by multiplication by $p_y$ is $\Gamma_yN$.
   Since $\deg p_y=2\ell(y)$, its graded rank in our convention is
   $\sum_\mu(N:\nabla_y(\mu))v^{\ell(y)-\mu}$.
   Item 8 identifies this with $\operatorname{Hom}(R_y,N)$; replacing
   $R_y$ by $\Delta_y=R_y(-\ell(y))$ subtracts $\ell(y)$ from map degrees,
   giving $\sum_\mu(N:\nabla_y(\mu))v^{-\mu}$, as required.
   In general one takes $x$ of maximal length with $(M:\Delta_x(d))\neq0$ for
   some $d$; then $\Gamma_xM\hookrightarrow M\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{coker}$
   is a short exact sequence with both outer terms in $F_\Delta$ and
   $(M:\Delta_y(d))=(\Gamma_xM:\Delta_y(d))+(\operatorname{coker}:\Delta_y(d))$,
   and Theorem 5.15 forces the sequence
   $\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{coker},N)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(M,N)\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma_xM,N)$
   to be exact for every $N\in\mathcal B$; being additive in $N$, it stays
   exact for every $N\in\operatorname{add}\mathcal B$, which closes the
   induction over the length of a $\Delta$-flag of $M$. The second case is
   analogous, using the dual maximality argument over a $\nabla$-flag of $N$.
7. *Bruhat-refining enumerations.* (Lemma 6.3 and its proof.) Let $B\in F_\nabla$
   and let $y_0,y_1,\ldots$ be an enumeration of $S_n$ in which Bruhat-larger
   elements have larger index; put $C(k)=\{y_0,\ldots,y_k\}$ and $y_k=y$. Then
   the evident map $\Gamma_{\le y}B/\Gamma_{<y}B\to\Gamma_{C(k)}B/\Gamma_{C(k-1)}B$
   is an isomorphism, both sides are finite direct sums of objects of the form
   $\nabla_y(\mu)$, and $\nabla_y(\mu)$ occurs in this quotient exactly
   $(B:\nabla_y(\mu))$ times as a direct summand; the analogous statement holds
   for $F_\Delta$ after reversing the enumeration and replacing the lower
   layer by $\Gamma_{\ge y}B/\Gamma_{>y}B$: for a descending Bruhat
   enumeration $z_0,z_1,\ldots$, $C'(k)=\{z_0,\ldots,z_k\}$ and $z_k=y$,
   this upper layer maps isomorphically to
   $\Gamma_{C'(k)}B/\Gamma_{C'(k-1)}B$ and is a direct sum of
   $\Delta_y(\mu)$ with multiplicities $(B:\Delta_y(\mu))$.
   The proof compares two
   such enumerations through the finitely many steps that swap two adjacent
   incomparable elements: incomparable elements differ by no reflection, so by
   the extension-vanishing input there is no $\mathrm{Ext}^1$ between the
   corresponding subquotients, and the two filtrations have the same
   subquotients up to order.
8. *Freeness over the invariant ring of a graph.* (Proposition 6.4.) For
   $B\in\mathcal B$ and $y\in S_n$ the graded modules
   $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}B$, $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}B$, $\Gamma^yB$ and
   $\Gamma_yB$ are free graded right $R$-modules on which the action of
   $R\otimes_kR$ factors through the graph ring $R_y$; in particular evaluation
   at the generator gives an isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}(R_y,B)\cong\Gamma_yB$,
   and $B\otimes_RR_x\in F_\Delta$ for every $x\in S_n$.

**(e) Opposite bimodules.** For a graded $(R,R)$-bimodule $M$ let
$M^{\mathrm{op}}$ be the graded $(R,R)$-bimodule with the same underlying
graded $k$-module and operations $r\cdot^{\mathrm{op}}m\cdot^{\mathrm{op}}s=s\,m\,r$;
since $R$ is commutative this is again a graded $(R,R)$-bimodule, and the
following four remarks are used by the later support-filtration items.

1. The flip $m\otimes n\mapsto n\otimes m$ is a well-defined degree-zero
   bijection $(M\otimes_RN)^{\mathrm{op}}\to N^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_RM^{\mathrm{op}}$,
   because $mr\otimes n=m\otimes rn$ maps to
   $n\otimes mr=(n\cdot^{\mathrm{op}}r)\otimes m=rn\otimes m$ in
   $N^{\mathrm{op}}\otimes_RM^{\mathrm{op}}$.
2. $R_x^{\mathrm{op}}\cong R_{x^{-1}}$ through $m\mapsto x^{-1}(m)$, and
   $B_s^{\mathrm{op}}\cong B_s$ through the flip $f\otimes g\mapsto g\otimes f$;
   both are degree-zero isomorphisms of graded $(R,R)$-bimodules.
3. Write $\tau$ for the flip of the two factors of $V\times V$. A nonzero
   element $m$ of a standard bimodule has support exactly the single graph of
   its standard, since the annihilator of the generator of $R_w$ is the prime
   ideal $I(Gr(w))$; for the same reason
   $\operatorname{Ann}_{M^{\mathrm{op}}}(m)=\sigma(\operatorname{Ann}_M(m))$ under
   the flip $\sigma(f\otimes g)=g\otimes f$, so
   $\operatorname{supp}_{M^{\mathrm{op}}}(m)=\tau(\operatorname{supp}_M(m))$.
   Since $\tau(Gr(x))=Gr(x^{-1})$ and $\ell(x^{-1})=\ell(x)$, the length
   filtrations satisfy $\Gamma_{\ge i}(M^{\mathrm{op}})=\Gamma_{\ge i}M$ and
   $\Gamma_{\le i}(M^{\mathrm{op}})=\Gamma_{\le i}M$ as $k$-submodules.
4. Consequently a $\Delta$-flag of $M$ with quotients $R_{x_j}\{a_j\}$ is a
   $\Delta$-flag of $M^{\mathrm{op}}$ with quotients $R_{x_j^{-1}}\{a_j\}$ in
   the same order, and a $\nabla$-flag of $M$ is a $\nabla$-flag of
   $M^{\mathrm{op}}$ in the same order: the graph index inverts, the length is
   unchanged by item 3, and the refined length filtration is preserved by the
   opposite functor, so this functor preserves *each* of the two flag
   categories, $M\in F_\Delta\iff M^{\mathrm{op}}\in F_\Delta$ and
   $M\in F_\nabla\iff M^{\mathrm{op}}\in F_\nabla$.
