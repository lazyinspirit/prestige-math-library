## Definition

**Graphs and supports.** Keep $k=\mathbb Q$, $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with
$\deg x_i=2$, and the affine scheme $V=\mathbb A^n_k=\operatorname{Spec}R$,
whose $k$-points are $V(k)=k^n$. Use the place-permuting action of $S_n$
from [[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]].
For $w\in S_n$ let
$$Gr(w):=\operatorname{Graph}(w:V\to V)\subseteq V\times_kV,\quad Gr(w)(k)=\{(w\lambda,\lambda):\lambda\in k^n\},\qquad Gr(A):=\bigcup_{w\in A}Gr(w)\ \ (A\subseteq S_n),$$
so that $V\times_kV=\operatorname{Spec}(R\otimes_kR)$ and $Gr(w)$ is the graph
of the map $\lambda\mapsto w\lambda$. A graded $(R,R)$-bimodule $M$ is the
same thing as a graded module over $R\otimes_kR$, and the support of $m\in M$ is
the zero set $\operatorname{supp}(m):=V(\operatorname{Ann}(m))\subseteq V\times V$
of its annihilator ideal in $R\otimes_kR$. For $A\subseteq S_n$ put
$$\Gamma_AM:=\{m\in M:\operatorname{supp}(m)\subseteq Gr(A)\},$$
a graded sub-bimodule of $M$. Since $Gr(A)$ is closed with radical ideal
$I(Gr(A))=\bigcap_{w\in A}I(Gr(w))$ (an intersection of prime ideals, since each
$Gr(w)$ is a linear subspace of $V\times V$ complementing the second factor), an
element $m$ lies in $\Gamma_AM$ exactly when $I(Gr(A))^N m=0$ for some $N\ge0$:
this is the Nullstellensatz together with the Noetherianity of
$R\otimes_kR$. The ideal itself need not annihilate such an element — in
$(R\otimes_kR)/I(Gr(e))^2$ the class of $1$ is supported on $Gr(e)$ but is not
killed by $I(Gr(e))$ — which is why the criterion is stated with a power of the
ideal. For $i\in\mathbb Z$ we write
$$\Gamma_{\ge i}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\ell(x)\ge i\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{\le i}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\ell(x)\le i\}}M,$$
using the length function of [[lem-type-a-reduced-words-are-connected-by-braid-moves]],
while for $y\in S_n$ the $y$-indexed symbols denote the *Bruhat
principal-set* cutoffs of Soergel's Notation 6.1,
$$\Gamma_{\le y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x\le y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{<y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x<y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{\ge y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x\ge y\}}M,\qquad \Gamma_{>y}M:=\Gamma_{\{x:\,x>y\}}M,$$
with $\Gamma_yM:=\Gamma_{\{y\}}M$ the elements concentrated on the single graph
$Gr(y)$ and $\Gamma_{\ne y}M:=\Gamma_{S_n\setminus\{y\}}M$ the elements
concentrated off $Gr(y)$. The integer-indexed symbols of the previous display are
the *length* cutoffs used to define the flags below, and they must not be
confused with the element-indexed Bruhat cutoffs: for $y=s_1$ in $S_4$ the
submodule $\Gamma_{\le1}M$ contains $Gr(s_3)$ while
$\Gamma_{\le y}M=\Gamma_{\{e,s_1\}}M$ does not, and the two cutoffs agree at
the extremes, $\Gamma_{\le w_0}=\Gamma_{\le\ell(w_0)}=M$ and
$\Gamma_{<e}=\Gamma_{\le-1}=0$. So
$\Gamma^yM:=M/\Gamma_{\ne y}M$ is Soergel's $y$-layer
*quotient*; following Soergel's
Notation 6.1 we write the two $y$-layer quotients with an underline,
$$\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M:=\Gamma_{\le y}M/\Gamma_{<y}M,\qquad \underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M:=\Gamma_{\ge y}M/\Gamma_{>y}M,$$
so that $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ is the quotient of the lower support
submodule by $\Gamma_{<y}M$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M$ the quotient of
the upper support submodule by the part supported strictly above $Gr(y)$.

**Standard bimodules.** For $w\in S_n$ let $R_w$ be the graded $(R,R)$-bimodule
which equals $R$ as a graded $k$-vector space and has
$$f\cdot x=fx,\qquad x\cdot g=w(g)\,x\qquad(f,g,x\in R),$$
where $w(g)(\lambda):=g(w^{-1}\lambda)$; $R_w$ is generated by the element $1$ of
degree $0$. Write $(k)$ for the *external* shift $M(k):=M\{-k\}$ of
[[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]], so that
$M(k)_d=M_{d+k}$; this is the shift convention of the type-A sources, and it is
the one in which $B_i=R\otimes_{R^{s_i}}R(1)$ of
[[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]] has $1\otimes1$ of
degree $-1$. Put
$$\Delta_w(d):=R_w(d-\ell(w)),\qquad \nabla_w(d):=R_w(d+\ell(w))\qquad(d\in\mathbb Z),$$
so that $\Delta_w(d)$ is the standard bimodule $R_w$ with its generator in
degree $\ell(w)-d$, and $\nabla_w(d)$ is $R_w$ with its generator in degree
$-\ell(w)-d$. Thus $\Delta_w(0)=R_w\{\ell(w)\}$,
$\nabla_w(0)=R_w\{-\ell(w)\}$ and $\Delta_e(d)=\nabla_e(d)=R(d)$ is generated
in degree $-d$; in particular $\Delta_e(0)=\nabla_e(0)=R_e=R$.

For graded free $R$-modules in this type-A page, a homogeneous free generator of degree $a$ contributes $v^a$ to graded rank. This is the $v\mapsto v^{-1}$ transform of Soergel's rank convention in his Notation 5.2; all rank formulas below use this convention.

**Support filtrations.** A $\Delta$-flag of a graded $R$-bimodule $M$ is a
finite chain $0=M_0\subseteq M_1\subseteq\cdots\subseteq M_k=M$ of graded
sub-bimodules which refines the length filtration
$$M=\Gamma_{\ge0}M\supseteq\Gamma_{\ge1}M\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\Gamma_{\ge\ell(w_0)+1}M=0$$
of $M$ (here $\Gamma_{\ge0}M=M$ for every bimodule supported on
$Gr(S_n)=\bigcup_{w}Gr(w)$, which all bimodules of this page are, and
$\Gamma_{\ge i}M=0$ for $i>\ell(w_0)$ because no graph $Gr(x)$ with
$\ell(x)>i$ remains) and whose successive quotients are
standard bimodules $M_j/M_{j-1}\cong R_{x_j}\{a_j\}$. We require each
length layer to be a finite direct sum of shifted graph bimodules of that
length, as in Soergel's Definition 5.4. Thus the
quotients carry a numbering with $\ell(x_1)\ge\cdots\ge\ell(x_k)$ and each
$R_{x_j}\{a_j\}$ is one graph, so that $M$ has a "support flag" in the sense of
Soergel's $F_\Delta$. A $\nabla$-flag has the same direct-sum condition on its length layers, with the reverse length
filtration $\Gamma_{\le i}M$ and $\ell(x_1)\le\cdots\le\ell(x_k)$. The
refinement condition is a condition on supports, not an ordering of incomparable
quotients of equal length; only the lengths are ordered. For $i=\ell(x)$ the
graded multiplicity is
$$(M:\Delta_x(d)):=\text{multiplicity of }\Delta_x(d)\text{ in } \Gamma_{\ge i}M/\Gamma_{\ge i+1}M,$$
and, for a $\nabla$-flag, $(M:\nabla_x(d))$ is the multiplicity of
$\nabla_x(d)$ in $\Gamma_{\le i}M/\Gamma_{\le i-1}M$; a priori both symbols
refer to a chosen compatible enumeration (the next items on this page prove that
they do not depend on it). With $T_x$ the Hecke basis of
[[lem-type-a-hecke-standard-basis-for-soergel-comparison]] and
$\widetilde T_x:=v^{\ell(x)}T_x$, the character sums attached to a flag are
$$h_\Delta(M):=\sum_{x,d}(M:\Delta_x(d))\,v^d\,\widetilde T_x,\qquad h_\nabla(M):=\sum_{x,d}(M:\nabla_x(d))\,v^{-d}\,\widetilde T_x,$$
and they are used only through the items that prove these sums intrinsic.

**Small $n$.** For $n\le1$ there is a single element $e$ and every object is a finite direct sum of shifts $\bigoplus_j R(d_j)$. Its one-graph flags have these summands as quotients, so $h_\Delta=\sum_jv^{d_j}\widetilde T_e$ and $h_\nabla=\sum_jv^{-d_j}\widetilde T_e$; in particular both characters send the unit $R$ to $1$.

## Remark

**(a) Maps between standard bimodules.** For $v,w\in S_n$ and $a,b\in\mathbb Z$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}\bigl(R_v(a),R_w(b)\bigr)\cong \begin{cases}R(b-a),&v=w,\\ 0,&v\ne w,\end{cases}$$
where $R(c)$ denotes the free graded $R$-module on one generator of degree $-c$.
Indeed a bimodule map is determined by $\varphi(1)=h\in R$, and $f\cdot1\cdot g\mapsto
f\,h\,w(g)$ must equal $v(g)\,f\,h$; since $R$ is a domain this forces
$v(g)h=w(g)h$ for all $g$, hence $h=0$ or $v=w$. The generator $1$ of $R_v(a)$
has degree $-a$ and maps to $h$, which has degree $\deg h-b$ as an element of
$R_w(b)$, so the homogeneous map $\varphi_h$ has degree $\deg h+a-b$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}(R_v(a),R_w(b))\cong R(b-a)$ in the case $v=w$.

**(b) Composition.** The $R$-balanced assignment
$$R_w\otimes_RR_v\longrightarrow R_{wv},\qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto a\,w(b),$$
where $w(b)(\lambda)=b(w^{-1}\lambda)$ and the product is taken in $R$, is a
well-defined degree-zero isomorphism of graded $(R,R)$-bimodules. It is well
defined because for $f\in R$ the relation $(a\cdot f)\otimes b=a\otimes(fb)$
reads $w(f)a\otimes b=a\otimes fb$ and maps to $w(f)\,a\,w(b)=a\,w(f)w(b)$ on
both sides; it is left $R$-linear, and it respects the right actions because
$w(bh)=w(b)w(h)$ for $h\in R$. It sends the $1$-tensor $1\otimes1$ of degree $0$
to $1$ and is therefore surjective, and both sides are free of rank one as left
$R$-modules, so it is an isomorphism. In particular
$R_w\otimes_RR_v\cong R_{wv}$ (and $R_w\otimes_RR_v\cong R_w$ only when $v=e$),
while tensoring with $w=e$ shows that the assignment is compatible with the
unit. The single assignment $1\otimes1\mapsto1$ does extend uniquely to a bimodule
map, and the map it extends to is exactly the twisted one above; what fails is
the untwisted formula $a\otimes b\mapsto ab$, which is not balanced when $w\ne e$: the equality $(1\cdot f)\otimes1=1\otimes f$ in $R_w\otimes_RR_v$ would map to $w(f)$ on the left and $f$ on the right, and these differ for a polynomial moved by $w$.

**(c) The rank-one calculus.** Let $s=s_i$, $R^s=R^{s_i}$, $\alpha=\alpha_i$,
$\delta:=\alpha/2$, and $B_s=R\otimes_{R^s}R(1)$ with the images
$u:=1\otimes1$ of degree $-1$ and $w_0:=1\otimes\delta$ of degree $1$
([[def-type-a-soergel-bimodule-for-a-simple-reflection]]); these two elements
form a graded left $R$-basis of $B_s$, and every $g\in R$ has a unique
decomposition $g=g^++\delta g^-$ with $g^\pm\in R^s$, given by
$g^+=\tfrac12(g+s(g))$. The right action on the basis is
$$u\cdot g=g^+u+g^-w_0,\qquad w_0\cdot g=\delta^2g^-u+g^+w_0,$$
as follows from $1\otimes g=1\otimes(g^++\delta g^-)=g^+u+g^-w_0$ and
$\delta g=\delta g^++\delta^2g^-$ with $\delta^2=\alpha^2/4\in R^s$. Hence
$$(\delta u+w_0)\cdot g=g\,(\delta u+w_0),\qquad (\delta u-w_0)\cdot g=s(g)(\delta u-w_0),$$
so $R(\delta u+w_0)\cong R(-1)$ and $R(\delta u-w_0)\cong R_s(-1)$ are
sub-bimodules of $B_s$, generated in degree $1$. Moreover for
$f,h\in R$ one has $(fu+hw_0)\mapsto f+h\delta$ under $f\otimes g\mapsto fg$
and $(fu+hw_0)\mapsto f-h\delta$ under $f\otimes g\mapsto f s(g)$, because
$1\otimes g=g^+u+g^-w_0$ and $s(g)=g^+-\delta g^-$; both maps are surjective,
their kernels are the two displayed sub-bimodules, and all four maps of
$$0\to R(-1)\xrightarrow{\;1\mapsto\delta u+w_0\;}B_s \xrightarrow{\;f\otimes g\mapsto f s(g)\;}R_s(1)\to0,$$
$$0\to R_s(-1)\xrightarrow{\;1\mapsto\delta u-w_0\;}B_s \xrightarrow{\;f\otimes g\mapsto fg\;}R(1)\to0$$
are degree zero: sources and targets are generated in degree whose shifts match
the degree-$1$ elements $\delta u\pm w_0\in B_s$. Finally the two quotients are
the image of $u$, a free rank-one $R$-module with right action
$u\cdot g\equiv gu$ modulo $\delta u-w_0$ and $u\cdot g\equiv s(g)u$ modulo
$\delta u+w_0$ respectively, that is $R(1)$ and $R_s(1)$.
