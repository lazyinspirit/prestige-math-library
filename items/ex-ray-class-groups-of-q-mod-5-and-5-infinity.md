## Example

Let $\mathfrak m_5=((5),\varnothing)$ and $\mathfrak m_{5\infty}=((5),\{\sigma_\infty\})$ for $\mathbb Q$, where $\sigma_\infty$ is its usual real embedding. Then $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m_5}(\mathbb Q)\cong C_2$ and $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m_{5\infty}}(\mathbb Q)\cong C_4$. In the first group the class of $(2)$ is the nonidentity element; in the second it is a generator.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two moduli in the Example and the exact ray-class sequence.

[F1] The ring of integers of $\mathbb Q$ is $\mathbb Z$. ([[cor-rational-algebraic-integers-are-integers]], [[def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]])

[F2] A fractional ideal becomes an integral ideal after multiplication by a nonzero rational; the ordinary class group is the quotient of fractional ideals by principal ideals. ([[def-fractional-ideal]], [[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]], [[lem-ideal-class-group-well-defined]])

[F3] The integers are a totally ordered ring; the units are those elements with multiplicative inverses. ([[def-integers]], [[lem-ring-units-form-a-group]])

[F4] The group $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times=\{1,2,3,4\}$ is cyclic generated by $2$, since $2^2=4\equiv-1$ and $2^4\equiv1\pmod5$. ([[def-integers]])

[F5] The exact sequence has residue/sign group $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times$ for $\mathfrak m_5$, and $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times\times\{\pm1\}$ for $\mathfrak m_{5\infty}$; the unit images are the residue and sign pairs from [F3]. ([[thm-ray-class-exact-sequence]])

[F6] Every nonempty set of positive integers has a least element, and every real number has an integer part $m$ with $m\le x<m+1$. ([[thm-well-ordering-principle]], [[lem-integer-part]])

## Proof


**Given:** The data in the Example.

1.1 By [F1], $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Z$. Let $I$ be a nonzero integral ideal of $\mathbb Z$ and choose its least positive element $n$ by [F6]. For any $a\in I$, put $q=\lfloor a/n\rfloor$ and $r=a-qn$. The integer-part property gives $0\le r<n$; since $r\in I$, minimality of $n$ forces $r=0$. Thus every element of $I$ is divisible by $n$, while $n\mathbb Z\subseteq I$, so $I=n\mathbb Z$. [F1, F6, given, choose]

1.2 If $u\in\mathbb Z^\times$, there is $v\in\mathbb Z$ with $uv=1$, so $u$ divides $1$ and therefore $u=1$ or $u=-1$; both are units. Their residues modulo $5$ are $1$ and $4$, and their real signs are $+$ and $-$. [F3, algebra]

2.1 If $J$ is a nonzero fractional ideal of $\mathbb Z$, choose a nonzero rational $a/b$ with $(a/b)J$ an integral ideal, where $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ are nonzero. Then $aJ=b((a/b)J)$ is also an integral ideal, so by step 1.1 it is $n\mathbb Z$ for some $n\ne0$. Hence $J=(n/a)\mathbb Z$ is principal. The ordinary ideal class group is therefore trivial. [F2, step 1.1, algebra, choose]

3.1 By [F5], the ray class map from the residue group onto $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m_5}(\mathbb Q)$ is surjective with kernel the image $\{1,4\}$ of the units, since the ordinary class group is trivial by step 2.1. By [F4] the quotient $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times/\{1,4\}$ has two elements; the class of $2$ is not in the unit image and is its nonidentity element. [F4, F5, step 2.1]

3.2 For $\mathfrak m_{5\infty}$, the unit image in [F5] is the diagonal subgroup $\{(1,+),(4,-)\}$ of the group of eight residue/sign pairs. Since the ordinary class group is trivial, exactness makes the ray class map onto with this kernel, so the quotient has four elements. [F3, F5, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 The pair $(2,+)$ is the image of the principal ideal $(2)$ because $2$ is positive and has residue $2$ modulo $5$. Its square is $(4,+)$, which is not in $\{(1,+),(4,-)\}$, while its fourth power is $(1,+)$. Therefore its coset has order exactly four and generates the quotient from step 3.2. [F4, F5, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
