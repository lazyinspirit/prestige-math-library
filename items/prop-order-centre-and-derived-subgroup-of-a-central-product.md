## Statement

Let $G$ and $H$ be groups with central subgroups $Z_1\le Z(G)$ and $Z_2\le Z(H)$
and an isomorphism $\alpha:Z_1\to Z_2$, and write $\bar g,\bar h$ for the
canonical images in $P=G\circ_\alpha H$. Then:

1. if $G$ and $H$ are finite, $\lvert P\rvert=\dfrac{\lvert G\rvert\,\lvert H\rvert}{\lvert Z_1\rvert}$;
2. $Z(P)=\{\bar g\bar h:g\in Z(G),\ h\in Z(H)\}$, the image of $Z(G)\times Z(H)$;
3. $[P,P]=\{\overline{c}\,\overline{d}:c\in[G,G],\ d\in[H,H]\}$, the image of $[G,G]\times[H,H]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Groups $G,H$, central subgroups $Z_1\le Z(G)$ and $Z_2\le Z(H)$, an isomorphism $\alpha:Z_1\to Z_2$, the quotient map $\pi:G\times H\to P$, and the canonical images $\bar g=\pi(g,e)$, $\bar h=\pi(e,h)$.

[F1] For groups $G,H$ with central subgroups $Z_1\le Z(G)$, $Z_2\le Z(H)$ and an isomorphism $\alpha:Z_1\to Z_2$, the central product $G\circ_\alpha H$ is the quotient of $G\times H$ by $N=\{(z,\alpha(z)^{-1}):z\in Z_1\}$ ([[def-central-product-of-two-groups-along-an-isomorphism-of-central-subgroups]]).

[F2] $Z(G):=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$ ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F3] For $g,h\in G$ the commutator is $[g,h]:=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[L1] The canonical maps $G\to G\circ_\alpha H$ and $H\to G\circ_\alpha H$ are injective homomorphisms whose images commute elementwise, generate $G\circ_\alpha H$, and meet in the image of $Z_1$ ([[prop-the-canonical-maps-into-a-central-product-are-injective-with-commuting-images]]).

[L2] If $G$ and $H$ are finite groups, then their external direct product is finite and has order $|G\times H|=|G|\,|H|$ ([[prop-order-of-finite-direct-product]]).

[L3] For a finite group $G$ and $H\le G$, $|G|=[G:H]\,|H|$ ([[thm-lagrange]]).

[L4] $\langle S\rangle$ is the smallest subgroup containing $S$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[L5] The subgroup $N=\{(z,\alpha(z)^{-1}):z\in Z_1\}$ of $G\times H$ is central, hence normal ([[lem-the-antidiagonal-subgroup-of-a-central-product-is-a-central-normal-subgroup]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The map $z\mapsto(z,\alpha(z)^{-1})$ is a bijection from $Z_1$ onto $N$, so $\lvert N\rvert=\lvert Z_1\rvert$; with $\lvert G\times H\rvert=\lvert G\rvert\lvert H\rvert$ and Lagrange applied to $N\le G\times H$, the number of cosets is $\lvert G\rvert\lvert H\rvert/\lvert Z_1\rvert$, which is the order of $P$. [F1, L2, L3, L5, algebra]

1.2 The images of $G$ and of $H$ in $P$ commute elementwise, generate $P$, and each canonical map is injective. [L1]

2.1 Let $\bar g\bar h$ be central in $P$ and let $g'\in G$. Then $\bar h$ commutes with $\overline{g'}$, so $\bar g$ commutes with $\overline{g'}$, hence $\overline{[g,g']}=\overline{g}\,\overline{g'}\,\overline{g}^{-1}\overline{g'}^{-1}$ is trivial; injectivity of the canonical map gives $[g,g']=e$, so $g\in Z(G)$, and symmetrically $h\in Z(H)$. [F2, F3, step 1.2]

2.2 Conversely, if $g\in Z(G)$ and $h\in Z(H)$ then $\bar g\bar h$ commutes with every $\overline{g'}$ and with every $\overline{h'}$, and those elements generate $P$, so $\bar g\bar h$ is central. [F2, step 1.2]

2.3 Because the two images commute elementwise, $[\bar g_1\bar h_1,\bar g_2\bar h_2]=[\bar g_1,\bar g_2][\bar h_1,\bar h_2]=\overline{[g_1,g_2]}\ \overline{[h_1,h_2]}$ for all $g_i\in G$ and $h_i\in H$. [F3, step 1.2]

3.1 Every element of $P$ is $\bar g\bar h$ by step 1.2, so steps 2.1 and 2.2 identify $Z(P)$ as the image of $Z(G)\times Z(H)$; and by step 2.3 the commutators of $P$ are exactly the products $\overline{[g_1,g_2]}\ \overline{[h_1,h_2]}$, whose generated subgroup is the image of $[G,G]\times[H,H]$. [F3, L4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3] ∎

## Remarks

Clause 1 needs both factors finite; clauses 2 and 3 do not, since they use only that the two images commute and generate. The order formula divides by $\lvert Z_1\rvert$ and not by $\lvert Z_1\rvert^2$: one copy of the identified subgroup survives inside the product, and it is the image described in the intersection clause of the canonical-maps proposition.
