## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$, $n\ge2$, with its diagonal
Cartan $\mathfrak h$, coordinate functionals $\varepsilon_i$ and
upper-triangular positive system, the adjoint representation has highest vector
$E_{1n}$ and highest weight $\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_n$, which is the highest
root
([[def-adjoint-representation-of-a-lie-algebra]],
[[def-height-of-a-root-and-highest-root]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, an integer $n\ge2$, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$ ([[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]]), its diagonal Cartan $\mathfrak h$, the coordinate functionals $\varepsilon_i(H)=H_{ii}$, the matrix units $E_{ij}$, the positive system $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ $(i<j)$ with base $\alpha_j=\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1}$ (verified in step 1.4), and the adjoint representation of $\mathfrak g$ on itself.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it covers the inherited highest-weight and root-order conventions ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The roots are $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$, $i\ne j$, with root spaces $\mathbb CE_{ij}$. This root and root-space description is supplied by [[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]]; the positive system is the choice in the Given, verified below.

[L2] The adjoint action is $[H,E_{ij}]=(H_{ii}-H_{jj})E_{ij}=(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j)(H)E_{ij}$ and $[E_{ij},E_{kl}]=\delta_{jk}E_{il}-\delta_{li}E_{kj}$. [given]

[L3] A positive system is specified by a regular vector, and its simple roots are its positive roots not expressible as a sum of two positive roots ([[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]]).

[L4] For $n\ge2$ the Killing form of $\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$ is $2n\operatorname{tr}(XY)$ and is nondegenerate ([[ex-classical-simple-lie-algebras-and-their-killing-forms]]); hence $\mathfrak g$ is semisimple by [[thm-cartans-semisimplicity-criterion]], as required by the highest-weight definition.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The vector $E_{1n}$ has weight $\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_n$: $[H,E_{1n}]=(H_{11}-H_{nn})E_{1n}$ by [L2]. [L2, A1]

1.2 $E_{1n}$ is killed by every positive root vector: for $i<j$ we have $[E_{ij},E_{1n}]=\delta_{j1}E_{in}-\delta_{in}E_{1j}$, and $\delta_{j1}=0$ because $i<j$ with $i\ge1$, while $\delta_{in}=0$ because $i<j\le n$ forces $i<n$; hence $[E_{ij},E_{1n}]=0$. [L1, L2]

1.3 The adjoint submodule generated by $E_{1n}$ is all of $\mathfrak{sl}_n$. It contains $H= [E_{n1},E_{1n}]=E_{nn}-E_{11}$; then $[E_{nk},H]$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $E_{nk}$ for every $k<n$. It also contains $E_{jn}=[E_{j1},E_{1n}]$ for $1<j<n$, as well as the original $E_{1n}$. Finally, $[E_{nk},E_{jn}]=\delta_{kj}E_{nn}-E_{jk}$ supplies every off-diagonal $E_{jk}$ with $j,k<n$ and every diagonal difference $E_{nn}-E_{jj}$. These matrices span $\mathfrak{sl}_n$. [L2, algebra]

1.4 In the Euclidean model of the roots take the traceless vector $v=(n-1,n-3,\ldots,1-n)$: $(v,\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j)=2(j-i)$, so it is regular and selects exactly $i<j$. Each positive root has the expansion $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j=\alpha_i+\cdots+\alpha_{j-1}$. The $n-1$ vectors $\alpha_j$ are independent: comparing successive coordinates in $\sum c_j(\varepsilon_j-\varepsilon_{j+1})=0$ gives every $c_j=0$. If $j>i+1$ the root splits at $i+1$ into two positive roots; an adjacent root cannot so split because the two nonempty interval expansions would have to sum to its single coefficient one. Thus these adjacent differences are exactly the simple roots. [L1, L3, given, algebra]

2.1 For every positive root $\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ with $i<j$, one has $$(\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_n)-(\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j) =(\alpha_1+\cdots+\alpha_{i-1})+(\alpha_j+\cdots+\alpha_{n-1}),$$ a nonnegative integral combination of simple roots. Thus $\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_n$ is the highest root. By steps 1.1–1.3, $E_{1n}$ has that weight, is killed by all positive root spaces, and generates the adjoint module, so it is a highest weight vector and the adjoint module has highest weight $\varepsilon_1-\varepsilon_n$. ([[def-height-of-a-root-and-highest-root]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, L4) ∎
