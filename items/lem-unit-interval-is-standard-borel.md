## Statement

Let $I=[0,1]$ with the subspace topology from the usual real topology and with its Borel sigma-algebra. Then $(I,\mathcal B(I))$ is a standard-Borel space ([[def-standard-borel-space]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The usual real metric $d(x,y)=|x-y|$ and the subspace $I=[0,1]$.

[F1] [[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]: the usual metric is $|x-y|$ and its open balls are $(x-r,x+r)$.

[F2] [[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]]: every Cauchy sequence in the usual real metric converges in $\mathbb R$.

[F3] [[thm-rationals-countable]]: $\mathbb Q$ is countably infinite, so there is a bijection from $\mathbb Q$ to $\mathbb N$.

[F4] [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]]: between any two distinct real numbers lies a rational.

[F5] [[lem-subset-of-countable]]: every subset of an at most countable set is at most countable; countable means finite or bijective with $\mathbb N$ ([[def-countable]], [[def-equinumerous]]).

[F6] [[thm-reals-ordered-field]]: the real order is total and translation preserves strict inequalities ([[def-real-order]], [[lem-of-add-order]]).

[F7] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets and is closed under complements.

[F8] [[thm-borel-sigma-algebra-of-a-subspace-is-the-trace]]: for a topological subspace, its Borel sigma-algebra is the trace of the ambient Borel sigma-algebra ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]).

[F9] [[def-polish-space]]: a space is Polish if it is separable and has a compatible complete metric; [[def-standard-borel-space]] identifies a measurable space with the Borel structure of a Polish presentation.

[F10] [[def-open-and-closed-in-r]]: a subset of $\mathbb R$ is closed when its complement is open.

[A1] The closed interval $I=[0,1]$ is $\{x\in\mathbb R:0\le x\le1\}$ ([[def-interval]]).

[A2] The topology on $I$ is the subspace topology inherited from $\mathbb R$ ([[def-subspace-topology-top]]).

[A3] The metric on $I$ is the restriction $d_I=d|_{I\times I}$ ([[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** $I=[0,1]$ with the usual subspace topology and Borel sigma-algebra.

1.1 The interval is closed in $\mathbb R$: for $z\notin I$, [F6] and [A1] give either $z<0$ or $z>1$. If $z<0$, set $r=-z>0$; then every $y\in B(z,r)$ satisfies $y<z+r=0$. If $z>1$, set $r=z-1>0$; then every $y\in B(z,r)$ satisfies $y>z-r=1$. By [F1] these are open neighborhoods disjoint from $I$, so its complement is open by [F10]. Thus $I$ is Borel by [F7]. [F1, F6, F7, F10, A1, algebra]

1.2 The set $D:=\mathbb Q\cap I$ is at most countable: restrict the bijection $\mathbb Q\to\mathbb N$ from [F3] to $D$, then apply [F5]. It is dense in $I$. Indeed, for $x\in I$ and $\varepsilon>0$, if $x=1$ choose $q=1$. If $x<1$, then $x+\varepsilon>x$; take $b=x+\varepsilon$ when $x+\varepsilon\le1$, and $b=1$ otherwise. In either case $b>x$ and $b-x\le\varepsilon$, with strict inequality in the second case. By [F4] choose a rational $q$ with $x<q<b$; then $q\in D$ and $|q-x|<\varepsilon$. The order comparisons use [F6] and the interval endpoints use [A1]. [F3, F4, F5, F6, A1, given, choose]

2.1 Let $(x_n)$ be a Cauchy sequence in $I$ under $d_I$. By [A2]–[A3], Cauchy distances are the same in $I$ and $\mathbb R$, so [F2] gives a real limit $x$. If $x\notin I$, step 1.1 gives an open ball about $x$ disjoint from $I$, while convergence puts all sufficiently late $x_n$ in that ball, a contradiction. Hence $x\in I$ and the sequence converges in the restricted metric; $(I,d_I)$ is complete. [A2, A3, F2, step 1.1, given]

3.1 By steps 1.2 and 2.1, $I$ is separable and completely metrizable, hence Polish by [F9]. Its Borel sigma-algebra is the trace of $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$ by [F8] and [A2]. The identity map from $(I,\mathcal B(\mathbb R)|_I)$ to the Polish subspace $I$ is therefore a bimeasurable bijection; [F9] makes $(I,\mathcal B(I))$ standard Borel. This proves the claim without any choice axiom. [A2, F8, F9, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
