## Statement

Let $\varphi:R\to S$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings, let $X$ be a set and let $R\langle X\rangle$ be the free $R$-algebra on $X$ ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]).

1. There is a unique $S$-algebra isomorphism $S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle\to S\langle X\rangle$ with $s\otimes x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n}\mapsto s\,x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n}$ on words; its inverse sends a word to $1\otimes$ that word.
2. If $I\subseteq R\langle X\rangle$ is a two-sided ideal, then there is a unique $S$-algebra isomorphism $S\otimes_R\bigl(R\langle X\rangle/I\bigr)\cong(S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle)/\operatorname{im}(S\otimes_RI)$, sending $s\otimes(y+I)$ to $(s\otimes y)+\operatorname{im}(S\otimes_RI)$, the image ideal being generated by the images of the relations. No flatness or freeness of $S$ over $R$ is assumed.
3. If an $R$-algebra $A$ is free as an $R$-module with basis $(a_i)$, then $S\otimes_RA$ is a free $S$-module with basis $(1\otimes a_i)$. Consequently an explicitly constructed basis isomorphism of a presentation may be tensored with $S$ to give a basis in every commutative specialization.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A homomorphism $\varphi:R\to S$ of commutative rings, a set $X$, and a two-sided ideal $I\subseteq R\langle X\rangle$.

[F1] $R\langle X\rangle$ is the free associative $R$-algebra on $X$: it is free as an $R$-module on the words in $X$, the product is concatenation, and every map $X\to A$ into a unital $R$-algebra $A$ extends uniquely to an $R$-algebra homomorphism $R\langle X\rangle\to A$ ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]).

[F2] Extension of scalars: $S$ is an $R$-module through $\varphi$ and also an $S$-module, so $S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$ is an $S$-module with $s'(s\otimes y)=s's\otimes y$, and balanced pairings induce linear maps out of tensor products ([[def-restriction-and-extension-of-scalars]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]).

[F3] For $R$-algebras the tensor product is an $R$-algebra with $(s\otimes y)(s'\otimes y')=ss'\otimes yy'$ and unit $1\otimes1$ ([[thm-tensor-product-of-algebras-over-a-commutative-ring]], [[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F4] Tensoring is right exact: the tensor of an exact sequence $A\to B\to C\to0$ is exact, so the kernel of the tensored surjection is the image of the tensored map on $A$ ([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).

[F5] A ring homomorphism killing a two-sided ideal factors uniquely through the quotient ring ([[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]], [[def-quotient-ring]]).

[F6] A free module on a set has a standard basis with unique finite expansions, and a set map from a basis extends uniquely to a linear map ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]], [[thm-universal-property-of-free-modules]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The map $\Theta$. Let $\rho:R\langle X\rangle\to S\langle X\rangle$ be the $R$-algebra homomorphism extending the generator map $X\to S\langle X\rangle$ (which need not be injective when $S$ is the zero ring), existing by [F1]. The pairing $\beta:S\times R\langle X\rangle\to S\langle X\rangle$, $\beta(s,y):=s\,\rho(y)$, is additive in each variable and satisfies $\beta(s\varphi(r),y)=s\varphi(r)\rho(y)=s\rho(ry)=\beta(s,ry)$ because $\rho$ is $R$-linear [F3, F1], so by [F2] it induces an $R$-linear map $\Theta:S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle\to S\langle X\rangle$ with $\Theta(s\otimes y)=s\rho(y)$; it is $S$-linear because $\Theta(s'(s\otimes y))=s's\rho(y)=s'\Theta(s\otimes y)$. The map $S\to S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$, $s\mapsto s\otimes1$, is unital and central by [F3], so the tensor product is an $S$-algebra with $(s\otimes y)(s'\otimes y')=ss'\otimes yy'$, and $\Theta((s\otimes y)(s'\otimes y'))=ss'\rho(yy')=ss'\rho(y)\rho(y')=\Theta(s\otimes y)\Theta(s'\otimes y')$ and $\Theta(1\otimes1)=1$; so $\Theta$ is a unital $S$-algebra homomorphism, and $\Theta(s\otimes w)=s\,w$ on a word $w$. [given, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Part 2. The quotient map $\pi:R\langle X\rangle\to R\langle X\rangle/I$ is a surjective $R$-algebra homomorphism with kernel $I$, so the sequence $I\to R\langle X\rangle\to R\langle X\rangle/I\to0$ is exact, and tensoring with $S$ over $R$ gives an exact sequence whose middle map is $\mathrm{id}_S\otimes\pi$ with kernel $J:=\operatorname{im}(S\otimes_RI)$ by [F4]. The set $J$ is a two-sided ideal of the $S$-algebra $S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$: it consists of finite sums $\sum_is_i\otimes e_i$ with $e_i\in I$ and is an additive subgroup, left multiplication by an elementary tensor gives $(s\otimes y)(s_i\otimes e_i)=ss_i\otimes ye_i$ with $ye_i\in I$, right multiplication gives $(s_i\otimes e_i)(s\otimes y)=s_is\otimes e_iy$ with $e_iy\in I$, and additivity extends both closures to all of $S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$. Moreover $J$ is generated as an ideal by the images $1\otimes e$ of the relations, since $s_i\otimes e_i=(s_i\otimes1)(1\otimes e_i)$. By [F5] the surjective $S$-algebra homomorphism $\mathrm{id}_S\otimes\pi$, which kills $J$, factors through a surjective $S$-algebra homomorphism $\overline{\Theta}:(S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle)/J\to S\otimes_R(R\langle X\rangle/I)$ with kernel $J/J=0$, hence an isomorphism. Its inverse is induced on the quotient by the pairing $(s,y+I)\mapsto s\otimes y+J$, which is well defined because $y'\in y+I$ gives $s\otimes y'-s\otimes y=s\otimes(y'-y)\in J$ and is bilinear by [F2]; the two maps are inverse because they are inverse on the spanning elements $s\otimes(y+I)$ and $s\otimes y+J$. The inverse is the unique $S$-algebra map with these prescribed values, since the tensors $s\otimes(y+I)$ span its source. [given, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.3 Part 3. Let $A$ be free with basis $(a_i)_{i\in I}$, so every $y\in A$ has a unique expansion $y=\sum_ic_ia_i$ by [F6]. The pairing $\gamma:S\times A\to S^{(I)}$, $\gamma(s,\sum_ic_ia_i):=\sum_i(s\varphi(c_i))e_i$, is additive in each variable and satisfies $\gamma(s\varphi(r),y)=\gamma(s,ry)$ because the coordinates of $ry$ are $rc_i$, and $\varphi(rc_i)=\varphi(r)\varphi(c_i)$; by [F2] it induces an $S$-linear map $\Gamma:S\otimes_RA\to S^{(I)}$ with $\Gamma(s\otimes y)=\sum_is\varphi(c_i)e_i$, in particular $\Gamma(1\otimes a_i)=e_i$, and the $S$-linear map $\Delta:S^{(I)}\to S\otimes_RA$ with $\Delta(e_i)=1\otimes a_i$, existing by [F6], satisfies $\Gamma\Delta=\mathrm{id}$ on the standard basis and $\Delta\Gamma(1\otimes a_i)=1\otimes a_i$; since elementary tensors and basis elements span, $\Gamma$ and $\Delta$ are mutually inverse isomorphisms. Hence $(1\otimes a_i)$ is an $S$-basis of $S\otimes_RA$, and an explicitly constructed basis isomorphism of a presented $R$-algebra may be tensored with $S$ to transport the basis to every commutative specialization. [given, F2, F3, F6, algebra]

2.1 Inverse for part 1. The map $X\to S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$, $x\mapsto1\otimes x$, extends by the universal property of [F1] applied over $S$ to an $S$-algebra homomorphism $\Lambda:S\langle X\rangle\to S\otimes_RR\langle X\rangle$, and for a word $w=x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n}$ one has $\Lambda(\rho(w))=\Lambda(x_{i_1}\cdots x_{i_n})=\prod_k\Lambda(x_{i_k})=\prod_k(1\otimes x_{i_k})=1\otimes w$ by multiplicativity and [F3]; hence $\Lambda\Theta(s\otimes w)=\Lambda(sw)=s(1\otimes w)=s\otimes w$ for every $s\in S$ and word $w$, so $\Lambda\Theta=\mathrm{id}$ on the spanning elementary tensors $s\otimes w$. Conversely $\Theta\Lambda$ and the identity of $S\langle X\rangle$ are unital $S$-algebra homomorphisms agreeing on the generators $x\in X$, so by the uniqueness in [F1] they are equal. Thus $\Theta$ is an $S$-algebra isomorphism, unique because it is determined on the spanning elementary tensors, and $\Lambda$ sends a word to $1\otimes w$. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra]

3.1 Collecting: step 1.1 constructs $\Theta$ and step 2.1 proves it is the unique $S$-algebra isomorphism of part 1 with the stated values; step 1.2 proves the quotient identification of part 2 with the image ideal generated by the relations and no flatness hypothesis; step 1.3 proves the free-basis transport of part 3. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
