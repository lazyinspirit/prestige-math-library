## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ be a nonempty measurable space, let $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ be a nonempty standard Borel space, and let $P$ be a probability measure on $(\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y,\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y)$. Put $P_X(C)=P(C\times\mathsf Y)$. There exists an everywhere probability kernel $K:(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)\to(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ such that

$$P(C\times A)=\int_C K(x,A)\,P_X(dx)\qquad(C\in\Sigma_X,\ A\in\Sigma_Y).$$

No standard-Borel, countable-generation, or completeness hypothesis is imposed on the instance space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the nonempty measurable instance space, the nonempty standard Borel label space, and the joint law $P$ in the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed for the regular-conditional-distribution existence supplier. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Under AC, a measurable random element with standard-Borel target has a regular conditional distribution given any sub-sigma-algebra; all sections are probability measures everywhere, with no completeness or countable-generation assumption on the conditioning sigma-algebra. [[thm-existence-of-regular-conditional-distributions-for-standard-borel-targets]]

[F2] A regular conditional distribution $L$ of $Y$ given $\mathcal G$ is a probability kernel from $(\Omega,\mathcal G)$ whose evaluations satisfy $\int_B L(\omega,A)P(d\omega)=P(B\cap\{Y\in A\})$ for every $B\in\mathcal G$ and $A\in\Sigma_Y$. In particular every evaluation is $\mathcal G$-measurable everywhere and every section is a probability measure. [[def-regular-conditional-distribution]], [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F3] Product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles; coordinate projections are measurable. A map is measurable when preimages of all target measurable sets are measurable, and this criterion is preserved by composition. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]], [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

[F4] Between two unequal real numbers there is a rational number. [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F5] If a random element $X$ has law $P_X$ and $g$ is nonnegative real-valued measurable, then $\int g(X(\omega))P(d\omega)=\int g(x)P_X(dx)$. [[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]

[F6] Restricting a nonnegative measurable function to a measurable set and setting it to zero elsewhere preserves measurability. [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]]

## Proof

1.1 Work on $\Omega=\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y$ with sigma-algebra $\mathcal F=\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y$ and probability $P$. Let $X(x,y)=x$ and $Y(x,y)=y$. These projections are measurable by [F3], and $X$ has law $P_X$. The family $\mathcal G:=\{X^{-1}(C):C\in\Sigma_X\}$ is a sigma-algebra, since inverse images preserve the full set, complements and countable unions; it is contained in $\mathcal F$ and is exactly $\sigma(X)$. Apply [F1] to the label coordinate $Y$ given this exact, uncompleted $\mathcal G$, obtaining a kernel $L$ as in [F2]. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Fix one $y_0\in\mathsf Y$ and define $s:\mathsf X\to\Omega$ by $s(x)=(x,y_0)$. As a map into $(\Omega,\mathcal G)$ it is measurable: for every $C\in\Sigma_X$, $s^{-1}(X^{-1}(C))=C$, and step 1.1 describes every set of $\mathcal G$ in this form. Also $X(s(x))=x$ for every $x$. [given, F3, step 1.1, choose, construct]

3.1 Every $\mathcal G$-measurable real function $v$ is constant on each $X$-fiber. Indeed, if $X(\omega)=X(\omega')$ but $v(\omega)<v(\omega')$, choose a rational $r$ strictly between these values by [F4]. The measurable set $\{v\le r\}$ equals $X^{-1}(C)$ for some $C\in\Sigma_X$ by step 1.1, and so cannot contain exactly one of $\omega,\omega'$, a contradiction. Reversing the two points rules out the other inequality. Applying this to $v(\omega)=L(\omega,A)\in[0,1]$ separately for each $A\in\Sigma_Y$ gives $L(\omega,A)=L(s(X(\omega)),A)$ for every $\omega$ and every $A$. These are pointwise identities, with no event-dependent null sets. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, cases]

4.1 Define $K(x,A):=L(s(x),A)$. Every $K(x,\cdot)$ is a probability measure by [F2]. For fixed $A$, the evaluation is the composition of the $\mathcal G$-measurable evaluation of $L$ with the measurable map $s$ from step 2.1, so it is $\Sigma_X$-measurable by [F3]. Thus $K$ is an everywhere probability kernel, and step 3.1 gives $K(X(\omega),A)=L(\omega,A)$ for every $\omega,A$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Fix $C\in\Sigma_X$ and $A\in\Sigma_Y$. The real function $g(x)=\mathbf1_C(x)K(x,A)$ is measurable, nonnegative and bounded by one by [F6] and step 4.1. Apply [F5] and then [F2] to obtain $\int_C K(x,A)P_X(dx)=\int_{X^{-1}(C)}K(X(\omega),A)P(d\omega)=\int_{X^{-1}(C)}L(\omega,A)P(d\omega)=P(X^{-1}(C)\cap Y^{-1}(A))=P(C\times A)$. Since $C,A$ were arbitrary, this proves the Statement on the original arbitrary measurable instance space. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
