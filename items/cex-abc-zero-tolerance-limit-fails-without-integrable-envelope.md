## Statement

There is a proper prior-simulator model with parameter space $\mathbb N_{\ge1}$, summary space $\mathbb R$, and observed summary $s_0=0$ such that every parameter-specific summary density is continuous at $0$ and its prior average there is positive and finite, but the indicator ABC targets do not converge in total variation to the posterior selected by those density values. In fact, any common integrable envelope on any neighborhood of $0$ fails to exist.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Countable Choice and the prior-simulator and indicator-target conventions in [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]].

[A1] Countable Choice, as assumed by the cited zero-tolerance theorem. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F1] A standard Borel space is measurably isomorphic to a Polish space with its Borel sigma-algebra. [[def-standard-borel-space]]

[F2] At most countable means finite or in bijection with $\mathbb N$; a Polish presentation is separable and has a compatible complete metric; the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by its open sets. [[def-countable]], [[def-polish-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F3] A weighted sum of probability measures with nonnegative weights is a measure, and its total mass is the sum of the weights. [[def-nonnegative-weighted-sum-of-measures]], [[thm-nonnegative-weighted-sums-of-measures]], [[def-dirac-measure]], [[prop-dirac-measure-is-a-probability-measure]]

[F4] The reciprocal sequence $1/(N+1)$ tends to zero in $\mathbb R$. [[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]]

[F5] An integrable nonnegative measurable density defines a measure by integration; interval and box indicators integrate to their Lebesgue volumes. [[def-measure-with-density]], [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]], [[def-lebesgue-measure-and-the-lebesgue-sigma-algebra]], [[thm-lebesgue-measure-of-a-box-of-every-kind]]

[F6] The ABC target is the prior-predictive law restricted to the tolerance event and normalized by its positive probability; the sufficient zero-tolerance theorem states the integrable-envelope hypothesis under Countable Choice. [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]], [[thm-abc-zero-tolerance-target-is-summary-posterior-under-density-regularity]]

[F7] Tonelli's theorem identifies the integral of a nonnegative product-measurable function with either iterated integral on sigma-finite product spaces. [[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

## Proof

**Given:** The conventions and axiom above.

1.1 Equip $\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ with the discrete metric $d(n,m)=0$ for $n=m$ and $1$ otherwise. A Cauchy sequence, meaning that its tail terms are pairwise less than every positive radius apart, is eventually constant by taking radius $1/2$, so this metric is complete. The set is countable and dense in itself; each singleton is open, so every subset is open and its Borel sigma-algebra is the power set. The identity is therefore a Borel isomorphism to this Polish presentation, and [F1] gives standard Borelness. [given, F1, F2, construct]

1.2 Put $\pi_n=1/[n(n+1)]$. The partial sums telescope: $\sum_{n=1}^N\pi_n=\sum_{n=1}^N\left(\frac1n-\frac1{n+1}\right)=1-\frac1{N+1}\longrightarrow1$ by [F4]. Therefore $\Pi=\sum_{n\ge1}\pi_n\delta_n$ is a probability measure by [F3]. [given, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Define $b(s)=\tfrac14\mathbf1_{[-1,1]}(s)$, $w_n=1/(100n^3)$, $\phi_n(s)=(4w_n)^{-1}\mathbf1_{[1/n-w_n,\,1/n+w_n]}(s)$, and $m_n=b+\phi_n$. The two summands are nonnegative measurable functions with integrals $1/2$ each, so $m_n$ is a probability density. Let $K(n,ds)=m_n(s)\,ds$. It is a probability kernel: every section is a probability measure, and every function of $n$ is measurable on the discrete parameter space. For measurable $A\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ and Lebesgue-measurable $C\subseteq\mathbb R$, the ABC definition gives $M(A\times C)=\sum_{n\in A}\pi_n\int_Cm_n(s)\,ds$. To extend this identity from rectangles, let $E\subseteq\mathbb N_{\ge1}\times\mathbb R$ be product-measurable and put $E_n=\{s:(n,s)\in E\}$. The section map $s\mapsto(n,s)$ is measurable, so each $E_n$ is Lebesgue-measurable; because $\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ is countable discrete, $E=\bigsqcup_{n\ge1}(\{n\}\times E_n)$. Countable additivity and the rectangle identity give $M(E)=\sum_{n\ge1}\pi_n\int_{E_n}m_n(s)\,\lambda_1(ds).$ The function $m(n,s):=m_n(s)$ is product-measurable because $\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ is countable discrete and every $m_n$ is measurable. The prior $\Pi$ is sigma-finite and Lebesgue measure is sigma-finite, so [F7] applied to $\mathbf1_E m$ gives $\int_E m(n,s)\,(\Pi\times\lambda_1)(d(n,s))=\sum_{n\ge1}\pi_n\int_{E_n}m_n(s)\,\lambda_1(ds).$ Thus $M$ has the specified jointly measurable density on the full product sigma-algebra, not only on rectangles. The identity summary is measurable. [given, F1, F5, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 For each fixed $n$, the spike interval is a positive distance from $0$, since $1/n-w_n>0$. On a sufficiently small neighborhood of $0$, $m_n(s)=1/4$; hence $m_n$ is continuous at $0$ and $m_n(0)=1/4$. Thus $z_0=\sum_n\pi_nm_n(0)=1/4$, and the density-selected zero-tolerance posterior is $\Pi$ itself. However, for every $\delta>0$, all sufficiently large $n$ have $1/n<\delta$, and on the neighborhood $(-\delta,\delta)$ any common pointwise envelope $H(n)$ must satisfy $H(n)\ge m_n(1/n)=\frac14+25n^3.$ Since $\pi_n\ge1/(2n^2)$, $\sum_n\pi_nH(n)\ge\frac{25}{2}\sum_n n=\infty$. Every prior atom has positive mass, so an almost-everywhere envelope condition also forces this bound for every $n$. No integrable local envelope exists. [given, F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 For integer $q\ge2$, take $\epsilon_q=1/q$ and $B_q=[-1/q,1/q]$. The base density contributes $1/(2q)$ to every parameter's ball probability by the box-volume formula [F5]. If $n<q$, then $1/n-w_n>1/q$: indeed $(q-n)/(nq)\ge1/(n(n+1))>1/(100n^3)$. If $n>q$, then $1/n+w_n<1/q$: here $(n-q)/(qn)\ge1/(q(q+1))>1/(100(q+1)^3)\ge1/(100n^3)$. Thus spikes for $n<q$ contribute zero; the $n=q$ ball cuts its symmetric spike at the center and captures half its mass, namely $1/4$; and spikes for $n>q$ lie wholly in the ball and contribute $1/2$. [given, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F6] and the telescoping tail $\sum_{n>q}\pi_n=1/(q+1)$, the acceptance probability is $p_q=\frac1{2q}+\frac{1}{4q(q+1)}+\frac1{2(q+1)},\qquad qp_q\longrightarrow1.$ For the fixed parameter event $A=\{1\}$, its unnormalized accepted mass is $\pi_1/(2q)=1/(4q)$, because its spike lies outside $B_q$. Hence $\Pi_{\epsilon_q}(A\mid0)=(1/(4q))/p_q\to1/4$, whereas the density-selected limit assigns $A$ mass $\Pi(A)=1/2$. The total-variation distance is at least this event-probability difference, which tends to $1/4$. Thus the claimed convergence fails without the integrable-envelope condition. [given, A1, F6, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
