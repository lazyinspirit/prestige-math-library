## Statement

Assume AC. Let n≥2 be an integer, let R be Noetherian with n invertible, and let `V=Spec B` be an affine finite-type R-scheme with an action of `μₙ=Spec R[T]/(Tⁿ−1)` whose stabilizer at every geometric point of V is trivial. Then `A=B^(μₙ)` is a finite-type R-algebra, the map `V→Spec A` is a finite étale μₙ-torsor of degree n, and `Spec A` represents the fppf quotient sheaf `V/μₙ`. The conclusion includes nonreduced R and V.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, R, V and the free geometric μₙ action of the Statement.

[F1] A μₙ action is a coaction of `R[T]/(Tⁿ−1)`, and its invariant subring consists of coaction-fixed elements. [[def-diagonalizable-group-and-character-module]]

[F2] Finite locally free morphisms of positive rank are faithfully flat. [[def-faithfully-flat-morphism-schemes]]

[F3] AC supplies algebraic closures of residue fields. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** character grading and geometric fibres.

1.1 By [F1], writing the coaction in the R-basis `1,T,…,T^(n−1)` gives a direct decomposition `B=⊕_(i mod n) B_i` with `B_i B_j⊂B_(i+j mod n)` and A=B₀. The coefficient identities follow from the counit and coassociativity. Each homogeneous b satisfies `bⁿ∈A`, so it is integral over A; sums of integral elements are integral, hence B is integral over A. Since B is finitely generated over R⊂A, B is finite over A. Choose finitely many homogeneous generators of B over R and let C be the R-subalgebra generated by their n-th powers. Then C is Noetherian, B is finite over C, and A is a C-submodule of B; hence A is finite over C and of finite type over R. Thus A is Noetherian. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix a geometric point of Spec A with algebraically closed residue field k. The character decomposition commutes with tensoring over A, so the invariants in `B⊗_A k` are exactly k. The finite k-scheme `Spec(B⊗_A k)` carries a free action of the constant geometric group of n elements. Its connected components are Artinian local schemes and form free orbits. On each orbit the n local algebras are isomorphic and the invariant algebra is one of them. As the total invariant algebra is k, there is exactly one orbit and its local algebra is k. Hence the fibre is n reduced points in a free transitive orbit. Its character components are one-dimensional, and multiplication of degrees i and −i surjects onto the degree-zero component. [F1, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 For each i mod n let Iᵢ be the image ideal of `B_i⊗_A B_(−i)→A`. It is finitely generated because B is finite over Noetherian A. Step 2.1 says Iᵢ has unit image in every geometric residue field, so Iᵢ=A. Choose finitely many products `∑ x_j y_j=1` with `x_j∈B_i` and `y_j∈B_(−i)`. They give mutually inverse finite dual-basis maps, proving each B_i is an invertible A-module and multiplication identifies `B_i⊗B_j` with `B_(i+j)`. Thus B is finite locally free of rank n over A, and the canonical map `B⊗_A B→B⊗_R R[T]/(Tⁿ−1)` is an isomorphism by the strong grading. It is precisely the μₙ-torsor identity. Since μₙ is finite étale over R, the torsor is finite étale; [F2] gives faithful flatness. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 The torsor identity says the two maps `V×_A V⇉V` are the action groupoid. Faithfully flat descent for the representable sheaf h_(Spec A) therefore identifies it with the fppf sheaf quotient of V by μₙ. This proves the universal quotient property, including nilpotent base rings. [F2, F3, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
