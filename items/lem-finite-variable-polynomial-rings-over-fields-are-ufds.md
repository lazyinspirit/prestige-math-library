## Statement

Let $k$ be a field and let $r\ge0$ be an integer. Then $k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a
unique factorisation domain ([[def-unique-factorisation-domain]]). Every
irreducible element of it is prime
([[def-irreducible-and-prime-elements-in-a-domain]]), and every prime ideal of
height one ([[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]]) is generated by an irreducible
element.

For $r=0$ the ring is the field $k$ itself
([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]); it has no irreducibles and
no height-one primes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and an integer $r\ge0$.

[L1] A UFD is an integral domain in which every nonzero nonunit is a finite product of irreducibles, and any two such products of the same element have the same length and matching factors up to order and associates ([[def-unique-factorisation-domain]]).

[L2] In a domain, a nonzero nonunit $p$ is irreducible when $p=ab$ forces $a$ or $b$ to be a unit, and prime when $p\mid ab$ implies $p\mid a$ or $p\mid b$ ([[def-irreducible-and-prime-elements-in-a-domain]]).

[L3] Let $R$ be a UFD with field of fractions $K$. A polynomial in $R[x]$ is primitive when its coefficients have no common nonunit divisor. Products of primitive polynomials are primitive; and a primitive polynomial of positive degree is irreducible in $R[x]$ if and only if it is irreducible in $K[x]$ ([[lem-gauss-lemma-over-a-ufd]]).

[L4] Every domain $R$ has a field of fractions $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ containing it as a subring ([[def-field-of-fractions]], [[thm-field-of-fractions-is-a-field-and-the-domain-embeds]]).

[L5] For every field $F$, the polynomial ring $F[x]$ is a UFD ([[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]]).

[L6] The iterated polynomial ring is $k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]=k[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}][x_r]$ for $r\ge1$, with $k[x_1,\ldots,x_0]=k$ ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L7] If $R$ is an integral domain then so is $R[x]$ ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]), and for nonzero polynomials over a domain $\deg(fg)=\deg f+\deg g$ ([[thm-polynomial-degree-of-a-product-over-a-domain]]). A quotient $R/\mathfrak p$ is an integral domain exactly when $\mathfrak p$ is prime ([[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]]), and a unital ring homomorphism $R\to S$ induces one $R[x]\to S[x]$ with $x\mapsto x$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]). A field is a commutative ring whose nonzero elements are units ([[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L8] The Krull dimension of a nonzero commutative ring is the supremum of the lengths of strict chains of prime ideals, and the height of a prime $\mathfrak p$ is $\dim R_{\mathfrak p}$; contraction along $R\to R_{\mathfrak p}$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}R_{\mathfrak p}$ onto the primes of $R$ contained in $\mathfrak p$ ([[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]], [[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction.

1.1 For $r=0$ the ring is the field $k$ by [L6]. Every nonzero element of a field is a unit by [L7], so there are no nonzero nonunits at all; the existence and uniqueness clauses of [L1] hold vacuously, and by [L2] there is no irreducible element, so the statement about irreducibles is vacuous too. This is the base case of the induction on $r$. [L1, L2, L6, L7, base]

1.2 Let $R$ be any UFD and $p\in R$ irreducible. Then $p$ is prime: if $p\mid ab$ with $a,b\ne0$, factor $a=\prod_ia_i$, $b=\prod_jb_j$ and write $ab=pc$ with $c\ne0$ factored as $\prod_kc_k$, as [L1] allows whenever the element in question is a nonzero nonunit, the unit cases being immediate from $p\mid ab$; comparing the two products of irreducibles for $ab$ and $pc$ by the uniqueness clause of [L1] shows that $p$ is associate to one of the $a_i$ or one of the $b_j$, hence divides $a$ or $b$. [L1, L2, algebra]

1.3 Now let $R$ be a UFD, $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ by [L4], and $0\ne f\in R[x]$. For each associate class $C$ of irreducibles in $R$, let $v_C(a)$ be the exponent of that class in the factorization of a nonzero $a\in R$; it is well-defined by the uniqueness clause of [L1]. Let $\mathcal C(f)$ be the finite set of such classes appearing in the factorizations of the nonzero coefficients of $f$, and for each $C\in\mathcal C(f)$ choose one representative $p_C$. Set $e_C=\min\{v_C(a):a\ne0\text{ is a coefficient of }f\}$ and $c(f)=\prod_{C\in\mathcal C(f)}p_C^{e_C}$. This finite product is a common divisor of the coefficients, every common divisor divides it up to associates, and $f^\ast:=f/c(f)$ is primitive in the sense of [L3]; thus $f=c(f)f^\ast$. For $\lambda\in K^\times$, define $v_C(\lambda)=v_C(a)-v_C(b)$ when $\lambda=a/b$ with nonzero $a,b\in R$; this is well-defined, and $v_C(\lambda)=0$ for every associate class exactly when $\lambda$ is a unit of $R$. Note also that an irreducible $f$ of positive degree is primitive: a nonunit constant $r\in R$ dividing all coefficients would write $f=r\cdot(f/r)$ with both factors nonunits, since $f/r$ has positive degree by [L7]. The content $c(f)$ factors into irreducibles of $R$ by [L1], and each such constant is irreducible in $R[x]$, because a factorization of a constant in $R[x]$ has both factors constant by [L7] and so is a factorization in $R$. For the primitive part, [L5] gives a factorization $f^\ast=u g_1\cdots g_n$ in $K[x]$, with $u\in K^\times$ and each $g_j$ irreducible in $K[x]$; if $n=0$, then $f^\ast$ is a nonzero constant in $R$ and, being primitive, is a unit of $R$. For each $j$ choose $a_j\in R\setminus\{0\}$ with $a_jg_j\in R[x]$ and write $a_jg_j=d_jh_j$, where $d_j\in R$ is a content and $h_j\in R[x]$ is primitive; then $g_j=(d_j/a_j)h_j$, so $h_j$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of $g_j$ in $K[x]$, hence irreducible in $K[x]$ and in $R[x]$ by [L3]. By [L3] the product $h_1\cdots h_n$ is primitive, and $f^\ast=\lambda\,h_1\cdots h_n$ with $\lambda=u\prod_j(d_j/a_j)\in K^\times$. Since both $f^\ast$ and $h_1\cdots h_n$ are primitive, comparison of the least exponents in each associate class gives $v_C(\lambda)=0$ for every $C$, so $\lambda$ is a unit of $R$. Thus $f$ is a product of irreducibles of $R[x]$. [L1, L3, L4, L5, L7, algebra]

1.4 Assume now that $r\ge1$ and that $k[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}]$ is a UFD in which every irreducible element is prime; this is the induction hypothesis. [ih]

2.1 For uniqueness, let $p_1\cdots p_m=q_1\cdots q_n$ be two factorizations of the same element $f\ne0$ of $R[x]$ into irreducibles. Multiply the positive-degree factors of each side together, using the primitivity noted in 1.3; the result is primitive by [L3] on each side, so comparing contents as in 1.3 shows that the two sides' constant factors are associates and that the products of positive-degree factors are associates of one another. Each positive-degree factor is irreducible in $K[x]$ by [L3], and $K[x]$ is a UFD by [L5], so the two products of positive-degree factors agree up to order, associates in $K[x]$, and a unit scalar; that scalar is a unit of $R$ by the exponent comparison of 1.3, so they agree up to order and associates in $R[x]$. The constant factors are products of irreducibles of $R$ and agree up to order and associates by the uniqueness clause of [L1]. Hence $R[x]$ satisfies the uniqueness clause of [L1], and with 1.3 it is a UFD. [L1, L3, L5, L7, step 1.3]

2.2 Every irreducible element of $R[x]$ is prime. Let $p\in R[x]$ be irreducible. If $\deg p=0$ then $p\in R$ is irreducible in $R$, hence prime in $R$ by 1.2; if $p\mid ab$ in $R[x]$, then the induced map $R[x]\to(R/(p))[x]$ of [L7] kills $ab$, and $(R/(p))[x]$ is a domain by [L7] because $R/(p)$ is a domain for the prime element $p$; so all coefficients of $a$ or all coefficients of $b$ lie in $(p)$, that is, $p\mid a$ or $p\mid b$. If $\deg p\ge1$, then $p$ is primitive by 1.3, hence irreducible in $K[x]$ by [L3], hence prime in the UFD $K[x]$ by 1.2; if $p\mid ab$ in $R[x]$, then $p\mid ab$ in $K[x]$, so after possibly swapping $a,b$ we have $a=pq$ for some $q\in K[x]$. Write $q=\lambda q^\ast$ with $\lambda\in K^\times$ and $q^\ast\in R[x]$ primitive, by the content construction of 1.3 applied to a polynomial clearing the denominators of $q$; then $a=\lambda\,(pq^\ast)$, and the product $pq^\ast$ is primitive by [L3]. Comparing contents in the equality $a=\lambda\,(pq^\ast)$ shows that $\lambda$ is associate to the content $c(a)\in R$ of step 1.3: clearing the denominators of $\lambda$ by some $u\ne0$ gives $ua=(\lambda u)(pq^\ast)$, whose left side has content $u\,c(a)$ and whose right side has content $\lambda u$ up to units because $pq^\ast$ is primitive, the exponents $v_p$ of step 1.3 being additive in a constant factor. Hence $\lambda\in R$ and $q=\lambda q^\ast\in R[x]$, so $a=pq$ with $q\in R[x]$ and $p\mid a$ in $R[x]$. [L2, L3, L5, L7, step 1.2, step 1.3]

2.3 In any UFD $A$, every prime ideal of height one is generated by an irreducible element. By [L8], $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=1=\dim A_{\mathfrak p}$ means that inside $\mathfrak p$ there is a strict chain of primes of length one and none of length two; in particular $\mathfrak p$ contains a nonzero element, so choose $0\ne f\in\mathfrak p$. Factoring $f$ into irreducibles and using that $\mathfrak p$ is prime, some irreducible factor $p$ lies in $\mathfrak p$ by [L1] and [L2]; then $p$ is prime by 1.2, so $(p)$ is a nonzero prime ideal contained in $\mathfrak p$. Were $(p)\subsetneq\mathfrak p$, the strict chain $0\subsetneq(p)\subsetneq\mathfrak p$ of primes of $A$ would, by the inclusion-preserving bijection of [L8], give a chain of length two in $\operatorname{Spec}A_{\mathfrak p}$, contradicting $\dim A_{\mathfrak p}=1$. Hence $\mathfrak p=(p)$. [L1, L2, L8, step 1.2]

3.1 By [L6] the ring $k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is the polynomial ring $A[x_r]$ over $A=k[x_1,\ldots,x_{r-1}]$, which is a UFD by step 1.4. Steps 1.3 and 2.1 therefore make $k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ a UFD, and step 2.2 shows that every irreducible element of it is prime, using the primitivity results quoted in those steps. So the UFD clause and the irreducible-is-prime clause hold for this $r\ge1$ whenever they hold for $r-1$. [L6, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The base case 1.1 and the induction step 3.1 prove the UFD clause and the irreducible-is-prime clause for every $r\ge0$. Finally, if $\mathfrak p\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a prime ideal of height one, then $k[x_1,\ldots,x_r]$ is a UFD by 3.1, so 2.3 exhibits an irreducible element generating $\mathfrak p$; for $r=0$ the case is vacuous, a field having no nonzero prime ideal. This proves all three clauses of the statement. [step 2.3, step 3.1, discharge-induction: step 1.1] ∎
