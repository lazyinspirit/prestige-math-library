## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group over $k$ with root datum $(X,\Phi,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$ ([[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Then: (a) $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$ is a finite constant group scheme and $W(G,T)(k')=N_G(T)(k')/T(k')$ for every field $k'\supseteq k$; (b) every split maximal torus of $G$ lies in a Borel subgroup, and every Borel subgroup of $G$ containing $T$ is of the form $P_G(\lambda)$ for a regular cocharacter $\lambda$, hence is split; (c) $W(G,T)$ acts simply transitively on the finite set of Borel subgroups of $G$ containing $T$, and the map sending a Borel subgroup to its system of positive roots is a bijection onto the set of positive systems; (d) there is a canonical isomorphism $W(G,T)\cong W(R)$ of finite groups, and $W(G,T)$ is generated by the simple reflections $s_\alpha$, $\alpha\in\Delta$, for any base $\Delta$; (e) for a Borel subgroup $B\supseteq T$, for $w\in W(G,T)$ and a representative $n_w\in N_G(T)(k)$, the double coset $Bn_wB$ depends only on $w$ and $n_wU_\alpha n_w^{-1}=U_{w\alpha}$ for all roots $\alpha$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with root datum $(X,\Phi,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$ and Weyl group $W(G,T)=N_G(T)/T$.

[F1] The root-subgroup theorem supplies the reduced root datum, generation of $G$ by $T$ and its root groups, $U_\alpha\cong\mathbf G_a$, the $k$-rational representatives $n_\alpha$ of root reflections, and $W(G,T)=\langle s_\alpha\rangle$ acting on the root groups. For a Borel $B\supseteq T$ with positive system $\Phi^+$, ordered multiplication $\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}U_\alpha\to R_u(B)$ is a $T$-equivariant variety isomorphism; its smooth $T$-stable subgroups are products of the root groups they contain, and their weight sets are exactly the quasi-closed subsets of $\Phi^+$ ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], clauses (b)-(f), [[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] For a split reductive pair $(G,T)$ over $k$, the groups $P_G(\lambda)$ for regular cocharacters $\lambda$ of $T$ are Borel subgroups containing $T$, and $\lambda\mapsto P_G(\lambda)$ induces a bijection from Weyl chambers onto these Borel subgroups; their Lie algebra is $\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle>0}\mathfrak g_\alpha$ ([[lem-borel-root-group-opposition]]). This is a statement over $k$; no Borel conjugacy theorem over $k$ is being assumed.

[F3] The abstract root datum has finite reflection group, generated by the simple reflections of any base and acting simply transitively on its chambers ([[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

[F4] Over an algebraically closed field, centralizers of tori in a reductive group are smooth connected reductive, and $C_G(T)=T$ for a maximal torus ([[thm-chevalley-centralizer-radical-and-reductive-centralizers]]). We apply this interface after extension to an algebraic closure.

[F5] For reductive $G$, the cocharacter-limit theorem gives smooth connected unipotent $U_G(\lambda)$, a group-scheme isomorphism $P_G(\lambda)=U_G(\lambda)\rtimes Z_G(\lambda)$, the Lie weight descriptions, and $R_u(P_G(\lambda))=U_G(\lambda)$ ([[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]]). For regular $\lambda$, pass to an algebraic closure: $Z_G(\lambda)$ contains $T$, has Lie algebra $\mathfrak t$, and is connected as a torus centralizer by [F4]. Thus $Z_G(\lambda)=T$ by dimension there, and this equality descends to $k$.

## Proof

1.1 The normalizer quotient $W=N_G(T)/T$ is finite étale by the structural root Definition. Its action on $X(T)$ is faithful: after algebraic closure, a normalizer element acting trivially on the character lattice centralizes $T$, and then lies in $C_G(T)=T$ by [F4]. The exact root-subgroup theorem [F1](e) identifies its geometric group with the reflection group, and [F1](c) supplies a $k$-rational normalizer representative for every root reflection. Products of these representatives therefore realize every geometric element of $W$ over $k$. A finite étale scheme all of whose geometric points are rational is a disjoint union of copies of $\operatorname{Spec}k$, so $W$ is constant. This proves constantness from the actual representatives, rather than from torsor lifting alone. [F1, F4, given]

1.2 Let $T'$ be any split maximal torus. Then $(G,T')$ is a split reductive pair, and there is an integral regular cocharacter $\lambda'$: outside finitely many rational hyperplanes in $X_*(T')\otimes\mathbb R$ choose a rational point and clear denominators. If there are no roots, the weight decomposition gives $\operatorname{Lie}G=\operatorname{Lie}T'$, hence $G=T'$ by smoothness and connectedness, and it is its own Borel. Otherwise [F2], applied to $(G,T')$, makes $P_G(\lambda')$ a Borel over $k$ containing $T'$. For every Borel $B\supseteq T$, [F2] gives $B=P_G(\lambda)$ for a regular $k$-cocharacter $\lambda$, and [F5] gives $B=U\rtimes T$ with $U=R_u(B)$. [F1, F2, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 For a field extension $k'/k$, constantness in step 1.1 identifies $W(k')$ with the same finite group as $W(k)$. Every element therefore has a $k$-rational normalizer representative, constructed in step 1.1 as a product of the root-reflection representatives supplied by [F1](c). Base change of this representative gives a point of $N_G(T)(k')$ mapping to the specified element of $W(k')$. Two such points have the same image precisely when their quotient lies in $T(k')$, since the scheme-theoretic kernel is $T$. Thus the quotient map induces the bijection $N_G(T)(k')/T(k')=W(k')$, completing (a). [F1, step 1.1, given]

2.2 By [F1](e), the faithful action of $W$ on $X(T)$ is precisely the group generated by the root reflections, canonically $W(R)$. The root combinatorics [F3] show that the simple reflections for any base generate it. This proves(d) directly from the supplier, without a backward reference to a later step or an unrelated claim about generating $G$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

2.3 To prove splitness, put $m_\alpha=\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle>0$ for $\alpha\in\Phi^+(B)$. In the ordered root coordinates of [F1], let $U_r$ be the product of the root groups with $m_\alpha\ge r$, for integers $r\ge1$. This root subset is quasi-closed, since every root $i\alpha+j\beta$ with $i,j>0$ has weight $im_\alpha+jm_\beta\ge r$. Thus [F1] makes each $U_r$ a smooth connected $T$-stable subgroup, with $U_1=U$ and $U_r=1$ for large $r$. For roots $\alpha,\beta\in\Phi^+$, every root coordinate of the commutator $[u_\alpha(x),u_\beta(y)]$ is a polynomial in $x,y$, is homogeneous for the $\lambda$-weights, and vanishes when either variable is zero. Each nonzero monomial $x^ay^b$ therefore has $a,b\ge1$ and weight $am_\alpha+bm_\beta\ge m_\alpha+m_\beta$. Conjugating a root generator of $U_r$ by a root generator of $U$ therefore keeps it in $U_r$, as does inverse conjugation, proving normality under $U$; $T$ preserves the weight subsets. Applying the same argument to $U_{r+1}$, the root-generator commutators vanish modulo this normal subgroup, so $[U,U_r]\subseteq U_{r+1}$. Thus $U_r$ is normal in $B=U\rtimes T$. Root coordinates of weight exactly $r$ induce $U_r/U_{r+1}\cong\prod_{m_\alpha=r}\mathbf G_a$: ordering those factors first gives a variety product with $U_{r+1}$, and collecting root factors modulo $U_{r+1}$ adds those coordinates, since their commutators have greater weight. The coordinate projection is a surjective group morphism with kernel $U_{r+1}$. Refining these vector-group quotients by coordinate subspaces yields a normal series with $\mathbf G_a$ quotients; the split torus quotient $B/U=T\cong\mathbf G_m^{\dim T}$ yields $\mathbf G_m$ quotients. Hence $B$ is split as a solvable group. Together with step 1.2 this proves (b). [F1, F5, step 1.2, algebra, construct]

3.1 The opposition theorem [F2] identifies Borels containing $T$ with the sign chambers of regular cocharacters, equivalently with positive root systems. Conjugation by a normalizer representative permutes root groups by [F1], hence transports these sign systems by its lattice action. Under the canonical identification in step2.2, [F3] gives the simple transitivity of that action and the bijection onto positive systems. This proves(c). [F1, F2, F3, step 2.2]

4.1 For (e), changing a representative $n_w$ by an element of $T(k)\subseteq B(k)$ preserves $Bn_wB$. Conjugation by $n_w$ carries $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ to $\mathfrak g_{w\alpha}$, and its conjugate root group is smooth, connected and $T$-stable. The root-group containment criterion of [F1](b), applied to that conjugate group and $U_{w\alpha}$, gives containment; both groups have dimension one, so they are equal. Thus $n_wU_\alpha n_w^{-1}=U_{w\alpha}$ scheme-theoretically. This proves (e). [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 2.3] ∎
