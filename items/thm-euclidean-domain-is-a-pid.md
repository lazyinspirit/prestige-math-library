## Statement

Every Euclidean domain is a principal ideal domain.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Euclidean domain $R$ with Euclidean function $\delta$, and an
ideal $I\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}R$.

[L1] Euclidean division gives $a=bq+r$ with $r=0$ or
$\delta(r)<\delta(b)$ whenever $b\ne0$
([[def-euclidean-domain]]).

[L2] An ideal is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by arbitrary
ring elements ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]).

[L3] The principal ideal $(d)$ is the ideal generated by $d$
([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[L4] Every nonempty subset of $\mathbb N$ has a least element
([[thm-well-ordering-principle]]).

[L5] A PID is an integral domain whose every ideal is principal
([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $I=\{0\}$, then $I=(0)$ and is principal. [L3]

1.2 Suppose $I\ne\{0\}$. The set $\{\delta(x):x\in I\setminus\{0\}\}$ is nonempty, so choose $d\in I\setminus\{0\}$ whose $\delta$-value is least. [L4, given, choose]

2.1 For $a\in I$, divide by $d$: $a=dq+r$ with $r=0$ or $\delta(r)<\delta(d)$. Since $r=a-dq\in I$, minimality in step 1.2 excludes a nonzero $r$; hence $r=0$. [step 1.2, L1, L2, given]

3.1 Step 2.1 gives $a=dq\in(d)$ for every $a\in I$, so $I\subseteq(d)$. Conversely $d\in I$ and ideal closure give $dq\in I$ for every $q\in R$, so $(d)\subseteq I$. Thus $I=(d)$. [step 2.1, L2, L3, given]

4.1 Every ideal is principal by step 1.1 or step 3.1; therefore $R$ is a PID. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L5] ∎
