## Statement refuted

> Every faithful realization of a finite-rank Coxeter system is reflection
> faithful in the sense of Elias–Williamson; in particular the canonical
> geometric realization of a Coxeter system is reflection faithful whenever its
> reflection representation is faithful.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The rank-two system $S=\{s,t\}$ with $m(s,t)=\infty$, the group $W=\langle s,t\mid s^{2}=t^{2}=1\rangle$, the space $V=\mathbb R^{S}=\mathbb Re_s\oplus\mathbb Re_t$, the Coxeter form $B$ and the canonical representation $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ with $\rho(s)=r_s$, $\rho(t)=r_t$, and the root system $\Phi$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]]).

[F1] The radicals of a bilinear form $B$ on $V$ are $\operatorname{rad}_L(B)=\{u:B(u,v)=0\text{ for every }v\}$ and $\operatorname{rad}_R(B)=\{v:B(u,v)=0\text{ for every }u\}$; for a symmetric form they coincide, and the form is degenerate exactly when the radical is nonzero. ([[def-matrix-radicals-rank-and-nondegeneracy-of-a-bilinear-form]], [[def-bilinear-symmetric-skew-and-alternating-forms]])

[F2] The matrix of a linear map $T:V\to W$ in ordered bases has as its columns the coordinate columns of the images of the basis vectors. ([[def-coordinate-column-and-matrix-of-a-linear-map]])

[F3] The subgroup generated by a set is contained in every subgroup containing that set; in particular every element of $W$ lies in the set of finite products of the generators and their inverses, which is a subgroup containing them. ([[def-generated-subgroup]])

## Counterexample

1.1 The representation is faithful: $\rho$ is injective by clause (3) of [[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]], applied to the system $S=\{s,t\}$, $m(s,t)=\infty$. Hence $\rho$ is a faithful canonical realization of the system, and by clause (4)(a) of [[lem-cg-hecke-and-lie-seam-contract-compatibility]] the canonical construction is a realization in the sense of Elias–Williamson, with $\mathfrak h=V$, $\alpha^\vee_s=e_s$ and $\alpha_s$ the functional $2B(-,e_s)$. [given]

1.2 The radical: since $m(s,t)=\infty$ one has $B(e_s,e_s)=B(e_t,e_t)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-1$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]]). Hence $B(e_s+e_t,e_s)=1-1=0$ and $B(e_s+e_t,e_t)=-1+1=0$, so $\mathbb R(e_s+e_t)\subseteq\operatorname{rad}(B)$; for $v=ae_s+be_t$, one has $B(v,e_s)=a-b$ and $B(v,e_t)=b-a$. Thus $v$ lies in the radical exactly when $a=b$. The radical is therefore the one-dimensional line $\operatorname{rad}(B)=\mathbb R(e_s+e_t)$, and is a hyperplane. [F1, algebra]

1.3 The radical is fixed pointwise by $W$: for $a=e_s$ or $e_t$ and $v\in\operatorname{rad}(B)$ one has $B(v,a)=0$, so the reflection formula gives $r_a(v)=v$ ([[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (2)); a composite of finitely many maps fixing a vector fixes it, and every element of $W$ is a finite product of the generators $s,t$ and their inverses by [F3]. Hence every element of $W$, in particular $\rho((st)^k)$ for $k\ne0$, fixes $\operatorname{rad}(B)$ pointwise. [F1, F3]

1.4 There are infinitely many reflections: by part (4) of [[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]], $st$ has infinite order in $W$. Put $r:=st$; since $sr^{-1}=sts=rs$, induction gives $sr^{-m}=r^{m}s$ and hence $r^{m}sr^{-m}=r^{2m}s$ for every $m\in\mathbb Z$. Each $r^{2m}s$ lies in the reflection set $T=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]] (2)), and the elements $r^{2m}s$, $m\in\mathbb Z$, are pairwise distinct: if $r^{2m}s=r^{2m'}s$ then right multiplication by $s$ gives $r^{2m}=r^{2m'}$, hence $r^{2(m-m')}=1$ and $m=m'$ because $r$ has infinite order. So $W$ has infinitely many reflections. Moreover $(st)^k$ for $k\ne0$ has infinite order, whereas every conjugate of $s$ or $t$ is an involution; hence these powers are not reflections. [given, algebra]

2.1 The matrices of the two generators in the basis $(e_s,e_t)$ follow from the reflection formula with $2c(s,t)=2$ for $m=\infty$: $r_s(e_s)=-e_s$ and $r_s(e_t)=e_t+2e_s$, and $r_t(e_t)=-e_t$, $r_t(e_s)=e_s+2e_t$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (3)). By step 1.2 the fixed space of $r_s$ is $\ker B(-,e_s)=\{ae_s+be_t:a-b=0\}=\mathbb R(e_s+e_t)=\operatorname{rad}(B)$, and similarly $\operatorname{Fix}(r_t)=\ker B(-,e_t)=\operatorname{rad}(B)$: the two distinct reflections $r_s\ne r_t$ have the same fixed hyperplane. In coordinates [F2]
$$[r_s]=\begin{pmatrix}-1&2\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad [r_t]=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\2&-1\end{pmatrix},$$
differ in their $(1,1)$-entries, so indeed $r_s\ne r_t$ while $\operatorname{Fix}(r_s)=\operatorname{Fix}(r_t)$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Every full fixed space of codimension one of an element of $W$ equals $\operatorname{rad}(B)$. Every element of $W$ is $r^{k}$ or $r^{k}s$ for some $k\in\mathbb Z$ with $r:=st$: repeatedly deleting adjacent equal letters from a word in $s,t$ (using $s^{2}=t^{2}=1$) leaves an alternating word, and the alternating words are $(st)^{k}$, $(st)^{k}s$, $(ts)^{k}=(st)^{-k}$ and $(ts)^{k}t=(st)^{-k-1}s$. For every reflection $waw^{-1}$ of $W$, with $a\in\{s,t\}$, its image is $\rho(waw^{-1})=r_{\rho(w)e_a}$ with $\rho(w)e_a\in\Phi$ ([[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]] (4)); its fixed space is $\ker B(-,\alpha)$, a hyperplane ([[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (2)) containing $\operatorname{rad}(B)$ by step 1.3, hence equal to $\operatorname{rad}(B)$. For the elements $r^{k}$ write $A:=\rho(r)$, whose matrix is the product of the two matrices of step 2.1, $A=\begin{pmatrix}3&-2\\2&-1\end{pmatrix}$; then $N:=A-I=\begin{pmatrix}2&-2\\2&-2\end{pmatrix}$ satisfies $N^{2}=0$, so $A^{k}=(I+N)^{k}=I+kN$ for every $k\in\mathbb Z$ (the case $k=-1$ being $(I+N)^{-1}=I-N$ when $N^{2}=0$), and $(I+kN)v=v$ with $k\ne0$ exactly when $Nv=0$, i.e. exactly when $v\in\ker(A-I)=\{ae_s+be_t:a=b\}=\operatorname{rad}(B)$; the identity $A^{0}=I$ fixes all of $V$, of codimension zero. For the elements $r^{k}s$ one has $(r^{k}s)^{2}=r^{k}(sr^{k}s)=r^{k}r^{-k}=1$ because $srs=r^{-1}$, so $\rho(r^{k}s)$ is an involution; it fixes $\operatorname{rad}(B)$ pointwise by step 1.3, while $\rho(r^{k}s)=A^{k}r_s=(I+kN)r_s=\begin{pmatrix}-1-2k&2+2k\\-2k&1+2k\end{pmatrix}\ne I$, so its fixed space is a proper subspace containing the hyperplane $\operatorname{rad}(B)$, hence equals $\operatorname{rad}(B)$. Therefore the only full fixed space of codimension one of an element of $W$ is $\operatorname{rad}(B)$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion: the assignment "reflection $\mapsto$ its fixed hyperplane" is not injective, since the distinct reflections $r_s\ne r_t$ of step 2.1 have the same image $\operatorname{rad}(B)$; it also cannot be a bijection onto the codimension-one fixed subspaces, since by step 1.4 the set of reflections is infinite while by step 3.1 the set of full fixed spaces of codimension one of elements of $W$ is the singleton $\{\operatorname{rad}(B)\}$. Hence the faithful canonical realization $\rho$ is not reflection faithful in the sense of Elias–Williamson ([[lem-cg-hecke-and-lie-seam-contract-compatibility]] (4)), and any Soergel-calculus or Kazhdan–Lusztig argument invoking reflection faithfulness must supply a realization satisfying that hypothesis separately. The failure is caused by the degeneracy of $B$: the radical is a codimension-one fixed subspace, fixed by non-reflections and by all reflections alike. [F1, step 1.4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Scope and choice: this is a counterexample in the rank-two system $m(s,t)=\infty$; it constructs no Soergel bimodule, no reflection-faithful realization and no Kazhdan–Lusztig object, and it claims nothing beyond the failure of reflection faithfulness for this canonical realization. Every argument is a finite matrix and line computation plus the supplied order, faithfulness and reflection facts, and no choice is used. [given] ∎
