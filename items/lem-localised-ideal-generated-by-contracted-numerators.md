## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, let $S\subseteq R$ be multiplicative and let
$\lambda_S\colon R\to S^{-1}R$, $r\mapsto r/1$, be the localisation map
([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]). Let $J$ be an ideal of
$S^{-1}R$ and let

$$\mathfrak a\;:=\;\lambda_S^{-1}(J)\;=\;\{r\in R\;:\;r/1\in J\}$$

be its contraction. Then $\mathfrak a$ is an ideal of $R$, and for **every**
subset $T\subseteq R$ with $\mathfrak a=(T)$ the ideal $J$ is generated in
$S^{-1}R$ by the image $\lambda_S(T)=\{t/1:t\in T\}$. In particular, if
$\mathfrak a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$ with $n\in\mathbb N$ then
$J=(a_1/1,\ldots,a_n/1)$.

Only the $S$-saturated half of the ideal correspondence is used. Nothing here
says that extension and contraction are mutually inverse on all ideals of $R$;
that is false in general, and it is the contraction of an ideal of $S^{-1}R$,
not an arbitrary ideal of $R$, that this lemma starts from.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a multiplicative subset $S\subseteq R$, an ideal $J$ of $S^{-1}R$, and a subset $T\subseteq R$ generating the contraction $\mathfrak a=\lambda_S^{-1}(J)$.

[L1] A subset $S\subseteq R$ of a commutative ring is **multiplicative** if $1\in S$ and $s,t\in S$ implies $st\in S$; the localisation $S^{-1}R$ has elements $r/s$ with the displayed arithmetic, and the localisation map is the ring homomorphism $\lambda_S:R\to S^{-1}R$, $r\mapsto r/1$ ([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]).

[L2] For a commutative ring $R$ and a multiplicative $S\subseteq R$, extension $I\mapsto S^{-1}I=\{r/s:r\in I,\ s\in S\}$ and contraction $J\mapsto\lambda_S^{-1}(J)$ give inverse inclusion-preserving bijections between $S$-saturated ideals of $R$ and ideals of $S^{-1}R$ ([[thm-ideal-correspondence-for-localisation]]).

[L3] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$; the empty sum is included and equals $0$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L4] For a subset $T$ of a ring, $(T)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals of that ring containing $T$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix the data of the Given line. The set $\mathfrak a=\lambda_S^{-1}(J)$ is the preimage of an ideal under the ring homomorphism $\lambda_S$. [L1, given]

2.1 The contraction of an ideal of $S^{-1}R$ is one of the two directions of the ideal correspondence, so $\mathfrak a$ is an $S$-saturated ideal of $R$, and because extension and contraction are mutually inverse on that class of ideals, its extension recovers $J$: $J=S^{-1}\mathfrak a=\{r/u:r\in\mathfrak a,\ u\in S\}$. [L2, step 1.1]

3.1 Every element of $J$ lies in the ideal generated by the image of $T$. Indeed such an element is $r/u$ with $r\in\mathfrak a=(T)$ and $u\in S$, and $r$ is then a finite sum $r=\sum_{i=1}^{k}c_it_i$ with $c_i\in R$ and $t_i\in T$; dividing by $u$ gives $r/u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(c_i/u)(t_i/1)$, a finite $S^{-1}R$-linear combination of elements of the image of $T$. The empty sum $k=0$ gives $r=0$ and $r/u=0$, which lies in every ideal. [L3, L4, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Conversely each $t/1$ with $t\in T$ lies in $J$, because $t\in\mathfrak a=\lambda_S^{-1}(J)$ says exactly that $\lambda_S(t)=t/1$ belongs to $J$; and $J$ is an ideal, so it contains the ideal generated by that image. [L4, step 2.1]

4.1 The two inclusions of steps 3.1 and 3.2 give $J=(\lambda_S(T))$. Taking $T=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$ finite yields $J=(a_1/1,\ldots,a_n/1)$. [L4, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎

## Remarks

- **What the cited correspondence does and does not give.** It is a bijection between the $S$-saturated ideals of $R$ and all ideals of $S^{-1}R$ ([[thm-ideal-correspondence-for-localisation]]). An arbitrary ideal $\mathfrak b$ of $R$ need not be $S$-saturated, and then $\lambda_S^{-1}(S^{-1}\mathfrak b)$ is strictly larger than $\mathfrak b$. The proof above never applies the correspondence to such a $\mathfrak b$: it starts from an ideal of $S^{-1}R$ and contracts.

- **The generating set is not required to be finite.** The argument uses only that each element of $\mathfrak a$ is a finite $R$-linear combination of elements of $T$; the finite case is stated separately because it is the one the Noetherian application needs.

- **The degenerate localisation is covered.** If $0\in S$ then $S^{-1}R$ is the zero ring, its only ideal is $0$, and the contraction is all of $R$; the conclusion holds because every subset of the zero ring generates its only ideal.
