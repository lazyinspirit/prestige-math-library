## Statement

Let $S$ be finite, $m$ a Coxeter matrix, $W$ the presented Coxeter group with length $\ell$, $V=\mathbb R^S$, the Coxeter form $B$, the canonical reflection homomorphism $\rho$ and the diagram $\Gamma$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]]).

**(1) Classification.** Assume $\Gamma$ is connected. Then $B$ is positive semidefinite of corank one ([[def-cg-irreducible-affine-coxeter-type]] (1)) if and only if $\Gamma$ is isomorphic as a labelled graph to one of the standard affine diagrams $$\tilde A_1;\quad \tilde A_n\ (n\ge2);\quad \tilde B_n\ (n\ge3);\quad \tilde C_n\ (n\ge2);\quad \tilde D_n\ (n\ge4);\quad \tilde E_6,\ \tilde E_7,\ \tilde E_8;\quad \tilde F_4;\quad \tilde G_2$$ ([[def-cg-standard-affine-diagrams]]), with the low-rank coincidences $\tilde A_1=\tilde C_1$, $\tilde B_2=\tilde C_2$, $\tilde D_3=\tilde A_3$, $\tilde E_4=\tilde A_4$, $\tilde E_5=\tilde D_5$; modulo these the list is duplicate-free, $\tilde A_1$ is the only diagram with a label $\infty$, and every other label lies in $\{3,4,6\}$. Equivalently: $\Gamma$ is connected, $B$ is positive semidefinite and $B$ is not positive definite.

**(2) The radical and the affine slice.** For a connected affine $\Gamma$: $\operatorname{rad}(B)=\mathbb R\delta$ for a positive radical vector $\delta$ ([[lem-cg-positive-radical-and-affine-gram-exclusions]] (1)); with the radical quotient $U$, the Euclidean form $b$, the affine slice $E=E_\delta$, the walls $H_s$ and the alcove $A$, the conclusions of [[lem-cg-affine-slice-simplex-and-wall-reflections]] hold: the dual action of $W$ on $E$ is faithful and by affine isometries, $\bar A=\operatorname{conv}\{v_s\}$ is a Euclidean simplex of dimension $|S|-1$ whose facets are the walls $H_s$, $\sum_s\delta_s\varphi(e_s)=1$ on $E$, the facet normals have Gram matrix $C=(B(e_s,e_t))$, distinct alcove interiors are disjoint and $w\bar A\cap\bar A$ is the closed face of type $S(w)$.

**(3) Realization and matching with the crystallographic alcoves.** If $\Gamma$ is one of the standard diagrams $D$, let $\Phi$ be an irreducible finite crystallographic root system of the matching Weyl type supplied by [[lem-cg-affine-type-crystallographic-alcove-diagrams]] (4), with affine Weyl group $W_a(\Phi)=Q^\vee\rtimes W(\Phi)$, fundamental alcove $A_\Phi$ and facet reflections $s_0,s_1,\dots$ ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]]). Then the facet-reflection matrix of $A_\Phi$ equals the Coxeter matrix of $D$ ([[lem-cg-affine-type-crystallographic-alcove-diagrams]] (1)), so $W\cong W(m)\cong W_a(\Phi)$ ([[thm-cg-affine-alcove-transitivity-presentation-and-length]] (2)); $W_a(\Phi)$ acts on its Euclidean space properly discontinuously and cocompactly, $\overline{A_\Phi}$ is a strict fundamental domain, and $W_a(\Phi)$ acts simply transitively on the alcoves, with $\ell(g)$ the number of walls separating $A_\Phi$ from $gA_\Phi$ (clauses (1)-(3) of that theorem). Moreover the inward unit normals of the facets of $A_\Phi$ have the same Gram matrix $C$ as the facet normals of the slice simplex $\bar A$ of (2), with facets matched by the labelling; by [[lem-cg-similar-euclidean-simplices-from-shared-facet-normal-gram]] the two simplices are similar with facets matched, so the similarity intertwines the two reflection group actions. Consequently, for the slice realization of (2): the alcoves $w\bar A$ ($w\in W$) tile $E$, $W$ acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly on $E$ with $\bar A$ a strict fundamental domain, $W$ acts simply transitively on the alcoves, and $\ell(w)$ is the number of walls separating $\bar A$ from $w\bar A$.

**(4) Consequences and conventions.** For connected $\Gamma$: $W$ is finite if and only if $B$ is positive definite ([[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1)); if $\Gamma$ is affine then $W$ is infinite and every proper standard parabolic $W_T$ $(T\subsetneq S)$ is finite ([[lem-cg-positive-radical-and-affine-gram-exclusions]] (2)), and conversely an infinite connected Coxeter system with all proper parabolics finite need not be affine: its form need not be positive semidefinite (the companion page gives an indefinite example). Equivalently, a connected system is of affine form type if and only if it has a faithful Euclidean simplex reflection realization with a bounded fundamental Coxeter chamber, whose interior dihedral angles are $\pi/m_{st}$ and whose facet-reflection matrix is its own Coxeter matrix, the realization being the one constructed in (2)-(3). The facet-generated group of this realization is $Q^\vee\rtimes W(\Phi)$; the extended group $P^\vee\rtimes W(\Phi)$, with $P^\vee$ the coweight lattice and $Q^\vee$ the coroot lattice ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]), strictly contains it when $P^\vee\ne Q^\vee$, and coincides with it when the two lattices are equal. No assertion is made about twisted Lie-theoretic diagrams. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite Coxeter matrix, group, canonical representation, diagram and form of the statement.

[F1] Connected positive-semidefinite non-positive-definite cosine forms have a positive radical ray, corank one and positive-definite proper principal submatrices; every proper standard parabolic is finite ([[lem-cg-positive-radical-and-affine-gram-exclusions]] (1)–(2)).

[F2] Connected affine form type diagrams are exactly the standard affine list, including the all-$3$ cycles, as proved by [[lem-cg-affine-diagram-enumeration]].

[F3] Each standard affine diagram has positive-semidefinite corank-one cosine form, occurs as a crystallographic alcove diagram, and has the listed aliases, labels and facet-normal Gram matrix ([[lem-cg-affine-type-crystallographic-alcove-diagrams]] (1)–(4),(7)).

[F4] The slice action is faithful and isometric, its closure is a bounded simplex with the stated vertices, facet normals, affine relation, facet-reflection action and intersection formula ([[lem-cg-affine-slice-simplex-and-wall-reflections]] (1)–(5)).

[F5] Every standard affine diagram occurs from a finite crystallographic root system of the matching Weyl type ([[lem-cg-affine-type-crystallographic-alcove-diagrams]] (4)).

[F6] Fundamental facet reflections generate the affine Weyl group, their actual Coxeter presentation is exact, the action on alcoves is simply transitive, and length counts separating walls ([[thm-cg-affine-alcove-transitivity-presentation-and-length]] (1)–(3)).

[F7] Bounded Euclidean simplices with the same inward unit-normal Gram matrix admit a facet-matching similarity conjugating their facet reflections ([[lem-cg-similar-euclidean-simplices-from-shared-facet-normal-gram]]).

[F8] Finiteness of a Coxeter group is equivalent to positive definiteness of its form ([[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1)).

[F9] A finite-dimensional real vector space is not a finite union of proper linear subspaces ([[lem-finite-dimensional-space-over-an-infinite-field-is-not-a-finite-union-of-proper-subspaces]]).

[F10] A geometric simplex is the convex hull of finitely many affinely independent vertices ([[def-geometric-simplex-spanned-by-affinely-independent-vertices]]).

[F11] The convex hull of finitely many points in a finite-dimensional real topological vector space is compact ([[lem-locally-convex-closures-and-finite-compact-convex-hulls]]).

[F12] The support criterion is $w\in W_J$ if and only if $S(w)\subseteq J$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (1)).

[F13] The fundamental root alcove is a bounded geometric simplex ([[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]] (2)).

[F14] Finite-dimensional normed spaces are locally compact ([[thm-locally-compact-normed-space-iff-finite-dimensional]]), and in a locally compact metric space every point has arbitrarily small compact closed balls ([[lem-compact-closed-balls-in-a-locally-compact-metric-space]]).

[F15] The affine-wall arrangement is locally finite: every compact set meets only finitely many walls ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]] (3)).

[F16] The affine Weyl group has the Euclidean decomposition $W_a=Q^\vee\rtimes W(\Phi)$ ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]] (4)).

[F17] The Weyl group of a finite crystallographic root system is finite ([[prop-the-weyl-group-is-finite-and-acts-faithfully-on-the-root-system]]).

[F18] The simple coroots form a real basis of the root-system space and integrally generate every coroot, so $Q^\vee=\bigoplus_s\mathbb Z\alpha_s^\vee$ ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]], Remark).

[F19] The real Coxeter form has entries $B(e_s,e_s)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ for finite labels ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] (2)).

[F20] A basis-coordinate map identifies a finite-dimensional normed space with its coordinate space and is continuous ([[thm-coordinate-map-for-a-finite-dimensional-normed-space]]).

[F21] $Q^\vee$ is contained in the coweight lattice $P^\vee$; both are the lattices defined from the roots and coroots ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[F22] $\pi>0$ ([[def-pi-via-first-positive-cosine-zero]]).

[F23] $\cos(2x)=2\cos^2x-1$ for every real $x$ ([[thm-double-angle-and-power-reduction-identities]]).

[F24] $\cos(\pi-x)=-\cos x$ for every real $x$ ([[thm-cofunction-supplementary-and-reflection-identities]]).

[F25] $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ ([[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]]).

[F26] Cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]).

[F27] Every nonnegative real has a unique nonnegative square root ([[thm-of-square-roots]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; all finite coordinate constructions use no choice principle.

1.1 For a connected $\Gamma$ with positive-semidefinite corank-one $B$, [F2] enumerates the standard affine diagram. Conversely [F3] proves that each standard diagram has positive-semidefinite corank-one cosine form and occurs as a crystallographic alcove diagram; a relabelling permutes matrix rows and columns and preserves these properties. The aliases, nonisomorphism and label assertions are also [F3]. Finally [F1] proves that connected positive-semidefinite non-positive-definite is equivalent to corank one, without assuming it in advance. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Under these equivalent conditions, [F1] gives $\operatorname{rad}(B)=\mathbb R\delta$ with all $\delta_s>0$. Apply [F4] to obtain exactly the radical quotient, Euclidean slice, faithful action, coordinates, simplex, normals and intersection conclusions in (2), including rank one. [F1, F4]

1.3 Choose the matching root system from [F5], using the actual affine type and aliases of [F3]. Its bounded fundamental alcove is supplied by [F13], and the inward unit normals have the same Gram matrix as the slice normals by [F3,F4]. The simplex similarity [F7] therefore matches their labelled facets and conjugates the corresponding reflections. By [F6], the fundamental reflections give the Coxeter group of this matrix and generate $W_a(\Phi)$. Thus this similarity intertwines the homomorphisms from $W$ to the two reflection groups and identifies $W$ with $W_a(\Phi)$, preserving its simple generators and lengths. [F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F13]

1.4 The finite criterion is [F8]. For affine $\Gamma$, its form is not positive definite, so $W$ is infinite, while every proper standard parabolic is finite by [F1]. The converse fails already for the triangle with labels $(3,3,4)$. Put $c_3=\cos(\pi/3)$. Since $0<\pi/3<\pi/2$ by [F22], strict decrease [F26] and [F25] give $c_3>0$; [F23] and [F24] give $2c_3^2-1=\cos(2\pi/3)=-c_3$, so $(2c_3-1)(c_3+1)=0$ and $c_3=1/2$. Put $c_4=\cos(\pi/4)$. Again $0<\pi/4<\pi/2$ gives $c_4>0$, and [F23,F25] give $2c_4^2-1=0$; by [F27], $c_4=\sqrt2/2$. Therefore the displayed Coxeter matrix [F19] has quadratic value $3+2(-c_3-c_3-c_4)=1-\sqrt2<0$ on $(1,1,1)$, while its value on each basis vector is $1$; the strict inequality follows from $(1-\sqrt2)(1+\sqrt2)=-1$ and $1+\sqrt2>0$. Every proper two-generator form has determinant $1-\cos^2(\pi/m)>0$ for $m=3,4$, so its group is finite by [F8]; the whole group is infinite by that same criterion. Thus infiniteness together with finite proper parabolics cannot replace positive semidefiniteness. [F1, F8, F19, F22, F23, F24, F25, F26, F27, algebra]

2.1 Every point $x$ of the root-system Euclidean space lies in the closure of an alcove. By [F14] choose a compact closed ball $K$ about $x$; by [F15] only finitely many walls meet $K$. The finitely many walls in $K$ not containing $x$ have positive distance from $x$, so a smaller open ball $U$ about $x$ misses all of them. The direction hyperplanes of the finitely many walls through $x$, together with the zero subspace, are proper linear subspaces because $\dim E\ge1$. By [F9] choose a nonzero direction $v$ outside their union. For all sufficiently small $\varepsilon>0$, the point $x+\varepsilon v$ lies in $U$ and lies on none of the walls through $x$, so the short ray lies in one connected component of the wall complement. Its closure contains $x$. By [F6], every alcove is a translate of $A_\Phi$, so the alcove closures cover the space. Their interiors are disjoint because they are distinct connected components. Hence the transported slice closures tile $E$. [F6, F9, F14, F15, step 1.3, choose, algebra]

3.1 The slice closed alcove meets each orbit in exactly one point. Coverage gives existence. If $x,y\in\bar A$ and $y=w x$, then $y\in w\bar A\cap\bar A$; [F4]'s intersection formula gives $y(e_s)=0$ for every $s\in S(w)$. Every corresponding facet reflection fixes $y$, and [F12] gives $w\in W_{S(w)}$, so $w y=y$. Consequently $x=w^{-1}y=y$. This proves the strict closed fundamental-domain assertion, which transfers to $\overline{A_\Phi}$. Simple transitivity on open alcoves and the separating-wall length formula transfer from [F6]; similarity sends the entire family of reflected walls bijectively to the slice walls, so it preserves which walls separate two alcoves. [F4, F6, F12, step 1.3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 By [F16], each affine Weyl element has a unique form $t_q u$ with $q\in Q^\vee$ and $u\in W(\Phi)$. If $(t_q u)K\cap K\ne\varnothing$ for a compact $K$, then $q\in K-uK$. There are finitely many $u$ by [F17]. A compact subset of a metric space is bounded (cover it by unit balls and take a finite subcover), and $u$ preserves the metric, so each $K-uK$ is bounded. By [F18], the simple coroots form a finite basis and integrally generate $Q^\vee$; after a finite enumeration, each basis-coordinate function is linear and continuous by [F20]. Its values on $K$ and on $uK$ are bounded: continuity gives a neighborhood of each point on which the coordinate differs by less than $1$ from its value at that point, and compactness gives a finite subcover and hence a finite bound. Since $u$ is an isometry, $uK$ is compact. Linearity then bounds the coordinate of each difference $x-uy$ by the sum of the two coordinate bounds, so the coordinates of $K-uK$ are bounded. Since $Q^\vee$ consists of integer coordinate tuples in this basis, only finitely many $q$ occur. Thus only finitely many affine Weyl elements meet $K$ back to itself, proving proper discontinuity. By [F13], $\overline{A_\Phi}$ is a geometric simplex, hence the convex hull of finitely many vertices by [F10]; it is compact by [F11]. Coverage by its translates proves cocompactness. Both properties transfer under the similarity in Step 1.3. [F10, F11, F13, F16, F17, F18, F20, step 1.3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $|S|=1$, then $B=[1]$ is positive definite. No faithful realization by reflections in facets of a bounded Euclidean simplex is possible: a positive-dimensional bounded simplex has at least two facets, whereas a zero-dimensional simplex has no codimension-one reflecting hyperplane. Thus both sides of the stated equivalence are false in this case. Otherwise suppose the stated faithful Euclidean realization has a bounded fundamental Coxeter simplex of dimension $n\ge1$, with its $n+1=|S|$ facets indexed by $S$ and labelled angles $\pi/m_{st}$ (and disjoint endpoints in dimension one). Its inward unit-normal Gram matrix is the cosine matrix: in a two-dimensional normal section through a codimension-two face, the angle between inward normals is $\pi-\pi/m_{st}$; in dimension one the two normals are opposite. The normals span the $n$-dimensional direction space, since otherwise a nonzero perpendicular direction would leave all facet inequalities unchanged and make the simplex unbounded. There are $n+1$ facets, so the Gram matrix is positive semidefinite of rank $n$, hence corank one. This proves the reverse Euclidean implication; Steps 1.2–3.2 prove the forward one. The facet-generated translation subgroup is exactly $Q^\vee$ by [F16]. Therefore adjoining translations in $P^\vee$ gives a strictly larger group if $P^\vee\ne Q^\vee$, and gives the same group if the lattices are equal by [F21]. No twisted-diagram claim is made. All arguments use finite bases and finite avoidance, without Choice. [F10, F16, F21, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
