## Example

In $G=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,2,3\})$, let
$N=\langle(123)\rangle$. Then

$$
N=\{\operatorname{id},(123),(132)\}
$$

is normal, and $G/N$ is the two-element group
$\{N,(12)N\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Permutations are composed from right to left.

[L1] The symmetric group on $\{1,2,3\}$ is a group under composition ([[def-symmetric-group]], [[lem-symmetric-group-is-a-group]]).

[F1] The subgroup generated by an element consists of its integral powers ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F2] The index $[G:N]$ is the cardinality of the left-coset set ([[def-index]]).

[L2] A subgroup of index two is normal ([[thm-index-two-subgroup-is-normal]]).

[L3] A normal subgroup gives a quotient group under coset multiplication ([[thm-quotient-group-laws]]).

[L4] A quotient with finite index has order equal to that index ([[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $(123)^2=(132)$ and $(123)^3=\operatorname{id}$, [F1] gives $N=\{\operatorname{id},(123),(132)\}$. [L1, F1, algebra]

2.1 The remaining three permutations are the transpositions, and $(12)N=\{(12),(23),(13)\}$. Thus $N$ and $(12)N$ are the two left cosets of $N$ in $G$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Consequently $[G:N]=2$ by [F2], so $N\trianglelefteq G$ by [L2]. [step 2.1, F2, L2]

4.1 The quotient group therefore exists by [L3], and its underlying coset set is precisely $\{N,(12)N\}$. Its order is two by [L4]. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L3, L4] ∎
