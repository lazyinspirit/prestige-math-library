## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $X$ be a smooth projective integral variety over an algebraically closed field $k$, and choose $e\in X(k)$. The rigidified Picard functor is represented by a separated locally finite-type group scheme $P$ with a universal rigidified line bundle. Its identity component $P^0$ is an open and closed finite-type subgroup scheme and is proper. The reduced identity component $(P^0)_{red}$ is a smooth connected proper group scheme, hence an abelian variety. Its $k$-points are exactly the subgroup of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ generated by differences of fibres in connected finite-type families of line bundles on $X$.

The full group scheme $P^0$ is not asserted to be reduced or smooth. In positive characteristic its nonreduced structure must not be identified with an abelian variety.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, DC, the smooth projective integral variety, algebraically closed field and chosen point in the Statement.

[F1] The full rigidified Picard functor of a projective integral variety over an algebraically closed field is represented by a separated locally finite-type group scheme with universal rigidified line bundle ([[lem-general-rigidified-picard-functor-is-representable]]).

[F2] Smooth schemes over a perfect field are regular; regular local rings are UFDs, and on a locally factorial integral Noetherian scheme the Cartier and Weil divisor groups agree and Picard classes are Weil divisor classes ([[thm-ag-perfect-field-jacobian-regularity]], [[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]], [[thm-cartier-weil-isomorphism-locally-factorial]], [[thm-cartier-divisors-mod-principal-to-picard]]).

[F3] A smooth commutative affine group over an algebraically closed field is trigonalizable; its smooth connected unipotent part has a series with $\mathbb G_a$ quotients, and the group splits as that part times a torus ([[prop-smooth-commutative-algebraic-groups-are-trigonalizable]], [[lem-smooth-trigonalizable-group-normal-series-refinement]], [[thm-trigonalizable-extensions-split-over-algebraically-closed-fields]]).

[F4] A finite-type multiplicative-type group splits over a finite separable field extension, and diagonalizable groups correspond contravariantly to their finitely generated character groups ([[lem-multiplicative-type-groups-split-separably]], [[lem-diagonalizable-character-antiequivalence]]).

[F5] A smooth connected separated finite-type group over a perfect field with no nontrivial smooth connected affine normal subgroup is proper, hence an abelian variety ([[thm-nonaffine-pseudo-abelian-perfect-field-is-complete]], [[def-abelian-variety-over-a-field]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use the rigidified Picard scheme, rule out affine one-parameter subgroups by a divisor calculation, and apply pseudo-abelian completeness.

1.1 By [F1] the full rigidified Picard functor is represented by a separated locally finite-type group scheme $P$ with universal line bundle. Because $k$ is perfect, the product of reduced finite-type $k$-schemes is reduced; consequently multiplication and inversion factor through the reduction $P_{red}$, making it a group scheme. The connected component $G=(P_{red})^0$ of the identity is open and closed in the locally Noetherian topological space $P_{red}$, and is a subgroup: products of connected $k$-schemes remain connected over algebraically closed $k$. A reduced finite-type $k$-scheme has a nonempty smooth locus on every irreducible component by [F2]. Translation by a $k$-point takes a smooth point of $P_{red}$ to any other closed point; the nonsmooth locus has no closed point and is empty. Thus $P_{red}$ is smooth. Its regular local rings are domains, so distinct irreducible components are disjoint; connected $G$ is irreducible. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Let $T$ be either $\mathbb A^1_k$ or $\mathbb G_{m,k}$ and put $Y=X\times T$. Smoothness makes $Y$ regular. By [F2], $Y$ is locally factorial and line bundles on it are represented by Weil divisors. For a line bundle $L$ on $Y$, its restriction to the generic fibre over $X$ is trivial: the coordinate ring $k(X)[t]$ or $k(X)[t,t^{-1}]$ is a principal ideal domain. Choose a rational trivializing section with no zeros or poles on that generic fibre. Its divisor is supported over a proper closed subset of $X$. Every codimension-one component of that support is $D\times T$ for a prime divisor $D\subset X$: its image has codimension one because all projection fibres have dimension one, and the generic fibre over $D$ is the integral curve $T_{k(D)}$. Thus the divisor is pulled back from a Weil divisor $D_0$ on $X$. By [F2], $D_0$ is Cartier and $L\cong\operatorname{pr}_X^*\mathcal O_X(D_0)$. This proves $\operatorname{Pic}(X\times T)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ via pullback. [F2, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose a nonempty affine open $U\subseteq G$. For every $g\in G(k)$, the nonempty opens $U$ and $gU$ meet because $G$ is irreducible; their intersection has a $k$-point, so $g$ lies in the image of $U\times U\to G$, $(u,v)\mapsto uv^{-1}$. On every finite-type open of $G$ this image is constructible. A nonempty constructible subset of a finite-type scheme over algebraically closed $k$ contains a closed $k$-point, so the image is all of $G$. Since $U\times U$ is quasi-compact, $G$ is quasi-compact and hence finite type over $k$. The identity component $P^0$ of $P$ has the same underlying space as $G$, so it is also open and closed and finite type; its reduction is exactly $G$. [given, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

2.2 Let $u:T\to P$ be any morphism with $T=\mathbb A^1$ or $\mathbb G_m$. Pull back the universal rigidified bundle from [F1] to obtain $L$ on $X\times T$. By step 1.2 its class is pulled back from $X$. The rigidification changes no represented class: $\operatorname{Pic}(T)=0$ for the principal ideal rings $k[t]$ and $k[t,t^{-1}]$, and [F1] identifies rigidified classes with the relative Picard quotient. Hence all $k$-points of $T$ map to the same point of $P$. The equalizer with that constant map is closed because $P$ is separated, and contains all closed points of the reduced finite-type curve $T$; it is all of $T$. Thus every morphism $\mathbb A^1\to P$ and $\mathbb G_m\to P$ is constant. [F1, given, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Suppose that the smooth connected commutative finite-type group $G$ has a nontrivial smooth connected affine subgroup $H$. By [F3], $H$ is trigonalizable and splits into its smooth connected unipotent part $U$ and a torus. If $U\ne1$, the last nontrivial term in its [F3] normal series is a subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb G_a$. If $U=1$, then $H$ is a nontrivial torus; by [F4] it splits over algebraically closed $k$ and its finitely generated character group is torsion-free because $H$ is smooth and connected. Hence $H\cong\mathbb G_m^r$ with $r>0$ and contains $\mathbb G_m$. In either case the subgroup inclusion is a nonconstant morphism from $\mathbb A^1$ or $\mathbb G_m$ to $P$, contradicting step 2.2. Thus $G$ has no nontrivial smooth connected affine subgroup. Since $G$ is commutative, every subgroup is normal. [F3, F4, given, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 By [F5], the smooth connected separated finite-type group $G$ is proper and is an abelian variety. The closed immersion $G=(P^0)_{red}\hookrightarrow P^0$ is a nilpotent thickening because $P^0$ is Noetherian. After any base change a nilpotent thickening is a homeomorphism on underlying spaces; thus universal closedness of $G\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ implies universal closedness of $P^0\to\operatorname{Spec}k$. The latter map is finite type and separated by steps 1.1 and 2.1, hence proper. The universal rigidified bundle is already supplied by [F1], and the $k$-points of $P^0$ and its reduction agree. This establishes properness without identifying a possibly nonreduced $P^0$ with the abelian variety $G$. [F1, F5, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 A line bundle $L$ on $X\times T$ for connected finite-type $T$ becomes rigidified after tensoring by the inverse pullback of its restriction to $e\times T$. By [F1] this normalized family gives a morphism $T\to P$. The differences of any two $k$-fibres lie in $P^0(k)$ because the image of connected $T$ is connected. Conversely, for any $x\in P^0(k)$ the universal rigidified bundle on the connected finite-type parameter scheme $P^0$ supplies a family whose fibre difference between $x$ and the identity is the corresponding Picard class. Hence the subgroup generated by all such fibre differences is exactly $P^0(k)=G(k)$. [F1, given, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
