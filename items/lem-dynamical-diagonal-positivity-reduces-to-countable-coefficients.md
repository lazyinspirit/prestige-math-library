## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $L$ be a complete algebraically closed nontrivially nonarchimedean valued field, and let $F$ be a homogeneous lift of a rational map $\phi$ of degree at least two. There is a complete algebraically closed nontrivially valued subfield $L_0\subset L$ containing the coefficients of $F$, with a countable dense subfield. Its Berkovich projective line is compact metrizable. If $\phi$ has genuinely bad reduction over $L$, it has genuinely bad reduction over $L_0$. If its Green diagonal is strictly positive at every point over $L_0$, then it is strictly positive at every point over $L$. Thus a proof of strict diagonal positivity for maps with genuinely bad reduction over all such countable models suffices for arbitrary $L$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, lift and map in the statement.

[F1] Completion of an algebraically closed valued field is algebraically closed. ([[lem-completion-of-algebraically-closed-valued-field-is-algebraically-closed]])

[F2] Potential good reduction persists under isometric embeddings between complete valued fields. ([[lem-potential-good-reduction-valued-extension-and-completion-descent]])

[F3] Restriction of seminorms gives the Green diagonal inequality across complete algebraically closed valued extensions. ([[lem-berkovich-kernel-under-complete-algebraically-closed-extension]])

[F4] The seminorm projective line is a compact Hausdorff two-disc quotient. Values on linear polynomials determine a disc seminorm, and the circle has an inversion involution. ([[lem-berkovich-projective-line-compactness-and-classical-density]], [[lem-berkovich-disc-nested-balls-and-hsia-kernel]], [[lem-berkovich-dynamical-green-kernel-continuous-extension]])

[A1] Full Choice supplies the countable enumerations and the completed algebraic coefficient field; it includes Countable Choice. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 Choose one nonunit $b\in L^\times$ and let $K$ be the field generated by $b$ and the finitely many coefficients of $F$ over the prime field. It is countable, since expressions in finitely many generators and rational operations form a countable set. Let $A$ be the set of elements of $L$ algebraic over $K$. It is an algebraic closure of $K$ inside $L$: any polynomial with coefficients in $A$ has its coefficients in a finite extension of $K$, and each of its roots in the algebraically closed field $L$ is algebraic over that extension, hence over $K$. Moreover $A$ is countable, because $K$ has countably many polynomials and each nonzero polynomial has finitely many roots. Its completion embeds isometrically into the complete field $L$ as its closure; call that subfield $L_0$. By [F1] it is algebraically closed. It contains $b$ and is therefore nontrivially valued, and $A$ is a countable dense subfield. [F1, A1, algebra]

1.2 Each unit seminorm on $L_0[T]$ is determined by its values on $T-a$ for the countably many $a\in A$ with $|a|\le1$. Indeed these centers are dense in the unit disc, and the strong inequality in both directions gives $|x(T-a)-x(T-a')|\le|a-a'|$. Thus any two distinct disc points differ at one of these coordinates. The disc embeds continuously into the countable cube $[0,1]^{\mathbb N}$ and is homeomorphic to its image by compactness and Hausdorffness in [F4]. Hence each disc has a countable base. Their disjoint union $Y$ has a countable base, and the quotient map $p:Y\to X_{L_0}$ is closed, as proved for this quotient in [F4]. Each fiber has at most two points. For any quotient-open $O$ and $x\in O$, cover the finite fiber $p^{-1}(x)$ by a finite union $V$ of basis members with $p^{-1}(x)\subset V\subset p^{-1}(O)$. Then $X_{L_0}\setminus p(Y\setminus V)$ is open, contains $x$ and is contained in $O$. These sets over the countably many finite basis unions give a countable quotient base. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 To obtain a compatible metric explicitly, take the countably many spherical-kernel slices at the disc points $x_{a,r}$ with $a\in A$, $|a|\le1$, and $r\in|A^\times|\cap(0,1]$, in both charts. Their $-\log$ slices are bounded continuous functions by [F4]. They separate two points in a common chart: choose a center in $A$ whose linear values differ, then a value-group radius strictly between those values; the two kernel values at that disc point are the maximum of that radius and the respective linear values, hence differ. If two points are in the strict interiors of different charts, a small disc about zero in the first chart gives kernel value less than1 at the first point and value1 at the other. Thus the resulting family $(f_i)$ separates all projective points. If $M_i$ bounds $|f_i|$, the map into the countable cube with coordinates $f_i/(1+M_i)$ is continuous and injective, hence is a homeomorphism onto its image by compactness and Hausdorffness. The pullback of the metric $\sum_i2^{-i}|u_i-v_i|$ is a compatible metric. [F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.1 If $\phi$ had potentially good reduction over $L_0$, [F2] would give it over $L$, so genuinely bad reduction over $L$ implies the same over $L_0$. Finally [F3] gives $g_{\phi,L}(z,z)\ge g_{\phi,L_0}(rz,rz)$ for every point $z$; strict positivity on the smaller line implies strict positivity on the larger. This proves the reduction while retaining all new larger-field points, including those not in the section image. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
