## Statement

Let $E/F$ and $E'/F$ be finite subextensions of a common field. Then their compositum is finite and

$$[EE':F]\le [E:F][E':F].$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite subextensions $E/F$ and $E'/F$ inside a field $\Omega$.

[L1] The compositum $EE'$ is the smallest subfield of $\Omega$ containing $E\cup E'$ ([[cor-composite-of-two-subfields]]).

[L2] Extension degree is the size of a finite basis ([[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]).

[L3] Assuming the Axiom of Choice, if $S\subseteq V$ spans $V$ then there is a basis $B$ of $V$ with $B\subseteq S$ ([[cor-every-spanning-set-contains-a-basis]]).

[L4] Every finite extension is algebraic ([[thm-finite-field-extensions-are-algebraic]]).

[L5] A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements is finite over the base ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose $F$-bases $(u_1,\ldots,u_m)$ of $E$ and $(v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ of $E'$. The $F$-span $A$ of the $mn$ products $u_iv_j$ contains $E$ and $E'$ and is closed under addition and multiplication, because products are reduced separately in the two bases. [given, L2, algebra]

2.1 By [L4], every $u_i$ and $v_j$ is algebraic over $F$, so [L5] makes $EE'=F(u_1,\ldots,u_m,v_1,\ldots,v_n)$ finite over $F$. Every $r\in A\subseteq EE'$ is therefore algebraic over $F$. [step 1.1, L1, L4, L5]

3.1 If $0\ne r\in A$, take a nonzero annihilating polynomial, factor out its largest power of $t$, and cancel the corresponding nonzero power of $r$ in the ambient field. This gives $c_0+c_1r+\cdots+c_dr^d=0$ with $c_0\ne0$. Then $r^{-1}=-c_0^{-1}(c_1+c_2r+\cdots+c_dr^{d-1})\in A$, so $A$ is a field. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Since $A$ is a field containing $E\cup E'$, [L1] gives $EE'\subseteq A$; the reverse inclusion is clear from the product span, so $A=EE'$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L1]

5.1 The $mn$ products span $EE'$. By [L3] they contain a basis of at most $mn$ elements, so [L2] yields $[EE':F]\le mn=[E:F][E':F]$. [step 4.1, L2, L3] ∎
