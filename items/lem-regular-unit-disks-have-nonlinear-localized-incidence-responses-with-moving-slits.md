## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $u_0,p_0:S_0\to\overline{\mathbb H},f_0:S_0\to\Sigma$ satisfy the complete geometric hypotheses of the moving-slit kernel lemma, including that the marked roots are not slit tips and inactive constants miss the active image. Suppose its ACTUAL scalar pole kernel has already been proved to equal $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p_0\}$. Choose $q\in S_0$ with $z_0=p_0(q)$ outside every branch value, and a small target-root disk $U$ about $f_0(q)$, identified with a disk about $q$ by $f_0$, on which $p_0$ is injective. Choose nested smaller disks and a compact $K\Subset U\setminus\{q\}$. Let $Z$ be a real Hilbert space and let $g:Z\to C^\infty_K(U;\Lambda^{0,1})$ be a specified bounded linear smoothing map: each fixed $C^r$ norm of $g(h)$ is bounded by $C_r\|h\|_Z$.

For $h$ in a neighborhood of zero there is a unique nearby node-fixed map $u_h$ in an actual $H^3$ disk-map chart satisfying the equations constructed below and the fixed-source-point condition that its selected simple root at $z_0$ equals $f_0(q)$. Its active incidence surface $S_h$ has the same labelled proper slits, whose endpoints and regular marked banks move smoothly with $h$. The active root map identifies it with its target image. The inverse root graph $p_h$ solves
$$\bar\partial_w p_h=g(h)(w)$$
on $U$, is holomorphic off $K$, and satisfies $p_h(q)=z_0$. It has the original real boundary values and simple boundary poles at its marked roots, not artificial poles at fixed slit tips. The inactive roots remain their fixed node values. Every fixed higher Sobolev topology and the actual local incidence and tip charts depend smoothly on $h$; on smaller fixed neighborhoods their response maps have bounded derivatives of each prescribed finite order after shrinking the parameter neighborhood. At $h=0$ this is the given disk.

In particular take a negative Hardy datum $G$ at $q$, use its actual outside Laurent extension $E_G$ and a cutoff $\chi$ whose derivative is supported in $K$, and put $g(h)=-\bar\partial(\chi E_G)$. This forcing is a bounded smoothing map from each fixed Hardy Sobolev space, by the separation of $K$ from the inner trace circle. Then on the active component with the CLOSED inner trace disk deleted
$$p_G^{\rm out}=p_h+\chi E_G$$
is genuinely holomorphic, has exactly the specified incoming negative Laurent trace, and its regular part near $q$ is the holomorphic function $p_h-p_0$, with value zero at $q$. Its other boundary poles and slit endpoints are those of the actual deformed map $u_h$. Thus this is a nonlinear component response with moving slits, rather than addition of fixed-tip Laurent poles to a nonlinear covering.

No statement here proves target-neck exhaustion, constructs an initial regular disk, or computes a count. The two-dimensional kernel condition is a verified input for a previously constructed component, not an assumption about all unit disks.

## Proof

1.1 Choose nested physical disks $V'\Subset V$ about $z_0$ avoiding every branch value and the physical boundary, so the selected root $v_0:V\to U$ is a holomorphic local diffeomorphism and all other roots there have separated target supports. Shrink the forcing support so $v_0^{-1}(K)\Subset V'$, and retain a still smaller target disk about $q$ on which every sufficiently $C^1$-close $v$ is injective with a common image neighborhood containing $K\cup\{q\}$. This follows by the inverse derivative bound and segment integration after composing with $v_0^{-1}$ on the chosen convex target disk. In the actual node-fixed $H^3$ map chart of [F2], selected-root coordinates therefore give a function $v(z)$ near $v_0$, with positive real Jacobian and a $C^1$ inverse. The value $v(z_0)$ is a smooth function of the map chart, since evaluation at one FIXED physical point is bounded and the root coordinate is smooth. We impose $v(z_0)=f_0(q)$, not a claimed smooth inverse-evaluation action on arbitrary rough maps. On $V$, replace the selected root's Cauchy--Riemann component by $$\bar\partial_z v+g(h)(v)\bigl(|v_z|^2-|v_{\bar z}|^2\bigr).$$ All other root components and all components outside this patch remain the ordinary equation. The new term vanishes on a common collar of $\partial V$, because $g(h)$ is supported in $K$ and all nearby maps send that collar outside $K$. It therefore defines a genuine global section, using the simple-root target coordinates on this patch. At $h=0$ it is exactly the ordinary holomorphic section, up to its harmless fixed factor two convention. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 This is an actual smooth $H^3\times Z\to H^2$ section. First derivatives of $v$ belong to $H^2$, which is an algebra in two dimensions. The smoothing hypothesis gives every coefficient derivative of $g(h)$ bounded by the parameter norm, so the explicit composition and Taylor estimates in [F2] prove smoothness of $g(h)(v)$ to every fixed order, jointly in $v,h$. Products with the displayed quadratic derivative term have the same bounds. At $(u_0,0)$ its map derivative is the ordinary node-fixed disk linearization. Append the two real equations $v(z_0)=f_0(q)$. Their derivative on its kernel is an isomorphism: by [F1], the kernel variation corresponds to $a+bp_0$, and its selected root value at $z_0$ is $-(a+bp_0(q))/p_{0,w}(q)$; $p_{0,w}(q)\ne0$ and $\operatorname{Im}p_0(q)>0$. Consequently the augmented derivative is invertible. Explicitly first solve the operator with its bounded kernel-complement inverse, then correct the two phase values by those two actual kernel sections. The actual parameter Newton theorem in [F2] now supplies a unique smooth map $h\mapsto u_h$ in one small $H^3$ neighborhood satisfying both the section and the fixed-point equations. In particular $\|u_h-u_0\|_{H^3}\le C\|h\|_Z$ after shrinking the ball. No unproved scalar component inverse has been used. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 On the selected root patch let $p_h$ be the real local inverse of $v_h$. The elementary inverse matrix formula in complex notation is $$ (p_h)_{\bar w}=-\frac{(v_h)_{\bar z}}{|(v_h)_z|^2-|(v_h)_{\bar z}|^2}\circ p_h.$$ The denominator is positive by step1.1. Substituting the actual modified equation gives $(p_h)_{\bar w}=g(h)(w)$ exactly. On a smaller disk the inverse belongs to $H^3$: differentiate its inverse identity through order three, use the bounded first derivative inverses, $H^1\subset L^4$ for the two second-derivative factors, and change variables by its bounded positive Jacobian to bound the remaining $L^2$ terms. Smooth approximants and the local inverse derivative bound justify these weak identities; their inverses converge uniformly and in the same weak derivative formulas. Since $g(h)$ is smooth, the plane Cauchy transform of a compactly supported extension has this same $\bar\partial$, so the difference is weakly holomorphic and hence smooth by the local estimates in [F2]. This makes $p_h$ smooth on smaller disks, with each fixed order bounded by its $H^3$ norm and the corresponding finite forcing norms, using [F3]. The ordinary Euclidean inverse theorem then makes $v_h$ smooth there. Elsewhere $u_h$ solves the ordinary holomorphic equation, including the physical boundary and node, so [F2]'s actual weak regularity makes it smooth everywhere. The phase equation gives $p_h(q)=z_0$ exactly. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Higher parameter smoothness does not assume differentiability of Sobolev inversion at a fixed rough order. Use higher map spaces $H^{k+1}\to H^k$, $k\ge2$, whose actual composition and algebra bounds follow from [F2]. At a solved map the augmented derivative is invertible at level three by continuity and Neumann inversion. Its inverse preserves every higher order: on the forced patch, differentiate the displayed inverse-root identity, which converts its principal equation to the scalar $\bar\partial$ equation for the variation at fixed target $w$; the coordinate maps are now smooth by step3.1. The inhomogeneity has exactly the given forcing order. On the complementary patches it is the ordinary geometric disk linearization with the real boundary trace, so [F2]'s local interior/boundary weak estimates apply. A finite patch cover gives the higher estimate with the already controlled lower norm, and the phase is finite dimensional. Thus every higher forcing has a unique higher solution with a bounded inverse. Apply the actual parameter implicit theorem at that higher order at each solved point. Inclusion in the lower map chart and its uniqueness identify the higher solution with $u_h$. Hence the SAME family is smooth in every fixed higher topology locally in $h$. In particular every fixed finite number of source and parameter derivatives is bounded on a sufficiently small parameter neighborhood. This proves the needed higher control without asserting smoothness of the inversion action on all rough $H^3$ diffeomorphisms. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 We justify the global incidence and slit assertions. Off the forced root patch the coefficients are holomorphic and close in every fixed higher norm to the original ones. Near each interior cover branch the original incidence curve has equation $z-p_0(w)=0$ times a holomorphic unit with nonzero $z$ derivative. The nearby coefficient curve consequently has a unique graph $z=p_h(w)$ there by the actual implicit inverse, so its root map is still a local biholomorphism. On the forced patch it is the graph of the orientation-positive inverse from step3.1. On the remaining simple-root patches the same statement follows by continuity. The inactive roots solve the ordinary holomorphic equation with fixed real boundary tangent circle and fixed node value; as actual root maps their images stay in one small holomorphic circle chart, so the imaginary-part gradient identity makes them the original constants. Near the physical boundary all roots are distinct and holomorphic, with real analytic circle values. The derivative of a labelled root has exactly the original finitely many simple zeros in the reflected tip disks: the holomorphic local inverse theorem for its derivative, and a lower bound on the remaining compact part of each disk, give one simple zero there; reflection makes that zero real. Equivalently the circle coefficient bound and its local power factor exclude any additional zero. On the compact boundary remainder the derivative stays nonzero. Thus no new INTERIOR root critical point is hidden near a tip. Thus each old proper slit has its two nearby simple tips and the same regular marked bank. The finite point-incidence degrees are unchanged by the small boundary-preserving map homotopy. All active local root maps are orientation preserving, including on the forced patch. Their actual topological degree over every open cut region is therefore still zero or one. Two different interior preimages, or an interior and boundary-bank preimage, would duplicate an open off-circle region and contradict this degree identity, exactly as in the unit-incidence proof. Hence the active root remains globally one-to-one in the interior; the bank/tip classification gives the same labelled slit image. Its near-tip coordinates have $w=a_h+\zeta_h^2$, obtained by factoring the simple derivative zero and taking the local square root of its nonzero holomorphic unit. Their coefficients depend smoothly on $h$ by step4.1 and the fixed-contour Cauchy formula. Away from tips use the target root charts; near tips use these actual source coordinates. They give a finite smooth family of incidence and boundary charts. All marked root coordinates are unchanged by the physical node constraint, and they stay off the tips. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally let $g(h)=-\bar\partial(\chi E_G)$ as in the Statement. Its forcing support lies in the fixed annulus $K$. In the chosen puncture coordinate, on that support every differentiated Laurent term is a polynomial in its index times a geometrically decreasing factor, because its radius is strictly larger than the incoming trace circle. Cauchy--Schwarz therefore bounds every $C^r$ norm by the fixed Hardy norm of $G$; this is exactly the required bounded smoothing map. On the component with that closed inner trace disk deleted, the sum $p_h+\chi E_G$ has zero $\bar\partial$ by step3.1 and is holomorphic elsewhere by step5.1. In the inner collar where $\chi=1$, the negative Laurent coefficients are exactly those of $G$; $p_h$ is holomorphic through $q$, so contributes only the regular part, with $p_h(q)=p_0(q)$. On all other boundaries $\chi=0$, so the actual moved slits and marked poles are those of $u_h$. Higher response estimates on the fixed puncture neighborhoods follow from steps3.1--4.1; the endpoint geometry likewise follows from the actual smooth tip charts in step5.1. The fixed-tip poles in the LINEAR variation are thus the derivatives of genuine moving-endpoint geometries, not poles inserted into a nonlinear disk cover. The construction requires an actually verified regular old component and proves its response; it supplies neither a base disk nor degeneration exhaustion or a global count. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
