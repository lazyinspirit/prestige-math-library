## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), hence also the Axiom of
Dependent Choice ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]],
[[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field and let $C$ be a normal proper
curve over $k$ ([[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]]), with function field
$K=k(C)$. Then for every $f\in K^{\times}$ the principal Weil divisor
$$\operatorname{div}_W(f)=\sum_{Z}\operatorname{ord}_Z(f)\,[Z]$$
of [[def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group]] is a finite integral
combination of closed points of $C$, and its $k$-degree
([[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]]) vanishes:
$$\deg_k\operatorname{div}_W(f)=\sum_{x\in C\text{ closed}}\operatorname{ord}_x(f)\,[\kappa(x):k]=0 .$$
The order $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)$ is the normalized valuation of $f$ in the
discrete valuation ring $\mathcal O_{C,x}$
([[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]]). No smoothness,
projectivity or separability hypothesis is imposed, and $f$ may be constant.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a normal proper curve $C$ over $k$ with generic point
$\eta$ and function field $K=\mathcal O_{C,\eta}=k(C)$, the Axiom of Choice, and
an element $f\in K^{\times}$.

[F1] $C$ is an integral, proper, one-dimensional $k$-scheme of finite type over
$k$, hence Noetherian; it is a normal locally Noetherian integral scheme. Its
prime divisors are exactly its closed points, and for every closed point $x$
the local ring $\mathcal O_{C,x}$ is a discrete valuation ring with fraction
field $K$, whose normalized valuation at $f$ is $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)$; in
particular $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)=0$ if and only if $f$ is a unit of
$\mathcal O_{C,x}$. The residue field $\kappa(x)$ is finite over $k$
([[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]],
[[def-weil-divisor-normal-noetherian-scheme]],
[[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]],
[[thm-height-one-localisation-of-normal-noetherian-domain-is-dvr]]).

[F2] The principal Weil divisor $\operatorname{div}_W(f)=\sum_Z
\operatorname{ord}_Z(f)[Z]$, summed over the prime divisors $Z$ of $C$, is a
well-defined element of the free abelian group $\operatorname{Div}(C)$
generated by the prime divisors, and on the integral curve $C$ it is the
finite sum $\sum_x\operatorname{ord}_x(f)[x]$ over the closed points $x$; its
$k$-degree is computed coefficientwise as $\sum_x
\operatorname{ord}_x(f)[\kappa(x):k]$, which is a finite sum with values in
$\mathbb Z$ ([[def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group]],
[[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]]).

[F3] The Axiom of Choice implies the Axiom of Dependent Choice, and the
finiteness statement just used by [F2] is proved from Dependent Choice
([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]).

[F4] If $f$ is algebraic over $k$, then $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are global units of
$C$, that is, $f\in\Gamma(C,\mathcal O_C)^{\times}$
([[lem-proper-normal-curve-rational-function-map]]).

[F5] If $f$ is transcendental over $k$, then there is a finite locally free
morphism $\varphi_f:C\to\mathbb P^1_k$ of degree $d=[K:k(f)]$ with
$\varphi_f^{\#}(t)=f$ on the standard chart $t$, and: the fibre
$\varphi_f^{-1}(0)$ consists exactly of the closed points $x$ with
$\operatorname{ord}_x(f)>0$, the fibre $\varphi_f^{-1}(\infty)$ consists
exactly of the closed points $x$ with $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)<0$, and
$$\sum_{\varphi_f(x)=0}\operatorname{ord}_x(f)\,[\kappa(x):k]=d,\qquad \sum_{\varphi_f(x)=\infty}\bigl(-\operatorname{ord}_x(f)\bigr)[\kappa(x):k]=d .$$
Both sums are finite and every summand is a positive integer
([[lem-proper-normal-curve-rational-function-map]],
[[lem-finite-flat-curve-fibre-degree]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Algebraic case.** Suppose that $f$ is algebraic over $k$. By [F4] both $f$ and $f^{-1}$ are global units of $C$, so the germ of $f$ in each local ring $\mathcal O_{C,x}$ is a unit; by [F1] $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)=0$ at every closed point $x$ of $C$. Hence every coefficient of $\operatorname{div}_W(f)$ vanishes, so $\operatorname{div}_W(f)=0$ and the degree sum of [F2] is empty, giving $\deg_k\operatorname{div}_W(f)=0$. [F1, F2, F4]

1.2 **Transcendental case: the coefficient partition.** Suppose that $f$ is transcendental over $k$, and let $S_+=\{\,x\text{ closed}:\operatorname{ord}_x(f)>0\,\}$, $S_-=\{\,x\text{ closed}:\operatorname{ord}_x(f)<0\,\}$ and $S_0=\{\,x\text{ closed}:\operatorname{ord}_x(f)=0\,\}$. By [F5] the set $S_+$ is the zero fibre of $\varphi_f$ and $S_-$ is the fibre over infinity, so both are finite; the three sets are pairwise disjoint and, since $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)$ is an integer, they partition the set of closed points. The coefficient of $x$ in $\operatorname{div}_W(f)$ is $\operatorname{ord}_x(f)$, which is $0$ for $x\in S_0$; therefore the degree sum of [F2] splits as
$$\deg_k\operatorname{div}_W(f)=\sum_{x\in S_+}\operatorname{ord}_x(f)[\kappa(x):k]+\sum_{x\in S_-}\operatorname{ord}_x(f)[\kappa(x):k].$$
[F1, F2, F5]

2.1 **Transcendental case: computation.** Let $d=[K:k(f)]$. By the two identities of [F5] the first sum in step 1.2 equals $d$, while the second equals the negative of the pole-fibre sum, namely $\sum_{x\in S_-}\operatorname{ord}_x(f)[\kappa(x):k]=-d$. Hence $\deg_k\operatorname{div}_W(f)=d-d=0$. [F5, step 1.2]

3.1 **Conclusion.** Every $f\in K^{\times}$ is either algebraic or transcendental over $k$, so steps 1.1 and 2.1 cover all cases and $\deg_k\operatorname{div}_W(f)=0$ for every $f\in K^{\times}$. The Axiom of Choice is used exactly as declared: it supplies the finite locally free morphism $\varphi_f$ and the fibre-degree identities of [F5], and through [F3] it supplies the Dependent Choice needed for the finiteness of $\operatorname{div}_W(f)$ in [F2]; the case distinction and the addition in steps 1.1–2.1 use no choice. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎

The constant function $f=1$ is algebraic over $k$, so it is covered by
step 1.1: its divisor is the zero divisor and the degree sum is the empty sum
$0$. In the algebraic case of step 1.1 the divisor of $f$ is the zero divisor,
so the theorem also covers the situation in which $\operatorname{div}_W(f)$
has empty support. In the transcendental case $d=[K:k(f)]\ge 1$ is a positive
integer, both fibres of [F5] are nonempty, and the divisor of $f$ has both
positive and negative coefficients, whose contributions cancel exactly. A
single closed point is handled inside the same coefficientwise sum, without a
separate case, and no smoothness or projectivity of $C$ is assumed beyond the
properness and normality needed by [F4] and [F5]; the target $\mathbb P^1_k$ is
used only through its two standard charts. Finally, the hypotheses are exactly
those of [F4] and [F5], so the theorem does not apply to non-normal curves,
where orders at closed points may fail to be defined.
