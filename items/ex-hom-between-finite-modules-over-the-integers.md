## Example

Let $m\in\mathbb N$ and let $n\ge1$. Then, as $\mathbb Z$-modules,

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}\big(\mathbb Z/m,\ \mathbb Z/n\big)\;\cong\;\mathbb Z/\gcd(m,n).$$

The hypothesis $n\ge1$ cannot be dropped. At $n=0$ the module $\mathbb Z/0$ is a
copy of $\mathbb Z$, and for $m\ge2$ the left-hand side is the zero module while
$\gcd(m,0)=m$ makes the right-hand side $\mathbb Z/m$, which is not zero.

Both modules here are finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$, so
[[cor-hom-of-finite-modules-is-finite-over-a-noetherian-ring]] predicts that the
homomorphism module is finitely generated; the computation below identifies it
outright.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Natural numbers $m$ and $n$ with $n\ge1$, and the classes $[a]_m\in\mathbb Z/m$ and $[b]_n\in\mathbb Z/n$.

[L1] The **integers modulo $n$** are the congruence classes $[a]_n=\{b\in\mathbb Z:b\equiv a\pmod n\}$, and $[a]_n=[b]_n$ exactly when $a\equiv b\pmod n$; at $n=0$ each class is a singleton ([[def-integers-modulo-n]]).

[L2] Addition and multiplication of congruence classes are given by $[a]_n+[b]_n=[a+b]_n$ and $[a]_n[b]_n=[ab]_n$ ([[def-addition-and-multiplication-modulo-n]]).

[L3] For every $n\in\mathbb N$, $(\mathbb Z/n,+,[0]_n)$ is an abelian group with $-[a]_n=[-a]_n$ ([[thm-integers-modulo-n-basic-algebra]]).

[L4] Every abelian group carries a unique $\mathbb Z$-module structure whose scalar action is integer multiplication, and abelian groups and $\mathbb Z$-modules have the same objects and morphisms ([[prop-abelian-groups-are-z-modules]]).

[L5] A function $f\colon M\to N$ between $R$-modules is an **$R$-module homomorphism** when $f(m+m')=f(m)+f(m')$ and $f(rm)=rf(m)$ ([[def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]]).

[L6] $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an abelian group under pointwise addition ([[def-hom-groups-and-induced-hom-maps]]).

[L7] Over a commutative ring $R$ the group $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an $R$-module under $(rf)(x)=r\,f(x)$, with the published addition unchanged ([[lem-hom-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L8] For $a,b\in\mathbb Z$, $a\mathbb Z+b\mathbb Z=\gcd(a,b)\mathbb Z$ ([[thm-gcd-generates-the-subgroup]]).

[L9] $\gcd(a,b)$ is the greatest common divisor of $a$ and $b$, with $\gcd(0,0):=0$; it satisfies $\gcd(a,b)\ge0$ always, and $\gcd(a,b)\ge1$ unless $a=b=0$ ([[def-common-divisor-and-gcd]]).

[L10] Over a Noetherian commutative ring the homomorphism module between two finitely generated modules is finitely generated ([[cor-hom-of-finite-modules-is-finite-over-a-noetherian-ring]]).

[L11] $\mathbb Z$ is a Noetherian ring ([[ex-noetherian-integers-and-fields]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $d:=\gcd(m,n)$, so $d\ge1$ because $n\ge1$, and $d$ divides both $m$ and $n$. Both $\mathbb Z/m$ and $\mathbb Z/n$ are abelian groups and hence $\mathbb Z$-modules with the integer-multiplication action, and a $\mathbb Z$-module homomorphism between them is exactly an additive map; $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/m,\mathbb Z/n)$ is a $\mathbb Z$-module under pointwise addition and the integer action. Each $\mathbb Z/m$ is generated by $[1]_m$, since $[a]_m=a\,[1]_m$. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, L7, L9, given]

2.1 Evaluation at $[1]_m$ is a $\mathbb Z$-module isomorphism from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/m,\mathbb Z/n)$ onto $A:=\{y\in\mathbb Z/n:my=0\}$. It lands in $A$, because $m[1]_m=[m]_m=[0]_m$ gives $m\,f([1]_m)=f([0]_m)=0$. It is injective, because $f([a]_m)=a\,f([1]_m)$ determines $f$. It is surjective: given $y\in A$, the assignment $[a]_m\mapsto ay$ is well defined, since $[a]_m=[a']_m$ means $m$ divides $a-a'$ and then $(a-a')y$ is a multiple of $my=0$, and it is additive. Additivity and $\mathbb Z$-homogeneity of the evaluation map are immediate from the pointwise operations. [L1, L2, L5, L6, L7, step 1.1]

2.2 $\{b\in\mathbb Z:n\text{ divides }mb\}=(n/d)\mathbb Z$. For the inclusion from right to left, $b=(n/d)t$ gives $mb=(m/d)\,n\,t$, a multiple of $n$, using that $d$ divides $m$. For the other inclusion, Bézout in the form $m\mathbb Z+n\mathbb Z=d\mathbb Z$ supplies integers $u,v$ with $d=um+vn$; if $n$ divides $mb$ then $n$ divides $b\,d=u(mb)+v(bn)$, say $bd=ns$, and dividing by $d\ge1$ gives $b=(n/d)s$. [L8, L9, step 1.1]

3.1 $A$ is the cyclic submodule generated by $[n/d]_n$, and $A\cong\mathbb Z/d$. Indeed $y=[b]_n$ lies in $A$ exactly when $[mb]_n=[0]_n$, that is when $n$ divides $mb$, which by step 2.2 says $b\in(n/d)\mathbb Z$; so $A=\{[(n/d)t]_n:t\in\mathbb Z\}$. The map $\mathbb Z/d\to\mathbb Z/n$ sending $[c]_d$ to $[c(n/d)]_n$ is well defined, since $d$ dividing $c-c'$ makes $n=(n/d)d$ divide $(c-c')(n/d)$; it is additive; its image is $A$; and it is injective, since $[c(n/d)]_n=[0]_n$ means $c(n/d)=nt=(n/d)dt$ for some $t$, whence $c=dt$ because $n/d\ge1$ is nonzero, so $[c]_d=[0]_d$. [L1, L2, L3, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Combining steps 2.1 and 3.1 gives $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/m,\mathbb Z/n)\cong\mathbb Z/d$ with $d=\gcd(m,n)$. This is consistent with the general finiteness statement: $\mathbb Z$ is Noetherian and $\mathbb Z/m$, $\mathbb Z/n$ are generated by one element each, so the homomorphism module had to be finitely generated, and here it is cyclic. The hypothesis $n\ge1$ is used in step 1.1 to make $d\ge1$ and in steps 2.2 and 3.1 to divide by $n/d$; at $n=0$ the conclusion is false for $m\ge2$, since an additive $f\colon\mathbb Z/m\to\mathbb Z/0$ has $m\,f([1]_m)=0$ in a copy of $\mathbb Z$, forcing $f([1]_m)=0$ and $f=0$, while $\gcd(m,0)=m$ makes the claimed answer $\mathbb Z/m$, which has more than one element. [L1, L9, L10, L11, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **The two boundary values behave differently, and only one of them is admitted.** At $m=0$ the module $\mathbb Z/0$ is a copy of $\mathbb Z$ and the formula reads $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/n)\cong\mathbb Z/n$, which is correct and is the case $d=\gcd(0,n)=n$. At $n=0$ it fails, as step 4.1 records. The asymmetry is the asymmetry between the source and the target of a homomorphism, not an artefact of the $\gcd$ convention.

- **The displayed isomorphism is canonical for these quotient presentations.** The classes $[1]_m$ and $[n/d]_n$ are distinguished by the standard quotient maps, not chosen generators. Evaluation at $[1]_m$, followed by the inverse of $[c]_d\mapsto[c(n/d)]_n$, therefore gives the isomorphism without an auxiliary choice.
