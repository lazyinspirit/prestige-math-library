## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\lambda\in X^*(T)$
and suppose that $\lambda+\rho$ lies on a Weyl wall, that is
$\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle=0$ for some root $\beta$
(equivalently, $\lambda+\rho$ is not regular, so $\lambda$ is not
dot-regular). Equivalently, let $\eta^+$ be the unique point of the
$W$-orbit of $\lambda+\rho$ in the closed dominant chamber; then
$\langle\eta^+,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=0$ for some simple root $\alpha_i$.
The dominant orbit point is unique even when the Weyl element carrying
$\lambda+\rho$ to it is not. In this case $H^i(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)=0$ for
every $i\ge0$, so all cohomology of $\mathcal L_\lambda$ vanishes and
$\mathcal L_\lambda$ is not the geometric realisation of an irreducible
representation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the group $G$, its Borel $B$, the flag variety $X=G/B$, a weight $\lambda$, and the assumption that $\lambda+\rho$ is annihilated by the coroot of some root.

[F1] The rank-one shift: for a simple root $\alpha$ and any weight $\nu$, if $\langle\nu,\alpha^\vee\rangle\ge-1$ then $H^i(X,\mathcal L_\nu)\cong H^{i+1}(X,\mathcal L_{s_\alpha\cdot\nu})$ for all $i\ge0$, and if $\langle\nu,\alpha^\vee\rangle=-1$ then $H^i(X,\mathcal L_\nu)=0$ for all $i\ge0$ ([[lem-rank-one-cohomology-shifts-across-a-simple-wall]]).

[F2] Dominance means $\langle\eta,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\ge0$ for every simple root $\alpha_i$, strict dominance means $>0$ for every $i$, and every $W$-orbit has exactly one point in the closed dominant chamber. The stabilizer of a point in that chamber is generated by the simple reflections whose simple-coroot pairings with it vanish. A dominant functional pairs nonnegatively with every positive root ([[def-open-and-closed-weyl-chambers]], [[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]]).

[F3] The simple reflection acts by $s_\alpha(\eta)=\eta-\langle\eta,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$, the pairing satisfies $\langle w\eta,(w\gamma)^\vee\rangle=\langle\eta,\gamma^\vee\rangle$ for all roots $\gamma$, and $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$ for the simple roots ([[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]], [[prop-weyl-vector-is-the-sum-of-fundamental-weights]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho]]).

[F4] The dot action is $w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$, so that $\lambda+\rho$ is regular exactly when $\lambda$ is dot-regular, and $s_\alpha\cdot\lambda$ has $(s_\alpha\cdot\lambda)+\rho=s_\alpha(\lambda+\rho)$ ([[def-dot-action-facets-and-single-wall-translation-data]]).

[F5] The flag variety $X=G/B$ is smooth projective of dimension $N=|\Phi^+|$, and Serre duality gives a perfect pairing $H^i(X,E)^\vee\cong H^{N-i}(X,E^\vee\otimes\omega_X)$ for every locally free sheaf $E$ and $0\le i\le N$ ([[thm-semisimple-flag-variety-smooth-projective]], [[thm-serre-duality-smooth-projective-variety-locally-free-sheaves]]).

[F6] The canonical bundle is $\omega_X\cong\mathcal L_{-2\rho}$, and the tensor and dual identifications of Borel-character line bundles give $\mathcal L_\lambda^\vee\otimes\omega_X\cong\mathcal L_{-\lambda-2\rho}$ ([[lem-flag-variety-canonical-bundle-weight-minus-two-rho]], [[def-borel-character-equivariant-line-bundle]]).

## Proof

1.1 Put $\eta_0=\lambda+\rho$ and $n_-(\eta)=\#\{\gamma\in\Phi^+:\langle\eta,\gamma^\vee\rangle<0\}$. Starting from $\lambda_0=\lambda$, write $\eta_j=\lambda_j+\rho$. Whenever $\eta_j$ is not dominant, choose a simple root $\alpha$ with $\langle\eta_j,\alpha^\vee\rangle<0$ and set $\lambda_{j+1}=s_\alpha\cdot\lambda_j$, so $\eta_{j+1}=s_\alpha\eta_j$. The reflection $s_\alpha$ permutes $\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$ and sends $\alpha$ to $-\alpha$; by [F3], $n_-(\eta_{j+1})=n_-(\eta_j)-1$. Continue whenever a negative simple pairing remains, including when other simple pairings vanish. After exactly $m=n_-(\eta_0)$ steps the count is zero, so all simple pairings are nonnegative and $\eta_m$ is dominant. Every $\eta_j$ remains singular because the Weyl group preserves root hyperplanes. [F2, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 At each of these $m$ steps, $\langle\lambda_j,\alpha^\vee\rangle=\langle\eta_j,\alpha^\vee\rangle-1\le-2$. The reverse form of [F1] gives $H^{i+1}(X,\mathcal L_{\lambda_j})\cong H^i(X,\mathcal L_{\lambda_{j+1}})$ for every $i\ge0$, and composing yields $H^{i+m}(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)\cong H^i(X,\mathcal L_{\lambda_m})$. Because $\eta_m$ is dominant and singular, its stabilizer is nontrivial; [F2] therefore gives a simple root $\alpha_i$ with $\langle\eta_m,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=0$. Thus $\langle\lambda_m,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=-1$, and [F1] makes every cohomology group of $\mathcal L_{\lambda_m}$ vanish. Conversely, a zero simple-coroot pairing puts a Weyl translate on a root hyperplane, so it implies that $\eta_0$ is singular. Therefore the wall condition is equivalent to the stated condition on the unique dominant orbit point. We obtain $H^q(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)=0$ for every $q\ge m$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $\mu=-\lambda-2\rho$, so $\mu+\rho=-\eta_0$ is singular. Applying steps 1.1-2.1 with $\mu$ in place of $\lambda$ gives $m'=n_-(-\eta_0)$ and $H^q(X,\mathcal L_\mu)=0$ for every $q\ge m'$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $N=|\Phi^+|=\dim X$. Since $\eta_0$ is singular, at least one positive-root coroot pairs to zero; each other positive root contributes to exactly one of $n_-(\eta_0)$ and $n_-(-\eta_0)$, so $m+m'\le N-1$. For $0\le i<m$, this implies $N-i\ge N-m+1>m'$. By Serre duality [F5] and the line-bundle identification [F6], $H^i(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)^\vee\cong H^{N-i}(X,\mathcal L_\lambda^\vee\otimes\omega_X)\cong H^{N-i}(X,\mathcal L_\mu)=0$ by step 3.1. Hence the remaining low-degree groups $H^i(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)$ also vanish. Together with step 2.1, which covers every degree $q\ge m$, this proves vanishing for all $i\ge0$. In particular $H^0(X,\mathcal L_\lambda)=0$, so the line bundle does not geometrically realise a nonzero irreducible representation. [F5, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The equivalence stated in the scaffold between vanishing on a positive-root
wall and vanishing of a simple-root pairing holds only for the *dominant*
translate of $\lambda+\rho$; it fails for $\lambda+\rho$ itself. In $B_2$ with
$\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle=1$ and
$\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=-2$ one has
$w(\lambda+\rho)=\omega_1$ with $\langle\omega_1,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=0$, but
both simple pairings of $\lambda+\rho$ are nonzero and
$\lambda+\rho$ lies on the wall of the positive root $\alpha_1+\alpha_2$,
whose coroot is $2\alpha_1^\vee+\alpha_2^\vee$. The proof above therefore runs
the monotone chain to the dominant translate and invokes the vanishing only
there.
