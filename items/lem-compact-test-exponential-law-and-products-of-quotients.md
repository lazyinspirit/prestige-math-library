## Statement

For CG spaces $X,Y,Z$, currying $g(x,y)=f(x)(y)$ is a natural bijection between continuous maps $X\times_k Y\to Z$ and $X\to C(Y,Z)$, and induces a natural homeomorphism
$$C(X\times_k Y,Z)\cong C(X,C(Y,Z)).$$
Products of quotient maps between CG spaces are quotient maps for k-products. Specifically, for $q:X\to Q$, the relation on $X\times_k Y$ is $(x,y)\sim(x',y')$ exactly when $q(x)=q(x')$ and $y=y'$. No WH hypothesis is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] CG-source continuity can be tested on compact Hausdorff domains; k-products are categorical and ordinary quotients are CG. [[lem-kification-compact-tests-and-finite-constructions]]

[F2] The mapping topology is generated by compact-test subbasic opens. [[def-compactly-generated-conventions-for-based-homotopy]]

[F3] Compact Hausdorff spaces are regular and normal. [[thm-a-compact-hausdorff-space-is-regular-and-normal]]

[F4] A closed test neighbourhood is compact. [[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]

[F5] An open set containing a compact fibre contains a tube. [[lem-tube-lemma-for-a-compact-factor]]

[F6] Fibre-constant continuous maps factor through a quotient. [[thm-quotient-universal-property]]


## Proof

**Given:** The spaces, maps, and hypotheses in the statement above.

1.1 For a compact Hausdorff test $(v,w):K\to Y\times_k C(Y,Z)$, consider $a\mapsto w(a)(v(a))$. If its value at $a$ lies in open $O$, regularity gives a closed neighbourhood $L$ of $a$ with $w(a)(v(L))\subseteq O$. The set $N=w^{-1}W(v|_L,L,O)$ is open and contains $a$. For $b\in N\cap\operatorname{int}L$, one has $w(b)(v(b))\in O$. Thus every test composite of evaluation is continuous, so evaluation $Y\times_k C(Y,Z)\to Z$ is continuous. [F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 Given continuous $g:X\times_k Y\to Z$, each function $f(x):y\mapsto g(x,y)$ is continuous by the slice map. For tests $v:L\to X$, $u:K\to Y$, the map $(b,a)\mapsto g(v(b),u(a))$ is continuous on the compact Hausdorff product. The tube lemma says that the set of $b$ for which all its values on $\{b\}\times K$ lie in $O$ is open. This is $(f v)^{-1}W(u,K,O)$. Testing on $L$ proves continuity $X\to C_0(Y,Z)$, and the CG-source property lifts it to $C(Y,Z)$. [F1, F2, F5]

2.1 Conversely, a continuous $f:X\to C(Y,Z)$ uncurries continuously by composing its product with the evaluation of step 1.1. The two constructions are inverse since both give the same value $f(x)(y)$ at every pair. Precomposition and postcomposition preserve this equality, proving naturality. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The map $(h,x,y)\mapsto h(x)(y)$ is a composite of two continuous evaluations. Twice applying the map correspondence of steps 1.2–2.1 makes the induced map $C(X,C(Y,Z))\to C(X\times_k Y,Z)$ continuous. Conversely, start with evaluation $(h,x,y)\mapsto h(x,y)$ and curry successively in $y$ and $x$. This gives the inverse continuous map. Product reassociations are homeomorphisms by F1, so the displayed bijection is a homeomorphism. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

3.2 Let $P$ be the ordinary quotient of $X\times_k Y$ by the stated relation and $p$ its quotient map. It is CG. The map $q\times\mathrm{id}$ descends to a continuous bijection $a:P\to Q\times_k Y$. The transpose $x\mapsto(y\mapsto p(x,y))$ is constant on each q-fibre and hence descends continuously to $Q\to C(Y,P)$. Uncurrying gives $b:Q\times_k Y\to P$ with $b(q(x),y)=p(x,y)$. Surjectivity of q and p gives $ab=\mathrm{id}$ and $ba=\mathrm{id}$. Thus $q\times\mathrm{id}$ is quotient. [F1, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 For quotient maps $q:X\to Q$ and $r:Y\to R$, factor $q\times r=(\mathrm{id}_Q\times r)(q\times\mathrm{id}_Y)$. Each factor is quotient by step 3.2 and symmetry, and their composite is quotient. Empty factors give empty products and the same inverse identities; no representative of a quotient fibre has been selected. [F1, F6, step 3.2] ∎
