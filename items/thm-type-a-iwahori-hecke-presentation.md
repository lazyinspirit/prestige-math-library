## Statement

Let $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with Borel $B$, let
$H=e_B\mathbb C[G]e_B$, and for $1\le i\le n-1$ let $T_{s_i}\in H$ be the
standard basis element attached to the simple transposition $s_i$
([[def-bruhat-double-coset-basis-of-the-finite-hecke-algebra]],
[[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]). Throughout, $T_1:=T_{\mathrm{id}}=e_B$
denotes the unit of $H$, so that the generators $T_{s_1},\dots,T_{s_{n-1}}$ are
distinct from $T_1$; in particular in the quadratic relation below the right
hand side $q\cdot1_H=q\,T_1$ is a scalar multiple of the unit. Then:

1. the elements $T_{s_1},\dots,T_{s_{n-1}}$ generate $H$;
2. they satisfy
$$T_{s_i}^2=(q-1)T_{s_i}+q\,1_H\ (1\le i<n),\qquad T_{s_i}T_{s_{i+1}}T_{s_i}=T_{s_{i+1}}T_{s_i}T_{s_{i+1}}\ (1\le i\le n-2),\qquad T_{s_i}T_{s_j}=T_{s_j}T_{s_i}\ (|i-j|>1);$$
3. these relations present $H$: if
$H(n):=\mathbb C\langle \tau_1,\dots,\tau_{n-1}\rangle/(\text{the three
families of relations displayed in (2), with }\tau_i\text{ in place of }T_{s_i}
\text{ and }1\text{ the unit of }H(n))$
is the abstract unital $\mathbb C$-algebra with generators $\tau_i$ and these
relations, then the natural map $H(n)\to H$, $\tau_i\mapsto T_{s_i}$, is an
isomorphism. Equivalently,
$H\cong\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]}H_v(n)$ under $v\mapsto q$. In
particular $H$ has dimension $n!$ and the images of the standard basis $T_w$
form a basis. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ with Borel $B$, the idempotent $e_B$, the finite Hecke algebra $H$ with standard basis $T_w$, the simple transpositions $s_i=(i\ i+1)$ with inversion length $\ell$, the generic type-A Hecke algebra $H_v(n)$ over $A=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm 1}]$, and the specialization $A\to\mathbb C$, $v\mapsto q$.

[F1] $H_v(n)$ is the quotient of the free unital associative $A$-algebra on $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ by the two-sided ideal generated by $T_i^2-(v-1)T_i-v$, the braid relators $T_iT_{i+1}T_i-T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}$ and the distant commutation relators $T_iT_j-T_jT_i$; its specialization at a unit $v_0\in R^\times$ is $R\otimes_AH_v(n)$, presented over $R$ by the same relations with $v$ replaced by $v_0$ and with the unit written explicitly ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] $H_v(n)$ is free over $A$ with basis the products $T_w$ along reduced words, so it has rank $n!$; its multiplication rule rewrites every monomial in the generators as an $A$-linear combination of the $T_w$ ([[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F3] The elements $T_w=q^{\ell(w)}e_B\dot we_B$, $w\in S_n$, form a $\mathbb C$-basis of $H$, with $T_1=e_B$ the unit, and $\dim_{\mathbb C}H=n!$ ([[def-bruhat-double-coset-basis-of-the-finite-hecke-algebra]]).

[F4] If $u,v\in S_n$ satisfy $\ell(uv)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)$, then $T_uT_v=T_{uv}$; in particular a product of generators along a reduced word for $w$ equals $T_w$ ([[lem-length-increasing-hecke-products]]).

[F5] For every simple transposition $s_i$ one has $T_{s_i}^2=(q-1)T_{s_i}+q\,T_1$ in $H$ ([[lem-rank-one-hecke-quadratic-relation]]).

[F6] $s_i=(i\ i+1)$, $\ell(s_i)=1$, and length is the inversion count ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[F7] Tensoring over a commutative ring preserves cokernels and surjections ([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For every $w\in S_n$ choose a reduced word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell}$; step by step the partial products have length $1,2,\dots,\ell$, so repeated application of [F4] gives $T_w=T_{s_{i_1}}\cdots T_{s_{i_\ell}}$. Since the $T_w$ form a basis of $H$ by [F3], every element of $H$ is a finite linear combination of products of the generators $T_{s_1},\dots,T_{s_{n-1}}$: clause (1). [F3, F4, algebra]

1.2 The quadratic relation of clause (2) is [F5]. For $|i-j|>1$ the simple transpositions commute, and both products in $T_{s_i}T_{s_j}=T_{s_is_j}=T_{s_js_i}=T_{s_j}T_{s_i}$ are length-additive because $s_is_j$ has exactly two inversions and $\ell(s_i)=\ell(s_j)=1$ by [F6]; [F4] applies. If $|i-j|=1$, the two permutations $s_is_{i+1}s_i$ and $s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}$ are equal, as is checked by their action on $i,i+1,i+2$ and on the remaining points, and each product of the three generators is length-additive since these permutations have exactly three inversions by [F6]; applying [F4] to both sides gives $T_{s_i}T_{s_{i+1}}T_{s_i}=T_{s_is_{i+1}s_i}=T_{s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}}=T_{s_{i+1}}T_{s_i}T_{s_{i+1}}$. [F4, F5, F6, algebra]

1.3 Let $F$ be the free unital associative $A$-algebra on $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ and $I\subseteq F$ the two-sided ideal generated by the relators of [F1], so that $H_v(n)=F/I$, and put $B:=\mathbb C\otimes_AH_v(n)$. By right exactness of tensoring [F7], $B\cong(\mathbb C\otimes_AF)/\operatorname{im}(\mathbb C\otimes_AI\to\mathbb C\otimes_AF)$ as $\mathbb C$-algebras; the base change of the free algebra is the free unital $\mathbb C$-algebra on the images $\tau_i:=1\otimes T_i$, and the image ideal is generated by the specialized relators: every element of $I$ is a finite sum of products $arb$ with $a,b\in F$ and $r$ a defining relator, so its image lies in that ideal, while every specialized relator and its products lie in the image. These relators are $\tau_i^2-(q-1)\tau_i-q\cdot1_F$, the braid relators and the commutation relators, with $1_F$ the unit. Hence $B$ is presented by the three families of relations of clause (2), that is, $B\cong H(n)$; the unit is the algebra unit in both cases, so the relation is $\tau_i^2=(q-1)\tau_i+q\cdot1$. [F1, F7, algebra]

2.1 By [F2] the algebra $H_v(n)$ is free over $A$ with basis $\{T_w\}$, so its base change $B\cong H(n)$ has $\mathbb C$-basis $\{1\otimes T_w\}$ and dimension $n!$. The assignment $\tau_i\mapsto T_{s_i}$ defines a unital $\mathbb C$-algebra homomorphism $\varphi:H(n)\to H$ because the three families of relations hold in $H$ by step 1.2; it is surjective by step 1.1. Since $\dim_{\mathbb C}H(n)=n!=\dim_{\mathbb C}H$ by [F3], a surjection between vector spaces of equal finite dimension is an isomorphism, so $H\cong H(n)\cong\mathbb C\otimes_AH_v(n)$ under $v\mapsto q$: clause (3). Under $\varphi$ the basis element $1\otimes T_w$ of $H_v(n)$ maps to the product $T_{s_{i_1}}\cdots T_{s_{i_\ell}}$ along a reduced word, which is $T_w$ by [F4]; hence the images of the standard basis $T_w$ form a basis of $H$, in agreement with [F3]. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Step 1.1 proves clause (1), step 1.2 proves clause (2) with the unit displayed explicitly, and steps 1.3 and 2.1 prove clause (3) and the specialization statement; all algebras are finite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$ or free of finite rank over $A$, the generators and permutation matrices are explicit, and no choice principle is used. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
