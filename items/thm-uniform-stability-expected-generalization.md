## Statement

Let $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ and $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ be nonempty measurable instance and label spaces, and equip $\mathsf Z=\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y$ with $\Sigma_Z=\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y$. Let $P$ be a probability measure on $(\mathsf Z,\Sigma_Z)$, let $\mathcal F$ be a nonempty class of measurable functions $f:(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)\to(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$, and let $\ell:\mathbb R\times\mathsf Y\to[0,\infty)$ be measurable with respect to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\otimes\Sigma_Y$. For $f\in\mathcal F$ and $z=(x,y)$ write $L_f(z)=\ell(f(x),y)$, $R_P(f)=\int_{\mathsf Z}L_f(z)\,P(dz)$, and $\widehat R_S(f)=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^mL_f(Z_i)$. Let $S=(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m)$ have product law $P^m$ on $(\mathsf Z^m,\Sigma_Z^{\otimes m})$, with $m\ge1$, and let $A$ be a deterministic, permutation-invariant size-indexed learning rule that outputs members of $\mathcal F$ and assume the maps $(S,z)\mapsto L_{A_S}(z)$ and $(S,z)\mapsto L_{A_{S\setminus i}}(z)$ for every $i\in[m]$ are jointly measurable with respect to $\Sigma_Z^{\otimes m}\otimes\Sigma_Z$. Assume also that the sample-dependent risk, empirical-risk, and leave-one-out maps in the conclusions are measurable. Assume $0\le L_{A_T}(z)\le M<\infty$ for every full or deleted sample $T$ and every $z\in\mathsf Z$, and assume $A$ has uniform deletion stability with a finite nonincreasing sequence $(\beta_n)_{n\ge1}$. Define $R_{\mathrm{loo}}(A,S)=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m L_{A_{S\setminus i}}(Z_i)$, using the size-$m$ deletion output. Then

$$\mathbb E_{S\sim P^m}\bigl[R_P(A_S)-\widehat R_S(A_S)\bigr]\le2\beta_m\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb E_{S\sim P^m}\bigl[R_P(A_S)-R_{\mathrm{loo}}(A,S)\bigr]\le\beta_m.$$

Moreover, for every sample $S$ and index $i\in[m]$,

$$|R_P(A_S)-R_P(A_{S\setminus i})|\le\beta_m.$$

All three conclusions are choice-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A supervised prediction problem with law $P$, an IID product sample $S\sim P^m$, a measurable size-indexed learning rule, bounded measurable losses, and uniform deletion-stability coefficient $\beta_m$.

[F1] A supervised prediction problem fixes measurable instance and label spaces, a joint sample law, a class of measurable predictors, and a measurable loss. [[def-supervised-learning-problem]]

[F2] Population risk is the integral of the predictor's loss against $P$, and empirical risk is the finite average of its sample losses. [[def-population-and-empirical-risk]]

[F3] IID means mutually independent random elements with the same law; here the coordinate sample is the finite product sample with common law $P$. [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]

[F4] The finite product sigma-algebra is generated by coordinate rectangles; finite product measures have the rectangle formula and are unique, so permuting coordinates preserves the product law. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]] [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]

[F5] For integrable measurable functions on a product, the product integral equals the iterated section integrals. [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]

[F6] A measurable function is in $L^1$ when its absolute value has finite integral; the stated uniform bound on a probability product gives this integrability. [[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]

[F7] The integral is linear on $L^1$. [[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]

[F8] For $h\in L^1$, $|\int h\,dP|\le\int|h|\,dP$. [[thm-integral-triangle-inequality]]

[F9] Uniform deletion stability bounds the loss change between the full output and its size-$m$ deleted output by $\beta_m$, uniformly over samples, deleted indices, and test points. [[def-uniform-algorithmic-stability]]

[A1] The sample-dependent loss and risk maps are jointly measurable and all losses used here lie in $[0,M]$.

## Proof

1.1 Fix $i\in[m]$ and extend the coordinate sample to $(\mathsf Z^{m+1},P^{m+1})$, writing $Z_i'$ for the last coordinate. By [A1] and [F6], $(S,z')\mapsto L_{A_S}(z')$ is integrable on this product space, and [F5] identifies $\mathbb E_S R_P(A_S)$ with $\mathbb E_{S,Z_i'}L_{A_S}(Z_i')$. On each all-coordinate rectangle, the $P^{m+1}$ mass is the product of its coordinate masses. Transposing coordinates $i$ and $m+1$ preserves that value; the generating and uniqueness statements in [F4] therefore show that the transposed product law is again $P^{m+1}$. If $S^{(i)}$ replaces $Z_i$ by $Z_i'$, this gives $\mathbb E_{S,Z_i'}L_{A_S}(Z_i')=\mathbb E_{S,Z_i'}L_{A_{S^{(i)}}}(Z_i)$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, construct]

1.2 By [F2] and [F7], $R_P(A_S)-R_P(A_{S\setminus i})=\int_{\mathsf Z}(L_{A_S}(z)-L_{A_{S\setminus i}}(z))\,P(dz)$. The integrand has absolute value at most $\beta_m$ by [F9], so [F8] gives $|R_P(A_S)-R_P(A_{S\setminus i})|\le\int\beta_m\,dP=\beta_m$. [A1, F2, F7, F8, F9, given]

1.3 For each $i$, the deleted output $A_{S\setminus i}$ depends only on the retained coordinates. Since $Z_i$ is independent of them and has law $P$, [F5] and [F6] give $\mathbb E_S L_{A_{S\setminus i}}(Z_i)=\mathbb E_S R_P(A_{S\setminus i})$. Averaging over $i$ therefore yields $\mathbb E_S[R_P(A_S)-R_{\mathrm{loo}}(A,S)]=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_S[R_P(A_S)-R_P(A_{S\setminus i})]$. [A1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, given, construct]

2.1 By [F2] and linearity, step 1.1 gives $\mathbb E_S[R_P(A_S)-\widehat R_S(A_S)]=m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^m\mathbb E_{S,Z_i'}[L_{A_{S^{(i)}}}(Z_i)-L_{A_S}(Z_i)]$. The indexed retained samples $S\setminus i$ and $S^{(i)}\setminus i$ coincide. Applying [F9] to each full output and that common deletion output, then the triangle inequality, bounds the absolute value of every summand by $2\beta_m$. Averaging gives the first expected bound. [F2, F9, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Each summand in the average in step 1.3 is at most $\beta_m$ by step 1.2, so its average is at most $\beta_m$. This proves the leave-one-out bound. [step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
