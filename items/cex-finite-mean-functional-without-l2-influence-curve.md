## Statement refuted

Every finite-mean law has a finite-variance influence curve for its mean functional in the dominated local model generated by bounded mean-zero score tilts.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The statement above and the definitions of DQM paths and parameter influence curves.

[F1] [[def-dqm-submodel-through-a-law]]: a one-sided common-dominated path is DQM when its square-root density has the stated first-order $L^2$ expansion.

[F2] [[def-pathwise-differentiable-functional]]: a finite-variance parameter influence curve is a vector in $L^2_0(P)$ representing path derivatives by the $L^2(P)$ pairing.

[F3] [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-inequality-for-l-two]]: $|P[fg]|\le\|f\|_2\|g\|_2$ for square-integrable $f,g$.

[F4] [[cor-taylor-remainder-bound]]: if the second derivative is bounded on an interval, the first-order Taylor polynomial has a remainder bounded by a constant times the square of the displacement.

## Counterexample

**Given:** Let $\mathcal X=\mathbb N_{\ge1}$, $P\{j\}=2^{-j}$, $f(j)=2^{j/2}$, and $\psi(Q)=Qf$. Allow all local tilts $dP_{t,s}=(1+ts)dP$ with bounded $s$ satisfying $Ps=0$.

1.1 The masses sum to one. The geometric series gives $Pf=\sum_{j\ge1}2^{-j/2}<\infty$, while $P[f^2]=\sum_{j\ge1}1=\infty$. Thus $\mu=Pf$ is finite and $u=f-\mu$ is integrable, centered, but not square-integrable. [given, algebra]

1.2 For any bounded centered $s$, choose $t\ge0$ so small that $|ts|\le1/2$ everywhere. Then $1+ts\ge0$ and $P(1+ts)=1$. On $[-1/2,1/2]$, $g(x)=\sqrt{1+x}$ has $g''(x)=-1/(4(1+x)^{3/2})$, so $|g''(x)|\le1$. [F4] with $n=1$ gives $\sqrt{1+ts}=1+\frac t2s+O(t^2\|s\|_\infty^2)$ uniformly; thus the tilt is DQM with score $s$. Since $f\in L^1(P)$ and $s$ is bounded, $\psi(P_{t,s})=Pf+tP[fs]$ and $\dot\psi_P(s)=P[us]$. [F1, F4, given, algebra]

1.3 For $M>0$, put $u_M=u\mathbf1_{\{|u|\le M\}}$, $c_M=Pu_M$, and $s_M=u_M-c_M$. Then $s_M$ is bounded and centered, $D_M:=P[u^2\mathbf1_{\{|u|\le M\}}]=P[us_M]$, and $\|s_M\|_2^2=D_M-c_M^2\le D_M$. For all sufficiently large $j$, $u(j)\ge2^{j/2}/2$, so each such included atom contributes at least $1/4$ to $D_M$; as $M\to\infty$, arbitrarily many atoms are included and $D_M\to\infty$. For large $M$, $s_M\ne0$ because the truncation vanishes on a positive-mass tail and is nonzero on included atoms. Hence $|\dot\psi_P(s_M)|/\|s_M\|_2\ge\sqrt{D_M}\to\infty$. [given, algebra]

2.1 If a finite-variance influence curve $\phi\in L^2_0(P)$ represented these derivatives, [F3] would give $|\dot\psi_P(s_M)|=|P[\phi s_M]|\le\|\phi\|_2\|s_M\|_2$ for every $M$, contradicting step 1.3. Thus the mean functional has no square-integrable influence curve in this model, refuting the statement. [F2, F3, step 1.3, given] ∎
