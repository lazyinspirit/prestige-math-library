## Statement

Let $m,n \ge 1$. Under the identification
$$\mathbb R^{m+n}=\mathbb R^m \times \mathbb R^n,$$
one has
$$\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n) = \mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n}).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Positive integers $m,n$ and the identification $\mathbb R^{m+n}=\mathbb R^m \times \mathbb R^n$.

[L1] The Borel sigma-algebra on a topological space is the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets. ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

[L2] Continuous maps pull Borel sets back to Borel sets. ([[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]])

[A1] The coordinate projections $\pi_1 : \mathbb R^{m+n} \to \mathbb R^m$ and $\pi_2 : \mathbb R^{m+n} \to \mathbb R^n$ are continuous.

[A2] Every open set in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ is a countable union of open rectangles $U \times V$ with $U \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ and $V \subseteq \mathbb R^n$ open.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $A \subseteq \mathbb R^m$ and $B \subseteq \mathbb R^n$ are Borel, then $$A \times B = \pi_1^{-1}(A) \cap \pi_2^{-1}(B),$$ so [L2] makes $A \times B$ Borel in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$. Therefore every measurable rectangle for $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$ belongs to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n})$, and hence $$\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n) \subseteq \mathcal B(\mathbb R^{m+n}).$$ [A1, L2]

2.1 By [A2], every open set in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ is a countable union of open rectangles, hence belongs to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^m)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. Since [L1] says a Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets, this gives the reverse inclusion. Combining with step 1.1 proves the equality. [step 1.1, A2, L1] ∎
