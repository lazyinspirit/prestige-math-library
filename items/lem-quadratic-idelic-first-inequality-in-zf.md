## Statement

Let $L/K$ be a quadratic extension of number fields, with involution $\sigma$. For a number field $F$, its idele group $I_F$ consists of tuples $(x_v)_v\in\prod_vF_v^\times$ that are units at all but finitely many finite places; its idele class group is $C_F=I_F/F^\times$, with the field embedded diagonally. Let $N=1+\sigma$ and $D=\sigma-1$ act in multiplicative notation. Then the finite groups $T_0(C_L)=C_L^\sigma/N(C_L)$ and $T_1(C_L)=\ker N/\operatorname{im}D$ satisfy $|T_0(C_L)|=2|T_1(C_L)|$. The fixed group $C_L^\sigma$ identifies with $C_K$, so $[I_K:K^\times N(I_L)]\ge2$. The norm subgroup $N(I_L)\subseteq I_K$ is open. Finally infinitely many finite primes of $K$ do not split in $L$. These assertions use no choice principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields, groups, and involution in the statement. The topology on $I_F$ has basic neighborhoods restricting finitely many components and requiring units at every remaining finite component.

[F1] Order-two Herbrand quotients multiply in short exact sequences and finite groups have quotient $1$. [[lem-order-two-herbrand-quotients-multiply]]

[F2] Quadratic finite local extensions have norm index $2$. For unramified quadratic extensions, unit norms are onto and norm-one units are quotients of conjugate units. Split pairs of local groups have trivial $T_0,T_1$. Finite local squares are open, and quadratic extensions generated by unit square roots are unramified outside dyadic primes. [[thm-quadratic-local-norm-group-has-index-two]], [[lem-local-quadratic-norm-and-square-class-preparations]]

[F3] The ideal class group is finite, and the unit group with finite primes inverted is finite cyclic torsion times a free group of rank the number of all inverted places minus one. Both results hold in ZF. [[thm-number-field-ideal-class-group-finite-in-zf]], [[thm-number-field-s-unit-structure-in-zf]]

[F4] Finite weak approximation and the completion tensor/norm formulas hold in ZF. [[thm-number-field-finite-weak-approximation-in-zf]], [[lem-number-field-completion-tensor-and-norm-in-zf]]

## Proof

1.1 For any finitely generated abelian group $A$ with involution, let $r_+,r_-$ be the dimensions of the invariant and anti-invariant subspaces of $A\otimes\mathbb Q$. Removing finite torsion changes no Herbrand quotient by [F1]. The invariant and anti-invariant integral subgroups together have finite index in the resulting free group, because $2x=(x+\sigma x)+(x-\sigma x)$. On an invariant copy of $\mathbb Z$ the quotient is $2$, and on an anti-invariant copy it is $1/2$. Thus $$h(A)=2^{r_+-r_-}.$$ This also proves that both quotient groups are finite. [F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix $L=K(\sqrt a)$ and choose a finite set $S$ of places of $K$ containing the infinite and dyadic places, the support of $a$, and primes beneath a finite generating list for the class group of $L$. Let $T$ be all places of $L$ above $S$, and let $U_{L,T}$ be the elements of $L^\times$ that are units outside $T$. Write $s=|S|$, $t=|T|$, and let $c$ be the number of places in $S$ having one place above them, so $t=2s-c$. By [F3], the total rank of $U_{L,T}$ is $t-1$, while its fixed subgroup is exactly $U_{K,S}$, of rank $s-1$. Passing to rational invariants does not change this rank: an invariant rational element can have its denominator cleared to become invariant integrally. Step 1.1 gives $$h(U_{L,T})=2^{2(s-1)-(t-1)}=2^{c-1}.$$ [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $I_{L,T}$ consist of ideles that are units outside $T$. At each place of $S$ with two places above it, the two local factors are exchanged and their $T_0,T_1$ vanish: in additive notation an invariant pair is $(x,x)=N(x,0)$, and a norm-zero pair is $(x,-x)=D(0,x)$. At a single finite place above $v\in S$, [F2] and the explicit Hilbert-90 formula give quotient $2$. At a single infinite place, necessarily $\mathbb C/\mathbb R$, the norm is $z\bar z$, its image is the positive reals, and its norm-one elements are conjugate quotients, again giving quotient $2$. At all places outside $S$ the corresponding unit factors have both quotients trivial by [F2], whether split or unramified quadratic. Since the maps act coordinatewise, the quotients of the product are the products of the local quotients; hence $$h(I_{L,T})=2^c.$$ No arbitrary product choices are needed: a fixed integral basis enumerates the field, its finite residue rings, and its primes; choose the least-coded lifts and uniformizers, and use the explicit residue-correction recursion and conjugate-quotient formula in [F2] on each nontrivial outside factor. The finitely many remaining factors require only finite choice. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The ideal-class condition on $T$ gives $I_L=L^\times I_{L,T}$. Indeed the valuations of an idele form a finite-support fractional ideal; multiplying by a principal idele removes its valuations outside $T$, since the classes of the primes in $T$ generate the class group. Also $L^\times\cap I_{L,T}=U_{L,T}$. Therefore $$1\longrightarrow U_{L,T}\longrightarrow I_{L,T}\longrightarrow C_L\longrightarrow1$$ is exact, and [F1], step 2.1, and step 3.1 give $h(C_L)=2$. To identify the fixed subgroup, suppose $[x]\in C_L$ is fixed, so $\sigma x/x=b\in L^\times$. Its norm is $1$. If $b\ne-1$, the element $y=1+b$ satisfies $y/\sigma y=b$; if $b=-1$, take $y=\sqrt a$. Thus $xy$ is a fixed idele, hence belongs to $I_K$ by [F4]. The map $C_K\to C_L$ is injective, since a diagonal element of $L^\times$ fixed as an idele belongs to $K^\times$. This proves $C_L^\sigma=C_K$, and the norm on this fixed group is the ordinary idele norm by [F4]. Consequently $$[I_K:K^\times N(I_L)]=|T_0(C_L)|=2|T_1(C_L)|\ge2.$$ [F1, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 At the finitely many places of $S$, each local norm group is open: at finite places it contains all squares, which are open by [F2]; at real places it is either the whole group or the positive half-line. Outside $S$, all units are local norms by [F2]. Their product is therefore an open subgroup of $I_K$ contained in $N(I_L)$, so the latter is open. If only finitely many primes failed to split in $L$, enlarge $S$ to contain them. Let $J$ be the subgroup of $I_K$ with component $1$ at $S$ and arbitrary components elsewhere. Outside $S$ all extensions split, so $J\subseteq N(I_L)$. Finite weak approximation [F4] shows $K^\times J$ is dense: match the finitely many prescribed components by an element of $K$, and use $J$ to match every other component exactly. The subgroup $K^\times N(I_L)$ would then be dense and open. An open subgroup is closed, since its complement is a union of open cosets. Hence it would be all of $I_K$, contradicting step 4.1. Therefore infinitely many primes do not split, completing all assertions. [F2, F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
