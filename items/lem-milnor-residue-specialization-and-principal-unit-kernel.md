## Statement

For any surjective discrete valuation on a field $F$, with residue field $k$ and chosen uniformizer $\pi$, the literal residue formula and leading-unit formula in [[def-milnor-boundary-map-for-a-discrete-valuation]] define homomorphisms $\partial_v:K_n^M(F)\to K_{n-1}^M(k)$ and $s_\pi:K_n^M(F)\to K_n^M(k)$ for every $n\ge1$. The residue $\partial_v$ is independent of $\pi$. If $U^1K_n^M(F)$ is generated by symbols with an entry in $1+\mathfrak m_v$, then
$$(s_\pi,\partial_v):K_n^M(F)/U^1K_n^M(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}K_n^M(k)\oplus K_{n-1}^M(k).$$
It sends a unit symbol to its residue symbol in the first summand and a unit symbol followed by $\pi$ to the residue symbol in the second. Completeness and residue finiteness are not assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The objects in the Statement.

[F1] The Milnor presentation and concatenation product define its universal symbolic maps. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

[F2] The boundary Definition supplies the formal symbol formulas and the decomposition $a=\pi^{v(a)}u$; their descent and independence are proved here, not assumed. [[def-milnor-boundary-map-for-a-discrete-valuation]]

[F3] Symbols are anticommutative, $\{a,a\}=\{a,-1\}$, and symbols containing $-1$ are killed by $2$. [[lem-milnor-symbol-anticommutativity-and-repeated-entry-identities]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and conventions above.

1.1 Put $A=K_*^M(k)$ and $\epsilon=\{-1\}\in A_1$. Define the sign automorphism $\eta(a)=(-1)^{\deg a}a$ on homogeneous elements of $A$. Form the skew polynomial ring with multiplication $(a t^r)(b t^s)=a\eta^r(b)t^{r+s}$; associativity follows from $\eta^r(b\eta^s(c))=\eta^r(b)\eta^{r+s}(c)$. By [F3], $2\epsilon=0$, so $\epsilon$ is central as an ordinary ring element and $\eta(\epsilon)=\epsilon$. Moreover $\epsilon\eta(a)=a\epsilon$ for homogeneous $a$: the two graded signs cancel. Thus $t^2-\epsilon t$ commutes with $A$ and with $t$. Quotient by this central monic relation. Division by the monic polynomial gives a unique remainder $a+bt$: a nonzero multiple has degree at least two, since its leading coefficient is unchanged. Consequently the resulting associative graded ring $B$ has $B_n=A_n\oplus A_{n-1}t$, with $ta=(-1)^{\deg a}at$ and $t^2=\epsilon t$. Its degree-one elements anticommute: this holds for $A_1$, for $t$ against $A_1$, and for $t$ against itself because $2t^2=2\epsilon t=0$. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For $x=\pi^r u\in F^\times$, assign $x\mapsto\{\bar u\}+rt\in B_1$. This is multiplicative-to-additive by uniqueness of the decomposition, so extends to the tensor algebra. It kills each Steinberg pair. If $v(x)>0$, then $1-x$ is a unit of residue $1$ and its assigned element is zero. If $v(x)=0$ and $v(1-x)>0$, the assigned element of $x$ is zero; if both are units, their residue symbols form a Steinberg pair in $A$. Finally if $r=v(x)<0$, also $v(1-x)=r$ and their leading units have residues $u_0,-u_0$. Writing $a=\{u_0\}$, the assigned product is $(a+rt)(a+\epsilon+rt)$. Its constant part is $a^2+a\epsilon=2a\epsilon=0$ by [F3]; the mixed $at+ta$ terms cancel; the remaining part is $r(r-1)\epsilon t=0$, because $r(r-1)$ is even. These cases cover all possibilities. Degree-one anticommutativity lets the same argument kill nonadjacent Steinberg entries. Thus a graded ring homomorphism $\Phi_\pi:K_*^M(F)\to B$ is defined. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Its constant component is precisely the leading-unit specialization. Expanding a product of $\{\bar u_i\}+r_i t$ gives the boundary formula of [F2] as its $t$ component. For a singleton selected index $j$, moving $t$ to the right gives the sign $(-1)^{n-j}$. For a selected subset $J$ of size $m\ge2$, repeated use of $t^2=\epsilon t$ gives the $(m-1)$-fold $-1$ symbol; any difference between reordering signs is immaterial because this term is killed by $2$. Hence the displayed subset-sign convention of [F2] is exactly that same component. For $n=1$ the coefficient is $v(x)$ in $\mathbb Z$, including negative values. In particular both components are homomorphisms, and they have the unit/uniformizer values asserted in the Statement. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Put $H=U^1K_n^M(F)$. A principal unit has leading unit $1$ and valuation zero, so $\Phi_\pi$ kills every generating symbol of $H$. Conversely a residue symbol has a well-defined unit-lift class in $K_n^M(F)/H$: changing any lift multiplies it by a principal unit, and the difference lies in $H$. Products of lifts prove multilinearity. For a residue Steinberg pair $\bar a+\bar b=1$, choose a lift $a$ of $\bar a$ and the lift $1-a$ of $\bar b$; both are units and their symbol is zero. Thus arbitrary unit lifts give the same zero class. This constructs a homomorphism $i_1:K_n^M(k)\to K_n^M(F)/H$, without choosing an entire section. The same argument with $\pi$ appended gives $i_2:K_{n-1}^M(k)\to K_n^M(F)/H$; for $n=1$, it is the map $\mathbb Z\to K_1^M(F)/H$ sending $1$ to $\{\pi\}$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Multilinearity and [F3] write every symbol as a sum of unit symbols and unit symbols followed by a single $\pi$: expand entries $\pi^{r_i}u_i$, reorder, and replace every pair of $\pi$ entries by $\pi,-1$. Therefore $i_1+i_2$ is onto. By step 3.1, its composite with $(s_\pi,\partial_v)$ is the identity on the two residue summands. Surjectivity then makes the maps mutually inverse. This proves exactly the kernel $H$ and the isomorphism in the Statement. If $\pi'=\pi w$ with $w$ a unit, the old residue sends a unit symbol followed by $\pi'$ to the same residue symbol as for $\pi$, since the added all-unit symbol has residue zero. The new residue has the same values on these generators and on all-unit symbols. The normal-form argument with $\pi'$ shows these generate all $K_n^M(F)$, so the two residues agree. No independence is claimed for the first component. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
