## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra. Then the assignment that sends a real form
$\mathfrak g_0$ of $\mathfrak g$ to the equivalence class of its Vogan diagram,
formed with the help of a Cartan involution, a maximally compact $\theta$-stable
Cartan subalgebra and a compatible positive system
([[def-vogan-diagram]]), is a bijection from the isomorphism classes of real
forms of $\mathfrak g$ onto the equivalence classes of abstract Vogan diagrams
over the root system of $\mathfrak g$.

The proof below establishes that the assignment is well defined and injective
(steps 1.1-10.1) and surjective (steps 11.1-16.1): every abstract Vogan diagram
over the root system of $\mathfrak g$ is realized by a real form of
$\mathfrak g$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$ with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and root system $\Phi$; the equivalence relation on abstract Vogan diagrams of [[def-vogan-diagram]]; and, when the surjectivity direction is under discussion, an abstract Vogan diagram $D=(\Phi,\tau,\varepsilon)$ over $\Phi$, where $\tau$ is an isometry of $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi$ with $\tau(\Phi)=\Phi$, $\tau^2=\mathrm{id}$ and $\tau(\Delta)=\Delta$ for a base $\Delta$, and $\varepsilon$ is the multiplicative marking determined by the painting of the $\tau$-fixed simple roots.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the structure theory quoted in [L1]-[L4] and through the existence and conjugacy theorems of [L5].

[L1] The Vogan diagram of a real form depends only on the form up to equivalence: [[thm-vogan-diagram-of-a-real-semisimple-lie-algebra-is-well-defined-up-to-equivalence]]; and Cartan involutions of a real form are all conjugate by inner automorphisms, so the class in question is independent of the Cartan involution as well ([[thm-conjugacy-of-cartan-involutions]]).

[L2] Every real semisimple Lie algebra has a Cartan involution, maximally compact $\theta$-stable Cartan subalgebras exist and are mutually conjugate by inner automorphisms, and the real-root and noncompact-imaginary Cayley transforms have the effect recorded in [[thm-cayley-transforms-connect-theta-stable-cartans-in-the-classification]] ([[thm-existence-of-a-cartan-involution]], [[def-cayley-transform-of-a-theta-stable-cartan-subalgebra]], [[def-theta-stable-cartan-subalgebra-and-compact-split-parts]]).

[L3] Every complex semisimple Lie algebra is presented by the Serre generators and relations of its Cartan matrix, and complex semisimple Lie algebras with isomorphic based root systems are isomorphic ([[thm-serre-presentation-theorem]], [[thm-isomorphism-theorem-for-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras]]); every reduced crystallographic root system is the root system of a complex semisimple Lie algebra ([[thm-existence-theorem-for-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras]]).

[L4] Every complex semisimple Lie algebra has a compact real form, any two compact real forms are conjugate by an inner automorphism, and it has a split real form, unique up to isomorphism ([[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-compact-real-forms]], [[thm-existence-and-uniqueness-up-to-isomorphism-of-the-split-real-form]], [[def-split-real-form]]).

[L5] For the compact group $K$ of the global Cartan decomposition of a real semisimple Lie algebra, $\operatorname{Ad}(K)\subseteq\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g_0)$, any two maximal tori of $K$ are conjugate, and the analytic Weyl group of $K$ agrees with the Weyl group of the root system ([[thm-global-cartan-decomposition-for-a-connected-finite-center-semisimple-lie-group]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]], [[def-torus-and-maximal-torus-in-a-compact-lie-group]], [[thm-analytic-and-root-system-weyl-groups-agree]]).

[L6] Positive systems and bases of a root system are related as in [[prop-every-positive-system-is-weyl-conjugate-and-bases-correspond-to-chambers]]; the Killing form $B$ is symmetric, invariant and nondegenerate, $B|_{\mathfrak h}$ is nondegenerate, $H_\alpha$ is the Killing-dual vector with $\alpha(H_\alpha)=B(H_\alpha,H_\alpha)$ and $h_\alpha=2H_\alpha/\alpha(H_\alpha)$ ([[prop-trace-forms-are-symmetric-and-invariant]], [[def-killing-dual-vector-of-a-root]], [[def-coroot-of-a-lie-algebra-root]], [[def-killing-form-of-a-finite-dimensional-lie-algebra]]); and every Cartan subalgebra of the complex semisimple $\mathfrak g$ is abelian and self-normalizing, $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h)=\mathfrak h$ ([[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-exactly-maximal-toral-subalgebras]], [[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]]).

[L8] In the root system $\Phi$ with base $\Delta$: the simple roots are linearly independent, and every root is a unique integral combination of them whose nonzero coefficients all have the same sign ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]); for $\alpha\in\Phi$ and $\beta\in\Phi\cup\{0\}$ the set $\{k\in\mathbb Z:\mathfrak g_{\beta+k\alpha}\ne0\}$ is a nonempty interval of consecutive integers $\{-p,\dots,q\}$ with $p\ge0$, $q\ge0$ and $p-q=\beta(h_\alpha)$ ([[thm-root-string-property]]); and the inner product $(\lambda,\mu)=B(H_\lambda,H_\mu)$ on $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi$ is positive definite ([[prop-the-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[L7] The root-vector basis of $\mathfrak g$ relative to $\mathfrak h$ can be chosen as $X_\alpha\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $[X_\alpha,X_{-\alpha}]=H_\alpha$ and $B(X_\alpha,X_{-\alpha})=1$ for every root, and with constants $C_{\alpha\beta}\in\mathbb R$, defined by $[X_\alpha,X_\beta]=C_{\alpha\beta}X_{\alpha+\beta}$, that satisfy $C_{\alpha\beta}=-C_{-\alpha,-\beta}$ ([[lem-chevalley-basis-and-real-structure-constants]]). The underlying vectors are rescalings $X_\alpha=\lambda_\alpha e_\alpha$ of a basis with $[e_\alpha,e_{-\alpha}]=h_\alpha$ and $N_{\alpha\beta}=\pm(p+1)$, where $\lambda_\alpha=((\alpha,\alpha)/2)^{1/2}>0$ and $\beta+n\alpha$, $-p\le n\le q$, is the $\alpha$-string through $\beta$, so that $$C_{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\lambda_\alpha\lambda_\beta}{\lambda_{\alpha+\beta}}N_{\alpha\beta},\qquad C_{\alpha\beta}^2=\frac{(\alpha,\alpha)(\beta,\beta)}{2(\alpha+\beta,\alpha+\beta)}(p+1)^2 .$$

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The assignment is well defined: by [L1] the Vogan diagram of a real form is determined up to the equivalence of [[def-vogan-diagram]] by the choices of Cartan involution, maximally compact Cartan subalgebra and compatible positive system, all of which enter only through the well-definedness statement of [L1], under the standing Axiom of Choice [A1]. [L1, A1]

1.2 If a real form $\mathfrak g_0$ realizes an abstract Vogan diagram $D$, then it realizes every abstract Vogan diagram obtained from $D$ by a change of base inside one marked root system: writing the realization with a triple $(\mathfrak h_0,\Delta^{+})$ whose marked root system is $(\Phi,\tau,\varepsilon)$ and whose base is $\Delta$, and letting $\Delta'$ be another $\tau$-stable base of the same marked root system, the nonnegative integral combinations of $\Delta'$ form a $\theta$-stable positive system $(\Delta')^{+}$ of $\Phi(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h_0)$, and the diagram of the triple $(\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak h_0,(\Delta')^{+})$ is exactly the diagram attached to the base $\Delta'$ of $(\Phi,\tau,\varepsilon)$: the induced involution is $\tau$ and the painting of a simple root $\beta$ is the value $\varepsilon(\beta)$. [L6]

2.1 Reduction of injectivity to the case of isomorphic diagrams: since the equivalence relation of [[def-vogan-diagram]] is generated by isomorphism moves and changes of base, and a chain of moves can be shortened to single moves, it suffices to show that (i) two real forms realizing isomorphic abstract Vogan diagrams are isomorphic and that (ii) if two real forms realize diagrams differing by a change of base then they are isomorphic; for (ii), if $\mathfrak g_0$ realizes $D$ and $\mathfrak g_0'$ realizes $D'$ with $D,D'$ diagrams of one marked root system, then by step 1.2 the form $\mathfrak g_0$ realizes $D'$ as well, so both forms realize the same diagram $D'$ and (i) applies. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 It remains to prove (i): two real forms of $\mathfrak g$ whose Vogan diagrams are isomorphic as abstract Vogan diagrams are isomorphic. Choose triples $(\mathfrak h_0,\Delta^{+})$, $(\mathfrak h_0',(\Delta')^{+})$ realizing the two diagrams, with $\mathfrak h_0 = \mathfrak t_0\oplus\mathfrak a_0$, $\mathfrak h_0' = \mathfrak t_0'\oplus\mathfrak a_0'$ and Cartan involutions $\theta,\theta'$. After transporting by the given isomorphism of abstract diagrams, there is no loss of generality in assuming that the diagrams are equal, that is: the complexification is the same $\mathfrak g$, the Cartan subalgebras have the same complexification $\mathfrak h$, the positive systems coincide, and $\theta,\theta'$ induce the same involution $\tau$ on $\mathfrak h^{*}$ and the same painting. [step 2.1, L1]

4.1 Aligning the compact forms: the forms $\mathfrak u_0=\mathfrak k_0\oplus i\mathfrak p_0$ and $\mathfrak u_0'=\mathfrak k_0'\oplus i\mathfrak p_0'$ are compact real forms of $\mathfrak g$, so by [L4] there is $x\in\operatorname{Int}(\mathfrak g)$ with $x(\mathfrak u_0')=\mathfrak u_0$; replacing the second triple by its image under $x$ changes neither the real form up to isomorphism nor the diagram class, so we may assume $\mathfrak u_0'=\mathfrak u_0$ and hence $\theta(\mathfrak u_0)=\mathfrak u_0=\theta'(\mathfrak u_0)$. [L4, step 3.1]

5.1 Aligning the Cartan subalgebras and positive systems: $\mathfrak t_0\oplus i\mathfrak a_0$ and $\mathfrak t_0'\oplus i\mathfrak a_0'$ are maximal abelian subspaces of the compact Lie algebra $\mathfrak u_0$, hence Lie algebras of maximal tori of the associated compact group, so by [L5] there is an inner automorphism $k$ of $\mathfrak g$ coming from that group with $k(\mathfrak t_0'\oplus i\mathfrak a_0')=\mathfrak t_0\oplus i\mathfrak a_0$; this gives $k(\mathfrak h')=\mathfrak h$. Replacing the second triple by its image under $k$ preserves the diagram class, so we may assume $\mathfrak h'=\mathfrak h$. The positive system $k((\Delta')^{+})$ and $\Delta^{+}$ are two $\tau$-stable positive systems of the same root system; by [L6] there is a unique Weyl group element carrying one to the other, and since the Weyl group is the analytic Weyl group of the compact group by [L5] it is implemented by an inner automorphism normalizing the common Cartan subalgebra; applying it we may assume in addition that the positive systems coincide, hence that $\Delta'=\Delta$. [L5, L6, step 4.1]

6.1 Since $\theta$ and $\theta'$ preserve the common compact form $\mathfrak u_0$ and induce the same involution $\tau$ on the simple roots of the common base $\Delta$, they agree on $\mathfrak h$: on $\mathfrak h$ both act by complex-linear involutions whose transposes on $\mathfrak h^{*}$ agree on the basis $\Delta$ and hence everywhere. Moreover, for an imaginary simple root $\alpha$ the two involutions act on $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with the same sign, because the painting is the same; write $\theta Y_\alpha = \theta' Y_\alpha$ for every imaginary simple root $\alpha$ after choosing normalized root vectors $Y_\alpha$. [step 5.1]

7.1 Normalized root vectors: by the construction of the compact form $\mathfrak u_0$ that underlies [L4], write $\kappa$ for its conjugation and choose root vectors $Y_\alpha$ relative to $\mathfrak h$ with $\kappa(Y_\alpha)=-Y_{-\alpha}$ and $B(Y_\alpha,Y_{-\alpha})=1$. Since $\theta$ and $\theta'$ preserve $\mathfrak u_0$, their complex-linear extensions commute with $\kappa$. For a complex simple root define $a_\alpha,b_\alpha$ by $\theta Y_\alpha=a_\alpha Y_{\tau\alpha}$ and $\theta'Y_\alpha=b_\alpha Y_{\tau\alpha}$. Applying $\kappa\theta=\theta\kappa$ gives $a_{-\alpha}=\overline{a_\alpha}$, and similarly $b_{-\alpha}=\overline{b_\alpha}$. Invariance of $B$ under $\theta$ also gives $a_\alpha a_{-\alpha}=B(\theta Y_\alpha,\theta Y_{-\alpha})=B(Y_\alpha,Y_{-\alpha})=1$, so $|a_\alpha|^2=1$; likewise $|b_\alpha|=1$. [step 6.1, L4, L6, algebra]

8.1 For a complex simple root $\alpha$ one has $a_\alpha a_{\tau\alpha} = 1$, because $\theta^{2}=1$ gives $Y_\alpha=\theta(a_\alpha Y_{\tau\alpha}) = a_\alpha a_{\tau\alpha}Y_\alpha$; hence we may choose square roots with $a_\alpha^{1/2}a_{\tau\alpha}^{1/2}=1$, and similarly $b_\alpha^{1/2}b_{\tau\alpha}^{1/2}=1$ for $\theta'$. Since $|a_\alpha|=|b_\alpha|=1$ and both involutions act trivially on the imaginary simple roots and on $\mathfrak h\cap\mathfrak u_0$ appropriately, there are $H,H'\in\mathfrak u_0\cap\mathfrak h$ with $\alpha(H)=\alpha(H')=0$ for every imaginary simple root $\alpha$ and with $\exp\bigl(\tfrac12\alpha(H)\bigr)=a_\alpha^{1/2}$, $\exp\bigl(\tfrac12\tau\alpha(H)\bigr)=a_{\tau\alpha}^{1/2}$ and likewise for $H'$ and $b$. [step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Let $A:=\operatorname{Ad}(\exp\frac12(H-H'))$. Then $\theta'\circ A = A\circ\theta$: the two sides agree on $\mathfrak h$ because $\theta,\theta'$ agree there and the $\operatorname{Ad}$-factor is trivial on $\mathfrak h$; they agree on $Y_\alpha$ for imaginary simple roots by step 6.1; and for a complex simple root $\alpha$ one computes $\theta' A Y_\alpha = \theta'(e^{\frac12\alpha(H-H')}Y_\alpha) = b_\alpha a_\alpha^{1/2}b_\alpha^{-1/2}Y_{\tau\alpha} = b_\alpha^{1/2}a_\alpha^{-1/2}\theta Y_\alpha = b_{\tau\alpha}^{-1/2}a_{\tau\alpha}^{1/2}\theta Y_\alpha = A\theta Y_\alpha$, using the choices of step 8.1; since the simple root vectors together with $\mathfrak h$ generate $\mathfrak g$, the displayed identity holds on all of $\mathfrak g$. [step 7.1, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Applying step 9.1 to $\mathfrak k$ and to $\mathfrak p$ shows that $A(\mathfrak k)\subseteq\mathfrak k'$ and $A(\mathfrak p)\subseteq\mathfrak p'$, and then equality holds in each inclusion; consequently $A(\mathfrak g_0)=A(\mathfrak k\oplus\mathfrak p)=\mathfrak k'\oplus\mathfrak p'=\mathfrak g_0'$, so the two real forms are isomorphic by the inner automorphism $A$. This proves (i) of step 2.1 and hence the injectivity of the assignment. [step 2.1, step 9.1, L2, algebra]

11.1 (Involution attached to the diagram) Retaining the conventions of the preceding block and the notation of the Given, choose the root-vector basis $X_\alpha$ of [L7]. Let $\Theta$ be the transfer of $\tau$ to $\mathfrak h$, defined by $\alpha(\Theta H)=(\tau^{-1}\alpha)(H)$ for $\alpha\in\Phi$ and $H\in\mathfrak h$; then $\Theta^2=\mathrm{id}$, the transpose $\Theta^*$ satisfies $\Theta^*\alpha=\tau\alpha$ and so preserves $\Phi$, and $\Theta(H_\alpha)=H_{\tau\alpha}$ for every root, because $B(\Theta H_\alpha,\Theta H)=B(H_\alpha,H)=\alpha(H)$ for all $H\in\mathfrak h$ by the $\Theta$-invariance of $B$ [L6] and $\Theta$ is invertible. Define $\Theta X_\alpha:=\varepsilon(\alpha)X_\alpha$ for a $\tau$-fixed simple root $\alpha$ and $\Theta X_\alpha:=X_{\tau\alpha}$ otherwise. On the Serre generators of the Serre algebra $\mathfrak g(A)$ of the Cartan matrix $A$ these prescriptions read $E_i\mapsto\varepsilon_iE_{\sigma(i)}$, $F_i\mapsto\varepsilon_iF_{\sigma(i)}$, $H_i\mapsto H_{\sigma(i)}$, where $\sigma:=\tau|_\Delta$ and $\varepsilon_i:=\varepsilon(\alpha_i)$ for $\tau$-fixed $\alpha_i$ while $\varepsilon_i:=1$ otherwise; the assignment preserves the defining relations: it fixes $[H_i,H_j]=0$, respects $[H_i,E_j]=a_{ij}E_j$ and $[H_i,F_j]=-a_{ij}F_j$ because $a_{\sigma(i)\sigma(j)}=a_{ij}$ for the isometry $\tau$, respects $[E_i,F_j]=\delta_{ij}H_i$ because $\varepsilon_i^2=1$, and carries the Serre relations for the pair $(i,j)$ to those for $(\sigma(i),\sigma(j))$. By the universal property of the presented algebra ([[def-lie-algebra-presented-by-generators-and-relations]]) it extends to an automorphism of $\mathfrak g(A)$, bijective because its inverse is built from $\sigma^{-1}=\sigma$; transported through the Serre presentation isomorphism $\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$ of [L3] it gives an automorphism of $\mathfrak g$, again written $\Theta$, agreeing with the transfer on $\mathfrak h$ and with the prescribed values on $X_{\alpha_i}$, $i=1,\dots,r$. Since $\Theta^2$ fixes $\mathfrak h$ and all $X_{\alpha_i}$, and these generate $\mathfrak g$ by [L3], $\Theta^2=\mathrm{id}$. For every root $\alpha$ the element $\Theta X_\alpha$ lies in $\mathfrak g_{\tau\alpha}$, so there is $d_\alpha\in\mathbb C^\times$ with $\Theta X_\alpha=d_\alpha X_{\tau\alpha}$. [L3, L6, L7, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 ($d_\alpha=\pm1$) For every root $\alpha$ one has $d_\alpha d_{-\alpha}=1$, because $$d_\alpha d_{-\alpha}=B(d_\alpha X_{\tau\alpha},d_{-\alpha}X_{-\tau\alpha})=B(\Theta X_\alpha,\Theta X_{-\alpha})=B(X_\alpha,X_{-\alpha})=1$$ by the $\Theta$-invariance of $B$ [L6] and $B(X_\gamma,X_{-\gamma})=1$ of [L7]. For $\alpha\in\Delta$ the value $d_\alpha$ is $\pm1$ by the definition of $\Theta$ on the simple root vectors. Let $\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta\in\Phi$; then $X_{\alpha+\beta}=C_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}[X_\alpha,X_\beta]$ with $C_{\alpha\beta}\ne0$ by [L7], and applying the automorphism $\Theta$ gives $$d_{\alpha+\beta}=C_{\alpha\beta}^{-1}C_{\tau\alpha,\tau\beta}\,d_\alpha d_\beta .$$ The isometry $\tau$ carries the $\alpha$-string through $\beta$ bijectively onto the $\tau\alpha$-string through $\tau\beta$, so the two strings have the same $p$ and $q$, and the formula of [L7] gives $C^2_{\alpha\beta}=C^2_{\tau\alpha,\tau\beta}\ne0$; hence $C_{\tau\alpha,\tau\beta}=\pm C_{\alpha\beta}$ and $d_{\alpha+\beta}=\pm d_\alpha d_\beta$. Every positive root of level $>1$ is a sum of two positive roots of smaller level: for $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ that is not simple, the inner product $(\alpha,\alpha)=\sum_jn_j(\alpha,\alpha_j)$ of [L8] is positive, so some simple root $\alpha_i$ has $\langle\alpha,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle>0$, whence the string property of [L8] gives $\alpha-\alpha_i\in\Phi$; since $\alpha_i=\alpha+(\alpha-\alpha_i)$ and a simple root is not a sum of two positive roots by the sign property of [L8], the root $\alpha-\alpha_i$ is positive, of smaller level. Induction on the level therefore yields $d_\alpha=\pm1$ for every positive root, and then $d_{-\alpha}=d_\alpha^{-1}=d_\alpha$ by the first display; so $d_\alpha\in\{\pm1\}$ for every root. [L6, L7, L8, step 11.1, algebra]

13.1 ($\Theta$ preserves a compact real form) Let $\mathfrak u_D$ be the real span $$\mathfrak u_D:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{iH_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{X_\alpha-X_{-\alpha}:\alpha\in\Phi\}+\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{i(X_\alpha+X_{-\alpha}):\alpha\in\Phi\}.$$ By the closure computation in the proof of [L4], applied to the normalized system $X_\alpha$ of [L7] (whose structure constants are real with $C_{\alpha\beta}=-C_{-\alpha,-\beta}$, whose vectors satisfy $B(X_\alpha,X_{-\alpha})=1$, and for which $B$ is positive definite on the span of the $H_\alpha$), the real span $\mathfrak u_D$ is closed under brackets, its three summands are pairwise $B$-orthogonal with $B$ negative definite on each, and $\mathfrak u_D^{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak g$ because it contains $X_\alpha$ and $iH_\alpha$; hence $\mathfrak u_D$ is a compact real form of $\mathfrak g$. We show $\Theta(\mathfrak u_D)=\mathfrak u_D$. For a root $\alpha$ and $z=x+iy\in\mathbb C$ with real $x,y$ one has $x(X_\alpha-X_{-\alpha})+yi(X_\alpha+X_{-\alpha})=zX_\alpha-\bar zX_{-\alpha}$, and by step 12.1 ($d_{-\alpha}=d_\alpha$ is $\pm1$, hence real) $$\Theta(zX_\alpha-\bar zX_{-\alpha})=zd_\alpha X_{\tau\alpha}-\bar zd_{-\alpha}X_{-\tau\alpha}=wX_{\tau\alpha}-\bar wX_{-\tau\alpha},\qquad w:=zd_\alpha,$$ so $\Theta$ carries the plane $\mathbb R(X_\alpha-X_{-\alpha})\oplus\mathbb Ri(X_\alpha+X_{-\alpha})$ onto the plane attached to $\tau\alpha$; the sum of these planes over all roots is therefore preserved. The Cartan summand is preserved as well, since $\Theta(iH_\alpha)=iH_{\tau\alpha}$ by step 11.1 and $\tau$ permutes $\Phi$. Hence $\Theta(\mathfrak u_D)=\mathfrak u_D$. [L4, L6, L7, step 11.1, step 12.1, algebra]

14.1 (The real form and its Cartan subalgebra) Let $\mathfrak k_D$ and $\mathfrak p_D$ be the $+1$- and $-1$-eigenspaces of the involution $\Theta$ in $\mathfrak g$, and put $$\mathfrak k_D^0:=\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak k_D,\qquad \mathfrak p_D^0:=i(\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak p_D),\qquad \mathfrak g_D:=\mathfrak k_D^0\oplus\mathfrak p_D^0 .$$ Since $\Theta(\mathfrak u_D)=\mathfrak u_D$ by step 13.1, one has $\mathfrak u_D=(\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak k_D)\oplus(\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak p_D)$, and $\mathfrak g_D$ is a real form of $\mathfrak g$: it is closed under brackets, because $\Theta$ is an automorphism with $\Theta^2=\mathrm{id}$ gives $[\mathfrak k_D,\mathfrak k_D]\subseteq\mathfrak k_D$, $[\mathfrak k_D,\mathfrak p_D]\subseteq\mathfrak p_D$ and $[\mathfrak p_D,\mathfrak p_D]\subseteq\mathfrak k_D$, and its complex span contains $\mathfrak k_D$ and $\mathfrak p_D$, hence equals $\mathfrak g$. The involution $\Theta$ restricts to $\mathfrak g_D$, acting as $+1$ on $\mathfrak k_D^0$ and $-1$ on $\mathfrak p_D^0$, and the form $B_\Theta(X,Y)=-B(X,\Theta Y)$ is positive definite on $\mathfrak g_D$: for $X=K+P$ with $K\in\mathfrak k_D^0$ and $P=iY$, $Y\in\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak p_D$, the two summands are $B$-orthogonal, $B(K,K)<0$ and $B(P,P)=-B(Y,Y)>0$ by the negative definiteness of $B$ on $\mathfrak u_D$ [L4], so $B(X,\Theta X)=B(K,K)-B(P,P)<0$ for $X\ne0$; hence $\Theta$ is a Cartan involution of $\mathfrak g_D$ ([[def-cartan-involution-of-a-real-semisimple-lie-algebra]]). Moreover $$\mathfrak h_D:=(\mathfrak k_D^0\cap\mathfrak h)\oplus(\mathfrak p_D^0\cap\mathfrak h)$$ is a $\Theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g_D$: it is abelian as a subspace of the abelian algebra $\mathfrak h$ [L6], the space $\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak h$ is a real form of $\mathfrak h$ because it contains $iH_\alpha$ for every $\alpha$, so $\mathfrak h_D^{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak h$, and if $X\in\mathfrak g_D$ satisfies $[X,\mathfrak h_D]\subseteq\mathfrak h_D$, then $[X,\mathfrak h]\subseteq\mathfrak h$, so $X\in\mathfrak h$ and hence $X\in\mathfrak g_D\cap\mathfrak h=\mathfrak h_D$. Finally $\mathfrak h_D$ has no real root: a real root of $\mathfrak h_D$ is by definition one with $\alpha\circ\Theta=-\alpha$ ([[def-cayley-transform-of-a-theta-stable-cartan-subalgebra]]), which reads $\tau\alpha=-\alpha$ for the transfer, and choosing the sign of $\alpha$ so that $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ gives $-\alpha=\tau\alpha\in\tau(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$, impossible. By the Cayley-transform description of [L2] (every $\theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra is carried by finitely many real-root Cayley transforms, each increasing the compact dimension, to a maximally compact one, and the maximally compact ones are conjugate) a $\theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra with no real root is maximally compact. [L2, L4, L6, step 13.1, algebra]

15.1 (The diagram of the constructed triple) The positive system $\Phi^+$ is compatible with $(\mathfrak h_D,\Theta)$: since $\tau$ permutes $\Delta$ and $\Theta^*=\tau$, the involution $\Theta$ permutes the simple roots, hence $\Theta(\Phi^+)=\Phi^+$. For $\Theta$ with $\Theta^*=\tau$ a root is imaginary exactly when it is $\tau$-fixed, so a $\tau$-fixed simple root is imaginary; the marking $\varepsilon'$ of the triple $(\mathfrak g_D,\mathfrak h_D,\Phi^+)$, defined by $\Theta X=\varepsilon'(\alpha)X$ on the (one-dimensional) root space $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ of an imaginary root $\alpha$, takes the value $\varepsilon(\alpha)$ at every $\tau$-fixed simple root by the construction of $\Theta$ in step 11.1. The marking $\varepsilon'$ is multiplicative on the $\tau$-fixed roots, because $\Theta[X,Y]=[\Theta X,\Theta Y]$ for $X\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$, $Y\in\mathfrak g_\beta$ with $\alpha,\beta,\alpha+\beta$ imaginary ([[def-vogan-diagram]]), and $\varepsilon$ is multiplicative on the lattice generated by the $\tau$-fixed simple roots, which contains every $\tau$-fixed root (a $\tau$-fixed root has a $\tau$-invariant coefficient vector, so only $\tau$-fixed simple roots occur in it); as the two agree on the $\tau$-fixed simple roots, they agree on every $\tau$-fixed root. Hence the Vogan diagram of the triple $(\mathfrak g_D,\mathfrak h_D,\Phi^+)$ has the involution $\tau|_\Delta$ on $\Delta$ and the painted set $\{\alpha\in\Delta^\tau:\varepsilon(\alpha)=-1\}$, that is, it is exactly the given abstract Vogan diagram $D$; in particular $D$ is realized by the real form $\mathfrak g_D$ of $\mathfrak g$. [L6, L8, step 11.1, step 14.1, algebra]

16.1 The assignment is well defined (step 1.1) and injective (step 10.1), and by steps 11.1-15.1 every abstract Vogan diagram over $\Phi$ is the Vogan diagram of a real form of $\mathfrak g$; hence the assignment is a bijection from the isomorphism classes of real forms of $\mathfrak g$ onto the equivalence classes of abstract Vogan diagrams over the root system of $\mathfrak g$. [A1, step 10.1, step 15.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Surjectivity (Knapp Theorem 6.88).** Steps 11.1-16.1 carry out the construction of Theorem 6.88 in Knapp, printed pp. 403-406, with the normalized root-vector system supplied by [[lem-chevalley-basis-and-real-structure-constants]] (Knapp's Theorem 6.6 with Lemma 6.4, printed pp. 350-353; Etingof \S 39.4): the diagram yields an involution $\Theta$ of $\mathfrak g$ by assigning to the Serre generators a sign or a permutation of the simple root vectors, the constants $d_\alpha$ defined by $\Theta X_\alpha=d_\alpha X_{\tau\alpha}$ satisfy $d_\alpha d_{-\alpha}=1$ and $d_{\alpha+\beta}=C^{-1}_{\alpha\beta}C_{\tau\alpha,\tau\beta}d_\alpha d_\beta$ with $C^2_{\alpha\beta}=C^2_{\tau\alpha,\tau\beta}$, so that $d_\alpha=\pm1$ by induction on the level, and the compact real form $\mathfrak u_D$ built from the same normalized system is $\Theta$-stable. The real form $\mathfrak g_D=(\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak k_D)\oplus i(\mathfrak u_D\cap\mathfrak p_D)$ and its maximally compact $\Theta$-stable Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h_D$ then realize the given abstract Vogan diagram exactly, not merely up to equivalence. Equivalently, in Etingof's formulation this is Theorem 40.1 with Proposition 40.3 (printed pp. 186-188), the polar-decomposition step there being replaced here by the explicit $\Theta$-stability of $\mathfrak u_D$; the earlier escalation of this item on the missing normalized Chevalley system is thereby resolved by [[lem-chevalley-basis-and-real-structure-constants]] and by [[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]].
