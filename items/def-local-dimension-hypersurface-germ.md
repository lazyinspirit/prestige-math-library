## Definition

Fix $n\ge1$, a point $p\in\mathbb C^n$ and a nonempty complex-analytic
hypersurface germ $X$ at $p$
([[def-complex-analytic-hypersurface-germ-and-reduced-equation]]). Write
$I_p(X)$ for the vanishing ideal of $X$, the ideal of all holomorphic germs at
$p$ vanishing on a representative of $X$ near $p$, and define the **local ring
of the germ** $X$ at $p$ as the quotient

$$A_p(X):=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/I_p(X).$$

Since $X$ is a hypersurface germ, $I_p(X)$ is a principal ideal: choosing a
defining equation $f$ of $X$ with square-free reduction $f_{\mathrm{red}}$, the
principal vanishing-ideal lemma gives $I_p(X)=(f_{\mathrm{red}})$, so that

$$A_p(X)=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f_{\mathrm{red}})$$

is the quotient of the holomorphic germ ring by a principal ideal generated by
a reduced germ ([[lem-vanishing-ideal-of-a-reduced-hypersurface-germ]],
[[def-reduced-holomorphic-germ-for-hypersurface]]). The ideal
$(f_{\mathrm{red}})$ is proper because $f_{\mathrm{red}}$ is a nonunit, so the
quotient is a nonzero commutative ring and its Krull dimension is defined
([[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]]).

The **local dimension** of $X$ at $p$ is

$$\dim_p X:=\dim A_p(X),$$

the Krull dimension of the local ring $A_p(X)$, that is, the supremum of the
lengths of strict chains of prime ideals of $A_p(X)$; the value is allowed to
be infinite, and it is a numerical invariant of the germ.

## Remarks

**Well-definedness.** The definition does not depend on the defining equation
or on the chosen representative. The vanishing ideal $I_p(X)$ is attached to
the set germ $X$ alone: two representatives of $X$ agree on a neighbourhood of
$p$, so they have the same vanishing ideal, and a germ vanishes on one
representative near $p$ exactly when it vanishes on the other. If $f'$ is any
other defining equation of $X$, then $f'_{\mathrm{red}}$ generates the same
principal ideal by the principal vanishing-ideal lemma, applied to $f'$ and to
$f$; hence
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f_{\mathrm{red}})=\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f'_{\mathrm{red}})$
as quotients of the same ring, with the same prime ideals and therefore the
same Krull dimension. Thus $\dim_p X$ depends only on the set germ $X$, and it
is computed by any reduced local equation. The translation convention of
[[def-reduced-holomorphic-germ-for-hypersurface]] identifies
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}$ with the germ ring at the origin, and the
definition is transported along it.

**Degenerate cases.** The definition applies only to nonempty hypersurface
germs, i.e. to nonzero nonunit equations, so the empty set germ and the whole
space germ are excluded; this is why $A_p(X)$ is not the zero ring. For $n=1$,
the square-free reduction of a nonzero nonunit germ of one variable is
$\zeta\mapsto\zeta-p$ up to a unit, so $X=\{p\}$ as a set germ and $A_p(X)$ is the quotient of
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb C,p}$ by its maximal ideal, a field; hence
$\dim_pX=0$ for $n=1$. The general computation of $\dim_pX$ for hypersurface
germs is the pure-codimension statement proved later on this page.
