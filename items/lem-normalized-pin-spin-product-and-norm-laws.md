## Statement

Let $\Gamma$, Pin, $\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}$, $N(x)=x\bar x$, and $\rho$ have the definitions in [[def-real-clifford-group-and-normalized-pin-spin]] and [[lem-real-clifford-involutions-and-norm]]. Then $N$ is scalar-valued and multiplicative on $\Gamma$. For $x=u_1\cdots u_k$ with anisotropic $u_j$,

$$N(x)=\prod_{j=1}^k(-Q(u_j)).$$

Pin is exactly $\{x\in\Gamma:N(x)\in\{1,-1\}\}$. If $\dim V>0$, it is the subgroup generated by $|Q(u)|=1$ vectors and consists of normalized anisotropic-vector products; its even part consists of the even products and is $\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}$. For $V=0$, $\operatorname{Pin}(0)=\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}(0)=\operatorname{Spin}_{N=1}(0)=\{1,-1\}$. In every dimension, $\operatorname{Spin}_{N=1}$ is the norm-one subgroup of the full even group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The real quadratic space and group definitions above.

[F1] The norm of a product of anisotropic vectors is the product of the scalar norms $-Q(u_j)$ ([[lem-real-clifford-involutions-and-norm]]).

[F2] In positive dimension, Pin is generated by vectors with $|Q(u)|=1$; in dimension zero, Pin is $\{1,-1\}$. Full Spin is its even subgroup ([[def-real-clifford-group-and-normalized-pin-spin]]).

[F3] $\Gamma$ is a group acting orthogonally; an anisotropic vector acts by its reflection. The action has scalar kernel and every element of $\Gamma$ is scalar times a product of anisotropic vectors ([[lem-twisted-clifford-adjoint-reflection-and-scalar-kernel]]).

[F4] Every orthogonal map factors into anisotropic reflections ([[lem-cartan-dieudonne-real-nondegenerate-reflection-factorization]]).

## Proof


**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For a vector product, [F1] makes $N(x)$ a real scalar. By [F3], every $x\in\Gamma$ is $\lambda u_1\cdots u_k$ for $\lambda\in\mathbb R^\times$ and anisotropic $u_j$; $N(\lambda)=\lambda^2$, so $N(x)$ is scalar. For scalar-valued norms, $N(xy)=xy\bar y\bar x=xN(y)\bar x=N(y)N(x)$, which proves multiplicativity. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 The empty product has norm $N(1)=1$. Every normalized vector belongs to $\Gamma$ by [F3], has inverse $u/Q(u)=\pm u$, and has norm $N(u)=-Q(u)\in\{1,-1\}$. Thus subgroup words are normalized-vector products, and multiplicativity in step 1.1 shows that they have norm $\pm1$. In dimension zero Pin is $\{1,-1\}$, and both scalars belong to $\Gamma$ and have norm $1$. Hence Pin is contained in the asserted norm locus in every dimension. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Conversely, take $x\in\Gamma$ with $N(x)=\pm1$. By [F4], factor $\rho_x$ into reflections and lift them by normalized vectors to a product $y$ with $\rho_y=\rho_x$. By [F3], $x y^{-1}=\lambda$ is a nonzero scalar, and step 1.1 gives $N(x)=\lambda^2N(y)$. Since both norms are $\pm1$ and $\lambda^2>0$, $\lambda^2=1$, so $\lambda=\pm1$. If $\dim V>0$, $-1$ is itself a product of normalized vectors: for $Q(u)=1$, $u(-u)=-1$, and for $Q(u)=-1$, $u^2=-1$. If $V=0$, it is included by definition. Thus every norm-$\pm1$ element lies in Pin. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The grading parity of a vector product is its number of factors modulo two, and nonzero scalars are even. In positive dimension, the even elements of Pin are therefore exactly the even normalized products, which are $\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}$ by [F2]. In dimension zero every Clifford element is scalar by [F3], so both signs are even and have norm $1$, giving all three groups $\{1,-1\}$. In every dimension the definition of $\operatorname{Spin}_{N=1}$ selects the norm-one subgroup; multiplicativity in step 1.1 ensures that it is a subgroup. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The norm of an even product can be $-1$ in mixed signature; “full even Spin” and “norm-one Spin” are intentionally distinct definitions.
