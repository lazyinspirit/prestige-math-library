## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group, let $A$ be a left $G$-module, and let $u\in H^2(G;A)$. Suppose that for every subgroup $H\le G$, $H^1(H;A)=0$ and $H^2(H;A)$ is cyclic of order $|H|$, generated by the restriction of $u$. Choose a normalized inhomogeneous $2$-cocycle $\varphi:G\times G\to A$ representing $u$, so $\varphi(1,g)=\varphi(g,1)=0$ and $g\varphi(h,k)-\varphi(gh,k)+\varphi(g,hk)-\varphi(g,h)=0$. Define the $G$-module $A[\varphi]=A\oplus\bigoplus_{g\ne1}\mathbb Zx_g$ by $h x_g=x_{hg}-x_h+\varphi(h,g)$, with $x_1=0$, and let $I_G$ be the augmentation ideal of $\mathbb Z[G]$. The cocycle gives exact sequences $0\to A\to A[\varphi]\to I_G\to0$ and $0\to I_G\to\mathbb Z[G]\to\mathbb Z\to0$. If $D$ is a left $G$-module with $\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathbb Z}_1(A,D)=0$, then for every subgroup $H\le G$ and every integer $r$, the composite of the two connecting maps from these sequences after tensoring with $D$ is an isomorphism
$$
\widehat H^r(H;D)\longrightarrow\widehat H^{r+2}(H;A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}D).
$$
Here $\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathbb Z}_1$ is the first derived functor of tensor product, and the connecting maps use the diagonal $H$-action on the tensor products.

Tor hypothesis in the original Statement, under the DC/supplied-resolution

[F2] Under DC and supplied resolutions, zero $H^1,H^2$ on every subgroup

[F4] The restriction of $u$ generates the cyclic group of order $|H|$ in
$H^2(H,A)$, for every subgroup, as stipulated in the Statement.

[F5] The specified first-variable Tor is computed by its projective

[F6] Under DC with the supplied ordinary/complete/Tor data, if $E$ is

$\operatorname{Tor}_1(E,D)=0$ or $\operatorname{Tor}_1(D,E)=0$, then
$E\otimes D$ with the actual diagonal action is Tate-acyclic on every

## Proof

**Proof technique:** split the fundamental cocycle, prove its splitting
module Tate-acyclic by all-subgroup low-degree vanishing, and separate the
general diagonal-tensor step at the same DC scope.

**Given:** The original data under [A1].

1.1 Let $E=A[\varphi]$ have the abelian group and action in the Statement.
Its cocycle equation gives $(hg)x_k=h(gx_k)$, and normalization gives
$x_1=0$ and the identity action. The map $E\to I_G$ with
$x_g\mapsto g-1$ and $A\mapsto0$ is equivariant and has kernel $A$;
the underlying abelian sequence is split by the specified bases. The
cochain $g\mapsto x_g$ has coboundary exactly $\varphi$, so the image
of $u$ in $H^2(G,E)$ is zero. This remains true after restriction to any
subgroup. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 On a subgroup $H$, $\mathbb Z[G]$ is a finite sum of regular induced
$H$-modules. Its positive cohomology vanishes by the finite
Hom/coinduction bar contraction inserting $1$; its negative homology
vanishes by the same underlying integral bar contraction, and its degree
$0,-1$ groups vanish by the invariant-sum and coefficient-sum formulas.
Thus it is Tate-acyclic. The long exact sequence of
$0\to I_G\to\mathbb Z[G]\to\mathbb Z\to0$ gives
$$\widehat H^1(H,I_G)=\widehat H^0(H,\mathbb Z)
=\mathbb Z/|H|\mathbb Z,\qquad
\widehat H^2(H,I_G)=H^1(H,\mathbb Z)=0.$$
Here $H^1(H,\mathbb Z)=\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbb Z)=0$ since $H$
is finite. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.2 The long exact sequence of $0\to A\to E\to I_G\to0$ now contains
$$0\to H^1(H,E)\to\widehat H^1(H,I_G)
\longrightarrow H^2(H,A)\longrightarrow H^2(H,E)\to0.$$
The initial zero uses the assumed $H^1(H,A)=0$ and the final zero uses
step 2.1. The middle map onto $H^2(H,A)$ is surjective, because the next
map kills its generator $\operatorname{Res}_H u$ by step 1.1 and [F4].
Both cyclic groups have order $|H|$, so it is an isomorphism. Hence
$H^1(H,E)=H^2(H,E)=0$ for every $H$. Apply the actual DC theorem [F2]
to obtain Tate acyclicity of $E$ on every subgroup, without AC or a
projective-dimension assertion. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

2.3 Without tensoring, the two exact sequences in the Statement therefore
have Tate-acyclic middle terms $E$ and $\mathbb Z[G]$. Their two
successive connecting maps give the asserted double-boundary isomorphism
when $D=\mathbb Z$, on every subgroup and in every integer degree.
The same argument gives any finite free abelian $D$ with trivial action
by finite direct sums. No AC hypothesis was used in these cases.
[F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.1 For the arbitrary $D$ in the Statement, the abelian sequence
$0\to A\to E\to I_G\to0$ is split by its displayed finite free
$I_G$ basis, so its tensor with $D$ is exact. The augmentation sequence
is abelian-split as well, so that tensor sequence is exact. As an abelian
group $E=A\oplus\mathbb Z^{|G|-1}$, so the specified
$\operatorname{Tor}_1(A,D)=0$ gives $\operatorname{Tor}_1(E,D)=0$:
add the finite free summand in degree zero to the supplied right resolution
of $A$. By step 2.2, $E$ is subgroupwise Tate-acyclic. Apply the full DC
tensor theorem [F6], obtaining subgroupwise acyclicity of $E\otimes D$.
The diagonal tensor $\mathbb Z[G]\otimes D$ is induced through
$g\otimes d\mapsto g\otimes g^{-1}d$, and is Tate-acyclic by the
explicit finite bar/norm argument of step 2.1. No nontrivial $G$-lattice
or uncountable canonical free group is treated as a trivial-action
projective module. [A1, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.2 The spliced tensor sequence
$0\to A\otimes D\to E\otimes D\to\mathbb Z[G]\otimes D\to D\to0$
has the two actual Tate-acyclic middle terms just proved. Its two coefficient
connecting maps are isomorphisms in every integer degree, on every subgroup,
by [F1]. They are exactly the composite in the original Statement, since
these are the same tensor sequences and inclusion/projection maps. This
proves the full original arbitrary-$D$ criterion under its DC and supplied
resolution conventions. The original Statement has not been weakened.
[A1, F1, F6, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remark

The proof also gives the identical double-boundary conclusion under the
reversed-order hypothesis $\operatorname{Tor}^{\mathbb Z}_1(D,A)=0$.
Indeed tensor its specified right resolution of $D$ with
$E=A\oplus\mathbb Z^{|G|-1}$: the finite free summand contributes no
positive homology, so $\operatorname{Tor}_1(D,E)=0$. Use the second
Tor branch of [F6] in step3.1; both abelian-split tensor sequences and
step3.2 are unchanged. This proves that reversed-order variant under DC,
without silently exchanging Tor variables. In either variant the splitting
module $E$ is subgroupwise CT by step2.2 and the proof gives the asserted
maps for every subgroup, integer degree and the trivial-group boundary.
