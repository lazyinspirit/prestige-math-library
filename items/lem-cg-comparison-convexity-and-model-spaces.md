## Statement

**(i) Euclidean comparison.** For all reals $a,b,c\ge0$ with $a\le b+c$, $b\le c+a$, $c\le a+b$ there are $\bar p,\bar q,\bar r\in\mathbb E^2$ with the three prescribed distances, and any two such triangles are related by an isometry of $\mathbb E^2$ ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]). Consequently every geodesic triangle in every metric space has a comparison triangle in $\mathbb E^2$, and the plane is CAT(0): for a triangle in $\mathbb E^2$ the comparison triangle is congruent to it and the defining inequality is an equality.

**(ii) The round sphere.** For $n\ge2$ the function $d_S(x,y)=\arccos(x\cdot y)$ is a metric on $S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb R^n$; $(S^{n-1},d_S)$ is a geodesic space whose geodesic segments are the minimal great-circle arcs; two points at distance $<\pi$ are joined by a unique geodesic segment; and every closed ball of radius $<\pi/2$ is convex ([[def-metric-ball]]). For every triple of side lengths with perimeter $<2\pi$ satisfying the triangle inequalities there is a comparison triangle in $S^2$, unique up to an isometry of $S^2$. Hence $S^2$ is CAT(1), the CAT(1) inequality for a triangle in $S^2$ of perimeter $<2\pi$ being an equality.

**(iii) Spherical cosine rule and midpoint identity.** For $A,B,C\in S^2$ with $a=d_S(B,C),b=d_S(C,A)\in(0,\pi)$, $c=d_S(A,B)<\pi$ and vertex angle $\gamma$ at $C$, $\cos c=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b\cos\gamma$. If $c<\pi$, $a$ is the midpoint of a geodesic $[y,z]$ of length $c$ and $x\in S^2$, then $$\cos d_S(x,a)=\frac{\cos d_S(x,y)+\cos d_S(x,z)}{2\cos(c/2)},\qquad \cos(c/2)>0.$$

**(iv) Consequences of CAT(0).** Let $X$ be CAT(0). Then: (a) geodesic segments between two points are unique and vary continuously with their endpoints ([[def-geodesic-and-geodesic-metric-space]]); (b) if $\gamma,\delta$ are geodesics with a common initial point and proportional parametrizations, then $d(\gamma(t),\delta(t))\le(1-t)d(\gamma(0),\delta(0))+t\,d(\gamma(1),\delta(1))$ for $t\in[0,1]$; (c) (hinged criterion) for a geodesic triangle with vertices $z,x,y$ and the point $p_t$ on $[x,y]$ at distance $t\,d(x,y)$ from $x$, the CAT(0) inequality $d(z,p_t)\le d_2(\bar z,\bar p_t)$ holds if and only if $$d(z,p_t)^2\le(1-t)d(z,x)^2+t\,d(z,y)^2-t(1-t)d(x,y)^2,$$ the right-hand side being the squared Euclidean comparison distance; consequently a geodesic space is CAT(0) if and only if for every geodesic triangle and every point $x$ on a side the comparison inequality $d(p,x)\le d_2(\bar p,\bar x)$ with the opposite vertex $p$ holds; (d) for every pair $y,y'$, every midpoint $m$ of a geodesic segment $[y,y']$ and every $z$, $d(z,m)^2\le\tfrac12(d(z,y)^2+d(z,y')^2)-\tfrac14 d(y,y')^2$.

**(v) CAT(1) short-geodesic estimates.** Let $X$ be CAT(1), $p\in X$, $0<r<\pi/2$ and $x,y,z\in B(p,r)$. Then $B(p,r)$ is convex, $[y,z]$ is unique with midpoint $a$, and, **whenever the CAT(1) test is admissible, i.e. $d(x,y)+d(y,z)+d(z,x)<2\pi$**, $$\cos d(x,a)\ge\frac{\cos d(x,y)+\cos d(x,z)}{2\cos(d(y,z)/2)},$$ where $d(y,z)\le2r<\pi$ makes the denominator positive. The admissibility bound is a hypothesis and not a consequence of $r<\pi/2$: a triple in such a ball has perimeter $<6r$ only, and the CAT(1) inequality is stated for triangles of perimeter $<2\pi$.

**(vi) The round circle.** For every $\ell>0$, $d_\ell$ is a metric on $S^1_\ell$ ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]); $(S^1_\ell,d_\ell)$ is a compact complete geodesic space locally isometric to $\mathbb R$, contains an isometrically embedded circle of length $\ell$, and is CAT(1) if and only if $\ell\ge2\pi$. For $\ell<2\pi$ the points $0,\ell/3,2\ell/3$ form a triangle of perimeter $\ell<2\pi$ whose side midpoint is at distance $\ell/2$ from the opposite vertex, which exceeds the distance in the spherical comparison triangle; for $\ell\ge2\pi$ every triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ lies in an arc of length $<\pi$, hence is degenerate and realizes its comparison triangle isometrically.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The conventions of [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]: the models $\mathbb E^2$ and $S^{n-1}$ with the metrics $d_2$ and $d_S(x,y)=\arccos(x\cdot y)$, geodesic triangles with their comparison triangles and comparison points, the CAT(0) and CAT(1) classes, and the circles $(S^1_\ell,d_\ell)$.

[F1] The Euclidean plane, the round sphere, the CAT(0) and CAT(1) inequalities with their perimeter restriction, and the round circle $S^1_\ell=\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z$ with $d_\ell(x,y)=\min\{|x-y+k\ell|:k\in\mathbb Z\}$ are those fixed in [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]].

[F2] $\arccos:[-1,1]\to[0,\pi]$ is the inverse of the restriction of $\cos$ to $[0,\pi]$, so $\cos(\arccos y)=y$ for $y\in[-1,1]$ ([[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]]).

[F3] Cosine is strictly decreasing on $[2m\pi,(2m+1)\pi]$ and sine is strictly increasing on $[-\pi/2+2m\pi,\pi/2+2m\pi]$; both have range $[-1,1]$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]).

[F4] $\cos(x+y)=\cos x\cos y-\sin x\sin y$ and $\sin(x+y)=\sin x\cos y+\cos x\sin y$ for all real $x,y$ ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]]).

[F5] $\sin\pi=0$ and $\sin x>0$ for $0<x<\pi$; in particular $\pi$ is a positive real ([[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]]).

[F6] $|\langle x,y\rangle|\le\|x\|\,\|y\|$, with equality if and only if $x,y$ are linearly dependent ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[F7] For $n\ge1$ the Euclidean metric $d_2(x,y)=\sqrt{\sum_{k<n}(x_k-y_k)^2}$ is a metric on $\mathbb R^n$ ([[lem-metrics-on-rn]]).

[F8] $S^{n-1}:=S_2(0,1)=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|_2=1\}$ is the unit sphere centred at the origin ([[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]]).

[F9] A function is an isometric embedding when it preserves distances, and two spaces are isometric when some bijective isometric embedding exists ([[def-isometry-and-metric-embedding]]).

[F10] The image of a compact metric space under a continuous map is compact ([[thm-continuous-image-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]).

[F11] A compact metric space is complete and totally bounded ([[thm-compact-implies-complete-and-totally-bounded]]).

[F12] A geodesic segment from $x$ to $y$ is a map $\gamma:[0,\ell]\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=x$, $\gamma(\ell)=y$ and $d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|s-t|$; then $\ell=d(x,y)$ ([[def-geodesic-and-geodesic-metric-space]]).

[F13] The reciprocal Archimedean property [[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]] gives an integer bound above every positive real, by applying it to the reciprocal of that real.

## Proof

1.1 The series of [[def-sine-and-cosine-by-power-series]] contain only even powers of $t$ in $\cos t$ and only odd powers in $\sin t$, so $\cos(-t)=\cos t$, $\sin(-t)=-\sin t$, $\cos0=1$ and $\sin0=0$; replacing $y$ by $-y$ in [F4] therefore gives $\cos(x-y)=\cos x\cos y+\sin x\sin y$ and $\sin(x-y)=\sin x\cos y-\cos x\sin y$, and the case $x=y=t$ of the cosine subtraction formula gives the Pythagorean identity $\cos^2t+\sin^2t=\cos0=1$, whence $\cos2t=2\cos^2t-1$. Since $\sin\pi=0$ by [F5], $\cos^2\pi=1$; $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ by [F3] with $\cos0=1$, so $\cos\pi<1$ and hence $\cos\pi=-1$; then $0=\cos\pi+1=2\cos^2(\pi/2)$ gives $\cos(\pi/2)=0$, and strict decrease gives $\cos t>0$ for $0\le t<\pi/2$. [F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 Let $a,b,c>0$ satisfy the three triangle inequalities and put $t:=(b^2+c^2-a^2)/(2b)$ and $s:=\sqrt{c^2-t^2}$, which is real because $c^2-t^2=\frac{(a+b+c)(b+c-a)(a+c-b)(a+b-c)}{4b^2}\ge0$; set $\bar p:=(0,0)$, $\bar r:=(b,0)$ and $\bar q:=(t,s)$ in $\mathbb R^2$. Then $d_2(\bar p,\bar r)=b$, $d_2(\bar p,\bar q)^2=t^2+s^2=c^2$ and $d_2(\bar r,\bar q)^2=(t-b)^2+s^2=t^2-2bt+b^2+s^2=c^2+b^2-2bt=a^2$, so the three prescribed distances are realised. This construction includes every positive degenerate triple because then $s=0$. If $b=0$, the inequalities force $a=c$, and $\bar p=\bar r=(0,0)$, $\bar q=(c,0)$ realise the triple; the other zero-side cases follow by relabeling. [F7, algebra]

1.3 For $x,y\in S^{n-1}$ the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality [F6] gives $|x\cdot y|\le1$, so $d_S(x,y)=\arccos(x\cdot y)$ is defined by [F2]; symmetry is $x\cdot y=y\cdot x$, separation at $x=y$ is $\arccos1=0$, and if $d_S(x,y)=0$ then $x\cdot y=1$, so $x,y$ are linearly dependent by the equality case of [F6]; as unit vectors they satisfy $y=\pm x$, and $x\cdot y=1$ excludes $y=-x$, so $x=y$. [F2, F6, F8, algebra]

1.4 Let $A,B,C\in S^{n-1}$ with $a:=d_S(B,C)$ and $b:=d_S(C,A)$ both in $(0,\pi)$, and let $c:=d_S(A,B)$, $u:=\bigl(B-(B\cdot C)C\bigr)/|B-(B\cdot C)C|$ and $v:=\bigl(A-(A\cdot C)C\bigr)/|A-(A\cdot C)C|$. Here $B\cdot C=\cos a$ and $A\cdot C=\cos b$ by [F2], so $|B-(B\cdot C)C|^2=1-\cos^2a=\sin^2a>0$ and likewise $|A-(A\cdot C)C|^2=\sin^2b>0$, using $\sin a>0$ and $\sin b>0$ from [F5]; hence $B=(\cos a)C+(\sin a)u$ and $A=(\cos b)C+(\sin b)v$ with $u,v$ unit vectors orthogonal to $C$. Expanding gives the spherical cosine rule $\cos c=A\cdot B=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b\,(u\cdot v)$, and $\cos\gamma:=u\cdot v\in[-1,1]$ by [F6] defines the vertex angle $\gamma\in[0,\pi]$ at $C$. [F2, F5, F6, algebra]

1.5 Let $X$ be a geodesic space with a geodesic triangle $z,x,y$ and $p_t$ on $[x,y]$ at distance $t\,d(x,y)$ from $x$; in the Euclidean comparison triangle $\bar p_t=(1-t)\bar x+t\bar y$, so the identity $|\bar z-\bar p_t|^2=(1-t)|\bar z-\bar x|^2+t|\bar z-\bar y|^2-t(1-t)|\bar x-\bar y|^2$ holds by expansion, and substituting the comparison distances shows that $d(z,p_t)\le d_2(\bar z,\bar p_t)$ is equivalent to its squared form, both sides being nonnegative. [F1, algebra]

1.6 On $S^1_\ell=\mathbb R/\ell\mathbb Z$ the minimum defining $d_\ell$ exists: for $\delta=x-y$, choose $N>2|\delta|/\ell$ by [F13]; when $|k|>N$, $|\delta+k\ell|\ge|k|\ell-|\delta|>|\delta|$, so a minimum occurs among the finitely many integers $|k|\le N$. Changing representatives just shifts these integers. Thus $d_\ell$ is well defined and symmetric and vanishes exactly on the diagonal, and $d_\ell(x,z)=\min_k|x-z+k\ell|\le\min_k|x-y+k\ell|+\min_j|y-z+j\ell|=d_\ell(x,y)+d_\ell(y,z)$ by choosing minimising integers; hence $d_\ell$ is a metric. The map $t\mapsto t+\ell\mathbb Z$ from $[0,\ell]$ onto $S^1_\ell$ is surjective: a finite integer bound from [F13] permits subtracting an integer multiple of $\ell$ to place each real representative in $[0,\ell)$ and satisfies $d_\ell(s,t)=\min(|s-t|,\ell-|s-t|)$ for $s,t\in[0,\ell]$, so it is continuous and sends the compact interval onto $S^1_\ell$, which is therefore compact, complete by [F11] and geodesic (the shorter interval between two parameters is mapped isometrically), and each point has a ball of radius $r<\ell/4$ isometric to an interval of $\mathbb R$, since differences between lifts in that ball are $<\ell/2$. A minimizing segment lifts successively through these interval charts, starting at any chosen lift; each chart restriction of its lift is affine of slope $+1$ or $-1$ in unit-speed parameters. Overlaps fix the same slope, so the lift is one straight interval. Therefore all minimizing segments are the shorter arcs, with two choices only at distance $\ell/2$; the identity map exhibits an isometrically embedded circle of length $\ell$ in the sense of [F9]. [F1, F9, F10, F11, F13, algebra]

2.1 Two triangles in $\mathbb R^2$ with the same three side lengths are related by an isometry: when all lengths are zero all vertices coincide, and a translation suffices; otherwise relabel so that the baseline has positive length. If $d_2(P,R)=b>0$, the map $\phi(x):=P+Bx$, with $B$ orthogonal sending $(1,0)$ to $(R-P)/b$, is an isometry of $\mathbb R^2$, and applying the coordinate computation of step 1.2 to $\phi^{-1}$ of a triangle shows that a point $Q$ at distance $c$ from $P$ and $a$ from $R$ has coordinates $(t,\pm s)$ with $t,s$ as in step 1.2; the two solutions differ by the reflection $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto(x_1,-x_2)$, so any two triangles with the prescribed side lengths are obtained from the normal form of step 1.2 by an isometry. [step 1.2, F9, algebra]

2.2 With the notation of step 1.4 and $a,b\in(0,\pi)$: if $a+b\le\pi$ then $\cos c\ge\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b=\cos(a+b)$ by [F4] and $\sin a\sin b\ge0$ from [F5], and since $c\le\pi$ and $\cos$ strictly decreases on $[0,\pi]$ by [F3], $c\le a+b$; if $a+b>\pi$ then $c\le\pi<a+b$. If $a=0$ then $C=B$ and $c=b=a+b$; if $b=0$ similarly $c=a$. Hence $d_S$ satisfies the triangle inequality, and by step 1.3 it is a metric on $S^{n-1}$. [step 1.3, step 1.4, F1, F3, F4, F5]

2.3 Let $x,y\in S^{n-1}$ and $\theta:=d_S(x,y)\in(0,\pi]$; when $\theta<\pi$ put $\xi:=(y-(x\cdot y)x)/|y-(x\cdot y)x|$, so that $y=(\cos\theta)x+(\sin\theta)\xi$ with $\xi$ a unit vector orthogonal to $x$ by step 1.3 and [F2], and choose any unit $\xi\perp x$ when $\theta=\pi$. Then $c(t):=(\cos t)x+(\sin t)\xi$ satisfies $c(0)=x$, $c(\theta)=y$, and $c(t)\cdot c(t')=\cos(t-t')$ for $t,t'\in[0,\theta]$ with $|t-t'|\le\theta\le\pi$ by step 1.1, so $d_S(c(t),c(t'))=|t-t'|$ by [F2]; thus minimal great-circle arcs are geodesic segments in the sense of [F12]. [step 1.1, step 1.3, F2, F12, algebra]

2.4 Let $a,b,c\ge0$ satisfy the triangle inequalities with $a+b+c<2\pi$. Then $a,b,c<\pi$, since $a\ge\pi$ forces $a+b+c\ge2a\ge2\pi$. If $a,b,c>0$, put $\gamma:=\arccos\frac{\cos c-\cos a\cos b}{\sin a\sin b}$; the argument lies in $[-1,1]$ directly from the length hypotheses. Indeed $c\ge|a-b|$ gives $\cos c\le\cos(a-b)=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b$. If $a+b\le\pi$, then $c\le a+b$ gives $\cos c\ge\cos(a+b)$; if $a+b>\pi$, the perimeter bound gives $c<2\pi-a-b<\pi$, hence $\cos c>\cos(2\pi-a-b)=\cos(a+b)$. In both cases $\cos c\ge\cos a\cos b-\sin a\sin b$, proving the required range without presupposing a spherical realization, and the construction $C:=(1,0,0)$, $B:=(\cos a)C+(\sin a)(0,1,0)$, $A:=(\cos b)C+(\sin b)\bigl(\cos\gamma\,(0,1,0)+\sin\gamma\,(0,0,1)\bigr)$ uses $\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1$ (step 1.1) to give unit vectors with $B\cdot C=\cos a$, $A\cdot C=\cos b$ and $A\cdot B=\cos a\cos b+\sin a\sin b\cos\gamma=\cos c$; if one of $a,b,c$ vanishes, say $a=0$, then $b=c$ and $C:=(1,0,0)$, $B:=C$, $A:=(\cos b)C+(\sin b)(0,1,0)$ realise the triple. Any two triples of unit vectors with equal pairwise inner products have spans related by a well-defined inner-product-preserving linear map, extended to an orthogonal map of $\mathbb R^3$ along orthonormal bases of the orthocomplements; so the comparison triangle is unique up to an isometry of $S^2$ by [F9]. A triangle in $S^2$ of perimeter $<2\pi$ is a comparison triangle for itself, so the CAT(1) inequality holds with equality for it and $S^2$ is CAT(1). [step 1.1, step 1.4, F1, F2, F6, F9]

2.5 Let $X$ be CAT(0) and let $\gamma,\delta$ be geodesic segments from $x$ to $y$ of length $L=d(x,y)$; the geodesic triangle with sides $\gamma,\delta$ and the degenerate third side has side lengths $L,L,0$, and its comparison triangle in $\mathbb E^2$ is degenerate with the comparison points of $\gamma(t)$ and $\delta(t)$ coinciding, both being at distance $t$ from the comparison vertex $\bar x$ along the same comparison side; hence $d(\gamma(t),\delta(t))\le d_2(\bar\gamma(t),\bar\delta(t))=0$ by the CAT(0) inequality, so $\gamma=\delta$ and geodesic segments are unique. [step 1.2, F1, F12]

3.1 A geodesic triangle in a metric space has side lengths obeying the triangle inequalities, because the metric satisfies (M3) of [[def-metric-space]]; in $\mathbb E^2$ every geodesic side is a straight segment: equality $|x-w|+|w-y|=|x-y|$ along a minimizing segment forces the two displacement vectors to be nonnegative multiples by the equality case of [F6], and their lengths fix $w$. By steps 1.2 and 2.1 every metric triangle therefore has a comparison triangle in $\mathbb E^2$, unique up to isometry, and if the triangle itself lies in $\mathbb E^2$ then it is a comparison triangle for itself by step 2.1, so the CAT(0) inequality is an equality for it and $\mathbb E^2$ is CAT(0). [step 1.2, step 2.1, F1, F6, F12]

3.2 Let $x,y\in S^{n-1}$, $\theta:=d_S(x,y)\in(0,\pi)$, and let $z\in S^{n-1}$ satisfy $d_S(x,z)+d_S(z,y)=\theta$; write $\alpha:=d_S(x,z)$ and $\beta:=d_S(z,y)$, so $\theta=\alpha+\beta$. The Gram matrix of $x,y,z$ has entries $x\cdot x=y\cdot y=z\cdot z=1$, $x\cdot y=\cos\theta$, $x\cdot z=\cos\alpha$, $y\cdot z=\cos\beta$, and is positive semidefinite because $v^{T}Gv=|\sum_iv_iu_i|^2\ge0$; by step 1.1, $\cos\theta=\cos(\alpha+\beta)=\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta$, so its determinant is $1+2\cos\alpha\cos\beta\cos\theta-\cos^2\alpha-\cos^2\beta-\cos^2\theta=\sin^2\alpha\sin^2\beta-(\cos\theta-\cos\alpha\cos\beta)^2=0$, the bracket being $\sin\alpha\sin\beta$. Hence $x,y,z$ are linearly dependent; since $\theta\in(0,\pi)$ makes $x,y$ independent, $z$ lies in the plane $P:=\mathrm{span}\{x,y\}$. Parametrise the unit circle of $P$ by $c(s)=(\cos s)x+(\sin s)\xi$ with $\xi$ as in step 2.3, so that $y=c(\theta)$; a unit vector of $P$ with $x\cdot z=\cos\alpha$ is $c(\alpha)$ or $c(-\alpha)$, and if $z=c(-\alpha)$ with $\alpha>0$ then $z\cdot y=c(-\alpha)\cdot c(\theta)=\cos(\theta+\alpha)\ne\cos(\theta-\alpha)=\cos\beta$ by steps 1.1 and 1.4, since equality would force $\sin\theta\sin\alpha=0$; so $z=c(\alpha)$, a point of the minimal arc from $x$ to $y$. [step 1.1, step 1.4, step 2.3, F2, F4, F6, algebra]

3.3 Let $p\in S^{n-1}$, $0<r<\pi/2$ and $x,y\in\bar B(p,r)$; if $x=y$ there is nothing to prove, so assume $x\ne y$ and put $\theta:=d_S(x,y)\in(0,\pi]$. If $\theta=\pi$ then $y=-x$ by [F2], so $p\cdot y=-p\cdot x\le-\cos r$ while $p\cdot y=\cos d_S(p,y)\ge\cos r$, a contradiction since $\cos r>0$; hence $\theta<\pi$. For $0\le t\le\theta$ the point $c(t)=\frac{\sin(\theta-t)}{\sin\theta}x+\frac{\sin t}{\sin\theta}y$ of the arc of step 2.3 has nonnegative coefficients by [F5], whose sum is $\cos(t-\theta/2)/\cos(\theta/2)\ge1$ by the addition formulas and decreasing cosine, so $p\cdot c(t)\ge\min(p\cdot x,p\cdot y)\ge\cos r>0$ and therefore $d_S(p,c(t))\le r$ by [F2] and [F3]; hence $\bar B(p,r)$ is convex. [step 2.3, F2, F3, F5, algebra]

3.4 Let $\gamma,\delta$ be geodesics with common initial point $x=\gamma(0)=\delta(0)$ and proportional parametrisations on $[0,1]$; if $\gamma(1)=\delta(1)$ the claim follows from step 2.5, so let $y:=\gamma(1)$, $z:=\delta(1)$ and consider the geodesic triangle $x,y,z$. In its Euclidean comparison triangle the points $\bar\gamma(t)$ and $\bar\delta(t)$ are at distance $t\,d(y,z)$ for every $t\in[0,1]$, by similarity; the CAT(0) inequality applied to the pair $\gamma(t),\delta(t)$ gives $d(\gamma(t),\delta(t))\le t\,d(y,z)=(1-t)d(\gamma(0),\delta(0))+t\,d(\gamma(1),\delta(1))$ since $\gamma(0)=\delta(0)$. [step 2.5, F1, algebra]

3.5 Let $X$ be a geodesic space in which the CAT(0) inequality holds for every geodesic triangle and every pair consisting of a vertex and a point of the opposite side. Then it holds for all pairs. If $x,y$ lie on one side the comparison preserves distances along that side; if one of $x,y$ is a vertex of the triangle, the claim is either the hypothesis (when the other point lies on the opposite side) or the equality of a side length with its comparison length (when the other point is a vertex or lies on an adjacent side and equals the shared vertex); and degenerate triangles, where some side has length $0$, reduce to these cases. It remains to treat a triangle with sides $[p,q],[q,r],[r,p]$, points $x\in[p,q]$ and $y\in[p,r]$ with $x\ne p\ne y$, $x\ne q$ and $y\ne r$, comparison triangle $(\bar p,\bar q,\bar r)$, comparison points $\bar x\in[\bar p,\bar q]$ and $\bar y\in[\bar p,\bar r]$, and the positive numbers $a:=d(p,x)$, $b:=d(p,y)$; write $\hat d:=d(x,y)$ and let $\hat\alpha$ be the vertex angle at the point corresponding to $p$ in the comparison triangle of $(p,x,y)$, so that $\hat d^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\hat\alpha$. Apply the hypothesis to the geodesic triangle $(p,x,r)$ with vertex $x$ and the point $y$ of the opposite side $[p,r]$: if $(\hat p',\hat x',\hat r')$ is the comparison triangle of $(p,x,r)$ and $\hat y'\in[\hat p',\hat r']$ is the comparison point of $y$, then $\hat d\le D:=d_2(\hat x',\hat y')$. The triangles $(\hat p',\hat x',\hat y')$ and the comparison triangle of $(p,x,y)$ have two sides in common, of lengths $a$ and $b$, and opposite sides $D\ge\hat d$, so the angle at $\hat p'$ of the former is at least $\hat\alpha$: for fixed $a,b>0$ the quantity $(a^2+b^2-s^2)/(2ab)$ is strictly decreasing in $s$ and $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ (F3), so the included angle is increasing in the opposite side. As $\hat y'$ lies on the side $[\hat p',\hat r']$, that angle is exactly the vertex angle $\hat\alpha'$ of the comparison triangle of $(p,x,r)$ at $\hat p'$, so $\hat\alpha'\ge\hat\alpha$. Apply the hypothesis again, to $(p,q,r)$ with vertex $r$ and the point $x$ of the opposite side $[p,q]$: $d(r,x)\le d_2(\bar r,\bar x)$, so the triangles $(\hat p',\hat x',\hat r')$ and $(\bar p,\bar x,\bar r)$ have two sides in common, of lengths $a$ and $d(p,r)$, and opposite sides $d(x,r)\le d_2(\bar x,\bar r)$; the same monotonicity of the included angle gives $\hat\alpha'\le\bar\alpha$, the vertex angle of $(\bar p,\bar q,\bar r)$ at $\bar p$, and $\bar x\in[\bar p,\bar q]$ with $\bar x\ne\bar p$ makes the ray from $\bar p$ to $\bar x$ the ray to $\bar q$, so this angle is exactly the vertex angle of $(\bar p,\bar x,\bar r)$ at $\bar p$. Hence $\hat\alpha\le\bar\alpha$, and the two laws of cosines $d(x,y)^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\hat\alpha$ and $d_2(\bar x,\bar y)^2=a^2+b^2-2ab\cos\bar\alpha$ give $d(x,y)\le d_2(\bar x,\bar y)$ because $\cos$ is decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ (F3). Thus a geodesic space is CAT(0) if and only if every vertex-opposite-side comparison holds. [step 1.5, step 2.1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

3.7 Let $\ell\ge2\pi$ and let $x,y,z\in S^1_\ell$ be the vertices of a triangle of perimeter $p<2\pi$, so that all three sides are $<\pi$; if vertices repeat, the two nonzero sides coincide by the circle interval charts: a minimizing circle path of length $<\ell/2$ has a lift with one fixed direction in the overlapping interval charts, hence is the shorter arc. Such triangles realize a degenerate comparison. For three distinct vertices write their cyclic gaps as $g_1,g_2,g_3>0$. If all $g_i\le\ell/2$ then the three pairwise distances are $g_1,g_2,g_3$ and $p=\ell\ge2\pi$, contrary to hypothesis; so some $g_3>\ell/2$, the complementary arc of length $s:=\ell-g_3$ contains all three points, and the two adjacent distances are $g_1,g_2$ while the third is $g_1+g_2=s$, so $p=2s<2\pi$ and $s<\pi$. Thus the triangle is degenerate and isometric, side by side, to a configuration on an arc of length $s$; placing three points of a great arc of $S^2$ of the same length $s<\pi$ realises the same three side lengths, so by uniqueness of comparison triangles in step 2.4 the comparison triangle is isometric to the triangle and the CAT(1) inequality holds with equality; hence $S^1_\ell$ is CAT(1) for $\ell\ge2\pi$. [step 2.4, F1, algebra]

4.1 Geodesic segments in a CAT(0) space vary continuously with their endpoints. Let $\gamma$ be the geodesic from $x$ to $y$ and $\gamma'$ the geodesic from $x'$ to $y'$, let $t\in[0,1]$, and let $\delta$ be the geodesic from $x$ to $y'$, so that $\gamma,\delta$ are geodesics from the common initial point $x$ with proportional parametrizations. Step 3.4 gives $d(\gamma(t),\delta(t))\le(1-t)d(\gamma(0),\delta(0))+t\,d(\gamma(1),\delta(1))=t\,d(y,y')$, and step 3.4 applied to the reversed geodesics $\bar\delta(s)=\delta(1-s)$ from $y'$ to $x$ and $\bar\gamma'(s)=\gamma'(1-s)$ from $y'$ to $x'$, which again have a common initial point and proportional parametrizations, gives $d(\delta(t),\gamma'(t))=d(\bar\delta(1-t),\bar\gamma'(1-t))\le(1-t)\,d(x,x')$. Hence $d(\gamma(t),\gamma'(t))\le(1-t)\,d(x,x')+t\,d(y,y')$ for every $t$, which tends to $0$ uniformly in $t$ as $x'\to x$ and $y'\to y$; since the geodesic segments are unique by step 2.5, they vary continuously with their endpoints as asserted in (iv)(a). [step 2.5, step 3.4, F1]


4.2 Let $\gamma:[0,\theta]\to S^{n-1}$ be a geodesic from $x$ to $y$ with $\theta<\pi$. If $\theta=0$ it is constant. Otherwise $d_S(x,\gamma(t))+d_S(\gamma(t),y)=\theta$, so step 3.2 puts $\gamma(t)$ on the unique minimal arc at position $t$, and step 2.3 identifies its parametrization. If $\theta=\pi$, choose $z=\gamma(\pi/2)$, perpendicular to $x$. Each half has length $\pi/2$ and is the unique short arc just proved; because $y=-x$, both halves lie in the plane spanned by $x,z$ and form one semicircle. This proves the claimed classification of all spherical geodesics. [step 2.3, step 3.2, F2, F12, algebra]

4.3 Let $X$ be CAT(1), $p\in X$, $0<r<\pi/2$ and $y,z\in B(p,r)$. Since $d(y,z)<2r<\pi$, segments exist. Two competing segments give a triangle with repeated vertex and perimeter $2d(y,z)<2\pi$; its comparison sides coincide, so CAT(1) forces equal-parameter points to coincide. Thus $[y,z]$ is unique. Choose $r'$ with $\max\{d(p,y),d(p,z)\}<r'<r$. The triangle $p,y,z$ has perimeter $<4r'<2\pi$. Its comparison side lies in the convex spherical ball $\bar B(\bar p,r')$ by step 3.3, so CAT(1) gives $d(p,w)\le r'<r$ for every $w\in[y,z]$. Hence $B(p,r)$ is convex, and the same argument with non-strict endpoint bounds proves convexity of closed balls of radius $<\pi/2$. [step 2.4, step 3.3, F1, F12, choose]

5.1 Let $y,z\in S^2$ with $c:=d_S(y,z)<\pi$ and $x\in S^2$; put $a:=(y+z)/|y+z|$, a unit vector because $y\ne-z$. Then $y\cdot a=z\cdot a=(1+\cos c)/|y+z|>0$, since $\cos c>-1=\cos\pi$ by step 1.1 and [F3], so $s:=d_S(y,a)=d_S(z,a)$ by [F2] and $\cos s>0$. Since $|y+z|^2=2+2\cos c$ (step 1.1) and $s=\arccos(y\cdot a)$, we get $\cos^2s=(1+\cos c)/2$, hence $\cos c=2\cos^2s-1=\cos2s$ by step 1.1; as $c,2s\in[0,\pi]$ and $\cos$ is injective there by [F3], $c=2s$ and $\cos(c/2)=\cos s>0$. Finally $\cos d_S(x,a)=x\cdot a=\frac{x\cdot y+x\cdot z}{|y+z|}=\frac{\cos d_S(x,y)+\cos d_S(x,z)}{2\cos(c/2)}$, using $|y+z|=2\cos(c/2)$. [step 1.1, step 4.2, F2, F3, algebra]

6.1 Let $X$ be CAT(1), $p\in X$, $0<r<\pi/2$ and $x,y,z\in B(p,r)$ with $d(x,y)+d(y,z)+d(z,x)<2\pi$; the sides are $<2r<\pi$, so $[y,z]$ is unique with midpoint $a$ by step 4.3 and the CAT(1) inequality applied to the pair $(x,a)$ of the triangle $x,y,z$ gives $d(x,a)\le d_S(\bar x,\bar a)$, where $\bar a$ is the midpoint of the comparison side $[\bar y,\bar z]$ of length $d(y,z)$. Step 5.1 applied in $S^2$ to $\bar x,\bar y,\bar z$ gives $\cos d_S(\bar x,\bar a)=\frac{\cos d_S(\bar x,\bar y)+\cos d_S(\bar x,\bar z)}{2\cos(d(y,z)/2)}$, and since $d(x,a)\le d_S(\bar x,\bar a)\le\pi$ and $\cos$ strictly decreases on $[0,\pi]$ by [F3], $\cos d(x,a)\ge\cos d_S(\bar x,\bar a)=\frac{\cos d(x,y)+\cos d(x,z)}{2\cos(d(y,z)/2)}$, the denominator being positive by step 5.1. [step 4.3, step 5.1, F1, F3]

6.2 Let $\ell<2\pi$ and let $x:=0$, $y:=\ell/3$, $z:=2\ell/3$ in $S^1_\ell$; the three pairwise distances are $\ell/3<\pi$, so the triangle has perimeter $\ell<2\pi$; let $a:=\ell/6$ be the midpoint of $[x,y]$, so $d_\ell(a,z)=\min\{\ell/2,\ell/2\}=\ell/2$. In the comparison triangle in $S^2$, whose sides all have length $\ell/3$, the midpoint $\bar a$ of a side satisfies $\cos d_S(\bar a,\bar z)=\frac{\cos(\ell/3)}{\cos(\ell/6)}$ by step 5.1, with $\cos(\ell/6)>0$ by step 1.1 since $\ell/6<\pi/3<\pi/2$; and step 1.1 gives $\cos(\ell/6)\cos(\ell/2)=\tfrac12\cos(2\ell/3)+\tfrac12\cos(\ell/3)<\tfrac12\cos(\ell/3)+\tfrac12\cos(\ell/3)=\cos(\ell/3)$, because $\cos(2\ell/3)-\cos(\ell/3)=-2\sin(\ell/2)\sin(\ell/6)<0$ by the addition formulas and [F5]. Dividing by $\cos(\ell/6)>0$ gives $\cos(\ell/2)<\frac{\cos(\ell/3)}{\cos(\ell/6)}=\cos d_S(\bar a,\bar z)$, that is $d_S(\bar a,\bar z)<\ell/2=d_\ell(a,z)$; the CAT(1) inequality fails at the pair $(a,z)$ by step 1.4 and [F3]. [step 1.1, step 1.4, step 5.1, F1, F3, algebra]
