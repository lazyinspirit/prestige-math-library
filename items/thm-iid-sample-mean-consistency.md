## Statement

Let $\mathcal X=\mathbb R$ with its Borel sigma-algebra, and let $\mathsf P$ be a parametric statistical experiment on $\mathcal X$. Define the mean estimand by $\mu(\theta)=\int x\,P_\theta(dx)$ and suppose it is finite for every $\theta$. For a fixed $\theta$, suppose an IID sequence $(X_i)_{i\ge1}$ with one-observation law $P_\theta$ is given and $\mathbb E_\theta|X_1|<\infty$. The sample-mean estimator $T_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$ is consistent in probability for $\mu$ at $\theta$ in the IID sample-size experiments.

The assertion is pointwise in $\theta$. The existence of a common probability space carrying the IID sequence is part of the hypothesis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The experiment, finite mean, and IID sequence in the statement.

[F1] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]: A family is IID when it is identically distributed and the whole family is independent; no moment assumption is implicit.

[F2] [[def-partial-sums-and-sample-means]]: For real random variables $X_1,X_2,\ldots$, $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$ and $\overline X_n=S_n/n$; these finite sums and means are random variables.

[F3] [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]: A finite independent family of random elements has joint law equal to the finite product of its marginal laws.

[F4] [[thm-khinchin-weak-law-for-iid-integrable-variables]]: If $(X_i)$ are IID real random variables and $\mathbb E|X_1|<\infty$, then $S_n/n\to\mathbb E X_1$ in probability.

[F5] [[def-estimator-sequence-in-iid-experiments]]: The size-$n$ sample space is $\mathcal X^n$ with law $P_\theta^{\otimes n}$, and the estimator is a measurable map on that space.

[F6] [[def-consistency-in-probability]]: The estimator is consistent at $\theta$ when $P_\theta^{\otimes n}\{|T_n-\mu(\theta)|>\varepsilon\}\to0$ for every $\varepsilon>0$.

[F7] [[def-estimand-on-a-statistical-model]]: An estimand is a specified function from the parameter set to its target set.

[F8] [[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]]: For an integrable real random variable, expectation is its finite Lebesgue integral against the probability measure.

[F9] [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]: Finite sums and scalar multiples of measurable real-valued functions are measurable whenever the operations are pointwise defined.

[F10] [[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]: If a real random variable $X$ has law $P_\theta$, $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ is Borel measurable, and $g(X)$ is integrable, then $\mathbb E_\theta[g(X)]=\int g(x)\,P_\theta(dx)$.

[F11] [[def-statistical-experiment]]: An experiment supplies the observation measurable space and its parameter-indexed family of sampling laws.

[F12] [[def-parametric-statistical-model]]: The parametric model is the map $\theta\mapsto P_\theta$ on that experiment.

[F13] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: The Borel sigma-algebra on $\mathbb R$ is generated by its open subsets.

[F14] [[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]: The usual absolute-value distance on $\mathbb R$ is a metric.

## Proof

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the statement.

1.1 The statistical experiment and parametric model in [F11]–[F12] specify the sampling law $P_\theta$. Each coordinate projection on $\mathbb R^n$ is measurable because the preimage of a Borel set $B\subseteq\mathbb R$ is the cylinder rectangle $\mathbb R^{i-1}\times B\times\mathbb R^{n-i}$ in the product sigma-algebra [F5]. Thus [F9] makes $T_n(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^n x_i$ measurable into the Borel target [F13], so it is the estimator in [F5]; applied to the given sequence, it is the finite sample mean in [F2]. By [F1] and [F3], the law of $(X_1,\ldots,X_n)$ is $P_\theta^{\otimes n}$. Since $X_1$ has law $P_\theta$ and is integrable, [F10] gives $\mathbb E_\theta X_1=\int x\,P_\theta(dx)=\mu(\theta)$; the mean is the estimand [F7]. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, F12, F13, given]

1.2 The hypotheses of [F4] hold for this IID sequence, so $\overline X_n\to\mathbb E_\theta X_1=\mu(\theta)$ in probability on its given common probability space. [F1, F4, given]

2.1 For each $n$, step 1.1 identifies the joint data law with $P_\theta^{\otimes n}$; applying the same measurable sample-mean map therefore gives exactly the error probabilities in [F6] for the absolute-value metric [F14]. Step 1.2 makes those probabilities tend to zero for every $\varepsilon>0$, which is consistency at $\theta$. No infinite-product extension is constructed or needed. [F5, F6, F14, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
