## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Every compact Lie group contains a maximal torus,
and every torus of $G$ is contained in a maximal torus.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact Lie group $G$ with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, and a torus $T_0\le G$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the Haar-based theory used in [L2] and [L4] and through the ambient theory of this page.

[L1] A torus is a compact connected abelian Lie group; a torus of $G$ is a closed Lie subgroup of $G$ that is a torus, and a maximal torus is a torus maximal under inclusion; a closed subgroup of a finite-dimensional real Lie group is an embedded Lie subgroup ([[def-torus-and-maximal-torus-in-a-compact-lie-group]], [[def-immersed-embedded-and-closed-lie-subgroup]]).

[L2] Every compact connected abelian Lie group is a torus: its exponential map is surjective with discrete kernel and it is isomorphic to $\mathfrak t/\Lambda\cong(S^1)^r$ ([[thm-structure-of-a-compact-connected-abelian-lie-group]], [[def-torus-and-maximal-torus-in-a-compact-lie-group]]).

[L3] Connected immersed Lie subgroups of $G$ correspond bijectively to Lie subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$: every subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ is the Lie algebra of a connected immersed subgroup $H$, unique up to the unique isomorphism over $G$ ([[thm-lie-subgroup-lie-subalgebra-correspondence]]).

[L4] The closure of a subgroup is a subgroup; the closure of a connected set is connected; a closed subset of the compact group $G$ is compact; the exponential image of an abelian subalgebra generates an abelian subgroup ([[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]], [[thm-closure-of-a-connected-set]], [[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The Lie algebra $\mathfrak t_0$ of the torus $T_0$ is abelian, because $T_0$ is abelian and the Lie bracket on $\operatorname{Lie}(T_0)$ is the differential of the commutator map. Among the abelian Lie subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$ containing $\mathfrak t_0$ choose one, $\mathfrak h$, of maximum dimension: such a maximum exists because the set of dimensions of abelian subalgebras containing $\mathfrak t_0$ is a nonempty subset of $\{0,1,\dots,\dim\mathfrak g\}$ and a maximum is attained there. [L1]

2.1 By [L3] the subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ is the Lie algebra of a connected immersed Lie subgroup $H\le G$; $H$ is abelian, because it is generated by the image of the exponential map of the abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak h$, and elements $\exp X_1\cdots\exp X_k$ and $\exp Y_1\cdots\exp Y_m$ with $X_i,Y_j\in\mathfrak h$ commute by the additivity of the exponential on commuting elements; moreover $T_0\subseteq H$, because $\mathfrak t_0\subseteq\mathfrak h$ and both $T_0$ and the analytic subgroup of $\mathfrak t_0$ are connected with Lie algebra $\mathfrak t_0$, hence coincide. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 The closure $\bar H$ of $H$ in the compact group $G$ is a closed subgroup by [L4], hence an embedded Lie subgroup by [L1], and the Lie algebra of $\bar H$ contains $\mathfrak h$; consequently the identity component $\bar H^0$ is a compact connected abelian Lie group, since it is closed in $G$, connected by definition, and abelian because $H$ and hence $\bar H$ are abelian. [L1, L4, step 2.1]

4.1 By [L2] the group $\bar H^0$ is a torus, and it contains $H$: indeed $H$ is connected and contained in $\bar H$, so $H\subseteq\bar H^0$. Thus $H$ lies in the torus $\bar H^0$, and $\bar H^0$ contains $T_0$ by step 2.1. [L2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 In fact $H=\bar H^0$: otherwise $\dim\bar H^0>\dim H=\dim\mathfrak h$, and the Lie algebra of $\bar H^0$, which is abelian and contains $\mathfrak h$, would be an abelian subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ containing $\mathfrak t_0$ of dimension larger than the maximum $\dim\mathfrak h$, a contradiction; here an abelian Lie group has abelian Lie algebra, and $\dim H=\dim\mathfrak h$ because $H$ is an immersed subgroup with that Lie algebra. [L1, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Hence $H=\bar H^0$ is a torus containing $T_0$ by step 4.1. If $S\supseteq H$ is any torus, then $\operatorname{Lie}(S)$ is an abelian subalgebra containing $\mathfrak h=\operatorname{Lie}(H)$, so $\dim\operatorname{Lie}(S)\le\dim\mathfrak h$ by maximality of $\dim\mathfrak h$; equality forces $S=H$, because a connected subgroup of the same dimension as a connected subgroup containing it is open in it and the larger group is connected. Therefore $H$ is a torus maximal under inclusion, i.e. a maximal torus, containing $T_0$; taking $T_0$ to be the trivial torus $\{e\}$ shows that $G$ contains a maximal torus, and taking $T_0$ arbitrary proves the containing statement. The Axiom of Choice entered only through the cited Haar-based and structure theory. [A1, L1, step 4.1, step 4.2] ∎
