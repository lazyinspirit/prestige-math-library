## Definition

Assume AC. Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space and let $\mathcal B(H)$ denote
its bounded linear operators. Applying AC to a family indexed by the natural numbers
supplies Countable Choice, the hypothesis of
[[thm-hilbert-adjoint-properties]], which provides adjoints on $\mathcal B(H)$.
A **concrete von Neumann algebra** on $H$ is a
unital $*$-subalgebra $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ that is closed in the
weak operator topology (WOT). Here unital means $I_H\in\mathcal A$; the zero
Hilbert space is allowed, with $I_H=0$ and sole unital algebra $\{0\}$. This is
the only use of AC in this definition.

For any set $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$, its **commutant** and
**double commutant** are
$$\mathcal S':=\{T\in\mathcal B(H):TS=ST\text{ for every }S\in\mathcal S\},\qquad \mathcal S'':=(\mathcal S')'.$$
Commutants are taken inside $\mathcal B(H)$. The **von Neumann algebra
generated by $\mathcal S$** is
$$W^*(\mathcal S):=\overline{\operatorname{Alg}^*(\mathcal S\cup\{I_H\})}^{\,\mathrm{WOT}},$$
the WOT closure in $\mathcal B(H)$ of the unital $*$-algebra generated by
$\mathcal S$. No bicommutant theorem is part of these definitions.

Two elementary properties will be used. For fixed $S\in\mathcal B(H)$,
$$\langle TS\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle T(S\xi),\eta\rangle,\qquad \langle ST\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle T\xi,S^*\eta\rangle$$
are WOT-continuous matrix coefficients as functions of $T$. Thus each equation
$TS=ST$ defines a WOT-closed set, and so $\mathcal S'$ is WOT-closed. If
$\mathcal S$ is self-adjoint, then $\mathcal S'$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra:
products preserve commutation with every $S$, and taking adjoints of $TS=ST$
gives $ST^*=T^*S$. These facts do not identify $W^*(\mathcal S)$ with
$\mathcal S''$.
