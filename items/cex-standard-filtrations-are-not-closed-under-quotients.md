## Statement refuted

Every quotient of a Verma-filtered object of $\mathcal O$ is Verma-filtered;
in particular the quotient of the standard module $\Delta(0)=M(0)$ by the
image of $\Delta(-2)\hookrightarrow M(0)$ is Verma-filtered.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with $\rho=1$ and dot action $s\mathbin\cdot\lambda=-\lambda-2$, and the standard modules $\Delta(\lambda)=M(\lambda)$.

[F1] The rank-one computation of the parent example gives the nonsplit sequence $0\to M(-2)\to M(0)\to L(0)\to0$ in which $M(-2)=\Delta(-2)=L(-2)$ is simple because $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle<0$ for $\lambda=-2$, while $M(0)$ is infinite-dimensional with basis $v_k$ and weights $\lambda(h)-2k$ ([[ex-projective-covers-in-the-regular-sl2-block]], [[cor-antidominant-verma-modules-are-simple]]).

[F2] A finite Verma flag of $Y$ gives $[Y]=\sum_\mu m_\mu[\Delta(\mu)]$ in $K_0(\mathcal O)$ with nonnegative integers $m_\mu$, and the standard classes $[\Delta(\mu)]=[M(\mu)]$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis; the class is additive on short exact sequences ([[def-verma-flag-and-its-multiplicities]], [[prop-the-grothendieck-group-of-o-has-simple-and-standard-bases]], [[lem-verma-flag-multiplicities-are-independent-of-the-flag]]).

## Counterexample

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

**Proof technique:** direct: compute the Grothendieck class of the simple quotient and read off a negative flag multiplicity.

1.1 The inclusion $M(-2)\hookrightarrow M(0)$ of [F1] is the map sending the highest-weight generator of $M(-2)$ to the singular vector $fv_0\in M(0)$, and $M(-2)=L(-2)$ is simple, so the quotient $M(0)/M(-2)$ has weights only in weight $0$: the $h$-coordinates of the weights of $M(0)$ are $0,-2,-4,\dots$ and those of $M(-2)$ are $-2,-4,\dots$, and $M(0)_0=\mathbb Cv_0$. The quotient is therefore the one-dimensional simple module $L(0)=\mathbb C v_0$ with $v_0$ the image of the highest-weight generator, and the sequence $0\to\Delta(-2)\to\Delta(0)\to L(0)\to0$ is nonsplit: a splitting would exhibit $L(0)$ as a one-dimensional submodule of $M(0)$, necessarily spanned by the weight-zero vector $v_0$, but the submodule generated by $v_0$ is all of the infinite-dimensional module $M(0)$. [F1, given]

1.2 Both $\Delta(-2)=M(-2)$ and $\Delta(0)=M(0)$ are Verma-filtered: each has the one-step flag $0\subseteq\Delta(\lambda)$. [F2]

2.1 The simple module $L(0)$ is not Verma-filtered. If it had a finite Verma flag, [F2] would give $[L(0)]=\sum_\mu m_\mu[\Delta(\mu)]$ with all $m_\mu\ge0$. On the other hand the exact sequence of step 1.1 gives $[L(0)]=[\Delta(0)]-[\Delta(-2)]$ in $K_0(\mathcal O)$. Since the standard classes form a $\mathbb Z$-basis, comparing the two expressions forces $m_0=1$ and $m_{-2}=-1$, contradicting $m_{-2}\ge0$. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Thus $\Delta(-2)$ and $\Delta(0)$ are Verma-filtered while their quotient $L(0)=M(0)/\Delta(-2)$ is not, refuting the statement; the quotient in the standard-filtration theorem for projectives is a direct summand rather than an arbitrary quotient, which is what makes that argument work. [step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
