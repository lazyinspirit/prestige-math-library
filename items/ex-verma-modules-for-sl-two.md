## Example

Let $\mathfrak{sl}_2=\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ have basis $e,f,h$ with
$[e,f]=h$, $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$
([[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]]), and for every complex number
$\lambda$ let $M(\lambda)$ be the induced module
$U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\otimes_{U(\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce)}\mathbb C_\lambda$,
realised concretely as $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)/J_\lambda$ with $J_\lambda$ the
left ideal generated by $e$ and $h-\lambda(h)$
([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]],
[[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]). Then $M(\lambda)$
has basis $v_k=f^k\cdot v_0$, $k\ge0$, on which
$$h\cdot v_k=(\lambda(h)-2k)v_k,\qquad f\cdot v_k=v_{k+1},\qquad e\cdot v_k=k(\lambda(h)-k+1)v_{k-1};$$
it is infinite-dimensional, and it has a finite-dimensional simple quotient
exactly when $\lambda(h)$ is a nonnegative integer. In that case the quotient
is $V(n)$ of [[ex-all-finite-dimensional-irreducible-sl-two-modules]] with
$n=\lambda(h)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ with its basis, a complex number $\lambda$ written $\lambda(h)=n_\lambda$, the Borel subalgebra $\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce$, and the quotient module $M(\lambda)$ with generator $v_0=1+J_\lambda$.

[L1] The monomials $f^ah^be^c$ in the ordered basis $(f,h,e)$ form a basis of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$; moreover $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)=U(\mathbb Cf)U(\mathbb Ch)U(\mathbb Ce)$ as a linear span ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L2] In $M(\lambda)$ one has $e\cdot v_0=0$ and $h\cdot v_0=\lambda(h)v_0$, and $f^k\cdot v_0$ is the class of $f^k$; the module is generated by $v_0$ ([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[L3] In $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ the commutation identity $[e,f^k]=kf^{k-1}(h-(k-1))$ holds for every $k\ge1$, by induction from $[e,f]=h$ and $[h,f]=-2f$. [L1]

[L4] A finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module of highest weight $m\ge0$ is isomorphic to $V(m)$ of [[ex-all-finite-dimensional-irreducible-sl-two-modules]].

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The relations $h\mapsto\lambda(h)$ and $e\mapsto0$ define a character $\chi_\lambda:U(\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce)\to\mathbb C$. By PBW, multiplication is a linear isomorphism $$U(\mathbb Cf)\otimes U(\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce) \longrightarrow U(\mathfrak{sl}_2).$$ Tensoring this factorisation over $U(\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce)$ with the one-dimensional module $\mathbb C_\lambda$ identifies $M(\lambda)$ linearly with $U(\mathbb Cf)$. Thus the classes of $f^k$, $k\ge0$, are a basis of $M(\lambda)$. [L1, L2]

2.1 Therefore $M(\lambda)$ is infinite-dimensional, and the action on $v_k=f^k\cdot v_0$ is $h\cdot v_k=(\lambda(h)-2k)v_k$ (eigenvalue computation), $f\cdot v_k=v_{k+1}$, and $e\cdot v_k=[e,f^k]\cdot v_0=k(\lambda(h)-k+1)v_{k-1}$ by [L3]; in particular $e\cdot v_k=0$ exactly when $k=0$ or $\lambda(h)=k-1$. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 If $\lambda(h)\notin\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, then $e\cdot v_k\ne0$ for every $k\ge1$. Given $0\ne w=\sum_{k=0}^Nc_kv_k$, choose the largest $K$ with $c_K\ne0$. For $k<K$ one has $e^K v_k=0$, whereas $$e^K v_K=K!\prod_{j=1}^{K}(\lambda(h)-j+1)v_0\ne0.$$ Thus $e^K w$ is a nonzero multiple of $v_0$. Hence $v_0\in U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)w$, so every nonzero submodule is all of $M(\lambda)$. The module is therefore simple and infinite-dimensional and has no nonzero finite-dimensional quotient. [step 2.1]

3.2 If $\lambda(h)=n$ is a nonnegative integer, then $e\cdot v_{n+1}=0$ by step 2.1, and $K=\sum_{k\ge n+1}\mathbb Cv_k$ is a nonzero proper submodule. Let $N$ be a submodule not contained in $K$, and choose a finite nonzero sum $w=\sum c_kv_k\in N\setminus K$. Since the $h$-eigenvalues $n-2k$ are pairwise distinct, a polynomial in $h$ isolates from this finite sum a nonzero term $c_jv_j\in N$ with $j\le n$. Then $e^jv_j=j!\prod_{r=1}^{j}(n-r+1)v_0\ne0$, so $v_0\in N$ and $N=M(\lambda)$. Consequently every proper submodule lies in $K$; hence $K$ is the unique maximal proper submodule and $M(\lambda)/K$ is the unique simple quotient. [step 2.1]

4.1 On $M(\lambda)/K$ the classes of $v_0,\dots,v_n$ satisfy exactly the relations of $V(n)$ from [[ex-all-finite-dimensional-irreducible-sl-two-modules]], with $h$-eigenvalues $n-2k$ and operators $f,e$ acting by $v_k\mapsto v_{k+1}$ and $v_k\mapsto k(n-k+1)v_{k-1}$; hence the quotient is the finite-dimensional simple module $V(n)$ by [L4]. [L4, step 3.2]

5.1 Collecting steps 1.1–4.1, $M(\lambda)$ is an infinite-dimensional highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$, and it has a finite-dimensional simple quotient exactly for $\lambda(h)\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, in which case that quotient is $V(\lambda(h))$; this proves all the assertions. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1] ∎
