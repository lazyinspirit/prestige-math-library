## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $X,Y$ integral separated finite-type $k$-schemes with rational points $x_0,y_0$. Suppose $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$. For every morphism $f:X\times Y\to Z$ to a separated finite-type $k$-scheme which is constant on $X\times\{y_0\}$, one has $f=f(x_0,-)\circ\operatorname{pr}_Y$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Global sections commute with scalar extension by any $k$-algebra, and morphisms to affine schemes are determined by these ring maps. ([[lem-nonaffine-global-sections-flat-field-base-change]], [[thm-morphisms-into-affine-scheme-global-sections]])

[F2] In a Noetherian local ring, the intersection of the powers of any ideal contained in the maximal ideal is zero. ([[thm-krull-intersection-theorem]])

## Proof

**Given:** AC, $k$, $X,Y,Z,f,x_0,y_0$ as above, and $z_0=f(x_0,y_0)$.

1.1 Let $Y_n=\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal O_{Y,y_0}/\mathfrak m_{y_0}^{n+1})$ and define $Z_n$ similarly at $z_0$. Constancy on the original fibre says the ideal of $z_0$ pulls back into the ideal of $X\times\{y_0\}$; its $(n+1)$st power pulls back into the corresponding power. Thus $f$ restricts to $f_n:X\times Y_n\to Z_n$. These finite infinitesimal neighbourhoods are affine. By [F1] and the constant-function hypothesis, $\Gamma(X\times Y_n,\mathcal O)=\Gamma(Y_n,\mathcal O)$. Hence $f_n$ factors through $Y_n$, and evaluation at $x_0$ identifies the factor with $f(x_0,-)|_{Y_n}$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $E$ be the closed equalizer of $f$ and $f(x_0,-)\circ\operatorname{pr}_Y$, using the closed diagonal of $Z$. Step 1.1 says $E$ contains $X\times Y_n$ for every $n$. In the Noetherian local ring of $X\times Y$ at $(x_0,y_0)$, the equalizer ideal is therefore contained in every power of the ideal generated by $\mathfrak m_{y_0}$. That ideal is contained in the local maximal ideal, so [F2] says the equalizer ideal is zero there. Since the ideal sheaf is coherent, $E$ contains an open neighbourhood of $(x_0,y_0)$. The product of integral varieties over the algebraically closed field is integral; an ideal on it vanishing on a nonempty open is zero, since it injects into the rational function field on every affine chart. Thus $E=X\times Y$, giving the claimed identity. AC is inherited from [F2] and the integral-variety suppliers. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
