## Definition

Let $V$ be a complex vector space and let $R\subseteq L(V)$ be a subset of the
free Lie algebra ([[def-free-lie-algebra-on-a-vector-space]]). The **Lie ideal
generated by $R$** is the smallest Lie ideal $I(R)\trianglelefteq L(V)$
containing $R$, namely the intersection of all ideals containing $R$; it
exists because $L(V)$ itself is such an ideal. The **Lie algebra presented by
the generators $V$ and the relations $R$** is the quotient Lie algebra
$$L(V)/I(R)$$
([[def-quotient-lie-algebra]]). The bracket on the quotient is well defined by
[[lem-lie-algebra-quotient-bracket-is-well-defined]]. The images of the
elements of $V$ generate $L(V)/I(R)$ as a Lie algebra, and a Lie algebra
homomorphism out of $L(V)/I(R)$ corresponds to a Lie algebra homomorphism
$L(V)\to\mathfrak g$ that annihilates $R$, by the universal property of the
free Lie algebra ([[thm-universal-property-of-the-free-lie-algebra]]) together
with the universal property of the quotient. In particular, to define a
homomorphism from a presented Lie algebra it suffices to prescribe the images
of the generators and to verify that all relations are satisfied.
