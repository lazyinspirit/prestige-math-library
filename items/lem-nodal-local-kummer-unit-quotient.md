## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and let $n\ge1$ be an integer invertible in $k$. Put $A=k\llbracket u,v\rrbracket$, $R=A[(uv)^{-1}]$, and $t=uv$. Every unit of $R$ has a unique expression $u^av^b h$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and $h\in A^\times$. Consequently the exponent map induces
$$R^\times/(R^\times)^n\cong(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^2,\qquad
R^\times/\bigl((R^\times)^n\langle t\rangle\bigr)\cong\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z,$$
where the first isomorphism sends $u,v$ to the standard basis and the second sends $u$ to a generator and $v$ to its negative. This is a unit-group calculation; it does not assert a nearby-cycles theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field $k$, the integer $n$ invertible in $k$, and the displayed rings and element $t$.

## Proof

1.1 The ring $A$ is a domain, and $u,v$ are prime elements because $A/(u)=k\llbracket v\rrbracket$ and $A/(v)=k\llbracket u\rrbracket$ are domains. A nonzero series has finite $u$- and $v$-orders, so if $u^av^bh=u^{a'}v^{b'}h'$ with $h,h'\in A^\times$, comparison of those orders gives $a=a'$ and $b=b'$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Let $r=f/(uv)^m$ be a unit of $R$, with inverse $g/(uv)^s$. Then $fg=(uv)^{m+s}$ in the domain $A$. Repeatedly cancel the prime elements $u$ and $v$ from $f$: the remaining factor divides $1$ after cancelling all factors of the right-hand side, so it is a unit of $A$. Hence $r=u^av^bh$ for integers $a,b$ and $h\in A^\times$; uniqueness is step 1.1. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Every $h\in A^\times$ is an $n$th power. Choose $c\in k$ whose $n$th power is the nonzero constant coefficient of $h$. Construct $g=c+\sum_{d\ge1}g_d$, with $g_d$ homogeneous of total degree $d$, recursively from $g^n=h$. At degree $d$, the unknown $g_d$ occurs as $n c^{n-1}g_d$ plus a known expression in earlier coefficients; the scalar $n c^{n-1}$ is invertible. Completeness of $A$ makes the resulting formal series an element of $A^\times$. Thus only the exponents modulo $n$ remain in $R^\times/(R^\times)^n$. [given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Under the exponent isomorphism, the class of $t=uv$ is $(1,1)$. Quotienting $(\mathbb Z/n)^2$ by the diagonal cyclic subgroup generated by $(1,1)$ gives $\mathbb Z/n$ by $(a,b)\mapsto a-b$. This sends the classes of $u,v$ to $1,-1$ respectively. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
