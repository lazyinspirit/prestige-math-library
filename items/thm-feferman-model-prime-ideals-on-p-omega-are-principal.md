## Statement

In $\mathsf F_{\mathrm{tf}}$, every prime ideal of the internal Boolean
algebra $\mathcal P(\omega)$ is principal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A prime proper ideal $I$ of $\mathcal P(\omega)$ in $\mathsf F_{\mathrm{tf}}$, represented by an HS name $\dot I$.

[F1] [[lem-feferman-tail-complement-automorphism]] says that, after choosing $m$ with $H_m\subseteq\operatorname{sym}(\dot I)$, a coordinate-$m$ tail flip can be chosen to fix any given finite condition and the name $\dot I$ while complementing $S_m$ modulo a finite set.

[F2] [[def-boolean-ideals-filters-and-primality]] gives downward and finite-union closure, propriety, and the prime implication $A\cap B\in I\Rightarrow A\in I$ or $B\in I$.

[F3] [[def-set-difference-and-symmetric-difference]] fixes the finite modification relation used below.

[F4] [[thm-forcing-theorem]] supplies the truth lemma used to obtain one condition forcing the actual prime-ideal decision.

[F5] [[lem-symmetry-lemma-for-forcing-automorphisms]] transports forced formulas and their names under a forcing automorphism.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** contradiction, with both alternatives forced by primality.

1.1 Suppose for contradiction that $I$ is not principal. For each $k<\omega$, primality applied to $\{k\}\cap(\omega\setminus\{k\})=\varnothing\in I$ puts one of the two factors in $I$. If $\omega\setminus\{k\}\in I$, downward closure and propriety give $I=\{A\subseteq\omega:k\notin A\}$, the principal prime ideal generated by that coatom. Thus $\{k\}\in I$ for every $k$, and finite-union closure puts every finite subset of $\omega$ in $I$. [F2, assume-contra]

1.2 Since $\dot I$ is HS, choose $m$ with $H_m\subseteq\operatorname{sym}(\dot I)$, as in F1. Put $A=S_m$, which belongs to $\mathsf F_{\mathrm{tf}}$ because its canonical name is supported by $H_{m+1}$. Since $A\cap(\omega\setminus A)=\varnothing$, F2 yields either $A\in I$ or $\omega\setminus A\in I$. Let $C$ denote the member selected by these two exhaustive cases, and let $\dot C$ be the corresponding canonical name. [F1, F2]

2.1 By F4 choose a finite $Q$ in the actual generic which forces $\dot C\in\dot I$. Apply F1 with the support bound $m$, condition $Q$, and coordinate $m$. Its tail automorphism $\pi$ fixes both $Q$ and $\dot I$. By F5, $Q=\pi Q$ forces $\pi\dot C\in\dot I$. Hence both $C$ and $\pi C$ belong to $I$ in the actual extension. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 If $C=A$, then F1 gives $\pi C\mathbin{\triangle}(\omega\setminus A)$ finite. If $C=\omega\setminus A$, automorphisms commute with Boolean complementation and F1 gives $\pi C\mathbin{\triangle}A$ finite. Thus in either case $\pi C$ differs finitely from $\omega\setminus C$. By step 1.1 the finite difference belongs to $I$; since $\omega\setminus C\subseteq\pi C\cup(\pi C\mathbin{\triangle}(\omega\setminus C))$, F2 puts $\omega\setminus C$ in $I$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Step 2.1 gives $C\in I$ and step 3.1 gives its complement in $I$. Finite-union closure then puts $\omega$ in $I$, contradicting propriety. Therefore the assumption in step 1.1 was false, and every prime ideal is principal. Both decisions in step 1.2 lead to the same contradiction, and every finite-modification use was derived from the finite singletons rather than assumed. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, discharge-contradiction] ∎
