## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $Z_1,\ldots,Z_n$ be independent random elements with laws $\mu_i$ on measurable spaces $(S_i,\Sigma_i)$, and let $\mu=\bigotimes_{i=1}^n\mu_i$. Let $f:\prod_{i=1}^nS_i\to\mathbb R$ be measurable with $\int|f|\,d\mu<\infty$. Suppose finite deterministic $c_i\ge0$ satisfy
$$|f(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},x_i,x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n)-f(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1},x_i',x_{i+1},\ldots,x_n)|\le c_i$$
for every $i$, every fixed choice of the other coordinates, and every $x_i,x_i'\in S_i$. For each $i$, define $M_i$ by integrating $f$ over the unrevealed product coordinates, using the measurable finite versions given by Fubini; set $M_0=\int f\,d\mu$ and $M_n=f(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)$. Put $\mathcal F_i=\sigma(Z_1,\ldots,Z_i)$ and $D_i=M_i-M_{i-1}$. Then $M_i$ is integrable and $\mathcal F_i$-measurable, and for every $A\in\mathcal F_{i-1}$,
$$\int_A D_i\,d\mathbb P=0.$$
Moreover, there are finite $\mathcal F_{i-1}$-measurable endpoints $A_i,B_i$ such that $A_i\le0\le B_i$, $A_i\le D_i\le B_i$ almost surely, and $B_i-A_i\le c_i$ almost surely. No choice axiom is used in this product-integral construction.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The independent random elements, their marginal laws, the product-measurable integrable function, and the deterministic coordinate oscillation bounds in the Statement.

[F1] Independence of random elements means independence of their generated sigma-algebras ([[def-independent-random-elements]]).

[F2] Independent random elements have joint law equal to the finite product of their marginal laws ([[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]).

[F3] Integrating a measurable function against a random element's law gives its expectation ([[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]).

[F4] Fubini for an $L^1$ function on a sigma-finite product gives integrable sections almost everywhere, measurable section-integral functions, and equality of iterated and product integrals ([[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]]).

[F5] The finite product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]).

[F6] Expectation of an integrable real random variable is its Lebesgue integral ([[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]]).

[F7] Each marginal law $\mu_i$ is a probability measure: the law definition is by preimages, so disjoint measurable sets have disjoint preimages and countable additivity and total mass one pass from $\mathbb P$ ([[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], the joint law of $(Z_1,\ldots,Z_n)$ is $\mu$. For $1\le i<n$, repeated application of [F4] gives a measurable version $H_i$ of the integral over the unrevealed coordinates; clause 3 of [F4] makes each such section-integral function integrable after assigning $0$ on its exceptional set. Set $H_0=\int f\,d\mu$ and $H_n=f$, which are integrable by the hypothesis. Let $M_i=H_i(Z_1,\ldots,Z_i)$. Then $M_i$ is integrable and $\mathcal F_i$-measurable by [F3], [F5], and the product-integral construction; [F6] identifies the expectation of each integrable $M_i$ with its Lebesgue integral. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6]

1.2 Fix $i$. Fubini applied to $|f|$ gives a measurable full-measure set of prefixes $p=(x_1,\ldots,x_{i-1})$ for which the integral of $|f(p,x_i,y)|$ over $\mu_i\otimes\mu_{i+1:n}$ is finite. For such a prefix there is a coordinate value $x_i^0$ whose future-section integral is finite. The coordinate oscillation assumption gives $|f(p,x_i,y)|\le|f(p,x_i^0,y)|+c_i$ for every $x_i,y$, so every future section is integrable and $g_p(x_i):=\int f(p,x_i,y)\,d\mu_{i+1:n}(y)$ is finite. Integrating the same pointwise oscillation bound shows $|g_p(x_i)-g_p(x_i')|\le c_i$ for all $x_i,x_i'$. [given, F4, algebra]

2.1 If $A\in\mathcal F_{i-1}$, the definition of the sigma-algebra generated by the prefix writes $A=\{(Z_1,\ldots,Z_{i-1})\in C\}$ for a product-measurable $C$. By [F2], [F3], and [F4], integrating $H_i$ first over coordinate $i$ gives the same integral over $A$ as integrating $H_{i-1}$. Hence $\int_A(M_i-M_{i-1})\,d\mathbb P=0$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 On the full-measure prefix set from step 1.2, define $u_i(p):=\inf\{r\in\mathbb R:\mu_i(g_p>r)=0\}$ and $\ell_i(p):=\sup\{r\in\mathbb R:\mu_i(g_p<r)=0\}$. They are finite because $g_p$ has finite range diameter at most $c_i$. The definitions and [F7] imply $g_p\le u_i(p)$ and $g_p\ge\ell_i(p)$ almost everywhere: the set of almost-everywhere upper bounds is upward closed, so $u_i(p)+1/m$ belongs to it for every integer $m\ge1$ by the defining infimum; similarly $\ell_i(p)-1/m$ is an almost-everywhere lower bound. Thus the explicitly defined sets $\{g_p>u_i(p)+1/m\}$ and $\{g_p<\ell_i(p)-1/m\}$ are null. Their countable union is null by [F7], and outside it letting $m\to\infty$ gives both endpoint inequalities. These sequences and exceptional sets are specified by formulas, with no choice axiom. Also $u_i(p)-\ell_i(p)\le c_i$: otherwise choose $\delta>0$ with $u_i(p)-\ell_i(p)>c_i+2\delta$. The sets $\{g_p>u_i(p)-\delta\}$ and $\{g_p<\ell_i(p)+\delta\}$ then both have positive $\mu_i$-measure by the definitions, so a point from each would violate the oscillation bound in step 1.2. For each $q\in\mathbb R$, $u_i(p)>q$ iff $\mu_i(g_p>q)>0$: if the latter holds, $\{g_p>q\}=\bigcup_{m\ge1}\{g_p>q+1/m\}$ and [F7] give a positive-measure higher level, while $q<u_i(p)$ means $q$ is not an almost-everywhere upper bound. Similarly, $\ell_i(p)<q$ iff $\mu_i(g_p<q)>0$, using $\{g_p<q\}=\bigcup_{m\ge1}\{g_p<q-1/m\}$ and [F7]. Repeated Fubini in [F4] makes $(p,x_i)\mapsto g_p(x_i)$ measurable on this set; applying [F4] to the indicators of these level sets makes $p\mapsto\mu_i(g_p>q)$ and $p\mapsto\mu_i(g_p<q)$ measurable. Thus both endpoints are measurable. [step 1.2, F4, F7]

3.1 Fubini gives $H_{i-1}(p)=\int g_p(x_i)\,d\mu_i(x_i)$ for almost every prefix, so $\ell_i(p)\le H_{i-1}(p)\le u_i(p)$. Set $A_i(p)=\ell_i(p)-H_{i-1}(p)$ and $B_i(p)=u_i(p)-H_{i-1}(p)$ there, and set both to $0$ on the exceptional prefix set. These are finite measurable endpoints, contain $0$, and have width at most $c_i$. For $\mu_i$-almost every $x_i$, $g_p(x_i)-H_{i-1}(p)$ lies between them; by the product law this is $D_i$ almost surely. Together with step 2.1 this proves the asserted endpoint and event-integral properties for every $i$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, given, F2, F3, F4] ∎
