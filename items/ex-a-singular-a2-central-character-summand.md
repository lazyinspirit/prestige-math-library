## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Fix a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, and a positive Borel $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^+$. Write $Q^+=\sum_i\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\alpha_i$, $\mu\leq\lambda$ when $\lambda-\mu\in Q^+$, and $w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$.

In type $A_2$, take $\lambda=-\omega_1$, with fundamental-weight coordinates $\rho=(1,1)$. Then $\lambda+\rho=\omega_2$ has Weyl stabilizer $\langle s_1\rangle$. The dot orbit consists of

$$-\omega_1,\qquad-2\omega_2,\qquad-2\omega_1-\omega_2.$$

These three simple labels form a single singular integral central-character block. Only labels and stabilizer are computed here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the setting above, and the hypotheses in the example.

[F1] The category $\mathcal O$ is the categorical direct sum of its generalized central-character subcategories $\mathcal O_\chi$, with exact component projections ([[thm-category-o-decomposes-by-generalized-central-character]]).

[F2] Fix a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, and a positive Borel $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^+$. Write $Q^+=\sum_i\mathbb Z_{\geq0}\alpha_i$, $\mu\leq\lambda$ when $\lambda-\mu\in Q^+$, and $w\cdot\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$. For a weight $\lambda$, define $$\Phi_\lambda=\{\alpha\in\Phi:\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z\},\qquad W_\lambda=\langle s_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi_\lambda\rangle\subset W.$$ Reflections and coroots are those of def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action, with the shift from def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system. The **integral-reflection linkage class** through $\lambda$ is $W_\lambda\cdot\lambda$. The word integral includes zero and negative integral pairings. If $\Phi_\lambda$ is empty the generated group is $\{1\}$. This definition uses generating reflections; no identification with a root-lattice-coset stabilizer is assumed. ([[def-integral-weyl-group-of-a-weight]])

[F3] Under the given Choice premise, let $\chi_\lambda$ and $\chi_\mu$ be the central characters obtained from highest weights $\lambda$ and $\mu$. Then $$\chi_\lambda=\chi_\mu \quad \text{if and only if} \quad \mu\in W\cdot \lambda,$$ where $W\cdot \lambda:=\{w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho : w\in W\}$. ([[cor-central-characters-are-dot-weyl-orbits]])

[F4] Let $\Phi$ be the root system from thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system. For a root $\alpha$, define the corresponding coroot by $$\alpha^\vee:=\frac{2H_\alpha}{\alpha(H_\alpha)} \in \mathfrak h,$$ where $H_\alpha$ is the vector from def-killing-dual-vector-attached-to-a-root. The associated **root reflection** is the linear map $$s_\alpha(\lambda):=\lambda-\lambda(\alpha^\vee)\alpha \qquad (\lambda\in \mathfrak h^*).$$ The subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(\mathfrak h^*)$ generated by these reflections is the **Weyl group** $W$, acting on $\mathfrak h^*$ in the usual way. ([[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]])

[F5] Every simple object of $\mathcal O$ is $L(\eta)$ for a unique highest weight $\eta$ ([[thm-simple-objects-of-category-o-are-highest-weight-modules]]). Its generalized-character membership means each $z-\chi(z)$ acts locally nilpotently ([[def-generalized-central-character-subcategory-of-o]]), while $z$ acts on any cyclic highest-weight module by the scalar $\chi_\eta(z)$ ([[lem-harish-chandra-projection-computes-highest-weight-scalars]]).

[F6] Under the given Choice premise, every object of $\mathcal O$ has a finite composition series ([[thm-every-category-o-object-has-finite-length]]). Every Verma module $M(\eta)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(\eta)$ ([[thm-verma-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]]).

[F7] A positive integral simple-root pairing gives a nonzero Verma embedding in the reflected direction ([[prop-simple-reflection-embedding-of-verma-modules]]).

## Verification

1.1 With $\alpha_1=2\omega_1-\omega_2$ and $\alpha_2=-\omega_1+2\omega_2$, the reflection formula gives $s_1(a,b)=(-a,a+b)$ and $s_2(a,b)=(a+b,-b)$. Acting on $(0,1)$ produces exactly $(0,1),(1,-1),(-1,0)$: both reflection formulas permute this set and the three displayed points are reachable. Subtracting $(1,1)$ gives $(-1,0),(0,-2),(-2,-1)$, the claimed labels. [F4, algebra]

2.1 Type $A_2$ has six Weyl elements, as is also seen by the six distinct matrices $1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,s_1s_2s_1$: multiplication by either generator permutes these six matrices. Exactly $1$ and $s_1$ fix $(0,1)$, by evaluating them. Thus the stabilizer is $\langle s_1\rangle$, and the orbit is singular. [algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 The pairings of $(0,1)$ with the positive coroots are $0,1,1$. All are integers, so $\Phi_\lambda=\Phi$ and $W_\lambda=W$ by F2; the zero pairing does not remove its reflection from the integral group. By F3 the three computed labels have one central character $\chi$. Conversely, a simple object of $\mathcal O_\chi$ is $L(\eta)$ by F5. On it every $z$ acts both by $\chi_\eta(z)$ and locally with generalized eigenvalue $\chi(z)$, so $\chi_\eta=\chi$; F3 restricts $\eta$ to the three labels in step 1.1. Thus these are exactly the simple objects of $\mathcal O_\chi$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Write the labels as $\lambda=-\omega_1$, $\mu=-2\omega_2=s_2\cdot\lambda$, and $\nu=-2\omega_1-\omega_2=s_1\cdot\mu$. Their shifted simple-root pairings are $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=1$ and $\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle=1$, so F7 gives $M(\nu)\hookrightarrow M(\mu)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$. By F3 and the highest-weight scalar action in F5, all three Vermas lie in $\mathcal O_\chi$. The unique-simple-quotient property in F6 makes $M(\lambda)$ indecomposable: in a decomposition into two nonzero submodules both would be proper, and their sum would lie in its unique maximal proper submodule. In any categorical direct-sum refinement of $\mathcal O_\chi$, $M(\lambda)$ must lie in one component; its subquotients $L(\lambda),L(\mu),L(\nu)$ therefore lie there too. These are all the simple objects by step 3.1, and F6 makes every nonzero object have a simple factor. Hence no other nonzero component exists: $\mathcal O_\chi$ is indecomposable. F1 makes it a direct summand of $\mathcal O$, so it is one singular integral block with exactly the three stated simple labels. [F1, F6, F7, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
