## Statement

Let $G$ be a profinite group and let $M$ be a free abelian group of finite rank with a continuous left $G$-action, where $M$ has the discrete topology. Then the first continuous cohomology group $H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,M)$, computed from continuous inhomogeneous cochains, is finite.

## Statement references

[[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A profinite group $G$ and a finite-rank free abelian group $M$ with continuous discrete $G$-action.

[F1] A profinite group is an inverse limit of finite discrete groups with the inverse-limit topology; finite-coordinate kernels form an identity-neighborhood basis. ([[def-profinite-group-by-inverse-limit]], [[def-inverse-limit-topology-for-finite-discrete-groups]])

[F2] In the cochain convention, a continuous $1$-cocycle is a continuous map $c:G\to M$ satisfying $c(gh)=c(g)+g c(h)$; coboundaries have the form $\delta m(g)=g m-m$. The cochain definition uses no choice axiom; the separate derived-functor comparison in the supplier assumes Dependent Choice and is not used here. ([[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]])

[F3] The continuity of the action on discrete $M$ implies that each element of $M$ has an open stabilizer. ([[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]])

## Proof

1.1 Fix a finite basis $e_1,\ldots,e_r$ of $M$. By [F3], the intersection of its stabilizers is the action kernel $U$, an open normal subgroup. By [F1], $U$ contains the kernel $N$ of a projection to finitely many finite coordinates, so $G/N$ and its quotient $Q:=G/U$ are finite. The action factors through $Q$. [given, F1, F3, construct]

1.2 Let $c:G\to M$ be a continuous $1$-cocycle. Since $U$ acts trivially, [F2] makes $c|_U$ a homomorphism. Its kernel is open in $U$ by continuity, and hence open in $G$ because $U$ is open. By [F1], this kernel contains the kernel $N'$ of a finite-coordinate projection; thus $U/N'$ is finite and $c(U)$ is finite. Since $M$ is torsion-free, its only finite subgroup is zero, so $c|_U=0$. Therefore $c(gu)=c(g)$ for $g\in G,u\in U$, and $c$ factors through a $1$-cocycle on $Q$. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

1.3 Inflation identifies $H^1(Q,M)$ with $H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,M)$: step 1.2 gives surjectivity on cocycles, and if an inflated cocycle is $g\mapsto gm-m$, the same formula on $Q$ makes the original cocycle a coboundary. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

1.4 For finite $Q$, the cocycle group $Z^1(Q,M)$ is a subgroup of $M^Q\cong\mathbb Z^{r|Q|}$ and is finitely generated. For $n=0$ the only subgroup of $\mathbb Z^0$ is zero; for $n\ge1$, any subgroup $H\le\mathbb Z^n$ is finitely generated by induction: its first-coordinate image is $d\mathbb Z$, its kernel is finitely generated in $\mathbb Z^{n-1}$, and if $d>0$ one element with first coordinate $d$ together with generators of the kernel generates $H$. Thus $H^1(Q,M)$ is finitely generated. For a cocycle $c$, put $S=\sum_{g\in Q}c(g)$. For each $h\in Q$, reindexing by $g\mapsto hg$ and using [F2] gives $S=|Q|c(h)+hS$, so $|Q|c(h)=h(-S)-(-S)$ is a coboundary. Hence $|Q|$ annihilates $H^1(Q,M)$. If a finitely generated abelian group has $m$ generators and is annihilated by $|Q|$, every element is represented by coefficients in $\{0,\ldots,|Q|-1\}$ on those generators, so it has at most $|Q|^m$ elements. Therefore $H^1(Q,M)$ is finite. [F2, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
