## Statement

Let $B_2$ be the braid group of
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]], with its single generator
$\sigma_1$, and let $\Delta$ be the half twist of
[[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]], so that
$\Delta=\sigma_1$ for $n=2$. Then $B_2$ is infinite cyclic,
$$B_2=\{\sigma_1^{k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}=\langle\Delta\rangle,$$
and $B_2$ is abelian; consequently
$$Z(B_2)=B_2=\langle\Delta\rangle.$$
This is the exceptional case $n=2$ of the centre theorem: for $n>2$ one has
$Z(B_n)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle\ne B_n$
([[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]]).
The argument is choice free; it uses the free-group description of $B_2$
rather than the free-group reduced-word theorem in the form "$\sigma_1\ne1$".

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The braid group $B_2$ with its single generator $\sigma_1$ and no relation, and the half twist $\Delta=\Delta_2$.

[F1] For $n=2$ the presentation of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] has the single generator $\sigma_1$; the braid relation $\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i\sigma_{i+1}$ requires $1\le i\le0$ and the commutation relation requires a pair $i,j\in\{1\}$ with $|i-j|>1$, so there is no relation at all. By [[def-group-presentation]] the presented group is the quotient $F(X)/\langle\!\langle R\rangle\!\rangle_{F(X)}$ with $X=\{\sigma_1\}$ and $R=\varnothing$, and the normal closure of the empty set is trivial.

[F2] The reduced words on $X\sqcup X^{-1}$ form a group with multiplication given by concatenation followed by free reduction, and the one-letter words realise the universal property of the free group on $X$ ([[thm-reduced-words-form-the-free-group]]).

[F3] $\Delta=\Delta_n=T_1T_2\cdots T_{n-1}$ with $T_k=\sigma_k\sigma_{k-1}\cdots\sigma_1$ ([[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]); for $n=2$ this is $\Delta=T_1=\sigma_1$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **$B_2$ is free on one generator.** By [F1] $B_2=F(\{\sigma_1\})/\langle\!\langle\varnothing\rangle\!\rangle$ and the normal closure of the empty set is the trivial subgroup, so $B_2\cong F(\{\sigma_1\})$ via the identity on the generator. [F1]

2.1 **The elements of $B_2$.** By [F2] the elements of $F(\{\sigma_1\})$ are the freely reduced words on $\{\sigma_1,\sigma_1^{-1}\}$. A word on this two-letter alphabet is reduced exactly when it contains no adjacent pair $\sigma_1\sigma_1^{-1}$ or $\sigma_1^{-1}\sigma_1$, i.e. exactly when it has the form $\sigma_1^{k}$ for a unique $k\in\mathbb Z$ (with $k=0$ for the empty word); for such a word no free reduction applies, so two of them represent different elements unless the exponents are equal. Hence $B_2=\{\sigma_1^{k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}$ with $\sigma_1^{k}\sigma_1^{l}=\sigma_1^{k+l}$, i.e. $B_2$ is infinite cyclic and abelian. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 **$\Delta=\sigma_1$ and the centre.** By [F3] with $n=2$, $\Delta=T_1=\sigma_1$, so $\langle\Delta\rangle=\langle\sigma_1\rangle=B_2$ by step 2.1. Since $B_2$ is abelian (step 2.1), every element commutes with every other, whence $Z(B_2)=B_2=\langle\Delta\rangle$. [F3, step 2.1]

4.1 **Assembly and contrast with $n>2$.** Steps 1.1, 2.1 and 3.1 give the infinite cyclic description and the centre. This is genuinely exceptional: for $n>2$ the centre is the proper subgroup $\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle$ generated by the full twist, and $\Delta\notin Z(B_n)$, as proved in [[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]]; the difference is that for $n=2$ the two atoms $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_{n-1}$ coincide. The item uses only the empty presentation of $B_2$ and the free-group description of its elements; no geometric statement about two-strand braids and no choice principle is used. ∎ [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

## Remarks

- **A shortcut avoided.** A tempting proof of the infinite order of
  $\sigma_1$ invokes torsion freeness of the free group together with the
  nonidentity of $\sigma_1$; the nonidentity is exactly what the algebraically
  presented free group gives by construction, and it is recorded here through
  the reduced-word description of $F(\{\sigma_1\})$ rather than through a
  separate torsion argument.
- **The two-strand exception.** The centre theorem for $n>2$ rules out odd
  powers of $\Delta$ because $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_{n-1}$ are distinct atoms;
  for $n=2$ there is only one atom, all powers of $\Delta=\sigma_1$ are
  central, and the centre is the whole group.
- Nothing here uses the Axiom of Choice or any weaker choice principle.
