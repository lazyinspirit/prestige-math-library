## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. There is exactly one probability measure $\mu$ on
$$\mathcal B(C([0,\infty),\mathbb R))$$
for which the coordinate process $\pi_t(f)=f(t)$ is centered Gaussian with
$$\operatorname{Cov}_\mu(\pi_s,\pi_t)=\min(s,t),\qquad s,t\ge0.$$
That probability is Wiener measure.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the uoc path space $C=C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)$, and its coordinate maps $\pi_t$.

[F1] Wiener measure exists as a probability on $\mathcal B(C)$, and its coordinate process has the Brownian finite-dimensional distributions. A Brownian motion is equivalently a centered Gaussian process with covariance $\min(s,t)$, in addition to path continuity. [[def-wiener-measure-on-continuous-path-space]] [[def-brownian-motion]] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F2] Gaussian processes with identical mean and covariance functions have identical finite-dimensional laws, including singular laws and repeated-time lists. [[lem-mean-and-covariance-determine-gaussian-finite-dimensional-laws]]

[F3] The Borel sigma-algebra of $C$ is generated by the nonnegative rational coordinate maps. [[lem-borel-sigma-algebra-of-continuous-path-space-is-generated-by-coordinates]]

[F4] A lambda-system contains the whole space, relative differences of nested members, and increasing countable unions. Measures are continuous from below, and a lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the sigma-algebra generated by that pi-system. [[def-lambda-system]] [[thm-continuity-from-below-for-measures]] [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], Wiener measure is a probability measure on $\mathcal B(C)$ and its coordinate process is centered Gaussian with covariance $\min(s,t)$. Thus at least one probability with the stated property exists. AC is used here through the normal-law and Brownian construction interfaces; the uniqueness argument below makes no further use of it. [F1]

1.2 Let $\mu$ and $\nu$ be two probabilities with the stated property. By [F2], for every $k\ge1$, every nonnegative rational list $q_1,\ldots,q_k$ (with repetitions allowed), and Borel sets $A_i\subseteq\mathbb R$, $$\mu\bigcap_{i=1}^k\{f:f(q_i)\in A_i\}=\nu\bigcap_{i=1}^k\{f:f(q_i)\in A_i\}.$$ Indeed this is equality of the two finite-dimensional laws on the Borel rectangle $A_1\times\cdots\times A_k$. The same equality is trivial for the empty intersection $C$. [F2]

2.1 Let $\mathcal P$ be the family of the finite intersections in step 1.2, including the empty intersection $C$. It is a pi-system because the intersection of two members is obtained by concatenating their two finite lists. Moreover $\sigma(\mathcal P)=\mathcal B(C)$: the family contains every one-coordinate set $\pi_q^{-1}(A)$, while every member is a finite intersection of such sets; now apply [F3]. [F3, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 Put $\mathcal D=\{A\in\mathcal B(C):\mu(A)=\nu(A)\}$. It contains $C$ because both measures have total mass one. If $A\subseteq B$ and $A,B\in\mathcal D$, finite additivity gives $\mu(B\setminus A)=\mu(B)-\mu(A)=\nu(B)-\nu(A)=\nu(B\setminus A)$. If $A_n\uparrow A$ with every $A_n\in\mathcal D$, continuity from below gives $\mu(A)=\lim_n\mu(A_n)=\lim_n\nu(A_n)=\nu(A)$. Hence $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system. Step 1.2 says $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal D$, so [F4] and step 2.1 give $\mathcal B(C)=\sigma(\mathcal P)\subseteq\mathcal D$. Thus $\mu=\nu$. [F4, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 Step 1.1 proves existence and step 3.1 proves uniqueness, so Wiener measure is exactly the asserted probability. The covariance at time zero is zero, singleton laws and singular repeated-time vectors are covered by [F2], and both directions of “exactly one” have been established. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F2] ∎
