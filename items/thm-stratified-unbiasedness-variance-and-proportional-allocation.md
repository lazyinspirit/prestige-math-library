## Statement

Use the setup of [[def-stratified-monte-carlo-estimator]] and suppose $\int_{\mathsf X} f^2\,dP<\infty$. Write $\mu=\int_{\mathsf X}f\,dP$, $\mu_h=E_{P_h}f$, and $\sigma_h^2=\operatorname{Var}_{P_h}(f)$. The estimator is unbiased and

$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_{\rm strat})=\sum_{h=1}^H\frac{p_h^2\sigma_h^2}{n_h}.$$

If, additionally, each $np_h$ is a positive integer and the fixed allocation is proportional, $n_h=np_h$, then the variance is no larger than that of the ordinary IID sample mean of the same total size $n$:

$$\operatorname{Var}(\widehat\mu_{\rm strat})=\frac1n\sum_h p_h\sigma_h^2\le\frac{\operatorname{Var}_P(f)}n.$$

The comparison is only asserted for this proportional allocation; a general fixed allocation has the variance in the first display and need not beat the IID comparator.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite measurable partition, positive stratum probabilities, fixed positive sample counts, independent within- and across-stratum samples, and square-integrable $f$ in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-stratified-monte-carlo-estimator]]: $P_h(B)=P(B\cap A_h)/p_h$, and $\widehat\mu_{\rm strat}=\sum_h p_h\bar f_h$.

[F2] [[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]]: The samples within a stratum are IID when their laws agree and the family is mutually independent.

[F3] [[thm-change-of-variables-for-expectation]]: Expectation of a measurable function of a variable with law $P_h$ is its integral against $P_h$.

[F4] [[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]: Expectation is linear for finite integrable sums.

[F5] [[thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables]]: Products of independent integrable variables have factored expectations.

[F6] [[def-moments-variance-and-covariance]]: Variance and covariance are centered second moments and products.

[F7] [[def-independent-random-elements]]: A family of random elements is independent when their generated sigma-algebras are independent.

[F8] [[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]]: Disjoint groups of an independent family of sigma-algebras generate independent sigma-algebras.

[F9] [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]: Finite sums and products of measurable real-valued functions are measurable.



## Proof

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 For each $h$, [F1] gives $\int_{\mathsf X}f^2\,dP_h=p_h^{-1}\int_{A_h}f^2\,dP<\infty$. Since $|f|\le1+f^2$, $f$ is integrable under $P$ and every $P_h$. Thus [F3] gives $\mu_h=p_h^{-1}\int_{A_h}f\,dP$, and [F4] plus the finite partition identity yield $E\widehat\mu_{\rm strat}=\sum_h p_h\mu_h=\sum_h\int_{A_h}f\,dP=\int f\,dP=\mu$. [F1, F3, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 For fixed $h$, expand the centered square of $\bar f_h$. The diagonal terms give $\sigma_h^2/n_h$; for distinct draws the centered product expectation is zero by independence and [F5]. Thus $\operatorname{Var}(\bar f_h)=\sigma_h^2/n_h$. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Splitting $\int(f-\mu)^2dP$ over the partition and writing $f-\mu=(f-\mu_h)+(\mu_h-\mu)$ on $A_h$, the cross term integrates to zero since $\int_{A_h}(f-\mu_h)dP=p_hE_{P_h}(f-\mu_h)=0$. Therefore $\operatorname{Var}_P(f)=\sum_h p_h\sigma_h^2+\sum_h p_h(\mu_h-\mu)^2$. [F1, F3, F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F1] and [F7], the sigma-algebras generated by all individual draws are independent. Grouping them by stratum and applying [F8] shows that the sigma-algebras generated by the disjoint stratum blocks are independent. Each $\bar f_h$ is measurable with respect to its block sigma-algebra because it is a finite sum of measurable values of $f$, using [F9]; hence the stratum averages are independent. Applying [F5] to their centered products and expanding the variance of $\sum_h p_h\bar f_h$ gives $\sum_h p_h^2\sigma_h^2/n_h$. [F1, F5, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Under $n_h=np_h$, step 3.1 becomes $n^{-1}\sum_h p_h\sigma_h^2$. The ordinary mean of $n$ IID $P$-draws has variance $\operatorname{Var}_P(f)/n$, by the same centered-square expansion as step 2.1. Step 2.2 shows the difference is $n^{-1}\sum_h p_h(\mu_h-\mu)^2\ge0$, proving the comparison. [F2, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
