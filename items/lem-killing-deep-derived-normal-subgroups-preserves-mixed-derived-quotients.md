## Statement

Let $r\ge0$, let $G$ be a group, and let $N\triangleleft G$ satisfy $N\subseteq G^{(r)}$, where $G^{(0)}=G$ and $G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]$. Put $H=G/N$ and let $q:G\to H$ be the quotient. Supply a finite list of commutative unital rings $R_0,\ldots,R_{r-1}$. For a group $L$ define, through index $r$,
$$P^0L=L,\qquad P^{i+1}L=\ker\!\left(P^iL\longrightarrow (P^iL/[P^iL,P^iL])\otimes_{\mathbb Z}R_i\right).$$
The tensor map sends an abelianized class $a$ to $a\otimes1$. Then for $0\le i\le r$, $N\subseteq P^iG$ and the restriction of $q$ induces
$$P^iG/N\cong P^iH,\qquad G/P^iG\cong H/P^iH.$$
For $0\le i<r$ it also induces
$$P^iG/P^{i+1}G\cong P^iH/P^{i+1}H.$$
These are the actual natural quotient maps. Every $P^iL$ is characteristic in $L$ and contains $L^{(i)}$. The same $q$ induces $G/G^{(r)}\cong H/H^{(r)}$. If all $R_i=\mathbb Q$, then $G/P^rG$ is PTFA. A final choice $R_i=\mathbb F_p$ is allowed in the quotient conclusions, but no PTFA assertion is made for such a series. No choice axiom is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The group, normal subgroup, integer and finite ring list in the Statement. A commutator subgroup means the subgroup of finite products of commutators and their inverses; all uses below are on such finite words. Tensor products are the abelian groups generated by pairs modulo the additive and balancing relations.

## Proof

1.1 Each $P^{i+1}L$ is a subgroup of $P^iL$. An automorphism of $L$ preserves $P^iL$ inductively, and induces an automorphism of its abelianization and of the displayed tensor group; their maps commute with $a\mapsto a\otimes1$. Its kernel is therefore preserved, proving that all $P^iL$ are characteristic and in particular normal. Also $L^{(i)}\subseteq P^iL$ by induction: the assertion is equality at zero, and commutators of elements of $L^{(i)}$ lie in $[P^iL,P^iL]$, which maps to zero under abelianization and hence lies in $P^{i+1}L$. Derived subgroups are descending by their finite commutator definition. Consequently $N\subseteq G^{(r)}\subseteq G^{(i)}\subseteq P^iG$ for every $i\le r$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We prove $P^iH=q(P^iG)$ through index $r$. It holds at zero. Suppose it holds at $i<r$ and put $A=P^iG$. The restriction $q:A\to P^iH$ is onto with kernel $N$. Moreover $$N\subseteq G^{(r)}\subseteq G^{(i+1)}=[G^{(i)},G^{(i)}]\subseteq[A,A].$$ The abelianization of $A/N$ is $A/[A,A]$: expanding commutators shows $[A/N,A/N]=[A,A]/N$, since every finite commutator word in the quotient lifts by finitely many individual lifts. Thus the induced abelianization map is an isomorphism. Tensoring this isomorphism with $R_i$ is an isomorphism too, because tensoring its inverse gives an inverse on every generator $a\otimes u$; exactness of tensor is not being assumed. The two maps to these isomorphic tensor groups commute with $q$. Their kernels consequently satisfy $P^{i+1}H=q(P^{i+1}G)$: a fixed element of the former has a lift in $A$, and its tensor value is zero by the isomorphism, hence that lift is in $P^{i+1}G$. This proves the finite induction. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The kernel of each restricted $q:P^iG\to P^iH$ is $N$, already contained in $P^iG$ by step 1.1, proving the first displayed isomorphism. The preimage of $P^iH=q(P^iG)$ is $P^iG$, since it already contains $N$; this proves the second. Similarly the preimage in $P^iG$ of $P^{i+1}H$ is $P^{i+1}G$, giving the successive-quotient isomorphisms. For ordinary derived subgroups, expanding commutators and lifting each finite commutator word proves inductively $q(G^{(i)})=H^{(i)}$. Since $N\subseteq G^{(r)}$, the same preimage argument gives $G/G^{(r)}\cong H/H^{(r)}$. None of these conclusions follows merely from characteristicness: their actual kernels and preimages have been identified. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 When all $R_i=\mathbb Q$, each quotient $P^iG/P^{i+1}G$ embeds, by the kernel definition, in the additive group $(P^iG/[P^iG,P^iG])\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$. That group is a rational vector space by scalar multiplication on the second tensor coordinate, so its additive group is torsion-free abelian: multiplication by a nonzero integer has inverse multiplication by its reciprocal. The finite normal series $$G/P^rG\ \trianglerighteq\ P^1G/P^rG\ \trianglerighteq\cdots\trianglerighteq\ P^rG/P^rG=1$$ therefore has torsion-free abelian factors and is precisely a PTFA series, with trivial factors permitted. For $r=0$ the quotient is trivial and the series is empty. For other ring lists steps 1.1–3.1 are unchanged, but a positive-characteristic tensor group can have additive torsion, so this last PTFA argument is intentionally not asserted. Every lift above concerned one finite word or one individual quotient element; no infinite set of representatives or vector-space basis was selected. This proves the full natural quotient assertions without a choice principle. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
