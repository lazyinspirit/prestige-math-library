## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $E$ be a splitting field of $t^{n}-1$ over $\mathbb Q$, let
$\zeta\in E$ be a primitive $n$-th root of unity
([[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]]), let $f\in\mathbb Q[t]$ be the minimal
polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\mathbb Q$
([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]), and let $p$ be a prime
([[def-prime]]) with $p\nmid n$ ([[def-divides-in-z]]). Then

$$f(\zeta^{p})=0 .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The data of the statement; $\mathbb Q$ is an ordered field ([[thm-rat-ordered-field]]), so $m\cdot1>0$ and in particular $m\cdot1\ne0$ for every $m\ge1$, whence $\operatorname{char}\mathbb Q=0$ ([[def-ring-characteristic]]), which divides no $n\ge1$, so $t^{n}-1$ is separable over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mu_n(E)$ is cyclic of order $n$ generated by $\zeta$ ([[thm-separability-of-x-n-minus-one-and-the-order-of-the-group-of-roots-of-unity]], [[prop-the-roots-of-unity-in-a-field-form-a-finite-cyclic-group]]); $\Phi_n$ denotes the cyclotomic polynomial ([[def-cyclotomic-polynomial]]) and also its images in $\mathbb Q[t]$ and in $(\mathbb Z/p)[t]$, where reduction is the ring homomorphism of [[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]] and $\mathbb Z/p$ is a field ([[thm-z-mod-p-is-a-field]]).

[L1] The roots of $\Phi_n$ in $E$ are exactly the primitive $n$-th roots of unity in $E$ ([[thm-the-roots-of-the-cyclotomic-polynomial-are-the-primitive-roots-of-unity]]); $\Phi_n$ is monic in $\mathbb Z[t]$ of degree $\varphi(n)$ and divides $t^{n}-1$ there ([[thm-cyclotomic-polynomials-are-monic-integer-polynomials-of-degree-euler-totient]], [[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]).

[L2] For $a$ algebraic over a field $K$ there is a unique monic irreducible $m_a\in K[t]$ with $h(a)=0$ if and only if $m_a\mid h$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[L3] For a commutative ring $R$ and monic $g\in R[x]$, every $h\in R[x]$ has unique $q,r\in R[x]$ with $h=qg+r$ and $r=0$ or $\deg r<\deg g$ ([[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]).

[L4] For nonzero $u,v\in\mathbb Z[x]$, $\operatorname{cont}(uv)=\operatorname{cont}(u)\operatorname{cont}(v)$ ([[lem-gauss-content-lemma]]); content is the nonnegative gcd of the coefficients and primitive means content $1$ ([[def-content-and-primitive-integer-polynomial]]); a nonzero integer polynomial is primitive exactly when no prime divides all its coefficients ([[lem-content-divisibility-characterisation]]).

[L5] In a field $L$ of characteristic $p>0$ the map $x\mapsto x^{p}$ is a field endomorphism ([[thm-frobenius-endomorphism-and-finite-field-automorphism]]); and every $c$ in a field with $p$ elements satisfies $c^{p}=c$ ([[prop-finite-fields-are-splitting-fields-of-x-q-minus-x]]).

[L6] $t^{n}-1$ is separable over $\mathbb Z/p$ when $p\nmid n$, that is it has no repeated root in any extension field ([[thm-separability-of-x-n-minus-one-and-the-order-of-the-group-of-roots-of-unity]], [[def-repeated-root-and-separable-polynomial]]); every nonzero polynomial over a field has a splitting field ([[thm-splitting-fields-exist-for-nonzero-polynomials]]).

[L7] $R[x]$ is an integral domain when $R$ is ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]), and $h(a)=0$ if and only if $x-a$ divides $h$ ([[cor-factor-theorem-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L8] In a cyclic group $\langle g\rangle$ of finite order $m$, $g^{a}$ generates the group if and only if $\gcd(a,m)=1$ ([[lem-a-finite-cyclic-group-has-euler-totient-many-generators]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** contradiction.

1.1 Suppose, for contradiction, that $f(\zeta^{p})\ne0$. [assume-contra]

1.2 $\zeta^{p}$ is again a primitive $n$-th root of unity: $\gcd(p,n)=1$ because $p$ is prime and $p\nmid n$, so [L8] makes $\zeta^{p}$ a generator of $\mu_n(E)$, of order $n$. Hence $\Phi_n(\zeta^{p})=0$ by [L1]. [L1, L8, given]

1.3 Since $\Phi_n(\zeta)=0$ by [L1], [L2] gives $f\mid\Phi_n$ in $\mathbb Q[t]$; write $\Phi_n=fg$ with $g\in\mathbb Q[t]$, monic because $f$ and $\Phi_n$ are. [L1, L2]

2.1 Both $f$ and $g$ lie in $\mathbb Z[t]$. Let $b\ge1$ be least with $bf\in\mathbb Z[t]$ and $d\ge1$ least with $dg\in\mathbb Z[t]$; these exist because clearing denominators gives some such integer. If a prime $q$ divided every coefficient of $bf$ then $q$ would divide its leading coefficient $b$, and $(b/q)f$ would lie in $\mathbb Z[t]$, contradicting minimality; so $bf$ is primitive by [L4], and likewise $dg$. Then [L4] gives $\operatorname{cont}(bd\,\Phi_n)=\operatorname{cont}\bigl((bf)(dg)\bigr)=1$, while $\operatorname{cont}(bd\,\Phi_n)=bd\operatorname{cont}(\Phi_n)=bd$ because $\Phi_n$ is monic in $\mathbb Z[t]$ and hence primitive by [L4]; so $bd=1$ and $b=d=1$. [step 1.3, L1, L4]

2.2 From step 1.2 and step 1.3, $0=\Phi_n(\zeta^{p})=f(\zeta^{p})g(\zeta^{p})$ in the field $E$, and $f(\zeta^{p})\ne0$ by step 1.1, so $g(\zeta^{p})=0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3]

3.1 Hence $\zeta$ is a root of the polynomial $g(t^{p})$, so $f\mid g(t^{p})$ in $\mathbb Q[t]$ by [L2]; writing $g(t^{p})=fh$ with $h\in\mathbb Q[t]$, the division of the monic $g(t^{p})\in\mathbb Z[t]$ by the monic $f\in\mathbb Z[t]$ has quotient and remainder in $\mathbb Z[t]$ by [L3], and by the uniqueness clause of [L3] read in $\mathbb Q[t]$ that quotient is $h$ and the remainder is $0$; so $h\in\mathbb Z[t]$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, L2, L3]

4.1 Reduce modulo $p$ and let $L$ be a splitting field of $\bar f$ over $\mathbb Z/p$, which exists by [L6]; $\bar f$ is monic of the same degree as $f$, which is at least $1$, so it has a root $a\in L$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L6, given]

5.1 Evaluating the reduction of step 3.1 at $a$ gives $\bar g(a^{p})=\bar f(a)\bar h(a)=0$. Writing $\bar g=\sum_i c_it^{i}$ with $c_i\in\mathbb Z/p$ and using [L5] twice, $\bar g(a)^{p}=\sum_i c_i^{p}a^{ip}=\sum_i c_i(a^{p})^{i}=\bar g(a^{p})=0$, so $\bar g(a)=0$ because $L$ is a field. [step 3.1, step 4.1, L5]

6.1 Thus $t-a$ divides both $\bar f$ and $\bar g$ in $L[t]$ by [L7], so $(t-a)^{2}$ divides $\bar f\bar g=\overline{\Phi_n}$, which divides $t^{n}-1$ in $(\mathbb Z/p)[t]$ by [L1]. Then $a$ is a repeated root of $t^{n}-1$ in the extension $L$ of $\mathbb Z/p$, contradicting [L6], since $p\nmid n$. The assumption of step 1.1 is therefore untenable and $f(\zeta^{p})=0$. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, L1, L6, L7, discharge-contradiction] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where $p\nmid n$ is used.** Twice, and both uses are essential: in step 1.2, to know that $\zeta^{p}$ is still primitive, and in step 6.1, to know that $t^{n}-1$ is separable modulo $p$. If $p$ divided $n$ the reduction $\overline{\Phi_n}$ could genuinely have a repeated factor and the argument would produce no contradiction.

- **Why the passage to $\mathbb Z[t]$ is not cosmetic.** Reduction modulo $p$ is defined on integer polynomials, so the factorisation $\Phi_n=fg$ has to be known to happen over $\mathbb Z$ before step 4.1 can start. That is exactly what step 2.1 supplies, and it is where Gauss's content lemma enters.
