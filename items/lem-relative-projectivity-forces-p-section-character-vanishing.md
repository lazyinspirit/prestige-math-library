## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(K,\mathcal O,k)$ be a splitting
$p$-modular system whose residue field $k$ is algebraically closed, let $H$
be finite, and let $Q\leq H$ be a $p$-subgroup. If an $\mathcal O H$-lattice
$M$ is relatively $Q$-projective and $\theta$ is the ordinary character of
$K\otimes_{\mathcal O}M$, then
$$  \theta(x)=0$$
for every $x\in H$ whose $p$-part is not $H$-conjugate to an element of $Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the modular system, groups, lattice, character, and element
in the Statement.

[F1] Relative $Q$-projectivity means that $M$ is a summand of its induction
from $Q$ ([[def-relative-projectivity-and-vertices-for-og-lattices]]).

[F2] Integral Mackey decomposition and induction transitivity hold for these
lattices ([[lem-integral-mackey-and-higman-for-og-lattices]]).

[F3] Finite-rank group lattices have Krull--Schmidt decompositions
([[thm-krull-schmidt-for-og-lattices]]).

[F4] Induction across a normal subgroup of index $p$ preserves
indecomposability when $k$ is algebraically closed
([[thm-green-indecomposability-for-index-p-integral-induction]] and
[[def-algebraically-closed-field]]).

[F5] AC is available ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). It is retained for the
pair's inherited foundation contract; the proof below uses only finite
coset sets and finite decompositions and makes no additional use of AC.

## Proof

1.1 The assertion is immediate if $M=0$, so assume otherwise. Let $t$ be the $p$-part of $x$. Since $1\in Q$, the hypothesis implies $t\ne1$; in particular $p$ divides $|x|$. Put $$C=\langle x\rangle\quad\text{and}\quad L=\langle x^p\rangle.$$ Then $L\trianglelefteq C$ and $[C:L]=p$. The subgroup generated by $t$ is the Sylow $p$-subgroup of the cyclic group $C$. If $R\leq C$ does not contain $t$, then $R\leq L$: indeed, any power $x^j\notin L$ has $p\nmid j$ and generates the whole Sylow $p$-subgroup of $C$. [given, algebra]

2.1 By F1, $M$ is a direct summand of $Y=\operatorname{Ind}_Q^H\operatorname{Res}_Q^H M$. Restricting to $C$ and using F2 gives $$ \operatorname{Res}_C^H Y\cong \bigoplus_{s\in C\backslash H/Q} \operatorname{Ind}_{R_s}^{C}V_s, \qquad R_s=C\cap sQs^{-1}, $$ for the corresponding integral $R_s$-lattices $V_s$. If some $R_s$ contained $t$, then $t$ would belong to the $H$-conjugate $sQs^{-1}$, contrary to the hypothesis. Thus step 1.1 gives $R_s\leq L$ for every $s$. By induction transitivity, $$ \operatorname{Ind}_{R_s}^{C}V_s \cong \operatorname{Ind}_{L}^{C} \bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_{R_s}^{L}V_s\bigr). $$ [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Decompose each nonzero $\mathcal O L$-lattice $\operatorname{Ind}_{R_s}^{L}V_s$ into indecomposables $W_{s,j}$ by F3. Because $L\trianglelefteq C$ has index $p$, F4 says that every $\operatorname{Ind}_L^C W_{s,j}$ is indecomposable. Hence step 2.1 is an indecomposable decomposition of $\operatorname{Res}_C^H Y$. Since $\operatorname{Res}_C^H M$ is a direct summand, Krull--Schmidt makes each of its indecomposable summands isomorphic to one of these induced lattices. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 For any $\mathcal O L$-lattice $W$, scalar extension identifies $K\otimes_{\mathcal O}\operatorname{Ind}_L^C W$ with $\operatorname{Ind}_L^C(K\otimes_{\mathcal O}W)$. Its direct-sum decomposition over the $p$ cosets of $L$ in $C$ is cyclically permuted by $x$, because $x\notin L$. Thus the matrix of $x$ has zero diagonal blocks, and its trace is zero. Applying this to every summand selected in step 3.1 and adding their traces yields $$ \theta(x)=\operatorname{tr}\bigl(x\mid K\otimes_{\mathcal O}M\bigr)=0. $$ Algebraic closedness is used exactly through Green indecomposability in step 3.1; all selections and sums are finite. [F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
