## Statement

Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system of finite type with $S$ finite, $n:=|S|$, and let $V=\mathbb R^S$ carry the Coxeter form $B$, the canonical reflection representation $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$, the root system $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$, the reflection set $T=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$, the chamber $C$ and the conventions of [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]], [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]] and [[thm-cg-root-sign-and-simple-reflection-positivity]]; every root has $B$-norm one and $\rho$ is faithful ([[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]] (3), [[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] (3)), while $B$ is positive definite ([[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]]). Form the complexification $V_{\mathbb C}:=\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}V$ ([[def-complexification-of-a-real-vector-space]]), the $\mathbb C$-bilinear extension $B_{\mathbb C}$ of $B$ characterized by $B_{\mathbb C}(z\otimes u,\,w\otimes v)=zw\,B(u,v)$, the complexified map $\rho_{\mathbb C}:=\mathrm{id}\otimes\rho$ ([[def-complexification-of-a-real-linear-map]]) and the linear forms $\ell_\alpha(x):=B_{\mathbb C}(x,\alpha)$ for $\alpha\in\Phi$ ([[def-eigenvalue-eigenvector-eigenspace-and-spectrum]] fixes the eigenspace language).

**(1) Complexification.** $\dim_{\mathbb C}V_{\mathbb C}=n$, the classes $1\otimes e_s$ $(s\in S)$ form a $\mathbb C$-basis, $\rho_{\mathbb C}$ is a homomorphism $W\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ that is faithful in the sense of [[def-intertwiner-equivalent-and-faithful-representations]], and $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)$ is a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ of order $|W|$.

**(2) Complex reflections.** For every $t\in T$, written $t=t_\alpha$ with its positive root $\alpha\in\Phi_+$ ([[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]] (1)), the operator $\rho_{\mathbb C}(t)$ is not the identity, its fixed space is the complex hyperplane $\{x\in V_{\mathbb C}:\ell_\alpha(x)=0\}$, and $\rho_{\mathbb C}(t)-\mathrm{id}_{V_{\mathbb C}}$ has image the line $\mathbb C\alpha$; that is, $\rho_{\mathbb C}(t)$ is a complex reflection. In particular every generator $\rho_{\mathbb C}(s)$ is a complex reflection and $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)$ is generated by complex reflections.

**(3) Essentiality.** $V_{\mathbb C}^{W}=\{x:\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)x=x\ \text{for all}\ w\in W\}=\{0\}$, and every $W$-invariant linear form on $V_{\mathbb C}$ is zero.

**(4) Application under AC.** Assume the Axiom of Choice. Then the conclusions of [[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]] apply to the faithful finite subgroup $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)\le\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ generated by complex reflections: the invariant algebra $S^{W}:=\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]^{W}$ is a polynomial $\mathbb C$-algebra on $n$ homogeneous algebraically independent basic invariants, and $\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ is a graded free $S^{W}$-module of rank $|W|$; moreover every minimal homogeneous invariant generating family of the ideal $S^{W}_+\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ generates $S^{W}$ and is algebraically independent ([[lem-finite-reflection-invariant-generators-are-algebraically-independent]], [[lem-reflection-basic-invariants-form-a-regular-sequence]]), and the coinvariant quotient satisfies the Hilbert-series and order conclusions of [[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]] and [[def-finite-linear-invariant-and-coinvariant-polynomial-algebras]].

**(5) Conventions and abstentions.** For $n=0$ (trivial group, $V=0$) all assertions are the empty ones. No irreducibility, crystallographic integrality or highest-root data is assumed: the statement covers the noncrystallographic types $I_2(m)$, $H_3$, $H_4$ and reducible systems equally, since only the real reflection geometry and complexification are used. Nothing is asserted here about eigenvalues of the Coxeter element, about degrees beyond their existence, or about the coinvariant algebra as a $W$-representation; those are later items. (4) is the only clause using Choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Coxeter system $(W,S)$ of finite type with $S$ finite, the space $V=\mathbb R^S$ with its Coxeter form $B$, the canonical reflection representation $\rho$, the root system $\Phi=\Phi_+\sqcup\Phi_-$ and the reflection set $T$.

[F1] The Coxeter form satisfies $B(e_s,e_s)=1$ and $B(e_s,e_t)=-\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ for finite $m(s,t)$, the reflection formula is $r_a(v)=v-\frac{2B(v,a)}{B(a,a)}a$, and $r_a$ is linear, involutive, fixes $\ker B(-,a)$ pointwise, and preserves $B$; $\ker B(-,a)$ is a hyperplane when $B(a,a)\ne0$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]]).

[F2] The canonical reflection homomorphism is the unique homomorphism $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ with $\rho(s)=r_{e_s}$, the root system is $\Phi=\{\rho(w)e_s\}$, and $T=\{wsw^{-1}\}$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]]).

[F3] $\rho$ preserves $B$, every root $\alpha\in\Phi$ has $B(\alpha,\alpha)=1$, and $\rho(wsw^{-1})=r_{\rho(w)e_s}$ for all $w\in W$, $s\in S$; moreover $\rho$ is injective ([[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]] (2)-(4), [[thm-cg-root-length-criterion-and-faithfulness]] (3)).

[F4] If $W$ is finite then $B$ is positive definite ([[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1)).

[F5] The map $\Phi_+\to T$, $\alpha\mapsto t_\alpha$, is a bijection, and $\rho(t_\alpha)=r_\alpha$ ([[thm-cg-root-inversion-formulas-and-strong-exchange]] (1)).

[F6] The complexification $V_{\mathbb C}=\mathbb C\otimes_{\mathbb R}V$ carries the scalar action $z\cdot(w\otimes v)=(zw)\otimes v$ and the embedding $\iota v=1\otimes v$ ([[def-complexification-of-a-real-vector-space]]). The tensor universal property induces a linear map from any balanced bilinear map ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]).

[F7] For a real-linear $T:V\to V$ the complexification $T_{\mathbb C}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}\otimes T$ is $\mathbb C$-linear with $T_{\mathbb C}(z\otimes v)=z\otimes T(v)$ ([[def-complexification-of-a-real-linear-map]]).

[F8] $E_\lambda(T)=\ker(T-\lambda I)$ is the eigenspace of $\lambda$, and a representation is faithful when $\rho(g)=\mathrm{id}$ implies $g=e$ ([[def-eigenvalue-eigenvector-eigenspace-and-spectrum]], [[def-intertwiner-equivalent-and-faithful-representations]]).

[F9] For a finite $G\le\mathrm{GL}(V)$ the invariant algebra is $R=S^G$, $R_+=\bigoplus_{d>0}R_d$ and $I=SR_+$ defines the coinvariant algebra $S/I$ ([[def-finite-linear-invariant-and-coinvariant-polynomial-algebras]]).

[F10] The Axiom of Choice is the statement that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F11] Assume AC. For a finite complex reflection group $G\le\mathrm{GL}(V)$: $S^G$ is a polynomial algebra on $n=\dim V$ homogeneous algebraically independent basic invariants and $S$ is free of rank $|G|$ over $S^G$; every minimal homogeneous invariant generating family of $I=SR_+$ generates $R$ and is algebraically independent; these generators form an $S$-regular sequence and homogeneous lifts of a homogeneous basis of $S/I$ form a graded free $R$-basis; and $\operatorname{Hilb}(S/I,t)=\prod_i(1+\cdots+t^{d_i-1})$, $\dim_{\mathbb C}(S/I)=\prod_i d_i=|G|$ ([[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]], [[lem-finite-reflection-invariant-generators-are-algebraically-independent]], [[lem-reflection-basic-invariants-form-a-regular-sequence]], [[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]]).

## Proof

1.1 Every elementary tensor expands as $z\otimes v=\sum_s zv(s)(1\otimes e_s)$, so the classes $1\otimes e_s$ span. For each $t\in S$, the balanced map $(z,v)\mapsto zv(t)$ induces by [F6] a map $p_t:V_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C$; it is complex-linear on elementary tensors and satisfies $p_t(1\otimes e_s)=\delta_{ts}$. Applying $p_t$ to a linear relation proves independence. Thus these classes form a $\mathbb C$-basis and $\dim_{\mathbb C}V_{\mathbb C}=n$. For $w\in W$ set $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w):=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb C}\otimes\rho(w)$. By [F7] each $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)$ is $\mathbb C$-linear with $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)(z\otimes v)=z\otimes\rho(w)v$; on elementary tensors $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)\rho_{\mathbb C}(w')(z\otimes v)=z\otimes\rho(w)\rho(w')v=\rho_{\mathbb C}(ww')(z\otimes v)$, and since elementary tensors span $V_{\mathbb C}$ this gives $\rho_{\mathbb C}(ww')=\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)\rho_{\mathbb C}(w')$, while $\rho_{\mathbb C}(1)=\mathrm{id}$; so $\rho_{\mathbb C}:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ is a homomorphism. If $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)=\mathrm{id}_{V_{\mathbb C}}$, then for every $s$ the basis element $1\otimes e_s$ is fixed, so $1\otimes\rho(w)e_s=1\otimes e_s$, and linear independence of the $1\otimes e_t$ gives $\rho(w)e_s=e_s$; as $(e_s)$ spans $V$, $\rho(w)=\mathrm{id}_V$, and injectivity of $\rho$ [F3] gives $w=1$. Hence $\rho_{\mathbb C}$ is faithful in the sense of [F8], and its image, the image of the finite group $W$, is a finite subgroup of $\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ of order $|W|$. [F2, F3, F6, F7, F8]

2.1 Let $s\in S$. By [F1], $B(e_s,e_s)=1$ and $r_s(v)=v-2B(v,e_s)e_s$ for all $v\in V$; equivalently $r_s-\mathrm{id}_V=-2B(-,e_s)e_s$ has image $\mathbb Re_s$ and kernel $\ker B(-,e_s)$, a hyperplane of $V$. Complexifying with [F7], for $x=z\otimes v$ we get $(\rho_{\mathbb C}(s)-\mathrm{id}_{V_{\mathbb C}})(x)=z\otimes(r_s-\mathrm{id}_V)v=-2B(v,e_s)\,(z\otimes e_s)=-2B_{\mathbb C}(x,e_s)\,e_s$, using $B_{\mathbb C}(z\otimes v,e_s)=zB(v,e_s)$; the same formula holds on all of $V_{\mathbb C}$ by linearity. The image is therefore the line $\mathbb C e_s$ (it contains $-2e_s\neq0$) and the kernel is $\{x:B_{\mathbb C}(x,e_s)=0\}$, a complex hyperplane because $B_{\mathbb C}(e_s,e_s)=1$ makes the linear form $B_{\mathbb C}(-,e_s)$ nonzero. In particular $\rho_{\mathbb C}(s)\neq\mathrm{id}_{V_{\mathbb C}}$ fixes that hyperplane pointwise: each generator is a complex reflection, and $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)=\langle\rho_{\mathbb C}(s):s\in S\rangle$ is generated by complex reflections. [F1, F2, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $t\in T$. By [F5] there is a unique $\alpha\in\Phi_+$ with $t=t_\alpha$ and $\rho(t_\alpha)=r_\alpha$; by [F3] every root has $B$-norm one, so $r_\alpha(x)=x-2B(x,\alpha)\alpha$ for $x\in V$, and $r_\alpha$ fixes $\ker B(-,\alpha)$ pointwise and maps $\alpha\mapsto-\alpha$. Complexifying as in 2.1 gives $(\rho_{\mathbb C}(t_\alpha)-\mathrm{id})(x)=-2B_{\mathbb C}(x,\alpha)\alpha$ for all $x\in V_{\mathbb C}$, so the image is the line $\mathbb C\alpha$ and, since $B_{\mathbb C}(\alpha,\alpha)=1$, the kernel $\{x:\ell_\alpha(x)=0\}$ is a complex hyperplane. Thus every $t\in T$ acts as a complex reflection, and by [F2] and [F3] every element of $T$ is conjugate in $W$ to a simple reflection, in agreement with 2.1. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.1]

3.2 Let $x=\sum_{s\in S}z_s\otimes e_s\in V_{\mathbb C}$ be fixed by every $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)$. Fixing $x$ under $\rho_{\mathbb C}(s)$ and applying the formula of 2.1 gives $-2B_{\mathbb C}(x,e_s)e_s=0$, hence $B_{\mathbb C}(x,e_s)=0$ for every $s\in S$. Let $G$ be the Gram matrix of $B$ in the basis $(e_s)$; by [F4] $B$ is positive definite, so $G$ is a real symmetric positive definite matrix, and $B_{\mathbb C}(x,e_s)=0$ for all $s$ says $Gz=0$ for the coefficient column $z=(z_s)$. Writing $z=a+bi$ with real columns $a,b$, the real part $a^{T}Ga+b^{T}Gb$ of $z^{*}Gz$ vanishes, and both summands are $\ge0$ with equality only for $a=b=0$; hence $z=0$ and $x=0$. So $V_{\mathbb C}^W=\{0\}$. If $\ell:V_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C$ is a $W$-invariant linear form, then $\ell(e_s)=\ell(\rho_{\mathbb C}(s)e_s)=\ell(-e_s)$ by 2.1, so $\ell(e_s)=0$ for every $s$, and since the $e_s$ span $V_{\mathbb C}$ over $\mathbb C$, $\ell=0$. These finite-dimensional calculations use no choice principle: the basis $(e_s)$, the Gram matrix and the coefficient column are explicitly given by the finite set $S$. [F1, F3, F4, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 Assume AC. By step 1.1 the subgroup $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)\le\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ is finite of order $|W|$, faithful and, by steps 2.1 and 3.1, generated by complex reflections; $V_{\mathbb C}$ has dimension $n$. Applying [F11] with $S=\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ and $G=\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)$ gives that $S^{W}$ is a polynomial $\mathbb C$-algebra on $n$ homogeneous algebraically independent basic invariants, that $S$ is graded free of rank $|W|$ over $S^{W}$, that every minimal homogeneous invariant generating family of $I=SS^{W}_+$ generates $S^{W}$ and is algebraically independent, that such generators form a regular sequence with the freeness conclusion, and the stated Hilbert-series and order conclusions for $S/I$; by [F9] this $I$ is exactly the positive-degree ideal of the coinvariant algebra of the definition. This is the only place where AC is used. [F9, F10, F11, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 For $n=0$ the group $W$ is trivial, $V=V_{\mathbb C}=0$, the basis family is empty, $\rho_{\mathbb C}$ is the unique map of the trivial groups, $S=\mathbb C$ and all products and families displayed in (4) are empty; every assertion above holds in this empty form. Nothing in steps 1.1-4.1 uses irreducibility of the diagram, crystallographic integrality, or highest-root data: only the finiteness of $W$, the positivity of $B$, the bijection $\Phi_+\to T$ and the reflection formula are used, so reducible systems and the noncrystallographic types $I_2(m)$, $H_3$, $H_4$ are covered. No claim is made here about eigenvalues of a Coxeter element, about the values of the degrees, or about $S/I$ as a $W$-representation; and the only clause depending on AC is (4), consumed through [F11]. [F4, F5, F11, step 4.1] ∎
