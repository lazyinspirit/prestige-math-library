## Statement

Let $k$ be a field, let $Z$ be a projective $k$-scheme, and suppose its Hilbert functor is represented by a Hilbert scheme. Let $W\subseteq Z$ be a closed subscheme represented by a point $[W]$. Define the Zariski tangent space at $[W]$ to be the set of first-order flat embedded deformations of $W$ over $k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. If $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_Z$ is the ideal sheaf of $W$, this tangent space is canonically $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Z}(\mathcal I,\mathcal O_W)$. If $W\hookrightarrow Z$ is a regular immersion, meaning that $\mathcal I$ is locally generated by a regular sequence, this is canonically $H^0(W,\mathcal N_{W/Z})$, where $\mathcal N_{W/Z}=\mathcal Hom_{\mathcal O_W}(\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2,\mathcal O_W)$ is the normal bundle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the projective scheme $Z$, the closed subscheme $W$, and its ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$.

[F1] By the definition of a represented Hilbert functor, maps from $\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ reducing to $[W]$ correspond to flat closed subschemes of $Z\times_k\operatorname{Spec}k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ reducing to $W$. [[def-hilbert-functor-of-flat-projective-subschemes]]

[F2] For a regular sequence $(f_1,\ldots,f_c)$, the classes of the $f_i$ generate $I/I^2$. If $\sum_i a_if_i\in I^2$, write $\sum_i a_if_i=\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}f_if_j$ and set $a_i'=a_i-\sum_jb_{ij}f_j$. Then $a_i-a_i'\in I$ and $\sum_i a_i'f_i=0$. Exactness of the Koszul complex in degree one says every such relation is a combination of the vectors $f_ie_j-f_je_i$, so each $a_i'\in I$ and hence each $a_i\in I$. Thus the classes of the $f_i$ are a basis, $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2$ is locally free, and its dual is the normal bundle. [[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]] [[thm-regular-sequences-give-acyclic-koszul-complexes]]

[F3] A module is flat if and only if tensoring the inclusion of every finitely generated ideal into the ring is injective. ([[thm-flatness-criteria-by-injections-and-ideals]])

## Proof
1.1 Let $\widetilde{\mathcal I}$ be the ideal of a first-order flat deformation from [F1]. For a local section $f\in\mathcal I$, choose a lift $f+\epsilon g\in\widetilde{\mathcal I}$ and set $\varphi(f)=g\bmod\mathcal I$. If two lifts are chosen, their difference is $\epsilon h\in\widetilde{\mathcal I}$; flatness of the quotient implies $h\bmod\mathcal I=0$, so $\varphi$ is well defined. Multiplication of a lift by a local function shows that $\varphi:\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_W$ is $\mathcal O_Z$-linear. [F1, given, choose, construct]

1.2 Conversely, given an $\mathcal O_Z$-linear map $\varphi:\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_W$, define locally $\widetilde{\mathcal I}_\varphi=\{f+\epsilon g:f\in\mathcal I,\ g\bmod\mathcal I=\varphi(f)\}$. Linearity makes this an ideal; its reduction modulo $\epsilon$ is $\mathcal I$. For the quotient module, if $\epsilon[a+\epsilon b]=0$, then $a\in\mathcal I$; the defining relation for $\widetilde{\mathcal I}_\varphi$ writes $[a+\epsilon b]=\epsilon[b-\varphi(a)]$. Thus the kernel of multiplication by $\epsilon$ equals its image. To justify flatness without invoking representability, put $D=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$. For any $D$-module $M$, the map $(\epsilon)\otimes_D M\to M$ identifies with $M/\epsilon M\to M$, $\bar m\mapsto\epsilon m$; it is injective exactly when $\ker(\epsilon:M\to M)=\epsilon M$. The only ideals of $D$ are $0,(\epsilon),D$. The ideal criterion for flatness therefore makes this equality equivalent to flatness; conversely flatness preserves the injection $(\epsilon)\hookrightarrow D$. The equality just proved applies to the deformation quotient, which is consequently flat. [F1, F3, given, construct]

2.1 The constructions in steps 1.1 and 1.2 are inverse: the lift relation defining $\widetilde{\mathcal I}_\varphi$ recovers $\varphi$, and the lift relation from an original deformation recovers its ideal. They glue on overlaps because $\varphi$ is a sheaf morphism. Therefore the tangent space is canonically $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal O_Z}(\mathcal I,\mathcal O_W)$. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Every $\mathcal O_Z$-linear map $\mathcal I\to\mathcal O_W$ kills $\mathcal I^2$, since $\mathcal I$ acts by zero on $\mathcal O_W$. It therefore factors uniquely through $\mathcal I/\mathcal I^2$. Under the regular-immersion hypothesis, [F2] identifies this Hom space with $H^0(W,\mathcal N_{W/Z})$. This proves both assertions. [F2, step 2.1] ∎
