## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group with root datum $(X,\Phi,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$ and a Borel subgroup $B\supseteq T$ with corresponding base $\Delta=\Delta(B)$ ([[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]), and let $I\subseteq\Delta$. Put $W_I=\langle s_\alpha\rangle_{\alpha\in I}\subseteq W$, $\Phi_I=\mathbb ZI\cap\Phi$, $T_I=(\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}\ker\alpha)_t\subseteq T$ and $L_I=C_G(T_I)$. Then: (a) $L_I$ is smooth connected reductive with maximal torus $T$, root system $\Phi_I$ and root datum $(X(T),\Phi_I,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$, and Weyl group $W_I$; the root groups of $L_I$ are exactly the $U_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi_I$; (b) $T_I$ is the largest subtorus of $T$ killed by all $\alpha\in I$, and $L_I$ is generated by $T$ and the root groups $U_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi_I$; (c) $B\cap L_I$ is a Borel subgroup $B_I$ of $L_I$ with positive system $\Phi_I\cap\Phi^+(B)$ and base $I$; (d) for each $\alpha\in\Delta$, choose a rational fundamental dual vector $\eta_\alpha\in X_*(T)\otimes\mathbb Q$ with $\langle\beta,\eta_\alpha\rangle=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, and let $\lambda_\alpha$ be any positive integral multiple lying in $X_*(T)$. For every $\beta\in\Delta\smallsetminus\{\alpha\}$, every representative of $s_\beta$ in $N_G(T)(k)$ lies in $P_G(\lambda_\alpha)(k)$, and this subgroup contains $G_\beta$. Rational dual vectors need not themselves be integral cocharacters; a possible central component does not change the parabolic.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel subgroup $B\supseteq T$ and base $\Delta=\Delta(B)$, a subset $I\subseteq\Delta$, and the subgroups $W_I,\Phi_I,T_I,L_I$ above.

[F1] Centralizers of tori in reductive groups are smooth, connected and reductive, with Lie algebra the fixed points under the adjoint action; the roots of a reductive group with a given maximal torus are the nonzero weights ([[thm-chevalley-centralizer-radical-and-reductive-centralizers]], [[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] $G$ is generated by $T$ and its root groups, and each root group is determined by its weight; the positive system of a Borel and the base are related as in [[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]] ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], [[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]]).

[F3] Cocharacter limit subgroups: for a cocharacter $\lambda$ of $T$, $P_G(\lambda)$ contains exactly the root groups $U_\alpha$ with $\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle\ge0$ and their root spaces ([[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]]).


[F4] A Borel intersected with a torus centralizer is a Borel of that centralizer; its root signs are inherited. ([[lem-borel-root-group-opposition]]; Milne17.72 and21.90.)

## Proof

1.1 $L_I=C_G(T_I)$ is smooth connected reductive by [F1]; its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_I}\mathfrak g_\alpha$ because the characters vanishing on $T_I$ lie in the rational span of $I$. Each root has integral coefficients in the simple basis, so a root in this rational span has zero coefficients outside $I$ and lies in $\mathbb ZI\cap\Phi$. Hence the roots of $(L_I,T)$ are precisely the elements of $\Phi_I$, and the corresponding root groups are $U_\alpha\subseteq L_I$; the coroot assignment restricts, and the Weyl group of $(L_I,T)$ is the subgroup $W_I$ generated by the $s_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in I$ (these are the reflections in the simple roots of the subsystem). This proves (a). [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 $T_I$ is the largest subtorus of $T$ killed by all $\alpha\in I$ because any such subtorus lies in $\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}\ker\alpha$ and, being a torus, in its maximal reduced subtorus; the character-module quotient by its torsion defines exactly that subtorus. By (a) and [F2] applied to $L_I$, the group $L_I$ is generated by $T$ and the root groups $U_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi_I$; this proves (b). For (c): $B\cap L_I$ is a Borel subgroup of $L_I$ by the centralizer-intersection input [F4]; its Lie algebra is $\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi_I\cap\Phi^+(B)}\mathfrak g_\alpha$, so its positive system is $\Phi_I\cap\Phi^+(B)$, and the simple roots of the subsystem relative to this positive system are exactly the elements of $I$ (a simple root of $\Phi$ lying in $\Phi_I$ is indecomposable in $\Phi_I$, and every root of $\Phi_I$ is a nonnegative combination of $I$). [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Fix $\alpha\in\Delta$. Finite linear algebra supplies $\eta_\alpha$ with the given simple-root pairings; clearing denominators gives integral $\lambda_\alpha=m_\alpha\eta_\alpha$, $m_\alpha>0$. For $\beta\ne\alpha$ one has $\langle\beta,\lambda_\alpha\rangle=0$, so both $U_\beta$ and $U_{-\beta}$ and the torus $T$ centralize this cocharacter. They generate $G_\beta$ by [F2], giving $G_\beta\subseteq Z_G(\lambda_\alpha)\subseteq P_G(\lambda_\alpha)$. The dual reflection formula gives $s_\beta(\lambda_\alpha)=\lambda_\alpha$; hence every representative of $s_\beta$ fixes $\lambda_\alpha$ by conjugation and lies in its centralizer, proving(d). The simple root $\alpha$ itself pairs positively, and its negative root group is excluded; no representative of $s_\alpha$ is claimed to lie in this parabolic. Central changes of a rational dual vector pair trivially with every root and do not affect these limit subgroups. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
