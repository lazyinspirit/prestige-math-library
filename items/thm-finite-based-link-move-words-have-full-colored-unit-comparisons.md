## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the supplied analytic toolkit. Fix finitely many based oriented colored LINK diagrams in $S^3$, with the actual same based link at each proposed comparison, and a finite relative Heegaard move word between each such pair. All marked disks and their labels are fixed. The permitted moves are mark-avoiding isotopies and disk slides on either side and ordinary genus stabilizations/destabilizations with a once-intersecting new meridian pair. A move changes no marked-point number. Point creation/deletion and surface saddle maps are separate operations. Supply the actual relative move geometry described in the assumptions, the new good arbitrary-link ends, and the stated full-marked polygon interface on actual admissible data.

There are ACTUAL polynomial full-marked comparison maps for this finite word, retaining all positive $z$ multiplicities. They intertwine the two full-marked disk operators with the same marking variables and curvature. After every square-zero coloring they are chain maps. In the normalized two Maslov gradings they have degree $(0,0)$; equivalently, after forgetting $z$ weights their minus version has Maslov degree zero and Alexander filtration degree zero. The map after forgetting $z$ and identifying the ordinary $w$ variables sends the normalized highest ordinary tower class to that class with coefficient one. The literal $w/z$ counterpart has the same property. These conclusions concern the chosen finite word. They do not assert independence of all move words or all decorated Cerf relations.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** [[lem-based-multipointed-link-diagrams-admit-finite-relative-move-words]] supplies the finite relative move word on the punctured Heegaard surfaces, fixed marked collars and actual bridge arcs. Each disk-slide pair of pants is disjoint from ALL marks. Cut the complete relevant disk system along all but the two involved disks. The slide band connects their two cocore edges in one cut-ball graph component with two edges and $r=1,2$, or $3$ vertices. Each vertex ball has ONE actual bridge pair. Regluing those two handles gives local data $(g_{\rm loc},n_{\rm loc},d_{\rm loc})=(3-r,r,2)$; the cases are the genus-two one-pair, genus-one two-pair, and planar three-pair slide packets. The handle feet, slide band and small Hamiltonian annuli avoid the bridge disks. Their four interior domain pieces are the same, while only the exterior-face gluing changes. The other meridians are represented by small Hamiltonian translates and the extra marked cut components by split-unlink packets. This graph construction includes separating slides, mixed slides and a dependent bounding pair of nonseparating disks; it does not infer genus two merely from two disk labels. In an auxiliary same-handlebody pair, each $w$ and the $z$ joined to it by that handlebody's ACTUAL bridge arc remain in the same auxiliary face. This does not require an arbitrary old $w,z$ pair to share a face of the original two-ended diagram. Ordinary genus handles and all isotopy/slide supports are disjoint from the marked collars. The endpoint identifications, labels, transported disk caps and chosen $S^3$ Spin-c sector are retained throughout the word. This is relative link move geometry, not knot-only connectivity.

## Proof

1.1 **Construct the actual full-colored slide auxiliary cycle.** Use the standard two-meridian slide core in the given cut-ball graph geometry. Its interior pieces are $D_1,D_2,D_3,D_4$: the first, third and fourth are disks, and the second is the slit annulus used below. In the one-vertex case the exterior is one marked region $D_5$. In the two- or three-vertex case its lobes remain as two or three DIFFERENT exterior marked faces. This is obtained by undoing one or both identifications between handle feet in the exterior; none of the four interior pieces or their conformal boundary labels changes. Each exterior face contains its own actual bridge pair. All three cases have symmetric degree two and the same four intersection generators. For the common top $y^+=(y_1^+,y_2^+)$ and the point $(y_1^-,y_2^+)$, the positive index-one domains with zero exterior multiplicity are exactly $D_1$, $D_2+D_3$, and $D_2+D_4$. To see the list, solve the four corner jumps: after fixing ALL exterior-face coefficients to zero the periodic directions are $D_3-D_4$ and $D_1-D_2-D_3$; nonnegativity leaves precisely these three domains. The first is a disk and has count one. The latter two require a genuine annulus calculation rather than a disk shortcut. Slit the common annulus $D_2$ along the segment on the pushed second circle, parametrized by $t\in(0,1)$. Uniformization gives an annulus with two specified boundary arcs. A degree-two projection to a disk exists precisely when their conformal angles agree: the deck involution must exchange the arcs. In coordinates $\{r<|u|<r^{-1}\}$ the involution is $u\mapsto c/u$, $|c|=1$, and is unique. Conversely that involution supplies the required branched cover and the inclusion gives the symmetric-divisor disk. Thus a regular crossing of the two angle functions counts exactly one disk. At $t=0$ the angle difference has opposite signs for the two slit choices. At $t=1$ they approach the SAME nonzero difference $\nu_1-\nu_2$ of the unslit annulus, chosen generic. Their total number of crossings modulo two is therefore one. Interior crossings may appear in pairs; no unjustified claim of a unique crossing is needed. The three counts sum to $1+1=0$. For the change in the second top coordinate the two ordinary disks cancel. Changes in both coordinates have a negative region coefficient. Finally the relative grading from the top to every other generator is positive. In the torsion auxiliary sector an index-one domain with positive exterior multiplicity satisfies $1=\operatorname{gr}(y^+,y)+2\sum_{i=1}^r n_{w_i}$. The common top has strictly positive relative grading to every other one of the four generators, and the same-endpoint index has even parity; hence this equation is impossible when any of the exterior coefficients is positive. This uses the SUM over the actual distinct marked exterior faces in the planar and mixed cases, not the one-mark formula copied from genus two. The relative gradings follow from the same interior disks, whose indices are unchanged by changing exterior handle identifications. Thus these arguments explicitly cover $r=2,3$ as well as $r=1$. Thus the common top is a cycle, before any variable specialization. All three canceling domains are disjoint from the marked exterior, so their FULL $U,V$ monomials agree and equal one. The other Hamiltonian handle tops and the actual separating-packet top transfers of [F2] now give an actual auxiliary cycle $\Theta_{\rm move}$ on the whole same-handlebody pair. The bridge-paired marks lie in the same auxiliary faces, so any exterior multiplicity uses the paired product $U_wV_z$; no one-color argument is being used to discard it. The top is simultaneously the $w$ and $z$ top. [given, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Actual finite admissible comparison data.** Choose small positive local areas in the slide packet with $a(D_3)=a(D_4)$ and $a(D_1)=a(D_2)+a(D_3)$. Its normalized periodic domains have area zero. Make every Hamiltonian signed-bigon difference have area zero and use the marked cut-ball pads to give each cut region area $1/n$. All local areas may first be chosen small, so the pads have positive area. Relative Moser transfer in the complement of the marked collars and protected packet witnesses supplies these choices while preserving the actual based link. For a triple consisting of one old opposite disk system and two same-handlebody systems, a periodic domain has zero boundary homology on the opposite side: that homology lies in the intersection of the two meridian Lagrangians, which is zero over the rationals because the relevant ambient end is $S^3$. Cap its null-homologous boundary by integral cut-region combinations; those caps bound actual compression-body cut balls. The remaining domain lies in the auxiliary kernel. That kernel has the signed Hamiltonian bigon directions, the two displayed slide directions and the marked cut-region differences. They have area zero and Chern evaluation zero, the last assertion using the actual cut-ball caps and torsion restriction on the auxiliary connected-sum boundary. Hence every nonzero normalized periodic domain has area zero and both signs, and has Chern evaluation zero. This is the actual strict $N=0$ condition, not a two-ended bound substituted for triple admissibility. For the ordinary slide/reverse four-tuple use the same area form, with the fourth system an exact small translate of the first same-handlebody system. Its periodic domains are sums of the adjacent paired and auxiliary triple domains: subtract their boundary meridian coefficients successively, leaving a zero-boundary constant domain, removed by normalization. They therefore also have area and Chern zero; the same positive form gives the required finite quadrilateral inventories. There is no nontrivial Spin-c orbit hidden by this reduction: a class restricting trivially on all capped boundary pairs is zero by the same meridian-boundary elimination. Apply [F1] to these actual finite configurations. Once the finite inventory is fixed, choose good generic core tensors fixed at all end tensors; finite intersections of their open regular choices and compatible collars suffice. This supplies the actual triangle, quadrilateral and end data used below. [given, F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Full-colored slide maps and ordinary units.** Count index-zero triangles with input the actual cycle of step 1.1, assigning to EVERY representative the product $\prod U_w^{n_w}\prod V_z^{n_z}$. The actual index-one collars of [F1] give $D'F=FD$, since $D\Theta_{\rm move}=0$. Marked boundary ends are handled by the supplied full-marked polygon compactification, with the same actual coloring and curvature on both old link ends. This constructs the colored map itself, not a transport inferred after $V=1$. To calibrate its ordinary effect, choose the reverse-slide system and then a small Hamiltonian translate of the first system. In the auxiliary three-system diagram, the top-product triangle has one small disk at each paired top corner. Solving the normalized region equations gives this as the only nonnegative zero-exterior class with those top endpoints. Each disk has one conformal representative, so the product count is one. Other output generators have smaller relative degree; an index-zero triangle to them would require negative exterior multiplicity. Thus the actual auxiliary product of the two top cycles is the Hamiltonian-pair top. Extra split packets preserve the product: retain their root coordinates and use their actual common-top count, with no removal of their factors. The ordinary quadrilateral collars from step 2.1 therefore identify the composite slide/reverse maps on homology with a small Hamiltonian comparison. That comparison is an isomorphism: its unique small triangles give nearest-point identity, while all other terms lower the area filtration by a fixed positive amount. At each fixed Maslov degree the ordinary polynomial module has finitely many generators, so its strictly lowering part is nilpotent there; the geometric-series inverse is a finite polynomial sum on every element. In particular no Laurent completion is used. Interchanging the two slide systems proves both injectivity and surjectivity of the ordinary slide map. This argument applies verbatim after literally interchanging the $w$ and $z$ marks and forgetting the OTHER weights: it proves an ordinary unit in BOTH conventions on this same actual colored map. It is not a general move-word independence assertion. [given, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Mark-avoiding isotopies and regular-data comparisons.** Use moving-boundary continuation strips with the entire isotopy on a compact source-boundary interval and FIXED transverse end diagrams. A possible intermediate tangency with the opposite curves occurs in the compact source, so no nontransverse asymptotic end is introduced. The principal symbol is the ordinary real Cauchy–Riemann boundary symbol at each source point; varying the boundary Lagrangian does not remove ellipticity. Domain-dependent tensors on the core give regular zero-dimensional continuation counts; the finite relative area bounds of step 2.1, applied after tracing the finitely many marked-avoiding isotopy caps, give compactness. All $w,z$ divisors are fixed outside the moving support, so every count and every homotopy retains its full marking monomial. Actual one-dimensional collars give the continuation chain equation. The reversed isotopy and its fixed-end path/backtrack contraction give inverse maps on ordinary homology in either forgetting convention. At the constant path, translation excludes nonconstant index-zero strips and the regular constant strips give identity. The same construction changes auxiliary regular tensors while fixing the geometric diagrams. Families use fixed end data and compact source variation throughout; no static family of degenerating asymptotic generators is assumed. Only these finitely many prescribed path and backtrack families are required. [given, F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The genuinely ordinary-genus stabilization count.** Add a mark-free torus $E$ with meridians meeting once at $c$, by plumbing a small disk in an old face to a point $\sigma_2\in E$. The generator correspondence is $x\mapsto x\times c$. A positive new formal domain collapses to the old domain, with torus coefficient its old multiplicity $k$ at the attachment face; local corner jumps leave no independent positive torus direction. Old periodic normalization and the Maslov index are unchanged, so the old finite domain/energy inventory bounds all classes through index two. The nontrivial local matching at $k>0$ is NOT a degree-one proper disk of positive genus. In the symmetric-divisor target it consists of the stationary $c$ sheet and $k$ fiber spheres in $\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)$. Indeed the addition map $\{a,b\}\mapsto a+b$ makes $\operatorname{Sym}^2(E)$ a ruled surface: its fiber is the quotient of $E$ by $u\mapsto v-u$ and is a projective line. The divisor $\{\sigma_2,y\}$ is a section of that ruling. Thus through each prescribed pair $\{\sigma_2,c\}$ there is exactly one fiber sphere, parametrized uniquely after centering and fixing its gluing derivative. Its tangent and normal holomorphic bundles are $\mathcal O(2)$ and $\mathcal O$, so their $H^1$ vanish. More generally the degree-$k$ divisor deformation is unobstructed: it is a zero set of a pullback positive degree-$k$ line bundle from $E$; $H^1$ of that bundle and $H^2$ of the ruled-surface structure sheaf vanish, so the quotient normal sheaf has $H^1=0$. For generic attachment incidence all roots are simple, so only the separated once-covered fibers occur in the index-one count. This identifies the actual local moduli and its evaluation, rather than postulating a genus-one disk response. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

5.2 **Uniform inverse and actual stabilization exhaustion.** Near each transverse old root choose coordinates in which its normal component is $az+O(z^2)$, $a\ne0$. Remove that root, retain its complex position and gluing derivative, and center the corresponding sphere by fixing its incidence with a second section. On each old punctured piece the extended operator is the old regular disk operator, with its translation kernel sliced. On the sphere pieces the preceding vanishing gives surjectivity; their retained evaluation/derivative coordinates map isomorphically onto the matching space. Use weighted $W^{1,p}_\delta$ normal complements, $p>2$, with $\delta$ below the first nonzero cylindrical Fourier rate, and retain the constant matching modes explicitly. The fibered-product operator is consequently onto with a bounded right inverse. Cutoffs whose derivatives are $O(1/T)$ patch it to the neck of length $T$, with error $O(1/T)$; a Neumann series bounds the actual inverse independently of $T$. The expansions $az+O(z^2)$ on both pieces give pregluing residual $O(e^{-bS})$ in the weighted norm for cutoff length $S$. Product integrable coordinates on the neck make its nonlinear remainder quadratic with a constant independent of $T$. The implicit theorem therefore gives one actual new strip for each old strip.
To prove surjectivity of this construction, degenerate the targets in the fixed nodal family $\Sigma\vee E$. The branched-cover domain multiplicity bounds give a uniform energy bound for a smooth taming form on that family: on its compact projectivized tangent cone the ratio of this form to the product area form is bounded. The perturbed region lies in a fixed product subset and has the same uniform bound. An index-one limit has a single regular old strip, stationary $c$ sheet and the indicated once-covered fiber spheres; extra old breaking or a positive-index old bubble would leave a forbidden negative-index principal part, while multiplicity at a generic root excludes repeated fiber incidence. In the tube subtract the constant mode of each holomorphic coordinate. Its nonzero Fourier series $\sum_{m\ne0}a_m e^{m(s+i\theta)}$ has weighted norm controlled by the two end collars. For the cylindrical target coordinate first subtract its degree-one standard cylinder map and lift the difference to the complex plane; the same estimate applies. Thus convergence is in the very weighted gluing ball, not merely away from the neck. Uniqueness of the implicit correction exhausts the count. At low old symmetric degree, a putative class with no old disk lift would still have to produce that regular old index-one strip; if no such strip class exists it is empty, rather than being added formally as a supplier. The rank-zero twice-marked sphere to one-crossing torus case is direct: the torus has no sphere class and the two meridian fundamental groups intersect trivially, so it has no nonconstant disk class. Every old mark remains away from the torus packet and has exactly its old multiplicity. Hence the actual full-marked stabilization differential is IDENTICAL under $x\mapsto x\times c$. This is a polynomial chain isomorphism in both directions, for the colored operator as well as either ordinary forgetting. [given, F1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **The two actual normalizations give degree zero.** The supplied determinant/cap formula makes each full-marked comparison homogeneous in $M_w$ and $M_z$, up to two constants. The auxiliary input in step 1.1 is the SAME top for both gradings: its bridge-paired marks share auxiliary faces and all its packet top shifts are common shifts. After forgetting $z$, steps 3.1–5.2 give a genuine homogeneous polynomial ordinary isomorphism. Both end complexes have the normalized highest ordinary degree $(n-1)/2$. A homogeneous isomorphism must send that highest class to the highest class: it cannot acquire a positive power of $U$, since the inverse is also polynomial and a positive power has no polynomial inverse. Its coefficient is one over $\mathbb F_2$, and its degree shift is zero. Apply the LITERAL same proof with $w$ and $z$ interchanged to determine the SECOND constant: it too is zero. This does not infer the colored map from its ordinary reduction. It calibrates the two constants of the already constructed homogeneous colored map. For each counted full-minus triangle term the cap formula now reads
$$A(y)-\sum_w n_w=A(x)-\sum_z n_z.$$
Since every $n_z\ge0$, the $V=1$ minus map has filtration degree at most zero, with normalized degree zero and ordinary tower coefficient one. Both markings, positive $z$ terms and actual based-link sector transport have been retained throughout. [given, F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, algebra]
