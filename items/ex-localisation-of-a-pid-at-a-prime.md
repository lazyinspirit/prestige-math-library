## Example

Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain and let $\mathfrak p=(\pi)$ be a nonzero
prime ideal. Then the localisation $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a discrete valuation
ring with uniformiser $\pi/1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A principal ideal domain $R$ and a nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p=(\pi)$.

[F1] In a PID every ideal is principal, and the ring is a domain
([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]).

[F2] A prime ideal is proper ([[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]]).

[F3] Localisation at $\mathfrak p$ means inverting the complement
$R\setminus\mathfrak p$ ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

[L1] Every principal ideal domain is Noetherian
([[cor-principal-ideal-domains-are-noetherian]]).

[L2] A nonfield domain is a DVR exactly when it is a local PID with nonzero
maximal ideal ([[thm-equivalent-characterisations-of-a-dvr]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $a\in R$ be nonzero. If $a\in\mathfrak p$, then $a=\pi a_1$ because $\mathfrak p=(\pi)$. If $a_1\in\mathfrak p$, divide by $\pi$ again, and continue. This process stops, for otherwise $(a)\subsetneq(a_1)\subsetneq(a_2)\subsetneq\cdots$ would be a strict ascending chain of principal ideals, contradicting [L1]. Thus $a=u\pi^n b$ with $n\ge0$, $u$ a unit of $R$, and $b\notin\mathfrak p$. [F1, F2, F3, L1, given, algebra]

2.1 Every nonzero element of $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is therefore $(a/s)=((ub)/s)(\pi/1)^n$ with $s\notin\mathfrak p$ and $b\notin\mathfrak p$. Since both $b$ and $s$ are in the denominator set, $(ub)/s$ is a unit of $R_{\mathfrak p}$. Hence $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is local with nonzero maximal ideal generated by $\pi/1$, and every ideal is principal by the same minimum-exponent argument as in a DVR. [F3, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The ring $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is not a field because $\pi/1$ lies in its maximal ideal and is not a unit. So [L2] applies and shows that $R_{\mathfrak p}$ is a discrete valuation ring with uniformiser $\pi/1$. [L2, step 2.1] ∎
