## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Dependent Choice. Let $G$ be a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group with dual $\widehat G$, and let $\mu$ be a finite regular complex Borel measure on $\widehat G$ ([[def-regular-complex-borel-measure-on-an-lch-space]]). If the inverse transform
$$x\mapsto\int_{\widehat G}\gamma(x)\,d\mu(\gamma)$$
vanishes for every $x\in G$, then $\mu=0$. Equivalently, two finite regular complex Borel measures on $\widehat G$ with the same inverse transform are equal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and Dependent Choice, a locally compact Hausdorff abelian group $G$ with Haar measure $m_G$ and dual $\widehat G$, a finite regular complex Borel measure $\mu$ on $\widehat G$, and the assumption that its inverse transform $\Phi(x):=\int_{\widehat G}\gamma(x)\,d\mu(\gamma)$ vanishes for every $x\in G$. Write $A=L^1(G,m_G)$ and $A^+=\mathbb C\oplus A$.

[F1] The Fourier transform $\widehat f(\gamma)=\int_Gf(x)\overline{\gamma(x)}\,dm_G(x)$ is a linear map $A\to C_0(\widehat G)$ with $|\widehat f(\gamma)|\le\|f\|_1$ for every $\gamma$ ([[def-fourier-transform-on-an-lca-group]], [[thm-riemann-lebesgue-lemma-on-lca-groups]]).

[F2] $\widehat{f*g}=\widehat f\,\widehat g$ and $\widehat{f^*}=\overline{\widehat f}$ for $f,g\in A$, so the transform algebra is a self-adjoint algebra ([[lem-lca-fourier-transform-intertwines-translation-modulation-and-convolution]]); the characters of $A^+$ are exactly $q(z,f)=z$ and $h_\gamma^+(z,f)=z+\widehat f(\gamma)$, $\Delta(A^+)$ is a compact Hausdorff space whose Gelfand topology is generated by the functions $\chi\mapsto\chi(a)$, the correspondence $\gamma\leftrightarrow h_\gamma^+$ is a bijection onto $\Delta(A^+)\setminus\{q\}$, and $\Delta(A^+)$ is homeomorphic to $\widehat G\cup\{q\}$ ([[lem-lca-scalar-unitization-character-space-and-spectrum]], [[def-gelfand-transform]], [[thm-maximal-ideal-space-is-compact-hausdorff]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F3] If $X$ is a compact Hausdorff space and $B\subseteq C(X,\mathbb C)$ is a point-separating self-adjoint complex function algebra with a unique common zero $x_0$, then the uniform closure of $B$ is exactly $\{F\in C(X,\mathbb C):F(x_0)=0\}$ ([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]]); functions on $\widehat G$ vanishing at infinity are the continuous functions on $\widehat G$ that extend continuously by $0$ at the point at infinity ([[def-compact-support-c-c-and-c-zero-on-an-lch-space]]).

[F4] For $f\in L^1(G,m_G)$ the set where $f\ne0$ is $\sigma$-finite for $m_G$, and $\mu$ is finite; Tonelli and Fubini therefore apply to the product of a $\sigma$-finite essential support of $f$ with $(\widehat G,\mu)$, and $|\mu|(\widehat G)<+\infty$ ([[thm-tonelli-theorem-for-sigma-finite-product-spaces]], [[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]], [[def-regular-complex-borel-measure-on-an-lch-space]], [[def-dependent-choice]]).

[F5] If two Radon measures on a locally compact Hausdorff space agree on every continuous compactly supported function, then they are equal ([[thm-rmk-uniqueness-among-radon-measures]], [[def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space]]); a finite regular complex Borel measure has finite regular real and imaginary parts whose Jordan decompositions are finite positive regular Borel measures, hence Radon measures ([[def-regular-complex-borel-measure-on-an-lch-space]], [[def-radon-measure-on-an-lch-space]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (The transform algebra separates the character space.) Put $\mathcal A:=\{\widehat f:f\in A\}$. By [F2] the map $f\mapsto\widehat f$ is a linear bijection onto $\mathcal A$ that carries convolution to pointwise multiplication and $*$ to complex conjugation, so $\mathcal A$ is a self-adjoint complex algebra of functions on $\widehat G$; by [F1] it is contained in $C_0(\widehat G)$. Viewed on $\Delta(A^+)$ through $\chi\mapsto\chi(0,f)$, it is a subalgebra of $C(\Delta(A^+))$ that contains $\widehat f$ and vanishes at $q$. If $\gamma\ne\eta$ in $\widehat G$ then $h_\gamma^+\ne h_\eta^+$, and since the Gelfand topology is Hausdorff there is $a=(z,f)\in A^+$ with $h_\gamma^+(a)\ne h_\eta^+(a)$; the two characters agree on constants, so $\widehat f(\gamma)\ne\widehat f(\eta)$. Likewise $h_\gamma^+\ne q$ gives $f\in A$ with $\widehat f(\gamma)\ne0$. Hence $\mathcal A$ separates points of $\widehat G$ and has no common zero there, while its extension to $\Delta(A^+)$ has the unique common zero $q$. [F1, F2]

1.2 (The pairing identity.) Let $f\in L^1(G,m_G)$. Then $$\int_{\widehat G}\widehat f(\gamma)\,d\mu(\gamma)=\int_{\widehat G}\int_Gf(x)\overline{\gamma(x)}\,dm_G(x)\,d\mu(\gamma).$$ The integrand is absolutely integrable over the product of a $\sigma$-finite essential support of $f$ with $(\widehat G,\mu)$ by [F4], so Fubini applies and, using $\overline{\gamma(x)}=\gamma(-x)$, $$\int_{\widehat G}\widehat f\,d\mu=\int_Gf(x)\Bigl(\int_{\widehat G}\gamma(-x)\,d\mu(\gamma)\Bigr)dm_G(x)=\int_Gf(x)\,\Phi(-x)\,dm_G(x)=0 .$$ [F1, F4]

2.1 (Uniform density of the transform algebra.) By step 1.1 the algebra $B:=\{\chi\mapsto\chi(0,f):f\in A\}\subseteq C(\Delta(A^+))$ is self-adjoint, point-separating, and its only common zero is $q$. The vanishing-at-one-point case of [F3] applied to $X=\Delta(A^+)$ and $x_0=q$ gives that the uniform closure of $B$ is $\{G\in C(\Delta(A^+)):G(q)=0\}$. Under the homeomorphism $\Delta(A^+)\cong\widehat G\cup\{q\}$ of [F2], this is exactly the space of continuous functions on $\widehat G$ vanishing at infinity; hence $\mathcal A=\{\widehat f:f\in A\}$ is uniformly dense in $C_0(\widehat G)$. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 (Vanishing against all of $C_0(\widehat G)$.) Let $H\in C_0(\widehat G)$ and $\varepsilon>0$. By step 2.1 choose $f\in A$ with $\|\widehat f-H\|_\infty<\varepsilon/(1+|\mu|(\widehat G))$. By step 1.2, $\int\widehat f\,d\mu=0$, so $$\Bigl|\int_{\widehat G}H\,d\mu\Bigr|\le\int_{\widehat G}|H-\widehat f|\,d|\mu|+\Bigl|\int_{\widehat G}\widehat f\,d\mu\Bigr|\le\|\widehat f-H\|_\infty\,|\mu|(\widehat G)<\varepsilon .$$ Hence $\int_{\widehat G}H\,d\mu=0$ for every $H\in C_0(\widehat G)$. [F4, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 (Conclusion $\mu=0$.) Every $h\in C_c(\widehat G)$ lies in $C_0(\widehat G)$, so by step 3.1 $\int h\,d\mu=0$ for all $h\in C_c(\widehat G)$. Write $\mu=\mu_1-\mu_2+i(\mu_3-\mu_4)$ with $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_4$ finite positive regular Borel measures, as in [F5]. Then for every real-valued $h\in C_c(\widehat G)$ one has $\int h\,d\mu_1=\int h\,d\mu_2$ and $\int h\,d\mu_3=\int h\,d\mu_4$; by [F5] applied to the Radon measures $\mu_1,\mu_2$ and then to $\mu_3,\mu_4$, all four equalities hold as measures, so $\mu=0$. [F5, step 3.1]

5.1 (Equivalence.) If finite regular complex Borel measures $\mu,\nu$ on $\widehat G$ have the same inverse transform, then $\sigma:=\mu-\nu$ is again a finite regular complex Borel measure and its inverse transform vanishes identically; step 4.1 gives $\sigma=0$, that is, $\mu=\nu$. [step 4.1] ∎
