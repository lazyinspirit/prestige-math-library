## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed Riemann surface with $m\ge1$ pairwise disjoint real analytic embedded circles $\gamma_1,\ldots,\gamma_m$. Assume their cut surface has a component $A$ which is a sphere with finitely many open disk interiors removed, with a supplied finite smooth triangulation. Boundary copies of the same $\gamma_j$ are counted separately. Let $D_A$ be the domain with coefficient one on the corresponding component of $\Sigma\setminus\gamma$ and zero on all other components, and let $L=q(\gamma_1\times\cdots\times\gamma_m)$.

Every nonconstant smooth standard-holomorphic disk $u:\overline{\mathbb D}\to\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma)$ with boundary on $L$ and actual domain $D_A$ has boundary Maslov index $2$. Consequently its compact-disk real Cauchy–Riemann linearization has Fredholm index $m+2$; imposing one fixed boundary value on its $H^3\to H^2$ realization has index $2$. These are index values, without an automatic surjectivity claim.

More precisely, let $e$ be the number of boundary copies of $A$ whose opposite copy lies on a different cut component, and let $\ell$ be the number of circles with BOTH boundary copies on $A$. There is exactly one active component $S$ of the degree-$m$ incidence surface. Its root map $f:S\to\Sigma$ is injective and unramified on the interior. For some $a\le\ell$, its image is obtained by filling the internal paired circles and then removing one proper slit arc on each of $a$ of those circles. Its compact normalization has
$$\chi(S)=\chi(A)-a,\qquad r:=\deg(p|_S)=e+a.$$
Every other incidence component is one constant root disk. The active root meets exactly $\ell-a$ of their constant values in its interior. If $R_p$ is the total interior ramification of $p|_S$, then the actual Maslov index is
$$2\chi(A)+R_p+2(\ell-a)=2.$$

In particular the proof does not assume that the active root image is exactly the cut component: filled internal circles and slit boundaries are essential. It includes the one-cut-component case if such a disk exists, but asserts no existence there, no count one for arbitrary $A$, no perturbed-disk regularity or gluing, and no full Maslov/domain formula for arbitrary Whitney classes.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The analytic circle system, supplied planar cut component and actual smooth holomorphic unit-domain disk.

[F1] The zero-one-domain proof constructs the actual degree-$m$ bordered incidence cover $p:C\to\overline{\mathbb D}$ with root map $f:C\to\Sigma$. It proves that all active interior roots together are injective and unramified; constant components have values on the circle system. Its local multiplicity identity is exact. ([[lem-integrable-zero-one-domain-boundary-disks-have-dense-injective-points]], Proof1.1–3.1)

[F2] A boundary-positive tangent field on a finite smooth triangulated surface has zero index sum equal to its Euler characteristic, by finite face winding and sector cancellation. ([[lem-finite-triangulated-surface-tangent-field-index-sum]])

[F3] In symmetric-product support charts $\det(dq)$ is a nonzero constant times the product of root differences within each cluster, including higher collisions. ([[lem-symmetric-product-quotient-determinant-has-vandermonde-ramification]])

[F4] The actual compact-disk operator with oriented totally real boundary has real index $m+\mu$, where $\mu$ is the squared determinant boundary winding. Fixing its boundary node is a codimension-$m$ domain constraint and lowers index by $m$; the range remains closed. No surjectivity is inferred. ([[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]], [[lem-node-fixed-boundary-disk-sobolev-moduli-affine-slices-and-cover-index]])

[F5] Finite supported variations and parametric transversality give a tangent field, prescribed nonzero on the circle collars, with finitely many isolated transverse zeros away from any additional finite set. ([[thm-parametric-transversality]], [[lem-manifold-bump-for-a-compact-set-inside-an-open-set]])

[F6] A real-valued boundary holomorphic function extends by conjugation across an analytic straight edge: its tangential derivative is real and its normal derivative is its $i$ multiple, so the reflected derivatives match and the $C^1$ Cauchy–Riemann theorem applies. Its nonconstant local germ has a finite power order. ([[thm-continuous-partials-and-cauchy-riemann-imply-holomorphic]], [[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]], [[thm-holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem-several-variables]])

## Proof

1.1 Use [F1]'s actual incidence cover. Every point of the open cut component belonging to $A$ has exactly one active interior inverse point, by its exact unit-degree identity; no point in another cut region has one. Each active component has an open image meeting $A$, and different active interior images are disjoint. Since $A$ is connected, there is exactly one active component $S$. Every other root component is constant at some $c_j\in\gamma_j$. At the physical boundary each circle label selects exactly one root of the tuple. Thus the incidence cover is unbranched there, each boundary circle has one label and maps with degree one to the physical source circle, and there is exactly one incidence boundary circle per label. A constant component therefore has covering degree one and is a constant root disk. In particular $p|_S$ has degree equal to the number of active boundary labels. The finite exceptional root set from [F1] contains all interior ramification of this map. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We justify the complete boundary alternatives. Near every point of $\partial S$, extend a real analytic regular parametrization of its labelled $\gamma_j$ by its convergent complex power series. Its derivative is nonzero, so [F6] gives a holomorphic inverse coordinate taking that circle arc to the real line. By [F6] the nonconstant root extends across the source boundary. It has local form a nonzero real multiple of $z^b$ after real-preserving local coordinates. Interior injectivity forces $b\le2$: a power of order at least three maps two different points of the open half-disk to the same point. The case $b=2$ is a simple slit tip; its open half-disk maps onto a punctured neighborhood minus one ray. The case $b=1$ is one regular bank. There are finitely many tips, since the extended derivative zeros are isolated and the boundary is compact. An interior root preimage of a boundary image is impossible: its open image is a full neighborhood, while that boundary preimage also supplies a nonempty open half-neighborhood, duplicating nearby points outside the circles, contrary to [F1]. Hence interior and boundary images are disjoint. [F1, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Choose a smooth field $V$ on $\Sigma$, tangent and nonzero on every $\gamma_j$, with finitely many isolated zeros and no zero at any of the finitely many root collision values. Start with a nonzero oriented tangent in signed circle collars, extend it by finite bumps, and perturb only on the compact complement using [F5]. A finite set can be avoided by prescribing a nonzero local value there before the perturbation. The zeros are then finite and transverse; move the supplied finite face edges away from them. On each cut component its zero-index sum equals its Euler characteristic by [F2]. To meet that supplier's POSITIVE boundary convention, if the chosen field points in the opposite boundary direction multiply it on that component by $e^{i\theta}$, where $\theta$ is zero or $\pi$ near the respective boundary circles and is extended across the component by finitely many collar bumps. This introduces no zero or index change and makes it positive on all boundaries. Hence the original field's zero indices in $A$ sum to $\chi(A)$. Its pullback along the active interior has one inverse point for every such zero, preserving its local index by [F1]'s orientation-positive local inverse. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If only one side of $\gamma_j$ belongs to $A$, every point of that circle is a boundary image: approach it from $A$, use compactness of $S$, and exclude an interior limit because it would cover the other side too. A tip would likewise cover both sides, so no tip occurs. Its active boundary circle is a local diffeomorphism onto the whole $\gamma_j$, with degree one by injectivity of interior banks. Thus each of the $e$ external copies supplies one full active boundary. If both sides belong to $A$ and its root is constant, no active boundary has that label; the same compactness argument makes every point of that circle an active INTERIOR image. If its root is active, let $K_j$ be its connected compact boundary image. At a regular point of $K_j$ both adjacent sides of $A$ must be covered, so there are exactly two boundary banks, one from each side. A tip value has only its one tip preimage: that tip already covers a punctured neighborhood except its slit ray, and any other preimage would duplicate an open set. Compactness of the remainder of the boundary then gives a neighborhood of that value in which only its tip neighborhood contributes; along $\gamma_j$ this image is one-sided. Therefore each tip is an endpoint of $K_j$. There must be a tip: without one a connected boundary circle maps as an unbranched circle covering of fixed orientation, whereas the required two banks have opposite orientations. The image cannot be the whole target circle, since it has this one-sided endpoint neighborhood. It is therefore one proper closed arc, with exactly two endpoints and their order-two tips, and exactly two regular banks elsewhere. Finally a circle neither of whose sides belongs to $A$ has a constant root, since a nonconstant boundary bank would cover an adjacent open region. This proves all the asserted alternatives. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let $a$ count the internal circles of the slit type. The active interior image consists exactly of $A$ with each internal pair filled and with those $a$ slit arcs removed. Indeed every point off the circle system was accounted for by [F1]; on an internal circle, a point outside its boundary image has an interior inverse point by compactness, while step2.1 excludes such a point on the boundary image. The compact source is its normalization with the two banks separated, by the regular-bank and quadratic-tip models of step2.1. Glue each internal pair of boundary circles of the planar $A$. This leaves Euler characteristic unchanged, since a circle has Euler characteristic zero. At each slit remove a small open regular-neighborhood disk about that embedded interior arc; its boundary contracts to the two banks and two quadratic tips. This is exactly the same compact surface normalization up to a collar change, so it decreases Euler characteristic by one. Consequently $\chi(S)=\chi(A)-a$, and step3.1 gives $r=e+a$. Counting finite cells of the degree-$r$ branched cover gives $\chi(S)=r-R_p$: triangulate its base disk with its finitely many branch values as vertices; edges and faces have $r$ lifts, while a point of local degree $b$ loses $b-1$ of the usual lifted vertex count. The boundary is unbranched. Thus $R_p=r-\chi(S)$. The constant roots met by the active interior are exactly those on the $\ell-a$ filled internal circles; each is met once, and roots on all other constant labels lie outside this image. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Off root collisions define a section $s$ of $(u^*\det TX)^{\otimes2}$ by taking the square of the wedge of $dq(V(z_1)),\ldots,dq(V(z_m))$. Permuting roots changes the wedge by a real sign only, so its square is well defined. By [F3] its local expression is a nonzero constant times the squared Vandermonde times $\prod_i V(z_i)^2$ in target tangent coordinates. It extends continuously by zero at collisions. At a zero of $V$ away from collisions its index is twice that field index, with multiplicities added if several roots have zeros at the same base point; step2.2 excludes zeros at collision values. At a branch of the active base map $p$ of local degree $b$, the active root map is a local coordinate by [F1]. The $b$ active roots are the $b$ roots of the base parameter up to a holomorphic coordinate change. Their squared Vandermonde has winding $b-1$, as the product of pair differences is a unit times its elementary power-model product. Thus the total contribution of such branches is $R_p$. Each collision of one constant root with the active root contributes an additional $2$: in the active root coordinate $w$, the product of its differences from that constant value over the local $p$-sheets is a unit times the base parameter, so its SQUARE has winding two, including when that point is also a branch of $p$. Distinct constant roots cannot coincide, since their labels are disjoint circles. Active roots have no other collisions except branches, by [F1]. These cases exhaust the zeros of $s$. The extension and index calculations use continuous nonzero unit factors, so no false smoothness assertion about a general smooth field in symmetric root coordinates is required. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 Along the physical boundary the tuple has distinct entries and every $V(z_i)$ is a nonzero tangent vector to its labelled circle. Their wedge is therefore a real basis determinant for $TL$ in the actual complex target frame; its square is the squared determinant boundary phase times a positive scalar. Trivialize $u^*TX$ over the disk by finite disk frames. The winding of this nonzero boundary section is exactly its Maslov index. Finite zero excision for the continuous section $s$ identifies it with the sum of the indices from step5.1. Consequently $$\mu(u)=2\chi(A)+R_p+2(\ell-a).$$ Since $A$ is planar with $e+2\ell$ boundary copies, $\chi(A)=2-e-2\ell$. Substitute step4.1's $R_p=e+a-(\chi(A)-a)$ to obtain $\mu(u)=\chi(A)+e+2\ell=2$. All signs come from the actual positive complex local coordinates and squared determinant section, not a presumed index-domain formula. [F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 2.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 The compact operator result [F4] now gives real Fredholm index $m+2$ on all its stated higher integer Sobolev realizations. On the $H^3\to H^2$ realization the point trace is bounded by [F4], and evaluation at a boundary point is onto on the full domain: in a small boundary chart multiply any chosen vector in $T_xL$ by a scalar bump equal to one there, and extend by zero. Its codimension-$m$ kernel is the fixed-node domain. Restricting a Fredholm operator to a closed finite-codimension domain lowers its index by that codimension; its range is closed, as follows by projecting to a complement of the finite-dimensional operator kernel. Thus the node-fixed index is two. This does not assert that node evaluation is onto on the operator kernel or that either operator is onto. The proof includes a possible singly pointed domain with all region coefficients one if a disk actually exists; it makes no existence or count claim. It retains all slit and filled-circle cases needed in the multipointed setting. [F4, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
