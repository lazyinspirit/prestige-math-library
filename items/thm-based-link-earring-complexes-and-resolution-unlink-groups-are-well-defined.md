## Statement

Assume full AC. For any smooth based link $L\subset S^3$ with a chosen nonzero normal vector at its marked point, add the small meridional earring on that marked component and its joining arc. With the corresponding determinant-one singular bundle data, finite nondegenerate critical points and all-index regular holonomy perturbations give a finite free integral cochain complex. Its differential counts all index-one classes and has square zero; its homology, denoted $I^\natural(L;\mathbb Z)$, is independent of regular metric and perturbation and invariant under isotopy of the based link data, with the same orientation sign convention as for knots. For a knot it agrees with the singular knot complex already constructed.

For the standard $n$-component unlink with a marked component, $n\ge1$, this group is free of rank $2^{n-1}$. It has a chosen representative complex with zero differential and sphere-pole generators, in the even relative degrees of the unlink model. This theorem does not supply a canonical tensor-product identification under arbitrary cobordisms, saddle maps, an exact triangle or a KHI identification.

## Proof

**Given:** The based link with $r\ge1$ components and its earring, whose singular locus has $r+1$ components. The joining arc meets the marked component and the earring once each and no other component.

1.1 **The actual extension of the local data and critical theory.** Restrict the arc dual class to the boundary tori. It is one on the two earring tori and zero on the others. Its oriented integral arc lift therefore supplies the exterior fixed determinant exactly by [F1]'s scalar cocycle construction. On the two odd tori use the longitudinal-axis-reversing local models of [F1]; on each additional torus use the longitudinal-axis-preserving model. The same two-dimensional boundary cell argument identifies their $SO(3)$ bundles with the prescribed exterior restrictions and glues the branch charts. All determinant-one stabilizers are still central, because a parallel gauge is already forced to be central on either of the two reversing-axis cores. The invariant Sobolev and slice proofs in [F1] are finite local-chart constructions and thus apply to this larger finite chart set without a change of operator domain or weight. Its holonomy field bounds, coefficient-tail compactness and critical curvature bootstrap likewise use no restriction on the number of cores. The finite comparison obstruction for changing an $SO(3)$ comparison to a determinant-one comparison is now $H^1$ of this finite-type exterior with $\mathbb Z/2$ coefficients, still a finite set. Pass to one obstruction class and compose with its fixed representative as in [F1]. This proves the same proper critical projection. At every critical connection the holonomy detector uses only its central stabilizer, just established. Finite critical-cover Sard perturbation therefore gives a finite nondegenerate critical set. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Periods, with the infinite restriction factors retained.** The actual gauge-cell construction has two reversing-axis restriction factors $\mathbb Z/2$ and $r-1$ preserving-axis winding factors $\mathbb Z$. Restriction is onto on components by the vanishing first two homotopy groups of $SU(2)$. The remaining gauges are generated by the relative three-cell integer, modulo any relations coming from loops of those restriction spaces. These are precisely the generations proved by the same finite cell argument in [F2]; neither a finite component quotient nor finiteness of classes at fixed index follows. On an ordinary ball generator the pair of periods is $(\mathcal E,I)=(8\pi^2,8)$. For each orientable-core winding generator the local equivariant clutch calculated in [F6] has odd cover degree and fixed-circle winding one; its relative periods are $(4\pi^2,4)$ modulo the ordinary pair. The energy value is the lifted integer curvature transgression divided by two, and the index value is its actual invariant clutch calculation. For each reversing-axis restriction generator its square is a remaining relative kernel power, so additivity gives the same identity $\mathcal E=\pi^2I$, just as in [F2]. Relations cannot spoil that identity because both sides are genuine additive periods. Thus all index periods are divisible by four and $E_z=\pi^2I_z+c_{\alpha\beta}$ for all relative classes, for fixed endpoint representatives. Finitely many endpoints bound $c_{\alpha\beta}$. Hence an upper index bound gives a common flow-energy bound and a lower index bound, and the possible positive energy VALUES in this band form a finite set even though its abstract path classes may be infinite. This distinction is essential in the next step. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Actual trajectory regularity and end convergence.** The space of gauge components is countable by the finite relative cell description in step 2.1, including its integer core windings. Thus [F3]'s countable class Sard argument applies. Its universal cokernel detector, velocity orthogonality, time uniqueness and cylinder functions flat at the critical jets require only the smooth slices, holonomy bounds, central stabilizers and finite critical set, all established in step 1.1. They make every nonconstant trajectory regular at all indices and give $I\ge1$. The local compactness proof [F2] uses only finitely many ordinary or order-two charts, bounded forcing and finite lifting obstructions, so is unchanged on the additional cores. In particular the atom quantum and index loss are the same. For an arbitrary finite-flow-energy solution, translated far-out windows have energy tending to zero; the same local argument produces no atoms and a constant critical limit. Finitely many critical slices select a single end orbit. The cutoff limiting-Hessian inverse and its small nonlinear coefficient give the unweighted tail $H^1$ bound first, then the weighted inverse and full exponential bootstrap exactly as in [F3]. No finite-class assumption is used in either the local argument or this end argument. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Compactness over ALL classes, followed by actual class finiteness.** For a sequence of index-one or index-two solutions, step 2.1 supplies a common energy budget and a positive minimum among the endpoint trajectory energies in that budget. Apply the local extraction after arbitrary time shifts as in [F3]. Every nonconstant level consumes that positive minimum and every bubble consumes its atom quantum, so the process terminates. Intervening intervals that stay in a critical slice have energy given by the difference of one local functional lift; when their endpoint norms tend to zero their energy tends to zero. A first exit otherwise extracts another level. The wholly constant, bubble-free alternative for a nonconstant sequence is excluded by zero energy in the one critical slice, exactly as in [F3]. Exact index splitting and all-index regularity then exclude every bubble at total index at most two. This extraction did not first fix a relative path class. Its quotient paths are now close on a finite cover of the nonconstant core windows by actual Hilbert slice charts; on the long critical intervals they lie in one contractible critical slice. Interpolate the close representatives in each such chart and patch the resulting path homotopies in their overlaps, or subdivide the core path into the corresponding successive slice balls. This gives an endpoint-fixed homotopy to the limiting path, or to its ordered concatenation in the broken case. Thus the relative class is locally constant at every such limit. For index one there is one nonconstant limit; the union over all classes is consequently a compact zero-manifold and is finite. This proves finiteness of the actual index-one classes as a consequence, rather than inferring it from the infinite abstract gauge group. For index two the possible broken pairs are then finite. The exhaustive normal gluing of [F3] applies to those pairs, using the same critical inverse and local-in-time holonomy bounds, and makes the union over all index-two classes a compact one-manifold. This also makes the actually occurring index-two classes finite. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 **The extra orientation-loop generators.** In the determinant proof [F4], a closing gauge on the compactified cylinder restricts on a reversing-axis core to a temporally based circle family whose temporal winding is equal to its negative and hence zero. On a preserving-axis core that argument instead leaves one integer temporal winding. Normalize its zero-winding part by the real angle lift, exactly as before. A winding-one remainder is represented by transporting the elementary singular insertion once around that core: cut a small disk in the surface cylinder, insert the quaternion clutch of [F4], carry its center around the longitudinal circle, and close the equal complements. Its restriction has temporal winding one, by the fixed-circle degree-one clutch. The finite relative cell construction then leaves only the ordinary transported instantons and supported four-cell transformations already treated in [F4]. For this new singular generator the finite determinant excision identifies its line with $\det(T\Sigma\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R_Q)$. Here the surface cylinder is oriented and the axis line $Q$ is orientable along this additional core, so that line has positive monodromy. The reversing-axis cores give no such temporal generator. Hence every closing gauge preserves the determinant orientation. Cross-class comparisons still use the local complex model orientations, their $(d,n)$ relation and supported four-cell comparison from [F4]; they apply to either core type, and now transport on a preserving-axis core has no twist at all. Their exact exterior-product associativity supplies the same critical orientation modules and boundary product signs. No global singular $SU(3)$ lift and no unnoticed contraction of these extra winding loops was used. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The integral based-link complex.** Sum its finitely many critical orientation modules. Count every index-one class, with the determinant identifications of step 4.2. Finiteness is step 4.1, so this defines an integral degree-one differential on a finite free group with the relative modulo-four grading from step 2.1. The compact oriented one-manifolds of step 4.1 have exactly the ordered broken pairs as boundary, by the actual nonlinear gluing and exhaustion, so their signed boundary identity gives $d^2=0$ over $\mathbb Z$. This constructs the stated complex for based links without changing the original knot Definition. When $r=1$ its models, gauge group, equation and orientation convention are exactly those of the already constructed knot complex, so the constructions agree. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 **Continuation without the false fixed-index class shortcut.** Use the actual varying-metric, compact-time-supported continuation equations of [F4]. Uniform elliptic constants, the time-dependent functional identity and bounded parameter derivatives give $E\le\pi^2I+C$ by step 2.1, uniformly on compact parameter families. Every extracted autonomous endpoint level has the positive minimum from the finite energy VALUE set in step 2.1; only one main continuation level is present. The same all-class extraction and local slice homotopies as step 4.1 therefore give compactness and local constancy of the total path label. Regular main levels in a $p$-parameter family have $I+p\ge0$, endpoint levels have positive index, and a bubble costs at least four; total dimensions zero and one exclude bubbles. The normal critical-end inverse gives exhaustive collars for these levels and for composition necks. Compact zero-dimensional parameter moduli, even though initially taken over all classes, are consequently finite. The actual signed-boundary argument of [F4] now gives the continuation chain maps, one-parameter chain homotopies and composition identity. Constant continuation has just its invertible critical cylinders at index zero, so it is the identity; reverse paths give inverse equivalences. The determinant argument in step 4.2 is unchanged along these parameter families. Ambient transport and continuation then prove based-link isotopy invariance just as in [F4]. All this uses the actual all-class compactness just supplied, not the knot theorem's finite restriction quotient. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 **The invariant unlink group, with its maps still separate.** For $U_n$, [F5] supplies exactly $2^{n-1}$ nondegenerate pole critical points and even relative indices in every class. The small all-index regularization in step 3.1 keeps their jets, and thus their relative grades, unchanged. No index-one solution exists, so the finite complex from step 5.1 has zero differential and free rank $2^{n-1}$. Step 6.1 identifies this chosen calculation with $I^\natural(U_n;\mathbb Z)$ for every other regular choice. The pole labels and their relative even gradings are the ones in [F5]; any basis choices have their orientation signs. A natural tensor identification for general unlink isotopies, or the coefficients of a saddle cobordism, would require the separate cobordism construction and are not claimed from rank or grading alone. This proves precisely the stated link extension and unlink calculation, leaving those original cube-map operations explicit. [F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
