## Statement

For every finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, let $S=S(\mathfrak g)$, $R=S^{\mathfrak g}$, $I=SR_+$ and let $\mathcal H$ be its Kostant harmonic subspace. Then
$$S_d=\mathcal H_d\oplus I_d\quad(d\ge0),\qquad S=\mathcal H\oplus S S^{\mathfrak g}_+.$$
The splitting is graded and $\mathfrak g$-equivariant. This includes $\mathfrak g=0$ and is choice-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The indicated semisimple Lie algebra and symmetric adjoint action.

[F1] The bilinear Killing Fischer pairing is perfect and symmetric in each degree, $\mathcal H_d=I_d^\perp$, and both $I$ and $\mathcal H$ are graded and adjoint stable, by [[def-kostant-harmonic-subspace-of-the-symmetric-algebra]].

[F2] Cartan and root decomposition, the positive real coroot span, one-dimensional root spaces, exact nonzero root strings and simple-triple generation are [[lem-finite-semisimple-cartan-root-and-string-structure]].

[F3] The explicit rank-one string formulas, including positive raising/lowering compositions between adjacent weights, are [[lem-finite-lie-triangularization-and-rank-one-complete-reducibility]].

## Proof

1.1 Fix a Cartan, positive roots and normalized simple triples $e_i,f_i,h_i$ from F2. Write $a_{ij}=\alpha_j(h_i)$; these are real integers, and the $\alpha_i$ and $h_i$ are bases. Construct an auxiliary Lie algebra $C$ as follows, without any presentation theorem for $\mathfrak g$. Take the vector space on all finite formally bracketed words in the finite symbols $\mathbf e_i,\mathbf f_i,\mathbf h_i$, give it the bilinear grafting bracket, and quotient by the ideal generated by antisymmetry and Jacobi. Evaluation of words gives the free universal Lie property directly. Quotient further by $[\mathbf h_i,\mathbf h_j]=0$, $[\mathbf h_i,\mathbf e_j]=a_{ij}\mathbf e_j$, $[\mathbf h_i,\mathbf f_j]=-a_{ij}\mathbf f_j$, $[\mathbf e_i,\mathbf f_j]=\delta_{ij}\mathbf h_i$. All these relations hold for the chosen triples: for $i\ne j$, $\alpha_i-\alpha_j$ is not a root because its simple coefficients have opposite signs. Hence evaluation gives a surjection $\pi:C\to\mathfrak g$ by simple generation. The span $C^0$ of the $\mathbf h_i$ injects under $\pi$, because their images are a basis of $\mathfrak h$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $C^+,C^-$ be the subalgebras generated by the $\mathbf e_i,\mathbf f_i$. Jacobi expresses every bracket word in one sign as a sum of words $[\mathbf e_i,u]$, or respectively $[\mathbf f_i,u]$, with $u$ shorter: repeatedly apply $\bigl[ [a,b],c\bigr]=[a,[b,c]]-[b,[a,c]]$ to shorten the left entry. The same identity and the relations in step 1.1 prove inductively that $[\mathbf f_i,C^+]\subseteq C^0+C^+$, with the bracket in $C^+$ for positive word length greater than one; the length-one case lies in $C^0$. Indeed $[\mathbf f_i,[\mathbf e_j,u]]=-\delta_{ij}[\mathbf h_i,u]+[\mathbf e_j,[\mathbf f_i,u]]$, and the induction handles the last bracket. Reversing signs proves $[\mathbf e_i,C^-]\subseteq C^0+C^-$. Bracketing with $\mathbf h_i$ preserves each half by the product rule. Thus $C^-+C^0+C^+$ is stable under every generator, contains them, and by the same bracket-word reduction contains all of $C$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Give the generators degrees $-\alpha_i,\alpha_i,0$ in the free abelian group on the simple roots. Every defining relation is homogeneous, so the quotient has a direct grading. Step 2.1 shows all nonzero degrees have either entirely nonnegative or entirely nonpositive simple coordinates, and the degree-zero space is exactly $C^0$. Induction with Jacobi gives $[\mathbf h_i,u]=\gamma(h_i)u$ in degree $\gamma$. Distinct degrees have distinct joint weights because the simple roots form a basis of $\mathfrak h^*$. Every ideal $J\subseteq C$ is graded: for an element with finite homogeneous support, choose a linear combination of the $\mathbf h_i$ separating those finitely many weights, and use its finite interpolation polynomials to project that element onto each component within $J$. A separating combination exists because finitely many nonzero linear polynomials cannot vanish on every point: restrict them to a polynomial curve in a finite basis and avoid finitely many scalar roots. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, algebra]

4.1 The sum $K_*$ of all ideals $J$ satisfying $J\cap C^0=0$ is an ideal with the same property: by step 3.1 each such ideal has zero degree-zero component, as does every finite sum of its elements. This is a sum over a subset of the power set of $C$, so defines a set and uses no selection. The kernel $K$ of $\pi$ meets $C^0$ trivially, so $K\subseteq K_*$. Every nonzero ideal of $\mathfrak g$ meets $\mathfrak h$ nontrivially: project a nonzero element onto its finitely many Cartan/root weight components by the same interpolation. A nonzero Cartan component already suffices; a root component spans its one-dimensional root space and brackets with the opposite root into a nonzero coroot by F2. Now $\pi(K_*)$ is an ideal disjoint from $\mathfrak h$. To check disjointness, if $\pi(z)=\pi(h)$ with $z\in K_*$ and $h\in C^0$, then $z-h\in K\subseteq K_*$, hence $h\in K_*\cap C^0=0$. Thus $\pi(K_*)=0$ and $K_*=K$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F2, algebra]

5.1 The conjugate-linear assignment $\mathbf e_i\mapsto-\mathbf f_i$, $\mathbf f_i\mapsto-\mathbf e_i$, $\mathbf h_i\mapsto-\mathbf h_i$ preserves the relations of step 1.1 because all $a_{ij}$ are real. For example $[-\mathbf f_i,-\mathbf e_j]=-\delta_{ij}\mathbf h_i$, and $[-\mathbf h_i,-\mathbf f_j]=-a_{ij}\mathbf f_j$. It therefore defines an involutive conjugate-linear Lie automorphism of $C$. It preserves $C^0$ and permutes the ideals disjoint from it, so preserves $K_*$ and descends by step 4.1 to an involution $\sigma$ of $\mathfrak g$. It sends $e_i$ to $-f_i$, $f_i$ to $-e_i$, and each real coroot to its negative. Trace in any finite basis shows $B(\sigma x,\sigma y)=\overline{B(x,y)}$: conjugation by a conjugate-linear invertible map conjugates the entries and trace of the represented complex-linear operator. Hence $\langle x,y\rangle=-B(x,\sigma y)$ is a Hermitian form, linear in its first variable. [step 1.1, step 4.1, F1, F2, algebra]

6.1 For $h\in\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ and $x\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$, applying $\sigma$ to $[h,x]=\alpha(h)x$ gives $[h,\sigma x]=-\alpha(h)\sigma x$, since $\alpha(h)$ is real. Thus $\sigma\mathfrak g_\alpha=\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$. F2's bilinear orthogonality makes the Cartan and individual root spaces pairwise orthogonal for $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$. The Cartan restriction is positive definite because it is the complex Hermitian extension of the positive form $B|_{\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}}$. Also $\langle e_i,e_i\rangle=B(e_i,f_i)=2/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)>0$. Invariance and $\sigma f_i=-e_i$ give $$\langle[e_i,x],y\rangle=\langle x,[f_i,y]\rangle.$$ Every positive nonsimple root space is obtained by raising a smaller positive root along a simple string, as F2 proves. If $0\ne x$ is in that smaller space and raising is nonzero, F3 gives $[f_i,[e_i,x]]=c x$ with real $c>0$. Therefore $\langle[e_i,x],[e_i,x]\rangle=c\langle x,x\rangle$. Induction on root height proves positivity on every positive root space. Moreover $\langle\sigma x,\sigma x\rangle=\langle x,x\rangle$, by symmetry of $B$ and reality of a Hermitian diagonal value, so negative root spaces are positive too. Orthogonality now proves the form positive definite on all of $\mathfrak g$. [step 5.1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

7.1 Extend $\sigma$ to a conjugate-linear graded algebra involution of $S$. It preserves $R$: for $p\in R$, $\operatorname{ad}_z(\sigma p)=\sigma(\operatorname{ad}_{\sigma z}p)=0$. Hence it preserves $I$ degreewise. On $S_d$ define $$\langle p,q\rangle_d=(-1)^d(p,\sigma q)_B.$$ On products of $d$ linear generators this equals the sum over bijections of products of the Hermitian pairings in step 6.1, by F1's explicit Fischer formula. A finite orthonormal basis of $\mathfrak g$ exists by successive Gram–Schmidt subtraction and division by positive real square roots. Its degree-$d$ monomials are orthogonal for this pairing, with strictly positive squared norms $\prod_j a_j!$. Thus $\langle\ ,\ \rangle_d$ is positive definite. Since $\sigma I_d=I_d$, its orthogonal complement of $I_d$ is precisely the bilinear annihilator $I_d^\perp=\mathcal H_d$ from F1. [step 5.1, step 6.1, F1, given, algebra]

8.1 In a finite-dimensional positive Hermitian space, choose a finite orthonormal basis $u_1,\ldots,u_k$ of a subspace. The decomposition $v=\sum_j\langle v,u_j\rangle u_j+(v-\sum_j\langle v,u_j\rangle u_j)$ splits it from its perpendicular complement, and positivity makes their intersection zero. Apply this to $I_d$ in step 7.1. It gives exactly $S_d=I_d\oplus\mathcal H_d$. Taking finite homogeneous sums gives the graded direct sum in the Statement. Both summands are $\mathfrak g$-stable by F1, so the unique projection onto either summand commutes with every adjoint operator; this proves equivariance without asserting that the positive form itself is invariant under complex $\mathfrak g$. In degree zero $I_0=0$ and $\mathcal H_0=\mathbb C$; for $\mathfrak g=0$ the same is the whole algebra. All actual basis and spectral choices were finite, and the free-word set and sums of ideals used explicit set constructions; no AC occurs. [step 7.1, F1, given, algebra] ∎
