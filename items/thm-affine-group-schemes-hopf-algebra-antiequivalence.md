## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field. (a) The assignment $G\mapsto(\mathcal O(G),\Delta,\varepsilon,S)$ of [[def-coordinate-hopf-algebra-of-affine-group-scheme]] is a contravariant functor from affine group schemes of finite type over $k$ ([[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]) to finitely generated commutative Hopf algebras over $k$ ([[def-commutative-hopf-algebra-over-a-field]]), and it is fully faithful. (b) A finitely generated commutative Hopf algebra $A$ over $k$ has $\operatorname{Spec}A$ an affine group scheme of finite type over $k$ under the multiplication $\operatorname{Spec}\Delta\colon\operatorname{Spec}A\times_k\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, the identity $\operatorname{Spec}\varepsilon$ and the inverse $\operatorname{Spec}S$, and the two constructions are inverse on isomorphism classes. Hence $G\mapsto\mathcal O(G)$ restricts to an antiequivalence of categories between affine group schemes of finite type over $k$ and finitely generated commutative Hopf algebras over $k$, with quasi-inverse $A\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}A$. AC is used in (a) through [[lem-affine-finite-type-scheme-coordinate-ring-finitely-generated]] and in (b) for affine quasi-compactness; the diagram reversal and compatibility of morphisms are choice-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The coordinate ring of an affine group scheme is a commutative Hopf algebra via $\Delta=\mathcal O(m)$, $\varepsilon=\mathcal O(e)$, $S=\mathcal O(i)$, and a morphism of affine group schemes induces a morphism of commutative Hopf algebras. ([[lem-coordinate-ring-of-affine-group-scheme-is-a-hopf-algebra]], [[def-coordinate-hopf-algebra-of-affine-group-scheme]])

[F2] An affine group scheme of finite type over $k$ has finitely generated coordinate ring; this is the declared use of AC. ([[lem-affine-finite-type-scheme-coordinate-ring-finitely-generated]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F3] The global-sections functor is a contravariant equivalence between affine $k$-schemes and commutative $k$-algebras, with quasi-inverse $\operatorname{Spec}$, and it is fully faithful; moreover $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_kB)\cong\operatorname{Spec}A\times_k\operatorname{Spec}B$. ([[thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]], [[thm-affine-fibre-product-tensor-ring]], [[thm-global-sections-affine-scheme]], [[def-affine-scheme]])

[F4] A $k$-algebra homomorphism $A\to k$ corresponds to a morphism $\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, and the composite of comorphisms is the comorphism of the composite in reverse order. ([[def-coordinate-hopf-algebra-of-affine-group-scheme]], [[def-morphism-and-closed-subgroup-scheme]])

## Proof

**Given:** A field $k$, the data of [F1]-[F4], and the Axiom of Choice for [F2] and affine quasi-compactness in part (b).

1.1 (Part (a), the functor.) For an affine group scheme $G$ of finite type over $k$, [F1] makes $\mathcal O(G)$ a commutative Hopf algebra, finitely generated by [F2]; for a morphism $f\colon G\to H$ of group schemes, [F1] makes $\mathcal O(f)\colon\mathcal O(H)\to\mathcal O(G)$ a morphism of Hopf algebras, and $\mathcal O$ of composites and identities is computed by pullback, giving the contravariant functor. [F1, F2, F4]

1.2 (Part (b), the group scheme.) Let $A$ be a finitely generated commutative Hopf algebra over $k$. By [F3] the maps $\Delta,\varepsilon,S$ have comorphisms $m=\operatorname{Spec}\Delta\colon\operatorname{Spec}A\times_k\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, $e=\operatorname{Spec}\varepsilon\colon\operatorname{Spec}k\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $i=\operatorname{Spec}S\colon\operatorname{Spec}A\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, using $\operatorname{Spec}(A\otimes_kA)\cong\operatorname{Spec}A\times_k\operatorname{Spec}A$. Reversing the argument of [[lem-coordinate-ring-of-affine-group-scheme-is-a-hopf-algebra]] turns the coassociativity, counit and antipode axioms into $m\circ(m\times\operatorname{id})=m\circ(\operatorname{id}\times m)$, $m\circ(e\times\operatorname{id})=\operatorname{id}=m\circ(\operatorname{id}\times e)$ and $m\circ(i,\operatorname{id})=e\circ p=m\circ(\operatorname{id},i)$, because the comorphism of $m\times\operatorname{id}$ is $\Delta\otimes\operatorname{id}$ and $\mathcal O$ is a functor; hence $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is a group scheme over $k$. It is of finite type: the identity chart exhibits $k\to A$ as finitely generated, and the structure morphism is quasi-compact because affine schemes are quasi-compact ([[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]]). [F3, F4]

2.1 (Part (a), full faithfulness.) The functor is the restriction of the fully faithful anti-equivalence of [F3] to group objects and cogroup objects with structure-preserving morphisms: a scheme morphism $f\colon G\to H$ is a group-scheme morphism exactly when it satisfies $f\circ m_G=m_H\circ(f\times f)$, $f\circ e_G=e_H$, $i_H\circ f=f\circ i_G$ ([[def-morphism-and-closed-subgroup-scheme]]), and passing to comorphisms this is exactly the condition that $\mathcal O(f)$ preserves $\Delta$, $\varepsilon$, $S$; the bijection on Hom-sets is inherited from [F3], so the functor is full and faithful. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 (Part (b), inverse constructions.) Starting from a finitely generated commutative Hopf algebra $A$, the coordinate Hopf algebra of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ is $(\Gamma(\operatorname{Spec}A,\mathcal O),\mathcal O(m),\mathcal O(e),\mathcal O(i))\cong(A,\Delta,\varepsilon,S)$ by [F3] and [F4], so the round trip recovers $A$ up to isomorphism. Starting from an affine group scheme $G$ of finite type, [F3] gives $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O(G)\cong G$ and the transposition of the group identities identifies the reconstructed group structure with the original one, so the round trip recovers $G$ up to isomorphism. [F3, F4, step 1.2]

3.1 (Antiequivalence and choice.) Steps 2.1 and 2.2 show that $G\mapsto\mathcal O(G)$ is fully faithful and essentially surjective onto finitely generated commutative Hopf algebras, with quasi-inverse $A\mapsto\operatorname{Spec}A$; hence it is an antiequivalence of categories. AC was used in [F2] for finite generation and in step 1.2 for affine quasi-compactness. The group-object construction, compatibility of morphisms and round-trip isomorphisms are formal consequences of the affine anti-equivalence. [F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, given] ∎
