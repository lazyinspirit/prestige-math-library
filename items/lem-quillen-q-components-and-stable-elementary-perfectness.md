## Statement

Let $R$ be an associative unital ring. Form the small Quillen category $Q_R$ of finite projective left modules with split-epimorphism/split-monomorphism spans and pullback composition, and write $BQ_R$ for the geometric realization of its nerve, based at the zero module. The span assignment $(P\leftarrow M\to Q)\mapsto[\ker(M\to P)]$ induces a canonical isomorphism $\pi_1(BQ_R,0)\cong K_0(R)$. Equivalently $\pi_0(\Omega BQ_R)\cong K_0(R)$, naturally in unital ring homomorphisms. In addition the stable elementary group $E_\infty(R)$ is perfect and is the kernel of $\mathrm{GL}_\infty(R)\to K_1(R)$. No axiom of choice, regularity or commutativity assumption is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The span category in the Statement, using a small model of finite projectives, for example the images of all finite idempotent matrices with their module maps. Morphisms are isomorphism classes of spans, not chosen splittings. The nerve has one simplex for each finite composable list of arrows; realization identifies faces and degeneracies.

[F1] Use the explicit Q-span and nerve constructions specified in the Statement and Given. Their category and low-dimensional realization properties are proved below; no higher group-completion theorem is assumed.

[F2] $K_0(R)$ is generated by finite projective classes with direct-sum relations; $K_1(R)$ is the stable general-linear abelianization. [[def-algebraic-k0-and-k1-of-a-ring]]

[F3] Projective modules split epimorphisms, and finite projectives have finite-free complements. [[thm-projective-module-characterizations]]

[F4] Cellular approximation is choice-free for a finite relative source, including a circle or a disk with its prescribed boundary. Compact CW images have finite cell support without choice. [[thm-cellular-approximation-for-maps-of-cw-pairs]] [[lem-compact-cw-images-have-finite-cell-support-without-choice]]

[F5] Seifert–van Kampen identifies the fundamental group of a path-connected open union with the pushout of the two groups over that of the path-connected overlap. [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]]

[F6] $E_\infty(R)$ is generated by finite elementary matrices and equals the stable general-linear commutator subgroup. [[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]] [[lem-the-stable-elementary-subgroup-is-normal-and-contains-the-commutator-subgroup]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** explicit edge loops and kernel composition, followed by actual elementary commutator witnesses.

1.1 The span rule makes sense over any associative unital ring, not only commutative rings. If $P\leftarrow M\hookrightarrow Q$ and $Q\leftarrow N\hookrightarrow T$ are such spans, their composite middle object is $W=M\times_QN$. The projection $W\to M$ is epic, with kernel $\ker(N\to Q)$, and splits because $M$ is projective. Thus $W$ is finite projective. The composite $W\to P$ is split epic, and $W\to T$ is a split injection: its quotient sits in an exact sequence with the projective quotients $Q/M$ and $T/N$, so is finite projective. Indeed $N/W\cong Q/M$ by the epimorphism $N\to Q$, and $0\to N/W\to T/W\to T/N\to0$ splits. Pullback uniqueness makes composition associative on isomorphism classes, with identity spans. The idempotent-image model is a set; each of these finite projectives is isomorphic to an object of that model by [F3], and its span isomorphism class is unambiguous, so composition selects no family of representatives or splittings. [F1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For a nerve realization the edges are category arrows and the2-simplex relation says that a path along two composable arrows is homotopic to their composite. These, inverse-edge cancellations and identity edges give the complete fundamental-group relations. Here is the required low-dimensional justification. Filtering by nondegenerate simplices constructs the realization as a CW complex: attach one standard closed simplex along its boundary for each nondegenerate list, with degenerate faces collapsed by their prescribed face maps. Boundary faces form a finite set, so closure finiteness and the weak CW topology follow directly from the realization quotient. A circle map or a based homotopy from a disk has finite cell support by [F4], and cellular approximation for that finite relative source places it in the1- or2-skeleton respectively. In a finite graph, subdividing an edge path and cancelling a backtracking edge gives its ordinary free path word. Attaching a2-simplex imposes exactly its oriented boundary word: apply [F5] to its open interior with a collar and the old graph with the complementary collar; the overlap retracts onto the attaching circle, so the pushout kills that boundary word and no other relations. Repeating through the finitely many2-simplices gives precisely the stated relations. Higher cells change none of them by the finite-source cellular approximation. Thus no infinite selection of paths or spanning trees is needed. [F4, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.3 By [F6] and [F2], $E_\infty(R)$ already equals the kernel of the stable map to $K_1(R)$. Its perfectness has explicit witnesses: for distinct $i,j,k$, direct multiplication using $E_{ij}E_{jk}=E_{ik}$ and the other relevant products zero gives $[e_{ij}(r),e_{jk}(1)]=e_{ik}(r)$. Each factor lies in $E_\infty(R)$. Every elementary generator $e_{ik}(r)$ is therefore a commutator in that group, using the least additional index $j$ after a finite stabilization if necessary. Since all elements are finite products of these generators, $E_\infty(R)=[E_\infty(R),E_\infty(R)]$. The indices and matrices are explicit finite witnesses, with no global choice of factorizations. [F2, F6, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $z_P:0\to P$ be the span $0\leftarrow0\to P$, and let $w_P:0\to P$ be $0\leftarrow P\xrightarrow1P$. The edges $z_P$ connect every object to0. Put $\kappa(P)=[w_Pz_P^{-1}]$, where paths are written in traversal order. For a span $\gamma:P\xleftarrow pM\xrightarrow iQ$ let $K=\ker p$, and write $j:K\to Q$ for its induced split injection. The2-simplex composition rule gives $z_P\gamma=w_Kj$, since the pullback of $0\to P$ along $p$ is exactly $K$. Also $z_Kj=z_Q$. Consequently its normalized based edge loop $z_P\gamma z_Q^{-1}$ equals $\kappa(K)$. Every based edge loop is a product of normalized edges and their inverses, by insertion of the cancelling paths $z_P^{-1}z_P$ between adjacent edges. Hence the $\kappa(P)$ generate $\pi_1(BQ_R,0)$. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Isomorphic projectives have the same $\kappa$: for an isomorphism $u:P\to Q$ the two2-simplex identities are $z_Pu=z_Q$ and $w_Pu=w_Q$. Moreover $\kappa(P)\kappa(Q)=\kappa(P\oplus Q)$. To check this, take $\gamma:P\to P\oplus Q$ represented by the projection $P\oplus Q\to P$ and the identity injection into $P\oplus Q$. Its kernel is $Q$, so its normalized loop is $\kappa(Q)$ by step 2.1. Composing it with $w_P$ gives $w_{P\oplus Q}$, which proves the equality by cancelling $z_P$. The block-swap isomorphism $P\oplus Q\cong Q\oplus P$ therefore makes these generators commute. The zero module gives the identity loop. Thus the presentation of [F2] gives a homomorphism $a:K_0(R)\to\pi_1(BQ_R,0)$, $[P]\mapsto\kappa(P)$. [F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Conversely assign the weight $[\ker p]\in K_0(R)$ to each span $P\xleftarrow pM\to Q$. It is invariant under span isomorphisms. For two composable spans in step 1.1, their composite kernel $K$ fits into $0\to\ker(N\to Q)\to K\to\ker(M\to P)\to0$: restrict $W\to M$ to the inverse image of $\ker(M\to P)$. The quotient is projective, so this sequence splits by [F3] and its class is the sum of the two weights. Identity weights are zero. Hence the weights respect every2-simplex and inverse-edge relation of step 1.2, defining $b:\pi_1(BQ_R,0)\to K_0(R)$. The weights of $w_P,z_P$ are respectively $[P],0$, so $ba$ is identity on generators. Step3.1 says that every normalized edge is $\kappa$ of its own weight, so $ab$ is identity on all loops. This proves the asserted isomorphism. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Scalar extension under an arbitrary unital map $R\to S$ preserves split exact sequences and their kernels: write a split epimorphism as a projection from its kernel direct sum its target and tensor that actual splitting. It likewise preserves the pullbacks used in step 1.1, as their kernels and split quotient descriptions show. It therefore induces the Q functor and sends $z_P,w_P$ to $z_{SP},w_{SP}$. Both $a,b$ commute with it by their explicit formulas. A based loop's path component in $\Omega BQ_R$ is its based homotopy class, so the same calculation identifies $\pi_0(\Omega BQ_R)$ with $K_0(R)$. This does not identify any higher homotopy or homology groups with stable general-linear groups. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Empty edge words, zero modules and the zero ring give the unit in every displayed construction. All chosen kernels and pullbacks are uniquely specified objects up to their isomorphism classes, and the only splittings used in the proof concern a finite diagram at a time. The finite-source clauses of [F4] suffice even though the nerve has arbitrarily many simplices. Thus all assertions, including naturality and perfectness, hold in the stated unqualified scope. The higher Q/group-completion and arithmetic stability comparisons remain separate assertions not proved by this lemma. [F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
