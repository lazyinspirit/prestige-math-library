## Statement

Let $(S,m)$ be a finite Coxeter matrix with presented group $W$, length $\ell$ and geometric representation $\sigma:W\to\mathrm{GL}_K(E)$ as in [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[def-hh-geometric-coxeter-representation-and-roots]] and [[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]], and let $J\subseteq S$.

1. **Support and reduction.** For every $w\in W$ the set $S(w)$ of letters occurring in a reduced expression of $w$ is independent of the reduced expression, and every word in $S$ representing $w$ can be transformed into a reduced expression by repeatedly deleting two letters (Tits reduction, using only letters already present). Consequently
$$W_J=\langle J\rangle=\{w\in W:S(w)\subseteq J\}.$$
2. **Intrinsic parabolic presentation.** Let $W_J^\ast$ be the group presented by the restricted Coxeter matrix $(J,m|_J)$ in the sense of [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]. The canonical homomorphism $W_J^\ast\to W_J$, $s\mapsto s$, is an isomorphism. Hence $(W_J,J)$ is a Coxeter system, its intrinsic length function $\ell_J$ agrees with the ambient length $\ell$ on $W_J$, and $W_J\cap S=J$.
3. **Minimal coset representatives.** Every right coset $W_J a:=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$ ($a\in W$, [[def-coset]]) has a unique element $d$ of minimal length; it is characterized by $\ell(sd)>\ell(d)$ for all $s\in J$, and it satisfies
$$\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)\qquad\text{for all }u\in W_J .$$
Equivalently, every $w\in W$ has a unique factorization $w=u\,d$ with $u\in W_J$ and $d$ the minimal representative of the right coset $W_J d$, and then $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$. By inversion ($w\mapsto w^{-1}$ preserves lengths and interchanges the two coset families $\{W_Ja\}$ and $\{aW_J\}$), every left coset $aW_J:=\{au:u\in W_J\}$ has a unique minimal element $d$, characterized by $\ell(ds)>\ell(d)$ for all $s\in J$ and satisfying $\ell(du)=\ell(d)+\ell(u)$ for all $u\in W_J$.
4. **Type A.** Let $n\ge2$, $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}\}$ and $m(s_i,s_j):=3$ if $|i-j|=1$, $m(s_i,s_j):=2$ if $|i-j|>1$ (a Coxeter matrix of type $A_{n-1}$). Then $s_i\mapsto(i\ i+1)$ extends to an isomorphism $W\to S_n$ (the letters $1,\dots,n$ carry the library's symmetric group by the order-preserving identification with $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, under which $(i\ i+1)$ is the adjacent transposition $(i-1\ i)$), and for every $w\in W$,
$$\ell(w)=\operatorname{inv}\bigl(\varphi(w)\bigr),$$
the inversion number of the corresponding permutation ([[def-inversions-inversion-number-and-sign]]). In particular a word in the $s_i$ is reduced if and only if its length equals the inversion number of its value.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the group $W$ with its length function $\ell$, the geometric representation $\sigma:W\to\mathrm{GL}_K(E)$, and a subset $J\subseteq S$ for parts (1)-(3); the type-A matrix and data of part (4).

[F1] [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]: $W=F(S)/N$ is the group presented by $(S,m)$ with relators $s^2$ $(s\in S)$ and $(st)^{m(s,t)}$ $(s\ne t,\ m(s,t)<\infty)$; for every group $G$ and every map $f:S\to G$ with $f(s)^2=1$ and $(f(s)f(t))^{m(s,t)}=1$ whenever $m(s,t)<\infty$ there is a unique homomorphism $W\to G$ with $s\mapsto f(s)$. The length $\ell(w)$ is the least length of a word in $S$ representing $w$, and $\ell(1)=0$; for $J\subseteq S$, $W_J=\langle\{s:s\in J\}\rangle$.

[F2] [[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]]: for all $w\in W$, $s\in S$ one has $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)\pm1$ and $\ell(ws)=\ell(w)\pm1$; if $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ is reduced and $\ell(sw)=k-1$ then $sw=s_1\cdots\widehat{s_i}\cdots s_k$ for some $i$; and a word is reduced if and only if it cannot be shortened by deleting two letters, that is, any non-reduced word $s_1\cdots s_k$ has $s_1\cdots\widehat{s_i}\cdots\widehat{s_j}\cdots s_k=s_1\cdots s_k$ for some $i<j$.

[F3] [[thm-hh-matsumoto-reduced-word-theorem]]: any two reduced expressions of the same element are braid-equivalent, where a braid move replaces an alternating subword of length $m(s,t)<\infty$ by the alternating word of the same length with the two letters interchanged; and a word is reduced if and only if it is M-reduced, that is, cannot be shortened by a sequence of braid moves and cancellations of consecutive equal pairs.

[F4] [[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]]: $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{0,1,\dots,n-1\})$ with composition $(\sigma\tau)(i)=\sigma(\tau(i))$, one-line notation $\sigma=[\sigma(0),\dots,\sigma(n-1)]$, and cycle notation; a $2$-cycle $(a\ b)$ is a transposition.

[F5] [[def-inversions-inversion-number-and-sign]]: $\operatorname{Inv}(\sigma)=\{(i,j):i<j,\ \sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\}$ and $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)=|\operatorname{Inv}(\sigma)|$.

[F6] [[lem-disjoint-cycles-commute]]: transpositions with disjoint supports commute. Generation by the zero-indexed adjacent transpositions is proved directly in step 1.2.

[F7] [[def-coset]]: "For $g\in G$, the **left coset** and **right coset** of $H$ represented by $g$ are $$gH:=\{gh:h\in H\},\qquad Hg:=\{hg:h\in H\}.$$", so the sets $W_Ja=\{ua:u\in W_J\}$ and $aW_J=\{au:u\in W_J\}$ are the two coset families of the subgroup $W_J$.

[F8] [[thm-well-ordering-principle]]: every nonempty subset of $\mathbb{N}$ has a least element.

## Proof

**Given:** The data of the statement.

1.1 **Support and Tits reduction.** Let $w\in W$. If $s_1\cdots s_k$ and $s'_1\cdots s'_k$ are reduced expressions of $w$, then by [F3] they are braid-equivalent, and a braid move replaces an alternating block in two letters by the other alternating word of the same length, so it preserves the set of letters occurring; hence the set $S(w)$ of letters of a reduced expression is independent of the chosen expression. Next, let $t_1\cdots t_r$ be a word in $S$ with value $w$ and length $r>\ell(w)$; by [F2] there are indices $i<j$ with $t_1\cdots\widehat{t_i}\cdots\widehat{t_j}\cdots t_r=t_1\cdots t_r=w$, so deleting the two letters leaves the value and uses only letters already present. Iterating, the length drops by two at each step until it reaches $\ell(w)$, and the resulting reduced expression of $w$ uses only letters of the original word. The values of finite words in $J$ form a subgroup: the empty word gives $1$, concatenation gives products, and reversal gives inverses because $j^{-1}=j$ for $j\in J$. This subgroup contains $J$ and lies in every subgroup containing $J$, so it equals $W_J$ by [[def-generated-subgroup]]. Consequently $w\in W_J$ if and only if $S(w)\subseteq J$: if $S(w)\subseteq J$, a reduced expression of $w$ is a word in the letters of $J$, so $w\in\langle J\rangle=W_J$; conversely, if $w\in W_J$, then $w$ is a product of elements of $J$, hence the value of some word in the letters $J$, which reduces as above to a reduced expression whose letters lie in $J$, so $S(w)\subseteq J$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 **Type A.** Let $n\ge2$ and let $W$ now be the group presented by the type-A matrix on $S=\{s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}\}$; we use the library's symmetric group $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{0,\dots,n-1\})$ and the transpositions $\tau_i=(i\ i+1)$ for $0\le i\le n-2$, which are the elements written $(i+1\ i+2)$ in the statement under the order-preserving identification of $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ with $\{1,\dots,n\}$. The relators hold in $S_n$: $\tau_i^2=\operatorname{id}$; $\tau_i\tau_j=\tau_j\tau_i$ when $|i-j|>1$, because the two transpositions have disjoint supports and disjoint cycles commute by [F6]; and $\tau_i\tau_{i+1}\tau_i=\tau_{i+1}\tau_i\tau_{i+1}$ by direct computation on the three symbols $i,i+1,i+2$. By the universal property in [F1] there is a homomorphism $\varphi:W\to S_n$ with $\varphi(s_{i+1})=\tau_i$. It is onto by the following direct argument. If a permutation $\pi$ is not the identity, its one-line entries are not increasing (the unique increasing bijection of $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ fixes every entry), so some $i$ has $\pi(i)>\pi(i+1)$. Right multiplication by $\tau_i$ swaps these neighbouring entries and lowers $\operatorname{inv}(\pi)$ by one: the total contributions of pairs involving either position and a third position are unchanged, while the inversion $(i,i+1)$ disappears. Repeating reaches inversion number zero and hence the identity, expressing $\pi$ as a product of adjacent transpositions; this uses only [F4] and [F5]. We show $|W|\le n!$. Let $H:=\langle s_1,\dots,s_{n-2}\rangle\le W$ (so $H=\{1\}$ when $n=2$), and for $1\le j\le n-1$ put $r_j:=s_{n-1}s_{n-2}\cdots s_j$, and put $r_n:=1$. Right multiplication by a generator obeys: if $i\le j-2$ then $r_js_i=s_ir_j$, because $s_i$ commutes with every factor of $r_j$; if $i=j-1$ then $r_js_i=r_{j-1}$; if $i=j$ then $r_js_i=r_{j+1}$, by $s_j^2=1$ (with $r_{n-1}s_{n-1}=r_n$); and if $i>j$ then $r_js_i=s_{i-1}r_j$, because moving the final $s_i$ left past $s_{i-2},\dots,s_j$ and using $s_is_{i-1}s_i=s_{i-1}s_is_{i-1}$ turns it into a leading $s_{i-1}$, which then commutes left past $s_{i+1},\dots,s_{n-1}$; for $r_n=1$ the same rules read $r_ns_i=s_ir_n$ for $i\le n-2$ and $r_ns_{n-1}=r_{n-1}$. In each case $r_js_i$ has the form $h\,r_k$ with $h\in H$: either $h=s_i$ with $i\le n-2$, or $h=1$, or $h=s_{i-1}$ with $i-1\le n-2$. Hence the union $T=\bigcup_{j=1}^n Hr_j$ contains $1=r_n$ and is stable under right multiplication by every generator; since every generator is its own inverse, every word in the generators lies in $T$, so $W=T$ and $|W|\le n\,|H|$. The relators of the type-A matrix on $n-2$ generators hold among $s_1,\dots,s_{n-2}$ in $W$, so by [F1] there is a surjection from the corresponding presented group onto $H$; by induction on $n$, whose base $n=2$ gives $|W|=2$, this yields $|H|\le(n-1)!$ and hence $|W|\le n!$. On the other hand $|S_n|=n!$, since a bijection of the $n$-element set $\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ is determined by choosing the image of $0$ in $n$ ways, then the image of $1$ in $n-1$ ways, and so on. Therefore the surjection $\varphi$ between the finite groups $W$ and $S_n$ is a bijection, hence an isomorphism. It remains to identify $\ell$ with the inversion number. Right multiplication by $\tau_i$ interchanges the entries at positions $i$ and $i+1$ in the one-line notation by [F4], so it exchanges the inversion statuses of the pairs $(p,i),(p,i+1)$ for $p<i$ and of the pairs $(i,q),(i+1,q)$ for $q>i+1$, while the pair $(i,i+1)$ becomes an inversion exactly when it was not one; hence $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma\tau_i)=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)\pm1$, with $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma\tau_i)=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)-1$ exactly when $\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)$ by [F5]. Therefore every word of length $k$ in the generators $\tau_i$ representing $\sigma$ has $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)\le k$ (each letter changes the inversion number by one), so $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)\le\ell_A(\sigma)$ for the length function $\ell_A$ of $S_n$ with respect to the generating set $\{\tau_i\}$; and if $\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)>0$ then some $i$ has $\sigma(i)>\sigma(i+1)$ (otherwise $\sigma$ would be increasing, hence the identity), so $\ell_A(\sigma)\le\ell_A(\sigma\tau_i)+1=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma\tau_i)+1=\operatorname{inv}(\sigma)$ by induction on the inversion number, giving $\ell_A=\operatorname{inv}$. Finally $\ell(w)=\ell_A(\varphi(w))$ for all $w\in W$: a word of length $\ell(w)$ in the $s_i$ representing $w$ maps to a word of the same length in the $\tau_i$ representing $\varphi(w)$, so $\ell_A(\varphi(w))\le\ell(w)$; conversely a word $\tau_{i_1}\cdots\tau_{i_k}$ of length $k=\ell_A(\varphi(w))$ for $\varphi(w)$ lifts to the word $s_{i_1+1}\cdots s_{i_k+1}$, which maps to $\varphi(w)$, so it represents $w$ by injectivity of $\varphi$ and $\ell(w)\le k$. Hence $\ell(w)=\operatorname{inv}(\varphi(w))$, and by [F2] a word in the $s_i$ is reduced exactly when its length equals the inversion number of its value. [F1, F4, F5, F6]

2.1 **The intrinsic parabolic presentation.** Let $W_J^\ast$ be the group presented by $(J,m|_J)$ as in [F1]. The map $f:J\to W_J\le W$, $j\mapsto j$, satisfies the relator conditions in $W$, because every relator of the restricted matrix is a relator of $(S,m)$; so [F1] gives a homomorphism $f:W_J^\ast\to W_J$, which is onto because the elements of $J$ generate $W_J$. For injectivity define $f':W_J\to W_J^\ast$ by choosing, for $w\in W_J$, a reduced expression $w=j_1\cdots j_q$ in $W$ and setting $f'(w):=j_1\cdots j_q$ (product in $W_J^\ast$); by step 1.1 all letters $j_l$ lie in $J$. This is well defined: another reduced expression $j'_1\cdots j'_q$ of $w$ is braid-equivalent to it by [F3], and every braid move involved replaces an alternating block in two letters of $J$ by the other alternating word, which is a defining relation of $W_J^\ast$, so the two products agree. To see that $f'$ is a homomorphism it suffices to show $f'(jw)=j\,f'(w)$ for $j\in J$ and $w\in W_J$. If $\ell(jw)=\ell(w)+1$, then prepending $j$ to a reduced expression of $w$ gives a reduced expression of $jw$ with letters in $J$, and the claim is immediate. If $\ell(jw)=\ell(w)-1$, then [F2] applied to a reduced expression $w=j_1\cdots j_q$ gives $jw=j_1\cdots\widehat{j_i}\cdots j_q$ for some $i$. The deleted word is reduced of length $q-1$, and prepending $j$ to it gives another reduced expression of $w$ of length $q$, with every letter in $J$. By [F3] these two reduced expressions of $w$ are braid-equivalent using only letters in $J$, so their products agree in $W_J^\ast$: $j_1\cdots j_q=j\,j_1\cdots\widehat{j_i}\cdots j_q$. Multiplying this equality by $j$ in $W_J^\ast$ gives $j\,f'(w)=j_1\cdots\widehat{j_i}\cdots j_q=f'(jw)$. Iterating $f'(jw)=j f'(w)$ along a word for any $u\in W_J$, and using $f'(1)=1$, gives $f'(uw)=f'(u)f'(w)$, so $f'$ is a homomorphism. By construction $ff'(w)=w$ for all $w\in W_J$, and $f'f$ fixes each generator of $W_J^\ast$ because $f'(j)=j$ for $j\in J$; hence $f'f=\mathrm{id}_{W_J^\ast}$. Thus $f$ and $f'$ are inverse isomorphisms, and $(W_J,J)$ is a Coxeter system. If $w\in W_J$ and $j_1\cdots j_q$ is a reduced expression of $w$ in $W$, then all $j_l\in J$ by step 1.1, so the same word of length $q$ is a word in the generators $J$ of $W_J^\ast$, giving $\ell_J(w)\le\ell(w)$; conversely a word in the letters $J$ representing $w$ is a word in $S$ representing $w$, so $\ell(w)\le\ell_J(w)$; hence $\ell_J=\ell$ on $W_J$. If $s\in S\cap W_J$, then $S(s)=\{s\}\subseteq J$ by step 1.1, so $s\in J$ and $W_J\cap S=J$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 **Minimal coset representatives.** Fix $a\in W$. The set $\{\ell(x):x\in W_Ja\}$ is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb{N}$ and so has a least element by [F8]; choose $d\in W_Ja$ of minimal length. For $s\in J$ one has $sd\in W_Ja$, so $\ell(sd)\ge\ell(d)$; by [F2] $\ell(sd)=\ell(d)\pm1$, and therefore $\ell(sd)=\ell(d)+1>\ell(d)$. Now let $u\in W_J$ with a reduced expression $u=u_1\cdots u_p$ and let $d=d_1\cdots d_q$ be reduced, so $q=\ell(d)$; by step 1.1 all $u_l$ lie in $J$. Applying the Tits reduction of step 1.1 to the concatenated word $u_1\cdots u_pd_1\cdots d_q$ produces a reduced expression of $ud$ that is a subsequence of it, hence splits as $u'\,d'$ with $u'$ a subsequence of $u_1\cdots u_p$ and $d'$ a subsequence of $d_1\cdots d_q$; write $u',d'$ also for their values. If $d'$ is not the full word $d_1\cdots d_q$, then $d'=(u')^{-1}ud\in W_Jd=W_Ja$, while $d'$ has fewer than $q$ letters, so $\ell(d')<q=\ell(d)$, contradicting the minimality of $d$. Hence $d'$ is the full $d$-word, so $u'd=ud$ and $u'=u$; since $u'$ is a subsequence of the reduced word $u_1\cdots u_p$ of length $p=\ell(u)$ and represents $u$, it must use all $p$ letters, so the concatenation is reduced and $\ell(ud)=p+q=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$. If $d''\in W_Ja$ also has minimal length, write $d''=ud$ with $u\in W_J$; then $\ell(d'')=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ with $\ell(d'')=\ell(d)$, so $\ell(u)=0$, $u=1$ and $d''=d$; this gives uniqueness. Conversely, let $d$ satisfy $\ell(sd)>\ell(d)$ for all $s\in J$ and write $d=u\,d_0$ with $d_0$ the minimal representative of $W_Jd$; additivity gives $\ell(d)=\ell(u)+\ell(d_0)$, and if $u\neq1$ and $u=s_1\cdots s_p$ is a reduced expression with $p\ge1$, then $\ell(s_1d)=\ell(s_2\cdots s_pd_0)\le(p-1)+\ell(d_0)<\ell(d)$, contradicting the hypothesis; hence $u=1$ and $d=d_0$ is the minimal representative. The factorization $w=ud$ of an arbitrary $w\in W$ is now obtained by taking $d$ minimal in $W_Jw$ and $u:=wd^{-1}\in W_J$, and its uniqueness follows from the uniqueness of $d$. For left cosets, note that $\ell(u^{-1})=\ell(u)$ for every $u$: reversing a reduced word for $u$ gives a word of the same length for $u^{-1}$, so $\ell(u^{-1})\le\ell(u)$, and applying this to $u^{-1}$ gives equality. Inversion is an anti-automorphism interchanging right and left cosets and fixing lengths, so applying the right-coset statement to inverses gives unique minimal elements of the left cosets $aW_J$, characterized by $\ell(ds)>\ell(d)$ for $s\in J$ and satisfying $\ell(du)=\ell(d)+\ell(u)$. [F1, F2, F7, F8, step 1.1] ∎
