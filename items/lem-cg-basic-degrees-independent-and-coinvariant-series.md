## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(W,S)$ be a Coxeter system of finite type, $n=|S|$, and let $V_{\mathbb C}$, $S=\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, $R=S^{W}$, $I=SR_+$, $A=S/I$, and a fixed basic family $f_1,\dots,f_n$ with degrees $d_1\le\dots\le d_n$ and exponents $e_i=d_i-1$ be as in [[def-cg-coxeter-basic-degrees-and-graded-coinvariants]] (so $f_1,\dots,f_n$ is a minimal homogeneous generating family of $I$, generates $R$, is algebraically independent, and gives a graded isomorphism $\mathbb C[y_1,\dots,y_n]\to R$, $y_i\mapsto f_i$, with $\deg y_i=d_i$).

**(1) Multiset independence.** If $f'_1,\dots,f'_n$ is any other minimal homogeneous generating family of $I$ with degrees $d'_i$, then $\{d_1,\dots,d_n\}=\{d'_1,\dots,d'_n\}$ as multisets. Hence the nondecreasing degree sequence $d_1\le\dots\le d_n$, the exponents $e_1\le\dots\le e_n$ and the degree count are invariants of the pair $(W,V_{\mathbb C})$.

**(2) Hilbert series.** $\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}$ and $\operatorname{Hilb}(A,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+t+\dots+t^{d_i-1})$ as formal power series ([[def-hilbert-function-and-hilbert-series]]).

**(3) Order formula.** $\dim_{\mathbb C}A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}d_i=|W|$, and $\sum_{i=1}^n e_i$ is the top degree of $A$.

**(4) Molien identity.** $\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}=\dfrac{1}{|W|}\sum_{w\in W}\det(1-tw\mid V_{\mathbb C}^*)^{-1}$ as formal power series, the determinants expanded as finite products over the eigenvalues of $\rho_{\mathbb C}(w)$ on $V_{\mathbb C}^*$.

**(5) Conventions.** For $n=0$ all products are empty and equal $1$, and $A=\mathbb C$. For reducible $S$ the degree multiset is the concatenation of the components' multisets (this is used, with its own argument, in the final determination theorem). All statements are under the stated AC.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Coxeter system $(W,S)$ of finite type, and a basic family $f_1,\dots,f_n$ as in the statement.

[F1] $f_1,\dots,f_n$ is a minimal finite family of homogeneous positive-degree invariants generating $I=SR_+$, it generates $R$ as a $\mathbb C$-algebra and is algebraically independent; under AC any minimal such family has exactly $n=|S|$ elements, and every $d_i\ge2$ ([[def-cg-coxeter-basic-degrees-and-graded-coinvariants]], [[lem-finite-reflection-invariant-generators-are-algebraically-independent]]).

[F2] The complexification $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)\le\mathrm{GL}(V_{\mathbb C})$ is a finite subgroup of order $|W|$, generated by complex reflections, faithful, and $\dim_{\mathbb C}V_{\mathbb C}=n$ ([[lem-cg-complexification-satisfies-reflection-invariant-hypotheses]]).

[F3] Assume AC. For the finite complex reflection group $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)$: $\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]^{W}$ is a polynomial algebra and $\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ is graded free of rank $|W|$ over it; minimal homogeneous invariant generators of $I$ form an $S$-regular sequence with common zero set $\{0\}$; and $\operatorname{Hilb}(S/I,t)=\prod_i(1+\cdots+t^{d_i-1})$, $\dim_{\mathbb C}(S/I)=\prod_i d_i=|W|$ ([[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]], [[lem-reflection-basic-invariants-form-a-regular-sequence]], [[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]]).

[F4] The Reynolds operator $\mathcal R(f)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot f$ is a graded $R$-linear projection of $S$ onto $R=S^G$, and it preserves each finite-dimensional graded piece $S_N$; $I=SR_+$ and the coinvariant algebra $A=S/I$ carry their quotient gradings ([[def-finite-linear-invariant-and-coinvariant-polynomial-algebras]]).

[F5] The Hilbert function of a graded module is $H_M(N)=\dim$ of its $N$-th piece when the pieces are finite-dimensional, and its formal Hilbert series is $\sum_N H_M(N)t^N$; products and divisions below are manipulations of formal power series with constant term one ([[def-hilbert-function-and-hilbert-series]], [[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]).

[F6] An operator of finite order on a finite-dimensional complex vector space is diagonalisable ([[cor-an-element-of-finite-order-acts-diagonalisably-over-an-algebraically-closed-field-of-characteristic-zero]]). Determinants are invariant under similarity ([[cor-determinant-is-invariant-under-similarity]]), so an eigenbasis computes the determinant factors of a diagonalisable operator.

[F7] If $\Gamma$ has connected components on $S_1,\dots,S_k$, then $W\cong W_{S_1}\times\cdots\times W_{S_k}$ via multiplication, and $V=V_1\oplus\cdots\oplus V_k$ is a $B$-orthogonal direct sum on which $\rho(W_i)$ preserves $V_i$ and fixes every $V_j$, $j\ne i$ ([[lem-cg-diagram-products-and-invariant-form-comparison]] (1),(2)); for finite type each component is one of the classified finite diagrams and $W$ is finite exactly when every component is ([[thm-cg-finite-coxeter-classification-including-h-and-dihedral]] (2), [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F8] $S=\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ is the iterated polynomial ring over $\mathbb C$, so polynomials may be expanded and compared blockwise in finitely many variables, and the monomials of one block are linearly independent over the polynomial ring of the remaining blocks ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

## Proof

1.1 Put $\mathfrak m=S_+=(x_1,\dots,x_n)$. For any finite minimal homogeneous generating family $g_1,\dots,g_r$ of $I$, its classes span the graded complex vector space $I/\mathfrak m I$: modulo $\mathfrak m I$, coefficients in an expression $\sum_j a_jg_j$ reduce to their constants. These classes are independent. Indeed, a nonzero homogeneous relation would give $\sum_{\deg g_j=D}c_jg_j\in\mathfrak m I$ with some $c_k\ne0$. Since the $g_j$ generate $I$, its right side can be written $\sum_j b_jg_j$ with $b_j\in\mathfrak m$ homogeneous of degree $D-\deg g_j$. Such $b_j$ vanish when $\deg g_j\ge D$, so solving for $g_k$ expresses it in the ideal generated by the other $g_j$, contrary to minimality. Every relation splits into homogeneous ones, proving independence. Thus the number of degree-$D$ members in any such family is $\dim_{\mathbb C}(I/\mathfrak m I)_D$. Applying this to the fixed invariant family and to an arbitrary other minimal homogeneous family gives equality of degree multisets and family sizes. This proves (1) without assuming that the other generators are invariant; for $I=0$ the minimal family and quotient are empty. [F1, F5, F8]

1.2 For the fixed invariant basic family, [F1] gives the graded isomorphism $\mathbb C[y_1,\dots,y_n]\to R$ with $y_i\mapsto f_i$ and $\deg y_i=d_i$. Counting its monomials gives $\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\sum_{N\ge0}\#\{a\in\mathbb N^n:\sum_i a_id_i=N\}t^N=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}$. Positive weights make every coefficient finite, so the product is a formal-power-series identity. This algebra isomorphism is asserted only for the invariant basic family. [F1, F5, F8]

2.1 Since $I$ is homogeneous, $R=\bigoplus R_N$ and $A=\bigoplus A_N$ are graded with finite-dimensional pieces, so the Hilbert series of [F5] apply. By step 1.2, $\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}$. Under the stated AC the group $\rho_{\mathbb C}(W)$ is a finite complex reflection group of order $|W|$ on the $n$-dimensional space $V_{\mathbb C}$ with invariant algebra $R$ and positive-degree ideal $I$, so [F3] gives $\operatorname{Hilb}(A,t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+t+\cdots+t^{d_i-1})$, and evaluating at $t=1$ gives $\dim_{\mathbb C}A=\prod_i d_i=|W|$; since each factor $1+t+\cdots+t^{d_i-1}$ is a polynomial of degree $d_i-1$, the product is a polynomial of degree $\sum_i e_i$ with leading coefficient $1$, so $\sum_ie_i$ is the top degree of $A$. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 Fix $w\in W$. By [F2] and [F6] the operator induced by $w$ on the finite-dimensional space $V_{\mathbb C}^*$ is diagonalisable; let $\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_n$ be its eigenvalues with multiplicity, and note that $\det(1-tw\mid V_{\mathbb C}^*)=\prod_{j=1}^n(1-\lambda_jt)$. The induced operator on $S_N=\operatorname{Sym}^N(V_{\mathbb C}^*)$ ($N\ge0$) has, in the monomial basis attached to an eigenbasis of $V_{\mathbb C}^*$, the eigenvalues $\lambda^a=\lambda_1^{a_1}\cdots\lambda_n^{a_n}$ over all $a$ with $|a|=N$; summing gives the formal identity $\sum_{N\ge0}\operatorname{tr}(w\mid S_N)t^N=\prod_{j=1}^{n}(1-\lambda_jt)^{-1}=\det(1-tw\mid V_{\mathbb C}^*)^{-1}$ [F5]. The Reynolds operator is a projection of $S_N$ onto $R_N$ [F4], and for a projection the trace equals the dimension of its image, so $\dim_{\mathbb C}R_N=|W|^{-1}\sum_{w\in W}\operatorname{tr}(w\mid S_N)$; summing over $N$ and using the previous identity gives $\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=|W|^{-1}\sum_{w\in W}\det(1-tw\mid V_{\mathbb C}^*)^{-1}$, and combining with $\operatorname{Hilb}(R,t)=\prod_i(1-t^{d_i})^{-1}$ of 2.1 proves the Molien identity (4). [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 For $n=0$ the group is trivial, $S=R=\mathbb C$, $I=0$, $A=\mathbb C$, and every displayed product is empty and equals $1$, so all four clauses hold in this empty form [F1, F5]. Let now $\Gamma$ have finitely many connected components and suppose each is of finite type; by [F7] $W\cong W_1\times\cdots\times W_k$ and $V_{\mathbb C}=V_{1,\mathbb C}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k,\mathbb C}$ with $\rho(W_i)$ preserving $V_i$ and fixing the other summands. Choose coordinates adapted to this decomposition, so that $S=\mathbb C[x^{(1)},\dots,x^{(k)}]$ with $x^{(i)}$ the coordinates of the $i$-th summand [F8]. For $f\in R=S^W$ and any $i$, the Reynolds operator $\mathcal R_i$ of $W_i$ acts only on the block $x^{(i)}$ and, because $f$ is $W_i$-invariant, leaves it unchanged; expanding $f$ in the monomials of the remaining blocks and applying $\mathcal R_i$ expresses $f$ as a finite sum of products of a $W_i$-invariant polynomial in the block $x^{(i)}$ with a polynomial in the other blocks. Applying $\mathcal R_k,\mathcal R_{k-1},\dots,\mathcal R_1$ successively (each application leaves $f$ unchanged and replaces one block factor by its invariant part) gives $f\in\mathbb C[x^{(1)}]^{W_1}\cdots\mathbb C[x^{(k)}]^{W_k}$: the invariant algebra is generated by the component invariant algebras [F4, F8]. If for each $i$ we fix a basic family of the component $W_i$, then the concatenated family is homogeneous of positive degrees, generates $R$, and is algebraically independent: generation follows from the preceding display, and a polynomial relation among the concatenated family, expanded in the block monomials and using the algebraic independence of each component's family, forces every coefficient polynomial to vanish [F1, F8]. The concatenation generates $I=SR_+$ because its members generate $R$ and have positive degrees. It is also minimal as an $S$-ideal generating family: any redundancy, after Reynolds averaging its coefficients [F4], would express one member as an $R$-linear combination of the others. Substituting their polynomial expressions in the algebraically independent family and setting all the other variables to zero would give the impossible identity $y_j=0$ in $\mathbb C[y_j]$. Thus the concatenation is a basic family of the reducible system, and its degree multiset is the concatenation of the components' multisets; by the multiset independence of 1.1 this is the degree multiset of every basic family of the reducible system. All statements above are under the stated AC, which enters only through the existence of the $n$-element basic families and the AC-scoped suppliers [F1, F3]; the series comparison, the trace computation and the componentwise argument are finite and choice-free. [F1, F3, F4, F7, F8, step 1.1] ∎
