## Statement

Let $0\ne f\in F[x]$ have degree $n$. There is a splitting field $E/F$, roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_k$ of $f$ in $E$, and positive integers $d_1,\ldots,d_k$ such that $E$ is spanned over $F$ by the root monomials
$$ \alpha_1^{e_1}\cdots\alpha_k^{e_k}\qquad(0\le e_j<d_j), $$
and $d_1\cdots d_k\le n!$. Thus $E$ has a spanning family of at most $n!$ explicit root monomials. When $n=0$, $k=0$, the sole empty monomial is $1$, and $0!=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$ and a nonzero polynomial $f$ of degree $n$.

[F1] Strong induction permits proving a statement at degree $n$ from all smaller degrees ([[thm-strong-induction]]).

[F2] For $n\ge1$, one may adjoin a root $\alpha$ and write $f=(x-\alpha)g$ with $\deg g=n-1$ ([[lem-adjoining-one-root-reduces-the-unsplit-degree]]).

[F3] The minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ divides every polynomial vanishing at $\alpha$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[F4] If that minimal polynomial has degree $d$, then $F(\alpha)$ has power basis $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$ ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]]).

[F5] The factorial satisfies $0!=1$ and $n!=n(n-1)!$ for $n\ge1$ ([[def-factorial-and-falling-factorial]]).

[F6] A splitting field is generated by the roots over the base field ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]).

[F7] For nonzero polynomials over an integral domain, the degree of a product is the sum of the degrees ([[thm-polynomial-degree-of-a-product-over-a-domain]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** strong induction on degree.

1.1 Let $P(n)$ be the full assertion in the Statement, quantified over all base fields. For $n=0$, take $E=F$ and $k=0$. The vector $1$ spans $F$ and the number of displayed empty monomials is $1=0!$. [F5, F6, base]

1.2 Let $n\ge1$ and assume $P(m)$ for every $m<n$. By [F2], choose a root $\alpha$ in the extension $K=F(\alpha)$ and write $f=(x-\alpha)g$ with $\deg g=n-1$. If the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ has degree $d$, then $d\ge1$, [F3] and [F7] give $d\le n$, and [F4] gives the $F$-basis $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$. [F2, F3, F4, F7, ih]

2.1 Apply the induction hypothesis over $K$ to $g$. It gives a splitting field $E/K$ spanned over $K$ by at most $(n-1)!$ monomials in roots $\alpha_2,\ldots,\alpha_k$ of $g$. Multiplying those monomials by $1,\alpha,\ldots,\alpha^{d-1}$ spans $E$ over $F$ by monomials in roots of $f$. [F4, F6, step 1.2, ih]

3.1 The number of resulting monomials is at most $d(n-1)!\le n(n-1)!=n!$ by $d\le n$ and [F5]. Moreover $E=K(\text{roots of }g)=F(\alpha,\text{roots of }g)$, so it is a splitting field of $f$. [F5, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 The base case and inductive step establish $P(n)$ for every natural $n$ by [F1]. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, discharge-induction] ∎
