## Statement

For every closed connected oriented rational homology three-sphere $Y$ and Spin$^c$ structure $\mathfrak t$, the absolutely graded infinity group $HF^\infty(Y,\mathfrak t;\mathbb F_2)$ is a single free Laurent tower. If $W:Y_0\to Y_1$ is a smooth connected rational homology cobordism between rational homology spheres, then for each fixed Spin$^c$ structure $\mathfrak s$ on $W$ the infinity map is a degree-zero isomorphism. The corresponding plus map commutes with the infinity-to-plus maps. This conclusion concerns each individual extending Spin$^c$ structure, including nonprimitive handle attachments and torsion labels.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F2] The finite integral-homology-sphere sequence of preferred $\pm1$ knot surgeries is supplied by [[lem-integral-homology-spheres-are-connected-by-unit-knot-surgeries]]. No stronger consequence of the Kirby interface is assumed.

[F3] The disk/polygon good-end counts and strict domain bounds are supplied by [[lem-os-disk-and-polygon-analytic-foundations]], [[thm-strictly-admissible-triple-free-polygons-have-full-marked-low-dimensional-counts]] and [[lem-strong-polygon-admissibility-bounds-indices-and-positive-domains]]. **External input for the generic closed-manifold relative theory:** Ozsváth–Szabó, *Holomorphic disks and topological invariants for closed three-manifolds*, arXiv:math/0101206v4, Theorem11.1, printed/PDF p.115, supplies fixed-Spin$^c$ Heegaard-move invariance and the commuting minus/infinity/plus sequence for strongly admissible singly pointed closed-manifold diagrams. Use its chain constructions over $\mathbb F_2$ (§4.1, p.36), with the chosen admissible generic almost-complex data. The relative grading has the periodic ambiguity $d(\mathfrak s)$ of equation(12), p.37; it is integral in torsion summands and only cyclic in a non-torsion summand. Thus the generic zero-surgery and auxiliary positive-first-Betti-number diagrams below use this published closed-manifold framework, at their actual Spin$^c$ labels, rather than an $S^3$-knot move theorem. The hat invariance interface is also supplied by [[thm-hat-heegaard-floer-is-a-closed-three-manifold-invariant]]. This imported relative framework does not supply infinity standardness or an individual cobordism-map isomorphism; those remain the local constructions below. The winding, truncation comparison and individual torsion-label algebra below are additional constructions.

## Proof

**Given:** The stated rational homology spheres and, for the second assertion, a rational homology cobordism with one fixed Spin$^c$ structure. Put $R=\mathbb F_2[U,U^{-1}]$ and $K=\mathbb F_2((U))$. Rank means Laurent rank, equivalently dimension after the faithful field extension to $K$. In a nonprimitive rank-lowering handle write its attaching class as $m h$, where $h$ is primitive in the free quotient; the extensions of a fixed incoming structure are indexed by $i\in\mathbb Z/m$. The individual maps are labelled before summing any counts.

1.1 **Finiteness and Laurent freeness in a torsion structure.** For a torsion Spin$^c$ structure the relative Maslov grading is integral, and the finite infinity complex is a finite free graded module over $R$, with $U$ of degree $-2$. Its homology is finitely generated and graded. The ring $R$ is a graded field: every nonzero homogeneous element is a Laurent monomial and is invertible. A homogeneous maximal independent set therefore spans, since the usual elimination can always divide by its nonzero homogeneous pivot. Thus this homology has a finite homogeneous Laurent basis. In particular rank after extending scalars to $K$ determines the number of free Laurent summands; there is no Laurent torsion in a torsion Spin$^c$ structure. This assertion is not applied to the non-torsion structures with cyclic Maslov grading used later. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 **Why an infinity triangle cannot be obtained by a naive sum.** For a fixed Spin-c cobordism the infinity triangle map has finite sums on generators. The surgery exact triangle instead sums over Spin-c structures. Its torus factors have basepoint multiplicities r(r+1)/2 for unbounded r. In CF⁺ only finitely many survive on any input; in CF∞ they need not. Accordingly a literal sum of all such maps into the Laurent-polynomial complex is not justified. Nor can CF⁺ be localized in U to recover CF∞: every plus-chain element is U-torsion, so that localization is zero. The correct finite-rank replacement uses K=F₂((U)), the field of formal Laurent series bounded below in the exponent. A finite free Laurent complex can be tensored with K. A family of disk or polygon terms with n_z tending to infinity then converges coefficientwise in K. At each bounded n_z the admissibility estimate supplies finitely many terms. This construction therefore permits the all-Spin-c surgery sums without falsely declaring them Laurent-polynomial maps. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3]

1.3 **The completed triangle from an actual torus triad.** Represent a knot exterior by a Heegaard surface with one missing meridian. Choose its three filling slopes β_g,γ_g,δ_g with pairwise intersection one and γ_g the smoothing of the oriented juxtaposition β_g+δ_g. The other β,γ,δ curves are small Hamiltonian translates. Shrink the γ smoothing towards that juxtaposition. The outgoing intersection generators then split into those nearest the β generators and those nearest the δ generators. This gives the vector-space splitting B=A⊕C, with inclusion ι, projection π, and section R of π. It is not initially a splitting of chain complexes. Make every periodic domain have signed area zero, using winding and an area form on the complement; choose the two local triangles of its torus periodic domain to have equal area. Let M>0 be the smallest area of the old complementary regions. Choose the smoothing and Hamiltonian translates so their total unsigned area ε is less than M/8. For each intersection generator choose a reference triangle from a fixed generator and define its energy level by minus that triangle's area. Periodic ambiguities have zero area. Changing its U exponent by n changes the level by minus n times the surface area. Thus the energy filtration is the U-adic filtration up to finitely many generator-dependent constants. The triangle maps f:A→B and g:B→C have leading terms ι and π on Im R. These terms are the unique small triangles in the smoothing bands, with n_z=0 and count one. Any other nonnegative triangle uses an old complementary region and has area at least M−ε. Consequently the error terms lower the filtration by at least M−2ε. The differential also strictly lowers it, since a nonconstant holomorphic disk has positive area. The composite gf has a rectangle nullhomotopy H. In the auxiliary torus there are two triangle families, one for each sign of the odd characteristic value, with the same n_z=r(r+1)/2 and each counted once. They cancel over F₂. The accepted rectangle-end/gluing identity then gives gf = dH+Hd. The rectangle used by H must use an old complementary region; there is no small-band rectangle with these four prescribed corners. Comparing its area with the two reference small triangles gives RH lower than ι by at least M−4ε. Thus all the following corrections strictly lower the energy filtration and converge in K: R′ = R Σ_(n≥0) (1−gR)^n, f′ = f − d(R′H) − (R′H)d. The series telescopes to gR′=1. Since g is a chain map, gf′=gf−dH−Hd=0, and f′ is a chain map. Its leading term is still ι. Let L be projection onto the A part of B=A⊕C. Then Lf′=1+N with N strictly filtration decreasing, so ΣN^n is its inverse. Hence f′ is injective. To prove exactness at B, write a cycle-free element b∈ker g as ιa+Rc. The equation gb=0 determines c from a because gR is invertible. This identifies ker g with the graph of a map A→C. The projection L therefore identifies ker g with A, and Lf′ is invertible, so Im f′=ker g. The resulting short exact sequence of completed chain complexes gives the surgery long exact sequence over K. This proves exactness by convergent Gaussian elimination, not by localizing the plus sequence. The construction keeps Spin-c labels on the knot exterior. The maps are the sums over their extending structures, with those exterior restrictions. The all-structure sums are essential here; the degree formula still applies to each fixed-structure summand, not to their inhomogeneous sum. [step 1.2, F1, F2, F3]

1.4 **A useful rank argument which does not assume scalar automorphisms.** Suppose a wound zero-surgery complex has been constructed as Cone(δ_in+δ_out:R→L), and that both δ_in and δ_out induce isomorphisms on infinity homology. These are the precise geometric inputs of the next paragraph, not conclusions of the completed triangle itself. Let r be the K-dimension of HF∞ of the integral homology sphere before surgery. In the torsion Spin-c summand of zero surgery the two maps have equal degree, so after one is inverted their sum is 1+A on an r-dimensional vector space, with A an automorphism. Put h=dim ker(1+A). Its cone has dimension 2h. This uses equality of kernel and cokernel dimensions of a square matrix; it does not assume A is a scalar or already the identity. In a non-torsion Spin-c summand the two maps differ in relative degree by 2n, n≠0. After homogeneous Laurent bases are chosen, the same cone is 1+U^|n|B, or an invertible Laurent multiple of it, with B a constant invertible matrix. It is invertible over K by its convergent geometric series. These summands therefore contribute no completed homology. Cyclic exactness by itself does not determine the map ranks, and hence does not supply an inequality comparing the two integral-sphere ranks. The additional actual triangle map must be retained: it vanishes on L and factors through the already constructed quotient quasi-isomorphism R→CF∞(Y). Its mapping cone cancels that R/CF∞(Y) pair and leaves L. Thus the completed surgery triangle gives rank preservation under the individual ±1 surgery. This use needs the quotient-map factorization; the dimension 2h of the zero-surgery group alone would not suffice. [step 1.3, F1, F2, F3]

1.5 **The winding-domain construction.** The geometric input is the following winding model. In a diagram for zero surgery wind one meridian curve N times around a transverse curve γ. For N large all generators of a fixed Spin-c summand occur in the winding strip, in paired left/right positions. A periodic domain has a coefficient at most C−N/2 on one strip side and at least −C+N/2 outside it. Hence an index-one nonnegative domain from right to left is either the inward class or that class plus one periodic domain with its index-correcting sphere term. Any further positive or negative multiple has a negative coefficient at one of those two places. There are no left-to-right index-one positive domains. This supplies the actual subcomplex L, quotient R, and two candidate connecting pieces. The inward piece is leading-term invertible: its thin paired bigon has count one and gives a bijection of the generator pairs after a U shift. Every other inward term uses an old region. Shrink the strip so N times its area is less than the smallest old-region area. The error then strictly lowers the energy level, and the geometric-series inverse is legitimate on each fixed relative degree, which contains finitely many generators. Likewise the small triangles identify R with the pre-surgery infinity complex, with the same leading-term argument. The inward leading term alone does not prove the outward statement. The following supplies the algebraic part of a more precise way to do that comparison and isolates its geometric hypothesis. [step 1.4, F1, F2, F3]

1.6 **A truncation-index argument for the outward map.** Suppose the basepoint/winding comparison has identified, for all sufficiently large positive integers n, the same two plus complexes R⁺,L⁺ and their connecting maps as δ_n = B + U^n A. Here A induces the inward infinity isomorphism, B is the outward map, and the zero-surgery Spin-c labels tend out of every finite set. The crucial geometric requirement is that the plus truncations are the **same** under these identifications: an infinity identification with n-dependent generator truncation levels is insufficient. This requirement is explicit rather than hidden in an infinity-only basepoint move. Then B induces an infinity isomorphism. Here is a full algebraic proof. R⁻ and L⁻ have finite free chain modules over F₂[U]. Their relative integral gradings force every homogeneous torsion summand of homology to be killed by a power of U. It follows from the negative/infinity/plus long exact sequence that H(R⁺) and H(L⁺) each consist of r standard plus towers and a finite-dimensional remainder, where r is their common infinity rank. The finite remainders and bottom shifts are independent of n by the stated truncation hypothesis. Infinity homology is free: a nonzero homogeneous Laurent-polynomial annihilator would have to be a monomial, hence a unit. Choose homogeneous Laurent bases, shifting their U powers so each parity has one standard degree. A and B are then constant matrices between the corresponding parity blocks, with A invertible; a fixed initial shift of n or a fixed monomial determinant factor has no effect on the argument. Write det(B+tA)=t^k q(t), q(0)≠0. This is a nonzero polynomial because A is invertible. If B is singular, k≥1. Substituting t=U^n gives determinant valuation nk, up to a fixed additive shift. For a matrix on plus towers, its finite-dimensional kernel-minus-cokernel index is this determinant valuation. To prove this, dualize a tower to F₂[[U]] and put the matrix into Smith normal form over that discrete valuation ring. Multiplication by U^a has a-dimensional kernel and zero cokernel on the plus tower; multiplication by a series with nonzero constant term is invertible there, by the locally finite geometric series. Adding the fixed finite remainders changes the index by the constant dim(R_reduced)−dim(L_reduced). Thus the index of δ_n is C+nk for a constant C independent of n. The zero-surgery plus homology vanishes for all sufficiently large labels. Indeed the accepted finite hat complex represents only finitely many Spin-c structures. Outside them the hat group is zero, so the exact hat/plus sequence makes U an isomorphism on HF⁺. Every individual plus homology class is killed by some power of U, so this isomorphism forces HF⁺=0. The short exact sequence L⁺→CF⁺→R⁺ therefore makes δ_n an isomorphism on homology for all large n, and its index is zero. The identity C+nk=0 for arbitrarily large n rules out k≥1. Hence B is nonsingular. Its homogeneous Laurent matrix has unit determinant, proving the claimed infinity isomorphism over the Laurent ring, not only after completion. [step 1.5, F1, F2, F3]

2.1 **Separate an infinity gauge from a plus identification.** Fix one equivalence class of γ-induced generators in a sufficiently wound zero-surgery diagram. Write its paired generators as x⁺_a,x⁻_a for a in a finite set S. Each of their α₁ coordinates lies on one of two strands of the winding finger. Only finitely many consecutive turns contain such corners: their turn numbers have the form k+aₓ with fixed offsets aₓ. The difference of two turn numbers is the γ component of their relative Spin-c difference, so these offsets do not grow with further winding. Cut α₁ at its central crossing p with the transverse meridian. Normalize the inward disk classes to cross this cut once, and outward classes to cross it zero times. The winding positivity estimate in the preceding construction gives these two possibilities for a positive index-one disk from the right strand to the left strand, and gives no reverse disks. For disks within either strand, the cut crossing is zero. Nonzero extra periodic multiples are excluded by the two winding coefficients of opposite signs, after the index-correcting sphere term is included. For a path of basepoints, the change of n_z for a disk is the signed intersection of its boundary with that path. On the graph of same-strand and outward disks this change is a coboundary. Indeed, summing along a closed graph path gives a periodic domain with zero crossing at p. Its coefficient on the winding periodic generator is therefore zero. The remaining doubly-periodic domains have no boundary on this part of the winding finger, and a sphere has multiplicity one at both endpoints of the basepoint path. Their changes of n_z are zero. Thus the sum around every graph cycle is zero. Choose one vertex in each connected component of that graph. Integrating the changes of n_z along graph paths gives integer potentials h(x⁺_a) and h(x⁻_a), independent of path by the preceding cycle calculation. Multiplication of the corresponding infinity generator by U^{h(x)} therefore identifies the same-strand differentials and outward maps at the two basepoints. These are invertible Laurent changes of basis. **They are used only in the infinity complexes.** No claim that they preserve the original plus cutoffs is made. This permits the initial basepoint to be moved to the cut-p side of the finitely many corner-containing turns. The target outward infinity map is conjugated by invertible Laurent changes of basis, so proving the outward map an isomorphism after this move proves it before the move too. [step 1.6, F1, F2, F3]

2.2 **A corridor between the cut and the corner block.** Choose the corridor on the cut-p side of the whole corner block. Along α₁, every corridor segment can therefore be followed back to p without passing a corner of any selected generator. Take the total winding N large enough that two desired levels M₁ and M₂ there lie far from the end of the winding finger and the two-coefficient positivity bound applies throughout this finite corridor. Equivalently choose N first larger than four times the largest corner offset and the largest Chern evaluation used in the two comparisons, with the fixed outside-domain bound included. The precise factor four is inessential; both signs of the periodic coefficient must dominate that fixed bound. The corridor is chosen in a thin transverse slice of the winding finger missing β. Cross one turn at a time through its corner-free pieces of α₁. The coefficient of a disk boundary on such a piece is constant until a corner is crossed. For a same-strand or outward domain that constant is zero: follow it towards the cut p, where it is zero by its normalization; the chosen route crosses no corner. For an inward domain the constant is one, by the same argument starting from its one crossing at p. Therefore the disk multiplicities change by Δn_z = 0  on same-strand and outward domains, Δn_z = 1  on inward domains. The assertion concerns the entire domain, not just its small leading bigon. An extra periodic domain would change its crossing at p, and has already been excluded by positivity and the index condition. Thus it cannot introduce an additional boundary coefficient in this corridor. Keep the attaching curves fixed during this finite basepoint comparison. The unweighted holomorphic disks, their Maslov indices, and their counts then stay the same. Choose the regular almost-complex datum to have the standard product form near the whole finite corridor; regularity and counts are supplied by the accepted analytic interface. The basepoint changes only the exponent n_z and the Spin-c name of this fixed equivalence class. The Spin-c names differ by PD[γ] at each turn, since the basepoint path and the two flow-line arcs sweep one γ cycle. In particular, identify [x⁺_a,j] at one corridor point with [x⁺_a,j] at the next, and likewise for x⁻_a. The integers j are **not changed**. The same-side differentials agree exactly, and each plus cutoff remains j≥0 for each of these same generators. This supplies the uniform plus identification which an arbitrary infinity gauge does not supply. After fixing one origin for the corridor parameter, the connecting maps are consequently δ_M = B + U^M A, on the same two plus complexes, with the same bottom cutoffs. B is the outward map, A the inward map with its fixed initial exponent removed. The two pieces are chain maps: splitting d²=0 by the crossing count at p separates the inward and outward terms. Their infinity versions have the same property. The inward infinity map A is the already constructed leading-bigon isomorphism. Only two sufficiently distant corridor points are needed; an assertion that the winding number N can be fixed while M tends to infinity is unnecessary. First choose two Spin-c labels outside the finite support of the accepted hat invariant, and then choose N large enough to contain their corridor. Independence of the hat invariant from the diagram ensures the zero-surgery plus group at both labels vanishes, by the locally U-nilpotent argument in the preceding phase. The associated connecting maps δ_{M₁},δ_{M₂} are therefore both isomorphisms on homology. [step 2.1, F1, F2, F3]

2.3 **Finish the outward isomorphism.** Apply the determinant/truncation-index calculation in the preceding truncation-index calculation. For the same two complexes its finite correction C is constant. If B were singular, writing det(B+tA)=t^k q(t), q(0)≠0, would give k≥1 and the two identities 0=C+M₁k,    0=C+M₂k. Subtracting them gives (M₂−M₁)k=0, a contradiction. Hence B induces an infinity isomorphism. Conjugate back by the explicitly separated Laurent gauge of the first section to recover the original outward map. No plus cutoff was transported by that first gauge, and no varying finite correction was used in the determinant argument. [step 2.2, F1, F2, F3]

3.1 **The actual finite label set.** The relative handle cochain group H²(W,Y;Z) is Z. The preceding map H¹(Y;Z)→H²(W,Y;Z) evaluates a cohomology class on K; its image is mZ. Exactness therefore identifies the kernel of H²(W;Z)→H²(Y;Z) with Z/m. Consequently the extensions of t are an affine Z/m-set. Choose one extension s₀ to fix its origin, and write the others as s_i, i mod m. This is the full extending structure, not just its outgoing restriction. In the wound triple choose a transverse cut p in the attaching curve. Trivialize the triangle's oriented two-plane data on the boundary pieces away from this cut. Passing an α-boundary arc once across p changes the relative two-handle obstruction by one: the normal framing on its core turns once in the corresponding relative two-cell. Changing the trivialization by an incoming gauge class changes this integer by its evaluation on K, hence by a multiple of m. Thus i(ψ) = #(∂_αψ crossing p) mod m is precisely the affine difference s_z(ψ)−s₀. This derives the residue from the relative obstruction calculation, rather than assigning labels after counting. In particular a triangle with residue j≠i is never a term of the fixed map F_(W,s_i), even when the two structures have the same outgoing restriction. [step 2.3, F1, F2, F3]

3.2 **The m winding branches.** Split the winding generators by that residual crossing number. They give right and left complexes R_i,L_i, i mod m. The inward family crosses p once and the outward family does not. Hence their connecting maps have the pattern a_i:R_i→L_(i+1),       b_i:R_i→L_i. All indices are cyclic. This pattern retains the integer boundary count before reducing modulo m; it is not an average of the m sectors. The inward and outward constructions in the preceding phases show each a_i and b_i is an infinity isomorphism. For the outward statement apply the uniform plus argument to the finite direct sum of all branches: the inward leading map is a cyclic permutation of the thin-bigon isomorphisms, while the outward map is block diagonal. The determinant argument makes this whole block diagonal map invertible, and therefore makes each b_i invertible. No individual b_i is inferred merely from a nonzero sum. For the chosen two-handle triple, its fixed map on sector i factors as C = Cone(⊕R_i → ⊕L_i) → R_i → CF∞(Y(K),s_i|_(Y(K))). The first arrow is the right-part projection in sector i. The second arrow q_i counts the actual meridional small triangles. In a planar lift, each right generator has one nearest output generator and one nonnegative small triangle with those three corners. It has µ=0 and count one, with its actual U exponent n_z. Its boundary crossing is i, so it represents s_i. The same obstruction calculation excludes every other residue from this fixed map. For a left generator the prescribed triangle has a negative coefficient at the protected winding corner; there are no counted left terms. These two assertions follow by following the α-boundary through the two winding strands: the right arc closes at the meridian without crossing the zero sheet, whereas the left arc would have to subtract that sheet. Every non-nearest right triangle uses an old complementary region. Choose the total area of the winding bands less than half the smallest such region's area. Then q_i is its nearest-generator bijection, with the indicated U shifts, plus strictly lower energy terms. On each fixed relative degree there are finitely many generators, so its triangular inverse is a finite series there. This proves q_i is an infinity quasi-isomorphism, individually for the retained Spin-c structure s_i. This is the labeled small-domain statement needed here; the all-sector sum of the q_i is not used in its proof. [step 3.1, F1, F2, F3]

3.3 **Why every labeled map is nonzero when the standard ranks are available.** Pass to infinity homology, and denote the maps a_i,b_i by the same letters. The connecting map δ is the cyclic block map (δr)_i = b_i r_i + a_(i−1) r_(i−1). If r∈ker δ, then all its coordinates are determined by any one: r_i = −b_i⁻¹ a_(i−1) r_(i−1). Since these transport maps are isomorphisms, a vector in ker δ with one zero coordinate is zero in every coordinate. Thus each projection ker δ→H(R_i) is injective. This is a statement for **each** residue, including m even; no division by m or action by m times a primitive homology class is involved. Let the common rank of H(R_i) be r, and assume the incoming infinity homology has rank 2r. These are exactly the ranks supplied by standard infinity modules when the first Betti number drops by one; they remain a prerequisite of this last paragraph. The cone exact sequence gives rank H(C) = rank ker δ + rank coker δ = 2 rank ker δ, because δ is a square map between two free modules of the same rank. Therefore rank ker δ=r. The projections above are consequently isomorphisms: in homogeneous Laurent bases, an injective full-rank degree-preserving map has constant invertible matrix after the U shifts are removed, so its determinant is a Laurent unit. Equivalently this can first be seen over the fraction field, then the same homogeneous grading excludes a nonunit determinant. The cone exact sequence maps H(C) onto ker δ. Composing with its i-th projection and q_i sends a class with any prescribed nonzero coordinate to that nonzero output in the **fixed** map F_(W,s_i). The kernel is the common image of ⊕H(L_i). Thus every extending s_i has the same-rank nonzero quotient map, independently of what happens in the other Spin-c sectors. For r=1 its nonzero coefficient is one times a Laurent unit, not an unspecified nonzero scalar obtained from a summed map. [step 3.2, F1, F2, F3]

4.1 **The Euler rank in each rational-sphere Spin-c summand.** Here is a finite diagram calculation which supplies the needed nonzero Euler rank, without assuming infinity standardness. A genus-g Heegaard diagram gives a balanced presentation of π₁(Y): the one-handle generators are a₁,…,a_g, and the other attaching curves give relators r₁,…,r_g. Let H=H₁(Y;Z), which is finite, and evaluate its Fox Jacobian in Z[H]. Each entry is the signed sum of crossings of that attaching curve with the corresponding meridian disk; its group element is the prefix of the attaching word at that crossing. In the determinant expansion, the choices of one crossing in every row and column are exactly the intersection generators of the two product tori. The sign is their local intersection sign. The product of the prefixes labels their affine H-valued Spin-c class: changing one crossing to another traces the α and β arcs of their difference cycle, exactly the generator-assignment rule. A change of the reference generator translates every label by one common element of H. For any nontrivial complex character χ of H, Fox's identity gives Σ_j χ(∂r_i/∂a_j)(χ(a_j)−1)=χ(r_i)−1=0. At least one χ(a_j)−1 is nonzero. Hence the evaluated Jacobian has a nonzero null vector and determinant zero. At the trivial character it is the ordinary abelian relation matrix, whose determinant is ±|H|. Fourier inversion on the finite abelian group H therefore gives det J = ±Σ_(h∈H) h. Both sides have integral coefficients, so equality over C[H] proves the integral equality. Choose the common intersection orientation to take the positive sign. The signed generator count is thus one in each Spin-c summand. The differential is odd, so its Euler rank over R, or over K, is also one: rank HF∞_even(Y,t) − rank HF∞_odd(Y,t) = 1. Here even/odd means the intersection-sign parity with this positive normalization, not reduction of a rational grading to an integer without choosing its fractional origin. [step 3.3, F1, F2, F3]

4.2 **Integral homology spheres: the exact map in the cone cancellation.** Let Y₁ be ±1 surgery on a knot in an integral homology sphere Y, and let Y₀ be its zero surgery. The winding construction gives the torsion sector of CF∞(Y₀) as a cone C of R₀→L₀, with R₀ quasi-isomorphic to CF∞(Y), and with L₀ quasi-isomorphic to R₀ by the inward winding map. Retain the actual triangle map C→CF∞(Y): there are no counted triangles from L₀, while on R₀ it is the already constructed small-triangle quasi-isomorphism q. The other non-torsion Spin-c sectors of Y₀ are acyclic after tensoring with K. Indeed their inward/outward maps are isomorphisms and differ in relative degree by 2n≠0; their cone matrix becomes 1+U^|n|A with constant invertible A, which is invertible over K. The completed surgery triangle identifies CF∞(Y₁)⊗K with the mapping cone of this C→CF∞(Y) map, up to the irrelevant shift. In that mapping cone, L₀ is a subcomplex and the quotient is Cone(q), which is acyclic. The long exact sequence therefore identifies its homology with H(L₀). Consequently infinity rank is preserved under this individual ±1 surgery. This uses the actual quotient map q; dimensions in an unlabelled cyclic exact sequence would not determine this conclusion. [step 4.1, F1, F2, F3]

4.3 **The integral-sphere induction base and its order.** The standard genus-zero diagram gives $HF^\infty(S^3)=R$ in even parity. Apply [[lem-integral-homology-spheres-are-connected-by-unit-knot-surgeries]] to choose a finite sequence of preferred-framing $+1$ or $-1$ knot surgeries from $S^3$ to the given integral homology sphere, every intermediate manifold an integral homology sphere. The preceding actual cone cancellation preserves infinity rank at each successive surgery. The Euler calculation in this phase gives signed rank one, so the resulting rank-one Laurent module is in even parity. This establishes the integral-sphere case before the rational order induction, and does not extract a unit-surgery sequence from Kirby classification alone. [step 4.2, F1, F2, F3]

4.4 **Rational induction: a decreasing, specified complexity.** Assume the integral-sphere base has been obtained. Induct on the positive integer |H₁(Y;Z)|, simultaneously for every Spin-c structure of every rational homology sphere with smaller order. Choose a knot representing a nonzero homology class in Y. Its exterior Z has a primitive rational longitude ℓ on its boundary torus, and the meridian m has a=m·ℓ>0 after orientation choices. Write H₁(Z)=Z⊕T. For a nonlongitudinal slope h, the filling kills a class with nonzero free coordinate, and |H₁(Y_h)| = c |h·ℓ| for one positive integer c independent of h: choose a basis of the torus with ℓ as one vector, and compute the order of (Z⊕T)/<h>. Thus a smaller positive value of h·ℓ gives strictly smaller order. If a>1, use the Euclidean algorithm to write m=h₁+h₂, with primitive h₁,h₂, h₁·h₂=1, and 0<h_i·ℓ<a. Explicitly choose a vector v with m·v=1 and replace it by v+km until its longitude coordinate lies between 0 and a; the other vector is m−v, with orientation adjusted to the stated intersection convention. The boundary case in which that coordinate is zero is moved to the neighbouring Euclidean pair. The two fillings have smaller homology order and their infinity modules are standard by induction. In the surgery triad on these slopes the map between the two smaller fillings is odd for the intersection-sign parities. This is a local orientation calculation: take h₁=(1,0), h₂=(0,1) in an oriented boundary basis, so the longitude lies in the quadrant making both h_i·ℓ positive. The three filling curves are the horizontal, vertical and their oriented smoothed sum. The canonical small corner changes the ordered tangent orientation once; the auxiliary Hamiltonian-translate factors do not. Normalizing the determinant signs of the two relation matrices positively therefore makes this map odd. The neighbouring maps in the completed triangle have the complementary parities. Since both smaller modules are supported in even parity, exactness forces every odd-parity group of Y_m to vanish. Its Euler rank is one in each individual Spin-c summand by the Fox computation, so each such summand has rank one. This step uses neither a nonzero summed map nor a rank hypothesis for Y_m. [step 4.3, F1, F2, F3]

4.5 **The divisibility case a=1.** Here the longitudinal filling Y_ℓ has b₁=1. Let d be the order of the image of ℓ in T, equivalently the integral divisibility of the rational longitude. Since the original knot is homologically nontrivial in Y_m, d>1: its core is represented by ℓ, and a multiple of ℓ can equal a multiple of m in H₁(Z) only with zero multiple of m, by its nonzero free coordinate. Thus |Tors H₁(Y_ℓ)| = |T|/d < |H₁(Y_m)|. Choose a knot J in Y_ℓ representing a primitive free generator. In its exterior the meridian is zero in H₁: the boundary map from H₂(Y_ℓ) evaluates once on J. Filling to kill that free generator therefore gives a rational homology sphere Y′ of order |Tors H₁(Y_ℓ)|. All its Spin-c infinity modules are standard by the strict induction hypothesis. Fix the target structure t on Y_m. Extend its restriction on Z over the longitudinal filling, and choose a non-torsion extension s_n with positive divisibility 2n. Such extensions exist in an unbounded arithmetic progression: their Chern classes differ by twice the dual of the filling core. Apply the winding decomposition first using the primitive knot J. There is one cyclic branch and its comparison module is the rank-one infinity module of Y′. Its connecting map is, up to invertible monomials, 1+U^n : R→R. The kernel is zero and the cokernel has F₂-dimension n. Hence dim_F₂ HF∞(Y_ℓ,s_n)=n. Now apply the finite-label winding construction using the original filling Y_m. Its d branches are joined by inward and outward infinity isomorphisms, so all branch modules have the same rank r, including the branch with outgoing structure t. Eliminate d−1 branches using those isomorphisms. The remaining cone is 1+U^n A : R^r→R^r, where A is a constant invertible matrix after homogeneous bases and fixed U shifts. The total exponent n is the evaluation of c₁(s_n)/2 on the primitive periodic surface: following the whole branch cycle crosses that surface once, rather than dividing the exponent by d. Its determinant has nonzero constant term and leading term U^(nr) det A. Thus the map is injective over R and its cokernel has F₂-dimension nr, by Smith normal form over F₂[U] and invariance under inverting U. Comparing with the primitive-knot calculation gives nr=n and hence r=1. In particular the desired individual t summand on Y_m is standard. The two comparisons concern the same physical (Y_ℓ,s_n), and the smaller Y′ is used only through the strict homology-order induction. The unknown rank r of Y_m is not inserted into its own proof. Full Spin-c labels are kept on the branch with t; no assertion that all restrictions are the same structure is required. [step 4.4, F1, F2, F3]

5.1 **A primitive circle action on the finite cyclic model.** Let a null-intersection two-handle kill K=m h modulo torsion, with h a primitive free homology class. Use the actual cyclic cone from `nonprimitive-fixed-spinc-triangle-operation.md`, retaining every s_i. The circle action needed below is that of h, not that of m h. Replacing one by the other over F₂ when m is even would be invalid. Represent h by an integral one-cycle transverse to the periodic surface dual to this free direction. Weight each disk by its intersection with that cycle, introducing a variable T. On a right or left branch, every periodic domain represents a surface on the outgoing side. The image of h there is torsion in this killed direction, so its intersection with each such surface is zero. The weight on same-branch disks is therefore a coboundary: integrate it from a reference generator. Multiplication of each branch generator by the corresponding power of T removes these weights from its differential. This is an infinity change of basis; no plus cutoff argument is used here. The remaining weights on the cyclic inward/outward edges have total one around the branch cycle. Indeed that full cycle is the primitive periodic surface on the incoming side, and h has intersection one with it. This statement uses the full branch cycle, not an individual attaching arc of K, which has divisibility m. A vertex change of T basis moves all weights to one selected edge: for an integer edge cochain on a circle, subtracting successive partial sums leaves zero on every edge except the last, where the value is the original total. Hence the weighted connecting matrix is the unweighted cyclic matrix with exactly one a_i replaced by T a_i. Differentiate its differential in T at T=1. On the chain level this is the circle-action count: differentiating T^w counts w modulo two times the disk count. The chain relation follows by differentiating d_T²=0. A T-dependent change of branch basis changes this action by a chain homotopy, since differentiating a conjugation adds a commutator with d. Thus the homology action A_h sends the right component across that one edge to the left component and has zero action on a class represented purely on the left. In particular Im H(L) ⊂ Ker A_h. The fixed map F_(W,s_i) factors through the right projection p_i and its individual quasi-isomorphism q_i. Therefore it kills Im H(L), and also kills Im A_h, because this action lands on the left. Conversely, if F_(W,s_i)(ξ)=0, the injectivity of every coordinate projection on ker δ in the fixed-label operation shows the entire right projection p(ξ)=0. The cone exact sequence then places ξ in Im H(L). Consequently Im A_h ⊂ Ker F_(W,s_i) ⊂ Ker A_h for every individual s_i. This argument keeps the finite Spin-c residue throughout and involves no division by m, summed map, or assumed scalar symmetry of the different torsion sectors. Suppose a submodule M of the incoming infinity group is an exterior module on b free directions, with A_h contraction in one primitive direction. Then Ker(A_h|M)=Im(A_h|M), so the two inclusions give Ker(F_(W,s_i)|M)=Ker(A_h|M). The map therefore injects M/Ker A_h into the outgoing infinity group, separately for each s_i. That image is an exterior module on b−1 directions. The remaining circle actions commute with the quotient map: apply the same weighting construction to passive cycles, whose branch weights can be kept on their old disks. Their disk/triangle broken ends give the commuting action identity. In particular the incoming top exterior generator survives and maps to the top exterior generator in this smaller image. No standardness of the *whole intermediate outgoing group* was assumed in this statement. [step 4.5, F1, F2, F3]

5.2 **An actual ordering of the rational-cobordism two-handles.** Let W be a rational homology cobordism between rational homology spheres. Choose an ordered handle decomposition with n₁ one-handles and n₃ three-handles, and no zero-/four-handles. Its relative rational cellular complex is exact, so its two-handle boundary map has rank n₁ and there are n₁+n₃ two-handles. Select n₁ linearly independent columns of that boundary matrix and put their actual attaching components first. This is a permutation of handles of the same index, not an unperformed rational linear combination of attaching knots. Each of these first handles kills a nonzero rational free homology class and lowers the boundary b₁ by one. Its integral class can be nonprimitive; that is why the individual finite-label operation was needed. After these n₁ handles the boundary Y_mid has b₁=0. The relative two-/one-handle matrix of this prefix is square and invertible over Q, so H₂ of the prefix vanishes. In particular Y_mid is a rational homology sphere. All the other attaching classes vanish in the old one-handle rational quotient. Every remaining two-handle creates a new free boundary class. To see why no later handle can instead preserve b₁ or kill an earlier new class, cap its rational Seifert surface in the partial cobordism. A nonzero rational framing would give a two-cycle of nonzero square. If it killed an earlier newly created free class, the two capped cores would have matrix [[0,ℓ],[ℓ,a]], ℓ≠0, of nonzero determinant. Either case gives a nondegenerate two-dimensional intersection subspace of a partial cobordism. Such a subspace cannot vanish when included in W: if a class vanished there, its intersections with all these same compact cycles would vanish, contradicting nondegeneracy. But H₂(W;Q)=0. Thus only the free-creating case occurs. The final n₃ three-handles remove these free directions. This proves the required kill-then-create order for the actual selected handles. [step 5.1, F1, F2, F3]

5.3 **Preserve the earlier image through every fixed handle.** Start with the standard infinity module of the incoming rational sphere. After the one-handles the whole ambient group is that module tensored with the exterior algebra on n₁ generators, by the reused tensor model. The actual one-handle map appends its top exterior generator. Apply the first killing two-handle with the restriction of the given global Spin-c structure, retaining its particular finite residue s_i. The preceding quotient calculation injects the exterior module modulo the contraction kernel. The image of the original one-handle top line is nonzero and is the top line of this image module. Repeat this on the smaller exterior image at each next killing handle. This tracks the actual earlier image; it does not assume that a nonzero map on the whole intermediate group is nonzero on that image. After n₁ killing handles the tracked image has Laurent rank one and contains the nonzero image of the original generator. The whole target HF∞(Y_mid) has Laurent rank one by rational-sphere standardness. The map is homogeneous, so between homogeneous rank-one Laurent modules its nonzero coefficient is a single U monomial, hence a unit. Thus the whole prefix map is an infinity isomorphism for the restriction of the given global structure. For the suffix, reverse the handle decomposition, starting from the outgoing rational sphere. The reversed prefix consists of its n₃ one-handles and the corresponding killing handles, so the same fixed-label argument gives an infinity isomorphism to Y_mid. The forward suffix map is its adjoint. On chosen complexes this follows by reflecting each holomorphic disk or triangle: the corner labels are reversed, n_z and its count are preserved, and the Laurent pairing matches dual generators. One- and three-handle top/bottom maps are dual by their explicit tensor definitions. The resulting pairing on infinity homology is perfect, since the finite free Laurent complexes are dual and their torsion Spin-c homology is free. An adjoint of an isomorphism under perfect pairings is an isomorphism. Composing prefix and suffix proves the fixed-structure infinity map of W is an isomorphism. Finally H₂(W;Q)=0, χ(W)=0 and σ(W)=0, so its already constructed degree formula is zero. The infinity/plus square is the chosen-handle square already constructed. [step 5.2, F1, F2, F3] ∎
