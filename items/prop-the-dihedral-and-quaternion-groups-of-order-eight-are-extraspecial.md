## Statement

Both $\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)$ and $Q_8$ are extraspecial groups of order $8$:
each is nonabelian, each has centre equal to its derived subgroup of order two,
and each has elementary abelian central quotient. In $\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)$
there are exactly six solutions of $x^2=1$, and in $Q_8$ exactly two.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The generalized dihedral group $\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)=\langle r\rangle\rtimes\langle s\rangle$ with $r$ of order four, and the quaternion group $Q_8=\{1,-1,i,-i,j,-j,k,-k\}$.

[F1] $Z(G):=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$ ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F2] For $g,h\in G$ the commutator is $[g,h]:=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F3] The generalized dihedral group of an abelian group $A$ is $\operatorname{Dih}(A)=A\rtimes C_2$ with the nonidentity element of $C_2$ acting by inversion ([[def-generalized-dihedral-group]]).

[F4] $Q_8:=\{1,-1,i,-i,j,-j,k,-k\}$ inside the nonzero quaternions, where $i^2=j^2=k^2=-1$, $ij=k$, $jk=i$, $ki=j$, $ji=-k$, $kj=-i$ and $ik=-j$ ([[def-quaternion-group-of-order-eight]], [[def-quaternions]]).

[L1] For $n\ge1$, $\operatorname{Dih}(C_n)=C_n\rtimes C_2$ has order $2n$, and with $C_n=\langle r\rangle$ and $C_2=\langle s\rangle$ one has $r^n=s^2=1$, $srs^{-1}=r^{-1}$, and every element has a unique form $r^i$ or $r^is$ with $0\le i<n$ ([[cor-dihedral-groups-as-semidirect-products]]).

[L2] $Q_8$ is a subgroup of the nonzero quaternions with $|Q_8|=8$; the element $1$ is the only element of order $1$, $-1$ is the only element of order $2$, and each of $\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$ has order $4$ ([[prop-the-quaternion-group-has-order-eight-and-a-unique-element-of-order-two]]).

[L3] For a finite $p$-group $P$ the following are equivalent: $P$ is extraspecial; $P$ is nonabelian, $|Z(P)|=p$ and $P/Z(P)$ is elementary abelian; $P$ is nonabelian and $Z(P)=P'=\Phi(P)$ has order $p$ ([[prop-equivalent-characterisations-of-an-extraspecial-p-group]]).

[L4] For a finite group $G$ and $H\le G$, $|G|=[G:H]\,|H|$ ([[thm-lagrange]]).

[L5] If the quotient group $G/Z(G)$ is cyclic, then $G$ is abelian ([[lem-cyclic-quotient-by-center-implies-abelian]]).

[L6] For $N\trianglelefteq G$, the quotient $G/N$ is abelian if and only if $[G,G]\subseteq N$ ([[thm-quotient-abelian-iff-contains-commutator-subgroup]]).

[L7] An elementary abelian $p$-group is a finite abelian $p$-group in which every nonidentity element has order $p$ ([[def-elementary-abelian-p-group]]).

[L8] $\langle S\rangle$ is the smallest subgroup of $G$ containing $S$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[L9] $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ is the least $k\ge1$ with $g^k=e$ ([[def-order-in-a-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $D=\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)$ the relation $srs^{-1}=r^{-1}$ gives $sr^{-1}=rs$, so $r(r^is)r^{-1}=r^{i+1}sr^{-1}=r^{i+2}s$, which differs from $r^is$ because $r^2\ne1$; and $r^i$ is central exactly when $r^{-i}=r^i$, that is when $r^{2i}=1$, that is when $i$ is $0$ or $2$. So $Z(D)=\{1,r^2\}$, of order two, and $D$ is nonabelian of order eight. [F1, F3, L1]

1.2 In $D$ one has $[s,r]=srs^{-1}r^{-1}=r^{-2}=r^{2}$; modulo $\langle r^{2}\rangle$ the images of $r$ and $s$ commute and generate, so $[D,D]\le\langle r^{2}\rangle$ and therefore $[D,D]=\langle r^{2}\rangle=Z(D)$. [F2, L1, L6, L8]

1.3 In $D$ the squares are $1^2=1$, $(r^2)^2=1$, $(r^{i}s)^{2}=r^{i}(sr^{i}s^{-1})s^{2}=r^{i}r^{-i}=1$ for each of the four values of $i$, while $r$ and $r^{3}$ have order four. So exactly six elements satisfy $x^2=1$. [L1, L9]

1.4 In $Q_8$ the element $-1$ is central, and $i$ is not, since $ij=k$ while $ji=-k$ and these are distinct elements of the eight-element set; the same computation excludes $\pm i,\pm j,\pm k$, since $-x$ is central exactly when $x$ is. So $Z(Q_8)=\{1,-1\}$, of order two, and $Q_8$ is nonabelian of order eight. [F1, F4, L2]

1.5 In $Q_8$ one has $[i,j]=iji^{-1}j^{-1}=k(-i)(-j)=(-j)(-j)=j^{2}=-1$; modulo $\{1,-1\}$ the images of $i$ and $j$ commute and generate, so $[Q_8,Q_8]=\{1,-1\}=Z(Q_8)$. [F2, F4, L2, L6, L8]

1.6 In $Q_8$ the only solutions of $x^2=1$ are $1$ and $-1$, because every other element has order four. [L2, L9]

2.1 For each of the two groups the central quotient has order four by Lagrange, is abelian because the derived subgroup equals the centre, and is not cyclic, since a cyclic central quotient would force commutativity; an abelian group of order four that is not cyclic has all nonidentity elements of order two, hence is elementary abelian. [L4, L5, L6, L7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.4, step 1.5]

3.1 Each group is therefore a nonabelian group of order $2^3$ with centre of order two and elementary abelian central quotient, so the second description in the characterisation makes both extraspecial; the solution counts are those of steps 1.3 and 1.6. [L3, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 1.6, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

The two solution counts are what separate the two groups: an isomorphism would carry solutions of $x^2=1$ to solutions of $x^2=1$, and six is not two. Nothing about the centres or the derived subgroups distinguishes them, since those agree.

Both sources write the dihedral group of order eight as $D_8$; this library writes $D_n$ for the dihedral group of order $2n$, so the group here is $\operatorname{Dih}(C_4)$, which is $D_4$ in that notation.
