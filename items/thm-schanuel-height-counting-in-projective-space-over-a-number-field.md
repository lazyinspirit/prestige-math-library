## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a number field of degree $N=[K:\mathbb Q]$, let $m\ge2$ be an integer, and let $C_{K,m}$ be the Schanuel constant. As $B\to\infty$,
$$
N^{\mathrm{rel}}_{K,m}(B)=\begin{cases}C_{K,m}B^m+O_{K,m}(B^{m-1/N}),&(m,N)\ne(2,1),\\ C_{K,m}B^2+O(B\log B),&(m,N)=(2,1),\end{cases}
$$
where $N^{\mathrm{rel}}_{K,m}$ counts points by relative multiplicative height. Equivalently, as $T\to\infty$,
$$
N^{\mathrm{abs}}_{K,m}(T)=\begin{cases}C_{K,m}T^{mN}+O_{K,m}(T^{mN-1}),&(m,N)\ne(2,1),\\ C_{K,m}T^2+O(T\log T),&(m,N)=(2,1),\end{cases}
$$
where $N^{\mathrm{abs}}_{K,m}$ counts by absolute multiplicative height. The implied constants depend only on $K$ and $m$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a number field $K$ of degree $N$, an integer $m\ge2$, and a real threshold $B\ge1$.

[A1] AC is assumed in the height, class-group, and unit-lattice suppliers used below. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] For each nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak a$, the number of nonzero tuples in $\mathfrak a^m$ modulo unit multiplication with $H_\infty(\mathbf x)\le YN\mathfrak a$ is
$$
\frac{R_K\kappa_{K,m}}{w_K}Y^m+O_{K,m}(Y^{m-1/N}),
$$
uniformly in $\mathfrak a$. ([[lem-schanuel-integral-tuple-count-mod-units]])

[F2] The ideal class group is finite, every class has an integral ideal representative, every nonzero integral ideal is invertible and has a unique prime-ideal factorization, and $(x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1})$ denotes the ideal generated by the tuple coordinates. ([[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]], [[thm-finiteness-of-the-number-field-class-group]], [[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]], [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]])

[F3] Ideal norms are multiplicative; every nonzero integral ideal has positive integer norm; and for a tuple generating an integral ideal $\mathfrak c$, its relative height is $H_K(P)=H_\infty(\mathbf x)/N\mathfrak c\ge1$. ([[thm-ideal-norm-is-multiplicative]], [[def-fractional-ideal-norm-number-field]], [[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]], [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]], [[def-absolute-multiplicative-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]])

[F4] There are finitely many integral ideals of norm at most any fixed $B\ge1$. ([[lem-finitely-many-number-field-ideals-of-bounded-norm]])

[F5] The ideal series for $\zeta_K(s)$ converges absolutely when the real number $s>1$ and has Euler product $\prod_{\mathfrak p}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}$. ([[def-dedekind-zeta-function]], [[thm-dedekind-zeta-euler-product]])

[F6] The absolute height satisfies $H(P)=H_K(P)^{1/N}$; degree one over $\mathbb Q$ means $K=\mathbb Q$, and the rational algebraic integers are exactly $\mathbb Z$. ([[def-absolute-multiplicative-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]], [[def-number-field]], [[cor-rational-algebraic-integers-are-integers]])

[F7] The natural logarithm is positive at $2$, obeys the product law, and is increasing on positive reals. ([[def-natural-logarithm]], [[thm-natural-logarithm-laws]])

[F8] The count functions satisfy $N^{\mathrm{abs}}_{K,m}(T)=N^{\mathrm{rel}}_{K,m}(T^N)$. ([[def-projective-height-counting-function]])

## Proof

1.1 Fix an integral ideal $\mathfrak a$. For integral ideals, write $\mathfrak d\mid\mathfrak b$ when $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak d\mathfrak e$ for an integral ideal $\mathfrak e$. Define $\mu_K(\mathfrak d)=0$ if a prime-ideal exponent in its factorization is at least two, and $\mu_K(\mathfrak d)=(-1)^j$ if $\mathfrak d$ is a product of $j$ distinct prime ideals. For $\mathbf x\in\mathfrak a^m\setminus\{0\}$, write $(x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1})=\mathfrak a\mathfrak b$ with integral $\mathfrak b$, using [F2]. The finite divisor sum $\sum_{\mathfrak d\mid\mathfrak b}\mu_K(\mathfrak d)$ equals $\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak b}(1-1)$, which is $1$ exactly when $\mathfrak b=\mathcal O_K$ and is $0$ otherwise. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Thus the indicator that $\mathbf x$ generates $\mathfrak a$ is the divisor sum in step 1.1. For every integral ideal $\mathfrak d$, $\mathbf x\in(\mathfrak a\mathfrak d)^m$ if and only if $(x_0,\ldots,x_{m-1})\subseteq\mathfrak a\mathfrak d$, which, after multiplying by the inverse of $\mathfrak a$, is equivalent to $\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak d$, that is, $\mathfrak d\mid\mathfrak b$. Also [F3] gives $H_\infty(\mathbf x)\ge N(\mathfrak a\mathfrak b)\ge N(\mathfrak a)N(\mathfrak d)$ for each such divisor, so the bound $H_\infty(\mathbf x)\le BN(\mathfrak a)$ forces $N(\mathfrak d)\le B$. The finite-support statement [F4] therefore lets us sum the indicators to obtain the number $P_{\mathfrak a}(B)$ of unit orbits of tuples generating $\mathfrak a$ as $P_{\mathfrak a}(B)=\sum_{N\mathfrak d\le B}\mu_K(\mathfrak d)S_{K,m,\mathfrak a\mathfrak d}(B/N\mathfrak d)$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $E=m-1/N$ and $c_0=R_K\kappa_{K,m}/w_K$. Applying [F1] to each term of step 2.1, with $Y=B/N\mathfrak d\ge1$, gives $P_{\mathfrak a}(B)=c_0B^m\sum_{N\mathfrak d\le B}\mu_K(\mathfrak d)/(N\mathfrak d)^m+O_{K,m}\!\left(B^E\sum_{N\mathfrak d\le B}(N\mathfrak d)^{-E}\right)$, with an error constant independent of $\mathfrak a$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Suppose $E>1$. Then [F5] bounds the error sum in step 3.1 by $\zeta_K(E)$. The absolutely convergent series of $\mu_K(\mathfrak d)(N\mathfrak d)^{-m}$ has Euler product $\prod_{\mathfrak p}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-m})=1/\zeta_K(m)$: finite expansion follows from the prime factorization in [F2], and absolute convergence follows by comparison with [F5]. Its omitted tail satisfies $\sum_{N\mathfrak d>B}(N\mathfrak d)^{-m}\le B^{-(m-E)}\sum_{\mathfrak d}(N\mathfrak d)^{-E}=B^{-1/N}\zeta_K(E)$. Consequently step 3.1 becomes $P_{\mathfrak a}(B)=c_0B^m/\zeta_K(m)+O_{K,m}(B^E)$, uniformly in $\mathfrak a$. [F2, F5, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 If $E=1$, then $m=2$ and $N=1$, so [F6] gives $K=\mathbb Q$ and $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z$. Every nonzero ideal is $n\mathbb Z$ for a unique positive integer $n$ (take its least positive element and use integer division), with norm $n$. For $2^j\le n<2^{j+1}$ there are $2^j$ integers and each reciprocal is at most $2^{-j}$, so the partial harmonic sum through $B$ is $O(1+\log B)$: the number of dyadic blocks is at most $1+\log B/\log2$ by [F7]. Put $J=\lfloor\log B/\log2\rfloor$, so $2^J\le B<2^{J+1}$ and $2^J>B/2$. The tail $\sum_{n>B}n^{-2}$ is at most $\sum_{j\ge J}2^j2^{-2j}=2^{1-J}=O(B^{-1})$. The ideal Möbius series at exponent $2$ converges absolutely by comparison with [F5]; expanding finite products gives its limit as $\prod_{\mathfrak p}(1-(N\mathfrak p)^{-2})=1/\zeta_K(2)$ by [F5]. Step 3.1 consequently gives $P_{\mathfrak a}(B)=c_0B^2/\zeta_K(2)+O(B\log B)$, uniformly in $\mathfrak a$. [F5, F6, F7, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Choose one integral ideal $\mathfrak a_j$ from each of the finitely many classes in [F2]. Every projective point has a representative tuple whose coordinate ideal is one of these ideals: scale its tuple by an element of $K^\times$ carrying its coordinate-ideal class to $[\mathfrak a_j]$. If two tuples both generate $\mathfrak a_j$ and represent the same projective point, their scalar ratio $\lambda$ satisfies $(\lambda)\mathfrak a_j=\mathfrak a_j$, hence $(\lambda)=\mathcal O_K$ after multiplying by $\mathfrak a_j^{-1}$, so $\lambda$ is a unit. Distinct ideal classes cannot represent the same point. For a tuple generating $\mathfrak a_j$, [F3] makes $H_K(P)\le B$ equivalent to $H_\infty(\mathbf x)\le BN\mathfrak a_j$. Summing the primitive counts of steps 4.1 and 4.2 over the finite class representatives gives $N^{\mathrm{rel}}_{K,m}(B)=h_KR_K\kappa_{K,m}B^m/(w_K\zeta_K(m))+O_{K,m}(B^E)$ when $E>1$, and the same main term with error $O(B\log B)$ when $E=1$. The coefficient is $C_{K,m}$ by its definition. [F2, F3, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 By [F8], substitute $B=T^N$ in step 5.1. If $E>1$, then $(T^N)^E=T^{mN-1}$; if $E=1$, the degree-one case has $N=1$, $m=2$, and $T^N\log(T^N)=T\log T$. These are exactly the asserted absolute-height asymptotics. [F8, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
