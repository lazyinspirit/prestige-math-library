## Statement

Let $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{0,\ldots,n-1\})$ with the composition convention of [[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]]. For $n\ge2$, $S_n$ is generated by the adjacent transpositions $\tau_i=(i\ i+1)$, $0\le i<n-1$. Equivalently, let $\kappa_n:\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\to\{1,\ldots,n\}$ be the increasing bijection $\kappa_n(i)=i+1$. Under $\sigma\mapsto\kappa_n\sigma\kappa_n^{-1}$, the generators are the one-based adjacent transpositions $s_j=(j\ j+1)$, $1\le j<n$, whose preimages in $S_n$ are $\tau_{j-1}$. For $n=0,1$, the empty set generates the trivial group $S_n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The symmetric group $S_n$ on $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ and, for $n\ge2$, the increasing letter bijection $\kappa_n$ from the Statement.

[F1] Every element of $S_n$ is a product of transpositions ([[thm-transpositions-generate-the-symmetric-group]]).

[F2] The subgroup generated by a set is the smallest subgroup containing it; the empty set generates the trivial subgroup ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F3] Products of permutations act with the right factor first ([[def-symmetric-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $0\le a<b<n$, pointwise calculation using [F3] gives $$ (a\ b)=\tau_a\tau_{a+1}\cdots\tau_{b-2}\tau_{b-1}\tau_{b-2}\cdots\tau_{a+1}\tau_a. $$ The product exchanges $a,b$ and fixes the other symbols; when $b=a+1$, it is just $\tau_a$. Every displayed index lies between $0$ and $n-2$. [F3, given, algebra]

2.1 Thus every transposition lies in the subgroup generated by the adjacent ones, and [F1]--[F2] show that this subgroup is $S_n$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Conjugation by the letter bijection $\kappa_n$ sends a permutation $\sigma$ of $\{0,\ldots,n-1\}$ to a permutation of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. It preserves multiplication by [F3] and has inverse conjugation by $\kappa_n^{-1}$. It sends $\tau_{j-1}$ to $(j\ j+1)=s_j$. Transporting the adjacent-transposition product expressions from step 2.1 therefore proves that the $s_j$, $1\le j<n$, generate the one-based model, with precisely the stated preimages in $S_n$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 If $n=0$ or $1$, the underlying set admits only its identity permutation, so $S_n$ is trivial and [F2] says the empty set generates it. No letter transport or nonexistent generator is used in these cases. [F2, F3, given] ∎
