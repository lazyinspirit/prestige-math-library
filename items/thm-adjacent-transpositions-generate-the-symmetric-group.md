## Statement

For $n\ge2$, $S_n$ is generated by the adjacent transpositions
$s_j=(j\ j+1)$, $1\le j<n$. For $n=0,1$, the empty set generates the trivial
group $S_n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The symmetric group $S_n$ and its standard ordering of symbols.

[F1] Every element of $S_n$ is a product of transpositions ([[thm-transpositions-generate-the-symmetric-group]]).

[F2] The subgroup generated by a set is the smallest subgroup containing it; the empty set generates the trivial subgroup ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F3] Products of permutations act with the right factor first ([[def-symmetric-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $1\le a<b\le n$, pointwise calculation using [F3] gives $$ (a\ b)=s_a s_{a+1}\cdots s_{b-2}s_{b-1}s_{b-2}\cdots s_{a+1}s_a. $$ When $b=a+1$, this word is just $s_a$. [F3, algebra]

2.1 Thus every transposition lies in the subgroup generated by the adjacent ones, and [F1]--[F2] show that this subgroup is $S_n$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 If $n=0$ or $1$, $S_n$ is trivial, so [F2] says the empty displayed set generates it. [F2, F3] ∎
