## Statement

Every rapid filter on $\omega$, regarded through characteristic functions as a
subset of Cantor space with coin measure (and hence through the standard coding
as a real set), is not Lebesgue measurable.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A filter $F$ on $\omega$ extending the Fréchet filter that is rapid in the sense of the definition item, viewed as $\{\chi_a:a\in F\}\subseteq 2^\omega$.

[F1] [[def-rapid-and-raisonnier-filters]]: rapidity and its uniform-bounding form.

[F2] [[lem-dyadic-coding-coin-measure-and-lebesgue-transfer]]: the coin measure $\nu$ on Cantor space, its completion, and the transfer to Lebesgue measure through the standard coding.

[F3] [[def-filter]]: $F$ is upward closed, closed under intersections, and does not contain $\varnothing$.

[F4] [[thm-lebesgue-density-theorem]]: almost every point of a measurable set of positive measure is a density point, so for every measurable $B$ and $\varepsilon>0$ the open set $\{s:\nu(B\cap[s])>(1-\varepsilon)\nu([s])\}$ covers $B$ modulo a null set.

[F5] [[def-lambda-system]] with [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]: Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem, used for the zero-one law below. Countable additivity and continuity from below are part of [F2].

[F6] [[def-countable-choice]]: the ambient choice hypothesis of this page, used through [F2] and [F4].

## Proof

1.1 Assume for contradiction that $F$ is measurable for the coin measure $\nu$. Since $F$ extends the Fréchet filter, it is closed under finite changes: if $a\in F$ and $a'$ differs from $a$ only below $k$, then $a\cap(\omega\setminus k)\in F$ and is contained in $a'$, so $a'\in F$. Membership in $F$ therefore depends only on the coordinates at and above any fixed level $n$. [assume-contra, F3, F6]

1.2 To contradict nullity, fix an arbitrary closed $B\subseteq2^\omega$ with $\nu(B)>0$. The density theorem supplies a finite nonempty family $T_0$ of binary strings such that every $s\in T_0$ satisfies $$\nu(B\cap[s])>(1-2^{-2})\nu([s]).$$ For example, enumerate canonically the positive-length strings satisfying the display and take the first one whose cylinder meets $B$. Put $n_0=\max\{\operatorname{lh}(s):s\in T_0\}$. [F4]

2.1 Zero-one law: for each $n$, the measurable tail event $F$ is independent of the sigma-algebra generated by the first $n$ coordinates. Hence the family of measurable $A$ satisfying $\nu(F\cap A)=\nu(F)\nu(A)$ contains every finite-coordinate cylinder. It is a lambda-system, while the cylinders are a generating pi-system, so [F5] makes the family the whole Borel sigma-algebra and then its completion. Taking $A=F$ gives $\nu(F)=\nu(F)^2$, hence $\nu(F)\in\{0,1\}$. [F2, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 Recursively, after finite nonempty $T_i$ and $n_i$ are fixed, let $S_i$ be the canonically enumerated set of strings $t$ with $\operatorname{lh}(t)>n_i$ and $$\nu(B\cap[t])>(1-2^{-(i+3)})\nu([t]).$$ The density theorem gives $\nu(B\setminus\bigcup_{t\in S_i}[t])=0$. Let $T_{i+1}$ be the first finite initial segment of this enumeration for which $$\nu\left(B\setminus\bigcup_{t\in T_{i+1}}[t]\right) <2^{-(n_i+i+2)},$$ and put $n_{i+1}=\max\{\operatorname{lh}(t):t\in T_{i+1}\}$. These choices are canonical, and every length in $T_{i+1}$ exceeds $n_i$, so $n_0<n_1<\cdots$. [F4, step 1.2]

3.1 The value is not one. The complement map $T(a)=\omega\setminus a$ is measure preserving, and $T[F]\cap F=\varnothing$: otherwise a filter would contain both $a$ and its complement and hence their empty intersection. If $\nu(F)=1$, then $\nu(T[F])=1$, contradicting additivity. Thus the assumed measurable filter is null. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

3.2 Apply rapidity to the increasing function $i\mapsto n_{i+1}$. There is $a\in F$ such that $|a\cap n_{i+1}|\leq i$ for every $i$. [F1, step 2.2]

4.1 Choose any $s_0\in T_0$ whose cylinder meets $B$; the canonical first such member suffices. Recursively suppose $s_i\in T_i$ has been chosen and has value $1$ on every coordinate in $a\cap\operatorname{lh}(s_i)$. Put $$ H_i=\{z\in2^\omega:z(m)=1\text{ for every } m\in a\cap[\operatorname{lh}(s_i),n_{i+1})\}. $$ The coordinates defining $H_i$ lie above those defining $[s_i]$, so the two events are independent, and step 3.2 gives $\nu(H_i)\geq2^{-i}$. The density bound for $s_i$ and $\operatorname{lh}(s_i)\leq n_i$ therefore give $$ \begin{aligned} \nu(H_i\cap[s_i]\cap B) &\geq \nu(H_i)\nu([s_i])-\nu([s_i]\setminus B)\\ &>(2^{-i}-2^{-(i+2)})2^{-\operatorname{lh}(s_i)}\\ &\geq3\cdot2^{-(n_i+i+2)}. \end{aligned} $$ For $i=0$ use the $T_0$ bound in step 1.2; for $i>0$ the defining bound on $T_i$ in step 2.2 is exactly the displayed $2^{-(i+2)}$ conditional error. The uncovered part of $B$ at level $T_{i+1}$ has measure below $2^{-(n_i+i+2)}$, so choose $z_i\in H_i\cap[s_i]\cap B\cap\bigcup_{t\in T_{i+1}}[t]$ and then the canonical $s_{i+1}\in T_{i+1}$ with $z_i\in[s_{i+1}]$. Since both strings are initial segments of $z_i$ and $\operatorname{lh}(s_{i+1})>n_i\geq\operatorname{lh}(s_i)$, we have $s_i\subsetneq s_{i+1}$; the definition of $H_i$ preserves the induction invariant. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.2]

5.1 Let $b=\bigcup_i s_i$. The points $z_i\in B$ converge to $b$, because both $z_i$ and $b$ extend $s_{i+1}$ and the string lengths tend to infinity; closedness gives $b\in B$. The induction invariant and unbounded lengths give $a\subseteq b$, so $b\in F$ by upward closure. Thus every closed positive-set $B$ meets $F$. [F3, step 4.1]

6.1 Hence $F$ has positive outer measure: if it had outer measure zero, an open $O\supseteq F$ with $\nu(O)<1$ would have a closed positive complement missing $F$, contrary to step 5.1. This contradicts $\nu(F)=0$ from step 3.1. The assumption of measurability is false, and [F2] transfers the conclusion to Lebesgue measure under the standard coding. [discharge-contradiction, F2, step 3.1, step 5.1] ∎
