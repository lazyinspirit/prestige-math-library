## Statement

Every rapid filter on $\omega$, regarded through characteristic functions as a
subset of Cantor space with coin measure (and hence through the standard coding
as a real set), is not Lebesgue measurable.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A filter $F$ on $\omega$ extending the Fréchet filter that is rapid in the sense of the definition item, viewed as $\{\chi_a:a\in F\}\subseteq 2^\omega$.

[F1] [[def-rapid-and-raisonnier-filters]]: rapidity and its uniform-bounding form.

[F2] [[lem-dyadic-coding-coin-measure-and-lebesgue-transfer]]: the coin measure $\nu$ on Cantor space, its completion, and the transfer to Lebesgue measure through the standard coding.

[F3] [[def-filter]]: $F$ is upward closed, closed under intersections, and does not contain $\varnothing$.

[F4] [[thm-lebesgue-density-theorem]]: almost every point of a measurable set of positive measure is a density point, so for every measurable $B$ and $\varepsilon>0$ the open set $\{s:\nu(B\cap[s])>(1-\varepsilon)\nu([s])\}$ covers $B$ modulo a null set.

[F5] [[def-lambda-system]] with [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]: Dynkin's $\pi$-$\lambda$ theorem, used for the zero-one law below. Countable additivity and continuity from below are part of [F2].

[F6] [[def-countable-choice]]: the ambient choice hypothesis of this page, used through [F2] and [F4].

## Proof

1.1 Assume for contradiction that the coin measure $\nu$ of the set $F$ is defined (that is, $F$ is $\nu$-measurable). Since $F$ extends the Fréchet filter, it is closed under finite changes: if $a\in F$ and $a'$ differs from $a$ in finitely many places, fix $k$ beyond all differences; then $\omega\setminus k\in F$ and $a\cap(\omega\setminus k)\subseteq a'$, so $a'\in F$ by [F3]. Membership in $F$ therefore depends only on the coordinates at and above any fixed level $n$, that is, $F$ belongs to the tail $\sigma$-algebra generated by the coordinates from $n$ on, for every $n$. [assume-contra, F3, F6]

1.2 Recursive construction of finite levels: build finite sets $T_i\subseteq 2^{<\omega}$ and heights $n_i=\sup\{\operatorname{lh}(s):s\in T_i\}$ such that $s\in T_i$, $t\in T_{i+1}$ implies $\operatorname{lh}(s)<\operatorname{lh}(t)$, that $\nu(B\setminus\bigcup_{t\in T_{i+1}}[t])<2^{-(n_i+i+2)}$, and that $\nu(B\cap[s])>1-2^{-(i+2)}$ for $s\in T_i$. Given $T_i$, let $S$ be the set of $s$ with $\operatorname{lh}(s)>n_i$ and $\nu(B\cap[s])>1-2^{-(i+2)}$, enumerated canonically, and let $T_{i+1}$ consist of the first $m$ members of the enumeration for the least $m$ with $\nu(\bigcup S\setminus\bigcup_{k<m}[t_k])<2^{-(n_i+i+2)}$. The density theorem gives $\nu(B\setminus\bigcup S)=0$, so such $m$ exists, and the construction is canonical in ZF, requiring no choices. [F4, F6]

2.1 Zero-one law: since $F$ is measurable and, for every $n$, independent of the $\sigma$-algebra of the first $n$ coordinates (step 1.1), the family of sets $A$ with $\nu(F\cap A)=\nu(F)\nu(A)$ contains every finite-dimensional cylinder, which is a $\pi$-system generating the ambient $\sigma$-algebra; it is a $\lambda$-system by finite additivity and continuity from below. Dynkin's theorem [F5] therefore makes it the whole $\sigma$-algebra, and applying it to $A=F$ gives $\nu(F)=\nu(F)^2$, so $\nu(F)\in\{0,1\}$. [F5, step 1.1]

2.2 Rapidity supplies a sparse witness: the heights built in step 1.2 satisfy $n_0<n_1<\cdots$, so $f(n)=n_{n+1}$ is an increasing function $\omega\to\omega$, and rapidity gives $a\in F$ with $|a\cap n_{i+1}|\le i$ for every $i$; this is the bound promised by the uniform-bounding form of rapidity, with room to spare. [F1, step 1.2]

3.1 The value is not one: the map $T(a)=\omega\setminus a$ is a $\nu$-preserving homeomorphism of Cantor space with $T[F]\cap F=\varnothing$; if $\nu(F)=1$ then also $\nu(T[F])=1$, contradicting additivity since the two sets are disjoint. Hence $\nu(F)=0$: a measurable rapid filter extending the Fréchet filter is null. [F2, step 2.1]

3.2 Recursive choice of points governed by $a$: define $s_{-1}=\varnothing$ and build $s_i\in T_i$ and $x_i$ such that $s_i\subsetneq s_{i+1}$, $x_i\in[s_i]\cap B\cap\bigcup_{t\in T_i}[t]$, and $s_i(m)=1$ whenever $m\in a\cap n_i$ and $m<\operatorname{lh}(s_i)$. At the inductive step put $H_i=\{x:x(m)=1$ for every $m\in a\cap[\operatorname{lh}(s_i), n_{i+1})\}$: this set depends only on coordinates at and above $\operatorname{lh}(s_i)$, so it is independent of $[s_i]$, and $|a\cap n_{i+1}|\le i$ gives $\nu(H_i)=2^{-|a\cap[\operatorname{lh}(s_i),n_{i+1})|}\ge2^{-i}$. Since $s_i\in T_i$ we have both $\nu(B\cap[s_i])>1-2^{-(i+2)}$ and $\operatorname{lh}(s_i)\le n_i$, so $\nu(H_i\cap[s_i]\cap B)\ge\nu(H_i)\nu([s_i])-\nu([s_i]\setminus B)>(2^{-i}-2^{-(i+2)})2^{-\operatorname{lh}(s_i)}\ge3\cdot2^{-(n_i+i+2)}>2^{-(n_i+i+2)}$; combined with the almost-covering clause for $T_{i+1}$, which bounds $\nu(B\setminus\bigcup_{t\in T_{i+1}}[t])$ by $2^{-(n_i+i+2)}$, this produces a point in $H_i\cap[s_i]\cap B\cap\bigcup_{t\in T_{i+1}}[t]$, and any $s_{i+1}\in T_{i+1}$ below that point works. [F4, step 2.2]

4.1 It remains to show that such a filter has positive outer measure, and for that it suffices to show that $F$ meets every closed set of positive measure. Fix a closed $B\subseteq 2^\omega$ with $\nu(B)>0$. [F2, step 3.1]

4.2 The limit point lies in $B\cap F$: let $b=\bigcup_i s_i$. The points $x_i$ converge to $b$ because each $x_i$ extends $s_i$ and the $s_i$ increase to $b$, so $b\in B$ by closedness. And $a\subseteq b$: for $m\in a$, all sufficiently large $i$ have $m\in a\cap n_i$, and the third clause of step 3.2 then gives $s_i(m)=1$, hence $b(m)=1$. Therefore $b\in F$ by upward closure of the filter. [F3, step 3.2]

5.1 Hence $F$ meets every closed set of positive measure, so its outer measure is positive: if the outer measure were zero, some open set $O\supseteq F$ would have $\nu(O)<1$, and the closed set $B=2^\omega\setminus O$ would have positive measure and miss $F$. This contradicts $\nu(F)=0$ from step 3.1, so the assumed measurability of $F$ fails; by the coding transfer [F2] the filter is not Lebesgue measurable as a set of reals. [discharge-contradiction, F2, step 3.1, step 4.2] ∎
