## Definition

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) and fix a finite horizon $T\ge0$. For
$0\le t\le T$, let
$(E_t,\mathcal E_t)$ be a standard-Borel state space; for $1\le t\le T$, let
$(F_t,\mathcal F_t)$ be a standard-Borel observation space
([[def-standard-borel-space]]), meaning a measurable space bimeasurably
isomorphic to a Polish space. A probability kernel has probability-measure
sections and measurable evaluations as in
[[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]. Specify an initial probability
measure $\mu_0$ on $E_0$ (a measure of total mass one, as in
[[def-probability-measure]]), transition probability kernels $K_t$ from
$E_{t-1}$ to $E_t$, and observation probability kernels $G_t$ from $E_t$ to
$F_t$, for $1\le t\le T$.

The trajectory space, with the finite product sigma-algebra generated by
measurable coordinate rectangles
([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]), is
$$\mathcal Z_T=E_0\times E_1\times F_1\times\cdots\times E_T\times F_T,$$
with $\mathcal Z_0=E_0$. Its finite-horizon law $P_T$ is the unique probability
law whose ordered iterated kernel integral is
$$\mu_0(dx_0)\prod_{t=1}^T K_t(x_{t-1},dx_t)G_t(x_t,dy_t).$$
The product notation means successive integration in the displayed order; it
does not assert the existence of densities. Existence and uniqueness under AC
are proved in [[thm-finite-horizon-state-space-kernel-law-exists-uniquely]].
Every finite product trajectory space is standard Borel by
[[lem-finite-products-of-standard-borel-spaces-are-standard-borel]].

The initial and transition laws specify the state evolution, while each
$G_t$ specifies the observation law given the current state in this model.
State evolution by itself therefore does not specify a joint state-observation
law. No density or time-homogeneity assumption is imposed, and no strict
stationarity assumption (shift invariance of all finite-dimensional laws) is
required ([[def-strict-and-weak-stationarity-for-time-series]]). This is a
single law, not a parameter-indexed family. The model contract records a
proposed law and makes no claim that it adequately describes an application
([[def-scientific-statistical-model-contract]]).
