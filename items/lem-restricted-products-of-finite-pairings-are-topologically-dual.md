## Statement

Assume AC. Let $I$ be a set. For each $i\in I$, let $A_i,B_i$ be finite abelian groups with a specified perfect bilinear pairing
$$e_i:A_i\times B_i\longrightarrow\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z,$$
where perfect means that each group maps isomorphically to the full group of homomorphisms from the other to $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. Let $U_i\le A_i$ and let $V_i=U_i^\perp\le B_i$ be its annihilator. Put
$$P=\prod_{i\in I}'(A_i,U_i),\qquad Q=\prod_{i\in I}'(B_i,V_i).$$
Thus a tuple lies in the displayed restricted product when its coordinates belong to the reference subgroup outside a finite subset. Give each restricted product its usual group topology: $\prod_i U_i$ (respectively $\prod_i V_i$) is open and carries its product topology, and its cosets are translates of that open subgroup. Give $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ the discrete topology. Then $P,Q$ are locally compact Hausdorff, the sum
$$e(x,y)=\sum_{i\in I}e_i(x_i,y_i)$$
has only finitely many nonzero terms, and the induced maps
$$Q\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm cts}(P,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z),\qquad
P\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{\rm cts}(Q,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$$
are group isomorphisms and homeomorphisms for the compact-open topology on the character groups. Every compact subset of $P$ is contained in
$$P_T=\prod_{i\in T}A_i\times\prod_{i\notin T}U_i$$
for some finite $T\subseteq I$, and likewise for $Q$. No common exponent bound on the finite groups is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the finite groups, specified perfect pairings, subgroups, index set and topologies in the Statement.

[F1] Under AC an arbitrary product of compact spaces is compact. In particular products of finite discrete groups are compact. [[thm-tychonoff]]

[F2] For arbitrary topological domains the compact-open topology is generated by the sets of continuous maps sending a compact subset into a prescribed open target subset. [[def-compact-open-topology-for-topological-domains]]

[A1] AC is an explicit hypothesis, spent in compactness of products. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Every $P_T$ is an open subgroup of $P$. It is a product of finite discrete groups and hence compact by [F1]. It contains the identity and is Hausdorff. These subgroups cover $P$, and $P_T\cup P_{T'}\subseteq P_{T\cup T'}$. The restricted-product topology is Hausdorff because distinct tuples are separated in one finite coordinate, so $P$ is locally compact Hausdorff. If $K\subseteq P$ is compact, its cover by the open $P_T$ has a finite subcover. Taking the union of those finitely many $T$ gives $K\subseteq P_T$. The same proof applies to $Q$. This includes the empty index set, when both groups are trivial. [A1, F1, given, construct]

2.1 If $x\in P,y\in Q$, then outside the union of their two finite exceptional sets one has $x_i\in U_i,y_i\in V_i$, so every summand is zero there. This defines the stated bilinear pairing. For fixed $y$, choose finite $T$ outside which $y_i\in V_i$. The character $e(-,y)$ vanishes on the open subgroup consisting of $x\in\prod_i U_i$ with $x_i=0$ for $i\in T$, and is therefore continuous into the discrete target. Its coordinates on the copies of $A_i$ are the characters specified by $y_i$. Perfection gives injectivity of $Q\to P^\vee$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.2 Conversely, let $\chi:P\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ be continuous. Each coordinate restriction gives a unique $b_i\in B_i$ by perfection. Its open kernel intersects $\prod_iU_i$ in a neighborhood of zero, so it contains
$$W_T=\{u\in\prod_iU_i:u_i=0\text{ for }i\in T\}$$
for some finite $T$. For $i\notin T$ the restriction of $\chi$ to $U_i$ is zero, hence $b_i\in V_i$; thus $b=(b_i)\in Q$. The subgroup of finite-support tuples in $\prod_iU_i$ is dense: every basic product neighborhood specifies only finitely many coordinates, and zero can be used in all remaining coordinates. On that dense subgroup, $\chi$ agrees with $e(-,b)$ by finite additivity. Both maps are continuous into a Hausdorff discrete group, so they agree on the entire compact reference subgroup. Any $x\in P$ differs from an element of that reference subgroup by a finite-support tuple, where agreement already holds. Hence $\chi=e(-,b)$ on $P$. [given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 At the zero character, [F2] gives a neighborhood basis containing the annihilators of compact sets: for any finite intersection of subbasic neighborhoods containing zero, requiring vanishing on the union of its finitely many compact sets is a stronger neighborhood. Conversely each such annihilator is open since the target singleton zero is open. By step 1.1 every compact set is contained in some $P_T$, so the annihilators of $P_T$ form a neighborhood basis at zero. Under the bijection of steps 2.1–2.2 they correspond exactly to
$$\{b\in Q:b_i=0\ (i\in T),\ b_i\in V_i\ (i\notin T)\}.$$
These are a neighborhood basis at zero for the restricted-product topology of $Q$: any basic neighborhood in its open product $\prod_iV_i$ contains one obtained by imposing zero at finitely many coordinates. Thus the bijection and its inverse are continuous at zero. Translation on character groups is a homeomorphism for the compact-open topology here. Indeed, for a fixed continuous character and a compact set, its image is finite; partitioning that compact set into its finitely many compact fibers translates a subbasic constraint into finitely many subbasic constraints. Therefore the bijection is a homeomorphism everywhere. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 To reverse the roles of $A_i,B_i$, it remains to verify $V_i^\perp=U_i$. This is finite algebra, without an extension theorem: a character of a subgroup of a finite abelian group extends to the group by adjoining one element at a time. If $a$ has least positive multiple $ma$ in the previous subgroup, choose a value $z\in\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$ with $mz=\chi(ma)$ and define the extension on its cosets by $\chi(h+ka)=\chi(h)+kz$; the defining relation makes this well defined. Finite induction suffices. Consequently restriction $\operatorname{Hom}(A_i,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\to\operatorname{Hom}(U_i,\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$ is surjective. A finite abelian group has exactly its order many such characters, by its finite cyclic decomposition and the explicit $d$ possible images of a cyclic generator of order $d$. Thus $|V_i|=|A_i|/|U_i|$, and perfection gives $|V_i^\perp|=|A_i|/|V_i|=|U_i|$. The inclusion $U_i\subseteq V_i^\perp$ is immediate, so equality follows. Applying the preceding three steps with the roles interchanged proves the second asserted topological isomorphism. This also treats zero reference subgroups and full reference subgroups without an exception. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
