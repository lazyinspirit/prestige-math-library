## Statement

Assume full AC. Let a compact smooth pair $(M,S)$ without boundary of dimension three or four have $S$ of codimension two, and supply finite branched charts $(y,w)\mapsto(y,w^2)$ with group $\mathbb Z/2$ acting by $w\mapsto-w$, together with their compatible coordinate lifts. Supply a smooth orbifold metric and an $SO(3)$ bundle in these charts with nontrivial isotropy a rotation by $\pi$ about a line. Use its determinant-one gauge transformations: in each local quaternionic lift they obey $u(y,-w)=\rho u(y,w)\rho^{-1}$, where $\rho=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$ and $\rho^2=-1$. These conjugation conditions are independent of the sign of the lift.

Finite-chart invariant Sobolev spaces $H^k$ are Hilbert spaces with smooth invariant density, compact $H^{k+1}\hookrightarrow H^k$ inclusion, and the usual dimension-three or dimension-four Sobolev product and embedding estimates. For $k\ge3$, the $H^{k+1}$ determinant-one gauge group is a Banach Lie group acting smoothly on the affine $H^k$ connection space. At an irreducible connection, Coulomb slices give a smooth unbased quotient and equation bundle; arbitrary connecting gauges between converging slice representatives have converging $H^{k+1}$ subsequences, up to their central ambiguity.

Every smooth first-order operator with scalar Clifford square, acting on these bundles and respecting the invariant local conditions, is Fredholm $H^k\to H^{k-1}$ on compact $M$. Its kernel and cokernel are smooth. A formally selfadjoint such operator $H$ has domain $H^1$ in $L^2$, compact resolvent and a complete real discrete spectrum of finite multiplicity. In particular this applies to the extended three-dimensional Hessian $H_B(\phi,b)=(d_B^*b,d_B\phi+*d_Bb)$, and in dimension four to $d_A^*\oplus\sqrt2d_A^+$.

On an infinite cylinder over the compact three-dimensional pair, suppose $H(t)$ has one fixed first-order principal part, shared by $H_\pm$, throughout the cylinder and differs from $H_\pm$ by smooth zero-order coefficients tending to zero with their first $k$ derivatives, while the selfadjoint limiting $H_\pm$ are invertible. The same assertion allows additional possibly nonlocal zero-order operators $R(t)$: every $\partial_t^aR(t)$ is bounded $H^j(Y)\to H^j(Y)$ for the needed integer orders, these bounds are uniform, and they converge on the ends in those operator norms; the two limiting full Hessians are selfadjoint and invertible on their $H^1$ domains. Then $\partial_t+H(t):H^k\to H^{k-1}$, including these additional terms, is Fredholm. The conclusion remains true on spaces weighted by $e^{\delta|t|}$ if $0\le\delta<\min\operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{spec}H_\pm)$. No index formula, nonlinear asymptotic convergence, transversality, trajectory compactification or Floer differential is asserted here.

Each operation is a local integral, cutoff, finite sum or coercive-form argument. [[thm-elliptic-differential-operators-on-compact-vector-bundles-have-parametrices-and-regularity]]

[F2] The actual Green/Hodge construction gives orthogonal kernel complements and elliptic estimates; Sobolev products and embeddings hold in the indicated dimensions. [[thm-sobolev-compactness-and-elliptic-estimates-give-fredholm-hodge-decomposition]] [[thm-higher-order-sobolev-embedding]]

[F3] Coercive forms have weak solutions, and complemented Banach inverse problems have local solutions. [[thm-lax-milgram]] [[thm-implicit-function-theorem-for-banach-spaces]]

[F4] The actual ordinary high-Sobolev slice proof gives the matrix product estimates, weak inverse bootstrap and arbitrary-gauge equation used below; these local estimates will be applied to invariant lifts, not by asserting that an orbifold is a manifold. [F5] A two-sided inverse modulo compact operators gives Fredholmness, which is unchanged by compact perturbations. Full AC supplies weak subsequences and the Hilbert spectral decompositions. [[thm-fredholm-index-is-stable-under-compact-perturbations]] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The finite compatible order-two charts, metric and bundle, the indicated Sobolev orders, and full AC.

1.1 **Invariant local spaces, without imposing a boundary at the singular set.** Choose finitely many smaller and larger lifted coordinate balls and invariant compactly supported bump functions, averaging each bump over the two-element group and normalizing their positive sum. In a local equivariant orthonormal trivialization let $R$ be its fiber involution. Such a trivialization is obtained by radial parallel transport for an invariant smooth connection, itself obtained by averaging a local connection; the radial paths commute with $w\mapsto-w$. The bounded projection on coefficient functions is $\Pi f(y,w)=\tfrac12(f(y,w)+R f(y,-w))$. For tensors include the differential of the base involution. It is bounded on every integer Sobolev space and commutes with an invariant radial mollifier. Define the norm by summing the localized lifted squared derivative integrals, with factor $1/2$ on a branched chart. Coordinate changes and their inverses have bounded derivatives on the compact supports, so the chain-rule and change-of-variables proof of [F1] proves norm equivalence. Completeness follows by taking the finitely many derivative limits and passing transition identities in $L^2$. Mollify, apply $\Pi$, cut off and sum to obtain smooth invariant density. Rellich and embeddings apply on the full lifted balls, not on a punctured domain; a finite diagonal subsequence retains all transition and invariance identities. The ordinary product inequalities restrict to these closed invariant spaces, with unchanged finite constants. In particular the singular set is not a boundary carrying an unproved extra boundary condition. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **The scalar-principal inverse on these exact spaces.** For an invariant formally symmetric Laplace-type operator $P$, the coercive integral form of [F1] is a form on the closed invariant $H^1$ space and has the same lower bound after finite localization. Lax–Milgram [F3] gives $(P+\lambda)^{-1}$ there for large $\lambda$. An invariant weak equation is a full distributional equation on each lifted chart: pair any compactly supported test with the equation and replace that test by its average $\Pi$; the pairing is unchanged by the finite coordinate action and invariant density. Thus the scalar difference-quotient proof of [F1] applies componentwise on full balls. It proves $\|u\|_{H^{j+2}}\le C_j(\|Pu\|_{H^j}+\|u\|_2)$, higher regularity and the exact $H^2$ selfadjoint domain, just as in that proof. The shifted inverse is compact on $L^2$ by step 1.1. The compact selfadjoint construction in [F2] gives its finite-dimensional smooth eigenspaces and their complete orthogonal decomposition; zero is handled by the finite kernel and its orthogonal complement. No quotient of an unproved singular parametrix is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The first-order Fredholm and selfadjoint statements.** Let $D:E\to F$ have scalar Clifford square. Both $D^*D$ and $DD^*$ are invariant Laplace-type operators. Set $B=(D^*D+1)^{-1}$ and $C=(DD^*+1)^{-1}$ by step 2.1; the shifts are already coercive since these squares are nonnegative. For smooth sections, $(D^*D+1)D^*=D^*(DD^*+1)$, and uniqueness of the weak inverses gives $BD^*=D^*C$. Therefore $Q=D^*C$ satisfies $DQ=1-C$ and $QD=1-B$. At order $H^k\to H^{k-1}$ the remainders improve two derivatives, hence are compact by step 1.1. This proves Fredholmness by [F5]. A distributional kernel is smooth by the square regularity; the cokernel is the adjoint kernel and is also smooth. If $D=H=H^*$, $(H\mp i)(H^2+1)^{-1}$ is an actual inverse of $H\pm i$ onto $H^1$. The symmetry and these two range identities show selfadjointness on precisely $H^1$: if $u$ is in the adjoint domain, subtract the inverse solution for $(H^*+i)u$ and use the orthogonal complement of $\operatorname{ran}(H-i)$ to conclude the difference is zero. Its resolvent improves one derivative and is compact. Diagonalize the compact positive inverse of $H^2+1$; its finite-dimensional eigenspaces are invariant under $H$ and diagonalize as real selfadjoint matrices, giving the asserted complete discrete real spectrum. Direct principal-symbol multiplication proves the squares are scalar for $d^*\oplus\sqrt2d^+$ and the displayed $H_B$; lower curvature terms do not change that calculation. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Gauge group and actual local slices.** In each chart a determinant-one gauge is a unit quaternionic matrix with the stated conjugation invariance. The $H^{k+1}$ algebra gives smooth multiplication and inversion, and the exponential and logarithm series near the identity converge in that algebra. Their terms obey the same involution, so they give Banach Lie charts on the invariant section space. The formula $A^u=u^{-1}Au+u^{-1}du$ is smooth to $H^k$. At an irreducible $A$, the scalar operator $d_A^*d_A$ has no zero kernel on adjoint zero-forms. Its coercive $H^1$ estimate follows by the same contradiction as [F4]: a violating normalized sequence has a strongly convergent invariant $L^2$ subsequence and a parallel limit, contradicting irreducibility. Solve weakly by [F3]. In lifted balls the actual multiplication bootstrap of [F4] upgrades its inverse to $H^{k+1}\to H^{k-1}$, including nonsmooth $H^k$ coefficients; finite averaging preserves every weak derivative equation. Thus $H^k=\ker d_A^*\oplus d_AH^{k+1}$, and the gauge action's derivative on this product is an isomorphism. The implicit theorem gives its local slice. If $u_n$ are arbitrary gauges joining converging representatives $A_n,B_n$, their exact local equation $du_n=u_nB_n-A_nu_n$, together with $|u_n|=1$, gives first a uniform $W^{1,\infty}$ bound and then, by successive product differentiation, a uniform $H^{k+1}$ bound. Rellich gives $H^k$ convergence; applying this equation to differences gives convergence of the highest derivative as well. Finite overlap compatibility and involution identities pass to the limit. At an irreducible center the limiting stabilizer is $\{\pm1\}$, so shrinking the slice excludes all other identifications exactly as in [F4]. This proves the unbased quotient and its equation bundle; it does not replace it by a based quotient. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 **Exact end inverses with their domains.** Let $H$ be an invertible limiting Hessian, and use step 3.1's orthonormal eigenbasis $e_\nu$, $He_\nu=\lambda_\nu e_\nu$, with $|\lambda_\nu|\ge a>0$. The inverse of $\partial_t+H$ on the whole real line is the modewise formula $u_\nu(t)=\int_{-\infty}^t e^{-\lambda_\nu(t-s)}f_\nu(s)ds$ for $\lambda_\nu>0$, and $u_\nu(t)=-\int_t^\infty e^{-\lambda_\nu(t-s)}f_\nu(s)ds$ for $\lambda_\nu<0$. Their kernels have $L^1$ norm $|\lambda_\nu|^{-1}$, so finite mode sums satisfy $\|Hu\|_2\le\|f\|_2$ and $\|\partial_tu\|_2\le2\|f\|_2$; Fourier transform in $t$ equivalently gives the exact multiplier $(i\tau+\lambda_\nu)^{-1}$. Spatial elliptic estimates from step 3.1 show $u\in H^1$ with a uniform bound. A bounded selfadjoint zero-order perturbation $R$ of a local Hessian has the same $H^1$ selfadjoint domain: for sufficiently large $c$, invert $H_0\pm ic$ and the remaining factor $1+R(H_0\pm ic)^{-1}$ by its norm-convergent Neumann series, since $\|(H_0\pm ic)^{-1}\|_{2\to2}\le c^{-1}$. Its resolvent remains compact. If $R$ preserves every integer Sobolev order, the equation $H_0u=Hu-Ru$ and successive local estimates show equivalence of the $H^j$ norm with its finite sum of $H$ graph norms. Thus the same spectral construction applies to the full limiting Hessians. Commuting time derivatives and powers of $H$, and using those graph-norm equivalences, gives a bounded $H^{k-1}\to H^k$ inverse; finite sums are dense and the estimates pass to their limits. Homogeneous $L^2$ modes vanish at both ends, proving uniqueness on this exact domain. Conjugating a weight $e^{\delta t}$ replaces $H$ by $H-\delta$, and a weight $e^{-\delta t}$ replaces it by $H+\delta$; both remain invertible for $\delta<a$. Smoothly rounding $|t|$ in a compact interval changes only a compactly supported zero-order coefficient. Thus the same end inverses hold with the asserted weights. [F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **The two-ended operator is Fredholm, not necessarily invertible.** On a bounded middle cylinder, identify the bundles by their given product form. Write $H(t)=H_0+V(t)$ for the fixed principal operator and smooth zero-order coefficient. Extend $V$ smoothly in an added bounded interval from its right-end jets to its left-end jets, using two disjoint collar cutoffs and interpolation of its remaining zero-order coefficients. This gives an actual periodic operator $\partial_t+H_0+\widetilde V(t)$ on $S^1\times Y$, equal to the original operator on a neighborhood of the bounded middle. Its square has the same positive scalar principal part, so step 3.1 gives its parametrix. Insert nested compact cutoffs, equal to one on the smaller middle interval. On each far end use the whole-line inverse of step 4.2, extending its input by zero past an inner cutoff, and multiply its output by an outer cutoff equal to one on the support of that input in the far direction. Add the three constructions with a partition whose sum is one. In both compositions with $\partial_t+H(t)$ the identity terms sum to one. Cutoff commutators are zero-order and supported in bounded intervals; followed or preceded by an inverse gaining one derivative, their remainders are compact by local Rellich. On the ends, $H(t)-H_\pm$ is zero-order with its first $k$ derivatives tending to zero. Its multiplication from $H^k$ to $H^{k-1}$ is compact: truncate to a bounded interval, use local Rellich and Leibniz, then let the interval increase; the tail operator norm tends to zero. For an additional nonlocal $R(t)$ the finite-cylinder operator is also compact $H^k\to H^{k-1}$: the time-derivative and spatial-order assumptions make it bounded on the full product $H^k$, and its compactly supported output then uses Rellich. The end differences are norm limits of these compact truncations by exactly the stipulated derivative operator bounds. These facts prove both remainder operators compact, including at higher order. Hence this actual two-sided parametrix proves Fredholmness. Apply the same construction after weight conjugation to obtain the weighted conclusion. Possible kernel and cokernel caused by the middle are retained; no unjustified global onto assertion, index computation or nonlinear gluing is inferred. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
