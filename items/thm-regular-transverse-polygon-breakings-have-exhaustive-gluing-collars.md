## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix actual nearly symmetric polygon data with common exact transverse generator charts and smooth source/target coefficient bounds. In each matching chart the boundary planes are $\mathbb R^m$ and $\prod_j e^{i\theta_j}\mathbb R$, with $0<\theta_j<\pi$; set $g$ to the positive finite minimum of $\theta_j$ and $\pi-\theta_j$ over the matching ends. Decay exponents and any weights are chosen below $g$, not below an assumed right angle. Consider either two regular polygon components joined at a transverse matching corner, or one regular polygon component and one regular nonconstant strip joined at an external corner. Include the components' actual conformal parameters in their derivatives, and quotient the strip component by its actual translation. Suppose the component count dimensions are zero. Fix representatives, using a smooth compact-source phase for the strip.

Suppose the actual coefficient data on the sewn sources agree asymptotically with the component data in their finite-tree neck charts as follows: on each fixed component core their difference and all required derivatives are $O(e^{-cT})$; they have uniform smooth bounds on the neck; and they are EXACTLY the common product structure wherever a map value is in the matching generator chart. The same estimates hold after differentiating in $T$. For polygon–polygon sewing $T$ is its actual new conformal neck parameter. For strip–polygon sewing it is the strip core's longitudinal position relative to the polygon core; the polygon has no translation quotient.

For all large $T$ there is a regular corrected polygon, and modulo the stated component phases these polygons form an injective half-open collar. Their index and count dimension are the actual sums: the glued count dimension is one. Every sequence converging to this unbubbled two-component breaking, with the actual strong compact convergence and no-neck convergence, is eventually in this collar. Kernel matching and bounded right inverses are proved below; they are not extra hypotheses. The collar is smooth for finite $T$ in the solution charts, and is a topological half-interval after adding its broken endpoint. No differentiability in a prescribed compactifying parameter at the endpoint is asserted.

More generally, for any fixed finite unbubbled tree of regular polygon and nonconstant strip components, the construction gives actual local product charts with the component solution coordinates and one positive gluing coordinate for each node. Their dimension is the sum of the component count dimensions plus the number of nodes. These charts exhaust every sequence with that component limit and strong compact and no-neck convergence. The constants may depend on that fixed component tree and small component charts. The assertion supplies neither a uniform inverse across unrelated components nor regularity of an arbitrary limiting component.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual regular components, transverse matching generators, exact product charts, source sewings, coefficient comparison bounds and component phases. For a stable vertex, regularity refers to its map operator augmented by its own conformal parameters. For a strip vertex it refers to its unquotiented geometric operator.

[F1] The actual source chart has a product of vertex conformal parameters and one new neck coordinate for a stable vertex–vertex sewing. Its transition is holomorphic and its thick source charts have uniform bounds. ([[lem-boundary-marked-disks-have-finite-tree-conformal-neck-charts]])

[F2] Actual $H^3\to H^2$ polygon map sections, higher Fredholm theory and local nonlinear derivative estimates have been constructed. The already proved strip pregluing has exact generator-tail equations, bounded component inverses, squared-cutoff right inverses and actual Newton correction. ([[lem-transverse-polygon-map-spaces-have-actual-hilbert-fredholm-sections]], [[lem-actual-regular-strip-pregluing-and-newton-correction]])

[F3] The actual whole-strip product inverse and its real Fourier modes give exponential tails and controlled kernel tails. The strip kernel-matching proof uses explicit projections and two Neumann compositions, not an assumed index formula. ([[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]], [[lem-kernel-matching-for-regular-strip-gluing]])

[F4] Actual quantitative correction and smooth split zero charts hold under Countable Choice. Strong compact convergence together with quantitative no-neck collapse has been proved for bounded-energy polygons. ([[lem-countable-choice-quantitative-newton-correction-and-smooth-zero-chart]], [[thm-bounded-energy-polygons-have-stable-source-bubble-and-strip-limits]])

## Proof

1.1 Use unweighted geometric Sobolev norms on the glued internal necks and product strip norms on the external ends. A fixed smooth connection and metric are chosen flat on each matching generator chart. On a component core choose any equivalent smooth finite-atlas norms. The component operators, augmented by their finite conformal parameters, are actual surjective Fredholm operators by the hypothesis and [F2]. Their kernel-complement $L^2\to H^1$ inverses preserve $H^2\to H^3$ by the actual higher estimates; denote them by $Q_v$. Finite parameter coordinates carry Euclidean norms. In chosen end charts every component map and kernel section satisfies the exact product equation, so its decaying Fourier modes give all-order exponential tails at every fixed rate below the stated angular gap as in [F3]. Components are fixed, hence the constants are finite and uniform on a small finite-dimensional component zero chart. For the zero-dimensional stable components that chart is a single solution; a strip has its single smooth translation direction before its chosen phase. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 On the actual sewn source retain the component maps on their cores, and on the neck interpolate their exponentially small target coordinates by one fixed-width real cutoff. For a strip vertex translate its fixed representative to the chosen position. This gives a genuine boundary-compatible pregluing $u_T$; the source gluing maps are holomorphic. In the generator chart its residual is exactly the cutoff derivative times the difference of the two tails. On each retained core the coefficient comparison adds an error $O(e^{-cT})$ times the fixed bounded component derivatives. The whole residual is therefore $O(e^{-c'T})$ in $H^2$, for a possibly smaller $c'>0$. The same statement holds after each fixed number of $T$ derivatives. The maps have uniform derivative bounds and local-addition radii. The explicit formulas in [F2] consequently give a uniform Lipschitz constant for the section derivative on a fixed small $H^3$ ball, including the finite vertex parameters. At finite $T$ the source-family and map coordinates are smooth; no operator-norm differentiability of arbitrary Sobolev translation is assumed. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Construct an actual right inverse for the derivative $D_T$ which includes the vertex conformal parameters but for now holds the new stable-edge length fixed. Choose real cutoffs on the neck with squared sum one, transition length comparable to $T$ and $k$th derivatives bounded by $C_kT^{-k}$. Cut the forcing, identify it with each component by the exact generator coordinates and source sewing, extend by zero, apply $Q_v$, and multiply its section output by the same cutoff before summing. Retain its finite parameter output without a cutoff, in the corresponding vertex parameter coordinate of [F1]. This defines a bounded map $P_T:H^2\to H^3\oplus\prod_v\mathbb R^{d_v}$ with a bound independent of $T$. Finite parameter derivatives of the component equation vanish on sufficiently far generator tails: the source coordinates there are fixed and all coefficient families have exactly the common tensor on that chart. Their uncut parameter contributions therefore match on the component cores; the coefficient-comparison remainder is exponentially small. Applying $D_T$ gives the squared-cutoff identity plus the outer cutoff commutators and the exponentially small core coefficient errors. Precisely $D_TP_T=I+K_T$ with $\|K_T\|\le C/T+Ce^{-c'T}$. The commutator estimate is the product estimate $\|(\partial\chi)\xi\|_{H^2}\le(C/T)\|\xi\|_{H^3}$, including its differentiated cutoff terms. For large $T$, $Q_T=P_T(I+K_T)^{-1}$ is an actual uniformly bounded right inverse. The norm-convergent Neumann series proves the inverse, rather than a presumed glued estimate. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Here is the kernel matching for these actual augmented operators. Cut and transplant the component kernel vectors using one cutoff to define $A_T$. Their exponential tails and the coefficient comparison give $\|D_TA_Tk\|\le Ce^{-c'T}\|k\|$. Project by $\Pi_T=I-Q_TD_T$ to get $\Gamma_T=\Pi_TA_T$ into $\ker D_T$. In the reverse direction cut a kernel section into components and apply $I-Q_vD_v$ to each, retaining its vertex parameter coordinates. Call this map $S_T$. Cutoff errors give $\|S_T\Gamma_T-I\|\le Ce^{-c'T}$ on the component kernels, and the squared-cutoff decomposition of an arbitrary glued kernel gives $\|I-\Gamma_TS_T\|\le C/T+Ce^{-c'T}$ on that kernel. The parameter outputs obey the same estimates because there are finitely many coordinates and their operator contributions vanish on the tail as checked in step3.1. Both compositions have Neumann inverses. Hence $\Gamma_T$ is a bijection with uniformly bounded inverse; this establishes actual kernel matching without assuming index additivity. All kernels are smooth and finite dimensional by [F2]. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Apply [F4] to the pregluing section, its actual $Q_T$, its exponentially small residual and the uniform derivative bound in step2.1. The explicit contraction gives a unique small correction in $\operatorname{im}Q_T$, of $H^3$ norm $O(e^{-c'T})$, and a regular corrected zero $g_T$. Its derivative differs from $D_T$ by the same small norm. The projections $I-Q_TD_T$ and $I-\widetilde Q_T\widetilde D_T$ therefore identify their kernels by the same two-Neumann-composition argument as step4.1. Smooth inverse families can be chosen locally in $T$ by $Q_{T_0}[I+(D_T-D_{T_0})Q_{T_0}]^{-1}$ and patched on an explicit locally finite interval grid, since convex sums of right inverses remain right inverses. The local Neumann formula and the uniform grid give bounded fixed-order derivatives of $Q_T$. Differentiating its correction equation then gives $\|\partial_T\xi_T\|_{H^3}=O(e^{-c'T})$: the highest derivative term is absorbed by the same contraction, the residual derivative has that bound by step2.1, and every remaining term contains the already exponentially small correction. Thus finite-$T$ solution dependence is smooth in the actual charts. On each shifted core the correction tends to zero in $C^1$ and the actual higher bootstrap gives smooth convergence. These conclusions use actual operator formulas and the specified complement, not yet a global uniqueness assertion. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Compute the count dimension and the gluing direction. In the polygon–polygon case the two regular stable component zero charts have dimension zero. Step4.1 gives $\ker D_T=0$, and step3.1 gives onto-ness, so it is invertible. Appending the one NEW conformal parameter from [F1] gives index one and a one-dimensional solution family. Its length coordinate is the actual conformal coordinate and distinguishes its points. In the strip–polygon case step4.1 gives a one-dimensional kernel, coming exactly from the strip translation before its phase quotient. There is no new conformal parameter: the polygon source has only its previous vertex parameters. Differentiating the pregluing with respect to the strip position gives that translated strip tangent plus exponentially small errors, and the corrected family has the same nonzero kernel projection by step5.1 and its differentiated residual estimate. Its count dimension is therefore one and $T$ is a genuine local zero-chart coordinate. No polygon translation has been divided out. For a finite tree the same calculation gives the sum of the stable vertex zero-chart dimensions, the unquotiented strip kernel dimensions and the actual stable-edge conformal parameters. After strip phases, each internal node has its one actual gluing coordinate. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

6.2 For the finite-tree extension choose disjoint matching generator regions and squared cutoffs on each long edge. Their derivatives are bounded by the reciprocal least neck length. Sum the finitely many transplanted component right inverses with their finite parameter outputs as in step3.1. The error norm is at most a fixed finite sum of $C/T_e+Ce^{-cT_e}$, so is small when the minimum length is large. The same explicit two-sided kernel estimates and Newton contraction apply with that sum. All constants depend on the fixed finite component tree and its uniform coefficient bounds. Shrink each regular component zero chart to a finite-dimensional ball with compact closure inside its chart; choose a compact-source phase on every nonconstant strip to remove precisely its translation. The component derivatives and inverse bounds are then uniform on these balls. Their actual solution families are smooth in all orders by the higher estimates, so their translated representatives and the pregluing depend smoothly on these FINITE-dimensional coordinates. This does not differentiate translation on a whole Sobolev space. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Prove exhaustion using the actual convergence assumption. For a sequence tending to the given breaking, its source conformal coordinates determine its stable-edge $T_n$ and vertex coordinates. At a strip breaking choose its longitudinal core position by a compact-source scalar phase with nonzero derivative on the limiting $u_s$; this phase is smooth even on $H^3$ maps, since source differentiation can be transferred to its smooth test function by change of variables. On fixed cores convergence is smooth. On the remaining long portions no-neck collapse puts both the sequence and component tails in the same exact generator chart. If $w_n$ is the coordinate difference from the pregluing, then $D_0w_n$ is minus its exponentially small cutoff residual on the join and is zero elsewhere on this generator neck. The whole-strip inverse after cutoff therefore gives $\|\chi w_n\|_{H^3}\le C(\|\chi'w_n\|_{H^2}+Ce^{-c'T_n})$. The cutoff bands lie in the already convergent cores. On a two-sided finite neck use two such bands; extension by zero is legitimate and the same inverse bound is independent of neck length. Thus the sequence is globally $H^3$ close to the actual pregluing, with the corresponding vertex coordinates. Exponentially small core coefficient comparison terms are included as additional right sides and tend to zero. In the stable–stable case its invertible fixed-$T_n$ zero chart now gives uniqueness directly. In the strip case the actual one-dimensional kernel coordinate from step6.1, together with this phase, adjusts $T_n$ by a quantity tending to zero and puts the remaining difference in $\operatorname{im}Q_{T_n}$; the uniform finite-dimensional inverse follows from its nonzero kernel projection. The Newton uniqueness in step5.1 then identifies the entire map with $g_{T_n}$. This is exhaustion, not an inference from set-theoretic convergence alone. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

7.2 The corrected pregluing derivative in each component solution coordinate is its transplanted kernel vector plus a quantity tending to zero in the glued norm. Indeed the derivative of the residual has the same exponential bound as the residual, the derivative of the inverse is bounded by its local Neumann formula, and differentiating the contraction absorbs the correction term as in step5.1. Kernel matching in step4.1 therefore identifies these derivatives with the component kernels by a matrix tending to the identity. For every stable-source edge append its actual conformal length coordinate from [F1]; projection onto those coordinates makes their independence literal. The remaining strip positions are detected by compact tests on their separate positive-energy cores: choose the tests nonzero on each corresponding $u_s$, normalize to one, and place their supports in those shifted cores. Their matrix on the strip-position derivatives tends to the identity, since other cores recede and the matched tails decay exponentially. After removing the strip phases, the derivative from all the stated component and gluing coordinates to the actual finite-dimensional zero chart is thus invertible, with bounded inverse on sufficiently small component balls and all sufficiently long necks. The smooth zero-chart inverse in [F4] gives a local product parametrization. Its dimension is the sum of the component count dimensions and one for each node: a strip translation becomes its relative core-position coordinate, while a stable-source edge has its new conformal coordinate. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.2, construct, algebra]

8.1 The collar is injective after increasing its lower length bound. Stable–stable sewings have distinct actual conformal coordinates. For a strip sewing, if two arbitrarily long positions gave the same polygon, an unbounded difference of positions would make one of its two fixed positive-energy strip cores recede from the other, contradicting the one-strip limit and no-neck energy. A bounded nonzero difference has a subsequential limit and would make the limiting strip invariant under that translation; finite energy forces the translation to be zero, since a positive-energy periodic rectangle would repeat infinitely often. Thus every such double representation has position difference tending to zero. The uniform nonzero phase derivative in step6.1 gives local injectivity on a common small interval, which then excludes them. The gluing neighborhoods are open by the actual finite-dimensional zero-chart inverses. Every nodal sequence enters them by step7.1. Adding the unique broken point therefore gives exactly a topological half-interval; no smoothness at $T=\infty$ is asserted. [F2, F3, F4, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, contradiction]

9.1 For exhaustion at this general regular tree, smooth convergence on the fixed cores and the same exact-neck cutoff estimate from step7.1 put the entire solution in a small glued Sobolev neighborhood, including its actual source coordinates. The finite-dimensional kernel coordinates are then adjusted by the inverse matrix in step7.2: move the component zero-chart coordinates and the strip positions so that the remaining difference lies in the chosen image of the glued right inverse. The adjusted coordinates converge to the limiting ones; the bound is uniform because that matrix and its inverse are uniform. Newton uniqueness identifies the remaining map with the corrected pregluing. Local injectivity follows from the same inverse matrix. If two representations were to leave every common local coordinate neighborhood, core convergence would first force their component coordinates to the same limiting values; an unbounded relative strip shift would lose its positive-energy core, and a bounded nonzero limiting shift would make that finite-energy strip periodic, exactly as in step8.1. They therefore eventually lie in one such neighborhood, where local injectivity excludes double representations. This proves the asserted product charts and exhaustion for each fixed regular tree. Compactness and regularity must still separately show which trees can occur in a particular moduli problem. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 6.2, step 7.2, construct, algebra] ∎
