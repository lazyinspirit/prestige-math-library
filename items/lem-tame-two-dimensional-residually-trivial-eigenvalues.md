## Statement

Let $p$ be an odd prime, and let $L$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero with a nonarchimedean valuation $v:L^\times\to\Gamma$ into a totally ordered abelian group. Write $v(0)=+\infty$, $\Omega=\{x:v(x)\geq0\}$, $\mathfrak m=\{x:v(x)>0\}$, and suppose the residue field $\Omega/\mathfrak m$ has characteristic $p$. Let $q>1$ be an integer with $q\equiv1\pmod p$. Suppose $A\in\operatorname{SL}_2(\Omega)$, $A\equiv I\pmod{\mathfrak m}$, and $B\in\operatorname{GL}_2(L)$ satisfy
$$
BAB^{-1}=A^q.
$$
The eigenvalues of $A$, counted with multiplicity, are $\alpha,\alpha^{-1}$ for a unit $\alpha\in\Omega^\times$ reducing to $1$. The order of $\alpha$ is a power of $p$ dividing $q-1$. In particular $\alpha^{q-1}=1$. If $a$ is the largest integer with $p^a\mid(q-1)$, the rule
$$
\xi:\mathbb Z/(q-1)\mathbb Z\longrightarrow\Omega^\times,\qquad \xi(\overline n)=\alpha^n
$$
is a well-defined character with image of order dividing $p^a$, and $\operatorname{tr}(A)=\xi(\overline1)+\xi(\overline1)^{-1}$. No assertion that $A$ is unipotent is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the field, valuation, odd prime, integer, matrices and conjugation relation in the Statement. A valuation satisfies $v(xy)=v(x)+v(y)$ and $v(x+y)\geq\min(v(x),v(y))$; if the two valuations differ, equality holds. A character here means a group homomorphism from the displayed finite cyclic additive group to the multiplicative group of units.

## Proof

1.1 The characteristic polynomial of $A$ is $X^2-tX+1$ with $t\in\Omega$. Its roots exist in $L$ by the supplied algebraic closedness, are nonzero, and have product $1$. A root $\alpha$ cannot have negative valuation: then $v(\alpha^2)=2v(\alpha)$ is strictly less than both $v(t\alpha)\geq v(\alpha)$ and $v(1)=0$, contradicting $\alpha^2-t\alpha+1=0$. Its inverse is also a root and has nonnegative valuation, so $v(\alpha)=0$. Since $t$ reduces to $2$, the reduced characteristic polynomial is $(X-1)^2$. Reducing the root equation in the residue field therefore gives $\overline\alpha=1$, and the other root is $\alpha^{-1}$. [given, algebra]

2.1 A matrix over an algebraically closed field is upper triangular in a basis starting with an eigenvector: its invariant eigenline has a one-dimensional quotient, and the induced scalar is the other eigenvalue. Thus $A^q$ has eigenvalues $\alpha^q,\alpha^{-q}$ even when $A$ is not diagonalizable. Similarity of $A$ and $A^q$ gives equality of these multisets with $\{\alpha,\alpha^{-1}\}$. In particular $\alpha^q$ is either $\alpha$ or $\alpha^{-1}$, so $\alpha^{q-1}=1$ or $\alpha^{q+1}=1$. Consequently $\alpha$ has finite order dividing $q^2-1$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 A finite-order unit $z$ reducing to $1$ has $p$-power order. Indeed, write its order as $p^r m$ with $p\nmid m$, and put $y=z^{p^r}$. Then $y^m=1$, $\overline y=1$, and $1+y+\cdots+y^{m-1}$ reduces to the nonzero element $m$ of the residue field. This sum is a unit: an element of valuation zero has its inverse in $\Omega$. The factorization $(y-1)(1+y+\cdots+y^{m-1})=0$ gives $y=1$, so the order of $z$ divides $p^r$. Apply this to $\alpha$ from steps 1.1 and 2.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Because $p$ is odd and $q\equiv1\pmod p$, the integer $q+1$ is prime to $p$. The order of $\alpha$ is a power of $p$ dividing $(q-1)(q+1)$, hence divides $q-1$, and indeed divides $p^a$. The formula for $\xi$ is independent of the representative of $\overline n$ because $\alpha^{q-1}=1$, is multiplicative under addition of exponents, and has image generated by $\alpha$. Finally the trace is the sum of the two roots of the characteristic polynomial, giving the asserted formula. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Source and application limits

This is a matrix-level adaptation, not an attribution of this exact statement to Zhang. The complete second-part proof of Proposition 4.3.3 was read in the local v5 text, printed p. 40. Zhang cites NSW08, Theorem 7.5.3 for the Galois-theoretic tame input; that source was not read here. The displayed conjugation relation is an explicit hypothesis, not a locally established statement about inertia. Passing from this finite cyclic character to a character of a residue-field multiplicative group requires its cyclic-group identification. Applying this lemma at generic deformation primes also requires a valid valued coefficient realization and an integral lattice. None is supplied by this lemma. The first part of Proposition 4.3.3, including its patching proof, remains outside this argument.
