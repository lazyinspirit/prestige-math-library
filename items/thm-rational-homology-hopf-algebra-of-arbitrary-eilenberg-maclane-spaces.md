## Statement

Let $A$ be any abelian group and $n\ge1$. For the Dold–Kan simplicial abelian group $\Gamma(A[n])$, addition induces a natural isomorphism of graded Hopf algebras
$$H_*(|\Gamma(A[n])|;\mathbb Q)\cong\operatorname{Sym}_{\mathrm{gr}}((A\otimes\mathbb Q)[n]),$$
where the generator is the rational Hurewicz image. No finite-generation hypothesis or axiom of choice is used. No identification with singular cohomology is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** One abelian group A and one integer n≥1.

[F1] The stated homology Hopf algebra formula holds naturally for finitely generated abelian groups. [[thm-rational-homology-hopf-algebra-of-finite-type-eilenberg-maclane-spaces]]

[F2] The explicit Dold–Kan inverse Γ is natural and is built in each simplicial degree from a finite direct sum of the input chain groups. [[thm-dold-kan-equivalence-for-simplicial-modules]]

[F3] A compact map into a CW complex has image in a finite subcomplex without a choice principle. [[lem-compact-cw-images-have-finite-cell-support-without-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Directed finite-subgroup approximation on actual simplices and finite singular chains.

1.1 Let I be the set of all finitely generated subgroups B⊂A, ordered by inclusion. It is directed: if B and C have finite generating lists, their union generates one subgroup containing both. Every element of A belongs to a cyclic such subgroup. This defines I as a set of actual subgroups and makes no selection of a generating list for every subgroup. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In each fixed simplicial degree, the explicit Γ-formula [F2] contains finitely many copies of A and face and degeneracy maps are finite sums of structure maps. Thus every simplex and every specified finite family of simplices of Γ(A[n]) belongs to Γ(B[n]) for one finitely generated B: take the subgroup generated by the finitely many coordinates actually occurring. The same applies to every specified finite family of face and degeneracy identities. Hence Γ(A[n]) is the directed union of its actual simplicial subgroups Γ(B[n]). Their realizations embed as CW subcomplexes, with one cell per nondegenerate simplex. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 A singular simplex into |Γ(A[n])| has compact image, so [F3] puts it in one finite CW subcomplex. That finite subcomplex contains only finitely many simplicial labels, hence lies in some |Γ(B[n])| by step 2.1. The same holds for one finite singular chain or one specified bounding chain. Therefore every rational homology class is represented at one finite stage, and two stage representatives that agree in the full space agree after enlarging to a common finite stage containing one bounding chain. This proves H_*(|Γ(A[n])|;Q)=colim_B H_*(|Γ(B[n])|;Q) directly on finite chains, including injectivity, without choosing a stage for every chain at once. [F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Tensoring A with Q and forming the graded-symmetric algebra are compatible with this directed union: every tensor or polynomial expression uses finitely many elements of A, and every equality in the tensor and relation quotients has a finite witness contained in one finitely generated subgroup. Thus Sym_gr((A⊗Q)[n]) is the directed colimit of the corresponding symmetric algebras for B. The natural finite-stage isomorphisms of [F1] commute with all subgroup inclusions, multiplication, diagonal and Hurewicz. Taking their colimit and applying step 3.1 gives the asserted Hopf algebra isomorphism for A. The zero group is included by the zero stage. Retain the finite-support construction of step 3.1. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra, discharge-construct] ∎
