## Statement refuted

**Refuted claim:** if two spaces have isomorphic singular homology groups in
every degree, then they are homotopy equivalent.

Take
$$X:=T^2,\qquad Y:=(S^1\vee S^1)\vee S^2.$$
May's torus calculation and Miller's CW-complex calculation give
$$H_0(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_0(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2,$$
$$H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z,\qquad H_n(X;\mathbb Z)\cong H_n(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong 0\text{ for }n\geq 3.$$
Nevertheless $X$ and $Y$ are not homotopy equivalent.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The spaces $X=T^2$ and $Y=(S^1\vee S^1)\vee S^2$.

[L1] The torus has fundamental group $\pi_1(T^2)\cong\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z$ ([[cor-fundamental-group-of-two-dimensional-torus]]).

[L2] The wedge of two circles has fundamental group freely generated by two loops ([[cor-fundamental-group-of-two-circle-wedge]]).

[L3] The sphere $S^2$ is simply connected ([[thm-higher-dimensional-spheres-are-simply-connected]]).

[L4] A simply connected overlap turns van Kampen into a free product ([[cor-seifert-van-kampen-simply-connected-overlap]]).

[L5] A deformation retract induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups at every basepoint of the retract ([[prop-retracts-inject-fundamental-groups]]).

[L6] Pointed maps induce functorial homomorphisms on fundamental groups, and pointed-homotopic maps induce the same homomorphism ([[thm-induced-fundamental-group-map-functoriality]]).

[L7] The wedge is the quotient obtained by identifying only the chosen basepoints ([[def-wedge-of-pointed-spaces]]).

[A1] The displayed singular-homology computations hold for the chosen spaces $X$ and $Y$.

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [A1], the spaces $X$ and $Y$ have isomorphic singular homology groups in every degree, so the refuted claim's hypothesis holds for this pair. [A1]

1.2 Write $W_2:=S^1\vee S^1$ and let $q:W_2\sqcup S^2\to Y$ be the quotient map from [L7], with both basepoints identified to $y_0\in Y$. Choose a small open disk $D\subseteq S^2$ around the sphere basepoint and small open arcs $O_1,O_2$ around the wedge point in the two circle summands of $W_2$. Put $$U:=q(W_2\sqcup D),\qquad V:=q((O_1\cup O_2)\sqcup S^2).$$ By the quotient description in [L7], these are open path-connected subsets of $Y$ covering $Y$. The set $U$ deformation retracts onto $W_2$ by contracting $D$ to $y_0$, the set $V$ deformation retracts onto $S^2$ by contracting the two arcs to $y_0$, and the overlap $U\cap V=q((O_1\cup O_2)\sqcup D)$ deformation retracts onto $y_0$. [L7, given, choose, construct]

1.3 By [L1], $\pi_1(X,([0],[0]))\cong\mathbb Z^2$. The group $\mathbb Z^2$ is abelian, while $F_2$ is not abelian because the reduced words $ab$ and $ba$ are distinct. Hence $\pi_1(X,([0],[0]))\not\cong\pi_1(Y,y_0)$. [L1, algebra]

2.1 By [L2] and [L5], the deformation retraction in step 1.2 gives $\pi_1(U,y_0)\cong F_2$. By [L3] and [L5], the corresponding deformation retraction gives $\pi_1(V,y_0)=1$. Since $U\cap V$ deformation retracts to the point $y_0$, it is simply connected. Applying [L4] to the open cover $Y=U\cup V$ therefore yields $$\pi_1(Y,y_0)\cong\pi_1(U,y_0)*\pi_1(V,y_0)\cong F_2.$$ [L2, L3, L4, L5, step 1.2]

2.2 If $X$ and $Y$ were homotopy equivalent, choose homotopy inverse maps $h:X\to Y$ and $k:Y\to X$, together with homotopies $H:k\circ h\simeq\operatorname{id}_X$ and $K:h\circ k\simeq\operatorname{id}_Y$. Put $\beta(t):=H(([0],[0]),t)$ and $\gamma(t):=K(y_0,t)$. [L6, step 1.3, construct]
For any path $\rho$ from $a$ to $b$, the assignment $[\alpha]\mapsto[\bar\rho*\alpha*\rho]$ transports loop classes from $\pi_1(X,a)$ to $\pi_1(X,b)$, and reversing $\rho$ gives the inverse transport because the inserted pairs $\rho*\bar\rho$ and $\bar\rho*\rho$ contract to constant loops. Applying this to $\beta$ and $\gamma$ yields isomorphisms
$$\beta_\#:\pi_1(X,k(h(([0],[0]))))\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_1(X,([0],[0])),\qquad \gamma_\#:\pi_1(Y,h(k(y_0)))\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_1(Y,y_0).$$
The maps $h:(X,([0],[0]))\to(Y,h(([0],[0])))$ and $k:(Y,y_0)\to(X,k(y_0))$ are pointed, so [L6] gives induced homomorphisms on fundamental groups. Precomposing $H$ and $K$ with based loops and then transporting the moving basepoints by $\beta_\#$ and $\gamma_\#$ shows that these induced homomorphisms are inverse isomorphisms up to the two basepoint transports. Therefore $\pi_1(X,([0],[0]))\cong\pi_1(Y,y_0)$, contradicting step 1.3.

3.1 Therefore $X$ and $Y$ are not homotopy equivalent even though step 1.1 shows that their singular homology groups agree. [step 1.1, step 2.2] ∎
