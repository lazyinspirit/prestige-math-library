## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group over $k$ and $\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)$ a root ([[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Then: (a) $G_\alpha=C_G(T_\alpha)$ is a split reductive subgroup of semisimple rank $1$ with $\operatorname{Lie}(G_\alpha)=\mathfrak t\oplus\mathfrak g_\alpha\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ and $\dim\mathfrak g_\alpha=\dim\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}=1$, and the rational multiples of $\alpha$ occurring in $\Phi$ are only $\pm\alpha$; (b) the root group $U_\alpha$ is $T$-stable, isomorphic to $\mathbf G_a$, and has $\operatorname{Lie}(U_\alpha)=\mathfrak g_\alpha$; a smooth $T$-stable subgroup $H\subseteq G$ contains $U_\alpha$ iff $\operatorname{Lie}(H)\supseteq\mathfrak g_\alpha$; (c) $W(G_\alpha,T)=\{1,s_\alpha\}$ with the nontrivial element represented by some $n_\alpha\in N_{G_\alpha}(T)(k)$, and there is a unique cocharacter $\alpha^\vee\in X_*(T)$ with $s_\alpha(x)=x-\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ for all $x\in X(T)$ and $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$; (d) $G$ is generated by $T$ and the root groups $U_\alpha$, $\alpha\in\Phi$; (e) $(X(T),\Phi,\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee)$ is a reduced root datum and $W(G,T)$ coincides with the subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(X(T))$ generated by the $s_\alpha$; (f) for a Borel subgroup $B\supseteq T$ with positive system $\Phi^+$, the multiplication map $\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}U_\alpha\to B_u=R_u(B)$ is a $T$-equivariant isomorphism of varieties for any ordering of $\Phi^+$, every smooth $T$-stable subgroup of $B_u$ is the product of the $U_\alpha$ it contains, and a subset $\Psi\subseteq\Phi^+$ is the weight set of a smooth connected $T$-stable subgroup of $B_u$ iff it is quasi-closed. Here quasi-closed means that for $\alpha,\beta\in\Psi$ and integers $i,j>0$, every root $i\alpha+j\beta$ whose Chevalley commutator coefficient $N_{\alpha,\beta;i,j}$ is nonzero in $k$ lies in $\Psi$. Every closed subset of $\Phi^+$ satisfies this condition; in characteristic $0$ or characteristic $p>3$, quasi-closure is equivalent to ordinary root closure, namely closure under sums that are roots.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a split reductive group $(G,T)$ and a root $\alpha\in\Phi(G,T)$ with $T_\alpha=(\ker\alpha)^\circ_{\mathrm{red}}$, the maximal subtorus of $\ker\alpha$, $G_\alpha=C_G(T_\alpha)$ and $U_\alpha=H_{(\alpha)}$.

[F1] A reductive group is the almost product of its largest central torus $Z(G)_t$ and its semisimple derived group ([[lem-reductive-center-radical-and-semisimple-quotient]]). $G_\alpha$ is smooth, connected and reductive (centralizers of tori in reductive groups are reductive), with Lie algebra $\mathfrak t\oplus\bigoplus_{\beta|_{T_\alpha}=1}\mathfrak g_\beta$ ([[thm-chevalley-centralizer-radical-and-reductive-centralizers]]); $U_\alpha$ is $T$-stable and connected with Lie algebra $\sum_{\beta\in(\alpha)\cap\Phi}\mathfrak g_\beta$ ([[thm-weight-subgroups-of-a-torus-action]], [[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]], [[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] $G_\alpha$ has semisimple rank at most $1$; the classification of split reductive groups of semisimple rank one gives a central isogeny from $\mathrm{SL}_2$ onto $G_\alpha\prime$, isomorphic on the two root groups, and a unique coroot in the rank-one torus of $G_\alpha\prime$ ([[thm-split-rank-one-reductive-classification]], [[lem-sl2-structure-and-root-coordinates]]); the generated subgroup of smooth connected torus-stable subgroups is smooth connected by [[thm-weight-subgroups-of-a-torus-action]](c); the Lie functor detects generation for smooth connected groups ([[lem-lie-functor-exactness-fixed-points-and-generation]]).

[F3] The abstract combinatorics: $(X(T),\Phi)$ with the coroots of (c) satisfies (rd1)-(rd3), and the chamber action of the reflection group agrees with the action of $W(G,T)$ on Borel subgroups containing $T$ (Milne, Theorem 21.37) ([[def-abstract-root-datum-and-its-weyl-group]], [[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]); the strictly contracting affine case of the Luna comparison detects isomorphisms of smooth affine varieties from an equivariant tangent-space isomorphism at their unique fixed points ([[thm-luna-map-and-bialynicki-birula-decomposition]]).

[F4] Fix split root coordinates $u_\gamma:\mathbf G_a\to U_\gamma$ obtained from a pinning over $\mathbb Z$. For nonopposite roots the Chevalley formula is $[u_\alpha(x),u_\beta(y)]=\prod_{i,j>0}u_{i\alpha+j\beta}(N_{\alpha,\beta;i,j}x^iy^j)$, over the roots that occur. Smooth connected subgroups containing $T$ correspond to quasi-closed root subsets; the nonzero structure constants have prime factors only $2$ and $3$ (Milne, Aside 21.95). The ordered multiplication of all positive root groups, and of the root groups contained in a smooth $T$-stable subgroup of $B_u$, is a $T$-equivariant variety isomorphism (Milne, Theorem 21.68(a),(c)).

## Proof


1.1 The subtorus $T_\alpha$ has codimension one in $T$ and is central in $G_\alpha$. Thus the semisimple rank of the split reductive group $G_\alpha$ is at most one. It is nonzero because its Lie algebra contains the nonzero root space $\mathfrak g_\alpha$, whereas a reductive group of semisimple rank zero is a torus. Apply [F2]: the central cover of $G_\alpha\prime$ has the two standard root groups of $\mathrm{SL}_2$, with one-dimensional root spaces. The central torus $Z(G_\alpha)_t$ contributes only zero weights, by the exact central-torus product clause of [F1], so $G_\alpha$ has exactly two nonzero weights, $\alpha$ and $-\alpha$. By [F1], the roots of $G$ restricting trivially to $T_\alpha$ are precisely the rational multiples of $\alpha$; hence those multiples are only $\pm\alpha$ and $\operatorname{Lie}(G_\alpha)=\mathfrak t\oplus\mathfrak g_\alpha\oplus\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$. This proves (a). [F2, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Choose the central cover $v:\mathrm{SL}_2\to G_\alpha\prime$ from [F2], taking its diagonal torus onto $T_1=(T\cap G_\alpha\prime)_t$. Its standard root groups map isomorphically onto $U_{\pm\alpha}$, giving (b), including the Lie algebras and the smooth $T$-stable subgroup containment criterion of [F1]. The element $v(n_\alpha)$ normalizes $T_1$ and centralizes the central torus of $G_\alpha$, hence normalizes $T=T_1Z(G_\alpha)_t$. The Weyl group therefore has the two standard rank-one elements. Choose the sign of the image under $v$ of the standard coroot so that its pairing with $\alpha$ is $2$. On $T_1$ conjugation by $v(n_\alpha)$ acts by inversion, and on the central torus it acts trivially. These two subtori generate $T$ up to finite intersection, so the rank-one reflection formula holds on $X(T)\otimes\mathbb Q$, and hence on $X(T)$: $s_\alpha(x)=x-\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$. This formula determines the pairing of $\alpha^\vee$ with every character, proving its uniqueness in $X_*(T)$. This proves (c). [F2, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For (d): each root space $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ is contained in $\operatorname{Lie}(T\cdot U_\alpha)$, so $\operatorname{Lie}$ of the subgroup $H$ generated by $T$ and the $U_\alpha$ contains $\mathfrak t$ and every root space, i.e. all of $\mathfrak g$; since $H$ is smooth (a generated subgroup of a reductive group by smooth subgroups is smooth, by the same generation criterion [F2]) and $G$ is connected, $H=G$ by the Lie-generation criterion [F2]. For (e): (rd1) is (c), (rd2) follows on roots from conjugation by $n_\alpha$, which sends $U_\beta$ to $U_{s_\alpha\beta}$; the uniqueness characterization of each coroot in(c) also sends $\beta^\vee$ to $(s_\alpha\beta)^\vee$ under the dual conjugation action, giving the required coroot-reflection invariance, and (rd3) holds because $W(G,T)$ is finite (it is a quotient of the normalizer of a maximal torus, finite over $k$); the reflection subgroup acts simply transitively on the Weyl chambers, while $W(G,T)$ acts faithfully and simply transitively on the Borel subgroups containing $T$. The Borel–chamber bijection identifies these two sets, so the reflection subgroup has the same order as $W(G,T)$ and equals it (Milne's Weyl identification theorem). [F2, F1, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 For (f), choose a cocharacter strictly positive on $\Phi^+$. Its conjugation contracts $U=B_u$ and each positive root group to the identity. For any ordering, multiplication $\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}U_\alpha\to U$ is equivariant and its differential at the identity is the direct-sum isomorphism of the positive root spaces. The strictly contracting smooth affine comparison in [F3] therefore makes it a global variety isomorphism; this is the argument of [F4] and uses no commutativity of the root groups. If $H\subseteq U$ is smooth and $T$-stable, the root-group containment criterion puts precisely the root groups corresponding to the weights of $\operatorname{Lie}H$ in $H^\circ$. The same contracting argument makes their ordered product isomorphic to $H^\circ$. To prove $H=H^\circ$, order those groups first in the coordinates of $U$. Those coordinates decompose $H$ as $H^\circ$ times a finite residual locus whose points have only the omitted root coordinates. The connected torus acts trivially on this finite component set, so that residual locus is fixed by $T$. But $U^T=U\cap C_G(T)=U\cap T=1$. Thus $H$ is connected and is exactly the ordered product of its root groups, proving the asserted subgroup description. [F3, F4, F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $\Psi\subseteq\Phi^+$ be the weights of such an $H$. The Chevalley formula in [F4] and uniqueness of ordered root coordinates show that if $\alpha,\beta\in\Psi$ and $N_{\alpha,\beta;i,j}$ is nonzero in $k$, the root $i\alpha+j\beta$ must be in $\Psi$: the corresponding coordinate of the commutator is a nonzero polynomial in $x,y$ and cannot be an omitted coordinate of $H$. Conversely, for quasi-closed $\Psi$, take the coordinate subvariety $H=\prod_{\alpha\in\Psi}U_\alpha$ in the coordinates of $U$. Collecting products and inverses using [F4] creates only root coordinates still in $\Psi$, so $H$ is a closed $T$-stable subgroup. Its product coordinates make it smooth connected with precisely those weights. Ordinary root closure implies quasi-closure by the root-string combinatorics. If $\operatorname{char}k=0$ or $\operatorname{char}k=p>3$, every structure coefficient that occurs in [F4] remains nonzero; in particular a root sum of two members of $\Psi$ must remain in $\Psi$. Thus quasi-closure and ordinary root closure coincide in those characteristics. This proves the corrected criterion. [F4, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
