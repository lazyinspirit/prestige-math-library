## Statement

Assume AC. Fix a finite horizon $T\ge0$, standard-Borel spaces
$E_0,\ldots,E_T$ and $F_1,\ldots,F_T$, an initial probability measure
$\mu_0$ on $E_0$, and transition and observation probability kernels
$K_t:E_{t-1}\to E_t$ and $G_t:E_t\to F_t$ for $1\le t\le T$, as in
[[def-general-state-space-model-by-kernels]]. On the interleaved finite
product space
$$\mathcal Z_T=E_0\times E_1\times F_1\times\cdots\times E_T\times F_T,$$
there is a unique probability measure whose rectangle probabilities are the
ordered iterated kernel integrals
$$\mu_0(dx_0)\prod_{t=1}^T K_t(x_{t-1},dx_t)G_t(x_t,dy_t).$$
The product notation means successive integration in the displayed order.
For $T=0$, the assertion is the initial law $\mu_0$ on $E_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume AC and the finite-horizon spaces, initial law, and kernels
in the Statement.

[A1] AC asserts that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] A finite-horizon model consists of standard-Borel state and observation
spaces, an initial law, transition probability kernels, and observation
probability kernels. ([[def-general-state-space-model-by-kernels]])

[F2] A probability kernel has probability-measure sections and measurable
evaluations. ([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]])

[F3] Under AC, an initial probability measure and a sequence of probability
kernels on measurable spaces give a unique path-space probability whose
finite-prefix laws are their iterated integrals.
([[thm-ionescu-tulcea-construction-of-a-markov-chain]])

[F4] Every finite product of standard-Borel spaces, with the finite-product
sigma-algebra, is standard Borel.
([[lem-finite-products-of-standard-borel-spaces-are-standard-borel]])

[F5] The finite-product sigma-algebra is generated by its measurable
rectangles. ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]])

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 If $T=0$, then $\mathcal Z_0=E_0$ and $\mu_0$ is the unique asserted law. For $T\ge1$, interleave the coordinate spaces as $D_0=E_0$, $D_{2t-1}=E_t$, and $D_{2t}=F_t$ for $1\le t\le T$. At the transition to $E_t$, define the lifted kernel by $L_{2t-2}(h,A)=K_t(\pi_{t-1}(h),A)$, where $\pi_{t-1}$ selects the $E_{t-1}$ coordinate of the preceding history $h$; at the next transition define $L_{2t-1}(h,B)=G_t(\pi_t(h),B)$, where $\pi_t$ selects the newly adjoined $E_t$ coordinate. These projections are measurable because inverse images of measurable sets are product cylinders. Each lifted section is a probability measure, and each evaluation is measurable by composition, so [F2] makes every lift a probability kernel. After coordinate $D_{2T}$, append singleton spaces with their unit point-mass kernels. [F1, F2, F5, given, construct]

2.1 By [A1] and [F3], Ionescu–Tulcea gives a unique probability measure on the countable interleaved product with these lifted kernels. Its marginal on the first $2T+1$ coordinates is a probability measure on $\mathcal Z_T$; the prescribed prefix formula is exactly the displayed iterated integral. The finite product is standard Borel by [F4]. [A1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Suppose $Q_T$ is another probability measure on $\mathcal Z_T$ with the same displayed rectangle probabilities. Append the singleton tail to the coordinate tuple by a measurable map and push $Q_T$ forward to the countable product. For any shorter prefix, set all later coordinate sets in the rectangle formula to their whole spaces; the later probability kernels integrate to one, so each shorter marginal is the prescribed iterated integral. Prefixes after $D_{2T}$ add only deterministic singleton coordinates. Ionescu–Tulcea uniqueness [F3] identifies this extension with the measure in step 2.1, so their finite-prefix marginals agree and $Q_T$ equals that marginal. For $T=0$, the formula already fixes $Q_0=\mu_0$. [A1, F3, F5, step 2.1] ∎
