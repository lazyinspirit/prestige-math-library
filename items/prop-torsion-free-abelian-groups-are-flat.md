## Statement

Every torsion-free abelian group is a flat $\mathbb Z$-module.

## Proof

**Given:** a torsion-free abelian group $M$.

1.1 First every subgroup $K\le\mathbb Z^r$ is finite free, by induction on the finite integer $r$. The case $r=0$ is immediate. Project $K$ to the last coordinate. If its image is zero, apply induction in $\mathbb Z^{r-1}$. Otherwise let $d$ be the least positive integer in the image, using [[thm-well-ordering-principle]]. Division with remainder by $d$, from [[thm-division-algorithm-in-z]], shows that every image element is a multiple of $d$: its remainder also lies in the image and cannot be positive and smaller than $d$. Choose one $u\in K$ with last coordinate $d$. Subtracting integer multiples of $u$ gives $K=\mathbb Zu\oplus\ker(\pi|_K)$, and the kernel is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z^{r-1}$, so induction completes the proof. These are finitely many existential choices, not a choice function on an infinite family. [given, algebra]

1.2 Let $f:A\to B$ be injective and $z=\sum_{i=1}^ta_i\otimes m_i\in A\otimes M$ have zero image in $B\otimes M$. By [[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], this equality to zero has a finite witness: the formal sum $\sum_i e_{(f(a_i),m_i)}$ is a finite integer linear combination of defining additivity and balancing relations in the free abelian group on $B\times M$. [given, algebra]

2.1 For any subgroup $H\le M$ generated by a finite list $x_1,\ldots,x_v$, argue as follows. Choose a maximal integer-linearly-independent sublist $b_1,\ldots,b_r$; there are only finitely many sublists. For each $x_i$, maximality supplies an equation $d_i x_i=\sum_j a_{ij}b_j$ with $d_i$ a positive integer: if $x_i$ is not already on the sublist, a dependence among it and the $b_j$ has nonzero coefficient at $x_i$, since the $b_j$ are independent. For a member of the sublist use $d_i=1$. The finitely many equations give $dH\subseteq G=\sum_j\mathbb Zb_j$ for $d=\prod_i d_i>0$, with empty product $1$. Multiplication by $d$ is injective on the torsion-free $H$, and $G\cong\mathbb Z^r$. Thus $H\cong dH$ is finite free by step 1.1. This also covers $H=0$ and $r=0$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For one finite basis $h_1,\ldots,h_s$ of $H$ and any abelian group $T$, the relations of [[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]] identify $T\otimes H$ with $T^s$: $a\otimes\sum_jn_jh_j\mapsto(n_ja)_j$, with inverse $(a_j)\mapsto\sum_ja_j\otimes h_j$. The composites are identities on elementary tensors and tuples. Therefore tensoring an injection with $H$ is the corresponding coordinatewise injection. No simultaneous choice of bases for all finitely generated subgroups is made. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $H$ be generated by all the elements of $M$ appearing in the original sum and in this finite witness. Every relation in the witness is then a valid relation in the tensor presentation for $B\otimes H$. Consequently the same sum $z_H\in A\otimes H$ maps to zero in $B\otimes H$. Step 3.1 implies $z_H=0$, so its image $z$ in $A\otimes M$ is zero. [step 3.1, step 1.2, algebra]

5.1 Thus $-\otimes M$ preserves injections. It is right exact by [[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]], whose commutative-ring hypothesis holds for $\mathbb Z$. It therefore preserves every short exact sequence and is exact, so $M$ is flat by [[def-left-and-right-flat-modules-over-an-arbitrary-ring]]. The finite-rank and finite-witness arguments require no choice axiom. [step 4.1, algebra] ∎
