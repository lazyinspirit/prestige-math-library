## Statement

Let $X$ be an integral scheme ([[def-integral-scheme]]), let $\mathcal L$ be
an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]) and let
$s\in\Gamma(X,K_X(\mathcal L))$ be a rational section, i.e. a nonzero
meromorphic section of $\mathcal L$
([[def-rational-section-line-bundle]]). Then:

1. (the divisor) the coefficients of $s$ in any trivialization glue to a
   well-defined Cartier divisor $\operatorname{div}_C(s)$ on $X$
   ([[def-cartier-divisor]]);
2. (the pair) there is a canonical isomorphism
   $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(\operatorname{div}_C(s))\to\mathcal L$ of
   $\mathcal O_X$-modules carrying the canonical rational section $1_D$ of
   $\mathcal O_X(D)$, $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$, to $s$; hence
   $(\mathcal L,s)\cong(\mathcal O_X(D),1_D)$
   ([[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]],
   [[lem-cartier-divisor-sheaf-invertible]]);
3. (surjectivity) conversely, for every Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ the
   canonical section $1_D$ is a rational section of the invertible sheaf
   $\mathcal O_X(D)$ and $\operatorname{div}_C(1_D)=D$;
4. (the equivalence relation) for two rational sections $s,s'$ one has
   $\operatorname{div}_C(s)=\operatorname{div}_C(s')$ if and only if there is
   an isomorphism $\psi:\mathcal L\to\mathcal L'$ of invertible sheaves with
   $\psi(s)=s'$; thus $\operatorname{div}_C$ induces a bijection from
   isomorphism classes of pairs $(\mathcal L,s)$ to Cartier divisors on $X$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integral scheme $X$ with generic point $\eta$ and function field $K(X)=\mathcal O_{X,\eta}$ ([[def-integral-scheme]]), an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal L$, and a nonzero meromorphic section $s\in\Gamma(X,K_X(\mathcal L))$, $\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L)=\mathcal L \otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal K_X$ ([[def-rational-section-line-bundle]], [[def-sheaf-tensor-product]]).

[F1] On the integral scheme $X$ the sheaf $\mathcal K_X$ is the constant sheaf with value $K(X)$, and $\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L)$ is the constant sheaf with value the stalk $\mathcal L_\eta$, a one-dimensional $K(X)$-vector space; every nonempty open subset of $X$ contains $\eta$, is irreducible and connected, and a rational section is a nonzero element of that one-dimensional space, equal to a $K(X)$-multiple of the germ of any chosen local generator ([[def-rational-section-line-bundle]], [[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]], [[def-integral-scheme]]).

[F2] $\mathcal L$ is invertible, i.e. locally free of rank one: for every $x\in X$ there is an open neighbourhood $U$ and a generator $e\in\mathcal L(U)$ such that $\mathcal O_U\to\mathcal L|_U$, $a\mapsto ae$, is an isomorphism; if two sections generate $\mathcal L$ on a common nonempty open $W$, then each is a unit multiple of the other, and the unit is a unit of $\mathcal O_X(W)$ ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F3] A Cartier divisor on $X$ is a global section of $\mathcal K_X^{\times}/\mathcal O_X^{\times}$; a family of meromorphic units $f_i\in\mathcal K_X(U_i)^{\times}$ with $f_i/f_j\in\mathcal O_X(U_i\cap U_j)^{\times}$ glues along the cover to such a global section, and refining the cover or replacing the $f_i$ by unit multiples does not change it ([[def-cartier-divisor]]).

[F4] For a Cartier divisor $D$ with local datum $(U_i,f_i)$ the sheaf $\mathcal O_X(D)\subseteq\mathcal K_X$ satisfies $\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}$, is invertible and freely generated by $f_i^{-1}$; the constant meromorphic function $1$ is a global section of $\mathcal K_X(\mathcal O_X(D))$, the canonical rational section $1_D$, with $1_D|_{U_i}=f_i\cdot f_i^{-1}$ ([[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]], [[lem-cartier-divisor-sheaf-invertible]]).

[F5] Sheaf sections and morphisms that agree on an open cover glue uniquely ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]]).

[F6] A Cartier divisor is effective exactly when its local equations lie in $\mathcal O_X$ and their germs act injectively by multiplication; this condition is unchanged by multiplying equations by regular units ([[def-effective-cartier-divisor]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Coefficients of a rational section.** Let $\mathcal A$ be the set of all pairs $(U,e)$ with $U\subseteq X$ a nonempty open subset and $e\in\mathcal L(U)$ a generator of $\mathcal L$ over $U$; by [F2] every point of $X$ lies in the first component of a member of $\mathcal A$, so these pairs cover $X$, and no choice is used because $\mathcal A$ is determined by a formula. By [F1] the sheaf $\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L)$ is the constant sheaf with value the one-dimensional $K(X)$-vector space $\mathcal L_\eta$ and the sections over the connected open $U$ are exactly $\mathcal L_\eta$, so for a pair $(U,e)$ the germ $e_\eta$ is a nonzero vector and there is a unique $f\in K(X)$ with $s|_U=fe$; since $s\neq0$, this $f$ lies in $K(X)^{\times}$. Write $f=f_{(U,e)}$ for this coefficient. [F1, F2]

2.1 **Unit ratios.** Let $(U,e),(V,e')$ be pairs of $\mathcal A$ with nonempty intersection $W$. Both $e|_W$ and $e'|_W$ generate $\mathcal L|_W$, so $e|_W=u\,e'|_W$ for a unit $u\in\mathcal O_X(W)^{\times}$ by [F2]. Restricting the identities $s|_U=f_{(U,e)}e$ and $s|_V=f_{(V,e')}e'$ to $W$ and substituting gives $f_{(U,e)}u\,e'|_W=f_{(V,e')}e'|_W$ in the one-dimensional $K(X)$-vector space $\mathcal L_\eta$ of [F1]; since the germ $e'_\eta$ is nonzero, $f_{(U,e)}u=f_{(V,e')}$ and hence $f_{(U,e)}/f_{(V,e')}=u^{-1}\in\mathcal O_X(W)^{\times}$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 **Every Cartier divisor arises.** Let $D$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$ with local datum $(U_i,f_i)$ consisting of meromorphic units with unit ratios on overlaps [F3]. Since $X$ is integral each $f_i$ lies in $K(X)^{\times}$ and, by [F4], $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is invertible with generator $f_i^{-1}$ on $U_i$ and canonical rational section $1_D$ satisfying $1_D|_{U_i}=f_i\cdot f_i^{-1}$. The coefficient of $1_D$ in the trivialization $f_i^{-1}$ is therefore $f_i$, so $\operatorname{div}_C(1_D)$ is represented by the same datum $(U_i,f_i)$ and equals $D$ by [F3]; in particular $1_D\neq0$ is a rational section and the construction is surjective onto $\operatorname{CaDiv}(X)$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 **The Cartier divisor.** The coefficients $f_{(U,e)}\in\mathcal K_X(U)^{\times}=K(X)^{\times}$ have unit ratios on overlaps by step 2.1, so by [F3] they glue to a global section $\operatorname{div}_C(s)\in\operatorname{CaDiv}(X)$, and step 2.1 shows moreover that any two pairs give local equations differing by a unit, so the class is independent of all choices. [F3, step 2.1]

3.2 **The local isomorphisms.** Fix a pair $(U,e)\in\mathcal A$ and put $f=f_{(U,e)}$. By [F4] the sheaf $\mathcal O_X(D)$, $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$, is freely generated by $f^{-1}$ over $U$, so there is a unique $\mathcal O_U$-linear map $\varphi_{(U,e)}:\mathcal O_X(D)|_U\to\mathcal L|_U$ with $\varphi_{(U,e)}(f^{-1})=e$; because it carries a generator to a generator, it is an isomorphism. Let $(V,e')$ be a second pair with coefficient $h=f_{(V,e')}$ and put $W=U\cap V$; by step 2.1 we have $h=fu$ and $e=ue'$ there for a unit $u$, so $f^{-1}=uh^{-1}$ and $\varphi_{(U,e)}(h^{-1})=\varphi_{(U,e)}(u^{-1}f^{-1})=u^{-1}e=e'=\varphi_{(V,e')}(h^{-1})$; the maps agree on the generator $h^{-1}$ of $\mathcal O_X(D)|_W$, hence agree on all sections. [F4, step 2.1]

4.1 **Gluing.** By step 3.2 the isomorphisms $\varphi_{(U,e)}$ agree on all intersections, so by [F5] they glue to an $\mathcal O_X$-linear morphism $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L$, which is an isomorphism because it restricts to an isomorphism on each member of the cover. [F5, step 3.2]

4.2 **Global sections and effectivity.** The inclusion $\mathcal O_X\hookrightarrow\mathcal K_X$ induces an inclusion $\mathcal L\hookrightarrow\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L)$, as seen in any frame. Thus $s$ belongs to $\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$ exactly when every frame coefficient $f_{(U,e)}$ belongs to $\mathcal O_X(U)$: these local sections then glue by [F5]. Each such coefficient has a nonzero germ at every point of $U$, since a zero germ would make it vanish on a nonempty open containing $\eta$, contrary to $f_{(U,e)}\neq0$ in $K(X)$. The local rings are domains, so multiplication by these germs is injective. By [F6], this is equivalent to $D=\operatorname{div}_C(s)$ being effective. Conversely, any effective local equation differs from a frame coefficient by a regular unit, so all frame coefficients lie in $\mathcal O_X$ and $s$ is a global section of $\mathcal L$. [F1, F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 **The canonical section maps to $s$.** The constant meromorphic function $1$ is the rational section $1_D$ of $\mathcal O_X(D)$ with $1_D|_U=f\cdot f^{-1}$ for every pair $(U,e)$ by [F4]. Under the induced map $\mathcal K_X(\varphi)$ its restriction to $U$ maps to $\mathcal K_X(\varphi_{(U,e)})(ff^{-1})=f\varphi_{(U,e)}(f^{-1})=fe=s|_U$ by step 3.2, so $\mathcal K_X(\varphi)(1_D)=s$ by the uniqueness in [F5]. Hence $(\mathcal L,s)\cong(\mathcal O_X(D),1_D)$, which proves 2. [F4, F5, step 4.1]

6.1 **Pair isomorphism and equality of divisors.** Let $s\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L))$ and $s'\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L'))$ be rational sections with $\operatorname{div}_C(s)=D$ and $\operatorname{div}_C(s')=D'$. If $D=D'$, steps 3.2, 4.1 and 5.1 produce isomorphisms $\varphi:\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L$ with $\varphi(1_D)=s$ and $\varphi':\mathcal O_X(D)\to\mathcal L'$ with $\varphi'(1_D)=s'$, so $\psi=\varphi'\circ\varphi^{-1}:\mathcal L\to\mathcal L'$ is an isomorphism with $\psi(s)=s'$. Conversely, if $\psi:\mathcal L\to\mathcal L'$ is an isomorphism with $\psi(s)=s'$ and $(U,e)$ is a pair for $\mathcal L$ with coefficient $f$, then $(U,\psi(e))$ is a pair for $\mathcal L'$ and $\psi(s)|_U=\psi(fe)=f\psi(e)$ shows that its coefficient is again $f$; hence the two families of local equations define the same Cartier divisor, that is, $\operatorname{div}_C(s)=\operatorname{div}_C(s')$. Therefore $\operatorname{div}_C$ is a bijection from isomorphism classes of pairs to Cartier divisors. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1]

7.1 **Conclusion.** Assertion 1 is step 3.1, assertion 2 is steps 3.2, 4.1 and 5.1, assertion 3 is step 2.2, and assertion 4 is step 6.1; this proves the theorem. [step 3.1, step 5.1, step 2.2, step 6.1] ∎

No choice principle is used: the family of pairs $(U,e)$ is determined by a formula, the coefficients are uniquely determined by $s$ and $e$, and the gluing maps are the unique maps on free generators. For $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_X$ and $s=1$ the coefficient is $1$ on every chart, so $\operatorname{div}_C(1)=0$ and $\varphi$ is the identity isomorphism $\mathcal O_X(0)=\mathcal O_X$. More generally for a principal rational function $s=g\in K(X)^{\times}$ on $\mathcal L=\mathcal O_X$ the divisor $\operatorname{div}_C(g)$ is the principal Cartier divisor of $g$. For example, on $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ and $D=-[0]$, $\mathcal O_X(D)=t\mathcal O_X$ and $1_D=1$ is rational but is not a global section of that sheaf. By step 4.2, $s$ belongs to $\Gamma(X,\mathcal L)$ exactly when $D$ is effective. This global-section condition is stronger than being a regular meromorphic section in the terminology of [[def-rational-section-line-bundle]], where “regular” means nonzero; the present theorem allows arbitrary poles. The scheme $X$ is integral, hence nonempty, so there is no empty case; and no Noetherian, normal or separatedness hypothesis is needed, since only the constant-sheaf description of $\mathcal K_X(\mathcal L)$ and the local description of $\mathcal O_X(D)$ are used.
