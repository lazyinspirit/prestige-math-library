## Example

Let $W=S_3=\langle s_1,s_2\mid s_1^2=s_2^2=1,\ s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2\rangle$, let $J=\{s_1\}$, and let $R=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$ with equal parameter $v$. Write $a=v-v^{-1}$ and let $H$ be the generic Hecke algebra with $T_i^2=aT_i+1$ and $T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2$. Let $M=Rm$ be the rank-one left $H_J$-module with $T_1m=vm$.

The two coset conventions give different displayed representative sets. The right cosets $W_Jd$ have minimal representatives $D_R(J)=\{1,s_2,s_2s_1\}$, and the left cosets $dW_J$ have minimal representatives $D_L(J)=\{1,s_2,s_1s_2\}$. Accordingly,
$$
H=H_J\oplus H_JT_2\oplus H_JT_2T_1\quad\text{as a left }H_J\text{-module}.
$$
$$
H=H_J\oplus T_2H_J\oplus T_1T_2H_J\quad\text{as a right }H_J\text{-module}.
$$

For $H\otimes_{H_J}M$, use the right-module basis and put
$$
b_0=1\otimes m,\qquad b_1=T_2\otimes m,\qquad b_2=T_1T_2\otimes m.
$$
The induced left $H$-action has matrices, in the ordered basis $(b_0,b_1,b_2)$,
$$
[T_1]=\begin{pmatrix}v&0&0\\0&0&1\\0&1&a\end{pmatrix},\qquad [T_2]=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\1&a&0\\0&0&v\end{pmatrix}.
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The type-A2 Coxeter system and universal equal-parameter ring above, the rank-one parabolic $J=\{s_1\}$, and the left $H_J$-module $M=Rm$ with $T_1m=vm$.

[F1] $H_J$ is the embedded subalgebra generated by $T_1$, and induction is the balanced tensor product $H\otimes_{H_J}M$. ([[def-hh-parabolic-hecke-subalgebra-and-induced-module]])

[F2] For the right $H_J$-module decomposition, the induced basis is $\{T_d\otimes m:d\in D_L(J)\}$ when $M$ has basis $\{m\}$. ([[thm-hh-parabolic-hecke-freeness-and-induced-bases]])

[F3] The Hecke generators satisfy $T_i^2=(v-v^{-1})T_i+1$ and the type-A2 braid relation $T_1T_2T_1=T_2T_1T_2$. ([[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]])

[F4] The elements $T_w$ form an $R$-basis of $H$, indexed by $w\in W$. ([[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]])

## Verification

**Given:** The data in Facts & Assumptions.

1.1 The six elements of $S_3$ are $1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,w_0=s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$, with lengths $0,1,1,2,2,3$. Multiplication by $s_1$ groups them into the right cosets $W_J$, $W_Js_2$, $W_Js_2s_1$, so the right-coset minimal representatives are $D_R(J)=\{1,s_2,s_2s_1\}$. Multiplication on the right by $s_1$ groups them into the left cosets $W_J$, $s_2W_J$, $s_1s_2W_J$, so the left-coset minimal representatives are $D_L(J)=\{1,s_2,s_1s_2\}$. The two direct sums in the Example follow from the two sides of [F2]. [F2, F4]

1.2 The action $T_1m=vm$ is well defined because $(v-v)(v+v^{-1})m=0$. By [F2], $H\otimes_{H_J}M$ has basis $(b_0,b_1,b_2)$. The balanced relation moves $T_1$ from the first tensor factor to $M$, so $T_1b_0=vb_0$; also $T_1b_1=b_2$ and $T_1b_2=T_1^2T_2\otimes m=b_1+ab_2$ by [F3]. For the other generator, $T_2b_0=b_1$, $T_2b_1=T_2^2\otimes m=b_0+ab_1$, and $T_2b_2=T_2T_1T_2\otimes m=T_1T_2T_1\otimes m=vb_2$, using the braid relation and the same balance. These six images give exactly the two displayed matrices. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The non-scalar $2\times2$ block in each matrix is $C=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&a\end{pmatrix}$. Direct multiplication gives $C^2=I+aC$, while the scalar block $v$ satisfies $v^2=1+av$ because $a=v-v^{-1}$. Hence both matrices satisfy $T_i^2=1+aT_i$. [F3, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 On $b_0$, both $T_1T_2T_1$ and $T_2T_1T_2$ give $vb_2$; on $b_1$, both give $vb_1+avb_2$. On $b_2$, the first gives $vb_0+avb_1+(a+a^2v)b_2$, while the second gives $vb_0+avb_1+av^2b_2$. These agree because $a+a^2v=a(1+av)=av^2$. Thus the matrices satisfy the braid relation on a basis, confirming the calculated action. [F3, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
