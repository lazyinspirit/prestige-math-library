## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $G$ be a smooth connected commutative $k$-group in an exact sequence $0\to T\to G\to B\to0$, with $T$ a torus and $B$ an abelian variety. If $H\subseteq G$ is a positive-dimensional smooth connected closed subgroup and $n\ge2$ is prime to $\operatorname{char}k$, then $H(k)$ contains a nonidentity point killed by $n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, DC, $k,G,T,B,H,n$ as stated.

[A1] AC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] A connected smooth group over a perfect field is an extension of an abelian variety by a smooth connected affine normal subgroup. [[thm-barsotti-chevalley-perfect-field-group-variety]]

[F2] The image of a homomorphism of finite-type group schemes is a closed group scheme, and an affine, smooth, connected source has an affine, smooth, connected image. [[lem-nonaffine-group-image-exact-quotient-properties]]

[F3] A smooth connected group over an algebraically closed field is integral; a proper integral affine scheme over that field is a point. [[lem-nonaffine-connected-group-geometrically-connected]], [[lem-proper-geometrically-integral-affine-scheme-is-point]]

[F4] On an abelian variety of positive dimension $g$, the kernel of multiplication by an integer $n$ invertible in $k$ is finite étale of rank $n^{2g}$. [[thm-nonaffine-abelian-multiplication-finite-faithfully-flat]], [[lem-arith-prime-to-characteristic-multiplication-etale]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify the affine part of $H$ inside the ambient torus.

1.1 By F1, $H$ has a smooth connected affine normal subgroup $N$ with abelian quotient $C=H/N$. Compose $N\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow G$ with $G\to B$. Its image is a closed, affine, smooth connected subgroup of the proper $B$ by F2. It is proper as a closed subscheme of $B$ and integral by F3, so F3 makes it a point. Hence $N$ is contained scheme-theoretically in the kernel $T$ of $G\to B$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Over $k$, choose a torus splitting $T\cong\mathbf G_m^r$. Since $N\subseteq T$ is closed, its coordinate Hopf algebra is a quotient of $k[\mathbb Z^r]$, generated by the images of the Laurent monomials. These images are group-like. Distinct group-like elements in a coalgebra are linearly independent: a shortest linear dependence, after applying the coproduct and subtracting its tensor product with one selected group-like element, gives a shorter dependence. Therefore the quotient identifies precisely those monomials whose images agree, and it is the group algebra $k[M]$ of a finitely generated quotient $M$ of $\mathbb Z^r$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The smooth connected group $N$ is integral by F3, so $k[M]$ is a domain. If $M$ had an element $m$ of finite order $d>1$, the nonzero elements $e_m-1$ and $1+e_m+\cdots+e_m^{d-1}$ would have product zero; the second is nonzero because the group-algebra monomials are a basis, including in positive characteristic. Thus $M$ is torsion-free, and finite generation gives $M\cong\mathbb Z^t$. Consequently $N\cong\mathbf G_m^t$. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $t>0$, choose a primitive $n$-th root of unity in $k$ and put it in one torus coordinate; this is a nonidentity point of $N(k)\subseteq H(k)$ killed by $n$. If $t=0$, then $N$ is trivial and $H\cong C$ is an abelian variety of positive dimension. By F4, its $n$-torsion has rank $n^{2\dim H}>1$ and is finite étale; all its geometric points are $k$-rational, so one is nonidentity. Either case proves the claim. [A1, F4, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
