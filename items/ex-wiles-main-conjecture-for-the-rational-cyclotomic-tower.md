## Example

Assume AC, Countable Choice and Dependent Choice for the current tower and pseudomeasure interfaces. Take $F=\mathbb Q$ and $p=5$. Then $\Delta=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\mu_5)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times$ has order $4$, the cyclotomic pro-$5$ group is generated by the element $\gamma$ with cyclotomic character $\chi(\gamma)=6$, and the odd index $i=1$ selects the even branch $\omega^2$. The character-form main-conjecture statement specializes to
$$\operatorname{char}_{\mathbb Z_5[\![T]\!]}(X_\infty^{(-1)})=W_{\omega^2}(T)\mathbb Z_5[\![T]\!],$$
where $T$ is determined by $\gamma\mapsto1+T$ and $W_{\omega^2}$ is normalized by the $p$-adic zeta branch for $\mathbb Q$ at $p=5$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** Finite-layer class-field control and a unit interpolation value.

1.1 Put K₀=Q(ζ₅). Its degree is 4 and signature (0,2). By [F4], its discriminant has absolute value 5³=125. The Minkowski bound of [F5] is (4/π)²(4!/4⁴)√125. Since π>3 and √125<12, it is strictly less than (16/9)(3/32)12=2. Every ideal class therefore contains an integral ideal of positive integer norm <2, hence norm 1, and is trivial. Thus Cl(O_{K₀})=0. The inequality π>3 follows, for example, by comparing a unit circle with its inscribed regular hexagon. [F4, F5, F10, given, algebra]

2.1 Let K_m=Q(ζ_{5^(m+1)}). The translated cyclotomic polynomial Φ_{5^(m+1)}(1+T) is Eisenstein at 5, since its constant term is 5 and modulo 5 it is a pure positive power of T. Thus 5 is totally ramified throughout Q₅(ζ_{5^(m+1)})/Q₅, and it is the unique prime over 5. The degree of K_m/K₀ is 5^m by [F2]; this unique prime is totally ramified in that cyclic extension. Every other finite prime is unramified in the cyclotomic tower: for ℓ≠5, the roots of T^(5^(m+1))−1 are distinct modulo ℓ and lift in an unramified residue extension. The fields are totally imaginary. [F2, F4, given, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 For any finite everywhere-unramified abelian 5-extension L/K_m, local unit norms are onto at every finite place by [F14]. Under AC choose local unit preimages simultaneously; together they form an idelic norm, while complex-place norms are onto. Global Artin [F15] kills those unit ideles, so Gal(L/K_m) is a quotient of the ordinary ideal class group. That class group is finite by [F6], so the degrees of such L are bounded. Their finite composita remain unramified, abelian and of 5-power degree. A compositum of maximal degree therefore contains every such L; call it B_m and put A_m=Gal(B_m/K_m). In particular A₀=0 because Cl(O_{K₀})=0. The extension B_m/K₀ is Galois since K_m/K₀ is Galois and the defining maximal unramified extension is canonical. At a prime over 5, [F7] maps inertia onto Gal(K_m/K₀) and its kernel is the trivial inertia of B_m/K_m. Thus inertia is a cyclic subgroup I isomorphic to Gal(K_m/K₀) and splits Gal(B_m/K₀)=A_m⋊I. Killing the normal closure of I gives A_m/(σ−1)A_m, where σ is a generator of Gal(K_m/K₀). This quotient is an abelian 5-extension of K₀ unramified at the unique prime over 5 and at all other finite and infinite places, by step 2.1 and conjugacy of inertia over one base prime. The Artin description [F6] makes it a quotient of A₀, hence zero by step 1.1. [F6, F7, F14, F15, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Since σ has order 5^m, in the group ring modulo 5 we have (σ−1)^(5^m)=σ^(5^m)−1=0. If A_m=(σ−1)A_m, the same equality holds modulo 5 and iteration of this nilpotent operator gives A_m/5A_m=0. The finite 5-group A_m then satisfies A_m=5A_m=5²A_m=⋯=0. This also covers m=0 directly. Hence all A_m vanish. Each finite-layer unramified abelian 5-extension is then trivial by the construction in step 3.1; by [F13] their union is the maximal one over the full tower. Thus X∞ and its ω^(−1) component vanish by [F3]. The characteristic ideal of the zero module is the empty product, namely Z₅[[T]]. [F3, F6, F13, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By [F8], the Kubota–Leopoldt pseudomeasure on Z₅^× has moment (1−5^(k−1))ζ(1−k) for every even k≥2. Push it along Z₅^×→Z₅^×/{±1}=G. To perform this in the total quotient algebras, represent the source pseudomeasure as its product with 1−[6] divided by that regular element; its image has the same regular denominator in Z₅[[G]]. Products by group differences remain integral after pushforward. The resulting G-pseudomeasure has the same even moments. It is the unique pseudomeasure with those moments: multiply the trivial finite-character component by γ−1 and leave the nontrivial component unchanged; both are integral power series. In either even residue class modulo 4, the even exponents j+4·5^r converge 5-adically and their γ-values 6^(j+4·5^r)−1 converge to 6^j−1 inside 5Z₅. A nonzero 5-adic power series cannot vanish at infinitely many distinct points converging inside that disk: translate to the limit and use the lowest nonzero coefficient. Hence the two integral components are determined by the moments. This identifies the pushed pseudomeasure with the normalized μ_{Q,5} used in the Example, without invoking the general Wiles equality. [F1, F8, F11, construct, algebra, step 4.1]

6.1 Let η=ω², the nontrivial character of the order-two real finite factor, and let d be its nontrivial element. Since (1−[d])μ is integral and η(d)=−1, its η-component μ_η is integral because 2 is a 5-adic unit. The normalized branch is W_η(T)=μ_η(6/(1+T)−1): evaluation at T=6^(−1)−1 gives the weight-2 moment. By [F8]–[F9], that value is (1−5)ζ(−1)=1/3, a 5-adic unit. Since 6^(−1)−1 lies in 5Z₅, evaluating an integral series there has the same residue modulo 5 as its constant coefficient. Thus W_η(0) is a 5-adic unit and W_η(T) is a unit in Z₅[[T]]. Both sides of the displayed equality in the unchanged Example are therefore the unit ideal, by step 4.1. This proves the p=5 instance directly and does not use the general Wiles equality. [F1, F8, F9, F10, F11, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
