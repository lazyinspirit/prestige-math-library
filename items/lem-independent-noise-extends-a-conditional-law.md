## Statement

Let $X,Y,Z$ be random elements with values in measurable spaces
$(E,\mathcal E)$, $(H,\mathcal H)$, and $(W,\mathcal W)$. Suppose $Z$ is
independent of the random element $(X,Y)$, and let
$\kappa:H\to E$ be a supplied conditional law of $X$ given $Y$. For each
$h\in H$, form the product probability
$\Lambda(h,\cdot)=\kappa(h,\cdot)\otimes\mathcal L(Z)$ on
$\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal W$. Then $\Lambda$ is a conditional law of
$(X,Z)$ given $Y$. If $\Phi:E\times W\to V$ is measurable into a measurable
space $(V,\mathcal V)$, then
$$\Lambda^\Phi(h,C):=\Lambda(h,\Phi^{-1}(C))$$
is a conditional law of $\Phi(X,Z)$ given $Y$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Random elements $X,Y,Z$, independence of $Z$ from $(X,Y)$, and a
supplied conditional law $\kappa$ of $X$ given $Y$.

[F1] A conditional law given a random element is an everywhere probability
kernel whose composition with that random element is a regular conditional
distribution. [[def-conditional-law-given-a-random-element]].

[F2] A probability kernel has probability-measure sections and measurable
evaluations. [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]].

[F3] For probability measures, the product measure exists, has the rectangle
formula, and its values are the iterated section integrals.
[[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]].

[F4] For a finite kernel and a nonnegative product-measurable function, the
section integral is measurable in the kernel parameter.
[[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]].

[F5] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles.
[[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]].

[F6] Independence of random elements means independence of their generated
sigma-algebras. [[def-independent-random-elements]].

[F7] Independent sigma-algebras factor probabilities of their events.
[[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]].

[F8] A regular conditional distribution satisfies the conditional event
identities for every conditioning event and target event.
[[def-regular-conditional-distribution]].

[F9] A pi-system contained in a lambda-system extends to the generated
sigma-algebra. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]].

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Write $\mu=\mathcal L(Z)$. For every $h$, the product $\Lambda(h,\cdot)=\kappa(h,\cdot)\otimes\mu$ is a probability measure by [F2] and [F3]. For each $D\in\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal W$, the section $x\mapsto\mu(D_x)$ is measurable by [F4] applied to the constant probability kernel $\mu$ and the indicator of $D$. The iterated-section formula in [F3] gives $\Lambda(h,D)=\int_E\mu(D_x)\,\kappa(h,dx)$, which is measurable in $h$ by [F4] applied to $\kappa$. Thus $\Lambda$ is a probability kernel. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given]

1.2 Fix $C\in\sigma(Y)$ and a measurable rectangle $A\times B$. The conditional-law identity [F1], followed by independence [F6]–[F7], gives $\int_C\Lambda(Y,A\times B)\,d\mathbb P=\mu(B)\int_C\kappa(Y,A)\,d\mathbb P=\mu(B)\mathbb P(C\cap\{X\in A\})=\mathbb P(C\cap\{X\in A,Z\in B\})$. For this fixed $C$, both sides define finite measures on $\mathcal E\otimes\mathcal W$. They agree on the rectangle pi-system, which generates that sigma-algebra by [F5]; [F9] extends the equality to every measurable target set. Therefore $\Lambda(Y,\cdot)$ is a regular conditional distribution of $(X,Z)$ given $\sigma(Y)$ by [F8]. [F1, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, algebra]

2.1 For measurable $\Phi$, every section of $\Lambda^\Phi$ is the pushforward of the probability $\Lambda(h,\cdot)$, and its evaluations are measurable because $\Phi^{-1}(C)$ is measurable and $\Lambda$ is a probability kernel. Applying the conditional identity from step 1.2 to $\Phi^{-1}(C)$ proves that $\Lambda^\Phi(Y,\cdot)$ is a regular conditional distribution of $\Phi(X,Z)$ given $\sigma(Y)$. Hence it is the required conditional law. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct] ∎
