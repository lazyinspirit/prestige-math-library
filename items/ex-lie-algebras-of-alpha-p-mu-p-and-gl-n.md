## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the finite-type assertions inherited from the matrix-group supplier. Let $k$ be a field, let $n\ge1$, and let $\operatorname{Lie}$ be as in [[def-lie-algebra-of-a-group-scheme]]. (a) $\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_a)\cong k$ and $\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_m)\cong k$, generated by the functionals dual to the cotangent classes $[t]$ and $[t-1]$, respectively; the bracket is zero because these groups are abelian, so the Lie algebras are the one-dimensional abelian Lie algebras. (b) If $\operatorname{char}k=p>0$, then $\operatorname{Lie}(\alpha_p)\cong k$ and $\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_p)\cong k$ with generators dual to the corresponding cotangent classes, so there are isomorphisms of $k$-Lie algebras $\operatorname{Lie}(\alpha_p)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_a)$ and $\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_p)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_m)$. (c) $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\mathfrak{gl}_n=M_n(k)$, the isomorphism sending $X$ to $I+\varepsilon X$, and $[X,Y]=XY-YX$ ([[lem-lie-algebra-of-the-general-linear-group]], [[thm-lie-bracket-and-adjoint-action-from-infinitesimals]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a field $k$, an integer $n\ge1$ and, in part (b), an integer $p=\operatorname{char}k>0$.

[F1] [[ex-additive-and-infinitesimal-group-schemes]]: $\mathbf G_a=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$, $\mathbf G_m=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$, and in characteristic $p$ the group schemes $\alpha_p=\operatorname{Spec}k[t]/(t^p)$ and $\mu_p=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]/(t^p-1)$, whose coordinate rings are both isomorphic to $k[s]/(s^p)$, with $s=t$ respectively $s=t-1$; these groups are commutative.

[F2] [[thm-cotangent-space-maximal-ideal-quotient]] and [[def-lie-algebra-of-a-group-scheme]]: for a $k$-rational point $e$, $\operatorname{Lie}(G)=\operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak m_e/\mathfrak m_e^2,k)$ and $\mathfrak m_e/\mathfrak m_e^2\cong\Omega_{G/k}\otimes\kappa(e)$, a nonzero class $[u]$ with $\mathfrak m_e=(u)$ is a cotangent basis, whose dual functional generates the Lie algebra.

[F3] [[def-lie-algebra-over-a-field]]: a one-dimensional $k$-Lie algebra has zero bracket, since $[aX,bX]=ab[X,X]=0$ by bilinearity and alternation.

[F4] [[lem-lie-algebra-of-the-general-linear-group]] and [[thm-lie-bracket-and-adjoint-action-from-infinitesimals]]: $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\mathfrak{gl}_n$ via $X\mapsto I_n+\varepsilon X$, and the bracket is the matrix commutator $[X,Y]=XY-YX$.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Cotangent spaces and dual generators. At the identity of $\mathbf G_a$ and $\mathbf G_m$, respectively, the local rings are $k[t]_{(t)}$ and $k[t,t^{-1}]_{(t-1)}$, with maximal ideals generated by $t$ and $t-1$. Their quotients by the squares of these ideals are $k[u]/(u^2)$, with $u=t$ or $u=t-1$: any denominator outside the maximal ideal has a nonzero constant term and is invertible in this square-zero quotient. Thus $[t]$ and $[t-1]$ are cotangent bases. In characteristic $p\ge2$, both infinitesimal coordinate rings are $k[s]/(s^p)$ by [F1]; every element outside $(s)$ is a unit by a finite geometric sum, so this ring is already local. Its cotangent quotient $(s)/(s)^2$ has basis $[s]$, regardless of whether higher powers survive in the ring. By [F2] the Lie algebras are the duals of these one-dimensional cotangent spaces, generated by the functionals $u^*$ with $u^*([u])=1$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 The general linear group. By [F4] the identification $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\mathfrak{gl}_n=M_n(k)$ sends $X$ to $I+\varepsilon X$ and the bracket is $[X,Y]=XY-YX$. [F4, given]

2.1 Zero brackets and the isomorphisms. By the bracket construction in parts (a)-(d) of [[thm-lie-bracket-and-adjoint-action-from-infinitesimals]], with Choice inherited through its matrix-group and adjoint-representation suppliers, these tangent spaces carry Lie brackets. They are one-dimensional by step 1.1, so [F3] makes each bracket zero. Sending the dual generator $s^*$ of $\operatorname{Lie}(\alpha_p)$ to $t^*$ of $\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_a)$ is therefore a Lie-algebra isomorphism; likewise sending the dual generator for $s=t-1$ to $(t-1)^*$ gives $\operatorname{Lie}(\mu_p)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_m)$. Together with step 1.2 this proves (a), (b) and (c). [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
