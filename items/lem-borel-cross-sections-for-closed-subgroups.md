## Statement

Assume AC. Let $G$ be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff topological
group, $H\le G$ a closed subgroup, and $q:G\to G/H$ the quotient map. Then
there is a Borel cross-section $s:G/H\to G$ with $q\circ s=\mathrm{id}_{G/H}$,
and for every $g\in G$ there is a unique $h(g,x)\in H$ with
$g\,s(x)=s(gx)\,h(g,x)$, where $x\in G/H$; the map
$(g,x)\mapsto h(g,x)$ is Borel into $H$. Consequently the map
$G\to G/H\times H$, $g\mapsto(q(g),s(q(g))^{-1}g)$, is a Borel isomorphism onto
$G/H\times H$, and the quasi-invariant measure class on $G/H$ may be
transported to a $\sigma$-finite measure on $G$ along $s$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a second-countable LCH group $G$, a closed subgroup $H\le G$, and the quotient map $q:G\to G/H$.

[F1] $G$ and $G/H$ are Polish, $q$ is continuous and open, and $G/H$ is locally compact Hausdorff ([[lem-second-countable-lch-spaces-are-standard-borel]], [[lem-closed-subgroup-quotient-averaging-and-compact-lifts]]).

[F2] A Polish space carries a compatible complete metric $d$; convergent sequences, Cauchy sequences, closed sets, diameters and compactness are read in this metric ([[def-polish-space]], [[def-metric-convergence]], [[def-metric-bounded-diameter]], [[def-complete-metric-space]]).

[F3] $G$ has a countable base of relatively compact open sets, and for every point $g$ and open $U\ni g$ there is a base element $V$ with $g\in V\subseteq\overline V\subseteq U$ and compact closure, with $\operatorname{diam}V$ as small as prescribed ([[def-second-countable-space]], [[lem-a-locally-compact-hausdorff-space-has-a-base-of-open-sets-with-compact-closure]], [[def-locally-compact-space]], [[def-compactness-variants]]).

[F4] By [F1], $G$ and $G/H$ are Polish and hence standard Borel; their Borel structures are generated by their open sets ([[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[F5] The quotient map satisfies $q(gs)=gq(s)$ for all $g,s\in G$, and $H=\{g:q(g)=eH\}$ is the stabilizer of the identity coset; multiplication and inversion of $G$ are continuous, so composites of Borel maps with the group operations are Borel ([[def-coset]], [[def-topological-group]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[F6] Countable Choice, a consequence of the standing AC, selects one point from each member of a countable family of nonempty sets, and in particular fixes a point in each member of a countable base of $G$ ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-compactness-variants-hierarchy]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** construct.

**Given:** AC, the second-countable LCH group $G$, the closed subgroup $H$, and the quotient map $q$.

1.1 Fix a compatible complete metric $d$ on $G$ by [F2]. By second countability and [F3], choose a countable base $(V_k)_{k\in\mathbb N}$ of relatively compact open sets such that for every open $U\subseteq G$, every $g\in U$, and every $\epsilon>0$ there is a $V_k$ with $g\in V_k\subseteq\overline{V_k}\subseteq U$ and $\operatorname{diam}V_k<\epsilon$: for each basic open set and each positive integer $m$, the refinements supplied by [F3] cover that basic open set, and second countability gives a countable subcover. By [F6] choose a point $c_k\in V_k$ for every $k$. [F2, F3, F6]

1.2 For $x\in G/H$ define indices $k_n(x)$ by recursion: $k_0(x)$ is the least $k$ with $x\in q(V_k)$ and $\operatorname{diam}V_k\le1$; such an index exists by choosing any $g\in q^{-1}(x)$ and applying [F3] inside $G$. Having defined $k_n(x)$, let $k_{n+1}(x)$ be the least $k$ with $\overline{V_k}\subseteq V_{k_n(x)}$, $x\in q(V_k)$, and $\operatorname{diam}V_k\le2^{-(n+1)}$. This index exists by choosing $g\in q^{-1}(x)\cap V_{k_n(x)}$ and applying [F3] inside $V_{k_n(x)}$ with the prescribed diameter. Each $k_n$ is Borel: for $n=0$ each candidate condition on $x$ is membership in the open set $q(V_k)$, and inductively the set of $x$ with $\overline{V_k}\subseteq V_{k_n(x)}$ is the union over countably many $j$ with $\overline{V_k}\subseteq V_j$ of $\{x:k_n(x)=j\}$; intersecting with $q(V_k)$ and the fixed diameter condition gives Borel candidate sets. Selecting the least index in a Borel family is Borel. [F3, F1, F5]

2.1 The points $p_n(x):=c_{k_n(x)}$ are Borel functions of $x$ and converge: for $m\ge n$, $p_m(x)\in V_{k_m(x)}\subseteq\overline{V_{k_n(x)}}$, so $d(p_m(x),p_n(x))\le\operatorname{diam}\overline{V_{k_n(x)}}\le2^{-n}$. Completeness gives a limit $s(x)\in\bigcap_n\overline{V_{k_n(x)}}$, whose diameter is zero. The map $s$ is Borel: for each nonempty closed $F\subseteq G$, $s(x)\in F$ iff $d(p_n(x),F)\to0$; this condition is a countable intersection of countable unions of Borel sets because $p_n$ is Borel and distance to $F$ is continuous. The empty closed set has empty preimage. [F2, step 1.2]

3.1 $s$ is a cross-section: for each $n$ choose $g_n\in V_{k_n(x)}$ with $q(g_n)=x$, which is possible because $x\in q(V_{k_n(x)})$ by [step 1.2]. Both $g_n$ and $s(x)$ lie in $\overline{V_{k_n(x)}}$, a set of diameter at most $2^{-n}$, so $d(g_n,s(x))\le2^{-n}\to0$ and the sequence $g_n$ converges to $s(x)$; continuity of $q$ gives $x=q(g_n)\to q(s(x))$, and since the left-hand sequence is constant, $q(s(x))=x$. [step 1.2, F1, F2, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 Transport of measures: for a $\sigma$-finite Borel measure $\mu$ on $G/H$, define $\nu(E):=\mu(s^{-1}(E))$ for Borel $E\subseteq G$. This is a Borel measure because $s$ is Borel. It is carried by the Borel image $s(G/H)=\{g\in G:s(q(g))=g\}$: the latter is Borel since $g\mapsto(s(q(g)),g)$ is Borel and the diagonal of the Polish space $G$ is closed. If $G/H=\bigcup_n A_n$ with $\mu(A_n)<\infty$, then $s(A_n)=s(G/H)\cap q^{-1}(A_n)$ is Borel and $\nu(s(A_n))=\mu(A_n)$, since $q\circ s=\mathrm{id}$. The sets $s(A_n)$ together with $G\setminus s(G/H)$ cover $G$, and the last set has $\nu$-measure zero, so $\nu$ is $\sigma$-finite. Equivalent measures have equivalent pushforwards by the definition of $\nu$, so the measure class is transported along $s$. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 For $g\in G$ and $x\in G/H$ one has $q(g s(x))=gq(s(x))=gx=q(s(gx))$ by [step 3.1], so $s(gx)^{-1}g s(x)\in q^{-1}(eH)=H$. Thus $h(g,x)=s(gx)^{-1}g s(x)$ is the unique element of $H$ with $g s(x)=s(gx)h(g,x)$. The map $(g,x)\mapsto h(g,x)$ is Borel as a composite of the Borel map $(g,x)\mapsto s(gx)$ (composition of $s$ with the continuous action map) with the continuous group operations. [step 3.1, F5]

4.3 The map $\Phi:G\to G/H\times H$, $g\mapsto(q(g),s(q(g))^{-1}g)$, is Borel and so is $\Psi:G/H\times H\to G$, $(x,h)\mapsto s(x)h$; they are mutually inverse: $q(s(x)h)=q(s(x))=x$ and $s(x)^{-1}s(x)h=h$, while $s(q(g))\bigl(s(q(g))^{-1}g\bigr)=g$. Hence $\Phi$ is a Borel isomorphism onto $G/H\times H$. [F5, step 3.1]

5.1 We have constructed a Borel cross-section $s$ of $q$ [step 3.1], the unique Borel section cocycle $h(g,x)=s(gx)^{-1}gs(x)$ [step 4.2], the Borel isomorphism $G\cong G/H\times H$ [step 4.3], and the transported $\sigma$-finite measure [step 4.1]. AC is inherited through the Polish-space input [F1], whose complete-metrization proof uses DC and the ultrafilter lemma, and supplies Countable Choice for the countable-base refinements and base points $c_k$ in step 1.1 and the fibre points $g_n$ in step 3.1. The least-index recursion and the Borel cocycle and product formulas add no choice requirement. [step 3.1, step 4.2, step 4.3, step 4.1, step 1.1] ∎


## Remarks

The section is constructed from a countable base by a deterministic least-index recursion, so its Borelness is proved rather than assumed; the compatibility of the presented metric with the group topology is the only metric input.
