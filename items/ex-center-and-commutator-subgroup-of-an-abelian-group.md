## Example

If $G$ is an abelian group with identity $e$, then every element is central and
every commutator is the identity. Consequently

$$Z(G)=G\quad\text{and}\quad[G,G]=\{e\}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An abelian group $G$.

[F1] The group axioms provide associativity, identity, and inverses ([[def-group]]).

[F2] The center is $Z(G)=\{z\in G:zg=gz\text{ for every }g\in G\}$ ([[def-center-of-a-group]]).

[F3] The commutator is $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and $[G,G]$ is the subgroup generated by all commutators ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F4] The subgroup generated by a set is the smallest subgroup containing it ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $G$ is abelian, every $z\in G$ commutes with every $g\in G$. Thus every element satisfies [F2], and $Z(G)=G$. [F2, given]

1.2 For $g,h\in G$, commutativity and the group laws give $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}=gg^{-1}hh^{-1}=e$. Hence the set of all commutators is $\{e\}$. [F1, F3, given]

2.1 The set $\{e\}$ is itself a subgroup, so by the minimality in [F4] the subgroup it generates is $\{e\}$. Therefore [F3] gives $[G,G]=\{e\}$. [step 1.2, F3, F4] ∎
