## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, and let $V$ be a representation generated by a highest weight
vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$
([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]). Then
$$V=U(\mathfrak n^-)v=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv ,$$
every weight $\mu$ of $V$ is of the form
$\mu=\lambda-\sum_in_i\alpha_i$ with $n_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, so that
$\mu\le\lambda$ in the root order
([[def-partial-order-on-weights]]), and
$$V_\lambda=\mathbb Cv .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system, and a module $V$ generated by a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory supplying [L1], [L2] and [L4] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The chosen positive system gives the direct sum $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak n^-\oplus\mathfrak h\oplus\mathfrak n^+$ with $\mathfrak n^\pm$ the span of the root spaces $\mathfrak g_{\pm\alpha}$, $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]]).

[L2] For an ordered basis $x_1,\dots,x_N$ of a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra, the monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_N^{a_N}$ form a basis of its universal enveloping algebra ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]); applied to $\mathfrak g$ with an ordered basis beginning with a basis of $\mathfrak n^-$, continuing with a basis of $\mathfrak h$ and ending with a basis of $\mathfrak n^+$, this gives $U(\mathfrak g)=U(\mathfrak n^-)U(\mathfrak h)U(\mathfrak n^+)$ as a linear span, and applied to $\mathfrak n^-$ it gives that the monomials in a basis of $\mathfrak n^-$ span $U(\mathfrak n^-)$.

[L3] The action of $\mathfrak g$ on $V$ extends to a unital action of $U(\mathfrak g)$, and the subrepresentation generated by $v$ is $U(\mathfrak g)v$ ([[thm-lie-algebra-representations-are-equivalent-to-unital-modules-over-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[L4] If $x\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and $w\in V_\mu$, then $x\cdot w\in V_{\mu+\alpha}$ ([[prop-root-vectors-shift-weight-spaces]]).

[L5] The positive roots are exactly the nonzero nonnegative integral combinations of the simple roots, each root space is one-dimensional, and the simple roots are linearly independent ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $V=U(\mathfrak g)v$ by [L3], because $V$ is the subrepresentation generated by $v$. [A1, L3]

2.1 Applying [L2] and using that $\mathfrak n^+\cdot v=0$ and $H\cdot v=\lambda(H)v$ for $H\in\mathfrak h$ gives $U(\mathfrak g)v=U(\mathfrak n^-)U(\mathfrak h)U(\mathfrak n^+)v=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv$. [A1, L1, L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Fix a basis $f_1,\dots,f_N$ of $\mathfrak n^-$ consisting of root vectors $f_j\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha^{(j)}}$ with $\alpha^{(j)}\in\Phi^+$; by [L2] the monomials in the $f_j$ span $U(\mathfrak n^-)$, so every element of $V$ is a linear combination of vectors $f_{j_1}^{a_1}\cdots f_{j_k}^{a_k}\cdot v$, and by [L4] and [L5] the weight of such a vector is $\lambda-\sum_ra_r\alpha^{(j_r)}$, a functional of the form $\lambda-\sum_in_i\alpha_i$ with $n_i\ge0$. [A1, L2, L4, L5, step 2.1]

4.1 By step 3.1 every weight of $V$ lies in $\lambda-Q_+$ and satisfies $\mu\le\lambda$ in the root order ([[def-partial-order-on-weights]]). [step 3.1]

4.2 For the top weight, a monomial $f_{j_1}^{a_1}\cdots f_{j_k}^{a_k}$ has weight $\lambda$ exactly when $\sum_ra_r\alpha^{(j_r)}=0$; since the $\alpha^{(j_r)}$ are nonzero elements of $Q_+$ and the simple roots are linearly independent by [L5], this forces $a_r=0$ for every $r$, so the only monomial of weight $\lambda$ is the empty one and $V_\lambda=\mathbb Cv$. [L5, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 2.1, 4.1 and 5.1 prove the three assertions. [step 2.1, step 4.1, step 4.2] ∎
