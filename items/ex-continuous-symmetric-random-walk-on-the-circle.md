## Example

Assume countable choice and let $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ carry its
normalized Haar probability $m_{\mathbb T}$. Fix $0<h<1/2$. The symmetric
random walk that adds $+h$ or $-h$ with equal probability has kernel
$$P(x,A)=\tfrac12\mathbf1_A(x+h)+\tfrac12\mathbf1_A(x-h),\qquad A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb T),$$
where addition is modulo one. This is a probability kernel reversible with
respect to $m_{\mathbb T}$, hence $m_{\mathbb T}$ is invariant. The transition
law is atomic; no transition density is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The circle $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, its normalized Haar
probability $m_{\mathbb T}$, and $0<h<1/2$.

[A1] Countable choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is assumed only through the
existing normalized-circle-measure interface; no full Axiom of Choice is
assumed. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] The one-dimensional torus carries the normalized Borel probability
$m_{\mathbb T}$; the supplied construction explicitly assumes
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. ([[def-the-one-dimensional-torus-and-normalized-haar-integral]])

[F2] Each translation $\tau_y([t])=[t+y]$ is continuous.
([[def-the-one-dimensional-torus-and-normalized-haar-integral]])

[F3] Each translation $\tau_y$ preserves $m_{\mathbb T}$.
([[def-the-one-dimensional-torus-and-normalized-haar-integral]])

[F4] A probability kernel has probability-measure rows and measurable event
evaluations. ([[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]])

[F5] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles.
([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]])

[F6] The joint flow measure satisfies the kernel iterated-integral identity
for every nonnegative product-measurable function. ([[lem-prior-and-model-kernel-define-joint-law]])

[F7] For a measure-preserving map $T$ and nonnegative measurable $g$,
$\int g\circ T\,dm=\int g\,dm$. ([[def-measure-preserving-transformation-and-system]], [[thm-integrals-are-invariant-under-measure-preserving-maps]])

[F8] Reversibility means equality of the joint flow measure with its
coordinate transpose. ([[def-general-state-reversibility-by-symmetric-flow-measure]])

[F9] Reversibility implies invariance. ([[thm-general-state-reversibility-implies-invariance]])

## Verification

**Given:** $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, $m_{\mathbb T}$,
$0<h<1/2$, and $P(x,A)=\frac12\mathbf1_A(\tau_hx)+\frac12\mathbf1_A(\tau_{-h}x)$.

**Proof technique:** Verify the kernel directly, write its joint flow on
measurable sets, and change variables by the measure-preserving translations.

1.1 For fixed $A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb T)$, $x\mapsto\mathbf1_A(\tau_{\pm h}x)$ is measurable by [F2]; the two translated points are distinct since $0<2h<1$, each row has total mass one, and disjoint-event indicators add pointwise, so [F4] makes $P$ a probability kernel. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given]

2.1 For product-measurable $C$, the graph maps $\Gamma_{\pm h}(x)=(x,\tau_{\pm h}x)$ are measurable because rectangle preimages are measurable by [F5]; with $g_{\pm}(x)=\mathbf1_C(x,\tau_{\pm h}x)$, the joint-law formula [F6] gives $\Lambda_{m_{\mathbb T},P}(C)=\frac12\int g_+\,dm_{\mathbb T}+\frac12\int g_-\,dm_{\mathbb T}$. [F5, F6, step 1.1, given]

3.1 The swap map is measurable by [F5]. The identities $\tau\circ\Gamma_h=\Gamma_{-h}\circ\tau_h$ and $\tau\circ\Gamma_{-h}=\Gamma_h\circ\tau_{-h}$ and [F6] give $\Lambda_{m_{\mathbb T},P}^{\top}(C)=\frac12\int g_-(\tau_hx)\,dm_{\mathbb T}+\frac12\int g_+(\tau_{-h}x)\,dm_{\mathbb T}$. By [F3] and [F7] this equals the expression for $\Lambda_{m_{\mathbb T},P}(C)$ in step 2.1, for every product-measurable $C$. [F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, step 2.1, given]

4.1 By [F8], symmetry makes $P$ reversible; [F9] then makes $m_{\mathbb T}$ invariant. Countable choice is inherited only through the supplied circle measure; the kernel calculation uses no further choice. [A1, F8, F9, step 3.1] ∎
