## Statement

Let $A$ be a Dedekind domain and let $\mathfrak l$ be a nonzero prime ideal. Write $A_{(\mathfrak l)}$ for the localization and $A_{\mathfrak l}=\varprojlim_{n\ge1}A/\mathfrak l^n$ for the completion. Then $A_{\mathfrak l}$ is a complete discrete valuation ring, its maximal ideal is generated by a uniformizer, and its residue field is $A/\mathfrak l$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Dedekind domain $A$ and a nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak l\subset A$.

[F1] [[def-dedekind-domain]]: a Dedekind domain is a Noetherian, integrally closed domain of Krull dimension one.

[F2] [[lem-dedekind-localisation-at-nonzero-prime-is-dvr]]: the localization $R=A_{(\mathfrak l)}$ is a discrete valuation ring.

## Proof

1.1 By [F2], $R=A_{(\mathfrak l)}$ is a discrete valuation ring. Choose a uniformizer $\pi\in R$; its maximal ideal is $\mathfrak lR$, and the residue field is $R/\mathfrak lR\cong A/\mathfrak l$. [F1, F2, given, choose]

2.1 For every $n\ge1$, the natural map $A/\mathfrak l^n\to R/\mathfrak l^nR$ is an isomorphism. Indeed, if $s\notin\mathfrak l$, then $s$ is invertible modulo $\mathfrak l$; from $st=1+r$ with $r\in\mathfrak l$, the finite geometric sum $t(1-r+r^2-\cdots+(-r)^{n-1})$ is an inverse for $s$ modulo $\mathfrak l^n$. Thus every localized residue class is represented by an element of $A$. If $a\in A$ maps to zero, then $sa\in\mathfrak l^n$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak l$; invertibility of $s$ modulo $\mathfrak l^n$ gives $a\in\mathfrak l^n$, proving injectivity. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Consequently $A_{\mathfrak l}\cong\widehat R=\varprojlim_nR/\pi^nR$. This inverse limit is complete and separated, and $\bigcap_n\pi^n\widehat R=0$. Every nonzero $x\in\widehat R$ therefore has a largest $m\ge0$ with $x\in\pi^m\widehat R$, so $x=\pi^m u$ with $u\notin\pi\widehat R$. The residue of $u$ is nonzero; lifting its inverse from $R/\pi R$ and summing the convergent geometric series for $(1+\pi z)^{-1}$ shows that $u$ is a unit. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every nonzero ideal $I\subset\widehat R$ has an element of least valuation, say $x=\pi^m u$; since $u$ is a unit, $\pi^m\in I$, while minimality gives $I\subseteq\pi^m\widehat R$. Thus $I=\pi^m\widehat R$. Hence $\widehat R$ is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal $\pi\widehat R$ and residue field $A/\mathfrak l$. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
