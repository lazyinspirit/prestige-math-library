## Statement

Every $W$-orbit in $E$ has exactly one point in $\overline{\mathcal C}$. For $\eta\in\overline{\mathcal C}$, its stabilizer is generated by those simple reflections $s_i$ with $(\eta,\alpha_i)=0$. The group acts simply transitively on open chambers. There is a unique longest element $w_0$, characterized by $w_0\Phi^+=\Phi^-$; it has length $|\Phi^+|$ and satisfies $w_0^2=1$. In particular every weight orbit has a unique dominant weight. These assertions include rank zero and singular wall points, without AC.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The root-system, lattice and chamber conventions of [[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]].

[F1] Positive-root expansions, finiteness, simple generation and lattice preservation are proved in [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]].

[F2] Length equals inversion count and reflection descent is determined by root sign, by [[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]].

## Proof

1.1 Choose $\rho\in E$ with $(\rho,\alpha_i)>0$ for every $i$, by taking the sum of the basis dual to the simple roots; in rank zero use zero. For $x\in E$, the finite orbit $Wx$ has a point $\eta$ maximizing $(\rho,\eta)$. If $(\eta,\alpha_i)<0$, then $(\rho,s_i\eta-\eta)=-2(\eta,\alpha_i)(\rho,\alpha_i)/(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)>0$, a contradiction. Thus $\eta\in\overline{\mathcal C}$. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Suppose $\lambda,\mu\in\overline{\mathcal C}$ and $\lambda=w\mu$. Choose among such $w$ one of least word length. If nonidentity, write a reduced expression $w=s_i v$. By F2, $w^{-1}\alpha_i<0$. Since $\mu$ pairs nonnegatively with every positive root by F1, $$(\lambda,\alpha_i)=(\mu,w^{-1}\alpha_i)\le0.$$ Dominance of $\lambda$ forces equality. Hence $s_i\lambda=\lambda$ and $v\mu=s_iw\mu=\lambda$, contradicting minimal length. Thus $w=1$ and $\lambda=\mu$. Lattice preservation then gives the unique dominant representative of each orbit in $P$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For $\eta\in\overline{\mathcal C}$ and any nonidentity $w$ fixing it, write a reduced expression $w=s_i v$. The same scalar-product argument as in step 1.2 gives $(\eta,\alpha_i)=0$. Then $s_i$ fixes $\eta$ and $v=s_iw$ does too, with smaller length. Induction writes $w$ as a product of zero-label simple reflections. Conversely each of those reflections visibly fixes $\eta$, proving exactly the stabilizer assertion, including $\eta=0$. [step 1.2, F1, F2, algebra]

2.2 For any regular point, all roots have fixed nonzero signs on its connected component of the hyperplane complement, since a continuous real linear functional cannot change sign without vanishing. Conversely a nonempty prescribed sign region is an intersection of open linear halfspaces, hence convex and connected. Thus the chambers are exactly those sign regions. F1 gives $\mathcal C=\{x:(x,\alpha)>0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi^+\}$. Step 1.1 sends every regular point into this region (its image remains regular), so $W$ acts transitively on chambers. If $w\mathcal C=\mathcal C$, then $w^{-1}$ preserves positive roots by testing their signs on this chamber. F2 gives length zero, so $w=1$. This proves simple transitivity without a chamber-faithfulness assumption in the proof of exchange. [step 1.1, F1, F2, given]

3.1 The negative region $-\mathcal C$ is a chamber, so step 2.2 gives a unique $w_0$ sending $\mathcal C$ to it. Equivalently $w_0^{-1}\Phi^+=\Phi^-$, hence also $w_0\Phi^+=\Phi^-$. F2 gives length $|\Phi^+|$, the largest possible inversion count. Any element of that length reverses all positive roots and hence has the same chamber image, so equals $w_0$. Moreover $w_0^2$ preserves the positive chamber and is therefore the identity. In rank zero the hyperplane complement and its unique chamber are the one-point space, all groups and stabilizer claims are trivial and $w_0=1$. [step 2.2, F1, F2, algebra] ∎
