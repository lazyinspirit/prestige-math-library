## Statement

If $G$ is finitely generated nilpotent of class $c\ge1$, then for every fixed $z\in\gamma_c(G)$ and finite generating set $S$ there is $C_z$ such that $|z^m|_S\le C_z|m|^{1/c}$ for every nonzero integer $m$. Also $z^0=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $S$ is finite and generates $G$; constants may depend on z and S but not m.

[F1] The last lower-central term is generated by finitely many c-fold commutators ([[lem-lower-central-quotients-of-finitely-generated-nilpotent-groups-are-finitely-generated-abelian]]).

[F2] Central-valued commutator pairings multiply over products and integer powers ([[lem-hall-malcev-commutator-product-identities]]).

[F3] Word length is subadditive and invariant under inversion ([[lem-word-length-is-well-defined-and-satisfies-the-length-laws]]).

[F4] The quotient by the last term has nilpotency class at most c-1 ([[thm-subgroups-quotients-and-finite-products-of-nilpotent-groups]]).

## Proof

1.1 Induct on $c$ for all finitely generated groups at once. For $c=1$, repeating a fixed word for $z$ gives $|z^m|_S\le |z|_S|m|$. The identity has length zero. Assume $c\ge2$. The group $H=\gamma_c$ is central, and by F1 it is generated by finitely many $t=[s,u]$ with $s\in S$, $u\in\gamma_{c-1}$, allowing inverses. It suffices first to bound each such t. [F1, F3, given]

2.1 For $m\ge1$ put $q=\lceil m^{1/c}\rceil$ and divide $m=a q^{c-1}+b$ with $0\le b<q^{c-1}$. Then $0\le a\le q$ and $q\le2m^{1/c}$. In $G/H$, the element $uH$ is in its last possible layer $\gamma_{c-1}(G/H)$. If that quotient has smaller class, $uH=1$ and take empty words. Otherwise the induction hypothesis gives words for $(uH)^{q^{c-1}}$ and $(uH)^b$ of length at most $Kq$ (take the empty word when $b=0$). Lift their letters to S-words $v,w$ of the same length. Then $u^{q^{c-1}}=v h_1$, $u^b=w h_2$ for $h_1,h_2\in H$. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Because $[G,\gamma_{c-1}]\subseteq H$ is central, F2 gives $t^m=[s^a,u^{q^{c-1}}][s,u^b]=[s^a,v][s,w]$: multiplying either second input by the central element $h_i$ changes no commutator. The displayed word has length at most $2(a+Kq)+2(1+Kq)\le(4+4K)q\le(8+8K)m^{1/c}$. Negative powers have the same length by inversion. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 For fixed $z\in H$ choose a finite expression $z=\prod_{j=1}^t t_j^{e_j}$ in these generators. Centrality gives $z^m=\prod_j t_j^{e_jm}$. Subadditivity and step 3.1 bound its length by $\sum_{e_j\ne0}C_{t_j}|e_j|^{1/c}|m|^{1/c}$. This finite sum is a valid $C_z$; the empty sum handles $z=1$. Exponent zero is the identity by the power convention. [F3, step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
