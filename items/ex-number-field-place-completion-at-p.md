## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Take the number field $F=\mathbb Q$ and its
finite place corresponding to the prime ideal $5\mathbb Z$. Its completion
$\mathbb Q_5$ has valuation ring
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q_5}=\{x\in\mathbb Q_5:v_5(x)\ge0\}$, maximal ideal
$5\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q_5}$, uniformizer $5$, residue field $\mathbb F_5$,
and normalized absolute value $|5|_5=1/5$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $F=\mathbb Q$, and the finite place at
$5\mathbb Z$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice supplies the field-place classification interface
used by the published completion definitions. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] [[def-number-field]]: A number field is a finite extension of
$\mathbb Q$; in particular $\mathbb Q/\mathbb Q$ has degree one.

[F2] [[def-completion-of-a-number-field-at-a-prime]]: At a nonzero prime
$P\subseteq\mathcal O_F$, the completion has normalized absolute value
$|x|_P=(NP)^{-\operatorname{ord}_P(x)}$, and its valuation ring has residue
field $\mathcal O_F/P$.

[F3] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]: A
complete discretely valued field with finite residue field is a
nonarchimedean local field; the normalized absolute value is
$q^{-v(x)}$ when the residue field has order $q$.

[F4] [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]: The degree of a finite
extension is its vector-space dimension; in particular
$[\mathbb Q:\mathbb Q]=1$.

## Verification

**Given:** The data above.


1.1 The field $\mathbb Q$ is a number field by [F1] and [F4], and $5\mathbb Z$ is a nonzero prime ideal of $\mathbb Z$. Its norm is $|\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z|=5$, its normalized valuation has $\operatorname{ord}_{5\mathbb Z}(5)=1$, and [F2] therefore gives the completion with $|5|_5=5^{-1}=1/5$ and residue field $\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb F_5$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 The valuation ring of the completion is the set where $v_5\ge0$; its maximal ideal is generated by $5$, since $v_5(5)=1$. The completion is complete by construction, and its residue field is finite by step 1.1. Thus [F3] identifies it as a nonarchimedean local field with uniformizer $5$. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
