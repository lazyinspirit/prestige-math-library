## Statement

If a group $G$ is generated by a finite set $S$, then $\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}$ is abelian and generated by images of the finitely many left-nested $i$-fold commutators in $S$ (with inverses allowed). If $G$ is nilpotent, each $\gamma_i$ is finitely generated.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $S$ is finite and generates $G$; the second assertion additionally assumes $\gamma_{c+1}=1$.

[F1] Lower-central commutator pairings are well-defined and biadditive ([[lem-hall-malcev-lower-central-commutators-add-weights]]).

[F2] Generation means every element is a finite product of generators and inverses ([[def-finitely-generated-group]]).

[F3] Nilpotency of class $c$ gives $\gamma_{c+1}=1$ ([[thm-upper-and-lower-central-characterizations-of-nilpotence]]).

## Proof

1.1 The $i=1$ quotient is generated by the images of $S$. Suppose $\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}$ is generated by the $i$-fold simple commutators. The next quotient is generated by $[u,g]\gamma_{i+2}$ for $u\in\gamma_i,g\in G$, since $[\gamma_i,G]=\gamma_{i+1}$. Expand the two entries in their factor generators. Biadditivity expresses this class as a product of the $(i+1)$-fold simple commutators and their inverses. Their number is at most $|S|^{i+1}$ before repetitions. This proves the claim by induction. [F1, F2]

2.1 Abelianness follows from centrality of $\gamma_i/\gamma_{i+1}$. In a nilpotent group start with the empty generating list for $\gamma_{c+1}=1$. If $\gamma_{i+1}$ has a finite generating list and $u_1,\ldots,u_t$ lift factor generators, then for any $g\in\gamma_i$ a word $w$ in the $u_j$ has the same coset, so $w^{-1}g\in\gamma_{i+1}$. The union of the two finite lists generates $\gamma_i$. Descending induction reaches $i=1$; terms after $c$ are trivial. If $S$ is empty, $G=1$ and all lists are empty. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1] ∎
