## Statement

Assume Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice. Let $n\ge2$, let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, let $A$ and $L_0$ be as in [[thm-de-giorgi-local-boundedness-for-homogeneous-subsolutions]], and let $f\in L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ with $q>n/2$. Let $u\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R)$ satisfy $u\ge0$ a.e. and
$$a_0(u,\varphi)\le\int_\Omega f\,\varphi\,dx\qquad\text{for every nonnegative }\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb R).$$
Then for every ball $B_R(x_0)\Subset\Omega$, every $0<\rho<1$ and every $p>0$,
$$\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_{\rho R}(x_0)}u\le C\Bigl[\Bigl(\frac{1}{|B_R(x_0)|}\int_{B_R(x_0)}u^p\,dx\Bigr)^{1/p}+R^{\,2-n/q}\|f^+\|_{L^q(B_R(x_0))}\Bigr],$$
with $C=C(n,q,\theta,M_a,\rho,p)$ independent of $R$ and $x_0$. The factor $R^{2-n/q}$ is dictated by dilation of the equation: the forcing term has the dimension of $u$ for every $q$. The strict threshold $q>n/2$ is an integrability hypothesis for this boundedness estimate, not a condition for dimensional consistency; the $n=2$ case admits any $q>1$ with the critical Sobolev embedding in place of the $2^*$ embedding.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice; an open set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $n\ge2$; constants $0<\theta\le M_a^2$; measurable symmetric coefficients $A$ with $\theta|\xi|^2\le\langle A(x)\xi,\xi\rangle\le M_a^2|\xi|^2$; the principal operator $L_0u=-D_i(a^{ij}D_ju)$ with form $a_0$; a source $f\in L^q_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$, $q>n/2$; a nonnegative $u\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R)$ with $a_0(u,\varphi)\le\int_\Omega f\varphi\,dx$ for all nonnegative $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb R)$; a ball $B_R(x_0)\Subset\Omega$ and $0<\rho<1$.

[F1] Assume Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice. Sobolev positive-part calculus: for $w\in H^1(\Omega;\mathbb R)$, $w^+\in H^1$ with $Dw^+=\mathbf1_{\{w>0\}}Dw$ and $Dw=0$ a.e. on $\{w=0\}$; multiplication by a compactly supported smooth factor obeys the weak product rule. If $a_0(w,v)\le0$ for every nonnegative $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$, then $w^+$ is a weak subsolution of the principal operator, so $a_0(w^+,v)\le0$ for every nonnegative $v\in H^1_0(\Omega)$. Here is the admissible truncation proof. For $\phi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, $\phi\ge0$, set $\eta_\epsilon(t)=\min\{1,t^+/\epsilon\}$. The chain and product rules give $\psi_\epsilon=\phi\eta_\epsilon(w)\in H^1$ with compact support in $\Omega$, hence $\psi_\epsilon\in H^1_0(\Omega)$; it is nonnegative, and $D_i\psi_\epsilon=\eta_\epsilon(w)D_i\phi+\epsilon^{-1}\phi\mathbf1_{\{0<w<\epsilon\}}D_iw$ (the level-set endpoints contribute zero because Sobolev gradients vanish a.e. on a level set). Thus
$$a_0(w,\psi_\epsilon)=\int_\Omega\eta_\epsilon(w)a^{ij}D_jwD_i\phi\,dx+\frac1\epsilon\int_{\{0<w<\epsilon\}}\phi\,a^{ij}D_jwD_iw\,dx\le0.$$
The second integral is nonnegative by symmetry, ellipticity, and $\phi\ge0$, so the first is nonpositive. As $\epsilon\downarrow0$, $\eta_\epsilon(w)\to\mathbf1_{\{w>0\}}$ pointwise and is bounded by $1$; dominated convergence applies because $A$ is bounded and $|Dw||D\phi|$ is integrable on $\operatorname{supp}\phi$. Using $Dw^+=\mathbf1_{\{w>0\}}Dw$ gives $a_0(w^+,\phi)\le0$. To extend from smooth tests to every nonnegative $v\in H^1_0$, choose $z_j\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$ with $z_j\to v$ in $H^1$ and pass to a subsequence with $z_j\to v$ a.e. The positive-part gradient formulas give
$$D z_j^+-Dv=\mathbf1_{\{z_j>0\}}(Dz_j-Dv)+(\mathbf1_{\{z_j>0\}}-\mathbf1_{\{v>0\}})Dv.$$
The first term tends to zero in $L^2$; the second does too by dominated convergence, since its indicator tends to zero on $\{v>0\}$ and $Dv=0$ a.e. on $\{v=0\}$. Also $z_j^+\to v$ in $L^2$ by the $1$-Lipschitz property, hence $z_j^+\to v$ in $H^1$. Each nonzero $z_j^+$ has compact support in $\Omega$; zero-extend it and convolve with a nonnegative unit-mass radial mollifier, chosen with support radius smaller than $\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{supp}z_j^+,\partial\Omega)$ (if $z_j^+=0$, keep the zero function). The mollified functions are nonnegative and in $C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, and converge to $z_j^+$ in $H^1$ by approximate-identity convergence applied to the function and its weak gradient. A diagonal choice gives nonnegative smooth tests converging to $v$. Boundedness of $a_0(w,\cdot)$ passes the inequality to $v$. In particular, no product of an indicator with an arbitrary test is asserted to lie in $H^1_0$. [F2] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Lax-Milgram and coercivity: $H^1(B_1)$ is Hilbert by [[thm-hk-is-a-hilbert-space]]. Its subspace $H^1_0(B_1)$ is closed and linear by its closure definition: a Cauchy sequence there converges in $H^1(B_1)$, and its limit still lies in the closure of the smooth tests. Thus the restricted derivative-sum inner product makes $H^1_0(B_1)$ Hilbert. The form $a_0$ is a bounded sesquilinear form on the Hilbert space $H^1_0(B_1)$ with coercivity constant $\alpha=\theta/(1+C_P(B_1)^2)$, and for every bounded conjugate-linear functional $G$ on $H^1_0(B_1)$ there is a unique $h\in H^1_0(B_1)$ with $a_0(h,v)=G(v)$ for all $v\in H^1_0(B_1)$, the weak Dirichlet solution; it satisfies $\alpha\|h\|_{H^1_0}\le\|G\|_{H^{-1}}$ ([[thm-lax-milgram]], [[lem-coercivity-of-the-principal-dirichlet-form]], [[lem-elliptic-form-is-well-defined-and-bounded]], [[def-h-minus-one-as-the-dual-of-h-one-zero]], [[def-weak-dirichlet-solution-for-a-divergence-form-operator]], [[def-wkp-zero-as-a-sobolev-closure]]).

[F3] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Embedding of $H^1_0$ into the dual exponents of $L^q$: for $n\ge3$ and $1\le r<2^*=2n/(n-2)$ there is $C_r$ with $\|w\|_{L^r(B_1)}\le C_r\|w\|_{H^1_0(B_1)}$ for all $w\in H^1_0(B_1)$, by Holder and the Sobolev inequality; for $n=2$ the same holds for every finite $r$ by the critical embedding $W^{1,2}_0(B_1)\hookrightarrow L^r(B_1)$ ([[cor-sobolev-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]], [[thm-critical-sobolev-embedding-into-every-finite-lq]], [[thm-holder-inequality-for-integrals]], [[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]]).

[F4] Assume the Axiom of Choice. The weak maximum principle with a source on the bounded $C^1$ domain $B_1$: if $v\in H^1(B_1;\mathbb R)$ satisfies $a_0(v,\varphi)\le\int_{B_1}g\varphi\,dx$ for every nonnegative $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$ with $g\in L^q(B_1)$, $q>n/2$, then $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_1}v\le\sup_{\partial B_1}v^++C_0\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}$ with $C_0=C_0(n,q,\theta,M_a,B_1)$; in particular for $v\in H^1_0(B_1)$ one has $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_1}v\le C_0\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}$, and if $g\le0$ then $v\le0$ a.e. ([[thm-weak-maximum-principle-for-coercive-divergence-form-equations]], [[thm-lp-trace-operator-on-a-bounded-c-one-domain]], [[thm-kernel-of-the-trace-is-w-one-p-zero]], [[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]).

[F5] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Homogeneous local boundedness: if $w\in H^1(B_1;\mathbb R)$ satisfies $w\ge0$ a.e. and $a_0(w,v)\le0$ for every nonnegative $v\in H^1_0(B_1)$, then for every $0<s<1$, every $0<\sigma<1$ and every $p>0$, $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_{\sigma s}}w\le C_1(\frac{1}{|B_s|}\int_{B_s}w^p\,dx)^{1/p}$; this is the homogeneous theorem applied on the compactly contained ball $B_s\Subset B_1$ ([[thm-de-giorgi-local-boundedness-for-homogeneous-subsolutions]]).

[F6] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Poincare inequality on $W^{1,2}_0$ ([[thm-poincare-inequality-for-w-one-p-zero]], [[def-essential-supremum-with-respect-to-a-measure]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; rescale the problem to the unit ball, remove the source by subtracting a barrier built with Lax-Milgram whose size is controlled by the weak maximum principle, apply the homogeneous local boundedness estimate to the positive part of the difference, and undo the rescalings.

1.1 Scaling the problem to the unit ball. Define $v(y):=u(x_0+Ry)$ for $y\in B_1$, $A_R(y):=A(x_0+Ry)$ and $g(y):=R^2f(x_0+Ry)$, so that $g\in L^q(B_1)$ with $\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}=R^{2-n/q}\|f^+\|_{L^q(B_R(x_0))}$ and $\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}v^p\,dy=\frac{1}{|B_R(x_0)|}\int_{B_R(x_0)}u^p\,dx$ by the change of variables $x=x_0+Ry$; the coefficients $A_R$ are again measurable, symmetric and uniformly elliptic with the same constants $\theta,M_a$. Since $f\in L^q(B_R)$ and $q>n/2$, Sobolev and Holder extend the local inequality by density to nonnegative $H^1_0(B_R)$ tests. For every such test $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$ the pullback $\varphi_R(x):=\varphi((x-x_0)/R)$ lies in $H^1_0(B_R(x_0))$ with $D\varphi_R(x)=R^{-1}D\varphi(y)$, hence $a_0^R(v,\varphi)=R^{2-n}\int_{B_R(x_0)}a^{ij}D_juD_i\varphi_R\,dx\le R^{2-n}\int_{B_R(x_0)}f\varphi_R\,dx=\int_{B_1}g\varphi\,dy$, where $a_0^R$ is the form of $A_R$; so $v\ge0$ satisfies the same subsolution inequality on $B_1$ with source $g$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Removing a small source by a barrier. Let $U\in H^1(B_1;\mathbb R)$ satisfy $U\ge0$ a.e. and $a_0(U,\varphi)\le\int_{B_1}g\varphi\,dx$ for all nonnegative $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$ with $\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}\le1$; then for every $0<\rho<1$, every $p>0$ and some $C_2=C_2(n,q,\theta,M_a,\rho,p)$ one has $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}U\le C_2(\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}U^p\,dx)^{1/p}+C_2$. Indeed, by [F3] the functional $\varphi\mapsto\int_{B_1}g^+\varphi\,dx$ is bounded on $H^1_0(B_1)$, so by [F2] there is a unique $h\in H^1_0(B_1)$ with $a_0(h,\varphi)=\int_{B_1}g^+\varphi\,dx$ for all $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$. Since $-h$ is a weak subsolution with source $-g^+\le0$ and zero boundary values, [F4] gives $h\ge0$; it also gives $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_1}h\le C_0\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}\le C_0$. The difference $w:=U-h$ satisfies $a_0(w,\varphi)\le-\int_{B_1}g^-\varphi\,dx\le0$ for all nonnegative $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$, so $w^+$ is a nonnegative homogeneous weak subsolution by [F1]. Since $h\ge0$ and $U\ge0$, $w^+=(U-h)^+\le U$. Fix $s:=(1+\rho)/2$, so $\rho<s<1$, and apply [F5] to $w^+$ on the outer ball $B_s\Subset B_1$ with inner ratio $\sigma=\rho/s$. This gives $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}w^+\le C(\frac{1}{|B_s|}\int_{B_s}(w^+)^p)^{1/p}\le C'(\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}U^p)^{1/p}$, where the volume ratio is absorbed into $C'$. Hence $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}U\le\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}w^++\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_1}h\le C_2(\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}U^p)^{1/p}+C_2$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5]

3.1 Undoing the rescaling and the normalisation. If $\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}=0$ then $v$ is itself a homogeneous weak subsolution and [F5] gives the claim directly with the forcing term absent. Otherwise put $F_R:=\|g^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}>0$ and $U:=v/F_R$, so that $U\ge0$, $a_0(U,\varphi)\le\int_{B_1}(g/F_R)\varphi\,dx$ for all nonnegative $\varphi\in H^1_0(B_1)$ and $\|(g/F_R)^+\|_{L^q(B_1)}=1$; step 2.1 applied to $U$ gives $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}U\le C_2(\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}U^p)^{1/p}+C_2$, and multiplying by $F_R$, $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_\rho}v\le C_2(\frac{1}{|B_1|}\int_{B_1}v^p)^{1/p}+C_2F_R$. Substituting the identities of step 1.1 gives $\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{B_{\rho R}(x_0)}u\le C_2(\frac{1}{|B_R(x_0)|}\int_{B_R(x_0)}u^p\,dx)^{1/p}+C_2R^{2-n/q}\|f^+\|_{L^q(B_R(x_0))}$, which is the asserted estimate with $C:=\max\{C_2,1\}$; the constant depends only on $n,q,\theta,M_a,\rho,p$, and the argument uses Countable Choice and the Axiom of Choice exactly through the cited suppliers. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F5, F6, algebra] ∎
