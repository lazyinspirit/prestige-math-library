## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $(E,\Phi)$ be a CM pair, let $F=E^r$ be its reflex field, and let $N_\Phi:F^\times\to E^\times$ and the corresponding fractional-ideal map be the determinant reflex norm. For every integer $m\ge1$ and every $\alpha\in F^\times$ satisfying
$$\alpha-1\in mO_{F,\mathfrak w}\qquad(\mathfrak w\mid m),$$
one has
$$N_\Phi(\alpha)-1\in mO_{E_i,\mathfrak v}\qquad(\mathfrak v\mid m,\ E_i\text{ a field factor of }E).$$
Thus $N_\Phi$ maps ideals prime to $mO_F$ to ideals prime to $mO_E$, maps ray-principal ideals into ray-principal ideals, and induces a homomorphism
$$\operatorname{Cl}_{mO_F}(F)\longrightarrow\prod_i\operatorname{Cl}_{mO_{E_i}}(E_i).$$
The moduli here have no infinite-place part. The same integer $m$ works in both fields; no exclusion of ramified primes or multiplication of the modulus is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the CM pair, the reflex norm and the integer modulus in the Statement.

[A1] AC is explicit ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The trace module $V$ over $E\otimes_{\mathbb Q}F$ is finite-dimensional, and $N_\Phi(\alpha)$ is the determinant of its $E$-linear action by $\alpha$. The associated ideal homomorphism is induced by finite-idelic valuations and sends local units to local units ([[lem-cm-type-module-exists-uniquely]], [[def-reflex-norm-associated-to-a-cm-type]]).

[F2] Integer rings have finite integral bases and are Dedekind domains; localization at a nonzero prime is a Noetherian local DVR. A finite torsion-free module over a Dedekind domain is projective, and a finite flat module over a Noetherian local ring is free ([[thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree]], [[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]], [[lem-dedekind-localisation-at-nonzero-prime-is-dvr]], [[lem-finite-torsionfree-modules-over-dedekind-domains-are-projective]], [[thm-finite-flat-modules-over-local-rings-are-free]]).

[F3] Nonzero fractional ideals in a number field have finite prime factorizations, with their usual prime valuations ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]]).

[F4] The ray class group is the group of fractional ideals prime to the finite modulus modulo principal ideals generated by elements locally congruent to $1$ at that modulus ([[def-ray-class-group]]).

## Proof

1.1 Choose a finite rational basis of $V$ from [F1]. Let $S$ be the image of $O_E\otimes_{\mathbb Z}O_F$ in $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Q}(V)$, with the two factors acting through their commuting module actions. By their integral bases in [F2], $S$ is a finite $\mathbb Z$-module; it is a unital ring. Let $M$ be the $S$-span of the chosen basis. It is a finite $\mathbb Z$-module, torsion-free as a subgroup of $V$, and spans $V$ over $\mathbb Q$. In particular $M$ is a full lattice stable under both integer rings. Split $M=\bigoplus_i e_iM$ by the factor idempotents of $O_E$. Each summand is finite and torsion-free over $O_{E_i}$: an integer generating set is also an $O_{E_i}$-generating set, and multiplication by any nonzero field element is injective. By [F2], its localization $M_{i,\mathfrak v}$ at any nonzero prime $\mathfrak v$ is finite free over $O_{E_i,\mathfrak v}$ and spans $e_iV$. The commuting $O_F$-action preserves this module. [A1, F1, F2, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Fix a rational prime $\ell\mid m$ and put $\beta=(\alpha-1)/m\in F$. The hypothesis implies that $\beta$ is integral at every prime of $F$ above $\ell$. By [F3], its principal fractional ideal has only finitely many negative valuations, all above rational primes different from $\ell$. Multiplying $\beta$ by sufficiently large powers of precisely those rational primes clears all these negative valuations without introducing another one. Hence there is a positive integer $a$ prime to $\ell$ with $a\beta\in O_F$. For every field factor $E_i$ and prime $\mathfrak v\mid\ell$, $a$ is a unit of $O_{E_i,\mathfrak v}$. Thus the action of $\beta=a^{-1}(a\beta)$ preserves $M_{i,\mathfrak v}$ from step 1.1. [F2, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 In a free basis of $M_{i,\mathfrak v}$, step 2.1 writes the action of $\alpha=1+m\beta$ as $I+mB$ with $B$ a matrix over $O_{E_i,\mathfrak v}$. The determinant expansion has constant term $1$, and every other term contains a factor $m$. Therefore $\det(I+mB)-1\in mO_{E_i,\mathfrak v}$. Determinants commute with scalar extension to the fraction field, so [F1] identifies this determinant with the $i$-th component of $N_\Phi(\alpha)$. This proves the required congruence at every prime above $\ell$, including primes ramified in either number field. Performing the argument separately for the finitely many $\ell\mid m$ proves every congruence in the Statement. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $\mathfrak b$ be a fractional ideal of $F$ prime to $m$. Represent it by a finite idele with zero valuation at every prime above $m$; these components are units. Under [F1] their reflex norms are units at every prime of $E$ above $m$. The induced ideal $N_\Phi(\mathfrak b)$ is therefore prime to $m$. If $\mathfrak b=(\alpha)$ is ray-principal, the ideal compatibility in [F1] gives $N_\Phi((\alpha))=(N_\Phi(\alpha))$, and step 3.1 makes this principal ideal ray-principal. Both maps are multiplicative by [F1]. The quotient definition [F4] now gives the asserted homomorphism, independent of representatives. [F1, F4, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 If $m=1$, there are no modulus primes and the local congruence conditions are empty. Step 4.1 then reduces to the ordinary class-group map, since [F1] maps all principal ideals to principal ideals. The construction uses only finite lattices and local determinant congruences, so it has introduced no unramifiedness condition, CFT input, or extra modulus. This proves the complete Statement. [F1, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
