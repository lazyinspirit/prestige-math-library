## Statement

Let $X$ be a discrete topological space ([[def-standard-topologies]]) and $Y$ a
topological space. Every compact subset of $X$ is finite
([[def-compact-space]]), and on $C(X,Y)$ the compact-open topology
([[def-compact-open-topology-for-topological-domains]]) coincides with the
topology of pointwise convergence ([[def-topology-of-pointwise-convergence]]);
hence on every $\mathcal F\subseteq C(X,Y)$ the compact-open subspace topology is
the subspace topology inherited from the product $Y^X$. In particular finite intersections of sets $\{f:f(x)\in V\}$, with $x\in X$ and $V\subseteq Y$ open, form a basis; these are open-coordinate constraints, not requirements that a coordinate equal a prescribed value.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] In the discrete topology on $X$ every subset is open, and a subset $K\subseteq X$ is compact exactly when every open cover of $K$ by open sets of $X$ (equivalently of the subspace $K$) has a finite subcover; the empty space and every finite space are compact. ([[def-standard-topologies]], [[def-compact-space]], [[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]])

[F2] The compact-open topology on $C(X,Y)$ is generated by the subbasis of sets $S(K,V)=\{f\in C(X,Y):f[K]\subseteq V\}$ with $K\subseteq X$ compact and $V\subseteq Y$ open. ([[def-compact-open-topology-for-topological-domains]])

[F3] The topology of pointwise convergence on $C(X,Y)$ is the subspace topology inherited from the product $Y^X$; its subbasis consists of the traces of the sets $\pi_x^{-1}[V]=\{f\in Y^X:f(x)\in V\}$, $x\in X$, $V\subseteq Y$ open, and its basic open sets impose open-set constraints at finitely many points of $X$. ([[def-topology-of-pointwise-convergence]], [[def-subspace-topology-top]])

[F4] In the product topology on $Y^X$ the basic open sets are the boxes that restrict only finitely many coordinates. ([[def-product-topology]])

## Proof

**Given:** A discrete space $X$, a topological space $Y$, and the two topologies on $C(X,Y)$.

1.1 Every compact subset $K\subseteq X$ is finite: the family of singleton subsets $\{x\}$, $x\in K$, is an open cover of the subspace $K$ by [F1]. Compactness of $K$ gives a finite subcover, exhibiting $K$ as the union of finitely many singletons; for $K=\varnothing$, the empty family suffices. [F1]

1.2 For every finite $K\subseteq X$ and every open $V\subseteq Y$ the subbasic compact-open set is the finite intersection $S(K,V)=\bigcap_{x\in K}\{f\in C(X,Y):f(x)\in V\}$. [F2, F3]

2.1 Each compact-open subbasic set is a finite intersection of pointwise subbasic sets by steps 1.1 and 1.2, and each pointwise subbasic set $\{f\in C(X,Y):f(x)\in V\}$ equals $S(\{x\},V)$, which is compact-open subbasic because the singleton $\{x\}$ is compact by [F1]. A topology containing a family contains the topology generated by it, so the two topologies on $C(X,Y)$ each contain the other, hence are equal; by [F3] this common topology is the subspace topology inherited from $Y^X$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F2, F3]

3.1 Restricting an equality of topologies to a subset preserves it: for $\mathcal F\subseteq C(X,Y)$ the traces on $\mathcal F$ of the two topologies coincide. The pointwise topology is the subspace topology from $Y^X$ by [F3], and its basic open sets impose open-set constraints on finitely many coordinates by [F3, F4]; hence on $C(X,Y)$, and on every $\mathcal F\subseteq C(X,Y)$, those sets form a basis. [step 2.1, F3, F4] ∎
