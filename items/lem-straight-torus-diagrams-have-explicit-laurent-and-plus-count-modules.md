## Statement

Assume countable choice. On the flat complex torus $T=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z)$ fix an integer $p\ge1$ and let $\alpha$ be the image of the real line, and for $\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}$ let $\beta_\varepsilon$ be the image of the line of direction $1+p\varepsilon i$. Then
$$\alpha\cap\beta_\varepsilon=\{(j/p,0):0\le j<p\}\pmod{\mathbb Z^2}.$$
For any basepoint off the circles, every integral periodic domain with zero coefficient at that basepoint is zero; here a periodic domain is a region two-chain whose boundary is an integral sum of the two full circles. Thus this diagram satisfies even the global mixed-sign admissibility condition.

By a relative disk from $x$ to $y$ mean a continuous map from a closed disk with two marked boundary points to $T$, taking the two boundary arcs into $\alpha,\beta_\varepsilon$ and their endpoints to $x,y$. If $x\ne y$, no such disk exists. If $x=y$, every such disk is homotopic to the constant disk at $x$, through maps with the same boundary conditions and fixed endpoints.

Every actual smooth finite-energy strip for the flat equation $u_s+i u_t=0$, with its two boundary edges on these circles and uniform intersection-generator ends, is constant. The actual linearization at any constant strip is a bounded real Hilbert isomorphism $H^1_{\alpha,\beta_\varepsilon}\to L^2$, and therefore has real index zero. In particular every moduli space of index-one finite-energy strips is empty, for any basepoint away from the curves. The corresponding diagram-level mod-two count operator is zero on its $p$ generators, so its chain homology has dimension $p$. This is an actual calculation for the specified diagram; it does not supply closed-manifold move invariance or identify that homology for every diagram.

Write $\Lambda=\mathbb F_2[U,U^{-1}]$ and $R=\mathbb F_2[U]$. With $U[x,i]=[x,i-1]$, the corresponding total diagram-level infinity count complex is $\Lambda^p$ with zero differential, its subcomplex generated by $i<0$ is $(UR)^p$, and its plus quotient is $(\Lambda/UR)^p$ with zero differential. If the $p$ basis elements $[x_j,0]$ are assigned degrees $a_j$, the quotient has one plus tower of bottom degree $a_j$ for each generator. These are computations of actual count modules for the specified diagram; no assignment of generators to Spin$^c$ labels, absolute lens-space grading or diagram independence is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The flat quotient, its two straight circles, the indicated disk or actual finite-energy strip, and its uniform ends.

[F1] [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]] gives an actual bounded inverse of $\partial_s+i\partial_t$ on the whole strip with boundary planes $\mathbb R$ and $e^{i\theta}\mathbb R$ for every $0<\theta<\pi$, on the actual $H^1$ boundary completion.

## Proof

1.1 Parametrize the second circle by $(r,p\varepsilon r)$ modulo $\mathbb Z^2$. It meets the first exactly when $pr$ is an integer. Modulo its period one these give $r=j/p$, $0\le j<p$, and the tangent vectors $(1,0),(1,p\varepsilon)$ are independent. The inverse images in the plane of the two circles are respectively the disjoint lines $y=a$, $a\in\mathbb Z$, and $p\varepsilon x-y=b$, $b\in\mathbb Z$. For the latter assertion, its primitive integer normal vector is $(p\varepsilon,-1)$; its integer level lines are exactly the lattice translates of its real direction line. Thus each fixed pair of lifts intersects once, at $y=a$, $x=(a+b)/(p\varepsilon)$. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Every continuous disk map $v$ to this torus has a continuous plane lift; here is an explicit construction without a general covering-classification theorem. Regard the source as a convex round disk and fix a source point $p_0$ and one lift $V_0$ of $v(p_0)$. In the flat quotient metric, two points at distance less than $1/4$ have a unique difference vector with both coordinates in $(-1/2,1/2)$; call it $d(a,b)$, pointing from $a$ to $b$. This vector depends continuously on such pairs, as is checked in ordinary small coordinate squares of the quotient. Uniform continuity on the compact source gives an integer $N$ such that adjacent points of every equally subdivided segment from $p_0$ to any $p$ have images this close. Explicitly choose the subdivision mesh smaller than a uniform-continuity radius; a uniform radius follows from a finite subcover by the inverse images of small torus coordinate squares. Put $p_j=(1-j/N)p_0+(j/N)p$ and $$V(p)=V_0+\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}d(v(p_j),v(p_{j+1})).$$ It is continuous and its quotient is $v(p)$ by telescoping. A lift of a smooth map is smooth locally because the quotient is a local translation diffeomorphism. Any two lifts of the same map differ by a continuous integer vector and hence by a constant on a connected source. All selections here were finite. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We verify the periodic-domain assertion directly. In the plane, the complementary regions are the parallelograms $a<y<a+1$, $b<p\varepsilon x-y<b+1$, for integers $a,b$. Each projects injectively to the torus: a difference by $(l,k)\in\mathbb Z^2$ within one such region forces $k=0$ from the first inequalities, then $l=0$ from the second. There are $p$ quotient regions, since shifting $a$ to zero leaves $b$ modulo $p$. The integral of each of the closed one-forms $dx,dy$ around every region boundary is zero, by the ordinary coordinate fundamental theorem on its plane parallelogram. Hence if a periodic domain has boundary $c\alpha+d\beta_\varepsilon$, these integrals give $c+d=0$ and $p\varepsilon d=0$. Thus $c=d=0$. Its region coefficients are consequently equal on opposite sides of every edge. They are all equal because a path between any two region interiors on the connected torus can be chosen to cross the finite edges away from vertices; equivalently the $p$ explicit parallelograms have connected adjacency graph. The zero basepoint coefficient therefore makes the entire domain zero. This calculation assumes no domain-index formula or admissibility theorem. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Lift a relative disk by step 1.2. On its first boundary arc the second coordinate of the lift is a continuous integer-valued function, so it is one constant $a$. On its second arc $p\varepsilon V_x-V_y$ is likewise one integer $b$. Therefore both lifted endpoints lie at the unique intersection of these two affine lines from step 1.1. Their projections must coincide, proving impossibility when $x\ne y$. In the remaining case call that intersection $c$. The affine homotopy $V_h=(1-h)V+hc$ fixes both endpoints and keeps each boundary arc in its own affine line. Its quotient is an actual relative homotopy to the constant disk at $x$. Thus the relative class assertion has been proved directly; no assertion about periodic domains or Maslov grading formulas was used. [step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 A strip with uniform ends compactifies continuously to such a disk: the explicit maps $z=e^{\pi(s+it)}$ and $z\mapsto(z-i)/(z+i)$ identify its interior with a round disk and its two ends with distinct boundary points. Its lift can equivalently be restricted from the compact-disk lift in step 1.2, so the lifted strip $V$ has both ends equal to $c$ and its boundaries on the two corresponding lines. Subtract $c$ and call the map $W$. The quotient is locally holomorphic with derivative the identity, hence $W_s+iW_t=0$. Its flat energy is unchanged by lifting, and $W$ tends uniformly to zero at both ends. For the real one-form $\lambda=(x\,dy-y\,dx)/2$ on the plane, direct differentiation gives $$\partial_s\lambda(W_t)-\partial_t\lambda(W_s)=\det(W_s,W_t)=|W_s|^2,$$ since the equation gives $W_t=iW_s$. On either horizontal edge $W$ and its tangential derivative lie in a single real line through zero, so $\lambda(W_s)=0$. Integrating the displayed identity on any finite strip rectangle by two one-variable fundamental theorems therefore expresses its nonnegative energy $\int|W_s|^2$ as the difference of the two vertical-edge integrals of $\lambda$. This proves the needed area identity without a stronger-choice Stokes supplier. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Finite energy makes $f(s)=\int_0^1|W_t(s,t)|^2dt$ integrable and continuous. Choose $S_n\in(n,n+1)$ and $R_n\in(n,n+1)$ so $f(S_n),f(-R_n)$ tend to zero. Such choices can be specified as the first rationals whose values are smaller than twice the corresponding interval integral plus $1/n$, because the average argument and continuity give a nonempty open set of such values. The vertical boundary integrals in step 3.1 have absolute values at most $$\tfrac12\sup_{0\le t\le1}|W(s,t)|\, f(s)^{1/2}.$$ They tend to zero at $s=S_n,-R_n$ by the uniform ends. The increasing rectangles $[-R_n,S_n]\times[0,1]$ exhaust the strip. Their nonnegative energy integrals therefore have limit zero, so the smooth nonnegative density $|W_s|^2$ vanishes identically. The equation gives $W_t=0$ as well. Thus every such actual strip is constant. This proof does not assume that all representatives in a null relative class are constant; it proves the assertion for the actual flat holomorphic equation. [step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 At a constant strip the geometric operator is exactly $D_0=\partial_s+i\partial_t$, with lower boundary line $\mathbb R$ and upper line of direction $1+p\varepsilon i$. The latter equals $e^{i\theta}\mathbb R$, with $\theta=\arctan p$ if $\varepsilon=1$ and $\theta=\pi-\arctan p$ if $\varepsilon=-1$. Both angles lie strictly between zero and $\pi$. The actual inverse [F1] consequently proves surjectivity and zero kernel, hence real index zero. Together with step 4.1 this leaves no actual index-one solution. The empty count is finite and zero for every basepoint restriction, and the finite $p$-element generator module from step 1.1 has zero differential and therefore $p$-dimensional homology. The sign choice was retained throughout, so the calculation treats both diagrams. Only Countable Choice, the explicitly stated linear supplier and finite or rational-recursive constructions were used. No closed-manifold invariance theorem was inferred. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The same empty actual index-one moduli make every coefficient in the infinity count operator zero, including all nonnegative basepoint multiplicities. Identifying $[x_j,i]=U^{-i}[x_j,0]$ gives the free module $\Lambda^p$. The submodule $i<0$ consists exactly of positive powers $U^n[x_j,0]$, $n\ge1$, hence is $(UR)^p$. Its quotient has basis $U^{-r}[x_j,0]$, $r\ge0$, in the $j$th summand. Multiplication by $U$ kills $r=0$ and sends $r$ to $r-1$ for $r>0$. If the basis degree is $a_j$, these classes have degrees $a_j+2r$, so the bottom is $a_j$ and the displayed quotient is the usual plus tower. All differentials are zero, so these also compute their diagram-level homology modules. The only geometric count used was the actual absence of index-one strips; no general Floer package, Spin$^c$ assignment, absolute grading formula or move invariance entered this computation. [given, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
