## Statement

Let F:D^{op}→Cat be a strict contravariant functor of small categories and suppose each F(a) induces a weak homotopy equivalence. For its actual Cartesian Grothendieck category E=∫_D F and each d, the strict fiber map BF(d)→hofib_d(BE→BD), given by the constant base path, is a weak homotopy equivalence on components and at every fiber basepoint. The covariant dual and the actual Cartesian/opfibered comma-model consequences hold with the specified transports. No choice principle or chosen inverse of spaces is assumed.

1.1 This proves one of the finite-coordinate realization steps required by the remaining Q argument. It does not assert the separate homotopy-fiber realization theorem. The statement is reconstructed from the actual definition of the two realization quotients, with no bisimplicial theorem imported. Let X be any bisimplicial set. Its double realization is the quotient of the disjoint union of X_(p,q)×Δ^p×Δ^q by both sets of face and degeneracy relations. Its diagonal simplicial set has n-simplices X_(n,n), with each simplicial operator applied in BOTH directions. There is a natural homeomorphism between the realization of this diagonal and the double realization, in ZF. 1. The immediate map. Send a diagonal point [y;(w_0,…,w_n)] to the double point [y;(w_0,…,w_n),(w_0,…,w_n)]. A common simplicial operator gives the same affine pushforward of barycentric coordinates in both factors, exactly one of the double-realization relations. Hence this defines a continuous natural map H from the diagonal realization to the double one. 2. The inverse by ordered-interval coupling. For x∈X_(p,q) and coordinates u∈Δ^p,v∈Δ^q, mark the cumulative sums of u and of v in the unit interval. Their finite union, including0 and1, gives consecutive intervals with lengths w_0,…,w_r. Omit zero lengths. On each open interval the u-partition has a constant index α(k)∈{0,…,p} and the v-partition a constant index β(k)∈{0,…,q}. Both α and β are weakly increasing. Pull x back in its two simplicial directions along these actual order maps to obtain $$y=(\alpha^*,\beta^*)x\in X_{r,r}.$$ Define G[x;u,v]=[y;w] in the diagonal realization. The length1 interval includes the case r=0; zero original coordinates simply skip the corresponding original indices. The construction is specified entirely by finitely many comparisons and differences of real numbers. It chooses no triangulation from a family. For any weakly increasing map θ on the horizontal index set, the realization relation replaces the horizontal simplex by θ*x and its coordinates by an affine pushforward θ_*u. The ordered partition of θ_*u is precisely the partition obtained by merging the consecutive u-intervals mapped by θ to the same new index; zero coordinates have the same interpretation. Refining also by the unchanged v-partition, the two coupling constructions have the same pairs of resulting indices and the same lengths, except that adjacent pairs that become identical have been merged. An identical adjacent pair means that BOTH pulled-back order maps repeat at that position. The corresponding diagonal simplex is therefore a diagonal degeneracy, and merging its two weights is exactly the diagonal realization relation. Deleting a zero weight is its face relation. This proves equality of the two values of G for a horizontal face or degeneracy relation. The same argument applies vertically. Since these generate the double quotient relations, G is well defined. Naturality in X follows at once: a bisimplicial map commutes with both of the displayed pullback operators and does not change the coordinates. 3. Actual continuity. On a fixed product Δ^p×Δ^q, order all cumulative sums of u and v. The finitely many possible weak orders divide it into finitely many closed polyhedra. On one such polyhedron, the index strings α,β are fixed; all new weights are the differences of consecutive cumulative sums and hence are affine functions of u,v. Consequently G restricted to that piece is a continuous map into one fixed diagonal simplex, followed by its realization quotient map. When two orders agree on a wall, at least one of those differences is zero. Deleting the corresponding zero-weight position gives the same pulled-back diagonal simplex on that wall, by the face relations checked in section2. Identical adjacent index pairs are likewise handled by the already checked degeneracy relation. Finite closed pasting therefore gives continuity on the entire product simplex. The final topology defining double realization now proves continuity of G. No local finiteness or uncountable gluing hypothesis has been assumed: continuity is tested on each quotient product simplex. 4. Check BOTH compositions. In double realization, [y;w,w], for y=(α*,β*)x, is identified with [x;α_*w,β_*w]. The total length of the coupling intervals whose first index is i is exactly u_i, and similarly the total length whose second index is j is v_j. Thus α_*w=u and β_*w=v, proving HG=id pointwise. Conversely start with a diagonal point [y;w]. The two ordered partitions used by G on [y;w,w] coincide. After omitting zero weights, their paired indices are exactly (i,i) in order with the original lengths w_i. The resulting common pullback of y is its corresponding common face after the zero indices are deleted; reinserting those zero weights is precisely the diagonal face relation. Hence GH=id, including degenerate simplices and the zero-dimensional cases. Therefore H and G are inverse continuous maps, naturally in the bisimplicial set. This is a HOMEOMORPHISM, not a homology comparison or a Whitehead upgrade. Replacing all realizations by their compactly generated versions preserves the two inverse maps and their identities.
