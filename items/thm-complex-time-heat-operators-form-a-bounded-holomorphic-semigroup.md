## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$ and $1\le p\le\infty$. For
$z\in S_\theta$ and $f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ define
$$H_zf:=\Gamma_z*f,$$
the convolution with the complex-time kernel of
[[def-complex-time-heat-kernel-on-a-proper-sector]]. Then:

(i) $H_z$ is a bounded operator on $L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ with
$\|H_z\|_{L^p\to L^p}\le(\cos\sigma)^{-n/2}$ whenever
$|\arg z|\le\sigma<\theta$;

(ii) $H_zH_w=H_{z+w}$ whenever $z,w,z+w\in S_\theta$;

(iii) for every $f$ the map $z\mapsto H_zf$ is complex differentiable on
$S_\theta$ in the norm of $L^p(\mathbb R^n)$, with derivative
$(\partial_z\Gamma_z)*f$; consequently $z\mapsto H_z$ is holomorphic in
operator norm on every proper subsector $S_\sigma$, $\sigma<\theta$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice, $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$, $1\le p\le\infty$, and $f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n)$.

[A1] Countable Choice is the ambient hypothesis ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The kernel satisfies $\|\Gamma_z\|_1\le(\cos\sigma)^{-n/2}$ for $|\arg z|\le\sigma<\theta$, and $\int_{\mathbb R^n}\Gamma_z(x)\,dx=1$ for every $z\in S_\theta$, with $\Re(1/z)>0$ throughout the sector ([[def-complex-time-heat-kernel-on-a-proper-sector]]).

[F2] The scalar identity theorem extends equality of holomorphic functions from the positive real axis to the connected sector ([[thm-identity-theorem-holomorphic-functions]]). The sector is a convex cone: $z\in S_\theta$ means $\operatorname{Re}z>0$ and $|\operatorname{Im}z|<\tan\theta\operatorname{Re}z$, so it is closed under addition. The kernels are bounded functions of space by their Gaussian bounds.

[F3] The map $z\mapsto\Gamma_z$ is complex differentiable into $L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ with derivative $\partial_z\Gamma_z$, and the convergence of the difference quotients is uniform on compact subsets of $S_\theta$ ([[lem-complex-time-heat-kernel-is-lone-differentiable-in-its-parameter]]).

[F4] Young's convolution inequality in the form used for the heat kernel: for $1\le p\le\infty$, $\|K*g\|_p\le\|K\|_1\|g\|_p$ ([[thm-young-convolution-inequality]], [[def-convolution-of-two-functions-on-rn]]); for real positive times the same computation with real kernels is the semigroup identity [[lem-heat-kernel-semigroup-identity]] ([[lem-heat-kernel-normalisation-scaling-and-derivatives]]).

[F5] The dominated convergence and compactness inputs used to pass to limits in the parameter are those of [[thm-dominated-convergence]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]] and [[def-metric-compactness]]; the Laplacian enters the real-time case only through [[def-laplacian-of-a-c2-function]].

## Proof

**Given:** Countable Choice, $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$, $1\le p\le\infty$, and $f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n)$.

1.1 For $|\arg z|\le\sigma<\theta$, [F4] and the $L^1$ bound of [F1] give $\|H_zf\|_p=\|\Gamma_z*f\|_p\le\|\Gamma_z\|_1\|f\|_p\le(\cos\sigma)^{-n/2}\|f\|_p$, which is (i). [A1, F1, F4, given]

1.2 Fix $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and a real $w>0$. The map $z\mapsto(\Gamma_z*\Gamma_w)(x)$ is holomorphic on $S_\theta$: pairing the $L^1$ difference quotients of [F3] with the bounded function $y\mapsto\Gamma_w(x-y)$ proves scalar differentiability. The map $z\mapsto\Gamma_{z+w}(x)$ is holomorphic by the kernel definition and [F2]. They agree for real $z>0$ by [F4], so [F2] gives equality for every complex $z\in S_\theta$. Now fix such a complex $z$. The same argument with the bounded kernel $\Gamma_z$ makes $w\mapsto(\Gamma_z*\Gamma_w)(x)$ holomorphic, and $w\mapsto\Gamma_{z+w}(x)$ is holomorphic because addition stays in the sector. They agree for real $w>0$ by the first application, so the identity theorem gives $\Gamma_z*\Gamma_w=\Gamma_{z+w}$ for all $z,w\in S_\theta$. Every scalar convolution is absolutely convergent since one kernel is bounded and the other is integrable. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given]


2.1 The nonnegative double integral $\iint|\Gamma_z(x-y)\Gamma_w(y-v)f(v)|dv\,dy$ is finite for almost every $x$: its $L^p$ bound is at most $\|\Gamma_z\|_1\|\Gamma_w\|_1\|f\|_p$ by two applications of Young [F4], with the pointwise bound for $p=\infty$. Thus Fubini ([[thm-tonelli-and-fubini-for-completed-product-measures]]) and step 1.2 give $H_zH_wf=(\Gamma_z*\Gamma_w)*f=\Gamma_{z+w}*f=H_{z+w}f$ as $L^p$ classes. This proves (ii), including both exponent endpoints. [step 1.2, F1, F4, given]


2.2 By [F3] and [F4], $\bigl\|(H_{z+h}f-H_zf)/h-(\partial_z\Gamma_z)*f\bigr\|_p\le\|f\|_p\bigl\|(\Gamma_{z+h}-\Gamma_z)/h-\partial_z\Gamma_z\bigr\|_1\to0$, so $z\mapsto H_zf$ is norm differentiable with the stated derivative; the convergence of the difference quotients in $L^1$ is uniform on compact subsets of $S_\theta$ by [F3], so the same estimate gives local convergence in operator norm on every proper subsector, i.e. $z\mapsto H_z$ is holomorphic in operator norm on $S_\sigma$, $\sigma<\theta$. [step 1.1, F3, F4, F5, given]

3.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1 and 2.2 establish the boundedness estimate (i), the semigroup law (ii) and the differentiability statement (iii) with its operator-norm consequence. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, given] ∎
