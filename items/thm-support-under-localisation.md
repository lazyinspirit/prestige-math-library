## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, let $S \subseteq R$ be multiplicative, and let $M$ be a left $R$-module. Under the prime-ideal correspondence
$$
\{\mathfrak p \subseteq R : \mathfrak p \cap S=\varnothing\}\longleftrightarrow \operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}R), \qquad \mathfrak p \longmapsto S^{-1}\mathfrak p,
$$
the support of $S^{-1}M$ corresponds exactly to
$$
\{\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(M) : \mathfrak p \cap S=\varnothing\}.
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a multiplicative subset $S \subseteq R$, and a left $R$-module $M$.

[L1] A prime ideal lies in the support of a module exactly when the localisation at that prime is nonzero ([[def-support-of-a-module]]).

[L2] Prime ideals of $S^{-1}R$ correspond to prime ideals of $R$ disjoint from $S$ ([[thm-ideal-correspondence-for-localisation]]).

[L3] Localising twice is the same as localising once at the multiplicative set generated by the two denominator sets ([[prop-iterated-localisation]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\mathfrak q$ be a prime ideal of $S^{-1}R$, and let $\mathfrak p \subseteq R$ be its contraction. By [L2], $\mathfrak p \cap S=\varnothing$ and $\mathfrak q=S^{-1}\mathfrak p$. [L2]

2.1 The localisation $(S^{-1}M)_{\mathfrak q}$ is obtained by localising $M$ first at $S$ and then at the complement of $\mathfrak q$. By [L3], this is the same as localising $M$ once at the multiplicative subset $R \setminus \mathfrak p$, so $(S^{-1}M)_{\mathfrak q}\cong M_{\mathfrak p}$. [step 1.1, L3]

3.1 By [L1], step 2.1 gives $\mathfrak q \in \operatorname{Supp}_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M)$ if and only if $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(M)$. Together with step 1.1, this proves the stated description of support under localisation. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
