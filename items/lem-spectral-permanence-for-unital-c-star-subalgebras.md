## Statement

Assume AC. If $B$ is a unital C\*-subalgebra of a unital C\*-algebra $A$ with the same identity, then $\sigma_B(b)=\sigma_A(b)$ for every $b\in B$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] A unital C\*-algebra is a nonzero complex Banach algebra with a unit of norm one and an involution satisfying the C\*-identity; a unital C\*-subalgebra with the same identity is a closed unital $\ast$-subalgebra containing that unit ([[def-c-star-algebra]], [[def-unital-banach-algebra]]).

[A2] For every element $b$ invertible in $A$ the adjoint $b^*$ is invertible with $(b^* )^{-1}=(b^{-1})^*$, and $b^*b$ is positive; positivity of an element means $x=y^*y$ for some element $y$ ([[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-elements-of-a-c-star-algebra]] for positivity, [[def-c-star-algebra]] for the involution rules).

[A3] If $B\subseteq A$ is a unital subalgebra with the same unit and $b\in B$, then $\sigma_A(b)\subseteq\sigma_B(b)$, since invertibility in $B$ implies invertibility in $A$ ([[def-spectrum-and-resolvent-set-in-a-banach-algebra]]).

[A4] For a nonzero unital commutative complex C\*-algebra $C$ the Gelfand transform $\Gamma:C\to C(\Delta(C))$ is an isometric unital $\ast$-isomorphism onto $C(\Delta(C))$, and $\Delta(C)$ is a nonempty compact Hausdorff space ([[thm-commutative-gelfand-naimark]], [[thm-maximal-ideal-space-is-compact-hausdorff]], [[def-character-and-maximal-ideal-space]]).

[A5] A unital self-adjoint complex function algebra that separates the points of a nonempty compact Hausdorff space is uniformly dense in all continuous complex functions ([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]]).

[A6] Characters of a unital commutative complex C\*-algebra satisfy $\chi(c^*)=\overline{\chi(c)}$ ([[lem-characters-on-a-commutative-c-star-algebra-preserve-star]]).

[A7] AC is the global hypothesis of the Gelfand-theoretic suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** A unital C\*-algebra $A$, a unital C\*-subalgebra $B\subseteq A$ with $1_B=1_A$, and $b\in B$.

1.1 Since invertibility in $B$ implies invertibility in $A$, one has $\sigma_A(b)\subseteq\sigma_B(b)$; it remains to prove the reverse inclusion, that is, that $b$ invertible in $A$ is invertible in $B$ with inverse in $B$. [A3]

1.2 If $b$ is invertible in $A$, then $x:=b^*b\in B$ is positive and invertible in $A$ with $x^{-1}=b^{-1}(b^{-1})^*$; and if $x$ positive and invertible in $A$ has $x^{-1}\in B$, then $(b^*b)^{-1}\in B$ and $b^{-1}=(b^*b)^{-1}b^*\in B$. [A1, A2, algebra]

1.3 It suffices to treat a self-adjoint $x\in B$ invertible in $A$, because the positive element $b^*b$ in the preceding reduction is self-adjoint. For such $x$, let $C:=C^*_A(1,x,x^{-1})$ be the closed unital star-subalgebra of $A$ generated by $x$ and $x^{-1}$, and let $D:=C^*_A(1,x)\subseteq B$. Both are commutative, because $x=x^*$ and $(x^{-1})^*=x^{-1}$, and the Gelfand transform is an isometric unital star-isomorphism $\Gamma:C\to C(\Delta(C))$ on the nonempty compact Hausdorff character space. [A1, A2, A4, A7, algebra]

2.1 The function $\widehat x$ separates the points of $\Delta(C)$. Indeed, if characters $h,h'$ have $h(x)=h'(x)$, then $h(x^{-1})=h(x)^{-1}=h'(x)^{-1}=h'(x^{-1})$; they therefore agree on the unital star-algebra generated by $x,x^{-1}$ and, by continuity, on its closure $C$, so $h=h'$. Moreover $\widehat x$ is real-valued because $x=x^*$ and characters preserve the involution. [step 1.3, A6, algebra]

3.1 Under $\Gamma$, the algebra $D$ is the closed unital self-adjoint function algebra generated by $\widehat x$. It separates points by step 2.1, so Stone--Weierstrass gives $\Gamma(D)=C(\Delta(C))=\Gamma(C)$. Injectivity of $\Gamma$ yields $D=C$, hence $x^{-1}\in D\subseteq B$. [step 2.1, A4, A5]

4.1 Therefore every $b\in B$ invertible in $A$ has $(b^*b)^{-1}\in B$ by step 3.1 and then $b^{-1}=(b^*b)^{-1}b^*\in B$ by step 1.2. Thus $\sigma_B(b)\subseteq\sigma_A(b)$; with step 1.1 this gives $\sigma_B(b)=\sigma_A(b)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1] ∎
