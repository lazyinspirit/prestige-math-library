## Statement

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum, use the notation $q_i$, $K_h$ and $K_i=K_{d_i h_i}$ of [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]], and put $m_{ij}=1-a_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$. Let $T^+$ and $T^-$ be the free $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebras on the symbols $E_i,K_h$ and $F_i,K_h$, respectively. Let $R^+$ and $R^-$ be the two-sided ideals generated by $K_0-1$, $K_hK_{h'}-K_{h+h'}$, and respectively $K_hE_i-q^{\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}E_iK_h$ or $K_hF_i-q^{-\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle}F_iK_h$. Define the toral-action algebras $B^+=T^+/R^+$ and $B^-=T^-/R^-$, and let $J^+$ and $J^-$ be their two-sided ideals generated by the images of the corresponding Drinfeld–Jimbo Serre elements.

The generator assignments

$$\Delta_+(K_h)=K_h\otimes K_h,\qquad \Delta_+(E_i)=E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}+1\otimes E_i,\qquad \Delta_-(K_h)=K_h\otimes K_h,\qquad \Delta_-(F_i)=F_i\otimes1+K_i\otimes F_i$$

define algebra homomorphisms $B^\pm\to B^\pm\otimes_{\mathbb Q(q)}B^\pm$. For every $i\ne j$, the Serre elements are quasiprimitive:

$$\Delta_+(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})=\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij}\otimes K_i^{-m_{ij}}K_j^{-1}+1\otimes\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij},\qquad \Delta_-(\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij})=\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}\otimes1+K_i^{m_{ij}}K_j\otimes\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}.$$

Consequently $\Delta_\pm(J^\pm)\subseteq J^\pm\otimes B^\pm+B^\pm\otimes J^\pm$. The canonical maps $B^\pm\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ send the displayed coproducts of the Serre elements to zero in $U_q(\mathfrak g)\otimes U_q(\mathfrak g)$; thus these formulas provide the Serre-family part of the check that the Drinfeld–Jimbo coproduct descends.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum over $\mathbb Q(q)$ and the defining relations and Serre sums in [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]].

[F1] For $a=a_{ij}$ and $m=1-a$, symmetrizability gives $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$, so $K_jE_iK_j^{-1}=q_i^aE_i$; also $K_iE_jK_i^{-1}=q_i^aE_j$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F2] The Drinfeld–Jimbo toral-action relations are those displayed in [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]].

[F3] The positive and negative Serre sums are those displayed in [[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]].

[F4] The symmetric Gaussian coefficients are factorial quotients $C_{N,r}=\binom Nr_i=[N]_i!/([r]_i![N-r]_i!)$ and satisfy $C_{N,r}=C_{N,N-r}$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F5] If $x,y$ lie in an algebra and $yx=q_i^2xy$, then $(x+y)^N=\sum_{r=0}^Nq_i^{r(N-r)}\binom Nr_i x^ry^{N-r}$ ([[lem-q-binomial-expansion-for-q-commuting-elements]]).

[F6] For every $N\ge1$, $\sum_{r=0}^N(-1)^r q_i^{r(N-1)}C_{N,r}=0$ ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

[F7] The tensor product has multiplication $(a\otimes b)(c\otimes d)=ac\otimes bd$ ([[thm-tensor-product-of-algebras-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F8] The tensor algebra on the free generators admits the unique algebra extension of every generator assignment ([[def-tensor-algebra-of-a-vector-space]], [[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]).

[F9] Quotient algebras and factor maps are as in [[def-quotient-ring]] and [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]].

[F10] A two-sided ideal generated by a subset is as in [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Work first in the toral-action quotients, expand each coproduct by the q-binomial theorem, and cancel every mixed bidegree by the alternating Gaussian identity [F6].

1.1 Write $a=a_{ij}$, $m=1-a$, $C_{n,r}=\binom nr_i$, and let $T^\pm,R^\pm,B^\pm,J^\pm$ be as in the statement. The algebra maps on $T^\pm$ prescribed by the displayed coproduct assignments exist by the tensor-algebra universal property in [F8], with the target tensor products made into algebras by [F7]. [given, F7, F8, construct]

1.2 For every $N\ge1$, the coefficient identity [F6] cancels the alternating sum with exponent $(N-1)r$. For $N=0$ the corresponding one-term sum equals $1$. These two cases will distinguish the mixed terms from the two extreme bidegrees below. [F6, algebra]

2.1 The images of $K_0-1$ and $K_hK_{h'}-K_{h+h'}$ vanish because $\Delta(K_0)=1\otimes1$ and $\Delta(K_hK_{h'})=K_hK_{h'}\otimes K_hK_{h'}=\Delta(K_{h+h'})$. For $b=\langle\alpha_i,h\rangle$, in the positive algebra $\Delta(K_h)\Delta(E_i)-q^b\Delta(E_i)\Delta(K_h)=0$: its two summands cancel by $K_hE_i=q^bE_iK_h$ in the first tensor factor and in the second tensor factor, while the toral elements commute. In the negative algebra, $\Delta(K_h)\Delta(F_i)-q^{-b}\Delta(F_i)\Delta(K_h)=0$ by $K_hF_i=q^{-b}F_iK_h$ in the first and second tensor factors and commutation of toral elements. Thus both maps kill $R^\pm$ and descend to $B^\pm$ by [F9]. [step 1.1, F2, F7, F9, algebra]

3.1 In $B^+$, put $x_i=E_i\otimes K_i^{-1}$ and $y_i=1\otimes E_i$. The toral-action relation gives $y_ix_i=q_i^2x_iy_i$, so [F5] yields $\Delta_+(E_i)^N=\sum_{r=0}^Nq_i^{r(N-r)}C_{N,r}E_i^r\otimes K_i^{-r}E_i^{N-r}$. Also $K_i$ is grouplike and invertible since $K_hK_{-h}=1$ in $B^\pm$. [step 2.1, F1, F2, F5, algebra]

4.1 Expand $\Delta_+(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})=\sum_{s=0}^m(-1)^sC_{m,s}\Delta_+(E_i)^{m-s}\Delta_+(E_j)\Delta_+(E_i)^s$ and first take the $E_j\otimes K_j^{-1}$ term from the middle factor. Fix $r,t\ge0$ with $M=m-r-t\ge0$, and put $k=s-t$; the allowed indices are $0\le k\le M$. Using [F5] on the two powers of $\Delta(E_i)$, the corresponding tensor word is $E_i^rE_jE_i^t\otimes K_i^{-(r+t)}K_j^{-1}E_i^M$, and its coefficient is $(-1)^{t+k}C_{m,r}C_{m-r,t}C_{M,k}q_i^{(r+a+2t)M+(M-1)k}$. Here $C_{m,s}C_{m-s,r}C_{s,t}=C_{m,r}C_{m-r,t}C_{M,k}$ follows by cancelling the factorial quotients, and the exponent comes from moving $E_i^{M-k}$ past $K_j^{-1}$ and $K_i^{-t}$. [step 3.1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

4.2 Now take the $1\otimes E_j$ term from the middle factor. Fix $u,v\ge0$ with $k=m-u-v\ge0$; after moving toral factors to the left, the tensor word is $E_i^k\otimes K_i^{-k}E_i^uE_jE_i^v$. Its coefficient, summed over $r+t=k$ with $t=k-r$, is $(-1)^{v+k}\binom{m}{u,v,k}_i q_i^{k(u+1-k)}\sum_{r=0}^k(-1)^rC_{k,r}q_i^{(k-1)r}$, where $\binom{m}{u,v,k}_i=[m]_i!/([u]_i![v]_i![k]_i!)$. Indeed the unsummed Gaussian factor is $C_{m,v+t}C_{u+r,r}C_{v+t,t}=\binom{m}{u,v,k}_iC_{k,r}$, and the two q-binomial expansions and toral crossings give exponent $ru+tv+t(a+2u)=k(u+1-k)+(k-1)r$, using $a+u+v=1-k$. The prefactor is independent of $r$ and is $1$ when $k=0$. [step 3.1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

5.1 If $M>0$, summing the coefficients in step 4.1 over $k$ gives a scalar multiple of $\sum_{k=0}^M(-1)^kC_{M,k}q_i^{(M-1)k}=0$ by step 1.2. If $M=0$, only $k=0$ remains; then $r+t=m$, and summing over these pairs gives $\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij}\otimes K_i^{-m}K_j^{-1}$, using $C_{m,r}=C_{m,m-r}$. Thus the $E_j\otimes K_j^{-1}$ part contributes exactly the first term in the claimed formula. [step 1.2, step 4.1, F4, F6, algebra]

6.1 For $k>0$, the sum in step 4.2 is zero by step 1.2. For $k=0$, the only term has $u+v=m$, and its coefficient is $(-1)^vC_{m,v}$; these terms sum to $1\otimes\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij}$. Combining this with step 5.1 proves the positive quasiprimitive identity. [step 1.2, step 4.2, step 5.1, F4, F6, algebra]

7.1 Define $\omega:B^+\to B^-$ by $K_h\mapsto K_{-h}$ and $E_i\mapsto-F_i$. The toral and positive action relations map to the toral and negative action relations (the action relation at $-h$), so this is a well-defined algebra map. Directly on generators, $\Delta_-\omega=\mathrm{flip}\circ(\omega\otimes\omega)\circ\Delta_+$: on $K_h$ both sides equal $K_{-h}\otimes K_{-h}$, and on $E_i$ both sides equal $-F_i\otimes1-K_i\otimes F_i$. Moreover $\omega(\mathrm{Serre}^+_{ij})=(-1)^{m+1}\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}$ by applying the substitution to its $m+1$ factors. Applying this identity to the positive quasiprimitive formula gives $\Delta_-(\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij})=\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}\otimes1+K_i^mK_j\otimes\mathrm{Serre}^-_{ij}$. [step 2.1, step 6.1, F2, F7, algebra]

8.1 The subspace $J^+\otimes B^++B^+\otimes J^+$ is a two-sided ideal of $B^+\otimes B^+$ by the tensor multiplication in [F7], and likewise for the negative sign. The two quasiprimitive identities therefore imply $\Delta_\pm(J^\pm)\subseteq J^\pm\otimes B^\pm+B^\pm\otimes J^\pm$, since each $J^\pm$ is generated as a two-sided ideal by the Serre elements and $\Delta_\pm$ is an algebra map. The canonical maps $B^\pm\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ send every Serre generator to zero; applying their tensor squares to the formulas gives zero, so the Serre relations are preserved by the prescribed coproduct in the full Drinfeld–Jimbo quotient. Once the other defining relation families are checked, the quotient universal property in [F9] gives the descended coproduct. [step 2.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, F2, F3, F7, F9, F10, algebra] ∎

## Source note

The Berkeley text defines its toral-action-only positive Borel and gives the positive quasiprimitivity statement in Lemma 13.1.3.9, then says the cited Jantzen proof is a tedious q-binomial computation; it does not print the computation or the explicit grouplike factors. Jeong–Kang–Kashiwara display the coproduct convention used here in (1.6) but assert the Hopf structure without proving relation preservation. The coefficient cancellations in steps 4.1–6.1 are supplied locally; the original scaffold's claim that Serre-only ideals in the completely free algebra are coideals was replaced because its toral commutations are not valid before quotienting by the toral-action relations.
