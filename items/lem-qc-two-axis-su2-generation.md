## Statement

For a matrix $M$, write $e^M=\sum_{j\ge0}M^j/j!$. Write
$SU(2):=\{M\in M_2(\mathbb C):M^*M=I_2,\det M=1\}$. Let
$a,b\in\mathbb R^3$ be nonparallel unit vectors, let $\sigma=(X,Y,Z)$ be the
Pauli triple, and put $A=i\,a\cdot\sigma$ and $B=i\,b\cdot\sigma$. If $H$ is
the operator-norm closure of the subgroup generated by
$\{e^{tA},e^{tB}:t\in\mathbb R\}$, then $H=SU(2)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Nonparallel unit vectors $a,b\in\mathbb R^3$ and the Pauli
matrices $X,Y,Z$.

[F1] The Pauli-vector product and commutator identities hold, and the Pauli
triple is a real basis of traceless Hermitian $2\times2$ matrices
([[lem-qc-pauli-vector-multiplication]]).

[F2] A normal complex matrix has an orthonormal eigenbasis
([[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]]).

[F3] The operator norm is submultiplicative
([[cor-operator-norm-submultiplicative-and-t-star-t-identity]]).

[F4] Euler's formula and the Pythagorean identity give
$e^{it}=\cos t+i\sin t$ and $\cos^2t+\sin^2t=1$
([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]).

## Proof

1.1 Put $P_a=a\cdot\sigma$. By [F1], $P_a=P_a^*$, $P_a^2=I$ and $\operatorname{tr}P_a=0$. Its eigenvalues are $1,-1$ by [F2], so the power series gives $e^{tA}=I\cos t+iP_a\sin t$ and its eigenvalues are $e^{it},e^{-it}$. By [F4] it is unitary, and its determinant is one. The same holds for $B$. Thus the generated subgroup lies in $SU(2)$; its closure $H$ also lies there because the defining matrix equations for $SU(2)$ are closed. Continuity of multiplication and inversion makes $H$ a subgroup. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F1], $[A,B]=-2i(a\times b)\cdot\sigma$. Let $c=a\times b\ne0$ and $s_0=\max(\|A\|,\|B\|)$. For $|t|\le1$, multiplying the four exponential series $e^{tA}e^{tB}e^{-tA}e^{-tB}$ gives degree-one coefficient $A+B-A-B=0$ and degree-two coefficient $AB-BA=[A,B]$. Submultiplicativity bounds the sum of norms of all degree-$m$ terms by $(4s_0)^m/m!$. Hence the terms of degree at least three have norm at most $K|t|^3$, where $K=(4s_0)^3e^{4s_0}/6$. For fixed $r\ge0$, put $t=\sqrt{r/n}$. The commutator factor is $X_n=I+r[A,B]/n+R_n$ with $\|R_n\|\le Kr^{3/2}n^{-3/2}$. Set $Y_n=I+r[A,B]/n$. For fixed $r$, the norms of $X_n^k$ and $Y_n^k$ are bounded uniformly over $0\le k\le n$, because each one-step norm is at most $1+C_r/n$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The telescoping identity $X_n^n-Y_n^n=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}X_n^{n-1-k}(X_n-Y_n)Y_n^k$ bounds their difference by $O(n^{-1/2})$. The binomial expansion of $Y_n^n$ converges to the exponential series for $e^{r[A,B]}$: its $k$th term is bounded by $(r\|[A,B]\|)^k/k!$, a summable sequence. Swapping $A$ and $B$ gives $e^{-r[A,B]}$ for $r\ge0$. Every approximant belongs to the generated subgroup, so closedness gives $e^{s[A,B]}\in H$ for every real $s$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The vectors $a,b,c$ form a basis of $\mathbb R^3$, since $c$ is nonzero and orthogonal to both $a$ and $b$. By [F1], every traceless skew-Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix $L$ is a real linear combination $xA+yB+z[A,B]$. For fixed matrices $S,T$, exponential-series expansion gives $e^{S/n}e^{T/n}=I+(S+T)/n+O(n^{-2})$ and $e^{(S+T)/n}=I+(S+T)/n+O(n^{-2})$. The one-step difference is $O(n^{-2})$; the norms of intermediate powers up to $n$ are bounded by $e^{\|S\|+\|T\|+1}$ for sufficiently large $n$. Telescoping therefore gives $(e^{S/n}e^{T/n})^n\to e^{S+T}$. The same estimate for three factors gives $(e^{xA/n}e^{yB/n}e^{z[A,B]/n})^n\to e^L$. Every factor lies in $H$ by step 1.1 and step 2.1, so closedness gives $e^L\in H$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every $U\in SU(2)$ is normal, so [F2] diagonalizes it. Its eigenvalues lie on the unit circle and their product is one, so they are $e^{i\theta},e^{-i\theta}$ for some real $\theta$. In that orthonormal eigenbasis, the matrix with diagonal entries $i\theta,-i\theta$ is traceless and skew-Hermitian, and its exponential is $U$. Step 3.1 gives $U\in H$. Since $H\subseteq SU(2)$ by step 1.1, $H=SU(2)$. [F2, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
