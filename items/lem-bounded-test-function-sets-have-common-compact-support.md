## Statement

A set $B\subseteq\mathcal D(\Omega)$ is bounded (absorbed by every zero-neighborhood) if and only if there is compact $K\subseteq\Omega$ containing every support of its members and $\sup_{f\in B}p_m(f)<\infty$ for each $m\ge0$. A convergent sequence together with its limit is bounded. These assertions hold in ZF; no sequence of witnesses escaping compact sets is selected.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The LF topology is generated by all seminorms continuous on each fixed-support stage. Its global derivative suprema $Q_m$ restrict to $p_m$, and stage inclusions are continuous ([[lem-test-function-lf-topology-universal-property]]).

## Proof

**Given:** $B\subseteq\mathcal D(\Omega)$.

1.1 In a seminorm-generated topology, boundedness is equivalent to $\sup_B q<\infty$ for every defining seminorm $q$. Indeed absorption by $q<1$ bounds $q$ on $B$. Conversely, for finitely many constraints $q_i<\varepsilon_i$, choose $t>\max_i(\sup_B q_i/\varepsilon_i)$, with $t=1$ if there are no constraints, to absorb $B$ in their intersection. By F1, a bounded $B$ therefore satisfies $\sup_B Q_m<\infty$ for every $m$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 Assume $B$ bounded and take the explicit compact exhaustion $K_j=\{x:|x|\le j,\ \operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb R^n\setminus\Omega)\ge1/j\}$ for $j\ge1$, with distance to the empty set infinity and $K_0=\varnothing$. These closed bounded sets are compactly inside $\Omega$, satisfy $K_j\subseteq\operatorname{int}K_{j+1}$, and their interiors cover $\Omega$. Put $A_j=K_j\setminus K_{j-1}$. The disjoint sets $A_j$ cover $\Omega$, and each compact subset meets only finitely many of them, by a finite subcover from the interiors. Define $a_j=\sup\{|f(x)|:f\in B,x\in A_j\}$, with empty supremum zero. Step 1.1 bounds every $a_j$ by the finite number $\sup_B Q_0$. [step 1.1, F1]

3.1 Set $w(x)=j/a_j$ for $x\in A_j$ when $a_j>0$, and zero on shells with $a_j=0$. This finite nonnegative function is bounded on every compact subset, since only finitely many shells meet that subset. Continuity of $w$ is unnecessary. The seminorm $q(f)=\sup_{x\in\Omega}w(x)|f(x)|$ is finite for each test and restricts to a seminorm bounded by $\sup_K w\,p_0$ on each $\mathcal D_K$, hence belongs to the defining family of F1. For any occupied shell ($a_j>0$), $\sup_{f\in B}q(f)\ge (j/a_j)a_j=j$ by the supremum definition. If no $K_N$ contains all supports, for every $N$ some member has a nonzero value outside $K_N$ (otherwise its support, the closure of nonzero values, would lie in the closed set $K_N$). Thus occupied indices are unbounded, forcing $\sup_B q=\infty$, contrary to step 1.1. A common compact $K_N$ must exist, and its derivative bounds are the $Q_m$ bounds already obtained. [step 2.1, step 1.1, F1]

4.1 Conversely suppose the stated common support and derivative bounds hold. On $\mathcal D_K$, any defining seminorm $q$ is continuous. Its unit ball contains $p_m<\varepsilon$ for some $m,\varepsilon>0$; scaling as in step 1.1 gives $q(f)\le(2/\varepsilon)p_m(f)$, with the zero-seminorm case handled by arbitrarily large scaling. Hence $q$ is bounded on $B$, and step 1.1 proves LF boundedness. Finally, if $f_j\to f$, continuity of each seminorm gives $q(f_j-f)\to0$, so $\{q(f),q(f_j):j\ge1\}$ is bounded by a finite initial maximum and a bounded tail. Step 1.1 proves boundedness of the sequence and its limit. Empty $B$ uses $K=\varnothing$ and zero suprema; empty $\Omega$ has only the zero test. The weights in step 3.1 are defined from suprema, without any countable choice. $\square$ [step 3.1, step 1.1, F1]
