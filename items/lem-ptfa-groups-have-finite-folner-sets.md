## Statement

Every group admitting a finite subnormal series with torsion-free abelian factors satisfies the left Folner condition: for each finite subset $S$ and each $\varepsilon>0$ there exists a finite nonempty subset $F$ with $|sF\triangle F|<\varepsilon|F|$ for every $s\in S$. This assertion requires no countability or finite-generation hypothesis and no choice axiom. More generally the finite Folner condition is preserved by arbitrary group extensions whose kernel and quotient satisfy it.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite Folner condition and a finite PTFA subnormal series.

[F1] The finite Folner condition is precisely the finite-set and symmetric-difference condition in the Statement ([[def-folner-set-and-folner-condition]]).

[F2] Finitely generated torsion-free abelian groups are finite-rank free abelian groups ([[lem-finitely-generated-torsion-free-abelian-freeness-without-choice]]).

[F3] A PTFA series has each successive subgroup normal in the next, with torsion-free abelian quotient; normality in the final group is not required ([[def-poly-torsion-free-abelian-group]]).

## Proof

**Given:** A finite test set and a positive error tolerance whenever the Folner condition is tested.

1.1 A torsion-free abelian group satisfies [F1]. Indeed the subgroup generated by a finite test set is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^r$ by [F2]. For $r=0$ use the singleton identity set. For $r>0$ bound every test vector's coordinates in absolute value by $M\ge0$ and take the integer cube $E_n=[-n,n]^r\cap\mathbb Z^r$, with $n\ge M$. A translate by any test vector has intersection with $E_n$ of cardinality at least $(2n+1-M)^r$, whereas both sets have cardinality $(2n+1)^r$. Thus its relative symmetric difference is at most $2[1-(1-M/(2n+1))^r]$, which tends to zero. The transported cube witnesses the required condition in the original group. All choices concern a single finitely generated subgroup and a finite test set. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Suppose $1\to H\to G\xrightarrow{\pi}Q\to1$ is any group extension and both $H,Q$ satisfy [F1]. Fix finite $S\subseteq G$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Choose a finite nonempty $(\pi(S),\varepsilon/2)$-Folner set $E\subseteq Q$. For the finite set $E\cup\pi(S)E$, choose lifts $t_q\in G$. For each $s\in S$ and $q\in E$ with $\pi(s)q\in E$, put $h_{s,q}=t_{\pi(s)q}^{-1}st_q\in H$. This is a finite collection of elements of $H$. Choose a finite nonempty set $B\subseteq H$ with $|h_{s,q}B\triangle B|<(\varepsilon/2)|B|$ for all of them, and let $F=\bigcup_{q\in E}t_qB$. Its cosets are disjoint, so $|F|=|E||B|$. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 For $s\in S$, the cosets present in exactly one of $sF,F$ contribute $|\pi(s)E\triangle E||B|$ elements to the symmetric difference. On a common coset indexed by $\pi(s)q$, the two subsets are $t_{\pi(s)q}h_{s,q}B$ and $t_{\pi(s)q}B$, whose difference has cardinality $|h_{s,q}B\triangle B|$. There are at most $|E|$ common cosets. Therefore $|sF\triangle F|\le|\pi(s)E\triangle E||B|+\sum_{q\in E,\,\pi(s)q\in E}|h_{s,q}B\triangle B|<\varepsilon|E||B|=\varepsilon|F|$. This proves the extension assertion for an arbitrary extension; the lifts were not required to form a homomorphism or a global transversal. [F1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The trivial group satisfies [F1] using its singleton set. Induct up the given finite subnormal series. At each stage the kernel is the previous subgroup and the quotient is torsion-free abelian, so step 1.1 supplies the quotient condition and step 2.1 supplies the extension condition. This proves [F1] for every PTFA group in the full subnormal convention of [F3]. The construction uses only finitely many witnesses at each of finitely many stages. It constructs finite sets directly, without invariant means, ultrafilters or simultaneous choices over a family of tests, and hence adds no choice hypothesis. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
