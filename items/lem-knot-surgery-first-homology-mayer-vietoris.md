## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented knot in the oriented $3$-sphere, choose a closed tubular neighborhood $N(K)$, and set $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ and $T_K=\partial E_K=\partial N(K)$; assume $E_K$ is connected. Let $\mu$ be the oriented boundary of a normal disk whose orientation, preceded by the oriented tangent to $K$, agrees with the orientation of $S^3$. Let $\lambda$ be a simple closed curve on $T_K$ whose degree under the normal-tube projection $T_K\to K$ is $+1$ and whose image in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is zero; such a curve is a preferred longitude. Assume $([\mu],[\lambda])$ is an ordered basis of $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$. For coprime integers $p,q$ not both zero, let $E_K(p/q)$ be the filling whose attached solid-torus meridian maps to $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$. Then
$$H_1(E_K(p/q);\mathbb Z)\cong\begin{cases}\mathbb Z/|p|\mathbb Z,&p\ne0,\\[2pt]\mathbb Z,&p=0.\end{cases}$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth oriented knot $K\subset S^3$, its exterior $E_K$, tubular neighborhood $N(K)$, boundary torus $T_K$, meridian $\mu$, preferred longitude $\lambda$, and coprime integers $p,q$ not both zero.

[F1] [[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]]: for an open cover $X=U\cup V$, the singular-homology Mayer–Vietoris sequence is exact, with the map from the overlap given by $c\mapsto(i_*c,-j_*c)$ for the two inclusions $i:U\cap V\hookrightarrow U$ and $j:U\cap V\hookrightarrow V$, and the next map by $(u,v)\mapsto i_*u+j_*v$.

[F2] [[cor-homology-of-spheres]]: for $n\ge1$, the reduced singular homology of $S^n$ is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $n$ and zero in all other degrees; in particular $H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z)=H_2(S^3;\mathbb Z)=0$ and $H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$.

[F3] [[ex-mayer-vietoris-computation-of-the-torus-first-homology]]: $H_1(T^2;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z$, generated by the two factor circles.

[F4] [[prop-singular-homology-is-invariant-under-deformation-retracts]]: a deformation-retract inclusion induces an isomorphism on singular homology.

[F5] [[def-oriented-slope-and-knot-dehn-filling]]: the filling glues a solid torus to $E_K$ with its meridian identified with the class $p[\mu]+q[\lambda]$.

[A1] $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ is the stated axiom [[def-countable-choice]]. The chosen positive meridian bounds its normal disk in $N(K)$, and the preferred-longitude definition supplies both degree $+1$ over the core and $[\lambda]=0$ in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$; the pair being a boundary homology basis is an explicit hypothesis [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]]. The proof derives that $[\mu]$ generates $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ in step 2.1 by Mayer–Vietoris and does not invoke the separate full-AC exterior-homology theorem.

[F6] For any space, $H_0(-;\mathbb Z)$ is free on its path components; in particular a path-connected space has $H_0\cong\mathbb Z$, and an inclusion between path-connected spaces sends the point generator to the point generator [[prop-zero-th-singular-homology-is-free-on-path-components]].

## Proof

1.1 Choose a bicollar of $T_K$ in $S^3$ and thicken $E_K$ and $N(K)$ slightly to an open cover. The two sets deformation-retract to $E_K$ and $N(K)$, and their intersection deformation-retracts to $T_K$. By [F1] and [F2], the segment $H_2(S^3;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)\to H_1(S^3;\mathbb Z)$ has zero groups at both ends, so the middle map is an isomorphism. [given, F1, F2, F4, construct]

1.2 Let $M=E_K(p/q)$ and choose a bicollar of the gluing torus. Thickening the exterior and attached solid torus across this collar gives an open cover of $M$ whose two sets retract to the pieces and whose intersection retracts to $T_K$. Write $\alpha=p\mu+q\lambda$, and choose $\beta=r\mu+s\lambda$ with $ps-qr=1$, so $\alpha,\beta$ form a basis of $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$. In the Mayer–Vietoris map, $\alpha$ maps to $(p[\mu],0)$ because it bounds the attached solid-torus meridian disk, and $\beta$ maps to $(r[\mu],\pm v)$ where $v$ generates the first homology of the attached solid torus; the coefficient is $\pm1$ because $\beta$ intersects $\alpha$ once. The pieces and their overlap are path-connected: the exterior is a connected manifold and hence path-connected, and the solid torus and boundary torus are path-connected. By [F6], the following $H_0$ map sends $1$ to $(1,-1)$ and is injective. Exactness therefore identifies $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$ with the cokernel of the $H_1$ map. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, construct]

2.1 By [F3], $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$, and by [F4] and [F2] the solid torus $N(K)\simeq S^1$ has $H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. Since $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\oplus\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z^2$, the direct summand $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is free of rank one. Under the isomorphism in step 1.1, $\mu$ maps to $([\mu],0)$ because it bounds a disk in $N(K)$. The longitude maps to $(0,\pm1)$: [A1] gives its zero exterior image, and degree $+1$ on the core gives a generator of $H_1(N(K);\mathbb Z)$ under the deformation-retraction identification. Since the map is an isomorphism, $[\mu]$ generates $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$. [step 1.1, A1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

3.1 Thus $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)$ has generators $[\mu],v$ and relations $p[\mu]=0$ and $r[\mu]\pm v=0$. Eliminating $v$ leaves $\mathbb Z\langle[\mu]\rangle/\langle p[\mu]\rangle$. If $p\ne0$, this is $\mathbb Z/|p|\mathbb Z$; if $p=0$, coprimality gives $q=\pm1$ and the group is $\mathbb Z$. [step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
