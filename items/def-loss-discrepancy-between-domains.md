## Definition

Let $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ and $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ be measurable spaces, let $P$ and $Q$ be probability measures on $\mathsf X$ (in particular, source and target input marginals), and let $\mathcal H$ be a nonempty class of measurable functions $h:\mathsf X\to\mathsf Y$. Let $L:\mathsf Y\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$ be product-measurable. For $h,h'\in\mathcal H$, the map $x\mapsto(h'(x),h(x))$ is measurable because the preimage of each measurable rectangle $A\times B$ is $h'^{-1}(A)\cap h^{-1}(B)$ and such rectangles generate the product sigma-algebra; composing with $L$ shows that $x\mapsto L(h'(x),h(x))$ is measurable. Assume that, for every $h,h'\in\mathcal H$, the two integrals below are finite, and define
$$L_P(h',h):=\int_{\mathsf X}L(h'(x),h(x))\,P(dx),\qquad L_Q(h',h):=\int_{\mathsf X}L(h'(x),h(x))\,Q(dx).$$
The **$L$-discrepancy** between $P$ and $Q$ over $\mathcal H$ is the extended nonnegative number
$$\operatorname{disc}_L(P,Q):=\sup_{h,h'\in\mathcal H}\left|L_P(h',h)-L_Q(h',h)\right|\in[0,+\infty].$$
The supremum need not be attained.
