## Statement

Let $D$ be a principal ideal domain with fraction field $K$, let $V$ be a nonzero finite-dimensional $K$-vector space, and let $(T_i)_{i\in I}$ be a possibly infinite family of pairwise commuting $K$-linear endomorphisms. Suppose each $T_i$ satisfies a monic polynomial in $D[X]$. Put $A=K[T_i:i\in I]\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(V)$ and $R=D[T_i:i\in I]$, with both algebras unital. Suppose the pairing $\beta(a,b)=\operatorname{tr}_V(ab)$ on $A$ is nondegenerate: if $\beta(a,b)=0$ for every $b\in A$, then $a=0$.

Then $R$ is finite free over $D$, its $K$-span is $A$, and $V$ contains a full $D$-lattice $L$ stable under every $T_i$. Here a full lattice means a finite free $D$-submodule whose $K$-span is $V$. For any full lattice $L_0$, one may take $L=RL_0$. The action identifies $R$ faithfully with the $D$-operator algebra generated by the restricted $T_i$ on $L$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The PID, fraction field, vector space, commuting operators, monic annihilating polynomials, and nondegenerate trace pairing in the Statement.

[F1] [[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]]: a submodule of a finite free module over a PID is finite free.

## Proof

1.1 Fix a $K$-basis $v_1,\ldots,v_n$ of $V$ and put $L_0=\sum_j Dv_j$. For a finite subfamily $T_{i_1},\ldots,T_{i_s}$, take monic annihilating polynomials of degrees $d_1,\ldots,d_s$. Commutativity and reduction by these equations show that its $D$-algebra $R_F$ is spanned by $T_{i_1}^{e_1}\cdots T_{i_s}^{e_s}$ with $0\le e_j<d_j$. Thus $L_F=R_FL_0$ is finitely generated, contains $L_0$, and is stable under $R_F$. There is a nonzero $c\in D$ clearing all coordinates of these finitely many monomials applied to the $v_j$, so $L_F\subseteq c^{-1}L_0$. By [F1], $L_F$ is free of rank $n$. Every element of $R_F$ consequently has a matrix over $D$ on $L_F$, and its trace on $V$ belongs to $D$. The same argument permits an empty subfamily, with $R_F=D$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Finite products of the $T_i$, including the empty product $1$, span $A$ over $K$. Since $A$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional space $\operatorname{End}_K(V)$, extract a $K$-basis $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ from these products; in particular $a_j\in R$. For each $r\in R$ and each $j$, its polynomial expression and the expression for $a_j$ involve only finitely many operators. Applying step 1.1 to that finite subfamily gives $\operatorname{tr}_V(ra_j)\in D$. No bound on the size of the subfamily is asserted or needed. [step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Nondegeneracy makes the Gram matrix $(\beta(a_i,a_j))$ invertible over $K$. Solve this finite linear system to obtain the dual basis $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ with $\beta(b_i,a_j)=\delta_{ij}$. For $x=\sum_i x_i b_i$, one has $\beta(x,a_j)=x_j$. Hence the module $C^*=\{x\in A:\beta(x,a_j)\in D\text{ for every }j\}$ equals $\sum_i Db_i$, a finite free module. Step 2.1 gives $R\subseteq C^*$. By [F1], $R$ is finite free; it contains the $K$-basis $a_1,\ldots,a_m$, so its rank is $m$ and its $K$-span is $A$. This argument bounds the entire infinite operator family inside one module. [F1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 For any full lattice $L_0$, a finite $D$-basis of $R$ and a finite basis of $L_0$ show that $L=RL_0$ is finitely generated. A common denominator for this finite set puts $L$ inside a scalar multiple of $L_0$. By [F1] it is free, and $L_0\subseteq L$ forces it to be full. Since $T_iR\subseteq R$, every $T_i$ preserves $L$. An element of $R\subseteq\operatorname{End}_K(V)$ vanishing on $L$ vanishes on its $K$-span $V$, so the restriction map is injective. Its image is exactly the unital algebra generated by the restricted operators, by the definition of $R$. [F1, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎

## Source and application boundary

The trace-dual containment adapts Milne's proof of Proposition 2.29 and Remark 2.33(a), printed pp. 35–36, from field trace to the explicitly assumed operator trace pairing. The finite-subfamily argument is supplied locally above; no theorem about the integral closure of a field is imported as an unproved premise.

For a modular application, one must still supply a finite-dimensional coefficient-field realization, pairwise commutativity, monic integral annihilating polynomials for every required Hecke/diamond operator including the prescribed bad-prime operator, and nondegeneracy of this particular trace pairing. These hypotheses are not proved here for the minimal Taylor–Wiles target. Only after their verification does this lemma supply the lattice input to the existing conditional finite-local-Hecke-algebra helper; a residual eigencharacter is still separate data.
