## Statement

Let $F$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$, and let $r\ge1$ be prime to $p$. For any uniformizer $\pi$ and $n\ge1$ the maps in the residue/specialization theorem give
$$K_n^M(F)/rK_n^M(F)\xrightarrow{\sim}K_n^M(k)/rK_n^M(k)\oplus K_{n-1}^M(k)/rK_{n-1}^M(k).$$
In particular $\partial_v$ gives the canonical isomorphism $K_2^M(F)/r\cong k^\times/k^{\times r}$, a cyclic group of order $\gcd(r,|k|-1)$, and $K_n^M(F)=rK_n^M(F)$ for $n\ge3$. No injectivity of multiplication by $r$ is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The objects in the Statement.

[F1] The field is complete with finite residue field and principal maximal ideal. [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]

[F2] The residue/specialization map has kernel $U^1K_n^M(F)$, generated by symbols with a principal-unit entry, and has target $K_n^M(k)\oplus K_{n-1}^M(k)$. [[lem-milnor-residue-specialization-and-principal-unit-kernel]]

[F3] The higher Milnor groups of a finite field vanish in all degrees at least two. [[lem-milnor-k-groups-of-finite-fields-vanish-in-degrees-at-least-two]]

[F4] Simple residue roots of monic polynomials lift by the deterministic Hensel construction. [[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]

[F5] A finite field has a cyclic multiplicative group. [[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and conventions above.

1.1 For a principal unit $u$, the polynomial $X^r-u$ has simple residue root $1$, because $p\nmid r$. By [F4] it has a root $w\in1+\mathfrak m_F$. Multilinearity then makes every symbol with entry $u=w^r$ an $r$-multiple. Thus the entire subgroup $H=U^1K_n^M(F)$ lies in $rK_n^M(F)$. This includes $r=1$ directly and requires no choices for a family of units. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F2], $K_n^M(F)/H\cong K_n^M(k)\oplus K_{n-1}^M(k)$. Since $H\subseteq rK_n^M(F)$, quotienting this isomorphism by $r$ gives exactly the displayed isomorphism. In degree two the first summand is zero by [F3] and the second is $K_1^M(k)/r=k^\times/k^{\times r}$, so the isomorphism is the uniformizer-independent residue. The finite field multiplicative group is cyclic by [F5]; its $r$th-power quotient has order $\gcd(r,|k|-1)$. For $n\ge3$ both summands vanish by [F3], giving the stated divisibility. The calculation establishes quotients and surjectivity of multiplication, and makes no torsion-freeness or residue-characteristic assertion. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
