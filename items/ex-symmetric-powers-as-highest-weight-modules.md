## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_n(\mathbb C)$ with
its diagonal Cartan $\mathfrak h$, coordinate functionals $\varepsilon_i$ and
upper-triangular positive system as in
[[ex-standard-and-dual-representations-of-sl-n-by-highest-weights]]. For every
$m\ge1$ the symmetric power $\operatorname{Sym}^m(\mathbb C^n)$ is an
irreducible module of highest weight $m\omega_1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, the standard module $V=\mathbb C^n$ with basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$, and $W=\operatorname{Sym}^m(V)$, identified with the homogeneous polynomials of degree $m$ in the variables $x_1,\dots,x_n$ on which $E_{ij}\cdot x_l=\delta_{jl}x_i$ and $H\cdot x_l=H_{ll}x_l$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space and highest-weight theory used below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The monomials $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ with $a_1+\dots+a_n=m$ form a basis of $W$, their weights $\sum_ia_i\varepsilon_i$ are pairwise distinct, and $\varepsilon_1=\omega_1$ ([[ex-standard-and-dual-representations-of-sl-n-by-highest-weights]], [[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[L2] For a finite set of pairwise distinct weights and one of them, there is an element of $U(\mathfrak h)$ acting as the projection onto the corresponding weight component, because $U(\mathfrak h)$ is the polynomial algebra on $\mathfrak h^*$ and polynomials separate finitely many distinct points ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L3] A nonzero module generated by a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda$ with one-dimensional top weight space is irreducible exactly when every nonzero submodule contains the whole monomial basis; a nonzero submodule of an irreducible module is the whole module ([[def-irreducible-completely-reducible-and-faithful-lie-algebra-representation]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The vector $x_1^m$ is a highest weight vector: $H\cdot x_1^m=mH_{11}x_1^m$, so its weight is $m\varepsilon_1=m\omega_1$; and $E_{ij}\cdot x_1=0$ for $i<j$ because $i\ne1$, so $E_{ij}\cdot x_1^m=0$ for every positive root vector. [L1, given, A1]

1.2 Let $0\ne U\subseteq W$ be a submodule; writing a nonzero element as a sum of distinct-weight monomials, [L2] produces an element of $U(\mathfrak h)$ that projects onto one of them, so $U$ contains a monomial $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ with $a_j>0$ for some $j>1$ unless it already contains $x_1^m$. [L1, L2]

2.1 From any monomial $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ with $a_j>0$, $j>1$, applying $E_{1j}$ exactly $a_j$ times replaces all $x_j$-factors by $x_1$-factors with nonzero coefficient $a_j!$, and repeating for $j=2,\dots,n$ reaches a nonzero multiple of $x_1^m$; hence $x_1^m\in U$ by step 1.2. [given, step 1.2]

2.2 Conversely, from $x_1^m$ the operators $E_{l1}$ with $l>1$ replace $x_1$-factors by $x_l$-factors, and applying them $a_l$ times successively for $l=2,\dots,n$ produces a nonzero multiple of $x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$; hence $U$ contains the whole monomial basis and $U=W$. [given, step 1.2]

3.1 Therefore every nonzero submodule of $W$ is $W$, so $W$ is irreducible, and by step 1.1 its highest weight is $m\omega_1=$ the weight of $x_1^m$; this proves the assertion. [L3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
