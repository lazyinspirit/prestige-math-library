## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Countable Choice. Let $L/K$ be a finite separable extension of global fields and let $v$ be a finite place of $K$. For every place $w$ of $L$ restricting to $v$, let $L_w$ and $K_v$ be the corresponding metric completions. The canonical maps induce an isomorphism of topological $K_v$-algebras
$$
L\otimes_K K_v\cong\prod_{w\mid v}L_w.
$$
The product has one factor for each place $w$ above $v$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and AC_omega; global fields $K,L$; a finite separable extension $L/K$; and a finite place $v$ of $K$.

[A1] AC supplies the place-classification assumption. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[A2] AC_omega supplies the metric-completion assumption. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] A global field is a number field or a one-variable function field over its exact finite constant field; a finite place has a nonarchimedean completion. ([[def-global-field]], [[def-place-of-a-global-field]])

[F2] A place completion has a dense isometric image of the original field and is a complete valued field under the stated completion assumptions. ([[def-completion-of-a-global-field-at-a-place]], [[thm-completion-of-an-absolutely-valued-field]])

[F3] A finite separable extension is generated by one element. ([[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]], [[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]], [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]])

[F4] Over a complete nonarchimedean field, each finite extension has a unique extending absolute value and is complete for it. ([[thm-unique-extension-of-a-nonarchimedean-absolute-value]])

[F5] A polynomial over a field is separable exactly when it is relatively prime to its formal derivative; a Bezout identity persists after extending the coefficient field. ([[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]])

[F6] Every nonzero nonunit polynomial over a field factors into irreducible polynomials. ([[lem-polynomial-factorisation-into-irreducibles]])

[F7] Pairwise comaximal polynomial ideals give a quotient-ring product by the Chinese remainder theorem. ([[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]])

## Proof


**Given:** The data and hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 By [F3], choose a primitive element $\theta$ and let $f\in K[X]$ be its monic minimal polynomial. Then $L=K[X]/(f)$, and the $K_v$-linear map sending $\theta^j\otimes a$ to $aX^j$ identifies $L\otimes_KK_v$ with $K_v[X]/(f)$. The map respects multiplication because both algebras are generated by the class of $X$ with relation $f(X)=0$. [F3, given, construct]

2.1 Since $f$ is separable, [F5] gives polynomials $A,B\in K[X]$ with $Af+Bf'=1$; the same identity holds in $K_v[X]$, so no irreducible factor of $f$ over $K_v$ repeats. By [F6], write $f=\prod_{i=1}^r f_i$ as a product of distinct monic irreducibles. Their principal ideals are pairwise comaximal, and [F7] gives $K_v[X]/(f)\cong\prod_{i=1}^rE_i$, where $E_i=K_v[X]/(f_i)$ is a finite separable field extension of $K_v$. [F5, F6, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F4], each $E_i$ is complete for its unique extending absolute value. Sending $\theta$ to the class of $X$ in $E_i$ defines an injective $K$-field map $L\to E_i$ and hence a place $w_i$ of $L$ above $v$. The image is dense: every element of $E_i$ is a finite sum $\sum_{j<\deg f_i}c_jX^j$ with $c_j\in K_v$, and each coefficient is a limit of elements of $K$ by [F2]. Therefore $E_i$ is the completion $L_{w_i}$. [F2, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 If two factors gave the same place $w$, uniqueness of the completion isomorphism in [F2] would give a continuous $K_v$-isomorphism between them commuting with the dense images of $L$. It would send the image of $\theta$ in the first factor to its image in the second, so their monic minimal polynomials over $K_v$ would be equal. Thus the factors were the same, and the places $w_i$ are distinct. [F2, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Conversely let $w$ be any place of $L$ above $v$. In its complete field $L_w$, the image of $\theta$ is a root of $f$; its minimal polynomial over $K_v$ is one of the factors $f_i$. The corresponding $E_i$ maps continuously and injectively into $L_w$, and its image contains the dense image of $L=K(\theta)$. Since $E_i$ is complete, its image is closed in $L_w$, so it equals $L_w$. Hence every place above $v$ occurs in step 3.1. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The algebraic product isomorphism in step 2.1 is the CRT map. It is continuous because each coordinate is polynomial evaluation; its inverse is a finite sum using the fixed CRT idempotents, so it is continuous. The factor identifications in steps 3.1 and 4.2 are the unique continuous extensions from dense images. Thus the isomorphism is topological and is indexed by exactly all $w\mid v$. [F2, F7, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.2] ∎

## Diagram

The square expresses compatibility with the principal embedding; the proof above establishes the algebraic and topological isomorphism without relying on the diagram.

```tikzcd
L \arrow[r, "\Delta_v"] \arrow[d, "\ell\mapsto\ell\otimes1"'] & \prod_{w\mid v}L_w \\
L\otimes_KK_v \arrow[ur, "\Phi_v"']
```

The top arrow sends $\ell$ to its images in all completions. The bottom arrow is the isomorphism proved in the theorem.

## Source notes

Milne's Proposition 8.2 gives the classical factorization route but its proof invokes a broader extension-of-absolute-value result whose proof is omitted in the nearby remark. This proof instead uses the published uniqueness theorem for complete nonarchimedean bases and verifies density and the factor-place correspondence.
