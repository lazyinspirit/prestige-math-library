## Statement

Let $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ be a finite mutually independent family of real random variables on a common probability space, with independence in the sense of [[def-independent-random-elements]]. For any real random variable $X$ on that space, use the extended nonnegative notation
$$M_X(t):=\int_\Omega\exp(tX)\,d\mathbb P=\mathbb E[\exp(tX)]\in[0,+\infty],\qquad t\in\mathbb R.$$
Then, for every real $t$,
$$M_{\sum_{i\in I}X_i}(t)=\prod_{i\in I}M_{X_i}(t).$$
The extended product uses the nonnegative expectation-product convention: a zero factor makes the product zero even if another is infinite. The identity requires no exponential-moment finiteness assumption. Whenever all individual $M_{X_i}(t)$ are finite, the left side is finite as well. In the original finite-probability-space convention of [[def-moment-generating-function]], the integrals are finite sums, so this is exactly the original everywhere-finite MGF identity. Finite-valued atom independence suffices in that case. For $I=\varnothing$, both sides equal $1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite mutually independent family $(X_i)_{i\in I}$ of real random variables on one probability space, and $t\in\mathbb R$.

[L1] The expectation of a nonnegative measurable variable is its extended nonnegative integral; the original finite-space MGF is $M_X(t)=\mathbb E[\exp(tX)]$. ([[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]], [[def-moment-generating-function]])

[L2] Expectation factors over finite products of mutually independent nonnegative real random variables in $[0,+\infty]$, with the stated zero-factor convention; finite-valued atom independence on the original finite space is sufficient. ([[thm-expectation-of-products-of-independent-random-variables]])

[L3] $\exp(x+y)=\exp(x)\exp(y)$ for all reals $x,y$, and $\exp$ is positive. ([[thm-exponential-addition-formula]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]])

[L4] Independence is independence of the coordinate inverse-image sigma-algebras. ([[def-independent-random-elements]])

[L5] The exponential is smooth, hence continuous, and the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets. ([[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In the original finite-valued atom-independence convention, summing joint atom probabilities over finite attained-value sets proves independence for Borel inverse-image events, as in the finite-valued bridge of [L2]. The map $f_t(x)=\exp(tx)$ is continuous by [L5]. The family $\{B\subseteq\mathbb R:f_t^{-1}(B)\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\}$ is a sigma-algebra, since inverse images preserve complements and countable unions, and it contains the open sets by continuity. Therefore it contains every Borel set by [L5], so $f_t$ is Borel measurable. Hence $Y_i=f_t(X_i)$ is a measurable nonnegative real variable by [L3]. For any Borel set $B$, $$\{Y_i\in B\}=\{X_i\in f_t^{-1}(B)\}\in\sigma(X_i).$$ Thus $\sigma(Y_i)\subseteq\sigma(X_i)$, and the independence in [L4] passes to the family $(Y_i)_{i\in I}$. This inverse-image argument uses no choice principle. [L2, L3, L4, L5]

2.1 Iterating [L3] gives $\exp(t\sum_iX_i)=\prod_iY_i$ pointwise. Apply the nonnegative branch of [L2] to the independent variables from step 1.1 and use [L1] in each factor to obtain the displayed equality in $[0,+\infty]$. If every individual MGF is finite at $t$, the finite product on the right is finite. On an original finite probability space, all exponential variables are bounded, so [L1] identifies these integrals with the finite MGFs, and [L2] also covers its finite-valued atom-independence convention. [step 1.1, L1, L2, L3]

3.1 For $I=\varnothing$, the sum is zero and the random product is $1$. Thus $M_0(t)=\mathbb E[\exp(0)]=1$ on a probability space, and the product on the right is the empty product $1$. [step 2.1, L1, L3] ∎
