## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, let $I\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}R[x]$ be an ideal,
put $S=R[x]/I$ and let $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$. Assume that
$R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$
([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]) and let
$S'\subseteq S$ be the integral closure of the image of $R$ in $S$
([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]). Then there is an
element $g\in S'\setminus\mathfrak q$ such that the canonical homomorphism
$S'_g\to S_g$ of localisations at $g$
([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]) is an isomorphism.

Thus, at a quasi-finite prime, a one-generator quotient $R[x]/I$ already agrees
with its relative integral closure after inverting a single element of that
closure. No regularity, Noetherian or finiteness hypothesis beyond the finite
type built into $R[x]/I$ is used, and $R\to S$ need not be injective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, an ideal $I\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}R[x]$, the quotient $S=R[x]/I$ with quotient map $\varphi:R[x]\to S$, a prime $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ with $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$, the hypothesis that $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$, and the relative integral closure $S'=\operatorname{Int}_R(S)\subseteq S$ of the image of $R$ in $S$.

[L1] The map $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$ when $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, equivalently finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ ([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]).

[L2] $S=R[x]/I$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by the class of $x$, hence is of finite type over $R$; a quotient of a polynomial ring by an ideal is of finite type by definition ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] For a multiplicative subset $T$ of a commutative ring $A$, the localisation $T^{-1}A$ consists of fractions $a/t$ with the usual arithmetic, every $t\in T$ becoming a unit; for an element $g\in A$ the notation $A_g$ means the localisation at the multiplicative set generated by $g$ ([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]).

[L4] For a unital ring map $R\to S$, the set $\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ of elements of $S$ integral over the map is a subring of $S$ containing the image of $R$ ([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]).

[L5] If $t\in S$ satisfies a relation $a_0+a_1t+\cdots+a_nt^n=0$ with $a_j$ in a ring $A\subseteq S$, then $a_nt$ is integral over $A$, even when $a_n$ is a zero divisor ([[lem-zmt-polynomial-relation-leading-coefficient-is-integral]]).

[L6] If $A\to B$ and $B\to C$ are integral ring maps, then the composite $A\to C$ is integral ([[thm-transitivity-of-integrality]]).

[L7] For an ideal $J$ of a commutative ring $A$ and a multiplicative subset $T\subseteq A$ there is a canonical isomorphism $(T^{-1}A)/(T^{-1}J)\cong\bar T^{-1}(A/J)$, where $\bar T$ is the image of $T$ in $A/J$ ([[thm-localisation-commutes-with-quotients]]).

[L8] For an ideal $J$ of a commutative ring $A$, the kernel of the canonical map $A[x]\to(A/J)[x]$ is the ideal $J A[x]$ of polynomials with coefficients in $J$, so $A[x]/JA[x]\cong(A/J)[x]$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]]).

[L9] The evaluation homomorphism $R[x]\to S$ extending a fixed unital map on constants and sending $x$ to a prescribed element is unique; in particular, evaluation of a polynomial of $A[x]$ at the indeterminate is that same polynomial, so a polynomial of $A[x]$ all of whose coefficients lie in an additive subgroup vanishes as an element of $A[x]$ only if all its coefficients do ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]).

[L10] If $D$ is an integral domain then $D[x]$ is an integral domain ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]).

[L11] For a multiplicative subset $T$ of a commutative ring $A$ one has $a/t=0$ in $T^{-1}A$ if and only if $ua=0$ for some $u\in T$, and $a/t=a'/t'$ if and only if $u(at'-a't)=0$ for some $u\in T$; consequently the localisation map is injective on a domain ([[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We first produce a relation over $S'$ of positive degree with a coefficient outside $\mathfrak q$. Suppose that every element of $I$ had all its coefficients in $\mathfrak p$, that is $I\subseteq\mathfrak pR[x]$. Then by [L8] the quotient $S/\mathfrak pS\cong R[x]/(I+\mathfrak pR[x])=R[x]/\mathfrak pR[x]\cong(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ is a polynomial ring over the domain $R/\mathfrak p$; writing $\bar{\mathfrak q}$ for the image of $\mathfrak q$ in $S/\mathfrak pS$ and using [L7] with $T=S\setminus\mathfrak q$, we get $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}\cong(S/\mathfrak pS)_{\bar{\mathfrak q}}$, a localisation of $(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$. [given, L2, L7, L8]

1.2 Such a localisation is infinite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$. Indeed the powers $1,\bar x,\bar x^2,\ldots$ of the class of $x$ in $(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ are $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linearly independent: a relation $\sum_ic_i\bar x^i=0$ with $c_i\in\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ becomes, after multiplying by a common nonzero denominator $d\in R/\mathfrak p$, a relation $\sum_i(dc_i)\bar x^i=0$ with coefficients in the domain $R/\mathfrak p$ and not all zero, which by [L9] is a nonzero polynomial of $(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ equal to zero — impossible by [L10] unless all coefficients vanish; and this linear independence is preserved in the localisation because the localisation map is injective on the domain $(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ by [L11]. [given, L9, L10, L11]

2.1 Therefore $I\not\subseteq\mathfrak pR[x]$: otherwise steps 1.1 and 1.2 would exhibit $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ as an infinite-dimensional $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra, contradicting the quasi-finiteness hypothesis through [L1]. So there are $n\ge0$ and $a_0,\ldots,a_n\in R$ with $f=\sum_ja_jx^j\in I$ and $a_j\notin\mathfrak p$ for at least one $j$; applying $\varphi$ and writing $b_j=\varphi(a_j)$ (the element of the relative integral closure $S'$ given by the image of $R$) gives a relation $\sum_{j=0}^nb_j\varphi(x)^j=0$ in $S$ with some $b_j\notin\mathfrak q$, because $a_j\in\mathfrak p$ if and only if $\varphi(a_j)\in\mathfrak q$. [given, step 1.1, step 1.2, L1, L4]

3.1 Among all relations $\sum_{j=0}^mb_j\varphi(x)^j=0$ in $S$ with all $b_j\in S'$ and at least one $b_j\notin\mathfrak q$, choose one of minimal degree $m$, which exists by step 2.1; then $m\ge1$, because a relation of degree $0$ reads $b_0=0$ with $b_0\in S'$, and $b_0\notin\mathfrak q$ would be impossible. We show that its leading coefficient satisfies $b_m\notin\mathfrak q$. [given, step 2.1]

4.1 Assume first that $b_m\notin\mathfrak q$. By [L5] applied to the relation of step 3.1 with $A=S'$ and $t=\varphi(x)$, the element $b_m\varphi(x)$ is integral over $S'$; hence the inclusion $S'\to S'[b_m\varphi(x)]$ is an integral ring map, and since $S'=\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ consists of elements integral over $R$ by [L4], the composite $R\to S'[b_m\varphi(x)]$ is integral by [L6]. So $b_m\varphi(x)$ is integral over $R$, that is $b_m\varphi(x)\in\operatorname{Int}_R(S)=S'$. [given, step 3.1, L4, L5, L6]

5.1 Assume instead that $b_m\in\mathfrak q$. The argument of step 4.1 still gives $b_m\varphi(x)\in S'$. Set $b'_{m-1}=b_m\varphi(x)+b_{m-1}\in S'$. Then $\sum_{j=0}^{m-2}b_j\varphi(x)^j+b'_{m-1}\varphi(x)^{m-1}=0$ by step 3.1, and $b'_{m-1}-b_{m-1}=b_m\varphi(x)\in\mathfrak q$ because $b_m\in\mathfrak q$; so $b'_{m-1}\notin\mathfrak q$ if $b_{m-1}\notin\mathfrak q$, and otherwise one of $b_{m-2},\ldots,b_0$ lies outside $\mathfrak q$ by step 3.1. Either way we have a relation of degree $m-1$ with all coefficients in $S'$ and at least one coefficient outside $\mathfrak q$, contradicting the choice of the minimal degree $m$. Hence the case $b_m\in\mathfrak q$ cannot occur. [given, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 By steps 3.1 and 5.1 the minimal relation of step 3.1 has $b_m\in S'\setminus\mathfrak q$, and $b_m\varphi(x)\in S'$ by step 4.1. Put $g=b_m$. In the localisation $S'_g$ the element $g$ is a unit by [L3], so $\varphi(x)=(b_m\varphi(x))/b_m\in S'_g$; since $S$ is generated as an $S'$-algebra by $\varphi(x)$ by [L2], the subring $S'_g\subseteq S_g$ already contains a generating set of $S_g$ over $S'_g$ and hence equals $S_g$. [given, step 4.1, step 5.1, L2, L3]

7.1 The canonical homomorphism $S'_g\to S_g$ is injective, so it is an isomorphism: if a fraction $a/g^k$ with $a\in S'$ maps to zero in $S_g$, then by [L11] there is $l\ge0$ with $g^la=0$ in $S$; since $a,g\in S'$, the product $g^la$ lies in $S'$, and [L11] again gives $a/g^k=0$ in $S'_g$. [given, step 6.1, L11]

8.1 Steps 2.1, 6.1 and 7.1 produce $g\in S'\setminus\mathfrak q$ with $S'_g\to S_g$ an isomorphism, which is the assertion. The argument is choice-free: the only selection is that of a relation of minimal degree in step 3.1, and the minimal-degree argument uses only that degrees are natural numbers. ∎ [step 2.1, step 6.1, step 7.1]
