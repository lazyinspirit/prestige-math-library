## Example

Let $k$ be a commutative ring, let $f\in k[x,y]$ be an arbitrary polynomial and
let $B=k[x,y]/(f)$ be the quotient by the principal ideal it generates. Writing
$f_x=\partial f/\partial x$ and $f_y=\partial f/\partial y$ for the partial
derivatives, the module of Kähler differentials of $B$ over $k$ is presented by
the single Jacobian relation of $f$:
$$\Omega_{B/k}\;\cong\;\bigl(B\,\mathrm dx\oplus B\,\mathrm dy\bigr)\Big/\, B\,\bigl(f_x\,\mathrm dx+f_y\,\mathrm dy\bigr).$$
No regularity, smoothness or non-vanishing hypothesis is imposed on $f$, and no
flatness is assumed of $B$ over $k$: the presentation holds for every
$f\in k[x,y]$, including $f=0$ and including polynomials whose first partial
derivatives both vanish in positive characteristic. For $f=0$ one recovers
$\Omega_{k[x,y]/k}\cong B^{2}$, and over a ring $k$ in which the integer $2\neq0$
the example $f=x^{2}$ has $f_x=2x\neq0\in B$, so the relation is a nonzero
cyclic submodule there.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $k$, the polynomial algebra $P=k[x,y]$, a polynomial $f\in P$, the ideal $I=(f)\subseteq P$, the quotient $B=P/I$, and the partial derivatives $f_x,f_y\in P$ with images in $B$ written the same way.

[F1] [[cor-jacobian-presentation-differentials]]: for a commutative ring $A$, the polynomial algebra $P=A[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, an ideal $I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)\subseteq P$ generated by finitely many elements and the quotient $B=P/I$, the module $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the cokernel of the $B$-linear map $B^{r}\to B^{n}$ whose $j$-th column is $(\partial f_j/\partial x_1,\dots,\partial f_j/\partial x_n)$, that is, $\Omega_{B/A}\cong B^{n}/\sum_j B(\partial f_j/\partial x_1,\dots,\partial f_j/\partial x_n)$; no flatness or minimality of $r$ is assumed.

[F2] [[lem-differentials-polynomial-algebra-free]] with $n=2$: $\Omega_{P/k}$ is free with basis $\mathrm dx,\mathrm dy$, the derivations $\partial/\partial x,\partial/\partial y$ satisfy $\partial x/\partial x=1$, $\partial x/\partial y=0$, $\partial y/\partial x=0$, $\partial y/\partial y=1$, and $\mathrm dg=(\partial g/\partial x)\mathrm dx+(\partial g/\partial y)\mathrm dy$ for every $g\in P$.

[F3] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: a $k$-derivation satisfies the Leibniz rule $D(gh)=gD(h)+hD(g)$ and annihilates $k$.

## Verification

1.1 Apply [F1] with $A=k$, $n=2$, $r=1$ and $f_1=f$: the ideal $I=(f)$ is generated by the single element $f$, and $\Omega_{B/k}$ is the cokernel of the $B$-linear map $B^{1}\to B^{2}$ with the single column $(f_x,f_y)$. Identifying $B^{2}=B\,\mathrm dx\oplus B\,\mathrm dy$ by the standard basis, the image is the cyclic submodule generated by $f_x\,\mathrm dx+f_y\,\mathrm dy$, so $\Omega_{B/k}\cong(B\,\mathrm dx\oplus B\,\mathrm dy)/B(f_x\,\mathrm dx+f_y\,\mathrm dy)$. [F1, given]

1.2 The derivatives of the powers of $x$: by the Leibniz rule [F3] and $\partial x/\partial x=1$, $\partial y/\partial x=0$ [F2], induction on $n\ge0$ gives $\partial(x^{n})/\partial x=n\,x^{n-1}$ and $\partial(y^{m})/\partial x=0$ for all $m\ge0$, with the case $n=0$ read as $\partial(1)/\partial x=0$. [F2, F3, induction]

2.1 The case $f=0$: then $B=P$ and $f_x=f_y=0$, so the relation submodule in step 1.1 is $B\cdot0=0$ and $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B^{2}$, which is the free module on $\mathrm dx,\mathrm dy$ already recorded in [F2]. [step 1.1, F2]

2.2 Suppose $k$ has characteristic $p>0$ and $f=x^{p}$. Then step 1.2 gives $f_x=\partial(x^{p})/\partial x=p\,x^{p-1}=0$ and $f_y=\partial(x^{p})/\partial y=0$ because $p=0$ in $k$, so again the relation submodule of step 1.1 vanishes and $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B^{2}$ even though $B=k[x,y]/(x^{p})$ is non-reduced: the presentation records no relation at all. If instead $2\ne0$ in $k$ and $f=x^{2}$, the relation is $B\cdot(2x\,\mathrm dx)$ and $2x\ne0$ in $B$: the ideal $(x^{2})$ contains no nonzero polynomial of degree one, so it cannot contain $2x$. Thus the relation is a nonzero cyclic submodule even when $2$ is a zero divisor in $k$; in either case the displayed presentation of step 1.1 is the one computed, and no hypothesis on $f$ beyond $f\in k[x,y]$ was used. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Combining steps 1.1 with the cases 2.1 and 2.2, for every $f\in k[x,y]$ the module $\Omega_{B/k}$ is the quotient displayed in the Example, the relation $f_x\,\mathrm dx+f_y\,\mathrm dy$ being determined by the partial derivatives of $f$ and possibly vanishing; since [F1] requires no flatness, smoothness or non-vanishing hypothesis, the presentation holds without any regularity assumption on $f$, and the map $P\to B$ is the surjection onto the quotient by $(f)$ in all cases. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
