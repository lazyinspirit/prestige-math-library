## Statement

Let $D>0$ be a nonsquare integer with $D\equiv0$ or $1\pmod4$. Every primitive
integral binary quadratic form of discriminant $D$ is indefinite, and its
proper $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)$-class corresponds to a narrow ideal class of
$\mathcal O_D$. If $Q=(a,b,c)$ and
$I_Q=a\mathbb Z+(b+\sqrt D)\mathbb Z/2$, the oriented class is
$[I_Q,\operatorname{sgn}(a)]$; the standard narrow class is $[I_Q]$ when
$a>0$ and $[\sqrt D I_Q]$ when $a<0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A positive nonsquare quadratic discriminant $D$ and a primitive integral form $Q=(a,b,c)$ of discriminant $D$.

[F1] [[thm-primitive-forms-correspond-to-proper-ideal-classes-of-quadratic-orders]]: the proper form classes correspond to oriented proper invertible ideal classes.

[F2] [[thm-proper-invertible-ideal-classes-form-abelian-group]]: the oriented class group is isomorphic to the standard narrow class group by $[I,+1]\mapsto[I]$ and $[I,-1]\mapsto[\sqrt D I]$.

[F3] [[def-oriented-proper-invertible-ideal-class-group-of-a-quadratic-order]]: the orientation of the basis $(a,\theta_Q)$ is $\operatorname{sgn}(a)$.

[F4] [[def-proper-invertible-ideal-class-group-of-a-quadratic-order]]: narrow classes quotient by ideals generated by scalars positive under both real embeddings.

## Proof

1.1 If $a=0$, then $D=b^2$ is a square, contrary to the hypothesis. Hence $a\ne0$. Since $D>0$, completing the square shows $Q$ is indefinite. [given, algebra]

2.1 By [F1] the proper class maps to $[I_Q,\operatorname{sgn}(a)]$. Applying the explicit isomorphism [F2] yields $[I_Q]$ for $a>0$ and $[\sqrt D I_Q]$ for $a<0$, exactly as stated. These are classes in the quotient [F4]. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
