## Definition

Let $\mathfrak g$ be a complex Lie algebra, let $Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ be the center
of its enveloping algebra ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra-as-a-tensor-quotient]]),
and let $\chi\colon Z(U(\mathfrak g))\to\mathbb C$ be a unital $\mathbb C$-algebra
homomorphism, that is, a central character
([[def-central-character-of-a-lie-algebra-module]]). Write
$\ker\chi=\{z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g)):\chi(z)=0\}$ and let

$$U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi=\Bigl\{\sum_{k=1}^m u_kz_k:m\ge0,\ u_k\in U(\mathfrak g),\ z_k\in\ker\chi\Bigr\}$$

be the two-sided ideal of $U(\mathfrak g)$ generated by $\ker\chi$
([[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]],
[[thm-sum-and-product-of-ideals-are-ideals]]). The **central reduction** of
$U(\mathfrak g)$ at $\chi$ is the quotient algebra

$$U_\chi:=U(\mathfrak g)\,\big/\,U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi.$$

It is a unital associative $\mathbb C$-algebra, the natural map
$U(\mathfrak g)\to U_\chi$ is a surjective algebra homomorphism with kernel
$U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$, the image of $Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ in $U_\chi$ is
$\mathbb C\cdot1$, and for a $U(\mathfrak g)$-module $M$ the following are
equivalent:

- (i) $M$ has central character $\chi$, i.e. every $z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ acts
  on $M$ by the scalar $\chi(z)$;
- (ii) $\ker\chi$ annihilates $M$;
- (iii) the action of $U(\mathfrak g)$ on $M$ factors uniquely through $U_\chi$.

Thus the $U(\mathfrak g)$-modules with central character $\chi$ are exactly the
$U_\chi$-modules.

## Remarks

- **The generating set is central, so the ideal is two-sided.** Every $z\in
  \ker\chi$ commutes with $U(\mathfrak g)$, hence $u_kz_k=z_ku_k$ and the set
  displayed above is already closed under left and right multiplication; it is
  an additive subgroup because finite sums of such terms are such terms, and it
  contains $0$ as the empty sum. This is the ideal generated by $\ker\chi$
  ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]).
- **Every central element is scalar modulo the ideal.** For $z\in
  Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ one has $z-\chi(z)\cdot1\in\ker\chi\subseteq
  U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$, so $z$ and the scalar $\chi(z)$ have the same image
  in $U_\chi$. Thus the image of the center consists of scalar classes; this
  does not imply that every element of $U_\chi$ is scalar.
- **The equivalences.** Condition (i) says $z$ acts by $\chi(z)$ for every
  central $z$, which is equivalent to $(z-\chi(z))m=0$ for all such $z$ and all
  $m$, hence to $\ker\chi$ annihilating $M$ because $\ker\chi$ consists exactly
  of the elements $z$ with $\chi(z)=0$. If $\rho\colon U(\mathfrak g)\to
  \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C}(M)$ denotes the action, then (ii) says
  $\ker\chi\subseteq\ker\rho$, so $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi\subseteq\ker\rho$
  because $\ker\rho$ is a two-sided ideal; the quotient universal property
  ([[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]) then produces a unique
  factorization of $\rho$ through $U_\chi$, which is (iii). Conversely a
  factorization through $U_\chi$ kills $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi$ and hence
  $\ker\chi$, which is (ii).
