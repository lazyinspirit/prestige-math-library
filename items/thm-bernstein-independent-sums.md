## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent centered square-integrable real random variables. Suppose $b\ge0$ is finite and $|X_i|\le b$ almost surely for each $i$. Put $v=\sum_{i=1}^n\operatorname{Var}(X_i)$. For $t>0$, if $v>0$ then
$$\mathbb P\left(\left|\sum_{i=1}^nX_i\right|\ge t\right)\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^2}{2(v+bt/3)}\right),$$
and if $v=0$ then this probability is $0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Independent centered square-integrable real random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_n$, a finite deterministic bound $b\ge0$, and $|X_i|\le b$ almost surely.

[F1] For centered $X_i$, $\operatorname{Var}(X_i)=\mathbb E[X_i^2]$ ([[def-moments-variance-and-covariance]]).

[F2] For independent real random variables and nonnegative Borel functions $g_i$, the expectation of their product factors as the product of their expectations ([[thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables]]).

[F3] If $Y\ge0$ and $r>0$, then $\mathbb P(Y\ge r)\le\mathbb E[Y]/r$ ([[cor-markov-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

[F4] $e^x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^k/k!$, and this series converges absolutely for every real $x$ ([[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]], [[lem-exponential-series-has-infinite-radius]]).

[F5] $1+x\le e^x$ for every real $x$ ([[lem-exponential-dominates-one-plus-x]]).

[F6] A nonnegative measurable function with integral $0$ vanishes almost everywhere ([[thm-nonnegative-integral-zero-iff-zero-almost-everywhere]]).

[F7] The probability of a finite union of events is at most the sum of their probabilities ([[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]).

[F8] Independence means independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the random variables ([[def-independent-random-elements]]).

[F9] Expectation is linear, monotone, and bounded in modulus by the expectation of the absolute value ([[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]).

[F10] The real exponential is continuous and strictly increasing, is positive, satisfies $e^{-x}=1/e^x$, and obeys $e^{x+y}=e^xe^y$ ([[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]]).

[F11] Finite sums of real-valued measurable functions are measurable ([[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $v_i=\mathbb E[X_i^2]$. If $v=0$, then every $v_i=0$ because $v_i\ge0$. By [F6], $X_i^2=0$ almost surely for every $i$, so the finite sum is $0$ almost surely and the stated tail probability is $0$. [given, F1, F6, F11]

1.2 Suppose $v>0$. If $b=0$, every $X_i=0$ almost surely and $v=0$, a contradiction; hence $b>0$. For each fixed real $\theta$, $g_\theta(x)=e^{\theta x}$ is continuous: when $\theta\ne0$, continuity of $x\mapsto\theta x$ follows from $|\theta x-\theta y|=|\theta||x-y|$, and continuity of its composition with $e^x$ follows from [F10]; when $\theta=0$ it is constant. Thus $g_\theta$ is Borel: the Borel sets whose inverse images under $g_\theta$ are Borel form a sigma-algebra containing every open set, hence contain the sigma-algebra generated by opens ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]). For each Borel $B$, $\{g_\theta(X_i)\in B\}=X_i^{-1}(g_\theta^{-1}(B))$ is measurable. Now fix $i$ and $\lambda$ with $0<|\lambda|<3/b$. For $N\ge2$, the exponential series tail on $[-b,b]$ is uniformly bounded by $r_N=\sum_{k>N}(|\lambda|b)^k/k!\to0$. Each partial sum at $X_i$ is integrable; the uniform bound and [F9] give $|\mathbb E[e^{\lambda X_i}]-\mathbb E[\sum_{k=0}^N(\lambda X_i)^k/k!]|\le r_N$, so expectation passes to the series limit. Centering removes the linear term, and for every $k\ge2$, $|\mathbb E[X_i^k]|\le b^{k-2}v_i$ by [F1] and monotonicity. Also $k!\ge2\cdot3^{k-2}$ for $k\ge2$: equality holds at $k=2,3$, and each subsequent factorial factor is at least $3$. Hence the geometric-series bound and $1+x\le e^x$ from [F5] give $\mathbb E e^{\lambda X_i}\le1+\lambda^2v_i/[2(1-|\lambda|b/3)]\le\exp(\lambda^2v_i/[2(1-|\lambda|b/3)])$. [given, F1, F4, F5, F9, F10, algebra, construct]

2.1 Let $S=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$, a measurable real random variable by [F11]. By the addition formula [F10], $e^{\lambda S}=\prod_i e^{\lambda X_i}$; independence [F8] and factorization [F2], applied to the nonnegative Borel functions established in step 1.2, give $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S}\le\exp(\lambda^2v/[2(1-\lambda b/3)])$ for $0<\lambda<3/b$. Since $e^x$ is positive and increasing [F10], $S\ge t$ implies $e^{\lambda S}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$, so [F3] and the reciprocal and addition formulas in [F10] give $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda^2v/[2(1-\lambda b/3)])$. Choose $\lambda=t/(v+bt/3)$; then $\lambda b/3<1$ and direct simplification gives $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le\exp(-t^2/[2(v+bt/3)])$. [F2, F3, F8, F10, F11, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 For $0<\lambda<3/b$, the bound in step 1.2 also holds at parameter $-\lambda$. By [F10], $e^{-\lambda S}=\prod_i e^{-\lambda X_i}$; the original variables remain independent by [F8], and [F2] factors these nonnegative Borel functions, giving $\mathbb E e^{-\lambda S}\le\exp(\lambda^2v/[2(1-\lambda b/3)])$. Since $S\le-t$ implies $e^{-\lambda S}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$, Markov [F3] and the same optimizing choice as in step 2.1 yield $\mathbb P(S\le-t)\le\exp(-t^2/[2(v+bt/3)])$. By [F7], the two one-sided bounds imply the displayed two-sided bound without asserting independence of the transformed variables $-X_i$. [F2, F3, F7, F8, F10, F11, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
