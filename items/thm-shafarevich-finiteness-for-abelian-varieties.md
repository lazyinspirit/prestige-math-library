## Statement

Assume AC, DC, and Countable Choice. Let $K/\mathbb Q$ be a finite extension, let $g\ge1$, and let $S$ be a finite set of finite (nonarchimedean) places of $K$. An abelian variety over $K$ is a proper geometrically connected group variety over $K$. It has good reduction at a finite place $v$ if it extends to an abelian scheme over the valuation ring $\mathcal O_{K,v}$. Two such varieties are $K$-isomorphic when an isomorphism is defined over $K$. There are only finitely many $K$-isomorphism classes of abelian varieties of dimension $g$ over $K$ that have good reduction at every finite place outside $S$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, DC, Countable Choice, a number field $K$, an integer $g\ge1$, and a finite set $S$ of finite places of $K$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC says every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.

[A2] [[def-dependent-choice]]: DC says that if a relation is entire on a nonempty set, then from any prescribed starting point there is a sequence following that relation.

[A3] [[def-countable-choice]]: Countable Choice says every sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function indexed by $\mathbb N$.

[F1] [[def-number-field]]: a number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$.

[F2] [[def-abelian-variety-over-a-field]]: an abelian variety over a field is a proper geometrically connected group variety over that field.

[F3] [[def-good-reduction-and-abelian-scheme-model]]: good reduction at $v$ means existence of an abelian scheme model over the valuation ring at $v$.

[F4] [[thm-faltings-isogeny-finiteness-theorem]]: under AC, DC, and Countable Choice, for fixed $K$, finite $S$, and $g\ge1$, only finitely many $K$-isogeny classes of dimension-$g$ abelian varieties over $K$ have good reduction outside $S$.

[F5] [[thm-faltings-finiteness-within-fixed-isogeny-class]]: under AC, DC, and Countable Choice, for a fixed $A/K$ with good reduction outside $S$, only finitely many $K$-isomorphism classes of abelian varieties $K$-isogenous to $A$ have good reduction outside $S$.

[F6] [[def-k-isogeny-of-abelian-varieties]]: a $K$-isogeny class is an equivalence class of $K$-isomorphism classes under the equivalence relation generated by surjective $K$-homomorphisms with finite kernel.

[L1] [[thm-faltings-finiteness-within-fixed-isogeny-class]]: the current carrier states [F5] but records proof provenance as not supplied and unresolved proof obligations; [F5] is therefore a required but not yet proof-closed input.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1]–[F6], AC, DC, and Countable Choice.

1.1 Let $\mathcal F$ be the set of $K$-isomorphism classes in the statement. By [F4], only finitely many $K$-isogeny classes meet $\mathcal F$; call this finite set $\mathcal I$. By [F6], these classes partition the $K$-isomorphism classes. For $C\in\mathcal I$, let $\mathcal F_C$ be the classes in $\mathcal F$ represented by members of $C$. The sets $\mathcal F_C$ are nonempty and partition $\mathcal F$. [given, A1, A2, A3, F4, F6]

2.1 For each $C\in\mathcal I$, choose an abelian variety $A_C$ representing a class in $\mathcal F_C$. Because $\mathcal I$ is finite, finite induction performs these finitely many choices without an additional choice axiom. Applying [F5] to $A_C$ shows that $\mathcal F_C$ is finite. [A1, A2, A3, F5, F6, step 1.1, choose]

3.1 The family $\mathcal F$ is the union of the finitely many finite sets $\mathcal F_C$ for $C\in\mathcal I$. A finite union of finite sets is finite, so $\mathcal F$ is finite. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
