## Statement

No closed nonorientable piecewise-linear surface embeds in the standard oriented piecewise-linear three-sphere $S^3$.

## Statement references

[[cor-homology-of-spheres]] — $H_2(S^3;\mathbb F_2)=0$.

[[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]] — finite simplicial and singular homology agree.

[[def-r-orientation-of-a-topological-manifold]] — orientation is a continuous generating section of the local integral homology system.

[[def-pl-embedding-and-subpolyhedron]] — the PL embedding and subpolyhedron convention.

[[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]] — a subdivision of a fixed triangulation makes the embedded surface a subcomplex.

[[def-oriented-link-in-s-three-and-ambient-isotopy]] — the standard orientation of $S^3$.

[[def-oriented-cellular-chain-group]] — oriented cellular generators and induced face signs.

[[def-induced-boundary-orientation]] — oriented cellular generators and induced face signs.

[[lem-the-cellular-boundary-squares-to-zero]] — the cellular boundary of a cellular boundary is zero.

[Hass–Lagarias–Pippenger, Corollary 5.5](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~hass/Research/Papers/Complexity/hlp.pdf) — a knot exterior has no embedded projective plane because it embeds in $S^3$.

[Rourke–Sanderson, §§1.7 and 2.12](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/rsplx.pdf) — the subpolyhedron convention and compatible finite subdivisions used below.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A closed PL surface $F$ embedded in the standard oriented PL $3$-sphere.

[F1] [[cor-homology-of-spheres]]: $H_2(S^3;\mathbb F_2)=0$.

[F2] [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]]: the finite triangulation used below computes singular homology with coefficients in $\mathbb F_2$.

[F3] [[def-cellular-homology]] and [[thm-cellular-boundary-is-the-incidence-degree-matrix]]: the cellular chain groups are generated by oriented cells, and each boundary coefficient is the signed sum of incident-cell coefficients.

[F4] [[def-r-orientation-of-a-topological-manifold]]: an orientation of an $n$-manifold is a continuous section choosing a generator of each local integral homology group $H_n(M,M\setminus\{x\};\mathbb Z)$; this is the definition used for both the ambient sphere and the surface $F$.

[F5] [[def-oriented-cellular-chain-group]]: an orientation of a tetrahedral cell selects its integral cellular generator.

[F6] [[lem-pl-embedded-polyhedron-admits-compatible-subcomplex-triangulation]]: a finite subdivision of the ambient triangulation contains $F$ as a subcomplex.

[F7] [[def-induced-boundary-orientation]]: the orientation induced on a face is determined by the outward-normal-first convention.

[F8] [[lem-the-cellular-boundary-squares-to-zero]]: the cellular boundary operator satisfies $\partial^2=0$.

[F9] [[def-oriented-link-in-s-three-and-ambient-isotopy]] and [[def-pl-embedding-and-subpolyhedron]]: the ambient is the standard oriented PL $3$-sphere. The finite triangulation in [F6] is a subdivision of its standard combinatorial triangulation, so every triangular face has link $S^0$ and exactly two tetrahedral cofaces.

## Proof

1.1 By [F6], take a finite subdivision of the standard triangulation in which $F$ is a subcomplex. Sum its triangular faces with coefficient $1$ in cellular $2$-chains over $\mathbb F_2$. Each edge of the closed surface is incident to exactly two of these triangles, so the sum $z$ has zero boundary. [given, F3, F6, construct]

2.1 By [F1] and [F2], $[z]=0$ in $H_2(S^3;\mathbb F_2)$, so $z=\partial c$ for a finite cellular $3$-chain $c$. Give each tetrahedron its coefficient $0$ or $1$ in $c$. [step 1.1, F1, F2]

3.1 Orient each tetrahedron by the local orientation of $S^3$ in [F4] and take the integral cellular chain $C$ with the same coefficients $0$ or $1$ as $c$; [F5] identifies these oriented cells with the integral chain generators. For any triangular face outside $F$, its coefficient in $\partial c$ is zero modulo $2$, so its two incident tetrahedra have equal coefficients. Their induced face orientations are opposite: in an oriented PL coordinate ball at an interior point of that face, the tetrahedra occupy opposite half-balls, and the outward-normal-first rule [F7] reverses the face sign. Their contributions to $\partial C$ therefore cancel. For a face in $F$, the coefficient of $\partial c$ is one modulo $2$, so exactly one incident tetrahedron has coefficient one; its contribution to $\partial C$ is $+1$ or $-1$. Thus $w=\partial C$ is supported on $F$ with coefficient $+1$ or $-1$ on each triangle. [given, F3, F4, F5, F7, F9, step 2.1]

4.1 By [F8], $\partial w=\partial^2C=0$. Hence along each edge of $F$ the two incident oriented triangles induce opposite edge orientations. The triangles around any vertex form a cycle because $F$ is a triangulated surface; these compatible edge orientations therefore give one local orientation on its star. Interior points of triangles, edges and vertex stars cover $F$, and the local generators agree on their overlaps. They define the continuous orientation section of [F4], contradicting nonorientability. [F4, F8, step 3.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
