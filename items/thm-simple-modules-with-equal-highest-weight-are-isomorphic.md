## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $V_1,V_2$ be
simple rational representations of a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with the same
highest weight $\lambda$
([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]]). Then
$V_1\cong V_2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; two simple rational representations $V_1,V_2$ of the split
reductive group $(G,T)$ with common highest weight $\lambda$.

[F1] *Primitive vectors of simple modules.* Each simple $V_i$ contains a
primitive vector $v_i$ whose weight is its highest weight $\lambda$, unique up
to multiplication by a nonzero scalar; moreover $V_i$ is generated as a
$G$-module by $v_i$, because $V_i$ is simple and $v_i\ne0$
([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]],
[[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F2] *Modules generated by a primitive vector.* If a rational representation $W$
is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector $w$ of weight $\lambda$, then
$W_\lambda=kw$ is one-dimensional and every weight of $W$ is of the form
$\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$
([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]]).

[F3] *Primitivity is closed under sums of equal weight.* A vector is primitive
of weight $\lambda$ if and only if it is fixed by the unipotent radical $U$ of
a Borel $B\supseteq T$ and is a $T$-eigenvector of weight $\lambda$; hence
$v_1+v_2\in V_1\oplus V_2$ is primitive of weight $\lambda$
([[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]],
[[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]]).

## Proof

**Given:** AC; two simple rational representations $V_1,V_2$ of the split
reductive group $(G,T)$ with common highest weight $\lambda$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] choose primitive vectors $v_i\in V_i$ of weight $\lambda$; then $v:=v_1+v_2$ is a nonzero primitive vector of weight $\lambda$ in $V_1\oplus V_2$ by [F3]. [F1, F3]

2.1 Let $V\subseteq V_1\oplus V_2$ be the $G$-submodule generated by $v$. By [F2] applied to $V$ and $v$, the weight space $V_\lambda$ equals the line $kv$; in particular the only elements of $V$ of weight $\lambda$ are the multiples of $v$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The projection $\varphi:V\to V_2$, $(x_1,x_2)\mapsto x_2$, is a $G$-homomorphism with $\varphi(v)=v_2\ne0$, so its image is a nonzero $G$-submodule of the simple module $V_2$; hence $\varphi$ is surjective. Its kernel is $V\cap V_1$, a $G$-submodule of the simple module $V_1$, so the kernel is either $0$ or $V_1$. If $V_1\subseteq V$, then $v_1\in V$ has weight $\lambda$, so $v_1=cv$ for some scalar $c$ by step 2.1; applying $\varphi$ gives $0=cv_2$, whence $c=0$ and $v_1=0$, a contradiction. Therefore the kernel is $0$ and $\varphi:V\to V_2$ is an isomorphism. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 The same argument with the projection onto the first factor shows that $V\to V_1$ is an isomorphism as well, so $V_1\cong V\cong V_2$. [step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- This is Milne's Theorem 22.19; the proof uses only that a module generated by a primitive vector has a one-dimensional top weight space, so that the diagonal line $k(v_1+v_2)$ meets neither summand.
- Uniqueness of the highest weight together with the existence theorem for dominant weights yields the classification of the simple rational representations of a split reductive group.
