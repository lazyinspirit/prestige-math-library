## Statement

Let $v=(1,1,\dots,1)^{T}\in\Lambda_1^n$ and let
$\sigma=(1,t,t^2,\dots,t^{n-1})$ be the row vector, so that
$\sigma(x)=\sum_{i=1}^n t^{i-1}x_i$ on column vectors
$x=(x_1,\dots,x_n)^T$. For the unreduced matrices $B_i$ of
[[def-unreduced-burau-matrices]]:

(a) $B_iv=v$ for every $i$, hence $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)v=v$ for every
$\beta\in B_n$ and the line $\Lambda_1v$ is a $B_n$-invariant submodule of
$\Lambda_1^n$;

(b) a row vector $\tau$ satisfies $\tau B_i=\tau$ for every $i$ if and only if
$\tau=c\,\sigma$ for some $c\in\Lambda_1$, so the invariant covectors form the
free rank-one $\Lambda_1$-module $\Lambda_1\sigma$;

(c) $\sigma(v)=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1}$, which is nonzero in the integral domain
$\Lambda_1$ ([[lem-units-and-powers-of-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]). No
choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, the ring $\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$, the column vector $v=(1,\dots,1)^T$, the row vector $\sigma=(1,t,\dots,t^{n-1})$, and the matrices $B_1,\dots,B_{n-1}$ with $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ as in [[lem-unreduced-burau-matrices-satisfy-the-artin-relations]].

[F1] $B_i$ has the block $\begin{pmatrix}1-t&t\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ in rows and columns $i,i+1$ and is the identity elsewhere; its action on the basis vectors is $e_i\mapsto(1-t)e_i+e_{i+1}$, $e_{i+1}\mapsto te_i$, and $e_j\mapsto e_j$ otherwise ([[def-unreduced-burau-matrices]]).

[F2] $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n:B_n\to\operatorname{GL}_n(\Lambda_1)$ is the homomorphism extending $\sigma_i\mapsto B_i$ ([[lem-unreduced-burau-matrices-satisfy-the-artin-relations]]), and $B_n$ is generated by $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]).

[F3] $\Lambda_1$ is an integral domain; the polynomial $p=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1}$ is nonzero in $\mathbb Z[t]$ for $n\ge1$ (its leading coefficient is $1$ in degree $n-1$), and the localisation map $\mathbb Z[t]\to\Lambda_1$ is injective because no power $t^k\ne0$ annihilates a nonzero polynomial in the domain $\mathbb Z[t]$ ([[lem-units-and-powers-of-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]], [[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]], [[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Clause (a).* For a fixed $i$ compute the two affected coordinates of $B_iv$ using the column convention: the $i$-th entry is $(1-t)\cdot1+t\cdot1=1$, and the $(i+1)$-st entry is $1\cdot1+0\cdot1=1$; all other entries coincide with those of $v$, so $B_iv=v$. Since the $\sigma_i$ generate $B_n$ by [F2] and $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n$ is a homomorphism, $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)v=v$ for every braid word $\beta$; hence the line $\Lambda_1v$ is mapped into itself by every $\rho^{\mathrm{mat}}_n(\beta)$. [F1, F2]

1.2 *Clause (b).* For a row vector $\tau=(\tau_1,\dots,\tau_n)$ compute the affected coordinates of $\tau B_i$: the $i$-th entry is $\tau_i(1-t)+\tau_{i+1}\cdot1$ and the $(i+1)$-st entry is $\tau_it+\tau_{i+1}\cdot0$; all other entries are unchanged. Hence $\tau B_i=\tau$ holds if and only if $\tau_i(1-t)+\tau_{i+1}=\tau_i$ and $\tau_it=\tau_{i+1}$, both of which are equivalent to $\tau_{i+1}=t\tau_i$. If $\tau B_i=\tau$ for every $i$, the recurrence gives $\tau_i=t^{i-1}\tau_1$ for $i=1,\dots,n$ by induction, so $\tau=\tau_1\sigma$; conversely $\sigma B_i=\sigma$ by the same formulas, and then $\tau B_i=\tau$ for $\tau=c\sigma$. This proves clause (b). [F1, algebra]

1.3 *Clause (c).* Evaluating the row vector $\sigma$ on $v$ gives $\sigma(v)=\sum_{i=1}^nt^{i-1}=1+t+\cdots+t^{n-1}$, the image of the nonzero polynomial $p$ under the localisation map, which is injective by [F3]; hence $\sigma(v)$ is a nonzero element of the domain $\Lambda_1$. [F3]

2.1 *Conclusion.* Clause (a) is step 1.1, clause (b) is step 1.2 and clause (c) is step 1.3; no choice principle was used. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
