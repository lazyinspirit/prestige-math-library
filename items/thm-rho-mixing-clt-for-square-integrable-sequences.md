## Statement

Assume AC. Let $(Y_k)_{k\ge0}$ be a centered strictly stationary real sequence with $\mathbb E[Y_0^2]<\infty$. If
$$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\rho_Y(n)<\infty,$$
then the covariance series
$$\sigma_Y^2:=\mathbb E[Y_0^2]+2\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_k)$$
converges absolutely and is nonnegative. If $\sigma_Y^2>0$, then for $S_n=\sum_{k=1}^nY_k$,
$$n^{-1/2}S_n\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_Y^2).$$
If $\sigma_Y^2=0$, [[lem-zero-long-run-variance-gives-degenerate-clt]] gives the degenerate normal limit.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the centered strictly stationary real sequence $Y$, finite second moment, and $\sum_{n\ge1}\rho_Y(n)<\infty$.

[A1] AC is the inherited assumption of the local extension and martingale CLT suppliers. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] The coefficient $\rho_Y(n)$ bounds absolute correlations between the finite past and separated future. ([[def-mixing-coefficients-for-stationary-markov-chain]])

[F2] The sequence has a two-sided stationary realization $X_k=X_0\circ T^k$ with the same nonnegative-index law and the same maximal-correlation upper bounds for the full past; if $\rho_Y(n)\to0$, its invertible shift is ergodic even for mod-null invariant events. ([[lem-two-sided-stationary-extension-preserves-rho-mixing]])

[F3] Conditional expectation is the $L^2$ orthogonal projection onto the conditioning subspace and preserves ordinary expectation. ([[thm-conditional-expectation-is-the-l2-orthogonal-projection]], [[thm-basic-algebra-and-order-properties-of-conditional-expectation]])

[F4] For a centered $L^2$ process generated by an invertible probability-preserving ergodic transformation, summability of $\|\mathbb E[X_j\mid\mathcal F_0]\|_2$ gives a normal CLT with variance $\mathbb E[D_1^2]\ge0$, including zero; under absolute covariance summability this variance equals the covariance series. ([[thm-summable-l-two-projections-give-stationary-clt]])

[F5] Covariance and variance are centered product and square moments. ([[def-moments-variance-and-covariance]])

## Proof

1.1 Summability of the nonnegative numbers $\rho_Y(n)$ implies $\rho_Y(n)\to0$. By [F2], realize $Y$ as the nonnegative restriction in law of the two-sided stationary sequence $X_k=X_0\circ T^k$ with an invertible probability-preserving shift $T$ that is ergodic for mod-null invariant events. Its full past $\mathcal F_k=\sigma(X_i:i\le k)$ satisfies $|\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)|\le\rho_Y(n)\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(U)\operatorname{Var}(V)}$ for past $U$ and future $V$ separated by $n$. All second moments of the coordinates agree with those of $Y$. [A1, F1, F2, given]

2.1 Put $W_j=\mathbb E[X_j\mid\mathcal F_0]$ for $j\ge1$. By [F3], $W_j$ is centered and square-integrable and $\mathbb E[W_jX_j]=\mathbb E[W_j^2]$. If $\|W_j\|_2=0$, the bound $\|W_j\|_2\le\rho_Y(j)\|X_0\|_2$ is immediate. Otherwise apply step 1.1's covariance inequality to $W_j$ and $X_j$, divide by $\|W_j\|_2$, and obtain that same bound. Consequently $\sum_{j\ge1}\|\mathbb E[X_j\mid\mathcal F_0]\|_2\le\|X_0\|_2\sum_{j\ge1}\rho_Y(j)<\infty$. [F3, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Put $\gamma_j=\operatorname{Cov}(X_0,X_j)$. Step 1.1 applied to the two coordinates gives $|\gamma_j|\le\rho_Y(j)\mathbb E[X_0^2]$ for $j\ge1$; if $X_0$ is constant, both sides vanish. Thus $\sum_{j\ge1}|\gamma_j|<\infty$. All hypotheses of [F4] now hold by steps 1.1 and 2.1. It follows that $n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^nX_k\Rightarrow N(0,\tau^2)$ and $\tau^2=\mathbb E[X_0^2]+2\sum_{j\ge1}\gamma_j\ge0$. This includes $\tau^2=0$ and proves absolute covariance convergence. Since $X$ and $Y$ have the same nonnegative-index law, the series is exactly $\sigma_Y^2$ and the finite sums have the original laws. Therefore the asserted nondegenerate and degenerate limits both hold for $Y$. [F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, given] ∎
