## Example

Let $I$ be a nonzero ideal in a Dedekind domain $R$, and choose $0\neq a\in I$.
Then the proof of the two-generator theorem constructs an element $b\in I$ such
that $I=(a,b)$ by correcting only the finitely many prime valuations at which
$(a)$ is too large.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Dedekind domain $R$, a nonzero ideal $I$, and a chosen nonzero element $a\in I$.

[L1] Every nonzero ideal of a Dedekind domain is generated by the chosen element $a$ together with one further element $b\in I$ ([[cor-ideals-in-a-dedekind-domain-are-two-generated]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The theorem [L1] identifies a finite set of bad primes, namely those for which $v_{\mathfrak p}(a)>v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$, chooses local correction terms at those primes, and combines them by the Chinese remainder step in its proof. [L1, given]

2.1 The resulting element $b\in I$ has exactly the missing prime valuations, so $(a,b)=I$. This is the concrete content of the two-generator construction. [L1, step 1.1] ∎
