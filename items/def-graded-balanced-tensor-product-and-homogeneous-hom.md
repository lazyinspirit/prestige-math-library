## Definition

**Total internal degree on the balanced tensor product.** Let $A$ be a graded
$k$-algebra, $M$ a graded right $A$-module and $N$ a graded left $A$-module
([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]). First use the canonical
decomposition of the ordinary tensor product of the underlying abelian groups
$$M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N\cong\bigoplus_{i,j\in\mathbb Z}M_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N_j.$$
Its inverse sends $m\otimes n$ to the finite sum
$\sum_{i,j}m_i\otimes n_j$, where $m=\sum_i m_i$ and $n=\sum_jn_j$ are their
homogeneous decompositions; the two maps are inverse because they agree with
the identity on homogeneous elementary tensors. Give the summand
$M_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N_j$ total degree $i+j$, and write
$G_d:=\bigoplus_{i+j=d}M_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N_j$.

The balanced tensor product
$M\otimes_AN=(M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N)/\mathcal R_A$
([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]), where
$\mathcal R_A$ is generated by the balance relations
$(ma)\otimes n-m\otimes(an)$. These relations are generated by the homogeneous
ones: for $m=\sum_i m_i$, $a=\sum_\ell a_\ell$ and $n=\sum_jn_j$, the relation
is the finite sum of
$(m_i a_\ell)\otimes n_j-m_i\otimes(a_\ell n_j)$ over $i,\ell,j$. Each such
homogeneous relation belongs to $G_{i+\ell+j}$, since the two module actions
preserve total degree. Thus $\mathcal R_A=\bigoplus_d(\mathcal R_A\cap G_d)$,
and

$$M\otimes_AN=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb Z}(M\otimes_AN)_d,\qquad (M\otimes_AN)_d:=(G_d+\mathcal R_A)/\mathcal R_A,$$

is a graded abelian group. For homogeneous $m\in M_i$ and $n\in N_j$ the
elementary tensor $m\otimes n$ has **total internal degree** $i+j$; every tensor
is a finite sum of homogeneous elementary tensors, and the balancing relations
used in the quotient are homogeneous. This grading is the only grading used
below, and it introduces no sign.

**Outer actions.** If $M$ is a graded $(B,A)$-bimodule and $N$ a graded left
$A$-module, then the action $b(m\otimes n):=(bm)\otimes n$ of
[[thm-bimodule-actions-induced-on-tensor-products]] makes $M\otimes_AN$ a graded
left $B$-module: the action is homogeneous because homogeneous $b\in B_l$ sends
$m\otimes n$ of degree $i+j$ to an element of degree $l+i+j$. Symmetrically, if
$N$ is a graded $(A,C)$-bimodule, the action
$(m\otimes n)c:=m\otimes(nc)$ makes $M\otimes_AN$ a graded right $C$-module, and
the two outer actions commute when both are present.

**Homogeneous Hom.** For graded left $A$-modules $P,Q$, with
$\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)$ the abelian group of all $A$-linear
maps $P\to Q$, put

$$\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,Q):=\bigl\{f\in\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)\mid f(P_i)\subseteq Q_{i+d}\ \text{for all }i\in\mathbb Z\bigr\},\qquad \operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q):=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,Q).$$

Each $\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,Q)$ is a subgroup of
$\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)$, and the sum is direct: if
$f=\sum_df_d=\sum_df'_d$ with $f_d,f'_d\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}$, then for
$p\in P_i$ the element $f(p)$ has homogeneous components
$f_d(p)\in Q_{i+d}$, and comparison of the graded components in $Q$ gives
$f_d(p)=f'_d(p)$ for every $d$ and $p$, so $f_d=f'_d$. Thus
$\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q)$ is a graded abelian group whose degree-$d$ part is
$\operatorname{Hom}_{A,d}(P,Q)$, and the degree-zero morphisms are
$\operatorname{Hom}_{A,0}(P,Q)$. By definition $\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q)$
consists of the finite sums of homogeneous $A$-linear maps, so
$\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)$
with equality only when every $A$-linear map has bounded spread in degree.

**The associative left action.** If $M$ is a graded $(B,A)$-bimodule and $Y$ a
graded left $B$-module, then $\operatorname{HOM}_B(M,Y)$ carries a left
$A$-module structure

$$(a\cdot f)(m):=f(ma)\qquad(a\in A,\ m\in M),$$

which is homogeneous: for $a\in A_i$ and $f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B,d}(M,Y)$ one
has $ma\in M_{l+i}$ for $m\in M_l$, hence $(a\cdot f)(m)\in Y_{l+i+d}$ and
$a\cdot f\in\operatorname{Hom}_{B,d+i}(M,Y)$. The action is associative and
unital by the module axioms of $M$, so $\operatorname{HOM}_B(M,Y)$ is a graded
left $A$-module and an object of $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$. The same formula
defines an action on the full ungraded $\operatorname{Hom}$ group, where it is
denoted by the same symbol.

## Remark

$\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q)$ need not be all of
$\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)$. Let $k$ be a field,
$A=k[x]$ with $\deg x=1$, and $Q=A$; let $P=\bigoplus_{j\ge0}A\{-j\}$ with
generator $u_j:=1_A\in A\{-j\}$, an element of degree $-j$, and define the
$A$-linear map

$$f:P\longrightarrow A,\qquad f(u_j):=x^{2j}\quad(j\ge0).$$

For each $j$ the map $f_j$ that sends $u_j$ to $x^{2j}$ and every other $u_l$ to
$0$ is $A$-linear and homogeneous of degree $3j$: it kills all pieces
$A\{-l\}_d$ with $l\ne j$, and on $A\{-j\}_d=A_{d+j}$ it sends $a u_j$ to
$ax^{2j}\in A_{d+3j}$. The pointwise sum $\sum_jf_j$ equals $f$, because every
element of $P$ has finite support, but it is not a finite sum: if
$f=\sum_{t\in T}g_t$ with $T$ finite and $g_t$ homogeneous of degree $t$, then
evaluating at $u_j$ gives $x^{2j}=\sum_tg_t(u_j)$ with
$g_t(u_j)\in A_{t-j}$, and since $x^{2j}$ is a nonzero homogeneous element of
$A$ of degree $2j$ the only contribution comes from $t-j=2j$. Hence
$g_{3j}(u_j)=x^{2j}\ne0$ for every $j\ge0$, so $T$ would have to contain the
infinitely many distinct degrees $3j$, contradicting finiteness. Therefore
$f\notin\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,A)$, and the inclusion
$\operatorname{HOM}_A(P,Q)\subseteq\operatorname{Hom}_A^{\mathrm{ungr}}(P,Q)$
is proper in general.
