## Statement

Assume AC. Every smooth quasiprojective complex variety $U$ admits a smooth projective compactification $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ with reduced boundary a simple normal crossings divisor $D$. Given finitely many such compactifications, there is another dominating all of them and restricting to the identity on $U$. A morphism of smooth quasiprojective varieties extends to a morphism of such compactifications after modifying the source compactification outside its open variety.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a smooth quasiprojective complex variety $U$, meaning a separated finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme ([[def-separated-morphism-schemes]], [[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]]) whose structure morphism to $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ is H-quasi-projective ([[def-quasi-projective-morphism]]); $U$ need not be connected or irreducible, and its finitely many connected components are treated separately. A **compactification** of $U$ is an open immersion $U\hookrightarrow X$ into a proper $\mathbb C$-scheme with dense image ([[def-open-immersion-schemes]]); it is **smooth projective** when $X$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$ ([[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]]) and $X\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ is H-projective ([[def-projective-morphism-pre-proj]]), and its **boundary** is the complement of the image with its reduced structure. An **SNC compactification** is one whose boundary is a reduced effective Cartier divisor with simple normal crossings support ([[def-simple-normal-crossings-divisors]]), interpreted on each pure-dimensional component when dimensions differ.

[F1] Quasi-projectivity unpacked: the structure morphism $U\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ factors, for some $n\ge0$, through a quasi-compact immersion $U\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$ followed by the projection; an immersion factors as a closed immersion into an open subscheme ([[def-quasi-projective-morphism]], [[def-locally-closed-immersion]], [[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]], [[def-closed-immersion-schemes]]).

[F2] Scheme-theoretic image: for a quasi-compact morphism $f:X\to Y$, the kernel $\mathcal K=\ker(\mathcal O_Y\to f_*\mathcal O_X)$ is a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf and $V(\mathcal K)$ is the smallest closed subscheme of $Y$ through which $f$ factors; its restriction to every open $W\subseteq Y$ is the scheme-theoretic image of $f^{-1}(W)\to W$ ([[def-scheme-theoretic-image]], [[thm-scheme-theoretic-image-quasi-compact-morphism]]).

[F3] Weak embedded desingularization: for a field $K$ of characteristic zero, a smooth finite-type $K$-scheme $P$ and a reduced closed subscheme $Y\subseteq P$, there is a sequence $P=P_0\leftarrow P_1\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow P_r$ of blowups of regular centers $C_{i-1}\subseteq P_{i-1}$ such that every center avoids the copy of the original smooth locus $\operatorname{Reg}(Y)$ in the successive strict transform $Y_{i-1}$, the strict transform $Y_r$ is smooth and has simple normal crossings with the exceptional divisor, and the induced morphism $Y_r\to Y$ is proper, birational on each irreducible component and an isomorphism over $\operatorname{Reg}(Y)$; the construction is canonical and commutes with smooth morphisms and ambient embeddings ([[thm-weak-embedded-desingularization]]). Centers may meet points of $Y_{i-1}$ that become smooth during the sequence.

[F4] Principalization: for a field $K$ of characteristic zero, a smooth finite-type $K$-scheme $X$ and a coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$ that is not identically zero on any irreducible component of $X$, there is a sequence $X=X_0\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow X_s$ of blowups of regular centers with $\sigma^*\mathcal I$ the ideal of an effective Cartier divisor whose support has simple normal crossings; the centers of the underlying canonical resolution of the marked ideal $(\mathcal I,\varnothing,1)$ lie in its successive supports, and the controlled transform satisfies $\sigma_i^*\mathcal I_{i-1}=\mathcal I(D_i)\cdot\mathcal I_i$, so that $\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal I_i)\subseteq\sigma_i^{-1}(\operatorname{supp}\mathcal I)$ ([[thm-principalization-of-ideals]], [[prop-canonical-resolution-of-marked-ideals]], [[def-multiple-test-blowup-and-controlled-transform]], [[def-simple-normal-crossings-divisors]]).

[F5] Projectivity devices: on a scheme projective over a Noetherian ring, twisting a coherent sheaf by a large power of an ample invertible sheaf makes it globally generated ([[lem-eventual-global-generation-coherent-twists]], [[def-ample-invertible-sheaf]]); for an integral finite-type $k$-scheme $X$, an invertible sheaf $L$ and meromorphic sections $s_0,\dots,s_n$ of $L$ not all zero, the blowup $B$ of the fractional ideal $I=(\sum_i\mathcal O_Xs_i)\otimes L^{-1}$ is the schematic closure of the graph of the ratio map and $(\pi,\psi):B\hookrightarrow X\times_k\mathbb P^n_k$ is a closed immersion, with $B=\operatorname{Proj}_X(\bigoplus_{d\ge0}I^d)$ ([[cor-rational-map-to-projective-space-resolved-by-base-ideal-blowup]], [[def-blowup-scheme-along-ideal]]); $\mathbb P^m_S\times_S\mathbb P^n_S\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{(m+1)(n+1)-1}_S$ is a closed immersion ([[thm-segre-line-bundle-external-tensor]]); the pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$ along a closed immersion is H-very ample, hence ample over an affine base ([[lem-very-ample-implies-ample]]); closed immersions are stable under base change, and a composite of closed immersions is a closed immersion, since a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and a surjection of structure sheaves compose to such ([[lem-base-change-open-closed-immersions]], [[def-closed-immersion-schemes]]).

[F6] Regularity and smoothness: a smooth morphism at a point has geometrically regular scheme-theoretic fibre there, so a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme is regular and reduced, regular local rings being domains ([[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]], [[thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay]], [[def-regular-local-ring-geometric-point]]); in characteristic zero a finite-type scheme over a field is smooth if and only if all its local rings are regular ([[rem-resolution-of-singularities-conventions]], [[lem-ag-geometric-regularity-field-tests]]); a regular point of a reduced Noetherian scheme lies on exactly one irreducible component ([[lem-regular-point-lies-on-one-component]], [[def-irreducible-component-scheme]]); smoothness is stable under base change and composition ([[thm-smooth-morphisms-stable-base-change-composition]]), and étale is smooth of relative dimension zero ([[def-etale-morphism-schemes]]); étale morphisms are stable under base change and composition ([[lem-etale-stable-base-change-composition]]).

[F7] Regular immersions and blowup charts: for a closed immersion $C\hookrightarrow Y$ of smooth finite-type schemes over a field with pure dimensions and constant codimension, locally in the étale topology the embedding is the inverse image of a coordinate inclusion $\mathbb A^n\subset\mathbb A^m$ ([[lem-smooth-immersion-normal-sequence-and-deformation-charts]]); blowups commute with flat base change along $g$ for the inverse image ideal, $\operatorname{Bl}_{g^{-1}\mathcal I}X'\cong\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}X\times_XX'$ ([[thm-blowup-base-change-flat]]); the blowup is an isomorphism off the zero scheme of the ideal ([[lem-blowup-isomorphism-off-center]]); blowups restrict to open subschemes of the base ([[lem-blowup-local-on-base-scheme]]); a blowup of a nonzero finite-type ideal on an integral scheme is integral ([[lem-blowup-reduced-integral-under-domain-rees]]), and is finite type and proper ([[thm-blowup-projective]]); affine blowup charts are $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/a]$ with $IA[I/a]=aA[I/a]$ and $(A[I/a])_a=A_a$ ([[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]], [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]).

[F8] Finite type versus coherent: on a locally Noetherian scheme a quasi-coherent module of finite type is coherent ([[thm-coherent-sheaves-abelian-noetherian-scheme]], [[def-coherent-module-scheme]], [[def-quasi-coherent-ideal-sheaf]]).

[A1] AC is assumed in the statement and consumed through the suppliers above (scheme-theoretic images, canonical resolution and principalization centers, Serre generation, the graph-closure corollary, Segre and ample-pullback items); the divisor, component and diagonal/graph bookkeeping below is choice-free. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: reduce the quasiprojective variety to a reduced projective closure, assemble projectivity and smoothness of blowups along regular centers from the published resolution and blowup development, and then desingularize the closure and principalize its boundary ideal.

1.1 Closure of a quasi-compact immersion. Let $f:W\to P$ be a quasi-compact immersion from a reduced finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme into a Noetherian scheme, factored as a closed immersion $W\hookrightarrow V$ followed by an open immersion $V\hookrightarrow P$. By [F2] its scheme-theoretic image $Z=V(\mathcal K)$, $\mathcal K=\ker(\mathcal O_P\to f_*\mathcal O_W)$, restricts on $V$ to $W$, so $W$ is open in $Z$ with the same local rings. On an affine open $T=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq P$, the kernel $\mathcal K(T)$ is radical: if $g^r$ restricts to zero on $f^{-1}(T)$, then $g$ restricts to zero because $W$ is reduced. Thus $A/\mathcal K(T)$ is reduced, proving that $Z$ is reduced. If $W$ were not dense in $Z$, its proper reduced closed closure in $Z$ would receive $f$: its defining sections restrict to functions zero at every point of the reduced scheme $W$, hence zero on every affine open of $W$. This contradicts minimality of the scheme-theoretic image, so $W$ is dense. Every irreducible component meets $W$: the complement of the finitely many other components is a nonempty open subset of $Z$ contained in that component, since an irreducible component cannot be contained in their finite union; density forces this open to meet $W$. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Blowups of integral projective schemes are projective. Let $Y$ be an integral finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme whose structure morphism is H-projective, and let $\mathcal I$ be a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf of finite type. If $\mathcal I=0$ then $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y=\operatorname{Proj}_Y(\mathcal O_Y\oplus0\oplus\cdots)=\varnothing$, which is projective as the empty closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^0_{\mathbb C}$; assume $\mathcal I\ne0$. Fix a closed immersion $Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_{\mathbb C}$ and let $L=\mathcal O_Y(1)$ be the pullback of $\mathcal O(1)$, which is H-very ample by construction and ample over the affine base $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$; $Y$ is Noetherian and $\mathcal I$ is coherent, so for some $m\ge0$ the twist $\mathcal I(m)=\mathcal I\otimes L^{\otimes m}$ is globally generated. Its evaluation map $\Gamma(Y,\mathcal I(m))\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_Y\to\mathcal I(m)$ is surjective and $Y$ is quasi-compact with $\mathcal I(m)$ of finite type, so finitely many global sections $s_0,\dots,s_n$ already generate $\mathcal I(m)$: on a finite affine cover, a finitely generated module generated by the image of the global sections is generated by finitely many of them. Put $L'=L^{\otimes m}$, view the $s_i$ as meromorphic sections of the invertible sheaf $L'$, and set $F=\sum_i\mathcal O_Ys_i=\mathcal I(m)$ and $I=F\otimes L'^{-1}=\mathcal I$, so the ratio map of the $s_i$ is $Y\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$. The graph-closure corollary gives $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y=\operatorname{Proj}_Y(\bigoplus_{d\ge0}I^d)=B$ with a closed immersion $(\pi,\psi):B\hookrightarrow Y\times_{\mathbb C}\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$. Composing with the closed immersion $Y\times_{\mathbb C}\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_{\mathbb C}\times_{\mathbb C}\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$ obtained by base change of $Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^N_{\mathbb C}$ and then with the Segre embedding, itself a closed immersion, exhibits $B$ as a closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^{(N+1)(n+1)-1}_{\mathbb C}$; hence the structure morphism of $B$ is H-projective. [F5, F7, F8, construct, algebra]

1.3 Disjoint unions of projective schemes are projective. Let $Z_1,\dots,Z_t\subseteq\mathbb P^M_{\mathbb C}$ be closed subschemes with pairwise disjoint underlying sets and ideals $\mathcal I_\alpha$. The product ideal $\mathcal J=\mathcal I_1\cdots\mathcal I_t$ equals the intersection $\bigcap_\alpha\mathcal I_\alpha$: on an affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}A$ the ideals $J_\alpha$ of the $Z_\alpha$ satisfy $V(J_\alpha)\cap V(J_\beta)=\varnothing$ for $\alpha\ne\beta$, so $J_\alpha+J_\beta=A$ and the product of pairwise comaximal ideals is their intersection. At a point $x\in Z_\alpha$ one has $\mathcal I_{\beta,x}=\mathcal O_x$ for $\beta\ne\alpha$, so $\mathcal J_x=\mathcal I_{\alpha,x}$; therefore $V(\mathcal J)=\bigsqcup_\alpha Z_\alpha$ as subschemes of $\mathbb P^M$, and the natural morphism $\bigsqcup_\alpha Z_\alpha\to\mathbb P^M_{\mathbb C}$ is a closed immersion. Now let $X_1,\dots,X_t$ be projective $\mathbb C$-schemes, say $X_\alpha\subseteq\mathbb P^{M_\alpha}_{\mathbb C}$ closed. The coordinate-block maps $\mathbb P^{M_\alpha}_{\mathbb C}\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^M_{\mathbb C}$, $M=\sum_\alpha(M_\alpha+1)-1$, identify the projective spaces with pairwise disjoint linear subspaces (vanishing of all coordinates outside one block), and are closed immersions; the images of the $X_\alpha$ are closed subschemes with pairwise disjoint underlying sets, so the preceding paragraph realizes $\bigsqcup_\alpha X_\alpha$ as a closed subscheme of $\mathbb P^M_{\mathbb C}$. Hence a finite disjoint union of projective $\mathbb C$-schemes is projective over $\mathbb C$. [F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.4 Local reduction to pure dimensions and constant codimension. Let $Y$ be a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme and $C\subseteq Y$ a regular closed subscheme with ideal sheaf $\mathcal I$, a coherent ideal. Fix $x\in C$; if $C=\varnothing$ there is nothing to prove. Since smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-schemes and regular finite-type $\mathbb C$-schemes are smooth over $\mathbb C$ in characteristic zero, $C$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$ and, being regular Noetherian and reduced, each of its points lies on exactly one irreducible component; the components of $C$ are therefore pairwise disjoint, finitely many, and each is a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme of pure dimension $n$, with local rings equal to those of $C$ at its points. Let $C_0$ be the component through $x$ and let $Y^\circ$ be the connected component of $Y$ containing $x$; it is a single irreducible component, hence pure of dimension $m$, and open in $Y$. Choose an affine open $U\subseteq Y^\circ$ containing $x$ and disjoint from $C\smallsetminus C_0$ (a finite union of closed sets missing $x$). Then $U$ is smooth of pure dimension $m$, $C\cap U=C_0\cap U$ is closed in $U$, smooth, irreducible of dimension $n$, and the closed immersion $C\cap U\hookrightarrow U$ has constant codimension $d=m-n$. [F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Regularity and components. A point $w\in W$ is a regular point of $Z$: the local rings of $Z$ and $W$ at $w$ coincide by step 1.1, and a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme has regular local rings. Every irreducible component of $Z$ meets $W$: for a component $Z_\alpha$ of the reduced Noetherian $Z$, the complement of the union of the finitely many other components is a nonempty open subset of $Z$ contained in $Z_\alpha$, nonemptiness holding because otherwise irreducibility of $Z_\alpha$ would place it inside one of the other components, contradicting maximality; and the dense open $W$ meets every nonempty open subset. If $Z$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$, as the schemes $P$, $P_i$ and $Z'$ below are, then its irreducible components are pairwise disjoint: a common point of two components would give the local ring there two distinct minimal primes and hence a non-domain, whereas a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme has regular local rings, which are domains. Consequently, for smooth $Z$, the components are finitely many pairwise disjoint integral closed subschemes, open and closed in $Z$. [F6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Blowups preserve finite disjoint unions of integral projective schemes. Let $X=\bigsqcup_\alpha X_\alpha$ with each $X_\alpha$ integral, finite type and H-projective over $\mathbb C$, and let $\mathcal I$ be a quasi-coherent ideal sheaf of finite type on $X$; write $\mathcal I_\alpha=\mathcal I|_{X_\alpha}$. Since the pieces are open and closed, the blowup restricts: $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}X\cong\bigsqcup_\alpha\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I_\alpha}X_\alpha$. If $\mathcal I_\alpha=0$ then that piece is empty; if $\mathcal I_\alpha\ne0$ then $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I_\alpha}X_\alpha$ is integral, finite type and H-projective by step 1.2. So the blowup is again a finite disjoint union of integral projective $\mathbb C$-schemes, and it is projective over $\mathbb C$ by step 1.3; the relevant ideals are quasi-coherent of finite type, as ideals of closed subschemes of finite-type $\mathbb C$-schemes (affine-locally the kernel of a surjection of Noetherian rings, hence finitely generated). [F7, F8, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 Smoothness of the blowup along a regular center. Keep the notation of step 1.4 and first blow up the coordinate subspace: for $A=\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_m]$ and $I=(x_{n+1},\dots,x_m)$, the chart of $\operatorname{Bl}_I\operatorname{Spec}A$ indexed by $x_j$ ($n<j\le m$) is $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/x_j]$, and $A[I/x_j]$ is generated over $A$ by the fractions $x_l/x_j$ ($n<l\le m$, $l\ne j$); substituting $x_l=y_lx_j$ exhibits it as the polynomial ring $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n,x_j,\ y_l\ (n<l\le m,\ l\ne j)]$, the two presentations agreeing inside $A_{x_j}$ because the $y_l$ are algebraically independent over $\mathbb C[x_1,\dots,x_n,x_j]$ (clearing denominators uses only that $x_j$ is a nonzerodivisor). Hence $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb A^n}\mathbb A^m$ is covered by affine spaces and is smooth over $\mathbb C$. Now by [F7] the closed immersion $C\cap U\hookrightarrow U$ of step 1.4 is, at each of its points, locally in the étale topology the inverse image of the coordinate inclusion $\mathbb A^n\subset\mathbb A^m$: there are a Zariski open $V\subseteq U$ and an étale morphism $\varphi:V\to\mathbb A^m_{\mathbb C}$ with $\varphi^{-1}(\mathbb A^n)=C\cap V$ as closed subschemes. Since $\varphi$ is flat and blowups commute with flat base change along the inverse image ideal, $\operatorname{Bl}_{C\cap V}V\cong\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb A^n}\mathbb A^m\times_{\mathbb A^m}V$. The projection of this fibre product to $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathbb A^n}\mathbb A^m$ is the base change of the étale morphism $\varphi$, hence étale, and étale morphisms are smooth; a composite of smooth morphisms is smooth, so the model base change, and therefore $\operatorname{Bl}_{C\cap V}V$, is smooth over $\mathbb C$. [F6, F7, step 1.4, construct, algebra]

3.1 Conclusion for one blowup and for sequences. With $\pi:\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y\to Y$ and $x\in C$ as in step 1.4, choose the open $V$ of step 2.3 around $x$; the open subscheme $\pi^{-1}(V)$ of the blowup is smooth over $\mathbb C$. Points of $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y$ mapping outside $C$ lie in the open subscheme $\pi^{-1}(Y\smallsetminus C)$, which is isomorphic to the smooth scheme $Y\smallsetminus C$; as $x$ ranges over $C$, these opens cover $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y$, and smoothness is local on the source, so $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}Y$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$. The blowup is finite type over $\mathbb C$, so the same statement applies to it in place of $Y$: if $C_1\subseteq Y_1$ is a regular closed subscheme of a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme $Y_1$, then $\operatorname{Bl}_{C_1}Y_1$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$. By induction, every scheme obtained from a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme by a finite sequence of blowups along regular closed subschemes is smooth over $\mathbb C$. [F6, F7, step 1.4, step 2.3, construct]

3.2 The model construction: desingularizing the closure. Let $P$ be a smooth projective $\mathbb C$-scheme, $Z\subseteq P$ a reduced closed subscheme and $W\subseteq Z$ an open dense subscheme that is smooth over $\mathbb C$. Apply [F3] to $Z\subseteq P$: there is a sequence $P=P_0\leftarrow P_1\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow P_r$ of blowups of regular centers with strict transforms $Z_i\subseteq P_i$, and $Z'=Z_r$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$ with the composite $P_r\to P$ an isomorphism over the smooth locus of $Z$, hence over $W$. Each $P_i$ is a finite disjoint union of integral projective $\mathbb C$-schemes: $P_0=P$ is smooth projective, so it is such a union by step 2.1, and each step is a blowup along the ideal sheaf of a regular closed subscheme, a coherent ideal, so step 2.2 applies inductively. Let $W'$ be the preimage of $W$ in $Z'$; it is open in $Z'$ and isomorphic to $W$. Every irreducible component of $Z'$ meets $W'$: the composite $Z'\to Z$ is proper and birational on every component, so a component of $Z'$ maps onto a component of $Z$; a component of $Z'$ disjoint from $W'$ would have image contained in $Z\smallsetminus W$, a closed set, but every component of $Z$ meets the dense open $W$ by step 2.1, a contradiction. Consequently the ideal $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_{Z'}$ of the reduced closed subscheme $Z'\smallsetminus W'$ is coherent and is not identically zero on any irreducible component of $Z'$. [F3, F8, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 The model construction: principalizing the boundary. Keep step 3.2 and decompose $Z'=\bigsqcup_\alpha Z'_\alpha$ into its finitely many components, open and closed in $Z'$ by step 2.1. For each $\alpha$, apply [F4] to the smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme $Z'_\alpha$ and the restricted ideal $\mathcal I_\alpha$ if $\mathcal I_\alpha\ne\mathcal O_{Z'_\alpha}$, and take the identity sequence if $\mathcal I_\alpha=\mathcal O_{Z'_\alpha}$; the centers lie in the successive supports, which map into $\operatorname{supp}\mathcal I_\alpha$, so at every step the blowup is an isomorphism over the complement of the preimage of $\operatorname{supp}\mathcal I_\alpha$, and the composite $\sigma_\alpha:X_\alpha\to Z'_\alpha$ is an isomorphism over $Z'_\alpha\smallsetminus\operatorname{supp}\mathcal I_\alpha$. Set $X=\bigsqcup_\alpha X_\alpha$ and $\sigma=\bigsqcup_\alpha\sigma_\alpha$. Each $X_\alpha$ is a finite sequence of blowups of the integral projective scheme $Z'_\alpha$ along regular centers: by step 2.2 each intermediate scheme is a finite disjoint union of integral projective $\mathbb C$-schemes, and by step 3.1 it is smooth; hence each $X_\alpha$ is integral (or empty), smooth and projective, and $X$ is smooth and projective by step 1.3. On each $Z'_\alpha$ with $\mathcal I_\alpha\ne\mathcal O_{Z'_\alpha}$, the total transform $\sigma_\alpha^*\mathcal I_\alpha$ is the ideal of an effective Cartier divisor with SNC support; on pieces with $\mathcal I_\alpha=\mathcal O_{Z'_\alpha}$ it is the unit ideal, defining the zero divisor with empty support. Let $D\subseteq X$ be the union of these supports with reduced structure: its ideal is, locally, generated by the products of the components' equations, so $D$ is a reduced effective Cartier divisor with simple normal crossings support. Finally $\sigma$ is an isomorphism over $Z'\smallsetminus\operatorname{supp}\mathcal I=W'$, so $X\smallsetminus D\cong W'\cong W$, and the composite morphism $c:X\to P_r\to\cdots\to P$ restricts to the given open immersion $W\hookrightarrow Z\subseteq P$. This proves: (C) for every smooth projective $P$, reduced closed $Z\subseteq P$ and open dense smooth $W\subseteq Z$, there are a smooth projective $X$, a reduced SNC divisor $D\subseteq X$, an isomorphism $X\smallsetminus D\cong W$ and a morphism $c:X\to P$ inducing the inclusion of $W$; moreover $X$ is a finite disjoint union of integral projective pieces. [F4, F6, F7, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Existence of an SNC projective compactification. By [F1] there is a quasi-compact immersion $\iota:U\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$; let $Z\subseteq\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$ be its scheme-theoretic image, so that $U\cong Z\cap V$ is open and dense in the reduced closed subscheme $Z$, with $U$ contained in the regular locus, by steps 1.1 and 2.1. Apply (C) of step 4.1 with $P=\mathbb P^n_{\mathbb C}$ and $W=U$: the output $X$ is a finite disjoint union of integral projective smooth $\mathbb C$-schemes with a reduced SNC boundary divisor $D$ and $X\smallsetminus D\cong U$. The exceptional boundary divisor misses the generic point of every component, so its intersection with each irreducible component is a proper closed subset and hence nowhere dense; therefore $X\smallsetminus D$ is dense in $X$, and $j:U\cong X\smallsetminus D\hookrightarrow X$ is a smooth projective compactification with reduced SNC boundary. If $U=\varnothing$ the same construction with $Z=\varnothing$ returns $X=\varnothing$ and the empty divisor, and all claims are vacuous. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, given, construct]

6.1 Common domination. Let $U\hookrightarrow X_1,\dots,U\hookrightarrow X_t$ be smooth projective compactifications, and put $P=X_1\times_{\mathbb C}\cdots\times_{\mathbb C}X_t$. Each $X_i$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$ and H-projective, so $P$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$ by stability of smoothness under base change and composition, and projective over $\mathbb C$: the product of closed immersions $X_1\times_{\mathbb C}\cdots\times_{\mathbb C}X_t\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^{N_1}_{\mathbb C}\times_{\mathbb C}\cdots\times_{\mathbb C}\mathbb P^{N_t}_{\mathbb C}$ is a closed immersion by iterated base change, and the iterated Segre embeddings are closed immersions. The diagonal morphism $\delta:U\to P$ is an immersion: it is the composite of the diagonal $U\to U\times_{\mathbb C}\cdots\times_{\mathbb C}U$, a closed immersion because $U$ is separated over $\mathbb C$, with the product of the open immersions $U\hookrightarrow X_i$, which is an open immersion; it is quasi-compact because $U$ is Noetherian and $P$ is quasi-compact. Let $Z$ be its scheme-theoretic image; by step 1.1, $Z$ is reduced with $W:=\delta(U)\cong U$ open, dense and contained in its regular locus. Apply (C) of step 4.1 to $(P,Z,W)$ and compose the resulting morphism $c:X\to P$ with the projections: the composites $X\to X_i$ restrict to the identity of $U$ under the identifications $X\smallsetminus D\cong U$, and $X$ is a smooth projective compactification of $U$ with reduced SNC boundary dominating all $X_i$. For the empty family, step 5.1 already supplies a compactification. [F1, F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 Extension of morphisms. Let $\varphi:U\to V$ be a morphism of smooth quasiprojective complex varieties and let $U\hookrightarrow\tilde U$, $V\hookrightarrow\tilde V$ be smooth projective compactifications. Set $P=\tilde U\times_{\mathbb C}\tilde V$; as in step 6.1 it is smooth and projective. Consider the morphism $\gamma=(\iota,\varphi):U\to P$, where $\iota$ is the open immersion $U\hookrightarrow\tilde U$; it factors as the graph morphism $U\to U\times_{\mathbb C}V$, a closed immersion because $V$ is separated over $\mathbb C$, followed by the open immersion $U\times_{\mathbb C}V\hookrightarrow\tilde U\times_{\mathbb C}\tilde V$, so $\gamma$ is an immersion, and it is quasi-compact because $U$ is Noetherian. Let $Z$ be the scheme-theoretic image of $\gamma$; by step 1.1, $Z$ is reduced and $W:=\gamma(U)\cong U$ is open, dense and smooth inside $Z$. Apply (C) of step 4.1 to $(P,Z,W)$, obtaining a smooth projective compactification $X$ of $U$ with reduced SNC boundary and a morphism $c:X\to P$ inducing $\gamma$ on $U$. Then the composite of $c$ with the second projection $P\to\tilde V$ is a morphism $X\to\tilde V$ restricting to $\varphi$ on $U$, and the composite with the first projection is a morphism $X\to\tilde U$ restricting to the identity on $U$; so $\varphi$ extends after modifying the source compactification outside $U$. Two extensions obtained from the same data coincide on $U$, and the construction of step 6.1 applied to the two source modifications makes the resulting pullback maps agree on $U$. ## Remarks

- The domination and extension clauses hold for arbitrary smooth projective compactifications of $U$, not only for those with SNC boundary produced by the first clause: steps 6.1 and 7.1 take for $X_1,\dots,X_t$ and for $\tilde U,\tilde V$ arbitrary smooth projective compactifications, the output $X$ of step 4.1 dominates each of them and restricts to the identity over $U$ while itself having reduced SNC boundary. This is the reading used by the consumers that must transfer a statement from an SNC compactification to an arbitrary smooth projective one.

- The construction is stated for possibly reducible and disconnected $U$; the connected components of a smooth finite-type $\mathbb C$-scheme are its irreducible components and are pairwise disjoint, and each is compactified separately. The SNC compactification produced by step 4.1 is a finite disjoint union of integral projective pieces, which is projective by step 1.3.

- The proof deliberately does not cite projectivity of a resolution: the projectivity of every blowup used is derived from global generation of a twist of the center ideal and the graph-closure corollary, and the smoothness of every blowup along a regular center is derived from the étale-local coordinate model of a regular immersion. The published resolution statements supply the sequences and the smoothness of the strict transform, not projectivity.
