## Statement

Let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent centered real random variables, with $X_i$ sub-exponential with parameters $(\nu_i^2,b_i)$ as defined in [[def-subgaussian-variable-and-vector]], where $n\ge1$ and $b_i>0$. Put $v=\sum_{i=1}^n\nu_i^2$, $b=\max_{1\le i\le n}b_i$, and $S=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$. For $t>0$, if $v>0$ then
$$\mathbb P(|S|\ge t)\le2\exp\!\left(-\frac12\min\!\left\{\frac{t^2}{v},\frac{t}{b}\right\}\right),$$
and if $v=0$ then $\mathbb P(|S|\ge t)=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Independent centered real random variables $X_i$ with sub-exponential parameters $(\nu_i^2,b_i)$, where $\nu_i^2\ge0$ and $b_i>0$.

[F1] Sub-exponentiality means $\mathbb E e^{\lambda X_i}\le e^{\lambda^2\nu_i^2/2}$ whenever $|\lambda|\le1/b_i$ ([[def-subgaussian-variable-and-vector]]).

[F2] Independence means independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the variables ([[def-independent-random-elements]]).

[F3] For independent real variables and nonnegative Borel functions, the expectation of the product factors ([[thm-factorization-of-expectations-for-independent-variables]]).

[F4] Markov's inequality gives $\mathbb P(Y\ge r)\le\mathbb E[Y]/r$ for $Y\ge0$ and $r>0$ ([[cor-markov-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

[F5] The exponential is continuous and strictly increasing, positive, satisfies $e^{-x}=1/e^x$ and $e^{x+y}=e^xe^y$, and has $e^0=1$; these imply $e^x+e^{-x}-2=e^{-x}(e^x-1)^2\ge0$, with equality only at $x=0$ ([[def-real-exponential-function-and-e]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]], [[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]], [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]]).

[F6] A nonnegative integrable function with integral $0$ vanishes almost everywhere ([[thm-nonnegative-integral-zero-iff-zero-almost-everywhere]]).

[F7] Probability is subadditive on finite unions ([[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]).

[F8] A nonempty finite set of real numbers has a maximum ([[lem-finite-set-has-max]], [[def-max-min]]).

[F9] Expectation is linear on integrable real random variables ([[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]).

[F10] Finite sums of real-valued measurable functions are measurable ([[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $v=0$, then every $\nu_i^2=0$. Fix $i$ and choose $\lambda_i=1/(2b_i)>0$. By [F1], both $\mathbb E e^{\lambda_iX_i}$ and $\mathbb E e^{-\lambda_iX_i}$ are at most $1$. The nonnegative random variable $Q_i=e^{\lambda_iX_i}+e^{-\lambda_iX_i}-2$ therefore has expectation at most $0$ by [F9]. By [F5], $Q_i=0$ only when $X_i=0$; [F6] gives $X_i=0$ almost surely. The finite sum $S$ is consequently $0$ almost surely by [F7]. [given, F1, F5, F6, F7, F9, F10, algebra]

1.2 Suppose $v>0$. By [F8], $b=\max_i b_i$ exists and is positive. For each real $\theta$, the map $g_\theta(x)=e^{\theta x}$ is continuous: scalar multiplication is continuous since $|\theta x-\theta y|=|\theta||x-y|$, and the exponential is continuous by [F5]. Its inverse images of open sets are open, so the collection of Borel sets with Borel preimage is a sigma-algebra containing the opens; hence $g_\theta$ is Borel by [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], For each Borel $B$, $\{g_\theta(X_i)\in B\}=X_i^{-1}(g_\theta^{-1}(B))$ is measurable. For $0\le\lambda\le1/b$, [F1] applies to every $X_i$. By [F5], $e^{\lambda S}=\prod_i e^{\lambda X_i}$; independence [F2] and factorization [F3] give $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S}=\prod_i\mathbb E e^{\lambda X_i}\le e^{\lambda^2v/2}$. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F8, given, construct]

2.1 For $t>0$ and $0<\lambda\le1/b$, [F10] makes $S$ a measurable real random variable, and $S\ge t$ implies $e^{\lambda S}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$ by [F5]. Markov [F4], step 1.2, and the reciprocal and addition formulas in [F5] yield $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda^2v/2)$. Choose $\lambda=\min\{t/v,1/b\}$. If $t\le v/b$, the exponent is $-t^2/(2v)$. If $t>v/b$, it is $-t/b+v/(2b^2)\le-t/(2b)$. Hence $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le\exp[-\tfrac12\min\{t^2/v,t/b\}]$. [F4, F5, F10, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 For $0<\lambda\le1/b$, [F1] applies at parameter $-\lambda$ to every original $X_i$. By [F5], $e^{-\lambda S}=\prod_i e^{-\lambda X_i}$; the original variables are independent by [F2], the functions $x\mapsto e^{-\lambda x}$ are nonnegative Borel by step 1.2, and factorization [F3] gives $\mathbb E e^{-\lambda S}\le e^{\lambda^2v/2}$. Markov [F4] and the same optimizing choice as in step 2.1 yield the stated lower-tail bound. The finite union bound [F7] combines the tails; step 1.1 handles $v=0$. No independence assertion about the transformed variables is needed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F7, F10, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
