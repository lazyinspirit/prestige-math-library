## Example

Take $\mathfrak a=(2x,\,3x^{2},\,x^{3})$ in $\mathbb Z[x]$. Its stage ideals
([[lem-leading-coefficient-ideals]]) are

$$\mathfrak a_0=0,\qquad \mathfrak a_1=(2),\qquad \mathfrak a_n=\mathbb Z\ \ \text{for } n\ge2,$$

so the chain stabilises at $N=2$. Choosing the realisers $2x$ at stage $1$ and
$x^{2}$ at stage $2$, the generating list produced by
[[lem-polynomial-ideal-finite-generation]] is $2x,\;x^{2}$, and indeed
$\mathfrak a=(2x,\,x^{2})$.

The reduction of [[lem-hilbert-basis-degree-cancellation]] applied to
$f=5x^{4}+2x\in\mathfrak a$ at stage $n=2$ subtracts $h=5x^{4}$ and leaves
$2x$, of degree $1$; applied again at stage $n=1$ it subtracts $2x$ and leaves
$0$.

The stage $\mathfrak a_1=(2)$ is generated by a non-unit, so no division by a
leading coefficient is available at that stage; the reduction uses the stage
ideal instead.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring $\mathbb Z[x]$ and the ideal $\mathfrak a=(2x,3x^{2},x^{3})$.

[L1] $R[x]$ is the set of finitely supported functions $\mathbb N\to R$, with coefficientwise addition and the convolution product $(ab)_i=\sum_{j+k=i}a_jb_k$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L2] For $0\ne f\in R[x]$ the degree is the largest index carrying a nonzero coefficient and the leading coefficient is the coefficient there; the zero polynomial has no degree and no leading coefficient ([[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]).

[L3] For $S\subseteq R$, $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, so $S\subseteq(S)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[L4] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L5] For an ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $R[x]$ and $n\in\mathbb N$, the set $\mathfrak a_n$ of leading coefficients of the nonzero degree-$n$ elements of $\mathfrak a$, together with $0$, is an ideal of $R$, and $\mathfrak a_n\subseteq\mathfrak a_{n+1}$ ([[lem-leading-coefficient-ideals]]).

[L6] Over a Noetherian ring, with $N$ a stabilisation index of the stage chain and with realisers chosen for generators of $\mathfrak a_n$ for each $n\le N$, those realisers generate $\mathfrak a$ ([[lem-polynomial-ideal-finite-generation]]).

[L7] With $\mathfrak a_n=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$ realised at stage $n$ by $g_1,\ldots,g_m$, a nonzero $f\in\mathfrak a$ of degree $d\ge n$ with $\operatorname{lc}(f)=\sum_jr_jc_j\in\mathfrak a_n$ admits $h=\sum_jr_jx^{d-n}g_j$ in the ideal generated by the $g_j$ with $f-h=0$ or $\deg(f-h)<d$ ([[lem-hilbert-basis-degree-cancellation]]).

[L8] If $R$ is a Noetherian commutative ring then $R[x]$ is Noetherian ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

[L9] $\mathbb Z$ is a Noetherian ring ([[ex-noetherian-integers-and-fields]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every element of $\mathfrak a$ is $a\cdot2x+b\cdot3x^{2}+c\cdot x^{3}$ with $a,b,c\in\mathbb Z[x]$. Its constant coefficient is $0$, since each of $2x$, $3x^{2}$, $x^{3}$ has support in the indices $\ge1$; and its coefficient at $x^{1}$ is $2a_0$, since the second and third products have support in the indices $\ge2$. So $\mathfrak a$ contains no nonzero constant, giving $\mathfrak a_0=0$, and every element of $\mathfrak a_1$ lies in $(2)$. [L1, L2, L3, L4, given]

2.1 The element $2x$ lies in $\mathfrak a$, is nonzero of degree $1$ and has leading coefficient $2$, so $2\in\mathfrak a_1$; with step 1.1 this gives $\mathfrak a_1=(2)$. The element $x^{2}=3x^{2}-x\cdot(2x)$ lies in $\mathfrak a$, is nonzero of degree $2$ and has leading coefficient $1$, so $1\in\mathfrak a_2$ and $\mathfrak a_2=\mathbb Z$. [L1, L2, L4, L5, step 1.1]

3.1 The stages ascend, so $\mathbb Z=\mathfrak a_2\subseteq\mathfrak a_n\subseteq\mathbb Z$ and $\mathfrak a_n=\mathbb Z$ for every $n\ge2$. The chain reads $0\subsetneq(2)\subsetneq\mathbb Z=\mathbb Z=\cdots$ and stabilises at $N=2$; it does not stabilise at $N=1$, since $\mathfrak a_1=(2)\ne\mathbb Z=\mathfrak a_2$. [L5, step 2.1]

4.1 Run the finite-generation lemma with $N=2$. Stage $0$ has $\mathfrak a_0=0$ and contributes no realiser; stage $1$ has the single generator $2$ of $(2)$, realised by $2x$; stage $2$ has the single generator $1$ of $\mathbb Z$, realised by $x^{2}$. So the lemma returns $\mathfrak a=(2x,x^{2})$. This is checkable by hand: $2x$ and $x^{2}$ lie in $\mathfrak a$ by steps 1.1 and 2.1, while $3x^{2}=3\cdot x^{2}$ and $x^{3}=x\cdot x^{2}$ lie in $(2x,x^{2})$. [L4, L6, L8, L9, step 3.1]

5.1 Take $f=5x^{4}+2x$, which lies in $\mathfrak a$ because $5x^{4}=5x^{2}\cdot x^{2}$ and $2x$ do. It is nonzero of degree $d=4$ with $\operatorname{lc}(f)=5\in\mathbb Z=\mathfrak a_2$, so the cancellation lemma applies at $n=\min(4,2)=2$ with the single realiser $x^{2}$ and coefficient $r_1=5$: it forms $h=5x^{4-2}\cdot x^{2}=5x^{4}$ and leaves $f-h=2x$, nonzero of degree $1<4$. Applying it again to $2x$ at $n=1$, with realiser $2x$ and coefficient $r_1=1$, forms $h=1\cdot x^{0}\cdot 2x=2x$ and leaves $0$. Adding the two corrections back recovers $f=5x^{4}+2x$ as an element of $(2x,x^{2})$. [L2, L7, step 4.1]

6.1 The point of the example is the non-monic stage. At stage $1$ the ideal $\mathfrak a_1=(2)$ has no unit generator, so the degree-$1$ part of an element of $\mathfrak a$ cannot be cleared by dividing by a leading coefficient; the reduction has to express $\operatorname{lc}(f)$ inside the stage ideal and use a realiser, which is exactly what steps 4.1 and 5.1 do. That $\mathbb Z[x]$ is Noetherian at all is the Hilbert basis theorem applied to the Noetherian ring $\mathbb Z$. [L5, L8, L9, step 5.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **The generating list is not minimal by construction, and here it happens to be short.** The lemma returns one realiser per generator of each stage up to $N$; a larger stabilisation index or a larger generating set of a stage would return a longer list generating the same ideal.

- **The original list is not the one the construction returns.** The ideal was presented as $(2x,3x^{2},x^{3})$ and the construction returns $(2x,x^{2})$; both generate $\mathfrak a$, and step 4.1 checks the agreement directly rather than inferring it.
