## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. The certified splitting-field-and-root algorithm terminates for every nonzero $F\in\mathbb Q[T]$. Its returned polynomial $h$ is monic, integral and irreducible; its distinct root list and multiplicities give the exact factorization of $F$ over $L=\mathbb Q[Z]/(h)$; and those roots generate $L$, so $L$ is a splitting field of $F$ over $\mathbb Q$. Nonzero constants and repeated roots are included. No approximation to a complex root is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Choice and the nonzero rational input polynomial of the algorithm.

[A1] AC implies the weaker choice principles of the complex-root supplier. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]])

[F1] Finite polynomial families have splitting fields; adjoining finitely many algebraic elements gives a finite extension, and a finite separable extension is simple. ([[cor-splitting-fields-exist-for-finite-families]], [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]], [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]])

[F2] Complex polynomials split. Algebraic integers form a subring and a rational algebraic integer is an integer; polynomial rings over fields have unique factorization. ([[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]], [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]], [[cor-rational-algebraic-integers-are-integers]], [[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]])

[F3] The algorithm enumerates field candidates and exact factorization certificates and restricts to the generated subfield. ([[alg-certified-splitting-field-and-exact-roots]])

## Proof

1.1 For a monic integer polynomial $g$ of degree $d$, every complex root $z$ has $|z|\le R=1+\max_{j<d}|g_j|$: if $|z|>R$, then $|z|^d\le(R-1)\sum_{j<d}|z|^j<|z|^d$, a contradiction. A monic rational factor of $g$ has roots among these roots, counted with multiplicity. Its coefficients are elementary symmetric functions in algebraic integers, so are algebraic integers by [F2]; being rational, they are integers. A degree-$a$ factor has coefficients bounded by $\binom a jR^j\le2^dR^d$. If $g$ is reducible, one monic factor has degree at most $d/2$. Thus the finite factor scan in [F3] detects every reducible $g$ and accepts precisely irreducible polynomials, including degree1. Every candidate-field test is consequently a finite exact calculation in a genuine field. [given, A1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The monic squarefree part $S$ has exactly the distinct roots of $F$: in characteristic zero, a factor $(T-\rho)^a$ occurs in $\gcd(F,F')$ with multiplicity $a-1$. This follows by differentiating that factorization and using that $a\ne0$; [F1] supplies a splitting field in which to check it. Its degree $t$ is therefore the number of distinct roots. A splitting field of $S$ is finite and separable, hence is generated by one algebraic $\theta$ by [F1]. Multiplying $\theta$ by a common denominator of its monic rational minimal polynomial gives an integral primitive generator: if that polynomial has degree $d$ and coefficients $c_i$, $e^d g(T/e)$ has integral coefficients when $e$ is a common multiple of their denominators. Thus a monic irreducible integer presentation containing all $t$ roots occurs in the enumeration, and the roots have finite rational residue expressions in it. This complete certificate is reached at a finite diagonal bound. The outer search therefore terminates, without needing any bounded-time assertion for the first successful field degree. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra]

3.1 Every accepted certificate is checked by exact coefficient equality, so all and only the $t$ distinct roots of $S$ occur. Division of $F$ by its displayed linear factors determines their multiplicities in finitely many steps, decreasing the degree at every successful division. Since $S$ has all its distinct roots, the remaining factor is the nonzero leading scalar. Thus the list and multiplicities give the exact factorization of $F$. [step 2.1, F2, F3, algebra]

4.1 Each basis extension in step4 of [F3] increases dimension inside the finite-dimensional temporary field, so the closure loop terminates. Its final span $E$ contains1 and all the roots and is stable under multiplying by each root, hence contains every polynomial in them. Conversely every vector adjoined is such a polynomial, so $E=\mathbb Q[\rho_1,\ldots,\rho_t]$. It is a finite-dimensional domain. For nonzero $a\in E$, multiplication by $a$ is an injective linear map on $E$ and therefore surjective; solving $ab=1$ gives $b\in E$. Thus $E$ is exactly the field generated by the roots. [step 3.1, F3, algebra]

5.1 By [F1], the finite characteristic-zero extension $E/\mathbb Q$ has a primitive element. It is a rational linear combination of the finite basis from step4; after multiplication by a positive integer it is an integer linear combination and remains primitive. The search in step5 therefore terminates. Independence of its first $s$ powers makes their minimal relation monic of exact degree $s$, and its irreducibility follows because $E$ is a field and no polynomial of smaller degree vanishes. Scaling by $e$ preserves that degree and irreducibility, and the displayed coefficient formula makes $h$ integral. Exact linear coordinate conversion preserves all root identities. The returned field is isomorphic to $E$, which is generated by the full root list, proving the splitting-field assertion. For a nonzero constant, the explicit initial return is $\mathbb Q$ with no roots, as required. [step 4.1, F1, F3, algebra] ∎
