## Statement

Assume Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $M$ be a smooth manifold, with boundary allowed, and let $\omega$ be a nowhere-vanishing smooth $1$-form on
$M$. (i) If $\alpha\in\Omega^1(M)$ satisfies $\alpha\wedge\omega=0$, then there is a
unique $f\in C^\infty(M)$ with $\alpha=f\omega$. (ii) If $\theta\in\Omega^2(M)$
satisfies $\theta\wedge\omega=0$, then there is $\beta\in\Omega^1(M)$ with
$\theta=\beta\wedge\omega$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A smooth manifold $M$ with a nowhere-vanishing smooth one-form $\omega$, a one-form $\alpha$ with $\alpha\wedge\omega=0$, and a two-form $\theta$ with $\theta\wedge\omega=0$.

[F1] The graded vector space $\Omega^*(M)$ with the wedge product is an associative graded-commutative algebra. ([[prop-differential-forms-form-a-graded-commutative-algebra]]).

[F2] If $(f_i)$ are smooth functions on the members of an open cover and $(\phi_i)$ is a smooth partition of unity subordinate to that cover, then $F(p)=\sum_i\phi_i(p)f_i(p)$ is a smooth function on $M$. ([[thm-smooth-functions-defined-locally-can-be-glued-by-a-partition-of-unity]]).

[F3] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, every smooth-manifold open cover admits a subordinate smooth partition of unity ([[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]], [[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds-with-boundary]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $p\in M$ and choose $v\in T_pM$ with $\omega_p(v)\neq0$, which exists because $\omega$ is nowhere vanishing; evaluating the two-form $\alpha\wedge\omega$ on a pair $(u,v)$ and using its definition for a one-form times a one-form gives $0=(\alpha\wedge\omega)(u,v)=\alpha_p(u)\omega_p(v)-\alpha_p(v)\omega_p(u)$ for every $u$, so $\alpha_p(u)=(\alpha_p(v)/\omega_p(v))\omega_p(u)$; the quotient $f(p):=\alpha_p(v)/\omega_p(v)$ is independent of the choice of $v$ because it equals the value of the unique scalar with $\alpha_p=f(p)\omega_p$, and such a scalar is unique as $\omega_p\neq0$. [given, algebra]

2.1 To see that $f$ is smooth, fix a chart domain $U$ with coordinates $x_1,\dots,x_m$ and write $\omega=\sum_i\omega_i\,dx_i$, $\alpha=\sum_j a_j\,dx_j$ with smooth coefficient functions; by [F1] the wedge product expands as $\alpha\wedge\omega=\sum_{j<k}(a_j\omega_k-a_k\omega_j)\,dx_j\wedge dx_k$, and the $dx_j\wedge dx_k$ are linearly independent over the coefficient functions, so $a_j\omega_k=a_k\omega_j$ on $U$; shrinking about any point at which some $\omega_i\neq0$, one gets $a_j=a_i\omega_j/\omega_i$ for all $j$, hence $\alpha=(a_i/\omega_i)\omega$ there with smooth quotient $a_i/\omega_i$; comparing with step 1.1 shows $f=a_i/\omega_i$ on that smaller domain, and since smoothness is local $f$ is smooth on all of $M$, giving (i). [F1, step 1.1]

2.2 For (ii), fix a chart domain $U$ with coordinates as above and write $\theta=\sum_{i<j}c_{ij}\,dx_i\wedge dx_j$ with $c_{ji}:=-c_{ij}$; by [F1] the wedge $\theta\wedge\omega$ has coefficient $c_{ij}\omega_k-c_{ik}\omega_j+c_{jk}\omega_i$ on $dx_i\wedge dx_j\wedge dx_k$ for each triple $i<j<k$, so $\theta\wedge\omega=0$ gives those three-term identities; shrinking to a domain on which a fixed coefficient $\omega_{i_0}$ is nowhere zero, define $b_i:=c_{ii_0}/\omega_{i_0}$ for $i\neq i_0$ and $b_{i_0}:=0$, and let $\beta=\sum_i b_i\,dx_i$; substituting these definitions into the three-term identities in each of the three index orders, and using $c_{ij}=-c_{ji}$, gives $c_{ij}=b_i\omega_j-b_j\omega_i$ for every pair $\{i,j\}$, hence $\theta=\beta\wedge\omega$ on $U$ with smooth $\beta$. [F1, step 1.1]

3.1 Cover $M$ by such chart domains $U$ with local solutions $\beta_U$, and use [F3] to obtain a smooth partition of unity $(\phi_U)$ subordinate to the cover; for overlapping domains, $(\beta_U-\beta_V)\wedge\omega=0$, so by (i) there is a smooth function $f_{UV}$ with $\beta_U-\beta_V=f_{UV}\omega$ on the overlap, and the local forms $\phi_U\beta_U$, extended by zero, satisfy $\sum_U\phi_U\beta_U\wedge\omega=\sum_U\phi_U\theta=\theta$; thus $\beta:=\sum_U\phi_U\beta_U$ is a globally defined smooth one-form by [F2] with $\theta=\beta\wedge\omega$, which is (ii). [F2, F3, step 2.2]

4.1 Part (i) follows from steps 1.1 and 2.1, and part (ii) from step 3.1. Countable choice is used for the subordinate partition in [F3]; the local coefficients are explicit formulas in each chart. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
