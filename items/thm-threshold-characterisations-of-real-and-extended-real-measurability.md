## Statement

Let $(X,\mathcal{A})$ be a measurable space and let
$f : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$. The following are equivalent:

1. $f$ is measurable;
2. $\{x : f(x) > a\} \in \mathcal{A}$ for every real $a$;
3. $\{x : f(x) \ge a\} \in \mathcal{A}$ for every real $a$;
4. $\{x : f(x) < a\} \in \mathcal{A}$ for every real $a$;
5. $\{x : f(x) \le a\} \in \mathcal{A}$ for every real $a$.

Moreover, in any one of conditions 2 through 5 it is enough to test only
rational thresholds $a \in \mathbb{Q}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A measurable space $(X,\mathcal{A})$ and a function $f : X \to \overline{\mathbb{R}}$.

[L1] The Borel sigma-algebra on $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ is generated by the rays $(a,+\infty]$ with $a \in \mathbb{R}$. ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra-on-the-extended-real-line]])

[L2] A generating family on the codomain suffices to test measurability. ([[thm-generating-family-criterion-for-measurable-functions]])

[L3] Between any two distinct real numbers there lies a rational number. ([[lem-rat-embeds-dense]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], the threshold set $\{x:f(x)>a\}$ is the preimage [L1, L2] $f^{-1}((a,+\infty])$. Therefore [L2] gives the equivalence of condition 1 and condition 2. [L1, L2]

1.2 Suppose the sets $\{f>q\}$ are measurable for every rational $q$. For a [L3, algebra] real $a$,

$$\{f \le a\} = X \setminus \{f > a\},\qquad \{f < a\} = X \setminus \{f \ge a\}.$$

So conditions 2 and 5 are equivalent, and conditions 3 and 4 are equivalent. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 For every real $a$, [step 1.1, algebra]

$$\{f \ge a\} = \bigcap_{n=1}^\infty \{f > a-1/n\},\qquad \{f > a\} = \bigcup_{n=1}^\infty \{f \ge a+1/n\},$$

so conditions 2 and 3 are equivalent. Combining this with steps 1.1 and 1.2 shows that conditions 1 through 5 are all equivalent. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 For every real $a$, [step 1.1, algebra]

$$\{f>a\} = \bigcup_{q \in \mathbb{Q},\, q>a} \{f>q\}.$$

Indeed, $f(x)>q>a$ implies $f(x)>a$, and if $f(x)>a$ then [L3] gives a rational $q$ with $a<q<f(x)$ unless $f(x)=+\infty$, in which case any rational $q>a$ works. Thus the real-threshold version of condition 2 follows from the rational one. The converse is immediate, so in condition 2 it is enough to test only rational thresholds. [L3, algebra]

3.1 The equivalences from steps 2.1 and 2.2 transfer the rational-threshold [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.2] reduction of step 1.2 to conditions 3 through 5. Therefore in any one of conditions 2 through 5 it is enough to test only rational thresholds. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.2] ∎
