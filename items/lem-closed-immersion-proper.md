## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Every closed immersion of schemes is finite, hence
proper; the empty closed immersion is included, no Noetherian, reducedness or
nonemptiness hypothesis is used, and the Axiom of Choice enters through the
affine quotient structure of closed immersions and the properness of finite
morphisms.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a closed immersion $i:Z\to Y$.

[F1] A morphism is a **closed immersion** when its underlying map is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset of its target and the structure-sheaf map is surjective. ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]])

[F2] Assume AC. For a closed immersion $i:Z\to Y$ and every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq Y$ there is a unique ideal $I\subseteq A$ with $i^{-1}(U)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$ over $U$; conversely every quotient map $A\to A/I$ induces a closed immersion, every base change of a closed immersion is a closed immersion, and the empty subscheme of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ corresponds to $I=A$. ([[lem-closed-immersion-affine-quotient-and-base-change]])

[F3] A morphism $f:X\to S$ is **finite** when for every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the inverse image is affine, $f^{-1}(U)=\operatorname{Spec}B$, and $B$ is module-finite over $A$; the zero ring is allowed. ([[def-finite-morphism-schemes]])

[F4] A module is **cyclic** when it is generated by one element and **finitely generated** when it is generated by a finite subset; the zero module is generated by the empty family. ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]])

[F5] An $R$-algebra $A$ is module-finite over $R$ when $A$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module. ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]])

[F6] Assume AC. Every finite morphism of schemes is proper. ([[cor-finite-morphism-proper]])

[F7] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: on each affine target the closed immersion is a quotient map, whose coordinate ring is a cyclic module, hence module-finite; the morphism is therefore finite, and finite morphisms are proper.

1.1 Let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be an affine open of $Y$. By [F1] and [F2] the restriction of $i$ over $U$ is a closed immersion with $i^{-1}(U)\cong\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$ over $U$ for the unique ideal $I\subseteq A$; in particular the inverse image is affine. [F1, F2]

1.2 The quotient $A/I$ is cyclic as an $A$-module: the class $1+I$ generates it, because every $a+I=a\cdot(1+I)$ lies in the submodule generated by $1+I$, while that submodule always lies in $A/I$; if $I=A$ then $A/I=0$ is generated by the empty family. Hence $A/I$ is finitely generated, equivalently module-finite, over $A$ by [F4] and [F5]. [F4, F5]

2.1 Steps 1.1 and 1.2 verify the condition of [F3] on every affine open $U$ of $Y$: the inverse image is affine, $i^{-1}(U)=\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$, and its coordinate algebra is module-finite over the coordinate algebra of $U$. Therefore the closed immersion $i$ is finite. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 By [F6] the finite morphism $i$ is proper. The empty closed immersion is included: over each affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ it corresponds by [F2] to the ideal $I=A$, so its inverse image is $\operatorname{Spec}0$, an affine chart whose coordinate ring is the zero ring and is module-finite by [F3], and the same argument applies to it. The Axiom of Choice [F7] is assumed and is used only through [F2] and [F6], the two AC-qualified suppliers; no further selection occurs, and no Noetherian, reducedness or nonemptiness hypothesis is used. [F2, F3, F6, F7, step 2.1] ∎
