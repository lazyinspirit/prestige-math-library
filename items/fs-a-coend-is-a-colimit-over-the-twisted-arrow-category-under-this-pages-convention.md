## Statement

**False claim:** with $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ and the projection $\pi$
as fixed on this page ([[def-twisted-arrow-category]]), the coend of a functor
$T:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C\to\mathcal D$ is the colimit over
$\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ of the very diagram $T\pi$ whose limit is the
end ([[def-end-and-coend]], [[def-limit-and-colimit-of-a-diagram]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The walking arrow $\mathcal C$, with objects $0$ and $1$ and one non-identity morphism $u:0\to1$, and its hom-bifunctor as the integrand.

[F6] Sets as objects and functions as morphisms form a large locally small category $\mathbf{Set}$ ([[prop-sets-and-functions-form-category-set]]).

[F4] The hom-assignment sends $(a,b)$ to $\mathcal C(a,b)$, and a morphism of the product category consisting of $h:a'\to a$ and $u:b\to b'$ acts by $\mathcal C(h,u):\mathcal C(a,b)\longrightarrow\mathcal C(a',b'),\qquad f\longmapsto u\circ f\circ h$ ([[def-hom-functors-and-hom-bifunctor]]).

[L3] For every locally small category $\mathcal C$, the hom-assignment $\mathcal C(-,-):\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C\to\mathbf{Set}$ is a functor ([[thm-hom-assignment-is-a-bifunctor]]).

[F1] The objects of $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ are the morphisms of $\mathcal C$, a morphism $f\to g$ is a pair $(a,b)$ with $bfa=g$, and $\pi$ sends $f:c\to c'$ to $(c,c')$ ([[def-twisted-arrow-category]]).

[F5] A colimit is an initial cocone: for every cocone $(X,\xi)$ there exists a unique morphism $u:Q\to X$ such that $u\rho_j=\xi_j$ for every $j$; a limit is a terminal cone, and Explicitly, for every cone $(X,\xi)$ there exists a unique morphism $u:X\to L$ such that $\lambda_j u=\xi_j$ for every $j$. ([[def-limit-and-colimit-of-a-diagram]]).

[F2] An end of $T$ is a terminal object of the category of wedges over $T$ and a coend an initial object of the category of cowedges under $T$; in short, an end is a terminal wedge and a coend an initial cowedge ([[def-end-and-coend]]).

[F3] The opposite category has the same objects and reverses every morphism: $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}(A,B)=\mathcal C(B,A)$ ([[def-opposite-category]]).

[L1] The wedges over $T$ are the cones over $T\pi$, so an end is the limit over the twisted arrow category; the coend is the colimit over $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)^{\mathrm{op}}$ of the integrand read with the domain and codomain of each arrow interchanged ([[thm-an-end-is-a-limit-over-the-twisted-arrow-category]]).

[L2] For small $\mathcal C$ and a set-valued integrand, the coend is the disjoint union of the diagonal values modulo the dinaturality relation, generated by the pairs $(c,T(f,1_c)(x))$ and $(c',T(1_{c'},f)(x))$ for $f:c\to c'$ and $x\in T(c',c)$ ([[thm-a-set-valued-coend-is-a-quotient-of-a-coproduct-by-the-dinaturality-relation]]).

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Take $\mathcal C$ to be the walking arrow and $T=\mathcal C(-,-)$, a functor into $\mathbf{Set}$ by [L3]. Its values are $\mathcal C(0,0)=\{1_0\}$, $\mathcal C(1,1)=\{1_1\}$, $\mathcal C(0,1)=\{u\}$ and $\mathcal C(1,0)=\varnothing$. By [F1] the objects of $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ are $1_0$, $1_1$ and $u$; a morphism $1_0\to u$ is the pair $(1_0,u)$ and a morphism $1_1\to u$ is the pair $(u,1_1)$, while a morphism out of $u$ would need a component in $\mathcal C(1,0)$ and there is none. So $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ is a cospan, and $T\pi$ takes the value $\{1_0\}$ at $1_0$, $\{1_1\}$ at $1_1$ and $\{u\}$ at $u$. [F1, F4, F6, L3, given, construct]

2.1 The colimit of $T\pi$ over $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ has one element. A cocone under a cospan is determined by its component at the codomain of the two arrows, the other two components being that one precomposed with the transition maps; so a cocone with apex $X$ is exactly a function $\{u\}\to X$, and by [F5] the initial such is $\{u\}$ itself. [F5, step 1.1]

2.2 The coend has two elements. By [L2] it is the quotient of $\{1_0\}\sqcup\{1_1\}$ by the relation generated by the pairs indexed by a morphism $f:c\to c'$ and an element $x$ of $T(c',c)$; the only non-identity morphism is $u:0\to1$, and $T(1,0)=\mathcal C(1,0)$ is empty, so it contributes no generating pair, while identity morphisms contribute only reflexive pairs. The relation is therefore equality and the coend is the two-element set. [F2, F4, L2, step 1.1]

3.1 One element is not two, so the coend is not the colimit of $T\pi$ over $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)$ and the claim is false. The correct description is [L1]: the coend is the colimit over $\operatorname{Tw}(\mathcal C)^{\mathrm{op}}$, by [F3] the same objects with every arrow reversed, of the functor sending $f:c\to c'$ to $T(c',c)$ — domain and codomain interchanged. On this witness that diagram takes the values $\{1_0\}$, $\{1_1\}$ and $\varnothing$, its index category is a span, and its colimit is the two-element set, as it must be. [F2, F3, L1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎

## Remarks

Two changes separate the correct description from the false one, and taking only one of them is what the false claim does. The index category must be reversed **and** the integrand must be reindexed; on this witness the reindexing is what moves the empty set from the $(1,0)$ position, where it contributed nothing, to the apex of the diagram, where it stops the two other values from being identified.

The witness is as small as it can be. Any category in which every hom-set is nonempty in both directions would hide the failure, because the two diagrams would then have the same shape of transition maps; the walking arrow is chosen precisely because $\mathcal C(1,0)$ is empty.
