## Definition

Let $S$ be a nonempty finite set, let $A(s)$ be a nonempty finite set for each $s\in S$, and let $D=\{(s,a):s\in S,\ a\in A(s)\}$. Here **finite** means bijective with a natural number. Put $S_0=|S|$ and $A_0=\max_{s\in S}|A(s)|$. Fix a discount $0\le\gamma<1$. Give $S$ and $D$ their discrete sigma-algebras and give $[0,1]$ its Borel sigma-algebra. A **bounded-reward finite discounted Markov decision process** is a probability kernel $K$ from $D$ to $S\times[0,1]$. At pair $(s,a)$, $K(\cdot\mid s,a)$ is the joint law of the next state and the reward; these two coordinates need not be independent. Define the state-transition probabilities and expected rewards by

$$P(s'\mid s,a)=K(\{s'\}\times[0,1]\mid s,a),\qquad r(s,a)=\int_{S\times[0,1]}\rho\,K(ds',d\rho\mid s,a).$$

A **history-dependent behavior** is a sequence of probability kernels from observed-history spaces $H_t$ to the finite action alphabet $\mathcal A=\bigcup_{s\in S}A(s)$, assigning probability zero to actions outside $A(s_t)$ at every history ending in state $s_t$. Here $H_t$ consists of histories $(s_0,a_0,R_1,s_1,\ldots,a_{t-1},R_t,s_t)$, with discrete sigma-algebras on finite state/action coordinates and the Borel sigma-algebra on each reward coordinate; it has the resulting finite-product sigma-algebra. A **stationary Markov policy** assigns one distribution on $A(s)$ to each state, independently of earlier history. During interaction, after choosing $a_t$ in state $s_t$, the next pair $(s_{t+1},R_{t+1})$ is drawn from $K(\cdot\mid s_t,a_t)$.

The reward-coordinate projection is measurable: the inverse image of a Borel set $B\subseteq[0,1]$ is the measurable rectangle $S\times B$. Since $0\le\rho\le1$, [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral|monotonicity of the nonnegative integral]] gives $0\le\int\rho\,dK\le\int1\,dK=K(S\times[0,1])=1$.
