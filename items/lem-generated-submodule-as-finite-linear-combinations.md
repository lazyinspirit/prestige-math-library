## Statement

Let $R$ be a ring, let $M$ be a left $R$-module and let $S\subseteq M$. Then the
submodule generated by $S$ ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]])
is the set of finite $R$-linear combinations of elements of $S$:

$$\langle S\rangle_R\;=\;\Big\{\,\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i\;:\;k\in\mathbb N,\ r_1,\ldots,r_k\in R,\ s_1,\ldots,s_k\in S\,\Big\},$$

where the term with $k=0$ is the empty sum $0_M$. In particular
$\langle\varnothing\rangle_R=0$. Commutativity of $R$ is not used, and the
elements $s_1,\ldots,s_k$ are not required to be distinct.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and a subset $S\subseteq M$. Write $L$ for the set displayed in the Statement.

[L1] The submodule generated by $S$ is $\langle S\rangle_R:=\bigcap\{N\le M:S\subseteq N\}$, the family being nonempty because $M\le M$; thus $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $S$ ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[L2] For a left $R$-module $M$, a nonempty subset $N\subseteq M$ is a submodule if and only if $ru+v\in N$ for all $r\in R$ and $u,v\in N$ ([[lem-submodule-criterion-sums-and-intersections]]).

[L3] A left $R$-module is an abelian group $(M,+,0_M)$ with an action satisfying $r(m+n)=rm+rn$, $(r+s)m=rm+sm$, $(rs)m=r(sm)$ and $1_Rm=m$ ([[def-left-and-right-modules]]).

[L4] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $L$ be the set of all sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_1,\ldots,r_k\in R$ and $s_1,\ldots,s_k\in S$, the value at $k=0$ being the empty sum $0_M$; in particular $0_M\in L$, so $L$ is nonempty even when $S$ is empty. [L1, given]

2.1 Every $s\in S$ lies in $L$, being the one-term sum $1_Rs$, which equals $s$ by the unitality axiom of a module; hence $S\subseteq L$. [L3, step 1.1, given]

2.2 $L$ is a submodule of $M$: it is nonempty, and for $r\in R$ and $u=\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$, $v=\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_ju_j$ in $L$ the element $ru+v$ is the sum $\sum_{i=1}^{k}(rr_i)s_i+\sum_{j=1}^{m}t_ju_j$, again a finite $R$-linear combination of elements of $S$ with $k+m$ terms, so $ru+v\in L$ and the submodule criterion applies. [L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 Every submodule $N\le M$ with $S\subseteq N$ contains $L$: each $s_i$ lies in $N$, so repeated use of the submodule criterion [L2] puts every $r_is_i$ and every finite sum of those elements in $N$; the case $k=0$ gives $0_M\in N$. [L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 $\langle S\rangle_R=L$: by steps 2.1 and 2.2 the set $L$ is one of the submodules over which the intersection defining $\langle S\rangle_R$ runs, so $\langle S\rangle_R\subseteq L$; and $\langle S\rangle_R$ is itself a submodule containing $S$, so step 2.3 applied to it gives $L\subseteq\langle S\rangle_R$. [L1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3]

4.1 Two consequences follow by reading the description at its extreme cases. Taking $S=\varnothing$ leaves only the empty sum, so $\langle\varnothing\rangle_R=0$. Taking $M$ to be the regular module over a commutative $R$, where the submodules are the ideals, the description becomes the finite-sum description of the ideal generated by $S$, so the two agree and this lemma extends that one from ideals to modules rather than competing with it. [L4, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **Unitality is where the containment $S\subseteq\langle S\rangle_R$ comes from.** Step 2.1 writes $s=1_Rs$, which is available because [[def-left-and-right-modules]] builds $1_Rm=m$ into the definition of a module. Over a non-unital action the set of finite $R$-linear combinations need not contain $S$, and then it is not the generated submodule.

- **No finiteness is assumed of $S$.** Each element of $\langle S\rangle_R$ carries its own finite list of coefficients and elements; different elements may use different lists, and no bound on the length is claimed.
