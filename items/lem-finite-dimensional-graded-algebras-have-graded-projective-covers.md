## Statement

Let $A$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathbb Z$-graded $k$-algebra over a field,
and let $X$ be a finite-dimensional graded left $A$-module. There is a
degree-zero epimorphism $p\colon P\twoheadrightarrow X$ in
$\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]])
with $P$ finite graded projective and
$K:=\ker p$ satisfying

$$ N+K=P\quad\Longrightarrow\quad N=P $$

for every graded submodule $N\le P$. We call such a map a **finite graded
projective cover**; superfluity of its kernel is tested among graded
submodules, as appropriate in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$
([[def-essential-epimorphism-and-projective-cover]]). Any two finite graded
projective covers of $X$ are isomorphic over $X$: if $q\colon Q\twoheadrightarrow
X$ is another, there is a degree-zero isomorphism $\phi\colon P\to Q$ with
$q\phi=p$ ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]). No positivity
assumption on the grading of $A$ is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional unital associative $\mathbb Z$-graded $k$-algebra $A$ over a field and a finite-dimensional graded left $A$-module $X$. The grading is arbitrary; no lower bound on its support is assumed. The construction uses only finite data and no axiom of choice.

**Source relation:** Webb's results give projective-cover existence and uniqueness for ordinary finite-dimensional modules. The construction here is adapted to $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$, with finite graded projectivity and superfluity among graded submodules proved directly. Kleshchev supplies only grading and shift conventions.

[L1] The internal shift is $A\{r\}_d=A_{d-r}$; multiplication by a homogeneous generator gives a degree-zero map from the matching shift ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

[L2] In $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$, kernels, images, cokernels and exactness are computed degreewise ([[lem-graded-module-kernels-cokernels-and-biproducts-are-degreewise]]).

[L3] Finite graded projectives are exactly degree-zero direct summands of finite sums of shifts of $A$, and every such finite sum is projective ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[L4] A graded projective object lifts degree-zero maps through degree-zero epimorphisms ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[L5] Finite graded projectives are finitely generated by homogeneous elements ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[L6] The cited terminology defines an ordinary projective cover using a superfluous kernel; this item explicitly adopts the corresponding criterion relative to $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$, testing only graded submodules ([[def-essential-epimorphism-and-projective-cover]]).

[L7] A degree-zero endomorphism of a finite-dimensional graded module has, for some $n\ge1$, the graded Fitting decomposition $M=\ker(f^n)\oplus\operatorname{im}(f^n)$ ([[lem-graded-fitting-decomposition-preserves-homogeneous-summands]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose a finite $k$-basis of $X$. Each basis vector has finitely many homogeneous components, and the collection of these components is a finite homogeneous generating family $x_1,\ldots,x_m$. If $X=0$, take $m=0$. [L1, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 Write $d_j=\deg(x_j)$ and set $E:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^m A\{d_j\}$. The map $\epsilon\colon E\to X$ given on the $j$th shifted generator by $1_j\mapsto x_j$ and extended by $a1_j\mapsto ax_j$ is degree-zero by [L1], and is surjective by step 1.1. It is an epimorphism in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ by [L2]. The empty case gives $E=X=0$. [L1, L2, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The family of graded direct summands $Q$ of $E$ for which $\epsilon|_Q\colon Q\to X$ is surjective is nonempty because it contains $E$. Their dimensions lie in the finite set $\{0,1,\ldots,\dim_k E\}$, so choose such a $P$ of minimum dimension and put $p:=\epsilon|_P$. By [L3], $P$ is finite graded projective. It is finite-dimensional because it is a submodule of the finite-dimensional $E$. [L3, step 2.1, choose]

4.1 Let $K=\ker p$ and let $N\le P$ be a graded submodule with $N+K=P$. Then $p|_N\colon N\to X$ is surjective, hence an epimorphism by [L2]. Since $P$ is graded projective, [L4] lifts $p$ through this map to a degree-zero $h\colon P\to N$ with $(p|_N)h=p$. After inclusion $N\hookrightarrow P$, this gives a degree-zero endomorphism $f$ of $P$ with $pf=p$, and therefore $pf^n=p$ for every $n\ge1$. [L2, L4, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Apply [L7] to $f$: for some $n\ge1$, $P=\ker(f^n)\oplus\operatorname{im}(f^n)$ by graded submodules. Since $pf^n=p$, the restriction of $p$ to $\operatorname{im}(f^n)$ is still surjective. This image is a graded direct summand of $P$, hence of $E$, so it is one of the candidates in step 3.1. Minimality gives $\dim_kP\le\dim_k\operatorname{im}(f^n)$; the reverse inequality follows from $\operatorname{im}(f^n)\subseteq P$, so $\operatorname{im}(f^n)=P$. As $\operatorname{im}(f^n)\subseteq\operatorname{im}(f)\subseteq N$, it follows that $N=P$. Thus $K$ is superfluous among graded submodules and, by the category-relative definition in the Statement (using the terminology of [L6]), $p$ is a finite graded projective cover. [L2, L6, L7, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Let $p\colon P\to X$ and $q\colon Q\to X$ be finite graded projective covers. By projectivity, there are degree-zero maps $f\colon P\to Q$ and $g\colon Q\to P$ with $qf=p$ and $pg=q$. Then $p(gf)=p$, so $P=gf(P)+\ker p$. The image $gf(P)$ is graded by [L2]; superfluity of $\ker p$ gives $gf(P)=P$. Similarly, $fg(Q)=Q$. [L2, L4, L6, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 By [L5] the sources $P,Q$ are finitely generated by homogeneous elements; since $A$ is finite-dimensional, both are finite-dimensional over $k$. The surjective endomorphisms $gf$ and $fg$ from step 6.1 are therefore bijective. It follows that $f$ is injective from injectivity of $gf$ and surjective from surjectivity of $fg$. The inverse is $A$-linear; it preserves degrees because a homogeneous element's preimage can have no nonzero components in other degrees under an injective degree-zero map. Hence $f$ is a degree-zero isomorphism and $qf=p$, proving uniqueness over $X$. [L5, step 6.1, given, algebra] ∎
