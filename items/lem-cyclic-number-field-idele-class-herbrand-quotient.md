## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Dependent Choice. Let $L/K$ be a cyclic extension of number fields of degree $n$, let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, and let $C_L=\mathbb A_L^\times/L^\times$. Its Tate cohomology groups in degrees $0$ and $-1$ are finite and satisfy

$$h_G(C_L):=\frac{|\widehat H^0(G,C_L)|}{|\widehat H^{-1}(G,C_L)|}=n.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and DC; a cyclic extension $L/K$ of number fields of degree $n$.

[A1] For a finite cyclic local extension, the local norm quotient has order the local degree, by the cyclic invariant/inflation proof; local Hilbert 90 gives vanishing of the negative first Tate group of the multiplicative group. This uses AC/DC and does not assume the still-held general local Artin theorem. [[lem-cyclic-local-norm-index-from-class-formation]] [[thm-hilbert-90-for-finite-galois-extension]]

[F1] For a cyclic finite group, Herbrand quotients are multiplicative in short exact sequences whenever the relevant Tate groups are finite. [[def-herbrand-quotient-for-finite-cyclic-group-modules]] [[lem-herbrand-quotient-multiplicativity-in-exact-sequences]]

[F2] For a finite cyclic local extension the unit group has Herbrand quotient $1$. In the unramified case the two Tate groups both vanish: the norm on units is onto and positive cohomology of units vanishes. [[thm-local-unit-herbrand-quotient-one]] [[lem-unramified-local-unit-norms-and-cohomology]]

[F3] The logarithmic image of the ordinary unit group is a full lattice in the product-formula hyperplane. [[thm-logarithmic-unit-image-is-a-full-lattice]] [[def-logarithmic-unit-embedding]]

[F4] The $S$-unit group is finitely generated with rank $r_1+r_2-1+|S_f|$ and torsion subgroup the roots of unity. [[thm-s-unit-theorem]]

[F5] The ideal class group is finite, so for a finite set $S_f$ of primes the valuation image of $S$-units contains the subgroup generated by $h e_v$, where $h$ is the class-group exponent. [[thm-finiteness-of-the-number-field-class-group]] [[def-s-integers-and-s-units-of-a-number-field]]

[F6] The number-field product formula places the logarithmic image in the hyperplane where the weighted coordinate sum is zero. [[thm-product-formula-for-number-fields]]

[F7] A discrete subgroup of a finite-dimensional real vector space is a lattice in its real span. [[lem-discrete-subgroups-of-real-vector-spaces-are-lattices]]

[F8] Ordinary Shapiro gives $H^j(G,\operatorname{Coind}_D^G M)=H^j(D,M)$ for $j\ge0$ under AC. For finite cyclic groups the explicit periodic resolution identifies $H^1=\ker N/(\sigma-1)M=\widehat H^{-1}$ and $H^2=M^G/NM=\widehat H^0$. Applying ordinary Shapiro in degrees $1,2$ therefore compares exactly the two Herbrand groups when $G$ and $D$ are cyclic. Finite-index induction and coinduction are isomorphic, since their respective direct sum and product over finitely many cosets coincide with the same action. [[thm-shapiro-lemma-for-group-cohomology]] [[lem-cyclic-group-cohomology-norm-and-inflation]]

[F9] The idèle class group is the quotient of the restricted product of local multiplicative groups by the diagonal field multiplicative group. [[def-idele-group-of-a-global-field]] [[def-idele-class-group-of-a-global-field]]

[F10] At an archimedean place the local norm quotient has order the local degree: it is trivial for $\mathbb C/\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb R/\mathbb R$, and $\mathbb R^\times/\mathbb R_{>0}$ for $\mathbb C/\mathbb R$. [[thm-archimedean-local-reciprocity]]

[A2] AC and DC are assumed through the local reciprocity and cohomology interfaces; the finite lattice and cohomology calculations below use no additional choice principle. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Choose a finite set $S$ of places of $K$ containing all archimedean and ramified places and enough finite primes to generate the ideal class group of $L$. Let $T$ be the places of $L$ above $S$, and let $I_{L,T}$ be the restricted ideles with arbitrary components at $T$ and unit components outside $T$. Since the primes in $T$ generate the class group, every idele class has a representative in $I_{L,T}$, and the exact sequence $1\to U_{L,T}\to I_{L,T}\to C_L\to1$ has $U_{L,T}=L^\times\cap I_{L,T}$, the $T$-unit group. [F4, F5, F9, given, construct]

1.2 Define the $S$-logarithm by $$u\longmapsto ((\log|\sigma(u)|)_\sigma,(-v_w(u)\log Nw)_{w\in T_f}),$$ using one real embedding and one from each complex pair, with complex coordinates doubled. The product formula [F6] places its image in the hyperplane $H_T$ whose weighted coordinate sum is zero. An element of its kernel has all finite valuations zero, hence is an ordinary unit. On ordinary units the map is the ordinary logarithmic embedding, whose domain has rank $r_1(L)+r_2(L)-1$ by [F4] and whose image is a full lattice of that rank by [F3]. Thus its kernel has rank zero and is finite. Every root of unity is in this kernel, and every finite-order unit is a root of unity, so the torsion assertion in [F4] identifies the kernel exactly. For each finite $w\in T_f$, let $h$ be the exponent of the finite ideal class group [F5]; choose $\pi_w$ with $(\pi_w)=w^h$. Its finite logarithm coordinates give $h\log(Nw)e_w$, so the projection of the full image to finite coordinates is a lattice. The kernel of that projection is the ordinary-unit logarithm lattice. A convergent sequence in the full image has eventually constant finite coordinates, after which discreteness of the kernel makes it eventually constant. The image is discrete. Its rank, by [F4], is $r_1(L)+r_2(L)-1+|T_f|=\dim H_T$, so [F7] makes it a full lattice in $H_T$. [A2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 The group $G$ is cyclic and every decomposition subgroup $G_v$ is cyclic. For each orbit of places use the finite-index induced/coinduced identification in [F8], then apply ordinary Shapiro in degrees $1,2$. The explicit cyclic formulas in [F8] turn these two ordinary groups into precisely $\widehat H^{-1}$ and $\widehat H^0$ on both sides; no negative-degree form of Shapiro is being assumed. For each finite $v\in S$, the local multiplicative factor is induced from the decomposition group $G_v$. Its negative first Tate group is zero by local Hilbert 90, and its zeroth group has order $[L_w:K_v]$ by [A1]. For each archimedean $v\in S$, the quotient order is [F10], and local Hilbert 90 again gives vanishing of the negative first Tate group. For finite $v\notin S$, both Tate groups of the local unit factor vanish by [F2]. Finite-group cochains commute with arbitrary products, since each cochain group is a finite power of its coefficient group; cocycle kernels are coordinatewise and AC selects coordinatewise preimages of coboundaries. Thus the entire product outside $S$ has zero Tate groups. There are only finitely many remaining factors, all with finite Tate groups by the local calculations. Shapiro and multiplicativity therefore give $h_G(I_{L,T})=\prod_{v\in S}[L_w:K_v]$. [A1, F2, F8, F10, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Add the invariant line $\mathbb Z\mathbf1$ to the logarithmic lattice to obtain a full $G$-stable lattice $\Lambda$ in $\mathbb R^T$; its intersection with $H_T$ is zero because the positive weighted coordinate sum defining $H_T$ is nonzero on $\mathbf1$. We must compare rational representations, not intersect arbitrary real lattices. Choose a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\Lambda$ and the standard basis of $\mathbb Q^T$. The intertwiner equations $A_gX=XP_g$, $g\in G$, have rational coefficients. Gaussian elimination over $\mathbb Q$ gives a rational basis $X_1,\ldots,X_s$ for their solution space, and the same row reduction over $\mathbb R$ shows that all real solutions are their real linear combinations. Since $\Lambda$ spans the real permutation representation, there is an invertible real solution; hence $\det(\sum t_iX_i)$ is a nonzero polynomial over $\mathbb Q$. A nonzero polynomial over an infinite field has a nonvanishing value in that field: induct on its variables, first specialize so one nonzero coefficient remains nonzero and then avoid the finitely many roots of the remaining one-variable polynomial. Thus an invertible rational intertwiner exists. It gives a $\mathbb QG$-isomorphism $\mathbb Q^T\to\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Q$. Transport the standard lattice to a $G$-stable lattice $M$ in $\Lambda\otimes\mathbb Q$. In finite bases, clearing the denominators of this isomorphism and its inverse gives a positive integer $d$ with $dM\subseteq\Lambda$ and $d\Lambda\subseteq M$. Consequently $J=M\cap\Lambda$ has finite index in both. By [F1] and quotient one for finite modules, $h_G(\Lambda)=h_G(M)=h_G(\mathbb Z^T)$. For the permutation lattice $\mathbb Z^T$, its orbit at $v$ is the induced trivial $\mathbb Z$-module from $G_v$. Ordinary Shapiro in degrees $1,2$ and the cyclic formulas of [F8] give $H^1(G_v,\mathbb Z)=0$ and $H^2(G_v,\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z/|G_v|\mathbb Z$. Consequently its Herbrand quotient is $\prod_{v\in S}|G_v|=\prod_{v\in S}[L_w:K_v]$. The trivial quotient $\Lambda/\log U_{L,T}\cong\mathbb Z$ has Herbrand quotient $n$, so multiplicativity gives $h_G(U_{L,T})=\prod_{v\in S}[L_w:K_v]/n$; the finite torsion kernel has quotient one. All group cohomology and local inputs retain [A2]. [F1, F8, step 1.2, algebra, A2]

3.1 Applying [F1] to the exact sequence in step 1.1 gives $h_G(C_L)=h_G(I_{L,T})/h_G(U_{L,T})=n$ by steps 2.1 and 2.2. The Tate groups are finite because the local groups and the finite-rank lattice cohomology in the construction are finite. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
