## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_3$ with simple roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, $W=S_3=\{e,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,w_0\}$, and let $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ be dominant integral. The BGG complex has

$$C_0=M(\lambda),\quad C_1=M(s_1\circ\lambda)\oplus M(s_2\circ\lambda),\quad C_2=M(s_1s_2\circ\lambda)\oplus M(s_2s_1\circ\lambda),\quad C_3=M(w_0\circ\lambda),$$

with $d_1$ given by the two embeddings $M(s_i\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$, $d_2$ given by the four cover embeddings ($s_1s_2\rhd s_1$, $s_1s_2\rhd s_2$, $s_2s_1\rhd s_2$, $s_2s_1\rhd s_1$) with compatible signs, and $d_3$ given by the two cover embeddings $w_0\rhd s_1s_2$ and $w_0\rhd s_2s_1$ with compatible signs. All Bruhat intervals of rank two are diamonds, so $d^2=0$ holds by the square condition, and the complex is exact by [[thm-bgg-resolution-of-a-finite-dimensional-simple-module]]. The weights $w\circ\lambda=w(\lambda+\rho)-\rho$ are pairwise distinct and the listed embeddings are the canonical submodules inside $M(\lambda)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the A2 root system with simple reflections $s_1,s_2$ and longest element $w_0=s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$, a dominant integral weight $\lambda$, and the BGG complex $C_\bullet(\lambda)$.

[F1] For type A2 the Weyl group is $W=S_3=\{e,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,w_0\}$ with $w_0=s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$ and lengths $0,1,1,2,2,3$. The length-adjacent pairs are $s_1\rhd e$, $s_2\rhd e$, $s_1s_2\rhd s_1$, $s_1s_2\rhd s_2$, $s_2s_1\rhd s_2$, $s_2s_1\rhd s_1$, $w_0\rhd s_1s_2$, $w_0\rhd s_2s_1$; each longer element has a reduced expression containing the shorter one as a subword ($w_0=s_1s_2s_1$ contains $s_1s_2$ in positions $1,2$ and $s_2s_1$ in positions $2,3$, and $s_1s_2$ contains $s_1$ and $s_2$), so each pair is a Bruhat cover, and since these are all the length-adjacent pairs they are exactly the covers of the A2 Bruhat graph ([[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]], [[lem-bruhat-covers-are-reflection-covers]], [[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]]).

[F2] $C_k(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=k}M(w\circ\lambda)$, so the terms are the four displayed sums, with $1+2+2+1=6$ Verma summands in total; the differential $d_k$ has $(w,w')$-component $\varepsilon(w,w')\iota_{w\to w'}$ for a cover $w\rhd w'$ and $0$ otherwise ([[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]], [[def-bgg-differential-from-signed-verma-maps]]).

[F3] For every cover $x\rhd y$ the map $\iota_{x\to y}\colon M(x\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(y\circ\lambda)$ is the canonical inclusion of the Verma submodule of $M(y\circ\lambda)$ generated by the singular vector of weight $x\circ\lambda$, and for a saturated path $x\rhd m\rhd y$ the composite is the canonical inclusion $M(x\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(y\circ\lambda)$, independent of $m$ ([[lem-dominant-integral-dot-translates-embed-in-the-verma-module]], [[lem-bruhat-covers-give-unique-verma-embeddings]]).

[F4] A rank-two Bruhat interval $\{y<m<x\}$ with $\ell(x)=\ell(y)+2$ has exactly two middle elements, both covered by $x$ and covering $y$; a compatible sign function exists, with opposite total signs on the two saturated paths of every such interval ([[lem-bruhat-rank-two-intervals-are-diamonds]], [[lem-compatible-signs-exist-on-the-bruhat-graph]]).

[F5] For a complex built from compatible signs as in [F2] one has $d_{k-1}\circ d_k=0$ for all $k\ge2$, because each component is a sum over the (zero or two) middle elements of a rank-two interval and the two path contributions cancel by [F4] ([[prop-the-bgg-differential-squares-to-zero]]).

[F6] For $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ the augmented BGG complex is exact, and the weights $w\circ\lambda$ are pairwise distinct ([[thm-bgg-resolution-of-a-finite-dimensional-simple-module]], [[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]]).

## Verification

1.1 The six summands: by [F1] the lengths are $0,1,1,2,2,3$, so $C_0=M(\lambda)$, $C_1=M(s_1\circ\lambda)\oplus M(s_2\circ\lambda)$, $C_2=M(s_1s_2\circ\lambda)\oplus M(s_2s_1\circ\lambda)$ and $C_3=M(w_0\circ\lambda)$: six Verma summands in total, as displayed. [F1, F2]

1.2 The differentials: the covers of [F1] fall into 2 (from length $1$ to $0$), 4 (from length $2$ to $1$) and 2 (from length $3$ to $2$); by [F2] the components of $d_1,d_2,d_3$ are exactly the signed cover embeddings listed in the Example, and by [F3] these listed embeddings are the canonical submodules of the ambient $M(\lambda)$ along the respective paths. [F1, F2, F3]

1.3 Exactness: by [F6] the full sequence $0\to C_3\to C_2\to C_1\to C_0\to L(\lambda)\to0$ is exact; this is the assertion that the displayed six-summand complex resolves $L(\lambda)$. The weights $w\circ\lambda$ for the six elements $w$ are pairwise distinct by [F6], so the six summands are pairwise non-isomorphic labelled Verma modules. [F6]

2.1 The rank-two intervals of the Bruhat poset of type $A_2$ are $[e,s_1s_2]$ with middles $s_1,s_2$, $[e,s_2s_1]$ with middles $s_1,s_2$, $[s_1,w_0]$ with middles $s_1s_2,s_2s_1$, and $[s_2,w_0]$ with middles $s_1s_2,s_2s_1$; each has exactly two middle elements by [F4]. Hence every component of $d^2$ either is zero (no middle) or cancels by the square condition, so $d_{k-1}\circ d_k=0$ for all $k\ge2$ by [F5]. [F4, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 Each listed structural claim is verified: the terms in step 1.1, the signed cover differentials in step 1.2, vanishing of $d^2$ in step 2.1, and exactness and weight distinctness in step 1.3. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 1.3] ∎
