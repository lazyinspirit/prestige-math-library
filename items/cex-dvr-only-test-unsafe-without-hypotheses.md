## Statement refuted

Every quasi-separated morphism $f:X\to S$ of schemes all of whose valuative
diagrams over discrete valuation rings have at most one lift is separated.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the subring $V=\bigcup_{n\ge1}k[t^{1/n}]_{(t^{1/n})}$ of $K=\bigcup_{n\ge1}k(t^{1/n})$, where the transition maps are indexed by divisibility, the scheme $X$ obtained by gluing two copies $U=\operatorname{Spec}V$ and $W=\operatorname{Spec}V$ along the identity on $\operatorname{Spec}K$, and the structure morphism $f:X\to S=\operatorname{Spec}V$ induced on each chart by the identity $V\to V$.

[F1] A subring $V\subseteq K$ of a field is a **valuation ring** of $K$ if for every $x\in K^\times$ at least one of $x$, $x^{-1}$ belongs to $V$. ([[def-valuation-ring]])

[F2] A **discrete valuation** on a field $K$ is a valuation $v:K\to\mathbb Z\cup\{\infty\}$ whose restriction $v:K^\times\to\mathbb Z$ is surjective; the associated discrete valuation ring is $V_v=\{x:v(x)\ge0\}$, and it is never a field. ([[def-discrete-valuation]], [[def-discrete-valuation-ring]])

[F3] Affine schemes equipped with open subschemes and isomorphisms on overlaps satisfying the identity and cocycle conditions glue to a scheme, uniquely up to unique isomorphism, with the given affine schemes as an open affine cover. ([[thm-gluing-affine-schemes]])

[F4] For affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$, $W=\operatorname{Spec}C$ of $X$ over a common affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A$ of the base, separatedness of $X\to S$ is equivalent to requiring for every such pair that: $U\cap W$ is affine and $B\otimes_AC\to\Gamma(U\cap W,\mathcal O_X)$ is surjective. ([[thm-separatedness-gluing-overlap-criterion]])

[F5] A **valuative diagram** for $f$ is a valuation ring $R\subseteq K'$ with fraction field $K'$ together with $\operatorname{Spec}K'\to X$ and $\operatorname{Spec}R\to S$ forming a commutative square; a lift is a compatible $\operatorname{Spec}R\to X$. ([[def-valuative-diagram-separatedness]])

[F6] A morphism of schemes induces local homomorphisms on stalks, and for $x\in X$ with $u(s)=x$ the stalk map $\mathcal O_{X,x}\to\mathcal O_{T,s}$ is local. ([[def-morphism-of-schemes]])

[F7] For $g\in A$ the morphism induced by $A\to A_g$ identifies $\operatorname{Spec}(A_g)$ with the open locally ringed subspace $D(g)$ of $\operatorname{Spec}A$. ([[lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion]])

[F10] If there is an affine open cover $S=\bigcup_iS_i$ and, for each $i$, an affine open cover $f^{-1}(S_i)=\bigcup_jX_{ij}$ such that every intersection $X_{ij}\cap X_{ij'}$ is a finite union of affine opens, then $f$ is quasi-separated. ([Stacks Project, Schemes, Lemma 26.21.6 (Tag 01KH)](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01KH))



## Counterexample

1.1 For each $n\ge1$, write $u=t^{1/n}$ and let $\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}$ be the usual order-of-vanishing discrete valuation on $k(u)$. Scale it by $1/n$. These valuations are compatible under the transition maps indexed by divisibility, since $t^{1/n}=(t^{1/m})^{m/n}$ when $n\mid m$; multiplicativity and the ultrametric inequality for $\operatorname{ord}_{u=0}$ are preserved by this positive rescaling, so they define a valuation $v:K\to\mathbb Q\cup\{\infty\}$ with $v(0)=\infty$ [F2]. Its value group is $\mathbb Q$, since $v(t^{1/n})=1/n$. At each stage, the elements of nonnegative value are exactly $k[t^{1/n}]_{(t^{1/n})}$, so $V=\{z\in K:v(z)\ge0\}$ is a valuation ring by [F1], with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_V=\{z:v(z)>0\}\cup\{0\}$ and residue field $k$ (taking residues at any stage gives the same element of $k$). It is not a discrete valuation ring by [F2]. It is not Noetherian: if $\mathfrak m_V$ were generated by finitely many nonzero elements, the least positive value $q$ of those generators would bound below the value of every element they generate, whereas $t^{1/n}\in\mathfrak m_V$ has value $1/n<q$ for all sufficiently large $n$. [F1, F2]


1.2 We show that every valuative diagram for $f$ over a discrete valuation ring $A$ has at most one lift. Let $A$ be a discrete valuation ring by [F2], with fraction field $L$, closed point $s$ and generic point $\eta_A$, and let $u,v:\operatorname{Spec}A\to X$ be two lifts of one diagram; then $u$ and $v$ agree on $\operatorname{Spec}L$, so $u(\eta_A)=v(\eta_A)=x$. [F2, F5]

2.1 Every nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of $V$ is $\mathfrak m_V$. Indeed, choose $0\ne g\in\mathfrak p$; since $\mathfrak p$ is proper, $v(g)>0$. For any nonzero $h\in\mathfrak m_V$, choose $N$ with $Nv(h)\ge v(g)$. Then $h^N/g\in V$, so $h^N\in gV\subseteq\mathfrak p$, and primality gives $h\in\mathfrak p$. Thus $\mathfrak m_V\subseteq\mathfrak p$, so equality holds. The only primes of $V$ are therefore $(0)$ and $\mathfrak m_V$. Fix $0\ne g\in\mathfrak m_V$. For any $z\in K$, if $v(z)\ge0$ then $z\in V$; if $v(z)<0$, choose $N$ with $v(z)+Nv(g)\ge0$, so $zg^N\in V$ and $z=(zg^N)/g^N\in V_g$. Hence $V_g=K$, and [F7] identifies $\operatorname{Spec}K$ with $D(g)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}V$. Now [F3] glues $U$ and $W$ along this open to a scheme $X$ with $U\cap W=\operatorname{Spec}K$ and $U\cup W=X$, and the identity $V\to V$ on each chart induces $f:X\to S=\operatorname{Spec}V$. [F3, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Apply [F10] to the one-member affine cover $S=\{S\}$ and the affine open cover $f^{-1}(S)=X=U\cup W$. The pairwise intersections in this cover are $U$, $W$, and $U\cap W=\operatorname{Spec}K$; all are affine by construction in step 2.1, hence each is a finite union of affine opens. Therefore $f$ is quasi-separated. [F10, step 2.1]

3.2 $X\to S$ is not separated. The pair $U,W$ consists of affine opens over the affine base $S$, and the map $V\otimes_VV\to\Gamma(U\cap W,\mathcal O_X)=K$ of [F4] is the multiplication $V\to K$, whose image $V$ is a proper subring of $K$: for instance $t^{-1}\in K$ satisfies $v(t^{-1})=-1<0$ and is therefore not in $V$ by step 1.1. So the criterion of [F4] fails and $X\to S$ is not separated. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.3 The point $x\in X$ is either the generic point $\eta$ with residue field $K$ or one of the two closed points $p_1\in U$, $p_2\in W$ with residue field $k$; these three are the only points of $X$ by step 2.1. [step 2.1]

4.1 Suppose $x=p_1$ (the case $x=p_2$ is symmetric). Since $\eta_A$ specializes to $s$, its image $x$ specializes to $u(s)$, and the closure of the closed point $p_1$ is $\{p_1\}$, so $u(s)=p_1$ and likewise $v(s)=p_1$. The stalk map at $p_1$ is a local homomorphism $V\to A$ by [F6], and its localization at the prime $(0)$ of $A$ is the stalk map at $\eta_A$, namely the map $V\to L$ induced by the generic map, which lands at the closed point $p_1$ and therefore kills $\mathfrak m_V$; since $A$ is a domain, an element of $A$ is zero exactly when its image in $L$ is zero, so $V\to A$ kills $\mathfrak m_V$ and factors through $V/\mathfrak m_V=k$. The resulting map $k\to A$ is determined by its composite $k\to A\to L$, which is fixed by the generic map; hence $u=v$. [F6, step 1.2, step 3.3]

4.2 Suppose $x=\eta$. If some lift $u$ had $u(s)=p_1$ or $u(s)=p_2$, its stalk map $V\to A$ at that closed point would be a local homomorphism by [F6] whose localization at $(0)\subseteq A$ equals the map $V\to K\to L$ induced by the generic map. Set $a=u^\#(t)\in A$. The generic morphism induces a field map $K\to L$, which is injective, so the image of the nonzero element $t\in K$ is nonzero; because $A\hookrightarrow L$, this gives $a\ne0$. Each $t^{1/n}$ lies in $\mathfrak m_V$, so its image $a_n\in A$ lies in the maximal ideal. Since $a_n^n=a\ne0$, we have $a_n\ne0$ and $v_A(a_n)\ge1$. Multiplicativity now gives $v_A(a)=n\,v_A(a_n)\ge n$ for every $n$, contradicting that the nonzero element $a$ has finite discrete valuation. Hence $u(s)=v(s)=\eta$, both lifts factor through the open subscheme $\operatorname{Spec}K\subseteq X$, and each is determined by a ring map $K\to A$ whose composite with $A\to L$ is the fixed map $K\to L$ induced by the generic map; since $K\to L$ is injective, that ring map is unique and $u=v$. [F6, step 1.2, step 3.3, algebra]

4.3 The diagram over the valuation ring $V$ itself does have two lifts: the two chart inclusions $U\hookrightarrow X$ and $W\hookrightarrow X$ are morphisms $\operatorname{Spec}V\to X$ whose composites with $f$ are the identity of $S$ and which agree on $\operatorname{Spec}K$, since the open subscheme $\operatorname{Spec}K$ is glued to itself; they differ at the closed point, which is sent to the distinct points $p_1$ and $p_2$ of step 3.3. [step 2.1, step 3.3]

5.1 Steps 4.1 and 4.2 cover both cases for the common generic image, so any two lifts of any valuative diagram for $f$ over a discrete valuation ring coincide: every such diagram has at most one lift. [step 4.1, step 4.2]

6.1 By step 3.2 the morphism $f$ is not separated and by step 3.1 it is quasi-separated, while by step 5.1 every valuative diagram over a discrete valuation ring has at most one lift; step 4.3 exhibits the failure over the non-discrete valuation ring $V$ and completes the refutation. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.3, step 5.1] ∎
