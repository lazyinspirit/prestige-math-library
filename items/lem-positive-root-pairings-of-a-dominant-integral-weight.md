## Statement

Let $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ be a dominant integral weight and let $\beta\in\Phi^+$. Then $\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$; in particular $\lambda+\rho$ is regular, so the stabilizer of $\lambda+\rho$ in $W$ is trivial and $w\circ\lambda=w'\circ\lambda$ implies $w=w'$. Moreover the pairing is $W$-invariant: for all $w\in W$, $\langle w(\lambda+\rho),(w\beta)^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle$, and $\langle w\circ\lambda+\rho,(w\beta)^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle$. Finally every positive coroot is a nonnegative integral combination of the simple coroots, so integrality and positivity are read off on simple coroots and extended by $W$-invariance.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi$ with positive system $\Phi^+$, simple roots $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r$ and simple coroots $\alpha_i^\vee$, the form $(\ ,\ )$ on $E$, the weight lattice $P=\{\lambda\in E:(\lambda,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z\text{ for all }\alpha\in\Phi\}$, the dominant integral weights $\Lambda^+=P\cap\overline{\mathcal C}$, the Weyl vector $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha$, and the Weyl group $W$ generated by the reflections $s_\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$.

[F1] The simple roots are a basis of $E$, the simple coroots are a basis of the dual space, the weight lattice is the lattice generated by the dual basis $\omega_1,\dots,\omega_r$ with $(\omega_i,\alpha_j^\vee)=\delta_{ij}$, and integrality of $\lambda$ means $\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ for every root $\alpha$ ([[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

[F2] Each simple reflection permutes $\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha_i\}$ and sends $\alpha_i$ to $-\alpha_i$; $s_\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$; the form is positive definite and $W$-invariant; every root is $W$-conjugate to a simple root; the only scalar multiples of a root in $\Phi$ are $\pm$ itself ([[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]], [[thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]]).

[F3] Every positive root is a nonnegative integral combination of the simple roots in which at least one coefficient is positive ([[thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]]).

[F4] $W$ acts simply transitively on open chambers; equivalently a vector lying on no root hyperplane has trivial stabilizer, and every $W$-orbit meets the closed chamber in exactly one point ([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]]).

## Proof

1.1 For every simple root $\alpha_i$ one has $\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ by dominance. Applying $s_i$ to $2\rho=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha$ and using that $s_i$ permutes the positive roots other than $\alpha_i$ while $s_i\alpha_i=-\alpha_i$ gives $2s_i\rho=2\rho-2\alpha_i$, whereas $s_i\rho=\rho-\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\alpha_i$. Comparing the two expressions gives $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$, so $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Let $\beta\in\Phi^+$ and write $\beta=\sum_in_i\alpha_i$ with $n_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, not all zero. Put $c_i:=\langle\omega_i,\beta^\vee\rangle=\frac{2(\omega_i,\beta)}{(\beta,\beta)}$. Then $c_i\in\mathbb Z$ because $\omega_i\in P$ and $\beta^\vee$ is the coroot of the root $\beta$, and $c_i\ge0$ because $(\omega_i,\beta)=\sum_jn_j(\omega_i,\alpha_j)=\frac{1}{2}n_i(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)\ge0$ and $(\beta,\beta)>0$. Since the $\omega_j$ are a basis of $E$ and both sides have the same pairing with every $\omega_j$, this gives the identity $\beta^\vee=\sum_ic_i\alpha_i^\vee$: every positive coroot is a nonnegative integral combination of the simple coroots, and for $\beta>0$ at least one $c_i$ is positive. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Combining the two previous steps, $\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle=\sum_ic_i\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle$ is a nonnegative integer combination in which at least one coefficient is positive and every paired simple coroot contributes at least $1$; hence it lies in $\mathbb Z_{>0}$. For a negative root $-\beta$ one has $\langle\lambda+\rho,(-\beta)^\vee\rangle=-\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle<0$, so $\lambda+\rho$ pairs nontrivially with the coroot of every root and is therefore regular: it lies on no root hyperplane. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 A regular vector lies in some open chamber, and $W$ acts simply transitively on open chambers, so its stabilizer is trivial. If $w\circ\lambda=w'\circ\lambda$, then $w(\lambda+\rho)=w'(\lambda+\rho)$ and hence $(w'^{-1}w)(\lambda+\rho)=\lambda+\rho$; triviality of the stabilizer gives $w'^{-1}w=1$, that is $w=w'$. [F4, step 2.1]

4.1 For $w\in W$ and $\beta\in\Phi$ one has $(w\beta)^\vee=w(\beta^\vee)$ because $w$ preserves the form, and pairing with $w(\lambda+\rho)$ against $w(\beta^\vee)$ equals pairing against $\beta^\vee$ because $w$ is an isometry. Hence $\langle w(\lambda+\rho),(w\beta)^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda+\rho,\beta^\vee\rangle$, and since $w\circ\lambda+\rho=w(\lambda+\rho)$ the second displayed identity is the same statement; the nonnegative-integral-combination statement at a simple coroot, transported along $W$, is the "$W$-invariance" used throughout. [F2, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
