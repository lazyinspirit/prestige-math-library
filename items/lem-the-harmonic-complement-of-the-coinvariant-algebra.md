## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from the published invariant-theory
items. Let $W$ act on the Cartan space $\mathfrak h$ by reflections and on
$S=S(\mathfrak h)$ by graded algebra automorphisms, with coinvariant algebra
$C=S/I$ as in [[def-the-coinvariant-algebra-of-a-finite-weyl-group]]. Then there
is a $W$-stable graded subspace $\mathcal H\subseteq S$, finite dimensional of
dimension $|W|$, such that the multiplication map
$$S^W\otimes_{\mathbb C}\mathcal H\longrightarrow S,\qquad p\otimes h\mapsto ph,$$
is an isomorphism of graded $S^W$-modules and of $W$-modules, and the
restriction of the quotient map $\rho:S\to C$ to $\mathcal H$ is an isomorphism
of graded $W$-modules $\mathcal H\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }C$. One may take
$$\mathcal H=\{f\in S:\partial_pf=0\text{ for all }p\in S^W_+\},$$
using constant-coefficient differential operators from a $W$-invariant
positive definite form on $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$. Any $W$-stable graded
complement of $I$ has the same multiplication and quotient properties. This
is the simultaneous kernel of all positive-degree invariant differential
operators, not in general the kernel of the quadratic Laplacian alone. In particular $\dim\mathcal H_d=\dim C_d$ for every $d$ and
$\sum_d\dim\mathcal H_d=|W|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Weyl reflection group $W$ of rank $r$ acting on the real Cartan space $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ and on $S=\mathbb C[\mathfrak h]$ (page degrees, $\deg\mathfrak h=2$), its invariant algebra $S^W$, the ideal $I=S\,S^W_+$ with basic invariants $w_1,\dots,w_r$, and the coinvariant algebra $C=S/I$ with quotient map $\rho$.

[F1] Under AC, $S^W=\mathbb C[w_1,\dots,w_r]$ is a polynomial algebra on homogeneous basic invariants of page degrees $2d_i>0$, and $S$ is a free graded $S^W$-module of rank $|W|$ ([[thm-chevalley-shephard-todd-for-finite-weyl-groups]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] $I=S\,S^W_+$ is the ideal generated by the basic invariants, $C=S/I$ is a commutative graded $\mathbb C$-algebra with $C_0=\mathbb C$ generated in degree two, $\rho$ is surjective with kernel $I$, and $\operatorname{Hilb}(C,t)=\prod_i(1+t^2+\cdots+t^{2(d_i-1)})$, $\dim_{\mathbb C}C=\prod_id_i=|W|$; the $w_i$ are minimal homogeneous invariant generators, equivalently a homogeneous $S$-regular sequence realizing $I$ ([[def-the-coinvariant-algebra-of-a-finite-weyl-group]], [[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]], [[lem-reflection-basic-invariants-form-a-regular-sequence]]).

[F3] Every symmetric bilinear form on a finite-dimensional vector space has an orthogonal basis, and positivity is preserved under the constructions used below ([[def-bilinear-symmetric-skew-and-alternating-forms]], [[thm-symmetric-bilinear-forms-have-an-orthogonal-basis]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose an arbitrary positive definite symmetric bilinear form on $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ and average it over the finite group $W$, obtaining a $W$-invariant positive definite form $(\ ,\ )$; choose an orthonormal basis $e_1,\dots,e_r$ of $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ for it. On the symmetric algebra $S=\mathbb C[e_1,\dots,e_r]$ let $\langle\ ,\ \rangle$ be the Fischer pairing, the sesquilinear form with $\langle e^{\alpha},e^{\beta}\rangle=\alpha!\,\delta_{\alpha\beta}$ on monomials $e^{\alpha}$ ($\alpha$ a multi-index); it is positive definite, and $W$ acts on $S$ by orthogonal transformations for it because the form on $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}$ is $W$-invariant. For $p\in S$ let $\partial_p=p(\partial_1,\dots,\partial_r)$ be substitution of the partial derivatives $\partial_i=\partial/\partial e_i$; then multiplication by $p$ has adjoint $\partial_{\overline p}$, i.e. $\langle pf,g\rangle=\langle f,\partial_{\overline p}g\rangle$, as checked on monomials. Choose real basic invariants: real and imaginary parts of complex invariants are invariant, and one may choose a real homogeneous basis of the indecomposables of the invariant algebra and lift it; its complexification gives basic generators. Complex conjugation preserves $S^W_+$, so simultaneous kernels are unchanged. Set $\mathcal H_d:=I_d^{\perp}$ inside the finite-dimensional space $S_d$, and $\mathcal H:=\bigoplus_d\mathcal H_d$. [F3, construct]

1.2 The invariant ring acts on $S$ with the expected Hilbert series: $S$ has a finite homogeneous $S^W$-basis by [F1], say of degrees $m_1,\dots,m_{|W|}$; their images in $S/S^W_+S=C$ form a $\mathbb C$-basis by graded Nakayama, so $\sum_jt^{m_j}=\operatorname{Hilb}(C,t)$ and therefore $\operatorname{Hilb}(S,t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W,t)\operatorname{Hilb}(C,t)$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 By step 1.1 the spaces $S_d=I_d\oplus\mathcal H_d$ are orthogonal direct sums and $\mathcal H$ is a graded subspace; it is $W$-stable because $W$ preserves the form and $I$ is a $W$-stable homogeneous ideal, and $\mathcal H$ is a graded complement of $I$. Moreover $\mathcal H_d=\{f\in S_d:\partial_pf=0\text{ for all }p\in S^W_+\}$: since $I=(w_1,\dots,w_r)$, one has $f\in I_d^{\perp}$ exactly when $\langle f,w_ig\rangle=0$ for every $i$ and every $g\in S_{d-2d_i}$, which by adjointness is $\langle\partial_{w_i}f,g\rangle=0$ for all such $g$, hence $\partial_{w_i}f=0$ by positive definiteness; and for $p=\sum_iq_iw_i$ one has $\partial_p=\sum_i\partial_{q_i}\partial_{w_i}$, so vanishing for the $w_i$ is vanishing for all of $S^W_+$. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The restriction $\rho|_{\mathcal H}:\mathcal H\to C$ is an isomorphism of graded vector spaces because $\rho$ has kernel $I$ and $\mathcal H$ is a complement of $I$ by step 1.1; it is a map of $W$-modules because $\rho$ is $W$-equivariant. Hence $\dim\mathcal H_d=\dim C_d$ for every $d$, and $\dim\mathcal H=\dim C=|W|$ by [F2]. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $\varphi:S^W\otimes\mathcal H\to S$, $p\otimes h\mapsto ph$. This is a homomorphism of graded $S^W$-modules and of $W$-modules. Its reduction modulo $S^W_+$, namely $\mathcal H\to C$, $h\mapsto\rho(h)$, is surjective because it is the isomorphism $\rho|_{\mathcal H}$ of step 2.2. [step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The map $\varphi$ is surjective: given homogeneous $f\in S_d$, choose $h\in\mathcal H_d$ with $\rho(f)=\rho(h)$ by step 3.1; then $f-h\in I_d=\sum_i(w_iS)_d=\sum_iw_iS_{d-2d_i}$, so by induction on $d$ each $w_ig$ with $g\in S_{d-2d_i}$ lies in $S^W\cdot\mathcal H$, and adding $h$ exhibits $f$ in the image. [step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The map $\varphi$ is injective: by steps 1.2 and 2.2 the two sides have the same Hilbert series, $$\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W\otimes\mathcal H,t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W,t)\operatorname{Hilb}(\mathcal H,t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(S^W,t)\operatorname{Hilb}(C,t)=\operatorname{Hilb}(S,t),$$ both are graded with finite-dimensional graded pieces, and by step 4.1 the kernel of the surjective graded $S^W$-linear map $\varphi$ would otherwise contribute positive Hilbert series. Hence $\varphi$ is an isomorphism. [step 1.2, step 2.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Steps 2.1, 2.2 and 5.1 give a $W$-stable graded complement $\mathcal H$ of $I$ with $\rho|_{\mathcal H}:\mathcal H\cong C$ and $S^W\otimes\mathcal H\cong S$; the description of $\mathcal H$ as the simultaneous invariant differential kernel follows from the adjointness $\langle pf,g\rangle=\langle f,\partial_{\overline p}g\rangle$ of step 1.1, and $\dim\mathcal H=|W|$ by step 2.2. The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the invariant-theory suppliers [F1] and [F2]; the construction itself uses only finite averages and finite-dimensional linear algebra. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 5.1] ∎
