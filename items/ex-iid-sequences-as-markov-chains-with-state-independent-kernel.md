## Statement

Assume Choice. If $(X_n)_{n\ge0}$ is IID with common law $\nu$ on
$(E,\mathcal E)$, then relative to its natural filtration it is a Markov chain
with the state-independent kernel
$$K(x,A)=\nu(A).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Choice and the IID sequence in the statement.

[F1] IID means that the entire family is mutually independent and every coordinate has the same law. ([[def-identically-distributed-and-iid-random-variables]])

[F2] Sigma-algebras generated by disjoint blocks of an independent family are independent. ([[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]])

[F3] The indicator and bounded-function versions of the Markov property are equivalent. ([[lem-bounded-function-form-of-the-markov-property]])

## Verification

1.1 For each $x$, $A\mapsto K(x,A)=\nu(A)$ is a probability measure; for each [given] $A$, $x\mapsto\nu(A)$ is constant and measurable. Thus $K$ is a probability kernel, including $\nu(A)=0$ and $1$ and a one-point state space. [given]

2.1 Put $\mathcal F_n=\sigma(X_0,\ldots,X_n)$. By [F1]--[F2], [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1] $\sigma(X_{n+1})$ is independent of $\mathcal F_n$. Hence, for $B\in\mathcal F_n$ and $A\in\mathcal E$, $$ \mathbb E[1_B1_{\{X_{n+1}\in A\}}] =\mathbb P(B)\nu(A) =\mathbb E[1_BK(X_n,A)]. $$ Therefore the constant $K(X_n,A)=\nu(A)$ is a version of the indicated conditional probability. By [F3], equivalently $\mathbb E[f(X_{n+1})\mid\mathcal F_n]=\int f\,d\nu$ for every bounded measurable $f$. This verifies the claim at $n=0$ and all later times. Choice is used only for those conditional-expectation classes. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1] ∎
