## Definition

Let $f$ be a normalized $p$-ordinary newform of weight $k\ge2$, level $N$ with $p\nmid N$, and trivial nebentypus. Let $L/\mathbb Q_p$ be a finite p-adic coefficient extension containing the image of its coefficient field, let $\rho_f:G_{\mathbb Q}\to\operatorname{GL}_L(V_f)$ be its attached representation, and choose a $G_{\mathbb Q}$-stable $\mathcal O_L$-lattice $T_f$. Use the ordinary filtration stated here: $V_f^+$ is a one-dimensional $G_{\mathbb Q_p}$-stable subspace whose quotient is unramified. Set
$$T_f^+=T_f\cap V_f^+,\quad T=T_f(\det\rho_f^{-1}),\quad T^+=T_f^+(\det\rho_f^{-1}),$$
and let $V=V_f(\det\rho_f^{-1})$, $V^+=V_f^+(\det\rho_f^{-1})$. Put
$$A=V/T=T\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}(L/\mathcal O_L),\qquad
A^+=V^+/T^+=T^+\otimes_{\mathcal O_L}(L/\mathcal O_L)\subseteq A,\qquad A^-=A/A^+.$$
The displayed tensor identifications send $t\otimes(a+\mathcal O_L)$ to $at+T$. They use the original chosen representation, ordinary line and lattice, and do not assert existence of those objects or any comparison with Bloch–Kato local conditions.

Let $\mathbb Q_\infty/\mathbb Q$ be the cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_p$-extension, let $\Gamma=\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q_\infty/\mathbb Q)$, and let $S$ contain $p$ and all primes dividing $N$. Write $G_{\mathbb Q_\infty,S}$ for the quotient of $G_{\mathbb Q_\infty}$ by the closed normal subgroup generated by inertia at finite places not above $S$. The supplied representation is unramified outside $Np$, so $A$ is a discrete module for this quotient. All degree-one cohomology below means continuous cocycles modulo continuous coboundaries, as in [[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]]. Fix compatible restriction embeddings, and let $w_p$ be the unique place above $p$ in the cyclotomic tower. For each finite place $w\nmid p$ of $\mathbb Q_\infty$, let $I_w$ be its inertia subgroup. Define the local maps
$$\ell_p:H^1(G_{\mathbb Q_\infty,S},A)\longrightarrow
H^1(\mathbb Q_{\infty,p},A)\longrightarrow H^1(\mathbb Q_{\infty,p},A^-),$$
$$\ell_w:H^1(G_{\mathbb Q_\infty,S},A)\longrightarrow H^1(I_w,A)\quad(w\nmid p),$$
where $\mathbb Q_{\infty,p}$ is the completion at $w_p$, the first maps are restriction and the second map in $\ell_p$ is induced by $A\twoheadrightarrow A^-$. At places outside $S$ the inertia map factors through the trivial subgroup by definition. Set
$$\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathbb Q_\infty}(f)=
\ker\!\left((\ell_p,(\ell_w)_{w\nmid p}):
H^1(G_{\mathbb Q_\infty,S},A)\longrightarrow
H^1(\mathbb Q_{\infty,p},A^-)\times\prod_{w\nmid p}H^1(I_w,A)\right).$$
The product ranges over ALL finite places not above $p$. This is exactly the original full local $H^1(A^-)$ kernel at $p$, together with unramifiedness elsewhere; it is not replaced by an inertia-only $p$-condition or a crystalline condition. Choices of prolongations only conjugate the maps and do not change their kernels.

Give this Selmer group its discrete topology. Its Pontryagin dual is
$$X_f=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{cont}}(\operatorname{Sel}_{\mathbb Q_\infty}(f),\mathbb Q_p/\mathbb Z_p),$$
with its compact pointwise topology. The $\Gamma$-action on cohomology comes from conjugation on $G_{\mathbb Q_\infty,S}$ and the given $G_{\mathbb Q}$-action on $A$; different lifts differ by an inner action and induce the same cohomology action. The local kernel is stable under it. The dual action is $(\gamma\phi)(a)=\phi(\gamma^{-1}a)$, and $(c\phi)(a)=\phi(ca)$ for $c\in\mathcal O_L$. It extends to
$$\Lambda=\varprojlim_{r\ge1}\mathcal O_L[\Gamma/\Gamma^{p^r}],$$
with inverse-limit multiplication. A topological generator $\gamma$ identifies $\Lambda$ with $\mathcal O_L\llbracket u\rrbracket$ by $\gamma\mapsto1+u$.

If $X_f$ is finitely generated torsion over $\Lambda$, put
$$n_{\mathfrak q}=\operatorname{length}_{\Lambda_{\mathfrak q}}((X_f)_{\mathfrak q})
\quad\text{for each height-one prime }\mathfrak q\subset\Lambda,$$
and define its characteristic ideal intrinsically by
$$\operatorname{char}_{\Lambda}(X_f)=
\{x\in\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda):v_{\mathfrak q}(x)\ge n_{\mathfrak q}
\text{ for every height-one }\mathfrak q\}.$$
Equivalently, since this one-variable coefficient ring is factorial, this is the finite product $\prod_{\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)=1}\mathfrak q^{n_{\mathfrak q}}$. Thus this spells out the original product of local characteristic ideals; it does not assert that the dual is finitely generated or torsion. The definition is intrinsic to $\Lambda$ and so does not depend on the displayed choice of generator. It asserts no lattice independence or Iwasawa main conjecture.

## Construction checks and scope

The sublattice $T^+$ is saturated: if a nonzero scalar multiple of $t\in T$ lies in the $L$-line $V^+$, then $t$ already lies in that line. Over the DVR $\mathcal O_L$, $T/T^+$ is therefore finite free. Tensoring its exact lattice sequence with $L/\mathcal O_L$ gives the stated injection $A^+\subseteq A$ and the quotient $A^-$. The local arrows are continuous equivariant maps, hence induce maps of cocycles and coboundaries. Their product kernel is a group. The ordinary line and attached representation are the ORIGINAL input data; no theorem constructing them from the scalar ordinary Fourier coefficient is being claimed.

A continuous cocycle on the compact Galois group with values in discrete $A$ has finite image, hence is killed by some power of $p$. The conjugation action is continuous on its cohomology class: choose an open normal subgroup in the ambient profinite Galois group which fixes the finitely many cocycle values and whose intersection with the subgroup on which the cocycle is defined lies in a neighborhood where that cocycle is constant on cosets. Conjugation by this open subgroup fixes the cocycle. Thus every class has an open stabilizer, and its $\mathcal O_L$-span of the finite $\Gamma$-orbit is finite. The dual is a closed subgroup of the product of the finite groups of possible values on individual torsion elements, hence compact under AC. Its continuous scalar/group action extends to $\Lambda$ by passage through these finite stable submodules. This supplies the module structure without a finite-generation or torsion theorem about $X_f$.

For completeness, the coefficient ring facts needed by the conditional characteristic ideal do not require a restriction to $L=\mathbb Q_p$. Put $\mathcal O=\mathcal O_L$ and let $\pi$ be a uniformizer. The group-ring identification follows after reduction modulo $\pi^a$ from the cofinality of the ideals $(\pi^a,(1+u)^{p^r}-1)$ with powers of $(\pi,u)$: modulo $\pi$, the second polynomial is $u^{p^r}$, so a power of $u$ belongs to $(\pi^a,(1+u)^{p^r}-1)$; conversely its finitely many low-degree coefficients tend to zero $\pi$-adically as $r$ grows. Taking the two compatible inverse limits gives $\Lambda=\mathcal O\llbracket u\rrbracket$.

Here is the finite algebra behind its Noetherian/factorial assertions. For a nonzero ideal $J\subset\mathcal O\llbracket u\rrbracket$, take the least common $\pi$-valuation of its elements' coefficients, say $m$. The ideal $\pi^{-m}J$ contains a primitive series; by [F5] it contains a monic distinguished polynomial $P$. Its quotient by $(P)$ is a submodule of the finite free $\mathcal O$-module $\mathcal O\llbracket u\rrbracket/(P)$, so is finitely generated. Thus $J$ is finitely generated. Preparation and [F6] factor every nonzero series into powers of $\pi$ and monic distinguished irreducible polynomials. The factor $\pi$ is prime. A distinguished irreducible polynomial $h$ is prime too: division identifies the quotient by $h$ with $\mathcal O[u]/(h)$, a domain by Gauss's lemma. This gives unique factorization and normality. Every height-one prime is generated by one such prime element; its localization is a DVR, by taking the exponent of that prime element. A finite generating list for a torsion module gives a single nonzero annihilator. Only its finitely many prime factors can support the localized module; at each such prime the module is killed by a power of the DVR uniformizer and has finite length. The characteristic product is therefore finite. These arguments cover every finite coefficient extension and $p=2$.

Only the local kernel and the CONDITIONAL characteristic-ideal convention are defined. Finite generation, torsionness, lattice independence, identification with a Bloch–Kato group, and any main-conjecture equality remain separate claims. The original supplied representation/filtration/lattice data are not manufactured by this definition. The retained Skinner–Urban introduction, printed page2, was read for the full $p$-local kernel, unramified-away-from-$p$ condition and Pontryagin-dual convention; its geometric representation/ordinary-line convention is not silently identified with a different convention here.
