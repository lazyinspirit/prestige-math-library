## Statement

Assume AC. For a commutative ring $A$ and a module-finite commutative $A$-algebra $D$, finite presentation as an $A$-module is equivalent to finite presentation as an $A$-algebra. Consequently $\operatorname{Spec}D\to\operatorname{Spec}A$ is finite étale if and only if $D$ is finitely presented and flat as an $A$-module and $\Omega_{D/A}=0$. Its underlying module is finite projective and locally free of finite rank; that rank is locally constant and is the number of geometric points in a fibre. No Noetherian hypothesis on $A$ is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a ring $A$, and a module-finite commutative $A$-algebra $D$.

[F1] A module-finite algebra is integral: if $D\ne0$, apply [[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]] to the image of $A$ in $D$, using $D$ as a faithful finite module over each subalgebra generated by one element. For $D=0$, the monic polynomial $T$ suffices. Étale means flat, unramified and locally finitely presented; a quasi-compact separated locally finitely presented morphism is finitely presented, so this applies to a finite affine map. For finite-type algebras unramifiedness is equivalent to $\Omega=0$ ([[thm-etale-equivalent-flat-unramified-fp]], [[thm-formally-unramified-differentials-zero]]).

[F2] Nakayama's lemma lifts a finite residue-field generating set and detects a zero finite module; a short exact sequence with flat quotient remains exact after tensoring ([[cor-nakayama-generators-modulo-an-ideal]], [[thm-flat-quotients-preserve-short-exact-tensor-sequences]]).

[F3] AC is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]); it is inherited through the étale criterion and the stated Nakayama supplier. The choices below are finite choices at each local ring.

## Proof

1.1 Suppose $D$ is finitely presented as an $A$-algebra. Choose finite algebra generators $d_1,\ldots,d_r$ and monic equations $P_i(d_i)=0$ by [F1]. The kernel of $A[T_1,\ldots,T_r]\to D$ is a finitely generated ideal: a finite presentation with a different generating list converts to this one by adding both finite lists, imposing the finitely many equations expressing each list in the other, and eliminating the extra variables. Put $C=A[T_1,\ldots,T_r]/(P_1,\ldots,P_r)$. Reduction by the monic polynomials gives a finite free $A$-basis of $C$ consisting of the monomials $\prod T_i^{e_i}$ with $0\le e_i<\deg P_i$. The kernel $J$ of $C\to D$ is a finitely generated $C$-ideal, so multiplication of its finitely many ideal generators by this finite $A$-basis generates $J$ as an $A$-module. Thus $D$ has a finite $A$-module presentation. [F1, algebra]

1.2 Let $M$ be any finitely presented flat $A$-module. At a prime $\mathfrak p$, choose lifts of a basis of $M_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pM_{\mathfrak p}$. By [F2] they give a surjection $A_{\mathfrak p}^n\to M_{\mathfrak p}$. Its kernel $K$ is finitely generated because $M_{\mathfrak p}$ is finitely presented: this follows for any finite free surjection by adjoining a fixed finite presentation and eliminating its finitely many auxiliary generators. Tensoring with the residue field is exact by flatness and [F2], and the last map is the chosen basis isomorphism. Thus $K/\mathfrak pK=0$, so $K=0$ by Nakayama. The local module is free. [F2, F3, construct]

2.1 Conversely, choose module generators $1=d_0,d_1,\ldots,d_r$ and finitely many generators of their $A$-linear relations. Present an algebra with variables $T_0,\ldots,T_r$, relation $T_0=1$, those finitely many linear relations, and the finitely many multiplication relations $T_iT_j=\sum_k a_{ijk}T_k$ expressing products in $D$. Every polynomial in this presented algebra reduces to an $A$-linear combination of the $T_i$, by induction on its degree. The map to $D$ is surjective; a linear combination mapping to zero is a combination of the imposed module relations, so the map is injective. This is a finite algebra presentation. Applying [F1] proves the asserted étale criterion. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The local basis established in step 1.2 spreads to a principal neighbourhood of $\mathfrak p$. Represent the basis and its inverse over the local ring using finitely many denominators. The inverse is a map from a finitely presented module, so its values on the finite generators and the finitely many relations are defined after inverting one element outside $\mathfrak p$. The two composition identities are identities on finitely many generators and therefore also hold after one further localization. Hence $M$ is free on a neighbourhood of every prime. The resulting rank function is locally constant. Applied to $D$, its geometric fibre is a finite-dimensional algebra with zero differentials over an algebraically closed field; étaleness, or equivalently the zero-dimensional geometrically regular fibre in its definition, makes it a product of copies of that field. The product description is [[thm-etale-over-algebraically-closed-field-discrete-smooth-points]]. Its vector-space dimension is the local rank of $D$, proving the fibre-count assertion. [F1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 A finite locally free, finitely presented module is projective. To see this explicitly, take finitely many principal opens $D(a_i)$ on which it is free. Local basis vectors and dual coordinate maps have finitely many denominators, since the module is finitely presented. Clearing these denominators expresses $a_i^{N_i}\operatorname{id}_M$ as a finite sum of maps $m\mapsto\lambda(m)v$, with $v\in M$ and $\lambda\in\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,A)$. The powers $a_i^{N_i}$ generate the unit ideal because the principal opens cover the spectrum. A linear combination equal to $1$ therefore expresses $\operatorname{id}_M$ as a finite dual-basis sum. The associated maps $M\to A^r\to M$ compose to the identity, so $M$ is a summand of a finite free module and hence projective. This completes the strengthened assertion. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
