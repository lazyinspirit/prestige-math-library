## Example

Take $n=3$, so that $B_3$ has the generators $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, the positive
monoid $B_3^{+}$ has the two atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, and the half twist is
$\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ of length $N=3$. The example verifies:

1. $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\cdot\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2
   =\Delta^{2}$, the full twist of [[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]];
2. $\sigma_i\Delta^{2}=\Delta^{2}\sigma_i$ for $i=1,2$, hence $\Delta^{2}$
   commutes with every element of $B_3$, i.e. $\Delta^{2}\in Z(B_3)$;
3. together with the centre theorem for $n=3>2$ this gives
   $Z(B_3)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle$, and that centre is infinite cyclic: the
   full twist $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$ is a free generator;
4. the half twist itself is *not* central: $\Delta\sigma_1=\sigma_2\Delta$ while
   $\sigma_2\ne\sigma_1$, so $\Delta\notin Z(B_3)$ and the passage from $\Delta$
   to its square is genuine.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The natural number $3$; the braid group $B_3$ with generators
$\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]; its
positive monoid $B_3^{+}$ with atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, length $\ell$ and half
twist $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ of length $3$.

[F1] $B_3^{+}$ is generated by the atoms, $\ell([w])=|w|$ is additive,
$\ell(z)=0$ forces $z=1$, the braid relation gives
$\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$, and
$\Delta=T_1T_2=\sigma_1(\sigma_2\sigma_1)=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ has length
$N=1+2=3$ ([[def-positive-braid-monoid]],
[[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]],
[[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

[F2] $\sigma_i\Delta=\Delta\sigma_{3-i}$ for $i=1,2$
([[lem-conjugation-by-delta-reverses-artin-generators]]).

[F3] $B_3^{+}$ is a submonoid of $B_3$ and the elements of $B_3^{+}$ are
multiplied in $B_3$ as in the monoid, so $\Delta$ is an invertible element of
the group $B_3$ and group cancellation is available
([[thm-the-ore-fraction-group-of-positive-braids-is-the-artin-braid-group]]).

[F4] In the presentation of [F1], every element of $B_3$ is a finite product
of the letters $\sigma_1^{\pm1},\sigma_2^{\pm1}$
([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]).

[F5] **The centre.** For $n>2$ one has
$Z(B_n)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle=\{\Delta^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, and this group
is infinite cyclic: $\Delta^{2}\ne1$ and $k\mapsto\Delta^{2k}$ is an
isomorphism $\mathbb Z\to Z(B_n)$
([[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]]).

[F6] $\pi\colon B_3^{+}\to S_3$ is a monoid homomorphism with
$\pi(\sigma_i)=s_i$, and in the composition convention of
[[def-symmetric-group]] the adjacent transpositions $s_1=(1\,2)$ and
$s_2=(2\,3)$ are distinct, so $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$
([[lem-reduced-adjacent-transposition-words-have-well-defined-positive-lifts]],
[[def-symmetric-group]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The half twist and its two words.** By [F1], $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ has length $3$ and the braid relation gives the second triangular word $\Delta=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$; by [F3] the monoid $B_3^{+}$ sits inside the group $B_3$, so $\Delta$ may be inverted and cancelled there. [F1, F3]

1.2 **Both generators commute with $\Delta^{2}$.** Applying the identity $\sigma_i\Delta=\Delta\sigma_{3-i}$ of [F2] twice, $\sigma_1\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\Delta)\Delta=(\Delta\sigma_2)\Delta=\Delta(\sigma_2\Delta)=\Delta(\Delta\sigma_1)=\Delta^{2}\sigma_1$, and likewise $\sigma_2\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_2\Delta)\Delta=(\Delta\sigma_1)\Delta=\Delta(\sigma_1\Delta)=\Delta(\Delta\sigma_2)=\Delta^{2}\sigma_2$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 **The cube of $\sigma_1\sigma_2$ is the square of the half twist.** Using associativity and the two words for $\Delta$ from step 1.1, $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1)(\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2)=\Delta\cdot\Delta=\Delta^{2}$. [F1, step 1.1]

2.2 **Centrality of the full twist.** By [F4] every $z\in B_3$ is a finite product of the letters $\sigma_1^{\pm1},\sigma_2^{\pm1}$. Step 1.2 gives $\Delta^{2}\sigma_i=\sigma_i\Delta^{2}$ for $i=1,2$, and this passes to inverses: from $\Delta^{2}g=g\Delta^{2}$ one gets $g^{-1}\Delta^{2}=\Delta^{2}g^{-1}$ by multiplying the identity on the left and on the right by $g^{-1}$. Induction on the number of letters therefore gives $\Delta^{2}z=z\Delta^{2}$ for every $z\in B_3$, that is $\Delta^{2}\in Z(B_3)$. [F4, step 1.2]

3.1 **The centre is infinite cyclic on the full twist.** By [F5] with $n=3$, $Z(B_3)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle=\{\Delta^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, the element $\Delta^{2}$ is not $1$, and $k\mapsto\Delta^{2k}$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb Z\to Z(B_3)$; combining this with step 2.1, the centre is infinite cyclic and generated by the full twist $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$, consistently with the local centrality verification of step 2.2. [F5, step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 **The half twist alone is not central.** By [F2] with $i=1$ one has $\Delta\sigma_1=\sigma_2\Delta$. If $\Delta$ were central then also $\Delta\sigma_1=\sigma_1\Delta$, so $\sigma_2\Delta=\sigma_1\Delta$ and right cancellation by the invertible element $\Delta$ in $B_3$ [F3] gives $\sigma_2=\sigma_1$, contradicting $\pi(\sigma_2)=s_2\ne s_1=\pi(\sigma_1)$ [F6]. Hence $\Delta\notin Z(B_3)$, although $\Delta^{2}\in Z(B_3)$ by step 2.2: the full twist is the square of the half twist and is not the half twist itself. [F2, F3, F6, step 2.2]

4.1 **Conclusion.** In $B_3$ the full twist satisfies $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$ by step 2.1, it is central by step 2.2, it generates the centre $Z(B_3)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle\cong\mathbb Z$ by step 3.1, and the half twist $\Delta$ is not central by step 3.2. The four assertions of the Example section are therefore verified. No choice principle is used: the identities are finite word computations and the only inverse taken is the explicit inverse of the displayed word $\Delta$. ∎ [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2]

## Remarks

- **Why the name.** Geometrically the half twist $\Delta$ is the half turn of the
  $n$ strands, whose square is the full turn; the star of the example is the
  algebraic counterpart: $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$ is the full twist
  of $B_3$, and [[thm-the-center-of-b-n-is-generated-by-the-full-twist-for-n-greater-than-two]]
  identifies it as the generator of the centre. The words
  $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ and $(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$ have
  exponent sums $3$ and $6$ respectively, matching their homogeneous lengths
  $\ell(\Delta)=3=N$ and $\ell(\Delta^{2})=6=2N$.
- **The local and the global verification.** Step 2.2 checks centrality directly
  from the two generator identities, using only that $B_3$ is generated by
  $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$; the appeal to the centre theorem in step 3.1 is
  then used only to conclude that no *other* central elements exist. This
  matches the source's proof of the general theorem, where the same generator
  identities supply centrality of $\Delta^{2}$ before the divisibility
  argument bounds the centre.
- **The asymmetry of the half twist.** Centrality of $\Delta^{2}$ and failure of
  centrality of $\Delta$ are both visible in the index reversal
  $\sigma_i\mapsto\sigma_{3-i}$: conjugation by $\Delta$ permutes the two
  generators instead of fixing them, whereas its square fixes both. No choice
  principle is used anywhere.
