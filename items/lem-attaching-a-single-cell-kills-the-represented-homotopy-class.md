## Statement

Let $X$ be a path-connected based CW complex, let $p\ge1$, and let
$f:S^p\to X$ be a based map with class $\alpha=[f]\in\pi_p(X)$. Form the cofiber
$Y=X\cup_fD^{p+1}$, based at the image of the base point of $X$, and let
$\iota\in\pi_{p+1}(Y,X)$ be the class of the characteristic disk. Then:

(i) the map $\pi_i(X)\to\pi_i(Y)$ is an isomorphism for $1\le i\le p-1$ and a
surjection for $i=p$;

(ii) the connecting homomorphism
$\partial:\pi_{p+1}(Y,X)\to\pi_p(X)$ sends $\iota$ to $\pm\alpha$, so
$\alpha\in\ker(\pi_p(X)\to\pi_p(Y))=\operatorname{im}(\partial)$: the
attachment kills the represented class and changes no lower homotopy group;

(iii) if $X$ is simply connected, then $\pi_{p+1}(Y,X)$ is infinite cyclic
generated by $\iota$, the connecting homomorphism has image the subgroup
generated by $\alpha$ (trivial when $\alpha=0$), and consequently
$$\pi_p(Y)\cong\pi_p(X)/\langle\alpha\rangle .$$

For $X$ not simply connected and $p\ge2$ the kernel is a subgroup of
$\pi_p(X)$ containing $\alpha$ whose exact description is not claimed here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a path-connected based CW complex $X$ with base point $x_0$, an integer $p\ge1$, a based map $f:S^p\to X$ with $\alpha=[f]\in\pi_p(X)$, and the cofiber $Y=X\cup_fD^{p+1}$ based at the image of $x_0$.

[F1] [[def-cell-attachment-by-a-characteristic-map]]: For a space $X$, an attaching map $f:S^{n-1}\to X$ and $n\ge1$, the attachment is the pushout $X\cup_fD^n=(X\sqcup D^n)/(z\sim f(z))$. The quotient map restricted to $D^n$ is the characteristic map; its image is the closed cell and the image of the open disk is the open cell.

[F2] [[lem-high-relative-cells-do-not-change-lower-homotopy]]: Let $(Z,A)$ be a CW pair all of whose cells outside $A$ have dimension at least $n\ge1$. At every $a\in A$ the homomorphism $\pi_i(A,a)\to\pi_i(Z,a)$ is an isomorphism for $1\le i<n-1$ and a surjection for $i=n-1\ge1$; also $\pi_0(A)\to\pi_0(Z)$ is surjective, and bijective when $n\ge2$. No choice principle is used.

[F3] [[def-relative-homotopy-group]]: For $n\ge1$, relative classes $\pi_n(Y,X,x_0)$ are classes of continuous maps $a:I^n\to Y$ with $a(I^{n-1}\times\{0\})\subseteq X$ and all other faces mapped to $x_0$, taken up to homotopy satisfying the same conditions at every time. The connecting homomorphism is induced by restricting such a representative to the distinguished face $I^{n-1}\times\{0\}$.

[F4] [[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-relative-homotopy-groups]]: For every based
pair $(Z,A,x_0)$ the sequence
$$\cdots\to\pi_{p+1}(A)\to\pi_{p+1}(Z)\to\pi_{p+1}(Z,A)\xrightarrow{\ \partial\ }\pi_p(A)\to\pi_p(Z)\to\cdots$$
is exact at each term with an incoming and an outgoing arrow.

[F5] [[lem-relative-cubical-disk-model-and-compression]]: Collapsing the union of the non-distinguished faces identifies the relative cubical triple with $(D^n,S^{n-1},b)$ for $n\ge1$; a disk representative $g:(D^n,S^{n-1},b)\to(Z,A,x_0)$ represents the distinguished relative class if and only if it is homotopic into $A$ with its entire boundary fixed.

[F6] [[lem-relative-single-cell-layer-has-compatible-homotopy-and-homology-bases]]: Let $A$ be a nonempty simply connected CW complex, $a\in A$ and $k\ge2$, and attach a set of oriented $k$-cells directly to $A$, with supplied characteristic maps $\chi_e:(D^k,S^{k-1})\to(Z,A)$. Then $\pi_k(Z,A,a)$ is free abelian on these cells; the basis element of a cell is represented by moving the marked boundary value of $\chi_e$ to $a$ through $A$ and extending that homotopy, and it is independent of those choices. This holds for arbitrary sets of cells and is choice-free.

[F7] [[thm-cellular-approximation-for-maps-of-cw-pairs]]: a map from a finite CW source has a cellular approximation without a choice assumption. The disk boundary is a subcomplex and its inclusion is a cofibration by [[prop-relative-cw-inclusions-are-cofibrations]].


## Proof

**Given:** the path-connected based CW complex $X$, the integer $p\ge1$, the based map $f:S^p\to X$ with class $\alpha$, and $Y=X\cup_fD^{p+1}$.

1.1 The map $f$ need not be cellular. By [F7] homotope it to a cellular $f':S^p\to X$ and form $Y'=X\cup_{f'}D^{p+1}$, an actual CW pair relative to $X$ with one new $(p+1)$-cell. The homotopy from $f$ to $f'$ gives a homotopy equivalence $Y\simeq Y'$ relative to $X$: map a concentric inner disk to the other characteristic disk and use the homotopy on the outer boundary annulus; the reverse homotopy gives the inverse, and the two annuli in each composite contract to give homotopies fixing $X$. Thus the required relative CW model is supplied rather than assumed. The equivalence fixes the basepoint in $X$, even when the cellular approximation homotopy was unbased. [F1, F7, given, construct]

1.2 The characteristic map $\Phi:(D^{p+1},S^p)\to(Y,X)$ of [F1] is a relative representative in the sense of [F3] once its marked boundary value in $X$ is moved to the base point along $X$; by the disk model [F5] it determines a class $\iota\in\pi_{p+1}(Y,X,x_0)$, using this specified basepoint data, and it is the based characteristic-disk class appearing in the statement. Since f is based, choose its specified marked boundary point; arbitrary transport paths are not asserted to give the same class when $\pi_1(X)$ acts nontrivially. [F1, F3, F5, given]

2.1 Apply [F2] to the CW pair $(Y',X)$ of step 1.1 and transport across its homotopy equivalence relative to $X$ with $n=p+1\ge2$: there are no cells outside $X$ of dimension below $p+1$, so at $x_0$ the homomorphism $\pi_i(X,x_0)\to\pi_i(Y,x_0)$ is an isomorphism for $1\le i\le p-1$ and a surjection for $i=p$. This is assertion (i). [F2, step 1.1]

2.2 The connecting homomorphism sends $\iota$ to $\pm\alpha$. Indeed $\Phi$ restricts on the boundary sphere $S^p$ to the composite $f$ followed by the inclusion $X\hookrightarrow Y$, and the boundary sphere $\partial D^{p+1}=S^p$ is identified with the image of $f$ by the pushout, so the restriction of $\Phi$ to the distinguished face of the disk model is a based representative of $\alpha$; the connecting homomorphism is induced by exactly that restriction, so $\partial\iota=\pm\alpha$, the sign being the boundary-orientation sign of the disk model. [F3, F5, step 1.2]

2.3 Now suppose $X$ is simply connected. Then [F6] applies to the CW model of step 1.1 with $A=X$, $k=p+1$ and its single attached cell. The relative equivalence transports its characteristic-disk class to the original one (the homotopy sits on its boundary annulus), and simple connectivity removes the transport-path ambiguity. Thus $\pi_{p+1}(Y,X,x_0)$ is free abelian on the class $\iota$ of the characteristic cell, hence infinite cyclic generated by $\iota$. [F6, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Consequently $\alpha\in\operatorname{im}(\partial)$, and exactness of the sequence of [F4] at $\pi_p(X)$ identifies $\operatorname{im}(\partial)=\ker(\pi_p(X)\to\pi_p(Y))$: the class $\alpha$ maps to zero in $\pi_p(Y)$, and the kernel of the induced map on $\pi_p$ is a subgroup of $\pi_p(X)$ containing $\alpha$. This is assertion (ii). [F4, step 2.2]

4.1 The connecting homomorphism restricted to this free cyclic group is determined by $\partial\iota=\pm\alpha$, so its image is the subgroup generated by $\alpha$: the trivial subgroup when $\alpha=0$, and the cyclic subgroup generated by $\alpha$ otherwise (which may be finite when $\alpha$ has finite order). By step 3.1 the kernel of $\pi_p(X)\to\pi_p(Y)$ equals that image, and the first isomorphism theorem gives $\pi_p(Y)\cong\pi_p(X)/\langle\alpha\rangle$. This is assertion (iii). [F4, step 2.2, step 2.3, algebra] ∎
