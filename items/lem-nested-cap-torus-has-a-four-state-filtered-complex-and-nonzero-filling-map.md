## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Give $T=\mathbb R^2/(\mathbb Z\times4\mathbb Z)$ the orientation $-dx\wedge dy$ and the integrable complex structure $-i$. Set $\alpha=\{y=0\}$. A lift of $\beta$, with period $(-1,0)$, is the polygonal arc through the following ordered points:
$$\begin{aligned}(0,0),(0,1/10),(1/8,3/10),(1/4,1/10),(1/4,0),(1/4,-1/10),(3/8,-3/10),(1/2,-1/10),(1/2,0),\\(1/2,1/10),(1/2,1),(-1/4,1),(-1/4,1/10),(-1/4,0),(-1/4,-1/10),(-1/4,-1),(-1,-1),(-1,-1/10),(-1,0).\end{aligned}$$
Round only its corners away from $y=0$ in disjoint sufficiently small disks, preserving the reflection $R(x,y)=(1/2-x,-y)$ and leaving the four vertical crossings unchanged. Write
$$a=(0,0),\quad b=(1/4,0),\quad d=(1/2,0),\quad c=(-1/4,0),\qquad w=(1/8,1/10),\quad z=(3/8,-1/10).$$
The two primitive attaching circles define $S^1\times S^2$. For this literal doubly-pointed datum, the actual integrable Maslov-index-one counts are regular and the full differential, including all positive $z$ multiplicities, is
$$\partial b=d+Ua,\qquad\partial a=\partial c=\partial d=0.$$
There are six contributing translation orbits before reduction modulo two: the two short bigons and two pairs of longer bigons. The relative Alexander grading is $A(a)=1,A(b)=A(c)=0,A(d)=-1$ after the explicitly specified origin $A(b)=0$. The actual hat homology has basis $[a],[c]$; its top relative-filtration class $[a]$ survives in total homology.

Add the circle $\delta=\{x=5/8\}$. It meets $\beta$ once and $\alpha$ once. The actual zero-$w$ triangle map with the unique beta-delta input sends $a$ to the unique alpha-delta generator $e$ with coefficient one and sends $b,c,d$ to zero. Its sole counted triangle has $n_z=1$. In particular the induced total hat map is nonzero on $[a]$ and zero on $[c]$.

This Statement is a calculation of the specified diagram, its relative origin and its actual filling map. Identification of its doubly-pointed knot with a particular link knotification, absolute Seifert centering or a crossing-change surgery presentation requires the corresponding geometric identification; none is inferred from the four-state matrix.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The displayed periodic polygon, small paired smoothings, both marked points, the lower-alpha/upper-beta strip convention, and negative triangle inputs alpha-beta and beta-delta.

[F1] The scalar strip operator in an actual global complex frame with lower real line and upper line $e^{i\theta(s)}\mathbb R$ has index $\lfloor\theta_-/\pi\rfloor-\lfloor\theta_+/\pi\rfloor$. Actual constant-angle ends have the full convergent Fourier expansions. ([[lem-scalar-totally-real-strip-boundary-winding-index]], [[lem-product-integrable-transverse-strip-end-exponential-asymptotics]])

[F2] Perron solves continuous Dirichlet data on regular bounded plane domains, and the Hopf boundary lemma holds when the boundary derivative is established. ([[thm-perron-solves-dirichlet-on-regular-plane-domains]], [[thm-hopf-boundary-point-lemma-for-the-laplacian]])

[F3] The compact real disk problem with tangent-circle boundary is onto, with the three-dimensional disk-automorphism kernel. Constant transverse line ends have actual bounded inverses. The three reflected tangent evaluations eliminate that kernel for a Jordan triangle with three convex corners. ([[lem-scalar-disk-riemann-hilbert-fredholm-index]], [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]], [[lem-local-torus-blowup-triangles-have-two-nonzero-spin-components]])

## Proof

1.1 The periodic beta curve is embedded. Its short upper cap lies in $0\le x\le1/4,0\le y\le3/10$ and its short lower cap in $1/4\le x\le1/2,-3/10\le y\le0$. Its long upper cap bounds the rectangle-like region $-1/4\le x\le1/2,0\le y\le1$, containing precisely the short upper cap; its long lower cap bounds $-1\le x\le-1/4,-1\le y\le0$, containing precisely the translated short lower cap. These descriptions verify disjointness of all arcs and their integer translates away from their prescribed consecutive endpoints. Vertical translates by four are disjoint. The indicated corner disks can be chosen smaller than the finite positive separations and paired by $R$; round by a fixed smooth monotone interpolation inside each disk. Thus no intersection or region incidence changes. Beta has primitive homology $-[\alpha]$, while both are primitive meridians. Gluing the corresponding solid tori identifies meridian to meridian: writing each as $D^2\times S^1$ and doubling the disk factor gives $S^2\times S^1$. The four intersections and all source conventions are therefore literal. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Name the short upper and lower bigon regions $I_0,I_1$. The long upper cap minus $I_0$ is a disk region $Q_+$; the long lower cap minus its translated $I_1$ is a disk region $Q_-$. The remaining component $L$ is an annulus and contains height $3/2$. Directly following full-circle edges gives the periodic chains $n(Q_+-Q_-)+k\Sigma$. For an actual strip, lift its disk to the plane. Its boundary has heights between $-11/10$ and $11/10$ and its ends have height zero. The maximum principle applied to its harmonic height, on expanding truncated strips and using its actual end convergence, bounds its entire image by those heights. Its degree at the stated point of $L$ is consequently zero. Hence $k=0$ in every difference of actual domains. This also excludes every nonzero nonnegative periodic direction, since $Q_+-Q_-$ has opposite coefficients. Nonconstant same-end strips are absent: their nonnegative domain would be such a periodic chain; zero domain has area and therefore energy zero. [given, F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Solve the remaining finite boundary-jump equations. The complete nonzero nonnegative domain list with $n_L=0$ is $$\begin{array}{c|c|c}(\text{input},\text{output})&D&(n_w,n_z)\\\hline(b,a)&I_0&(1,0)\\(b,d)&I_1&(0,1)\\(a,c)&I_1+Q_+,\ I_1+Q_-&(0,1)\\(d,c)&I_0+Q_+,\ I_0+Q_-&(1,0)\\(b,c)&I_0+I_1+Q_+,\ I_0+I_1+Q_-&(1,1).\end{array}$$ Here is an explicit way to reproduce the elimination without assuming domain-class injectivity. Lift an endpoint to the displayed period, follow the chosen beta arc to an integer translate of the other endpoint, and close by the horizontal alpha segment. Its signed local winding at $w,z,Q_+,Q_-$ is its region coefficient. Increasing the number of beta periods adds $(0,0,1,-1)$ in those four coefficients. The nonnegative interval therefore has at most two consecutive integers. For the two adjacent short arcs it has one entry, and for paths ending at $c$ it has the two entries shown; all opposite orientations have a negative $I_0$ or $I_1$ coefficient, and the remaining pair $a,d$ has one of these negative. These assertions also follow immediately by reading the four cap boundaries in step 1.1. For example the two $a$-to-$c$ polygons use beta paths $c\to d\to b\to a$ in the displayed lift and $c\to a-1$; their horizontal returns contain none of the intermediate beta intersections. The two $d$-to-$c$ polygons use $c\to d$ and $c\to a-1\to b-1\to d-1$. This specifies all six candidate bigons as actual Jordan boundaries, including their winding signs in the surface orientation. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Establish their actual conformal parametrizations without a full-choice mapping theorem. For any one of these bounded Jordan disks $\Omega$ choose an interior point $p$. Its boundary is smooth apart from its two right-angle corners, and is straight near those corners. Exterior-circle and sector barriers show that it is regular for [F2]. Solve the continuous boundary problem $h|_{\partial\Omega}=\log|\zeta-p|$ and put $G=h-\log|\zeta-p|$. The minimum principle on punctured domains gives $G>0$ inside and $G=0$ on the boundary. A conjugate of $G$ exists locally; its increment about $p$ is minus $2\pi$, so $F=\exp(-G-iG^*)$ is single-valued, holomorphic, and has exactly one simple zero, at $p$. It tends uniformly to the unit circle on the boundary. Consequently $F$ is proper to the disk, and the argument principle gives degree one: the only preimage of zero is its simple zero, and proper holomorphic degree is constant. Thus $F$ is biholomorphic. At a smooth edge its harmonic boundary remainder is smooth: exterior-circle barriers give a Lipschitz bound and then a bounded interior gradient; flattening the edge and testing tangential difference quotients with $-D_{-h}(\eta^2D_hv)$ gives all second tangential derivatives and their mixed derivatives in $L^2$, and the uniformly elliptic equation recovers the second normal derivative. Differentiate, use nested cutoffs and repeat to obtain every Sobolev order. Finite reflected extensions with matching derivatives and the Fourier Cauchy--Schwarz inequality give every continuous derivative. This uses only separable Hilbert weak subsequences under Countable Choice. Hopf now gives nonzero edge normal derivative. At a straight corner, the power $\zeta^{\pi/\omega}$ changes its sector to a half-disk; odd reflection of the zero-boundary harmonic function gives the corresponding positive corner power and all differentiated end estimates. The boundary values of $F$ therefore parametrize its two edges and distinct corners. A disk automorphism puts those corners at the two strip ends, giving a finite-energy strip, unique up to real translation. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 Verify the actual filling triangle, not just the rank of a possible homology map. The vertical delta circle has its unique beta intersection at $q=(-3/8,-1)$ and its unique alpha intersection $e=(-3/8,0)$. Lift $a$ to $(-1,0)$. The beta path $q\to a-1$, the alpha segment $a-1\to e$, and the delta segment $e\to q$ bound a positive Jordan triangle for the surface orientation. It contains $z$ once and no translate of $w$. To check completeness, allow every beta path from $q$ to a lift of each state and close it by its alpha segment and the fixed delta segment. Height again lies between $-11/10$ and $11/10$, so a positive domain has zero coefficient on the exterior annulus. Changing the beta period adds $Q_+-Q_-$. Delta subdivides the long-cap regions; the displayed $a$ triangle has coefficient zero on one delta-cut piece of $Q_-$ and on $Q_+$, so both nonzero signs of that periodic addition create a negative coefficient. The finite nonnegative list therefore consists of this $a$ triangle, the analogous $b$ path with marks $(1,1)$, and the analogous $d$ path with marks $(1,0)$; there is none for $c$. These are obtained by following the consecutive displayed vertices from $q$ towards $a-1,b-1,d-1$ respectively. Thus the $a$ triangle is the only zero-$w$ positive domain. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Check both index and onto-ness before counting. If $u_s-iu_t=0$, put $v(s,t)=u(-s,t)$, so $v$ is an ordinary holomorphic strip with horizontal lower alpha boundary and curved upper beta boundary; this conjugates the actual real operators and reverses their endpoint labels consistently. Write $\tau=v_s$. On an upper boundary interval with smooth tangent angle $\theta$, choose a compactly supported smooth extension of $\theta$ to a line; its holomorphic Cauchy integral has imaginary boundary trace $\theta$. Multiplication by its exponential inverse makes $\tau$ real, so Schwarz reflection proves isolated boundary zeros of integer order. The usual argument principle on a truncated strip, with clockwise half-disk indents at these zeros and [F1]'s end expansions, gives $$\mu=1+k_-+k_++Z_{\partial}+2Z_{\mathrm{int}},\qquad k_\pm,Z_{\partial},Z_{\mathrm{int}}\ge0.$$ Indeed, writing $\theta_\pm=a_\pm\pi+r_\pm$, $0<r_\pm<\pi$, the leading rates are $r_-+k_-\pi$ and $r_+-\pi-k_+\pi$; their substitution into $2\pi Z_{\mathrm{int}}=\lambda_+-\lambda_-+\theta_--\theta_+-\pi Z_{\partial}$ gives the displayed identity using [F1]. A Jordan conformal bigon has no derivative zero and the minimal two corner modes, hence actual index one. Its kernel quotient $\xi/\tau$ is holomorphic, real on both boundary edges and bounded at both ends by the same exact Fourier modes. Its imaginary part is zero by the maximum principle on expanding rectangles, so the quotient is a real constant. Thus its real kernel is the translation line, and the actual Fredholm index one gives zero cokernel. This proves regularity and count one for each of the six Jordan bigons. The last two domains in step 3.1 have index two: they are concatenations of the actual $b$-to-$a$ and $a$-to-$c$ classes. In a global scalar frame the tangent-angle floor difference in [F1] telescopes under that concatenation, so their indices add. Any class with the same domain has the same lifted boundary arcs up to their full-circle period, which was already fixed in step 3.1; the contractible plane adds no sphere class. It has that same index. The table therefore excludes every other actual index-one map. [F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Each short bigon contributes once, while the two $a$-to-$c$ orbits have identical weights one and the two $d$-to-$c$ orbits have identical weights $U$. Modulo two these pairs cancel separately, proving exactly $\partial b=d+Ua$ and the three zero columns. Both marks have been counted in step 3.1; no zero-$z$ restriction has been made. The relative grading equations along the actual classes give $A(b)-A(a)=-1$, $A(b)-A(d)=1$, $A(a)-A(c)=1$ and $A(d)-A(c)=-1$. With the explicitly stipulated origin $A(b)=0$ these are precisely the four displayed values. Setting $U=0$ gives the single nonzero arrow $b\mapsto d$; hence $[a],[c]$ are a basis of total hat homology and the top relative-filtration cycle $a$ survives. These are finite algebraic conclusions from actual counts, not an absolute grading normalization. [step 3.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Construct its marked disk map by the Green-function argument of step 4.1, with its three convex right-angle corners, and assign its three distinct boundary points by a disk automorphism. The actual operator is an isomorphism. To see this under exactly the given smooth-edge hypotheses, first solve arbitrary forcing on the three constant-angle ends by [F3] and cut off, leaving compact forcing. Write the remaining section as $f'\eta$. The compact tangent-circle problem in [F3] is onto. Near the three corners its forcing is zero; reflection bounds the three tangent evaluations by Cauchy estimates. Evaluation on its kernel $a+ib\zeta-\overline a\zeta^2$ is invertible at three distinct boundary points, since the three resulting columns are $1,\cos\theta,\sin\theta$. Subtract that unique kernel section. The vanishing evaluations give exactly the positive corner powers of $f'\eta$, so its cylindrical Sobolev norm is bounded. A homogeneous actual section similarly divides by $f'$ to a compact tangent-circle kernel section vanishing at all three points, and is zero. This proves onto-ness, kernel zero and actual index zero without an illegitimate $H^1$ point trace. It therefore counts one. The beta-delta and alpha-delta pairs have one generator and no nonconstant holomorphic disks: their slope determinants are one, so every pair disk lifts with zero boundary winding, contracts in the plane and has zero area. The zero-$w$ triangle map thus sends $a$ to $e$ and the other three states to zero by step 4.2. It is an actual chain map, also directly since the only differential column $b\mapsto d$ has zero image. Its induced map on the actual total hat homology is the asserted nonzero projection. This completes the literal local operation and makes no additional knot-admission or absolute-centering assertion. [F3, step 4.1, step 6.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
