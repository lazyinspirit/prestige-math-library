## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $C$ be a pinched regular neighborhood of a connected convergent infinite disk-like capped tower in a topological four-manifold. Its first story has grope height at least three. The tower is given by countably many parameterized finite standard tower models with their attaching maps, product handle charts and normal framings. Convergence means that the diameters of components in successive stories tend to zero. The attaching face is $A=S^1\times D^2$, with a prescribed product normal germ.

Assume the following finite-construction inputs, relative to the unchanged first-story body and its attaching collar. Caps may be replaced inside their neighborhoods to raise the finite grope height arbitrarily. A sufficiently high top story may be replaced by a two-story capped tower, with arbitrarily small components, whose first story has any prescribed finite grope height and whose second has grope height at least four. Upper stories of a finite uncapped tower with at least two stories may be contracted to cap its first story, without changing that first-story body. These operations can be iterated to obtain a convergent tower. A pinched regular neighborhood of such a tower is its body-neighborhood endpoint compactification: in a dual model, each component of the intersection of the nested dual two-handles is identified to one point. All these operations are supplied in the given standard models, with their stated support and parameterized attaching collars.

Then a smaller such neighborhood $C'\subset C$ admits a singular quotient model $p:D^4\to C'$, a closed partial parameterization domain $P\subset D^4$, an embedding $q:P\to C'$, and onto maps $\alpha:D^4\to Q$, $\beta:C'\to Q$ to a common compact metric space, with $\beta q=\alpha|_P$. The only nonsingleton fibers of $p$ are arcs of an embedded collar of a closed subset of its outer boundary; extending that collar over its unchanged complementary face gives a possibly wild collar of $S^3$. The restriction of $q$ to $\partial D^4$ is a homeomorphism onto the distinguished boundary $\partial C'$.

Both quotient-fiber families are null, meaning that only finitely many fibers have diameter at least any given positive number. The closures in $Q$ of their nontrivial-fiber image sets have empty interior. Every nontrivial $\alpha$-fiber is a tame closed four-ball or a flat embedded two-disk united with a framed regular neighborhood $S^1\times D^3$ of its boundary circle. For $\partial Q=\alpha(\partial D^4)$, both boundary maps are homeomorphisms onto $\partial Q$, with $\alpha^{-1}(\partial Q)=\partial D^4$ and $\beta^{-1}(\partial Q)=\partial C'$; all those fibers are singletons. A smaller closed product collar $G$ of $A$ has a neighborhood contained in $P$ on which $q$ is the prescribed regular product identification and both quotient maps have singleton fibers. The constructions take place in $C$, preserve the attaching normal germ, and require no preservation of an arbitrary interior marked set.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Countable Choice is assumed as in [[def-countable-choice]]. The proof uses it, if needed, only for independent chart packages indexed by the fixed countable family of input tower pieces. Subsequent dependent stages use least admissible finite template codes, as specified below.

[A1] The parameterized tower, convergence, endpoint compactification and relative finite operations are the inputs stated above. They are hypotheses of this lemma; the lemma does not establish their availability for an arbitrary disk problem.

## Proof

**Given:** The parameterized convergent tower, its framed attaching face, the relative finite operations and Countable Choice.

1.1 Work in the standard dual model $D^4=D^2\times D^2$. The complement of a regular neighborhood of a finite tower body is a finite union of dual two-handles $H_{i,m}\cong D^2\times D^2$, with boundary solid tori $\partial_0H_{i,m}=D^2\times S^1$ on the outer face. Replacing caps puts subsequent dual handles inside their predecessors. Thus, for $K=\bigcap_i\bigcup_mH_{i,m}$, the endpoint-compactification input gives a quotient identifying each component of $K$ separately. It fixes $A$ and is a homeomorphism on the outer face onto its frontier. We arrange the components of $K$ to be collar arcs. [A1, construct]

1.2 A zero-surgery on a disk has two dual disks, obtained by tracing the two sides of its surgery band through the normal product. Their boundary curves form a Bing double in the boundary solid torus. A further grope stage repeats this replacement inside the previous dual handle. To control its size, consider a core whose angular projection travels monotonically out and back a length $r$, with its remaining joining arc in an angular interval of length $\varepsilon$. Place the two Bing clasps near the ends of the outgoing interval and slide one inward by $\varepsilon$. Parallel outgoing and returning strands then give, in each component, a monotone out-and-back interval of length at most $r-\varepsilon$, with the remaining turn in an $\varepsilon$ interval. Opposite normal coordinates at the clasps keep the curves disjoint; the slides take place inside the tubular neighborhood and preserve the Bing-double pattern. [step 1.1, construct]

1.3 The first Bing replacement has its monotone length less than half the angular circumference. Repeating step 1.2 more than that half-circumference divided by $\varepsilon$ places every component in an $\varepsilon$ angular interval. Radially compress its normal disks. Its whole boundary solid torus now lies in an arbitrarily small ball centered on $\{0\}\times S^1$. Finitely many components permit one common finite height. The additional finger moves producing tower caps occur inside these handles, so their dual tori retain the size bound. This gives the finite small-boundary-dual operation. [step 1.2, construct]

1.4 Choose dual handles and collars $c_i$ inductively. On each $\partial_0H_{i,m}$, the collar is the standard collar inside that handle. Require $c_{i+1}=c_i$ except over $\bigcup_m\partial_0H_{i,m}\times[0,2^{-i}]$, and require its modified image there to remain inside the old handle. Also require both $\partial_0H_{i,m}$ and $H_{i,m}(D^2\times(1/2+2^{-i})D^2)$ to have diameter less than $2^{-i}$. The half-radius here is the normal coordinate inside a dual handle. [step 1.3, construct]

1.5 For the induction step, retain the first $n$ old stories. Uniform continuity in each of its finitely many last dual handles gives $\delta>0$ such that model sets of diameter less than $\delta$ have image diameter less than $2^{-n-1}$. Use step 1.3 to choose a sufficiently high first story in a two-story replacement; take its second-story height at least four, and its target components small enough for convergence. Match new collars with old ones for times $[2^{-n},1]$, compress their inner normal disks to meet the larger-half-radius bound, and interpolate only below time $2^{-n}$. All changes remain in the old handles. This proves the inductive requirements and constructs a smaller neighborhood $C'\subset C$. [A1, step 1.4, construct]

1.6 A nested sequence of boundary tori has a single intersection point $y$ by the diameter bounds. The collar changes are supported in shrinking time intervals, and their inner endpoints lie in the nested small half-handles. Consequently the collars have a continuous embedded limit $c$, including these endpoints. Distinct addresses give disjoint nested half-handles, so distinct endpoints and distinct collar arcs. The intersection of the corresponding nested full handles is exactly $c(\{y\}\times[0,1])$. Hence the quotient $p:D^4\to C'$ has only singleton fibers and these arcs. The inner end of this collar may be wild. The closed address set lies in the outer solid-torus face; over its complement the collar extends by the unchanged regular coordinates. [step 1.5, construct]

2.1 Here is an explicit finite initial collar model, including the root block. Round $D^4$ and split $S^3=V\cup W$, with $V\cong D^2\times S^1$ and $W\cong S^1\times D^2$, the attaching face. Choose a full boundary collar and an inner open ball $B$. In coordinates it is $F(u,t)=(r_0+(1-r_0)t)u$, where $u\in S^3$, $0\le t\le1$ and $0<r_0<1$. Choose its $W$ side in the unchanged first-story body, disjoint from the finitely many first dual handles. This is possible since their attaching tori lie compactly in $\operatorname{int}V$ and their interiors avoid the attaching face. [step 1.1, construct]

2.2 To make the collar choice explicit before normalizing coordinates, take a continuous radial width $a:S^3\to(0,1)$ sufficiently close to 1 over $W$, and use $F_a(u,t)=(a(u)+(1-a(u))t)u$. Its complementary region is the star-shaped ball $\{\rho u:0\le\rho<a(u)\}$. The raywise homeomorphism linear from $[0,r_0]$ to $[0,a(u)]$ and from $[r_0,1]$ to $[a(u),1]$ fixes $S^3$ and transports all handles, maps and collars simultaneously to the uniform $F$ coordinates. Thus disjointness from the $W$ collar is preserved. No comparison of independently selected collar systems is needed. [step 2.1, construct]

2.3 Set $T_{\varnothing}=V$, $J_{\varnothing}=[1/3,2/3]$, and $R=F(V\times J_{\varnothing})$. After rounding corners, $R\cong S^1\times D^3$. A longitude $\lambda$ of $V$ is the meridian of $W$. Choose its standard meridian disk $\Delta=\{a\}\times D^2\subset W$ and put $\Delta_0=F(\Delta\times\{4/9\})$. Then $R\cap\Delta_0=\partial\Delta_0$, a section $S^1\times\{y\}$ of $\partial R$ with its product framing. Thus $E_{\varnothing}=R\cup\Delta_0$ is precisely a flat disk united with a regular neighborhood of its boundary, and is disjoint from $\overline B$. [step 2.2, construct]

2.4 For $0<\eta<1/3$, the protected attaching germ $G=F(W\times[1-\eta,1])$ misses $E_{\varnothing}$ and $\overline B$. All later nonempty-word tori lie in $\operatorname{int}V$ and their graph heights lie in the upper or lower thirds of $J_{\varnothing}$, so they miss the root block and root cap. The first-level tori are compactly contained in $\operatorname{int}V$; a neighborhood of the corner of $G$ can therefore be retained too. All finite models keep the prescribed attaching coordinates on this neighborhood. [step 2.3, construct]

2.5 The support assertion needed for later insertions is stronger than equality on an outer face. Let $L$ be the unchanged first-story body, the complement of the interiors of its first dual handles. Contracting upper stories takes place inside those handles; replacing caps, raising their heights, and the subsequent nested collar adjustments also take place there. Define each new map to be the old map on $L$ and the new singular map inside each handle, matching the prescribed whole common collar. The maps glue continuously and their images meet only on these collars: new pieces lie inside old handle images, while the old map on the complement is injective and its nonsingleton fibers lie in nested subhandles. Therefore an insertion agrees with the old map on the whole $W$ collar and its image, not merely on its boundary. New images in handles are disjoint from the old $W$ image. In particular the root cap and its neighborhood keep their old coordinates. [A1, step 1.5, step 2.1, construct]

3.1 Index the construction by finite words $s$ in $\{0,2\}$; $ds$ deletes the last symbol and $|s|$ is the length. Write $.s$ for its ternary value. Let $J_s=[.s1,.s2]$, with lower and upper thirds $J_s^-=[.s,.s1]$ and $J_s^+=[.s2,.s(0:2)]$. Choose finite families of disjoint solid tori $T_{s,m}$, for nonempty $s$, in the appropriate parent component. Require diameter less than $2^{-|s|}$ and require the eventual image of each controlling parent product $T_{ds,m}\times J_s$ to have diameter less than $2^{-|ds|}$. [step 2.5, construct]

3.2 Each such dual torus is a regular neighborhood of the boundary of a framed immersed disk in its parent, with disjoint double arcs and no triple points. For the one-finger model, trace its two parallel boundary sheets into the boundary collar: their intersections are exactly the arcs across the finger band. Different bands have disjoint product neighborhoods. Higher finite stages insert copies in disjoint old tori, preserving the same property. This proves the disk description for these dual families, rather than asserting it for arbitrary links. [step 1.2, construct]

3.3 Define $P=D^4\setminus\bigl(B\cup\bigcup_{s,m}\operatorname{int}(T_{s,m}\times J_s)\bigr)$, including the empty-word root block among the deletions. This is closed. The $V$ part is the closure of the upper and lower pieces $(T_s\setminus T_{s2})\times J_s^+$ and $(T_s\setminus T_{s0})\times J_s^-$. The $W$ collar is retained. The empty-word cap is $\Delta_0$; the immersed disks of step 3.2 refer to nonempty words. [step 2.3, step 3.1, construct]

3.4 Choose levels $n(i)$ with $n(i+1)\ge n(i)+2$, sufficiently deep that their dual boundary tori and their images have diameter less than $2^{-i}$. Initially use only the words $2^i$. Set $T_{2^i}$ to be these boundary tori. The restriction of $p$ to the corresponding upper-third region is injective: its closure meets each singular collar arc only at its upper endpoint. On the retained $W$ collar use its regular product map. [step 1.6, step 2.5, construct]

3.5 The correct truncated handle is $H'_s=H_{n(i),m}\setminus(T_s\times(.s1,1])$ for $s=2^i$: it removes the outer collar, not the initial interval. Its new outer face has exterior collar $T_s\times J_s$. Initialize $n(0)=0$, $H_0=D^4$, $T_{\varnothing}=V$. Then $H'_{\varnothing}$ is a disk product with its outer $V$ collar beyond time $1/3$ shaved off; it contains the lower third. Rounding is an inward displacement of the $V$ boundary joined through the corner collar to the unchanged $W$ boundary, hence again a disk product. Choose $n(1)\ge2$. The difference $H'_{\varnothing}\setminus H'_2$ is an uncapped tower with at least two stories. [step 3.3, step 3.4, construct]

3.6 Contract that difference's upper stories, cap its first story, and insert a convergent singularly parameterized tower. By step 2.5 its map $p_{1,\varnothing}$ equals $p$ on the unchanged first-story body, including the retained $W$ region. Choose its deep dual levels and put their tori in the lower-third intervals, giving the words $02^j$. Their upper-third pieces define $q$ on the root lower-third first-zero pieces. They match the old map on the common collars and their images avoid the retained $W$ image. This supplies the actual empty-word base of the recursion. [A1, step 2.5, step 3.5, construct]

3.7 For each other $s=2^i$, the difference $H'_{ds}\setminus H'_s$ is likewise a neighborhood of an uncapped unconnected tower of height $n(i)-n(i-1)\ge2$, on which the old map is a homeomorphism. Contract upper stories and make the same relative insertion, keeping its first-story body. The resulting maps extend the old map on common collars and give all remaining words with one zero. Repeat inside the separate inner tower components. At stage $m$, this gives all pieces whose words have at most $m$ zeros; new images lie in mutually disjoint inner components, away from earlier images. The finite-stage maps are therefore injective and compatible. [A1, step 2.5, step 3.6, construct]

3.8 The dyadic image bounds give a unique continuous extension $q$ to the closure $P$. A new point has a nested torus and ternary-interval address. If its word has finitely many zeros it already belongs to a finite-stage domain. A genuinely new address therefore has infinitely many zeros. At its first zero its image enters an inner component disjoint from the earlier image, and repetition shows it misses every finite-stage image. Two different new addresses either separate at a zero insertion, giving disjoint inner components, or first separate in their tori and then separate at their next zero insertion. Thus their images differ. The extension is injective; compactness of $P$ makes $q$ a homeomorphism onto its image. It retains the prescribed attaching collar. [step 3.1, step 3.7, construct]

4.1 For each nonempty $s$, let $D_{s,m}$ be its immersed parameter disk. Lift it to a graph of a height $e_{s,m}$ taking values in $(.s0,.s22)$. Require height $.s11$ where its sheet meets its own boundary torus, and constant nonendpoint Cantor heights where it meets other boundary tori. Inductively, the earlier graphs have finitely many such constant heights over a given parent. Choose $.s01<a<.s1$ and $.s2<b<.s22$ enclosing none of them, and make the new heights lie in $(a,b)$. This separates them from the old graphs. [step 3.2, step 3.7, construct]

4.2 At each double arc there are exactly two sheet preimages and disjoint small sheet neighborhoods. Give their heights $.s11+\varepsilon\chi$ and $.s11-\varepsilon\chi$, with $\chi$ positive on the intersection and tapering off in these neighborhoods, and with $\varepsilon$ smaller than the clearance from $a,b$. Different double arcs are disjoint. Near their ends use the prescribed constants on the disjoint boundary-torus neighborhoods, tapering only once the sheets separate. Away from these neighborhoods the sheets were already disjoint. Thus all lifted disks are flat embedded disks with disjoint graphs, meeting their own boundary-neighborhood blocks in the required attaching annuli and avoiding all other blocks. [step 4.1, construct]

4.3 Put $E_{s,m}=(T_{s,m}\times J_s)\cup\operatorname{graph}(e_{s,m})$. Together with $E_{\varnothing}$ and $\overline B$, these are pairwise disjoint compact sets in $\operatorname{int}D^4$. Each non-root $E_{s,m}$ is a flat disk union a framed regular neighborhood of its boundary. The family is null: a member lies over its dyadically small parent torus and in a ternary interval tending to zero, with only finitely many members at bounded word length. It misses a neighborhood of the protected attaching germ. [step 2.3, step 2.4, step 4.2, construct]

5.1 Let $\alpha:D^4\to Q$ collapse $\overline B$, $E_{\varnothing}$ and each $E_{s,m}$ separately, leaving all other fibers singletons. This decomposition is upper semicontinuous. Indeed, from an open neighborhood $U$ of one compact fiber remove all fibers meeting $U$ but not contained in it. Each removed fiber meets the complement of $U$. Any sequence of distinct removed fibers has diameter tending to zero, so its accumulation points lie outside $U$. Their union is therefore relatively closed in $U$; what remains is an open saturated neighborhood of the chosen fiber inside $U$. Distinct compact fibers first have disjoint ordinary neighborhoods, then disjoint saturated ones. Hence $Q$ is compact Hausdorff. [step 4.3, construct]

5.2 For completeness, compact Hausdorff alone does not imply metrizable. Let $V$ range over finite unions from a fixed countable base of $D^4$. The sets $Q\setminus\alpha(D^4\setminus V)$ are open because $\alpha$ is closed. For a point in an open set $O$, cover its compact fiber by one such finite union $V\subset\alpha^{-1}(O)$. The resulting set is a neighborhood contained in $O$. Thus $Q$ has a countable base and is metrizable. [step 5.1, construct]

5.3 On $q(P)$ define $\beta=\alpha q^{-1}$. Each component of $C'\setminus q(P)$ has frontier corresponding to $\partial B$ or to the boundary of a deleted product block. Its frontier is sent by $\alpha$ to one point. Extend $\beta$ constantly over that component. This is continuous across each frontier. At an accumulation of distinct components the controlling dyadic image diameters tend to zero, which gives continuity there too. Explicitly, the inverse image of a closed set is closed on $q(P)$ together with whole complementary components whose frontiers belong to it; an accumulation point outside those components lies in $q(P)$ and has the same limiting value by the diameter bound. Every $\alpha$-fiber meets $P$, including the ball fiber through $\partial B$, so $\beta$ is onto and $\beta q=\alpha|_P$. [step 3.8, step 5.1, construct]

5.4 A nontrivial $\beta$-fiber consists of the corresponding complementary-component closure together with the $q$-image of its graph part in $P$. Apart from finitely many initial exceptions, it lies in the controlling image $q(T_{ds}\times J_s)$, of dyadically small diameter. Thus its fiber family is null. The common exceptional values are the countably many values associated with the ball and the disk-with-boundary-neighborhood sets. [step 3.1, step 5.3, construct]

5.5 These exceptional values have nowhere dense closure. An open quotient set containing such a value has preimage containing the whole associated exceptional set. In its parent product block remove the finitely many same-level tori and disk projections, together with the finitely many earlier graphs. A small open ball remains arbitrarily near that set: the child tori are proper disjoint interior sub-tori, the disk projections have dimension two in the three-dimensional parent, and the earlier graphs have empty interior. Choose a smaller ball whose closure misses these sets. All descendants lie in the removed child blocks, so this ball is disjoint from every exceptional fiber and its image gives an open quotient subset avoiding their closure. A neighborhood containing only an accumulation value also contains an exceptional value and reduces to this case. The finite initial ball and root fibers have exterior collar points, where the same argument applies. [step 4.3, step 5.4, construct]

5.6 Put $\partial Q=\alpha(\partial D^4)$. All nontrivial $\alpha$-fibers are interior, so its boundary is saturated and has singleton fibers. The map $q$ takes the attaching face to the prescribed attaching face and its complementary boundary face homeomorphically to the outer frontier, hence takes $\partial D^4$ homeomorphically onto $\partial C'$. Each nontrivial $\beta$-fiber has compact closure in the interior: its frontier is the image of an interior deleted-block boundary or $\partial B$, together with an interior graph piece. Consequently $\beta^{-1}(\partial Q)=\partial C'$ with singleton fibers. Distinct exceptional fibers may accumulate at this boundary, but no individual exceptional fiber contains a boundary point. The boundary restrictions of both quotient maps are homeomorphisms. [step 1.6, step 3.8, step 5.3, construct]

5.7 The $W$ collar of step 2.4 is retained by every insertion, and every exceptional set misses a neighborhood of its smaller closed germ $G$. There $q$ is the original product identification and both quotient maps have singleton fibers. All target replacements occurred in $C$ and in specified old handle images, so the support stays in $C$ and the attaching normal framing is retained. This proves the geometric and quotient conclusions. [step 2.4, step 2.5, step 5.6, construct]

6.1 We account for choices explicitly. The given tower has a fixed countable family of parameterized finite pieces. If chart packages are not already included, Countable Choice selects one finite package independently for each of those already specified pieces. Their attaching maps stay part of the input. All subsequent work is in standard finite PL models transported through these fixed maps; no triangulation of the ambient topological four-manifold or independent PL compatibility of its chart transitions is asserted. [F1, A1, construct]

6.2 Fix enumerations of finite words, integers, positive dyadic numbers, rational points and rational PL subdivisions in each finite model. Include finite templates for the dual surgery disks, Bing clasps and strand slides, parallel-sheet finger bands, collar interpolation, radial compression, corner rounding and the two sheet-height perturbations. These templates are structurally the identity on the prescribed unchanged bodies and matching faces. Thus exact matching is retained by composing the templates with the old maps, rather than approximating arbitrary boundary data by rational data. Finite template compositions and subdivisions have finite integer codes. [step 6.1, construct]

6.3 Every free choice above is available in this countable template family. Disjoint compact sets have positive clearance and maps on compact models are uniformly continuous, so dyadic widths and rational PL parameters can satisfy every strict separation and diameter bound. Heights and the numbers of Bing replacements are integers. The finite finger-band templates give rationally subdivisible sheet neighborhoods and PL bump functions with dyadic small amplitudes. Previous prescribed constants are retained symbolically. For nonendpoint Cantor values use finite ternary words followed by the periodic tail $020202\ldots$; this is a fixed countable dense set of nonendpoints. There are only finitely many forbidden previous constants in each relevant parent, so the required gaps and constants exist in the fixed lists. The collar width in step 2.2 can likewise be a rational PL function on a sufficiently fine fixed subdivision. [step 1.3, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 6.2, construct]

6.4 At stage $n$ choose the least finite template code satisfying its exact support, matching, containment, separation and strict diameter requirements for the already constructed finite stage. Existence follows from the finite operations and the compact-clearance argument of step 6.3. This defines a unique successor; decidability of that predicate is neither asserted nor needed. Similarly take the least admissible deeper level, common height or dyadic radius. Fixed word and component orders fix the order of operations. Ordinary functional recursion on the natural numbers therefore constructs the entire sequence without Dependent Choice. [step 6.3, construct]

6.5 No branch is selected in the nested-handle arguments: the constructions quantify over all addresses and use their unique limits. The continuous extensions, exceptional sets, quotient and extended map $\beta$ are uniquely determined. The countable-base metrization can use least finite-basis codes for all normal shrinkings, so it introduces no unspecified dependent selection. Thus this construction uses no general Dependent Choice or arbitrary-index Choice. With parameterized input pieces its subsequent selections are definable least-code selections; any initial choice of their independent fixed-piece chart packages uses precisely Countable Choice. Together with the preceding construction this proves the lemma. [step 5.7, step 6.1, step 6.4] ∎
