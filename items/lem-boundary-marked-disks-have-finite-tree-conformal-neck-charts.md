## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. For $n\ge3$, let $\mathcal R_n$ be the ordered configurations of $n$ cyclically labelled points on the boundary of the upper half-plane, modulo real fractional-linear biholomorphisms preserving its orientation. Distinguish the output label. The punctured sources in this statement are these actual upper half-planes, with their standard complex structure; no uniformization assertion about an arbitrary presented complex disk is needed.

The space $\mathcal R_n$ has a compactification by finite planar rooted trees of such sources. Every vertex has at least three special boundary points, counting its incident nodes. If a tree has $b$ internal edges and vertex valences $n_v$, a neighborhood of its stratum has coordinates consisting of $\prod_v\mathcal R_{n_v}$ near the specified vertex configurations and $b$ nonnegative real parameters $\rho_e$. Its dimension is $n-3=\sum_v(n_v-3)+b$. Setting $\rho_e=0$ retains the corresponding boundary node. The compactification is a compact Hausdorff second-countable smooth manifold with corners. Its codimension-one faces are exactly the two-vertex splittings at a consecutive block of input labels; compatible splittings meet in the indicated trees.

There are actual holomorphic neck coordinates in these charts with transition $zw=-\rho_e$ and neck length $T_e=-\pi^{-1}\log\rho_e+O(1)$. On fixed vertex compact subsets all source coordinates and their derivatives converge as the incident $T_e$ tend to infinity. One can choose end coordinates, cutoffs and metrics smoothly on each chart, with uniform bounds on each fixed thick compact subset. Changes between intersecting tree charts are smooth in the nonnegative parameters. Simultaneous sewings on distinct edges commute as identifications of these actual sources. The three-marked source has no nontrivial automorphism; two-ended strips, when used as extra broken levels, retain their real translation automorphism instead.

This is source geometry. It asserts neither a holomorphic-map compactness theorem nor Fredholm regularity, perturbation coherence or analytic gluing.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The cyclic label order, output label, standard half-plane complex structure, and the orientation-preserving real fractional-linear action. A stable rooted tree means a finite planar tree with those external labels and at least three flags at each vertex.

[F1] The principal logarithm and exponential give inverse holomorphic coordinates between a half-annulus and a rectangular strip, with strip width equal to its angle divided by $\pi$. ([[thm-principal-logarithm-biholomorphism-to-the-principal-strip]])

## Proof

1.1 Put the output at infinity. Every remaining automorphism is $x\mapsto ax+b$ with $a>0$. Sending the first and last finite labels to zero and one gives a unique representative. Thus $\mathcal R_n$ is the open simplex $0<x_2<\cdots<x_{n-2}<1$, of dimension $n-3$. More generally any three distinct special points determine a unique real fractional-linear normalization: solve the three linear equations for its coefficients, whose denominators are products of nonzero point differences. This proves smooth dependence and uniqueness of every normalization used below. A fractional-linear map fixing three distinct boundary points is the identity. No translation quotient remains for a triangle. With only the two strip ends, the half-plane normalization leaves positive dilations; their logarithms are precisely real translations in a strip coordinate. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 We describe a tree chart explicitly, including its inverse. Direct every edge toward the output. At each vertex normalize its outgoing flag to infinity, its first incoming flag to zero and its last incoming flag to one. The other incoming flag positions are strictly ordered between zero and one. For a child vertex attached at the parent position $c$, insert its finite points by $x=c+\rho y$, with $\rho>0$ small. Iterate this affine formula down the finite tree; an external input acquires the resulting real coordinate. For small parameters these coordinates have the prescribed order: different parent clusters are separated by the positive minimum of their flag gaps, whereas each inserted finite cluster has diameter bounded by a fixed constant times its scale. Finally normalize the resulting external configuration by step1.1 if desired. There is no hidden scale ambiguity. For every vertex $v$, let $A_v$ be the external leaf reached by repeatedly taking its first incoming flag, and let $B_v$ be the leaf reached by taking its last incoming flag once and then first flags. In the vertex coordinate these two leaf positions are exactly zero and one, independently of all deeper scales. Normalize the actual external configuration at $A_{\rm root},B_{\rm root}$ and the output. Its vertex scales are exactly $s_v=x(B_v)-x(A_v)>0$. Recover $\rho_{v}=s_v/s_{\operatorname{parent}(v)}$ and recover every parent flag position from $(x(A_v)-x(A_{\operatorname{parent}(v)}))/s_{\operatorname{parent}(v)}$; for a leaf use its own coordinate in this formula. These inverse formulas recover every parameter, not just its leading term. They give a genuine smooth coordinate chart on the open configurations where the stated clusters are small and separated. At zero scale retain the normalized child configuration rather than divide by zero. The resulting boundary coordinate data are exactly a collection of stable vertex sources joined at their labelled flags. [given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 These charts fit smoothly and distinguish all boundary points. A refinement inserts further separated consecutive clusters inside a vertex chart. Apply precisely the same difference and ratio formulas there; on an overlap, a cluster scale is a product of the edge scales between the two normalizations, multiplied by a smooth positive ratio of separated flag differences. Positions are obtained by finite affine substitutions followed, if a different output normalization is used, by the fractional-linear formulas in step1.1. All denominators after the common scale factors are cancelled are differences of distinct flags on one limiting vertex, hence bounded away from zero on a smaller chart. The transition and its inverse consequently extend smoothly to all shared zero-scale faces. If two proposed limiting trees differ, either they have a different collapsed consecutive cluster, or some common vertex has a different normalized configuration. In the latter case a separated normalized flag difference distinguishes neighborhoods. In the former case the ratio of that cluster's diameter to the distance to the nearest point outside it tends to zero for one tree and stays bounded away from zero for the other, unless both trees refine the same cluster; in that case compare inside that common vertex and repeat. The finite trees make this recursion terminate. Thus unequal stable configurations have disjoint chart neighborhoods. This proves the Hausdorff property, and also shows that the only chart identifications at zero scale are those retaining the same labelled stable tree. Each chart is a finite-dimensional orthant chart; rational smaller boxes give a countable base because there are finitely many planar labelled trees for fixed $n$. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The neck transition is exact. At an attachment point $c$ on the parent use $z=x-c$; on the child use $w=-1/y$ near its outgoing point infinity. Both map the relevant upper half-plane sides holomorphically, and the affine insertion $x=c+\rho y$ gives $zw=-\rho$. Fix disjoint radius bounds $r$ about these node coordinates on a smaller vertex-configuration chart. The overlap is $\rho/r<|z|<r$ with the real boundary removed. Applying [F1] gives a strip of width one and length $\pi^{-1}\log(r^2/\rho)$. Changing the fixed buffered radii changes length by a bounded smooth function, giving the stated $T$. Outside these disks the vertex coordinates and all derivatives are fixed or smooth finite affine/fractional-linear substitutions; inside buffered end bands they are the displayed exact logarithmic coordinates. They therefore give the asserted convergence and uniform thick-region bounds. Pull back fixed smooth strip cutoffs and product metrics on these bands, and interpolate on a finite compact remainder by an explicit finite smooth partition. The parameter dependence is smooth wherever $\rho>0$, with the stated vertex convergence at zero. For distinct edges the annuli are disjoint on each vertex after reducing the radii; gluing them in either order imposes exactly the same relations $z_ew_e=-\rho_e$. This proves commuting sewings without any uniformization theorem. [F1, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Here are compactness and completeness of the boundary description. Given a sequence, use step1.1 to put its external inputs in $[0,1]$. Pass to convergent subsequences of this finite tuple. If some but not all consecutive points coalesce, record each maximal coalescing block and magnify it by the difference between its two extreme points. Its magnified positions again lie in $[0,1]$ with extreme points zero and one; extract their limits and repeat inside its coalescing blocks. Each new vertex separates at least two flags which were previously indistinguishable, so this procedure creates at most $n-2$ vertices and terminates after finitely many extractions. The resulting stable tree has separated vertex flag configurations, and the exact inverse formulas of step2.1 show convergence in its tree chart, including all relative scales. There is no other possible degeneration: outside these finitely many clusters all flag differences are bounded below. Conversely step2.1 realizes every stable configuration by positive small scales. Thus every sequence has a convergent subsequence in the constructed space. It is second-countable by step3.1, so sequential compactness implies compactness under Countable Choice: choose a countable subordinate subcover of an open cover from its countable base, and if no finite part covers, choose a point outside the first $j$ members for each $j$; its convergent subsequence contradicts the member containing its limit. Sequences already on boundary strata are handled by the same finite procedure on their finitely many vertex configurations and edge scales, after choosing a constant combinatorial type subsequence. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, contradiction]

4.2 The local dimension and faces follow directly from these coordinates. If the tree has $V$ vertices and $b=V-1$ internal edges, counting flags gives $\sum_v n_v=n+2b$. Therefore $\sum_v(n_v-3)+b=n+3b-3V=n-3$. Each zero scale is an independent orthant coordinate in step2.1. One zero edge separates the external inputs into a consecutive block and its complement, with the output on the root side; each side has at least two original or nodal incoming flags by stability. Conversely every such stable split is an edge of a unique two-vertex labelled tree. Several splits can occur together exactly when their consecutive clusters are nested or disjoint; these are exactly the edges of a planar rooted tree. The stratum and face assertions, including the corner intersections, are thus assertions about the actual chart coordinates, not a presumed associahedron. [given, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 All constructions used only finitely many real configurations, finite trees, explicit coordinate changes and finite subsequence extractions before the final countable-base compactness argument. They preserve every external label and every flag order. In particular they provide the source conformal parameters required when domains break, with exactly one neck parameter for each node, and no fictitious automorphism parameter on a three-marked component. These assertions concern only the specified actual sources; nothing in the construction identifies solutions of a Cauchy–Riemann equation, provides an inverse to its linearization, or establishes regularity, compactness or gluing of maps. Those are separate analytic obligations. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 3.2, algebra] ∎
