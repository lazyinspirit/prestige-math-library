## Statement

Let $n,N\ge1$ and let $P_1,\ldots,P_N$ be orthogonal projections in
$\mathbb R^n$ onto fixed finite-dimensional subspaces $V_1,\ldots,V_N$. Let
$c_1,\ldots,c_N\in\mathbb R$ be fixed. For $y\in\mathbb R^n$, define
$q_i(y):=n^{-1}\|y-P_i y\|_2^2+c_i$, choose the least index attaining
$\min_{1\le i\le N}q_i(y)$, and return its fitted vector $P_i y$. The resulting
map $\widehat m:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^n$ is Borel measurable. The index map
is Borel measurable when $\{1,\ldots,N\}$ has its discrete sigma-algebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite ordered list of fixed subspaces and their orthogonal
projections, and fixed real penalties.

[F1] Each finite-dimensional orthogonal projection is linear and continuous
([[lem-finite-dimensional-projection-is-linear-and-contractive]]).

[F2] The Borel sigma-algebra is the sigma-algebra generated by open sets
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-topological-space]]).

[F3] A measurable map is one whose inverse image of every target measurable set
is measurable ([[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]).

[F4] Every nonempty finite set of real scores has a minimum
([[def-max-min]], [[lem-finite-set-has-max]]).

[F5] A globally continuous map has open inverse images of open sets and closed
inverse images of closed sets ([[thm-continuity-characterisations-top]]).

[F6] The Euclidean inner product satisfies finite-dimensional Cauchy--Schwarz
([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-finite]]).

[F7] The Euclidean norm satisfies the triangle inequality, and expansion of
squared norms gives $\|a\|_2^2-\|b\|_2^2=\langle a-b,a+b\rangle$
([[cor-triangle-inequality-for-inner-product-norm]],
[[prop-pythagorean-parallelogram-and-polarisation-identities]]).

## Proof

1.1 Put $R_i=I-P_i$. By [F1] and [F7], $R_i$ is linear and $\|R_i z\|_2\le2\|z\|_2$. For $y,z\in\mathbb R^n$, [F6] and [F7] give $$|\|R_i y\|_2^2-\|R_i z\|_2^2| \le\|R_i(y-z)\|_2\,\|R_i(y+z)\|_2 \le4\|y-z\|_2\,\|y+z\|_2.$$ For fixed $z$, the last factor is bounded when $y$ is near $z$, so the squared residual and hence $q_i$ are continuous. [F1, F6, F7, algebra]

2.1 By [F4], a minimum score exists. Its least minimizing index is $i$ exactly on the set $$A_i=\bigcap_{j<i}\{y:q_i(y)<q_j(y)\}\cap\bigcap_{j>i}\{y:q_i(y)\le q_j(y)\}.$$ Each strict comparison is open and each non-strict comparison is closed because the score differences are continuous and [F5] applies to open and closed sets. Thus every $A_i$ is Borel by [F2]; the sets are disjoint and cover $\mathbb R^n$. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For any open $O\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $\widehat m^{-1}(O)=\bigcup_{i=1}^N(A_i\cap P_i^{-1}(O))$. Each $P_i^{-1}(O)$ is open by [F1, F5], so this finite union is Borel. If $B$ ranges over subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ whose inverse images under $\widehat m$ are Borel, those subsets form a sigma-algebra containing every open set; [F2] therefore puts every Borel $B$ in this family. By [F3], $\widehat m$ is Borel measurable. The index-map preimage of any set of indices is a finite union of the corresponding $A_i$, so the index map is measurable as well. [F1, F2, F3, F5, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
