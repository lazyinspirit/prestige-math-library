## Statement

Assume AC. For an integer $h\ge0$ or a half-integer $h=n+1/2$, let $\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{sm}}$ be the set of smooth concordance classes whose preferred-longitude zero surgery bounds an integral $h$-solution. Use the integral lifted-pair definition: $H_1$ is identified with the boundary meridian $\mathbb Z$, $H_2$ is free of rank $2r$, there are exactly $r$ zero/unit pairs over the indicated derived quotient, and at a half level the isotropic classes lift one further derived stage. Every smooth pattern preserves smooth knot concordance. Then $\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{sm}}$ is a subgroup of smooth knot concordance, $\mathcal F_{h'}^{\mathrm{sm}}\subseteq\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{sm}}$ for $h'\ge h$, and every smooth knot lies in $\mathcal F_0^{\mathrm{sm}}$.

More specifically, let $R$ be a smooth slice knot and let $\eta$ be a supplied unknotted infection axis in its exterior with $[\eta]\in\pi_1(S^3\setminus R)^{(k)}$, where $k\ge1$. For every smooth knot $J$, the infection $R(\eta,J)$ has an actual smooth integral $k$-solution. All constructions use finite smooth disk-band, peripheral product-tube, collar and path data; these data may be chosen under the stated AC.

Consequently, if $P$ is a winding-zero smooth pattern, then for every $k\ge1$ and every smooth knot $J$,
$$[P^k(J)]-[P^k(U)]\in\mathcal F_k^{\mathrm{sm}},\qquad \langle P^k(\mathcal C_{\mathrm{sm}})\rangle\subseteq\mathcal F_k^{\mathrm{sm}}+\langle[P^k(U)]\rangle.$$
No locally flat tubular neighborhood or TOP realization theorem is assumed or concluded.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, smooth finite geometric presentations and the indicated integral solution data.

[A1] Full AC implies the countable-choice hypotheses of the smooth suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The integral solution and half-solution conventions are the lifted-pair conditions in the Statement. [[def-integral-half-solutions-strebel-and-l2-signatures]]

[F2] Under AC the explicit regular-diagram Seifert construction supplies a finite smooth disk-band surface for every smooth knot. [[thm-every-oriented-knot-bounds-a-seifert-surface]] For a supplied genus-$g$ disk-band surface there is an actual smooth $W_F$ with boundary its oriented zero surgery, fundamental group $\mathbb Z$ generated by the meridian, and the explicit decomposition $V_F\cup_{F\times S^1}(H_g\times S^1)$. Its cut and product Mayer–Vietoris calculations and its $g$ disjoint rim tori are explicit. [[lem-finite-disk-band-surfaces-give-cyclic-zero-surgery-bounding-manifolds]]

[F3] $R\#(-R)$ has an actual smooth proper disk in the standard ball. A given smooth proper disk has a product normal disk tube and exterior with $H_1=\mathbb Z[\mu]$, higher reduced homology zero, and actual boundary the oriented zero surgery: the full tube and framing construction is in Proof8.1 of the smooth obstruction theorem. [[lem-general-knot-reverse-mirror-sum-is-ribbon]], [[thm-finite-prime-smooth-infection-obstructions-use-actual-coefficients]]

[F4] Standard smooth infection and connected-sum pieces are collared product gluings. Their ordinary $H_2$ is generated by incoming boundary surfaces, their stated meridian and longitude maps come from their product spines, and same-piece equivariant pairings are preserved by collared inclusion. Knot exterior and surgery groups are normally generated by the meridian. [[lem-standard-infection-cobordisms-have-boundary-generated-second-homology]], [[lem-finite-disk-band-knot-longitudes-lie-in-the-second-derived-subgroup]], [[lem-equivariant-intersection-pairings-are-preserved-by-collared-bordism-gluing]]

[F5] Integral Mayer–Vietoris, Poincare–Lefschetz duality, integral cohomology universal coefficients and van Kampen apply to these actual collared manifolds and pairs. [[thm-excision-and-mayer-vietoris-with-local-coefficients]], [[thm-poincare-lefschetz-duality-with-local-coefficients]], [[thm-universal-coefficient-theorem-for-cohomology-over-a-pid]], [[thm-seifert-van-kampen]]

[F6] The smooth branch of knot concordance is an abelian group. Local smooth linear ODEs with compact parameters have smooth solution operators. [[thm-knot-concordance-is-an-abelian-group]], [[thm-time-dependent-vector-fields-have-local-smooth-evolution-operators]]

## Proof

**Given:** The stated smooth finite data and AC.

1.1 First retain integral coefficients in [F2]'s actual product computation. The rim map $H_2(F\times S^1;\mathbb Z)\to H_2(V_F;\mathbb Z)$ is an isomorphism $\mathbb Z^{2g}\to\mathbb Z^{2g}$ by Mayer–Vietoris in the ball. For the gluing to $H_g\times S^1$, the degree-two overlap map is the graph $(x,j_*x)$, with $j_*:\mathbb Z^{2g}\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z^g$ the standard primitive handlebody projection. Its cokernel is $\mathbb Z^g$, and the kernel of the degree-one overlap map $(x,a)\mapsto(a,j_*x,a)$ is $\ker j_*\cong\mathbb Z^g$. Thus $H_2(W_F;\mathbb Z)$ is an extension of $\mathbb Z^g$ by $\mathbb Z^g$, hence free of rank $2g$, and the image of $H_2(H_g\times S^1;\mathbb Z)$ is a primitive rank-$g$ subgroup. Its disjoint product tori give an integral isotropic subgroup. The boundary and $W_F$ both have $H_1=\mathbb Z[\mu]$, with inclusion the identity by [F2]. [F2, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For later gluing, if any integral solution $V$ has its required boundary $H_1$ isomorphism, the integral pair sequence and duality with universal coefficients show that the incoming boundary $H_2$ has zero image in $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)$. Indeed the absolute-to-relative map is a surjection between free groups of equal rank and is therefore an isomorphism. This calculation needs only $H_2(V)$ free and $H_1(V)=\mathbb Z$, not its lifted pair conditions. [F5, algebra]

1.3 Here the smooth concordance passage uses an actual tube rather than a TOP neighborhood assertion. For a supplied smooth proper annulus $A\subset S^3\times I$, use the explicit product embedding $S^3\times I\subset\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R$ with its product metric. The oriented two-planes normal to $A$ within the ambient tangent planes have smooth orthogonal projection matrices $Q$; the supplied product end collars make $Q$ independent of the interval coordinate there. Parallel transport a frame once around its first circle by $\dot U=[\dot Q,Q]U$. Its return differs by a rotation. Choose a real angle for this one rotation and multiply the frame along that circle by the opposite constant-speed rotation; the transport equation with periodic coefficients shows that all derivatives match at the seam. Transport this periodic frame along the $I$ coordinate by the same equation. Thus the normal plane bundle is smoothly trivial. The compact normal exponential/IFT argument in [F3] now gives a uniform smooth product disk tube, product-shaped in the supplied end collars. A small nonzero normal section gives a disjoint parallel annulus $A'$. Its induced framing numbers on the two ends are equal: cap $A$ by Seifert surfaces at the ends to obtain an absolute integral two-cycle in $S^3\times I$; its class is zero because $H_2(S^3\times I)=0$. Its intersection with the relative two-cycle $A'$ is therefore zero. The annulus interiors do not intersect, and the end cap intersections are exactly the two linking numbers with opposite boundary signs. Hence those linking numbers agree. Twisting the global frame by the opposite common integer makes both boundary framings preferred zero framings. Inserting a fixed smooth pattern crossed with $I$ in this actual solid-torus product tube gives a smooth proper annulus between its two zero-framed satellites, proving concordance preservation for every smooth pattern. [A1, F3, F5, F6, construct, algebra]

2.1 The boundary is a closed oriented three-manifold with $H_1=\mathbb Z$, so its $H_2=\mathbb Z$. The relative sequence and the $H_1$ isomorphism make $H_2(W_F)\to H_2(W_F,\partial W_F)$ onto. By duality and universal coefficients the latter group is $H^2(W_F)\cong\operatorname{Hom}(H_2(W_F),\mathbb Z)$, since $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z)=0$. Source and target are free of rank $2g$, so this surjection is an isomorphism and the integral intersection form is unimodular. Complete the primitive isotropic basis to an integral basis; its matrix is $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&B\\B^T&D\end{smallmatrix}\right)$. Unimodularity gives $(\det B)^2=1$. Replacing the complementary basis by its product with $B^{-1}$ gives exactly $g$ zero/unit pairs. Therefore $W_F$ is an integral $0$-solution. This uses no spin or Arf-zero assertion and allows the empty basis when $g=0$. [F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Take two integral $h$-solutions and attach them to the two incoming ends of the orientation-reversed actual connected-sum piece $-C$ in [F4]. The integral Mayer–Vietoris sequence first identifies the meridians: the two gluing $H_1$ generators map independently to their own solution summands and to the common meridian in $H_1(C)$. This map is injective, its cokernel is $\mathbb Z$, and its degree-zero difference map is injective. The outgoing meridian remains the generator. The incoming $H_2$ generates all $H_2(C)$, while its images in the two solutions are zero by step 1.2. Exactness therefore identifies $H_2$ of the glued solution with the direct sum of their $H_2$ groups. Each inclusion induces a homomorphism of fundamental groups and therefore of every derived quotient. Push forward the given lifted cycles by this homomorphism; the old identity pairings stay the identity, cross-piece pairings are zero by disjoint supported cochains, and [F4] preserves each same-piece pairing. The concatenated lists give exactly the required number of zero/unit pairs and, at a half level, the extra isotropic lifts. Thus connected sum preserves integral $h$-solvability. [F1, F4, F5, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Orientation reversal changes the intersection pairing to its negative. The marked boundary of $-V$ is the surgery on the reverse mirror; keep every isotropic $x_i$ and replace $y_i$ by $-y_i$. This restores unit cross-pairings, and does the same at the additional half level. The unknot bounds the standard disk whose exterior is an integral homology circle with $H_2=0$ by [F3], so it admits the empty lists at every height. Projection from a higher derived quotient to a lower one pushes the lifted classes and their pairings forward. In particular an integral $(n+1)$-solution is an integral $(n+1/2)$-solution by using its isotropic classes as the extra lifts, and a half-solution is an integer solution after forgetting those lifts. These operations prove inverses, identity and the filtration inclusions for knot types. [F1, F3, F4, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 For the infection assertion choose the given smooth slice disk for $R$, with exterior $V_R$ supplied by [F3], and use $W_J$ from step 2.1. Glue the positively oriented $V_R$ and $W_J$ to their actual incoming ends of $-E(R,J)$, leaving its outgoing end with the prescribed positive satellite-surgery orientation. The axis is in the first derived subgroup, so its ordinary linking with $R$ is zero. Thus the infection spine maps the companion meridian to zero in $H_1(E)=\mathbb Z[\mu_R]$. The two incoming gluing generators map to $H_1(E)\oplus H_1(V_R)\oplus H_1(W_J)$ as $(a,a,b)$ up to the fixed difference signs. This map is injective with cokernel $\mathbb Z$, retaining the outgoing meridian. The incoming boundary classes generate $H_2(E)$ and vanish in the other pieces by step 1.2 and $H_2(V_R)=0$. Exactness gives $H_2(V)\cong H_2(W_J)\cong\mathbb Z^{2g}$. [F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Remove that annulus tube. Each end knot exterior includes into the resulting exterior by an integral homology isomorphism. To verify this statement without assuming a homology-cobordism theorem, compare Mayer–Vietoris for the end decomposition $S^3=X_i\cup(S^1\times D^2)$ with that for $S^3\times I=E_A\cup(A\times D^2)$. The maps on ambient spaces, on the tubes and on their overlap tori are homology isomorphisms, the latter two by the actual product coordinates. Exactness therefore gives the isomorphism on exterior groups in every degree. Fill the tube boundary with $D^2\times S^1\times I$ along the two zero longitudes fixed in step 1.3. The same Mayer–Vietoris comparison identifies each oriented zero surgery with the corresponding end of an actual smooth integral homology cobordism $Z$. Attach $Z$ to an integral $h$-solution along one end. The homology-cobordism isomorphisms in Mayer–Vietoris identify the new $H_1,H_2$ with the old ones, retaining the marked meridian, and inclusion pushes forward all lifted pairs with their same-piece values by [F4]. Hence the other end has an integral $h$-solution. Together with steps 2.2 and 3.1 this proves that $\mathcal F_h^{\mathrm{sm}}$ is a subgroup on actual smooth concordance classes. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

4.2 In the actual gluing group the companion meridian equals the marked axis. The homomorphism from the exterior of $R$ carries its $k$th derived subgroup into $\pi_1(V)^{(k)}$, so that meridian lies there. Since $\pi_1(W_J)=\mathbb Z$ generated by this meridian, its entire image is in $\pi_1(V)^{(k)}$. Consequently the local system of the cover $\pi_1(V)/\pi_1(V)^{(k)}$ restricts trivially to $W_J$. Each ordinary integral basis cycle in $W_J$ lifts to this cover: in a component over $W_J$ the pulled-back covering is the trivial covering, and choose one component using the supplied base path. Push the zero/unit pairs of step 2.1 into $V$ using these lifts. Their pairings are the original ordinary values by [F4]; distinct deck translates of that component are disjoint, so no extra coefficients appear. With the rank calculation in step 3.2 this gives exactly an integral $k$-solution for $R(\eta,J)$. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Finally the actual composed-pattern axis has depth $k$. For one winding-zero infection, the companion meridian has linking zero with the resulting knot, so is in its exterior commutator subgroup. The companion exterior group is normally generated by that meridian, hence its entire image lies in that commutator subgroup. Any homomorphism with image in $G'$ sends the $j$th derived subgroup into $G^{(j+1)}$, by induction on commutators. Apply this observation to each successive companion-exterior inclusion in the finite nested solid tori for $P^k(U)$. The innermost meridian, which is the axis of the composed pattern, is therefore in the $k$th derived subgroup of its actual exterior. Form the connected-sum pattern $P^k\#(-P^k(U))$ by tying the fixed reverse-mirror summand in a disjoint ball away from this axis. The exterior of the first summand includes in the connected-sum exterior away from the summing ball, so the same derived-depth conclusion persists. Its closure at $U$ is $P^k(U)\#(-P^k(U))$, which is smoothly slice by [F3]; infection along the retained axis gives exactly $P^k(J)\#(-P^k(U))$, with the stated peripheral markings. Step 4.2 now proves the claimed membership. Subgroup closure from step 4.1 lets one sum any finite signed list of these differences, proving the generated-subgroup inclusion. This completes all assertions in the smooth category only. [F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 4.2, construct, algebra] ∎
