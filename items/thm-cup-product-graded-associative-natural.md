## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) and the setting of
[[def-cup-product-sheaf-cohomology]]: $X$ is a topological space, $H^q(X,-)$ is
sheaf cohomology computed from the supplied functorial injective resolution
datum of [[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]] under the standing
smallness or supplied cofinal-denominator hypothesis of
[[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]], $\mathbb Z_X$ is the constant
sheaf with value $\mathbb Z$
([[lem-constant-sheaf-is-the-sheaf-of-locally-constant-functions]]),
$\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}$ is the derived tensor product with comparison
$c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$ of clauses 2 and 4 of
[[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], $\alpha\mapsto\tilde\alpha$
is the canonical isomorphism of
[[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]] and $\kappa(p,q)$ is the
shift multiplication of clause 2 of
[[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]]. For a tensor pairing
$\mu:\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G\to\mathcal H$
([[def-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]]) let
$\cup_\mu:H^p(X,\mathcal F)\times H^q(X,\mathcal G)\to H^{p+q}(X,\mathcal H)$
be the cup product of
[[def-cup-product-sheaf-cohomology]], with representing morphisms
$\widetilde{\alpha\cup_\mu\beta}\in
\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathrm{Ab}(X))}\bigl(\mathbb Z_X[-p-q],\mathcal H\bigr)$.

1. **(Bilinearity.)** For every tensor pairing $\mu$ and all $p,q\ge0$ the
   pairing $\cup_\mu$ is additive in each variable: for
   $\alpha,\alpha'\in H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ and
   $\beta,\beta'\in H^q(X,\mathcal G)$ one has
   $(\alpha+\alpha')\cup_\mu\beta=\alpha\cup_\mu\beta+\alpha'\cup_\mu\beta$
   and
   $\alpha\cup_\mu(\beta+\beta')=\alpha\cup_\mu\beta+\alpha\cup_\mu\beta'$.
   Consequently there is a unique homomorphism of abelian groups, again written
   $\cup_\mu$,
   $$H^p(X,\mathcal F)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}H^q(X,\mathcal G)\longrightarrow H^{p+q}(X,\mathcal H)$$
   with $\alpha\otimes\beta\mapsto\alpha\cup_\mu\beta$.

2. **(Naturality.)** Let $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F'$, $g:\mathcal G\to\mathcal G'$
   and $h:\mathcal H\to\mathcal H'$ be morphisms of abelian sheaves and let
   $\mu:\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G\to\mathcal H$ and
   $\mu':\mathcal F'\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G'\to\mathcal H'$ be tensor
   pairings with $h\circ\mu=\mu'\circ(f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g)$, where
   $f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g$ is the morphism induced by $f$ and $g$
   ([[def-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]]). Then for all
   $\alpha\in H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ and $\beta\in H^q(X,\mathcal G)$
   $$H^{p+q}(X,h)\bigl(\alpha\cup_\mu\beta\bigr) =H^p(X,f)(\alpha)\cup_{\mu'}H^q(X,g)(\beta).$$

3. **(Unit.)** Let $\mu=\operatorname{id}_{F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X}$ and
   $\mathcal H=\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X$ be the identity pairing
   of the pair $(\mathcal F,\mathbb Z_X)$, and respectively
   $\mu=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal F}$ and
   $\mathcal H=\mathbb Z_X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal F$ the identity pairing of
   the pair $(\mathbb Z_X,\mathcal F)$. Let $1_X\in H^0(X,\mathbb Z_X)$ be the
   class of the constant section $1_X\in\Gamma(X,\mathbb Z_X)$ under
   $H^0(X,\mathbb Z_X)\cong\Gamma(X,\mathbb Z_X)$ (clause 2 of
   [[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]]). With the canonical
   isomorphisms $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)$ and $\rho^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)$
   of clause 2 of [[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]],
   $$Q(\lambda_{\mathcal F})\circ\widetilde{1_X\cup\alpha}=\tilde\alpha \qquad\text{and}\qquad Q(\rho_{\mathcal F})\circ\widetilde{\alpha\cup1_X}=\tilde\alpha$$
   for every $\alpha\in H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ and every $p\ge0$; in this sense
   the ordinary sheaf-level unitors carry the resulting classes to $\alpha$.

4. **(Associativity.)** Let $\alpha\in H^p(X,\mathcal F)$,
   $\beta\in H^q(X,\mathcal G)$, $\gamma\in H^r(X,\mathcal H)$, both cup
   products being taken with the identity pairings, and let
   $\alpha_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H}:(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H\to\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}(\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H)$
   be the sheaf-level associator of clause 2 of
   [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]]. Then
   $$H^{p+q+r}\bigl(X,\alpha_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H}\bigr) \bigl((\alpha\cup\beta)\cup\gamma\bigr)=\alpha\cup(\beta\cup\gamma).$$

5. **(Sheaves of unital rings.)** Let $\mathcal R$ be an abelian sheaf with a
   tensor pairing $\mu:\mathcal R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal R\to\mathcal R$ and
   a morphism $u:\mathbb Z_X\to\mathcal R$ such that
   $$\mu\circ(\mu\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal R}) =\mu\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal R}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mu)\circ\alpha_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R,\mathcal R},\qquad \mu\circ(u\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal R})=\lambda_{\mathcal R},\qquad \mu\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal R}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}u)=\rho_{\mathcal R},$$
   the unitors and associator being those of clause 2 of
   [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]]. Let
   $e\in H^0(X,\mathcal R)$ be the class corresponding to the morphism $u$
   (clause 2 of [[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]]), so that
   $e$ is the class of the unit section $u_X(1_X)\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal R)$. Then
   for all classes $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ of $H^*(X,\mathcal R)$
   $$(\alpha\cup_\mu\beta)\cup_\mu\gamma=\alpha\cup_\mu(\beta\cup_\mu\gamma), \qquad e\cup_\mu\alpha=\alpha,\qquad \alpha\cup_\mu e=\alpha,$$
   where the first identity is an identity in $H^{p+q+r}(X,\mathcal R)$. If in
   addition $\mu\circ\sigma_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}=\mu$ for the symmetry
   $\sigma_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}$ of clause 2 of
   [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], then
   $\beta\cup_\mu\alpha=(-1)^{pq}\bigl(\alpha\cup_\mu\beta\bigr)$ for
   $\alpha\in H^p(X,\mathcal R)$ and $\beta\in H^q(X,\mathcal R)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_{D(\mathrm{Ab}(X))}(\mathbb Z_X[-p],\mathcal F)\to H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ of [[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]] is a group isomorphism, additive and natural in $\mathcal F$: for every morphism $\psi:\mathcal F\to\mathcal G$ the square comparing composition with $Q(\psi)$ and $H^p(X,\psi)$ commutes.

[F2] The identity of $\mathbb Z_X$ corresponds to $1_X\in\Gamma(X,\mathbb Z_X)$ under the composite isomorphism with $H^0(X,\mathbb Z_X)\cong\Gamma(X,\mathbb Z_X)$; hence the representing morphism of the class $1_X$ of the constant section is $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X}$ ([[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]], clause 2).

[F3] The derived tensor product $\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}$ is a bifunctor on bounded-above complexes, additive in each variable, computed on left roofs by $(s,h)\otimes(t,k)=Q\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal P h\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal P k)\circ Q\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal P s\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal P t)^{-1}$, and a quasi-isomorphism in either variable induces an isomorphism of derived tensor products ([[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], clause 2).

[F4] The comparison $c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}:\mathcal F\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G\to\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G$ is natural in $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G$, and the map it induces on $H^0$ is an isomorphism ([[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], clause 4).

[F5] The canonical replacement is functorial, with $\mathcal P(\operatorname{id})=\operatorname{id}$ and $\mathcal P(g\circ f)=\mathcal P(g)\circ\mathcal P(f)$, its augmentation $\alpha$ is a natural transformation ($\alpha_{\mathcal D}\circ\mathcal P(f)=f\circ\alpha_{\mathcal C}$ for every cochain map $f$), and $\mathcal P(f)$ is a quasi-isomorphism whenever $f$ is ([[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], clause 1).

[F6] For every bounded-above complex $\mathcal C$ the canonical augmentation $\alpha_{\mathcal C}:\mathcal P(\mathcal C)\to\mathcal C$ is a bounded-above flat replacement: $\mathcal P(\mathcal C)$ is a bounded-above complex of flat sheaves and $\alpha_{\mathcal C}$ is a termwise surjective quasi-isomorphism ([[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], clause 1; [[lem-abelian-sheaves-admit-bounded-above-flat-resolutions]], clause 1).

[F7] Every bounded-above complex of flat abelian sheaves is K-flat ([[lem-abelian-sheaves-admit-bounded-above-flat-resolutions]], clause 2).

[F8] If $\mathcal K$ is a K-flat bounded-above complex of abelian sheaves and $s$ a quasi-isomorphism of bounded-above complexes, then $\operatorname{Tot}(s\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal K})$ and $\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal K}\otimes s)$ are quasi-isomorphisms ([[lem-k-flat-abelian-sheaf-complexes-preserve-quasi-isomorphisms]], clauses 1 and 2).

[F9] The Koszul associator $A:\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H)\to\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H))$ is an isomorphism of cochain complexes, natural in the three arguments, and on the summand $\mathcal F^i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G^j\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H^k$ it equals the sheaf-level associator of clause 2 ([[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], clause 3).

[F10] The sheaf-level symmetry $\sigma_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$, associator $\alpha_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H}$ and unitors $\lambda_{\mathcal F},\rho_{\mathcal F}$ exist and are natural, $\sigma$ is involutive with $\sigma_{\mathcal G,\mathcal F}\circ\sigma_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}=\operatorname{id}$, and $\lambda(f\otimes s)=f\cdot s=\rho(s\otimes f)$ ([[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], clause 2; the left unitor and unit identification $\mathbb Z_X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X\cong\mathbb Z_X$ come from [[lem-stalks-and-colimits-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], clause 5, while the right unitor is $\rho=\lambda\circ\sigma$ as constructed in [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], clause 2).

[F11] $\sigma^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)=Q(S(\mathcal P\mathcal F,\mathcal P\mathcal G))$ is an isomorphism, natural in $\mathcal F$ and $\mathcal G$, and involutive ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 1).

[F12] For all $p,q\ge0$ the morphism $\kappa(p,q)$ of clause 2 is a canonical isomorphism defined by $\kappa(p,q)=Q(\operatorname{Tot}(\alpha_{\mathbb Z_X[-p]}\otimes\alpha_{\mathbb Z_X[-q]}))^{-1}\circ Q(\kappa_{p,q}^{-1})$, where the strict cochain map $\kappa_{p,q}:\operatorname{Tot}(\mathbb Z_X[-p]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X[-q])\to\mathbb Z_X[-p-q]$ has as its only nonzero component the degree-$(p+q)$ unit identification $\mathbb Z_X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X\to\mathbb Z_X$ of clause 3 of [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]]; moreover $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathbb Z_X[-q])\circ\kappa(0,q)=\operatorname{id}$ and $\rho^{\mathbf L}(\mathbb Z_X[-q])\circ\kappa(q,0)=\operatorname{id}$ ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 2).

[F13] $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F):\mathbb Z_X\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal F\to\mathcal F$ and $\rho^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F):\mathcal F\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z_X\to\mathcal F$ are canonical isomorphisms, natural in $\mathcal F$ ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 2).

[F14] For an abelian sheaf $\mathcal F$ read in degree zero, $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)=Q(\lambda_{\mathcal F})\circ c_{\mathbb Z_X,\mathcal F}$ and $\rho^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)=Q(\rho_{\mathcal F})\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathbb Z_X}$ ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 2).

[F15] $\alpha^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H):(\mathcal F\otimes^{\mathbf L}\mathcal G)\otimes^{\mathbf L}\mathcal H\to\mathcal F\otimes^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal G\otimes^{\mathbf L}\mathcal H)$ is a canonical isomorphism in $D^-$, natural in the three arguments ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 3).

[F16] The comparison is compatible with the symmetry and the associator: $c_{\mathcal G,\mathcal F}\circ\sigma^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G)=Q(\sigma_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G})\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$, and $c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal H}\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal F}\otimes^{\mathbf L}c_{\mathcal G,\mathcal H})\circ\alpha^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H)=Q(\alpha_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H})\circ c_{\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G,\mathcal H}\circ(c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\otimes^{\mathbf L}\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal H})$ ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 4(b)).

[F17] The symmetry at shifted units is the sign: $\sigma^{\mathbf L}(\mathbb Z_X[-p],\mathbb Z_X[-q])=\kappa(q,p)\circ((-1)^{pq}\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X[-p-q]})\circ\kappa(p,q)^{-1}$ ([[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], clause 4(c)).

[F18] In the localization of an additive category composition is bilinear ([[lem-addition-of-roofs-makes-an-additive-localization]]).

[F19] For abelian groups $A,B$, regarded as $\mathbb Z$-modules, the group $A\otimes_{\mathbb Z}B$ is the tensor product over $R=\mathbb Z$ of [[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]] ([[def-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]]).

[F20] For bounded-above complexes the tensor-product total complex has degree-$n$ term the direct sum $\bigoplus_{i+j=n}\mathcal F^i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G^j$ ([[def-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]]).

[F21] Every balanced map $b:M\times N\to A$ out of abelian groups regarded as $\mathbb Z$-modules ([[def-balanced-and-bilinear-maps]]) factors uniquely through $M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N$: there is a unique group homomorphism $\overline b:M\otimes_{\mathbb Z}N\to A$ with $\overline b(m\otimes n)=b(m,n)$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]); a map additive in each variable is balanced over $\mathbb Z$, since the scalar condition is the trivial identity $b(mr,n)=b(m,rn)$ for the unique $\mathbb Z$-actions.

[F22] In the cochain reading the shift satisfies $Z[k]^n=Z^{n+k}$, so $\mathbb Z_X[-p]$ has its single nonzero term, the constant sheaf $\mathbb Z_X$, in degree $p$ ([[def-derived-category-of-an-abelian-category]]).

[F23] A cochain map $f:C^\bullet\to D^\bullet$ is a family of morphisms $f^n:C^n\to D^n$ commuting with the differentials; hence a cochain map between complexes each having a single nonzero term is determined by its component in that degree ([[def-cochain-map]]).

[F24] Morphisms $\varphi:\mathbb Z_X\to\mathcal F$ of abelian sheaves are in bijection with $\Gamma(X,\mathcal F)$ by $\varphi\mapsto\varphi_X(1_X)$, so a morphism of sheaves with source $\mathbb Z_X$ is determined by the image of the global section $1_X$ ([[lem-morphisms-from-the-constant-sheaf-are-global-sections]]).

[F25] The Axiom of Choice implies Dependent Choice, and DC implies countable choice ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]); AC gives enough injectives on $\mathrm{Ab}(X)$ ([[thm-abelian-sheaves-have-enough-injectives]]), and the K-injectivity of bounded-below injective complexes uses dependent choice for the countable successive homotopy extensions ([[thm-a-bounded-below-complex-of-injectives-is-homotopically-injective]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a topological space $X$, the supplied functorial injective resolution datum with the resulting sheaf cohomology groups $H^q(X,-)$ and the canonical isomorphism $\alpha\mapsto\tilde\alpha$ of [[lem-sheaf-cohomology-classes-as-derived-morphisms]], the derived tensor product with comparison $c$ of [[lem-derived-tensor-product-of-abelian-sheaves]], the canonical morphisms $\kappa(p,q),\sigma^{\mathbf L},\lambda^{\mathbf L},\rho^{\mathbf L},\alpha^{\mathbf L}$ of [[lem-koszul-coherence-for-derived-sheaf-tensor]], the sheaf-level structure maps $\alpha,\sigma,\lambda,\rho$ of [[lem-koszul-structure-of-the-abelian-sheaf-tensor-product]], abelian sheaves $\mathcal F,\mathcal G,\mathcal H,\mathcal F',\mathcal G',\mathcal H'$ with tensor pairings $\mu,\mu'$, and degrees $p,q,r\ge0$ with classes $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$.

1.1 Fix a tensor pairing $\mu:\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G\to\mathcal H$. The assignment $\alpha\mapsto\tilde\alpha$ is a bijection and, being a group isomorphism [F1], additive: the representing morphism of $\alpha+\alpha'$ is $\tilde\alpha+\tilde\alpha'$. By the definition of the cup product the morphism representing $(\alpha+\alpha')\cup_\mu\beta$ is $\mu\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\circ((\tilde\alpha+\tilde\alpha')\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)$; the derived tensor product is additive in each variable [F3] and composition is bilinear [F18], so this composite equals $\mu\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)+\mu\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\circ(\tilde\alpha'\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)$, which by additivity of [F1] represents $\alpha\cup_\mu\beta+\alpha'\cup_\mu\beta$; hence $(\alpha+\alpha')\cup_\mu\beta=\alpha\cup_\mu\beta+\alpha'\cup_\mu\beta$. The same computation with additivity of the derived tensor product in the second variable gives $\alpha\cup_\mu(\beta+\beta')=\alpha\cup_\mu\beta+\alpha\cup_\mu\beta'$. The assignment $(\alpha,\beta)\mapsto\alpha\cup_\mu\beta$ is therefore additive in each variable and hence balanced over $\mathbb Z$ [F21]; by the universal property of the tensor product of abelian groups [F21], which is the tensor product over $R=\mathbb Z$ of the abelian groups $H^p(X,\mathcal F)$ and $H^q(X,\mathcal G)$ [F19], there is a unique homomorphism $H^p(X,\mathcal F)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}H^q(X,\mathcal G)\to H^{p+q}(X,\mathcal H)$ with $\alpha\otimes\beta\mapsto\alpha\cup_\mu\beta$, which is the homomorphism of clause 1. [F1, F3, F18, F19, F21]

1.2 Let $f,g,h,\mu,\mu'$ satisfy $h\circ\mu=\mu'\circ(f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g)$. By naturality of [F1] the representing morphisms of $H^p(X,f)(\alpha)$ and $H^q(X,g)(\beta)$ are $f\circ\tilde\alpha$ and $g\circ\tilde\beta$, so by bifunctoriality of the derived tensor product [F3] the tensor $((f\circ\tilde\alpha)\otimes^{\mathbf L}(g\circ\tilde\beta))$ equals $(f\otimes^{\mathbf L}g)\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)$. Naturality of the comparison [F4] gives $c_{\mathcal F',\mathcal G'}\circ(f\otimes^{\mathbf L}g)=(f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g)\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$; hence the morphism representing $H^p(X,f)(\alpha)\cup_{\mu'}H^q(X,g)(\beta)$ is $\mu'\circ(f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g)\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)$, which by the identity $h\circ\mu=\mu'\circ(f\otimes_{\mathbb Z}g)$ equals $Q(h)\circ\mu\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)$. This is $Q(h)$ composed with the representing morphism of $\alpha\cup_\mu\beta$, so by naturality of [F1] applied to the sheaf morphism $h$ its class is $H^{p+q}(X,h)(\alpha\cup_\mu\beta)$, which is clause 2. [F1, F3, F4]

1.3 By [F2] the representing morphism of $1_X$ is $\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X}$. With the identity pairing of $(\mathbb Z_X,\mathcal F)$, the morphism representing $1_X\cup\alpha$ is $c_{\mathbb Z_X,\mathcal F}\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X}\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\alpha)\circ\kappa(0,p)$. The source of $\widetilde{1_X\cup\alpha}$ is $Z_p=\mathbb Z_X[-p]$ and its target is the **ordinary** tensor sheaf $\mathbb Z_X\otimes\mathcal F$ in degree zero. Therefore the typed unit composite is
$$Q(\lambda_{\mathcal F})\circ\widetilde{1_X\cup\alpha}=\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)\circ(\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Z_X}\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\alpha)\circ\kappa(0,p),$$
using $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)=Q(\lambda_{\mathcal F})\circ c_{\mathbb Z_X,\mathcal F}$ [F14]. Naturality of the derived unitor [F13] moves $\tilde\alpha$ past it, and the shifted-unit law [F12] gives $\lambda^{\mathbf L}(Z_p)\circ\kappa(0,p)=\operatorname{id}_{Z_p}$; hence this composite is $\tilde\alpha$. The same argument with $\rho^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal F)=Q(\rho_{\mathcal F})\circ c_{\mathcal F,\mathbb Z_X}$ and $\rho^{\mathbf L}(Z_p)\circ\kappa(p,0)=\operatorname{id}$ proves $Q(\rho_{\mathcal F})\circ\widetilde{\alpha\cup1_X}=\tilde\alpha$. [F2, F12, F13, F14]

1.4 For bounded-above complexes $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$ define the natural comparison $C_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}:=Q\operatorname{Tot}(\alpha_{\mathcal F}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\alpha_{\mathcal G}):\mathcal F\otimes^{\mathbf L}_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G\to Q\operatorname{Tot}(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal G)$. It is generally **not** an isomorphism: on a one-point space with both inputs $\mathbb Z/2[0]$, the derived tensor has $H^{-1}=\operatorname{Tor}_1^{\mathbb Z}(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z/2)=\mathbb Z/2$, whereas the ordinary tensor complex has no degree $-1$ term. If both inputs are bounded-above K-flat complexes, however, $C_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$ is invertible. Factor its chain representative as $\operatorname{Tot}(\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal P\mathcal F}\otimes\alpha_{\mathcal G})$ followed by $\operatorname{Tot}(\alpha_{\mathcal F}\otimes\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal G})$: the first is a quasi-isomorphism because $\mathcal P\mathcal F$ is K-flat [F6, F7], and the second because $\mathcal G$ is K-flat [F8]. For cochain maps $f:\mathcal F\to\mathcal F'$ and $g:\mathcal G\to\mathcal G'$, naturality of $\alpha$ [F5] gives the square $C_{\mathcal F',\mathcal G'}\circ(Qf\otimes^{\mathbf L}Qg)=Q\operatorname{Tot}(f\otimes g)\circ C_{\mathcal F,\mathcal G}$. If all four complexes are K-flat, both comparisons are invertible, so this square may also be used in its conjugated form. No inverse of $C$ is used for arbitrary complexes. [F3, F5, F6, F7, F8]

1.5 Let $\mu:\mathcal R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal R\to\mathcal R$ satisfy $\mu\circ\sigma_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}=\mu$ for the sheaf-level symmetry $\sigma_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}$ of clause 2 [F10], and let $\alpha\in H^p(X,\mathcal R)$, $\beta\in H^q(X,\mathcal R)$. The representing morphisms of $\alpha\cup_\mu\beta$ and $\beta\cup_\mu\alpha$ are $\mu\circ c_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)$ and $\mu\circ c_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}\circ(\tilde\beta\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\alpha)\circ\kappa(q,p)$. By naturality of $\sigma^{\mathbf L}$ [F11] applied to $\tilde\alpha$ and $\tilde\beta$ one has $(\tilde\beta\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\alpha)\circ\sigma^{\mathbf L}(Z_p,Z_q)=\sigma^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal R,\mathcal R)\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)$; the symmetry at shifted units [F17] reads $\sigma^{\mathbf L}(Z_p,Z_q)=\kappa(q,p)\circ((-1)^{pq}\operatorname{id})\circ\kappa(p,q)^{-1}$, so $\kappa(q,p)=\sigma^{\mathbf L}(Z_p,Z_q)\circ\kappa(p,q)\circ((-1)^{pq}\operatorname{id}_{Z_{p+q}})$ and the representing morphism of $\beta\cup_\mu\alpha$ equals $\mu\circ c_{\mathcal R,\mathcal R}\circ\sigma^{\mathbf L}(\mathcal R,\mathcal R)\circ(\tilde\alpha\otimes^{\mathbf L}\tilde\beta)\circ\kappa(p,q)\circ((-1)^{pq}\operatorname{id}_{Z_{p+q}})$.
