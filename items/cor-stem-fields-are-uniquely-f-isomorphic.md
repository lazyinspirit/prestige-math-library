## Statement

Let $p\in F[x]$ be monic and irreducible. If $L=F(\alpha)$ and $M=F(\beta)$
with $p(\alpha)=p(\beta)=0$, then there is a unique $F$-isomorphism
$L\to M$ sending $\alpha$ to $\beta$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial, extensions, and distinguished roots in the statement.

[F1] The minimal polynomial of an algebraic element is the unique monic irreducible generator of its evaluation kernel, and it divides every polynomial vanishing at that element ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[F2] A root of a monic irreducible polynomial determines a unique homomorphism from its adjoining quotient, fixing $F$ and matching the root ([[thm-universal-property-of-adjoining-an-irreducible-root]]).

[F3] A simple algebraic extension generated by a root is isomorphic to its minimal-polynomial quotient ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], the minimal polynomials of $\alpha$ and $\beta$ divide $p$; monic irreducibility of all three makes both minimal polynomials equal to $p$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Using [F2] and the quotient identifications in [F3], obtain $F$-homomorphisms $\varphi:L\to M$ and $\psi:M\to L$ with $\varphi(\alpha)=\beta$ and $\psi(\beta)=\alpha$. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The composite $\psi\varphi$ fixes $F$ and $\alpha$, so uniqueness in [F2] makes it the identity on $L$; similarly $\varphi\psi$ is the identity on $M$. [F2, step 2.1]

4.1 Hence $\varphi$ is an $F$-isomorphism, and the same uniqueness clause shows that no other such isomorphism can send $\alpha$ to $\beta$. [F2, step 3.1] ∎
