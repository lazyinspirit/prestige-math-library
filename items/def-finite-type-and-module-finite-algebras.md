## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $A$ be a commutative $R$-algebra with
structure map $\eta_A\colon R\to A$ ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

**The subalgebra generated by finitely many elements.** Let $n\in\mathbb N$ and
$a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A$. Iterating the universal property of a polynomial ring
([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]) along the recursion
$R[x_1,\ldots,x_{k+1}]=R[x_1,\ldots,x_k][x_{k+1}]$ of
[[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]] gives a unique unital ring
homomorphism

$$\operatorname{ev}\colon R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\longrightarrow A$$

that agrees with $\eta_A$ on constants and sends $x_i$ to $a_i$ for each $i$;
at each step of the recursion the one-variable universal property supplies
existence and uniqueness of the extension, and at $n=0$ the map is $\eta_A$
itself. Its image is written $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ and is called the
**$R$-subalgebra of $A$ generated by $a_1,\ldots,a_n$**. It is a subring of $A$
containing $\eta_A(R)$, and it is the smallest such subring containing
$a_1,\ldots,a_n$, since any subring with those properties is closed under the
sums and products that make up a polynomial expression. At $n=0$ it is
$\eta_A(R)$, the image of $R$ in $A$.

**Finite type.** $A$ is **of finite type over $R$**, equivalently a **finitely
generated $R$-algebra**, when $A=R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$ and
some $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in A$. Equivalently, $A$ is isomorphic as an $R$-algebra to
a quotient $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/\mathfrak a$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$ and some
ideal $\mathfrak a$: the map $\operatorname{ev}$ above is then surjective, and
[[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]] identifies $A$ with
$R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/\ker\operatorname{ev}$; conversely the composite of the
canonical projection with the inclusion of the indeterminates exhibits any such
quotient as generated by the residues of $x_1,\ldots,x_n$.

**Module-finite.** $A$ is **module-finite over $R$**, equivalently a **finite
$R$-algebra**, when $A$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module
([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]) for the action
$r\cdot a=\eta_A(r)a$.

**Module-finite implies finite type.** If $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ generate $A$ as an
$R$-module then $R[b_1,\ldots,b_n]$, being a subring of $A$ that contains
$\eta_A(R)$ and every $b_i$, contains every $R$-linear combination
$\sum_i\eta_A(r_i)b_i$ and hence all of $A$; so $A=R[b_1,\ldots,b_n]$. The
converse fails, and the companion examples page carries a witness.

## Remarks

- **Three conditions, three rings.** "Finitely generated" is ambiguous on its
  own: an ideal may be finitely generated as an ideal, a module as a module, and
  an algebra as an algebra, and the three are different requirements. This page
  writes "of finite type" for the algebra condition and "module-finite" for the
  module condition, and always names the ring over which the condition is taken.
  Sources differ in vocabulary: Totaro and Milne write "finite algebra" for what
  is called module-finite here, and Altman–Kleiman write "module finite" and
  "algebra finite".

- **The generators need not be algebraically independent.** Nothing above asks
  $\operatorname{ev}$ to be injective. When it is, $A$ is a polynomial ring and
  the ideal $\mathfrak a$ is zero; that is a special case, not the definition.

- **The empty list is allowed and is not the same as $A=R$.** At $n=0$ the
  subalgebra generated is $\eta_A(R)$, which is a quotient of $R$ rather than a
  copy of it unless $\eta_A$ is injective.
