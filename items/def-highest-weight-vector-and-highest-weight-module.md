## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive
system with nilpotent subalgebra $\mathfrak n^+$
([[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]]),
and let $V$ be a representation of $\mathfrak g$. A **highest weight vector**
of $V$ is a nonzero vector $v\in V_\lambda$, for some
$\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$, with
$$\mathfrak n^+\cdot v=0$$
([[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]); the
functional $\lambda$ is then called the **weight** of $v$. A **highest weight
module** of highest weight $\lambda$ is a representation $V$ generated, as a
$\mathfrak g$-module, by a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda$: the
smallest subrepresentation of $V$ containing $v$ is $V$ itself.

By
[[thm-lie-algebra-representations-are-equivalent-to-unital-modules-over-the-enveloping-algebra]]
the action of $\mathfrak g$ extends uniquely to a unital action of $U(\mathfrak g)$,
so the subrepresentation generated by $v$ is exactly $U(\mathfrak g)v$; this
is the content of the word "generated" above and makes the notion independent
of any choice of generators. A highest weight vector $v$ satisfies
$H\cdot v=\lambda(H)v$ for all $H\in\mathfrak h$ by the definition of
$V_\lambda$. The zero representation is not a highest weight module, since a
highest weight vector is required to be nonzero.
