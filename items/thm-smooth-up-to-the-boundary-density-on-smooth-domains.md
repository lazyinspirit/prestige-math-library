## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k\ge1$, $1\le p<\infty$,
$\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$, and let $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$ be a
bounded $C^k$ domain in the graph sense of
[[def-bounded-c-k-domain-and-boundary-charts]]. Then the set of restrictions
to $\Omega$ of functions in $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ is dense in
$W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$: for every $u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ and
every $\delta>0$ there is $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ with
$\|\varphi|_\Omega-u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}<\delta$.

The exponent range is $1\le p<\infty$; the result asserts no density in the
$W^{k,\infty}$ norm, and it does not hold on arbitrary open sets, as the
companion examples page shows.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice; $k\ge1$; $1\le p<\infty$; $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$; a bounded $C^k$ domain $\Omega\subset\mathbb R^n$; and a class $u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$.

[F1] Extension: for every open $V$ with $\overline\Omega\subseteq V$ there is a bounded linear extension operator $E:W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)\to W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ with $(Eu)|_\Omega=u$ almost everywhere and $\operatorname{supp}(Eu)$ a compact subset of $V$, for the given $k\ge1$ and $1\le p<\infty$ ([[thm-extension-theorem-for-bounded-smooth-domains]]).

[F2] Smooth bumps: for $0<r<R$ there is a smooth $\eta:\mathbb R^n\to[0,1]$ equal to one on $\overline B_r(0)$ with $\operatorname{supp}\eta\subseteq B_R(0)$ ([[lem-smooth-bump-between-concentric-euclidean-balls]]).

[F3] Interior commutation: for $u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$, a nonnegative unit-mass $\rho\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ with $\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\overline B_1(0)$, and $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$, the convolution $\rho_\varepsilon*u$ is defined and smooth on $\Omega_\varepsilon=\{x\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb R^n\setminus\Omega)>\varepsilon\}$, which equals $\mathbb R^n$ when $\Omega=\mathbb R^n$, and $D^\alpha(\rho_\varepsilon*u)=\rho_\varepsilon*(D^\alpha u)$ there for every $|\alpha|\le k$, as almost-everywhere classes ([[lem-mollification-commutes-with-weak-derivatives-in-the-interior]]).

[F4] The family $\rho_\varepsilon$ generated by a smooth unit-mass $\rho$ with $\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq\overline B_1(0)$ is an $L^1$ approximate identity ([[prop-mollifier-families-are-l-one-approximate-identities]]).

[F5] Real convergence: an $L^1$ approximate identity $(K_\varepsilon)$ on $\mathbb R^n$ satisfies $\|f*K_\varepsilon-f\|_p\to0$ for $1\le p<\infty$ and $f\in L^p(\mathbb R^n)$ ([[thm-l-one-approximate-identities-converge-in-l-p]]).

[F6] Complex interface: for complex $K\in L^1(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb C)$ and $f\in L^p$, $\|K*f\|_p\le\|K\|_1\|f\|_p$, and if $\int K_\varepsilon=1$, $\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\|K_\varepsilon\|_1<\infty$ and $\int_{|y|\ge\delta}|K_\varepsilon|\to0$ for every $\delta>0$, then $K_\varepsilon*f\to f$ in $L^p$ for $p<\infty$; the rescalings $\varepsilon^{-n}K(x/\varepsilon)$ of a unit-mass $K\in L^1$ have these properties ([[lem-complex-translation-and-approximate-identity-interfaces]]).

[F7] Restriction is a contraction: for open $U\subseteq\Omega$, restriction defines a contraction $W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)\to W^{k,p}(U;\mathbb K)$ ([[lem-bounded-restriction-and-cutoff-localisation-in-sobolev-spaces]]).

[F8] Sobolev norm: for $1\le p<\infty$, $\|w\|_{W^{k,p}(U)}=\left(\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\|D^\alpha w\|_{L^p(U)}^p\right)^{1/p}$ ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]).

**Choice use.** The assumed Axiom of Choice supplies the hypotheses of the extension interface [F1] in step 1.1 and the restriction interface [F7] in step 5.1. It also implies the Countable Choice assumed by [F3]–[F6], [F8] and the bounded $C^k$-domain definition. Fixing one bump from [F2] and normalising it in step 1.2 requires no further choice.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $u\in W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$. Since $\Omega$ is bounded, choose a bounded open $V$ with $\overline\Omega\subseteq V$, and let $E$ be the extension operator supplied by [F1] for this $V$; put $F:=Eu\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$. Then $F|_\Omega=u$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$ and $\operatorname{supp}F$ is a compact subset of $V$. [F1, given]

1.2 Let $\eta$ be a bump as in [F2] with $r=1/2$, $R=1$, so that $\eta=1$ on $\overline B_{1/2}(0)$ and $\operatorname{supp}\eta\subseteq B_1(0)$; then $0<\int_{\mathbb R^n}\eta<\infty$ and $\rho:=\eta/\int\eta$ is nonnegative, of class $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$, of unit mass, with $\operatorname{supp}\rho\subseteq B_1(0)\subseteq\overline B_1(0)$. Put $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$ for $\varepsilon>0$, so $\rho_\varepsilon$ is nonnegative with $\int\rho_\varepsilon=1$ and $\operatorname{supp}\rho_\varepsilon\subseteq\overline B_\varepsilon(0)$. [F2, given]

2.1 For every multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le k$, the class $D^\alpha F\in L^p(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ exists, and the commutation clause of [F3] applied with $\Omega=\mathbb R^n$ (so that $\Omega_\varepsilon=\mathbb R^n$ for every $\varepsilon$) gives $D^\alpha(\rho_\varepsilon*F)=\rho_\varepsilon*(D^\alpha F)$ as almost-everywhere classes on $\mathbb R^n$. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

2.2 For each $\varepsilon>0$ the convolution $v_\varepsilon:=\rho_\varepsilon*F$ is of class $C^\infty$ on $\mathbb R^n$ by the smoothness clause of [F3], and $\operatorname{supp}v_\varepsilon\subseteq\operatorname{supp}F+\operatorname{supp}\rho_\varepsilon\subseteq\operatorname{supp}F+\overline B_\varepsilon(0)$ is compact, first because the support of a convolution is contained in the sum of the supports and then because $\operatorname{supp}F$ is compact; hence $v_\varepsilon\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ and its restriction is an admissible approximant. [F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For each $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\le k$, $\|\rho_\varepsilon*(D^\alpha F)-D^\alpha F\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}\to0$ as $\varepsilon\to0^+$: for $\mathbb K=\mathbb R$ this is the real convergence of [F5] applied to the $L^1$ approximate identity of [F4] and the class $D^\alpha F\in L^p$; for $\mathbb K=\mathbb C$ the complex interface of [F6] applies to $D^\alpha F$ directly, a real class being a complex class. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 By the norm formula of [F8] and step 3.1, the smooth convolutions converge in the whole-space Sobolev norm: $\|\rho_\varepsilon*F-F\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)}^p=\sum_{|\alpha|\le k}\|\rho_\varepsilon*(D^\alpha F)-D^\alpha F\|_{L^p(\mathbb R^n)}^p\to0$. [F8, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 By the restriction contraction [F7] applied to $v_\varepsilon-F\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ and the identity $F|_\Omega=u$ of step 1.1, $\|v_\varepsilon|_\Omega-u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}\le\|v_\varepsilon-F\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb R^n)}$, which tends to zero by step 4.1; given $\delta>0$ choose $\varepsilon>0$ with $\|v_\varepsilon|_\Omega-u\|_{W^{k,p}(\Omega)}<\delta$. [F7, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 2.2]

6.1 Therefore the restrictions of $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ functions are dense in $W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ for $1\le p<\infty$, while nothing is asserted at $p=\infty$: the convergence inputs [F5] and [F6] are stated for finite exponents only, and step 3.1 fails for the essential-supremum norm. [F5, F6, step 5.1, given] ∎
