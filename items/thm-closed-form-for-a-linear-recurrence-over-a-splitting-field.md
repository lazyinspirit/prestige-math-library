## Statement

Let $K$ be a field of characteristic zero, let $(a_n)_{n\ge0}$ satisfy an order-$d$ recurrence from zero, and let $E/K$ be a splitting field of its characteristic polynomial. If

$$\chi(t)=\prod_{i=1}^{s}(t-\lambda_i)^{m_i}$$

in $E[t]$, with distinct roots $\lambda_i$, then there are unique polynomials $p_i\in E[z]$ with $\deg p_i<m_i$ such that

$$a_n=\sum_{i=1}^{s}p_i(n)\lambda_i^n\qquad(n\ge0).$$

Conversely, every sequence of this form satisfies the recurrence whose characteristic polynomial is the displayed product. Equality is in $E$, and no identification with $\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$ is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A characteristic-zero field $K$, a sequence satisfying a recurrence from zero, a named splitting field $E/K$, and the displayed factorisation of its characteristic polynomial.

[L1] A recurrence from zero has a proper rational generating function with its reciprocal denominator ([[thm-linear-recurrence-iff-rational-generating-function]]).

[L2] The factorisation $\chi(t)=\prod_i(t-\lambda_i)^{m_i}$ corresponds to $Q(x)=\prod_i(1-\lambda_i x)^{m_i}$ ([[rem-reciprocal-root-convention-for-recurrences]]).

[L3] A proper fraction with that split denominator has a unique expansion $\sum_{i,j}b_{ij}(1-\lambda_i x)^{-j}$ ([[lem-partial-fractions-for-a-split-recurrence-denominator]]).

[L4] Formally, $[x^n](1-\lambda x)^{-j}=\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}\lambda^n$ ([[lem-binomial-series-for-a-repeated-pole]]).

[L5] A splitting field is generated by the roots over the base field, and repeated factors record their multiplicities ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]).

[L6] Characteristic zero means that no positive natural multiple of the field identity is zero ([[def-ring-characteristic]]).

[L7] The binomial coefficient satisfies $\binom nk k!=n^{\underline{k}}$ when $k\le n$ ([[thm-binomial-closed-formula]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the generating function is $P/Q$ with $P=0$ or $\deg P<d$, and [L2] identifies $Q$ with the split product $\prod_i(1-\lambda_i x)^{m_i}$ in $E[x]$. [given, L1, L2, L5]

1.2 By [L6], every positive factorial is nonzero and hence invertible in $E$. For $1\le j\le m$ put $q_j(z)=\big((j-1)!\big)^{-1}(z+1)(z+2)\cdots(z+j-1)\in E[z]$, the product being empty for $j=1$, so $q_1=1$. Each $q_j$ has degree $j-1$ and leading coefficient $((j-1)!)^{-1}\ne0$, and for every natural $n$ the identity $\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}(j-1)!=(n+j-1)^{\underline{j-1}}=(n+1)\cdots(n+j-1)$ from [L7] gives $q_j(n)=\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}$. The degrees $0,\ldots,m-1$ are distinct, so triangular elimination makes $q_1,\ldots,q_m$ a basis of the polynomials in $E[z]$ of degree below $m$. [L6, L7, algebra]

2.1 Conversely, expand each $p_i$ in the binomial-polynomial basis from step 1.2. Then [L4] shows that the generating function of $p_i(n)\lambda_i^n$ has denominator dividing $(1-\lambda_i x)^{m_i}$; summing gives a rational function with denominator dividing $Q$, so [L1] gives the recurrence with characteristic polynomial dividing the displayed product. Multiplying by any missing factors gives the displayed order-$d$ recurrence itself. [step 1.2, L1, L2, L4, algebra]

2.2 Apply [L3] and then [L4] to obtain $a_n=\sum_{i=1}^{s}\sum_{j=1}^{m_i}b_{ij}\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}\lambda_i^n$. [step 1.1, L3, L4]

3.1 Step 1.2 rewrites each coefficient $\binom{n+j-1}{j-1}$ as $q_j(n)$, so grouping the terms of step 2.2 with the same $i$ gives $a_n=\sum_ip_i(n)\lambda_i^n$ with $p_i=\sum_{j=1}^{m_i}b_{ij}q_j$, a polynomial in $E[z]$ of degree below $m_i$. Uniqueness follows from the uniqueness in [L3], the basis property in step 1.2, and coefficient extensionality. [step 2.2, step 1.2, L3]

4.1 Steps 3.1 and 2.1 prove both directions, including repeated roots and the case of one root. The condition $c_d\ne0$ in the recurrence excludes zero among the $\lambda_i$. [step 3.1, step 2.1, L2] ∎
