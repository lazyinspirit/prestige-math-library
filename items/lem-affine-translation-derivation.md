## Statement

For $V^k(\mathfrak g)$ from [[def-universal-affine-vacuum-module]], the assignment on $\widehat{\mathfrak g}$
$$D(K)=0,\qquad D(x_n)=-n x_{n-1}$$
is a Lie-algebra derivation preserving $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak g[t]\oplus\mathbb CK$. It induces an operator $T$ on $V^k(\mathfrak g)$ such that $T\mathbf1=0$ and $[T,x_n]=-n x_{n-1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The normalized affine central extension, its current modes, and the induced module at level $k$.

[F1] [[def-untwisted-affine-central-extension]]: $[x_m,y_n]=[x,y]_{m+n}+m\delta_{m,-n}B(x,y)K$, where $K$ is central.

[F2] [[def-universal-affine-vacuum-module]] and [[lem-affine-vacuum-character-at-level-k]]: $V^k(\mathfrak g)=U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})\otimes_{U(\mathfrak p)}\mathbb C_k$, $\mathfrak g[t]$ acts as zero on $\mathbb C_k$, and $K$ acts as $k$.

## Proof

1.1 For current modes, $D([x_m,y_n])=-(m+n)[x,y]_{m+n-1}$, whereas $[D(x_m),y_n]+[x_m,D(y_n)]=-(m+n)[x,y]_{m+n-1}-m((m-1)+n)B(x,y)\delta_{m+n-1,0}K$. If the Kronecker factor is nonzero then $m+n=1$, so its central coefficient $m(m+n-1)$ is zero. Thus the two expressions agree. Brackets with $K$ also satisfy the derivation identity because $K$ is central and $D(K)=0$. Bilinearity proves that $D$ is a Lie derivation on all finite sums. [F1, algebra]

2.1 If $n\ge1$, then $D(x_n)=-nx_{n-1}\in\mathfrak g[t]$, while $D(x_0)=0$ and $D(K)=0$. Hence $D(\mathfrak p)\subseteq\mathfrak p$. Extend $D$ to the tensor algebra by the Leibniz rule. It preserves the ideal generated by $uv-vu-[u,v]$, since its derivative is a sum of the same defining relations with $u$ or $v$ replaced by $D(u)$ or $D(v)$. Therefore it descends to a derivation $D_U$ of $U(\widehat{\mathfrak g})$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Define $T(u\otimes1)=D_U(u)\otimes1$. This respects the induced-module relation $ua\otimes1=u\otimes a1$ for $a\in U(\mathfrak p)$: the Leibniz rule gives the same $D_U(u)a$ term on both sides, and the remaining term $uD_U(a)\otimes1$ equals $u\otimes D_U(a)1=0$ because $D(\mathfrak p)\subseteq\mathfrak p$ and the vacuum character kills $\mathfrak g[t]$ and $D(K)=0$. Thus $T$ is well-defined. Since $D_U(1)=0$, $T\mathbf1=0$, and for every current mode the defining derivation action gives $[T,x_n]=D(x_n)=-nx_{n-1}$. [F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
