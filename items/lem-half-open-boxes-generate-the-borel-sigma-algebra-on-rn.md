## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$, let $\mathcal{H}_n$ be the family of half-open boxes in
$\mathbb{R}^n$ ([[def-half-open-box]]) and let $\mathcal{E}_n$ be the family of
elementary sets ([[def-elementary-set-in-rn]]). With $\mathbb{R}^n$ carrying its
product topology, which is the metric topology of the Euclidean metric
([[cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology]]),

$$\sigma\big(\mathcal{H}_n\big) \;=\; \sigma\big(\mathcal{E}_n\big) \;=\; \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$$

([[def-generated-sigma-algebra]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n \ge 1$, the topology $\mathcal{T}$ of $(\mathbb{R}^n,d_2)$, the family $\mathcal{H}_n$ of half-open boxes and the family $\mathcal{E}_n$ of elementary sets.

[L1] $B(a,b) := \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : a_i < x_i \le b_i \ \text{ for every } i < n \,\}$, with parameters in $\overline{\mathbb{R}}$ ([[def-half-open-box]]).

[L2] A subset $E\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n$ is an **elementary set** when there are a natural number $m$ and a list $B_0, \dots, B_{m-1}$ of half-open boxes with $E=\bigcup_{j<m}B_j$; at $m = 1$ every half-open box is elementary ([[def-elementary-set-in-rn]]).

[L3] Every open $U \subseteq \mathbb{R}^n$ is the union of an at most countable family of pairwise disjoint dyadic cubes ([[thm-open-subsets-of-rn-are-countable-disjoint-unions-of-dyadic-cubes]]), and a dyadic cube is the half-open box $Q_{k,m} = \{\, x : m_i 2^{-k} < x_i \le (m_i+1)2^{-k} \text{ for every } i<n \,\}$ ([[def-dyadic-cube-in-rn]]).

[L4] Each of the following families generates $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$: all open sets; and all rational half-open boxes $\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i]$ with rational endpoints $a_i<b_i$ ([[thm-rational-box-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-rn]]).

[F1] The **Borel sigma-algebra of $X$** is the sigma-algebra generated by its open sets, $\mathcal B(X):=\sigma_X(\mathcal T)$ ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]), and $\sigma_X(\mathcal E)$ is the unique smallest sigma-algebra on $X$ containing $\mathcal E$ ([[def-generated-sigma-algebra]], [[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]).

[F2] If $\mathcal E\subseteq\sigma_X(\mathcal F)$ and $\mathcal F\subseteq\sigma_X(\mathcal E)$, then $\sigma_X(\mathcal E)=\sigma_X(\mathcal F)$ ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-comparison-criterion]]).

[F3] A sigma-algebra is closed under countable unions and under countable intersections ([[def-sigma-algebra]], [[thm-sigma-algebra-closure-laws]]).

[F4] The product topology on $\mathbb{R}^n$ **is** the metric topology of $d_2$ ([[cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology]], claim 1; [[def-metric-topology]]).

[F5] A subset $U \subseteq X$ is open in $(X,d)$ if for every $x \in U$ there is a real $r > 0$ with $B(x,r) \subseteq U$, and a finite intersection of open sets is open ([[def-metric-topology]], [[def-metric-ball]], [[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]], claim 3).

[F6] For every $x \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$, $\lVert x\rVert_\infty \le \lVert x\rVert_2$, and $\lVert x-y\rVert_2 = d_2(x,y)$, $\lVert x-y\rVert_\infty = d_\infty(x,y)$, where $d_\infty(x,y) = \max\{|x_k-y_k| : k<n\}$ ([[lem-every-norm-on-rn-is-continuous-for-the-euclidean-metric]], claim 3; [[lem-p-norms-are-norms-and-induce-the-published-metrics]], claim 3; [[def-p-norms-on-rn]]; [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]).

[F7] For every real $\varepsilon > 0$ there is a natural number $k \ge 1$ with $1/k < \varepsilon$ ([[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]]).

[F8] For $a,b \in \overline{\mathbb{R}}$, $a + b := +\infty$ when $a = +\infty$ and $b \ne -\infty$, and $a + b := -\infty$ when $a = -\infty$ and $b \ne +\infty$ ([[def-extended-reals]]).

[F9] An at most countable family may always be presented as a sequence ([[def-countable]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For parameters $a,c$ the set $V(a,c) := \{\, x \in \mathbb{R}^n : a_i < x_i < c_i \text{ for every } i<n \,\}$ is open: given $x \in V(a,c)$, the finitely many quantities $x_i-a_i$ with $a_i$ real and $c_i-x_i$ with $c_i$ real are strictly positive, so their minimum $\rho$ is a positive real, or $\rho := 1$ if there are none, and $d_2(x,y)<\rho$ forces $|y_i-x_i| \le d_2(x,y) < \rho$ in every coordinate, hence $a_i<y_i<c_i$ throughout. [F5, F6]

1.2 Every half-open box is a countable intersection of sets of the form $V(a,c)$, namely $B(a,b) = \bigcap_{q \ge 1}V\big(a,\,b+(1/q)\mathbf{1}\big)$: a point of $B(a,b)$ satisfies $x_i \le b_i < b_i+1/q$ when $b_i$ is real and $x_i < +\infty$ when $b_i = +\infty$, while a point of every $V\big(a,b+(1/q)\mathbf{1}\big)$ satisfies $a_i<x_i$ and, for real $b_i$, cannot have $x_i>b_i$, since some $1/q$ is below $x_i-b_i$; a coordinate with $b_i=-\infty$ makes both sides empty. [L1, F7, F8]

1.3 In the other direction every open $U$ lies in $\sigma(\mathcal{H}_n)$: it is the union of an at most countable family of dyadic cubes, that family may be presented as a sequence, and each dyadic cube is a half-open box. [L3, F3, F9]

1.4 The published generator theorem gives that same inclusion by a second route, since the rational half-open boxes $\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i]$ with rational $a_i<b_i$ are half-open boxes in the sense of [L1] and already generate $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. [L1, L4, F1]

2.1 Every half-open box is therefore a Borel set, being a countable intersection of open sets, so $\mathcal{H}_n \subseteq \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and, $\mathcal{E}_n$ consisting of finite unions of half-open boxes, also $\mathcal{E}_n \subseteq \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L2, F1, F3, F4]

3.1 By steps 1.3 and 2.1 the families $\mathcal{H}_n$ and $\mathcal{T}$ lie in each other's generated sigma-algebras, so $\sigma(\mathcal{H}_n)=\sigma(\mathcal{T})=\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$; and $\mathcal{H}_n \subseteq \mathcal{E}_n \subseteq \sigma(\mathcal{H}_n)$ gives $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_n)=\sigma(\mathcal{H}_n)$ by the same criterion. [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, L2, F1, F2, F4] ∎

## Remarks

- **The convention is the published one.** [[thm-rational-box-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-rn]] states its half-open generators as $\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i]$, and [[def-half-open-box]] uses the same face. Step 1.4 is exactly the point at which a second convention would have shown up as a mismatch rather than as a silent redefinition.
