## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite
residue field $k$ of characteristic $p$, let $n\ge1$, and suppose
$|\mu_n(K)|=n$. For $a,b\in K^\times$, choose $\alpha^n=a$ and set
$$H_n(a,b):=\operatorname{rec}_K(b)(\alpha)/\alpha.$$
Then $H_n(a,b)H_n(b,a)=1$. If additionally $p\nmid n$,
$u\in\mathcal O_K^\times$ and $b\in K^\times$ has valuation $r$, then
$$H_n(u,b)=\rho_n(u)^r,$$
where $\rho_n(u)$ is the unique element of $\mu_n$ whose residue is
$\bar u^{(q-1)/n}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and DC; the finite-residue local field, integer, roots of unity,
local Artin map and local Hilbert symbol in the Statement.

[F1] The current local Hilbert symbol is
$(x,y)_{K,n}=\operatorname{rec}_K(x)(\beta)/\beta$ for $\beta^n=y$;
its Kummer-cup expression is
$(x,y)_{K,n}=\eta(\kappa(y)\smile\kappa(x))$.
[[def-local-hilbert-symbol-over-field-containing-roots-of-unity]]
[[thm-local-hilbert-symbol-cup-product-norm-criterion]]

[F2] For a finite unramified extension of complete discretely valued fields
with finite residue fields, every base-field unit is a norm.
[[lem-unramified-local-unit-norms-and-cohomology]]

[F3] The local Artin map has norm subgroup as its kernel and sends a
uniformizer to arithmetic Frobenius on an unramified extension.
[[thm-local-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]]
[[thm-local-artin-unramified-frobenius-normalization]]

[F4] A finite unramified extension is determined by its residue extension,
and simple roots lift by Hensel's lemma.
[[thm-finite-unramified-local-extensions-classified-by-residue-extensions]]
[[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]

[F5] If $n$ is prime to the residue characteristic, reduction embeds
$\mu_n(K)$ as the subgroup of all $n$-th roots in $k^\times$, so
$n\mid(q-1)$. The cyclicity of $k^\times$ shows that
$\bar u^{(q-1)/n}$ is in this subgroup, and it therefore has a unique
preimage in $\mu_n(K)$. This is the local quantity $\rho_n(u)$.
[[lem-prime-to-n-roots-of-unity-reduce-injectively]]
[[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use the Kummer cup formula for skew-symmetry and local
arithmetic Frobenius for the unramified Kummer extension generated by a unit.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 The hypothesis $|\mu_n(K)|=n$ implies that $\operatorname{char}K$
does not divide $n$: otherwise in characteristic $\ell>0$ the polynomial
$X^n-1$ is an $\ell$-th power and has at most $n/\ell$ distinct roots.
The finite group $\mu_n(K)$ is cyclic. Indeed for each prime choose an
element whose order has the largest occurring power of that prime;
taking the corresponding prime-primary powers and multiplying them gives
an element of order equal to the group's exponent $m$. Every group
element is a root of $X^m-1$, so $n\le m$, while the constructed
element gives $m\le n$; hence $m=n$. This is a finite argument.
Fix such a primitive root $\zeta\in\mu_n$ and write the Kummer characters
$\kappa(a)(\sigma)=\zeta^{f(\sigma)}$ and
$\kappa(b)(\sigma)=\zeta^{g(\sigma)}$. Since $\mu_n\subseteq K$, both
characters have trivial Galois action on their values and $f,g$ are
homomorphisms to $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$. The multiplicative $1$-cochain
$c(\sigma)=\zeta^{f(\sigma)g(\sigma)}$ has coboundary
$$\frac{c(\sigma)c(\tau)}{c(\sigma\tau)}
=\zeta^{-f(\sigma)g(\tau)-f(\tau)g(\sigma)}.$$
Thus the two swapped cup-product classes are negatives of each other.
[F1, given, algebra]

1.2 By [F1], $H_n(a,b)$ is the local invariant image of
$\kappa(a)\smile\kappa(b)$, while $H_n(b,a)$ is the image of
$\kappa(b)\smile\kappa(a)$. Step 1.1 makes their product $1$.
[F1, step 1.1]

2.1 Now assume $p\nmid n$, suppose $u$ is a unit, and choose $\alpha^n=u$.
The reduction of
$X^n-u$ is separable because $n$ and $\bar\alpha$ are nonzero in $k$.
After a finite residue-field extension it has a simple root; by [F4] and
Hensel lifting, $K(\alpha)/K$ is unramified. Since $\mu_n\subseteq K$,
this extension is also finite abelian, so the local Artin interface applies.
[F4, given, construct]

2.2 Write $b=\pi^r w$ with $w$ a unit and $\pi$ a uniformizer. By [F2],
$w$ is a norm from $K(\alpha)$, so [F3] makes $\operatorname{rec}_K(w)$
trivial on that extension. The arithmetic Frobenius image of $\pi$ sends
$\bar\alpha$ to $\bar\alpha^q$, and its ratio on $\alpha$ is the unique
$n$-th root of unity reducing to
$$\bar\alpha^{q-1}=\bar u^{(q-1)/n}.$$
Thus $\operatorname{rec}_K(\pi)(\alpha)/\alpha=\rho_n(u)$, and the
homomorphism law gives
$H_n(u,b)=\operatorname{rec}_K(\pi)^r(\alpha)/\alpha=\rho_n(u)^r$
for every integer $r$. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

3.1 Steps 1.2 and 2.2 prove the skew-symmetry and tame residue formula,
respectively. [step 1.2, step 2.2] ∎
