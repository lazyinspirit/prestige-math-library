## Statement

With the declared use of the Axiom of Choice in the general integrable-module
decomposition, the empty connected component of the full partition crystal of
[[thm-the-crystal-of-level-one-fock-space-is-the-good-node-partition-graph]] is
the canonical highest weight crystal $B(\Lambda_0)$, with exactly the
$e$-regular labels. Its module is $M=U_q(\mathfrak g)|\varnothing\rangle$ and
its lower $A_0$-lattice is $M\cap F_0$; it occurs once. More precisely

$$F=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)^{\oplus p(k)},$$

a locally weight-finite indexed direct sum in which $p(k)$ is the number of
ordinary partitions of $k$; consequently
$\mathrm{ch}\,F=\mathrm{ch}\,V(\Lambda_0)\prod_{r\ge1}(1-e^{-r\delta})^{-1}$.
A highest **crystal** label need not be a highest **field** standard vector, and
the ambient canonical basis need not split these summands.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The full partition crystal with its empty component, the module $M=V(\Lambda_0)$, the decomposition theorem for integrable modules, and the highest weight crystal $B(\Lambda_0)$.

[F1] The empty component of the Fock crystal consists exactly of the $e$-regular partitions and contains the vacuum; the full crystal is a graph on all partitions ([[thm-the-crystal-of-level-one-fock-space-is-the-good-node-partition-graph]]).

[F2] The vacuum generates the simple integrable $V(\Lambda_0)$, whose canonical lower lattice is $M\cap F_0$ and whose residue crystal identifies with the empty component and the canonical $B(\Lambda_0)$ ([[prop-the-empty-partition-generates-an-integrable-highest-weight-submodule]], [[thm-the-highest-weight-crystal-b-lambda-exists]]).

[F3] Every chosen integrable weight module with finite-dimensional label spaces and support in a finite union of downward cones admits a module isomorphism to a locally weight-finite direct sum of simple highest weight modules carrying its crystal lattice and basis to the corresponding canonical highest weight lattices and crystals. Its connected crystal components and constituent multiplicities are thereby determined, but the field-module splitting is not asserted to be canonical. The decomposition uses the Axiom of Choice as declared ([[thm-crystal-bases-of-integrable-modules-decompose-into-highest-weight-crystals]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** inspect the highest crystal vertices by cancellation, count their weights, and apply the decomposition theorem with the declared choice.

1.1 The $\Lambda_0$ isotypic part. The weight $\Lambda_0$ weight space of $F$ is one-dimensional, spanned by the vacuum; since $\Lambda_0$ is a maximal weight, the top isotypic submodule is generated by the vacuum and equals the simple module $M=V(\Lambda_0)$ by [F2], whose crystal is the empty component $B(\Lambda_0)$ by [F1]. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 All highest crystal vertices. In decreasing-content order a block of $a$ equal rows of length $L$, starting at row $r$, contributes its addable corner of content $L-r+1$ followed by its removable corner of content $L-r-a+1$. These are consecutive in the full boundary list: the next block's addable content is strictly smaller than this removable content. For the top block, its only preceding plus is this addable corner, so its minus can cancel exactly when $a\equiv0\pmod e$. If not, that minus survives: $+-$ reduction cannot pair it with a later plus. If $e\mid a$, the two same-residue signs cancel and can be deleted from the signature; deleting the top block from the diagram shifts all remaining contents by $a$, preserving their residues. Induction over the blocks proves that all minuses cancel exactly when every positive part multiplicity is divisible by $e$. Such a shape repeats every row of a unique ordinary partition $\rho$ exactly $e$ times. In each stack of $e$ equal rows, every column has one cell of each residue, so $m_i=|\rho|$ for all $i$. Its weight is $\Lambda_0-|\rho|\delta$, and the number of these highest labels at weight $\Lambda_0-k\delta$ is $p(k)$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 The decomposition. The full Fock module has finite label spaces and support in $\Lambda_0-Q^+$, so [F3] applies and identifies the field constituents of the highest crystal labels of step 1.2; combined with step 1.1 this gives $F=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)^{\oplus p(k)}$ as a locally weight-finite indexed sum, and taking characters gives the displayed product formula. The raw standard vectors attached to the non-regular highest labels are not asserted to be field-highest vectors, and the ambient basis is not asserted to be component-compatible. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F3, given, algebra]

3.1 Conclusion. The empty component is the canonical $B(\Lambda_0)$ with the $e$-regular labels, its module and lower lattice are $M$ and $M\cap F_0$, it occurs once, and the full decomposition has $p(k)$ copies of $V(\Lambda_0-k\delta)$, with the Axiom of Choice used only through the declared decomposition [F3]. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

The module isomorphism in [F3] involves choices of summands and lifts. For example, at $k=2$ its two isomorphic constituents can be mixed by module automorphisms. This does not change their multiplicity or the canonical highest weight crystal assigned to each connected component.
