## Definition

Retain the binary colouring $c(\alpha,\beta)=o(\alpha,\beta)\bmod2$ from
[[thm-moore-oscillation-colouring-pattern]].  For $\alpha<\omega_1$, put

$$W_\alpha=\{\alpha\}\cup\{\beta:\alpha<\beta<\omega_1\text{ and }c(\alpha,\beta)=1\}.$$

For $X\subseteq\omega_1$, let $\tau[X]$ be the topology on $X$ generated by
declaring $W_\xi\cap X$ clopen for every $\xi\in X$.  Equivalently, finite
intersections of sets $W_\xi\cap X$ and $X\setminus W_\xi$, with
$\xi\in X$, form a clopen base.  The empty intersection is $X$; when $X$ is
empty this gives its unique topology.

There is a useful product representation with no extra generators.  Let
$\mathbb T$ be the unit circle and define

$$e_X:X\longrightarrow\{-1,1\}^{\omega_1}\subseteq\mathbb T^{\omega_1}$$

by

$$e_X(x)(\xi)=\begin{cases}-1,&\xi\in X\text{ and }x\in W_\xi,\\1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}$$

For $\xi\in X$, the inverse images of the two coordinate values are
$W_\xi\cap X$ and its complement; for $\xi\notin X$, the coordinate is
constant.  Thus the product-induced topology is exactly $\tau[X]$.  If
$x<y$ are in $X$, then $x\notin W_y$ while $y\in W_y$, so the $y$-coordinate
separates their images.  Hence $e_X$ is injective and is an embedding onto its
image.

The family $\{W_\xi\cap X:\xi\in X\}$ is point-countable and point-separating:
if $x\in W_\xi$, then $\xi\leq x$, and the countable ordinal $x+1$ has only
countably many members.  The displayed clopen base and point separation make
every $(X,\tau[X])$ zero-dimensional Hausdorff, hence regular under the
library convention.

Coordinates outside $X$ were deliberately padded by the constant $1$.
Allowing membership in $W_\xi$ at such a coordinate would add subbasic sets
that Moore did not put into $\tau[X]$.
