## Statement

Assume Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Let $F$ be a codimension-$q$ regular foliation of a smooth manifold $M$ with normal
bundle $\nu=TM/TF$, let $\nabla^{B}$ be the Bott partial connection, and let $\nabla$ be
any connection on $\nu$ extending it: $\nabla_X=\nabla^B_X$ for every $X\in\Gamma(TF)$.
Let $I\subseteq\Omega^*(M)$ be the differential ideal generated by the $1$-forms that
vanish on $TF$, i.e. the ideal locally generated by a coframe of the annihilator bundle
of $TF$. Then, in a local frame of $\nu$ that is parallel along the leaves for
$\nabla^B$, the curvature matrix entries of $\nabla$ lie in $I$; consequently every
coefficient of the curvature two-form $R^\nabla\in\Omega^2(M;\mathrm{End}\,\nu)$ lies in
$I$, and $I^{q+1}=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. A codimension-$q$ regular foliation $F$ with tangent distribution $TF$ and normal bundle $\nu=TM/TF$, the Bott partial connection $\nabla^B$ on $\nu$, and a connection $\nabla$ on $\nu$ with $\nabla_X=\nabla^B_X$ for every $X\in\Gamma(TF)$.

[F1] The Bott partial connection is well defined, is $C^\infty$-linear in the vector-field variable, satisfies the Leibniz rule, and has vanishing curvature $R^B(X,Y)s=0$ for leaf-tangent fields $X,Y$. ([[lem-the-bott-partial-connection-is-well-defined-and-flat-in-leaf-directions]]).

[F3] In a local frame with connection matrix $\omega$ and curvature matrix $\Omega$, the structure equation $\Omega=d\omega+\omega\wedge\omega$ holds. ([[thm-curvature-two-form-structure-equation]]).

[F4] For homogeneous smooth forms $\alpha,\beta$ of degrees $p,q$ one has $d(\alpha\wedge\beta)=d\alpha\wedge\beta+(-1)^p\alpha\wedge d\beta$. ([[thm-the-exterior-derivative-is-a-graded-derivation]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In a foliation chart $(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-q},y_1,\ldots,y_q)$, the annihilator of $TF$ is spanned by $dy_1,\ldots,dy_q$. Thus the ideal $I$ consists locally of sums $\sum_jdy_j\wedge\alpha_j$, and is closed under $d$, because $d(dy_j)=0$ and $d(dy_j\wedge\alpha_j)=-dy_j\wedge d\alpha_j$. The classes $e_j=\pi(\partial_{y_j})$ form a local frame of $\nu$ parallel for the Bott connection: if $X=\sum_iX^i\partial_{x_i}$ then $[X,\partial_{y_j}]=-\sum_i(\partial_{y_j}X^i)\partial_{x_i}$ is leaf-tangent. [given, F1, F4]

2.1 Use the extending connection supplied in the statement. Its connection entries $\theta^a{}_b$ in the frame of step 1.1 vanish on every leaf direction, since $\nabla_Xe_b=\nabla^B_Xe_b=0$ there. Hence $\theta^a{}_b\in I$. [given, step 1.1]

3.1 The structure equation gives $\Omega^a{}_b=d\theta^a{}_b+\sum_c\theta^a{}_c\wedge\theta^c{}_b$. Both summands lie in $I$, by its differential-ideal property and step 2.1. A frame change conjugates the curvature matrix by smooth function matrices, so every curvature entry in every frame lies in $I$. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Every product of $q+1$ local elements of $I$ contains $q+1$ factors drawn from the $q$ forms $dy_j$; alternating multiplication forces a repeated factor and gives zero. Thus $I^{q+1}=0$, including $q=0$, where $I=0$. This proves the assertions without asserting existence of an extension or choosing a global cover. [step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
