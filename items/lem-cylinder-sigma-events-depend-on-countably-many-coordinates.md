## Statement

Assume countable choice. For every $A\in\mathcal C_I$ there is a countable
$J\subseteq I$ such that $x|_J=y|_J$ implies
$x\in A\Longleftrightarrow y\in A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable choice, the arbitrary product $E$, and its cylinder sigma-algebra.

[F1] The cylinder sigma-algebra is generated by finite-coordinate cylinders. ([[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]])

[F2] Under countable choice, a countable union of countable sets is countable. ([[thm-countable-union-of-countable]])

## Proof

1.1 Let $\mathcal D$ be the events determined by some countable coordinate set. Complements preserve the same support. For a countable family in $\mathcal D$, countable choice selects one countable support for each event, and [F2] makes their union countable; that union determines the union of the events. Thus $\mathcal D$ is a sigma-algebra. [F2]

2.1 Every finite-coordinate cylinder belongs to $\mathcal D$, using its finite support. By generated-sigma minimality and [F1], $\mathcal C_I\subseteq\mathcal D$. [F1] ∎
