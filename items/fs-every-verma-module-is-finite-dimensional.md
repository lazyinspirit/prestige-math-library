## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Every Verma module for a complex semisimple Lie
algebra is finite-dimensional.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with basis $e,f,h$ and bracket $[e,f]=h$, $[h,e]=2e$, $[h,f]=-2f$ ([[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]], [[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]), the Borel subalgebra $\mathfrak b=\mathbb Ch\oplus\mathbb Ce$ ([[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]]), and the functional $\lambda=0$ on $\mathfrak b$ with $\lambda(\mathfrak b)=0$. In this item the **Verma module** attached to $\lambda$ is the induced module $M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak b)}\mathbb C_\lambda$; for $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ and $\lambda=0$ we realise it concretely as $M=U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)/J$, where $J$ is the left ideal generated by $e$ and $h$, with $v=1+J$ ([[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space and highest-weight theory used below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] The set $\{f,h,e\}$ is an ordered basis of $\mathfrak{sl}_2$, so by PBW the monomials $f^ah^be^c$ form a basis of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$, and the monomials $f^k$ with $k\ge0$ form a basis of $U(\mathbb Cf)$, the enveloping algebra of the span of $f$ ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]]).

[L2] In the quotient $M=U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)/J$ one has $e\cdot v=0$ and $h\cdot v=0$, because $e,h\in J$; and $f^k\cdot v$ is the class of $f^k$. [definition of $M$]

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The classes of the monomials $f^k$, $k\ge0$, are linearly independent in $M$: let $\pi:U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\to U(\mathbb Cf)$ be the linear map sending a PBW monomial $f^ah^be^c$ to $f^a$ when $b=0$ and $c=0$ and to $0$ otherwise, so that $\pi(u)=u$ for $u\in U(\mathbb Cf)$; then $\pi$ vanishes on $J=U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)e+U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)h$, because every element of $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)e$ has zero component along $f^a$ in the ordered PBW basis of [L1], and for $uh$ with $u=\sum_Af^Au_A$, $u_A\in U(\mathfrak h\oplus\mathbb Ce)$, the component of $f^Au_Ah$ along $f^A$ equals the augmentation $\varepsilon(u_Ah)=\varepsilon(u_A)\varepsilon(h)=0$; hence $\sum_kc_kf^k\in J$ forces $\sum_kc_kf^k=\pi(\sum_kc_kf^k)=0$ and all $c_k=0$. [L1, L2, A1]

2.1 Therefore the vectors $f^k\cdot v$, $k\ge0$, form an infinite linearly independent family in $M$; in particular $M$ is infinite-dimensional. [L2, step 1.1]

3.1 Since $M$ is a Verma module (it is the induced module $U(\mathfrak{sl}_2)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak b)}\mathbb C_0$ in the realisation above) and its dimension is infinite, the universal statement of the Statement section is false. [step 2.1] ∎
