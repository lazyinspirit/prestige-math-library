## Statement

Let $(W_1,\theta_0,\theta_1)$ be a bordism from a closed smooth $n$-manifold
$M_0$ to a closed smooth $n$-manifold $M_1$, and let
$(W_2,\theta'_0,\theta'_1)$ be a bordism from $M_1$ to a closed smooth
$n$-manifold $M_2$, with the same $M_1$ in both
([[def-unoriented-smooth-cobordism-of-closed-manifolds]]). The outgoing collar
$\theta_1:(-1,0]\times M_1\to W_1$ of the first and the incoming collar
$\theta'_0:[0,1)\times M_1\to W_2$ of the second agree on $M_1\times\{0\}$ after the seam identification and
combine to a bi-collar $M_1\times(-1,1)\to W_1\cup_{M_1}W_2$; thereby they
define a smooth structure on the glued space $W=W_1\cup_{M_1}W_2$ making it a
compact smooth $(n+1)$-manifold with boundary whose seam $M_1$ is interior.
The structure is canonical for the given data: it is the maximal atlas
generated by the two given smooth structures and the bi-collar, and, assuming $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]), a change of
collar presentation changes it at most by a diffeomorphism fixed off a
neighbourhood of the seam, by the collar-comparison argument in
[[thm-the-double-has-a-well-defined-smooth-structure]]. The boundary
decomposes as $M_0\sqcup M_2$, and the outer collars $\theta_0$ and
$\theta'_1$ make $W$ a bordism from $M_0$ to $M_2$.

In the oriented theory, if both bordisms are oriented
([[def-oriented-smooth-cobordism]]), the induced orientations on the common
boundary component are opposite, the orientations glue to an orientation of
$W$, and with that orientation $W$ is an oriented bordism from $M_0$ to $M_2$.
The glued bordism and transitivity use only the supplied collars and require
no choice principle. The additional comparison of different collar systems
uses $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ through the cited comparison theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Bordisms $(W_1,\theta_0,\theta_1)$ from $M_0$ to $M_1$ and $(W_2,\theta'_0,\theta'_1)$ from $M_1$ to $M_2$, with $\theta_1:(-1,0]\times M_1\to W_1$ and $\theta'_0:[0,1)\times M_1\to W_2$. In the oriented case, orientations of $W_1,W_2$ satisfying the oriented bordism conditions.

[F1] Each $W_i$ is a compact smooth manifold with boundary; the boundary parts $(\partial W_i)_0,(\partial W_i)_1$ are open and closed in $\partial W_i$, and the collars are smooth embeddings onto open collar neighbourhoods with $\theta_i(\{0\}\times M_i)=(\partial W_i)_i$ ([[def-unoriented-smooth-cobordism-of-closed-manifolds]], [[def-smooth-collar-of-a-manifold-boundary]], [[def-smooth-immersion-and-embedding-for-manifolds-with-boundary]]).

[F2] An oriented bordism condition is equivalent to its collars being orientation-preserving for the interval-first product orientations; the induced boundary orientation is outward-normal-first and independent of the outward field ([[def-oriented-smooth-cobordism]], [[def-induced-boundary-orientation]], [[prop-boundary-orientation-is-independent-of-the-outward-vector-field]]).

[F3] A quotient of a topological space carries the quotient topology, characterised by the universal property for continuous maps out of it ([[def-quotient-topology]]).

[F4] A smooth structure is a maximal smooth atlas; compatibility of charts is smoothness of transitions in the local-extension sense; smooth embeddings are smooth maps that are homeomorphisms onto their images, and open subsets carry restricted smooth structures ([[def-smooth-chart-atlas-and-structure-on-a-manifold-with-boundary]], [[def-smooth-manifold]], [[def-smooth-map-between-manifolds-with-boundary]], [[def-diffeomorphism-and-local-diffeomorphism-of-manifolds]], [[prop-an-open-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold-has-a-canonical-restricted-smooth-structure]]); products carry the product smooth structure ([[prop-products-of-smooth-manifolds-have-a-canonical-product-smooth-structure]]).

[F5] Assuming $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[def-countable-choice]]), the double admits comparison diffeomorphisms for different collars ([[thm-the-double-has-a-well-defined-smooth-structure]], proof steps 4.1–9.1). That proof constructs on a labelled half a boundary-fixing diffeomorphism $H$ with $H(c_0(p,t))=c_1(p,t)$ near the boundary. Its cutoff can be supported in a chosen neighbourhood of the boundary. The same construction applies near an open-and-closed boundary part, leaving the other parts fixed; it is used only in step 3.2 below.

[F6] Continuous images of compact spaces are compact ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]]), finite products of Hausdorff spaces are Hausdorff ([[lem-products-preserve-t0-t1-and-hausdorff]]), and subspaces of second-countable spaces are second-countable ([[prop-second-countability-is-hereditary]]). A binary product of second-countable spaces needs no countable choice: instantiate one countable basis in each factor, and enumerate their products by pairs of natural numbers. A finite union of bases of an open cover is a basis of the whole space.

## Proof

1.1 (The glued space and the bi-collar.) Let $X:=W_1\sqcup W_2$ and let $\sim$ be the equivalence relation on $X$ generated by $\theta_1(0,x)\sim\theta'_0(0,x)$ for $x\in M_1$; put $W:=X/{\sim}$ with the quotient topology. Define $$\Theta:M_1\times(-1,1)\to W,\qquad \Theta(x,s)=\begin{cases}[\theta_1(s,x)],&s\le0,\\ [\theta'_0(s,x)],&s>0.\end{cases}$$ The two formulas agree at $s=0$ and each is continuous on its closed half, so $\Theta$ is continuous; it is injective because $\theta_1,\theta'_0$ are injective and their images meet exactly in the identified seam; and it is open onto its image: for a relatively open subset of $M_1\times(-1,1)$ the two half-images are open in the collar neighbourhoods of $W_1$ and $W_2$, whose union is saturated. Hence $O:=\Theta(M_1\times(-1,1))$ is open in $W$ and $\Theta$ is a homeomorphism onto $O$. The images $A,B$ of $W_1\setminus(\partial W_1)_1$ and $W_2\setminus(\partial W_2)_0$ are open in $W$ and homeomorphic to those open submanifolds, and $W=A\cup B\cup O$. [F1, F3]

2.1 (The smooth atlas.) For a chart $(V,\psi)$ of $M_1$ define the seam chart $$\Psi:\Theta(V\times(-1,1))\to\psi(V)\times(-1,1),\qquad \Psi(\Theta(x,s))=(\psi(x),s).$$ Take the atlas consisting of the charts of $W_1$ with domain disjoint from $(\partial W_1)_1$, the charts of $W_2$ with domain disjoint from $(\partial W_2)_0$, and all seam charts $\Psi$. Its domains cover $W$ by step 1.1 and are open in $W$. Transitions between two charts from the same half are smooth by the given structures; a chart of the first half and one of the second have disjoint domains; two seam charts have transition $(\psi_2\circ\psi_1^{-1},\operatorname{id})$, smooth in $M_1$ coordinates; and a seam chart with a chart of $W_1$ (or $W_2$) has overlap in $s<0$ (or $s>0$), where it equals the smooth expression $\alpha\circ\theta_1$ (or $\alpha\circ\theta'_0$) in the coordinates $(\psi(x),s)$. Thus the generated maximal atlas is a smooth structure of dimension $n+1$ with boundary; each point of the seam lies in the seam chart and has a Euclidean neighbourhood. [F4, step 1.1]

3.1 (Boundary, outer collars and bordism structure.) The seam is $O_0:=\Theta(M_1\times\{0\})$, which every seam chart exhibits as an interior hypersurface $\{s=0\}$; hence the boundary of $W$ is the image of $(\partial W_1)_0\sqcup(\partial W_2)_1$. Put $(\partial W)_0:=\operatorname{im}(\partial W_1)_0$ and $(\partial W)_1:=\operatorname{im}(\partial W_2)_1$; these are open and closed in $\partial W$ and cover it. The outer collars $\theta_0:[0,1)\times M_0\to W_1\subset W$ and $\theta'_1:(-1,0]\times M_2\to W_2\subset W$ are unchanged smooth embeddings onto open collar neighbourhoods of these parts with the required normalisation, so $(W,\theta_0,\theta'_1)$ is a bordism from $M_0$ to $M_2$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 (Canonicity and conditional collar comparison.) The atlas of step 2.1 is determined by the supplied collars: changing a chart of $M_1$ changes a seam chart by $(\psi_2\circ\psi_1^{-1},\operatorname{id})$, so the generated maximal atlas is unchanged. For the additional comparison assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. Normalize outgoing collars by $c(p,t)=\theta_1(-t,p)$ and incoming collars by $c(p,t)=\theta'_0(t,p)$. Apply the boundary-fixing comparison construction [F5] separately in $W_1$ and $W_2$, supported near their seam parts, to obtain $H_1,H_2$ intertwining the old and new collars near the seam. They descend to a bijection of the glued spaces because both fix the seam points; in the old source and new target seam coordinates the descended map is $(p,s)\mapsto(p,s)$ near $s=0$. Away from the seam it and its inverse are the smooth maps $H_i,H_i^{-1}$. Thus it is a diffeomorphism fixed off a neighbourhood of the seam. Only this comparison uses [F5]; construction and transitivity use the given collars directly. [F5, step 2.1]

4.1 (The orientations glue.) Assume both bordisms oriented. By [F2], $\theta_1$ is orientation-preserving for the product orientation $ds\wedge o_1$ of $(-1,0]\times M_1$, and $\theta'_0$ is orientation-preserving for the product orientation $ds\wedge o_1$ of $[0,1)\times M_1$: both use the same orientation $o_1$ of the seam and the interval coordinate first. In the bi-collar coordinates $(x,s)$ of step 1.1 this says that a basis $(\partial_s,\partial_x)$ is positive in the orientation of $W_1$ for $s<0$ and in the orientation of $W_2$ for $s>0$. Therefore the chart system that declares all charts of the first half positive for the orientation of $W_1$ and all charts of the second half positive for the orientation of $W_2$ is consistent on overlaps: the transition across the seam is the identity in the coordinates $(x,s)$ with Jacobian determinant $+1$. By step 2.1 this defines one orientation of $W$. Equivalently, the induced orientations of the common boundary component are $+M_1$ as the outgoing face of $W_1$ and $-M_1$ as the incoming face of $W_2$, so they are opposite and glue. On the outer faces the induced orientations are $-M_0$ and $M_2$, unchanged from the two bordisms, and the outer collars remain orientation-preserving; hence $W$ with this orientation is an oriented bordism from $M_0$ to $M_2$. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 (Compactness, Hausdorffness and second countability.) The finite disjoint union $X=W_1\sqcup W_2$ is compact Hausdorff: finite subcovers of its two summands combine, and separation is checked within a summand or by the disjoint summands. Let $q:X\to W$ be the quotient. It is closed: the saturation of a closed $C\subseteq X$ adds only the images of its intersections with the two closed seam parts under the seam-identifying homeomorphism, hence is closed. For distinct $u,v\in W$, the fibres $q^{-1}(u),q^{-1}(v)$ are finite and disjoint. Hausdorffness of $X$ gives disjoint open sets $U,V$ containing these two fibres (intersect and unite finitely many separating neighbourhoods). The open sets $W\setminus q(X\setminus U)$ and $W\setminus q(X\setminus V)$ contain $u,v$ and are disjoint, proving Hausdorffness. Compactness follows from [F6]. Choose countable bases of $W_1,W_2,M_1$; their restrictions give bases of $A,B$, and the products of the basis of $M_1$ with rational intervals give a countable basis of $O$. Their union is a countable basis of $W$ by [F6]. Together with steps 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1 this makes $W$ the required compact smooth bordism, oriented when the supplied bordisms are. No choice axiom is needed for transitivity. [F1, F3, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
