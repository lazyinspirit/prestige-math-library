## Statement

Let $\lambda\in P^+$ and let $V(\lambda)=L(\lambda)$ be the simple integrable highest weight module with highest weight vector $v_\lambda$ ([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]]). Let $\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal B(\lambda),\mathcal L(\infty),\mathcal B(\infty)$ be the lattices and generating residue sets of [[def-kashiwara-lattices-lambda-and-infinity]], let $\tilde e_i,\tilde f_i$ be the Kashiwara operators, let $\pi_\lambda:U_q^-\to V(\lambda)$, $\pi_\lambda(P)=Pv_\lambda$, and let $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}$ be the tensor maps of [[lem-highest-weight-tensor-maps-and-their-adjunction]]. For $\alpha=\sum_in_i\alpha_i\in Q_+$ write $|\alpha|:=\sum_in_i$ and $Q_+(r):=\{\alpha\in Q_+:\ |\alpha|\le r\}$.

(i) For every integer $r\ge0$ the following interlocking statements are all true.

- A(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)$ for all $i\in I$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- B(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda)\cup\{0\}$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- C(r): for all $i$, $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $b\in\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha+\alpha_i}$ and $b'\in\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$, one has $\tilde f_ib=b'$ if and only if $b=\tilde e_ib'$.
- D(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}/q\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$.
- E(r): for all $\lambda,\mu\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}(\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$.
- F(r): for all $\lambda,\mu\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}((\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)$.
- G(r): $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}((\mathcal B(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal B(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha})\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda+\mu)\cup\{0\}$ for all $\lambda,\mu$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- H(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal L(\infty)$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- I(r): $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal B(\infty)\cup\{0\}$ for all $i$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- J(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$ and $b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ with $\tilde e_ib\neq0$, one has $b=\tilde f_i\tilde e_ib$.
- K(r): $\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}/q\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- L(r): $\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha})=\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$ for all $\lambda\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$.
- M(r): for all $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $i\in I$, $\lambda\in P^+$ and $P\in\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha+\alpha_i}$, one has $\tilde f_i(Pv_\lambda)\equiv(\tilde f_iP)v_\lambda\bmod q\mathcal L(\lambda)$.
- N(r): for all $\lambda\in P^+$ and $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$, $\pi_\lambda$ induces a bijection $\{b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}:\pi_\lambda(b)\neq0\}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal B(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$.
- O(r): for all $\lambda\in P^+$, $\alpha\in Q_+(r)$ and $b\in\mathcal B(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ with $\pi_\lambda(b)\neq0$, one has $\pi_\lambda(\tilde e_ib)=\tilde e_i\pi_\lambda(b)$.

(ii) Consequently $(\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal B(\lambda))$ is a crystal basis of $V(\lambda)$ in the sense of [[def-crystal-lattice-and-crystal-basis]]: $\mathcal L(\lambda)$ is a crystal lattice of $V(\lambda)$, $\mathcal B(\lambda)$ is a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $\mathcal L(\lambda)/q\mathcal L(\lambda)$ with $\mathcal B(\lambda)=\bigsqcup_\mu\mathcal B(\lambda)_\mu$, the operators satisfy $\tilde e_i\mathcal B(\lambda)\subseteq\mathcal B(\lambda)\cup\{0\}$ and the partial-inverse axiom, and $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V(\lambda)_\mu=\#\mathcal B(\lambda)_\mu$ for all $\mu$. The lattices $\mathcal L(\lambda)_\mu$ and $\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha}$ are free $A_0$-modules.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Dominant integral weights $\lambda,\mu$, the simple integrable highest weight modules and the lattices $\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal L(\infty)$ generated by the lowering words, the tensor maps $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu},S_{\lambda,\mu}$ and the operators with their string decompositions.

[F1] $V(\lambda)$ is simple and universally integrable cyclic of highest weight $\lambda$, with finite-dimensional weight spaces and support contained in $\lambda-Q_+$ ([[thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique]], [[def-quantum-verma-module-and-simple-highest-weight-quotient]]).

[F2] $\mathcal L(\lambda)=\bigoplus_\mu\mathcal L(\lambda)_\mu$, $\mathcal L(\infty)=\bigoplus_\nu\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\nu}$ are generated over $A_0$ by the lowering words, with the recursive spanning $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\sum_i\tilde f_i\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha+\alpha_i}$ for $\alpha\neq0$ and the same for $\mathcal L(\infty)$; each component is finitely generated, torsionfree and free over the DVR $A_0$ ([[def-kashiwara-lattices-lambda-and-infinity]], [[cor-finitely-generated-torsion-free-modules-over-a-pid-are-free]]).

[F3] Module operators: $\tilde f_i\tilde e_i=\mathrm{id}-p_{\mathrm{top}}$, $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}-p_{\mathrm{bottom}}$, $\ker\tilde e_i=\ker E_i$, $\ker\tilde f_i=\ker F_i$, and the string of an $E_i$-highest vector of rank-one weight $a$ has the positions $0,\dots,a$; the negative-half operators satisfy $\tilde e_i\tilde f_i=\mathrm{id}$, $\tilde e_iu=0$ iff $u\in\ker e_i'$, and $\tilde e_i$ lowers the root height by one ([[def-kashiwara-crystal-operators]], [[thm-the-i-string-decomposition-of-the-negative-half]]).

[F4] The maps $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu},\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}$ are the unique grading-preserving module maps with the prescribed top images, $\Psi_{\lambda,\mu}\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}=\mathrm{id}$, $\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}$ is an isometry onto the cyclic highest summand, and they are adjoint for the product form. The prescribed $S_{\lambda,\mu}$ is a $U_q^-$-linear retraction; its composite $S_{\lambda,\mu}\Phi_{\lambda,\mu}$ is the unique $U_q^-$-linear surjection $V(\lambda+\mu)\to V(\lambda)$ sending $v_{\lambda+\mu}$ to $v_\lambda$ ([[lem-highest-weight-tensor-maps-and-their-adjunction]]).

[F5] Rank-one tensor rule: for tensors of rank-one divided strings, modulo $q$ the operator $\tilde f_i$ acts on the first factor when $\varphi_i(\text{first})>\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$ and on the second factor otherwise, $\tilde e_i$ acts on the first factor when $\varphi_i(\text{first})\ge\varepsilon_i(\text{second})$ and on the second otherwise, with the partial-inverse property on the resulting chains; the chains partition the rectangle of divided positions ([[lem-rank-one-tensor-strings-have-the-crystal-limit-rule]]).

[F6] Finite-support regularization: there is an auxiliary regular Cartan datum with a finite coordinate summand containing the finitely many top labels used and all simple roots, with integral fundamental weights $\Lambda_i$, independent simple roots, the same $d_i$ and Cartan matrix, and $K_i=K_{d_iH_i}$; it transports the original finite-cone modules and their operations to the auxiliary datum. Fundamental and large auxiliary weights need not be in the transported label subgroup; they are auxiliary comparison modules. We prove the loop for all dominant auxiliary weights and then restrict to the transported original modules ([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]).

[F7] Negative-half form: the bilinear form on $U_q^-$ is symmetric, nondegenerate and $A$-valued on the word lattice, with the exact $A_{\mathrm{loc}}$ denominator bound on the divided lattice, the distinct module forms are the contravariant forms of [F4]. Their comparison with the half form is derived in step 8.1; no exact equality through $\pi_\lambda$ is asserted. The nondegeneracy and rank comparison inherit the axiom of choice declared in [[def-axiom-of-choice]] ([[thm-the-negative-half-bilinear-form-is-symmetric-nondegenerate-and-integral]], [[def-the-lusztig-kashiwara-bilinear-form-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F8] Finite matrix algebra: over a commutative ring a square matrix whose determinant is a unit is invertible, and over a local ring a finitely generated module is zero when its reduction modulo the maximal ideal is zero, by Nakayama ([[lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama]], [[def-the-local-ring-at-q-zero]]).

[F9] The axiom of choice may be used as declared; all other arguments below are finite and choice-free ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** induction. Prove the interlocking clauses A(r)-O(r) simultaneously by induction on $r=|\alpha|$, in the order of the JKK grand loop: field spanning and the base cases, then string-component extraction, the rank-one tensor computations, the large-weight microlocal congruences, the lattice projections and the pairing steps, closing each clause at height $r$ before the next.

**JKK clause map.** The stages below follow [JKK §7] in the owner-corrected order: string components (7.3)-(7.4), rank-one tensors (7.5), E (7.6), the last-color-change splitting (7.7), the tensor-lattice decomposition (7.8), the large-weight microlocal congruences (7.10)-(7.11), M and L (7.12)-(7.14), A and H (7.15)-(7.17), I, O and B (7.21)-(7.24), the module pairing (7.25), the large-weight normalization (7.26, corrected), the dual transfer and special F (7.27)-(7.28), J (7.29), C (7.30), D and K (7.31), N (7.34), and general F and G (7.33 and 7.36, with Lemmas 7.32 and 7.35).

1.1 Field spanning. On any fixed label space, $\operatorname{im}F_i=\operatorname{im}\tilde f_i$: in a string expansion $x=\sum_nF_i^{(n)}x_n$, one has $F_ix=\tilde f_i\sum_n[n+1]_iF_i^{(n)}x_n$, and conversely $\tilde f_ix=F_i\sum_n[n+1]_i^{-1}F_i^{(n)}x_n$. Both sums have the same label as $x$. The same identities hold on the negative half. Since every positive-height space is $\sum_iF_i$ of the preceding spaces, it is also $\sum_i\tilde f_i$ of those spaces. Induction from the highest line or the unit therefore proves that the lowering words span every field component. Together with [F2] this makes each $\mathcal L$ component a finite free full lattice. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 The base. [base] At height zero the top and unit have norm one, raising kills them and $\Phi,\Psi$ identify the tensor top. At degree $\alpha_i$, put $m=\lambda(h_i)$ and $n=\mu(h_i)$. The module lattice is $A_0F_iv_\lambda$ if $m>0$ and zero otherwise; the half lattice is $A_0F_i$. All projection, raising, basis and inverse clauses follow from these one-dimensional strings. In the tensor degree, $\Phi(F_iv_{\lambda+\mu})=F_iv_\lambda\otimes v_\mu+q_i^mv_\lambda\otimes F_iv_\mu$. If $m+n>0$, adjunction gives $\Psi(F_iv_\lambda\otimes v_\mu)=d(m,1)d(m+n,1)^{-1}F_iv_{\lambda+\mu}$ and $\Psi(v_\lambda\otimes F_iv_\mu)=q_i^md(n,1)d(m+n,1)^{-1}F_iv_{\lambda+\mu}$, where $d(k,1)=(1-q_i^{2k})/(1-q_i^2)$ and absent vectors contribute zero. These coefficients lie in $A_0$; their residues are respectively $1,0$ if $m>0$, and $0,1$ if $m=0<n$. If $m=n=0$ the degree vanishes. Thus E, F and G hold at height one as well, and all A-O base clauses hold. [base, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

1.3 Induction hypothesis. [ih] Fix $r\ge2$ and assume A-O at every height $<r$, simultaneously for all dominant weights in one auxiliary regular datum supplied by [F6]. All subsequent large weights and fundamental weights are taken in that datum. The transported original highest-weight and tensor modules have the same $E_i,F_i,K_i$, string operators, normalized forms and lowering words; consequently the final conclusions on them restrict to the original labels. [ih, F6, given]

2.1 String components below $r$. For $u=\sum_{n=0}^NF_i^{(n)}u_n\in\mathcal L_{\lambda-\alpha}$ with $|\alpha|<r$, the shift identity gives $u_N=\tilde e_i^Nu\in\mathcal L$. Subtract $\tilde f_i^Nu_N=F_i^{(N)}u_N$ and descend on $N$; each coefficient is integral. The same argument, using H$(r-1)$, applies to the half, and also applies to $q^k\mathcal L$ for every integer $k$. If $u$ represents a basis word residue, induct on $N$: when $\tilde e_iu\in q\mathcal L$, extracting its coefficients gives $u_n\in q\mathcal L$ for all $n\ge1$; otherwise its residue is a lower-height basis word by B or I, and C or J gives $u\equiv\tilde f_i\tilde e_iu\pmod{q\mathcal L}$. Induction and component extraction then leave exactly one nonzero coefficient residue, which is a highest word residue. This proves the lower-height single-string assertion without assuming it at height $r$. [F2, F3, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Lower-height tensor control. Extract the string coefficients of two lattice inputs of factor heights $<r$ by step 2.1. For each pair of nonzero highest coefficients $h,h'$, the full divided-string rectangle $\sum_{j,k}A_0F_i^{(j)}h\otimes F_i^{(k)}h'$ lies in the tensor lattice, since each divided position is an iterate of $\tilde f_i$. The rank-one lemma [F5] proves stability of this rectangle and its residue rule, and linearity gives stability for the original inputs. For basis residues, the single-string assertion of step 2.1 gives the strict/non-strict tensor comparisons and their partial inverses. A lower-height module basis residue killed by every raising must be its top: otherwise its recursive lowering predecessor and C$(r-1)$ supply a nonzero raising. Hence a tensor basis residue of lower factor heights killed by all raisings has first factor $v_\lambda$. Moreover, on a top-second tensor, lowering agrees modulo $q$ with lowering the first factor whenever that first lowering is nonzero; iterating this rule along a word that survives in the first factor gives the same word on the tensor top modulo $q$. [F2, F3, F5, step 2.1, step 1.3]

3.2 Large-weight estimates. Write $T_i=q_i^{-1}K_iE_i$. On $F_i^{(n)}u_n$ with top integer $a$, its coefficient to the preceding position is $q_i^{a-2n+1}[a-n+1]_i=q_i^{1-n}(1-q_i^{2(a-n+1)})/(1-q_i^2)$, whose inverse lies in $A_0$ for admissible $n\ge1$. If $T_iu\in q^N\mathcal L$ in the preceding degree, step 2.1 extracts each $u_n\in q^N\mathcal L$ for $n\ge1$. For fixed degree and fixed $m$, the differences between $\tilde f_i^mu$, $F_i^{(m)}u$ and between $\tilde e_iF_i^{(m)}u$, $F_i^{(m-1)}u$ involve only these positive components with Gaussian coefficients of bounded pole order; the second comparison requires $m\le\langle h_i,\mathrm{wt}(u)\rangle$, so the top term is not killed at its bottom. Taking $N$ larger than those finitely many pole orders makes both differences lie in $q\mathcal L$. Now for $e_i'Q=0$, the commutator gives $T_i(Qv_\lambda)=-(1-q_i^2)^{-1}q_i^{2\langle h_i,\lambda+\alpha_i+\mathrm{wt}Q\rangle}(e_i''Q)v_\lambda$. The last vector has degree below $r$; express it in a fixed finite set of half lowering words and apply L$(r-1)$ to obtain a pole bound independent of $\lambda$. The displayed power makes this arbitrarily small when $\lambda$ is large. Applying the two comparisons to each fixed half string $F_i^{(n)}Q$ proves the large-weight operator congruences needed at degrees $r-1$ and $r$, uniformly on any specified finite family of vectors. Finally $\pi_\lambda$ is a field isomorphism at degree $\alpha$ when $\lambda(h_j)\ge|\alpha|$: the highest-weight quotient ideal is generated by $F_j^{(\lambda(h_j)+1)}v_\lambda$, which are highest vectors by the rank-one formula and $[E_i,F_j]=0$ for $i\ne j$. Their generated submodules have degrees at least $\lambda(h_j)+1$, so they cannot meet that degree. [F1, F2, F3, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 E(r). The height-$r$ module lattice is the sum of $\tilde f_i$ of height-$r-1$ components. By naturality, $\Phi\tilde f_i=\tilde f_i\Phi$. E$(r-1)$ puts each preceding image in the tensor lattice; every tensor summand there has both factor heights $<r$, so step 3.1 proves the required lowering stability on those inputs. Thus $\Phi\mathcal L(\lambda+\mu)_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha}\subseteq\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$. [F2, F4, step 1.3, step 3.1]

4.2 Splitting at the last color change. For a nonpure word $\tilde f_{i_1}\cdots\tilde f_{i_r}$ take $t<r$ with $i_t\ne i_{t+1}=\cdots=i_r$, put $\lambda_0=\Lambda_{i_t}$ and take $\mu$ large. The repeated tail acts only on the second factor of $v_{\lambda_0}\otimes v_\mu$, because $\lambda_0(h_{i_r})=0$. The resulting tensor is $E_{i_t}$-highest, since $E_{i_t}$ commutes with that different-color tail; its next lowering equals $F_{i_t}v_{\lambda_0}\otimes\text{tail}+q_{i_t}v_{\lambda_0}\otimes F_{i_t}\text{tail}$. The second term lies in $q$ times the tensor lattice, and the first has both factor heights positive. Applying the remaining prefix uses only the lower factor-height rules of step 3.1. Consequently the full tensor word is congruent to a product of basis word residues, or zero, with both factor heights strictly between $0$ and $r$. This decomposition is used for large total weight $\lambda_0+\mu$, not for an arbitrary $\lambda$ lacking such a dominant subtraction. [F4, F5, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 M(r), L(r) and large-weight lattice isomorphism. For $P\in\mathcal L(\infty)$ of degree at most $r-1$, take $\mu$ large and apply $\Phi$ to the large-weight lowering congruence of step 3.2. E(r), already proved, carries its error into $q$ times the tensor lattice; L$(r-1)$ and E$(r-1)$ put $P(v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu)$ in that lattice. The retraction $S$ sends the tensor lattice onto $\mathcal L(\lambda)$: it extracts the second top coefficient. It commutes with lowering modulo $q$ on these inputs by step 3.1: string tensors with a positive-degree second factor are killed both before and after lowering, while a second top gives the first-factor lowering or a zero first-factor endpoint. Applying $S$ therefore gives M(r). Iterating along a word gives integrality of its projection and equality with the module word modulo $q$. Hence $\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}=\pi_\lambda(\mathcal L(\infty)_{-\alpha})+q\mathcal L(\lambda)_{\lambda-\alpha}$; Nakayama on the finitely generated quotient proves L(r). Nonzero projected word residues are precisely $\mathcal B(\lambda)$. For large $\lambda$, step 3.2 makes $\pi_\lambda$ a field isomorphism; L(r) then makes it a lattice isomorphism and hence a bijection on nonzero word residues, before any independence claim at height $r$. [F2, F4, F8, step 1.3, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2]

5.2 Tensor-lattice decomposition. At total degree $\alpha$ of height $r$, put $T=(\mathcal L(\lambda)\otimes\mathcal L(\mu))_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha}$ and $T'=\sum_i\tilde f_iT_{\lambda+\mu-\alpha+\alpha_i}+v_\lambda\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)_{\mu-\alpha}$. Step 3.1 gives $T'\subseteq T$ on the preceding degrees. An interior product basis residue has both factor heights below $r$ and positive first height; the top detection and partial inverse in step 3.1 give a nonzero raising and write it as the lowering of its predecessor. Thus every interior tensor lies in $T'+qT$. The second-top boundary is generated by module lowering words tensored with $v_\mu$; each nonzero such residue is congruent to its word on $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ by step 3.1, and that tensor word belongs to the lowering-image part of $T'$. Zero word residues already lie in $qT$. The remaining boundary is exactly $v_\lambda\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)_{\mu-\alpha}$. Hence $T=T'+qT$. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Nakayama upgrade. The quotient $T/T'$ is finitely generated, and step 5.2 says $T/T'=q(T/T')$. Nakayama [F8] makes it zero. Thus the tensor decomposition is an exact lattice equality at height $r$, with the first-top boundary $v_\lambda\otimes\mathcal L(\mu)$; it does not require a basis of either factor at height $r$. [F2, F8, step 5.2]

6.2 A(r) and H(r). Fix $i,\alpha$ and a finite set of dominant weights containing the desired $\tau$ and all $\Lambda_j$. Finite full lattices give a common pole bound $N$ for raising on these components and on the half. Steps 3.2 and 5.1 extend that bound to every sufficiently large weight. The same bound applies to raising on the total-degree tensor lattice: interior inputs are already integral by step 3.1; at a boundary, extracting the positive string coefficients of the raised factor in $q^{-N}\mathcal L$ uses only preceding degrees, and applying the rank-one rectangles bounds their tensor raising by $q^{-N}$. If $N>0$, a pure-color word raises integrally by rank one. For a nonpure word at large weight $\Lambda_j+\mu$, step 4.2 splits its tensor lift into an integral interior residue plus $qT$. Raising therefore lies in $T+q^{1-N}T=q^{1-N}T$, and applying $\Psi$ in raised degree uses F$(r-1)$ to improve its module bound to $q^{1-N}$. For any fixed $\tau$ and word $u$, if $u\in q\mathcal L(\tau)$ the old bound already improves by one; otherwise its tensor word is congruent to $u\otimes v_\mu$. The improved bound at large $\tau+\mu$, followed by E$(r-1)$, bounds the raising of that tensor word by $q^{1-N}T$. The $qT$ error also raises into $q^{1-N}T$. Extracting the positive string coefficients of $u$ into $q^{-N}\mathcal L(\tau)$ and using the rank-one top-second rule gives $\tilde e_i(u\otimes v_\mu)\equiv(\tilde e_iu)\otimes v_\mu\pmod{q^{1-N}T}$; extracting the second top then improves the bound for $u$. The large-weight operator congruence and lattice isomorphism similarly improve the half bound. All three bounds thus improve from $N$ to $N-1$; repeating finitely many times gives $N=0$, proving A(r), H(r). Component extraction as in step 2.1 now holds at height $r$, so all tensor lattice inputs of total height $r$ are stable under both operators by the same rectangle argument. [F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, step 4.2, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 I(r), O(r) and B(r). For a nonpure negative-half word use the interior tensor splitting of step 4.2, apply the rank-one tensor raising rule and the lower-height clauses B and G, then transport through the large-weight projection of step 3.2; the pure-color case is rank one, giving I(r). For O(r), take a surviving word residue in $V(\lambda)$: tensoring with a large second top gives the residue $u\otimes v_\mu$; raising on a top-second tensor is congruent to $\tilde e_iu\otimes v_\mu$ by the rank-one rule, and comparing with the large-weight negative-half raising calculation, applying $\Phi$ and then $S$, gives O(r). Then I(r), O(r) and the residue-image statement of step 5.1 give B(r), with no same-height C or F premise. [F4, F5, step 4.2, step 3.2, step 5.1, step 6.2]

7.2 The module lattice pairing. String extraction is justified by A(r). Homogeneous top string components of different positions are orthogonal, because repeated contravariance reduces their pairing to a highest vector paired with a positive $F_i$-power, which is zero; at equal position $n$ with top rank-one weight $a$, the exact norm factor is $q_i^{n(a-n)}\binom{a}{n}_i\in1+qA_0$. For a positive-height pair, recursively write one input as $\tilde f_i$ of a lower-height input, extract both strings and apply these unit norm ratios; this gives $\langle\mathcal L(\lambda),\mathcal L(\lambda)\rangle\subseteq A_0$ and $\langle\tilde f_iu,v\rangle\equiv\langle u,\tilde e_iv\rangle\bmod q$. At $n=0$ or $a=0$ the endpoint terms are zero, so no finite-string identity is misused. [F3, F4, step 6.2, algebra]

8.1 The large-weight pairing normalization, corrected. 9.1 A finite alternative for the half form. The recurrence in step 8.1 also constructs the half form without choice: on any fixed degree the module pairing is a polynomial in $x_i=q_i^{2\lambda(h_i)}$ with rational-function coefficients, by repeated first-letter removal. To prove nondegeneracy without its AC premise, first derive $e_i'e_j''=q_i^{a_{ij}}e_j''e_i'$ on words: commuting both sides past $F_k$ uses the two skew relations; the remaining coefficients agree because $q_j^{a_{ji}}=q_i^{a_{ij}}$, and induction starts at $1$. If a positive-degree $P$ is killed by every $e_i'$, then every $e_j''P$ has smaller degree and is also killed by every $e_i'$. Induction makes all $e_j''P=0$. The commutator then gives $E_iP=PE_i$ for every $i$. At large weight $Pv_\lambda$ is therefore a highest vector of strictly lower label; its cyclic submodule cannot contain $v_\lambda$, by triangular highest-weight support, so simplicity forces $Pv_\lambda=0$. Field injectivity from step 3.2 gives $P=0$. Finally a nonzero radical vector $P$ would have all $e_i'P$ in the lower-degree radical by the adjunction and symmetry; induction gives $e_i'P=0$ for all $i$, contradicting this separation. This supplies a choice-free form on each finite component whenever that qualification is needed.
