## Statement

Let $k\subseteq K$ be a field extension. If $K$ is finitely generated as a
$k$-algebra, then $K$ is a finite field extension of $k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field extension $k\subseteq K$ with $K$ finitely generated as a $k$-algebra.

[L1] A finitely generated field over $k$ is integral over a localization of a polynomial ring on any transcendence basis ([[lem-zariski-lemma-transcendence-basis-reduction]]).

[L2] A localization $k[t_1,\ldots,t_r][1/s]$ with $r>0$ is not a field ([[lem-zariski-lemma-localised-polynomial-ring-not-field]]).

[L3] A field generated by finitely many algebraic elements over $k$ is a finite extension of $k$ ([[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** contradiction.

1.1 Let $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ be a transcendence basis of $K$ over $k$. By [L1] there exists a nonzero $s\in k[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ such that $K$ is integral over $R:=k[t_1,\ldots,t_r][1/s]$. [L1, given, choose]

2.1 Assume $r>0$. Because $K$ is a field and integral over $R$, every nonzero element $a\in R$ is a unit of $R$: the inverse $a^{-1}\in K$ satisfies a monic equation over $R$, and multiplying by a large power of $a$ rewrites that equation as $$ a^{-1}=-(c_{n-1}+c_{n-2}a+\cdots+c_0a^{n-1})\in R. $$ Thus $R$ would be a field, contradicting [L2]. [L2, step 1.1, assume-contra, contradiction]

3.1 Therefore $r=0$, so the transcendence basis is empty and $K/k$ is algebraic. Since $K$ is finitely generated as a $k$-algebra, choose algebra generators $a_1,\ldots,a_m$; they are algebraic over $k$, and [L3] makes $K=k(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ finite over $k$. [L3, step 2.1, discharge-contradiction] ∎
