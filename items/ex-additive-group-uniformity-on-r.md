## Example

Under addition, $\mathbb R$ is a topological group. Its left, right, upper, and Roelcke uniformities all equal the uniformity generated by $|x-y|<\varepsilon$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The additive group $\mathbb R$ with its usual topology.

[L1] The group formulas for the left and right entourages are $x^{-1}y\in U$ and $yx^{-1}\in U$ ([[def-left-and-right-uniformities-of-a-topological-group]], [[def-group]]).

[L2] The upper and Roelcke structures are respectively generated by intersections and composites of left and right entourages ([[def-upper-and-roelcke-uniformities-of-a-topological-group]]).

[L3] The usual metric entourages are $|x-y|<\varepsilon$ ([[lem-metric-uniformity-dictionary]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In additive notation, both left and right conditions are $y-x\in U$; for symmetric interval neighbourhoods $U=(-\varepsilon,\varepsilon)$ this is $|x-y|<\varepsilon$. [L1, L3]

2.1 Hence the left and right uniformities equal the metric uniformity. [step 1.1]

3.1 Their upper join and Roelcke meet equal that same uniformity because the two inputs already agree. [step 2.1, L2] ∎
