## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a
split reductive group over $k$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, base
$\Delta=\{\alpha_i\}_{i\in I}$ and fundamental weights $\omega_i$
([[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]],
[[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). For every $i\in I$ there exist
a finite-dimensional rational representation $V_i$ of $G$ and a primitive vector
$v_i\in V_i$ whose weight $\lambda_i$ satisfies
$$\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=0\ (j\ne i),\qquad \langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle>0.$$
When the root datum is semisimple,
$\lambda_i=\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\omega_i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, base
$\Delta=\{\alpha_i\}_{i\in I}$ and $i\in I$.

[F1] *Standard maximal parabolic.* $P_i=P_{\Delta\smallsetminus\{\alpha_i\}}$
is the standard parabolic subgroup containing $B$ whose Levi subgroup contains
$T$ and the root groups $U_{\pm\alpha_j}$ for $j\ne i$, and $P_i$ does not
contain $U_{-\alpha_i}$
([[thm-parabolics-and-levi-decomposition]],
[[lem-standard-levi-subgroup]],
[[def-parabolic-subgroup-of-an-affine-algebraic-group]],
[[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] *Chevalley's theorem.* There is a finite-dimensional rational
representation $V$ of $G$ with a line $L\subseteq V$ such that
$\operatorname{Stab}_G(L)=P_i$ scheme-theoretically
([[thm-chevalley-line-stabilizer-of-an-algebraic-subgroup]]).

[F3] *Generated modules.* If $V$ is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive
vector of weight $\lambda$, then $\lambda$ is dominant, the weights of $V$ below
$\lambda$ are of the form $\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with
$m_\alpha\ge0$, and $\lambda$ has multiplicity one
([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]],
[[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F4] *Normalizer action and reflections.* For a representative $n$ of a Weyl
group element $w$, the translate of a weight vector $v\in V_\mu$ by $n$ has
weight $w\mu$; the simple reflection acts by
$s_{\alpha_j}(\mu)=\mu-\langle\mu,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\alpha_j$
([[lem-normalizer-action-permutes-weight-spaces]],
[[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]]).

[F5] *Coefficient uniqueness.* An element of $\mathbb Z\Phi$ has a unique
expression as $\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with
$m_\alpha\in\mathbb Z$
([[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

[F6] *Semisimple root datum.* If the root datum is semisimple, then
$X_0=\{x\in X:\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle=0\ \forall\alpha\}=0$ and the
fundamental weights form a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $X\otimes\mathbb Q$
([[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]],
[[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $(V,L)$ be as in [F2] and let $v\ne0$ generate $L$. Since $P_i\supseteq B\supseteq U$ and $P_i\supseteq T$, the line $L$ is $B$-stable, so $v$ is primitive; let $\lambda_i$ be its weight. Replacing $V$ by the $G$-submodule generated by $v$ does not change the line $L$, and by [F3] the weight $\lambda_i$ is then dominant. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 For $j\ne i$ choose a representative $n\in P_i(k)$ of the simple reflection $s_{\alpha_j}$, which exists because $P_i$ contains $U_{\pm\alpha_j}$ and $T$ by [F1]. Since $n$ stabilizes $L$, the vector $n\cdot v$ is a nonzero multiple of $v$, hence has weight $\lambda_i$; on the other hand by [F4] it has weight $s_{\alpha_j}(\lambda_i)=\lambda_i-\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\alpha_j$. Therefore $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\alpha_j=0$ and $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle=0$. [F1, F4, step 1.1]

2.2 For the remaining index, suppose $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=0$. Then $s_{\alpha_i}$ fixes $\lambda_i$; choose a representative $n_i\in N_G(T)(k)$ of this simple reflection. It takes $v$ to a nonzero vector of weight $s_{\alpha_i}(\lambda_i)=\lambda_i$. By [F3], the weight-$\lambda_i$ space in the module generated by $v$ is one-dimensional, so $n_i v$ is a nonzero multiple of $v$ and $n_i$ stabilizes $L$. Since $\operatorname{Stab}_G(L)=P_i$, this gives $n_i\in P_i$. But $U_{\alpha_i}\subseteq B\subseteq P_i$, and conjugation by $n_i$ carries $U_{\alpha_i}$ onto $U_{-\alpha_i}$; this contradicts [F1], which says $U_{-\alpha_i}\not\subseteq P_i$. Thus $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\ne0$, and dominance gives $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle>0$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 If the root datum is semisimple, then by [F6] the element $\lambda_i$ has the unique expression $\sum_j\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_j^\vee\rangle\omega_j$ in the basis of fundamental weights; by steps 2.1 and 2.2 only the $i$-th coefficient is nonzero, so $\lambda_i=\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\omega_i$ with $\langle\lambda_i,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle>0$. [F5, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Steps 1.1 to 2.1 produce, for each $i\in I$, a finite-dimensional representation and a primitive vector with the required pairings, and the stated semisimple form of the weight. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The proof uses Chevalley's line-stabilizer theorem to produce the maximal
  parabolic as a stabilizer, and then the Weyl-group action (the normalizer
  lemma) to read off the simple-coroot pairings; this is the intrinsic form of
  the Mostow argument recorded in the source's NOTES.
- For a general reductive root datum the element $\lambda_i$ need not be a
  multiple of a fundamental weight, because the annihilator of the coroots can
  be nonzero; only the pairings are asserted.
