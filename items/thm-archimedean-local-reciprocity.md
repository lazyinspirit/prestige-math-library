## Statement

For the archimedean local field $\mathbb C$, the absolute Galois group is
trivial and the local reciprocity quotient is trivial. For
$\mathbb R$, the maximal abelian extension is $\mathbb C$, and the map
$\mathbb R^\times/\mathbb R_{>0}\to\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb C/\mathbb R)$
that sends the class of $-1$ to complex conjugation is an isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fundamental theorem of algebra and the usual topology and multiplication on $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$.

[F1] Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a root, so $\mathbb C$ is algebraically closed. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-minimum-modulus-proof]]

[F2] The norm is the determinant of multiplication on the finite-dimensional extension. [[def-field-norm-and-trace]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify finite extensions and compute the quotient directly.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 By [F1], $\mathbb C$ has no nontrivial finite algebraic extension, hence its absolute Galois group is trivial and its reciprocity quotient has one element. [F1, given]

2.1 The only irreducible polynomials over $\mathbb R$ have degree one or two: for any $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\mathbb R$, its conjugate pair gives the irreducible real quadratic $(X-z)(X-\bar z)$, and [F1] splits every complex polynomial into linear factors. More explicitly, if a finite extension $E/\mathbb R$ is nontrivial, choose $\alpha\in E\setminus\mathbb R$. Its irreducible polynomial is quadratic, with negative discriminant, so $\mathbb R(\alpha)$ is isomorphic over $\mathbb R$ to $\mathbb C$. This subfield is algebraically closed by [F1], and $E$ is algebraic over it; hence $E=\mathbb R(\alpha)$. Thus the only finite extensions up to $\mathbb R$-isomorphism are $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$. The two $\mathbb R$-automorphisms of $\mathbb C=\mathbb R(i)$ send $i$ to $i$ and $-i$, so form the two-element group generated by conjugation. Every algebraic element lies in a finite extension; consequently $\mathbb C$ is also an algebraic closure and the maximal abelian extension of $\mathbb R$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $z=a+bi\ne0$, multiplication by $z$ on the real basis $1,i$ has matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&-b\\b&a\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ and determinant $a^2+b^2>0$. Every positive real is such a determinant, by taking $b=0$ and a positive square root for $a$. Thus the norm subgroup of $\mathbb C^\times$ is exactly $\mathbb R_{>0}$. The sign homomorphism has kernel $\mathbb R_{>0}$, because a nonzero real number is positive exactly when it is a square in $\mathbb R^\times$. Therefore $\mathbb R^\times/\mathbb R_{>0}$ has two classes, represented by $1,-1$. Sending these to identity and conjugation is a bijective homomorphism, as required. [F2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
