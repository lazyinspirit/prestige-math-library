## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice and DC. Let $K$ be a number field and $S$ a finite set of its places. Fix compatible primitive roots $\zeta_{2^r}$ and put $\eta_{2^r}=\zeta_{2^r}+\zeta_{2^r}^{-1}$. Let $s$ be the largest integer such that $\eta_{2^s}\in K$. For $m=2^tm'$ with $m'$ odd, put
$$P(m,S)=\{a\in K^\times:a\in K_v^{\times m}\text{ for every finite }v\notin S\}.$$
If $\operatorname{Gal}(K(\zeta_{2^t})/K)$ is cyclic, then $P(m,S)=K^{\times m}$. Otherwise $t>s\ge2$, $-1$ and $d=2+\eta_{2^s}$ are nonsquares in $K$, and every class in $P(m,S)/K^{\times m}$ is represented by $1$ or $a_0=d^{m/2}$. If $-d$ is a square in $K$, the second class is also trivial. If $-d$ is a nonsquare, put
$$S_0=\{v\mid2:\ -1,d,-d\text{ are all nonsquares in }K_v^\times\}.$$
Then $a_0\notin K^{\times m}$ and $a_0\in K_v^{\times m}$ at every finite place outside $S_0$, but not at places in $S_0$. Consequently $P(m,S)=K^{\times m}$ when $S_0\nsubseteq S$, and $P(m,S)=K^{\times m}\cup a_0K^{\times m}$ when $S_0\subseteq S$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The number field, finite place set, compatible roots, exponent and explicit choices of the Statement.

[A1] AC, Countable Choice and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] Odd exponents and cyclic prime-power cyclotomic extensions have the stated local-to-global power property. [[lem-odd-and-cyclic-prime-power-local-global-powers]]

[F2] Cyclotomic Galois groups embed in the unit group of the exponent ring. For a rational cyclotomic field, the stated residue degrees and prime counts imply unramifiedness by the degree formula; unramifiedness is preserved under local base extension. Irreducibility and the cyclotomic degree formula make the real cyclotomic degrees grow. ## Proof

**Proof technique:** reduce to the quadratic cyclotomic subfield, compute the two possible root classes, and use the full local decomposition group in the exceptional case.

1.1 The real cyclotomic degrees grow without bound, so $s$ exists; $\eta_4=0$ gives $s\ge2$. For $r\ge2$, $\zeta_{2^r}\in\mathbb Q(\eta_{2^r},i)$: its real part is $\eta_{2^r}/2$, and its imaginary part is the real part of $\zeta_{2^r}^{2^{r-2}-1}$, a polynomial in $\eta_{2^r}$ by the recurrence $z^{j+1}+z^{-(j+1)}=(z+z^{-1})(z^j+z^{-j})-(z^{j-1}+z^{-(j-1)})$. Thus if $t\le s$, $K(\zeta_{2^t})\subset K(i)$ and the cyclotomic extension is cyclic. If $i\in K$, the cyclotomic Galois group embeds by [F2] into the cyclic subgroup of units congruent to $1$ modulo $4$, again cyclic. Hence noncyclicity implies $t>s$ and $i\notin K$. Also $d=\eta_{2^{s+1}}^2$ is nonsquare in $K$, since $\eta_{2^{s+1}}\notin K$. The group of units modulo $2^t$ is $C_2\times C_{2^{t-2}}$, generated by $-1$ and $5$; a noncyclic subgroup has rank two, so its quotient by squares has order four. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If the cyclotomic extension is cyclic, [F1] gives $a\in K^{\times2^t}$ for $a\in P(m,S)$, and [F1] also gives $a\in K^{\times m'}$; coprime Bézout combines the two powers into an $m$th power. Assume now it is noncyclic. Put $K_1=K(i)$. Its cyclotomic extension of exponent $2^t$ has cyclic Galois group, so [F1] applied over $K_1$ gives $a=y^{2^t}$ with $y\in K_1$. If $\sigma$ is the nontrivial automorphism of $K_1/K$, the ratio $y/\sigma(y)$ is a $2^t$-root of unity inverted by $\sigma$. If it has order $2^\lambda$, the sum of that primitive root and its inverse belongs to $K$, hence $\lambda\le s$. As $\zeta_{2^s}\in K_1$ and $\sigma$ inverts it by step 1.1, write $y/\sigma(y)=\zeta_{2^s}^j$. Since $(1+\zeta_{2^s})/\sigma(1+\zeta_{2^s})=\zeta_{2^s}$, the element $y'=y/(1+\zeta_{2^s})^j$ lies in $K$. The identity $(1+\zeta_{2^s})^2=\zeta_{2^s}d$ and $t>s$ give $(1+\zeta_{2^s})^{2^t}=d^{2^{t-1}}$. Thus $a$ is either a $2^t$th power or $d^{2^{t-1}}$ times one. Since both $a$ and $a_0=d^{2^{t-1}m'}$ are $m'$th powers and $m'$ is odd, coprime Bézout gives the asserted two possible $m$th-power classes. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $-d$ is a square in $K$, then $a_0=(-d)^{m/2}$ is an $m$th power because $m/2$ is even. Assume instead that $-1,d,-d$ are all nonsquares. Then $F=K(i,\sqrt d)=K(\zeta_{2^{s+1}})$ is biquadratic over $K$ and is contained in $L=K(\zeta_{2^t})$. Its Galois group is the maximal exponent-two quotient of $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$: step 1.1 gives $|G/G^2|=4$, while $F/K$ has degree four. For any finite place $v$, if at least one of $-1,d,-d$ is a local square, then $a_0$ is an $m$th power locally: use respectively the roots $1+\zeta_{2^s}$, $\sqrt d$, or $i\sqrt d$; their $m$th powers all equal $a_0$ because $4\mid m$. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 If all three elements are nonsquares in $K_v$, their square classes are independent, so the local biquadratic extension $F_v/K_v$ has degree four. The decomposition group $D_v\le G$ maps onto $G/G^2$. Since $G$ is a finite abelian $2$-group, this forces $D_v=G$: otherwise the nonzero finite $2$-group $G/D_v$ cannot equal twice itself. At $v\nmid2$, [F2] makes $L/K$ unramified and its decomposition group cyclic, contradicting the noncyclicity of $G$. Thus every such $v$ lies above $2$, as claimed for $S_0$. [F2, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 To show $a_0$ is not an $m$th power at $v\in S_0$, suppose it were. It is then a $2^t$th power, whose roots are $b_r=\zeta_{2^t}^r(1+\zeta_{2^s})^{m'}\in L$. Some $b_r$ lies in $K_v$, so the decomposition group $D_v=G$ fixes it and $b_r\in K$. Squaring gives $b_r^2=\zeta_{2^t}^{2r+2^{t-s}m'}d^{m'}\in K^2$. The root-of-unity factor is therefore in $K$; because $i\notin K$, the only $2$-power roots of unity in $K$ are $\pm1$. Since $m'$ is odd, this would make either $d$ or $-d$ a square in $K$, contrary to the assumptions. The same argument without a place shows $a_0\notin K^{\times m}$. Combining this with steps 2.1–4.1 proves the two alternatives for $P(m,S)$, including empty $S_0$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, contradiction, algebra] ∎
