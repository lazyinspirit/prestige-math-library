## Statement

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a commutative Noetherian local ring, $M$ a finitely generated $R$-module, and $I$ an ideal such that $\ell_R(M/IM)<\infty$. For every integer $n\geq0$, $M/I^{n+1}M$ has finite length. There is a unique $P\in\mathbb Q[T]$ with $P(n)=\ell_R(M/I^{n+1}M)$ for all sufficiently large integers $n$. If $I=R$ or $M=0$, then $P=0$. No degree/dimension assertion is part of this lemma.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$, a finite module $M$, and an ideal $I$ with $\ell_R(M/IM)<\infty$.

[F1] Associated graded pieces and their multiplication are defined in [[def-associated-graded-ring-and-module]].

[F2] Length counts simple factors, including length zero for the zero module: [[def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module]].

[F3] Finite length passes to submodules and quotients and is additive in short exact sequences: [[cor-length-is-additive-in-short-exact-sequences]].

[F4] Polynomial extension preserves Noetherianity: [[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]].

[F5] A finite module over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian (every submodule is finite): [[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]].

[F6] Quotients of Noetherian rings are Noetherian: [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]].

[F7] The local ring has unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and residue field $k=R/\mathfrak m$: [[def-local-ring]].

[F8] Every ideal of a Noetherian ring is finitely generated, using only the choice-free equivalence of conditions 1 and 2 in [[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $I=R$ or $M=0$, every quotient in the statement is zero, giving the polynomial zero. More generally put $N=M/IM$. If $N=0$, then $M=IM$ implies $M=I^aM$ by repeated multiplication for every $a\geq1$, so again all quotients are zero. Uniqueness in these cases follows from the polynomial argument at the end. Henceforth $I\ne R$ and $c=\ell_R(N)>0$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For a simple $R$-module $S$ and $0\ne s\in S$, $Rs=S$; the map $R\to S$ taking $a$ to $as$ is onto and its kernel is maximal, since a nontrivial intermediate ideal would give a nontrivial submodule. Thus the kernel is $\mathfrak m$ and $S\cong k$. A composition series $0=N_0\subset\cdots\subset N_c=N$ therefore satisfies $\mathfrak mN_j\subseteq N_{j-1}$. Iteration gives $\mathfrak m^cN=0$. [F2, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Set $A=R/\mathfrak m^c$, a Noetherian ring. Each $\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}$ for $0\leq j<c$ is generated by finitely many elements and killed by $\mathfrak m$, hence is a finite-dimensional $k$-space. Indeed a finite spanning list can be reduced, by deleting any member in the span of the others, to a basis; the flag of initial basis spans has simple factors $k$. Its $R$-length is its dimension. Applying length additivity to the finite $\mathfrak m$-adic filtration gives $\ell_R(A)<\infty$. [F3, F6, F8, step 2.1]

4.1 Choose generators $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ of $I$. For each $j\geq0$, multiplication by the finitely many monomials $f^\alpha$ with $|\alpha|=j$ gives a surjection from copies of $N$ onto $G_j=I^jM/I^{j+1}M$: coefficients modulo $IM$ suffice because $f^\alpha IM\subseteq I^{j+1}M$. In particular every $G_j$ has finite length and is killed by $\mathfrak m^c$. The actions $X_i\bar m=\overline{f_im}$ commute, and define $G=\bigoplus_{j\geq0}G_j$ as a finite graded module over $A[X_1,\ldots,X_s]$, generated by a finite generating list of $N$ in degree zero. [F1, F3, F8, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 For any finite graded $A[X_1,\ldots,X_s]$-module $V$, replacing a finite generating list by all its homogeneous components gives homogeneous generators. Their degrees have a lower bound. Each $V_j$ is a quotient of finitely many copies of $A$, indexed by monomials of the required degree times these generators. Thus $H_V(t)=\sum_j\ell_R(V_j)t^j$ is a well-defined formal Laurent series. When $s=0$, only the finitely many generator degrees can occur, so $H_V$ is a Laurent polynomial. [F3, step 3.1]

5.1 Suppose $s>0$ and rationality with denominator $(1-t)^{s-1}$ has been proved for every finite graded module over $A[X_1,\ldots,X_{s-1}]$. Put $V(-1)_j=V_{j-1}$, $U=\ker(X_s:V(-1)\to V)$ and $W=V/X_sV$. Iterating polynomial extension from the Noetherian ring $A$ makes $A[X_1,\ldots,X_s]$ Noetherian. Consequently $U$ is finite as a submodule of finite $V(-1)$; $W$ is finite as a quotient. Both are killed by $X_s$, so their same finite generators generate them over the ring with $s-1$ variables. [F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.2]

6.1 In degree $j$, the exact sequence $0\to U_j\to V_{j-1}\xrightarrow{X_s}V_j\to W_j\to0$, split at its image, yields $\ell_R(V_j)-\ell_R(V_{j-1})=\ell_R(W_j)-\ell_R(U_j)$. Hence $(1-t)H_V=H_W-H_U$. The induction hypothesis gives a Laurent polynomial numerator divided by $(1-t)^s$. Together with the base case this proves that form for all $s$. [F3, step 4.2, step 5.1]

7.1 The filtration of $M/I^{n+1}M$ has successive factors $G_0,\ldots,G_n$, so its length is $h(n)=\sum_{j=0}^n\ell_R(G_j)$. Its generating series is $H_G(t)/(1-t)=Q(t)/(1-t)^{s+1}$ for a Laurent polynomial $Q(t)=\sum_a q_at^a$. The geometric series and repeated convolution give the coefficient $\binom{n-a+s}{s}$ of $t^n$ in $t^a/(1-t)^{s+1}$ when $n\geq a$. One can count the convolution terms as nonnegative $(s+1)$-tuples with sum $n-a$, separated by $s$ dividers. Thus for $n$ beyond the finite set of exponents of $Q$, $h(n)=\sum_a q_a\binom{n-a+s}{s}$, a rational polynomial in $n$. For $s=0$ the binomial is $1$, as required for $I=0$. [F3, step 4.1, step 6.1]

8.1 If two rational polynomials eventually equal $h(n)$, their difference vanishes at infinitely many distinct integers. Division by $T-a$ at a root lowers the degree by one, so a nonzero polynomial of degree $d$ has at most $d$ distinct roots. The difference is therefore zero. This also proves the uniqueness left open in the zero cases. [step 1.1, step 7.1, algebra] ∎
