## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the local factoriality and the equivalence
argument below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $S$ be a **regular surface**: a
locally Noetherian scheme of pure dimension two all of whose local rings are
regular ([[def-local-ring]], [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]).
Let $D\subseteq S$ be a **reduced curve**, that is, a reduced closed subscheme of
pure dimension one; equivalently, a reduced effective Cartier divisor on $S$ of pure dimension one
([[def-effective-cartier-divisor]], [[def-cartier-divisor]]).

Then $D$ is a **strict normal crossings divisor** (an **SNC divisor**) on $S$ if
at every closed point $p$ of $S$ one of the following holds:

(a) no irreducible component of $D$ passes through $p$;

(b) exactly one irreducible component of $D$ passes through $p$, and it is
regular at $p$;

(c) exactly two irreducible components $Y_1,Y_2$ of $D$ pass through $p$, both
are regular at $p$, and they meet **transversally** at $p$, that is,
$m_p(Y_1\cap Y_2)=1$
([[def-intersection-multiplicity-of-closed-subschemes]]).

Equivalently (Stacks, Lemma 41.21.2, in the locally Noetherian case): $D$ is
reduced, every irreducible component of $D$ is a regular scheme, every nonempty
scheme-theoretic intersection of two distinct irreducible components of $D$ is a
regular scheme of dimension zero, and no three distinct irreducible components
of $D$ meet at a point. The equivalent local equation form is: at every point
$p\in S$ the stalk ideal $I_{D,p}\subseteq\mathcal O_{S,p}$ is principal, with a
generator of the form $x_1\cdots x_r$ where $r\ge0$ and $x_1,\dots,x_r$ extend
to a regular system of parameters of $\mathcal O_{S,p}$ (for a closed point of
the surface, $0\le r\le2$; $r=0$ means the unit ideal).

No smoothness over a ground field is part of the definition; over a non-perfect
field the components are regular but need not be smooth. The empty curve
$D=\varnothing$ and a single regular component are SNC divisors by (a) and (b).

## Remarks

[R1] *The pointwise conditions and the regular-intersection criterion agree.*
At a closed point $p\in D$, the local ring of an integral curve component through $p$ has dimension one. Its defining prime in $A=\mathcal O_{S,p}$ is nonzero, since that component has dimension one and is not an irreducible component of the pure two-dimensional surface. The chain from the zero prime of the regular local domain $A$ through this prime to its maximal ideal has length two. Thus $A$ has dimension two, and is a unique
factorization domain ([[thm-nonaffine-regular-local-ring-is-ufd]]). The
irreducible components of $D$ through $p$ correspond to the height-one prime
ideals $\mathfrak p_1,\dots,\mathfrak p_r$ containing the radical ideal $I_{D,p}$,
and $I_{D,p}=\mathfrak p_1\cap\cdots\cap\mathfrak p_r$ is generated by the
product of prime generators $x_1\cdots x_r$ of those primes, a nonzerodivisor;
so near $p$ the curve has the local equation $x_1\cdots x_r$. A component $Y_i$
is regular at $p$ exactly when its local equation $x_i$ extends to a regular
system of parameters of $A$ ([[lem-regular-local-quotient-by-parameter-is-regular]]),
which for one element of a two-dimensional regular local ring means that $x_i$
is not in $\mathfrak m_p^2$. Conversely, if $A/(x_i)$ is regular of dimension one, its cotangent space has dimension one; if $x_i$ lay in $\mathfrak m_p^2$, the quotient cotangent space would still be the two-dimensional $\mathfrak m_p/\mathfrak m_p^2$, a contradiction. For two distinct components $Y_1,Y_2$ the
scheme-theoretic intersection near $p$ is cut out by $(x_1,x_2)$; it is a
regular scheme of dimension zero precisely when $(x_1,x_2)=\mathfrak m_p$, that
is, when $x_1,x_2$ form a regular system of parameters, and this is exactly
$m_p(Y_1\cap Y_2)=1$. Three distinct components cannot meet at $p$ when the
criterion holds, because $\bigcap_{j\in J}D_j$ for a three-element set $J$ would
have to be regular of codimension three inside the surface
$S$ and therefore empty. Conversely, the pointwise conditions give the
criterion: components are regular, every local intersection of two distinct
components is a reduced point, and no three components meet. This is the
surface case of the finite-subset form of Stacks Lemma 41.21.2.

[R2] *Why the two descriptions of the ambient object agree.* For a reduced
closed subscheme $D\subseteq S$ of pure dimension one, being an effective
Cartier divisor is a local condition: at $p$ the ideal $I_{D,p}$ is generated by
one nonzerodivisor. If $p\notin D$ it is the unit ideal; if $p$ is the generic
point of a component, $\mathcal O_{S,p}$ is a discrete valuation ring and
$I_{D,p}$ is principal; if $p$ is a closed point, [R1] exhibits the principal
generator $x_1\cdots x_r$. Thus every reduced curve on a regular surface is a
reduced effective Cartier divisor, and conversely a reduced effective Cartier
divisor of pure dimension one is a reduced curve.

[R3] *Closed points suffice.* On a scheme of dimension at most two, every
non-closed point of $D$ is the generic point of an irreducible component, where
the local ring of $D$ is a field and no two distinct components meet; so
regularity of the components and all pairwise contacts are detected at closed
points. This is why the definition tests only closed points.
