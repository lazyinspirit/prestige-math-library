## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X$ be an integral finite-type $k$-scheme,
let $L$ be invertible, and let $s_0,\ldots,s_n$ be meromorphic sections of
$L$, not all zero. Their ratios define $\varphi:X\dashrightarrow\mathbb P^n_k$.
Put $F=\sum_i\mathcal O_Xs_i\subseteq K_X(L)$ and define the finite-type
quasi-coherent fractional ideal $I=F\otimes L^{-1}\subseteq K_X$.
Its fractional blowup is
$$B=\operatorname{Proj}_X\Bigl(\bigoplus_{d\ge0}I^d\Bigr),\qquad I^0=\mathcal O_X.$$
Then $B$ is integral, its projection $\pi:B\to X$ resolves $\varphi$, and
$(\pi,\psi):B\hookrightarrow X\times_k\mathbb P^n_k$ is the schematic
closure of its graph. The generating line bundle is
$M=\pi^*L\otimes(I\mathcal O_B)$.
If $f:Y\to X$ is a $k$-morphism from an integral scheme, $D\subseteq X$
is the domain of a representative of $\varphi$, $f^{-1}(D)\ne\varnothing$,
and $\theta:Y\to\mathbb P^n_k$ extends that representative composed with
$f$, then $f$ factors uniquely through $B$. The nonempty inverse-image
condition ensures that this induced rational map is defined.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the integral finite-type $k$-scheme $X$, $L$, the meromorphic tuple $(s_i)$, its fractional ideal $I$, and $B$ as above.

[F1] [[def-rational-map-integral-schemes]] and [[def-rational-section-line-bundle]]: Meromorphic sections are elements of the one-dimensional generic fiber of $L$ (zero is allowed here); a nonzero tuple gives projective ratios on a nonempty open. Representatives agree on nonempty opens and their target is separated.

[F2] [[def-blowup-fractional-ideal]] and [[def-relative-proj-quasi-coherent-graded-algebra]]: A fractional ideal of form $a^{-1}J$ on an affine domain, with $a\ne0$ and $J$ an ordinary ideal, has Rees Proj canonically isomorphic to the blowup of $J$, by degreewise rescaling. Relative Proj glues quasi-coherent graded algebras.

[F3] [[thm-pullback-center-ideal-invertible]]: The extended ordinary center ideal on its blowup is invertible.

[F4] [[thm-projective-map-line-bundle-data-equivalence]]: An invertible sheaf with $n+1$ generating global sections gives a morphism to projective $n$-space; its coordinates on chart $j$ are the section ratios.

[F5] [[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]] and [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]: For $J=(p_0,\ldots,p_n)$ in a domain $A$, chart $j$ is $A[J/p_j]\subseteq A_{p_j}$, generated by the ratios $p_i/p_j$; zero generators give empty charts.

[F6] [[thm-blowup-projective]]: The surjection $A[T_0,\ldots,T_n]\to R(J)$ gives a closed immersion of the blowup into $\operatorname{Spec}A\times\mathbb P^n_k$ with the displayed chart ratios.

[F7] [[lem-blowup-reduced-integral-under-domain-rees]]: A blowup of a nonzero finite-type ideal on an integral scheme is integral. Its unchanged nonempty open is dense in every nonempty domain chart.

## Proof

1.1 Trivialize $L$ by $e$ on an affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$. Write $s_i=h_ie$ and clear denominators by $a\in A\setminus\{0\}$, obtaining $p_i=ah_i\in A$. Then $I|_U=a^{-1}J$ with $J=(p_i)$, so it is quasi-coherent of finite type. Its powers are also quasi-coherent. Degreewise multiplication by $a^d$ identifies its Rees algebra with $R(J)$. Changing $a$ or the frame rescales these maps in each degree, inducing the same degree-zero ratio maps on Proj. Thus the relative Proj is defined and locally the ordinary blowup of $J$; $J\ne0$, so $B$ is integral. [F1, F2, F7]

2.1 The extended fractional ideal $I\mathcal O_B=a^{-1}(J\mathcal O_B)$ is invertible by [F3]. The sections $\pi^*s_i$ lie in $M=\pi^*L\otimes(I\mathcal O_B)$ and generate it. If $b$ generates $J\mathcal O_B$ on a local chart, the frame of $M$ is $\pi^*e\otimes(b/a)$, and the coefficients of these sections are $p_i/b$, which are regular and generate the unit ideal. Therefore [F4] gives $\psi:B\to\mathbb P^n_k$, with coordinates $p_i/p_j=h_i/h_j$ on chart $j$, resolving $\varphi$. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 Locally on $X$, [F6] makes $(\pi,\psi)$ a closed immersion with chart rings $A[J/p_j]$. These immersions agree on overlaps because their coordinate ratios agree, hence glue. On the nonempty open where a nonzero $p_j$ is invertible, it is the graph of $\varphi$; this open is dense in the corresponding domain chart by [F5]. Consequently $B$ is the schematic closure of that graph: a function on a domain chart vanishing on this dense principal open is zero. For any representative $\varphi_D$ the graph over $D$ is closed in $D\times\mathbb P^n_k$ by separatedness, and its nonempty dense part is the same graph just considered. The closure restricted to $D$ is therefore exactly that graph, with its reduced scheme structure. [F1, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $f,\theta,D$ satisfy the stated hypotheses. The nonempty open $f^{-1}(D)$ is dense in the integral scheme $Y$, and $(f,\theta)$ lands in $B$ there by step 3.1. The pullback of the ideal of the closed immersion $B\hookrightarrow X\times\mathbb P^n_k$ vanishes on this dense open. It vanishes everywhere: on each nonempty affine open of an integral scheme, a regular function zero on a dense open is zero in its domain coordinate ring. Thus $(f,\theta)$ factors through $B$. Any other $X$-lift has the same projective component on $f^{-1}(D)$, since $B$ over $D$ is the graph. Two maps from a reduced integral scheme to separated projective space agreeing on a dense open agree everywhere, by the same ideal-vanishing argument applied to the diagonal. Hence the two lifts agree, as $B$ is a closed subscheme of the product. [F1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

The base ideal is fractional when the sections have poles. Clearing denominators supplies ordinary ideals locally; their rescalings need not define a single ordinary ideal globally. Multiplying the tuple by a nonzero rational scalar preserves its ratios and its blowup. A morphism whose image is entirely outside every representative's domain has no induced rational map to extend; no factoring claim is made for it.
