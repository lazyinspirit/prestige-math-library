## Statement

Let $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$, $\langle V\rangle$ and $U_\hbar\mathfrak n^+$ be as in [[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]].

(i) In $\langle V\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ the Serre sums vanish: for all $i\ne j$,
$$\sum_{s=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^s\binom{1-a_{ij}}{s}_{q_i}[v_i]^{*(1-a_{ij}-s)}*[v_j]*[v_i]^{*s}=0,$$
where $*$ is the shuffle product of [[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]].

(ii) Let $V^*=\bigoplus_i\mathbb C\xi_i$ be the dual space with $\langle\xi_i,v_j\rangle=d_i^{-1}\delta_{ij}$, let $T(V^*)=\bigoplus_{k\ge0}(V^*)^{\otimes k}\llbracket\hbar\rrbracket$ be the tensor algebra on $V^*$ with concatenation product, graded by $\deg\xi_i=-\epsilon_i$, and let $\Delta:T(V^*)\to T(V^*)\otimes_R T(V^*)$ be the braided coproduct with $\Delta(\xi_i)=\xi_i\otimes1+1\otimes\xi_i$, the target carrying the braided product $(u\otimes v)(u'\otimes v')=q^{-\langle\deg u',\deg v\rangle}uu'\otimes vv'$. Let $J_-\trianglelefteq T(V^*)$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the negative Serre elements $\sum_{s=0}^{1-a_{ij}}(-1)^s\binom{1-a_{ij}}{s}_{q_i}\xi_i^{1-a_{ij}-s}\xi_j\xi_i^s$, $i\ne j$. Let
$$\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathrm{Sh}(V)\times T(V^*)}:\mathrm{Sh}(V)\times T(V^*)\to\mathbb C((\hbar)),\qquad \langle[v_{i_1}|\cdots|v_{i_k}],\xi_{j_1}\cdots\xi_{j_l}\rangle=\delta_{kl}\,\hbar^{-k}\prod_{t=1}^{k}\langle v_{i_t},\xi_{j_t}\rangle,$$
be the wordwise pairing, extended bilinearly from the tensor-word bases. Then $J_-$ annihilates the shuffle half $\langle V\rangle$:
$$\langle x,y\rangle=0\qquad\text{for every }x\in\langle V\rangle,\ y\in J_-,$$
equivalently the wordwise pairing descends to a bilinear pairing $\langle V\rangle\times\bigl(T(V^*)/J_-\bigr)\to\mathbb C((\hbar))$. The twisted product on $T(V^*)\otimes_R T(V^*)$ is used only in the definition of the braided coproduct, and no claim is made about the radical of the pairing on all of $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ (see the note after the proof).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The formal shuffle Borel of a finite symmetrizable Cartan datum and the wordwise pairing stated in part (ii).

[F1] For $i\ne j$, $a_{ij}\le0$, $a_{ii}=2$, $d_i>0$, and $d_i a_{ij}=d_j a_{ji}$, so $\langle\epsilon_i,\epsilon_j\rangle=d_i a_{ij}$ is a symmetric bilinear form ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]).

[F2] $q_i=q^{d_i}$, $[m]_i!$ is the product of the symmetric $q_i$-integers, and $C_{m,r}=\binom mr_i=[m]_i!/([r]_i![m-r]_i!)$ with $C_{m,0}=1$ ([[def-quantum-integers-factorials-and-divided-powers-at-q-i]]).

[F3] $B_{m,r}(t)=[m]_t!/([r]_t![m-r]_t!)$ are the coefficients in the q-binomial expansion when $yx=txy$, and $C_{m,r}=C_{m,m-r}$ ([[lem-q-binomial-expansion-for-q-commuting-elements]], [[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

[F4] The symmetric Gaussian coefficients are Laurent polynomials in $q_i$ and satisfy the q-Pascal and Gauss product identities ([[lem-quantum-pascal-recurrence-and-gaussian-integrality]]).

[F5] The wordwise quantum shuffle product on $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ uses inversion weight $q^{-\langle\deg z_i,\deg z_j\rangle}$ with $\langle\epsilon_i,\epsilon_j\rangle=d_i a_{ij}$; one-letter words are elements of $V$ ([[def-formal-quantum-shuffle-borel-for-a-symmetrizable-cartan-datum]]).

[F6] The pairing of part (ii) is diagonal in the tensor-word bases: for tensor words $u=[v_{i_1}|\cdots|v_{i_k}]$ and $v=\xi_{j_1}\cdots\xi_{j_l}$ it equals $\delta_{kl}\delta_{i_1j_1}\cdots\delta_{i_kj_k}\hbar^{-k}\prod_td_{i_t}^{-1}$; in particular it carries no sign and it is invariant under the interchange $i\leftrightarrow j$ of letters. The letter weights $w(i,i)=2d_i$ and $w(i,j)=d_ia_{ij}=w(j,i)$ are symmetric.

[F7] The proof uses only finite word shuffles, coefficient identities, explicit word comparisons and the finitely many permutations of $N+1$ letters; no axiom of choice is used.



## Proof

**Proof technique:** Write $*$ for the shuffle product and expand the positive Serre expression one word at a time. Then compute the pairing of a shuffle product with the negative Serre element by an explicit permutation count, and compare it with the vanishing alternating sum just obtained.

1.1 Put $N=1-a_{ij}$, $x=[v_i]$, $y=[v_j]$, $t=q_i^{-2}$, and $B_{m,r}(t)=0$ outside $0\le r\le m$. Let $W_m=[v_i|\cdots|v_i]$ (with $m$ letters), and let $w_p$ be the word with $p$ copies of $v_i$, then $v_j$, then $N-p$ copies of $v_i$ (omitting a zero-length block). Repeatedly shuffling one $x$ into a block of $m$ equal $x$-letters gives $x^{*m}=A_m W_m$, where $A_m=q_i^{-m(m-1)/2}[m]_i!$: the insertion weights sum to $1+q_i^{-2}+\cdots+q_i^{-2m}=q_i^{-m}[m+1]_i$. In a three-block shuffle $W_a*y*W_b$, with $a+b=N$, fix $w_p$, and let $u$ (respectively $v$) count the $b$-block letters before (respectively after) $y$, so $u+v=b$. The prefix interleavings contribute $B_{p,u}(t)$, the suffix interleavings contribute $B_{N-p,v}(t)$, the $u$ letters before $y$ cross $y$ and the $N-p-v$ letters after it, and each of those $u$ letters crosses those $N-p-v$ suffix $x$-letters; therefore $\operatorname{coeff}_{w_p}(W_a*y*W_b)=\sum_{u+v=b}B_{p,u}(t)B_{N-p,v}(t)q_i^{-a_{ij}(u+N-p-v)-2u(N-p-v)}$. [F1, F2, F3, F5, given, algebra]

1.2 The pairing is diagonal in the tensor-word bases: for tensor words $u=[v_{i_1}|\cdots|v_{i_k}]$ and $v=\xi_{j_1}\cdots\xi_{j_l}$ the defining formula gives $\langle u,v\rangle=\delta_{kl}\delta_{i_1j_1}\cdots\delta_{i_kj_k}\hbar^{-k}\prod_td_{i_t}^{-1}$, and $\prod_td_{i_t}^{-1}$ depends only on $u$. Consequently, for $x=\sum_wx_w[w]$ and tensor words $y,y'$ with letter-sequences $u,u'$, both sides of $\langle x,yy'\rangle=\sum_{k\ge0}\langle x_{\le k},y\rangle\langle x_{>k},y'\rangle$ equal $x_{uu'}\hbar^{-(|u|+|u'|)}\bigl(\prod_{s\in u}d_s^{-1}\bigr)\bigl(\prod_{s\in u'}d_s^{-1}\bigr)$: the left side because only the word $uu'$ has nonzero pairing with $yy'$, the right side because only the cut $k=|u|$ contributes, the product in $T(V^*)$ being concatenation and the cut coproduct of the word $[u]$ being $\Delta([u])=\sum_k[u_{\le k}]\otimes[u_{>k}]$. Extending by bilinearity, this adjunction rule holds for all $x\in\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ and all $y,y'\in T(V^*)$. [F5, F6, given, algebra]

1.3 For homogeneous one-letter words $[x_1],\ldots,[x_m]\in V$ put $w(a,b):=\langle\deg x_a,\deg x_b\rangle$. Then $[x_1]*\cdots*[x_m]=\sum_{\sigma\in S_m}q^{-\sum_{a<b,\ \sigma(a)>\sigma(b)}w(a,b)}[x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}|\cdots|x_{\sigma^{-1}(m)}]$. Here $\sigma$ maps each original letter position to its output position, consistently with [F5]. For $m=1$ this is the identity. If it holds for $m-1$, write the product as $([x_1]*\cdots*[x_{m-1}])*[x_m]$; inserting $[x_m]$ into the $k$-th position of a word $[x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}|\cdots|x_{\sigma^{-1}(m-1)}]$ multiplies its coefficient by $q^{-\sum_{a<m,\ \sigma(a)\ge k}w(a,m)}$, exactly the sum of the weights of the pairs $(a,m)$ inverted by the resulting permutation, so the block formula reproduces the displayed expansion; the map $(\sigma,k)\mapsto$ the resulting permutation is a bijection onto $S_m$. [F5, given, algebra]

2.1 In the coefficient of $w_p$ in $\sum_{s=0}^{N}(-1)^sC_{N,s}x^{*(N-s)}*y*x^{*s}$, set $Q=N-p$, write $u+v=s$, and use $A_{N-s}A_sC_{N,s}=q_i^{-((N-s)(N-s-1)+s(s-1))/2}[N]_i!$ and $-a_{ij}=N-1$. The coefficient simplifies to $[N]_i!q_i^{-N(N-1)/2+(N-1)Q}\bigl(\sum_{u=0}^{p}(-1)^uB_{p,u}(t)q_i^{u(2p-1)-u^2}\bigr)\bigl(\sum_{v=0}^{Q}(-1)^vB_{Q,v}(t)q_i^{v-v^2}\bigr)$. The quotient definition gives $B_{m,r}(t)=B_{m-1,r}(t)+t^{m-r}B_{m-1,r-1}(t)$; induction then yields $\sum_{r=0}^{m}(-1)^rt^{r(r-1)/2}B_{m,r}(t)z^r=\prod_{j=0}^{m-1}(1-zt^j)$. Thus the first factor in parentheses is $\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}(1-q_i^{2p-2-2j})$ and vanishes when $p>0$; the second is $\prod_{j=0}^{Q-1}(1-q_i^{-2j})$ and vanishes when $Q>0$. Since $p+Q=N\ge1$, at least one factor vanishes for every $p=0,\ldots,N$. These words exhaust the color degree $N\epsilon_i+\epsilon_j$, proving part (i). [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Fix $i\ne j$ and $N=1-a_{ij}$, and put $w(i,i)=2d_i$, $w(i,j)=w(j,i)=d_ia_{ij}$; these are the values $\langle\deg v_a,\deg v_b\rangle$, and $w$ is symmetric by [F1]. Let $z:=\sum_{p=0}^{N}(-1)^pC_{N,p}\xi_i^p\xi_j\xi_i^{N-p}$; since $C_{N,N-p}=C_{N,p}$, the element $z$ differs from the printed Serre generator $\sum_s(-1)^sC_{N,s}\xi_i^{N-s}\xi_j\xi_i^s$ by the global sign $(-1)^N$, so the two generate the same two-sided ideal. For $t\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$ let $\zeta_t:=[v_i]^{*t}*[v_j]*[v_i]^{*(N-t)}$, and for $p\in\{0,\ldots,N\}$ let $w_p$ be the tensor word with $p$ letters $v_i$, then $v_j$, then $N-p$ letters $v_i$. By [F6] and steps 1.2 and 1.3, $\langle\zeta_t,z\rangle=\hbar^{-(N+1)}d_i^{-N}d_j^{-1}\sum_{p=0}^{N}(-1)^pC_{N,p}M(t,p)$, where $M(t,p):=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{N+1},\ \sigma(t+1)=p+1}q^{-\sum_{a<b,\ \sigma(a)>\sigma(b)}W(a,b)}$ and $W(a,b)$ is the weight $w$ of the letters at positions $a,b$ of the sequence $(i^t,j,i^{N-t})$. Then $M(t,p)=M(p,t)$: the map $\sigma\mapsto\sigma^{-1}$ is a bijection from the permutations with $\sigma(t+1)=p+1$ onto those with $\tau(p+1)=t+1$, and it preserves the exponent, because the inversion pairs of $\sigma$ correspond to those of $\tau=\sigma^{-1}$ with the same pair of letters: if $c=\sigma(b)<d=\sigma(a)$ for an inverted pair $a<b$, then $\tau(c)=b>a=\tau(d)$, and the weight of the pair $\{a,b\}$ equals the weight of the letter pair at the positions $d,c$ of the transposed sequence $(i^p,j,i^{N-p})$, since $\sigma$ carries the position $t+1$ of $j$ onto the position $p+1$, so the letter at position $\sigma(x)$ of the transposed sequence is the letter at position $x$ of the original sequence for every $x$. [F5, F6, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 The cut coproduct is an algebra map from $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ to its tensor square with product $(u\otimes v)(u'\otimes v')=q^{-\langle\deg u',\deg v\rangle}(u*u')\otimes(v*v')$. Indeed a shuffle followed by a cut consists uniquely of shuffles of the two prefixes and two suffixes; its cross-cut inversions contribute $q^{-\langle\deg u_-,\deg v_+\rangle}$, exactly the scalar in this tensor product. On a letter the coproduct is primitive, so it sends every product of letters into $\langle V\rangle\otimes_R\langle V\rangle$. Write this finite sum as $\Delta(x)=\sum_a x_a'\otimes x_a''$ with $x_a',x_a''\in\langle V\rangle$; individual prefixes of ambient tensor words need not themselves belong to the generated half. If $y$ annihilates $\langle V\rangle$, step 1.2 gives $\langle x,yu\rangle=\sum_a\langle x_a',y\rangle\langle x_a'',u\rangle=0$ and likewise $\langle x,uy\rangle=0$. Thus $\langle V\rangle^\perp$ is a two-sided ideal. [F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 By part (i) the element $\zeta:=\sum_{s=0}^{N}(-1)^sC_{N,s}\zeta_{N-s}$ is zero in $\langle V\rangle\subseteq\mathrm{Sh}(V)$, so all of its tensor-word coefficients vanish. By step 1.3 the coefficient of $w_p$ in $\zeta_{N-s}$ is $M(N-s,p)$, hence $\sum_s(-1)^sC_{N,s}M(N-s,p)=0$ for every $p$. Substituting $t=N-s$ and using $C_{N,N-t}=C_{N,t}$ (so that $(-1)^{N-t}$ is the global factor $(-1)^N$) gives $\sum_t(-1)^tC_{N,t}M(t,p)=0$ for every $p$; by the symmetry of step 2.2 this is $\sum_p(-1)^pC_{N,p}M(t,p)=0$ for every $t$. Hence step 2.2 gives $\langle\zeta_t,z\rangle=0$ for every $t$. [F3, step 1.3, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 A shuffle product $[x_1]*\cdots*[x_m]$ of one-letter words is homogeneous of color degree $\deg x_1+\cdots+\deg x_m$, and $\langle V\rangle$ is generated by the one-letter words, so every $x\in\langle V\rangle$ is a finite sum of such products. Unless the letters are exactly $N$ copies of $v_i$ and one copy of $v_j$, their color degree differs from that of $z$, so the diagonal pairing vanishes. In the remaining case the letter weights agree with the two-letter computation of step 2.2, and step 3.1 gives $\langle[x_1]*\cdots*[x_m],z\rangle=0$. By linearity $\langle x,z\rangle=0$ for every $x\in\langle V\rangle$, that is, $z\in\langle V\rangle^\perp$. Consequently every Serre generator lies in the two-sided ideal $\langle V\rangle^\perp$ of step 2.3, and hence so does the ideal $J_-$ they generate: $\langle\langle V\rangle,J_-\rangle=0$. This is the stated annihilation and the asserted descent of the wordwise pairing. [step 2.2, step 3.1, step 2.3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The literal analogue of (ii) with all of $\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ in place of $\langle V\rangle$ is false, so the restriction of the domain is necessary: the tensor word $[v_i|\cdots|v_i|v_j]$ with $N$ copies of $v_i$ pairs with the $p=0$ term of the Serre generator and with no other term, giving $\hbar^{-(N+1)}d_i^{-N}d_j^{-1}\ne0$. The source's literal full-$\mathrm{Sh}(V)$ radical clause therefore fails for the diagonal pairing. The restricted generated-half annihilation used here is proved independently above and supplies the final half-to-half duality. Printed formula (28) also has a single $\hbar^{-1}$ prefactor; the multiplicative adjunction with generator weights $\hbar^{-1}d_i^{-1}$ requires the length-$k$ weight $\hbar^{-k}\prod_t d_{i_t}^{-1}$ used in this item and verified in step 1.2.
