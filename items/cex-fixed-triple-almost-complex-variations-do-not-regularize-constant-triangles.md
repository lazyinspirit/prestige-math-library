## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. There is a genus-one pointed Heegaard triple whose three attaching systems are pairwise transverse and whose sum-of-doubly-periodic-domain lattice, normalized at the basepoint, is zero, hence satisfies the strict strong-admissibility test for every given Spin-c structure. It has a constant triangle of actual fixed-source Fredholm index minus one. Its actual linearization has zero kernel and one-dimensional real cokernel. The same negative index persists for every sufficiently small compatible almost-complex perturbation on this FIXED triple.

In particular changing only the almost-complex data, even allowing source dependence, cannot make the one-parameter universal index-minus-one triangle space regular: its constant triangles persist for every parameter, their parameter derivative in the equation is zero, and the actual universal derivative is not onto although its expected dimension is zero. Any analytic statement for generic triangles must therefore specify suitable curve genericity or an actual enlargement of the perturbation problem. Pairwise transversality alone is insufficient. No claim about the regularity of the nonconstant triangles is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The flat torus $T=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+e^{i\pi/3}\mathbb Z)$ and the three straight primitive attaching circles through zero with tangent directions $1,e^{i\pi/3},e^{2i\pi/3}$. On the upper half-plane with punctures $0,1,\infty$ label the boundary intervals $(-\infty,0),(0,1),(1,\infty)$ by those three lines, respectively. All corner values are zero. Use strip-end metrics and the actual $H^1$ real-boundary completion with vanishing corner limits.

[F1] The actual finite-ended Fredholm theorem and its higher-realization theorem give closed range and the same index, kernel and cokernel on $H^1\to L^2$ and $H^3\to H^2$. ([[lem-finite-transverse-strip-ended-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm]], [[lem-transverse-strip-ended-disk-fredholm-realizations-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]])

[F2] The plane Cauchy kernel $1/(\pi z)$ has distributional derivative $\bar\partial(1/(\pi z))=\delta_0$, with the actual polar-coordinate proof in the similarity theorem. The real strip mode basis proves that holomorphic $H^1$ sections have only their decaying end modes. ([[lem-countable-choice-cauchy-riemann-similarity-and-unique-continuation]], [[lem-transverse-linear-strip-cauchy-riemann-isomorphism]])

## Proof

1.1 Every pair of the three primitive torus circles has determinant of absolute value one in the displayed lattice basis, hence intersects at the sole point zero. Each is a meridian of a solid torus with one complete compression disk. The complement of either pair is an open disk: cutting along the first primitive circle gives an annulus and the second gives a single arc across it, whose cut is a disk. Therefore a normalized doubly periodic domain for that pair is zero, since there is just one region and its basepoint coefficient is zero. The sum of the three such normalized lattices is zero. Choose the basepoints off the circles, even both in a small disk in one triple region if two marks are desired. The constant triangle at zero is an actual finite-energy map with these boundary and corner values. The strong-admissibility condition on nonzero sums of doubly periodic domains is vacuous. The pairs give the standard genus-one three-sphere splittings: their meridians intersect once. Equivalently this is the genus-one surgery triad of the unknot, with a two-marked small unknot in a ball disjoint from the attaching-handle region when a decorated knot is required. Its small bridge arcs are chosen in that same common triple-region disk, so their product cylinder is disjoint from the two-handle. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Compute its actual scalar operator, not a combinatorial index. It is $D=2\bar\partial$ with the three real boundary lines in the Statement. On the upper half-plane take the branches with arguments in $(0,\pi)$ and put $g(z)=e^{2\pi i/3}z^{2/3}(z-1)^{2/3}$. Its boundary phases are zero modulo $\pi$ on $x<0$, $\pi/3$ modulo $\pi$ on $0<x<1$, and $2\pi/3$ modulo $\pi$ on $x>1$. Thus $u/g$ has real boundary values whenever $u$ has the prescribed tangent boundary values. The operator is Fredholm by [F1]: its three strip-end frames have constant transverse lines and the exact constant equation, with positive angle gap. For a holomorphic kernel section [F2]'s decaying modes at zero and one have least exponent $2/3$, and at infinity least decaying exponent $2/3$. Indeed imposing the displayed two boundary phases on a local power gives exponents $2/3+\mathbb Z$, and $H^1$ on the logarithmic end excludes nonpositive exponents. Consequently $h=u/g$ is bounded at zero and one and is $O(z^{-2})$ at infinity. Schwarz reflection, obtained by matching the real boundary values and the Cauchy–Riemann derivatives, extends $h$ across every other real point. Its bounded punctures at zero and one are removable by the circle Laurent series, and it is an entire function tending to zero at infinity. The circle coefficient formula on arbitrarily large circles makes all its coefficients zero. Thus $\ker D=0$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Define the real functional on compactly supported interior forcing coefficients by $\mathcal L(f)=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb H}f(z)/g(z)\,dA(z)$. It extends boundedly to the actual strip-end $L^2$ target. To verify this with the target-form coordinate change, let $\zeta$ be a logarithmic strip coordinate and $z=z(\zeta)$. The forcing coefficient transforms by $f_\zeta=\overline{z'(\zeta)}f_z$. Hence its integral pairing has coefficient $z'(\zeta)/g(z(\zeta))$ against $f_\zeta\,dA_\zeta$. Its magnitude decays as $|z|^{1/3}$ at zero, $|z-1|^{1/3}$ at one, and $|z|^{-1/3}$ at infinity; each is exponentially integrable on its strip end. On a compact core it is smooth and bounded. Thus Cauchy–Schwarz proves the assertion. For a defining compactly supported smooth boundary-compatible section set $h=u/g$. Then $Du/g=2\bar\partial h$, and the planar Green formula shows $\operatorname{Re}\int2\bar\partial h=0$: along the real boundary $h$ and $dz$ are real, while the factor $1/i$ in that formula is purely imaginary; the end cutoffs vanish. Passage through the actual $H^1$ completion and boundedness prove $\mathcal L(Du)=0$ on its whole domain. The functional is nonzero, since $f=g\phi$ for a nonzero nonnegative smooth interior bump has $\mathcal L(f)>0$. Thus the range has codimension at least one. [F1, F2, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Prove that this is the entire cokernel. First let $f$ be smooth and compactly supported in the interior, with $\mathcal L(f)=0$. Put $F=f/(2g)$ on the upper half-plane, extend it to the lower half-plane by $F(\bar z)=\overline{F(z)}$, and put $h=(1/(\pi z))*F$. The extended forcing is smooth and compactly supported away from the real axis. [F2]'s kernel identity gives $\bar\partial h=F$ and its reflection symmetry gives $h(\bar z)=\overline{h(z)}$, so $h$ has real boundary values. Expansion of the integral outside its compact support gives $h(z)=(\int F)/(\pi z)+O(z^{-2})$. Its first coefficient is zero because $\int_{\mathbb C}F=\operatorname{Re}\int_{\mathbb H}f/g=\mathcal L(f)=0$. Thus $u=gh$ solves $Du=f$, has the prescribed real boundary lines, and has decays $O(|z|^{2/3})$, $O(|z-1|^{2/3})$ and $O(|z|^{-2/3})$ at the three ends. These give actual $H^1$ membership, including the real trace completion: cut off on the ends, and use smooth boundary-compatible approximation in the real boundary charts. Now compactly supported smooth interior targets are dense in $L^2$. Fix one such target $f_0$ with $\mathcal L(f_0)=1$; if $f_j\to f\in\ker\mathcal L$, then $f_j-\mathcal L(f_j)f_0\to f$ are dense targets in that same kernel and are all in the range by the construction. The range is closed by [F1], so it contains $\ker\mathcal L$. Combined with step2.1 this proves $\operatorname{Ran}D=\ker\mathcal L$. The cokernel has real dimension one and, by step1.2, the index is exactly minus one. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Small compatible changes of $J$ at the constant map give a norm-continuous family of actual Fredholm operators with the same index. The fixed finite-ended parametrices, or their finite Schur-block index proof, show index local constancy; transverse angle gaps persist. The index therefore remains minus one throughout a sufficiently small connected nearly symmetric coefficient neighborhood. Each such operator has a cokernel, since its kernel dimension is nonnegative. Higher realization does not remove it by [F1]. The map itself remains a solution for EVERY such source-dependent $J$, because its derivative is zero. No positivity of a domain or energy estimate can exclude this zero-energy map. [F1, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 In a one-real-parameter family $J_\lambda$, the actual derivative of the universal section at this constant map is $(\xi,a)\mapsto D_{J_\lambda}\xi+(\partial_\lambda J_\lambda)du\circ j\,a=D_{J_\lambda}\xi$. Its cokernel is still nonzero, while its Fredholm index is $-1+1=0$. The constant maps parametrized by $\lambda$ persist as an actual interval of solutions. Thus these universal index-minus-one spaces are not regular for ANY such coefficient family, and choosing a generic family within this fixed-curve perturbation class cannot repair them. This establishes the stated obstruction and the need to specify an actual adequate curve or equation perturbation convention in any stronger analytic claim. [given, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
