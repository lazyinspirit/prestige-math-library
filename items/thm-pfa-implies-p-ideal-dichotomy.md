## Statement

In ZFC, PFA implies PID: every P-ideal of countable subsets of an arbitrary
set satisfies one of the two alternatives in the P-ideal dichotomy.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** PFA and a P-ideal $\mathcal I\subseteq[S]^{\leq\omega}$.

[F1] PFA supplies a filter meeting at most $\omega_1$ dense sets in every proper forcing. [[def-proper-forcing-axiom]]

[F2] The P-ideal property supplies modulo-finite pseudounions, and PID's two conclusions are an uncountable $Z$ with all countable subsets in $\mathcal I$ or a countable cover by sets orthogonal to $\mathcal I$. [[def-p-ideals-pid-pseudointersection-number-and-s-spaces]]

[F3] A model-generic condition forces the model-generic intersection and the ground/ordinal trace properties used below. [[lem-proper-master-condition-characterizations]]

[A1] AC supplies simultaneous P-ideal bounds, well-ordered elementary models, finite-chain choices, names, and the omega-one recursions. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix a large regular $\theta$. For every countable $\mathcal X\subseteq\mathcal I$, use F2 and A1 to fix $I_{\mathcal X}\in\mathcal I$ with $a\subseteq^*I_{\mathcal X}$ for all $a\in\mathcal X$, and put $I_N=I_{N\cap\mathcal I}$ for a countable $N\prec H_\theta$. Define $Q_{\mathcal I}$ as follows. A condition $p=(Z_p,\mathcal N_p)$ has finite $Z_p\subseteq S$ and a finite membership-chain $\mathcal N_p$ of countable elementary submodels containing $\mathcal I$; distinct points of $Z_p$ are separated by some $N\in\mathcal N_p$; and if $N\in\mathcal N_p$ and $X\in N\cap\mathcal I^\perp$, then $X\cap Z_p\subseteq N$. Put $p\leq q$ when $Z_q\subseteq Z_p$, $\mathcal N_q\subseteq\mathcal N_p$, and $(Z_p\setminus Z_q)\cap N\subseteq I_N$ for every $N\in\mathcal N_q$. These clauses are preserved by extension and make the empty pair a greatest condition. [F2, A1, Given]

2.1 We verify properness, including the combinatorial compatibility step. Let $M$ be suitable, $p_0\in Q_{\mathcal I}\cap M$, and add $N=M\cap H_\theta$ to its side chain; the result $q$ is a condition below $p_0$. Fix once and for all the well-order of $H_\theta$ carried by the elementary structure. For a condition $u$ and a model $K\in\mathcal N_u$, define the condition trace $$u\mathbin{\restriction}K=(Z_u\cap K,\mathcal N_u\cap K),$$ and enumerate the finite set $Z_u\setminus K$ in the fixed well-order, writing $t_{u,K}\in S^{|Z_u\setminus K|}$ for the resulting tuple. It suffices by F3 to take $r\leq q$ and dense $D\in M$, first strengthen $r$ into $D$, and then find a member of $D\cap M$ compatible with this strengthening; rename the strengthened condition $r$. Put $r_0=r\mathbin{\restriction}N$ and $n=|Z_r\setminus N|$. By elementarity, restrict $D$ to the conditions $s$ carrying a distinguished $N_s\in\mathcal N_s$ such that $s\mathbin{\restriction}N_s=r_0$ and $|Z_s\setminus N_s|=n$; the witnesses for $r$ are $N_r=N$ and $t_{r,N}$. Let $$T=\{t_{s,N_s}:s\in D\} \subseteq S^n$$ and let $\mathcal J$ be the sigma-ideal generated by $\mathcal I^\perp$. For $U\subseteq S^n$, let $\partial U$ retain the tuples $u$ for which, at every coordinate $k<n$, the fibre of possible $k$th entries above $u\restriction k$ is $\mathcal J$-positive. The derivative claim in Moore's cited tutorial says that $T'=\partial^nT$ is a nonempty $\mathcal J^+$-splitting member of $M$ and contains the external tuple $t_{r,N}$. Its finite induction uses the membership chain and clause 4 of the forcing: if $t_{r,N}$ first disappeared, the least bad fibre's countable $\mathcal I^\perp$ decomposition, coded in the relevant side model, would put one of the corresponding outside points of $Z_r$ in a member of $\mathcal I^\perp$ from that model, contrary to clause 4. In particular, this claim does not assume that $t_{r,N}$ itself belongs to $M$. Starting with the empty tuple, choose successively in $M$ an initial segment extendible in $T'$. Its next-coordinate set $C\in M$ is not in $\mathcal J$, hence is not orthogonal to $\mathcal I$; elementarity gives an infinite $H\in\mathcal I\cap M$ with $H\subseteq C$. For every one of the finitely many outer models $P\in\mathcal N_r\setminus M$, membership-chain coherence gives $H\in P\cap\mathcal I$, so $H\subseteq^*I_P$. Choose the next coordinate in $H\cap\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal N_r\setminus M}I_P$. After $n$ choices, elementarity supplies $s\in D\cap M$ whose tuple $t_{s,N_s}$ is the chosen one, and hence $Z_s\setminus N_s$ is contained in every $I_P$. Then $(Z_s\cup Z_r,\mathcal N_s\cup\mathcal N_r)$ is a common extension. Thus $q$ is an $(M,Q_{\mathcal I})$-master and $Q_{\mathcal I}$ is proper. [F2, F3, A1, step 1.1]

3.1 If $S$ is a countable union of members of $\mathcal I^\perp$, PID's second alternative holds. Otherwise choose a suitable countable $M$ and $x\in S\setminus\bigcup(M\cap\mathcal I^\perp)$. Then $q=(\{x\},\{M\cap H_\theta\})$ is a condition and, by step 2.1, an $M$-master. For a $Q_{\mathcal I}$-generic $G$ containing $q$, set $\dot Z=\bigcup_{p\in\dot G}Z_p$ and $\dot{\mathcal N}=\bigcup_{p\in\dot G}\mathcal N_p$. The master condition forces $\dot Z$ uncountable: if an $M$-name enumerated it countably, F3 would put all of its ground points in $M$, contrary to $x\in\dot Z\setminus M$. Properness gives the ground-model countable-covering property by the same master-name argument, so every countable subset of $\dot Z$ is contained in some side model $N\in\dot{\mathcal N}$. The order clause gives $N\cap\dot Z\subseteq^*I_N$, and therefore $N\cap\dot Z\in\mathcal I$. Hence $q$ forces every countable subset of $\dot Z$ to lie in $\mathcal I$. [F2, F3, A1, step 2.1]

4.1 Work in the proper cone $Q_{\mathcal I}\mathbin{\restriction}q$. Choose names $\dot f:\omega_1\to\dot Z$ and $\dot g:\omega_1\to\mathcal I$ such that $q$ forces $\dot f$ injective and $\dot f``\xi\subseteq\dot g(\xi)$ for every $\xi<\omega_1$; step 3.1 supplies them. For each $\xi$, the set $D_\xi$ of conditions deciding both values is dense. By F1 there is a filter meeting all $D_\xi$. Compatibility within the filter makes the decided values coherent, producing in the ground universe an injection $f:\omega_1\to S$ and $g:\omega_1\to\mathcal I$ with $f``\xi\subseteq g(\xi)$. Put $Z=f``\omega_1$. If $a\subseteq Z$ is countable, the set of its $f$-indices is bounded by some $\xi<\omega_1$, so $a\subseteq f``\xi\subseteq g(\xi)\in\mathcal I$ and downward closure gives $a\in\mathcal I$. Thus $Z$ witnesses PID's first alternative. Together with the first sentence of step 3.1, this proves PID. AC is used exactly through A1 and the stated ZFC suppliers. [F1, F2, A1, step 3.1] ∎
