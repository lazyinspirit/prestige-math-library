## Statement

Let $K$ be a number field and $R=\mathcal O_K$. For every $n\ge2$ and every $M\in\operatorname{SL}_n(R)$ there is a finite sequence of elementary row and column operations taking $M$ to $\operatorname{diag}(N,I_{n-2})$ for some $N\in\operatorname{SL}_2(R)$. Equivalently the image of $\operatorname{SL}_2(R)$ surjects onto $\operatorname{SL}_\infty(R)/E_\infty(R)$. Here $E_\infty(R)$ is the subgroup generated by finite transvections $I+aE_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$; these have determinant one and inverse $I-aE_{ij}$. The construction uses no axiom of choice. No assertion that this quotient vanishes is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The number ring $R$, $n\ge2$ and $M\in\operatorname{SL}_n(R)$.

[F1] A quotient of $R$ by a nonzero integral ideal is finite. [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]]

[F2] The Chinese remainder map for a finite family of pairwise comaximal ideals is onto. [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]

[F3] The stable elementary group is the kernel of the stable general-linear abelianization, hence is normal. [[lem-quillen-q-components-and-stable-elementary-perfectness]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Finite residue-ring and Chinese remainder reduction of one unimodular row.

1.1 If n=2 there is nothing to do. For n≥3 let the last row be (x₁,…,xₙ). Since M has inverse over R, the entries generate R: the last column of M⁻¹ supplies coefficients y_i with Σx_i y_i=1. If x₂=0, add Σ_{i≠2} y_i times column i to column 2, one column operation at a time. The new x₂ is 1 and hence the first n−1 entries already generate R. Retain the resulting matrix. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Suppose x₂≠0. The finite ring R/(x₂) from [F1] has only finitely many ideals, hence finitely many maximal ideals; their preimages are exactly the maximal ideals of R containing x₂. Keep those containing x₂,…,x_(n−1), and list them as m₁,…,m_s. If the list is empty put y=0. Otherwise distinct maximal ideals are pairwise comaximal, so [F2] gives one y congruent to 1 modulo each m_i containing x₁ and to 0 modulo each remaining m_i. Add y times the last column to the first. At each listed m_i the new first entry x₁+xₙy is nonzero modulo m_i: if x₁∉m_i then y=0 there, while if x₁∈m_i then xₙ∉m_i by unimodularity and y=1. If the first n−1 new entries generated a proper ideal, its quotient would be a nonzero quotient of the finite ring R/(x₂), hence have a maximal ideal by choosing an ideal of largest cardinality; its preimage would be one of the listed m_i, a contradiction. Thus the first n−1 entries generate R. Only one finite family of residue classes is chosen. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 In either case, choose finitely many coefficients expressing 1 as a linear combination of the first n−1 last-row entries. Add an appropriate multiple of each of those columns to the last column so its last entry becomes 1. Subtract each last-row entry times the last column from its column; the last row is then (0,…,0,1). Subtract each upper last-column entry times the last row from its row; the matrix is diag(M′,1). All operations are transvections, hence have determinant one, so det(M′)=1. This explicitly reduces rank n to n−1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Repeat step 3.1 for the resulting matrix, a finite induction ending at rank 2. Embed the finitely many operations into the stable general-linear group. Their products lie in E∞, which is normal by [F3], so M and diag(N,I) have the same class in SL∞/E∞; every quotient class is represented by a finite matrix, proving surjectivity. Empty residue lists and zero entries were handled above. Retain the finite construction of step 3.1. [F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra, discharge-construct] ∎
