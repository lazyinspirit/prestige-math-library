## Definition

A **finite quiver** is a quadruple $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$ in which $Q_0$ and $Q_1$
are finite sets, whose elements are called **vertices** and **arrows**, together
with maps $s,t:Q_1\to Q_0$ assigning to an arrow $a$ its **source** $s(a)$ and
its **target** $t(a)$; one draws $a:s(a)\to t(a)$. Both sets are finite and no
further structure is assumed: $Q$ may have several arrows with the same source
and target, and an arrow may be a loop, although the quivers used below have
neither property.

A **directed path** in $Q$ of length $l\ge0$ consists of vertices
$v_0,\ldots,v_l$ and specified arrows $a_j:v_{j-1}\to v_j$ for $1\le j\le l$.
Write it as $p=(v_0\xrightarrow{a_1}v_1\xrightarrow{a_2}\cdots\xrightarrow{a_l}v_l)$.
The arrows are part of the data: distinct parallel arrows give distinct paths.
A path of length zero is a single vertex $(v)$, called a **vertex idempotent**.
Write $|p|:=l$, $s(p):=v_0$ and $t(p):=v_l$ for its **length**, **source** and
**target**. When the arrows are determined by their endpoints, as in the doubled
lines used below, abbreviate this by $(v_0|\cdots|v_l)$; in formulas for a
general quiver this abbreviation retains the specified arrows as implicit data.
Paths $p$ and $q$ are **composable** if $t(p)=s(q)$. Their **concatenation**
$pq$ traverses the arrow sequence of $p$ first and then that of $q$, retaining
all arrow labels; in the abbreviated notation it is
$$(v_0|\cdots|v_l)(v_l|w_1|\cdots|w_{l'})=(v_0|\cdots|v_l|w_1|\cdots|w_{l'}).$$

The **integral path ring** $\mathbb ZQ$ is the free abelian group on the set of
all directed paths of $Q$ ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]),
with multiplication defined on basis paths by
$$(v_0|\cdots|v_l)(w_0|\cdots|w_{l'}):= \begin{cases}(v_0|\cdots|v_l|w_1|\cdots|w_{l'}),&v_l=w_0,\\ 0,&v_l\ne w_0,\end{cases}$$
and extended by $\mathbb Z$-bilinearity. Its unit is the finite sum
$$1_{\mathbb ZQ}=\sum_{v\in Q_0}(v)$$
of the vertex idempotents, which is a finite sum because $Q_0$ is finite.

**Endpoint conventions.** For a path $p$ and vertices $u,v$ one has
$$(u)\,p=\begin{cases}p,&s(p)=u,\\ 0,&s(p)\ne u,\end{cases} \qquad p\,(v)=\begin{cases}p,&t(p)=v,\\ 0,&t(p)\ne v.\end{cases}$$
Consequently the vertex idempotents are mutually orthogonal,
$(u)(v)=\delta_{uv}(v)$, and they decompose the ring as
$$\mathbb ZQ=\bigoplus_{u,v\in Q_0}(u)\,\mathbb ZQ\,(v),$$
the sum of the free abelian groups spanned by the paths with source $u$ and
target $v$. In the quotient rings used below, the analogous statements read:
the right ideal generated by the image of $(u)$ is spanned by the classes of paths with source
$u$, and the left ideal generated by the image of $(v)$ is spanned by the classes of paths with target
$v$, so that $A\,e_i$ consists of the paths **ending** at $i$ and $e_iA$ of the
paths **beginning** at $i$ whenever $A$ is a quotient of $\mathbb ZQ$ and
$e_i$ is the image of $(i)$.

**Empty quiver.** For $Q_0=Q_1=\varnothing$ the set of paths is empty, so
$\mathbb ZQ$ is the zero abelian group, the displayed sum for $1$ is empty and
$1=0$; this is the zero ring, and every statement below is trivial there. All
quivers occurring on this page are the doubled lines $\Gamma_m$ with $m\ge1$,
which have $m+1\ge2$ vertices.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite quiver $Q=(Q_0,Q_1,s,t)$, its set of directed paths, and the free abelian group $\mathbb ZQ$ on that set, with multiplication defined on basis paths as displayed and extended bilinearly.

[F1] For a set $X$ there is a free abelian group $\mathbb Z^{(X)}$ on $X$, whose elements are the finite formal sums $\sum_{x\in F}n_xe_x$ with $F\subseteq X$ finite, and a $\mathbb Z$-linear map out of $\mathbb Z^{(X)}$ is determined by, and may be specified by, arbitrary values on the basis elements $e_x$; a function $X\to G$ into an abelian group $G$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism $\mathbb Z^{(X)}\to G$ ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[F2] A ring is a set with addition and multiplication making it an abelian group under addition and a monoid under multiplication, with both distributive laws; multiplication is not assumed commutative ([[def-ring]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *The product is well defined.* Let $P$ be the set of directed paths of $Q$. The concatenation rule defines a function $P\times P\to\mathbb Z^{(P)}$, the set of composable pairs going to their concatenation and all other pairs to $0$; by [F1] it extends uniquely to a $\mathbb Z$-bilinear map $\mathbb Z^{(P)}\times\mathbb Z^{(P)}\to\mathbb Z^{(P)}$, which is the multiplication displayed in the definition. Every element of $\mathbb ZQ$ is a finite sum of basis paths by [F1], so no further well-definedness issue arises. [F1]

1.2 *Associativity on basis paths.* Let $p=(v_0|\cdots|v_l)$, $q=(w_0|\cdots|w_{l'})$ and $r=(x_0|\cdots|x_{l''})$ be paths. If $t(p)\ne s(q)$ or $t(q)\ne s(r)$ then both $(pq)r$ and $p(qr)$ are $0$, since a non-composable adjacent pair makes the corresponding product vanish and a vanishing factor stays zero. If $t(p)=s(q)$ and $t(q)=s(r)$, then all three concatenate and
$$(pq)r=(v_0|\cdots|v_l|w_1|\cdots|w_{l'}|x_1|\cdots|x_{l''})=p(qr).$$
Lengths add, $|pq|=|p|+|q|$, and sources and targets multiply as $s(pq)=s(p)$, $t(pq)=t(q)$. [algebra]

1.3 *Unit.* Let $p=(v_0|\cdots|v_l)$. By the endpoint formulas, $(v)p=p$ for $v=v_0$ and $(v)p=0$ for $v\ne v_0$, so $1_{\mathbb ZQ}\cdot p=\sum_v(v)p=p$; symmetrically $p\cdot1_{\mathbb ZQ}=p$, using $t(p)=v_l$. [algebra]

1.4 *Orthogonality and the block decomposition.* For vertices $u,v$ the product $(u)(v)$ is the concatenation of the two length-zero paths, which is composable exactly when $u=v$ and then equals $(u)$; hence $(u)(v)=\delta_{uv}(u)$. The displayed decomposition is the direct sum over the sets of paths with fixed source and target, and these sets partition the path set, so it is a direct sum decomposition of the free abelian group. [F1]

2.1 *Distributivity and the ring axioms.* Bilinearity of the product, established in step 1.1, gives $x(y+z)=xy+xz$ and $(y+z)x=yx+zx$ for all $x,y,z\in\mathbb ZQ$, and the additive group structure is the one coming from the free abelian group. Associativity of multiplication holds on basis paths by step 1.2 and hence on all elements by bilinearity, and $1_{\mathbb ZQ}$ is a two-sided identity by step 1.3. Thus [F2] applies. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, F2]

3.1 *Conclusion.* The free abelian group $\mathbb ZQ$ on the directed paths of $Q$, with the displayed concatenation product, is a ring with unit $\sum_v(v)$; the endpoint formulas, orthogonality and the block decomposition of steps 1.3, 1.4 hold, and a path of length zero at $u$ acts as a two-sided identity exactly on the paths with source $u$ on the left and target $u$ on the right. In a quotient ring of $\mathbb ZQ$ these identities are read in the quotient, so $Ae_i$ and $e_iA$ have the stated descriptions. For the empty quiver the construction gives the zero ring, and for the doubled lines used below there are at least two vertices. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1] ∎
