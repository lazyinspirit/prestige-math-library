## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $F$ be a codimension-$q$ regular foliation of a smooth manifold $M$ with normal
bundle $\nu=TM/TF$. Then every real Pontryagin monomial of total cohomological degree
greater than $2q$ in the normal bundle vanishes: for every homogeneous $\operatorname{GL}_q(\mathbb R)$-invariant polynomial
$\varphi$ representing a Pontryagin monomial and of polynomial degree $k>q$, the characteristic class
$\varphi(\nu)\in H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb R)$ obtained from the Pontryagin classes
of $\nu$ by [[def-pontryagin-classes-by-complexification]] is zero. No integral
statement is made: the vanishing is of the real Chern-Weil form, hence of the real
class.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A codimension-$q$ regular foliation $F$ of a smooth manifold $M$ with normal bundle $\nu=TM/TF$ and an invariant polynomial $\varphi$ of degree $k$ on the structure group of $\nu$.

[F1] For a connection $\nabla$ on $\nu$ extending the Bott partial connection, in a leaf-parallel frame the curvature matrix entries lie in the differential ideal $I$ generated by the one-forms vanishing on $TF$, and $I^{q+1}=0$. ([[lem-curvature-of-an-extending-bott-connection-lies-in-the-transverse-differential-ideal]]).

[F2] The Chern-Weil construction is independent of the choice of connection and is natural under pullback. ([[thm-chern-weil-homomorphism-is-independent-of-connection-and-natural]]).

[F3] The de Rham class of the Chern-Weil form of an invariant polynomial represents the corresponding real characteristic class of the bundle. ([[thm-chern-weil-forms-represent-the-at-characteristic-classes-over-the-reals]]).

[F4] Under AC a smooth real bundle admits a connection ([[thm-every-smooth-vector-bundle-admits-a-connection]]), and a smooth manifold admits a Riemannian metric under the implied countable choice ([[thm-every-smooth-manifold-admits-a-riemannian-metric]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F4] choose a metric on $TM$, the orthogonal projection $\Pi:TM\to TF$, and a connection $\nabla^0$ on $\nu$. Define $\nabla_Xs=\nabla^B_{\Pi X}s+\nabla^0_{X-\Pi X}s$. Its direction-linearity and Leibniz rule follow from those of the two summands, since $(\Pi X)(f)+(X-\Pi X)(f)=X(f)$; it extends the Bott partial connection. Now [F1] puts all its curvature entries in $I$ with $I^{q+1}=0$. [F1, F4, given, construct]

2.1 Evaluating the invariant polynomial $\varphi$ of degree $k$ on the curvature gives a sum of products of $k$ matrix entries $\Omega^{a_i}{}_{b_i}$, possibly with constant coefficients and traces; each such product is an element of $I^k$, so $\varphi(R^\nabla)\in I^k$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $k>q$ then $I^k\subseteq I^{q+1}=0$, so the Chern-Weil form $\varphi(R^\nabla)$ is identically zero and represents the zero class; by [F2] the Chern-Weil class is independent of the connection and by [F3] it represents the real characteristic class of $\nu$, so the real Pontryagin monomial $\varphi(\nu)$ vanishes in $H^{2k}_{\mathrm{dR}}(M;\mathbb R)$; no integral refinement is claimed with AC used for [F4] and the topological-to-real comparison [F3]. [F2, F3, step 2.1] ∎
