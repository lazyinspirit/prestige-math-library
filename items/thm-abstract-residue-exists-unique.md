## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the linear algebra suppliers
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field, $K$ a commutative $k$-algebra
([[def-vector-space]], [[def-linear-map]]), $V$ a $K$-module, and
$A\subseteq V$ a $k$-subspace with $fA<A$ for every $f\in K$, in the sense of
[[def-commensurable-subspaces-and-ideals-of-endomorphisms]], so that the
subspaces $E,E_1,E_2,E_0$ of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ are defined. Then there
is a unique $k$-linear map
$$\operatorname{res}_V\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\longrightarrow k$$
([[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]], [[cor-derivations-represented-by-differentials]])
such that
$$\operatorname{res}_V(f\,\mathrm{d}g)=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$$
for all $f,g\in K$ and all endomorphisms $f_1,g_1\in E$ satisfying
$f_1\equiv f\pmod{E_2}$, $g_1\equiv g\pmod{E_2}$ and with $f_1\in E_1$ or
$g_1\in E_1$. Here $\operatorname{Tr}_V$ is the finite potent trace of
[[lem-finite-potent-trace-linearity-and-conjugation]] and
$[f_1,g_1]=f_1g_1-g_1f_1$. Existence uses that $E=E_1+E_2$, so such lifts
always exist; the value is independent of the lifts by
[[lem-e-ideals-and-commutator-trace]], and $k$-bilinear in $(f,g)$ by the
linearity of the trace on the finite potent subspace $E_0$. The map is denoted
$\operatorname{res}_V$, or $\operatorname{res}_A$, and is the abstract residue
attached to the pair $(V,A)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$, a commutative $k$-algebra $K$, a $K$-module $V$, a $k$-subspace $A\subseteq V$ with $fA<A$ for all $f\in K$, and the resulting subspaces $E,E_1,E_2,E_0\subseteq\operatorname{End}_k(V)$; elements $f,g,h,f'\in K$ and, for the displayed formula, lifts $f_1,g_1\in E$ of $f,g$ as in the statement.

[F1] $V$ is a $k$-vector space, $K$ acts on $V$ through a $k$-algebra homomorphism $K\to\operatorname{End}_k(V)$, so elements of $K$ act as $k$-linear endomorphisms and any two of them commute; $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$ is a $k$-algebra for composition. ([[def-vector-space]], [[def-linear-map]])

[F2] Assume the Axiom of Choice, as inherited from the linear algebra and differential suppliers. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F3] $E=\{\theta:\theta A<A\}$, $E_1=\{\theta:\theta V<A\}$, $E_2=\{\theta:\theta A\text{ finite-dimensional}\}$, $E_0=E_1\cap E_2$; these are $k$-subspaces of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$, and the image of $K$ is contained in $E$ by the assumed condition $fA<A$; they depend only on the commensurability class of $A$, and $A<B$ means that $(A+B)/B$ is finite-dimensional. ([[def-commensurable-subspaces-and-ideals-of-endomorphisms]])

[F4] $E$ is a $k$-subalgebra of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$, the spaces $E_1,E_2$ are two-sided ideals of $E$, $E_1+E_2=E$, $E_1\cap E_2=E_0$, and $E_0$ is a finite potent subspace on which $\operatorname{Tr}_V$ is defined and $k$-linear; moreover $[\gamma,\psi]\in E_0$ with $\operatorname{Tr}_V([\gamma,\psi])=0$ whenever $\gamma\in E_0,\psi\in E$, and whenever $\gamma\in E_1,\psi\in E_2$. ([[lem-e-ideals-and-commutator-trace]])

[F5] (T4) and (T6)(b) of [[lem-finite-potent-trace-linearity-and-conjugation]]: the trace is $k$-linear on every finite potent subspace of $\operatorname{End}_k(V)$, and if $\gamma\in E_1,\psi\in E_2$ then $[\gamma,\psi]\in E_0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_V([\gamma,\psi])=0$.

[F6] $(\Omega^1_{K/k},\mathrm{d})$ is a Kähler differential module for $k\to K$: it is a $K$-module generated by the elements $\mathrm{d}g$, $g\in K$, and the map $\mathrm{d}$ is a $k$-derivation, so $\mathrm{d}(g+h)=\mathrm{d}g+\mathrm{d}h$, $\mathrm{d}(gh)=g\,\mathrm{d}h+h\,\mathrm{d}g$ and $\mathrm{d}\lambda=0$ for $\lambda\in k$. ([[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]])

[F7] For every $K$-module $M$, composition with $\mathrm{d}$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_K(\Omega^1_{K/k},M)\to\operatorname{Der}_k(K,M)$: for each $k$-derivation $D\colon K\to M$ there is a unique $K$-linear map $\ell\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\to M$ with $\ell\circ\mathrm{d}=D$. ([[cor-derivations-represented-by-differentials]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Setup) By [F4] one has $E=E_1+E_2$, so every $f\in K$, viewed in $E$, can be written $f=f_1+f_2$ with $f_1\in E_1$ and $f_2\in E_2$, i.e. every $f$ has a lift $f_1\in E_1$ with $f_1\equiv f\pmod{E_2}$; and for lifts $f_1,g_1\in E_1$ of $f,g$ the commutator $[f_1,g_1]$ lies in $E_1$ because $E_1$ is a two-sided ideal, while $[f_1,g_1]\equiv[f,g]=0\pmod{E_2}$ because $E_2$ is a two-sided ideal and elements of $K$ commute; hence $[f_1,g_1]\in E_1\cap E_2=E_0$ and $\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$ is defined by [F4]. [given, F1, F3, F4]

2.1 (Independence of the lift of the first argument) If $f_1,f_1'\in E_1$ both lift $f$ and $g_1\in E_1$, then $\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1',g_1])-\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1'-f_1,g_1])=0$, because $f_1'-f_1\in E_2$, $g_1\in E_1$, and the commutator of an element of $E_1$ with an element of $E_2$ lies in $E_0$ with zero trace by [F5]; the same computation with the roles of the arguments exchanged shows independence of the lift of the second argument. [step 1.1, F4, F5]

3.1 (Well-definedness of $r$) Define $r(f,g):=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$ for lifts $f_1,g_1\in E_1$; step 2.1 shows $r$ is well defined. Moreover, if $(f_1,g_1)$ is any pair satisfying the hypotheses of the statement, say with $f_1\in E_1$, and $g_1''\in E_1$ is a lift of $g$, then $\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1''])=r(f,g)$, the first equality because $g_1''-g_1\in E_2$ and $f_1\in E_1$ and the second by step 2.1; the case $g_1\in E_1$ is symmetric. [step 2.1, F4]

4.1 ($k$-bilinearity) $r$ is $k$-bilinear: for lifts $f_1,f_1',g_1\in E_1$ the sums and scalar multiples $f_1+f_1'$ and $\lambda f_1$ are again lifts in $E_1$, and $\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1+f_1',g_1])=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])+\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1',g_1])$, $\operatorname{Tr}_V([\lambda f_1,g_1])=\lambda\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$ because $\operatorname{Tr}_V$ is $k$-linear on the finite potent subspace $E_0$, and the second argument is treated identically. [step 3.1, F4]

5.1 (The Leibniz relation) For $f,g,h\in K$ choose lifts $f_1,g_1,h_1\in E_1$; then $g_1h_1$, $f_1g_1$ and $h_1f_1$ are lifts in $E_1$ of $gh$, $fg$ and $fh$ (products of elements of the ideal $E_1$ are in $E_1$, and products of elements congruent to $f,g,h$ modulo $E_2$ are congruent modulo $E_2$), and the identity $[f_1,g_1h_1]-[f_1g_1,h_1]-[h_1f_1,g_1]=0$, which holds in any associative algebra by cancellation of $f_1g_1h_1$, gives $r(f,gh)=r(fg,h)+r(fh,g)$. [step 4.1, F1, F4]

6.1 (Descent to $\Omega^1_{K/k}$) Let $T$ be the quotient of the free $k$-vector space on symbols $f\otimes g$ ($f,g\in K$) by all bilinearity relations and the Leibniz relations $f\otimes gh-fg\otimes h-fh\otimes g$. Give $T$ the $K$-module action $a\cdot(f\otimes g)=(af)\otimes g$. This action is well defined on the quotient: it preserves each bilinearity relation, and sends a Leibniz relation to $(af)\otimes gh-(af)g\otimes h-(af)h\otimes g$, again a Leibniz relation. Steps 4.1 and 5.1 make $f\otimes g\mapsto r(f,g)$ a well-defined $k$-linear map $\rho_T\colon T\to k$. The assignment $D_T(g)=1\otimes g$ is a $k$-derivation $K\to T$: additivity and $k$-homogeneity follow from bilinearity, and the Leibniz relation with first factor $1$ gives $D_T(gh)=gD_T(h)+hD_T(g)$. The relation with $f=g=h=1$ gives $1\otimes1=0$, so $D_T(\lambda)=1\otimes\lambda=\lambda(1\otimes1)=0$ for every $\lambda\in k$. By [F7], there is a unique $K$-linear map $\eta\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\to T$ with $\eta(\mathrm{d}g)=1\otimes g$. Conversely, $\mu(f\otimes g)=f\,\mathrm{d}g$ defines a $K$-linear map $\mu\colon T\to\Omega^1_{K/k}$ because $\mathrm{d}$ satisfies the Leibniz rule [F6]. These maps are inverse: $\mu\eta$ is the identity on the generators $\mathrm{d}g$, while $\eta\mu(f\otimes g)=f\cdot(1\otimes g)=f\otimes g$ on every generator of $T$. Hence $\eta$ identifies $\Omega^1_{K/k}$ with $T$. [step 5.1, F1, F6, F7]

7.1 (The residue map) Define $\operatorname{res}_V=\rho_T\circ\eta\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\to k$, using the isomorphism $\eta$ of step 6.1. It is $k$-linear and satisfies $\operatorname{res}_V(f\,\mathrm{d}g)=r(f,g)=\operatorname{Tr}_V([f_1,g_1])$ for all lifts $f_1,g_1\in E_1$ of $f,g$, and by step 3.1 also for every pair $f_1,g_1\in E$ satisfying the hypotheses of the statement. [step 6.1, F3]

8.1 (Uniqueness) If a $k$-linear map $\operatorname{res}'\colon\Omega^1_{K/k}\to k$ satisfies the displayed formula for all admissible pairs, then $\operatorname{res}'$ and $\operatorname{res}_V$ agree on every element $f\,\mathrm{d}g$; since these elements generate $\Omega^1_{K/k}$ as a $K$-module by step 6.1, and hence as a $k$-vector space, $\operatorname{res}'=\operatorname{res}_V$. [step 7.1, F6]

9.1 The map $\operatorname{res}_V$ therefore exists with the required values on all pairs of lifts satisfying the stated congruences and the condition $f_1\in E_1$ or $g_1\in E_1$ by steps 1.1, 3.1 and 7.1, and it is unique in the sense of step 8.1; the Axiom of Choice enters only through the cited suppliers, and no further choice is made. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, F2] ∎
