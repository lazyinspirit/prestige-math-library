## Definition

Set $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$, $K=\mathbb Q(\omega)$, $O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$, and
$D=O[1/2]=\mathbb Z[\omega,1/2]$. The relation $\omega^4=-1$ reduces every
element of $O$ to four integer coefficients in the power basis
$1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3$. The canonical dyadic encoding of a nonzero
$z\in D$ is

$$z=(a_0+a_1\omega+a_2\omega^2+a_3\omega^3)/2^r,$$

where $r\ge0$ is least and, if $r>0$, the four integers are not all even.
Zero is encoded with $r=0$ and all four coefficients zero. Arithmetic is exact:
multiply polynomials, reduce with $\omega^4=-1$, combine denominators, and
remove the common power of $2$ from the coefficients. The integral-basis
supplier [[thm-cyclotomic-ring-of-integers]] and the proof of
[[lem-qc-dyadic-coefficient-encoding]] justify existence and uniqueness.

## Remarks

The ring $D$ is a subring of $K\subset\mathbb C$. All coefficients and equality
tests in this page use the displayed power basis; no floating-point comparison
is implicit.
