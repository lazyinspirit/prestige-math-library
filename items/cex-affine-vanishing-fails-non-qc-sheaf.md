## Statement refuted

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Quasi-coherence is a necessary hypothesis in the affine vanishing theorem
[[thm-qc-sheaf-affine-higher-cohomology-vanishes]]: there is an
$\mathcal O_X$-module on an affine scheme with nonvanishing higher cohomology.
Explicitly, let $k$ be any field, $A=k[t]$, $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, let
$Z=\{(t),(t-1)\}=V(t)\cup V(t-1)$ be the closed subset consisting of the two
closed points, let $U=X\setminus Z$ with inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$, and
let
$$\mathcal F=j_!\,\mathcal O_U$$
be the extension by zero ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]]) of the
structure sheaf of the open subscheme $U$, equipped with its natural
$\mathcal O_X$-module structure. Then $\mathcal F$ is not quasi-coherent
([[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]) and
$$H^1(X,\mathcal F)\;\cong\;\bigl(A_{(t)}\times A_{(t-1)}\bigr)/A\;\neq\;0,$$
the quotient of the product of the two localisations by the diagonal copy of
$A$, with sheaf cohomology as in
[[def-sheaf-cohomology-derived-global-sections]]. The field $k$ is arbitrary,
including $k=\mathbb F_2$; $Z$ is nonempty and discrete, and $U$ is nonempty,
so $\mathcal F\neq0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, A field $k$, the ring $A=k[t]$, the scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$,
the closed subset $Z=\{(t),(t-1)\}$, its open complement $U$ with inclusion $j$
and the sheaf $\mathcal F=j_!\mathcal O_U$.

[F1] For the open inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ and a sheaf of abelian
groups $\mathcal G$ on $U$, the extension by zero $j_!\mathcal G$ has sections
over an open $V\subseteq X$ consisting of those $s\in\mathcal G(V\cap U)$ whose
support is closed in $V$; for $V\subseteq U$ all sections qualify, so
$(j_!\mathcal G)(V)=\mathcal G(V)$.
([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]])

[F2] If $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ is an open subspace with closed complement
$i:Z\hookrightarrow X$, then for every sheaf of abelian groups $\mathcal G$ on
$X$ there is a short exact sequence
$0\to j_!(\mathcal G|_U)\to\mathcal G\to i_*(\mathcal G|_Z)\to0$ of sheaves of
abelian groups on $X$. ([[lem-extension-by-zero-short-exact-sequence]])

[F3] A short exact sequence of abelian sheaves on a topological space induces a
natural long exact sequence in sheaf cohomology, connecting each $H^q$ of the
quotient to $H^{q+1}$ of the subsheaf.
([[thm-long-exact-sequence-sheaf-cohomology]])

[F4] On the affine scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ the structure sheaf is the
associated sheaf $\widetilde A$ of the free module of rank one, hence
quasi-coherent; the affine vanishing theorem gives $H^q(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$ for
every $q>0$, and more generally $H^q(X,\mathcal G)=0$ for $q>0$ and every
quasi-coherent $\mathcal G$. The affine quasi-coherent equivalence and affine vanishing suppliers are now authored and their current statements are used here.
([[def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]],
[[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]],
[[thm-qc-sheaf-affine-higher-cohomology-vanishes]])

[F5] For a prime $\mathfrak p$ of a ring $A$ the stalk of the structure sheaf
is $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Spec}A,\mathfrak p}\cong A_{\mathfrak p}$; the
closed points $(t)$ and $(t-1)$ of $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ are the maximal
ideals generated by the irreducible polynomials $t$ and $t-1$. Sections of a
sheaf on a discrete space are the product of the stalks over its points, by the
sheaf condition. ([[thm-stalk-structure-sheaf-prime-localization]],
[[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]])

[F6] An $\mathcal O_X$-module is a sheaf whose section groups are modules over
the section rings, compatibly with restriction; an $\mathcal O_X$-module
structure on a subsheaf of $\mathcal O_X$ is inherited from the multiplication
of the structure sheaf. ([[def-module-on-ringed-space]])

[F7] The Axiom of Choice is the choice-function principle ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). It licenses the AC-qualified supplier used at step 4.1.

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct: the extension-by-zero module sits in the standard complement exact sequence, and the long exact sequence computes its first cohomology as a cokernel of the diagonal embedding of the polynomial ring into a product of two localisations.

1.1 The sheaf $\mathcal F=j_!\mathcal O_U$ carries the structure of an $\mathcal O_X$-module. Indeed $\mathcal O_U$ is the restriction $\mathcal O_X|_U$ ([[def-extension-by-zero-abelian-sheaf]], [[def-module-on-ringed-space]]), and on an open $V\subseteq X$ the group $\mathcal F(V)\subseteq\mathcal O_X(V\cap U)$ consists of the sections whose support is closed in $V$; multiplying such a section by the restriction of a section $a\in\mathcal O_X(V)$ preserves the support condition, and the restriction maps of $\mathcal F$ are those of $\mathcal O_X$, so the presheaf-level multiplication makes $\mathcal F$ a sheaf of $\mathcal O_X$-modules by [F6]. The inclusion of $\mathcal F$ into $\mathcal O_X$ and the quotient map to $i_*(\mathcal O_X|_Z)$ are $\mathcal O_X$-linear. [F1, F6]

1.2 The closed subset $Z=\{(t),(t-1)\}$ is discrete: the two points are the maximal ideals $(t)$ and $(t-1)$, their defining closed sets $V(t)$ and $V(t-1)$ are disjoint because $t$ and $t-1$ generate the unit ideal of $k[t]$, and $Z=V(t(t-1))$ is closed. Hence $\Gamma(X,i_*(\mathcal O_X|_Z))=\mathcal O_{X,(t)}\times\mathcal O_{X,(t-1)}$, the product of the two stalks at the points of $Z$, and by [F5] this is $A_{(t)}\times A_{(t-1)}$. The structure sheaf is quasi-coherent with $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=A$, so $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$ and $H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)=A$ by [F4]. [F4, F5]

1.3 Applying [F2] to the abelian sheaf $\mathcal G=\mathcal O_X$ and the open inclusion $j:U\hookrightarrow X$ with closed complement $Z$ gives the short exact sequence of abelian sheaves $0\to\mathcal F\to\mathcal O_X\to i_*(\mathcal O_X|_Z)\to0$. [F1, F2]

2.1 The long exact cohomology sequence of step 1.3 begins $0\to H^0(X,\mathcal F)\to H^0(X,\mathcal O_X)\to H^0(X,i_*(\mathcal O_X|_Z))\to H^1(X,\mathcal F)\to H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)$. Substituting the identifications of step 1.2 and $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X)=0$, this reads $0\to H^0(X,\mathcal F)\to A\xrightarrow{\Delta}A_{(t)}\times A_{(t-1)}\to H^1(X,\mathcal F)\to0$, where $\Delta(f)=(f/1,f/1)$ is the diagonal embedding; exactness at the last two terms gives $H^1(X,\mathcal F)\cong(A_{(t)}\times A_{(t-1)})/\Delta(A)$. [F3, step 1.2, step 1.3]

3.1 The quotient of step 2.1 is nonzero: the class of the element $(0,1)$ is not in the image of $\Delta$, since $\Delta(f)=(0,1)$ would force $f=0$ in $A_{(t)}$ (as $A\to A_{(t)}$ is injective, $A$ being a domain) and simultaneously $f/1=1$ in $A_{(t-1)}$, a contradiction. Hence $H^1(X,\mathcal F)\neq0$. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Finally, $\mathcal F$ is not quasi-coherent. If it were, then since $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ is affine the AC-qualified affine vanishing theorem [F4], licensed by [F7], would give $H^1(X,\mathcal F)=0$, contradicting step 3.1. Thus the displayed $\mathcal O_X$-module on the affine scheme $X$ has nonvanishing $H^1$, so the quasi-coherence hypothesis of affine vanishing cannot be dropped; the module is nonzero because $U\neq\varnothing$ and $(j_!\mathcal O_U)(U)=\mathcal O_X(U)\neq0$ by [F1]. The Axiom of Choice is inherited from [F3] and [F4], and the only selections made are the two closed points already named. [F1, F3, F4, F7, step 3.1] ∎
