## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field ([[def-field]]) and let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be an affine $k$-scheme ([[def-affine-scheme]]). If $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is of finite type ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]]), then $A$ is a finitely generated $k$-algebra ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]). Consequently the coordinate ring of a group scheme of finite type over $k$ ([[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]) whose underlying scheme is affine is finitely generated. The Axiom of Choice is used for affine-chart quasi-compactness and to turn a finite distinguished-open cover into a unit-ideal relation ([[lem-spectrum-compactness-open-cover-to-unit-ideal]]); no other choice is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A morphism of finite type is quasi-compact and locally of finite type; locally of finite type over $\operatorname{Spec}k$ means that every point of $X$ has an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $k\to B$ of finite type, that is, $B$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra. ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]], [[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]])

[F2] For every Zariski-open $U\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ and every point $\mathfrak p\in U$ there is $f\in A$ with $\mathfrak p\in D(f)\subseteq U$. ([[lem-distinguished-open-refinement-at-a-point]], [[def-principal-distinguished-subset-of-spectrum]])

[F3] For $f\in A$ the basic open $D(f)$ has $\Gamma(D(f),\mathcal O_X)=A_f$ and is identified with the open subscheme $\operatorname{Spec}A_f$, compatibly with restriction of sections. ([[thm-sections-basic-open-affine-scheme]], [[lem-spectrum-localization-open-immersion]], [[def-principal-localisation]])

[F4] Under the assumed Axiom of Choice, every affine scheme is quasi-compact; the published proof uses the AC-dependent quasi-compactness of distinguished opens. ([[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]])

[F5] If $\operatorname{Spec}A=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}D(f_\lambda)$, then the ideal generated by the $f_\lambda$ is the unit ideal. This is the unit-ideal use of the Axiom of Choice, in addition to [F4]. ([[lem-spectrum-compactness-open-cover-to-unit-ideal]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a field $k$, an affine $k$-scheme $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and the hypothesis that $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is of finite type.

1.1 By [F1] the morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is quasi-compact and locally of finite type, so its affine open charts $\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $B$ a finitely generated $k$-algebra cover $X$; quasi-compactness extracts finitely many of them, giving $X=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_i$ with $U_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$ and $B_i$ finitely generated over $k$. [F1, given]

2.1 Each $U_i$ is affine, hence quasi-compact by [F4], and by [F2] every point of $U_i$ lies in some distinguished open $D(f)\subseteq U_i$ with $f\in A$; finitely many such sets cover $U_i$. Collecting the finitely many results over $i=1,\dots,n$, there are $f_1,\dots,f_m\in A$ with $X=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}D(f_j)$ and $D(f_j)\subseteq U_{i(j)}$ for a suitable index $i(j)$. [F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Fix $j$ and put $i=i(j)$, so that $D(f_j)\subseteq U_i=\operatorname{Spec}B_i$; let $b\in B_i$ be the restriction of the global section $f_j$ to $U_i$. A prime $\mathfrak q\in U_i$ has image $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap A$ under the open immersion, so $f_j\notin\mathfrak p$ if and only if $b\notin\mathfrak q$, and therefore $D(f_j)=D(b)$ as open subsets of $U_i$. The structure sheaf of $X$ restricted to $U_i$ is that of $U_i$, so by [F3] $A_{f_j}=\Gamma(D(f_j),\mathcal O_X)=\Gamma(D(b),\mathcal O_{U_i})=(B_i)_b$, a principal localization of a finitely generated $k$-algebra and hence again a finitely generated $k$-algebra. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For each $j$ choose finitely many $k$-algebra generators of $A_{f_j}$ and write them as fractions with numerators in $A$. By [F5] choose a unit relation $1=\sum_j\lambda_jf_j$ with $\lambda_j\in A$. Let $B\subseteq A$ be the $k$-subalgebra generated by all $f_j$, all these numerators, and all $\lambda_j$. It is finitely generated, and the natural map $B_{f_j}\to A_{f_j}$ is surjective for each $j$, since its image contains the chosen algebra generators. [F5, step 3.1, choose, construct]

5.1 Fix $a\in A$. For every $j$, surjectivity in step 4.1 gives $a/1=b_j/f_j^{r_j}$ in $A_{f_j}$ for some $b_j\in B$; equality of localization fractions gives $f_j^{t_j}(f_j^{r_j}a-b_j)=0$ in $A$ for some $t_j\ge0$. Thus $f_j^{r_j+t_j}a=f_j^{t_j}b_j\in B$. Choose $N\ge1$ exceeding the finitely many exponents $r_j+t_j$; then $f_j^Na\in B$ for all $j$. Expanding $(\sum_j\lambda_jf_j)^{mN}=1$ shows $1=\sum_jc_jf_j^N$ with $c_j\in B$, because each monomial has some $f_j$-exponent at least $N$ and all $\lambda_j,f_j$ belong to $B$. Hence $a=\sum_jc_j(f_j^Na)\in B$. This argument chooses $N$ separately for each $a$, and proves $A=B$. If $X$ is empty, the same unit-ideal conclusion gives $A=0$, which is finitely generated as well. [F5, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For the consequence, if $G$ is a group scheme of finite type over $k$ whose underlying scheme is affine, say $G=\operatorname{Spec}A$, then by definition its structure morphism $G\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is of finite type, so the argument above makes its coordinate ring finitely generated over $k$. The AC-dependent inputs were affine-chart quasi-compactness [F4] and the unit-ideal relation [F5]; the subsequent selections were finite. [F5, step 5.1, given] ∎
