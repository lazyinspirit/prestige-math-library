## Statement refuted

Every set-theoretic section of a quotient map of groups is automatically a
homomorphism.

The quotient map $C_4\to C_2$ with section $[0]\mapsto[0]$, $[1]\mapsto[1]$ is
a counterexample.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The additive cyclic groups $C_4=\mathbb Z/4$ and $C_2=\mathbb Z/2$.

[L1] The false claim being refuted is the one stated in
[[fs-a-set-theoretic-section-of-an-extension-is-automatically-a-homomorphism]].

[L2] Cyclic groups are generated by one element, so the displayed additive
models are valid presentations of $C_4$ and $C_2$
([[thm-classification-of-cyclic-groups]]).

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Reduction mod $2$ is a surjective homomorphism $\pi:C_4\to C_2$, and the displayed map $s$ is a set-theoretic section because $\pi(s([0]))=[0]$ and $\pi(s([1]))=[1]$. [given, L2, L1]

2.1 But $s([1]+[1])=s([0])=[0]\neq[2]=s([1])+s([1])$ in $C_4$. Hence $s$ is not a homomorphism. Therefore the universal claim [L1] is false. [L1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
