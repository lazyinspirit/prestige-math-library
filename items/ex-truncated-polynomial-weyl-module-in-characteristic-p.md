## Statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, where $p$ is prime. On $M=k[t]/(t^p)$, multiplication by $t$ and formal differentiation induce a nonzero $p$-dimensional unital left module over $A_1(k)=W_1(k;1)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ of characteristic $p>0$, the polynomial ring $k[t]$, and its ideal $(t^p)$ generated by $t^p$.

[F1] [[def-polynomial-weyl-algebra]]: $A_1(k)=T(E_1)/J_{1,1}$, with $E_1$ spanned by $x_1,p_1$ and $J_{1,1}$ the two-sided ideal generated by the same-type commutators and $p_1x_1-x_1p_1-1$.

[F2] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: the ideal generated by a set is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing it; in particular, $(t^p)$ is the two-sided ideal generated by $t^p$ in $k[t]$.

[F3] [[def-quotient-ring]]: the quotient uses cosets modulo that ideal, with multiplication induced from $k[t]$.

[F4] [[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]]: every polynomial is a finite $k$-linear combination of monomials $t^m$.

[F5] [[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]: a linear assignment from the generators to a unital associative algebra extends to a unital algebra homomorphism from the tensor algebra.

[F6] [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]: a ring homomorphism whose kernel contains a two-sided ideal factors uniquely through the quotient ring.

[F7] [[def-left-and-right-modules]]: a unital algebra homomorphism into endomorphisms defines a unital left module action by evaluation.

## Verification

1.1 Define $D(f)=f'$ by $D(t^m)=m t^{m-1}$ and extend $k$-linearly. For each $m\geq0$, $D(t^pt^m)=(p+m)t^{p+m-1}=m t^{p+m-1}=t^pD(t^m)$ because $p=0$ in $k$. By [F4], every polynomial is a finite linear combination of monomials, so linearity gives $D(t^pf)=t^pD(f)$ for every $f$; hence $D$ preserves $(t^p)$. Multiplication $X[f]=[tf]$ also descends because $t(t^p f)\in(t^p)$. [given, F2, F3, F4, construct, algebra]

2.1 For each monomial $t^m$, $D(t\,t^m)-tD(t^m)=(m+1)t^m-mt^m=t^m$. By [F4] and linearity the identity holds for every polynomial, hence on $M$ it gives $DX-XD=I_M$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Pointwise addition and scalar multiplication and composition make $\operatorname{End}_k(M)$ a unital associative $k$-algebra: composition is associative, $I_M$ is its unit, and $k$-bilinearity follows by evaluating on vectors. Send the generators $x_1,p_1$ to $X,D$. By [F5] this linear assignment extends to a unital algebra map from $T(E_1)$. Step 2.1 and $[X,X]=[D,D]=0$ show that each defining relator in [F1] maps to zero. The map's kernel is a two-sided ideal because it is an algebra homomorphism, so it contains their generated ideal by [F2]. By [F6] the map factors through the quotient $A_1(k)$; write $\rho$ for this factor map, which is $k$-linear because the quotient map is surjective. Its evaluation $a\cdot m:=\rho(a)m$ is a unital left module action by [F7]. [F1, F2, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 By [F4], every polynomial has finitely many monomials; subtracting suitable multiples of $t^p$ removes every term whose exponent is at least $p$, so each coset is represented by a linear combination of $1,t,\ldots,t^{p-1}$. A polynomial multiple of $t^p$ has only monomials with exponent at least $p$, so no nonzero linear combination of those $p$ lower monomials lies in $(t^p)$. Hence $1,[t],\ldots,[t^{p-1}]$ is a basis of $M$, and $M$ is nonzero of dimension $p$. This is the positive-characteristic exception to the characteristic-zero obstruction. [given, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
