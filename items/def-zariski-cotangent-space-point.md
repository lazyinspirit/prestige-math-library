## Definition

Let $X$ be a scheme and $x\in X$. Write $A=\mathcal O_{X,x}$ and let
$\mathfrak m_x$ be the maximal ideal of this local ring. The **intrinsic
Zariski cotangent space** of $X$ at $x$ is
$$ C_xX:=\mathfrak m_x/\mathfrak m_x^2. $$
It is a vector space over the residue field
$\kappa(x)=\mathcal O_{X,x}/\mathfrak m_x$
([[def-residue-field-scheme-point]]).

The scalar action is induced by multiplication in $A$: the class of $a\in A$
acts on the class of $b\in\mathfrak m_x$ by the class of $ab$. This action
depends only on the residue class of $a$, since replacing $a$ by an element
congruent modulo $\mathfrak m_x$ changes $ab$ by an element of
$\mathfrak m_x^2$. Thus the action factors through the field $\kappa(x)$.

For a classical variety over an algebraically closed field at a closed
$k$-rational point, the residue field is $k$ and this is the usual cotangent
space $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$ of the local ring. The definition above also
applies to nonclosed points of arbitrary schemes.

This is the cotangent space of the underlying scheme at the point. No
identification with a relative cotangent space over a chosen base is included
in this definition.

For a direct check of the extreme dimensions, at the origin of
$\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$ the local ring is $k[t]_{(t)}$ and its maximal ideal
is generated by $t$, so $C_0X=(t)/(t^2)\cong k$ with basis the class of $t$.
At the generic point $\eta$ of $\operatorname{Spec} k[t]$, the local ring is
the field $k(t)$, so its maximal ideal is zero and $C_\eta X=0$.
