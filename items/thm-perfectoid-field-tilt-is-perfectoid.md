## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Dependent Choice. Let $K$ be a perfectoid field and choose
$\varpi\in K$ with $|p|_K<|\varpi|_K<1$, where $|p|_K=0$ if
$\operatorname{char}K=p$. Put
$A_\varpi=\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^p}K^\circ/\varpi K^\circ$. Then
$A_\varpi$ is a complete rank-one valuation domain of characteristic $p$,
and its fraction field $K^\flat=\operatorname{Frac}(A_\varpi)$ is complete
for the valuation induced by $A_\varpi$. Its ring of integers is
$A_\varpi$, its value group is $|K^\times|_K$, and it is a perfectoid field
of characteristic $p$. The map $x^\flat\mapsto x^\sharp$ identifies the
underlying multiplicative monoid of $K^\flat$ with
$\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^p}K$; this identification shows that the resulting
field is independent of $\varpi$ up to canonical isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Dependent Choice, the perfectoid field $K$, and $\varpi$ as in the
Statement.

[A1] For every nonempty set with a serial relation and every prescribed
starting point, Dependent Choice supplies an infinite sequence following that
relation. ([[def-dependent-choice]])

[F1] [[def-perfectoid-field-and-its-tilt]]: $K$ is complete, its value group
$\Gamma_K=|K^\times|_K\subset\mathbb R_{>0}$ is nondiscrete, its residue
field has characteristic $p$, and Frobenius on $K^\circ/pK^\circ$ is
surjective. The tilt ring associated with $\varpi$ is
$A_\varpi=\varprojlim_F K^\circ/\varpi K^\circ$.

[F2] [[def-complete-nonarchimedean-field]]: The absolute value on $K$ is
real-valued, multiplicative and nonarchimedean, and $K$ is complete for
$d_K(x,y)=|x-y|_K$.

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 If $\operatorname{char}K=p$, then $K^\circ$ is perfect because Frobenius on $K^\circ/pK^\circ=K^\circ$ is surjective; taking a root of an element of value at most $1$ and taking inverses for values greater than $1$ shows $\Gamma_K=\Gamma_K^p$. Suppose instead $\operatorname{char}K=0$ and write $I=\{\gamma\in\Gamma_K:|p|_K<\gamma\le1\}$. For $\gamma\in I$, choose $x\in K^\circ$ with $|x|_K=\gamma$ and, by [F1], choose $y\in K^\circ$ with $y^p\equiv x\pmod{pK^\circ}$. Since $|p|_K<|x|_K$, the ultrametric inequality gives $|y|_K^p=\gamma$. Nondiscreteness supplies $\delta\in I$ with $\delta<1$; both $\delta$ and $|p|_K/\delta$ lie in $I$, so $|p|_K$ lies in the subgroup generated by $I$. Every element of $\Gamma_K$ becomes an element of $I$ after multiplication by an integral power of $|p|_K$, so $I$ generates $\Gamma_K$. Since $\Gamma_K^p=\{\gamma^p:\gamma\in\Gamma_K\}$ is a subgroup containing $I$, $\Gamma_K=\Gamma_K^p$. [F1, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 Choose $u\in K^\times$ with $|u|_K^p=|\varpi|_K$, using 1.1. Its class $\bar u$ in $K^\circ/\varpi K^\circ$ is nonzero and satisfies $\bar u^p=0$, because $|u|_K>|\varpi|_K$ and $|u^p|_K=|\varpi|_K$. Frobenius on $K^\circ/\varpi K^\circ$ is surjective: take a $p$th root modulo $p$ using [F1], then reduce modulo $\varpi$, since $pK^\circ\subseteq\varpi K^\circ$. Starting with $\bar u_0=0$ and $\bar u_1=\bar u$, each finite compatible $p$-root sequence extends by one term. Apply [A1] to the set of such finite sequences, ordered by one-term extension, to obtain $\varpi^\flat=(0,\bar u_1,\bar u_2,\ldots)\in A_\varpi$. It is nonzero because $\bar u_1\ne0$. [A1, F1, step 1.1, given, choose, construct]

3.1 For $x=(x_0,x_1,\ldots)\in A_\varpi$, use [A1] to choose lifts $\widetilde x_n\in K^\circ$ and set $x^\sharp=\lim_{n\to\infty}\widetilde x_n^{p^n}$. Consecutive lifts satisfy $\widetilde x_{n+1}^{p}-\widetilde x_n\in\varpi K^\circ$. If $\operatorname{char}K=0$, let $v_p(m)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in a positive integer $m$. For $0<k<p^n$, the identity $k\binom{p^n}{k}=p^n\binom{p^n-1}{k-1}$ gives $v_p\!\left(\binom{p^n}{k}\right)\ge n-v_p(k)$. In the binomial expansion of the difference of the $p^n$th powers, the term of index $k$ has norm at most $|p|_K^{n-v_p(k)}|\varpi|_K^k$, since residue characteristic $p$ makes every integer prime to $p$ a unit of norm $1$, and both lifts have norm at most $1$. For $k<p^{n/2}$, $v_p(k)<n/2$, so this is at most $|p|_K^{n/2}$; for $k\ge p^{n/2}$ it is at most $|\varpi|_K^{p^{n/2}}$. The end term is bounded by $|\varpi|_K^{p^n}$. These bounds tend to zero, so the sequence of lifted powers is Cauchy. If $\operatorname{char}K=p$, all intermediate binomial coefficients vanish and the end term again tends to zero. The same estimates applied to two choices of lifts show that the limit is independent of the lifts. Multiplication commutes with the limit, so $\sharp$ is multiplicative. The same calculation also gives the uniform ideal bound $u^{p^n}-v^{p^n}\in\varpi^{n+1}K^\circ$ whenever $u-v\in\varpi K^\circ$: use $p\in\varpi K^\circ$ and $n-v_p(k)+k\ge n+1$, with the final term bounded by $\varpi^{p^n}$. Consequently $x^\sharp-\widetilde x_n^{p^n}\in\varpi^{n+1}K^\circ$. For the sequence constructed in 2.1, the first lifted power has norm $|\varpi|_K$ and every subsequent difference has norm strictly less than $|\varpi|_K$ (for the first transition use the exact coefficient $p$; for $n\ge2$ use the displayed bounds). Hence $|\varpi^\flat|_\flat=|\varpi|_K$. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The map $A_\varpi\to\varprojlim_{x\mapsto x^p}K^\circ$, $x\mapsto(x^\sharp,(x^{1/p})^\sharp,\ldots)$, is inverse to reduction of a compatible sequence in $K^\circ$ modulo $\varpi$. The lift formula in 3.1 shows both compositions are identities. Reduction to each quotient is continuous because $\varpi K^\circ$ is open. If two inverse-limit elements have the same $N$th coordinate, their sharp values differ by at most $|\varpi|_K^{N+1}$ by the uniform bound in 3.1; applying the same argument after every fixed shift proves continuity of every exact root coordinate. Thus both maps are continuous, giving the multiplicative homeomorphism. [F2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 For the root sequence $(z_0,z_1,\ldots)$ corresponding to $x\in A_\varpi$ by 4.1, $z_n^p=z_{n-1}$ for $n\ge1$. If $z_0=0$, then every $z_n=0$ and 4.1 gives $x=0$. Thus for $x\ne0$ all $z_n$ are nonzero and $|z_n|_K^{p^n}=|z_0|_K$. Define $|x|_\flat=|z_0|_K$. It is positive and multiplicative. Choose coordinate lifts $\widetilde x_n$ and $\widetilde y_n$ as in 3.1. Their sums lift the coordinatewise sum $x+y$, so the lift formula and the ultrametric inequality give $|x+y|_\flat=\lim_n|\widetilde x_n+\widetilde y_n|_K^{p^n} \le\lim_n\max(|\widetilde x_n|_K,|\widetilde y_n|_K)^{p^n} =\max(|x|_\flat,|y|_\flat).$ Hence $A_\varpi$ has no zero divisors. If $x,y\in A_\varpi$ are nonzero and $|x|_\flat\le|y|_\flat$, then each coordinate quotient $z_n(x)/z_n(y)$ lies in $K^\circ$, and these quotients form a compatible $p$-power root sequence. By 4.1 this sequence comes from an element of $A_\varpi$. Thus every fraction of norm at most $1$ lies in $A_\varpi$, which is the valuation ring of $K^\flat=\operatorname{Frac}(A_\varpi)$. [F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, given, algebra]

6.1 The kernel of the $n$th inverse-limit coordinate projection consists exactly of elements with $|x|_\flat\le|\varpi|_K^{p^n}$, because the $n$th root coordinate has norm $|x|_\flat^{1/p^n}$. By 5.1 and the valuation-ring description, this kernel is $(\varpi^\flat)^{p^n}A_\varpi$. The inverse limit of the discrete quotient rings is complete, and these kernels form a neighborhood basis cofinal among the powers of $\varpi^\flat$. Thus the inverse-limit topology is the valuation topology and $A_\varpi$ is complete. A Cauchy sequence in its fraction field has bounded values: after some index all terms differ from one fixed term by elements of norm at most $1$, and the finitely many earlier terms have a finite maximum norm. Multiplication by a fixed power of $\varpi^\flat$ therefore puts the sequence in $A_\varpi$; completeness there gives a limit in the fraction field. The value group of $K^\flat$ is contained in $\Gamma_K$ because $|x|_\flat=|z_0|_K$. Conversely, for $\gamma\in\Gamma_K$ choose an integer $m$ such that $\gamma'=\gamma|\varpi|_K^m$ lies in $(|\varpi|_K,1]$, and choose $a\in K^\circ$ with $|a|_K=\gamma'$. Frobenius surjectivity on $K^\circ/\varpi K^\circ$ lets us choose recursively classes $a_0=a\bmod\varpi,a_1,a_2,\ldots$ with $a_{j+1}^p=a_j$. Every lift of $a_j$ has norm $\gamma'^{1/p^j}$: at each step the congruence error has norm at most $|\varpi|_K$, strictly less than the norm of the preceding term, so the ultrametric inequality gives equality of the norms of the $p$th powers. The same estimates as in 3.1 show that successive sharp approximants differ by norm strictly less than $\gamma'$; hence their limit has norm $\gamma'$. Dividing it by $(\varpi^\flat)^m$ gives an element of norm $\gamma$. Hence the value groups agree. The ring $A_\varpi$ has characteristic $p$ and bijective Frobenius by the inverse-limit shift, so $K^\flat$ is perfectoid. To extend 4.1 from the valuation ring to the whole multiplicative monoid, let $(z_n)$ be any exact $p$-power root sequence in $K$. Its norms satisfy $|z_n|_K^{p^n}=|z_0|_K$. Write $(t_n)$ for the exact root sequence of $\varpi^\flat$. Choose an integer $m\ge0$ with $|z_0t_0^m|_K\le1$; then every $z_nt_n^m$ lies in $K^\circ$, so 4.1 represents the sequence by an element of $A_\varpi$ divided by $(\varpi^\flat)^m$. This proves surjectivity on the whole monoid, and injectivity follows by multiplying two fractions by a common such denominator and using 4.1. The subsets on which $|z_0t_0^m|_K\le1$ are open: they are inverse images under the first projection of nonarchimedean closed balls, which are also open. Multiplication by the fixed root sequence $(t_n^m)$ identifies each such subset with the valuation-ring case of 4.1. The corresponding bounded balls in $K^\flat$ are open as well. These subsets cover both sides, so the whole-field bijection and its inverse are continuous. In exact root-sequence coordinates, addition is characterized, first on $A_\varpi$, by $z_n(x+y)=\lim_{j\to\infty}(z_{n+j}(x)+z_{n+j}(y))^{p^j}$, directly from the lift formula in 3.1 applied to the shifted coordinates; multiplication is coordinatewise. These formulas are independent of $\varpi$, and scaling by a common denominator extends them to the fraction field. They prove that the canonical multiplicative identifications also preserve addition and hence give the asserted canonical field isomorphisms. [A1, F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
