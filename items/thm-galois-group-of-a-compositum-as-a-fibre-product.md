## Statement

Let $E_1/F$ and $E_2/F$ be finite Galois extensions inside a common overfield, and put $D=E_1\cap E_2$. Then $E_1E_2/F$ is finite Galois and restriction identifies its Galois group with the fibre product

$$\{(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_1/F)\times\operatorname{Gal}(E_2/F):\sigma_1|_D=\sigma_2|_D\}.$$

This image is the full direct product exactly when $D=F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite Galois extensions $E_1/F$ and $E_2/F$; writing $f_i$ for a separable polynomial with splitting field $E_i$, their compositum is the splitting field over $F$ of the product of the distinct irreducible factors of $f_1f_2$, a polynomial with the same roots as $f_1f_2$ and no repeated one, so the compositum is Galois by [[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]; restriction from a Galois extension onto a Galois intermediate field is surjective by [[thm-normal-subgroups-and-quotients-in-the-galois-correspondence]]; and the fixed field of a full finite Galois group is the base field by [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]].

[L1] For finite Galois $E/L_0$ and any extension $L/L_0$, restriction gives $\operatorname{Gal}(EL/L)\cong\operatorname{Gal}(E/E\cap L)$ ([[thm-galois-translation-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Restriction sends $\operatorname{Gal}(E_1E_2/F)$ injectively into the product of the two relative Galois groups, since $E_1E_2$ is generated by $E_1$ and $E_2$. Both restrictions agree on $D$, so the image lies in the displayed fibre product. [given]

2.1 Conversely, let $(\sigma_1,\sigma_2)$ have equal restrictions to $D$. Extend $\sigma_2$ to some $\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_1E_2/F)$ using surjectivity of restriction. Then $\rho:=\sigma_1(\tau|_{E_1})^{-1}$ fixes $D$, and [L1] supplies $h\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_1E_2/E_2)$ with $h|_{E_1}=\rho$. The automorphism $h\tau$ restricts to $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$, proving that every compatible pair is in the image. [step 1.1, L1, given]

3.1 For the forward implication of the last assertion, if $D=F$ then compatibility is automatic and step 2.1 gives the full product. For the reverse implication, if the image is the full product, every pair $(1,\sigma_2)$ is compatible, so every $\sigma_2\in\operatorname{Gal}(E_2/F)$ fixes $D$; its fixed field is $F$, hence $D=F$. If $E_1=E_2$, the fibre product is instead the diagonal subgroup, as the formula requires. [step 2.1, given] ∎
