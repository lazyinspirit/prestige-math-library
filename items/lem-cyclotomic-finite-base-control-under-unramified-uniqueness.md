## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice and DC. Let $K$ be a totally real number field, $p$ any prime, and $K_\infty/K$ its cyclotomic $\mathbb Z_p$-extension with degree-$p^n$ layers $K_n$, Galois group $\Gamma=\langle\gamma\rangle$, class groups $A_n=\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_{K_n})[p^\infty]$ and norm limit $X=\varprojlim A_n$. Choose a stable index $m$ as in the cyclotomic inertia-trace theorem, so every prime above $p$ is totally ramified after level $m$, and let $t$ be their number in $K_m$. Let $L_\infty/K_\infty$ be the maximal finite-place-unramified abelian pro-$p$ extension that is totally real, and put $\omega_n=\gamma^{p^n}-1$. Then:

1. $X/\omega_mX$ is finite if and only if $K_\infty$ is the only $\mathbb Z_p$-extension of $K_m$ contained in $L_\infty$.
2. Under these equivalent conditions, the norm-induced maps $X/\omega_nX\to A_n$ are surjective with kernel orders bounded independently of $n\geq m$.
3. If $t=1$, all these maps are isomorphisms for $n\geq m$, without the uniqueness hypothesis.

The uniqueness condition is not asserted for arbitrary totally real $K$, and no general finite-base or general control theorem is claimed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The totally real cyclotomic tower, stable index, ordinary class groups and unramified tower in the Statement.

[A1] AC, Countable Choice and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The cyclotomic class-field tower has surjective norm projections with stable inertia-trace kernels $Y_n=\omega_nX+\sum_{i=1}^{t-1}\mathbb Z_p\nu_{n,m}g_i$ and $Y_n=\nu_{n,m}Y_m$. [[lem-cyclotomic-class-field-tower-realizes-inertia-trace-kernels]]

[F2] Given a finite base quotient, the trace-kernel formula gives uniform finite bounds on the ordinary coinvariant maps. [[lem-trace-kernels-give-uniform-coinvariant-control-under-finite-base]]

[F3] Finite unramified Galois extensions descend and form the maximal unramified inverse-limit tower, including the totally real condition. [[lem-unramified-finite-galois-extensions-descend-in-number-field-towers]]

[F4] Finite ray-class Artin maps identify class fields and their Galois groups, with local norm compatibility. [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]] [[lem-canonical-local-reciprocity-is-compatible-with-field-norms]]

[F5] Inertia groups in finite Galois towers restrict surjectively and intersect the corresponding fixing subgroups as stated. [[thm-decomposition-and-inertia-in-towers]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** split the unramified tower by a stable inertia group and take its abelianization.

1.1 The ordinary Hilbert class fields and [F3]–[F4] identify $X$ with $\operatorname{Gal}(L_\infty/K_\infty)$, compatibly with norms and restrictions. The maximal field is invariant under $\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K_m)$, so it is Galois over $K_m$. Choose a prime above a stable prime over $p$. At each finite Galois level, inertia restricts surjectively by [F5]; the compatible finite inertia groups have a nonempty inverse limit. Its image in $\Gamma_m=\operatorname{Gal}(K_\infty/K_m)$ is all of $\Gamma_m$ because the prime is totally ramified in $K_\infty/K_m$. Its intersection with $X$ is trivial because $L_\infty/K_\infty$ is unramified. Hence it maps isomorphically to $\Gamma_m$, giving a continuous splitting $G_m=\operatorname{Gal}(L_\infty/K_m)\cong X\rtimes\Gamma_m$. [A1, F3, F4, F5, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Since $X$ and $\Gamma_m$ are abelian, the closed commutator subgroup of $G_m$ is $\omega_mX$. Thus $G_m^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong(X/\omega_mX)\oplus\Gamma_m$. At $n=m$, [F1] gives $Y_m/\omega_mX$ generated by the finitely many images of $g_i$ over $\mathbb Z_p$, and its quotient is the finite group $A_m$. Therefore $X/\omega_mX$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_p$-module. [A1, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $X/\omega_mX$ is finite, every continuous map from $G_m^{\mathrm{ab}}$ to the torsion-free group $\mathbb Z_p$ kills that summand; every surjection factors through $\Gamma_m$ with the same kernel, so its fixed field is $K_\infty$. Conversely, if the finitely generated $\mathbb Z_p$-module $X/\omega_mX$ is infinite, it has a quotient isomorphic to $\mathbb Z_p$. Projection onto that quotient defines a $\mathbb Z_p$-extension inside $L_\infty$ distinct from $K_\infty$. This proves the equivalence in part 1. [A1, step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra]

4.1 Under these equivalent conditions, $X/\omega_mX$ is finite. The subgroup $Y_m$ has finite index and contains $\omega_mX$, while [F1] gives $Y_n=\nu_{n,m}Y_m$. Applying [F2] proves the surjection and uniform kernel bound in part 2. If $t=1$, the sum in [F1] is empty, so $Y_n=\omega_nX$ and the maps are isomorphisms without the uniqueness hypothesis. No assertion of uniqueness when $t>1$ is made. [F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
