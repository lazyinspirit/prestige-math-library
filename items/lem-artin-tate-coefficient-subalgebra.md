## Statement

Let $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$ be commutative rings, each a subring of the next
([[def-subring]]), with $A$ Noetherian. Suppose

* $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$ is of finite type over $A$, with $r\in\mathbb N$ and
  $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in C$ ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]);
* $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in C$ generate $C$ as a $B$-module, with $n\ge1$ and
  $y_1=1_C$.

Choose elements $z_{ij}\in B$ for $1\le i\le r$ and $1\le j\le n$, and
$z_{ijk}\in B$ for $1\le i,j,k\le n$, such that

$$t_i=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{ij}y_j,\qquad y_iy_j=\sum_{k=1}^{n}z_{ijk}y_k .$$

Let $A'$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ generated by all the $z_{ij}$ and all the
$z_{ijk}$. Then $A\subseteq A'\subseteq B$, the algebra $A'$ is of finite type
over $A$, and $A'$ is a Noetherian ring.

Such coefficients exist, because the $y_j$ generate $C$ as a $B$-module and
$t_i$ and $y_iy_j$ are elements of $C$. They are chosen, not canonical: only
their existence and their finite number are used, and a different choice gives
a possibly different $A'$ with the same properties.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Commutative rings $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$, each a subring of the next, with $A$ Noetherian; a finite list $t_1,\ldots,t_r$ with $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$; a list $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ generating $C$ as a $B$-module with $y_1=1_C$; and coefficients $z_{ij},z_{ijk}\in B$ as displayed.

[L1] $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is the image of the unital ring homomorphism $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\to A$ agreeing with the structure map on constants and sending $x_i$ to $a_i$, and it is the smallest subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_n$; an algebra is **of finite type** over $R$ when it equals $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some finite list ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] Every commutative algebra of finite type over a Noetherian commutative ring is a Noetherian ring ([[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]]).

[L3] For a ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_i\in R$ and $s_i\in S$ ([[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

[L4] A subring contains the identity of the ambient ring and shares its zero, identity and additive inverses ([[def-subring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The coefficients exist. Each $t_i$ and each product $y_iy_j$ is an element of $C$, and $C$ is generated as a $B$-module by $y_1,\ldots,y_n$, so by the finite-sum description each of them is a $B$-linear combination of $y_1,\ldots,y_n$; fix one such expression for each, which is a selection over a finite index set. [L3, L4, given]

2.1 The elements $z_{ij}$ and $z_{ijk}$ form a finite list of elements of $B$, indexed by the finitely many pairs $(i,j)$ with $i\le r$, $j\le n$ and the finitely many triples $(i,j,k)$ with $i,j,k\le n$. Let $A'$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ they generate, that is, the smallest subring of $B$ containing $A$ and all of them. Then $A\subseteq A'\subseteq B$, and $A'$ is of finite type over $A$ by definition, being generated as an $A$-algebra by a finite list. [L1, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 The base ring $A$ is Noetherian and $A'$ is a commutative $A$-algebra of finite type, so $A'$ is a Noetherian ring. [L2, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Nothing here uses that $B$ is Noetherian, and nothing may.** Whether $B$ is Noetherian is not among the hypotheses of the Artin–Tate lemma; the point of passing to $A'$ is to obtain a Noetherian ring inside $B$ without assuming one.

- **The normalisation $y_1=1_C$ costs nothing.** A finite $B$-module generating list of $C$ stays finite and generating when $1_C$ is adjoined to it, so it may always be arranged; it is used where the relations are read back, not here.
