## Definition

A set $B\subseteq\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ is **bounded** when
$\sup_{\varphi\in B}p_{\alpha\beta}(\varphi)<\infty$ for every pair of
multi-indices.  The **weak topology** $\sigma(\mathcal S',\mathcal S)$ on
[[def-tempered-distribution|tempered distributions]] is generated by

$$p_\varphi(u)=|\langle u,\varphi\rangle|\qquad(\varphi\in\mathcal S).$$

The **strong topology** $\beta(\mathcal S',\mathcal S)$ is generated by

$$p_B(u)=\sup_{\varphi\in B}|\langle u,\varphi\rangle|$$

as $B$ ranges over bounded subsets of $\mathcal S$.  The empty-set supremum
is zero.  This supremum is finite: continuity of $u$ gives a basic
zero-neighborhood $U$ on which $|u|<1$, and the finitely many seminorm bounds
defining $U$ imply $B\subseteq tU$ for some finite $t>0$; hence
$p_B(u)\leq t$.  The triangle inequality and homogeneity follow pointwise.

Thus a net $(u_i)$ converges weakly to $u$ exactly when
$\langle u_i-u,\varphi\rangle\to0$ for every $\varphi\in\mathcal S$, and it
converges strongly exactly when $p_B(u_i-u)\to0$ for every bounded $B$.
Singletons are bounded, so strong convergence implies weak convergence.  Both
topologies are Hausdorff, since distinct functionals differ on some test.  No
identification with either topology on $\mathcal D'$ is asserted, and no
choice axiom is used.
