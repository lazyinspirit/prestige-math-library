## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be any perfect field of characteristic $p>0$, let
$E/k$ be finitely generated of positive transcendence degree, and let
$D/k$ be a smooth proper geometrically integral curve of genus at least
two. Then for each integer $e\ge0$ the set
$$D(E)\setminus D(E^{p^e})$$
is finite. Here $k\subseteq E^{p^e}$ because $k$ is perfect, so these
point sets are defined using the same constant curve. Neither $k$ nor the
full point set $D(E)$ is assumed finite. For $e=0$ the difference is empty.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the perfect constants, field extension, constant curve
and integer of the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Over algebraically closed constants, a constant smooth proper
geometrically integral high-genus curve has only finitely many points
outside the $p$th-power subfield's point set.
[[lem-constant-high-genus-curve-has-finitely-many-nonhorizontal-points]]

[F2] Under AC fields have algebraic closures; algebraic elements form
a subfield and algebraicity is transitive. Transcendence degree is additive
in finite-transcendence-degree towers. A perfect characteristic-$p$ field
is its own $p$th-power field.
[[thm-existence-of-algebraic-closures]]
[[thm-algebraic-elements-form-a-subfield]] [[thm-transitivity-of-algebraicity]]
[[cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity]]
[[thm-perfect-field-characterizations]]

[F3] Algebraic extensions of a perfect field are separable. An
algebraic extension generated by separable elements is separable, and an
extension both separable and purely inseparable is trivial.
[[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]]
[[thm-extension-generated-by-separable-elements-is-separable]]
[[cor-separable-and-purely-inseparable-extension-is-trivial]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited from [F1] and supplies an algebraic closure. The
iteration for the fixed integer $e$ has only finitely many steps.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; descend the algebraically-closed-constant
finite exceptional set through a separable constant extension.

1.1 Inside an algebraic closure of $E$, take the subfield $\overline k$ of elements algebraic over $k$. By [F2] it is an algebraic closure of $k$, and $\overline E=E\overline k$ is finitely generated over $\overline k$ of the same positive transcendence degree. It is separable algebraic over $E$. Indeed every element of $\overline k$ is separable over perfect $k$ by [F3]; its minimal polynomial over $E$ divides a separable polynomial over $k$, and therefore is separable. The generated extension is then separable by [F3]. Consequently $E\cap\overline E^p=E^p$. For if $a=b^p\in E$ with $b\in\overline E$, the extension $E(b)/E$ is purely inseparable and also separable as a subextension of $\overline E/E$. By [F3] it is trivial, so $b\in E$ and $a\in E^p$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. [F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F1] to $D_{\overline k}$ over $\overline E/\overline k$. The set $D(\overline E)\setminus D(\overline E^p)$ is finite. Moreover $D(E)\cap D(\overline E^p)=D(E^p)$ inside $D(\overline E)$. Choose an affine $k$-chart of $D$ containing the image of a common point. Its finitely many algebra generators have values in both $E$ and $\overline E^p$, hence in $E^p$ by step 1.1. These values satisfy the same defining equations over $k\subseteq E^p$, so give an $E^p$-point. Conversely every $E^p$-point lies in both sets. Thus $D(E)\setminus D(E^p)$ injects into the finite exceptional set just obtained. [F1, F2, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 For $j\ge0$, the field $E^{p^j}$ contains $k$ and is finitely generated over it of the same positive transcendence degree: take the $p^j$th powers of any finite field generating list, using $k^{p^j}=k$ from [F2]. Apply step 2.1 to each of the finitely many fields $E,E^p,\ldots,E^{p^{e-1}}$. The union of their finite point differences is $D(E)\setminus D(E^{p^e})$, so is finite. For $e=0$ this is the empty union. This proves the Statement while retaining the possible infinitude of every power-field point set. [F2, given, step 2.1] ∎
