## Statement

Let $A\xrightarrow{\varphi}B$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings. Let $F$ be
the free $B$-module on the set underlying $B$, with basis written $[b]$ for
$b\in B$, let $R\subseteq F$ be the $B$-submodule generated by all elements

$$[b+b']-[b]-[b'],\qquad [bb']-b\,[b']-b'\,[b],\qquad [\varphi(a)]$$

for $b,b'\in B$ and $a\in A$, and put $\Omega_{B/A}:=F/R$ with
$\mathrm{d}\colon B\to\Omega_{B/A}$, $\mathrm{d}b:=[b]+R$. Then
$(\Omega_{B/A},\mathrm{d})$ is a Kähler differential module for $A\to B$ in the
sense of [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]]: for every $B$-module $M$ the
assignment $g\mapsto g\circ\mathrm{d}$ is a bijection
$\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\to\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$, natural in
$M$. In particular a Kähler differential module exists for every ring
homomorphism $A\to B$, with no finiteness hypothesis on $B$ over $A$, and
$\Omega_{B/A}$ is generated as a $B$-module by the classes $\mathrm{d}b$ of the
elements of $B$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A homomorphism $A\xrightarrow{\varphi}B$ of commutative rings, the free $B$-module $F$ on the set underlying $B$, the submodule $R\subseteq F$ of the three relator families, and the pair $(\Omega_{B/A},\mathrm{d})$ with $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ and $\mathrm{d}b=[b]+R$.

[F1] [[def-ag-universal-algebraic-differentials]]: the module of algebraic differentials is $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ for the free $B$-module $F$ on the set underlying $B$ with basis symbol $[b]$, modulo the submodule generated by $[b+b']-[b]-[b']$, $[bb']-b\,[b']-b'\,[b]$ and $[\varphi(a)]$, and $\mathrm{d}b=[b]+R$ is its universal $A$-derivation.

[F2] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: an $A$-derivation of $B$ into a $B$-module $M$ is an additive, $A$-constant map satisfying $D(bb')=b\,D(b')+b'\,D(b)$, and $\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$ is the $B$-module of all such maps.

[F3] [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]]: $(\Omega_{B/A},\mathrm{d})$ is a Kähler differential module for $A\to B$ when $g\mapsto g\circ\mathrm{d}$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\to\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$ for every $B$-module $M$, naturally in $M$.

## Proof

1.1 The pair of [F1] is a $B$-module with an $A$-derivation. The free module $F$ is a $B$-module and $R$ is a $B$-submodule by construction, so $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ is a $B$-module and $\mathrm{d}$ is a map $B\to\Omega_{B/A}$. Each of the three relator families lies in $R$, hence vanishes in the quotient: $[b+b']-[b]-[b']\in R$ gives $\mathrm{d}(b+b')=\mathrm{d}b+\mathrm{d}b'$, the element $[bb']-b\,[b']-b'\,[b]\in R$ gives $\mathrm{d}(bb')=b\,\mathrm{d}b'+b'\,\mathrm{d}b$, and $[\varphi(a)]\in R$ gives $\mathrm{d}(\varphi(a))=0$. So $\mathrm{d}$ is additive, $A$-constant and satisfies Leibniz, that is, $\mathrm{d}\in\operatorname{Der}_A(B,\Omega_{B/A})$ by [F2]. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Every derivation descends to a map out of $\Omega_{B/A}$. Let $M$ be a $B$-module and $D\in\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$. Since $F$ is free with basis $\{[b]:b\in B\}$, there is a unique $B$-linear map $G\colon F\to M$ with $G([b])=D(b)$ for all $b\in B$. By [F2] the map $D$ is additive, $A$-constant and satisfies Leibniz, so $G$ kills each relator: $G([b+b']-[b]-[b'])=D(b+b')-D(b)-D(b')=0$, similarly $G([bb']-b\,[b']-b'\,[b])=D(bb')-b\,D(b')-b'\,D(b)=0$, and $G([\varphi(a)])=D(\varphi(a))=0$; here we used that $G$ is $B$-linear, so that $G(b\,[b'])=b\,G([b'])=b\,D(b')$. The three families generate $R$ as a $B$-submodule and [F1] presents $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$, so $G$ factors through a $B$-linear $\bar G\colon\Omega_{B/A}\to M$ with $\bar G([b]+R)=D(b)$, that is, $\bar G\circ\mathrm{d}=D$. [step 1.1, F1, F2, algebra]

3.1 The construction of step 2.1 is the unique inverse. Let $h\colon\Omega_{B/A}\to M$ be $B$-linear with $h\circ\mathrm{d}=D$ for some $D\in\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$. Evaluating on $\mathrm{d}b$ gives $h(\mathrm{d}b)=h([b]+R)=D(b)=\bar G(\mathrm{d}b)$ for every $b\in B$, where $\bar G$ is the map produced in step 2.1. The classes $[b]+R=\mathrm{d}b$ range over the images of a basis of the free module $F$, so they generate $\Omega_{B/A}=F/R$ as a $B$-module, and two $B$-linear maps agreeing on a generating set are equal; hence $h=\bar G$. Therefore $g\mapsto g\circ\mathrm{d}$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)\to\operatorname{Der}_A(B,M)$ for every $B$-module $M$. [step 2.1, F1, algebra]

4.1 Naturality in $M$. Let $t\colon M\to N$ be $B$-linear and let $h\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{B/A},M)$. Then $t\circ(h\circ\mathrm{d})=(t\circ h)\circ\mathrm{d}$ as maps $B\to N$, since both sides send $b$ to $t(h(\mathrm{d}b))$, and $t\circ h$ is again $B$-linear, so the assignment of step 3.1 carries $h$ followed by $t$ to the derivation $t\circ(h\circ\mathrm{d})$; this is exactly the commutativity required of the bijections in [F3]. [step 3.1, F3, algebra]

5.1 Conclusion. Steps 1.1, 2.1 and 3.1 verify both clauses of [F3] for the pair $(\Omega_{B/A},\mathrm{d})$ of [F1], and step 4.1 verifies naturality, so that pair is a Kähler differential module for $A\to B$. The construction used only the free module on the set $B$ and the submodule generated by the three relator families, so it exists for every ring homomorphism $A\to B$ with no finiteness hypothesis, and step 3.1 exhibits the classes $\mathrm{d}b$ as a generating set of $\Omega_{B/A}$ over $B$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, F1, F3] ∎
