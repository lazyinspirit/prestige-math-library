## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $X=\varprojlim_iX_i$ be a cofiltered inverse limit of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes with affine transition morphisms. Fix a stage $i_0$ and an abelian sheaf $F_{i_0}$ on its small étale site; write $F_i$ and $F$ for its inverse images. For every integer $q\geq0$, the canonical map
$$\varinjlim_{i\geq i_0}H^q_{\mathrm{et}}(X_i,F_i)\longrightarrow H^q_{\mathrm{et}}(X,F)$$
is an isomorphism. A section, cohomology class, or equality/zero of fixed classes has a finite-stage witness. For a fixed finite commutative coefficient ring $\Lambda$, every constructible $\Lambda$-module sheaf on $X$ and every morphism between such objects descends to a stage; two stage morphisms that become equal are equal at a later stage. Here constructible means a finite locally closed constructible stratification with finite locally constant restrictions; over Noetherian schemes this is the finite-coefficient constructible-sheaf convention.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct finite-presentation, finite-cover and finite-cochain witnesses under the explicit AC hypothesis.

1.1 There is a more direct finite-witness route than the AC-qualified standard-étale theorem. Use the actual public output and proof presentations of the theorem in [F1], already proved separately. At each quasi-finite point of an fp étale U→Spec(A), that theorem provides an étale neighbourhood V→Spec(A), with a specified point above the image, and a finite open W⊂U×A V containing the chosen lift. Its V is explicitly a composite of principal base shrinks and TWO universal monic-factorization charts localized at their resultants. The chart algebra before localizing at the resultant is finite free after its ordered-root presentation, and the proof supplies its flatness and étale fibres without invoking the general standard-étale criterion. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Because U→Spec(A) is already étale, W→V is finite étale: it is an open restriction of its base change and it is finite by construction. W→U is étale as an open restriction of U×A V→U. The family of ALL such W→U covers U. If U is qc, retain finitely many W_j. This uses all finite witnesses and then quasi-compactness, not a choice of a neighbourhood for every point. The finite fibre/finite residue-extension condition needed at a point follows from the zero-dimensional regular finite-type field fibre in the étale definition; the finite-dimensional normalization argument is already part of the installed localization proof. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 Now let A be a filtered colimit of A_i and let U=Spec(B) be fp étale over A. Descend a finite presentation of B to B_i and every one of the finitely many V_j presentations to A_i. Those V_j,i are STILL étale: they have the identical two universal factor/resultant presentations, and the already-proved elementary flatness/fibre calculation applies over every A_i. No flatness-descent theorem has been invoked here. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Each finite étale W_j over affine V_j has a finite projective algebra presentation. Choose an idempotent matrix e_j with its image the underlying module, multiplication and unit maps, their finitely many algebra identities, and the perfect trace pairing together with its inverse. These are finite matrices and finitely many equations, and therefore descend to some common i. The descended algebra is finite projective and its trace pairing remains perfect. For clarity, the finite-algebra trace test used here is algebraic: over a field, after a finite splitting extension the trace pairing of a finite commutative algebra is nondegenerate exactly when that algebra is a product of separable fields. Any nonzero nilpotent ideal is orthogonal to the whole algebra since multiplication by a nilpotent element has trace zero; an inseparable field factor has identically degenerate field trace. Conversely a finite separable field factor has its usual nondegenerate trace pairing, checked by the Vandermonde matrix of its finitely many distinct embeddings in a finite splitting field. Thus the descended W_j,i→V_j,i has geometrically regular zero-dimensional fibres and is finite étale. Only finitely many polynomial roots in finite extensions are used in this calculation. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 The open immersion W_j⊂U×A V_j and its presentation descend as well. To see what must be descended, cover that qc open by finitely many principal opens of U×A V_j. Their algebra maps, the finite transition maps, and the inverse maps expressing their gluing with W_j involve finite presentations and finitely many equations. Descend those equations; the descended principal opens consequently glue to the descended W_j,i as an actual open subscheme of U_i×Ai V_j,i. This is descent of a specified open immersion by principal localizations, not a claim that an arbitrary property descends automatically. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 Under the public AC hypothesis, [F4] turns a covering finite principal family into an actual unit-ideal equation, which descends to a finite stage. The images of the finite étale maps W_j,i→U_i are qc opens, so their covering condition descends by that finite equation. The disjoint union is flat and surjective on spectra, hence faithfully flat by [F5] at its stated AC scope. Each W_j,i is flat over A_i by composition of finite étale W_j,i→V_j,i and étale V_j,i→Spec(A_i). Flatness of U_i over A_i now follows by faithfully flat descent: tensor an injection of A_i-modules with O(U_i), and test its resulting kernel after the faithfully flat cover algebra. That kernel vanishes after the test because the cover algebra is A_i-flat, hence vanishes before the cover. No claim that flatness itself is a finite list of equations is used. [A1, F1, F3, F4, F5, step 5.1, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally U_i is finitely presented and its relative differentials vanish after increasing i, since their finite generating differentials and the equations killing them descend from B. Alternatively, the just-constructed étale cover W_j,i of U_i has étale structure maps to Spec(A_i), so étaleness is local on the source for the étale topology; the same fibre calculation and flat descent prove the definition directly. Either way U_i→Spec(A_i) is étale at the original unqualified scope. [F1, F2, F3, step 6.1, given, construct, algebra]

8.1 For a sheaf pulled back from a stage, its value on a descended qc étale object is the filtered colimit of its values on later stages. To check this directly, form the presheaf on descended affine objects whose value is this colimit. Its restriction/gluing equalizer for any finite covering is the filtered colimit of the stage equalizers: filtered colimits of sets commute with finite products and equalizers. It is therefore already a sheaf on the coherent basis, and its universal property is the inverse-image sheaf's universal property. Every étale covering of a qc basis object has a finite subcover; hence this basis sheaf is a sheaf for all coverings. A particular section and an equality of two particular sections therefore have finite-stage witnesses. [F1, F2, F3, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 A finite locally constant sheaf on a qc affine scheme has a finite affine étale covering on which it is represented by a finite disjoint union of copies of the base. Indeed take the family of all trivializing affine objects; their open images cover, and quasi-compactness gives a finite subcover. Their disjoint union Spec(B)→Spec(A) is faithfully flat and finitely presented. The local representing algebras B^E have the permutation descent isomorphisms induced by the sheaf's transition bijections; after allowing finitely many trivializing pieces this is a finite projective B-algebra D with a cocycle descent datum. Define C as the equalizer of its two descent maps to D⊗_A B. [F1, F2, F3, step 8.1, given, construct, algebra]

10.1 The map B⊗_A C→D is an isomorphism. To prove it, tensor the defining equalizer with B, which is flat. Over B⊗_A B the cocycle identifies the two maps with the first two maps of the augmented Amitsur complex of the descended module. That augmented complex is split after adjoining one more B factor: multiplication on the first two B factors is the extra-degeneracy contraction. Hence the base-changed map is an isomorphism; faithful flatness reflects kernel and cokernel zero, proving the original map is an isomorphism. This simultaneously proves uniqueness of C and descent of algebra maps, since equalizer elements and the contraction commute with multiplication and units. [F1, F2, F3, step 9.1, given, construct, algebra]

11.1 Finiteness also descends with finite witnesses. Choose finitely many B-module generators of D. Under D=B⊗C each is a finite sum of pure tensors. The finitely many resulting C-elements generate C because their quotient becomes zero after tensoring with B. After taking A^r→C, flatness of B identifies its relation module after tensor with the finite relation module of D; use the same finite pure-tensor argument to descend finite generation of that relation module. Thus C is finitely presented as an A-module. Flatness descends by testing injectivity after the faithfully flat tensor functor, so C is finite locally free. The relative differentials vanish after tensoring with B, where D is finite étale, and hence vanish before tensoring. Every field fibre is étale: after the faithfully flat base change its finite algebra is a product of finite separable field extensions, and faithful-flat descent of a nilpotent ideal and separability after a field extension gives the same property before base change. Consequently Spec(C) is finite étale. The identical equalizer construction glues these representing schemes on a finite affine cover of a qcqs scheme. This is finite descent of a finite étale algebra, not a use of the AC-qualified pi1 equivalence. [F1, F2, F3, step 10.1, given, construct, algebra]

12.1 Now order a finite constructible stratification so a closed stratum Z is removed first. Inductively obtain finitely many generators on its open complement. On Z the finite locally constant underlying sheaf is represented by a finite étale V→Z, by the preceding construction. The inverse-image sheaf definition supplies a covering of V by étale-local restrictions of actual sections of F on ambient étale objects. Every such representative determines a map into V, and these maps have open images. Their all-witness family covers V; ordinary quasi-compactness of V supplies finitely many of them. Together with the generators on the open complement, these give an epimorphism from a finite coproduct of qc étale representables to the underlying sheaf F. Apply the same construction to the relation sheaf F0×_F F0, which is finite constructible after intersecting its finitely many locally constant strata. This gives a finite coequalizer presentation. [F1, F2, F3, step 11.1, given, construct, algebra]

13.1 For finite-ring module sheaves, retain the finite underlying-set presentation and include its addition, scalar multiplications and zero maps. There are only finitely many scalar multiplication maps. Products of finite presentations are finite presentations on the coherent basis: take pairwise fibre products of the finitely many generating objects and their finite relation diagrams. Each structural map is determined by finitely many section images satisfying finitely many relations. The unit, addition, scalar and module identities are equalities of these maps; the finite section argument descends every map and equality to a common stage. Hence, using the finite coequalizer construction proved above on the coherent basis, both the coefficient object and all its module structure descend, and maps between such objects descend by the same finite generator/relation calculation. [F1, F2, F3, step 12.1, given, construct, algebra]

14.1 The apparent arbitrary-quotient lifting bridge in the closed-stratum paragraph can be resolved directly from the Kan-extension/sheafification formula. Let t be the tautological section of F|Z over its representing finite étale V. There is a covering V_a→V and, for each a, an ambient fp étale U_a→X, an actual s_a∈F(U_a), and a map V_a→U_a×X Z such that the pullback of s_a is t|V_a. Pulling s_a itself to U_a×X Z gives a section of F|Z and therefore a map U_a×X Z→V. Its composite with V_a→U_a×X Z is V_a→V. The images of these maps consequently cover V. The map U_a×X Z→V is étale because it is a morphism between étale Z-schemes; hence its image is open. Ordinary quasi-compactness of V extracts finitely many a. This is exactly the finite set of ambient sections needed above. No lifting of an arbitrary étale Z-algebra to X was required. The category of all such representatives is used, so no family of representatives indexed by arbitrary points is chosen. [F1, F2, F3, step 13.1, given, construct, algebra]

15.1 For completeness, the Noetherian generic-chart argument in the previous paragraph can be made explicit. For an integral affine Noetherian base Spec(A), with fraction field K, let U→Spec(A) be qc fp étale. Cover U by finitely many affine charts. Each generic chart algebra is a finite product of finite separable extensions of K. For its finitely many algebra generators, take monic equations over K and clear their finitely many denominators, as well as the finitely many annihilators of the resulting equation errors. After inverting one nonzero a∈A, each chart is finite over A_a and is still étale, hence finite étale. The finitely many overlaps are qc open subschemes of these finite charts. Their generic fibres are unions of components. Descend the corresponding finitely many generic idempotents by clearing denominators in the finite chart algebra. The complements of the desired overlaps in these selected components are closed, with proper image in Spec(A_a) and empty generic fibre. Their finite union is removed by inverting another nonzero element. The overlaps are then clopen unions of components. Gluing finitely many finite étale schemes along such clopen component identifications is finite étale: the coordinate algebra is obtained by retaining one copy of each of the finitely many identified components, and this assertion is checked after their common finite étale splitting cover, with finite faithfully-flat descent by the finite descent calculation. [F1, F2, F3, step 14.1, given, construct, algebra]

16.1 Thus U is finite étale over some dense open of each reduced irreducible component of X. Remove the pairwise intersections of the finitely many components, apply this argument on each component, and continue on the closed complement by Noetherian induction. This gives a finite locally-closed reduced stratification on which h_U is finite locally constant. No assertion that the original X is reduced is required: the strata in the constructible definition have reduced induced structure and exhaust the underlying set. For a finite collection of qc étale U, their products, and their morphisms, intersect the finitely many resulting stratifications and trivializing covers. Finite limits, images and equivalence-relation quotients of finite locally constant sets remain finite locally constant on that common cover; their finite transition maps descend. This proves the finite constructibility claims needed in §5 at the Noetherian stage. [F1, F2, F3, step 15.1, given, construct, algebra]

17.1 The finite presentation and morphism descent step is now formal. For F=coeq(F1⇉F0), with F0,F1 finite coproducts of h_U, maps into a pulled-back sheaf G are the finite equalizer of the corresponding G(U) values. By the finite section argument this equalizer commutes with the filtered limit of stages. Descend the finitely many U and the two arrows, form their sheaf coequalizer at that stage, and use the preceding constructibility calculation to see that it is constructible. For a finite coefficient ring Λ, descend the finitely many addition, zero and scalar maps as well; all module identities are finitely many equations of these maps. This proves finite-coefficient object and morphism descent once the geometric fp-étale descent at the start of the finite section argument is supplied. [F1, F2, F3, step 16.1, given, construct, algebra]

18.1 To justify the actual cohomology models under AC, abelian sheaves on a fixed small site form a locally small Grothendieck abelian category: limits are computed as for presheaves, colimits are sheafified presheaf colimits, and exact sheafification makes filtered colimits exact. The direct sum of the free abelian sheaves on all representable site objects is a generator: a nonzero section on one object induces a nonzero map from its free representable summand. The same argument works for sheaves of modules over a fixed coefficient ring, using free coefficient-module sheaves. Thus [F6] supplies a functorial injective embedding. Iterate it on the cokernel using ordinary recursion to obtain an actual injective resolution, with no new hypothesis beyond [A1]. The slice/restriction calculations are [F2]. [A1, F2, F6, step 17.1, construct, algebra]

18.2 Here is the precise cohomology model used by the finite-witness argument. Use étale hypercovers whose levels through q+1 are finite coproducts of affine fp étale objects. All matching objects through that degree are formed by finite limits and covered by finitely many affine fp étale objects. The alternating-face complex of F-sections through q+1 is enough to specify a degree-q cocycle and whether a given degree-(q-1) cochain bounds it. [F1, F2, F3, step 17.1, given, construct, algebra]

19.1 The comparison with actual sheaf cohomology in degree q can be proved in finite degrees, rather than assumed as a whole infinite-hypercover theorem. Resolve F by an actual injective complex I•. The augmented free-abelian sheaf chain complex of a hypercover is exact: after a covering where a matching tuple has a lift, adjoining that lift gives the usual extra-degeneracy contraction in that degree. Exactness is local, so these contractions prove exactness of the sheaf chain complex through the required degree. Applying Hom(-,I^r) preserves its exactness since I^r is injective. The resulting augmented double complex therefore computes H^q(X,F) after totalization; in total degrees through q only finitely many columns and rows occur. [F1, F2, F3, step 18.2, given, construct, algebra]

20.1 For surjectivity of the section-level hypercover comparison in degree q, start with a representative in I^q(X). Exactness of F→I• permits its successive local primitives on covering objects. Build a covering of the relevant matching objects at each of the finitely many degrees, and retain finite affine subcovers. Each obstruction is killed locally because the resolution is exact as a complex of sheaves. The resulting total cocycle is represented in degree q by F-sections on a finite truncated hypercover. For injectivity, apply the identical construction to a total bounding cochain in one lower total degree, after taking a common refinement of the two finite diagrams. The degree-q class is zero precisely when it is a coboundary after such a finite refinement. At no stage is there an infinitely indexed choice of local primitives: use the family of all pairs (cover object, primitive), then extract a finite subcover of each of the finitely many qc matching objects. All remaining selections are finite. [F1, F2, F3, step 19.1, given, construct, algebra]

21.1 This is a finite-diagram proof of the needed two directions, not a claim that Čech cohomology for a single cover computes all sheaf cohomology. Matching refinements, including the q+1 matching degree needed for the cocycle equation, are essential. [F1, F2, F3, step 20.1, given, construct, algebra]

22.1 A degree-q class on X is represented by one of the finite diagrams just described. The objects, matching maps, covers, finitely many F-sections and their cocycle equalities descend to a common stage by the finite section calculation. They give a class at that stage whose pullback is the original class. This proves surjectivity of colim_i H^q(X_i,F_i)→H^q(X,F). [F1, F2, F3, step 21.1, given, construct, algebra]

23.1 If two stage classes become equal, their difference is zero. By the finite hypercover argument that equality is witnessed by one finite hypercover refinement and a finite bounding cochain. Descend this finite refinement, its sections and its coboundary equations by the finite section calculation. The two classes are already equal at some later stage. This proves injectivity and naturality of the canonical cohomology map. Degree zero is exactly the finite section argument, and the same proof works after restricting to any qcqs object pulled back from a stage. [F1, F2, F3, step 22.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
