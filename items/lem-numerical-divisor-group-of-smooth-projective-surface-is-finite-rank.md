## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and let $X$ be an integral smooth projective $k$-surface. The numerical divisor group
$$N^1(X)=\operatorname{Pic}(X)/\{L:L\cdot M=0\text{ for every }M\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)\}$$
is a finitely generated free abelian group. This concerns numerical equivalence; it does not assert that the kernel from algebraic-equivalence classes to numerical classes is finite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field and smooth projective integral surface in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The surface intersection pairing descends to an integral symmetric bilinear pairing on $N^1(X)$, and this group is torsion-free. [[def-numerical-equivalence-and-neron-severi-space]]

[F2] For an ample class $H$ and any line bundle $L$ with $L\cdot H=0$, one has $L^2\leq0$, with equality exactly for numerically trivial $L$. [[thm-hodge-index-theorem-ample-case]]

[F3] Higher direct images of finite constructible torsion sheaves under proper maps are finite constructible. [[lem-proper-torsion-constructibility-under-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** combine finite reduction modulo a prime with an integral positive-definite descent.

1.1 Choose a prime $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$. The étale Kummer sequence injects $\operatorname{Pic}(X)/\ell\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ into $H^2(X,\mu_\ell)$. The latter group is finite by [F3] applied to the proper map $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$: $\mu_\ell$ is finite locally constant and a constructible sheaf on the algebraically closed point has finite stalk. Since $N^1(X)$ is a quotient of $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$, the group $N^1(X)/\ell N^1(X)$ is finite. [A1, F1, F3, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Choose an ample line bundle $H$ and put $N=N^1(X)$. The map $\varphi:N\to\mathbb Z$, $x\mapsto x\cdot H$, has nonzero image since $H^2>0$. Its image is a subgroup of $\mathbb Z$, hence free; choosing a lift of its positive generator splits $N\cong K\oplus\mathbb Z$, where $K=\ker\varphi$. In particular $K/\ell K$ is finite by step 1.1. On $K$, set $b(x,y)=-x\cdot y$ and $q(x)=b(x,x)$. By [F1]–[F2], $b$ is integral symmetric bilinear and $q(x)>0$ for every nonzero $x\in K$. Applying positivity to $ax+by$ for integers $a,b$ gives Cauchy–Schwarz, so $\|x\|=\sqrt{q(x)}$ satisfies the triangle inequality. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 We show that every bounded $q$-ball in $K$ is finite. The finite group $K/\ell K$ implies by successive quotients that $K/\ell^mK$ is finite for every $m\geq1$. Fix $R\geq0$ and choose $m$ with $\ell^m>2R$. If two elements of norm at most $R$ have the same class modulo $\ell^mK$, their difference is $\ell^m z$; if $z\ne0$, integrality and positivity give $\|\ell^m z\|\geq\ell^m$, contradicting the triangle inequality. Thus the ball injects into the finite group $K/\ell^mK$. [A1, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 Choose a finite set $E$ of representatives of $K/\ell K$ and set $B=\max_{e\in E}\|e\|$. For $x\in K$ choose $e\in E$ with $x-e=\ell y$. Then $\|y\|\leq(\|x\|+B)/\ell$. If $\|x\|>B/(\ell-1)$, this strictly decreases the nonnegative integer $q(x)$. Repeating reaches the finite ball of that radius, by step 3.1; back-substitution writes $x$ in the subgroup generated by that ball and $E$. Hence $K$, and therefore $N\cong K\oplus\mathbb Z$, is finitely generated. It is free because it is torsion-free by [F1]. [A1, F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, choose, construct, algebra] ∎
