## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field with finite residue field and let $L/F$ be a finite abelian extension. For every integer $n\ge2$, the canonical Milnor transfer
$$N_{L/F}:K_n^M(L)\longrightarrow K_n^M(F)$$
is surjective. No surjectivity of $N_{L/F}:L^\times\to F^\times$ is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the field, extension, degree and choice assumptions in the Statement.

[F1] Canonical Milnor transfer is transitive, satisfies $N\operatorname{res}=[L:F]$, has determinant field norm in degree one, and satisfies the projection formula for the graded Milnor product. [[thm-bass-tate-kato-norm-transfer-for-milnor-k-groups]]

[F2] For $G=K_2^M(F)$, the Moore map has finite cyclic quotient $\mu(F)$ and divisible kernel, and $\mu(F)$ is finite cyclic. [[thm-local-field-k2-moore-sequence-and-divisible-kernel]] [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F3] When $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}F$ and $\mu_\ell\subset F$, the Hilbert pairing using the distinguished canonical Artin map has trivial left kernel on $F^\times/F^{\times\ell}$. Its Steinberg map is $H_\ell:K_2^M(F)\to\mu_\ell$. [[thm-local-hilbert-symbol-nondegenerate-pairing]] [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F4] For the same distinguished canonical Artin map and every finite abelian extension, the field-norm subgroup has index $[L:F]$. This is the full finite norm-kernel interface, not only unramified normalization. [[thm-local-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]]

[F5] Under AC, every finite extension of a complete discrete valuation field is complete discretely valued, with finite residue extension. In particular every intermediate finite extension of $F$ is again a finite-residue local field. [[lem-complete-discrete-valuation-extension-degree-and-tame-norm]] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]

[F6] A finite abelian group is a product of cyclic groups. Finite Galois theory identifies its subgroup chains with intermediate towers. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-abelian-groups-invariant-factor-form]] [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]

[F7] Milnor groups are generated by multilinear symbols, and multiplication concatenates entries. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

[A1] AC and DC are literal assumptions for [F1]–[F5]; all chosen roots, extensions and canonical Artin maps are taken in that branch. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 A finite abelian group has a chain with prime cyclic factors: decompose it by [F6], factor the orders of its cyclic factors, and refine the cyclic-factor chain one prime at a time. All subgroups are normal. Galois correspondence gives a tower from $F$ to $L$ with cyclic prime-degree steps, and all fields in it are local by [F5]. The identity extension has identity transfer. By transitivity, surjectivity for every prime step implies the Statement. Fix such a step of degree $\ell$, and put $G=K_2^M(F)$, $m=|\mu(F)|$. [A1, F1, F5, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $D$ be the divisible kernel in [F2]. Since $D=\ell D\subseteq\ell G$, the quotient $G/\ell G$ identifies with $\mu(F)/\mu(F)^\ell$: indeed the inverse image of $\mu(F)^\ell$ is $\ell G+D=\ell G$. It is cyclic of order $\gcd(\ell,m)$. Let $I$ be the norm image in $G$. By [F1], $\ell G\subseteq I$. If $\ell\nmid m$, this gives $I=G$. No torsion-freeness of $D$ is used. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $\ell\mid m$. Let $\zeta\in F$ have maximal $\ell$-power order $\ell^s$, with $s\ge1$. If $\zeta^i=c^\ell$ for $0<i<\ell$, then $c$ is a root of unity of order $\ell^{s+1}$, a contradiction. Roots of unity are units because their valuations are torsion in $\mathbb Z$. If $\pi$ is a uniformizer, the $\ell^2$ classes $\pi^j\zeta^i$ for $0\le i,j<\ell$ are distinct modulo $F^{\times\ell}$: equality first forces equal $j$ by valuation, then equal $i$ by the preceding order argument. Thus $|F^\times/F^{\times\ell}|\ge\ell^2$. This includes $\ell=2$. [F2, F5, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 Let $H=N_{L/F}L^\times$. The determinant norm of a scalar $c\in F^\times$ is $c^\ell$, so $F^{\times\ell}\subseteq H$. By [F4], $[F^\times:H]=\ell$. Step 3.1 therefore implies $H\ne F^{\times\ell}$. Choose $a\in H\setminus F^{\times\ell}$ and $c\in L^\times$ with $N_{L/F}c=a$. As $\mu_\ell\subset F$ is nontrivial, $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}F$. By [F3] choose $b\in F^\times$ with $H_\ell\{a,b\}\ne1$. This Hilbert map kills $\ell G$, so $\{a,b\}$ has nonzero image in the cyclic group $G/\ell G$ of order $\ell$; hence its image generates that group. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 Projection gives $\{a,b\}=N_{L/F}\{c,b\}$, where $b$ in the right-hand symbol denotes its restriction to $L$. Thus $I$ contains a generator modulo $\ell G$ and contains $\ell G$, so $I=G$. This completes the degree-two prime-step proof. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 For $n>2$ and a target symbol $\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}$, choose $y\in K_2^M(L)$ with $Ny=\{a_1,a_2\}$. Projection gives $$N\bigl(y\cdot\operatorname{res}\{a_3,\ldots,a_n\}\bigr)=\{a_1,\ldots,a_n\}.$$ Finite sums of symbols generate, so the norm is onto in degree $n$ as well. In degree $n=2$ the empty remaining product is the unit of $K_0^M=\mathbb Z$. Combine the prime steps by step 1.1. The argument uses neither the cyclic $K_2$ norm-kernel identity nor a classification of $K_2$ torsion. [A1, F1, F7, step 1.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
