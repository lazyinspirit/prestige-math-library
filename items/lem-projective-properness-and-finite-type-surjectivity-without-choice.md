## Statement

Without a choice principle, projective space over any base scheme is proper. A closed immersion is proper, and proper morphisms remain proper under base change and composition. If $X\to S$ is proper and $Y\to S$ is separated, every $S$-morphism $X\to Y$ is proper. A surjective finite-type morphism of schemes remains surjective after every base change. In particular a projective proper surjection from a scheme proper over its base proves that its separated finite-type target is proper over that base.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The schemes and morphisms in the respective clauses, and the displayed finiteness and separation hypotheses.

[F1] Projective space is covered by finitely many standard affine polynomial charts; a projective closed subset is given by the vanishing of a homogeneous ideal. [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]]

[F2] Proper means separated, finite type and universally closed. [[def-proper-morphism]]

[L1] A nonzero finite-type algebra over a specified field has a residue field finite over it. Steps 1.1–1.3 prove this for one algebra, without an arbitrary maximal-ideal selection principle.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite polynomial normalization and finite projective equation witnesses.

1.1 Let $D=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]/I$ be a nonzero finite-type algebra over a field. Induct on $n$. If $I=0$, evaluating all generators at zero gives a $K$-point. Otherwise choose one nonzero $f\in I$. A triangular substitution $x_n=y_n$, $x_i=y_i+y_n^{M^i}$ for sufficiently large $M$ gives the distinct monomials of $f$ distinct top $y_n$-degrees, by their base-$M$ exponent digits. Divide by the nonzero leading coefficient. Thus $y_n$ satisfies a monic equation over the subalgebra $E$ generated by the other $y_i$, and $D$ is a finite $E$-module. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 By finite induction $E$ has a maximal ideal $\mathfrak a$ with finite residue field extension $K'/K$. The finite $E$-module $D$ has $D/\mathfrak aD\ne0$: otherwise localize at the specified $\mathfrak a$ and apply determinant Nakayama to the finite module $D_{\mathfrak a}$; some $s\notin\mathfrak a$ would annihilate $1$, contradicting $E\subseteq D$. A proper ideal of maximal vector-space dimension in the nonzero finite-dimensional $K'$-algebra $D/\mathfrak aD$ is maximal. Its residue field is finite over $K'$, proving [L1]. Only finitely many selections were made for this one algebra. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $Z\subseteq\mathbb P^n_R$ be closed and fix a prime $\mathfrak p\subset R$ whose fibre is empty. For every standard chart, the fibre coordinate algebra is finite type over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ and has no prime. By [L1] it is zero. If $J$ is a homogeneous ideal defining $Z$, this gives $X_i^{r_i}\in J\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ for each of the finitely many $i$. For sufficiently large $N$, every degree-$N$ monomial is in $J\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. [F1, L1, construct, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 The degree-$N$ module $(R[X_0,\ldots,X_n]/J)_N$ is finite over $R$. Its residue at $\mathfrak p$ is zero, so determinant Nakayama makes its localization zero. Multiplying finitely many denominators gives $s\notin\mathfrak p$ annihilating the whole module. All higher degrees vanish after localizing at $s$ because the graded algebra is generated in degree one. Therefore $Z$ has empty inverse image over $D(s)$; the image of every closed subset is closed. The argument applies to every base change. Standard chart and diagonal equations show that $\mathbb P^n_S\to S$ is separated and finite type, hence proper. [F1, F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 A closed immersion is proper by its quotient-ring description. Base change and composition preserve the three properties in [F2]. The graph of an $S$-map from a proper $X$ to a separated $Y$ is closed in $X\times_SY$; the projection is a base change of $X\to S$. Hence the map is proper. All these assertions follow from finite affine equations and the definition of universal closedness. [F2, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Let $f:X\to Y$ be surjective and finite type and let $y'\to Y$ be a point after an arbitrary base change, with image $y$. The fibre $X_y$ is nonempty and finite type over $\kappa(y)$. By [L1] it has a point with finite residue extension $L/\kappa(y)$. The algebra $L\otimes_{\kappa(y)}\kappa(y')$ is nonzero and finite-dimensional over $\kappa(y')$, so the finite-dimensional maximal-ideal argument in step 2.1 gives a point over $y'$. Thus $f$ is universally surjective. [L1, step 2.1, given, construct, step 5.1]

7.1 For a proper surjection $g:Z\to X$ with $Z\to S$ proper, the inverse image of a closed set after any base change has closed image in the base; by step 6.1 this image is exactly that of the original closed set. If $X\to S$ is separated and finite type, it is therefore proper. The projective case follows from steps 4.1 and 5.1. [F2, step 5.1, step 6.1, given] ∎
