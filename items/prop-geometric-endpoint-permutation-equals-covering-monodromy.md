## Statement

Fix $n\in\mathbb N$ and the explicit geometric base tuple $Q$ of
[[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]]. Use the same basepoint $[Q]$ in
$B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}=\pi_1(C_n(D^2),[Q])$ and in the endpoint monodromy map
$\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}:B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}\to S_n$. Let
$\iota^C:C_n(\operatorname{int}D^2)\hookrightarrow C_n(D^2)$ be the
open-to-closed inclusion, and let $\iota^C_*$ be its induced map on fundamental
groups at $[Q]$. For every geometric braid class $[\beta]\in G_n$, with slice
loop $S(\beta)$ and the inverse-loop isomorphism
$\Phi([\beta])=(\iota^C_*[S(\beta)])^{-1}$ of
[[thm-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-unordered-configurations]],
we have
$$\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}\bigl(\iota^C_*[S(\beta)]\bigr)=\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])^{-1},\qquad \pi_{\mathrm{conf}}\bigl(\Phi([\beta])\bigr)=\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta]).$$
Thus raw slicing has inverse endpoint monodromy after its class is carried to
the closed-disc configuration group, and the inverse-loop map has exactly the
geometric endpoint permutation. The formulas hold for every $n\ge0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n$, the explicit tuple $Q$, a geometric braid class $[\beta]$ based at $Q$, its point motions and endpoint permutation, the slice loop, and the fixed-basepoint isomorphism $\Phi$.

[L1] The geometric braid classes based at $Q$ form $G_n$, and the endpoint map $\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}:G_n\to S_n$ is a well-defined homomorphism; on a braid representative, $z_j(1)=q_{\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}(\beta)(j)}$ ([[thm-geometric-braids-form-a-group]], [[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]]).

[L2] The slice $S(\beta)(t)=[(z_1(t),\ldots,z_n(t))]$ is a continuous based loop in $C_n(\operatorname{int}D^2)$ at $[Q]$ ([[lem-a-geometric-braid-slices-to-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L3] A based loop at $[Q]$ has a unique lift to $F_n(D^2)$ starting at $Q$ ([[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L4] The open-to-closed inclusions induce maps $\iota^F_*$ and $\iota^C_*$ at the same basepoints, and the quotient square commutes: $$\iota^C\circ p_{\operatorname{int}D^2}=p_{D^2}\circ\iota^F$$ ([[lem-interior-and-closed-disk-configuration-spaces-are-homotopy-equivalent]]).

[L5] The induced map on fundamental groups satisfies $f_*([\alpha])=[f\circ\alpha]$ ([[def-induced-homomorphism-on-fundamental-groups]]).

[L6] The fundamental group is a group under the first-loop-then-second product, with identity and two-sided inverses ([[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]], [[thm-fundamental-group-laws]]).

[L7] Each coordinate motion is continuous, remains in the open disc, and is pairwise distinct from the others. The coordinate tuple therefore defines a continuous path into $F_n(\operatorname{int}D^2)$; continuity into the product follows because the product topology is generated by projection preimages, and distinctness puts the tuple in the ordered-configuration subspace ([[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], [[def-motion-of-an-unordered-point-configuration]], [[def-product-topology]], [[def-ordered-configuration-space]]).

[L8] For $n=0$ there is one empty geometric braid and $S_0$ is trivial ([[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], [[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L9] At the fixed basepoint $Q$, $\Phi([\beta])=(\iota^C_*[S(\beta)])^{-1}$ is the isomorphism from $G_n$ to $B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}$ ([[thm-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-unordered-configurations]]).

[L10] The endpoint monodromy $\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}:B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}\to S_n$ is a group homomorphism ([[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L11] If the terminal tuple of a lifted loop is recorded by $\widetilde\alpha(1)_i=q_{e(i)}$, then its endpoint record satisfies $e=\sigma^{-1}$ ([[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L12] For $n=1$ every geometric braid has identity endpoint permutation, and $S_1$ is trivial ([[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], [[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

[L13] If the unique lift of a based loop at $[Q]$ from $Q$ ends at $\sigma\cdot Q$, then its endpoint monodromy is $\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}([\alpha])=\sigma$ ([[def-endpoint-monodromy-of-a-configuration-loop]]).

The tuple $Q$ and every coordinate path are specified. The ordered lift used below is the unique lift from $Q$, so the Axiom of Choice is not used.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Lift the raw slice and read its endpoint labels.* Use the fixed real-to-complex coordinate identification of [L7] and put $z_\beta(t)=(z_1(t),\ldots,z_n(t))\in F_n(\operatorname{int}D^2)$; [L7] proves this is a path. By [L2], $p_{\operatorname{int}D^2}\circ z_\beta=S(\beta)$. The quotient square [L4] gives $$p_{D^2}\circ\iota^F\circ z_\beta =\iota^C\circ p_{\operatorname{int}D^2}\circ z_\beta =\iota^C\circ S(\beta).$$ Thus $\iota^F\circ z_\beta$ is the lift from $Q$ of the closed-disc loop $\iota^C\circ S(\beta)$, and by [L3] it is the unique such lift. By [L13] its endpoint permutation is $\sigma=\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}([\iota^C\circ S(\beta)])$. Its terminal coordinate $j$ is $q_{\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])(j)}$ by [L1], so its label record is $e=\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])$. By [L11], $e=\sigma^{-1}$. The induced-map formula [L5] therefore yields $$\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}\bigl(\iota^C_*[S(\beta)]\bigr) =\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])^{-1}.$$ [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L7, L11, L13]

2.1 *Apply the inverse-loop isomorphism.* Let $x:=\iota^C_*[S(\beta)]$. By [L9], $\Phi([\beta])=x^{-1}$. Since $\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}$ is a homomorphism by [L10], and $B_n^{\mathrm{conf}}$ is a group by [L6], $$\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(x)\,\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(x^{-1}) =\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(xx^{-1})=1,$$ so $\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(x^{-1})=\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(x)^{-1}$. Applying step 1.1 gives $$\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(\Phi([\beta])) =\bigl(\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])^{-1}\bigr)^{-1} =\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta]).$$ [L6, L9, L10, step 1.1]

3.1 *Zero- and one-strand cases.* When $n=0$, [L8] gives the unique empty braid and the trivial permutation target; the isomorphism [L9] then makes the configuration braid group a singleton, so both equations hold. When $n=1$, [L12] gives identity endpoint permutation and trivial $S_1$, so both monodromy values are the identity. [L8, L9, L12] ∎
