## Definition

For each positive integer $n$, let $(\Omega_n,\mathcal A_n,P_n)$ be a probability space and let $X_n:\Omega_n\to\mathbb R^d$ be measurable into the Borel sigma-algebra generated by the Euclidean open sets, where $d\ge1$ is fixed and $\mathbb R^d$ has the Euclidean norm $\|\cdot\|_2$ ([[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]], [[def-random-element-and-real-random-variable]], [[def-probability-measure]]). The spaces and measures may depend on $n$; no common probability space is required.

Write $X_n=o_P(1)$ if for every $\varepsilon>0$,
$$P_n(\|X_n\|_2>\varepsilon)\longrightarrow0.$$
Write $X_n=O_P(1)$, or say that $(X_n)$ is bounded in probability, if for every $\eta>0$ there is $M>0$ such that
$$\limsup_{n\to\infty}P_n(\|X_n\|_2>M)\le\eta.$$
For real-valued variables on one common probability space, $o_P(1)$ is convergence in probability to zero as defined in [[def-convergence-in-probability]].

The norms and tail probabilities are part of the definition. Independence is not implicit in either notation.
