## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group and let $p$ be a prime. The following are equivalent.

(a) $G$ has a normal $p$-complement.
(b) $G$ has a normal $p'$-subgroup $K$ with $[G:K]$ a power of $p$.
(c) There is a Sylow $p$-subgroup $Q$ of $G$ and an epimorphism $\theta:G\to Q$.
(d) The product of any two $p'$-elements of $G$ is a $p'$-element.
(e) Every $p'$-element of $G$ lies in $O_{p'}(G)$.

Moreover, if these conditions hold, then $O_{p'}(G)$ is the normal
$p$-complement of $G$, it is the unique normal $p$-complement of $G$, it equals
the set of $p'$-elements of $G$ and the subgroup generated by them, and it is
the kernel of every epimorphism $\theta$ as in (c).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$ and a prime $p$, with $p'$-element terminology and the $p'$-core as in [[def-p-prime-core-of-a-finite-group]] and normal $p$-complement as in [[def-normal-p-complement-and-p-nilpotent-group]].

[F1] A normal $p$-complement is a normal subgroup $K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ with $p\nmid|K|$ and $[G:K]$ a power of $p$; for $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$ it is equivalent to $G=KP$ with $K\cap P=\{1\}$, and $[G:K]=|P|$ ([[def-normal-p-complement-and-p-nilpotent-group]], [[def-internal-semidirect-product]], [[thm-lagrange]]).

[F2] $O_{p'}(G)$ is a normal $p'$-subgroup of $G$ containing every normal $p'$-subgroup of $G$ ([[def-p-prime-core-of-a-finite-group]]).

[F3] $G$ has a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$, of order $p^{a}$ where $|G|=p^{a}m$ with $p\nmid m$; thus $|G|=[G:P]\,|P|$ and $p\nmid[G:P]$ ([[thm-sylow-first-theorem]], [[def-sylow-p-subgroup]], [[thm-lagrange]]).

[F4] For $K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ the quotient $G/K$ is a group with $|G/K|=[G:K]$, and the natural map $G\to G/K$ is an epimorphism with kernel $K$ ([[def-quotient-group]], [[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]]).

[F5] For a homomorphism $\theta:G\to H$ one has $G/\ker\theta\cong\operatorname{im}\theta$, $\ker\theta\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ and $\operatorname{im}\theta\le H$; hence $|G|=|\ker\theta|\cdot|\operatorname{im}\theta|$ ([[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-groups]], [[thm-image-subgroup-and-kernel-normal]], [[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]]).

[F6] If $K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ and $P\le G$ then $P/(P\cap K)\cong PK/K$ and $PK\le G$, so $|PK|\,|P\cap K|=|P|\,|K|$ ([[thm-second-isomorphism-theorem-groups]], [[lem-product-with-normal-subgroup]]).

[F7] If $\theta:G\to H$ is a homomorphism and $u\in G$, then $\theta(u)^{\operatorname{ord}(u)}=e$, so $\operatorname{ord}(\theta(u))$ divides $\operatorname{ord}(u)$, while $\operatorname{ord}(v)$ divides $|H|$ for every $v$ in the finite group $H$; and $\operatorname{ord}(v)=1$ exactly when $v=e$ ([[lem-order-characterisation]], [[lem-group-homomorphism-basic-properties]], [[cor-order-of-element-divides-group-order]], [[def-order-in-a-group]]).

[F8] If $H\le G$ then $|H|$ divides $|G|$, so a divisor of a power of $p$ that is prime to $p$ equals $1$; a divisor of a $p'$-number is a $p'$-number; and every integer greater than $1$ has a prime divisor ([[thm-lagrange]], [[lem-divisibility-basic]], [[lem-every-integer-above-one-has-a-prime-divisor]], [[def-finite-p-group]]).

[F9] If the prime $p$ divides the order of a finite group $H$, then $H$ has an element of order $p$ ([[thm-cauchys-theorem-for-finite-groups]]).

[F10] The subgroup generated by a set $Y$ consists of finite products of elements of $Y$ and their inverses, and conjugation is an automorphism of $G$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]], [[thm-conjugation-is-an-automorphism]]).

[F11] $x^{n}=e$ if and only if $(x^{-1})^{n}=e$, so $\operatorname{ord}(x^{-1})=\operatorname{ord}(x)$ ([[lem-group-power-laws]], [[def-order-in-a-group]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (a) implies (b) directly: a normal $p$-complement is by definition a normal $p'$-subgroup of $p$-power index. [F1, given]

1.2 (b) implies (c): let $K$ be a normal $p'$-subgroup with $[G:K]=p^{r}$, so that $G/K$ is a finite group of order $p^{r}$ by [F4]. By [F6] applied to $K\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ and a Sylow $P$ of $G$ given by [F3], $P/(P\cap K)\cong PK/K\le G/K$; here $|P\cap K|$ divides $|P|=p^{a}$ and also divides $|K|$, which is prime to $p$, so $|P\cap K|=1$ by [F8]. Hence $|PK/K|=|P|=p^{a}$ divides $|G/K|=p^{r}$, so $r\ge a$. Since $|K|$ divides $|G|=p^{a}m$ and is prime to $p$, every prime divisor of $|K|$ divides $m$, so $|K|$ divides $m$ and $m/|K|$ is an integer prime to $p$; the identity $p^{r}=|G|/|K|=p^{a}(m/|K|)$ therefore forces $r=a$ and $|K|=m$. Hence $|PK|=|P|\,|K|/|P\cap K|=p^{a}|K|=p^{a}m=|G|$ by [F6], so $PK=G$. [F3, F4, F6, F8, algebra]

1.3 (d) implies (e): let $x$ be a $p'$-element and let $Y$ be the set of conjugates of $x$; by (d) and [F11] every finite product of elements of $Y$ is a $p'$-element, so every element of $\langle Y\rangle$ is a $p'$-element by [F10]. If a prime $\ell$ divided $|\langle Y\rangle|$, then $\langle Y\rangle$ would contain an element of order $\ell$ by [F9], which forces $\ell\ne p$; hence $p\nmid|\langle Y\rangle|$, and $\langle Y\rangle$ is a $p'$-subgroup. It is normal: $g\langle Y\rangle g^{-1}=\langle gYg^{-1}\rangle=\langle Y\rangle$ for every $g\in G$, by [F10] and the fact that $Y$ consists of all conjugates of $x$. Hence $\langle Y\rangle$ is a normal $p'$-subgroup and $\langle Y\rangle\le O_{p'}(G)$ by [F2], so $x\in O_{p'}(G)$. [F2, F9, F10, F11, assume-hyp]

1.4 (e) implies (a): write $|G|=p^{a}m$ with $p\nmid m$ as in [F3], and let $Q$ be a Sylow $q$-subgroup of $G$ for each prime $q\ne p$ dividing $|G|$, which exists by [F3] applied to $Q$ in place of $P$ ([[thm-sylow-first-theorem]]). Every element of $Q$ has order dividing $|Q|=q^{b}$, hence prime to $p$, so every element of $Q$ lies in $O_{p'}(G)$ by (e); thus $Q\le O_{p'}(G)$ and $|Q|$ divides $|O_{p'}(G)|$ by [F8]. Multiplying over the primes $q\ne p$, the $p'$-number $m$ divides $|O_{p'}(G)|$; since $O_{p'}(G)$ is a $p'$-group by [F2], $|O_{p'}(G)|$ divides $m$, so $|O_{p'}(G)|=m$. [F2, F3, F8, assume-hyp]

1.5 A subgroup is a $p'$-group exactly when all its elements are $p'$-elements; hence, by [F2] and (e), the set of $p'$-elements of $G$ is contained in $O_{p'}(G)$ and contains all elements of $O_{p'}(G)$, so it equals $O_{p'}(G)$ and generates it. [F2, F7, assume-hyp]

2.1 Every $g\in G$ therefore has a unique expression $g=uk$ with $u\in P$, $k\in K$: existence is step 1.2, and uniqueness follows from $P\cap K=\{1\}$, since $uk=u'k'$ gives $u'^{-1}u=k'k^{-1}\in P\cap K$. Defining $\theta(g):=u$, one has $\theta(g_1g_2)=\theta(g_1)\theta(g_2)$ for all $g_1,g_2$: writing $g_i=u_ik_i$ and using $k_1u_2=u_2(u_2^{-1}k_1u_2)$ with $u_2^{-1}k_1u_2\in K$, the product is $(u_1u_2)(u_2^{-1}k_1u_2\,k_2)$. Thus $\theta:G\to P$ is a homomorphism, it is onto because $\theta(u)=u$, and its kernel is $K$. [F1, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 (c) implies (d): let $\theta:G\to Q$ be an epimorphism onto a Sylow $Q$ of $G$. By [F5], $|G|=|\ker\theta|\cdot|Q|$, so $|\ker\theta|=|G|/|Q|=[G:P]$, a $p'$-number by [F3]. For a $p'$-element $x\in G$, [F7] makes $\operatorname{ord}(\theta(x))$ a divisor of $\operatorname{ord}(x)$, hence prime to $p$, and also a divisor of $|Q|=p^{a}$, hence a power of $p$; so $\operatorname{ord}(\theta(x))=1$ and $\theta(x)=e$ by [F8] and [F7]. Thus every $p'$-element of $G$ lies in $\ker\theta$, and since $\ker\theta$ is a $p'$-group, any product of two $p'$-elements lies in $\ker\theta$ and has order dividing $|\ker\theta|$, hence is a $p'$-element by [F7] and [F8]. [F3, F5, F7, F8, step 1.2]

2.3 Consequently $|P\cap O_{p'}(G)|=1$: the order of the intersection divides both $p^{a}$ and the $p'$-number $m$ by [F8], so $|P\,O_{p'}(G)|=|P|\,|O_{p'}(G)|=p^{a}m=|G|$ by [F6], and $P\,O_{p'}(G)=G$. Since $O_{p'}(G)\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ by [F2] and $[G:O_{p'}(G)]=|G|/m=p^{a}$ is a power of $p$, $O_{p'}(G)$ is a normal $p$-complement of $G$: (a) holds. [F1, F2, F6, F8, step 1.4]

2.4 The moreover clauses. Assume (a)–(e) hold. Any normal $p$-complement $K$ is a normal $p'$-subgroup, so $K\le O_{p'}(G)$ by [F2]; and $|K|=|G|/[G:K]=m=|O_{p'}(G)|$ by [F1], [F3] and [F8], because $[G:K]$ is a $p$-power dividing $|G|=p^{a}m$ and $|K|$ is a $p'$-number. Hence $K=O_{p'}(G)$, so $O_{p'}(G)$ is the unique normal $p$-complement. [F1, F2, F3, F8, step 1.4]

3.1 Finally let $\theta:G\to Q$ be an epimorphism as in (c). By step 2.2 every $p'$-element lies in $\ker\theta$, while $|\ker\theta|=[G:P]=m$ is a $p'$-number; so $\ker\theta$ is a normal $p'$-subgroup with $[G:\ker\theta]=|P|$ a $p$-power, hence a normal $p$-complement, hence equal to $O_{p'}(G)$ by step 2.4. ∎ [F1, F5, step 2.2, step 2.4]
