## Statement

Let $K$ be a probability kernel from $(S,\Sigma)$ to $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$. Define
$$a(s,t):=K(s,(t,\infty)),\qquad b(s,t):=K(s,\{t\}).$$
Both maps are $\Sigma\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$-measurable. If $S_0$ and $T_0$ are measurable random elements of $S$ and $\mathbb R$ on one probability space, and $U$ is any measurable real random variable on that space, then $a(S_0,T_0)$, $a(S_0,T_0)+b(S_0,T_0)$, and $a(S_0,T_0)+U b(S_0,T_0)$ are measurable. The middle expression equals $K(S_0,[T_0,\infty))$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A probability kernel $K$, measurable random elements $S_0,T_0$, and a measurable real random variable $U$.

[F1] For every measurable target set $C$, $s\mapsto K(s,C)$ is measurable, and each section $K(s,\cdot)$ is a probability measure. [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F2] Product sigma-algebras are generated by measurable rectangles. [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F3] The rationals are countable and dense in $\mathbb R$. [[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]]

[F4] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets, so every open interval is Borel. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F5] Integrating a nonnegative product-measurable function against a probability kernel gives a measurable function of the kernel parameter, with infinity allowed. [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]]

[F6] A random element is a measurable map into its specified measurable space. [[def-random-element-and-real-random-variable]]

[F7] Continuous maps have Borel-measurable preimages, and measurable maps compose. [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]], [[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $\pi_S:S\times\mathbb R\to S$ be the first-coordinate projection and define $\widetilde K((s,t),C):=K(s,C)$. The projection is measurable because $\pi_S^{-1}(A)=A\times\mathbb R$ for every $A\in\Sigma$; hence [F1] and [F2] show that $\widetilde K$ is a probability kernel from $(S\times\mathbb R,\Sigma\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$ to $\mathbb R$. [F1, F2, construct]

1.2 The relation $\{(t,y):y>t\}$ is $\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q}(-\infty,q)\times(q,\infty)$: density in [F3] supplies a rational strictly between $t$ and $y$, and every point in a displayed rectangle has $t<y$. The relation $\{(t,y):y<t\}$ has the analogous representation $\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q}(q,\infty)\times(-\infty,q)$. These are countable unions of Borel rectangles by [F3] and [F4], and the equality relation is their joint complement. Therefore all three relations belong to $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$. The coordinate map $(s,t,y)\mapsto(t,y)$ is measurable because inverse images of generating rectangles are $S\times B\times C$; taking inverse images now proves the two relation indicators in the domain of $\widetilde K$ are product-measurable. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 Apply [F5] to $\widetilde K$ and the two indicator functions of the relation sets in step 1.2. Their section integrals are $\int 1_{\{y>t\}}K(s,dy)=K(s,(t,\infty))=a(s,t)$ and $\int 1_{\{y=t\}}K(s,dy)=K(s,\{t\})=b(s,t)$, so both maps are product-measurable. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For $n=2,3$, fix a bijection $e:\mathbb Q\to\mathbb N$ supplied by [F3]. Each rational open box is encoded by a finite tuple of natural numbers, and iterating the injection $(a,b)\mapsto 2^a(2b+1)$ shows there are only countably many such boxes. Density of $\mathbb Q$ then shows they form a base of $\mathbb R^n$: each point of an open set lies in such a box contained in that set. Thus each open set is a countable union of rectangles in the finite product sigma-algebra, giving $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)\subseteq\mathcal B(\mathbb R)^{\otimes n}$. Conversely, each coordinate projection $\pi_i:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ is continuous, so [F7] makes $\pi_i^{-1}(A)$ Borel for every $A\in\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$; finite intersections show every measurable rectangle is Borel, giving the reverse inclusion. Hence the Borel product and Euclidean Borel sigma-algebras agree in both dimensions. The map $\omega\mapsto(S_0(\omega),T_0(\omega))$ is measurable because the inverse image of each generating rectangle $A\times B$ is $S_0^{-1}(A)\cap T_0^{-1}(B)$; therefore $a(S_0,T_0)$ and $b(S_0,T_0)$ are measurable by [F6] and [F7]. The sets $(t,\infty)$ and $\{t\}$ are disjoint and their union is $[t,\infty)$, so finite additivity gives $a(S_0,T_0)+b(S_0,T_0)=K(S_0,[T_0,\infty))$. The map $(x,z)\mapsto x+z$ is continuous by $|(x+z)-(x_0+z_0)|\le |x-x_0|+|z-z_0|$. The map $(x,z,u)\mapsto x+uz$ is continuous because $|(x+uz)-(x_0+u_0z_0)|\le |x-x_0|+|u|\cdot|z-z_0|+|z_0|\cdot|u-u_0|$, and $|u|$ is bounded in a sufficiently small neighborhood of $u_0$. The pairs and triples of the real measurable functions already obtained and $U$ are measurable because inverse images of generating product rectangles are finite intersections of coordinate preimages. The established Borel-product equalities make these continuous maps measurable by [F7]. Composition by [F7] proves measurability of the asserted sums. [F1, F2, F3, F6, F7, step 2.1] ∎
