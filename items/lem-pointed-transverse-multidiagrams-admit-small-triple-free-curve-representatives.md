## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix finitely many labelled attaching-circle families on a closed oriented surface with positive area form, finitely many protected points off all circles, and one witness point in every complementary region. Circles within a family are disjoint. Circles in different families are transverse, so their labelled pair intersections are finite; common points of three or more families are initially permitted.

Arbitrarily small smooth Hamiltonian isotopies of the individual families, supported away from all the protected and witness points, give pairwise transverse representatives with NO point on three curves from different families. Their pair intersections are canonically followed through the small isotopies, without birth or death. When the corresponding product tori have common POSITIVE rank $m>0$, no point belongs to three DISTINCT torus labels. At rank zero the empty product tori all equal the point of $\operatorname{Sym}^0\Sigma$; no exclusion of their triple intersection is claimed, and their source-dependent target transport is the identity. The complete disk-cut and based-link data can be transported by the family isotopies; no topology of the underlying handlebodies is changed.

Full-curve periodic-domain lattices and their sublattices generated by pairwise periodic domains transport bijectively by the swept annuli. All coefficients at the original witness and protected points are unchanged. Consequently any strict weak or strong admissibility criterion witnessed by these coefficients is preserved, when its indexing and linear evaluation are transported under this lattice map. Identification of that evaluation with a geometric Spin-c or Fredholm quantity is a separate topological assertion, not part of this lemma.

For an actual boundary-marked polygon source, the relative map spaces with fixed corresponding corner labels are homeomorphic by an actual smooth source-dependent target isotopy. It is the identity near protected incidence divisors and the diagonal and is fixed in the longitudinal coordinate on each far end. This induces bijections on literal continuous relative homotopy classes, without quotienting by a fundamental-group action. No symmetric power of a nonholomorphic surface map is used as a global smooth target map. For smooth fixed-end reference maps the transported actual Fredholm index is unchanged. These are class and admissibility transports, not an equality of holomorphic counts or an isotopy-invariance theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite transverse multi-diagram, area form, protected points, original region witnesses and sufficiently small neighborhoods avoiding those points. Repeated uses of one torus label must first be interpreted as distinct chosen transverse representatives; the no-triple assertion concerns distinct labels.

[F1] Finite-dimensional parametric transversality and Sard for the stated supported evaluation maps hold under Countable Choice. ([[thm-parametric-transversality]])

[F2] Actual smooth local inverses follow from the finite-dimensional inverse theorem. Fredholm index is locally constant under norm-continuous finite-ended Hilbert operator deformations by the finite Schur-block proof. ([[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]], [[lem-countable-choice-hilbert-parametrices-and-index-stability]])

## Proof

1.1 Choose thin finite tubular neighborhoods of all the circles avoiding the protected and region witness points. They exist by compactness and the finite point list. Smooth Hamiltonian vector fields supported in these neighborhoods can span the tangent plane at every point of a given family: in a local area coordinate choose compactly supported functions whose differentials form a basis on a smaller chart; contraction with the nondegenerate area form turns them into a tangent basis. A finite such cover gives finitely many Hamiltonians. The time-one flow of their small linear combination depends smoothly on its finite parameter vector. For completeness this uses only finite-dimensional Picard iteration: on each compact coordinate ball a small-time integral map is a contraction with a geometric remainder bound; differentiating its equation gives the smooth variational solutions; a finite compact cover and finitely many time steps extend the flow, and the negative-time flow is its inverse. The Hamiltonian identity $\mathcal L_{X_H}\eta=d(\iota_{X_H}\eta)=0$ shows that it preserves area, by differentiating the pullback. Every flow is the identity near the finite forbidden points. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Perturb the families in their fixed finite order. For the current family its supported flow evaluation $A\times(\coprod S^1)\to\Sigma$ is a submersion at zero, since the Hamiltonian values in step1.1 span at every point. Compactness makes it a submersion on a small common parameter ball. Apply [F1] to all earlier curves and to all finitely many intersections of pairs of earlier families. A common arbitrarily small parameter makes the current curves transverse to the earlier curves and disjoint from those intersection points; the latter transversality has negative expected intersection dimension. The single flow moves the entire current family and therefore retains its internal disjointness. Finitely many such steps remove every triple point. Choose the steps small enough that ALL original pair intersections persist uniquely: in a local chart at an original crossing [F2] solves the two transverse defining equations smoothly, while outside finitely many smaller crossing charts the old two compact curves have positive distance. Uniform smallness of positions and first derivatives gives no other crossing and no loss of the old ones. The same bound holds along the entire small flow path, so the labelled crossings have actual paths, not just a numerical intersection count. Arbitrary prescribed smooth neighborhoods of the original curves impose only finitely many derivative bounds, which can be met by decreasing these finite parameters. [F1, F2, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For positive common rank $m>0$, a product torus point has at least one constituent point and consists of one point on each of its disjoint labelled circles. If a tuple belonged to three distinct product tori, EACH of its constituent surface points would lie on a circle from each of those three families. This is a forbidden surface triple point. Hence distinct POSITIVE-RANK product tori have no common triple generator. If the common rank is zero, all families are empty, the target is one point and the target transport below is the identity; no argument by a constituent point is available or needed. Each moved circle remains isotopic to the old one in the pointed surface. Transport its compression disks using an extension of its boundary isotopy to the handlebody: in a boundary collar extend the surface flow with a normal cutoff and use its actual compact flow, then keep the interior fixed. Apply this extension separately for each handlebody whose family was moved. Disk disjointness, cut-ball diffeomorphisms and the based bridge arcs and witnesses are carried by these diffeomorphisms. Thus the construction does not impose a different handlebody or a stronger neatness condition on its disk witnesses. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 Track a labelled oriented circle through its small isotopy. Its swept cylinder gives an integral two-chain with boundary equal to the new minus old circle, supported in the chosen thin neighborhood. Its coefficient at every protected or old witness point is zero. If an old periodic domain has full-curve boundary $\sum a_j\gamma_j$, add $\sum a_j$ times these swept chains, with the appropriate new-minus-old orientation, to obtain a new periodic domain with the SAME coefficients at all those points. On either diagram, a two-chain with prescribed full-curve boundary is unique after fixing one coefficient: their difference has zero boundary on the connected closed surface and hence is an integer multiple of the whole surface. Conversely subtract the same swept chains from a new periodic domain. These two operations are inverse with the same normalization. There is no ambiguity in the old boundary coefficients because distinct transverse circle labels have a nonempty open arc off every other curve; the boundary jump there reads its coefficient. The same holds after movement. This proves an actual lattice bijection, preserving sums and carrying the pairwise sublattice to its corresponding pairwise sublattice. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Every coefficient which proved an OLD strict admissibility inequality is still present at its old region witness, which has avoided all swept tracks. Thus if an old normalized periodic domain has a positive and a negative coefficient, so does its transport. If it has a coefficient strictly greater than a transported linear evaluation, the transported domain has that same coefficient at that same witness point. Apply the inverse lattice map of step3.2 to each nonzero new periodic domain to prove the corresponding admissibility condition on the new diagram. This argument proves preservation from the original data, even though new thin regions may have been created. It does not assume that old and new regions are in bijection, or that each new thin-region coefficient is an old coefficient. A geometric interpretation of the transported evaluation, such as its Spin-c first-Chern value, must be supplied separately. [given, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

4.2 We construct the promised target isotopy carefully. On the regular ordered locus the product of a surface-family flow on its distinct circle coordinates is smooth, equivariant under permutations, and gives the actual motion of its product torus. These torus tracks are compact and avoid the diagonal and every protected incidence divisor. Near the intersection of the two adjacent tori at a corner, take smooth defining functions for the two MOVING submanifolds. Their combined derivative is invertible by their transverse intersection. [F2] therefore gives joint moving coordinates carrying those two submanifolds to the two fixed coordinate planes, and their crossing to zero. The inverse moving chart supplies a target velocity field realizing both motions at once. Along one boundary edge away from corners extend its single torus velocity in an ordinary submanifold chart. Patch these vector fields with a SOURCE-dependent smooth partition: in a far corner strip use the two-torus field, along each remaining physical edge only fields with its required torus normal velocity have nonzero weight, and in the source interior there is no boundary constraint. On boundary overlaps the fields have the same prescribed normal velocity; their difference is tangent to that moving torus, so affine patching preserves its motion. Cut them off in a fixed target neighborhood of the union of these compact tracks, disjoint from the protected loci, and extend by zero. Their parameter-time flow is a genuine smooth target diffeomorphism $\Phi_{a,z}$, the identity near those loci. It maps each old assigned boundary torus to its moved torus and each corner to its followed pair intersection. On a far end the construction is independent of its longitudinal variable. No global map $\operatorname{Sym}^m(F)$ for a nonholomorphic surface diffeomorphism has been asserted. [F2, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For each continuous polygon map define $u_a(z)=\Phi_{a,z}(u_0(z))$. The far-end property in step4.2 gives the prescribed new corner limits and a continuous map on the compactified source. Its inverse is pointwise $\Phi_{a,z}^{-1}$, and both operations carry boundary-preserving fixed-corner homotopies to such homotopies. They give actual inverse bijections on the literal relative homotopy sets. Protected divisor degrees stay fixed since the flow is the identity near each divisor and at all times avoids moving the boundary across it. For smooth fixed-end representatives their real boundary bundles and principal operators vary smoothly with $a$; end transversality persists and the local corner coordinates give common completed Hilbert domains after their smooth frame identifications. The resulting actual Fredholm family is norm-continuous, so its index is unchanged by [F2]. One can choose the transported almost-complex tensors as the pushforwards on these boundary charts and interpolate on the compact source interior; smallness preserves tameness. This is an index and class homotopy argument, not a claim that $u_a$ is holomorphic. [F2, given, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.2, construct, algebra]

6.1 The construction is finite in the number of families and crossings. It uses finite Hamiltonian parameters, the actual finite-dimensional transversality theorem and finite flow and inverse constructions, so Countable Choice suffices. It explicitly provides surface triple-free representatives, product-torus triple exclusion in positive rank, literal class transport and coefficient-witness admissibility preservation. To infer that a particular geometric Spin-c-labelled strong condition is preserved, combine step4.1 with its separately proved Spin-c and first-Chern transport. To infer equality of counts across the curve movement requires an actual analytic continuation or moving-boundary polygon argument; it is NOT a consequence of these class bijections. This distinction is essential when repairing a fixed-triple genericity statement obstructed by constant triangles. [F1, F2, given, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
