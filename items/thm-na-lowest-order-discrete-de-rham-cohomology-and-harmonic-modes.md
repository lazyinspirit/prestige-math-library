## Statement

Assume Countable Choice for the H(curl), H(div), and real $L^2$ inner-product interfaces used here; no full Axiom of Choice is assumed. Let $\mathcal T$ be a finite matching tetrahedral mesh with realization $|\mathcal T|=\overline\Omega$. Define $C_k(\mathcal T;\mathbb R)$ by extending the oriented chain groups of [[def-oriented-simplicial-cell-chains]] to real coefficients, and put $C^k=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(C_k,\mathbb R)$ with coboundary $\delta^k=\partial_{k+1}^*$. The degrees of freedom give a cochain isomorphism from
$$W_h^0\xrightarrow{\nabla}N_h^1\xrightarrow{\operatorname{curl}}N_h^2\xrightarrow{\operatorname{div}}N_h^3$$
to $C^0\xrightarrow{\delta^0}C^1\xrightarrow{\delta^1}C^2\xrightarrow{\delta^2}C^3$. Consequently the discrete cohomology in degree $k$ is naturally isomorphic to $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(H_k(|\mathcal T|;\mathbb R),\mathbb R)$. If $\overline\Omega$ is nonempty, connected, and contractible, then the sequence is exact at every positive-degree space, $\ker\nabla$ consists of constants, and $\operatorname{div}:N_h^2\to N_h^3$ is onto. For general topology, define
$$\mathcal H_h^1:=\ker(\operatorname{curl}|_{N_h^1})\cap(\nabla W_h^0)^\perp,\qquad \mathcal H_h^2:=\ker(\operatorname{div}|_{N_h^2})\cap(\operatorname{curl}N_h^1)^\perp$$
using the $L^2$ inner product. Then $\ker\operatorname{curl}=\nabla W_h^0\oplus\mathcal H_h^1$ and $\ker\operatorname{div}=\operatorname{curl}N_h^1\oplus\mathcal H_h^2$, with $\dim\mathcal H_h^k=\dim H_k(|\mathcal T|;\mathbb R)$ for $k=1,2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite matching tetrahedral mesh $\mathcal T$, its oriented cell chains, the lowest-order spaces $W_h^0,N_h^1,N_h^2,N_h^3$ of [[def-na-lowest-order-whitney-de-rham-spaces]], and the real simplicial cochain complex dual to its finite chain complex.

[A1] Countable Choice is assumed by the weak graph-derivative uniqueness and real $L^2$ inner-product interfaces defining these spaces; the finite cochain and basis arguments below require no stronger choice principle. ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-na-hcurl-space]], [[def-na-hdiv-space]], [[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]])

[F1] $W_h^0$ consists of continuous piecewise $P_1$ functions; $N_h^1$ consists of piecewise Whitney edge fields with one oriented edge moment per mesh edge; $N_h^2$ consists of piecewise $RT_0$ fields with one oriented face flux per mesh face; and $N_h^3$ consists of cellwise constants. ([[def-na-lowest-order-whitney-de-rham-spaces]])

[F2] The six oriented edge circulations are unisolvent for the local first-family Nedelec space. ([[lem-na-nedelec-edge-unisolvence]])

[F3] The edge and face moment assignments assemble into global H(curl) and H(div) fields; the four oriented face fluxes are unisolvent on each $RT_0(K)$, and the vertex, edge, face, and cell interpolants commute cellwise with gradient, curl, and divergence. ([[lem-na-whitney-moments-commute-with-grad-curl-div]])

[F4] The oriented simplicial chain complex is freely generated by oriented mesh simplices and has alternating boundary $\partial[v_0,\ldots,v_k]=\sum_i(-1)^i[v_0,\ldots,\widehat v_i,\ldots,v_k]$. ([[def-oriented-simplicial-cell-chains]])

[F5] The canonical map identifies simplicial homology of a complex with singular homology of its realization. ([[thm-simplicial-and-singular-homology-agree-for-simplicial-complexes]])

[F6] A nonempty contractible space has the singular homology of a point for every abelian coefficient group. ([[cor-contractible-nonempty-spaces-have-the-homology-of-a-point]])

[F7] Contractible means that every continuous map from the space to any target is homotopic to a constant map. ([[def-nullhomotopic-map-and-contractible-space]])

[F8] The alternating simplicial boundary satisfies $\partial_{k-1}\partial_k=0$, so the dual coboundaries form a cochain complex and $B_k:=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}$ is contained in $Z_k:=\ker\partial_k$. ([[lem-simplicial-boundary-squared-zero]])

[F9] The componentwise real $L^2$ pairing is a well-defined positive-definite inner product on the finite-element vector spaces under Countable Choice. ([[def-na-hcurl-space]], [[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]])

[F10] Every subspace of a finite-dimensional real inner-product space has an orthogonal direct-sum complement. ([[thm-finite-dimensional-orthogonal-decomposition]])

[F11] The empty space has no singular simplices, so all of its singular chain groups with real coefficients are zero. ([[def-singular-simplex-and-singular-chain-group-with-coefficients]])

[F12] The cells of a matching mesh have union $\overline\Omega$ and $\Omega$ is the interior of that union; an empty mesh therefore has empty realization and domain. ([[def-matching-affine-simplicial-meshes]])

## Proof

1.1 If $\mathcal T=\varnothing$, [F12] gives $\Omega=\varnothing$; by [F1] and [F4] all real chain, cochain, and finite-element spaces are zero, and [F11] gives $H_k(\varnothing;\mathbb R)=0$. All cohomology and harmonic statements then reduce to the unique maps and decompositions of zero spaces, and the nonempty contractible branch is inapplicable. Suppose henceforth that $\mathcal T\ne\varnothing$. In degree zero, the vertex basis $\lambda_i$ is dual to vertex evaluation; a value at each vertex determines a unique affine function on each incident tetrahedron, and common-face traces agree because they agree at the face vertices. Thus $J^0:W_h^0\to C^0$ is a bijection. In degree one, [F2] makes the six local edge moments a bijection between each local Nedelec space and its six edge values. The definition of $N_h^1$ imposes one value for each globally oriented edge. Any global assignment therefore determines the unique local fields on each cell, and [F3] makes their assembly a global H(curl) field; conversely, zero global moments force every cell restriction to vanish by [F2]. Thus $J^1:N_h^1\to C^1$ is a bijection. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, F11, F12, given, construct]

2.1 In degree two, the four oriented face fluxes determine each local $RT_0$ field by [F3]. The definition of $N_h^2$ imposes one value for every global oriented face; these values determine unique local fields and [F3] assembles them into a global H(div) field. Zero global fluxes force every local field to vanish, so $J^2:N_h^2\to C^2$ is a bijection. In degree three, a constant is determined by its cell integral, so $J^3:N_h^3\to C^3$ is a bijection. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The dual differential $\delta^k$ evaluates a cochain on the oriented boundary of each $(k+1)$-simplex. For $k=0$, its value on an oriented edge is the terminal nodal value minus the initial value; for $k=1$, its value on a face is the signed sum of its boundary-edge circulations; and for $k=2$, its value on a tetrahedron is the signed sum of its boundary-face fluxes. By [F3] these are exactly the degrees of freedom of $\nabla$, $\operatorname{curl}$, and $\operatorname{div}$. Thus $J^{k+1}d_h=\delta^kJ^k$ for each differential. By [F8], $\delta^{k+1}\delta^k=0$, so these maps are cochain complexes and the bijections induce isomorphisms on cohomology. [A1, F3, F4, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Write $Z_k=\ker\partial_k$ and $B_k=\operatorname{im}\partial_{k+1}$; [F8] gives $B_k\subseteq Z_k$. Identify each cochain with its coordinate functional on the oriented simplex basis. Then $\ker\delta^k$ consists exactly of functionals vanishing on $B_k$, namely $B_k^\perp$. The image of $\delta^{k-1}=\partial_k^*$ lies in $Z_k^\perp$, since $(\partial_k^*\ell)(z)=\ell(\partial_kz)=0$ for $z\in Z_k$. These two subspaces have the same dimension: $\dim\operatorname{im}\partial_k^*=\operatorname{rank}\partial_k=\dim C_k-\dim Z_k=\dim Z_k^\perp$, so $\operatorname{im}\partial_k^*=Z_k^\perp$. Restricting a functional in $B_k^\perp$ to $Z_k$ therefore descends to $Z_k/B_k$ and has kernel $Z_k^\perp$. Every functional on $Z_k/B_k$ pulls back to a functional on $Z_k$ vanishing on $B_k$; extend a finite basis of $Z_k$ to one of $C_k$ and set the added basis values to zero. This proves surjectivity of restriction and hence $H^k(C^*)=B_k^\perp/Z_k^\perp\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(Z_k/B_k,\mathbb R)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb R}(H_k^{\mathrm{simp}}(\mathcal T;\mathbb R),\mathbb R)$. Since these spaces are finite dimensional, their real duals have the same dimension. [F4, F8, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 By [F5], $H_k^{\mathrm{simp}}(\mathcal T;\mathbb R)\cong H_k(|\mathcal T|;\mathbb R)$. If $|\mathcal T|=\overline\Omega$ is nonempty, connected, and contractible in the sense of [F7], [F6] makes this group $\mathbb R$ for $k=0$ and zero for $k>0$. Steps 1.1–2.2 therefore show that the discrete cohomology is one-dimensional in degree zero and zero in positive degrees, which is exactness of the displayed finite sequence, including surjectivity of its last map. The globally constant functions lie in $W_h^0$ and have zero gradient, so the one-dimensional degree-zero kernel consists exactly of constants. [A1, F1, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 For arbitrary topology, let $Z_h^1=\ker\operatorname{curl}$ and $B_h^1=\nabla W_h^0$. By step 3.1, $B_h^1\subseteq Z_h^1$. The space $Z_h^1$ is finite dimensional as a subspace of $N_h^1$, so [F10] applied inside $Z_h^1$ gives $Z_h^1=B_h^1\oplus(Z_h^1\cap(B_h^1)^\perp)$ with orthogonality from [F9]. The second summand has dimension $\dim(Z_h^1/B_h^1)=\dim H^1(C^*)=\dim H_1(|\mathcal T|;\mathbb R)$ by steps 3.1–2.2 and the duality proved in step 4.1. Applying the same [F10] argument to $Z_h^2=\ker\operatorname{div}$ and $B_h^2=\operatorname{curl}N_h^1\subseteq Z_h^2$ gives the asserted second harmonic space and dimension. These are discrete cohomology components, not an assertion that a continuum sequence is exact. [A1, F1, F9, F10, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
