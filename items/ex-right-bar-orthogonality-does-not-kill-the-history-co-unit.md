## Example

Construct the finite strictly unital DG category with objects $D,E$, diagonal endomorphisms $\mathbb C$, $\operatorname{Hom}(D,E)=0$, and $\operatorname{Hom}(E,D)=\mathbb C\delta$ in degree one. For $U=D\oplus E$ with twisted differential $\delta$, show that the right-bar map $Y_D\to Y_U$ has cone quasi-isomorphic to $Y_E$, while $\operatorname{Hom}(E,U)$ is acyclic and $\operatorname{Hom}(E,D)[1]$ retains a nonzero class. Show that the full co-unit certificate $r,s$ does not exist. Extend this to a finite two-block history complex and calculate the nonzero residual $w=-\pi_WJh_P\beta$ although $T_U^0=0$. This finite algebra example demonstrates insufficiency of the proposed inference; it does not refute the geometric generation theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The cohomological DG and right-module conventions in the preceding definition. All complexes and morphisms are over $\mathbb C$.

[F1] [[def-stopped-fukaya-category-algebraic-conventions]] defines right representable modules $Y_X=\operatorname{hom}(-,X)$, finite twisted objects, cones, and the full twisted differential.

[A1] The DG category in the Statement has zero differential on its four morphism spaces before twisting; its only nonzero compositions are scalar multiplication by the two identities.

## Verification

1.1 The specified compositions are associative: a composable string containing a nonidentity $\delta:E\to D$ has no further nonzero nonidentity factor, and identities act as units. The differential is zero and obeys the Leibniz rule. Thus these data define a strictly unital DG category. The matrix $q$ with sole entry $q_{D,E}=\delta$ is strictly upper triangular and satisfies $q^2=0$, so $U=(D\oplus E,q)$ is a finite twisted object. The inclusion $\varepsilon:D\to U$ is closed because $q$ vanishes on the $D$-summand. The normalized bar over the full subcategory generated by $D$ has no nonidentity letters, since $\operatorname{End}(D)=\mathbb C1_D$; its evaluation map is therefore $Y_\varepsilon:Y_D\to Y_U$. [F1, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 At $E$, the right representable $Y_U(E)=\operatorname{Hom}(E,U)$ has basis $a:E\to E\hookrightarrow U$ in degree zero and $\varepsilon\delta$ in degree one, with differential $da=\varepsilon\delta$. Hence it is acyclic. Also $\operatorname{Hom}(E,D)[1]=\mathbb C\delta[1]$ is concentrated in degree zero with zero differential, so its class is nonzero. [A1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 A degree-zero map $r:U\to D$ is multiplication by some $\lambda\in\mathbb C$ on the $D$-summand and is zero on the $E$-summand, since $\operatorname{Hom}^0(E,D)=0$. The twisted Hom differential is $dr=-r q=-\lambda\delta$. Thus $dr=0$ forces $\lambda=0$, whereas $r\varepsilon=1_D$ forces $\lambda=1$; no closed $r$ can satisfy the co-unit equation. Moreover $\operatorname{Hom}^{-1}(D,D)=0$, so there is no $s$ with $1_D-r\varepsilon=ds$ that repairs this failure. [F1, A1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The bar map $Y_\varepsilon:Y_D\to Y_U$ is an objectwise chain map. At $D$ it is the identity $C\to C$, because $\operatorname{Hom}(D,U)=\mathbb C\varepsilon$; its cone is contractible. At $E$ its source is $C\delta$ in degree one and it sends $\delta$ to $\varepsilon\delta$. The cone contains the degree-zero cycle $(a,-\delta[1])$, since the two differential contributions are both $\varepsilon\delta$ and cancel. Projection $\pi:U\to E$ is closed, and the induced cone map sends this cycle to $1_E$. The E-evaluation cone has no other cohomology: its only differential pairs the source copy of $\delta[1]$ with $\varepsilon\delta$, leaving the class of $a-\delta[1]$. Consequently the cone map is a quasi-isomorphism at E and D, hence a quasi-isomorphism of these finite right modules. The cone is represented by $E$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 In the quotient that makes $D$ contractible, adjoin $\epsilon_D\in\operatorname{Hom}^{-1}(D,D)$ with $d\epsilon_D=1_D$. The element $v=a-\varepsilon\epsilon_D\delta$ has degree zero and $ dv=\varepsilon\delta-\varepsilon(d\epsilon_D)\delta=0. $ Since $\pi\varepsilon=0$ and $\pi a=1_E$, one has $\pi v=1_E$. The inverse-letter word therefore retains the identity of $E$, even though the ordinary old-$U$ test is acyclic. The calculation proves that finite quotient words and right-side acyclicity alone do not supply the missing full co-unit nullhomotopy. No nontrivial quotient word can occur in $\operatorname{Hom}(E,E)$: every word through $D$ would require a nonzero final morphism $D\to E$, which does not exist. Thus $1_E$ remains nonboundary in the quotient. This calculation asserts neither existence nor nonexistence of an additional geometric primitive. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Form a finite upper history complex $Q_U^H=Q_U^0\oplus W$, where $Q_U^0=\operatorname{Hom}(E,U)=\mathbb C a^0\to\mathbb C(\varepsilon\delta)^1$ is the ordinary acyclic complex from step 2.1, $d_0a=\varepsilon\delta$, and the added localization-word space $W=\mathbb C v^0$ has $d_W=0$, with zero off-diagonal block $B=0$. The contraction $h_U(\varepsilon\delta)=a,\ h_U(a)=0$ retracts $Q_U^0$ to $T_U=0$, so $T_U^0=0$. Take $Q_P^H=\mathbb C c^{-1}\xrightarrow{d_P}\mathbb C\beta^0$, $d_Pc=\beta,\ d_P\beta=0$, with $h_P\beta=c,\ h_Pc=0$; direct substitution gives $d_Ph_P+h_Pd_P=1$. Define the degree-one map $J:Q_P^H\to Q_U^H$ by $Jc=v,\ J\beta=0$. It obeys $d_UJ+Jd_P=0$ because $d_Uv=0=J\beta$. Hence the full differential $D(a,b)=(d_Ua+Jb,d_Pb)$ squares to zero. Then $F=(0,\beta)$ is a closed full-block arrow, and $D(0,c)=(v,\beta)$ gives $F-D(0,c)=(-v,0)$. Its lower contraction gives the upper residual $ w=-\pi_WJh_P\beta=-v. $ The class of $v$ is nonzero in $H^0(W)$ because $W^{-1}=0$ and $v$ is a nonzero basis vector. Thus the actual transfer formulas admit a finite chain-level model with acyclic ordinary upper test and $T_U^0=0$, but nonzero history residual and no $z\in W^{-1}$ with $d_Wz=w$. This construction is an algebraic countermodel to the implication, not a geometric history count. It leaves every separate geometric primitive-count obligation unresolved. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The finite operation/history and geometric counted-primitive problems motivate this example but supply no premise of its calculations. Those geometric constructions remain separate unresolved obligations. The finite DG and two-block examples prove only the stated failure of an algebraic inference; they do not refute a geometric generation theorem.
