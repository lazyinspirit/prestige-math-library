## Example

Let
$$ L_1=\{a^n b^n:n\ge 0\},\qquad L_2=\{c^n d^n:n\ge 0\}. $$
Standard grammars for them are
$$ S_1\to aS_1b\mid\varepsilon,\qquad S_2\to cS_2d\mid\varepsilon. $$

Then:

- $L_1\cup L_2$ is generated by $S\to S_1\mid S_2$,
- $L_1L_2$ is generated by $S\to S_1S_2$,
- $L_1^*$ is generated by $S\to \varepsilon\mid S_1S$,
- if $h(a)=0$ and $h(b)=1$, then $\widehat h(L_1)=\{0^n1^n:n\ge 0\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two grammars for $L_1$ and $L_2$, and the alphabet map with
$h(a)=0$ and $h(b)=1$.

[L1] Context-free languages are closed under union, concatenation, Kleene star,
and homomorphism, by
[[thm-cfl-closure-under-union-concatenation-star-and-homomorphism]].

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The bridge rules $S\to S_1\mid S_2$ and $S\to S_1S_2$ give the listed grammars for the union and concatenation constructions, and the rule $S\to \varepsilon\mid S_1S$ lists any finite concatenation of words from $L_1$. [given]

1.2 Replacing each terminal $a$ by $0$ and each terminal $b$ by $1$ turns the grammar for $L_1$ into one for $\{0^n1^n:n\ge 0\}$, which is exactly $\widehat h(L_1)$. [given]

2.1 These concrete constructions realize all four closure operations asserted in [L1]. [L1, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
