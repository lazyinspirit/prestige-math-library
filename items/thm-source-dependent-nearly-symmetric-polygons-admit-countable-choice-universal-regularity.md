## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the actual polygon map charts and nearly symmetric target geometry of the two named suppliers, with a fixed finite list of boundary labels and corners. Work over a finite-dimensional smooth open source/conformal/auxiliary parameter space $B$ of dimension $d$, with all neck lengths finite. Let $K_{b,z}$ be a smooth nearly symmetric coefficient family, equal to the specified transverse end structures near corner images, with uniform coefficient bounds on compact parameter subsets. Choose disjoint closed protected target neighborhoods $D,N$, with $D$ containing the diagonal and all chosen incidence divisors and $N$ a neighborhood of all the boundary tori and corner charts. The family is integrable on a neighborhood of $D$ and is tame elsewhere. Perturbations below fix neighborhoods of BOTH $D$ and $N$.

There is an explicitly constructed separable real Hilbert perturbation ball whose coefficient map is smooth into every fixed smooth compact-source norm, decays with every derivative faster than any exponential on each strip end, and has dense tangent fields on each compact subset of $B\times S\times(X\setminus(D\cup N))$. At EVERY actual nonconstant solution its universal Cauchy–Riemann derivative is onto on the actual $H^3_\delta\to H^2_\delta$ realizations. Consequently a residual set in this ball gives regular universal nonconstant polygon moduli of dimension $\mu+d$. In particular the spaces of negative such dimension are empty. When $d=0$ these are fixed-data regularity assertions; when $d=1$ slice counts are taken at regular parameter values, while the parameter is included in the universal dimension.

The construction can be restricted to an arbitrary open parameter region by multiplying its bumps by smooth parameter cutoffs, with universal surjectivity wherever those cutoffs are positive. It retains the chosen end limits and exact coefficients near every corner image; it does not claim that an infinite sum of source bumps is globally equal to the end data outside one common compact source subset. Nor does it claim regularity of constant polygons, compactness, coherent extension over nodal conformal faces, or count identities. Constant polygons must be treated or excluded separately in the application.

## Facts & Assumptions

The local similarity proof gives unique continuation for its resulting smooth real-linear equation. ## Proof

1.1 Choose countably many relatively compact rational coordinate patches in the source-parameter family and target free region. On each use rational smooth bump approximations to matrix fields and project their values to endomorphisms anti-commuting with $K$ by $A\mapsto(A+KAK)/2$. Include every rational smaller support and every constant rational matrix; smooth finite partitions and uniform polynomial approximation on compact rectangles show that their finite spans are dense in each required fixed $C^r$ norm for compactly supported anti-$K$ fields. Enumerate them as $A_j$. Choose a smooth positive pointwise safe radius $\varepsilon(b,z)$ below the Cayley taming radius for $K_{b,z}$: on each relatively compact rational source-parameter patch the target is compact and gives a positive minimum radius, and a locally finite subordinate partition with smaller positive constants gives such a function. Include the global seminorm $\sup|A_j|/\varepsilon$, finite for each of these compactly supported fields, among the constraints below. Choose positive numbers $c_j$ explicitly so $\sum_j c_j^2\|A_j\|_{C^r(K_r)}^2<\infty$ for every member of a fixed countable compact exhaustion and every derivative order. Also require the same inequality after multiplying end derivatives by $e^{r s}$, for each positive integer $r$; each individual bump has compact source support so these are finite constraints. For example divide $2^{-j}$ by one plus the maximum of the first $j$ such seminorms of $A_j$. Cauchy–Schwarz now makes $B_a=\sum_j c_ja_jA_j$, $a\in\ell^2$, converge with all these derivatives. Its finite tangents still have the stated density because each $c_j$ is nonzero. The actual structures $J_a=K(I+B_a)(I-B_a)^{-1}$ square to minus identity since $B_aK=-KB_a$. On a sufficiently small ball this formula and its matrix Neumann inverse are smooth and tame, with exactly the original protected values and rapid end decay. The argument on a noncompact parameter space uses a locally finite compact exhaustion; at each point only the estimates from its compact neighborhood are needed. [given, F1, construct, algebra]

2.1 At a smooth solution write $P_0:H^1_\delta\to L^2_\delta$ for its actual geometric Fredholm operator and $P_2:H^3_\delta\to H^2_\delta$ for the higher realization. The parameter derivative $T$ takes values in $H^2_\delta$: its local expression is $(\partial_aJ[b])du\circ j$, with the harmless target-coordinate convention, and the coefficients have the rapid bounds of step1.1 while the map has the smooth weighted tails in [F1]. Both augmented ranges are closed. Indeed modulo the finite-dimensional cokernel of $P_i$ their image is a linear subspace of a finite-dimensional vector space, hence closed; its inverse image under the quotient is precisely the augmented range. They are onto at level zero if and only if they are onto at level two: for a higher target subtract its parameter contribution and apply [F1]'s actual higher range regularity; conversely the closed lower range contains the dense higher targets if the higher operator is onto. Thus it suffices to exclude actual $L^2$ annihilators of both $P_0$ and $T$. This is the finite-dimensional quotient argument, not an identification of an $H^2$-dual distribution with a function. [F1, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Here is the local adjoint argument in the present polygon scope. Conjugate the weight and take a smooth interior real frame and metric matrix $G$. If $\eta\in L^2$ annihilates $P_0$, compactly supported interior tests give for $v=G\eta$ the distributional equation $v_s+J^{\mathsf T}v_t+(\partial_tJ^{\mathsf T}-A^{\mathsf T})v=0$, where $P=\partial_s+J\partial_t+A$. The coefficients are smooth on each interior patch. As proved locally in [F3], $\Phi=(I-J^{\mathsf T}i)/2$ reduces its principal structure to $i$ on a smaller patch. A compactly supported $L^2$ solution of $D_0w=f\in L^2$ has $H^1$ weak derivatives by convolution, the exact identity $\|D_0w\|_2^2=\|w_s\|_2^2+\|w_t\|_2^2$ and bounded-test Hilbert representation. Nested cutoffs repeat this to every order, since its lower-order coefficients are smooth. Consequently $\eta$ is smooth in the source interior. This proof is entirely local and uses no strip adjoint boundary condition. The actual unique-continuation theorem in [F3] then implies that a nonzero such section cannot vanish on an open subset of the connected source. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 By [F2] the nonconstant solution has an open source-interior region with nonzero derivative and free target image. In holomorphic source coordinates its $t$ derivative is nonzero there whenever the differential is nonzero. If the obstruction of step3.1 is nonzero, unique continuation gives a point in that open set where it is nonzero. At that point anti-$J$ endomorphisms send the nonzero vector $u_t$ to an arbitrary target vector. Explicitly on the real two-plane spanned by $u_t,Ju_t$ prescribe $C u_t=v$ and $CJu_t=-Jv$, and take zero on its $J$-invariant orthogonal complement for the metric $g+g(J\cdot,J\cdot)$. This is a finite-dimensional smooth construction near the point. The derivative of the small Cayley chart in step1.1 is a pointwise isomorphism from anti-$K$ fields to tangent anti-$J$ fields: at zero it is $C\mapsto2KC$, and its inverse persists by the finite-matrix Neumann bound. Choose the corresponding localized field so its pullback pairing with $\eta$ is positive on a small source disk, and multiply by a nonnegative bump there. It is allowed to depend on the source point itself, so no other target preimage contributes. Its target support can remain in the free region. Density of the actual finite parameter tangents in step1.1 makes one such tangent have nonzero total pairing as well, contradicting annihilation of $T$. Hence no nonzero obstruction exists. The universal derivative is onto at both actual Sobolev levels by step2.1. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The universal zero set of NONCONSTANT maps is an actual Hilbert manifold. Nonconstancy is open in a neighborhood of any such map because one fixed pair of its source values is unequal and evaluation is continuous in $H^3$. For the split inverse, start with the bounded kernel-complement inverse of the fixed Fredholm operator; select finitely many parameter vectors whose images span its finite cokernel, and use their finite-dimensional inverse to remove that cokernel. This gives an explicit bounded right inverse for the universal derivative. [F4] gives the zero chart. Projection to the Hilbert perturbation ball is Fredholm of index $\mu+d$: its kernel is the fixed-parameter kernel, and its image is the inverse image of the negative fixed finite-family cokernel under the parameter quotient map, exactly the finite block calculation in step2.1. Thus the difference of kernel dimension and image codimension equals the fixed finite-family Fredholm index from [F1]. The configuration space and coefficient space are second-countable and separable, so their open zero submanifold is too. Countably many source charts suffice; index values are integers and locally constant, giving only countably many index strata. [F1, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Apply the actual Hilbert Sard–Smale theorem [F4] to this projection on each index stratum and to the finitely or countably many fixed corner/source charts. All maps are smooth, so its differentiability threshold is met at every finite index. Countable intersection gives a residual set of perturbations for which the nonconstant universal moduli are smooth of the asserted index. A negative-dimensional manifold is empty by its local zero chart. For a one-dimensional auxiliary parameter family the projection from each resulting finite-dimensional universal zero chart to that parameter is smooth; its regular values are the parameters at which the corresponding slice has the fixed-parameter regularity. In particular one must not subtract the parameter dimension before applying the universal dimension statement. If endpoints are fixed and regular, perform the same construction with cutoffs positive on the interior and zero on fixed endpoint collars; interior universal surjectivity is unchanged, and the endpoint zero charts have their ordinary boundary half-space description. This last observation requires the stated endpoint regularity rather than deriving it from a cutoff which vanishes there. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The proof applies to nonconstant solutions across ALL actual homotopy classes in these map charts; it never assumes a countable or finite classification of those classes. All density statements used explicit countable rational fields and the actual smoothing series, and all inverses were finite-dimensional or the Countable Choice Hilbert suppliers. Coefficient variations are fixed on whole target neighborhoods of the tori and divisors, yet [F2] supplies their free support. Their rapid source decay preserves the compatible end LIMITS and all exact generator equations, but does not fabricate a common global compact support. Finally $du=0$ for a constant map, so its coefficient derivative is zero and step4.1 does not apply. Coherent nodal-face data, constant configurations and compactified counts remain genuine further tasks; none is inferred from this universal-regularity proof alone. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra] ∎
