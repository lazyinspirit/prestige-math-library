## Statement

Assume countable choice. In the elementary symmetric coordinates
$$\operatorname{Sym}^2(\mathbb C)\cong\mathbb C^2,\qquad \{w_1,w_2\}\longmapsto(a,b)=(w_1+w_2,w_1w_2),$$
there is a smooth family $u_\tau:\overline{B_4(0)}\to\mathbb C^2$, $-1/64\le\tau\le1/64$, constant with value $(0,-1)$ on a fixed boundary collar, with these properties. For $\tau<0$ it misses the diagonal $\Delta=\{a^2-4b=0\}$. For $\tau>0$ it has exactly two transverse intersections with $\Delta$, one positive and one negative. At $\tau=0$ they meet at the only, nontransverse intersection. Every $u_\tau$ is explicitly homotopic to the constant map relative to the boundary collar.

For $\tau\ne0$ the actual incidence surface
$$S_\tau=\{(z,w)\in\overline{B_4(0)}\times\mathbb C:w\text{ is one of the two roots of }u_\tau(z)\}$$
is a compact smooth surface with boundary. Its projection to the source is an unramified two-sheeted disk union for $\tau<0$, and a connected positive two-sheeted branched cover with two simple branch values and two boundary components for $\tau>0$. In the latter case it is topologically an annulus. At its two ramification points the root projection to $\mathbb C$ has opposite local degrees, $+1$ and $-1$, when the surface is oriented by its positive source projection.

Consequently this model can be inserted into a source disk on which a given Whitney representative is constant with two distinct coordinates in one surface coordinate disk and all remaining coordinates outside it. After scaling the model to that coordinate disk and gluing along its constant collar, the resulting representative is in exactly the same relative homotopy class, with its original boundary, generators and all other coordinates fixed. This proves an actual local paired-branch insertion. It does not realize arbitrary Whitney classes by special incidence surfaces, turn arbitrary tubes into collapsed tubes, or prove a global domain-index formula.

## Proof

**Given:** The oriented complex coordinate planes and the stated local constant-tuple insertion data. A positive source branched cover has local oriented model $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$; the root projection need not preserve that orientation.

1.1 Choose a smooth function $\beta:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ equal to one for $|s|\le1/4$ and zero for $|s|\ge1/2$, using for example normalized translates of $e^{-1/x}$ for $x>0$ and zero for $x\le0$. Put $\chi(s,t)=\beta(s)\beta(t)$ and $$D_\tau(s,t)=1+\chi(s,t)(s^2-\tau-1+it),\qquad u_\tau(s,t)=(0,-D_\tau(s,t)).$$ Its support of variation lies in $[-1/2,1/2]^2\subset B_4(0)$, so all maps equal $(0,-1)$ on the same boundary collar and depend smoothly on the parameter. Moreover $|D_\tau|\le1$: it is a convex combination of $1$ and $s^2-\tau+it$ where $|s|,|t|\le1/2$, and the latter has squared modulus at most $(17/64)^2+1/4<1$. Both roots therefore have modulus at most one. The elementary symmetric identification is actual: the polynomial with coefficients $(a,b)$ is $w^2-aw+b$, its two roots form the unordered pair, and their elementary symmetric coordinates are exactly $(a,b)$. Thus this family specifies genuine symmetric-product maps, not an ordering of square roots across a branch point. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 If $D_\tau=0$, its imaginary part $\chi t$ is zero. The case $\chi=0$ gives $D_\tau=1$, so $t=0$. If $|s|>1/4$, the real part is the convex combination $(1-\chi)+\chi(s^2-\tau)>0$, because $s^2-\tau>3/64$. If $|s|\le1/4$ and $t=0$, the cutoff is one and $D_\tau=s^2-\tau$. Hence for positive $\tau$ the only zeros are $(\pm\sqrt\tau,0)$; for negative $\tau$ there are none; and at zero there is only the origin. Near these points $D_\tau=s^2-\tau+it$, whose real Jacobian determinant at either positive-parameter zero is $2s$. The defining complex normal coordinate of $\Delta$ pulls back as $a^2-4b=4D_\tau$. Thus the intersections are transverse with the claimed opposite signs; at the origin its derivative has rank one. Multiplication by the positive real constant four changes neither transversality nor sign. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $\tau\ne0$ write the incidence equation as $w^2=D_\tau(z)$. If $w\ne0$, rotate $D_\tau$ near its value into the right half-plane and use the formula $A=\sqrt{(|d|+\operatorname{Re}d)/2}$, $B=\operatorname{Im}d/(2A)$ for $d$ in that half-plane, multiplying by the inverse half-angle phase; this gives the two ordinary smooth local graphs. At $w=0$ and either zero from step 2.1, the equation is exactly $s^2-\tau+it=w^2$. Its local inverse is $s=\pm\sqrt{\tau+\operatorname{Re}(w^2)}$, $t=\operatorname{Im}(w^2)$, with the sign of the chosen root, so $w$ is a smooth surface coordinate. At the positive-Jacobian zero, use $w$ as its oriented coordinate and $D_\tau$ as the oriented base coordinate; the projection is $w\mapsto w^2$. At the negative-Jacobian zero, use $\zeta=\overline w$ as the oriented surface coordinate and $\overline{D_\tau}$ as the oriented base coordinate, giving $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^2$. These orientations agree with the pulled-back source orientation off the two branch points, so the source projection is positive everywhere. The root projection is respectively $w=\zeta$ and $w=\overline\zeta$, with local degrees $+1,-1$. Near the physical source boundary $D_\tau=1$, so the surface is exactly the two smooth collars $w=1,w=-1$. The bound in step 1.1 and the closed equation prove compactness. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The relative homotopy is completely explicit in symmetric coordinates: $$H_{r,\tau}(z)=(0,-((1-r)+rD_\tau(z))),\qquad0\le r\le1.$$ At $r=0$ it is the constant tuple $\{1,-1\}$ and at $r=1$ it is $u_\tau$. It is constant on the same source collar for every parameter. The intermediate discriminant may vanish, which is allowed in the symmetric product; the map to its elementary symmetric coordinates is smooth there. The same convex-disk bound as in step 1.1 keeps every intermediate root in the closed unit disk. To insert this at any two distinct points in a supplied complex coordinate disk, first take its coordinate image to be the unit disk. Its automorphism $f(z)=(z-a)/(1-\overline a z)$ sends the first point $a$ to zero. Rotate so the image of the second point is $q\in(0,1)$. Put $r=q/(1+\sqrt{1-q^2})\in(0,1)$; then $q=2r/(1+r^2)$ and $h(z)=(z-r)/(1-rz)$ sends $0,q$ to $-r,r$. These are actual holomorphic disk automorphisms: their inverses are the reversed fractional formulas, and $1-|h(z)|^2=(1-r^2)(1-|z|^2)/|1-rz|^2>0$, with the analogous identity for $f$. In this new coordinate multiply every model root by $r$. All roots of the scaled homotopy have modulus at most $r<1$, and its collar is the required constant pair. Transporting back gives the desired smooth local insertion inside the original coordinate disk, without assuming additional room around the original pair. The other fixed coordinates lie outside that disk. Their product with this two-point symmetric map gives a smooth map into the full symmetric product, including the one possible double point in the moving pair. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $\tau<0$, the real part $(1-\chi)+\chi(s^2+|\tau|)$ is strictly positive. The smooth square root on that half-plane is explicit: for $d=x+iy$ with $x>0$, take $A=\sqrt{(|d|+x)/2}>0$ and $B=y/(2A)$, so $(A+iB)^2=d$. The two graphs $w=\pm(A+iB)$ therefore identify the incidence surface with two disjoint source disks. For $\tau>0$ the preimage of the nonpositive real axis under $D_\tau$ is precisely the closed source segment $I=[-\sqrt\tau,\sqrt\tau]\times\{0\}$. This follows from the same imaginary-part argument as step 2.1 and its real sign calculation on $t=0$. On the complement of $I$ the principal square root is smooth. Its two values give the two sheets, which glue crosswise over the two banks of $I$, as the local power charts of step 3.1 show. A small loop about either endpoint interchanges the sheets, so this surface is connected. Its boundary has two components, since its two boundary collars have the globally constant roots $1,-1$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 The slit description also proves the annulus assertion directly, without classifying arbitrary surfaces. Write $c=\sqrt\tau$. A disk of radius four cut open along $[-c,c]$ is an annulus. Explicit coordinates for this cut disk are $$z=c\cosh(\rho+i\theta),\qquad \theta\in\mathbb R/2\pi\mathbb Z,\quad0\le\rho\le R(\theta),$$ where $R(\theta)=\operatorname{arsinh}\sqrt{16/c^2-\cos^2\theta}>0$. Indeed $|z|^2=c^2(\sinh^2\rho+\cos^2\theta)$; for $\rho>0$ the map is one-to-one onto the disk minus its slit, as is verified by solving $e^{\rho+i\theta}+e^{-\rho-i\theta}=2z/c$ and choosing the unique root of modulus greater than one. At $\rho=0$ its circle parametrizes the two slit banks, with the two tips retained as distinct points of that boundary circle. Replacing $\rho$ by $\rho/R(\theta)$ exhibits the cut disk as a topological annulus. The two copies in step 4.1 are glued along their full inner circles by the bank exchange, which sends $\theta$ to $-\theta$. Give their radial parameters opposite signs and reverse the angular coordinate on one copy. Their union is $S^1\times[-1,1]$. The tips are smooth in the actual surface by step 3.1; this last coordinate argument only establishes its topology. [step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Insert that scaled map on the specified interior source disk and retain the old representative outside it. The constant collars make both the inserted map and the homotopy of step 3.2 glue smoothly to the old map. This homotopy is stationary on the original physical boundary, on both end generators and on every other coordinate, so it is an actual relative homotopy of Whitney representatives. The represented class is therefore unchanged by its defining equivalence relation. Reversing the homotopy removes the pair. The two diagonal intersections have total signed number zero by step 2.1, while step 5.1 describes the genuine local topological change in the incidence surface. No global normal-form or collapsed-tube assertion has been deduced from this local operation. All constructions are finite formulas and local inverses, so they need no stronger choice axiom than the stated one. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra] ∎
