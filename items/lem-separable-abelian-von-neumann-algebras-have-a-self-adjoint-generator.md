## Statement

Assume AC. Let $H$ be a nonzero separable complex Hilbert space and let
$\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ be an abelian von Neumann algebra (so its
elements commute pairwise). Then there is a bounded self-adjoint
$S\in\mathcal A$ such that
$$\mathcal A=W^*(S).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Separability means that some at most countable subset is dense. In the library, “at most countable” means finite or countably infinite. Since $H$ is nonzero, a dense subset is nonempty; its finite or countable listing, with one point repeated in the finite case, gives a dense sequence ([[def-separable-space]], [[def-countable]]).

[F2] Weak operator tests are the scalar functionals $T\mapsto f(Tx)$ ([[def-strong-and-weak-operator-topologies]]). Under the first-variable-linear inner-product convention, Riesz representation under Countable Choice writes each bounded linear $f$ as $f(v)=\langle v,y\rangle$ for some $y\in H$ ([[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], [[thm-riesz-representation-for-hilbert-space]], [[def-countable-choice]]). Thus WOT on $\mathcal B(H)$ is generated by the matrix coefficients $T\mapsto\langle Tx,y\rangle$.

[F3] The topology of $\mathbb C$ is the $d_2$ topology on $\mathbb R^2$; the product topology there is the $d_2$ topology, and rational open boxes give a countable basis. Hence $\mathbb C$ is second countable ([[def-complex-metric-convergence-and-continuity]], [[lem-product-topology-on-rn]], [[thm-rational-points-and-boxes-in-rn]], [[def-second-countable-space]]).

[F4] There is a bijection $\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\times\mathbb N$ ([[thm-n-cross-n-countable]]). Under Countable Choice, a countable product of second-countable spaces is second countable ([[def-product-topology]], [[thm-countable-products-of-second-countable-spaces]]), and every subspace of a second-countable space is second countable ([[prop-second-countability-is-hereditary]]). AC supplies the Countable Choice hypothesis by [[def-axiom-of-choice]] and [[def-countable-choice]].

[F5] $\mathcal A$ is a unital star-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(H)$ closed in WOT; “abelian” means that its elements commute pairwise. Also $W^*(S)$ is the WOT closure of the unital star-algebra generated by $S$ ([[def-von-neumann-algebra-and-commutant]]).

[F6] The operator norm is subadditive and absolutely homogeneous, as follows from its unit-ball supremum definition ([[def-operator-norm]]).

[F7] A Hilbert space is Banach ([[def-hilbert-space]]), so $\mathcal B(H)$ is Banach by [[thm-bounded-operator-space-is-banach]]. Under composition it is a nonzero unital complex Banach algebra: composition is associative and submultiplicative ([[lem-composition-operator-norm-inequality]]), the bounded-operator space has pointwise linear operations ([[def-space-of-bounded-linear-operators]]), $I_H$ is its unit and has norm $1$ by the operator-norm definition on nonzero $H$ ([[def-operator-norm]]), and $I_H\ne0$. The operator spectrum is the spectrum in $\mathcal B(H)$: by the two definitions, $\lambda I-T$ has a bounded everywhere-defined inverse exactly when it is invertible as an element of $\mathcal B(H)$ ([[def-spectrum-and-resolvent-of-a-bounded-operator]], [[def-spectrum-and-resolvent-set-in-a-banach-algebra]]). Thus if $T$ is self-adjoint, $\sigma(T)\subseteq\mathbb R$ ([[lem-spectrum-of-a-self-adjoint-operator-is-real]]) and $\sigma(T)$ lies in the closed disk of radius $\|T\|$ by the Banach-algebra spectral bound ([[def-unital-banach-algebra]], [[thm-spectrum-is-nonempty-compact-and-norm-bounded]]).

[F8] The continuous self-adjoint calculus is isometric, sends the coordinate function to $T$, and has range $C^*(I,T)$ ([[thm-continuous-functional-calculus-for-bounded-self-adjoint-operators]]). For normal $T$, $C^*(I,T)$ is the norm closure of the unital star-algebra generated by $T$ ([[def-c-star-algebra-generated-by-a-normal-operator]]).

[F9] For bounded normal $T$, the Borel calculus extends the continuous calculus, is a unital star-homomorphism, sends $\mathbf 1_B$ to the spectral projection $E(B)$, has $\|f(T)\|\le\|f\|_\infty$, and uniformly bounded pointwise spectral-a.e. convergent Borel functions converge in SOT ([[thm-borel-functional-calculus-for-bounded-normal-operators]]). A spectral PVM takes values in orthogonal projections, which are contractive ([[def-projection-valued-measure]]). SOT is stronger than WOT, and norm convergence implies WOT convergence, by the operator-topology definitions and boundedness of each WOT functional ([[def-strong-and-weak-operator-topologies]]).

[F10] Real polynomials uniformly approximate every continuous real function on a closed interval ([[cor-weierstrass-approximation-on-a-closed-interval]]).

[F11] AC is the choice-function axiom and in particular applies to any family of nonempty basic open sets ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F12] Hilbert adjoints obey $(R+T)^*=R^*+T^*$ and $(aR)^*=\overline a R^*$, with $\|R^*\|=\|R\|$ ([[thm-hilbert-adjoint-properties]]).

[F13] Composition of bounded operators satisfies $\|RT\|\le\|R\|\,\|T\|$ ([[lem-composition-operator-norm-inequality]]).

[F14] Every self-adjoint bounded operator is normal ([[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-operator]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** AC, a nonzero separable complex Hilbert space $H$, and an abelian von Neumann algebra $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$.

1.1 By [F1], choose a dense sequence $(x_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ in $H$. On the operator unit ball $\mathcal B_1(H)$ consider the coefficient map $$\Phi(T)=(\langle T x_n,x_m\rangle)_{(n,m)\in\mathbb N^2}. $$ Its coordinates are WOT-continuous by [F2]. Conversely, they induce the WOT on this ball: if $T,U\in\mathcal B_1(H)$, then $\|U-T\|\le2$, and for dense sequence approximants $x_i\to x$, $x_j\to y$, $$|\langle (U-T)x,y\rangle-\langle (U-T)x_i,x_j\rangle| \le2\|x-x_i\|\,\|y\|+2\|x_i\|\,\|y-x_j\|.$$ Thus every finite matrix-coefficient neighborhood can be refined by one involving only the countable family of coordinates of $\Phi$. [F2, F6]

1.2 Fix $k\ge1$. For a digit string $\eta=(\eta_1,\ldots,\eta_k)\in \{0,1\}^k$, set $$s_\eta:=\sum_{j=1}^k3^{-j}\eta_j,\qquad I_\eta:=[s_\eta,s_\eta+\tfrac12 3^{-k}].$$ These finitely many intervals lie in $[0,1/2]$ and are pairwise separated. Indeed, if two strings first differ at index $\ell\le k$, the difference of their left endpoints has absolute value at least $$3^{-\ell}-\sum_{j=\ell+1}^k3^{-j} =\tfrac12 3^{-\ell}+\tfrac12 3^{-k};$$ subtracting their common interval length $\tfrac12 3^{-k}$ leaves a gap at least $\tfrac12 3^{-\ell}>0$. Assign value $\eta_k$ on $I_\eta$; define $f_k$ linearly across each gap between consecutive intervals, with endpoint values inherited from those intervals, and constantly on each exterior interval with the value at the adjacent endpoint. This defines a continuous real piecewise-linear function on $[-1/2,1/2]$. Its values on $[0,1/2]$ are thus $0$ or $1$ on the cylinder intervals according to the $k$-th digit. [construct]

2.1 By [F3, F4], the product $\mathbb C^{\mathbb N^2}$ is second countable. Pulling its countable basis back by $\Phi$ gives a countable basis for $\mathcal B_1(H)$, because step 1.1 shows that its initial topology is the relative WOT. Its subspace $\mathcal A_1:=\{T\in\mathcal A:\|T\|\le1\}$ is second countable by [F4]. Fix a countable basis of $\mathcal A_1$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 For each nonempty member of this basis, AC [F11] selects one operator in that member; for an empty member put $0$. Enumerating the countable basis, and repeating an entry if it is finite, gives a sequence $(T_j)_{j\in\mathbb N}$ WOT-dense in $\mathcal A_1$. Indeed every nonempty relatively open subset contains a basis member and hence a selected point. [F5, F11, step 2.1]

4.1 For $j\in\mathbb N$ define $$X_{j,0}:=\frac{T_j+T_j^*}{2},\qquad X_{j,1}:=\frac{T_j-T_j^*}{2i}.$$ Because $\mathcal A$ is a star-subalgebra, both lie in $\mathcal A$; [F6, F12] show they are self-adjoint contractions, and $T_j=X_{j,0}+iX_{j,1}$. All these operators commute because $\mathcal A$ is abelian. [F5, F6, F12, step 3.1]

5.1 For each $X=X_{j,\tau}$ and real threshold $q$, let $$P_X(q):=E_X((q,\infty)).$$ This projection belongs to $\mathcal A$. To see this directly, on $\sigma(X)\subseteq[-1,1]$ set $$g_m(t):=\min\{1,m\max\{t-q,0\}\}.$$ Each $g_m$ is continuous, lies between $0$ and $1$, and converges pointwise to $\mathbf1_{(q,\infty)}$, including value $0$ at $t=q$. By [F8], $g_m(X)$ lies in the norm closure of the unital star-algebra generated by $X$, hence in $\mathcal A$: norm convergence implies WOT convergence and $\mathcal A$ is WOT-closed by [F5], [F9]. By [F9], $g_m(X)$ converges strongly, therefore weakly, to $P_X(q)$; WOT-closedness gives $P_X(q)\in\mathcal A$. The spectral bound $\sigma(X)\subseteq[-1,1]$ is [F7]. [F5, F7, F8, F9, step 4.1]

6.1 For $n\in\mathbb N$ and $0\le\ell\le2^n$ put $$r_{n,\ell}:=-2+4\ell 2^{-n}.$$ The projections $P_{X_{j,\tau}}(r_{n,\ell})$, indexed by $(j,\tau,n,\ell)$, form an explicitly countable family: repeated pairing from [F4] enumerates $\mathbb N^4$, and invalid $\tau$ or $\ell$ indices may be assigned the zero projection. List the family, allowing repetitions, as $(P_k)_{k\ge1}$. Every $P_k$ is in $\mathcal A$ by step 5.1, so these projections commute pairwise. [F4, F5, step 5.1]

7.1 Fix $X=X_{j,\tau}$. For each $n$, the intervals $(r_{n,\ell-1},r_{n,\ell}]$, $1\le\ell\le2^n$, partition $(-2,2]$ and cover $\sigma(X)$. Their spectral projections are $$Q_{n,\ell}=P_X(r_{n,\ell-1})-P_X(r_{n,\ell}).$$ The step function taking value $r_{n,\ell-1}$ on this interval differs from the identity function by at most $4\cdot2^{-n}$ on $\sigma(X)$. The norm formula for the Borel calculus in [F9] therefore gives $$\left\|X-\sum_{\ell=1}^{2^n}r_{n,\ell-1}Q_{n,\ell}\right\| \le4\cdot2^{-n}.$$ Each approximant is a finite linear combination of the listed projections, so every $X_{j,\tau}$ is a norm, hence WOT, limit of such combinations. [F7, F9, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1]

7.2 Define $$S_N:=\sum_{k=1}^N3^{-k}P_k,\qquad S:=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}3^{-k}P_k.$$ By [F9], $\|P_k\|\le1$. Since $\sum_{k\ge1}3^{-k}=1/2$, the series converges in operator norm. Each $S_N$ is self-adjoint and belongs to $\mathcal A$; its norm limit $S$ is self-adjoint and belongs to $\mathcal A$ by WOT-closedness. Also $\|S_N\|,\|S\|\le1/2$. [F5, F6, F9, F12, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Let $\mathcal C$ be the unital star-algebra generated by $(P_k)$. It is contained in $\mathcal A$, and its WOT closure is contained in $\mathcal A$ by [F5]. The WOT closure of a linear subspace is linear because addition and scalar multiplication are WOT-continuous. Step 7.1 puts every $X_{j,\tau}$ in that closure, hence every $T_j=X_{j,0}+iX_{j,1}$ there. Since $(T_j)$ is WOT-dense in $\mathcal A_1$ by step 3.1, the closure contains $\mathcal A_1$. Every $T\in\mathcal A$ is a scalar multiple of an element of $\mathcal A_1$ (with $T=0$ immediate), so $\overline{\mathcal C}^{\mathrm{WOT}}=\mathcal A$. [F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 7.1]

8.2 For $N\ge k$ and $\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^N$, put $$R_{j,1}=P_j,\quad R_{j,0}=I-P_j,\qquad Q_\epsilon:=\prod_{j=1}^N R_{j,\epsilon_j}.$$ The $P_j$ commute, so the $Q_\epsilon$ are pairwise orthogonal projections whose sum is $I$, and $$S_N=\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^N} \left(\sum_{j=1}^N3^{-j}\epsilon_j\right)Q_\epsilon.$$ For every polynomial $p$, the finite orthogonal resolution gives $p(S_N)=\sum_\epsilon p(\sum_{j=1}^N3^{-j}\epsilon_j)Q_\epsilon$. Uniform polynomial approximation and the isometric calculus [F8], [F10] pass this identity to $f_k$, so $$f_k(S_N)=\sum_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^N} f_k\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^N3^{-j}\epsilon_j\right)Q_\epsilon=P_k,$$ because the scalar sum belongs to $I_{(\epsilon_1,\ldots,\epsilon_k)}$ and the function has value $\epsilon_k$ there. The last equality uses $\sum_{\epsilon:\epsilon_k=1}Q_\epsilon=P_k$. [F5, F8, F10, step 6.1, step 7.2, step 1.2]

8.3 The continuous calculus values converge in norm: $$f_k(S_N)\longrightarrow f_k(S).$$ For any $\delta>0$, [F10] gives a real polynomial $p$ with $\sup_{[-1/2,1/2]}|f_k-p|<\delta$. By [F7, F8], the spectra of $S_N$ and $S$ lie in this interval and the calculus is isometric, so each of $\|f_k(S_N)-p(S_N)\|$ and $\|f_k(S)-p(S)\|$ is below $\delta$. Since $S_N\to S$ in norm and multiplication is norm-continuous by [F13], $p(S_N)\to p(S)$; addition and scalar multiplication are norm-continuous by [F6]. Letting $\delta\downarrow0$ proves the convergence. [F6, F7, F8, F10, F13, step 7.2, step 1.2]

9.1 Steps 8.2 and 8.3 show $P_k=f_k(S)$ for every $k$. The operator $S$ is self-adjoint and hence normal by [F14]; by [F8], $f_k(S)\in C^*(I,S)$, which is contained in the norm closure of the unital star-algebra generated by $S$ and therefore in $W^*(S)$. Hence $P_k\in W^*(S)$. [F5, F8, F14, step 8.2, step 8.3]

10.1 By [F5], $W^*(S)$ is the WOT closure of the unital star-algebra $D$ generated by $S$. This closure is itself a unital star-algebra: adjoint and each fixed left or right multiplication are WOT-continuous, as their matrix coefficients are WOT tests from [F2] and the adjoint identity [F12]. If $x,y\in\overline D^{\mathrm{WOT}}$, first approximate $y$ by elements of $D$ with left multiplication fixed at any $d\in D$ to get $dy\in\overline D^{\mathrm{WOT}}$; then approximate $x$ with right multiplication fixed at $y$ to get $xy\in\overline D^{\mathrm{WOT}}$. The adjoint map preserves the closure by the same continuity, and $I\in D$. Hence $W^*(S)$ is a WOT-closed unital star-algebra. It contains every $P_k$ by step 9.1, so it contains $\mathcal C$ and its WOT closure $\mathcal A$ by step 8.1. Conversely, $S\in\mathcal A$ by step 7.2 and $\mathcal A$ is a WOT-closed unital star-algebra, so the WOT closure of the unital star-algebra generated by $S$ is contained in $\mathcal A$. Thus $\mathcal A=W^*(S)$, with $S$ bounded and self-adjoint as required. [F2, F5, F12, step 8.1, step 7.2, step 9.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The nonzero hypothesis is required by the continuous and Borel spectral calculus suppliers. One-dimensional $H$ and the scalar algebra $\mathcal A=\mathbb C I$ are included: the threshold projections may be only $0$ and $I$, and the same argument still produces a generator.
- The grid $[-2,2]$ places the full spectrum inside the half-open cells $(r_{n,\ell-1},r_{n,\ell}]$, so spectral atoms at either spectral endpoint are included. Values at a threshold itself lie in the interval to its left, consistent with $P_X(q)=E_X((q,\infty))$.
- AC is used only at the declared WOT-base selection and through the exact Countable Choice qualifications recorded above; the threshold enumeration and ternary coding are explicit.
