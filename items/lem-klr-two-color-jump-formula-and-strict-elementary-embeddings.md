## Statement

Let $k$ be any field and $R(Q)$ the graded KLR algebra for a real symmetrizable
Cartan datum and any homogeneous $Q$ satisfying the standing symmetry and
nonzero extremal-coefficient conditions. Graded simple modules are compared up
to internal shift. Set $c_i(S)=\epsilon_i^L(S)$ and
$J_i(S)=\max\{n\ge0:c_i(f_i^nS)=c_i(S)\}$. This maximum is finite and
$J_i(S)=\epsilon_i(S)+c_i(S)-\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$ for
$S$ of root weight $\beta\in Q_+$. For $i\ne j$ one has $c_i(f_jS)=c_i(S)$; for
$j=i$ it stays constant or increases by $1$, and increase is equivalent to
$f_iS=f_i^LS$ and to simplicity of $\operatorname{Ind}(S,L(i))$. Once it
increases, it increases at every later right-$i$ step. Put
$\operatorname{wt}(S)=-\beta$ and $\phi_i=\epsilon_i+\langle h_i,
\operatorname{wt}\rangle$. The maps
$\Psi_i(S)=(e_i^L)^{c_i(S)}S\otimes b_i(-c_i(S))$ are strict injective
elementary-crystal maps, and every nonvacuum simple has positive extraction at
some $i$. The simple graph is lowering-generated with every generic $f_i$
nonzero. In any first-strand cyclotomic quotient containing $S$ the actual
surviving lowering length is
$\lambda_i+\epsilon_i(S)-\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$. All containing-weight
arguments take place in the auxiliary primitive-coroot finite-support datum,
leaving $R,A,D,Q$ unchanged, so no positive-dominant-label assumption on the
original $P$ is added. Linear divided restriction/$G_0$ operators satisfy the
quantum Serre relation; the simple-socle crystal operators are not asserted to
satisfy a linear Serre identity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A graded KLR algebra $R$ for a real symmetrizable Cartan datum, a graded simple $R(\beta)$-module $S$, and the simple socle/head operators $e_i,f_i$ of [[lem-klr-one-step-simple-restriction-induction-and-first-dot-index]]; write $\epsilon_i,\epsilon_i^L$ for the right and first-end tail lengths.

[F1] One-step results of [[lem-klr-one-step-simple-restriction-induction-and-first-dot-index]]: $e_iS$ has simple socle and head once with $\epsilon_i=r-1$; $\operatorname{Ind}(S\boxtimes L(i^n))$ has a unique simple head $f_i^nS$ occurring once, with $\epsilon_i=r+n$; the one-step socle and head are partial inverses; the maximal-tail restriction/induction statements of [[lem-klr-maximal-tail-restriction-and-induction-have-a-unique-simple-head]] hold, with exact block induction left adjoint to block restriction and restriction left adjoint to the shifted reversed-block coinduction ([[thm-klr-induction-and-restriction-are-biadjoint-and-preserve-projectives]]).

[F2] Finite-support regularization ([[lem-finite-support-regularization-of-quantum-label-weight-modules]]): for every finite containing-weight argument one restricts to the finite colour support of the modules and the selected strings; the auxiliary primitive coroots carry independently prescribed nonnegative labels $\lambda_l$, and $A,D,Q$ and all KLR module, shuffle, restriction and induction calculations are unchanged.

[F3] Finite graded modules have the Krull-Schmidt property and semisimple socles; simple graded modules are absolutely simple and finite dimensional ([[lem-klr-module-categories-are-krull-schmidt-with-shift-orbit-bases]], [[lem-klr-maximal-tail-restriction-and-induction-have-a-unique-simple-head]]).

[F4] The constructive monic-position and all-$i$-tail bounds of [[lem-cyclotomic-klr-dot-annihilators-and-finite-root-strings]]: for fixed finite support, labels $\lambda$, root weight $\beta$ and colour $i$, there is a finite integer $B_i(\beta,\lambda)$ such that $R^\lambda(\beta+n\alpha_i)=0$ for every $n\ge B_i(\beta,\lambda)$.

[F5] The split projective divided-power Serre identity of [[thm-klr-projective-grothendieck-group-satisfies-the-negative-quantum-serre-relations]], and the unscaled projective/simple pairing of [F1]. Applying homogeneous Hom to that identity gives the linear divided restriction relation on finite-module classes.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** prove the two-colour structure directly with nil-Hecke columns, obtain a finite jump from a containing cyclotomic quotient, calculate its initial value by containing-weight annihilation and induction from the vacuum, and verify every elementary tensor branch.

1.1 Work on the finite support supplied by [F2], prescribing the cyclotomic labels independently there. Write $L_r=L(i^r)$, $c_i=\epsilon_i^L$, and write $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B)$ for induction of the external tensor product; all module identifications below are on shift orbits. Two consequences of exact induction will be used. A surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(A,H)\to T$ with $T$ simple induces a surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(A,U)\to T$ for some simple composition factor $U$ of $H$: take the first nonzero image in an induced composition series. If $\epsilon_i(A)=0$, a nonzero map $\operatorname{Ind}(A,U)\to T$ with $A,U,T$ simple forces $\epsilon_i(T)=\epsilon_i(U)$, since the shuffle bound gives the upper bound and adjunction injects $A\boxtimes U$ into the defining restriction, giving the lower bound. More particularly, a surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B,L_h)\to T$ with $\epsilon_i(A)=0$ factors through $\operatorname{Ind}(A,f_i^hB)\to T$: its tail is $\epsilon_i(B)+h$, whereas every constituent of the kernel of $\operatorname{Ind}(B,L_h)\to f_i^hB$ has smaller tail by [F1], so an induced composition series of that kernel has zero image in $T$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 Here is the rank-two structure needed below, including its parameter calculation. Fix $j\ne i$, put $a=-a_{ij}$ and $t=[u^a]Q_{ij}(u,v)\in k^\times$. For $p+q\le a$, define $L(i^pji^q)$ to have only the word $i^pji^q$ in its support, with column $L_p\boxtimes L(j)\boxtimes L_q$, the $j$-dot zero and both crossings adjacent to $j$ zero. All relations hold: $Q_{ij}(u,0)=tu^a$, which kills either neighbouring column, and the native $i,j,i$ braid correction is $-t\sum_{r=0}^{a-1}x^rz^{a-1-r}$. Each monomial kills the column since $r\ge p$ or $a-1-r\ge q$ when $p+q\le a$. The other mixed and braid equations have zero terms or are internal nil-Hecke equations. This module is simple by the matrix-column calculation in [F1], with right tail $q$, first tail $p$ and ungraded character $p!q!i^pji^q$. These are all simples of weight $ci+j$ when $c\le a$: induct on $c$; a simple with positive right tail is the unique $f_i$-image of its predecessor, whose tail identifies the constructed column; a simple of right tail zero is supported only on $i^cj$, and the same column argument gives $L(i^cj)$. For $c>a$ no simple can have right tail zero: such a module would again be the column $L_c\boxtimes L(j)$ supported only on $i^cj$, but the last mixed crossing has square $t x_c^a$, nonzero on $L_c$ since its dot index is $c$. This contradicts its zero crossing. [F1, F3, algebra, construct]

2.1 Adjunction and the shuffle bound give $c_i(M)\le c_i(f_jM)\le c_i(M)+\delta_{ij}$; first-end symmetry gives $\epsilon_j(f_i^LM)=\epsilon_j(M)$ for $j\ne i$. If $c_i(f_iM)=c_i(M)+1$, then $f_iM=f_i^LM$: the two orders of induction have the same character at $q=1$ and hence the same ungraded composition multiplicities, while the first order's factor with maximum first tail is uniquely $f_i^LM$, once. The character implication here follows from the triangular divided-word/projective pairing in [F1]: divided-word corner dimensions are obtained from ordinary word-corner dimensions by dividing the corresponding nil-Hecke block factorials at $q=1$, and their triangular matrix with diagonal $1$ remains invertible there. Conversely, equality of the two simple heads forces the increase. The coinduction formula in [F1], together with maximal-tail restriction, gives $\operatorname{soc}\operatorname{Ind}(M,L_h)=(f_i^L)^hM$ and $\operatorname{soc}\operatorname{Ind}(L_h,M)=f_i^hM$: coinduction has only one simple socle, since every simple subobject has the maximum tail by adjunction and its simple maximum corner detects it. Thus $f_iM=f_i^LM$ forces $\operatorname{Ind}(M,L_1)$ simple: its head and socle agree and this factor occurs once; in a nonsimple module the socle would lie in the radical and supply a second occurrence. Conversely simplicity forces its socle and head to agree. Commuting $M$ successively past the $h$ single-colour factors, and using $\operatorname{Ind}(L_r,L_s)=L_{r+s}$, gives $\operatorname{Ind}(M,L_h)=\operatorname{Ind}(L_h,M)$; its simple head equals its simple socle and occurs once, so it too is simple. Hence one increase forces increases at all later steps. [step 1.1, F1, F3, algebra]

3.1 Put $D_n=L(i^{a-n}ji^n)$ for $0\le n\le a$. The module $\operatorname{Ind}(D_n,L_1)$ has only two word corners, $i^{a-n}ji^{n+1}$ and $i^{a-n+1}ji^n$, each a simple product of nil-Hecke columns by [F1]. Its simple head has the first corner nonzero. The linear divided restriction Serre relation forces the second corner nonzero too: when applied to that head at weight $(a+1)i+j$, all terms $E_i^{(a+1-s)}E_jE_i^{(s)}$ vanish except $s=n,n+1$, and the $s=n+1$ term is nonzero. The relation is available on finite modules by applying homogeneous Hom to the split projective Serre identity; exact projective Hom and the unscaled pairing in [F1] identify these Hom spaces with the divided corners. Thus the head fills both simple corners and induction is simple, so $f_iD_n=f_i^LD_n$. Step 2.1 proves $\operatorname{Ind}(D_n,L_h)=\operatorname{Ind}(L_h,D_n)$ simple for every $h$. For $c\ge a$, induction on $c$ now classifies all simples of weight $ci+j$ as $\operatorname{Ind}(D_n,L_{c-a})$, $0\le n\le a$, with right tail $n+c-a$: step 1.2 excludes right tail zero for $c>a$, and the one-step partial inverse identifies every positive-tail simple from the preceding weight. For $a=0$, $Q_{ij}$ is a nonzero constant, the mixed crossing is invertible, and $\operatorname{Ind}(L(j),L_h)=\operatorname{Ind}(L_h,L(j))$ is the unique simple, giving the same classification. No characteristic, splitting-field or special binomial-parameter assumption was used. [step 2.1, step 1.2, F1, F5, algebra]

3.2 Define $v_i^\lambda(M)$ as the number of surviving right-$i$ lowering steps in a quotient with labels $\lambda$ containing $M$. It is finite by [F4]. Choose such labels with $\lambda_l\ge c_l(M)$, possible by [F2] and [F1]. If $c_i(f_i^nM)$ never increased, step 2.1 would keep every first tail within those labels for every $n$, contrary to [F4]. Hence $J_i(M)$ is finite; more explicitly, the bound $B_i(\beta,\lambda)$ of [F4] with $R^\lambda(\beta+B_i\alpha_i)=0$ gives $J_i(M)<B_i(\beta,\lambda)$. Step 2.1 gives, for all $h\ge0$, $c_i(f_i^hM)=c_i(M)+\max(0,h-J_i(M))$, and the other first tails are unchanged. The survival criterion in [F1] therefore gives $ v_i^\lambda(M)=J_i(M)+\lambda_i-c_i(M)$. In particular differences $v_i^\lambda(X)-v_i^\lambda(Y)$ for two surviving simples are independent of the containing labels, and $J_i(f_iM)=\max(0,J_i(M)-1)$. This proves finiteness and the recurrence without using the numerical jump formula. [step 2.1, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

3.3 For extraction put $c=c_i(M)$ and $U=(e_i^L)^cM$, so $M=(f_i^L)^cU$ and $c_i(U)=0$. If $c_i(f_jM)=c$, then $(e_i^L)^cf_jM=f_jU$. Indeed induce $L_c,U,L(j)$ onto $f_jM$ and apply maximal first-$i$ restriction, then its simple quotient. In the Mackey filtration the $U$-contribution of first $i$'s is zero; for $j\ne i$ the only surviving factors after forgetting the first column are shifts of $\operatorname{Ind}(U,L(j))$. Thus the target is its unique simple head. For $j=i$ there can also be shifts of $\operatorname{Ind}(L(i),U)$, but these have no nonzero map to the extracted target of first tail zero, by adjunction; the same unique-head conclusion follows. If $c_i(f_iM)=c+1$, step 2.1 instead gives $e_i^Lf_iM=M$ and therefore extraction yields $U$. For $j\ne i$, $\epsilon_j(U)=\epsilon_j(M)$ because first-$i$ lowering preserves the right $j$-tail (step 2.1). The preceding lowering compatibility and partial inverses then give extraction compatibility with $e_j$ whenever $e_jM\ne0$; when $e_jM=0$, the equality of tails makes $e_jU=0$ too. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F1, algebra]

4.1 We record the two containing-weight annihilation tests, with their proofs. If $A$ survives and $k=v_i^\lambda(A)$, then the quotient of $\operatorname{Ind}(A,L_{k+1})$ is zero: its only simple head is the killed $f_i^{k+1}A$, while any nonzero finite quotient has a simple head. Appending any further block preserves zero, since a cyclotomic relation on an initial block is also a relation on the larger initial block. Consequently $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B)$ has zero quotient if $c_i(B)>k$, by the surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(L_{c_i(B)},(e_i^L)^{c_i(B)}B)\to B$. Conversely suppose $\epsilon_i(A)=\epsilon_j(A)=0$, $B$ has weight $ci+dj$, and $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B)\to T$ is surjective and simple. If $b=c_i(B)\le k$, write $C=(e_i^L)^bB$ and induce $A,L_b,C$. Its map to $T$ factors through $f_i^bA,C$: all other constituents $U$ of $\operatorname{Ind}(A,L_b)$ have $\epsilon_i(U)<b$. The defining corner of $T$ with an initial block of weight $\operatorname{wt}_{\rm root}(A)$ followed by a suffix of colours $i,j$ and beginning with $i^b$ is nonzero by adjunction. Every word in any such $U$ has a non-$i,j$ letter after the original $A$-block length: otherwise its last $A$-letter, necessarily different from $i,j$, would already lie in that initial block and all $b$ added $i$'s would be a tail, contradicting $\epsilon_i(U)<b$. Shuffling with $C$ preserves that non-$i,j$ letter in the suffix, so this corner vanishes on $\operatorname{Ind}(U,C)$. Thus no such factor maps onto $T$, proving the factorisation. The shuffle bound gives $c_i(T)\le c_i(f_i^bA)+c_i(C)\le\lambda_i$. Taking $\lambda_j$ larger than the total root-height of the finitely many modules being considered makes the $j$ inequality automatic; other first tails of $T$ are at most those of $A$. Hence this converse guarantees survival. In these comparisons we may enlarge every label except $\lambda_i$ to cover the fixed non-$i$ content of all the $i$-strings, so no assertion of a converse for a small $\lambda_j$ is needed. [step 1.1, step 3.2, F1, F2, algebra]

4.2 First calculate the containing-length change in rank two. Any simple $N$ of weight $ci+dj$ with $\epsilon_i(N)=0$ is a quotient of $\operatorname{Ind}(L(i^{b_1}j),\ldots,L(i^{b_r}j))$ with $0\le b_s\le a$: strip one right $j$, then its maximal $i$-tail, and repeat. A positive-height $N$ has a right $j$ since its right $i$ is zero; the factors of $\operatorname{Ind}(L_b,L(j))$ are classified by step 3.1, so tail zero forces $b\le a$ and the unique factor $L(i^bj)$. This justifies each factor selection by step 1.1 and decreases height, so the process terminates. For arbitrary $M$, put $m=\epsilon_i(M)$ and strip that tail to obtain such an $N$. The simple $f_jM$ is a quotient induced from $N$ and a factor $U$ of $\operatorname{Ind}(L_m,L(j))$. Step 1.1 determines its tail $\gamma=\epsilon_i(U)$; by step 3.1 it equals $m-a+n$ for some $0\le n\le a$ and $ f_i^aU=\operatorname{Ind}(D_n,L_m)$. This holds also for $m<a$, where $U=L(i^{a-n}ji^{m-a+n})$ and $m-a+n\ge0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For any simple $B$, every simple quotient of $\operatorname{Ind}(B,D_n)$ has right tail $\epsilon_i(B)+n$: strip the right tail $L_{\epsilon_i(B)}$ of $B$, commute it past $D_n$ by step 3.1, and split $D_n$ as the head of $L(i^{a-n}j),L_n$. The resulting surjection with terminal block $L_{\epsilon_i(B)+n}$ gives the lower bound by adjunction; the shuffle bound gives the upper bound. This also holds for a string of $D_n$ factors by iteration. Let $d_s=a-b_s$ and $D=\sum_s d_s$. For $m+h\ge D$, the surjection from the factors in step 4.2 to $f_i^hM$ can be rewritten as a surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(D_{d_1},\ldots,D_{d_r},L_{m+h-D})\to f_i^hM$. To verify it, start at the rightmost $L(i^{b_r}j)$: the unique constituent of $\operatorname{Ind}(L(i^{b_r}j),L_{m+h})$ with tail $m+h$ is $\operatorname{Ind}(D_{d_r},L_{m+h-d_r})$ by step 3.1. Every other constituent has smaller tail and cannot map to the target through the preceding tail-zero factors by step 1.1. Commute this $D_{d_r}$ past the remaining pure-colour block using step 3.1 and repeat with $r-1,\ldots,1$. Each suffix of the $d_s$ has sum at most $D$, so all consumed block lengths are nonnegative. If a simple $Q$ is a quotient of $\operatorname{Ind}(D_{n_1},\ldots,D_{n_r},L_g)$, its right and first tails are exactly $g+\sum_s n_s$ and $g+\sum_s(a-n_s)$. Indeed commute $L_g$ past each $D_{n_s}$, split $D_{n_s}$ as the head of $L(i^{a-n_s}j),L_{n_s}$, and move its $L_{n_s}$ to the right; adjunction gives the lower right-tail bound, the shuffle bound the matching upper bound. Repeat with the first-end split to obtain the first-tail bound. Therefore, with $C=m-ar+2\sum_s b_s$, we have $c_i(f_i^hM)=h+C$ for $h$ sufficiently large. For $f_i^hf_jM$ first absorb $a$ pure strands into $U$ using step 4.2 and the factorisation of step 1.1, and perform the same replacements on the preceding factors. For $h\ge a+D$ this gives a quotient of $\operatorname{Ind}(D_{d_1},\ldots,D_{d_r},D_n,L_{m+h-a-D})$, whose first tail is $h+C-n$. Choose $h$ still larger so both these tails are nonnegative and take the containing $i$ label to be $\omega_i=h+C$, with other labels large. The next first tail of $M$ is $\omega_i+1$, whereas $f_jM$ survives exactly $h+n$ steps. Thus $v_i^\omega(f_jM)-v_i^\omega(M)=n$; label independence in step 3.2 gives for every quotient containing both $ v_i^\lambda(f_jM)-v_i^\lambda(M)=n=\epsilon_i(f_jM)-\epsilon_i(M)+a$. This is the rank-two containing-length calculation, with an actual finite saturation threshold. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.2, F2, algebra]

6.1 Reduce an arbitrary $M$ and $f_jM$ to that calculation. First strip the right $i$-tail of $M$, then alternately strip maximal right $j$- and $i$-tails until the remaining simple $A$ has $\epsilon_i(A)=\epsilon_j(A)=0$; each nonzero stripping decreases root-height. Group the removed blocks into a simple rank-two factor $B_0$ with a surjection $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B_0)\to e_i^{\epsilon_i(M)}M$, by step 1.1. Append the removed $i$-tail and apply the factorisation of step 1.1 to get $\operatorname{Ind}(A,B)\to M$ with $B=f_i^{\epsilon_i(M)}B_0$. For both $l=i,j$, the corner lower bound and shuffle upper bound give $\epsilon_l(B)=\epsilon_l(M)$. Appending $j$ and applying step 1.1 with colour $j$ gives $\operatorname{Ind}(A,f_jB)\to f_jM$, also with $\epsilon_i(f_jB)=\epsilon_i(f_jM)$. The same factorisation gives $\operatorname{Ind}(A,f_i^hB)\to f_i^hM$ and $\operatorname{Ind}(A,f_i^{h'}f_jB)\to f_i^{h'}f_jM$ for all $h,h'$. Choose containing labels $\omega$ for these initial modules with all non-$i$ labels large, and set $h=v_i^\omega(M)$, $h'=v_i^\omega(f_jM)$ and $k=v_i^\omega(A)$. The two annihilation tests in step 4.1 imply $c_i(f_i^hB)\le k<c_i(f_i^{h+1}B)$ and $c_i(f_i^{h'}f_jB)\le k<c_i(f_i^{h'+1}f_jB)$. Step 2.1 makes the inequalities equalities at the surviving endpoints and gives zero jump there. Step 3.2, applied to $B$ and $f_jB$, now gives $v_i^\omega(B)=h+\omega_i-k$ and $v_i^\omega(f_jB)=h'+\omega_i-k$. Their difference equals $h'-h$; step 5.1 evaluates it as $\epsilon_i(f_jM)-\epsilon_i(M)+a$. Label independence in step 3.2 transfers this to any quotient containing $M,f_jM$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, F2, algebra]

7.1 The initial value is now fixed, not inferred from a decrement recurrence. On the vacuum, $f_i^n\mathbf1=L_n$ has $c_i=n$, so its containing length is exactly $\lambda_i$ by [F1]. Every nonvacuum simple has some positive right tail (choose the last letter of any nonzero word corner), so write $M=f_jN$ with $N=e_jM$ of lower height. Both survive whenever $M$ survives by [F1]. If $j=i$, surviving length decreases by one, right tail increases by one and $\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$ increases by two. If $j\ne i$, step 6.1 gives the change in surviving length as $\epsilon_i(M)-\epsilon_i(N)-a_{ij}$. Induction on height from the vacuum therefore yields $ v_i^\lambda(M)=\lambda_i+\epsilon_i(M)-\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$. Combining this with the independently proved formula in step 3.2 gives $J_i(M)=\epsilon_i(M)+c_i(M)-\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$. In particular the initial zero-jump test is precisely $\epsilon_i(M)+c_i(M)=\langle h_i,\beta\rangle$; its value follows from the rank-two/annihilation calculation and the vacuum base, rather than the recurrence alone. The formula is symmetric in the two ends, so the corresponding first-end jump and its decrement formula also hold. [step 3.2, step 6.1, F1, algebra]

8.1 Define the elementary crystal $B_i$ by $\operatorname{wt}(b_i(n))=n\alpha_i$, $\epsilon_i(b_i(n))=-n$, $\phi_i(b_i(n))=n$, $e_ib_i(n)=b_i(n+1)$, $f_ib_i(n)=b_i(n-1)$; other-colour epsilon and phi are $-\infty$ and other-colour operators zero. On a tensor $X\otimes Y$, epsilon is $\max(\epsilon_l(X),\epsilon_l(Y)-\langle h_l,\operatorname{wt}(X)\rangle)$; $e_l$ acts on $X$ iff $\phi_l(X)\ge\epsilon_l(Y)$ and $f_l$ acts on $X$ iff $\phi_l(X)>\epsilon_l(Y)$, otherwise on $Y$. To check the $i$-data and both branches set $s=J_i(U)=\epsilon_i(U)+\langle h_i,\operatorname{wt}(U)\rangle\ge0$. Along the first-$i$ string from $U$, the symmetric jump recurrence and step 2.1 give $\epsilon_i(M)=\epsilon_i(U)+\max(0,c-s)$. This is exactly $\max(\epsilon_i(U),c-\langle h_i,\operatorname{wt}(U)\rangle)$, the tensor epsilon. Weight is also preserved since $\operatorname{wt}(U)=\operatorname{wt}(M)+c\alpha_i$. For lowering, if $s>c$ then $J_i(M)=s-c>0$, $c_i(f_iM)=c$, and step 3.3 gives extracted factor $f_iU$; if $s\le c$ then $J_i(M)=0$, $c_i(f_iM)=c+1$ and its extracted factor is $U$. These are precisely the strict $f_i$ branches. For raising, if $s<c$ then the predecessor $(f_i^L)^{c-1}U$ has zero jump, so $e_iM=e_i^LM$, first tail $c-1$ and extracted factor $U$. If $s\ge c>0$, that predecessor has positive jump; a zero jump at $e_iM$ would force $e_iM=e_i^LM$ by step 2.1 and contradict that positive jump.
