## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a compact connected semisimple Lie group
with maximal torus $T$, character lattice $X^*(T)$ and root system $\Phi$ with
root lattice $Q$ and weight lattice $P$ in the real span of the roots
([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]). Then
$$Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq P .$$
Moreover the simply connected form has $X^*(T)=P$ and the adjoint form has
$X^*(T)=Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected semisimple $G$ with maximal torus $T$, and the lattices $Q\subseteq P$ of the root system.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the integration and covering theory of [L3] and [L5].

[L1] Roots are characters of $T$ with differentials forming a reduced crystallographic root system; characters differentiate to integral functionals and pair perfectly with cocharacters ([[def-roots-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]], [[prop-differentiation-identifies-characters-with-the-integral-weight-lattice-of-t]], [[def-root-datum-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]]).

[L2] $Q$ is the lattice generated by the roots and $P=\{\lambda:(\lambda,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z\ \forall\alpha\}$ is the dual lattice of the coroot lattice; $Q\subseteq P$ ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[L3] Every connected real Lie group is a central quotient of its simply connected cover; every Lie-algebra homomorphism from a simply connected group integrates uniquely ([[thm-connected-lie-groups-are-central-quotients-of-their-simply-connected-integrations]], [[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]]).

[L4] Finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations of the semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ are classified by dominant integral weights in $P$, and the fundamental weights form a basis of $P$ and are dominant integral ([[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]], [[def-fundamental-weights]]).

[L5] A finite-sheeted covering has compact total space exactly when its base is compact ([[thm-compactness-is-invariant-under-finite-sheeted-coverings]]). Every element of a compact connected Lie group lies in a maximal torus, and all maximal tori are conjugate ([[thm-every-element-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group-lies-in-a-maximal-torus]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]]). A finitely generated abelian group is a finite direct sum of infinite and finite cyclic groups ([[cor-fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups-from-pid-modules]]), and a torus is its Lie algebra modulo the full lattice given by the kernel of its exponential map ([[thm-structure-of-a-compact-connected-abelian-lie-group]]).

[L6] The semisimple complexification has the root decomposition with Cartan $\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}$, roots real on $i\mathfrak t$, and zero weight space $\mathfrak t_{\mathbb C}$. The compact root homomorphism has cocharacter tangent $ih_\alpha$ for the Lie-algebra coroot $h_\alpha$. Also $C_G(T)=T$ ([[thm-compact-group-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system-on-the-semisimple-part]], [[thm-analytic-and-root-system-weyl-groups-agree]], [[thm-compact-group-weyl-group-is-finite]]). Simple roots are a basis and every root has integral coordinates in that basis ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

[L7] Exponentials are natural under homomorphisms, $d\operatorname{Ad}=\operatorname{ad}$, closed subgroups are embedded, normal closed quotients are Lie groups with the quotient Lie algebra, and exponentials give local charts at the identity ([[prop-exponential-map-is-natural-for-lie-group-homomorphisms]], [[thm-the-differential-of-adjoint-is-ad]], [[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]], [[thm-quotient-by-a-closed-normal-subgroup-is-a-lie-group]], [[cor-the-exponential-map-is-a-local-diffeomorphism-at-zero]]).

[L8] Semisimple Lie algebras are centerless ([[cor-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-centerless-and-perfect]]). A full-rank integer sublattice given by a nonsingular integer matrix A has index $|\det A|$ ([[cor-index-of-a-full-rank-integer-sublattice-is-the-absolute-determinant]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Each root $\alpha$ is an honest character of $T$ by [L1], so $\mathbb Z\alpha\subseteq X^*(T)$ for each root and hence $Q\subseteq X^*(T)$. [L1, L2]

1.2 If $\chi\in X^*(T)$, then for every root $\alpha$ with cocharacter $\alpha^\vee$ the composite $\chi\circ\alpha^\vee:S^1\to S^1$ is $z\mapsto z^{n}$ for some $n\in\mathbb Z$, and $n=\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle$; and by [L6] and the normalization in [L1] this integer is $d\chi(ih_\alpha)/i=d\chi(h_\alpha)=(\chi,\alpha^\vee)$; hence $(\chi,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z$ for every root and $\chi\in P$. Thus $X^*(T)\subseteq P$. [L1, L2, L6]

1.3 Since $Q$ and $P$ have full rank by [L2], a basis of $Q$ is a nonsingular integer matrix in a basis of $P$, so $[P:Q]<\infty$ by [L8]. In rank zero both are zero and the index is one. [L2, L8]

1.4 Let $p:\widetilde G\to G$ be the simply connected covering with discrete central kernel $\Gamma$ from [L3]. Choose a symmetric relatively compact identity neighborhood $U$. The subgroup it generates is open, so equals connected $\widetilde G$. Finitely many sets $p(a_iU)$ cover compact $G$. Include $a_i=e$ and set $C=\bigcup_i a_i\overline U$, so $\widetilde G=C\Gamma$ and $e\in C$. For $\gamma=u_1\cdots u_n\in\Gamma$, write the partial products $u_1\cdots u_j=c_j\gamma_j$ with $c_j\in C$, $\gamma_j\in\Gamma$, choosing endpoints $(c_0,\gamma_0)=(e,e)$ and $(c_n,\gamma_n)=(e,\gamma)$. Centrality gives $\gamma_j\gamma_{j-1}^{-1}=c_j^{-1}c_{j-1}u_j$. The intersection $\Gamma\cap C^{-1}C\overline U$ is finite: it is a closed discrete subset of a compact set. It generates $\Gamma$ by multiplying these increments. Thus $\Gamma$ is finitely generated abelian. [L3]

2.1 Suppose $\Gamma$ were infinite. Its decomposition in [L5] then has a nonzero free summand, so it has subgroups $K$ with finite indices $|\Gamma/K|$ arbitrarily large (reduce one free coordinate modulo any positive integer). Each $K$ is closed and central in $\widetilde G$, so $G_K=\widetilde G/K$ is a connected Lie group by [L7]. The induced map $p_K:G_K\to G$ is a finite covering with kernel $D=\Gamma/K$: an evenly covered neighborhood for p descends to disjoint sheets indexed by $\Gamma/K$. Hence $G_K$ is compact by [L5], with the same semisimple Lie algebra. Let $T_K$ be maximal. Every central element of $D$ lies in $T_K$, since [L5] puts it in some maximal torus and conjugacy moves that torus to $T_K$ while fixing the element. The image $T=p_K(T_K)$ is a closed connected abelian Lie subgroup, hence a torus. It is maximal: if $T'$ contains it, the inverse image of $\operatorname{Lie}(T')$ under the Lie-algebra isomorphism $dp_K$ is abelian and contains $\mathfrak t_K$. The latter is maximal abelian, since exponentiating a larger abelian algebra and taking its compact connected closure would give a larger torus. Thus $\operatorname{Lie}(T')=\operatorname{Lie}(T)$; exponential charts make $T$ open in connected $T'$, so $T'=T$. [L5, L6, L7, step 1.4]

2.2 For the adjoint endpoint put $G_{ad}=G/Z(G)$. The center is closed, so this quotient is a compact connected Lie group by [L7]. Its Lie algebra is the original semisimple algebra: the center has zero Lie algebra, because a vector whose exponentials are central has zero adjoint bracket and semisimplicity is centerless. The adjoint action of $G_{ad}$ is faithful, since an element of connected G acting trivially on its Lie algebra commutes with every exponential and hence with the group they generate. Write a character $\chi$ of the maximal torus $T_{ad}$ of the adjoint group in the basis of simple roots: $\chi=\sum_jc_j\alpha_j$ with $c_j\in\mathbb R$. Let $H_j\in\mathfrak t$ be defined by $\alpha_i(H_j)=2\pi i\,\delta_{ij}$. Every root has integral simple-root coordinates, so $\operatorname{Ad}(\exp H_j)$ acts trivially on the root spaces and on the torus, and since its adjoint action is faithful we get $\exp H_j=1$; analytic integrality of $\chi$ then forces $\chi(H_j)=2\pi i\,c_j\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ for every $j$, so all $c_j$ are integers and $\chi\in Q$. Together with step 1.1 this gives $X^*(T_{ad})=Q$. [L1, L6, L7, L8, step 1.1]

3.1 Identify the two torus Lie algebras by $dp_K$. Naturality gives exponential lattices $\Lambda_K\subseteq\Lambda$, and surjectivity of $\exp_{T_K}$ identifies $D$ with $\Lambda/\Lambda_K$. If a basis of $\Lambda_K$ is the matrix A in a basis of $\Lambda$, [L8] gives $|D|=|\det A|$. With character coordinates $d\chi(Y_j)/(2\pi i)$, restriction of characters from T to $T_K$ is the integer matrix $A^{\mathsf T}$, so [L1, L8] give $[X^*(T_K):X^*(T)]=|D|$. Rank zero has trivial lattices and D and needs no determinant theorem. The roots agree under the differential by [L6], so steps 1.1–1.2 give $Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq X^*(T_K)\subseteq P$. Thus $|D|\le[P:Q]$, contrary to step 2.1 and step 1.3. Consequently $\Gamma$ is finite and $G_{sc}=\widetilde G$ is compact by [L5]. [L1, L5, L6, L7, L8, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

4.1 Every dominant integral element of $P$, in particular every fundamental weight, is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ by [L4]; restricting the infinitesimal representation to the compact real form $\operatorname{Lie}(G_{sc})$ and integrating it by [L3] gives a finite-dimensional representation of the simply connected compact group $G_{sc}$, whose highest weight line is invariant under $T_{sc}$: for $H\in\mathfrak t_{sc}$ its exponential acts on that line by $e^{\lambda(H)}$ by [L7], and those exponentials generate $T_{sc}$. The line therefore defines a continuous homomorphism $T_{sc}\to\mathbb C^\times$. Its image is compact, so its modulus is identically one (a nontrivial positive modulus has unbounded positive or negative powers). It is a character with differential that fundamental weight; hence $P\subseteq X^*(T_{sc})$ and, with step 1.2, $X^*(T_{sc})=P$. [L3, L4, L7, step 1.2, step 3.1]


5.1 Steps 1.1 and 1.2 give the sandwich $Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq P$ for every compact connected semisimple $G$, and steps 2.2 and 4.1 realise the two endpoints; the Axiom of Choice entered only through the integration and covering theory used. [A1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 4.1] ∎
