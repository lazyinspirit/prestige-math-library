## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a compact connected semisimple Lie group
with maximal torus $T$, character lattice $X^*(T)$ and root system $\Phi$ with
root lattice $Q$ and weight lattice $P$ in the real span of the roots
([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]). Then
$$Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq P .$$
Moreover the simply connected form has $X^*(T)=P$ and the adjoint form has
$X^*(T)=Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected semisimple $G$ with maximal torus $T$, and the lattices $Q\subseteq P$ of the root system.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the integration and covering theory of [L3] and [L5].

[L1] Roots are characters of $T$ with differentials forming a reduced crystallographic root system; characters differentiate to integral functionals and pair perfectly with cocharacters ([[def-roots-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]], [[prop-differentiation-identifies-characters-with-the-integral-weight-lattice-of-t]], [[def-root-datum-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group]]).

[L2] $Q$ is the lattice generated by the roots and $P=\{\lambda:(\lambda,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z\ \forall\alpha\}$ is the dual lattice of the coroot lattice; $Q\subseteq P$ ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[L3] Every connected real Lie group is a central quotient of its simply connected cover; the Lie functor on connected simply connected groups is an equivalence with finite-dimensional real Lie algebras, and every Lie-algebra homomorphism from a simply connected group integrates uniquely ([[thm-connected-lie-groups-are-central-quotients-of-their-simply-connected-integrations]], [[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]]).

[L4] Finite-dimensional irreducible complex representations of the semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ are classified by dominant integral weights in $P$, with the fundamental weights realised by the fundamental representations ([[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]], [[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[L5] A finite-sheeted covering has compact total space exactly when its base is compact ([[thm-compactness-is-invariant-under-finite-sheeted-coverings]]). Every element of a compact connected Lie group lies in a maximal torus, and all maximal tori are conjugate ([[thm-every-element-of-a-compact-connected-lie-group-lies-in-a-maximal-torus]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]]). A finitely generated abelian group is a finite direct sum of infinite and finite cyclic groups ([[cor-fundamental-theorem-of-finitely-generated-abelian-groups-from-pid-modules]]), and a torus is its Lie algebra modulo the full lattice given by the kernel of its exponential map ([[thm-structure-of-a-compact-connected-abelian-lie-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Each root $\alpha$ is an honest character of $T$ by [L1], so $\mathbb Z\alpha\subseteq X^*(T)$ for each root and hence $Q\subseteq X^*(T)$. [L1, L2]

1.2 If $\chi\in X^*(T)$, then for every root $\alpha$ with cocharacter $\alpha^\vee$ the composite $\chi\circ\alpha^\vee:S^1\to S^1$ is $z\mapsto z^{n}$ for some $n\in\mathbb Z$, and $n=\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle$; hence $(\chi,\alpha^\vee)\in\mathbb Z$ for every root and $\chi\in P$. Thus $X^*(T)\subseteq P$. [L1, L2]

1.3 Let $p:\widetilde G\to G$ be the simply connected covering group from [L3], with discrete central kernel $\Gamma$. We first prove that $\Gamma$ is finitely generated. Choose a symmetric relatively compact identity neighbourhood $U$ in $\widetilde G$. The subgroup generated by $U$ is open, and hence is all of the connected group $\widetilde G$. Compactness of $G=\widetilde G/\Gamma$ supplies finitely many $a_i$ for which the sets $p(a_iU)$ cover $G$. After adjoining the identity, the compact set $C:=\bigcup_i a_i\overline U$ satisfies $\widetilde G=C\Gamma$.

For $\gamma\in\Gamma$, write $\gamma=u_1\cdots u_n$ with $u_j\in U$ and write each partial product as $u_1\cdots u_j=c_j\gamma_j$ with $c_j\in C$ and $\gamma_j\in\Gamma$, taking $(c_0,\gamma_0)=(e,e)$ and $(c_n,\gamma_n)=(e,\gamma)$. Centrality of $\Gamma$ gives $$\gamma_j\gamma_{j-1}^{-1}=c_j^{-1}c_{j-1}u_j \in \Gamma\cap C^{-1}C\overline U.$$ The set on the right is finite, because it is the intersection of the discrete closed subgroup $\Gamma$ with a compact set. It therefore generates $\Gamma$.

Suppose that $\Gamma$ were infinite. By [L5], it would have finite-index subgroups $K$ for which $|\Gamma/K|$ is arbitrarily large. The quotient $G_K:=\widetilde G/K$ is a finite-sheeted cover of $G$, hence is compact by [L5], and is connected and semisimple. Choose a maximal torus $T_K$ of $G_K$. The finite central kernel $D:=\Gamma/K$ lies in $T_K$: each of its elements lies in some maximal torus, and conjugacy moves that torus to $T_K$ without moving the central element. The image $T$ of $T_K$ in $G$ is maximal (a strictly larger torus would lift to a strictly larger torus), and $T_K\to T$ has kernel $D$.

Identify the two torus Lie algebras by the differential. Their exponential kernels are full lattices $\Lambda_K\subseteq\Lambda$, with $D\cong\Lambda/\Lambda_K$, while differentiation identifies $X^*(T)$ with the dual lattice of $\Lambda$ and $X^*(T_K)$ with the dual lattice of $\Lambda_K$. Choosing elementary-divisor bases for the finite-index inclusion gives $$[X^*(T_K):X^*(T)]=[\Lambda:\Lambda_K]=|D|.$$ Both groups have the same root system under the differential. Steps 1.1 and 1.2 therefore give $$Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq X^*(T_K)\subseteq P,$$ so $|D|\le [P:Q]$, contradicting the arbitrarily large choices of $D$. Thus $\Gamma$ is finite, and $G_{sc}:=\widetilde G$ is a finite-sheeted cover of the compact group $G$, hence compact by [L5]. [L3, L5, step 1.1, step 1.2]

2.1 For the adjoint endpoint, write a character $\chi$ of the maximal torus $T_{ad}$ of the adjoint group in the basis of simple roots: $\chi=\sum_jc_j\alpha_j$ with $c_j\in\mathbb R$. Let $H_j\in\mathfrak t$ be defined by $\alpha_i(H_j)=2\pi i\,\delta_{ij}$. Every root has integral simple-root coordinates, so $\operatorname{Ad}(\exp H_j)$ acts trivially on the root spaces and on the torus, and since the adjoint group has trivial centre we get $\exp H_j=1$; analytic integrality of $\chi$ then forces $\chi(H_j)=2\pi i\,c_j\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ for every $j$, so all $c_j$ are integers and $\chi\in Q$. Together with step 1.1 this gives $X^*(T_{ad})=Q$. [L1, step 1.1]

2.2 Every dominant integral element of $P$, in particular every fundamental weight, is the highest weight of a finite-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ by [L4]; restricting the infinitesimal representation to the compact real form $\operatorname{Lie}(G_{sc})$ and integrating it by [L3] gives a finite-dimensional representation of the simply connected compact group $G_{sc}$, whose highest weight character is an element of $X^*(T_{sc})$ with differential that fundamental weight; hence $P\subseteq X^*(T_{sc})$ and, with step 1.2, $X^*(T_{sc})=P$. [L3, L4, step 1.3]


3.1 Steps 1.1 and 1.2 give the sandwich $Q\subseteq X^*(T)\subseteq P$ for every compact connected semisimple $G$, and steps 2.1 and 2.2 realise the two endpoints; the Axiom of Choice entered only through the integration and covering theory used. [A1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
