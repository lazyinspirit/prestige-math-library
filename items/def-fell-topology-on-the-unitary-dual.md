## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a topological group and let
$\mathcal R$ be a set of unitary equivalence classes of strongly continuous
unitary representations of $G$ containing the unitary dual $\widehat G$
([[def-unitary-dual-of-a-locally-compact-group]]). For a representation
$\pi$ with class in $\mathcal R$, finitely many functions of positive type
$\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n$ associated to $\pi$ (that is, each is a single diagonal
matrix coefficient $\phi_i(g)=\langle\pi(g)\xi_i,\xi_i\rangle$ of $\pi$,
[[def-matrix-coefficient-of-a-unitary-representation]],
[[def-continuous-function-of-positive-type]]), a compact set $Q\subseteq G$ and
$\epsilon>0$, put
$$W(\pi;\phi_1,\dots,\phi_n,Q,\epsilon):=\Bigl\{\rho\in\mathcal R:\ \text{each }\phi_i\text{ is within }\epsilon\text{ on }Q\text{ of a finite sum of functions of positive type associated to }\rho\Bigr\}.$$
The **Fell topology** on $\mathcal R$, and on the unitary dual
$\widehat G\subseteq\mathcal R$ in particular, is the topology generated by
these sets: a subset is open when it is a union of sets of this form. This is
the coefficient topology of [BeH–19, §1.C]; in the notation of
[[def-weak-containment-of-unitary-representations]] the condition defining
$W(\pi;\phi,Q,\epsilon)$ is the compact-uniform approximation of $\phi$ by
coefficients of $\rho$, one function at a time.

## Remarks

- **The displayed family is a basis.** Every displayed set contains its center. An empty test list gives the whole space. For a nonempty list and $\rho$ in the displayed set, choose its finite coefficient witnesses for each test; insert a zero coefficient if a witness list is empty. Thus $N=\sum_i n_i>0$. Let $\delta$ be the minimum of the positive error margins $\epsilon-\sup_Q|\phi_i-\sum_j c_{\eta_{i,j},\eta_{i,j}}|$. The set centered at $\rho$ testing all these individual coefficients on $Q$ to accuracy $\delta/N$ is contained in the original set: the sum of their new errors is strictly less than $n_i\delta/N\le\delta$, which fits each error margin. For finitely many displayed sets containing $\rho$, first perform this refinement separately for each set. All resulting tests are coefficients of the SAME representation $\rho$, so their union, the finite union of compact test sets, and the minimum of their tolerances give a displayed set containing $\rho$ inside the intersection. This proves both the refinement and finite-intersection basis axioms without treating tests from unrelated centers as coefficients of one representation.
- **Finite sums as tests.** Allowing finite sums of diagonal coefficients as test functions generates the same topology. For a test $\sum_{j=1}^m c_{\xi_j,\xi_j}$ and error $\epsilon>0$, testing its $m>0$ summands separately with error $\epsilon/m$ ensures that their finite-sum witnesses add to a witness for the original test. An empty sum is the zero coefficient. Conversely, every single coefficient is a one-term sum. Thus this enlargement changes the displayed basis but not the topology; it does not make every finite sum a single diagonal coefficient of the given representation.
- **Hausdorffness and discreteness are not asserted.** The definition
  guarantees only that these neighbourhoods form a topology; the companion
  examples page exhibits a second-countable locally compact group whose dual
  is not Hausdorff. Nothing here asserts that the Fell topology is discrete,
  and for non-compact groups it need not be.
- **Choice.** The Axiom of Choice is inherited from the construction of the
  unitary dual and from the GNS machinery used to compare coefficients; the
  basis verifications above use none ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).
