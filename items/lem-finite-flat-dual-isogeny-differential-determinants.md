## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $R$ be a complete DVR, and let $N$ be a commutative finite flat $R$-group of rank $n$ with Cartier dual $N^D$. Suppose $\phi:H\to H'$ and $\psi:V\to V'$ are finite flat homomorphisms of smooth affine commutative $R$-groups of equal relative dimensions within each pair, with respective kernels $N$ and $N^D$. Let $J_\phi$ and $J_\psi$ be the principal ideals generated by the determinants of their maps on invariant differentials. Then $J_\phi J_\psi=(n)$ as ideals of $R$. The assertion does not assume $n$ is invertible.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The complete DVR, group schemes and two supplied finite-flat homomorphisms in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice and Dependent Choice are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The Cartier dual coordinate algebra is the finite linear dual Hopf algebra. [[def-finite-flat-group-scheme-and-cartier-dual]]

[F2] Finite free commutative and cocommutative Hopf algebras over a local ring have normalized integral generators whose counits multiply to their rank. [[lem-finite-commutative-hopf-normalized-integrals-rank-identity]]

[F3] For a regular sequence in a formal power-series ring, the coefficient determinant freely generates the augmentation-ideal annihilator and its augmentation is the differential determinant. [[lem-complete-intersection-koszul-annihilator-determinant]]

[F4] A finite-dimensional commutative algebra over a field is a finite product of local algebras. [[lem-finite-dimensional-commutative-algebras-decompose-into-local-factors]]

[F5] Under AC, every system of parameters of a Cohen–Macaulay local ring is a regular sequence. [[thm-parameters-and-regular-sequences-in-cohen-macaulay-modules]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** identify each integral counit with a formal Jacobian, then multiply the normalized Hopf integrals.

1.1 Let $A=\mathcal O(N)$, $B=\mathcal O(N^D)=A^\vee$, and let $I_A$ and $I_B$ denote the respective integral modules. By [F1] and [F2], choose generators $t\in I_A$, $\lambda\in I_B$ with $\lambda(t)=1$ and $n=\epsilon_A(t)\epsilon_B(\lambda)$. It suffices to identify the ideals of the two counits with $J_\phi$ and $J_\psi$. [F1, F2, given, choose, algebra]

1.2 Work first with $\phi$. Its identity points are $k$-rational for $k=R/\mathfrak m$. Smoothness and the identity sections give completed local rings $R\llbracket X_1,\dots,X_d\rrbracket$ and $R\llbracket Y_1,\dots,Y_d\rrbracket$: choose relative parameters reducing to a regular parameter system on the special fibre, then lift the inverse coordinate maps through successive nilpotent thickenings by formal smoothness; completeness makes the compatible maps inverse. The completed homomorphism sends $Y_i$ to a series $f_i$ of zero constant term. Completion of the kernel square at the identity gives the quotient $R\llbracket X\rrbracket/(f_1,\dots,f_d)$. By [F4], $A/\pi A$ is a product of finitely many local algebras. Lift each residue idempotent to an idempotent of the $\pi$-adically complete algebra $A$ by the Newton iteration $e\mapsto e-(e^2-e)(2e-1)^{-1}$: the denominator is a unit and the error squares at each step. Two lifts are equal because their difference lies in $\pi A$ and their sum minus one is a unit. The unique lifts give a product decomposition of $A$ into finite free local factors; finite flat direct summands over the DVR are free. The factor $A_0$ at the identity is complete, so the completed kernel quotient is $A_0$. [F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ring $S=R\llbracket X_1,\dots,X_d\rrbracket$ is regular local of dimension $d+1$, hence Cohen–Macaulay; its maximal ideal is generated by the uniformizer $\pi$ and the variables. Since $A_0$ is finite over $R$, $S/(f_1,\dots,f_d,\pi)$ is Artinian, so $(f_1,\dots,f_d,\pi)$ is a system of parameters. By [A1] and [F5] it is regular, and its initial sequence $f_1,\dots,f_d$ is regular. Express $f_i=\sum_j a_{ij}X_j$. [F3] gives the generator $\Delta=\det(a_{ij})$ of the $R$-free annihilator of $(X_1,\dots,X_d)$ in $A_0$, with augmentation $\det(a_{ij}(0))$. [A1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 The augmentation $A\to R$ vanishes on every factor except $A_0$ and contains the idempotent of the product of the other factors in its kernel. Thus the annihilator of the global augmentation ideal is precisely the annihilator from step 2.1, extended by zero on other factors. The integral equation $at=\epsilon_A(a)t$ identifies this annihilator with $I_A$, and $t$ is its generator by step 1.1. Hence its projection differs from $\Delta$ by a unit of $R$. Taking augmentations yields $(\epsilon_A(t))=(\det(a_{ij}(0)))=J_\phi$, since the latter matrix is the map on cotangent spaces at the identity, equivalently on invariant differentials. All determinant ideals are independent of the chosen bases. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Apply steps 1.2–3.1 to $\psi$ and the dual Hopf algebra $B$, using its possibly different relative dimension. The normalized generator $\lambda$ gives $(\epsilon_B(\lambda))=J_\psi$. Multiplying with step 3.1 and the rank identity of step 1.1 proves $J_\phi J_\psi=(n)$. The empty-coordinate case gives determinant one and is included throughout. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, A1, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
