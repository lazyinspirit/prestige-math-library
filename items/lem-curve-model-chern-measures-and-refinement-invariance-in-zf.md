## Statement

Work in ZF. Let $k$ be algebraically closed, $B/k$ a smooth projective integral curve, $K=k(B)$, and $Y/K$ an integral projective curve. Let $\mathcal Y\to B$ be an integral projective flat model of $Y$ and $\mathcal L$ an invertible sheaf on it, restricting to $L$. At a closed point $v\in B$ normalize the absolute value by $|t_v|=e^{-1}$. Normalize the model, and write its special-fibre cycle as $\sum_V b_VV$, using reduced one-dimensional components. There are canonically specified divisorial points $\xi_V\in Y_{K_v}^{\mathrm{an}}$, and the finite signed Radon measure
$$c_1(L,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal L,v})=\sum_V b_V\deg_V(\mathcal L|_V)\,\delta_{\xi_V}$$
(where $\mathcal L$ on the right denotes its pullback to the normalization) has total mass $\deg_Y L$. The measure is additive under tensor products, positive if $\mathcal L$ has nonnegative degree on every integral vertical curve, and invariant under projective refinements and replacement by any model inducing the same metric on $L$. In particular it is positive for the restriction of a globally nef model line bundle. This includes singular $Y$, nonreduced special fibres and inseparable function-field phenomena.

On a normal model, for a vertical Cartier divisor $D=\sum_V a_VV$ supported at $v$, the model function $\phi_D=-\log\|1_D\|$ has value $a_V/b_V$ at $\xi_V$. Consequently its integral against this measure is the finite model pairing $\sum_V a_V\deg_V(\mathcal L|_V)$. This lemma constructs measures for model metrics on curves; it does not assert the existence of uniform-limit measures or mixed-nef positivity in higher dimension.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field, smooth base curve, integral generic curve, model and line bundle in the Statement; a specified closed point $v$ when a local construction is made.

[F1] Curve degrees satisfy tensor additivity, normalization invariance and the projection formula for every nonconstant morphism; pullback from a point has degree zero. [[lem-projective-curve-degrees-and-projection-formula-in-zf]]

[F2] In an integral projective flat curve model over a DVR with infinite residue field, the generic degree equals the sum of component degrees weighted by their special-fibre lengths; embedded finite supports contribute zero. [[lem-degree-specialization-in-projective-flat-curve-models-in-zf]]

[F3] Finitely many projective flat models with identified integral generic fibre admit a projective flat common refinement by the generic diagonal closure. [[lem-projective-flat-models-over-a-regular-curve-have-common-refinements]]

[F4] The normalization of a finite-type domain over a field is module-finite, without Choice. [[thm-integral-closure-finite-finite-type-domain-over-field]]

[F5] On normal finite-type charts over a field and their localizations, regularity is tested at height one; Cartier effectivity and zero are tested by their height-one orders. [[lem-finite-type-normal-domain-codimension-one-test-in-zf]]

[F6] A nonzero finite-type algebra over a field is module-finite over a polynomial subalgebra. [[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]]

[F7] A module over a PID is flat exactly when it is torsion-free. [[thm-over-a-pid-flat-is-equivalent-to-torsion-free]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F4], normalize the model on a finite affine cover and glue: integral closure commutes with localization, by clearing the finite denominators in a monic equation. This normalization is finite. It is projective over the original projective model, as follows by a finite construction. If $A$ is its finite coherent algebra, finite chart-denominator clearing gives a surjection $O(-m)^N\to A$ after twisting by a projective ample bundle. Thus its relative spectrum is a closed subscheme of the corresponding affine vector bundle. In the projective completion of that bundle the homogenized monic equations for the finitely many algebra generators have no point at infinity: there the leading powers force every affine coordinate to be zero. These assertions can be checked on the finitely many trivializing charts. They give a closed projective embedding; the projective bundle of a finite sum of ample twists is itself projective over the base by twisting and the Segre coordinates. The normal model remains integral and torsion-free over each base DVR, hence flat by [F7]. Its generic fibre is the finite normalization $\widetilde Y$ of $Y$. By [F1] it preserves $\deg L$. For now work on this normal model and its normal generic curve; pushforward to $Y$ will be specified below. [F1, F4, F7, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The special fibre has finitely many components and has dimension at most one. Here the needed finite-type dimension bounds follow directly from [F6]: in a finite-type domain $A$ finite over a polynomial subring $R$, a nonzero prime contracts nontrivially to $R$. Indeed, for $0\ne a$ in that prime a monic equation of least degree over $R$ has nonzero constant term, which belongs to the contraction. The quotient is integral over that polynomial quotient, so its fraction-field transcendence degree drops by at least one. Repeating this argument on quotient domains bounds any strict prime chain by the initial transcendence degree. Our total function field has transcendence degree two over $k$, and the equation $t_v\ne0$ cuts the fibre, giving the asserted bound. At a prime $q$ minimal over $t_v$ the height is one: if not, [F5] in $A_q$ would make $1/t_v$ regular, since no height-one prime below $q$ could contain $t_v$, contradicting that $t_v$ is a nonunit. Each such normal local ring is a DVR, by the finite ideal argument: for $0\ne b$ in its maximal ideal $m$, choose the least $r$ with $m^r\subseteq(b)$ and $x\in m^{r-1}\setminus(b)$; $z=x/b$ satisfies $zm\subseteq A_q$. If $zm\subseteq m$, the determinant trick makes $z$ integral, a contradiction. Otherwise $zy$ is a unit for some $y\in m$, so $m=(y)$; the finitely generated intersection of its powers is zero by the determinant trick. Thus every nonzero element has an integer order. In particular $b_V=\operatorname{ord}_V(t_v)$ is positive and is exactly the length of the special-fibre local ring at $\eta_V$. [F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $F=K(Y)=K(\widetilde Y)$ and $D_V=O_{\widetilde{\mathcal Y},\eta_V}$. The rule $|f|_V=\exp(-\operatorname{ord}_V(f)/b_V)$ on $F$ restricts to the normalized absolute value on $K$. To obtain an actual point after completing $K$, form $\widehat D_V=\varprojlim_nD_V/(\pi_V^n)$. This is a DVR: reduction to the first nonzero coordinate defines its integer order, division by $\pi_V$ on the kernel of reduction is unique modulo each power, and an element with nonzero residue has a unique inverse in every quotient. The compatible inverses define its inverse. The natural map from $D_V$ is injective by the intersection argument of step 2.1. Since $k\subset D_V$ and $t_v$ has order $b_V$, series $\sum c_i t_v^i$ define a ring map $k[[t_v]]\to\widehat D_V$, by their compatible finite partial sums, and the leading term proves injectivity and order scaling. The completion of $K$ at $v$ is $k((t_v))$: successive residue subtraction gives its unique coefficients, and its finite truncations are in $K$. Hence $K_v$ embeds isometrically into $\operatorname{Frac}\widehat D_V$. For each affine generic chart $\operatorname{Spec}C$ this gives an evaluation homomorphism $C\otimes_KK_v\to\operatorname{Frac}\widehat D_V$ and a multiplicative seminorm extending the absolute value on $K_v$. The seminorms agree under localization and therefore give a point $\xi_V$ of $\widetilde Y_{K_v}^{\mathrm{an}}$, and then of $Y_{K_v}^{\mathrm{an}}$ by restriction along the normalization. This construction uses inverse systems with uniquely specified compatible operations, not choices of representatives for infinitely many residue classes. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Define the displayed measure on a Borel set $U$ by the indicated finite sum over those $\xi_V\in U$. Dirac masses here are Radon: each singleton is compact and closed in the analytic space, so finite subsets give inner regularity, and deleting the finitely many atoms outside a set gives outer regularity. The closed-point assertion follows directly from projective seminorm charts: any two analytic points lie in a common affine hyperplane complement, since finite linear avoidance over the infinite field $K_v$ chooses a hyperplane avoiding both supports. That chart is open, evaluations of affine functions separate its distinct seminorms, and their real-valued evaluations are continuous. Thus distinct points have disjoint open neighbourhoods. Thus no representation theorem for positive functionals or measure-extension principle is required. Apply [F2] over $O_{B,v}$ to the normal model. Step 2.1 identifies its length multiplicities with $b_V$, and [F1] identifies its generic degree with $\deg_Y L$. Hence the mass is precisely that degree. Additivity follows coefficient by coefficient from [F1]; negative degrees give a signed measure with finite total variation. If all vertical curve degrees are nonnegative, all coefficients are nonnegative. A globally nef model line bundle has that property by definition. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Let $f:\mathcal Z\to\mathcal T$ be a projective morphism of normal integral models inducing the identity on their normal generic curve. For a one-dimensional component $W$ of $\mathcal Z_v$, its image is either a point or a component $V$ of $\mathcal T_v$. The point case has $\deg_Wf^*\mathcal L=0$ by [F1]. Above the generic point of $V$, the inverse generic map extends from $\operatorname{Spec}F$ to $\operatorname{Spec}D_V$: take a projective embedding over $\mathcal T$, scale its finitely many homogeneous coordinates by the inverse of a coordinate of smallest valuation, and apply the closed defining equations. Its special centre $z$ maps to $\eta_V$. Its residue field contains $k(V)$, so it is not a closed point of a fibre curve. The dimension bound in step 2.1 therefore makes it the generic point of a one-dimensional component $W$. The resulting local maps embed $D_V\subseteq D_W\subseteq F$. A unit of $D_V$ is a unit of $D_W$, so if $\pi$ is a uniformizer of $D_V$, every element of $F^\times$ has $D_W$-order a multiple of $\operatorname{ord}_W(\pi)>0$. As the value group of $D_W$ on this same $F$ is $\mathbb Z$, that multiple is one. Thus $D_V=D_W$, the residue extension is the identity, and $b_W=b_V$. Separatedness, checked by equality of the projective coordinates, makes the extension from this DVR unique. Consequently this is the unique component dominating $V$, and it has the identical analytic point. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Apply the projection formula [F1] to $W\to V$ in step 5.1. Its function-field degree is one, so its coefficient $b_W\deg_W f^*\mathcal L$ is $b_V\deg_V\mathcal L$. Every component with point image contributes zero. This proves exact equality of the two atomic measures. Pulling back a model line bundle also preserves its metric: at any analytic point, take its residue field with its seminorm and its valuation ring. A projective model has a centre over this valuation ring by the same finite-coordinate scaling used in step 5.1; finite coordinate values are totally ordered, so a minimum exists without discreteness. A local frame at this centre has norm one. Its pulled-back frame has the same value, proving equality of the two norms on every section. Thus the equality of measures in this step is the equality attached to the unchanged model metric. [F1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Two normal models with the same identified normal generic curve have a common projective flat model by [F3]. Normalize its generic diagonal closure as in step 1.1. The normalization is finite, projective and flat, and its generic fibre stays that normal curve. Both projections are projective. Step 6.1 now compares either measure on this common normal refinement. Starting instead with nonnormal generic $Y$ and nonnormal models, normalize each model first. Their generic fibres are the same finite normalization $\widetilde Y$ by localization of integral closure. Use its common normal refinement and then push the seminorm points to $Y$ as in step 3.1. The coefficient equality survives that finite pushforward, including coincidences among atoms. This proves refinement invariance for the original arbitrary integral curve and its original models. Pullback of a line bundle nef on the original model stays nonnegative on vertical curves: a curve image is a point or a curve, and [F1] gives zero or a positive integer multiple of its original degree. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Suppose two models induce the same metric on the specified generic line bundle $L$. On the common normal refinement from step 7.1 their quotient line bundle has its specified rational section $1$ from the generic identification. Its divisor $D$ is vertical: on the generic fibre that section is nowhere zero. For each component of the fibre at $v$, evaluate at its seminorm point. If its local equation is $f$ and its local frame is $e$, then $1=fe$ and $\|e\|=1$, so equality of the original metrics gives $0=-\log\|1\|(\xi_V)=\operatorname{ord}_V(f)/b_V$. This also applies to any zero-dimensional fibre component, whose height-one local DVR and seminorm were constructed in steps 2.1–3.1; such a component carries no degree weight. After localizing the base at $v$, every horizontal coefficient of $D$ is zero by generic triviality and every vertical coefficient is zero by this calculation. The localized codimension-one zero test [F5] makes $D=0$ there as a Cartier divisor. Hence the quotient line bundle is trivial on that entire local model, so its restriction has degree zero on each fibre curve. The coefficient measures in step 4.1 agree. Applying step 7.1 before and after this comparison proves independence of the representing model for the fixed metric, without invoking an arbitrary-ring Cartier-to-Weil injection. [F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Finally let $D$ be a vertical Cartier divisor supported at $v$ on a normal model. Its canonical rational section is locally $f$ times a model frame, with $\operatorname{ord}_V(f)=a_V$. The very seminorm of step 3.1 therefore gives $\phi_D(\xi_V)=a_V/b_V$. Integration against the finite measure of step 4.1 yields
$$\int\phi_D\,c_1(L,\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal L,v})=\sum_V b_V\deg_V(\mathcal L|_V)\frac{a_V}{b_V}=\sum_V a_V\deg_V(\mathcal L|_V).$$
All multiplicities are positive, so no zero denominator occurs. The trivial bundle, zero divisor and empty coefficient sum give zero. This is the asserted finite vertical intersection pairing; the proof used no convergence of measures or higher-dimensional positivity. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
