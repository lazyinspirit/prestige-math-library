## Definition

Let $K\ge2$, let $D$ be a Borel probability measure on $\mathbb R^d\times\{1,\ldots,K\}$, and let $F:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^K$ be a continuous score map. At $(x,y)$ its **multiclass margin** is
$$m_F(x,y):=F(x)_y-\max_{j\ne y}F(x)_j.$$
The predicted label is
$$\widehat y_F(x):=\min\{j\in\{1,\ldots,K\}:F(x)_j=\max_{1\le l\le K}F(x)_l\}.$$
Its population zero-one error is
$$L_0(F):=D\bigl(\{(x,y):\widehat y_F(x)\ne y\}\bigr).$$
For $\gamma>0$, the **ramp loss** is
$$\ell_\gamma(r):=\begin{cases}0,&r< -\gamma,\\1+r/\gamma,&-\gamma\le r\le0,\\1,&r>0.\end{cases}$$
The population ramp risk is $R_\gamma(F):=\int\ell_\gamma(-m_F(x,y))\,D(d(x,y))$. For a sample $S=((x_1,y_1),\ldots,(x_n,y_n))$ of length $n\ge1$, the empirical ramp risk and empirical margin loss are
$$\widehat R_{\gamma,S}(F):=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\ell_\gamma(-m_F(x_i,y_i)),\qquad\widehat L_{\gamma,S}(F):=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf1\{m_F(x_i,y_i)\le\gamma\}.$$
The score coordinates are continuous, and finite maxima, comparisons, and the piecewise-linear ramp preserve Borel measurability. Thus the classification event and ramp loss in the displayed integrals are Borel sets or functions, so the risks are well-defined for $D$.
