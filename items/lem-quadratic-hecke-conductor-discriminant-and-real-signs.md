## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice and Dependent Choice. Let $L/K$ be any quadratic extension of number fields, put $n=[K:\mathbb Q]$ and $D_M=|d_M|$ for a number field $M$, and let $\chi=\chi_{L/K}$ be the canonical nontrivial primitive quadratic ray/idele Hecke character of this given extension supplied by the forward construction. Write its least modulus as $\mathfrak f_\chi=(\mathfrak f_{\chi,0},S_\chi)$. Then
$$\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}=\mathfrak d_{L/K},\qquad S_\chi=\{\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R:L\otimes_{K,\sigma}\mathbb R\cong\mathbb C\}.$$
Here $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is the integral relative trace-Gram discriminant ideal. Every real parity $\epsilon_\sigma$ in the primitive Hecke completion equals $1$ on $S_\chi$ and $0$ at every other real embedding; all complex local character components are trivial. Moreover
$$[L:\mathbb Q]=2n,\qquad D_L=D_K^2N\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}.$$
The finite conductor-discriminant parameter in the actual primitive completion is exactly
$$d_\chi:=D_KN\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}=D_L/D_K,$$
so its finite prefactor is $(D_L/D_K)^{s/2}$ with these real parities. For every real $Q\ge1$, $d_\chi\le Q$ implies $D_K\le Q$ and $D_L\le Q^2$. All dyadic, unramified and split cases, empty finite conductor support and $K=\mathbb Q$ are included. This is a forward assertion for each given quadratic extension; it does not realize an arbitrary quadratic Hecke character as an extension, identify a global norm kernel, prove the general CC-only conductor–discriminant theorem, or establish a full Stark conclusion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stated quadratic extension and all three choice hypotheses.

[F1] Under AC/CC/DC, every given quadratic extension has a canonical nontrivial continuous primitive ray/idele character. At a nonsplit finite place its component is the unique sign character with the actual local norm kernel; at split places it is trivial. Its least finite modulus exponent is the least $m\ge0$ killing $U_v^m$, with $U_v^0=\mathcal O_v^\times$. Its real component is sign exactly at the real places becoming complex, and its complex components are trivial. [[lem-quadratic-number-field-principal-product-and-canonical-hecke-character]]

[F2] The actual canonical quadratic local norm character has conductor $f_w\delta_w$, where $f_w$ is residue degree and $\delta_w$ is local different exponent. This includes wild dyadic and unramified quadratic fields. The split quadratic algebra has trivial norm character and unit trace-Gram discriminant. [[lem-quadratic-local-norm-character-conductor-equals-different]]

[F3] Under AC, finite separable number-field completion factors are the distinct local polynomial factors, and $L\otimes_KK_v\cong\prod_{w\mid v}L_w$, with the sum of factor degrees equal to $[L:K]$. [[lem-number-field-completions-as-local-polynomial-factors]]

[F4] Under AC the integral relative discriminant has valuation $\sum_{w\mid v}f_w\delta_w$. Its actual definition is the ideal generated by integral relative trace-Gram determinants. The localization proof supplies the integral tensor/basis/Gram-index comparison, not only a field tensor decomposition. [[lem-number-field-relative-discriminant-local-exponent]] [[def-relative-discriminant-ideal-of-a-number-field-extension]]

[F5] Nonzero integral number-field ideals have unique finite prime factorization, including the empty product for the unit ideal. [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]]

[F6] The AC relative-discriminant tower formula is $\mathfrak d_{L/\mathbb Q}=(d_K)^{[L:K]}(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{L/K})$ as ideals of $\mathbb Z$. Integral bases give a well-defined nonzero signed integer discriminant. Finite field degrees multiply in towers. [[thm-relative-discriminant-ideal-is-nonzero-integral-and-transitive]] [[thm-number-field-discriminant-is-well-defined-and-nonzero]] [[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]

[F7] The actual primitive Hecke Definition uses every real parity $\epsilon_\sigma\in\{0,1\}$, zero outside the infinite modulus, and completed finite factor $(|d_K|N\mathfrak f_{\chi,0})^{s/2}$. Its gamma conventions are $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+\epsilon_\sigma)$ and $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)$. [[def-finite-order-ray-class-hecke-character-and-l-function]]

[F8] For a nonzero integral ideal the norm is its finite additive quotient cardinality and is therefore a positive integer, including one for the unit ideal. [[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]] [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]]

[A1] AC, Countable Choice and DC are retained literally at their actual supplied interfaces. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement.

1.1 Take the actual canonical character $\chi=\chi_{L/K}$ supplied by [F1], already nontrivial and primitive for its least modulus; no converse realization is used. For each finite place $v$ let $n_v=\min\{m\ge0:\chi_v(U_v^m)=1\}$, exactly as in [F1]. Choose any $\alpha\in L\setminus K$; since $[L:K]=2$, its minimal polynomial has degree two and is separable in characteristic zero. By [F3] its factors over $K_v$ are either one irreducible quadratic or two distinct linear factors. Thus a nonsplit place has precisely one quadratic field factor $L_w/K_v$, and a split place has precisely two identity factors $K_v$. At a nonsplit place the local character in [F2] and the component in [F1] have the same actual norm kernel and are both the unique nontrivial sign character of that order-two quotient. Hence they are exactly the same character, not merely abstractly isomorphic characters. [F1, F2, F3, A1, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 At a nonsplit place, step 1.1 and [F2] give $n_v=f_w\delta_w$. At a split place $n_v=0$, and both degree-one factors have different exponent zero: their trace is identity and their trace dual is $\{x\in K_v:x\mathcal O_v\subseteq\mathcal O_v\}=\mathcal O_v$, whose inverse is the unit ideal. Consequently in all cases $n_v=\sum_{w\mid v}f_w\delta_w$. By the exact integral localization and Gram/index identity [F4], this equals $v(\mathfrak d_{L/K})$. The finite conductor $\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}=\prod_v\mathfrak p_v^{n_v}$ is a nonzero integral ideal with finite support by [F1], and the relative discriminant is nonzero integral by [F6]. Unique factorization [F5] therefore gives $\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}=\mathfrak d_{L/K}$, including the unit ideal when all exponents vanish. This uses the full integral comparison in [F4]; equality of field tensor products alone was not substituted for it. All dyadic and unramified cases are covered by the exact local exponents in [F2]. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The finite equality in step 2.1 leaves the infinite modulus to determine. By [F1], the real local character is sign precisely when $L\otimes_{K,\sigma}\mathbb R\cong\mathbb C$, and is trivial at each split real place; every complex component is trivial. The least infinite support in [F1] is exactly this nontrivial sign set, since deleting such a place would force triviality on its real factor. In the primitive convention [F7], the real parity is consequently one on that set and zero at every other real embedding. This also matches the ray/idele principal-ideal convention: the ray value on $(a)$, for $a\in K^\times$ prime to the finite modulus, is the inverse of the product of the omitted finite and archimedean local components, but these characters have order two, so inversion changes neither finite values nor real sign parity. Thus the actual real parities, not an independently chosen sign convention, are the asserted ones. The $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}$ normalization in [F7] remains unchanged. No assertion that an empty finite conductor implies a trivial character is needed; nontriviality and primitivity already come from [F1]. [F1, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By the ordinary degree tower law [F6], $[L:\mathbb Q]=[L:K][K:\mathbb Q]=2n$. To identify the absolute discriminants in its ideal tower identity, for any number field $M$ choose an integral basis defining $d_M$ in [F6]. Every integral $\mathbb Q$-basis of $M$ has an integer coordinate matrix $C$ in that integral basis, so its trace-Gram determinant is $d_M(\det C)^2$. The integral basis itself occurs among the tuples in the relative-discriminant definition [F4]. Hence $\mathfrak d_{M/\mathbb Q}=(d_M)$, by both ideal inclusions. Specializing the ideal tower formula [F6] to $[L:K]=2$ and taking positive generators in $\mathbb Z$ gives $D_L=D_K^2N\mathfrak d_{L/K}$. Substituting the finite ideal equality from step 2.1 proves $D_L=D_K^2N\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}$. The absolute values remove the signed discriminants without changing any prime exponent or inserting an archimedean factor. [F4, F6, F8, step 2.1, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 The actual primitive completion [F7] has finite prefactor $(D_KN\mathfrak f_{\chi,0})^{s/2}$. Since $D_K>0$, step 4.1 gives the exact finite parameter $d_\chi=D_KN\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}=D_L/D_K$ and thus the displayed prefactor $(D_L/D_K)^{s/2}$, with the real parities of step 3.1. By [F6] and [F8], $D_K$ and $N\mathfrak f_{\chi,0}$ are positive integers, so $d_\chi\ge D_K\ge1$. For every real $Q\ge1$ with $d_\chi\le Q$, this yields $D_K\le Q$ and $D_L=D_Kd_\chi\le Q^2$. This is only the finite completion scale; no height-dependent analytic conductor or inequality such as $\log(QT^n)\ge n$ is inferred. Together with steps 1.1–4.1 it proves all asserted identities and boundary cases for the given extension. It does not establish converse character realization, a global norm-kernel theorem, the general CC-only conductor formula or a full Stark conclusion. [F1, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
