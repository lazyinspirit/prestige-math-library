## Statement

Every $z\in D$ has exactly one canonical dyadic encoding specified in
[[def-qc-dyadic-cyclotomic-ring]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$, $O=\mathbb Z[\omega]$, and $D=O[1/2]$.

[F1] [[thm-cyclotomic-ring-of-integers]]: for $n=8$, $1,\omega,\omega^2,\omega^3$ is an integral basis of $O_{\mathbb Q(\omega)}=\mathbb Z[\omega]$.

## Proof


1.1 For $z\in D$, its definition gives $z=a/2^r$ for some $a\in O$ and integer $r\ge0$. Expand $a$ in the integral basis [F1]. Among all such exponents choose the least $r$; if $r>0$ and all four coefficients of $a$ were even, replacing $(a,r)$ by $(a/2,r-1)$ would contradict minimality. This gives an encoding of the required form. [given, F1, choose]

2.1 Suppose $a/2^r=b/2^s$ are two canonical encodings. If $r<s$, then $b=2^{s-r}a$, so every coefficient of $b$ is even, contrary to its canonical condition because $s>0$. If $s<r$, the same argument makes every coefficient of $a$ even. Hence $r=s$, and then $a=b$; uniqueness of the four coefficients follows from the basis property [F1]. The zero element is represented only by the all-zero numerator, and minimality forces its exponent to be $0$. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The proof uses only the integral-basis statement for $n=8$; it does not use
unique factorization of ideals or a choice axiom.
