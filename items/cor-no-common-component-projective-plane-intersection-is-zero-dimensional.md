## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be any field, let $R=k[x_0,x_1,x_2]$ and let
$F,G\in R$ be nonzero homogeneous forms of positive degrees with no common
nonconstant factor. Put $S=R/(F,G)$, a standard graded $k$-algebra with the
images of the variables of degree one, and $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$ with its
standard charts ([[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]]). Then:

1. $X\ne\varnothing$;
2. each standard chart $D_+(x_i)=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ with
   $A_i=(S_{x_i})_0$ is either empty or has Krull dimension
   $0$ ([[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]]), and $X$ has no strict chain
   $Z_0\subsetneq Z_1$ of nonempty irreducible closed subsets; in particular the
   chain dimension of the underlying space of $X$ is $0$
   ([[def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space]]);
3. $\dim S=1$: the homogeneous coordinate ring of $X$ has ring dimension one
   and never ring dimension zero, and it is not Artinian.

The argument is valid over an arbitrary field and uses the Axiom of Choice only
through the cited prime-existence, height-theorem, irreducible-closed-subset
and Noetherian-spectrum suppliers.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the polynomial ring $R=k[x_0,x_1,x_2]$ with its standard grading, its maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(x_0,x_1,x_2)$, nonzero homogeneous $F,G\in R$ of positive degrees with no common nonconstant factor, the quotient $S=R/(F,G)$, and $X=\operatorname{Proj}S$ with standard charts $D_+(x_i)=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$, $A_i=(S_{x_i})_0$.

[L1] $\dim k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]=n$ for a field $k$ and $n\ge0$ ([[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]]); the height of a prime is the dimension of the localization at it and the dimension of a nonzero ring is the supremum of the lengths of strict chains of primes ([[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]]), so a strict chain $P_0\subsetneq P_1$ of primes satisfies $\operatorname{ht}(P_0)+1\le\operatorname{ht}(P_1)$.

[L2] $R$ is an integral domain and each quotient of $R$ by a prime ideal is a domain: polynomial rings over a domain are domains and a proper ideal is prime exactly when its quotient is a domain ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]], [[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]]).

[L3] Contraction along $R\to R/I$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(R/I)$ onto the primes of $R$ containing $I$, and it restricts to a bijection on homogeneous primes; a proper homogeneous prime of $R$ contains no nonzero element of degree zero and hence lies in $\mathfrak m$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]], [[def-homogeneous-polynomial-and-homogeneous-ideal]]).

[L4] If nonzero homogeneous plane forms of positive degrees have no common nonconstant factor, then no height-one prime of $R$ contains both of them ([[lem-coprime-plane-forms-form-a-homogeneous-regular-sequence]]).

[L5] Assume AC. A prime minimal over an ideal generated by $n\ge1$ elements of a Noetherian ring has height at most $n$ ([[thm-krull-height-theorem]]); over a Noetherian ring every proper ideal has a minimal prime over it ([[lem-minimal-prime-over-an-ideal-exists]]).

[L6] $X$ has as points the homogeneous primes of $S$ with $S_+\nsubseteq\mathfrak p$, its standard charts are the affine schemes $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ and they cover $X$ ([[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]]); for each $i$ the map $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak pS_{x_i}\cap A_i$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from the homogeneous primes of $S$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak p$ onto the points of $D_+(x_i)$ ([[lem-projective-standard-chart-prime-and-local-ring-correspondence]]).

[L7] Assume AC. A nonempty Zariski-closed subset $Z\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ is irreducible exactly when its radical defining ideal is prime ([[thm-irreducible-closed-subsets-and-prime-ideals]]), irreducibility being the property that the space is nonempty and not the union of two proper closed subsets ([[def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset]]); the chain dimension of a Noetherian space is the supremum of the lengths of strict chains of nonempty irreducible closed subsets ([[def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space]]).

[L8] The Axiom of Choice is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L9] A field is Noetherian, since its only ideals are $0$ and the whole ring; if a commutative ring is Noetherian, then its polynomial ring in finitely many variables is Noetherian ([[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]). Hence $R=k[x_0,x_1,x_2]$ is Noetherian.

[L10] Assume AC. Each standard chart ring $A_i=(S_{x_i})_0$ is a finite-type $k$-algebra: it is generated by the two ratios $x_j/x_i$ for $j\ne i$, since every degree-zero fraction $s/x_i^m$ has $s\in S_m$ spanned by degree-$m$ monomials. Therefore each $A_i$ is Noetherian by [[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]], and each chart $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ is a Noetherian topological space by [[thm-noetherian-ring-has-noetherian-spectrum]]. The three standard charts are a finite open cover of $X$ ([[def-projective-scheme-from-a-homogeneous-quotient]]); a descending chain of closed subsets of $X$ stabilizes on each chart and then stabilizes on $X$ because the cover is finite. Thus $X$ is Noetherian, as required to apply the chain-dimension definition in [L7].



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The ring $R$ has dimension $3$ by [L1], and $0\subsetneq(x_0)\subsetneq(x_0,x_1)\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ is a strict chain of primes of $R$: each displayed ideal is prime, since the successive quotients are $k[x_1,x_2]$, $k[x_1]$, $k$ and polynomial rings over a domain are domains by [L2]. Hence $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\ge3$, while $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)\le\dim R=3$ because chains of primes below $\mathfrak m$ are chains of primes of $R$; so $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=3$, and every prime $P\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ satisfies $\operatorname{ht}(P)+1\le\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)$, that is, $\operatorname{ht}(P)\le2$. Finally, every proper homogeneous prime $P$ of $R$ lies in $\mathfrak m$ by [L3]. [L1, L2, L3, algebra]

1.2 Let $\mathfrak q\subseteq R$ be a prime with $(F,G)\subseteq\mathfrak q$. Then $\mathfrak q\ne0$ because $F\in\mathfrak q$ and $F\ne0$, so $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)\ge1$ by the chain $0\subsetneq\mathfrak q$ of primes of the domain $R$; if $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)=1$, then $\mathfrak q$ is a height-one prime containing both $F$ and $G$, contradicting [L4]. Hence every prime of $R$ containing $(F,G)$ has height at least two. [L1, L2, L4]

2.1 By [L3] the primes of $S=R/(F,G)$ correspond inclusion-preservingly to the primes of $R$ containing $(F,G)$, and homogeneous primes to homogeneous primes; moreover the irrelevant ideal is $S_+=\mathfrak m/(F,G)$. Hence for a prime $\mathfrak q\subseteq S$ with preimage $Q\subseteq R$ one has $S_+\nsubseteq\mathfrak q$ if and only if $\mathfrak m\nsubseteq Q$; since a proper homogeneous prime $Q$ of $R$ satisfies $Q\subseteq\mathfrak m$ by 1.1, this is equivalent to $Q\subsetneq\mathfrak m$. [L3, step 1.1]

2.2 By [L9], $R$ is Noetherian. The ideal $(F,G)$ is proper because positive-degree homogeneous forms lie in $\mathfrak m=(x_0,x_1,x_2)\ne R$. Let $\mathfrak P$ be a prime minimal over $(F,G)$, which exists by [L5]. Then $\mathfrak P$ is homogeneous. Indeed, let $\mathfrak P'$ be the ideal generated by all homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak P$; it is a homogeneous ideal with $(F,G)\subseteq\mathfrak P'\subseteq\mathfrak P$, since $F,G$ are homogeneous elements of $\mathfrak P$. It is prime: for homogeneous $a,b$ with $ab\in\mathfrak P'$ we have $ab\in\mathfrak P$, so $a\in\mathfrak P$ or $b\in\mathfrak P$, hence $a\in\mathfrak P'$ or $b\in\mathfrak P'$; an ideal generated by homogeneous elements with this property is prime, because for arbitrary $x,y$ with $xy\in\mathfrak P'$ one inducts on $\deg_{\max}(x)+\deg_{\max}(y)$, where $\deg_{\max}$ is the largest degree of a homogeneous component: the components of top degree $d$ of $x$ and $e$ of $y$ multiply to the top-degree component $x_dy_e\in\mathfrak P'$, so $x_d\in\mathfrak P'$ or $y_e\in\mathfrak P'$, and in the first case $(x-x_d)y\in\mathfrak P'$ with $\deg_{\max}(x-x_d)<d$ gives $x-x_d\in\mathfrak P'$ or $y\in\mathfrak P'$ by induction, whence $x\in\mathfrak P'$ or $y\in\mathfrak P'$, the other case being symmetric. Thus $\mathfrak P'$ is a prime containing $(F,G)$ and contained in $\mathfrak P$, so minimality forces $\mathfrak P'=\mathfrak P$ and $\mathfrak P$ is homogeneous. By 1.2, $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak P)\ge2$, and by the Noetherian Krull height theorem in [L5] applied to the two generators $F,G$ we have $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak P)\le2$, so $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak P)=2$; since $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=3$ by 1.1, the inclusion $\mathfrak P\subseteq\mathfrak m$ of 1.1 is strict: $\mathfrak P\subsetneq\mathfrak m$. [L5, L9, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Every standard chart ring $A_i$ is either zero or of Krull dimension $0$. By [L6] the primes of $A_i$ correspond inclusion-preservingly to the homogeneous primes of $S$ avoiding $x_i$, so it suffices to rule out a strict chain $\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\mathfrak p_1$ of homogeneous primes of $S$ with $x_i\notin\mathfrak p_1$. By 2.1 such a chain lifts to homogeneous primes $P_0\subsetneq P_1$ of $R$ containing $(F,G)$ and, since $x_i$ lies in neither $\mathfrak p_j$, with $x_i\notin P_1$. Then $\operatorname{ht}(P_0)\ge2$ by 1.2; on the other hand $P_1$ is a proper homogeneous prime with $P_1\ne\mathfrak m$, so $P_1\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ by 1.1 and $\operatorname{ht}(P_1)\le2$; and $\operatorname{ht}(P_0)+1\le\operatorname{ht}(P_1)$ by [L1]. Hence $2\le\operatorname{ht}(P_0)<\operatorname{ht}(P_1)\le2$, a contradiction. Therefore $A_i$ admits no strict chain of two primes. [L1, L6, step 2.1, step 1.2]

3.2 By 2.2 the prime $\mathfrak P$ is homogeneous with $\mathfrak P\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ and $(F,G)\subseteq\mathfrak P$, so by 2.1 its image $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak P/(F,G)$ is a homogeneous prime of $S$ with $S_+\nsubseteq\mathfrak p$; hence $\mathfrak p$ is a point of $X$ by [L6] and $X\ne\varnothing$. [L6, step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.3 $\dim S=1$. For the lower bound, $\mathfrak P\subsetneq\mathfrak m$ from 2.2 gives via 2.1 a strict chain of primes of $S$, so $\dim S\ge1$ and $S\ne0$. For the upper bound, suppose $\mathfrak q_0\subsetneq\mathfrak q_1\subsetneq\mathfrak q_2$ is a strict chain of primes of $S$; by 2.1 it lifts to primes $Q_0\subsetneq Q_1\subsetneq Q_2$ of $R$ all containing $(F,G)$. By 2.2 we have $\operatorname{ht}(Q_0)\ge2$, so there is a strict chain of length two of primes below $Q_0$; adjoining $Q_0\subsetneq Q_1\subsetneq Q_2$ gives a strict chain of length $4$ in $R$, contradicting $\dim R=3$ from 1.1. Hence $\dim S=1$, and in particular $S$ is not zero-dimensional: it is also not Artinian, because in an Artinian ring every prime is maximal ([[thm-artinian-ring-primes-are-maximal]]), which would force $\dim S=0$. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 2.2]

4.1 By [L10], $X$ is Noetherian, so its chain dimension in [L7] is defined. $X$ has no strict chain $Z_0\subsetneq Z_1$ of nonempty irreducible closed subsets. Suppose such a chain is given. Some standard chart $D=D_+(x_i)$ meets $Z_0$, since the charts cover $X$ by [L6]; then the subsets $W_j=Z_j\cap D$ of the affine chart $D=\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$ are nonempty, closed in $D$, and satisfy $W_0\subseteq W_1$. A nonempty open subset $U$ of an irreducible space $Z$ is irreducible and dense: if $U=F_1\cup F_2$ with $F_1,F_2$ closed in $U$, then $Z=\overline{F_1}\cup\overline{F_2}\cup(Z\setminus U)$ is a union of closed subsets, so irreducibility of $Z$ forces one of the three to equal $Z$, and since $Z\setminus U\ne Z$ while $\overline{F_k}\cap U=F_k$, this gives $F_1=U$ or $F_2=U$; and if the closure of $U$ in $Z$ were a proper closed subset, then $Z=\overline U\cup(Z\setminus U)$ would be a union of two proper closed subsets. Applying this to the open subset $W_j$ of the irreducible space $Z_j$ shows that $W_j$ is irreducible and dense in $Z_j$, hence that its closure in $X$ is $Z_j$; thus $W_0\ne W_1$, since $W_0=W_1$ would give $Z_0=Z_1$. The $W_j$ are nonempty irreducible closed subsets of $\operatorname{Spec}(A_i)$, so by [L7] their radical defining ideals are distinct primes and form a strict chain of two primes of $A_i$, contradicting 3.1. Hence no such chain $Z_0\subsetneq Z_1$ exists, and the chain dimension of $X$ is $0$. [L6, L7, L10, step 3.1]

5.1 Claim 1 is 3.2, claim 2 is 3.1 together with 4.1, and claim 3 is 3.3. The hypotheses actually used are: $F,G$ nonzero homogeneous of positive degrees with no common nonconstant factor, over an arbitrary field $k$; the Axiom of Choice enters only through the minimal-prime and height-theorem suppliers of [L5] the irreducible-closed-subset characterisation of [L7], and the Noetherian-spectrum supplier of [L10], and it is the standing assumption [L8]. Noetherianity of the chart rings is used in [L10] to justify the topological dimension convention in 4.1. Finiteness of $X$ is not assumed, and the minimal prime chosen in 2.2 is proved homogeneous rather than chosen inside the homogeneous locus. [L8, L10, step 3.1, step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.1] ∎
