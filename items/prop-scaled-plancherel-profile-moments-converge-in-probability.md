## Statement

For $n\ge1$, let $\lambda$ range over $Y_n$ under the Plancherel measures $P_n$, let $\bar\lambda$ be the $\sqrt n$-scaled Russian profile of [[def-russian-profile-and-sqrt-n-scaling-of-a-young-diagram]], and let $\Omega$ be the limit profile of [[def-logan-shepp-vershik-kerov-limit-profile]]. Under the Plancherel measures $P_n$, for every integer $k\ge0$,
$$\int_{\mathbb R}\bigl(\bar\lambda(x)-\Omega(x)\bigr)x^k\,dx\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{in probability as }n\to\infty.$$
Equivalently, $\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]\to\tilde p_j[\Omega]$ in probability for every $j\ge2$. Moreover, for every $f$ in the algebra $A=\mathbb R[\tilde p_2,\tilde p_3,\dots]$,
$$\mathbb E_{P_n}\bigl[f(\bar\lambda)\bigr]\longrightarrow f[\Omega].$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$; the probability space $(Y_n,P_n)$ of [[def-plancherel-measure-on-partitions]] and [[prop-plancherel-weights-sum-to-one]]; the observables $p_\rho^\#$ and the profile moments $\tilde p_k[\omega]=k(k-1)\int_{\mathbb R}x^{k-2}\sigma_\omega(x)\,dx$ with $\sigma_\omega=\tfrac12(\omega-|x|)$, together with the scaling identity $\tilde p_k[\bar\lambda]=n^{-k/2}\tilde p_k[\lambda(\cdot)]$ ([[def-shifted-character-observables-and-profile-moments]], [[def-russian-profile-and-sqrt-n-scaling-of-a-young-diagram]]); the algebra $A=\mathbb R[\tilde p_2,\tilde p_3,\dots]$; and $\Omega\in D_0$ ([[def-logan-shepp-vershik-kerov-limit-profile]]).

[F1] For every partition $\rho$ with $r=|\rho|$ and every $n\ge0$, $\mathbb E_{P_n}[p_\rho^\#]=n^{\downarrow r}$ for $\rho=(1^r)$ and $\mathbb E_{P_n}[p_\rho^\#]=0$ for $\rho\ne(1^r)$ ([[prop-plancherel-expectations-of-shifted-character-observables]]).

[F2] The scaling of profile moments is $\tilde p_k[\bar\lambda]=n^{-k/2}\tilde p_k[\lambda(\cdot)]$ for $\lambda\vdash n$ with $n\ge1$, and $\sigma_\omega=\tfrac12(\omega-|x|)$ for $\omega\in D_0$ ([[def-shifted-character-observables-and-profile-moments]], [[def-russian-profile-and-sqrt-n-scaling-of-a-young-diagram]]).

[F3] The family $\{p_\rho^\#\}$ is a linear basis of $A$, the weights $\operatorname{wt}(p_\rho^\#)=|\rho|+\ell(\rho)$ define an algebra filtration of $A$ coinciding with the weight filtration generated by $\operatorname{wt}(\tilde p_k)=k$, and this filtration dominates the degree filtration $\deg_1(p_\rho^\#)=|\rho|+m_1(\rho)\le\operatorname{wt}(p_\rho^\#)$ ([[thm-shifted-character-basis-and-weight-filtration]]).

[F4] The functionals $L_k(p_\rho^\#)=1$ for $k$ even and $\rho=(1^{k/2})$, and $L_k(p_\rho^\#)=0$ otherwise, are multiplicative on weight-homogeneous elements, and $L_{2m}(\tilde p_{2m})=\binom{2m}{m}$ and $L_k(\tilde p_k)=0$ for odd $k$ ([[lem-profile-moment-generators-and-shifted-character-basis]]).

[F5] For the limit profile, $\tilde p_{2m}[\Omega]=\binom{2m}{m}$ for $m\ge1$ and $\tilde p_k[\Omega]=0$ for odd $k$ ([[prop-limit-profile-moments-are-central-binomial-coefficients]]).

[F6] $\Omega\in D_0$ with $\sigma_\Omega=\tfrac12(\Omega-|x|)$ supported in $[-2,2]$ ([[def-logan-shepp-vershik-kerov-limit-profile]]).

[F7] Convergence in probability means for every $\varepsilon>0$, $\mathbb P(|X_n-X|>\varepsilon)\to0$ ([[def-convergence-in-probability]]), and for a random variable $Y$ with finite variance and mean $\mu$, $\mathbb P(|Y-\mu|\ge\varepsilon)\le\operatorname{Var}(Y)/\varepsilon^2$ ([[cor-chebyshev-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Monomial expansion: let $m=\tilde p_{j_1}\cdots\tilde p_{j_i}$ be a monomial in the generators of $A$ of total weight $k=j_1+\cdots+j_i$, and fix $n\ge1$; then $m$ lies in the weight filtration level $k$ of [F3], so its expansion $m=\sum_\rho m_\rho\,p_\rho^\#$ in the basis of [F3] has $m_\rho=0$ whenever $\operatorname{wt}(p_\rho^\#)>k$; and by [F2] the scaling identity applied to the $i$ factors gives $m(\bar\lambda)=n^{-k/2}m(\lambda(\cdot))=n^{-k/2}\sum_{\operatorname{wt}(\rho)\le k}m_\rho\,p_\rho^\#(\lambda)$. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Pointwise integral identity: for every $k\ge0$, every $\lambda\vdash n$ and $\omega\in D_0$ the definition of the profile moments in [F2] gives $\tilde p_{k+2}[\omega]=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{2}\int_{\mathbb R}x^k(\omega(x)-|x|)\,dx$; subtracting the same identity for $\omega=\Omega$, which lies in $D_0$ by [F6], yields the pointwise identity $\int_{\mathbb R}(\bar\lambda(x)-\Omega(x))x^k\,dx=\frac{2}{(k+1)(k+2)}\bigl(\tilde p_{k+2}[\bar\lambda]-\tilde p_{k+2}[\Omega]\bigr)$ on $Y_n$. [given, F2, F6, algebra]

2.1 Limit of expectations: by step 1.1 and [F1], $\mathbb E_{P_n}[m(\bar\lambda)]=n^{-k/2}\sum_{2r\le k}m_{(1^r)}\,n^{\downarrow r}$, and $n^{-k/2}n^{\downarrow r}=n^{r-k/2}\prod_{s=0}^{r-1}(1-s/n)$ tends to $1$ when $2r=k$ and to $0$ when $2r<k$; hence $\mathbb E_{P_n}[m(\bar\lambda)]$ tends to $m_{(1^{k/2})}$ if $k$ is even and to $0$ if $k$ is odd. Define the linear functional $L$ on $A$ by $L(m):=m_{(1^{k/2})}$ for a monomial of even weight $k$ and $L(m):=0$ for $k$ odd, extended linearly over the finitely many monomials of an element of $A$; then $\mathbb E_{P_n}[f(\bar\lambda)]\to L(f)$ for every $f\in A$. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Multiplicativity: the functional $L$ of step 2.1 is the linear extension of the functionals $L_k$ of [F4], since for a monomial of weight $k$ the coefficient of $p_{(1^{k/2})}^\#$ is exactly the value of $L_k$ on its weight-$k$ component and lower-weight components contribute nothing; hence by the multiplicativity clause of [F4], applied to the weight-homogeneous components of two monomials, $L(mm')=L(m)L(m')$ for all monomials, and by linearity $L$ is multiplicative on $A$. [given, F3, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Evaluation at the limit profile: by [F4] one has $L(\tilde p_{2m})=\binom{2m}{m}$ and $L(\tilde p_k)=0$ for odd $k$, while by [F5] the evaluation functional $f\mapsto f[\Omega]$ has exactly the same values on the generators $\tilde p_k$ and is multiplicative with $1[\Omega]=1$; since $A$ is generated by the $\tilde p_k$, $L(f)=f[\Omega]$ for every $f\in A$, and step 2.1 gives $\mathbb E_{P_n}[f(\bar\lambda)]\to f[\Omega]$ for every $f\in A$. [given, F4, F5, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Convergence of the moments in probability: applying step 4.1 to $f=\tilde p_j$ and to $f=\tilde p_j^2$, and using that evaluation at $\Omega$ is multiplicative, gives $\mathbb E_{P_n}[\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]]\to\tilde p_j[\Omega]$ and $\mathbb E_{P_n}[\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]^2]\to\tilde p_j[\Omega]^2$, so the variances of the random variables $\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]$ on the finite probability space $(Y_n,P_n)$ tend to $0$; for every $\varepsilon>0$, the mean differs from $\tilde p_j[\Omega]$ by less than $\varepsilon/2$ for all sufficiently large $n$. The event $|\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]-\tilde p_j[\Omega]|\ge\varepsilon$ is then contained in the event of deviation at least $\varepsilon/2$ from the current mean, so Chebyshev [F7] gives $\mathbb P(|\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]-\tilde p_j[\Omega]|\ge\varepsilon)\le4\operatorname{Var}(\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda])/\varepsilon^2\to0$, that is, $\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]\to\tilde p_j[\Omega]$ in probability for every $j\ge2$. [given, F7, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Conclusion: for each fixed integer $k\ge0$, put $j=k+2$; step 5.1 gives $\tilde p_j[\bar\lambda]\to\tilde p_j[\Omega]$ in probability, and the identity of step 1.2 exhibits $\int_{\mathbb R}(\bar\lambda-\Omega)x^k\,dx$ as a fixed nonzero multiple of the difference, so $\int_{\mathbb R}(\bar\lambda-\Omega)x^k\,dx\to0$ in probability as well; conversely the same identity transfers the latter convergence to the former. This proves the integral display and its equivalent moment form, and step 4.1 proves the assertion about every $f\in A$. No choice principle is used: all steps are finite computations on the finite probability spaces $(Y_n,P_n)$. [given, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
