## Example

Let $(X,d)$ be a compact metric space. Let $L$ be the smallest real vector sublattice of $C(X,\mathbb R)$ containing every constant function and every distance function
$$d_a:X\longrightarrow\mathbb R,\qquad d_a(x):=d(a,x)\quad(a\in X).$$
Then $L$ is uniformly dense in $C(X,\mathbb R)$: for every $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $g\in L$ with $|g(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon$ for every $x\in X$. When $X$ is nonempty this is density for the topology of uniform convergence.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A compact metric space $(X,d)$ and the real vector sublattice $L$ generated by constants and the distance functions $d_a$.

[L1] On a compact Hausdorff space, a unital point-separating real vector sublattice contains, for every $f\in C(X,\mathbb R)$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, a member within $\varepsilon$ of $f$ at every point; for nonempty $X$ this is density for the topology of uniform convergence ([[thm-lattice-stone-weierstrass]]).

[L2] A unital real vector sublattice contains all constants and separates points when every distinct pair is distinguished by one member ([[def-separating-real-function-lattice]]).

[L3] A metric satisfies $d(x,y)=0$ exactly when $x=y$, symmetry, and $d(x,z)\le d(x,y)+d(y,z)$ ([[def-metric-space]]).

[L4] A metric space is compact if and only if it is compact as a topological space in its metric topology ([[thm-compactness-agrees-with-metric-compactness]], clause 1).

[L5] Every metric space is Hausdorff: distinct points are separated by disjoint open balls ([[thm-metric-hausdorff-separation]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L4] and [L5], the metric topology makes $X$ a compact Hausdorff space. [L4, L5]

1.2 For $a,x,y\in X$, the triangle inequality and symmetry in [L3] give $d(a,x)\le d(a,y)+d(x,y)$ and $d(a,y)\le d(a,x)+d(x,y)$; hence $|d_a(x)-d_a(y)|\le d(x,y)$, so every $d_a$ is continuous. [L3, algebra]

2.1 The generated lattice $L$ is unital by construction. If $x\ne y$, then $d_x(x)=0$ while $d_x(y)=d(x,y)\ne0$ by [L3], so $d_x\in L$ separates $x$ and $y$; thus $L$ is point-separating in the sense of [L2]. [step 1.2, L2, L3]

3.1 Apply [L1] to the unital point-separating real vector sublattice $L$ on the compact Hausdorff space of step 1.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L1] ∎
