## Statement

Assume AC. Let $G$ be a finite group and $p$ a prime. In the Grothendieck group $R_{\mathbb F_p}(G)$ of finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p[G]$-modules, the rational vector space $R_{\mathbb F_p}(G)\otimes\mathbb Q$ is generated by modules induced from cyclic subgroups of order prime to $p$. More precisely, choose an Artin permutation relation
$$|G|1_G=\sum_i a_i\operatorname{Ind}_{C_i}^G1_{C_i}$$
with $C_i$ cyclic and $a_i\in\mathbb Z$. Write $C_i=P_i\times C_i'$ for the Sylow $p$-subgroup and its prime-to-$p$ complement. Then every finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p[G]$-module $M$ satisfies the integral relation
$$|G|^2[M]=\sum_i a_i\frac{|G|}{|P_i|}\,[\operatorname{Ind}_{C_i'}^G\operatorname{Res}_{C_i'}^G M].$$
All coefficients are integers. No semisimplicity of $\mathbb F_p[G]$ is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite group, prime, modular representation and explicit AC in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The displayed integer Artin permutation relation exists. [[lem-artin-cyclic-permutation-relation]]

[F2] For a splitting $p$-modular system, irreducible Brauer characters are independent on the $p$-regular elements and Brauer characters are additive on short exact sequences. [[thm-irreducible-brauer-characters-are-linearly-independent-on-p-regular-elements]] [[thm-brauer-character-is-additive-on-short-exact-sequences]]

[F3] A semisimple ring is a finite product of matrix rings over division rings, and finite division rings are fields. [[thm-wedderburn-artin-theorem]] [[lem-finite-division-rings-are-fields]]

[F4] Characteristic-zero finite-group representations are semisimple, finite residue extensions lift to unramified local extensions, and a splitting modular system splits every subgroup. [[thm-maschkes-theorem-for-finite-groups-over-fields-whose-characteristic-does-not-divide-the-group-order]] [[thm-finite-unramified-local-extensions-classified-by-residue-extensions]] [[def-splitting-p-modular-system-for-a-finite-group]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** evaluate Artin's permutation identity on p-regular elements and remove the p-part of each cyclic inducing subgroup.

1.1 Choose a finite splitting $p$-modular system $(K,\mathcal O,k)$ with finite residue extension $k/\mathbb F_p$ for $G$. To see that such a choice is available under AC, over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb Q_p$ the characteristic-zero group algebras of all finitely many subgroups are semisimple and have finitely many finite-dimensional simple modules; their finitely many matrix entries lie in one finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$. Over an algebraic closure of $\mathbb F_p$, the modular group algebras of those subgroups have finitely many simple modules, whose finitely many matrix entries lie in one finite residue extension. Adjoining the corresponding finite unramified residue extension to the first field gives a finite local field that splits both characteristic-zero and residue representations. Its valuation ring is a complete DVR. A further finite cyclotomic extension supplies the roots needed for the fixed complex realization of Brauer values. [A1, F3, F4, given, choose, construct, algebra]

1.2 For a finite-dimensional $kG$-module $N$, its Brauer character on $p$-regular elements is additive by [F2], so defines a homomorphism from $R_k(G)$. By the independence part of [F2] this homomorphism is injective. Tensor products multiply Brauer characters, since a $p$-regular element acts semisimply with prime-to-$p$ root-of-unity eigenvalues and tensor-product eigenvalues are their pairwise products. Induction has the usual finite-coset character formula: a $p$-regular element contributes the traces from precisely those cosets fixed by it, with their conjugated actions. This follows directly from the block permutation matrix on coset summands and needs no modular semisimplicity. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Scalar extension $R_{\mathbb F_p}(G)\to R_k(G)$ is injective. Indeed $\mathbb F_p[G]$ is a finite-dimensional algebra, its radical is nilpotent, and every simple module factors through the semisimple quotient. By [F3] that quotient is a finite product $\prod_i M_{d_i}(\mathbb F_{p^{f_i}})$. The chosen splitting field contains all these finite centers. After tensoring with $k$, each factor splits as a product of matrix algebras, one for each embedding of its center into $k$. The simple modules coming from different original factors have disjoint sets of split composition factors; hence their scalar-extension classes are linearly independent in the free Grothendieck group on simple modules. [F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $C=P\times C'$ be cyclic, with $P$ its $p$-part, and let $N$ be a $kC$-module. Every $p$-regular element of $C$ lies in $C'$. At such an element, the finite-coset formula in step 1.2 gives the Brauer character of $\operatorname{Ind}_{C'}^C\operatorname{Res}_{C'}^C N$ as $|P|$ times that of $N$. Step 1.2 therefore proves $[\operatorname{Ind}_{C'}^C\operatorname{Res}_{C'}^C N]=|P|[N]$ in $R_k(C)$. The same conclusion holds in $R_{\mathbb F_p}(C)$ by step 2.1. [step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra]

4.1 Multiply the Artin identity [F1] pointwise on $p$-regular elements by the Brauer character of $M\otimes k$. The finite-coset formula and the tensor identity $k[G/C]\otimes(M\otimes k)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_C^G\operatorname{Res}_C^G(M\otimes k)$ identify the right side with the sum of the corresponding induced Brauer characters. Step 1.2 makes this an equality in $R_k(G)$; step 2.1 descends it to $R_{\mathbb F_p}(G)$. Replace each cyclic $C_i$ by its prime-to-$p$ complement using step 3.1 and multiply by $|G|$, divisible by every $|P_i|$. This gives the displayed integral relation. Dividing by $|G|^2$ proves the rational generation. The trivial group and $M=0$ are included. [F1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
