## Statement

Assume AC. Let $n\ge1$ and let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent integrable real random variables on one probability space. Suppose finite real numbers $a_i\le b_i$ satisfy $a_i\le X_i\le b_i$ almost surely for every $i$. Put $V=\sum_{i=1}^n(b_i-a_i)^2$ and $S=\sum_{i=1}^n(X_i-\mathbb E X_i)$. For every $t>0$, if $V>0$ then
$$\mathbb P(|S|\ge t)\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{2t^2}{V}\right),$$
and if $V=0$ then $\mathbb P(|S|\ge t)=0$. The two one-sided tails are each bounded by $\exp(-2t^2/V)$ when $V>0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $n\ge1$, independent integrable real random variables $X_i$, and deterministic finite intervals $[a_i,b_i]$ containing $X_i$ almost surely.

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice, namely every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). AC is used by the supplied conditional-expectation and conditional-Hoeffding results.

[F1] Independence means that the sigma-algebras generated by the random variables are an independent family ([[def-independent-random-elements]]).

[F2] Disjoint groups from an independent family generate independent sigma-algebras ([[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]]).

[F3] If an integrable random variable is independent of a sigma-algebra, its conditional expectation given that sigma-algebra is its ordinary expectation ([[lem-conditioning-a-known-variable-and-an-independent-variable]]).

[F4] Expectation is monotone: $X\le Y$ almost surely implies $\mathbb E X\le\mathbb E Y$ ([[cor-expectation-linearity-monotonicity-and-modulus-bound]]).

[F5] If $D\in L^1$, $\mathbb E[D\mid\mathcal G]=0$, and finite $\mathcal G$-measurable endpoints $A\le D\le B$ have deterministic width $B-A\le c$, then $\mathbb E[e^{\lambda D}\mid\mathcal G]\le e^{\lambda^2c^2/8}$ almost surely ([[lem-conditional-hoeffding-bound-for-bounded-martingale-differences]]).

[F6] Conditional expectation is linear, preserves constants, and preserves the unconditional expectation, so $\mathbb E[\mathbb E[Y\mid\mathcal G]]=\mathbb E Y$ ([[thm-basic-algebra-and-order-properties-of-conditional-expectation]]).

[F7] A bounded measurable factor known under $\mathcal G$ can be taken outside conditional expectation ([[thm-taking-out-what-is-known]]).

[F8] For a nonnegative random variable $Y$ and $r>0$, $\mathbb P(Y\ge r)\le\mathbb E[Y]/r$ ([[cor-markov-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

[F9] The probability of a finite union of events is at most the sum of their probabilities ([[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]).

[F10] The real exponential is continuous and strictly increasing, positive, and satisfies $e^{-x}=1/e^x$ and $e^{x+y}=e^xe^y$ ([[cor-exponential-reciprocal-and-positivity]], [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]], [[thm-exponential-addition-formula]]).

[F11] Every closed bounded interval $[a,b]$ with $a\le b$ is compact ([[thm-heine-borel-r]]).

[F12] A continuous real function is bounded on a compact subset of $\mathbb R$ ([[cor-boundedness-theorem-r]]).

[F13] Sums and differences of measurable real-valued functions are measurable ([[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]).

## Proof

1.1 Set $\mathcal F_0=\{\varnothing,\Omega\}$, $\mathcal F_i=\sigma(X_1,\ldots,X_i)$, $D_i=X_i-\mathbb E X_i$, and $c_i=b_i-a_i$. By [F13], $D_i$ is $\mathcal F_i$-measurable. By [F1] and [F2], $X_i$ is independent of $\mathcal F_{i-1}$; [F3] gives $\mathbb E[X_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]=\mathbb E X_i$, and conditional linearity and preservation of constants in [F6] therefore give $\mathbb E[D_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]=0$ almost surely. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, F6, F13]

1.2 By [F4], $a_i\le\mathbb E X_i\le b_i$. Therefore $D_i$ lies almost surely in the finite interval $[a_i-\mathbb E X_i,b_i-\mathbb E X_i]$, whose endpoints are $\mathcal F_{i-1}$-measurable, contain $0$, and have width $c_i$; in particular $|D_i|\le c_i$. [given, F4]

2.1 Apply [F5] to each $D_i$ and the filtration $\mathcal F_{i-1}$ from step 1.1. For every real $\lambda$, $\mathbb E[e^{\lambda D_i}\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}]\le e^{\lambda^2c_i^2/8}$ almost surely. [A1, F5, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Let $S_k=\sum_{i=1}^kD_i$ and $S_0=0$. Step 1.2 gives $|D_i|\le c_i$, hence $S_{k-1}\in[-C,C]$ for $C:=\sum_{i<k}c_i$. This closed bounded interval is compact by [F11], and $x\mapsto e^{\lambda x}$ is continuous by [F10] and continuity of scalar multiplication, so [F12] makes $e^{\lambda S_{k-1}}$ bounded. Also $x\mapsto e^{\lambda x}$ is Borel: its open-set preimages are open, and the class of Borel sets whose preimages are Borel is a sigma-algebra containing the opens, hence contains the Borel sigma-algebra ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]). For each Borel $B$, the preimage of $B$ under $e^{\lambda S_{k-1}}$ is $S_{k-1}^{-1}( (x\mapsto e^{\lambda x})^{-1}(B))$, so this bounded factor is $\mathcal F_{k-1}$-measurable. By the addition formula in [F10], $e^{\lambda S_k}=e^{\lambda S_{k-1}}e^{\lambda D_k}$; conditional expectation preservation [F6], taking out the known factor [F7], and step 2.1 give $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S_k}\le e^{\lambda^2c_k^2/8}\mathbb E e^{\lambda S_{k-1}}$. Finite induction gives $\mathbb E e^{\lambda S_n}\le e^{\lambda^2V/8}$. [F6, F7, F10, F11, F12, F13, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra, construct]

4.1 If $V=0$, every $c_i=0$, so each $X_i=a_i=b_i$ almost surely and $S=0$ almost surely. If $V>0$ and $\lambda>0$, then $e^{\lambda S}$ is nonnegative and $S\ge t$ implies $e^{\lambda S}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$ by [F10]. Markov [F8] and step 3.1 give $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le e^{\lambda^2V/8}/e^{\lambda t}=\exp(-\lambda t+\lambda^2V/8)$, using the reciprocal and addition formulas in [F10]. Choosing $\lambda=4t/V$ gives $\mathbb P(S\ge t)\le e^{-2t^2/V}$. [given, F8, F10, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 If $V>0$, substitute $-\lambda$ in the all-real MGF bound from step 3.1. Since $e^{-\lambda S}$ is nonnegative and $S\le-t$ implies $e^{-\lambda S}\ge e^{\lambda t}>0$ by [F10], Markov [F8] gives $\mathbb P(S\le-t)\le e^{\lambda^2V/8}/e^{\lambda t}$; choosing $\lambda=4t/V$ yields $e^{-2t^2/V}$. The finite union bound [F9] combines the two tails; step 4.1 already handles $V=0$. This lower-tail argument uses the same original variables and does not assert independence of their negatives. [F8, F9, F10, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
