## Example

Let $X=Y=\mathbb R$, $D=\{-1,1\}$, and let $\mu_0$ be the atomic Borel probability on $\mathbb R$ given by $\mu_0(A)=\tfrac12\mathbf 1_{\{1\in A\}}+\tfrac12\mathbf 1_{\{-1\in A\}}$ for Borel $A$. For each fixed datum $y\in\mathbb R$, use the finite potential $\Phi(u;y)=\tfrac12(y-u)^2$. Its evidence and posterior mass at $1$ are
$$Z(y)=\tfrac12\left(e^{-(y-1)^2/2}+e^{-(y+1)^2/2}\right)>0,\qquad \Pi^y(\{1\})=\frac{e^{2y}}{1+e^{2y}}.$$
For every $R>0$, this potential satisfies the weighted envelope and data-Lipschitz assumptions of the Hellinger data-stability theorem; hence some $C_R<\infty$ satisfies $h(\Pi^y,\Pi^{y'})\le C_R|y-y'|$ for all $y,y'\in[-R,R]$. These are pointwise fixed-data updates; no observation law or conditional-law version is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two-point prior, finite potential, pointwise evidence/posterior convention, and a data interval $[-R,R]$ with $R>0$.

[F1] The fixed-data posterior density relative to $\mu_0$ is $e^{-\Phi(u;y)}/Z(y)$ when $0<Z(y)<\infty$. [[def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law]]

[F2] The data-stability theorem gives a root-L2 Hellinger Lipschitz bound for every pair of data in a bounded ball, and the defining Hellinger distance for the supplied posterior densities is their root-L2 distance. [[thm-ip-hellinger-data-stability]], [[def-hellinger-distance-for-dominated-laws]]

[F3] The specified two-atom law is a Borel probability with full mass on the finite Borel set $D$; $X=Y=\mathbb R$ are taken with their standard separable Banach structure and norm metric. [[def-probability-measure]], [[def-ip-prior-on-a-forward-domain]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-banach-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-norm-and-normed-space]]

[F4] For each fixed $u\in D$, $y\mapsto\tfrac12(y-u)^2$ is a continuous real polynomial; the open slices $\{u\}\times\mathbb R$ show that $\Phi$ is jointly continuous. Every open $O\subseteq D\times\mathbb R$ is $\bigcup_{u\in D}\{u\}\times O_u$ with each $O_u$ open in $\mathbb R$, and each such rectangle belongs to the product sigma-algebra. Thus every open preimage under $\Phi$ is product-measurable; since open sets generate the real Borel sets and the extended-real Borel sigma-algebra restricts to the real one, $\Phi$ is measurable as an extended-real-valued map. [[def-continuous-map-top]], [[def-subspace-topology-top]], [[def-product-topology]], [[thm-algebra-of-continuous-functions]], [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-extended-reals]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra-on-the-extended-real-line]], [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

## Verification

**Given:** $X=Y=\mathbb R$, $D=\{-1,1\}$, the equal-atom prior, and $\Phi(u;y)=\tfrac12(y-u)^2$.

1.1 For any $a\in\mathbb R$, the intervals $(x-|x-a|/2,x+|x-a|/2)$, indexed by $x\ne a$, cover $\mathbb R\setminus\{a\}$ and omit $a$; hence $\mathbb R\setminus\{a\}$ is open and every singleton is closed. Therefore $D$ is Borel as a finite union of closed sets, and it is bounded because $|u|=1$ on $D$. The open intervals $(1/2,3/2)$ and $(-3/2,-1/2)$ intersect $D$ in $\{1\}$ and $\{-1\}$, respectively, so every singleton is relatively open and $D$ has the discrete relative topology. The displayed finite atomic measure is countably additive and its two atom masses sum to one, so it is a Borel probability with $\mu_0(D)=1$. Thus $D$ itself is a bounded Borel subset of positive prior mass, as required by the theorem. The real line is a separable Banach space. By [F4], for any open $O\subseteq\mathbb R$, $\Phi^{-1}(O)$ intersects each open slice $\{u\}\times\mathbb R$ in an open subset of that slice, hence open in $D\times\mathbb R$; taking the union over $u\in D$ proves joint continuity. The potential is nonnegative. For $|y|,|y'|\le R$ and $u\in D$, $|\Phi(u;y)-\Phi(u;y')|=\tfrac12|y-y'||y+y'-2u|\le(R+|u|)|y-y'|.$ Here $|y+y'-2u|\le |y|+|y'|+2|u|\le2R+2|u|$ by the triangle inequality. Thus choose $M_1(r,s)=0$ and $M_2(r,s)=r+s$, which are Borel and nondecreasing in each argument. Then $\int_D e^{M_1(R,|u|)}(1+M_2(R,|u|)^2)\,d\mu_0(u)=1+(R+1)^2<\infty,$ so [F2] applies and gives the stated Hellinger Lipschitz stability on $[-R,R]$. [F2, F3, F4, given, algebra, construct]

2.1 By [F1], the evidence is the displayed two-term sum and is positive. The posterior mass ratio is $\frac{\Pi^y(\{1\})}{\Pi^y(\{-1\})}=\frac{e^{-(y-1)^2/2}}{e^{-(y+1)^2/2}}=e^{2y};$ the two masses sum to one, so $\Pi^y(\{1\})=e^{2y}/(1+e^{2y})$. The verified evidence and the explicit formula determine the whole two-point posterior. [F1, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
