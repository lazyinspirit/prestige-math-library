## Statement

Let $A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_n$ be a Noetherian graded commutative ring whose degree-zero part $A_0$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_0$, and assume that $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\oplus\bigoplus_{n\neq0}A_n$ is an ideal of $A$. It is then a homogeneous maximal ideal, since $A/\mathfrak m=A_0/\mathfrak m_0$. For a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$, the following are equivalent: (a) $M=\mathfrak mM$; (b) $M_{\mathfrak m}=0$; (c) $M=0$. More generally, if $N\subseteq M$ are finitely generated graded $A$-modules, then $M=N+\mathfrak mM$ iff $M_{\mathfrak m}=N_{\mathfrak m}$ iff $M=N$. For the following regularity assertion assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), as required by its regular-local suppliers. If moreover $\mathfrak a\neq A$ is a graded ideal and $\mathfrak b$ is the ideal generated by $\mathfrak a_0+\sum_{n<0}A_n$, then regularity of $A_{\mathfrak m}$ and $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak m}$ implies regularity of $(A/\mathfrak b)_{\mathfrak m}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian graded commutative ring $A=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}A_n$ with $A_0$ local of maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_0$, the assumed homogeneous maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_0\oplus\bigoplus_{n\neq0}A_n$, a finitely generated graded $A$-module $M$ and a graded ideal $\mathfrak a\neq A$.

[F1] $A$ Noetherian means every ideal is finitely generated; $A_0$ local means it has the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m_0$ ([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-local-ring]]).

[F2] The Krull dimension of a local ring is the supremum of the lengths of its chains of prime ideals, and a Noetherian local ring $R$ is regular when its maximal ideal is generated by $\dim R$ elements, equivalently when $\dim_{R/\mathfrak n}\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2=\dim R$ ([[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]], [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]).

[F3] Assuming AC, a cotangent basis in a regular local ring lifts to regular parameters ([[lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis]]); repeated application of [[lem-regular-local-quotient-by-parameter-is-regular]] makes a quotient by part of those parameters regular of complementary dimension. Its associated graded ring is the polynomial ring on the cotangent basis ([[thm-associated-graded-ring-of-a-regular-local-ring]]). The intersection of the powers of a Noetherian local ring's maximal ideal is zero ([[thm-krull-intersection-theorem]]): by that theorem's choice-free first clause each element in the intersection is killed by some $1-a$, with $a$ in the maximal ideal, and such $1-a$ is a unit.

## Proof

1.1 Assume (a), $M=\mathfrak mM$. Localization is exact and commutes with the action, so $M_{\mathfrak m}=\mathfrak mM_{\mathfrak m}$; the ring $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$ and $M_{\mathfrak m}$ is finitely generated over it. If $M_{\mathfrak m}\neq0$, choose a minimal generating tuple $x_1,\dots,x_k$; then $x_k=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_ix_i$ with all $a_i\in\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$, whence $(1-a_k)x_k=\sum_{i<k}a_ix_i$, and $1-a_k$ is a unit, contradicting minimality. So $M_{\mathfrak m}=0$ and (a) implies (b). Conversely assume (b) and let $x\in M$ be homogeneous. Since $x/1=0$ in $M_{\mathfrak m}$, there is $a\in A\smallsetminus\mathfrak m$ with $ax=0$. Write $a=\sum_na_n$; comparing homogeneous components of $ax$ gives $a_nx=0$ for every $n$. As $a\notin\mathfrak m$ we have $a_0\notin\mathfrak m_0$, so $a_0$ is a unit of the local ring $A_0$, and $a_0x=0$ forces $x=0$. A finitely generated graded module is generated by homogeneous elements, so $M=0$, which is (c); (c) trivially implies (a). [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Let $N\subseteq M$ be finitely generated graded modules. The quotient $M/N$ is a graded $A$-module, finitely generated because $A$ is Noetherian, and localization is exact, so $(M/N)_{\mathfrak m}=M_{\mathfrak m}/N_{\mathfrak m}$. Applying step 1.1 to $M/N$ translates the three conditions: $M/N=\mathfrak m(M/N)$ is $M=N+\mathfrak mM$, while $M_{\mathfrak m}/N_{\mathfrak m}=0$ is $M_{\mathfrak m}=N_{\mathfrak m}$ and $M/N=0$ is $M=N$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Assume AC for this and the next step, and suppose $A_{\mathfrak m}$ regular of dimension $d$ and $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak m}$ regular of dimension $d-r$, where $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak m$ because $\mathfrak a$ is graded and proper. Put $\mathfrak n=(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak m)/\mathfrak a$, the maximal ideal of $A/\mathfrak a$, so that $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak m}$ is the localization of $A/\mathfrak a$ at $\mathfrak n$. The exact sequence of $\kappa$-vector spaces $0\to(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak m^2)/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2\to0$, together with $\dim_\kappa\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2=d$ and $\dim_\kappa\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2=d-r$ from regularity [F2], gives $\dim_\kappa(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak m^2)/\mathfrak m^2=r$. These spaces are spanned by images of homogeneous elements, so choose homogeneous $x_1,\dots,x_r\in\mathfrak a$ whose images form a $\kappa$-basis of $(\mathfrak a+\mathfrak m^2)/\mathfrak m^2$, and extend by homogeneous $x_{r+1},\dots,x_d\in\mathfrak m$ whose images complete a $\kappa$-basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$. Since $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is regular of dimension $d$, the images of $x_1,\dots,x_d$ generate $\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$ minimally and form a regular system of parameters there; hence $\mathfrak a_0:=(x_1,\dots,x_r)\subseteq\mathfrak a$ has $A_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak a_0A_{\mathfrak m}$ regular of dimension $d-r$ by [F3], and it surjects onto the regular local ring $(A/\mathfrak a)_{\mathfrak m}$ of the same dimension $d-r$. A surjective local homomorphism of regular local rings of equal dimension is an isomorphism: it induces a surjection of cotangent spaces of equal dimension, hence an isomorphism of associated graded rings, and its kernel lies in $\bigcap_i(\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m})^i=0$ by Krull's intersection theorem [F3]. Therefore $\mathfrak aA_{\mathfrak m}=\mathfrak a_0A_{\mathfrak m}$, and step 2.1 applied to the finitely generated graded modules $\mathfrak a_0\subseteq\mathfrak a$ gives $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak a_0$. The same local argument with $\mathfrak m$ in place of $\mathfrak a$ shows $\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}=(x_1,\dots,x_d)A_{\mathfrak m}$, so step 2.1 gives $\mathfrak m=(x_1,\dots,x_d)$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $\mathfrak b$ be the ideal generated by $L=\mathfrak a_0+\sum_{n<0}A_n$ (with $\mathfrak a_0=\mathfrak a\cap A_0$) and let $\mathfrak b_0$ be the ideal generated by those $x_i$ lying in $L$, that is, by the $x_i$ with $\deg x_i<0$, together with $x_1,\dots,x_r$ of degree $\le0$. Then $\mathfrak b_0$ is generated by a subset of the regular system of parameters $x_1,\dots,x_d$ of $A_{\mathfrak m}$, so $(A/\mathfrak b_0)_{\mathfrak m}$ is regular by [F3]. Every element of $L$ lies in $\mathfrak b_0+\mathfrak m\mathfrak b$: if $b\in A_n$ with $n<0$, then $b\in\mathfrak m=(x_1,\dots,x_d)$ by step 3.1, so $b=\sum_ia_ix_i$ with $a_i$ homogeneous, and for each $i$ either $\deg x_i<0$, whence $x_i\in\mathfrak b_0$ and $a_ix_i\in\mathfrak b_0$, or $\deg x_i\ge0$, whence $\deg a_i<0$, so $a_i\in\sum_{n<0}A_n\subseteq\mathfrak b$ and $a_ix_i\in\mathfrak m\mathfrak b$; if $b\in\mathfrak a\cap A_0$, then $b\in\mathfrak a=(x_1,\dots,x_r)$ and $b=\sum_{i\le r}x_ib_i$ with $b_i$ homogeneous and for each $i$ either $\deg x_i\le0$, whence $x_i\in\mathfrak b_0$, or $\deg x_i>0$, whence $\deg b_i<0$ and $b_i\in\mathfrak b$, so $x_ib_i\in\mathfrak b_0\cup\mathfrak m\mathfrak b$. Since $L$ generates $\mathfrak b$, this gives $\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak b_0+\mathfrak m\mathfrak b$, that is, $\mathfrak b/\mathfrak b_0=\mathfrak m(\mathfrak b/\mathfrak b_0)$; step 2.1 applied to $\mathfrak b_0\subseteq\mathfrak b$ yields $\mathfrak b=\mathfrak b_0$. Hence $(A/\mathfrak b)_{\mathfrak m}=(A/\mathfrak b_0)_{\mathfrak m}$ is regular. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
