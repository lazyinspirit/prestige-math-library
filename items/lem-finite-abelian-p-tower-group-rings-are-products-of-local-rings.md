## Statement

Assume AC. Let p be prime, let P and P' be finite abelian p-groups, let G be a finite abelian group, and let `P'→P` be a surjection. Put `A=Z_p[P×G]` and `A'=Z_p[P'×G]`. Both are finite free Z_p-modules and finite products of commutative local rings. The kernel of the induced surjection `A'→A` is contained in the Jacobson radical of A'. In particular these conclusions apply to the finite quotient group rings of the specified infinite-rank Iwasawa tower whenever the group quotient kernels are finite p-groups.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, p, finite abelian p-groups P and P', finite abelian G, and the specified surjection.

[F1] The completed Iwasawa algebra is the inverse limit of its finite quotient group rings. [[def-infinite-rank-p-adic-iwasawa-algebra]]

[F2] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** idempotent lifting and nilpotent augmentation.

1.1 The group elements form a finite Z_p-basis of each ring, so both are commutative finite free Z_p-algebras and complete for the p-adic topology. In any such algebra R, each element of pR is in the Jacobson radical: `1−px` has inverse `∑_(j≥0)(px)^j`. The finite-dimensional F_p-algebra R/pR is Artinian and is a finite product of local Artinian rings, with orthogonal idempotents summing to one. Each idempotent lifts uniquely through the p-adic completion: if e is idempotent modulo p^r, then `2e−1` is a unit modulo p, so the Newton correction for `e²−e` lifts e modulo p^(2r). Uniqueness makes the lifted idempotents orthogonal and their sum one. The corresponding factors of R are local, since their reductions are local and pR lies in every maximal ideal. Thus R is a finite product of local rings. [F1, F2, given, construct, induction, algebra]

2.1 The transition kernel is generated by `h−1` for h in the finite p-group `ker(P'→P)`. If h has order p^r, then in characteristic p one has `(h−1)^(p^r)=h^(p^r)−1=0`. The finitely generated ideal they span in A'/pA' is therefore nilpotent because the group ring is commutative. Every maximal ideal of A' contains pA', so it also contains the preimage of that nilpotent ideal and hence the transition kernel. The kernel is therefore contained in the Jacobson radical. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎

## Source and authoring record

This verifies the elementary finite-ring hypotheses of a square-presentation bridge. It does not prove Weil–étale duality, perfect complexes, derived descent or the Iwasawa structure theorem.
