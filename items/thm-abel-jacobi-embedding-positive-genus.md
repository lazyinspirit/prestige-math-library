## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) inherited from the Jacobian
construction. Let $X$ be a compact connected Riemann surface of genus $g\ge1$
and let $u=u_{p_0}:X\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ be the Abel-Jacobi map with base
point $p_0$ ([[def-abel-jacobi-map]]). Then:

1. **Injectivity.** $u$ is injective. Consequently, for $g=1$, $u$ is a
biholomorphism $X\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ onto the one-dimensional torus
$\operatorname{Jac}(X)$.
2. **Immersivity.** $u$ is a holomorphic immersion: in a holomorphic chart $z$
at $p$ and a holomorphic lift $\xi$ of $u$, the derivative of $\xi$ at $p$ is
nonzero, because some holomorphic differential does not vanish at $p$
([[lem-holomorphic-differentials-separate-generic-points]]).
3. **Embedding.** $u$ is a closed topological embedding: it is a homeomorphism
of the compact space $X$ onto its image, and every point of the image has a
chart of the complex torus $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ in which the image of a
neighbourhood of the corresponding point of $X$ is the graph of a holomorphic
map; in this sense $u(X)$ is a compact one-dimensional complex submanifold of
the complex torus $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$.
4. **Generation.** The subgroup of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ generated by the
image of $X$ (equivalently, by the differences
$u(p)-u(q)$, $p,q\in X$) is all of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$, by Jacobi inversion.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a compact connected Riemann surface $X$ of genus $g\ge1$, its Jacobian $\operatorname{Jac}(X)=\Omega(X)^*/\Lambda$, a base point $p_0$, and the Abel-Jacobi map $u$.

[F1] $u$ is holomorphic in the atlas of the Jacobian definition, and in a chart at $p$ the derivative of a holomorphic lift is the evaluation map $\omega\mapsto\omega(\partial_z)$; it is nonzero exactly when some holomorphic differential does not vanish at $p$ ([[def-abel-jacobi-map]], [[lem-abel-jacobi-map-is-well-defined-and-base-point-independent]], [[def-path-integral-of-a-holomorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]]).

[F2] For every $p\in X$, evaluation $\operatorname{ev}_p:\Omega(X)\to(K_X)_p$ is nonzero, equivalently $\ell(K-p)=g-1$; for $g\ge1$ this holds at every point ([[lem-holomorphic-differentials-separate-generic-points]], [[def-divisor-principal-and-canonical-divisor-riemann-surface]]).

[F3] On $\operatorname{Div}^0(X)$ the map $u$ is a base-point-free homomorphism and $\ker u=\operatorname{Prin}(X)$, the principal divisors ([[def-abel-jacobi-map]], [[lem-abel-jacobi-map-is-well-defined-and-base-point-independent]], [[thm-abels-theorem-for-divisors]]).

[F4] A principal divisor $(q)-(p)$ with $p\ne q$ is the divisor of a nonconstant meromorphic function $f:X\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ of degree $1$ (its only zero and only pole are simple); a degree-one holomorphic map between compact connected Riemann surfaces is a biholomorphism ([[def-divisor-principal-and-canonical-divisor-riemann-surface]], [[thm-proper-holomorphic-map-riemann-surfaces-has-degree]], [[lem-degree-one-holomorphic-map-of-compact-riemann-surfaces-is-an-isomorphism]], [[def-biholomorphic-map]]).

[F5] A continuous bijection from a compact space onto a Hausdorff space is a homeomorphism, and continuous images of compact sets are compact ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]]). Compact subsets of a Hausdorff space are closed ([[thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]]). Genus is preserved by homeomorphisms ([[def-genus-and-euler-characteristic-compact-riemann-surface]]).

[F6] A holomorphic map of several variables with invertible complex Jacobian at a point is a local biholomorphism; a nonzero complex-linear map $\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^g$ extends to an invertible linear map of $\mathbb C^g$ ([[thm-holomorphic-inverse-function-theorem-several-variables]], [[def-holomorphic-function-in-several-complex-variables]], [[def-holomorphic-map-and-complex-jacobian]], [[def-linear-map]]).

[F7] The degree-zero divisor extension $u:\operatorname{Div}^0(X)\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ is surjective ([[thm-jacobi-inversion]]).

[F8] Full AC is inherited from the Jacobian construction; no new selection is made ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[cor-complex-analytic-functions-have-local-primitives]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Suppose $p\ne q$ and $u(p)=u(q)$. By [F3], $u((q)-(p))=u(q)-u(p)=0$, so Abel's theorem makes $(q)-(p)$ a principal divisor. By [F4] there is a meromorphic $f$ with $(f)=(q)-(p)$, whose associated map $X\to\widehat{\mathbb C}$ has degree $1$ and is therefore a biholomorphism; hence $X\cong\widehat{\mathbb C}$ has genus $0$, contradicting $g\ge1$ by [F5]. Thus $u$ is injective. [F3, F4, F5]

1.2 By [F1] the derivative of a holomorphic lift $\xi$ at any $p$ is the evaluation map $\omega\mapsto\omega(\partial_z)$ on the chart tangent direction; by [F2] some holomorphic differential is nonzero at $p$, so this linear map $\mathbb C\to\mathbb C^g$ is nonzero, hence injective. Thus $u$ is an immersion at every point. [F1, F2]

2.1 For the generation statement, every difference $u(p)-u(q)$ lies in the subgroup generated by $u(X)-u(p_0)=\{u(x)-u(p_0):x\in X\}$, and conversely every element of that subgroup is a finite combination of such differences. Every degree-zero divisor is a finite $\mathbb Z$-combination of point differences, so its image under $u$ lies in the subgroup generated by $u(X)-u(p_0)$; by [F7] The degree-zero divisor extension $u:\operatorname{Div}^0(X)\to\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ is surjective, so that subgroup is all of $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$. [F7, step 1.1]

2.2 Fix $p$ and a holomorphic lift $\xi$ on a coordinate disk centered at $p$, translating the target so $\xi(p)=0$. By step 1.2 its derivative is nonzero, so an invertible complex-linear change of target coordinates makes the first component $\xi_1$ have nonzero derivative. The inverse function theorem [F6] in complex dimension one gives a smaller disk on which $s=\xi_1(z)$ is a holomorphic coordinate with holomorphic inverse $z=h(s)$. In these coordinates the lifted image is exactly $(s,\xi_2(h(s)),\ldots,\xi_g(h(s)))$, a holomorphic graph; for $g=1$ there are no remaining components. The quotient atlas transfers this graph description to a neighborhood in the Jacobian. [F1, F6, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 Since $X$ is compact and the Jacobian is Hausdorff, the continuous injection $u$ is a homeomorphism onto its compact image by [F5]. Fix a graph disk $U$ about $p$ as in step 2.2 and a smaller neighborhood $U'$ whose closure lies in $U$. The compact set $X\setminus U'$ has compact, hence closed image disjoint from $u(p)$. Shrink the target graph chart about $u(p)$ to miss this image. In that chart the entire image $u(X)$ is the graph portion from $U$, so no other branches occur. This proves the complex-submanifold chart assertion as well as the closed topological embedding. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.2]

4.1 If $g=1$, then $u$ is injective by step 1.1 and an immersion by step 1.2, so in the local model of step 2.2 it is locally biholomorphic onto its image; the image is open (by local biholomorphy) and compact (by step 3.1), hence closed, and the connected torus $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$ is therefore entirely covered by the nonempty image: $u$ is a biholomorphism onto $\operatorname{Jac}(X)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 1.1, 1.2, 2.2 and 3.1 establish the four statements, and step 4.1 the genus-one clause, all under the inherited AC of [F8]. [F8, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎


## Source notes

The injectivity argument is McMullen's proof of Theorem 15.7 (*Riemann Surfaces*, printed p. 130): a vanishing $(q)-(p)$ would produce a degree-one map to the sphere, forcing genus zero; the same argument is Forster's §21.8 and Looijenga's Corollary 7.7 (printed p. 61) in the genus-one case. Immersivity is the basepoint-freeness of $|K|$ used by McMullen ($D\varphi_P\ne0$). The item states the embedding through an explicit local graph chart, because the library has no general definition of a complex submanifold of a complex torus; the continuous-inverse theorem supplies the homeomorphism onto the image.
