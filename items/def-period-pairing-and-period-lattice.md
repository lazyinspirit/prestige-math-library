## Definition

Assume full AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), used for the symplectic basis and
$\dim_{\mathbb C}\Omega(X)=g$ interfaces below. Let $X$ be a compact connected
Riemann surface of genus $g$, with a symplectic basis
$a_1,b_1,\ldots,a_g,b_g$ of $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ supplied by the paired side
loops of a one-polygon normal form
([[thm-symplectic-homology-basis-compact-riemann-surface]]). Write
$A_1,B_1,\ldots,A_g,B_g$ for those fixed continuous closed side-loop
representatives on $X$. The path integral of a holomorphic differential along
each such path is defined by
[[def-path-integral-of-a-holomorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]].

For $\omega\in\Omega(X)$, the **period pairing** on this fixed basis and its
chosen representatives is
$$P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^g(m_i a_i+n_i b_i),\omega\right):=\sum_{i=1}^g\left(m_i\int_{A_i}\omega+n_i\int_{B_i}\omega\right),\qquad m_i,n_i\in\mathbb Z.$$
The symplectic basis makes the coordinates $m_i,n_i$ unique. Thus $P$ is
additive in its homology-class argument and $\mathbb C$-linear in $\omega$.
This definition uses the fixed polygon representatives; representative- and
basis-independence are separate claims.

Define $e:H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\to\Omega(X)^*$ by
$e(\gamma)(\omega):=P(\gamma,\omega)$, and let
$$\Lambda:=e(H_1(X;\mathbb Z))\subseteq\Omega(X)^*.$$
This is the **period subgroup**, generated by the $2g$ period vectors
$e(a_1),e(b_1),\ldots,e(a_g),e(b_g)$. The later bilinear-relations result
establishes when this subgroup is a full lattice; discreteness and fullness are
not asserted here. Since $\dim_{\mathbb C}\Omega(X)=g$, after choosing a
$\mathbb C$-basis $\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_g$ of $\Omega(X)$ the **period
matrix** is the $g\times2g$ matrix whose columns are the coordinates of those
period vectors. For $g=0$, both bases are empty, $P=0$, $\Lambda=\{0\}$, and
the period matrix is the empty $0\times0$ matrix.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a compact connected Riemann surface $X$ of genus $g$, a
chosen symplectic basis with its polygon side-loop representatives, and
$\omega\in\Omega(X)$.

[F1] Under full AC, the one-polygon side-loop classes form a symplectic basis
of $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$; every class has unique integer coordinates in that
basis, and the genus-zero group is zero
([[thm-symplectic-homology-basis-compact-riemann-surface]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F2] Under full AC, $\Omega(X)$ is a finite-dimensional complex vector space
of dimension $g$ ([[lem-holomorphic-differentials-form-a-g-dimensional-space]],
[[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F3] The path integral of a holomorphic differential along a continuous path
exists independently of its chart subdivision and local primitives; it is
additive under concatenation and $\mathbb C$-linear in the differential
([[def-path-integral-of-a-holomorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]]).

[F4] $\Omega(X)$ denotes the complex vector space of holomorphic
differentials on $X$ ([[def-meromorphic-differential-on-a-riemann-surface]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The objects and hypotheses in the Definition.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Each side loop $A_i$ or $B_i$ is a continuous path, so [F3] defines its period integral for every $\omega\in\Omega(X)$. When $g=0$ there are no side loops and the finite sum is empty. [F1, F3, F4, given]

2.1 Every $\gamma\in H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$ has unique integer coordinates in the chosen basis by [F1]. Substituting those coordinates and the already-defined side-loop integrals from step 1.1 therefore gives one value of $P(\gamma,\omega)$ for the fixed basis data. [F1, step 1.1, given]

3.1 The finite coordinate formula and [F3] show that $P$ is additive in $\gamma$ and complex-linear in $\omega$. Hence for each $\gamma$, $e(\gamma)$ is a complex-linear functional on $\Omega(X)$, and $e$ is a homomorphism of abelian groups. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Because the basis generates $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)$, the image $\Lambda$ is the subgroup generated by the $2g$ values $e(a_i),e(b_i)$; for $g=0$ it is the zero subgroup. By [F2], the algebraic dual has complex dimension $g$, so choosing a basis of $\Omega(X)$ gives exactly $g$ coordinates for each of the $2g$ period vectors and hence the stated $g\times2g$ period matrix. This verifies the definition without asserting that $\Lambda$ is discrete or full. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
## Remarks

The side loops are topological representatives from the polygonal normal form;
the local-primitive path integral defines their periods even if those fixed
representatives are only continuous. The later well-definedness result proves
that the resulting pairing agrees with integration over arbitrary smooth
cycles and is independent of representative and symplectic basis. The later
bilinear relations prove that the period subgroup is a full lattice.
