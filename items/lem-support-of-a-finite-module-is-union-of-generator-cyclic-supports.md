## Statement

If a left $R$-module $M$ is generated by $m_1,\dots,m_r$, then
$$
\operatorname{Supp}_R(M)=\bigcup_{i=1}^r \operatorname{Supp}_R(R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i)).
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$, and generators $m_1,\dots,m_r$ of $M$.

[L1] A prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ lies in $\operatorname{Supp}_R(M)$ exactly when $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)\subseteq\mathfrak p$ for some $m \in M$ ([[lem-support-membership-by-annihilator]]).

[L2] For an ideal $I$, the support of $R/I$ is the set of primes containing $I$ ([[lem-support-of-a-cyclic-module-is-its-vanishing-set]]).

[L3] The generators $m_1,\dots,m_r$ generate every element of $M$ by finite $R$-linear combinations ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $r=0$, then $M=0$ because the empty list generates only the zero submodule. Both sides are therefore empty, so the formula holds. Hence assume $r\ge1$. [L3, algebra]

1.2 Suppose $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(M)$. By [L1], choose $m \in M$ with $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m)\subseteq\mathfrak p$, and write $m=\sum_i r_i m_i$ using [L3]. If no $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i)$ were contained in $\mathfrak p$, choose $t_i \in \operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i)\setminus\mathfrak p$ for every $i$; then $t=t_1\cdots t_r \notin \mathfrak p$ because $\mathfrak p$ is prime, but $tm=0$, so $t \in \operatorname{Ann}_R(m)\subseteq\mathfrak p$, a contradiction. Thus $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i)\subseteq\mathfrak p$ for some $i$, and [L2] gives $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i))$. [L1, L2, L3, choose]

1.3 Conversely, if $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(R/\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i))$ for some $i$, then [L2] gives $\operatorname{Ann}_R(m_i)\subseteq\mathfrak p$, and [L1] applied to the element $m_i \in M$ gives $\mathfrak p \in \operatorname{Supp}_R(M)$. [L1, L2]

2.1 Steps 1.1, 1.2, and 1.3 prove the union formula. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
