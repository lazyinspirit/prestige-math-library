## Statement

Let $K$ be a field and $n\ge1$. Then

$$t^{n}-1\text{ is separable over }K\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \operatorname{char}K\nmid n$$

([[def-repeated-root-and-separable-polynomial]], [[def-ring-characteristic]];
divisibility of integers being that of [[def-divides-in-z]], under which $0\mid n$
holds only for $n=0$, so characteristic $0$ never divides $n\ge1$).

When $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$ and $E$ is a splitting field of $t^{n}-1$ over
$K$, the group $\mu_n(E)$ is cyclic of order exactly $n$, it contains exactly
$\varphi(n)$ primitive $n$-th roots of unity, and

$$E=K(\zeta)$$

for every primitive $n$-th root of unity $\zeta\in E$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $K$, an integer $n\ge1$, the polynomial $f:=t^{n}-1\in K[t]$, and the element $n\cdot1_K$ obtained by adding $1_K$ to itself $n$ times.

[L1] $f'=\sum_{i\ge1}ia_ix^{i-1}$ for $f=\sum_i a_ix^{i}$ ([[def-formal-derivative-of-a-polynomial]]); $(x^{k})'=kx^{k-1}$ for $k\ge1$ and constants have derivative $0$, and the derivative is additive ([[prop-formal-derivative-laws]]). Hence $f'=(n\cdot1_K)\,t^{n-1}$.

[L2] For a field $F$ and $0\ne f\in F[x]$: $f$ is separable over $F$ if and only if $\gcd(f,f')=1$ in $F[x]$ ([[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]).

[L3] For $f,g\in F[x]$ not both zero, $d=\gcd(f,g)$ is monic, divides both $f$ and $g$, and every common divisor of $f$ and $g$ divides $d$ ([[thm-bezout-identity-for-polynomials]]).

[L4] For a ring $R$ and $n\in\mathbb Z$, one has $n\cdot1_R=0_R$ if and only if $\operatorname{char}(R)$ divides $n$ ([[lem-characteristic-and-additive-order]], [[def-ring-characteristic]]); the characteristic of a field is $0$ or a prime ([[thm-characteristic-of-a-field-is-zero-or-prime]]).

[L5] Every nonzero $f\in K[x]$ has a splitting field over $K$ ([[thm-splitting-fields-exist-for-nonzero-polynomials]]); $f$ of degree $m$ splits over $E$ when $f=c\prod_{j=1}^{m}(t-\alpha_j)$ with $c\in K^{\times}$ and $\alpha_j\in E$, repetitions allowed, and a splitting field is generated over $K$ by the roots ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]), the notation $K(S)$ for the subfield generated by $K$ and a finite set $S$ being that of [[def-finitely-generated-field-extension]].

[L6] $\mu_n(F)$ is a finite cyclic subgroup of $F^{\times}$ of order dividing $n$; it contains a primitive $n$-th root of unity exactly when its order is $n$, and there are then $\varphi(n)$ of them, namely the generators ([[prop-the-roots-of-unity-in-a-field-form-a-finite-cyclic-group]], [[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]], [[def-unit-group-modulo-n-and-euler-totient]]).

[L7] $f$ is separable over $K$ when it has no repeated root in any extension field of $K$, where $a$ is a repeated root of $f$ in $E$ when $(t-a)^{2}$ divides the image of $f$ in $E[t]$ ([[def-repeated-root-and-separable-polynomial]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** cases.

1.1 In the case $n\cdot1_K\ne0$, put $d:=\gcd(f,f')$. By [L1] $f'=(n\cdot1_K)t^{n-1}$ with $n\cdot1_K$ a unit of $K$, so $d\mid t^{n-1}$ by [L3], hence $d\mid t\cdot t^{n-1}=t^{n}$; and $d\mid f=t^{n}-1$, so $d$ divides $t^{n}-(t^{n}-1)=1$ and, being monic, $d=1$. By [L2] the polynomial $f$ is separable over $K$. [assume-case pos, L1, L2, L3]

1.2 In the case $n\cdot1_K=0$, [L1] gives $f'=0$, so every polynomial dividing $f$ is a common divisor of $f$ and $f'$; by [L3] the monic $\gcd(f,0)$ is divisible by every such divisor and divides $f$, so it is $f$ itself, of degree $n\ge1$. Thus $\gcd(f,f')\ne1$ and $f$ is not separable over $K$ by [L2]. [assume-case zero, L1, L2, L3]

2.1 The two cases are exhaustive and, by [L4], $n\cdot1_K\ne0$ says exactly $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$; so $f$ is separable over $K$ if and only if $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L4, cases-exhaustive]

3.1 Assume now $\operatorname{char}K\nmid n$ and let $E$ be a splitting field of $f$ over $K$, which exists by [L5]. Over $E$ one has $f=c\prod_{j=1}^{n}(t-\alpha_j)$ with $\alpha_j\in E$, and comparing leading coefficients of the monic $f$ gives $c=1$. [step 2.1, L5]

4.1 The $\alpha_j$ are pairwise distinct: if $\alpha_i=\alpha_j$ for $i\ne j$ then $(t-\alpha_i)^{2}$ divides $f$ in $E[t]$, making $\alpha_i$ a repeated root of $f$ in the extension $E$ of $K$, which contradicts the separability supplied by step 2.1 through [L7]. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L7]

5.1 Hence $f$ has exactly $n$ distinct roots in $E$, that is $\bigl|\mu_n(E)\bigr|=n$; by [L6] the group $\mu_n(E)$ is cyclic of order $n$ and contains exactly $\varphi(n)$ primitive $n$-th roots of unity. [step 3.1, step 4.1, L6]

6.1 Fix such a $\zeta$. Every root of $f$ in $E$ lies in $\mu_n(E)=\langle\zeta\rangle$, so is a power of $\zeta$; since $E$ is generated over $K$ by those roots by [L5], $E=K(\zeta)$. At $n=1$ the polynomial is $t-1$, $\mu_1(E)=\{1\}$, $\varphi(1)=1$, $\zeta=1$ and $E=K$. [step 5.1, L5, L6] ∎

## Remarks

- **The failing direction is inseparability, not a shortage of roots.** When $\operatorname{char}K=p$ divides $n$, the derivative vanishes identically and no extension can separate the roots: writing $n=p^{k}m$ with $p\nmid m$, one has $\mu_n=\mu_m$ in every field of characteristic $p$ ([[prop-p-power-roots-of-unity-in-characteristic-p]]). Passing to a larger field does not help, which is why the hypothesis is carried on every later statement rather than removed by enlarging $K$.
