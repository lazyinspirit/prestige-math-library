## Statement

If $R$ is a commutative Noetherian ring, $M$ is a finitely generated
$R$-module and $N\subseteq M$ is a submodule, then $N$ is finitely generated.
This finite-list proof uses no choice axiom.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a commutative Noetherian ring $R$, a finitely generated $R$-module $M$ and a submodule $N\le M$.

[L1] A ring is left Noetherian when its left regular module is Noetherian, and in the commutative case no side distinction occurs ([[def-noetherian-ring]]).

[L2] A module is Noetherian when every submodule of it is finitely generated, a submodule being a subgroup closed under scalars ([[def-noetherian-module]], [[def-submodule]]).

[L3] For a module $M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ generated by a set $S$ is the smallest submodule containing $S$, and $M$ is finitely generated when $M=\langle m_1,\ldots,m_n\rangle_R$ for finitely many elements; the free module $R^{(X)}$ on a set $X$ has standard basis $(e_x)_{x\in X}$ and every element has a unique expression $\sum_{x\in F}r_xe_x$, addition and scalar multiplication being coefficientwise ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]], [[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]).

[L4] For every set map $u:X\to M$ into a module $M$ there is a unique homomorphism of $R$-modules $R^{(X)}\to M$ sending $e_x\mapsto u(x)$, namely $\sum_xr_xe_x\mapsto\sum_xr_xu(x)$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-free-modules]]).

[L5] A function $f:M\to N$ is a module homomorphism when it is additive and $f(rm)=rf(m)$; its kernel and image are then submodules of $M$ and $N$ respectively ([[def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]], [[thm-module-kernel-image-and-injectivity]]).

[L6] The regular left module ${}_RR$ has action $r\cdot x=rx$. A subset is its submodule exactly when it is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by every $r\in R$, which is exactly the definition of a left ideal; in a commutative ring this is an ideal. The ideal $(S)$ is the smallest ideal containing $S$ ([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-left-and-right-modules]], [[def-submodule]], [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We show first that for every $n\in\mathbb N$ and the set $X_n:=\{1,\ldots,n\}$ every submodule $P\le R^{(X_n)}$ is finitely generated, by induction on $n$. For $n=0$ the module $R^{(\varnothing)}=0$ has $\{0\}=\langle\varnothing\rangle_R$ as its only submodule, so the claim holds. Let $n\ge1$ and suppose the claim known for $n-1$. The coordinate map $\pi:R^{(X_n)}\to R$, $\pi(\sum_{i\in F}r_ie_i):=r_n$, is well defined by uniqueness of the expressions in [L3] and is a module homomorphism, because the expressions of $f+g$ and $rf$ have coefficientwise entries. Hence $\pi(P)$ is a submodule of the regular module ${}_RR$, that is an ideal of $R$ by [L6], and since $R$ is Noetherian [L1] and [L2] provide a finite generating list $\pi(P)=\langle a_1,\ldots,a_r\rangle_R$; choose $p_1,\ldots,p_r\in P$ with $\pi(p_j)=a_j$ for each $j$, a selection from a finite list. Similarly $\ker\pi=\{f:f_n=0\}$ is the image of $R^{(X_{n-1})}$ under the coefficientwise inclusion, and $P\cap\ker\pi$ corresponds to a submodule $Q\le R^{(X_{n-1})}$, which is finitely generated by the induction hypothesis, say $Q=\langle q_1,\ldots,q_s\rangle_R$; let $q'_1,\ldots,q'_s$ be the corresponding elements of $P\cap\ker\pi$. Then $P=\langle p_1,\ldots,p_r,q'_1,\ldots,q'_s\rangle_R$: it contains the displayed elements, and for $p\in P$ the element $\pi(p)=\sum_j\lambda_ja_j$ with $\lambda_j\in R$ satisfies $\pi(p-\sum_j\lambda_jp_j)=0$, so $p-\sum_j\lambda_jp_j=\sum_k\mu_kq'_k$ for some $\mu_k\in R$ by [L3]. This completes the induction. [L1, L2, L3, L6, base, induction, discharge-induction: case n=0]

2.1 Let $m_1,\ldots,m_n\in M$ satisfy $M=\langle m_1,\ldots,m_n\rangle_R$, as exists by finite generation of $M$ [L3]. By [L4] the set map $e_i\mapsto m_i$ on the standard basis of $R^{(X_n)}$ extends to a homomorphism $\varphi:R^{(X_n)}\to M$. Its image is a submodule of $M$ by [L5] containing each $m_i$, hence containing $\langle m_1,\ldots,m_n\rangle_R=M$ by [L3], so $\varphi(\varphi^{-1}(N))=N$ and $\varphi$ is surjective. The preimage $\varphi^{-1}(N)$ is a submodule of $R^{(X_n)}$: it is nonempty because $0$ maps to $0\in N$, and it is closed under addition and under scalars because $\varphi$ is additive and $R$-linear and $N$ is a submodule. By step 1.1 it is finitely generated, say $\varphi^{-1}(N)=\langle u_1,\ldots,u_t\rangle_R$. Then $N=\langle\varphi(u_1),\ldots,\varphi(u_t)\rangle_R$: each $\varphi(u_i)$ lies in $N$, and for $y\in N$ surjectivity gives $u\in R^{(X_n)}$ with $\varphi(u)=y$, so $u\in\varphi^{-1}(N)$; writing $u=\sum_i\lambda_iu_i$ with $\lambda_i\in R$ by [L3] and applying $\varphi$ expresses $y=\sum_i\lambda_i\varphi(u_i)$. [L3, L4, L5, step 1.1]

3.1 Combining steps 1.1 and 2.1, every submodule $N$ of a finitely generated module $M$ over a commutative Noetherian ring $R$ is generated by the finite list $\varphi(u_1),\ldots,\varphi(u_t)$; equivalently, the module $M$ is Noetherian in the sense of [L2]. The only selections used were from finite lists: the finite generating list of $M$, the finite lists of generators of $\pi(P)$, the generators of the finitely many submodules met in the induction, and the finitely many lifts $p_j$; the induction runs on $\mathbb N$. Hence no choice principle is used. [L1, L2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
