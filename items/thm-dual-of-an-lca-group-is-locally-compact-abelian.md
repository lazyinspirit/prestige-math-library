## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). If $G$ is a locally
compact Hausdorff abelian group ([[def-locally-compact-space]]), then
$\widehat G$ is a locally compact Hausdorff abelian topological group, and the
sets $\{\gamma:\gamma[K]\subseteq V\}$ with $K\subseteq G$ compact and
$V\subseteq\mathbb T$ open with $1\in V$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of the identity
character ([[def-neighbourhood-top]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] $\widehat G$ is a Hausdorff topological abelian group, translations are homeomorphisms, and its topology is generated by the subbasis $S(K,V)=\{\gamma:\gamma[K]\subseteq V\}$ with $K\subseteq G$ compact and $V\subseteq\mathbb T$ open. ([[lem-compact-open-character-group-operations-are-continuous]], [[def-pontryagin-dual-and-compact-open-topology]], [[def-compact-open-topology-for-topological-domains]], [[lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]])

[F2] Assume the Axiom of Choice. For every symmetric compact neighbourhood $K$ of $0$ in $G$, the set $N_{K}:=\{\gamma\in\widehat G:\gamma[K]\subseteq D\}$ with $D=\{|z-1|\le1/2\}$ is equicontinuous, is compact in the compact-open topology of $C(G,\mathbb T)$, and is a neighbourhood of the identity character in $\widehat G$. ([[lem-dual-identity-neighbourhood-is-compact]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F3] In a locally compact Hausdorff space every point has a compact neighbourhood, and we may take it symmetric in a topological group: if $N$ is a compact neighbourhood of $0$ then so is $N\cap(-N)$. ([[def-locally-compact-space]], [[lem-a-locally-compact-hausdorff-space-has-a-base-of-open-sets-with-compact-closure]], [[lem-topological-group-translations-and-inversion]], [[thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]], [[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]])

[F4] A composite of continuous maps is continuous. ([[lem-continuity-is-local-and-pastes]])

## Proof

**Given:** A locally compact Hausdorff abelian group $G$, and $\widehat G$ with the compact-open topology.

1.1 $\widehat G$ is a Hausdorff abelian topological group by [F1]. [F1]

1.2 The sets $S(K,V)$ with $K$ compact and $V$ open containing $1$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of $1$ in $\widehat G$: any open neighbourhood of $1$ contains a finite intersection $\bigcap_{i\le n}S(K_{i},V_{i})$ of subbasic sets each containing $1$ by [F1], and that intersection contains the subbasic set $S(\bigcup_{i\le n}K_{i},\bigcap_{i\le n}V_{i})$, where the union of finitely many compact sets is compact, the finite intersection of open sets is open and contains $1$, and $1(K)=\{1\}$ lies in every $V$ with $1\in V$. [F1]

2.1 $\widehat G$ is locally compact: by [F3] choose a symmetric compact neighbourhood $K$ of $0$ in $G$ (a compact neighbourhood intersected with its inverse image under the continuous inversion is compact and symmetric). By [F2] the set $N_{K}=\{\gamma:\gamma[K]\subseteq D\}$ is a compact neighbourhood of $1$ in $\widehat G$; translations are homeomorphisms of $\widehat G$ by [F1], so every character has a compact neighbourhood, and $\widehat G$ is locally compact by [F1]. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3, F4]

3.1 By steps 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1 the dual $\widehat G$ is a locally compact Hausdorff abelian topological group and the compact-open sets $S(K,V)$ form a basis of neighbourhoods of the identity character, as asserted. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
