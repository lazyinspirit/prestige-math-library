## Statement

Assume AC. Let $E\to B$ be a numerable real vector bundle of rank $n\geq1$ over
an admissible base $B$; in particular $B$ may be any CW complex. Let
$P(E)\to B$ be its projective bundle and $x=x_E\in H^1(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ the
tautological degree-one class. Then $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ is a free
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module with basis $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$; there are unique
classes $c_i(E)\in H^i(B;\mathbb F_2)$ with
$$x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)=0\qquad\text{in }H^n(P(E);\mathbb F_2),$$
and this monic relation generates all polynomial relations: the
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-algebra homomorphism
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ sending $x$ to $x_E$ and
coefficients by pullback has kernel exactly the principal ideal generated by
$x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a numerable real rank-$n$ bundle $E\to B$ with $n\geq1$ over an admissible base, and the classes $x=x_E$ of [[lem-tautological-degree-one-class-is-well-defined-and-fiber-generating]].

[F1] $P(E)\to B$ is a numerable locally trivial fiber bundle with fiber $\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ ([[def-real-projective-bundle-and-tautological-line]]), and a numerable fiber bundle is a Hurewicz fibration, hence in particular a Serre fibration ([[thm-numerable-fiber-bundles-are-hurewicz-fibrations]]).

[F2] The restrictions of $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ to every fiber $P(E_b)\cong\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ form an $\mathbb F_2$-basis of $H^*(P(E_b);\mathbb F_2)$ ([[lem-tautological-degree-one-class-is-well-defined-and-fiber-generating]]).

[F3] Let $F\to E'\xrightarrow{p}B'$ be a Serre fibration over a path-connected CW complex and let finitely many homogeneous classes $e_i\in H^*(E';R)$ restrict to an $R$-basis of $H^*(F;R)$ on every fiber. Then $\Phi((a_i))=\sum_ip^*a_i\smile e_i$ is an $H^*(B';R)$-module isomorphism $\bigoplus_iH^{*-|e_i|}(B';R)\to H^*(E';R)$, natural in maps of such fibrations that pull the specified classes back to the specified classes ([[thm-leray-hirsch-module-isomorphism]]).

[F4] Pullback is a unital ring homomorphism and cup products are natural ([[prop-cup-product-is-natural-unital-and-associative]]).

[F5] For a Serre fibration, the long exact sequence $\cdots\to\pi_n(F)\to\pi_n(E')\to\pi_n(B')\to\pi_{n-1}(F)\to\cdots$ is exact and natural for maps of fibrations that restrict to homeomorphisms on the fiber ([[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-homotopy-groups-of-a-fibration]]).

[F6] Under AC, for every space $X$, abelian group $G$ and $n\geq0$ there is a natural short exact sequence $0\to\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z}(H_{n-1}(X;\mathbb Z),G)\to H^n(X;G)\to \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(H_n(X;\mathbb Z),G)\to0$ ([[thm-topological-universal-coefficient-short-exact-sequence-for-cohomology]]).

[F7] Every weak homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms on integral singular homology, with no choice principle and no CW hypothesis ([[lem-weak-homotopy-equivalences-induce-integral-homology-isomorphisms-without-choice]]).

[F8] Pullback is canonically functorial: $\operatorname{id}^*E\cong E$ and $f^*(g^*E)\cong(gf)^*E$ ([[prop-vector-bundle-pullback-is-functorial-up-to-canonical-isomorphism]]).

[F9] The total space of a numerable compact-fiber bundle over an admissible base is admissible ([[lem-compact-fibre-numerable-bundle-totals-are-paracompact-hausdorff-of-cw-type]]).

[A1] AC is the Axiom of Choice in the form fixed by [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof
1.1 The projection $P(E)\to B$ is a Serre fibration with fiber $\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ whose classes $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ restrict to a basis of the fiber cohomology. This is [F1] combined with [F2]: the fiber over $b$ is $P(E_b)$ and the restrictions of the displayed classes are a basis. [F1, F2]

2.1 Suppose first that $B$ is a CW complex. Then [F3] applies to the Serre fibration of step 1.1 with the classes $e_i=x^i$, $i=0,\ldots,n-1$, which are homogeneous of degree $i$ and restrict to a basis by step 1.1, provided the base is path-connected. If $B$ is path-connected, the conclusion is that $$\Phi:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}H^{*-i}(B;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2),\qquad (a_i)\longmapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^*a_i\smile x^i,$$ is an $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module isomorphism, so $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ is free over $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$ on $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$. If $B$ is a general CW complex, its path components $B_\alpha$ are open and closed subcomplexes, because a CW complex is locally path-connected and cells are connected; restricting $E$ gives a numerable bundle over the path-connected CW complex $B_\alpha$, and $P(E)=\coprod_\alpha P(E|_{B_\alpha})$. For a disjoint union $X=\coprod_\alpha X_\alpha$ of open and closed pieces the singular chain complex is the direct sum of the piece complexes, since the connected simplex $\Delta^k$ maps into a single piece; dualising gives a product of cochain complexes, whose cycles are exactly the families of cycles and whose coboundaries are exactly the families of coboundaries, the latter using AC to select one primitive at each index. Hence $H^k(X;R)\cong\prod_\alpha H^k(X_\alpha;R)$, and applying this to $B$ and to $P(E)$ turns the componentwise isomorphisms into the displayed $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module isomorphism. [F3, A1, step 1.1]

3.1 Now let $B$ be an arbitrary admissible base and choose a homotopy equivalence $g:K\to B$ from a CW complex, which exists by the definition of admissibility; the total spaces $P(E)$ and $P(E')$ are admissible by [F9], so all spaces in the following comparison lie in the page's convention. Put $E'=g^*E$ and let $g':P(E')\to P(E)$ be the map induced by $g$ on projective bundles; by [F8] the construction is compatible with the canonical pullback comparisons, so $P(E')\cong g^*P(E)$ and $g'$ is the projection of that pullback. The map $g'$ is a weak homotopy equivalence: the projections $P(E')\to K$ and $P(E)\to B$ are Serre fibrations with fiber $\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ by step 1.1, the map $g'$ covers $g$ and restricts to a homeomorphism on every fiber, so [F5] gives a map of exact sequences that is an isomorphism on the fiber and base terms, and the five lemma makes $g'$ an isomorphism on all homotopy groups of each component; the component bijection follows from that of $g$. Hence $g'$ induces isomorphisms on integral homology by [F7], and therefore on $\mathbb F_2$-cohomology by the natural universal coefficient sequence [F6] and the five lemma. Since $g$ is a homotopy equivalence it also induces an isomorphism $g^*$ on $\mathbb F_2$-cohomology. Writing $\Phi_K$ for the map of step 2.1 over the CW complex $K$ and $\Phi_B$ for the corresponding map over $B$, naturality of pullback [F4] gives $g'^*\Phi_B=\Phi_Kg^*$. The right side and the outer maps are isomorphisms, so $\Phi_B$ is an isomorphism as well; this proves the freeness clause over every admissible base, with module basis $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$. [F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1]

4.1 Existence and uniqueness of the coefficients. By step 3.1 the elements $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ form a module basis of $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ over $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$. The class $x^n\in H^n(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ therefore has a unique expansion $x^n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^*b_i\smile x^i$ with $b_i\in H^{n-i}(B;\mathbb F_2)$. Setting $c_i(E):=b_{n-i}\in H^i(B;\mathbb F_2)$, moving the terms to one side and using that the coefficient ring has characteristic two so that signs are trivial gives the unique relation $x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)=0$, whose leading coefficient is $1$. [step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The relation generates all relations. Let $\varphi:H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ be the $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-algebra homomorphism with $\varphi(x)=x$ and $\varphi(a)=p^*a$; it is well defined because [F4] makes $p^*$ a unital ring homomorphism. By step 3.1 it is surjective, since the module basis lies in its image. Let $f=x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)$ be the monic relation of step 4.1, of degree $n$. Division with remainder by a monic polynomial is available over any commutative ring, so every element of $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]$ has a unique representative $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}g_ix^i$ modulo the principal ideal $(f)$, and the classes $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ are a module basis of $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]/(f)$. The induced map $\bar\varphi:H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]/(f)\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ sends that basis to the module basis of step 3.1 and is $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-linear, so it is an isomorphism and $\ker\varphi=(f)$. [F4, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Boundary cases. For $n=1$ the basis is $1$ alone and the relation is $x+c_1(E)=0$, so the argument above applies verbatim. For a disconnected base the identification of $H^*$ with the product over components was recorded in step 2.1 and transferred in step 3.1. If $B=\varnothing$ then $P(E)=\varnothing$ and all groups are zero, so the statement is vacuous with the empty family of coefficients. The one-point fiber $\mathbb{RP}^0$ enters only through the basis statement for $n=1$. AC is used through [F1], [F3], the componentwise primitives of step 2.1 and the universal coefficient sequence of step 3.1, as recorded. [F1, F3, F6, A1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
