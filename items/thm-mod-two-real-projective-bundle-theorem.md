## Statement

Assume AC. Let $E\to B$ be a numerable real vector bundle of rank $n\geq1$ over
a paracompact Hausdorff CGWH base $B$ of CW homotopy type; in particular $B$ may be any CW complex. Let
$P(E)\to B$ be its projective bundle and $x=x_E\in H^1(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ the
tautological degree-one class. Then $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ is a free
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module with basis $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$; there are unique
classes $c_i(E)\in H^i(B;\mathbb F_2)$ with
$$x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)=0\qquad\text{in }H^n(P(E);\mathbb F_2),$$
and this monic relation generates all polynomial relations: the
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-algebra homomorphism
$H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ sending $x$ to $x_E$ and
coefficients by pullback has kernel exactly the principal ideal generated by
$x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a numerable real rank-$n$ bundle $E\to B$ with $n\geq1$ over a paracompact Hausdorff CGWH base of CW homotopy type, and the classes $x=x_E$ of [[lem-tautological-degree-one-class-is-well-defined-and-fiber-generating]].

[F1] $P(E)\to B$ is a numerable locally trivial fiber bundle with fiber $\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ ([[def-real-projective-bundle-and-tautological-line]]), and a numerable fiber bundle is a Hurewicz fibration, hence in particular a Serre fibration ([[thm-numerable-fiber-bundles-are-hurewicz-fibrations]]).

[F2] The restrictions of $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ to every fiber $P(E_b)\cong\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ form an $\mathbb F_2$-basis of $H^*(P(E_b);\mathbb F_2)$ ([[lem-tautological-degree-one-class-is-well-defined-and-fiber-generating]]).

[F3] Let $F\to E'\xrightarrow{p}B'$ be a Serre fibration over a path-connected CW complex and let finitely many homogeneous classes $e_i\in H^*(E';R)$ restrict to an $R$-basis of $H^*(F;R)$ on every fiber. Then $\Phi((a_i))=\sum_ip^*a_i\smile e_i$ is an $H^*(B';R)$-module isomorphism $\bigoplus_iH^{*-|e_i|}(B';R)\to H^*(E';R)$, natural in maps of such fibrations that pull the specified classes back to the specified classes ([[thm-leray-hirsch-module-isomorphism]]).

[F4] Pullback is a unital ring homomorphism and cup products are natural ([[prop-cup-product-is-natural-unital-and-associative]]).

[F5] The homotopy long exact sequence of a Serre fibration is exact and natural, including the component tail ([[thm-long-exact-sequence-of-homotopy-groups-of-a-fibration]], [[thm-fibration-sequence-is-natural]]).

[F6] Under AC, for every space $X$, abelian group $G$ and $n\geq0$ there is a natural short exact sequence $0\to\operatorname{Ext}^1_{\mathbb Z}(H_{n-1}(X;\mathbb Z),G)\to H^n(X;G)\to \operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z}(H_n(X;\mathbb Z),G)\to0$ ([[thm-topological-universal-coefficient-short-exact-sequence-for-cohomology]]).

[F7] Every weak homotopy equivalence induces isomorphisms on integral singular homology, with no choice principle and no CW hypothesis ([[lem-weak-homotopy-equivalences-induce-integral-homology-isomorphisms-without-choice]]).

[F8] Pullback is canonically functorial: $\operatorname{id}^*E\cong E$ and $f^*(g^*E)\cong(gf)^*E$ ([[prop-vector-bundle-pullback-is-functorial-up-to-canonical-isomorphism]]).

[F9] Under AC, a numerable bundle with compact Hausdorff CW-type fiber over a paracompact Hausdorff CW-type base has paracompact Hausdorff CW-type total space ([[lem-compact-fibre-numerable-bundle-totals-are-paracompact-hausdorff-of-cw-type]]).

[A1] AC is the Axiom of Choice in the form fixed by [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[F10] Homotopy equivalences induce cohomology isomorphisms ([[thm-homotopic-maps-induce-equal-maps-in-singular-cohomology]]); the module five lemma applies to diagrams of the natural cohomological universal coefficient sequences ([[thm-five-lemma-for-modules]]).

[F11] Singular cohomology is graded-commutative; over $\mathbb F_2$ it is therefore commutative without degree restrictions ([[thm-singular-cohomology-is-graded-commutative]]).

## Proof
1.1 The projection $P(E)\to B$ is a Serre fibration with fiber $\mathbb{RP}^{n-1}$ whose classes $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ restrict to a basis of the fiber cohomology. This is [F1] combined with [F2]: the fiber over $b$ is $P(E_b)$ and the restrictions of the displayed classes are a basis. [F1, F2]

1.2 Choose a homotopy equivalence $g:K\to B$ from a CW complex, put $E'=g^*E$, and form $g':P(E')\to P(E)$. Projectivization commutes with this pullback: in a pulled-back linear chart the identification sends $(k,\ell)$ to $(k,(g(k),\ell))$, and the formulas agree on overlaps since both have the same pulled-back linear transitions. Hence $P(E')\cong g^*P(E)$, with $g'$ restricting to the identity on each projective fiber. Both projective totals are paracompact Hausdorff of CW type by [F9]. Both projections are Serre fibrations by [F1]. In their natural homotopy sequences [F5], the fiber and base maps are isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. A direct exactness chase gives the same for the total map, including degree one: to lift a target total class, lift its base image by the base isomorphism; its fiber boundary vanishes by fiber injectivity, so it lifts to the source total group. Correct the difference using surjectivity on the fiber group. For injectivity, a source class killed in the target has zero base image, so comes from a fiber class. Its image is a target base boundary; lift that boundary through the base isomorphism and use fiber injectivity to see the original total class vanishes. The degree-one chase uses products rather than sums and the fact that the fiber is path connected, so the boundary to its component set is trivial. Path lifting with path-connected fibers identifies total components with base components. Thus $g'$ is a weak homotopy equivalence. [F1, F5, F8, F9]

2.1 Suppose first that $B$ is a CW complex. Then [F3] applies to the Serre fibration of step 1.1 with the classes $e_i=x^i$, $i=0,\ldots,n-1$, which are homogeneous of degree $i$ and restrict to a basis by step 1.1, provided the base is path-connected. If $B$ is path-connected, the conclusion is that $$\Phi:\bigoplus_{i=0}^{n-1}H^{*-i}(B;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2),\qquad (a_i)\longmapsto\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^*a_i\smile x^i,$$ is an $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module isomorphism, so $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ is free over $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$ on $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$. If $B$ is a general CW complex, its path components $B_\alpha$ are open and closed subcomplexes, because a CW complex is locally path-connected and cells are connected; restricting $E$ gives a numerable bundle over the path-connected CW complex $B_\alpha$, and $P(E)=\coprod_\alpha P(E|_{B_\alpha})$. For a disjoint union $X=\coprod_\alpha X_\alpha$ of open and closed pieces the singular chain complex is the direct sum of the piece complexes, since the connected simplex $\Delta^k$ maps into a single piece; dualising gives a product of cochain complexes, whose cycles are exactly the families of cycles and whose coboundaries are exactly the families of coboundaries, the latter using AC to select one primitive at each index. Hence $H^k(X;R)\cong\prod_\alpha H^k(X_\alpha;R)$, and applying this to $B$ and to $P(E)$ turns the componentwise isomorphisms into the displayed $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-module isomorphism. [F3, A1, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F7], $g'$ is an integral homology isomorphism, and by [F6] and the module five lemma [F10], $g'^*$ is an isomorphism on $\mathbb F_2$-cohomology. Also $g^*$ is a cohomology isomorphism by [F10]. Apply Leray–Hirsch over each CW component of $K$ using the specified classes $e_i=g'^*(x^i)$, not an unproved identification with a separately defined tautological class on $P(E')$. They restrict to a fiber basis because $g'$ is the identity on each fiber and [F2] supplies that basis. The component argument of step 2.1 gives an isomorphism $\Phi_K$ for these classes. Naturality [F4] gives $g'^*\Phi_B=\Phi_K(\bigoplus_i g^*)$. The other three maps are isomorphisms, so $\Phi_B$ is an isomorphism. Finite sums indexed by $0\leq i<n$ commute with the degreewise component products. This proves the module claim on the stated general base. [F2, F3, F4, F6, F7, F10, step 2.1, step 1.2]

4.1 Existence and uniqueness of the coefficients. By step 3.1 the elements $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ form a module basis of $H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ over $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$. The class $x^n\in H^n(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ therefore has a unique expansion $x^n=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}p^*b_i\smile x^i$ with $b_i\in H^{n-i}(B;\mathbb F_2)$. Setting $c_i(E):=b_{n-i}\in H^i(B;\mathbb F_2)$, moving the terms to one side and using that the coefficient ring has characteristic two so that signs are trivial gives the unique relation $x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)=0$, whose leading coefficient is $1$. [step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The relation generates all relations. Let $\varphi:H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ be the $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-algebra homomorphism with $\varphi(x)=x$ and $\varphi(a)=p^*a$; it is well defined because [F4] makes $p^*$ a unital ring homomorphism and [F11] makes all classes commute. By step 3.1 it is surjective, since the module basis lies in its image. Let $f=x^n+c_1(E)x^{n-1}+\cdots+c_n(E)$ be the monic relation of step 4.1, of degree $n$. Division with remainder by a monic polynomial is available over any commutative ring, so every element of $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]$ has a unique representative $\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}g_ix^i$ modulo the principal ideal $(f)$, and the classes $1,x,\ldots,x^{n-1}$ are a module basis of $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]/(f)$. The induced map $\bar\varphi:H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)[x]/(f)\to H^*(P(E);\mathbb F_2)$ sends that basis to the module basis of step 3.1 and is $H^*(B;\mathbb F_2)$-linear, so it is an isomorphism and $\ker\varphi=(f)$. [F4, F11, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Boundary cases. For $n=1$ the basis is $1$ alone and the relation is $x+c_1(E)=0$, so the argument above applies verbatim. For a disconnected base the degreewise identification of cohomology with the product over components was recorded in step 2.1 and transferred in step 3.1. If $B=\varnothing$ then $P(E)=\varnothing$ and all groups are zero, so the statement is valid with the unique $n$ zero coefficients. The one-point fiber $\mathbb{RP}^0$ enters only through the basis statement for $n=1$. AC is used through [F1], [F3], the componentwise primitives of step 2.1 and the universal coefficient sequence of step 3.1, as recorded. [F1, F3, F6, A1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
