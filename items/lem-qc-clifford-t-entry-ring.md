## Statement

Every finite circuit on $n$ ordered wires using only the matrices in
[[def-qc-clifford-t-gate-library]] has all matrix entries in $D$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, chronological gate order, and the exact matrices in the gate-library definition.

[F1] [[def-qc-dyadic-cyclotomic-ring]]: $D=\mathbb Z[\omega,1/2]$ is a subring of $\mathbb C$ and contains $\omega$ and $1/2$.

[F2] [[def-qc-clifford-t-gate-library]]: $H$ has entries $\pm1/\sqrt2$, $T,T^\dagger,S,Z$ have entries in $\{0\}\cup\{\omega^j:j\in\mathbb Z\}$, and CNOT is a computational-basis permutation matrix.

[F3] [[lem-qc-local-gate-wire-embedding]]: each gate occurrence acts on its named ordered wires and as identity on the complementary wires.

[F4] [[def-qc-circuit-denotational-semantics]]: a circuit matrix is the finite product of its layer matrices, with later chronological layers multiplying on the left.

## Proof


1.1 Since $1/\sqrt2=\sqrt2/2=(\omega-\omega^3)/2\in D$, every entry of $H$ lies in $D$. The displayed diagonal gates have entries $0$ or powers of $\omega$, the scalar Clifford phases have entries powers of $\omega$, and the displayed CNOT has entries $0$ or $1$, so each primitive gate matrix has entries in $D$ by [F1] and [F2]. The embeddings in [F3] are obtained from tensoring with identity matrices and permuting factors; these operations only insert zeros and copy entries, hence preserve membership in $D$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 The product of two matrices with entries in a subring has entries that are finite sums of products in that subring. By the finite product interpretation [F4], induction on the circuit's finite gate list therefore places every entry of its matrix in $D$; the empty word is the identity matrix and also has entries in $D$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
