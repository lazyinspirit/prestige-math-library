## Example

Let $S$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}$ be the free
$\mathcal O_S$-module of rank $r\ge0$. Then, with the projective bundle
$\mathbb P_S(E)=\operatorname{Proj}_S\operatorname{Sym}(E)$ in the quotient
convention ([[def-projective-bundle-scheme]]):

1. for $r\ge1$ there is a canonical isomorphism
   $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S^{\,r})\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}_S$ of $S$-schemes,
   carrying the tautological quotient
   $\pi^*\mathcal O_S^{\,r}\to\mathcal O(1)$ to the standard quotient
   $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{r-1}_S}(1)$ whose components
   are the coordinate sections;
2. for $r=1$ one has $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S)=S$, and under the
   identification $\mathcal O_{\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S)}(1)\cong\mathcal O_S$
   given by the coordinate frame the tautological quotient is the identity
   morphism $\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal O_S$;
3. for $r=0$ one has $\mathbb P_S(0)=\varnothing$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $S$, an integer $r\ge0$, the free $\mathcal O_S$-module $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}$, and the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the relative Proj construction.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] Symmetric algebra: for a commutative ring $A$ and the free module $A^r$ one has $\operatorname{Sym}_A(A^r)=A[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ with $\deg T_i=1$, generated by its degree-one part; for a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_S$-module $F$ the symmetric algebra $\operatorname{Sym}(F)$ is the quasi-coherent graded $\mathcal O_S$-algebra glued from these affine models, with $\operatorname{Sym}^0(F)=\mathcal O_S$ and $\operatorname{Sym}^1(F)=F$, and it is generated as an $\mathcal O_S$-algebra by $F$. ([[def-symmetric-algebra-qc-module]], [[def-relative-proj-quasi-coherent-graded-algebra]])

[F2] Projective bundle: $\mathbb P_S(E)=\operatorname{Proj}_S\operatorname{Sym}(E)$ with structural morphism $\pi$ and tautological quotient $\pi^*E\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P_S(E)}(1)$; if $E|_U\cong\mathcal O_U^{\,r}$ with $r\ge1$ over an open $U\subseteq S$, then $\mathbb P_S(E)|_U\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}_U$ by the absolute case of relative Proj, the twist $\mathcal O(1)|_U$ corresponds to the standard twist, and the tautological quotient restricts to the standard quotient $\mathcal O_U^{\,r}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{r-1}_U}(1)$ whose components are the coordinate sections; if $E=0$ then $\mathbb P_S(0)=\varnothing$. The standard charts of $\mathbb P^{r-1}_S$ are the affine spaces $\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal O_S[x^{(i)}_\ell:\ell\ne i]$, with twisting sheaf glued from frames $e_i$ and coordinate sections $x_j$ satisfying $x_j|_{U_i}=x^{(i)}_je_i$ for $j\ne i$ and $x_i|_{U_i}=e_i$; the case $r-1=0$ gives $\mathbb P^0_S\cong S$ with a single frame. ([[def-projective-bundle-scheme]], [[def-relative-proj-quasi-coherent-graded-algebra]], [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]], [[def-very-ample-invertible-sheaf-relative]], [[thm-projective-space-as-proj]])

[F3] Representing property: for every $S$-scheme $g:T\to S$, $S$-morphisms $T\to\mathbb P_S(E)$ correspond naturally to isomorphism classes of surjections $g^*E\to L$ with $L$ invertible on $T$, the universal element being the tautological quotient. ([[thm-projective-bundle-represents-line-quotients]])

[F4] A surjective morphism between invertible sheaves is an isomorphism: locally on an affine chart both sides are free of rank one, so the morphism is multiplication by a section which must be a unit at each point of the source, and invertibility of the map is local. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]], [[def-pullback-module-ringed-spaces]])

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct: identify the symmetric algebra of a free module with a polynomial algebra, apply the frame description of the projective bundle on the whole of $S$, and compute the cases $r=1$ and $r=0$ from the chart and rank-zero descriptions.

1.1 The symmetric algebra. Since $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}$ is free of rank $r$, [F1] identifies $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal O_S^{\,r})$ with the graded $\mathcal O_S$-algebra $\mathcal O_S[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ with $\deg T_i=1$, generated by its degree-one part; hence $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S^{\,r})=\operatorname{Proj}_S\mathcal O_S[T_1,\dots,T_r]$. [F1, F2]

1.2 The case $r=0$. The zero module $E=0$ has $\operatorname{Sym}(0)=\mathcal O_S$ concentrated in degree $0$ and $\mathbb P_S(0)=\varnothing$ by [F2]; consistently, for every $S$-scheme $T$ a surjection $0\to L$ onto an invertible sheaf exists only when $T=\varnothing$, since an invertible sheaf on a nonempty scheme is nonzero, and morphisms $T\to\varnothing$ likewise exist only for $T=\varnothing$. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 The isomorphism with projective space. Applying [F2] with $U=S$, where $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}|_S=\mathcal O_S^{\,r}$ is free of rank $r\ge1$, gives $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S^{\,r})\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}_S$; under this isomorphism the twist $\mathcal O(1)$ corresponds to the standard twist and the tautological quotient restricts to the standard quotient $\mathcal O_S^{\,r}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^{r-1}_S}(1)$ whose components are the coordinate sections $x_0,\dots,x_{r-1}$. This is the isomorphism of (1), and it is canonical because it is the chart-gluing identification of the two constructions. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 The case $r=1$. Here $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S)\cong\mathbb P^0_S=S$ by step 2.1 and [F2], and $\mathbb P^0_S$ has the single chart $U_0=S$ with frame $e_0$, so $\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_S$ with frame the coordinate section $x_0$; the tautological quotient $\mathcal O_S=\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathcal O_S)\to\mathcal O(1)$ sends the generator to the coordinate section, which is the frame $e_0$, hence is an isomorphism $\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_S$: under the identification by the frame it is the identity. Equivalently, by [F3] the right side for $E=\mathcal O_S$ consists of isomorphism classes of surjections $g^*\mathcal O_S=\mathcal O_T\to L$ with $L$ invertible, and every such surjection is an isomorphism by [F4], so there is exactly one class; correspondingly $T\to\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S)$ is the single structure morphism $T\to S$, and the two descriptions agree. [F2, F3, F4, step 2.1]

4.1 Conclusion. Steps 1.1 and 1.2 give the isomorphism $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S^{\,r})\cong\mathbb P^{r-1}_S$ for $r\ge1$ together with the identification of the universal quotients, step 3.1 computes the case $r=1$ as $\mathbb P_S(\mathcal O_S)=S$ with tautological quotient the identity $\mathcal O_S\to\mathcal O_S$, and step 1.2 records $\mathbb P_S(0)=\varnothing$. The identification of universal quotients is what makes the isomorphism an isomorphism "in the quotient convention" of [F3]: for $E=\mathcal O_S^{\,r}$ the functor $T\mapsto\{S\text{-morphisms }T\to\mathbb P^{r-1}_S\}$ is the functor of surjections $\mathcal O_T^{\,r}\to L$ in both models. The Axiom of Choice [A1] is inherited from the relative Proj construction; no further choice is made. [A1, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, cases: r=0 and r=1 and r at least 2]
\qed
