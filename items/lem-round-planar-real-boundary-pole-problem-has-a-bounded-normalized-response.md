## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $A$ be a bounded round disk with $e-1$ disjoint closed round disks removed, and let $x_j$ lie on its $e$ boundary circles. Choose the upper-half-plane Möbius coordinates $w_j$ and positive boundary-pole kernels $P_j$ of the planar period theorem. Choose disjoint real smooth cutoffs $\chi_j$ equal to one near $x_j$, and put $q_j=-\chi_j/w_j$.

Let $X$ consist of functions $u=v+\sum_jr_jq_j$, where $r_j\in\mathbb R$ and $v\in H^1(A;\mathbb C)$ has real boundary trace. Give $X$ the real Hilbert norm $(\|v\|_{H^1}^2+|r|^2)^{1/2}$; the coefficients are uniquely determined. The actual operator $\bar\partial:X\to L^2(A;\mathbb C)$ is bounded and onto, has a bounded real-linear right inverse, and its real kernel is exactly $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{1,p_0\}$, where $p_0$ is holomorphic with imaginary part the positive zero-period kernel $\sum_jc_jP_j$. Thus its real Fredholm index is two.

For any fixed $a\in A$ and compact $K\Subset A\setminus\{a\}$, there is a unique solution $S_af\in X$ of $\bar\partial u=f$, $u(a)=0$, for every $f\in L^2$ supported in $K$. The value is well defined because the solution is holomorphic near $a$. This solution operator is bounded. If $f\in H^q$, its renormalized part belongs to $H^{q+1}$ and
$$|r|+\left\|S_af-\sum_jr_jq_j\right\|_{H^{q+1}}\le C_q\|f\|_{H^q}.$$
In particular its traces and any fixed number of derivatives on interior circles a positive distance from $K$ are bounded by the forcing norm. No point evaluation on arbitrary $H^1$ functions is imposed.

This is an actual component response for the planar meromorphic boundary problem. It supplies neither the higher-genus slit problem nor the coupled target-neck obstruction or count.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed finite round planar domain, boundary points and Möbius coordinates, cutoffs and the optional normalization point and forcing support. Sobolev spaces are the actual weak spaces; real boundary traces use the finite smooth edge charts.

[F1] The actual kernels $P_j$ are positive harmonic functions with local singular part $\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)$ and smooth zero-edge remainder. Their period matrix has rank $e-1$, image the zero-total-period hyperplane, and kernel a positive one-dimensional line generated by $c=(c_j)$. ([[lem-round-planar-boundary-pole-kernels-have-a-positive-period-kernel]])

[F2] Countable-Choice Hilbert orthogonal projection is constructed in the Hilbert parametrix proof. Plancherel gives the plane Cauchy-transform derivative multiplier. The geometric disk proof gives actual local real-boundary $H^1$ approximation and weak higher Cauchy--Riemann estimates using tangential difference quotients and the equation for normal derivatives. Those local estimates apply in the finitely many round-edge charts here. The local-addition proof constructs the finite-dimensional smooth flows used in the zero-trace approximation. ([[lem-boundary-preserving-local-addition-for-transverse-product-tori]], [[lem-countable-choice-hilbert-parametrices-and-index-stability]], [[thm-plancherel]], [[lem-geometric-oriented-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm-and-higher-regularity]])

[F3] The circle Cauchy formula controls derivatives of a holomorphic function on a smaller interior disk by its values on a containing circle; its circle and radial Cauchy--Schwarz versions give the corresponding interior $L^2$ estimates. ([[thm-cauchy-integral-formula-circle]])

## Proof

1.1 We record the elementary weak-domain facts used below. Finite smooth edge flattenings and the radial fundamental theorem of calculus give a bounded $H^1$ trace, exactly as in [F2]'s disk proof. Real trace is closed. Zero trace is precisely $H^1_0(A)$: zero extension has weak square-integrable derivatives because its seam integration-by-parts terms are the zero trace; push that extension inward by the smooth flow of a vector field strictly inward on all boundary circles, and then convolve on a radius smaller than its distance to the boundary. These compactly supported smooth approximants converge in $H^1$ by translation continuity and the bounded smooth flow Jacobians. Such a field exists by finitely many circle collars and cutoffs. The zero-trace Poincare inequality follows by zero extension into a fixed box and integrating a coordinate derivative along its full line; hence $H^1_0$ is Hilbert in the gradient norm. We also use $\|g-\operatorname{av}_A g\|_2\le C\|\nabla g\|_2$ on this connected domain. A direct proof covers its closure by finitely many interior disks and flattened half-disks with connected overlap graph. On each convex patch the coordinate-segment double-integral variance proof gives this estimate with a local mean. Means on overlapping patches differ by their deviations on an overlap of positive area. A finite chain in the overlap graph bounds every mean by one fixed patch mean; summing proves the inequality. For initially only locally square-integrable $g$ with globally square-integrable gradient, apply these estimates on nested smaller convex patches containing one fixed subdisk; their constants stay bounded, and monotone exhaustion gives the same conclusion after subtracting that subdisk mean. These arguments use only finite patches and the prescribed Countable Choice Hilbert procedures. [F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Extend $f\in L^2(A)$ by zero to the plane and set $Vf(z)=\pi^{-1}\int_A f(\zeta)/(z-\zeta)\,d\zeta$. This is a bounded map to $H^1(A)$ with $\bar\partial Vf=f$. Indeed the kernel restricted to a fixed surrounding ball is $L^1$, so its convolution bounds the local $L^2$ norm by the integral triangle inequality. For smooth compact data the distribution identity $\bar\partial(1/(\pi z))=\delta_0$ follows by polar integration, and the other derivative is the Fourier multiplier $(i\xi_x+\xi_y)/(i\xi_x-\xi_y)$ of modulus one. The derivative is in $L^2$: differentiating the convolution of smooth compact data and subtracting its zero integral at infinity gives $O(|z|^{-2})$ there, and local convolution is smooth. Plancherel therefore proves its bound without a point-mass term at frequency zero. Approximation in $L^2$ passes these distribution identities and bounds to all the data. Let $B$ be the weak harmonic function with boundary trace $\operatorname{Im}Vf$. Construct it as $B=\operatorname{Im}Vf+w$, where $w\in H^1_0$ solves $\int\nabla w\cdot\nabla\phi=-\int\nabla\operatorname{Im}Vf\cdot\nabla\phi$. Gradient-norm Hilbert representation gives the unique $w$ and its bound. Representation follows from [F2]'s projection by projecting one vector of functional value one onto its closed kernel, so imports no full-choice variational theorem. Thus $\|B\|_{H^1}\le C\|f\|_2$. Weak harmonicity gives interior smoothness and bounds by a local high-frequency Laplace inverse: its order-minus-two symbol, cut off at frequency one, gains two Sobolev orders, while its cutoff commutator has order one; iterating on nested disks gives every local order, and Fourier Cauchy--Schwarz gives smoothness. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 The conjugate differential $\alpha_B=B_y\,dx-B_x\,dy$ is closed. Its periods on fixed interior parallels of the boundary circles sum to zero by the weak harmonic equation and ordinary compact interior Green integration. Their absolute values are bounded by $C\|B\|_{H^1}$ by the interior estimates in step2.1. By [F1], choose real $r_j$ depending linearly and boundedly on this period vector so that $H=B+\sum_jr_jP_j$ has zero periods; a fixed nonzero $(e-1)$-minor of its finite period matrix gives an explicit bounded right inverse onto the zero-sum hyperplane. The differential $\alpha_H$ then has a single-valued real primitive $A_H$. To justify this finite planar assertion, connect each inner round circle to the outer one by disjoint piecewise smooth arcs forming a tree, chosen successively with finite polygonal detours around the finitely many other disks and earlier arcs. Cutting along those arcs gives a disk. On that disk closed-form path integrals are independent by elementary plane Green integration, and the jumps when the banks are rejoined are exactly the chosen boundary periods. Their vanishing makes the primitive single valued on $A$. The holomorphic function $F=A_H+iH$ has the SAME imaginary boundary trace as $Vf$ away from its prescribed poles, because every $P_j$ has zero regular boundary trace. Locally each $P_j$ differs from $\operatorname{Im}(-1/w_j)$ by a smooth zero-edge harmonic function, so $F+r_j/w_j$ has $H^1$ regular part near that pole. Globally this also follows quantitatively: subtract the finitely many cutoff principal parts from $H$ and $A_H$; the remaining gradient norms are bounded by $C(\|B\|_{H^1}+|r|)$, because all cutoff derivatives are supported away from the poles. Choose the remaining real additive constant to give the regular real part mean zero. Step1.1's mean inequality gives its $L^2$ bound. Thus $F-\sum_jr_jq_j\in H^1$ with norm at most $C\|f\|_2$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Put $u=Vf-F$. Its imaginary regular boundary trace is zero, and its pole coefficients are the real numbers $-r_j$. Thus $u\in X$, $\bar\partial u=f$, and its renormalized $H^1$ norm plus coefficient norm is bounded by $C\|f\|_2$. This construction is real linear with the fixed minor and mean normalization just chosen, so is an actual bounded right inverse. The representation of $X$ is unique since $1/w_j$ is not locally $L^2$ at its own boundary point whereas its prescribed regular remainder is; different singular supports cannot cancel. Also $\bar\partial q_j$ is smooth with support away from its pole, so the operator on $X$ is bounded. For its kernel the imaginary part has zero regular boundary trace. Subtract its prescribed combination of the $P_j$; the remainder is weak harmonic, in $H^1_0$, and zero by testing its weak equation against itself. Hence every kernel imaginary part is $\sum d_jP_j$. Existence of a single-valued conjugate forces $Cd=0$, so [F1] makes it a real multiple of $\sum c_jP_j$. Integrating its zero-period conjugate constructs $p_0$, unique up to a real constant, with that positive imaginary part. Kernel elements are exactly a real constant plus a real multiple of $p_0$. The onto bounded operator between the actual Hilbert spaces therefore has real index two, with closed range all of its target. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For $f$ supported in the fixed compact $K$ disjoint from $a$, this solution is weakly holomorphic near $a$ and hence smooth there by the local estimates in [F2]. The interior Cauchy estimate [F3] bounds $|u(a)|$ by its local $L^2$ norm on a fixed disk away from $K$ and the boundary poles. Since $\operatorname{Im}p_0(a)>0$, evaluation of the two-dimensional real kernel at $a$ is an isomorphism onto $\mathbb C$. Explicitly subtract $t p_0+s$, where $t=\operatorname{Im}u(a)/\operatorname{Im}p_0(a)$ and $s=\operatorname{Re}u(a)-t\operatorname{Re}p_0(a)$. The result is the unique solution with value zero at $a$ and is bounded by $C\|f\|_2$ in the renormalized norm. This evaluation was applied only to actual solutions holomorphic on the fixed disk, never to arbitrary $H^1$ sections. For higher forcing orders, subtract the cutoff principal parts. Its remainder has real $H^1$ trace and smooth inhomogeneity from those cutoffs. Flatten each round edge by a Möbius coordinate and apply the actual tangential difference quotient estimate from [F2]; the equation recovers normal derivatives. On nested patches this gives $H^{q+1}$ with bound by $\|f\|_{H^q}$ and the already bounded $H^1$ remainder and coefficients. Interior patches use the same integration identity without a boundary. A finite cover gives the stated estimate with no extra higher trace condition. On interior circles separated from $K$, the solution is holomorphic on a fixed collar; [F3]'s Cauchy estimates bound every fixed derivative and trace there by its controlled local $L^2$ norm. These are the asserted genuine normalized response operators, without a higher-genus or target-neck inference. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
