## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-module
with formal character $\operatorname{ch}V\in\mathcal R$, the completed
character ring of
[[def-completed-formal-character-ring-for-downward-cones]], and let $A$ be
the Weyl alternation operator of [[def-weyl-alternation-operator]], with
$A(\eta)=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w\eta}$ for $\eta\in\mathfrak h^*$ and
$A(\rho)$ the Weyl denominator. For every $\nu\in\Lambda^+$ the coefficient of
$e^{\nu+\rho}$ in $A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}V\in\mathcal R$ equals the
multiplicity $[V:L(\nu)]$ of
[[def-tensor-product-multiplicity-for-highest-weight-modules]]. Explicitly,
$$\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}[V:L(\mu)]\operatorname{ch}L(\mu) \qquad\text{and}\qquad A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}[V:L(\mu)]\,A(\mu+\rho),$$
and $[e^{\nu+\rho}]A(\mu+\rho)=\delta_{\mu\nu}$ for $\mu,\nu\in\Lambda^+$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-module $V$ with decomposition $V\cong\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}L(\mu)^{\oplus[V:L(\mu)]}$ and a dominant integral weight $\nu$.

[F1] Weyl character formula: $\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)=A(\mu+\rho)/A(\rho)$ for $\mu\in\Lambda^+$, i.e. $A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)=A(\mu+\rho)$; the alternants have finite support, the completed ring $\mathcal R$ contains $A(\rho)$ as an invertible element with inverse the Weyl-denominator geometric series, and $A(\eta)=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w\eta}$ ([[thm-weyl-character-formula]], [[def-weyl-alternation-operator]], [[lem-geometric-series-invertibility-in-the-completed-character-ring]], [[def-completed-formal-character-ring-for-downward-cones]]).

[F2] The formal character is additive over direct sums and multiplicative over tensor products, and it determines the multiplicities: the coefficient of $\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)$ in $\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_\mu a_\mu\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)$ satisfies $a_\mu=[V:L(\mu)]$ ([[prop-formal-characters-are-additive-and-multiplicative]], [[prop-tensor-product-multiplicities-are-character-structure-constants]], [[thm-weyls-complete-reducibility-theorem]], [[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]]).

[F3] For each $\nu\in\Lambda^+$, $\nu+\rho$ is strictly dominant ([[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]]). Each real Weyl orbit has one closed-dominant representative, and the stabilizer of that representative is generated by the simple reflections whose walls contain it. Thus the stabilizer of $\nu+\rho$ is trivial, and if $w(\mu+\rho)=\nu+\rho$ for dominant integral $\mu,\nu$, uniqueness first gives $\mu=\nu$, then triviality of the stabilizer gives $w=1$ ([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]], [[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]]).

## Proof

1.1 The decomposition of $V$ into simple summands and the additivity of the formal character [F2] give $\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}[V:L(\mu)]\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)$, a finite sum. Multiplying by the ring element $A(\rho)$ and using $A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}L(\mu)=A(\mu+\rho)$ from [F1] gives $$A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}[V:L(\mu)]A(\mu+\rho).$$ [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 Coefficient of a dominant translate. For $\nu\in\Lambda^+$ and any $\mu\in\Lambda^+$, $$[e^{\nu+\rho}]A(\mu+\rho)=[e^{\nu+\rho}]\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}e^{w(\mu+\rho)} =\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}[w(\mu+\rho)=\nu+\rho],$$ a finite sum. The term $w=1$ contributes $1$ when $\mu=\nu$. If $w\ne1$ and $w(\mu+\rho)=\nu+\rho$, then $\mu+\rho=w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)$ would be a strictly dominant weight conjugate to the strictly dominant weight $\nu+\rho$, which by [F3] forces $w=1$, a contradiction; hence all such terms are $0$ and $[e^{\nu+\rho}]A(\mu+\rho)=\delta_{\mu\nu}$. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 Extraction. Taking the coefficient of $e^{\nu+\rho}$ in step 1.1 and using step 1.2 gives $$[e^{\nu+\rho}]A(\rho)\operatorname{ch}V=\sum_{\mu\in\Lambda^+}[V:L(\mu)]\,\delta_{\mu\nu}=[V:L(\nu)],$$ which is the asserted extraction formula; here the sum over $\mu$ is finite because $V$ is finite-dimensional. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
