## Statement

Let $k$ be any field, let $t$ be an indeterminate and let
$A:=k[t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}]\subseteq k[t]$ be the $k$-subalgebra generated by
$t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}$. Then the integral closure of $A$ in the rational function
field $k(t)$ is exactly $k[t]$, and $k[t]=A+At+At^{2}$ is a finite $A$-module.
The conductor ideal of $A$ in $k[t]$ is $(t^{3},t^{4},t^{5})$ and is strictly
smaller than $k[t]$; it is not the object computed here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $k$ and the $k$-subalgebras $A=k[t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}]\subseteq k[t]\subseteq k(t)$ of the polynomial ring in one indeterminate and its fraction field.

[L1] For a field $k$ and elements $s_1,\ldots,s_r$ of a $k$-algebra, the subalgebra generated by them is written $k[s_1,\ldots,s_r]$ and is the smallest subring containing $k$ and the $s_i$; $\operatorname{Frac}(D)$ is the fraction field of a domain $D$ ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]], [[def-field-extension-generated-subfields-and-simple-extension]], [[def-field-of-fractions]]).

[L2] The polynomial ring $k[t]$ over a field is an integral domain with fraction field $k(t)$ ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]], [[def-zero-divisor-and-integral-domain]], [[def-field-of-fractions]]).

[L3] For every field $F$ and $d\ge0$ the polynomial ring $F[y_1,\ldots,y_d]$ is an integrally closed domain ([[lem-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-integrally-closed]], [[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).

[L4] Integrality over a subring means being a root of a monic polynomial over that subring; integrality is transitive along domain inclusions; integral elements form a subring; an integrally closed domain contains every integral element of its fraction field ([[def-integral-element-and-algebraic-integer]], [[thm-transitivity-of-integrality]], [[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]], [[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $t=t^{4}/t^{3}$ is a quotient of two elements of $A$ with $t^{3}\ne0$, so $t\in\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ by [L1]; hence $k[t]\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)$, and $k(t)=\operatorname{Frac}(k[t])\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(A)\subseteq k(t)$ by [L2] because $A\subseteq k[t]$. Thus $\operatorname{Frac}(A)=k(t)$. [L1, L2, given]

1.2 The element $t$ is integral over $A$, being a root of the monic polynomial $T^{3}-t^{3}\in A[T]$; and $k[t]=A+At+At^{2}$ is a finite $A$-module, because every polynomial in $t$ is a finite $k$-linear combination of powers of $t$ and the relation $t^{3}=t^{3}\in A$ reduces the exponents modulo $3$: $t^{3m+r}=t^{3m}t^{r}=(t^{3})^{m}t^{r}$ with $t^{3}\in A$ and $r\in\{0,1,2\}$. Hence every element of $k[t]$ is integral over $A$. [L4, given]

2.1 As in the cusp computation: if $z\in k(t)=\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ is integral over $A$, every monic equation for $z$ over $A$ also has coefficients in $k[t]$, so $z$ is integral over $k[t]$, and since $k[t]$ is integrally closed in its fraction field $k(t)$ by [L3] and [L2], $z\in k[t]$. With step 1.2 this shows that the integral closure of $A=k[t^{3},t^{4},t^{5}]$ in $k(t)$ is exactly $k[t]=A+At+At^{2}$, a finite $A$-module. The conductor ideal $(t^{3},t^{4},t^{5})\subseteq A$ is a proper ideal of $A$ contained in $A$, not in the closure beyond $A$, and plays no role in the identification of the closure. [L2, L3, L4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
