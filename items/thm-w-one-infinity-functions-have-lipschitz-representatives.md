## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $n\ge1$ and let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ be open, bounded and convex. If $u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$, then $u$ has a representative $u^{*}$ with
$$|u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(y)|\le\|Du\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\,|x-y|\qquad(x,y\in\Omega),$$
and $u^{*}=u$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$. Conversely, every Lipschitz function $f:\Omega\to\mathbb K$ with constant $L$ lies in $W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)$ and $\|Df\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le L$ for real scalars, while $\|Df\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}\le\sqrt2 L$ for complex scalars. In both cases the real-linear derivative has operator norm at most $L$ almost everywhere.

**Gradient convention.** For a weak gradient $Dv=(D_1v,\ldots,D_nv)$ we write $|Dv|=(\sum_{i=1}^n|D_iv|^2)^{1/2}$ for its Euclidean norm and $\|Dv\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}:=\||Dv|\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}$ for the essential supremum of that norm, as in [[lem-pointwise-potential-bound-for-compactly-supported-smooth-functions]]. For real scalars this norm equals the derivative operator norm. For complex scalars it is the Frobenius norm of the real-linear map $\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^2$ and can exceed its operator norm: $f(x)=x_1+ix_2$ is $1$-Lipschitz but $|Df|=\sqrt2$. If $\Omega=\varnothing$ both assertions hold vacuously.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; an integer $n\ge1$; an open, bounded, convex set $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$; a field $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$; for the first assertion a class $u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ and the finite number $L:=\|Du\|_{L^\infty(\Omega)}$; for the second assertion a function $f:\Omega\to\mathbb K$ that is Lipschitz with constant $L$.

[F1] $W^{k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ consists of the $L^p$ classes whose weak derivatives of order at most $k$ exist as $L^p$ classes; membership of $u$ in $W^{1,\infty}$ means that $u$ and all $D_iu$ lie in $L^\infty$ ([[def-sobolev-space-wkp-and-its-norm]]); an element of $L^p$ is an almost-everywhere class of measurable functions ([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]]).

[F2] $D_iu$ is the weak $\partial_i$-derivative of $u$ exactly when $\int_\Omega u\,\partial_i\varphi=-\int_\Omega D_iu\,\varphi$ for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega)$, and weak derivatives are linear in the class ([[def-weak-derivative-of-a-locally-integrable-function]], [[lem-weak-derivative-linearity-locality-and-commutation]]).

[F3] The Axiom of Choice gives choice functions for arbitrary families of nonempty sets and implies Countable Choice and the prescribed-start form of Dependent Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]], [[lem-ac-supplies-countable-and-dependent-choice-for-banach-integration]]).

[F4] Every Euclidean ball has positive finite Lebesgue measure, measures are monotone under inclusion, and a bounded subset of $\mathbb R^n$ has finite measure; in particular a nonempty open subset of $\mathbb R^n$ contains a ball ([[lem-euclidean-balls-have-positive-finite-lebesgue-measure]], [[prop-measure-monotonicity]], [[prop-lebesgue-measure-is-sigma-finite-and-finite-on-bounded-sets]]).

[F5] On a finite measure space every $L^\infty$ class lies in $L^p$ for every finite $p$, with $\|g\|_p\le\mu(X)^{1/p}\|g\|_\infty$ ([[thm-finite-measure-l-r-includes-into-l-p-for-p-less-r]]).

[F6] Under Countable Choice the interior mollifications $u_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*\widetilde u$ of a representative of $u$ extended by zero are smooth on $\mathbb R^n$ and satisfy $u_\varepsilon\to u$ in $W^{k,p}(U;\mathbb K)$ for every open $U\subset\subset\Omega$, for $1\le p<\infty$ ([[thm-local-smooth-approximation-in-wkp]]).

[F7] $L^p$ is complete and every norm-convergent sequence in $L^p$ has an almost-everywhere convergent subsequence ([[thm-riesz-fischer-completeness-of-l-p]], [[thm-complex-lp-completeness-and-almost-everywhere-subsequences]]).

[F8] The ball average $A_rf(x)=|B(x,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}f$ is defined for $f\in L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb R^n)$, and $A_rf(x)\to f(x)$ as $r\downarrow0$ for almost every $x$ ([[def-ball-average-operator-on-r-n]], [[thm-lebesgue-differentiation-theorem-for-locally-integrable-functions-on-r-n]]).

[F9] On completed sigma-finite products, nonnegative measurable functions may be integrated in either order (Tonelli-Fubini) ([[thm-tonelli-and-fubini-for-completed-product-measures]]).

[F10] For a $C^1$ diffeomorphism $T:U\to V$ and nonnegative Borel $h$ one has $\int_Vh=\int_Uh\circ T\,|\det DT|$ ([[lem-c-one-change-of-variables-for-nonnegative-borel-functions-via-radon-uniqueness]]); an invertible linear map scales Lebesgue measure by $|\det|$ ([[thm-linear-change-of-variables-for-lebesgue-measure]]).

[F11] If $v:[a,b]\to\mathbb R^m$ is differentiable with integrable derivative then $\int_a^bv'=v(b)-v(a)$, and the chain rule computes the derivative of $t\mapsto u_\varepsilon(x+t(y-x))$ ([[cor-vector-valued-ftc-and-lipschitz-bound]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]).

[F12] A Lipschitz function on a compact interval is absolutely continuous, and an absolutely continuous function is differentiable almost everywhere with derivative in $L^1$ and satisfies the fundamental theorem of calculus ([[thm-c1-lipschitz-ac-bv-hierarchy]], [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-absolutely-continuous-functions]]).

[F13] If $g_k\to g$ almost everywhere and $|g_k|\le G$ almost everywhere for a single integrable $G$, then $\int g_k\to\int g$ ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F14] A uniformly continuous map from a dense subset of a metric space into a complete metric space extends uniquely to a continuous map of the whole space ([[thm-uniformly-continuous-extension-from-dense]]).

[F15] Lipschitz with constant $L$ means $|f(x)-f(y)|\le L|x-y|$ for all $x,y$ ([[def-lipschitz-holder-contraction]]).

[F16] Under Countable Choice, Lebesgue measure is the completion of Borel Lebesgue measure, and completion-measurable functions have almost-everywhere equal Borel representatives ([[cor-lebesgue-sigma-algebra-is-the-completion-of-borel-lebesgue-measure]], [[thm-completion-measurable-functions-have-base-measurable-representatives]]); apply this to each real component.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Prepare the $W^{1,1}$ class. If $\Omega=\varnothing$ both assertions hold vacuously; assume $\Omega\ne\varnothing$. Then $0<\lambda(\Omega)<\infty$ by [F4]. Since $u\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$, the class $u$ and every $D_iu$ lie in $L^\infty$ by [F1], and $|Du|\le L$ almost everywhere. By [F5] applied to the finite measure space $\Omega$, also $u\in L^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ and $D_iu\in L^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$. The test-function identities of [F2] that define the weak derivatives $D_iu$ are the same identities with the same test functions, so they remain valid with the $L^1$ in place of the $L^\infty$ classes: $u\in W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$, its $W^{1,1}$ weak gradient is the given $Du$, and $|Du|\le L$ almost everywhere. [F1, F2, F4, F5, given, algebra]

1.2 Choose smooth approximations and a single subsequence. Let $u_\varepsilon=\rho_\varepsilon*\widetilde u$ be the interior mollifications of [F6], where $\widetilde u$ is the zero extension of a representative of $u$; then $u_\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^n;\mathbb K)$ and $u_\varepsilon\to u$ in $W^{1,1}(U;\mathbb K)$ for every open $U\subset\subset\Omega$. Put $U_m:=\{x\in\Omega:\operatorname{dist}(x,\mathbb R^n\setminus\Omega)>1/m\}\cap B(0,m)$ for $m\ge1$; each $U_m$ is open and convex, $U_m\subseteq U_{m+1}\subset\subset\Omega$, and $\bigcup_mU_m=\Omega$. Countable Choice, available from the Axiom of Choice by [F3], is what [F6] uses here. For every $m$ the family $(u_\varepsilon)_{\varepsilon>0}$ converges to $u$ in $W^{1,1}(U_m;\mathbb K)$ as $\varepsilon\to0$, so by [F7] we may extract recursively for $m=1,2,\dots$ a subsequence converging almost everywhere on $U_m$; the resulting diagonal sequence, rewritten as $\varepsilon_k\downarrow0$, satisfies: $u_{\varepsilon_k}\to u$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$, and for every $m$, $u_{\varepsilon_k}\to u$ in $W^{1,1}(U_m;\mathbb K)$ and $Du_{\varepsilon_k}\to Du$ in $L^1(U_m;\mathbb K)$. Each $u_{\varepsilon_k}$ is smooth on all of $\mathbb R^n$. [F3, F6, F7, given, construct]

1.3 A multiplicity estimate. Let $U\subseteq\Omega$ be open convex with $\lambda(U)<\infty$ and let $g:\mathbb R^n\to[0,\infty]$ be Borel measurable. Then $\int_U\int_U\int_0^1g((1-t)x+ty)\,dt\,dy\,dx\le C(n)\lambda(U)\int_{\mathbb R^n}g$, where $C(n):=2\int_{1/2}^1t^{-n}\,dt$ is finite and depends only on $n$. Indeed, fix $t\in[1/2,1]$ and $x\in U$. The map $y\mapsto z=(1-t)x+ty$ is an affine diffeomorphism of $\mathbb R^n$ with linear part $tI$ and $|\det(tI)|=t^n$, so change of variables [F10] gives $\int_Ug((1-t)x+ty)\,dy=t^{-n}\int_{(1-t)x+tU}g(z)\,dz\le t^{-n}\int_{\mathbb R^n}g$; integrating over $x\in U$ and $t\in[1/2,1]$ gives at most $(\int_{1/2}^1t^{-n}\,dt)\lambda(U)\int g$. Symmetrically, for fixed $t\in[0,1/2]$ and $y\in U$ the substitution $x\mapsto z=(1-t)x+ty$ has linear part $(1-t)I$, so $\int_Ug((1-t)x+ty)\,dx\le(1-t)^{-n}\int g$; integrating over $y\in U$ and $t\in[0,1/2]$ and substituting $t\mapsto1-s$ gives the same bound. Tonelli's theorem [F9] justifies all iterated integrals, and adding the two halves gives the estimate. [F9, F10, algebra]

1.4 The converse: $f$ is bounded and its a.e. partial derivatives are bounded functions. Assume $\Omega\ne\varnothing$ and fix $x_0\in\Omega$. For every $x\in\Omega$ the Lipschitz condition [F15] gives $|f(x)|\le|f(x_0)|+L|x-x_0|\le|f(x_0)|+L\operatorname{diam}(\Omega)<\infty$, so $f$ is bounded, hence $f\in L^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ and $f\in L^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ by [F5]. For $i\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ define $g_i(x)$ to be the limit of $(f(x+e_i/k)-f(x))/(1/k)$ where that limit exists, and $0$ elsewhere, the quotient being declared $0$ when $x+e_i/k\notin\Omega$. Each quotient is continuous on its open domain and has modulus at most $L$ there, so $g_i$ is measurable with $|g_i|\le L$ everywhere (use componentwise convergence for complex values) on $\Omega$. Moreover $g_i=\partial_if$ almost everywhere: for each fixed value of the other $n-1$ coordinates the section $t\mapsto f(\dots,t,\dots)$ is $L$-Lipschitz on an interval, hence absolutely continuous and differentiable at almost every $t$ by [F12], and by Fubini [F9] the set of $x\in\Omega$ at which the classical partial derivative fails to exist has measure zero. [F5, F9, F12, F15, given, construct]

2.1 Segment identities and the integrated error. Each $u_{\varepsilon_k}$ is smooth on $\mathbb R^n$, so for all $x,y\in\mathbb R^n$ the chain rule and the fundamental theorem of calculus [F11] give $u_{\varepsilon_k}(y)-u_{\varepsilon_k}(x)=\int_0^1Du_{\varepsilon_k}(x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,dt$. Choose finite-valued Borel representatives of the components of $Du$ by [F16]. Fix $m$ and apply step 1.3 with $U=U_m$ and $g=\mathbf1_{U_m}|Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du|$, extended by zero: by convexity $x+t(y-x)\in U_m$ whenever $x,y\in U_m$, so $\int_{U_m}\int_{U_m}\int_0^1|Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du|(x+t(y-x))\,dt\,dy\,dx\le C(n)\lambda(U_m)\|Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du\|_{L^1(U_m;\mathbb K)}$, and the right-hand side tends to $0$ as $k\to\infty$ by step 1.2. Passing to a further subsequence, once more chosen diagonally over $m$ and relabelled, we may suppose that for every $m$ and almost every $(x,y)\in U_m\times U_m$ the integral $\int_0^1|Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du|(x+t(y-x))\,dt$ tends to $0$. [F7, F11, F16, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 The converse: the functions $g_i$ are the weak derivatives and $f\in W^{1,\infty}$. Let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb K)$. Fix $i$ and write $h=1/k$. Since $\varphi$ is smooth with compact support, the difference quotients $(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-he_i))/h$ converge uniformly to $\partial_i\varphi$ as $h\to0$, and $f\in L^1(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ by step 1.4, so $\int_\Omega f(x)(\varphi(x)-\varphi(x-he_i))/h\,dx\to\int_\Omega f\,\partial_i\varphi$. Substituting $x=y+he_i$ and using the translation case of the change-of-variables identity [F10] (determinant one) turns that integral into $\int_\Omega(f(x)-f(x+he_i))/h\,\varphi(x)\,dx$. On the compact set $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset\subset\Omega$ and for $k$ large both $x$ and $x+e_i/k$ lie in $\Omega$, so there the quotient $q_k(x):=(f(x+e_i/k)-f(x))/(1/k)$ is a Lipschitz difference quotient with $|q_k|\le L$, and $q_k\to\partial_if$ almost everywhere by step 1.4; dominated convergence [F13] with dominating function $L|\varphi|$ on the finite measure set $\operatorname{supp}\varphi$ therefore gives $\int_\Omega f\,\partial_i\varphi=-\int_\Omega g_i\varphi$. By the defining identity [F2], $g_i=D_if$ for every $i$, and with $f\in L^\infty$ from step 1.4 and $g_i\in L^\infty$ this shows $f\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb K)$. [F2, F10, F13, step 1.4, given, algebra]

3.1 The almost-everywhere pair bound. Fix $m$. By Fubini [F9], for almost every $(x,y)\in U_m\times U_m$ both $x$ and $y$ are points of almost-everywhere convergence of $u_{\varepsilon_k}$ toward $u$ and at the same time the integral convergence of step 2.1 holds. For such a pair define $u^{*}(x):=\lim_ku_{\varepsilon_k}(x)$ and $u^{*}(y)$ similarly, and extend $u^{*}$ arbitrarily elsewhere. Passing to the limit in the identity of step 2.1, the left-hand side tends to $u^{*}(y)-u^{*}(x)$, while the difference of the right-hand sides obeys $\bigl|\int_0^1[Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du](x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,dt\bigr|\le|y-x|\int_0^1|Du_{\varepsilon_k}-Du|(x+t(y-x))\,dt\to0$; hence $u^{*}(y)-u^{*}(x)=\int_0^1Du(x+t(y-x))\cdot(y-x)\,dt$, where $|Du|\le L$ for almost every parameter on almost every pair segment: apply step 1.3 to the Borel null set where the chosen gradient exceeds $L$, whose indicator has zero integral. Thus the integral is finite for these pairs. Therefore $|u^{*}(y)-u^{*}(x)|\le L|y-x|$. Since $\Omega\times\Omega=\bigcup_m(U_m\times U_m)$ and a countable union of null sets is null, there is one measurable representative $u^{*}$ of $u$ (the almost-everywhere limit of the subsequence of step 1.2) and one null set $N\subseteq\Omega\times\Omega$ such that $|u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(y)|\le L|x-y|$ whenever $(x,y)\notin N$. [F9, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, given, algebra]

3.2 The converse: the derivative operator norm bound. Let $v$ be a unit vector. Choose $j$ with $v_j\ne0$ and define the invertible linear map $T$ by $Te_j=v$ and $Te_i=e_i$ for $i\ne j$; then $|\det T|=|v_j|>0$. The function $g:=f\circ T$ is Lipschitz on the open set $T^{-1}\Omega$, and for each fixed value of the other coordinates its sections $t\mapsto g(\dots,t,\dots)$ are Lipschitz on intervals, hence differentiable at almost every $t$ by [F12]; Fubini [F9] shows that the set of $w\in T^{-1}\Omega$ at which the $e_j$-directional derivative of $g$ fails to exist is null, and therefore, by the linear change-of-variables identity [F10], the set of $x\in\Omega$ at which the $v$-directional derivative of $f$ fails to exist is null. Define $q_k(x):=(f(x+v/k)-f(x))/(1/k)$ when $x\in\Omega$ and $x+v/k\in\Omega$, and $q_k(x):=0$ otherwise; then $q_k$ is measurable, $|q_k|\le L$ wherever the quotient is defined, and $q_k\to\partial_vf$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$. For $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\Omega;\mathbb K)$ the same difference-quotient computation as in step 2.2, now in the direction $v$, gives $\int_\Omega f\,\partial_v\varphi=-\lim_k\int_\Omega q_k\varphi=-\int_\Omega h_v\varphi$, where $h_v$ is the limit of $q_k$ where it exists, and $0$ elsewhere satisfies $|h_v|\le L$ everywhere and equals $\partial_vf$ almost everywhere. Hence $h_v$ is a representative of the weak directional derivative $D_vf:=\sum_iv_iD_if$ of [F2], and $\bigl|\sum_iv_iD_if\bigr|\le L$ almost everywhere for every unit vector $v$. Now let $V$ be a countable dense subset of the unit sphere. Choosing representatives of $D_1f,\dots,D_nf$, for each $v\in V$ the identity $\sum_iv_iD_if=h_v$ holds almost everywhere, so on a set of full measure all the countably many inequalities $\bigl|\sum_iv_iD_if\bigr|\le L$ hold simultaneously. At every such $x$ one has $\|Df(x)\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{|w|=1}\bigl|\sum_iw_iD_if(x)\bigr|=\sup_{v\in V}\bigl|\sum_iv_iD_if(x)\bigr|\le L$, because $w\mapsto\sum_iw_iD_if(x)$ is continuous and $V$ is dense in the unit sphere. For real $f$ this operator norm is $|Df|$. For $f=a+ib$, each row gradient satisfies $|Da|,|Db|\le L$, so $|Df|^2=|Da|^2+|Db|^2\le2L^2$. This gives the asserted real and complex Euclidean bounds and completes the converse. [F2, F9, F10, F12, F13, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 Lebesgue points and the doubling comparison. Let $A_rf$ be the ball average of [F8], applied to the zero extension of a representative of $u$; by [F8] the set $E$ of $x\in\Omega$ with $A_ru(x)\to u(x)$ as $r\downarrow0$ has full measure in $\Omega$, and on $E$ the limit equals $u^{*}(x)$ because $u^{*}=u$ almost everywhere. Replace $E$ by its intersection with the full-measure set where $u^*=u$. For $x,y\in E$ and $0<r<\min(\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial\Omega),\operatorname{dist}(y,\partial\Omega))$ one has $A_ru(x)-A_ru(y)=|B(x,r)|^{-1}|B(y,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}\int_{B(y,r)}(u^{*}(z)-u^{*}(z'))\,dz'\,dz$, so using that the exceptional set $N$ of step 3.1 is null, $|A_ru(x)-A_ru(y)|\le L|B(x,r)|^{-1}|B(y,r)|^{-1}\int_{B(x,r)}\int_{B(y,r)}|z-z'|\,dz'\,dz\le L(|x-y|+2r)$, because $|z-z'|\le|z-x|+|x-y|+|y-z'|\le2r+|x-y|$ on the two balls. Letting $r\downarrow0$ yields $|u^{*}(x)-u^{*}(y)|\le L|x-y|$ for all $x,y\in E$. [F8, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Extending to a Lipschitz representative and completing the first assertion. The full-measure set $E$ is dense in $\Omega$: otherwise $E$ would be disjoint from some ball $B\subseteq\Omega$, and $\lambda(B)>0$ by [F4], contradicting $\lambda(\Omega\setminus E)=0$. On $E$ the restriction of $u^{*}$ is $L$-Lipschitz, hence uniformly continuous. By [F14] applied with $X:=\overline\Omega$, the dense subset $E$ and the complete target $\mathbb K$, there is a continuous $g:\overline\Omega\to\mathbb K$ with $g|_E=u^{*}|_E$. For arbitrary $x,y\in\overline\Omega$ choose $x_j,y_j\in E$ with $x_j\to x$ and $y_j\to y$; by continuity $g(x)=\lim_jg(x_j)$ and $g(y)=\lim_jg(y_j)$, so step 4.1 gives $|g(x)-g(y)|=\lim_j|u^{*}(x_j)-u^{*}(y_j)|\le L\lim_j|x_j-y_j|=L|x-y|$. Thus $g|_\Omega$ is an $L$-Lipschitz representative of $u$ on $\Omega$ that equals $u$ almost everywhere, and the first assertion of the theorem is proved. [F4, F14, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
## Source notes

Kinnunen proves Theorem 3.31 in $\mathbb R^n$ by reducing to $W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for $p>n$, importing the $p>n$ Sobolev embedding and finishing by mollification with uniform convergence. That route is not available at this position in the reading order, where the $p>n$ theory is still to come, so the proof above uses only the density of smooth functions, Fubini, the one-dimensional theory of absolutely continuous functions and Lebesgue's differentiation theorem. The key substitute for the $p>n$ embedding is the multiplicity estimate 1.3, which lets the classical segment identities for smooth approximations pass to the limit for almost every pair of points; the sharp constant is then obtained from ball averages and the dense-subset extension theorem. The converse direction identifies the weak gradient of a Lipschitz function by difference quotients rather than by Rademacher's theorem; the $v$-directional derivative is transferred to a coordinate direction by an explicit invertible linear map, whose only measure-theoretic input is the linear change-of-variables identity for Lebesgue measure. Hunter's notes and Teschl's chapter cover the Lipschitz/absolute-continuity interface used in the converse; no result of those sources is used beyond that interface.
