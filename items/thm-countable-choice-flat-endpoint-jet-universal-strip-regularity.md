## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix compact transverse strip data with finite generator set $K=L_0\cap L_1$, and a smooth background path $J_t^b$ constant on endpoint collars, with fixed totally real boundary structures and exact all-$t$ charts taking the two boundaries to $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$ and $J_t^b$ to $i$. Choose pairwise disjoint compact neighborhoods $A_x$ of the generators inside those charts and a compact set $D$ disjoint from their union. Choose smaller working generator charts with closures inside $\operatorname{Int}(A_x)$. All references to preserved exact generator charts below mean these smaller working charts; the original larger background charts remain available to analyze a strip whose entire image lies in $A_x$. Put $P_X=D\cup\bigcup_xA_x$, and require all desired spatially protected values on $P_X$. For an optional tameness requirement, assume the closure of $X\setminus P_X$ is a compact subset of an open region carrying the specified smooth two-form and that $J^b$ tames that form there.

There is an actual separable Hilbert ball of smooth paths $J_t(a)$ preserving the protected spatial values, generator charts, boundary structures and optional tameness, such that every derivative of $J(a)-J^b$ at $t=0,1$ vanishes. Finite parameter tangent combinations are dense in every finite coefficient norm in the smooth anti-$J(a)$ fields compactly supported in $(0,1)\times(X\setminus P_X)$. The actual universal strip section is a submersion at every nonconstant finite-energy strip, including those with the same end generator. A dense residual set of parameters makes all these actual strip linearizations surjective in $H^1\to L^2$ and in every integer higher Hilbert Sobolev realization simultaneously.

These auxiliary paths are not asserted to be constant on any endpoint collar. Every one is, however, a smooth coefficient limit of paths $J^{(\epsilon)}$ which are constant on genuine endpoint collars, have the same protected spatial and boundary values and exact generator charts, and retain tameness. No regularity of those approximating paths is asserted here. Transferring regularity to them requires a separate compactness and operator argument; a limit of shrinking collars is not treated as an admissible fixed-collar path.

## Proof

1.1 Put $O=(0,1)\times(X\setminus P_X)$. Enumerate smooth anti-$J^b$ fields $B_n$ compactly supported in rational coordinate balls relatively compact in $O$ by the actual construction in [F1]: matrix units times rational-center smooth bumps, projected by $T\mapsto(T+J^bTJ^b)/2$. Their finite span is dense in each finite $C^k$ norm on compactly supported such fields. Indeed partition a given compact support into finitely many such charts, convolve its smooth coefficient functions with rational-radius bumps, and approximate each convolution and its first $k$ derivatives by finite rational-center Riemann sums. Uniform continuity on that compact support gives convergence of every prescribed derivative; projecting fixes the original anti-$J^b$ field. At any point of $O$, the projected matrix bumps span the anti-complex-linear fiber. Define $$c_n=\frac{2^{-n}}{1+\max_{j\le n}\|B_n\|_{C^j}},\qquad B(a)=\sum_na_nc_nB_n,\qquad a\in\ell^2.$$ For each $k$, Cauchy–Schwarz gives uniform convergence of all derivatives through order $k$, since $\sum_nc_n^2\|B_n\|_{C^k}^2<\infty$. The coordinate fundamental theorem identifies their limits as the derivatives of $B(a)$. Every term and each of its derivatives is zero at the endpoints, and uniform convergence therefore gives every endpoint derivative of $B(a)$ equal to zero. This argument proves flat endpoint jets even though there need be no common zero collar. It also proves a bounded linear map $a\mapsto B(a)$ into every $C^k$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 On a sufficiently small real Hilbert ball put $$J(a)=J^b(I+B(a))(I-B(a))^{-1}.$$ Anti-commutation gives $J(a)^2=-I$. The uniformly convergent matrix inverse series and its differentiated identity give smooth compatible maps $a\mapsto J(a)$ into every $C^k$, with all mixed derivatives bounded on smaller balls. The protected values and the smaller working generator charts are unchanged because every $B_n$ vanishes on $P_X$, which contains their closures. Variations may change the background structure on parts of the original larger charts outside $A_x$; no preservation of those parts is claimed. All endpoint derivatives of $J(a)-J^b$ vanish by the product and inverse derivative identities and step 1.1. Optional tameness follows from the positive uniform minimum of the background taming form on unit vectors over the stipulated compact free spatial closure, by shrinking the ball in $C^0$. At a fixed parameter, the tangent formula $$dJ[C]=2J^b(I-B)^{-1}C(I-B)^{-1}$$ is a smooth fiber isomorphism on anti-complex-linear fields, with inverse $C=-\tfrac12(I-B)J^bY(I-B)$, as checked by multiplication in [F1]. It preserves supports and is bounded in each finite $C^k$ norm. Thus the density and pointwise spanning in step 1.1 transfer to the actual anti-$J(a)$ tangent fields. In particular for $v\ne0$ any prescribed $w$ is $Yv$ for an anti-$J(a)$ endomorphism: set $Yv=w$, $YJ(a)v=-J(a)w$ on their real plane and zero on its invariant-metric complex orthogonal complement. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Every nonconstant actual strip has a source point in $O$ after evaluation by $(s,t)\mapsto(t,u(s,t))$. Otherwise its entire interior image lies in $P_X$, and uniform convergence extends this connected image to include its end generators. A compact $A_x$ is open and closed relative to $P_X$, since the finitely many compact pieces are separated by positive distances. The connected extended image must therefore lie in the one $A_x$ containing its negative end, and the positive end must be that same generator. In that exact product chart the whole coordinate map $v$ solves $D_0v=0$ with the two fixed transverse boundary planes. It has finite energy and tends to zero at both ends. The all-order exponential ends in [F3] give $v\in H^1$; the actual transverse-strip inverse there has zero kernel, so $v=0$, contradicting nonconstancy. Consequently some evaluated point is free. Its inverse image is open and nonempty in the interior. This argument did not prescribe a uniform distance of that point from the time endpoints. [F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The actual configuration and range bundles in [F2] are independent of the parameter: the boundary structures and exact generator charts are unchanged. Their local section formula with $J(a)$ is jointly smooth by the already proved $H^2$ algebra and $H^3$ coefficient-composition estimates there, since step 2.1 gives bounded parameter coefficient derivatives of every finite order. On longitudinal tails the parameter coefficients vanish near the generators, so no nonintegrable offset occurs. At a zero the actual derivative is $P\xi+(d_aJ[b])_t(u)u_t$. If it were not onto, [F2] would give a nonzero $L^2$ annihilator $\eta$, smooth in the interior and solving the actual adjoint equation. By [F3] its interior zeros are discrete. The free source open set in step 3.1 therefore contains a point $p=(s_0,t_0)$ with $\eta(p)\ne0$, $u_s(p)\ne0$ and no same-time partner, by the dense time-slice theorem. The equation gives $u_t(p)\ne0$. The map $F(s,t)=(t,u(s,t))$ is a local embedding there by choosing a target coordinate with nonzero $s$ derivative. Moreover a sufficiently small target-time neighborhood $W\Subset O$ of $F(p)$ has its whole preimage in that embedding patch. Otherwise a sequence of outside preimages approaching $F(p)$ has bounded longitudinal coordinates, since uniform ends tend to generators whereas $u(p)$ is free; a subsequence converges to another same-time preimage, or to $p$ outside its chosen open patch, both contradictions. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Prescribe an anti-$J(a)$ field $Y$ at $F(p)$ by $Y u_t(p)=\eta(p)$, using step 2.1. Extend it smoothly in a local frame and project to the anti-$J(a)$ fields. Its pairing $\langle\eta,Yu_t\rangle$ is positive near $p$. Shrink the source embedding patch to that positive set and then $W$ as in step 4.1; multiply $Y$ by a nonnegative bump supported in $W$ and positive at $F(p)$. All its source preimages lie in the positive patch. Thus $$I=\int\langle\eta,Y_t(u)u_t\rangle>0.$$ It is finite by the $L^2$ bound on $\eta$ and finite strip energy. Step 2.1 approximates $Y$ in $C^0$ by actual finite parameter tangent combinations $d_aJ[b_n]$. Their integrals differ from $I$ by at most $\|\eta\|_2\|u_t\|_2\|d_aJ[b_n]-Y\|_{C^0}$, tending to zero. This contradicts annihilation of every parameter derivative. Hence the actual universal derivative is onto in $H^1\to L^2$, and the exact bridge in [F2] makes it onto in $H^3\to H^2$. [F2, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 We supply the actual Hilbert projection to which Sard–Smale applies. The strip operator $P:E\to Y$ is Fredholm by [F2]. Let $q:Y\to C=Y/\operatorname{Ran}P$ be its finite-dimensional quotient, and let $K$ be the parameter derivative. Step 5.1 says $qK$ is onto. Lift a basis of $C$ to finitely many parameter vectors, giving a bounded right inverse $j:C\to H$; finite choice suffices. The actual closed-range inverse $Q$ on the $H^1$ orthogonal kernel complement restricts boundedly to the higher forcing/solution spaces by [F2]. Then $f\mapsto(jqf,Q(f-Kjqf))$ is a specified right inverse of the universal derivative. Apply [F4] to obtain its actual smooth split zero chart. Restrict to nonconstant solutions, an open subset since a nonzero first derivative at one point persists in the $H^3$ chart topology. Its parameter projection has kernel $\ker P$ and image $\ker(qK)$, hence is Fredholm with index $\operatorname{ind}P$. Its derivative is onto exactly when $C=0$, that is, exactly when $P$ is onto. The zero manifold is second-countable as a subspace of the actual second-countable configuration-parameter product. Local constancy of its projection index follows by splitting the finite kernel and cokernel, eliminating the invertible infinite range block by its Neumann series, and observing that the remaining finite block has index equal to the fixed difference of its domain and target dimensions. Thus its index-$d$ subsets are open second-countable Hilbert manifolds for each integer $d$. Apply [F4] to each such fixed-index projection and each of the finitely many ordered generator pairs, including equal pairs. Countable Baire intersection gives a dense residual set where every nonconstant actual strip operator is onto. The higher range agreement in [F2] gives the same assertion at every integer Sobolev order. [F2, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally fix one parameter and construct the claimed constant-collar approximations. Choose smooth scalar cutoffs $\chi_\epsilon(t)$ which vanish for $t\le\epsilon$ and $t\ge1-\epsilon$, equal one on $[2\epsilon,1-2\epsilon]$, and have derivatives of order $j$ bounded by $C_j\epsilon^{-j}$. Replace $B(a)$ by $B_\epsilon=\chi_\epsilon B(a)$ in the Cayley formula of step 2.1. Every resulting path has the original protected values, exact generator charts, and genuine background constant collars once $\epsilon$ is smaller than their widths. It retains tameness by the same uniform small-ball estimate since $|B_\epsilon|\le|B|$. Taylor's one-variable integral remainder and the vanishing of all endpoint jets show, for every $k,N$, that $\|B(a)\|_{C^k(\{t<2\epsilon\}\cup\{t>1-2\epsilon\})}\le C_{k,N}\epsilon^N$. Leibniz and the cutoff derivative bounds therefore give $B_\epsilon\to B(a)$ in every $C^k$; smooth inversion gives $J^{(\epsilon)}\to J(a)$ in every $C^k$. These approximants have not been proved regular. The conclusion of step 6.1 concerns the explicitly auxiliary flat-jet family, and is not substituted for the required constant-collar existence statement. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
