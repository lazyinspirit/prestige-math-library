## Statement

Assume AC. Let $L/F$ be a finite Galois extension of number fields with group $H$, let $p$ be a prime not dividing $|H|$, and let $T$ be a finite $H$-stable set of finite places of $L$. Put $U_T=\mathcal O_{L,T}^{\times}$ and let $\mu(L)$ be the roots of unity in $L$. In the Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional $\mathbb F_p[H]$-modules, with $S_{\infty}(L)$ the set of archimedean places of $L$, one has
$$[(U_T/\mu(L))\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb F_p]=[\mathbb F_p[S_{\infty}(L)]]+[\mathbb F_p[T]]-[\mathbb F_p].$$
The permutation actions are the actual actions on places. No Poitou–Tate or Euler-characteristic statement is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite Galois extension, prime-to-$p$ group order, stable finite set of places and module conventions in the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The S-unit group is defined by vanishing valuations outside $T$. Its quotient by roots of unity is a free abelian group of rank $|S_{\infty}(L)|-1+|T|$, and the weighted logarithms of the ordinary units form a full lattice in the archimedean sum-zero hyperplane. [[def-s-integers-and-s-units-of-a-number-field]] [[thm-number-field-s-unit-structure-in-zf]]

[F2] The ideal class group of a number field is finite. [[thm-number-field-ideal-class-group-finite-in-zf]]

[F3] On the $p$-regular elements, Brauer characters are additive and the irreducible split Brauer characters are linearly independent. [[thm-brauer-character-is-additive-on-short-exact-sequences]] [[thm-irreducible-brauer-characters-are-linearly-independent-on-p-regular-elements]]

[F4] Finite-dimensional semisimple algebras are products of matrix algebras over division rings, and finite division rings are fields. [[thm-wedderburn-artin-theorem]] [[lem-finite-division-rings-are-fields]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** compare the rational logarithmic and valuation representations, then reduce their finite-order character values modulo $p$.

1.1 The valuation homomorphism $U_T\to\mathbb Z[T]$ is $H$-equivariant, with kernel $\mathcal O_L^\times$ by [F1]. If $h$ annihilates the finite ideal class group in [F2], every prime in $T$ has its $h$th power principal, generated by a $T$-unit. Hence the valuation image has finite index in $\mathbb Z[T]$. Tensoring with $\mathbb Q$ gives an exact sequence of $\mathbb Q[H]$-modules from ordinary units modulo torsion to $U_T/\mu(L)$ and then to $\mathbb Q[T]$. [F1, F2, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 By [F1], the weighted logarithmic map sends ordinary units modulo roots of unity injectively to a full lattice in the sum-zero hyperplane of $\mathbb R[S_{\infty}(L)]$. Galois conjugation permutes the place coordinates, with the same real or complex weight on each orbit, so the map is $H$-equivariant. Its real span is the full hyperplane; the quotient by that hyperplane is the trivial one-dimensional representation via summation. Together with step 1.1, this yields equality of ordinary character values $\chi_{U_T/\mu}+1=\chi_{\mathbb Q[S_\infty]}+\chi_{\mathbb Q[T]}$ on every element of $H$. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a finite splitting $p$-modular system for $H$ and its subgroups under [A1]. Every $h\in H$ has order prime to $p$, so its eigenvalues on the free integral lattice $U_T/\mu(L)$ are roots of unity of order prime to $p$. Their reductions are distinct roots of the same orders and their Teichmüller lifts recover the original eigenvalues after the fixed cyclotomic embedding. Consequently the Brauer character of the reduced lattice at $h$ is its ordinary character in step 2.1. The same is true for the integral permutation and trivial lattices. Thus the two sides of the displayed Statement have equal Brauer characters on all elements of $H$. [A1, F1, step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra]

4.1 By [F3] the equality of split Brauer characters is equality in the split Grothendieck group. Scalar extension from $\mathbb F_p$ to the finite splitting field is injective on Grothendieck groups: by [F4] the semisimple quotient of $\mathbb F_p[H]$ is a product of matrix algebras over finite fields, whose distinct factors split into disjoint collections of simple modules after extending scalars. Therefore the equality descends to $\mathbb F_p$. Empty $T$ and the trivial group are included. [F3, F4, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
