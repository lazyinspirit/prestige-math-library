## Statement

Let $L/K$ be a finite separable extension of number fields of degree $d$. If $\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb R$ is a real embedding, let $r$ be the number of real embeddings $\tau:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb R$ extending $\sigma$, and let $s$ be the number of conjugate pairs of nonreal embeddings $\tau:L\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ extending $\sigma$. With $\mathbb R$ and $\mathbb C$ carrying their usual topologies, the natural map gives a topological $\mathbb R$-algebra isomorphism
$$
L\otimes_{K,\sigma}\mathbb R\cong\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb C^s,\qquad r+2s=d.
$$
These factors are the archimedean completions of $L$ above the real place represented by $\sigma$. If $\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ is a complex embedding, then
$$
L\otimes_{K,\sigma}\mathbb C\cong\mathbb C^d
$$
as topological $\mathbb C$-algebras, with factors indexed by the embeddings of $L$ extending the chosen $\sigma$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite separable extension $L/K$ of number fields, its degree $d$, and a real or complex embedding $\sigma$ of $K$.

[F1] A number field is a finite extension of $\mathbb Q$; the real and complex embeddings and their conjugate-pair convention are as stated in the signature definition. ([[def-number-field]], [[def-archimedean-embeddings-and-number-field-signature]])

[F2] A finite separable extension is generated by one element. ([[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]])

[F3] Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a complex root, so a degree-$d$ polynomial splits into $d$ linear factors. ([[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]])

[F4] A nonzero real polynomial factors into linear factors and irreducible real quadratics. ([[cor-every-real-polynomial-factors-into-linear-and-irreducible-quadratic-factors]])

[F5] Separability is equivalent to being relatively prime to the derivative; the resulting Bezout identity remains valid after extending scalars. ([[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]])

[F6] Pairwise comaximal polynomial factors yield a product decomposition of the quotient algebra. ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-euclidean]], [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]])

[F7] The usual real and complex fields are complete, and the rational numbers are dense in the reals. ([[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]], [[thm-complex-plane-is-complete]], [[lem-q-and-irrationals-dense-r]])

[F8] The complex numbers are $\mathbb R[X]/(X^2+1)$, with $i^2=-1$ and the usual complex conjugation. ([[def-complex-numbers-and-arithmetic]])

## Proof


**Given:** The extension and embeddings in the Statement.

1.1 Choose a primitive element $\theta$ for $L/K$ and let $f\in K[X]$ be its monic minimal polynomial. For a real embedding $\sigma$, scalar extension gives $L\otimes_{K,\sigma}\mathbb R\cong\mathbb R[X]/(\sigma f)$. By [F5], $\sigma f$ has no repeated factor. By [F4], it is a product of distinct linear factors and distinct irreducible quadratics. [F2, F4, F5, given, choose]

2.1 Each irreducible real quadratic factor has a nonreal root $z$; evaluation at $z$ identifies its quotient algebra with $\mathbb C$, since the quotient has real basis $1,X$ and the real-linear map $(a,b)\mapsto a+bz$ has determinant $\operatorname{Im}(z)\ne0$. The Chinese remainder theorem [F6] therefore gives $\mathbb R[X]/(\sigma f)\cong\mathbb R^r\times\mathbb C^s$. Roots are the images of $\theta$ under the embeddings extending $\sigma$, and nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs, so $r+2s=d$. [F3, F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The image of each real embedding $\tau:L\to\mathbb R$ contains $\mathbb Q$, which is dense by [F7]; since $\mathbb R$ is complete, its completion is $\mathbb R$. For a nonreal embedding $\tau:L\to\mathbb C$, choose $a\in L$ with $z=\tau(a)$ nonreal. The real-linear map $(x,y)\mapsto x+yz$ has determinant $\operatorname{Im}(z)\ne0$, so it maps the dense set $\mathbb Q^2$ onto a dense subset $\mathbb Q+\mathbb Qz$ of $\mathbb C$. Since $\mathbb C$ is complete by [F7], its completion is $\mathbb C$. Thus the factors in step 2.1 are exactly the archimedean completions above $\sigma$. [F7, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $\sigma:K\to\mathbb C$ is complex, [F3] splits $\sigma f$ into $d$ distinct linear factors, with distinctness from [F5]. The Chinese remainder theorem gives $\mathbb C[X]/(\sigma f)\cong\mathbb C^d$. Each root defines one embedding $\tau:L\to\mathbb C$ extending the chosen $\sigma$. Its image contains $\sigma(K)$, which contains a nonreal $z$ because $\sigma$ is complex; the determinant calculation of step 3.1 shows $\mathbb Q+\mathbb Qz$ is dense in $\mathbb C$. Completeness in [F7] identifies each factor with the corresponding completion. [F3, F5, F7, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The factor maps in steps 2.1 and 4.1 are evaluation maps, hence continuous; the inverse CRT maps use finitely many fixed idempotents and are continuous. Therefore both isomorphisms are topological as well as algebraic. [F6, step 2.1, step 4.1] ∎
