## Statement

Let $R$ be an associative unital ring and let $R^n$ be the free right $R$-module of column vectors.

1. An **elementary basis change** of $R^n$, that is, one whose change-of-basis matrix is a finite product of elementary matrices $I+rE_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$ and their inverses, changes the class in $K_1(R)$ by $0$.
2. A **reordering** of a finite basis changes the class in $K_1(R)$ by $0$ or by $[-1]$; in particular it changes nothing in $\tilde K_1(R)$ or in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$.
3. For $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$: replacing the chosen oriented lift of one cell of a finite CW complex by another, or reversing its orientation, is a change of basis in which exactly one basis vector is replaced by a unit $\pm g$ with $g\in\pi$; its class in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ is $0$. In the same situation a change of basepoint path conjugates $\pi_1$ and acts trivially on $\mathrm{Wh}$, and two different basepoint paths act the same way.
4. The quotients are genuinely distinct: there is a unit of a group ring that is not killed by the passage from $K_1$ to $\mathrm{Wh}$. Concretely, for $\pi=C_5=\langle t\mid t^5=1\rangle$ and $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$, the element $u=1-t^2-t^3$ is a unit of $R$ and its class $[u]$ is a nonzero element of $\mathrm{Wh}(C_5)$.

The clauses about $K_1$ use no commutativity of $R$; clause 4 uses the determinant of the commutative ring $\mathbb Z[C_5]$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An associative unital ring $R$, and for clauses 3 and 4 a discrete group $\pi$ with integral group ring $\mathbb Z[\pi]$.

[F1] For any unital ring $R$, $E(R)$ is the subgroup of $GL(R)=\bigcup_n GL_n(R)$ generated by the stabilized elementary matrices $e_{ij}(r)=I+rE_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$, and it is normal in $GL(R)$ with $E(R)=[GL(R),GL(R)]$; composition of right-linear maps of free right modules is ordinary matrix multiplication in the displayed order ([[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]], [[lem-the-stable-elementary-subgroup-is-normal-and-contains-the-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F2] $K_1(R)=GL(R)/E(R)$ is written additively with $[AB]=[A]+[B]$, $[I]=0$ and $[A^{-1}]=-[A]$; $\tilde K_1(R)=K_1(R)/\langle[-1]\rangle$, and for a discrete group $\pi$ the Whitehead group is $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)=K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])/\langle[\pm g]:g\in\pi\rangle=\tilde K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])/\langle[g]:g\in\pi\rangle$, where $[\pm g]$ is the class of the $1\times1$ unit matrix $\pm g$. A ring homomorphism induces maps on $K_1$ and $\tilde K_1$, a group homomorphism induces a map on $\mathrm{Wh}$, and an inner automorphism of $\pi$ induces the identity on $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ because on matrices it acts as conjugation by a scalar matrix $gI_n$ ([[def-k-one-of-a-ring-and-the-whitehead-group-of-a-discrete-group]]).

[F3] If the new degree-$n$ basis of a bounded contractible based free right $R$-complex is the old basis right-multiplied by $P_n$ in column coordinates, then $\tau_{\text{new}}(C)=\tau_{\text{old}}(C)+\sum_n(-1)^{n+1}[P_n]$ in $\tilde K_1(R)$ ([[lem-basis-change-and-direct-sum-formulas-for-chain-torsion]]).

[F4] In the based cellular chains of a universal cover, choosing one oriented lift of every cell makes each $C_n$ a finite free right $\mathbb Z[\pi]$-module on those lifts, and the lifts of a single cell are exactly the cells $T_g\widetilde e$ for one chosen lift $\widetilde e$, with $T_g$ the deck transformation attached to $g$; in the right action $c\cdot g=T_g^{-1}c$ the basis vector is therefore replaced by a group-ring unit ([[def-based-cellular-chain-complex-of-a-universal-cover]]).

[F5] Over a commutative unital ring $\det(AB)=\det(A)\det(B)$, $\det(I_n)=1$, adding a multiple of one row to a distinct row leaves the determinant unchanged, and an invertible matrix has unit determinant ([[thm-determinant-multiplicative]], [[thm-determinant-under-elementary-row-operations]], [[cor-invertible-matrix-has-unit-determinant]], [[thm-leibniz-determinant-is-alternating-multilinear-and-normalized]]).

[F6] Determinant on $M_n(R)$ over a commutative ring is the unique normalized alternating column-multilinear function, and it is also alternating and multilinear in the rows ([[thm-determinant-is-the-unique-normalized-alternating-multilinear-function]], [[cor-determinant-is-alternating-multilinear-in-the-rows]]).

[F7] For a group $\pi$ the group ring $\mathbb Z[\pi]$ is a unital ring which as a $\mathbb Z$-module is free with basis the elements $[g]$, with $[g][h]=[gh]$ and $[g]$ invertible with inverse $[g^{-1}]$ ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]).

[F8] Paths concatenate in traversal order and have reversals ([[def-path-connected]]). Based loops modulo homotopy rel endpoints form the fundamental group, with concatenation as product ([[def-based-loops-and-fundamental-group]], [[thm-fundamental-group-laws]], [[def-homotopy-relative-and-path-homotopy]]). Continuous maps defined compatibly on a finite closed cover paste to a continuous map ([[lem-continuity-is-local-and-pastes]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $n\ge1$ and let $P\in GL_n(R)$ be a product of elementary matrices $e_{ij}(r)$ and their inverses. By [F1] each factor lies in $E(R)$, so $P\in E(R)$; as $K_1(R)=GL(R)/E(R)$, its class satisfies $[P]=0$, and by additivity of the class in [F2] every finite product of elementary matrices and inverses has class $0$ as well. [F1, F2]

1.2 In $M_2(R)$ put $S=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ and $U=e_{12}(1)e_{21}(-1)e_{12}(1)$. Multiplying the three matrices gives $U=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$, hence $S=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)\,U$ with $U\in E(R)$; since $\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)$ is the stabilization of the $1\times1$ matrix $-1$, [F2] gives $[S]=[\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)]+[U]=[-1]$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 Let $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$ and let a based cellular complex over the universal cover be given as in [F4]. Replacing the chosen lift $\widetilde e$ of a cell $e$ by the lift $T_h\widetilde e$ replaces one basis vector by $T_h\widetilde e=\widetilde e\cdot h^{-1}$, and reversing the orientation replaces one basis vector by its negative; the change-of-basis matrix is therefore diagonal with one entry $h^{-1}$ or $-1$ and all other entries $1$. Its class in $K_1(R)$ is $[h^{-1}]$ or $[-1]$, both of which are $0$ in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ by the definition of $\mathrm{Wh}$ in [F2]. [F2, F3, F4]

1.4 For a path $\gamma:x\to x'$, define $\varphi_\gamma([\alpha])=[\bar\gamma*\alpha*\gamma]$ on loops at $x$. Concatenating a homotopy rel endpoints with the fixed outer paths gives a homotopy rel endpoints, so the map is well defined and preserves products after inserting the cancellable middle path $\gamma*\bar\gamma$. Explicitly, for any path $\lambda$, the two formulas $H(s,t)=\lambda(2s(1-t))$ for $s\le\tfrac12$ and $H(s,t)=\lambda(2(1-s)(1-t))$ for $s\ge\tfrac12$ agree at $s=\tfrac12$; by pasting they contract $\lambda*\bar\lambda$ rel endpoints. Reparametrising by $\lambda((1-t)r(s)+ts)$ likewise identifies bracketings and deletes constant paths. Thus $\varphi_{\bar\gamma}$ is inverse to $\varphi_\gamma$. For a second path $\gamma':x\to x'$, the composite $\varphi_{\gamma'}^{-1}\circ\varphi_\gamma$ carries $[\alpha]$ to $[\gamma'*\bar\gamma*\alpha*\gamma*\bar\gamma']$, conjugation by the loop $\gamma'*\bar\gamma$ at $x$. By [F2] inner automorphisms act trivially on $\mathrm{Wh}$, so the two paths induce the same Whitehead-group map. [F2, F8]

1.5 Let $\pi=C_5=\{1,t,t^2,t^3,t^4\}$ and $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$. In $R$ distribute and use $[t^a][t^b]=[t^{a+b}]$ with $t^5=1$: $$(1-t^2-t^3)(1-t-t^4)=1-t-t^4-t^2+t^3+t^6-t^3+t^4+t^7=1-t-t^2+t^6+t^7=1,$$ the last step because $t^6=t$ and $t^7=t^2$. Hence $u=1-t^2-t^3$ is a unit of $R$ with inverse $1-t-t^4$. [F7, algebra]

1.6 By [F7] the elements $1,t,t^2,t^3,t^4$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $R$. Every element of the subgroup $\langle\pm t^k\rangle$ is a finite product of monomials $\pm t^k$, hence is itself $\pm t^m$ for some $m$, an element whose coefficient vector in that basis has exactly one nonzero entry; the coefficient vector of $u=1+(-1)t^2+(-1)t^3$ has the three nonzero entries $1,-1,-1$. Therefore $u\notin\langle\pm t^k\rangle$ and $u\ne0$. [F7]

1.7 Let $S$ be a commutative unital ring. Since $\det$ is multiplicative with $\det(I_n)=1$ by [F5] and $\det(e_{ij}(r))=1$ because $e_{ij}(r)$ is obtained from $I_n$ by adding $r$ times the $j$-th row to the $i$-th row, the determinant is unchanged under right multiplication by any product of elementary matrices; moreover $\det(\operatorname{diag}(B,1))=\det(B)$ for $B\in M_n(S)$: the function $B\mapsto\det(\operatorname{diag}(B,1))$ is normalized, column-multilinear and alternating in the columns of $B$, so it equals $\det$ by the uniqueness in [F6]. Hence $\det$ is compatible with stabilization and descends to a well-defined homomorphism $\det:K_1(S)\to S^\times$ with $\det[u]=u$ for every unit $u\in S^\times$. [F5, F6]

2.1 The matrix of a permutation of a finite basis that is a product of $m$ transpositions is a product of $m$ copies of $S$ padded by identity blocks, because permutation matrices for $0/1$ entries multiply by the composition rule of [F1] and the padded matrices are the stabilized transpositions. Hence by [F2] its class is $m[-1]$, which is $[I]=0$ for $m$ even and $[-1]$ for $m$ odd, so every reordering has class $0$ or $[-1]$ in $K_1(R)$, and class $0$ in $\tilde K_1(R)$ and in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ where $[-1]=0$. [F1, F2, step 1.2]

2.2 For $S=R=\mathbb Z[C_5]$ the homomorphism of step 1.7 sends the class $[\pm t^k]$ of the $1\times1$ matrix $\pm t^k$ to $\pm t^k$, so it induces a homomorphism $\mathrm{Wh}(C_5)=K_1(R)/\langle[\pm t^k]\rangle\to R^\times/\langle\pm t^k\rangle$ carrying the class of the unit $u$ of step 1.5 to the coset $u\langle\pm t^k\rangle$. [F2, step 1.5, step 1.7]

3.1 By the basis-change formula of [F3], a change of the displayed bases whose change-of-basis matrices all have class $0$ in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ leaves the torsion class in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$ unchanged; combining with steps 2.1 and 1.3, neither a reordering of the cells, nor a reversal of an orientation, nor a change of the chosen lifts alters the torsion class in $\mathrm{Wh}(\pi)$, and an elementary basis change does not alter the class in $K_1(R)$. [F2, F3, step 2.1, step 1.3]

3.2 Since $u\notin\langle\pm t^k\rangle$ by step 1.6, that coset is not the identity coset, so the image of $[u]$ in $\mathrm{Wh}(C_5)$ is nonzero: the quotient map $K_1(R)\to\mathrm{Wh}(C_5)$ does not kill every unit. [step 1.6, step 2.2]

4.1 Clauses 1 and 2 are steps 1.1 and 2.1, clause 3 is steps 1.3 and 1.4, and clause 4 is steps 1.5, 1.6, 1.7, 2.2 and 3.2; no step assumed commutativity of $R$ except in clauses about the commutative group ring $\mathbb Z[C_5]$, and no step used any choice principle. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 3.2] ∎
