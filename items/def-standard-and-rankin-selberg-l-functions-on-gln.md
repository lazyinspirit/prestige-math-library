## Definition

Let $F$ be a number field. For a finite place $v$, the local Weil group $W_{F_v}$ is the inverse image of the infinite cyclic subgroup generated by Frobenius in the absolute Galois group of $F_v$; its inertia subgroup $I_v$ is the kernel of the quotient map to that cyclic group.

At a finite place $v$, the local Weil group $W_{F_v}$ has quotient generated by a Frobenius element $\mathrm{Fr}_v$; normalize $|w|_v=q_v^{-a}$ when $w$ maps to $\mathrm{Fr}_v^a$, where $q_v$ is the residue-field cardinality. A Weil–Deligne parameter is a pair $(r,N)$ in which $r$ is a finite-dimensional representation of $W_{F_v}$ with finite inertia image and $N$ is nilpotent with $r(w)Nr(w)^{-1}=|w|_vN$. For a local component $\pi_v$ of an adelic automorphic representation, let $(r_{\pi_v},N_{\pi_v})$ be its standard local Langlands parameter. For a finite-dimensional parameter $(r,N)$, define its local factor by

$$L_v(s,(r,N))=\det\!\left(1-q_v^{-s}r(\mathrm{Fr}_v)\mid(\ker N)^{I_v}\right)^{-1},$$

where $(\ker N)^{I_v}$ is the subspace of $\ker N$ fixed by every element of inertia. Set $L_v(s,\pi_v)=L_v(s,(r_{\pi_v},N_{\pi_v}))$. If $\pi_v$ is unramified, then $N_{\pi_v}=0$ and inertia acts trivially; the eigenvalues $\alpha_{1,v},\ldots,\alpha_{n,v}$ of $r_{\pi_v}(\mathrm{Fr}_v)$ are its Satake parameters, and

$$L_v(s,\pi_v)=\prod_{i=1}^n(1-\alpha_{i,v}q_v^{-s})^{-1}.$$

For unitary cuspidal generic representations $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb A_F)$ and $\pi'$ of $\mathrm{GL}_m(\mathbb A_F)$, define the local Rankin–Selberg factor using the tensor parameter $(r_{\pi_v}\otimes r_{\pi'_v},\,N_{\pi_v}\otimes 1+1\otimes N_{\pi'_v})$. At a finite place where both components are unramified with Satake parameters $\alpha_{i,v}$ and $\beta_{j,v}$, this gives

$$L_v(s,\pi_v\times\pi'_v)=\prod_{i=1}^n\prod_{j=1}^m(1-\alpha_{i,v}\beta_{j,v}q_v^{-s})^{-1}.$$

For $\operatorname{Re}(s)>0$, Euler's gamma function is $\Gamma(s)=\int_0^\infty e^{-x}x^{s-1}\,dx$. At an archimedean place $v$, write $\Gamma_v=\Gamma_{\mathbb R}$ if $v$ is real and $\Gamma_v=\Gamma_{\mathbb C}$ if $v$ is complex, where $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)$ and $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}(s)=(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)$. The standard local factor is $L_v(s,\pi_v)=\prod_{i=1}^n\Gamma_v(s+\mu_{i,v})$, with the shifts $\mu_{i,v}$ from the archimedean Langlands parameter; the Rankin–Selberg factor is the local factor of the tensor parameter.

For a pure isobaric sum $\Pi=\boxplus_{j=1}^k\pi_j$, its local standard factor is defined by $L_v(s,\Pi_v)=\prod_{j=1}^kL_v(s,\pi_{j,v})$, consistently with $L(s,\Pi)=\prod_{j=1}^kL(s,\pi_j)$.

For a pure isobaric representation $\Pi=\boxplus_{j=1}^k\pi_j$, define $L(s,\Pi)=\prod_{j=1}^kL(s,\pi_j)$.

$$L(s,\pi)=\prod_{v\text{ finite}}L_v(s,\pi_v),\qquad L(s,\pi\times\pi')=\prod_{v\text{ finite}}L_v(s,\pi_v\times\pi'_v),$$

In any common half-plane where the Euler products converge absolutely, these are the finite standard and Rankin–Selberg L-functions. The completed functions are obtained by multiplying the finite products by all archimedean local factors.

## Remarks

These are finite-part and completed-factor conventions only. This definition asserts no meromorphic continuation, functional equation, nonvanishing, or bound. The global continuation and functional equation for Rankin–Selberg convolutions require separate theorem items.
