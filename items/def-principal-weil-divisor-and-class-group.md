## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]). Let $X$ be a
normal Noetherian integral scheme
([[def-weil-divisor-normal-noetherian-scheme]], [[def-integral-scheme]]).
Because $X$ is integral, $\mathcal K_X$ is the constant sheaf with value the
function field $K(X)$ ([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]]), so the global
meromorphic units of $X$ are exactly the nonzero elements of $K(X)$:
$$\Gamma(X,\mathcal K_X^\times)=K(X)^\times.$$

For $f\in K(X)^\times$ define the **principal Weil divisor**
$$\operatorname{div}_W(f)\;:=\;\sum_{Z}\operatorname{ord}_Z(f)\,[Z]\;\in\;\operatorname{Div}(X),$$
where $Z$ runs over the prime divisors of $X$ and
$\operatorname{ord}_Z(f)\in\mathbb Z$ is the order of $f$ along $Z$
([[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]]). This is a legitimate Weil
divisor: its coefficients are integers, and its support is locally finite by
[[lem-principal-weil-divisor-locally-finite]], a lemma whose only choice input
is Dependent Choice through the finiteness of the minimal primes of a Noetherian
ring; since $X$ is quasi-compact, the support is in fact finite. Here
$\operatorname{Div}(X)$ is the group of Weil divisors of
[[def-weil-divisor-normal-noetherian-scheme]].

The map $\operatorname{div}_W:K(X)^\times\to\operatorname{Div}(X)$ is a group
homomorphism: for $f,g\in K(X)^\times$ and every prime divisor $Z$ the order is
additive, $\operatorname{ord}_Z(fg)=\operatorname{ord}_Z(f)+\operatorname{ord}_Z(g)$
and $\operatorname{ord}_Z(1)=0$
([[def-order-codimension-one-rational-function]]), so the coefficients of
$\operatorname{div}_W(fg)$ and $\operatorname{div}_W(f)+\operatorname{div}_W(g)$
agree at every $Z$; hence
$$\operatorname{div}_W(fg)=\operatorname{div}_W(f)+\operatorname{div}_W(g),$$
and $\operatorname{div}_W(f^{-1})=-\operatorname{div}_W(f)$. In particular
$\operatorname{div}_W(1)=0$, and a global regular unit $u\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X^\times)$
has $\operatorname{ord}_Z(u)=0$ at every prime divisor, so
$\operatorname{div}_W(u)=0$.

The image
$$P(X)\;:=\;\{\operatorname{div}_W(f):f\in K(X)^\times\}\;\subseteq\;\operatorname{Div}(X)$$
is a subgroup, because $\operatorname{div}_W$ is a group homomorphism and the
image of a homomorphism is a subgroup
([[def-group-homomorphism]], [[def-subgroup]]). The **(Weil) divisor class
group** of $X$ is the quotient group
$$\operatorname{Cl}(X)\;:=\;\operatorname{Div}(X)\big/P(X)\;=\;\operatorname{Div}(X)\big/\{\operatorname{div}_W(f):f\in K(X)^\times\}$$
([[def-quotient-group]]). Thus $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is an abelian group, and
two Weil divisors $D,D'$ have the same class in $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ exactly
when $D-D'=\operatorname{div}_W(f)$ for some $f\in K(X)^\times$; one then says
that $D$ and $D'$ are **linearly equivalent as Weil divisors**. This relation is
an equivalence relation: it is reflexive via $f=1$, symmetric via
$\operatorname{div}_W(f^{-1})=-\operatorname{div}_W(f)$, and transitive via the
homomorphism property.

Three boundary cases are worth recording. First, an integral scheme is
nonempty by definition, so the empty scheme is not an instance of this
definition and no empty divisor group is being described. Second, if $X$ has no
prime divisors (for instance $X=\operatorname{Spec}k$ for a field $k$), then
$\operatorname{Div}(X)=0$, and $\operatorname{Cl}(X)=0$ as well: every nonzero function
field element is a unit and the zero divisor is principal. Third, the constant
function $f=1$ realises the zero class, so $\operatorname{Cl}(X)$ is the
quotient by the subgroup generated by the divisors of the form
$\operatorname{div}_W(f)$; no effectiveness hypothesis is imposed on the
elements of $\operatorname{Div}(X)$ or on the divisors defining a class.
