## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Finite linear combinations of matrix coefficients
of finite-dimensional unitary representations are uniformly dense in $C(G)$ for
a compact Lie group $G$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact Lie group $G$ with normalized Haar measure, and $f\in C(G)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through the Haar and Hilbert-space theory cited.

[L1] The normalized matrix coefficients form a Hilbert basis of $L^2(G)$, so every element of $L^2(G)$ is the finite-subset-net limit of finite matrix-coefficient sums ([[thm-peter-weyl-for-compact-lie-groups]]).

[L2] There are continuous central functions $k_n$ of integral one with $T_{k_n}f\to f$ uniformly for continuous $f$; $C(G)\subseteq L^2(G)$ because $G$ is compact ([[lem-compact-lie-groups-admit-central-continuous-approximate-identities]]).

[L3] Every unital point-separating self-adjoint complex function algebra on a compact Hausdorff space is uniformly dense in the continuous complex functions ([[thm-complex-stone-weierstrass-self-adjoint]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $f$ is continuous on the compact group, $f\in L^2(G)$ and $\|f\|_2<+\infty$; fix $\varepsilon>0$ and choose $n$ with $\sup_x|T_{k_n}f(x)-f(x)|<\varepsilon/2$ by [L2], so that $|f(x)-\int_Gk_n(u)f(xu)\,du|<\varepsilon/2$ for every $x$. [L2]

1.2 By [L1] applied to the $L^2$ class of $k_n$, there is a finite linear combination $p$ of matrix coefficients with $\|k_n-p\|_2<\varepsilon/(2\|f\|_2)$ when $f\ne0$ (and the conclusion below is trivial when $f=0$). [L1]

2.1 For every $x$, $|f*p(x)-f*T_{k_n}(x)|\le\int_G|f(y)||p(y^{-1}x)-k_n(y^{-1}x)|\,dy\le\|f\|_2\|p-k_n\|_2<\varepsilon/2$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, while $\int_Gk_n(u)f(xu)\,du=(f*T_{k_n})(x)$; hence $\|f*p-f\|_\infty<\varepsilon$. The function $f*p$ is a finite linear combination of matrix coefficients: expanding $p=\sum c_j\pi^j_{i_jj_j}$ and using $\pi(y^{-1}x)=\pi(y^{-1})\pi(x)$ expresses $f*p$ as a finite sum of products of constants $\int_Gf(y)\pi_{i k}(y^{-1})\,dy$ with matrix coefficients $\pi_{kj}(x)$. [L1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Independent check by Stone–Weierstrass: let $A$ be the complex algebra generated by all matrix coefficients. It contains the constants (the trivial representation) and is closed under conjugation and under complex linear combination, and it separates points: if $x\ne y$ with every irreducible $\pi$ satisfying $\pi(y^{-1}x)=\mathrm{id}$, then left translation by $y^{-1}x$ fixes the dense span of matrix coefficients and hence all of $L^2(G)$ by [L1], contradicting the existence of a continuous bump supported in a small identity neighbourhood disjoint from its translate by $y^{-1}x$. By [L3] the uniform closure of $A$ is $C(G)$, giving the same conclusion without invoking the approximate identity. [A1, L1, L3]∎
