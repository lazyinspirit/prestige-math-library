## Example

For $K=\mathbb Q$, use $f(X)=X$, integral basis $1$, discriminant $\Delta=1$, and finite prime set $T=\varnothing$. The bounded S-unit matrix algorithm has $B=1$, $d=1$, $m_T=1$, trace Gram matrix $G=(1)$, and coordinate box $\{-1,0,1\}$. Filtering retains $g_1=-1,g_2=1$. The valuation matrix has shape $0\times2$, its kernel basis returns these unit generators, and its cokernel gives the trivial ideal class group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $\mathbb Q$, the polynomial $X$, and the bounded S-unit matrix algorithm.

[F1] The algorithm sets $B=|\Delta|$ and builds its coordinate box from the trace Gram matrix ([[alg-compute-units-and-class-group-from-bounded-s-units]]).

[F2] The only units of $\mathbb Z$ are $1$ and $-1$, and every ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is a cyclic additive subgroup, hence is principal; the ideal class group is the quotient by principal ideals ([[lem-units-of-z]], [[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]], [[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]]).

[F3] The Smith algorithm handles zero-dimensional row spaces and returns integer kernel and cokernel data ([[alg-integer-smith-normal-form]], [[thm-integer-smith-normal-form-computes-kernel-and-cokernel]]).

## Proof



1.1 Here $n=1$, $r_2=0$, $\Delta=1$, and $B=1$. No prime ideal has norm at most $1$, so $T=\varnothing$ and $m_T=1$; also $d=(2/\pi)^0\sqrt1=1$. The only nonleading coefficient of $f=X$ is zero, so $R=1$, and for $P_1(X)=1$ one has $W_1=1$. Since $G=(\operatorname{Tr}(1))=(1)$, the formula gives $C_1=\lceil1\cdot\lceil1\rceil\rceil=1$. Thus the enumerated nonzero elements are exactly $-1$ and $1$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Both elements have norm of absolute value $1$, so there are no rational prime divisors to test; both pass the $T$-unit filter. By [F2], these are exactly the generators needed for $\mathbb Z^\times$, while the valuation matrix has zero rows. The $0\times2$ zero matrix has identity column transformation, kernel $\mathbb Z^2$ generated by its two standard basis vectors, and zero cokernel. Their products give $-1$ and $1$, and the cokernel records the trivial class group. [F2, F3, step 1.1] ∎
