## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and Countable Choice. Let $q>1$ and let $\chi$ be a primitive Dirichlet character modulo $q$. There is a primitive finite-order ray-class Hecke character $\widetilde\chi$ over $\mathbb Q$ with modulus $((q),\varnothing)$ if $\chi(-1)=1$, and modulus $((q),\{\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Q}\})$ if $\chi(-1)=-1$. Its finite conductor ideal is $(q)$, its archimedean part is empty in the even case and consists of the real place in the odd case, and $L(s,\widetilde\chi)=L(s,\chi)$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. The field degree is $1$, the absolute field discriminant is $1$, and therefore $|d_{\mathbb Q}|N\mathfrak f_{\widetilde\chi}=q$.

[Cambridge Part IV notes, Chapter 2, §2.1](https://dec41.user.srcf.net/notes/IV_L/topics_in_number_theory_trim.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice, a positive integer $q>1$, and a primitive Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $q$.

[A1] The finite-order ray-class Hecke-character and L-series interface is stated under AC and Countable Choice. ([[def-finite-order-ray-class-hecke-character-and-l-function]])

[F1] A Dirichlet character modulo $q$ is a homomorphism on $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$, extended by zero to integers not coprime to $q$; its Dirichlet L-series is $\sum_{n\ge1}\chi(n)n^{-s}$ for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$, and primitive means it is not induced from a proper divisor of $q$. ([[def-dirichlet-character-modulo-q]], [[def-dirichlet-l-function]], [[def-primitive-dirichlet-character-and-conductor]])

[F2] The ray class group modulo a finite ideal and selected real places is the group of fractional ideals prime to that finite ideal, modulo principal ideals generated by scalars congruent to $1$ there and positive at the selected real places. ([[def-global-field-modulus]], [[def-ray-class-group]])

[F3] A finite-order ray-class Hecke character is a finite-image homomorphism of this ray class group; its L-series sums the character values over nonzero integral ideals, assigning value zero to ideals not coprime to the modulus. ([[def-finite-order-ray-class-hecke-character-and-l-function]])

[F4] The ring of integers, field discriminant, and ideal norm have their standard trace-pairing and quotient-cardinality definitions. ([[def-number-field]], [[def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]], [[def-discriminant-of-a-number-field-basis-and-order]], [[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]])

[F5] The Chinese remainder theorem combines prescribed residues at finitely many pairwise coprime integer moduli. ([[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem]])

[F6] The units of $\mathbb Z$ are exactly $1$ and $-1$. ([[lem-units-of-z]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data and assumptions in the Statement.

1.1 First $\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q}=\mathbb Z$: if $a/b$ is in lowest terms and satisfies a monic polynomial over $\mathbb Z$, clearing denominators shows $b\mid a^m$ for the degree $m$, hence $b=1$; every integer is integral. The single-element basis $\{1\}$ has trace-pairing matrix $(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb Q/\mathbb Q}(1))=(1)$, so $[\mathbb Q:\mathbb Q]=1$ and $d_{\mathbb Q}=1$. Also $N(q\mathbb Z)=|\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z|=q$. [F4, given, algebra]

2.1 Every nonzero fractional ideal $I\subseteq\mathbb Q$ of $\mathbb Z$ is $r\mathbb Z$ for a unique $r>0$: choose $d\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}$ with $dI\subseteq\mathbb Z$; the nonzero integral ideal $dI$ has a least positive element $n$, and division with remainder shows $dI=n\mathbb Z$, so $I=(n/d)\mathbb Z$ and changing the generator's sign gives the unique positive one. Write this generator in lowest terms as $r=a/b$ with $a,b>0$. The ideal $r\mathbb Z$ is prime to $(q)$ exactly when neither $a$ nor $b$ is divisible by any prime dividing $q$, equivalently $(ab,q)=1$: a prime dividing $a$ contributes that prime to the ideal, while a prime dividing $b$ contributes it to the denominator with negative exponent. In that case $r\bmod q$ means $a b^{-1}\in(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.1 For a modulus $((q),\varnothing)$, send the ray class of $r\mathbb Z$ to $r\bmod q$ modulo the subgroup $\{1,-1\}$. For a modulus $((q),\{\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb Q}\})$, send it to $r\bmod q$ itself. These maps are homomorphisms. If two positive generators $r,s$ define the same ray class, then $(r/s)=(\alpha)$ for a scalar $\alpha$ satisfying the ray congruence; [F6] says the only generators of the same principal fractional ideal differ by $\pm1$, so the finite-only case gives $r/s\equiv\pm1\pmod q$, while the modulus with the real place forces $\alpha=r/s>0$ and gives $r/s\equiv1\pmod q$. Conversely either stated residue relation supplies such a ray-principal generator. Thus the maps are injective. Every unit residue modulo $q$ has a positive integer representative coprime to $q$, so both maps are surjective. Therefore $$\operatorname{Cl}_{(q)}(\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\},\qquad \operatorname{Cl}_{(q,\infty)}(\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times.$$ [F2, F6, step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra]

4.1 Since $\chi(-1)^2=1$, its value at $-1$ is either $1$ or $-1$. If $\chi(-1)=1$, it descends to a character on $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}$; compose with the first isomorphism in step 3.1. If $\chi(-1)=-1$, compose it with the second isomorphism. Both source groups are finite, so the resulting homomorphism has finite image and is a finite-order ray-class Hecke character by [F3]. For positive rational $r$ coprime to $q$, its value on $(r)$ is $\chi(r\bmod q)$. For negative rational $r$, the positive generator is $-r$; in the even case $\chi(-r)=\chi(r)$, and in the odd case $\chi(-r)=-\chi(r)=\chi(r)\operatorname{sgn}(r)$. Thus the finite residue character is $\chi$ and the real parity is $0$ in the even case and $1$ in the odd case. [F1, F3, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Suppose $\widetilde\chi$ factored through a proper divisor $((d),S')$ of its selected modulus, so $d\mid q$ and either $d<q$ or the selected real place was removed. If $d<q$, positive integers $m,n$ coprime to $q$ with $m\equiv n\pmod d$ define the same class modulo $((d),S')$, because $m/n>0$ and $m/n\equiv1\pmod d$. Hence $\chi(m)=\chi(n)$. Reduction $(\mathbb Z/q\mathbb Z)^\times\to(\mathbb Z/d\mathbb Z)^\times$ is surjective: for each unit residue $a\bmod d$, first choose an integer representative $a$ coprime to $d$. For each prime $p\mid q$ with $p\nmid d$, choose a residue for $k\bmod p$ other than the unique residue satisfying $a+kd\equiv0\pmod p$; [F5] combines these choices, and adding a sufficiently large multiple of their product to $k$ makes $a+kd$ positive. Primes dividing $d$ do not divide this lift because $a$ is a unit modulo $d$. The equality just proved makes $\chi$ constant on each reduction fiber. Define $\chi_d(\bar a)$ to be the value of $\chi$ on any unit lift of $\bar a$; fiber constancy makes this well-defined, and multiplying lifts shows $\chi_d$ is a homomorphism. Thus $\chi$ is induced from modulus $d$, contradicting [F1]. If the real place is removed, then necessarily $\chi$ is odd and $d=q$. If $q=2$, no odd character exists because $-1\equiv1\pmod2$; otherwise choose $n=q-1>0$, so $n\equiv-1\pmod q$ and $(n,q)=1$. In the finite-only ray group the positive integers $1$ and $n$ define the same class modulo $\pm1$, whereas their character values are $1$ and $\chi(-1)=-1$. Thus factoring is impossible in this last case too. The constructed Hecke character is primitive, with finite conductor ideal $(q)$ and the asserted archimedean part. [F1, F2, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, choose, construct, algebra]

6.1 Every nonzero integral ideal of $\mathbb Z$ is $n\mathbb Z$ for a unique integer $n\ge1$, by the least-positive-element argument of step 2.1, and its quotient has representatives $0,1,\ldots,n-1$, so its norm is $n$. The Hecke coefficient is $\chi(n)$ when $(n,q)=1$ and zero otherwise, exactly the Dirichlet coefficient by [F1] and [F3]. The two absolutely convergent Dirichlet series therefore agree term by term for $\operatorname{Re}s>1$. By step 1.1, the Hecke conductor-discriminant product is $1\cdot N(q\mathbb Z)=q$. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
