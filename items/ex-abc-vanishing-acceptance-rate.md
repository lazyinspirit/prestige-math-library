## Statement

Let $\Theta=\{0,1\}$ have equal prior probabilities and let $Y\mid\theta$ be uniform on $[\theta,\theta+1]$. With identity summary and observed value $s_0=1/2$, the ABC acceptance probability equals $\epsilon$ for $0<\epsilon<1/2$, so it tends to zero, while every such ABC target is $\delta_0$ and the density-selected zero-tolerance posterior is also $\delta_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and the model, ABC target, and rejection conventions in [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]] and [[alg-abc-rejection]].

[F1] The measure with a nonnegative measurable density is defined by integration. [[def-measure-with-density]], [[thm-indefinite-integral-of-a-nonnegative-function-is-a-measure]]

[F2] The usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is complete, the rationals are countable and dense, and its metric balls generate the usual topology. [[thm-reals-cauchy-complete]], [[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]], [[def-metric-ball]], [[def-metric-topology]]

[F3] A Polish presentation is separable and has a compatible complete metric; the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by its open sets. [[def-standard-borel-space]], [[def-polish-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F4] Countable Choice is the axiom assumed by the density-limit result invoked below. [[def-countable-choice]]

[F5] Positive-tolerance ABC conditions the prior-predictive law on its tolerance event, and rejection returns that target. [[def-approximate-bayesian-computation]], [[thm-abc-rejection-target-is-tolerance-conditioned-posterior]]

[F6] Under Countable Choice, the zero-tolerance theorem identifies the density-selected limit when the specified version is continuous at the observed summary and has an integrable local envelope and positive finite averaged density. [[thm-abc-zero-tolerance-target-is-summary-posterior-under-density-regularity]]

## Example

Take $\Theta=\{0,1\}$ with prior $\Pi(\{0\})=\Pi(\{1\})=1/2$, data space $\mathbb R$, summary $S(y)=y$, and densities
$$m_0(s)=\mathbf1_{[0,1]}(s),\qquad m_1(s)=\mathbf1_{[1,2]}(s).$$

## Verification

**Given:** The model, prior, and densities in the Example.

1.1 Give $\Theta$ its discrete metric. Since Cauchy means that tail distances are eventually below every positive radius, a Cauchy sequence is eventually constant at radius $1/2$, so the metric is complete; the finite set is countable and dense in itself, and its singletons are open. Its Borel sigma-algebra is the power set, so [F3] gives standard Borelness. By [F2], the usual metric on $\mathbb R$ is complete and has countable dense subset $\mathbb Q$; its balls are ordinary intervals, so it induces the usual topology. Thus both the data and summary Borel spaces are standard Borel by [F3]. Each displayed density is nonnegative measurable and integrates to one because its supporting interval has length one. Thus $K(\theta,ds)=m_\theta(s)\,ds$ is a probability kernel on the finite parameter space, and the identity summary is measurable. For every product-measurable $E\subseteq\Theta\times\mathbb R$, the section maps $s\mapsto(\theta,s)$ are measurable, so $E_\theta=\{s:(\theta,s)\in E\}$ is measurable, and $E=\bigsqcup_{\theta\in\Theta}\{\theta\}\times E_\theta$. Since $\Theta$ is finite, the rectangle identity extends by this finite disjoint decomposition to $M(E)=\sum_{\theta\in\Theta}\Pi(\{\theta\})\int_{E_\theta}m_\theta(s)\,ds=\int_E m_\theta(s)\,(\Pi\times\lambda_1)(d\theta,ds).$ Thus the finite-parameter summary-predictive law has the specified joint density required by the zero-tolerance theorem. [given, F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 For $0<\epsilon<1/2$, the tolerance ball about $1/2$ is $[1/2-\epsilon,1/2+\epsilon]$. Its intersection with $[0,1]$ has length $2\epsilon$, while it is disjoint from $[1,2]$ because its upper endpoint is strictly below $1$. The parameter-conditional acceptance probabilities are therefore $2\epsilon$ at $0$ and $0$ at $1$. Averaging against the equal prior gives $p_\epsilon=\epsilon>0$, and conditioning gives ABC target $\delta_0$. In particular $p_\epsilon\to0$. By [F5], the accepted parameter also has law $\delta_0$. [given, F1, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 On the neighborhood $[1/4,3/4]$ of $s_0=1/2$, $m_0$ is identically $1$ and $m_1$ is identically $0$. Both densities are continuous at $s_0$, and the constant envelope $H(\theta)=1$ is integrable under $\Pi$. The averaged density is $z_0=\tfrac12m_0(1/2)+\tfrac12m_1(1/2)=1/2$, so the density-selected posterior is $\delta_0$. [given, F6, step 1.1]

3.1 The hypotheses of [F6] hold under the Countable Choice assumption [F4] (with, for example, neighborhood radius $1/4$), so the ABC targets converge in total variation to $\delta_0$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Here step 2.1 shows the stronger exact identity $\Pi_\epsilon=\delta_0$ for every $0<\epsilon<1/2$, even though the acceptance probability vanishes. [given, F4, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
