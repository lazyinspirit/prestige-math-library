## Statement

For a finite tetrahedral triangulation, let $B$ record the six local edge-intersection counts in every tetrahedron from its seven normal coordinates. Let $u,v,d$ be nonnegative real normal-coordinate arrays, each using at most one quadrilateral type in each tetrahedron. They need not satisfy matching equations. If $\alpha,\beta>0$ and

$$\alpha Bu+\beta Bv=(\alpha+\beta)Bd,$$

then

$$\alpha u+\beta v=(\alpha+\beta)d.$$

In particular, if $u,v,d$ are actual normal-surface vectors, the hypothesis may be checked on their global triangulation-edge intersection counts. If those counts for $u$ and $v$ average to those for $d$, then their normal-coordinate vectors also average to $d$.

If $d$ is lexicographically least in any set of vectors containing $u,v,d$, this averaging identity forces $u=v=d$. Thus two essential normal disks of the same least edge weight whose edge counts average to those of a lexicographically least such disk must have exactly that disk's normal vector.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The three arrays, positive weights and local edge-count equality of the Statement; fix any ordering of all normal coordinates for the lexicographic assertion.

[F1] Label the four vertices of a tetrahedron $A,B,C,D$. A triangle at a vertex meets precisely its three incident edges. Denote by $q_1,q_2,q_3$ the quadrilateral types avoiding respectively the pairs $AB,CD$, the pairs $AC,BD$, and the pairs $AD,BC$. A quadrilateral meets the other four edges once. ([[def-normal-surface-coordinate-box]])

[F2] Each of the arrays has nonnegative coordinates and at most one positive quadrilateral coordinate in each tetrahedron. ([[def-normal-surface-coordinate-box]])

## Proof

1.1 Fix one tetrahedron and put $\delta=\alpha u+\beta v-(\alpha+\beta)d$ on its seven coordinates. Write its triangle coordinates as $a,b,c,e$ at $A,B,C,D$, and its quadrilateral coordinates as $x,y,z$ at $q_1,q_2,q_3$. The hypothesis $B\delta=0$ and [F1] give the six equations $$\begin{aligned}a+b+y+z&=0,&c+e+y+z&=0,\\a+c+x+z&=0,&b+e+x+z&=0,\\a+e+x+y&=0,&b+c+x+y&=0.\end{aligned}$$ They are listed in opposite-edge pairs. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 Subtracting the members of those pairs gives $a+b=c+e$, $a+c=b+e$ and $a+e=b+c$. The first two imply $b=c$ and $a=e$; substituting in the third gives $a=b$. Thus all four triangle differences equal one number $t$. The first equation in each pair now gives $y+z=x+z=x+y=-2t$, so $x=y=z=-t$. Hence the kernel of the six local edge equations is exactly the line generated by $(1,1,1,1,-1,-1,-1)$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Put $\gamma=-t$. For each of the three quad types, $$\alpha u_{q_i}+\beta v_{q_i}=(\alpha+\beta)d_{q_i}+\gamma.$$ If $\gamma<0$, choose a quad coordinate with $d_{q_i}=0$, which exists by [F2]. Its left side is nonnegative, whereas its right side is negative, a contradiction. If $\gamma>0$, all three left sides are strictly positive. Positivity of $\alpha,\beta$ would then require the union of the positive quad supports of $u$ and $v$ to contain all three types. Each support has size at most one by [F2], so that union has size at most two. This is again a contradiction. Therefore $\gamma=0$. [F2, step 2.1, contradiction, algebra]

4.1 It follows that $t=0$ and every coordinate of $\delta$ is zero. Apply the same argument independently in each tetrahedron to obtain $\alpha u+\beta v=(\alpha+\beta)d$ on the whole array. No matching equation was used. [step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For an actual normal surface, its local count on any tetrahedral edge is the number of its intersection points with that global edge: the normal disks in each incident tetrahedron meet exactly those points. Hence an equality of global edge counts gives the six local equalities in every tetrahedron, and step 4.1 proves the surface-vector assertion. [F1, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 If $u\ne d$, let $j$ be the first coordinate where they differ. The averaging identity gives $\beta(v-d)=-\alpha(u-d)$. Therefore $v$ also agrees with $d$ before $j$, and the differences at $j$ have opposite nonzero signs. One of $u,v$ is consequently lexicographically smaller than $d$, contradicting the assumed minimality. Thus $u=d$, and the same identity then gives $v=d$. Applying this to the set of essential normal disk vectors at the least edge weight proves the final assertion. [given, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Scope of the averaging assertion

The two-array restriction matters. In one tetrahedron let $d$ have one copy of each triangle and no quadrilateral, and let $u_i$ have three copies of $q_i$ and no triangles, for $i=1,2,3$. All four arrays satisfy the quadrilateral restriction. The kernel identity in step 2.1 gives $(Bu_1+Bu_2+Bu_3)/3=Bd$, while $(u_1+u_2+u_3)/3\ne d$. Thus the lemma cannot be applied to an arbitrary number of differently supported compression outputs without a further argument.
