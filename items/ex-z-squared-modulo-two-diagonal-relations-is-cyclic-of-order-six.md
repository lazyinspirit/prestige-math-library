## Example

The quotient $\mathbb Z^2/\langle(2,0),(0,3)\rangle$ is cyclic of order six, generated by the class of $(1,1)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The displayed relation matrix and elementary integer row and column operations.

[L1] $R/(ab)\cong R/(a)\oplus R/(b)$ when $a$ and $b$ are coprime ([[lem-coprime-cyclic-pid-quotients-split]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The relation matrix is $\operatorname{diag}(2,3)$. Since $2$ and $3$ are coprime, elementary integer row and column operations using $3-2=1$ transform it to $\operatorname{diag}(1,6)$, which is its Smith form. [given, algebra]

2.1 Directly, the quotient is $\mathbb Z/2\oplus\mathbb Z/3$, and [L1] identifies this with $\mathbb Z/6$; the unit Smith factor in step 1.1 contributes no summand. [step 1.1, L1]

3.1 The class of $(1,1)$ has order the least common multiple of $2$ and $3$, namely $6$, so it generates the order-six quotient. Its lower positive multiples are nonzero because neither coordinate has simultaneously reached its relation. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎
