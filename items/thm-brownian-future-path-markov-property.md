## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, let $B$ be a standard Brownian motion
[[def-brownian-motion]] with raw natural filtration $(\mathcal F^0_t)$ and
usual augmentation $(\mathcal F_t)$
[[def-natural-and-usual-augmented-brownian-filtrations]], and let $\mu$ be
Wiener measure on $C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)$
[[def-wiener-measure-on-continuous-path-space]].

Write $u\mapsto u(t)$ for a point of the product space
$\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$, whose product sigma-algebra is by definition
generated by all finite coordinate cylinders (it exists by
[[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]). Here Borel on the
product means measurable for that sigma-algebra. The inclusion
$j:C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$ is measurable
because its coordinates are continuous. For a bounded Borel
functional $\Phi:\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}\to\mathbb R$ put
$$\Psi_\Phi(x):=\int_{C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)}\Phi(x+w)\,\mu(dw),\qquad x\in\mathbb R,$$
where $x+w$ denotes the point $t\mapsto x+w(t)$.

1. **Increment process.** For every $s\ge0$ the random element
   $Z:=(B_{s+t}-B_s)_{t\ge0}$ is independent of $\mathcal F_s$ and has the law
   of $B$; that is, for every finite cylinder event
   $\Gamma=\{z:z(t_1)\in B_1,\dots,z(t_k)\in B_k\}$ and every $A\in\mathcal F_s$,
   $P(A\cap\{Z\in\Gamma\})=P(A)\,\mu_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\Gamma)$, where
   $\mu_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\Gamma)=\mu(\{w:w(t_i)\in B_i\ \forall i\})$, and the
   analogous identity holds for every Borel set of the product sigma-algebra.
2. **Conditional future law.** For every $s\ge0$ and every bounded Borel
   functional $\Phi$ on $\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$,
   $$E\bigl[\Phi\bigl((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0}\bigr)\bigm|\mathcal F_s\bigr] =\Psi_\Phi(B_s)\quad\text{almost surely},$$
   and the same identity holds with $\mathcal F^0_s$ in place of $\mathcal F_s$.
   In particular $E[\Phi((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F_s]$ is a function of the
   single state $B_s$.

The continuous-path formulation uses a common measurable probability-one
event $A_*$ on which all paths of $B$ are continuous. Define
$V^s(\omega)(t)=B_{s+t}(\omega)$ on $A_*$ and let $V^s(\omega)$ be the zero
path off $A_*$. For every bounded Borel $\phi:C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R$,
the same conditional identity holds with $\phi(V^s)$ on the left and
$\int\phi(B_s+w)\mu(dw)$ on the right, for either past sigma-algebra.
This convention is independent almost surely of the choice of $A_*$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a standard Brownian motion $B$, $s\ge0$ and a bounded Borel functional $\Phi$ on $\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$.

[F1] Brownian increments along finite strictly increasing lists are independent with laws $N(0,\Delta t)$, and $B_0=0$ almost surely. [[def-brownian-motion]]

[F2] Grouping finite independent families and the pi-system criterion for independent sigma-algebras. [[thm-grouping-independent-sigma-algebras]] [[thm-pi-system-criterion-for-independent-sigma-algebras]] [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]] [[def-independent-random-elements]]

[F3] Conditioning a known state on independent noise: $E[h(X,Y)|\mathcal G]=H(X)$ for $H(x)=\int h(x,y)\mu_Y(dy)$. [[lem-conditioning-a-known-state-and-independent-noise]]

[F4] Wiener measure is the law of a continuous Brownian motion, so its finite-dimensional marginals are the Brownian increment laws of [F1], and its Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the coordinates on a countable dense set. [[def-wiener-measure-on-continuous-path-space]] [[lem-borel-sigma-algebra-of-continuous-path-space-is-generated-by-coordinates]]

[F5] Conditional-expectation versions are characterized by event integrals and are unique almost surely; the tower identity holds for nested sigma-algebras. [[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]] [[lem-conditional-expectation-is-unique-almost-surely]] [[thm-tower-property-of-conditional-expectation]]

[F6] A lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the generated sigma-algebra. By the convention in the statement, finite coordinate cylinders generate the product sigma-algebra; coordinate cylinders generate the Borel sigma-algebra of continuous path space. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]] [[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]] [[lem-borel-sigma-algebra-of-continuous-path-space-is-generated-by-coordinates]]

[F7] Simple approximation, monotone convergence and dominated convergence on the probability spaces used below; measurability of $x\mapsto\int\Phi(x+w)\mu(dw)$ by the constant-kernel integration theorem. [[thm-increasing-simple-approximation-of-a-nonnegative-measurable-function]] [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]] [[thm-dominated-convergence]] [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]] [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F8] Every set of the completed raw sigma-algebra $\overline{\mathcal F}{}^0_u$ differs from a set of $\mathcal F^0_u$ by a null set, and bounded integrals over the two sets agree; AC supplies the conditional-expectation interface. [[def-natural-and-usual-augmented-brownian-filtrations]] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix distinct times $0\le t_1<\cdots<t_k$ and a bounded Borel $G:\mathbb R^k\to\mathbb R$, and let $\Phi$ be the cylinder functional $\Phi(u)=G(u(t_1),\dots,u(t_k))$. Then $Y:=(B_{s+t_i}-B_s)_{i\le k}$ is a random element of $\mathbb R^k$ independent of $\mathcal F^0_s$ with the law $\nu$ of $(W_{t_i})_{i\le k}$ under Wiener measure: independence follows as in the finite-cylinder argument by deleting repetitions, expressing $(B_s,B_{s+t_1},\dots,B_{s+t_k})$ through the independent increments of [F1] and applying [F2]; for any finite past times $r_j\le s$, include the $r_j$, $s$ and the $s+t_i$ in a common ordered grid. The increments before and after $s$ are independent, while $B_0=0$ almost surely causes no change in event probabilities. This proves independence from every finite past cylinder, and [F2] extends it to their generated sigma-algebra $\mathcal F^0_s$. The law identity holds because both $Y$ and $(W_{t_i})$ are obtained from independent $N(0,t_i-t_{i-1})$ increments (with $t_0=0$) by the same cumulative-sum map, the Wiener marginal being [F4]. [F1, F2, F4]

2.1 With $\Phi$ and $Y$ as in step 1.1 apply [F3] to $X=B_s$, the sigma-algebra $\mathcal F^0_s$, the noise $Y$, and $h(x,y):=G(x+y_1,\dots,x+y_k)$: $H(x)=\int G(x+y_1,\dots,x+y_k)\nu(dy)$ is Borel and $E[\Phi((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F^0_s]=H(B_s)$ almost surely. By step 1.1's law identity, $H(x)=\int\Phi(x+w)\mu(dw)=\Psi_\Phi(x)$ for every $x$, because $\Phi(x+w)=G(x+w(t_1),\dots,x+w(t_k))$ and the marginal of $(w(t_i))$ under $\mu$ is $\nu$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $\mathcal D$ be the class of product-measurable sets $\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$ such that $E[1_\Gamma((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F^0_s]=\mu_\Gamma(B_s)$ almost surely, where $\mu_\Gamma(x):=\mu(x+W\in\Gamma)$. Each $\mu_\Gamma$ is Borel by [F7] applied to the constant kernel $\mu$, since $(x,w)\mapsto x+w$ is product measurable. Finite cylinder sets lie in $\mathcal D$ by step 2.1, and $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system: it contains the whole space because $\mu_{\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}}\equiv1$; it is closed under complements because $\mu_{\Gamma^c}=1-\mu_\Gamma$ and conditional expectations are additive on bounded inputs; and for disjoint sets in the class, countable additivity of the kernel and monotone convergence of the nonnegative finite sums give the event-integral identity for their union. Thus it is closed under disjoint countable unions, as required for a lambda-system. [F3, F5, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 The finite cylinder sets are a pi-system containing the whole space and generate the product sigma-algebra, so [F6] gives $\mathcal D=$ all product-measurable sets: for every product-measurable $\Gamma$, $E[1_\Gamma((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F^0_s]=\mu_\Gamma(B_s)$ almost surely. [F6, step 3.1]

5.1 For bounded nonnegative $\Phi$, choose simple functionals $s_n\uparrow\Phi$ and use step 4.1 together with linearity of the integral to get $\int_A s_n((B_{s+t}))\,dP=\int_A\Psi_{s_n}(B_s)\,dP$ for every $A\in\mathcal F^0_s$; monotone convergence [F7] applied to both sides gives $\int_A\Phi((B_{s+t}))\,dP=\int_A\Psi_\Phi(B_s)\,dP$. Since $\Psi_\Phi(B_s)$ is bounded and $\mathcal F^0_s$-measurable, [F5] gives $E[\Phi((B_{s+t}))|\mathcal F^0_s]=\Psi_\Phi(B_s)$ almost surely, and splitting a bounded real $\Phi$ into positive and negative parts extends this to all bounded Borel $\Phi$. This is the raw-filtration half of assertion 2. [F5, F7, step 4.1]

6.1 We next prove the identity for the usual augmentation, first for a bounded continuous cylinder $\Phi(u)=G(u(t_1),\ldots,u(t_k))$. Set $u_n=s+1/n$. For $A\in\mathcal F_s\subseteq\overline{\mathcal F}{}^0_{u_n}$, the raw identity of step 5.1 at time $u_n$ extends from $\mathcal F^0_{u_n}$ to its completion by [F8], and gives $\int_A\Phi((B_{u_n+t})_{t\ge0})dP=\int_A\Psi_\Phi(B_{u_n})dP$. Brownian continuity makes the left integrand converge almost surely to $\Phi((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})$. Also $B_{u_n}\to B_s$ almost surely and $\Psi_\Phi$ is continuous, because bounded convergence under Wiener measure applies to $\Phi(x_n+w)\to\Phi(x+w)$ for a continuous cylinder. Dominated convergence therefore yields $\int_A\Phi((B_{s+t}))dP=\int_A\Psi_\Phi(B_s)dP$. [F1, F4, F5, F7, F8, step 5.1]

7.1 Approximate each half-line coordinate-cylinder indicator by decreasing bounded continuous cylinder functions using $g_{m,c}(x)=\max(0,1-m\max(x-c,0))$ and finite products of these functions; dominated convergence in step 6.1 gives the same event-integral identity for all half-line cylinders. The class of product-measurable sets for which that identity holds for every $A\in\mathcal F_s$ is a lambda-system by the same event-integral and disjoint-union calculation as step 3.1; half-line cylinders form a generating pi-system, so it is the whole product sigma-algebra by [F6]. Increasing simple approximation and monotone convergence then extend the identity to every bounded nonnegative Borel $\Phi$, and positive-minus-negative decomposition to every bounded real $\Phi$. Since $\Psi_\Phi(B_s)$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_s$-measurable by [F7], [F5] identifies it as $E[\Phi((B_{s+t}))\mid\mathcal F_s]$. This completes assertion 2 without reversing the tower property. [F5, F6, F7, step 6.1]

8.1 For assertion 1, fix a finite cylinder $\Gamma=\{z:z(t_1)\in B_1,\dots,z(t_k)\in B_k\}$ and let $\Phi_\Gamma(u):=1_\Gamma((u(t)-u(0))_{t\ge0})$, a bounded Borel functional. For every $x$, $\Phi_\Gamma(x+w)=1_\Gamma((w(t)-w(0))_{t\ge0})$ is independent of $x$, so $\Psi_{\Phi_\Gamma}(x)=\mu_{\mathrm{cyl}}(\Gamma)$ is the constant $q_\Gamma$; step 7.1 therefore gives $P(A\cap\{Z\in\Gamma\})=q_\Gamma P(A)$ for every $A\in\mathcal F_s$, and $q_\Gamma=P(Z\in\Gamma)$. Hence $\sigma(Z)$ is independent of $\mathcal F_s$: the class of product-measurable $\Gamma$ satisfying $P(A\cap\{Z\in\Gamma\})=P(A)P(Z\in\Gamma)$ for all $A\in\mathcal F_s$ is a lambda-system containing the cylinder pi-system, so equals the product sigma-algebra by [F6]. Its finite-dimensional marginals are those of $\mu$ by step 1.1's law identity, so the law of $Z$ is the pushforward $j_*\mu$ on the product sigma-algebra; this is the assertion that the increment process is a Brownian motion independent of the past. [F6, step 7.1]

8.2 For the continuous-path formulation, every coordinate of $V^s$ is ambient-measurable and every value is in continuous path space, so [F4] makes $V^s$ a Borel random element. For a continuous-path cylinder functional its evaluation at $V^s$ agrees almost surely with the corresponding product cylinder evaluated on the original future, simultaneously in every time on $A_*$. Thus steps 2.1 and 6.1 give its raw and augmented event-integral identities. The translation map $(x,w)\mapsto x+w$ into continuous path space is measurable because all its coordinates are measurable and [F4] gives the target sigma-algebra. Use the half-line approximation of step 7.1 for the augmented identity, then apply the lambda-system calculation of step 3.1 to cylinder sets in continuous path space, then [F6] and the simple approximation of step 5.1, to obtain both identities for every bounded Borel $\phi$. This argument asserts no adaptedness of the normalized coordinates: the right side is measurable with respect to the past because it is a Borel function of the original $B_s$. Two choices of $A_*$ give identical paths on their probability-one intersection, proving the asserted independence of the convention. [F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1]

9.1 The degenerate and endpoint cases are covered and consistent: if $k=0$ or $\Phi$ is constant, both sides equal that constant; if $t_1=0$ then $\Phi(u)=G(u(0),u(t_2),\dots)$ and the coordinate $B_{s+0}-B_s=0$, so $Y$ has a degenerate first coordinate and $\nu$ charges it correspondingly, while $\mu$'s marginal at time $0$ is the point mass at $w(0)$ — the identity of step 1.1 remains valid because both laws are the same law of a vector with a deterministic coordinate; for $s=0$ one has $B_0=0$ almost surely, $\Psi_\Phi(B_0)=\Psi_\Phi(0)$, and the past sigma-algebras $\mathcal F^0_0=\sigma(B_0)$ and $\mathcal F_0=\bigcap_{u>0}\overline{\mathcal F}{}^0_u$ are handled by steps 5.1 and 7.1 unchanged. AC is inherited from the Brownian and Wiener interfaces and from [F8], including ambient completion and the conditional-expectation interface. The time sequence is explicit and no pathwise selection is made. [F8, given, step 1.1, step 7.1] ∎

## Source notes

Durrett, Section 7.2, and Sousi's argument preceding Theorem 6.13, state the future-path Markov property for the completed filtration. The proof above separates the finite-cylinder identity, the Dynkin extension over future-path events, and the passage from the raw past to the usual augmentation, so that Blumenthal's law and the strong Markov theorem can cite exactly the half they need.
