## Statement

Fix $d\ge1$ and continuous $w_i:\mathbb R\to[0,\infty)$ with finite improper Riemann integrals $W_i=\int_{\mathbb R}w_i$. Put $w(t)=\prod_{i=1}^dw_i(t_i)$. If a continuous complex function $f$ satisfies $|f(t)|\le A w(t)$ for finite $A\ge0$, its absolute improper integral exists, is the limit on the canonical boxes $[-N,N]^d$, and equals every iterated improper integral in every coordinate order. If continuous $f_n$ satisfy $|f_n(t)|\le a_nw(t)$ for nonnegative $a_n$ with $\sum_na_n<\infty$, their series converges locally uniformly, and its sum can be integrated termwise in these same senses. For a jointly continuous family $f(u,t)$, on any fixed compact parameter rectangle a majorant $A w(t)$ uniform in $u$ gives a continuous parameter integral. These assertions also hold on products of half-lines with continuous integrands up to their finite endpoints, and for finite sums of these integrals. This is only a separable-majorant, uniformly summable-series assertion; it is not a general Riemann dominated-convergence theorem.

**One pair and parameters.** Fix $0<\sigma<1/2$ and put $D_\sigma=\{(z,z'):-\sigma<\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Re}z'<100\}$. There exist $\beta,\delta,C>0$, depending only on $\sigma$ and the supplied absolute zeta constants, with the following properties. Write $L=\log R$, $R\ge3$, $\Gamma_1(t)=L^{-1}+it$, $\Gamma_0(t)=-\beta/\log(|t|+2)+it$, all oriented upwards, and
$$K(z)=\frac1{\zeta(1+z)\zeta(1-z)z^4}.$$
For every bounded holomorphic $f:D_\sigma\to\mathbb C$, with $M=\sup_{D_\sigma}|f|$, the integrals below converge absolutely and
$$\frac1{(2\pi i)^2}\int_{\Gamma_1}\int_{\Gamma_1}f(z,z')\frac{\zeta(1+z+z')R^{z+z'}}{\zeta(1+z)\zeta(1+z')z^2(z')^2}\,dz'\,dz =L f(0,0)+Tf+E_R(f),$$
where
$$Tf=\partial_{z'}f(0,0)+\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_0}f(z,-z)K(z)\,dz,\qquad |Tf|\le CM,\qquad |E_R(f)|\le CM e^{-\delta\sqrt L}.$$
All three operations are linear in $f$. The absolute integral of the one-pair kernel on $\Gamma_1^2$, including the factor $(2\pi)^{-2}$, is at most $C(1+L)^3$. Absolutely convergent finite products of these parametrized contour integrals may be exchanged by the restricted Riemann calculus below.

More generally, for any open $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C^q$ and bounded jointly holomorphic $F(u,z,z')$ on $\Omega\times D_\sigma$, applying $T$ in the last pair gives a bounded jointly holomorphic function on $\Omega$, with $\|TF\|_{C^0(\Omega)}\le C\|F\|_{C^0(\Omega\times D_\sigma)}$. External complex partial derivatives commute with $T$ whenever the relevant bounded derivatives exist. In particular,
$$\|TF\|_{C^j(\Omega)}\le C\|F\|_{C^{j+1}(\Omega\times D_\sigma)}\quad(j\ge0),$$
when the norm on the right is finite. Norms take all complex partial derivatives of total order at most the indicated order. The same one-pair error bound is uniform in $u$, using only the global supremum of $F$.

**Multivariable asymptotic.** Fix an integer $m\ge1$, $0<\sigma<1/(6m)$, and $R\ge3$. For $z=(z_1,\ldots,z_m)$ and $z'=(z'_1,\ldots,z'_m)$, let
$$D_\sigma^m=\{(z,z'):-\sigma<\operatorname{Re}z_j,\operatorname{Re}z'_j<100\text{ for }1\le j\le m\}.$$
For a holomorphic $G$ on this domain, let $\|G\|_{C^j}$ be the supremum of the absolute values of all its complex partial derivatives of total order at most $j$ on $D_\sigma^m$. Suppose these norms are finite for $0\le j\le m$ and $\|G\|_{C^0}\le \exp(C_G(\log R)^{1/3})$ for a constant $C_G$ independent of $R$. With $\Gamma_1=\{1/\log R+it:t\in\mathbb R\}$ oriented upward, define
$$I_m(G;R):=\frac1{(2\pi i)^{2m}}\int_{\Gamma_1}\cdots\int_{\Gamma_1}G(z,z')\prod_{j=1}^m\frac{\zeta(1+z_j+z'_j)}{\zeta(1+z_j)\zeta(1+z'_j)}\frac{R^{z_j+z'_j}}{z_j^2(z'_j)^2}\,dz_j\,dz'_j.$$
Here $R^z:=\exp(z\log R)$. The integral converges absolutely and, for some $\delta=\delta(m,\sigma)>0$,
$$I_m(G;R)=G(0,0)(\log R)^m+\sum_{j=1}^m O_{m,\sigma}(\|G\|_{C^j}(\log R)^{m-j})+O_{m,\sigma,C_G}(e^{-\delta\sqrt{\log R}}).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The continuous majorants, holomorphic functions, and original finite higher norms and C0 envelope in the Statement; no choice premise.

## Proof

1.1 On each finite rectangle, [F6] makes the real and imaginary parts of a continuous complex function integrable and permits every section order. Its complex triangle inequality follows from the real one: for a nonzero integral value multiply by its single unit phase making that value positive real, and use pointwise $\operatorname{Re}(\xi f)\le|f|$; the zero case is immediate. In particular, if $Q_N=[-N,N]^d$, finite rectangular Fubini gives $\int_{Q_N}w=\prod_i\int_{-N}^Nw_i\le\prod_iW_i$. Every compact Jordan subset of $\mathbb R^d$ lies in some finite cube; proper comparison on its zero extension therefore bounds its $|f|$ integral by $A\prod_iW_i$. Thus [F6] gives absolute improper integrability. Writing $V_i(N)=\int_{|t|>N}w_i(t)\,dt$, a finite coordinate decomposition of $Q_M\setminus Q_N$ for $M>N$ gives the explicit bound $$\left|\int_{Q_M}f-\int_{Q_N}f\right|\le A\sum_{i=1}^dV_i(N)\prod_{j\ne i}W_j.$$ Here every $V_i(N)\to0$ by its one-variable absolute improper convergence. The cubes are prescribed, not selected, so their integrals converge to the absolute improper integral by [F6]. Zero weights or $A=0$ cause no division and are included. [F6, given, algebra]

1.2 Choose $$0<\beta<\min(b/2,\sigma\log2/8,\log2/64,1/100).$$ Put $a(t)=\beta/\log(|t|+2)$ and $\Gamma_2(t)=1+it$. On $\Gamma_0$, $|z|\asymp_\beta 1+|t|$ and the piecewise-$C^1$ speed is bounded. Both $z$ and $-z$ have real parts of absolute value less than $\sigma/8$, so all evaluations below lie strictly inside $D_\sigma$. By [F1–F2], $1/\zeta(1+z)$ is holomorphic on the regions between $\Gamma_0$ and $\Gamma_2$, with its removable value zero at $z=0$, and is bounded there by $C\log(|\operatorname{Im}z|+2)$. The whole shifted region has $\operatorname{Re}(1+z)\le2$, as required by the low-height clause of [F2]. Thus on $\Gamma_0$, $|K(z)|\ll_\sigma \log^2(|t|+2)(1+|t|)^{-4}$, since $1-z$ is also in the narrower zero-free region. In particular $K\in L^1(\Gamma_0)$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix all coordinates except the first. The first-coordinate integral exists by the one-variable majorant, with bound $A W_1\prod_{j>1}w_j$. Its truncations have tail at most $A V_1(N)\prod_{j>1}w_j$, uniformly on every compact rectangle of the remaining variables because those weights are bounded there. A proper truncated integral is continuous in its remaining parameters: on a slightly larger fixed compact rectangle, uniform continuity in [F6] bounds its change by the integration volume times its supremum change. The uniform tail then makes the improper section integral continuous locally. It has the same product majorant in dimension $d-1$. Induct on $d$ to integrate these remaining coordinates. The cost of replacing the inner infinite line by $[-N,N]$ is at most $A V_1(N)\prod_{j>1}W_j$, and the cost of truncating the other coordinates is bounded by the other terms of step 1.1. Thus every iterated integral equals the canonical joint limit by finite Fubini and these explicit tails. Permuting the coordinate labels proves every order. On a compact external parameter rectangle the identical argument with a uniform $A$ gives continuity, using uniform continuity on the product of two finite rectangles. Half-lines are identical with $[0,N]$ in place of $[-N,N]$; endpoint values have no effect on the integrals. [F6, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 For each fixed $A>1$, $B\ge0$, splitting the parameter integral at $T=e^{\sqrt L}$ gives $$\int_{\Gamma_0}R^{\operatorname{Re}z}\log^B(|z|+2)|z|^{-A}|dz|\ll_{A,B,\sigma}e^{-d_{A,B,\sigma}\sqrt L}.$$ Indeed below $T$, $R^{-a(t)}\le\exp(-\beta L/\log(T+2))$, whereas the remaining weight has a bounded integral over the entire line. Above $T$, discard $R^{-a(t)}\le1$ and bound the tail by $C T^{1-A}(1+\log T)^B$. Reducing the positive decay constant absorbs this polynomial. On $\Gamma_1$, $R^{\operatorname{Re}z}=e$ and $$\int_{\Gamma_1}\log^B(|z|+2)|z|^{-2}|dz|\ll_B L,$$ by splitting at $|t|=L^{-1},1$. Also $|\zeta(1+z+z')|\le\zeta(1+2/L)\le1+L/2$ on $\Gamma_1^2$, by the series and integral comparison. These bounds and step 1.2 prove absolute convergence there, with total absolute kernel integral $O_\sigma((1+L)^3)$. The same argument proves absolute convergence on $\Gamma_1\times\Gamma_2$ and $\Gamma_0\times\Gamma_2$, since their numerator zeta arguments have real parts bounded away from $1$. [F2, F4, step 1.2, given, algebra]

3.1 For the tuple series, on each $Q_N$ the continuous $w$ is bounded, so $\sum_nf_n$ converges uniformly by the numerical tail $\sum_{n>K}a_n$. The sum is continuous there, and its proper integral is the limit of the partial-sum integrals: linearity and the triangle inequality bound the difference by $\operatorname{vol}(Q_N)\sup_{Q_N}|\text{tail}|$. More usefully, proper comparison bounds it by $(\sum_{n>K}a_n)\prod_iW_i$, UNIFORMLY in $N$. Each partial sum has the canonical integral of step 1.1 and every iterated order of step 2.1. The same uniform estimate and the explicit cube tails let $K,N\to\infty$ in either order, proving the termwise integral assertion in every coordinate order. The argument works with the finite-shell enumeration of a divisor tuple or of multi-indices, since their numerical absolute majorants are summable. Piecewise-C1 contours with the sole corner at heightzero are split into their two smooth half-contours, with the appropriate orientation signs; a finite number of half-contour products then has exactly the continuous integrands and half-line bounds just proved. No arbitrary pointwise-convergence principle or Lebesgue product measure is used. [F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, given, algebra]

3.2 We need a bound that also covers cancellation of large imaginary parts on $\Gamma_0^2$. Write $z=-a(t)+it$, $z'=-a(t')+it'$, $S=2+|t|+|t'|$, $u=t+t'$, and $b=a(t)+a(t')$. Then $2\beta/\log S\le b\le2\beta/\log2<1/32$, and $\operatorname{Re}(1+z+z')=1-b$. If $|u|\le1$, [F4] and the bounded fractional-part integral give $|\zeta(1-b+iu)|\ll1+b^{-1}\ll_\sigma\log S$. If $|u|>1$, apply [F3] with $X=\lfloor|u|\rfloor+2\ll S$. Since $b^{-1}\ll_\sigma\log S$ and $X^b\ll S^{2\beta/\log2}$, it gives $|\zeta(1+z+z')|\ll_\sigma S^{1/4}$. In the first case absorb $\log S$ into $S^{1/4}$ as well. Now $S^{1/4}\ll(1+|t|)^{1/4}(1+|t'|)^{1/4}$. The absolute double-contour integrand is therefore bounded by $$CM\prod_{v\in\{t,t'\}}R^{-a(v)}\log(|v|+2)(1+|v|)^{-7/4}.$$ Step 2.2 bounds its integral by $CM e^{-d\sqrt L}$. This proves both absolute convergence and the required smallness without assuming $t+t'$ is large. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

3.3 Fix $z\in\Gamma_1$. Shift the $z'$ contour from $\Gamma_1$ to $\Gamma_2$. There are no poles: the numerator pole $z'=-z$ and the possible denominator pole $z'=0$ lie to the left. The horizontal pieces tend to zero, since the numerator is bounded by $\zeta(1+2/L)$ and their integrands are $O_{z,R}(M\log T/T^2)$ at heights $\pm T$. Next, for fixed $z'\in\Gamma_2$, shift $z$ from $\Gamma_1$ to $\Gamma_0$. Its numerator has real part at least $2-\beta/\log2>3/2$ throughout the shift. The only enclosed pole is $z=0$, simple because $1/\zeta(1+z)=z+O(z^2)$ from [F4]. Its residue is $f(0,z')R^{z'}/(z')^2$. The horizontal integrals again tend to zero by the $O(\log T/T^2)$ bound. The finite curved rectangles are admissible cycles inside the zero-free region; the corners of $\Gamma_0$ at height zero cause no difficulty. All the resulting double integrals are absolutely convergent by step 2.2, so the restricted calculus of the restricted Riemann argument, using [F6], permits integration of these fixed-parameter identities. Hence the original integral is $I_1+I_2$, where $$I_1=\frac1{2\pi i}\int_{\Gamma_2}f(0,z')\frac{R^{z'}}{(z')^2}\,dz',$$ and $I_2$ is the original double integrand integrated over $\Gamma_0\times\Gamma_2$. [F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Shift $I_1$ to $\Gamma_0$. Its double pole at zero has residue $L f(0,0)+\partial_{z'}f(0,0)$; its remaining integral has absolute value $\le CM e^{-d\sqrt L}$ by step 2.2 with $A=2$. For $I_2$, first fix $z\in\Gamma_0$, and only then shift $z'$ from $\Gamma_2$ to $\Gamma_0$, using the deterministic heights $T_n=\lceil|\operatorname{Im}z|\rceil+2+n$ tending to infinity. The enclosed poles are $z'=0$ and $z'=-z$, with respective residues $$f(z,0)R^z/z^2,\qquad f(z,-z)K(z).$$ Here the first residue uses cancellation of $\zeta(1+z)$ against its reciprocal, and the second uses the residue one of zeta and $(-z)^2=z^2$. For this fixed $z$, the horizontal integrals vanish: for large $T$, the numerator has imaginary part of size $T$ and real part in a fixed positive strip, and [F3–F4] bound it by $O_{z,R}(T^{1/4}\log T)$ on the portion to the left of $1$, and by $O_{z,R}(\log T)$ on or to the right of $1$. The latter bound follows from the finite-sum identity in [F3], obtained directly from [F4], with $X=\lfloor T\rfloor+2$; for real part at least $1$, $\sum_{n\le X}n^{-s}=O(\log X)$, the pole term is bounded, and the tail is $O(1)$. The resulting $T^{-2}$ denominator makes these horizontal integrals vanish. Crucially this is a fixed-$z$ argument, not a uniform shift across the moving pole. Only after taking that limit do we integrate over $z\in\Gamma_0$. The three resulting pieces are absolutely integrable: step 2.2 bounds the first residue by $CM e^{-d\sqrt L}$, step 1.2 bounds the second by $CM$, and step 3.2 bounds the remaining double integral by $CM e^{-d\sqrt L}$. Thus the restricted Riemann argument, using [F6], justify the integrated identity and gives the stated formula and linear error bound. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 3.3, algebra]

5.1 The disk $|z'|<\sigma/2$ with $z=0$ is strictly inside the supplied strip. Cauchy's estimate on its interior circle $|z'|=\sigma/4$ gives $|\partial_{z'}f(0,0)|\le4M/\sigma$. Combining this with the $L^1$ kernel bound proves $|Tf|\le CM$. This uses a genuinely available smaller domain and no bound on the global derivative of $f$. The same argument works uniformly in external parameters $u$. [F7, step 1.2, step 4.1, given, algebra]

6.1 To verify joint holomorphy rather than assume an interchange of derivatives and an improper contour, fix $u_0\in\Omega$ and a polydisc whose closure lies inside a larger polydisc in $\Omega$. For each $z\in\Gamma_0$, expand $F(u,z,-z)$ in $u-u_0$ by [F7], choosing coefficient circles on the larger polydisc. The coefficient bound is $M\prod r_i^{-\alpha_i}$ uniformly in $z$. Integrating these coefficients against $K(z)\,dz/(2\pi i)$ gives coefficients bounded by $CM\prod r_i^{-\alpha_i}$. On every smaller polydisc the geometric series of these bounds is summable, so the numerical-geometric majorant and step 3.1, with [F6], exchange the series and the absolutely convergent contour integral. By [F7] the resulting series defines a jointly holomorphic function. The derivative-at-zero part of $TF$ has the same argument with a Cauchy circle of radius $\sigma/4$ in the consumed $z'$ variable; its coefficient bound is $4M\sigma^{-1}\prod r_i^{-\alpha_i}$. These same Cauchy coefficient formulas identify external derivatives with applying $T$ to the corresponding derivatives of $F$. If the latter are bounded through order $j+1$, their derivative-at-zero terms are bounded by $\|F\|_{C^{j+1}}$, and their contour terms by $C\|F\|_{C^j}$. This proves the asserted $C^{j+1}$-to-$C^j$ estimate. The case $q=0$ is step 5.1. Uniformity and linearity of the error follow directly from the three remainder integrals in step 4.1, completing the one-pair and parameter-operator assertions. [F6, F7, step 1.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 Every exchange in the preceding contour argument is an instance of the restricted Riemann argument. On $\Gamma_1$ its weights are $\log^B(2+|t|)/(L^{-2}+t^2)$; the numerator has the finite $O(1+L)$ bound. On $\Gamma_0$, the single residue uses $C\log^2(2+|t|)(1+|t|)^{-4}$, and the left double remainder uses the separable weights $C R^{-\beta/\log(2+|t|)}\log(2+|t|)(1+|t|)^{-7/4}$. On $\Gamma_0\times\Gamma_2$ the numerator has real part bounded away from1 and the same reciprocal and quadratic denominator weights apply. Each listed weight is continuous on its smooth half-line and has the explicit finite integral established above. For the moving-pole shift, the limit is taken at each fixed $z$ using the deterministic heights in step 4.1, and only the resulting identity, whose pieces have these majorants, is integrated. There is no unproved joint uniform horizontal-limit interchange. External Cauchy coefficients have a summable geometric numerical bound on a smaller polydisc, so their kernel-weighted series are covered by step 3.1 as well. [F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 For completeness prove the multivariable assertion directly from the established one-pair identity. Put $M=\|G\|_{C^0}$ and $I_0(c)=c$. The absolute one-pair $\Gamma_1^2$ kernel bound is $C_\sigma(1+L)^3$, so the full absolute integral is at most $C_{m,\sigma}M(1+L)^{3m}$ by step 7.1. Grouping by pairs is valid by the restricted Riemann calculus. We inductively prove the stronger estimate with final remainder $O_{m,\sigma}(M e^{-d_{m,\sigma}\sqrt L})$, without imposing a growth envelope during this induction. Apply the one-pair identity to the last pair, with evaluation operator $E_m$ and residue operator $T_m$. Uniformity of its linear-C0 error and the remaining absolute kernel bounds give $$I_m(G)=L I_{m-1}(E_mG)+I_{m-1}(T_mG)+O_{m,\sigma}(M(1+L)^{3(m-1)}e^{-d\sqrt L}).$$ The functions are holomorphic on the exact remaining strip, with $\|E_mG\|_{C^j}\le\|G\|_{C^j}$, $\|T_mG\|_{C^0}\le CM$, and $\|T_mG\|_{C^j}\le C\|G\|_{C^{j+1}}$. These are the proved interior Cauchy/operator bounds; no higher-derivative growth envelope is imposed. Both meet the induction hypotheses through order $m-1$, and $\sigma<1/(6m)$ implies the bound for $m-1$ when $m>1$. [F6, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, given, algebra]

9.1 The $L I_{m-1}(E_mG)$ term has leading part $G(0)L^m$ and errors $O(\|G\|_{C^j}L^{m-j})$ for $1\le j\le m-1$. The leading part of the other term is $O(\|G\|_{C^1}L^{m-1})$, while its $j$th error is $O(\|G\|_{C^{j+1}}L^{m-1-j})$, $1\le j\le m-1$. These give exactly the displayed derivative-error orders. For $m=1$ this is just the one-pair identity. All induction remainders use only $M$ or $CM$; multiplying by any fixed polynomial in $L$ is absorbed by decreasing the positive exponent constant in $e^{-d\sqrt L}$. Thus the stronger linear-$M$ remainder assertion holds for every $m$. Finally $M\le e^{C_G L^{1/3}}$ and $C_G L^{1/3}\le(d/2)\sqrt L$ for sufficiently large $L$; enlarging the implicit constant covers $L\ge\log3$. This gives the claimed remainder $O_{m,\sigma,C_G}(e^{-\delta\sqrt L})$ with $\delta>0$ independent of $C_G$. Every operation used explicit finite, canonical-tail, or uniform numerical-series limits. This proves the original C0-envelope asymptotic without a choice premise. [step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, given, algebra] ∎
