## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative Noetherian ring and let $A$ be a commutative $R$-algebra of finite type. Then $A$ is a Noetherian ring. The zero ring is included.

## Statement references

[J. S. Milne, A Primer of Commutative Algebra, §3, Theorem 3.7](https://www.jmilne.org/math/xnotes/CA.pdf)

[Altman–Kleiman, A Term of Commutative Algebra, §16.12](https://web.mit.edu/18.705/www/13Ed.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative Noetherian ring $R$ and a commutative finite-type $R$-algebra $A$.

[F1] Noetherian means every submodule of the regular module is finitely generated; since ideals of a commutative ring are exactly its submodules as a module over itself, every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated. [[def-noetherian-ring]] [[def-noetherian-module]] [[def-submodule]] [[def-commutative-ring]]

[F2] The polynomial ring $R[x]$ consists of finitely supported coefficient sequences, and multiplication by $x$ shifts each coefficient one place upward. Polynomial rings in finitely many variables are defined by iterating the one-variable construction. [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]] [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]

[F3] A finite-type algebra $A$ is generated by a finite list $a_1,\ldots,a_m$ as an $R$-algebra, so the evaluation map $R[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\to A$ sending $x_i$ to $a_i$ is surjective. [[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]

[F4] An ideal generated by a finite list consists of finite sums of multiples of those generators. [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]

## Proof

**Given:** The commutative Noetherian ring $R$ and finite-type $R$-algebra $A$ in the Statement.

1.1 Fix an ideal $I\subseteq R[x]$. For each $n\in\mathbb N$, let $L_n$ be the set of coefficients $a\in R$ for which there is $f=\sum_i a_ix^i\in I$ with $a_n=a$ and $a_i=0$ for every $i>n$. The zero polynomial witnesses $0\in L_n$. If $a,b\in L_n$ have witnesses $f,g$, then $f+g$ witnesses $a+b$ and $-f$ witnesses $-a$; for $r\in R$, $rf$ witnesses $ra$. Thus $L_n$ is an ideal of $R$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Multiplication of a witness by $x$ gives $L_n\subseteq L_{n+1}$. The union $L=\bigcup_{n\ge0}L_n$ is an ideal because the $L_n$ form an ascending chain. By [F1], choose finite generators $a_1,\ldots,a_s$ of $L$. Each generator lies in some $L_{n_j}$; the finite maximum $N=\max_j n_j$ gives $L=L_N$, and therefore $L_n=L_N$ for every $n\ge N$. If $L=0$, take $N=0$ and the empty generating list. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $L=0$, any nonzero $f\in I$ has a largest nonzero coefficient $c_d$, which belongs to $L_d\subseteq L$, a contradiction; hence $I=0$ is finitely generated. Otherwise, for each $0\le n\le N$, choose finite generators $a_{n1},\ldots,a_{nr_n}$ of $L_n$ by [F1], and for each generator choose a witnessing polynomial $f_{nj}\in I$ from the definition of $L_n$. This is a finite collection. We prove by induction on the largest nonzero coefficient index $d$ that every $f\in I$ is generated by these $f_{nj}$. The zero polynomial is immediate. For nonzero $f$, its coefficient $c_d$ at its largest nonzero index belongs to $L_d=L_m$, where $m=\min(d,N)$. Write $c_d=\sum_j r_j a_{mj}$ using [F4]. The polynomial $f-\sum_j r_jx^{d-m}f_{mj}$ lies in $I$; its coefficient at $x^d$ is zero and all coefficients above $d$ are zero, so it is zero or has smaller largest nonzero index. Induction proves the claim. Thus every ideal $I$ of $R[x]$ is finitely generated, and $R[x]$ is Noetherian by the same definition as in [F1]. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra, discharge-induction]

4.1 Inducting on the number of variables, $R[x_1,\ldots,x_m]$ is Noetherian: the case $m=0$ is $R$, and the passage from $m-1$ to $m$ applies steps 1.1–3.1 to the Noetherian coefficient ring $R[x_1,\ldots,x_{m-1}]$. [F2, step 3.1, construct, discharge-induction, step 1.1]

5.1 By [F3], choose a finite generator list for $A$ and its surjection $\varphi:R[x_1,\ldots,x_m]\to A$. For any ideal $J\subseteq A$, its preimage $\varphi^{-1}(J)$ is an ideal of the Noetherian polynomial ring by step 4.1, so choose finite generators $p_1,\ldots,p_t$ for that preimage. Their images generate $J$: every $a\in J$ has a preimage $p=\sum_jq_jp_j$, and applying $\varphi$ gives $a=\sum_j\varphi(q_j)\varphi(p_j)$. Hence every ideal of $A$ is finitely generated, which is exactly that $A$ is Noetherian. All choices above are finite; no choice axiom or ACC equivalence is used. [F1, F3, F4, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
