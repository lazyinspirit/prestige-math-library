## Definition

Assume AC ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) through the smooth-differential supplier. Let $X$ be a smooth $K$-scheme ([[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]]) with sheaf of relative differentials $\Omega_{X/K}$ ([[def-sheaf-relative-differentials]], [[thm-kahler-differentials-existence-presentation]]) and derivation sheaf $\operatorname{Der}_K(\mathcal O_X)$ ([[def-derivation-algebra]], [[cor-derivations-represented-by-differentials]]).
For a coherent ideal sheaf $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X$ ([[def-coherent-module-scheme]]) define the first derivative (extension) $\mathcal D(\mathcal I)$ intrinsically as the ideal generated by $\mathcal I$ and all $D(f)$, for local sections $f$ of $\mathcal I$ and $K$-derivations $D$. The smooth-differential theorem [[thm-differentials-smooth-locally-free]] makes $\Omega_{X/K}$ finite locally free. Choose functions $u_1,\ldots,u_n$ whose differentials form a basis on a neighbourhood and let $\partial/\partial u_i$ be the dual derivations. Here $n$ is the relative dimension over $K$, which need not equal the local-ring dimension at a nonclosed point. For generators $f_1,\ldots,f_s$ of $\mathcal I$, the same ideal is generated by the $f_j$ and $\partial f_j/\partial u_i$: Leibniz reduces derivatives of arbitrary $\sum a_jf_j$ to these generators, and every derivation is a linear combination of the dual basis. This finite generator list proves coherence and independence of generators and differential coordinates.
Set $\mathcal D^0(\mathcal I):=\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal D^i(\mathcal I):=\mathcal D(\mathcal D^{i-1}(\mathcal I))$.
The ideal is generated by $f_j$ and their derivatives of order at most $i$ and is independent of the chosen generators and coordinates; for a marked ideal put $\mathcal D^i(\mathcal I,\mu):=(\mathcal D^i(\mathcal I),\mu-i)$ for $0\le i\le\mu$.
In characteristic $p>0$, ordinary derivations need not lower order: on $\mathbb A^1_K$ for a field $K$ of characteristic $p$, the ideal $\mathcal I=(x^p)$ satisfies $\mathcal D(\mathcal I)=\mathcal I$.
