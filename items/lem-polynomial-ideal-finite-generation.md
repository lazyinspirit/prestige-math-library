## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $\mathfrak a$ be an ideal of
$R[x]$. Let $\mathfrak a_0\subseteq\mathfrak a_1\subseteq\cdots$ be the stage
ideals of [[lem-leading-coefficient-ideals]] and let $N\in\mathbb N$ be an index
at which that chain stabilises, so $\mathfrak a_n=\mathfrak a_N$ for every
$n\ge N$. For each $n\le N$ choose finitely many nonzero elements
$c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,m_n}$ of $\mathfrak a_n$ generating it, and for each of
them a polynomial $g_{n,j}\in\mathfrak a$, nonzero of degree $n$, with
$\operatorname{lc}(g_{n,j})=c_{n,j}$.

Then the finitely many polynomials $g_{n,j}$, for $n\le N$ and $1\le j\le m_n$,
generate $\mathfrak a$ as an ideal of $R[x]$. In particular every ideal of
$R[x]$ is finitely generated.

The selections are possible: each $\mathfrak a_n$ is an ideal of the Noetherian
ring $R$, hence has a finite generating set, from which the zero element may be
discarded without loss, and every nonzero element of $\mathfrak a_n$ is by
definition the leading coefficient of some nonzero degree-$n$ element of
$\mathfrak a$. Only finitely many selections are made, so no choice axiom is
used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$, an ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $R[x]$, and the stage ideals $\mathfrak a_n$ for $n\in\mathbb N$.

[L1] For a commutative ring, being Noetherian is equivalent to every ideal being finitely generated, and to every ascending chain of ideals indexed by $\mathbb N$ stabilising ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[L2] For an ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $R[x]$ and $n\in\mathbb N$, the set $\mathfrak a_n$ of leading coefficients of the nonzero degree-$n$ elements of $\mathfrak a$, together with $0$, is an ideal of $R$, and $\mathfrak a_n\subseteq\mathfrak a_{n+1}$ ([[lem-leading-coefficient-ideals]]).

[L3] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$; the empty sum is included and equals $0$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L4] With $\mathfrak a_n=(c_1,\ldots,c_m)$ realised at stage $n$ by $g_1,\ldots,g_m\in\mathfrak a$, every nonzero $f\in\mathfrak a$ of degree $d\ge n$ with $\operatorname{lc}(f)\in\mathfrak a_n$ admits an $h$ in the ideal generated by $g_1,\ldots,g_m$ with $f-h=0$ or $\deg(f-h)<d$ ([[lem-hilbert-basis-degree-cancellation]]).

[L5] For $0\ne f\in R[x]$ the degree is the largest index carrying a nonzero coefficient and the leading coefficient is the coefficient there; the zero polynomial has no degree ([[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]]).

[L6] Every nonempty subset $S\subseteq\mathbb N$ has a least element: there is $\ell\in S$ with $\ell\le s$ for all $s\in S$ ([[thm-well-ordering-principle]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** contradiction.

1.1 The stage ideals form an ascending chain of ideals of $R$ indexed by $\mathbb N$, and $R$ is Noetherian, so that chain stabilises: fix $N\in\mathbb N$ with $\mathfrak a_n=\mathfrak a_N$ for every $n\ge N$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 For each $n\le N$ the ideal $\mathfrak a_n$ of $R$ is finitely generated; discard the zero element from a finite generating set, which changes nothing it generates, and realise each remaining generator $c_{n,j}$ by a nonzero $g_{n,j}\in\mathfrak a$ of degree $n$. This is a selection over the finitely many pairs $(n,j)$ with $n\le N$, so it is a finite selection. Let $\mathfrak b$ be the ideal of $R[x]$ generated by all the $g_{n,j}$; since every $g_{n,j}$ lies in $\mathfrak a$, we have $\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathfrak a$. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Suppose $\mathfrak b\neq\mathfrak a$, so that $\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak b$ is nonempty. The zero polynomial lies in $\mathfrak b$, so every element of $\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak b$ is nonzero and therefore has a degree; the set of those degrees is a nonempty subset of $\mathbb N$ and so has a least element $d$. Fix $f\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak b$ with $\deg f=d$. [assume-contra, L3, L5, L6, step 2.1]

4.1 Put $n=\min(d,N)$, so $n\le N$ and $d\ge n$. If $d\le N$ then $n=d$ and $\operatorname{lc}(f)$ lies in $\mathfrak a_d=\mathfrak a_n$ by the definition of the stage; if $d>N$ then $n=N$ and $\operatorname{lc}(f)\in\mathfrak a_d=\mathfrak a_N=\mathfrak a_n$ by the stabilisation of step 1.1. The polynomials $g_{n,1},\ldots,g_{n,m_n}$ realise generators of $\mathfrak a_n$ at stage $n$, so the cancellation lemma applies and yields $h$ in the ideal generated by them, hence $h\in\mathfrak b$, with $f-h=0$ or $\deg(f-h)<d$. [L2, L4, L5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Both alternatives are impossible. If $f-h=0$ then $f=h\in\mathfrak b$, contradicting $f\in\mathfrak a\setminus\mathfrak b$. If $f-h\ne0$ with $\deg(f-h)<d$, then $f-h$ lies in $\mathfrak a$ and not in $\mathfrak b$, since $h\in\mathfrak b$ and $f\notin\mathfrak b$, so its degree belongs to the set whose least element is $d$, contradicting $\deg(f-h)<d$. Therefore $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak b$, and $\mathfrak a$ is generated by the finitely many $g_{n,j}$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, discharge-contradiction] ∎

## Remarks

- **The generating list is not canonical.** It depends on the stabilisation index $N$, on the finite generating sets chosen for the stages, and on the realisers; a larger $N$ or a larger generating set produces a longer list that generates the same ideal. Nothing above claims minimality.

- **Where the Noetherian hypothesis is used, and where it is not.** It is used twice, in step 1.1 for the stabilisation of the chain and in step 2.1 for finite generation of each stage. [[lem-leading-coefficient-ideals]] and [[lem-hilbert-basis-degree-cancellation]] hold over any commutative ring; only the passage from "one cancellation step" to "a finite generating list" needs the chain condition.

- **The minimal-degree argument replaces an induction that would not terminate on its own.** A single cancellation step lowers the degree, but iterating it from an arbitrary element gives no bound; choosing a counterexample of least degree turns one step into a contradiction.
