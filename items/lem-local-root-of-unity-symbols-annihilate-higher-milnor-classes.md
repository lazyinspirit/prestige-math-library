## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field with finite residue field, let $\ell$ be a prime, and let $\xi\in F^\times$ satisfy $\xi^\ell=1$. For every integer $n\ge2$,
$$\{\xi\}\cdot K_n^M(F)=0\quad\text{in }K_{n+1}^M(F).$$
This includes $\ell=2$ and both possible characteristics of $F$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the field, prime, root and choice assumptions in the Statement.

[F1] Milnor symbols are multilinear and anticommute; $\{a,a\}=\{a,-1\}$ and $\{a,-a\}=0$. Higher Milnor groups are generated by products of degree-two symbols with remaining degree-one entries. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]] [[lem-milnor-symbol-anticommutativity-and-repeated-entry-identities]]

[F2] The group $\mu(F)$ is finite cyclic. Moore has a divisible kernel and cyclic quotient $\mu(F)$; hence $G/\ell G$, for $G=K_2^M(F)$, is cyclic of order $\gcd(\ell,|\mu(F)|)$ by taking that quotient. [[thm-local-field-k2-moore-sequence-and-divisible-kernel]] [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F3] When $\mu_\ell\subset F$ and $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}F$, the actual canonical Hilbert pairing has trivial left radical modulo $\ell$th powers and induces a Steinberg map $H_\ell:G\to\mu_\ell$. [[thm-local-hilbert-symbol-nondegenerate-pairing]] [[lem-local-roots-of-unity-and-hilbert-milnor-bridge]]

[F4] Finite abelian local extensions have onto norm on $K_2$, and canonical transfer satisfies projection. [[lem-finite-abelian-local-milnor-k-norms-are-surjective]] [[thm-bass-tate-kato-norm-transfer-for-milnor-k-groups]]

[F5] Finite extensions of a finite-residue complete discretely valued field are again such fields under AC. [[lem-complete-discrete-valuation-extension-degree-and-tame-norm]] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]

[A1] AC and DC remain literal in [F2]–[F5], including the canonical Hilbert and transfer branches. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 If $\xi=1$, its degree-one class is zero and the Statement follows. In particular this covers characteristic $\ell$, where $(X-1)^\ell=X^\ell-1$. Otherwise $\xi$ has order $\ell$, and let $\zeta$ generate the maximal $\ell$-primary roots, of order $\ell^s$ with $s\ge1$. Every $\xi$ is $\zeta^{c\ell^{s-1}}$ for an integer $c$. A primitive root of maximal order is not an $\ell$th power, since any such root would have order $\ell^{s+1}$. By [F2], $G/\ell G$ is cyclic of order $\ell$. Multiplication by $\{\xi\}$ kills $\ell G$, since $\ell\{\xi\}=\{\xi^\ell\}=0$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Suppose $\ell$ is odd. Since $-1=(-1)^\ell$, the element $-\zeta$ is not an $\ell$th power. By [F3] there is $b$ such that $z=\{-\zeta,b\}$ has nontrivial $H_\ell$ image, so $z$ generates $G/\ell G$. The exact identity $\{\zeta,-\zeta\}=0$ gives $$\{\xi\}z=c\ell^{s-1}\{\zeta,-\zeta,b\}=0.$$ Together with $\{\xi\}\ell G=0$ this proves annihilation of $G$ in this case. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

2.2 Suppose $\ell=2$ and $s\ge2$. Then $\xi=-1$. Choose $b$ such that $z=\{\zeta,b\}$ generates $G/2G$, using [F3] and the fact that $\zeta$ is not a square. Since $-1=\zeta^{2^{s-1}}$ and $\{\zeta,-1\}$ is killed by $2$, [F1] gives $$\{-1,\zeta\}=2^{s-1}\{\zeta,\zeta\}=2^{s-1}\{\zeta,-1\}=0.$$ Consequently $\{-1\}z=0$. Step 1.1 again shows that $\{-1\}$ kills all of $G$. [A1, F1, F3, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $\ell=2$ and $s=1$. Then $i^2=-1$ has no root in $F$, since such a root would have order $4$. As $\operatorname{char}F\ne2$, the extension $E=F(i)$ is quadratic Galois and abelian. It is local by [F5], and its maximal $2$-power root order is at least $4$. Thus the already proved step 2.2, applied over $E$, says that $\{-1\}K_2^M(E)=0$. For $x\in G$, choose $y\in K_2^M(E)$ with $Ny=x$ by [F4]. Projection gives $$\{-1\}x=N_{E/F}\bigl(\{-1\}y\bigr)=0.$$ There is no induction on a tower here: step 2.2 proves all fields with $s\ge2$ before this separate remaining case. It applies also when the residue characteristic is $2$. [A1, F4, F5, step 2.2, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 Each generator of $K_n^M(F)$ for $n\ge2$ is a product of a $K_2$ symbol with the remaining entries. The established degree-two annihilation, multiplied by those entries, therefore kills every such generator and every finite sum. This proves the Statement. No assertion about $K_2(F)[\ell]$ or torsion in a divisible kernel was used. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
