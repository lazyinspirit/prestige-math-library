## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $k\subseteq K,L$ be number fields in a common algebraic closure and put $M=KL$. The relative different satisfies
$$\mathfrak D_{M/K}\mid\mathfrak D_{L/k}\mathcal O_M.$$
For absolute discriminants $D_F=|\operatorname{disc}(F/\mathbb Q)|$ this implies
$$D_M\le D_K^{[M:K]}D_L^{[M:L]}.$$
In particular, if $K_1,\dots,K_r$ are number fields, $M=K_1\cdots K_r$, and $n_i=[K_i:\mathbb Q]$, then
$$D_M\le\prod_{i=1}^rD_{K_i}^{[M:\mathbb Q]/n_i}.$$
No linear-disjointness hypothesis is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The number fields and compositum in the Statement.

[F1] Under CC the ring of integers of every finite local extension with finite residue fields is generated by one integral element. Its monic minimal polynomial has coefficients in the base valuation ring. [[lem-local-integers-of-finite-separable-local-extension-are-monogenic]]

[F2] In a finite $p$-adic extension generated integrally by $\alpha$, the different is generated by the derivative of its monic minimal polynomial at $\alpha$. Different ideals are integral and transitive in finite local towers. [[lem-choice-free-finite-padic-different-and-base-change-calculus]]

[F3] Finite global extensions decompose after completion into their local field factors, and relative discriminant exponents are sums of local different exponents weighted by residue degrees. Global different/discriminant ideals are integral and transitive. Ramification indices and residue degrees multiply in towers. [[thm-global-field-completion-tensor-product-decomposition]] [[lem-number-field-relative-discriminant-local-exponent]] [[thm-relative-discriminant-ideal-is-nonzero-integral-and-transitive]] [[thm-ramification-and-residue-degrees-in-towers]] [[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]]

[A1] Countable Choice is needed only at the local monogenic interface. [[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix a finite place $w$ of $M$ and let $u,v,t$ be its restrictions to $L,K,k$. The completion decomposition [F3] identifies $M_w$ with the compositum of the corresponding finite local extensions $K_v$ and $L_u$ inside a common algebraic closure of $k_t$. By [F1] choose $\alpha$ with $\mathcal O_{L_u}=\mathcal O_{k_t}[\alpha]$, and write its monic minimal polynomial as $f\in\mathcal O_{k_t}[X]$. Since $M_w=K_v(\alpha)$, let $g\in K_v[X]$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $K_v$. It divides $f$ in $K_v[X]$. Both monic factors $g$ and $h=f/g$ have integral coefficients: their roots are roots of $f$, hence integral, and their coefficients are symmetric sums of integral roots lying in $K_v$. The element $\alpha$ is integral over $\mathcal O_{K_v}$, and the different of $M_w/K_v$ divides $g'(\alpha)\mathcal O_{M_w}$ by the standard trace-dual power-basis calculation: write $(g(T)-g(\alpha))/(T-\alpha)=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b_iT^i$ with $b_i\in\mathcal O_{M_w}$. Finite Lagrange interpolation over the distinct conjugates $\alpha_j$ gives $\sum_j\alpha_j^k b_i(\alpha_j)/g'(\alpha_j)=\delta_{ik}$ for $0\le i,k<d$, so the trace-dual basis is $b_i(\alpha)/g'(\alpha)$. Hence the order's trace dual lies in $g'(\alpha)^{-1}\mathcal O_{M_w}$. The full integral ring contains the order, so its codifferent is contained in that trace dual; inversion of these principal fractional ideals gives the divisibility. By [F2], $f'(\alpha)$ generates $\mathfrak D_{L_u/k_t}$, while $f'(\alpha)=g'(\alpha)h(\alpha)$ and $h(\alpha)$ is integral. Therefore $$\mathfrak D_{M_w/K_v}\mid g'(\alpha)\mathcal O_{M_w}\mid f'(\alpha)\mathcal O_{M_w}=\mathfrak D_{L_u/k_t}\mathcal O_{M_w}.$$ This argument applies also when $[M_w:K_v]<[L_u:k_t]$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra]

2.1 The local different inequality of step 1.1 holds at every finite place $w$ of $M$. Different ideals are determined by their valuations at finite primes, so these inequalities yield the global ideal divisibility in the Statement, including ramified primes and all local degree drops. Taking norms to $\mathbb Q$ and using different transitivity [F3] in the towers $\mathbb Q\subseteq K\subseteq M$ and $\mathbb Q\subseteq L\subseteq M$ gives $D_M=D_K^{[M:K]}N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{M/K}$ and bounds the second factor by $D_L^{[M:L]}$: the norm of the extended different has absolute norm $D_L^{[M:L]}$ after including the residue-degree weights. Thus the displayed two-field inequality follows. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Apply step 2.1 iteratively to $M_j=K_1\cdots K_j$. Raising the earlier bound to $[M_j:M_{j-1}]$ multiplies each existing exponent to $[M_j:K_i]=[M_j:\mathbb Q]/n_i$, while the new factor has exponent $[M_j:K_j]$. Induction on the finite number of fields gives the final inequality. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎
