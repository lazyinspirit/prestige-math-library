## Definition

Let $X$ be a scheme and let $F$ be an $\mathcal O_X$-module, for instance a
quasi-coherent sheaf ([[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]). The **evaluation
map** of $F$ is the morphism of $\mathcal O_X$-modules
$$\operatorname{ev}_F:\Gamma(X,F)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_X \longrightarrow F,\qquad f\otimes g\longmapsto g\cdot f|_U\ \text{ on } U\subseteq X,$$ where the tensor product is taken over $\mathbb Z$ because the global sections $\Gamma(X,F)$ are only an abelian group in general and the sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ carries the $\mathcal O_X$-module structure. Concretely, on an open $U$ the map sends a finite sum $\sum_i f_i\otimes g_i$ with $f_i\in\Gamma(X,F)$, $g_i\in\Gamma(U,\mathcal O_X)$ to $\sum_i g_i\cdot f_i|_U$. The sheaf $F$ is **globally generated** when $\operatorname{ev}_F$ is surjective. Equivalently, for every point $x\in X$ the images of the global sections generate the stalk $F_x$ as an $\mathcal O_{X,x}$-module: passing to stalks, $(\operatorname{ev}_F)_x$ is the map $\Gamma(X,F)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_{X,x}\to F_x$, and a morphism of sheaves is surjective exactly when it is surjective on every stalk. For an **invertible** sheaf $L$ the condition takes the following form ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]): if there are finitely many global sections $s_0,\dots,s_n\in\Gamma(X,L)$ such that the induced morphism $$\mathcal O_X^{\,n+1}\longrightarrow L,\qquad (g_0,\dots,g_n)\longmapsto \sum_i g_i s_i,$$ is surjective, then $L$ is globally generated, since this morphism factors the evaluation map. The converse holds when $X$ is quasi-compact: see the Remarks.

## Remarks

- **Finiteness on a quasi-compact scheme.** Let $L$ be invertible on a quasi-compact scheme $X$ and suppose $L$ is globally generated. Then there exist finitely many global sections $s_0,\dots,s_n$ of $L$ with $\mathcal O_X^{n+1}\to L$ surjective. For each global section $t$, let $X_t$ be the locus where its germ generates $L$. In a local frame, $t$ has coefficient $f$, and this locus is where $f_x$ is a unit. A germ inverse extends to a neighbourhood and its product with $f$ equals $1$ after shrinking, so $X_t$ is open. These opens, indexed by all global sections, cover $X$: if every section had coefficient in the maximal ideal at some $x$, their germs could not generate the free rank-one stalk $L_x$. Quasi-compactness gives a finite subcover $X_{s_0},\dots,X_{s_n}$, and the corresponding sections generate at every stalk. This proves the asserted surjectivity. If $X$ is empty, take the single zero section. The argument uses the local freeness of $L$, not a general openness assertion for surjectivity loci.
- **Terminology.** The phrase *generated by global sections* is used interchangeably with *globally generated*.
- **Relation to ampleness.** Ampleness, defined at [[def-ample-invertible-sheaf]], is not the same condition as global generation, in either direction. The interaction is a theorem, not a convention: under the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), on a Noetherian scheme an invertible sheaf is ample exactly when every coherent sheaf twisted by a sufficiently high power is globally generated, so in particular high powers of an ample invertible sheaf are globally generated; the precise statement is [[thm-serre-criterion-ampleness]].
