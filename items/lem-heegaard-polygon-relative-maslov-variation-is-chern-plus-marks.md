## Statement

Assume countable choice and the finite balanced polygon Morse data, with $q\ge3$, $m=h+k-1$, and polygon cobordism $W$. Let $u,v$ be actual Whitney polygons with the same corner generators and the same assigned Spin-c structure $\mathfrak s$ on $W$. Put $P=D(u)-D(v)$. Fix identical real-plane corner caps for the two maps and compute their relative Maslov number as the difference of the squared determinant boundary windings. Then
$$\mu_{\rm rel}(u,v)=\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(P)\rangle+2\sum_l n_{w_l}(P).$$
If every marked multiplicity agrees, $P$ is a sum of normalized adjacent doubly-periodic domains, and the Chern term is equivalently the sum of the Chern evaluations of the restricted Spin-c structures on their actual capped pair classes. That sum is independent of the chosen decomposition. The formula applies also for genus one and two, for multiple marks, and for zero or negative realized indices. It is compatible with insertion and gluing along fixed corner caps.

The two-edge case is included separately: for actual strips with fixed corner generators in one boundary-pair Spin-c sector, take $W=Y\times[0,1]$ and its pulled-back Spin-c structure. Then every formal pair-periodic domain lies in $L$, and the same formula computes the difference between the two actual strip indices, even when the incoming and outgoing generators differ.

For actual finite-ended polygon Cauchy–Riemann operators in the standard symmetric-product complex bundle, or with an actual supplied comparison homotopy to it preserving the totally real boundary tori and specified transverse corner caps, this is also their real Fredholm-index difference. The protected nearly symmetric tame class has this comparison homotopy. Coefficients may tend to those models with the derivatives needed in their integer Sobolev realization. Common conformal or external parameter columns cancel from the difference; an added actual parameter contributes its one extra index. Existence, regularity, compactness, gluing of nonlinear solution spaces, or finiteness of literal homotopy classes is not assumed or asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite balanced Morse data, two realized same-corner polygons with equal Spin-c label, and identical corner real-plane caps.

[F1] The actual finite polygon Spin-c construction gives $\mathfrak s(u)-\mathfrak s(v)=j\operatorname{PD}H(P)$. With equal labels and all $w$ degrees zero, $P$ is a sum of adjacent doubly-periodic domains normalized at all marks. A one-system cut component $A^i_l$ caps to zero and has $n_{w_j}(A^i_l)=\delta_{jl}$. [[lem-heegaard-polygon-spin-c-difference-is-the-capped-periodic-domain]]

[F2] Every realized periodic strip has actual Maslov number $\langle c_1(\mathfrak s_{\mathbf w}(\mathbf x)),H(Q)\rangle+2\sum_l n_{w_l}(Q)$. [[lem-realized-periodic-whitney-index-is-morse-chern-plus-marks]]

[F3] For $m\ge2$ an actual based sphere of domain $\Sigma$ exists. Its Maslov shift is $2k$, and every sphere of zero point-divisor degree has zero determinant Chern degree. [[lem-finite-heegaard-surface-polygon-supplies-unit-divisor-spheres]] [[lem-multipointed-symmetric-product-spheres-have-index-shift-two-k]]

[F4] For $k\ge2$ each one-system cut component has an actual based one-torus disk of that domain and Maslov number $2$, including negative integer multiples. [[lem-balanced-boundary-disk-domains-have-twice-component-sum-maslov-index]]

[F5] Actual domain edge jumps equal the labelled circle-path windings on each torus edge. The proof uses a finite ordered-root cover and applies to each edge separately. [[lem-equal-whitney-domains-have-equal-boundary-windings-and-fredholm-index]]

[F6] The symmetric quotient determinant is the product tangent determinant times the pair-diagonal line. Finite commutator polygons also give actual circle maps realizing the $2h$ integral coordinate functionals on $H_1(\Sigma)$. [[lem-symmetric-product-quotient-determinant-has-vandermonde-ramification]] [[lem-finite-polygon-surface-diagonal-degree-pairing]]

[F7] Actual finite-ended operators with the same transverse product end models have relative real Fredholm index equal to their compact-cap squared determinant winding difference, including asymptotic coefficients and higher integer Sobolev realizations. Finite parameter columns have their actual finite-dimensional index contributions. [[lem-finite-ended-cauchy-riemann-relative-index-is-compact-determinant-winding]]

## Proof

1.1 **The finite fundamental-group calculation needed for existence.** For $m\ge2$, the map moving one point of a divisor induces $\pi_1(\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma)=H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$. Here is the finite argument rather than an appeal to a homotopy classification. A smooth loop can avoid the codimension-two collision locus. Its ordered roots may be permuted at the end. Cancel that permutation by finitely many local exchanges along arcs between the roots; each exchange contracts in the symmetric product through the disk where its two roots coalesce. The resulting ordered loop is homotopic, in the product and then its quotient, to the successive motions of its individual roots. Change the base divisor along a finite point-moving path to $m\cdot p$. At that divisor every individual-root loop is the same loop, by permutation symmetry, and any two commute: the map $\Sigma\times\Sigma\to\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$ moving two separate roots provides the commutation square. A loop on the surface is a finite word in the polygon's $2h$ edges, by cutting it off a point in its face and retracting to those edges. Thus zero coordinate exponents give an actual finite null-homotopy after commuting and cancelling its letters. Conversely the product of any of the circle maps [F6] on all roots detects the corresponding exponent, so there are no extra relations among these abelian generators. This proves both the asserted fundamental group and the explicit zero-homology filling needed below, not any higher homotopy classification. [F6, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 **Equal-domain polygons have equal relative determinant number.** For two actual polygons of the same domain, [F5] gives zero relative winding on every labelled boundary-circle factor. Paths on each circle with the same endpoints and winding have an explicit real-angular homotopy relative endpoints; take their product on each torus edge and fix the corner caps. After these boundary homotopies glue one polygon to the oppositely oriented other polygon. This is an actual sphere, of point-divisor degree zero. Its determinant degree is zero by [F3]'s sphere calculation, which itself applies also to $m=1$. The two boundary determinant windings differ by twice precisely that degree: frame the determinant on the two disks, cancel the common edge and corner frames, and read the transition on their seam. Hence the relative Maslov number is zero. This conclusion uses actual maps and determinant lines, not injectivity of domains into homotopy classes. [F3, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Realize formal pair-periodic domains when $m\ge2$.** Given a pair-periodic domain $Q$ and a corner generator, trace the specified full-circle coefficients of $\partial Q$ along the two torus edges based at that generator, with their boundary signs. This loop has zero homology in $\Sigma$, because it is the boundary of the given region chain. Step1.1 fills it by an actual disk in the symmetric product. Its domain $Q'$ has exactly the same edge jumps by [F5], so $Q'-Q=c\Sigma$ for one integer $c$, since $\Sigma$ is connected. Attach $-c$ actual unit-divisor spheres from [F3]. The resulting actual periodic strip has domain exactly $Q$. Its index is therefore the value [F2]. This is existence only: the filling is not claimed unique, and no sphere action on genus-two classes is divided out. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **Insert the realized pair strips and the mark-normalizing disks.** Suppose first $m\ge2$. The same argument covers a strip between possibly different generators by taking $W=Y\times[0,1]$: its Spin-c structure is pulled back from the fixed sector, every capped pair class is in the image of a boundary copy of $Y$, and its $j\operatorname{PD}$ is zero. The normalized remainder below is then already a pair-periodic domain, so no polygon Spin-c construction is needed in this two-edge case. Put $b_l=n_{w_l}(P)$. Insert at one edge of $v$ the based one-torus disks $b_lA^0_l$ from [F4] if $k\ge2$. If $k=1$, use $b_1$ unit-divisor spheres instead, since $A^0_1=\Sigma$; their index is $2b_1$ by [F3]. These insertions change the domain by $\sum_l b_lA^0_l$, the relative determinant number by $2\sum_l b_l$, and the Spin-c structure by zero, since these domains cap to zero. Now [F1] decomposes the remaining difference into normalized adjacent pair domains $Q_i$. Realize each by step 2.1 at its corresponding corner and insert the resulting periodic strip. Determinant frames on the glued edges cancel, so the relative numbers add; this is elementary winding additivity, with no nonlinear gluing premise. Their Chern sum is $\langle c_1(\mathfrak s),H(P)\rangle$: each pair label is the restriction of the given $\mathfrak s$, its capped class maps into $W$, and all the $A^0_l$ cap to zero. The resulting polygon has exactly $D(u)$, so step 1.2 identifies its relative number with that of $u$. This proves the formula for $m\ge2$. Independence of decomposition follows also directly: a relation among the capped pair classes evaluates to zero under the one fixed cohomology class $c_1(\mathfrak s)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Treat $m=1$ without a nonexistent torus sphere.** Choose a small disk in one region of all attaching systems, disjoint from their marks. In it add, to each system, one small contractible attaching circle, all passing transversely through a common point $a$ and all enclosing one new mark $w'$. The circles are mutually transverse at $a$ and remain disjoint from their own old system. This is just an additional cancelling index-zero/index-one pair in each given handlebody; the handlebodies and $W$ are unchanged. The cut count becomes $k+1$ and the symmetric degree becomes $2$. Send a polygon to $u'=u+\{a\}$ and likewise for $v$. The extra root track and cancelling Morse modifications are supported in the region consisting of the polygon times that small surface disk and the edge products with its handlebody half-balls. This region is an actual four-ball: the polygon-disk product is a ball, and, after rounding and shortening the edge collars, each additional product half-ball is attached along one three-ball; absorb these finite product collars successively. Outside this ball the old and new field constructions agree. The finite lift-clutching calculation [F1] therefore identifies their Spin-c structures on $W$, since a ball and its boundary three-sphere have zero second cohomology. In particular the new common label is the original $\mathfrak s$, including any torsion information, and the Chern evaluation on $H(P)$ is unchanged. Apply step 3.1 to $u',v'$. The new mark contribution is $2n_{w'}(P)=2n_a(P)$, since the whole added disk lies in one old region. On the other hand [F6], restricted to $z\mapsto z+a$, says that the new relative boundary determinant has the original factor, a constant tangent factor at $a$, and the collision line $\mathcal O(a)$. The constant factor and its chosen corner caps cancel between $u'$ and $v'$, and the collision line contributes $2n_a(P)$ to the squared determinant winding. Subtracting exactly that contribution from step 3.1 proves the original degree-one formula. This uses a genuine degree-two calculation, not a claim that every normalized periodic domain on a torus is itself realized or that a unit-divisor torus sphere exists. [F1, F6, step 1.2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Gluing and the stated scope.** Fixed matching corner and torus frames cancel under every insertion or concatenation. The preceding computation therefore respects polygon gluing, with the Spin-c extension orbit already supplied in [F1]; it does not choose a unique extension. The point target has zero relative determinant winding and zero realized domain, so its assertion is immediate. For the stated actual finite-ended operators use their specified standard complex bundle or the actual boundary-preserving comparison homotopy. The latter preserves determinant winding; in the protected nearly symmetric tame class it is the Cayley contraction proved in [F4]. Then apply the proved common-cap relative-index identity [F7] to identify their index difference with the fixed-cap determinant difference just computed. Exact and asymptotic transverse end models and the higher integer Sobolev realizations are precisely those of [F7]. Common parameter columns cancel; an actually appended free parameter adds one by its finite-block calculation. Neither that application nor the finite topological constructions asserts transversality, compactness, holomorphic representativity, or finiteness of literal classes. [F1, F7, step 1.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
