## Statement

Let $\mathcal A$ be a sigma-algebra on $X$. If there is an injective sequence
$e:\mathbb N\to\mathcal A$, then there is a sequence $(D_n)_{n\in\mathbb N}$ of
pairwise disjoint nonempty members of $\mathcal A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A sigma-algebra $\mathcal A$ on $X$ and an injective sequence $e:\mathbb N\to\mathcal A$.

[L1] A sigma-algebra is closed under complements and countable unions, hence under finite Boolean operations ([[def-sigma-algebra]]).

[L2] A countably infinite set admits a bijective listing by $\mathbb N$ ([[def-countable]]).

[L3] A seed and a function determine a sequence by recursion on $\mathbb N$ ([[thm-recursion]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 Let $\mathcal B$ be the Boolean algebra generated by the sets $e(n)$. Finite Boolean expressions can be coded by natural numbers, so deleting repeated values in least-code order gives a listing of $\mathcal B$. It is infinite because it contains the distinct sets $e(n)$. [given, L1, L2, construct]

2.1 Call a nonempty $B\in\mathcal B$ an atom when it has no nonempty proper member in $\mathcal B$. If $\mathcal B$ has infinitely many atoms, list them in least-code order. Otherwise let $R_0$ be the complement of the union of its finitely many atoms. This complement is nonempty: if the atoms covered $X$, then intersecting any member of $\mathcal B$ with each atom would show that every member is a union of those finitely many atoms, contradicting that $\mathcal B$ is infinite. The set $R_0$ contains no atom. Given nonempty atomless $R_n\in\mathcal B$, take the least listed $B$ that splits it, put $D_n:=R_n\cap B$ and $R_{n+1}:=R_n\setminus B$, and use [L3] to continue. Both new sets are nonempty by the choice of $B$. [step 1.1, L1, L3, construct]

3.1 In the first case the listed atoms are pairwise disjoint nonempty members of $\mathcal A$. In the second, each $D_n\subseteq R_n$ is nonempty, $R_{n+1}$ is disjoint from $D_n$, and all later $D_m$ lie in $R_{n+1}$; hence the $D_n$ are pairwise disjoint members of $\mathcal A$. This constructs the required sequence without a choice principle. [step 2.1, discharge-construct] ∎
