## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and let $R=\mathbb Z[A^{\pm1}]$. Identify
the solid torus with $\mathcal A\times[-1,1]$, where $\mathcal A$ is a planar
annulus. A simple closed curve is called essential if it does not bound a disk
in $\mathcal A$. Every framed link in this product admits a regular annular
diagram and an integer framing correction from its blackboard framing. Let
$z$ be one blackboard-framed annular core curve, and for $n\geq1$ let $z^n$
denote $n$ parallel copies of that curve, with $z^0$ the empty link. In the polynomial
ring $R[z]$, $z$ is also the formal variable sent to the core class $[z]$.
Then the classes $[z^n]$, $n\geq0$, form an $R$-basis of
$\mathcal K_A(\mathcal A\times[-1,1])$. Equivalently, the map
$J:R[z]\to\mathcal K_A(\mathcal A\times[-1,1])$ sending $z^n$ to $[z^n]$ is
an $R$-module isomorphism.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the annulus-product model of the solid torus, and its framed-link skein module.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice, written $\mathrm{AC}$ ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[A2] Countable choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, means every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[A3] In ZF, $\mathrm{AC}$ implies $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The module quotient is generated by framed links and imposes the crossing and zero-framed trivial-circle relations ([[def-kauffman-bracket-skein-module-of-a-three-manifold]]).

[F2] Every framed link in $\mathcal A\times(-1,1)$ admits a regular annular diagram, with its framing correction recorded by signed full-twist marks ([[lem-framed-link-diagram-calculus]]).

[F3] The state expansion $\Psi$ is well-defined on the skein module and sends a diagram with twist correction $m$ to $(-A^3)^m\sum_S A^{a(S)-b(S)}\delta^{u(S)}z^{e(S)}$ ([[lem-annular-kauffman-bracket-state-expansion]]).

[F4] A positive or negative full framing twist on a component multiplies its skein class by $-A^3$ or $-A^{-3}$, respectively ([[lem-kauffman-bracket-module-framing-twist-factor]]).

## Proof


1.1 Define $J$ by the stated $R$-linear assignment. On $z^n$ take the crossingless annular diagram of $n$ parallel essential circles with blackboard framing. It has $m=0$, $a=b=u=0$, and $e=n$, so [F3] gives $\Psi(J(z^n))=z^n$. By linearity, $\Psi\circ J$ is the identity on $R[z]$, and hence $J$ is injective. [A1, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $L$ be any framed link in the solid torus. By [F2], choose a regular annular diagram $D$ and write $m_j$ for the framing correction on its $j$th component, with $m=\sum_jm_j$. Apply [F4] componentwise: each positive twist contributes $-A^3$, each negative twist contributes $-A^{-3}=(-A^3)^{-1}$, so $[L]=(-A^3)^m[D]$, where $D$ carries its blackboard framing. Induct on the number of crossings. At one crossing, [F1] replaces $D$ by its two smoothing diagrams with coefficients $A$ and $A^{-1}$, and each smoothing has one fewer crossing. Applying the induction hypothesis to each term yields $[D]=\sum_S A^{a(S)-b(S)}[D_S],$ where each $D_S$ is a crossingless annular state with blackboard framing. Every embedded state circle in the annulus either bounds a disk or is essential and cuts the annulus into two annuli, so it is isotopic to a core circle. For finitely many disjoint essential circles, cutting along them gives annular regions between consecutive circles; thus they are parallel copies of the core. Among the contractible circles choose one whose disk contains no other state circle; its disk has a small thickening disjoint from the remaining link, so the trivial-circle relation in [F1] removes it with factor $\delta$. Repeating removes all contractible circles. Therefore $[L]=(-A^3)^m\sum_S A^{a(S)-b(S)}\delta^{u(S)}[z^{e(S)}] =J(\Psi([L])),$ where the last equality is [F3]. Thus $J\circ\Psi$ is the identity on every link generator, and by $R$-linearity on the whole quotient. Together with step 1.1 this proves $J$ and $\Psi$ are inverse isomorphisms, so the displayed classes are an $R$-basis. [A1, A2, A3, F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, induction, cases, algebra] ∎
