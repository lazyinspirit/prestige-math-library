## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice, and DC. Let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic field,
let $\mathcal O_K$ be its maximal order, and put $\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K$
for $f\ge1$. In the ray class field $K_f$ of modulus $f\mathcal O_K$, let
$\Delta_f$ be the image under arithmetic Artin reciprocity of the subgroup
generated by principal ideals $(u)$ with $u\in\mathbb Z$ and $(u,f)=1$. Define
the ring class field $K_{\mathcal O_f}=K_f^{\Delta_f}$. Then
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathcal O_f}/K)\cong\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)$$
by arithmetic Artin reciprocity. The extension $K_{\mathcal O_f}/\mathbb Q$
is Galois, and complex conjugation acts on
$\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathcal O_f}/K)$ by inversion. When $f=1$ this is the
Hilbert class field of $K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, Countable Choice, DC, an imaginary quadratic field $K$, and an integer $f\ge1$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; the global class-field suppliers assume AC.

[A2] [[def-countable-choice]]: every sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function; the global class-field suppliers assume Countable Choice.

[A3] [[def-dependent-choice]]: every total relation on a nonempty set admits an infinite related sequence from any specified start; the global class-field suppliers assume DC.

[F1] [[thm-ring-class-group-is-ray-class-group-quotient-by-scalar-residues-choice-free]]: $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)$ is the ray class group modulo $f\mathcal O_K$ divided by the image of scalar residues.

[F2] [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]]: the ray class field $K_f/K$ is finite abelian and arithmetic Artin reciprocity identifies its Galois group with the ray class group. The current supplier proof is conditional on unresolved global CFT inputs.

[F3] [[lem-conjugation-inverts-quadratic-ideal-classes]]: complex conjugation inverts classes in $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)$.

[F4] [[def-ray-class-group]]: the modulus $f\mathcal O_K$ is stable under complex conjugation and the ray group is defined by ideals modulo principal ideals congruent to $1$.

## Proof

1.1 Apply [F2] under [A1]–[A3] to the modulus $f\mathcal O_K$. The fixed field $K_f^{\Delta_f}$ is finite over $K$, and Galois correspondence gives
$$\operatorname{Gal}(K_f^{\Delta_f}/K)=\operatorname{Gal}(K_f/K)/\Delta_f.$$
By [F1], the right side is canonically $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)$, with the isomorphism induced by arithmetic Artin reciprocity. [A1, A2, A3, F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Complex conjugation preserves the ray modulus and the scalar subgroup $\Delta_f$, since it fixes every rational integer residue. By the uniqueness in [F2], conjugating the ray class field gives the same field, and its fixed subfield $K_f^{\Delta_f}$ is stable as well; since it is Galois over $K$ and stable under the nontrivial automorphism of $K/\mathbb Q$, it is Galois over $\mathbb Q$. [A1, A2, A3, F2, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Under the quotient isomorphism [F1], conjugation on $\operatorname{Gal}(K_{\mathcal O_f}/K)$ corresponds to conjugation on ideal classes. By [F3] this is inversion, so the asserted action is inversion. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $f=1$, the scalar-residue subgroup is trivial and the ray class group modulo the unit ideal is the ordinary ideal class group. Its class field is the maximal unramified abelian extension of $K$, namely the Hilbert class field. [A1, A2, A3, F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
