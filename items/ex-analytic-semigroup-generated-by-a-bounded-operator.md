## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

Let $X$ be a complex Banach space and let $A\in\mathcal B(X)$. The exponential series
$E(z):=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{z^n}{n!}A^n$
([[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]]) converges in the operator
norm for every $z\in\mathbb C$ and defines an entire function
$E:\mathbb C\to\mathcal B(X)$ with $E(z+w)=E(z)E(w)$; its restriction
$T:=E|_{[0,\infty)}$ is a uniformly continuous strongly continuous semigroup
with generator the bounded operator $A$. Moreover:

(1) $T$ admits a bounded analytic extension to $\Sigma_\delta$ for some
$\delta\in(0,\pi/2]$ if and only if $A$ satisfies the sectorial resolvent
condition with exponent $\delta$ in the $e^{tA}$ convention
([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]]), equivalently
$\sigma(A)\cap\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}=\varnothing$ and the sectorial resolvent
bound holds on $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon}$;

(2) $E$ can be unbounded on every sector of positive angle even though it is
entire: for the nilpotent Jordan block
$A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ on $\mathbb C^2$ one has
$E(z)=I+zA$, which is unbounded along every ray, and for $A=1$ on
$\mathbb C$ one has $E(z)=e^z$, which is unbounded on every sector that meets
the open right half-plane;

(3) if $X$ is a complex Hilbert space and the numerical range
([[def-numerical-range-and-numerical-radius]]) of $A$ lies in the closed sector
$\{\mu:|\arg(-\mu)|\le\pi/2-\theta\}\cup\{0\}$ for some
$\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$, then $T$ is bounded analytic on $\Sigma_\theta$, so its
maximal analytic angle is at least $\theta$. The example shows that sector
boundedness of the exponential is a property of $A$, not a consequence of
boundedness of $A$.

For a real Banach space the complex-time assertions are applied to $A_{\mathbb C}$ on the canonical complexification; its real-time restriction is the original exponential semigroup ([[lem-canonical-banach-complexification-of-a-real-banach-space]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Banach space $X$, an operator $A\in\mathcal B(X)$, the exponential series $E(z)=\sum_{n\ge0}z^nA^n/n!$, and the restriction $T=E|_{[0,\infty)}$.

[L1] $E(t)=\sum_{n\ge0}t^nA^n/n!$ converges absolutely in operator norm, uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb R$, satisfies $E(0)=I$, $E(t+s)=E(t)E(s)$, $\|E(t)\|\le e^{|t|\|A\|}$, $E'(t)=AE(t)=E(t)A$, and $(E(t)-I)/t\to A$ in operator norm; $E$ is a uniformly continuous strongly continuous group whose generator is $A$ ([[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]]).

[L2] The bounded operator $A$ satisfies the sectorial resolvent condition with exponent $\delta$ exactly when $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}\subseteq\rho(A)$ and for every $\varepsilon\in(0,\delta)$ there is $M_\varepsilon$ with $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le M_\varepsilon/|\lambda|$ on $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta-\varepsilon}$; the characterization theorem makes this equivalent to generation of a bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta$ by $A$ ([[def-sectorial-operator-with-the-semigroup-sign-convention]], [[thm-sectorial-resolvent-characterisation-of-bounded-analytic-semigroups]]).

[L3] For a bounded operator the resolvent set contains $\{|\lambda|>\|A\|\}$ with $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le(|\lambda|-\|A\|)^{-1}$ by the Neumann series, and $R(\cdot,A)$ is holomorphic on the open set $\rho(A)$ ([[lem-neumann-series-and-small-perturbations-of-bounded-inverses]], [[lem-resolvent-identity-and-holomorphy-for-closed-operators]], [[def-spectrum-and-resolvent-of-a-bounded-operator]], [[def-operator-norm]], [[def-bounded-linear-operator]]).

[L4] The numerical range $W(T)$ is the set of $\langle Tx,x\rangle$ on the unit sphere ([[def-numerical-range-and-numerical-radius]]).

[L5] A bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta$ has generator the infinitesimal generator of its restriction to $[0,\infty)$ ([[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]], [[def-infinitesimal-generator-of-a-c-zero-semigroup]], [[def-strongly-continuous-semigroup]]).

[L6] Two strongly continuous semigroups with the same generator coincide when the generator is bounded: if $U$ is such a semigroup and $x\in X=D(A)$, then $U(t)x-x=\int_0^tAU(s)x\,ds$, so $t\mapsto U(t)x$ solves $u'=Au$, $u(0)=x$, and $E(-t)u(t)$ has vanishing derivative by the fundamental theorem, whence $u(t)=E(t)x$ ([[lem-exponential-series-of-a-bounded-operator]], [[lem-fundamental-theorem-of-calculus-for-banach-valued-continuous-curves]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The exponential and its generator. By [L1] the series $E(z)$ converges for every complex $z$ (the scalar series $\sum|z|^n\|A\|^n/n!$ bounds it), its derivative series converges uniformly on $|z|\le R$ since it is bounded by $\|A\|e^{R\|A\|}$. For the difference quotients the remainder after the linear term is bounded by $|h|\|A\|^2e^{(R+|h|)\|A\|}/2$, so $E'(z)=AE(z)$ and $E$ is entire. Absolute convergence permits regrouping the double product series; the binomial identity then gives $E(z+w)=E(z)E(w)$, and its restriction $T$ is a uniformly continuous strongly continuous semigroup with $(T(t)-I)/t\to A$, so its generator is the bounded operator $A$ with $D(A)=X$; [L6] identifies $T$ with any other strongly continuous semigroup having generator $A$. [L1, L5, L6, given, algebra]

1.2 Unboundedness examples. For $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{pmatrix}$ on $\mathbb C^2$ the series terminates: $A^0=I$, $A^1=A$ and $A^n=0$ for $n\ge2$, so $E(z)=I+zA$; on the unit vector $e_2$ one has $E(z)e_2=(z,1)$ with Euclidean norm $\sqrt{1+|z|^2}$, so $\|E(z)\|\ge\sqrt{1+|z|^2}$ by [L3] and the operator norm is unbounded along every ray; for $A=1$ on $\mathbb C$ the series gives $E(z)=e^{z}$ with $|E(z)|=e^{\operatorname{Re}z}$, which is unbounded on every sector that meets the open right half-plane. [L1, L3, given, algebra]

1.3 The resolvent estimate remains a separate condition. For bounded $A$, the Neumann-series estimate of [L3] controls $R(\lambda,A)$ when $|\lambda|>2\|A\|$, but it does not control behavior as $\lambda\to0$. The sectorial resolvent condition in [L2] therefore retains the separate $O(|\lambda|^{-1})$ bound on every smaller sector; spectrum avoidance alone is not used to infer it. [L2, L3, given]

2.1 The sectorial extension criterion. By [L2], for bounded $A$ the sectorial resolvent condition with exponent $\delta$ is exactly the conjunction of $\Sigma_{\pi/2+\delta}\subseteq\rho(A)$ and the resolvent bounds on the smaller sectors; step 1.3 explains why the bound at the vertex must remain explicit. The same characterization makes this condition equivalent to generation of a bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta$. By [step 1.1], $T$ is the semigroup generated by $A$, so it has such an extension exactly under that sectorial condition; the spectrum formulation in (1) is just the equivalent resolvent-set clause together with the bound. [step 1.1, step 1.3, L2, L5, L6, given, algebra]

3.1 The numerical range case. Assume $X$ is a complex Hilbert space and $W(A)$ lies in the closed sector $C_\theta:=\{\mu:|\arg(-\mu)|\le\pi/2-\theta\}\cup\{0\}$ with $\theta\in(0,\pi/2)$; for $\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi/2+\theta-\varepsilon}$ and $u\ne0$ the normalised value $z_u:=\langle Au,u\rangle/\|u\|^2$ lies in $C_\theta$ by [L4] and the angular distance from $\lambda$ to $C_\theta$ is at least $\varepsilon$, so $|\lambda-z_u|\ge|\lambda|\sin\varepsilon$ and Cauchy-Schwarz gives $\|(\lambda I-A)u\|\ge|\lambda|\sin\varepsilon\|u\|$; hence $\lambda I-A$ is injective with closed range and, since for $|\lambda|>\|A\|$ it is invertible by [L3] and the set of surjectivity points is open (Neumann series) and closed in the connected complement of $C_\theta$: if $\lambda_n\to\lambda$ there, the positive distance of $\lambda$ to $C_\theta$ bounds $\|R(\lambda_n,A)\|$ uniformly for large $n$, and the resolvent identity makes these inverses Cauchy in operator norm. Their limit $R$ satisfies $(\lambda I-A)R=R(\lambda I-A)=I$ by boundedness of $A$. Thus surjectivity is closed, every such $\lambda$ is in $\rho(A)$ with $\|R(\lambda,A)\|\le1/(|\lambda|\sin\varepsilon)$; by [step 2.1] the sectorial condition with exponent $\theta$ holds and $T$ extends to a bounded analytic semigroup on $\Sigma_\theta$, so its maximal analytic angle is at least $\theta$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L3, L4, given, algebra] ∎
