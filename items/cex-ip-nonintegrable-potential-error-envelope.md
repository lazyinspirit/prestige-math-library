## Statement refuted

For every separable real Banach space $X$, Borel set $D\subseteq X$, and Borel probability $\mu_0$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$ and positive mass on a bounded subset of $D$. Give $D$ the relative norm topology. Let $\Phi,\Phi_N:D\to\mathbb R$ be finite continuous potentials indexed by integers $N\ge1$, let $M_1,M_2:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ be Borel and nondecreasing, and let $\psi_N\ge0$ tend to zero. If $\Phi,\Phi_N\ge-M_1(\|u\|_X)$ and $|\Phi_N(u)-\Phi(u)|\le M_2(\|u\|_X)\psi_N$ for every $u,N$, and their evidence normalizers are positive and finite, then there are $C<\infty$ and $N_0$ such that the library root-L2 Hellinger distance satisfies $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)\le C\psi_N$ for every $N\ge N_0$, even without assuming $\int_D e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}(1+M_2(\|u\|_X)^2)\,d\mu_0<\infty$.

## Counterexample

Let $X=\mathbb R$ with its usual norm, $D=\mathbb N_{\ge1}$ with the trace Borel sigma-algebra, and $c=(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2})^{-1}$. For Borel $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, define $\mu_0(A):=c\sum_{n\in A\cap D}n^{-2}$, so $\mu_0(\{n\})=c/n^2$. Let $\Phi(n)=0$, $\Phi_N(n)=n/N$ for integers $N\ge1$, and set $M_1(s)=0$, $M_2(s)=s$, and $\psi_N=1/N$. The potentials have common lower envelope zero and satisfy $|\Phi_N(n)-\Phi(n)|=M_2(|n|)\psi_N$, but the weighted error envelope is not prior-integrable.

1.1 By [F3], $0<\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-2}<\infty$, so $c$ is finite and positive and [F7] makes $\mu_0$ a probability with $\mu_0(D)=1$. By [F6], $D$ is Borel and has the discrete relative topology, so every real-valued function on $D$, including $\Phi,\Phi_N$, is continuous. The real line with its usual norm is a separable Banach space by [F6]. Thus all $\Phi,\Phi_N$ are finite continuous functions on $D$. They satisfy $\Phi,\Phi_N\ge0$ and $|\Phi_N(n)-\Phi(n)|=n/N=M_2(|n|)\psi_N$. But $\int_D e^{M_1(|u|)}(1+M_2(|u|)^2)\,\mu_0(du)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c}{n^2}(1+n^2)=+\infty,$ since the second summand contributes $c$ for each $n$. This is precisely the integrability hypothesis used by [F4], and it fails here. [F3, F4, F6, F7, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The base evidence is $Z=1$. For each $N$, the approximate evidence $Z_N=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c}{n^2}e^{-n/N}$ is positive and at most one, so both posterior updates exist. Moreover $Z_N\to1$: given $\epsilon>0$, choose $m$ with $\sum_{n>m}c/n^2<\epsilon/2$ by [F3]. The finite sum $\sum_{n=1}^m(c/n^2)(1-e^{-n/N})$ tends to zero by continuity of the exponential [F5], while the remaining tail of $1-Z_N$ is at most $\sum_{n>m}c/n^2<\epsilon/2$. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, given, choose, algebra]

3.1 Since $e^{-1}<1$, the number $\alpha:=(1+e^{-1})/2$ is less than one. From $Z_N\to1$, for all sufficiently large $N$ we have $Z_N\ge\alpha$. For $n\ge N$, $\frac{e^{-n/N}}{Z_N}\le\frac{e^{-1}}{\alpha}=\frac{2}{e+1}<1,$ so $1-\sqrt{e^{-n/N}/Z_N}\ge\kappa:=1-\sqrt{2/(e+1)}>0$. [F5, step 2.1, given, algebra]

4.1 The posterior density of the base law relative to $\mu_0$ is $1$, and that of the approximation is $e^{-n/N}/Z_N$. By [F2] and step 3.1, $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)^2=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{c}{n^2}\left(1-\sqrt{\frac{e^{-n/N}}{Z_N}}\right)^2\ge\kappa^2\sum_{n=N}^{2N-1}\frac{c}{n^2}\ge\frac{\kappa^2c}{4N},$ because the block has $N$ terms and each $n$ in it satisfies $n<2N$. Therefore $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)\ge(\kappa\sqrt c/2)N^{-1/2}$, while $\psi_N=N^{-1}$. The ratio $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)/\psi_N$ is at least $(\kappa\sqrt c/2)\sqrt N$ and is unbounded, so the claimed $O(\psi_N)$ rate fails. [F2, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
