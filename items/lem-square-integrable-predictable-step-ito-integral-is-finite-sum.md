## Statement

Assume AC. Fix $T>0$ and a finite partition $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T$.
Let $W$ be a scalar Brownian motion adapted to $(\mathcal F_t)$, with
$W_t-W_s$ independent of $\mathcal F_s$ and distributed as $N(0,t-s)$ for
every $0\le s<t\le T$. If each $\xi_k$ is real-valued,
$\mathcal F_{t_k}$-measurable and square-integrable, then the predictable
step process
$$H_s=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\xi_k1_{(t_k,t_{k+1}]}(s)$$
is square-integrable. Every continuous version of its Itô integral agrees, at every time on one
measurable event of probability one, with
$$S_t=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\xi_k\bigl(W_{t\wedge t_{k+1}}-W_{t\wedge t_k}\bigr),\qquad 0\le t\le T.$$
If the probability space is complete, this agreement is indistinguishability.
The coefficients need not be bounded.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $T>0$, a finite partition $0=t_0<t_1<\cdots<t_N=T$, a scalar Brownian motion adapted to $(\mathcal F_t)$ whose every future increment is independent of the current filtration, and square-integrable $\mathcal F_{t_k}$-measurable coefficients $\xi_k$.

[F1] [[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]]: increments over a positive time interval have the corresponding centered normal law.

[F12] [[def-d-dimensional-brownian-motion]]: Brownian paths are continuous on one common measurable event of probability one.

[F2] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]: the predictable sigma-algebra is generated by time-zero sets and rectangles $(s,t]\times A$ with $A\in\mathcal F_s$; a process is predictable when its time-sample map is measurable for that sigma-algebra.

[F3] [[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]]: a bounded finite step process with coefficients measurable at the left endpoints is elementary predictable, and changing values at finitely many deterministic times does not change its $dt\otimes P$ class.

[F4] [[def-ito-integral-of-an-elementary-predictable-process]]: the elementary integral at time $t$ is $\sum_k\xi_k(W_{t\wedge t_{k+1}}-W_{t\wedge t_k})$.

[F5] [[def-ito-integral-for-square-integrable-predictable-processes]]: the general integral is the $L^2(P)$ limit of elementary integrals approximating the predictable integrand in $L^2(dt\otimes P)$, with truncation to $[0,t]$ for its value at time $t$.

[F6] [[thm-ito-integral-process-has-a-continuous-martingale-version]]: every finite-energy predictable Itô integrand has an adapted continuous integral version, unique among continuous versions on one measurable probability-one event.

[F7] [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]: expectations of increasing nonnegative sequences converge to the expectation of their pointwise limit.

[F8] [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]]: rational times, together with the endpoint $T$, are dense in $[0,T]$.

[F9] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]: the deterministic indicators $1_{[0,u]}$ and $1_{(0,u]}$ are predictable for every deterministic $u\ge0$.

[F10] [[thm-the-l-p-norm-descends-to-the-quotient-and-makes-l-p-a-normed-space]]: the $L^2(P)$ quotient norm satisfies the triangle inequality and separates equivalence classes.

[F11] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]: $N_1(0,\delta)$ has mean zero and variance, hence second moment, equal to $\delta$.

[A1] For every $0\le s<t\le T$, $W_t-W_s$ is independent of $\mathcal F_s$. This filtration-relative independence is an explicit hypothesis, not a consequence of the pathwise Brownian definition alone.

[A2] AC is inherited from the Brownian and Itô-integral interfaces above; the truncation and finite-sum argument makes no additional choice. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 For every Borel set $C\subseteq\mathbb R$, $H^{-1}(C)$ is the finite union of the predictable rectangles $(t_k,t_{k+1}]\times\{\xi_k\in C\}$, together with $\{0\}\times\Omega$ when $0\in C$; hence [F2] makes $H$ predictable. The intervals partition $(0,T]$, and [F3] makes the time-zero endpoint irrelevant to the product integral, so $E\int_0^T H_s^2ds=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}(t_{k+1}-t_k)E|\xi_k|^2<\infty$. [F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 For $n\ge1$, clip each coefficient by $\xi_{k,n}=(-n)\vee(\xi_k\wedge n)$ and put $H^n_s=\sum_k\xi_{k,n}1_{(t_k,t_{k+1}]}(s)$. Each $H^n$ is bounded and elementary by [F3]. Since $|\xi_{k,n}-\xi_k|^2\le |\xi_k|^2 1_{\{|\xi_k|>n\}}$ and $E|\xi_k|^2<\infty$, [F7] applied to $|\xi_k|^2 1_{\{|\xi_k|\le n\}}\uparrow |\xi_k|^2$ gives $E|\xi_{k,n}-\xi_k|^2\to0$. The finite partition then gives $\|H^n-H\|_{L^2(dt\otimes P)}^2=\sum_k(t_{k+1}-t_k)E|\xi_{k,n}-\xi_k|^2\to0$. [F3, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 For each fixed $t\in[0,T]$, [F5] defines $\int_0^tH_s\,dW_s$ as an $L^2(P)$ limit of the elementary integrals of $H^n1_{[0,t]}$. By [F4] those approximants are $S_t^n=\sum_k\xi_{k,n}(W_{t\wedge t_{k+1}}-W_{t\wedge t_k})$. Hence $S_t^n\to\int_0^tH_s\,dW_s$ in $L^2(P)$. [F4, F5, step 1.2, given]

3.1 Put $\delta_k(t)=(t\wedge t_{k+1})-(t\wedge t_k)\ge0$. When $\delta_k(t)=0$ the increment in the $k$th summand vanishes. When $\delta_k(t)>0$, [F1] gives its $N(0,\delta_k(t))$ law, [F11] gives second moment $\delta_k(t)$, and [A1] makes it independent of $\xi_{k,n}-\xi_k$. Thus the $L^2$ norm of that summand's difference between $S_t^n$ and $S_t$ is $\sqrt{\delta_k(t)}\,\|\xi_{k,n}-\xi_k\|_2$. The triangle inequality [F10] and the finite number of intervals imply $S_t^n\to S_t$ in $L^2(P)$. Since also $S_t^n\to\int_0^tH_s\,dW_s$ by step 2.1, the triangle inequality gives $\|S_t-\int_0^tH_s\,dW_s\|_2=0$, and separation [F10] makes these classes equal almost surely. [A1, F1, F10, F11, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Extend $H$ by zero after $T$; [F9] makes this extension predictable and its energy stays finite on every finite horizon. The process $S$ has continuous paths on the common Brownian continuity event [F12], because it is a finite sum of fixed random coefficients times stopped Brownian increments. Let $M$ be the continuous integral version supplied by [F6]. Step 3.1 gives $M_t=S_t$ almost surely at each $t$ in the countable dense set of [F8]; intersecting those measurable probability-one events with the continuity events and using continuity gives equality for every $t\in[0,T]$ on one measurable probability-one event. If the probability space is complete, the complement of the full equality set is a subset of the measurable null complement of this event, hence is measurable and null; the versions are then indistinguishable. AC is inherited through [F6] as recorded in [A2]. [A2, F6, F8, F9, F12, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

Van der Vaart, §5.3, Definitions 5.20 and 5.25 and Lemmas 5.21–5.23, constructs the square-integrable integral by continuous extension from bounded simple predictable integrands. The proof above supplies the finite-partition truncation and common continuous-version argument for the exact step processes used by Euler–Maruyama.
