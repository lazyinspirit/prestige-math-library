## Definition

Let $p$ be a prime, let $G$ be a finite group, and let $e$ be a nonzero central idempotent of $\mathbb Z_p[G]$. Put $A=e\mathbb Q_p[G]$ and $\Lambda=e\mathbb Z_p[G]$, and let $M$ be a finitely presented left $\Lambda$-module. The algebra $A$ is semisimple; for any ring $B$, write $\zeta(B)=\{z\in B:zb=bz\text{ for every }b\in B\}$ for its centre. Write $\operatorname{nr}_A$ for the reduced norm on $A$, the centre-valued determinant defined componentwise on the simple factors. For a finite presentation $\Lambda^a\xrightarrow{h}\Lambda^b\twoheadrightarrow M$, the Fitting invariant of $h$ is the equivalence class of the $\zeta(\Lambda)$-submodule of $\zeta(A)$ generated by $\operatorname{nr}_A(H)$ for all $b\times b$ submatrices $H$ of a matrix for $h$; it is zero when $a<b$. Two such submodules $I,J$ are equivalent if $I=\operatorname{nr}_A(U)J$ for some $n\ge1$ and $U\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\Lambda)$. Write $[I]\le[J]$ if every representative of $[I]$ is contained in some representative of $[J]$. The maximal noncommutative Fitting invariant $\operatorname{Fitt}^{\max}_\Lambda(M)$ is the largest of these equivalence classes among finite presentations of $M$. An element $x\in\zeta(A)$ generates this invariant if $x\zeta(\Lambda)$ represents the maximal class.
