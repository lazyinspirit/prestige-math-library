## Statement

Assume AC. Let $G$ be a second-countable locally compact Hausdorff topological
group with left Haar measure $\mu$. (i) If $A\subseteq G$ is Borel with
$0<\mu(A)<\infty$, then $AA^{-1}$ contains an open neighbourhood of the
identity. (ii) If $T$ is a second-countable topological group and
$\varphi:G\to T$ is a Borel-measurable group homomorphism, then $\varphi$ is
continuous. In particular, a Borel homomorphism from a second-countable
locally compact group into the unitary group $U(K)$ of a separable Hilbert
space, with the strong operator topology, is strongly continuous.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a second-countable LCH group $G$ with left Haar measure $\mu$; in part (ii) a second-countable topological group $T$ and a Borel homomorphism $\varphi$.

[F1] $G$ carries a nonzero left Haar measure $\mu$, positive on nonempty open sets and finite on compact sets; left translates of Borel sets preserve $\mu$ ([[thm-existence-of-a-left-haar-integral]], [[def-left-haar-integral-and-left-haar-measure]], [[lem-haar-measure-is-positive-on-nonempty-open-sets-and-finite-on-compact-sets]]).

[F2] For $f\in L^2(G)$ the map $g\mapsto L_gf$, $L_gf(x)=f(g^{-1}x)$, is norm continuous; hence so is $g\mapsto\langle L_gf,f\rangle=\int_Gf(g^{-1}x)\overline{f(x)}\,dx$ ([[lem-haar-translations-are-strongly-continuous-on-lp-one-and-two]]).

[F3] In a topological group, inversion is continuous, multiplication is continuous, every neighbourhood of the identity contains a symmetric neighbourhood, and a homomorphism continuous at the identity is continuous everywhere ([[def-topological-group]]).

[F4] The Borel $\sigma$-algebra is generated by the open sets, and a Borel homomorphism is a group homomorphism measurable for the Borel structures; preimages of open sets are Borel ([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]).

[F5] A second-countable space is Lindelöf: for an open cover, the members of a fixed countable base contained in some member of the cover form a countable refinement, and choosing one containing cover member for each of them uses Countable Choice, which follows from AC ([[def-second-countable-space]], [[thm-compactness-variants-hierarchy]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F6] The strong operator topology on $\mathcal B(K)$ is the initial topology of the maps $T\mapsto Tx$, $x\in K$ ([[def-strong-and-weak-operator-topologies]]); $U(K)$ is the group of unitary operators on $K$ ([[def-strongly-continuous-unitary-representation]]).

[F7] A separable Hilbert space has a finite or countable orthonormal basis, which may be padded by zero vectors to a sequence indexed by $\mathbb N$ ([[thm-separable-hilbert-space-has-a-countable-orthonormal-basis]]).

[F8] $G$ is locally compact Hausdorff, so points have compact neighbourhoods and the open sets with compact closure form a base ([[def-locally-compact-space]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** AC, the group $G$ with Haar measure $\mu$, and in part (ii) the group $T$ and the Borel homomorphism $\varphi$.

1.1 Part (i): let $A$ be Borel with $0<\mu(A)<\infty$ and put $f=\mathbf 1_A\in L^2(G)$. For $g\in G$, $L_gf=\mathbf 1_{gA}$, so $\langle L_gf,f\rangle=\mu(gA\cap A)$; by [F2] this function of $g$ is continuous and equals $\mu(A)>0$ at $g=e$. Hence there is an open neighbourhood $W$ of $e$ with $\mu(gA\cap A)>0$, hence $gA\cap A\ne\varnothing$, for every $g\in W$; writing $g a_1=a_2$ with $a_1,a_2\in A$ gives $g=a_2a_1^{-1}\in AA^{-1}$. Thus $W\subseteq AA^{-1}$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Part (ii), reduction: $\varphi$ is a homomorphism, so for $g_0\in G$, $\varphi(g)\varphi(g_0)^{-1}=\varphi(gg_0^{-1})$; since multiplication and inversion in $T$ are continuous by [F3], $\varphi$ is continuous at every point as soon as it is continuous at $e$. It therefore suffices to show that $\varphi^{-1}(U)$ is a neighbourhood of $e_G$ for every neighbourhood $U$ of $e_T$. [F3]

1.3 A second-countable space is Lindelöf, with the argument of [F5]: fixing a countable base, the basic open sets contained in some member of an open cover form a countable refinement, and Countable Choice (a consequence of AC) selects a cover member for each of them. We apply this to $\varphi(G)$ with the subspace topology, which is second countable as a subspace of $T$. [F5]

1.4 The unitary group $U(K)$ of a separable Hilbert space is second countable in the strong operator topology: fix a finite or countable orthonormal basis $(e_n)$ padded to a sequence by [F7] and consider $\Psi:U(K)\to K^{\mathbb N}$, $\Psi(u)=(ue_n)_n$. It is injective (a unitary vanishing on a complete orthonormal system is zero) and continuous for the SOT by [F6]; conversely, if $u_i\to u$ in the initial topology of the coordinate maps $u\mapsto ue_n$, then for $\xi=\sum_nc_ne_n\in K$ and $\varepsilon>0$ choose $N$ with $\sum_{n>N}|c_n|^2<\varepsilon^2/16$; then $\|(u_i-u)\xi\|\le\sum_{n\le N}|c_n|\,\|(u_i-u)e_n\|+2\bigl(\sum_{n>N}|c_n|^2\bigr)^{1/2}<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2$ for all $i$ beyond a suitable index, so $u_i\to u$ strongly. Thus the SOT on $U(K)$ is the initial topology of the countable family $(u\mapsto ue_n)_n$, making it homeomorphic to a subspace of the second-countable space $K^{\mathbb N}$, hence second countable. [F6, F7]

1.5 $U(K)$ with the strong operator topology is a topological group: if $u_i\to u$ and $v_i\to v$ strongly, then $\|(u_iv_i-uv)\xi\|\le\|u_i(v_i-v)\xi\|+\|(u_i-u)v\xi\|\le\|(v_i-v)\xi\|+\|(u_i-u)v\xi\|\to0$; and if $u_i\to u$ strongly with $u_i,u$ unitary, then $\|(u_i^{-1}-u^{-1})\xi\|=\|u_i^{-1}(u-u_i)u^{-1}\xi\|=\|(u-u_i)u^{-1}\xi\|\to0$, so inversion is continuous. [F6, algebra]

2.1 Fix a neighbourhood $U$ of $e_T$ and choose a symmetric neighbourhood $V$ of $e_T$ with $V^2\subseteq U$, using continuity of multiplication at $(e_T,e_T)$ and symmetry of neighbourhoods ([F3]). The family $\{\varphi(g)V\cap\varphi(G):g\in G\}$ is an open cover of $\varphi(G)$, because $\varphi(g)\in\varphi(g)V$; by [step 1.3] it has a countable subcover with centres $\varphi(g_n)$, $n\in\mathbb N$, chosen with $g_n\in G$. [step 1.3, F3]

3.1 The preimage $\varphi^{-1}(V)$ has positive measure: it is Borel by [F4], and if $\mu(\varphi^{-1}(V))=0$, then for every $g\in G$ the left translate $g\varphi^{-1}(V)$ is null by [F1] and the sets $g_n\varphi^{-1}(V)$ cover $G$, since $\varphi(g)\in\varphi(g_n)V$ gives $\varphi(g_n)^{-1}\varphi(g)=\varphi(g_n^{-1}g)\in V$, that is, $g_n^{-1}g\in\varphi^{-1}(V)$ and $g\in g_n\varphi^{-1}(V)$. A countable cover of the nonempty open set $G$ by null sets would give $\mu(G)=0$, contradicting positivity of $\mu$ on the nonempty open set $G$ in [F1]. [step 2.1, F1, F4]

4.1 Choose a Borel set $A\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(V)$ with $0<\mu(A)<\infty$: by [F8] and second countability, the members of a countable base with compact closure cover $G$, so $G=\bigcup_mK_m$ with $K_m$ compact; if $\mu(\varphi^{-1}(V)\cap K_m)=0$ for every $m$ then countable additivity would give $\mu(\varphi^{-1}(V))=0$, contrary to [step 3.1], so some $A:=\varphi^{-1}(V)\cap K_m$ is Borel with $0<\mu(A)\le\mu(K_m)<\infty$ by [F1]. [step 3.1, F1, F8]

5.1 By part (i), [step 1.1], the set $AA^{-1}$ contains an open neighbourhood of $e_G$, and $AA^{-1}\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(V)\varphi^{-1}(V)^{-1}\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(VV^{-1})=\varphi^{-1}(V^2)\subseteq\varphi^{-1}(U)$, because $\varphi$ is a homomorphism and $V$ is symmetric. Hence $\varphi^{-1}(U)$ is a neighbourhood of $e_G$; by [step 1.2] $\varphi$ is continuous. This proves (ii). [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Let $\varphi:G\to U(K)$ be a Borel homomorphism from the second-countable LCH group $G$ into $U(K)$ with the strong operator topology. By [step 1.4] $U(K)$ is second countable and by [step 1.5] it is a topological group, so part (ii) proved in [step 5.1] applies and $\varphi$ is strongly continuous. Together with part (i) of [step 1.1] this proves every assertion of the statement. [step 1.1, step 5.1, step 1.4, step 1.5] ∎

## Remarks

The proof of (ii) uses only the positive measure of the preimage of a
neighbourhood of the identity, extracted through a countable subcover of the
orbit cover; no countability of the group of values is assumed beyond second
countability of the target.
