## Statement

Let $K$ be a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field, let $n\ge1$, put $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(K)$, and let $N=\{u\in G:u_{ii}=1,\ u_{ij}=0\text{ for }i>j\}$ be the upper unitriangular subgroup. Let $w_0$ be the permutation matrix defined by $w_0e_i=e_{n+1-i}$. For a nontrivial continuous additive character $\psi:K\to\mathbb C^\times$, define $\psi_N(u)=\psi(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i,i+1})$ and $\sigma(g)=w_0g^{\mathsf T}w_0$. Let $C_c^\infty(G)$ denote the complex vector space of locally constant, compactly supported functions on $G$. For $(u,v)\in N\times N$, define $((u,v)\cdot f)(g)=f(u^{-1}gv)$ and $\chi(u,v)=\psi_N(u)\psi_N(v)^{-1}$. Every complex-linear functional $D:C_c^\infty(G)\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $D((u,v)\cdot f)=\chi(u,v)D(f)$ for all $u,v\in N$ and $f\in C_c^\infty(G)$ satisfies $D(f\circ\sigma)=D(f)$ for every $f\in C_c^\infty(G)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact field, representation and character data stated above. All selections in this proof are finite.

[A1] $K$ is complete and discretely valued with finite residue field, $n\ge1$, and $\psi:K\to\mathbb C^\times$ is nontrivial and continuous, as stated in the Statement.



## Proof

**Given:** The stated data and the listed facts.

1.1 Elementary N-double-coset decomposition, including uniqueness. Use the convention $w e_j=e_{w(j)}$. Every $g\in G$ has a factorization $g=u w a v$, with $u,v\in N$ and $a=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)$, $a_j\in K^\times$. In the first column take its lowest nonzero entry, in row $i$. Add multiples of row $i$ to rows above $i$ to eliminate their first-column entries; these are left upper-unitriangular operations. Entries below $i$ already vanish. Then add multiples of column $1$ to columns $j>1$ to eliminate the remaining entries of row $i$; these are right upper-unitriangular operations. Leave the nonzero pivot value unchanged. The submatrix on the remaining ordered rows and columns $2,\ldots,n$ is invertible. Repeat the same procedure there. All later row and column operations stay upper-unitriangular in the original order and preserve earlier cleared pivot rows and columns. The result is a monomial matrix $wa$, with column-$j$ pivot equal to $a_j$. Inverting the elimination operations gives the displayed factorization. The permutation is unique: the ranks $r_{ij}(g)$ of the submatrix in rows $i,\ldots,n$ and columns $1,\ldots,j$ are invariant under left and right upper-triangular operations. For $wa$ they equal $\#\{k\le j:w(k)\ge i\}$, which determine $w$ by successive rank differences. For this fixed $w$, the diagonal $a$ is also unique under $N$-double equivalence. If $u w a v=w a'$ with $u,v\in N$, then $w^{-1}u w=a'v^{-1}a^{-1}$. The left matrix has every diagonal entry $1$; the right matrix has diagonal entries $a'_j/a_j$. Thus $a'=a$. This proves that $wa$ indexes $N\backslash G/N$ without assuming a Bruhat-coordinate result. [given, A1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Exact stabilizer and its root generators. For g=wa, putH_g=N∩gNg^−1. Since diagonal conjugation preservesN, H_g=N∩wNw^−1. A matrixh∈N belongs toH_g exactly when h_ij=0 wheneveri<j butw^−1(i)>w^−1(j). Hence its allowed root subgroups are x_ij(t)=I+tE_ij, i<j, r=w^−1(i)<s=w^−1(j). These roots generateH_g. To prove it directly, eliminate allowed entries successively by increasing superdiagonal heightj−i. Multiplying byx_ij(−h_ij) kills the current entry and only affects higher-height entries(i,k). If a new such entry can occur, the pattern has both(i,j) and(j,k), so r_i<r_j<r_k and(i,k) is allowed. Thus elimination never leavesH_g and terminates after finitely many heights. This proves generation, in every characteristic. The mapψ_N is a group homomorphism because the entries immediately above the diagonal add under multiplication. On a root subgroup it isψ(t) ifj=i+1, and1 otherwise. Direct conjugation gives g^−1x_ij(t)g=x_rs((a_s/a_r)t). [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Compatible representatives. The stabilizer comparison for a bi-equivariant distribution at $g=wa$ requires $\psi_N(u)=\psi_N(g^{-1}ug)$ for every $u\in N\cap gNg^{-1}$. Its root subgroups are $x_{ij}(t)=I+tE_{ij}$, where $i<j$ and $r=w^{-1}(i)<s=w^{-1}(j)$. Direct conjugation gives $g^{-1}x_{ij}(t)g=x_{rs}((a_s/a_r)t)$. The two character restrictions agree on that root subgroup exactly when either both roots are nonsimple, or both are simple and $a_s=a_r$. If only one root is simple, one restriction is trivial and the other is nontrivial. If both are simple, equality for every $t$ forces $a_s/a_r=1$: otherwise multiplication by the nonzero difference is onto $K$, contradicting nontriviality of $\psi$. Thus every ascent $w(r)<w(r+1)$ must be by one. The maximal ascending runs are intervals of consecutive values in increasing order, and those intervals occur in decreasing order. Indeed, a lower interval immediately before a higher one would create an ascent across their boundary; any ordering of disjoint consecutive intervals that is not decreasing has such an adjacent upward transition. Conversely, for intervals arranged in decreasing order, any two increasing values whose preimages occur increasingly belong to the same interval. The root-character condition then holds. Within each positional run, the scalar constraints are exactly equality of its $a_j$. Let $w_0$ reverse the $n$ coordinates and set $\sigma(g)=w_0g^{\mathsf T}w_0$. The compatible permutations have the form $w=w_0r$, where $r$ reverses each positional run; hence $w_0w^{-1}w_0=w$. Therefore $\sigma(wa)=wa'$, where $a'_j=a_{n+1-w(j)}=a_{r(j)}$. Since $r$ only reverses each run and $a_j$ is constant on that run, $a'=a$. Every compatible representative is fixed by $\sigma$. [step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Tests, finite integration, and compact averaging. Write O for the valuation ring and pi for a uniformizer. Additive integration on K is defined by vol(pi^a O)=q^-a: a locally constant compactly supported function has a finite decomposition into cosets of one sufficiently small pi^a O, and its integral is the corresponding weighted finite sum. Refining to a smaller coset partition leaves it unchanged. Existence of a common partition follows from a finite subcover of its compact support; enlarge the support to a compact-open neighborhood before choosing the scale. This proves translation invariance and the finite-product Fubini rule directly. Finite-residue completeness makes O compact by the finite-branch cylinder argument: a cover without a finite subcover would have a least bad child at each level and hence a single uncovered limit. Initial choices of uniformizer and finitely many residue representatives are finite choices. The ordered strict-upper entries identify N with a finite product of K. Matrix multiplication, when entries are ordered by increasing height j-i, changes a coordinate by a translation depending only on earlier coordinates. The product integration is consequently both left and right invariant, by iterated finite Fubini and additive translation. Inversion is also triangular with leading coefficient -1 and preserves it. The same argument works for a root-pattern subgroup closed under addition of roots. Thus every unipotent group used below has an explicit positive unimodular test integration; no Haar theorem is being cited. The character psi is locally constant. Indeed choose a neighborhood of 1 in C× containing no nontrivial subgroup (for example require modulus near 1 and argument in a fixed sufficiently short arc; powers of every nonidentity number leave it). Some pi^a O maps into that neighborhood. Its image is a subgroup, so is trivial. Hence chi has an open kernel. For m>=0 let d_m=diag(pi^(-(n-1)m),...,pi^-m,1), and set N_m=d_m N(O)d_m^-1. Its (i,j) entries have valuation at least -(j-i)m. These are increasing compact-open subgroups with union N. Hence H_m=N_m×N_m are increasing compact-open subgroups with union H. On any smooth H-module V define E_m v as the normalized chi^-1-weighted average of h v over H_m. This is a FINITE sum: choose an open subgroup fixing v and on which chi is trivial, intersect with H_m, and pass to its finite core in H_m. The resulting finite quotient average is independent of refinement. It satisfies E_m(hv)=chi(h)E_m v for h in H_m. Moreover v-E_m v is a finite linear combination of hv-chi(h)v, h in H_m. Consequently the twisted-coinvariant functor V_(H,chi)=V/span{hv-chi(h)v} is exact on smooth H-modules. Right exactness is the ordinary quotient calculation. For injectivity, suppose a vector a in a submodule A becomes zero in the coinvariants of B. Write its vanishing as one finite sum of relations in B and choose m containing all the group elements in that sum. Then E_m a=0. Since a-E_m a is a sum of the SAME TYPE of relations in A, the class of a was zero in A_(H,chi). No exactness of an infinite compact-invariant functor is assumed. [step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Extension and a fiberwise localization lemma. All spaces here are locally compact Hausdorff and zero-dimensional. If Y is closed in X, restriction C_c^infinity(X)→C_c^infinity(Y) is onto. To see this, the nonzero support of a test on Y splits into finitely many compact-open value sets. For each such set choose finitely many compact-open neighborhoods in X avoiding its closed complement in Y; their union intersects Y in exactly that set. Subtract preceding unions to make the finitely many neighborhoods disjoint. The corresponding finite linear combination extends the test. Its support is compact. Thus 0→C_c^infinity(X\Y)→C_c^infinity(X)→C_c^infinity(Y)→0 is exact whenever Y is closed in X. Here is the localization statement needed later. Let p:X→B be continuous and H-invariant, with B locally compact Hausdorff zero-dimensional. If a test f has zero class in the twisted coinvariants of EVERY fiber X_b, then its class in C_c^infinity(X)_(H,chi) is zero. For a fixed b write the restriction f|X_b as one finite sum of twisted relations. Extend the finitely many fiber tests to X by the preceding extension lemma; the error is a compactly supported locally constant function vanishing on X_b. The projection of its support is compact, hence closed in B, and avoids b. There is therefore a compact-open neighborhood W of b on which the error vanishes. Multiplying the extended tests by 1_(p^-1 W) gives the desired relation on that neighborhood, since p is H-invariant. Cover the compact set p(supp f) by finitely many such W and take their finite disjoint refinement. Summing the local relations proves the claim. This argument uses the cover consisting of ALL admissible finite witness data and then a finite subcover; it does not choose witnesses simultaneously for infinitely many b. [step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The homogeneous-fiber calculation. Fix g=w a with w a permutation matrix and a diagonal invertible matrix. Put P_w=N intersect w N w^-1, U_w=N intersect w N^- w^-1. Both are root-pattern groups, and multiplication U_w×P_w→N is a polynomial bijection with polynomial inverse. Explicitly solve the entry of height j-i by assigning it to the unique one of the two complementary root patterns; cross terms have smaller height and are already known. Each pattern is closed under addition of roots, so all resulting factors remain in their groups. The stabilizer of g for H is L_g={(h,g^-1 h g):h in P_w}. It is closed and unipotent. The map U_w×N→H/L_g,    (u,v)↦(u,v^-1)L_g is a homeomorphism, and identifies H/L_g with the orbit N g N. To check this, factor the first H-coordinate n1=u h; then n1 g n2^-1=u g v with v=(g^-1 h g)n2^-1. Uniqueness follows from U_w∩P_w=1. These formulas and their inverses are polynomial, allowing division only by the nonzero diagonal entries of a. In coordinates (u,v,h), product integration on H is du dv dh: the factorization N=U_w P_w has unit triangular Jacobian; for fixed h the second coordinate is a translate/inverse of v. Hence P(phi)(x L_g)=integral_(L_g) phi(x l) dl is an onto H-equivariant map from C_c^infinity(H) to C_c^infinity(H/L_g). Surjectivity follows in the displayed GLOBAL product coordinates by lifting a quotient test f(u,v) to f(u,v)eta(h), where eta has compact support and integral one. Its kernel is generated by right L_g-translation differences. For completeness, expand a compact test in the product coordinates into a finite sum of compact-open rectangular tests. Refine the finitely many quotient-coordinate partitions. On each resulting quotient piece, the remaining L_g-test has integral zero. A zero-integral compact test on L_g is a finite sum of right-translation differences: choose a common sufficiently small compact-open subgroup J on whose left cosets it is constant; all such cosets have the same measure, so its coefficients sum to zero. Subtract one fixed coset indicator from all the others. The function is then a finite sum of right translates of 1_J minus 1_J. Existence of a common left stabilizer follows from the same finite-cover argument used for test integration. In the global product coordinates, right L_g translation acts only on the last coordinate, so these are exactly the asserted kernel relations. The left twisted coinvariants of C_c^infinity(H) have dimension one, identified by phi↦integral_H chi(h)phi(h)dh. Indeed refine a test to finitely many translates of an indicator 1_J with chi|J=1. Its class is the weighted finite sum of their scalar coefficients. Refining J multiplies its class and its volume by the same finite index, so this description is independent of J. A test 1_J has nonzero integral. Right translation by l acts on this one-dimensional quotient by chi(l)^-1. Taking the further quotient by the kernel of P therefore proves C_c^infinity(H/L_g)_(H,chi) = 0       if chi|L_g is nontrivial, C_c^infinity(H/L_g)_(H,chi) = C       if chi|L_g=1. In the second case its nonzero functional is weighted orbital integration: chi descends to the quotient and one integrates chi(x)f(xL_g) against the positive invariant measure du dv. These assertions are proved from finite test partitions, not an invoked Frobenius-descent theorem. [step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Compatible representatives and sigma on each surviving fiber. The required character condition chi|L_g=1 says exactly psi_N(h)=psi_N(g^-1 h g) on P_w. For every allowed root x_ij(t), where i<j and r=w^-1(i)<s=w^-1(j), direct conjugation gives x_rs((a_s/a_r)t). Compatibility is therefore: both roots nonsimple; or both simple and a_s=a_r. Exactly one simple root is incompatible. Equality psi(ct)=psi(dt) for EVERY t forces c=d, since a nonzero c-d multiplies K onto itself. No faithfulness assumption on psi is used. The elementary block calculation then gives exactly w=w0 r, where r reverses each positional block in a composition of n, and a is constant on each such block. Here sigma(w a)=w a': a'_j=a_(n+1-w(j))=a_(r(j))=a_j. Thus every compatible representative is literally fixed. The details are in the explicitly written common matrix calculation; all its steps are valid over any field, not finite-field counting identities. Set theta(u,v)=(sigma(v)^-1,sigma(u)^-1). This is an automorphism of H with theta^2=1 and chi composed with theta=chi. It satisfies sigma(h g)=theta(h)sigma(g) for the H-action. At a compatible g it preserves L_g and gives the induced involution sigma on H/L_g. The positive invariant integration on H/L_g is unique up to a positive scalar. This follows from the homogeneous-fiber calculation with the TRIVIAL character: every invariant linear functional is a scalar multiple of du dv, and positivity makes the scalar positive for a nonzero positive integration. Pushforward by sigma is another positive invariant integration, hence c times it with c>0. Since sigma^2=1, c^2=1 and c=1. As theta preserves chi, weighted orbital integration is also sigma-invariant. Thus sigma is the identity on the one-dimensional twisted coinvariant of every compatible fiber. Incompatible fibers have zero coinvariant. No normal-derivative assertion has entered this argument. [step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 An elementary sigma-stable finite filtration. For g in G let r_ij(g) be the rank of the submatrix with rows i,...,n and columns 1,...,j, and put R(g)=sum_(i,j) r_ij(g). Each rank bound r_ij<=a is closed, by vanishing minors. Hence the set Z_c={R<=c} is closed: it is a finite union of intersections of such rank bounds. It is H-invariant. The operation sigma exchanges r_ij with r_(n+1-j,n+1-i), so R and each Z_c are sigma-invariant. There are finitely many possible c, with Z_c eventually empty or all G. Gaussian elimination gives G as the finite disjoint union of S_w=N w T N, and the rank vector determines w. Its explicit coordinates are U_w×T×N, by the same elimination and factorization used above; pivots nonzero on the fixed cell make the coordinate inverse continuous. Within the layer Z_c\Z_(c-1), each occurring S_w is closed and open. Indeed impose all the closed bounds r_ij<=r_ij(w). If their sum equals R(w)=c, every bound must be an equality, hence the rank vector and w agree. There are only finitely many cells in the layer, so their closedness also gives openness. This avoids importing a full Bruhat-closure theorem. Let X be such a layer and B the finite disjoint union of its tori T, one for each w. The coordinate projection p:X→B is continuous and H-invariant; its fibers are precisely the homogeneous orbits of step 6.1. Sigma permutes the base points by its explicit action on N-double cosets. At every compatible base point it fixes that point by step 7.1. For any test f on X, the restriction of f-f composed with sigma has zero twisted-coinvariant class on each fiber: on an incompatible fiber the whole quotient is zero, and on a compatible fiber sigma fixes the base point and acts as the identity on its one-dimensional quotient. The localization lemma of step 5.1 shows [f composed with sigma]=[f] in C_c^infinity(X)_(H,chi). Thus sigma is the identity on every layer's twisted coinvariants, despite the fact that it may permute incompatible individual Bruhat cells. [step 6.1, step 5.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 Passing from layers to G. Take the finite filtration of tests by the open sets G\Z_c, in reverse order. The extension exact sequence of step 5.1 identifies its successive quotients with the test spaces of the closed layers in the corresponding open subspaces. Apply the exact twisted-coinvariant functor from step 4.1. This gives a finite filtration of Q=C_c^infinity(G)_(H,chi) whose graded quotients carry the identity sigma action by step 8.1. Therefore A=sigma-1 lowers that filtration and A^M=0 for some finite M. But sigma^2=1 gives A(A+2)=0. The operator A+2 has inverse (1/2) sum_(j=0)^(M-1) (-A/2)^j. Hence A=0. Every equivariant functional D factors through Q, so D(f composed with sigma)=D(f), as claimed. This is an algebraic conclusion; no Hahn–Banach separation of a quotient is needed. [step 5.1, step 4.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra] ∎
