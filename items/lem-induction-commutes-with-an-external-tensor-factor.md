## Statement

Let $C\le P$ and $D\le Q$ be subgroups of finite groups. Let $X$ be a finite-dimensional complex $C$-module and $Y$ a finite-dimensional complex $D$-module, and let $X\boxtimes Y$ be the external tensor product, a $(C\times D)$-module ([[def-subgroup]], [[def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]], [[def-finite-dimensional-representation-of-a-group-over-a-field]], [[def-tensor-product-of-complex-representations]]). Then:

(i) There is a natural isomorphism of complex $(P\times Q)$-modules

$$
\operatorname{Ind}_{C\times D}^{P\times Q}(X\boxtimes Y)\cong\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_C^PX\bigr)\boxtimes\bigl(\operatorname{Ind}_D^QY\bigr).
$$

On characters, $\operatorname{Ind}_{C\times D}^{P\times Q}(\chi\boxtimes\psi)=(\operatorname{Ind}_C^P\chi)\boxtimes(\operatorname{Ind}_D^Q\psi)$ in $R(P\times Q)$ ([[def-induced-character-of-a-complex-representation]], [[def-virtual-character-and-character-ring-of-a-finite-group]]).

(ii) If $D=Q$, this reads $\operatorname{Ind}_{C\times Q}^{P\times Q}(X\boxtimes Y)\cong(\operatorname{Ind}_C^PX)\boxtimes Y$. If also $C=P$, then $\operatorname{Ind}_P^PX\cong X$. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite groups $P,Q$, subgroups $C\le P,D\le Q$, and finite-dimensional complex modules $X,Y$ for $C,D$.

[F1] The componentwise product $P\times Q$ is a group; the product $C\times D$ is a subgroup, with identity, products, and inverses inherited coordinatewise ([[def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]], [[def-subgroup]]).

[F2] For a finite-index inclusion $H\le G$ and a commutative ring $R$, the covariant-function model of $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G W$ is naturally isomorphic to $R[G]\otimes_{R[H]}W$ ([[def-induced-r-linear-g-module-by-h-covariant-functions]], [[rem-induced-representation-agrees-with-the-tensor-product-model]]).

[F3] The group ring $R[G]$ has basis $[g]$ and multiplication $[g][h]=[gh]$. For a subgroup $H\le G$, the basis inclusion $R[H]\hookrightarrow R[G]$ respects multiplication, so $R[G]$ is an $(R[G],R[H])$-bimodule ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]], [[def-bimodule]]).

[F4] Tensor products are generated by elementary tensors, and balanced pairings descend uniquely to homomorphisms on tensor products ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]).

[F5] The external tensor product has action $(c,d)(x\otimes y)=cx\otimes dy$ ([[def-tensor-product-of-complex-representations]]).

[F6] The character of a tensor product representation is the product of its characters ([[thm-characters-of-direct-sums-tensor-products-and-duals]]).

[F7] $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G\chi_W$ is the character of the induced representation ([[def-induced-character-of-a-complex-representation]]).

[F8] A finite-dimensional representation over a field is a finite-dimensional vector space with a group action ([[def-finite-dimensional-representation-of-a-group-over-a-field]]).

[F9] The character ring $R(G)$ is the integral span of honest complex characters ([[def-virtual-character-and-character-ring-of-a-finite-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $C\le P$ and $D\le Q$, the componentwise product $C\times D$ contains the identity and is closed under products and inverses in $P\times Q$, so it is a subgroup. The basis inclusion $\mathbb C[C\times D]\hookrightarrow\mathbb C[P\times Q]$ respects multiplication, giving the right subgroup-ring action used below. All three group indices are finite. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 On group-basis and module generators define $\Phi([(p,q)]\otimes(x\otimes y)):=([p]\otimes x)\otimes([q]\otimes y)$. For fixed $(p,q)$, the formula is complex-bilinear in $x,y$, so it descends to $X\otimes_{\mathbb C}Y$. For $(c,d)\in C\times D$, the images of $[(pc,qd)]\otimes(x\otimes y)$ and $[(p,q)]\otimes(cx\otimes dy)$ both equal $([p]\otimes cx)\otimes([q]\otimes dy)$. Thus the pairing is balanced over $\mathbb C[C\times D]$, and [F4] gives a well-defined map on the source tensor product. [F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

1.3 Define $\Psi(([p]\otimes x)\otimes([q]\otimes y)):= [(p,q)]\otimes(x\otimes y)$. Moving $c\in C$ across the first factor preserves its value because $[(pc,q)]=[(p,q)(c,1)]$ and the balanced relation moves $(c,1)$ to the action $cx$; the same check with $d\in D$ uses $[(p,qd)]=[(p,q)(1,d)]$. The formula is complex-bilinear in the two outer factors, so [F4] gives a well-defined reverse map. [F2, F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

2.1 The finite group rings in [F3] have finite bases, so their tensor models on the finite-dimensional inputs [F8] are finite-dimensional. Apply [F2] with $R=\mathbb C$ to the subgroup inclusions $C\times D\le P\times Q$, $C\le P$, and $D\le Q$; it identifies the induction terms with the group-ring tensor models used above. [F2, F3, F8, step 1.1]

2.2 The composites $\Psi\Phi$ and $\Phi\Psi$ fix every displayed group-basis and pure-tensor generator. These generators span their respective tensor products by [F3, F4], so the composites are identity maps and $\Phi$ is an isomorphism. [F3, F4, step 1.2, step 1.3]

2.3 Left multiplication by $(p_0,q_0)$ sends $[(p,q)]$ to $[(p_0p,q_0q)]$, which $\Phi$ sends to the corresponding left actions on both target factors. The same formula commutes with $C$- and $D$-module homomorphisms on $X,Y$, so the isomorphism is $(P\times Q)$-equivariant and natural. [F2, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 The tensor-model identifications in step 2.1 turn $\Phi$ into the module isomorphism in (i). If $D=Q$, the canonical map $\mathbb C[Q]\otimes_{\mathbb C[Q]}Y\to Y$, $[q]\otimes y\mapsto qy$, is an isomorphism with inverse $y\mapsto[e_Q]\otimes y$; if also $C=P$, the same identity gives $\operatorname{Ind}_P^PX\cong X$. Thus (ii) holds. [F1, F2, step 2.2, step 2.3]

4.1 Since $X,Y$ are finite-dimensional [F8] and the finite group rings in [F3] have finite bases, the tensor models of step 2.1 are finite-dimensional and have characters. Taking characters of the isomorphism in step 3.1 and applying [F6] to the two representations pulled back along the coordinate projections of $P\times Q$ gives the stated identity in $R(P\times Q)$ by [F7] and [F9]. All maps were defined explicitly, so no choice principle is used. [F3, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 3.1] ∎
