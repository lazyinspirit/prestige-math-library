## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Fix $0<r<1$ and put $L:=-\log r>0$, and
consider the vertical strip, the finite annulus, the left half-plane and the
punctured disc

$$S_r:=\{w\in\mathbb C:\log r<\operatorname{Re}w<0\},\qquad A_r:=\{z\in\mathbb C:r<|z|<1\}=A(0;r,1),$$

$$H_-:=\{w\in\mathbb C:\operatorname{Re}w<0\},\qquad \mathbb D^*:=\{z\in\mathbb C:0<|z|<1\}=A(0;0,1).$$

Then the following hold.

1. $\exp:S_r\to A_r$ and $\exp:H_-\to\mathbb D^*$ are covering maps, and
   $\exp^{-1}(A_r)=S_r$, $\exp^{-1}(\mathbb D^*)=H_-$: the exponential maps
   the vertical strip onto the finite annulus and the left half-plane onto the
   punctured disc.
2. $\operatorname{Deck}(\exp|_{S_r})=\{\,w\mapsto w+2\pi ik:k\in\mathbb Z\,\}$
   and $\operatorname{Deck}(\exp|_{H_-})=\{\,w\mapsto w+2\pi ik:k\in\mathbb Z\,\}$:
   both deck groups are infinite cyclic, generated by the translation
   $\tau(w)=w+2\pi i$.
3. $S_r$ and $H_-$ are biholomorphic to $\mathbb D$; consequently both are
   simply connected, the two exponential maps are universal covering spaces,
   and $A_r$ and $\mathbb D^*$ have hyperbolic universal-covering type
   ([[def-universal-covering-type-riemann-surface]]).

**On the modulus contrast.** The finite annulus $A_r$ carries the modulus
parameter $(-\log r)/(2\pi)$ determined by its inner radius, while the
punctured disc $\mathbb D^*$ has a cusp end at the puncture; the two surfaces
are homeomorphic, and this example does not attempt to prove that they are
non-biholomorphic, which needs a conformal invariant such as extremal length.
By the cover classification every connected Riemann surface, in particular each
of $A_r$ and $\mathbb D^*$, is biholomorphic to a quotient of exactly one of
the three models by a group of holomorphic automorphisms acting freely and
properly discontinuously
([[cor-universal-cover-classification-riemann-surfaces]]).

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a real number $0<r<1$ and the sets $S_r$,
$A_r$, $H_-$, $\mathbb D^*$ above
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-natural-logarithm]],
[[def-complex-annulus]], [[def-riemann-surface-and-holomorphic-atlas]]).

[A1] The Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]) is used only through the
universal-covering-type definition [F11] and the cover classification [F12],
both of which assume it, and as Countable Choice
([[def-countable-choice]]) in the lifted structure [F21]; the remaining argument makes only finite or canonical
choices.

[F1] Kernel and fibres of the exponential ([[thm-kernel-and-fibres-of-complex-exponential]]): $\ker(\exp)=2\pi i\mathbb Z$, and $\exp z=\exp w$ holds exactly when $z-w\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$.

[F2] Cartesian form and modulus ([[cor-complex-exponential-cartesian-form-modulus-and-eulers-identity]]): for real $x,y$, $\exp(x+iy)=e^x(\cos y+i\sin y)$ and $|\exp(x+iy)|=e^x$.

[F3] The real exponential is onto $(0,\infty)$ ([[cor-exponential-is-a-bijection-onto-positive-reals]]): $\exp:\mathbb R\to(0,\infty)$ is a bijection.

[F4] Strict monotonicity ([[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]]): $x\mapsto e^x$ is continuous and strictly increasing on $\mathbb R$.

[F5] The real logarithm ([[def-natural-logarithm]]): for $x>0$, $\log x$ is the unique real $y$ with $e^y=x$, so $\log$ is the inverse function of the real exponential and both are increasing.

[F6] The principal logarithm ([[thm-principal-logarithm-biholomorphism-to-the-principal-strip]]): the principal logarithm is a biholomorphism from the slit plane $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$ onto the horizontal strip $\{w:-\pi<\operatorname{Im}w<\pi\}$, with inverse the exponential restricted to that strip; in particular $\operatorname{Log}$ is holomorphic on the slit plane, $\exp(\operatorname{Log}Z)=Z$ there, and $\operatorname{Log}(\exp w)=w$ for $|\operatorname{Im}w|<\pi$.

[F7] The open mapping theorem ([[thm-open-mapping-theorem-holomorphic-functions]]): every nonconstant holomorphic function on a complex domain is an open map.

[F8] Covering maps and evenly covered neighbourhoods ([[def-covering-map-and-evenly-covered-neighbourhoods]]): a covering map is a continuous surjection every point of whose base has an open neighbourhood $U$ with $p^{-1}(U)$ a disjoint union of open sheets, each mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ by $p$.

[F9] Deck transformations ([[def-deck-transformation-and-deck-group]]): a deck transformation of $p:E\to B$ is an isomorphism $h:E\to E$ over $B$, that is, a homeomorphism with $p\circ h=p$, and the deck transformations form a group.

[F10] Universal covering spaces ([[def-universal-covering-space]]): a universal covering space of $B$ is a covering map $p:\widetilde B\to B$ with $\widetilde B$ simply connected.

[F11] The universal-covering type ([[def-universal-covering-type-riemann-surface]]): the holomorphic universal cover of a connected Riemann surface is biholomorphic to exactly one of the Riemann sphere, the plane and the disc, the label is independent of the chosen cover, and the surface is hyperbolic precisely when its cover is biholomorphic to $\mathbb D$.

[F12] Cover classification ([[cor-universal-cover-classification-riemann-surfaces]]): under the Axiom of Choice every connected Riemann surface is biholomorphic to the quotient of exactly one of the Riemann sphere, the complex plane and the unit disc by a group of holomorphic automorphisms acting freely and properly discontinuously.

[F13] The three models ([[lem-three-simply-connected-models-are-inequivalent]]): $\widehat{\mathbb C}$, $\mathbb C$ and $\mathbb D$ are simply connected Riemann surfaces and no two of them are biholomorphic.

[F14] The Cayley map ([[ex-hyperbolic-disc-and-half-plane-geodesics]]): the map $C(\zeta)=(\zeta-i)/(\zeta+i)$ maps the upper half-plane $\mathbb H=\{\operatorname{Im}>0\}$ biholomorphically onto $\mathbb D$.

[F15] Annuli ([[def-complex-annulus]]): $A(a;r,R)=\{z:r<|z-a|<R\}$ is the annulus about $a$ with inner radius $r$ and outer radius $R$, and $A(a;0,R)$ is the punctured disc $0<|z-a|<R$.

[F16] Riemann surfaces ([[def-riemann-surface-and-holomorphic-atlas]]): a Riemann surface is a nonempty connected Hausdorff second countable space with a holomorphic atlas, and every nonempty connected open subset of $\mathbb C$ is one with the atlas of inclusions.

[F17] The circle parametrization ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-parametrize-the-unit-circle]]): every point of the unit circle is $(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ for a unique $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$.

[F18] Biholomorphisms ([[def-biholomorphic-map]]): a bijective holomorphic map with holomorphic inverse is a biholomorphism, and compositions and inverses of biholomorphisms are again biholomorphic.

[F19] Fundamental groups of homeomorphic spaces ([[prop-fundamental-group-is-a-functor-on-pointed-spaces]]): a homeomorphism induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups, so simple connectivity is a topological property.

[F20] Simply connected spaces ([[def-simply-connected]]): a space is simply connected when it is nonempty and path connected and its fundamental group is trivial.

[F21] The lifted holomorphic structure ([[lem-holomorphic-structure-lifts-to-covering-surface]]): under Countable Choice, a topological universal covering of a Riemann surface carries a unique complex structure making the projection a holomorphic unbranched covering, its total space is second countable, and its deck transformations are biholomorphic.

[F22] Deck transformations are isometries ([[thm-deck-transformations-are-hyperbolic-isometries]]): for a disc uniformization of a hyperbolic surface, every deck transformation preserves the pulled-back Poincaré metric, its lengths and its distance, and the quotient metric is the surface Poincaré metric.



**Proof technique:** direct.

## Verification

1.1 **Setup.** $S_r$ and $H_-$ are open vertical strips and half-planes, hence convex and connected, and $A_r=A(0;r,1)$, $\mathbb D^*=A(0;0,1)$ are the annuli of inner radius $r$ and $0$; all four sets are nonempty connected open subsets of $\mathbb C$, hence connected Riemann surfaces with the atlas of inclusions. [F15, F16, given]

1.2 **Injectivity on small discs.** If $|w-w'|<2\pi$ and $\exp w=\exp w'$, then $w-w'\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ [F1], so either $w=w'$ or $|w-w'|\ge2\pi$, a contradiction; hence $\exp$ is injective on every subset of diameter $<2\pi$, in particular on every disc of radius at most $\pi$. [F1]

1.3 **The principal logarithm.** The principal logarithm $\operatorname{Log}$ is a biholomorphism from the slit plane $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$ onto the horizontal strip $P=\{w:|\operatorname{Im}w|<\pi\}$, with inverse $\exp|_P$; thus $\operatorname{Log}$ is holomorphic, $\exp(\operatorname{Log}Z)=Z$ for $Z$ in the slit plane, and $\operatorname{Log}(\exp w)=w$ whenever $|\operatorname{Im}w|<\pi$ [F6]. [F6]

2.1 **The preimages of the two bases.** For $w\in\mathbb C$ one has $|\exp w|=e^{\operatorname{Re}w}$ [F2], so $\exp w\in A_r$ if and only if $r<e^{\operatorname{Re}w}<1$, if and only if $\log r<\operatorname{Re}w<0$, and $\exp w\in\mathbb D^*$ if and only if $0<e^{\operatorname{Re}w}<1$, that is $\operatorname{Re}w<0$; the equivalences use that $x\mapsto e^x$ is strictly increasing with inverse $\log$ [F3, F4, F5]. Hence $\exp^{-1}(A_r)=S_r$ and $\exp^{-1}(\mathbb D^*)=H_-$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 **Translation invariance.** For each $k\in\mathbb Z$ the translation $\tau_k(w):=w+2\pi ik$ preserves real parts, hence maps $S_r$ onto $S_r$ and $H_-$ onto $H_-$, with inverse $\tau_{-k}$; and $\exp\circ\tau_k=\exp$ on all of $\mathbb C$ because $2\pi ik\in\ker\exp$ [F1]. In particular $\tau:=\tau_1$ is the translation by $2\pi i$. [F1, step 1.1]

2.3 **The strip is biholomorphic to the half-plane.** Define $\varphi(w):=\exp\bigl(i\pi(w-\log r)/L\bigr)$ on $\mathbb C$. For $w=x+iy\in S_r$ one has $\varphi(w)=e^{-\pi y/L}e^{i\pi(x-\log r)/L}$ [F2], where the modulus is positive and the argument $\pi(x-\log r)/L$ lies in $(0,\pi)$, so $\varphi(w)\in\mathbb H$. Conversely, for $Z\in\mathbb H$ put $\psi(Z):=\log r-\frac{iL}{\pi}\operatorname{Log}Z$; since $\mathbb H$ is contained in the slit plane, $\psi$ is holomorphic [F6, F18], and writing $\operatorname{Log}Z=u+iv$ with $v\in(-\pi,\pi)$ one has $\operatorname{Im}Z=e^u\sin v>0$, so $v\in(0,\pi)$ and $\psi(Z)=\log r+\frac{Lv}{\pi}-\frac{iLu}{\pi}$ has real part in $(\log r,0)$, that is $\psi(Z)\in S_r$. The identity $\frac{i\pi(\psi(Z)-\log r)}{L}=\operatorname{Log}Z$ gives $\varphi(\psi(Z))=\exp(\operatorname{Log}Z)=Z$ [F6], and for $w\in S_r$ the number $\frac{i\pi(w-\log r)}{L}$ lies in $P$ and exponentiates to $\varphi(w)$, so it equals $\operatorname{Log}\varphi(w)$ by the injectivity of $\exp|_P$ in [F6], and therefore $\psi(\varphi(w))=w$. Thus $\varphi:S_r\to\mathbb H$ is a bijection, holomorphic with holomorphic inverse: a biholomorphism [F18]. [F2, F6, F18, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Both restrictions are onto.** Let $z\in A_r$; by [F17] there is $\theta\in[0,2\pi)$ with $z=|z|(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)$, and by [F3, F5] there is a unique real $x\in(\log r,0)$ with $e^x=|z|$; then $\exp(x+i\theta)=e^x(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta)=z$ [F2], so $z\in\exp(S_r)$, and with step 2.1 the image of $S_r$ is exactly $A_r$. The same argument with $x<0$ and arbitrary $\theta$ gives $\exp(H_-)=\mathbb D^*$. [F2, F3, F5, F17, step 2.1]

3.2 **Biholomorphisms onto the disc.** The map $w\mapsto-iw$ is a biholomorphism $H_-\to\mathbb H$ with inverse $w\mapsto iw$, since it is complex linear, bijective, and $\operatorname{Im}(-iw)=-\operatorname{Re}w>0$ exactly when $w\in H_-$ [F18]; and by [F14] the Cayley map $C$ is a biholomorphism $\mathbb H\to\mathbb D$. Hence $C\circ(-i\,\cdot\,)$ is a biholomorphism $H_-\to\mathbb D$ and $C\circ\varphi$ is a biholomorphism $S_r\to\mathbb D$, compositions of biholomorphisms being biholomorphic [F18]. [F14, F18, step 2.3]

4.1 **Evenly covered neighbourhoods over the annulus.** Let $z_0\in A_r$ and, by step 3.1, choose $w_0\in S_r$ with $\exp w_0=z_0$. Since $S_r$ is open and $w_0\in S_r$, choose $0<\delta<\pi$ with $D(w_0,\delta)\subseteq S_r$, and put $U:=\exp(D(w_0,\delta))$. Then $U$ is open, because $\exp$ is a nonconstant holomorphic function on the domain $D(w_0,\delta)$ [F7], and $z_0\in U\subseteq A_r$ by step 2.1. By [F1], $\exp^{-1}(U)=\bigcup_{k\in\mathbb Z}\bigl(D(w_0,\delta)+2\pi ik\bigr)$: indeed $\exp w\in U$ holds exactly when $\exp w=\exp w'$ for some $w'\in D(w_0,\delta)$, that is, when $w-w'\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ for such a $w'$. By step 2.2 every sheet $D(w_0,\delta)+2\pi ik$ is contained in $S_r$, and two distinct ones are disjoint, since an element of their intersection would exhibit $w_1,w_2\in D(w_0,\delta)$ with $w_1-w_2=2\pi i(l-k)\ne0$ of modulus $\ge2\pi$, while $|w_1-w_2|<2\delta<2\pi$. Finally, $\exp$ is injective on each sheet by step 1.2 and maps it onto $U$, using $\exp(w'+2\pi ik)=\exp w'$ [F1]; hence $U$ is an evenly covered neighbourhood of $z_0$ with sheets $D(w_0,\delta)+2\pi ik$. [F1, F7, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 1.2]

4.2 **Simple connectivity of the covering domains.** The disc $\mathbb D$ is simply connected [F13], hence nonempty, path connected and with trivial fundamental group [F20]; a biholomorphism is a homeomorphism, and a homeomorphism induces an isomorphism of fundamental groups [F19], so the biholomorphic images $S_r$ and $H_-$ are nonempty, path connected and have trivial fundamental group: they are simply connected [F19, F20, step 3.2]. [F13, F19, F20, step 3.2]

5.1 **Evenly covered neighbourhoods over the punctured disc.** Let $z_0\in\mathbb D^*$ and choose $w_0\in H_-$ with $\exp w_0=z_0$ (step 3.1), and $0<\delta<\min(\pi,-\operatorname{Re}w_0)$, so that $D(w_0,\delta)\subseteq H_-$; the same computation as in step 4.1 shows that $U:=\exp(D(w_0,\delta))$ is an open neighbourhood of $z_0$ contained in $\mathbb D^*$ whose preimage is the disjoint union of the sheets $D(w_0,\delta)+2\pi ik$, each mapped homeomorphically onto $U$ by $\exp$. [F1, F7, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 1.2]

5.2 **The exponential over the annulus is a covering.** The map $\exp:S_r\to A_r$ is continuous, its image is all of $A_r$ (step 3.1), and every $z_0\in A_r$ has the evenly covered neighbourhood produced in step 4.1; hence it is a covering map [F8], and $\exp^{-1}(A_r)=S_r$ is step 2.1. [F8, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 **The exponential over the punctured disc is a covering.** The same argument with step 5.1 shows that $\exp:H_-\to\mathbb D^*$ is a covering map, and $\exp^{-1}(\mathbb D^*)=H_-$ is step 2.1. [F8, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1]

6.2 **The deck group over the annulus.** Since $\exp:S_r\to A_r$ is a covering map (step 5.2), its deck transformations are the homeomorphisms $h:S_r\to S_r$ with $\exp\circ h=\exp$ [F9]; let $h$ be one of them. For $w\in S_r$ the equality $\exp(h(w))=\exp(w)$ gives $h(w)-w\in2\pi i\mathbb Z$ [F1], and $w\mapsto h(w)-w$ is a continuous map from the connected strip $S_r$ into the discrete set $2\pi i\mathbb Z$, hence is constant: $h(w)=w+2\pi ik$ for a fixed $k\in\mathbb Z$. Conversely every $\tau_k$ is a homeomorphism of $S_r$ onto itself with $\exp\circ\tau_k=\exp$ (step 2.2). Therefore $\operatorname{Deck}(\exp|_{S_r})=\{\tau_k:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, an infinite cyclic group generated by $\tau_1$, since $\tau_k\circ\tau_l=\tau_{k+l}$ and $\tau_1^k=\tau_k$. [F1, F9, step 1.1, step 2.2]

7.1 **The deck group over the punctured disc.** Since $\exp:H_-\to\mathbb D^*$ is a covering map (step 6.1), the identical argument on the connected half-plane $H_-$ gives $\operatorname{Deck}(\exp|_{H_-})=\{\tau_k:k\in\mathbb Z\}$, again infinite cyclic and generated by $\tau_1$. [F1, F9, step 1.1, step 2.2]

7.2 **Universal covers and hyperbolic type.** By steps 5.2, 6.1 and 4.2 the two exponential maps are covering maps with simply connected total space, hence universal covering spaces [F10]; the complex structures of $S_r$ and $H_-$ as open subsets of $\mathbb C$ make $\exp$ holomorphic, so by the uniqueness of the lifted structure [F21] these are the holomorphic universal covers of $A_r$ and $\mathbb D^*$. The type definition [F11] then assigns to each of the two connected Riemann surfaces its unique type; since the exhibited covers are biholomorphic to $\mathbb D$ (steps 2.3, 3.2) and no two of the three models are biholomorphic [F13], both $A_r$ and $\mathbb D^*$ have hyperbolic universal-covering type. Under the Axiom of Choice [A1], the cover classification [F12] moreover exhibits each of the two surfaces as a quotient of $\mathbb D$ by a group of holomorphic automorphisms acting freely and properly discontinuously, namely the conjugates of the deck groups of steps 6.2 and 7.1 under the uniformizations of steps 2.3 and 3.2. [A1, F10, F11, F12, F13, F21, step 2.3, step 3.2, step 4.2, step 5.2, step 6.1]

8.1 **Conclusion.** Steps 6.2 and 7.1 identify the two deck groups as the infinite cyclic groups generated by the translation $\tau(w)=w+2\pi i$, and step 7.2 identifies both base surfaces as hyperbolic; moreover, by [F22] the deck translations preserve the pulled-back Poincaré metric, its lengths and its distance of the corresponding uniformization. This proves all three assertions of the Example. [F22, step 6.2, step 7.1, step 7.2] ∎
