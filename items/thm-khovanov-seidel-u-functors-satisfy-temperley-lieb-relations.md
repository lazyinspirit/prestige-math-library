## Statement

Fix $m\ge1$, and for $1\le i\le m$ let $U_i=P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP$ be the
graded $(A_m,A_m)$-bimodule of
[[def-two-sided-projective-khovanov-seidel-bimodule-functors]], with the tensor
functor $U_i(-)=U_i\otimes_{A_m}-$ on the category
$A_m\text{-mod}$ of [[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]].

1. **Square.** For every $1\le i\le m$ there is an isomorphism of graded
   $(A_m,A_m)$-bimodules
   $$U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_i\;\cong\;U_i\oplus U_i\{1\},$$
   hence an isomorphism of graded functors
   $U_iU_i\cong U_i\oplus U_i\{1\}$ natural in the argument module.
2. **Triple.** For every $1\le i\le m$ with $1\le i\pm1\le m$ there is an
   isomorphism of graded $(A_m,A_m)$-bimodules
   $$U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_{i\pm1}\otimes_{A_m}U_i\;\cong\;U_i\{1\},$$
   again natural as an isomorphism of graded functors.
3. **Orthogonality.** If $|i-j|>1$ then $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_j=0$ as a graded
   bimodule, so $U_iU_j$ is the zero functor.

Here $U_iU_j$ abbreviates the composite tensor functor
$M\mapsto U_i\otimes_{A_m}(U_j\otimes_{A_m}M)$, which is identified with
$(U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_j)\otimes_{A_m}-$ by the associativity isomorphism of
[[lem-graded-balanced-tensor-and-shift-isomorphisms]]. The three families are
the Temperley–Lieb relations of the source in the form needed on this page;
they do **not** assert the braid relations between the invertible complexes
$R_i$, which belong to a later stage.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the algebra $A_m$ with vertices $(j)$, arrows $(j|j\pm1)$ and returns $(j|j-1|j)$, the vertex projectives $P_i=A_me_i$ and ${}_iP=e_iA_m$, and indices $1\le i,j\le m$.

[F1] The product in $A_m$ is the left-to-right concatenation of paths, $0$ when the paths do not compose; a path lies in $P_j=A_me_j$ exactly when it ends at $j$ and in ${}_jP=e_jA_m$ exactly when it begins at $j$; $A_m=\bigoplus_jP_j=\bigoplus_j{}_jP$ ([[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]], [[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]]).

[L2] The classes of the $m+1$ vertices, the $2m$ arrows $(j|j\pm1)$ and the $m$ returns $(j|j-1|j)$ for $1\le j\le m$ form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $A_m$; every path of length at least three has class $0$, the monotone and reverse monotone length-two paths have class $0$, the return $(0|1|0)$ has class $0$, and at an interior vertex the two returns have the same class ([[lem-the-khovanov-seidel-algebra-has-the-four-m-plus-one-path-basis]]).

[L3] $A_m$ is graded by internal degree with $\deg e_j=\deg(j|j+1)=0$ and $\deg(j+1|j)=1$, additively over concatenation, and $A_m\text{-mod}$ is abelian with degreewise kernels and cokernels; the internal shift $M\{r\}$ is an automorphism and every $P_j$ and ${}_jP$ is finite graded projective, generated by $e_j$ ([[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]], [[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]]).

[L4] For graded bimodules over graded algebras the balanced tensor is associative, the tensor-unit maps $R\otimes_RN\to N$ and $M\otimes_RR\to M$ are degree-zero isomorphisms compatible with outer actions, and $M\{r\}\otimes_RN\{s\}\cong(M\otimes_RN)\{r+s\}$ naturally ([[lem-graded-balanced-tensor-and-shift-isomorphisms]]).

[L5] The balanced tensor of graded modules carries the total-degree grading in which a homogeneous elementary tensor has degree the sum of the degrees of its factors, and outer actions make it a graded module on the appropriate side ([[def-graded-balanced-tensor-product-and-homogeneous-hom]]).

[L6] If a graded $(B,A)$-bimodule $M$ is flat as an underlying right $A$-module, then $M\otimes_A-$ is exact on graded left $A$-modules ([[thm-bimodule-tensor-exactness-and-projective-preservation]]).

[F7] Every projective left or right module over an arbitrary unital ring is flat on its side, without the Axiom of Choice ([[lem-projective-modules-are-flat-over-an-arbitrary-ring]]).

[L8] A graded module is finite graded projective if and only if it is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of internal shifts of the regular module, and every direct summand of a projective object is projective ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]], [[thm-a-direct-summand-of-a-projective-is-projective]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Corners.* For $0\le i,j\le m$ the graded abelian group $e_iA_me_j$ has $\mathbb Z$-basis consisting of the classes of the paths from $i$ to $j$ of length at most two: the vertex $e_i$ when $j=i$, the arrow $(i|i+1)$ when $j=i+1$, the arrow $(i|i-1)$ when $j=i-1$, and the return $(i|i-1|i)$ when $j=i\ge1$; in particular $e_iA_me_j=0$ whenever $|i-j|>1$, and $e_iA_me_i=\mathbb Ze_i\oplus\mathbb Z(i|i-1|i)$ is free of rank $2$ for $1\le i\le m$, while $e_0A_me_0=\mathbb Ze_0$ has rank $1$. Indeed every basis path of [L2] of length $\le2$ has prescribed source and target, the length-two basis elements are the returns $(1|0|1),\dots,(m|m-1|m)$, all other length-two paths are $0$ in $A_m$ by [L2], and all longer paths vanish by [L2]. [L2, F1]

2.1 *The tensor cancellation ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}P_j\cong e_iA_me_j$.* By [L3] the module ${}_iP$ is finite graded projective on the right, hence its underlying ungraded right $A_m$-module is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of shifts of $A_m$ by [L8], hence is a direct summand of a free module and projective by [L8], and hence flat by [F7]; by [L6] the functor ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}-$ is exact on graded left $A_m$-modules. The kernel of the degree-zero surjection $A_m\to P_j$, $x\mapsto xe_j$, is the graded submodule $\bigoplus_{k\ne j}P_k$ of [F1], so exactness gives an isomorphism ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}P_j\cong{}_iP\otimes_{A_m}A_m\big/\text{image of }{}_iP\otimes_{A_m}\bigoplus_{k\ne j}P_k$; the unit isomorphism of [L4] identifies ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}A_m\cong{}_iP$ by the multiplication map, and the image of the kernel is ${}_iP\cdot\bigoplus_{k\ne j}P_k=\sum_{k\ne j}e_iA_me_k$, so ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}P_j\cong e_iA_me_j$, the multiplication map $y\otimes x\mapsto yx$ being the isomorphism. [step 1.1, L3, L4, L6, L8, F7]

3.1 *Products and orthogonality.* By the associativity and unit isomorphisms of [L4] and step 2.1,
$$U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_j=P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP\otimes_{A_m}P_j\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_jP\cong P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}e_iA_me_j\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_jP,$$
with the total-degree grading of [L5], as graded $(A_m,A_m)$-bimodules. If $|i-j|>1$ then $e_iA_me_j=0$ by step 1.1, so $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_j=0$ and claim 3 holds. [step 1.1, L4, L5]

4.1 *The square relation.* Take $j=i$ in step 3.1 and use the basis $e_iA_me_i=\mathbb Ze_i\oplus\mathbb Z(i|i-1|i)$ of step 1.1, valid because $i\ge1$: distributing the tensor over the direct sum, $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_i\cong\bigl(P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Ze_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP\bigr)\oplus\bigl(P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z(i|i-1|i)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP\bigr)$. The first summand is $U_i$ up to the degree-zero isomorphism $x\otimes e_i\otimes y\mapsto x\otimes y$, since $xe_i=x$ for $x\in P_i$; the second is $U_i\{1\}$ by the shift clause of [L4], because the return $(i|i-1|i)$ has degree $1$ by [L3] and $\mathbb Z(i|i-1|i)$ placed in degree $1$ is a shift of the free rank-one module; hence $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_i\cong U_i\oplus U_i\{1\}$ as graded bimodules, including $i=m$, where $(m|m-1|m)$ is the unique return at $m$; applying $-\otimes_{A_m}M$ termwise gives the natural isomorphism of functors $U_iU_i\cong U_i\oplus U_i\{1\}$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L3, L4, L5]

4.2 *The triple relations.* Apply step 3.1 twice, to $(i,i\pm1)$ and to $(i\pm1,i)$: the $A_m$-pairings have already been consumed by the identifications ${}_iP\otimes_{A_m}P_{i\pm1}\cong e_iA_me_{i\pm1}$ and ${}_{i\pm1}P\otimes_{A_m}P_i\cong e_{i\pm1}A_me_i$ of step 2.1, and the remaining pairings are the $\mathbb Z$-pairings of the outer factors, so by the associativity of [L4] the triple tensor is $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_{i\pm1}\otimes_{A_m}U_i\cong P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\bigl(e_iA_me_{i\pm1}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}e_{i\pm1}A_me_i\bigr)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP$. By step 1.1 the corner $e_iA_me_{i+1}=\mathbb Z(i|i+1)$ is free of rank one on a degree-zero element and $e_{i+1}A_me_i=\mathbb Z(i+1|i)$ on a degree-one element, so their tensor over $\mathbb Z$ is the free rank-one group generated by $(i|i+1)\otimes(i+1|i)$, which is nonzero because it is the elementary tensor of two basis elements in free rank-one abelian groups; its path product is $(i|i+1|i)$, equal at the interior vertex $i$, where $0<i<m$ because $1\le i\le m-1$ here, to the nonzero basis element $(i|i-1|i)$ of $e_iA_me_i$ of step 1.1; its total degree is $0+1=1$. Likewise $e_iA_me_{i-1}=\mathbb Z(i|i-1)$ is generated by a degree-one element and $e_{i-1}A_me_i=\mathbb Z(i-1|i)$ by a degree-zero element, so the middle tensor is free of rank one in total degree $1$ in both cases, on the basis element $(i|i-1|i)$ of step 1.1 in the second case. Therefore $U_i\otimes_{A_m}U_{i\pm1}\otimes_{A_m}U_i\cong P_i\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z\{1\}\otimes_{\mathbb Z}{}_iP\cong U_i\{1\}$ by the shift clause of [L4], and tensoring with $M$ gives the natural isomorphism of functors; the intermediate factor is placed in degree $1$ in both the $i+1$ and the $i-1$ case, so the two arrows of the triple contribute total degree $1$ regardless of direction. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L2, L3, L4]

5.1 *Conclusion.* The square, triple and orthogonality relations of claims 1 to 3 are steps 4.1, 4.2 and 3.1. Every isomorphism displayed is induced by the identity on the outer factors $P_i$ and ${}_iP$ together with the canonical tensor and unit isomorphisms of [L4] and the rank-one degree computations of steps 1.1 and 4.2, so each is a degree-zero bimodule isomorphism natural in the factor that carries the argument module, and each arises from the identity on the path basis; hence $U_iU_i\cong U_i\oplus U_i\{1\}$, $U_iU_{i\pm1}U_i\cong U_i\{1\}$ and $U_iU_j=0$ for $|i-j|>1$ as natural graded functors. Nothing here compares the two-sided complexes $R_i$ and $R_{i+1}$, and no braid relation is asserted. The bases used are finite, the shifts are indexed by the finitely many corner paths of step 1.1, and no choice principle is used. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2] ∎
