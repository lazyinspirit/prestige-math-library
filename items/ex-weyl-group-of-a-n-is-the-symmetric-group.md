## Example

$W(A_n)\cong S_{n+1}$, acting on the sum-zero subspace of
$\mathbb R^{n+1}$ by permuting coordinates.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The model $A_n=\{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j:i\ne j\}\subseteq\{x:\sum_ix_i=0\}$ and its reflections.

[L1] The reflection $s_{\alpha}$ for $\alpha=\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j$ acts on the coordinate vectors by $e_i\mapsto e_j$, $e_j\mapsto e_i$ and $e_k\mapsto e_k$ for $k\notin\{i,j\}$ ([[ex-classical-root-systems-in-euclidean-coordinates]]).

[L2] The Weyl group is generated by the root reflections ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every root reflection $s_{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j}$ is the transposition $(ij)$ acting on the coordinates; since the coordinate count is $n+1$, the map $s_{\varepsilon_i-\varepsilon_j}\mapsto(ij)$ extends to a group homomorphism $W(A_n)\to S_{n+1}$ whose image contains the transpositions, hence is all of $S_{n+1}$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 The homomorphism is injective: an element of $W(A_n)$ acting trivially on all coordinates is the identity on $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, hence on the sum-zero subspace. Therefore $W(A_n)\cong S_{n+1}$, acting by coordinate permutations as asserted. [L1, L2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
