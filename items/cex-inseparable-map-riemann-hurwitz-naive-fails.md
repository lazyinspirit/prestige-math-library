## Statement refuted

The proposed extension
$$\omega_C\cong f^*\omega_D\otimes\mathcal O_C(R_f^{\mathrm{tor}})$$
of the canonical bundle formula to every finite surjective morphism of smooth
proper geometrically integral curves is false when
$$R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}=\sum_p\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{C,p}}\bigl(\operatorname{tors}(\Omega_{C/D})_p\bigr)[p],$$
where the summation is over closed points and $\operatorname{tors}$ means the
torsion subsheaf of the relative differentials. The separable theorem
[[thm-canonical-bundle-ramification-formula]] defines its different divisor
only when $k(C)/k(D)$ is separable; $R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}$ here is a proposed
candidate extension, not that theorem's different divisor. The witness is the
$p$-th-power map in characteristic $p>0$,
$\varphi\colon\mathbb P^1_k\to\mathbb P^1_k$,
$[s:t]\mapsto[s^p:t^p]$. Its relative differentials are invertible, so their
torsion subsheaf is zero and $R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}=0$. But
$\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O(-2[\infty])$ and
$\varphi^*\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O(-2p[\infty])$ are not isomorphic.
This refutes extending the separable formula by the torsion-submodule recipe;
it does not assign the separable different divisor to an inseparable map.
The indices at $0$ and $\infty$ are both $p$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ of characteristic $p>0$, the projective line with coordinates $x$ on $U_0$ and $y=x^{-1}$ on $U_\infty$, and the morphism $\varphi$ given by $x\mapsto x^p$ and $y\mapsto y^p$. The Axiom of Choice is assumed wherever required by the cited projective-line, finite-map, and principal-divisor suppliers below.

[F1] The projective line has the two affine charts $U_0=\operatorname{Spec}k[x]$ and $U_\infty=\operatorname{Spec}k[y]$, with $xy=1$ on their overlap; it is a smooth proper geometrically integral curve, and its closed-point local rings are discrete valuation rings. The point $\infty$ has uniformizer $y$. ([[def-projective-line-two-affine-cover-and-twisting-sheaf]], [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]], [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]], [[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]])

[F2] A nonconstant rational function on a smooth proper geometrically integral curve defines a finite locally free map to $\mathbb P^1$ of degree the corresponding function-field extension; for $x^p$, $[k(x):k(x^p)]=p$, with basis $1,x,\ldots,x^{p-1}$. The fibre degree formula is $\sum_{r\mapsto q}e_r[\kappa(r):\kappa(q)]=\deg(\varphi)$. ([[lem-function-with-poles-defines-map-p1]], [[lem-fibre-degree-sum-ramification-residue]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F3] At a closed point on a smooth curve the local ring is a discrete valuation ring. The ramification index is the order of the pullback of a target uniformizer; a uniformizer has order one, and orders are additive. ([[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]], [[thm-dvr-element-normal-form]], [[def-ramification-index-curve-map]])

[F4] For $B=A[T]/(g(T))$, the relative differentials are generated by $\mathrm dT$ with relation $g'(T)\,\mathrm dT=0$. ([[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]])

[F5] For smooth curves the canonical sheaf is $\omega=\Omega^1_{-/k}$ and is invertible. The different divisor in [[def-different-divisor-curve-map]] is defined from the lengths of the full relative-differential stalks only when the function-field extension is separable. ([[def-canonical-line-bundle-curve]], [[def-sheaf-relative-differentials]], [[def-different-divisor-curve-map]], [[thm-canonical-bundle-ramification-formula]])

[F6] For ring maps $A\to B\to C$, the Kähler differential sequence $$C\otimes_B\Omega_{B/A}\longrightarrow\Omega_{C/A}\longrightarrow\Omega_{C/B}\longrightarrow0$$ is exact; its first map need not be injective. This applies without separability. ([[lem-ag-differentials-transitivity]])

[F7] On $\mathbb P^1_k$, $\mathrm dx$ is a rational section of the canonical line bundle and its divisor is $-2[\infty]$, as follows from $\mathrm dx=-y^{-2}\mathrm dy$. The rational-section and Cartier-divisor dictionary identifies a line bundle with the sheaf of a divisor of any nonzero rational section; for the finite flat map $\varphi$, pullback of a Cartier divisor computes the pullback of its line bundle. An isomorphism of divisor line bundles makes their difference principal. Principal divisors on a proper curve have degree zero, and $\deg_k(\sum n_q[q])=\sum n_q[\kappa(q):k]$. ([[def-canonical-line-bundle-curve]], [[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]], [[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]], [[def-pullback-cartier-divisor]], [[lem-pullback-cartier-divisor-line-bundle]], [[thm-cartier-divisors-mod-principal-to-picard]], [[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]], [[thm-principal-divisor-degree-zero-proper-curve]])

## Construction

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$ and let $\varphi\colon\mathbb P^1_k\to\mathbb P^1_k$ be the morphism with $\varphi^{\sharp}(x)=x^p$ on the standard chart, that is $\varphi([s:t])=[s^p:t^p]$ in homogeneous coordinates. Its degree is $p$, and $e_0=e_\infty=p$. After base change to an algebraic closure, every geometric closed point is index-ramified with index $p$; the two displayed points are not the only geometric ramification points.

## Verification

1.1 The morphism, its degree and its ramification. The coordinate function $x$ has $\operatorname{ord}_\infty(x)=-1$ by [F1], so $x^p$ is nonconstant, and [F2] gives the finite surjective morphism $\varphi$ of degree $[k(\mathbb P^1_k):k(x^p)]=p$, since $1,x,\ldots,x^{p-1}$ is a basis over $k(x^p)$. On the two charts the maps are $k[x]\to k[x]$, $x\mapsto x^p$, and $k[y]\to k[y]$, $y\mapsto y^p$, because $y=x^{-1}$. The points $0$ and $\infty$ are $k$-rational with uniformizers $x$ and $y$ by [F1], so [F3] gives $e_0=\operatorname{ord}_0(x^p)=p$ and $e_\infty=\operatorname{ord}_\infty(y^p)=p$. The zero and pole fibres are supported respectively at $0$ and $\infty$; the fibre degree formula [F2] at $0$ is consistent with $\deg(\varphi)=e_0[\kappa(0):\kappa(0)]=p$. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 Ramification after geometric base change. Over $\bar k$, let $a$ be any finite target point and choose $b\in\bar k$ with $b^p=a$. The pullback of the target parameter $x-a$ is $x^p-a=(x-b)^p$, so the index at the geometric point $b$ is $p$. On the infinity chart the same calculation is $y\mapsto y^p$. Thus every geometric closed point is index-ramified. Over an imperfect original field the indices of its closed points need not all be $p$: for example, if $k=\mathbb F_p(a)$ with $a$ not a $p$-th power, the target point $t=a$ has preimage defined by the irreducible polynomial $x^p-a$. Its local uniformizer is $x^p-a$, exactly the pullback of $t-a$, so its index is $1$, while the residue extension is purely inseparable. [F3, algebra]

2.1 The pullback of differentials is the zero map. The canonical bundle of the target is generated on $U_0$ by $\mathrm dx$, and the pullback map $\varphi^{*}\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\to\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}$ sends its generator $\varphi^{*}(\mathrm dx)$ to $\mathrm d(x^p)=p\,x^{p-1}\mathrm dx=0$, because $p=0$ in $k$. The same computation in coordinate $y$ on $U_\infty$ gives $\varphi^{*}(\mathrm dy)\mapsto\mathrm d(y^p)=0$. By the right-exact transitivity sequence [F6], the map $\varphi^{*}\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\to\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}$ is followed by the quotient to $\Omega_{C/D}$. Since the first map is zero, this quotient is an isomorphism. Thus the pullback of differentials is not injective; the injectivity assertion in the canonical-bundle theorem [F5] is unavailable because its separability hypothesis fails. [F5, F6, step 1.1]

2.2 The relative differentials are invertible, with no torsion. On the source chart $U_0$ the map is $A=k[x]\to B=k[z]$, $x\mapsto z^p$, so $B=A[T]/(T^p-x)$ with $T\mapsto z$; the derivative of $g(T)=T^p-x$ is $g'(T)=pT^{p-1}=0$, and [F4] gives $\Omega_{B/A}\cong B/(g')=B$, free of rank one on $\mathrm dT$. The same holds on $U_\infty$ in coordinate $y$. Thus the relative differential sheaf for $\varphi$, $\Omega_{C/D}$, is invertible, so its torsion subsheaf is zero. Set $l_p^{\mathrm{tor}}:=\operatorname{length}_{\mathcal O_{C,p}}(\operatorname{tors}(\Omega_{C/D})_p)$; then $l_p^{\mathrm{tor}}=0$ for every closed point $p$. [F4, step 1.1]

3.1 The recipe gives $R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}=0$, but the isomorphism fails. By step 2.2 all coefficients $l_p^{\mathrm{tor}}$ vanish. The canonical divisor computation is $\operatorname{div}(\mathrm dx)=-2[\infty]$: $\mathrm dx$ is a frame on $U_0$, and $\mathrm dx=\mathrm d(y^{-1})=-y^{-2}\mathrm dy$ on $U_\infty$. Thus $\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1_k}(-2[\infty])$. The pullback divisor is $\varphi^*[\infty]=p[\infty]$, since its fibre is supported at $\infty$ with index $p$ from step 1.1; [F7] gives $\varphi^*\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O_{\mathbb P^1_k}(-2p[\infty])$. If the proposed formula held with $R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}=0$, these divisor line bundles would be isomorphic, so $(2p-2)[\infty]$ would be principal by [F7]. Its degree is $2p-2>0$, contradicting [F7], which gives degree zero for principal divisors. [F5, F7, step 1.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Conclusion. The $p$-th-power map is finite surjective of degree $p$; its indices at $0$ and $\infty$ are $p$, and after base change every geometric closed point has index $p$. Its relative differentials are invertible with zero torsion, so the proposed torsion-submodule recipe gives $R_f^{\mathrm{tor}}=0$. The formula fails because $\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O(-2[\infty])$ and $\varphi^{*}\omega_{\mathbb P^1_k}\cong\mathcal O(-2p[\infty])$ are not isomorphic. Thus separability cannot be dropped when extending the formula by this recipe, and this conclusion does not define the separable different divisor for an inseparable map. [F1, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎
