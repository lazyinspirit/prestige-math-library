## Example

Assume the dependent-choice/supplied-resolution convention for continuous
cohomology. Let $\Gamma=\langle\sigma\mid\sigma^6=1\rangle$ have
the discrete topology, and let $M=\mathbb Z/12\mathbb Z$ with the action
$\sigma\cdot m=5m$. This is a discrete continuous $\Gamma$-module, and
$H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,M)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$, generated by the
class of the normalized cocycle whose value at $\sigma$ is $2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite cyclic group $\Gamma$, the $\Gamma$-module $M$, and the
dependent-choice/supplied-resolution convention.

[A1] Dependent Choice and supplied injective-resolution data are the convention
for derived-functor cohomology ([[def-dependent-choice]],
[[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]]).

[F1] [[def-continuous-cohomology-of-a-profinite-group-with-discrete-module]]:
$H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}$ is computed by normalized continuous $1$-cocycles modulo
coboundaries.

[F2] [[thm-continuous-profinite-cohomology-as-direct-limit-of-finite-quotients]]:
$H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(G,M)$ is the filtered colimit of
$H^1(G/U,M^U)$ over open normal subgroups $U$, in particular in the compact
group branch without assuming Choice.

## Verification

1.1 Since $\Gamma$ is finite discrete, it is compact profinite; the trivial subgroup is terminal in the reverse-inclusion system of its open normal subgroups, and $M^{\{1\}}=M$. The compact branch of [F2] therefore identifies $H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,M)$ with $H^1(\Gamma,M)$. [A1, F2, algebra]

1.2 The action is well-defined because $5^2\equiv1\pmod {12}$, so $5^6\equiv1\pmod {12}$. The norm operator is multiplication by $1+5+1+5+1+5=18\equiv6\pmod {12}$, whose kernel is the even residue classes. Coboundaries have value $(\sigma-1)m=4m$ at $\sigma$, so their subgroup is $\{0,4,8\}$. [given, algebra]

2.1 A normalized $1$-cocycle $c$ is determined by $a=c(\sigma)$, since the cocycle equation gives $c(\sigma^j)=\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\sigma^i a$. The relation $\sigma^6=1$ forces $(1+\sigma+\cdots+\sigma^5)a=0$. Conversely, if this sum is zero, the displayed formula for $j=0,\ldots,5$ is well-defined modulo $6$ and satisfies $c(gh)=c(g)+g\cdot c(h)$, so it defines a normalized $1$-cocycle. [F1, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The quotient of the even residue classes by $\{0,4,8\}$ has two elements, and the class of $2$ is its nonzero element. Steps 1.1–2.1 therefore give $H^1_{\mathrm{cts}}(\Gamma,M)\cong\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ with the stated generator. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
