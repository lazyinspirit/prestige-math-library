## Statement refuted

**Refuted claim.** The **exponent sum** $\varepsilon\colon B_n\to\mathbb Z$,
defined on words by $\sigma_i^{\pm1}\mapsto\pm1$, is a *complete braid normal
form*: for all $x,y\in B_n$, if $\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon(y)$ then $x=y$.

The witness is the pair $\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in B_3$: both braids have exponent
sum $1$, and they are distinct. The refutation is robust — the two witnesses
agree not only in $\varepsilon$ but also in the $\Delta$-exponent $p=0$ and in
the number $r=1$ of simple factors of their left Garside normal forms — so no
invariant of the coarse Garside data $(\varepsilon(x),p(x),r(x))$ distinguishes
them either. In the left normal form of the theorem of Garside--Elrifai--Morton
they differ only in *which* simple factor occurs, $\sigma_1$ against
$\sigma_2$, that is, in their permutations $s_1=(1\,2)$ against
$s_2=(2\,3)$.

**What is and is not claimed.** Nothing here is a claim against the usefulness
of the exponent sum: for $n\ge2$ it is a surjective homomorphism onto $\mathbb Z$ (for $n\le1$ it is the zero homomorphism on the trivial group), it
separates $\sigma_1$ from $\sigma_1^{2}$, and for $n\ge3$ it also separates $\sigma_1$ from $\Delta$; being abelian it
is insensitive to the braid relations. What is refuted is only *completeness*.
Nor is it claimed that braid groups have no complete normal forms: the left
Garside normal form is one, and it is precisely the additional simple-factor
data (the first factor $\sigma_1$ versus $\sigma_2$) that separates the two
witnesses below.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The braid group $B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2\rangle$, that is, the case $n=3$ of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]; its positive monoid $B_3^{+}$ with the two atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, the length $\ell$ and the half twist $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1$ of length $3$; the symmetric group $S_3$ with adjacent transpositions $s_i=(i\ i+1)$; and the two elements $\sigma_1,\sigma_2\in B_3$.

[F1] $B_3=\langle\sigma_1,\sigma_2\mid R\rangle$ is a presented group in the sense of [[def-group-presentation]], where $R$ consists of the single braid relation $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]]). If $u$ is a function on the generators whose evaluation sends every relator to the identity, then $u$ extends uniquely to a homomorphism of $B_3$ ([[thm-von-dyck]], [[def-group-presentation]]).

[F2] In $S_n$ the adjacent transpositions satisfy $s_i^{2}=\mathrm{id}$, $s_is_j=s_js_i$ for $|i-j|>1$, and $s_is_{i+1}s_i=s_{i+1}s_is_{i+1}$; in the composition convention of [[def-symmetric-group]] the transpositions $s_1=(1\,2)$ and $s_2=(2\,3)$ are distinct ([[lem-reduced-adjacent-transposition-words-have-well-defined-positive-lifts]], [[def-symmetric-group]]).

[F3] $B_3^{+}$ is generated by the atoms $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$, $\ell([w])=|w|$ is additive with $\ell(z)=0$ only for $z=1$, and the half twist $\Delta=T_1T_2=\sigma_1\,(\sigma_2\sigma_1)$ of $B_3$ satisfies $\Delta=\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ ([[def-positive-braid-monoid]], [[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]], [[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]).

[F4] $a\preccurlyeq_Lb$ means $b=ac$ with $c\in B_3^{+}$, and then $\ell(a)\le\ell(b)$; in particular every left divisor of an atom has length $0$ or $1$ ([[def-left-and-right-divisibility-for-positive-braids]], [[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]]).

[F5] $B_3^{+}$ is a submonoid of $B_3$ ([[thm-the-ore-fraction-group-of-positive-braids-is-the-artin-braid-group]]).

[F6] Every $x\in B_3$ has a unique left normal form $x=\Delta^{p}a_1\cdots a_r$ with $p\in\mathbb Z$, $r\in\mathbb N$, every $a_i$ a proper simple braid and $a_i=\Delta\wedge_L(a_i\cdots a_r)$; in it $p=p(x)$ is the largest integer with $\Delta^{p}\preccurlyeq_Lx$, the product $a_1\cdots a_r$ is $A(x)=\Delta^{-p(x)}x$, and $p(x)\ge0$ exactly when $x\in B_3^{+}$ ([[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]]).



## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The exponent sum is a well-defined homomorphism.** Define $u(\sigma_i):=1\in\mathbb Z$ on the generators of [F1]. The single relator $\sigma_1\sigma_2\sigma_1=\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2$ evaluates to $1+1+1-(1+1+1)=0$. By von Dyck [F1] there is therefore a unique homomorphism $\varepsilon\colon B_3\to\mathbb Z$ with $\varepsilon(\sigma_i)=1$ for $i=1,2$; it satisfies $\varepsilon(\sigma_i^{-1})=-1$ and $\varepsilon(xy)=\varepsilon(x)+\varepsilon(y)$. [F1]

1.2 **The permutation homomorphism exists.** Define $v(\sigma_i):=s_i$. By the type-A relation of [F2], $v$ sends the three-term relator to $s_1s_2s_1(s_2s_1s_2)^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$; the further type-A relations $s_i^{2}=\mathrm{id}$ and the far commutation are properties of $S_3$ and impose no condition, because the corresponding words are not relators of [F1]. By von Dyck [F1] there is a homomorphism $\pi\colon B_3\to S_3$ with $\pi(\sigma_i)=s_i$. [F1, F2]

2.1 **The two atoms are distinct.** By step 1.2, $\pi(\sigma_1)=s_1=(1\,2)$ and $\pi(\sigma_2)=s_2=(2\,3)$, and these are distinct by [F2]. If $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$ held in $B_3$, then applying the function $\pi$ to the two sides would give $s_1=s_2$, a contradiction; hence $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$. [F2, step 1.2]

3.1 **Their exponent sums agree.** By step 1.1, $\varepsilon(\sigma_1)=1=\varepsilon(\sigma_2)$, and since $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_2$ by step 2.1, the assignment $\varepsilon$ is not injective: two distinct braids have equal exponent sum. [step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 **Both witnesses are one-factor left normal forms with $p=0$.** Fix $i\in\{1,2\}$. Since $\sigma_i\in B_3^{+}$ [F3], [F6] gives $p(\sigma_i)\ge0$; also $\Delta\not\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_i$, because a left divisor $d$ of the atom $\sigma_i$ has $\ell(d)\le1$ by [F4] while $\ell(\Delta)=3$ by [F3], so $\ell(d)<\ell(\Delta)$ and $d\ne\Delta$. For every $p\ge1$, $\Delta^{p}=\Delta\Delta^{p-1}$, so $\Delta^{p}\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_i$ would imply $\Delta\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_i$ by transitivity, which was just ruled out. Hence $0$ is the largest integer $p$ with $\Delta^{p}\preccurlyeq_L\sigma_i$, that is $p(\sigma_i)=0$ and $A(\sigma_i)=\Delta^{0}\sigma_i=\sigma_i$. Further $\Delta=\sigma_1(\sigma_2\sigma_1)$ for $i=1$ and $\Delta=\sigma_2(\sigma_1\sigma_2)$ for $i=2$ by [F3], so $\sigma_i\preccurlyeq_L\Delta$, and the greedy first factor of [F6] is $a_1=\Delta\wedge_L\sigma_i=\sigma_i$, with remainder $\sigma_i^{-1}\sigma_i=1$ computed in the group $B_3$, in which $B_3^{+}$ is a submonoid by [F5]; the factor is proper because $\sigma_i\ne1$ and $\sigma_i\ne\Delta$ by [F3], and it is simple because $\sigma_i\preccurlyeq_L\Delta$. So the left normal form of each witness is $\sigma_i=\Delta^{0}\cdot\sigma_i$ with $r=1$ simple factor, and the two witnesses also agree in the data $p=0$ and $r=1$. [F3, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 **Conclusion.** The braids $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ are distinct by step 2.1, while $\varepsilon(\sigma_1)=\varepsilon(\sigma_2)=1$ by step 3.1; moreover both have left normal form $\Delta^{0}\cdot\sigma_i$ with one proper simple factor by step 3.2. The exponent sum therefore fails to be a complete normal form, and even the triple $\bigl(\varepsilon(x),p(x),r(x)\bigr)$ fails to determine a braid. The refuted claim is false, already in $B_3$. No choice principle is used: both homomorphisms are obtained from the explicit generator assignments $u(\sigma_i)=1$ and $v(\sigma_i)=s_i$, and every computation is a finite word computation. ∎ [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2]

## Remarks

- **Why the failure is minimal.** The two witnesses are the two distinct generators of the smallest braid group that has more than one of them, $B_3$; on one generator there is no such pair to exhibit. The failure is not a defect of the presentation used to define $\varepsilon$: the homomorphism is well defined by von Dyck's theorem, as step 1.1 checks.
- **What the exponent sum does see.** It is additive, it takes the value $-1$ on each inverse letter, it is surjective for $n\ge2$ because $\varepsilon(\sigma_1)=1$, and for $n=3$ it takes the value $3$ on the half twist $\Delta$ and $6$ on the full twist $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{3}$. Being abelian data, it cannot see the non-abelian structure of the braid group, and the pair $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ is the smallest instance of that insensitivity.
- **The extra data that does separate them.** The two braids have the same $\Delta$-exponent and the same number of simple factors but different first (and only) simple factors, $\sigma_1$ against $\sigma_2$, corresponding to the distinct permutations $s_1$ and $s_2$. This is exactly the refinement supplied by the left Garside normal form of [[thm-left-garside-normal-form-is-unique]].
- No choice principle is used; only the explicitly displayed generator assignments and finite word evaluations occur.
