## Example

For every base scheme $S$, the empty morphism $\varnothing\to S$ is finite and
proper, and it is projective: with the convention that a projective morphism
is a closed immersion into some $\mathbb P^n_S$, the empty morphism factors as
the closed immersion $\varnothing\to\mathbb P^0_S$ followed by the isomorphism
$\mathbb P^0_S\cong S$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A base scheme $S$ and the unique morphism $\varnothing\to S$ from the empty scheme.

[F1] For a ring $A$ the points of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ are the prime ideals of $A$, and for the zero ring there are no proper prime ideals, so $\operatorname{Spec}0$ is empty; $\operatorname{Spec}A$ with its structure sheaf is an affine scheme, and the empty locally ringed space is a scheme. ([[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]], [[def-affine-scheme]], [[def-scheme]])

[F2] $f:X\to S$ is **finite** when for every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the inverse image is affine, $f^{-1}(U)=\operatorname{Spec}B$, and $B$ is module-finite over $A$. ([[def-finite-morphism-schemes]])

[F3] A commutative $R$-algebra $A$ is module-finite when it is generated as an $R$-module by finitely many elements; at $n=0$ the subalgebra generated by the empty family is the image of $R$ in $A$, so the zero ring is module-finite over every $R$. ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]])

[F4] $f$ is of finite type when it is locally of finite type and quasi-compact; quasi-compactness of $f$ means that $f^{-1}(V)$ is quasi-compact for every quasi-compact open $V\subseteq S$. ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]], [[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]])

[F5] A morphism $i:Z\to X$ is a **closed immersion** when its underlying map is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and $\mathcal O_X\to i_*\mathcal O_Z$ is surjective. ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]])

[F6] $f:X\to S$ is **separated** when its diagonal $\Delta_{X/S}:X\to X\times_SX$ is a closed immersion. ([[def-separated-morphism-schemes]])

[F7] $f$ is **universally closed** when for every $S$-scheme $T$ the base-changed projection $X\times_ST\to T$ is a closed map. ([[def-universally-closed-morphism]])

[F8] $f$ is **proper** when it is separated, of finite type, and universally closed. ([[def-proper-morphism]])

[F9] $f:X\to S$ is **projective on this page** when for some $n\ge0$ it factors over $S$ as $X\xrightarrow{i}\mathbb P^n_S\to S$ with $i$ a closed immersion. ([[def-projective-morphism-pre-proj]])

[F10] For $n=0$ there is one standard chart, $\mathbb P^0_S\cong S$, and for $S=\varnothing$ also $\mathbb P^n_\varnothing=\varnothing$. ([[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]])

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct: check the three properness conditions on the empty scheme, then exhibit the projective factorization.

1.1 The empty scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}0$ by [F1], so it is a scheme and the empty morphism $\varnothing\to S$ is a morphism of schemes. For every affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ the inverse image is empty, that is $\operatorname{Spec}0$, and the zero ring is module-finite over $A$ by [F3]. Hence $\varnothing\to S$ is finite by [F2]. [F1, F2, F3]

1.2 The morphism is of finite type: it is locally of finite type because the empty inverse image $\operatorname{Spec}0$ of every affine open is affine with coordinate ring $0$, which is a finitely generated $A$-algebra by [F3], and it is quasi-compact because an empty inverse image is covered by the empty finite subcover, so the condition of [F4] holds for every quasi-compact open of $S$. [F3, F4]

1.3 The morphism is separated. Its diagonal is a morphism $\Delta:\varnothing\to\varnothing\times_S\varnothing$; the fibre product of two empty schemes is empty, since both projections would have to map into the empty scheme. Thus $\Delta$ is the empty morphism $\varnothing\to\varnothing$, whose underlying map is a homeomorphism onto the closed subset $\varnothing$ of $\varnothing$ and whose structure map $\mathcal O_\varnothing\to \Delta_*\mathcal O_\varnothing$ has zero target, hence is surjective. By [F5], $\Delta$ is a closed immersion, so [F6] makes $\varnothing\to S$ separated. [F1, F5, F6]

1.4 The morphism is universally closed. For any $S$-scheme $T$ the base change $\varnothing\times_ST$ is empty, because the projection to $\varnothing$ must map into the empty scheme; the base-changed projection therefore has empty domain, and the image of its only closed subset $\varnothing$ is $\varnothing$, which is closed in $|T|$. Hence the condition of [F7] holds for every $T$, and $\varnothing\to S$ is universally closed. [F7]

2.1 Steps 1.2, 1.3 and 1.4 give finite type, separatedness and universal closedness, so $\varnothing\to S$ is proper by [F8]. [F8, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4]

3.1 Since [F10] gives $\mathbb P^0_S\cong S$, the unique morphism $i:\varnothing\to\mathbb P^0_S$ is a closed immersion by the same argument as step 1.3, and its composite with the isomorphism $\mathbb P^0_S\to S$ is the empty morphism. Thus $\varnothing\to S$ factors as a closed immersion into $\mathbb P^0_S$ followed by the projection, so it is projective by [F9]. The argument is choice-free, and the case $S=\varnothing$ is included: then the empty morphism is the identity of the empty scheme, which steps 1.1-2.1 still treat through $0=\operatorname{Spec}0$. [F1, F9, F10, step 1.3] ∎
