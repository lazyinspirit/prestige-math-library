## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $M$ be a finite flat
$R$-module. Then $M$ is finite projective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$ and a finite flat $R$-module $M$.

[L1] Over a Noetherian ring, a finite module has a finite presentation ([[thm-finite-generation-and-finite-presentation-over-a-noetherian-ring]]).

[L2] Flatness gives the equational criterion for each finite relation ([[thm-equational-criterion-for-flatness]]).

[L3] A module is projective exactly when every map from it lifts across an epimorphism ([[thm-projective-module-characterizations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.


1.1 By [L1], choose a finite presentation $R^m\xrightarrow{D}R^n\xrightarrow{\pi}M\to0$. Put $x_i=\pi(e_i)$, and write the columns of $D$ as relation rows $u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(m)}\in R^n$, so $\ker\pi$ is generated by those rows. Start with $x=A y$, where $A$ is the identity matrix and $y$ is the column of the $x_i$. [L1, given, choose]


2.1 Process the $m$ relation rows in order. Suppose $x=A y$ and the first $h-1$ rows satisfy $u^{(j)}A=0$. For the next row $u^{(h)}$, the equality $u^{(h)}x=0$ says $(u^{(h)}A)y=0$. Applying [L2] to this one finite relation gives a finite column $z$ of elements of $M$ and a matrix $B$ with $y=Bz$ and $(u^{(h)}A)B=0$. Replace $(A,y)$ by $(AB,z)$. The new matrix satisfies $u^{(h)}AB=0$, and each earlier relation remains zero because $u^{(j)}AB=(u^{(j)}A)B=0$. After the finite last stage, write $x=Cz$ with $u^{(j)}C=0$ for every $j$. This finite induction makes only finitely many choices. [L2, step 1.1, induction]


3.1 Let $z$ have $t$ entries, and define $s_0:R^n\to R^t$ by sending $e_i$ to the $i$th row of $C$. Every generator $u^{(j)}$ of $\ker\pi$ is killed by $s_0$ because $u^{(j)}C=0$. Thus $s_0$ descends to $s:M\to R^t$. Define $q:R^t\to M$ by sending its basis vectors to the entries of $z$. The identity $x=Cz$ says $qs(x_i)=x_i$ for every generator $x_i$, hence $qs=1_M$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 Let $E\twoheadrightarrow N$ be any epimorphism and $g:M\to N$ any map. For each of the finitely many basis vectors of $R^t$, choose a preimage in $E$ of its image under $gq$, and extend linearly to a lift $h:R^t\to E$. Then $hs$ lifts $g$ because $qs=1_M$. By [L3], $M$ is projective. It is finite by hypothesis, so it is finite projective. [L3, step 3.1, choose] ∎
