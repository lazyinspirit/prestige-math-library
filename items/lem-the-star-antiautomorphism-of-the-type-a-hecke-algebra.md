## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $q\in R^{\times}$, and let $H=H_{R,q}(S_n)$ be
the specialized type-A Hecke algebra with generators $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ and
relations $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$, the braid relations and the distant commutations
([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]],
[[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]). Then the
assignment $T_i\mapsto T_i$ ($1\le i<n$) extends uniquely to an $R$-algebra
anti-automorphism $*:H\to H$, i.e. an $R$-linear map with $(ab)^*=b^*a^*$ and
$1^*=1$; it is an involution, $**=\mathrm{id}_H$, and on the standard basis
$$T_w^{*}=T_{w^{-1}}\qquad(w\in S_n).$$ In particular
$(T_wT_{w^{-1}})^*=T_wT_{w^{-1}}$ and, for the symmetrizers
$m_\lambda=\sum_{w\in S_\lambda}T_w$, one has $m_\lambda^*=m_\lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a unit $q\in R^{\times}$, and the specialized type-A Hecke algebra $H=H_{R,q}(S_n)$, presented over $R$ by generators $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$ with the relations $T_i^2=(q-1)T_i+q$, $T_iT_{i+1}T_i=T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}$ ($1\le i\le n-2$) and $T_iT_j=T_jT_i$ ($|i-j|>1$).

[F1] The generic algebra and its specialization: $H_v(n)$ is the quotient of the free unital associative $A$-algebra, $A=\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$, on the $T_i$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the quadratic and braid relators; a reduced expression of $w\in S_n$ is a word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell}$ of minimal length $\ell(w)$ representing $w$, and $T_w:=T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell}$, the empty product for $w=1$; the specialization at $v_0$ is the $R$-algebra $R\otimes_AH_v(n)$, and for $v_0=q$ it is $H$, presented over $R$ by the images of the $T_i$ subject to the relations above ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] Standard basis of the generic algebra: $\{T_w:w\in S_n\}$ is an $A$-basis of $H_v(n)$, so $T_w$ is independent of the reduced expression chosen for $w$ ([[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]).

[F3] Length is inversion invariant: $\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(w)$ for every $w\in S_n$ ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[F4] For a set $X$, the finite words $X^*$ form a monoid under concatenation with the empty word as identity ([[lem-words-satisfy-the-free-monoid-universal-property]]).

[F5] The free $R$-module $R^{(X^*)}$ on the set $X^*$ has the words as an $R$-basis: every element is a unique finite $R$-linear combination $\sum_wa_ww$ ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[F6] Two-sided ideals are additive subgroups closed under multiplication on both sides; for $S\subseteq F$ the ideal $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, so any two-sided ideal containing $S$ contains $(S)$ ([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[F7] The quotient ring $F/I$ has additive cosets with $(x+I)(y+I)=xy+I$ and $R$-scalar action inherited from $F$ ([[def-quotient-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $X:=\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ and $F:=R^{(X^*)}$, the free $R$-module with the finite words in $X$ as basis [F5]; write the basis word corresponding to $i_1,\dots,i_k$ as $T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k}$ and the empty word as $1$. Extending concatenation of words $R$-bilinearly makes $F$ a unital associative $R$-algebra: concatenation is associative with two-sided identity the empty word by [F4], and distributivity holds by the bilinear extension. Let $I\subseteq F$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the relators $r_i:=T_i^2-(q-1)T_i-q\cdot1$, $b_i:=T_iT_{i+1}T_i-T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}$ and $d_{ij}:=T_iT_j-T_jT_i$, and put $H:=F/I$, which by [F1] is the presented algebra $H_{R,q}(S_n)$, the image of $T_i$ being again written $T_i$; every element of $H$ is the image of an $R$-linear combination of words in the $T_i$, so $H$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by $T_1,\dots,T_{n-1}$. [given, F1, F4, F5, F7]

1.2 Define $\rho:F\to F$ on the word basis by $\rho(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k}):=T_{i_k}\cdots T_{i_1}$ and $\rho(1):=1$, extended $R$-linearly. Then $\rho$ is an $R$-linear involution and reverses products, $\rho(xy)=\rho(y)\rho(x)$, because reversal of a concatenation is the concatenation of the reversals in the opposite order and both sides are $R$-bilinear; in particular $\rho^2=\mathrm{id}_F$ and $\rho(T_i)=T_i$ for each generator. [F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 $\rho$ preserves $I$, i.e. $\rho(I)\subseteq I$: the relators are fixed up to sign, $\rho(r_i)=r_i$, $\rho(b_i)=b_i$ and $\rho(d_{ij})=-d_{ij}$ by step 1.2, so each lies in $I$; the set $J:=\{x\in F:\rho(x)\in I\}$ contains $0$, is closed under addition and additive inverses because $\rho$ is $R$-linear and $I$ is an additive subgroup, and for $x\in J$ and $a\in F$ one has $\rho(ax)=\rho(x)\rho(a)\in I$ and $\rho(xa)=\rho(a)\rho(x)\in I$ because $I$ is a two-sided ideal [F6]; hence $J$ is a two-sided ideal containing all relators, so $(S)\subseteq J$ and $I=(S)\subseteq J$, that is, $\rho(I)\subseteq I$. [step 1.2, F6, algebra]

3.1 Consequently $\bar\rho:H\to H$, $\bar\rho(x+I):=\rho(x)+I$, is well defined: if $x-y\in I$ then $\rho(x)-\rho(y)=\rho(x-y)\in I$ by step 2.1. It is an $R$-linear anti-automorphism of $H$ with $\bar\rho^2=\mathrm{id}$ and $\bar\rho(T_i)=T_i$: the quotient operations of [F7] and step 1.2 give $\bar\rho((x+I)(y+I))=\rho(xy)+I=\rho(y)\rho(x)+I=\bar\rho(y+I)\bar\rho(x+I)$, and similarly for addition, scalars and the unit. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, F7, algebra]

4.1 $\bar\rho$ is the unique $R$-algebra anti-automorphism of $H$ with $\bar\rho(T_i)=T_i$ for all $i$: if $\alpha$ is any such anti-automorphism, then $\alpha$ and $\bar\rho$ agree on $1$, and for a word $T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k}$ one has $\alpha(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k})=\alpha(T_{i_k})\cdots\alpha(T_{i_1})=T_{i_k}\cdots T_{i_1}=\bar\rho(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_k})$; the words span $H$ by step 1.1, so $\alpha=\bar\rho$ by $R$-linearity. [step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 For $w\in S_n$ and a reduced expression $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_\ell}$ one has $T_w=T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell}$ in the generic algebra and hence $T_w=T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell}\in H$; thus $T_w^*=\bar\rho(T_{i_1}\cdots T_{i_\ell})=T_{i_\ell}\cdots T_{i_1}$ by step 3.1. The reversed word $s_{i_\ell}\cdots s_{i_1}$ represents $w^{-1}$ and has $\ell$ letters, and $\ell=\ell(w)=\ell(w^{-1})$ by [F3], so it is a reduced expression of $w^{-1}$ and therefore $T_{w^{-1}}=T_{i_\ell}\cdots T_{i_1}\in H$; hence $T_w^*=T_{w^{-1}}$ for every $w$, and $T_w$ is well defined in $H$ independently of the chosen reduced expression by [F1] and [F2]. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The assertions of the statement follow: $*:=\bar\rho$ is the unique extension of the assignment $T_i\mapsto T_i$, an involution by step 3.1; $T_w^*=T_{w^{-1}}$ is step 4.2; therefore $(T_wT_{w^{-1}})^*=T_{w^{-1}}^*T_w^*=T_{(w^{-1})^{-1}}T_{w^{-1}}=T_wT_{w^{-1}}$; and for the parabolic subgroup $S_\lambda$ the map $w\mapsto w^{-1}$ is a bijection of $S_\lambda$, so $m_\lambda^*=\sum_{w\in S_\lambda}T_{w^{-1}}=\sum_{v\in S_\lambda}T_v=m_\lambda$. No choice principle is used: the constructions are explicit on the word basis. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
