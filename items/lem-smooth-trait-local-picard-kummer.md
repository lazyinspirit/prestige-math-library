## Statement

Let $(A,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian henselian local domain containing its algebraically closed residue field $k$. Suppose $\mathfrak m=(s,x)$, the ideal $(s)$ is prime, and $x$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A/(s)$. Let $n\ge1$ be invertible in $k$ and put $R=A[s^{-1}]$. Then $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$ and $R^\times/(R^\times)^n\cong\mathbb Z/n$, generated by $s$. The group of étale $\mu_n$-torsor classes on $\operatorname{Spec}R$ is canonically this quotient. The proof uses finite selections and does not assume the Axiom of Choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The local ring, coefficient field, parameters and integer $n$ satisfying the displayed hypotheses.

## Proof

1.1 Since $A$ is a domain and $x$ is a nonzerodivisor modulo $s$, the two-parameter Koszul complex is a free resolution of $k=A/(s,x)$. For a finite $A$-module $M$, choose a minimal finite free presentation by lifting a basis of $M/\mathfrak m M$, then repeat for its finite syzygies through degree four. Each differential has entries in $\mathfrak m$, because the chosen generators are minimal. Tensoring this partial resolution with $k$ has zero differentials. Computing Tor instead with the length-two Koszul resolution gives $\operatorname{Tor}_3^A(M,k)=0$, so the degree-three free module has zero reduction modulo $\mathfrak m$ and vanishes by finite Nakayama. Thus $M$ has a finite free resolution of length at most two. Only finitely many finite bases and lifts were chosen. [given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $L$ be an invertible $R$-module. A finite presentation of $L$ over $R$ has finitely many matrix entries; clear powers of $s$ to obtain a finite $A$-module $M$ with $M[s^{-1}]\cong L$. Localize the finite free resolution from step 1.1. Since $L$ is projective, the surjection onto it splits; its kernel is projective, so the next surjection also splits. Taking determinant lines in these split exact sequences expresses $L$ as an alternating tensor product of determinants of finite free modules. Those determinants are trivial, hence $L$ is free of rank one and $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$. [step 1.1, choose, algebra]

3.1 If $f/s^m$ is a unit of $R$, an inverse gives $fg=s^N$ in $A$ for some $g\in A$. The element $s$ is prime because $A/(s)$ is a domain. Repeated cancellation of $s$ shows $f=s^q h$ with $h\in A^\times$; the exponent is unique because $s$ is a nonunit. Every $h\in A^\times$ has an $n$th root: its residue has one in algebraically closed $k$, and the derivative of $T^n-h$ at that nonzero root is a unit, so Hensel lifting applies. Therefore the exponent of $s$ gives the asserted unit quotient. [given, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The finite étale group $\mu_n$ is split over $A$. The character idempotents of a $\mu_n$-torsor's coordinate algebra give invertible eigenspaces; their multiplication identifies the $n$th tensor power of the weight-one line with $R$. By step 2.1 this line is free, and a generator has $n$th power a unit $f$. The torsor is $z^n=f$; two such torsors are isomorphic precisely when their units differ by an $n$th power. Thus the torsor group is the unit quotient from step 3.1. [step 2.1, step 3.1, choose, algebra] ∎
