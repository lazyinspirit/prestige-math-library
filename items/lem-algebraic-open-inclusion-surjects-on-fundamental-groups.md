## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). If $U$ is a nonempty
Zariski open subset of a connected smooth complex algebraic variety $S$, then
$U$ is path connected and $\pi_1(U,s)\to\pi_1(S,s)$ is surjective for every
$s\in U$ ([[def-fundamental-groupoid-of-a-space]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a connected smooth complex algebraic variety $S$ of dimension $n$, a nonempty Zariski open $U\subseteq S$ with complement $Z=S\setminus U$, and $s\in U$. A continuous based loop is a continuous map from the unit circle $S^1=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb R^2:x^2+y^2=1\}$ with base point $*=(1,0)$ to $S$ sending $*$ to $s$; its class in $\pi_1(S,s)$ is the path-homotopy class of the corresponding loop of [[def-fundamental-groupoid-of-a-space]], and based homotopies of circle maps induce path-homotopies fixing $s$.

[F1] AC implies DC, and DC implies the Axiom of Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]).

[F2] Parametric transversality under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$: for a smooth family $\mathcal F:M\times P\to N$ of maps and an embedded submanifold $Z\subseteq N$, if $\mathcal F\pitchfork Z$ then the set of parameters $p\in P$ for which the slice $\mathcal F_p$ fails to be transverse to $Z$ is a null subset of $P$ ([[thm-parametric-transversality]]). Transversality means $dF_x(T_xM)+T_{F(x)}Z=T_{F(x)}N$ at every $x\in F^{-1}(Z)$, and the condition is vacuous when $F(M)\cap Z=\varnothing$ ([[def-a-smooth-map-transverse-to-an-embedded-submanifold]]); a submersion is transverse to every embedded submanifold ([[cor-a-submersion-is-transverse-to-every-embedded-submanifold]]); slices are the maps $\mathcal F_s(x)=\mathcal F(x,s)$ ([[def-smooth-family-of-maps-and-evaluation-map]]).

[F3] Perturbation family under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$: for every smooth $f:M\to N$ there are an open ball $B\subseteq\mathbb R^m$ containing $0$ and a smooth family $\mathcal F:M\times B\to N$ with $\mathcal F_0=f$ and with every parameter map $a\mapsto\mathcal F(x,a)$ a submersion ([[lem-a-tubular-target-produces-a-submersive-finite-dimensional-perturbation-family]]); its construction embeds the nonempty target in some Euclidean space and takes the open unit ball of that space as $B$, so for nonempty $N$ the ball $B$ is positive-dimensional.

[F4] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ every open cover of a smooth manifold admits a subordinate smooth partition of unity ([[thm-smooth-partitions-of-unity-exist-on-manifolds]]).

[F5] Relative Whitney approximation under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$: if $F:M\to N$ is continuous, $A\subseteq M$ is closed and $F$ is smooth on an open neighbourhood of $A$, then there are a smooth map $\widetilde F:M\to N$ and an open neighbourhood $W$ of $A$ such that $\widetilde F=F$ on $W$ and $F$ is homotopic to $\widetilde F$ by a homotopy fixed on $W$ ([[thm-relative-whitney-approximation-for-manifold-valued-maps]]).

[F6] Manifold null sets ([[def-null-subset-of-a-smooth-manifold]]): every subset of a null set and every countable union of null sets is null ([[prop-countable-unions-and-subsets-of-manifold-null-sets-are-null]]), and the complement of a null set in a positive-dimensional smooth manifold is dense ([[prop-a-null-set-has-dense-complement-in-a-positive-dimensional-manifold]]).

[F7] Complex algebraic input: the analytic topology of a complex classical variety, smoothness at a point via regularity of its local ring, and the reduced induced structure on locally closed subvarieties are as in [[def-analytic-topology-and-finite-smooth-algebraic-stratifications]]; over a perfect field regularity of all local rings, classical smoothness and scheme-theoretic smoothness of $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ agree ([[cor-smooth-variety-classical-scheme-conventions-agree]]); a scheme-smooth point admits a local presentation whose Jacobian has an invertible minor ([[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]]); the holomorphic implicit function theorem turns the zero locus of such a presentation into a local graph ([[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]]); chain dimension is as in [[def-dimension-classical-variety]]; nonempty opens of an irreducible variety have its dimension ([[lem-dimension-nonempty-open-subset]]), dimension at a closed point is the maximum dimension of its components ([[lem-local-dimension-reduced-variety-components]]), and a zero-dimensional classical variety has finitely many points ([[lem-zero-dimensional-classical-variety-finite]]); classical varieties are Noetherian with finitely many irreducible components ([[lem-classical-variety-noetherian-components]]), these correspond locally to minimal primes ([[thm-irreducible-components-and-minimal-primes]]), and a regular local ring is a domain ([[thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay]]); a dominant morphism of irreducible characteristic-zero classical varieties is smooth on a nonempty open subset of its source ([[cor-generic-smoothness-on-source-characteristic-zero]]), the smooth locus of a morphism locally of finite presentation is open ([[thm-smooth-locus-open]]), classical varieties are finite-type separated schemes ([[thm-classical-varieties-equivalent-integral-separated-finite-type-schemes]]).

[F8] The class of a based loop lies in the automorphism group $\pi_1(S,s)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Pi_1(S)}(s,s)$ of the fundamental groupoid, and an inclusion $U\hookrightarrow S$ induces a groupoid functor and hence a homomorphism $\pi_1(U,s)\to\pi_1(S,s)$; a based loop lying in $U$ represents an element of the image ([[def-fundamental-groupoid-of-a-space]]).

[F9] A connected locally path-connected space is path connected ([[thm-connected-and-locally-path-connected-implies-path-connected]]), and paths are continuous maps from the unit interval ([[def-path-connected]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Charts and path-connected neighbourhoods. The regular local rings of $S$ are domains by [F7]. The minimal-prime correspondence shows that only one irreducible component passes through any point; the finitely many components are therefore disjoint and both closed and open. Connectedness leaves one component. We may consequently view $S$ as an integral finite-type separated scheme by the classical-scheme equivalence in [F7]; smoothness of the algebraic variety means every local ring is regular, so by the equivalence of [F7] the structure morphism $S\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C$ is scheme-smooth. Fix $x\in S$. Applying the Jacobian criterion of [F7] to this morphism gives an affine neighbourhood of $x$ with a presentation $C_h\cong(\mathbb C[t_1,\dots,t_m]/(f_1,\dots,f_r))_g$ in which some $r\times r$ minor of the Jacobian matrix $(\partial f_j/\partial t_i)$ is invertible at $x$, and whose fibres have dimension $m-r$; here the fibre is the local variety itself, so its chart dimension is $m-r$, and the nonempty-open dimension equality of [F7] gives $m-r=n=\dim S$. If $r=0$ the chart is already open in affine space. For $r>0$, after relabelling the coordinates and applying the holomorphic implicit function theorem of [F7] (adding an unused parameter if the free-coordinate block is empty) to the holomorphic map $(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ near the image of $x$, the common zero locus is locally the graph of a holomorphic function of the remaining $m-r=n$ coordinates; the graph coordinate map identifies a neighbourhood of $x$ in the analytic topology with an open ball in $\mathbb C^n$, and this analytic topology is the one of [F7]. The same presentation and implicit-function argument applied to the reduced induced structure of a locally closed smooth complex subvariety exhibits its points as complex charts of dimension equal to its complex dimension. Consequently $S$, and every smooth locally closed complex subvariety of $S$, is a complex manifold for the analytic topology, hence in particular a smooth real manifold of twice its complex dimension; the chart balls are path-connected neighbourhoods, so $S$ and its open subsets are locally path connected. [F7, given, construct]

1.2 Smoothing a based loop. Let $\gamma:S^1\to S$ be a continuous based loop. Choose a continuous surjection $\psi:S^1\to S^1$ with $\psi(*)=*$ that is constant on a neighbourhood of $*$: in angular coordinates it is induced by a nondecreasing surjection $\lambda:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ constant at $0$ and $1$ on endpoint collars. The interpolation $(1-u)t+u\lambda(t)$ descends to a homotopy from the identity fixing $*$; then $\gamma_1=\gamma\circ\psi$ is a continuous based loop, constant on an open arc $W_0\ni*$ where $\gamma_1= s$, and it is based-homotopic to $\gamma$. The map $\gamma_1$ is smooth (indeed constant) on an open neighbourhood of the closed set $A=\{*\}$, so the relative Whitney approximation of [F5] applies: there are a smooth based loop $\gamma_2:S^1\to S$ and an open neighbourhood $W$ of $*$ such that $\gamma_2=\gamma_1$ on $W$ and $\gamma_1$ is homotopic to $\gamma_2$ by a homotopy fixed on $W$. Thus $\gamma_2$ is a smooth based loop, equal to $\gamma_1=s$ on the open neighbourhood $W\cap W_0$ of the base point, with $[\gamma_2]=[\gamma_1]=[\gamma]$ in $\pi_1(S,s)$. [F1, F5, F8, given, construct]

2.1 A finite decomposition of the complement into codimension-two smooth pieces. The complement $Z=S\setminus U$ is closed and proper, and $S$ is irreducible by step 1.1. We construct pairwise disjoint smooth locally closed complex subvarieties $(Z_j)$ covering $Z$, each of complex dimension at most $n-1$, by downward induction on dimension. Every irreducible component $C$ of $Z$ is a proper closed irreducible subvariety of the irreducible $S$, so $\dim C\le n-1$: append $S$ to any chain of irreducible closed subsets of $C$ to obtain a chain one term longer in $S$, using the chain-dimension definition in [F7]. The same argument gives the dimension drop for every proper closed subset of an irreducible component below. Suppose a closed algebraic subset $T\subseteq Z$ is given in which every irreducible component has dimension at most $d$. For each irreducible component $C$ of $T$ of dimension exactly $d$, the regular locus $C_{\mathrm{reg}}$ is a nonempty Zariski open subset of $C$: the generic-smoothness corollary of [F7], applied to the constant morphism from the irreducible classical variety $C$ to a point, produces a nonempty open subset of the regular locus, which is open by [F7]. The sets $C_{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\bigcup_{C'\ne C}C'$, for the finitely many top-dimensional components $C$, are pairwise disjoint smooth locally closed complex subvarieties of dimension $d$. What they do not cover of $T$ is the union of the lower-dimensional components, the singular loci of the top-dimensional components, and the intersections of a top-dimensional component with another component; the lower-dimensional components have dimension at most $d-1$ by assumption and the other sets are proper closed subsets of top-dimensional irreducible components, hence have dimension at most $d-1$ by the chain argument, so the leftover $T'$ is a closed algebraic subset all of whose components have dimension at most $d-1$. Iterating from $T=Z$ and $d=n-1$ produces finitely many disjoint smooth locally closed pieces, and the iteration stops at dimension $0$, where the pieces are points and nothing is left over. Each piece has complex dimension at most $n-1$, so its real dimension is at most $2n-2$ and its real codimension in the smooth real manifold $S$ of step 1.1 is at least two; being a smooth locally closed complex subvariety, each piece is an embedded submanifold of $S$. [F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 A cutoff vanishing at the base point and equal to one over the bad set. Let $\gamma_2$ be the smooth loop of step 1.2, denoting by $\gamma_2^{-1}(Z)\subseteq S^1$ the compact bad set. If it is empty, then $\gamma_2$ already lies in $U$ and its class is represented in $U$; assume it is nonempty. Since $\gamma_2$ equals the constant $s\in U$ on the open neighbourhood $W_2=W\cap W_0$ of $*$, the compact set $K=\gamma_2^{-1}(Z)$ is disjoint from $W\cap W_0$. Choose an open arc $W'\ni*$ with $\overline{W'}\subseteq W_2$, put $U_1=S^1\setminus\overline{W'}$ and $U_2=W_2$; these open sets cover $S^1$, and $K\subseteq S^1\setminus W_2\subseteq U_1$. By the partition-of-unity theorem [F4] applied to the cover $\{U_1,U_2\}$ choose a subordinate smooth partition of unity $(\chi_1,\chi_2)$ and set $\chi=\chi_1$. Then $\chi$ is smooth with values in $[0,1]$, equal to $1$ on the open set $S^1\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\chi_2)\supseteq S^1\setminus W_2\supseteq K$, and equal to $0$ on the open set $S^1\setminus\operatorname{supp}(\chi_1)\supseteq S^1\setminus U_1=\overline{W'}\ni*$; moreover whenever $\chi(p)=0$ we have $\chi_2(p)=1$, so $p\in\operatorname{supp}(\chi_2)\subseteq W_2$ and hence $\gamma_2(p)=s\in U$. In particular $\chi(*)=0$. [F1, F4, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 The truncated perturbation family is transverse to every piece. Apply the perturbation lemma [F3] to the smooth loop $\gamma_2:S^1\to S$: there are an open ball $B\subseteq\mathbb R^m$, positive-dimensional as recorded in [F3] because $S\ne\varnothing$, and a smooth family $\mathcal F:S^1\times B\to S$ with $\mathcal F_0=\gamma_2$ and with every parameter map $a\mapsto\mathcal F(p,a)$ a submersion; in particular the evaluation map $\mathcal F$ is a submersion, hence transverse to every embedded submanifold by [F2]. Define $\widetilde{\mathcal F}:S^1\times B\to S$ by $\widetilde{\mathcal F}(p,a)=\mathcal F(p,\chi(p)a)$; since the ball $B$ is centred at $0$ and $0\le\chi(p)\le1$ this is well defined and smooth, and $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_0=\mathcal F_0=\gamma_2$. For every $a\in B$ one has $\widetilde{\mathcal F}(*,a)=\mathcal F(*,\chi(*)a)=\mathcal F(*,0)=\gamma_2(*)=s$, so every slice $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a$ is a based loop. At a point $(p,a)$ with $\chi(p)>0$ the derivative of $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ in the parameter direction is $\chi(p)$ times the parameter derivative of $\mathcal F$ at $(p,\chi(p)a)$, which is surjective, so $\widetilde{\mathcal F}$ is a submersion there. Hence $\widetilde{\mathcal F}\pitchfork Z_j$ for every piece $Z_j$ of step 2.1: if $\widetilde{\mathcal F}(p,a)\in Z_j\subseteq Z$ then $\chi(p)>0$, since $\chi(p)=0$ would give $\widetilde{\mathcal F}(p,a)=\mathcal F(p,0)=\gamma_2(p)$ with $\gamma_2(p)\in U$ by step 2.2, and at a point with $\chi(p)>0$ the map is a submersion and therefore transverse by [F2]. [F1, F3, F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 A parameter outside all bad sets, and surjectivity. For each piece $Z_j$ of step 2.1 the set $N_j\subseteq B$ of parameters $a$ with $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a$ not transverse to $Z_j$ is null by the parametric transversality theorem [F2]. Since there are finitely many pieces, the union $N=\bigcup_jN_j$ is null in the positive-dimensional smooth manifold $B$ by [F6], so its complement is dense by [F6] and in particular nonempty; choose $a\in B\setminus N$. Then the slice $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a:S^1\to S$ is a smooth based loop transverse to every $Z_j$. A transverse map from a one-dimensional manifold cannot meet a piece of real codimension at least two: if $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a(p)\in Z_j$, transversality would give $\dim T_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a(p)}S\le\dim\operatorname{im}(d(\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a)_p)+\dim T_{\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a(p)}Z_j\le1+(2n-2)<2n$, a contradiction. Hence $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a(S^1)$ meets no $Z_j$ and therefore lies in $U$. The map $(p,u)\mapsto\widetilde{\mathcal F}(p,ua)$, $0\le u\le1$, is a homotopy from $\gamma_2$ to $\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a$ through based loops, so $[\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a]=[\gamma_2]=[\gamma]$ in $\pi_1(S,s)$ by [F8], while $[\widetilde{\mathcal F}_a]$ lies in the image of $\pi_1(U,s)\to\pi_1(S,s)$ by [F8]. In the discarded case $K=\varnothing$ of step 2.2 the loop $\gamma_2$ itself already lies in $U$, so the same conclusion holds. Since every class in $\pi_1(S,s)$ has a continuous based loop representative, the map $\pi_1(U,s)\to\pi_1(S,s)$ is surjective. [F2, F6, F8, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Path connectedness. Let $x,y\in U$. The chart balls of step 1.1 make $S$ locally path connected, so connectedness and [F9] give a continuous path $\beta$ from $x$ to $y$; reparametrize it to be constant near its endpoints. Traverse $\beta$ on the first quarter of $S^1$, its reverse on the second quarter, and the constant path at $x$ on the second half. This gives a continuous circle map constant near all junctions. Apply [F5] to this circle map, relative to the finite closed set of junctions, to obtain a smooth loop equal to the original on neighbourhoods of those junctions. Its bad set is disjoint from those neighbourhoods. Repeat step 2.2 with $W_2$ the union of these constant neighbourhoods and $W'$ a smaller union with closure in $W_2$. The same two-set partition supplies a cutoff vanishing near all junctions, equal to one near the bad set, and with every zero lying where the loop is in $U$. Steps 3.1 and 4.1 then give a perturbed loop in $U$ agreeing with the original near the junctions. Its first quarter joins $x$ to $y$ in $U$. Thus $U$ is path connected; when $Z=\varnothing$ the unperturbed path already suffices. [F9, F5, F4, F3, F2, F6, step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct] ∎

## Source notes

The stratification of the complement, the smoothing of loops, and the cutoff-truncated perturbation family are supplied locally from the published approximation, partition-of-unity, tubular-perturbation and parametric-transversality suppliers; the argument replaces the informal "perturbation supported away from the collar" of the authoring draft by an explicit post-composition with a cutoff, so that no unstated localization of the published perturbation family is assumed. The topological codimension-two step is the one Deligne II 4.1.1 uses on printed p. 41. The Axiom of Choice is used exactly to supply $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$ for the three quoted transversality and approximation suppliers.
