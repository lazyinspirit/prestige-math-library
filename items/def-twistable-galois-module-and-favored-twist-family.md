## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Dependent Choice for the place and derived-cohomology interfaces below. Fix a rational prime $\ell$, an integer $k_0\ge1$, and the quotient ring
$$R_\ell=\mathbb Z_\ell[x]/\left(1+x^{\ell^{k_0-1}}+x^{2\ell^{k_0-1}}+\cdots+x^{(\ell-1)\ell^{k_0-1}}\right).$$
Write $\xi$ for the image of $x$, $\omega=\xi-1$, and $\Gamma=\langle\xi\rangle$ for the cyclic group generated by $\xi$, of order $\ell^{k_0}$; then $R_\ell/(\omega)\cong\mathbb F_\ell$. Let $K$ be a number field, $G_K$ its absolute Galois group, and $V_0$ a finite set of places of $K$. For a place $v$, $G_v$ and $I_v$ denote a decomposition group and its inertia subgroup under a choice of place above $v$; for any subgroup $G$ acting on $M$, $H^0(G,M)=M^G$ denotes the subgroup fixed by $G$.

A **twistable module** over $K$ is a discrete topological $R_\ell$-module $N$ whose underlying topological group is isomorphic to $(\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell)^r$ for some finite $r>0$, equipped with a continuous $G_K$-action commuting with $\xi$, such that the inertia group $I_v$ acts trivially on $N$ for every place $v\notin V_0$. For a continuous character $\chi:G_K\to\Gamma$, the **twist** $N_\chi$ is an $R_\ell$-module isomorphic to $N$ under a map $\beta_\chi:N_\chi\to N$ for which
$$\beta_\chi(\sigma n)=\chi(\sigma)\,\sigma\beta_\chi(n)\qquad(\sigma\in G_K,\ n\in N_\chi).$$
For any module $M$, write $M[\omega^j]=\ker(\omega^j:M\to M)$; in particular $N[\omega]$ is finite.

A tuple $(\chi_v)_{v\in V_0}$ of local characters is globally represented if it is the restriction of some continuous global character $\chi:G_K\to\Gamma$. For each $v\in V_0$ and each local character $\chi_v:G_v\to\Gamma$, choose an $\ell$-divisible subgroup $W_v(\chi_v)\subseteq H^1(G_v,N_{\chi_v})$ stable under $\xi$. Here $\ell$-divisible means multiplication by $\ell$ is surjective. At $v\notin V_0$, set $W_v(\chi_v)=H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}(G_v,N_{\chi_v})$ if $\chi_v$ is unramified and set it to $0$ otherwise; $H^1_{\mathrm{ur}}$ is the kernel of restriction to inertia. For $v\in V_0$, let $W_{v,1}(\chi_v)$ be the inverse image of $W_v(\chi_v)$ under $H^1(G_v,N_{\chi_v}[\omega])\to H^1(G_v,N_{\chi_v})$. If these local conditions are fixed, define
$$\operatorname{Sel}(N_\chi)=\ker\left(H^1(G_K,N_\chi)\longrightarrow\prod_v H^1(G_v,N_\chi)/W_v(\chi|_{G_v})\right).$$
For $j\ge1$, let $\operatorname{Sel}_{\omega^j}(N_\chi)$ be the preimage of this group in $H^1(G_K,N_\chi[\omega^j])$. The connecting map $\delta_\chi:H^0(G_K,N_\chi[\omega])\to H^1(G_K,N_\chi[\omega])$ comes from $0\to N_\chi[\omega]\to N_\chi[\omega^2]\xrightarrow{\omega}N_\chi[\omega]\to0$. Define
$$r_{\omega^j}(N_\chi)=\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}\left(\omega^{j-1}\operatorname{Sel}_{\omega^j}(N_\chi)/\operatorname{im}(\delta_\chi)\right).$$

A continuous $\chi:G_K\to\Gamma$ is **admissible** if, outside $V_0$, it can ramify only at places where its reduction to $\operatorname{Hom}(G_K,\Gamma/\ell\Gamma)$ is ramified. Set
$$\mathfrak h_K(\chi)=\prod_{v\notin V_0,\ \chi\text{ ramified at }v}\mathfrak p_v,\qquad h_K(\chi)=N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathfrak h_K(\chi)),\qquad X_K(H)=\{\chi\text{ admissible}:h_K(\chi)\le H\},$$
where $\mathfrak p_v$ is the prime ideal corresponding to the finite place $v$. For a $G_K$-submodule $T\subseteq N[\omega]$, put
$$T_{N,T}(\chi)=\prod_{v\mid\mathfrak h_K(\chi)}\frac{|(N/T)[\omega]^{G_v}|}{|N[\omega]^{G_v}|}.$$
A twist is **favored** if $T_{N,T}(\chi)\le1$ for every such $T$; write $X^{\mathrm{fav}}_{K,N}(H)$ for favored twists in $X_K(H)$. The module $N$ is **potentially favored** if $\lim_{H\to\infty}|X^{\mathrm{fav}}_{K,N}(H)|/|X_K(H)|>0$. Let $K_1=K(\mu_{\ell^{k_0}})$. A submodule $T\subseteq N[\omega]$ is **cofavored** if for every $\sigma\in G_{K_1}$,
$$\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}H^0(\langle\sigma\rangle,(N/T)[\omega])=\dim_{\mathbb F_\ell}H^0(\langle\sigma\rangle,N[\omega]).$$
The module is **uncofavored** if its only cofavored submodules are $0$ and $N[\omega]$.

Here $\mu_{\ell^\infty}$ is the group of all roots of unity of $\ell$-power order in a fixed algebraic closure. The dual twistable module $N^\vee$ is obtained from $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}(N,\mu_{\ell^\infty})$ by the induced Galois action and the action $(\xi\varphi)(n)=\varphi(\xi^{-1}n)$, then tensoring with $\mathbb Q_\ell[\xi]$ and quotienting by the original dual module. For $\sigma\in G_{K_1}$, the connecting map $\delta_\sigma:H^0(\langle\sigma\rangle,N[\omega])\to H^1(\langle\sigma\rangle,N[\omega])$ is associated to $0\to N[\omega]\to N[\omega^2]\xrightarrow{\omega}N[\omega]\to0$; define $\delta^\vee_\sigma$ in the same way for $N^\vee$. The module has **self-dual structure** if an equivariant isomorphism $N[\omega]\to N^\vee[\omega]$ intertwines all $\delta_\sigma$ and $\delta^\vee_\sigma$. An automorphism of $N[\omega]$ commutes with connecting maps if it commutes with every $\delta_\sigma$. A global class in $H^1(G_K,N[\omega])$ is a **universal $\omega$-Selmer element** for prescribed local twists if its image in the twisted cohomology is in the corresponding $\omega$-Selmer group for every admissible twist satisfying those local twists.

Let $V(N_\chi)=\varprojlim(N_\chi\xleftarrow{\ell}N_\chi\xleftarrow{\ell}\cdots)$; at each place, the local condition on $V(N_\chi)$ is the unique $\mathbb Q_\ell$-subspace mapping to $W_v(\chi)$, and the dual local conditions are its orthogonal complements under the local Tate pairing on $H^1(G_v,V(N_\chi))\times H^1(G_v,V((N^\vee)_\chi))$, and their images define the local conditions on $(N^\vee)_\chi$. A module has **alternating structure** if an equivariant isomorphism $N\to N^\vee$ identifies the chosen local conditions with their dual local conditions and induces an alternating pairing (a pairing with $\langle x,x\rangle=0$ for every $x$) on every $N[\omega^j]$. Given globally represented local twists at $V_0$, the **non-self-dual case** consists of a potentially favored, uncofavored module without self-dual structure, for which the only automorphisms of $N[\omega]$ commuting with connecting maps are scalar multiplications by $\mathbb F_\ell^\times$, and neither $N$ nor $N^\vee$ has a nonzero universal $\omega$-Selmer element. The **alternating case** consists of $\ell=2$, $k_0=1$, $\xi=-1$, a potentially favored and uncofavored module with alternating structure, with only the identity automorphism of $N[\omega]$ commuting with connecting maps, and with no nonzero universal $\omega$-Selmer element.
