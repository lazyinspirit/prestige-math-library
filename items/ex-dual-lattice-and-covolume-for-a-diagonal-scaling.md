## Example

Let $a_1,\dots,a_n\in\mathbb R\setminus\{0\}$ and let
$A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\dots,a_n)$. For $\Lambda=A\mathbb Z^n$ the covolume
is $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^n|a_i|$ and the dual lattice is
$$\Lambda^*=\operatorname{diag}(a_1^{-1},\dots,a_n^{-1})\mathbb Z^n=\Bigl\{\Bigl(\frac{m_1}{a_1},\dots,\frac{m_n}{a_n}\Bigr):m\in\mathbb Z^n\Bigr\},$$
since $A^{-T}=A^{-1}$ for a diagonal matrix. In one dimension,
$\operatorname{covol}(a\mathbb Z)=|a|$ and $(a\mathbb Z)^*=a^{-1}\mathbb Z$;
for the sampling spacing $a=h>0$ this is the pair $h\mathbb Z$ and
$h^{-1}\mathbb Z$ of the sampling results on this pair.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Reals $a_1,\dots,a_n\ne0$, the diagonal matrix $A=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\dots,a_n)$ with entries $A_{ij}=a_i$ when $i=j$ and $A_{ij}=0$ otherwise, and the lattice $\Lambda=A\mathbb Z^n$ with the covolume and dual lattice of [[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]].

[F1] For a diagonal matrix the only nonzero term of the Leibniz sum $\det A=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i=1}^nA_{\sigma(i),i}$ ([[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]) is the identity permutation, and $A^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(a_i^{-1})$ because $(AA^{-1})_{ij}=\sum_kA_{ik}a_k^{-1}\delta_{kj}=\delta_{ij}$ ([[def-matrix-product-and-identity-matrix]], [[def-invertible-matrix-and-general-linear-group]]).

[F2] $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)=|\det A|$ and the dual lattice is $\Lambda^*=A^{-T}\mathbb Z^n$ with $A^{-T}=(A^{-1})^{T}$ ([[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]]); the transpose of a diagonal matrix is itself.

## Verification

1.1 Each off-diagonal entry of $A$ vanishes, so for every permutation $\sigma\ne\operatorname{id}$ some factor $A_{\sigma(i),i}$ with $\sigma(i)\ne i$ is zero and only $\sigma=\operatorname{id}$ contributes: $\det A=\prod_{i=1}^na_i\ne0$ by [F1]. Hence $A$ is invertible with inverse $\operatorname{diag}(a_1^{-1},\dots,a_n^{-1})$, as the product computation $(AA^{-1})_{ij}=\delta_{ij}$ shows [F1]. Therefore $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)=|\det A|=\prod_{i=1}^n|a_i|$, and since a diagonal matrix equals its transpose, $A^{-T}=A^{-1}=\operatorname{diag}(a_i^{-1})$, so $\Lambda^*=\operatorname{diag}(a_1^{-1},\dots,a_n^{-1})\mathbb Z^n$, which is the displayed set of tuples $(m_1/a_1,\dots,m_n/a_n)$ with $m\in\mathbb Z^n$ by [F2] and the definition of $A\mathbb Z^n$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For $n=1$ the matrix is $(a_1)$ with $a_1=a\ne0$, giving $\operatorname{covol}(a\mathbb Z)=|a|$ and $(a\mathbb Z)^*=a^{-1}\mathbb Z$; at the sampling spacing $a=h>0$ this is exactly the pair $h\mathbb Z$, $h^{-1}\mathbb Z$ used by the sampling results, whose dual lattice is again full-rank and satisfies $\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda^*)=\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)^{-1}=h^{-1}$ [F1, F2, given, algebra] ∎
