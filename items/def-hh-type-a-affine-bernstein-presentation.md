## Definition

Let $n\ge2$, $R_0=\mathbb Z[Q,Q^{-1}]$, and $A=R_0[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_n^{\pm1}]$ as in [[def-hh-type-a-weight-lattice-laurent-ring-and-divided-differences]]. Let $F$ be the free associative unital $R_0$-algebra on symbols $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1},X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_n^{\pm1}$ and let $I$ be the two-sided ideal generated by the following relations:

1. The $X_j$ commute and $X_jX_j^{-1}=X_j^{-1}X_j=1$.
2. $(T_i-Q)(T_i+1)=0$ for $1\le i<n$.
3. The $T_i$ satisfy the type-$A_{n-1}$ braid relations: $T_iT_{i+1}T_i=T_{i+1}T_iT_{i+1}$ for $1\le i<n-1$, and $T_iT_j=T_jT_i$ when $|i-j|>1$.
4. The Bernstein cross-relations on the Laurent generators are
$$
T_iX_j=s_i(X_j)T_i+(Q-1)D_i(X_j)\qquad(1\le i<n,\ 1\le j\le n).
$$

Define
$$
H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}:=F/I.
$$
The coefficient map $A\to H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}$ sends each Laurent monomial to the corresponding word in the $X_j$; its injectivity and the resulting $A$-module basis are the conclusion of [[thm-hh-affine-type-a-bernstein-pbw]], not an assumption in this definition. The normalization is multiplicative: $Q$ is a unit, $T_i^2=(Q-1)T_i+Q$, and $T_i$ is invertible. It matches the finite Hecke normalization of [[lem-hh-hecke-anti-involution-bar-and-normalization]] under $Q=v^2$ and $T_i=vU_i$, where $(U_i-v)(U_i+v^{-1})=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge2$, the Laurent algebra $A$, its type-$A$ action and divided differences, and the free associative algebra and quotient convention.

[F1] [[def-hh-type-a-weight-lattice-laurent-ring-and-divided-differences]]: $A$ is generated as a commutative $R_0$-algebra by $X_j^{\pm1}$, $D_i(f)\in A$, and $D_i(fg)=D_i(f)g+s_i(f)D_i(g)$.

[F2] [[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]: the free associative algebra on a generator set exists over any commutative coefficient ring, and quotienting by the two-sided ideal of the listed relations defines the presented unital associative algebra with its universal property.

[F3] [[lem-hh-hecke-anti-involution-bar-and-normalization]]: in the finite Coxeter Hecke algebra, if the odd-connected type-A generators share parameter $v$, then $U_i$ satisfies $(U_i-v)(U_i+v^{-1})=0$, and $T_i=vU_i$, $Q=v^2$ gives $(T_i-Q)(T_i+1)=0$.

## Proof

**Given:** The data of the Definition.

1.1 The coefficient ring $R_0=\mathbb Z[Q,Q^{-1}]$ is commutative and $Q$ is a unit. By [F2], the free associative algebra $F$ and the two-sided ideal generated by the displayed relation set exist, so $H_n^{\mathrm{aff}}=F/I$ is a unital associative $R_0$-algebra with the stated generator-and-relation interpretation. [given, F2]

1.2 The divided differences on the Laurent generators are $D_i(X_i)=-X_{i+1}$, $D_i(X_{i+1})=X_{i+1}$, and $D_i(X_j)=0$ when $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$, directly from their definition and the action of $s_i$. Substitution into relation 4 gives $T_iX_i-X_{i+1}T_i=-(Q-1)X_{i+1}$, $T_iX_{i+1}-X_iT_i=(Q-1)X_{i+1}$, and $T_iX_j=X_jT_i$ for the other variables. [F1, algebra]

2.1 If the Bernstein relation holds for $f,g\in A$, then using it twice and [F1] gives $T_i(fg)-s_i(fg)T_i=(Q-1)(D_i(f)g+s_i(f)D_i(g))=(Q-1)D_i(fg)$. For invertible $f\in A$, the Leibniz rule applied to $ff^{-1}=1$ gives $D_i(f^{-1})=-s_i(f)^{-1}D_i(f)f^{-1}$. From $T_if=s_i(f)T_i+(Q-1)D_i(f)$, multiply on the right by $f^{-1}$ and on the left by $s_i(f)^{-1}$ to obtain $T_if^{-1}-s_i(f)^{-1}T_i=-(Q-1)s_i(f)^{-1}D_i(f)f^{-1}=(Q-1)D_i(f^{-1})$. Since every Laurent monomial is a product of the $X_j$ and their inverses, additivity and induction on products prove the Bernstein relation for every $f\in A$. [F1, step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 The quadratic relation is equivalent to $T_i^2=(Q-1)T_i+Q$, so step 1.2 gives $T_iX_iT_i=X_{i+1}T_i^2-(Q-1)X_{i+1}T_i=QX_{i+1}$. Conversely, in an algebra satisfying $T_i^2=(Q-1)T_i+Q$, with $Q\in R_0^\times$, and $T_iX_iT_i=QX_{i+1}$, multiplying the last equality on the right by $T_i$ and using the quadratic relation gives $T_iX_i-X_{i+1}T_i=-(Q-1)X_{i+1}$; multiplying on the left by $T_i$ gives $T_iX_{i+1}-X_iT_i=(Q-1)X_{i+1}$. Together with the stated commutation for $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$, these are exactly the generator cross-relations of part 4. Thus the Bernstein relations and the compact relation are equivalent under the declared conventions. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The quadratic relation gives $T_i(T_i-(Q-1))=Q=(T_i-(Q-1))T_i$. Since $Q$ is a unit, $T_i^{-1}=Q^{-1}(T_i-(Q-1))$, proving the invertibility claim. The finite Hecke normalization agrees with [F3]: substituting $T_i=vU_i$ and $Q=v^2$ into $(U_i-v)(U_i+v^{-1})=0$ gives $(T_i-Q)(T_i+1)=v^2(U_i-v)(U_i+v^{-1})=0$. Every finite braid word has the same number of generators on each side, so scaling every generator by $v$ multiplies both sides by the same power of $v$ and preserves the braid relations. [F3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The quotient presentation itself does not assume a PBW basis or faithful inclusion of $A$. The theorem listed in justified_by proves that inclusion and the $A$-module basis.
