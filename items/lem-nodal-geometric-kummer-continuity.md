## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$, let $n\ge1$ be prime to $p$, and let $S=\operatorname{Spec}k\llbracket t\rrbracket$ with generic field $K=k((t))$. Let $H$ be the strict henselization at the node of $k\llbracket t\rrbracket[u,v]/(uv-t)$, and let a separable closure $K^s/K$ be specified. For every finite subextension $K'/K$, let $S'$ be its valuation ring with uniformizer $s$, and let $H'$ be the henselization at the closed node of the base-changed model. Assume explicitly that, for every such $K'$, the ring $R'=H'[s^{-1}]$ has $\operatorname{Pic}(R')=0$ and its unit quotient modulo $n$th powers is freely generated by the classes of $u$ and $s$ as $(\mathbb Z/n)^2$. Then
$$H^1_{\acute et}\!\left(\operatorname{Spec}H\times_S\operatorname{Spec}K^s,\mu_n\right)\cong\mathbb Z/n,$$
with generator the torsor $z^n=u$. The natural inertia action on this group is trivial. Under the stalk definition of nearby cycles, this is the first $\mu_n$ nearby-cycle group at the node. The finite-level Picard and unit hypothesis is part of this Statement and is not proved by this lemma.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, node, specified separable closure, and explicit finite-level Picard and unit hypothesis in the Statement.

## Proof

1.1 Each finite $K'/K$ is complete discretely valued with residue field $k$: its finite residue extension of algebraically closed $k$ is trivial. Write $t=s^e a(s)$ with $a(s)$ a unit. The finite $H$-algebra $H\otimes_{k\llbracket t\rrbracket}S'$ has a unique closed point, because its closed fibre is the local Artinian algebra $k[s]/(s^e)$. It is henselian local: idempotents in any finite algebra lift from the closed fibre by the henselian property of $H$, and the radicals of the two closed-fibre ideals agree. [given, algebra]

2.1 The universal property of henselization gives a local map $H'\to H\otimes S'$. Conversely the henselian local ring $H'$ receives compatible maps from $H$ and $S'$, hence from their tensor product. The two maps are inverse: their composites fix the original local model, and the uniqueness in the henselization property fixes $H'$ and $H$; they also fix $S'$. Therefore $H'\cong H\otimes S'$ and $R'\cong H\otimes_{k\llbracket t\rrbracket}K'$. The geometric generic coordinate ring is the filtered colimit of these $R'$. [step 1.1, algebra] [step 1.1]

3.1 Étale $\mu_n$-torsor classes commute with this filtered affine colimit. A torsor has a finite locally free coordinate algebra with multiplication, unit and $\mu_n$-coaction; these are finitely many matrix entries and equations after presenting the finite projective module by an idempotent matrix. The torsor condition is an isomorphism between finite modules, so its inverse is additional finite matrix data. All entries, equations and inverses descend to one finite stage, and any isomorphism between two descended torsors descends after passing to a further stage. Conversely base change preserves torsors. Thus the colimit of finite-level torsor groups is the torsor group over the geometric generic ring. [step 2.1, construct, algebra] [step 2.1]

4.1 At each finite stage, the finite-level hypothesis and the split character idempotents give the Kummer classification: every $\mu_n$-torsor is $z^n=f$ for a unit $f$, because its weight-one line is trivial, and two units give isomorphic torsors exactly when their quotient is an $n$th power. The group is $(\mathbb Z/n)^2$ on $[u],[s]$. For a further finite extension, $u$ is unchanged and $s$ becomes a power of a new uniformizer times a unit. Every local unit has an $n$th root by Hensel's simple-root lifting and algebraic closedness of $k$, so the unit contributes no class. [given, step 2.1, algebra] [step 3.1]

5.1 Every uniformizer class dies in the filtered colimit: adjoining an $n$th root of it is a finite separable extension since $n$ is prime to $p$. The class $[u]$ stays a generator at every stage by the stated finite-level hypothesis. Hence the colimit in step 3.1 is $\mathbb Z/n$ generated by $z^n=u$. The torsor is defined before extending $K$, so every element of $\operatorname{Gal}(K^s/K)$ fixes its class; the inertia action is trivial. [step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] [step 4.1]

6.1 Étale $H^1$ of the abelian sheaf $\mu_n$ classifies its étale torsors: local trivializations give a Čech cocycle, and conversely gluing copies of $\mu_n$ along a cocycle gives the torsor; changing trivializations changes the cocycle by a coboundary. The stalk of the first nearby-cycle sheaf at the node is, by its stalk construction, this $H^1$ on the strict-henselian geometric generic fibre. Step 5.1 proves the displayed identification and inertia assertion. No higher nearby-cycle group or global monodromy sequence is asserted. [step 2.1, step 5.1, algebra] [step 5.1] ∎
