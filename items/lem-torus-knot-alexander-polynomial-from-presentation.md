## Statement

Assume the full Axiom of Choice. Let $p,q$ be coprime nonzero integers and let $K=T(p,q)$. Choose generators $x,y$ for the two-generator presentation $\pi_1(E_K)=\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle$. Let $P_{p,q}$ be the two-dimensional presentation complex with one vertex, oriented edges $x,y$, and one two-cell attached along $r=x^py^{-q}$. The assignment $\phi(x)=q$, $\phi(y)=p$ defines an epimorphism $\phi:\pi_1(P_{p,q})\to\mathbb Z$; let $\widetilde P_\phi$ be its connected cyclic cover classified by $\ker\phi$. Let $A_K=H_1(\widetilde{S^3\setminus K};\mathbb Z)$ be the one-variable Alexander module for the infinite cyclic cover classified by total linking, with $t$ acting by the deck transformation of the positive meridian. Put $\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ and define
$$\delta_{p,q}(t)=\frac{(t^{pq}-1)(t-1)}{(t^p-1)(t^q-1)}.$$
Then $\delta_{p,q}(t)$ is a Laurent polynomial, $A_K\cong\Lambda/(\delta_{p,q})$, and the Alexander polynomial $\Delta_K$, defined as a generator of the zeroth elementary ideal $E_0(A_K)$, satisfies $\Delta_K(t)\doteq\delta_{p,q}(t)$, where $f\doteq g$ means $f=\pm t^k g$ for some integer $k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, coprime nonzero integers $p,q$, the torus knot $K=T(p,q)$, its exterior, and the total-linking cover.

[A1] Full AC is the choice-function axiom used by the one-variable Alexander-module and polynomial definitions ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] Full AC implies Countable Choice, the hypothesis of the tubular-exterior retraction supplier ([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] [[thm-torus-knot-group-presentation]] gives $\pi_1(E_K)=\langle x,y\mid x^p=y^q\rangle$ and a positive meridian $\mu=y^\alpha x^\beta$ with $p\alpha+q\beta=1$.

[F2] [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] says $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by $[\mu]$ and the positive meridian maps to $1$ in total linking.

[F3] [[lem-knot-tubular-exterior-and-boundary]] says the open complement $S^3\setminus K$ strongly deformation retracts onto the compact exterior $E_K$.

[F4] [[def-one-variable-alexander-module-of-an-oriented-link]] defines $A_K$ as the first homology of the connected infinite cyclic cover with positive meridian deck action.

[F5] [[thm-cellular-homology-computes-singular-homology]] identifies cellular-chain homology with singular homology, and [[prop-the-first-hurewicz-map-in-degree-one-is-abelianization]] identifies first homology with the abelianization of the fundamental group.

[F6] [[def-elementary-ideals-of-a-finitely-presented-module]] defines $E_0$ by the maximal minors of a presentation matrix, [[def-alexander-polynomial-from-the-first-elementary-ideal]] defines $\Delta_K$ as its gcd generator up to units $\pm t^k$, and [[lem-the-laurent-polynomial-ring-is-noetherian-and-a-unique-factorisation-domain]] supplies unique factorization in $\Lambda$.

## Proof

**Given:** Full AC and the data in the Statement.

1.1 Abelianizing the presentation in [F1] gives the relation $p[x]=q[y]$. The map $\phi(x)=q$, $\phi(y)=p$ is onto because $\gcd(p,q)=1$; its kernel in $\mathbb Z^2$ is generated by $(p,-q)$, exactly the abelianized relation vector, so the abelianization is $\mathbb Z$. The meridian in [F1] maps to $p\alpha+q\beta=1$, so the epimorphism $\phi$ in the Statement is precisely the positive total-linking homomorphism that classifies the cover in [F4]. [A1, F1, F2, F4, algebra]

1.2 For every integer $n$, $(z^n-1)/(z-1)$ is a finite Laurent sum; for $n<0$ it equals $-(z^{-1}+\cdots+z^n)$. In the lifted attaching word, a positive edge contributes the current deck translate and a negative edge contributes the negative of the preceding translate. The $x^p$ block therefore contributes $(x^p-1)/(x-1)$ to the $x$-edge coordinate. The following $y^{-q}$ block starts after the $x^p$ translate and contributes $x^p(y^{-q}-1)/(y-1)$ to the $y$-edge coordinate. These are the complete signed edge contributions of the two-cell in $\widetilde P_\phi$. [given, algebra]

2.1 The cyclic cover in the Statement has cellular chain groups $C_2=\Lambda$, $C_1=\Lambda^2$, $C_0=\Lambda$. Its first boundary is $d_1=(t^q-1,\ t^p-1)$ because lifted $x$- and $y$-edges end $q$ and $p$ deck steps from their starts. Evaluating the two edge contributions in step 1.2 at $x=t^q$, $y=t^p$ gives the second boundary column; the $y$-coordinate is $t^{pq}(t^{-pq}-1)/(t^p-1)=-(t^{pq}-1)/(t^p-1)$:
$$d_2(1)=\left(\frac{t^{pq}-1}{t^q-1},\ -\frac{t^{pq}-1}{t^p-1}\right)^T.$$
This is the cellular boundary of the lifted two-cell. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 In $\Lambda$, $\gcd(t^p-1,t^q-1)\doteq t-1$: for positive exponents the Euclidean algorithm gives this because $\gcd(|p|,|q|)=1$, and negative exponents differ from the positive ones by units. Hence $a=(t^q-1)/(t-1)$ and $b=(t^p-1)/(t-1)$ are coprime. The kernel of $d_1$ is the free rank-one submodule generated by $(b,-a)^T$: if $au+bv=0$, unique factorization implies $a\mid v$, and then $(u,v)$ is a multiple of $(b,-a)$. The column in step 2.1 is $\delta_{p,q}(b,-a)^T$. To show $\delta_{p,q}\in\Lambda$, replace signed exponents by $m=|p|$, $n=|q|$, which changes each factor only by a unit. Set $A=(t^m-1)/(t-1)$ and $B=(t^n-1)/(t-1)$. They are coprime; since $m,n$ divide $mn$, both divide $(t^{mn}-1)/(t-1)$. Unique factorization gives $AB\mid(t^{mn}-1)/(t-1)$, hence $\delta_{m,n}=((t^{mn}-1)/(t-1))/(AB)\in\Lambda$, and the signed quotient is a unit multiple. If $|p|=1$ or $|q|=1$, this quotient is a unit, so the same cyclic presentation gives $A_K=0$. [F6, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By [F5], $H_1$ of the cyclic cover of $P_{p,q}$ is $\ker d_1/\operatorname{im}d_2\cong\Lambda/(\delta_{p,q})$. The presentation complex and $E_K$ have the same fundamental group and the same homomorphism $\phi$; the fundamental groups of their cyclic covers are the corresponding kernels, and the deck action on their abelianizations is conjugation by a preimage of $1\in\mathbb Z$. Hurewicz therefore identifies their first-homology $\Lambda$-modules. Full AC [A1] supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of [F3] by [L1]; the deformation retraction then identifies the cyclic-cover homology of $E_K$ with that of $S^3\setminus K$. Thus $A_K\cong\Lambda/(\delta_{p,q})$. [A1, L1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The one-by-one presentation $[\delta_{p,q}]$ of $\Lambda/(\delta_{p,q})$ has $E_0$ generated by its sole $1\times1$ minor, namely $\delta_{p,q}$. Isomorphism invariance of the Alexander module and the definition [F6] give $E_0(A_K)=(\delta_{p,q})$ and hence $\Delta_K\doteq\delta_{p,q}$. [F4, F6, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
