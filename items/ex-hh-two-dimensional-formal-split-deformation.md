## Example

Let $R:=\mathbb Q\llbracket h\rrbracket$, let $S:=1+h\in R^\times$, and set $A:=R[X]/(X^2-S^2)$ with $x$ the class of $X$. The special fiber is $\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-1)$ and the generic fiber is over $K:=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. The idempotents below explicitly exhibit the two simple labels in both fibers.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $R=\mathbb Q\llbracket h\rrbracket$, $S=1+h$, $A=R[X]/(X^2-S^2)$, and $x=X+(X^2-S^2)$.

[F1] For a finite free $k\llbracket h\rrbracket$-algebra with split reduction $\prod_\lambda\operatorname{Mat}_{d_\lambda}(k)$, the special and generic fibers have the same simple-block labels and dimensions ([[thm-hh-split-deformation-labels-and-exceptional-parameters]]).

[F2] A finite free algebra over $k\llbracket h\rrbracket$ with split reduction is a product of full matrix algebras over $k\llbracket h\rrbracket$, via lifts of the reduction's matrix units ([[lem-hh-formal-deformation-matrix-unit-lifting]]).

[F3] For $R=k\llbracket h\rrbracket$, the residue map gives $R/hR\cong k$, and $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ is a field containing $R$ ([[lem-hh-one-parameter-formal-series-local-ring]]).

[F4] A polynomial over a commutative ring is a finitely supported coefficient function, with addition and multiplication given coefficientwise and by finite convolution ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]); these operations make $R[X]$ a commutative ring ([[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F5] In the commutative ring $R[X]$, the principal ideal $(p)$ is the ideal generated by $p$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]) and consists of the polynomial multiples $R[X]p$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F6] The quotient by an ideal is a ring with coset operations, and the quotient multiplication is well defined ([[def-quotient-ring]], [[thm-quotient-ring-laws]]).

[F7] A power series is a coefficient function with finite Cauchy convolution, and a series is a unit exactly when its constant coefficient is a unit ([[def-formal-power-series-and-coefficient-extraction]], [[thm-formal-power-series-unit-criterion]]).

[F8] A simple module is nonzero with no proper nonzero submodule ([[def-simple-module]]); a left module has a unital scalar action satisfying the module laws ([[def-left-and-right-modules]]).

[F9] A finite free module has a basis with unique finite coordinates ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[F10] An $R$-algebra is a unital ring with a central unital map from the commutative coefficient ring ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F11] The rationals are a field ([[thm-rat-field]]); in every field nonzero elements are invertible ([[def-field]]). The local-series lemma gives that $K$ is a field ([[lem-hh-one-parameter-formal-series-local-ring]]).

## Verification


**Given:** The algebra $A$ and element $x$ in the example.

1.1 Put $p(X):=X^2-S^2$. If a polynomial has degree $m\ge2$ and leading coefficient $a_m$, subtracting $a_mX^{m-2}p(X)$ cancels its degree-$m$ term and lowers its degree; finitely many repetitions give a representative $a+bX$. If $g\ne0$ has degree $n$ and leading coefficient $b_n\ne0$, then the leading term of $p(X)g(X)$ is $b_nX^{n+2}$ because $p$ is monic, so no nonzero multiple of $p$ has degree at most one. Thus every quotient class has a unique representative $a+bX$, and $(1,x)$ is an $R$-basis. The principal ideal is $R[X]p$ by [F5], and the quotient ring operations are well defined by [F6]. Reducing coefficients modulo $h$ using [F3] gives $\bar A=\mathbb Q[X]/(X^2-1)$. The elements $\bar e_\pm:=(1\pm\bar x)/2$ satisfy $\bar e_\pm^2=\bar e_\pm$, $\bar e_+\bar e_-=0$, and $\bar e_++\bar e_-=1$ by $\bar x^2=1$; their coefficient matrix in $(1,\bar x)$ has determinant $-1/2\ne0$ by [F11]. Hence the map $\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q\to\bar A$, $(a,b)\mapsto a\bar e_++b\bar e_-$, is a unital algebra isomorphism. The quotient map $R[X]\to A$ is unital, and $A$ is commutative, so the coefficient map $R\to A$ is central; thus $A$ is an $R$-algebra as in [F10]. [given, F3, F4, F5, F6, F9, F10, F11, algebra]

2.1 Define $S^{-1}$ by coefficients $[h^n]S^{-1}=(-1)^n$. By [F7], the constant coefficient of $(1+h)S^{-1}$ is $1$, and its coefficient at every $n\ge1$ is $(-1)^n+(-1)^{n-1}=0$; hence $(1+h)S^{-1}=1$. Put $u:=S^{-1}x$; the relation $x^2=S^2$ gives $u^2=1$. Direct expansion now gives $e_\pm:=(1\pm u)/2$ with $e_\pm^2=e_\pm$, $e_+e_-=e_-e_+=0$, and $e_++e_-=1$. Their reductions are $\bar e_\pm$. [step 1.1, F7, given, algebra]

3.1 The identities $e_++e_-=1$ and $e_+-e_-=S^{-1}x$ give $1=e_++e_-$ and $x=S(e_+-e_-)$. The definitions express each $e_\pm$ as an $R$-linear combination of $1,x$, while these identities express $1,x$ as combinations of $e_+,e_-$. Since $2$ is a unit by [F11] and $S$ is a unit by [F7], the changes of coordinates are inverse, so $(e_+,e_-)$ is an $R$-basis by step 1.1 and [F9]. The map $R\times R\to A$, $(a,b)\mapsto ae_++be_-$, is multiplicative because the idempotents are orthogonal and idempotent, is unital because they sum to $1$, and is bijective because they form a basis. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F7, F9, F11, algebra]

4.1 Reducing the isomorphism from step 3.1 gives $\bar A\cong\mathbb Q\times\mathbb Q$; extending scalars to $K$ gives $K\otimes_R A\cong K\times K$ by [F3]. For a product of two fields, the central coordinate idempotents split any module into their two images; a simple nonzero module has exactly one nonzero image, on which that coordinate field acts. For any field $L$, if $M$ is a simple nonzero left $L$-module and $0\ne m\in M$, then $L\to M$, $a\mapsto am$, is injective because nonzero $a$ is invertible by [F11], and its nonzero image is all of $M$ by [F8]. Thus each coordinate field has exactly one simple module up to isomorphism, namely itself. The labels $+$ and $-$ are the reductions of $e_+$ and $e_-$, in agreement with the split-label theorem [F1] and the matrix decomposition [F2]. [step 3.1, F1, F2, F3, F8, F11, algebra] ∎
