## Statement

Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, let $\mathfrak p$ be a nonzero prime ideal of $R$, and let $v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$ denote the prime-ideal valuation of a nonzero fractional ideal $I$. For every integer $n\in\mathbb Z$, there exists $\alpha\in K^\times$ such that $v_{\mathfrak p}((\alpha))=n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Dedekind domain $R$, its fraction field $K$, and a nonzero prime ideal $\mathfrak p\subset R$.

[F1] A Dedekind domain has the stated one-dimensional normal-domain meaning, and localizing it at a nonzero prime gives a discrete valuation ring. ([[def-dedekind-domain]], [[lem-dedekind-localisation-at-nonzero-prime-is-dvr]])

[F2] A discrete valuation ring is the valuation ring of a surjective discrete valuation on its fraction field; a uniformizer has value $1$ and generates its maximal ideal. ([[def-discrete-valuation-ring]], [[def-uniformising-parameter]])

[F3] For every nonzero fractional ideal $I$, $v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$ is the unique integer $j$ such that $I_{\mathfrak p}=\mathfrak p^jR_{\mathfrak p}$. ([[def-fractional-ideal]], [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]])

## Proof

1.1 Put $V=R_{\mathfrak p}$. By [F1], $V$ is a DVR, and its fraction field is $K$ because $R\subseteq V\subseteq K$ and $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. By [F2], choose a uniformizer $\pi\in V\subseteq K$; it generates the maximal ideal $\mathfrak pV$. [F1, F2, given, choose]

2.1 Given any $n\in\mathbb Z$, set $\alpha=\pi^n\in K^\times$. Then $(\alpha)V=\pi^nV=(\mathfrak pV)^n=\mathfrak p^nV$, including negative $n$ as an equality of fractional ideals. By [F3], the unique exponent of $(\alpha)_{\mathfrak p}$ is $v_{\mathfrak p}((\alpha))=n$. Since $n$ was arbitrary, every integer is attained. [F3, step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
