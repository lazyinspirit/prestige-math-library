## Example

Let $n\ge1$, $\theta>0$, and take the experiment with sample space
$\mathcal X=\mathbb R^n$ and its Borel sigma-algebra, where under each
$\theta$ the coordinate random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ are independent,
each with
$P_\theta(X_i\le t)=0$ for $t<0$, $P_\theta(X_i\le t)=t/\theta$ for
$0\le t\le\theta$, and $P_\theta(X_i\le t)=1$ for $t>\theta$.
Write $M=\max_iX_i$ and let $0<\alpha<1$. The pivot $M/\theta$ has
distribution function $u^n$ on $[0,1]$. Setting $v=\alpha^{1/n}$ gives
the exact confidence set $C(X)=[M,M/v]\cap(0,\infty)$
with coverage $1-\alpha$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The $\mathbb R^n$ experiment specified in the Example, $n\ge1$,
and $0<\alpha<1$.

[F1] [[def-independent-random-elements]]: independence means independence of
the sigma-algebras generated by the coordinate random elements.

[F2] [[thm-independent-random-elements-have-product-joint-law]]: the joint law
of finitely many independent random elements is the product of their
marginal laws.

[F3] [[def-pivot]]: a pivot has parameter-free pushforward law.

[F4] [[def-confidence-set-and-coverage]]: coverage is the probability that
the true parameter is in the set.

[F5] [[thm-pivot-inversion-exact-coverage]]: pivot inversion of a measurable
acceptance set with mass $1-\alpha$ has exact coverage $1-\alpha$.

[F6] [[def-statistical-experiment]]: an experiment assigns a probability law
to each parameter on one measurable observation space.

[F7] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the
open sets of the underlying topological space.

## Verification

1.1 The sample sigma-algebra is the Borel sigma-algebra of $\mathbb R^n$ by [F7]. The maximum map $m(x)=\max_i x_i$ satisfies $|m(x)-m(y)|\le\max_i|x_i-y_i|$, so it is continuous. The preimages of open sets are therefore open; since the Borel sigma-algebra is generated by open sets, preimages of every Borel set are Borel and $M=m(X)$ is measurable. Also $m/\theta$ is continuous because $\theta>0$. The coordinate variables are independent by [F1], and [F2] gives their product joint law. [given, F1, F2, F6, F7, algebra]

1.2 If $0\le u\le1$, then $P_\theta(M/\theta\le u)=P_\theta(X_1\le u\theta,\ldots,X_n\le u\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^n u=u^n$. For $u<0$ the probability is $0$, and for $u\ge1$ it is $1$. This law does not depend on $\theta$, so $M/\theta$ is a pivot by [F3]. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The distribution function $u^n$ established in step 1.2 is continuous. Therefore $P_\theta(\alpha^{1/n}\le M/\theta\le1)=1-(\alpha^{1/n})^n=1-\alpha$. By [F5], pivot inversion gives exact coverage. [given, F3, F5, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Since $\theta>0$, the inequality $\alpha^{1/n}\le M/\theta\le1$ is equivalent to $M\le\theta\le M/\alpha^{1/n}$. Thus the inverted set is $[M,M/\alpha^{1/n}]\cap(0,\infty)$, and [F4] identifies its coverage with the probability computed in step 2.1. [given, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
