## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $\pi:A\to S$ be a projective abelian scheme over a connected smooth quasiprojective complex variety. Let $T$ be a finite tensor construction from $R^1\pi^{an}_*\mathbb Q$, its dual, finite sums and Tate lines. Let $V\subseteq T$ be a rational local subsystem whose fibres consist of Hodge tensors of type $(0,0)$, and suppose its monodromy preserves a positive-definite rational form. If every element of $V$ at one complex point is an absolute Hodge tensor in the first-cohomology realizations, then every element of $V$ at every complex point is absolute after parallel transport. No higher-degree étale comparison or general absolute-Hodge theorem is asserted.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** trivialize finite monodromy, establish the degree-one comparisons and algebraic horizontal sections, and descend the common rational tensor on every conjugate curve. Each successive construction has its own temporary auxiliary symbols; after each construction, return to the notation of the Statement.

1.1 Let $M=R_1\pi_*^{an}\mathbb Z$ and let $T(M)$ contain the integral realization of the given rational tensor construction, omitting the constant Tate lines from the monodromy calculation. The intersection of $V$ with this integral lattice is saturated and full. Apply the following construction with that intersection as $L$; the positive rational form is in particular a positive real invariant form. [given, construct]

2.1 By [F1], $A[3]\to S$ is finite étale. By [F2], a geometric fibre is a complex torus $V/\Lambda$, whose $3$-torsion is canonically $\tfrac13\Lambda/\Lambda\cong\Lambda/3\Lambda\cong(\mathbb Z/3)^{2g}$. The finite set of ordered bases of this fibre is acted on continuously by the étale fundamental group. By [F1] it is represented by a finite étale cover $P\to S$; it is nonempty and its pullback trivializes $A[3]$. Choose a connected component $S'$ of $P$. A nonempty component of a finite étale cover of connected $S$ maps both openly and closedly to $S$, hence surjects. It is smooth and connected over $\mathbb C$, and its pullback still trivializes $A[3]$. [F1, F2, given, choose, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

2.2 The image of monodromy on $L$ is finite. Indeed a positive-definite form bounds the Euclidean lengths of the images of a chosen lattice basis, and only finitely many lattice vectors have bounded length. Also, if $B$ is a finite-order integral matrix congruent to $I$ modulo $3$, then $B=I$: otherwise replace $B$ by a power of prime order $p$ and write $B=I+3^kC$, $k\ge1$, with $C$ not all divisible by $3$. For $p\ne3$, the binomial expansion modulo $3^{k+1}$ contradicts $B^p=I$. For $p=3$, expansion modulo $3^{k+2}$ gives $B^3\equiv I+3^{k+1}C$, again a contradiction. [given, choose, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 We identify the analytic local system $A[3]^{\mathrm{an}}$ with $M/3M$ without using cohomology comparison. For a point $x$ of order dividing $3$ on a torus, choose a path $\gamma$ from $0$ to $x$ and take the homology class of the loop $3\gamma$, modulo $3$. Changing $\gamma$ changes this class by $3$ times a loop. In the uniformization $V/\Lambda$, lifting $\gamma$ ends at $\lambda/3$ for a unique class $\lambda\bmod3\Lambda$, so the resulting map is the standard isomorphism $A[3]\cong H_1(A,\mathbb Z)/3$. It is natural under group homomorphisms. It also commutes with transport along the base: a local smooth trivialization can be built by patching local right inverses to the differential with the partitions of unity in [F3] and lifting radial base paths by the resulting horizontal vector fields. Properness keeps those lifts in a compact set, and the ODE existence and smooth-dependence statements in [F3] give the trivialization. Translate it fibrewise to fix zero; a nearby torsion point can be joined to the transported endpoint by a short path. The resulting loops vary continuously, so their integral homology classes are locally constant. By [F3] and finiteness, the analytification of $A[3]\to S$ is a topological covering. Thus the fibrewise isomorphisms form an isomorphism of local systems. In particular the monodromy of $M$ on $S'$ is the identity modulo $3$. [F1, F2, F3, given, step 2.1, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

4.1 On $S'$, step 3.1 makes monodromy on $M$ congruent to the identity modulo $3$. The same is true on $M^*$, all tensor products and their finite direct sums. For $v\in L$, write $(\gamma-1)v=3w$ with $w\in T(M)$; stability and saturation imply $w\in L$. Thus monodromy on $L$ is congruent to the identity modulo $3$. Its image is finite by step 2.2, so the torsion-free congruence calculation there makes it trivial. A local system with trivial monodromy is constant. The constructions of $A[3]$, paths and the homology identification commute with algebraic group endomorphisms, giving the final naturality assertion. No comparison of étale cohomology with Betti cohomology was used. [given, step 3.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

5.1 For the remainder of this calculation, let $A=\mathcal A$ and let $n\ge1$ be arbitrary. The following construction applies to every projective complex abelian scheme over every smooth connected complex base, including every field conjugate and every base change used below. [given, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 Fix a geometric fibre and write it temporarily as $A$. By [F4], $A^{\mathrm{an}}=V/\Lambda$ with $V$ contractible. Every connected finite topological cover is the quotient $V/H\to V/\Lambda$ for a finite-index subgroup $H\subseteq\Lambda$, by [F4]. To algebraize it, use [F5] to choose a positive closed $(1,1)$ form $\omega$ on $A^{\mathrm{an}}$ whose cohomology class is integral. Average its translates over the compact real torus $V/\Lambda$, using normalized Lebesgue measure on a fundamental parallelepiped. The average $\bar\omega$ is translation-invariant, positive, closed and of type $(1,1)$. Each translation is smoothly homotopic to the identity, so $[\bar\omega]=[\omega]$. The invariant form is a constant alternating form $\psi$ on $V_\mathbb R$; its values on lattice pairs are the integral periods of $[\omega]$ on the corresponding two-dimensional subtori, so $\psi(\Lambda,\Lambda)\subseteq\mathbb Z$. Positivity gives $\psi(Jv,Jw)=\psi(v,w)$ and $\psi(v,Jv)>0$. Its restriction to $H$ is an integral positive Riemann form. [F4, F5, given, choose, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

6.2 Let $Y\to A$ be a connected finite étale cover. It is smooth and projective by [F6]. Its analytification is a finite topological cover by the local implicit theorem in [F9] and properness. It is connected: otherwise its compact analytic connected components are finitely many disjoint projectively embedded complex manifolds. By [F7], each is the complete complex point set of a closed algebraic subvariety. These disjoint closed subvarieties cover every complex point of reduced $Y$, hence cover $Y$ scheme-theoretically; more than one would contradict connectedness. The same reasoning applies componentwise to an arbitrary finite étale cover. [F6, F7, F9, given, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

6.3 Let $G=\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and let $\pi:A\to S$ again denote the given family. By [F11], the kernel $A[n]$ is a finite étale group scheme and $[n]:A\to A$ is an $A[n]$-torsor. The sheaf $\mathcal H=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_S(A[n],G)$ is finite locally constant: after a finite étale cover trivializing $A[n]$, it is a finite constant set of group homomorphisms, and [F11] descends the associated finite action. For $\chi\in\mathcal H(T)$, push out the multiplication torsor along $\chi$, by taking the finite étale quotient of $A_T\times G$ under $A[n]_T$; this gives a $G$-torsor on $A_T$ and is functorial in $T$. Thus it defines a homomorphism of étale sheaves $\mathcal H\to R^1\pi_*G$. Here degree-one cohomology is the group of $G$-torsors: from an injective resolution, a representative of a cohomology class has a sheaf of local lifts which is a $G$-torsor, and conversely local torsor differences give the class; the two constructions are inverse. Local triviality and effective descent in [F11] represent these torsors by finite étale schemes. [F11, given, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

7.1 By [F5], the torus $V/H$ has a holomorphic projective embedding and hence an abelian-variety model $B$. The natural quotient $V/H\to V/\Lambda$ is a holomorphic group homomorphism, so [F6] algebraizes it to $q:B\to A$. It is proper and has finite complex fibres, because analytically it is a finite covering. The non-quasi-finite locus is closed by [F8] and has no complex point, hence is empty; [F8] makes $q$ finite. Its analytic differential is invertible at every complex point, so the algebraic differential is invertible there. The algebraic Jacobian criterion in [F9] gives an étale neighbourhood of every complex point; the non-étale locus is closed and has no complex point, so $q$ is finite étale. Thus every connected finite topological cover of $A^{\mathrm{an}}$ is the analytification of a finite étale cover. Finite disjoint unions give essential surjectivity for all finite covers. [F5, F6, F8, F9, given, step 6.1, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

8.1 Let $Y,Z$ be smooth projective complex varieties, with $Y$ connected, and let $h:Y^{\mathrm{an}}\to Z^{\mathrm{an}}$ be holomorphic. Its graph is a compact connected holomorphically embedded manifold in a product of projective spaces. By [F7] its reduced algebraic closure $G$ has precisely that graph as complex points. The projection $G\to Y$ is proper and bijective on complex points. As in step 7.1 it is quasi-finite everywhere and hence finite by [F8]. Its function-field extension is separable in characteristic zero; a primitive element and a nonzero discriminant show that a degree greater than one would give a fibre with more than one complex point. Thus it is finite birational, and the smooth integral target $Y$ is normal; [F8] makes the projection an isomorphism. The second projection therefore algebraizes $h$ uniquely. A continuous map over $A^{\mathrm{an}}$ between analytic finite covers is locally a composite of local inverse charts of the covers, so it is holomorphic. Applying this graph argument componentwise proves that analytification is full; equality of algebraic maps can be checked on complex points, so it is faithful. [F7, F8, F9, given, step 6.2, step 7.1, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

9.1 Steps 7.1 and 8.1 give the equivalence of finite-cover categories. It respects the fibre over the identity and is natural in $A$ because analytification and pullback are functorial. By [F4], the topological fundamental group is $\Lambda$; by [F9], finite étale covers are finite continuous sets for $\pi_1^{\mathrm{et}}$. Comparing automorphisms of the common fibre functor gives $\pi_1^{\mathrm{et}}(A,0)\cong\widehat\Lambda$. Continuous homomorphisms to a finite abelian group are therefore $\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,G)$, which is the degree-one singular cohomology of a connected torus with constant coefficients: a $1$-cocycle records its additive holonomy on loops, and every homomorphism is realized on the standard circle-product cell complex. For $g=0$, both categories have only finite disjoint unions of points and both groups are zero. [F4, F9, given, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

10.1 At a geometric point $\bar s$ of $S$, the stalk of $R^1\pi_*G$ is $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(A_{S^{sh}_{\bar s}},G)$: the strict henselization is the inverse limit of affine étale neighbourhoods, and [F10] supplies the finite-stage cohomology identification. The family over that ring is proper finitely presented, so [F10] identifies this group with $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(A_{\bar s},G)$. For any geometric fibre over an algebraically closed field $K$ of characteristic zero, choose a finitely generated subfield over which its equations and group law are defined, take its algebraic closure $k_0$ inside $K$, and embed $k_0$ into $\mathbb C$. The fibre descends to $k_0$, and [F13] identifies its finite étale covers after extension to either $K$ or $\mathbb C$. Thus step 9.1, applied over $\mathbb C$, identify its finite étale torsors with finite topological torsors and give $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(A_{\bar s},G)=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,G)$. The multiplication-by-$n$ torsor has monodromy $\Lambda\to\Lambda/n\Lambda\simeq A[n]_{\bar s}$, and every homomorphism to $G$ factors uniquely through this quotient. Hence the stalk of the map in step 6.3 is an isomorphism. A sheaf morphism that is an isomorphism on all geometric stalks is an isomorphism; therefore $R^1\pi_*G\cong\mathcal H$. [F10, F11, F13, given, step 9.1, step 6.3, algebra, step 5.1]

10.2 Analytification of the finite étale group scheme $A[n]$ is a topological covering: [F12] gives local holomorphic inverse charts, and finiteness and properness exclude additional nearby sheets. On a fibre $V/\Lambda$, map a torsion point $x$ to the class of the loop $n\gamma$ modulo $n$, where $\gamma$ is any path from $0$ to $x$. Different choices of $\gamma$ change the class by a multiple of $n$, and uniformization identifies this map with $\tfrac1n\Lambda/\Lambda\simeq\Lambda/n\Lambda$. It is natural for group homomorphisms. It is also locally constant in a family: construct local smooth fibre trivializations by patching local right inverses to the differential with the partitions in [F12] and lifting radial paths by the horizontal ODEs; properness keeps lifts in a compact set. Translate the trivialization to fix the identity and join a nearby torsion point to the transported endpoint by a short path. The resulting loops vary continuously, so their homology classes are locally constant. 39.1 For this local construction write $S=C$; let $G\to S$ be any finite étale scheme representing a finite-level tensor local system. The following calculation algebraizes every continuous section of $G^{an}\to C^{an}$; it will then be applied to all levels. [given, step 38.1, construct]

40.1 The connected components of $G$ are open and closed. Since $G$ is smooth over $\mathbb C$, its local rings are regular and therefore domains by [F34]. Two distinct irreducible components cannot meet, since their local germs would give distinct minimal primes of a domain. Thus each connected component is irreducible. By [F33], its analytification is connected. In particular $S^{\mathrm{an}}$ is connected by the same argument. [F33, F34, given, algebra, step 39.1]

41.1 The morphism $G^{\mathrm{an}}\to S^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a finite covering. Indeed [F35] gives a local analytic inverse near each point of a finite fibre. Shrink the base neighbourhood so that these finitely many inverse charts are disjoint; finiteness makes the map proper, so no further sheet can enter after shrinking. On an evenly covered ball a continuous section takes values in exactly one sheet, so its image is open and closed in $G^{\mathrm{an}}$. The section image is connected because $S^{\mathrm{an}}$ is connected. By step 40.1 it is therefore precisely the analytification of a single algebraic connected component $G_0$. [F35, given, step 40.1, construct, algebra, step 39.1]

42.1 The map $G_0^{\mathrm{an}}\to S^{\mathrm{an}}$ is a one-sheeted covering, so its fibre over every complex point consists of one point. Since $G_0\to S$ is finite étale, it is finite locally free of locally constant rank; its rank is one at every complex point and hence everywhere on the connected finite-type base. The unit map $\mathcal O_S\to (G_0)_*\mathcal O_{G_0}$ is a map of rank-one locally free modules whose fibre at each complex point is the unit map $\mathbb C\to\mathbb C$, hence an isomorphism. Its cokernel vanishes by [F36]; a surjective map between rank-one locally free modules is an isomorphism, so the unit map is an isomorphism. Consequently $G_0\to S$ is an isomorphism and its inverse is an algebraic section. Two algebraic sections agreeing on complex points agree as maps, so it is the unique algebraic section with the given analytification. [F36, given, step 41.1, algebra, step 39.1]

43.1 Choose a rational tensor $v$ in the initial fibre of $V$ and use the first construction to regard it as a global rational Betti section after the finite étale cover. Multiply by one positive integer so it lies in one integral tensor lattice. The relative finite étale–Betti comparison constructed above extends by finite sums, tensors, duals and Tate lines. For each $n$, pass to a finite étale cover trivializing the finite étale groups $A[n]$ and $\mu_n$. By the effective finite étale descent of [F11], it descends to a finite étale $C$-group scheme, and its analytification is the corresponding topological tensor local system. The reduction of $v$ is a continuous section; the preceding finite-section argument makes it algebraic at every level. Uniqueness makes these sections compatible under divisibility of $n$. Taking their inverse limit and dividing by the one common integer gives a global finite-adelic étale tensor section. Its fibre is the Betti tensor $v$ under the constructed comparison. [F11, given, step 42.1, construct, algebra]

44.1 The relative de Rham–Betti comparison constructed above identifies $v$ on $C$ with a single-valued analytic horizontal section of the relative de Rham tensor bundle. The logarithmic curve calculation above makes this section algebraic. Because every fibre of $V$ is of Hodge type $(0,0)$ and the comparison is filtered, the algebraic section lies fibrewise in the algebraic Hodge subbundle $F^0$. The algebraic de Rham and finite-adelic étale sections and this $F^0$ equality can therefore all be transported along every field automorphism of $\mathbb C$. [given, step 43.1, construct, algebra]

45.1 Fix $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C)$ and pass to the conjugate connected curve and conjugate family. Naturality of the constructed degree-one comparisons identifies the two conjugate global sections with sections of $V_\sigma\otimes\mathbb C$ and $V_\sigma\otimes\mathbb A_f$ for the rational Betti tensor local system $V_\sigma$. At the conjugated initial point the assumption of absoluteness says both are scalar extensions of the same rational Betti vector $w$. If $g$ is a monodromy operator, invariance of the complexified section gives $(gw-w)\otimes1=0$; scalar extension from $\mathbb Q$ to $\mathbb C$ is injective, so $gw=w$. Thus $w$ defines a global rational section, and uniqueness of locally constant continuation makes both realization sections its scalar extensions everywhere. At the terminal point their values are therefore the same rational Betti tensor. The conjugated algebraic $F^0$ equality gives its Hodge type: for a rational weight-zero class, complex conjugation turns $F^0$ into the opposite filtration, whose intersection is the $(0,0)$ part. This holds for every $\sigma$, so the terminal tensor is absolute. Since $s_1$ and the original tensor were arbitrary, the theorem follows. [given, step 44.1, construct, algebra] ∎
