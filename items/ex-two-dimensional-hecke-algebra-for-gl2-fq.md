## Example

For $n=2$, $G=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)$, $B$ the upper triangular Borel and
$e_B=|B|^{-1}\sum_{b\in B}b$, the finite Hecke algebra
$H=e_B\mathbb C[G]e_B$ has $\mathbb C$-basis $\{T_1=e_B,T_s\}$, where $s$ is the
nontrivial element of $S_2$ and $T_s=|B|^{-1}\sum_{x\in B\dot sB}x$, and the
multiplication is
$$T_s^2=(q-1)T_s+q\,T_1.$$
Hence $H\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$ (the two projections onto the simple
$H$-modules), and the abstract presentation is
$H\cong\mathbb C[T]/(T^2-(q-1)T-q)$; for $q=2$ this is
$\mathbb C[T]/((T-2)(T+1))\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$. The normalization is
the one fixed in
[[def-bruhat-double-coset-basis-of-the-finite-hecke-algebra]]; in particular
$\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{End}_G(\mathbb C[\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)])=2$
and the opposite-algebra ambiguity is invisible because
$H\cong H^{\mathrm{op}}$. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A prime power $q$, the group $G=\operatorname{GL}_2(\mathbb F_q)$ with
upper triangular Borel $B$, the idempotent $e_B$, the finite Hecke algebra
$H=e_B\mathbb C[G]e_B$, the nontrivial element $s$ of $S_2$ and its permutation
matrix $\dot s$.

[F1] The standard basis elements $T_w=q^{\ell(w)}e_B\dot we_B=|B|^{-1}\sum_{x\in B\dot wB}x$,
$w\in S_2$, form a $\mathbb C$-basis of $H$, with $T_1=e_B$ the unit and
$\dim_{\mathbb C}H=2!$
([[def-bruhat-double-coset-basis-of-the-finite-hecke-algebra]]).

[F2] The rank-one quadratic relation is $T_s^2=(q-1)T_s+q\,T_1$
([[lem-rank-one-hecke-quadratic-relation]]).

[F3] The finite Hecke algebra is the specialization at $v=q$ of the generic
type-A Hecke algebra, presented by one generator $\tau$ with
$\tau^2=(q-1)\tau+q\cdot1$ when $n=2$, the braid and commutation families being
vacuous
([[thm-type-a-iwahori-hecke-presentation]]).

[F4] $H$ is a finite-dimensional semisimple $\mathbb C$-algebra and right
multiplication identifies $H$ with
$\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb C[G]}(\mathbb C[G]e_B)^{\mathrm{op}}\cong\operatorname{End}_G(\mathbb C[G/B])^{\mathrm{op}}$,
while $\mathbb C[G/B]\cong\mathbb C[\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)]$ as $G$-modules
([[thm-finite-hecke-algebra-as-convolution-corner-and-endomorphisms]],
[[lem-spherical-principal-series-is-the-flag-permutation-module]]).

[F5] For comaximal ideals $I,J$ of a commutative ring $R$ one has
$R/(I\cap J)\cong R/I\times R/J$; in the polynomial ring $\mathbb C[T]$ the
principal ideals $(T-q)$ and $(T+1)$ are comaximal when $q\ne-1$, and
$\mathbb C[T]/(T-\lambda)\cong\mathbb C$
([[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] applied to $S_2=\{1,s\}$ the algebra $H$ has the $\mathbb C$-basis $T_1=e_B$ and $T_s=q\,e_B\dot se_B=|B|^{-1}\sum_{x\in B\dot sB}x$, so $\dim_{\mathbb C}H=2$. [F1]

2.1 The algebra $H$ is generated by $T_s$, because the unital algebra generated by $T_s$ contains the unit $T_1$ and every element is a combination of the two basis elements; it is commutative because $T_s^2=(q-1)T_s+q\,T_1$ lies in the span of $1$ and $T_s$ by [F2]. Hence evaluation $\mathbb C[T]\to H$, $T\mapsto T_s$, is a surjective unital algebra homomorphism whose kernel contains $T^2-(q-1)T-q$; the induced map $\mathbb C[T]/(T^2-(q-1)T-q)\to H$ is a surjection from a $2$-dimensional algebra onto a $2$-dimensional algebra, hence an isomorphism. In particular the displayed multiplication rule of [F2] is the complete multiplication table of $H$ in this basis, matching the presentation of [F3]. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Factoring $T^2-(q-1)T-q=(T-q)(T+1)$ and noting that $q\ne-1$ because $q\ge2$, the ideals $(T-q)$ and $(T+1)$ of $\mathbb C[T]$ are comaximal, so [F5] gives an isomorphism $\mathbb C[T]/((T-q)(T+1))\cong\mathbb C[T]/(T-q)\times\mathbb C[T]/(T+1)\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$, hence $H\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$ by step 2.1. Under this isomorphism the two factors are the two simple $H$-modules and the two orthogonal idempotents of $H$ are the corresponding projections; for $q=2$ the factorization reads $(T-2)(T+1)$. [F5, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 By [F4] right multiplication identifies $H$ with $\operatorname{End}_G(\mathbb C[G/B])^{\mathrm{op}}$ and $\mathbb C[G/B]\cong\mathbb C[\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)]$, so $\dim_{\mathbb C}\operatorname{End}_G(\mathbb C[\mathbb P^1(\mathbb F_q)])=\dim_{\mathbb C}H=2$ by step 1.1; since $H$ is commutative by step 2.1, the opposite algebra is $H$ itself and the opposite-algebra ambiguity is invisible. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Step 1.1 gives the basis $T_1=e_B$, $T_s$ and the dimension, step 2.1 gives the quadratic multiplication and the presentation $\mathbb C[T]/(T^2-(q-1)T-q)$, step 3.1 gives the splitting $H\cong\mathbb C\oplus\mathbb C$, and step 3.2 gives the endomorphism-algebra dimension and the opposite-algebra statement. All algebras are finite-dimensional over $\mathbb C$, all sums are finite, and no choice principle is used. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
