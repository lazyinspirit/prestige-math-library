## Counterexample

Let $k$ be a field and let $X=\mathbb P^1_k=\operatorname{Proj}k[x_0,x_1]$
with its structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ ([[thm-projective-space-as-proj]]).
Then:

1. $\mathcal O_X$ is globally generated by its unit section
   $1\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)$ ([[def-globally-generated-sheaf]]);
2. $\mathcal O_X$ is **not H-very ample** over $\operatorname{Spec}k$
   ([[def-very-ample-invertible-sheaf-relative]]): the datum
   $(\mathcal O_X;1)$ with its single generating section $1$ has $n=0$ and
   corresponds to the constant structure morphism
   $X\to\mathbb P^0_k=\operatorname{Spec}k$, and for no $n\ge0$ is there a
   quasi-compact $\operatorname{Spec}k$-immersion $i:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ with
   $i^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_X$.

So global generation of an invertible sheaf does not imply relative very
ampleness, even over a field. This distinguishes global generation from relative very ampleness.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the scheme $X=\mathbb P^1_k=\operatorname{Proj}S$ with $S=k[x_0,x_1]$ graded by total degree, the standard opens $D_+(x_0)$, $D_+(x_1)$ and the coordinate $t=x_1/x_0$ on $D_+(x_0)$, the structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X=\widetilde S$, and the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the projective-space and sheaf constructions.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] For homogeneous $f\in S_+$ of positive degree the standard open $D_+(f)$ is the affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$ of $X$; the charts $D_+(x_0)$ and $D_+(x_1)$ cover $X$ with overlap $D_+(x_0x_1)$; and $\Gamma(D_+(f),\mathcal O_X(n))=S(n)_{(f)}$, with restrictions induced by homogeneous localisation, so that $\Gamma(D_+(f),\mathcal O_X)=S_{(f)}$ for the structure sheaf. ([[def-standard-open-proj]], [[thm-proj-structure-sheaf-scheme]], [[def-twisting-sheaf-proj]], [[lem-proj-associated-sheaf-basic-sections]])

[F2] A section of a sheaf on $X$ is the same as a compatible family of sections on the members of an open cover, and compatible local sections glue uniquely; the charts $U_0=D_+(x_0)$ and $U_1=D_+(x_1)$ are affine, hence quasi-compact, so their union $X$ is quasi-compact, and the structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is quasi-compact. ([[def-sheaf-on-topological-space]], [[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]], [[thm-closed-subspace-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], [[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-scheme]], [[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]])

[F3] The structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ is invertible; for a section $s$ of an invertible sheaf the nonvanishing locus $X_s$ is the set of points at which the image of $s$ in the fibre is nonzero; and the evaluation morphism of $\mathcal O_X$ with its unit section is the canonical identification. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-ample-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-globally-generated-sheaf]])

[F4] $\mathbb P^n_k$ has standard charts $U_i=\operatorname{Spec}k[x^{(i)}_\ell:\ell\ne i]$, and $\mathcal O(1)$ is glued from frames $e_i$ on $U_i$ by $e_j=x^{(i)}_je_i$, with coordinate sections $x_j\in\Gamma(\mathbb P^n_k,\mathcal O(1))$ restricting to $x^{(i)}_je_i$ on $U_i$ and to $e_j$ on $U_j$; also $\mathbb P^0_k=\operatorname{Spec}k$. A line bundle $L$ on $X$ is H-very ample relative to $\operatorname{Spec}k$ exactly when there are an integer $n\ge0$ and a quasi-compact $\operatorname{Spec}k$-immersion $i:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ with $i^*\mathcal O(1)\cong L$. ([[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]], [[def-very-ample-invertible-sheaf-relative]])

[F5] For every $S$-scheme $Y$ the assignment $\varphi\mapsto(\varphi^*\mathcal O(1);\varphi^*x_0,\dots,\varphi^*x_n)$ is a natural bijection from $S$-morphisms $Y\to\mathbb P^n_S$ to isomorphism classes of pairs $(L;s_0,\dots,s_n)$ with $L$ invertible and $s_0,\dots,s_n$ generating $L$; the $S$-morphism $\varphi$ attached to such data satisfies $\varphi^{-1}(D_+(x_i))=X_{s_i}$ and $x^{(i)}_j\circ\varphi=s_j/s_i$ on $X_{s_i}$, the quotient being a regular function because $s_i$ trivialises $L$ there. ([[thm-projective-map-line-bundle-data-equivalence]], [[thm-line-bundle-sections-define-projective-map]])

[F6] For a scheme $Y$ and a ring $A$, taking global sections is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}(Y,\operatorname{Spec}A)\to\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathrm{CRing}}(A,\Gamma(Y,\mathcal O_Y))$, so a morphism into an affine scheme is determined by its ring map on global sections; a morphism into the affine space $\mathbf A^n_S=\operatorname{Spec}_{\mathcal O_S}\mathcal O_S[t_1,\dots,t_n]$ is given by its $n$ coordinate functions. ([[thm-morphisms-into-affine-scheme-global-sections]], [[ex-affine-n-space-over-arbitrary-base]], [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]])

[F7] A closed immersion is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset and an open immersion identifies its source with an open subscheme, so both are injective on points; an immersion is a composite of a closed immersion into an open subscheme followed by the inclusion of that open subscheme, hence is injective on points. A morphism that factors through a one-point scheme is constant on points, so it is not injective whenever its source has at least two points. ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]], [[def-open-immersion-schemes]], [[def-locally-closed-immersion]])

[F8] The polynomial ring $k[t]$ over the field $k$ is a domain, and $(0)$ and $(t)$ are two distinct prime ideals of $k[t]$; since $U_0\cong\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ is an open subscheme of $X$, the space $X$ has at least two points. ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]], [[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]], [[thm-projective-space-as-proj]])

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct: compute the global sections of the structure sheaf by gluing its sections over the two standard charts and find that all of them are constants; then show that any morphism to projective space whose generating data consist of these constant sections factors through the structure morphism to the one-point scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k$, hence is constant, while an immersion is injective.

1.1 The chart rings. Every element of $S_{(x_0)}=(S[x_0^{-1}])_0$ is a class $a/x_0^m$ with $a\in S_m$ homogeneous of degree $m$, and division by $x_0$ rewrites it as a polynomial in $t=x_1/x_0$; conversely every polynomial in $t$ arises this way. Hence $S_{(x_0)}=k[t]$, and symmetrically $S_{(x_1)}=k[t^{-1}]$, so by [F1] one has $\Gamma(U_0,\mathcal O_X)=k[t]$ and $\Gamma(U_1,\mathcal O_X)=k[t^{-1}]$. [F1, algebra]
2.1 The overlap. The intersection $U_0\cap U_1=D_+(x_0x_1)$ has $S_{(x_0x_1)}=k[t,t^{-1}]$, and by [F1] the restriction maps are the homogeneous localisations $\Gamma(U_0,\mathcal O_X)\to\Gamma(U_0\cap U_1,\mathcal O_X)$ and $\Gamma(U_1,\mathcal O_X)\to\Gamma(U_0\cap U_1,\mathcal O_X)$, that is, the inclusions $k[t]\hookrightarrow k[t,t^{-1}]$ and $k[t^{-1}]\hookrightarrow k[t,t^{-1}]$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]
3.1 Global sections of the structure sheaf. By [F2] a global section of $\mathcal O_X$ is exactly a pair $(a,b)$ with $a\in k[t]$, $b\in k[t^{-1}]$ whose images in $k[t,t^{-1}]$ agree, that is, $a(t)=b(t^{-1})$. Every element of $k[t,t^{-1}]$ is a finite sum $\sum_{j}\alpha_jt^j$ with $\alpha_j\in k$; the left side involves only powers $j\ge0$ and the right side only powers $j\le0$, so all $\alpha_j$ with $j\ne0$ vanish and $a=b=\alpha_0\in k$. Hence $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, consisting of the constant global functions. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]
4.1 The unit section generates. The unit section $1\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$ is nonzero, and the evaluation morphism $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal O_X$ sends $1\otimes g$ to $g\cdot1=g$ on every open $U\subseteq X$, so it is surjective and $\mathcal O_X$ is globally generated by $1$. With [F3] it follows that $X_{1}=X$, and more generally $X_{c\cdot1}=X$ for $c\in k^\times$ while $X_{0\cdot1}=\varnothing$, since a constant section has the same nonzero or zero value in every fibre. [F3, step 3.1, algebra]
5.1 A hypothetical immersion and its data. Suppose $\mathcal O_X$ were H-very ample over $\operatorname{Spec}k$. By [F4] there are $n\ge0$ and a quasi-compact $\operatorname{Spec}k$-immersion $i:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ with $i^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_X$; the structure morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ is quasi-compact by [F2], so the definition applies. By [F5] the morphism $i$ is the one attached to the data $(i^*\mathcal O(1);s_0,\dots,s_n)$ with $s_j=i^*x_j$, and these sections generate $i^*\mathcal O(1)$. Under the isomorphism $i^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_X$ and the identification $\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$ of step 3.1 the sections $s_j$ correspond to constants $c_j\cdot1$ with $c_j\in k$, and not all $c_j$ vanish, since the $c_j\cdot1$ generate the nonzero sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ on the nonempty scheme $X$; fix $j$ with $c_j\ne0$. [F2, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1]
6.1 The image lies in one affine chart. By [F5] the morphism $i$ satisfies $i^{-1}(D_+(x_j))=X_{s_j}$, and under the isomorphism $i^*\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O_X$ the nonvanishing locus $X_{s_j}$ is the nonvanishing locus of $c_j\cdot1$, which is all of $X$ by step 4.1 because $c_j\ne0$. Hence $i^{-1}(D_+(x_j))=X$: the image of $i$ is contained in the single standard chart $U_j=D_+(x_j)=\operatorname{Spec}k[x^{(j)}_\ell:\ell\ne j]$. [F4, F5, step 4.1, step 5.1]
7.1 The chart coordinates are constant. Again by [F5], on $X_{s_j}=X$ one has $x^{(j)}_\ell\circ i=s_\ell/s_j$ for every $\ell\ne j$; under the identifications of step 5.1 this quotient is $(c_\ell\cdot1)/(c_j\cdot1)=c_\ell/c_j\in k$, a constant regular function on $X$. [F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]
8.1 The morphism is constant. Write $R_j=k[x^{(j)}_\ell:\ell\ne j]$, so that $U_j=\operatorname{Spec}R_j$; since $i$ maps into $U_j$, it factors as $\iota\circ i'$ with $i':X\to U_j$ and $\iota:U_j\hookrightarrow\mathbb P^n_k$ the open immersion. By [F6] the morphism $i'$ is determined by the ring map $(i')^\#:R_j\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$, which sends $x^{(j)}_\ell$ to the global function $x^{(j)}_\ell\circ i'=c_\ell/c_j$ of step 7.1. This ring map is the composite of the ring homomorphism $R_j\to k$, $x^{(j)}_\ell\mapsto c_\ell/c_j$, with the structure map $k\to\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=k$; let $d:\operatorname{Spec}k\to U_j$ be the morphism corresponding to $R_j\to k$, $x^{(j)}_\ell\mapsto c_\ell/c_j$ under the bijection of [F6], and let $p:X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$ be the structure morphism. Then $(d\circ p)^\#=(i')^\#$, so $i'=d\circ p$ and $i=\iota\circ d\circ p$ factors through the one-point scheme $\operatorname{Spec}k$. [F6, step 6.1, step 7.1]
9.1 Contradiction. By step 8.1 the morphism $i$ is constant on points, its image being the single point $d(\operatorname{Spec}k)$; but by [F7] the immersion $i$ is injective on points, and by [F8] the source $X$ has at least two points. A constant map from a set with at least two points into any set is not injective, so no such $i$ exists for any $n\ge0$, and $\mathcal O_X$ is not H-very ample over $\operatorname{Spec}k$. [F7, F8, step 5.1, step 8.1]
10.1 Conclusion. The structure sheaf $\mathcal O_X$ is globally generated by its unit section by step 4.1, while steps 5.1 to 9.1 show that it is not H-very ample over $\operatorname{Spec}k$. For $n=0$ the same computation reads: the data $(\mathcal O_X;1)$ consist of a single generating section and correspond by [F5] to the morphism $X\to\mathbb P^0_k=\operatorname{Spec}k$, which is the structure morphism and is constant, so the associated map to $\mathbb P^0_k$ is constant and is not an immersion. Thus global generation does not imply relative very ampleness; $\mathcal O_X$ is generated by one global section but is not H-very ample. The Axiom of Choice [A1] is inherited through the projective-space and data-equivalence suppliers; the only further data used are the two charts and the finite list of constants, so no choice is made here. [A1, F5, step 4.1, step 9.1, cases: n=0 and n at least 1]
\qed
