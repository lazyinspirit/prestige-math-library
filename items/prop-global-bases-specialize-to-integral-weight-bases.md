## Statement

For every dominant $\lambda$ and every symmetrizable datum, the integral highest lattice $V_A(\lambda)=U_A^-v_\lambda$ has its genuine lower global $A$-basis $G_\lambda(\mathcal B(\lambda))$ of [[def-lower-global-basis-of-a-quantum-highest-weight-module]]. Hence for any unital base change $A\to S$ of $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$, including nonflat specializations at a root of unity with $\zeta$ invertible, the tensor fibre $V_A(\lambda)\otimes_AS$ has the corresponding $S$-basis $G_\lambda(b)\otimes1$, indexed by the same crystal elements, and for $S\ne0$ has identical finite weight ranks.

At a characteristic-zero field specialization $q=1$, the positive toral quotient and the local $q=1$ comparison identify the fibre with the classical simple integrable highest weight module and its Weyl–Kac character. The zero and infinity chart lattices are obtained separately from the $\mathbb Z[q]$- and $\mathbb Z[q^{-1}]$-spans of the global vectors followed by rational discrete-valuation-ring extension; there is no map $A\to A_0$ sending the unit $q$ to $0$. At roots of unity the free integral fibre basis still survives, but no simplicity, semisimplicity or equality with a naive ordinary-generator specialization is asserted. For a specialization to a field, if $\zeta_i^2$ has order $l>1$, then the Gaussian factorial $[r]_{q_i^2}!$ vanishes once $r\ge l$; if $\zeta_i^2=1$ in characteristic zero every factor equals $j$ and that vanishing argument does not apply. Thus the algebra or module root-of-unity boundary is distinguished from the unconditional free-basis base-change fact.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The lower global $A$-basis $G_\lambda(\mathcal B(\lambda))$ of $V_A(\lambda)$ with its finite free weight components, the balanced chart factorization for $V_A(\lambda)$, the local $q=1$ comparison theorems, and the geometric Gaussian factorials of [[lem-lusztig-and-kashiwara-normalizations-match-by-root-color]].

[F1] Integral global basis ([[thm-existence-bar-invariance-and-uniqueness-of-the-lower-global-basis]], [[thm-global-basis-projection-from-the-negative-half-to-a-highest-weight-module]], [[def-lower-global-basis-of-a-quantum-highest-weight-module]]): $V_A(\lambda)$ is a free $A$-module with basis $G_\lambda(\mathcal B(\lambda))$, each label-weight component $V_A(\lambda)_\mu$ is a finite free $A$-module, and $V_A(\lambda)$ spans the field module $V(\lambda)$.

[F2] Base change for free modules: if a module over a commutative ring $A$ is free with basis $(e_b)_{b\in B}$ and $A\to S$ is a unital ring map, then $M\otimes_AS$ is free with basis $(e_b\otimes1)$ and the same index set; no flatness or injectivity of the map is used. For finitely generated free $A$-modules the rank is preserved under every unital base change to a nonzero ring.

[F3] The $q=1$ comparison ([[prop-specialization-at-q-one-recovers-the-classical-enveloping-relations]], [[lem-q-one-toral-specialization-identifies-the-classical-cartan-generators]], [[prop-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-modules-are-semisimple]], [[lem-local-q-one-deformation-proves-integrable-highest-weight-universality]], [[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]): at a characteristic-zero field specialization $q=1$ the positive toral quotient and the local deformation comparison identify the specialized module with the classical simple integrable highest weight module of the chosen realization, weight by weight, with the classical Weyl–Kac character.

[F4] Chart factorization ([[def-balanced-triple-and-globalization-map]], [[thm-existence-bar-invariance-and-uniqueness-of-the-lower-global-basis]]): the balanced triple of $V_A(\lambda)$ gives $V_A(\lambda)\cap\mathcal L(\lambda)\cong\mathbb Z[q]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}E_{\mathbb Z}$ and $V_A(\lambda)\cap\psi(\mathcal L(\lambda))\cong\mathbb Z[q^{-1}]\otimes_{\mathbb Z}E_{\mathbb Z}$ with the same free $\mathbb Z$-basis $E_{\mathbb Z}=\bigoplus_b\mathbb ZG_\lambda(b)$; the genuine chart lattices over $A_0,A_\infty$ are the localizations of these $\mathbb Z[q]$- and $\mathbb Z[q^{-1}]$-forms, and $q$ is a unit of $A$, so no specialization $A\to A_0$ with $q\mapsto0$ exists.

[F5] Root-of-unity scalars: at a specialization $q=\zeta$ to a field with $\zeta$ invertible the Gaussian factorial is $[r]_{q_i^2}!=\prod_{j=1}^r(1+q_i^2+\cdots+q_i^{2(j-1)})$. If $\zeta_i^2$ has finite order $l>1$, the factor with $j=l$ is $0$, so $[r]_{q_i^2}!=0$ for all $r\ge l$; if $\zeta_i^2=1$ and the ground field has characteristic zero, each factor equals $j$, so $[r]_{q_i^2}!=r!\ne0$. These are exact evaluations of the defining product.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Unconditional base change.** By [F1] and [F2] the fibre $V_A(\lambda)\otimes_AS$ is free over $S$ with basis $G_\lambda(b)\otimes1$, for every unital $S$; the rank of each finite weight component over $S$ equals $\dim_{\mathbb Q(q)}V(\lambda)_\mu$ when $S$ is nontrivial, and the fibre still spans the extended module. No flatness is needed because the basis itself tensors. [F1, F2]

2.1 **The $q=1$ specialization.** Put $R=\mathbb Q[q]_{(q-1)}$. The $R$-span of $V_A(\lambda)$ is exactly the ordinary-word lattice $V_R$ of the local comparison in [F3]: every divided word becomes an ordinary word times a product of the $R$-unit inverses $[r]_i!^{-1}$, and ordinary words already belong to $V_A(\lambda)$. Hence a characteristic-zero field fibre is the scalar extension of $V_R/(q-1)V_R$, which [F3] identifies with the classical simple integrable highest module. [[thm-weyl-kac-character-formula]] gives its Weyl–Kac character; in finite type the formal sum and product are finite, giving the Weyl character. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Chart lattices.** The two chart lattices are read off from the $\mathbb Z[q]$- and $\mathbb Z[q^{-1}]$-forms of the same free $\mathbb Z$-basis in [F4] and then localized at $0$ and $\infty$; since $q$ is a unit of $A$, there is no ring map $A\to A_0$ sending $q$ to $0$, so the charts are genuinely distinct from the base changes of step 1.1 and no specialization to $0$ is asserted. [F4]

2.3 **The root-of-unity boundary.** At a field specialization $q=\zeta$ with $\zeta$ invertible, [F5] vanishes the geometric factorial as soon as the index reaches the order $l>1$ of $\zeta_i^2$; since the divided powers in the integral basis are normalized by these factorials, the naive ordinary-generator description of the specialization may then fail, while the free basis $G_\lambda(b)\otimes1$ of step 1.1 is unaffected. If instead $\zeta_i^2=1$ in characteristic zero, [F5] gives $r!$ and no vanishing occurs, so that particular root-of-unity obstruction is absent. [F5, step 1.1]

3.1 **Conclusion.** Step 1.1 gives the unconditional free-basis base change with preserved finite weight ranks; step 2.1 gives the classical $q=1$ module and character; step 2.2 separates the chart lattices from specializations; step 2.3 identifies the exact scalar root-of-unity boundary, asserting no simplicity or semisimplicity claim. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3] ∎
