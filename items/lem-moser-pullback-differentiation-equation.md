## Statement

Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$. If $\phi_t=\Phi_{t,0}$ is the evolution of a
smooth time-dependent vector field $X_t$ and $\omega_t$ is a smooth family of
forms, then

$$\frac d{dt}(\phi_t^*\omega_t)=\phi_t^*(\dot\omega_t+\mathcal L_{X_t}\omega_t).$$

If the $\omega_t$ are closed two-forms and $\sigma_t$ is a smooth family of
one-forms satisfying $\dot\omega_t=d\sigma_t$, then any solution of
$\iota_{X_t}\omega_t=-\sigma_t$ satisfies
$\frac d{dt}(\phi_t^*\omega_t)=0$. When $\omega_t$ is nondegenerate, that
contraction equation has a unique smooth solution $X_t$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, a smooth family $\omega_t$, a smooth family
$\sigma_t$ when the second assertion is used, and a local evolution $\phi_t$
generated by $X_t$.

[F1] Differentiation along a time-dependent evolution gives the displayed
pullback derivative. The supplier's definition of such fields assumes
$\mathrm{AC}_\omega$.
[[thm-differentiation-of-a-pulled-back-form-along-a-time-dependent-flow]].

[F2] Cartan's formula is
$\mathcal L_X\eta=d(\iota_X\eta)+\iota_Xd\eta$.
[[thm-cartans-magic-formula]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The first formula is [F1] with initial time zero. If $d\omega_t=0$, [F2] turns its parenthesis into $d\sigma_t+d(\iota_{X_t}\omega_t)$. Thus the Moser equation $\iota_{X_t}\omega_t=-\sigma_t$ makes the derivative zero. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 For nondegenerate $\omega_t$, the smooth bundle map $\omega_t^\flat:TM\to T^*M$ is invertible, so the unique solution is $X_t=-(\omega_t^\flat)^{-1}\sigma_t$. Matrix inversion in local coordinates proves joint smoothness in $(t,p)$. [step 1.1, given, algebra] ∎
