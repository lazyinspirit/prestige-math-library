## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice, and Dependent Choice. Let $(A,\iota)$ be a complex abelian variety with CM by a CM algebra $E$, type $\Phi$, and $\operatorname{End}(A_{\mathbb C})\cap E=O_E$. Let $E^r$ be the reflex field and let $m\geq1$. For every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/E^r)$ there is a fractional ideal $\mathfrak a(\sigma)$ of $E$, prime to $m$, and an $E$-linear isogeny $\lambda:A\to\sigma A$ such that $\lambda(x)=\sigma x$ for every $x\in A[m]$. Its ray class $[\mathfrak a(\sigma)]\in\operatorname{Cl}_{m}(E)$ is uniquely determined. For every sufficiently divisible integer $m$ (using the moduli $mO_E$ and $mO_{E^r}$), if $\mathfrak b$ is a fractional ideal of $E^r$ prime to $m$ and the arithmetic Artin symbol satisfies $\sigma|_{L_m}=\operatorname{Art}_{m,E^r}(\mathfrak b)$, then
$$[\mathfrak a(\sigma)]=[N_\Phi(\mathfrak b)]\quad\text{in }\operatorname{Cl}_{m}(E).$$
Here, for a number field $F$, $\operatorname{Cl}_m(F)$ is the quotient of fractional ideals prime to $mO_F$ by principal ideals generated by elements congruent to $1$ modulo $mO_F$ at every prime dividing $m$; for the CM algebra $E=\prod_jE_j$, set $\operatorname{Cl}_m(E)=\prod_j\operatorname{Cl}_m(E_j)$. The map $N_\Phi$ on ideals is induced by its finite-idèle determinant. The field $L_m$ is the ray class field of $E^r$, and the ideal Artin convention sends a prime ideal to arithmetic Frobenius $x\mapsto x^{N\mathfrak p}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the hypotheses in the unchanged Statement, including AC, Countable Choice and DC; no number-field model or ray class field is added as a Given.

[A1] The three choice hypotheses are explicit in the Statement and permit the supplied algebraic, analytic and CFT interfaces. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The reflex field is the fixed field of the stabilizer of the CM type. Pairs of the same CM type are E-isogenous. [[def-reflex-field-and-reflex-type-of-a-cm-type]] [[thm-complex-cm-abelian-varieties-classified-up-to-isogeny-by-cm-pairs]]

[F2] An existing E-isogeny with full integer-ring actions can be adjusted to every prescribed OE-linear m-torsion isomorphism. Its prime-to-m kernel ideal is c I J^{-1}, its kernel is A[a], and its ray class is independent of the chosen adjustment. [[lem-cm-isogeny-can-be-adjusted-to-prescribed-torsion-level]]

[F3] Every complex CM variety and all its endomorphisms have a number-field model. Multiplication by m has finite etale m-torsion in characteristic zero. Finite Galois correspondence identifies normal subgroup quotients with Galois groups of the corresponding intermediate fields. [[thm-complex-cm-abelian-variety-and-endomorphisms-descend-to-number-field]] [[lem-multiplication-by-n-on-abelian-scheme]] [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]] [[thm-normal-subgroups-and-quotients-in-the-galois-correspondence]]

[F4] A number-field abelian variety and finitely many endomorphisms have a projective good model outside a finite prime set. For geometrically E-isogenous maximal-order CM good models, specialization is an isomorphism on the entire geometric OE-linear Hom group, and a special isogeny lifts to an actual generic isogeny. [[lem-number-field-abelian-variety-and-endomorphisms-have-good-model-away-from-finite-set]] [[lem-cm-good-reduction-specialization-is-fully-faithful-for-maximal-order-homomorphisms]]

[F5] Good-reduction endomorphism specialization is injective. The full finite-residue-field Frobenius is induced by pi in OE, with principal ideal the product of the transported prime norms stated in the original Shimura–Taniyama theorem, at primes unramified in E. [[lem-good-reduction-specialization-injective-on-abelian-variety-endomorphisms]] [[thm-shimura-taniyama-frobenius-ideal-factorization-for-cm-abelian-varieties]]

[F6] The trace module exists and has trace character the sum of Phi. The reflex norm is its E-linear determinant on elements and finite ideles, and preserves the ray subgroups for the same integer modulus. Nonzero integral ideals have unique finite prime factorization; clearing a denominator gives the corresponding uniqueness for fractional ideals. [[lem-cm-type-module-exists-uniquely]] [[def-reflex-norm-associated-to-a-cm-type]] [[lem-reflex-norm-preserves-ray-subgroups]] [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]]

[F7] Prime-to-residue-characteristic geometric torsion specializes isomorphically and naturally for homomorphisms of abelian schemes. [[lem-arith-abelian-scheme-torsion-specialization-unramified]]

[F8] The global Artin map is the continuous product of the local arithmetic maps, kills principal elements, and gives the norm-quotient isomorphism for every actual finite abelian extension. The ray-class Artin theorem states existence of the finite abelian ray field with its exact ray-subgroup kernel and arithmetic Artin isomorphism. [F9] Every ray class has prime representatives of positive Dirichlet density; deletion of finitely many primes leaves a representative in every class. [[thm-ray-class-primes-dirichlet-density]]

## Proof

1.1 Write $F=E^r$ and fix the integer $m\ge1$. For $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb C/F)$, conjugating the tangent representation carries its characters to $\sigma\Phi$. The reflex-field stabilizer description gives $\sigma\Phi=\Phi$. The complex classification supplies an actual $E$-linear isogeny $A\to\sigma A$. The map $x\mapsto\sigma x$ on $m$-torsion is $O_E$-linear for the transported action. Apply the cited supplier to get an actual isogeny $\lambda_\sigma$ of degree prime to $m$ agreeing with $\sigma$ on $A[m]$. That supplier assigns the integral prime-to-$m$ ideal $$a(\lambda_\sigma)=cI J^{-1},$$ using compatible rational $E$-lattice coordinates, and proves independence of its ray class from the isogeny choice. Denote the resulting class by $c_m(\sigma)\in\operatorname{Cl}_{m}(E)$. This proves the existence and uniqueness part of the original Statement for every $m$, including $m=1$. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 For every number field $K$ containing all conjugates of $E$ and $F$, the determinant definition of the reflex norm gives, on elements and finite ideles, $$N_\Phi(N_{K/F}u)=\prod_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi^{-1}N_{K/\varphi E}(u).\tag{*}$$ Here and below apply the formula factorwise for a product CM algebra. To prove it, let $V_\Phi$ be the actual trace module of the cited supplier. Over $K$ it splits as $\bigoplus_{\varphi\in\Phi}K_\varphi$ as an $E\otimes K$-module, by its trace character and uniqueness. On $W=V_\Phi\otimes_F K$, the $E$-determinant of multiplication by $u$ is consequently the right side of (*). On the other hand, choose an $F$-basis of $K$ and let $M_u$ be its multiplication matrix. The determinant of this matrix is $N_{K/F}u$. To compute its determinant after acting on the commuting $E,F$-module $V_\Phi$, extend the coefficient field of each factor of $E$ to an algebraic closure and decompose the $F$-action into embedding characters $\rho$, with multiplicities $d_\rho$. The determinant is $\prod_\rho\rho(\det_F M_u)^{d_\rho}$, exactly the $E$-determinant of $N_{K/F}u$ on $V_\Phi$. Equality descends from the splitting field. The same finite matrix identity works after every scalar extension, including finite adeles, which proves (*).  Taking valuations gives the identical formula for fractional ideals. One can check this valuation passage directly at the primes used below, which are unramified in $K/\mathbb Q$: each relevant local field extension is unramified, a common uniformizer has norm valuation equal to its residue degree, and a local unit has unit determinant. Thus the ideal associated to $N_{K/L}$ is the prime-ideal norm $P\mapsto(P\cap O_L)^{f(P/(P\cap O_L))}$ used in the Frobenius theorem. In particular, for a prime $P$ of $K$ above $\mathfrak p$ of $F$ and $d=f(P/\mathfrak p)$, $$\prod_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi^{-1}N_{K/\varphi E}(P)=N_\Phi(\mathfrak p)^d.\tag{**}$$ [F6, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ideal above is intrinsic: its kernel is $A[a]$, and $\operatorname{Ann}_{O_E}(A[a])=a$. To see the last equality, use the invertible integral lattice $I$: the kernel is $a^{-1}I/I$, whose annihilator is $\{r:r a^{-1}I\subset I\}=a$. Conjugating an isogeny transports its kernel and the labelled $O_E$-action, so it preserves this intrinsic ideal. Composition multiplies ideals: for maps with lattice multipliers $c:I\to J$ and $d:J\to K$, their ideals multiply as $(cIJ^{-1})(dJK^{-1})=dcIK^{-1}$. Thus $\sigma\lambda_\tau\circ\lambda_\sigma$ has ideal $a(\lambda_\tau)a(\lambda_\sigma)$ and agrees with $\sigma\tau$ on $A[m]$. The uniqueness in step 1.1 gives $c_m(\sigma\tau)=c_m(\sigma)c_m(\tau)$.  The the cited supplier supplies a number field over which $A$ and all its endomorphisms are defined. Enlarge it to a finite Galois extension $K/F$ containing all conjugates of $E$ and every $m$-torsion point of $A$. The latter extension is finite: multiplication by $m$ is finite etale in characteristic zero by the cited supplier, so the finitely many points have coordinates algebraic over the number field. If $\sigma$ fixes $K$, then $\sigma A=A$ with its specified action and it fixes all $m$-torsion. The identity isogeny is an admissible choice and gives $c_m(\sigma)=1$. Therefore $c_m$ factors through the finite group $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$. Its image is abelian; the fixed field of its kernel is a finite abelian extension $H/F$. This proves the required finite factorization and continuity directly, without importing any class-field existence theorem. [F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Enlarge the same $K$ if necessary so it is Galois over $F$ and still contains all torsion and endomorphism data. The good-model lemma gives good reduction outside finitely many primes. Exclude those primes, primes over $m$, and primes ramified in $K/\mathbb Q$ or in the factors of $E$; these are finite sets. Finiteness of the ramified set follows, for example, by choosing integral primitive generators: outside their finitely many nonzero polynomial discriminants the monogenic algebras are etale and so are the localized integer rings. Let $\mathfrak p$ be a retained prime of $F$, choose $P$ above it, and put $\sigma=(P,K/F)$, arithmetic Frobenius. Use a lift to the decomposition group of the fixed algebraic closure at $P$. Put $Q=N\mathfrak p$ and $d=f(P/\mathfrak p)$, so $|\kappa(P)|=Q^d$. Since $\sigma$ acts on $\kappa(P)$ by $Q$-power Frobenius, the reduction of $\sigma A$ is the $Q$-Frobenius twist of $A_0$. The relative $Q$-power Frobenius is therefore an $O_E$-linear isogeny $$\mu_0:A_0\longrightarrow(\sigma A)_0.$$ Its $O_E$-linearity follows by applying the $Q$-power map to the coefficients of each endomorphism: that is precisely the transported action on the twist. The the cited supplier lifts $\mu_0$ uniquely to an $E$-linear isogeny $\lambda:A\to\sigma A$ after a finite field extension. It is the actual projective-ideal/Hom lifting theorem, not a claim that arbitrary special endomorphisms lift. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra]

3.2 For the actual finite abelian extension $H/F$ of step 2.1, the global Artin theorem and the continuous product-of-local-maps definition give a continuous idele-class map to $\operatorname{Gal}(H/F)$. Its kernel contains a ray subgroup for some integer modulus $M$ divisible by $m$. Indeed a basic identity neighborhood in the finite restricted product contains all local units outside a finite set and sufficiently deep principal units at that finite set; enlarge $M$ to impose those finitely many conditions. The reflex field is CM, so its archimedean local maps are trivial. Global reciprocity kills the principal subgroup. Therefore the kernel contains the full ray subgroup for $MO_F$. By [F8] the ray field $L_M$ exists with Artin kernel precisely that subgroup, and hence $H\subseteq L_M$. To verify the inclusion without assuming it, apply the global Artin theorem to the finite abelian compositum $HL_M$: every element fixing $L_M$ is the Artin image of an idele in its ray kernel, hence fixes $H$ too. Thus $c_m$ factors through $\operatorname{Gal}(L_M/F)$. [F1, F8, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Compose the $d$ conjugates: $$\nu=\sigma^{d-1}\lambda\circ\cdots\circ\sigma\lambda\circ\lambda:A\longrightarrow A.$$ The target is $A$ because $\sigma^d$ is the identity on its field of definition $K$. The decomposition-group choice makes its reduction the $Q^d$-power Frobenius. The original Shimura–Taniyama theorem supplies $\pi\in O_E$ inducing precisely that Frobenius and with $$ (\pi)=\prod_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi^{-1}N_{K/\varphi E}(P).$$ After a finite extension defining all maps, injectivity of endomorphism specialization gives $\nu=\pi$. If $a$ is the integral ideal of $\lambda$, step 2.1 gives $a^d=(\pi)$, and (**) gives $a^d=N_\Phi(\mathfrak p)^d$. The group of fractional ideals is a free abelian group on primes, factorwise for $E$, so equality of these positive powers implies $$a=N_\Phi(\mathfrak p).$$  The same $\lambda$ agrees with the chosen $\sigma$ on $A[m]$: their reductions agree because relative Frobenius is arithmetic Frobenius on residue coordinates, and prime-to-residue-characteristic torsion specializes injectively and naturally by the cited supplier. All the $m$-torsion was defined over $K$. The induced torsion map is an isomorphism, so the generic kernel has no prime-order subgroup over any prime dividing $m$; its order is therefore prime to $m$ by its finite integral-lattice description. This is an admissible isogeny in step 1.1. Consequently $$c_m(\operatorname{Frob}_{\mathfrak p})=[N_\Phi(\mathfrak p)]\quad\hbox{in }\operatorname{Cl}_m(E)\tag{***}$$ at every retained prime. This is the full missing prime comparison, including residue degree $d>1$; it does not assume the prime splits in $K$. [F2, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 There are now two homomorphisms $\operatorname{Cl}_{M}(F)\to\operatorname{Cl}_m(E)$: the character $c_m$ transported by arithmetic Artin, and the determinant reflex norm followed by reduction from modulus $M$ to $m$. The latter is well defined by the already the cited supplier, since $M$ is a multiple of $m$. By (***) these homomorphisms agree on every prime outside the finite exceptional set of step 3.1. The the cited supplier gives positive density $1/|\operatorname{Cl}_{M}(F)|$ in each ray class, so deleting finitely many primes still leaves a representative in every class. Thus the two homomorphisms agree on all classes, with no unproved Chebotarev theorem used. [F6, F8, F9, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 3.2, algebra]

6.1 The reflex norm on $\operatorname{Cl}_M(F)$ factors through $\operatorname{Cl}_m(F)$ by the same ray-subgroup lemma. Hence the equality of step 5.1 shows $c_m$ factors through the smaller ray field $L_m$. Compatibility of the global Artin maps under restriction identifies the resulting homomorphism with $$\operatorname{Cl}_m(F)\xrightarrow{N_\Phi}\operatorname{Cl}_m(E).$$ Thus for every fractional ideal $\mathfrak b$ prime to $m$ and every $\sigma$ whose restriction to $L_m$ is its arithmetic Artin symbol, one has $c_m(\sigma)=[N_\Phi(\mathfrak b)]$, exactly the displayed equality in the original Statement. The use of an auxiliary $M$ in the proof does not change the public torsion level or either public modulus. In fact the argument would prove this for every $m\ge1$; the original sufficiently-divisible formulation is retained without change. [F6, F8, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
