## Example

Let $C/\mathbb R$ be the smooth projective conic $X^2+Y^2+Z^2=0$. Then $C(\mathbb R)=\varnothing$, its Jacobian is the trivial group scheme, and $\operatorname{Pic}^1_{C/\mathbb R}\cong\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$ has a rational point, although $C$ has no degree-one line bundle.

[Poonen, Picard-scheme points versus line bundles, §5.7.1](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/Qpoints.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the projective conic $C=V_+(X^2+Y^2+Z^2)\subset\mathbb P^2_{\mathbb R}$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed by the curve, Picard-scheme and divisor suppliers used below. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[A2] The Axiom of Choice also gives Dependent Choice: for a serial relation on a nonempty set, choose one successor for each element and iterate the resulting function from the prescribed starting point. [A1]

[F1] A smooth plane curve of degree $d$ has genus $(d-1)(d-2)/2$. [[cor-genus-degree-smooth-plane-curve]]

[F2] The Jacobian is a smooth proper geometrically connected group scheme of dimension equal to the genus; in dimension zero, its rational identity makes it trivial. [[thm-picard-zero-represented-by-jacobian]]

[F3] The degree-one Picard component is an fppf torsor under the Jacobian. A torsor under the trivial group sheaf has exactly one section locally and hence exactly one section globally by the sheaf gluing axiom. [[thm-picard-degree-one-component-is-a-jacobian-torsor]] [[def-picard-degree-one-torsor-under-jacobian]] [[def-fppf-sheaf-and-sheafification]]

[F4] Every line bundle on an integral curve has a nonzero rational section and hence is isomorphic to $\mathcal O_C(D)$ for a divisor $D$. Degree is the sum $\sum_x n_x[\kappa(x):\mathbb R]$ over its closed points, and every closed-point residue field is finite over $\mathbb R$. [[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]] [[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]] [[lem-maximal-ideal-residue-field-of-an-affine-algebra-is-finite]]

[F5] Every finite extension of $\mathbb R$ is separable, is generated by one element, and has a minimal polynomial whose degree equals the field-extension degree; every irreducible real polynomial has degree one or two. A degree-one closed point on a real curve is an $\mathbb R$-point. [[cor-fields-of-characteristic-zero-and-finite-fields-are-perfect]] [[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]] [[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]] [[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]] [[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]] [[cor-irreducible-real-polynomials-have-degree-one-or-two]] [[def-residue-field-scheme-point]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** compute the conic's real points and geometric genus, identify its Jacobian after complexification, apply the degree-one torsor property, and compute degrees of all real divisors.

1.1 For real coordinates, $X^2+Y^2+Z^2=0$ forces $X=Y=Z=0$, which is not a projective point; hence $C(\mathbb R)=\varnothing$. The gradient $(2X,2Y,2Z)$ vanishes only at the zero vector, so the conic is smooth over $\mathbb R$ and over $\mathbb C$. A reducible plane conic over $\mathbb C$ is a union of two lines (possibly equal), and the lines meet at a singular point; hence this smooth conic is geometrically integral. It is projective, so proper, and [F1] gives genus zero. [A1, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F2], the Jacobian has dimension zero and is smooth proper geometrically connected; its identity is rational, so $J_C=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$. The dual is also trivial. [A2, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F3], $\operatorname{Pic}^1_{C/\mathbb R}$ is an fppf torsor under this trivial group. It is locally nonempty and its action map to its square is an isomorphism; since the group has one section, any two local sections agree, so the sheaf gluing axiom gives a unique global section. Thus the representing scheme is $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb R$ and has a rational point. [F3, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $\mathcal L$ be a line bundle on $C$. By [F4] write $\mathcal L\cong\mathcal O_C(D)$ for a divisor $D=\sum_x n_x[x]$. Each residue field $\kappa(x)$ is finite over $\mathbb R$ by [F4]; it is generated by a root of an irreducible real polynomial, so [F5] gives degree one or two. Degree one would make $x$ a real point, which step 1.1 excludes. Hence every closed point has residue degree two, and $\deg D=\sum_x2n_x$ is even. No line bundle on $C$ has degree one. [F4, F5, step 1.1] ∎

## Axiom use

AC supplies DC by [A2] and is inherited from the smooth-curve finite-type and Picard-scheme suppliers [F1]–[F4]. The explicit real-coordinate calculation and the even-degree sum use no further choice.
