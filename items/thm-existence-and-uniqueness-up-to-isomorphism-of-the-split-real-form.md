## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Every finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie
algebra $\mathfrak g$ has a split real form
([[def-split-real-form]]), and it is unique up to isomorphism of real Lie
algebras.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$; after the choices justified in [L0], write $\mathfrak h$ for a Cartan subalgebra, $\Phi$ for its root system and $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ for a base with Cartan matrix $A$; when a split real form of $\mathfrak g$ is under discussion, write $\mathfrak h_1$ for a Cartan subalgebra of it as in [L3].

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is inherited from the Serre presentation theorem and root data of [L1] and [L2], from the conjugacy theorem of [L4] and from the Chevalley-basis statement [L6], whose statements carry the assumption.

[L0] Every finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra has a Cartan subalgebra. For its finite reduced root system, a regular vector determines a positive system and its indecomposable positive roots form a base; the simple roots form a basis of the real root span and every root has integral coefficients of one sign ([[thm-cartan-subalgebras-exist-in-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras]], [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

[L1] The Serre presentation theorem, in both directions: $\mathfrak g\cong\mathfrak g(A)$ through the assignment $E_i\mapsto e_i$, $F_i\mapsto f_i$, $H_i\mapsto h_i$ determined by a root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$ over the base $\Delta$ ([[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]), the elements $e_i,f_i,h_i$ generating $\mathfrak g$ and satisfying exactly the relations of the Serre algebra $\mathfrak g(A)$; conversely, for any Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h'$ of $\mathfrak g$, any base $\Delta'$ of $\Phi(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h')$ with Cartan matrix $A$ and any root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triples $(e_i',f_i',h_i')$ over $\Delta'$, the elements $e_i',f_i',h_i'$ generate $\mathfrak g$ and satisfy exactly the relations of $\mathfrak g(A)$, so that $E_i\mapsto e_i'$, $F_i\mapsto f_i'$, $H_i\mapsto h_i'$ extends to an isomorphism $\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$; a homomorphism out of $\mathfrak g(A)$ is defined by prescribing the images of the generators and checking the relations ([[thm-serre-presentation-theorem]], [[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]], [[def-lie-algebra-presented-by-generators-and-relations]], [[thm-root-sl-two-triple]]).

[L2] Let $\mathfrak h'$ be a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$, $\Phi'=\Phi(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h')$ its root system and $h_{\beta}\in\mathfrak h'$ the coroot of a root $\beta$, characterized by $\beta(h_\beta)=2$ and $B(h_\beta,H)=2\beta(H)/\beta(H_\beta)$ for $H\in\mathfrak h'$. Then $\Phi'$ is finite, $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h'\oplus\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Phi'}\mathfrak g_\beta$ with one-dimensional root spaces, and for a base $\Delta'=\{\alpha_1',\dots,\alpha_r'\}$ of $\Phi'$ the Cartan matrix is $a'_{ij}=\alpha'_j(h_{\alpha'_i})=\langle\alpha'_j,\alpha_i'^\vee\rangle=2(\alpha'_j,\alpha'_i)/(\alpha'_i,\alpha'_i)$, an integer determined by the inner product $(\lambda,\mu)=B(H_\lambda,H_\mu)$ on $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Phi'$ ([[thm-roots-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]], [[def-root-and-root-space-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]], [[def-coroot-of-a-lie-algebra-root]], [[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]], [[cor-cartan-integers-are-integral]]).

[L3] A real form $\mathfrak g_1$ of $\mathfrak g$ ([[def-real-form-of-a-complex-lie-algebra]]) is split if it contains a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h_1$ of $\mathfrak g_1$ ([[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]]) such that all operators $\operatorname{ad}_H$, $H\in\mathfrak h_1$, are simultaneously diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$; equivalently $\mathfrak g_1$ has a basis in which all these operators are diagonal with real eigenvalues ([[def-split-real-form]]).

[L4] A subalgebra $\mathfrak h'$ of $\mathfrak g$ is a Cartan subalgebra if and only if it is nilpotent and $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h')=\mathfrak h'$; every Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ is abelian and satisfies $N_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h')=C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h')=\mathfrak h'$, and any two Cartan subalgebras of $\mathfrak g$ are carried to one another by an inner automorphism in the connected adjoint group of $\mathfrak g$ ([[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]], [[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-exactly-maximal-toral-subalgebras]], [[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-conjugate]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L5] $B$ is symmetric, invariant, nondegenerate, and preserved by every automorphism $\psi$ of $\mathfrak g$: $B(\psi x,\psi y)=B(x,y)$, because $\operatorname{ad}_{\psi x}=\psi\circ\operatorname{ad}_x\circ\psi^{-1}$ and $B$ is a trace form. For $x\in\mathfrak g_\beta$ and $y\in\mathfrak g_{-\beta}$ one has $[x,y]=B(x,y)H_\beta$, where $H_\beta$ is the Killing-dual of $\beta$, and the pairing $\mathfrak g_\beta\times\mathfrak g_{-\beta}\to\mathbb C$ given by $B$ is nondegenerate ([[def-killing-form-of-a-finite-dimensional-lie-algebra]], [[prop-trace-forms-are-symmetric-and-invariant]], [[def-killing-dual-vector-of-a-root]], [[cor-opposite-root-spaces-pair-nondegenerately]], [[prop-brackets-of-root-spaces]]).

[L6] The root-vector basis of $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h)$ can be rescaled so that $[e_\alpha,e_{-\alpha}]=h_\alpha$ for every root and the structure constants $N_{\alpha\beta}$, defined by $[e_\alpha,e_\beta]=N_{\alpha\beta}e_{\alpha+\beta}$, are integers satisfying $N_{\alpha\beta}=-N_{-\alpha,-\beta}$; the real span $\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{h_\alpha,e_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$ is then a split real form of $\mathfrak g$ with integer structure constants in the basis $\{h_{\alpha_1},\dots,h_{\alpha_r}\}\cup\{e_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$ ([[lem-chevalley-basis-and-real-structure-constants]]).

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose a Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a base $\Delta$ by [L0]. By [L6] the root vectors of this Cartan data can be rescaled so that $[e_\alpha,e_{-\alpha}]=h_\alpha$, the structure constants $N_{\alpha\beta}$ are integers with $N_{\alpha\beta}=-N_{-\alpha,-\beta}$, and $$\mathfrak g_0:=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{h_\alpha,e_\alpha:\alpha\in\Phi\}$$ is a split real form of $\mathfrak g$ with integer structure constants in the basis $\{h_{\alpha_1},\dots,h_{\alpha_r}\}\cup\{e_\alpha\}$. Write $h_i:=h_{\alpha_i}$, $e_i:=e_{\alpha_i}$ and $f_i:=e_{-\alpha_i}$; then $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$ is a root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple over $\Delta$, and the real span $\mathfrak a$ of the iterated brackets of $h_i,e_i,f_i$ is a subalgebra of $\mathfrak g_0$ containing these generators. Its complexification is a complex subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ containing $e_i,f_i,h_i$, which generate $\mathfrak g$ by [L1], so it equals $\mathfrak g$; hence $\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak a=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak g=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g_0$ and $\mathfrak a=\mathfrak g_0$. In particular $\mathfrak g$ has a split real form, generated by the Serre triple $(e_i,f_i,h_i)$. [A1, L0, L1, L6, algebra]

1.2 Let $\mathfrak g_1$ be a split real form of $\mathfrak g$ with split Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h_1$ as in [L3], and put $\mathfrak h':=\mathfrak h_1^{\mathbb C}$. Then $\mathfrak h'$ is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$: it is nilpotent, because the lower central series of the real nilpotent Lie algebra $\mathfrak h_1$ complexifies to the lower central series of $\mathfrak h'$; and it is self-normalizing, because if $x=u+iv$ with $u,v\in\mathfrak g_1$ satisfies $[x,\mathfrak h']\subseteq\mathfrak h'$, then $[u,\mathfrak h_1],[v,\mathfrak h_1]\subseteq\mathfrak h'\cap\mathfrak g_1=\mathfrak h_1$, so $u,v\in N_{\mathfrak g_1}(\mathfrak h_1)=\mathfrak h_1$ and $x\in\mathfrak h'$. [L3, L4, algebra]

1.3 By [L4] there is an inner automorphism $\psi$ of $\mathfrak g$ with $\psi(\mathfrak h')=\mathfrak h$. The transpose $\psi_*$, defined by $\psi_*\beta=\beta\circ\psi^{-1}$ on $\mathfrak h'$, carries $\Phi':=\Phi(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h')$ bijectively onto $\Phi$, and since $B$ is $\psi$-invariant by [L5] it is an isometry for the inner products $(\lambda,\mu)=B(H_\lambda,H_\mu)$ of [L2]; therefore it preserves Cartan integers $\langle\beta,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2(\beta,\alpha)/(\alpha,\alpha)$. Hence $\Delta':=\psi_*^{-1}(\Delta)$ is a base of $\Phi'$: it is a linearly independent subset of $\Phi'$ with $r$ elements, and every $\beta'\in\Phi'$ is a nonnegative or nonpositive integral combination of $\Delta'$, because $\psi_*\beta'$ is such a combination of the base $\Delta$. Its Cartan matrix $A'$ equals $A$, since $$a'_{ij}=\langle\alpha'_j,\alpha_i'^\vee\rangle=\langle\psi_*\alpha'_j,\psi_*\alpha_i'^\vee\rangle=\langle\alpha_j,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=a_{ij}.$$ [L2, L4, L5, algebra]

1.4 For every $\beta\in\Phi'$ the space $\mathfrak g_1\cap\mathfrak g_\beta$ is a real line. Indeed, by [L3] the operators $\operatorname{ad}_H$ with $H\in\mathfrak h_1$ are simultaneously diagonalizable over $\mathbb R$ on $\mathfrak g_1$; let $\mathfrak g_1=\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda$ be the decomposition into common eigenspaces. Complexifying gives $\mathfrak g=\bigoplus_\lambda V_\lambda^{\mathbb C}$ with $V_\lambda^{\mathbb C}\subseteq\mathfrak g_{\lambda^{\mathbb C}}$, where $\lambda^{\mathbb C}$ is the $\mathbb C$-linear extension of $\lambda$; since $\mathfrak h'$ is a Cartan subalgebra with $C_{\mathfrak g}(\mathfrak h')=\mathfrak h'$ by [L4] and the weight space of weight $0$ is $\mathfrak h'$ by [L2], the zero eigenspace is $V_0=\mathfrak h_1$, the intersection $\mathfrak h'\cap\mathfrak g_1$ being $\mathfrak h_1$. Every nonzero $\lambda$ with $V_\lambda\ne0$ satisfies $V_\lambda^{\mathbb C}\subseteq\mathfrak g_{\lambda^{\mathbb C}}\ne0$, so $\lambda^{\mathbb C}\in\Phi'$, distinct nonzero $\lambda$ have distinct extensions $\lambda^{\mathbb C}$, and $\dim_{\mathbb R}V_\lambda=\dim_{\mathbb C}V_\lambda^{\mathbb C}\le1$. Hence $${\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g_1}=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak h_1+\sum_{\lambda\ne0}\dim_{\mathbb R}V_\lambda\le\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak h_1+\#\{\lambda\ne0:V_\lambda\ne0\}\le\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak h_1+|\Phi'|=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak h'+\dim_{\mathbb C}\bigoplus_{\beta\in\Phi'}\mathfrak g_\beta=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathfrak g=\dim_{\mathbb R}\mathfrak g_1,$$ so all inequalities are equalities: each occurring nonzero $\lambda$ contributes a distinct root and $\dim_{\mathbb R}V_\lambda=1$, every root of $\Phi'$ occurs, and $\mathfrak g_1\cap\mathfrak g_\beta=V_\beta$ is one-dimensional over $\mathbb R$. [L2, L3, L4, algebra]

2.1 Choose $0\ne x_i\in\mathfrak g_1\cap\mathfrak g_{\alpha_i'}$ and let $y_i\in\mathfrak g_1\cap\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i'}$ be the vector with $[x_i,y_i]=h_{\alpha_i'}$; it exists and is unique because by [L5] one has $[x_i,y]=B(x_i,y)H_{\alpha_i'}$ for $y\in\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i'}$, where $H_{\alpha_i'}$ is the Killing-dual, and $y\mapsto B(x_i,y)$ is a nonzero $\mathbb R$-linear functional on the real line $\mathfrak g_1\cap\mathfrak g_{-\alpha_i'}$ by the nondegeneracy of the pairing in [L5]; moreover $H_{\alpha_i'}\in\mathfrak h_1$, since writing $H_{\alpha_i'}=u+iv$ with $u,v\in\mathfrak h_1$ and evaluating $B(H_{\alpha_i'},H)=\alpha_i'(H)\in\mathbb R$ on $H\in\mathfrak h_1$ gives $B(v,\mathfrak h_1)=0$ and hence $v=0$, so the image of $y\mapsto[x_i,y]$ is the real line $\mathbb R h_{\alpha_i'}$. Put $z_i:=h_{\alpha_i'}$. Then $z_i\in\mathfrak h_1$, the relations $[z_i,x_i]=\alpha_i'(z_i)x_i=2x_i$ and $[z_i,y_i]=-2y_i$ hold by [L2], and $[x_i,y_i]=z_i$ by construction; so $(x_i,y_i,z_i)$ is a root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple over $\Delta'$ with coroot $z_i$. Applying [L1] to the Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h'$ and the base $\Delta'$ of $\Phi'$, the elements $x_i,y_i,z_i$ generate $\mathfrak g$ and satisfy exactly the relations of $\mathfrak g(A')=\mathfrak g(A)$ (step 1.3). [L1, L2, L5, step 1.3, step 1.4]

3.1 Let $\mathfrak g(A)$ be the Serre algebra of $A$. By [L1] the assignment $E_i\mapsto e_i$, $F_i\mapsto f_i$, $H_i\mapsto h_i$ extends to an isomorphism $\rho_0:\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$, and by [L1] and step 2.1 the assignment $E_i\mapsto x_i$, $F_i\mapsto y_i$, $H_i\mapsto z_i$ extends to a homomorphism $\rho_1:\mathfrak g(A)\to\mathfrak g$. The image of $\rho_1$ contains $x_i,y_i,z_i$, which generate $\mathfrak g$, so $\rho_1$ is surjective; since $\dim\mathfrak g(A)=\dim\mathfrak g$ by [L1], it is an isomorphism. Let $\mathfrak g(A)_{\mathbb R}$ be the real span of all iterated brackets of $E_i,F_i,H_i$ in $\mathfrak g(A)$; it is a real form of $\mathfrak g(A)$, because these iterated brackets span $\mathfrak g(A)$ over $\mathbb C$. Now $\rho_0(\mathfrak g(A)_{\mathbb R})$ is the real span of the iterated brackets of $e_i,f_i,h_i$, which is $\mathfrak g_0$ by step 1.1; and $\rho_1(\mathfrak g(A)_{\mathbb R})$ is the real span of the iterated brackets of $x_i,y_i,z_i$, a real subalgebra of $\mathfrak g_1$ whose complexification is the complex span of the same brackets, namely $\mathfrak g$ by step 2.1, so that it equals $\mathfrak g_1$. Hence the restrictions of $\rho_0$ and $\rho_1$ to $\mathfrak g(A)_{\mathbb R}$ are isomorphisms of real Lie algebras onto $\mathfrak g_0$ and $\mathfrak g_1$, and $\mathfrak g_0\cong\mathfrak g_1$ by composition. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 By step 1.1 a split real form of $\mathfrak g$ exists, and by steps 1.2-3.1 every split real form $\mathfrak g_1$ of $\mathfrak g$ is isomorphic to $\mathfrak g_0$. Hence every finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra has a split real form and any two split real forms are isomorphic as real Lie algebras, that is, the split real form is unique up to isomorphism. [A1, step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

The uniqueness comparison no longer assumes an integral Chevalley basis inside an arbitrary split real form. What the comparison actually uses is: the complexified split Cartan $\mathfrak h'=\mathfrak h_1^{\mathbb C}$ is a Cartan subalgebra of $\mathfrak g$ (step 1.2), so the conjugacy theorem of [L4] makes the based root system of $\mathfrak h'$ isomorphic to the given one with the same Cartan matrix (step 1.3); the split condition makes each root space of $\mathfrak g_1$ a real line (step 1.4, from the simultaneous diagonalization in [L3]); the Serre presentation theorem [L1] then supplies a root $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple over that base whose elements generate $\mathfrak g$ and satisfy exactly the relations of $\mathfrak g(A)$ (step 2.1); and the comparison is completed through the Serre algebra $\mathfrak g(A)$ and its real form $\mathfrak g(A)_{\mathbb R}$ (step 3.1). The Chevalley normalization of [L6] --- Knapp's Theorem 6.6 with Lemma 6.4, printed pp. 350-353; equivalently the Chevalley presentation of Etingof \S 39.2 --- is used for the existence of the split real form $\mathfrak g_0$ and its integral structure constants. The earlier draft additionally claimed that $\mathfrak g_1$ carries an integral Chevalley-type basis; that claim is needed nowhere and is not asserted here.
