## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and supplied projective-resolution data for group
homology. For every abelian group $A$, there is a natural isomorphism
$M(A)\cong\bigwedge^2 A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The stated choice and resolution hypotheses and a free presentation
$A=F/R$. Choice supplies a free presentation if one has not been supplied,
and implies the dependent-choice hypothesis of
[[thm-hopf-formula-for-the-schur-multiplier]]. Since $A$ is abelian,
$[F,F]\le R$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $F/[F,R]$, the subgroup $R/[F,R]$ is central. Since $[F,F]\le R$, this quotient has nilpotency class at most two. Thus its commutator is alternating and bilinear. Changing either lift by an element of $R$ does not change its commutator, so the rule $\bar x\wedge\bar y\mapsto[x,y][F,R]$ defines a surjective homomorphism $\Phi:\bigwedge^2 A\to [F,F]/[F,R]$. [given, algebra]

1.2 To check that there are no further relations, write $X=F_{\rm ab}$. The class-two quotient $F/[F,[F,F]]$ is the free class-two group on the chosen free basis. Its central commutator subgroup is $\bigwedge^2 X$: ordering the basis, every commutator word reduces using $[xy,z]=[x,z][y,z]$ and $[x,yz]=[x,y][x,z]$ to a product of basic commutators $[x_i,x_j]$ with $i<j$. Their independence follows by mapping the basis into the explicit class-two group $(X\oplus\bigwedge^2X,\cdot)$ with $(u,a)\cdot(v,b)=(u+v,a+b+\sum_{i>j}u_i v_j(e_i\wedge e_j))$; each word has finite support, so the displayed sum is finite. [given, algebra]

2.1 The image of $[F,R]$ in $\bigwedge^2X$ is generated by $x\wedge r$ with $x\in X$ and $r$ in the image $K$ of $R$ in $X$. The alternating universal property identifies $(\bigwedge^2X)/(X\wedge K)$ with $\bigwedge^2(X/K)=\bigwedge^2A$. Hence $\Phi$ is injective as well as surjective. Since $[F,F]\le R$, [[thm-hopf-formula-for-the-schur-multiplier]] identifies $M(A)=(R\cap[F,F])/[F,R]=[F,F]/[F,R]$ under the stated hypotheses. Presentation maps preserve commutators and exterior products, and the canonical Hopf identification is natural, so the resulting isomorphism is natural in $A$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
