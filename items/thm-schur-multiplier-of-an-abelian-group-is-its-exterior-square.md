## Statement

For every abelian group $A$, there is a natural isomorphism
$M(A)\cong\bigwedge^2 A$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Choose a free presentation $A=F/R$; since $A$ is abelian, $[F,F]\le R$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The rule $\bar x\wedge\bar y\mapsto[x,y]\,[F,R]$ is independent of the chosen lifts: changing a lift by an element of $R$ changes the commutator by an element of $[F,R]$.  It is alternating and bilinear modulo $[F,R]$, so the universal property gives a homomorphism $\bigwedge^2A\to [F,F]/[F,R]$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Conversely, the commutator quotient $[F,F]/[F,R]$ is generated by the classes of $[x,y]$, subject exactly to the alternating bilinear commutator relations; sending such a class to $\bar x\wedge\bar y$ is therefore a well-defined inverse.  Hopf's formula gives $M(A)=[F,F]/[F,R]\cong\bigwedge^2A$, and the construction is natural in $A$. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
