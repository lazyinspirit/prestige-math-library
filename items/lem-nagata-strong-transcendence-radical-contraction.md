## Statement

Let $D=C[x,t]$ be a reduced commutative ring with $C\subseteq D$. Suppose $x$ is strongly transcendental over $C$ and $t$ is integral over $C[x]$. For any ideal $I\subseteq C$,
$$tx\in\sqrt{ID}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad t\in\sqrt{ID}.$$
Equivalently, under the premise the localization of $D$ at the multiplicative subset $U=\{t^m+z:m\geq0,\ z\in ID\}$ is the zero ring. No Noetherianity or choice hypothesis is required. Zero rings and zero or unit ideals are included.

## Statement references

[Alonso–Coquand–Lombardi, Proposition2.17, pp.8–9](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00597)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The reduced ring, subring, strongly transcendental element, integral generator, ideal and radical premise in the Statement.

[F1] For an integral inclusion $A\subseteq B$, radical membership contracts: $\sqrt{JB}\cap A=\sqrt J$. Every element of $\sqrt{JB}$ satisfies a monic equation with nonleading coefficients in $J$. [[lem-nagata-integral-radical-contraction-by-determinants]]

[F2] Strong transcendence persists under localization and under integral scalar extension inside a reduced algebra. [[lem-nagata-strong-transcendence-survives-integral-scalars]]

[F3] For a monic factor of a monic polynomial over any ring, its nonleading coefficients satisfy monic equations over the ideal generated by the nonleading dividend coefficients in the subring generated by those dividend coefficients. [[lem-nagata-monic-factor-coefficients-are-integral-over-coefficient-ideal]]

[F4] Over a reduced ring, the explicit subresultants $S_j$ of monic $P$ and arbitrary $Q$ satisfy: if the previous principal coefficients vanish and the current one is invertible, the normalized $S_j$ is monic, divides both polynomials, and belongs to their ideal. If all principal coefficients below $\deg P$ vanish, $P$ divides $Q$. [[lem-nagata-subresultant-gcd-over-reduced-rings]]

[F5] If $b$ is integral over the image of $C[x]$ and $s(x)b$ belongs to that image, where $a$ is the leading coefficient of a chosen polynomial $s(X)\in C[X]$, then $a^m b-q(x)$ is integral over $C$ for some $m\geq0$ and $q(X)\in C[X]$. No regularity or injectivity is required. [[lem-zmt-one-variable-integral-correction]]

[F6] Monic division has unique remainder, radicals are ideals, and a localization is zero precisely when its denominator set contains zero. In a reduced ring an element whose principal localization is zero is zero. [[thm-monic-polynomial-division]] [[lem-radical-is-an-ideal]] [[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]]

## Proof

1.1 Since $t$ is integral, $C[x]\subseteq D$ is integral. Apply [F1] to $tx\in\sqrt{ID}$, with base ideal $IC[x]$, to obtain a monic relation $Q(x,t)=0$ of the form $Q(X,T)=X^nT^n+\sum_{j=1}^n\mu_j(X)X^{n-j}T^{n-j}$, where $n\geq1$ and $\mu_j(X)\in IC[X]$. Choose also a monic $P(X,T)\in C[X,T]$ of positive $T$-degree $d$ with $P(x,t)=0$. Put $R=D_U$. The set $U$ is multiplicative because products of two expressions $t^m+z$ have the same form. By [F2], $R$ is reduced and $x$ remains strongly transcendental over $C$ there. We will prove $R=0$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Record the following finite obstruction, valid in any $D$-algebra $E$ in which all elements of the original $U$ are units. Suppose a subring $L\subseteq E$ contains the image of $C$, and a monic positive-$T$-degree polynomial $G(X,T)\in L[X,T]$ divides this $Q(X,T)$ formally and satisfies $G(x,t)=0$. Substitute $T=X^N$, where $N$ exceeds the degrees of the finitely many coefficient polynomials of $G$ and of the $\mu_j$. Both substituted polynomials are monic in $X$. Their coefficient blocks are disjoint when necessary to read off the coefficients of $G$, and every nonleading coefficient of the substituted $Q$ belongs to the image of $I$. By [F3], each coefficient of each nonleading $T$-coefficient of $G$ satisfies a monic equation whose nonleading coefficients belong to the image of $I$ in the ORIGINAL image of $C$: the subring generated by the coefficients of substituted $Q$ lies in that image. Let $B\subseteq E$ be the algebra generated over the image $D_0$ of $D$ by these finitely many coefficients. It is integral over $D_0$. Those coefficients lie in $\sqrt{IB}$ by their monic equations, hence the equation $G(x,t)=0$ gives $t\in\sqrt{IB}$. Contract by [F1] along $D_0\subseteq B$ to get $t\in\sqrt{ID_0}$. Thus some original expression $t^m+z\in U$ maps to zero in $E$, while it is a unit there. Consequently $E=0$. This contraction is why coefficients introduced after localization do not enlarge the original denominator set without justification. [F1, F3, F6, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Compute the subresultants in [F4] for $P(X,T),Q(X,T)$ over $C[X]$, choosing a finite bound $\delta\geq d$ for the $T$-degree of $Q$. Write their principal coefficients as $s_j(X)$. Inductively suppose $s_i(x)=0$ in $R$ for $i<j<d$ and put $E=R[1/s_j(x)]$. If $j=0$, the element $s_0(x)=S_0(x)$ belongs to the ideal of $P(x,T),Q(x,T)$; evaluation at $t$ makes it zero. Since it is invertible in $E$, $E=0$. For $j>0$, [F4] gives the monic polynomial $G(T)=s_j(x)^{-1}S_j(x,T)$ over $E$, dividing $P(x,T)$ and $Q(x,T)$ and satisfying $G(t)=0$. Each nonleading coefficient $b_i$ of $G$ is integral over the image of $C[x]$, by [F3] applied to its divisibility into the monic $P$. Moreover $s_j(x)b_i=c_i(x)$ for a polynomial $c_i(X)\in C[X]$ from the subresultant. [F3, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $a$ be the leading coefficient of a nonzero polynomial expression $s(X)\in C[X]$ that evaluates to the unit $s_j(x)$ in $E$. Start with $s=s_j$, unless it is already the zero polynomial, in which case $E=0$. Apply [F5] to the finitely many $b_i$ and this $s$, obtaining $a^{m_i}b_i-q_i(x)=z_i$ with $z_i$ integral over the image of $C$ in $E$. In $E_a$, the coefficients $b_i$ consequently belong to $L[x]$, where $L$ is the image of $C[z_1,\ldots,z_j][1/a]$ in $E_a$. By [F2], $x$ is strongly transcendental over $L$. Choose polynomial expressions over $L$ for the $b_i$, giving a formal monic $G(X,T)\in L[X,T]$. It divides $Q$ formally: divide $Q$ by $G$ over $L[X]$ using [F6]; the remainder evaluates to zero at $X=x$ by step 2.2, and strong transcendence with multiplier $1$ makes every scalar coefficient of that remainder zero. Also $G(x,t)=0$. Step 2.1 now applies with this $E_a$, so $E_a=0$. Since $E$ is reduced, [F6] gives $a=0$ in $E$. [F2, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

4.1 Delete this leading term from the expression $s(X)$. The resulting polynomial has smaller degree and still evaluates to the same unit $s_j(x)$ in $E$, because $a=0$ there. The memberships $s(x)b_i=c_i(x)$ are unchanged, so step 3.1 applies again with this shorter expression. After finitely many deletions every coefficient has vanished in $E$; thus $s_j(x)=0$ in $E$, which is a unit, and $E=0$. It follows by reducedness and [F6] that $s_j(x)=0$ in $R$. Together with the $j=0$ case in step 2.2 this completes induction through all $j<d$. No choice of a first nonzero element of an infinite family is involved: the subresultant list and each degree-deletion list are finite. [F6, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Apply [F4] with index $d$ over the reduced ring $R$: all preceding principal coefficients vanish by step 4.1, so $P(x,T)$ divides $Q(x,T)$ in $R[T]$. Monic division over the image of $C[X]$, followed by strong transcendence, shows that this divisibility holds formally over the image of $C$ in $R$, exactly as in step 3.1. Apply step 2.1 with $E=R$ and $G=P$ to conclude $R=0$. Finally [F6] says $0\in U$, so $t^m+z=0$ for some $m\geq0$ and $z\in ID$. If $m>0$, this is $t\in\sqrt{ID}$. If $m=0$, it says $ID=D$, and the same conclusion holds. Conversely $t\in\sqrt{ID}$ immediately puts zero in $U$, proving the stated equivalence. If $D$ is zero or $I$ unit this argument is already trivial, and zero relations or zero localizations at intermediate stages simply terminate the corresponding finite argument. All stated cases are therefore covered. [F2, F4, F6, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
