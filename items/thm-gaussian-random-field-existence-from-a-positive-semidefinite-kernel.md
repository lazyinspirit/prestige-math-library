## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, $d\ge1$, be an
index set, let $m:T\to\mathbb R$, and let $K:T\times T\to\mathbb R$ be a
symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel, meaning that every finite real
quadratic form $\sum_{i,j}a_i a_jK(t_i,t_j)$ is nonnegative. There is a unique
probability measure $\mu$ on the cylinder sigma-algebra $\mathcal C_T$ of
$\mathbb R^T$ whose nonempty finite-coordinate marginal on
$F=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\}\subseteq T$ is
$$N_n\big((m(t_i))_{i=1}^n,[K(t_i,t_j)]_{i,j=1}^n\big).$$
The empty-coordinate marginal is the unique law on the singleton empty
product. Under $\mu$, the coordinate maps form a Gaussian field with mean
function $m$ and covariance kernel $K$. No sample-path continuity or joint
measurability is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a set $T\subseteq\mathbb R^d$, a function $m:T\to\mathbb R$,
and a real symmetric positive-semidefinite kernel $K$ on $T$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice asserts that every family of nonempty sets has a
choice function. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]]: under AC, every finite real symmetric
positive-semidefinite matrix and mean vector define a possibly singular
multivariate normal law with the stated mean and covariance.

[F2] [[lem-characteristic-function-of-a-multivariate-normal-law]]: under AC,
$N_n(a,\Sigma)$ has characteristic function
$u\mapsto\exp(iu^Ta-u^T\Sigma u/2)$, and this transform uniquely determines
the law, including when $\Sigma$ is singular.

[F3] [[lem-second-countable-lch-spaces-are-standard-borel]]: under AC, every
second-countable locally compact Hausdorff space is Polish and hence standard
Borel.

[F4] [[def-second-countable-space]], [[def-locally-compact-space]],
[[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]],
[[thm-rationals-countable]], [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]], and
[[thm-product-of-countable]]: the usual real line is second-countable and
locally compact Hausdorff. Rational-endpoint intervals give a countable base;
closed bounded intervals are compact.

[F5] [[def-consistent-family-of-finite-dimensional-distributions]]: finite
coordinate laws form a consistent family when every restriction pushforward
agrees with the law on the smaller coordinate set.

[F6] [[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]]: $\mathcal C_T$ is
generated by all finite-coordinate cylinders, and each coordinate projection
$\mathbb R^T\to\mathbb R$ is measurable for this sigma-algebra.

[F7] [[thm-kolmogorov-extension-for-standard-borel-coordinate-spaces]]:
under AC, any consistent family of finite-dimensional laws on standard-Borel
coordinate spaces extends uniquely to a probability measure on the cylinder
sigma-algebra.

[F8] [[def-gaussian-process]]: a real process is Gaussian when every finite
coordinate vector has a possibly singular multivariate normal law; its
probability-law construction follows the library's AC convention.

[F9] [[def-random-field-stationarity-covariance-and-variogram]]: a real field
is a family of real random variables on one common probability space, with
each coordinate measurable there.

**Axiom use:** AC selects the finite coordinate enumerations and is used by the finite Gaussian-law supplier [F1] in step 1.1 and by Kolmogorov extension [F7] in step 3.1. Dependent choice is not used.

## Proof

**Given:** AC, $T$, $m$, and $K$ as in the statement.

1.1 For each nonempty finite $F\subseteq T$, use AC to choose an enumeration $e_F:\{1,\ldots,n_F\}\to F$. Put $t_i=e_F(i)$, $m_F=(m(t_i))_{i=1}^{n_F}$, and $K_F=[K(t_i,t_j)]_{i,j=1}^{n_F}$. Let $r_F:\mathbb R^{n_F}\to\mathbb R^F$ relabel coordinates by $r_F(x)(e_F(i))=x_i$. The matrix $K_F$ is symmetric positive semidefinite by the hypothesis on $K$, so [F1] supplies $N_{n_F}(m_F,K_F)$; define the probability measure on the actual coordinate space by $\mu_F:=(r_F)_\#N_{n_F}(m_F,K_F)$. If $F=\varnothing$, use the unique probability law on the singleton empty product. Changing the enumeration permutes $m_F$ and both indices of $K_F$ by the same permutation. The characteristic-function formula in [F2] shows that the corresponding permutation of $\mathbb R^{n_F}$ carries one Gaussian law to the other; after relabelling, the resulting measure on $\mathbb R^F$ is unchanged. Thus $\mu_F$ is independent of the chosen enumeration. [A1, F1, F2, given, construct]

1.2 The intervals $(p,q)$ with rational endpoints form a countable family by the countability of $\mathbb Q$ and [F4]. They form a base: if $U$ is open and $x\in U$, choose $r>0$ with $(x-r,x+r)\subseteq U$; rational density supplies $p,q\in\mathbb Q$ with $x-r<p<x<q<x+r$, so $x\in(p,q)\subseteq U$. Thus $\mathbb R$ is second-countable by [F4]. If $x\ne y$, the open balls about $x$ and $y$ of radius $|x-y|/3$ are disjoint: a point in both would give $|x-y|<2|x-y|/3$ by the triangle inequality. Hence the metric topology is Hausdorff. It is locally compact since each $x$ has the compact interval $[x-1,x+1]$ as a neighborhood by [F4]. Therefore [F3] makes $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$ standard Borel. [F3, F4, given, choose]

2.1 Let $F\subseteq H$ be finite and nonempty, and use the enumerations fixed in step 1.1. Write $\pi_{H,F}:\mathbb R^H\to\mathbb R^F$ for restriction. For $u\in\mathbb R^F$, define $\widetilde u\in\mathbb R^{n_H}$ by $\widetilde u_j=u(e_H(j))$ when $e_H(j)\in F$, and $\widetilde u_j=0$ otherwise. Under the coordinate relabelling $r_H$, the characteristic function of $(\pi_{H,F})_\#\mu_H$ at $u$ is the characteristic function of $N_{n_H}(m_H,K_H)$ at $\widetilde u$. By [F2] this is $$\exp\!\left(i\sum_{t\in F}u(t)m(t)-\frac12\sum_{s,t\in F}u(s)u(t)K(s,t)\right).$$ This is also the characteristic function of $\mu_F=(r_F)_\#N_{n_F}(m_F,K_F)$ at $u$, by [F2]. Identifying $\mathbb R^F$ with $\mathbb R^{n_F}$ through $r_F$, uniqueness of characteristic functions in [F2] gives $(\pi_{H,F})_\#\mu_H=\mu_F$. Empty sets give the unique singleton marginal. Hence the family is consistent in the sense of [F5]. [F2, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The consistent family from step 2.1 now satisfies the standard-Borel hypotheses of [F7]. Kolmogorov extension gives a unique probability measure $\mu$ on $\mathcal C_T$ with the specified finite-coordinate marginals. [A1, F3, F5, F7, step 1.2, construct]

4.1 On $(\mathbb R^T,\mathcal C_T,\mu)$ define $X(t)(\omega):=\omega(t)$. Each coordinate is measurable by [F6], and for every finite set $F$ its joint law is $\mu_F$ by [F7]. The means and covariances are therefore $m(t)$ and $K(s,t)$ by [F1]. For any finite list $t_1,\ldots,t_n$, possibly with repeated sites, and coefficients $a_1,\ldots,a_n$, collect the distinct sites in $F=\{t_1,\ldots,t_n\}$ and put $b_t:=\sum_{j:t_j=t}a_j$. Then $\sum_j a_jX(t_j)=\sum_{t\in F}b_tX(t)$ is a linear projection of the $F$-coordinate Gaussian vector, so it has a normal law by [F1]. This is the Gaussian-process condition [F8], and the coordinates form a real field by [F9]. The extension supplies only the cylinder sigma-algebra; no continuity or joint-measurability conclusion was used. [F1, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 3.1] ∎
