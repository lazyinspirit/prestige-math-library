## Example

Assume AC as in the Gaussian and Brownian interfaces.

Let $G\sim N(0,h)$ with $h>0$, fix $c>0$, and repeatedly draw independent
copies until the first draw $R$ with $|R|\le c$. Then
$$\mathbb P\{R\in A\}=\frac{\mathbb P\{G\in A,\ |G|\le c\}} {\mathbb P\{|G|\le c\}}$$
for every Borel set $A$, and $\operatorname{Var}(R)<h$. Thus rejecting and
redrawing a Brownian increment after inspecting its endpoint does not preserve
the increment law. The dyadic tree instead retains the sampled parent and
splits it using [[alg-dyadic-brownian-bridge-increment-tree]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $h>0$, $c>0$, and an independent sequence $G_1,G_2,\ldots$ of $N(0,h)$ variables.

[F1] A centered $N(0,h)$ variable has a strictly positive continuous density, mean zero and variance $h$. [[def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws]]

[F2] When a sampled Brownian interval is refined, its parent is retained and the children are generated by the midpoint bridge split. [[alg-dyadic-brownian-bridge-increment-tree]]

[A1] AC is inherited through the Gaussian and bridge-sampling interfaces; no further selection is made. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Put $I=[-c,c]$ and $p=\mathbb P\{G_1\in I\}>0$. For each Borel $A$, the event that the first accepted draw lies in $A$ is the disjoint union over $k\ge0$ of the events that the first $k$ draws lie outside $I$ and draw $k+1$ lies in $A\cap I$. Independence therefore gives $\mathbb P\{R\in A\}=\sum_{k=0}^\infty(1-p)^k \mathbb P\{G_1\in A\cap I\}=\frac{\mathbb P\{G_1\in A\cap I\}}p.$ This is exactly the stated truncated-normal law. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The law is symmetric, so $\mathbb ER=0$. Both $I$ and its complement have positive probability. On $I$, $G_1^2\le c^2$ with strict inequality on a positive-probability subset; on $I^c$, $G_1^2>c^2$. Hence $\mathbb E[G_1^2\mid G_1\in I]<c^2< \mathbb E[G_1^2\mid G_1\notin I].$ The unconditional variance $h=\mathbb EG_1^2$ is the strict weighted average of these two conditional second moments. Therefore $\operatorname{Var}(R)=\mathbb E[G_1^2\mid G_1\in I]<h$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The resampled increment is thus not $N(0,h)$, whereas bridge refinement in [F2] preserves the already sampled parent increment by exact sum consistency. This example concerns endpoint-dependent rejection; it does not contradict the predictable-grid error theorem, whose step lengths are fixed from current information. [A1, F2, step 2.1, given] ∎
