## Statement

For any weight $\lambda$ of a finite generalized Cartan matrix over $\mathbb C$, the Verma module $M_A(\lambda)$ has a unique maximal proper submodule $N_\lambda$. The quotient $L_A(\lambda)=M_A(\lambda)/N_\lambda$ is nonzero, simple and highest weight $\lambda$. Every simple module generated by a nonzero highest vector of weight $\lambda$ is isomorphic to this quotient.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Verma module and its highest vector $v_\lambda=1\otimes1$.

[F1] Universality, $\mathcal O$ membership, and the one-dimensional top space follow from [[thm-universal-property-and-pbw-character-of-kac-moody-verma-modules]].

[F2] Submodules and quotients of $\mathcal O$ modules are weight modules, with their corresponding weight intersections and quotients ([[def-kac-moody-category-o]]).

## Proof

1.1 A submodule $N$ is a sum of its weight intersections by F2. Explicitly, for a finite weight sum $x=\sum_{j=1}^r x_{\mu_j}\in N$, choose $h\in\mathfrak h$ with all $\mu_j(h)$ distinct. Such an $h$ exists: in a finite basis $h_1,\ldots,h_d$, the curve $h(t)=\sum_{a=1}^dt^{a-1}h_a$ makes each nonzero difference $(\mu_j-\mu_l)(h(t))$ a nonzero polynomial, and finitely many such polynomials exclude only finitely many complex $t$. Applying $\prod_{l\ne j}(h-\mu_l(h))/(\mu_j(h)-\mu_l(h))$ to $x$ gives $x_{\mu_j}\in N$. If $N$ has nonzero top space, it contains $v_\lambda$ because that space is one dimensional; since $v_\lambda$ generates $M_A(\lambda)$, then $N=M_A(\lambda)$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 Form the algebraic sum $N_\lambda$ of all proper submodules, meaning all finite sums of their elements. This is a submodule. Each summand has zero top component by 1.1, so every such finite sum has zero top component. Thus $N_\lambda$ is proper and contains every proper submodule. A strictly larger submodule must be the whole module, proving both maximality and uniqueness. Its quotient is nonzero since the top vector survives; any proper nonzero submodule of the quotient would lift to a submodule strictly between $N_\lambda$ and the whole module, which is impossible. The surviving top vector generates the quotient, so it is simple highest weight $\lambda$. [step 1.1, given]

3.1 If a simple module $V$ has a specified nonzero highest vector of weight $\lambda$, F1 supplies a map $M_A(\lambda)\to V$ whose image is the cyclic submodule generated by that vector, hence all of $V$. Its kernel is maximal proper, because an intermediate submodule would give a nonzero proper submodule of $V$. By 2.1 this kernel is $N_\lambda$, giving the stated isomorphism. If $M_A(\lambda)$ itself is simple, the sum in 2.1 is just zero and the same construction applies. Finite interpolation includes the single-weight case with empty product $1$. No Zorn argument, arbitrary family of choices or finite-dimensionality of the whole module is used. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
