## Statement

Let $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$ be commutative rings, each a subring of the next
([[def-subring]]), with $A$ Noetherian. Suppose $C$ is of finite type over $A$
and module-finite over $B$
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]). Then $B$ is of finite type
over $A$.

No Noetherian hypothesis is placed on $B$; that is what the argument has to do
without, and it is why a Noetherian subring of $B$ is manufactured on the way.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Commutative rings $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$, each a subring of the next, with $A$ Noetherian, $C$ of finite type over $A$ and $C$ module-finite over $B$.

[L1] $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ is the smallest subring of $A$ containing the image of $R$ and $a_1,\ldots,a_n$; an algebra is **of finite type** over $R$ when it equals $R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some finite list, and **module-finite** over $R$ when it is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] For a ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_i\in R$ and $s_i\in S$ ([[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

[L3] With $A\subseteq B\subseteq C$ as above, $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, a $B$-module generating list $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ of $C$ with $y_1=1_C$ and coefficients $z_{ij},z_{ijk}\in B$ satisfying $t_i=\sum_jz_{ij}y_j$ and $y_iy_j=\sum_kz_{ijk}y_k$: the $A$-subalgebra $A'\subseteq B$ generated by those coefficients satisfies $A\subseteq A'\subseteq B$, is of finite type over $A$, and is a Noetherian ring ([[lem-artin-tate-coefficient-subalgebra]]).

[L4] In that same setup $C$ is module-finite over $A'$, and $B$ is module-finite over $A'$ ([[lem-artin-tate-intermediate-ring-is-module-finite-over-the-coefficient-subalgebra]]).

[L5] A subring contains the identity of the ambient ring and is closed under sums, additive inverses and products ([[def-subring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $r\in\mathbb N$ and $t_1,\ldots,t_r\in C$ with $C=A[t_1,\ldots,t_r]$, available because $C$ is of finite type over $A$. Fix a finite list generating $C$ as a $B$-module and adjoin $1_C$ to it; the result $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ is still finite and still generates, and it may be indexed so that $n\ge1$ and $y_1=1_C$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 Each $t_i$ and each product $y_iy_j$ lies in $C$, so each is a $B$-linear combination of $y_1,\ldots,y_n$; fix coefficients $z_{ij},z_{ijk}\in B$ with $t_i=\sum_{j}z_{ij}y_j$ and $y_iy_j=\sum_{k}z_{ijk}y_k$, and let $A'$ be the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ they generate. Then $A\subseteq A'\subseteq B$, $A'$ is of finite type over $A$, and $A'$ is a Noetherian ring. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 In this setup $B$ is module-finite over $A'$: fix $s\in\mathbb N$ and $w_1,\ldots,w_s\in B$ generating $B$ as an $A'$-module, so that every element of $B$ is $\sum_{l=1}^{s}a_lw_l$ with $a_l\in A'$. [L2, L4, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $D$ be the smallest subring of $B$ containing $A$, all the coefficients $z_{ij}$ and $z_{ijk}$, and $w_1,\ldots,w_s$; that is, $D$ is the $A$-subalgebra of $B$ generated by that finite list. Then $D$ contains $A$ and the coefficients, hence contains the smallest subring of $B$ containing them, which is $A'$; and $D$ contains each $w_l$ and is closed under products and sums, so $D$ contains every $\sum_la_lw_l$ with $a_l\in A'$, that is all of $B$. Since also $D\subseteq B$, we get $B=D$, so $B$ is generated as an $A$-algebra by a finite list and is of finite type over $A$. [L1, L2, L5, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **The Noetherian ring in play is $A'$, never $B$.** The hypothesis is that $A$ is Noetherian, and $A'$ inherits that as a finite-type algebra over $A$; the conclusion about $B$ is then read off from $B$ being a finite $A'$-module.

- **The generating list of $B$ is explicit.** It consists of the structure coefficients $z_{ij}$ and $z_{ijk}$ together with the $A'$-module generators $w_l$ of $B$, all of which depend on the choices made in steps 1.1 to 3.1. No minimality is claimed.
