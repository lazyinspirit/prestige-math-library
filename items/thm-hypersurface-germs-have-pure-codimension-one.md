## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $n\ge1$, let
$p\in\mathbb C^n$ and let $X$ be a nonempty reduced complex-analytic
hypersurface germ at $p$, with reduced defining germ $f$. Then

1. $\dim_pX=n-1$, where the local dimension is the Krull dimension of the
   local ring $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f)$
   ([[def-local-dimension-hypersurface-germ]]);
2. every irreducible component of $X$
   ([[def-irreducible-hypersurface-germ]]) has local dimension $n-1$ and has a
   defining prime ideal of height one: writing the components as $Z(q_i)$
   with $q_i$ irreducible, the ideals $(q_i)$ are prime and
   $\operatorname{ht}\bigl((q_i)\bigr)=1$.

The statement concerns the principal ideal generated by a single reduced
equation; it asserts nothing about arbitrary analytic ideals or set germs not
cut out by one equation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a reduced nonzero nonunit germ $f$ at $p\in\mathbb C^n$, and its zero germ $X=Z(f)$ with irreducible factorisation $f=u\,q_1\cdots q_r$.

[F1] The local dimension is $\dim_pX=\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f)$ for the reduced equation $f$, and it equals the Krull dimension of that quotient ([[def-local-dimension-hypersurface-germ]], [[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]]).

[F2] The factorisation $f=u\,q_1\cdots q_r$ exists with $u$ a unit and $q_1,\dots,q_r$ pairwise nonassociate irreducibles; $X=\bigcup_iZ(q_i)$, each $Z(q_i)$ is an irreducible hypersurface germ, and these are exactly the irreducible components of $X$, uniquely determined with their number $r\ge1$ ([[thm-local-irreducible-decomposition-hypersurface-germ]], [[def-irreducible-hypersurface-germ]]).

[F3] For an ideal $I$ with $R/I\ne0$, $\dim(R/I)=\sup\{n:\mathfrak p_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq\mathfrak p_n\ \text{is a strict chain of primes all containing }I\}$ ([[cor-dimension-of-a-quotient-as-chains-above-an-ideal]]).

[F4] Each $q_i$ is a nonzero germ, so some invertible complex-linear map $T_i$ makes $T_i(q_i)$ regular in the last variable of some order $d_i\ge1$; then $T_i(q_i)=v_iW_i$ with $v_i$ a unit and $W_i$ a Weierstrass polynomial of degree $d_i$, and $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,0}/(W_i)$ is a finitely generated $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}$-module generated by $1,z_n,\dots,z_n^{d_i-1}$, with $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^0,0}=\mathbb C$ when $n=1$ ([[lem-generic-linear-coordinate-makes-a-holomorphic-germ-regular]], [[thm-weierstrass-preparation-theorem]], [[lem-weierstrass-quotient-is-a-finite-module]]).

[F5] The induced map $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}\to\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,0}/(W_i)$ is injective: if $h\in\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}$ lies in $(W_i)$, say $h=W_ig$, then dividing $h$ by $W_i$ both as $h=0\cdot W_i+h$ and as $h=W_ig+0$ and invoking uniqueness of the Weierstrass remainder forces $h=0$ ([[thm-weierstrass-division-theorem]]).

[F6] Assume the Axiom of Choice. For an injective integral extension $A\subseteq B$ of nonzero commutative rings one has $\dim A=\dim B$; a finite module extension is integral, so a ring that is a finitely generated module over a subring is integral over it ([[cor-dimension-preserved-by-integral-extensions]], [[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

[F7] Assume the Axiom of Choice. $\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^m,0}=m$ for every $m\ge0$ ([[lem-dimension-of-holomorphic-germ-ring]]).

[F8] Assume the Axiom of Choice. In a Noetherian commutative ring, if $x$ is a nonzerodivisor and $\mathfrak p$ is a prime ideal minimal over $(x)$, then $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)=1$ ([[cor-minimal-prime-over-a-nonzerodivisor-has-height-one]]). The germ ring is Noetherian ([[thm-holomorphic-germ-ring-is-noetherian]]) and a domain in which a nonzero irreducible germ is prime ([[thm-holomorphic-germ-ring-is-a-ufd]], [[lem-irreducible-holomorphic-germ-is-prime]]).



**Proof technique:** direct — prove each branch quotient has dimension $n-1$ by a finite integral extension, compare prime chains for the union, and apply the principal ideal height theorem.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1] and [F2] the local dimension of $X$ is $\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(f)$, that of the component $Z(q_i)$ is $\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(q_i)$, and $X=\bigcup_iZ(q_i)$ with the $Z(q_i)$ the irreducible components; note $r\ge1$. [given, F1, F2]

2.1 For every $i$ one has $\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^n,p}/(q_i)=n-1$. By [F4] choose the linear coordinates $T_i$, the degree $d_i\ge1$ and the Weierstrass polynomial $W_i$; the ring automorphism induced by the invertible linear change carries $(q_i)$ to $(T_iq_i)=(W_i)$, so $\dim\mathcal O/(q_i)=\dim\mathcal O/(W_i)$. The residue classes $1,\dots,z_n^{d_i-1}$ generate $\mathcal O/(W_i)$ as an $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}$-module by [F4], and the structure map is injective by [F5]; hence $\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}\subseteq\mathcal O/(W_i)$ is an injective integral extension of nonzero rings by [F6], so $\dim\mathcal O/(W_i)=\dim\mathcal O_{\mathbb C^{n-1},0}=n-1$ by [F6] and [F7]. [step 1.1, F4, F5, F6, F7]

2.2 For every $i$ the ideal $(q_i)$ is a prime ideal of height one. It is prime because $q_i$ is irreducible and irreducible germs are prime by [F8]; it is trivially minimal over itself, its generator $q_i$ is a nonzero nonzerodivisor since $\mathcal O$ is a domain, and the ring is Noetherian by [F8]; therefore $\operatorname{ht}\bigl((q_i)\bigr)=1$ by the height-one corollary in [F8]. [step 1.1, F8]

3.1 The local dimension of $X$ is $n-1$. Every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ containing $(f)$ contains the product $q_1\cdots q_r$ of the irreducible factors up to the unit $u$, hence contains some $(q_j)$ by primality of the $q_j$ in [F8]; consequently, in a strict chain of primes all containing $(f)$, the smallest member already contains some $(q_j)$, so every member of the chain contains $(q_j)$ and the chain is a chain of primes containing $(q_j)$. Conversely every chain of primes containing $(q_j)$ contains $(f)\subseteq(q_j)$. By [F3] this gives $\dim\mathcal O/(f)=\max_i\dim\mathcal O/(q_i)=n-1$ by step 2.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, F3, F8]

4.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1, 2.2 and 3.1 establish all assertions: $\dim_pX=\dim\mathcal O/(f)=n-1$; each irreducible component $Z(q_i)$ has local dimension $\dim\mathcal O/(q_i)=n-1$ and defining prime $(q_i)$ of height one; and $r\ge1$ with the components uniquely determined. The Axiom of Choice is used exactly through the integral-extension dimension preservation [F6], the numerical dimension of the germ ring [F7] and the height-one corollary [F8], as declared in the Statement; no choice is used beyond these. [step 3.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, F1, F6, F7, F8] ∎
