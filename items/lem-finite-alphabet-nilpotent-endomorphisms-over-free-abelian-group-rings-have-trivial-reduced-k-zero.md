## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let $r\geq0$ be finite, put $R=\mathbb Z[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_r^{\pm1}]$, and let $I$ be a finite set. Let $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}(R,I)$ be the exact category of finitely generated projective $R$-modules $P$ with endomorphisms $\nu_i$, $i\in I$, for which there exists an integer $N\geq1$ such that every product of $N$ of the endomorphisms is zero. Morphisms intertwine all endomorphisms, and exact sequences are exact on underlying modules. The bound $N$ may depend on the object. Then the forgetful map $K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}(R,I))\to K_0(R)$ is an isomorphism, with inverse assigning all zero endomorphisms. Thus its kernel and the cokernel of the zero-endomorphism section both vanish. In particular, for one nilpotent endomorphism, the reduced nilpotent Grothendieck group $\widetilde K_0(\operatorname{Nil}(R))$ is zero.

Moreover, if $T$ is a unital $R$-algebra whose elements commute with the image of $R$, and $t_i\in T$, then the automorphism $1+\sum_{i\in I}\nu_i t_i$ of $P\otimes_R T$ has zero class in $K_1(T)$. For a projective module this class means extending the automorphism by the identity on a finite complementary projective module so that the total module is free.

Here $K_0$ of an exact category has generators its object classes and the relations from its short exact sequences. No higher nilpotent K-group assertion is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] Countable choice is assumed as in [[def-countable-choice]]. It is used to lift a countable free basis through a surjection. All modules in the bounded resolution arguments below are countable.

## Proof

**Given:** $r$, $R$, $I$, $T$ and $t_i$ as in the statement, under [A1]. Write $\mathcal N_{\mathcal M}(R,I)$ for the same nilpotent data on all finitely generated $R$-modules. We first establish the needed resolution bound locally.

1.1 Polynomial and Laurent rings here have enumerations by finite integer coefficient and exponent codes. A specified countable generating list enumerates its module; submodules inherit enumerations by listing an enumerated element when it lies in the submodule and zero otherwise. A countably free module is projective under [A1]: choose lifts of its countably many basis images through the given surjection. Summands are projective, and a countably generated projective is a summand of a countably free module by splitting its countable free surjection. For $H\subset\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb N}\mathbb Ze_n$, put $H_n=H\cap\bigoplus_{i<n}\mathbb Ze_i$. The new-coordinate map identifies $H_{n+1}/H_n$ with $d_n\mathbb Z$. If $d_n>0$, choose the vector of least natural-number code realizing that positive coordinate; it splits $H_{n+1}=H_n\oplus\mathbb Zv_n$. If $d_n=0$, add no vector. The added vectors form a basis of $H$, since each element has finite support. Thus each countably generated abelian group has a countably generated projective resolution of length at most one. [given, A1, construct, algebra]

1.2 Suppose every countably generated module over a particular enumerated ring $D$ has a countably generated projective resolution of length at most $d$. A countably generated $D[t]$-module $M$ is countably generated over $D$ by the elements $t^jm_i$ from its generators. The sequence $0\to D[t]\otimes_D M\xrightarrow{\delta}D[t]\otimes_D M\xrightarrow{\epsilon}M\to0$, with $\delta(t^j\otimes m)=t^{j+1}\otimes m-t^j\otimes tm$ and $\epsilon(t^j\otimes m)=t^jm$, is exact: the greatest coefficient proves injectivity and reduction to constants proves middle exactness. Induce a length-$d$ $D$-resolution for the two induced terms, lift $\delta$ to a chain map and take its cone. This gives a countably generated projective resolution of length at most $d+1$. Each lift is justified by step 1.1; a fixed finite number of variables requires only finitely many resolutions and lifting constructions. For a specified countable multiplicative set $S$, a module generated over $S^{-1}D$ by $m_i$ is generated over $D$ by $m_i/s$. Localize its bounded $D$-resolution; exactness and projective summand decompositions are preserved. Consequently every countably generated $R$-module has a countably generated projective resolution of length at most $r+1$. No assertion about uncountably generated modules is needed. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 The rings are noetherian. In a polynomial extension, the ideals of leading coefficients stabilize; finite polynomials realizing their generators reduce every greater degree, and the remaining bounded-degree coefficient submodule is finitely generated. Localized ideals are generated by denominators cleared from finite generators of their contractions. For a finite $R$-module, take finite free surjections onto successive finite kernels. Its $(r+1)$st syzygy is projective: pullbacks of two projective epimorphisms split over both sources and give Schanuel's isomorphism $H\oplus P'\cong H'\oplus P$; iteration against the bounded countable resolution from step 1.2 makes the last kernel a summand of a projective module. We also need the exact kernel bound. If $P\twoheadrightarrow M$ is projective and $M$ has resolution length $e>0$, compare it to the first map $Q_0\twoheadrightarrow M$ of that resolution, with kernel $L$ of length at most $e-1$. Then $K\oplus Q_0\cong L\oplus P$ for $K=\ker(P\to M)$. Resolve the right side, lift the inclusion of $Q_0$ through the degree-zero augmentation, and use the augmentation projection to split off $Q_0$ as a degree-zero projective subcomplex. The remaining complex resolves $K$ with length at most $e-1$. For $e=0$, split $P\twoheadrightarrow M$ directly. [step 1.1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

2.1 The category $\mathcal N_{\mathcal M}$ is closed under kernels. The category $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$ is closed under kernels of admissible epimorphisms between its objects, because the underlying surjection onto a projective splits; no arbitrary-kernel closure is asserted. Extensions of objects with nilpotence bounds $N,N'$ have bound $N+N'$. Every object $(M,(\nu_i))$ of $\mathcal N_{\mathcal M}$ has a finite $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$-resolution. Choose a finite free surjection $F\twoheadrightarrow M$ and a bound $N$. On $\bigoplus_{|w|<N}F$, indexed by words in $I$, let $\sigma_i$ prepend $i$, or give zero at length $N$. Send its $w$-summand to $M$ by $\nu_w$ composed with the surjection. The map is equivariant and surjective because of the empty word. Its kernel is finite by step 1.3, has the same nil bound, and has smaller underlying projective resolution length until projective. Repeating this construction terminates by steps 1.2–1.3. [step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

2.2 The Euler comparison in this resolution argument can be made fully nil-equivariant. At a common bound $N$ use $\Lambda_N=R\langle z_i:i\in I\rangle/(\text{all words of length }N)$. Objects with bound $N$ are $\Lambda_N$-modules, and the word cover in step 2.1 is $\Lambda_N\otimes_R F$, finite free over $\Lambda_N$ and over $R$. Repeated covers give a partial $\Lambda_N$-free resolution of any finite length $L$; when $L\geq r+1$, its last kernel is $R$-projective by step 1.3. Given a finite $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$-resolution $Q_*\to M$, take $N$ bounding all its objects and $L$ at least its length and $r+1$. A truncated word-free resolution $P_*\to M$ of length $L$ maps to $Q_*$: below degree $L$ use $\Lambda_N$-projectivity for the lifts, which therefore intertwine every endomorphism. In degree $L$, the induced map lands in the last cycle of $Q_*$, which is zero if its length is smaller, or is uniquely the image of its terminal term if its length is $L$. The last map thus exists without requiring the final kernel to be $\Lambda_N$-projective. Two resolutions admit the same comparison using common $N,L$. [step 1.3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 The comparison cones are finite acyclic complexes with terms in $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$. Their cycles are $R$-projective: induct from the degree-zero term, splitting each underlying surjection onto the preceding projective cycle. Their kernels are invariant under the nil endomorphisms. The cycle exact sequences are therefore admissible in $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$, and their Euler sums telescope to zero. Hence the Euler sum $\chi(M)$ of a finite $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$-resolution is independent of resolution. For a short exact sequence, compare a sufficiently long word-free resolution of the subobject with a finite resolution of the middle object at a common bound; its cone resolves the quotient and gives $\chi(M'')=\chi(M)-\chi(M')$. Thus $\chi$ is an additive inverse to $K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal P})\to K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal M})$. The empty-alphabet instance is the ordinary Euler comparison $K_0(R)\cong G_0(R)$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $J^kM$ be the sum of images of all products $\nu_w$ of length $k$. This finite filtration terminates, its submodules are finite by noetherianity, and the endomorphisms on each quotient are zero. The same filtration on the underlying module with zero endomorphisms gives the same sum. Consequently $[(M,(\nu_i))]=[(M,(0))]$ in $K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal M})$. Forgetting endomorphisms is an isomorphism to $G_0(R)$, with the zero-endomorphism inverse. Combining the commuting forgetful diagram with step 2.3 gives the asserted isomorphism $K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal P})\to K_0(R)$. Its zero-endomorphism section is its inverse, so both reduced descriptions in the statement vanish. With one letter this is exactly $\widetilde K_0(\operatorname{Nil}(R))=0$. [step 1.3, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

3.2 For $(P,(\nu_i))$ in $\mathcal N_{\mathcal P}$, the nonidentity part of $1+\sum_i\nu_it_i$ is nilpotent: expand a sufficiently high power and use joint nilpotence, commuting the central $R$-coefficients with the $t_i$. The finite geometric series gives an inverse. Extend by the identity on a finite complement to obtain a free-module matrix; adding complements and conjugating shows its stabilized class is independent of the complement. A short exact sequence of nilpotent projectives splits on underlying $R$-modules; the resulting automorphism is block triangular, so its $K_1(T)$ class is the sum of the diagonal classes. This defines an additive homomorphism from $K_0(\mathcal N_{\mathcal P})$ to $K_1(T)$. It vanishes on every zero-endomorphism object and hence on every object by step 3.1. All basis lifting was justified by [A1] and all remaining choices were finite or least-coded, proving the statement under countable choice. [A1, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
