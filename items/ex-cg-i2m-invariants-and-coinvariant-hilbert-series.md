## Statement

Let $m\ge3$, let $W=I_2(m)$ be the finite Coxeter system of type $I_2(m)$ ([[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]], [[thm-cg-finite-coxeter-classification-including-h-and-dihedral]] (1)), and realise it on $\mathbb R^2$ as the dihedral group of order $2m$ generated by the reflection in the $x$-axis and the rotation by $2\pi/m$; in the coordinates $u=x+iy$, $z=x-iy$ the generators act by $u\leftrightarrow z$ (the reflection) and $u\mapsto\zeta u$, $z\mapsto\zeta^{-1}z$ with $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/m}$ (the rotation). This is the standard rank-two reflection geometry of $B(e_u,e_u)=B(e_z,e_z)=1$, $B(e_u,e_z)=-\cos(\pi/m)$ under the identification of [[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]] and [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]]. Then:

**(1)** $\mathbb C[V]^{W}=\mathbb C[x^2+y^2,\ \mathrm{Re}((x+iy)^m)]=\mathbb C[uz,\ u^m+z^m]$, the two generators $$a:=uz=x^2+y^2\quad(\deg 2),\qquad b:=u^m+z^m=2\,\mathrm{Re}((x+iy)^m)\quad(\deg m)$$ are algebraically independent, and they are the basic invariants of the definition [[def-cg-coxeter-basic-degrees-and-graded-coinvariants]]; the exponents are $e_1=1$, $e_2=m-1$.

**(2)** The coinvariant algebra $A=\mathbb C[u,z]/(uz,\ u^m+z^m)$ has $\mathbb C$-basis the $2m$ classes $$1,\ u,\ u^2,\dots,u^{m-1},\ z,\ z^2,\dots,z^{m-1},\ u^m\quad(\text{with }z^m\equiv-u^m),$$ Hilbert series $1+2t+2t^2+\dots+2t^{m-1}+t^m=(1+t)(1+t+\dots+t^{m-1})$, and dimension $2m=|I_2(m)|$; all of this is proved directly, without invoking the AC-scoped invariant-theory suppliers.

**(3) Noncrystallographic contrast.** For $m\notin\{3,4,6\}$ the group $I_2(m)$ is not isomorphic to the Weyl group of any reduced crystallographic Euclidean root system. This holds even for abstract group isomorphisms: the proof below uses the orders of all products of root reflections, rather than assuming a root system with this Weyl group has rank two. Thus $I_2(5)=H_2$ and all $I_2(m)$ with $m\notin\{3,4,6\}$ have a polynomial invariant algebra by (1)-(2) but no reduced crystallographic root-system realization and no integral root lattice. For $m\in\{3,4,6\}$ the same computation recovers $A_2=I_2(3)$, $B_2=I_2(4)$ and $G_2=I_2(6)$, whose Weyl-group degrees $2,3$, $2,4$ and $2,6$ agree with [[lem-cg-classical-coxeter-spectra-from-reflection-models]] and the final theorem of the page.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge3$, the Coxeter system $W=I_2(m)$ with its two simple reflections $s_1,s_2$, and the plane model with coordinates $u=x+iy$, $z=x-iy$.

[F1] $I_2(m)$ is the two-vertex diagram with one edge labelled $m$; the presented group $W=\langle s_1,s_2\rangle$ has $s_i^2=1$, $(s_1s_2)^m=1$, is of finite type, and has the universal property that a map on the generators satisfying these relators extends uniquely to a homomorphism ([[def-cg-coxeter-diagram-components-and-finite-type]], [[thm-cg-finite-coxeter-classification-including-h-and-dihedral]] (1), [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F2] $V=\mathbb R^S$ is two-dimensional with $B(e_1,e_1)=B(e_2,e_2)=1$, $B(e_1,e_2)=-\cos(\pi/m)$, the reflection formula is $r_a(v)=v-2B(v,a)a/B(a,a)$, and the canonical homomorphism $\rho$ satisfies $\rho(s_i)=r_{e_i}$; $B$ is positive definite and $\rho$ is faithful ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]] (2), [[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[thm-cg-finite-type-positive-definite-criterion]] (1), [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]] (3), [[lem-cg-complexification-satisfies-reflection-invariant-hypotheses]] (1)).

[F3] For the action $(g\cdot f)(v)=f(\rho(g)^{-1}v)$, $S=\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ is a graded algebra with invariant algebra $R=S^{W}$, positive part $R_+$ and coinvariant algebra $A=S/I$, $I=SR_+$; $\mathbb C[V]$ and $\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]$ are the polynomial ring in the coordinates, and a polynomial identity may be checked monomial by monomial ([[def-finite-linear-invariant-and-coinvariant-polynomial-algebras]], [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]], [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F4] The formal partial derivatives act on monomials by the Leibniz monomial rule and are extended linearly; the univariate formal derivative satisfies the sum, scalar, product and power rules and lowers degree, and algebraic independence of a finite tuple means injectivity of the evaluation map ([[def-jacobian-matrix-affine-algebraic-set]], [[def-formal-derivative-of-a-polynomial]], [[prop-formal-derivative-laws]], [[def-algebraically-independent-finite-tuples-over-a-field]]).

[F5] The $m$-th roots of unity in $\mathbb C$ are exactly the $m$ distinct numbers $\exp(2\pi ik/m)$, $k=0,\dots,m-1$, and $\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/m)$ has order exactly $m$ in $\mathbb C^\times$ ([[thm-complex-nth-roots-and-roots-of-unity]], [[def-roots-of-unity-in-a-field]]).

[F6] Rank-two crystallographic facts: for a reduced crystallographic Euclidean root system the reflection formula is $s_\alpha(x)=x-2(x,\alpha)\alpha/(\alpha,\alpha)$, the Weyl group is generated by the $s_\alpha$, the Cartan integers of a base are integers with nonpositive off-diagonal entries, and the rank-two classification gives $n_{\alpha\beta}n_{\beta\alpha}=4\cos^2\theta\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ for nonproportional roots with angle $\theta$ ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]], [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]], [[def-cartan-matrix-of-a-based-root-system]], [[prop-distinct-simple-roots-have-nonpositive-inner-product]], [[thm-rank-two-root-system-classification]]).

[F7] Trigonometric facts: cosine is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ and range $[-1,1]$, and for all real $x,y$ one has $\cos(x+y)=\cos x\cos y-\sin x\sin y$, so $\cos 2x=2\cos^2x-1$ ([[def-sine-and-cosine-by-power-series]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]]).

[F8] For every $m\ge3$ the Coxeter element of $I_2(m)$ has order $m$ and spectral exponents $\{1,m-1\}$ ([[lem-cg-classical-coxeter-spectra-from-reflection-models]] (1.2)).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** explicit computation in the reflection model, with a rank-two obstruction for the contrast.

1.1 (Identification of the model.) Let $D\le\mathrm{O}(2)$ be the group generated by the reflection $\sigma$ in the $x$-axis and the rotation $\rho_{2\pi/m}$ by angle $2\pi/m$; it consists of the $m$ rotations by $2\pi k/m$ and the $m$ reflections $\rho_{2\pi k/m}\sigma$, so $|D|=2m$. The reflection $\tau$ in the line at angle $\pi/m$ satisfies $\tau=\rho_{2\pi/m}\sigma$, so $D=\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle$ with $\sigma,\tau$ involutions whose product has order $m$; hence the universal property of [F1] gives a homomorphism $\pi:W\to D$ with $\pi(s_1)=\sigma$, $\pi(s_2)=\tau$, and $\pi$ is onto. Conversely, put $t:=s_1s_2\in W$: then $t^m=1$, $s_2=s_1t$ and $s_1ts_1=s_2s_1=t^{-1}$, so every element of $W$ is a word in $s_1$ and $t$ and $W$ is a quotient of the group $\langle u,t\mid u^2=t^m=1,\ utu=t^{-1}\rangle$, every element of which is $t^k$ or $ut^k$ with $k\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}$; hence $|W|\le2m$, and together with the surjection $\pi$ onto the group of order $2m$ this gives $|W|=2m$ and that $\pi$ is an isomorphism. Let $\varphi:V\to\mathbb R^2$ be the linear isometry sending $e_1$ to the unit normal of the $x$-axis and $e_2$ to a unit normal of the line at angle $\pi/m$ chosen with $(e_1,e_2)$-image equal to $-\cos(\pi/m)$ (flipping a normal does not change its reflection); as in the reflection-model identification, $\varphi\rho(s_i)\varphi^{-1}$ is the reflection of the model in the line fixed by $\sigma$ resp. $\tau$, so $\varphi\rho\varphi^{-1}=\pi$ on the generators and hence on all of $W$: the canonical representation of $W$ is conjugate to the model action, and the invariant rings correspond. Therefore $\mathbb C[V_{\mathbb C}]^{W}$ may be computed in the coordinates $u,z$, where the generators act by $u\leftrightarrow z$ and $u\mapsto\zeta u$, $z\mapsto\zeta^{-1}z$ with $\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/m)$ [F2, F5]. [F1, F2, F5]

2.1 (Generation of the invariants.) Let $f=\sum_{p,q\ge0}c_{pq}u^pz^q\in\mathbb C[u,z]$ be invariant. Applying the rotation gives $\sum c_{pq}\zeta^{p-q}u^pz^q=\sum c_{pq}u^pz^q$, so $c_{pq}(\zeta^{p-q}-1)=0$ monomial by monomial; by [F5] $\zeta^{k}=1$ only when $m\mid k$, so every monomial of $f$ has $p\equiv q\pmod m$ [F3]. Applying the reflection $u\leftrightarrow z$ pairs the monomials $u^pz^q$ and $u^qz^p$ and forces $c_{pq}=c_{qp}$; together with the rotation condition this shows that the invariants are spanned by the elements $u^pz^p=(uz)^p$ and the sums $u^pz^q+u^qz^p$ with $p>q$ and $m\mid p-q$; writing $p=q+km$, $k\ge1$, such a sum equals $(uz)^q(u^{km}+z^{km})$. The polynomials $S_k:=u^{km}+z^{km}$ satisfy $S_0=2$, $S_1=u^m+z^m=:b$ and, by expanding $(u^m+z^m)(u^{km}+z^{km})-(uz)^m(u^{(k-1)m}+z^{(k-1)m})=u^{(k+1)m}+z^{(k+1)m}$, the recurrence $S_{k+1}=bS_k-a^mS_{k-1}$ with $a:=uz$; induction gives $S_k\in\mathbb C[a,b]$ for every $k\ge0$. Hence every invariant lies in $\mathbb C[a,b]$, and conversely $a$ and $b$ are invariant, so $\mathbb C[u,z]^{W}=\mathbb C[a,b]$. [F3, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 (The coinvariant algebra.) In $B_0:=\mathbb C[u,z]/(uz)$ every class corresponds uniquely to a pair $(p,q)$ with $p,q\in\mathbb C[t]$ and $p(0)=q(0)$, represented by $p(u)+q(z)-p(0)$, and monomials give the basis $1,\ u^p\ (p\ge1),\ z^q\ (q\ge1)$; the ring is a fibre product in which multiplication is componentwise, $(p,q)(p',q')=(pp',qq')$. Multiplication by $u^m+z^m$, i.e. by the pair $(t^m,t^m)$, preserves this decomposition and is injective because $\mathbb C[t]$ is a domain; its image consists of the pairs $(t^mp,t^mq)$ with $p(0)=q(0)$. Hence $0\to B_0\xrightarrow{\cdot(u^m+z^m)}B_0\to A\to0$ is exact, $B_0$ has Hilbert series $1+\sum_{p\ge1}t^p+\sum_{q\ge1}t^q=(1+t)/(1-t)$, and the multiplicativity of the Hilbert series in a short exact sequence of graded spaces gives $\operatorname{Hilb}(A,t)=(1-t^m)(1+t)/(1-t)=(1+t)(1+t+\dots+t^{m-1})$ [F3]. On the other hand the relations $uz=0$ and $z^m=-u^m$ show that the $2m$ displayed classes span: in $B_0$ the classes of $u^p$ and $z^q$ span, and $u^{m+k}=u^k(-z^m)=0$ for $k\ge1$, likewise for $z$, so only $1,u,\dots,u^{m-1},z,\dots,z^{m-1},u^m$ remain, with $z^m\equiv-u^m$. Comparing with the Hilbert series degree by degree, these classes are linearly independent and form a basis, and $\dim_{\mathbb C}A=2m=|W|$ by step 1.1. [F3, step 1.1]

3.1 (Algebraic independence and the basic family.) The formal partials satisfy the product and power rules on monomials and hence, by linearity, on all polynomials; consequently $\partial_u$ and $\partial_z$ satisfy the two-variable chain rule for polynomial compositions. Suppose $0\ne H\in\mathbb C[y_1,y_2]$ has minimal total degree among the nonzero polynomials with $H(a,b)=0$. Its partial derivatives $H_1,H_2$ are not both zero and have smaller total degree, and differentiating $H(a,b)=0$ with respect to $u$ and $z$ gives the two equations $H_1(a,b)\partial_ua+H_2(a,b)\partial_ub=0$ and $H_1(a,b)\partial_za+H_2(a,b)\partial_zb=0$, i.e. $J^{\mathsf T}\cdot(H_1(a,b),H_2(a,b))^{T}=0$ for $J=\begin{pmatrix}\partial_ua&\partial_za\\ \partial_ub&\partial_zb\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}z&u\\ mu^{m-1}&mz^{m-1}\end{pmatrix}$. Multiplying these two equations by the explicit adjugate $\begin{pmatrix}mz^{m-1}&-mu^{m-1}\\-u&z\end{pmatrix}$ of $J^{\mathsf T}$ gives $m(z^m-u^m)H_j(a,b)=0$ for $j=1,2$. Since $m(z^m-u^m)\ne0$ and $\mathbb C[u,z]$ is a domain, both $H_1(a,b)$ and $H_2(a,b)$ vanish; a nonzero partial of $H$ is nonconstant (a nonzero constant cannot evaluate to $0$), so it is a nonzero polynomial of smaller total degree vanishing on $(a,b)$, contradicting minimality. Thus $a,b$ are algebraically independent [F4]. They are homogeneous of degrees $2$ and $m$, generate $R=\mathbb C[a,b]$, and are minimal: $a\notin(b)$ because $\deg(b\cdot s)\ge m>2=\deg a$ for $s\ne0$, and $b\notin(a)$ because substituting $u=0$ gives $b(0,z)=z^m\ne0=(uz\cdot s)(0,z)$. Since $R_+=(a,b)R$, the ideal is $I=SR_+=(a,b)S$, so $a,b$ are the basic invariants of the definition with degrees $2,m$ and exponents $1,m-1$. [F4, step 2.1]

4.1 (Noncrystallographic contrast in every rank.) In a crystallographic root system, a product of two root reflections has order in $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$: proportional roots give the identity; otherwise the product fixes the orthogonal complement of their plane and rotates that plane by twice the angle of the two reflecting lines, and [F6] gives exactly the angles with these orders. Suppose its Weyl group were abstractly $D_m=\langle r,s\mid r^m=s^2=1, srs=r^{-1}\rangle$. The images of the root reflections form a generating set $U$ of involutions. Since the only possible nonidentity involution in $\langle r\rangle$ is $r^{m/2}$, some member lies outside this cyclic subgroup; relabel that member $s$. Every other member of $U$ is $r^ks$ or, for even $m$, $r^{m/2}$. Products of the fixed root reflection $s$ with the other coset members give rotations $r^{-k}$ of orders in $\{1,2,3,4,6\}$. These rotations, together with the possible central involution, generate $\langle r\rangle$: after replacing each coset generator by its product with $s$, all generators except $s$ are rotations, and $s$ merely inverts them. A cyclic group generated by elements of these orders has order dividing $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3,4,6)=12$. For $m\ge3$ this leaves $m=3,4,6,12$. If $m=12$, the possible exponents $k$ of coset members are $0,2,3,4,6,8,9,10$, and every difference of two such exponents also gives a product of root reflections, so cannot have order $12$. If $3$ or $9$ occurs, none of $2,4,8,10$ can occur, as their differences are coprime to $12$; all rotations generated then have exponents divisible by $3$, including the central exponent $6$. If neither occurs, all exponents are even, again including $6$. Neither case generates the full rotation subgroup of order $12$, a contradiction. Thus an abstract Weyl-group isomorphism is possible only for $m=3,4,6$, proving the strongest asserted contrast without assuming the root-system rank is two. In particular, the prescribed plane action has no crystallographic root realization when $m\notin\{3,4,6\}$. It also preserves no full lattice: if it did, the rotation would have an integer matrix and integer trace $2\cos(2\pi/m)$; this trace is $-1,0,1$ for $m=3,4,6$, lies strictly between $0$ and $1$ for $m=5$, and strictly between $1$ and $2$ for $m>6$, by [F7], excluding every other $m\ge3$. For $m=3,4,6$, step 3.1 gives degrees $2,3$, $2,4$, $2,6$, equal to one plus the spectral exponents in [F8]. [F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Steps 1.1-4.1 prove all three clauses: the invariant algebra is the polynomial algebra $\mathbb C[a,b]$ on the algebraically independent basic invariants of degrees $2,m$, the coinvariant algebra has the displayed $2m$-element basis and Hilbert series with $\dim_{\mathbb C}A=2m=|W|$, and no reduced crystallographic root system realizes $I_2(m)$ for $m\notin\{3,4,6\}$; the computation is a finite monomial and derivative calculation, uses no AC-scoped invariant-theory supplier, and the only choice made is the explicit order of the two colour classes, which does not enter the results. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 4.1] ∎
