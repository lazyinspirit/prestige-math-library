## Statement

Let $F=\mathbb Q(q)$, let $N\ge1$, and let $U_N$ be the presented algebra and bialgebra of [[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]] and [[lem-hh-quantum-presentation-and-tensor-action-descent]]. The candidate $\widetilde S$ of [[def-hh-type-a-quantum-antipode-formula]] descends to a well-defined $F$-linear map $S:U_N\to U_N$ satisfying $S(xy)=S(y)S(x)$. It obeys

$$m(S\otimes\operatorname{id})\Delta=u\epsilon=m(\operatorname{id}\otimes S)\Delta.$$

Here a **Hopf algebra** means a unital associative algebra with the coassociative coproduct and counit of a bialgebra and a linear map satisfying these two identities; no bijectivity of $S$ is asserted. Thus $U_N$ is a Hopf algebra in this sense.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $F=\mathbb Q(q)$, $N\ge1$, the generators and relations of $U_N$, its coproduct and counit, and the candidate $\widetilde S$. In step 5.1, $\Delta(a)=\sum a_{(1)}\otimes a_{(2)}$ denotes a finite expression for the actual tensor $\Delta(a)$; each displayed sum is a contraction induced by the algebra multiplication and the specified linear maps, so it does not depend on that expression.

[F1] The $L_j$ commute and $L_jL_j^{-1}=L_j^{-1}L_j=1$ ([[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]]).

[F2] The weights are $L_jE_iL_j^{-1}=q^{\delta_{j,i}-\delta_{j,i+1}}E_i$ and $L_jF_iL_j^{-1}=q^{-\delta_{j,i}+\delta_{j,i+1}}F_i$ ([[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]]).

[F3] The mixed commutator relation is $[E_i,F_j]=\delta_{ij}(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q-q^{-1})$ ([[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]]).

[F4] For $|i-j|>1$ the $E_i,E_j$ and $F_i,F_j$ commute, and for $|i-j|=1$ both families satisfy the displayed quantum Serre relation ([[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]]).

[F5] The candidate reverses words and sends $L_j\mapsto L_j^{-1}$, $L_j^{-1}\mapsto L_j$, $E_i\mapsto-E_iK_i^{-1}$, and $F_i\mapsto-K_iF_i$, where $K_i=L_iL_{i+1}^{-1}$ ([[def-hh-type-a-quantum-antipode-formula]]).

[F6] A map from the free associative algebra descends through a presentation when it preserves the two-sided ideal generated by the displayed relations; that ideal consists of finite sums $arb$ with $r$ a defining relation ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]]).

[F7] The displayed coproduct and counit descend to algebra homomorphisms, the coproduct is coassociative, and both counit identities hold ([[lem-hh-quantum-presentation-and-tensor-action-descent]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The data in [F1]–[F7].

1.1 Put $a_{rs}=2\delta_{rs}-\delta_{r,s+1}-\delta_{r+1,s}$ for $1\le r,s<N$. Multiplying the two $L$-weight formulas in [F2], and using that the $L_j$ commute by [F1], gives $K_rE_sK_r^{-1}=q^{a_{rs}}E_s$ and $K_rF_sK_r^{-1}=q^{-a_{rs}}F_s$. In particular $a_{rr}=2$, $a_{rs}=a_{sr}$, adjacent distinct indices have $a_{rs}=-1$, and nonadjacent distinct indices have $a_{rs}=0$. Reversing the words $K_r=L_rL_{r+1}^{-1}$ and $K_r^{-1}=L_{r+1}L_r^{-1}$ gives $\widetilde S(K_r)=K_r^{-1}$ and $\widetilde S(K_r^{-1})=K_r$. [given, F1, F2, F5, algebra]

2.1 The inverse relations map to $L_jL_j^{-1}=L_j^{-1}L_j=1$, and each commutator $L_jL_k-L_kL_j$ maps to $L_k^{-1}L_j^{-1}-L_j^{-1}L_k^{-1}=0$ by [F1]. For $w=\delta_{j,i}-\delta_{j,i+1}$, the positive weight relation maps to $L_j(-E_iK_i^{-1})L_j^{-1}-q^w(-E_iK_i^{-1})=0$, since $L_j$ commutes with $K_i$ and [F2] gives $L_jE_iL_j^{-1}=q^wE_i$. The negative weight relation maps to $L_j(-K_iF_i)L_j^{-1}-q^{-w}(-K_iF_i)=0$ by the same commutation and [F2]. [given, F1, F2, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Write $C_i=(K_i-K_i^{-1})/(q-q^{-1})$. By [F5], $S(C_i)=-C_i$, so the image of $E_iF_j-F_jE_i-\delta_{ij}C_i$ is $K_jF_jE_iK_i^{-1}-E_iK_i^{-1}K_jF_j+\delta_{ij}C_i$. If $i\ne j$, [F3] gives $F_jE_i=E_iF_j$; the two first terms become respectively $q^{a_{ji}}E_iK_jF_jK_i^{-1}$ and $q^{a_{ij}}E_iK_jF_jK_i^{-1}$, which are equal by $a_{ij}=a_{ji}$. If $i=j$, then $K_iF_iE_iK_i^{-1}=(K_iF_iK_i^{-1})(K_iE_iK_i^{-1})=F_iE_i$, so the first two terms equal $F_iE_i-E_iF_i=-C_i$ by [F3] and cancel $+C_i$. Thus every mixed commutator relation is preserved. [given, F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.3 If $|i-j|>1$, then $a_{ij}=a_{ji}=0$. Hence $K_i,K_j$ commute with the opposite-index $E$ and $F$ generators; [F4] gives that the original $E_i,E_j$ and $F_i,F_j$ commute. The formulas in [F5] therefore show directly that the images of both distant commutators are zero. [given, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.4 Let $\gamma=q+q^{-1}$ and take adjacent $i,j$. Set $X_r=E_rK_r^{-1}$ and $Y_r=K_rF_r$. Moving the Cartan factors with the weights from step 1.1 gives $X_i^2X_j=E_i^2E_jK_i^{-2}K_j^{-1}$, $X_iX_jX_i=E_iE_jE_iK_i^{-2}K_j^{-1}$, and $X_jX_i^2=E_jE_i^2K_i^{-2}K_j^{-1}$; hence the $X$-Serre polynomial is the original $E$-Serre polynomial followed by $K_i^{-2}K_j^{-1}$. The same weight moves give $Y_i^2Y_j=K_i^2K_jF_i^2F_j$, $Y_iY_jY_i=K_i^2K_jF_iF_jF_i$, and $Y_jY_i^2=K_i^2K_jF_jF_i^2$, so the $Y$-Serre polynomial is $K_i^2K_j$ followed by the original $F$-Serre polynomial. Both vanish by [F4]. Reversing each cubic word and accounting for its three minus signs gives $\widetilde S(E_i^2E_j-\gamma E_iE_jE_i+E_jE_i^2)=-(X_i^2X_j-\gamma X_iX_jX_i+X_jX_i^2)$ and the analogous equality with $F,Y$; thus both Serre relations are preserved. [given, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Steps 2.1–2.4 show that the image of every defining relation under $\widetilde S$ lies in the defining ideal. By [F6] every element of that ideal is a finite sum of terms $arb$; reversal sends each such term to $\widetilde S(b)\widetilde S(r)\widetilde S(a)$, which remains in the ideal. Therefore $S(a+I):=\widetilde S(a)+I$ is well-defined on the quotient and is an anti-algebra map. The empty word maps to $1$, so $S(1)=1$. [given, F5, F6, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, algebra]

4.1 On $L_j^{\pm1}$, both products $S(x_{(1)})x_{(2)}$ and $x_{(1)}S(x_{(2)})$ equal $1=\epsilon(x)1$. On $E_i$, the two products from [F7] are $S(E_i)K_i+E_i=0$ and $E_iS(K_i)+S(E_i)=0$. On $F_i$, they are $S(F_i)+S(K_i^{-1})F_i=0$ and $F_i+K_i^{-1}S(F_i)=0$. Thus both antipode identities hold on every algebra generator and its inverse. [given, F5, F7, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For products $ab$, anti-multiplicativity and the fact that $\Delta,\epsilon$ are algebra homomorphisms [F7] give $\sum S(a_{(1)}b_{(1)})a_{(2)}b_{(2)}=\sum S(b_{(1)})(S(a_{(1)})a_{(2)})b_{(2)}=\epsilon(a)\epsilon(b)1$ whenever the left identity holds for $a,b$; likewise $\sum a_{(1)}b_{(1)}S(a_{(2)}b_{(2)})=\sum a_{(1)}(b_{(1)}S(b_{(2)}))S(a_{(2)})=\epsilon(a)\epsilon(b)1$ for the right identity. Induction extends both identities from the generators in step 4.1 to every word, and $F$-linearity extends them to all of $U_N$. If $N=1$, there are no $E_i,F_i$ or Serre relations, and the same generator checks on $L_1^{\pm1}$ prove the claim. [given, F7, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
