## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let
$B=A[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ be graded by total degree, $n\ge0$, let
$\mathfrak b=(x_0,\dots,x_n)=B_+$ be the irrelevant ideal, and let
$I\subseteq B$ be a homogeneous ideal. Write
$$I^{\mathrm{sat}}=\textstyle\bigcup_{r\ge0}(I:\mathfrak b^r)=\{\,h\in B\;:\;\mathfrak b^rh\subseteq I\ \text{for some }r\ge0\,\}$$
for the saturation of $I$ with respect to $\mathfrak b$, and put
$X=\operatorname{Proj}B=\mathbb P^n_A$ with standard charts
$D_+(x_i)=\operatorname{Spec}B_{(x_i)}$ ([[thm-projective-space-as-proj]]).

Then:

1. For a homogeneous $h\in B$ of degree $d$ one has
   $h\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ if and only if $h/x_i^d$ lies in the degree-zero
   part $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$ of the localised ideal, inside
   $(B[x_i^{-1}])_0=A[x_0/x_i,\dots,x_n/x_i]$, for every $i=0,\dots,n$.
2. Consequently $I$ and $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ define the same ideals on every
   chart: $((I^{\mathrm{sat}})[x_i^{-1}])_0=(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for every $i$.
3. Two saturated homogeneous ideals $I,J\subseteq B$ (that is,
   $I=I^{\mathrm{sat}}$, $J=J^{\mathrm{sat}}$) with
   $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0=(J[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for all $i$ are equal. In particular the
   chart ideals determine $I$ uniquely.

The case $n=0$, where there is a single chart $D_+(x_0)=\operatorname{Spec}A$
and $\mathfrak b=(x_0)$, is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $A$, the graded polynomial ring $B=A[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ with $n\ge0$, a homogeneous ideal $I\subseteq B$, the ideal $\mathfrak b=(x_0,\dots,x_n)$, and the Axiom of Choice as inherited from the Proj construction.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] The chart $D_+(x_i)$ of $\operatorname{Proj}B=\mathbb P^n_A$ is $\operatorname{Spec}B_{(x_i)}$ with $B_{(x_i)}=A[x_0/x_i,\dots,x_n/x_i]$, the variable $x_i/x_i$ being the unit, and the charts cover $X$. ([[thm-projective-space-as-proj]])

[F2] For a graded $B$-module $M$ and homogeneous $f$ of positive degree, $\Gamma(D_+(f),\widetilde M)=M_{(f)}$, the ideals on charts being the degree-zero parts of the localised ideals; in particular the ideal of $D_+(x_i)$ cut out by a homogeneous ideal $I$ is $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$. ([[lem-proj-associated-sheaf-basic-sections]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: translate chart membership into divisibility data, convert finitely many divisibility conditions into one power of the irrelevant ideal by a pigeonhole count over monomials, and compare two saturated ideals chart by chart.

1.1 Chart membership is a divisibility condition. Let $h\in B$ be homogeneous of degree $d$ and fix $i$. Because a homogeneous ideal is generated by its homogeneous elements, the condition $h/x_i^d\in(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$ means that there are $k\ge0$ and $c\in I$ homogeneous of degree $k$ with $h/x_i^d=c/x_i^k$ in $B[x_i^{-1}]$, and equality of these fractions means that $x_i^N(x_i^k h-x_i^d c)=0$ for some $N\ge0$. Multiplication by the polynomial variable $x_i$ is injective on $B=A[x_0,\dots,x_n]$ for every coefficient ring $A$, because it shifts the $A$-basis of monomials injectively; hence $x_i^k h=x_i^d c$. If $k\ge d$, cancellation gives $x_i^{k-d}h=c\in I$; if $k<d$, it gives $h=x_i^{d-k}c\in I$. Conversely either containment $x_i^{k'}h\in I$ represents $h/x_i^d$ by an element of $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$. Thus chart membership is equivalent to $x_i^{k'}h\in I$ for some $k'\ge0$, without assuming $A$ is a domain. [F2, algebra]
1.2 From chart conditions to one power of $\mathfrak b$. Suppose $h$ is homogeneous of degree $d$ and $x_i^{r_i}h\in I$ for every $i$ with $0\le r_i<\infty$. Put $r=r_0+\dots+r_n$. Every monomial $x_0^{a_0}\cdots x_n^{a_n}$ of total degree $r$ has some $a_i\ge r_i$: otherwise $a_i\le r_i-1$ for all $i$ would give $r=\sum a_i\le\sum(r_i-1)=r-(n+1)<r$. For such an $i$ the product equals $x_i^{a_i-r_i}\cdot(x_i^{r_i}h)\in I$, since $x_i^{r_i}h\in I$ and $I$ is an ideal; a monomial with total degree $r$ times $h$ is precisely one of these products. As $\mathfrak b^r$ is spanned by the monomials of total degree $r$, we get $\mathfrak b^rh\subseteq I$, hence $h\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}$. [algebra]
2.1 The saturation criterion. If $h\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ then $\mathfrak b^rh\subseteq I$ for some $r$, and in particular $x_i^rh\in I$ for every $i$, so step 1.1 gives $h/x_i^d\in(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for every $i$. Conversely, if $h/x_i^d\in(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for every $i$, then step 1.1 provides exponents $r_i$ with $x_i^{r_i}h\in I$, and step 1.2 gives $\mathfrak b^rh\subseteq I$ for $r=\sum_ir_i$, that is $h\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}$. This is claim (1) as stated for homogeneous $h$. Since $I$ and $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ are homogeneous ideals, their membership for an arbitrary polynomial can also be checked componentwise. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]
3.1 Same chart ideals. Since $I\subseteq I^{\mathrm{sat}}$, we have $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0\subseteq((I^{\mathrm{sat}})[x_i^{-1}])_0$. Conversely let $g/x_i^k\in((I^{\mathrm{sat}})[x_i^{-1}])_0$ with $g\in I^{\mathrm{sat}}$ homogeneous of degree $k$; then $x_i^{s}g\in I$ for some $s\ge0$ by definition of $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$, so $g/x_i^k=(x_i^{s}g)/x_i^{k+s}\in(I[x_i^{-1}])_0$, giving the reverse inclusion and claim (2). [step 2.1, algebra]
3.2 Saturated ideals with equal charts are equal. Let $I,J$ be saturated homogeneous ideals with $(I[x_i^{-1}])_0=(J[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for all $i$, and let $h\in I$ be homogeneous. By step 2.1 applied with $I^{\mathrm{sat}}=I$, the element $h$ satisfies $h/x_i^d\in(I[x_i^{-1}])_0=(J[x_i^{-1}])_0$ for all $i$ of the same degree $d=\deg h$, so step 2.1 applied with $J^{\mathrm{sat}}=J$ gives $h\in J$. Hence $I\subseteq J$, and symmetrically $J\subseteq I$; this is claim (3). [step 2.1, algebra]
4.1 Conclusion. Steps 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1 prove the chartwise saturation criterion (1), step 3.1 identifies the chart ideals of $I$ and $I^{\mathrm{sat}}$, and step 3.2 shows that a saturated homogeneous ideal is determined by its chart ideals. For $n=0$ the index set is $\{0\}$, the pigeonhole count in step 1.2 reduces to $r=r_0$, and all statements read $x_0^{r}h\in I$ on the single chart $D_+(x_0)=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of [F1], so the case is included. The Axiom of Choice [A1] is inherited from the Proj construction and is not used again. [A1, F1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, cases: n=0]
\qed
