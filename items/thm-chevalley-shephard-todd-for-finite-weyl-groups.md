## Statement

Let $W$ be a finite Weyl group acting faithfully on its $r$-dimensional complex reflection representation $V$, and put $S=\mathbb C[V]$. Then $S^W$ is a polynomial algebra on $r$ homogeneous algebraically independent basic invariants, and $S$ is free of rank $|W|$ over $S^W$. More generally these conclusions hold for every finite subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(V)$ generated by complex reflections (nonidentity transformations fixing a hyperplane pointwise). In the Weyl case the generating root reflections have order two; no classification or Coxeter presentation is needed here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A faithful finite reflection group; in the Weyl specialization its action is generated by its root reflections.

[F1] Minimal homogeneous invariant generators of $SS^W_+$ exist, generate $S^W$ as an algebra and are algebraically independent ([[lem-finite-reflection-invariant-generators-are-algebraically-independent]]).

[F2] Their number is $r$, and homogeneous coinvariant basis lifts are a graded free $S^W$-basis of $S$ ([[lem-reflection-basic-invariants-form-a-regular-sequence]]).

[F3] The coinvariant dimension is the product of basic degrees and equals $|W|$ ([[lem-weyl-coinvariant-hilbert-series-has-order-w-dimension]]).

## Proof

1.1 Apply F1 to the given finite subgroup: each generating reflection is precisely a complex reflection under the stated hyperplane definition. If its minimal invariant ideal generators are $f_1,\ldots,f_m$, the map $\mathbb C[t_1,\ldots,t_m]\to S^W$, $t_i\mapsto f_i$, is surjective by algebra generation and injective by algebraic independence. Giving $t_i$ degree $\deg f_i$ makes this a graded algebra isomorphism. [F1, given]

2.1 F2 gives $m=r$ and a finite homogeneous basis of $S/(f_1,\ldots,f_r)S$ whose arbitrary homogeneous lifts form a free $S^W$-basis. F3 says this basis has $|W|$ elements, proving the asserted rank as well as polynomiality. These suppliers concern the same ideal $SS^W_+$ and the same faithful action; no assertion about a Lie algebra restriction map is involved. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 When $r=0$, the subgroup of $\operatorname{GL}(0)$ is trivial, the invariant algebra is the polynomial algebra in zero variables $\mathbb C$, and its module rank is one. Fixed directions and degree-one generators are allowed by F1 and F2. Only a finite coinvariant basis and finitely many lifts were selected, so the assertion uses no AC. This proves the stated reflection-group direction; no converse characterizing arbitrary invariant-polynomial groups is asserted. [F1, F2, step 2.1] ∎
