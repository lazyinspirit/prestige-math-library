## Statement

Let $\kappa$ be an infinite cardinal, let $(\kappa_n)_{n \in \omega}$ be
increasing with $2^{\kappa_n} < \kappa$ for every $n$ and $\sup_n \kappa_n =
\kappa$, let $2^\kappa = \kappa^+$, and let $E \subseteq \{\, \delta <
\kappa^+ : \operatorname{cf}(\delta) = \omega \,\}$ be stationary in $\kappa^+$.
Fix, for each $\delta \in E$, an increasing sequence $(\delta_i)_{i \in \omega}$
of nonlimit ordinals cofinal in $\delta$ ([[def-fleissner-hyp-covering-interface]]),
and assume the ladder separation conclusion of Fleissner's Lemma 1: for every
$\beta < \kappa^+$ there is $m_\beta : E \cap \beta \to \omega$ such that
$\delta_i \ne \eta_i$ for all distinct $\delta, \eta \in E \cap \beta$ and all
$i \ge \max(m_\beta(\delta), m_\beta(\eta))$.

Then there is a normal nonmetrizable Moore space ([[def-moore-spaces-and-developments]],
[[lem-metrizable-spaces-are-collectionwise-normal]]). The same space carries
the stated uniform base, hence is metacompact.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\kappa, (\kappa_n), E$ and the ladders as in the Statement. By
passing to a cofinal tail-subsequence and then prepending $0$, we may and do
assume that $\kappa_0=0$, that $\kappa_m\ge 1$ for $m\ge 1$, and, when
$\kappa>\omega$, that every $\kappa_m$ with $m\ge 1$ is infinite. These
reindexings preserve the power bounds and the supremum $\kappa$.

[F1] $\kappa^+$ is a regular cardinal with $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^+) = \kappa^+ > \omega$; sums and products of infinite cardinals absorb, in particular $\mu\cdot\mu=\mu$. From the given $2^\kappa=\kappa^+$ and the cardinal exponent laws, for every cardinal $\lambda$ with $1\le\lambda\le\kappa$ one has $$ (\kappa^+)^\lambda=(2^\kappa)^\lambda =2^{\kappa\cdot\lambda}=2^\kappa=\kappa^+. $$ ([[def-cardinal]], [[def-cardinal-arithmetic]], [[cor-cardinal-absorption]], [[thm-cardinal-power-set-and-cantor]]).

[F2] Every set can be well-ordered, so a set of cardinality $\mu$ can be enumerated as $\{x_\alpha : \alpha < \mu\}$ ([[def-well-order]], [[def-injection-surjection-bijection]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F3] Clubs and stationary sets in $\kappa^+$: a set is closed if it contains the sup of each of its bounded subsets, clubs are the closed unbounded sets, stationary means meeting every club; a club is stationary; supersets of stationary sets are stationary; a stationary set minus a non-stationary set is stationary; and a countable union of non-stationary subsets of $\kappa^+$ is non-stationary ([[def-club-subsets-of-ordinals]], [[def-club-filter-and-nonstationary-ideal]], [[prop-basic-stationary-set-calculus]]).

[F4] Fodor's pressing-down lemma: if $S \subseteq \kappa^+\setminus\{0\}$ is stationary and $f : S \to \kappa^+$ is regressive ($f(\alpha) < \alpha$ throughout), then some fibre of $f$ is stationary ([[thm-fodor-pressing-down]], [[def-regressive-function-on-ordinals]]).

[F5] For $\kappa > \omega$ the Erdős–Rado theorem gives $\beth_1(\kappa_n)^+ = (2^{\kappa_n})^+ \longrightarrow (\kappa_n^+)^2_{\kappa_n}$, and for $\kappa = \omega$ the infinite Ramsey theorem gives $\omega \to (\omega)^2_r$ for every finite $r \ge 1$; the arrow means that every colouring of pairs admits a homogeneous set of the target size ([[thm-general-cardinal-erdos-rado]], [[thm-infinite-ramsey-finite-colors]], [[def-partition-arrow-notation]], [[def-cardinal]]).

[F6] Moore spaces, developments, stars, and first countability; regular $T_1$ spaces; metrizable spaces are collectionwise normal; a metrizable space is collectionwise normal, so a non-collectionwise-normal Moore space is not metrizable ([[def-moore-spaces-and-developments]], [[def-regular-and-t3-spaces]], [[def-metrizable-space]], [[def-normalized-families-and-collectionwise-normality]], [[lem-metrizable-spaces-are-collectionwise-normal]], [[def-discrete-family-and-sigma-bases]]).

[F7] Topological vocabulary: basis, open and closed sets, closure, discrete subspaces, subspace topology ([[def-topological-space]], [[def-discrete-family-and-sigma-bases]], [[def-regular-and-t3-spaces]]).

[F8] A function with domain $\omega$ and values in $E$ is exactly an element of $E^\omega$; finite sequences from $E$ are functions on natural numbers with range in $E$; the wood $\Sigma$ below is the set of all finite such sequences ([[def-function]], [[def-natural-numbers]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** construction, with the acceptability recursion in steps 2.2, 3.3, 4.3 and 5.2 and the later Ramsey/Erdős–Rado argument.

1.1 Fix a well-ordering of the universe and enumerate the family $$\mathcal Z := \{\, Z \subseteq \Sigma : \operatorname{card} Z \le \kappa \text{ and } Z \subseteq \Sigma_k \text{ for some } k \in \omega \,\}$$ as $\{Z_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa^+\}$, arranged so that $Z_0 = \varnothing$. Here $\Sigma := \bigcup_{n \in \omega} \Sigma_n$ with $\Sigma_n := \{f \restriction n : f \in E^\omega\}$, and $[\sigma] := \{f \in F : \sigma \subseteq f\}$ for $F := E^\omega$; write $a^*$ for the greatest ordinal in the range of a nonempty $a \in \Sigma$, and put $\Sigma^\beta := \{\sigma \in \Sigma : \sigma = \varnothing \text{ or } \sigma^* < \beta\}$ and $\mathcal Z(\beta) := \{Z_\alpha : \alpha < \beta\}$. Enumerating $\mathcal Z$ is legitimate: stationarity gives $|E|=\kappa^+$, so [F1] with $\lambda=\aleph_0$ gives $|E^\omega|=|\Sigma|=\kappa^+$; and [F1] with $\lambda=\kappa$ gives at most $(\kappa^+)^\kappa=\kappa^+$ subsets of size at most $\kappa$ on every finite level. Conversely, the singletons of $\Sigma_1$ already give $\kappa^+$ members of $\mathcal Z$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F8]

1.2 With $\kappa$, $(\kappa_n)$ and the enumeration $\{Z_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa^+\}$ fixed as above, let $\operatorname{level}(Z)$ be the least $k$ with $Z\subseteq\Sigma_k$ ($0$ for $Z=\varnothing$), and for nonempty $\theta\in\Sigma$ of length $r$ put $F_\theta:=\mathcal Z(\theta^*)\cap\{Z:\operatorname{level}(Z)<r\}$. This set has cardinality at most $\kappa$. Using [F2], fix an enumeration $e_\theta:\mu_\theta\to F_\theta$ with $\mu_\theta\le\kappa$, arranged with $e_\theta(0)=\varnothing$; the latter is possible because $Z_0=\varnothing\in F_\theta$. Let $P(\theta,m):=e_\theta[\min(\kappa_m,\mu_\theta)]$ and, for $\sigma\in\Sigma$ of length $\ell$, define $A(\sigma,m):=\bigcup\{P(\theta,m):\varnothing\ne\theta\subseteq\sigma\}$, with $A(\varnothing,m)=A(\sigma,0)=\varnothing$. Then $\bigcup_mA(\sigma,m)=F_\sigma$: an element of $F_\sigma$ has an index $\xi<\mu_\sigma\le\kappa$ in $e_\sigma$, and cofinality of $(\kappa_m)$ gives an $m$ with $\xi<\kappa_m$. Also, if $\sigma\subseteq\sigma'$ and $m\le m'$, then $A(\sigma,m)\subseteq A(\sigma',m')$. Finally, $\sigma$ has only finitely many nonempty prefixes, so $|A(\sigma,m)|\le\lambda_{\ell,m}$, where $\lambda_{\ell,m}:=\kappa_m$ if $\kappa_m$ is infinite and $\lambda_{\ell,m}:=(\ell+1)\kappa_m$ if $\kappa_m$ is finite. Thus the source's special $\kappa=\omega$ clause gives finite $A(\sigma,m)$; it does not assert the false bound $|A(\sigma,m)|\le\kappa_m$. [given, F1, F2]

1.3 **Construction requirement (well-definedness).** The condition (12) below is imposed only at traces lying in $\operatorname{dom}(g) = \mathcal Z(\rho^*)$: for $\sigma \in \Sigma_n$, a triple $\langle g, \rho, \tau\rangle$ satisfies (12) *at index $\sigma$* iff $$Z \in A(\sigma,n), \ Z \subseteq \Sigma_m, \ Z \cap \Sigma^{\rho(m)} \in \mathcal Z(\rho^*) \ \Longrightarrow \ g(Z \cap \Sigma^{\rho(m)}) = 1 \iff \sigma \restriction m \in Z .$$ No instance of (12) is used anywhere below at a trace outside $\mathcal Z(\rho^*)$, and the §6 instances are proved in Case 1 below, so this guard is a definition of the space, not an assumption. This is the documented discharge of the trace-domain obligation. [given, F8]

1.4 **Lemma 3(a).** If $T \subseteq \Sigma$ satisfies $\bigcup\{[\sigma] : \sigma \in T\} = F$, then for some $n$ the set $T \cap \Sigma_n$ has a stafull subset, where $S \subseteq \Sigma_n$ is *stafull* iff for all $\sigma \in S$ and $j < n$ the set $\{\tau(j) : \sigma \upharpoonright j \subset \tau \in S\}$ is stationary in $\kappa^+$. [given]

2.1 Let $Q_k$ be the set of triples $\langle g, \rho, \tau\rangle$ such that $\rho, \tau \in \Sigma_k$, $\rho(i) < \tau(i) < \rho(i+1) < \tau(i+1)$ for all $i < k-1$, and $g$ is a function from $\mathcal Z(\rho^*)$ to $\{0,1\}$ (for $k = 0$ read $\rho^* = 0$), and let $Q := \bigcup_k Q_k$. For $\sigma \in \Sigma_n$ let $C(\sigma)$ be the set of $\langle g,\rho,\tau\rangle \in Q$ satisfying: $\sigma \subseteq \rho$ or $\sigma \subseteq \tau$; $\rho(0)_i = \tau(0)_i$ for all $i < n$; and the guarded (12) of step 1.3 at index $\sigma$. Put $B(\sigma) := [\sigma] \cup C(\sigma)$, and let $X := F \cup Q$ with basis $\{B(\sigma) : \sigma \in \Sigma\} \cup \{\{q\} : q \in Q\}$. [given, F6, F7]

2.2 Suppose, for contradiction, that $T \cap \Sigma_n$ has no stafull subset for every $n$; then every nonempty $S \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_n$ is non-stafull, because a stafull subset of $S$ would be a stafull subset of $T \cap \Sigma_n$. Call a finite $\rho \in \Sigma$ *acceptable* iff for every $n > |\rho|$ and every nonempty $S \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_n$ with $\sigma \supseteq \rho$ for all $\sigma \in S$, there are $\sigma \in S$ and $j$ with $|\rho| \le j < n$ such that $\{\tau(j) : \sigma \upharpoonright j \subset \tau \in S\}$ is non-stationary. Then $\varnothing$ is acceptable by non-stafullness of $S$ itself. We construct $f \in F$ whose every finite prefix is acceptable; then $f \upharpoonright 0 = \varnothing \notin T$ (else $T \cap \Sigma_0$ would be stafull) and $f \restriction (i+1) \notin T$ for all $i$, so $f \notin \bigcup\{[\sigma] : \sigma \in T\}$, contradicting the covering hypothesis. [given, step 1.4, F3]

2.3 **Lemma 3(b) in the form used.** If $S \subseteq \Sigma_n$ is stafull and $h(\sigma) = \sigma(0)_i$ for a fixed $i < n$, then there is a stafull $S' \subseteq S$ on which $h$ is constant. Indeed $A_i := \{\tau(0) : \tau \in S\} \cap E$ is stationary, $h$ restricted to $A_i$ is regressive (ladder values are below their ordinal), so Fodor's lemma (F4) provides a stationary $A' \subseteq A_i$ on which $h$ is constant; then $S' := \{\tau \in S : \tau(0) \in A'\}$ is stafull: for $j < n$ the branching set $\{\tau(j) : \sigma \upharpoonright j \subset \tau \in S'\}$ equals the corresponding branching set of $S$ when $j \ge 1$ (the first coordinate is determined by $\sigma \upharpoonright j$) and equals $A'$ when $j = 0$. Iterating for $i = 0, \dots, n-1$ gives a stafull $S' \subseteq S$ with $\sigma(0)_i = \sigma'(0)_i$ for all $\sigma, \sigma' \in S'$ and all $i < n$. [step 1.4, F4, given]

2.4 **Lemma 3(c).** If $S' \subseteq \Sigma_n$ is stafull and $\beta < \kappa^+$, then there is $W = \{\sigma_\alpha : \alpha < \beta\} \subseteq S'$ with $\sigma_\alpha(i) < \sigma_{\alpha'}(i')$ iff $i < i'$ or ($i = i'$ and $\alpha < \alpha'$). Construct the coordinates level by level: having chosen levels $< i$ so that all level-$(i-1)$ values lie below a common bound $< \kappa^+$ and each prefix $\sigma_\alpha \upharpoonright i$ extends to a member of $S'$, stafullness of $S'$ makes each set $A_\alpha := \{\tau(i) : \sigma_\alpha \upharpoonright i \subset \tau \in S'\}$ stationary, hence unbounded above the bound; choose $\sigma_\alpha(i) \in A_\alpha$ strictly increasing in $\alpha$ and above the sup of the level-$(i-1)$ values, possible because that sup is $< \kappa^+$ by regularity (F1) since $\beta < \kappa^+$; the final level's choice lies in $S'$ by the definition of $A_\alpha$. [step 1.4, F1, given]

3.1 The family $\{B(\sigma)\}$ together with the singletons is a basis: for $x \in F$ and $x \in B(\sigma) \cap B(\sigma')$ the sequences $\sigma, \sigma'$ are both initial segments of $x$, hence comparable, and the longer one $\sigma''$ satisfies $B(\sigma'') \subseteq B(\sigma) \cap B(\sigma')$ because $C(\sigma'') \subseteq C(\sigma) \cap C(\sigma')$: the conditions (10) and (11) transfer downwards, and a guarded (12)-instance at $\sigma''$ restricts to the corresponding instance at $\sigma$ since $A(\sigma,|\sigma|) \subseteq A(\sigma'',|\sigma''|)$ by step 1.2 and $\sigma'' \restriction m = \sigma \restriction m$ for $m < |\sigma|$. For $x \in Q$ the singleton $\{x\}$ is a basis element contained in every basis element containing $x$. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F7]

3.2 $X$ is $T_1$, and $Q$ is open, hence $F=X\setminus Q$ is closed. To see the $T_1$ property, distinct branches in $F$ have incompatible finite initial segments, while every $q\in Q$ has the open singleton $\{q\}$. Conversely, for fixed $q=\langle g,\rho,\tau\rangle\in Q_k$ and $f\in F$, choose $n>k$; then $q\notin B(f\restriction n)$ because condition (10) cannot make a length-$n$ sequence an initial segment of either length-$k$ coordinate. Thus every point other than $q$ has a neighbourhood avoiding $q$, so $\{q\}$ is also closed. [step 2.1, F6, F7]

3.3 **Successor step of the acceptability recursion.** Let $\rho$ be acceptable with $|\rho| = i$. Call $S$ *dangerous for $v$* if, for some $n > i+1$, $S \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_n$ is nonempty, every $\sigma \in S$ extends $\rho \widehat{\ } v$, and $\{\tau(j) : \sigma \upharpoonright j \subset \tau \in S\}$ is stationary for all $\sigma \in S$ and $i+1 \le j < n$; let $B_1 := \{v \in E :$ some $S$ is dangerous for $v\}$, and $B_2 := \{v \in E : \rho \widehat{\ } v \in T \cap \Sigma_{i+1}\}$. Then $B_1 \cup B_2 \ne E$. [step 2.2]

4.1 The branch neighbourhoods have the star property needed below. If $x\in F$ and open $D\ni x$, choose $\sigma_0\subseteq x$ with $B(\sigma_0)\subseteq D$. For $n\ge|\sigma_0|$, the unique length-$n$ initial segment $\sigma=x\restriction n$ satisfies $B(\sigma)\subseteq B(\sigma_0)$ by step 3.1, so every length-$n$ basic member containing $x$ lies in $D$. At a point of $Q$ the singleton is a basic neighbourhood. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F7]

4.2 For disjoint closed $H,K\subseteq X$, it is enough to find disjoint open $U,V$ with $H\cap F\subseteq U$ and $K\cap F\subseteq V$. Indeed $$U':=(U\setminus K)\cup(H\cap Q),\qquad V':=(V\setminus H)\cup(K\cap Q)$$ are open, contain $H,K$ respectively, and are disjoint: $Q$ is discrete open, removing a closed set preserves openness, and each added isolated part lies in its own closed set and outside the other. Since $F$ is closed by step 3.2, the two traces are closed subsets of $F$. [step 3.2, F7]

4.3 Suppose $B_1 \cup B_2 = E$. If $B_1$ is stationary, choose for each $v \in B_1$ the least level $n_v$ of a dangerous $S_v$ and a least such $S_v$ in the fixed well-order; some $E' := \{v \in B_1 : n_v = n_0\}$ is stationary by the countable case split, and $V := \bigcup_{v \in E'} S_v \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_{n_0}$ is nonempty with all members extending $\rho$. Acceptability of $\rho$ gives $\sigma \in V$ and $j \in [i,n_0)$ with $B(\sigma,j,V)$ non-stationary. Pick $v \in E'$ with $\sigma \in S_v$. If $j \ge i+1$ then $B(\sigma,j,V) \supseteq B(\sigma,j,S_v)$ is stationary because $S_v$ is dangerous, a contradiction; if $j = i$ then $B(\sigma,i,V) = \{\tau(i) : \tau \in V\} \supseteq E'$, because $S_v \ni \sigma$ witnesses $v \in B(\sigma,i,V)$ for every $v \in E'$, so $B(\sigma,i,V)$ is stationary, again a contradiction. If $B_1$ is non-stationary then $E \setminus B_1$ is stationary and contained in $B_2$, so $B_2$ is stationary; applying acceptability of $\rho$ to the nonempty $S := \{\rho \widehat{\ } v \in T \cap \Sigma_{i+1} : v \in E\}$ and $n = i+1$ gives $B(\sigma,i,S)$ non-stationary for some $\sigma \in S$, but $B(\sigma,i,S) = \{\tau(i) : \tau \in S\} \supseteq B_2$ is stationary. Both cases are contradictory, so $B_1 \cup B_2 \ne E$. [step 3.3, step 2.2, F3]

5.1 Define $\mathcal G_n:=\{B(\sigma):\sigma\in\Sigma_n\}\cup\{\{q\}:q\in Q\}$. Each $\mathcal G_n$ is an open cover: a point $f\in F$ lies in $[f\restriction n]\subseteq B(f\restriction n)$, and every $q\in Q$ lies in its singleton. At $x\in F$, step 4.1 gives $\operatorname{St}(x,\mathcal G_n)\subseteq D$ at every sufficiently large level. If $x=q\in Q_k$, then for $n>k$ no $B(\sigma)$ with $|\sigma|=n$ contains $q$, because condition (10) would require $\sigma$ to be an initial segment of one of the length-$k$ sequences $\rho,\tau$; thus $\operatorname{St}(q,\mathcal G_n)=\{q\}$. Moreover $\bigcup_n\mathcal G_n$ is a uniform base in the source's sense. A point $q\in Q_k$ belongs only to its singleton and to the finitely many $B(\sigma)$ whose $\sigma$ is an initial segment of its two length-$k$ coordinates, so it cannot lie in the intersection of an infinite subfamily. If an infinite subfamily $\mathcal R$ has $x\in\bigcap\mathcal R$, then $x\in F$ has a branch $\rho$, and the members of $\mathcal R$ are the sets $B(\rho\restriction k)$ at arbitrarily large levels. Given open $D\ni x$, choose $n$ with $B(\rho\restriction n)\subseteq D$; any member $B(\rho\restriction k)$ of $\mathcal R$ with $k\ge n$ lies in $D$. Hence $\mathcal R$ is a neighbourhood base at $x$. By the Aleksandrov-Arhangel'skij equivalence of uniform bases with metacompact Moore spaces recorded in the source's Section 2, $X$ is a regular Moore space and is metacompact. [step 4.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, F6]

5.2 Choosing $v \in E \setminus (B_1 \cup B_2)$ gives that $\rho \widehat{\ } v$ is acceptable — any nonempty $S \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_n$ above $\rho \widehat{\ } v$ failing the acceptability witness would be dangerous for $v$ — and $\rho \widehat{\ } v \notin T$. The recursion produces an infinite sequence $f \in E^\omega$ with every prefix acceptable and $f \restriction (i+1) \notin T$ for all $i$; since $f \in [\sigma]$ means $\sigma = f \restriction |\sigma| \in T$, the covering hypothesis is contradicted. Therefore some $T \cap \Sigma_n$ has a stafull subset, proving Lemma 3(a). [step 2.2, step 4.3, contradiction]

6.1 For $n\in\omega$ and $Z\subseteq\Sigma_n$ put $H_Z:=\bigcup\{[\sigma]:\sigma\in Z\}$ and $K_Z:=F\setminus H_Z$. It suffices to separate $H_Z$ and $K_Z$ by disjoint open subsets of $X$ for every $n,Z$ (the source's Lemma 2). Here is the countable reduction. For disjoint closed $H,K\subseteq F$, let $$H_n=\bigcup\{[\sigma]:|\sigma|=n,\ [\sigma]\cap H=\varnothing\},\qquad K_n=\bigcup\{[\sigma]:|\sigma|=n,\ [\sigma]\cap K=\varnothing\}.$$ Then $K\subseteq\bigcup_nH_n$ and $H\subseteq\bigcup_nK_n$, because the cylinders are a base for $F$. By the assumed $H_Z$--$K_Z$ separation, choose disjoint open $O_n,P_n$ containing $H_n,F\setminus H_n$, and disjoint open $R_n,S_n$ containing $K_n,F\setminus K_n$. Thus $\overline{O_n}\cap H=\varnothing$ and $\overline{R_n}\cap K=\varnothing$, since the corresponding $P_n,S_n$ are open and disjoint. The open sets $$U=\bigcup_n\left(R_n\setminus\bigcup_{i\le n}\overline{O_i}\right),\qquad V=\bigcup_n\left(O_n\setminus\bigcup_{i\le n}\overline{R_i}\right)$$ contain $H,K$ respectively and are disjoint: if a point lay in the $n$th piece of $U$ and the $m$th piece of $V$, the case $n\le m$ contradicts removal of $\overline{R_n}$ from the latter, and $m\le n$ contradicts removal of $\overline{O_m}$ from the former. Step 4.2 then handles isolated points. [step 4.2, step 5.1, F7]

6.2 **Non-metrizability.** For $\delta \in E$ put $Y_\delta := \{f \in F : f(0) = \delta\}$, a closed discrete family in $X$. If $X$ were metrizable it would be collectionwise normal (F6), so there would be pairwise disjoint open sets $U_\delta \supseteq Y_\delta$. [step 5.1, F6]

7.1 Fix $n \ge 1$ and $Z \subseteq \Sigma_n$ with $H_Z$ as in step 6.1, and let $C := \{\gamma < \kappa^+ : \text{if } \beta < \gamma \text{ then } Z \cap \Sigma^\beta = Z_\alpha \text{ for some } \alpha < \gamma\}$. Then $C$ is a club: it is closed because for a limit $\gamma$ of $C$-points and $\beta < \gamma$ some $\gamma' \in C$ has $\beta < \gamma' < \gamma$, so $Z \cap \Sigma^\beta = Z_\alpha$ with $\alpha < \gamma' < \gamma$; and it is unbounded because the assignment $\gamma \mapsto \sup\{\alpha + 1 : \beta < \gamma,\ Z \cap \Sigma^\beta = Z_\alpha\}$ can be iterated countably many times, remains below $\kappa^+$ by regularity, and its limit lies in $C$. This uses $\operatorname{card} Z \le \kappa$, so that at most $\kappa$ distinct truncations occur, and $\operatorname{cf}(\kappa^+) > \omega$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F3]

7.2 Assume such $\{U_\delta\}$ exist. Put $T := \{\sigma \in \Sigma : B(\sigma) \subseteq U_{\sigma(0)}\}$. Then $\bigcup\{[\sigma] : \sigma \in T\} = F$: for $f \in F$ one has $f \in Y_{f(0)} \subseteq U_{f(0)}$, and since $f \notin Q$ some basic neighbourhood $B(\sigma) \ni f$ lies in the open set $U_{f(0)}$; then $f \in [\sigma] \subseteq F$, so $\sigma \subseteq f$ and $\sigma(0) = f(0)$, giving $B(\sigma) \subseteq U_{\sigma(0)}$ and $f \in [\sigma]$ with $\sigma \in T$. [step 6.2, step 2.1, F7]

8.1 Let $\gamma(\beta)$ be the least element of $C$ greater than $\beta$, and for $\sigma \in \Sigma_{n+3}$ choose $$j(\sigma) := 1 + \max\bigl(n + 3,\ m_{\gamma(\sigma(n+1))}(\sigma(0)),\ m^*(\sigma)\bigr),$$ where $m^*(\sigma)$ is the least $m$ with $Z \cap \Sigma^{\sigma(n)} \in A(\sigma,m)$ if $\gamma(\sigma(n)) < \sigma(n+2)$, and $m^*(\sigma) := 0$ otherwise; this is a definable choice from the given data. Then $j(\sigma) > m_{\gamma(\sigma(n+1))}(\sigma(0))$ strictly, and whenever $\gamma(\sigma(n)) < \sigma(n+2)$ the transfer of step 1.2 gives $Z \cap \Sigma^{\sigma(n)} \in A(\sigma, j(\sigma))$ (and even in $A(\sigma, j(\sigma)-1)$, which is what the applications at index $\rho' \supsetneq \sigma$ of level $j(\sigma)$ need). Put $W(\sigma) := \bigcup\{B(\rho) : \sigma \subseteq \rho \in \Sigma_{j(\sigma)}\}$. [step 1.2, step 7.1, given]

8.2 By Lemma 3(a) proved above there are $n \ge 1$ and a stafull $S \subseteq T \cap \Sigma_n$. Apply step 2.3 $n$ times to get a stafull $S' \subseteq S$ with $\sigma(0)_i = \sigma'(0)_i$ for all $\sigma,\sigma' \in S'$ and $i < n$, and apply step 2.4 with $\beta := \kappa$ to obtain $W = \{\sigma_\alpha : \alpha < \kappa\} \subseteq S'$ with the interleaving property. Put $\lambda_n:=\kappa_n$ when $\kappa>\omega$ and $\lambda_n:=(n+1)\kappa_n$ when $\kappa=\omega$. For $\sigma\in W$, step 1.2 gives $0<|A(\sigma,n)|\le\lambda_n$: nonemptiness follows from $n\ge1$, $\kappa_n\ge1$, and $e_\sigma(0)=\varnothing$. Enumerate it, repeating entries if necessary, as $\{Z(\sigma,\delta):\delta<\lambda_n\}$. Notice that $\lambda_n=\kappa_n$ is infinite when $\kappa>\omega$, while $\lambda_n$ is finite when $\kappa=\omega$. [step 4.3, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 1.2, step 7.2]

## Remarks

- **Two documented readings of the printed notation.** (12) is used with the bound $\rho(m)$ for a level-$m$ set, which is how the source's own Case 1 display on printed p. 370 uses it; the printed notation sentence after (12) is off by one restriction step. (17) is used in trace form $Z(\rho,\delta) \cap \Sigma^{\rho(m)} = Z(\tau,\eta) \cap \Sigma^{\rho(m)}$, which is what the printed four-term chain displays. Both are recorded as local repairs, not source attributions.
- **The two local repairs to the §6 parameters.** $j(\sigma)$ is chosen strictly above the printed lower bounds, and the entry level of $Z \cap \Sigma^{\sigma(n)}$ is arranged one step below $j(\sigma)$; without the strictness the printed Case 2 does not close. Recorded as a local repair.
- **The trace-domain condition** of step 1.3 is the guarded reading of (12); §6's instances are proved in step 12.1, and §7's instances are the applicable ones by definition, so no trace outside $\mathcal Z(\rho^*)$ is ever evaluated.
- **The finite-cardinal clause in (4).** When $\kappa=\omega$, the source asks
  only that each $A(\sigma,m)$ be finite. Step 1.2 supplies the uniform finite
  bound $(|\sigma|+1)\kappa_m$, and step 11.4 uses that finite bound as the
  Ramsey colour set. No absorption identity is applied to a finite
  $\kappa_m$.
- **AC is used** in the enumeration of $\mathcal Z$ and of $E \cap \beta$, in the choice of the ladders and the $j(\sigma)$, in the countable recursion of step 3.3, and in the Ramsey/Erdős–Rado step; it is declared as a dependency and no choice-free reading is claimed.
