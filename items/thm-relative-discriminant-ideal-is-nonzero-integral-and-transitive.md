## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathbb Q\subseteq K\subseteq L$ be number fields. The relative discriminant $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is a nonzero integral ideal of $\mathcal O_K$. If $d_K$ is the signed discriminant of an integral basis of $\mathcal O_K$, then, as ideals of $\mathbb Z$,
$$\mathfrak d_{L/\mathbb Q}=(d_K)^{[L:K]}\,(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{L/K}),$$
where $N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak a:=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|$ and the last factor denotes the principal ideal of $\mathbb Z$ generated by this positive integer.

## Facts & Assumptions


**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a tower of number fields $\mathbb Q\subseteq K\subseteq L$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice asserts that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] For finite separable $L/K$, $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is generated by determinants of relative trace Gram matrices of integral $K$-bases ([[def-relative-discriminant-ideal-of-a-number-field-extension]]).

[F2] The relative trace pairing is nondegenerate in a finite separable extension ([[thm-trace-form-is-nondegenerate-iff-separable]]).

[F3] Norm and trace are determinants and traces of multiplication operators and are transitive in towers ([[thm-basic-laws-for-field-norm-and-trace]]).

[F4] A finite torsion-free module over a Dedekind domain is a direct sum of invertible fractional ideals, hence projective ([[thm-finite-torsionfree-modules-over-dedekind-domains]]); the ring of integers of a number field is Dedekind ([[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]]).

[F5] The integral closure of a Noetherian integrally closed domain in a finite separable field extension is finite as a module ([[thm-finite-integral-closure-in-a-finite-separable-extension]]).

[F6] Every algebraic number has a nonzero integer multiple that is an algebraic integer ([[thm-clearing-denominators-for-an-algebraic-number]]), and algebraic integers are closed under products ([[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]]).

[F7] For a nonzero prime $\mathfrak p$ of a Dedekind domain, its localization is a discrete valuation ring ([[lem-dedekind-localisation-at-nonzero-prime-is-dvr]]).

[F8] The ring of integers of a number field is a free $\mathbb Z$-module of rank its degree ([[thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree]]).

[F9] Nonzero integral ideals factor into finitely many prime ideals, and pairwise comaximal ideal powers satisfy the Chinese remainder theorem ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]], [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]).

[F10] The discriminant of a basis is its trace-Gram determinant and also the square of the embedding determinant ([[def-discriminant-of-a-number-field-basis-and-order]], [[thm-discriminant-as-an-embedding-determinant]]).

[F11] The number-field completions identify $L\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak p}$ with the product of the completions above $\mathfrak p$, compatibly with trace and norm ([[def-completion-of-a-number-field-at-a-prime]], [[lem-number-field-completions-as-local-polynomial-factors]]).

[F12] For a nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak a$, $N\mathfrak a=|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|$ is finite ([[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]], [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]]). Norms multiply over ideal products ([[thm-ideal-norm-is-multiplicative]]), and a prime ideal above the rational prime $p$ has norm $p^f$ for its residue degree $f$ ([[cor-norm-of-a-prime-ideal]]).

[F13] The column-Hermite algorithm terminates and returns a basis of the integer column lattice ([[alg-integer-column-hermite-normal-form]], [[thm-integer-column-hnf-terminates-and-is-canonical]]).

[F14] In a separable extension, trace is the sum over field embeddings, and norm is the product ([[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]]).

[F15] The $I$-adic completion of a module is the inverse limit of its quotients by powers of $I$ ([[def-adic-completion-of-a-module]]).

[F16] A number field is finite over $\mathbb Q$, and its ring of integers is the integral closure of $\mathbb Z$ ([[def-number-field]], [[def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]]).

[F17] For nonzero $\alpha\in\mathcal O_K$, $N((\alpha))=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)|$ ([[thm-principal-ideal-norm-is-absolute-field-norm]]).

## Proof

1.1 All number-field extensions are finite separable by [F16] and characteristic zero. For any such $L/K$, a $K$-basis of integral elements exists: start with a $K$-basis and multiply each vector by a nonzero integer that clears its algebraic denominators by [F6]. Its relative trace Gram determinant is nonzero by [F2]. Each entry belongs to $\mathcal O_K$, since products of algebraic integers are integral by [F6], their relative traces are sums of integral conjugates by [F14], and those traces lie in $K$, hence in $\mathcal O_K$ by [F16]. Therefore [F1] generates a nonzero integral ideal. The same argument applies to $L/\mathbb Q$ and $K/\mathbb Q$. [F1, F2, F6, F14, F16, given, algebra, choose]

1.2 Fix a rational prime $p$. Factor $p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid p}\mathfrak p^{e_{\mathfrak p}}$. The factors are pairwise comaximal. For each $m$, [F9] identifies $\mathcal O_K/p^m\mathcal O_K$ with $\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid p}\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}$. If $s\notin\mathfrak p$, then $(s)+\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}=\mathcal O_K$: raise a Bezout relation $sa+b=1$, with $b\in\mathfrak p$, to a power at least $me_{\mathfrak p}$. Thus localization induces $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}\cong(\mathcal O_K)_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}(\mathcal O_K)_{\mathfrak p}$. Indeed, if $a/1$ lies in the localized ideal, then $sa\in\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}$ for some $s\notin\mathfrak p$; since $s$ is invertible modulo that ideal, $a\in\mathfrak p^{me_{\mathfrak p}}$. The reverse inclusion is immediate. The exponents $me_{\mathfrak p}$ are cofinal in the $\mathfrak p$-adic powers, so taking inverse limits identifies $\mathcal O_K\otimes\mathbb Z_p$ with the finite product of the completed local integer rings at $\mathfrak p\mid p$. Here [F15] supplies the inverse-limit completion, and [F8] identifies the inverse limit modulo $p^m$ with the tensor product because $\mathcal O_K$ is finite free over $\mathbb Z$. The same argument applies to $\mathcal O_L\otimes\mathbb Z_p$. [A1, F7, F8, F9, F15, given, algebra]

1.3 Put $A_{\mathfrak p}=(\mathcal O_K)_{\mathfrak p}$ and $B_{\mathfrak p}=\mathcal O_L\otimes_{\mathcal O_K}A_{\mathfrak p}$. By [F5], $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is finite over $A_{\mathfrak p}$. It is torsion-free: as a localization of the domain $\mathcal O_L$, it embeds in $L$. The natural map $L\to L\otimes_K K_{\mathfrak p}$ is injective, since extending a $K$-basis of $L$ gives a $K_{\mathfrak p}$-basis after scalar extension; [F11] identifies this tensor product with a finite product of fields. By [F4], $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is a finite direct sum of invertible fractional ideals over $A_{\mathfrak p}$. By [F7], this ring is a DVR, so each such ideal is principal: after scaling a fractional ideal to an integral ideal, an element of least uniformizer valuation divides every element and generates it. Hence $B_{\mathfrak p}$ is free. Its rank is $[L:K]$, because tensoring with $K$ gives the $K$-vector space $L$. Thus it has a local basis of $n=[L:K]$ elements. [A1, F4, F5, F7, F11, given, algebra]

2.1 For this rational prime $p$, the finite product of local modules in step 1.2 has rank $n$ over every factor. For each prime $\mathfrak p\mid p$, the quotient $\mathcal O_L/\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L$ is unchanged by localization at $\mathfrak p$, since every denominator outside $\mathfrak p$ acts on it as a nonzero scalar; it is therefore an $n$-dimensional vector space over $\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p$. Choose a basis of this quotient. The ideals $\mathfrak p\mathcal O_L$ are pairwise comaximal, so the Chinese remainder theorem [F9] lifts these finite lists to global integral elements $z_1,\ldots,z_n\in\mathcal O_L$. At each $\mathfrak p$, their coordinate matrix relative to a local basis has invertible reduction modulo the maximal ideal; its determinant is therefore a unit, and the adjugate formula makes the matrix invertible over the local DVR. Thus the same $z_i$ form a $K$-basis of $L$ and a basis of $B_{\mathfrak p}$ for every $\mathfrak p\mid p$. [A1, F4, F5, F7, F9, F11, step 1.2, step 1.3, choose, algebra]

3.1 Put $\delta=\det(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(z_i z_j))$, which is nonzero by [F2]. At each prime $\mathfrak p\mid p$, any global integral $K$-basis has a change matrix over the local DVR relative to $z_i$, so its trace determinant is a multiple of $\delta$ by a square. Since $z_i$ itself occurs among the global bases, the ideal [F1] localizes to $(\delta)$ at every such $\mathfrak p$. [F1, F2, F7, F14, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Let $u_1,\ldots,u_d$ be an integral $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathcal O_K$. The products $u_jz_i$ form a $\mathbb Q$-basis of $L$ and an integral full-rank sublattice $M$ of $\mathcal O_L$. Expressing them in an integral basis of $\mathcal O_L$ gives a full-rank integer matrix. Its column-Hermite form is upper triangular with positive diagonal entries. Projection of the quotient lattice onto the last coordinate has index $h_{nn}$, and its kernel is the quotient from the upper-left $(n-1)$-dimensional block; induction shows that the full index is the product of the diagonal entries. Thus [F13] computes the exact index $[\mathcal O_L:M]$. Because the $z_i$ are bases at all $\mathfrak p\mid p$, $M\otimes\mathbb Z_p=\mathcal O_L\otimes\mathbb Z_p$, so this index is a $p$-adic unit. The trace-Gram matrix changes by $C^{t}GC$ under the integer coordinate matrix $C$, so the product-basis determinant is $d_L[\mathcal O_L:M]^2$ by [F10] and has the same $p$-adic valuation as $d_L$. [F8, F10, F13, step 2.1, algebra, choose]

4.1 For the product basis $u_jz_i$, let $V=(\tau(u_j))_{\tau,j}$ be the embedding matrix of $K$ into $\mathbb C$. For each embedding $\tau:K\to\mathbb C$, choose the $n$ embeddings $\rho:L\to\mathbb C$ extending $\tau$, and let $E_\tau=(\rho(z_i))_{\rho,i}$. By [F14], the relative trace Gram matrix $H=(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(z_i z_k))$ satisfies $\tau(H)=E_\tau^tE_\tau$, so $\det\tau(H)=\det(E_\tau)^2$. After ordering rows and columns by the embedding pairs, the embedding matrix of $L$ on the product basis is $\operatorname{diag}(E_\tau)_\tau(V\otimes I_n)$; its determinant is $\prod_\tau\det(E_\tau)\det(V)^n$. Squaring and applying [F10] shows that its trace-Gram determinant is $d_K^nN_{K/\mathbb Q}(\delta)$; [F3] and [F14] identify this norm as the determinant/product over the embeddings. By step 3.1, $(\delta)$ agrees with $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ at every prime above $p$. By [F17], [F12], and prime ideal factorization [F9], this gives $v_p(N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\delta))=v_p(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{L/K})$. [F3, F9, F10, F12, F14, F17, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Let $\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_{[L:\mathbb Q]}$ be an integral $\mathbb Z$-basis of $\mathcal O_L$, which exists by [F8]. Every integral $\mathbb Q$-basis $x_1,\ldots,x_{[L:\mathbb Q]}$ has an integer coordinate matrix $C$ in this basis, because its entries lie in $\mathcal O_L$. Since it is a $\mathbb Q$-basis, $\det C\ne0$; its trace-Gram determinant is $d_L(\det C)^2$ by [F10]. The basis $\beta_i$ itself occurs among the generators [F1], so $\mathfrak d_{L/\mathbb Q}=(d_L)$. The same argument gives $\mathfrak d_{K/\mathbb Q}=(d_K)$. Steps 3.2 and 4.1 show for the fixed rational prime $p$ that the valuations of $\mathfrak d_{L/\mathbb Q}$ and $(d_K)^{[L:K]}(N_{K/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak d_{L/K})$ agree. Since $p$ was arbitrary and nonzero ideals of $\mathbb Z$ are determined by their prime valuations, the stated tower identity follows. [F1, F8, F9, F10, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
