## Statement

Let $X$ be a scheme, let $L$ be an invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module
([[def-invertible-sheaf]]), let $s\in\Gamma(X,L)$ be a global section, and let
$U\subseteq X$ be an affine open subscheme ([[def-affine-open-subscheme]]).
With $X_s=\{\,x\in X:\text{the image of }s\text{ in }L\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}
\kappa(x)\text{ is nonzero}\,\}$, the intersection
$$U\cap X_s$$
is an affine open subscheme of $X$. The statement includes a nowhere-vanishing
section, for which $U\cap X_s=U$, and the zero section, for which
$U\cap X_s=\varnothing$.

Moreover, if $M$ is a second invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module and
$t\in\Gamma(X,M)$, then
$$X_s\cap X_t=X_{s\otimes t}$$
inside $X$, where $s\otimes t\in\Gamma(X,L\otimes M)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$, an invertible sheaf $L$ on $X$, a section $s\in\Gamma(X,L)$, and an affine open $U\subseteq X$; in the second part also an invertible sheaf $M$ and a section $t\in\Gamma(X,M)$.

[F1] An invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module is a locally free sheaf of rank exactly one; over an affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}R$ it corresponds to an $R$-module $N$ whose localisation $N_{\mathfrak p}$ at every prime is free of rank one over $R_{\mathfrak p}$. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]])

[F2] An affine open subscheme of $X$ is an open subscheme which, with its restricted structure sheaf, is an affine scheme $\operatorname{Spec}R$; the empty scheme is affine, being the spectrum of the zero ring. ([[def-affine-open-subscheme]], [[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]])

[F3] *(algebra)* For an $R$-module $N$, the tensor algebra $T_R(N)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}N^{\otimes_R n}$ has $T_R(N)\otimes_R R'\cong T_{R'}(N\otimes_R R')$ for every ring map $R\to R'$, because tensor products commute with direct sums; and for any ideal $J\subseteq T_R(N)$ with image $J'\subseteq T_{R'}(N\otimes_R R')$, the base change of the quotient is the quotient by the image, $(T_R(N)/J)\otimes_R R'\cong T_{R'}(N\otimes_R R')/J'$, because $-\otimes_RR'$ is right exact. These identifications are compatible with a further base change $R'\to R''$.

[F4] *(algebra)* Let $R$ be a local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, let $N$ be a free $R$-module of rank one, and let $s\in N$ with $s\notin\mathfrak mN$. Then $s$ generates $N$: the class of $s$ generates the one-dimensional vector space $N/\mathfrak mN$, so Nakayama's lemma applies to the finitely generated module $N/\langle s\rangle$, whose localisation $N/\langle s\rangle\otimes_R\kappa$ vanishes; and a free module of rank one generated by one element is free on that element.

[F5] *(algebra)* Let $R\to R'$ be a ring map and let $N'$ be an $R'$-module. If the images of an element $s\in N'$ generate $N'_{\mathfrak p}$ for every prime $\mathfrak p\subseteq R'$, then $s$ generates $N'$, because the quotient $N'/\langle s\rangle$ has all localisations at primes zero.

[F6] *(schemes)* A morphism $f:Y\to Z$ of schemes is an isomorphism if and only if there is an open cover $Z=\bigcup_i Z_i$ with $f^{-1}(Z_i)\to Z_i$ an isomorphism for every $i$; consequently a morphism whose image lies in an open subscheme $V\subseteq Z$ factors through $V$, and if it becomes an isomorphism over an open cover of $V$, then $Y\to V$ is an isomorphism.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: reduce to the affine algebra problem $R$, $N$, $s\in N$, build the quotient $B$ of the tensor algebra by $s-1$, and identify $\operatorname{Spec}B$ with the locus where $s$ generates $N$ chartwise.

1.1 Reduction. Write $U=\operatorname{Spec}R$ by [F2] and put $N=\Gamma(U,L|_U)$. By [F1] the $R$-module $N$ is locally free of rank one, so $N_{\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}$ for every prime $\mathfrak p$; as the fibre of $L$ at the point $\mathfrak p$ is $N\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, the point lies in $U\cap X_s$ exactly when the image of $s$ in $N\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is nonzero. Hence it suffices to prove that for a commutative ring $R$, a locally free rank-one $R$-module $N$ and an element $s\in N$, the set $V=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R:s\notin\mathfrak p N_{\mathfrak p}\}$ is an affine open subscheme of $\operatorname{Spec}R$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 The algebra $B$. Let $T=T_R(N)=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}N^{\otimes_R n}$ be the tensor algebra, and let $J\subseteq T$ be the two-sided ideal generated by all elements $m\otimes n-n\otimes m$ with $m,n\in N$ and by the element $s-1\in T$, where $s\in N=T_1$. Put $B=T/J$. Then $B$ is a commutative $R$-algebra, because the degree-one generators of $T$ commute modulo $J$ and every element of $T$ is a sum of products of degree-one elements; it is generated as an $R$-algebra by the image of $N$. The relation $s=1$ need not preserve the tensor grading, and no grading on $B$ is used. [F3, algebra]

1.3 The tensor identity. Let $M$ be a second invertible sheaf and let $t\in\Gamma(X,M)$; then $L\otimes M$ is invertible, and at every point $x$ the fibre of $L\otimes M$ is $(L\otimes\kappa(x))\otimes_{\kappa(x)}(M\otimes\kappa(x))$, a tensor product of one-dimensional vector spaces, in which the image of $s\otimes t$ is the product of the images of $s$ and of $t$. Hence the image of $s\otimes t$ is nonzero exactly when both are, so $X_{s\otimes t}=X_s\cap X_t$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Base change of $B$. For every ring map $R\to R'$ the identifications of [F3] give a canonical isomorphism $B\otimes_R R'\cong T_{R'}(N\otimes_R R')$ modulo the ideal generated by the images of the generators of $J$, namely by the commutators and by the image of $s-1$; these identifications are compatible with a further base change. In the special case that $N=R'$ is free on the basis element $s$ one has $T_{R'}(N)=R'[s]$ and $B\otimes_R R'=R'[s]/(s-1)\cong R'$. [F3, step 1.2]

3.1 Empty fibre where $s$ vanishes. Let $\mathfrak p\subseteq R$ be a prime with $s\in\mathfrak pN_{\mathfrak p}$, and put $\kappa=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, $N'=N\otimes_R\kappa$. Then $N'$ is one-dimensional over $\kappa$ by [F1], the image $s'$ of $s$ in $N'$ is zero, and step 2.1 gives $B\otimes_R\kappa=\kappa[u]/(0-1)=0$, the quotient of the polynomial algebra $\kappa[u]$ by the unit relation $s'-1=-1$. Hence the fibre $\operatorname{Spec}(B\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p))$ of $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ over $\mathfrak p$ is empty. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Nonempty fibre where $s$ generates. Let $\mathfrak p\subseteq R$ be a prime with $s\notin\mathfrak pN_{\mathfrak p}$. Since $N_{\mathfrak p}$ is free of rank one over the local ring $R_{\mathfrak p}$, [F4] shows that $s$ is a basis of $N_{\mathfrak p}$; localising the isomorphism of step 2.1 at $\mathfrak p$ gives $B_{\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}[s]/(s-1)\cong R_{\mathfrak p}$, so $B_{\mathfrak p}\neq0$ and $B\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p)\neq0$. [F4, step 2.1]

4.1 The morphism factors through $V$. By steps 3.1 and 3.2 the fibre of $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ is empty exactly over the primes outside $V$; in particular the set-theoretic image is contained in $V$, so by [F6] the affine morphism factors as a morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R$ into the open subscheme $V$. [F6, step 3.1, step 3.2]

5.1 It is an isomorphism onto $V$. Let $\operatorname{Spec}R'\subseteq V$ be an affine open, and let $N'=N\otimes_RR'$. For every prime $\mathfrak p\subseteq R'$ the image of $s$ in $N_{\mathfrak p}$ generates, so $s$ generates $N'$ by [F5]; since $N'$ is locally free of rank one, $N'$ is free on the basis element $s$. Step 2.1 then gives $B\otimes_RR'\cong R'[s]/(s-1)\cong R'$, that is, the base change of the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to V$ of step 4.1 over the affine open $\operatorname{Spec}R'$ is an isomorphism. The affine opens $\operatorname{Spec}R'\subseteq V$ cover $V$, so by the local criterion of [F6] the morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to V$ is an isomorphism; in particular $V$ is affine, with coordinate ring $B$, and step 1.1 identifies $U\cap X_s$ with $V$. [F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Boundaries. If $s$ is nowhere vanishing then $X_s=X$ and $U\cap X_s=U$ is affine by hypothesis. If $s=0$ then $s\in\mathfrak pN_{\mathfrak p}$ for every prime, so $V=\varnothing$ by step 3.1, which is affine because it is the spectrum of the zero ring by [F2], while step 1.3 gives $X_0\cap X_t=\varnothing=X_{0\otimes t}$. Together with steps 1.1-5.1 this proves both claims of the Statement. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 5.1]
\qed
