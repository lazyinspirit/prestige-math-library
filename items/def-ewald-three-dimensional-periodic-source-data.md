## Definition

Let $A\in GL_3(\mathbb R)$, let $\Lambda=A\mathbb Z^3$, let
$V=|\det A|$, and let $\mathbb T_A=\mathbb R^3/\Lambda$. These are the
full-rank lattice and covolume conventions of
[[def-full-rank-lattice-covolume-and-dual-lattice]]. The reciprocal lattice is
$\Lambda^*=A^{-T}\mathbb Z^3$. For $k\in\mathbb Z^3$ write
$$\kappa_k=2\pi A^{-T}k,\qquad e_k(x)=e^{i\kappa_k\cdot x}.$$
Thus $e_k$ is a character of $\mathbb T_A$; the ordinary Fourier frequency is
$A^{-T}k$, while $\kappa_k$ is the angular physical frequency.

A finite point-source configuration consists of an integer $n\ge0$, points
$x_1,\ldots,x_n\in\mathbb T_A$ that are pairwise distinct when $n>1$, and
real charges $q_1,\ldots,q_n$. It is **neutral** when
$\sum_{i=1}^n q_i=0$. For a smooth periodic test function $\varphi$, the
point-source distribution is the linear functional
$$\langle\rho,\varphi\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^nq_i\varphi(x_i).$$
Here smooth means that the periodic lift to $\mathbb R^3$ has continuous
derivatives of every order, and the test-function topology is generated by the
seminorms $\max_{x\in A[0,1]^3}|\partial^\beta\varphi(x)|$ over all derivative
multi-indices $\beta$. A periodic distribution is a continuous complex-linear
functional for this topology. Its distributional Laplacian is defined by
$\langle-\Delta u,\varphi\rangle=\langle u,-\Delta\varphi\rangle$.

For a smooth periodic $\varphi$, its Fourier coefficient is
$$\widehat\varphi(k)=\frac1V\int_{A(0,1]^3}\varphi(x)e^{-i\kappa_k\cdot x}\,dx.$$
The source coefficient is
$$\widehat\rho(k)=\frac1V\sum_{i=1}^nq_i e^{-i\kappa_k\cdot x_i};$$
in particular, neutral data have $\widehat\rho(0)=0$. For a periodic
distribution $u$, define $\widehat u(k)=V^{-1}\langle u,e_{-k}\rangle$. The
**mean-zero periodic Poisson solution** is a periodic distribution $u$ satisfying
$-\Delta u=4\pi\rho$ and $\widehat u(0)=0$. This definition fixes the
zero-mode convention; no nonneutral periodic solution is implicit.

The Coulomb energy convention is the half-sum of each source charge times the
regular part of its potential, after subtracting its own singular term
$q_i/|x-x_i|$. The resulting finite part is called the **renormalized
self-energy convention**. Its Ewald formula is established in this development.

## Remarks

- The Coulomb normalization is $-\Delta u=4\pi\rho$ in three dimensions. No
  dielectric factor, surface term, nonneutralizing background, or alternative
  boundary condition is part of this definition.
- The dual lattice in the Poisson formula uses exponent $2\pi i\xi\cdot x$;
  multiplying its frequency $\xi=A^{-T}k$ by $2\pi$ gives $\kappa_k$.
