## Statement

Put $C=C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)$, give $C$ the uniform-on-compacts topology,
and write $\pi_t(f)=f(t)$. Then
$$\mathcal B(C)=\sigma(\pi_t:t\ge0)=\sigma(\pi_q:q\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,\infty)).$$
Here each expression on the right denotes the smallest sigma-algebra on $C$
making every displayed coordinate map measurable. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The path space $C$, its uoc metric $d$, and its coordinate maps as in the Statement. Write $\mathcal G$ for the sigma-algebra generated by all coordinates and $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$ for that generated by the nonnegative rational coordinates.

[F1] The uoc metric is $$d(f,g)=\sum_{n\ge1}2^{-n}(1\wedge M_n(f,g)),\qquad M_n(f,g)=\max_{0\le t\le n}|f(t)-g(t)|,$$ and its topology is compact convergence. [[def-uniform-on-compacts-metric-on-continuous-path-space]]

[F2] A Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets, and a family of sets has a unique smallest generated sigma-algebra. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]] [[def-generated-sigma-algebra]] [[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]

[F3] Countable suprema and pointwise limits of measurable real functions are measurable; finite sums, scalar multiples, positive parts, and absolute values preserve measurability. [[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]] [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]]

[F4] The rationals are countable and dense in the reals. There is a fixed bijection between $\mathbb N^2$ and $\mathbb N$, recursion constructs nested finite codes, finite products of countable sets are countable, and a subset of a countable set is countable. [[thm-rationals-countable]] [[lem-rat-embeds-dense]] [[thm-n-cross-n-countable]] [[thm-recursion]] [[thm-product-of-countable]] [[lem-subset-of-countable]]

[F5] Closed bounded real intervals are compact, continuous real functions on them are uniformly continuous, and for every $\delta>0$ there is $m\ge1$ with $1/m<\delta$. [[thm-heine-borel-rn]] [[thm-heine-cantor-metric]] [[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]]

[F6] Every nonnegative real has an integer part, and $2^{-j}\to0$. The geometric-series formula controls every tail $\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}$. [[lem-integer-part]] [[lem-geometric-sequence-null]] [[thm-geometric-series]]

[F7] Metric balls form a base for the metric topology. [[def-metric-topology]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $t\ge0$ and choose an integer $n\ge1$ with $t\le n$. If $\varepsilon>0$, then $$d(f,g)<2^{-n}\min\{1,\varepsilon\}$$ implies $2^{-n}(1\wedge M_n(f,g))<2^{-n}\min\{1,\varepsilon\}$ and hence $|\pi_t(f)-\pi_t(g)|\le M_n(f,g)<\varepsilon$. Thus every $\pi_t$ is continuous, so it is Borel measurable. Minimality in [F2] gives $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}\subseteq\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal B(C)$. [F1, F2]

1.2 Fix $t\ge0$ and put $q_j=2^{-j}\lfloor2^jt\rfloor$. Then $q_j\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,\infty)$ and $0\le t-q_j<2^{-j}$, so $q_j\to t$ by [F6]. For every $f\in C$, continuity gives $\pi_{q_j}(f)\to\pi_t(f)$. Every $\pi_{q_j}$ is $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$-measurable by definition, so [F3] makes $\pi_t$ measurable. Hence $\mathcal G\subseteq\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$ by minimality, and therefore $\mathcal G=\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$. [F2, F3, F6]

1.3 Let $D$ be the family of paths which, for some integers $N,m\ge1$, are affine on each interval $[k/m,(k+1)/m]$ for $0\le k<mN$, take rational values at all grid points $k/m$, and are constant after $N$. This family is countable without choice. Indeed, fix bijections $e:\mathbb N\to\mathbb Q$ and $p:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N^2$. Nested use of $p^{-1}$ codes every finite sequence of naturals by one natural (and repeated application of $p$ decodes it); composing entries with $e$ codes every finite rational sequence. A further finite nesting codes $(N,m)$ together with that sequence, giving a surjection from a subset of $\mathbb N$ onto $D$. Thus [F4] makes $D$ at most countable. [F4, construct]

1.4 The family $D$ is uoc dense. Given $f\in C$ and $\varepsilon>0$, use [F6] to choose $N$ with $\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}<\varepsilon/2$. By [F5], $f$ is uniformly continuous on $[0,N]$; choose $m$ so that the oscillation of $f$ over distances at most $1/m$ is less than $\varepsilon/4$. By rational density, choose rational $a_k$ with $|a_k-f(k/m)|<\varepsilon/4$ for the finitely many $0\le k\le mN$ (finite induction, not a choice principle), and let $g\in D$ interpolate these values and remain constant after $N$. On a grid interval, convex interpolation and the triangle inequality give $|g(t)-f(t)|<\varepsilon/2$. Consequently $$d(f,g)\le\sum_{n=1}^N2^{-n}M_n(f,g)+\sum_{n>N}2^{-n}<\varepsilon.$$ This also covers $t=0$ and $t=N$. [F1, F4, F5, F6, construct]

1.5 Fix $f\in C$. For $n\ge1$, continuity and rational density give $$M_n(g,f)=\sup_{q\in\mathbb Q\cap[0,n]}|\pi_q(g)-f(q)|.$$ Enumerating that rational set, [F3] makes $M_n(\,·\,,f)$ $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$-measurable. The finite partial sums of the metric formula are measurable by [F3] and converge pointwise to $d(\,·\,,f)$, so this distance is measurable. Hence every metric ball, with an arbitrary center, belongs to $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$. [F1, F3, F4]

2.1 The balls $B(h,r)$ with $h\in D$ and positive rational $r$ form a countable base: countability follows from [F4], while density from step 1.4 and the triangle inequality put such a ball around every point inside any prescribed open ball. For an open $U$, let $\mathscr V_U$ be the subfamily of these basic balls which are contained in $U$. It is countable by [F4], every member belongs to $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$ by step 1.5, and $U=\bigcup\mathscr V_U$. Thus every open set is in $\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$; [F2] yields $\mathcal B(C)\subseteq\mathcal G_{\mathbb Q}$. Combining this with steps 1.1 and 1.2 proves all stated equalities. The zero path shows $C$ and $D$ are nonempty; singleton and zero-time coordinates cause no exception, and no countable family of nonempty sets was selected. [F2, F4, F7, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.4, step 1.5] ∎

## Source notes

Van der Vaart and Wellner use the standard fact that the Borel sigma-algebra
on a separable continuous-function space is generated by evaluations. The
proof above supplies the complete uoc and rational-coordinate argument,
including an explicit choice-free countable basis.
