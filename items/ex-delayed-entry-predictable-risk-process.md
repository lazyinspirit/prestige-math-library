## Example

Fix $0\le L<T$ and $\lambda>0$. Let $W$ be exponential with rate $\lambda$,
put $U=L+W$, and observe one event at $U$. With
$N_t=\mathbf1_{\{U\le t\}}$ and the natural filtration
$\mathcal F_t=\sigma(N_s:0\le s\le t)$, use the left-open entry and
right-closed event convention
$$Y_0:=0,\qquad Y_t:=\mathbf1_{\{L<t\le U\}}=\mathbf1_{\{t>L\}}(1-N_{t-})\quad(t>0),\qquad A_t=\int_0^tY_s\lambda\,ds=\lambda((t-L)^+\wedge W).$$
Then $Y$ is predictable, $A$ is a continuous integrable compensator, and
$M=N-A$ is a square-integrable martingale on $[0,T]$. The value $Y_L=0$ says
the subject is not yet at risk at entry; $Y_U=1$ records the subject as at
risk immediately before the event jump.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $0\le L<T$, $\lambda>0$, and a waiting time $W$ with density $f_W(w)=\lambda e^{-\lambda w}$ on $[0,\infty)$, hence $P(W>w)=e^{-\lambda w}$; use the natural filtration of the one-event process.

[F1] Predictability is measurability for the sigma-algebra generated by $(s,t]\times B$, $B\in\mathcal F_s$, and time-zero sets; it is closed under pointwise limits and products, and deterministic Borel time functions are predictable. ([[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]])

[F2] Under the finite-horizon model contract, $A=\int Y\lambda ds$ is the compensator when $N-A$ is a square-integrable martingale. ([[def-counting-process-intensity-and-compensator-contract]])

[F3] Conditional expectation is characterized by its event integrals on the conditioning sigma-algebra. ([[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]])

[A1] AC is inherited from conditional expectation in the continuous-time martingale convention. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Verification

1.1 The process $N$ is adapted, càdlàg, has one unit jump, and starts at zero. On dyadic time grids on $[0,\infty)$, left-step processes with value $N_{t_j}$ on $(t_j,t_{j+1}]$ are predictable by [F1] and converge pointwise to $N_{t-}$ for each $t>0$, so the left-limit process on $(0,\infty)$ is predictable. Since $t\mapsto\mathbf1_{\{t>L\}}$ is deterministic Borel, the displayed formula makes $Y$ predictable on $(0,\infty)$; its separately assigned value $Y_0=0$ is predictable on the time-zero slice. This also gives $Y_L=0$ and $Y_U=1$. [F1, given, construct]

1.2 Direct integration gives $A_t=\lambda((t-L)^+\wedge W)$, so $A$ is continuous and increasing, $A_0=0$, and $A_t\le\lambda T$. Left Riemann sums make $A_t$ $\mathcal F_t$-measurable; its continuous adapted left-step approximations are predictable by [F1]. The exponential density gives $\mathbb E A_T=\lambda(\int_0^{T-L}u\lambda e^{-\lambda u}du+(T-L)e^{-\lambda(T-L)})=1-e^{-\lambda(T-L)}<\infty$, and for every $t>L$ the same calculation with $T$ replaced by $t$ gives $\mathbb E A_t=1-e^{-\lambda(t-L)}=\mathbb P(U\le t)=\mathbb E N_t$. Thus the compensator integrability requirements in [F2] hold. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 If $s<L$ and $t\le L$, then $N$ and $A$ are zero on $[s,t]$; if $s<L<t$, then $\mathcal F_s$ is trivial and step 1.2 gives $\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_s]=0=M_s$. If $s\ge L$ and $U\le s$, the martingale increment is zero. On $\{U>s\}$ the event-free history gives $W>s-L$, and the residual survival ratio is $\mathbb P(W-(s-L)>u\mid W>s-L)=e^{-\lambda u}$. With $h=t-s$, the conditional expected event increment is $1-e^{-\lambda h}$, and the same residual density gives $\lambda\mathbb E[\min(h,W-(s-L))\mid W>s-L]=\lambda(\int_0^h u\lambda e^{-\lambda u}du+h e^{-\lambda h})=1-e^{-\lambda h}$. Thus every conditional increment has mean zero. Finally $|M_t|\le1+\lambda T$, so $M$ is square-integrable and satisfies [F2]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
