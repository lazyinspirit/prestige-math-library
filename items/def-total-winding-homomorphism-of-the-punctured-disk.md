## Definition

Let $n\ge1$, let $D^2=\{z\in\mathbb C:|z|\le1\}$, let $Q_n=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ be
the base configuration of the boundary-fixed punctured disk
([[def-boundary-fixed-mapping-class-group-of-a-punctured-disk]]), put
$X=D^2\setminus Q_n$ with basepoint $d$, and identify
$\pi_1(X,d)=F_n=\langle x_1,\dots,x_n\rangle$ with the free group
([[def-free-group]]) through the standard meridians $x_i$ of
[[def-standard-meridians-of-a-punctured-disk]]
([[thm-the-punctured-disk-fundamental-group-is-free-on-standard-meridians]]).
The **total winding homomorphism** is the unique group homomorphism
([[def-group-homomorphism]])
$$\omega:F_n\longrightarrow\mathbb Z,\qquad \omega(x_i)=1\quad(1\le i\le n),$$
whose existence and uniqueness come from the universal property of the free
basis. On a word in the $x_i^{\pm1}$ it is the sum of the exponents, and
$\omega(x_1\cdots x_n)=n$. It is surjective and its kernel is the subgroup of
words of exponent sum $0$.

**Clauses.** (1) $\omega$ is well defined and independent of all choices,
because the standard meridians form a free basis. (2) **Invariance under the
braid action:** $\omega\circ\rho(\beta)=\omega$ for every $\beta\in B_n$, where
$\rho$ is the Artin representation of
[[def-the-artin-representation-on-a-free-group]]; and, under AC,
$\omega\circ h_*=\omega$ for every homeomorphism representative $h$ of a braid
mapping class.

**Caveats.** The functional $\omega$ is the winding about the punctures *in
total*, not about a single puncture: no winding functional about a single
$q_i$ is used on this page.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, the punctured disk $X=D^2\setminus Q_n$, the basepoint $d$, the identification $F_n\cong\pi_1(X,d)$, $x_i\mapsto[x_i]$, and a braid word $\beta\in B_n$.

[F1] The classes $[x_1],\dots,[x_n]$ form a free basis of $\pi_1(X,d)$; by the universal property of the free group, every function $\{x_1,\dots,x_n\}\to G$ into a group $G$ extends to a unique homomorphism $F_n\to G$ ([[def-free-group]], [[thm-the-punctured-disk-fundamental-group-is-free-on-standard-meridians]]).

[F2] The Artin representation $\rho:B_n\to\operatorname{Aut}(F_n)$ is the unique homomorphism with $\rho(\sigma_i)(x_i)=x_ix_{i+1}x_i^{-1}$, $\rho(\sigma_i)(x_{i+1})=x_i$ and $\rho(\sigma_i)(x_j)=x_j$ for $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$; the braid group $B_n$ of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] is generated by $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ ([[def-the-artin-representation-on-a-free-group]], [[def-artin-automorphisms-of-the-free-group]]).

[F3] Assuming AC, for every braid word $\beta$ the automorphism of $F_n$ induced by the mapping class of $\beta$ under the identification of [F1] equals $\rho(\beta)$ ([[prop-the-geometric-action-on-meridians-is-the-artin-representation]], [[def-boundary-fixed-mapping-class-group-of-a-punctured-disk]]).

[F4] AC is the assertion that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Well-definedness and basic properties.* By [F1] the assignment $x_i\mapsto1$ extends to a unique homomorphism $\omega:F_n\to\mathbb Z$, so $\omega$ is well defined and independent of every choice made in the definition of the standard meridians. A word $x_{i_1}^{\varepsilon_1}\cdots x_{i_m}^{\varepsilon_m}$ has image $\sum_{j=1}^m\varepsilon_j$ by the homomorphism law, the kernel of $\omega$ is therefore exactly the set of words of exponent sum $0$, and $\omega(x_1)=1$ shows that $\omega$ is surjective. [F1]

2.1 *Invariance under the Artin action.* The set $H=\{\beta\in B_n:\omega\circ\rho(\beta)=\omega\}$ is a subgroup of $B_n$: $\rho$ is a homomorphism, $\rho(1)=\operatorname{id}$ gives $\omega\circ\rho(1)=\omega$, and if $\omega\circ\rho(\beta)=\omega$ and $\omega\circ\rho(\beta')=\omega$ then $\omega\circ\rho(\beta\beta')=(\omega\circ\rho(\beta))\circ\rho(\beta') =\omega\circ\rho(\beta')=\omega$ and $\omega\circ\rho(\beta^{-1})=\omega\circ\rho(\beta)^{-1}=\omega$, because $\rho(\beta)$ is an automorphism. By [F2] it suffices to show $\sigma_i\in H$ for every $i$. For $\rho(\sigma_i)$, the images of the basis elements $x_i\mapsto x_ix_{i+1}x_i^{-1}$, $x_{i+1}\mapsto x_i$ and $x_j\mapsto x_j$ all have exponent sum $1=\omega(x_j)$, so $\omega\circ\rho(\sigma_i)$ and $\omega$ agree on the free basis and hence, by [F1], on all of $F_n$. Therefore $H=B_n$, which is the first assertion of clause (2). [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 *Geometric representative.* Assume AC and let $h$ be a homeomorphism representative of the braid mapping class of $\beta$, i.e. a boundary-fixed homeomorphism whose mapping class is the image of $\beta$ ([[def-boundary-fixed-mapping-class-group-of-a-punctured-disk]]). By [F3] the automorphism $h_*$ induced on $\pi_1(X,d)=F_n$ equals $\rho(\beta)$, so $\omega\circ h_*=\omega\circ\rho(\beta)=\omega$ by step 2.1. AC is used only here, through [F3], and the statement of step 2.1 is choice free. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
