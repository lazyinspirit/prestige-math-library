## Example

Let $T=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega)$ with $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$, and let
$\zeta_{64}=e^{2\pi i/64}$. The gate $T$ has exact period eight, while
$R_z(\pi/16)$ is not an exact one-qubit Clifford+T word.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\omega=e^{i\pi/4}$, $\zeta_{64}=e^{i\pi/32}$, and
$R_z(\theta)=\operatorname{diag}(e^{-i\theta/2},e^{i\theta/2})$.

[F1] [[lem-qc-clifford-t-entry-ring]]: every exact one-qubit Clifford+T word has entries in $D=\mathbb Z[\zeta_8,1/2]\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$.

[F2] [[thm-cyclotomic-polynomials-are-irreducible-over-the-rationals]]: the cyclotomic polynomial $\Phi_n$ is irreducible over $\mathbb Q$.

[F3] [[thm-cyclotomic-polynomials-are-monic-integer-polynomials-of-degree-euler-totient]]: $\deg\Phi_n=\varphi(n)$.

## Verification


1.1 Since $\omega^8=1$, direct matrix powering gives $T^8=\operatorname{diag}(1,\omega^8)=I$. No smaller positive power is the identity because $\omega$ has order eight. [given, algebra]

2.1 The lower-right entry of $R_z(\pi/16)$ is $e^{i\pi/32}=\zeta_{64}$. If an exact H/T word equaled this rotation, [F1] would put $\zeta_{64}$ in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)$. But $\zeta_8=\zeta_{64}^8$, so $\mathbb Q(\zeta_8)\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_{64})$; containing $\zeta_{64}$ in the smaller field would force equality. By [F2]–[F3], these fields have degrees $\varphi(8)=4$ and $\varphi(64)=32$ respectively: among residues modulo $2^m$, exactly the $2^{m-1}$ odd residues are coprime. The unequal degrees contradict equality, so no exact H/T word implements this rotation. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The example uses the necessary direction of the ring characterization only; it
does not rely on the converse theorem or on an approximation
algorithm.
