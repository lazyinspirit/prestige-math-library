## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $L$ be a nonsplit oriented smooth link in the three-sphere. Let $S,T$ be two finite systems of pairwise disjoint smooth spheres meeting $L$ transversely twice each, such that capping every region with standard trivial-arc balls gives nontrivial prime links. Here a prime capped link has no twice-punctured sphere splitting with two nontrivial capped factors. Retain the actual cap-ball parameters and boundary collars, the global oriented component names, and any distinguished root point away from the sphere boundaries.

There is a comparison of the two systems by finitely many sphere surgeries, suppression of trivial capped connectors, and passage to a common refinement. It gives a bijection of the nontrivial capped prime factors and actual oriented capped-factor isotopies. Every factor correspondence preserves its global component names. Each change of attachment description is a transfer of whole parameterized subtree balls along their same named oriented global component, together with interchange of successive subtrees on that component. Every other cap of a suppressed trivial connector is retained in its actual order, including at a connector of valence greater than two. The root point, cap endpoint pairings, and transported boundary collars are retained.

Suppose additionally that connected reduced alternating diagrams $D_0,D_1$ of the same oriented link have supplied the two sphere systems by diagrammatic splitting, together with connected reduced alternating diagrams for their capped prime factors. Identify their links with $L$ by a specified oriented link isotopy, thereby fixing the global component correspondence. Assume the following marked prime input: for every corresponding pair of capped prime diagrams needed in the comparison, a finite sequence of the prescribed flypes and sphere planar isotopies is supplied whose transported oriented component bijection is the one induced by the actual capped-factor isotopy. Then $D_0,D_1$ are related by finitely many of those flypes and sphere planar isotopies, with exactly the prescribed global oriented component correspondence. Cut locations may be corrected by those moves; no prime sequence relative to all attachment punctures is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The move convention is that of [[def-flype-crossing-ball-and-alternating-tangle-data]]: a flyping circle meets the diagram in a crossing and two other transverse points; the moving tangle is turned over, with projection reflection and reversal of its crossings. The opposite fixed disk is allowed. A crossing-free fixed tangle gives an entire flype, including the stated opposite-side view of the whole diagram. Orientations and closed components are carried.

[F2] A smooth embedded two-sphere in the three-sphere bounds smooth balls on both sides. ([[thm-smooth-schoenflies-for-embedded-two-sphere-in-s-three]])

[F3] A smooth isotopy with compact source extends to an ambient isotopy, with the specified fixed collars and support control. ([[lem-choice-free-isotopy-extension-for-a-compact-smooth-source]])

[F4] Smooth Jordan disks have disk parameters and prescribed collars; the finite disk and collar matching used below retains its marked intervals. ([[lem-choice-free-smooth-jordan-disk-parameters-and-prescribed-collars]])

[A1] Only the diagram-lifting conclusion assumes the supplied marked prime sequences in the Statement. This is an explicit hypothesis, not a supplier theorem or a conclusion of the geometric sphere-system comparison.

## Proof

**Given:** The actual nonsplit oriented link, finite sphere systems, cap parameters, component names and root data in the Statement; for the diagram conclusion, the given alternating prime-tree diagrams and the marked prime sequences of [A1].

1.1 A region is a component of the complement of the spheres, with boundary collars included. Each complementary component of a region is a ball. This follows by induction on the sphere system: each additional disjoint sphere separates a previously connected region, and in the ambient three-sphere each sphere bounds balls on both sides. The dual graph is a finite tree. Cap a boundary of a region by a ball containing an oriented trivial arc, with a specified standard chart matching the two link germs. The capped region is again a three-sphere containing its capped link. At a dual-tree edge record both boundary occurrences and their cap parameters, rather than identifying occurrences by a component name alone. [F2, F4]

1.2 A sphere meets only one global component. Indeed each closed component meets a separating sphere evenly, so two total punctures cannot have distinct component names. Moreover a capped region has at most one capped component bearing each global name. Its complementary balls remove disjoint intervals from that global component, and each cap reconnects the two ends of the same removed interval. Removing and reconnecting such disjoint intervals leaves one cyclic itinerary. Completely outside components disappear; they cannot create extra copies of a name inside the region. This observation applies to arbitrary valence, not just a chain decomposition. [F2, F4]

1.3 Thus a vertex has a capped component for each global component actually present there. An incident edge is attached at an interval on its named component. For any one global name, the vertices and edges carrying it form a connected subtree: these are precisely the regions visited by that circle and the boundary spheres it crosses. In particular every path used below to transfer an attachment stays on this subtree. It never changes the attaching component. [F2, F4]

1.4 First, a capped region of a nonsplit link is nonsplit. Let a sphere split the capped link. It misses each compact cap arc. In the cap's standard chart shrink its transverse coordinates by a positive factor, fixing its axial arc pointwise, until the ball misses that sphere. The axial endpoints and adjacent link germs are fixed. The vector field extends over a slightly larger neighborhood with a cutoff vanishing on the link; disjoint cap neighborhoods permit simultaneous shrinking. Pull the splitting sphere back. It now misses all original cap balls and lies in the actual region. Substituting the original complementary balls for the caps puts each entire substituted ball on one side of the sphere. Both sides still contain original link: a capped component contains an actual region strand or an actual closed region component, since cap arcs alone have disjoint endpoints and cannot form a component. The sphere would therefore split the original link, a contradiction. [F2, F3, F4]

1.5 Second, a capped unknot region, together with all its incident caps, is an ordered connector, rather than an erasable vertex. To see this with charts retained, isotope its oriented unknot to a round circle and carry all cap charts by that isotopy. Before doing so shrink their transverse coordinates as above. Their supports and slightly larger neighborhoods can be disjoint, so afterwards every cap lies in an arbitrarily small tubular neighborhood of its own interval on the circle. The intervals have their actual cyclic order. The transported charts are still the charts; no unidentified homeomorphism of their boundaries is introduced. One may use them directly as bead charts. If axial frames are desired, straighten each small chart within its own tube, correcting its normal frame by an angle lift on the interval. The map in tube coordinates is `(s,r,phi) -> (s,r,phi-t theta(s) chi(r))`; it fixes the axis and is a diffeomorphism for every `t`, since its derivative in `phi` is one. Its chart-end values are carried to the adjacent collars, rather than assumed zero. This changes neither interval order nor endpoint pairing. Shrinking before straightening makes the nonlinear chart corrections local, by the usual rescaling of a smooth embedding to its derivative. [F2, F3, F4]

1.6 Cut the circle at a distinguished cap. The other caps are now an ordered linear chain on a trivial oriented proper arc, with their entire charts attached. Substitute an arbitrary actual one-string subtree in each bead ball and carry it by its chart. The same maps extend over those whole balls and agree with the exterior collar maps. Consequently this is an isotopy of the substituted link, not a capping argument that has forgotten the subtrees. Replacing the distinguished cap by a neighboring factor inserts exactly this ordered chain on its same named component. A root point in the connector is retained on the corresponding connector interval throughout. [F2, F3, F4]

1.7 For completeness, the one-string triviality used here has its relative meaning. If capping a proper arc in a ball gives the unknot, its proper arc is boundary parallel. Take an unknot spanning disk transverse to the ball boundary. There is one intersection arc joining the two punctures, together with circles. A circle has a sphere-disk side avoiding that intersection arc; choose an innermost circle there. Its disk interior misses the spanning disk. Replace the spanning-disk subdisk bounded by the circle by that sphere disk and push off. This gives an embedded spanning disk with fewer intersection circles. After finitely many replacements only the one intersection arc remains. The two pieces of the spanning disk give boundary-parallelism disks for the proper arcs on the two sides. This also proves that splitting a capped unknot by a twice-punctured sphere gives two capped unknots. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 Put the two systems `S,T` transverse, first moving punctures apart in small link collars. No intersection circle then contains a puncture. On a `T` sphere meeting `S`, choose a disk `D` bounded by one intersection circle, with interior disjoint from `S`, containing at most one puncture. Such a disk exists: the disjoint circles on a sphere have at least two disjoint innermost disk regions, whereas the sphere has only two punctures. If there is only one circle, one of its two sides has at most one puncture. Let its boundary lie on `A` in `S`, and let its interior lie in the `S` region `R`. Let `Q` denote the region on the other side of `A`. [F2, F3, F4]

2.2 Surgery replaces `A` by the two spheres formed from its two disks and two parallel copies of `D`. Keep all other spheres. The local three-dimensional cobordism has three spherical boundary components: the old `A` and its two replacements. In the one-puncture case it has one link interval between each pair of boundaries. Each boundary has two ends, and those three intervals are the complete local link. This triangle of intervals is important: it specifies both new attachment occurrences on `Q`, including the one leading into the temporary trivial connector. It is not a free choice of a new edge pairing. [F2, F3, F4]

2.3 If `D` has no puncture, componentwise parity puts both `A` punctures on one disk of `A`. The other surgery sphere is unpunctured. In capped `R` it misses the capped link. By the preceding nonsplitting fact it has an empty ball side. Shrink the cap balls, fixing the capped link, until they miss that sphere. None lies on the empty side, since its own cap arc would then lie there. The empty-ball removal therefore loses no incident cap or substituted subtree. The remaining twice-punctured surgery sphere gives the same capped factors and the same attachments up to the resulting capped-factor isotopies and transported charts. The isotopy is used to compare factor data; it need not be applied to the actual surgery spheres when counting intersections with `T`. [F2, F3, F4]

2.4 Suppose instead that `D` has one puncture. Each disk of `A` has one puncture: otherwise a surgery sphere would have an odd total. More strongly the puncture of `D` has the same global name `C` as the punctures of `A`: otherwise either surgery sphere would meet each of two distinct global components once, contradicting parity for those components separately. [F2, F3, F4]

2.5 In the old `A` cap of `R`, let `E0` be the closed boundary disk bounded by the surgery circle `gamma` and containing one cap endpoint `p`; the other endpoint `q` lies outside `E0`. Construct the required spanning disk in the actual cap, without extending an arbitrary boundary homeomorphism. Use its given smooth trivial-arc chart to choose an inward boundary collar whose fibers near `p` agree with the cap arc. Choose disjoint endpoint windows, the `q` window disjoint from `E0`. Outside these windows the remaining compact cap arc has positive distance from the boundary; therefore a sufficiently thin collar over `E0` misses it. In this collar replace `E0` by the graph `x -> (x,epsilon f(x))`, where `f` is smooth, positive in the disk interior, zero on `gamma`, has positive inward derivative at `gamma`, and is constant positive on a smaller `p` window. Choose `epsilon` smaller than the preceding separation bound. The graph is an embedded proper disk, fixes `gamma` pointwise, and meets the cap arc exactly once in that smaller window. The meeting is transverse because the cap arc is a collar fiber there and the graph is a collar section. It misses every other portion of the cap arc and every other cap ball. Its orientation and endpoint label are those of the actual cap chart. Joining this graph disk to `D` and rounding along their common boundary gives the twice-punctured sphere in the capped link `P` of `R`. No equatorial-disk extension or unidentified gluing map is used. [F2, F3, F4]

2.6 If `P` is nontrivial prime, one resulting capped factor is an unknot, and the other is isotopic to `P`. If `P` is an unknot, both are unknots by the spanning-disk argument above. In either case the trivial side has exactly one capped component, with name `C`. Every other named component of `P` remains in its nontrivial side. The isotopy identifying the surviving factor with `P` is the actual deletion of a boundary-parallel proper arc using its disk; it preserves the component names and supplies the capped-factor map needed later. [F2, F3, F4]

2.7 On the `Q` side the local three-boundary cobordism simply replaces its old cap by two caps. Capping both new boundaries recovers the old single trivial cap arc, by the triangle-of-intervals model. Thus its capped link is still `Q`, with its actual component identification. Suppress the sphere between `Q` and the newly trivial region by merging those regions. All other boundaries of that trivial region are retained. The ordered-connector construction inserts their entire charted subtree balls into `Q` on `C`, in the order in which they occur on that trivial component. One of these balls can be the surviving `P` region itself. The other incident branches of `P` remain with `P`; only branches in the trivial region are transferred. No cap is deleted because its vertex has been called an unknot. [F2, F3, F4]

2.8 This describes the surgery's complete change of attachment data. It is also reversible at that level: split off the ordered chain, with its retained bead charts, and replace the three-boundary connector. Relative to the old description, transferred branch balls have moved from intervals beside `P` to intervals beside `Q`, or conversely, along global `C`. Their route passes through the connecting `C` intervals and through the appropriate oriented component of `P` or `Q`. Both exist because the edge and all the transferred caps carry `C`. The branch is transported as one whole ball, including every closed component and every nested attachment inside it. Consecutive branches may change order only on that same component. This is the precise tree transfer asserted by the comparison; it does not claim that the new surgery sphere is isotopic to the old one relative to all branches. [F2, F3, F4]

2.9 Push the two copies of `D` slightly off the whole `T` system in their product collar. All other intersections of the replacement spheres with `T` lie on the old two disks of `A`. Their total is exactly the old count for `A` minus the chosen circle. Suppressing a sphere can only decrease it further. The bookkeeping isotopies of capped factors are not performed on this physical system. Hence the integer `|S intersect T|`, counting circles, strictly decreases. Repeating gives a system disjoint from `T`, with the original prime factors and a recorded finite sequence of the specified component-preserving factor identifications and transfers. In the nontrivial-prime case each step retains those prime vertices and removes just a trivial connector; no new nontrivial factor is introduced. [F2, F3, F4]

2.10 Finally take the union of the two disjoint systems. Adding one union sphere inside a capped prime region splits it into the same prime and an unknot; adding one inside a capped unknot region gives two unknots. This assertion includes caps already present: the new sphere lies in the actual region, hence misses them, and is also a sphere in its capped link. Perform these additions one at a time. Each original prime region therefore contains exactly one nontrivial prime region in the refinement, with all remaining pieces trivial connectors. The same ordered-connector analysis retains all incident balls when those trivial vertices are collapsed. Both descriptions are consequently related to this one actual common refinement by component-preserving prime-factor isotopies and the recorded same-component branch transfers. Composing gives the desired marked comparison. The tree roots and any distinguished point are carried on their actual intervals; rerooting the finite dual tree is only a choice of description and makes no change to the link. [F2, F3, F4]

3.1 Here are the finite diagram operations required by the preceding geometric comparison. They concern isolated one-string factor disks; a factor may include any number of closed components. Its two connector strips are crossing free. The full branch, including all descendants, can always be regarded as this one isolated disk by the recursive connected-sum construction of a tree diagram. [F1, F3, F4]

3.2 For clarity the local passage used in the construction has an explicit branch trace. Put the moving factor on the upper through arc of a tangle to the right of a crossing whose overpass goes from lower left to upper right. Denote the exterior left ports by `a-,a+`, the near tangle ports by `n-,n+`, and the far ports by `f-,f+`. Before the flype the overpassing route is `a- -> c(over) -> n+ -> B -> f+ -> b+`. Afterwards it is `a- -> n- -> hB -> f- -> c(over) -> b+`. The other route is respectively `a+ -> c(under) -> n- -> f- -> b-` and `a+ -> n+ -> f+ -> c(under) -> b-`. Here `h` is the marked horizontal turnover: it reflects the factor in the projection and reverses all its crossings, carrying orientations and closed components. Thus one actual registered flype passes the isolated factor through the adjacent crossing on the same global branch, and applies exactly `h` to its chart. The underpassing and reverse-route versions follow by changing which through arc is decorated and reversing the same trace. This proves the local operation without identifying any arbitrary spatial summand slide with it. [F1, F3, F4]

3.3 Follow a finite crossing-by-crossing route on the complement's specified component, using this passage and small edge isotopies. The complement's closed diagram stays fixed when the factor disk is forgotten; the transported disk undergoes one `h` per encounter. This implements every transfer above along the actual named component. It supplies an explicit new alternating connected-sum diagram for the new tree description. Keep its resulting chart and turnover flag; the geometry did not prescribe a preferred projection of its capped factor, so there is no reason to erase the flag by an unproved endpoint identification. The carried factor is spatially the same oriented one-string factor, under the actual half-turn, with the same component map. [F1, F3, F4]

3.4 If two consecutive branches on one component must be interchanged, pass the first through the whole proper through arc of the second. The number of encounters is even. Self-crossings count twice. A closed projected component has even intersection number with that proper arc, since both endpoints are outside its containing factor disk; mod-two resolution of the closed curve into circles proves this directly. Thus the total turnover is the identity `h^(2k)`. Both marked branch diagrams are retained exactly. At no stage is a branch switched to a different global component. [F1, F3, F4]

3.5 The remaining endpoint issue is handled with the following finite attachment-congruence argument. Suppose a component-tracked flype sequence connects two closed factor diagrams. A cut point for an attached branch can be kept away from each move's finitely many active points by an arbitrarily short edge isotopy. Choose its branch disk small. It lies wholly in one tangle disk of that move, and is either fixed or carried with that tangle. Thus the closed factor sequence extends over the attached branch. Forgetting that branch recovers exactly the original move, and its component map is the required one. The branch's flag and cut point may have changed. [F1, F3, F4]

3.6 For two factors, correct the final attachment by transporting the second along the first to its desired cut edge, and then the first along the second to its desired abstract cut edge. Abstract edge names are carried under `h`. After these corrections the only possible discrepancies are turnover flags `a,b` on the two entire factors. On a nontrivial connected alternating factor the selected component has an even, nonzero sequence of crossing encounters. Its first phase after cutting is toggled by `h`, while the last is its opposite. Alternation of the two connectors therefore forces `a=b` when compared with the prescribed alternating target assembly. If both are one, use the actual entire-flype move specified in the retained Definition interface explained next; it turns over the two-factor diagram together. If both are zero the diagrams already agree, up to shortening their crossing-free strips by disk isotopies. When one factor is a crossingless unknot it is an identity connector and can be omitted; no first phase is then invoked. This proves exact attachment congruence, retaining component labels and the chosen abstract cut windows. [F1, F3, F4]

3.7 To use this move choose any crossing `c` of the nontrivial two-factor diagram. In its crossing chart choose a small smooth flyping circle through `c`, transverse to its two branches there, enclosing just the two short half-strands on one side and meeting them once each before leaving that chart. The circle has exactly the required one crossing and two other transverse diagram intersections. After narrowing its annulus, the small-side tangle consists of two crossing-free arcs. Fix that tangle and turn the complementary tangle by the Definition's move. This is precisely its crossing-centered entire flype, not an additional move without a central crossing. Its opposite-side-view conclusion gives simultaneous projection reflection and crossing reversal of the entire connected-sum diagram, up to sphere planar isotopy, carrying both factor charts and all component orientations. Thus it toggles both flags and preserves their abstract cut windows. A diagram with no crossing needs no such move, and has already been handled as the trivial connector case. The use here is exactly the stated entire-flype interface; it does not authorize independently turning over a factor while fixing an arbitrary exterior. [F1, F3, F4]

3.8 For a finite tree apply this argument inductively by removing an end vertex. The remaining whole tree and the end branch are two factors. Extension and endpoint correction retain the already carried component names of both, so induction restores every incident branch. This proves the needed multiattachment extension rather than assuming a relative prime theorem with all punctures fixed. Normal angles of the supplied cap charts are carried with their balls; the connected-sum diagrams use these charts, and the final edge/collar disk isotopies match their transported parametrizations. There is no independent change of a gluing boundary map. [F1, F3, F4]

4.1 Start from the first alternating diagram's prime tree diagram. Follow the geometric sphere-system comparison. At each transfer use the explicit branch passage above, carrying the resulting charts and turnover flags. At each identification of corresponding capped prime factors use the marked prime input for the particular component bijection supplied by the geometric comparison, and extend it over incident branches by attachment congruence. All newly used factor diagrams are the old alternating prime diagrams, their carried turnovers, or the target alternating prime diagrams. No projection of an arbitrary intermediate spatial surgery region is asserted to be alternating. The tree gives their connected sums with the phases inherited from the actual diagram moves. [F1, A1, construct]

4.2 At the common refinement, choose one diagram for each corresponding prime factor, and one set of attachment intervals and connector orders. From either side, same-component transfers and branch interchanges reach those choices; marked prime replacements and the exact endpoint congruence reach the same decorated tree diagram. Reverse the second side's finite sequence. The original diagrams are therefore related by finitely many registered flypes and sphere planar isotopies. Throughout, closed components inside branch balls are carried, all global component names are retained, and the terminal component bijection is the one induced by the original link identification. This proves the stated composite reduction with no knot-only uniqueness theorem imported as a link theorem. [F1, A1, construct] ∎

## Remarks

This is a newly authored local proof of the marked composite comparison and its conditional diagram lift. The marked prime input is essential: an unlabelled prime flyping statement alone does not determine the attaching component when a prime link has component-permuting symmetries. The lemma neither supplies that input nor constructs any checkerboard cap.

The exact retained move interface is the Definition paragraph and its following turnover-convention paragraph in the flype-data definition. The owner supplied that interface directly; this author did not reread a repaired supplier proof or claim a fresh acceptance report. The Kindred reference records the actual ordinary and entire move conventions only. No fresh primary-source reading, or attribution of this whole component-marked comparison to that source, is asserted.
