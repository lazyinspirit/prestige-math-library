## Example

For every commutative ring $R$ and ideal $I$, $\operatorname{depth}_I(0)=+\infty$.
There are also nonzero examples with $IM=M$: for
$R=k\times k$, $I=k\times0$, and $M=I$, one has $IM=M$ and hence
$\operatorname{depth}_I(M)=+\infty$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $M=I$ is generated by the idempotent $(1,0)$ and is nonzero.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The exceptional clause in the definition applies to the zero module because $I0=0$. In the product-ring example, $I^2=I$, so $IM=I^2=M$. [given]

2.1 `lem-depth-infinity-when-ideal-acts-surjectively` therefore assigns $+\infty$ in both cases. This does not conflict with Nakayama: the ideal in the nonzero example is not contained in the Jacobson radical. [step 1.1, algebra] ∎
