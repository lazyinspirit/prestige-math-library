## Statement

Work in ZF over an infinite field $k$. A line bundle $L$ on a specified projective $k$-scheme $X$ is called ample precisely when $X$ is quasi-compact and every point belongs to an affine nonvanishing locus $X_s$ of a global section of some positive power of $L$. Very ample means the pullback of $O(1)$ under a specified closed immersion into a finite projective space. No reducedness, normality, smoothness or Cohen–Macaulay condition is imposed.

The following finite field-scope criteria hold.

1. A projective morphism $f:T\to Y$ of finite-type $k$-schemes whose fibres have dimension at most zero is finite. In fact a zero-dimensional or empty fibre has an open neighbourhood of its base point over which $f$ is finite. A finite morphism $X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ pulls $O(1)$ back to an ample line bundle.
2. If $L$ is ample and $F$ coherent on $X$, then $F\otimes L^m$ is generated by a finite family of global sections and $H^q(X,F\otimes L^m)=0$ for every $q>0$ and every sufficiently large integer $m$.
3. $L$ is ample if and only if some positive power is very ample. The tensor product of a very ample line bundle with a globally generated line bundle is very ample. Restrictions of ample bundles to closed subschemes are ample.
4. $L$ is ample if and only if its restriction to every reduced irreducible component of $X$ is ample. This includes nilpotent and embedded structure on $X$.
5. If $L$ is globally generated and $\deg_C(L|_C)>0$ for every integral projective curve $C\subseteq X$, then $L$ is ample. The condition is vacuous for a zero-dimensional or empty $X$.

The schemes and projective embeddings are finite data. The conclusions do not assert a criterion over an arbitrary Noetherian base and do not invoke a Choice-qualified general Serre or quasi-finite proper theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The infinite field, finite-type schemes and projective morphism in clause 1, and the projective embedding, coherent sheaf and line bundles with exactly the hypotheses stated in clauses 2–5.

[F1] Every nonzero finite-type algebra over a field is module-finite over a polynomial subalgebra, and finite-variable polynomial field algebras are Noetherian by finite generators. [[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]] [[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-algebras-over-fields-are-noetherian-direct]]

[F2] Coherent sheaves on specified projective field schemes have intrinsic finite affine Cech cohomology, finite graded presentations, finite-dimensional cohomology, and eventual vanishing and finite global generation for twists by the specified $O(1)$. The finite affine comparison holds for finite-type field charts and their principal localizations. [[lem-finite-graded-resolutions-and-projective-cech-cohomology-in-zf]]

[F3] Every coherent sheaf on a projective scheme over an infinite field has a finite filtration by twists $O_V(-a)$ for reduced integral projective subvarieties. A globally generated bundle admits a section acting injectively on a given coherent sheaf, with lower-dimensional cokernel. [[lem-nef-and-generated-mixed-intersection-positivity-in-zf]]

[F4] For integral projective $V$ of dimension $d$, a very ample $H$ has strictly positive top mixed degree $J_{O_V}(H,\ldots,H)$. [[lem-finite-projection-top-cech-duality-and-polarization-degree-in-zf]]

[F5] Mixed Euler differences are exact-sequence additive, top coherent-twist invariant and vanish when their number of entries exceeds support dimension. [[lem-projective-finite-difference-intersections-and-generated-positivity-in-zf]]

[F6] Curve degree agrees with the Euler difference; a line bundle pulled back along a point-image morphism from an integral projective curve has degree zero. [[lem-projective-curve-degrees-and-projection-formula-in-zf]]

## Proof

1.1 We record finite algebraic point tests used below. If a nonzero finite-type algebra $B$ over a field $K$ is finite over $R=K[t_1,\ldots,t_e]$ as in [F1], then $B/(t_1,\ldots,t_e)B\ne0$. Indeed if this ideal generated $B$ as module, expressing finite module generators in it and taking a determinant would give an element $1+r$, with $r\in(t_1,\ldots,t_e)R$, annihilating $B$. Since $R\hookrightarrow B$, this is impossible. The nonzero quotient is finite-dimensional over $K$. Among its proper ideals take one of largest vector-space dimension; its quotient is a finite-dimensional field. This is one choice from a nonempty set of integers and a single finite-dimensional witness, and uses no maximal-ideal existence principle. Consequently every nonzero finite-type field algebra has a finite field quotient. Applying this to $B[1/b]$ proves that a function vanishing at every point is nilpotent. Every nonempty finite-type field scheme has a closed point: take this quotient on a nonempty affine chart; a point with residue field finite over $K$ is closed throughout the scheme, because on any affine chart containing a specialization its residue algebra is a finite-dimensional domain over $K$, hence a field. The same point test applies to nonempty closed subschemes and to all field extensions of $k$. [F1, construct, algebra]

1.2 A finite module $M$ over a ring $A$ with $M\otimes\kappa(p)=0$ vanishes on a principal neighbourhood of $p$. Over $A_p$, write finite generators in $pA_pM$ and take the determinant of the identity minus the resulting matrix. The determinant lies in $1+pA_p$ and annihilates $M_p$, so $M_p=0$. A finite list of denominators annihilates the original generators on one principal neighbourhood. We use this finite determinant argument, including for graded pieces, without a general prime-selection or module-choice principle. Finite-type field algebras and their principal localizations are Noetherian by [F1]; their finite modules and coherent chart kernels have finite presentations. [F1, F2, construct, algebra]

1.3 Work near $p\in Y$ in clause 1, with an affine neighbourhood $\operatorname{Spec}A$ and a projective presentation $T=\operatorname{Proj}S$, where $S=A[x_0,\ldots,x_N]/I$ is standard graded. The fibre over $p$ has finitely many points if it is zero-dimensional: its finite integral-component filtration [F3], over the infinite field $\kappa(p)$, lists all its reduced components, and each zero-dimensional integral component is a single point. Choose a linear form $h$ nonvanishing at all these finitely many points. Each forbidden condition is a proper linear subspace of the coefficient space over $\kappa(p)$; coefficients $(1,c,\ldots,c^N)$ avoid them because each nonzero evaluation polynomial has only finitely many roots and $\kappa(p)$ is infinite. An empty fibre imposes no conditions. Lift the finitely many coefficients to $A_p$ and clear denominators not in $p$, so $h$ is defined on a principal neighbourhood. [F3, given, construct, algebra]

1.4 We need actual curves over $k$, not a base-change assertion about numerical nefness. Every positive-dimensional integral projective $Z\subseteq X$ contains such a curve. With $d=\dim Z\ge1$ and $H$ restricted from a projective embedding, start from $O_Z$ and apply the regular-section construction [F3] to $H$, successively $d-1$ times. The coherent cokernels have support dimension at most $d-1,d-2,\ldots,1$ and stay supported on $Z$. Exact sequences and top coherent-twist invariance [F5] identify the one-entry difference $J_Q(H)$ of the final cokernel with $J_{O_Z}(H,\ldots,H)>0$ by [F4]. Explicitly an injective section gives $0\to G\to G\otimes H\to Q'\to0$, so $J_{Q'}(H^{[e-1]})=J_G(H^{[e]})$ when $G$ has support dimension at most $e$; this follows from additivity and the definition of the extra Euler difference. Thus the final sheaf cannot have zero-dimensional support, by [F5]. Its finite reduced support components [F3] include an integral projective curve $C\subseteq Z$. If $d=1$, simply take $C=Z$. [F3, F4, F5, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put $Q=S/(h)$. Its projective fibre is empty. Each coordinate $x_i$ is therefore nilpotent in $Q\otimes_A\kappa(p)$: otherwise its degree-zero localization gives a nonzero finite-type $\kappa(p)$-algebra, and step 1.1 supplies a point in the forbidden projective fibre. With finitely many coordinates, a single integer $b$ makes every monomial of degree $b$ zero. Thus $Q_b\otimes_A\kappa(p)=0$. This is a finite $A$-module, so step 1.2 gives a single principal neighbourhood on which $Q_b=0$. Standard grading implies $Q_j=0$ for every $j\ge b$. On that neighbourhood $S_j=hS_{j-1}$ for $j\ge b$, whence $S$ is generated as an $A[h]$-module by finitely many homogeneous generators in degrees less than $b$. Moreover every point of $\operatorname{Proj}S$ lies in $D_+(h)$, because the coordinates of $S/(h)$ are nilpotent. Hence $T=\operatorname{Spec}(S_h)_0$ there. If $u$ is one of the finite homogeneous generators, $u/h^{\deg u}$ generates this degree-zero algebra as an $A$-module: homogeneous terms of larger degree reduce repeatedly by $h$. The morphism is finite on this neighbourhood. This proves the asserted local result, and if all fibres have dimension at most zero the collection of all such witness neighbourhoods covers $Y$. Finiteness is affine-local on the target, by restriction and gluing of these finite module presentations. No choice of one witness for every point is necessary. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, construct, algebra]

3.1 For a quasi-compact scheme the given ampleness condition is equivalent to a finite cover by affine positive-power section loci. Only a finite subcover is needed. If $s_i$ have degrees $r_i>0$, raise each to a power to obtain a common degree $r$, keeping exactly the same opens. They then generate $L^r$ and define a morphism $f:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ with $f^*O(1)=L^r$ and $f^{-1}D_+(x_i)=X_{s_i}$ affine. Thus $f$ is affine. It is also projective: the graph of $f$ is closed in $X\times_k\mathbb P^n_k$, since the projective target has closed diagonal, and a specified embedding of $X$ makes this a closed immersion into $\mathbb P^m_{\mathbb P^n_k}$. Each fibre is both affine and projective over its residue field. By [F2], its algebra of global functions is finite-dimensional over that field; because it is affine, it is a finite-dimensional affine scheme and has dimension at most zero. Step 2.1 makes $f$ finite. Conversely, if $f:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$ is finite, the inverse images of the standard affine section opens are affine and cover $X$. Their defining sections belong to $f^*O(1)$, so that bundle is ample by the exact given definition. The same argument shows that if a positive power of a bundle is ample, the bundle is ample. [F2, given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Suppose $L$ is ample and choose the finite map $f$ and exponent $r$ in step 3.1. For each integer $a$ with $0\le a<r$, the sheaf $G_a=f_*(F\otimes L^a)$ is coherent: on affine target charts this is restriction of a finite module along a finite algebra, and the presentations are finite. The projection formula on those charts is the elementary module identity for tensoring with a rank-one free module. Finite affine intersections and the affine comparison [F2] identify their Cech complexes and give $H^q(X,F\otimes L^{rm+a})=H^q(\mathbb P^n_k,G_a(m))$. By [F2] the right side vanishes for $q>0$ when $m$ is large, and $G_a(m)$ has a finite global generating family. Its pullback generates $F\otimes L^{rm+a}$, because on a finite affine chart the evaluation map $B\otimes_A M\to M$, $b\otimes u\mapsto bu$, is surjective. There are finitely many residues $a$, so a single bound works for every sufficiently large exponent, proving clause 2. This invokes finite field-chart cohomology only. [F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 Now let $L$ satisfy clause 5 and choose finite generators defining $g:X\to\mathbb P^n_k$, with $L=g^*O(1)$. This is projective by the same graph construction as step 3.1. For a closed target point $y$, its fibre is a closed projective $k$-subscheme of $X$. If it has positive dimension, one of its reduced components has positive dimension and step 1.4 gives an integral projective $k$-curve $C$ in that fibre. Its image is a point, so $\deg_C L=0$ by [F6], contradicting the strict degree hypothesis. Therefore every closed fibre has dimension at most zero. By step 2.1 every closed target point has an open neighbourhood over which $g$ is finite. The union of all such neighbourhoods covers projective space: its complement is closed, and if nonempty has a closed point by step 1.1, a contradiction. Thus $g$ is finite and step 3.1 makes $L$ ample. This avoids both a field-extension assertion for curve positivity and an unproved fibre-dimension semicontinuity theorem. [F6, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.4, construct, algebra]

5.1 If $H$ is very ample and $G$ globally generated, choose a finite generating family of $G$ by quasi-compactness. The embedding defined by $H$ together with the map defined by that family is a closed immersion $X\to\mathbb P^u_k\times\mathbb P^v_k$: it is a graph followed by the closed immersion on the first factor. The Segre coordinates $z_{ij}=x_i y_j$ give a closed immersion of this product into finite projective space, with equations the $2$ by $2$ minors. On the chart $z_{ij}\ne0$ the inverse is given by the coordinate ratios $x_a/x_i=z_{aj}/z_{ij}$ and $y_b/y_j=z_{ib}/z_{ij}$, which also verifies the claimed immersion and pullback directly. The composite pulls $O(1)$ back to $H\otimes G$. Thus this tensor product is very ample. For ample $L$, step 4.1 applied to $F=H^{-1}$ makes $L^m\otimes H^{-1}$ globally generated for large $m$, so $L^m$ is very ample by the tensor assertion. Conversely a very ample bundle has the affine coordinate section cover; a positive very ample power implies ampleness by step 3.1. A closed subscheme meets each affine section locus in an affine closed subscheme, proving restriction of ampleness. This proves clause 3. [F2, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Suppose $L$ is ample on every reduced irreducible component of $X$. There are finitely many such components by [F3]. For a coherent $F$, use its finite filtration from [F3], whose factors are $O_V(-a)$ for reduced integral closed subvarieties $V$ of $X$. Every $V$ lies in one of the finitely many components, so step 5.1 makes $L|_V$ ample. Step 4.1 on $V$ therefore proves eventual vanishing and global generation for each factor tensored with $L^m$. Cohomology of the closed pushforward agrees with that on $V$ by its identical finite affine Cech complexes [F2]. In a short exact filtration step, eventual positive-cohomology vanishing follows from the long exact sequence. If the subobject and quotient are generated, the quotient's finite global generators lift when the subobject has zero $H^1$; combined with the subobject's generators, these lifted sections generate the middle object. Finite induction gives eventual vanishing and generation for $F\otimes L^m$. In particular use $F=H^{-1}$ and step 5.1 to make $L^m$ very ample, hence $L$ ample. The converse is restriction. The filtration covers all nilpotent and embedded parts, not just the reduced scheme. [F2, F3, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Empty schemes and empty fibres were allowed in steps 1.3 and 2.1; for an empty $X$ the ample condition is vacuous, the unique bundle has an empty-projective embedding and all coherent groups vanish. A zero-dimensional $X$ has no curves, but step 4.2 still makes every generated bundle ample. Positive powers and common exponents always have strictly positive integer degree, including exponent one; the finitely many residue classes in step 4.1 include $a=0$. Steps 5.1 and 6.1 prove both directions of their equivalences explicitly. All point, component, module and section constructions use finite witnesses and finite induction. At no point was an arbitrary ideal given a maximal ideal, a section selected for an infinite family, or an AC-qualified general ampleness theorem invoked. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
