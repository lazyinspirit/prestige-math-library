## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, and let $\lambda$ be dominant integral. Then the simple
module $L(\lambda)$ of
[[lem-a-dominant-cyclic-highest-weight-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]]
is finite-dimensional and is a simple highest weight module of highest weight
$\lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system and a dominant integral $\lambda$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the suppliers of [L1] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ is finite dimensional ([[lem-simple-root-integrability-bounds-the-dominant-cyclic-module]]).

[L2] $L(\lambda)=M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)/N_\lambda$ is a nonzero simple quotient of $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$; its canonical generator, the image of $v_\lambda$, is nonzero and is killed by $\mathfrak n^+$ and has weight $\lambda$ ([[lem-a-dominant-cyclic-highest-weight-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]], [[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]]).

[L3] The quotient of a finite-dimensional module by a submodule is finite dimensional, and a nonzero simple module generated by a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda$ is a highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ ([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[def-subrepresentation-quotient-representation-and-intertwiner]], [[def-irreducible-completely-reducible-and-faithful-lie-algebra-representation]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the module $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ is finite dimensional, and $L(\lambda)$ is its quotient by the submodule $N_\lambda$, so $L(\lambda)$ is finite dimensional by [L3]. [L1, L2, L3, A1]

1.2 By [L2] the image $\bar v$ of $v_\lambda$ in $L(\lambda)$ is nonzero, is killed by $\mathfrak n^+$, and has weight $\lambda$; since $v_\lambda$ generates $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$, its image generates $L(\lambda)$, so $L(\lambda)$ is a highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$. [L2, L3]

2.1 The module $L(\lambda)$ is simple by [L2], hence a simple highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ that is finite dimensional. [L2, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
