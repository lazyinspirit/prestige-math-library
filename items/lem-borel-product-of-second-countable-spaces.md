## Statement

Let $X,Y$ be second-countable topological spaces and give $X\times Y$ the
product topology. Then
$$\mathcal B(X\times Y)=\mathcal B(X)\otimes\mathcal B(Y).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Second-countable topological spaces $X,Y$ with their product
topology and the product sigma-algebra.

[F1] A second-countable space has an at most countable basis, and every open
set is a union of basis elements. ([[def-second-countable-space]])

[F2] Finite products of at most countable sets are at most countable.
([[thm-product-of-countable]])

[F3] The product topology is generated by coordinate projections; in the
binary product its basic open sets are open rectangles.
([[def-product-topology]])

[F4] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets.
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]])

[F5] The product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles.
([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]])

## Proof

**Given:** Second-countable spaces $X,Y$.

1.1 Choose at most countable bases $\mathcal U$ for $X$ and $\mathcal V$ for $Y$ using [F1]. Their product $\mathcal U\times\mathcal V$ is at most countable by [F2], and its members $U\times V$ form a basis for $X\times Y$ by [F3]. Each such rectangle lies in $\mathcal B(X)\otimes\mathcal B(Y)$; every open subset of $X\times Y$ is a union of a subfamily of this countable basis, hence a countable union of those rectangles. By [F4], this proves $\mathcal B(X\times Y)\subseteq\mathcal B(X)\otimes\mathcal B(Y)$. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4]

2.1 The coordinate projections $\pi_X:X\times Y\to X$ and $\pi_Y:X\times Y\to Y$ are continuous by the definition of the product topology [F3]. For Borel $A\subseteq X$ and $C\subseteq Y$, $A\times C=\pi_X^{-1}(A)\cap\pi_Y^{-1}(C)$ is Borel in $X\times Y$. Thus every generator rectangle of the product sigma-algebra [F5] belongs to $\mathcal B(X\times Y)$, giving the reverse inclusion. [given, F3, F4, F5, algebra] ∎
