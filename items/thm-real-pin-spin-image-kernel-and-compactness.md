## Statement

Let $(V,B)$ be a finite-dimensional nondegenerate real quadratic space, $Q(v)=B(v,v)$, and let $\rho$ be the twisted action of [[def-real-clifford-group-and-normalized-pin-spin]]. Then

$$1\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Pin}(V,Q)\xrightarrow{\rho}O(V,B)\longrightarrow1$$

and

$$1\longrightarrow\{1,-1\}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spin}_{\mathrm{full}}(V,Q)\xrightarrow{\rho}SO(V,B)\longrightarrow1$$

are exact central group extensions. For a definite form, both groups are compact in the topology inherited from the finite-dimensional Clifford algebra. These are algebraic exactness statements; the covering-space assertion is proved separately.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-dimensional nondegenerate real quadratic space and the group definitions in the Statement.

[F1] Every orthogonal map is a product of at most $n=\dim V$ anisotropic reflections, with even parity exactly for determinant $1$ ([[lem-cartan-dieudonne-real-nondegenerate-reflection-factorization]]).

[F2] An anisotropic vector acts by its hyperplane reflection; $\rho$ is a homomorphism on the homogeneous Clifford group and has scalar kernel ([[lem-twisted-clifford-adjoint-reflection-and-scalar-kernel]]).

[F3] Pin consists exactly of the norm-$\pm1$ normalizer elements. In positive dimension it is generated by normalized anisotropic vectors; in dimension zero Pin and full Spin are $\{1,-1\}$. Its even subgroup is full Spin ([[lem-normalized-pin-spin-product-and-norm-laws]]).

## Proof


**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $A\in O(V,B)$. By [F1], write $A=r_{u_1}\cdots r_{u_k}$. Replacing each anisotropic $u_j$ by $u_j/\sqrt{|Q(u_j)|}$ preserves its reflection and gives a Pin generator; [F2] shows their product maps to $A$. Thus $\rho$ maps Pin onto $O(V,B)$. [given, F1, F2, construct]

1.2 Each normalized vector has determinant $-1$ under $\rho$. Therefore an even product maps into $SO(V,B)$, and every $A\in SO(V,B)$ has an even reflection factorization by [F1]; its normalized lift lies in full Spin. Hence $\rho$ maps full Spin onto $SO(V,B)$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 The kernel of either restriction is contained in the scalar kernel $\mathbb R^\times$ by [F2]. If a scalar $\lambda$ lies in Pin, [F3] gives $N(\lambda)=\lambda^2\in\{1,-1\}$. Since $\lambda$ is real, $\lambda^2>0$, so $\lambda^2=1$ and $\lambda=\pm1$. Conversely both scalars belong to Pin and full Spin and act trivially. This proves exactness with kernel $\{1,-1\}$. [F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Scalars are central in the Clifford algebra, so the two kernels are central. For a definite form with $n>0$, the normalized-vector set $U=\{u:|Q(u)|=1\}$ is a compact sphere. Given $x\in\operatorname{Pin}$, factor $\rho_x$ by [F1] into at most $n$ reflections and lift them to a normalized-vector product $y$. Then $xy^{-1}$ is scalar by [F2], and step 1.3 makes it $\pm1$; hence every Pin element is $\pm$ a product of at most $n$ vectors. If $x$ is full-even Spin, its image has determinant $1$, so [F1] gives an even reflection factorization and the same argument gives an even product of at most $n$ vectors. Each fixed-length product is the continuous image of a finite product of $U$, hence compact; the groups are finite unions of these images. In dimension zero both are the finite group $\{1,-1\}$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.3, construct] ∎

## Remarks

In mixed signature, the norm-one even subgroup may map only onto $SO_0(p,q)$; that distinct statement is proved in [[lem-real-spin-norm-detects-components]].
