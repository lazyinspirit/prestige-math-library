## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and $e\ge1$. Put
$$B=k\llbracket u,v,s\rrbracket/(uv-s^e),\qquad R=B[s^{-1}].$$
Every invertible $R$-module is free of rank one; equivalently $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$. This includes indices $e$ divisible by the characteristic of $k$. No geometric nearby-cycles comparison is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the positive integer $e$, the two displayed rings and an invertible $R$-module.

## Proof

1.1 The formal plane $A=k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$ admits the following division. If $f\equiv b^d\pmod a$, split each series $g_j=b^dq_j+r_j$ with $\deg_b r_j<d$, put $g_0=g$ and $g_{j+1}=-(f-b^d)q_j$. The corrections gain one $a$-adic order at each step, so their sums give $g=Qf+P$, $\deg_bP<d$. Uniqueness follows by reducing a relation $Qf=P$ modulo $a$ and iterating. Dividing $b^d$ by $f$ gives $f$ as a unit times a monic polynomial of degree $d$ in $b$ whose lower coefficients lie in $a k\llbracket a\rrbracket$. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The ring $A$ is factorial. For a nonzero nonunit, choose one scalar $c\in k$ for which a finite linear change $a\mapsto a+cb$ makes its reduction modulo $a$ have finite order $d$; such $c$ exists because $k$ is infinite. Normalize that reduction to $b^d$ and use step 1.1. Factor the resulting monic polynomial over $K=k((a))$; each monic factor has coefficients in $k\llbracket a\rrbracket$, since its roots are integral and this discrete valuation ring is integrally closed. Its reduction modulo $a$ is a power of $b$, so division by it is available from step 1.1. If it is irreducible over $K$, the unique-remainder map embeds its quotient of $A$ into the field $K[b]/(P)$; hence it is prime in $A$. This gives a finite prime factorization, and prime cancellation gives uniqueness. The scalar and polynomial factors are selected only finitely for each series. [step 1.1, choose, algebra] [step 1.1]


3.1 Give $A=k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$ the $\mathbb Z/e$ grading with weights $1,-1$. The map $u\mapsto a^e$, $v\mapsto b^e$, $s\mapsto ab$ identifies $B$ with the weight-zero subring: every monomial $a^i b^j$ of weight zero is a product of $a^e,b^e,ab$, and reduction by $uv=s^e$ gives a unique formal normal form whose monomials have distinct images. Thus the map is an isomorphism onto that subring, without requiring $e$ to be invertible in $k$. [given, construct, algebra] [step 2.1]

4.1 The embedding from step 3.1 makes $B$ and $R$ domains. An invertible $R$-module embeds into its one-dimensional generic fibre; choosing one nonzero generic vector represents it by a finitely generated invertible fractional ideal $I\subset\operatorname{Frac}(B)$. Choose generators $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ with $f_1\ne0$ and a nonzero common denominator $d\in B$ with $df_i\in B$. In $A$ put $J=(df_1,\ldots,df_m)A$. Its generators all have weight zero, so $J$ is a graded ideal. [step 3.1, given, choose, construct, algebra] [step 3.1]

5.1 By step 2.1, a greatest common divisor $g$ of the finitely many nonzero generators of $J$ exists. Regard $J^\vee=\{x\in\operatorname{Frac}(A):xJ\subseteq A\}$. Writing a rational element as a reduced quotient of products of prime elements shows $J^\vee=g^{-1}A$: its denominator divides every generator precisely when it divides $g$. Therefore $J^{\vee\vee}=gA$ is principal. [step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra] [step 4.1]

6.1 The ideal $J^{\vee\vee}$ is graded. Indeed, for $x\in J^\vee$, multiplication by the nonzero weight-zero element $df_1$ puts $x$ in $A$; decomposing that numerator into its finitely many homogeneous pieces shows each piece of $x$ lies in $J^\vee$. Apply the same argument to its dual. Write a generator $g$ of $J^{\vee\vee}$ as $\sum_{r\in\mathbb Z/e}g_r$ in $A$. Each homogeneous component belongs to the graded principal ideal, so $g_r=gc_r$ for some $c_r\in A$. Since $\sum_r c_r=1$ and $A$ is local, one $c_r$ is a unit. The corresponding $g_r$ is therefore a homogeneous generator of $J^{\vee\vee}$. [step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] [step 5.1]

7.1 Put $A'=A[(ab)^{-1}]$. Localization commutes with the two duals of the finitely generated ideal $J$: a common power of $ab$ clears the finitely many denominators in the defining containment. The ideal $J A'=d I A'$ is invertible after base change from $R$, hence equals its double dual. Thus $J A'$ is generated by the homogeneous element $g_r$ of step 6.1. Since $a$ is a unit of weight $1$ in $A'$, multiplying $g_r$ by a suitable power of $a$ gives a weight-zero generator $h$ of $J A'$. [step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra] [step 6.1]

8.1 The weight-zero part of $A'$ is $R$ by step 3.1, because $ab=s$ has weight zero. As $dI$ is generated by weight-zero elements, the weight-zero part of $dI A'$ is exactly $dI$. The weight-zero part of $hA'$ is $hR$, so $dI=hR$ and $I=(h/d)R$ is principal. Its module is therefore free of rank one. All grading arguments use congruence weights, not a reduced group of $e$th roots of unity, and remain valid when the characteristic divides $e$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, step 7.1, algebra] [step 7.1] ∎
