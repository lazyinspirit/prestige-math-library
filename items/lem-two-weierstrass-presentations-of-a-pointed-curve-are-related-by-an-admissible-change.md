## Statement

Let $K$ be a field and let $C$ be a smooth projective geometrically integral curve over $K$ with a point $O$ whose residue field is $K$. Suppose two generalized Weierstrass equations have smooth projective completions, each equipped with an isomorphism to $C$ that sends its point at infinity to $O$. Write $x,y$ and $x',y'$ for their coordinate functions transported to $C$. Then there are $u\in K^\times$ and $r,s,t\in K$ such that
$$x=u^2x'+r,\qquad y=u^3y'+su^2x'+t.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The curve and the two pointed Weierstrass presentations in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-weierstrass-model-and-discriminant-invariants]]: in affine coordinates each equation has the form $y^2+a_1xy+a_3y=x^3+a_2x^2+a_4x+a_6$, its affine coordinate ring is the polynomial quotient by that equation, and its point at infinity is the distinguished point.

[F2] [[thm-krull-intersection-theorem]]: for a Noetherian commutative ring $B$, ideal $I$, and finite $B$-module $M$, the intersection $\bigcap_{n\ge0}I^nM$ consists exactly of the elements annihilated by $1-a$ for some $a\in I$; this first clause is choice-free.

[F3] [[lem-field-is-noetherian]]: the field $K$ is a Noetherian commutative ring.

[F4] [[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]] and [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]: the coordinate algebra of the chart $Y\ne0$ is Noetherian, and so is its localization at $O$.

[F5] [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]]: under the stated curve convention, $C$ is integral and one-dimensional; the residue field at the specified point $O$ is $K$.

## Proof

1.1 For one presentation, use homogeneous coordinates $[X:Y:Z]$ and the chart $Y\ne0$ at infinity, with $U=X/Y$ and $V=Z/Y$. Its projective equation is $Y^2Z+a_1XYZ+a_3YZ^2=X^3+a_2X^2Z+a_4XZ^2+a_6Z^3$, so the chart equation is $V(1+a_1U+a_3V-a_2U^2-a_4UV-a_6V^2)=U^3$. The parenthesized factor is a unit at $O$. Put $h=(1+a_1U+a_3V-a_2U^2-a_4UV-a_6V^2)^{-1}$, whose residue at $O$ is $1$; then $V=U^3h$ and the maximal ideal $(U,V)$ is $(U)$. The local ring $B=\mathcal O_{C,O}$ is a domain by [F5] and Noetherian by [F3] and [F4]; applying [F2] with $I=(U)$ and $M=B$ shows $\bigcap_{n\ge0}(U)^n=0$, since every $1-a$ for $a\in(U)$ is a unit. Thus every nonzero element of $B$ is a unit times a unique power of $U$, and this exponent defines its order; for a nonzero fraction $f/g$, extend it by $\operatorname{ord}_O(f/g)=\operatorname{ord}_O(f)-\operatorname{ord}_O(g)$. The leading coefficient of a nonzero fraction $f$ is the residue of $U^{-\operatorname{ord}_O(f)}f$ in $B/(U)=K$, using the residue-field clause of [F5]. Since $x=U/V=U^{-2}h^{-1}$ and $y=1/V=U^{-3}h^{-1}$, their pole orders are $2$ and $3$ and their leading coefficients relative to $U$ are one. For the second presentation its parameter $U'$ also generates the same maximal ideal: write $U'=aU$ and $U=bU'$; since $B$ is a domain and $U\ne0$, $ab=1$, so $a$ is a unit. Applying the same local equation calculation in that presentation shows that $x'$ and $y'$ have pole orders $2$ and $3$ in the $U$-order. Setting $Z=0$ in the homogeneous equation gives $X=0$, so $O$ is the only point at infinity. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, given, algebra]

2.1 The common affine curve $C\setminus\{O\}$ is the affine chart $Z\ne0$ of either presentation, so its coordinate ring is generated by $x,y$ with the first Weierstrass relation. Since that relation is monic of degree two in $y$, every element has a unique expression $P(x)+yQ(x)$ with $P,Q\in K[x]$. The nonzero powers $x^i$ have distinct even pole orders $2i$, while the nonzero terms $yx^j$ have distinct odd pole orders $3+2j$; the two lists are disjoint, so leading terms cannot cancel. Consequently the functions with pole order at most two have basis $1,x$, and those with pole order at most three have basis $1,x,y$. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 It follows that $x'=\alpha x+\beta$ and $y'=\gamma y+\delta x+\epsilon$ for scalars in $K$, with $\alpha,\gamma\ne0$ because $x',y'$ have exact pole orders two and three. Relative to the parameter $U$, step 1.1 gives leading coefficients one for $x$ and $y$, hence leading coefficients $\alpha$ and $\gamma$ for $x'$ and $y'$. In the second Weierstrass equation, the terms $y'^2$ and $x'^3$ have pole order six and all other terms have smaller pole order; comparing their leading coefficients gives $\gamma^2=\alpha^3$. Set $u=\alpha/\gamma$, $r=-\beta/\alpha$, $s=-\delta/\gamma$, and $t=-(\delta r+\epsilon)/\gamma$. Then $u^2=\alpha^{-1}$ and $u^3=\gamma^{-1}$, and substitution into the expressions for $x'$ and $y'$ gives the two coordinate-change identities in the Statement. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
