## Statement

Let $X$ be a simply connected CW complex with supplied characteristic maps. Suppose that $H_i(X;\mathbb Z)$ is finitely generated for every $i\ge0$. Then $\pi_i(X)$ is a finitely generated abelian group for every $i\ge2$. The argument uses only finitely many homology groups and finitely many finite witnesses for each fixed homotopy degree, and requires no axiom of choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The simply connected CW complex, supplied characteristic maps and integral finite-generation hypothesis in the Statement.

[F1] The actual relative Hurewicz map of a sufficiently connected CW pair with simply connected subspace is an isomorphism in the first nonzero degree without choice, and lower relative homology vanishes. [[lem-relative-hurewicz-comparison-through-a-choice-free-weak-model]]

[F2] Every weak homotopy equivalence of spaces induces integral homology isomorphisms without a choice principle. [[lem-weak-homotopy-equivalences-induce-integral-homology-isomorphisms-without-choice]]

[F3] Normalization and the explicitly constructed inverse Gamma give a natural equivalence between simplicial abelian groups and nonnegative chain complexes without choice. [[thm-dold-kan-equivalence-for-simplicial-modules]]

[F4] The homological Serre spectral sequence is choice-free, with local coefficients and the finite strong abutment filtration stated there. [[thm-homological-serre-spectral-sequence]]

[F5] A first-quadrant double complex has its column spectral sequence with the stated finite total-degree convergence filtration. [[thm-the-column-filtration-spectral-sequence-of-a-first-quadrant-double-complex]]

[F6] The normalized and alternating simplicial complexes are naturally chain-homotopy equivalent, with finite prism formulas. [[lem-simplicial-normalization-prism-and-trivial-fibration-criterion]]

[F7] The specified successive cell attachments have the CW weak topology and characteristic maps. [[lem-cellular-attachments-with-finite-boundary-support-form-a-cw-complex]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Finite CW Postnikov stages, simplicial abelian truncation, finite-range chain models and Serre transgression.

1.1 For a simplicial set S, its realization has a CW cell for each nondegenerate simplex. A compact sphere or compact cylinder in |S| has image in a finite subcomplex. Barycentrically subdivide that finite complex and the finite source triangulation until simplicial approximation applies; the face-poset subdivision can be triangulated by finite vertex chains. Thus one sphere map or one specified homotopy is represented by finitely many simplices. A horn in a simplicial abelian group M is filled by starting at 0 and, face by face, adding appropriate degeneracies of the difference between the desired and actual face. The simplicial identities make the corrected earlier faces remain correct; after finitely many faces the horn is filled. If M -> N is degreewise surjective, first lift the desired top simplex and apply the same correction in the kernel, whose required horn is compatible. This proves a Kan fibration for that given surjection using only finitely many lifts. [F3, F6, construct, algebra]

2.1 In a simplicial abelian group, a based simplicial n-sphere is represented by x in the intersection of the kernels of its faces; its homotopy class changes by the differential of an (n+1)-simplex with all other relevant faces zero. The degeneracy normalizing operator, a finite product of (1-s_i d_i), converts an arbitrary representative to the chosen normalized convention. Therefore simplicial homotopy is H_n(NM), with the n=1 group operation the abelian addition. For a degreewise-surjective map, lifting a representative and its specified homotopy and reading the missing face defines the boundary into the kernel; exactness follows by the same finite horn corrections. These maps give the homotopy long exact sequence without an independent family of choices. To compare with realization, cover the finite target subcomplex by the open stars of its finitely many vertices. Pull these back to the finite triangulated sphere or disk. Compactness gives one Lebesgue number, and repeated barycentric subdivision makes every closed vertex star smaller than it. Choose, for each of the finitely many new vertices, a target vertex whose open star contains its source star. Vertices spanning one source simplex have intersecting stars, so their chosen target vertices span a simplex. The straight-line track within that simplex joins the map to its finite simplicial approximation; subdivision is relative to a supplied boundary by taking a common finite subdivision and collar. Any two such finite approximations to the same map are connected after a further common subdivision by the same star argument applied to one cylinder. A Kan filler replaces the finitely many relative simplices added by such a subdivision by a single simplicial homotopy class; the normalization formula above identifies those classes with H_n(NM). This supplies the comparison on the fixed source, with no simultaneous selections over all source maps. Retain the construction of step 1.1. [F3, F6, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 For a space F the evaluation |Sing(F)| -> F induces isomorphisms on all homotopy groups. Given one sphere or disk map to F, triangulate its source and use the restrictions to finite simplices as the required singular simplices; evaluation recovers the map. Conversely, after the finite approximation in the first paragraph, a sphere map into |Sing(F)| is represented by finite singular simplex labels. If its evaluation is nullhomotopic, triangulate that one nullhomotopy; its restrictions give the finite comparison after one common subdivision, and the prism between the old and subdivided labelings is an explicit finite sum. This proves injectivity as well as surjectivity without choosing approximations for a family of maps. Retain the construction of step 2.1. [F2, F6, construct, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 Let C be a nonnegative chain complex of free abelian groups with H_i(C) finitely generated through i=N. Construct a finite-rank free chain complex F and a chain map F -> C inducing isomorphisms through degree N. Start with F=0. At stage i, add finitely many degree-i generators whose specified images are cycles representing generators of H_i(C). Then H_i(F) -> H_i(C) is onto. The kernel is a finitely generated subgroup of H_i(F), because the present complex has finite rank in each relevant degree. Pick finitely many cycle representatives of that kernel and finitely many specified bounding (i+1)-chains in C, one for each representative. Adjoin an (i+1)-generator with each such cycle as differential and the corresponding bounding chain as image. This kills precisely the kernel, without altering the already achieved lower-degree isomorphisms. Run i=0,...,N, a finite construction. Any required generator sets and bounding chains are finite and may be chosen for this one N; no infinite selection is made. Retain the construction of step 3.1. [F6, construct, algebra, step 3.1]

5.1 If D is another nonnegative complex of free abelian groups, tensoring F -> C with D remains a homology isomorphism through N. Indeed its mapping cone Q is free and acyclic through N. Filter Q tensor D by the D-degree. For a cycle in total degree at most N, its highest filtration component is a finite sum of elements of Q tensored with basis elements of D; exactness of Q in the relevant degree fills each of the finitely many coefficients. Subtract the differential of those fillers and repeat through the finite filtration. This proves acyclicity through N without choosing splittings for all cycles. Applying the same argument to finite-range models for C and D shows that H_i(C tensor D) is finitely generated through N: each degree of F tensor G is finite-rank free, and subgroups and quotients of finitely generated abelian groups are finitely generated. No AC-qualified Künneth theorem is invoked. Retain the construction of step 4.1. [F6, construct, algebra, step 4.1]

6.1 If A is a finitely generated abelian group, finite decomposition into copies of Z and Z/d plus the chain short exact sequence 0 -> C --d-> C -> C tensor Z/d -> 0 gives finite generation of H_i(C tensor A): its quotient and subobject are respectively H_i(C)/d and H_(i-1)(C)[d], both finitely generated. The decomposition and all generator selections are made once for this fixed A. This also proves the coefficient finite-generation assertion for the simply connected bases used below, without the AC-qualified universal-coefficient theorem. Retain the construction of step 5.1. [F4, construct, algebra, step 5.1]

7.1 We now identify the homology of an arbitrary pointed (j-1)-connected j-type F, for j>=2, with that of the explicit K(A,j), where A=pi_j(F). Take the reduced free simplicial abelian group Z_tilde[Sing F], meaning the free group on singular simplices modulo the constant basepoint simplex. Let C be its normalized chain complex. The canonical map |Sing F| -> F is weak by the finite triangulation comparison above; the choice-free relative Hurewicz lemma applied to the CW pair (|Sing F|,*) gives H_i(C)=0 for i<j and the actual Hurewicz isomorphism pi_j(F) -> H_j(C). For i=0 this uses reduced chains and connectedness. The normalized simplex chain complex computes reduced singular homology: the degenerate singular chains contract by the finite first-degeneracy-position filtration and evaluation of the realization uses the same finite simplex-prism comparison. Retain the construction of step 6.1. [F1, F2, F3, F6, construct, algebra, step 6.1]

8.1 Define the canonical good truncation T by T_i=C_i for i<j, T_j=C_j/im(d_(j+1)), and T_i=0 for i>j, with the induced differential. The projection C -> T is a chain map. Since d_j vanishes on im(d_(j+1)), the group A=H_j(C) embeds canonically as ker(T_j -> T_(j-1)), giving a chain map A[j] -> T which is a homology isomorphism in every degree. Use the explicit no-AC Dold–Kan inverse Gamma. The canonical based simplicial map Sing F -> Z_tilde[Sing F], composed with the natural normalization/decomposition isomorphism and Gamma(C -> T), gives f:|Sing F| -> |Gamma T|. On pi_j, this is precisely the Hurewicz map followed by the canonical identification H_j(T)=H_j(C), because each singular sphere is sent to its fundamental sum of singular simplices. The higher homotopy of |Gamma T| vanishes and its lower positive homotopy vanishes by the normalization/homotopy comparison above, as it does for F; hence f is a weak equivalence. The natural map |Gamma(A[j])| -> |Gamma T| is likewise a weak equivalence, since its normalized chain map induces an isomorphism on homology. Apply [F2] to both arrows. This is a canonical weak-equivalence zigzag, not a selected inverse of spaces; it compares F with the explicit K(A,j) and never asks for a cocycle, an extension from an arbitrary subgroup of a free group, a global choice, or a UCT splitting. The two maps are literal functions built from quotient and normalization formulas. Retain the construction of step 7.1. [F1, F2, F3, construct, algebra, step 7.1]

9.1 Let A be finitely generated. Choose once a finite decomposition into Z and Z/d summands. Give Z the two-term free resolution over Z[t,t^-1] with differential t-1. For Z/d=<g>, use the periodic rank-one free resolution over Z[Z/d] with alternating differentials g-1 and N=1+g+...+g^(d-1); the coefficient sums show directly that the kernel of g-1 is generated by N and the kernel of N is generated by g-1. Tensor the finitely many resolutions over Z. Their underlying augmented Z-complexes split by explicit finite coefficient sums, so the tensor complex is a free resolution over ZA, with finite rank in every degree. Tensoring it with the trivial ZA-module Z therefore gives finitely generated group homology in every degree. The normalized simplicial bar of A is a free ZA-resolution of Z, with the usual extra-degeneracy contraction. The needed Z-linear contracting homotopies are explicit. For the finite cyclic resolution put s_(even)(g^i)=sum_(k=0)^(i-1) g^k and s_(odd)(g^i)=1 if i=d-1, zero otherwise, and send 1 in Z to 1 in ZA. Direct substitution gives ds+sd=1 in each degree. For Z use s_0(t^i)=sum_(k=0)^(i-1)t^k for i>0, zero for i=0, and -sum_(k=i)^(-1)t^k for i<0, with s_1=0. Tensor contractions by s_(C tensor D)=s_C tensor 1+(eta_C epsilon_C) tensor s_D, including the usual degree sign. The normalized bar resolution has its extra-degeneracy contraction. Define each equivariant comparison on free generators recursively by applying the target contraction to the already-defined image of its boundary; extend ZA-linearly. The same recursion on the difference from the identity constructs the chain homotopies, so these are comparison equivalences without a simultaneous lifting choice. Retain the construction of step 8.1. [F6, construct, algebra, step 8.1]

10.1 For m>=1 let G=K(A,m) be the simplicial abelian group obtained by applying the explicit inverse Dold–Kan functor to the complex A[m]. Form the bisimplicial one-sided bar B_(p,q)=G_q^p, with horizontal faces multiplying neighboring entries and deleting the endpoints, and vertical operators applying the simplicial operators of G. Define E_(p,q)=G_q^(p+1), with horizontal faces deleting entries, and let E -> B send (g_0,...,g_p) to (g_0^-1 g_1,...,g_(p-1)^-1 g_p). On the diagonal this is a homomorphism of simplicial abelian groups, with kernel the diagonal copy of G and a degreewise surjection given by (h_1,...,h_n) |-> (1,h_1,h_1 h_2,...,h_1...h_n). Horizontal insertion of 1 is an extra degeneracy of E, natural in q; the finite shuffle of that contraction with the vertical simplicial operators contracts its diagonal. For the map diag E -> diag B, horn lifts are constructed by lifting one simplex using the displayed section and correcting its finitely many prescribed faces with degeneracies in the kernel; this is the finite simplicial-abelian Kan filling calculation. The homotopy long exact sequence follows by lifting each given horn and finite simplicial homotopy, one at a time. Hence diag B has exactly one nonzero homotopy group, namely A in degree m+1. Comparing it to K(A,m+1) by the canonical truncation construction in steps 7.1 and 8.1 shows their realizations have the same integral homology. The contraction is the prism obtained by inserting the extra-degeneracy vertex in each possible position; paired faces cancel and the two ends give identity and the constant simplex. Applying this finite prism to each simplex on the diagonal gives the stated contraction. The source-subdivision and degeneracy-normalization constructions are given in the first section; this paragraph does not import the AC-qualified additive Kan supplier. Retain the construction of step 9.1. [F3, F6, construct, algebra, step 9.1]

11.1 For its homology form the first-quadrant double complex C_(p,q)=Z[B_(p,q)] with horizontal and vertical alternating face differentials. For the diagonal-to-total equivalence use a representable bisimplex Q_(a,b)=Z[Delta(-,[a]) x Delta(-,[b])]. In its diagonal, prepend vertex (0,0); on its double total use h_a tensor 1 + e_a tensor h_b with the usual Koszul sign, where h prepends 0 and e is augmentation followed by that vertex. Both have boundary contraction 1-e. Construct the diagonal-to-total map recursively in each degree by filling the already constructed image of the universal boundary with the total contraction; construct the reverse map using the diagonal contraction. The same recursion on their composites minus the identity gives the two chain homotopies. Every step is a finite sum of face and degeneracy operators, natural by evaluation on representables; therefore it applies to C without a choice of fillings. This explicitly proves the bisimplicial diagonal/total equivalence used here. The normalized simplex chains of any simplicial set compute the cellular chains of its realization: every nondegenerate simplex is a cell, while the degenerate subcomplex is acyclic: filter it by the first i+1 degeneracy positions, and on the associated quotient insert an additional degeneracy at the first such position with sign (-1)^i. The paired adjacent faces give the identity and all remaining newly earlier degeneracies vanish in the quotient. Any fixed-degree chain meets only finitely many positions, so repeated subtraction of those explicit boundaries terminates. Thus Tot C computes H_*(|diag B|;Z). Retain the construction of step 10.1. [F3, F5, F6, construct, algebra, step 10.1]

12.1 Filter Tot C by p. Its first page is H_q(Z[G_•^p]), the homology of the finite simplicial product G^p. Apply the same diagonal-to-total finite-operator comparison to the bisimplicial product of two simplicial sets, iterating finitely; it identifies this homology with that of the tensor product of their free simplex chain complexes. Steps 4.1 and 5.1 show these groups are finitely generated whenever H_*(|G|;Z) is degreewise finitely generated. Each total degree N contains only the N+1 columns 0<=p<=N. The row/column spectral sequences have a finite image filtration of the target, so all associated graded groups and H_N(|diag B|;Z) are finitely generated. Starting with the finite-resolution calculation for K(A,1), induction on the one fixed m proves degreewise finite generation for K(A,m). No rational or finite-rank substitution for integral torsion is used. Retain the construction of step 11.1. [F5, F6, construct, algebra, step 11.1]

13.1 An explicit finite-stage construction avoids the choice-qualified Postnikov supplier. For each j>=1 let X(j,0)=X. Given X(j,k), attach one (r+1)-cell along EVERY based map S^r -> X(j,k), for every r>j, to obtain X(j,k+1). Define P_j as the CW union over k. All attaching maps are a set, and all cell characteristic maps are supplied by their labels. Finite-source cellular approximation shows that attachments in dimensions >=j+2 preserve pi_i for i<=j. Every based map from a sphere has compact image contained in a finite subcomplex, hence factors through some finite stage k and is killed by its very own labelled cell at stage k+1. Thus P_j has the required homotopy groups. No choice of one representative from each homotopy class is made. The constructions for neighboring j are naturally nested: each labelled sphere in X(j,k) maps to the same sphere in X(j-1,k), where its corresponding cell is also attached. This gives explicit maps P_j -> P_(j-1) compatible with X. Replace such a map by its mapping-path fibration. Its fiber is (j-1)-connected with sole nonzero homotopy group A=pi_j(X) in degree j, by the homotopy long exact sequence. The same applies to the homotopy fiber of X -> P_(n-1). Retain the construction of step 12.1. [F7, construct, algebra, step 12.1]

14.1 Assume pi_j(X) is finitely generated for 2<=j<=r. Take a finite Postnikov stage P_r, with P_1 a point and successive mapping-path fibrations with fibers weakly equivalent to K(pi_j(X),j). The base is simply connected throughout. By steps 9.1, 10.1, 11.1, 12.1, 7.1 and 8.1 the fibers have degreewise finitely generated homology. If the base has the same property, each coefficient group H_q(fiber;Z) is finitely generated. Since the base is simply connected, the Serre E2 term is H_p(P_{j-1};H_q(fiber;Z)); step 6.1 gives finite generation because both H_*(P_{j-1};Z) and the coefficient group are finitely generated. Each total degree has finitely many bidegrees, so strong convergence and finite filtration give degreewise finitely generated homology of P_j. Induct on j, a finite range. Retain the construction of step 13.1. [F4, construct, algebra, step 13.1]

15.1 Suppose n>=2 and pi_j(X) is finitely generated for 2<=j<n. By step 14.1, H_i(P_{n-1};Z) is finitely generated. Let F be the homotopy fiber of the canonical map X -> P_{n-1}. The long exact homotopy sequence makes F (n-1)-connected and identifies pi_n(F) with pi_n(X). Use a choice-free CW weak model of F. The existing choice-free relative Hurewicz lemma applied to (F,*) gives H_q(F;Z)=0 for 0<q<n and identifies pi_n(F) with H_n(F;Z). No arbitrary family of nullhomotopies is chosen. Retain the construction of step 14.1. [F1, F2, F4, construct, algebra, step 14.1]

16.1 The actual homological Serre spectral sequence for F -> X -> P_{n-1} has E2_{p,q}=H_p(P_{n-1};H_q(F;Z)). The only possible incoming differential to E_{0,n} is d_{n+1} from a subquotient of H_{n+1}(P_{n-1};Z): for 2<=r<=n its source has 0<q=n-r+1<n and is zero. No differential leaves the nonnegative p=0 axis. Consequently H_n(F;Z)/im(d_{n+1}) is E-infinity_{0,n}, a filtration subgroup of H_n(X;Z). The source of d_{n+1} is a subquotient of the finitely generated H_{n+1}(P_{n-1};Z), and E-infinity_{0,n} is finitely generated as a subgroup of H_n(X;Z). Thus H_n(F;Z), an extension of these finitely generated groups, is finitely generated. Therefore pi_n(X) is finitely generated. Retain the construction of step 15.1. [F4, construct, algebra, step 15.1]

17.1 This induction starts at n=2 with P_1 a point. Therefore each $\pi_n(X)$ is finitely generated for every $n\ge2$, by the construction and finite induction above. Retain the construction of step 16.1. [construct, algebra, discharge-construct, step 16.1] ∎
