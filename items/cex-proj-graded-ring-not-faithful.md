## Counterexample

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a field and let $S=k[x,y]$ be graded by total degree, so
$\deg x=\deg y=1$, with second Veronese regrading
$$T=S^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_{2n}=k[x^2,xy,y^2]$$
(graded so that $S_{2n}$ sits in degree $n$; [[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]],
[[lem-proj-veronese-invariance]]). Then:

1. $\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}T$ canonically, and under this
   isomorphism the twist $\mathcal O_T(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal O_S(2)$,
   not to $\mathcal O_S(1)$;
2. nevertheless $S$ and $T$ are **not** isomorphic as graded $k$-algebras:
   $\dim_kS_1=2$ while $\dim_kT_1=3$.

So $\operatorname{Proj}S$ does not determine the graded ring up to graded
isomorphism, and the twist data must be carried separately
.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, A field $k$, the graded polynomial ring $S=k[x,y]$ with $\deg x=\deg y=1$, its second Veronese regrading $T=S^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_{2n}$, and the scheme $\operatorname{Proj}S=\mathbb P^1_k$.

[F1] For every commutative nonnegatively graded ring $R$ and every $d\ge1$ there is a canonical isomorphism of schemes $\operatorname{Proj}R\cong\operatorname{Proj}R^{(d)}$ mapping the chart $D_+(f)$, for homogeneous $f\in R_+$ of positive degree, to $D_+(f^d)$ with the same coordinate ring, and under it the twist $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}R^{(d)}}(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}R}(d)$. ([[lem-proj-veronese-invariance]])

[F2] A nonnegatively graded ring is a commutative ring $R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}R_n$ with $R_iR_j\subseteq R_{i+j}$; a homomorphism of graded rings is a ring homomorphism carrying $R_n$ into $R_n'$ for every $n$, so an isomorphism of graded $k$-algebras restricts to a $k$-linear isomorphism of degree-one parts. ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]])

[F3] $\operatorname{Proj}k[x_0,x_1]=\mathbb P^1_k$ with the standard charts $D_+(x_i)$, so the two constructions of the counterexample take place on the same scheme. ([[thm-projective-space-as-proj]])

[F4] On $\mathbb P^1_k$ the twist $\mathcal O(n)$ has frames $x^n$ on $D_+(x)$ and $y^n$ on $D_+(y)$, related by $y^n=t^nx^n$ on the overlap, where $t=y/x$; the chart rings are $k[t]$, $k[t^{-1}]$ and $k[t,t^{-1}]$ respectively. ([[ex-twisting-sheaf-projective-line-transitions]])

[F5] The Axiom of Choice is the choice-function principle ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). It licenses the AC-qualified supplier used at step 2.1.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct: compute the degree-one parts of the two rings, apply Veronese invariance with $d=2$ to identify the Proj's and the twists, and rule out a graded isomorphism by comparing dimensions.

1.1 The rings and their degree-one parts. By [F2] the ring $S=k[x,y]$ with $\deg x=\deg y=1$ is nonnegatively graded with $S_1=kx\oplus ky$, of dimension $2$, and the Veronese $T=S^{(2)}=\bigoplus_nS_{2n}$ is the graded $k$-subalgebra $k[x^2,xy,y^2]\subseteq S$ generated by the three degree-two monomials: every $S_{2n}$ is spanned by the monomials $x^ay^{2n-a}=(x^2)^{a'}(xy)^{b}(y^2)^{c}$ with $2a'+b=a$ and $b+2c=2n-a$. Hence $T_1=S_2=kx^2\oplus kxy\oplus ky^2$, of dimension $3$, so $\dim_kS_1=2$ and $\dim_kT_1=3$. [F2, algebra]
1.2 The twists are different. By [F4], a global section of $\mathcal O_S(n)$ for $n=1$ or $2$ is a pair $a(t)x^n$ on $D_+(x)$ and $b(t^{-1})y^n$ on $D_+(y)$, where $a\in k[t]$, $b\in k[t^{-1}]$, and the overlap condition is $a(t)=t^nb(t^{-1})$. This holds exactly when $a(t)$ has degree at most $n$, with $b(t^{-1})=t^{-n}a(t)$. Hence $\dim_k\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\mathcal O_S(1))=2$ and $\dim_k\Gamma(\mathbb P^1_k,\mathcal O_S(2))=3$. An isomorphism of these sheaves would give an isomorphism of their global-section $k$-vector spaces, which is impossible. Thus the two twists are not isomorphic. [F3, F4, algebra]
2.1 Same Proj. Under the AC premise [F5], since $T=S^{(2)}$, [F1] with $R=S$ and $d=2$ gives a canonical isomorphism $\operatorname{Proj}S\cong\operatorname{Proj}T$ which maps the chart $D_+(f)$ of $\operatorname{Proj}S$ to the chart $D_+(f^2)$ of $\operatorname{Proj}T$, and under which $\mathcal O_T(1)=\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}T}(1)$ corresponds to $\mathcal O_{\operatorname{Proj}S}(2)$; by [F3] the scheme $\operatorname{Proj}S$ is the projective line $\mathbb P^1_k$. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1]
2.2 No graded isomorphism. Suppose $\varphi:S\to T$ is an isomorphism of graded $k$-algebras, that is, a $k$-algebra isomorphism with $\varphi(S_n)=T_n$ for all $n$; by [F2] it restricts to a $k$-linear isomorphism $S_1\to T_1$, so $\dim_kS_1=\dim_kT_1$. By step 1.1 this would require $2=3$, which is impossible; hence $S$ and $T$ are not isomorphic as graded $k$-algebras. [F2, step 1.1]
3.1 Conclusion. Steps 2.1 and 2.2 exhibit the two graded $k$-algebras $S=k[x,y]$ and $T=k[x^2,xy,y^2]$ with canonically isomorphic Proj but no graded isomorphism between them; steps 2.1 and 1.2 show that the isomorphism matches $\mathcal O_T(1)$ with $\mathcal O_S(2)$ and not with $\mathcal O_S(1)$, so not even the degree-one twists correspond. Since $k[x,y]$ and its Veronese are nonnegatively graded with nonzero degree-one parts, neither Proj is empty and the invariant $\dim_k(-)_1$ is defined; the case $d=1$ of [F1] is excluded here because the two rings are then equal, while $d=2\ge1$ is the smallest regrading for which $S^{(d)}\neq S$ in this example. No choice principle is used beyond the inherited Proj construction. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 1.2, cases: d=1 excluded and d=2 smallest]
\qed
