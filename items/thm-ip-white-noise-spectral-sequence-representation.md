## Statement

Let $H$ be a separable real Hilbert space with a fixed complete orthonormal basis $(u_j)$, indexed by $J=\{1,\ldots,m\}$ for $m\in\mathbb N_0$ or by $J=\mathbb N_{>0}$; the empty basis covers $H=\{0\}$. Let $Y_x(g)=\langle Kx,g\rangle_H+\epsilon Z(g)$ be the isonormal white-noise experiment with $\epsilon>0$, and write $\mu_j=\langle Kx,u_j\rangle_H$. For every parameter $x$ and every basis index $j$, the observations $Y_x(u_j)$ are independent and have laws $N(\mu_j,\epsilon^2)$, where $N$ is the scalar normal law defined in the experiment.

For $g\in H$, set $P_Ng=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}\langle g,u_j\rangle_Hu_j$, with $P_N=I$ once all vectors of a finite basis have been included. Then

$$Y_x(g)=L^2(\mathbb P)\text{-}\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}\langle g,u_j\rangle_HY_x(u_j).$$

Thus every finite collection of observation functionals is reconstructed from the coefficient sequence in mean square. This does not assert almost-sure convergence of the full sequence of partial sums, a simultaneous path identity for all test vectors, or an $H$-valued observation.

More precisely, let $\mathcal C_H$ be the cylinder sigma-algebra on $\mathbb R^H$, let $\mathcal C_J$ be the sigma-algebra on $\mathbb R^J$ generated by its coordinate evaluations, and let $P_x$ be the field sampling law. Define $S:\mathbb R^H\to\mathbb R^J$ by $S(y)_j=y(u_j)$ and $Q_x=S_*P_x$, where a pushforward is $S_*P_x(B)=P_x(S^{-1}(B))$. There is a measurable map $T:(\mathbb R^J,\mathcal C_J)\to(\mathbb R^H,\mathcal C_H)$, depending only on the indexed basis, such that for every parameter $x$,

$$S_*P_x=Q_x,\qquad T_*Q_x=P_x,\qquad S\circ T=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R^J}.$$

These parameter-independent maps give equivalence of the field cylinder experiment and its coefficient experiment. One such map is the following. For $k\ge1$ and $g\in H$, let $N_k(g)$ be the least integer $N\ge k$ with $\|g-P_Ng\|_H\le2^{-k}$ and put

$$a_{g,k}(z)=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N_k(g)}\langle g,u_j\rangle_Hz_j,\qquad T(z)(g)=\begin{cases}\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{g,k}(z),&\text{if the limit exists in }\mathbb R,\\0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}$$

For each fixed $x$ and $g$, $T(S(Y_x))(g)=Y_x(g)$ almost surely, and the same holds jointly for each fixed finite list of test vectors. The exceptional event may depend on $x$ and on that list; no intersection over all $g\in H$ is asserted. In dimension zero the coefficient space is a singleton and $T$ returns the field with value zero at $g=0$.

If $H=L^2([0,1])$ and the basis is one of the wavelet or trigonometric bases used to define the negative Sobolev carrier $H(\delta)$, then under countable choice and for $\delta>1/2$ the same coordinate sequence is a Borel Gaussian random element in $H(\delta)$ with mean coefficients $Kx$ and noise level $\epsilon$. In infinite dimension it is not an $L^2([0,1])$-valued sample.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-ip-isonormal-white-noise-experiment]]: The process is linear into $L^2(\mathbb P)$, centered, has covariance $\mathbb E[Z(g)Z(h)]=\langle g,h\rangle_H$, and has independent standard-normal coordinates on every finite orthonormal tuple.

[F2] [[def-orthonormal-family-complete-orthonormal-system-and-hilbert-basis]]: A complete orthonormal family has dense finite linear span in $H$; an orthogonal projection onto a finite span is given by the corresponding finite coefficient sum.

[F3] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]: For vectors $v,w$ in a real or complex inner-product space, $|\langle v,w\rangle|\le\|v\|\,\|w\|$.

[F4] [[def-negative-sobolev-space-for-white-noise]]: For $\delta>0$, $H(\delta)$ has squared coefficient norm $\sum_{l,k}a_l^{-1}(\log a_l)^{-2\delta}|c_{lk}|^2$, and the coefficient map embeds $L^2([0,1])$ continuously into $H(\delta)$.

[F5] [[lem-white-noise-is-borel-gaussian-on-negative-sobolev-space]]: Under countable choice, for the specified wavelet or trigonometric basis and $\delta>1/2$, the independent standard-normal coefficient sequence is almost surely a Borel Gaussian random element of $H(\delta)$ and is not an $L^2([0,1])$-valued random element.

[F6] [[thm-chebyshev-markov-inequality-for-the-integral]]: For any nonnegative measurable $f$ on a measure space and $t>0$, $\mu\{f\ge t\}\le t^{-1}\int f\,d\mu$; the space need not be finite or discrete.

[F7] [[thm-first-borel-cantelli]]: If measurable events have summable probabilities, their limsup has probability zero; independence is not required.

[F8] [[thm-sequential-suprema-infima-limsup-liminf-and-pointwise-limits-are-measurable]]: The limsup and liminf of a sequence of measurable real functions are measurable, as is their equality set.

[F9] [[lem-finite-measure-uniqueness-on-a-pi-system]]: Finite measures agreeing on a generating pi-system and on the whole space agree on the generated sigma-algebra.

[F10] [[lem-law-of-a-random-element-is-a-probability-measure]]: The pushforward law of a measurable random element is a probability measure.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]]: Countable choice states that every sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function.

**Given:** The experiment and hypotheses in the Statement, a fixed complete finite or countable orthonormal basis, and, for the final clause, the stated $L^2([0,1])$ basis and $\delta>1/2$.

## Proof

1.1 For $N$ let $V_N=\operatorname{span}\{u_j:j\in J,\ j\le N\}$ and let $P_Ng=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}\langle g,u_j\rangle_Hu_j$. Orthonormality gives $g-P_Ng\perp V_N$. For any $v\in H$, expanding the inner product of $v=P_Nv+(v-P_Nv)$ gives $\|v\|^2=\|P_Nv\|^2+\|v-P_Nv\|^2$, so $\|P_Nv\|\le\|v\|$. Given $\eta>0$, density of the finite span in [F2] gives $w\in V_M$ with $\|g-w\|<\eta$; for $N\ge M$, $P_Nw=w$, so $\|g-P_Ng\|\le\|g-w\|+\|P_N(w-g)\|\le2\|g-w\|<2\eta$. Thus $P_Ng\to g$ in norm, with the finite-basis case eventually equal to $g$. [given, F2, choose, algebra]

1.2 For distinct indices $j_1,\ldots,j_m$, [F1] gives independent standard-normal variables $Z(u_{j_1}),\ldots,Z(u_{j_m})$. Since $\epsilon>0$, the affine map $z\mapsto\mu_{j_r}+\epsilon z$ and its inverse are continuous, so $Y_x(u_{j_r})$ and its noise coordinate generate the same sigma-algebra; independence therefore transfers. The affine-image definition of $N$ gives each law $N(\mu_{j_r},\epsilon^2)$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.3 Put $c_j=\langle g,u_j\rangle_H$. The linearity clause in [F1] applied to the finite sum defining $P_Ng$ gives $Z(P_Ng)=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}c_jZ(u_j)$; linearity of the inner product gives $\langle Kx,P_Ng\rangle_H=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}c_j\mu_j$. Combining these identities yields $Y_x(P_Ng)=\sum_{j\in J,\ j\le N}c_jY_x(u_j)$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.4 In the carrier case covered by [F4]–[F5], [F1] supplies independent coordinates with the common standard-normal density and [A1] supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of [F5]. Hence [F5] makes the noise coefficient sequence a Borel Gaussian random element of $H(\delta)$; [F4] puts each deterministic signal $Kx$ in the same space. Their sum $Kx+\epsilon W$ is therefore an $H(\delta)$-valued Borel random element, and every continuous linear functional of it is a translate and positive rescaling of the corresponding centered Gaussian functional. [given, A1, F1, F4, F5]

1.5 In the infinite-dimensional function-space case, if $Kx+\epsilon W$ were an $H$-valued sample on an event of probability one, then $W=((Kx+\epsilon W)-Kx)/\epsilon$ would also be $H$-valued there, since $Kx\in H$ and $\epsilon>0$. This contradicts [F5], so the sample is not $H$-valued. [given, F4, F5, algebra]

2.1 With $r_N=g-P_Ng$, linearity gives $Y_x(g)-Y_x(P_Ng)=\langle Kx,r_N\rangle_H+\epsilon Z(r_N)$. Centering and the covariance identity in [F1] give $\mathbb E Z(r_N)=0$ and $\mathbb E[Z(r_N)^2]=\|r_N\|_H^2$; expanding the square therefore shows that the squared mean-square error is $|\langle Kx,r_N\rangle_H|^2+\epsilon^2\|r_N\|_H^2$. By [F3] and step 1.1, both terms tend to zero, proving the asserted mean-square reconstruction. For any finite list of test vectors, the sum of their finitely many squared errors also tends to zero, so the coordinate reconstructions converge jointly in product mean square. [given, F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 By step 1.1, for each $g$ and $k\ge1$ the set of integers $N\ge k$ with $\|g-P_Ng\|_H\le2^{-k}$ is nonempty. Its least element $N_k(g)$ is uniquely defined, with no choice and no dependence on $x$, $Kx$ or the observations. For fixed $g,k$, $a_{g,k}$ is a finite linear combination of coordinate evaluations on $\mathbb R^J$, hence is measurable. By [F8], its limsup and liminf are measurable; their equality set, intersected with the set where their common value is finite, is the measurable real-convergence set $A_g$. Defining $T(z)(g)$ to equal that limit on $A_g$ and zero on its complement is measurable: for a Borel set $B\subseteq\mathbb R$ its preimage is $A_g\cap\{\limsup_k a_{g,k}\in B\}$, together with $A_g^c$ if $0\in B$. Thus every evaluation of $T$ is measurable, which makes $T$ measurable into $\mathcal C_H$. Each coordinate of $S$ is a field evaluation, so $S$ is measurable into $\mathcal C_J$ as well. [given, F8, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 Fix $x$ and $g$. By steps 1.3 and 2.1, the real random variable $D_k=a_{g,k}(S(Y_x))-Y_x(g)$ satisfies $\mathbb E|D_k|^2\le(\|Kx\|_H^2+\epsilon^2)4^{-k}$. For every positive integer $r$, [F6] applied to $|D_k|^2$ at threshold $r^{-2}$ gives $\mathbb P\{|D_k|\ge1/r\}\le r^2(\|Kx\|_H^2+\epsilon^2)4^{-k}$. The right-hand side is summable in $k$, so [F7] makes $|D_k|<1/r$ eventually almost surely for each $r$. The union over $r\ge1$ of these null exceptional events is null: disjointifying the union and using countable additivity bounds its probability by the sum of zeros. Off that union $D_k\to0$, proving $T(S(Y_x))(g)=Y_x(g)$ almost surely. All limits here concern the explicitly specified representatives at a fixed $x,g$; the $L^2$ equalities used to compute the error hold almost surely and therefore give exactly the same second moments. [given, F3, F6, F7, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The coordinate law $Q_x=S_*P_x$ and the reconstructed law $T_*Q_x$ are probability measures by [F10]; they use only pushforwards of the given process, with no new product-existence assumption. For any finite list $g_1,\ldots,g_n$, intersect the finitely many probability-one events in step 3.1. On that intersection the reconstructed and original evaluation vectors agree, so $T_*Q_x$ and $P_x$ agree on every finite cylinder $\{y:(y(g_1),\ldots,y(g_n))\in B\}$ for Borel $B\subseteq\mathbb R^n$. These cylinders form a generating pi-system for $\mathcal C_H$, and both measures have total mass one, so [F9] gives $T_*Q_x=P_x$ on $\mathcal C_H$. This proves equality of laws without any uncountable intersection of probability-one events. [given, F9, F10, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 For every basis index $j$, $N_k(u_j)\ge k\ge j$ once $k\ge j$, so orthonormality gives $a_{u_j,k}(z)=z_j$ then, for every $z\in\mathbb R^J$. Consequently $T(z)(u_j)=z_j$ and $S\circ T=\operatorname{id}_{\mathbb R^J}$ everywhere. If $J=\varnothing$, completeness forces $H=\{0\}$, every sum defining $a_{0,k}$ is zero, and $T$ returns the zero field; linearity gives $Y_x(0)=0$ almost surely. If $J$ is nonempty and finite, $P_N=I$ after its last index, so the same construction reduces to the finite linear reconstruction and all preceding arguments apply. Thus the maps in the Statement establish both sampling-law identities in every allowed dimension. The least-index construction spends no choice principle; the only countable-choice use remains step 1.4. [given, F1, F2, step 1.4, step 2.2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Coordinate diagram
