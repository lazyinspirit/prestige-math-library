## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, let $B$ be a standard Brownian motion, and let
$\mathcal F^0_{0+}$ be the germ sigma-algebra at zero
[[def-germ-sigma-algebra-at-zero]]. Then every event
$A\in\mathcal F^0_{0+}$ has $P(A)\in\{0,1\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a standard Brownian motion $B$, and an event $A\in\mathcal F^0_{0+}$.

[F1] The germ sigma-algebra is the intersection of the raw pasts at positive times, and it is contained in the usual time-zero sigma-algebra $\mathcal F_0$. [[def-germ-sigma-algebra-at-zero]] [[def-natural-and-usual-augmented-brownian-filtrations]]

[F2] For every bounded Borel functional $\Phi$ of the future path and every $s\ge0$, $E[\Phi((B_{s+t})_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F_s]=\Psi_\Phi(B_s)$ almost surely, where $\Psi_\Phi(x)=\int\Phi(x+w)\mu(dw)$, and likewise with the raw past in place of $\mathcal F_s$. [[thm-brownian-future-path-markov-property]]

[F3] Conditional-expectation versions are characterized by their event integrals, and $B_0=0$ almost surely. [[def-conditional-expectation-as-an-ae-class]] [[lem-conditional-expectation-is-unique-almost-surely]] [[def-brownian-motion]]

[F4] AC supplies the conditional-expectation interface of [F3]. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $A\in\mathcal F^0_{0+}=\bigcap_{t>0}\mathcal F^0_t$, one has $A\in\mathcal F^0_t$ for every $t>0$, hence $A\in\sigma\bigl(\bigcup_{t>0}\mathcal F^0_t\bigr)=\mathcal F^0_\infty=\sigma(B_t:t\ge0)$; equivalently $A=\{B\in A_0\}$ for the corresponding product-measurable set $A_0$ of the path space, that is, $A$ is an event of the sigma-algebra generated by the whole future path $(B_t)_{t\ge0}$. By [F1] we also have $A\in\mathcal F_0$. [F1, given]

1.2 Fix any $\mathcal F_0$-event $A'$ and any product-measurable path set $\Gamma\subseteq\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$. Let $j:C([0,\infty),\mathbb R)\to\mathbb R^{[0,\infty)}$ be the measurable inclusion from [F2]. By [F2] at $s=0$ for the bounded Borel functional $1_\Gamma$ of the future path, $E[1_\Gamma((B_t)_{t\ge0})|\mathcal F_0]=\Psi_{1_\Gamma}(B_0)$ almost surely; because $B_0=0$ almost surely and $\mu$ is Wiener measure on the continuous path space, $\Psi_{1_\Gamma}(0)=\int_C1_\Gamma(j(w))\mu(dw)=\mu(j^{-1}(\Gamma))$. Thus the conditional expectation is the constant $\mu(j^{-1}(\Gamma))$. The event-integral characterization in [F3] therefore gives $P(A'\cap\{(B_t)_{t\ge0}\in\Gamma\})=P(A')\mu(j^{-1}(\Gamma))$; in particular, the product-space future path is independent of the completed time-zero sigma-algebra. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 Apply step 1.2 to $A'=A$ and to the path set $\Gamma:=A_0$ supplied by step 1.1, so that $\{(B_t)_{t\ge0}\in\Gamma\}=A$. Taking $A'=\Omega$ in step 1.2 also gives $P(A)=\mu(j^{-1}(\Gamma))$. Hence $P(A)=P(A\cap A)=P(A)\mu(j^{-1}(\Gamma))=P(A)^2$, so $P(A)\in\{0,1\}$. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Step 2.1 classifies the number $P(A)$, not the set $A$. For example, a Brownian realization may contain a null exceptional outcome at which $B_0\ne0$; then $\{B_0\ne0\}\in\mathcal F^0_0\subseteq\mathcal F^0_{0+}$ can be nonempty and proper while having probability zero. No right-continuity of the raw filtration at zero is used, only the containment of the germ in the usual time-zero sigma-algebra and the future-path independence at $s=0$. AC is used only through [F4] in the conditional-expectation characterization of step 1.2. [F3, F4, step 2.1] ∎

## Source notes

Durrett, Theorem 7.2.3, and Sousi, Theorem 6.13, prove the zero-one law from the independence of the future increments from the germ. The proof above consumes the future-path theorem at $s=0$ in the completed filtration, so completion causes no gap, and it avoids the reverse-martingale formulation.
