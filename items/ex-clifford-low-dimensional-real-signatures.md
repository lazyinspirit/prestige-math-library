## Example

Write $\mathbb H$ for the real algebra with basis $1,i,j,k$, relations $i^2=j^2=k^2=ijk=-1$, and $ij=k$. Under the positive-square-first convention, the algebras $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}$, $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1}$, and $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,2}$ are respectively $\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R$, $\mathbb C$, $M_2(\mathbb R)$, $M_2(\mathbb R)$, and $\mathbb H$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The real Clifford quotient with relation $v^2=Q(v)1$ and the explicitly defined target algebras above.

[F1] The Clifford algebra is the quotient generated by canonical vectors satisfying $\bar v^{\,2}=Q(v)1$ ([[def-clifford-algebra-of-a-quadratic-space]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** explicit representations and dimension bounds from the relations.

1.1 For $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,0}$, send its positive generator to $(1,-1)\in\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R$; its square is $(1,1)$. The image contains the linearly independent vectors $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$, so the homomorphism is onto. Every word in one generator reduces using $e^2=1$ to a linear combination of $1,e$, so the source has dimension at most two; the target has dimension two, hence the map is an isomorphism. For $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,1}$, send its negative generator to $i\in\mathbb C$; $i^2=-1$, and $1,i$ span the target. Again every word reduces to $1,e$, so the surjection between spaces of dimension at most two and two is an isomorphism. [given, F1, construct, algebra]


1.2 Put $A=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $B_+=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$, and $B_-=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Direct multiplication gives $A^2=B_+^2=I$, $B_-^2=-I$, and $AB_+=-B_+A$, $AB_-=-B_-A$. Thus $A,B_+$ define a homomorphism from $\mathrm{Cl}_{2,0}$ and $A,B_-$ one from $\mathrm{Cl}_{1,1}$. In the positive case the four matrices are $I,A,B_+,B_-$; in the mixed case they are $I,A,B_-,B_+$. For any real matrix $\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}$, its unique expansion in $I,A,B_+,B_-$ has coefficients $(a+d)/2,(a-d)/2,(b+c)/2,(b-c)/2$, so these four matrices are a basis and each displayed list spans. The defining relations reduce every word to a linear combination of $1,e_1,e_2,e_1e_2$, so each source has dimension at most four; surjectivity onto the four-dimensional matrix algebra makes both maps isomorphisms. [given, F1, construct, algebra]


2.1 In $\mathbb H$, the elements $i,j$ square to $-1$ and anticommute. They therefore define a homomorphism from $\mathrm{Cl}_{0,2}$; its image contains $1,i,j,ij=k$, which is a basis of $\mathbb H$. Every word reduces to a linear combination of $1,e_1,e_2,e_1e_2$, so the source has dimension at most four. The surjection onto the four-dimensional $\mathbb H$ is an isomorphism. [given, F1, construct, algebra] ∎
