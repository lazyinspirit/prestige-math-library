## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be the connected simply connected complex
semisimple affine algebraic group with maximal torus $T$ and Borel subgroup
$B=T\ltimes U$ of
[[def-complex-semisimple-algebraic-group-borel-and-flag-variety]] and
[[lem-semisimple-borel-root-factorization]]. Let $Y$ be a projective
$B$-variety over $\mathbb C$: a projective $\mathbb C$-scheme of finite type
together with a morphism $B\times Y\to Y$ defining an action of the group $B$.
Then every nonempty $B$-stable closed subvariety $Z\subseteq Y$ contains a
$B$-fixed point.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the group $B=T\ltimes U$ of [F1], a projective $B$-variety $Y$ over $\mathbb C$, and a nonempty $B$-stable closed subvariety $Z\subseteq Y$.

[F1] $U$ is a closed connected unipotent subgroup normalized by $T$, $T\cap U=1$, and $B=T\cdot U=T\ltimes U$ is a closed connected solvable subgroup of $G$. ([[lem-semisimple-borel-root-factorization]])

[F2] Every projective morphism in the finite-dimensional $H$-projective convention of the source item is proper: a morphism factoring as a closed immersion into $\mathbb P^n_S$ followed by the projection is proper. ([[thm-projective-morphism-proper]])

[F3] For a morphism of schemes of finite type and quasi-separated, properness is equivalent to existence and uniqueness of lifts of every valuative diagram over an arbitrary valuation ring. ([[thm-valuative-criterion-properness]])

[F4] If $Y\to S$ is separated, $X$ is an $S$-scheme and $U\subseteq X$ is an open subscheme with $\mathcal O_X\to j_*\mathcal O_U$ injective, then two $S$-morphisms $X\to Y$ agreeing on $U$ are equal; in particular this holds for a topologically dense open $U$ in a reduced $X$. ([[cor-morphisms-equal-on-dense-open-reduced-source]])

[F5] $G$ is an affine group scheme of finite type over $\mathbb C$ whose underlying scheme is connected and smooth, with Lie algebra $\mathfrak g=\operatorname{Lie}G$. ([[def-complex-semisimple-algebraic-group-borel-and-flag-variety]])

## Proof

1.1 Build a normal one-dimensional filtration of the specified $B=T\ltimes U$. Order the positive roots $\beta_1,\dots,\beta_m$ by decreasing height, breaking ties arbitrarily, and put $S_j=\{\beta_1,\dots,\beta_j\}$, $U_j=\prod_{\beta\in S_j}U_\beta$ with $U_0=1$. If $\beta\in S_j$, $\gamma\in\Phi^+$ and $\beta+\gamma$ is a root, then $\operatorname{ht}(\beta+\gamma)>\operatorname{ht}(\beta)$, so $\beta+\gamma\in S_j$. The height-raising BCH commutator law and polynomial root coordinates of [F1] therefore make each $U_j$ a closed connected subgroup normalized by $U$; $T$ normalizes it because it scales every root coordinate, so $U_j\triangleleft B$. The multiplication $U_{j-1}\times U_{\beta_j}\to U_j$ is a polynomial isomorphism by the same triangular root-coordinate recursion, and $U_{\beta_j}\cong\mathbb G_a$; thus $U_j$ is generated by $U_{j-1}$ and one copy of $\mathbb G_a$. Choose a coordinate decomposition $T\cong\mathbb G_m^l$ and let $T_k\cong\mathbb G_m^k$ be the first $k$ factors, $0\le k\le l$. The preimages $B_{m+k}:=U\rtimes T_k$ of $T_k$ under $B\to T$ are closed and normal in $B$, and each is generated by $B_{m+k-1}$ and the next coordinate copy of $\mathbb G_m$. With $B_j:=U_j$ for $0\le j\le m$, this gives $1=B_0\triangleleft B_1\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft B_{m+l}=B$, with each extension generated by its predecessor and one algebraic root or torus subgroup isomorphic to $\mathbb G_a$ or $\mathbb G_m$. [F1, F5, given]

1.2 Base case of $\mathbb G_a$. Let $Y$ be projective with a $\mathbb G_a$ action, let $Z\subseteq Y$ be a nonempty stable closed subvariety, choose $y\in Z$, and write $F:\mathbb A^1_t\to Y$, $t\mapsto t\cdot y$. Projectivity makes $Y$ proper by [F2], so the valuative criterion [F3] extends the generic map $\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb C(t)\to Y$ uniquely to the discrete valuation ring $\mathbb C[s]_{(s)}$ at $\infty$, where $s=t^{-1}$. This local-ring map extends to an actual Zariski neighbourhood: choose an affine open $V\subseteq Y$ containing the image of the closed point; the map from the local spectrum factors through $V$, and the images of finitely many generators of $\mathcal O(V)$ are fractions in $\mathbb C[s]_{(s)}$ with denominators nonzero at $s=0$. Invert their product $h(s)$, with $h(0)\ne0$, to obtain a morphism $D(h)\subseteq\mathbb A^1_s\to V\subseteq Y$ agreeing with the valuation-ring lift. On the integral overlap $D(h)\cap\mathbb A^1_t$ it and $F$ agree at the generic point; because $Y$ is separated, their equalizer is closed, and because the overlap is reduced and irreducible, a closed equalizer containing its generic point is the whole overlap as a scheme. Thus they glue over the open cover $\mathbb P^1=\mathbb A^1_t\cup D(h)$ to a morphism $\Phi:\mathbb P^1\to Y$. For $a\in\mathbb C$, translation $\tau_a(t)=t+a$ extends to an automorphism of $\mathbb P^1$ fixing $\infty$, so $a\cdot\Phi(t)$ and $\Phi(t+a)$ agree on $\mathbb A^1_t$ and therefore on $\mathbb P^1$ by [F4]. Evaluating at $\infty$ gives $a\cdot\Phi(\infty)=\Phi(\infty)$. The point $\Phi(\infty)$ lies in $Z$ because $Z$ is closed and contains the dense-open image $\Phi(\mathbb A^1_t)$, so it is the required $\mathbb G_a$-fixed point. [F2, F3, F4, given]

2.1 Base case of $\mathbb G_m$. Let $Y$ be projective with a $\mathbb G_m$ action, let $Z\subseteq Y$ be a nonempty stable closed subvariety, choose $y\in Z$, and put $\varphi:\mathbb G_m\to Y$, $t\mapsto t\cdot y$. Apply [F3] to the generic map at the two missing points $0,\infty$ of $\mathbb P^1$. At $0$ use the local ring $\mathbb C[t]_{(t)}$, and at $\infty$ use $\mathbb C[s]_{(s)}$ with $s=t^{-1}$; each lift extends to an affine open neighbourhood $D(h_0)$ or $D(h_\infty)$ by the finite-generator denominator argument of step 1.2. The three maps on $\mathbb G_m$, $D(h_0)$ and $D(h_\infty)$ agree on each integral pairwise overlap: their equalizer is closed because $Y$ is separated, contains the generic point, and therefore equals the reduced irreducible overlap as a scheme. Hence they glue to $\Phi:\mathbb P^1\to Y$. For $a\in\mathbb G_m$, multiplication $t\mapsto at$ extends to an automorphism of $\mathbb P^1$ fixing $0$, and the maps $a\cdot\Phi(t)$ and $\Phi(at)$ agree on the dense open $\mathbb G_m$, hence everywhere by [F4]. Thus $\Phi(0)$ is $\mathbb G_m$-fixed; it lies in $Z$ because $Z$ is closed and contains the dense-open image $\Phi(\mathbb G_m)$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, given]

3.1 Induct along the filtration of step 1.1. For any nonempty projective $B$-variety $Y$, set $Y_0=Y$ and let $Y_j$ be the reduced closed subscheme of points fixed by $B_j$. It is closed: for each $b\in B_j(\mathbb C)$ the equalizer of the automorphism $y\mapsto b\cdot y$ with $\operatorname{id}_Y$ is closed because $Y$ is separated, and the intersection of these closed subsets is closed; taking the reduced induced structure gives $Y_j$. Since $B_j\triangleleft B$, the action of $B$ preserves $Y_j$ setwise, and the restricted action factors through the reduced closed subscheme $Y_j$: the source $B\times Y_j$ is reduced over the perfect field $\mathbb C$, so a morphism whose closed-point image lies in $Y_j$ annihilates its radical ideal. Each $Y_j$ is projective as a closed subscheme of $Y$. Suppose $Y_{j-1}$ is nonempty. The next one-dimensional subgroup $H_j\cong\mathbb G_a$ or $\mathbb G_m$ from step 1.1 acts on the nonempty projective variety $Y_{j-1}$, since $B_{j-1}$ is normal in $B$; step 1.2 or step 2.1 gives an $H_j$-fixed point there. As $B_j$ is generated by $B_{j-1}$ and $H_j$, that point lies in $Y_j$, so $Y_j$ is nonempty. Induction from $Y_0$ to $Y_{m+l}=Y^B$ yields a $B$-fixed point. This uses normality of the chosen root-height and torus subgroups, without asserting that an arbitrary kernel of a vector-group character is normal. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 The closed subvariety $Z$ is projective over $\mathbb C$ (a closed subvariety of a projective scheme in the same projective embedding) and nonempty and $B$-stable, so it is a nonempty projective $B$-variety; applying step 3.1 with $Y=Z$ yields $Z^B\neq\emptyset$, that is, a $B$-fixed point of $Z$. The Axiom of Choice is assumed in the statement and declared as the dependency [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is inherited by the suppliers [F1], [F2], [F3] and [F4], each of which assumes it, and no additional choice is made in the argument beyond the choice of the point $y$ inside the nonempty variety. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, discharge-construct] ∎
