## Statement

Let $A\to B$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings, let $I\subseteq J$ be ideals of $A$, and let $C$ be an ideal of $B$. Write $IB$ and $JB$ for the ideals generated by the respective images, and suppose
$$JB\subseteq C+IB.$$
For $a\in J$, let $A'=A[J/a]\subseteq A[1/a]$ be the subalgebra generated by the image of $A$ and the fractions $j/a$ for $j\in J$, and put $B'=B\otimes_AA'$. Define
$$L=\ker\!\left(A'\longrightarrow A[1/a]/IA[1/a]\right),\qquad K=\ker\!\left(B'\longrightarrow B'[1/a]/CB'[1/a]\right).$$
Here $a$ and $C$ in the second formula denote their images under the canonical maps. Then
$$K+LB'=B'.$$
No flatness, reducedness, domain, finite-generation or Noetherian hypothesis is needed. Zero rings, $a=0$, and the zero or unit ideals are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The ring homomorphism, ideals, inclusion, chosen $a\in J$, subalgebra $A'$, tensor algebra $B'$, and kernel ideals $L,K$ in the Statement.

[F1] Elements of an ideal generated by a subset are finite sums of ring multiples of elements of that subset. A sum of two ideals consists of sums of one element of each ideal. [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]] [[def-sum-and-product-of-ideals]]

[F2] Localization inverts each element of its multiplicative set and has the stated fraction arithmetic; if zero is inverted, the resulting ring is the zero ring. Quotients send the elements of their defining ideal to zero. [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]] [[def-quotient-ring]]

[F3] The tensor product of two commutative $A$-algebras has canonical algebra maps from the factors, and the images of the two copies of each element of $A$ agree. [[thm-coproduct-property-of-tensor-products-of-commutative-algebras]] [[def-commutative-ring]] [[def-ring-homomorphism]]

## Proof

1.1 The image of $a$ in $B$ belongs to $JB\subseteq C+IB$. By [F1] there are $c\in C$, a finite integer $m\ge0$, elements $b_1,\ldots,b_m\in B$ and $f_1,\ldots,f_m\in I$ such that $a=c+\sum_{i=1}^m b_if_i$ in $B$. Each $f_i$ lies in $J$, so its fraction $f_i/a$ belongs to $A'$ by the definition of that subalgebra. Its image in $A[1/a]/IA[1/a]$ is zero by [F2], hence $f_i/a\in L$. Consequently $s=\sum_{i=1}^m b_i(f_i/a)$ is an element of $LB'$, using the canonical factor maps in [F3]. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In $B'[1/a]$, the two factor maps agree on $A$ by [F3], so $a(f_i/a)=f_i$ and the identity in step 1.1 can be divided by the now invertible $a$. It becomes $1=c/a+s$. In the quotient $B'[1/a]/CB'[1/a]$ the term $c/a$ is zero by [F2]. Thus the image of $1-s\in B'$ is zero there, which says $1-s\in K$ by the defining kernel formula. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Combining $s\in LB'$ with $1-s\in K$ gives $1=(1-s)+s\in K+LB'$. An ideal containing the identity is the whole ring, so $K+LB'=B'$. The proof uses no cancellation in $A'$ or $B'$: only the localization in step 2.1 inverts $a$, so arbitrary $a$-torsion created by tensoring is allowed. If a localization or tensor ring is zero, the same equations hold with $1=0$. An empty sum has $s=0$ and the same conclusion follows. Thus the degenerate cases in the Statement are included. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

For an affine morphism $\operatorname{Spec}B\to\operatorname{Spec}A$, the inclusion $I\subseteq J$ is the reversed ideal form of closed subschemes $F\supseteq G$, and $JB\subseteq C+IB$ is exactly the scheme-theoretic condition $Y\cap p^{-1}(F)\subseteq p^{-1}(G)$. The two kernels in the Statement are the affine closure ideals used on the $a$-chart in corrected Deligne Lemma 0.3. Their comaximality is the required separation calculation. This lemma supplies that calculation without treating the later quasi-domination or Nagata compactification theorem as a premise.
