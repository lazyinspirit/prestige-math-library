## Statement

Let $n\ge2$ and put $s_i:=(i\ i+1)$ for $1\le i\le n-1$, the Coxeter
generators of $S_n$. Then in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$, hence in $R[S_n]$ for every
commutative ring $R$: (a) $s_iX_j=X_js_i$ whenever $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$;
(b) $s_iX_i+1=X_{i+1}s_i$; equivalently $s_iX_is_i+s_i=X_{i+1}$ and
$s_iX_{i+1}=X_is_i+1$. In particular the subalgebra generated by
$s_i,X_i,X_{i+1}$ satisfies the local $H(2)$ relations $s_i^2=1$,
$X_iX_{i+1}=X_{i+1}X_i$, $s_iX_i+1=X_{i+1}s_i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge2$, an index $1\le i\le n-1$, the adjacent transposition $s_i=(i\ i+1)$, the transposition sums $T_k=\sum_{1\le p<q\le k}(p\ q)$, and the Jucys-Murphy elements $X_k=\sum_{j<k}(j\ k)=T_k-T_{k-1}$ ([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]]).

[F1] For $1\le k\le m\le n$ the element $X_k$ of $\mathbb Z[S_m]$ maps to $X_k$ under the inclusion $\mathbb Z[S_m]\hookrightarrow\mathbb Z[S_n]$, and each base change $\mathbb Z[S_n]\to R[S_n]$ is a unital ring homomorphism carrying $X_k$ to $X_k$ for $1\le k\le n$: the coefficient map $\sum_g a_g g\mapsto\sum_g(a_g1_R)g$ preserves the group-basis product and the identity ([[def-jucys-murphy-elements-of-the-symmetric-group-algebra]]).

[F2] The elements $s_1,\dots,s_{n-1}$ generate $S_n$ subject to the Coxeter relations; in particular $s_i^2=1$ and $s_i^{-1}=s_i$ ([[thm-the-symmetric-group-has-the-coxeter-presentation]]).

[F3] For $h\in S_n$ and distinct $a,b\in\{1,\dots,n\}$ one has $h\,(a\ b)\,h^{-1}=(h(a)\ h(b))$ ([[lem-conjugating-a-cycle-relabels-its-entries]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct computation in the group basis.

1.1 Case $j<i$ of (a). For such $j$ one has $X_j=\sum_{l<j}(l\ j)$ with $l<j<i$, and $s_i$ fixes both letters $l$ and $j$; [F3] and [F2] give $s_i(l\ j)s_i=(s_i(l)\ s_i(j))=(l\ j)$, hence $s_i(l\ j)=(l\ j)s_i$. Summing over $l<j$ gives $s_iX_j=X_js_i$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 Case $j>i+1$ of (a). Here $s_i$ fixes $j$, and [F3] gives $s_iX_js_i=\sum_{l<j}(s_i(l)\ s_i(j))=\sum_{l<j}(s_i(l)\ j)$. The map $l\mapsto s_i(l)$ is a bijection of $\{1,\dots,j-1\}$ onto itself, because it interchanges the two elements $i,i+1$ of that set and fixes all others; hence $\sum_{l<j}(s_i(l)\ j)=\sum_{l<j}(l\ j)=X_j$ and $s_iX_j=X_js_i$. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 Part (b). For $j<i$ the two permutations $(i\ i+1)(j\ i)$ and $(j\ i+1)(i\ i+1)$ agree on $j$, on $i$ and on $i+1$ and fix every other letter, hence are equal; and $X_{i+1}=\sum_{j<i}(j\ i+1)+(i\ i+1)$. Using $X_i=\sum_{j<i}(j\ i)$ and $s_i=(i\ i+1)$ we therefore get $s_iX_i=\sum_{j<i}(i\ i+1)(j\ i)=\sum_{j<i}(j\ i+1)(i\ i+1)=\bigl(\sum_{j<i}(j\ i+1)\bigr)s_i=(X_{i+1}-s_i)s_i=X_{i+1}s_i-s_i^2=X_{i+1}s_i-1$ by [F2]. Hence $s_iX_i+1=X_{i+1}s_i$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.4 The product $X_iX_{i+1}$. The element $T_k$ is central in $\mathbb Z[S_k]$ for every $k$: by [F3] conjugation by $h\in S_k$ sends each transposition $(p\ q)$ to the transposition $(h(p)\ h(q))$, and $\{p,q\}\mapsto\{h(p),h(q)\}$ is a bijection of the two-element subsets of $\{1,\dots,k\}$, so $hT_kh^{-1}=T_k$. Since $T_{i+1}=T_i+X_{i+1}$ and $X_i\in\mathbb Z[S_i]$, centrality of $T_{i+1}\in\mathbb Z[S_{i+1}]$ and of $T_i\in\mathbb Z[S_i]$ gives $X_iX_{i+1}=X_i(T_{i+1}-T_i)=X_iT_{i+1}-X_iT_i=T_{i+1}X_i-T_iX_i=(T_{i+1}-T_i)X_i=X_{i+1}X_i$. [F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Equivalent forms. Multiplying $s_iX_i+1=X_{i+1}s_i$ on the right by $s_i$ and using $s_i^2=1$ from [F2] gives $s_iX_is_i+s_i=X_{i+1}$; multiplying that identity on the left by $s_i$ gives $X_is_i+1=s_iX_{i+1}$. Thus all three displayed forms of (b) hold. [step 1.3, F2, algebra]

3.1 Collecting steps 1.1 and 1.2 covers every $j\notin\{i,i+1\}$, since $j<i$ and $j>i+1$ are the only possibilities for $1\le j\le n$; step 1.3 and step 2.1 give the three listed forms of (b); step 1.4 and [F2] give $X_iX_{i+1}=X_{i+1}X_i$ and $s_i^2=1$. All these identities are equalities in $\mathbb Z[S_n]$, and by [F1] the base change $\mathbb Z[S_n]\to R[S_n]$ carries them to the corresponding identities in $R[S_n]$. Hence (a) and (b) hold, and the subalgebra generated by $s_i,X_i,X_{i+1}$ satisfies the three listed $H(2)$ relations. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 1.4, F1, F2] ∎

## Remarks

- **Local meaning of the relations.** The identity (b) says that $X_{i+1}$ is obtained from $X_i$ by conjugating with $s_i$ and adding $s_i$, so the pair $X_i,X_{i+1}$ together with $s_i$ generates a local subalgebra satisfying the $H(2)$ relations: on a joint eigenline of the $X_k$ on which $X_i$ acts by $a$ and $s_i$ by $\pm1$, the relation forces $X_{i+1}$ to act by $a\pm1$. This is the computation behind the weight-transposition analysis of [[thm-jucys-murphy-joint-spectrum-is-the-set-of-tableau-content-vectors]].

- **What is not claimed.** The statement records that the three listed relations hold in the subalgebra generated by $s_i,X_i,X_{i+1}$; no claim is made that this subalgebra has a presentation with exactly these generators and relations, and no dimension count for it is used on this page.
