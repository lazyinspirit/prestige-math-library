## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $k$ be a
field and $G=\mathrm{SL}_2$ with its diagonal maximal torus $T_2$, upper
triangular Borel $B$ and root datum $X(T_2)=\mathbb Z\chi$, $\alpha=2\chi$,
$\alpha^\vee=\chi^\vee$ ([[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]],
[[lem-sl2-structure-and-root-coordinates]]). The fundamental weight is
$\omega=\chi$, the dominant characters are
$X(T_2)^+=\{m\omega:m\ge0\}$, and
[[thm-dominant-weights-classify-simple-rational-modules-for-split-reductive-groups]]
attaches to each $m\ge0$ exactly one simple module $L(m)$, with $L(0)$ the
trivial representation and $L(1)=k^2$ the standard representation
([[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]]). For every
$m$, $L(m)_{m\chi}$ is one-dimensional and all other weights of $L(m)$ belong
to $\{m\chi,(m-2)\chi,\dots,-m\chi\}$, each with multiplicity at most one; in
particular $\dim_kL(m)\le m+1$. If $p=\operatorname{char}(k)>0$, the vectors
$e_1^p,e_2^p$ span a two-dimensional simple submodule of the symmetric power
$S^p(k^2)$ with highest weight $p\chi$, so this submodule is isomorphic to
$L(p)$, realized as the Frobenius twist of the standard module $L(1)$ through
$g\cdot e_i^p=(g\cdot e_i)^p$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a field $k$, $G=\mathrm{SL}_2$ with diagonal torus $T_2$, upper triangular Borel $B=T_2\ltimes U^+$, root groups $U^+=\{\binom{1\ a}{0\ 1}\}$ and $U^-=\{\binom{1\ 0}{a\ 1}\}$, and a prime $p=\operatorname{char}(k)>0$ in the last part.

[F1] *Root coordinates of $\mathrm{SL}_2$.* $X(T_2)=\mathbb Z\chi$, $\Phi=\{\pm2\chi\}$, $\alpha=2\chi$, $\alpha^\vee=\chi^\vee$ with $\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle=1$, the Weyl group is $\{1,s_\alpha\}$ acting by $s_\alpha(\chi)=\chi-\langle\chi,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha=-\chi$, $u_\alpha(a)=\binom{1\ a}{0\ 1}$, $u_{-\alpha}(a)=\binom{1\ 0}{a\ 1}$, and $\mathrm{SL}_2$ is generated by $U^+$ and $U^-$ ([[lem-sl2-structure-and-root-coordinates]], [[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] *Classification of simple modules.* The map sending a simple rational representation of $G$ to its highest weight is a bijection onto $X(T_2)^+$; we write $L(m)$ for the simple module of highest weight $m\chi$ ([[thm-dominant-weights-classify-simple-rational-modules-for-split-reductive-groups]], [[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F3] *Simple modules and primitive vectors.* Every simple rational representation $V$ of $G$ contains a primitive vector $v$, unique up to a nonzero scalar, whose weight is the highest weight $\lambda$ of $V$; one has $V_\lambda=kv$, every weight $\mu$ of $V$ satisfies $\mu=\lambda-\sum m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$, and the set of weights is stable under the Weyl group ([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]], [[lem-normalizer-action-permutes-weight-spaces]]).

[F4] *Modules generated by a primitive vector.* If a rational representation $W$ of $G$ is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$, then $W$ is generated as a $U^-$-module by $v$, and $W=kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}W_\mu$ with $W_\lambda=kv$ ([[prop-module-generated-by-a-primitive-vector]], [[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F5] *Root-group expansion.* For a weight vector $v\in V_\lambda$ and any root $\beta$ there are $v_i\in V_{\lambda+i\beta}$ with $u_\beta(c)\cdot v=v+\sum_{i\ge1}c^iv_i$, finitely many nonzero ([[lem-root-group-expansion-of-a-weight-vector]]).

[F6] *Weight decompositions.* The weight spaces of a rational $T_2$-representation are the eigenspaces of the diagonalizable group $T_2$, and every subrepresentation is the direct sum of its intersections with those weight spaces; in particular a nonzero subrepresentation contains a nonzero weight vector ([[lem-representations-of-diagonalizable-groups-are-sums-of-eigenspaces]], [[def-rational-representation-and-comodule-of-an-affine-group-scheme]]).

[F7] *Symmetric powers.* For a $k$-vector space $V$, $S^p(V)$ is the quotient of $V^{\otimes p}$ by the symmetric relations, with basis $e_1^ae_2^{p-a}$ ($0\le a\le p$) when $V=k^2$; a representation of $G$ on $V$ induces a rational representation on $S^p(V)$ by functoriality, and in characteristic $p$ the Frobenius identity $(x+y)^p=x^p+y^p$ and the binomial expansion hold ([[def-symmetric-algebra-of-a-vector-space]], [[def-rational-representation-and-comodule-of-an-affine-group-scheme]]).

For the basis assertion, the maps $e_1\mapsto x$, $e_2\mapsto y$ identify $S(k^2)$ with $k[x,y]$: the map from the tensor algebra kills the commutator relations, and the inverse sends $x,y$ to the commuting generators $e_1,e_2$. Both composites fix the generators, hence are identities. The degree-$p$ monomials therefore form the stated basis. The substitution action of any $g\in\mathrm{SL}_2(R)$ preserves degree and respects the group law; its coefficients are polynomials in the matrix entries, so each symmetric-power action is rational.

## Verification

**Given:** AC; a field $k$, $G=\mathrm{SL}_2$ with diagonal torus $T_2$, upper triangular Borel $B=T_2\ltimes U^+$, root groups $U^\pm$, and a prime $p=\operatorname{char}(k)>0$ in the last part.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 With the identification $X(T_2)=\mathbb Z\chi$ of [F1], the fundamental weight is $\omega=\chi$ and $X(T_2)^+=\{m\chi:m\ge0\}$, so [F2] gives the simple modules $L(m)=V(m\chi)$ for $m\ge0$. The trivial representation is one-dimensional hence simple with highest weight $0$, so $L(0)$ is the trivial representation. The standard representation $k^2$ is simple: for any nonzero $v=(a,b)$ one has $\binom{a\ 0}{b\ a^{-1}}e_1=v$ when $a\ne0$ and $\binom{0\ -b^{-1}}{b\ 0}e_1=v$ when $a=0$, so the submodule generated by any nonzero vector is all of $k^2$; moreover $e_1$ is fixed by $U^+$ and is a $T_2$-eigenvector of weight $\chi$, so $e_1$ is primitive of weight $\chi=\omega$ and $k^2\cong L(1)$ by [F2]. [F1, F2]

1.2 Fix $m\ge0$ and let $v\in L(m)$ be a primitive vector of weight $m\chi$, which exists and is unique up to scalar by [F3]. By [F4] $L(m)$ is generated as a $U^-$-module by $v$, and $U^-=U_{-\alpha}\cong\mathbf G_a$. By [F5] write $u_{-\alpha}(c)\cdot v=\sum_{i\ge0}c^iv_i$, where $v_0=v$, $v_i\in L(m)_{(m-2i)\chi}$, and only finitely many $v_i$ are nonzero. Let $W=\operatorname{span}_k\{v_i:i\ge0\}$. For every $k$-algebra $R$ and $a\in R$, the group law gives $u_{-\alpha}(a)\cdot(u_{-\alpha}(c)\cdot v)=u_{-\alpha}(a+c)\cdot v$; comparing coefficients of $c^j$ in these polynomial expressions yields $u_{-\alpha}(a)\cdot v_j=\sum_{i\ge j}\binom{i}{j}a^{i-j}v_i$, so $W_R$ is stable under every $U^-(R)$-point. Thus $W$ is a $U^-$-submodule containing $v$, and [F4] implies $L(m)=W$. The weights $(m-2i)\chi$ are distinct, so this is a direct sum of one-dimensional spaces $kv_i$ for the nonzero coefficients; hence every weight space of $L(m)$ has dimension at most one. [F3, F4, F5]

1.3 Assume now $p=\operatorname{char}(k)>0$ and put $W=ke_1^p\oplus ke_2^p\subseteq S^p(k^2)$. For $g\in\mathrm{SL}_2(R)$ with $ge_1=ae_1+ce_2$, the multinomial expansion in characteristic $p$ gives $g\cdot e_1^p=(ae_1+ce_2)^p=a^pe_1^p+c^pe_2^p\in W_R$, and likewise $g\cdot e_2^p=(be_1+de_2)^p=b^pe_1^p+d^pe_2^p\in W_R$; thus $W$ is a $G$-submodule of $S^p(k^2)$, of dimension two because $e_1^p,e_2^p$ are distinct basis monomials. The same computation is the identity $g\cdot e_i^p=(g\cdot e_i)^p$. [F7, given]

2.1 If $v_i\ne0$, then $(m-2i)\chi$ is a weight of $L(m)$, so by the Weyl-group stability of [F3] and $s_\alpha(\chi)=-\chi$ of [F1], the character $(2i-m)\chi$ is again a weight; by [F3] applied to $L(m)$ it has the form $(m-2j)\chi$ with $j\ge0$, so $2i-m=m-2j$, that is $i+j=m$ and $0\le i\le m$. Hence at most $m+1$ of the vectors $v_i$ are nonzero and $\dim_kL(m)\le m+1$; every weight other than $m\chi$ lies in $\{(m-2)\chi,\dots,-m\chi\}$ with multiplicity at most one. [F1, F3, step 1.2]

2.2 The submodule $W$ is $T_2$-stable with weight spaces $ke_1^p$ of weight $p\chi$ and $ke_2^p$ of weight $-p\chi$. Let $S\subseteq W$ be a nonzero submodule; by [F6] $S$ contains a nonzero weight vector, hence a nonzero multiple of $e_1^p$ or of $e_2^p$. If $e_1^p\in S$, then $u_{-\alpha}(1)\cdot e_1^p=e_1^p+e_2^p\in S$ and hence $e_2^p\in S$; if $e_2^p\in S$, then $u_\alpha(1)\cdot e_2^p=e_2^p+e_1^p\in S$ and hence $e_1^p\in S$. In both cases $S=W$, so $W$ is simple. [F1, F6, F7, step 1.3]

3.1 Finally $e_1^p$ is fixed by $U^+$ and is a $T_2$-eigenvector of weight $p\chi$, so it is a primitive vector of weight $p\chi$ in the simple module $W$; the submodule it generates is contained in $W$ by step 1.3 and contains it by step 2.2, so it is all of $W$, and $p\chi$ is dominant. By the classification [F2] the simple module with highest weight $p\chi=p\omega$ is $L(p)$, whence $W\cong L(p)$. In the basis $e_1^p,e_2^p$ of $W$, step 1.3 gives the entrywise $p$-th powers of the standard representation matrices. These are the action matrices of the Frobenius twist $L(1)^{(p)}$. The $k$-linear map sending its two basis vectors to $e_1^p,e_2^p$ is therefore a $G$-module isomorphism onto $W$, over every field $k$ of characteristic $p$. [F2, F3, step 1.3, step 2.2] ∎

## Remarks

- The first part of the verification is the standard weight analysis of the simple $\mathrm{SL}_2$-modules: the $U^-$-orbit of a highest weight vector has one nonzero coefficient in each admissible weight space, so the weight spaces are one-dimensional and the weights lie between $m\chi$ and $-m\chi$.
- The computation $g\cdot e_i^p=(g\cdot e_i)^p$ shows that the $p$-th symmetric power always contains the Frobenius twist $L(1)^{(p)}\cong L(p)$. For $0\le m<p$, $S^m(k^2)$ is simple: a nonzero submodule contains a weight monomial $e_1^ae_2^{m-a}$ by [F6]; the coefficient of $t^{m-a}$ in its universal $U^+$-translate is $e_1^m$, so the submodule contains $e_1^m$. The universal $U^-$-translate of $e_1^m$ has coefficients $\binom mi e_1^{m-i}e_2^i$, all nonzero because $m<p$, so the submodule is the whole symmetric power. Coefficients belong to the submodule by its coaction criterion, rather than by interpolation over $k$.
