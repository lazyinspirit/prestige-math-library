## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a fixed
positive system. Then the maps
$$V\longmapsto\lambda(V),\qquad \lambda\longmapsto L(\lambda)$$
are mutually inverse bijections between the isomorphism classes of
finite-dimensional irreducible representations of $\mathfrak g$ and the
dominant integral weights
([[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]); here $\lambda(V)$
is the highest weight of $V$
([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]) and $L(\lambda)$ is
the simple quotient of $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$ and a fixed positive system.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory used by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] Every nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible module $V$ contains a highest weight vector and, when generated by one, satisfies $V=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv$ with all weights $\le\lambda$; the highest weight is unique ([[lem-every-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representation-has-a-highest-weight-vector]], [[prop-a-finite-dimensional-irreducible-module-is-generated-by-any-highest-weight-vector]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]).

[L2] The highest weight of a finite-dimensional irreducible module is dominant integral ([[lem-highest-weight-of-a-finite-dimensional-module-is-dominant-integral]]).

[L3] For dominant integral $\lambda$ the module $L(\lambda)$ is a finite-dimensional simple highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ ([[lem-a-dominant-cyclic-highest-weight-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]], [[thm-finite-dimensionality-of-lambda-highest-weight-simple-modules-for-dominant-integral-lambda]]).

[L4] Two finite-dimensional simple highest weight modules are isomorphic if and only if their highest weights agree ([[thm-simple-highest-weight-modules-are-classified-by-their-highest-weight]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The assignment $V\mapsto\lambda(V)$ is well defined on isomorphism classes of nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible modules by [L1] and takes values in the dominant integral weights by [L2]. [L1, L2, A1]

1.2 The assignment $\lambda\mapsto L(\lambda)$ is defined on all dominant integral weights by [L3], and $L(\lambda)$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible module whose highest weight is $\lambda$. [L3]

1.3 For every nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible $V$ we have $V\cong L(\lambda(V))$: both are finite-dimensional simple highest weight modules with the same highest weight $\lambda(V)$, so [L4] applies. [L1, L2, L3, L4]

2.1 Conversely, if $\lambda$ is dominant integral then the highest weight of $L(\lambda)$ is $\lambda$ by [L3]; hence the two assignments are inverse to one another on isomorphism classes. [L3, step 1.3]

3.1 Every dominant integral weight therefore occurs (through $L(\lambda)$), and no two distinct dominant integral weights give isomorphic modules by [L4]; every finite-dimensional irreducible representation occurs as $L(\lambda(V))$ by step 1.3. This is the asserted bijection. [L4, step 1.3, step 2.1] ∎
