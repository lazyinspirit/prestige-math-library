## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. If $f:X\to S$ is étale and $x\in X$, then
there are affine neighbourhoods $U=\operatorname{Spec}B$ of $x$ and
$V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ of $f(x)$, with $f(U)\subseteq V$, such that after
possibly shrinking $U$ and $V$ the $A$-algebra $B$ is standard étale:
$$B\cong\bigl(A[T]/(P)\bigr)_g$$
for a monic $P\in A[T]$ and an element $g$ whose localization makes $P'$
a unit ([[def-standard-etale-algebra]]). The reverse implication, that
every such chart is étale, is included.

The proof first reduces to a monogenic finite algebra near $x$, then uses
vanishing of relative differentials and flatness to construct a monic
one-equation presentation with invertible derivative.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The morphism and point of the Statement.

[F1] Étale morphisms are smooth of relative dimension zero, locally of
finite presentation and flat. The Jacobian criterion gives a standard
smooth chart with as many equations as variables
([[def-etale-morphism-schemes]], [[thm-jacobian-criterion-smooth-morphism]],
[[thm-etale-equivalent-flat-unramified-fp]]).

[F2] The residue extension at an étale point is finite separable, and the
map is quasi-finite there; the quasi-finite locus of a finite-type map is
open ([[lem-etale-residue-extensions-finite-separable]],
[[cor-quasi-finite-locus-open-finite-type-algebra]]).

[F3] A quasi-finite finite-type algebra is, after replacing it by a finite
type localization, an open subscheme of the spectrum of a finite algebra
over the base ([[thm-quasi-finite-algebra-open-finite-factorization]]).

[F4] A finite-dimensional algebra over a field is Artinian and splits into
its finitely many local Artinian factors
([[thm-structure-theorem-for-artinian-rings]]). A finite separable field
extension is generated by one element
([[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]).
For a finite module over a local ring, vanishing after reduction modulo
the maximal ideal implies vanishing by Nakayama ([[thm-nakayama-lemma]]).

[F5] A monic one-equation presentation with invertible derivative is
standard étale and hence étale ([[def-standard-etale-algebra]]). Étale maps
are the formally étale maps of local finite presentation
([[thm-etale-formally-etale-finite-presentation]]).

[F6] AC is the choice-function axiom ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F7] For an $A$-algebra $C$, the module $\Omega_{C/A}$ represents
$A$-derivations; for $C=A[T]/I$, the universal derivation of $T$ gives
$\Omega_{C/A}=C\,\mathrm dT/(H'(t)\,\mathrm dT:H\in I)$
([[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]]).





## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite-algebra reduction, a monic derivative construction, and local Nakayama.

1.1 Choose affine charts $V=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq S$ and $U_0=\operatorname{Spec}B_0\subseteq X$ around $f(x)$ and $x$, with $A\to B_0$ finitely presented. Let $\mathfrak q\subseteq B_0$ lie over $\mathfrak p\subseteq A$. By [F2] the map is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$; shrink to a principal open around $\mathfrak q$ on which it is quasi-finite everywhere. Applying [F3] to that finite-type algebra gives a finite $A$-algebra $T$ and an open immersion from the shrunk chart into $\operatorname{Spec}T$, identifying local rings at the selected point. The map $A\to T$ is therefore étale at the corresponding prime $\mathfrak r\subseteq T$, although it need not be étale elsewhere. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 The fibre $T\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is finite dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so [F4] splits it into local Artinian factors. The factor at $\mathfrak r$ is the finite separable field $\kappa(\mathfrak r)$ by [F2], because étaleness makes the selected zero-dimensional local fibre reduced. Choose a nonzero primitive element $\bar t$ for this field extension (take $\bar t=1$ when $\kappa(\mathfrak r)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$) and take $\bar t=0$ in every other factor; lift this element of the fibre to $t\in T$. Put $C=A[t]\subseteq T$. The prime $\mathfrak q'=\mathfrak r\cap C$ has no other prime of $T$ above it in the closed fibre: the selected factor has a nonzero primitive value, whereas every other factor has value zero. The localization $T_{\mathfrak q'}$ is therefore local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak rT_{\mathfrak q'}$; moreover its closed fibre over $\mathfrak p$ is the selected field factor $\kappa(\mathfrak r)$, generated by the image of $t$. [F2, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Since $T$ is finite over $A$, it is finite over $C$ as a module, and the map $C_{\mathfrak q'}\to T_{\mathfrak q'}$ is an injective local map of finite modules with the same residue field. The closed fibre $T_{\mathfrak q'}/\mathfrak pT_{\mathfrak q'}=\kappa(\mathfrak r)$ from step 2.1 is generated by $t$, hence also equals $T_{\mathfrak q'}/\mathfrak q'T_{\mathfrak q'}$. Nakayama applied to the finite cokernel over the local ring $C_{\mathfrak q'}$ gives surjectivity, and the map is already injective. Thus the local rings are isomorphic. The finite cokernel is zero after inverting one element of $C\smallsetminus\mathfrak q'$, so after a further principal shrinking the finite algebra $T$ and the monogenic subalgebra $C=A[t]$ coincide. Shrinking inside the open image of $U_0$ then identifies an affine neighbourhood of $x$ with a localization of $C$. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Construct a monic relation with invertible derivative. After step 3.1, work at the corresponding prime $\mathfrak q$ of $C=A[t]$; this algebra is étale there because it agrees with the original étale chart on a neighbourhood. Let $I=\ker(A[T]\to C)$ and let $Q\subseteq A[T]$ be the inverse image of $\mathfrak q$. The polynomial derivation computation in [F7] gives $\Omega_{C/A,\mathfrak q}\cong C_{\mathfrak q}/(H'(t):H\in I)$; étaleness makes this module zero by [F1]. Thus finitely many $H_i\in I$ and coefficients $u_i\in C_{\mathfrak q}$ have $\sum_i u_iH_i'(t)=1$. Lift the $u_i$ to fractions in $A[T]_Q$ and clear a common denominator outside $Q$: a single $P_1\in I_Q$ has derivative a unit at $\mathfrak q$. Since $t$ is integral over $A$, there is a monic $P_0\in I$. Multiplying $P_1$ by a denominator outside $Q$ if necessary gives a global polynomial in $I$ whose derivative remains a unit at $\mathfrak q$; call it again $P_1$. For $N\ge2$ with $N\deg P_0>\deg P_1$, the polynomial $P=P_0^N+P_1$ is monic, belongs to $I$, and satisfies $P'(t)=P_1'(t)$ because $P_0(t)=0$. [F1, F4, F7, step 3.1]

5.1 Put $C'=A[T]/(P)$, with its surjection onto $C$, and localize at the selected prime $Q'=Q/(P)$, over $\mathfrak p\subseteq A$. Let $h$ be the irreducible factor of $\overline P\in\kappa(\mathfrak p)[T]$ selected by $Q'$. Because $P'(t)$ is a unit modulo $Q'$, $h$ occurs with multiplicity one in $\overline P$ and $h'\ne0$ modulo $h$. Localizing the finite fibre ring at $(h)$ therefore removes all other factors and gives $C'_{Q'}/\mathfrak pC'_{Q'}\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p)[T]/(h)=\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, with no nilpotent thickening. Since $C$ is étale at $\mathfrak q$, its closed-fibre local ring $C_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pC_{\mathfrak q}$ is the same field. Thus the surjection between these fibre rings is an isomorphism. Let $K$ be the localized kernel $\ker(C'_{Q'}\to C_{\mathfrak q})$. The source $C'_{Q'}$ is flat over $A_{\mathfrak p}$ because $P$ is monic (so $C'$ is finite free over $A$) and localization preserves flatness; the target is flat over $A_{\mathfrak p}$ by étaleness. Tensoring the exact sequence $0\to K\to C'_{Q'}\to C_{\mathfrak q}\to0$ with $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ remains exact, whence $K/\mathfrak pK=0$. The kernel $K$ is finitely generated over $C'_{Q'}$: local finite presentation of the étale algebra $C$ says that, after localizing the polynomial map $A[T]\to C$ around $Q$, its relation ideal $I_Q$ is finitely generated; the kernel of $C'_{Q'}\to C_{\mathfrak q}$ is the quotient $I_Q/(P)$, hence finite. This finite relation set also permits the later principal shrinking. Since $\mathfrak pC'_{Q'}$ lies in its maximal ideal, Nakayama [F4] yields $K=0$. Finite generation lets us invert one element outside $Q'$ so that $C'\to C$ is an isomorphism on a principal neighbourhood; invert also $P'(t)$. This gives the required standard étale chart by [F5], proving the forward direction. Conversely every standard étale chart is étale by [F5], and étaleness is local on source and target. [F1, F4, F5, step 4.1]

6.1 If $X=\varnothing$ there is no point $x$ and the assertion is vacuous. The zero-degree polynomial case yields the empty chart, so no nonempty point lies there. AC is used through the quasi-finite factorization, primitive element and finite-prime results [F2]–[F4]; all other selections are finite. Both directions are established by steps 4.1 and 5.1. [F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
