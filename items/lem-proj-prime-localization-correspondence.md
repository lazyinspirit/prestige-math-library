## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from the ambient affine-spectrum and
prime-existence interfaces. Let $S=\bigoplus_{e\ge0}S_e$ be a commutative
nonnegatively graded ring, let $f\in S_+$ be homogeneous of positive degree
$d=\deg f$, let $S_f=S[f^{-1}]$ be its principal localisation
([[def-principal-localisation]]) with its localisation map, and put
$$S_{(f)}=\{\,a/f^k\in S_f\;:\;a\in S\ \text{homogeneous},\ \deg a=kd\,\},$$
a subring of $S_f$ containing $0=0/f^0$ and $1=f^0/f^0$; this is the
degree-zero part $S_{(f)}=(S[f^{-1}])_0$ of the graded localisation
([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]). Then:

1. For every prime ideal $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{(f)}$
   ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]) the set
   $$P(\mathfrak q)=\{\,a\in S\ \text{homogeneous}\;:\;a^d/f^{\deg a}\in\mathfrak q\,\}$$
   spans a homogeneous prime ideal $\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)\subseteq S$ with
   $f\notin\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$.
2. For every homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ with
   $f\notin\mathfrak p$, the set
   $$Q(\mathfrak p)=\{\,a/f^k\;:\;k\ge0,\ a\in\mathfrak p\cap S_{kd}\,\}$$
   is a prime ideal of $S_{(f)}$.
3. The assignments $\mathfrak q\mapsto\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$ and
   $\mathfrak p\mapsto Q(\mathfrak p)$ are mutually inverse bijections between
   the prime ideals of $S_{(f)}$ and the homogeneous primes of $S$ avoiding
   $f$, that is, the points of $D_+(f)$ ([[def-standard-open-proj]]). Both
   sets are empty when $f$ is nilpotent.
4. For homogeneous $g\in S_+$ of positive degree $e$, the bijection of (3)
   carries $D_+(fg)=D_+(f)\cap D_+(g)$ onto the distinguished open
   $D(g^d/f^e)=\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}:g^d/f^e\notin
   \mathfrak q\}$ of the affine spectrum of $S_{(f)}$
   ([[def-affine-scheme-spectrum]]).

The constructions and both inverse identities are choice-free; the Axiom of
Choice is carried only as the inherited hypothesis on the ambient affine
spectrum, and no prime-existence principle is used below.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; a commutative nonnegatively graded ring $S$; a homogeneous $f\in S_+$ of positive degree $d$; the principal localisation $S_f=S[f^{-1}]$ and its subring $S_{(f)}$ of the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F1] $\operatorname{Proj}S$ is the set of homogeneous prime ideals $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ with $S_+\not\subseteq\mathfrak p$, where $S_+=\bigoplus_{e>0}S_e$, and $V_+(I)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}S:I\subseteq\mathfrak p\}$ are the closed sets. ([[def-proj-graded-ring-points]])

[F2] For homogeneous $f\in S_+$ of positive degree, $D_+(f)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Proj}S:f\notin\mathfrak p\}$ is open, and $D_+(f)\cap D_+(g)=D_+(fg)$ for homogeneous $g\in S_+$. ([[def-standard-open-proj]])

[F3] A proper ideal $P\subsetneq R$ is **prime** when $ab\in P$ implies $a\in P$ or $b\in P$; in particular $1\notin P$, and a prime ideal is radical: from $a^m\in P$ with $m\ge1$ and $a\notin P$ one gets $a^{m-1}\in P$, and induction gives $a\in P$, a contradiction. ([[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]])

[F4] For $f\in S$ the principal localisation $S_f=S_f^{-1}S$ consists of the fractions $a/f^k$, and $S_0$ is the zero ring while $S_1\cong S$. ([[def-principal-localisation]])

[F5] For a multiplicative subset and fractions, $a/s=a'/s'$ holds if and only if $u(as'-a's)=0$ for some $u$ in the multiplicative subset, and $a/s=0$ if and only if $ua=0$ for some such $u$. ([[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]])

[F6] A homogeneous element of $S$ lies in a homogeneous ideal if and only if each of its homogeneous components does; in particular an ideal generated by homogeneous elements contains every homogeneous component of each of its elements. ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct: build mutually inverse maps between the two prime sets from the degree-$d$ power test $a\mapsto a^d/f^{\deg a}$, verify the ideal and primality axioms on each side, and compute the basic open $D_+(fg)$ through the second map.

1.1 The set $S_{(f)}$ of the Statement is a subring of $S_f$: it contains $0=0/f^0$ and $1=f^0/f^0$, and if $a/f^k$ and $b/f^m$ have $\deg a=kd$ and $\deg b=md$, then $(a/f^k)+(b/f^m)=(af^m+bf^k)/f^{k+m}$ has homogeneous numerator $af^m+bf^k$ of degree $(k+m)d$, while $(a/f^k)(b/f^m)=ab/f^{k+m}$ has homogeneous numerator $ab$ of degree $(k+m)d$. For a homogeneous $a$ of degree $kd$ the element $a^d/f^{\deg a}=a^d/f^{kd}=(a/f^k)^d$ lies in $S_{(f)}$, since $a^d$ is homogeneous of degree $kd^2=kd\cdot d$ with $k d^2=(kd)\cdot d$; this power test is the basis of both constructions. [F4, F5]

2.1 For a prime $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{(f)}$, the set $P(\mathfrak q)$ is closed under addition inside each degree: if $a,b\in P(\mathfrak q)\cap S_e$, then expanding the $2d$-th power and grouping the terms of $(a+b)^{2d}/f^{2e}$ by the number $i$ of $a$-factors, every monomial $a^ib^{2d-i}/f^{2e}$ has $i\ge d$ or $2d-i\ge d$, and correspondingly equals $(a^d/f^e)\cdot(a^{i-d}b^{2d-i}/f^e)$ or $(b^d/f^e)\cdot(a^ib^{d-i}/f^e)$, a product of an element of $\mathfrak q$ (namely $a^d/f^e$ or $b^d/f^e$, which lies in $\mathfrak q$ because $a,b\in P(\mathfrak q)$ and $e=\deg a=\deg b$) with an element of $S_{(f)}$ as in step 1.1; hence $((a+b)^d/f^e)^2=(a+b)^{2d}/f^{2e}\in\mathfrak q$, and since $\mathfrak q$ is prime and radical, $(a+b)^d/f^e\in\mathfrak q$, that is, $a+b\in P(\mathfrak q)\cap S_e$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For a homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p\subseteq S$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$, membership in $Q(\mathfrak p)$ is independent of a fraction representation: if $a/f^k=b/f^m$ in $S_f$, [F5] gives $f^N(f^ma-f^kb)=0$ for some $N$, and primality with $f\notin\mathfrak p$ gives $a\in\mathfrak p$ if and only if $b\in\mathfrak p$. The set $Q(\mathfrak p)$ of the Statement is therefore an ideal of $S_{(f)}$: it contains $0=0/f^0$; if $a/f^k,b/f^m\in Q(\mathfrak p)$ then their sum has numerator $af^m+bf^k\in\mathfrak p$, homogeneous of degree $(k+m)d$, and multiplication by any fraction $c/f^\ell\in S_{(f)}$ gives numerator $ac\in\mathfrak p$. It is proper because $1=f^k/f^k\in Q(\mathfrak p)$ would force $f\in\mathfrak p$. It is prime because if a product represented by $ab/f^{k+m}$ lies in $Q(\mathfrak p)$, then $ab\in\mathfrak p$, so the homogeneous numerator $a$ or $b$ lies in $\mathfrak p$ and the corresponding factor lies in $Q(\mathfrak p)$. [F3, F5, step 1.1]

3.1 For a prime $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{(f)}$, the span $\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)=\bigoplus_e\bigl(P(\mathfrak q)\cap S_e\bigr)$ is a homogeneous ideal of $S$: it contains $0$ and is closed under addition componentwise by step 2.1. For homogeneous $s\in S_m$ and $a\in P(\mathfrak q)\cap S_e$ one has $(sa)^d/f^{e+m}=(a^d/f^e)\cdot(s^d/f^m)\in\mathfrak q$ because $s^d/f^m\in S_{(f)}$, so $sa\in P(\mathfrak q)$. Distributing products of finite sums of homogeneous elements extends this closure to arbitrary $s\in S$ and arbitrary elements of the direct sum. Thus the direct sum is an ideal, and it is exactly the ideal generated by the homogeneous set $P(\mathfrak q)$. [F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 For a prime $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{(f)}$ the ideal $\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$ is prime and $f\notin\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$: if $a,b\in S$ are homogeneous with $ab\in\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$, then $(ab)^d/f^{\deg a+\deg b}=(a^d/f^{\deg a})(b^d/f^{\deg b})\in\mathfrak q$, so one of the two factors lies in $\mathfrak q$, that is, $a\in P(\mathfrak q)$ or $b\in P(\mathfrak q)$. This homogeneous test makes the homogeneous ideal prime: if arbitrary $u,v$ have nonzero images in $S/\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$, take their highest-degree nonzero homogeneous components; their product is the unique component of the highest possible degree in $uv$ and is nonzero by the homogeneous test. Thus $uv\notin\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$. Moreover $f^d/f^d=1\notin\mathfrak q$, so $f\notin P(\mathfrak q)$ and, $f$ being homogeneous, $f\notin\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$. [F3, F6, step 3.1]

4.2 The composite $Q\circ\mathfrak p(\cdot)$ is the identity: for a prime $\mathfrak q\subseteq S_{(f)}$ and an element $y=a/f^k\in S_{(f)}$ with $\deg a=kd$, one has $y\in Q(\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q))$ if and only if $a\in\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$, if and only if $y^d=a^d/f^{kd}\in\mathfrak q$, if and only if $y\in\mathfrak q$, the last step because $\mathfrak q$ is prime and hence radical in both directions. [F3, step 3.1, step 2.2]

5.1 The composite $\mathfrak p\circ Q$ is the identity: for a homogeneous prime $\mathfrak p$ with $f\notin\mathfrak p$ and homogeneous $a\in S$, one has $a\in P(Q(\mathfrak p))$ if and only if $a^d/f^{\deg a}\in Q(\mathfrak p)$, if and only if $a^d\in\mathfrak p$, if and only if $a\in\mathfrak p$, the last step because $\mathfrak p$ is prime and hence radical. Since $\mathfrak p(\mathfrak q)$ and both prime sets consist of homogeneous data, steps 4.1, 2.2, 4.2 and 5.1 exhibit mutually inverse bijections between the primes of $S_{(f)}$ and the homogeneous primes of $S$ avoiding $f$, which are exactly the points of $D_+(f)$ by [F1] and [F2]. [F1, F3, step 4.1, step 2.2, step 4.2]

6.1 The nilpotent case is consistent with the bijection and needs no prime-existence input: if $f^m=0$ for some $m\ge1$, then $1/1=f^m/f^m=0$ in $S_f$ by [F5], so $S_f=0$ and $S_{(f)}=0$ has no prime ideals, while $f$ lies in every prime ideal of $S$ and hence $D_+(f)=\varnothing$; both sides of the bijection are empty. If $f$ is not nilpotent then $S_f$ has $1\neq0$ by [F5]; the bijection is between two sets that are simultaneously empty or nonempty by steps 4.2 and 5.1, and no converse emptiness statement is claimed here. [F5, step 5.1]

6.2 Distinguished opens: let $g\in S_+$ be homogeneous of positive degree $e$ and put $\tau=g^d/f^e\in S_{(f)}$, a well-defined element because $g^d$ is homogeneous of degree $de=ed$. For $\mathfrak p$ a homogeneous prime with $f\notin\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak q=Q(\mathfrak p)$, step 5.1 gives $\tau\in\mathfrak q=Q(\mathfrak p)$ if and only if $g^d\in\mathfrak p$, if and only if $g\in\mathfrak p$, the last because $\mathfrak p$ is prime and radical. Hence $\mathfrak p\in D_+(fg)$ if and only if $fg\notin\mathfrak p$, if and only if $g\notin\mathfrak p$ (as $f\notin\mathfrak p$ and $\mathfrak p$ is prime), if and only if $\tau\notin Q(\mathfrak p)$, that is, $Q(\mathfrak p)\in D(\tau)$. By [F2] we have $D_+(fg)=D_+(f)\cap D_+(g)$, so the bijection of step 5.1 restricts to a bijection $D_+(fg)\to D(\tau)$ onto the distinguished open of $\tau$ in $\operatorname{Spec}S_{(f)}$. [F2, F3, step 5.1]

7.1 Summary. Steps 1.1-5.1 prove clauses (1)-(3) of the Statement and step 6.2 proves clause (4); the maps are given by explicit formulas in terms of the power test $a\mapsto a^d/f^{\deg a}$, no choice is made in either construction, and [A1] enters only as the inherited hypothesis on the ambient affine spectrum, so the bijection itself is choice-free. [A1, step 5.1, step 6.2] ∎
