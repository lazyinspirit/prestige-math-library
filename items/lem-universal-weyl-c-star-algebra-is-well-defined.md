## Statement

Let $A_0(V,\omega,\lambda)$, its positive unital functional set, and
$\|\cdot\|_u$ be as in
[[def-universal-weyl-c-star-algebra]]. Under Countable Choice, the twisted
multiplication and involution make $A_0$ a unital $*$-algebra, the displayed
supremum is a finite nonzero C*-seminorm, and its kernel quotient completion is
the universal C*-algebra generated by unitaries $u_v$ with
$$u_vu_w=e^{-i\lambda\omega(v,w)/2}u_{v+w},\qquad u_v^*=u_{-v}.$$
Every unital $*$-representation of the algebraic relations extends uniquely to
a bounded $*$-representation of this completion. No strong continuity of
$v\mapsto u_v$ is part of this universal property.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional real symplectic space $(V,\omega)$, a real $\lambda$, the finite-support space $A_0(V,\omega,\lambda)$, and Countable Choice.

[F1] The construction in [[def-universal-weyl-c-star-algebra]] uses $m_\lambda(v,w)=e^{-i\lambda\omega(v,w)/2}$, basis symbols $\delta_v$, $\delta_v\delta_w=m_\lambda(v,w)\delta_{v+w}$, $\delta_v^*=\delta_{-v}$, the positive unital functionals, and the universal seminorm defined from their GNS representations.

[F2] A complex C*-algebra is a complete normed $*$-algebra satisfying $\|a^*a\|=\|a\|^2$; bounded $*$-homomorphisms use the operator norm ([[def-c-star-algebra]], [[def-bounded-linear-operator]], [[def-operator-norm]]).

[F3] For every nonzero complex Hilbert space $K$, $\mathcal B(K)$ with the operator norm and Hilbert adjoint is a C*-algebra; in particular $\|T^*T\|=\|T\|^2$ ([[lem-bounded-hilbert-operators-form-a-c-star-algebra]]).

[A1] Countable Choice permits the metric completions of the GNS pre-Hilbert spaces and the universal norm quotient; the inner-product and norm-completion results give the required Hilbert and Banach structures ([[def-countable-choice]], [[thm-metric-completion-exists]], [[thm-metric-completion-carries-a-unique-banach-space-structure]], [[thm-completion-of-an-inner-product-space-is-hilbert]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The construction and hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Bilinearity gives $m_\lambda(v,w)m_\lambda(v+w,z)=m_\lambda(w,z)m_\lambda(v,w+z)$, so the two parenthesizations of $\delta_v\delta_w\delta_z$ agree. Also $m_\lambda(0,v)=m_\lambda(v,0)=1$, so $\delta_0$ is a two-sided unit. Since $\omega(v,-v)=0$, $\delta_v^*\delta_v=\delta_v\delta_v^*=\delta_0$. Finally, $\overline{m_\lambda(v,w)}=m_\lambda(-w,-v)$, which gives $(\delta_v\delta_w)^*=\delta_w^*\delta_v^*$; by bilinearity and conjugate-linearity these identities extend to all finite sums. [F1, algebra]

1.2 For any positive unital $\varphi$, the form $B_\varphi(a,b)=\varphi(b^*a)$ is positive semidefinite. Positivity of $B_\varphi(a+tb,a+tb)$ for every complex $t$ gives $|B_\varphi(a,b)|^2\le B_\varphi(a,a)B_\varphi(b,b)$: if $B_\varphi(b,b)>0$, minimize the quadratic in $t$; if it is zero, varying the phase and magnitude of $t$ forces $B_\varphi(a,b)=0$. Therefore its null space $N_\varphi=\{a:B_\varphi(a,a)=0\}$ is orthogonal to every vector and the quotient has the inner product specified in [F1]. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Write $a=\sum_v a_v\delta_v$ with finite support and define $\varphi_0(a)=a_0$. The coefficient of $\delta_0$ in $a^*a$ is $\sum_v\overline{a_v}a_vm_\lambda(-v,v)=\sum_v|a_v|^2$, because $\omega(v,-v)=0$. Thus $\varphi_0(a^*a)\ge0$ and $\varphi_0(\delta_0)=1$; the positive unital functional set in [F1] is nonempty. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Left multiplication by $\delta_v$ is an isometry on this quotient: $B_\varphi(\delta_va,\delta_va)=\varphi(a^*\delta_v^*\delta_va)=\varphi(a^*a)=B_\varphi(a,a)$. Hence it preserves the null space. For a finite sum $a=\sum_v a_v\delta_v$, the triangle inequality gives $\|L_a[b]\|\le(\sum_v|a_v|)\|[b]\|$. Left multiplication therefore extends to a bounded operator on the Hilbert completion of the quotient, and its adjoint is $L_{a^*}$ because $B_\varphi(ab,c)=B_\varphi(b,a^*c)$. Associativity from step 1.1 makes $\pi_\varphi(a):=L_a$ a unital $*$-representation. [A1, F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra, construct]

3.1 The supremum in [F1] is over a set of functionals (a subset of the function set $\mathbb C^{A_0}$), is nonempty by step 2.1, and is finite since step 2.2 gives $\|\pi_\varphi(a)\|\le\sum_v|a_v|$ for every $\varphi$. Each GNS Hilbert space is nonzero because $\|[\delta_0]\|^2=\varphi(\delta_0)=1$, so [F3] applies to every $\pi_\varphi$. Since each $\pi_\varphi$ is a $*$-representation, $\|a^*a\|_u=\sup_\varphi\|\pi_\varphi(a)^*\pi_\varphi(a)\|=\sup_\varphi\|\pi_\varphi(a)\|^2=\|a\|_u^2$. Submultiplicativity and $\|a^*\|_u=\|a\|_u$ follow by the same operator-norm calculation. Thus $\|\cdot\|_u$ is a nonzero C*-seminorm; its kernel is a $*$-ideal, and the induced norm on $A_0/\ker\|\cdot\|_u$ is a C*-norm. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Let $\rho:A_0\to\mathcal B(\mathcal K)$ be any unital $*$-representation. If $\mathcal K=\{0\}$, the unique zero representation extends; assume otherwise and choose a unit vector $\xi\in\mathcal K$. The vector functional $\varphi_\xi(a)=\langle\rho(a)\xi,\xi\rangle$ is positive and unital. The map $[a]\mapsto\rho(a)\xi$ preserves the inner product on the GNS quotient, extends to a unitary from its completion onto the cyclic subspace $\overline{\rho(A_0)\xi}$, and intertwines $\pi_{\varphi_\xi}$ with the restriction of $\rho$. Therefore $\|\rho(a)\xi\|\le\|\pi_{\varphi_\xi}(a)\|\le\|a\|_u$; taking the supremum over unit vectors gives $\|\rho(a)\|\le\|a\|_u$. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.2, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 The quotient completion in [F1] is a C*-algebra: multiplication and $*$ extend continuously by submultiplicativity and isometry, and the C*-identity passes to limits. Step 4.1 makes every unital $*$-representation contractive for $\|\cdot\|_u$, so it extends uniquely to the completion; conversely each representation of the completion restricts to one of $A_0$. The state $\varphi_0$ from step 2.1 sends $\delta_0$ to a unit vector in its GNS space, so $\|\delta_0\|_u=1$ and the completion is nonzero. This proves the universal property and all claims. [A1, F1, F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
