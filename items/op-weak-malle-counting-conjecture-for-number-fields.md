## Open problem

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Fix a number field $K$, an integer $n\ge2$, and a nontrivial transitive permutation group $G\le S_n$. For each real $X\ge1$, let $N_{K,G}(X)$ count degree-$n$ extensions $L/K$ up to field isomorphisms fixing $K$ pointwise, whose smallest Galois extension $\widetilde L/K$ containing $L$ has Galois group acting on the embeddings $L\hookrightarrow\overline K$ as a permutation group isomorphic to $G$, and whose relative discriminant ideal $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ has norm $|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak d_{L/K}|\le X$. Here $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is generated by $\det(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x_i x_j))$ over all ordered $K$-bases of $L$ consisting of algebraic integers. For $g\in G$, put $\operatorname{ind}(g)=n-\#\{\text{cycles of }g\text{ on }\{1,\ldots,n\}\}$, including fixed points, and $a(G)=\bigl(\min_{1\ne g\in G}\operatorname{ind}(g)\bigr)^{-1}$. Is it true that for every $\epsilon>0$ there are constants $c_{K,G,\epsilon},C_{K,G,\epsilon}>0$ and $X_0\ge1$ such that
$$c_{K,G,\epsilon}X^{a(G)-\epsilon}\le N_{K,G}(X)\le C_{K,G,\epsilon}X^{a(G)+\epsilon}\qquad\text{for every }X\ge X_0?$$

## Status

Status reviewed on 2026-10-26; this was a bounded source review, not an exhaustive search of every recent preprint. The problem asks for the weaker exponent form adopted in this page's design. The classical weak Malle statement in much of the literature includes the stronger lower bound $N_{K,G}(X)\gg X^{a(G)}$; that classical pair implies the exponent form above, but the converse is false. The reviewed literature leaves the universal exponent question unresolved: Alberts–Lemke Oliver–Wang–Wood prove the classical weak pair for many specified families and also give strong-form counterexamples; Ray proves lower bounds for specified symplectic groups; Alberts proves lower bounds for specified families; and the Klüners counterexample shows failure of the strong logarithmic prediction while retaining the weak form for that particular group. Wang's 2025 paper concerns counterexamples to Türkelli's modification, not a resolution of the universal exponent question. No universal proof or counterexample was found in the sources checked.
