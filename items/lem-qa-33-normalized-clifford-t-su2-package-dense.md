## Statement

Use the exact one-qubit matrices $H$ and $T=\operatorname{diag}(1,e^{i\pi/4})$ of [[def-qc-one-qubit-gate-alphabet-and-rotations]]. The matrices $h=iH$ and $t=e^{-i\pi/8}T$, together with their inverses, are determinant-one representatives in $SU(2)$. Their generated subgroup is dense in $SU(2)$. Replacing each normalized letter in a word by its underlying physical $H,T,T^\dagger$ letter changes the product only by one scalar phase, so an unconditional one-qubit circuit can approximate every $SU(2)$ target up to an irrelevant global phase. A normalized letter must not be placed under a control without correcting its scalar phase.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact one-qubit $H,T$ matrices and the dense-projective Clifford–T result.

[F1] [[lem-qc-clifford-t-projective-density]]: $h=iH$ and $t=e^{-i\pi/8}T$ lie in $SU(2)$ and generate a dense subgroup there.

[F2] [[def-qc-one-qubit-gate-alphabet-and-rotations]]: $H$ and $T$ have their displayed exact phases; a scalar phase is part of a matrix when it is controlled.

## Proof

1.1 Direct determinants give $\det(iH)=i^2\det(H)=1$ because $\det(H)=-1$, and $\det(e^{-i\pi/8}T)=e^{-i\pi/4}e^{i\pi/4}=1$. Unitarity is preserved by unit-modulus scalars, so $h,t\in SU(2)$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 Density of the group generated by $h,t$ is [F1]. For any fixed finite word, replacing each normalized generator by its physical gate multiplies the whole word by the product of the corresponding scalar phases, which is a single scalar on the entire one-qubit subcircuit. Such a scalar does not affect any outcome probability when the subcircuit is applied unconditionally. If the word is controlled, this scalar acts only on the active control block and is therefore not irrelevant; the statement explicitly excludes that use without correction. [F1, F2, algebra] ∎
