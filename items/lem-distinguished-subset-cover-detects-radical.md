## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and let $f,f_1,\dots,f_n\in R$ with $n\ge 1$. Then
$$ D(f)\subseteq D(f_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(f_n) $$
if and only if
$$ f\in\sqrt{(f_1,\dots,f_n)}. $$
Equivalently, some positive power of $f$ lies in the ideal $(f_1,\dots,f_n)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, elements $f,f_1,\dots,f_n\in R$, an integer $n\ge 1$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[L1] Two ideals have the same vanishing set exactly when their radicals agree ([[lem-vanishing-sets-detect-radicals]]).

[L2] For any $h\in R$, the principal distinguished subset $D(h)$ is the complement of $V((h))$ in $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ ([[def-principal-distinguished-subset-of-spectrum]]), where $(h)$ denotes the principal ideal generated by $h$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $J=(f_1,\dots,f_n)$. If $f\in\sqrt{J}$ and $\mathfrak p\in D(f)$, then $f\notin\mathfrak p$. A prime ideal containing $J$ would contain every element of $\sqrt{J}$ and therefore would contain $f$, so $\mathfrak p$ cannot contain $J$. Hence at least one $f_i$ is omitted by $\mathfrak p$, which means $\mathfrak p\in D(f_i)$ for some $i$. Thus $D(f)\subseteq D(f_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(f_n)$. [L2, given, algebra]

1.2 Conversely, assume $D(f)\subseteq D(f_1)\cup\cdots\cup D(f_n)$. If $\mathfrak p$ contains $J$ and omitted $f$, then $\mathfrak p$ would lie in the left-hand side and hence in some $D(f_i)$, contradicting $f_i\in J\subseteq\mathfrak p$. Therefore every prime ideal containing $J$ also contains $f$, so $V(J)=V(J+(f))$. By [L1], the radicals of $J$ and $J+(f)$ agree; since $f\in J+(f)$, this forces $f\in\sqrt{J}$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 Steps 1.1 and 1.2 prove the equivalence. The final sentence is just the definition of membership in a radical ideal. [step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
