## Statement

Let $w\in W$ and put $\Pi_w=\{\alpha\in\Phi^+:w^{-1}\alpha\in\Phi^-\}$. Then $\#\Pi_w=\ell(w)$, $w\circ0=-\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_w}\alpha$, and for $\rho$ the half-sum of positive roots $\rho-w\rho=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_w}\alpha$. If $S\subseteq\Phi^+$ satisfies $\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_w}\alpha$, then $S=\Pi_w$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite reduced crystallographic root system $\Phi$ with positive system $\Phi^+$, the Weyl group $W$ generated by the $s_\alpha$, the length function $\ell$, the Weyl vector $\rho=\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\alpha$, and the dot action $w\circ0=w\rho-\rho$.

[F1] For a positive-root reflection $s_\alpha$: $\ell(s_\alpha w)<\ell(w)$ if and only if $w^{-1}\alpha<0$, and for a simple reflection $s_i$ one has $\ell(s_iw)=\ell(w)\pm1$; also $\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(w)$ ([[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]]).

[F2] Every positive root is a nonnegative integral combination of the simple roots, with at least one positive coefficient. Each simple reflection permutes $\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha_i\}$ and sends $\alpha_i$ to $-\alpha_i$; the reflections $s_\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ act on $E$ ([[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]).

[F3] $\rho$ is the half-sum of the positive roots and the dot action is $w\circ0=w(\rho)-\rho$ ([[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]], [[lem-rho-shift-intertwines-the-dot-and-ordinary-weyl-actions]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $w\ne1$, choose a reduced word $w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}$ with $k=\ell(w)>0$. The product $s_{i_1}w=s_{i_2}\cdots s_{i_k}$ is represented by a word of length $k-1$, so $\ell(s_{i_1}w)\le k-1=\ell(w)-1$; by [F1] the length changes by exactly one, so $\ell(s_{i_1}w)=\ell(w)-1$ and, with $\alpha:=\alpha_{i_1}$, the criterion of [F1] gives $w^{-1}\alpha<0$. Write $w=s_\alpha w'$ with $w'=s_\alpha w$, so $\ell(w)=\ell(w')+1$ and $\alpha\in\Pi_w$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 For $\alpha$ as in step 1.1 one has $s_\alpha(\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\})=\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$ by [F2] and $\alpha\notin w'(\Phi^-)$ because $(w')^{-1}\alpha=w^{-1}s_\alpha\alpha=-w^{-1}\alpha>0$. Hence $s_\alpha(\Pi_{w'})=s_\alpha(\Phi^+)\cap s_\alpha(w'(\Phi^-))=((\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\})\cup\{-\alpha\})\cap w(\Phi^-)=(\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\})\cap w(\Phi^-)=\Pi_w\setminus\{\alpha\}$, so $\Pi_w=s_\alpha(\Pi_{w'})\cup\{\alpha\}$ is a disjoint union. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Induction on $\ell(w)$ proves the three identities simultaneously. For $w=1$ all three sides vanish. For $w\ne1$, apply step 2.1 and the induction hypothesis to $w'$: $\#\Pi_w=\#\Pi_{w'}+1=\ell(w')+1=\ell(w)$; similarly $\rho-w\rho=(\rho-s_\alpha\rho)+s_\alpha(\rho-w'\rho)=\alpha+s_\alpha\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{w'}}\beta=\alpha+\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{w'}}s_\alpha\beta=\sum_{\gamma\in\Pi_w}\gamma$, using $s_\alpha\rho=\rho-\alpha$; and therefore $w\circ0=w\rho-\rho=-\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_w}\alpha$. [F1, F2, F3, step 2.1, induction]

4.1 It remains to prove uniqueness. Let $S\subseteq\Phi^+$ with $\sum_{\alpha\in S}\alpha=\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi_w}\alpha$. We induct on $\ell(w)$. For $w=1$, $\Pi_1=\varnothing$ and the assumed sum of $S$ is zero. Every positive root has nonnegative simple-root coefficients with at least one positive coefficient, so a nonempty set of positive roots has a sum with at least one positive coefficient and cannot sum to zero. Thus $S=\varnothing=\Pi_1$. For $w\ne1$ use the element $\alpha$ of step 1.1. If $\alpha\in S$, put $S'=s_\alpha(S\setminus\{\alpha\})$. Then $S'\subseteq\Phi^+$ by [F2], and using step 3.1 for $w'$ and linearity of $s_\alpha$ one computes $\sum_{\beta\in S'}\beta=s_\alpha(\sum_{\beta\in S}\beta-\alpha)=s_\alpha(\rho-w\rho-\alpha)=\rho-w'\rho=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{w'}}\beta$. By induction $S'=\Pi_{w'}$, hence $S=s_\alpha(\Pi_{w'})\cup\{\alpha\}=\Pi_w$. [F2, step 2.1, step 3.1, induction, algebra]

5.1 If $\alpha\notin S$, then $S\subseteq\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$, so $s_\alpha(S)\subseteq\Phi^+\setminus\{\alpha\}$ and $s_\alpha(S)\cup\{\alpha\}\subseteq\Phi^+$ is a disjoint union with $\sum_{\beta\in s_\alpha(S)\cup\{\alpha\}}\beta=s_\alpha(\rho-w\rho)+\alpha=\rho-w'\rho=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{w'}}\beta$. By induction $s_\alpha(S)\cup\{\alpha\}=\Pi_{w'}$, but $\alpha\notin\Pi_{w'}$ because $(w')^{-1}\alpha=-w^{-1}\alpha>0$ by step 1.1. This contradiction rules out $\alpha\notin S$, so the previous case applies and $S=\Pi_w$ always. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
