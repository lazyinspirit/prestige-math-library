## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the standing hypothesis (H) of
[[def-elementary-predictable-brownian-integrand]]. Let
$X_t=X_0+\int_0^tb_s\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma_s\,dB_s$ be a real continuous Brownian
Ito process [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]] and let
$f\in C^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R)$, meaning that $f$, its first time
derivative $\partial_tf$ and its first two space derivatives
$\partial_xf,\partial^2_{x}f$ exist and are continuous on
$[0,\infty)\times\mathbb R$. Then, up to indistinguishability, for every
$t\ge0$
$$f(t,X_t)=f(0,X_0)+\int_0^t\Bigl(\partial_tf+b\,\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf\Bigr)(s,X_s)\,ds+\int_0^t\sigma_s\,\partial_xf(s,X_s)\,dB_s,$$
where the stochastic integral is the localized Ito integral of the predictable
locally square-integrable process $\sigma_s\partial_xf(s,X_s)$
[[thm-localized-ito-integral]] and the Lebesgue integral is the pathwise
integral of the continuous process $s\mapsto(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf)(s,X_s)$,
which is finite almost surely for every $t$. In differential notation,
$df(t,X_t)=(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_xf)(t,X_t)\,dt+\sigma_t\partial_xf(t,X_t)\,dB_t$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, (H), a real continuous Brownian Ito process $X=X_0+A+M$ with $A_t=\int_0^tb_s\,ds$ and $M=\int\sigma\,dB$, a function $f\in C^{1,2}([0,\infty)\times\mathbb R)$, a finite horizon $T>0$, and an arbitrary deterministic partition sequence $(\pi_n)$ of $[0,T]$ with mesh $\delta_n\to0$. The stopping time $\rho_c$ is defined as in [F7] below.
 
[F1] **Paths, predictability and local boundedness.** $X$ is adapted with continuous paths, hence predictable, and on each time interval a continuous path is bounded; every continuous function of $s$ and $X_s$ is again predictable, and products with $b$ and $\sigma$ are predictable. [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]] [[lem-adapted-continuous-processes-are-progressively-measurable]] [[def-progressively-measurable-and-predictable-process]]
 
[F2] **Localized-integral interfaces.** For a predictable $G$ with finite energy: $E(\int_0^TG\,dB)^2=E\int_0^TG^2ds$, the integral over a subinterval is the integral of the restriction, the stopping identity holds, the elementary sums converge to the integral, and the Doob maximal bound $E\sup_{t\le T}(\int_0^tG\,dB)^2\le4E\int_0^TG^2ds$ holds. A locally square-integrable predictable $G$ has a localized integral whose stopped pieces are the finite-energy integrals of $G1_{(0,\rho]}$; and if $c$ is bounded and $\mathcal F_u$-measurable then the localized integral of $cG$ over an interval inside $(u,\infty)$ equals $c$ times that of $G$, because the finite-energy case follows from the elementary case and the $L^2$ isometry and the general case by stopping and uniqueness. [[thm-localized-ito-integral]] [[thm-stopping-an-ito-integral]] [[thm-ito-isometry-and-linearity-in-predictable-l2]] [[thm-doob-maximal-bound-for-the-ito-integral]] [[thm-ito-integral-process-has-a-continuous-martingale-version]] [[def-ito-integral-for-square-integrable-predictable-processes]] [[def-locally-square-integrable-predictable-brownian-integrand]]
 
[F3] **Quadratic variation and covariation of the class.** $[X]_t=\int_0^t\sigma_s^2\,ds$ and, more generally, $\sum_j\Delta_jX\Delta_jY\to[X,Y]=\int\xi\eta\,ds$ uniformly in probability for class processes; in particular the step-convention quadratic sums $Q_n(t)=\sum_{j:\,s_j\le t}\Delta_jX^2$ satisfy $\sup_{t\le T}|Q_n(t)-\int_0^t\sigma_s^2ds|\to0$ in probability for every deterministic vanishing-mesh sequence and both conventions. [[thm-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-quadratic-variation-along-a-partition-sequence]]
 
[F4] **Taylor expansion with a third-order remainder.** Let $f\in C^3$ on an open set containing the closed segment from $a=(t_0,x_0)$ to $a+h=(t_0+h_1,x_0+h_2)$. Then $f(a+h)=f(a)+\partial_tf(a)h_1+\partial_xf(a)h_2+\tfrac12\bigl(\partial^2_{tt}f(a)h_1^2+2\partial_t\partial_xf(a)h_1h_2+\partial^2_{xx}f(a)h_2^2\bigr)+R$ with $|R|\le M_3|h|^3$, where $M_3$ bounds the third partial derivatives on a ball containing the segment: apply the one-variable Taylor formula with remainder bound to $u\mapsto f(a+uh)$ on $[0,1]$. [[cor-second-order-taylor-expansion-with-the-hessian]] [[cor-multivariable-taylor-formula-with-peano-remainder]] [[def-multivariable-taylor-polynomial]] [[def-taylor-polynomial-and-remainder]] [[cor-taylor-remainder-bound]]
 
[F5] **Staircase comparison and the weighted pullback of quadratic variation.** (a) If $G$ is a continuous adapted process and $G^{(n)}$ its left-endpoint staircase on $\pi_n$, then for a locally square-integrable predictable $H$ the integrals of $G^{(n)}H$ converge to that of $GH$ in $L^2(P)$ on each event of uniform convergence of $G^{(n)}$ to $G$ with finite energy of $H$. (b) If $w$ is continuous adapted with $|w|\le K$ and $M=\int\sigma\,dB$ is a finite-energy integral, then $\sum_jw(t_j)\Delta_jM^2\to\int_0^Tw_s\sigma_s^2\,ds$ in probability, and the convergence is uniform in the upper summation limit; this is the weighted pullback of the quadratic variation, proved in steps 1.4--2.1 below. [[thm-ito-isometry-and-linearity-in-predictable-l2]] [[thm-quadratic-covariation-of-brownian-ito-processes]] [[def-ito-integral-of-an-elementary-predictable-process]]
 
[F6] **Bounded Riemann integrals.** If $Z$ is continuous on $[0,T]$ and $h$ pathwise Lebesgue-integrable, then $\sum_jZ_{t_j}\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}h_s\,ds\to\int_0^TZ_sh_s\,ds$ along vanishing meshes, the error being at most $\max_j\sup_{I_j}|Z-Z_{t_j}|\int_0^T|h|$. A continuous function on the compact set $[0,T]$ and a continuous function on a compact cylinder are uniformly continuous, so maximal oscillations on the mesh intervals vanish. [[thm-heine-cantor-r]] [[def-continuity-real]]
 
[F7] **Localization of the class and of the coefficients.** For $c>0$ put $\rho_c:=\inf\{t:\max(|X_t|,\int_0^t|b|,\int_0^t\sigma^2)\ge c\}\wedge T$. Then $\rho_c$ is a stopping time, the stopped process $X^{\rho_c}$ is a continuous Brownian Ito process with coefficients $b1_{[0,\rho_c]},\sigma1_{[0,\rho_c]}$, on $\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ the process and all its integrals over $[0,T]$ coincide with the stopped ones, and $\rho_c\uparrow\infty$ almost surely as $c\to\infty$ because $X$ is continuous and $\int|b|,\int\sigma^2$ are finite and continuous in the upper limit almost surely. [[def-continuous-brownian-ito-process]] [[thm-localized-ito-integral]] [[thm-stopping-an-ito-integral]] [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]] [[thm-heine-cantor-r]]
 
[F8] **Cutoff and mollification on a cylinder.** Let $g\in C^{1,2}$ on an open set containing a compact cylinder $K=[-1,T+1]\times[-c-1,c+1]$. There are a function $\chi\in C_c^\infty$ equal to $1$ on $K$ and a mollifier family $\rho_\varepsilon$ such that $g_\varepsilon:=(\chi g)*\rho_\varepsilon$ is smooth and compactly supported, and on every compact subset of the interior of $\{\chi=1\}$ the functions $g_\varepsilon,\partial_tg_\varepsilon,\partial_xg_\varepsilon,\partial^2_{xx}g_\varepsilon$ converge uniformly to $g,\partial_tg,\partial_xg,\partial^2_{xx}g$. Reflection across $t=0$ by $g(t,x):=f(t,x)$ for $t\ge0$ and $g(t,x):=2f(0,x)-f(-t,x)$ for $t<0$ extends $f$ to a $C^{1,2}$ function on a neighbourhood of $K$. [[def-c-c-and-c-c-infinity-on-rn]] [[def-mollifier-family-generated-by-a-unit-mass-smooth-bump]] [[thm-convolution-with-a-mollifier-is-smooth-and-differentiates-under-the-integral-sign]] [[lem-a-compact-set-inside-a-bounded-open-set-admits-an-explicit-compactly-supported-cutoff]] [[thm-c-c-infinity-rn-is-dense-in-l-p-of-rn]]
 
[F9] **Convergence tools and estimates.** Cauchy--Schwarz for sums and expectations; dominated convergence for pathwise Lebesgue integrals; Fatou's lemma; and the fact that a sequence bounded by $R_n$ with $ER_n\to0$ converges to $0$ in probability. [[cor-cauchy-schwarz-for-random-variables]] [[thm-dominated-convergence]] [[thm-fatou-lemma]] [[def-convergence-in-probability]]
 
[F10] **AC bookkeeping.** Choice is declared for the ambient conditional-expectation, completeness and density interfaces; all stopping levels, partitions and mollification scales used below are canonical functions of the given data. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]
 
 
 
 

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.
 
1.1 Reduction to a bounded localized problem: fix $c>0$ and replace $X$ by $X^{\rho_c}$ as in [F7]. On $\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ the two sides of the claimed identity for the stopped and the original process agree whenever they agree for the stopped one, because $X,A,M,b,\sigma$ coincide on $[0,\rho_c]$. On $\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ one also has $|X_s|\le c$, $\int_0^T|b|\le c$ and $\int_0^T\sigma^2\le c$, so $X$ stays in the compact cylinder $[-c,c]$, the stochastic integrand $\sigma\partial_xf(\cdot,X)$ has finite energy bounded by $c\sup|\partial_xf|^2$, and every continuous function of $(s,X_s)$ is bounded on $[0,T]$. It remains to prove the identity under these bounds; the localization is removed at the end by letting $c\to\infty$. [F1, F2, F7, given]
 
1.2 Setup of the $C^3$ case: assume first that $f\in C^3$ on a neighbourhood of the cylinder $[0,T]\times[-c,c]$ with finite bounds $M_0,M_1,M_2,M_3$ for its partial derivatives of orders $0,1,2,3$ there. For the partition $\pi_n$ write $\Delta t_j=t_{j+1}-t_j$, $\Delta X_j=X_{t_{j+1}}-X_{t_j}=\Delta A_j+\Delta M_j$. [F1, F3, given]
 
1.3 Weighted pullback of the quadratic variation: let $w$ be continuous adapted with $|w|\le K$ and let $H=\sigma$ have finite energy; then $\sum_jw(t_j)\Delta_jM^2\to\int_0^Tw_sH_s^2ds$ in probability, uniformly in the upper summation limit. [F3, F5, F6]
 
1.4 Weighted pullback, elementary weights: let $w=\sum_a\lambda_a1_{(u_a,u_{a+1}]}$ be elementary with bounded coefficients and fixed deterministic partition points $u_a$ of $[0,T]$; refine $\pi_n$ by adding the finitely many points $u_a$ to get a deterministic partition sequence $\rho_n$ with mesh $\to0$. For the step sums $Q^{\rho}_n(v):=\sum_{j:\,s_j\le v}\Delta_jM^2$ of that refined sequence, [F3] gives $\sup_v|Q^{\rho}_n(v)-\int_0^vH^2ds|\to0$ in probability, and the cross sums of block $a$ equal $Q^{\rho}_n(u_{a+1})-Q^{\rho}_n(u_a)$ up to the at most two boundary intervals of size $O(\max_j|\Delta_jM|^2)$, which vanish by continuity of the path of $M$. Hence $\sum_jw(t_j)\Delta_jM^2\to\sum_a\lambda_a\int_{u_a}^{u_{a+1}}H^2ds=\int_0^TwH^2ds$ in probability, uniformly in the upper limit. [F3, F6, given]
 
1.5 Remainder of the Taylor expansion: with $h_j=(t_{j+1}-t_j,X_{t_{j+1}}-X_{t_j})$ the Taylor expansion [F4] gives, for each $j$, $f(t_{j+1},X_{t_{j+1}})-f(t_j,X_{t_j})=\partial_tfh_1+\partial_xfh_2+\tfrac12(\partial^2_{tt}fh_1^2+2\partial_t\partial_xfh_1h_2+\partial^2_{xx}fh_2^2)+R_j$ with $|R_j|\le M_3(|h_1|+|h_2|)^3\le4M_3(|\Delta t_j|^3+|\Delta X_j|^3)$. Summing: $\sum_j|\Delta t_j|^3\le\operatorname{mesh}(\pi_n)^2T\to0$ and $\sum_j|\Delta X_j|^3\le\max_j|\Delta X_j|\sum_j|\Delta X_j|^2\le\max_j|\Delta X_j|(2\sum_j\Delta A_j^2+2Q_n(T))$, where $\max_j|\Delta X_j|\to0$ almost surely by continuity of the paths of $A$ and $M$ and $Q_n(T)-[X]_T\to0$ in probability by [F3], so the sum of remainders tends to $0$ in probability. [F3, F4, F6]
 
1.6 First-order terms: $\sum_j\partial_tf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta t_j\to\int_0^T\partial_tf(s,X_s)ds$ and $\sum_j\partial_xf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta A_j\to\int_0^T\partial_xf(s,X_s)b_s\,ds$ both almost surely by the Riemann estimate [F6] applied with the continuous bounded weights $\partial_tf(\cdot,X)$ and $\partial_xf(\cdot,X)$; and the stochastic part satisfies $\sum_j\partial_xf(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta_jM=\int_0^Tg^{(n)}\sigma\,dB$ for the left-endpoint staircase $g^{(n)}$ of $s\mapsto\partial_xf(s,X_s)$, which converges in $L^2(P)$ to $\int_0^T\partial_xf(s,X_s)\sigma_s\,dB$ by the staircase comparison [F5](a) and [F2]. [F2, F5, F6, given]
 
2.1 Weighted pullback, continuous weights: for continuous adapted $w$ with $|w|\le K$ and its left-endpoint staircase $w^{(m)}$ on the deterministic grid of mesh $2^{-m}T$, uniform continuity of $s\mapsto w_s$ on $[0,T]$ gives $\sup_s|w^{(m)}_s-w_s|\to0$; therefore $|\sum_j(w(t_j)-w^{(m)}(t_j))\Delta_jM^2|\le\sup_s|w^{(m)}_s-w_s|\,Q_n(T)\to0$ in probability because $Q_n(T)\to\int_0^TH^2ds$ is bounded in probability, and $w^{(m)}$ is elementary with bounded $\mathcal F_{t_a}$-measurable coefficients, so step 1.4 applies to it and $\int_0^Tw^{(m)}H^2ds\to\int_0^TwH^2ds$ by dominated convergence with the bound $Kc$. This proves [F5](b). [F5, F6, step 1.4]
 
3.1 Second-order terms: by step 2.1 applied to $w=\partial^2_{xx}f(\cdot,X)$, $\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{xx}f(t_j,X_{t_j})\Delta_jM^2\to\tfrac12\int_0^T\partial^2_{xx}f(s,X_s)\sigma_s^2ds$ in probability. The mixed drift--martingale term satisfies $\bigl|\sum_j\partial^2_{xx}f\Delta_jA\Delta_jM\bigr|\le M_2(\sum_j\Delta_jA^2)^{1/2}(\sum_j\Delta_jM^2)^{1/2}\le M_2\bigl(\max_j|\Delta_jA|\int_0^T|b|\bigr)^{1/2}Q_n(T)^{1/2}\to0$ in probability, because $\max_j|\Delta_jA_j|\le\max_j\int_{t_j}^{t_{j+1}}|b|\to0$ by the bounded Riemann estimate [F6] and $Q_n(T)$ is bounded in probability; and the two purely drift/time terms satisfy $\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{xx}f\Delta_jA^2\le\tfrac{M_2}{2}\max_j|\Delta_jA|\int_0^T|b|\to0$ and $\sum_j\bigl(\partial_t\partial_xf\Delta t_j\Delta X_j\bigr)+\sum_j\tfrac12\partial^2_{tt}f\Delta t_j^2\le(M_2+\tfrac{M_2}{2})\operatorname{mesh}(\pi_n)T+ M_2T\max_j|\Delta X_j|\to0$. [F3, F6, step 2.1]
 
4.1 Assemble the $C^3$ case at $T$: summing the exact expansion [step 1.5] over $j$, the left side telescopes to $f(T,X_T)-f(0,X_0)$, while the right side is the sum of the terms controlled in steps 1.5, 1.6 and 3.1; passing to the limit along $\pi_n$ gives $f(T,X_T)-f(0,X_0)=\int_0^T(\partial_tf+b\partial_xf+\tfrac12\sigma^2\partial^2_{xx}f)(s,X_s)ds+\int_0^T\sigma_s\partial_xf(s,X_s)dB_s$ in probability, and since the left side is measurable for the given filtration the identity holds almost surely. For an arbitrary $t\in[0,T]$ apply the same argument on $[0,t]$ with the restricted, $t$-augmented partition sequence; both sides are continuous in $t$, so the identity holds for all $t$ up to indistinguishability. [F2, F3, step 1.5, step 1.6, step 3.1]
 
5.1 Reduction to $C^{1,2}$ by cutoff and mollification: let $f\in C^{1,2}$ and let $g$ be the reflected extension of [F8], which is $C^{1,2}$ on a neighbourhood of the cylinder $K=[-1,T+1]\times[-c-1,c+1]$, and let $g_\varepsilon=(\chi g)*\rho_\varepsilon$ be the smoothed cutoff. On the inner cylinder $[0,T]\times[-c,c]$ the functions $g_\varepsilon$ and their derivatives $\partial_tg_\varepsilon,\partial_xg_\varepsilon,\partial^2_{xx}g_\varepsilon$ converge uniformly to $g$ and its corresponding derivatives, and $g=f$ on $K$. Applying the $C^3$ result of step 4.1 to $g_\varepsilon$ (which is smooth) gives the identity for $g_\varepsilon$ at $T$; passing to the limit $\varepsilon\to0$: the drift term converges by dominated convergence with the dominating function $K'(1+|b_s|+\sigma_s^2)$, whose integral is bounded by $K'(T+2c)$ on the localized event; the stochastic term converges in $L^2(P)$ by [F2], since $E\bigl(\int_0^T\sigma(\partial_xg_\varepsilon-\partial_xg)(s,X_s)dB\bigr)^2=E\int_0^T\sigma_s^2(\partial_xg_\varepsilon-\partial_xg)(s,X_s)^2ds\to0$ by dominated convergence and $\int_0^T\sigma^2\le c$; and the left side converges to $g(T,X_T)=f(T,X_T)$ because the path of $X$ lies in the inner cylinder where $\chi=1$ and $g_\varepsilon\to g$. Hence the identity holds for $f$ at $T$, and then for every $t$ by the same continuity argument. [F2, F5, F7, F8, F9, step 4.1]
 
6.1 Removal of the localization and conclusion: the identity has now been proved on each event $\{\rho_c\ge T\}$ for every $c$ and every $T$; since $\rho_c\uparrow\infty$ almost surely, the identity holds almost surely at every deterministic time, and by continuity of both sides up to indistinguishability. The stochastic integrand $\sigma\partial_xf(\cdot,X)$ is predictable and locally square-integrable by [F1] and [F2], the Lebesgue integrand is continuous and pathwise integrable, and the displayed statement follows. [F1, F2, F7, step 5.1]
 
7.1 Boundary and consistency cases: for $t=0$ both sides equal $f(0,X_0)$; for $f$ independent of $x$ the formula reduces to $f(t)=f(0)+\int_0^t\partial_tf(s)ds$, the fundamental theorem for the deterministic continuous function $\partial_tf$; for $f(t,x)=x$ it reduces to the definition of $X$; for $f(t,x)=x^2$ and $b=0,\sigma=1$ it gives $B_t^2=2\int_0^tB_sdB_s+t$; if $\sigma\equiv0$ then $X$ is pathwise absolutely continuous and the formula is the chain rule with the second-order term absent, consistent with the vanishing covariation of finite-variation parts; if $X\equiv X_0$ is deterministic the formula is the fundamental theorem along the deterministic time variable; and if the diffusion coefficient is unbounded the localization of step 1.1 is what makes every integral finite, with no additional hypothesis. AC enters only through [F10], and all localization and mollification parameters are canonical. [F2, F3, F10, step 6.1] ∎

## Source notes

Van der Vaart, Theorem 5.79, proves the formula for a continuous local martingale and an adapted finite-variation process by Taylor expansion along partitions after localization; Lawler, Section 3.3, computes the same formula for Brownian motion and smooth test functions. The $C^3$-first route of steps 1.2 through 4.1 is written out in full because the sources present the argument only for their own bounded or stopped settings; the passage to $C^{1,2}$ by reflection, cutoff and mollification in step 5.1 is the standard smoothing argument, included here so that the stated $C^{1,2}$ hypothesis is proved rather than asserted.
