## Statement

Let $R$, $v_s$, $H$, $T_s$ be as in [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]], let $(S,m)$, $W$, $\ell$ be as in [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], and let $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ be the standard basis of $H$ ([[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]]).

1. **Reversal anti-involution.** There is a unique $R$-algebra anti-automorphism $\#:H\to H$ with $T_s^{\#}=T_s$ for all $s\in S$; it is an involution and satisfies $T_w^{\#}=T_{w^{-1}}$ for all $w\in W$.
2. **Invertibility.** Each generator is a unit: $T_s^{-1}=T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1})$, and each $T_w=T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ is a unit with $T_w^{-1}=T_{s_k}^{-1}\cdots T_{s_1}^{-1}$.
3. **Bar operator.** The assignment $v_i\mapsto v_i^{-1}$ defines an involutive ring automorphism $\bar{\;}:R\to R$ ([[lem-hh-finite-polynomial-and-localization-constructions]], part 2); there is a unique ring homomorphism $\bar{\;}:H\to H$ which is semilinear over it (i.e. $\overline{rh}=\bar r\,\bar h$) and satisfies $\overline{T_s}=T_s^{-1}$ for all $s\in S$. It is involutive, and $\overline{T_w}=T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}$ for all $w\in W$.
4. **Multiplicative normalization.** Put $Q_s:=v_s^2\in R$ and $S_s:=v_sT_s\in H$. Then $S_s$ is a unit and
$$(S_s-Q_s)(S_s+1)=0,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad S_s^2=(Q_s-1)S_s+Q_s,$$
and $T_s=v_s^{-1}S_s$. If $v_s=v$ for all $s\in S$ (one odd component) and $Q:=v^2$, both relations read $(S_s-Q)(S_s+1)=0$ for every $s$. This is the multiplicative (or $S$-) normalization used by the type-$A$, affine and cyclotomic applications, and it must be matched through the conversion $T_s=v_s^{-1}S_s$ before those pages are compared.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the group $W$ with length $\ell$, the coefficient ring $R$ with parameters $v_s$, and the algebra $H$ with standard basis $\{T_w\}$.

[F1] $F=R\langle T_s:s\in S\rangle$ is free as an $R$-module on the finite words in the generators, with product concatenation of words; $H=F/I$ where $I$ is the two-sided ideal generated by the relations (Q) and (B), and membership in $I$ is preserved by left and right multiplication. ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]])

[F2] $H$ is generated as a ring by the images $T_s$ together with the coefficients from $R$, the quadratic relation reads $T_s^2-(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s-1=0$, the braid relation identifies the two alternating products of $m(s,t)$ factors for $s\ne t$ with $m(s,t)<\infty$, and every $v_s$ is a unit of $R$. ([[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]])

[F3] For each $w\in W$ with reduced expression $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ one has the well-defined element $T_w=T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$, and $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ is an $R$-basis of $H$. ([[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]])

[F4] $\ell(w)$ is the least length of a word in $S$ representing $w$, and a word of that length is a reduced expression. ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]])

[F5] The Laurent ring $R=\Lambda_{\mathbb Z,c}$ of [[lem-hh-finite-polynomial-and-localization-constructions]], part 2 has the universal property that a choice of units $u_1,\dots,u_c$ in a commutative ring $A$ extends uniquely to a ring homomorphism $R\to A$ with $v_i\mapsto u_i$; applied with $A=R$ and $u_i=v_i$, this says the identity is the only ring endomorphism of $R$ fixing every $v_i$. ([[lem-hh-finite-polynomial-and-localization-constructions]])

## Proof

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the group $W$ with length $\ell$, the parameters $R$, $v_s$, and the algebra $H$ with standard basis $\{T_w\}$.

1.1 Define $\#_F:F\to F$ by $R$-linear extension of word reversal, $(T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k})^{\#}=T_{s_k}\cdots T_{s_1}$ and the empty word fixed. Reversal is an involutive anti-automorphism of the word monoid, so $\#_F$ is an $R$-linear involutive anti-automorphism of $F$ ([F1]). The ideal $I$ is preserved: the quadratic generator $T_s^2-(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s-1$ is a polynomial in $T_s$ with central coefficients and is fixed by $\#_F$, and for $s\ne t$ with $m:=m(s,t)<\infty$ the alternating products $a_1=T_sT_tT_s\cdots$ and $a_2=T_tT_sT_t\cdots$ of $m$ factors are each reversed into one of the two: the alternating word $s\,t\,s\cdots$ of length $m$ is a palindrome exactly when $m$ is odd, so $a_1^{\#}=a_1$, $a_2^{\#}=a_2$ for odd $m$ and $a_1^{\#}=a_2$, $a_2^{\#}=a_1$ for even $m$, and in both cases $(a_1-a_2)^{\#}=\pm(a_1-a_2)$; since the two-sided ideal generated by these elements consists of finite sums $\sum_ic_ix_ig_iy_i$ with $g_i$ among the generators ([F1]) and $\#_F$ reverses products, $\#_F$ maps each such sum to a sum of the same shape (a generator $g_i$ being replaced by $\pm$ itself), so $\#_F(I)\subseteq I$. Therefore $\#(x+I):=\#_F(x)+I$ is a well-defined $R$-linear anti-automorphism of $H$ with $\#^2=\mathrm{id}$: if $x-y\in I$ then $\#_Fx-\#_Fy=\#_F(x-y)\in I$. It is determined by $T_s^{\#}=T_s$ because $H$ is generated as a ring by the $T_s$ and the coefficients ([F2]), and for a reduced expression $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ one has $\#(T_w)=T_{s_k}\cdots T_{s_1}=T_{s_k\cdots s_1}=T_{w^{-1}}$, since reversing a word of length $k$ representing $w$ represents $w^{-1}$, whence $\ell(w^{-1})=\ell(w)=k$ by [F4] and the reversed word is a reduced expression of $w^{-1}$ ([F3]). [F1, F2, F3, F4]

1.2 By the quadratic relation of [F2], $T_s^2-(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s-1=0$ in $H$, so $T_s(T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1}))=1=(T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1}))T_s$ and $T_s^{-1}=T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1})$. Inverses multiply in reverse order, so for $T_w=T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ one has $T_w^{-1}=T_{s_k}^{-1}\cdots T_{s_1}^{-1}$, and $T_w$ is a unit. [F2, algebra]

1.3 The assignment $v_i\mapsto v_i^{-1}$ gives units of $R$, so by the universal property of the Laurent ring it extends to a unique ring endomorphism $\sigma:R\to R$ with $\sigma(v_i)=v_i^{-1}$ ([F5]); note that $\sigma$ is not $R$-linear (it does not fix the coefficients), only a ring endomorphism, which is all that is used below. Since $\sigma^2$ is again a ring endomorphism fixing every $v_i$, uniqueness of the extension identifies $\sigma^2=\mathrm{id}$, so $\sigma$ is an involution. [F5]

2.1 Extend $\sigma$ to $\bar{\;}$ on the free algebra: define $\bar{\;}:F\to F$ on words by $\overline{T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}}:=\bar T_{s_1}\cdots\bar T_{s_k}$ with $\bar T_s:=T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1})$, and on $\sum_wr_ww$ by $\sum_w\sigma(r_w)\overline w$; this is the unique $\sigma$-semilinear ring endomorphism of $F$ with those generator images, because $F$ is free on the words ([F1]). It maps $I$ into itself: the quadratic generator satisfies $\overline{(T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})}=(\bar T_s-v_s^{-1})(\bar T_s+v_s)=(T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})\in I$, using $\sigma(v_s)=v_s^{-1}$ and $\sigma(v_s^{-1})=v_s$; and for the braid generator, $\bar a_1=T_s^{-1}T_t^{-1}\cdots$ equals, by step 1.2, the inverse of the product along the reversed alternating word, and the reversed alternating word has the same length $m$ and alternates between $s$ and $t$, so its product is the same element $A:=a_1=a_2$ of $H$ by relation (B) ([F2]); hence $\bar a_1=A^{-1}=\bar a_2$ and $\overline{(a_1-a_2)}\in I$; the general element of $I$ is a finite sum as above and $\bar{\;}$ is additive, so $\bar{\;}(I)\subseteq I$. Hence $\bar{\;}(x+I):=\bar x+I$ is well defined on $H$, is a $\sigma$-semilinear ring endomorphism, and satisfies $\overline{T_s}=T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1})=T_s^{-1}$ by step 1.2. It is involutive: $\bar{\;}^2$ is $\sigma^2$-semilinear, hence $R$-linear, and ring-endomorphic, it fixes $R$ pointwise because $\sigma^2=\mathrm{id}$ (step 1.3), and it fixes each generator because $\bar{\;}^2(T_s)=\bar{\;}(T_s^{-1})=\bar{\;}(T_s)^{-1}=(T_s^{-1})^{-1}=T_s$; since $H$ is generated by these ([F2]), $\bar{\;}^2=\mathrm{id}$. Finally, multiplicativity of $\bar{\;}$ gives $\overline{T_w}=\overline{T_{s_1}}\cdots\overline{T_{s_k}}=T_{s_1}^{-1}\cdots T_{s_k}^{-1}=(T_{s_k}\cdots T_{s_1})^{-1}=T_{w^{-1}}^{-1}$ for a reduced expression, using step 1.2 and $T_{s_k\cdots s_1}=T_{w^{-1}}$ ([F3]). [F1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 1.3]

2.2 Put $S_s:=v_sT_s$ and $Q_s:=v_s^2$. Substituting $T_s=v_s^{-1}S_s$ in $(T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})=0$ ([F2]) gives $v_s^{-1}(S_s-Q_s)\cdot v_s^{-1}(S_s+1)=0$, and multiplying by the unit $v_s^2$ gives $(S_s-Q_s)(S_s+1)=0$, equivalently $S_s^2=(Q_s-1)S_s+Q_s$. Since $v_s$ is a unit of $R$ ([F2]) and $T_s$ is a unit with inverse $T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1})$ (step 1.2), also $S_s$ is a unit with $S_s^{-1}=v_s^{-1}(T_s-(v_s-v_s^{-1}))$, and $T_s=v_s^{-1}S_s$ by construction. If $v_s=v$ for every $s$ then $Q_s=Q=v^2$ for every $s$, so the displayed relation is uniform in $s$. No other identification between the parameters is made. [F2, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Assembly: the reversal anti-involution of part 1 is step 1.1, the invertibility statement of part 2 is step 1.2, the bar operator of part 3 is steps 1.3 and 2.1, and the normalization of part 4 is step 2.2. No choice is used: word reversal and the monomial substitution $v_i\mapsto v_i^{-1}$ are explicit, and the standard basis is used only to name the elements $T_w$, never to select them. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
