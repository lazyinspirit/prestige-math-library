## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field, $e,n\ge1$ integers with $n$ invertible in $k$, and put
$$B_e=k\llbracket u,v,s\rrbracket/(uv-s^e),\qquad R_e=B_e[s^{-1}].$$
Every element of $R_e^\times$ has a unique expression $u^a s^b h$ with $a,b\in\mathbb Z$ and $h\in B_e^\times$. Consequently
$$R_e^\times/(R_e^\times)^n\cong(\mathbb Z/n)^2,\qquad R_e^\times/\bigl((R_e^\times)^n\langle s\rangle\bigr)\cong\mathbb Z/n,$$
with $u$ giving the generator of the second quotient. If $e\mid e'$ and the transition sends $u\mapsto u$, $v\mapsto v$ and $s\mapsto (s')^{e'/e}$, then the induced map between the second quotients sends that generator to itself. The assertions also hold when the characteristic of $k$ divides $e$ or $e'$; no nearby-cycles or étale-cohomology comparison is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the positive integers $e,n$ with $n$ invertible in $k$, and the displayed rings.

## Proof

1.1 Map $k\llbracket u,v,s\rrbracket$ to $k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$ by $u\mapsto a^e$, $v\mapsto b^e$, $s\mapsto ab$. Its image consists exactly of the formal series whose monomials $a^i b^j$ satisfy $i-j\equiv0\pmod e$: if $i\ge j$ such a monomial is $(a^e)^{(i-j)/e}(ab)^j$, and the other case is analogous. Every series in the three variables has, modulo $uv=s^e$, a convergent normal form containing no monomial divisible by both $u$ and $v$. Distinct normal-form monomials map to distinct monomials in $a,b$, so the induced map from $B_e$ is injective and identifies it with that subring. This argument uses the congruence grading and does not require $e$ to be invertible. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $f\in R_e^\times$. Write $f=F/s^h$ and $f^{-1}=G/s^j$ with $F,G\in B_e$ and $h,j\ge0$, so $FG=s^{h+j}$. In $k\llbracket a,b\rrbracket$, the elements $a,b$ are prime and $s=ab$; repeated cancellation gives $F=a^i b^j H$ with $H$ a unit. Since $F$ belongs to the congruence-graded subring and $H$ has nonzero constant term, its lowest monomial shows $i-j$ is divisible by $e$. Thus $a^i b^j=u^m s^r$ for integers $m,r$, and dividing $F$ by this monomial inside the power-series ring shows $H$ has weight zero. Hence $H\in B_e^\times$, and $f=u^a s^b H$. The $a$- and $b$-orders in the power-series ring prove uniqueness. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Every unit of $B_e$ has an $n$th root in $B_e$: choose an $n$th root of its nonzero residue in $k$, then determine the successive coefficients in the complete local ring, solving at each order a linear equation with invertible coefficient $n c^{n-1}$. Step 2.1 therefore identifies $R_e^\times/(R_e^\times)^n$ with $(\mathbb Z/n)^2$ via the exponents of $u,s$. Factoring out the base parameter $s$ leaves $\mathbb Z/n$ generated by $u$. [given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For $e\mid e'$, the stated transition takes $u$ to $u$ and $s$ to a power of $s'$. It therefore sends the generator $[u]$ of the quotient modulo the base parameter to the generator $[u]$ at level $e'$. No step used invertibility of $e$ or $e'$. [step 3.1, algebra] ∎
