## Definition

Let $G$ be a finite group, and let $R(G)$ be its complex character ring
([[def-virtual-character-and-character-ring-of-a-finite-group]]).

The **rational representation ring** $R_{\mathbb Q}(G)$ is the subgroup of
$R(G)$ generated by the complex characters of finite-dimensional
$\mathbb Q$-representations of $G$
([[def-finite-dimensional-representation-of-a-group-over-a-field]]).
Equivalently, $R_{\mathbb Q}(G)$ is the Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional
$\mathbb QG$-modules, viewed inside the complex character ring by extension of
scalars from $\mathbb Q$ to $\mathbb C$.

A class function $\alpha:G\to\mathbb C$ is **rational-valued** when
$\alpha(g)\in\mathbb Q$ for every $g\in G$.

## Remarks

- On this page, an unqualified **rational character** means an element of
  $R_{\mathbb Q}(G)$, not merely a rational-valued class function.

- Every element of $R_{\mathbb Q}(G)$ is rational-valued, because the trace of a
  $\mathbb Q$-linear operator is rational and the character-ring operations are
  integral linear combinations of such traces.

- The converse can fail: the companion page records a rational-valued
  irreducible character of $Q_8$ that is not afforded by any
  $\mathbb Q$-representation.
