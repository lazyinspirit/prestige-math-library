## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and work over the field of complex numbers. Let A be a small uncurved cohomological A-infinity category with the stated unsuspended Stasheff convention. Use the negative suspended bar differential, unsuspended module coefficients, and explicit left, right and opposite formulas of the preceding algebraic definition. These formulas define dg categories of full module premorphisms and an involutive dg isomorphism from left A-modules to right opposite-A-modules, preserving all Taylor components, objectwise quasi-isomorphisms and homological units. The two representables are Hom(-,X) on the right and Hom(X,-) on the left.

If A and N are homologically unital and $e_X$ is any closed representative of the indicated unit, evaluation $f↦f_0(e_X)$ is a quasi-isomorphism from the full product module-Hom complex from the appropriate representable to N, to the value complex N(X). There are explicit signed all-arity Yoneda cochain maps and coefficientwise homotopies as given in the proof; injectivity on cochains is not asserted for merely homological units. No strict units, boundedness or finite-dimensionality are required. The augmented unnormalized direct-sum bar complex is objectwise contractible, retaining every higher unit-input operation. These module-Hom complexes compute derived Hom for objectwise quasi-isomorphisms. In particular representables and their finite cones are compact in the homologically unital derived module category.

For any already-defined parameter-first family of right opposite-A bar maps, homotopies and higher comparison maps, conjugation by the displayed reversal transports every counted Taylor coefficient to the left A convention. It preserves the parameter-first differential, composition and face equations and leaves empty-word counts unchanged. This is a transport of given algebraic count maps, not an assertion that a geometric count or its analytic compactification exists.


Finite shifts and twisted objects use exactly the graded-line and curvature convention of the Definition. The complete finite insertion formulas define square-zero twisted category and module differentials, transport full premorphisms and all parameter-face equations, and retain the usual DG cone specialization. For any supplied full counted family $F_P$ from a representable, evaluation at the same closed proper PSS unit and the explicit full-Hom contraction in the proof give an all-arity comparison to the actual signed Yoneda map. This comparison homotopy is algebraic; its being a geometric unit-flag count is not asserted.

The formal directed scalar diagonal has the complete all-arity module realization and proper-PSS interface constructed below: its identity acts as the literal module identity, and an explicit full-Hom homotopy compares this action with the actual PSS-unit Yoneda action. Formal and geometric representables, and their self-complexes, are not identified.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the category A, the stated left or right module N, and any supplied family of count maps. Modules in the Yoneda assertion are homologically unital. All sums on a single word are finite; module Hom uses products over arity and object strings.

[F1] [[def-stopped-fukaya-category-algebraic-conventions]] specifies $s$ of degree $-1$, $c_d=d(d-1)/2$, $b_d=-(-1)^{c_d}s\mu^d(s^{-1})^{\otimes d}$, the right prefix components $q_d^R$, left suffix components $q_d^L$, object labels, Hom differentials, the opposite and the degree-zero reversal $\Theta$.

[F2] [[def-koszul-sign-rule-for-graded-tensors]] fixes every tensor-map interchange; in particular a degree-$h$ suffix map crosses a prefix of degree $S$ with sign $(-1)^{hS}$.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]] permits splittings of arbitrary graded vector-space complexes. There is no finiteness, analytic or strict-unit hypothesis hidden in these choices.

## Proof

**Given:** [F1], [F2], [A1]. Write $V=s\operatorname{hom}_{A}$, $S=\sum_i|v_i|$ and $P=\sum_{i<j}|v_i||v_j|$ on each homogeneous composable word. Object labels in every formula are those of its input/output, so no sum over unspecified objects is taken.

1.1 At total arity n, expanding two suspended insertions gives $(b^2)_n=(-1)^{c_n}s\bigl[\sum_{r+k+t=n}(-1)^{r+kt}\mu^{r+1+t}(1^{\otimes r}\otimes\mu^k\otimes1^{\otimes t})\bigr](s^{-1})^{\otimes n}$, where $k\ge1$ and $r,t\ge0$. The tensor-input degree factors agree with those of the displayed composite; the remaining scalar equality is $c_k+c_{n-k+1}+(n-k)(k+1)\equiv c_n\pmod2$. The two common minus signs in the two b insertions cancel. Hence the exact unsuspended Stasheff identity implies $b^2=0$ in every arity. The right formula in [F1] follows by restricting the category bar differential to a word whose first suspended letter is a representable value: conjugate by suspension of that first coefficient and multiply the degree-one differential by $-1$. The two minus signs, this conjugation and the common minus in $b$, give $q_d^R(n;sa_1,\ldots,sa_d)=(-1)^{c_{d+1}+d(|n|-1)+\sum_j(d-j)(|a_j|-1)}\mu^{d+1}(n,a_1,\ldots,a_d)$. The suffix insertion has sign $(-1)^{|n|+\sum_{i\le r}|v_i|}$. For a last suspended coefficient the tensor map $1\otimes s$ itself has sign $(-1)^S$; the same negative conjugation gives $q_d^L=(-1)^{c_{d+1}+\sum_j(d-j)|v_j|}\mu^{d+1}(a_1,\ldots,a_d,n)$, with the left insertion formula of [F1]. Both squares are zero because the original restricted square is zero. Thus the representables have value differential $\mu^1$ and ordinary binary actions, rather than actions inferred from an unspecified bar convention. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 The graded reversal of suspended words with factor $(-1)^d$ is an involutive coalgebra anti-isomorphism: its reversal-pair Koszul sign is $(-1)^P$, and for any cut the pair signs crossing that cut are exactly the tensor symmetry between its two blocks. Conjugating a degree-one coderivation by it gives another coderivation and preserves its square. The arity-$d$ component is $(-1)^{d+1}b_d$ applied in reverse suspended order with its Koszul sign. Undoing suspension gives exponent $d+1+(d-1)\sum_i(|a_i|-1)+\sum_{i<j}(|a_i|-1)(|a_j|-1)$, which is $c_{d-1}+\sum_{i<j}|a_i||a_j|$ modulo two. This proves the opposite formula, including $\mu^1_{\rm op}=\mu^1$ and the usual graded opposite product for $d=2$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 **Finite shifts with fixed signs.** For a shift X[k] use a one-dimensional graded line $L_k$ of degree -k. Set $\operatorname{Hom}(X[k],Y[l])=\operatorname{Hom}(L_k,L_l) \otimes \operatorname{Hom}(X,Y)$, with its total grading. Identify s(f tensor a) with $(-1)^{|f|}f\otimes sa$; call this identification J. On homogeneous matrix words $f_i \otimes v_i$, put $p_i=|f_i|, w_i=|v_i|$ and $C=\sum _{i<j}w_i p_j$. Define the shifted suspended operation, before conjugating by J, as $(-1)^{C+\sum p_i}(f_1...f_d) \otimes b_d(v_1,...,v_d)$. Sum over matching finite matrix indices, and conjugate by J to obtain the shifted additive category. This is an explicit definition of all shift signs, rather than an instruction to choose them. Its square is zero in every arity: for an inner block I with prefix P and suffix S, its contraction changes the crossing exponent to $C-C_I+\sum _{j\in S}p_j$. Including the inner operation, the outer operation and the coderivation prefix sign yields total exponent $C+\sum _{i\in P}w_i$; the p-prefix and p-suffix terms each occur twice and cancel. Thus the entire two-insertion sum is the common matrix coefficient times the original b-square sum. This proves all Stasheff identities. At arity one the unsuspended differential is $(-1)^{|f|}f \otimes μ^1(a)$, as required by the cohomological shifts. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 Moving an unsuspended value $n$ through a word gives $(-1)^{|n|S}$. Combining this with the preceding reversal gives precisely $\Theta_N=(-1)^{d+P+|n|S}$, which is involutive after reversing object labels. Conjugate the left suffix differential, including its prefix tensor signs, by this degree-zero map. The new core is $q^{R,\rm op}_d(n;v_1,\ldots,v_d)=(-1)^{d+P+|n|S}q_d^L(v_d,\ldots,v_1;n)$; its remaining terms are the right suffix insertions of $b^{\rm op}$. Substituting the two $q$ formulas cancels their $c_{d+1}$ factors and gives the unsuspended module exponent $c_d+\sum_{i<j}|a_i||a_j|+|n|\sum_i|a_i|$ in [F1]. The identical core calculation gives the stated premorphism formula, with no extra $|f|$ factor; its suffix extension already has $(-1)^{|f|S}$. Conjugation therefore commutes with differential and composition. Its arity-zero component is identity, so it preserves objectwise quasi-isomorphisms, unit action and full Taylor products. The representable left module becomes exactly the right representable for the opposite category. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 **The curvature equation and the necessary public correction.** In the shifted additive category of step 2.2 let q be a degree-one matrix strictly decreasing a fixed finite filtration, and set $t=-sq$, of degree zero. Put $κ(q)=\sum _{d≥1}b_d(t,...,t)$. Koszul suspension on degree-one inputs has no input-degree factor, so $κ(q)=s\sum _{d≥1}(-1)^{c_{d-1}}μ^d(q,...,q)$, since $d+1+c_d≡c_{d-1}$ modulo two. Consequently the curvature-zero equation in THIS convention is $\sum _{d≥1}(-1)^{c_{d-1}}μ^d(q,...,q)=0$. It starts $μ^1(q)+μ^2(q,q)-μ^3(q,q,q)-μ^4(q,q,q,q)+$..., not the unsigned sum. Every sum is finite by the strict filtration. This is the necessary correction to the earlier unsigned equation, with no restriction on higher operations or matrix length. It agrees with dq+q²=0 for every DG category. [F1, F2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Here are the actual Yoneda maps. For a homogeneous $n\in N(X)$ of degree $h$ and $x$ a representable input, the right map is $I_R(n)_d(x;sa_1,\ldots,sa_d)=(-1)^{c_d+d(h+|x|)+\sum_j(d-j)|sa_j|}m_N^{d+1|1}(n;x,a_1,\ldots,a_d)$. The left map is $I_L(n)_d(sa_1,\ldots,sa_d;x)=(-1)^{c_d+\sum_j(d-j)|sa_j|+h(|x|+\sum_j|sa_j|)}\ell_N^{d+1|1}(a_1,\ldots,a_d,x;n)$. Both have degree $h$. On the right the same formula is $(-1)^h q^R_{d+1}(n;sx,sa_1,\ldots,sa_d)$. To prove its chain equation, put $J_n(x;v)=n\otimes sx\otimes v$, a degree-$h-1$ map of the underlying word complexes, not a claimed comodule map. Direct expansion gives $D_NJ_n-(-1)^{h-1}J_nD_{Y_X}=J_{dn}+F_n$, where $(F_n)_d=q^R_{d+1}(n;sx,v)$. The category terms agree because suspension of the representable coefficient conjugates its differential to $-b$. Applying the Hom differential once more gives $\partial F_n=-F_{dn}$; multiplication by $(-1)^h$ proves $\partial I_R(n)=I_R(dn)$. Conjugating by step 3.1 gives exactly the displayed left exponent and its chain equation, not a separately guessed action. [F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

4.2 **All insertion signs and all arities.** For twisted objects indexed 0,...,d, insert any number $r_i$ of their $t_i$ into gap i of a suspended word, and denote the resulting finite map by $E_t$. Each gap has a bounded number of insertions since $t_i$ strictly decreases the relevant finite filtration. Deconcatenation splits each inserted string at every possible cut, so $E_t$ is a coalgebra isomorphism with inverse $E_{-t}$: for a string with r identical insertions the composite coefficient is $\sum _{j=0}^r \operatorname{binom}(r,j)(-1)^j$, equal to zero for $r>0$. Conjugate b by $E_t$, namely $b^t=E_{-t}bE_t$. Its curvature is κ, its square is zero, and its positive Taylor components are $b_d^t(v_1,...,v_d)=\sum _{r_0,...,r_d}b_{d+R}(t_0^{r_0},v_1,t_1^{r_1},...,v_d,t_d^{r_d}), R=\sum r_i$. Indeed projection to one output letter after $E_{-t}$ retains only the one-letter component of $bE_t$; inserted output letters only increase length. Thus κ=0 gives an uncurved category in all arities. For unsuspended homogeneous inputs $a_i$ of degrees $α_i$, undoing the SAME suspension gives $μ_{tw}^d(a_1,...,a_d)=\sum _r(-1)^ε μ^{d+R}(q_0^{r_0},a_1,q_1^{r_1},...,a_d,q_d^{r_d})$, where $ε=R+c_{d+R}+c_d+\sum _{i=1}^d(r_i+...+r_d)(α_i-1)$ modulo two. This follows by comparing each original suspended $a_i$'s tensor exponent before and after the insertion; t contributes $(-1)^R$. In particular the morphism differential is the full $d=1$ formula. In a DG category it is $D(a)=da+q_{out} a-(-1)^{|a|}a q_{in}$, so its two-block cone is the usual cohomological cone. This verifies every insertion sign rather than testing finitely many arities. [F1, F2, step 3.2, algebra, construct]

5.1 Evaluation $E(f)=f_0(e_X)$ is a chain map because $e_X$ is closed. The displayed inclusions have $I_R(n)_0(x)=m_N^{1|1}(n;x)$ and $I_L(n)_0(x)=(-1)^{h|x|}\ell_N^{1|1}(x;n)$. Hence $EI$ is the actual binary unit action, identity on cohomology for homologically unital N. In particular the left degree-$h$ DG specialization is $f(ax)=(-1)^{h|a|}af(x)$, while the right specialization is $f(xa)=f(x)a$. Taking the free graded algebra on odd generators $x,y$, $n=y$, gives $I_L(y)_0(x)=-xy$; this checks the essential sign which degree-zero examples miss. [F1, F2, step 4.1, algebra]

5.2 **An explicit reason the unsigned equation cannot be retained.** Take four objects 0,1,2,3, degree-one arrows a:1→0, b:2→1, c:3→2 and r:3→0, and a degree-two arrow h:3→0. Let $μ^1(r)=h$ and $μ^3(a,b,c)=-h$; all other nonidentity operations vanish. Adjoin strict identities with the binary rules of [F1]. The Stasheff equations hold: $μ^1$ squares to zero, the only nonzero ternary value is closed, any insertion of a nonzero unary value into that ternary operation is impossible by its source/target labels, and two ternary operations cannot be nested in the length-three directed graph. The identities with one formal unit cancel adjacent binary/unit insertions with their Stasheff signs; with two or more units they reduce to the ordinary associative unit identity or zero. For the finite upper-triangular matrix $q=a+b+c+r$ the unsigned sum is $h-h=0$, whereas the corrected sum is $h+h=2h$. The insertion formula of step 4.2 on the diagonal projector $p_0$ gives $Dp_0=-a-r, Da=-μ^3(a,b,c)=h$ and $Dr=h$, so $D²p_0=-2h$. Over the stipulated field this is nonzero. Thus the earlier unsigned equation together with the fixed b convention can define a square-nonzero morphism differential. The correction is compulsory, rather than a silent weakening or an optional sign choice. [F1, F2, step 3.2, step 4.2, algebra, construct]

6.1 We prove that E is a quasi-isomorphism without assuming strictly unital higher operations or invoking a minimal-model comparison theorem. Split every value and category Hom complex into its cohomology representatives and contractible pairs, choosing $e_X$ itself as representative of its nonzero unit class. If its class is zero, all Hom spaces to and from X and N(X) have zero cohomology by the unit assumption; the same argument uses their zero cohomology and needs no representative for a zero vector. Write each contraction $pi=1$, $1-ip=dh+hd$, $h^2=hi=ph=0$. Tensor contractions on each finite input word use $h_{U\otimes W}=h_U\otimes1+i_Up_U\otimes h_W$ with the tensor signs of [F2]. For the internal Hom complex from the finite input tensor U to a value N, set $p_H(f)=p_Nfi_U$, $i_H(g)=i_Ngp_U$, and $h_H(f)=h_Nf+(-1)^{|f|}i_Np_Nfh_U$. Expansion of its Hom differential gives $1-i_Hp_H=\partial_0h_H+h_H\partial_0$, with the side conditions. These operations act componentwise on the full Taylor product and preserve arity; unbounded or infinite-dimensional vector spaces cause no convergence problem. [F1, F2, A1, step 5.1, choose, algebra]

7.1 Write the full Hom differential as $\partial_0+\delta$, where $\partial_0$ is unary/internal and every term of $\delta$ raises Taylor arity by at least one. The perturbation formulas $T=\delta(1+h_H\delta)^{-1}$, $i'=i_H-h_HTi_H$, $p'=p_H-p_HTh_H$, $h'=h_H-h_HTh_H$, and $d_C=p_HTi_H$ give a contraction onto the cohomology-input product with differential $d_C$. Every inverse is its geometric series and is finite at each prescribed output arity. Substitution of $\partial_0^2=0$, $\partial_0\delta+\delta\partial_0=-\delta^2$ and the preceding contraction cancels adjacent factors to give $(\partial_0+\delta)i'=i'd_C$, $p'(\partial_0+\delta)=d_Cp'$, $p'i'=1$ and $1-i'p'=(\partial_0+\delta)h'+h'(\partial_0+\delta)$. There is no infinite tail at a fixed arity. Its arity-one differential $d_1$ is precisely the binary graded-category/module differential on cohomology; $d_C-d_1$ raises arity by at least two, including all transferred higher unit-input terms. [F1, F2, A1, step 6.1, algebra, construct]

8.1 On this cohomology-input product evaluation $E_0$ at $[e_X]$ has $E_0p'=p_NE$: terms of $p'$ beyond $p_H$ raise arity and cannot contribute to arity zero. Likewise $p_NEi'=E_0$. The binary actions are strictly unital on cohomology, even though higher operations containing the unit have not vanished. The binary Yoneda inclusion $I_0$ has only arity-zero term, given on the right by $n x$ and on the left by $(-1)^{|n||x|}x n$. It satisfies $E_0I_0=1$. If $[e_X]=0$, the cohomology-input source representable and the target are both zero, so evaluation is already a quasi-isomorphism by step 7.1. We henceforth treat the nonzero class. [F1, F2, step 7.1, algebra]

8.2 The direct-sum bar assertion has the opposite convergence direction. At K the augmented left word complex is $C_N^L(K)[-1]$: a positive word is written $(x;v_1,\ldots,v_d;n)$ with outer $x$ unsuspended, and identified with $(sx,v_1,\ldots,v_d;n)[-1]$; its degree-zero augmentation is $-q^L_{d+1}(sx,v_1,\ldots,v_d;n)$. Include the empty-word $N(K)[-1]$ summand. Its degree-one differential is exactly $-D_N^L$ under this identification; hence it squares to zero, includes the full higher augmentation and has the correct negative target differential on the shifted summand. This specifies every sign, including the binary augmentation $x n$. Transfer the unary part by the tensor contractions of step 6.1 on each finite word, including the empty word. Now nonunary terms lower word length, so the first perturbation series is finite on each direct-sum word. On cohomology its leading binary differential is $d_0$ and its higher terms lower length by at least two. [F1, F2, A1, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra, construct]

9.1 For the binary-only Hom differential, the right homotopy on a degree-$h$ premorphism has components $(k_0^Rf)_d(x;v_1,\ldots,v_d)=(-1)^h f_{d+1}(e_X;sx,v_1,\ldots,v_d)$. It lowers Taylor arity by one. Its left conjugate is $(k_0^Lf)_d(v_1,\ldots,v_d;x)=(-1)^{h+1+\sum_i|v_i|}f_{d+1}(v_1,\ldots,v_d,sx;e_X)$. To check the right contraction in all degrees, the arity-d component of $d_1f$ is the last-action term with exponent $h+|x|+\sum_{i<d}|v_i|$, the first-action term with exponent $h+|x|+1$, and each internal product term at i with exponent $h+|x|+\sum_{j\le i}|v_j|+1$. In $d_1k_0+k_0d_1$ these pair with opposite signs; the unpaired first face is $f_d(x;v)$ and the exceptional arity-zero face is $-f_0(e_X)x$. Thus $d_1k_0+k_0d_1=1-I_0E_0$. The left formula follows by inserting the exact reversal signs from step 3.1, giving the extra $1+\sum_i|v_i|$ rather than omitting it. [F1, F2, step 8.1, algebra, construct]

9.2 The binary extra degeneracy is $h_0(v;n)=(se_K,v;n)$ in the shifted augmented coordinates; for the empty word it inserts just $se_K$. Its degree is minus one. Since $q_1^L(se_K;n)=-e_Kn$ and $b_2(se_K,sa)=-s(e_Ka)$, the outer face of $D_N^Lh_0+h_0D_N^L$ is minus identity, while all internal faces cancel across the odd $se_K$. Therefore $d_0h_0+h_0d_0=1$ for the augmented differential $-D_N^L$. Replace $h_0$ by $h=h_0d_0h_0$ to have $h^2=0$ while still increasing length by one. If $\Delta$ is the remaining transferred differential, it lowers length by at least two; thus $h_{\rm full}=h(1+\Delta h)^{-1}$ is finite on each direct-sum word and contracts the full transferred augmented complex. Composing with its unary contraction contracts the original augmented complex. Reversal transports this result to the right convention. Empty object sets are vacuous; if the unit class at K is zero, the unary cohomology of every word ending at K and of N(K) is zero and the first contraction already suffices. [F1, F2, A1, step 8.2, algebra]

10.1 Normalize this contraction: with $P=I_0E_0$, put $k_1=(1-P)k_0(1-P)$ and $k=k_1d_1k_1$. On the complementary complex, $d_1k_1+k_1d_1=1$, whence $d_1k_1^2=k_1^2d_1$ and $k^2=0$; also $E_0k=kI_0=0$. It still lowers arity by one. Put $\eta=d_C-d_1$. Then $\eta k$ and $k\eta$ raise arity by at least one, so $K=k(1+\eta k)^{-1}$ and $I_*=(1+k\eta)^{-1}I_0$ are coefficientwise finite. Since $E_0\eta=0$, the projection stays $E_0$ and the transferred target differential is zero. The same perturbation identities give $E_0I_*=1$ and $d_CK+Kd_C=1-I_*E_0$. This retains every higher unit-input operation; it uses no normalized-unit quotient. [F1, F2, step 9.1, algebra]

11.1 Combine the two contractions: $i_C=i'I_*$, $p_C=E_0p'=p_NE$ and $H_C=h'+i'Kp'$ give $p_Ci_C=1$ and $1-i_Cp_C=\partial H_C+H_C\partial$. Thus E induces a cohomology isomorphism. Explicitly a quasi-inverse as a map of complexes is $J=i_Cp_N:N(X)\to\operatorname{hom}_{\rm mod}(Y_X,N)$; $JE-1=-\partial H_C-H_C\partial$. The other composite has homotopy $h_NEi_Cp_N-h_N$, since $p_NEi_C=1$ and $1-i_Np_N=d_Nh_N+h_Nd_N$. To compare the actual all-arity inclusion I of step 4.1 with J, let $u=EI-1$. It is a chain endomorphism inducing zero on cohomology; the internal-Hom contraction gives $H_u=h_Nu+i_Np_Nu h_N$ and $d_NH_u+H_ud_N=u$. Consequently $I-J=\partial(H_CI+JH_u)+(H_CI+JH_u)d_N$. These are explicit product homotopies for the actual graded Yoneda inclusion, not a replacement of it by a unary action or by strict geometric units. [F1, F2, A1, step 4.1, step 7.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 For an objectwise acyclic target module Q, choose contractions of its value complexes. On each arity of Hom(M,Q) the unary contraction is just postcomposition with that contraction; the perturbation raises arity, so the same coefficientwise formulas contract the full Hom product. Thus Hom(M,-) sends objectwise acyclic cones to acyclic complexes. An objectwise quasi-isomorphism f therefore induces a quasi-isomorphism on Hom(M,-) for every M. Surjectivity on $H^0\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{target}f,f)$ supplies a homotopy right inverse; injectivity on $H^0\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{source}f,f)$ then supplies the left inverse identity. Hence these quasi-isomorphisms are homotopy equivalences and the Hom model computes derived Hom. The proved Yoneda formula identifies derived Hom from a representable with evaluation; evaluation commutes with arbitrary coproducts. Sums of homologically unital modules remain such on value cohomology, and finite cones retain the unit equation. Representables and finite cones are consequently compact in the stated derived module category. [F1, F2, A1, step 11.1, algebra]

12.2 Finally fix a supplied parameter cell P of dimension r, a right opposite-A map $c_P$ of degree $-r$, and its supplied homotopies/comparison maps. Define $c_P^L=\Theta_N^{-1}c_P^{R,\rm op}\Theta_M$, and identically define $R_P^L,H_P^L,K_{P,Q}^L$ and every higher family. In components, $(c_P^L)_d(v_1,\ldots,v_d;m)=(-1)^{d+\sum_{i<j}|v_i||v_j|+|m|\sum_i|v_i|}(c_P^{R,\rm op})_d(m;v_d,\ldots,v_1)$. This is a reindexing of the SAME tensor/count coefficient, not a new moduli count or a reflection of its domain. Apply the same reversal to its category arrows, opposite operations and module structure simultaneously.
