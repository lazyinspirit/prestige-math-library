## Statement

The highest weight of a tensor product $V\otimes W$ of finite-dimensional
irreducible modules over a complex semisimple Lie algebra determines the
complete irreducible decomposition of $V\otimes W$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb C)$ with basis $e,f,h$ and its Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h=\mathbb Ch$ ([[def-special-linear-lie-algebra-sl-two]]), the standard two-dimensional module $V=\mathbb C^2$ with basis $u_1,u_2$ on which $h\cdot u_1=u_1$, $h\cdot u_2=-u_2$, $e\cdot u_1=0$, $e\cdot u_2=u_1$, $f\cdot u_1=u_2$, $f\cdot u_2=0$, and the tensor product $V\otimes V$ with the usual action $x\cdot(u\otimes w)=x\cdot u\otimes w+u\otimes x\cdot w$ ([[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[L1] A finite-dimensional $\mathfrak{sl}_2$-module is a direct sum of irreducible submodules, and an irreducible submodule with highest weight $m\ge0$ (that is, with $h$ acting with top eigenvalue $m$) has dimension $m+1$ with $h$-eigenvalues $m,m-2,\dots,-m$ ([[thm-finite-dimensional-representations-of-sl-two]]).

[L2] The module $V$ is irreducible of top weight $1$: its only weight vectors are multiples of $u_1$ and $u_2$, the submodule generated by $u_2$ contains $u_1=e\cdot u_2$ and hence is $V$, and the submodule generated by $u_1$ contains $u_2=f\cdot u_1$ and hence is $V$; the vector $u_1$ is killed by $e$ and has $h$-eigenvalue $1$, so it is a nonzero vector killed by the positive root vector $e$ and its weight $1$ is the maximum of the weights of $V$ ([[def-irreducible-completely-reducible-and-faithful-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[L3] The vector $u_1\otimes u_1\ne0$ is killed by $e$ and has $h$-eigenvalue $2$, since $e\cdot u_1=0$ and $h\cdot u_1=u_1$; hence it is a nonzero weight vector killed by $e$ with top weight $2$, and the submodule it generates has dimension $3$ by [L1]. [L3]

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The tensor product $V\otimes V$ has top weight $2$ by the Given line and [L3], and is four-dimensional. [L1, L3]

2.1 By [L1] the module $V\otimes V$ is a direct sum of irreducible summands; by [L3] the submodule generated by $u_1\otimes u_1$ is irreducible of top weight $2$ and dimension $3$, so the remaining part has dimension $4-3=1$ and is a trivial irreducible summand of top weight $0$; hence $V\otimes V\cong L(2)\oplus L(0)$ has two irreducible summands. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 The irreducible module $L(2)$ of top weight $2$ exists by [L1] (it is the module generated by $u_1\otimes u_1$ above) and is an irreducible module with the same top weight $2$ whose decomposition has a single summand. [L1, step 2.1]

4.1 Two modules with the same top weight $2$, namely $V\otimes V$ and $L(2)$, have different irreducible decompositions (two summands versus one), so the top weight of a tensor product does not determine its complete decomposition; the failed conclusion is the uniqueness of the constituent list. [step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
