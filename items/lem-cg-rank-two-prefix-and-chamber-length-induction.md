## Statement

Let $S$ be a finite set, $m$ a Coxeter matrix on $S$, $W$ the group presented by $(S,m)$ with length function $\ell$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]), $V=\mathbb R^S$, $B$ the Coxeter form, $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$ the canonical reflection homomorphism with root system $\Phi$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]), and let $V^*$ be the algebraic dual with its dual action, its chamber $C$, its interior $C^\circ$ and its root hyperplanes $H_\alpha$ ([[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]]). For $s\in S$ put
$$B_s:=\{f\in V^*:f(e_s)>0\};$$
then $C^\circ=\bigcap_{s\in S}B_s$ and the translate $sB_s=\{f\in V^*:f(e_s)<0\}$ is the opposite open half-space. For distinct $s,t$ let $W_{s,t}:=\langle s,t\rangle\le W$ be the standard parabolic subgroup, with intrinsic length $\ell_{s,t}$, which agrees with $\ell$ on $W_{s,t}$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (2)). For $s\ne t$ and $k\ge0$ let $u_k$ be the alternating word $s\,t\,s\cdots$ of $k$ letters beginning with $s$, so $u_0=1$.

**(1) Rank-two half-space alternative.** Let $s\ne t$ and $v\in W_{s,t}$. For each $s'\in\{s,t\}$ exactly one of
$$v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq B_{s'},\qquad v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq s'B_{s'}$$
holds, and if the second holds then $\ell_{s,t}(s'v)=\ell_{s,t}(v)-1$. Moreover, if $m(s,t)<\infty$ then the elements $u_0,\dots,u_{2m(s,t)-1}$ exhaust $W_{s,t}$, with $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=\min(k,2m(s,t)-k)$, and the unique element of $W_{s,t}$ having both $s$ and $t$ as left descents is $u_{m(s,t)}$; if $m(s,t)=\infty$ then $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=k$ for every $k\ge0$, the elements $u_k$ are pairwise distinct, and no element of $W_{s,t}$ has both $s$ and $t$ as left descents.

**(2) The simultaneous induction.** For $n\ge0$ let
$$(\mathrm P_n):\ \text{for all }w\in W\text{ with }\ell(w)=n\text{ and all }s\in S,\ wC^\circ\subseteq B_s\text{, or }wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s\text{ and }\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1;$$
$$(\mathrm Q_n):\ \text{for all }w\in W\text{ with }\ell(w)=n\text{ and all }s\ne t\text{ there is }u\in W_{s,t}\text{ with }wC^\circ\subseteq u(B_s\cap B_t)\text{ and }\ell(w)=\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w).$$
Then $(\mathrm P_n)$ and $(\mathrm Q_n)$ hold for every $n\ge0$. Explicitly: $(\mathrm P_0)$ and $(\mathrm Q_0)$ are immediate; (1) together with $(\mathrm Q_n)$ yields $(\mathrm P_{n+1})$; and (1) together with $(\mathrm Q_n)$ and $(\mathrm P_m)$ for all $m\le n+1$ yields $(\mathrm Q_{n+1})$.

**(3) Canonical factorisation.** Let $w\in W$ and $s\ne t$. Write $w=u\,d$ with $u\in W_{s,t}$ and $d$ the minimal representative of the right coset $W_{s,t}w$. Then $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ and $\ell(s'd)>\ell(d)$ for $s'\in\{s,t\}$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]] (3)), and
$$wC^\circ\subseteq u(B_s\cap B_t),\qquad \ell(w)=\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w).$$

**(4) Chamber-descent equivalence.** For every $w\in W$ and $s\in S$ one has $wC^\circ\subseteq B_s$ or $wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s$; moreover
$$wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s\iff \ell(sw)<\ell(w).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a finite set $S$, a Coxeter matrix $m$, the presented group $W$ with length function $\ell$, the space $V=\mathbb R^S$ with its canonical basis $(e_s)$, the Coxeter form $B$, the canonical reflection homomorphism $\rho$ with root system $\Phi$, the dual action on $V^*$ with chamber $C$, interior $C^\circ$ and root hyperplanes $H_\alpha$, and for $s\ne t$ the standard parabolic subgroup $W_{s,t}=\langle s,t\rangle$ with intrinsic length $\ell_{s,t}$ and alternating words $u_k=s\,t\,s\cdots$ of $k$ letters beginning with $s$.

[F1] For $s\ne t$ put $c:=c(s,t)$, equal to $\cos(\pi/m(s,t))$ when $m(s,t)<\infty$ and to $1$ when $m(s,t)=\infty$; then $B(e_s,e_s)=1$, $B(e_s,e_t)=-c$, and with $P:=\mathbb Re_s+\mathbb Re_t$, $P^\perp=\{v\in V:B(v,e_s)=B(v,e_t)=0\}$ the reflections $r_s,r_t$ preserve $P$, fix $P^\perp$ pointwise, and satisfy $r_s(e_s)=-e_s$, $r_s(e_t)=e_t+2ce_s$ ([[def-cg-real-coxeter-form-and-reflection]], [[lem-cg-reflection-form-invariance-and-rank-two-orders]]).

[F2] The assignment $s\mapsto r_s$ induces the group homomorphism $\rho:W\to\mathrm{GL}(V)$, the root system is $\Phi=\{\rho(w)e_s:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$, every root satisfies $B(\alpha,\alpha)=1$, and $\rho(wsw^{-1})=r_{\rho(w)e_s}$ for all $w\in W$, $s\in S$ ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]], [[lem-cg-reflection-representation-descends-and-root-norms]]).

[F3] The formula $(w\cdot f)(v):=f(\rho(w)^{-1}v)$ defines a left action of $W$ on $V^*$ by linear maps, $C^\circ=\{f\in V^*:f(e_r)>0\text{ for all }r\in S\}$ is nonempty, and $H_\alpha=\{f\in V^*:f(\alpha)=0\}$. For $s\ne t$, in the coordinates $(y_s,y_t)$ of $P^*=\mathbb Rf_s+\mathbb Rf_t$ with dual basis $f_s(e_t)=\delta_{st}$, the dual generators act by $(y_s,y_t)\mapsto(-y_s,\ 2cy_s+y_t)$ for $s$ and $(y_s,y_t)\mapsto(y_s+2cy_t,\ -y_t)$ for $t$, each fixing the line $H_{e_s}\cap P^*$ respectively $H_{e_t}\cap P^*$ pointwise; when $m(s,t)=\infty$ the functional $f\mapsto f(e_s+e_t)$ is $W_{s,t}$-invariant ([[def-cg-dual-chambers-and-reflection-hyperplanes]], [[lem-cg-dual-action-and-chamber-faces-exist]]).

[F4] Let $C_P:=\{f\in P^*:f(e_s)\ge0,\ f(e_t)\ge0\}$ and $\Phi_P:=W_{s,t}\{e_s,e_t\}$. If $m:=m(s,t)<\infty$, then $W_{s,t}$ has order $2m$ and the $2m$ chambers $wC_P$ $(w\in W_{s,t})$ are exactly the $2m$ closed sectors cut out in $P^*$ by the $m$ root lines $H_\beta\cap P^*$ $(\beta\in\Phi_P)$; they have pairwise disjoint interiors, their union is $P^*$, $W_{s,t}$ acts simply transitively on them, and distinct chambers are separated by a root line. If $m=\infty$, the chambers $wC_P$ have pairwise disjoint interiors with union $\{f\in P^*:f(e_s+e_t)>0\}\cup\{0\}$ (so every chamber meets the boundary line $\{f:f(e_s+e_t)=0\}$ only in $0$), distinct chambers are separated by a root line, and the traces of the root lines on the affine line $\{f:f(e_s+e_t)=1\}$ are exactly the integers in the coordinate $y_s$ ([[lem-cg-dual-action-and-chamber-faces-exist]]).

[F5] There is a homomorphism $\operatorname{sgn}:W\to\{\pm1\}$ with $\operatorname{sgn}(s)=-1$ and $\operatorname{sgn}(w)=(-1)^{\ell(w)}$ for all $w\in W$; hence $\ell(ws)=\ell(w)\pm1$ for all $w\in W$ and $s\in S$. Also $\ell(s)=1$ for every $s\in S$, so $s\ne1$ in $W$. Distinct generators are distinct in $W$, the product $st$ has order exactly $m(s,t)$ in $W$, the standard parabolic subgroup $W_{s,t}$ is the Coxeter system presented by the restricted matrix on $\{s,t\}$ with intrinsic length $\ell_{s,t}$ equal to $\ell$ on $W_{s,t}$, and every right coset $W_{s,t}a$ has a unique minimal-length representative $d$, characterized by $\ell(s'd)>\ell(d)$ for $s'\in\{s,t\}$ and satisfying $\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ for all $u\in W_{s,t}$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], [[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]], [[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]], [[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]).

[F6] For $s\ne t$ and $q\ge1$, if $m(s,t)<\infty$ and $q\le m(s,t)$, or if $m(s,t)=\infty$, then the alternating word of length $q$ beginning with $s$ has length $q$ in $W_{s,t}$ ([[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]]); the same holds for the alternating word beginning with $t$.

[F7] Induction principle on the natural numbers: if a property holds for $0$ and its validity at all $m\le n$ implies it at $n+1$, then it holds for every $n\ge0$ ([[thm-induction-principle]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Set-up.** For the rank-two calculation fix $s\ne t$ in $S$ and adopt the notation of [F1] and [F3]. For $r\in S$ the sets $B_r=\{f\in V^*:f(e_r)>0\}$ and $rB_r=\{f\in V^*:f(e_r)<0\}$ are disjoint and $C^\circ=\bigcap_{r\in S}B_r$; the dual action satisfies $v(B_s\cap B_t)=\{v\cdot f:f\in B_s\cap B_t\}$, and the action of $v\in W_{s,t}$ on $V^*$ is linear. By [F5] the intrinsic length satisfies $\ell_{s,t}(x)=\ell(x)$ for $x\in W_{s,t}$. [F1, F3, F5, given]

1.2 **Reduction to the rank-two plane.** Put $C_P^\circ:=\{g\in P^*:g(e_s)>0,\ g(e_t)>0\}$ and let $\pi:V^*\to P^*$, $\pi(f):=f|_P$. Then $\pi$ is linear and surjective, because a functional prescribed on $P$ extends to a functional on $V$ by $0$ on the remaining basis vectors. Every $v\in W_{s,t}$ preserves $P$ and fixes $P^\perp$ pointwise by [F1], so for $x\in P$ and $f\in V^*$ one has $(v\cdot f)(x)=f(\rho(v)^{-1}x)$ with $\rho(v)^{-1}x\in P$; hence $\pi(v\cdot f)=v\cdot\pi(f)$ and $\pi^{-1}(C_P^\circ)=B_s\cap B_t$, $\pi^{-1}\{g:g(e_{s'})>0\}=B_{s'}$, $\pi^{-1}\{g:g(e_{s'})<0\}=s'B_{s'}$ for $s'\in\{s,t\}$. Consequently $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq B_{s'}$ if and only if $vC_P^\circ\subseteq\{g:g(e_{s'})>0\}$, and $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq s'B_{s'}$ if and only if $vC_P^\circ\subseteq\{g:g(e_{s'})<0\}$. [F1, F3, algebra]

1.3 **Sectors avoid the walls.** By [F4] the interiors of the chambers $wC_P$ $(w\in W_{s,t})$ are convex open cones that are connected components of the complement of the union of the root lines in $P^*$ (respectively in $\{f\in P^*:f(e_s+e_t)>0\}$ when $m(s,t)=\infty$), so each is disjoint from every root line $H_\beta\cap P^*$ $(\beta\in\Phi_P)$, and $H_{e_s}\cap P^*$, $H_{e_t}\cap P^*$ are root lines because $e_s,e_t\in\Phi_P$. An open convex set disjoint from a line lies in exactly one of the two open half-planes bounded by it. Hence for every $v\in W_{s,t}$ and $s'\in\{s,t\}$ exactly one of $vC_P^\circ\subseteq\{g:g(e_{s'})>0\}$ and $vC_P^\circ\subseteq\{g:g(e_{s'})<0\}$ holds. [F4, F2, algebra]

1.4 **The finite dihedral group.** Assume $m:=m(s,t)<\infty$ and write $u:=st$. Since $u^m=1$ by [F5], the set $\{u_0,\dots,u_{2m-1}\}$ is closed under right multiplication by $s$ and $t$: indeed $u_{2j}s=u_{2j+1}$ and $u_{2j+1}s=u_{2j}$ for $2j<2m$; $u_{2j}t=u_{2j-1}$ for $j\ge1$; $u_0t=u_{2m-1}$ because $(st)^{m-1}s=(st)^{-1}s=(ts)s=t$; and $u_{2j+1}t=u_{2j+2}$ for $2j+1<2m$, $u_{2m-1}t=1$. Since $W_{s,t}$ is generated by $s,t$ and contains $1$, it equals $\{u_0,\dots,u_{2m-1}\}$. The $u_k$ are pairwise distinct: if $u_j=u_k$ with $j<k<2m$, then $1=u_j^{-1}u_k$; for $j,k$ of equal parity this equals $u^{\pm(k-j)/2}$ with $0<(k-j)/2<m$, contradicting the exact order $m$ of $u$ from [F5], while for parities differing it equals $s\,u^{p}$ or $u^{p}s$ with $2|p|<2m$, so it would give $u^{p}=s$, impossible because $\operatorname{sgn}(u^{p})=1$ while $\operatorname{sgn}(s)=-1$. Hence $|W_{s,t}|=2m$, the $u_k$ are distinct, $u_{k+2m}=u_k$, and the pairs $u_k,u_{k+1}$ and $u_{2m-1},u_0$ are edges of the Cayley graph of $W_{s,t}$ for the generating set $\{s,t\}$. That graph is connected, and every vertex has degree exactly $2$ because $x\ne xs$ and $xs\ne xt$ for all $x$ (as $s\ne1$ and $s\ne t$ by [F5]); it therefore *is* the displayed $2m$-cycle, so $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=\min(k,2m-k)$ is the graph distance from $u_0$. Moreover $su_k=u_{\sigma(k)}$ and $tu_k=u_{\tau(k)}$ with $\sigma(k)\equiv1-k\pmod{2m}$ and $\tau(k)\equiv-1-k\pmod{2m}$ for $0\le k<2m$: these are $s(st)^{j}=u^{-j}s$, $t(st)^{j}=u^{-j}t=u^{-j-1}s$ applied to $u_{2j}=u^{j}$ and $u_{2j+1}=u^{j}s$, together with the case $k=0$. [F5, F6, algebra]

1.5 **The infinite dihedral group.** Assume $m(s,t)=\infty$, write $u:=st$, and let $u'_k$ be the alternating word of length $k$ beginning with $t$. By [F5], $u$ has infinite order, and by [F6] $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=k=\ell_{s,t}(u'_k)$ for all $k\ge0$. The union $X:=\{u_k:k\ge0\}\cup\{u'_k:k\ge0\}$ is closed under right multiplication by $s$ and $t$ ($u_{2j}s=u_{2j+1}$, $u_{2j+1}s=u_{2j}$, $u_{2j}t=u_{2j-1}$ for $j\ge1$, $u_0t=u'_1$, $u_{2j+1}t=u_{2j+2}$, symmetrically for $u'_k$), so $X=W_{s,t}$; by [F6] the elements $u_k$ are pairwise distinct with $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=k$ for every $k\ge0$, and likewise the $u'_k$. The Cayley graph of $W_{s,t}$ for $\{s,t\}$ is connected, and every vertex has degree exactly $2$ (as in 1.4), so it is the two-way infinite path $\cdots u'_2,u'_1,1,u_1,u_2\cdots$, and the graph distance from $1$ to $u_k$, or to $u'_k$, equals $\ell_{s,t}$ of that element, namely $k$. On the affine line $\{f\in P^*:f(e_s+e_t)=1\}$, parametrized by $y_s$, the generators act by $s:y_s\mapsto-y_s$ and $t:y_s\mapsto 2-y_s$ by [F3] (here $c=1$); consequently the affine map of $u_k$ is $y_s\mapsto y_s-2j$ for $k=2j$ and $y_s\mapsto-y_s-2j$ for $k=2j+1$, and that of $u'_k$ is $y_s\mapsto y_s+2j$ for $k=2j$ and $y_s\mapsto-y_s+2j+2$ for $k=2j+1$, by induction on $k$ (each step composes with $s$ or $t$ alternately). Hence the trace of $u_kC_P^\circ$ is the interval $(-k,-k+1)$ and that of $u'_kC_P^\circ$ is $(k,k+1)$. Since these intervals are pairwise distinct, an element of $W_{s,t}=X$ is determined by the trace of its chamber. Writing $v_j$ $(j\in\mathbb Z)$ for the element with trace $(j,j+1)$, namely $v_j=u_{-j}$ for $j\le0$ and $v_j=u'_j$ for $j\ge0$, one therefore has $\ell_{s,t}(v_j)=|j|$ together with $s\cdot v_j=v_{-j-1}$ and $t\cdot v_j=v_{1-j}$, because the affine maps send the interval $(j,j+1)$ to $(-j-1,-j)$, respectively $(1-j,2-j)$. [F3, F5, F6, algebra]

1.6 **Signs in the infinite case.** In the situation of 1.5 the sign of $y_s$ on $v_jC_P^\circ$ is the sign of $y_s$ on the interval $(j,j+1)$, hence negative exactly when $j\le-1$, and the sign of $y_t=1-y_s$ is negative exactly when $j\ge1$. [F3, algebra]

1.7 **(P_0) and (Q_0).** For $w=1$ one has $\ell(w)=0$, $wC^\circ=C^\circ\subseteq B_r$ for every $r\in S$ by 1.1, and choosing $u=1$ gives $C^\circ\subseteq B_s\cap B_t$ and $\ell(1)=0=\ell_{s,t}(1)+\ell(1)$ for all $s\ne t$. [F3, given]

2.1 **Signs of the finite sectors.** In the finite case let $C_k:=u_kC_P^\circ$ $(0\le k<2m)$; by [F4] these are the interiors of all $2m$ sectors. Consecutive $u_k$ differ by right multiplication by a generator $r$, so $u_kC_P$ and $u_krC_P$ share the image under $u_k$ of the wall of $C_P$ fixed by $r$ and lie on its opposite sides, by [F3]. They are adjacent sectors. The distinct vertices of the Cayley cycle in 1.4 therefore list the sectors in cyclic order. The sign of $y_s$ is constant on each $C_k$ (no $C_k$ meets the line $y_s=0$ by 1.3), is $+$ on $C_0$, and is $-$ on $C_1=sC_P^\circ$ because $s$ negates $y_s$ by [F3]. It changes exactly at those transitions whose common boundary ray lies on the line $y_s=0$: that line contains exactly two boundary rays of the arrangement, hence exactly two transitions; and the involution $s$ maps each sector to the sector on the opposite side of $y_s=0$ and fixes no sector, because $s$ swaps the two half-planes and no sector meets the wall. Hence exactly $m$ sectors lie on each side, and the negative side is the contiguous block of $m$ sectors containing $C_1$ but not $C_0$, that is $\{k:y_s<0\text{ on }C_k\}=\{1,\dots,m\}$. Applying the same argument to $t$, which is $u_{2m-1}$ and negates $y_t$, gives $\{k:y_t<0\text{ on }C_k\}=\{m,\dots,2m-1\}$. [F3, F4, step 1.3, step 1.4, algebra]

2.2 **Descents in the infinite case.** In the situation of 1.5, let $v=v_j$ be the element with trace $(j,j+1)$; by 1.5, $\ell_{s,t}(v)=|j|$ and $\ell_{s,t}(sv)=|j+1|$, so $\ell_{s,t}(sv)=\ell_{s,t}(v)-1$ exactly when $j\le-1$, which by 1.6 is exactly the side $y_s<0$; and $\ell_{s,t}(tv)=|j-1|$, so $\ell_{s,t}(tv)=\ell_{s,t}(v)-1$ exactly when $j\ge1$, exactly the side $y_t<0$. In particular no element has both left descents, and $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=k$ for all $k\ge0$ with the $u_k$ pairwise distinct. [step 1.5, step 1.6, algebra]

2.3 **The dichotomy in the original space.** Combining 1.2 with 1.3: for every $v\in W_{s,t}$ and $s'\in\{s,t\}$ exactly one of $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq B_{s'}$ and $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq s'B_{s'}$ holds, according to the side of $H_{e_{s'}}\cap P^*$ carrying $vC_P^\circ$. [step 1.2, step 1.3]

3.1 **Descents in the finite case.** Let $m(s,t)<\infty$ and $v=u_k$ $(0\le k<2m)$. By 1.4, $\ell_{s,t}(su_k)$ equals the distance from $0$ to $\sigma(k)\equiv1-k$, namely $k-1$ when $1\le k\le m$ and $2m+1-k$ when $m<k<2m$, and equals $1$ when $k=0$; comparing with $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=\min(k,2m-k)$ gives $\ell_{s,t}(su_k)=\ell_{s,t}(u_k)-1$ exactly for $1\le k\le m$. Likewise $\ell_{s,t}(tu_k)=\ell_{s,t}(u_k)-1$ exactly for $m\le k\le2m-1$, and $\ell_{s,t}(tu_k)=\ell_{s,t}(u_k)+1$ for $0\le k<m$. Hence, by 2.1, the left descent for $s$ occurs exactly on the side $y_s<0$ and the left descent for $t$ exactly on the side $y_t<0$, and $u_m$ is the unique element with both descents. [step 1.4, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 **Part (1).** Let $v\in W_{s,t}$ and $s'\in\{s,t\}$. The dichotomy is 2.3, and its two alternatives correspond to the signs of $y_{s'}$ on $vC_P^\circ$. If $m(s,t)<\infty$ then $v=u_k$ for a unique $k$ by 1.4 and $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=\min(k,2m-k)$; the negative side alternative holds exactly for $k\in\{1,\dots,m\}$ when $s'=s$ and for $k\in\{m,\dots,2m-1\}$ when $s'=t$ by 2.1, and exactly there $s'v$ shortens by 3.1; the elements $u_0,\dots,u_{2m-1}$ exhaust $W_{s,t}$, and the unique double-descent element is $u_m$. If $m(s,t)=\infty$ then every $v$ has a trace $(j,j+1)$ and $\ell_{s,t}(u_k)=k$ with the $u_k$ pairwise distinct by 2.2; the negative side alternative for $s'$ holds exactly for $j\le-1$ (respectively $j\ge1$), and exactly there $s'v$ shortens by 2.2, and no element has both descents. This proves all clauses of (1) in both cases. [step 1.4, step 1.5, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 2.3]

5.1 **The conditional step for $(\mathrm P)$.** Fix $n\ge0$ and suppose only $(\mathrm Q_n)$. If $S$ is empty there is no $s$ to check. If $S=\{s\}$ then $W=\{1,s\}$ by $s^2=1$ and [F5], and the only positive-length chamber is $sC^\circ\subseteq sB_s$, with $\ell(s)=1$, proving $(\mathrm P_{n+1})$ directly. Now suppose $|S|\ge2$. First $(\mathrm Q_n)$ and (1) imply $(\mathrm P_n)$: for any $a$ of length $n$ and generator $r$, choose $q\ne r$ and use $(\mathrm Q_n)$ to write $a=ud$, with $aC^\circ\subseteq u(B_r\cap B_q)$ and $n=\ell_{r,q}(u)+\ell(d)$. The rank-two dichotomy gives the positive alternative or the negative alternative with $\ell_{r,q}(ru)=\ell_{r,q}(u)-1$; in the latter case $\ell(ra)\le\ell_{r,q}(ru)+\ell(d)=n-1$, hence equality by [F5]. This is $(\mathrm P_n)$. Let $w\in W$ with $\ell(w)=n+1$ and let $s\in S$; choose $t$ with $w=tw'$ and $\ell(w')=n$. If $s=t$, then $(\mathrm P_n)$ applied to $w'$ gives $w'C^\circ\subseteq B_s$: the second alternative would give $\ell(sw')=\ell(w')-1=n-1$, whereas $\ell(sw')=\ell(w)=n+1$ here. Hence $wC^\circ=t(w'C^\circ)\subseteq tB_s=sB_s$ and $\ell(sw)=\ell(w')=n=\ell(w)-1$, the second alternative of $(\mathrm P_{n+1})$. If $s\ne t$, apply $(\mathrm Q_n)$ to $w'$: there is $u\in W_{s,t}$ with $w'C^\circ\subseteq u(B_s\cap B_t)$ and $\ell(w')=\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w')$. Put $v:=tu\in W_{s,t}$. If $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq B_s$, then $wC^\circ\subseteq tu(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq B_s$. Otherwise $v(B_s\cap B_t)\subseteq sB_s$ and $\ell_{s,t}(sv)=\ell_{s,t}(v)-1$ by (1) from 4.1, so with $d:=u^{-1}w'$ one has $wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s$ and $\ell(sw)=\ell(stu\,d)\le\ell_{s,t}(stu)+\ell(d)=\ell_{s,t}(v)-1+\ell(d)\le\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(d)=\ell(w')=\ell(w)-1$, where $\ell_{s,t}(tu)\le1+\ell_{s,t}(u)$ follows by prepending $t$ to a shortest word for $u$. The reverse bound $\ell(sw)\ge\ell(w)-1$ follows from [F5], so $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1$, the second alternative of $(\mathrm P_{n+1})$. [step 4.1, F3, F5, algebra]

6.1 **The conditional step for $(\mathrm Q)$.** Suppose $(\mathrm Q_n)$ and $(\mathrm P_m)$ for all $m\le n+1$, as in the second conditional implication of (2), and let $w\in W$ with $\ell(w)=n+1$ and $s\ne t$. By [F5] factor $w=u\,d$ with $u\in W_{s,t}$ and $d$ the minimal representative of $W_{s,t}w$; then $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ and $\ell(s'd)>\ell(d)$ for $s'\in\{s,t\}$. Since $\ell(d)\le n+1$, $(\mathrm P_{\ell(d)})$ is available: it is part of the stated supposition. Applying it to $d$ and $s'\in\{s,t\}$, the second alternative would give $\ell(s'd)=\ell(d)-1$, contradicting $\ell(s'd)>\ell(d)$; hence $dC^\circ\subseteq B_s\cap B_t$. Therefore $wC^\circ=u(dC^\circ)\subseteq u(B_s\cap B_t)$ and $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)=\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w)$, which is $(\mathrm Q_{n+1})$. [step 5.1, F5, algebra]

7.1 **The induction.** $(\mathrm P_0)$ and $(\mathrm Q_0)$ hold by 1.7, and 5.1 and 6.1 show that the validity of $(\mathrm P_m)$ and $(\mathrm Q_m)$ for all $m\le n$ implies $(\mathrm P_{n+1})$ and $(\mathrm Q_{n+1})$, for every $n\ge0$. By the induction principle [F7], $(\mathrm P_n)$ and $(\mathrm Q_n)$ hold for every $n\ge0$. [step 1.7, step 5.1, step 6.1, F7]

8.1 **Part (3).** Let $w\in W$ and $s\ne t$, and factor $w=u\,d$ with $u\in W_{s,t}$ and $d$ the minimal representative of $W_{s,t}w$. By [F5], $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ and $\ell(s'd)>\ell(d)$ for $s'\in\{s,t\}$; since $\ell(d)\le\ell(w)$, $(\mathrm P_{\ell(d)})$ from 7.1 applies to $d$, and the second alternative would give $\ell(s'd)=\ell(d)-1$, a contradiction; hence $dC^\circ\subseteq B_s\cap B_t$ and $wC^\circ=u(dC^\circ)\subseteq u(B_s\cap B_t)$, with $\ell(w)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)=\ell_{s,t}(u)+\ell(u^{-1}w)$ because $u^{-1}w=d$ and $\ell_{s,t}(u)=\ell(u)$. [step 7.1, F5, algebra]

8.2 **Forward direction of (4).** Let $w\in W$ and $s\in S$. By $(\mathrm P_{\ell(w)})$ from 7.1, $wC^\circ\subseteq B_s$, or $wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s$ and $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1$; in particular $wC^\circ\subseteq sB_s$ implies $\ell(sw)<\ell(w)$. [step 7.1]

9.1 **Converse direction of (4) and conclusion.** Suppose $\ell(sw)<\ell(w)$ and put $w':=sw$, so that $\ell(w')=\ell(w)-1$ and $w=sw'$. By $(\mathrm P_{\ell(w')})$ from 7.1 applied to $w'$, either $w'C^\circ\subseteq B_s$, or $w'C^\circ\subseteq sB_s$ and $\ell(sw')=\ell(w')-1$; the second alternative would give $\ell(w)=\ell(sw')=\ell(w')-1=\ell(w)-2$, which is impossible, so $w'C^\circ\subseteq B_s$. Applying the linear bijection $f\mapsto s\cdot f$ of [F3] gives $wC^\circ=s(w'C^\circ)\subseteq sB_s$. Together with 8.2 this is the equivalence (4); and (1) is 4.1, (2) is 7.1 and (3) is 8.1, so all four clauses of the statement hold. [step 8.2, step 7.1, step 4.1, step 8.1] ∎
