## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and let $d_1\colon\bigoplus_{i}M(s_i\circ\lambda)\to M(\lambda)$ be the first BGG differential of [[def-bgg-differential-from-signed-verma-maps]], indexed by the simple reflections $s_i$. Then $\operatorname{im}d_1=\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)=\ker\pi$, where $\pi\colon M(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L(\lambda)$ is the canonical surjection and $M(s_i\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(\lambda)$ is the canonical submodule generated by the singular vector $f_i^{\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle+1}v_\lambda$ of [[lem-simple-root-singular-vector-in-a-verma-module]]. Consequently the augmented complex is exact at $C_0$ and $L(\lambda)=\operatorname{coker}(d_1)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a dominant integral weight $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$, the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ with highest weight vector $v_\lambda$, its canonical simple quotient $\pi\colon M(\lambda)\twoheadrightarrow L(\lambda)$, and the first BGG differential $d_1=\bigoplus_i(d_1)_{s_i,e}$.

[F1] The elements of $W$ of length $1$ are exactly the simple reflections $s_i$, and the arrows into the identity are the covers $s_i\rhd e$ with label $\alpha_i$; for every $i$ the component $(d_1)_{s_i,e}=\varepsilon(s_i,e)\,\iota_{s_i\to e}$ with $\varepsilon(s_i,e)=\pm1$ is a nonzero scalar multiple of the canonical injective embedding $\iota_{s_i\to e}\colon M(s_i\circ\lambda)\hookrightarrow M(\lambda)$, and all other components vanish ([[def-bgg-differential-from-signed-verma-maps]], [[lem-bruhat-covers-give-unique-verma-embeddings]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]], [[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]]).

[F2] $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is the $\mathfrak g$-submodule of $M(\lambda)$ generated by the singular vector $f_i^{m_i+1}v_\lambda$, where $m_i=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\ge0$; this vector has weight $s_i\circ\lambda\ne\lambda$, and every weight of $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is $\le s_i\circ\lambda$ in the root order, so $\lambda$ is not a weight of $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$ ([[lem-dominant-integral-dot-translates-embed-in-the-verma-module]], [[lem-simple-root-singular-vector-in-a-verma-module]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]]).

[F3] $M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)/K$ as a left $U(\mathfrak g)$-module, where $K$ is the left ideal generated by $\mathfrak n^+$ and by $\{H-\lambda(H):H\in\mathfrak h\}$, with $v_\lambda=1+K$; equivalently $M(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U(\mathfrak b)}\mathbb C_\lambda$ with the induced universal property ([[def-verma-module]], [[thm-universal-property-of-verma-modules]], [[thm-pbw-model-of-a-verma-module]]).

[F4] The cyclic module $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)=U(\mathfrak g)/I_\lambda$ of [[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]] is generated by $v_\lambda=1+I_\lambda$ with $\mathfrak n^+v_\lambda=0$, $Hv_\lambda=\lambda(H)v_\lambda$, $f_i^{m_i+1}v_\lambda=0$, and it is finite-dimensional ([[lem-simple-root-integrability-bounds-the-dominant-cyclic-module]]).

[F5] The sum $J(\lambda)$ of all proper submodules of $M(\lambda)$ is the unique maximal submodule and $\ker\pi=J(\lambda)$; $L(\lambda)=M(\lambda)/J(\lambda)$ is simple, and $L(\lambda)$ is the unique simple quotient of $M(\lambda)$ ([[thm-verma-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]], [[lem-sum-of-all-proper-submodules-of-a-verma-module-is-proper]], [[lem-a-proper-verma-submodule-misses-the-highest-weight-line]]).

[F6] Finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-modules are completely reducible, and the finite-dimensional simple $\mathfrak g$-modules are exactly the $L(\mu)$ for $\mu\in\Lambda^+$, with $L(\mu)\cong L(\mu')$ only for $\mu=\mu'$; every weight of $L(\mu)$ is $\le\mu$ ([[cor-every-finite-dimensional-representation-is-a-direct-sum-of-highest-weight-modules]], [[thm-finite-dimensional-simple-modules-are-classified-by-dominant-highest-weights]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]]).

[F7] For every simple root $\alpha_i$, $\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=\langle\lambda,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle+1\ge1$, and $s_i\circ\lambda=\lambda-\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle\alpha_i\ne\lambda$ ([[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]]).

## Proof

1.1 The image of $d_1$ is the sum of the images of its components. By [F1] the only nonzero components are the $(s_i,e)$-components, each of which is a nonzero scalar multiple of the injective embedding $\iota_{s_i\to e}$; hence the image of the $i$-th summand is exactly $M(s_i\circ\lambda)\subseteq M(\lambda)$, and $\operatorname{im}d_1=\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$. [F1]

1.2 Each $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is a proper submodule: $s_i\circ\lambda\ne\lambda$ by [F7], and a submodule of $M(\lambda)$ containing $v_\lambda$ would be all of $M(\lambda)$ and would contain the weight $\lambda$, which by [F2] is not a weight of $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$. Hence $\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)\subseteq J(\lambda)=\ker\pi$. [F2, F5, F7]

1.3 The quotient $Q:=M(\lambda)/\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is isomorphic to $M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$. Indeed, by [F3] the preimage in $U(\mathfrak g)$ of $\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is the left ideal generated by $K$ together with the elements $f_i^{m_i+1}$, because the submodule generated by the vectors $f_i^{m_i+1}v_\lambda$ has preimage $K+\sum_iU(\mathfrak g)f_i^{m_i+1}$ and $M(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is exactly that submodule by [F2]; this preimage is precisely the left ideal $I_\lambda$ of [F4]. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 $Q$ is finite-dimensional by [F4] and step 1.3, and its generator $\bar v=v_\lambda+\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is nonzero of weight $\lambda$: it is nonzero because the sum is proper by step 1.2, and $Q=U(\mathfrak g)\bar v$ with $Q_\lambda=\mathbb C\bar v$ because every weight of $Q$ is $\le\lambda$ and the weight-$\lambda$ space of the quotient is the image of $M(\lambda)_\lambda=\mathbb Cv_\lambda$. [step 1.3, F2, F4]

3.1 We identify $Q$ with $L(\lambda)$. As a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g$-module, $Q$ is completely reducible, $Q\cong\bigoplus_jL(\mu_j)$, and the multiplicity of $L(\lambda)$ in $Q$ equals $\dim Q_\lambda=1$: a summand $L(\mu)$ has a weight-$\lambda$ vector only if $\lambda\le\mu$, and all weights $\mu_j$ of $Q$ satisfy $\mu_j\le\lambda$ because $Q$ is a quotient of $M(\lambda)$; hence $\mu=\lambda$, and $L(\lambda)$ occurs with multiplicity $\dim Q_\lambda=1$. Since $Q$ is generated by $\bar v\in Q_\lambda$, which lies in the unique $L(\lambda)$-summand, $Q$ equals that summand: $Q\cong L(\lambda)$. [F4, F5, F6, step 2.1]

4.1 Since $Q=M(\lambda)/\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is simple by step 3.1, the submodule $\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)$ is maximal in $M(\lambda)$. It is contained in $J(\lambda)$ by step 1.2, and $J(\lambda)$ is the unique maximal submodule by [F5], so $\sum_iM(s_i\circ\lambda)=J(\lambda)=\ker\pi$. Combining with step 1.1 gives $\operatorname{im}d_1=\ker\pi=\ker d_0$: the augmented complex is exact at $C_0$, and $\operatorname{coker}(d_1)=M(\lambda)/\operatorname{im}d_1\cong L(\lambda)$. [F5, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 3.1] ∎
