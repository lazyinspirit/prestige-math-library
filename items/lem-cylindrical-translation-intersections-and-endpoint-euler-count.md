## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $\Sigma$ be an oriented closed surface with transverse finite attaching-curve systems, and give $W=\Sigma\times\mathbb R_s\times[0,1]_t$ the orientation of $\Sigma$ followed by $ds\wedge dt$. Suppose an actual smooth proper embedded oriented surface $U=(g,f):S\to W$ has the following supplied data.

* The compactification $\widehat S$ has $2m$ boundary corners, $m\ge1$, with $m$ at each end. The base projection $f$ is an oriented degree-$m$ branched cover of the compactified strip, topologically unramified on its boundary and with finitely many interior branches. At each horizontal boundary its $m$ sheets lie on the $m$ distinct corresponding attaching curves. The finite cut-cover and adapted cellulation data for $g$ satisfy the finite sector-cover theorem. Away from corners $g$ is an oriented local diffeomorphism except at finitely many positive power-model branch points, possibly on the horizontal boundary; none of their images is an alpha-beta crossing. It has no other critical points.
* At each branch of $g$, $f$ is regular, and in a positive target complex chart $g$, as a function of the base coordinate $z=s+it$, is holomorphic near that point. At a boundary branch the chart makes its attaching curve real and this holomorphic function extends across the real base boundary and is real there. This is an explicit local analytic hypothesis.
* There are supplied annular coordinates $(X,Y)$ about each attaching curve, with curve $Y=0$, compatible with the generator charts below. In the sheet graphs over a boundary collar, $X_t=0$ at $t=0$, and $X_t=0$ at $t=1$, respectively. Here one may equivalently use the inward coordinate $1-t$ at the top. These conditions specify the boundary first jet needed for reflection.
* There are distinct endpoint points $x_1,\ldots,x_m$ and $y_1,\ldots,y_m$, with the two sets disjoint. In disjoint positive generator charts taking the attaching curves to the real and imaginary axes, all sufficiently far ends are exactly the graphs
$$g_j(s,t)=x_j+a_j e^{\pi s/2}e^{i\pi t/2}\quad(s<-C),\qquad g_j(s,t)=y_j+b_j e^{-\pi s/2}e^{-i\pi t/2}\quad(s>C),$$
where $a_j,b_j$ are nonzero real constants and the base projection is $(s,t)$. Thus all image end sectors are acute. The annular coordinates agree with these charts near the endpoints.

Let $D$ have the region multiplicities of this finite cut-cover map. For an alpha-beta crossing $p$, let $n_p(D)$ be the average of its four adjacent region multiplicities, and put $n_{\mathbf x}=\sum_jn_{x_j}$ and $n_{\mathbf y}=\sum_jn_{y_j}$. Then
$$\chi(\widehat S)=m-n_{\mathbf x}(D)-n_{\mathbf y}(D)+e(D).$$
More precisely, the algebraic intersection of $U$ with its nonzero real translate, counting boundary intersections by one half, is conserved through the actual translation and end-flattening homotopies used in the proof. For a small positive translate it equals $R_{\mathrm{int}}+R_{\mathrm{bdry}}/2$, the sum of target-projection ramification defects with half weight on the boundary. For a sufficiently large translate followed by the explicit end flattenings it equals $n_{\mathbf x}+n_{\mathbf y}-m/2$. No genericity of the intervening intersections is required.

If these data also constitute a clean incidence presentation of an actual strip under the hypotheses of the clean-incidence strip-index theorem, its actual real index is consequently $e(D)+n_{\mathbf x}(D)+n_{\mathbf y}(D)$, before translation and with the same higher Sobolev and weighted realizations specified there. In particular that additional clean-incidence hypothesis rules out boundary target ramification. No assertion that arbitrary Whitney classes have these data, or treatment of shared endpoint coordinates, constant incidence components or degenerate endpoints, is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice and exactly the actual embedded cylindrical, finite cover, local analytic, boundary-jet, annular and exact-end data in the Statement. Write $T_R(p,s,t)=(p,s+R,t)$.

[F1] Finite-dimensional local inverses exist at invertible derivatives and are as smooth as the given map ([[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]], [[thm-higher-regularity-of-local-inverses]]).

[F2] The finite sector-cover identity, for the $2m$ acute corners here, reads $e(D)=\chi(\widehat S)-m/2+R_{\mathrm{int}}+R_{\mathrm{bdry}}/2$ ([[lem-finite-sector-cover-euler-measure-and-boundary-ramification]]).

[F3] For a compact finite-triangulated source, the isolated end zero-index sums of a complex-line section are invariant through an arbitrary continuous homotopy nonzero on its boundary. A closed codimension-two submanifold with an actual metric normal tube supplies its divisor line and hence actual intersection conservation. A reflection double divides the doubled conservation law by two; the translated local power graph has full count $r$ and half-boundary count $r/2$ ([[lem-finite-surface-section-index-and-relative-intersection-homotopy]]).

[F4] Under its explicit clean incidence, field, finite chart and rounding hypotheses, the actual strip real index is $m-\chi(\widehat S)+2e(D)$, with the same higher integer Hilbert Sobolev and smooth weighted index ([[lem-clean-incidence-cover-strip-index-and-corner-comparison]]).

## Proof

1.1 **All small-translation intersections are the target branches.** On a compact core, an embedded surface has local transverse coordinates by [F1]. The normal component of a small translation has first-order term $R$ times the normal projection of $\partial_s$, with uniformly bounded remainder $O(R^2)$ on finitely many charts. The normal projection vanishes precisely where $\partial_s$ is tangent to $U$, equivalently where $dg$ has kernel: if $\partial_s=dU(v)$ then $dg(v)=0$, and conversely at a target branch $df$ is invertible so a tangent vector maps to $\partial_s$. Thus outside small neighborhoods of the finitely many target branches no small-translation intersections occur. Compact embeddedness also excludes intersections of separated source neighborhoods: otherwise sequences of their points at distance tending to zero have the same limiting image, contradicting local injectivity. The exact tail graphs have differences $a_j e^{\pi s/2}(1-e^{-\pi R/2})e^{i\pi t/2}$ at the negative end and $b_j e^{-\pi s/2}(1-e^{\pi R/2})e^{-i\pi t/2}$ at the positive end; neither vanishes for $R>0$. Distinct end charts are disjoint. Hence no intersections are hidden arbitrarily far out. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Count every branch, including higher order and the boundary.** At a target branch of defect $r$, use the base coordinate centered there. Its local expression is $g(z)=c z^{r+1}+O(z^{r+2})$ with $c\ne0$. Intersections with a small translate solve $g(z+R)=g(z)$. After $z=R\zeta$, the quotient by $cR^{r+1}$ tends with all first derivatives on compact $\zeta$ sets to $(\zeta+1)^{r+1}-\zeta^{r+1}$. Its $r$ roots are distinct by the explicit power computation in [F3]; [F1] gives one nearby root for each, all transverse. No further roots occur on a bounded rescaled disk by a positive minimum off those root neighborhoods. In the remaining small annulus $|z|>KR$, Taylor gives $g(z+R)-g(z)=R(r+1)c z^r(1+O(R/|z|)+O(|z|))$, nonzero for $K$ large and the chart small. Since the two local graph equations are holomorphic, every transverse local intersection is positive. At a boundary branch the stated real analytic extension pairs roots by conjugation; a simple real limiting root stays real by uniqueness, and nonreal pairs stay on opposite sides. Counting boundary roots by one half gives exactly $r/2$. We obtain a small $\varepsilon>0$ with finite transverse endpoint intersections and weighted total $I_\varepsilon=R_{\mathrm{int}}+R_{\mathrm{bdry}}/2$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 **Prepare and count the large-translation endpoint.** Increase $C$ so that every finite preimage under $g$ of any $x_j,y_j$ lies in $|s|<C$; the exact tails never hit these points, and target branches avoid crossings, so the remaining preimages are regular and form a finite set in the compact core. Choose a real cutoff equal to one on that core and zero sufficiently far along the positive ends of $U$, replacing each $y_j+b_j e^{-\pi s/2}e^{-i\pi t/2}$ by $y_j+\chi_+(s)b_j e^{-\pi s/2}e^{-i\pi t/2}$. On the negative ends of $T_RU$ similarly replace the exponential displacement from $x_j$ by $\chi_-(s-R)$ times it. The cutoffs lie in $[0,1]$. These are actual embeddings: each affected sheet is a graph over the unchanged base and stays in its own separated endpoint chart. Take $R$ larger than twice the enlarged transition-core width. The two cores are disjoint in $s$. In the left core the second surface is exactly the vertical fiber union over $\mathbf x$; in the right core the first surface is exactly the fiber union over $\mathbf y$; between them the two fiber unions are disjoint because the endpoint sets are disjoint. Thus intersections are exactly the finite preimages of the $x_j$ in the original core and the translated preimages of the $y_j$. An interior regular preimage has positive intersection sign one, a boundary regular preimage has the same positive doubled sign with weight one half, by the positive local derivative of $g$. These intersections are transverse even on the boundary, since the normal and tangential derivatives of $g$ there are nonzero independent directions by its regularity and the stated zero tangential normal jet. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Identify the quarter contributions, rather than assuming them.** For any crossing $p$ among the two endpoint sets, each interior regular preimage covers all four local quadrants and contributes one to their average. A boundary regular preimage covers exactly the two quadrants on one side of its attaching curve and contributes one half. Each end corner covers just one quadrant by its exact acute exponential chart and contributes one quarter. These are all preimages: compactness of $\widehat S$ and the supplied finite cut-cover data exclude additional sheets near $p$. Since the two endpoint sets are disjoint, each $x_j$ has exactly its one negative-end corner and no positive-end corner, and each $y_j$ exactly its one positive-end corner. Therefore the count in step 3.1 is $I_{\mathrm{large}}=(n_{\mathbf x}-m/4)+(n_{\mathbf y}-m/4)=n_{\mathbf x}+n_{\mathbf y}-m/2$. This is a finite local sector count, with no identification of different source preimages omitted. [given, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 **The connecting homotopy has an actual compact intersection region.** First translate through $R\in[\varepsilon,R_{\mathrm{large}}]$, and then insert the two cutoffs continuously. Throughout, the far negative-end difference is a nonzero constant chart factor times $1-b(s,R)e^{-\pi R/2}$ with $0\le b\le1$; the far positive-end difference is a factor times $a(s,R)-e^{\pi R/2}$ with $0\le a\le1$. Their absolute scalar factors are bounded below by $1-e^{-\pi\varepsilon/2}$ and $e^{\pi\varepsilon/2}-1$, respectively. Distinct endpoint chart components stay separated. Because the parameter interval is compact and the core shifts are bounded, all intersections therefore lie in one finite $s$ window. The source pieces covering that window are compact by properness of $f$. Make the parameterization constant near both ends of its parameter interval and extend it slightly past those endpoints; this permits using a trace without a parameter boundary. No claim that intermediate intersections are transverse or isolated is being made. [step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Construct a smooth reflection double for this homotopy.** Boundary unramifiedness of the finite cover supplies finitely many sheet graphs over thin collars of $t=0,1$, with no base branches in them. In the supplied annular coordinates write such a graph at the bottom as $(X(a,b),Y(a,b),s=a,t=b)$, where $Y(a,0)=0$ and $X_b(a,0)=0$. In a thinner collar replace it by $(X(a,0),bY_b(a,0),a,b)$, interpolating with a fixed smooth cutoff in $b$ and retaining the original graph outside the collar. At the top use $b=1-t$. Angle differences in $X$ are taken in its unique sufficiently small lift in the annulus. Taylor's formula gives an arbitrarily small $C^1$ change as the collar shrinks, uniformly on the compact core and the parameter interval; the boundary values and first jets are exactly unchanged. Graph form and separation of the distinct attaching curves preserve embeddedness. All endpoint intersections in steps 2.1–3.1 are finite and transverse, so their values and signs stay fixed: boundary roots are fixed with their first jets, and [F1] gives uniqueness near each root under a sufficiently small $C^1$ change; outside these finitely many neighborhoods compact separation excludes new roots. Tail regularization retains a separated exponential factor $e^{\pm\pi s/2}H(t)$ with $H(t)\ne0$, because the boundary annuli agree with the end charts; choose the collars so its component near the relevant real or imaginary axis stays nonzero. Thus step 5.1's uniform exclusion of tail intersections persists. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 **The doubled geometry and its signs are explicit.** Take two copies of the ambient strip manifold and glue their boundary annular neighborhoods by $(X,Y,s)\mapsto(X,-Y,s)$ at $t=0$, and the analogous map at $t=1$. Use closed thinner annuli for gluing and remove their outer rim seams; they are disjoint from every boundary image of the surfaces. This gives a Hausdorff manifold neighborhood of all doubled surfaces. Near a glued annulus the smooth coordinates on the second copy are $(X,-Y,s,-b)$ relative to the first, so the copy exchange sends $(Y,b)$ to $(-Y,-b)$ and fixes the real boundary cylinder. The regularized graphs of step 6.1 become exactly $X=X(a,0)$, $Y=bY_b(a,0)$ across $b=0$, and therefore double smoothly, including their smooth parameter dependence. Ambient orientation is preserved by the two normal sign reversals. Each doubled surface's orientation on the second source copy reverses, so the signs of an interior intersection and its reflected partner agree. A boundary intersection has one doubled representative, with exactly the sign determined by its unchanged two tangent planes. Hence the doubled endpoint totals are $2I_\varepsilon$ and $2I_{\mathrm{large}}$. Extra ambient boundary left outside the glued annuli is disjoint from the surfaces; source end truncation boundaries can be placed beyond the compact intersection window of step 5.1. [step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 **Supply the normal tube and conserve through the full joint trace.** In the doubled ambient manifold times a slightly extended open parameter interval, the trace of the first moving surface is an actual closed embedded codimension-two submanifold: the parameter coordinate separates its slices, and properness in the bounded-shift base coordinate makes it closed. Restrict to a slightly larger finite $s$ window than needed. It has a metric normal disk tube on that region. Here are the required finite-dimensional details. Patch Euclidean metrics in finitely many charts by smooth bumps. The geodesic equation in those charts is a smooth first-order equation for position and velocity; Picard iteration on a compact coordinate ball converges with successive bounds $M L^n\tau^{n+1}/(n+1)!$. The same estimates on the differentiated integral equations give smooth dependence on initial data of every order. This defines a smooth local exponential, whose derivative on the zero normal section is the isomorphism $T Z\oplus\nu Z\to TW$. Apply [F1] at each point and use a finite cover. A uniform sufficiently small radius is injective over the compact enlarged core: otherwise choose a pair of coincident images with normal lengths below $1/n$ for every $n$, using Countable Choice. Compactness gives base subsequences with the same limit, while the local inverse there contradicts coincidence. Shrink the radius smoothly toward the outer window edges so the tube stays in the chosen open region. Pull the resulting divisor line of [F3] back by the second moving surface together with its parameter. Its compact source truncation has no boundary zeros by step 5.1. Finite source triangulations can be made from the given finite sheet and face data, cutting and subdividing finitely many chart rectangles if needed; choose edge perturbations avoiding the finitely many endpoint roots. Thus [F3] applies to this actual trace and compact source, regardless of intermediate zero sets. It gives equality of the doubled endpoint intersection sums and hence $I_\varepsilon=I_{\mathrm{large}}$ by step 7.1. [F1, F3, step 5.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 **Conclude the Euler and conditional actual index formulas.** Combining steps 2.1, 4.1 and 8.1 gives $R_{\mathrm{int}}+R_{\mathrm{bdry}}/2=n_{\mathbf x}+n_{\mathbf y}-m/2$. Substituting in [F2] yields $\chi(\widehat S)=e(D)-n_{\mathbf x}-n_{\mathbf y}+m$, as asserted. If the additional actual clean-incidence index hypotheses are present, [F4] then gives $\operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb R}P=m-(m-n_{\mathbf x}-n_{\mathbf y}+e(D))+2e(D)=e(D)+n_{\mathbf x}+n_{\mathbf y}$, with its already specified higher Sobolev and weighted realizations. Every hypothesis concerning the model, finite cover, exact ends and disjoint endpoint supports has been retained; arbitrary Whitney-class representativity and the excluded endpoint cases are separate obligations. [F2, F4, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎
