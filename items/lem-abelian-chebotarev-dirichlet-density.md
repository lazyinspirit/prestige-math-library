## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice, and Dependent Choice. Let $E/F$ be a finite abelian extension of number fields, put $G=\operatorname{Gal}(E/F)$, and fix $\sigma\in G$. Let $S_\sigma$ be the set of nonzero prime ideals $\mathfrak p$ of $\mathcal O_F$ that are unramified in $E$ and whose arithmetic Frobenius element is $\sigma$. Its Dirichlet density is
$$\delta_F(S_\sigma)=\lim_{s\downarrow1,\ s>1}\frac{\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S_\sigma}(N_{F/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak p)^{-s}}{\log(1/(s-1))}=\frac1{|G|},$$
where $N_{F/\mathbb Q}\mathfrak p=|\mathcal O_F/\mathfrak p|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, Countable Choice, Dependent Choice, a finite abelian extension $E/F$ of number fields, its Galois group $G$, and $\sigma\in G$.

[F1] [[def-dirichlet-density-of-prime-ideals]]: The Dirichlet density of a set $S$ of prime ideals of a number field $F$ is the logarithmically normalized limit $\lim_{s\downarrow1,\ s>1}\sum_{\mathfrak p\in S}(N\mathfrak p)^{-s}/\log(1/(s-1))$, when the limit exists.

[F2] [[def-ray-class-group]]: For a number field $F$ and a modulus $\mathfrak m$, the ray class group is the quotient of the group of fractional ideals prime to $\mathfrak m$ by the subgroup of principal ideals generated by elements congruent to $1$ at the selected finite prime powers and positive at the selected real places.

[F3] [[thm-ray-class-group-finite]]: Under AC and Countable Choice, the ray class group for a number-field modulus is finite.

[F5] For this given finite abelian $E/F$, its norm subgroup is open, and the now supplied global Artin map identifies the quotient with $G$. Choose a modulus $\mathfrak m$ whose finite part contains every ramified prime and whose finite principal-unit factors are sufficiently small to lie in that open norm kernel; include every real place in its infinite part, so that the positive real factors are in the kernel. This is possible from the restricted-product neighbourhood basis, since the actual archimedean Artin factors are sign maps or trivial. The ray congruence subgroup is thus contained in $N_{E/F}C_E$. Consequently the supplied identification $\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(F)=C_F/C_{\mathfrak m}$ induces a surjection $\psi_{\mathfrak m}:\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(F)\twoheadrightarrow G$. At every $\mathfrak p\nmid\mathfrak m_0$, a uniformizer idele represents $[\mathfrak p]$, and the actual Artin normalization sends it to arithmetic Frobenius. This supplies the exact modulus and Frobenius-compatible quotient used below; no missing class-field theorem is assumed. [[lem-global-idele-norm-subgroup-is-open]] [[def-global-artin-map-as-product-of-local-reciprocity-maps]] [[thm-local-artin-unramified-frobenius-normalization]] [[thm-global-artin-reciprocity-and-norm-index]] [[thm-global-class-field-existence-number-field]] [[lem-number-field-open-idelic-subgroup-contains-norm-group]] [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]]

[F4] Every abstract ray class has prime-ideal Dirichlet density $1/|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(F)|$, by the supplied analytic nonvanishing and finite-character argument. This analytic result supplies the abstract ray-class density; [F5] supplies its arithmetic Frobenius identification. [[lem-ray-class-prime-sums-from-hecke-nonvanishing]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 By [F5], fix its modulus and surjection, and put $h=|\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(F)|$, finite by [F3]. For any $g_1,g_2\in G$, surjectivity gives a class $x$ with $\psi_{\mathfrak m}(x)=g_2g_1^{-1}$; left multiplication by $x$ bijects $\psi_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}(g_1)$ with $\psi_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}(g_2)$. Thus all $|G|$ fibers have the same finite cardinality; since they partition the $h$ ray classes, each has size $h/|G|$. [F5, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F4], each ray-class fiber contributes density $1/h$, and the finitely many classes in $\psi_{\mathfrak m}^{-1}(\sigma)$ are disjoint. By [F5], every prime not dividing $\mathfrak m_0$ is unramified in $E/F$ and its ray-class image equals its arithmetic Frobenius, so this union is $S_\sigma$ with the finite set of primes dividing $\mathfrak m_0$ removed. That finite set has bounded Dirichlet sum as $s\downarrow1$, hence zero Dirichlet density by [F1]. The union's density is therefore $(h/|G|)(1/h)=1/|G|$, as claimed. [F5, F1, F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
