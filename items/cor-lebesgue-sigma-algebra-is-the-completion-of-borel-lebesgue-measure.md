## Statement

Let $n \ge 1$ and **assume the Axiom of Countable Choice**
([[def-countable-choice]]). Write $\beta_n$ for the restriction of $\lambda_n$ to
the Borel sigma-algebra $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]). Then $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is exactly the
completion domain of $(\mathbb{R}^n, \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n), \beta_n)$
([[def-completion-of-a-measure-space]]), and $\lambda_n$ is the completed measure
there. Explicitly,

$$E \in \mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n) \iff E = A\cup N \text{ for some } A, Z \in \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n) \text{ with } N \subseteq Z \text{ and } \beta_n(Z) = 0,$$

and then $\lambda_n(E) = \beta_n(A)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n \ge 1$, the Axiom of Countable Choice, Lebesgue measure $\lambda_n$ on $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and the restriction $\beta_n$ of $\lambda_n$ to $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$.

[L1] Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice and let $\mu_0$ be a sigma-finite premeasure; the Carathéodory sigma-algebra of its induced outer measure is exactly the completion of $\sigma(\mathcal A_0)$ under the extended measure, and the Carathéodory restriction equals the completed measure there ([[thm-caratheodory-domain-is-the-completion-under-sigma-finiteness]]).

[L2] Elementary volume $\mu_0$ is a sigma-finite premeasure on the algebra $\mathcal{E}_n$ of elementary sets ([[thm-box-volume-is-a-premeasure-on-the-elementary-algebra]], [[def-elementary-set-in-rn]]).

[L3] $\lambda_n^*$ is the outer set function induced by the premeasure $\mu_0$ on $\mathcal{E}_n$ ([[def-lebesgue-outer-measure]]), and $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is the family of sets Carathéodory measurable for $\lambda_n^*$, with $\lambda_n$ its restriction ([[def-lebesgue-measure-and-the-lebesgue-sigma-algebra]]).

[L4] $\sigma(\mathcal{E}_n) = \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ ([[lem-half-open-boxes-generate-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-rn]]).

[L5] Assuming countable choice, $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ is a sigma-algebra and $\lambda_n$ is a complete measure on it ([[thm-lebesgue-measure-is-a-complete-measure]]), and every Borel set is Lebesgue measurable ([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]).

[F1] The **completion domain** of $(X,\mathcal A,\mu)$ is $\overline{\mathcal A}:=\{E\subseteq X: E=A\cup N$ for some $A,Z\in\mathcal A$ and $N\subseteq Z$ with $\mu(Z)=0\}$, and the completed set function is $\overline\mu(E):=\mu(A)$ ([[def-completion-of-a-measure-space]]).

[F2] Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice; then $\overline\mu$ is a complete measure on $\overline{\mathcal A}$ extending $\mu$, and it is the unique complete measure on $\overline{\mathcal A}$ that extends $\mu$ ([[thm-completion-of-a-measure-space]]).

[F3] The **Axiom of Countable Choice** says that for every family $(X_n)_{n \in \mathbb{N}}$ of nonempty sets indexed by $\mathbb{N}$ there is a function $f$ with domain $\mathbb{N}$ such that $f(n) \in X_n$ for every $n \in \mathbb{N}$ ([[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Elementary volume is a sigma-finite premeasure on the algebra $\mathcal{E}_n$, and $\lambda_n^*$ is exactly the outer set function it induces, so the hypotheses of the Carathéodory-domain theorem are met with $\mathcal{A}_0 := \mathcal{E}_n$. [L2, L3, F3]

1.2 The sigma-algebra generated by $\mathcal{E}_n$ is $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, and the measure that the extension theorem places on it is the restriction $\beta_n$ of $\lambda_n$, since every Borel set is Lebesgue measurable and $\lambda_n$ is the restriction of $\lambda_n^*$. [L3, L4, L5]

2.1 The Carathéodory-domain theorem therefore says that $\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{R}^n)$, the Carathéodory sigma-algebra of $\lambda_n^*$, is the completion domain of $\beta_n$ on $\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ and that $\lambda_n$ agrees there with the completed measure; unwinding the published description of that domain gives the displayed equivalence and the value $\lambda_n(E)=\beta_n(A)$, which is well posed because the completed measure is a measure extending $\beta_n$ and is the unique complete one. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L1, F1, F2] ∎
