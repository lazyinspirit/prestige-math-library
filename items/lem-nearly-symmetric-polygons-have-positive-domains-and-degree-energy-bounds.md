## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Fix a closed complex surface curve $(\Sigma,j)$ with positive area form $\eta$, and put $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m\Sigma$, $m>0$. Let $L_0,\ldots,L_{n-1}$ be finitely many attaching product tori. Choose one interior point $q_R$ of each region cut out by all their attaching curves, and include any prescribed basepoints in this finite protected list. Let $D$ be a closed neighborhood of the repeated-point locus and these incidence divisors, disjoint from all the product tori. Write $\omega$ for the descended product area form off the repeated-point locus.

Let $u$ be a smooth finite-energy polygon on an actual punctured half-plane source with these successive boundary labels and uniform transverse corner limits. Suppose its source-dependent structure $J_z$ equals $\operatorname{Sym}^m(j)$ on one fixed open neighborhood of $D$ and tames $\omega$ elsewhere, with uniform taming and coefficient bounds. Then every region and marked multiplicity is a nonnegative integer and
$$E(u)\le C\sum_R n_R(u)\operatorname{Area}_\eta(R).$$
The constant depends on the target metrics, protected neighborhoods and uniform structure bounds, but not on the conformal positions of the punctures. The same bound applies to a family with these common bounds. Domains are locally constant under boundary-preserving fixed-corner homotopies.

Choose a closed neighborhood $N$ of the union of the tori disjoint from $D$. Every nonconstant such polygon has a source-interior point where $du\ne0$ and $u$ lies outside $D\cup N$. Thus a nonconstant polygon has a nonempty open set of nonzero derivatives in a free target region even when perturbations fix a whole neighborhood of all boundary tori. The statement does not assert that a constant polygon can be regularized by changing $J$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fixed complex surface, area form, finite tori, regions, protected sets and actual finite-energy polygon. All corner tuples lie off the diagonal and every protected incidence divisor.

[F1] The actual local coefficient-coordinate divisor proof identifies $E_q$ as a complex hypersurface and gives positive isolated zeros whenever the equation there is the fixed integrable equation and the map is not contained in the divisor. ([[lem-pointed-heegaard-integrable-divisor-positivity]])

[F2] The actual ordered-lift construction uses finite covering monodromy, fills each cycle by $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^r$, and extends bounded holomorphic roots. The strip domain-energy proof establishes the covering energy comparison and the proper degree-area integral identity without assuming that individual roots are holomorphic outside the protected neighborhood. ([[lem-holomorphic-symmetric-product-curves-admit-finite-branched-ordered-lifts]], [[lem-nearly-symmetric-heegaard-strip-domain-energy-bound]])

[F3] The actual surface degree and regular-value integral formulas are independent of the chosen compactly supported test form. ([[lem-degree-is-well-defined-and-independent-of-the-normalized-top-form]], [[thm-regular-value-formula-for-compact-support-degree]])

## Proof

1.1 Near the inverse image of any protected divisor, the source-dependent equation is the ordinary integrable equation because its target value there is fixed, independently of the source. The local hypersurface composition is holomorphic. Its zero set is isolated unless it vanishes locally. Local vanishing would propagate across its closed preimage: at a limiting point use another integrable divisor chart and holomorphic identity, exactly as in [F1]. The connected source would then map entirely to that divisor, contrary to its corner limits. The same argument with the discriminant excludes containment in the diagonal and shows that its inverse image is isolated. Boundary tori avoid all these protected sets. Uniform corner convergence excludes sufficiently far ends, and compactness of the remaining boundary image excludes a boundary collar. Thus all these intersections are finite interior sets. Local zero orders in [F1] prove the nonnegative integer intersections at every chosen region point and basepoint. This argument depends on a fixed integrable TARGET neighborhood, so no derivative of the source-dependent $J_z$ enters the divisor equation. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Pull the finite ordered covering back over the complement of the diagonal preimages. Its degree is $d=m!$. Complete it at each omitted point by its finite monodromy cycles, with local projection $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^r$. On the protected neighborhood the lifted roots are bounded holomorphic functions and therefore extend, as proved in [F2]. This gives a smooth branched cover $p:F\to S$ of the actual polygon and a smooth ordered lift $\widetilde u:F\to\Sigma^m$, holomorphic wherever the image lies in the protected neighborhood. There is no claim that this lift is holomorphic elsewhere. It has finitely many ends and the given coordinate limits on their compactification. Finitely many branch charts and bounded inverse quotient differentials on the remaining ends show that its coordinate Dirichlet energies are finite. None of this construction uses a special translation coordinate on a strip; only the actual source complex charts and its finitely many ends were used. [F2, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $\Omega=\sum_l\operatorname{pr}_l^*\eta$. On the complement of a smaller diagonal neighborhood, uniform tameness gives the pointwise bound of the polygon energy density by $C\omega(u_a,J_z u_a)$, in any positively oriented conformal source chart $(a,b)$. The equation says $u_b=J_z u_a$, with the corresponding fixed sign convention. Pulling this inequality to $F$ gives the bound by $C/d$ times its lifted $\Omega$ integral. Near the diagonal the lift is holomorphic for the product structure; its product energy equals its $\Omega$ area, and the smooth quotient map on compact $\Sigma^m$ has bounded differential. The covering change of variables and conformal invariance of the two-dimensional energy therefore give on this part the same bound. Both lifted area densities are nonnegative in their respective regions. Adding them proves $E(u)\le (C/d)\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega$. This is precisely the pointwise calculation in [F2], with $J_z$ in place of $J_t$; no integration by parts differentiates $J$, and no conformal modulus appears in $C$. Finite energy of the lift from step2.1 makes each signed coordinate area integral absolutely integrable. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For each coordinate map $f_l:F\to\Sigma$ and each region $R$, its restriction over $R$ is proper: boundary and corner limits lie on attaching curves, so preimages of a compact subset of $R$ stay in a compact part away from those boundaries and ends. Apply [F3] componentwise to compactly supported two-forms on $R$. Only finitely many components can have nonzero degree, since each contains a point over a common regular value and that proper regular fiber is finite. Exhausting $R$ by compact cutoffs and using the absolute integrability in step3.1 gives $\int_{f_l^{-1}(R)}f_l^*\eta=d_{l,R}\operatorname{Area}(R)$ for the total signed degree $d_{l,R}$. At a common regular point off the finite branch images, the ordered lift has exactly $d$ copies of each tuple intersection, with the normal orientation equal to its root-coordinate orientation. Hence $\sum_l d_{l,R}=d\,n_R(u)$. This also proves that the intersection degree is independent of its generic point in $R$, so it equals the nonnegative degree at the chosen protected point in step1.1. The attaching curves have zero contribution to the area integral: at rank-deficient points the pulled-back two-form vanishes, and at regular points their preimages are arcs of measure zero in a countable atlas. Summing gives $\int_F\widetilde u^*\Omega=d\sum_R n_R(u)\operatorname{Area}(R)$, proving the bound. Actual boundary-preserving fixed-corner homotopies keep these degrees fixed by [F3]'s degree invariance, or by the equivalent normal divisor degree. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 If the whole image were contained in $D\cup N$, its connectedness and its corner values in $N$ would put it in $N$, because the two compact sets are disjoint and have positive separation. Then it misses every chosen region divisor, so all its region degrees are zero. The proved energy inequality gives $E(u)=0$ and $u$ is constant. A nonconstant polygon consequently has a point in the open complement of $D\cup N$. Choose a smooth nonnegative target bump supported in this complement with positive value at that point. Its composite with $u$ is positive there and tends to zero at every corner. Along a finite smooth source path from a sufficiently far corner point to that point it is nonconstant, so the one-variable mean value theorem gives a point where its derivative is nonzero. At that point $du\ne0$ and the target bump is nonzero, hence the point is in the free region. Continuity gives an open neighborhood with those properties. This does not use a false assertion that all nonconstant maps have unique target preimages; a source-local perturbation can use this neighborhood. [given, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The same finite cover and taming constants work for every source-dependent coefficient family with the specified uniform target bounds. The proof used only conformal invariance on the source, so its constant is unchanged when punctures coalesce, even though later map compactness may require a stable-source extraction. In particular any separately proved finite domain set immediately gives a uniform energy bound, including all actual classes having those domains; injectivity of the domain map on homotopy classes is unnecessary. This is an actual positivity and energy theorem and a free-image observation. It supplies neither the domain-finiteness topology nor universal regularity or compactification. Constant maps have zero derivative and were not claimed to admit a nonzero $J$-variation. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
