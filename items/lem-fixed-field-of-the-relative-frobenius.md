## Statement

Let $\mathbb F_q$ be a finite field of order $q$ and let $E$ be a finite field
having $\mathbb F_q$ as a subfield. Then

$$\{\,x\in E: x^{q}=x\,\}=\mathbb F_q .$$

Equivalently, the fixed field of the cyclic group generated by the relative
Frobenius $\sigma_q$ ([[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]]) is
the base field:

$$E^{\langle\sigma_q\rangle}=\mathbb F_q .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite fields $\mathbb F_q\subseteq E$ with $|\mathbb F_q|=q$ ([[def-finite-field-and-its-order]]), and the set $S:=\{\,x\in E:x^{q}=x\,\}$.

[L1] The relative Frobenius is $\sigma_q(x)=x^{q}$, an $\mathbb F_q$-automorphism of $E$ ([[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]]).

[L2] If $F$ is a field with $q$ elements, then every $a\in F$ satisfies $a^{q}=a$ ([[prop-finite-fields-are-splitting-fields-of-x-q-minus-x]]).

[L3] Let $D$ be an integral domain. A nonzero polynomial $f\in D[x]$ of degree $n$ has at most $n$ distinct roots in $D$ ([[thm-root-bound-for-polynomials-over-a-domain]]).

[L4] The fixed field of a subgroup $G$ of the automorphism group of $K$ is $K^{G}=\{\,x\in K:\sigma(x)=x\text{ for every }\sigma\in G\,\}$ ([[def-fixed-field-of-an-automorphism-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Applying [L2] to the field $\mathbb F_q$, which has exactly $q$ elements, every $a\in\mathbb F_q$ satisfies $a^{q}=a$; hence $\mathbb F_q\subseteq S$. [L2, given]

1.2 $S$ is the set of roots in $E$ of the polynomial $t^{q}-t\in E[t]$, which is nonzero of degree $q$; a field is an integral domain, so [L3] gives $|S|\le q$. [L3, given]

2.1 Since $\mathbb F_q\subseteq S$, $|\mathbb F_q|=q$ and $|S|\le q$, the finite sets $\mathbb F_q$ and $S$ coincide: $\{\,x\in E:x^{q}=x\,\}=\mathbb F_q$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, given]

3.1 An element of $E$ is fixed by every power of $\sigma_q$ precisely when it is fixed by $\sigma_q$ itself, so $E^{\langle\sigma_q\rangle}=\{\,x\in E:x^{q}=x\,\}$ by [L1] and [L4], and step 2.1 identifies this with $\mathbb F_q$. [step 2.1, L1, L4] ∎

## Remarks

- **What forces equality is a count, not an inclusion.** The inclusion $\mathbb F_q\subseteq S$ is immediate; the content is that $t^{q}-t$ cannot have more than $q$ roots, so the $q$ elements of $\mathbb F_q$ already use them all up. The same count identifies the intermediate fields of an extension of finite fields ([[thm-intermediate-fields-of-an-extension-of-finite-fields]]).
