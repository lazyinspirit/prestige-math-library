## Definition

Let $n\ge0$, let $\lambda\vdash n$, and let $\Omega_\lambda$ be the finite set
of $\lambda$-tabloids; for a $\lambda$-tableau $t$ write $\{t\}$ for its
tabloid ([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]). The
**integral tabloid module** is the free abelian group on $\Omega_\lambda$,

$$M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}:=\mathbb Z^{(\Omega_\lambda)} =\Bigl\{\sum_{T\in\Omega_\lambda}a_T\,T:\ a_T\in\mathbb Z,\ \text{only finitely many }a_T\ne0\Bigr\},$$

with the tabloids as standard $\mathbb Z$-basis
([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]). The left action of $S_n$ on
tabloids extends $\mathbb Z$-linearly to $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$, so that
$M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ is a $\mathbb Z[S_n]$-module. For every $\lambda$-tableau
$t$ put

$$\kappa_t:=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\gamma \in\mathbb Z[S_n],\qquad e_t:=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\, \{\gamma\cdot t\}\in M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z},$$

the **integral column polytabloid**; every coefficient lies in
$\{0,1,-1\}$ ([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).
The **integral Specht lattice** is the $\mathbb Z[S_n]$-submodule

$$S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}:=\text{the }\mathbb Z\text{-span of } \{\,e_s:\ s\text{ a }\lambda\text{-tableau}\,\}\subseteq M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}.$$

For a commutative ring $R$ let $M^\lambda_R:=R^{(\Omega_\lambda)}$ be the free
$R$-module on the tabloids and let $S^\lambda_R\subseteq M^\lambda_R$ be the
$R$-span of the polytabloids $\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)
\{\gamma\cdot t\}$, $t$ a $\lambda$-tableau. The **standard polytabloids** are
the $e_s$ with $s$ a standard $\lambda$-tableau
([[def-young-tableau-standard-tableau-and-shape]]).

This item records three facts. First, the standard polytabloids form a
$\mathbb Z$-basis of $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$, so the integral Specht lattice has
explicit finite rank. Second, for every commutative ring $R$ the canonical map
$R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\to R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}
M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\cong M^\lambda_R$ is injective onto $S^\lambda_R$: the
lattice is a direct summand of the free tabloid module and is compatible with
base change, including rings of prime characteristic. Third, for
$\lambda=\varnothing$ the module has rank one and is generated by the empty
polytabloid.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge0$, a partition $\lambda\vdash n$, and the definitions above.

[F1] The tabloids form a basis of $M^\lambda$, the tabloid of $t$ is $\{t\}=\{\rho\cdot t:\rho\in R_t\}$, and the left action of $S_n$ extends linearly ([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F2] $\kappa_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\gamma$ and $e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}$ ([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F3] $C_t\cap R_t=\{1\}$ for every tableau, so the tabloids $\gamma\cdot\{t\}$ for $\gamma\in C_t$ are pairwise distinct and the coefficient of $\{t\}$ in $e_t$ is $1$ ([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F4] The tabloid order is a finite strict total order on $\Omega_\lambda$ and the column order $\prec$ is a finite strict total order on the column-standard $\lambda$-tableaux ([[def-tabloid-and-column-orders-for-specht-straightening]]).

[F5] If $X,Y$ lie in adjacent columns and $|X|+|Y|>\lambda'_j$, then for every left-coset transversal $T$ containing $1$ the integral group-algebra element $G_{X,Y}=\sum_{g\in T}\operatorname{sgn}(g)g$ satisfies $G_{X,Y}e_t=0$ over $\mathbb C$ ([[lem-adjacent-column-garnir-relation]]).

[F6] Over $\mathbb C$, every polytabloid is a finite complex linear combination of standard polytabloids ([[lem-garnir-straightening-of-polytabloids]]).

[F7] For a column-standard tableau $t$, the coefficient of $\{t\}$ in $e_t$ is $1$ and every other tabloid occurring in $e_t$ is strictly below $\{t\}$ in the tabloid order; consequently the standard polytabloids are linearly independent over $\mathbb C$ ([[lem-leading-tabloid-coefficient-of-a-standard-polytabloid]]).

[F8] Over $\mathbb C$ one has $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t$ for $\sigma\in S_n$ and $\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t$ for $\gamma\in C_t$ ([[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F9] $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z^{(\Omega_\lambda)}$ is free with the tabloids as a $\mathbb Z$-basis, so every element has a unique finite integer coordinate expression and a vector vanishes exactly when all its tabloid coefficients vanish ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[F10] For a commutative ring $R$ there are natural isomorphisms $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\bigl(\bigoplus_{T} \mathbb Z\bigr)\cong \bigoplus_{T}\bigl(R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z\bigr)$ and $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Z\cong R$, hence a natural isomorphism $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\cong M^\lambda_R$ carrying $1\otimes T$ to the tabloid $T$ ([[thm-tensor-products-commute-with-arbitrary-direct-sums]], [[thm-unit-isomorphisms-for-module-tensor-products]]).

[F11] The standard $\lambda$-tableaux are the tableaux strictly increasing along rows and down columns; for $\lambda=\varnothing$ the empty tableau is the unique standard tableau ([[def-young-tableau-standard-tableau-and-shape]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F3] the tabloids $\gamma\cdot\{t\}$ with $\gamma\in C_t$ are pairwise distinct, so in the expansion $e_t=\sum_{\gamma}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\{\gamma\cdot t\}$ no tabloid occurs twice; every coefficient is $0$ or $\pm1$, the coefficient of $\{t\}$ is $1$, and $e_t\ne0$. In particular $e_t$ is a genuine nonzero integral vector of $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

1.2 For $\sigma\in S_n$ the column sets satisfy $C_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma C_t\sigma^{-1}$, and the substitution $\gamma=\sigma\delta\sigma^{-1}$ gives $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sum_{\delta\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\delta)\{\sigma\delta\cdot t\}=\sigma\cdot e_t$. This identity of two integral vectors holds over $\mathbb C$ by [F8], and by the uniqueness of tabloid coordinates it therefore holds in $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$. Since every $\lambda$-tableau is $\sigma\cdot t_0$ for the tableau $t_0$ that lists $1,\dots,n$ along the rows of $[\lambda]$, the lattice $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ is generated over $\mathbb Z[S_n]$ by the single polytabloid $e_{t_0}$. [given, F1, F2, F8, algebra]

1.3 The Garnir element of [F5] is an integral group-algebra element, and $G_{X,Y}e_t=\sum_{g\in T}\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(g)\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\{g\gamma\cdot t\}$ has integer coefficients in the tabloid basis. The identity $G_{X,Y}e_t=0$ holds in $M^\lambda_{\mathbb C}$ between two integral vectors, so by uniqueness of tabloid coordinates it holds in $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$: the Garnir relation is integral. [given, F5, algebra]

1.4 For $\lambda=\varnothing$ there is exactly one tabloid and one tableau, with $C_t=\{1\}$, $\kappa_t=1$ and $e_t=\{\varnothing\}$; the empty tableau is standard by [F11]. Thus $M^{\varnothing}_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z\{\varnothing\}\cong\mathbb Z$ and $S^{\varnothing}_{\mathbb Z}=\mathbb Z\{\varnothing\}$ has rank one with the empty polytabloid as basis. [given, F1, F2, F11]

2.1 Run the published spanning argument inside $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$: for column-standard $s$ that is not standard, the integral Garnir relation of step 1.3 with the explicit transversal containing $1$ isolates the identity term and gives $e_s=-\sum_{A\ne X}\operatorname{sgn}(g_A)e_{g_A\cdot s}$ with each $g_A$ an explicit product of disjoint swaps; sorting columns and using the covariance of step 1.2 rewrites each $e_{g_A\cdot s}$ as $\pm e_u$ with $u$ column-standard and $s\prec u$ in the finite order [F4]; and any tableau is taken to column-standard form by a column permutation, again by step 1.2. Reverse induction along [F4] therefore gives, for every $\lambda$-tableau $t$, an identity $e_t=\sum_{u\ \text{standard}}c_{tu}e_u$ with integer coefficients $c_{tu}$. Hence the standard polytabloids span $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ over $\mathbb Z$. [given, F4, F5, F6, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 Order the standard $\lambda$-tableaux $t_1,\dots,t_d$ so that $\{t_1\}<\cdots<\{t_d\}$ in the tabloid order of [F4], possible since distinct standard tableaux have distinct tabloids and the order is total. By [F7], $e_{t_i}=\{t_i\}+\sum_{T<\{t_i\}}c_TT$ with integer $c_T$, so the coefficient of $\{t_j\}$ in $e_{t_i}$ is $0$ for $j>i$ and $1$ for $j=i$. A relation $\sum_i a_ie_{t_i}=0$ with integral $a_i$ therefore forces $a_d=0$, then $a_{d-1}=0$, and so on; the standard polytabloids are $\mathbb Z$-linearly independent and, with step 2.1, form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$. [given, F4, F7, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $P:\mathbb Z^d\to M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ send the $i$-th standard basis vector to $e_{t_i}$, so that $\operatorname{im}P=S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ by step 3.1, and let $\pi:M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\to\mathbb Z^d$ take tabloid coordinates at $\{t_1\},\dots,\{t_d\}$. By step 3.1 the matrix of $\pi\circ P$ is upper unitriangular with integer entries, hence invertible over $\mathbb Z$ by integer back-substitution, and $\rho:=(\pi\circ P)^{-1}\circ\pi$ satisfies $\rho\circ P=\mathrm{id}$. Thus $P$ is injective and split, $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ is a direct summand of the free $\mathbb Z$-module $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$, and $M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}/S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ is free: the lattice is saturated. [given, F9, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Let $R$ be a commutative ring and use the identification $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}M^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\cong M^\lambda_R$ of [F10]. The map $\mathrm{id}_R\otimes P$ has image $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ and $\mathrm{id}_R\otimes\rho$ is a left inverse of it, so $\mathrm{id}_R\otimes P$ is injective; hence the canonical map $R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}\to M^\lambda_R$ is injective onto the $R$-span of the vectors $1\otimes e_{t_i}$, which is the $R$-span of the standard polytabloids. By the identity of step 2.1 every polytabloid is an integral combination of standard ones, so this span is exactly $S^\lambda_R$; therefore $S^\lambda_R\cong R\otimes_{\mathbb Z}S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$ for every commutative ring $R$, including rings of prime characteristic. [given, F10, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Taking $R=\mathbb Z$ in step 5.1 returns $S^\lambda_{\mathbb Z}$, and taking $\lambda=\varnothing$ returns the rank-one lattice $\mathbb Z\{\varnothing\}$ of step 1.4; the standard-polytabloid basis is step 3.1 and saturation is step 4.1. This proves the three asserted properties for all $n\ge0$, including $n=0$. [given, step 1.4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1] ∎
