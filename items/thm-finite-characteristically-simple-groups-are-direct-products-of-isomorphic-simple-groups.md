## Statement

Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite characteristically simple group. Then there is a finite
simple group $T$ and an integer $r \ge 1$ such that

$$G \cong T^r.$$

In particular, $G$ is an internal direct product of pairwise isomorphic simple
normal subgroups.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nontrivial finite characteristically simple group $G$.

[A1] Every nontrivial finite group has a minimal normal subgroup.

[A2] If $N$ is a minimal normal subgroup of a finite characteristically simple group $G$, then every automorphic image of $N$ is again a minimal normal subgroup, distinct images centralize one another, and the subgroup generated by all such images is characteristic in $G$.

[L1] Normal subgroups $N_1,\ldots,N_r$ form an internal direct product when
they generate the ambient group and
$N_i\cap\langle N_j:j\ne i\rangle=1$ for every $i$
([[def-internal-direct-product-of-subgroups]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [A1], choose a minimal normal subgroup $N \trianglelefteq G$. [A1, choose]

2.1 Let $N_1,\dots,N_r$ be an irredundant family of automorphic images of $N$ that generates the subgroup $H$ generated by all automorphic images. By [A2], the subgroup $H$ is characteristic in $G$, so $H=G$ because $G$ is characteristically simple and $N \neq 1$. [A2, step 1.1, choose]

3.1 Fix $i$ and put $P_i=\langle N_j:j\ne i\rangle$.  The intersection $N_i\cap P_i$ is normal in $G$: both factors are normal, and the intersection is preserved by conjugation. Minimality of $N_i$ makes this intersection either $1$ or $N_i$. The latter would put $N_i$ inside the subgroup generated by the other images, contradicting irredundancy. Hence $N_i\cap P_i=1$ for every $i$. Together with step 2.1, [A2], and [L1], this makes $G=N_1\times\cdots\times N_r$ an internal direct product. [L1, A2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $K\trianglelefteq N_i$. Since the other direct factors centralize $N_i$, conjugation by them fixes $K$, while conjugation by $N_i$ preserves $K$ by normality. Step 3.1 says these factors generate $G$, so $K\trianglelefteq G$. Minimality of $N_i$ gives $K=1$ or $K=N_i$; thus $N_i$ is simple. All $N_i$ are automorphic images of $N$, so they are pairwise isomorphic to one finite simple group $T$. Therefore $G\cong T^r$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
