## Statement

For finite $p$-groups $P$ and $Q$,

$$\Phi(P\times Q)=\Phi(P)\times\Phi(Q).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Finite $p$-groups $P,Q$.

[F1] The external direct product has componentwise multiplication, and this operation makes $P\times Q$ a group ([[def-external-direct-product-of-groups]], [[thm-external-direct-product-is-a-group]]).

[F2] The commutator subgroup is generated by the elements $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, and natural powers are defined recursively from the group operation ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]], [[def-group-power]]).

[L1] For every finite $p$-group $R$, $\Phi(R)=R'R^p$ ([[thm-frattini-subgroup-formula-for-a-finite-p-group]]).

[L2] The Cartesian product of two finite sets is finite with cardinality the product of their cardinalities ([[thm-product-rule]]). A finite $p$-group has order $p^m$ for some $m\in\mathbb N$ ([[def-finite-p-group]]), and $p^{m+n}=p^mp^n$ in natural-number arithmetic ([[def-nat-power]], clause (c)).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Write $|P|=p^m$ and $|Q|=p^n$. By [F1] the product is a group, and [L2] gives that it is finite with $|P\times Q|=p^mp^n=p^{m+n}$, so it is a finite $p$-group, including when either factor is trivial. [given, F1, L2, algebra]

1.2 Componentwise commutators and powers give $(P\times Q)'=P'\times Q'$ and $(P\times Q)^p=P^p\times Q^p$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 Apply [L1] to the finite $p$-group established in step 1.1 and use step 1.2: $\Phi(P\times Q)=(P'\times Q')(P^p\times Q^p)=P'P^p\times Q'Q^p=\Phi(P)\times\Phi(Q)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L1, algebra] ∎
