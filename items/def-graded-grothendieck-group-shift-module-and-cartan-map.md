## Definition

Let $k$ be a field and let $A=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb Z}A_i$ be a finite-dimensional, unital, associative, $\mathbb Z$-graded $k$-algebra. Let $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ be the category of finite-dimensional graded left $A$-modules and degree-zero maps, and let $\mathcal P^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ be its full subcategory of finite graded projective modules. Define

$$G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A):=G_0\!\left(\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)\right),\qquad K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A):=K_0^{\mathrm{split}}\!\left(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)\right).$$

Here $G_0$ uses all short-exact-sequence relations, while $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}$ uses only direct-sum relations. Let $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ be the category of finite-dimensional ungraded left $A$-modules and put $G_0(A):=G_0(\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fd}}(A))$. The notation $K_0(A)$ is the split Grothendieck group of finite-dimensional projective left $A$-modules from [[def-split-grothendieck-group-of-an-additive-category]].

For $r\in\mathbb Z$, internal shift is $(M\{r\})_d:=M_{d-r}$. It acts on generators by

$$v^r[M]:=[M\{r\}],\qquad v^r[P]:=[P\{r\}],$$

for $[M]\in G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ and $[P]\in K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$. These actions make both groups modules over $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$, with $v[M]=[M\{1\}]$ and $v[P]=[P\{1\}]$.

The graded Cartan homomorphism is

$$c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}:K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)\longrightarrow G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A),\qquad [P]\longmapsto[P].$$

It is $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$-linear. The ungraded Cartan homomorphism is

$$c_A:K_0(A)\longrightarrow G_0(A),\qquad [P]\longmapsto[P].$$

Neither map is asserted to be injective or surjective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, the finite-dimensional graded algebra $A$, and the graded and ungraded left modules described in the Definition. All maps in $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ preserve degree. No axiom of choice is used.

[F1] For an essentially small abelian category, $G_0$ is the free abelian group on isomorphism classes modulo the relations from every short exact sequence ([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]]).

[F2] For an essentially small additive category, $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}$ is the free abelian group on isomorphism classes modulo direct-sum relations; $K_0(A)$ uses finite-dimensional projective left modules ([[def-split-grothendieck-group-of-an-additive-category]]).

[F3] Internal shift is $M\{r\}_d=M_{d-r}$, is invertible with inverse $\{-r\}$, and composes by addition of shifts ([[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]).

[F4] $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ is abelian, with kernels, images, cokernels, finite biproducts, and exactness computed degreewise ([[lem-graded-module-kernels-cokernels-and-biproducts-are-degreewise]]).

[F5] A finite graded projective is a degree-zero direct summand of a finite direct sum of internal shifts of $A$ ([[thm-finite-graded-projectives-are-summands-of-finite-graded-free-modules]]).

[F6] A finite graded projective is projective in $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ and generated by finitely many homogeneous elements ([[def-finitely-generated-graded-projective-module]]).

[F7] Additive class functions on isomorphism classes factor uniquely through the split Grothendieck group; exact-sequence-additive class functions factor uniquely through $G_0$ ([[thm-grothendieck-group-universal-properties-and-functoriality]]).

[F8] For a linear map with finite-dimensional domain, its kernel and image are finite-dimensional ([[thm-rank-nullity]]).

[F9] An additive category has a zero object and finite biproducts ([[def-additive-category]]).

[F10] An abelian category is additive, has kernels and cokernels, and its coimage-to-image comparison is an isomorphism ([[def-abelian-category]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F4], $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A)$ is abelian and its degreewise kernels, cokernels, and finite biproducts have the stated graded structures. For a map between finite-dimensional modules, its kernel is a subspace of the finite-dimensional domain and its cokernel is a quotient of the finite-dimensional codomain; [F8] gives finite-dimensionality of kernels and images. Thus the full subcategory $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ is closed under kernels, cokernels, images, and finite biproducts. The ambient coimage and image of any map therefore remain in this full subcategory, as does their canonical isomorphism; hence [F10] makes it abelian. It is essentially small: each object has finite support and a finite homogeneous basis; for each finite-support dimension vector, the graded $k$-space with those dimensions has a fixed standard model, and its $A$-actions are a set of families of linear maps satisfying the algebra-action identities. Every object is isomorphic to one of these set-many models. This objectwise coordinate argument selects no basis simultaneously from an arbitrary family. Hence [F1] defines $G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$. [F1, F4, F8, F10, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Every finite-dimensional graded module has a finite homogeneous basis and so is finitely generated. Conversely, a finite graded projective has finitely many homogeneous generators by [F6]; sending the homogeneous generator of each matching shift $A\{r\}$ to a generator of $P$ gives a degree-zero surjection from a finite sum of shifts of $A$, so $P$ is finite-dimensional. The category $\mathcal P^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$ is essentially small as a full subcategory of $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$. It inherits preadditive hom groups from that abelian category. By [F5], the zero module and a finite direct sum of finite graded projectives are again finite graded projective; their module biproducts therefore make this an additive category by [F9]. Thus [F2] defines $K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$. [F2, F4, F5, F6, F9, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.2 Write $A^{\flat}$ for the underlying ungraded algebra given the trivial grading, and regard an ungraded module as an $A^{\flat}$-module concentrated in degree zero. This identifies the ungraded module category with a full subcategory of $\operatorname{GrMod}_0(A^{\flat})$ that is closed under kernels, cokernels, images, and finite biproducts by [F4]. Its finite-dimensional subcategory remains closed because kernels and images are subspaces and cokernels are quotients of finite-dimensional vector spaces. The ambient coimage-to-image isomorphisms remain in this full subcategory, so it is abelian by [F10]; it is essentially small by the same finite-dimensional action-matrix argument as in step 1.1. Therefore [F1] defines $G_0(A)$. [F1, F4, F8, F10, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

2.3 For each $r\in\mathbb Z$, shift sends a finite-dimensional graded module to a finite-dimensional graded module and leaves degree-zero maps degree-zero. It is exact because degreewise kernels and cokernels in [F4] are merely reindexed, and its inverse is shift by $-r$ by [F3]. Thus shift is an exact autoequivalence of $\mathcal M^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$, and [F7] induces an automorphism $\sigma_r$ of $G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ with $\sigma_r([M])=[M\{r\}]$. [F3, F4, F7, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $P$ is finite graded projective, [F5] exhibits it as a summand of a finite direct sum of shifts $A\{s\}$. Shifting this splitting by $r$ exhibits $P\{r\}$ as a summand of a finite direct sum of shifts $A\{s+r\}$, so [F5] shows $P\{r\}$ is finite graded projective. The shift functor and its inverse are additive on this category; [F7] therefore induces inverse automorphisms $\tau_r$ and $\tau_{-r}$ on $K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$, with $\tau_r([P])=[P\{r\}]$. [F3, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The class function $[P]\mapsto[P]$ from $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal P^{\mathrm{gr}}_{\mathrm{fd}}(A))$ to $G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ is additive: the split sequence $0\to P\to P\oplus Q\to Q\to0$ gives $[P\oplus Q]=[P]+[Q]$ by [F1]. Hence [F7] gives a unique homomorphism $c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}$ with $c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}([P])=[P]$. [F1, F2, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The identities $\sigma_0=\tau_0=1$, $\sigma_r\sigma_s=\sigma_{r+s}$, and $\tau_r\tau_s=\tau_{r+s}$ follow on generators from $(M\{s\})\{r\}=M\{r+s\}$ and $(P\{s\})\{r\}=P\{r+s\}$ in [F3]. Therefore $v^r\cdot x:=\sigma_r(x)$ on $G_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ and $v^r\cdot y:=\tau_r(y)$ on $K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ extend by finite integer linear combinations to $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$-module structures. [F3, step 2.3, step 3.1, given, algebra]

5.1 On every projective-class generator, $c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}(v[P])=c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}([P\{1\}])=[P\{1\}]=v[P]=v\,c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}([P])$ by [F3]. Since these generators generate $K_0^{\mathrm{gr}}(A)$ as an abelian group and $v$ is invertible, $c_A^{\mathrm{gr}}$ commutes with every Laurent polynomial action and is $\mathbb Z[v,v^{-1}]$-linear. [F3, F7, step 4.1, step 3.2, algebra]

6.1 The ungraded class function $[P]\mapsto[P]$ from finite-dimensional projective left $A$-modules to $G_0(A)$ is additive by the split short exact sequence $0\to P\to P\oplus Q\to Q\to0$ in $\mathcal M_{\mathrm{fd}}(A)$. By [F7] it factors uniquely through the split group $K_0(A)$, giving $c_A([P])=[P]$. [F1, F2, F7, step 2.2, construct, algebra] ∎
