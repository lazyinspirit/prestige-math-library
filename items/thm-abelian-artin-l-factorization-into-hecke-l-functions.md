## Statement

Assume AC, Countable Choice, and Dependent Choice. Let $K$ be a number field, let $\mathfrak m$ be a modulus, let $L/K$ be its ray class field, and let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$. For $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$,
$$\zeta_L(s)=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat G}L_{L/K}(s,\chi),$$
where $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb C^\times)$ and $L_{L/K}(s,\chi)$ is the Artin L-series with inertia-invariant local factors. At every prime ideal not dividing $\mathfrak m_0$, the local factors are the Hecke Euler factors of the corresponding ray-class characters; any difference between the full Artin and Hecke Euler products is supported on the finite set of primes dividing $\mathfrak m_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, Countable Choice, DC, a number field $K$, a modulus $\mathfrak m$, its ray class field $L/K$, its finite abelian Galois group $G$, and $s$ with $\sigma=\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$.

[A1] The ray-class existence and Artin isomorphism suppliers assert a finite abelian extension $L/K$ with $G\cong\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)$. Their current proofs are escalated through the unresolved global norm-cofinality and global reciprocity prerequisites. [[thm-global-class-field-existence-number-field]] [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]]

[F1] The local Artin factor for a one-dimensional character is $(1-\chi(\widetilde{\operatorname{Frob}}_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p})(N\mathfrak p)^{-s})^{-1}$ when $\chi$ is trivial on inertia, where the tilde denotes any lift of the Frobenius coset, and is $1$ otherwise. [[def-artin-l-series-for-a-linear-character]]

[F2] The quotient $D(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)/I(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)$ is the cyclic Galois group of residue fields, of order $f=f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)$ and generated by arithmetic Frobenius. [[def-inertia-group-of-a-prime]] [[thm-decomposition-inertia-exact-sequence]] [[def-arithmetic-frobenius-coset]]

[F3] Any two primes above $\mathfrak p$ are conjugate; conjugation carries their decomposition and inertia groups and Frobenius cosets to one another. Because $G$ is abelian, the character values and local factors agree. [[thm-galois-action-on-primes-above-a-prime-is-transitive]] [[def-arithmetic-frobenius-coset]]

[F4] In a finite Galois extension, all primes over $\mathfrak p$ have the same residue degree $f$ and ramification index $e$; if there are $g$ such primes, then $efg=[L:K]$. [[cor-galois-prime-decomposition-efg]]

[F5] The decomposition group has order $[L_{\mathfrak P}:K_{\mathfrak p}]=ef$, while $|I(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)|=e$; the residue quotient has order $f$. [[lem-number-field-decomposition-group-order-is-local-degree]] [[thm-finite-extension-local-degree-equals-ramification-index-times-residue-degree]] [[thm-decomposition-inertia-exact-sequence]]

[F6] For a finite abelian group $A$, its characters satisfy $\sum_{\chi\in\widehat A}\chi(x)\overline{\chi(y)}=|A|$ if $x=y$ and $0$ otherwise. [[lem-character-orthogonality-for-finite-abelian-groups]]

[F7] The Dedekind zeta functions of $K$ and $L$ have absolutely convergent ideal Euler products for $\operatorname{Re}(s)>1$. [[thm-dedekind-zeta-euler-product]]

[F8] The Hecke L-series of each finite-order ray-class character has the Euler product over primes not dividing $\mathfrak m_0$. [[thm-finite-order-hecke-l-euler-product]]



**Given:** The data and assumptions in the Statement.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $K$, write $q=N\mathfrak p$, choose $\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p$, and let $D$, $I$, and $\overline F\in D/I$ be its decomposition group, inertia group, and arithmetic Frobenius coset. The local factor is independent of this choice by [F3]. By [F2], $\overline F$ has order $f=f(\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p)$. By [F4] there are $g$ primes above $\mathfrak p$, and [F5] gives $|I|=e$; hence $|G/I|=efg/e=gf$. The local factor of $\zeta_L(s)$ at $\mathfrak p$ is $(1-q^{-fs})^{-g}$. [F2, F3, F4, F5, given, algebra]

1.2 The characters of $G$ trivial on $I$ are exactly the characters of $G/I$. For $x=q^{-s}$, the values $\eta(\overline F)$, $\eta\in\widehat{G/I}$, are $f$th roots of unity. Write $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/f}$ and let $n_j$ count the characters with $\eta(\overline F)=\zeta^j$. For $0\le k<f$, [F6] gives $\sum_{j=0}^{f-1}n_j\zeta^{jk}=|G/I|$ for $k=0$ and $0$ for $k>0$, because $\overline F^k=1$ exactly when $k=0$ in this range. Finite Fourier inversion yields $n_j=f^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{f-1}(\sum_\ell n_\ell\zeta^{\ell k})\zeta^{-jk}=|G/I|/f=g$. Hence
$$\prod_{\eta\in\widehat{G/I}}(1-\eta(\overline F)x)^{-1}=\left(\prod_{\omega^f=1}(1-\omega x)^{-1}\right)^g=(1-x^f)^{-g}.$$
Characters nontrivial on $I$ contribute local factor $1$ by [F1]. The product of the local Artin factors therefore equals the local factor of $\zeta_L(s)$. [F1, F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.1 Multiplying the local identities over all $\mathfrak p$ gives the claimed equality: both sides converge absolutely in $\sigma>1$ by [F7] and the bound $|1-\eta(\overline F)q^{-s}|^{-1}\le(1-q^{-\sigma})^{-1}$. [F1, F7, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 If $\mathfrak p\nmid\mathfrak m_0$, the Artin isomorphism sends its ray class to arithmetic Frobenius, so [F1] gives exactly the Hecke Euler factor of [F8]. Only primes dividing $\mathfrak m_0$ can differ, and there are finitely many of them. This proves the final assertion without identifying any missing ramified factors as $1$. [A1, F1, F8, step 2.1] ∎
