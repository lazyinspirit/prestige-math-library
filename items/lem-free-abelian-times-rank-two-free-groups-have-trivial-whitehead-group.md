## Statement

Assume countable choice. Let $r\geq0$ be finite, put $C=\mathbb Z[x_1^{\pm1},\ldots,x_r^{\pm1}]$, and let $F(a,b)$ be the free group on $a,b$. The two vertex inclusions induce a surjection
$$K_1(C[a,a^{-1}])\oplus K_1(C[b,b^{-1}])\longrightarrow K_1(C[F(a,b)]).$$
Consequently $\mathrm{Wh}(\mathbb Z^r\times F(a,b))=0$. Here $K_1$ is the stabilized general linear group modulo elementary matrices, and the Whitehead group is its quotient by the classes of integral group-ring units $\pm g$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[A1] Countable choice is assumed as in [[def-countable-choice]].

[F1] By [[lem-finite-alphabet-nilpotent-endomorphisms-over-free-abelian-group-rings-have-trivial-reduced-k-zero]], if finite projective $C$-modules carry finitely many jointly nilpotent coefficient endomorphisms $\nu_i$, then $1+\sum_i\nu_it_i$ has zero $K_1$ class in any central $C$-algebra containing the chosen elements $t_i$.

[F2] By [[lem-free-abelian-integral-group-rings-have-trivial-reduced-k-zero-and-whitehead-groups]], $K_0(C)=\mathbb Z[C]$ and the same holds after one Laurent extension; the $K_1$ groups of those commutative Laurent rings are generated by $-1$ and their Laurent variables.

## Proof

**Given:** $r$ and the central coefficient ring $C$ under [A1]. Write $A=C[a,a^{-1}]$, $B=C[b,b^{-1}]$ and $S=C[F(a,b)]$. Matrices act on columns; the coefficient modules below are right modules, and $C$ is central.

1.1 Split $A=C\oplus A'$ and $B=C\oplus B'$ by constant coefficients, where $A'$ and $B'$ have $C$-bases $a^k$ and $b^k$, respectively, for $k\ne0$. The reduced syllable words in the free group give an explicit $C$-basis of $S$. Let $V$ span the nonempty reduced words whose last syllable is in $A'$, and $W$ those ending in $B'$. Concatenating different syllable types gives $S=C\oplus V\oplus W$, $V=A'\oplus(W\otimes_C A')$ and $W=B'\oplus(V\otimes_C B')$. Multiplication also gives $AV\subset C\oplus V$, $BV\subset V$, $AW\subset W$ and $BW\subset C\oplus W$. Finally $S=(C\oplus W)\otimes_C A$ as a right $A$-module, with the indicated literal reduced-word bases. [given, construct, algebra]

1.2 We first prove an inverse test. Let $P,Q$ be finite projective $C$-modules and let $p:P\to Q\otimes_C A'$, $q:Q\to P\otimes_C B'$. Extend them over $S$ and suppose $\kappa=1+p+q$ is invertible. In $(P\oplus Q)\otimes_C S$, call a word component allowed if it is constant, or belongs to $P$ and starts in $B'$, or belongs to $Q$ and starts in $A'$. These components form a submodule $H$ invariant under $\kappa$: the nonidentity maps prefix a different syllable type, so no contraction occurs. On the quotient by $H$, the nonidentity part strictly decreases syllable length. Indeed, prefixing $p$ to a forbidden $P$-word beginning in $A'$ multiplies two $A$-syllables. Its $A'$ part is allowed in $Q$, while its constant part removes the first syllable and leaves a shorter forbidden word; the $q$ case is identical. Therefore the quotient action of $\kappa$ is injective, since a greatest-length identity contribution cannot be cancelled by shorter ones. In particular the inverse of every constant vector lies in $H$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

1.3 Inside $H$, let $T=p+q$; it increases syllable length exactly by one. For a constant vector $z$, the equation $w+Tw=z$ for $w=\kappa^{-1}z$ gives its homogeneous terms $w_k=(-T)^kz$. The inverse has finite word support on a finite set of $C$-generators of $P\oplus Q$, so there is a common $N$ with $T^Nz=0$ on all these generators. Independence of alternating syllable words forces each coefficient product of length $N$ to be zero. Products that do not alternate already vanish because the coefficient maps of $p$ and $q$ go in opposite off-diagonal directions. Only finitely many coefficient maps occur, since the two modules are finite and their images have finite support in $A',B'$. These coefficient endomorphisms of $P\oplus Q$ are thus jointly nilpotent. By [F1], $[\kappa]=0$ in $K_1(S)$. This proves the inverse test together with its torsion conclusion. [F1, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Take $g\in\mathrm{GL}_m(S)$. Remove a longest syllable word of length at least two in one matrix entry by writing that term as $uv$, with the coefficient and all but the last syllable in $u$ and the last syllable in $v$. Rectangular blocks supported on that entry give elementary operations transforming $\operatorname{diag}(g,I)$ into $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}g-uv&u\\-v&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]$. Both new words are shorter; the number of words of the previous greatest length decreases. Induction on this pair of finite parameters reduces to an invertible matrix $T_A+T_B$ with entries of $T_A$ in $A$ and of $T_B$ in $B$, preserving the stable $K_1$ class. If its size is $n$, right-multiply $\operatorname{diag}(T_A+T_B,I)$ by $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&0\\I&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]$ and left-multiply by $\left[\begin{smallmatrix}I&-T_B\\0&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]$. We obtain the representative $M=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}T_A&-T_B\\I&I\end{smallmatrix}\right]=(M_A,M_B)$ with $M_A$ over $A$ and $M_B$ over $B$. Write its inverse as the stacked block rows $N=(N^1;N^2)$. Then $N^1M_A=I$, $N^1M_B=0$, $N^2M_A=0$ and $N^2M_B=I$. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 Decompose $N^i=N_C^i+N_V^i+N_W^i$ by step 1.1 and define $K=M_AN_C^1+M_AN_V^1+M_BN_V^2$ and $L=M_BN_C^2+M_BN_W^2+M_AN_W^1$. Their sum is $MN=I$. The first has entries in $C\oplus V$ and the second in $C\oplus W$; hence both are constant $C$-matrices. Multiplication gives $NK=(N_C^1+N_V^1;N_V^2)$. Comparing the constant, $V$ and $W$ parts, since $K$ is constant, gives $N_C^1K=N_C^1$, $N_V^1K=N_V^1$, $N_W^1K=0$, $N_C^2K=0$, $N_V^2K=N_V^2$ and $N_W^2K=0$. These identities give $NK^2=NK$, so $K^2=K$. Since $L=I-K$, also $L^2=L$ and $KL=LK=0$. Put $P=KC^{2n}$ and $Q=LC^{2n}$; these are finite projective $C$-modules and $C^{2n}=P\oplus Q$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

2.3 Split $N_V^i=N_{A'}^i+N_{WA'}^i$ and $N_W^i=N_{B'}^i+N_{VB'}^i$, and put $E=M_A(N_C^1+N_{A'}^1)$ and $F=M_B(N_C^2+N_{B'}^2)$. Their entries are in $A$ and $B$, respectively. The formula $K-E=M_AN_{WA'}^1+M_BN_{WA'}^2+M_BN_{A'}^2$ lies in $A'\oplus WA'$ by the word relations, and also in $A$; therefore it has entries in $A'$. Similarly $L-F$ has entries in $B'$. The decomposition of $N^1M_A=I$ separates its $A$ part from its $WA'\oplus W$ part and gives $(N_C^1+N_{A'}^1)M_A=I$. Thus $EM_A=M_A$ and $E^2=E$. Extracting the $A'$ part of $N_V^1K=N_V^1$ also gives $EK=E$. Swapping the roles gives $FM_B=M_B$ and $F^2=FL=F$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

2.4 To check the remaining projection identities, set $H=M_BC^n\oplus MW^{2n}\subset(C\oplus W)^{2n}$. The sum is direct because $M$ is invertible and its second input $C^n$ is disjoint from $W^{2n}$; the inclusion follows from the word multiplication relations. The formulas for $K,L$ put $Q$ inside $H$ and $P$ inside $M_AC^n\oplus MV^{2n}$. Since $S^{2n}=M_AC^n\oplus M_BC^n\oplus MV^{2n}\oplus MW^{2n}$, we have $P\cap H=0$ and therefore $C^{2n}\cap H=Q$. The displayed formula for $K-E$ in step 2.3 shows each of its columns lies both in $C^{2n}\otimes_C A'$ and in $H\otimes_C A'$, inside $(C\oplus W)^{2n}\otimes_C A'$. Tensoring with $A'$ preserves this intersection because its explicit $C$-basis makes tensoring a direct sum of copies, with componentwise intersections. Those columns therefore lie in $Q\otimes_C A'$. The constant projection $K$ kills them, so $KE=K(K-(K-E))=K$. Interchanging the two vertex rings gives $LF=L$. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, construct, algebra]

2.5 The relations imply $\ker E=\ker K$ and $\ker F=\ker L$, while $\operatorname{im}E=M_AS^n$ and $\operatorname{im}F=M_BS^n$. Therefore $\kappa=E+F$ is invertible: $\kappa K=E$ and $\kappa L=F$ show its image contains the two complementary images of the invertible $M$; if $\kappa z=0$, those independent images force $Ez=Fz=0$ and then $Kz=Lz=0$. Put $d=E-K$, $e=F-L$. From the verified relations, $d=LdK$ and $e=KeL$, with entries in $A'$ and $B'$, respectively. Relative to $P\oplus Q$, these are maps $P\to Q\otimes A'$ and $Q\to P\otimes B'$, and $\kappa=I+d+e$. The inverse test in steps 1.2–1.3 applies and proves $[\kappa]=0$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, construct, algebra]

3.1 Set $\Gamma=(KM_A,LM_B)$. Then $\kappa\Gamma=(EM_A,FM_B)=M$. The $A$-map $KM_A:A^n\to P\otimes_C A$ is an isomorphism: applying $E$ proves injectivity, since $EKM_A=EM_A=M_A$, and its image is all $KA^{2n}$ because $KM_AA^n=KEA^{2n}=KA^{2n}$. Similarly $LM_B:B^n\to Q\otimes_C B$ is an isomorphism. By [F2], extension on $K_0$ sends the free rank-one generator to the free rank-one generator, so $P,Q$ both have $C$-rank $n$. They are stably free: $[P]=n[C]$ gives $P\oplus T\cong C^n\oplus T$ for a finite projective $T$; adding a finite complement of $T$ gives $P\oplus C^s\cong C^{n+s}$. Do likewise for $Q$ and enlarge $s$ to a common value. Add these identity summands to $\Gamma$. In $C$-bases of the two now free summands, this stabilized matrix is a $C$-invertible change of target basis followed by a block diagonal matrix over $A$ and $B$. The domain and target permutations are also $C$-invertible. Thus $[\Gamma]$ is in the sum of the two vertex images; its $C$-change-of-basis class is already in either image. Since $[M]=[\kappa]+[\Gamma]=[\Gamma]$, every original $[g]$ is in those images. This proves the asserted surjectivity. [F2, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 2.5, construct, algebra]

3.2 By [F2], $K_1(A)$ and $K_1(B)$ are generated by $-1$, the $r$ central Laurent variables, and their respective extra variable $a$ or $b$. All their images in $K_1(S)$ are classes of units $\pm g$ of $\mathbb Z[\mathbb Z^r\times F(a,b)]$. Surjectivity in step 3.1 therefore makes the quotient by these units zero. This is exactly the required Whitehead group, under the countable-choice hypothesis [A1] carried by the two suppliers. [A1, F1, F2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
