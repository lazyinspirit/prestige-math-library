## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field and let
$S\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$. If $(S)$ denotes the ideal generated by $S$,
then
$$ V(S)=V((S))=V(\sqrt{(S)}). $$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An algebraically closed field $k$, a natural number $n$, and a set $S\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$.

[L1] For any subset $T\subseteq k[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$, $$ V(T)=\{a\in k^n:f(a)=0\text{ for every }f\in T\} $$ ([[def-affine-algebraic-set]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Because $S\subseteq (S)$, every point annihilating $(S)$ certainly annihilates $S$, so $V((S))\subseteq V(S)$. Conversely, if $a\in V(S)$ and $g=\sum_{i=1}^r h_i f_i\in (S)$ with $f_i\in S$, then $g(a)=\sum_{i=1}^r h_i(a)f_i(a)=0$. Hence $a\in V((S))$. Therefore $V(S)=V((S))$. [L1, given, algebra]

2.1 Since $(S)\subseteq \sqrt{(S)}$, we have $V(\sqrt{(S)})\subseteq V((S))$. Conversely, if $a\in V((S))$ and $g\in\sqrt{(S)}$, choose $m\ge 1$ with $g^m\in (S)$. Then $g(a)^m=g^m(a)=0$, and a field has no nonzero nilpotent elements, so $g(a)=0$. Thus $a\in V(\sqrt{(S)})$, proving $V((S))=V(\sqrt{(S)})$. [step 1.1, L1, algebra]

3.1 Combining steps 1.1 and 2.1 gives $V(S)=V((S))=V(\sqrt{(S)})$. [step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎
