## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group and let $\vartheta\in R(G)$ be a complex virtual
character ([[def-virtual-character-and-character-ring-of-a-finite-group]]).
Then $\vartheta$ is an integral linear combination

$$ \vartheta=\sum_{i=1}^n c_i\operatorname{Ind}_{K_i}^G\lambda_i, \qquad c_i\in\mathbb Z, $$

in which each $K_i\le G$ is a $p_i$-elementary subgroup of $G$ for some prime
$p_i$ ([[def-p-elementary-and-p-hyperelementary-finite-groups]]) and each
$\lambda_i$ is a linear character of $K_i$; that is, $\vartheta$ is an integral
combination of monomial characters induced from elementary subgroups
([[def-monomial-representation-and-m-group]]). Equivalently, the integral span
of those monomial characters is all of $R(G)$. The coefficients $c_i$ may be
negative; the statement does not assert that $G$ or a given irreducible
character of $G$ is monomial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$, a complex virtual character $\vartheta\in R(G)$, and the family $\mathcal E$ of all elementary subgroups of $G$, namely all subgroups that are $p$-elementary for some prime $p$.

[F1] For a family $\mathcal F$ of subgroups of $G$, $I_{\mathcal F}(G)=\sum_{H\in\mathcal F}\operatorname{Ind}_H^GR(H)\subseteq R(G)$; its elements are exactly the finite sums $\sum_i\operatorname{Ind}_{H_i}^G\theta_i$ with $H_i\in\mathcal F$ and $\theta_i\in R(H_i)$. ([[def-induction-ideal-of-a-family-of-subgroups]]).

[F2] $I_{\mathcal F}(G)$ is an ideal of $R(G)$ for every family $\mathcal F$ of subgroups. ([[lem-induction-ideal-of-a-subgroup-family-is-an-ideal]]).

[F3] If $|G|=p^nl$ with $p\nmid l$, then $l\,1_G\in I_{\mathcal E_p}(G)$, where $\mathcal E_p$ is the family of $p$-elementary subgroups of $G$. ([[lem-elementary-detection-at-a-fixed-element]]).

[F4] Every finite $p$-elementary group is supersolvable. ([[lem-p-elementary-groups-are-supersolvable]]).

[F5] Every subgroup of a finite $p$-elementary group is $p$-elementary. ([[lem-elementary-and-hyperelementary-subgroups-are-subgroup-closed]]).

[F6] A finite supersolvable group, in the normal-series convention of the definition, has every irreducible finite-dimensional complex representation isomorphic to $\operatorname{Ind}_K^H\lambda$ for some subgroup $K\le H$ and some linear character $\lambda$ of $K$. ([[thm-supersolvable-groups-are-m-groups]], [[def-supersolvable-groups-and-monomial-characters]]).

[F7] Induction is transitive: $\operatorname{Ind}_H^G(\operatorname{Ind}_K^HW)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_K^GW$ for $K\le H\le G$. ([[thm-transitivity-of-induction-for-finite-groups]]).

[F8] Every irreducible complex character of a finite group is a virtual character, every virtual character is an integral combination of irreducible characters, and the character of an induced module is the induced character, so that each $\operatorname{Ind}_K^G\lambda$ with $\lambda$ linear is a monomial character of $G$. ([[def-virtual-character-and-character-ring-of-a-finite-group]], [[def-irreducible-complex-character]], [[def-induced-character-of-a-complex-representation]], [[def-monomial-representation-and-m-group]]).

## Proof

1.1 If $G=1$, then $R(G)=\mathbb Z\cdot1_G$ and $1_G=\operatorname{Ind}_G^G1_G$: by the definition of the trivial group as an elementary group, $G$ itself is $p$-elementary for every prime $p$. Hence every virtual character $\vartheta$ of the trivial group is an integral multiple of a monomial character induced from an elementary subgroup. [F8, given]

1.2 Suppose $|G|>1$ and write $|G|=p^{n_p}l_p$ with $p\nmid l_p$ for each prime $p\mid|G|$. By [F3] each $l_p1_G$ lies in $I_{\mathcal E_p}(G)$, hence in $I_{\mathcal E}(G)$ because $\mathcal E_p\subseteq\mathcal E$. The integers $l_p$, $p\mid|G|$, have greatest common divisor $1$: no prime $q\mid|G|$ divides $l_q$. Bézout's identity therefore provides integers $a_p$ with $\sum_{p\mid|G|}a_pl_p=1$, so $1_G=\sum_{p\mid|G|}a_p\,(l_p1_G)\in I_{\mathcal E}(G)$ as this is an additive subgroup. [F3, given, algebra]

2.1 In either case $1_G\in I_{\mathcal E}(G)$ (step 1.1 for $G=1$ and step 1.2 otherwise), and by [F2] the subgroup $I_{\mathcal E}(G)$ is an ideal of $R(G)$. Multiplying the virtual character $\vartheta$ by $1_G$ therefore gives $\vartheta=\vartheta\cdot1_G\in I_{\mathcal E}(G)$. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 By [F1] there are finitely many subgroups $H_1,\dots,H_m\in\mathcal E$ and virtual characters $\theta_i\in R(H_i)$ with $\vartheta=\sum_{i=1}^m\operatorname{Ind}_{H_i}^G\theta_i$. Fix such an expression and write each $\theta_i=\sum_jn_{ij}\theta_{ij}$ as an integral combination of the irreducible complex characters $\theta_{ij}$ of $H_i$. [F1, F8, step 2.1]

4.1 Each $H_i$ is $p_i$-elementary for some prime $p_i$, hence supersolvable by [F4]; [F6] therefore expresses each irreducible constituent $\theta_{ij}$ as $\theta_{ij}=\operatorname{Ind}_{K_{ij}}^{H_i}\lambda_{ij}$ for a subgroup $K_{ij}\le H_i$ and a linear character $\lambda_{ij}$ of $K_{ij}$. [F4, F6, step 3.1]

5.1 Since $K_{ij}\le H_i$ and $H_i$ is $p_i$-elementary, [F5] makes $K_{ij}$ a $p_i$-elementary, hence elementary, subgroup of $G$. So each of the subgroups $K_{ij}$ belongs to the family $\mathcal E$. [F5, step 4.1]

6.1 Consequently $\operatorname{Ind}_{H_i}^G\operatorname{Ind}_{K_{ij}}^{H_i}\lambda_{ij}=\operatorname{Ind}_{K_{ij}}^G\lambda_{ij}$ as characters, by [F7] and the definition of the induced character. Substituting the expressions of step 4.1 into the expression of step 3.1 and collecting the integer multiplicities $n_{ij}$ expresses $\vartheta$ as an integral linear combination of the monomial characters $\operatorname{Ind}_{K_{ij}}^G\lambda_{ij}$ induced from the elementary subgroups $K_{ij}$, which is the assertion. ∎ [F7, F8, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1]
