## Statement

Let $K$ be an imaginary quadratic field, let $\mathcal O_K$ be its maximal
order, let $f\ge1$, and set $\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K$. Let
$I_K(f)$ be the group of fractional $\mathcal O_K$-ideals $\mathfrak a$ with
$v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)=0$ for every prime ideal $\mathfrak p\mid
f\mathcal O_K$. For $\alpha\in K^\times$, write $\alpha\equiv1\pmod{f\mathcal
O_K}$ when $v_{\mathfrak p}(\alpha-1)\ge v_{\mathfrak p}(f)$ at every
$\mathfrak p\mid f\mathcal O_K$, and let $P_1(f)$ be the subgroup of $I_K(f)$
consisting of principal ideals $(\alpha)$ with this congruence. Thus
$I_K(f)/P_1(f)$ is the ray class group of modulus $f\mathcal O_K$. Let $S_f$
be its subgroup generated by the classes $(u)$ for integers $u$ relatively
prime to $f$. Then extension and contraction identify the
ordinary proper ideal class group of $\mathcal O_f$ with
$$\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)\cong \bigl(I_K(f)/P_1(f)\bigr)/S_f.$$
All maps are induced by ideal extension and contraction; no choice of class
representatives is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $K$, $\mathcal O_K$, $f$, and $\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K$.

[F1] [[lem-coprime-ideal-extension-contraction-for-quadratic-orders]]: extension and contraction are inverse multiplicative bijections on integral ideals prime to $f$.

[F2] [[def-quotient-group]]: for a group $G$ and normal subgroup $N$, the quotient group $G/N$ has cosets as elements and the induced group operation.

[F3] [[def-proper-invertible-ideal-class-group-of-a-quadratic-order]]: the ring class group is the proper invertible fractional ideal group modulo principal fractional ideals.

[F4] [[thm-weak-approximation-for-inequivalent-absolute-values]]: for a number field and finitely many distinct finite-place absolute values, simultaneous open conditions around prescribed local targets can be met by one field element; the proof uses no choice principle.

[F5] [[def-fractional-ideal]] and [[def-product-and-colon-of-fractional-ideals]]: a fractional ideal is a nonzero submodule of the fraction field contained in a scalar multiple of the order; ideal products and principal scaling have their stated meanings.

[F6] [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]]: at a maximal-order prime, $v_{\mathfrak p}(I)$ is the exponent of the prime in the localized fractional ideal; for a principal ideal this gives the corresponding field-element valuation.

[F7] [[lem-ray-class-congruence-subgroup-well-defined]]: principal ideals generated by elements congruent to $1$ modulo the finite modulus form a subgroup of the ideals prime to that modulus; its proof uses no choice principle.

## Proof

1.1 Extension and contraction [F1] identify the integral ideals prime to $f$ in the two orders. Every invertible fractional $\mathcal O_f$-ideal $A$ has a representative of this type. For each of the finitely many maximal ideals $\mathfrak m$ of $\mathcal O_f$ containing the conductor, invertibility makes $A_{\mathfrak m}=x_{\mathfrak m}(\mathcal O_f)_{\mathfrak m}$ for some $x_{\mathfrak m}\in K^\times$; an invertible module over a local ring is principal, since in an identity $1=\sum_i a_i b_i$ for $A_{\mathfrak m}A^{-1}_{\mathfrak m}$ at least one product is a unit and its $a_i$ generates. The group of units of each local order is open in the product of the finite completions above $\mathfrak m$. By [F4], choose $x\in K^\times$ simultaneously in the open sets $x_{\mathfrak m}(\mathcal O_f)_{\mathfrak m}^{\times}$; then $(x^{-1}A)_{\mathfrak m}=(\mathcal O_f)_{\mathfrak m}$ for every conductor prime. Multiplying $x^{-1}A$ by an integer supported only on rational primes away from $f$ clears its finitely many remaining denominators and preserves these local equalities. The resulting integral ideal is prime to the conductor and represents $[A]$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, given, choose]

2.1 Under [F1], a principal $\mathcal O_f$-ideal prime to $f$ is generated by $\alpha\in\mathcal O_f$ whose residue modulo $f\mathcal O_K$ is a scalar unit: integrality puts $\alpha$ in $\mathcal O_f$, and coprimality makes its residue invertible; every residue of $\mathcal O_f=\mathbb Z+f\mathcal O_K$ is scalar. Conversely, such an $\alpha$ generates an integral ideal prime to the conductor. Thus the ring-principal subgroup corresponds to the ray-principal ideals $(\alpha)$ with scalar-unit residue. [F1, F3, F5, F6, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $\alpha\equiv u\pmod{f\mathcal O_K}$ for an integer $u$ coprime to $f$, then $\alpha/u\equiv1\pmod{f\mathcal O_K}$. Thus the ray class of $(\alpha)$ equals the class of $(u)$, since their quotient is the principal ideal $(\alpha/u)\in P_1(f)$. Conversely, every $(u)$ with $(u,f)=1$ contracts to the principal ideal $u\mathcal O_f$, so its class is killed by the map to $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_f)$. Hence the kernel of the induced surjection from the ray group is exactly $S_f$. [F1, F2, F7, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Taking the quotient by this kernel gives the asserted isomorphism. Each construction used only extension, contraction, and quotient maps, so the identification is choice-free. [F1, F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The scalar-residue subgroup is the image of $(\mathbb Z/f\mathbb Z)^\times$ under $u\mapsto[(u)]$; it is not a quotient by a group of raw cardinality $\varphi(f)$. If $[(u)]$ is trivial in the ray group, then $(u)=(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha\equiv1\pmod f$, so $u/\alpha\in\mathcal O_K^\times$ and the scalar residue of $u$ is the residue of a global unit. Conversely, if $u$ has the same residue as a global unit $\epsilon$, then $u/\epsilon\equiv1\pmod f$ and $(u)=(u/\epsilon)$ is ray-principal. Thus the kernel is precisely the scalar residues of global units; the subgroup used in the quotient is the image, which is defined without choosing representatives. [F2, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
