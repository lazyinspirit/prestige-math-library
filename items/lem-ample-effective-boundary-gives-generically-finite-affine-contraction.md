## Statement

Assume AC. Let $X$ be an integral scheme proper over a field $k$, of
dimension $d\ge1$, and let $D\subseteq X$ be a nonempty effective Cartier
divisor. Assume $D$ is integral and projective over $k$, has dimension $d-1$,
and that $N=\mathcal O_X(D)|_D$ is ample. Then, for all sufficiently large
integers $m$, restriction
$$H^0(X,\mathcal O_X(mD))\longrightarrow H^0(D,N^{\otimes m})$$
is surjective. There are finitely many sections $s_0,s_1,\ldots,s_r$ of
$\mathcal O_X(mD)$ with $s_0$ the canonical section vanishing on $mD$,
such that they have no common zero and define a proper morphism
$\Phi:X\to\mathbb P^r_k$. Its restriction to $D$ is a closed immersion
into the hyperplane $z_0=0$, and
$$\Phi^{-1}(z_0\ne0)=X\setminus D.$$
The restriction $X\setminus D\to\mathbb A^r_k$ is proper. Its reduced
image $Y$ is affine integral of dimension $d$, and the induced dominant
map $X\setminus D\to Y$ has finite function-field extension. No claim
that this map is finite or birational is included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, $X,D,N,d$ as in the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] Coherent cohomology of a proper scheme over a field is
finite-dimensional. [[cor-projective-cohomology-finite-dimensional-field]]

[F2] For an ample invertible sheaf on a projective scheme over a field,
all its positive-degree coherent cohomology vanishes in high powers; its
sufficiently large powers give closed projective embeddings.
[[thm-serre-vanishing]] [[thm-ample-powers-very-ample-proper-base]]

[F3] A short exact sequence of sheaves gives its natural long exact
cohomology sequence. For a closed immersion $i:D\hookrightarrow X$,
$H^q(X,i_*M)=H^q(D,M)$ for quasi-coherent $M$.
[[thm-long-exact-sequence-sheaf-cohomology]]
[[lem-closed-immersion-cohomology-pushforward]]

[F4] Finitely many sections generating a line bundle define a morphism to
projective space; their ratios are its affine coordinates, and the inverse
image of a coordinate nonvanishing locus is the corresponding section's
nonvanishing locus. Projective space is separated: its diagonal is cut out
by the homogeneous equations $z_iw_j-z_jw_i=0$.
[[thm-line-bundle-sections-define-projective-map]]

[F5] A map from a proper scheme to a separated scheme over the same base
is proper; properness is preserved by base change, and a proper map is
closed. [[lem-proper-source-to-separated-target-proper]]
[[lem-proper-stable-base-change]] [[thm-proper-morphism-closed-image]]

[F6] The dimension of a finite-type domain over a field equals its
function-field transcendence degree. An extension generated by finitely
many algebraic elements is finite.
[[thm-affine-domain-dimension-transcendence-degree]]
[[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited through the proper-cohomology, projective-space and
properness suppliers [F1]–[F5]. Only finitely many boundary sections are
lifted after an integer threshold has been found.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; stabilize the finite dimensions of successive
first cohomology groups and lift a projective embedding of the boundary.

1.1 Locally let $t$ be an equation of the effective Cartier divisor. Multiplication by $t$ gives the exact sequence $0\to\mathcal O_X((m-1)D)\to\mathcal O_X(mD)\to N^{\otimes m}\to0$, with the last sheaf supported on $D$. Indeed on a trivializing open this is multiplication by a nonzerodivisor followed by quotient modulo $t$, and these local sequences glue. By [F2], $H^1(D,N^{\otimes m})=0$ for all $m$ at least some $m_0$. Thus [F3] makes $H^1(X,\mathcal O_X((m-1)D))\to H^1(X,\mathcal O_X(mD))$ surjective for all such $m$. Their dimensions are finite by [F1], so this sequence of nonnegative integers is eventually constant. From some threshold on these surjections are isomorphisms. Exactness in [F3] then forces the preceding restriction map on $H^0$ to be surjective. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 Increase the threshold so that $N^{\otimes m}$ is very ample by [F2]. Choose finitely many sections giving a closed immersion of $D$, and lift them to $s_1,\ldots,s_r$ on $X$ by step 1.1. Include the canonical section $s_0$ of $\mathcal O_X(mD)$. On $D$ the lifted sections have no common zero, and outside $D$ the section $s_0$ is nonzero. Thus the whole family generates the line bundle. By [F4] it defines $\Phi$. The restriction to $D$ is the chosen closed immersion into the hyperplane $z_0=0$, and its inverse image of $z_0\ne0$ is exactly $G=X\setminus D$. Since $X$ is proper, $\Phi$ is proper by [F5]. Its base change to that affine chart is proper as well. [F2, F4, F5, given, step 1.1]

3.1 Let $Z$ be the reduced closed image of $X$ in $\mathbb P^r_k$, and let $Y=Z\cap\mathbb A^r_k$. These images are closed by [F5], and are integral since $X$ and its nonempty open $G$ are integral. Explicitly on the affine chart, $Y$ has coordinate ring the image of $k[z_1,\ldots,z_r]$ in $k(X)$ under the ratios $s_i/s_0$. The generic point of $G$ maps to the kernel of that coordinate map, so its image is dense in this integral affine scheme; proper closedness makes it all of $Y$. The factor $G\to Y$ is proper by [F5] over the affine chart. [F4, F5, given, step 2.1]

4.1 The closed integral subset $\Phi(D)$ of $Z$ has dimension $d-1$. It is proper in $Z$ because the nonempty set $\Phi(G)$ is disjoint from it. Appending $Z$ to a maximal chain in $\Phi(D)$ gives $\dim Z\ge d$. On the other hand dominance embeds $k(Z)$ into $k(X)$, so [F6] gives $\dim Z\le d$. Therefore $\dim Z=d$, and the nonempty affine open $Y$ has the same function field and dimension. The extension $k(X)/k(Y)$ is finitely generated and algebraic, by equality of transcendence degrees; it is finite by [F6]. Thus the proper affine-chart morphism is generically finite onto the stated image, without assuming a separability or finite-fibre condition. [F6, given, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
