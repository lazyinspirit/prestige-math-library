## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $X$ be a noncompact Riemann surface
([[def-riemann-surface-and-holomorphic-atlas]]). The smooth structure used
below is the one carried by the holomorphic atlas: holomorphic transition
maps are smooth ([[cor-holomorphic-functions-are-real-analytic-and-smooth]]),
so the holomorphic charts are smooth charts ([[def-smooth-manifold]]).

1. **Exhaustion.** There are connected relatively compact domains
   $D_1\subseteq D_2\subseteq\cdots$ in $X$ with
   $\overline{D_n}\subseteq D_{n+1}$ for every $n$, such that each $\partial D_n$
   is a nonempty compact smooth embedded $1$-submanifold of $X$ with
   $D_n=\operatorname{int}\overline{D_n}$, and
   $$X=\bigcup_{n\ge1}D_n .$$

2. **Dirichlet problem.** For every connected relatively compact domain
   $D\subseteq X$ whose boundary $\partial D$ is a nonempty compact smooth
   embedded $1$-submanifold of $X$ and satisfies
   $D=\operatorname{int}\overline D$, and for every continuous boundary datum
   $\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb R$, there is a unique continuous function
   $u:\overline D\to\mathbb R$ that is harmonic on $D$
   ([[def-harmonic-and-subharmonic-riemann-surface-functions]]), continuous on
   $\overline D$, and satisfies $u|_{\partial D}=\varphi$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** Countable Choice; a noncompact Riemann surface $X$; a connected relatively compact domain $D\subseteq X$ with smooth boundary and a continuous datum $\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb R$ for part 2. Charts of the holomorphic atlas are used interchangeably with their restrictions to smaller open sets, which are again compatible charts.

[A1] Countable Choice: every countable family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] A Riemann surface is a nonempty connected Hausdorff second countable space with a holomorphic atlas; holomorphic functions between plane domains are smooth ([[def-riemann-surface-and-holomorphic-atlas]], [[cor-holomorphic-functions-are-real-analytic-and-smooth]], [[def-smooth-manifold]]).

[F2] Chartwise harmonicity and subharmonicity on $X$: $u$ is harmonic, resp. subharmonic, on an open $W\subseteq X$ when every chart expression of $u$ is plane harmonic ([[def-plane-harmonic-function]]), resp. plane subharmonic on each connected component ([[def-plane-subharmonic-function]]), and the notions do not depend on the atlas. Every real part of a function holomorphic in a chart is harmonic ([[thm-c2-holomorphic-components-are-harmonic]]), finite constants are harmonic, and a subharmonic function is upper semicontinuous and is not identically $-\infty$ on any component of its domain ([[def-plane-subharmonic-function]], [[def-harmonic-and-subharmonic-riemann-surface-functions]]).

[F3] Plane subharmonic toolkit. Restrictions of subharmonic functions to open subsets are subharmonic, and on a plane domain: nonnegative linear combinations and finite maxima of subharmonic functions are subharmonic ([[lem-positive-linear-combinations-and-finite-maxima-preserve-subharmonicity]]); a subharmonic function attaining a finite interior maximum on a domain is constant there ([[thm-maximum-principle-for-plane-subharmonic-functions]]); the upper-semicontinuous regularization of the supremum of a locally bounded-above family of subharmonic functions is subharmonic ([[thm-upper-envelope-theorem-for-plane-subharmonic-functions]]); subharmonic pieces glue across a seam when the inside limsup is at most the outside value ([[lem-gluing-lemma-for-plane-subharmonic-functions]]); subharmonicity is equivalent to the harmonic comparison on all compactly contained discs ([[thm-harmonic-majorant-characterization-of-plane-subharmonicity]]); and biholomorphic change of coordinates preserves subharmonicity in both directions ([[lem-biholomorphic-invariance-of-plane-subharmonicity]]).

[F4] Plane harmonic and Poisson toolkit: the Poisson modification $P_Du$ of a subharmonic function on a compactly contained disc is well defined, harmonic on the disc, subharmonic and at least $u$ on the ambient domain ([[def-poisson-modification-of-a-subharmonic-function]], [[thm-poisson-modification-preserves-subharmonicity-and-majorizes]]); harmonic extensions of continuous circle data are given by the Poisson integral with a positive kernel ([[thm-poisson-integral-solves-the-disc-dirichlet-problem]], [[lem-poisson-kernel-properties-on-the-disc]]); positive harmonic functions on a disc satisfy Harnack's inequality, so a nonnegative harmonic function on $D(a,R)$ satisfies $u(z)\le\frac{R+\rho}{R-\rho}u(a)$ whenever $|z-a|=\rho<R$ ([[thm-harnack-inequality-on-a-disc]]). For this open-disc version and nonnegative $u$, apply the supplied estimate to $u+\delta$ on closed discs of radius $S<R$, then let $S\uparrow R$ and $\delta\downarrow0$; harmonic functions have the circle mean-value property, and a continuous function with the local mean-value property is harmonic ([[thm-mean-value-property-for-plane-harmonic-functions]], [[thm-converse-mean-value-property-for-plane-functions]]).

[F5] Upper-semicontinuous regularization: $v^*(z)=\limsup_{w\to z}v(w)$ is upper semicontinuous, satisfies $v\le v^*$, and is the least upper semicontinuous majorant of $v$ ([[def-upper-semicontinuous-regularization]]).

[F6] Under Countable Choice every smooth manifold $M$ admits a smooth proper function $h:M\to[0,\infty)$ ([[thm-every-smooth-manifold-admits-a-smooth-proper-exhaustion-function]]).

[F7] Morse-Sard under Countable Choice: the critical values of a smooth map between smooth manifolds form a null set, so the regular values are dense; for $f:M\to\mathbb R$ one writes $M^a=f^{-1}((-\infty,a])$ and $f^{-1}([a,b])$ for the closed bands ([[thm-morse-sard-for-smooth-manifolds]], [[cor-regular-values-have-null-complement-and-are-dense]], [[def-closed-sublevel-and-level-set-of-a-smooth-function]]).

[F8] Submersions and regular level sets: a smooth map which is a submersion at a point has coordinates near that point in which it is a linear coordinate (a projection), so at a regular value the level set is a smooth embedded hypersurface and the sublevel set is locally a half-space ([[def-immersion-submersion-and-constant-rank-map]], [[cor-local-normal-form-for-submersions]], [[def-embedded-smooth-submanifold-with-boundary]]). A smooth plane curve through $0$ with nonvanishing gradient is locally a graph of a $C^k$ function over its tangent line ([[thm-euclidean-implicit-function-theorem]]).

[F9] Topology of manifolds: every topological manifold is locally compact and locally path-connected, with a neighbourhood basis of open sets with compact closures; in a locally path-connected space the connected components are open and agree with the path components, so a connected locally path-connected space is path-connected; connected components are closed and the closure of a connected set is connected ([[prop-topological-manifolds-are-locally-compact-and-locally-path-connected]], [[thm-connected-and-locally-path-connected-implies-path-connected]], [[def-connected-component-and-quasicomponent]]). Continuous images of compact sets are compact and compact subsets of Hausdorff spaces are closed ([[thm-compactness-under-continuous-maps]], [[thm-compact-subset-of-a-hausdorff-space-is-closed]]).

[F10] The cited semicontinuous extreme-value theorem applies to finite-valued upper-semicontinuous maps on compact subsets of $\mathbb R^n$ and is used for the real-valued boundary datum in step 11.1, not for the extended-valued $s=f-k$ in step 1.2 ([[thm-euclidean-semicontinuous-extreme-value-theorem]]). For extended-valued upper-semicontinuous $s:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)$, each superlevel set $\{s\ge b\}$ is closed directly from the definition; finite maxima remain upper-semicontinuous because their strict sublevel sets are finite intersections of open strict sublevel sets.

[F11] The principal logarithm is holomorphic on $\mathbb C\setminus(-\infty,0]$ ([[cor-principal-logarithm-is-holomorphic-on-the-slit-plane]]), the complex exponential is entire ([[thm-complex-exponential-is-entire-with-derivative-itself]]), and composites are holomorphic by [[thm-chain-rule-for-complex-derivatives]]. Thus $\exp(\mu\operatorname{Log}(-iw))$ is holomorphic on any sector whose rotation by $-i$ avoids that slit, for every real $\mu$.



**Proof technique:** direct.

## Proof
1.1 The holomorphic charts of $X$ have holomorphic transition maps, and holomorphic functions of one variable are smooth; hence these charts form a $C^\infty$ atlas on the connected Hausdorff second countable space $X$. So $X$ is a smooth $2$-manifold, and the hypotheses of [F6] are met. [F1, given]

1.2 **Locality of plane subharmonicity.** Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C$ be a plane domain and let $f:\Omega\to[-\infty,\infty)$ be upper semicontinuous, not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $\Omega$, and subharmonic near each of its points. To use the harmonic-majorant characterization, fix a closed disc $\overline{D(a,r)}\subseteq\Omega$ and a continuous $k$ on it, harmonic on $D(a,r)$, with $k\ge f$ on the boundary. If $k<f$ somewhere, set $s:=f-k$. Then $s$ is extended-valued upper semicontinuous, is positive somewhere, and satisfies $s\le0$ on the boundary; it may equal $-\infty$. It is bounded above on the compact disc: the open sets $\{s<n\}$ for positive integers $n$ cover it, so a finite subcover and nesting give a common upper bound. Thus $M:=\sup s$ is finite and positive. For each real $b<M$, the set $E_b:=\{s\ge b\}$ is closed by [F10] and nonempty by the definition of $M$; these sets have the finite-intersection property, so compactness gives a point $z_1\in\bigcap_{b<M}E_b$, where $s(z_1)=M$. Since $s\le0$ on the boundary, $z_1$ is interior. Let $Z:=\{z\in D(a,r):s(z)=M\}$; it is nonempty and closed in the open disc by upper semicontinuity. [F2, F3]

2.1 By [F6] and [A1] there is a smooth proper function $h:X\to[0,\infty)$ whose sublevel sets $\{h\le c\}$ are compact for every $c\in\mathbb R$. Fix a point $x_0\in X$ and put $M_0:=h(x_0)$, a nonnegative real number. [A1, F6, step 1.1, choose]

2.2 $Z$ is open in $D(a,r)$: given $z\in Z$, choose $\rho>0$ with $\overline{D(z,\rho)}\subseteq D(a,r)$ and with $D(z,\rho)$ contained in a neighbourhood of $z$ on which $f$ is subharmonic. On the disc $D(z,\rho)$ the function $f$ is subharmonic and $-k$ is subharmonic (it is harmonic), so $s=f+(-k)$ is subharmonic on $D(z,\rho)$; it attains the maximum $M$ at the interior point $z$ and therefore is constant equal to $M$ on $D(z,\rho)$ by the strong maximum principle, that is, $D(z,\rho)\subseteq Z$. Since $D(a,r)$ is connected and $Z\neq\varnothing$ is both open and closed in it, $Z=D(a,r)$ and $s\equiv M$ on the disc. [F3, step 1.2]

3.1 For each $n\ge1$ the open interval $(M_0+n,M_0+n+1)$ is nonempty and, by [F7], it contains a regular value of $h$, since the regular values of $h$ are dense in $\mathbb R$. By [A1] fix such a regular value $c_n$ for every $n$. Then $c_n<M_0+n+1\le M_0+n+2$ and $c_{n+1}>M_0+n+1>c_n$, so $c_1<c_2<\cdots$ and $c_n\to\infty$. [A1, F7, step 2.1, choose]

3.2 Step 2.2 leads to a contradiction, completing step 1.2: for $b\in\partial D(a,r)$ the upper semicontinuity of $s$ at $b$ (as a function defined on a neighbourhood of $\overline{D(a,r)}$) gives $s(b)\ge\limsup_{D(a,r)\ni z\to b}s(z)=M>0$, contradicting $s\le0$ on the boundary circle. Hence no such counterexample exists, the harmonic comparison of step 1.2 always holds, and the harmonic-majorant characterization makes $f$ subharmonic on $\Omega$. [F3, step 2.2]

4.1 For each $n$ let $K_n$ be the connected component of the closed set $\{h\le c_n\}$ containing $x_0$ and set $D_n:=\operatorname{int}K_n$. Since $\{h\le c_n\}$ is compact and $K_n$ is a component of it, $K_n$ is closed in $X$ and compact. Also $D_n=K_n\cap\{h<c_n\}$: a point $p\in K_n$ with $h(p)<c_n$ has a ball $B$ with $p\in B\subseteq\{h<c_n\}\subseteq\{h\le c_n\}$; this ball is connected and contains $p\in K_n$, hence lies in the component $K_n$, so $p$ is interior to $K_n$; conversely an interior point $p$ of $K_n$ cannot have $h(p)=c_n$, because $c_n$ is a regular value, so $h$ takes values larger than $c_n$ arbitrarily close to $p$ and no neighbourhood of $p$ is contained in $\{h\le c_n\}$. In particular $x_0\in D_n$ for every $n$. [F7, F8, step 3.1, construct]

4.2 At every point $p$ of $h^{-1}(c_n)$ the differential of $h$ is nonzero, so $h$ is a submersion at $p$ and by [F8] there are smooth coordinates $(u,v)$ around $p$ with $u=h-c_n$. Hence $h^{-1}(c_n)$ is a smooth embedded $1$-submanifold of $X$, and near each of its points the set $\{h\le c_n\}$ is the closed half-space $\{u\le 0\}$ in those coordinates, a set which is path-connected after shrinking the chart. [F8, step 3.1]

4.3 **Gluing subharmonic functions on a surface.** Let $\Omega\subseteq X$ be open, let $u$ be subharmonic on $\Omega$, let $B\subseteq\Omega$ be any open subset, let $v$ be subharmonic on $B$, and assume $\limsup_{z\to\eta,\,z\in B}v(z)\le u(\eta)$ for every $\eta\in\partial B\cap\Omega$. Define $w:=u$ on $\Omega\setminus B$ and $w:=\max\{u,v\}$ on $B$. Then $w$ is upper semicontinuous on $\Omega$: at points of $B$, $w$ is a finite maximum of upper semicontinuous functions; at points of $\Omega\setminus\overline B$ it equals $u$; and at $\eta\in\partial B\cap\Omega$ the limsup of $w$ is the larger of the limsups of $u$ and of $v$ along $B$, at most $\max\{u(\eta),u(\eta)\}=u(\eta)=w(\eta)$ by the seam hypothesis. Also $w\ge u$, and $u$ is not identically $-\infty$ on any component of $\Omega$, so neither is $w$. [F2, F3, step 3.2, construct]

5.1 $K_n=\overline{D_n}$, and $D_n$ is connected. Indeed $D_n\subseteq K_n$ and $K_n$ is closed, so $\overline{D_n}\subseteq K_n$; conversely, for $p\in K_n$ with $h(p)=c_n$ the local half-space from step 4.2 contains points $q$ with $h(q)<c_n$ arbitrarily close to $p$, and the path-connectedness of the local half-space puts each such $q$ in the same component of $\{h\le c_n\}$ as $p$, that is, in $K_n\cap\{h<c_n\}=D_n$; a point of $K_n$ with $h(p)<c_n$ already lies in $D_n$. So $K_n\subseteq\overline{D_n}$ and equality holds. If $D_n=A\cup B$ were a disjoint union of nonempty open subsets, then $K_n=\overline A\cup\overline B$ would be a union of two nonempty sets; if $\overline A\cap\overline B=\varnothing$ this contradicts connectedness of $K_n$, so pick $p\in\overline A\cap\overline B$. If $p\in D_n$ then $p$ lies in the open set $A$ (say) which is disjoint from $B$, contradicting $p\in\overline B$; so $p\in\partial K_n$, and near $p$ the set $D_n$ contains the path-connected local half-space $\{h<c_n\}$ intersected with the chart, which must lie entirely in $A$ or entirely in $B$, say in $A$; then all points of $D_n$ near $p$ lie in $A$, contradicting $p\in\overline B$. Hence $D_n$ is connected. [F9, step 4.1, step 4.2, cases]

5.2 For every point $p\in\Omega$ there is a chart $\theta$ with $p$ in its domain such that the chart expression $w_\theta$ is plane subharmonic on a neighbourhood of $\theta(p)$. If $p\notin\overline B$, take a chart whose domain is contained in $\Omega\setminus\overline B$; then $w_\theta=u_\theta$ is plane subharmonic by [F2]. If $p\in B$, take a chart with domain contained in $B$; then $w_\theta=\max\{u_\theta,v_\theta\}$ is a finite maximum of plane subharmonic functions, hence plane subharmonic by [F3]. If $p\in\partial B\cap\Omega$, take a coordinate-disc neighbourhood $T$ of $p$ contained in $\Omega$, with chart $\theta:T\to D(0,r)$. Apply the plane gluing lemma [F3] with ambient domain $\theta(T)$ and open inside set $\theta(T\cap B)$. The chart expression of $u$ is subharmonic on $\theta(T)$, that of $v$ is subharmonic on each component of $\theta(T\cap B)$, and every seam point inside $\theta(T)$ corresponds to a point of $\partial B\cap T$, where step 4.3 supplies the limsup bound. Thus $w_\theta$ is subharmonic near $\theta(p)$. [F2, F3, step 4.3, cases]

6.1 For every $n$, $\partial D_n$ is a nonempty compact smooth embedded $1$-submanifold of $X$ and $D_n=\operatorname{int}\overline{D_n}$. Indeed $\partial D_n=\overline{D_n}\setminus D_n=K_n\setminus D_n=K_n\cap h^{-1}(c_n)$ by step 5.1, which is closed in the compact set $K_n$, hence compact, and is a smooth $1$-submanifold by step 4.2. If $\partial D_n$ were empty then $D_n=K_n$ would be open and closed in the connected space $X$, so $D_n=X$ and $X$ would be compact, contradicting the hypothesis that $X$ is noncompact; thus $\partial D_n\neq\varnothing$. [F9, step 5.1]

6.2 For every $n$ one has $K_n\subseteq D_{n+1}$. The set $K_n$ is connected, contains $x_0$, and satisfies $K_n\subseteq\{h\le c_n\}\subseteq\{h<c_{n+1}\}$ because $c_n<c_{n+1}$; hence $K_n$ lies in the component $K_{n+1}$ of $\{h\le c_{n+1}\}$ containing $x_0$, and since $h<c_{n+1}$ on all of $K_n$ no point of $K_n$ lies in $h^{-1}(c_{n+1})$; by step 4.1 applied at level $n+1$, $K_n\subseteq K_{n+1}\cap\{h<c_{n+1}\}=D_{n+1}$. In particular $\overline{D_n}=K_n\subseteq D_{n+1}$ and $D_n\subseteq D_{n+1}$. [step 4.1, step 5.1]

6.3 $w$ is subharmonic on $\Omega$. Let $\theta$ be any chart with $\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap\Omega\neq\varnothing$. By step 5.2 and the biholomorphic invariance of subharmonicity [F3], every point of $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap\Omega)$ has a plane neighbourhood on which $w_\theta$ is subharmonic: for a point $\theta(p)$, use the chart $\theta_p$ of step 5.2 and write $w_\theta=w_{\theta_p}\circ(\theta_p\circ\theta^{-1})$ on the overlap, a composition of the plane subharmonic function $w_{\theta_p}$ with the biholomorphism $\theta_p\circ\theta^{-1}$ between plane domains. Moreover $w_\theta$ is upper semicontinuous and is not identically $-\infty$ on any component, because these properties hold for $w$ by step 4.3 and are read in charts. By the locality of plane subharmonicity, step 3.2, applied separately to each component, $w_\theta$ is plane subharmonic on every component of $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap\Omega)$. As $\theta$ was an arbitrary chart, $w$ is subharmonic on $\Omega$. [F2, F3, step 3.2, step 5.2]

7.1 $X=\bigcup_{n\ge1}D_n$. Let $x\in X$. By [F9] the connected manifold $X$ is path-connected and locally path-connected, so choose a path $\gamma$ from $x_0$ to $x$; its image $C$ is compact, so $h(C)$ is a compact subset of $[0,\infty)$ and is bounded, say by $N$. Choose $n$ with $c_n\ge N$, possible because $c_n\to\infty$. Then $C\subseteq\{h\le c_n\}$ is connected and contains $x_0$ and $x$, so $x\in K_n\subseteq D_{n+1}$ by step 6.2. Hence every point of $X$ lies in some $D_n$. [F9, step 3.1, step 6.2]

8.1 Steps 6.1, 6.2 and 7.1 prove part 1: $(D_n)$ is a sequence of connected relatively compact domains with $\overline{D_n}\subseteq D_{n+1}$, each with nonempty smooth boundary and $D_n=\operatorname{int}\overline{D_n}$, whose union is $X$. [step 6.1, step 6.2, step 7.1]

9.1 **Maximum principle.** Let $v$ be subharmonic on $D$ and let $B\in\mathbb R$ satisfy $\limsup_{q\to\eta,\,q\in D}v(q)\le B$ for every $\eta\in\partial D$. Then $v\le B$ on $D$. For any real $b>B$, the set $E_b:=\{q\in D:v(q)\ge b\}$ is closed in $\overline D$: it is closed inside $D$ by upper semicontinuity, and its closure cannot meet $\partial D$ by the boundary limsup bound. Thus $E_b$ is compact. If $s:=\sup_Dv=+\infty$, the nonempty nested compact sets $E_n$ for integers $n>B$ have the finite-intersection property, so some point satisfies $v(q)\ge n$ for every such $n$, impossible because subharmonic functions have no $+\infty$ values. Hence $s<+\infty$. If $s>B$, the nested nonempty compact sets $E_b$ for $B<b<s$ again have the finite-intersection property; a point in their intersection has $v(q)\ge b$ for every $b<s$, so $v(q)=s$. Since $s>B$, the point is interior, and the strong maximum principle forces $v\equiv s$ on connected $D$. Taking any boundary point then gives $s=\limsup_{q\to\eta}v(q)\le B$, a contradiction. Therefore $s\le B$. [F2, F3, F9, step 8.1]

10.1 In the situation of step 9.1 the maximum set $\{q\in D:v(q)=s\}$ is open: for $q$ in it, take a chart $\theta$ around $q$ with $\theta(q)=0$ and $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap D)$ a plane domain; the chart expression $v_\theta$ is plane subharmonic and attains the finite maximum $s$ at the interior point $0$, so by the strong maximum principle $v_\theta$ is constant on $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap D)$ and $v$ is constant on a neighbourhood of $q$. The set is also closed in $D$, because it is $D\cap\{v\ge s\}$ and superlevel sets of the upper semicontinuous function $v$ are closed. Since $D$ is connected the set is all of $D$, so $v\equiv s>B$ on $D$. But $\partial D\neq\varnothing$: otherwise $D$ would be open and closed in the connected space $X$, forcing $D=X$ and contradicting compactness of $\overline D$. At any $\eta\in\partial D$ the boundary hypothesis then gives the contradiction $s=\limsup_{q\to\eta}v(q)\le B$, so $s\le B$ and the maximum principle holds for all $v$. [F2, F3, F9, step 9.1, cases]

11.1 **The Perron family.** Fix a connected relatively compact domain $D\subseteq X$ with smooth boundary and a continuous datum $\varphi:\partial D\to\mathbb R$, and define $$\mathcal P:=\Bigl\{v \text{ subharmonic on } D:\ \limsup_{q\to\eta,\,q\in D}v(q)\le\varphi(\eta)\ \text{ for every }\eta\in\partial D\Bigr\}.$$ The boundary $\partial D$ is compact (it is closed in the compact set $\overline D$), so its continuous image $\varphi(\partial D)$ is a compact subset of $\mathbb R$ [F9]. The real-valued extreme-value theorem [F10] gives $m:=\min_{\partial D}\varphi$ and $M:=\max_{\partial D}\varphi$. The constant function $m$ is harmonic, hence subharmonic, on the nonempty open set $D$, and its limsup at each $\eta$ equals $m\le\varphi(\eta)$; so $m\in\mathcal P$ and $\mathcal P$ is nonempty. By the maximum principle step 10.1 every $v\in\mathcal P$ satisfies $v\le M$ on $D$. Hence the pointwise supremum $U(q):=\sup\{v(q):v\in\mathcal P\}$ satisfies $m\le U\le M$ on $D$. [F2, F9, F10, step 10.1, step 6.3, construct]

12.1 **The envelope.** Let $H$ be the upper-semicontinuous regularization of $U$, $H(x):=\limsup_{y\to x,\,y\in D}U(y)$, equivalently the least upper semicontinuous majorant of $U$ [F5]. Then $U\le H$ and, since $m\le U\le M$, also $m\le H\le M$. Moreover $H$ is subharmonic on $D$: for every chart $\theta$ the family of plane subharmonic functions $\{v_\theta:v\in\mathcal P\}$ on the plane domain $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap D)$ is nonempty and bounded above by $M$, its pointwise supremum is $U_\theta$ and its upper-semicontinuous regularization is $H_\theta$ (regularization is a local, hence chart-invariant, operation); so the upper envelope theorem [F3] makes $H_\theta$ plane subharmonic. As $\theta$ was arbitrary, $H$ is subharmonic on $D$. [F3, F5, step 11.1]

13.1 **Poisson modification on the surface.** Let $B\subseteq D$ be a coordinate disc with $\overline B\subseteq D$, written $B=\theta^{-1}(D(w_0,r))$ for a chart $\theta$ and an open disc $D(w_0,r)$ whose closure lies in $\theta(\operatorname{dom}\theta\cap D)$. For $v\in\mathcal P$ let $P_\theta v$ denote the plane Poisson modification of $v_\theta$ on the disc $D(w_0,r)$, which is well defined by [F4], and define the surface modification $$\tilde v:=\begin{cases}P_\theta v\circ\theta & \text{on }B,\\ v & \text{on }D\setminus B.\end{cases}$$ Then $\tilde v$ is harmonic on $B$ and $\tilde v\ge v$ on $B$, because the plane modification has these properties and harmonicity and the inequality are read in the chart $\theta$; and $\limsup_{z\to\eta,\,z\in B}\tilde v(z)\le v(\eta)$ for every $\eta\in\partial B$: if $\varphi_n\downarrow v_\theta$ is the decreasing sequence of continuous boundary approximants used to define the modification and $h_n$ are their harmonic Poisson extensions, then $P_\theta v=\inf_nh_n\le h_n$ on the disc, so $\limsup_{z\to\eta,\,z\in B}\tilde v(z)\le\limsup h_n(\theta(z))=\varphi_n(\theta(\eta))$ for every $n$, and letting $n\to\infty$ gives the bound $v(\eta)$. [F4, step 12.1]

14.1 $\tilde v$ is subharmonic on $D$ and belongs to $\mathcal P$. Indeed $\tilde v=v$ on the open set $D\setminus\overline B$, so its boundary limsups at points of $\partial D$ equal those of $v$ and are at most $\varphi$; and subharmonicity follows from the gluing lemma step 6.3 applied with $\Omega:=D$, the subharmonic function $v$, the coordinate disc $B$, and the inside function $\tilde v|_B$, whose seam limsups are at most $v$ by step 13.1: the glued function is $\max\{v,\tilde v\}=\tilde v$ on $B$ and $v$ outside $B$, that is, $\tilde v$ itself. Hence $\tilde v\in\mathcal P$, and in particular $\tilde v\le M$. [step 6.3, step 11.1, step 13.1]

15.1 **Monotonicity and directedness.** If $v,w\in\mathcal P$ satisfy $v\le w$ on $D$, then $\tilde v\le\tilde w$ on $B$: choose decreasing continuous approximants $\alpha_n\downarrow v_\theta$ and $\beta_n\downarrow w_\theta$ on the circle $\partial D(w_0,r)$ and set $\gamma_n:=\min\{\alpha_n,\beta_n\}$; then the $\gamma_n$ are continuous and decrease to $\min\{v_\theta,w_\theta\}=v_\theta$, while $\gamma_n\le\beta_n$, so positivity of the Poisson kernel [F4] gives $P[\gamma_n]\le P[\beta_n]$ on $D(w_0,r)$ and hence $P_\theta v=\inf_nP[\gamma_n]\le\inf_nP[\beta_n]=P_\theta w$, that is, $\tilde v\le\tilde w$. Also, if $v,w\in\mathcal P$ then $t:=\max\{v,w\}\in\mathcal P$: it is subharmonic on $D$ since both are, and at each $\eta\in\partial D$ its limsup is at most $\max\{\limsup v,\limsup w\}\le\varphi(\eta)$. By monotonicity $\tilde t\ge\tilde v,\tilde w$ on $B$. [F3, F4, step 14.1]

16.1 **Harnack control.** Define $h_*(q):=\sup\{\tilde v(q):v\in\mathcal P\}$ on $B$. Every $\tilde v$ is harmonic on $B$ and satisfies $\tilde v\le U\le H$ on $B$, so $h_*\le H$ on $B$; and $h_*\ge m$ since $m\in\mathcal P$ and $\tilde m\ge m$. Fix $a\in B$ and $\varepsilon>0$. Since $h_*(a)$ is a real number and is the supremum of the values $\tilde v(a)$, there is $v\in\mathcal P$ with $\tilde v(a)>h_*(a)-\varepsilon$. For any $w\in\mathcal P$ put $t:=\max\{v,w\}$; then $\tilde t\ge\tilde v,\tilde w$ and $$0\le(\tilde t-\tilde v)(a)\le h_*(a)-\tilde v(a)<\varepsilon,$$ while $\tilde t-\tilde v$ is harmonic and nonnegative on $B$ by step 15.1. [F4, step 12.1, step 14.1, step 15.1, choose]

17.1 $h_*$ is harmonic on $B$. Choose a chart subdisc $\theta^{-1}(D(a,\rho))\subseteq B$ centred at the point $a$ of step 16.1, and fix $r<\rho$. For $\varepsilon>0$ choose $v$ as in step 16.1 and let $w\in\mathcal P$ be arbitrary. The nonnegative harmonic function $u:=\tilde t-\tilde v$ on $B$ satisfies $u(a)<\varepsilon$, so Harnack's inequality [F4] applied on the disc $D(a,\rho)$ gives $$0\le\tilde t(z)-\tilde v(z)=u(z)\le\frac{\rho+r}{\rho-r}\,u(a)<C\varepsilon \qquad(z\in\overline{D(a,r)}),$$ with $C:=(\rho+r)/(\rho-r)$ independent of $w$. Hence $\tilde w\le\tilde v+C\varepsilon$ on $\overline{D(a,r)}$ for every $w\in\mathcal P$, and taking the supremum over $w$ gives $h_*-\tilde v\le C\varepsilon$ on $\overline{D(a,r)}$, while $h_*\ge\tilde v$. So for every $\varepsilon>0$ there is a function harmonic on $B$ that approximates $h_*$ uniformly on $\overline{D(a,r)}$ within $C\varepsilon$; in particular $h_*(b)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}k_\varepsilon(b)$ where $k_\varepsilon$ is such an approximant, and the circle average of $h_*$ over $\partial D(b,s)$ differs from $h_*(b)$ by at most $2C\varepsilon$ for every $\varepsilon>0$, hence equals it. Thus $h_*$ satisfies the local mean-value property on $B$ and is continuous, so it is harmonic on $B$ by the converse of the mean-value property [F4]. [F4, step 16.1]

17.2 $h_*=H$ on $B$. Fix $a\in B$ and $\varepsilon>0$, and choose $D(a,\rho)\subseteq\theta(B)$ centred at $\theta(a)$, denoting this coordinate again by $a$. Choose $0<r<\rho$ so small that $$\left(\frac{\rho+r}{\rho-r}-1\right)(M-H(a)+2\varepsilon)<\varepsilon.$$ By the definition of the regularization in step 12.1, there is $z\in D(a,r)$ with $U(z)>H(a)-\varepsilon$, and then some $v\in\mathcal P$ has $v(z)>U(z)-\varepsilon>H(a)-2\varepsilon$. Thus $\tilde v(z)\ge v(z)>H(a)-2\varepsilon$. The nonnegative function $k:=M-\tilde v$ is harmonic on $B$ by step 14.1. Harnack's inequality [F4], applied at the actual distance $|z-a|<r$, gives $$k(a)\le\frac{\rho+|z-a|}{\rho-|z-a|}k(z)\le\frac{\rho+r}{\rho-r}(M-H(a)+2\varepsilon)<M-H(a)+3\varepsilon.$$ Hence $h_*(a)\ge\tilde v(a)>H(a)-3\varepsilon$. Letting $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ gives $h_*(a)\ge H(a)$; the reverse inequality is step 16.1. [F4, step 12.1, step 14.1, step 16.1]
18.1 $H$ is harmonic on $D$: every point of the open set $D$ has, by local compactness of the manifold $X$ [F9], a coordinate disc $B$ around it with $\overline B\subseteq D$, and $H=h_*$ is harmonic on $B$ by steps 17.1 and 17.2. [F9, step 17.1, step 17.2]

19.1 **A local peak function.** Fix $\zeta\in\partial D$ and a chart $\theta$ with $\theta(\zeta)=0$ whose domain $U$ is a coordinate disc $\theta(U)=D(0,2\rho_0)$. Since $\partial D$ is a smooth embedded curve, after composing $\theta$ with a rotation we may assume that near $0$ the image $\theta(\partial D\cap U)$ is the graph of a smooth function $\gamma$ with $\gamma(0)=\gamma'(0)=0$, and that $$\theta(D\cap U)\cap D(0,2\rho_0)=\{(x,y):x^2+y^2<(2\rho_0)^2,\ y>\gamma(x)\};$$ shrinking $\rho_0$ we may also assume $|\gamma'(x)|\le\frac14$ for $|x|\le2\rho_0$. Then every point $w=(x,y)$ of $\theta(D\cap U')$, where $U':=\theta^{-1}(D(0,2\rho_0))$, satisfies $y>\gamma(x)\ge-\tfrac14|x|>-\tfrac12|x|$; that is, $\theta(D\cap U')$ is contained in the sector $S:=\{y>-\tfrac12|x|\}$, whose half-angle at the origin, measured from the positive vertical axis, is $\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac12$. Put $\mu:=\pi\big/\bigl(\pi+2\arctan\frac12\bigr)$ and define, for $w\in S$, $$Q(w):=\operatorname{Re}\bigl((-iw)^{\mu}\bigr),$$ where $(-iw)^{\mu}:=\exp(\mu\operatorname{Log}(-iw))$. Since $|\arg(-iw)|<\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac12<\pi$ on $S$, this is holomorphic by [F11]; writing $w=re^{i(\pi/2+\vartheta)}$ gives $Q(w)=r^{\mu}\cos(\mu\vartheta)$. Finally define $$q(z):=-Q(\theta(z))\qquad(z\in D\cap U').$$ [F2, F8, F11, step 18.1, construct]

20.1 The function $q$ has the following properties: it is subharmonic on $D\cap U'$; it is negative there; $q(z)\to0$ as $z\to\zeta$; and with $c_0:=\cos\bigl(\mu(\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac14)\bigr)>0$ one has $$q(z)\le-c_0\,|\theta(z)|^{\mu}\qquad(z\in D\cap U').$$ Indeed $Q$ is harmonic on $S\setminus\{0\}$ (it is the real part of a holomorphic function) and $0\notin\theta(D\cap U')$, so $q$ is harmonic, hence subharmonic, on $D\cap U'$; and for $w\in\theta(D\cap U')$ the angle $\vartheta$ of $w$ relative to the positive vertical axis satisfies $|\vartheta|\le\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac14$, because $y>-\frac14|x|$; since $\mu\bigl(\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac14\bigr)<\mu\bigl(\frac\pi2+\arctan\frac12\bigr)=\frac\pi2$, we get $\cos(\mu\vartheta)\ge c_0>0$, whence $q(w)=-|w|^{\mu}\cos(\mu\vartheta)\le-c_0|w|^{\mu}<0$ and $q\to0$ at $0$. [F2, F8, F11, step 19.1, algebra]

21.1 **Boundary limit from above.** Fix $\zeta\in\partial D$ and $\varepsilon>0$, and take the chart, $\rho_0$, $U'$ and $q$ of steps 19.1 and 20.1. Shrinking $U'$ further, using continuity of $\varphi$ at $\zeta$, we may assume $|\varphi(\eta)-\varphi(\zeta)|<\varepsilon$ for every $\eta\in\partial D\cap U'$. Let $B:=\theta^{-1}(D(0,\rho_0))$, so that $q\le-c_0\rho_0^{\mu}$ on $\partial B\cap D$ by step 20.1. Fix $v\in\mathcal P$ and choose an integer $A\ge0$ with $Ac_0\rho_0^{\mu}\ge M-\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon$. Define $$G:=\max\{\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon,\ v+Aq\}\ \text{ on }D\cap B,\qquad G:=\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon\ \text{ on }D\setminus B.$$ At every $\eta\in\partial B\cap D$ the limsup of $v+Aq$ is at most $v(\eta)+Aq(\eta)\le M-Ac_0\rho_0^{\mu}\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$, and the constant $\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$ is subharmonic, so the gluing step 6.3, with open inside set $D\cap B\subseteq D$, shows that $G$ is subharmonic on $D$. Its boundary limsup at every $\eta\in\partial D$ is at most $\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$: for $\eta\in\partial D\setminus B$ this is the definition, and for $\eta\in\partial D\cap B$ it is $\max\{\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon,\limsup(v+Aq)\}\le\max\{\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon,\varphi(\eta)\}=\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$, because $q<0$ on $D\cap B$ and $\varphi(\eta)<\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$. By the maximum principle step 10.1, $G\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$ on $D$. Because the chosen $A$ depends only on $M,\varphi(\zeta),\varepsilon$ and the fixed chart, it is independent of $v$. Thus $U\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon-Aq$ on $D\cap B$. The right-hand side is continuous there, so its upper-semicontinuous regularization satisfies $H=U^*\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon-Aq$; since $q(z)\to0$ as $z\to\zeta$, $\limsup_{z\to\zeta}H(z)\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$. [step 10.1, step 6.3, step 11.1, step 20.1, choose]

22.1 **Boundary limit from below.** In the setting of step 21.1 choose an integer $A'\ge0$ with $A'c_0\rho_0^{\mu}\ge\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon-m$ and define $$\ell:=\max\{m,\ \varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon+A'q\}\ \text{ on }D\cap B,\qquad \ell:=m\ \text{ on }D\setminus B.$$ On $\partial B\cap D$ one has $\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon+A'q\le\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon-A'c_0\rho_0^{\mu}\le m$, so the gluing step 6.3 with inside set $D\cap B$ and the constant subharmonic function $m$ shows that $\ell$ is subharmonic on $D$. Its limsup at every $\eta\in\partial D$ is at most $\varphi(\eta)$: at $\eta\in\partial D\setminus B$ it equals $m\le\varphi(\eta)$, and at $\eta\in\partial D\cap B$ it is at most $\max\{m,\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon\}\le\varphi(\eta)$ because $m\le\varphi(\eta)$ and $\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon<\varphi(\eta)$. Hence $\ell\in\mathcal P$, so $U\ge\ell$ and $H\ge U\ge\ell$ on $D$; since $\max\{m,\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon+A'q\}\ge\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon+A'q$ and $q(z)\to0$ as $z\to\zeta$, this gives $\liminf_{z\to\zeta}H(z)\ge\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon$. [step 6.3, step 11.1, step 20.1, step 21.1, choose]

23.1 Steps 21.1 and 22.1 give, for every $\zeta\in\partial D$ and every $\varepsilon>0$, the two bounds $\limsup_{z\to\zeta}H(z)\le\varphi(\zeta)+\varepsilon$ and $\liminf_{z\to\zeta}H(z)\ge\varphi(\zeta)-\varepsilon$; hence $\lim_{z\to\zeta}H(z)=\varphi(\zeta)$ for every boundary point $\zeta$ and the limit is a genuine two-sided limit along $D$. [step 21.1, step 22.1]

24.1 **Conclusion of part 2.** Define $u:\overline D\to\mathbb R$ by $u:=H$ on $D$ and $u:=\varphi$ on $\partial D$. Then $u$ is harmonic on $D$ by step 18.1 and continuous on $\overline D$: at interior points $H$ is harmonic, hence continuous, and at a boundary point $\zeta$ the limit of $u$ along $D$ is $\varphi(\zeta)=u(\zeta)$ by step 23.1 while along $\partial D$ it is $\varphi(\zeta)$ by continuity of $\varphi$. Uniqueness: if $u_1,u_2$ both have the required properties, then $u_1-u_2$ is continuous on $\overline D$, harmonic on $D$ and vanishes on $\partial D$, so the maximum principle step 10.1 applied to $u_1-u_2$ and to $u_2-u_1$ gives $u_1-u_2\le0$ and $u_1-u_2\ge0$, hence $u_1=u_2$. Together with step 8.1 this proves both assertions of the statement. [step 8.1, step 10.1, step 18.1, step 23.1] ∎
