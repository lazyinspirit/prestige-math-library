## Statement

Let $R$ be a Jacobson ring, let $A$ be a finite-type $R$-algebra, and let
$\mathfrak m$ be a maximal ideal of $A$. Put $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m\cap R$.
Then the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak m)=A_{\mathfrak m}/\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak m}$
is a finite field extension of
$$
\kappa(\mathfrak p)=R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}.
$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Jacobson ring $R$, a finite-type $R$-algebra $A$, and a maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\subseteq A$ with contraction $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak m\cap R$.

[L1] Finite type means generated by finitely many algebra elements ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] Localizing at a prime uses the denominator set outside that prime ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

[L3] The localization at a prime is a local ring with the extended prime as its maximal ideal ([[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]).

[L4] The residue field at a prime is the fraction field of the quotient by that prime ([[cor-residue-field-of-a-localisation-at-a-prime]]).

[L5] A field finitely generated as an algebra over a field is a finite extension ([[cor-field-finite-type-over-a-field-is-a-finite-extension]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], choose generators $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ of $A$ over $R$. Localizing at $\mathfrak p$ gives $$ A_{\mathfrak p}\cong R_{\mathfrak p}[a_1/1,\ldots,a_n/1], $$ so $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a finite-type $R_{\mathfrak p}$-algebra. [L1, L2, given, choose]

2.1 The maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ extends to a maximal ideal $\mathfrak mA_{\mathfrak p}$ of $A_{\mathfrak p}$, and localizing further at that maximal ideal yields the local ring $A_{\mathfrak m}$ with residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak m)$. By [L4], the base residue field is $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$. [L3, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 Passing to residue fields sends the finite-type algebra $A_{\mathfrak p}$ over $R_{\mathfrak p}$ to the finite-type $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra $$ \kappa(\mathfrak p)\otimes_{R_{\mathfrak p}} A_{\mathfrak p} \twoheadrightarrow \kappa(\mathfrak m). $$ Because $\kappa(\mathfrak m)$ is a field, [L5] implies that it is a finite extension of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. [L4, L5, step 2.1]

4.1 Hence $\kappa(\mathfrak m)/\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is finite. [step 3.1] ∎
