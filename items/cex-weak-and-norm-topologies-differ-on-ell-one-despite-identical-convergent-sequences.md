## Statement

On each of the infinite-dimensional spaces $\ell^1(\mathbb R)$ and
$\ell^1(\mathbb C)$, the weak topology is strictly coarser than the norm
topology.  Nevertheless, a sequence converges weakly if and only if it
converges in norm.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scalar field $\mathbb K\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$ and $X=\ell^1(\mathbb K)$.

[F1] The weak topology is generated by finite intersections of inverse images of scalar open sets under members of $X^*$; it is contained in the norm topology ([[def-weak-topology-on-a-normed-space]]).

[F2] The norm induces the metric $d(x,y)=\|x-y\|$, and every metric-open set, including the open unit ball, is available as a norm-open set ([[def-norm-and-normed-space]]).

[F3] The coordinate sequences $e_n$ belong to $\ell^1(\mathbb K)$ and satisfy $\|e_n\|_1=1$; finite coordinate sums have their usual $\ell^1$ norm ([[lem-finite-truncations-are-dense-in-c0-and-ell-one]]).

[F4] If a linear map has finite-dimensional domain, then its domain dimension is its nullity plus its rank ([[thm-rank-nullity]]).

[F5] Both real and complex $\ell^1$ have the Schur property: weak convergence of sequences implies norm convergence ([[thm-ell-one-has-the-schur-property]]).

## Refutation

**Proof technique:** Assume the norm-open unit ball were weakly open; a finite basic weak neighborhood inside it would contain a nonzero common kernel and all its scalar multiples.

1.1 Let $B=\{x\in X:\|x\|_1<1\}$.  It is norm-open by [F2].  Suppose for contradiction that it is weakly open.  Since $0\in B$, [F1] supplies a finite basic weak neighborhood $$ U=\bigcap_{j=1}^{m}f_j^{-1}(V_j) $$ with $0\in U\subseteq B$, where $f_j\in X^*$ and each scalar-open $V_j$ contains zero.  The case $m=0$ means $U=X$ and already contradicts $U\subseteq B$, since $2e_0\notin B$. [F1, F2, F3, assume-contra]

2.1 Assume $m\ge1$.  Let $E=\operatorname{span}\{e_0,\ldots,e_m\}$ and define the linear map $$ A:E\longrightarrow\mathbb K^m, \qquad A(x)=(f_1(x),\ldots,f_m(x)). $$ The coordinate vectors are linearly independent by their explicit coordinates, so $\dim E=m+1$, whereas $\operatorname{rank}A\le m$.  Rank--nullity [F4] therefore gives a nonzero $v\in\ker A$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 For every scalar $\lambda$, each $f_j(\lambda v)=0\in V_j$, so $\lambda v\in U$.  Since $v\ne0$, choose the positive real scalar $\lambda=2/\|v\|_1$.  Absolute homogeneity gives $\|\lambda v\|_1=2$, hence $\lambda v\notin B$.  This contradicts $U\subseteq B$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, discharge-contradiction: step 1.1]

4.1 Thus the norm-open ball $B$ is not weakly open.  Since [F1] says the weak topology is contained in the norm topology, the containment is strict over both scalar fields. [step 1.1, step 3.1, F1]

5.1 Norm convergence implies weak convergence because the weak topology is coarser by [F1].  Conversely, [F5] turns every weakly convergent sequence in $X$ into a norm-convergent sequence.  The two topologies therefore have exactly the same convergent sequences even though step 4.1 proves that they are different. [F1, F5, step 4.1]

6.1 The witness uses $m+1$ explicit coordinate vectors for an arbitrary finite list of $m$ functionals, so it also covers one functional and a list containing zero or repeated functionals.  The empty list was handled in step 1.1.  Only finite-dimensional rank--nullity and one formula-defined rescaling are used; there is no choice principle, and no assertion about nets having the same convergence behavior is made. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 5.1, F3, F4] ∎
