## Statement

Assume countable choice. Retain the compact target, totally real boundary submanifolds, fixed path $J_t$ and exact product generator charts of the smooth relative-homotopy index lemma. A continuous Whitney representative from $x$ to $y$ is a continuous disk map with two specified boundary points mapped to $x,y$ and its two boundary arcs mapped into $L_0,L_1$. Equivalence means an actual continuous relative homotopy fixing those points and preserving the two boundary conditions.

Every such class has a smooth fixed-end strip representative, constant on full tails. If two smooth fixed-end representatives are continuously relatively homotopic, there is a smooth relative homotopy between them with one common pair of constant tails and the same fixed endpoints. Consequently the quotient of smooth fixed-end representatives by those smooth homotopies identifies with the continuous Whitney-class set. The actual real Fredholm index of its fixed-end Cauchy–Riemann-symbol linearization therefore defines an integer $\mu(\phi)$ on every continuous Whitney class; it agrees with the actual index of each smooth fixed-end model, is additive under class concatenation, is zero on the constant class, and changes sign under reversal.

For an actual smooth finite-energy holomorphic strip with uniform generator ends, its actual Fredholm index equals this index of its continuously compactified Whitney class.

This proves a continuous-class index using genuine representatives and relative homotopies. It does not identify that integer with a domain Euler measure, corner multiplicities or a first-Chern pairing; no nonlinear moduli counts or their regularity are asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual continuous representative or relative homotopy, the compact smooth target, the boundary submanifolds and fixed product charts, and, when endpoints of a homotopy are prescribed, its two actual smooth fixed-end maps.

[F1] [[lem-smooth-relative-whitney-homotopies-preserve-strip-fredholm-index]] proves actual fixed-end Fredholmness and smooth relative-homotopy invariance with common constant tails, connection independence, concatenation, constant and reversal index laws for the specified target data.

[F2] [[lem-relative-strip-maslov-determinant-excision-and-local-models]] identifies the actual Fredholm index of every smooth Cauchy–Riemann-symbol strip problem with exact product operator ends with its squared boundary-phase winding on a compact core, regardless of whether its underlying map is eventually constant.

## Proof

1.1 We first flatten a continuous disk map near its two marked boundary points. In a small half-disk source neighborhood of a marked point its image lies in the specified product target chart about its generator, by continuity. The target chart can be restricted to a small Euclidean ball about zero; its two boundary submanifolds there are the real planes $\mathbb R^m,i\mathbb R^m$. Choose a smooth source cutoff $\chi$ equal to zero on a smaller half-disk and one outside a larger one within this neighborhood. Replace the coordinate map $v$ by $\chi v$. The straight homotopy $v\mapsto(1-a+a\chi)v$ stays in the ball, fixes its generator value and agrees with the original outside the neighborhood. It preserves each boundary condition because real scalar multiplication preserves both real planes. The two source neighborhoods are disjoint, so these operations make the map constant near both marked points through an actual continuous relative homotopy. For a compact parameter family the neighborhoods and target ball can be chosen uniformly, by uniform continuity on the compact product. If its endpoint maps already are constant near the marked points, choose the modified neighborhoods inside those constant regions; the operation then fixes these endpoint maps exactly. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Here is the needed local finite approximation, with boundary preservation. In a source coordinate disk whose image lies in one target chart, write the continuous coordinate map as $v$. On a smaller relatively compact patch its convolution $v_\epsilon$ with an ordinary smooth radial nonnegative kernel of integral one is smooth and converges uniformly to $v$. Explicitly normalize the kernel $\exp(-1/(1-|z|^2))$ on $|z|<1$, zero outside; convolution differentiates its smooth compactly supported kernel, and uniform continuity bounds $|v_\epsilon-v|$ by the oscillation of $v$ at distances at most $\epsilon$. For a boundary half-disk lying on one specified $L_j$, use an adapted target chart taking $L_j$ to a linear coordinate subspace. Extend the tangential components of $v$ evenly across the source boundary and its normal components oddly. The extension is continuous because the normal components vanish on that boundary. Radial convolution leaves every normal component zero on the boundary by oddness. The same construction works with extra parameter coordinates: reflect only the source boundary-normal coordinate, and convolve all variables. The adapted charts used here exist by the submanifold coordinate definition; after step 1.1 no patch requiring smoothing meets a marked point where the boundary condition changes. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 To make a global approximation use only finitely many of these patches. Cover the remaining compact source set by smaller coordinate disks or boundary half-disks with larger patches whose target images lie in the chosen target charts. Choose smooth scalar cutoffs $\lambda$ supported in the larger patches and equal to one on the smaller ones. In one patch replace $v$ by $(1-\lambda)v+\lambda v_\epsilon$. Choose $\epsilon$ so small that the segment between these coordinate values stays in the target chart; compactness of the cutoff support and its positive distance from the chart boundary allow this. It preserves the boundary linear subspace in a boundary patch. It is smoothly defined on the entire smaller patch, since $\lambda=1$ there, and leaves every previously smooth source neighborhood smooth: both the old coordinate map and the new mollified map are smooth on such a neighborhood, and transitions between smooth target charts are smooth. Outside the support the map is unchanged. The replacement and its straight coordinate homotopy are continuous globally and preserve all boundary data. Apply this operation to the finitely many patches in order. The changes may be chosen successively uniformly small enough to retain the image condition in all patches still to be processed: use the minimum of their finitely many compact-image chart margins and a fixed finite upper bound on their coordinate distortion, shrinking their supports first if necessary. Thus after finitely many operations each point has been in a smaller patch on which the map became smooth and stayed smooth. The already constant marked-point neighborhoods can be excluded from all supports. This proves actual smooth approximation relative to those neighborhoods, through a continuous relative homotopy, without an infinite approximation process or an arbitrary family of choices. [step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Apply the same construction to a continuous relative homotopy whose two endpoint representatives are smooth. First precompose its parameter $h$ by a smooth function $b:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ equal to zero near zero and one near one, with $b(0)=0,b(1)=1$. This gives a homotopy constant in $h$ on endpoint collars and equal there to the prescribed smooth maps. It is continuously homotopic to the original through $h\mapsto(1-a)h+ab(h)$, relative to both endpoints. Use the uniform marked-point flattening of step 1.1 with neighborhoods inside the constant regions of the two endpoint maps. The resulting product map is smooth on collars of $h=0,1$ and on its two marked-point product neighborhoods. Smooth the remaining compact subset of the three-dimensional parameter-source product by steps 2.1–3.1. The supports can avoid smaller closed endpoint collars and smaller marked-point neighborhoods; all boundary patches still involve only one of $L_0,L_1$, and reflection in its source boundary-normal coordinate preserves it. Previously smooth neighborhoods remain smooth as in step 3.1. The result is an actual smooth homotopy on the whole compact product, fixed at its two endpoint maps and constant in one common neighborhood of each marked point. It retains the same fixed generators and boundary submanifolds throughout. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Identify the punctured disk with the strip by an explicit smooth conformal parametrization: $z=e^{\pi(s+it)}$ maps the open strip to the upper half-plane, and $(z-i)/(z+i)$ maps that half-plane to the disk, taking $s=-\infty,+\infty$ to $-1,1$. Compose with a fixed disk Möbius map to send these to the prescribed marked points $a,b$: the Cayley map $w\mapsto i(b+w)/(b-w)$ sends $b$ to infinity, and subtracting its real value at $a$ sends $a$ to zero; its inverse composed with the standard Cayley map gives the required disk automorphism. Precomposing the constructed smooth disk map gives a smooth strip map, constant on full tails because the disk map is constant on neighborhoods of those marked points. The same conversion of step 4.1 has common tails by its common neighborhoods. Conversely an eventually constant smooth strip map extends smoothly across both marked disk points by its constant values, and a smooth common-tail homotopy extends likewise. Steps 1.1–3.1 therefore give surjectivity from smooth fixed-end classes to continuous Whitney classes, and step 4.1 gives injectivity. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Define $\mu(\phi)$ to be the actual index of any smooth fixed-end model supplied by step 5.1. The injectivity assertion there and [F1] prove independence of that model and of its connection. For concatenation, take smooth models of the two classes and join their constant matching tails. Their joined map is a representative of the topological concatenation: the usual disk gluing along the common marked end can be effected inside the constant neighborhoods, and changing the finite length of a constant strip only reparametrizes such a collar. Its index is the sum by [F1]. A constant class has its constant model, with index zero by [F1]. Reversing $s$ gives a smooth model of the reversed class and negates the index by [F1]. Thus every asserted law holds for the continuous Whitney-class quotient itself. The construction used actual finite chart operations and actual homotopies; it has not postulated a smooth representative or imported a full-choice approximation theorem. No step computed a domain Euler, corner or Chern formula or a nonlinear moduli count. [F1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally take the actual holomorphic strip in the additional assertion. Uniform ends put its two sufficiently remote tails in the fixed product generator charts. On those tails its coordinate linearization is exactly $D_0$ and its boundary tangent planes are exactly the two fixed product planes. Multiply each tail coordinate map by a smooth real cutoff, equal to one on an inner part of that tail and zero farther out. This preserves both boundary planes, agrees with the strip on a common compact core, and gives an eventually constant smooth model of its continuous compactification; the straight scaling homotopy proves the relative-class assertion. Both actual bundle operators have exactly the same complex bundle, boundary planes and operator on that core, and exact $D_0$ in the specified frames outside it. Their boundary-phase loops can consequently be computed on the same core rectangle with the same vertical caps, and have the same winding. The actual Fredholm index formula in [F2] therefore identifies their indices. The model index is $\mu$ of its continuous class by step 6.1, proving the asserted agreement with the actual holomorphic linearization. No claim that the cutoff map remains holomorphic was needed or made. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra] ∎
