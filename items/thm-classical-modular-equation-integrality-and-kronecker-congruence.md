## Statement

For every integer $m\ge2$, the classical modular equation has
$\Phi_m(X,Y)\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]$ and is monic in $X$. If $m$ is not a square,
then $\Phi_m(X,X)$ is a nonconstant polynomial whose leading coefficient is
$1$ or $-1$. For every prime $p$,
$$\Phi_p(X,Y)\equiv (X^p-Y)(X-Y^p)\pmod {p\mathbb Z[X,Y]}.$$

[[def-classical-modular-equation-for-cyclic-isogenies|The polynomial is the product over cyclic sublattices of index $m$.]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge2$, the level-one modular function $j$, and the product definition of $\Phi_m$.

[F1] [[def-modular-discriminant-and-j-invariant]]: $j=q^{-1}+744+O(q)$, is invariant under $SL_2(\mathbb Z)$, and is holomorphic on $\mathfrak H$.

[F2] [[lem-jacobi-product-formula-for-the-discriminant]] and [[thm-eisenstein-series-are-modular-forms]]: the $q$-expansions of $\Delta$ and $E_4$ have integer coefficients, and $\Delta=q+O(q^2)$; consequently the full $q$-expansion of $j=E_4^3/\Delta$ lies in $\mathbb Z((q))$.

[F3] [[thm-q-expansion-principle-at-the-cusp]]: an identity of level-one modular functions that is verified by its Laurent expansion at the cusp is an identity of the functions.

[F4] [[thm-level-one-modular-functions-with-only-cusp-poles-are-polynomials-in-j]]: every level-one modular function holomorphic on the upper half-plane and meromorphic only at the cusp is a polynomial in $j$.

[F5] [[thm-j-invariant-classifies-complex-tori]]: changing a lattice basis or replacing a lattice by a homothetic lattice preserves $j$.

## Proof

1.1 The cyclic sublattices of index $m$ have representatives with normalized parameter $(a\tau+b)/d$, where $ad=m$, $0\le b<d$, and $\gcd(a,b,d)=1$. A change of basis permutes these sublattices, and [F5] preserves the associated $j$-values, so each elementary symmetric coefficient of the product defining $\Phi_m(X,j(\tau))$ is a level-one modular function, holomorphic on $\mathfrak H$ and meromorphic only at the cusp. By [F4], each coefficient is a polynomial in $j$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, given, algebra]

1.2 Let $p$ be prime. Its cyclic sublattices are represented by $p\tau$ and $(\tau+b)/p$ for $0\le b<p$. Modulo the prime ideal generated by $1-\zeta_p$, the latter $p$ expansions agree, since each coefficient differs by a power of $\zeta_p$ and $\zeta_p\equiv1$. The integral $q$-series of $j$ satisfies $j(q^p)\equiv j(q)^p\pmod p$ and $j(q^{1/p})^p\equiv j(q)\pmod p$, by the binomial theorem and $c^p\equiv c\pmod p$ for every integer coefficient $c$. Multiplying the factors gives the asserted product congruence after reduction modulo $1-\zeta_p$. [F2, given, algebra]

2.1 In the expansions at the cusp, every factor $j((a\tau+b)/d)$ has coefficients in a cyclotomic integer ring and powers in $q^{1/m}$. Galois automorphisms of that cyclotomic ring send $b$ to $cb\pmod d$ for $c\in(\mathbb Z/d)^\times$, permuting the representatives; hence each symmetric coefficient has rational algebraic-integer Laurent coefficients, which are ordinary integers, and only integral powers of $q$ by its invariance under $\tau\mapsto\tau+1$. If its polynomial in $j$ had a nonintegral coefficient, subtracting the already determined higher-degree integer multiples of $j^r$ successively would give a nonintegral coefficient at the first remaining pole order, contradicting the integer Laurent expansion from [F2]. Thus all coefficients lie in $\mathbb Z[j]$, proving $\Phi_m\in\mathbb Z[X,Y]$; the product is monic in $X$ by definition. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $m$ is nonsquare. In each product factor $j-j((a\tau+b)/d)$, the two leading pole orders differ because $a/d\ne1$. The leading coefficient of the factor is therefore either $1$ or a root of unity; the product's leading coefficient is a root of unity. By step 2.1 the diagonal specialization is an integer polynomial in $j$, so the coefficient of its highest pole is an integer. An integer that is a root of unity is $1$ or $-1$, and the product is nonzero because none of the factors has equal leading pole order. Since $j=q^{-1}+O(1)$, this pole coefficient is the leading coefficient of the nonconstant polynomial $\Phi_m(X,X)$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The coefficients of the difference in the claimed congruence are rational integers by steps 1.1–1.2. The contraction to $\mathbb Z$ of the ideal $(1-\zeta_p)$ in $\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]$ is $p\mathbb Z$, because the quotient map $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z[\zeta_p]/(1-\zeta_p)$ has kernel $(p)$. Thus those integer coefficients are divisible by $p$. The $q$-expansion principle [F3] lifts the coefficientwise congruence to $\mathbb Z[X,Y]$, proving the Kronecker congruence. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
