## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a field, let $\Omega/K$ be an algebraic closure, let $K^s=\{x\in\Omega:x\text{ is separable over }K\}$, and let $G_K=\operatorname{Aut}_K(K^s)$. For an abelian variety $A/K$, the points fixed by the natural $G_K$-action on $A(K^s)$ are exactly the points in the image of $A(K)$.

## Facts & Assumptions


**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a field $K$, an algebraic closure $\Omega/K$, its relative separable closure $K^s$, the group $G_K=\operatorname{Aut}_K(K^s)$, and an abelian variety $A/K$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] [[def-separable-closure-in-an-algebraic-extension]]: $K^s$ is the subfield of $\Omega$ consisting of elements separable over $K$.

[F2] [[thm-finitely-generated-algebraic-extensions-are-finite]]: a field generated by finitely many algebraic elements is finite over its base.

[F3] [[thm-separability-is-transitive]]: separability in an algebraic tower is transitive.

[F4] [[thm-galois-closures-exist-and-are-minimal]]: a finite separable subextension of an algebraic closure is contained in a finite Galois extension of the base.

[F5] [[thm-algebraic-embedding-extension]]: under Choice, an embedding of a base field into an algebraically closed field extends across any algebraic extension of its domain.

[F6] [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]: for a finite Galois extension $E/K$, the fixed field of $\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$ is $K$.

[F7] [[thm-abelian-variety-is-projective]]: under Choice, an abelian variety over $K$ admits a closed immersion into some projective space $\mathbf P^N_K$.

## Proof


1.1 To see that $K^s$ is separably closed in $\Omega$, let $z\in\Omega$ be separable over $K^s$ and let its minimal polynomial over $K^s$ have coefficients $c_1,\ldots,c_r$. The field $E=K(c_1,\ldots,c_r)$ is finite over $K$ by [F2], and $E\subseteq K^s$ by [F1]. The polynomial is separable over $K^s$, hence also over $E$, so $z$ is separable over $E$. Since $E/K$ is separable by [F1], [F3] makes $z/K$ separable, and therefore $z\in K^s$. As $\Omega$ is algebraically closed, every separable polynomial over $K^s$ has all its roots in $\Omega$; the argument just given puts every such root in $K^s$, so $K^s$ is separably closed. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 Let $E/K$ be any finite Galois subextension of $K^s$, and take $\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$. The extension $K^s/E$ is algebraic, so [F5] extends $\tau:E\to\Omega$ to a $K$-embedding $\widetilde\tau:K^s\to\Omega$. Every element of $K^s$ is separable over $K$, and a $K$-embedding preserves its minimal polynomial; hence $S:=\widetilde\tau(K^s)\subseteq K^s$. [A1, F1, F5, given, construct]

1.3 For $a\in K^s$, the extension $K(a)/K$ is finite by [F2] and separable by [F1]. By [F4] it is contained in a finite Galois extension $E/K$ inside $\Omega$; since $E/K$ is separable, the definition of $K^s$ gives $E\subseteq K^s$. [F1, F2, F4, given, choose]

2.1 The field $S$ is isomorphic to the separably closed field $K^s$ from step 1.1. For any $y\in K^s$, its minimal polynomial over $K$ is separable and remains separable over $S$; the minimal polynomial of $y$ over $S$ divides that polynomial. Thus $y$ is separable over $S$, and separable closedness gives $y\in S$. Consequently $S=K^s$, so $\widetilde\tau$ is an automorphism extending $\tau$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 If $a$ is fixed by every element of $G_K$, then every $\tau\in\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)$ fixes $a$: step 2.1 extends $\tau$ to an element of $G_K$. By [F6], $E^{\operatorname{Gal}(E/K)}=K$, so $a\in K$. Conversely every element of $K$ is fixed. Therefore $(K^s)^{G_K}=K$. [F4, F6, step 2.1, step 1.3, given]

4.1 Use [F7] under Choice [A1] to view $A$ as a closed subscheme of $\mathbf P^N_K$. For a fixed point $P\in A(K^s)$, choose homogeneous coordinates $[x_0:\cdots:x_N]$ and an index $j$ with $x_j\ne0$. Since the embedding is defined over $K$ and $P$ is fixed, every ratio $x_i/x_j$ is fixed by $G_K$; step 3.1 puts each ratio in $K$. These coordinates define a $K$-point of $\mathbf P^N_K$. The closed-immersion equations for $A$ vanish after extension to $K^s$ because the point is $P$; injectivity of $K\hookrightarrow K^s$ makes them vanish over $K$, so this point factors through $A$. Thus $P$ comes from $A(K)$. Every $K$-point is fixed, proving the assertion. [A1, F7, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
