## Definition

Let $A\in M_{m\times n}(R)$ over a principal ideal domain $R$. For $k\ge1$,
the **$k$-th determinantal ideal** $D_k(A)$ is the ideal generated by the
determinants of all $k\times k$ minors of $A$
([[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]], [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).
Set $D_0(A)=R$. If $k>\min(m,n)$, there are no such minors and $D_k(A)=(0)$.

Since $R$ is a PID ([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]), write
$D_k(A)=(\Delta_k(A))$. The associate class of $\Delta_k(A)$ is the **$k$-th
determinantal divisor**. Thus $\Delta_0(A)$ is a unit up to associates, while a
vanishing determinantal ideal has divisor $0$.
