## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity, $\delta\in R$, and $n\ge0$. Write $B_{n,k}(T)\in\mathbb Z[T]$ for the quantum-binomial polynomial of [[lem-temperley-lieb-quantum-binomial-digit-criterion]]. A normalized Jones–Wenzl projector in $\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$ exists if and only if every $B_{n,k}(\delta)$, $0\le k\le n$, is a unit in $R$. Here normalization means that the identity-diagram coefficient is $1$ and $d_ie=0$ for all $1\le i<n$. Such an element is automatically idempotent, annihilated by every $d_i$ on both sides, and unique. If it exists, its image under every homomorphism of coefficient rings preserving the loop parameter is the unique normalized projector over the target ring. No hypothesis of reducedness, being a domain, characteristic zero, or invertibility of all individual quantum integers is required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The commutative ring, loop parameter and rank in the Statement.

[F1] Diagram modules are free in their noncrossing-diagram bases, composition is associative and cannot increase propagation, and every endomorphism diagram is a word in the elementary generators ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]]).

[F2] Over $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$ the word-space representation has an $A$-linear left inverse that survives every base change. It preserves the number of ones and represents each generator by the symmetric middle block $\begin{pmatrix}q&1\\1&q^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$ ([[lem-temperley-lieb-integral-spin-representation-is-split-injective]]).

[F3] For the inversion polynomial $G_{n,k}(Q)=\sum_wQ^{\operatorname{inv}(w)}$, one has $G_{n,k}(q^2)=q^{k(n-k)}B_{n,k}(q+q^{-1})$. Each of these polynomials is nonzero before specialization ([[lem-temperley-lieb-quantum-binomial-digit-criterion]]).

[F4] Over a field, nonvanishing of the entire row $B_{n,k}(\delta)$ gives a normalized two-sided Jones–Wenzl projector ([[lem-temperley-lieb-jones-wenzl-quantum-binomial-criterion-over-fields]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The specified $R,\delta,n$ and the identity-coefficient map $\varepsilon$.

1.1 By propagation in [F1], the span of the nonidentity diagrams is an ideal, so $\varepsilon$ is a ring homomorphism; that ideal is spanned by nonempty generator words. Thus the linear equations $d_ie=0$, $\varepsilon(e)=1$ imply $ae=\varepsilon(a)e$ for every $a$, and in particular $e^2=e$. Horizontal reflection defines an anti-involution $*$ fixing generators and preserving $\varepsilon$. The element $f=ee^*$ is killed by the generators on both sides and has augmentation $1$. Right annihilation gives $fe=f$, while left annihilation of $e$ gives $fe=e$; hence $e=f$ is two-sided. For two such elements $e,e'$, the same argument gives $ee'=e$ by right annihilation of $e$ and $ee'=e'$ by left annihilation of $e'$. They are equal. These arguments use no division and work over every commutative coefficient ring. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 First work over any commutative ring $C$ containing a unit $q$ with $\delta=q+q^{-1}$. In the weight-$k$ word module of [F2], the equations for the common kernel are $a_{u10v}=-q a_{u01v}$. Successive adjacent swaps reduce each word to $0^{n-k}1^k$ and change its inversion number by one. Thus this kernel is exactly the free line generated by $v_k=\sum_w(-q)^{\operatorname{inv}(w)}w$, whose baseword coefficient is $1$. If $e$ is normalized, step 1.1 and symmetry in [F2] put both the columns and rows of its weight block in this line. First comparing the baseword column, and then each row, expresses the block as $\alpha_kv_kv_k^{\mathsf T}$ for some $\alpha_k\in C$. Every nonempty generator word kills $v_k$ by its rightmost factor; consequently $(e-1)v_k=0$. Comparing the baseword coefficient in $ev_k=v_k$ gives $\alpha_kG_{n,k}(q^2)=1$. In particular all these Gaussian values are units. This conclusion remains valid with nilpotents or zero divisors. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For general $R$, put $R'=R[q]/(q^2-\delta q+1)$. Division by this monic quadratic gives a unique representative $a+bq$: subtract successive leading terms to prove existence, while a nonzero multiple of a monic polynomial has degree at least two, proving uniqueness. Thus $R'=R\oplus Rq$ as an $R$-module, $R$ injects into $R'$, and $q$ is a unit with inverse $\delta-q$. Base-changing an existing $e$ to $R'$ and applying step 2.1 makes $G_{n,k}(q^2)$ a unit. By [F3] the factor $q^{k(n-k)}$ is a unit, so $B=B_{n,k}(\delta)$ is a unit in $R'$. Write its inverse $a+bq$. The equality $B(a+bq)=1$ in the indicated free basis gives $Ba=1$ in $R$. Hence $B$ is already a unit in $R$. [F1, F3, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 To prove sufficiency construct a universal projector with only the named denominators. Use an indeterminate $q$, $A=\mathbb Z[q,q^{-1}]$, and its fraction field $L=\mathbb Q(q)$. Facts [F3, F4] give a normalized projector $p\in\mathrm{TL}_n(L,q+q^{-1})$. Step 2.1 determines each of its weight blocks as $v_kv_k^{\mathsf T}/G_{n,k}(q^2)$. Let $S=A[G_{n,k}(q^2)^{-1}:0\le k\le n]\subset L$. That matrix has entries in $S$. Apply the base-changed linear left inverse of [F2] to obtain a diagram-coefficient vector $z\in\mathrm{TL}_n(S,q+q^{-1})$. After extension to $L$ it equals $p$. Since $S$ injects into $L$ and the diagram module is free, the same identities already hold over $S$: $\varepsilon(z)=1$, $d_iz=zd_i=0$ and $z^2=z$. Thus no unproved assertion about the image or centralizer of a quantum-group action is needed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Suppose now every $B_{n,k}(\delta)$ is a unit in $R$, and form $R'$ as in step 3.1. Fact [F3] makes all evaluated $G_{n,k}(q^2)$ units in $R'$. Therefore evaluation defines a ring homomorphism $S\to R'$: send a fraction $a/\prod G_{n,k}^{e_k}$ to the evaluated numerator times the inverses of the indicated evaluated denominators; the cross-multiplied equality defining equal fractions remains true after evaluation. Specialize $z$ from step 3.2 to get a normalized annihilator $e'\in\mathrm{TL}_n(R',\delta)$. Let $\pi:R'\to R$ be the $R$-linear coefficient projection $\pi(a+bq)=a$ and apply $\pi$ to every diagram coefficient of $e'$, obtaining $e\in\mathrm{TL}_n(R,\delta)$. Left multiplication by each $d_i$ has matrix entries in $R$, so it commutes with this coefficient projection. Hence $d_ie=0$ and $\varepsilon(e)=\pi(1)=1$. Step 1.1 now makes $e$ the required unique two-sided idempotent. The projection is used only on these linear equations; it need not preserve multiplication. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 Any homomorphism $R\to T$ sends the diagram coefficients and their defining linear identities to those over $T$, so the image of the projector is the unique projector there by step 1.1. For $n=0,1$ there are no generator equations, the identity is the projector, and every binomial in the row is $1$. The same argument includes the zero ring under the convention that its identity is its unique element. This establishes all stated implications and boundaries. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
