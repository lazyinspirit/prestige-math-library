## Example

Let $k$ be any field and let $n\ge1$. Write
$$R:=k[x_{ij}:(i,j)\in n\times n]$$
for the polynomial ring on the $n^2$ indeterminates $x_{ij}$, let
$X:=(x_{ij})\in M_n(R)$ be the generic $n\times n$ matrix, and let
$d:=\det(X)$ and $\operatorname{tr}(X)$ be its Leibniz determinant and its
trace over the commutative ring $R$. Put
$$S:=R/(d-1),\qquad T:=R_d=S_d^{-1}R,\qquad S_d:=\{d^m:m\in\mathbb N\},$$
so that $S$ is the quotient by the principal ideal generated by $d-1$ and $T$
is the principal localisation of $R$ inverting $d$. Let
$$\operatorname{SL}_n:=\operatorname{Spec}S,\qquad \operatorname{GL}_n:=\operatorname{Spec}T$$
be the affine $k$-schemes presented by the determinant condition and by the
invertibility of the determinant, and let $e$ be the identity point of either
scheme, induced by the evaluation $\operatorname{ev}(x_{ij})=(I_n)_{ij}$ at the
identity matrix. Then
$$T_e\operatorname{GL}_n\cong M_n(k),\qquad T_e\operatorname{SL}_n\cong\{B\in M_n(k):\operatorname{tr}(B)=0\},$$
where $T_e$ denotes the intrinsic Zariski tangent space at the $k$-point $e$.
These are tangent-space computations only: no Lie bracket and no Lie-algebra
structure on either tangent space is asserted or used, and no characteristic,
perfectness or reducedness hypothesis is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ and an integer $n\ge1$; the polynomial ring $R=k[x_{ij}:(i,j)\in n\times n]$ on the finite family of $n^2$ indeterminates; the generic matrix $X=(x_{ij})\in M_n(R)$ with determinant $d=\det(X)$ and trace $\operatorname{tr}(X)$; the principal ideal $(d-1)\subseteq R$; the quotient $S=R/(d-1)$ with coset map $\pi:R\to S$; the multiplicative set $S_d=\{d^m:m\in\mathbb N\}$ and the principal localisation $T=R_d=S_d^{-1}R$ with localisation map $\lambda:R\to T$; the affine $k$-schemes $\operatorname{SL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}S$ and $\operatorname{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}T$; the evaluation homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}:R\to k$ with $\operatorname{ev}(x_{ij})=(I_n)_{ij}$; the dual-number ring $D=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ with reduction $\rho:D\to k$ sending $\epsilon$ to zero; and the identity matrix $I_n\in M_n(k)$.


[F1] [[lem-tangent-vectors-as-dual-number-points]]: for a $k$-scheme $X$ and a $k$-rational point $x\in X(k)$, the intrinsic tangent space $T_xX$ is naturally isomorphic, as a $k$-vector space, to the fibre over $x$ of the based dual-number points $\operatorname{Hom}_k(\operatorname{Spec}D,X)\to X(k)$, and equivalently to $\operatorname{Der}_k(\mathcal O_{X,x},k)$; the bijection writes a local $k$-algebra map as $a\mapsto a(x)+\epsilon D(a)$.

[F2] [[def-dual-numbers-scheme]]: the dual-numbers scheme is $D_k=\operatorname{Spec}(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2))$, whose class $\epsilon$ is nilpotent; thus $D=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^2)$ is a commutative $k$-algebra in which every element has a unique form $c+\epsilon b$ with $c,b\in k$, and $\epsilon^2=0$.

[F3] [[def-scheme-over-base]]: a $k$-morphism is a morphism commuting with the structure maps to $\operatorname{Spec}k$.

[F4] [[thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]]: ring maps between commutative rings correspond contravariantly to morphisms of affine schemes, so $k$-algebra maps $A\to k$ are the $k$-points of $\operatorname{Spec}A$.

[F5] [[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family]]: a ring homomorphism $R_0\to S_0$ and a family $(s_i)$ in $S_0$ determine a unique ring homomorphism $R_0[x_i:i\in I]\to S_0$ restricting to it and sending $x_i\mapsto s_i$.

[F6] [[thm-quotient-ring-universal-property]]: a ring homomorphism whose kernel contains a two-sided ideal factors uniquely through the quotient ring.

[F7] [[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]]: a unital homomorphism $f:R_0\to A$ of commutative rings that sends every element of a multiplicative subset $S_0$ to a unit factors uniquely as $\tilde f\circ\lambda_{S_0}$ through $S_0^{-1}R_0$, with $\tilde f(r/s)=f(r)f(s)^{-1}$.

[F8] [[def-quotient-ring]]: the quotient ring $R_0/I$ is formed from the additive cosets $r+I$ with $(r+I)(s+I)=rs+I$, so the coset map $R_0\to R_0/I$ is a surjective ring homomorphism.

[F9] [[def-principal-localisation]]: for $f\in R_0$ the powers $S_f=\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}=\{f^n:n\in\mathbb N\}$ form a multiplicative subset, the principal localisation is $R_f=S_f^{-1}R_0$, and its elements may be written $r/f^n$.

[F10] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: for a subset $S_0$ of a ring, $(S_0)$ is the smallest two-sided ideal containing it, and for an element $a$ the ideal $(\{a\})$ is written $(a)$ and called principal.

[F11] [[def-matrices-over-a-commutative-ring]]: an $m\times n$ matrix over a commutative ring is a function $m\times n\to R_0$ with value $a_{ij}$ at $(i,j)$, and $M_n(R_0):=M_{n\times n}(R_0)$.

[F12] [[def-ring-matrix-product-identity-and-transpose]]: matrices are added and scaled entrywise, $(AB)_{ik}=\sum_{j<n}a_{ij}b_{jk}$, the identity matrix $I_n$ has entry $1$ on the diagonal and $0$ elsewhere, and transpose is $(A^{\mathsf T})_{ji}=a_{ij}$.

[F13] [[def-trace-of-a-square-matrix-over-a-commutative-ring]]: the trace of $A=(a_{ij})\in M_p(R_0)$ is $\operatorname{tr}_R(A)=\sum_{i<p}a_{ii}$, the empty sum being $0$.

[F14] [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]: for $n\ge1$ and a commutative ring $R_0$ the determinant is the finite Leibniz sum $\det(A)=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i<n}a_{\sigma(i),i}$, the signs acting through the ring identities.

[F15] [[thm-leibniz-determinant-is-alternating-multilinear-and-normalized]]: over every commutative ring and $n\ge1$ the Leibniz determinant is column-multilinear, alternating and normalized.

[F16] [[def-multilinear-alternating-normalized-and-antisymmetric-matrix-functions]]: column-multilinear means additive and scalar-compatible in each selected column with the other columns fixed; alternating means vanishing whenever two columns are equal; normalized means the value $1$ at $I_n$.

[F17] [[thm-ring-matrix-arithmetic-laws]]: over a commutative ring, matrix multiplication is associative, $I_n$ is a two-sided identity, and multiplication distributes over addition on both sides.

[F18] [[def-invertible-matrix-and-similarity-over-a-commutative-ring]]: a matrix $A\in M_n(R_0)$ is invertible when there is $B$ with $AB=I_n=BA$, the inverse is unique, and the general linear group over $R_0$ is the set of such matrices.

[F19] [[cor-invertible-matrix-has-unit-determinant]]: for $n\ge1$, if $A\in M_n(R_0)$ is invertible over the commutative ring $R_0$, then $\det(A)$ is a unit of $R_0$, and $\det(A^{-1})$ is its inverse.

[F20] [[lem-ring-units-form-a-group]]: the units of a commutative ring form a group under multiplication, so products of units are units and inverses are unique.

[F21] [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]: every field is a commutative ring with $1\ne0$.

[F22] [[def-ring-homomorphism]]: a ring homomorphism is additive and multiplicative and sends $1$ to $1$, hence preserves finite sums, finite products and the values of the determinant and trace polynomials.




## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Setup at the identity: $k$ is a commutative ring [F21], so $R$ is a commutative $k$-algebra on which the family $(x_{ij})$ is tautological; the generic matrix $X=(x_{ij})$ and its Leibniz determinant $d=\sum_{\sigma\in S_n}\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma)\prod_{i<n}x_{\sigma(i),i}$ and trace $\operatorname{tr}(X)=\sum_{i<n}x_{ii}$ are elements of $R$ [F11, F13, F14]; $S=R/(d-1)$ is the quotient by the principal ideal generated by $d-1$ [F8, F10]; the evaluation $\operatorname{ev}:R\to k$ with $\operatorname{ev}(x_{ij})=(I_n)_{ij}$ exists and is the unique such $k$-algebra map [F5]; and $\operatorname{ev}(d)=\det(I_n)=1$, because $\operatorname{ev}$, being a unital ring homomorphism [F22], sends the finite Leibniz sum of monomials in the indeterminates to the same sum evaluated at the entries of $I_n$ [F14], and the Leibniz determinant is normalized [F15, F16]; consequently $\operatorname{ev}(d-1)=0$, $\operatorname{ev}(d^m)=1$ for every $m\in\mathbb N$, and $d\ne0$ because $\operatorname{ev}(d)=1\ne0$. [given, F5, F8, F10, F11, F13, F14, F15, F16, F21, F22, algebra]

1.2 The identity points: the quotient universal property [F6] applied to $\operatorname{ev}$ and the ideal $(d-1)$ gives a unique ring homomorphism $e_S:S\to k$ with $e_S\circ\pi=\operatorname{ev}$; the localisation universal property [F7] applied to $\operatorname{ev}$ and the multiplicative set $S_d$ [F9] gives a unique ring homomorphism $e_T:T\to k$ with $e_T\circ\lambda=\operatorname{ev}$, its hypothesis holding because $\operatorname{ev}(d^m)=\operatorname{ev}(d)^m=1$ is a unit of $k$ for every $m$ [F20]; both are $k$-algebra maps, since the $k$-algebra structures of $S$ and $T$ are induced by $k\to R$ followed by $\pi$ and $\lambda$ and $e_S\circ\pi=\operatorname{ev}=e_T\circ\lambda$ restricts to the identity on $k$ [F22]; by the anti-equivalence [F4] these $k$-algebra maps $S\to k$ and $T\to k$ are $k$-points $e$ of $\operatorname{SL}_n$ and of $\operatorname{GL}_n$, the identity points of the two schemes, and $T_e$ denotes the intrinsic tangent space at these points [F1, F3]. [given, F1, F3, F4, F6, F7, F9, F20, F22, algebra]

1.3 The determinant of $I_n+\epsilon B$: fix $B\in M_n(k)$ and put $Y:=I_n+\epsilon B\in M_n(D)$, so that $Y_{ij}=(I_n)_{ij}+\epsilon b_{ij}$ by the entrywise rules [F2, F11, F12]; the $j$-th column of $Y$ is the sum of the $j$-th standard basis column $e_j$ of $I_n$ and the $\epsilon$-multiple of the $j$-th column $B^{(j)}$ of $B$; iterating the column-additivity of the determinant through the $n$ columns [F15, F16] and discarding every term containing the factor $\epsilon^2=0$ [F2] gives $\det(Y)=\det(I_n)+\epsilon\sum_{j<n}\det(A_j)$, where $A_j$ is obtained from $I_n$ by replacing column $j$ by $B^{(j)}$; expanding $B^{(j)}=\sum_{i<n}b_{ij}e_i$ in that column by column-additivity [F15, F16], every term with $i\ne j$ has the column $e_i$ occurring twice and has determinant $0$ by alternation, while the term $i=j$ is $I_n$ and has determinant $1$ by normalization [F15, F16]; therefore $\det(A_j)=b_{jj}$ and $\det(I_n+\epsilon B)=1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ with $\operatorname{tr}(B)=\sum_{j<n}b_{jj}$ [F13]; replacing $B$ by $-B$ and using the entrywise identity $\operatorname{tr}(-B)=-\operatorname{tr}(B)$ gives $\det(I_n-\epsilon B)=1-\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$. [given, F2, F11, F12, F13, F15, F16, algebra]

2.1 The determinant of $I_n+\epsilon B$ is a unit: by associativity, distributivity and the identity laws for matrices over the commutative ring $D$ [F17], $(I_n+\epsilon B)(I_n-\epsilon B)=I_n+\epsilon B-\epsilon B-\epsilon^2B^2=I_n$, because $\epsilon^2=0$ [F2]; hence $I_n+\epsilon B$ is invertible in $M_n(D)$ with inverse $I_n-\epsilon B$ [F18]; the invertible-matrix corollary [F19] then makes $\det(I_n+\epsilon B)$ a unit of $D$ whose inverse is $\det(I_n-\epsilon B)$, so by step 1.3 the element $1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ is a unit of $D$ with inverse $1-\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$, for every $B\in M_n(k)$. [step 1.3, F2, F17, F18, F19, algebra]

2.2 The tangent space of $\operatorname{SL}_n$: by [F1] applied to the $k$-scheme $\operatorname{SL}_n$ and its $k$-point $e$ of step 1.2, $T_e\operatorname{SL}_n$ is $k$-linearly isomorphic to the based dual-number points, that is to the $k$-algebra maps $\varphi:S\to D$ with $\rho\circ\varphi=e_S$ [F2, F3, F4]; by the quotient universal property [F6] such $\varphi$ correspond bijectively to the $k$-algebra maps $\psi:R\to D$ with $\rho\circ\psi=\operatorname{ev}$ and $\psi(d-1)=0$; by the polynomial universal property [F5] such $\psi$ correspond bijectively to families $(y_{ij})\in D^{n\times n}$, that is to matrices $Y\in M_n(D)$ [F11], and $\psi(d)=\det(Y)$ because $\psi$ is a unital ring homomorphism [F22] applied to the Leibniz formula [F14]; the reduction condition says that $y_{ij}$ has constant term $(I_n)_{ij}$, so by the unique form $c+\epsilon b$ of elements of $D$ [F2] one has $Y=I_n+\epsilon B$ for a unique $B\in M_n(k)$ [F11, F12]; the condition $\psi(d-1)=0$ says $\det(Y)=1$, which by step 1.3 reads $1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)=1$, equivalently $\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)=0$, equivalently $\operatorname{tr}(B)=0$ by the uniqueness of that form [F2]; the assignment $B\mapsto\varphi_B$ is $k$-linear, since $\epsilon(b+b')=\epsilon b+\epsilon b'$ and $\epsilon(cb)=c\,\epsilon b$ in $D$ [F2], so under the $k$-linear isomorphism of [F1] we obtain $T_e\operatorname{SL}_n\cong\{B\in M_n(k):\operatorname{tr}(B)=0\}$, both directions being proved: every based dual-number point yields such a $B$, and every such $B$ yields a based dual-number point. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F11, F12, F14, F22, step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 The tangent space of $\operatorname{GL}_n$: by [F1] applied to the $k$-scheme $\operatorname{GL}_n$ and its $k$-point $e$ of step 1.2, $T_e\operatorname{GL}_n$ is $k$-linearly isomorphic to the based dual-number points, that is to the $k$-algebra maps $\varphi:T\to D$ with $\rho\circ\varphi=e_T$ [F2, F3, F4]; by the localisation universal property [F7] such $\varphi$ correspond bijectively to the $k$-algebra maps $\psi:R\to D$ with $\rho\circ\psi=\operatorname{ev}$ and $\psi(d)$ a unit of $D$, the further conditions that each $\psi(d^m)=\psi(d)^m$ be a unit being automatic because units form a group [F20]; as in step 2.2 such $\psi$ correspond bijectively to the matrices $Y=I_n+\epsilon B$ with $B\in M_n(k)$ unique [F5, F11, F12], and $\psi(d)=\det(Y)=1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ [F14, F22, step 1.3], which by step 2.1 is a unit of $D$ for every $B\in M_n(k)$, with the explicit inverse $1-\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$; hence the unit condition excludes no matrix and the correspondence is a $k$-linear isomorphism $T_e\operatorname{GL}_n\cong M_n(k)$, the inverse of $1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ being exhibited explicitly. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F7, F11, F12, F14, F20, F22, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion: for every field $k$ and every $n\ge1$, the identity point $e$ of $\operatorname{SL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x_{ij}]/(\det(X)-1))$ has $T_e\operatorname{SL}_n\cong\{B\in M_n(k):\operatorname{tr}(B)=0\}$, the trace-zero matrices, and the identity point of $\operatorname{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x_{ij}]_{\det})$ has $T_e\operatorname{GL}_n\cong M_n(k)$, the full matrix space; the zero matrix corresponds to the identity map $\psi=\operatorname{ev}$ in both computations, so it is the zero tangent vector, and no Lie bracket, Lie-algebra structure, characteristic hypothesis or choice principle is involved. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Boundary and scope dispositions: neither scheme is empty and no list here is empty, because $n\ge1$ makes $n\times n$ nonempty, the identity matrix $I_n$ exists, step 1.2 exhibits the $k$-points $e$, and the families $(x_{ij})$, $(y_{ij})$ and the generating list $\{d-1\}$ of the principal ideal are nonempty (steps 1.1 and 1.2); the zero cases are the zero matrix $B=0$, which gives $\psi=\operatorname{ev}$ with $\det(I_n)=1$ and $\operatorname{tr}(0)=0$ by [F13], the vanishing $\operatorname{ev}(d-1)=0$ of step 1.1, and the zero element of $M_n(k)$, while $M_n(k)$ is nonzero for every $n\ge1$, and the trace-zero space is zero for $n=1$ and nonzero for $n\ge2$ (it contains the nonzero off-diagonal matrix unit $E_{01}$); there is one determinant $d$, one equation $d-1$, one localisation $T=R_d$, one trace functional and one correspondence per scheme, each with two directions (steps 2.2 and 3.1); the degenerate cases are handled: characteristic plays no role because the expansion of step 1.3 uses only $\epsilon^2=0$ and never divides by an integer [F2], the matrix $B$ itself need not be invertible although $I_n+\epsilon B$ always is, and when $\operatorname{tr}(B)\ne0$ the element $1+\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ is a unit different from $1$ with inverse $1-\epsilon\operatorname{tr}(B)$ [F20], so the trace-zero condition is strict; the endpoints are $n=1$, where $\operatorname{GL}_1=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x]_{x})$ has tangent space $M_1(k)=k$ and $\operatorname{SL}_1=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x]/(x-1))$ has tangent space $\{0\}$ because $\operatorname{tr}(B)=0$ forces $B=0$, together with the exponent endpoints $d^0=1$ and the general power $d^m$ in $S_d$; both directions of the equivalence $\det(I_n+\epsilon B)=1\iff\operatorname{tr}(B)=0$ are proved in step 2.2 and both directions of the factorisation through $T$ are proved in step 3.1; and no Axiom of Choice or dependent choice is used, every map, matrix and unit inverse being exhibited explicitly and every cited supplier being choice-free, with no def-axiom-of-choice dependency declared. [F2, F13, F20, step 1.1, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎



## Source qualification

Milne, *Algebraic Geometry* v6.10, §4j (printed pp. 97–98) introduces the
dual-number description of tangent spaces for group varieties given by their
points functors. In Example 4.46 the source observes that a matrix
$I+\epsilon A$ has inverse $I-\epsilon A$ in $M_n(k[\epsilon])$, so that it
lies in $\operatorname{GL}_n(k[\epsilon])$, and concludes
$$T_e(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\{I+\epsilon A:A\in M_n\}\cong M_n(k);$$
this is exactly the invertibility computation of step 2.1, and the present item
records the same fact scheme-theoretically through the localisation condition
on the map $\psi$. In Example 4.47 the source expands $\det(I+\epsilon A)$ as a
sum of signed products and uses $\epsilon^2=0$ to obtain
$\det(I+\epsilon A)=1+\epsilon\operatorname{trace}(A)$, hence
$T_e(\operatorname{SL}_n)\cong\{A\in M_n(k):\operatorname{trace}(A)=0\}$; the
present item reproduces this expansion as the column-multilinear computation
of step 1.3 and derives both inclusions from the quotient condition. Arapura,
*Notes on Basic Algebraic Geometry*, Exercise 5.1.4 (printed p. 35) asks for
the identity $\det(I+B\epsilon)=1+\operatorname{trace}(B)\epsilon$ and the
conclusion $T_I\operatorname{SL}_n(k)=\{B\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times
n}(k):\operatorname{trace}(B)=0\}$; it is an exercise, so it is cited as a
source for the statement and not as a proof. Milne's chapter works with
classical varieties over an algebraically closed field and Milne's Aside 4.49
(the Lie bracket on $T_e\operatorname{GL}_n$) is deliberately not used: the
item asserts only the two $k$-vector-space isomorphisms and infers no bracket.
The item adds the affine presentations
$\operatorname{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x_{ij}]_{\det})$ and
$\operatorname{SL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}(k[x_{ij}]/(\det(X)-1))$ over an
arbitrary field, the passage to the two universal properties of the quotient
and the localisation, and the identification of the based dual-number points
with the matrices $I_n+\epsilon B$; no characteristic, perfectness,
reducedness or algebraic-closedness hypothesis is used anywhere.
