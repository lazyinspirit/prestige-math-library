## Definition

Fix an integer $n\geq1$.  A **tempered distribution** on $\mathbb R^n$ is a
continuous complex-linear functional

$$u:\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)\longrightarrow\mathbb C,$$

where Schwartz space and its locally convex topology are those of
[[def-schwartz-space-and-its-seminorms]] and
[[def-schwartz-topology-and-convergence]].  The space of all such functionals
is denoted $\mathcal S'(\mathbb R^n)$.

We write $\langle u,\varphi\rangle=u(\varphi)$.  This pairing is bilinear:
the test variable is not conjugated.  Thus $u(a\varphi+b\psi)=
a\langle u,\varphi\rangle+b\langle u,\psi\rangle$ for $a,b\in\mathbb C$.
The zero functional is tempered.  Continuity is topological continuity, and,
because the Schwartz topology is generated by a countable cofinal family of
seminorms, it is equivalently continuity on every convergent sequence.  No
choice principle is used in this definition.
