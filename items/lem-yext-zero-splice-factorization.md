## Statement

Let $E$ be $0\to A\to B\xrightarrow{p}C\to0$ and let $F$ be
$0\to C\to D\to X\to0$ in an abelian category. If their splice is zero in
$\operatorname{YExt}^2(X,A)$, then there is an extension $G$ of $X$ by $B$
whose pushout along $p:B\to C$ is equivalent to $F$. Conversely every such
pushout has zero splice with $E$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The two displayed short exact sequences in an arbitrary abelian category. Write their maps as $i:A\to B$, $p:B\to C$, $j:C\to D$, and $q:D\to X$.

[F1] Two-fold equivalence is generated by finite zigzags of endpoint-fixed chain maps ([[def-n-fold-yoneda-extension]], [[def-equivalence-of-n-fold-extensions]]). Its zero representative is $Z:0\to A\xrightarrow{1}A\xrightarrow{0}X\xrightarrow{1}X\to0$.

[F2] Pullback and pushout short extensions are exact ([[def-pullback-and-pushout-of-an-extension]]). An endpoint-fixed map between short exact sequences is an isomorphism: its kernel is zero by first factoring through the left kernel, and its cokernel is zero by first factoring through the right cokernel; the coimage-image axiom then makes it invertible ([[def-abelian-category]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Call a filling of the displayed two-extension an object $T$ with an exact sequence $0\to B\xrightarrow{b}T\xrightarrow{t}X\to0$ and a map $r:T\to D$ such that $rb=jp$ and $qr=t$, for which $0\to A\xrightarrow{bi}T\xrightarrow{r}D\to0$ is exact. A lift $G$ in the statement is exactly such a filling: its pushout along $p$ is $T/A$, and an endpoint-fixed equivalence to $F$ is an isomorphism by [F2]. Conversely the quotient map $r$ identifies that pushout with $F$. Thus it suffices to prove that existence of a filling is invariant under each direction of an endpoint-fixed chain map of two-extensions. [F2, construct]

1.2 Consider any such chain map with middle maps $u:B\to B'$ and $v:D\to D'$. Factoring the differentials through their images gives $c:C\to C'$ satisfying $ui=i'$, $p'u=cp$, $vj=j'c$, and $q'v=q$. The natural maps $B\to B'\times_{C'}C$ and $C'\amalg_C D\to D'$ induce identities on the endpoints of the respective short exact sequences. They are isomorphisms by [F2]. In particular $B=B'\times_{C'}C$ and $F'=c_*F$, up to these specified isomorphisms. [F1, F2, construct]

2.1 If the unprimed two-extension has a filling $T$, push $0\to B\to T\to X\to0$ out along $u$ to obtain $0\to B'\to T'\to X\to0$. The maps $T\xrightarrow{r}D\xrightarrow{v}D'$ and $B'\xrightarrow{p'}C'\xrightarrow{j'}D'$ agree on $B$, so induce $r':T'\to D'$. Quotienting the pushout by the common copy of $A$ gives $T'/A\cong C'\amalg_C(T/A)\cong C'\amalg_C D\cong D'$. These isomorphisms follow directly from the universal property of a cokernel and a pushout: a map out of either side is precisely a compatible pair of maps annihilating $A$. Consequently $\ker r'=A$ and $r'$ is epic. This is a filling of the primed extension. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

2.2 If instead the primed extension has a filling $T'$, form $T=T'\times_{D'}D$ using its quotient $r':T'\twoheadrightarrow D'$ and $v$. The projection $r:T\to D$ is epic with kernel $A$ by pullback exactness. Set $t=qr$, which is epic. Its kernel is $T'\times_{D'}C$, where $C\to D'$ is $j'c$. Since the inverse image of $C'\subset D'$ in $T'$ is $B'$, this kernel is $B'\times_{C'}C\cong B$ by step 1.2. The induced $B\to T$ satisfies $rb=jp$ and identifies its copy of $A$ with $\ker r$. Thus $T$ is a filling of the unprimed extension. Every object and morphism used here is a finite pullback or pushout; the argument uses neither resolutions nor a choice principle. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, construct]

3.1 The zero representative $Z$ has filling $A\oplus X$, with its two evident split short exact sequences. If the given splice is zero, [F1] supplies an actual finite zigzag from it to $Z$. Traverse this zigzag backwards starting with that filling, applying step 2.1 when the traversal follows a chain map and step 2.2 when it opposes one. Finite induction produces a filling of the given splice, hence the required $G$. This explicitly supplies the factorization data through every switch, including switches whose intermediate kernels change. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct]

4.1 Conversely let a filling $T$ be given. Form $H=B\times_C B$, with first projection $h:H\to B$ and second projection $s:H\to B$. The sequence $0\to A\xrightarrow{(i,0)}H\xrightarrow{s}B\to0$ is split by the diagonal and has a retraction $\rho:H\to A$ specified by $i\rho=h-s$. Splice this split extension with $0\to B\xrightarrow{b}T\to X\to0$ to obtain $L:0\to A\to H\xrightarrow{bs}T\to X\to0$. There is an endpoint-fixed chain map $L\to EF$, with middle maps $h$ and $r$, since $jp h=jp s=rb s$. There is also an endpoint-fixed chain map $L\to Z$, with middle maps $\rho$ and $t$; the differential square commutes because $tb s=0$. Hence $EF\leftarrow L\to Z$ is a concrete finite zigzag, proving the splice is zero. [F1, F2, step 1.1, construct] ∎

## Source notes

The proof supplies directly, for degree two, the finite-switch data underlying Mitchell VII, Lemma 4.1. It uses the endpoint-fixed chain-map formulation of equivalence rather than assuming an isomorphism of the middle terms. It does not invoke the general higher-degree switch lemma.
