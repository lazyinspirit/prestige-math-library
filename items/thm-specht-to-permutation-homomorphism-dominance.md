## Statement

For $\lambda,\mu\vdash n$ over $\mathbb C$, a nonzero $S_n$-map
$\phi:S^\lambda\to M^\mu$ implies $\lambda\unrhd\mu$. At $\mu=\lambda$,
every $S_n$-map $S^\lambda\to M^\lambda$ is a scalar multiple of the
inclusion; equivalently,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C[S_n]}(S^\lambda,M^\lambda)\cong\mathbb C.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge0$, partitions $\lambda,\mu\vdash n$, and a complex
$\mathbb C[S_n]$-module homomorphism $\phi:S^\lambda\to M^\mu$.

[F1] $S_n$ is the group of bijections of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, with $S_0$
trivial ([[def-symmetric-group]], [[lem-symmetric-group-is-a-group]],
[[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F2] Each $M^\nu$ is a finite-dimensional complex representation of $S_n$
([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F3] The $\nu$-tabloids form a basis of $M^\nu$, with the linear extension of
the left $S_n$-action ([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F4] For a $\lambda$-tableau $t$,
$\kappa_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\gamma$,
$e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}$, and $S^\lambda$ is the complex span of the
$e_s$ ([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F5] Every $e_t$ is nonzero, since its coefficient at $\{t\}$ is $1$
([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).

[F6] $S^\lambda$ is an $S_n$-subrepresentation generated by any one $e_t$
([[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F7] A subrepresentation is a linear subspace stable under each group element
([[def-subrepresentation-and-irreducible-representation]]).

[F8] For a finite group over a field whose characteristic does not divide the
group order, every subrepresentation has a complementary subrepresentation;
this applies over $\mathbb C$ ([[thm-maschkes-theorem-for-finite-groups-over-fields-whose-characteristic-does-not-divide-the-group-order]]).

[F9] The space $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(V,W)$ consists of complex-linear
$S_n$-equivariant maps and is a subspace of the complex vector space of linear
maps ([[def-intertwiner-equivalent-and-faithful-representations]]).

[F10] A complex-linear map is $S_n$-equivariant exactly when it is a
$\mathbb C[S_n]$-module homomorphism
([[thm-group-actions-and-group-ring-modules-correspond]]).

[F11] If $\kappa_tM^\mu\ne0$, then $\lambda\unrhd\mu$
([[lem-column-antisymmetrizer-detects-dominance]]).

[F12] For every $\lambda$-tableau $t$,
$\kappa_tM^\lambda=\mathbb C e_t$ ([[lem-antisymmetrizer-image-on-its-tabloid-module-is-one-dimensional]]).

[F13] $\lambda\unrhd\mu$ means every prefix sum of $\lambda$ is at least
the corresponding prefix sum of $\mu$ ([[def-dominance-order-on-partitions]]).

[F14] Every shape has a canonical standard row-filled tableau $t_0$
([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F15] The label set $\{1,\ldots,n\}$ is finite (empty when $n=0$), and the
set of bijections of a finite set is finite; hence $S_n$ is finite
([[def-natural-numbers]],
[[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]],
[[thm-number-of-bijections-of-a-finite-set]]).

No form of the Axiom of Choice is used. The proof uses one complement supplied
by Maschke's theorem and existential witnesses from spanning families; it
does not choose from an arbitrary indexed family.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F15], $S_n$ is a finite group; by [F2], $M^\lambda$ is a finite-dimensional complex representation; by [F6]-[F7], $S^\lambda$ is a subrepresentation. Since $\operatorname{char}\mathbb C=0$ does not divide $|S_n|$, [F8] gives a subrepresentation $T$ with $M^\lambda=S^\lambda\oplus T$. [given, F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, F15]

2.1 Define $p:M^\lambda\to S^\lambda$ by $p(s+t)=s$ for $s\in S^\lambda,t\in T$. The direct sum makes $p$ a well-defined linear projection with $p|_{S^\lambda}=\mathrm{id}$; since both summands are stable, $p(\sigma(s+t))=\sigma s=\sigma p(s+t)$ for every $\sigma\in S_n$, so $p$ is equivariant. [given, F6, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Set $\Phi:=\phi\circ p:M^\lambda\to M^\mu$. By [F9]-[F10] and step 2.1, $\Phi$ is a $\mathbb C[S_n]$-module homomorphism, and $\Phi|_{S^\lambda}=\phi$. [given, F9, F10, step 2.1]

4.1 If $\phi\ne0$, some $\lambda$-tableau $t$ has $\phi(e_t)\ne0$ because the polytabloids span $S^\lambda$ by [F4]. The tabloid $\{t\}$ is a basis vector by [F3]; then [F4] and step 3.1 give $\phi(e_t)=\Phi(e_t)=\Phi(\kappa_t\cdot\{t\})=\kappa_t\cdot\Phi(\{t\})\ne0$, so $\kappa_tM^\mu\ne0$. [given, F3, F4, F10, step 3.1]

5.1 If $\phi\ne0$, step 4.1 and [F11] imply $\lambda\unrhd\mu$, which by [F13] is the stated dominance order; if $\phi=0$, the nonzero-map implication is vacuous. [given, F11, F13, step 4.1]

5.2 Suppose $\mu=\lambda$ and $\phi\ne0$, and use the tableau from step 4.1. By [F12], $\phi(e_t)=\kappa_t\Phi(\{t\})\in\mathbb C e_t$; since $e_t\ne0$ by [F5], write $\phi(e_t)=c e_t$ with $c\ne0$. For every $\sigma\in S_n$, equivariance gives $\phi(\sigma e_t)=c\sigma e_t$; by [F6], this extends linearly to all of $S^\lambda$. Hence $\phi=c\iota_\lambda$, where $\iota_\lambda:S^\lambda\hookrightarrow M^\lambda$ is inclusion. [given, F5, F6, F9, F12, step 4.1]

6.1 If $\phi=0$, it is $0\iota_\lambda$; step 5.2 covers every nonzero map when $\mu=\lambda$. Each scalar multiple of inclusion is equivariant by [F6]. The canonical $t_0$ from [F14] has $e_{t_0}\in S^\lambda$ by [F4] and $e_{t_0}\ne0$ by [F5], so $\iota_\lambda$ is nonzero. Therefore $c\mapsto c\iota_\lambda$ is a linear bijection from $\mathbb C$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb C[S_n]}(S^\lambda,M^\lambda)$. [given, F4, F5, F6, F9, F10, F14, step 5.2] ∎
