## Definition

Let $X$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-coherent
$\mathcal O_X$-module ([[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]). For $n\ge0$ write
$\mathcal O_X^n$ for the sheaf $U\mapsto\mathcal O_X(U)^n$ with componentwise
restriction, a sheaf of $\mathcal O_X$-modules
([[def-module-on-ringed-space]]); it is the free $\mathcal O_X$-module of rank
$n$, and $\mathcal O_X^0=0$.

**Finite type.** $\mathcal F$ is **of finite type** if every point $x\in X$ has
an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ together with an
$A$-module $M$ and an isomorphism $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ such that $M$
is finitely generated
([[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]).

**Finitely presented.** $\mathcal F$ is **finitely presented** if every point
$x\in X$ has an affine open neighbourhood $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ together
with an $A$-module $M$ and an isomorphism $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ such
that $M$ is finitely presented.

**The equivalent local form under AC.** Assume the Axiom of Choice
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), inherited from the associated-sheaf existence
theorem in the equivalence argument below. Because of the local nature of the conditions,
$\mathcal F$ is of finite type if and only if $X$ can be covered by affine
opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ on which there is a finite family of sections
$s_1,\dots,s_n\in\mathcal F(U)$ generating $\mathcal F|_U$, meaning that the
induced morphism of $\mathcal O_U$-modules
$\mathcal O_U^n\to\mathcal F|_U$ is an epimorphism, equivalently that
$$\mathcal O_U^{\,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow0$$
is an exact sequence of sheaves ([[def-exact-sequence-sheaves]]). Likewise
$\mathcal F$ is finitely presented if and only if $X$ can be covered by affine
opens $U$ admitting an exact sequence
$$\mathcal O_U^{\,m}\longrightarrow\mathcal O_U^{\,n}\longrightarrow\mathcal F|_U\longrightarrow0$$
with $m,n$ finite.

*Why the two forms agree under AC.* Let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be affine and let
$\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ for an $A$-module $M$. First
$\widetilde{A^n}\cong\mathcal O_U^n$, since the two sheaves have the same
sections $(A_f)^n$ on every distinguished open and sheaves on the basis are
determined by those sections ([[thm-associated-module-sheaf-exists]]). Every
morphism $\widetilde{A^n}\to\widetilde M$ of $\mathcal O_U$-modules is induced
by its component on global sections, an $A$-linear map $\psi:A^n\to M$: a
morphism is determined by its components on distinguished opens
([[thm-associated-module-sheaf-exists]]), a general $A$-linear map induces
compatible maps $A_f^n\to M_f$ on distinguished opens, and these two
constructions are inverse by the functoriality of the localisations recorded in
[[def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]].
If $M$ is generated by the images of the standard basis under $\psi$, then
localisation is exact ([[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]]), so each
component $A_f^n\to M_f$ is surjective, and a morphism whose components on a
basis are surjective is an epimorphism: a germ of $\widetilde M$ at a point is
represented on some distinguished open and can be lifted there. Conversely, let
$\psi:A^n\to M$ correspond to an epimorphism $\varphi$ and put
$N=M/\psi(A^n)$; the components of the composite
$\widetilde{A^n}\to\widetilde M\to\widetilde N$ are
$A_f^n\to M_f\to N_f=0$, because localisation is right exact, so this composite
is the zero morphism on every distinguished open and hence is zero; it is also
a composite of epimorphisms, so its target has all stalks zero, and then
$N=\Gamma(U,\widetilde N)=0$ by the identification of global sections
([[thm-associated-module-sheaf-exists]]). Thus $\psi$ is surjective, so $M$ is
generated by $n$ elements and $\varphi$ exhibits the finite family of global
sections $\psi(e_1),\dots,\psi(e_n)$. Applying the same translation to the
kernel of a surjection $A^n\to M$ gives the finitely presented form. The
elementwise criterion for equality and vanishing in a localisation
([[def-localisation-of-a-module]]) is what turns the componentwise statements
into statements about $M$.

**Immediate consequences.** The conditions are local on $X$ and invariant under
isomorphism of $\mathcal O_X$-modules; a finitely presented quasi-coherent
module is of finite type, since a finitely presented module is generated by the
images of the standard basis; restrictions to open subschemes again satisfy the
corresponding condition; and the zero module is finitely presented (take
$m=n=0$), hence of finite type, on every chart. No Noetherian, separatedness or
finiteness hypothesis on $X$ is built into the definition, and the definition
says nothing about existence of local frames, which is the stronger condition
treated separately on this page.
