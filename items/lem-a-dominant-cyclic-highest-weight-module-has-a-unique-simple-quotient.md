## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, let $\lambda$ be dominant integral, and let
$M=M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ with canonical generator $v_\lambda$
([[def-dominant-integrable-highest-weight-cyclic-module]]). Then $M$ has a
unique maximal proper submodule $N_\lambda$, and the quotient
$$L(\lambda):=M/N_\lambda$$
is a nonzero simple $\mathfrak g$-module; in particular $M$ has, up to
isomorphism, a unique simple quotient.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system, a dominant integral $\lambda$, and $M=M_{\mathrm{int}}(\lambda)$ with generator $v_\lambda$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory used by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] $v_\lambda\ne0$ has weight $\lambda$, is killed by $\mathfrak n^+$, and generates $M$; moreover $M=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv_\lambda$, all weights of $M$ satisfy $\mu\le\lambda$, and $M_\lambda=\mathbb Cv_\lambda$ ([[lem-pbw-shows-the-dominant-cyclic-highest-weight-generator-survives]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]]).

[L2] The action of $\mathfrak g$ extends to a unital action of $U(\mathfrak g)$; submodules are the $U(\mathfrak g)$-submodules, and a submodule generated by one vector $v$ is $U(\mathfrak g)v$ ([[thm-lie-algebra-representations-are-equivalent-to-unital-modules-over-the-enveloping-algebra]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[def-subrepresentation-quotient-representation-and-intertwiner]]).

[L3] For an ordered basis of the abelian Lie algebra $\mathfrak h$ the PBW monomials form a basis of $U(\mathfrak h)$, so $U(\mathfrak h)$ is the commutative polynomial algebra in these variables and acts on a weight vector of weight $\mu$ through evaluation at $\mu$ ([[thm-poincare-birkhoff-witt]], [[def-universal-enveloping-algebra]]).

[L4] Every element of $M$ is a finite sum of weight vectors: this follows from the spanning set $U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv_\lambda$ of [L1] and the shift property of root vectors ([[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every proper submodule $N$ of $M$ misses $M_\lambda=\mathbb Cv_\lambda$: if $v_\lambda\in N$, then $N$ contains $U(\mathfrak g)v_\lambda=M$ by [L1] and [L2], so $N=M$, contrary to being proper; hence $N\cap M_\lambda=0$. [L1, L2, A1]

1.2 For a submodule $N$, every element $n\in N$ has all its weight components in $N$: write $n$ as a finite sum of weight vectors by [L4], with finite support $S$; for $\mu\in S$, Lagrange interpolation on the finitely many distinct functionals of $S$ gives a polynomial $p_\mu$ with $p_\mu(\mu)=1$ and $p_\mu(\nu)=0$ for $\nu\in S\setminus\{\mu\}$, and the corresponding element of $U(\mathfrak h)$ acts on each weight vector by these values by [L3]; since $N$ is $\mathfrak h$-stable and $\mathfrak h\subseteq U(\mathfrak g)$, the vector $p_\mu\cdot n$ is the $\mu$-weight component of $n$ and lies in $N$. [L3, L4]

2.1 Hence $N=(N\cap M_\mu)$-direct sum over weights, and by step 1.1 every proper submodule $N$ satisfies $N\cap M_\lambda=0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 Let $N_\lambda=\sum\{N:N\text{ is a proper submodule of }M\}$; this is a submodule of $M$, and every element of it is a finite sum of elements lying in finitely many proper submodules, so by step 2.1 its $\lambda$-component is zero; since $M_\lambda=\mathbb Cv_\lambda\ne0$, the submodule $N_\lambda$ is proper, and by construction it contains every proper submodule of $M$. Thus $N_\lambda$ is the unique maximal proper submodule. [L1, step 2.1]

4.1 The quotient $L(\lambda)=M/N_\lambda$ is nonzero because $N_\lambda$ is proper, and it is simple: a nonzero proper submodule of $L(\lambda)$ would have as preimage a proper submodule of $M$ strictly containing $N_\lambda$, contradicting the maximality of $N_\lambda$; hence the simple quotient is unique, since any simple quotient of $M$ has kernel a maximal proper submodule, which equals $N_\lambda$. [L2, step 3.1]

5.1 Therefore $M$ has a unique maximal proper submodule and a unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$, as asserted. [step 4.1] ∎
