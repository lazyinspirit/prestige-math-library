## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be finite-dimensional complex semisimple with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$, positive system $\Phi^+$, $\mathfrak n^+=\bigoplus_{\alpha>0}\mathfrak g_\alpha$, Killing form $B$, $\lambda\in\Lambda^+$ and $V=L(\lambda)$. Choose a compact real form of $\mathfrak g$ with conjugation $\tau$ and root vectors normalized by $B(e_\alpha,f_\alpha)=1$, $f_\alpha=-\tau(e_\alpha)$; give $\mathfrak n^+$ the Hermitian form $\langle x,y\rangle=-B(x,\tau y)$ and $V$ a positive Hermitian form invariant under the compact real form. Such a compact form and form exist by [[thm-compact-connected-semisimple-lie-groups-are-classified-up-to-isogeny-by-root-systems]], [[thm-compact-connected-lie-groups-are-classified-by-root-data]] and [[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]], and the representation is unitarizable by [[thm-every-finite-dimensional-continuous-representation-of-a-compact-lie-group-is-unitarizable]]; let $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ also denote the induced tensor form on $C^q=\Lambda^q(\mathfrak n^+)^*\otimes V$. Let $d$ be the Chevalley–Eilenberg differential [[def-chevalley-eilenberg-differential]], let $\delta=d^*$ and $\square=d\delta+\delta d$. Write $d=A+D$ with $A=\sum_{\alpha>0}\varepsilon_\alpha\pi(e_\alpha)$ and $D=-\frac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_\alpha\varepsilon_\beta\iota_\gamma$ for the structure constants $[e_\alpha,e_\beta]=\sum_\gamma c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}e_\gamma$, and set $T(H)=-\sum_{\alpha>0}\alpha(H)N_\alpha$, $N_\alpha=\varepsilon_\alpha\iota_\alpha$, $\Theta(H)=\pi(H)+T(H)$. Then:
(i) $\langle\square c,c\rangle=\|dc\|^2+\|\delta c\|^2\ge0$ for all $c$, with equality exactly on $\ker\square$; hence $C^q=\operatorname{im}d\oplus\ker\square\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta$ and harmonic representatives identify $H^q(\mathfrak n^+,V)\cong\ker\square\cap C^q$;
(ii) $\{D,D^*\}=-\frac12\sum_jT(H_j)^2-T(H_\rho)$ and $\{A,A^*\}+\{A,D^*\}+\{D,A^*\}=\sum_{\alpha>0}\pi(f_\alpha)\pi(e_\alpha)-\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)$ for a $B$-orthonormal basis $H_j$ of the real Cartan span;
(iii) with the polarized Casimir form of [[def-quadratic-casimir-element]] and [[prop-the-quadratic-casimir-element-is-central]],
$$2\square=1\otimes\pi(C_{\mathfrak g})-\sum_j\Theta(H_j)^2-2\Theta(H_\rho),$$
so on every weight component $C^\bullet_\mu$ actually occurring in the finite cochain space,
$$\square|_{C^\bullet_\mu}=\tfrac12\bigl(\|\lambda+\rho\|^2-\|\mu+\rho\|^2\bigr)\operatorname{id},$$
the norm coming from $B$. The scalar is nonnegative, and it vanishes exactly when $\mu\in W\cdot\lambda$ lies in the dot orbit. The normalization of $B$, of $C_{\mathfrak g}$ and of the root-vector metric is common and fixed once and for all; rescaling one of them independently changes the scalar.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; the data $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h,\Phi^+,B,\lambda,V)$; a compact real form with conjugation $\tau$ and root vectors $e_\alpha,f_\alpha$ normalized by $B(e_\alpha,f_\alpha)=1$ and $f_\alpha=-\tau(e_\alpha)$; the Hermitian forms of the Statement; a $B$-orthonormal basis $H_j$ of the real Cartan span.

[F1] Root-space data: $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak h\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with one-dimensional root spaces, $[e_\alpha,e_\beta]\in\mathfrak g_{\alpha+\beta}$, $B$ is invariant and nondegenerate and pairs $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$, and $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=H_\alpha$ is the $B$-dual vector of $\alpha$ ([[thm-root-space-decomposition-relative-to-a-cartan-subalgebra]], [[prop-root-space-brackets-add-their-roots]], [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]], [[prop-killing-form-pairs-only-opposite-root-spaces]], [[def-killing-dual-vector-attached-to-a-root]], [[prop-opposite-root-spaces-bracket-to-the-killing-dual-line]], [[prop-killing-form-is-invariant-and-nondegenerate-on-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]]).

[F2] The compact real form, its conjugation $\tau$, its integration, the invariant form on $V$ and the unitarizability exist as in the Statement ([[thm-compact-connected-semisimple-lie-groups-are-classified-up-to-isogeny-by-root-systems]], [[thm-compact-connected-lie-groups-are-classified-by-root-data]], [[thm-isomorphism-theorem-for-complex-semisimple-lie-algebras]], [[thm-cartan-subalgebras-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-conjugate]], [[prop-root-and-weight-lattice-sandwich-for-a-compact-semisimple-group]], [[thm-lie-second-fundamental-theorem]], [[thm-every-finite-dimensional-continuous-representation-of-a-compact-lie-group-is-unitarizable]], [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]]); consequently $\pi(e_\alpha)^*=\pi(f_\alpha)$ and the $\varepsilon_\alpha$ form an orthonormal basis of the dual of $\mathfrak n^+$.

[F3] Chevalley–Eilenberg cochains, differential and cohomology ([[def-chevalley-eilenberg-cochains]], [[def-chevalley-eilenberg-differential]], [[thm-the-chevalley-eilenberg-differential-squares-to-zero]], [[def-lie-algebra-cohomology]]).

[F4] Exterior algebra operators: $\varepsilon_\alpha$ denotes wedge multiplication and $\iota_\alpha$ its adjoint, the interior product, with the graded anticommutation relations $\{\varepsilon_\alpha,\iota_\beta\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, $\{\varepsilon_\alpha,\varepsilon_\beta\}=0$, $\{\iota_\alpha,\iota_\beta\}=0$ and the explicit contraction formula ([[def-interior-product-on-the-exterior-algebra]], [[prop-wedge-and-interior-product-satisfy-the-graded-anticommutation-identity]], [[thm-interior-product-is-adjoint-to-exterior-multiplication]]).

[F5] The Casimir element $C_{\mathfrak g}$ and its value: for dual bases, $C_{\mathfrak g}=\sum_jH_j^2+2H_\rho+2\sum_{\alpha>0}f_\alpha e_\alpha$, it is central, and it acts on the highest-weight module $L(\lambda)$ by the scalar $(\lambda,\lambda+2\rho)=\|\lambda+\rho\|^2-\|\rho\|^2$ ([[def-quadratic-casimir-element]], [[prop-the-quadratic-casimir-element-is-central]], [[prop-casimir-eigenvalue-on-a-highest-weight-module]], [[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]], [[prop-the-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[F6] $C^q$ is finite dimensional and the tensor form is positive definite, Hermitian and linear in the first argument; distinct $\mathfrak h$-weight components of $C^\bullet$ are orthogonal, and $C^\bullet_\mu$ is nonzero only for weights $\mu$ occurring in the weight decomposition of $\Lambda^\bullet(\mathfrak n^+)^*\otimes V$ ([[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]], [[prop-finite-dimensional-representations-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-decompose-into-weight-spaces]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]], [[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]]).

[F7] The equality case and cochain multiplicity of [[lem-extremal-weight-cochain-for-a-weyl-element-is-closed]], in particular the cochain weights $\mu=\eta-\langle S\rangle$ and the vanishing of $C^q_{w\cdot\lambda}$ for $q\neq\ell(w)$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** establish the Hodge decomposition, compute the two anticommutators of $d$ from the exterior algebra relations, and identify the resulting operator with the Casimir.

1.1 (The metric data.) Realize the root system by a compact connected semisimple group. By complex semisimple classification and Cartan conjugacy [F2], identify its complexified Lie algebra and Cartan with $(\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h)$, transporting the compact form and conjugation $\tau$. Its simply connected compact form exists by [F2]. Integrate the restriction of the given representation $\pi$ to the compact real Lie algebra to this simply connected group by Lie's second theorem, and unitarize the resulting representation. This gives a positive Hermitian form invariant under the compact real form $\mathfrak u$. Unitarize also its adjoint representation by [F2]. For $X\in\mathfrak u$, $\operatorname{ad}X$ is skew-adjoint, so $B(X,X)=\operatorname{tr}((\operatorname{ad}X)^2)<0$ for $X\ne0$: nondegeneracy of $B$ makes the center zero. For $x=X+iY$ with $X,Y\in\mathfrak u$, $-B(x,\tau x)=-B(X,X)-B(Y,Y)>0$ if $x\ne0$. Transport the complexified maximal-torus Cartan in the same identification; its real root span is $\mathfrak h_{\mathbb R}=i\operatorname{Lie}(T)$, with $\tau(H)=-H$, so $\tau(\mathfrak g_\alpha)=\mathfrak g_{-\alpha}$ and each root vector can be scaled to $B(e_\alpha,-\tau e_\alpha)=1$. Invariance gives $\pi(X)^*=-\pi(X)$ on $\mathfrak u$; complex linearity gives $\pi(x)^*=-\pi(\tau x)$, hence $\pi(e_\alpha)^*=\pi(f_\alpha)$ and $\pi(H)^*=\pi(H)$ on the real Cartan span. The $\varepsilon_\alpha$ are orthonormal and the tensor form is positive Hermitian. The total Cartan operators $\Theta(H)$ are self-adjoint on this span, so distinct weights are orthogonal. [F1, F2, F6]

1.2 (Hodge decomposition.) Since $C^q$ is finite dimensional and the form is positive definite, $\langle\square c,c\rangle=\langle d\delta c,c\rangle+\langle\delta dc,c\rangle=\|\delta c\|^2+\|dc\|^2\ge0$, with equality precisely when $dc=\delta c=0$, which is precisely $\square c=0$; moreover $\operatorname{im}d\perp\operatorname{im}\delta$ because $\langle d\alpha,\delta\beta\rangle=\langle\alpha,\delta^2\beta\rangle=0$, and $(\operatorname{im}d+\operatorname{im}\delta)^\perp=\ker\square$, so $C^q=\operatorname{im}d\oplus\ker\square\oplus\operatorname{im}\delta$. As $\ker d=\operatorname{im}d\oplus\ker\square$ and $\ker\square\cap\operatorname{im}d=0$, harmonic representatives give $H^q(\mathfrak n^+,V)\cong\ker\square\cap C^q$, proving (i). [F3, F6, algebra]

1.3 (Operator form and adjoints.) In the orthonormal basis, the differential of [F3] is $d=A+D$ with $A=\sum_\alpha\varepsilon_\alpha\pi(e_\alpha)$ and $D=-\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_\alpha\varepsilon_\beta\iota_\gamma$: the first sum of the two-sum formula gives the action terms with the signs $(-1)^i$ after the exterior sign bookkeeping, and the second sum inserts the bracket into the first slot, which in the basis is the contraction expression displayed; the two sides are equal on the basis cochains $\varepsilon_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\varepsilon_{i_q}\otimes v$. For complex orthonormal exterior covectors, contraction deletes each occurrence with its alternating sign. On the orthonormal wedge basis this is the adjoint of wedge multiplication and satisfies the same anticommutation relations [F4], so the cited real-space formulas apply here by this basis computation. Taking adjoints with the linear-first Hermitian convention, using $\varepsilon_\alpha^*=\iota_\alpha$, $\pi(e_\alpha)^*=\pi(f_\alpha)$ and the reversal of operator order with conjugated coefficients, gives $A^*=\sum_\alpha\pi(f_\alpha)\iota_\alpha$ and $D^*=-\tfrac12\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\overline{c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}}\varepsilon_\gamma\iota_\beta\iota_\alpha$. [F2, F3, F4, algebra]

1.4 (Mixed anticommutators.) Using $\{\varepsilon_\alpha,\iota_\beta\}=\delta_{\alpha\beta}$, expansion of $AA^*+A^*A$ gives $\{A,A^*\}=\sum_\alpha\pi(f_\alpha)\pi(e_\alpha)+\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\varepsilon_\alpha\iota_\beta\pi([e_\alpha,f_\beta])$, the double-sum term coming from $\iota_\beta\varepsilon_\alpha=\delta_{\alpha\beta}-\varepsilon_\alpha\iota_\beta$ after moving the $\pi$-operators past the exterior operators. Likewise $AD^*+D^*A=-\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}\overline{c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}}\varepsilon_\gamma\iota_\beta\pi(e_\alpha)$ and $DA^*+A^*D=\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\gamma}c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}\varepsilon_\alpha\iota_\gamma\pi(f_\beta)$: the degree-three exterior terms cancel between the two orders because $\varepsilon_\alpha\varepsilon_\beta$ is alternating while $c^\gamma_{\alpha\beta}$ is skew in $\alpha,\beta$, and similarly for the degree-three contractions. [F4, algebra]

1.5 (Cancellation of root terms.) Under the normalization $B(e_\alpha,f_\alpha)=1$ and $f_\alpha=-\tau(e_\alpha)$, the compact conjugation gives, whenever the stated differences are roots, $[e_\alpha,f_\beta]=-\overline{c^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}}e_\gamma$ if $\alpha=\beta+\gamma$, $[e_\alpha,f_\beta]=-c^\beta_{\alpha\gamma}f_\gamma$ if $\beta=\alpha+\gamma$, and $[e_\alpha,f_\alpha]=H_\alpha$; hence $[e_\beta,f_\gamma]=\mathbf 1_{\beta=\gamma}H_\beta+\sum_{s:\beta=s+\gamma}\overline{c^\beta_{s\gamma}}e_s-\sum_{s:\gamma=\beta+s}c^\gamma_{\beta s}f_s$. Substituting this into the double sum of $\{A,A^*\}$ term by term: every $e_s$-term cancels the corresponding term of $AD^*+D^*A$ and every $f_s$-term cancels the corresponding term of $DA^*+A^*D$, the index identifications being $\beta=s+\gamma$ against $\gamma'=\alpha+\beta'$ and $\gamma=\beta+s$ against $\gamma'=\alpha'+\beta'$; the surviving diagonal is $\sum_\alpha N_\alpha\pi(H_\alpha)=-\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)$, because $\sum_j\alpha(H_j)\pi(H_j)=\pi(H_\alpha)$. Hence $\{A,A^*\}+\{A,D^*\}+\{D,A^*\}=\sum_\alpha\pi(f_\alpha)\pi(e_\alpha)-\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)$, the second identity of (ii). [F1, F4, algebra]

1.6 (Exterior anticommutator.) Normal-ordering $DD^*+D^*D$ with the relations of [F4], moving all contractions to the right, cancels all degree-six terms and yields $\{D,D^*\}=\tfrac12\sum_{p,q,r}|c^r_{pq}|^2N_r+\sum_{a<b,\,c<d}K_{ab;cd}\varepsilon_a\varepsilon_b\iota_c\iota_d$ with $K_{ab;cd}=-\sum_s c^s_{ab}\overline{c^s_{cd}}-\sum_s\overline{c^a_{sc}}c^d_{sb}+\sum_s\overline{c^b_{sc}}c^d_{sa}+\sum_s\overline{c^a_{sd}}c^c_{sb}-\sum_s\overline{c^b_{sd}}c^c_{sa}$ for a fixed total order on the positive roots: the normal-ordering identities $\iota_r\varepsilon_c=\delta_{rc}-\varepsilon_c\iota_r$ applied to the two orderings of the products produce, alongside the diagonal $\tfrac12\sum|c|^2N_r$ read off the two $\delta\delta$ contractions, exactly the four ordered quartic monomials with the listed coefficient. [F4, algebra]

1.7 (Jacobi reduction of the quartic coefficient.) Write $X_{ac}=[e_a,f_c]=X^+_{ac}+X^0_{ac}+X^-_{ac}$ with $X^+_{ac}=\sum_{s:a=s+c}\overline{c^a_{sc}}e_s$, $X^0_{ac}=\mathbf 1_{a=c}H_a$ and $X^-_{ac}=-\sum_{s:c=a+s}c^c_{as}f_s$, and put $S(X,Y)=B(X^+,Y^-)+B(Y^+,X^-)$. The four nonleading sums in $K$ combine with this notation as $-\sum_s\overline{c^a_{sc}}c^d_{sb}-\sum_s\overline{c^b_{sd}}c^c_{sa}+\cdots=-S(X_{ac},X_{bd})+S(X_{ad},X_{bc})$ after reindexing, and the leading sum is $B([e_a,e_b],[f_c,f_d])$ because $[f_c,f_d]=-\sum_s\overline{c^s_{cd}}f_s$ and $B(e_s,f_t)=\delta_{st}$. Jacobi paired with $f_d$, using invariance $B([x,y],z)=B(y,[z,x])$, gives the identity $B([e_a,e_b],[f_c,f_d])=B(X_{ac},X_{bd})-B(X_{ad},X_{bc})$; since $B$ pairs only opposite root spaces, $B(X,Y)=S(X,Y)+B(X^0,Y^0)$, so the root terms cancel and $K_{ab;cd}=B(X^0_{ac},X^0_{bd})-B(X^0_{ad},X^0_{bc})=(a,b)(\mathbf 1_{a=c}\mathbf 1_{b=d}-\mathbf 1_{a=d}\mathbf 1_{b=c})$. With the fixed order $a<b$, $c<d$ the quartic part is therefore $-\sum_{a<b}(a,b)N_aN_b$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 (Linear coefficient.) Fix a positive root $r$, and let $P:\mathfrak g\to\mathfrak n^+$ be the projection along $\mathfrak n^-\oplus\mathfrak h$. On $\mathfrak n^+$ put $E=\operatorname{ad}e_r$ and $F=P\operatorname{ad}f_r$; these are endomorphisms, so $\operatorname{tr}(EF-FE)=0$, by interchanging the indices in the finite matrix sums for their traces. Since $\operatorname{ad}e_r$ preserves $\mathfrak n^+$, one has $P\operatorname{ad}H_r|_{\mathfrak n^+}=[E,F]+R$, with $R=P\operatorname{ad}e_r(1-P)\operatorname{ad}f_r|_{\mathfrak n^+}$. Its diagonal is explicit: on $e_r$, $[f_r,e_r]=-H_r$ and $[e_r,-H_r]=\|r\|^2e_r$; for $\beta>0$ with $r=\beta+\gamma$, $[f_r,e_\beta]=c^r_{\beta\gamma}f_\gamma$ and $[e_r,f_\gamma]=\overline{c^r_{\beta\gamma}}e_\beta$, so $Re_\beta=|c^r_{\beta\gamma}|^2e_\beta$; all other root vectors give zero. Taking traces yields $2(\rho,r)=\|r\|^2+\sum_{\beta+\gamma=r}|c^r_{\beta\gamma}|^2$, hence $\tfrac12\sum_{a,b}|c^r_{ab}|^2=(\rho,r)-\tfrac12\|r\|^2$. With $N_r^2=N_r$, steps 1.6 and 1.7 therefore give $\{D,D^*\}=\sum_r((\rho,r)-\tfrac12\|r\|^2)N_r-\sum_{a<b}(a,b)N_aN_b=-\tfrac12\sum_jT(H_j)^2-T(H_\rho)$, proving the first identity of (ii). [F1, F4, step 1.6, step 1.7, algebra]

3.1 (Assembly and the scalar.) Since $\square=d\delta+\delta d=\{d,d^*\}$ and $d=A+D$, steps 1.5 and 2.1 give $\square=\sum_\alpha\pi(f_\alpha)\pi(e_\alpha)-\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)-\tfrac12\sum_jT(H_j)^2-T(H_\rho)$; expanding $\sum_j\Theta(H_j)^2+2\Theta(H_\rho)=\sum_j\pi(H_j)^2+2\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)+\sum_jT(H_j)^2+2\pi(H_\rho)+2T(H_\rho)$ and using $C_{\mathfrak g}=\sum_jH_j^2+2H_\rho+2\sum_\alpha f_\alpha e_\alpha$ from [F5] gives $1\otimes\pi(C_{\mathfrak g})-\sum_j\Theta(H_j)^2-2\Theta(H_\rho)=2\bigl[\sum_\alpha\pi(f_\alpha)\pi(e_\alpha)-\sum_j\pi(H_j)T(H_j)-\tfrac12\sum_jT(H_j)^2-T(H_\rho)\bigr]$, hence $2\square=1\otimes\pi(C_{\mathfrak g})-\sum_j\Theta(H_j)^2-2\Theta(H_\rho)$, the first identity of (iii). On a cochain of weight $\mu$ the operator $\Theta(H)$ acts by $\mu(H)$, and $\pi(C_{\mathfrak g})$ acts on $V=L(\lambda)$ by the scalar $\|\lambda+\rho\|^2-\|\rho\|^2$ by [F5], so $\square$ acts on $C^\bullet_\mu$ by the scalar $\tfrac12(\|\lambda+\rho\|^2-\|\rho\|^2-\|\mu\|^2-2(\mu,\rho))=\tfrac12(\|\lambda+\rho\|^2-\|\mu+\rho\|^2)$; this is nonnegative, and it vanishes exactly when $\|\mu+\rho\|=\|\lambda+\rho\|$, which by the equality case of [F7] happens exactly for $\mu\in W\cdot\lambda$, with the corresponding cochain spaces one-dimensional in degree $\ell(w)$. The normalization is common: $B$ fixes $H_j$, $C_{\mathfrak g}$ and the root-vector metric $B(e_\alpha,f_\alpha)=1$ simultaneously, and rescaling one of them independently changes the displayed scalar. [F5, F7, step 1.5, step 2.1] ∎
