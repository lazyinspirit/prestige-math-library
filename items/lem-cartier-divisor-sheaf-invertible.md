## Statement

Let $X$ be a scheme and let $D$ be a Cartier divisor on $X$, represented by
meromorphic units $f_i\in\mathcal K_X(U_i)^{\times}$ with unit ratios on the
overlaps ([[def-cartier-divisor]]), and let $\mathcal O_X(D)$ be the
$\mathcal O_X$-submodule sheaf of $\mathcal K_X$ of
[[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]]. Then $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is an
invertible $\mathcal O_X$-module ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]), and on each
$U_i$ it is freely generated by $f_i^{-1}$, so that the map
$\mathcal O_{U_i}\to\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}$, $a\mapsto a f_i^{-1}$, is an
isomorphism. If $X$ is integral ([[def-integral-scheme]]), then $\mathcal K_X$
is the constant sheaf with value the function field $K(X)$
([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]]), and $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is a fractional
$\mathcal O_X$-subsheaf of $K(X)$: an $\mathcal O_X$-submodule of the constant
sheaf $K(X)$ which is locally of the form $g\,\mathcal O_X$ with
$g\in K(X)^{\times}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$, a Cartier divisor $D$ on $X$ with a local-equation
datum $\{(U_i,f_i)\}_{i\in I}$.

[F1] $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is the subsheaf of $\mathcal K_X$ whose sections over
an open $V$ are the $g\in\mathcal K_X(V)$ with $f_i g|_{V\cap U_i}\in
\mathcal O_X(V\cap U_i)$ for all $i$; equivalently
$\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}$, and this is well defined,
independent of the datum, and closed under addition and regular scalar
multiplication ([[def-invertible-sheaf-of-cartier-divisor]]).

[F2] An $\mathcal O_X$-module is invertible if and only if every point has an
open neighbourhood on which it admits a generator, i.e. a section $s$ such
that $\mathcal O_U\to\mathcal L|_U$, $a\mapsto a s$, is an isomorphism
([[def-invertible-sheaf]]).

[F3] On an integral scheme $X$ the sheaf $\mathcal K_X$ is the constant sheaf
with value the function field $K(X)$, and the structure map
$\mathcal O_X\to\mathcal K_X$ is injective
([[def-sheaf-total-quotient-rings]], [[def-integral-scheme]]).

## Proof

1.1 **Local generator.** For each $i$ the section $f_i^{-1}\in\mathcal K_X(U_i)$ lies in $\mathcal O_X(D)(U_i)$: multiplying it by $f_i$ gives $1\in\mathcal O_X(U_i)$. The map $\varphi_i:\mathcal O_{U_i}\to\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}$, $a\mapsto a f_i^{-1}$, is an isomorphism, with inverse induced by $g\mapsto f_i g$: for $g\in\mathcal O_X(D)(V\cap U_i)$ the product $f_i g$ lies in $\mathcal O_X(V\cap U_i)$ by the defining condition of [F1], and the two maps are mutually inverse because $f_i$ is a unit of $\mathcal K_X$. [F1]

1.2 **Integral case.** If $X$ is integral, then $\mathcal K_X$ is the constant sheaf with value $K(X)$ by [F3], so $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is a subsheaf of that constant sheaf; it is an $\mathcal O_X$-submodule by [F1]. On $U_i$ the description $\mathcal O_X(D)|_{U_i}=f_i^{-1}\mathcal O_{U_i}$ of [F1] exhibits it as $g\,\mathcal O_X$ with $g=f_i^{-1}\in K(X)^{\times}$, the meromorphic unit $f_i$ being an element of the field $K(X)$. Hence $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is a fractional $\mathcal O_X$-subsheaf of $K(X)$. [F1, F3]

2.1 **Invertibility.** Since the $U_i$ cover $X$, step 1.1 exhibits on every point an open neighbourhood on which $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is freely generated by $f_i^{-1}$, so $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is invertible. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 **Conclusion.** $\mathcal O_X(D)$ is invertible and locally freely generated by the sections $f_i^{-1}$, and on an integral $X$ it is a fractional subsheaf of $K(X)$. [step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎

No choice principle is used: the local generators are the given equations of
the divisor, and no trivialisation or atlas is selected. For the zero divisor
one may take $f_i=1$ on the whole of $X$, so $\mathcal O_X(0)=\mathcal O_X$ is
generated by $1$. On the empty scheme the formula gives the unique module
sheaf, which satisfies the local rank-one condition vacuously.
