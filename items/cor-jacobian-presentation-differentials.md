## Statement

Let $A$ be a commutative ring, let $P=A[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and let
$I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)\subseteq P$ be the ideal generated by finitely many elements,
with quotient $B=P/I$. Then $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the cokernel of the $B$-linear map
$B^{r}\to B^{n}$ whose $j$-th column is the vector of partial derivatives
$\bigl(\partial f_j/\partial x_i\bigr)_{i=1}^{n}$, that is,

$$\Omega_{B/A}\;\cong\;B^{n}\Big/\sum_{j=1}^{r}B\cdot\bigl(\partial f_j/\partial x_1,\dots,\partial f_j/\partial x_n\bigr).$$

This is a presentation of $\Omega_{B/A}$ by $r$ relations and is **not** by
itself a smoothness criterion: it carries no flatness or fibre hypothesis, and
the number $r$ of generators of $I$ is not asserted to be minimal.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $A$, the polynomial algebra $P=A[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, elements $f_1,\dots,f_r\in P$, the ideal $I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ and $B=P/I$.

[F1] [[lem-differentials-polynomial-algebra-free]]: $\Omega_{P/A}$ is free with basis $\mathrm{d}x_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}x_n$, and for the derivations $\partial/\partial x_i$ with $\partial x_j/\partial x_i=\delta_{ij}$ one has $\mathrm{d}f=\sum_i(\partial f/\partial x_i)\,\mathrm{d}x_i$ for every $f\in P$.

[F2] [[thm-conormal-exact-sequence-algebra]]: for every ideal $J\subseteq P$ and $C=P/J$ the sequence $J/J^{2}\to C\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/A}\to\Omega_{C/A}\to0$ is exact, the first map sending the class of $x\in J$ to $1\otimes\mathrm{d}x$.

[F3] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: an $A$-derivation is additive, kills $A$ and satisfies Leibniz. The particular universal derivation on $P$ and the free $P$-basis $\mathrm{d}x_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}x_n$ of $\Omega_{P/A}$ come from [F1].

## Proof

1.1 The middle term. By [F1] the elements $\mathrm{d}x_1,\dots,\mathrm{d}x_n$ form a $P$-basis of $\Omega_{P/A}$. Since extension of scalars along $P\to B$ carries a free module with basis $\mathrm{d}x_i$ to the free $B$-module with basis $1\otimes\mathrm{d}x_i$, there is an isomorphism of $B$-modules $B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/A}\to B^{n}$ sending $1\otimes\mathrm{d}x_i$ to the standard basis vector $e_i$. [F1, algebra]

1.2 The conormal term. In $I/I^{2}$, every class is a $B$-linear combination of the classes $[f_1],\dots,[f_r]$: an element of $I$ has the form $\sum_{j}p_{j}f_{j}$ with $p_{j}\in P$, and by bilinearity of the class map $[xy]=x\,[y]$ for $x\in P$, $y\in I$, its class is $\sum_{j}(p_{j}+I)[f_{j}]$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 The Jacobian columns. By [F1] the universal derivation, which obeys the laws of [F3], satisfies $\mathrm{d}f_{j}=\sum_{i}(\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{i})\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}$, so the first map of the conormal sequence of [F2] sends $[f_{j}]$ to $1\otimes\mathrm{d}f_{j}=\sum_{i}(\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{i})\,(1\otimes\mathrm{d}x_{i})$, which under the identification of step 1.1 is the $j$-th column $(\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{1},\dots,\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{n})$ of the Jacobian matrix. [step 1.1, F1, F2, F3]

3.1 Conclusion. By the exactness of [F2] applied to the quotient $B=P/I$, the module $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the cokernel of the first map $I/I^{2}\to B\otimes_{P}\Omega_{P/A}$, which by steps 1.2 and 2.1 is the $B$-linear map with the Jacobian columns on a generating set of $I/I^{2}$; under step 1.1 this is the displayed presentation $\Omega_{B/A}\cong B^{n}/\sum_{j}B\cdot(\partial f_{j}/\partial x_{i})_{i}$. Since $A$, $n$ and the generating family $f_1,\dots,f_r$ were arbitrary, the presentation holds without additional hypotheses, and no smoothness conclusion is drawn from it. [step 1.2, step 2.1, F2] ∎
