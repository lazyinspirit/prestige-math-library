## Statement

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $p>0$, and let $K/F$ be a finite extension
of degree $p$. Then the following are equivalent:

1. $K/F$ is cyclic.
2. There exists $\alpha\in K$ and $a\in F$ such that $\alpha^p-\alpha=a$ and
   $x^p-x-a$ is irreducible over $F$.

When these conditions hold, the roots of $x^p-x-a$ in $K$ are exactly
$\alpha+i$ for $i\in\mathbb F_p$, so $K$ is already the splitting field and its
Galois group is generated by $\alpha\mapsto\alpha+1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$ of characteristic $p>0$ and a degree-$p$ extension $K/F$.

[F1] A cyclic extension is a finite Galois extension with cyclic Galois group ([[def-cyclic-extension]]).

[L1] In a cyclic extension, trace zero is equivalent to being of the form $\alpha-\sigma(\alpha)$ ([[thm-additive-hilberts-theorem-90]]).

[L2] A finite extension is Galois exactly when it is the splitting field of a separable polynomial ([[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For the forward direction, assume $K/F$ is cyclic and choose a generator $\sigma$ of its Galois group. Since $[K:F]=p$ and $\operatorname{char}F=p$, one has $$\operatorname{Tr}_{K/F}(-1)=-p=0.$$ By [L1], choose $\alpha\in K$ with $$-1=\alpha-\sigma(\alpha),$$ so $\sigma(\alpha)=\alpha+1$. [F1, L1, choose]

1.2 For the converse direction, assume $\alpha^p-\alpha=a\in F$ and $x^p-x-a$ is irreducible. For each $i\in\mathbb F_p$ one has $$(\alpha+i)^p-(\alpha+i)=\alpha^p-\alpha=a,$$ so the roots of the polynomial are exactly $\alpha+i$ for $i\in\mathbb F_p$. Thus all roots lie in $F(\alpha)$, the derivative is $-1\ne0$, and [L2] makes $F(\alpha)/F$ Galois. Because the polynomial is irreducible of degree $p$, this extension has degree $p$. [L2, algebra]

2.1 In characteristic $p$, one has $$(\alpha+1)^p-(\alpha+1)=\alpha^p-\alpha,$$ so the element $a:=\alpha^p-\alpha$ is fixed by $\sigma$ and therefore lies in $F$. Also $\alpha\notin F$ because step 1.1 gives $\sigma(\alpha)\ne\alpha$. Since $[K:F]=p$ is prime and $$F\subseteq F(\alpha)\subseteq K,$$ one must have $F(\alpha)=K$. The polynomial $x^p-x-a\in F[x]$ has root $\alpha$ and degree $p=[F(\alpha):F]$, so it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $F$ and is irreducible. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 The rule $\tau(\alpha)=\alpha+1$ permutes the root set and fixes $F$, so it extends to an $F$-automorphism of $F(\alpha)$. Its $p$-th power fixes $\alpha$ and each smaller positive power moves $\alpha$, so $\tau$ has order $p$. A degree-$p$ finite Galois extension has at most $p$ automorphisms, hence exactly the cyclic group generated by $\tau$. Therefore $F(\alpha)/F$ is cyclic of degree $p$. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 prove the equivalence, and the displayed root set in step 1.2 proves the final sentence. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
