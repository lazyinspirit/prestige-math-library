## Definition

Let $f:R\to S$ be a homomorphism from a unital associative ring to a noetherian ring $S$ (not necessarily commutative), assume $S$ is flat as a right $R$-module, and assume every finitely generated left $S$-module has a finite projective resolution. Fix universal determinant functors $g_R:D_{\mathrm{perf}}(R)\to\mathcal W(R)$ and $g_S:D_{\mathrm{perf}}(S)\to\mathcal W(S)$, a monoidal functor $N:\mathcal W(R)\to\mathcal W(S)$, a monoidal isomorphism $\mu:N\circ g_R\cong g_S\circ(S\otimes_R-)$, and the comparison isomorphism $\eta:K_0(R,S)\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi_0\mathcal F(N)$ of the relative determinant-fiber comparison theorem. For a perfect object $C\in D_{\mathrm{perf}}(R)$ write $C_S=S\otimes_R C$ and $H^{\mathrm{ev}}(C_S)=\bigoplus_{i\text{ even}}H^i(C_S)$, $H^{\mathrm{od}}(C_S)=\bigoplus_{i\text{ odd}}H^i(C_S)$. Given an $S$-module isomorphism $t:H^{\mathrm{ev}}(C_S)\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{\mathrm{od}}(C_S)$, the **refined relative Euler class** $\chi^{\mathrm{tri}}(C,t)\in K_0(R,S)$ is the inverse image under $\eta$ of the isomorphism class of $(g_R(C),\epsilon_t)\in\mathcal F(N)$. Here $\epsilon_t:N(g_R(C))\to1_{\mathcal W(S)}$ is induced by $\mu_C$ and the determinant isomorphism from $C_S$ to the alternating tensor product of the determinant objects of its cohomology modules, followed by the isomorphism induced by $t$ and cancellation of the even and odd factors. The determinant comparison is constructed from the truncation triangles and does not assert that $C_S$ is isomorphic to the direct sum of its cohomology modules. If $C_S\simeq0$, both cohomology modules are zero and $t:0\to0$ is unique; this yields the relative Euler class of the torsion perfect complex. For any finitely generated $R$-module $M$ with $S\otimes_R M=0$ and with the degree-zero stalk complex $S^0(M)$ perfect, write $\kappa_{R,S}(M)=\chi^{\mathrm{tri}}(S^0(M),0)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[def-relative-algebraic-k0-group]]: $K_0(R,S)$ is the group generated by relative triples with exact-sequence and composition relations.

[F2] [[def-universal-determinant-functor-on-a-perfect-derived-category]]: a universal determinant functor assigns an object of a Picard category to each perfect object and an isomorphism to each distinguished triangle.

[F3] [[thm-relative-k0-determinant-fiber-comparison]]: under the stated flatness and finite-projective-resolution hypotheses, the triple group is isomorphic to the component group of the determinant-functor homotopy fiber.

[F4] [[def-zero-and-stalk-complex]]: $S^0(M)$ is the complex with $M$ in degree zero and zero modules in every other degree.


[F5] Over a left Noetherian ring every finitely generated module is Noetherian, meaning each submodule is finitely generated. [[thm-finitely-generated-modules-over-noetherian-rings-are-noetherian]] [[def-noetherian-module]]

[F6] Projective modules split epimorphisms, and a finite projective is a summand of a finite free module. [[thm-projective-module-characterizations]]

[F7] Perfect objects form a triangulated subcategory, so are closed under finite shifts and cones. [[lem-perfect-complexes-form-a-triangulated-subcategory]]

[F8] The canonical triangles $\tau^{\leq i-1}X\to\tau^{\leq i}X\to H^i(X)[-i]\to$ are natural and distinguished. [[thm-canonical-truncations-fit-a-distinguished-triangle]]

## Construction and well-definedness

The comparison isomorphism $\eta$ and the universal determinant data are explicitly fixed in the original Definition. The construction below uses those supplied data; it does not replace the separate relative determinant-fiber comparison theorem by a claim that its existence has been proved here.

Represent $C$ by a bounded finite-projective complex $P$. Because $S$ is right $R$-flat, its scalar extension is represented by $S\otimes_RP$, still bounded finite projective. Its kernels, images and cohomology are finitely generated by [F5]. Every finitely generated $S$-module $M$ is perfect under the stated finite-resolution hypothesis, even if the supplied finite projective resolution initially has non-finitely-generated terms: choose finite free covers of successive syzygies. The syzygies remain finitely generated by [F5]. To see that this process stops in projectives after the length $d$ of a supplied projective resolution, compare the two resolutions by finite Schanuel induction. For two projective presentations $P\to M$, $Q\to M$ with kernels $K,L$, their pullback splits over both projectives by [F6], giving $K\oplus Q\cong L\oplus P$. Repeating this identity on the successive presentations shows the $d$th finite-free syzygy is stably isomorphic to the last projective syzygy of the supplied resolution, with only projective summands added. It is therefore projective. It is finitely generated by construction, so truncation yields a bounded finite-projective resolution. Length zero is the given projective module itself. Only finitely many covers and splittings are chosen for each of the finitely many modules at issue.

Put $X=C_S$. All its cohomology stalks are thus perfect. If its cohomology is supported in $[a,b]$, start with $\tau^{\leq a-1}X=0$ in the derived category and use the triangles in [F8] successively up to $b$. By [F7] all their objects are perfect, so $g_S$ applies. The resulting finite composite gives an isomorphism from $g_S(X)$ to the ordered tensor of $g_S(H^i(X)[-i])$. Each shift triangle $Y\to0\to Y[1]\to$ gives the evaluation identifying $g_S(Y[1])$ with the inverse of $g_S(Y)$; iterate this specified evaluation finitely often to replace the shifted factors by the alternating positive and inverse factors. Use the split-triangle maps to identify the even factors with $g_S(H^{\mathrm{ev}}(X))$ and the odd factors with the inverse of $g_S(H^{\mathrm{od}}(X))$.

Here and throughout, order the degree factors increasingly before collecting parities; keep the actual symmetry maps of the Picard category, including self-symmetries. For an inverse object the evaluation uniquely specifies its arrows: tensoring by an invertible object is fully faithful. Reassociation and evaluation cancellation therefore respect the associativity, symmetry and unit equations. Changing the finite parenthesization is a sequence of those equations; changing the truncation bracketing is the octahedral-filtration equation of the determinant functor in [F2]. Padding either endpoint by zero adds unit maps only. Naturality of [F8] and of the determinant maps proves that this cohomology comparison respects isomorphisms of $X$. In particular it is independent of the bounded representative and finite resolutions used to establish perfectness: those resolutions are not used to define any arrow of the comparison. No splitting of $X$ into its cohomology objects is used.

Now compose $\mu_C$ with this comparison. Apply $g_S(t)$ on the positive even factor and evaluate it against the inverse odd factor. This gives exactly $\epsilon_t:N(g_R(C))\to1$. It is an isomorphism, so $(g_R(C),\epsilon_t)$ is an object of the stated fiber category. Its isomorphism class has a unique inverse image under the supplied isomorphism $\eta$, defining $\chi^{\mathrm{tri}}(C,t)$. If $X\simeq0$, all cohomology stalks vanish and every map above reduces to the unit comparison; the only map $t:0\to0$ is its identity. Finally a supplied perfect stalk $S^0(M)$ with $S\otimes_RM=0$ lies in this acyclic branch, giving $\kappa_{R,S}(M)$ as stated. Zero modules, the zero ring and empty degree supports are included. The construction uses neither a maximal-order assumption nor Smith form; arbitrary noncommutative projective-order inputs satisfying the original hypotheses remain within its scope.
