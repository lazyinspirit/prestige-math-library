## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, the Vogan classes of its real forms and their realized Satake classes both classify the real forms up to real Lie isomorphism. In particular, for real forms $\mathfrak g_0,\mathfrak g_0'$,
$$V(\mathfrak g_0)=V(\mathfrak g_0')\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathfrak g_0\cong\mathfrak g_0'\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S(\mathfrak g_0)=S(\mathfrak g_0').$$
Thus $V(\mathfrak g_0)\mapsto S(\mathfrak g_0)$ is a bijection onto the realized Satake classes. Satake equivalence means isomorphism of decorated Dynkin diagrams, as in [[def-satake-diagram]]. Vogan equivalence is that of [[def-vogan-diagram]]. These decorations are different: Vogan uses a maximally compact Cartan and paints noncompact imaginary simple roots, whereas Satake uses a maximally split Cartan, blackens imaginary simple roots, and pairs white simple roots with equal nonzero restrictions to the split part.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebra and real forms of the statement. For a real form write $\theta$ for a Cartan involution, $\sigma$ for real conjugation, $\mathfrak h_0=\mathfrak t_0\oplus\mathfrak a_0$ for a maximally split theta-stable Cartan, and $\Delta_0\subseteq\Delta$ for its black simple roots. Put $Q=\mathbb Z\Delta$ and $Q_0=\mathbb Z\Delta_0$.

[A1] AC is assumed as [[def-axiom-of-choice]], including the structure-theory suppliers below.

[L1] Vogan classes classify real forms. Its proof constructs initial theta-stable Cartans and normalizes simple triples by $f_i=-\kappa e_i$, $[e_i,f_i]=h_i$, where $\kappa=\theta\sigma$ is compact conjugation ([[thm-classification-of-real-forms-by-vogan-diagrams]]).

[L2] Cartan involutions are conjugate. The Cayley theorem supplies maximally split Cartans, their conjugacy by real inner automorphisms, and absence of noncompact imaginary roots. For a fixed Cartan involution, maximal abelian subspaces of $\mathfrak p_0$ are conjugate by the compact group $K$ ([[thm-conjugacy-of-cartan-involutions]], [[thm-cayley-transforms-connect-theta-stable-cartans-in-the-classification]], [[thm-maximal-abelian-subspaces-of-p-are-conjugate-by-k]]).

[L3] The Satake definition proves compatible bases exist. If $t$ denotes the root action of $\theta$, then $t\delta=\delta$ for black $\delta$, and for each white simple $\alpha$ there is a unique white $\epsilon\alpha$ with $-t\alpha-\epsilon\alpha\in Q_0$. The involution $\epsilon$ fixes unpaired white vertices and exchanges each arrow pair ([[def-satake-diagram]]). The integral assertion follows because these are integral simple-root expansions.

[L4] The complex root decomposition has one-dimensional nonzero spaces, root brackets add weights, opposite brackets are $[E,F]=B(E,F)H_\alpha$, and $B$ is invariant and pairs only opposite root spaces. Simple roots are a basis of the root lattice. The root metric is positive definite and preserved by automorphisms ([[thm-root-space-decomposition-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[thm-root-spaces-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-are-one-dimensional]], [[prop-brackets-of-root-spaces]], [[prop-bracket-of-opposite-root-spaces-is-the-root-line-in-the-cartan-subalgebra]], [[prop-killing-form-orthogonality-of-root-spaces]], [[prop-trace-forms-are-symmetric-and-invariant]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[prop-the-roots-form-a-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[L5] Serre presentations identify the algebras from normalized positive and negative simple generators, and assignments preserving the relations extend uniquely ([[thm-serre-presentation-theorem]], [[def-serre-lie-algebra-of-a-finite-type-cartan-matrix]]). Smith normal form applies to an integer matrix ([[thm-smith-normal-form-existence-over-a-pid]]).

[L6] For a Cartan decomposition, $B_\theta$ is positive, $B$ is negative on $\mathfrak k_0$ and positive on $\mathfrak p_0$, with the standard bracket inclusions. Restricted spaces are real eigenspaces, $\theta\mathfrak g_0^\lambda=\mathfrak g_0^{-\lambda}$, and their zero space is $\mathfrak m\oplus\mathfrak a_0$, where $\mathfrak m=Z_{\mathfrak k_0}(\mathfrak a_0)$ ([[prop-bracket-relations-and-killing-signs-in-a-cartan-decomposition]], [[thm-restricted-root-space-decomposition]]).

[L7] The automorphism group of a real semisimple algebra is a closed Lie group with Lie algebra $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak g_0$, and the algebra has zero center. Closed subgroups are Lie groups; maximal tori of a compact connected group are conjugate ([[cor-the-lie-algebra-of-the-automorphism-group-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra]], [[cor-semisimple-lie-algebras-are-centerless-and-perfect]], [[thm-cartans-closed-subgroup-theorem]], [[thm-conjugacy-of-maximal-tori]]). Weyl chambers of a reduced root system are conjugate by root reflections ([[thm-the-weyl-group-acts-simply-transitively-on-weyl-chambers]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We first justify choice independence for Satake diagrams. Use $K=(\operatorname{Aut}(\mathfrak g_0)\cap O(B_\theta))^0$. This is compact and connected by [L7], has Lie algebra $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak k_0$, and commutes with $\theta$: preservation of both $B$ and $B_\theta$ is equivalent to that commutation. Differentiation, centerlessness and exponentiation give the Lie algebra assertion. Its elements are products of $\exp(\operatorname{ad}X)$ with $X\in\mathfrak k_0$, since an exponential identity neighborhood generates a connected group. Changes of involution can be absorbed by real automorphisms by [L2]. For fixed $\theta$, the split part $\mathfrak a_0$ of a maximally split Cartan is maximal abelian in $\mathfrak p_0$: [L6] gives $Z_{\mathfrak g_0}(\mathfrak a_0)=\mathfrak m\oplus\mathfrak a_0$ with $\mathfrak m\subseteq\mathfrak k_0$. Thus [L2] supplies $k\in K$ aligning the split parts of two choices. After this alignment their compact parts are maximal abelian in $\mathfrak m=Z_{\mathfrak k_0}(\mathfrak a_0)$, since any further commuting element would centralize the Cartan and hence belong to it by self-normalization. Maximal-torus conjugacy in $Z_K(\mathfrak a_0)^0$ then aligns those compact parts without moving $\mathfrak a_0$. Hence the whole maximally split Cartans are $K$-conjugate. It remains to compare compatible positive systems on one Cartan. [A1, L2, L6, L7, [[def-cartan-subalgebra-of-a-lie-algebra]], algebra]

1.2 The decorated diagram determines the full root involution $t$. Its action on black roots is the identity. For a white simple root $\alpha$, write $t\alpha=-\epsilon\alpha+v$, with $v\in\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Delta_0$ by [L3]. For every black $\delta$, orthogonality and $t\delta=\delta$ give $(v,\delta)=(\alpha+\epsilon\alpha,\delta)$. The Gram matrix on the independent black roots is positive definite, so these equations determine $v$ uniquely. The root metric can be chosen canonically from the Killing form on each simple component: the trace formula $B(H,H')=\sum_{\gamma\in\Phi}\gamma(H)\gamma(H')$ shows that a based root isomorphism preserves this normalization, including permuted isomorphic components. Thus a decorated diagram isomorphism intertwines the two root involutions. [L3, L4, algebra]

2.1 Each reflection of a restricted-root hyperplane is realized in $K$. Choose $0\ne E\in\mathfrak g_0^\lambda$. By [L6], $[E,\theta E]$ lies in $\mathfrak a_0$, and pairing it with $H\in\mathfrak a_0$ gives $[E,\theta E]=B(E,\theta E)H_\lambda$, where $B(H_\lambda,H)=\lambda(H)$ and $B(E,\theta E)<0$. Rescale $E$ positively so this coefficient is $-2/|\lambda|^2$, and put $h=2H_\lambda/|\lambda|^2$. Then $(E,-\theta E,h)$ is an $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ triple. The automorphism $k=\exp((\pi/2)\operatorname{ad}(E+\theta E))$ belongs to $K$, fixes $\ker\lambda$ in $\mathfrak a_0$, and sends $h$ to $-h$: on $h$ and $E-\theta E$ the adjoint generator gives a rotation through $\pi$. Thus it induces the reflection and permutes restricted roots. These reflections act transitively on restricted chambers: a generic polygonal path between two regular points crosses one distinct root hyperplane at a time, and reflection in the crossing wall interchanges its two adjacent chambers while preserving the entire finite arrangement. Induction on the crossings proves transitivity. Coincident hyperplanes from $\lambda$ and $2\lambda$ are a single wall, so nonreduced systems cause no problem. [L6, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Suppose two real forms have isomorphic Satake diagrams. In each choose normalized simple triples with compact conjugation $\kappa=\theta\sigma$: the argument in [L1] uses only theta-stability of the Cartan, not maximal compactness. Explicitly $\mathfrak u=\mathfrak k_0\oplus i\mathfrak p_0$ is compact, $-B(Z,\kappa Z)>0$, and after positive rescaling $f_i=-\kappa e_i$ satisfies $[e_i,f_i]=h_i$. Match their based diagrams and use [L5] to identify all $e_i,f_i,h_i$. This identifies the compact conjugations, since their actions are $e_i\mapsto-f_i$, $f_i\mapsto-e_i$, $h_i\mapsto-h_i$, antilinearly. Hence work in one complex algebra with one $\kappa$ and two compact-preserving involutions $\theta,\theta'$ having the same root action $t$, by step 1.2. [L1, L4, L5, L6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Choose a product of these reflections to align the restricted positive systems. Its representative $k\in N_K(\mathfrak a_0)$ might move $\mathfrak t_0$. Both $k\mathfrak t_0$ and $\mathfrak t_0$ are maximal abelian in $\mathfrak m$: any further commuting compact vector centralizes the Cartan and hence belongs to it. The compact group $M=Z_K(\mathfrak a_0)$ has Lie algebra $\operatorname{ad}\mathfrak m$. Applying maximal-torus conjugacy in $M^0$, using the maximal-abelian/torus correspondence of [L2], gives $m\in M^0$ aligning these compact parts. Thus $mk$ normalizes the whole Cartan while still aligning restricted positives. After transport, the two compatible positive systems differ only on imaginary roots. Those roots form the reduced root subsystem $\Phi\cap\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\Delta_0$: a positive root restricting to zero has no white coefficient, since each nonzero simple restriction is positive on a common regular split vector. Apply [L7] to this subsystem to align its positive systems. Each of its reflections fixes $\mathfrak a_0$ and commutes with $t$, so preserves all nonzero root restrictions. Consequently it aligns the full positive systems and preserves the black vertices and pair relation. Their Satake diagrams are therefore isomorphic. Empty restricted or imaginary systems require no reflections. Real isomorphisms transport all these data, proving the Satake assignment is well defined. [L2, L3, L4, L7, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 The automorphism $A=\theta'\theta^{-1}$ fixes the Cartan pointwise and multiplies each root line by a scalar $c(\alpha)$. These scalars extend to a character $c:Q\to U(1)$. Indeed the simple scalars define a character on the free lattice; the corresponding diagonal assignment $e_i\mapsto c_i e_i$, $f_i\mapsto c_i^{-1}f_i$, $h_i\mapsto h_i$ preserves all Serre relations and agrees with $A$ on the generators, so agrees everywhere. Compact preservation gives $|c_i|=1$ by pairing $e_i$ with $-\kappa e_i$. From $\theta'^2=1$ follows $c(t\lambda)=c(\lambda)^{-1}$. On every black simple root both involutions act as $+1$, because the root is compact imaginary, so $c$ is trivial on $Q_0$. [L2, L4, L5, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The character $c$ is trivial on $Q^t=\{\lambda\in Q:t\lambda=\lambda\}$. To prove it, reduce the simple-root coefficient vector of such a $\lambda$ modulo $Q_0$. By [L3], $t$ acts on white classes by minus the permutation $\epsilon$. A fixed vector has zero coefficient at every unpaired white vertex and opposite coefficients at the two vertices of each pair. For a chosen member $\alpha$ of each pair, the invariant vector $\alpha+t\alpha$ has white coefficient vector $[\alpha]-[\epsilon\alpha]$. Subtract the appropriate integral multiples of these invariant vectors from $\lambda$; the remainder lies in $Q_0$. But $c(\alpha+t\alpha)=c(\alpha)c(t\alpha)=1$ by step 3.2 and $c(Q_0)=1$, proving the claim. This uses the invariant lattice, not just its real span. [L3, step 3.2, algebra]

5.1 Define a character on $(t-1)Q$ by $b((t-1)\lambda)=c(t\lambda)$. It is well defined: two preimages differ by a member of $Q^t$, on which $c$ is one. It extends to a character $b:Q\to U(1)$. In fact Smith normal form in [L5] gives a basis $q_1,\ldots,q_r$ of $Q$ in which $(t-1)Q$ is generated by $d_1q_1,\ldots,d_sq_s$, with positive integers $d_j$. Choose a $d_j$-th root in $U(1)$ of each prescribed value to define $b(q_j)$, and set the remaining basis values to one. Only finitely many roots are selected. Let $D$ be the diagonal Serre automorphism with root scalar $b(\alpha)$. Unit modulus implies $D\kappa=\kappa D$ on all generators. On the $\alpha$ root line, $D\theta D^{-1}$ differs from $\theta$ by $b(t\alpha)/b(\alpha)=c(t\alpha)$, exactly the factor in $\theta'=A\theta$. The maps also agree on the Cartan, hence $D\theta D^{-1}=\theta'$. Consequently $D$ intertwines $\sigma=\theta\kappa$ with $\sigma'=\theta'\kappa$ and restricts to an isomorphism of real forms. Satake classes are therefore injective on real isomorphism classes. [L5, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 By [L1] Vogan classes classify the real forms. By step 3.1 and step 5.1 Satake classes do also, with surjectivity onto the realized classes by their definition. Composing these two classifications gives the asserted equivalences and bijection. The zero algebra has one empty diagram; all-black diagrams have $Q_0=Q$, while all-white diagrams have $Q_0=0$, and the invariant-lattice proof treats both. Rank-one lattices and permuted components are covered by the same finite-basis argument. The stated difference between the decorations is their defining choice of Cartan and root labels, not an assertion that their drawings coincide. [A1, L1, L3, step 3.1, step 5.1] ∎
