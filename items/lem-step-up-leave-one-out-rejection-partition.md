## Statement

Let $m\ge1$, $p=(p_1,\ldots,p_m)\in[0,1]^m$, and critical values $0<c_1\le\cdots\le c_m\le1$. Give $[0,1]^m$ its finite product of the trace Borel sigma-algebras on $[0,1]$. Define the step-up rule by

$$R:=\max\bigl(\{k\in\{1,\ldots,m\}:p_{(k)}\le c_k\}\cup\{0\}\bigr),$$

rejecting exactly ranks $1,\ldots,R$. Fix $i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$. Give the remaining-coordinate space $[0,1]^{m-1}$ its finite product of the trace Borel sigma-algebras, with the empty product on the singleton empty tuple when $m=1$. Write the remaining values in increasing order as $y_{(1)}\le\cdots\le y_{(m-1)}$, and set $y_{(0)}=0$. For $1\le k\le m$, let

$$C_{i,k}:=\{y_{(k-1)}\le c_k\}\cap\bigcap_{r=k}^{m-1}\{y_{(r)}>c_{r+1}\},$$

with an empty intersection interpreted as the whole remaining-coordinate space. Put $D_{i,k}:=\bigcup_{j=1}^k C_{i,j}$. For $x\in[0,1]^m$, let $I_i(x)=1$ if the rule rejects coordinate $i$, and $I_i(x)=0$ otherwise. Then:

1. For every $k$, the following identity of subsets of $[0,1]^m$ holds:
   $$\{x\in[0,1]^m:I_i(x)=1,\ R(x)=k\}=\{x\in[0,1]^m:x_i\le c_k\}\cap\{x\in[0,1]^m:x_{-i}\in C_{i,k}\}.$$
   In particular, every input in the right-hand side has exactly $k$ rejections and rejects $i$.
2. The sets $C_{i,1},\ldots,C_{i,m}$ are a disjoint Borel partition of $[0,1]^{m-1}$.
3. For $k<m$,
   $$D_{i,k}=\bigcap_{r=k}^{m-1}\{y_{(r)}>c_{r+1}\},$$
   and $D_{i,m}$ is the whole space. Each $D_{i,k}$ is a coordinatewise increasing Borel set.

For $m=1$, the remaining-coordinate space is the singleton empty tuple with its full sigma-algebra, and $C_{i,1}=D_{i,1}$ is the whole space.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $m\ge1$, a p-value vector, nondecreasing critical values $0<c_1\le\cdots\le c_m\le1$, and the displayed step-up rule.

[F1] [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]: closed half-lines and finite unions and intersections of their inverse images are Borel.

[F2] [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]: a map is measurable when the preimage of each Borel set is measurable.

[F3] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]: the finite product sigma-algebra is generated by measurable rectangles, so coordinate cylinders with Borel bases are measurable.

[F4] [[thm-seven-generators-of-the-borel-sigma-algebra-on-r]]: the closed left rays $(-\infty,t]$ generate $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$, since they are complements of the open right rays in the theorem's list.

## Proof

1.1 For $1\le r\le m-1$ and $t\in\mathbb R$, $\{y_{(r)}\le t\}$ is the finite union over $r$-element index sets $J$ of $\bigcap_{j\in J}\{y_j\le t\}$, since at least $r$ remaining values are at most $t$ exactly when the $r$th ordered value is at most $t$. Each coordinate cylinder is measurable in the stated finite product structure by [F1] and [F3], so these sublevel sets are Borel. Since the closed left rays generate $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$ by [F4], each order statistic is Borel by [F2]. The coordinate-deletion projection from $[0,1]^m$ to $[0,1]^{m-1}$ is measurable: preimages of measurable rectangles are finite intersections of coordinate cylinders, and the rectangles generate the product sigma-algebra by [F3]. Thus lifting a Borel set in the remaining-coordinate space gives a Borel set in the full product. If $y\le z$ coordinatewise, at least $r$ coordinates of $z$ are at most $z_{(r)}$; their corresponding coordinates of $y$ are also at most $z_{(r)}$, so $y_{(r)}\le z_{(r)}$. Every remaining-coordinate order statistic is therefore coordinatewise nondecreasing. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Fix $k$ and suppose $p_i\le c_k$. Rank $k$ passes exactly when at least $k-1$ other coordinates are at most $c_k$, which is $y_{(k-1)}\le c_k$; for $k=1$ this holds by $y_{(0)}=0$. For each $j>k$, since $p_i\le c_k\le c_j$, rank $j$ passes exactly when at least $j-1$ other coordinates are at most $c_j$, namely $y_{(j-1)}\le c_j$. Hence $R=k$ exactly when the first condition of $C_{i,k}$ holds and every higher rank fails, which is $p_{-i}\in C_{i,k}$. If $k=m$, $i$ is automatically among the first $k$ ranks. If $k<m$ and $i$ is not among the first $k$ ranks, then $p_{(k+1)}\le p_i\le c_k\le c_{k+1}$ would make rank $k+1$ pass. Conversely, if $R=k$ and $i$ is rejected, then $p_i\le p_{(k)}\le c_k$, rank $k$ passes and every higher rank fails, so the conditions defining $C_{i,k}$ hold. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For any remaining vector $y$, replace coordinate $i$ by $0$. Since $c_1>0$, rank one passes. Every rank occupied by a zero-valued coordinate also passes because all critical values are positive, so $R$ includes all zero-valued coordinates and rejects the inserted coordinate $i$. Thus the resulting rejection count is some $r\in\{1,\ldots,m\}$. By step 2.1, $y\in C_{i,r}$; hence the sets cover the space. If $k<\ell$ and $y\in C_{i,k}$, its condition at $r=\ell-1$ gives $y_{(\ell-1)}>c_\ell$, while membership in $C_{i,\ell}$ would give $y_{(\ell-1)}\le c_\ell$. Thus the sets are disjoint. They are Borel by step 1.1 and their finite defining inequalities. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $y\in C_{i,j}$ for some $j\le k<m$, the defining inequalities include $y_{(r)}>c_{r+1}$ for every $r\ge k$, so $D_{i,k}$ is contained in the displayed intersection. Conversely, suppose those inequalities hold and set $p_i=0$. Rank one passes, and by the zero-coordinate argument in step 3.1 the inserted coordinate $i$ is rejected. No rank $\ell\ge k+1$ can pass: that would require $y_{(\ell-1)}\le c_\ell$, contrary to the assumed condition at $r=\ell-1$. Thus the rejection count is some $j\le k$, and step 2.1 puts $y$ in $C_{i,j}$. This proves the formula; when $k=m$, step 3.1 gives $D_{i,m}$ equal to the whole space. The formula is a finite intersection of Borel order-statistic superlevel sets and is coordinatewise increasing by step 1.1. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The result is choice-free. It applies to BH and BY after substituting their respective nondecreasing critical-value sequences.
