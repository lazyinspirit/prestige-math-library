## Statement

For a reflection $t=(a\,b)$ with $a<b$, fix the hyperplane equation $c_t:=\beta_{ab}=x_a-x_b$ of [[def-type-a-reflection-realization-and-polynomial-ring]]. For $y\in S_n$ put $p_y:=\prod_{t:\,yt<y}c_t\in R$. Let $M$ be a
Bott–Samelson bimodule or a direct summand of one, with its support submodules
$\Gamma_yM\subseteq\Gamma_{\le y}M\subseteq M$ and
$\Gamma_yM\subseteq\Gamma_{\ge y}M\subseteq M$ and the $y$-layer quotients
$\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M=\Gamma_{\le y}M/\Gamma_{<y}M$ and
$\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M=\Gamma_{\ge y}M/\Gamma_{>y}M$ of
[[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]], and
write $\Gamma^yM:=M/\Gamma_{\ne y}M$ for Soergel's $y$-layer quotient. Then:

1. the natural morphism $\Gamma_yM\to\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$, the
   inclusion of the $y$-layer into the lower support submodule followed by the
   quotient map, is injective with image the submodule
   $p_y\cdot\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ obtained as the image of right
   multiplication by $p_y$, so it becomes an isomorphism after inverting $p_y$,
   that is $\Gamma_yM\cong(\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M)p_y$, and the $y$-layer
   is generated by the lower $y$-layer after inverting the hyperplane equations
   of the reflections $t$ with $yt<y$;
2. dually, the natural morphism $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\to\Gamma^yM=M/\Gamma_{\ne y}M$
   is injective with image the image $p_y\cdot\Gamma^yM$ of right multiplication
   by $p_y$, so it becomes an isomorphism after the same inversion, that is
   $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\cong(\Gamma^yM)p_y$ and the upper $y$-layer is
   determined by the $y$-layer quotient after inverting $p_y$;
3. in particular the graded ranks satisfy
   $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma_yM=\sum_\nu(M:\nabla_y(\nu))v^{\ell(y)-\nu}$ and
   $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma^yM=\sum_\nu(M:\Delta_y(\nu))v^{-\ell(y)-\nu}$, so
   that each graded rank determines the corresponding $y$-multiplicities of its support flag.

The inclusion chains displayed above are two separate chains: the lower and the
upper support submodule are not nested in general, and because the cutoffs of
[[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]] are the
Bruhat principal sets $\{x:x\le y\}$ and $\{x:x\ge y\}$ of Soergel's
Notation 6.1, their intersection is exactly
$\Gamma_yM=\Gamma_{\le y}M\cap\Gamma_{\ge y}M$. This is Soergel's Satz 6.6 in
the notation of his Notation 6.1; the localization input is his Lemma 6.10, quoted in the Facts
block, and the rank identities are the computation of Satz 6.6's proof.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** An element $y\in S_n$, the polynomial ring $R$ with the reflections of $S_n$ and their fixed hyperplanes in $V=\operatorname{Spec}R$, a Bott–Samelson bimodule $M$ or a direct summand of one, and the elements $p_y=\prod_{yt<y}c_t$.


[F1] $\Gamma_yM$ is the support sub-bimodule on the single graph $Gr(y)$; $\Gamma_{\le y}M=\Gamma_{\{x: x\le y\}}M$ and $\Gamma_{\ge y}M=\Gamma_{\{x: x\ge y\}}M$ are the Bruhat lower and upper support submodules, so that both contain $\Gamma_yM$ and their intersection is $\Gamma_yM$; $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M=\Gamma_{\le y}M/\Gamma_{<y}M$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M=\Gamma_{\ge y}M/\Gamma_{>y}M$ are the two $y$-layer quotients and $\Gamma^yM=M/\Gamma_{\ne y}M$ is the $y$-layer quotient of Notation 6.1; the filtration multiplicity $(M:\Delta_y(d))$ counts $\Delta_y(d)$ in the upper $y$-layer and $(M:\nabla_y(d))$ counts $\nabla_y(d)$ in the lower $y$-layer ([[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]).

[F2] $M$ has finitely many support layers, all of the form $Gr(x)$ for $x\in S_n$, and each layer is a finite direct sum of standard bimodules, so every $\Gamma$-submodule of $M$ is finitely generated and the multiplicities of [F1] are defined and intrinsic ([[lem-type-a-support-filtration-multiplicities-are-intrinsic]], [[lem-bott-samelson-bimodules-have-delta-and-nabla-support-filtrations]]).

[F3] The two $y$-layer quotients are finite direct sums of shifted standards, $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M\cong\bigoplus_\nu\nabla_y(\nu)^{(M:\nabla_y(\nu))}$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\cong\bigoplus_\nu\Delta_y(\nu)^{(M:\Delta_y(\nu))}$, with the multiplicities of [F1] (Bruhat-refining enumeration of Soergel's Lemma 6.3, recorded in the definition) ([[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]).

[F4] For a Bott–Samelson bimodule $B$, evaluation at the generator identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_{R\text{-}R}(R_y,B)$ with $\Gamma_yB$, and the four $y$-layers $\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}B$, $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}B$, $\Gamma^yB$ and $\Gamma_yB$ are free graded right $R$-modules on which $R\otimes_kR$ acts through $R_y$ (Soergel Proposition 6.4, recorded in the definition). These properties pass to a direct summand $M=eB$: the support functors, their quotients, and the evaluation map commute with the idempotent $e$, while a graded summand of a finite free module over the positively graded polynomial ring $R$ is graded free by graded Nakayama. Thus the same evaluation and freeness statements hold for every $M$ in the statement ([[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]).

[F5] Imported (Soergel Satz 6.6, with the observation at the start of the proof of Lemma 6.13 that the isomorphism $\Gamma_yN\cong(\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}N)p_y$ of Satz 6.6 holds for every $N$ in the additive closure of the Bott–Samelson bimodules as well): for $M$ a Bott–Samelson bimodule or a direct summand of one the natural morphisms $\Gamma_yM\to\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\to\Gamma^yM$ are injective with images the images $p_y\cdot\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ and $p_y\cdot\Gamma^yM$ of right multiplication by $p_y$, and they induce the isomorphisms $\Gamma_yM\cong(\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M)p_y$ and $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\cong(\Gamma^yM)p_y$ ([[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]).



## Proof

1.1 Injectivity and image: [F5] provides both natural morphisms with their injectivity and their images, and the two localisation statements are equivalent to these: localising the inclusion $p_y\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M\subseteq\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ at $p_y$ gives an equality because every element $n$ of the localised target is $(1/p_y)(p_y n)$ with $p_y n$ in the image, and conversely a morphism which is injective with $p_y$-divisible image has localisation an isomorphism; the same argument applies to $\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M\to\Gamma^yM$. [F5]
1.2 Ranks of the layer quotients: by [F1] and [F3] the two $y$-layer quotients are direct sums of copies of $\nabla_y(\nu)=R_y\{-\ell(y)-\nu\}$ and of $\Delta_y(\nu)=R_y\{\ell(y)-\nu\}$, so their graded ranks are $\operatorname{rk}\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M=\sum_\nu(M:\nabla_y(\nu))v^{-\ell(y)-\nu}$ and $\operatorname{rk}\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M=\sum_\nu(M:\Delta_y(\nu))v^{\ell(y)-\nu}$; the element $p_y$ is homogeneous of degree $2\ell(y)$, because each $c_t$ is a linear form of degree $2$ and the number of reflections $t$ with $yt<y$ equals $\ell(y)$, and multiplication by a nonzero homogeneous element of a free graded right $R$-module is injective and raises degrees by its own degree; the sums are finite and their coefficients are the intrinsic multiplicities of [F2]. [F1, F2, F3, F4]
2.1 The two rank identities: by step 1.1 the modules $\Gamma_yM$ and $p_y\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M$ are isomorphic, so by step 1.2 $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma_yM=v^{2\ell(y)}\operatorname{rk}\underline{\Gamma}_{\le y}M=\sum_\nu(M:\nabla_y(\nu))v^{\ell(y)-\nu}$; dually $\Gamma^yM$ is isomorphic to the quotient, inverse to the inclusion, of $p_y\Gamma^yM$, so $\operatorname{rk}\Gamma^yM=v^{-2\ell(y)}\operatorname{rk}\underline{\Gamma}_{\ge y}M=\sum_\nu(M:\Delta_y(\nu))v^{-\ell(y)-\nu}$, both computations using that the four modules of [F4] are free and hence $p_y$-torsion-free. [F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]
3.1 Conclusion: claims (1) and (2) are steps 1.1 and 2.1 read together with [F5], and claim (3) is the pair of rank identities of step 2.1; the result is stated for Bott–Samelson bimodules and their direct summands because [F5] is, and no statement about the full Hom formula for arbitrary summands is used. ∎ [step 1.1, step 2.1]
