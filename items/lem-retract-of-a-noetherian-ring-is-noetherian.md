## Statement

Let $R'$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $R\subseteq R'$ be a subring
([[def-subring]]), so that $R'$ becomes an $R$-algebra through the inclusion and
in particular an $R$-module ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]). Suppose
there is a map

$$\rho\colon R'\longrightarrow R$$

that is $R$-linear ([[def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]]) and
restricts to the identity on $R$, that is $\rho(x)=x$ for every $x\in R$. Then
$R$ is Noetherian.

The map $\rho$ is **not** assumed to be a ring homomorphism; additivity and
$\rho(ax)=a\rho(x)$ for $a\in R$ are all that is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R'$, a subring $R\subseteq R'$, and an $R$-linear $\rho:R'\to R$ with $\rho(x)=x$ for $x\in R$. For an ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $R$ write $\mathfrak aR'$ for the ideal of $R'$ generated by the subset $\mathfrak a$.

[L1] A subset $S \subseteq R$ is a **subring** of $R$ when (T1) $1_R \in S$; (T2) $x, y \in S$ implies $x + y \in S$; (T3) $x \in S$ implies $-x \in S$; (T4) $x, y \in S$ implies $xy \in S$ ([[def-subring]]).

[L2] An **$R$-algebra** is a unital ring $A$ together with a unital ring homomorphism $\eta_A:R\to A$ whose image is central; the induced scalar action is $ra:=\eta_A(r)a$, making $A$ an $R$-module ([[def-algebra-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L3] A function $f:M\to N$ between left $R$-modules is an **$R$-module homomorphism** if $f(m+m')=f(m)+f(m')$ and $f(rm)=rf(m)$ for all $m,m'\in M$ and $r\in R$ ([[def-module-homomorphism-kernel-image-and-cokernel]]).

[L4] For a commutative ring, being Noetherian is equivalent to every ideal being finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[L5] If a left module $M$ over a ring is finitely generated and $S\subseteq M$ satisfies $\langle S\rangle_R=M$, then some finite subset of $S$ already generates $M$ ([[lem-finite-generating-subset-of-a-generating-set]]).

[L6] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and $(a)=Ra$; the empty sum is included and equals $0$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L7] For a ring $R$, a left $R$-module $M$ and $S\subseteq M$, the submodule $\langle S\rangle_R$ is the set of finite sums $\sum_{i=1}^{k}r_is_i$ with $k\in\mathbb N$, $r_i\in R$ and $s_i\in S$, the term with $k=0$ being $0_M$ ([[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix an ideal $\mathfrak a$ of $R$. Because $R$ is a subring of $R'$ the two rings share the identity, so $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak aR'$, each $a\in\mathfrak a$ being $a\cdot 1_{R'}$; and $\rho$ is additive with $\rho(ax)=a\rho(x)$ for $a\in R$ and $x\in R'$, since $R'$ carries the $R$-action $a\cdot x=ax$ coming from the inclusion. [L1, L2, L3, given]

2.1 The ideal $\mathfrak aR'$ of $R'$ is exactly the set of finite sums $\sum_i a_ix_i$ with $a_i\in\mathfrak a$ and $x_i\in R'$, which is also the $R'$-submodule of $R'$ generated by the subset $\mathfrak a$; and $\mathfrak aR'$ is finitely generated because $R'$ is Noetherian. Applying the finite-subset lemma to the generating set $\mathfrak a$ of that module produces finitely many elements $a_1,\ldots,a_n\in\mathfrak a$, with $n\in\mathbb N$, generating $\mathfrak aR'$. [L4, L5, L6, L7, step 1.1, given]

3.1 Let $a\in\mathfrak a$. By step 2.1 and the description of a generated ideal, $a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_ia_i$ for some $x_1,\ldots,x_n\in R'$. Applying $\rho$ and using $\rho(a)=a$ together with $R$-linearity, and noting $a_i\in R$, gives $a=\rho(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_i\rho(x_i)$ with every $\rho(x_i)\in R$. [L3, L6, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Hence $\mathfrak a\subseteq(a_1,\ldots,a_n)R$, and the reverse inclusion holds because each $a_i$ lies in $\mathfrak a$; so $\mathfrak a=(a_1,\ldots,a_n)R$ is finitely generated. As $\mathfrak a$ was arbitrary, every ideal of $R$ is finitely generated and $R$ is Noetherian. [L4, L6, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Why a ring retraction is not asked for.** The only properties of $\rho$ used are additivity and $R$-homogeneity, and both are used only in step 3.1, to push the relation $a=\sum x_ia_i$ down into $R$. Requiring $\rho$ to be multiplicative would exclude the averaging maps that are the standard source of such retractions.

- **The subring hypothesis is what makes $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathfrak aR'$ available.** A subring contains $1_{R'}$ by (T1) of [[def-subring]], so each $a\in\mathfrak a$ is visibly a member of the ideal it generates in $R'$. Without a shared identity the inclusion can fail and the retraction would have nothing to act on.

- **Every ideal of $R$ needs its own finite list.** The list $a_1,\ldots,a_n$ produced in step 2.1 depends on $\mathfrak a$, and no bound uniform in $\mathfrak a$ is claimed or available.
