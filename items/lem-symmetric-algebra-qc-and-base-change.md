## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from the associated-sheaf, affine
quasi-coherent equivalence and gluing machinery ([[def-axiom-of-choice]],
[[thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence]]). Let $X$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal F$ be
a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module
([[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]), with symmetric algebra
$\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ ([[def-symmetric-algebra-qc-module]]). Then:

1. $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ is a quasi-coherent graded commutative
   $\mathcal O_X$-algebra, and for every open subscheme $W\subseteq X$ there is
   a canonical isomorphism of graded $\mathcal O_W$-algebras
   $$\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)|_W\;\cong\;\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F|_W),$$
   compatible with inclusions of open subschemes;
2. for every morphism of schemes $f:Y\to X$ there is a canonical isomorphism of
   graded $\mathcal O_Y$-algebras
   $$f^*\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)\;\cong\;\operatorname{Sym}(f^*\mathcal F),$$
   where $f^*$ is the pullback of modules
   ([[def-pullback-module-ringed-spaces]]); and
3. for the free module $\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X^{\,r}$, $r\ge0$, one has
   $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)\cong\mathcal O_X[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ with
   $\deg T_i=1$.

No finiteness, Noetherian, separatedness or local-freeness hypothesis on
$\mathcal F$ is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A scheme $X$, a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal F$,
and in the affine arguments a commutative ring $A$, an $A$-module $M$, a
commutative $A$-algebra $C$ and a ring map $A\to B$.

[F1] Symmetric algebra of a quasi-coherent module
([[def-symmetric-algebra-qc-module]]): $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ is
glued from the affine models $(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$ over the cover
of $X$ by all affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with
$\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$, using the canonical base-change
identifications $\theta_{U,V}$; it is determined up to unique isomorphism by
these models. On such a chart $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)|_U\cong
(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$, the grading is degreewise with
$\operatorname{Sym}^0(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$ and
$\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathcal F)=\mathcal F$, multiplication is induced by the
affine models, the algebra is generated by its degree-one part, it is a
quasi-coherent graded commutative $\mathcal O_X$-algebra, and
$\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal O_X^{\,r})\cong\mathcal O_X[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ with
$\deg T_i=1$. Moreover $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ satisfies the
universal property that every $\mathcal O_X$-linear map
$\mathcal F\to\mathcal A$ into a sheaf of commutative $\mathcal O_X$-algebras
([[def-module-on-ringed-space]])
extends uniquely to an $\mathcal O_X$-algebra map
$\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)\to\mathcal A$.

[F2] Affine equivalence ([[thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence]],
[[def-associated-sheaf-module-affine-scheme]]): on an affine scheme
$V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ the functors $M\mapsto\widetilde M$ and
$\Gamma(V,-)$ are quasi-inverse equivalences between $A$-modules and
quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_V$-modules; every quasi-coherent sheaf is canonically
associated to its module of global sections, $\Gamma(V,\widetilde M)=M$, and
morphisms correspond bijectively and naturally.

[F3] Tensor product of quasi-coherent modules
([[lem-tensor-qc-modules-quasi-coherent]], [[def-sheaf-tensor-product]]): for
affine $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$ with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$
and $\mathcal G|_U\cong\widetilde N$ there is a canonical isomorphism
$(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal G)|_U\cong\widetilde{(M\otimes_AN)}$,
natural in both variables; under the affine equivalence this identification
carries multiplication of the algebra
$(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$ to the multiplication of
$\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)$.

[F4] Pullback of quasi-coherent modules
([[lem-pullback-qc-module-quasi-coherent]],
[[def-pullback-module-ringed-spaces]]): if $f:Y\to X$ is a morphism and
$\mathcal G$ is quasi-coherent on $X$, then $f^*\mathcal G$ is quasi-coherent on
$Y$; and for affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}B\subseteq Y$ and
$U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$ with $f(V)\subseteq U$ and
$\mathcal G|_U\cong\widetilde N$ there is a canonical isomorphism
$f^*\mathcal G|_V\cong\widetilde{(B\otimes_AN)}$.

[F5] Gluing of sheaves ([[thm-gluing-sheaves]],
[[def-gluing-datum-sheaves]]): a gluing datum for sheaves on an open cover has
a glued sheaf, unique up to unique isomorphism; in particular compatible
isomorphisms on the members of an open cover define an isomorphism of sheaves.

[F6] Tensor products and base change at the ring level
([[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]],
[[thm-coproduct-property-of-tensor-products-of-commutative-algebras]],
[[thm-associativity-of-balanced-tensor-products]],
[[thm-unit-isomorphisms-for-module-tensor-products]]): for a commutative
$B$-algebra $D$, an $A$-linear map $u:M\to D$ gives the additive,
$A$-balanced map $(b,m)\mapsto b\,u(m)$. The induced map
$B\otimes_AM\to D$ is $B$-linear, since multiplication on the first
factor multiplies its value by the same scalar. Conversely a $B$-linear map
$v$ gives the $A$-linear map $m\mapsto v(1\otimes m)$; these constructions
are inverse. Thus there is a natural bijection
$\operatorname{Hom}_B(B\otimes_AM,D)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,D)$, and the
coproduct property of the tensor product of algebras gives a natural bijection
$B\text{-}\mathrm{Alg}(B\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M),D)\cong
A\text{-}\mathrm{Alg}(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M),D)$; the associativity and unit
isomorphisms give canonical identifications
$C\otimes_B(B\otimes_AN)\cong C\otimes_AN$ for a $B$-algebra $C$ and a
$A$-module $N$.

[F7] The Axiom of Choice, inherited from the associated-sheaf and gluing
machinery and from the affine equivalence [F2], whose proof uses compactness
of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ to obtain a finite principal cover
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-affine-quasi-coherent-equivalence]]).

**Proof technique:** direct; prove the affine base-change isomorphism for
symmetric algebras of modules by universal properties, then glue it
chartwise for restrictions and pullbacks.



## Proof

1.1 Universal property of the affine model: for a commutative ring $A$, an $A$-module $M$ and a commutative $A$-algebra $C$, the map $g\mapsto g\circ\eta$ induced by the degree-one inclusion $\eta:M\to\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)$ is a bijection $A\text{-}\mathrm{Alg}(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M),C)\to\operatorname{Hom}_A(M,C)$: apply the universal property of [F1] on $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ to $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$ and the sheaf of commutative $\mathcal O_X$-algebras $\widetilde C$, and translate along the affine equivalence [F2], under which $\mathcal O_X$-linear maps $\widetilde M\to\widetilde C$ are the $A$-linear maps $M\to C$, while $\operatorname{Sym}(\widetilde M)\cong(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$ by the affine model of [F1] and the tensor identifications [F3] match the multiplications, so $\mathcal O_X$-algebra maps $\operatorname{Sym}(\widetilde M)\to\widetilde C$ are the $A$-algebra maps $\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\to C$; the bijection is natural in $C$ because the equivalence and the universal property are. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Base change of the affine model: for a ring map $A\to B$ and an $A$-module $M$ there is a canonical isomorphism of graded $B$-algebras $\beta:B\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\to\operatorname{Sym}_B(B\otimes_AM)$ characterized by $\beta(1\otimes\eta_A(m))=\eta_B(1\otimes m)$: both sides represent the same functor of commutative $B$-algebras, since [F6] identifies $B$-algebra maps $B\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\to D$ with $A$-algebra maps $\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\to D$, step 1.1 with $A$-linear maps $M\to D$, [F6] with $B$-linear maps $B\otimes_AM\to D$, and step 1.1 over $B$ with $B$-algebra maps $\operatorname{Sym}_B(B\otimes_AM)\to D$; the identity of $\operatorname{Sym}_B(B\otimes_AM)$ corresponds to a map $\beta$ and the identity of $B\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)$ to a map $\gamma$, and naturality in $D$ gives $\beta\circ\gamma=\mathrm{id}$ and $\gamma\circ\beta=\mathrm{id}$. Under this correspondence $\beta$ is the unique $B$-algebra map with $\beta(1\otimes\eta_A(m))=\eta_B(1\otimes m)$, so it preserves the degree-one generators and hence the grading, and for $A\to B\to C$ both composites $C\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M)\to\operatorname{Sym}_C(C\otimes_AM)$ in the claim are $C$-algebra maps sending $1\otimes\eta_A(m)$ to $\eta_C(1\otimes m)$; since the source is generated as a $C$-algebra by these elements, the composites agree, the identifications $C\otimes_B(B\otimes_AN)\cong C\otimes_AN$ being those of [F6]. [F6, step 1.1]

3.1 Restriction to open subschemes: for an open subscheme $W\subseteq X$ there is a canonical isomorphism of graded $\mathcal O_W$-algebras $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)|_W\cong\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F|_W)$, compatible with inclusions $W'\subseteq W$: the affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq W$ with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ cover $W$ because $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent, and on each of them $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)|_U\cong(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$ by the affine model, while applying the same model to the quasi-coherent sheaf $\mathcal F|_W$ on $W$ gives $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F|_W)|_U\cong(\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))^{\sim}$ with the same module $M=\Gamma(U,\mathcal F)$; for affine $U'\subseteq U$ both identifications restrict by the canonical base-change isomorphism of step 2.1 for the ring map $\Gamma(U,\mathcal O)\to\Gamma(U',\mathcal O)$, so the chartwise isomorphisms agree on overlaps and glue by [F5] to a graded $\mathcal O_W$-algebra isomorphism, and passing to the subcover inside $W'\subseteq W$ shows compatibility with inclusions. [F1, F5, step 2.1]

3.2 Pullback along an arbitrary morphism: for $f:Y\to X$ there is a canonical isomorphism of graded $\mathcal O_Y$-algebras $f^*\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)\cong\operatorname{Sym}(f^*\mathcal F)$: cover $Y$ by affine opens $V=\operatorname{Spec}B$ with $f(V)\subseteq U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ for an affine $U\subseteq X$ on which $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$, which is possible because affine opens form a basis and $\mathcal F$ is quasi-coherent; on such $V$ the affine form of the pullback [F4] applied to $\mathcal F$ and to the quasi-coherent $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ gives $f^*\mathcal F|_V\cong\widetilde{(B\otimes_AM)}$ and $f^*\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)|_V\cong\widetilde{(B\otimes_A\operatorname{Sym}_A(M))}$, while the affine model [F1] applied to the quasi-coherent $f^*\mathcal F$ on $V$ gives $\operatorname{Sym}(f^*\mathcal F)|_V\cong(\operatorname{Sym}_B\Gamma(V,f^*\mathcal F))^{\sim}=(\operatorname{Sym}_B(B\otimes_AM))^{\sim}$ using $\Gamma(V,\widetilde{(B\otimes_AM)})=B\otimes_AM$ [F2]; the base-change isomorphism $\beta$ of step 2.1 is a graded $\mathcal O_V$-algebra isomorphism between these two models, and on an affine $V'\subseteq V$ the two restrictions agree because $\beta$ is compatible with the ring map $B\to\Gamma(V',\mathcal O)$ by step 2.1, on overlaps from different target charts they also agree: both send each local pulled-back degree-one section to its degree-one image, and such sections generate the source algebra. Thus the local isomorphisms glue over the cover of $Y$ by [F5] to a canonical isomorphism which is graded and multiplicative because the local ones are. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 2.1]

3.3 The remaining claims: $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)$ is a quasi-coherent graded commutative $\mathcal O_X$-algebra with $\operatorname{Sym}^0(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$ and $\operatorname{Sym}^1(\mathcal F)=\mathcal F$, and for $\mathcal F=\mathcal O_X^{\,r}$ it is $\mathcal O_X[T_1,\dots,T_r]$ with $\deg T_i=1$, by the affine models of [F1]; the base-change isomorphism of step 2.1 for $M=A^r$ gives the same polynomial algebra after extension of scalars, so the two descriptions are consistent. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 Choice and degenerate cases: all identifications in steps 1.1 to 3.3 are canonical, being induced by the canonical base-change isomorphisms and universal properties, so no chart, module or isomorphism is selected and the only Axiom of Choice is the inherited one recorded in [F7], including the compactness use in the affine equivalence [F2] invoked in steps 1.1 and 3.2; for $\mathcal F=0$ one has $\operatorname{Sym}(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$ concentrated in degree zero and $\beta$ is the canonical identification $B\otimes_AA\cong B$, and for $X=\varnothing$ both sides of every statement are the zero sheaf. [F1, F2, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
