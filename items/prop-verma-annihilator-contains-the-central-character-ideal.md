## Statement

Let $\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$, let
$\chi_\lambda\colon Z(U(\mathfrak g))\to\mathbb C$ be the central character
determined by $\lambda$ through the Harish-Chandra projection, so that every
$z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ acts on the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ by the scalar
$\chi_\lambda(z)=\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda)$. Then

$$U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}L(\lambda)=I(\lambda),$$

and every element of the two-sided ideal generated by $\ker\chi_\lambda$
annihilates every cyclic highest-weight module of highest weight $\lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A weight $\lambda\in\mathfrak h^*$, the Verma module $M(\lambda)$ with its unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$, and the central character $\chi_\lambda$ with $\chi_\lambda(z)=\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda)$.

[F1] Every cyclic highest-weight module $M=U(\mathfrak g)v$ of highest weight $\lambda$ has a central character, and every $z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ acts on $M$ by the scalar $\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda)$ ([[lem-central-action-on-a-cyclic-highest-weight-module-is-scalar]], [[lem-harish-chandra-projection-computes-highest-weight-scalars]], [[def-central-character-of-a-lie-algebra-module]], [[def-highest-weight-vector-and-cyclic-highest-weight-module]]).

[F2] The Verma module $M(\lambda)$ has a unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)=M(\lambda)/J(\lambda)$, and $L(\lambda)$ is simple ([[thm-verma-module-has-a-unique-simple-quotient]], [[def-verma-module]]).

[F3] The annihilator of a module is a two-sided ideal, and annihilators grow when passing to quotients: if $M\to Q$ is a surjection of $U(\mathfrak g)$-modules and $uM=0$, then $uQ=0$ ([[def-annihilator-ideal-of-a-lie-algebra-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1], $z\in Z(U(\mathfrak g))$ acts on $M(\lambda)$ by the scalar $\operatorname{pr}(z)(\lambda)=\chi_\lambda(z)$. Hence $z\in\ker\chi_\lambda$ acts on $M(\lambda)$ by $0$, that is, $\ker\chi_\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)$. [F1, given]

1.2 More generally, let $M=U(\mathfrak g)v$ be any cyclic highest-weight module of highest weight $\lambda$ and let $u\in U(\mathfrak g)$, $z\in\ker\chi_\lambda$. For $a\in U(\mathfrak g)$ one has $(uz)(av)=u(zav)=u(azv)=ua\chi_\lambda(z)v=0$ by [F1] and centrality of $z$; since every element of $M$ has the form $av$, the element $uz$ annihilates $M$. As products $uz$ span $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_\lambda$, every element of that ideal annihilates every cyclic highest-weight module of highest weight $\lambda$. [F1, algebra]

2.1 Since $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)$ is a two-sided ideal by [F3], it contains all products $uz$ with $u\in U(\mathfrak g)$ and $z\in\ker\chi_\lambda$, hence contains the two-sided ideal $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_\lambda$ generated by $\ker\chi_\lambda$. Thus $U(\mathfrak g)\ker\chi_\lambda\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)$. [step 1.1, F3, algebra]

3.1 The Verma module $M(\lambda)$ has its unique simple quotient $L(\lambda)$ by [F2], and an operator annihilating $M(\lambda)$ annihilates each quotient by [F3], so $\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}M(\lambda)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{U(\mathfrak g)}L(\lambda)=I(\lambda)$. Together with step 2.1 this gives the displayed chain, and step 1.2 gives the final assertion. [step 2.1, step 1.2, F2, F3] ∎
