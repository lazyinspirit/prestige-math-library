## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, $q\in R^{\times}$, $d,n\ge0$, and $H=H_{R,q}(S_n)$ ([[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]). Let $\Lambda(d,n)$ be the set of weak compositions $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_d)$ of $n$ (so $\mu_i\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ and $\sum_i\mu_i=n$; zero parts are allowed and give empty rows). For $\mu\in\Lambda(d,n)$ let $m_\mu=\sum_{w\in S_\mu}T_w$ ([[def-murphy-elements-and-murphy-tableau-elements]]) and let $M^{\mu}:=m_\mu H$ be the right ideal it generates, the **(Hecke) permutation module**; $M^{\mu}$ is free over $R$ with basis $\{m_\mu T_{d(t)}:t\text{ row-standard }\mu\text{-tableau}\}$ and is the Hecke analogue of the module induced from the trivial representation of the Young subgroup ([[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

For a weak composition $\mu\in\Lambda(d,n)$, define $T_0(\lambda,\mu)$ to be the fillings of $[\lambda]$ by $1,\ldots,d$ with exactly $\mu_i$ entries equal to $i$, weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns. Put $T_0(\lambda)=\bigcup_{\mu\in\Lambda(d,n)}T_0(\lambda,\mu)$ and $K_{\lambda\mu}:=|T_0(\lambda,\mu)|$. This explicitly extends the partition-content definition of [[def-semistandard-tableau-and-kostka-number]] to weak compositions, including internal zero counts; a zero count means that its label is absent. The set is finite, since at most $d^n$ fillings occur for $n>0$, and at $n=0$ its unique member is the empty filling.

Here is the freeness statement in detail: the cosets $S_\mu d$ with $d\in D_\mu$ partition $S_n$, and $\ell(ud)=\ell(u)+\ell(d)$ for $u\in S_\mu$, $d\in D_\mu$ ([[def-murphy-elements-and-murphy-tableau-elements]]), so the standard basis theorem ([[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]) gives $T_uT_{d(t)}=T_{ud(t)}$ and $m_\mu T_{d(t)}=\sum_{u\in S_\mu}T_{u\,d(t)}$; these are the standard basis elements indexed by the single coset $S_\mu d(t)$, and the cosets are disjoint, whence $M^\mu=\bigoplus_tR\,m_\mu T_{d(t)}$ is free with the displayed basis as $t$ runs over the row-standard $\mu$-tableaux.

Put $M:=\bigoplus_{\mu\in\Lambda(d,n)}M^{\mu}$ ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]) and define the **q-Schur algebra** $$S(d,n):=\mathrm{End}_H(M),$$ the $R$-algebra of right $H$-module endomorphisms of $M$ ([[def-left-and-right-modules]]).

**Cellular structure.** For all $\mu,\nu\in\Lambda(d,n)$, $\mathrm{Hom}_H(M^{\nu},M^{\mu})$ is free with the semistandard-pair basis constructed below; for every partition $\lambda$ of $n$ with at most $d$ parts and all semistandard $\lambda$-tableaux $S,T$ there is an element $\varphi_{ST}\in S(d,n)$ ([[def-semistandard-tableau-and-kostka-number]]), and the family $$\{\varphi_{ST}:\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}(d,n),\ S,T\in T_0(\lambda)\},$$ where $\Lambda^{+}(d,n)$ is the set of partitions of $n$ with at most $d$ parts ordered by dominance $\unrhd$ ([[def-dominance-order-on-partitions]]), is an $R$-basis of $S(d,n)$; the map $\varphi_{ST}\mapsto\varphi_{TS}$ is an algebra anti-automorphism and the triangular axiom holds modulo the span of the $\varphi_{UV}$ with $\mu\rhd\lambda$. Hence $(\varphi,\Lambda^{+}(d,n))$ is a dominance-cellular basis of $S(d,n)$ ([[def-cellular-algebra-basis-and-cell-module]]) and its cell modules are the **Weyl modules** $W^{\lambda}$, free over $R$ with basis $\{\varphi_T:T\in T_0(\lambda)\}$. Over a field $F$, $W^{\lambda}$ has nonzero simple head $L^{\lambda}$, the $L^{\lambda}$ for $\lambda\in\Lambda^{+}(d,n)$ form a complete set of pairwise non-isomorphic absolutely irreducible $S(d,n)$-modules, and the decomposition matrix satisfies $d_{\lambda\lambda}=1$ and $d_{\lambda\mu}\ne0\Rightarrow\lambda\unrhd\mu$.

**Explicit normalization of the semistandard maps.** For a standard $\lambda$-tableau $s$, let $\mu(s)$ replace each entry by the row of that entry in the canonical $\mu$-tableau. For semistandard $S,T$ of respective types $\mu,\nu$, put $$m_{ST}:=\sum_{\substack{s,t\in\operatorname{Std}(\lambda)\\ \mu(s)=S,\ \nu(t)=T}}m_{st}.$$ The normalized basis map $\varphi_{ST}$ is zero on the other summands and sends $m_\nu h\in M^\nu$ to $m_{ST}h\in M^\mu$. The intersection $m_{ST}\in M^\mu\cap(M^\nu)^*$ makes this well-defined: if $m_\nu h=0$, write $m_{ST}=a m_\nu$ and obtain $m_{ST}h=0$. The integral intersection and Hom construction below proves that these are exactly the Hom basis; their composition is ordinary composition of endomorphisms. When $d\ge n$, the regular type $\omega=(1^n,0^{d-n})\in\Lambda(d,n)$ has $M^\omega=H$; its fibers are singleton standard tableaux, so evaluation at $1$ identifies the corresponding regular-type corner map with $m_{st}$. For $d<n$ this regular type is absent and no such corner is asserted. This fixes the actual module-and-form normalization used by the corner and weight-Gram calculations (Mathas (4.13), with Corollary 4.12 and Theorem 4.14). No quantum factorial is inverted in this definition.

**Weight idempotents and weight spaces.** For each composition $\nu\in\Lambda(d,n)$, let $1_\nu\in\operatorname{End}_H(M)$ be the identity on the summand $M^\nu$ and zero on every other summand. These weight projections satisfy $$1_\nu^2=1_\nu,\qquad 1_\mu1_\nu=0\ (\mu\ne\nu),\qquad   \sum_\nu1_\nu=1.$$ They are not all of the diagonal cellular elements $\varphi_{TT}$: a general cellular diagonal is not asserted idempotent or orthogonal. For a partition $\nu$ the canonical tableau $T^\nu$ of shape and type $\nu$ gives $1_\nu=\varphi_{T^\nu T^\nu}$; for a general composition the projection is defined directly as above, without pretending that an arbitrary composition is a partition-shaped semistandard tableau. The basis map $\varphi_{ST}$ is supported between the summands indicated by the types of $S$ and $T$. Thus the fixed-first-index Weyl basis vector $\varphi_T$ survives right multiplication by $1_\nu$ exactly when $T$ has type $\nu$. Over any field, $$\dim_F(W^\lambda1_\nu)=|T_0(\lambda,\nu)|=K_{\lambda\nu}.$$ This follows by applying the projection to the displayed free Weyl basis, not by assuming every diagonal cellular element is a projection ([[def-semistandard-tableau-and-kostka-number]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The commutative ring $R$, its unit $q$, the finite Hecke algebra $H$, its row ideals $M^\mu=m_\mu H$, and $S=\operatorname{End}_H(\bigoplus_\mu M^\mu)$ in the Definition. Multiplication in $S$ is ordinary composition: $(fg)(x)=f(g(x))$.

[F1] The standard Hecke basis and the length-additive distinguished coset representatives give $m_\mu T_d=\sum_{u\in S_\mu}T_{ud}$ and $m_\mu T_i=q m_\mu$ for each adjacent generator of $S_\mu$ ([[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]], [[def-murphy-elements-and-murphy-tableau-elements]]). The involution satisfies $T_w^*=T_{w^{-1}}$ and $m_\mu^*=m_\mu$.

[F2] The proved integral permutation-module basis is $m_{(S,t)}=\sum_{s:\,\mu(s)=S}m_{st}$, with $S$ semistandard of type $\mu$ and $t$ standard of the same shape ([[lem-type-a-hecke-permutation-modules-have-semistandard-murphy-bases-and-specht-filtrations]]). Thus, in Murphy coordinates, membership in $M^\mu$ means that the first tableau has semistandard type $\mu$, its coefficient is constant on each first-index fibre, and every other coefficient is zero. This is the exact basis assertion, over every $R$ and unit $q$, rather than an inference from coset freeness.

[F3] The Murphy elements form an integral cellular basis of $H$. In particular $m_{st}h\equiv\sum_v r_{tv}(h)m_{sv}$ modulo the span $I^{\rhd\lambda}$ of strictly dominant shapes, with $r_{tv}(h)$ independent of $s$ ([[thm-murphy-tableau-elements-form-a-cellular-basis]]).

[F4] For a finite-dimensional cellular algebra over a field, its nonzero cell-form quotients are a complete set of distinct absolutely simple modules and are simple heads; their decomposition numbers satisfy diagonal $1$ and dominance support ([[thm-nonzero-cell-heads-classify-the-simple-modules-of-a-cellular-algebra]], clause 1 of [[lem-finite-cellular-nonzero-form-heads-purity-and-shared-factor-linkage]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The integral Hom test. The cyclic right module $M^\nu$ has presentation $H/\sum_i(T_i-q)H$, where $i$ ranges over the generators of $S_\nu$. Indeed, a length-additive decomposition $w=ud$ reduces $T_w$ in this quotient to $q^{\ell(u)}T_d$, and the map $T_d\mapsto m_\nu T_d$ maps the resulting spanning family bijectively to the independent disjoint-coset basis. Therefore evaluation at $m_\nu$ identifies $\operatorname{Hom}_H(M^\nu,M^\mu)$ with the elements $y\in M^\mu$ satisfying $yT_i=qy$ for those generators. Pairing $T_w,T_{ws_i}$ with $\ell(ws_i)=\ell(w)+1$ in the standard basis shows that this equation forces their two coefficients to be equal: the coefficient equations are $qb=qa$ and $a+(q-1)b=qb$, so $a=b$ since $q$ is a unit. Requiring all these equalities makes the coefficients constant on each right $S_\nu$-coset. Its unweighted coset sum is $T_c m_\nu$ for the minimal right-coset representative $c$, by length additivity. Hence this simultaneous right eigenspace is $Hm_\nu=(M^\nu)^*$, over every coefficient ring. Evaluation consequently gives an isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_H(M^\nu,M^\mu)\cong M^\mu\cap Hm_\nu$. For $y=a m_\nu$ its inverse is $m_\nu h\mapsto yh$, which is well defined because $m_\nu h=0$ implies $yh=a m_\nu h=0$. [given, F1, algebra, construct]

2.1 The semistandard intersection. Expand $y\in H$ uniquely in the Murphy basis. By [F2], membership in $M^\mu$ requires zero coefficients outside semistandard first-index fibres and constant coefficients inside each fibre. Apply $*$ to [F2] to see that membership in $Hm_\nu$ imposes the identical condition on the second-index fibres of type $\nu$. The two requirements together say precisely that the coefficients are constant on each rectangle $\{(s,t):\mu(s)=S,\nu(t)=T\}$, and zero outside these rectangles. Thus their unweighted sums $m_{ST}$ form a basis of the intersection. The rectangles are disjoint and have a coefficient $1$ at every member, so independence and coefficient extraction hold over arbitrary $R$, including singular parameters; no quantum factorial is cancelled. Every nonempty rectangle has shape with at most $d$ rows, since a strictly increasing column can use only $d$ labels. Combining this with step 1.1 gives exactly the maps $\varphi_{ST}$ and the asserted basis of each Hom block, hence of $S$. The construction also commutes with arbitrary base change, because the same free basis and formulas describe each specialized Hom block. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 The involution under composition. Define $f^*$ on a Hom block by replacing its evaluation element $y=f(m_\nu)\in M^\mu\cap Hm_\nu$ by $y^*\in M^\nu\cap Hm_\mu$, and extend linearly to $S$. Step 2.1 gives $\varphi_{ST}^*=\varphi_{TS}$, so this is an involution. To check reversal, let $f:M^\nu\to M^\mu$ have $f(m_\nu)=a m_\nu$ and $g:M^\kappa\to M^\nu$ have $g(m_\kappa)=b m_\kappa=m_\nu h$. Then $(fg)(m_\kappa)=a b m_\kappa$, so $(fg)^*(m_\mu)=m_\kappa b^*a^*$. On the other hand $f^*(m_\mu)=m_\nu a^*$ and $g^*(m_\nu)=m_\kappa b^*$, whence $(g^*f^*)(m_\mu)=m_\kappa b^*a^*$. Noncomposable blocks give zero on both sides. Since each block map is determined by its generating element, $(fg)^*=g^*f^*$ as claimed. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The cellular multiplication law. Fix $T$ of shape $\lambda$ and type $\nu$, and first take $f:M^\kappa\to M^\nu$. Write $f(m_\kappa)=m_\nu h$. Then $(\varphi_{ST}f)(m_\kappa)=m_{ST}h$. By [F3], its shape-$\lambda$ Murphy coefficient at $(s,v)$ is $\sum_{t:\nu(t)=T}r_{tv}(h)$ if $\mu(s)=S$, and zero otherwise; all remaining shapes strictly dominate $\lambda$. This coefficient is independent of $s$. The full element lies in $M^\mu\cap Hm_\kappa$ by step 1.1, so step 2.1 forces these coefficients to be constant on semistandard second-index fibres $\kappa(v)=V$ and zero on the other fibres. For a fixed $T,h$, these constants are independent of $S$: choose any member $s$ of its nonempty first fibre and read the displayed sum, which contains no $s$. Calling the constants $r_V$, evaluation and the intersection basis give $\varphi_{ST}f\equiv\sum_{V\in T_0(\lambda,\kappa)}r_V\varphi_{SV}$ modulo the span of strictly dominant Schur shapes, for every $S$. Sum over Hom blocks for arbitrary $f\in S$. Step 3.1 gives the corresponding left law, so each upward span is a two-sided ideal and the cellular axioms hold. This proves cellularity by actual composition and Murphy coefficient comparison. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Partition projections and nonzero forms. For the canonical semistandard tableau $T^\lambda$ of shape and type $\lambda$, its refinement fibre consists just of $t^\lambda$, so $m_{T^\lambda T^\lambda}=m_\lambda$. Accordingly $\varphi_{T^\lambda T^\lambda}$ is the identity on $M^\lambda$ and zero on other summands; it is the genuine projection $1_\lambda$. Squaring it gives $1_\lambda^2=1_\lambda$. In the free shape-$\lambda$ cell quotient, the cell product is $1_\lambda^2\equiv B_\lambda(\varphi_{T^\lambda},\varphi_{T^\lambda})1_\lambda$, and the coefficient of this basis vector is therefore $1$. Over a field this form is not identically zero. Step 4.1 and [F4] consequently show that every $W^\lambda$ has the nonzero absolutely simple head $L^\lambda$, that these heads give all simple modules exactly once, and that the stated diagonal and dominance decomposition conditions hold. [step 2.1, step 4.1, F4, algebra]

6.1 Weight ranks and edge cases. Direct summand projections $1_\nu$ satisfy the displayed orthogonality and sum-to-one relations. The source support of $\varphi_{ST}$ implies $\varphi_{ST}1_\nu=\delta_{\operatorname{type}(T),\nu}\varphi_{ST}$, so the same formula holds on the right Weyl cell basis, proving its weight rank $K_{\lambda\nu}$. It asserts no idempotence for other cellular diagonal elements. Deleting empty rows leaves the Young subgroup unchanged and identifies the row-type fibres after the order-preserving relabelling of the remaining row labels; hence all constructions allow zero parts. At $n=0$ the unique empty tableau and empty partition give $H=R$, $S=R$ and cell form $1$. If $d=0<n$, the indexing set of weak compositions and the direct sum are empty, $S=0$, and every basis and simple-label assertion is empty. No division except by powers of the unit $q$, generic semisimplicity or choice principle enters any step. [given, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
