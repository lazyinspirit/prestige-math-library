## Example

Let $k$ be a field and, for $m\in\mathbb N$, write $R_m:=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]$ for
the iterated polynomial ring, so that $R_0=k$ and $R_{m+1}=R_m[x_{m+1}]$
([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]). Identify $R_m$ with the
subring of constant polynomials in $R_{m+1}$, which is the identification the
published iterative definition already makes and which is legitimate because the
constant-polynomial map is an injective unital ring homomorphism
([[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]]). Under it

$$R_0\subseteq R_1\subseteq R_2\subseteq\cdots,$$

and the union

$$R_\infty:=\bigcup_{m\in\mathbb N}R_m$$

carries well-defined operations, making it a commutative ring: the polynomial
ring in the countably many indeterminates $x_1,x_2,\ldots$ over $k$.

Then $R_\infty$ is **not** Noetherian. Writing
$I_m:=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$ for the ideal of $R_\infty$ generated by the first $m$
indeterminates, with $I_0=(\varnothing)=0$, the chain

$$I_0\subsetneq I_1\subsetneq I_2\subsetneq\cdots$$

is strictly ascending and therefore never stabilises.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, the rings $R_m=k[x_1,\ldots,x_m]$ for $m\in\mathbb N$, and their union $R_\infty$.

[L1] Polynomial rings in finitely many commuting indeterminates are defined by $R[x_1,\ldots,x_0]:=R$ and $R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}]:=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n][x_{n+1}]$; at each stage the coefficient ring embeds as the constant polynomials, so all preceding indeterminates remain present ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L2] For every commutative ring $R$ the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is a commutative ring, and the constant-polynomial map $R\to R[x]$ is an injective unital ring homomorphism ([[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L3] A subset $S$ of a ring is a **subring** when $1\in S$ and $S$ is closed under addition, additive inverses and multiplication; it is then a ring with the same zero and identity ([[def-subring]]).

[L4] For $S\subseteq R$, $(S)$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, so $S\subseteq(S)$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]).

[L5] In a commutative ring, $(S)$ consists of finite sums $\sum r_is_i$, and the empty sum is included and equals $0$ ([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L6] For commutative rings $R,S$, a unital ring homomorphism $\varphi\colon R\to S$ and $s\in S$, there is a unique unital ring homomorphism $R[x]\to S$ extending $\varphi$ on constants and sending $x$ to $s$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]).

[L7] For a commutative ring, being Noetherian is equivalent to every ascending chain of ideals indexed by $\mathbb N$ stabilising ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Under the identification of $R_m$ with the constants of $R_{m+1}$, each $R_m$ is a subring of $R_{m+1}$, so the family is increasing and any two of its members are comparable. [L1, L2, L3, given]

2.1 The union $R_\infty$ is a commutative ring. Given $f,g\in R_\infty$ there is $m$ with $f,g\in R_m$, by comparability of the two stages containing them; define $f+g$ and $fg$ there. The value does not depend on the stage chosen, because a larger stage contains the smaller as a subring and the operations of a subring are the restrictions of the ambient ones. Each ring axiom involves finitely many elements, which again lie in a common $R_m$, where the axiom holds. The identity is $1_k$ and the zero is $0_k$. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 For $m\in\mathbb N$ let $I_m:=(x_1,\ldots,x_m)$ be the ideal of $R_\infty$ generated by $x_1,\ldots,x_m$; at $m=0$ the generating set is empty and $I_0=0$. Since the generating sets increase with $m$, so do the ideals: $I_m\subseteq I_{m+1}$. [L4, L5, step 2.1]

4.1 The inclusions are strict, because $x_{m+1}\notin I_m$. Suppose $x_{m+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_ix_i$ with $f_i\in R_\infty$; all the $f_i$ lie in a common $R_N$ with $N\ge m+1$, so the equation holds in $R_N=k[x_1,\ldots,x_N]$. Iterating the one-variable universal property along the tower defining $R_N$ produces a $k$-algebra homomorphism $\theta\colon R_N\to k[T]$ with $\theta(x_{m+1})=T$ and $\theta(x_j)=0$ for every $j\le N$ with $j\ne m+1$. Applying $\theta$ to the supposed equation gives $T=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\theta(f_i)\cdot0=0$ in $k[T]$, which is false. So $x_{m+1}\in I_{m+1}\setminus I_m$ and $I_m\subsetneq I_{m+1}$. [L1, L4, L6, step 1.1, step 3.1]

5.1 The chain $I_0\subseteq I_1\subseteq\cdots$ is an ascending chain of ideals of $R_\infty$ indexed by $\mathbb N$ in which every inclusion is strict, so no index $N$ has $I_n=I_N$ for all $n\ge N$: already $I_{N+1}\ne I_N$. The ascending chain condition therefore fails, and $R_\infty$ is not Noetherian. [L7, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Every finite stage is Noetherian and the union is not.** Each $R_m$ is Noetherian by [[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]]; the Noetherian condition is not preserved by unions of increasing chains of rings, and this is the standard witness for that.

- **The identification of $R_m$ with the constants of $R_{m+1}$ is the one the published definition makes.** [[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]] states that the coefficient ring embeds at each stage and that all preceding indeterminates remain present; the union above is taken along exactly those embeddings, and the injectivity that makes the identification harmless is [[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]].

- **$T$ is needed in the substitution.** Sending every indeterminate to $0$ would kill $x_{m+1}$ as well and prove nothing; the target $k[T]$ is what keeps one indeterminate alive while all the others vanish.
