## Statement

Assume AC and $n\ge2$, and work with the abstract Artin group $B_n$ of
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]], the words $x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}$ of
[[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]] and the published surjection
$\varphi\colon B_n\to G_n$ of
[[prop-the-artin-presentation-surjects-onto-geometric-braids]]. Let $W_1$ be a
word in $x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_{n-1}^{\pm1}$ and $W_2$ a word in
$\sigma_1^{\pm1},\dots,\sigma_{n-2}^{\pm1}$ such that $\varphi(W_1W_2)$ is the
trivial geometric braid; such a pair exists for every combed word by
[[lem-every-trivial-braid-word-combs-as-w-one-w-two]]. Write
$\Psi_n\colon G_n^{\mathrm{pure}}\to PB_n$ for the canonical isomorphism of
[[cor-pure-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-ordered-configurations]],
and $\rho\colon PB_n\to PB_{n-1}$ for the forgetting map of
[[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]]. Write
$A_{ij}\in G_n$ for the geometric generators of
[[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]], and write
$\widetilde A_{ij}:=\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i^2
\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}\in B_n$ for their Artin-word lifts,
so $\varphi(\widetilde A_{ij})=A_{ij}$. Put
$\widetilde P_i:=\widetilde A_{i+1,n}\cdots\widetilde A_{n-1,n}$ in $B_n$ and
$P_i:=A_{i+1,n}\cdots A_{n-1,n}$ in $G_n$, with both empty when $i=n-1$.
Then:

**(i)** every $\varphi(x_i)$ is a pure braid and
$$\varphi(x_i)=P_i^{-1}\,A_{in}\,P_i,$$
so that the conjugating element lies in the subgroup generated by the standard
generators with larger first index; at the word level this is
$x_i\equiv\widetilde P_i^{-1}\widetilde A_{in}\widetilde P_i$ in $B_n$.
Moreover
$\Psi_n(\varphi(x_1)),\dots,\Psi_n(\varphi(x_{n-1}))$ form a free basis of the
free kernel $\ker\rho$ of the forgetting map of
[[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]];

**(ii)** $\varphi(W_1)=1$, and $W_1$, as a word in the alphabet
$x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_{n-1}^{\pm1}$, reduces to the empty word by free
cancellations of adjacent inverse pairs $x_i^{\pm1}x_i^{\mp1}$ (each of which,
after expanding both $x_i$'s, is a permitted deletion of $\sigma$-pairs);

**(iii)** $\varphi(W_2)=1$, and, viewing the same letter word as an element of
$B_{n-1}$, its geometric image under the rank-$(n-1)$ surjection
$\varphi_{n-1}\colon B_{n-1}\to G_{n-1}$ is trivial:
$\varphi_{n-1}(W_2)=1\in G_{n-1}$. This asserts triviality of the geometric
image, not that $W_2=1$ in $B_{n-1}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge2$, the groups $B_n$ and $G_n$ of [[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] and [[thm-geometric-braids-form-a-group]], the words $\alpha_j,x_j$ of [[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]], words $W_1$ in $x_1^{\pm1},\dots,x_{n-1}^{\pm1}$ and $W_2$ in $\sigma_1^{\pm1},\dots,\sigma_{n-2}^{\pm1}$ with $\varphi(W_1W_2)=1$, the standard pure braid generators $A_{ij}$ of [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]], and the identifications of [[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]] and [[cor-pure-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-ordered-configurations]].

[F1] In $B_n$ the two Artin relations hold and adjacent inverse $\sigma$-pairs may be freely inserted and deleted. For $1\le i<j\le n$, set $\widetilde A_{ij}:=\sigma_{j-1}\cdots\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_i^{2}\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots\sigma_{j-1}^{-1}\in B_n$; its image under $\varphi$ is the geometric standard generator $A_{ij}$ of [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]], since $\varphi(\sigma_r)=[\sigma_r]$ and $\varphi$ preserves the displayed stacking product ([[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]], [[prop-the-artin-presentation-surjects-onto-geometric-braids]], [[def-standard-pure-braid-generators]]). The words $\alpha_j$ satisfy $\alpha_j=\sigma_j\cdots\sigma_{n-1}$ and $\alpha_n=1$, and $x_i=\alpha_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_i^{2}\alpha_{i+1}$ ([[def-zariski-braid-combing-words-alpha-and-x]]).

[F2] Assumption AC, and the published consequences that are used to identify the free kernel: AC implies dependent choice and countable choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]]), so that the Fadell--Neuwirth fibrations of [[thm-pure-braid-forgetting-a-strand-short-exact-sequence]] are available: forgetting the last strand is a homomorphism $\rho\colon PB_n\to PB_{n-1}$ with kernel a free group of rank $n-1$, and under the fiber-inclusion identification the elements $\Psi_n(A_{1n}),\dots,\Psi_n(A_{n-1,n})$ are a free basis of that kernel ([[lem-standard-pure-braids-generate-each-free-kernel]]). Forgetting a strand is realized by the coordinate projection: under the identifications [F3] at ranks $n$ and $n-1$, a pure braid whose coordinate loop is $(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$ satisfies $\rho(\Psi_n([\beta]))=(\iota^F_*[(Z_1,\dots,Z_{n-1})])^{-1}$, with target basepoint $(q_1,\dots,q_{n-1})$. This follows because coordinate projection commutes with the open-to-closed inclusion and its induced homomorphism preserves inverses.

[F3] For every rank $m\ge0$, $\Psi_m\colon G_m^{\mathrm{pure}}=\ker\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}\to PB_m$ is an isomorphism, and for a pure braid $[\beta]$ with coordinate path $z_\beta$ it is $\Psi_m([\beta])=(\iota^F_*[z_\beta])^{-1}$, the inverse of the class of the coordinate loop carried to the closed-disc configuration space; the same sign convention is used at every rank, so the inverse cancels in the comparisons between ranks below. At every interior base configuration the open-to-closed inclusion induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups ([[lem-interior-and-closed-disk-configuration-spaces-are-homotopy-equivalent]]). Consequently $[\beta]=1$ if and only if $[z_\beta]=1$ ([[cor-pure-geometric-braids-are-the-fundamental-group-of-ordered-configurations]], [[def-ordered-configuration-space]], [[def-induced-homomorphism-on-fundamental-groups]]). Moreover $\pi_{\mathrm{conf}}(\Phi([\beta]))=\pi_{\mathrm{geo}}([\beta])$, so a geometric braid is pure exactly when its class lies in $PB_n$ under these identifications ([[prop-geometric-endpoint-permutation-equals-covering-monodromy]]).

[F4] Geometric conventions. The base configuration is $Q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ with $q_j=((2j-n-1)h,0)$, $h=\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$, and the (n−1)-strand base configuration is $Q^{(n-1)}=(q'_1,\dots,q'_{n-1})$ with $q'_j=((2j-n)h',0)$, $h'=\frac{1}{4n}$ ([[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]]). The elementary half twist $\sigma_i$ acts as $m_i+\rho(t),\,m_i-\rho(t)$ on the two points $q_i,q_{i+1}$ and fixes all others, where $m_i=q_i+(h,0)$ and $\rho$ is the diamond path of size $h$ ([[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]]); stacking is first-under-second with $\pi(\gamma\star\beta)=\pi(\gamma)\circ\pi(\beta)$ ([[prop-stacking-of-geometric-braids-is-well-defined]], [[thm-geometric-braids-form-a-group]]). The two configurations are compared through the similarity $A(w):=aw-\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$ with $a:=\frac{n}{n+1}$. Since $\frac{1}{4(n+1)}=\frac{1-a}{4}$, this is the dilation about $c=-\frac14$, namely $A(w)=c+a(w-c)$. It satisfies $A(Q^{(n-1)})=(q_1,\dots,q_{n-1})$; its image $A(D^\circ)$ is the open disc of radius $a$ centered at $A(0)=-\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$, whose closure lies in $D^\circ$ because $a+\frac{1}{4(n+1)}<1$.

[F5] Moving-homotopy transport: for a path $\gamma\colon x_0\to x_1$ in a space $X$ and each $n\ge1$ there is a transport isomorphism $\beta_\gamma\colon\pi_n(X,x_1)\to\pi_n(X,x_0)$ depending only on the endpoint-fixed class of $\gamma$; if $H\colon f\simeq g$ is a homotopy with basepoint track $\gamma$, then $f_*=\beta_\gamma\circ g_*$. In degree one $\beta_\gamma[a]=[\gamma*a*\bar\gamma]$ ([[prop-higher-homotopy-basepoint-transport-and-moving-homotopies]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The combing identity, by free cancellation.** For $1\le j\le n-1$ put $\delta_j:=\sigma_{n-1}\sigma_{n-2}\cdots\sigma_{j+1}$ and $\gamma_j:=\sigma_{j+1}\sigma_{j+2}\cdots\sigma_{n-1}$ (empty words for $j=n-1$); then $\delta_j=\delta_{j+1}\sigma_{j+1}$ and $\gamma_j=\sigma_{j+1}\gamma_{j+1}$ as words, and by [F1] the Artin word $\widetilde A_{jn}=\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\delta_j^{-1}$ lifts the geometric generator $A_{jn}$, while $x_j=\gamma_j^{-1}\sigma_j^{2}\gamma_j$ is the combing word. First, for every $j$ the Artin word $\widetilde Q_j:=\widetilde A_{j,n}\widetilde A_{j+1,n}\cdots\widetilde A_{n-1,n}$ freely reduces to $\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\gamma_j$: this is a downward induction on $j$, in which both words are $\sigma_{n-1}^{2}$ for $j=n-1$, and in which the substitution of $\widetilde A_{j,n}=\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\delta_j^{-1}$ and of the reduced form $\widetilde Q_{j+1}\equiv\delta_{j+1}\sigma_{j+1}^{2}\gamma_{j+1}$ gives $$\widetilde Q_j\equiv\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\bigl(\delta_j^{-1}\delta_{j+1}\bigr)\sigma_{j+1}^{2}\gamma_{j+1}=\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\sigma_{j+1}^{-1}\bigl(\delta_{j+1}^{-1}\delta_{j+1}\bigr)\sigma_{j+1}^{2}\gamma_{j+1},$$ where the block $\delta_{j+1}^{-1}\delta_{j+1}$ cancels freely to the empty word -- its middle pair $\sigma_{n-1}^{-1}\sigma_{n-1}$ cancels first, after which the next pair is adjacent, and so on outwards -- leaving $\delta_j\sigma_j^{2}\gamma_j$. For $1\le i\le n-2$ this gives $\widetilde P_i:=\widetilde Q_{i+1}\equiv\delta_{i+1}\sigma_{i+1}^{2}\gamma_{i+1}$, and the inverse word is $\widetilde P_i^{-1}\equiv\gamma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_{i+1}^{-2}\delta_{i+1}^{-1}$; substituting these reduced forms into $\widetilde P_i^{-1}\widetilde A_{in}\widetilde P_i$, and using $\delta_{i+1}^{-1}\delta_i\equiv\sigma_{i+1}$ and $\delta_i^{-1}\delta_{i+1}\equiv\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}$ (the same middle-outward cancellation applied to $\delta_i=\delta_{i+1}\sigma_{i+1}$), gives $$\widetilde P_i^{-1}\widetilde A_{in}\widetilde P_i\equiv\gamma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_{i+1}^{-2}\bigl(\delta_{i+1}^{-1}\delta_i\bigr)\sigma_i^{2}\bigl(\delta_i^{-1}\delta_{i+1}\bigr)\sigma_{i+1}^{2}\gamma_{i+1}\equiv\gamma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_{i+1}^{-1}\sigma_i^{2}\sigma_{i+1}\gamma_{i+1}=\gamma_i^{-1}\sigma_i^{2}\gamma_i=x_i.$$ The remaining index $i=n-1$ has the empty product $\widetilde P_{n-1}$ and $x_{n-1}=\widetilde A_{n-1,n}=\sigma_{n-1}^{2}$, so $x_{n-1}=\widetilde P_{n-1}^{-1}\widetilde A_{n-1,n}\widetilde P_{n-1}$ holds trivially. No Artin relation is used in this step, only free insertions and deletions. Applying $\varphi$ gives the stated geometric identity. [F1]

1.2 **The two models of a lower-rank word.** Put $a:=n/(n+1)$ and consider the similarity $A(w)=aw-h_n$ with $h_n=\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$, so that $A(w)=aw-\frac1{4(n+1)}$. One computes $A(q'_j)=q_j$ for $1\le j\le n-1$, $A(m'_i)=m_i$ for $1\le i\le n-2$ where $m'_i=q'_i+(h',0)$ is the (n−1)-strand midpoint, and $a\rho'(t)=\rho(t)$ for the two displacement paths, since $a h'=h$ and each diamond-path coordinate is linear in its size. The reflected paths for the negative letters satisfy the same scaling identity. Since $A$ is affine, $A(m'_i\pm\rho'(t))=A(m'_i)\pm a\rho'(t)=m_i\pm\rho(t)$. Thus for every $1\le i\le n-2$ and both signs, $$A\bigl((\sigma_i^{(n-1)})_j(t)\bigr)=(\sigma_i^{(n)})_j(t)\quad(1\le j\le n-1),\qquad A(q'_j)=q_j,$$ where $\sigma_i^{(k)}$ is the elementary half twist of the $k$-strand model of [F4]. Every lower-rank moving point stays in the support disc $U'_i$ of the letter currently being run, or is one of the fixed base points. Relative to $c:=(-\frac14,0)$, these base points have radii $|q'_j-c|=j/(2n)\le(n-1)/(2n)$, and the support disc $U'_i$ has center $m'_i$ with $|m'_i-c|=(2i+1)/(4n)$ and radius $3/(8n)$; hence every such path point has radius at most $(4n-3)/(8n)$. This local agreement is all that will be used: $A$ maps the lower standard-generator paths to the corresponding $n$-strand paths, but it is not used to map arbitrary lower braids into the complement of $q_n$. Consequently, for every word $V$ in $\sigma_1^{\pm1},\dots,\sigma_{n-2}^{\pm1}$, if $z=(z_1,\dots,z_{n-1})$ is its (n−1)-strand coordinate motion and $Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$ its n-strand coordinate motion, then $$Z_j=A\circ z_j\quad(1\le j\le n-1),\qquad Z_n\equiv q_n,$$ by induction on its letters: the generator paths agree by the displayed identity, and stacking has the same coupling permutation on $\{1,\dots,n-1\}$ in both models and fixes $n$. Thus $\varphi_n(V)$ is represented by $(A\circ z_1,\dots,A\circ z_{n-1},q_n)$. [F4]

2.1 **The straight-strand extension is a well-defined injective homomorphism.** Let $c:=(-\frac14,0)$, $a:=n/(n+1)$, $R_0:=\frac12-\frac1{4n}$ and $r_n:=n/(2(n+1))$. For $\theta\in\mathbb R$ let $u_\theta:=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and let $$R(\theta):=\frac14\cos\theta+\sqrt{1-\frac1{16}\sin^2\theta},$$ the distance from $c$ to the boundary of $D^\circ$ along the ray $c+ru_\theta$; in particular $R(\theta)\ge\frac34>R_0$. Define the strictly increasing radial function $$\lambda(r,\theta):=\begin{cases}ar,&0\le r\le R_0,\\[2pt]aR_0+(r_n-aR_0)\dfrac{r-R_0}{R(\theta)-R_0},&R_0\le r<R(\theta),\end{cases}$$ and define $E(c+ru_\theta):=c+\lambda(r,\theta)u_\theta$, with $E(c):=c$. Since $aR_0<r_n$, each ray is mapped increasingly onto the ray segment of length $r_n$; thus $E$ is a homeomorphism from $D^\circ$ onto the open disk $L_n:=B(c,r_n)$. The closure of $L_n$ lies in $D^\circ$ because $\lVert c\rVert_2+r_n<\frac34<1$, and $q_n$ lies on $\partial L_n$ because $|q_n-c|=n/(2(n+1))=r_n$. Moreover, $E=A$ whenever $|w-c|\le R_0$. The basepoints satisfy $|q'_j-c|=j/(2n)\le(n-1)/(2n)<R_0$, and for $n\ge3$ every lower support disc satisfies $$\max_{1\le i\le n-2}\sup_{w\in U'_i}|w-c|\le\frac{4n-3}{8n}<R_0$$ (for $n=2$ there are no such support discs). Therefore $E(Q^{(n-1)})=(q_1,\dots,q_{n-1})$, and [F4] with step 1.2 shows that $E$ maps every lower-rank standard-generator path to its corresponding n-strand path. If $\beta\in G_{n-1}^{\mathrm{pure}}$ with representative coordinate loop $z=(z_1,\dots,z_{n-1})$, define $\check s(\beta)$ to be the class of $(E\circ z_1,\dots,E\circ z_{n-1},q_n)$. The tuple is a pure n-strand braid: $E$ is injective, its image $L_n$ avoids $q_n$, and the endpoints are $E(Q^{(n-1)})=(q_1,\dots,q_{n-1})$. Applying $E$ to a braid isotopy preserves pairwise distinctness and avoids $q_n$, so $\check s$ is well defined on isotopy classes; applying $E$ to the stacking formula shows it is a homomorphism. To prove injectivity, first note that the induced map $E_*:\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),Q^{(n-1)})\to\pi_1(F_{n-1}(D^\circ),E(Q^{(n-1)}))$ is injective. Indeed, for $0\le t\le1$ let $\lambda_t(r,\theta):=(1-t)r+t\lambda(r,\theta)$ and $E_t(c+ru_\theta):=c+\lambda_t(r,\theta)u_\theta$. For each $t$, $\lambda_t$ is strictly increasing along every ray and, when $r<R(\theta)$, $$\lambda_t(r,\theta)<(1-t)R(\theta)+tr_n\le R(\theta),$$ so $E_t$ is an embedding $D^\circ\to D^\circ$, with $E_0=\operatorname{id}$ and $E_1=E$. Hence $F_{n-1}(E_t)$ is a homotopy on the ordered configuration space from the identity to $F_{n-1}(E)$, whose basepoint follows $\eta(t):=F_{n-1}(E_t)(Q^{(n-1)})$; by [F5], $\operatorname{id}_*=\beta_\eta\circ E_*$, and since $\beta_\eta$ is an isomorphism, $E_*$ is injective. If $\check s(\beta)=1$, [F3] and the coordinate-projection description of [F2] imply $E_*[z]=1$; injectivity gives $[z]=1$, and [F3] implies $\beta=1$. [F2, F3, F4, F5]

2.2 **The conjugating identity in the geometric group.** Applying $\varphi$ to the word identity of step 1.1 gives $$\varphi(x_i)=P_i^{-1}A_{in}P_i\quad\text{in }G_n,$$ where $P_i=A_{i+1,n}\cdots A_{n-1,n}$ is the geometric product defined in the Statement. Each factor $A_{j,n}$ of $P_i$ and $A_{in}$ itself lies in the pure subgroup and maps under $\Psi_n$ into $\ker\rho$ by [F2, F3]. Since $\ker\rho$ is a subgroup, $\Psi_n(\varphi(x_i))$ lies in $\ker\rho$; in particular $\varphi(x_i)$ is pure. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 **The straight-strand extensions meet the kernel trivially.** By [F2] the forgetting map acts on coordinate loops by dropping the last coordinate. For the straight-strand extension of step 2.1 the coordinate loop is $(E\circ z_1,\dots,E\circ z_{n-1},q_n)$, whose dropped loop is $E\circ z$. Thus $\rho(\Psi_n(\check s(\beta)))=\iota^F_*(E_*[z])^{-1}$ at the basepoint $(q_1,\dots,q_{n-1})=E(Q^{(n-1)})$. If $\gamma=\Psi_n(\check s(\beta))$ also lies in $\ker\rho$, this class is trivial. The inclusion map $\iota^F_*$ is injective at that basepoint by [F3], so $E_*[z]=1$. Injectivity of $E_*$ (step 2.1) gives $[z]=1$, and [F3] gives $\beta=1$ and $\gamma=1$. In particular $\Psi_n(\operatorname{im}\check s)\cap\ker\rho=\{1\}$, which is the uniqueness statement used below. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

3.2 **The free basis by the left-inverse argument.** Let $K:=\ker\rho\le PB_n$ be the free kernel, and let $F:=F(a_1,\dots,a_{n-1})$ be the abstract free group. The map $\jmath:F\to K$ given by $\jmath(a_j):=\Psi_n(A_{jn})$ is an isomorphism by the free-basis clause of [F2]. Write $w_i:=a_{i+1}a_{i+2}\cdots a_{n-1}\in F$ and $t_i:=w_i^{-1}a_iw_i\in F$. By step 2.2 and the homomorphism property of $\Psi_n$, $\jmath(t_i)=\Psi_n(\varphi(x_i))$. First, the elements $t_1,\dots,t_{n-1}$ generate $F$: downward induction on $i$ shows $\langle t_i,a_{i+1},\dots,a_{n-1}\rangle=\langle a_i,\dots,a_{n-1}\rangle$, because $a_i=w_it_iw_i^{-1}$ lies in the left side and $t_i$ lies in the right side; at $i=n-1$ one has $w_{n-1}=1$ and $t_{n-1}=a_{n-1}$. Second, they are independent: define an endomorphism $\theta$ of $F$ by descending recursion on $i$ by $\theta(a_i):=\theta(w_i)a_i\theta(w_i)^{-1}$, where $\theta(w_i)$ is the image of the word $w_i$ under the already defined $\theta$ on the generators $a_{i+1},\dots,a_{n-1}$ (at $i=n-1$ this gives $\theta(a_{n-1})=a_{n-1}$); this prescription specifies an element $\theta(a_i)\in F$ for every free generator, hence defines a unique endomorphism $\theta\colon F\to F$. Then $\theta(t_i)=\theta(w_i)^{-1}\theta(a_i)\theta(w_i)=\theta(w_i)^{-1}\theta(w_i)a_i\theta(w_i)^{-1}\theta(w_i)=a_i$ for every $i$. If $\rho'\colon F\to F$ denotes the homomorphism with $\rho'(a_i)=t_i$, then $\theta(\rho'(a_i))=\theta(t_i)=a_i$, so $\theta\circ\rho'$ is the identity on a free basis, hence $\theta\circ\rho'=\operatorname{id}_F$ and $\rho'$ is injective. Therefore $t_1,\dots,t_{n-1}$ freely generate $F$, and applying $\Psi_n^{-1}\circ\jmath$ carries this free basis to the free basis $\varphi(x_1),\dots,\varphi(x_{n-1})$ of $\Psi_n^{-1}(K)$. [F2, step 2.2]

4.1 **The three claims.** (i) is step 2.2 together with step 3.2, the free-basis clause of [F2] being what turns $\Psi_n(A_{1n}),\dots,\Psi_n(A_{n-1,n})$ into a basis of the free kernel. For (ii) and (iii), note first that $\varphi(W_1)$ is a product of the pure braids $\varphi(x_i)$ of step 2.2 and that $\varphi(W_2)=\varphi(W_1)^{-1}$ because $\varphi(W_1W_2)=1$; by (i) the elements $\varphi(x_1),\dots,\varphi(x_{n-1})$ generate $\ker\rho$, so both $\varphi(W_1)$ and $\varphi(W_2)$ lie in the free kernel. The lower-rank word $W_2$ has the same reading in the two models, so the endpoint permutation of its n-strand realization is the identity on the labels $1,\dots,n-1$ (the last strand is fixed) and the permuted labels $1,\dots,n-1$ agree with those of its (n-1)-strand realization; since the permutation of $\varphi(W_2)$ is trivial, the (n-1)-strand realization is a pure braid and step 1.2 identifies $\varphi(W_2)=\check s(\varphi_{n-1}(W_2))$ in $G_n^{\mathrm{pure}}$. Hence $\Psi_n(\varphi(W_2))\in\Psi_n(\operatorname{im}\check s)\cap\ker\rho=\{1\}$ by step 3.1, so $\varphi(W_2)=1$; then injectivity of $\check s$ (step 2.1) gives $\varphi_{n-1}(W_2)=1$, which is (iii), and $\varphi(W_1)=\varphi(W_2)^{-1}=1$. Finally, since $x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}$ are carried to a free basis of $\ker\rho$ by (i), the homomorphism from the free group on the letters $x_1,\dots,x_{n-1}$ to $G_n^{\mathrm{pure}}$ sending $x_i\mapsto\varphi(x_i)$ is injective; a word in this alphabet whose image is $\varphi(W_1)=1$ is therefore freely trivial, and each cancellation of an adjacent pair $x_i^{\pm1}x_i^{\mp1}$ expands, after writing out both expanded words $x_i^{\pm1}$, into a sequence of permitted deletions of adjacent inverse $\sigma$-pairs. ## Remarks
