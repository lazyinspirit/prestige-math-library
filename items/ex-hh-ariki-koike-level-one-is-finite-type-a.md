## Example

Let $R_1=\mathbb Z[Q^{\pm1},u^{\pm1}]$. Let $H_Q(n)=R_1\otimes_{\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]}H_v(n)$ be the finite type-A Hecke algebra obtained from [[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]] by $v\mapsto Q$. At cyclotomic level $r=1$, the Ariki–Koike algebra $H_{1,n}$ is isomorphic to $H_Q(n)$ by the map fixing each $T_i$, and its basis is $\{T_w:w\in S_n\}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $n\ge1$, the multiplicative level-one presentation over $R_1$, and the finite type-A Hecke algebra $H_Q(n)$.

[F1] [[def-hh-multiplicative-cyclotomic-hecke-algebra]]: the cyclotomic quotient imposes $p(X_1)=X_1-u=0$.

[F2] [[def-hh-multiplicative-cyclotomic-hecke-algebra]]: the affine recursion is $X_{i+1}=Q^{-1}T_iX_iT_i$.

[F3] [[def-hh-multiplicative-cyclotomic-hecke-algebra]]: $T_i^{-1}=Q^{-1}(T_i-(Q-1))$.

[F4] [[def-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]: specializing $H_v(n)$ by $v\mapsto Q$ gives the finite type-A presentation over $R_1$ with quadratic relation $(T_i-Q)(T_i+1)=0$.

[F5] [[thm-standard-basis-of-the-generic-type-a-hecke-algebra]]: the reduced-word elements $T_w$ form a basis of $H_v(n)$ over $\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]$.

[F6] [[thm-hh-ariki-koike-basis-and-generic-splitness]]: the elements $X_1^{a_1}\cdots X_n^{a_n}T_w$, $0\le a_j<r$, form an $R$-basis of $H_{r,n}$.

## Verification

**Given:** The algebras and presentations in the example, with the homomorphism on generators $T_i\mapsto T_i$.

1.1 At $r=1$, [F1] gives $p(X_1)=X_1-u=0$, so $X_1=u$ and $X_1^{-1}=u^{-1}$. By the affine recursion [F2], each $X_{i+1}$ lies in the subalgebra generated by $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}$ if $X_i$ does. Also $X_{i+1}^{-1}=Q T_i^{-1}X_i^{-1}T_i^{-1}$, which lies in the same subalgebra by [F3]. The generator relations agree with the specialization [F4], so the assignment $T_i\mapsto T_i$ defines a surjective homomorphism $H_Q(n)\to H_{1,n}$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 By [F5], $H_v(n)$ is the direct sum of the coefficient modules $\mathbb Z[v^{\pm1}]T_w$. Tensoring this explicit direct sum with $R_1$ shows that $H_Q(n)$ is free over $R_1$ with basis the images of $T_w$. By [F6] with $r=1$, the same family is an $R_1$-basis of $H_{1,n}$. The surjection of step 1.1 sends the source basis to this target basis, so its kernel is zero and it is an isomorphism. [F5, F6, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
