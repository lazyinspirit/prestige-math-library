## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $X$ be an indeterminate, and let $n\ge1$. For each
monic irreducible polynomial $P\in F[X]$ of positive degree, let
$v_P$ be the discrete valuation of $F(X)$ given by the exponent of $P$,
and let $k(P)=F[X]/(P)$ be its residue field. The inclusion of constants
induces $j:K_n^M(F)\to K_n^M(F(X))$. The residue maps give a split exact
sequence of abelian groups
$$ 0\longrightarrow K_n^M(F)\xrightarrow{\,j\,}K_n^M(F(X))\xrightarrow{\,\oplus_{P}\partial_{v_P}\,}\bigoplus_{P\text{ monic irreducible},\ \deg P>0}K_{n-1}^M(k(P))\longrightarrow0. $$
The direct sum means every element has finite support; the sequence is split
as a sequence of abelian groups.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, indeterminate, degree and finite-point valuations in the Statement.

[F1] Milnor groups are generated by multilinear Steinberg symbols and field inclusions act entrywise. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

[F2] Symbols anticommute and repeated entries reduce by $\{a,a\}=\{a,-1\}$. [[lem-milnor-symbol-anticommutativity-and-repeated-entry-identities]]

[F3] The actual residue and leading-unit maps are well-defined; the residue of units followed by a uniformizer is the residue unit symbol and all-unit symbols have residue zero. The leading-unit map is a homomorphism and fixes constants under their residue identification. [[lem-milnor-residue-specialization-and-principal-unit-kernel]] [[def-milnor-boundary-map-for-a-discrete-valuation]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** prove the degree-graded residue isomorphisms, then perform finite downward elimination and split using the global specialization retraction.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Specialization at infinity, with uniformizer $X^{-1}$, is a left inverse $s$ to the inclusion $j$, by [F3]. Thus $j$ is injective. For $m\ge0$ let $A_m$ be generated by symbols with nonzero polynomial entries of degree at most $m$. Factoring numerators and denominators and using multilinearity shows that every symbol lies in some $A_m$, and $A_0=jK_n^M(F)$. At any fixed symbol only its finitely many numerator/denominator irreducible factors can have nonzero residue, by [F3]; thus all residue tuples have finite support. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 For distinct monic polynomials $p,q$ of the same degree $m>0$, put $r=p-q$, so $0\ne r$ and $\deg r<m$. The Steinberg pair $r/p,q/p$ sums to $1$. Expansion and [F2] give $0=\{r,q\}-\{-r,p\}-\{p,q\}$. For example the last two terms result from $-\{r,p\}-\{p,q\}+\{p,p\}$ and $\{p,p\}=\{p,-1\}=-\{-1,p\}$. Factoring every entry into constants and monic irreducibles, this identity lowers the number of distinct degree-$m$ entries in each symbol; repeated occurrences of the same entry reduce to a single one by [F2]. Therefore $A_m/A_{m-1}$ is generated by $\{g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1},P\}$, where $P$ is monic irreducible of degree $m$ and each $g_i$ has degree less than $m$. This reduction is finite: each use removes at least one degree-$m$ occurrence in each resulting term, and all lower entries remain of degree less than $m$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For each such $P$, use the unique degree-less-than-$m$ representatives of nonzero classes in $k(P)$. Sending a residue symbol to $\{g_1,\ldots,g_{n-1},P\}$ modulo $A_{m-1}$ is multilinear. To check multiplication, write $gh=Pq+r$ with $0\ne r$ the residue representative. If $q=0$, the equality of symbols is exact. Otherwise $\deg q<m$ and $z=gh/r$ satisfies $1-z=-Pq/r$. Expansion of $0=\{z,1-z\}$ gives $\{z,P\}=-\{z,-q/r\}\in A_{m-1}$, because $g,h,r,q$ all have degree less than $m$. Multiplying by the remaining low-degree entries proves the required relation. If two residue representatives satisfy $\bar g_i+\bar g_j=1$, then $g_i+g_j-1$ has degree less than $m$ and is divisible by $P$, so it is zero. Their lifted Steinberg symbol is already zero. This constructs a homomorphism $f_P:K_{n-1}^M(k(P))\to A_m/A_{m-1}$. For $n=1$, define $f_P:\mathbb Z\to A_m/A_{m-1}$ by $1\mapsto\{P\}$; no Steinberg check is needed. [F1, F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The sum $f_m=\bigoplus_{\deg P=m}f_P$ is onto by step 2.1. Every degree-$m$ residue vanishes on $A_{m-1}$; on the proposed lift for $P$ the residue at $P$ is the original symbol, and the residue at any other degree-$m$ irreducible is zero. Thus these residues induce a homomorphism on $A_m/A_{m-1}$ whose composite with $f_m$ is the identity. Since $f_m$ is onto, they are mutually inverse. In particular a class in $A_m$ with zero degree-$m$ residues lies in $A_{m-1}$. No splitting of the filtered inclusion $A_{m-1}\subset A_m$ is inferred from this quotient statement. [F3, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 If all finite residues of $x\in A_m$ vanish, step 4.1 puts $x$ in $A_{m-1}$; repeat finitely until $x\in A_0$. This proves the claimed kernel. To lift a finitely supported tuple, take its maximum degree $m$ (the zero tuple has lift zero), and use step 3.1 to lift the degree-$m$ components to an actual finite sum of symbols. The difference of its residue tuple and the desired tuple has finite support in smaller degrees. Induct downward to correct that difference. At each stage only finitely many symbols and lifts are needed; a residue group element is by definition a finite sum of symbols. The process terminates at degree one, proving surjectivity without any family choice. [step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Finally the global retraction $s$ in step 1.1 splits the exact sequence. Every residue tuple has a lift $x$ by step 5.1; replace it by $x-j(sx)$ to obtain a lift in $\ker s$. This lift is unique: the difference of two such lifts is $j(a)$ by the kernel result, and applying $s$ gives $a=0$. The unique lift assignment is a homomorphism, because residues and $s$ are homomorphisms. It supplies the asserted section without choosing splittings of any graded piece. This includes $n=1$ and all fields, with no choice hypothesis added. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
