## Example

Let $k$ be a commutative ring and let
$B=k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})$ be the algebra of dual numbers, with class
$\epsilon$ satisfying $\epsilon^{2}=0$; for $k$ a field, $B$ is the coordinate
ring of the dual-numbers scheme $D_k$ ([[def-dual-numbers-scheme]]). Then the
Jacobian presentation of $\epsilon^{2}$ gives
$$\Omega_{B/k}\;=\;B\,\mathrm d\epsilon\big/\bigl(2\epsilon\,\mathrm d\epsilon\bigr)\;\cong\;B/(2\epsilon),$$
without any assumption on the characteristic, since the derivative of
$t^{2}$ is $2t$ and the relation is the cyclic submodule generated by
$2\epsilon\,\mathrm d\epsilon$. Consequently:

1. if $k$ has characteristic $2$, that is $2=0$ in $k$, then $2\epsilon=0$ and
   $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B\,\mathrm d\epsilon$ is free of rank one over $B$ with
   basis $\mathrm d\epsilon$;
2. if $k$ is a field of characteristic $\neq2$, then $2$ is invertible, so the
   ideal $(2\epsilon)$ is $(\epsilon)$, and
   $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B/(\epsilon)\cong k$ is one-dimensional over $k$ with
   basis $\mathrm d\epsilon$; it is not free over $B$;

in neither case is the answer obtained by inverting $2$ when it is not
invertible, and the relation can be trivial without the ring becoming a field.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $k$, the polynomial algebra $k[t]$, the element $f=t^{2}$, the quotient $B=k[t]/(t^{2})$ with the class $\epsilon$ of $t$, and (in the last case) a field $k$ of characteristic $\neq2$.

[F1] [[cor-jacobian-presentation-differentials]]: for $P=A[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ and $B=P/I$ with $I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$, the module $\Omega_{B/A}$ is the cokernel of the $B$-linear map $B^{r}\to B^{n}$ whose $j$-th column is $(\partial f_j/\partial x_1,\dots,\partial f_j/\partial x_n)$; in particular for $n=r=1$ and $I=(f)$ one gets $\Omega_{B/A}\cong B/(f')$ with $f'$ the derivative, the cokernel of multiplication by $f'$ on $B$.

[F2] [[def-dual-numbers-scheme]]: for a field $k$ the dual-numbers scheme is $D_k=\operatorname{Spec}\bigl(k[\epsilon]/(\epsilon^{2})\bigr)$, so the ring $B$ above is its coordinate ring and $\Omega_{B/k}$ is the module whose associated sheaf is $\Omega_{D_k/k}$.

[F3] [[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]]: for a ring homomorphism $\varphi\colon R\to S$ there is an isomorphism $R/\ker\varphi\cong\operatorname{im}\varphi$; applied to the evaluation $B\to k$, $\epsilon\mapsto0$, it identifies $B/(\epsilon)\cong k$ because that map is surjective with kernel the ideal $(\epsilon)$.

## Verification

1.1 Apply [F1] with $A=k$, $n=1$, $r=1$, $P=k[t]$, $f_1=t^{2}$ and $B=k[t]/(t^{2})$: the derivative is $f'=2t$, whose class in $B$ is $2\epsilon$, so the cokernel of multiplication by $2\epsilon$ on $B$ is $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B/(2\epsilon)$. Writing the image of the free generator for $B^{1}$ as $\mathrm d\epsilon$, this reads $\Omega_{B/k}=B\,\mathrm d\epsilon/(2\epsilon\,\mathrm d\epsilon)$, the submodule $B(2\epsilon\,\mathrm d\epsilon)$ corresponding to the ideal $(2\epsilon)\subseteq B$ under the identification $B\,\mathrm d\epsilon\cong B$. [F1, given]

2.1 Suppose $2=0$ in $k$. Then $2\epsilon=0\in B$, so the ideal $(2\epsilon)$ is the zero ideal [step 1.1], and $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B/(0)=B$: the assignment $b\mapsto b\,\mathrm d\epsilon$ is a $B$-module isomorphism $B\to\Omega_{B/k}$ with inverse induced by $\mathrm d\epsilon\mapsto1$, so $\mathrm d\epsilon$ is a basis of $\Omega_{B/k}$ over $B$ and $\Omega_{B/k}$ is free of rank one. [step 1.1, given]

2.2 Suppose $k$ is a field of characteristic $\neq2$. Then $2\neq0$ in $k$, so $2$ is a unit of $k$ and hence of $B$, and $(2\epsilon)=(\epsilon)$: the two generators differ by the unit $2$ [step 1.1]. Hence $\Omega_{B/k}\cong B/(\epsilon)$. By [F3], applied to the surjection $B\to k$ with $\epsilon\mapsto0$ whose kernel is $(\epsilon)$, one has $B/(\epsilon)\cong k$, so $\Omega_{B/k}\cong k$ is one-dimensional over $k$ with basis image of $\mathrm d\epsilon$, and $\epsilon\,\mathrm d\epsilon=0$ holds in $\Omega_{B/k}$ while $\mathrm d\epsilon\neq0$. [step 1.1, F3]

3.1 In the case of step 2.2 the module $\Omega_{B/k}$ is not free over $B$: it has $k$-dimension $1$, whereas a free $B$-module of rank one has $k$-dimension equal to $\dim_k B=2$, since $\{1,\epsilon\}$ is a $k$-basis of $B$ (every class in $k[t]/(t^{2})$ is uniquely $c+d\epsilon$ with $c,d\in k$). In the case of step 2.1 the dimension count is reversed and $\Omega_{B/k}$ is free of rank one, so the two characteristics genuinely give different answers, and the presentation of step 1.1 is the common source of both. By [F2] these computations are those of the relative differentials of the dual-numbers scheme over $k$ when $k$ is a field. [step 2.1, step 2.2, F2, given] ∎
