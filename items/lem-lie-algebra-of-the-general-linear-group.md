## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the finite-type assertions inherited from the matrix-group supplier. Let $k$ be a field and $n\ge1$. Under the identification $\operatorname{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[x_{ij},d^{-1}]$ of [[lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]], the tangent space at the identity is $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)\cong M_n(k)=\mathfrak{gl}_n$, the isomorphism sending a matrix $X$ to the dual-number point $I_n+\varepsilon X$; in particular $\operatorname{Lie}(\mathbf G_m)\cong k$ for $n=1$, generated by the functional dual to the cotangent class $[x_{11}-1]=[t-1]$. For matrices $X,Y$ the commutator of the lifts $I+tX$ and $I+t'Y$ in $\operatorname{GL}_n\bigl(k[t,t']/(t^2,t'^2)\bigr)$ is $I+tt'(XY-YX)$, and the adjoint representation of [[lem-adjoint-representation-of-an-affine-group-scheme]] satisfies $\operatorname{Ad}(A)X=AXA^{-1}$ for all commutative $k$-algebras $R$, $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ and $X\in M_n(R)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and a field $k$, an integer $n\ge1$, the coordinate ring $A=k[x_{ij},d^{-1}]$ with $d=\det(x_{ij})$, and matrices $X,Y\in M_n(k)$.

[F1] [[lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]]: $\operatorname{GL}_n=\operatorname{Spec}k[x_{ij},d^{-1}]$ is a group scheme of finite type over $k$ whose comultiplication is $\Delta(x_{ij})=\sum_lx_{il}\otimes x_{lj}$ and whose $R$-points are the invertible $n\times n$ matrices over $R$; for $n=1$ this is $\mathbf G_m=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$ with $t=x_{11}$.

[F2] [[def-lie-algebra-of-a-group-scheme]] and [[thm-cotangent-space-maximal-ideal-quotient]]: at the $k$-rational identity $e$ one has $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)=\operatorname{Hom}_k(\mathfrak m_e/\mathfrak m_e^2,k)$, and the cotangent space is $\Omega\otimes_{\mathcal O_{G,e}}\kappa(e)\cong\mathfrak m_e/\mathfrak m_e^2$ with $\mathrm dx\otimes1\leftrightarrow[x-\varepsilon(x)]$.

[F3] [[lem-lie-algebra-tangent-space-and-functoriality]]: for every commutative $k$-algebra $R$ one has $\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)(R)\cong\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)\otimes_kR$ and the elements $e^{\varepsilon X}$ correspond to $X$; the group law is addition, so the point $e^{\varepsilon X}$ with $\varepsilon$-coefficient $X$ is the base-changed dual-number point.

[F4] [[thm-ring-matrix-arithmetic-laws]], [[cor-inverse-matrix-by-adjugate]], [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]] and [[def-trace-of-a-square-matrix-over-a-commutative-ring]]: matrix multiplication is associative and distributive on both sides, $(I+\varepsilon X)^{-1}=I-\varepsilon X$ when $\varepsilon^2=0$, and expanding $\det(I+\varepsilon X)$ by the Leibniz formula over permutations shows $\det(I+\varepsilon X)=1+\varepsilon\operatorname{tr}X$, a unit of $k[\varepsilon]$.

[F5] [[def-linear-basis]]: a basis is an independent spanning set; for a finite basis $v_{ij}$ of $V$, the functionals $v_{ij}^*$ defined by $v_{ij}^*(v_{ab})=\delta_{ia}\delta_{jb}$ form a basis of $\operatorname{Hom}_k(V,k)$, since a functional is determined by these finitely many values.

[F6] [[lem-adjoint-representation-of-an-affine-group-scheme]]: $\operatorname{Ad}:G\to\operatorname{GL}_{\mathfrak g}$ satisfies $x\,e^{\varepsilon X}\,x^{-1}=e^{\varepsilon\operatorname{Ad}(x)X}$ for $x\in G(R)$ and $X\in\mathfrak g\otimes_kR$.

## Proof

1.1 The cotangent space and dual-number points. Set $y_{ij}=x_{ij}-\delta_{ij}$ and $J=(y_{ij})\subseteq A$. Then $A=k[y_{ij}][D^{-1}]$ with $D=\det(\delta_{ij}+y_{ij})\equiv1\bmod J$, so $A/J^2\cong k[y_{ij}]/(y_{ij})^2$ has basis $1,[y_{ij}]$. Here $J$ is the augmentation ideal in $A$, and $\mathfrak m_e=JA_J$ is the maximal ideal in $\mathcal O_{G,e}=A_J$. Every element of $A\setminus J$ has nonzero constant term and is a unit modulo $J^2$, with inverse given by the square-zero formula; thus localization does not change this quotient and $\mathfrak m_e/\mathfrak m_e^2$ has basis $[y_{ij}]$. Its dual is $M_n(k)$ via $\theta_X([y_{ij}])=X_{ij}$ by [F2] and [F5]. The coefficient correspondence sends $\theta_X$ to the algebra map $A\to k[\varepsilon]$, $y_{ij}\mapsto\varepsilon X_{ij}$, since $\det(I+\varepsilon X)=1+\varepsilon\operatorname{tr}X$ is a unit by [F4]; hence $e^{\varepsilon X}=I+\varepsilon X$. By [F3] this identification extends naturally to $M_n(R)$ for every $R$. For $n=1$ the Lie-algebra generator is the functional taking $[t-1]$ to $1$, dual to the cotangent generator. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

1.2 The commutator of the two lifts. In $B=k[t,t']/(t^2,t'^2)$ one has $t^2=t'^2=0$, so $(I+tX)^{-1}=I-tX$ and $(I+t'Y)^{-1}=I-t'Y$ by [F4], and using associativity and distributivity one computes $(I+tX)(I+t'Y)(I-tX)(I-t'Y)=I+tt'(XY-YX)$: the terms linear in $t$ or $t'$ cancel in pairs and the only surviving second-order term is $tt'XY-tt'YX$, the other products $t^2,t'^2$ vanishing. [F4, algebra]

2.1 The adjoint action. Let $R$ be a commutative $k$-algebra, $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n(R)$ and $X\in M_n(R)$. By step 1.1 applied over $R$, the element $e^{\varepsilon X}\in\operatorname{Lie}(\operatorname{GL}_n)(R)$ is the point $I+\varepsilon X$ of $\operatorname{GL}_n(R[\varepsilon])$, and $A$ is invertible in $M_n(R)\subseteq M_n(R[\varepsilon])$; conjugating with $A$ and using the matrix arithmetic of [F4] gives $A(I+\varepsilon X)A^{-1}=I+\varepsilon(AXA^{-1})$. Comparing with the defining identity $A\,e^{\varepsilon X}A^{-1}=e^{\varepsilon\operatorname{Ad}(A)X}$ of [F6] identifies the $\varepsilon$-coefficient, so $\operatorname{Ad}(A)X=AXA^{-1}$. [F4, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Conclusion. Step 1.1 identifies the tangent space at the identity with $M_n(k)$ via $X\mapsto I+\varepsilon X$ and gives the $n=1$ case; step 1.2 computes the commutator of the two independent lifts as $I+tt'(XY-YX)$; and step 2.1 computes the adjoint representation as conjugation. This proves all the displayed claims. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks
