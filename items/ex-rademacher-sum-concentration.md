## Example

Assume AC. Let $n\ge1$ and let $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ be independent Rademacher variables, meaning $\mathbb P(X_i=1)=\mathbb P(X_i=-1)=1/2$, and put $S_n=\sum_{i=1}^nX_i$. For every $t>0$,
$$\mathbb P(|S_n|\ge t)\le2e^{-t^2/(2n)}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, an integer $n\ge1$, and independent real random variables $X_1,\ldots,X_n$ with the two-point laws stated in the Example.

[A1] Assume AC as required by the Hoeffding supplier used here ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] A real random variable is a measurable map to $(\mathbb R,\mathcal B(\mathbb R))$ ([[def-random-element-and-real-random-variable]]).

[F2] Independence means independence of the sigma-algebras generated by the variables ([[def-independent-random-elements]]).

[F3] Expectation is integration against the probability law ([[def-expectation-of-a-nonnegative-or-integrable-random-variable]]).

[F4] For independent variables in finite ranges $[a_i,b_i]$, Hoeffding gives $\mathbb P(|\sum_i(X_i-\mathbb E X_i)|\ge t)\le2e^{-2t^2/\sum_i(b_i-a_i)^2}$ when the denominator is positive ([[thm-hoeffding-independent-bounded-sums]]).

## Verification

1.1 Each $X_i$ is a real random variable by [F1], and their given independence is understood by [F2]. Their two-point laws make them integrable with mean $(1/2)\cdot1+(1/2)\cdot(-1)=0$ by [F3], and they lie in $[-1,1]$ almost surely. Thus the Hoeffding width sum is $\sum_i(1-(-1))^2=4n$. [given, A1, F1, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 Apply [F4] with those endpoints. The exponent is $-2t^2/(4n)=-t^2/(2n)$, yielding the stated bound. [F4, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
