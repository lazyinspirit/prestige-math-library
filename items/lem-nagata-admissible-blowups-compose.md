## Statement

Let $X$ be Noetherian, let $\mathcal I$ be a coherent ideal, and put $q:X_1=\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I}(X)\to X$. For every coherent ideal $\mathcal K$ on $X_1$, there exist a nonnegative integer $N$ and a coherent ideal $\mathcal M$ on $X$ such that, as image ideals on $X_1$,
$$\mathcal M\mathcal O_{X_1}=\mathcal K(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{X_1})^N.$$
Consequently there is an isomorphism over $X$
$$\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal K}(X_1)\cong\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I\mathcal M}(X),$$
compatible with its canonical map to $X_1$. If $\mathcal I$ is the unit ideal on an open $U\subseteq X$ and $\mathcal K$ is the unit ideal over $U$, then $\mathcal M$ and $\mathcal I\mathcal M$ are unit ideals on $U$. Thus a finite composition of coherent-ideal blowups whose centers avoid $U$ is a single coherent-ideal blowup whose center avoids $U$. Empty schemes and zero and unit ideals are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian scheme, coherent ideals, and, for the admissibility assertion, the open $U$ with the stated unit restrictions.

[F1] Finite affine blowup charts have the form $A[I/a]\subseteq A_a$, with $a$ regular on the chart and $I$ generated there by $a$. They glue compatibly with localization and are Noetherian over a Noetherian base. [[lem-nagata-finite-affine-blowup-chart-gluing]]

[F2] Blowups satisfy the universal property for invertible image ideals and are proper, separated and isomorphisms away from their center. [[lem-nagata-admissible-blowup-universal-property-and-properness]]

[F3] For coherent base ideals $I,J$, the blowup of $IJ$ is canonically the blowup of the image of $J$ on $\operatorname{Bl}_I X$; either order gives the same result. [[lem-nagata-product-blowup-dominates-factor-blowups]]

[F4] Localization commutes with kernels and finite equalizers. Intersections of affine opens in a separated scheme over an affine scheme are affine. [[cor-localisation-commutes-with-kernels-images-and-cokernels]] [[thm-separatedness-gluing-overlap-criterion]]

## Proof

1.1 First let $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, write $I=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$, and put $B_i=A[I/a_i]$. On each nonempty chart choose a finite generating family $k_{i\ell}$ for $\mathcal K$. By the fraction description [F1], after taking a common exponent $n\geq0$ these generators have the form $k_{i\ell}=c_{i\ell}/a_i^n$ with $c_{i\ell}\in I^n$. Indeed, every finite polynomial in fractions with numerators in $I$ can be put over one such power; increasing the power multiplies the numerator by a power of $a_i$. On chart $i$ the section $c_{i\ell}$ therefore belongs to $\mathcal K(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{X_1})^n$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 On chart $j$, its overlap with chart $i$ is the principal open where $u_{ji}=a_i/a_j$ is invertible. The preceding membership holds on that overlap. Thus the class of $c_{i\ell}$ in $B_j/(\mathcal K B_j\,a_j^n)$ becomes zero after localization at $u_{ji}$. By the definition of localization some power of $u_{ji}$ kills it. There are only finitely many indices and generators, so one common $m\geq0$ works for all of them. Multiplying this membership by $a_j^m$ gives $$a_i^m c_{i\ell}\in\mathcal K B_j\,a_j^{n+m}.$$ Let $L\subseteq A$ be generated by the finitely many $e_{i\ell}=a_i^m c_{i\ell}$ and put $N=n+m$. The displayed containment proves $L\mathcal O_{X_1}\subseteq\mathcal K(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{X_1})^N$. On chart $i$, its own generators satisfy $e_{i\ell}=a_i^N k_{i\ell}$; hence they generate the reverse inclusion there. This proves equality globally over this affine base. Zero charts make no contribution and impose no condition. [F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Choose a finite affine cover of the Noetherian $X$ and apply steps 1.1–2.1 to it. Choose a common $N$ at least as large as the finitely many resulting exponents. Multiplying each local ideal by the corresponding additional power of $\mathcal I$ makes its pullback equal to the same ideal $\mathcal H=\mathcal K(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{X_1})^N$. Let $D\subseteq X_1$ be the closed subscheme defined by $\mathcal H$, and define canonically $$\mathcal M=\ker(\mathcal O_X\longrightarrow q_*\mathcal O_D).$$ This is a coherent ideal, as follows directly on an affine $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$. Its inverse image in $D$ is Noetherian and has a finite affine cover; pairwise intersections are affine by separatedness and [F4]. Sections on that inverse image are the equalizer of the two maps between the finite products of the cover and intersection coordinate rings. Restricting to $D(h)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$ localizes all those rings at $h$. Exact localization and finite products therefore identify the restricted equalizer with the localization of the original one. The kernel ideal $M$ in $A$ consequently restricts to $M_h$ on each principal open. This gives a quasi-coherent ideal, and Noetherianity makes this ideal and its finite relation kernels finitely generated, proving coherence directly. No coherent-pushforward theorem is used. [F2, F4, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 By its definition, $\mathcal M\mathcal O_{X_1}\subseteq\mathcal H$: its sections vanish on $D$. Each local ideal constructed in step 3.1 is contained in $\mathcal M$, since its sections pull back into $\mathcal H$ and hence vanish on $D$. Those local ideals generate $\mathcal H$ after pullback. Thus $\mathcal M\mathcal O_{X_1}=\mathcal H$, establishing the first assertion. If the two given ideals are unit over $U$, then $q$ is the identity there and $\mathcal H$ is unit there. Hence $D$ has empty inverse image over $U$, and the defining kernel is the unit ideal on $U$. [F2, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 An invertible ideal $\mathcal L$ has identity blowup: the identity morphism satisfies the universal property [F2], giving inverse maps by uniqueness. Apply [F3] on $X_1$ to $\mathcal K$ and the invertible ideal $(\mathcal I\mathcal O_{X_1})^N$. Its blowup is the identity, so that formula identifies the blowup of their product with $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal K}(X_1)$. By step 4.1 the product is $\mathcal M\mathcal O_{X_1}$. A second application of [F3], now on $X$ to $\mathcal I,\mathcal M$, identifies that blowup with $\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathcal I\mathcal M}(X)$, compatibly with its map to $X_1$. The unit assertion follows from step 4.1. Induction proves the finite-composition assertion; the empty composition is the blowup of the unit ideal. If $X_1$ is empty, the kernel construction gives $\mathcal M=\mathcal O_X$ and both schemes in the asserted isomorphism are empty. Zero and unit ideals and empty original schemes are therefore covered as well. [F1, F2, F3, step 4.1, construct, algebra] ∎
