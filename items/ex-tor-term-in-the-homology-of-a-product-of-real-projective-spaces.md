## Example

Assume AC. There is an abstract isomorphism $H_3(\mathbb{RP}^3\times\mathbb{RP}^3;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\oplus\mathbb Z/2$. Its order-two summand is the degree-three Tor correction from the two degree-one homology groups. The tensor cross products alone do not generate this group.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] [[lem-real-projective-space-cellular-homology-and-pinch-map]] gives the integral homology of $\mathbb{RP}^3$ as $\mathbb Z,\mathbb Z/2,0,\mathbb Z$ in degrees zero through three and zero elsewhere.

[F2] [[thm-topological-kunneth-short-exact-sequence-for-homology]] gives the tensor and Tor diagonals, and [[prop-the-homology-kunneth-sequence-splits-nonnaturally]] splits their exact sequence.

[F3] [[def-balanced-tor-bifunctor]] computes Tor from supplied projective resolutions. [[thm-free-modules-are-projective-with-choice-boundary]] makes the displayed free modules projective. We assume [[def-axiom-of-choice]], including the AC/DC uses of [F2].

## Proof

**Given:** $X=Y=\mathbb{RP}^3$, integral coefficients, total degree three and AC.

1.1 The pairs of nonnegative indices with sum three are $(0,3),(1,2),(2,1),(3,0)$. Using [F1], their tensor groups are respectively $\mathbb Z,0,0,\mathbb Z$: tensor with zero is zero, and $\mathbb Z\otimes\mathbb Z\cong\mathbb Z$ by multiplication with inverse $a\mapsto a\otimes1$. Thus the tensor diagonal is $K=\mathbb Z^2$, generated by the top class of each factor crossed with a point in the other. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 The Tor indices have sum two: $(0,2),(1,1),(2,0)$. The outer pairs have a zero input and hence zero Tor, computed with the zero resolution in that variable. For the middle pair use the exact free resolution $0\to\mathbb Z\xrightarrow{2}\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z/2\to0$. Tensoring with $\mathbb Z/2$ makes its only differential multiplication by two, hence zero. Its degree-one homology is the kernel of that zero map, all of $\mathbb Z/2$, with no incoming degree-two boundary. Therefore $Q=\operatorname{Tor}_1(\mathbb Z/2,\mathbb Z/2)=\mathbb Z/2$. [F1, F3, given]

2.1 Substitution in [F2] gives $0\to\mathbb Z^2\xrightarrow{\times}H_3(X\times Y;\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\gamma}\mathbb Z/2\to0$. Let $j$ be a section supplied there. Then $t=j(\overline1)$ is nonzero since $\gamma t=\overline1$, and $2t=j(0)=0$. Every class $w$ has a unique expression $\alpha(k)+j(q)$: take $q=\gamma w$, then $w-j(q)$ belongs to the image of the injective cross product $\alpha$. Uniqueness follows by applying $\gamma$ and then injectivity of $\alpha$. This proves the asserted direct sum and exhibits its nonzero order-two class. Its choice depends on the section; the nonzero Tor quotient does not. [F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 The witness $t$ cannot belong to the tensor image, since that image is the kernel of $\gamma$ while $\gamma t=\overline1\ne0$. The two free generators cannot cancel this correction. The zero element of the Tor group lifts to zero under $j$, and its sole nonzero element has order exactly two. The dimensions are fixed at three; no claim is made by substituting $\mathbb{RP}^0$ or an empty factor. AC is inherited from the Kunneth sequence and its splitting, and from its balanced-Tor convention; these finite resolution calculations introduce no additional choices. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
