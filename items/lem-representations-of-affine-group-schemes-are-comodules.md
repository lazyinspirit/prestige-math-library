## Statement

Let $k$ be a field, let $G$ be an affine group scheme of finite type over $k$ with coordinate Hopf algebra $A=\mathcal O(G)$ ([[def-coordinate-hopf-algebra-of-affine-group-scheme]]), and let $V$ be a $k$-vector space. The construction of [[def-rational-representation-and-comodule-of-an-affine-group-scheme]] is a bijection, natural in $V$ and in $G$, between $A$-comodule structures $\rho\colon V\to V\otimes_kA$ and rational representations $r\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_V$, and it maps subcomodules to subrepresentations. If $V$ is finite dimensional with basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ and $\rho(e_j)=\sum_ie_i\otimes a_{ij}$, then the matrix coefficients satisfy
$$\Delta(a_{ij})=\sum_ka_{ik}\otimes a_{kj},\qquad\varepsilon(a_{ij})=\delta_{ij},$$
and the comorphism of the associated morphism $G\to\operatorname{GL}_n$ is $x_{ij}\mapsto a_{ij}$. No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] A rational representation is a natural family of group homomorphisms $r_R\colon G(R)\to\operatorname{Aut}_R(V\otimes_kR)$, and an $A$-comodule structure is a $k$-linear $\rho\colon V\to V\otimes_kA$ with $(\rho\otimes\operatorname{id}_A)\rho=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\Delta)\rho$ and $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\varepsilon)\rho=\operatorname{id}_V$. ([[def-rational-representation-and-comodule-of-an-affine-group-scheme]], [[def-commutative-hopf-algebra-over-a-field]])

[F2] $G(R)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k\text{-alg}}(A,R)$ is a group under the convolution product $g\ast h=m_R\circ(g\otimes h)\circ\Delta$, with identity the algebra map $A\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}k\to R$, and the universal points $p_1,p_2\colon A\to A\otimes_kA$, $p_i(a)=$ the two tensor embeddings, satisfy $p_1\ast p_2=\Delta$. ([[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]], [[def-functor-of-points-affine-scheme]])

[F3] The Yoneda lemma identifies natural transformations $h_A\to F$ on $k$-algebras with elements of $F(A)$, naturally; for $F=\operatorname{GL}_V$ the functoriality is base change of $A$-linear automorphisms along $A\to R$. ([[thm-yoneda-lemma-is-natural-in-both-variables]], [[def-functor-of-points-affine-scheme]])

[F4] The choice-free coordinate and point constructions of the matrix supplier (without its finite-type conclusion) give the coordinate ring of $\operatorname{GL}_n$ is $k[x_{ij},d^{-1}]$ with $d=\det(x_{ij})$ and points the invertible matrices, and the antipode identity together with [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]] makes $\det(a_{ij})$ a unit when $(a_{ij})$ arises from a comodule. ([[lem-general-linear-group-scheme-and-its-coordinate-ring]], [[cor-square-matrix-invertible-iff-determinant-is-a-unit]])

[F5] Ring homomorphisms correspond to morphisms of affine spectra contravariantly. ([[thm-affine-scheme-ring-anti-equivalence]])

[F6] A finite basis of a vector space gives explicit coordinate functionals, and the tensor product is generated by elementary tensors subject to the universal property. ([[def-linear-basis]], [[def-vector-space]], [[def-linear-map]], [[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]])

## Proof

**Given:** A field $k$, an affine group scheme $G$ of finite type over $k$ with coordinate Hopf algebra $A=\mathcal O(G)$, a $k$-vector space $V$, and the definitions [F1].

1.1 (From a comodule to a representation.) Let $\rho\colon V\to V\otimes_kA$ satisfy the comodule axioms. For a commutative unital $k$-algebra $R$ and $g\in G(R)$, define $r_R(g)$ to be the $R$-linear endomorphism of $V\otimes_kR$ with $r_R(g)(v\otimes x)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\rho(v)\cdot x$. This is natural in $R$. If $\rho(v)=\sum_iv_i\otimes a_i$, then $r_R(g)r_R(h)(v\otimes1)=\sum_i(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\rho(v_i)\cdot h(a_i)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes(gh))\rho(v)=r_R(gh)(v\otimes1)$ by coassociativity and the convolution product of [F2], and by $R$-linearity this proves multiplicativity; the counit identity gives $r_R(e_R)=\operatorname{id}$, since $e_R$ factors through $\varepsilon$. Hence each $r_R(g)$ is invertible with inverse $r_R(g^{-1})$ and $r$ is a rational representation. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 (From a representation to a comodule.) Let $r\colon G\to\operatorname{GL}_V$ be a rational representation. By [F3] it corresponds to the element $\varphi=r_A(\operatorname{id}_A)\in\operatorname{GL}_V(A)=\operatorname{Aut}_A(V\otimes_kA)$, and naturality at $g\colon A\to R$ gives $r_R(g)(v\otimes1)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\varphi(v\otimes1)$. Indeed $(V\otimes_kA)\otimes_A R\cong V\otimes_kR$ by $(v\otimes a)\otimes x\mapsto v\otimes g(a)x$, so the base-changed automorphism sends $v\otimes1$ to $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\varphi(v\otimes1)$. Put $\rho(v)=\varphi(v\otimes1)$; then $\varphi(v\otimes a)=\rho(v)a$ by $A$-linearity, and $r_R(g)(v\otimes1)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\rho(v)$. The identity element of $G(k)$ is $\varepsilon$, so $r_k(\varepsilon)=\operatorname{id}$ gives $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\varepsilon)\rho=\operatorname{id}_V$. For coassociativity, $p_1\ast p_2=\Delta$ in $G(A\otimes_kA)$ by [F2], so $r_{A\otimes_kA}(\Delta)=r_{A\otimes_kA}(p_1)r_{A\otimes_kA}(p_2)$, and evaluating at $v\otimes1$ gives $\sum_i\rho(v_i)\otimes a_i=\sum_iv_i\otimes\Delta(a_i)$, that is, $(\rho\otimes\operatorname{id}_A)\rho=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\Delta)\rho$. Hence $\rho$ is a comodule structure. [F1, F2, F3, algebra]

1.3 (Matrix coefficients.) Let $V$ be finite dimensional with basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$ and $\rho(e_j)=\sum_ie_i\otimes a_{ij}$. Comparing the components of $e_i$ under the finite coordinate functionals of $V$ in the coassociativity identity gives $\Delta(a_{ij})=\sum_la_{il}\otimes a_{lj}$, and comparing them in $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\varepsilon)\rho(e_j)=e_j$ gives $\varepsilon(a_{ij})=\delta_{ij}$. [F1, F6, algebra]

2.1 (The two constructions are inverse.) If $\rho$ gives $r$ by step 1.1 and $r$ gives $\rho'$ by step 1.2, then $\rho'(v)=r_A(\operatorname{id}_A)(v\otimes1)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\operatorname{id}_A)\rho(v)=\rho(v)$; conversely if $r$ gives $\rho$ and $\rho$ gives $r'$, then $r'_R(g)$ and $r_R(g)$ are $R$-linear and agree on all $v\otimes1$ by step 1.2, hence $r'=r$. For a linear map $T\colon V\to W$ between two such structures, $(T\otimes\operatorname{id}_A)\rho_V=\rho_W T$ implies $(T\otimes\operatorname{id}_R)r_{V,R}(g)=r_{W,R}(g)(T\otimes\operatorname{id}_R)$ by evaluation; conversely the latter identities at $R=A$, $g=\operatorname{id}_A$ give the former. Pullback along a group morphism $f\colon H\to G$ is $(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes\mathcal O(f))\rho$ on coactions and $r\circ f$ on actions. These formulas specify the asserted naturality. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2]

2.2 (Subcomodules and subrepresentations.) If $N\subseteq V$ is a subcomodule, then $r_R(g)(N\otimes_kR)=(\operatorname{id}_V\otimes g)\rho(N)\cdot R\subseteq N\otimes_kR$ for every $R$ and $g$ by step 1.1, so $N$ is a subrepresentation. Conversely, if $N$ is stable under every $r_R(g)$, then taking $R=A$ and $g=\operatorname{id}_A$ in step 1.2 gives $\rho(N)=r_A(\operatorname{id}_A)(N\otimes1)\subseteq N\otimes_kA$, so $N$ is a subcomodule. The correspondence preserves inclusions. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 (The comorphism of the associated morphism.) If $V=0$, both structures are unique and the associated morphism is $G\to\operatorname{GL}_0=\operatorname{Spec}k$, with comorphism the structure map $k\to A$; the coefficient identities are empty. Otherwise $n\ge1$. In the situation of step 1.3, the two antipode identities give $\sum_lS(a_{il})a_{lj}=\delta_{ij}=\sum_la_{il}S(a_{lj})$, so the matrix $(a_{ij})$ over the commutative ring $A$ has two-sided inverse $(S(a_{ij}))$ and therefore unit determinant by [F4]. Hence $\varphi\colon k[x_{ij},d^{-1}]\to A$, $x_{ij}\mapsto a_{ij}$, $d^{-1}\mapsto\det(a_{ij})^{-1}$, is a well-defined $k$-algebra homomorphism, and by [F5] it is the comorphism of a morphism $G\to\operatorname{GL}_n$. On $R$-points this morphism sends $g$ to the matrix $(\varphi(x_{ij})(g))=(g(a_{ij}))$, which by step 1.1 and step 1.3 is exactly the matrix of $r_R(g)$ in the basis $e_1,\dots,e_n$; by [F4] this identifies the morphism associated with $r$ under the basis with $\operatorname{GL}_n$ and its comorphism with $x_{ij}\mapsto a_{ij}$. All constructions used the given coaction or point-action, explicit finite bases and the functorialities of [F3]-[F5]; no choice principle is used. [F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 1.3] ∎
