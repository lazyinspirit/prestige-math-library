## Statement refuted

False claim: Bezout's identity $\sum_pI_p(C,D)=de$ holds for all pairs of plane curves, without the hypothesis that the curves have no common component.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC [[def-axiom-of-choice]], the algebraically closed field $k$, the plane projective curves $C=V(x_0)$ of degree one and $D=V(x_0x_1)$ of degree two, and an arbitrary point $p$ of the shared line. In a chart $x_i=1$ containing $p$ (where $i=1$ or $2$), write $f=x_0/x_i$ and $g=(x_0/x_i)(x_1/x_i)$ for the local equations.

[F1] $x_0$ and $x_0x_1$ are nonconstant square-free forms, so $C$ and $D$ are plane projective curves; $V(x_0)$ is an irreducible component of both, so $C$ and $D$ share the component $V(x_0)$ [[def-plane-projective-curve]].

[F2] At $p\in V(x_0)$ the local ideal is $(f,g)=(x_0/x_i)$, whose nonunit irreducible generator is a local coordinate vanishing on the line; hence $O/(f,g)$ is not of finite length and the definition records $I_p(C,D)=\infty$ [[def-local-intersection-multiplicity-plane-curves]], [[lem-local-intersection-length-finite]], [[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]].

[F3] The Bezout theorem is stated under the no-common-component hypothesis; it computes a finite sum of finite multiplicities [[thm-bezout-plane-curves]]. A module of infinite length is not a finite summand [[def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module]].

## Counterexample

1.1 The curves $C$ and $D$ share the line $V(x_0)$, by [F1], and at every point $p$ of that line the local ideal is generated by the common irreducible factor $x_0/x_i$. [F1, given]

1.2 At such a point the quotient $O/(x_0/x_i)$ is the local ring of the line, of infinite length as an $O$-module, so $I_p(C,D)=\infty$ by [F2]; in particular the local multiplicities do not form a finite sum. [F2, given]

2.1 The line has infinitely many points, already the points $[0:1:b]$ for $b\in k$ with $k$ infinite by [[def-plane-projective-curve]] (Remarks). Since the sum $\sum_pI_p(C,D)$ contains infinitely many infinite terms, it is not the finite number $de=2$: the hypothesis of no common component cannot be dropped from the Bezout identity, whose statements are those of [F3]. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F3] ∎
