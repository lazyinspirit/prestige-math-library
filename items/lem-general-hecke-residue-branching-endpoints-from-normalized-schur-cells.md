## Statement

Let $F$ be any field, $q\in F^\times$ have finite quantum characteristic $e\ge2$, and let $H_d=H_{F,q}(S_d)$ with $(T_j-q)(T_j+1)=0$. Use the actual Murphy cell-form quotients $D(\lambda)$, indexed by $e$-restricted partitions, converted to left modules by star. For additive Hecke JM elements $L_d$, let $E_i$ be the $[i]_q$-generalized eigenspace in restriction to $H_{d-1}$; let $F_i$ be induction followed by its commuting right $[i]_q$-primary projector. These functors are defined over $F$, for $i\in\mathbb Z/e$, with $[i]_1=i$ and $[i]_q=(q^i-1)/(q-1)$ otherwise.

For every restricted $\lambda$ the nonzero $E_iD(\lambda)$ have simple socle and simple head $D(\widetilde e_i\lambda)$, each once, and $E_iD(\lambda)=0$ exactly when the good $i$-deletion is undefined. Similarly the nonzero $F_iD(\mu)$ have simple socle and simple head $D(\widetilde f_i\mu)$, each once, and vanish exactly when the cogood $i$-addition is undefined. Here signatures use content $c-r$, top-to-bottom order, cancellation of $-+$, first surviving minus and last surviving plus ([[def-residue-signature-normal-good-conormal-and-cogood-nodes]]). In particular the restriction socle is the multiplicity-free sum over good residues. This proves endpoints, not total composition multiplicities. Composite $e$, arbitrary characteristic, nonsquare $q$, and rank zero are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, parameter, actual cellular labels, residue convention and functors of the Statement.

[F1] The first-level Weyl filtration, one-dimensional primitive/Hom bound and coefficient test, and the actual integral permutation/tensor corner identification are proved in [[lem-polynomial-quantum-schur-first-level-weyl-filtration-and-hecke-corner]]. That supplier constructs the abstract highest norm-one form directly on the actual finite cell; it has radical the maximal submodule and gives self-dual simples. Its complete local finite-cell construction is used; no Choice-bearing free global highest-lattice isomorphism is invoked.

[F2] Central symmetric JM operators act on actual Schur Weyl cells by their node-content scalars, and every constituent retains their full residue multiset ([[lem-q-schur-central-hecke-jm-scalars-give-residue-separation]]).

[F3] The actual corner and restricted-label classifier identifies the surviving Schur simple with the actual same-label Murphy simple; these are complete, distinct and absolutely simple over the original field ([[thm-schur-functor-sends-weyl-and-simple-modules-to-specht-and-hecke-simple-modules]]). The standard trace is symmetric and satisfies $\tau(T_u^*T_w)=\delta_{u,w}q^{\ell(w)}$ ([[lem-dj-bridge-murphy-cellular-basis-and-cell-form]]). Additive JM elements commute, centralize the preceding algebra, are star-fixed, and have triangular q-content spectra ([[thm-hecke-jucys-murphy-action-is-dominance-triangular]]).

[F4] At $q=1$, the actual group restriction endpoint is supplied by the initial restriction construction (the first seventeen phases) of [[thm-cogood-node-gives-the-simple-socle-and-head-of-residue-induction]], independently of its final composition-multiplicity argument. Its actual restricted-label convention is retained. No inverse of $q-1$ is specialized to zero.

## Proof

1.1 First construct the coefficient polynomials without specializing denominators. For $i<j$, $I=(i,j)$, $D\subseteq I$ and $p_D(t)=\max(\{i\}\cup\{d\in D:d<t\})$, define $$H(A,B)=\sum_{D\subseteq B\setminus A}(-1)^{|D|}\frac{\prod_{t\in A}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}{\prod_{t\in B}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}.$$ Cancel common $A\cap B$. For disjoint sets let $h=\min(A\cup B)$. If $h\in A$, multiply $H(A\setminus\{h\},B)$ by $x_h-x_i$. If $h\in B$, put $P=H(A,B\setminus\{h\})$; splitting the sum according to $h\in D$ gives $(P-P|_{x_i=x_h})/(x_h-x_i)$. Divided difference of $x_i^m$ is $-\sum_{a=0}^{m-1}x_i^ax_h^{m-1-a}$, so induction proves $H\in\mathbb Z[x]$ even at coincident coordinates. Also $H(\varnothing,B)=\delta_{B,\varnothing}$ by the same recursion. For $k\in A$, $h=\max([i,k)\setminus A)$, splitting $x_k-x_{p_D(k)}=(x_k-x_h)+(x_h-x_{p_D(k)})$ gives $$H(A,B)=(x_k-x_h)H(A\setminus\{k\},B)+\mathbf1_{h\ne i}H((A\setminus\{k\})\cup\{h\},B)+\mathbf1_{k\in B}H_{i,k}(A\cap(i,k),B\cap(i,k))H_{k,j}(A\cap(k,j),B\cap(k,j)).$$ Indeed $(h,k)\subseteq A$, so common factors there first cancel. The second difference is zero when $h\in D$; otherwise its predecessor equals $p_D(h)$, giving the second term. The extra $k\in D$ terms have negative sign and split independently at $k$, giving the positive third product after subtraction. This accounts for every summand. [given, algebra, construct]

2.1 Put $C=I\setminus A$ and $$K(A)=\sum_{B\subseteq I}H(A,B)\prod_{t\in\{i\}\cup B}(y_{t+1}-x_t).$$ Expansion of each $y_{t+1}-x_{p_D(t+1)}$ as $(y_{t+1}-x_t)+(x_t-x_{p_D(t+1)})$ gives $$K(A)=\sum_{D\subseteq C}(-1)^{|D|}\frac{\prod_{t=i}^{j-1}(y_{t+1}-x_{p_D(t+1)})}{\prod_{t\in C}(x_t-x_{p_D(t)})}.$$ Selected difference factors vanish when their index belongs to $D$; the remaining predecessor is $p_D(t)$, and collection gives exactly $H(A,B)$ by cancellation of the complement numerator/denominator sets. For a bijection $\theta:C\to B_0\subseteq I$ with $\theta(c)\ge c$, this implies the matched-product identity $$K(A)\equiv\prod_{t\in[i,j)\setminus B_0}(y_{t+1}-x_i)\pmod{(y_{\theta(c)+1}-x_c:c\in C)}.$$ Here is an integral induction proof. If $C=\varnothing$, the alternating expression is the asserted full product. Otherwise take $h=\min C$, remove its source and target, and apply induction on $(h,j)$. Splitting the alternating sum by $h\in D$ gives the prefix product times the divided difference of $K_{h,j}$ in $x_h$. The prefix is $\prod_{t=i}^{h-1}(y_{t+1}-x_i)$ and the divided difference is $(K_{h,j}|_{x_h=x_i}-K_{h,j})/(x_h-x_i)$. All remaining match generators are independent of $x_h$, so divided differencing retains their ideal. The induction product contains the target $\theta(h)$; modulo its match it vanishes before substitution, and after substitution its factor is $x_h-x_i$. Cancel that integral factor to leave exactly the tail product with this target removed. No target occurs below $h$, since targets are at least their sources. The prefix completes the stated product. All divisions here are polynomial divided differences, not evaluation of rational functions. [step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Suppose $q\ne1$ and temporarily extend $F$ to a field $K$ containing $v$ with $v^2=q$. Take $N>d$, $W=W_N(\lambda)$, $L=W/\operatorname{rad}B_\tau$, and $z=z_\lambda$. Use the actual normalized root operators $$\widehat F_{i,j}=v^{-j}K_jF_{i,j}K_iv^{-i},\quad c_{i,j}=\frac{v^{-2i-1}K_i^2-v^{-2j-1}K_j^2}{v-v^{-1}},\quad b_{i,j}=\frac{v^{-2i-1}K_i^2-v^{-2j-1}K_{j+1}^2}{v-v^{-1}}.$$ The denominator is a field unit since $q\ne1$. Define $S_{i,j}(A)=\sum_{B\subseteq(i,j)}\widehat F_{i,b_1}\cdots\widehat F_{b_s,j}H(A,B)$, evaluating $x_k=-v^{-2k-1}K_k^2/(v-v^{-1})$; use $S_{i,i}=1$. The recurrence of step 1.1 gives $$S_{i,j}(A)=S_{i,j}(A\setminus\{k\})c_{h,k}+\mathbf1_{h\ne i}S_{i,j}((A\setminus\{k\})\cup\{h\})+S_{i,k}(A\cap(i,k))S_{k,j}(A\cap(k,j)).$$ Left-interval toral coefficients commute with right-interval lower roots, so the product order is exactly as displayed. The empty-set operator is $\widehat F_{i,j}$. Rank-one commutation and the root recurrence give $E_i\widehat F_{i,i+1}=\widehat F_{i,i+1}E_i+vb_{i,i}$, $E_i\widehat F_{i,j}=v^{-1}\widehat F_{i,j}E_i-v\widehat F_{i+1,j}$ if $j>i+1$, and $E_{j-1}\widehat F_{i,j}=v\widehat F_{i,j}E_{j-1}+\widehat F_{i,j-1}$ if $i<j-1$. Disjoint indices commute; adjacent exterior indices contribute precisely their $K$-weight factors. These follow by induction inserting $F_{i,j}=vF_{i+1,j}F_i-F_iF_{i+1,j}$ into the simple defining commutators. [F1, step 1.1, algebra, construct]

3.1 Modulo the left ideal $UE_l$, the recurrence gives this complete table: $E_lS_{i,j}(A)=0$ if $l+1\in A$, or $l\notin\{i\}\cup A$ and $l+1\notin A\cup\{j\}$; it is $-vS_{i,l}(A\cap(i,l))S_{l+1,j}(A\cap(l+1,j))$ if $l\in\{i\}\cup A$, $l+1\notin A\cup\{j\}$; it is $S_{i,j-1}(A)$ if $l=j-1\notin\{i\}\cup A$; finally for $j-1\in A$, $h=\max([i,j-1)\setminus A)$ it is $S_{i,j-1}(A\setminus\{j-1\})b_{h,j-1}+\mathbf1_{h\ne i}S_{i,j-1}((A\setminus\{j-1\})\cup\{h\})$. To verify the table induct on $\sum_{a\in A}a$ using step 2.2. Empty $A$ is the elementary table. For $A=\{l+1\}$, $l>i$, expand again at $l$; the two cut remainders are $\widehat F_{i,l}\widehat F_{l+1,j}$ and its negative and cancel. At $l=i$ the cancellation is $b_{i,i}\widehat F_{i+1,j}=\widehat F_{i+1,j}c_{i,i+1}$. For any other $k\in A$ the recurrence splits into intervals, the remote factor commutes, and the other factor uses induction. For the split row reduce to $l=i$ by expanding at $k=l$; if the replacement is $i+1$ its remainder is the vanishing row, and otherwise all terms combine into the recurrence for $S_{i+1,j}$. The shortened-endpoint row uses the same induction at the other end. Expanding at $k=j-1$ gives the final row: $E_{j-1}\widehat F_{j-1,j}$ contributes $vb_{j-1,j-1}$, its weight factor is $v^{-1}$, and $c_{h,j-1}+b_{j-1,j-1}=b_{h,j-1}$. These exhaust all positions. Successive commutation through a cut product gives $E_i\cdots E_{j-1}\widehat F^Bz=v\prod_{t\in\{i\}\cup B}b_{t,t}(\lambda)z$. Summing proves $E_i\cdots E_{j-1}S_{i,j}(A)z=vK(A)(\lambda)z$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Evaluate $b_{i,k}(\lambda)=(q^{\lambda_i-i}-q^{\lambda_{k+1}-k})/(q-1)$ and $c_{i,k}(\lambda)=(q^{\lambda_i-i}-q^{\lambda_k-k})/(q-1)$. Let $B_{i,j}$ be the zero-$b$ set in $[i,j)$ and $C_{i,j}$ the zero-$c$ set in $(i,j)$. Their vanishing is exactly the corresponding equal-residue comparison. If $c_{i,k+1}=0$, then $b_{i,k}\ne0$, because $c_{i,k+1}-b_{i,k}=q^{\lambda_{k+1}-k-1}$ is a unit. Write $B\downarrow C$ for an injection of demands $B$ into supports $C$, sending each demand to a weakly smaller support. For $C\subseteq C_{i,j}$, we claim nonzero $S_{i,j}((i,j)\setminus C)z$ in $L$ implies $B_{i,j}\downarrow C$. Induct on $j-i$. At length one the elementary commutator and positive detection give equivalence with $b_{i,i}\ne0$. At larger length some $E_l$ has nonzero image: otherwise all positive divided powers kill this vector, since its deficit has each simple-root coefficient zero or one, so powers greater than one have absent target weights; triangular spanning would generate a proper second highest submodule of the simple $L$. Use the table of step 3.1. In the split case its disjoint factors commute, so both their vectors are nonzero; induction gives matchings on both intervals, and $c_{i,l+1}=0$, $b_{i,l}\ne0$ identify the split demands with the full ones. If $j-1\in C$, induction on the shortened interval matches old demands and the extra demand, when present, uses $j-1$. Otherwise put $h=\max(\{i\}\cup C)$ in the last table row. A nonzero first term has $b_{h,j-1}=b_{i,j-1}\ne0$, so there is no new demand. A nonzero second term matches old demands into $C\setminus\{h\}$; the released largest support $h$ handles the only possible new demand $j-1$. This proves the claim in every case. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 A primitive line of $L^1$ at $\lambda(i)$ exists exactly when $B_{i,N}\downarrow C_{i,N}$. For necessity, dualize a nonzero primitive map using [F1] to get $L^1\to\nabla(\lambda(i))$. Its highest coefficient functional on $W^1$ is nonzero at $\widehat F_{i,N}z$ and zero at all nonempty cut products, by [F1]'s exact coefficient test. On $S_{i,N}(A)z$, $A=(i,N)\setminus C_{i,N}$, its value is therefore that nonzero value times $H(A,\varnothing)(\lambda)=\prod_{t\in A}c_{i,t}(\lambda)\ne0$. This vector survives the radical, so step 4.1 gives necessity. Conversely choose a matching with image $C\subseteq C_{i,N}$ of size $|B_{i,N}|$ and invert it to a bijection $C\to B_{i,N}$ with targets at least their sources. The polynomial match generators in step 2.1 vanish, so $K((i,N)\setminus C)(\lambda)=\prod_{t\in[i,N)\setminus B_{i,N}}b_{i,t}(\lambda)\ne0$. Step 3.1 makes the resulting $w$ nonzero in $L$. Its lower-Levi commutators vanish in $L$: a potentially nonzero split has $l+1\in C$ and demands $B_{i,l}\cup B_{l+1,N}$. If its first factor survives, step 4.1 gives $|B_{i,l}|\le|C\cap(i,l)|$; since $|B_{i,N}|=|C|$ and $l+1\in C$, the suffix demands outnumber suffix supports, forcing the second factor into the radical. Higher divided generators have absent target weights. Thus $w$ is primitive. [F1] gives one-dimensionality and excludes every other primitive highest label. [F1, step 2.1, step 4.1, step 3.1, algebra]

6.1 Let $v_i$ be the nonzero primitive line for a matched removable row $i$ and $\Psi_i$ its cyclic lower-Schur module. If it has a proper nonzero submodule, that submodule contains a simple submodule, whose highest line is $v_h$ by [F1] and step 5.1. Triangular spanning puts its weight strictly below $\lambda(i)$, so $h<i$; equality would contain the generating line. The universal Weyl map onto $\Psi_i$ and [F2] force $\lambda(h)$ and $\lambda(i)$ to have the same residue multiset, hence $c_{h,i}=0$. Conversely suppose $h<i$ are matched and $c_{h,i}=0$. The demand/support sets split as $B_{h,N}=B_{h,i}\cup B_{i,N}$ and $C_{h,N}=C_{h,i}\cup\{i\}\cup C_{i,N}$. A prefix matching exists by restricting any matching for $h$ to demands below $i$; these cannot use later supports. Combine it with a matching for $i$, giving an image $C$ omitting $i$ with exactly $|B_{h,i}|$ earlier and $|B_{i,N}|$ later supports. Let $A=(h,N)\setminus C$, $k=\max(\{h\}\cup(C\cap(h,i)))$. Then $c_{k,i}=0$, and the recurrence at $i$ gives $$S_{h,N}(A)z=\mathbf1_{k\ne h}S_{h,N}((A\setminus\{i\})\cup\{k\})z+S_{h,i}(A\cap(h,i))S_{i,N}(A\cap(i,N))z.$$ When present, the first term replaces support $k<i$ by $i$; its earlier demands now outnumber earlier supports, so step 4.1 kills it in $L$. The other full and suffix vectors are the nonzero primitive vectors of step 5.1. Hence $v_h$ lies in $\Psi_i$ and generates a proper submodule. Thus $\Psi_i$ is simple precisely for the first matched row of its residue, and the full first-level socle is the multiplicity-free sum of these simples. [F1, F2, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 2.2, algebra]

7.1 The matching criterion is exactly the signature criterion. A zero $c_{i,k}$ records a later removable push of the same residue and a zero $b_{i,k}$ a later addable pop; nonremovable equal rows have both slots and cancel as a neutral pair. Removing common demand/support slots preserves the prefix inequalities for $B\downarrow C$. The remaining prefix inequalities say that the minus at row $i$ survives all later pops. Thus matched rows are normal, and the first matched row of a residue is its good row. Good deletion preserves restrictedness: its only possible new gap $e$ is above the deleted row, when $\lambda_{i-1}-\lambda_i=e-1$. The adjacent removable corners then have the same residue and no intervening same-residue boundary corner; if the lower minus survives, the upper minus survives too, contradicting first-surviving selection. The dual adjacent-plus argument proves cogood addition preserves restrictedness. Flipping the selected minus to plus changes no other $i$-boundary status, since all other changed corners have residues $i\pm1$, different from $i$ even at $e=2$. Before the first surviving minus the stack is empty, and every suffix prefix has at least as many remaining minus pushes as plus pops; after the flip these are exactly the conditions for the last surviving plus. Hence good deletion and cogood addition are inverse partial moves on restricted partitions. [step 4.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 Transfer the socle through the actual corner, using more than exactness. By steps 6.1 and 7.1 all Schur socle labels of $V=L_N(\lambda)^1$ are restricted; [F3] makes their lower corners nonzero simples. Therefore $V$ has no nonzero corner-killed submodule, since every nonzero finite-dimensional submodule contains a simple submodule. For a simple lower-Hecke injection $D\hookrightarrow e_{\rm lower}V$, the corner adjunction gives $Ae_{\rm lower}\otimes_{e_{\rm lower}Ae_{\rm lower}}D\to V$, whose image $I$ is generated by $D$ and satisfies $e_{\rm lower}I=D$. A proper nonzero submodule $J<I$ has corner zero or $D$. The first contradicts the no-killed-submodule property; the second contains $D$ and thus generates $I$. Hence $I$ is simple. Conversely every surviving Schur socle simple has simple corner by [F3]. These inclusions prove $\operatorname{soc}_H(e_{\rm lower}V)=e_{\rm lower}\operatorname{soc}_A(V)$, with multiplicities and actual labels. [F1]'s explicit corner identifies this with the actual Hecke restriction of $D(\lambda)$. [F1, F3, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 Define the primary functors over $F$. By [F3] the spectra of $L_d$ in the regular module are q-contents, hence among the distinct $[i]_q$, $i\in\mathbb Z/e$; cellular layers and their common triangular flags give this for the regular action as well. The polynomial CRT applied to $\prod_i(X-[i]_q)^{d!}$ gives mutually orthogonal projectors $P_{d,i}$, with sum one, in $F[L_d]$. They centralize $H_{d-1}$ and are star-fixed. Taking exponent $d!$ is sufficient for every Jordan block of the regular action and therefore every module; absent eigenvalues give the zero projector. On a good lower simple $D(\lambda\setminus A)$ in restriction, $L_d=\sum_{r\le d}L_r-\sum_{r<d}L_r$ has scalar $[c(A)]_q$: the symmetric sums have their actual cell scalars by [F2]. Thus its primary projector selects exactly the good residue. Every nonzero primary summand contains a simple socle submodule, so a summand with no good residue is zero. The form quotient makes $D(\lambda)$ star self-dual, and its star-fixed projector inherits a nondegenerate restricted form; its head is dual to its simple socle and has the same label. This proves the restriction endpoints when $q\ne1$. At $q=1$ use the actual group endpoint [F4], with the same primary construction and residue normalization. [F2, F3, F4, step 8.1, algebra]

10.1 Induction and restriction are biadjoint over $F$. Let $B=H_{d+1}$, $H=H_d$, and let $E_H:B\to H$ retain precisely the lower standard-basis terms. It is an $H$-bimodule map. For distinguished parabolic cosets $c,t$, the perfect trace gives $E_H(T_c^*T_t)=\delta_{c,t}q^{\ell(t)}$: pair against each lower basis element using $\tau_B(hE_H(a))=\tau_B(ha)$ and the disjoint length-additive coset supports. All diagonal values are units. Hence the relative Frobenius pairing gives $B\otimes_HX\cong\operatorname{Hom}_H(B,X)$ naturally. For a commuting primary projector $p=P_{d+1,i}$, cyclicity of the trace and commutation with $H$ give $E_H(pa)=E_H(ap)$, by pairing both expressions against the lower algebra's perfect trace. Under this identification right multiplication by $p$ corresponds to selecting $p$ on restriction. Ordinary tensor adjunction and this Frobenius adjunction therefore give both $\operatorname{Hom}_B(F_iX,Y)=\operatorname{Hom}_H(X,E_iY)$ and $\operatorname{Hom}_B(Y,F_iX)=\operatorname{Hom}_H(E_iY,X)$. [F3, step 9.1, algebra, construct]

11.1 For every simple label $\eta$ of rank $d+1$, step 9.1 and absolute simplicity give each of these Hom dimensions with $X=D(\mu)$, $Y=D(\eta)$ as one exactly when the good $i$-deletion of $\eta$ is $\mu$, and zero otherwise. Step 7.1 makes its inverse exactly the cogood addition, unique when defined. The complete cellular classifier [F3] covers every simple, so these two Hom tests identify respectively the simple head and simple socle of $F_iD(\mu)$, once each. If no cogood addition exists both tests vanish for every simple, forcing $F_iD(\mu)=0$ by finite dimensionality. At rank zero $H_0=H_1=F$, $L_1=0$, and the only move adds the residue-zero node; these formulas give $F_0F=F$ and every other summand zero. [F3, step 7.1, step 9.1, step 10.1, algebra]

12.1 Descend the temporary square-root extension. The Hecke modules, their cell forms, all primary projectors and functors above are defined over $F$. The cellular quotients of [F3] remain absolutely simple under $F\to K$, and finite-dimensional Hom spaces are kernels of finite linear systems over $F$, so their dimensions are unchanged by this field extension. Step 9.1's restriction and step 11.1's induction Hom tests therefore hold over $F$ itself and give exactly the stated endpoints. This argument also permits a purely inseparable square-root extension in characteristic two; no Galois averaging is used. The polynomial, integral module, actual corner and coefficient-functional constructions never divide a specialized Gram determinant. No primality condition on $e$ or Fock-space label classification entered the proof; no Choice principle is used. [F1, F3, step 9.1, step 11.1, algebra] ∎
