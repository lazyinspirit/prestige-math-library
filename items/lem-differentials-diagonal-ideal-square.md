## Statement

Let $A\to B$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings, let
$$\mu\colon B\otimes_AB\longrightarrow B,\qquad \mu(b\otimes b')=bb',$$
be the multiplication, and let $J=\ker\mu$. Then $J/J^2$ is a $B$-module through
$b\cdot x:=(b\otimes1)x=(1\otimes b)x$, and the map

$$\Omega_{B/A}\longrightarrow J/J^2,\qquad \mathrm db\longmapsto[\,1\otimes b-b\otimes1\,],$$

is an isomorphism of $B$-modules, natural in the ring map $A\to B$. Its inverse
sends the class of $1\otimes b-b\otimes1$ to $\mathrm db$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ring map $A\to B$, the ring $B\otimes_AB$ with multiplication $\mu$, and $J=\ker\mu$.

[F1] [[thm-kahler-differentials-existence-presentation]] and [[cor-derivations-represented-by-differentials]]: $\Omega_{B/A}$ exists and every $A$-derivation $D\colon B\to M$ into a $B$-module factors uniquely as $D=\alpha\circ\mathrm d$ with $\alpha$ $B$-linear.

[F2] [[thm-coproduct-property-of-tensor-products-of-commutative-algebras]] and [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]: $B\otimes_AB$ is the coproduct of the two $A$-algebras $B$, with the $A$-algebra maps $b\mapsto b\otimes1$ and $b'\mapsto1\otimes b'$, and $A$-bilinear maps on $B\times B$ correspond to $A-linear$ maps on $B\otimes_AB$.

[F3] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: an $A$-derivation is additive, $A$-constant and satisfies the Leibniz rule.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The class map is a derivation. Put $j_b:=1\otimes b-b\otimes1\in J$ and $D(b):=[j_b]\in J/J^2$. For $b,c\in B$, expansion in $B\otimes_AB$ gives $j_{bc}-[(b\otimes1)j_c+(c\otimes1)j_b]=j_bj_c\in J^2$, so $D(bc)=bD(c)+cD(b)$. The factors $b\otimes1$ and $1\otimes b$ act identically on $J/J^2$, since their difference lies in $J$ and $J(J/J^2)=0$. Also $D$ is additive and $D(a)=0$ for $a\in A$, since $1\otimes a=a\otimes1$. Hence $D$ is an $A$-derivation into the $B$-module $J/J^2$, and [F1] gives a unique $B$-linear $\alpha\colon\Omega_{B/A}\to J/J^2$ with $\alpha(\mathrm db)=D(b)$. [F1, F3]

2.1 $\alpha$ is surjective. Every $x=\sum_ib_i\otimes c_i\in J$ satisfies $\sum_ib_ic_i=0$, hence $x=\sum_i(b_i\otimes1)(1\otimes c_i-c_i\otimes1)=\sum_i(b_i\otimes1)j_{c_i}$ in $B\otimes_AB$, using $(b_i\otimes1)(c_i\otimes1)=b_ic_i\otimes1$ and $\sum_ib_ic_i\otimes1=0$. Thus $J$ is generated as a left $B$-module by the $j_c$, and $J/J^2$ is generated by their classes $D(c)$, which lie in the image of $\alpha$. Hence $\alpha$ is surjective. [step 1.1, F2]

3.1 A left inverse. The assignment $(b,c)\mapsto b\,\mathrm dc$ is $A$-bilinear, so [F2] defines an $A$-linear map $\Psi\colon B\otimes_AB\to\Omega_{B/A}$ with $\Psi(b\otimes c)=b\,\mathrm dc$. It is linear for the left $B$-action $a\cdot(b\otimes c)=(ab)\otimes c$. For $b,c\in B$, expand $j_bj_c=1\otimes bc-c\otimes b-b\otimes c+bc\otimes1$. Then $\Psi(j_bj_c)=\mathrm d(bc)-c\,\mathrm db-b\,\mathrm dc+bc\,\mathrm d1=0$ by the Leibniz rule. By step 2.1, $J$ is generated as a left $B$-module by the $j_b$, so $J^2$ is generated as a left $B$-module by their pairwise products; left $B$-linearity of $\Psi$ therefore gives $\Psi(J^2)=0$. Restricting $\Psi$ to $J$ and passing to the quotient gives a $B$-linear map $\beta\colon J/J^2\to\Omega_{B/A}$ with $\beta([j_b])=\mathrm db$. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 The maps are inverse. For $b\in B$ one has $\beta(\alpha(\mathrm db))=\Psi(j_b)=\Psi(1\otimes b-b\otimes1)=\mathrm db-b\,\mathrm d1=\mathrm db$. The differentials $\mathrm db$ generate $\Omega_{B/A}$, so $\beta\circ\alpha=\mathrm{id}$. Since $\alpha$ is surjective by step 2.1, it follows also that $\alpha\circ\beta=\mathrm{id}$. Thus $\alpha$ is an isomorphism. The formulas defining $j_b$ and $\Psi$ commute with maps of ring homomorphisms $A\to B$, so the isomorphism is natural. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
