## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, $R>0$, $x_0\in\mathbb R^n$, and let $L=a^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j+b^i\partial_i+c$ be uniformly elliptic on $B_R(x_0)$ with constants $\lambda,\Lambda$, $[A]_{0,\alpha;B_R(x_0)}\le K$, and $b,c\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_R(x_0))$ with finite global Hölder seminorms. Let $u\in C^2(B_R(x_0))\cap L^\infty(B_R(x_0))$ satisfy $Lu=f$ pointwise with $f\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_R(x_0))$. Then $u\in C^{2,\alpha}(B_{R/2}(x_0))$ and
$$\|u\|^{*}_{2,\alpha;B_{R/2}(x_0)}\le C\bigl(\|u\|_{\infty;B_R(x_0)}+R^2\|f\|_{\infty;B_R(x_0)}+R^{2+\alpha}[f]_{0,\alpha;B_R(x_0)}\bigr),$$
where $C$ depends only on $n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda$ and the finite dimensionless coefficient bounds $R^\alpha K+R\|b\|_\infty+R^{1+\alpha}[b]_{0,\alpha}+R^2\|c\|_\infty+R^{2+\alpha}[c]_{0,\alpha}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$, $R>0$, $x_0$, the operator $L$ with the stated bounds, $u\in C^2(B_R(x_0))\cap L^\infty$ and $f\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_R(x_0))$ with $Lu=f$ pointwise.

[A1] The only choice assumption is Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, used through the measure, potential and estimate interfaces below. No full Axiom of Choice is used. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] The scaled norms are $\|w\|^{*}_{2,\alpha;B_\rho}=\sum_{j=0}^2\rho^j\max_{|\beta|=j}\sup_{B_\rho}|D^\beta w|+\rho^{2+\alpha}\max_{|\beta|=2}[D^\beta w]_{0,\alpha;B_\rho}$ and $\|g\|^{*}_{0,\alpha;B_\rho}=\sup_{B_\rho}|g|+\rho^\alpha[g]_{0,\alpha;B_\rho}$; the scaling identity $\|w\circ\sigma_\rho\|^{*}_{2,\alpha;B_1}=\|w\|^{*}_{2,\alpha;B_\rho}$ holds for $\sigma_\rho(z)=x_0+\rho z$. ([[def-local-holder-and-c-two-alpha-norms-on-euclidean-balls]], [[def-holder-spaces-c-k-alpha-and-their-scaled-norms]])

[F2] The interior Poisson (Laplace) estimate: for $n\ge2$, $0<\alpha<1$ and $v\in C^2(B_\tau)\cap L^\infty(B_\tau)$ with $-\Delta v=g\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_\tau)$ pointwise, one has $\|v\|^{*}_{2,\alpha;B_{\tau/2}}\le C_{n,\alpha}(\|v\|_{\infty;B_\tau}+\tau^2\|g\|_{\infty;B_\tau}+\tau^{2+\alpha}[g]_{0,\alpha;B_\tau})$. ([[thm-interior-estimate-for-poisson-equation-with-holder-data]])

[F3] Uniform small-ball freezing ([[lem-freezing-coefficients-and-schauder-error-estimate]]): for every $\epsilon>0$ there are $\eta_\epsilon\in(0,1]$ and $C_\epsilon$, depending only on the dimensionless coefficient bounds and ellipticity, such that the freezing bound holds for every $0<r\le R\eta_\epsilon$ on a ball centered at the frozen point. For a recentered patch with center $x\in B_{3R/4}(x_0)$, apply that lemma on the ambient ball $B_{R/8}(x)$, which lies in $B_{7R/8}(x_0)$ and has dimensionless coefficient bounds no larger than the original ones; hence the same normalized cutoff gives the bound for every $0<r\le R\eta_\epsilon/8$ on that patch.

[F4] Linear normalization and ellipsoid-to-ball geometry. If $A_0$ is symmetric positive definite with spectrum in $[\lambda,\Lambda]$, put $S=A_0^{1/2}$ and $v(y)=w(x+Sy)$. Then $A_0:D_x^2w(x+Sy)=\Delta_yv(y)$, $\|S\|\le\sqrt\Lambda$ and $\|S^{-1}\|\le1/\sqrt\lambda$ ([[def-uniformly-elliptic-nondivergence-operator]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]]). For every $r>0$, $S(B_{r/\sqrt\Lambda})\subseteq B_r$ and $B_{\theta_{\rm geo}r}\subseteq S(B_{r/(2\sqrt\Lambda)})$ with $\theta_{\rm geo}:=\frac12\sqrt{\lambda/\Lambda}$. Consequently, for every $0<\theta\le\theta_{\rm geo}$, the Poisson estimate on $B_{r/\sqrt\Lambda}$ controls the original solution on $B_{\theta r}$, with norm-comparison constants depending only on $\lambda,\Lambda$.

[F5] Local $L^2$ Hessian bound for Newtonian potentials ([[lem-interior-w-two-p-regularity-for-the-laplacian]]): if $g\in L^2_c(\mathbb R^n)$ and $w=Ng$, then $w\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and its Hessian has the local estimate proved by Newtonian–Riesz representation. For $g$ supported in a ball $B_r$ with $\int g=0$, rescaling the fundamental solution and using the local Young bound for $N$ and $\nabla N$ gives, for every fixed $C_0>1$, $\|w\|_{L^2(B_{C_0r})}\le C r^2\|g\|_{L^2(B_r)}$; in dimension $2$ the logarithmic scaling term vanishes because $\int g=0$. Rescaling the local $W^{2,2}$ estimate on concentric balls then gives $\|D^2w\|_{L^2(B_r)}\le C\|g\|_{L^2(B_r)}$. The same conclusion for a frozen operator $A_0:D^2$ follows from [F4], with constants depending on $n,\lambda,\Lambda$. ([[lem-interior-w-two-p-regularity-for-the-laplacian]])

[F6] Harmonic excess decay. If $h$ is $A_0$-harmonic on $B_r(x)$, then for every sufficiently small $\theta=\theta(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,\theta_{\rm geo}]$,
$$\left(\frac{1}{|B_{\theta r}(x)|}\int_{B_{\theta r}(x)}|D^2h-(D^2h)_{B_{\theta r}(x)}|^2\right)^{1/2}\le C_0\theta\left(\frac{1}{|B_r(x)|}\int_{B_r(x)}|D^2h-(D^2h)_{B_r(x)}|^2\right)^{1/2}.$$
Indeed each component of $D^2h-(D^2h)_{B_r}$ is harmonic; transform by [F4], apply the interior derivative estimate for harmonic functions, and compare the contained and containing balls. Weakly harmonic components are smooth by [[cor-locally-integrable-weakly-harmonic-functions-are-smooth]], and the derivative bound is [[thm-interior-derivative-estimates-for-harmonic-functions]].

[F7] For $G\in L^1(B)$, write $(G)_B:=|B|^{-1}\int_BG$; this is the ball-average operator of [[def-ball-average-operator-on-r-n]]. In particular, $(G)_{B_r(x)}$ is constant in $x$ for each fixed ball and the volume ratio of concentric balls is $(r/s)^n$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Ellipsoid geometry for freezing. Fix $x\in B_{3R/4}(x_0)$, a radius $0<r\le R/8$ with $B_r(x)\subset B_{7R/8}(x_0)$, and set $A_0=A(x)$, $S=A_0^{1/2}$, and $\tau=r/\sqrt\Lambda$. For $v(y):=u(x+Sy)$, [F4] gives $\Delta v=(L_{x}u)\circ(x+S\cdot)$ on $B_\tau$, since $S(B_\tau)\subset B_r(x)$. Equivalently, $-\Delta v=-f\circ(x+S\cdot)+((L-L_x)u)\circ(x+S\cdot)$, so the estimate [F2] applies with this signed right-hand side. Fix any $0<\theta\le\theta_{\rm geo}$; the target ball $B_{\theta r}(x)$ lies in $x+S(B_{\tau/2})$. Thus [F2], restricted from this ellipsoid to the target ball, yields $\|u\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_{\theta r}(x)}\le C\Bigl(\|u\|_{\infty;B_r(x)}+r^2\|f\|_{\infty;B_r(x)}+r^{2+\alpha}[f]_{0,\alpha;B_r(x)}+r^2\|(L-L_x)u\|^*_{0,\alpha;B_r(x)}\Bigr),$ with $C=C(n,\alpha,\lambda,\Lambda)$. This estimate is conditional on the Hessian having a finite Hölder seminorm on the patch. The independent bootstrap below establishes that condition before its later quantitative use; $C^2$ alone does not establish it. [F1, F2, F4, given, algebra]

1.2 A noncircular Campanato bootstrap. Put $H=D^2u$ and $Q:=1+\|u\|_{C^2(\overline{B_{7R/8}(x_0)})}+\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_{7R/8})}+\|b\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_R)}+\|c\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(B_R)}$. For $x\in B_{3R/4}(x_0)$ and $0<r\le R/8$, let $E_x(r):=\left(\frac{1}{|B_r(x)|}\int_{B_r(x)}|H-(H)_{B_r(x)}|^2\right)^{1/2},\qquad g_x:=f-b\cdot Du-cu-(A-A(x)):D^2u.$ Then $A(x):D^2u=g_x$ pointwise. The product oscillation estimate, $[A]_{0,\alpha}\le K$, and the boundedness of $Du,D^2u$ on $\overline{B_{7R/8}}$ give $\operatorname{osc}_2(g_x;B_r(x))\le C K r^\alpha\bigl(E_x(r)+|(H)_{B_r(x)}|\bigr)+C_Qr^\alpha\le C r^\alpha E_x(r)+C_Qr^\alpha,$ where $\operatorname{osc}_2(q;B):=(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_B|q-(q)_B|^2)^{1/2}$; $C_Q$ is finite and may depend on the preliminary bound $Q$. No Hölder regularity of $D^2u$ is used here. [F1, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 Constant-coefficient replacement and excess decay. Let $m_x=(g_x)_{B_r(x)}$. Extend $g_x-m_x$ by zero from $B_r(x)$, transform the frozen operator by [F4], and take $w_x$ to be the negative of its Newtonian potential, so $A(x):D^2w_x=g_x-m_x$ because $N$ is normalized by $-\Delta Ng=g$. The datum has mean zero, so [F5] gives $\|D^2w_x\|_{L^2(B_r(x))}\le C\|g_x-m_x\|_{L^2(B_r(x))}$. Choose a quadratic $q_x$ with $A(x):D^2q_x=m_x$ and put $h_x=u-w_x-q_x$. Then $h_x$ is weakly $A(x)$-harmonic on $B_r(x)$; its Hessian is smooth there by [F6], and $D^2q_x$ is constant. Apply [F6] to $h_x$, use $E_x(r)\le E_{h_x}(r)+C\operatorname{osc}_2(g_x;B_r)$ and the volume ratio between $B_r$ and $B_{\theta r}$ to obtain $E_x(\theta r)\le C_0\theta E_x(r)+C_1\theta^{-n/2}\operatorname{osc}_2(g_x;B_r)\le qE_x(r)+C_Qr^\alpha.$ Choose $\theta\le\theta_{\rm geo}$ so $C_0\theta<\frac14\theta^\alpha$, then choose a uniform $r_0\le R/8$ so $C_1\theta^{-n/2}CKr_0^\alpha<\frac14\theta^\alpha$; thus $q<\frac12\theta^\alpha$. Iterating over $r_j=\theta^jr_0$ and using $E_x(r_0)\le2\|H\|_{L^\infty(B_{7R/8})}$ gives $E_x(r_j)\le C_Qr_j^\alpha$. For intermediate radii, $E_x(s)\le(r_j/s)^{n/2}E_x(r_j)$ when $r_{j+1}<s\le r_j$, so the same bound holds for every $0<s\le r_0$, uniformly for $x\in B_{3R/4}$. [step 1.2, F4, F5, F6, F7, A1, algebra]

3.1 Campanato embedding on the nested-patch region. Since $H$ is continuous, the means $(H)_{B_s(x)}$ converge to $H(x)$ as $s\downarrow0$. Telescoping the dyadic means and using step 2.1 gives $|H(x)-(H)_{B_s(x)}|\le C_Qs^\alpha$ for every $x\in B_{3R/4}$ and $0<s\le r_0$. If $x,y\in B_{5R/8}$ and $d:=|x-y|<r_0/8$, then $B_{2d}(x)$ and $B_{2d}(y)$ are both contained in $B_{4d}(x)$; comparing each of their means with $(H)_{B_{4d}(x)}$ costs only the fixed volume ratio $|B_{4d}|/|B_{2d}|=2^n$ and is bounded by $C E_x(4d)$. Since $4d<r_0/2$, the Step 2.1 excess estimate applies at this radius, and the telescoping estimates for $x$ and $y$ give $|H(x)-H(y)|\le C_Qd^\alpha$. For $d\ge r_0/8$, the preliminary bound $2\|H\|_\infty\le C_Qd^\alpha$ applies. Therefore $D^2u\in C^{0,\alpha}(B_{5R/8})$, in particular on $B_{R/2}$, before the quantitative Schauder estimate is invoked. The preliminary constant $C_Q$ is used only to prove finiteness. [step 2.1, F7, given, algebra]

4.1 A local finite-norm estimate with corrected nested radii. Now that the norm is finite, apply [F3] in step 1.1 with arbitrary small error $\epsilon$ to the term $(L-L_x)u$ on any recentered patch of radius $r\le R\eta_\epsilon/8$. Use part (i) of the Hölder interpolation lemma to bound the first-derivative term in the local scaled norm by an arbitrarily small multiple of $r^2\|D^2u\|_\infty+r^{2+\alpha}[D^2u]_{0,\alpha}$ plus $C_\epsilon\|u\|_\infty$. Thus, for any prescribed $\eta>0$, the choices of the freezing and interpolation parameters give $\|u\|^*_{2,\alpha;B_{\vartheta r}(x)}\le C_\eta\bigl(\|u\|_{\infty;B_r(x)}+r^2\|f\|_{\infty;B_r(x)}+r^{2+\alpha}[f]_{0,\alpha;B_r(x)}\bigr)+\eta\bigl(r^2\|D^2u\|_{\infty;B_r(x)}+r^{2+\alpha}[D^2u]_{0,\alpha;B_r(x)}\bigr).$ The constant $C_\eta$ may depend on the dimensionless coefficient bounds, ellipticity, and the chosen normalized patch radius; the small factor $\eta$ multiplies only the top-order terms after interpolation. [F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra, step 3.1]

5.1 Hole filling with interpolation of the separated-pair term. Write $H(s):=R^{2+\alpha}\max_{|\beta|=2}[D^\beta u]_{0,\alpha;B_s(x_0)}$, $M(s):=R^2\max_{|\beta|=2}\sup_{B_s(x_0)}|D^\beta u|$, and $S_R:=\|u\|_{\infty;B_R}+R^2\|f\|_{\infty;B_R}+R^{2+\alpha}[f]_{0,\alpha;B_R}$. Fix $\gamma\in(0,2^{-(2+\alpha)})$. For $R/2\le s<t\le5R/8$, put $r=(t-s)/2$, requiring $r\le R\eta_\epsilon/8$ as in step 4.1. Apply that estimate centered at every $x\in B_s(x_0)$, with one fixed target fraction $\vartheta>0$. If $x,y\in B_s$ and $|x-y|<\vartheta r/2$, the pair lies in the target ball centered at $x$, so dividing its local Hessian seminorm estimate by $r^{2+\alpha}$ bounds the corresponding quotient. For pairs with $|x-y|\ge\vartheta r/2$, use $|D^\beta u(x)-D^\beta u(y)|\le2\sup_{B_t}|D^\beta u|$. Thus, for $0<\eta\le1$, $$H(s)\le C_\eta(R/r)^{2+\alpha}S_R+C_0\eta H(t)+C_1(R/r)^\alpha M(t),$$ with $C_0,C_1$ fixed by $n,\alpha,\vartheta$; the last term includes both the local Hessian-sup error and the separated pairs. Part (ii) of the interpolation lemma on $B_t$, with $R/2\le t\le5R/8$, gives $M(t)\le\zeta H(t)+C(1+\zeta^{-2/\alpha})\|u\|_{\infty;B_t}$ for $0<\zeta\le1$: this quantitative dependence follows directly from its order-two difference estimate by choosing the normalized difference scale proportional to $\zeta^{1/\alpha}$. Choose $\eta$ with $C_0\eta\le\gamma/2$, then choose $\zeta=\min\{1,\gamma/(2C_1)\}(r/R)^\alpha$. The term containing $M(t)$ is at most $\gamma H(t)/2+C_\gamma(R/r)^{2+\alpha}S_R$. Consequently $$H(s)\le C_\gamma(R/r)^{2+\alpha}S_R+\gamma H(t).$$ After these choices, let $\delta_0=\min\{R/16,R\eta_\epsilon/4\}$, $\delta_j=\delta_0 2^{-j}$, $s_0=R/2$ and $s_{j+1}=s_j+\delta_j$. Then $r_j=\delta_j/2$ meets the local-radius restriction and $s_j\uparrow s_\infty\le5R/8$. Iteration has data series bounded by $C_\gamma(2R/\delta_0)^{2+\alpha}\sum_{j\ge0}(\gamma2^{2+\alpha})^jS_R$; its terminal term tends to zero because $H(s_j)\le H(5R/8)<\infty$ by step 3.1. Hence $H(R/2)\le CS_R$. Parts (ii) and (i) of the interpolation lemma, now at a fixed parameter on $B_{R/2}$, bound the scaled second- and first-derivative suprema by $C(H(R/2)+\|u\|_\infty)$. This proves the full scaled norm estimate, with constants depending only on the stated dimensionless bounds and not on the preliminary bootstrap constant $Q$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F1, F3, induction, algebra]

6.1 Conclusion. Step 3.1 establishes the claimed local regularity noncircularly, and step 5.1 proves the displayed quantitative estimate on the original half ball. The constant is uniform in $R$ when the stated dimensionless coefficient bounds are uniform. The strict range $0<\alpha<1$ enters through the Poisson estimate, the freezing lemma, and the Campanato iteration; no boundary condition is used. [step 3.1, step 5.1, given] ∎

## Remarks

- The two ingredients are exactly the constant-coefficient estimate for the frozen operator and the small-ball absorption of the coefficient oscillation; the lower-order coefficients are treated as data inside the freezing error.
- The dependence of the constant on $R$ enters only through the ratio $R/\rho$, where $\rho$ is determined by the dimensionless coefficient bounds; this is why those bounds are the natural parameters of the estimate.
