## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $H$ be a complex Hilbert space and let $M\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ be a unital $*$-subalgebra closed in the weak operator topology. Then $M''=M$, and $M$ is also closed in the strong operator topology. Consequently the concrete von Neumann algebras of [[def-von-neumann-algebra-and-commutant]] are exactly the unital $*$-subalgebras that are closed in either of these topologies. For an arbitrary set $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ one has $W^*(\mathcal S)''=W^*(\mathcal S)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a complex Hilbert space $H$, the bounded-operator space $\mathcal B(H)$, and either a unital $*$-subalgebra $M$ closed in WOT or a set $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$.

[F1] A concrete von Neumann algebra is a unital $*$-subalgebra closed in WOT; for any set $\mathcal A$, its commutant is WOT-closed, and if $\mathcal A$ is self-adjoint then $\mathcal A'$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra. The generated algebra $W^*(\mathcal S)$ is the WOT closure of the unital $*$-algebra generated by $\mathcal S$ ([[def-von-neumann-algebra-and-commutant]]).

[F2] SOT is initial for the maps $T\mapsto T\xi$ in norm, whereas WOT is initial for the scalar maps $T\mapsto \varphi(T\xi)$; hence SOT is finer than WOT ([[def-strong-and-weak-operator-topologies]]).

[F3] The finite Hilbert direct sum $H^{\oplus n}$ has norm $\|(\xi_j)\|^2=\sum_{j=1}^n\|\xi_j\|^2$, and its coordinate inclusions and projections are bounded ([[def-hilbert-direct-sum-of-unitary-representations]]).

[F4] Every closed linear subspace $C$ of a Hilbert space has a unique orthogonal decomposition $H^{\oplus n}=C\oplus C^\perp$; the orthogonal projection $P_C$ is the map selecting the $C$-component ([[thm-orthogonal-decomposition-by-a-closed-subspace]], [[def-hilbert-orthogonal-projection]]).

[F5] Every bounded operator has a unique Hilbert adjoint satisfying $\langle R\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,R^*\eta\rangle$, and adjoints respect composition; for a diagonal operator on $H^{\oplus n}$, the same identity on each coordinate gives $(R^{\oplus n})^*=(R^*)^{\oplus n}$ ([[def-hilbert-space-adjoint]], [[thm-hilbert-adjoint-properties]]).

[F6] The operator norm is the unit-ball supremum and satisfies $\|R\xi\|\le\|R\|\|\xi\|$ ([[def-operator-norm]]).

[F7] AC implies Countable Choice, which is the premise of the orthogonal-decomposition and Hilbert-adjoint suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct finite-block approximation.

**Given:** AC, $H$, and the algebra or set in the Statement.

1.1 Let $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ be any unital $*$-subalgebra and fix $T\in\mathcal A''$. Fix a finite tuple $\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n\in H$; if $n=0$ there is nothing to prove, so assume $n\ge1$. Put $K=H^{\oplus n}$ and $\xi=(\xi_1,\ldots,\xi_n)$, and define $D_R=R^{\oplus n}$ for $R\in\mathcal B(H)$. By [F3,F6], $\|D_R\zeta\|^2=\sum_{j=1}^n\|R\zeta_j\|^2\le\|R\|^2\|\zeta\|^2$, so $D_R$ is bounded; [F5] gives $D_R^*=D_{R^*}$. The orbit $\{D_R\xi:R\in\mathcal A\}$ is a linear subspace because $R\mapsto D_R\xi$ is linear, so its closure $C$ is a closed subspace. For each $R\in\mathcal A$, $D_R D_S\xi=D_{RS}\xi$ for $S\in\mathcal A$, so $D_R C\subseteq C$ by continuity of the bounded operator $D_R$; because $R^*\in\mathcal A$, the same argument gives $D_R^*C\subseteq C$. If $\zeta\in C^\perp$ and $c\in C$, then $\langle D_R\zeta,c\rangle=\langle\zeta,D_R^*c\rangle=0$, so $D_R C^\perp\subseteq C^\perp$. Let $P_C$ be the orthogonal projection from [F4]. Uniqueness of the orthogonal decomposition makes $P_C$ linear, and orthogonality gives $\|\zeta\|^2=\|P_C\zeta\|^2+\|\zeta-P_C\zeta\|^2$, so it is bounded. Since $D_R$ preserves both $C$ and $C^\perp$, it commutes with $P_C$. For coordinate inclusions $\iota_j:H\to K$ and projections $\pi_i:K\to H$, put $P_{ij}=\pi_iP_C\iota_j\in\mathcal B(H)$. Comparing the $(i,j)$ blocks of $P_CD_R=D_RP_C$ gives $P_{ij}R=RP_{ij}$; equality of all finite blocks is equality of the operators for every $R\in\mathcal A$, so $P_{ij}\in\mathcal A'$. The condition $T\in\mathcal A''$ gives $TP_{ij}=P_{ij}T$ for every $i,j$, hence $D_TP_C=P_CD_T$. Since $I_H\in\mathcal A$, $\xi=D_{I_H}\xi\in C$ and $P_C\xi=\xi$; therefore $D_T\xi=P_CD_T\xi\in C$. For any $\varepsilon>0$, the definition of $C$ supplies $R\in\mathcal A$ with $\|D_T\xi-D_R\xi\|<\varepsilon$, which yields $\|T\xi_j-R\xi_j\|<\varepsilon$ for every $j$. Thus one element of $\mathcal A$ approximates $T$ simultaneously on any prescribed finite tuple. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, construct]

2.1 If $M$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra closed in WOT and $T\in M''$, step 1.1 puts $T$ in the SOT closure of $M$, since its finite-tuple conclusion is exactly the SOT neighborhood test [F2]. WOT is coarser than SOT [F2], so a WOT-closed set is SOT-closed and $T\in M$. Conversely, $M\subseteq M''$ by the commutant definition [F1]. Hence $M''=M$, and $M$ is SOT-closed. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 If $\mathcal A$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra closed in SOT, then $\mathcal A\subseteq\mathcal A''$ and step 1.1 gives $\mathcal A''\subseteq\overline{\mathcal A}^{\,\mathrm{SOT}}=\mathcal A$. Thus $\mathcal A=\mathcal A''$. Since $\mathcal A$ is self-adjoint, $\mathcal A'$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra and is WOT-closed [F1]; its commutant $\mathcal A''$ is WOT-closed as well [F1]. Therefore $\mathcal A$ is WOT-closed, proving the reverse closure implication. [F1, step 1.1]

3.1 For any $\mathcal S\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$, let $\mathcal A=\operatorname{Alg}^*(\mathcal S\cup\{I_H\})$ and $W=\overline{\mathcal A}^{\,\mathrm{WOT}}=W^*(\mathcal S)$ [F1]. Step 1.1 and the SOT-to-WOT continuity in [F2] give $\mathcal A''\subseteq W$. On the other hand, $\mathcal A''$ is WOT-closed and contains $\mathcal A$ [F1], so the minimality of WOT closure gives $W\subseteq\mathcal A''$. Thus $W=\mathcal A''$; in particular $W$ is a unital $*$-subalgebra closed in WOT, and step 2.1 applied to $W$ gives $W''=W$. Therefore $W^*(\mathcal S)''=W^*(\mathcal S)$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎

## Source qualifications

Blackadar's I.9.1.1 explicitly labels its proof an outline: it reduces finite-tuple approximation to a one-vector orbit and cites I.2.5.4 for the tensor-block computation. The argument above writes the finite direct-sum block computation out. Bekka--de la Harpe state the closure/bicommutant equivalences in Theorem A.K.1 and refer its proof to Dixmier--von Neumann, Chapter I, §3, no. 4; their cited theorem is not treated as a proof here.
