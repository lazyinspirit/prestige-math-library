## Statement

Let $(X,\mathcal{A})$ and $(Y,\mathcal{B})$ be measurable spaces, let
$\mathcal{G} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(Y)$ generate $\mathcal{B}$, and let
$f : X \to Y$ be a function. If

$$f^{-1}(G) \in \mathcal{A} \qquad \text{for every } G \in \mathcal{G},$$

then $f$ is measurable as a map $(X,\mathcal{A}) \to (Y,\mathcal{B})$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Measurable spaces $(X,\mathcal{A})$ and $(Y,\mathcal{B})$, a function
$f : X \to Y$, and a generating family $\mathcal{G}$ for $\mathcal{B}$.

[L1] A function is measurable exactly when the preimage of every measurable set
in the codomain is measurable in the domain.
([[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let [L1, algebra]

$$\mathcal{C} := \{\, B \subseteq Y : f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{A} \,\}.$$

Preimages preserve complements and countable unions, so $\mathcal{C}$ is a
sigma-algebra on $Y$. [L1, algebra]

2.1 By hypothesis, every member of $\mathcal{G}$ lies in $\mathcal{C}$. Since [given, step 1.1]
$\mathcal{B}$ is the sigma-algebra generated by $\mathcal{G}$, one has
$\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{C}$. [given, step 1.1]

3.1 Therefore $f^{-1}(B) \in \mathcal{A}$ for every $B \in \mathcal{B}$, and [step 2.1, L1]
[L1] says exactly that $f$ is measurable. [step 2.1, L1] ∎
