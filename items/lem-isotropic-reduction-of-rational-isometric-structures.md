## Statement

Let $(W,B,T)$ be a rational isometric structure and let $L\subset W$ be a $T$-invariant totally isotropic subspace. Then the induced symmetric form and isometry on $Q=L^\perp/L$ make $Q$ a rational isometric structure, and $[W,B,T]=[Q,\overline B,\overline T]$ in the rational Witt group. The original structure is metabolic if and only if the quotient structure is metabolic. More generally, a rational isometric structure represents zero in that Witt group if and only if it is itself metabolic. These assertions use no choice principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite even-dimensional rational vector space $W$, a nonsingular symmetric bilinear form $B$, a $B$-isometry $T$ with $T$ and $I-T$ invertible, and a $T$-invariant isotropic subspace $L$.

[F1] A metabolic structure has an invariant isotropic subspace of half its dimension. The Witt group is generated by isometry classes, with direct sum giving addition and metabolic structures zero. [[def-integral-witt-and-seifert-isometric-structures]]

[A1] All spaces, bases, and sums selected in this argument are finite; no infinite choice is used.

## Proof

**Given:** The displayed finite-dimensional data.

1.1 Write $\dim W=2g$ and $\dim L=r$. Nonsingularity identifies $W$ with its dual, so $\dim L^\perp=2g-r$ and $(L^\perp)^\perp=L$. Thus the radical of $B|_{L^\perp}$ is exactly $L$, and $B$ induces a nonsingular symmetric form on $Q=L^\perp/L$, of dimension $2g-2r$. Since $T$ is an isometry and $T(L)=L$, it preserves $L^\perp$. Its restriction to each invariant finite-dimensional space $L$ and $L^\perp$ is invertible; the same holds for $I-T$, because its restrictions are injective and therefore surjective. Hence $\overline T$ and $I-\overline T$ are invertible on the quotient. This proves that the quotient is a rational isometric structure. [A1, F1, given, algebra]

2.1 In the orthogonal direct sum $W\oplus(-Q)$ take $P=\{(x,x+L):x\in L^\perp\}$. It is invariant, is isotropic because $B(x,y)-\overline B(x+L,y+L)=0$, and has dimension $2g-r$, exactly half the dimension of $W\oplus(-Q)$. Thus that sum is metabolic. Also any structure $A$ satisfies $A\oplus(-A)$ metabolic, using its diagonal invariant subspace. Therefore $[-A]=-[A]$, and the preceding metabolic graph proves $[W]=[Q]$. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.2 If $Q$ has an invariant isotropic half-dimensional subspace $N$, its inverse image in $L^\perp$ is invariant and isotropic in $W$ and has dimension $\dim N+r=(g-r)+r=g$. Thus metabolicity of $Q$ implies that of $W$. Conversely let $P\subset W$ be an invariant isotropic subspace of dimension $g$, so $P=P^\perp$. The pairing map $P\to L^*$ has annihilator in $L$ equal to $L\cap P^\perp=L\cap P$, hence rank $r-\dim(L\cap P)$. Its kernel is $P\cap L^\perp$. Consequently $\dim(P\cap L^\perp)=g-r+\dim(L\cap P)$. The subspace $((P\cap L^\perp)+L)/L$ of $Q$ is invariant and isotropic and has dimension $g-r$. It is therefore a metabolizer of $Q$. This proves the metabolicity equivalence. [F1, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Finally, a zero class in the group presentation of [F1] implies the existence of metabolic structures $M,N$ and an arbitrary structure $A$ with $W\oplus M\oplus A$ isometric to $N\oplus A$. To see this formally, first take the Grothendieck group of the direct-sum monoid. Being zero after imposing the metabolic relations says $[W]$ is a finite difference of sums of metabolic classes; the equality in the Grothendieck group means that one common monoid summand $A$ can be added to both sides. Append $-A$ to both sides. Since $A\oplus(-A)$ is metabolic by step 2.1, this yields an isometry $W\oplus M'\cong N'$ with both $M'$ and $N'$ metabolic. Let $L'$ be a metabolizer in $M'$, regarded as a subspace of $W\oplus M'$. Isotropic reduction by $L'$ is precisely $W$. By step 2.2, a reduction of the metabolic structure $N'$ is metabolic; hence $W$ is metabolic. The converse follows directly from its defining zero relation. All selections and sums in this argument are finite. [A1, F1, step 2.1, step 2.2, construct, algebra] ∎
