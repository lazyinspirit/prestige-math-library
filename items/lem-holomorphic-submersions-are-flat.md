## Statement

Assume AC. Let $f:X\to S$ be a holomorphic submersion of finite-dimensional complex manifolds. For every $x\in X$ the induced map of analytic local rings $\mathcal O_{S,f(x)}\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ is flat. Thus $\mathcal O_X$ is flat over $\mathcal O_S$ in the analytic local-ring sense. The empty source and zero-dimensional factors are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the holomorphic submersion in the Statement.

[F1] A holomorphic map with surjective differential is locally a projection in holomorphic coordinates ([[thm-holomorphic-implicit-function-theorem]]).

[F2] A germ regular in the last variable is a unit times a monic Weierstrass polynomial, and division by that polynomial has a unique finite-degree remainder ([[thm-weierstrass-preparation-theorem]], [[thm-weierstrass-division-theorem]]).

[F3] Completion of a Noetherian local ring is Noetherian and faithfully flat, while completion of any Noetherian ring at an ideal is flat ([[thm-completion-of-a-noetherian-local-ring]], [[thm-flatness-of-noetherian-completion]]).

[F4] A polynomial ring over a Noetherian ring is Noetherian ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use Weierstrass division to prove Noetherianity, then descend flatness from formal completions.

1.1 The ring $\mathbb C\{t_1,\ldots,t_m\}$ of convergent power-series germs is Noetherian, by induction on $m$. The case $m=0$ is a field. For a nonzero ideal $I$, choose $f\in I$ nonzero. Its first nonzero homogeneous term is a nonzero polynomial, so after an invertible complex linear change of variables it is nonzero on the last coordinate axis; $f$ is then regular in $t_m$. By [F2], $f=uW$ with $u$ a unit and $W\in I$ monic in $t_m$. Division by $W$ identifies the quotient by $(W)$ with the free module of rank $\deg W$ over $\mathbb C\{t_1,\ldots,t_{m-1}\}$. The image of $I$ in that quotient is finitely generated by the induction hypothesis. Lift finitely many generators to $I$; together with $W$ they generate $I$. Hence every ideal is finitely generated. [F2, given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 The maximal ideal of $A=\mathbb C\{t_1,\ldots,t_m\}$ consists of the series with zero constant term and is generated by the coordinates. Modulo its $n$th power a germ is exactly its polynomial of total degree less than $n$: Taylor expansion gives the decomposition, and every polynomial is convergent. Taking inverse limits gives $\widehat A\cong\mathbb C\llbracket t_1,\ldots,t_m\rrbracket$. The same argument for $B=\mathbb C\{t_1,\ldots,t_m,z_1,\ldots,z_q\}$ gives $\widehat B\cong\mathbb C\llbracket t_1,\ldots,t_m,z_1,\ldots,z_q\rrbracket$. By step 1.1 and [F3], both completions are Noetherian and $B\to\widehat B$ is faithfully flat. [F3, given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [F3], $A\to\widehat A$ is flat. By [F4], the polynomial ring $R=\widehat A[z_1,\ldots,z_q]$ is Noetherian and is a free, hence flat, $\widehat A$-module. Its $(z_1,\ldots,z_q)$-adic completion is $\widehat A\llbracket z_1,\ldots,z_q\rrbracket$, canonically the formal ring $\widehat B$ from step 2.1. By [F3] the completion is flat over $R$, so $\widehat B$ is flat over $\widehat A$ and hence over $A$. If a sequence of $A$-modules becomes nonexact after tensoring with $B$, it remains nonexact after the faithfully flat extension $B\to\widehat B$; but tensoring directly with $\widehat B$ is exact. Thus $B$ is flat over $A$. [F3, F4, given, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 By [F1], near any $x\in X$ there are holomorphic coordinates in which $f$ is projection $(t,z)\mapsto t$. The induced map on local rings is exactly $A\to B$ of step 3.1, up to coordinate isomorphism, and is flat. Since $x$ was arbitrary, $\mathcal O_X$ is flat over $\mathcal O_S$. When $q=0$ the map is an isomorphism; when $m=0$ the source local ring is $\mathbb C$; an empty source has no stalks to check. [F1, given, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
