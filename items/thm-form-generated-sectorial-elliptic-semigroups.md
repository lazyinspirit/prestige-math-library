## Statement

Assume Dependent Choice ([[def-dependent-choice]]) for the cited integral and semigroup suppliers.

The declared Dependent Choice assumption supplies equivalence of the shifted form norm with the prescribed norm on $V$, by [[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]].

Let $H$ and $V$ be complex Hilbert spaces with $V\subseteq H$ dense and continuously embedded. Choose any $\kappa>0$ satisfying $\|v\|_H\le\kappa\|v\|_V$ for every $v\in V$; when $H=\{0\}$ any positive $\kappa$ is admissible. Let $a$ be a closed sectorial form on $V$ with lower-bound constant $M\ge0$ and sector half-angle $\theta\in[0,\pi/2)$, with associated operator $A$ ([[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]]). Then $B:=A-MI$ is sectorial with vertex $0$ and every exponent $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$. It generates a bounded analytic semigroup $S$ on each such $\Sigma_\delta$, and
$$T(z):=e^{Mz}S(z)$$
is the analytic semigroup generated by $A$, satisfying $\|T(z)\|\le C_{\delta'}e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ on every smaller sector $\Sigma_{\delta'}$ with $\delta'<\delta$. Thus the form assumptions guarantee analyticity on every sector strictly narrower than $\pi/2-\theta$; they do not assert boundedness of the unshifted semigroup or that this lower angle is maximal. If the form is coercive with constant $\alpha>0$, then $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\alpha t/\kappa^2}$. In particular, for the symmetric Dirichlet form on a nonempty open $\Omega$ the associated operator is $\Delta_D$ and the abstract heat flow of [[cor-dirichlet-laplacian-generates-an-analytic-heat-semigroup]] is recovered. No symmetry is assumed. Dependent Choice is assumed for the semigroup suppliers; Countable Choice is inherited from the Lax-Milgram step; sectorial generation uses the declared Dependent Choice assumption.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Complex Hilbert spaces $V\subseteq H$ with dense continuous inclusion and a chosen positive embedding bound $\kappa>0$ satisfying $\|v\|_H\le\kappa\|v\|_V$; a closed sectorial form $a$ on $V$ with lower-bound constant $M\ge0$ and sector half-angle $\theta\in[0,\pi/2)$ in the sense of [L2], with associated operator $A$ defined by $\langle Au,v\rangle=-a(u,v)$ for all $v\in V$; the shifted operator $B:=A-MI$; and, in the coercive clause, a constant $\alpha>0$ with $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_V^2$ for all $u\in V$.

[L1] For a closed sectorial form with constants $M,\theta$ and associated operator $A$, using the chosen positive embedding bound $\kappa$: $A$ is closed and $D(A)$ is dense in $H$; $A$ is sectorial with vertex $M$ and every exponent $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$; $B=A-MI$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup $S$ on every sector $\Sigma_\delta$ with $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$; and $T(z)=e^{Mz}S(z)$ is the analytic semigroup generated by $A$, with $\|T(z)\|\le C_{\delta'}e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ on each $\Sigma_{\delta'}$ with $\delta'<\delta$. Dependent Choice is assumed for the semigroup suppliers; Countable Choice is inherited from the Lax-Milgram step ([[lem-coercive-sectorial-form-resolvents-define-a-closed-m-sectorial-operator]], [[def-countable-choice]]).

[L2] A closed sectorial form on $V\subseteq H$ is a bounded sesquilinear form admitting $M\ge0$, $\theta\in[0,\pi/2)$ with $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge-M\|u\|_H^2$ and $|\operatorname{Im}a(u,u)|\le\tan\theta\,(\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)+M\|u\|_H^2)$, and complete for the shifted form norm; its equivalence to the prescribed $V$ norm follows under the declared Dependent Choice assumption; its associated operator is defined by $\langle Au,v\rangle=-a(u,v)$ for every $v\in V$, and coercivity means $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_V^2$ ([[def-closed-sectorial-form-and-its-associated-operator]]).

[L3] A bounded analytic semigroup of angle $\delta$ is a strongly continuous-in-the-vertex, operator-norm holomorphic family on $\Sigma_\delta$ satisfying the functional equation and bounded on every strictly smaller sector; its angle is the supremum of the admissible $\delta$ ([[def-complex-sector-and-bounded-analytic-semigroup]]).

[L4] For the principal Dirichlet form $a_0$ on a nonempty open $\Omega$, the associated operator $A$ of the weak identity $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$ is densely defined and self-adjoint with $\langle Au,u\rangle=-a_0(u,u)\le0$, hence $A=\Delta_D$ and it generates a contraction analytic semigroup of maximal allowed angle $\pi/2$ ([[cor-dirichlet-laplacian-generates-an-analytic-heat-semigroup]]).

[L5] The contour semigroup is the unique strongly continuous semigroup with a given sectorial generator $A$ within the class of exponentially bounded semigroups ([[lem-generator-of-the-contour-semigroup-is-the-sectorial-operator]]).

[L6] If $a$ is coercive with constant $\alpha>0$ and $\kappa>0$ satisfies the chosen embedding bound $\|v\|_H\le\kappa\|v\|_V$, then $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\alpha t/\kappa^2}$ for $t\ge0$ ([[lem-coercive-sectorial-form-resolvents-define-a-closed-m-sectorial-operator]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The form-generated semigroup. By [L1], applied to the given closed sectorial form with constants $M,\theta$, the associated operator $A$ is closed and densely defined, is sectorial with vertex $M$ and every exponent $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$, and $B=A-MI$ generates a bounded analytic semigroup $S$ on every $\Sigma_\delta$ with $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$; moreover $B$ is sectorial with vertex $0$ and the same exponents, because $\lambda I-B=(\lambda+M)I-A$ for every $\lambda$, so $\lambda\in\rho(B)$ exactly when $\lambda+M\in\rho(A)$ with $R(\lambda,B)=R(\lambda+M,A)$ and the defining bound $\|R(\lambda+M,A)\|\le K_\varepsilon/|\lambda+M-M|$ becomes $\|R(\lambda,B)\|\le K_\varepsilon/|\lambda|$; finally $T(z)=e^{Mz}S(z)$ is the analytic semigroup generated by $A$ with $\|T(z)\|\le C_{\delta'}e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ on every smaller sector $\Sigma_{\delta'}$, $\delta'<\delta$, by [L1] and the definition of the analytic-semigroup angle in [L3]. [L1, L2, L3, given, algebra]

1.2 The coercive case. If $\operatorname{Re}a(u,u)\ge\alpha\|u\|_V^2$ with $\alpha>0$, then [L6] gives $\|T(t)\|\le e^{-\alpha t/\kappa^2}$ for every $t\ge0$ using the chosen positive embedding bound. If $H=\{0\}$, density forces $V=\{0\}$ and the semigroup has norm $0$, so the same estimate holds directly. [L6, L2, given]

1.3 The angle caveat. The assertion is that for every $\delta<\pi/2-\theta$ the shifted family $S$ is analytic and bounded on the sector $\Sigma_\delta$, and on each strictly smaller $\Sigma_{\delta'}$ the bound carries the factor $e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$; the definition [L3] asserts no family on $\Sigma_{\pi/2-\theta}$ itself and makes no maximality claim, and when $M>0$ the factor $e^{M\operatorname{Re}z}$ is unbounded on any sector, so the unshifted semigroup $T$ is not asserted to be bounded; only the shifted semigroup $S$ is bounded, and no symmetry of the form is assumed, so none of the stronger conclusions of [L4] applies to a general $a$. [L1, L2, L3, given]

2.1 The Dirichlet specialisation. For a nonempty open $\Omega$, the principal form $a_0(u,v)=\int_\Omega\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx$ is a closed sectorial form on $V=H^1_0(\Omega)\subseteq H=L^2(\Omega)$ with $M=0$ and $\theta=0$: it is bounded on $V$ by Cauchy-Schwarz, its real part is $\|Du\|_{L^2}^2\ge0$ with vanishing imaginary part, and the shifted form norm is the complete $H^1$ norm; the associated operator of [L2] is exactly the operator $A$ of [L4], since both are defined by $a_0(u,v)=-(Au,v)_{L^2}$; the abstract construction of [step 1.1] therefore produces a bounded analytic semigroup generated by this $A$, and [L4] identifies $A=\Delta_D$ and exhibits the contraction analytic heat semigroup generated by it; by [L5] the semigroup constructed here and the heat semigroup of [L4] are the same exponentially bounded semigroup with generator $A$, so the abstract heat flow is recovered, and no elliptic regularity or domain identification beyond [L4] is used. [step 1.1, L2, L4, L5, given, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

Dependent Choice is assumed for the semigroup suppliers; Countable Choice is inherited from the Lax-Milgram step of [L1] and from the vocabulary of [L2]; the rescalings and Euler exponentials of steps 1.1-2.1 use no choice principle. The theorem is a consolidation of the closed-form resolvent lemma [L1] with the Dirichlet specialisation of [L4]; no spatial domain identification is asserted for a general form, that role being reserved for the elliptic-regularity results cited in [L4].
