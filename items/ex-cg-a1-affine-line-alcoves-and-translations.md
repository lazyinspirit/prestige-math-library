## Example

Let $E=\mathbb R$ with $B(x,y)=xy$, and let $\Phi=\{\alpha,-\alpha\}$ with $\alpha=1$. Then $\alpha^\vee=2$, $W=\{1,s_\alpha\}$, $Q=\mathbb Z$, and $Q^\vee=2\mathbb Z$. The affine walls are the integer points, and the alcoves are the intervals $A_n=(n,n+1)$, $n\in\mathbb Z$. The affine reflections are $r_{\alpha,k}(x)=2k-x$, so $W_a=\{x\mapsto x+2m,\ x\mapsto -x+2m:m\in\mathbb Z\}$.

The fundamental alcove is $A=A_0=(0,1)$, with facet reflections $s_0(x)=-x$ and $s_1(x)=2-x$. These reflections act simply transitively on the alcoves; $s_1s_0(x)=x+2$, and the stabilizer of $A$ is trivial. The two facets have the same type when read from either adjacent alcove.

For $g(A)=A_n$, the word length $\ell(g)$ in $s_0,s_1$ equals
$$\ell(g)=|\operatorname{Sep}(A,g(A))|=|n|.$$
The presentation is the infinite dihedral presentation $\langle s_0,s_1\mid s_0^2=s_1^2=1\rangle$ with $m_{01}=\infty$. Under the alternative convention $H^\vee_{\alpha,k}=\{x:B(x,\alpha^\vee)=k\}$, the translations are by $Q=\mathbb Z$ rather than $Q^\vee=2\mathbb Z$; the two conventions must not be mixed.

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct coordinate and gallery calculations.

**Given:** $E=\mathbb R$, $B(x,y)=xy$, $\Phi=\{1,-1\}$, and the affine notation above.

[F1] A reduced crystallographic root system is finite, spans its ambient space, is preserved by its root reflections, has integral Cartan integers, and has only the two signs on each root line ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[F2] The affine walls are $H_{\alpha,k}=\{x:B(x,\alpha)=k\}$, and $W_a$ is generated by their reflections ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]]).

[F3] For this convention, $W_a=Q^\vee\rtimes W$ and $r_{\alpha,k}=t_{k\alpha^\vee}s_\alpha$ ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F4] For an $A_1$ component the fundamental alcove is $0<B(x,\alpha)<1$, with its two facet walls at levels $0$ and $1$ ([[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]]).

[F5] A facet reflection separates its adjacent alcoves by exactly that wall, and the fundamental-alcove stabilizer is trivial ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F6] The coroot is $\alpha^\vee=2\alpha/B(\alpha,\alpha)$, and the coroot of a dual root is the original root ([[def-coroot-and-dual-root-system]]).

[F7] The Weyl group is generated by the orthogonal root reflections ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]]).

[F8] The root and coroot lattices are $Q=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb Z\alpha$ and $Q^\vee=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb Z\alpha^\vee$ ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[F9] The affine reflection group is generated by the maps $r_{\alpha,k}$ ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]]).

[F10] An alcove is a connected component of the complement of the affine walls ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]]).

[F11] For any supplied finite reduced crystallographic root system, the map $\psi:Q^\vee\rtimes W\to\mathrm{Isom}(E)$ has image $W_a$, so its affine translation subgroup is its coroot lattice ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F12] Adjacent alcoves in $W_a\cdot A$ read the type of their shared facet identically ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F13] The coroot set $\Phi^\vee$ is itself reduced crystallographic with the same reflections $s_{\alpha^\vee}=s_\alpha$ ([[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

1.1 The set $\Phi=\{1,-1\}$ is finite, spans $\mathbb R$, omits $0$, and meets the line $\mathbb R\alpha$ only in its two signs. The reflection in $0$ is $s_\alpha(x)=-x$, which exchanges the two roots, and the Cartan integers are $\pm2$. Thus $\Phi$ is a reduced crystallographic root system; its Weyl group is $\{1,s_\alpha\}$ by [F7]. [F1, F7, algebra]

1.2 For $\alpha=1$, $H_{\alpha,k}=\{k\}$, while $H_{-\alpha,k}=\{-k\}$; their union is $\mathbb Z$. By [F10], alcoves are the connected components of $\mathbb R\setminus\mathbb Z$, exactly the open intervals $A_n=(n,n+1)$: each such interval is connected and contains no integer, and any connected set meeting two of them would contain an intervening integer. [F2, F10, algebra]

1.3 By [F3] and [F6], $\alpha^\vee=2$ and the affine reflection is $r_{\alpha,k}(x)=x-(x-k)2=2k-x$. Composing two such reflections gives $x\mapsto x+2(k-l)$, so all translations by $2m$ occur. Every word is a composition of maps $x\mapsto 2k-x$; pairing consecutive factors shows it is either an even translation or a reflection of the same form. Conversely, $r_{\alpha,m}=(x\mapsto -x+2m)$ and $r_{\alpha,m}r_{\alpha,0}=(x\mapsto x+2m)$, so [F9] gives exactly $W_a=\{x\mapsto x+2m,\ x\mapsto -x+2m:m\in\mathbb Z\}$; its translation subgroup is $2\mathbb Z=Q^\vee$. Also $Q=\mathbb Z$ by [F8]. [F3, F6, F8, F9, algebra]

2.1 The item [[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]] gives the fundamental factor $A=(0,1)$, with facet walls $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$. Their reflections are $s_0(x)=-x$ and $s_1(x)=2-x$, and $s_1s_0(x)=x+2$. [F4, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 Every element has one of the two forms $x\mapsto x+2m$ or $x\mapsto -x+2m$. The first sends $A$ to $(2m,2m+1)$, while the second sends it to $(2m-1,2m)$. These intervals are all distinct and exhaust the $A_n$, so each alcove is the image of $A$ under exactly one element; hence the action is simply transitive and $\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a}(A)=\{1\}$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, algebra]

3.1 The left and right facets of $A_0$ have types $0$ and $1$, respectively, and $s_0,s_1$ exchange $A_0$ with $A_{-1},A_1$. By step 2.2 every interval is uniquely $g(A_0)$; applying $g$ to these two adjacencies gives every adjacent pair, since the maps $x\mapsto x+2m$ and $x\mapsto -x+2m$ send their shared endpoints through all integers. Thus right multiplication by a facet generator moves the interval index by $+1$ or $-1$, and [F12] guarantees that the shared endpoint has the same type from either interval. By [F5], each move crosses exactly its shared wall. [F4, F5, F12, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 The relations $s_0^2=s_1^2=1$ reduce every word to an alternating word. Every nonempty alternating word of even length $2q$ is a nonzero translation by $2q$ or $-2q$; every alternating word of odd length is a reflection with slope $-1$. Hence no nonempty reduced alternating word is the identity, the only defining relations are the two involution relations, and $(s_1s_0)^q=t_{2q}\ne1$ for $q>0$. Thus $m_{01}=\infty$ and $W_a\cong\langle s_0,s_1\mid s_0^2=s_1^2=1\rangle$. [step 1.3, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 If $g(A)=A_n$, the walls separating $A_0$ and $A_n$ are the integer points between their intervals, and there are exactly $|n|$ of them: for $n>0$ they are $1,\ldots,n$, for $n<0$ they are $n+1,\ldots,0$, and for $n=0$ there are none. A word of length $m$ gives a gallery of $m$ adjacent intervals by step 3.1, so it must cross each separating wall and $m\ge |n|$. For $q\ge0$, the maps $t_{2q}=(s_1s_0)^q$ and $t_{-2q}=(s_0s_1)^q$ send $A_0$ to $A_{2q}$ and $A_{-2q}$; the maps $t_{2q}s_1$ and $t_{-2q}s_0$ send it to $A_{2q+1}$ and $A_{-(2q+1)}$, respectively. These words have lengths equal to the absolute values of their indices, so the lower bound is attained for every $n$ and $\ell(g)=|\operatorname{Sep}(A,g(A))|=|n|$. [step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The standard root lattice is $Q=\mathbb Z$ and the standard coroot translation lattice is $Q^\vee=2\mathbb Z$ by step 1.3. Under the dual-normal convention, the walls are $2x=k$; the coroot of $\alpha^\vee$ is $\alpha$, so reflection in $k/2$ is $x\mapsto k-x$, and composing the reflections at $0$ and $1/2$ gives translation by $1$. By [F13], $\Phi^\vee$ meets the affine lemma's root-system hypotheses; applying [F11] to it gives translation subgroup $Q^\vee(\Phi^\vee)=Q=\mathbb Z$ by [F6, F8]. Thus the alternative convention has translations by $Q$, with the two cosets $2\mathbb Z$ and $1+2\mathbb Z$ showing $[Q:Q^\vee]=2$. All computations use finite root lists and explicit integer arithmetic; no axiom of choice is used. [F6, F8, F11, F13, step 1.3, algebra] ∎
