## Statement

Let $\mathfrak g_0$ be a finite-dimensional real Lie algebra with
complexification $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak g_0\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$,
real embedding $\varepsilon$ and bracket $[X\otimes z,Y\otimes w]=[X,Y]\otimes zw$
([[def-complexification-of-a-real-lie-algebra]]). Then the following hold.

1. The bracket is well-defined and makes $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ a complex Lie
   algebra, and $\varepsilon$ is an injective real Lie-algebra homomorphism.
2. The assignment $\sigma(X\otimes z)=X\otimes\overline z$, extended by
   $\sigma(Z_1+Z_2)=\sigma(Z_1)+\sigma(Z_2)$, is a well-defined
   conjugate-linear involution of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$, and
   $\sigma([Z,W])=[\sigma Z,\sigma W]$ for all $Z,W$.
3. The fixed locus $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}^{\sigma}=\{Z:\sigma Z=Z\}$ equals
   $\varepsilon(\mathfrak g_0)$, so that $\varepsilon(\mathfrak g_0)$ is a real Lie
   subalgebra of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ naturally identified with
   $\mathfrak g_0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional real Lie algebra $\mathfrak g_0$, its real tensor product $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}=\mathfrak g_0\otimes_{\mathbb R}\mathbb C$, and the assignment $\theta(X\otimes z)=X\otimes\overline z$ on pure tensors, extended additively to $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$.

[L1] $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ is the free $\mathbb Z$-module on $\mathfrak g_0\times\mathbb C$ modulo the subgroup generated by the relations $(X+X',z)-(X,z)-(X',z)$, $(X,z+z')-(X,z)-(X,z')$, $(cX,z)-(X,cz)$ and $(X,cz)-(cX,z)$ for $c\in\mathbb R$, and the elementary tensors generate it additively ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]).

[L2] By the universal property of the tensor product, for every real vector space $U$ and every $\mathbb R$-bilinear map $\beta\colon\mathfrak g_0\times\mathbb C\to U$ there is a unique $\mathbb R$-linear $\widetilde\beta\colon\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to U$ with $\widetilde\beta(X\otimes z)=\beta(X,z)$. The additive factorization is [[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]]; real linearity follows by checking $\widetilde\beta(c(X\otimes z))=\beta(X,cz)=c\beta(X,z)$ on elementary tensors.

[L3] $\mathfrak g_0$ is a real Lie algebra with bilinear, alternating bracket satisfying the Jacobi identity, and scalar multiplication by $\mathbb C$ transports to $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ by $t(X\otimes z)=X\otimes tz$ ([[def-lie-algebra-over-a-field]], [[def-complexification-of-a-real-lie-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The bracket formula of [L3] is $\mathbb R$-bilinear in the pair $(X\otimes z,Y\otimes w)$ and vanishes on each tensor-product relation in either slot, since $[X+X',Y]\otimes zw=[X,Y]\otimes zw+[X',Y]\otimes zw$ and $[cX,Y]\otimes zw=c\,[X,Y]\otimes zw$ for $c\in\mathbb R$; hence [L2] gives a well-defined $\mathbb R$-bilinear map $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\times\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ with the stated values on pure tensors. It is complex-bilinear because $[(tZ),W]=t[Z,W]$ and $[Z,tW]=t[Z,W]$, both checked on pure tensors and extended by $\mathbb R$-linearity in each argument. [L1, L2, L3, algebra]

1.2 The assignment $\theta$ is additive on pure tensors and vanishes on every relation of [L1], since $(X+X')\otimes\overline z=X\otimes\overline z+X'\otimes\overline z$, $X\otimes\overline{z+z'}=X\otimes\overline z+X\otimes\overline{z'}$, and conjugation is $\mathbb R$-linear, so that the relation $(X,cz)-(cX,z)$ for $c\in\mathbb R$ becomes $X\otimes c\overline z-cX\otimes\overline z=0$; by [L1] and the additive generation of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ by elementary tensors, $\theta$ extends uniquely to a well-defined additive map $\sigma\colon\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ with $\sigma(X\otimes z)=X\otimes\overline z$. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 For pure tensors $u=X\otimes z$, $v=Y\otimes w$, alternation in $\mathfrak g_0$ implies $[X,Y]=-[Y,X]$ by expansion of $[X+Y,X+Y]=0$. Thus $[u,v]+[v,u]=([X,Y]+[Y,X])\otimes zw=0$ and $[u,u]=0$. For an arbitrary finite tensor sum $Z=\sum_a u_a$, bilinearity now gives $[Z,Z]=\sum_a[u_a,u_a]+\sum_{a<b}([u_a,u_b]+[u_b,u_a])=0$. Jacobi, unlike alternation, is trilinear: on three pure tensors it is the Jacobi expression in $\mathfrak g_0$ tensored with the product of their scalars, hence zero, and trilinearity extends it to arbitrary finite sums. This proves the complex Lie-algebra axioms. [L1, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 $\sigma$ is conjugate-linear: it is additive by construction, and $\sigma(t(X\otimes z))=X\otimes\overline{tz}=\overline t\,(X\otimes\overline z)=\overline t\,\sigma(X\otimes z)$ for complex $t$; both sides are additive in the argument, so the identity extends to all of $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$. It is involutive because $\sigma^2(X\otimes z)=X\otimes z$ on pure tensors and $\sigma^2$ is additive. Finally $\sigma$ preserves brackets: on pure tensors $\sigma([X\otimes z,Y\otimes w])=[X,Y]\otimes\overline{zw}=[X\otimes\overline z,Y\otimes\overline w]=[\sigma(X\otimes z),\sigma(Y\otimes w)]$, and since both sides are additive and the bracket and $\sigma$ are additive, the identity extends to all pairs. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Choose a real basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$ of $\mathfrak g_0$. Every tensor has an expression $Z=\sum_j e_j\otimes z_j$: expand each real factor of a finite sum of pure tensors in this basis and use the tensor relations. This expression is unique, because for the dual basis functional $e_j^*$ the bilinear map $(Y,z)\mapsto e_j^*(Y)z$ induces by [L2] a real-linear map $\varphi_j:\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C$ with $\varphi_j(Z)=z_j$. Consequently $\sigma Z=Z$ if and only if $\overline{z_j}=z_j$ for every $j$, equivalently $z_j\in\mathbb R$ for every $j$. In that case $Z=\sum_j e_j\otimes z_j=(\sum_jz_je_j)\otimes1=\varepsilon(\sum_jz_je_j)$, while every element of $\varepsilon(\mathfrak g_0)$ is visibly fixed. Thus the fixed locus is exactly $\varepsilon(\mathfrak g_0)$. [L1, L2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 $\varepsilon$ is injective: if $X\ne0$, extend $X$ to an $\mathbb R$-basis of $\mathfrak g_0$ and let $\lambda$ be the dual basis functional with $\lambda(X)=1$; the $\mathbb R$-bilinear map $(Y,z)\mapsto\lambda(Y)z$ induces by [L2] an $\mathbb R$-linear $\varphi\colon\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}\to\mathbb C$ with $\varphi(Y\otimes z)=\lambda(Y)z$, so $\varphi(X\otimes1)=1$ and $X\otimes1\ne0$. Moreover $\varepsilon([X,Y])=[X,Y]\otimes1=[X\otimes1,Y\otimes1]=[\varepsilon X,\varepsilon Y]$ by the bracket formula, so $\varepsilon$ is an injective real Lie-algebra homomorphism. Together with step 3.1 this identifies $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}^\sigma$ with $\mathfrak g_0$ as a real Lie subalgebra. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
