## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $C$ be a smooth, proper, geometrically connected curve over a field $k$ and let $d\ge0$. The functor of effective relative Cartier divisors on $C$ whose geometric fibers have degree $d$ is represented by a projective $k$-scheme $C^{(d)}$ of dimension $d$. The sum-of-points morphism $C^d\to C^{(d)}$ is finite and surjective, so $C^{(d)}$ is geometrically irreducible; $C^{(0)}=\operatorname{Spec}k$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a smooth proper geometrically connected curve $C/k$, and an integer $d\ge0$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice also gives Dependent Choice: for a serial relation on a nonempty set, choose one successor for each element and iterate the resulting function from the prescribed starting point. [given]

[F1] A smooth proper curve is projective, and for a projective scheme the Hilbert scheme represents flat projective families with fixed Hilbert polynomial and is projective over the base. [[cor-projective-embedding-every-smooth-proper-curve]] [[thm-hilbert-scheme-represents-projective-flat-families]]

[F2] In a smooth curve every local ring at a closed point is a discrete valuation ring, so every ideal of finite colength in that local ring is principal. In a flat Hilbert family the locus where the universal ideal cuts out a relative effective Cartier divisor is open. [[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]] [[lem-arith-hilbert-divisor-charts-and-picard-diagonal]]

[F3] A finite subscheme of a curve of length $d$ has constant Hilbert polynomial $d$ for any ample line bundle, and the length equals the sum of its geometric point multiplicities. [[thm-hilbert-scheme-represents-projective-flat-families]]

[F4] A proper quasi-finite morphism is finite, finite integral extensions preserve dimension, and dimensions add under products of varieties over the algebraically closed field. [[thm-proper-quasi-finite-is-finite]] [[cor-dimension-preserved-by-integral-extensions]] [[thm-dimension-product-varieties]]

[F5] A smooth connected curve over an algebraically closed field is integral: smoothness makes its local rings regular, regular local rings are domains, so distinct irreducible components cannot meet; connectedness leaves only one component. [[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]] [[thm-regular-local-rings-are-domains-and-cohen-macaulay]]

## Proof

**Given:** $C$, $k$, $d$, and the hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 By [F1], choose a projective embedding of $C$. The Hilbert scheme of closed subschemes with Hilbert polynomial $d$ is projective over $k$ and represents flat families of finite subschemes whose geometric fibers have length $d$, by [F3]. Denote this Hilbert scheme by $H_d(C)$. [A1, F1, F3, given, construct]

2.1 Every geometric fiber $Z$ of the universal family over $H_d(C)$ is an effective Cartier divisor on the smooth curve $C_{\bar k}$: at each support point its ideal is a finite-colength ideal in a discrete valuation ring, hence generated by a nonzero uniformizer power, and away from its support the ideal is the unit ideal. Therefore every geometric point of $H_d(C)$ lies in the open relative-Cartier locus of [F2]. An open subscheme containing every point is the whole scheme, so the universal family on $C\times H_d(C)$ is a relative effective Cartier divisor. Conversely, an effective relative Cartier divisor with fibers of degree $d$ is finite flat of degree $d$ over its base and hence is exactly a family represented by $H_d(C)$. Thus $H_d(C)$ represents the stated divisor functor; set $C^{(d)}=H_d(C)$. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 If $d=0$, the only effective divisor is empty, so $C^{(0)}=\operatorname{Spec}k$ and it has dimension zero; the sum-of-points map $C^0\to C^{(0)}$ is the identity, hence finite and surjective. Suppose $d>0$ and extend scalars to $\bar k$. The $d$ graph divisors on $C_{\bar k}\times C_{\bar k}^{\,d}$ add to an effective relative Cartier divisor of degree $d$; by the representing property in step 2.1 they define the sum-of-points morphism $\sigma:C_{\bar k}^{\,d}\to C^{(d)}_{\bar k}$. Every effective divisor of degree $d$ over the algebraically closed field is a sum of $d$ geometric points counted with multiplicity, so $\sigma$ is surjective on geometric points and hence on the scheme. Each fiber has only the finitely many orderings of the finite support of its divisor, so $\sigma$ is quasi-finite; it is proper because its source is projective and its target separated. By [F4], $\sigma$ is finite. Its source is irreducible by [F5], so its image $C^{(d)}_{\bar k}$ is irreducible; finite surjectivity gives $\dim C^{(d)}=\dim C_{\bar k}^{\,d}=d$ by [F4]. Projectivity follows from step 1.1. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 2.1, algebra, construct] ∎

## Axiom use

The Axiom of Choice supplies Dependent Choice by [A1] and is used through [F1] in the projective Hilbert-scheme and curve-projectivity suppliers. The finite pointwise DVR check in step 2.1 and the finite product construction in step 3.1 require no further choice.
