## Definition

Fix an integer $m\ge1$. The **doubled line quiver** $\Gamma_m$ is the finite
quiver of [[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]] with vertices
$0,1,\dots,m$ and, for every $0\le i\le m-1$, exactly two arrows between the
consecutive vertices $i$ and $i+1$, one in each direction. Its directed paths of
length $l$ are therefore the tuples
$$(i_1|i_2|\cdots|i_{l+1}),\qquad i_j\in\{0,1,\dots,m\},\quad |i_{j+1}-i_j|=1,$$
and the product of the path ring $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ is the left-to-right
concatenation of [[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]].

**The algebra.** For $m\ge1$, let $I_m\trianglelefteq\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ be the
two-sided ideal generated by the elements
$$(i-1|i|i+1),\qquad (i+1|i|i-1),\qquad (i|i+1|i)-(i|i-1|i),\qquad (0|1|0),$$
where the first three families are over all $0<i<m$; when $m=1$ the middle
family is empty and only $(0|1|0)$ is imposed. The **Khovanov–Seidel type A
algebra** is the quotient ring
$$A_m:=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m\big/I_m$$
([[def-quotient-ring]]), whose elements are written as cosets of paths.

**Internal degree.** The **internal degree** of a path is the integer
$$\deg(v_0|v_1|\cdots|v_l):=\#\{\,j:0\le j<l,\ v_{j+1}=v_j-1\,\},$$
the number of steps in which the path moves **down**; so a vertex and an
ascending arrow $(i|i+1)$ have degree $0$, while a descending arrow
$(i+1|i)$ has degree $1$. This number is additive under concatenation,
$\deg(pq)=\deg(p)+\deg(q)$.

**Grading.** Write $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$ for the free abelian group spanned by
the paths of degree $d$, so that $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$
and $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_a(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_b\subseteq(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_{a+b}$
for all $a,b$. The quotient $A_m$ is graded,
$$A_m=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(A_m)_d,\qquad (A_m)_d:=\bigl((\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d+I_m\bigr)/I_m,$$
with $(A_m)_a(A_m)_b\subseteq(A_m)_{a+b}$ and $1_{A_m}\in(A_m)_0$, as proved
below. Elements of $(A_m)_d$ are called homogeneous of degree $d$, and
homogeneous elements of the two lowest degrees are written
$$e_i:=(i),\qquad u_i:=(i|i+1),\qquad d_i:=(i+1|i)\qquad(0\le i\le m-1),$$
so that $\deg e_i=0=\deg u_i$ and $\deg d_i=1$. In keeping with the tuple
notation of the path ring, the left entry of $(i+1|i)$ is its source, so
$(i+1|i)$ is the arrow from $i+1$ to $i$; likewise $(i-1|i|i+1)$ is the
two-step path $i-1\to i\to i+1$ and $(i|i-1|i)$ is the return $i\to i-1\to i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the doubled line quiver $\Gamma_m$ with its path ring $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$, the two-sided ideal $I_m$ generated by the displayed elements, and the internal degree of paths.

[F1] $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ is the free abelian group on the directed paths of $\Gamma_m$, with left-to-right concatenation product, unit $\sum_{i=0}^m(i)$, and the endpoint formulas $(u)p=p$ if $s(p)=u$ and $0$ otherwise, $p(v)=p$ if $t(p)=v$ and $0$ otherwise ([[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]]).

[L2] For a two-sided ideal $I$ of a ring $R$ the quotient $R/I$ is a ring with multiplication $(r+I)(s+I)=rs+I$, defined on cosets of the additive quotient group ([[def-quotient-ring]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Additivity of the internal degree.* Every path is a concatenation of its consecutive arrows, and a step moves down exactly when it contributes $1$ to the count; splitting a path at a vertex therefore splits the set of steps, so $\deg(pq)=\deg(p)+\deg(q)$ for composable paths, and $\deg$ is additive over concatenations of any finite number of arrows. In particular $\deg$ is a function on the path set, and each path has one degree. [algebra]

2.1 *The path ring is a graded ring for this degree.* Put $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$ for the subgroup spanned by the paths of degree $d$. Distinct paths form a basis of $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ by [F1], so $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$; the product of paths of degrees $a$ and $b$ is $0$ or a path of degree $a+b$ by step 1.1, so $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_a(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_b\subseteq(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_{a+b}$ by bilinearity; and $1=\sum_i(i)$ lies in degree $0$. [step 1.1, F1]

2.2 *Each relation generator is homogeneous.* The generator $(i-1|i|i+1)$ is a path of degree $0$, the generator $(i+1|i|i-1)$ is a path of degree $2$, the two sides of $(i|i+1|i)-(i|i-1|i)$ are paths of degree $1$, and $(0|1|0)$ is a path of degree $1$. Hence every generator lies in a single summand $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$ for its own $d$. [step 1.1]

3.1 *The ideal is homogeneous.* The two-sided ideal generated by homogeneous elements of a graded ring is homogeneous: the set of finite sums $\sum_j r_js_jt_j$ with $s_j$ among the generators, $r_j,t_j\in\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$, is a two-sided ideal, and decomposing $r_j$ and $t_j$ into homogeneous components expresses each such element as a sum of elements lying in single summands of the form $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_{d'}(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_{d}(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_{d''}$; hence $I_m=\bigoplus_d\bigl(I_m\cap(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d\bigr)$. [step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 *The quotient is a graded ring.* By step 3.1 each $(A_m)_d:=\bigl((\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d+I_m\bigr)/I_m$ is the image of $(\mathbb Z\Gamma_m)_d$ in the quotient, the sum $\sum_d(A_m)_d$ is direct because the sum decomposition of $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ is direct modulo the homogeneous ideal, and $A_m=\bigoplus_{d\ge0}(A_m)_d$. Products satisfy $(A_m)_a(A_m)_b\subseteq(A_m)_{a+b}$ by step 2.1 and surjectivity of the quotient map, and the image of $1_{\mathbb Z\Gamma_m}$ lies in $(A_m)_0$. By [L2] the quotient is a ring, so $A_m$ is a ring graded by internal degree with $1\in(A_m)_0$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L2]

5.1 *Conclusion and conventions.* The operations of $A_m$ and its grading are those displayed, the generators of $I_m$ hold as equalities in $A_m$, and the elements $e_i,u_i,d_i$ are homogeneous of degrees $0,0,1$. For $m=1$ the families of relations indexed by $0<i<m$ are empty, and $A_1$ is the quotient of $\mathbb Z\Gamma_1$ by $(0|1|0)$ alone. [step 4.1, algebra] ∎
