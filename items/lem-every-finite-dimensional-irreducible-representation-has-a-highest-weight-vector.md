## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, and let $V\ne0$ be a finite-dimensional irreducible
representation of $\mathfrak g$
([[def-irreducible-completely-reducible-and-faithful-lie-algebra-representation]]).
Then $V$ contains a highest weight vector for the chosen positive roots
([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]); consequently $V$ is
a highest weight module for some highest weight $\lambda$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system, and a nonzero finite-dimensional irreducible module $V$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; among the facts used below, it enters through the weight decomposition [L1] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). The algebraic shift property [L2] has no choice hypothesis.

[L1] $V=\bigoplus_\mu V_\mu$ is a direct sum over its finitely many weights, and each weight space is finite dimensional ([[prop-finite-dimensional-representations-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra-decompose-into-weight-spaces]]).

[L2] If $x\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ and $w\in V_\mu$, then $x\cdot w\in V_{\mu+\alpha}$, with $0$ allowed ([[prop-root-vectors-shift-weight-spaces]]).

[L3] The root order $\le$ is a partial order on the weights, and a positive root $\alpha$ satisfies $\alpha\in Q_+\setminus\{0\}$, so $\lambda+\alpha>\lambda$ for every weight $\lambda$ ([[def-partial-order-on-weights]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

[L4] $\mathfrak n^+$ is the sum of the root spaces $\mathfrak g_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ ([[def-positive-and-negative-nilpotent-subalgebras-and-borel-subalgebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] the set $P(V)$ of weights of $V$ is finite and nonempty, because $V\ne0$ has a nonzero weight space. [A1, L1]

2.1 $P(V)$ has a maximal element: enumerating $P(V)=\{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_N\}$ and starting from $\lambda_1$, replace the current element by a strictly larger element of $P(V)$ whenever one exists; the resulting chain is strictly increasing in the partial order [L3] and therefore has at most $N$ terms, so the procedure stops at a weight $\lambda$ above which no weight of $V$ lies. [L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Choose $0\ne v\in V_\lambda$, which is possible because $\lambda$ is a weight by step 2.1. [L1, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $\alpha\in\Phi^+$ and $x\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$. If $x\cdot v\ne0$, then $x\cdot v\in V_{\lambda+\alpha}$ by [L2], so $\lambda+\alpha$ would be a weight of $V$ strictly above $\lambda$ by [L3], contradicting the maximality of $\lambda$ from step 2.1. Hence $x\cdot v=0$ for every $x$ in every positive root space, and therefore $\mathfrak n^+\cdot v=0$ by [L4]. [L2, L3, L4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 By step 4.1 the vector $v$ is a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda$ ([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]); since $V$ is irreducible and nonzero, the subrepresentation generated by $v$ is all of $V$, so $V$ is a highest weight module of highest weight $\lambda$. [step 4.1]

6.1 The existence of a highest weight vector in $V$ is proved. [step 5.1] ∎
