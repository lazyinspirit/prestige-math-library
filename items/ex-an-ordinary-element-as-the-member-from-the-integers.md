## Example

Let $A$ be an abelian group and let $a \in A$. The homomorphism
$$x_a:\mathbb Z \to A,\qquad x_a(n) = n a,$$
is a member of $A$ in the sense of [[def-member-of-an-object]]. Its associated
subobject is the cyclic subgroup generated by $a$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An abelian group $A$ and an element $a \in A$.

[L1] A member of an object is just a morphism into it
([[def-member-of-an-object]]).

[L2] Member classes correspond to subobjects
([[thm-members-modulo-equivalence-correspond-to-subobjects]]).

[L3] The category $\mathbf{Ab}$ is abelian
([[thm-abelian-groups-form-an-abelian-category]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The map $x_a$ is a group homomorphism $\mathbb Z \to A$, hence a member of $A$ by [L1]. [L1, L3]

2.1 Its image is the subgroup $\langle a \rangle$, so [L2] identifies the corresponding subobject with the cyclic subgroup generated by $a$. [L2, step 1.1] ∎
