## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $B$ be a complete nonarchimedean valued field and let $\phi$ be a rational map over $B$ of degree at least two. If $B\hookrightarrow C$ is an isometric embedding into another complete nonarchimedean valued field and $\phi$ has potentially good reduction over $B$, then its base change has potentially good reduction over $C$. If $A$ is an algebraic closure of $B$, endowed with the unique extension of the absolute value through its finite subextensions, and $\widehat A$ is its completion, then $\phi$ has potentially good reduction over $B$ if and only if a Möbius conjugate has good reduction over $\widehat A$. Positive real powers of any absolute value do not change good or potentially good reduction. All assertions allow arbitrary characteristic, inseparable extensions and the trivial valuation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields, absolute values and map in the statement.

[F1] Potential good reduction supplies a finite valued extension and a Möbius conjugate admitting an integral unit-resultant lift. ([[def-good-and-potential-good-reduction-rational-map]])

[F2] A finite extension of a complete nonarchimedean field has exactly one extending absolute value. ([[thm-unique-extension-of-a-nonarchimedean-absolute-value]])

[F3] Algebraic closures exist under Choice, and an algebraic closure with its coherent extending valuation has a complete algebraically closed completion in which it is dense. ([[thm-existence-of-algebraic-closures]], [[thm-completion-of-an-absolutely-valued-field]], [[lem-completion-of-algebraically-closed-valued-field-is-algebraically-closed]])

[A1] Full Choice is used for the closures and completions in [F3]; the finite field embeddings and approximations below require only finitely many choices at each application. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 Let $B'/B$ and a matrix and scalar over $B'$ witness potential good reduction as in [F1], and choose an algebraic closure $\Omega$ of $C$ by [F3]. There is a $B$-embedding $B'\hookrightarrow\Omega$: write $B'$ as generated by finitely many algebraic elements and extend the embedding successively by a root of the transported minimal polynomial. At each stage irreducibility identifies the simple extension with the quotient by that minimal polynomial, so the map is injective; separability is not needed. Let $E$ be the compositum of $C$ and the image of $B'$, a finite extension of $C$. Give $E$ its extending valuation by [F2]. Its restriction to $B'$ equals the original one by uniqueness over the complete field $B$. Thus all the coefficient bounds and the unit resultant of the good lift persist in $E$, proving potential good reduction over $C$. [F1, F2, F3, A1, algebra]

2.1 If $\phi$ has potentially good reduction over $B$, apply step 1.1 with $C=\widehat A$, which is complete and algebraically closed by [F3]. A finite algebraic extension of an algebraically closed field is itself, so the good conjugacy already exists over $\widehat A$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Conversely, fix a lift $F$ over $B$ and suppose $cM^{-1}FM$ is a good lift over $\widehat A$, with $M\in\operatorname{GL}_2(\widehat A)$ and $c\ne0$. As functions of the four matrix entries and $c$, the coefficients of $cM^{-1}FM$ are rational functions whose only denominators are powers of $\det M$; its resultant is a polynomial in those coefficients. These functions are continuous on $\det M\ne0$. The conditions that the finitely many coefficients have absolute value at most $1$ and that the resultant has absolute value exactly $1$ are open conditions in a nonarchimedean field: a perturbation of an integral element by modulus less than $1$ remains integral, and such a perturbation of a unit remains a unit. Nonvanishing of $c$ and $\det M$ is open as well. Hence all good-lift conditions hold in some product neighborhood of $(M,c)$. By density of $A$ in $\widehat A$, choose entries $M',c'$ in this neighborhood lying in $A$. They belong to a finite extension $B''/B$, and $c'(M')^{-1}FM'$ is good over its inherited valuation. This is the finite witness required in [F1]. [F1, F3, A1, algebra]

3.1 Replacing $|\cdot|$ by $|\cdot|^t$ with $t>0$ preserves each inequality $|a|\le1$ and equality $|R|=1$, so it preserves good reduction. Raising an extending valuation to that power preserves its extension property; conversely take its positive $t$th root. Thus potential good reduction is preserved in both directions. This applies equally in the trivial case and completes the proof. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
