## Definition

**Setting.** Keep $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ with $\deg x_i=2$ and the
standard graph bimodules $R_w$ of
[[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]]: for
$w\in S_n$ the graded $(R,R)$-bimodule $R_w$ equals $R$ as a graded
$\mathbb Q$-vector space with $f\cdot x=fx$ and $x\cdot g=w(g)x$, where
$w(g)(\lambda)=g(w^{-1}\lambda)$, generated by the element $1$ of degree $0$.
Recall the degree-zero isomorphism of graded $(R,R)$-bimodules
$$\mu_{w,v}:R_w\otimes_RR_v\longrightarrow R_{wv},\qquad a\otimes b\longmapsto a\,w(b),$$
for $w,v\in S_n$, with inverse $1\mapsto1\otimes1$ (the composition remark of
that item, where the assignment is checked to be balanced, $R$-linear on both
sides and surjective between free rank-one left $R$-modules). Here the internal
shifts are those of [[def-graded-ring-module-bimodule-and-internal-shift]]:
$\mu$ is homogeneous of degree zero.

**Word comparisons.** Let $t=(x_1,\ldots,x_r)$ and $u=(y_1,\ldots,y_s)$ be
finite words in $S_n$ (the empty word allowed) with the same product
$$x_1\cdots x_r=y_1\cdots y_s=w .$$
Fix the left-to-right iteration of $\mu$ and write
$$\mu_t:R_{x_1}\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_RR_{x_r}\longrightarrow R_w,\qquad a_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_r\longmapsto a_1\,x_1(a_2)\,(x_1x_2)(a_3)\cdots(x_1\cdots x_{r-1})(a_r),$$
the composite of the binary multiplications $\mu_{x_1,x_2},\mu_{x_1x_2,x_3},\ldots$;
for $r=0$ the source is the unit bimodule $R=R_e$ and $\mu_t=\mathrm{id}_R$,
and for $r=1$ it is $\mathrm{id}_{R_{x_1}}$. Each $\mu_t$ is a degree-zero
isomorphism of graded $(R,R)$-bimodules with inverse
$1\mapsto1\otimes1\otimes\cdots\otimes1$. The **canonical comparison** between
the two word tensors is
$$c_{t,u}:=\mu_u^{-1}\circ\mu_t:R_{x_1}\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_RR_{x_r}\longrightarrow R_{y_1}\otimes_R\cdots\otimes_RR_{y_s}.$$

**Basic properties.** The comparisons form a transitive system: $c_{t,t}=\mathrm{id}$
for every word $t$, and for three words $t,u,v$ with the same product
$c_{u,v}\circ c_{t,u}=c_{t,v}$, because
$\mu_v^{-1}\mu_u\mu_u^{-1}\mu_t=\mu_v^{-1}\mu_t$. In particular
$c_{t,u}$ is inverse to $c_{u,t}$. When one of the words has length one the
comparison is the corresponding display: for $r=1$, $t=(x_1)$, one has
$c_{t,u}=\mu_u^{-1}$; for $s=1$, $c_{t,u}=\mu_t$. The identifications are
associative in the sense that for a threefold product the two iterated
comparisons built from the binary $\mu$'s coincide, this being the equality of
the explicit formula above under any rebracketing.

**Homogeneity of the Hom spaces.** For $w,w'\in S_n$,
$$\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{R^e}(R_w,R_{w'})=\begin{cases}\cong R,&w=w',\\0,&w\ne w',\end{cases}$$
where the underlined Hom is the direct sum over all homogeneous internal
degrees. For $w=w'$ the identification sends a homogeneous map $\varphi$ to
$\varphi(1)\in R$ and the internal degree of $\varphi$ is the degree of
$\varphi(1)$; hence the internal degree-zero part is one-dimensional over
$\mathbb Q$, spanned by $\mathrm{id}_{R_w}$. The vanishing for $w\ne w'$ is the
statement that the supports $Gr(w)\ne Gr(w')$ are distinct graphs, and the
description for $w=w'$ is the map remark of
[[def-type-a-standard-graph-bimodules-support-filtrations-and-character]].

**Small cases.** If $r=s=0$ then $w=e$ and $c_{t,u}=\mathrm{id}_R$; if $t$ and
$u$ are both words for $w$, the comparison $c_{t,u}$ is the isomorphism used to
compare the two word models of the same standard bimodule $R_w$. No choice is
made: $\mu_t$ is the unique degree-zero bimodule isomorphism with
$\mu_t(1\otimes\cdots\otimes1)=1$, and $c_{t,u}$ is determined by $t$ and $u$
alone.
