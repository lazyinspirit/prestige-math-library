## Statement

Let $F=\mathbb R$ or $\mathbb C$, let $n\ge1$, and let $\psi:F\to\mathbb C^\times$ be a nontrivial continuous additive character. Put $G=\mathrm{GL}_n(F)$, $K=O(n)$ if $F=\mathbb R$ and $K=U(n)$ if $F=\mathbb C$, and $N=\{u\in G:u_{ii}=1,\ u_{ij}=0\text{ for }i>j\}$. Define $\psi_N(u)=\psi(\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}u_{i,i+1})$. A Casselman–Wallach representation here is a smooth representation $\pi$ of moderate growth on a complete metrizable locally convex complex vector space $V$. Let $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ be the complexification of the Lie algebra of $G$ as a real Lie group; a vector is $K$-finite if its $K$-span is finite-dimensional. The $K$-finite vectors form an admissible finite-length $(\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C},K)$-module: admissible means each irreducible finite-dimensional continuous representation of the compact group $K$ occurs with finite multiplicity, and finite length means a finite composition series by subspaces stable under $\mathfrak g_{\mathbb C}$ and $K$. Smooth means each orbit map $g\mapsto\pi(g)v$ is smooth. Moderate growth means that for every continuous seminorm $p$ there are a continuous seminorm $q$, constants $C>0$ and $d\ge0$ with $p(\pi(g)v)\le C\max(1,\|g\|,\|g^{-1}\|)^d q(v)$, where the norms are the standard Euclidean or Hermitian operator norms. Irreducible means that $V$ has no nonzero proper closed $G$-invariant subspace. A continuous $\psi_N$-equivariant linear form is a continuous complex-linear map $\ell:V\to\mathbb C$ satisfying $\ell(\pi(u)v)=\psi_N(u)\ell(v)$ for all $u\in N$ and $v\in V$. For every irreducible Casselman–Wallach representation $\pi$, the vector space of such forms has dimension at most one. If it is nonzero (the meaning of locally generic here), its dimension is exactly one.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The exact field, representation and character data stated above. All selections in this proof are finite.

[F1] For a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra with an ordered basis, the ordered monomials form a basis of its enveloping algebra, and the associated graded algebra is its symmetric algebra. [[thm-pbw-ordered-monomial-basis-for-the-enveloping-algebra]]

[F2] In characteristic zero, the symmetrization map from the symmetric algebra to the enveloping algebra is an equivariant filtered vector-space isomorphism. [[thm-pbw-symmetrization-is-a-vector-space-isomorphism-in-characteristic-zero]]

[A1] In the case where the space in the conclusion is nonzero, fix one nonzero continuous $\psi_N$-equivariant linear form $\lambda_0$; if that space is zero, the asserted upper bound is immediate.

For any continuous seminorm p, continuity of the orbit map makes p(pi(k)v-v) small near the identity, and compactness bounds it on K. Splitting the integral over that neighborhood and its complement therefore proves S_m(v)->v in every seminorm. The K-translates of the polynomial function (1+Re tr(k)/n)^m span a finite-dimensional vector space: translation replaces every matrix entry by a finite linear combination of matrix entries, and leaves its total polynomial degree bounded by m. 5.1 The honest Hilbert contragredient and its smooth vectors. All Hilbert-space constructions here can be carried out at the original choice scope. Hilbert projection on a closed subspace uses a minimizing sequence chosen canonically, not Countable Choice: a nonempty closed subset of a complete metric space with a fixed countable dense list has a selector by recursively taking the least basic ball whose closure lies in the previous ball and which meets the closed set, with radii tending to zero. The centers are Cauchy and their limit lies in the closed set. Apply this selector to the closed intersections of the subspace with balls of radii approaching the infimum distance; the parallelogram identity makes the selected minimizing sequence Cauchy. This gives the projection and the finite Hilbert-space extensions used below without arbitrary simultaneous choices. Let E^* be the continuous complex-linear Hilbert dual, with pi^*(g)ell=ell composed with pi(g^-1). Its action is strongly continuous. Here is an explicit argument. 9.1 A finite constant-coefficient system for the central orbit. First establish the finite central-orbit assertion. A simple (g_C,K)-module is countable-dimensional over C, since the finite K-span of a nonzero vector generates it over U(g_C), whose finite Lie words form a countable spanning list. If a central endomorphism z on such a simple module were transcendental, every z-a, a in C, would be invertible: its kernel and image are submodules, and z-a is nonzero. For fixed nonzero v the vectors (z-a)^(-1)v would be linearly independent. Indeed multiply any finite relation by the product of the distinct z-a factors; the resulting partial-fraction polynomials are linearly independent, and no nonzero polynomial in z can kill v, since that polynomial would then vanish on the simple module it generates. This contradicts countable dimension over the uncountable field C. Thus z is algebraic; factoring an annihilating polynomial over C gives an eigenvalue, and its nonzero eigenspace is a submodule, so z acts by a scalar. On a finite composition series, the product of the scalar-annihilating linear factors kills the whole module: each factor lowers the corresponding series layer. In particular every central element has a finite-dimensional orbit on each vector. Use the invariant real trace form B(X,Y)=Re tr(XY), with Re omitted over R. Write the real Lie algebra as k plus p, the skew-symmetric/skew-Hermitian and symmetric/Hermitian parts. Choose a basis Y_j orthonormal for -B on k and X_i orthonormal for B on p. Since C commutes with K, the finite-dimensional span of all C^j v remains in that same compact type, and Omega_K has the same value c there.
