## Statement

Let $k$ be a field, let $A$ be a $k$-algebra of finite type, and let
$y_1,\ldots,y_n\in A$ generate $A$ as a $k$-algebra. Suppose there exists a
monic polynomial
$$
Y^m+b_{m-1}Y^{m-1}+\cdots+b_0\in k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}][Y]
$$
that vanishes at $Y=y_n$. Then $y_n$ is integral over the subalgebra
$k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$, and hence $A$ is integral over that subalgebra.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$, a finite-type $k$-algebra $A$, generators $y_1,\ldots,y_n\in A$, and a monic polynomial relation for $y_n$ over $k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$.

[L1] The notation $k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$ denotes the $k$-subalgebra generated by $y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}$, and finite type means generated by finitely many elements as an algebra ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L2] An element is integral over a base ring exactly when it satisfies a monic polynomial over that ring ([[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $B=k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$. By [L1], $B$ is a subalgebra of $A$. The displayed relation is a monic polynomial in $B[Y]$ with value zero at $y_n$, so [L2] shows that $y_n$ is integral over $B$. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 Since $A$ is generated by $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ over $k$, it is generated by $y_n$ as a $B$-algebra: $A=B[y_n]$. Every element of $B$ is integral over $B$, and step 1.1 gives integrality of $y_n$ over $B$, so every element of $A=B[y_n]$ is integral over $B$. [step 1.1, L1, algebra]

3.1 Therefore $A$ is integral over the subalgebra $k[y_1,\ldots,y_{n-1}]$. [step 2.1] ∎
