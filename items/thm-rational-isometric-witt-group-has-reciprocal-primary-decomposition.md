## Statement

For a monic irreducible polynomial $p\in\mathbb Q[t]$ with $p(0)\ne0$, put $p^*(t)=p(0)^{-1}t^{\deg p}p(t^{-1})$. For each such self-reciprocal polynomial $p=p^*$ other than $t-1$, let $G_p$ be the subgroup of the rational isometric Witt group generated by structures whose characteristic polynomial is a power of $p$. Then restriction to the generalized primary subspaces induces an isomorphism
$$
G_{\mathbb Q}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{p=p^*,\ p\ne t-1}G_p.
$$
A pair of distinct reciprocal primary factors contributes a metabolic structure and therefore contributes zero. Only finitely many components of any given class are nonzero. No choice principle is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A rational isometric structure $(W,B,T)$, so $B$ is nonsingular symmetric, $T$ is an invertible $B$-isometry, and $I-T$ is invertible.

[F1] Metabolic structures have invariant isotropic subspaces of half the total dimension and represent zero in $G_{\mathbb Q}$. [[def-integral-witt-and-seifert-isometric-structures]]

[A1] Polynomial factorizations, Bezout identities, decompositions, and selected bases below are finite. They use no infinite choice.

## Proof

**Given:** $(W,B,T)$ as above.

1.1 Factor the minimal polynomial as $m_T=\prod_p p^{a_p}$. Its factors have nonzero constant term because $T$ is invertible, and $t-1$ does not occur because $I-T$ is invertible. Bezout identities for the pairwise coprime powers give polynomial idempotent projections summing to the identity and yield $W=\bigoplus_p W_p$, where $W_p=\ker p(T)^{a_p}$. Explicitly, choose $u_p$ with $u_p(m_T/p^{a_p})\equiv1\pmod {p^{a_p}}$ and set $e_p=u_p(T)(m_T/p^{a_p})(T)$. On $W_p$ this is the identity and on every other $W_q$ it is zero. The reciprocal operation takes monic irreducibles to monic irreducibles and is an involution. [A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The isometry identity says $B(Tx,y)=B(x,T^{-1}y)$, so $B(f(T)x,y)=B(x,f(T^{-1})y)$ for every polynomial $f$. If $q\ne p^*$, the operator $p(T^{-1})$ is invertible on $W_q$: up to an invertible power of $T$ and a nonzero rational scalar it equals $p^*(T)$, and Bezout with $q^{a_q}$ gives its inverse. For $x\in W_p$ and $y\in W_q$, choose $z\in W_q$ with $y=p(T^{-1})^{a_p}z$. Then $B(x,y)=B(p(T)^{a_p}x,z)=0$. Thus $W_p$ pairs only with $W_{p^*}$. Nonsingularity of $B$ shows that every occurring $p$ has an occurring $p^*$ and that this pairing is perfect. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $p\ne p^*$, the restriction of $B$ to $W_p$ itself is zero, while $B$ is nonsingular on $W_p\oplus W_{p^*}$ and the two summands have equal dimension by the perfect pairing. The invariant subspace $W_p$ is a metabolizer of this pair. If $p=p^*$, the restriction to $W_p$ is nonsingular. Each such self-reciprocal block has even dimension: an irreducible self-reciprocal polynomial other than $t\pm1$ has even degree, since its distinct roots are paired by $z\mapsto z^{-1}$ and none is $\pm1$. Its primary block dimension is a multiple of that degree. The only remaining possible self-reciprocal factor is $t+1$; all other blocks, including the paired non-self-reciprocal ones, have even dimension, so its dimension is also even because $\dim W$ is even. Restrictions of $T$ and $I-T$ to these invariant subspaces remain invertible by injectivity and finite dimension. Hence every self-reciprocal block and every reciprocal pair is an admissible rational isometric structure, and the latter pairs are zero by [F1]. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 To see that restriction passes to the Witt group, let $L$ be an invariant metabolizer of $W$. Every polynomial in $T$ preserves $L$, hence the idempotents in step 1.1 give $L=\bigoplus_p(L\cap W_p)$. Since $L=L^\perp$, for a self-reciprocal $p$ the orthogonality in step 2.1 implies that $(L\cap W_p)^{\perp_{W_p}}=L\cap W_p$. Thus that intersection is invariant, isotropic, and half-dimensional in $W_p$, so the restriction of any metabolic structure to this block is metabolic. Restriction also respects isomorphisms and direct sums because the primary subspaces are kernels of polynomials in $T$. It therefore defines homomorphisms to all $G_p$, with finite support. Their direct sum is the displayed map. Conversely direct-sum inclusion of the blocks gives a homomorphism from the displayed direct sum to $G_{\mathbb Q}$. Restriction after inclusion is the identity on each component. Inclusion after restriction is the identity on every class because its actual orthogonal primary decomposition differs only by the metabolic reciprocal pairs from step 3.1. The two homomorphisms are inverse, proving the theorem. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra] ∎
