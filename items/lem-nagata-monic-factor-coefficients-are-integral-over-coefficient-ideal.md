## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring. Suppose the monic polynomial $f(T)=T^d+a_1T^{d-1}+\cdots+a_d$ divides the monic polynomial $g(T)=T^n+b_1T^{n-1}+\cdots+b_n$ in $R[T]$. Put $A=\mathbb Z[b_1,\ldots,b_n]\subseteq R$, meaning the subring generated by the image of the integers and the listed coefficients, and put $I=(b_1,\ldots,b_n)A$. For each $a_i$ there is a monic equation
$$a_i^{e_i}+c_{i1}a_i^{e_i-1}+\cdots+c_{ie_i}=0,\qquad c_{ij}\in I.$$
In particular every nonleading coefficient of the monic factor is integral over $A$. This includes degree-zero monic factors and arbitrary zero divisors and nilpotents in $R$; no Noetherianity or choice hypothesis is required.

## Statement references

[Alonso–Coquand–Lombardi, Lemma2.3(1), p.5](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00597)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The coefficient ring and monic divisibility in the Statement.

[F1] A monic polynomial admits division with unique remainder of smaller degree. [[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]

[F2] Finitely many integral elements generate a finite module algebra, and the elements integral over a coefficient ring form a subring. [[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]] [[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]

[F3] If $A\subseteq B$ is integral, every element of $\sqrt{IB}$ satisfies a monic equation with its nonleading coefficients in $I$. The radical of an ideal is an ideal. [[lem-nagata-integral-radical-contraction-by-determinants]] [[lem-radical-is-an-ideal]]

## Proof

1.1 Construct an injective splitting algebra for $f$ without choosing a field or a prime. If $d>0$, set $R_1=R[t_1]/(f(t_1))$. By monic division [F1], this is a free $R$-module on $1,t_1,\ldots,t_1^{d-1}$, so the map $R\to R_1$ is injective. Division by $T-t_1$ gives $f(T)=(T-t_1)f_1(T)$ in $R_1[T]$, with $f_1$ monic of degree $d-1$: the remainder is $f(t_1)=0$. Repeat this construction with $f_1$, then its monic quotient, until a ring $E$ with $R\subseteq E$ and $f(T)=\prod_{j=1}^d(T-t_j)$ is obtained. There are exactly $d$ stages, each injective and finite free. For $d=0$ set $E=R$ and take the empty product. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Since $f$ divides $g$, every $t_j$ is a root of $g$ in $E$. Its monic equation $g(t_j)=0$ has coefficients in $A$, so $t_j$ is integral over $A$. Let $B=A[t_1,\ldots,t_d]\subseteq E$. It is integral and module-finite over $A$ by [F2]. Expanding the finite product for $f$ shows that $a_i$ is the signed $i$th elementary symmetric polynomial in the $t_j$, so each $a_i$ belongs to $B$. This equality holds in $E$ and retains its original meaning in the embedded ring $R$. [F2, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For every $j$ the equation for $t_j$ gives $t_j^n\in IB$, so $t_j\in\sqrt{IB}$ when $n>0$. Each $a_i$ with $i>0$ is a sum of positive-degree products of the $t_j$. Because $\sqrt{IB}$ is an ideal by [F3], it follows that $a_i\in\sqrt{IB}$. Apply [F3] to the integral inclusion $A\subseteq B$ to obtain exactly the asserted monic equation with coefficients in $I$. Its equality in $B\subseteq E$ is an equality in $R$ because $R\subseteq E$ is injective. [F3, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $d=0$, there are no nonleading coefficients and the assertion is vacuous. If $R$ is nonzero and $g=1$, monic division implies $d=0$, since the degree of a product by a monic polynomial cannot be smaller than its degree. If $R$ is the zero ring, every coefficient is zero and satisfies the equation $T=0$. Thus the degree-zero and zero-ring cases also hold, and all constructions in the other cases used only the given finite degrees and explicit monic quotients. [F1, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
