## Definition

Use the conditionally linear-Gaussian model of
[[def-conditionally-linear-gaussian-state-space-model]] and a fixed
observation path $y_{1:T}$. For each $0\le t\le T$, let $\bar P_t$ be the
nonlinear path law generated by $\mu_0$ and the path-extension kernels from
[[lem-nonlinear-path-extension-is-a-probability-kernel]], and
put
$$c_t(y_{1:t})=\int_{\mathsf Z_{0:t}}\prod_{s=1}^t\ell_s(z_{0:s};y_{1:s})\,\bar P_t(dz_{0:t}),\qquad c_0=1.$$
Here $\ell_s$ is the conditional observation density supplied by
[[lem-kalman-filter-is-exact-conditional-on-nonlinear-path]].
For the claim that the procedure targets the nonlinear filtering law at every
prefix, assume $0<c_t(y_{1:t})<\infty$ for every $t$; this is the finite-positive
normalizer condition for the normalized Feynman–Kac target. The Kalman
likelihood factorization identifies $c_t$ with the observation
evidence density and the normalized path target with the exact conditional
law of $Z_{0:t}$ given $Y_{1:t}=y_{1:t}$. The particle procedure itself uses
finite positive per-particle likelihoods, so each of
its empirical weight normalizers is finite positive. The particle state at
time $t$ is the full
nonlinear history $z_{0:t}$; finite products of the standard-Borel state
spaces are standard Borel. Its transition appends a draw
$z_t\sim M_t(z_{t-1},\cdot)$; the resulting path-extension probability
kernel and standard-Borel path space are supplied by
[[lem-nonlinear-path-extension-is-a-probability-kernel]]. Set the initial Feynman–Kac potential to
$G_0\equiv1$ and the time-$t$ potential to the conditional observation
density
$$G_t(z_{0:t})=\ell_t(z_{0:t};y_{1:t})$$
from [[lem-kalman-filter-is-exact-conditional-on-nonlinear-path]]. Use the
same path-extension kernel as target and proposal, so the initial and
transition Radon–Nikodym weights are identically one. Run
[[alg-sequential-importance-resampling]] with multinomial resampling to
approximate the filtering law of the nonlinear path. Each particle stores
alongside its path the conditional Gaussian moments of $U_t$.

Initialize $N\ge1$ particles by drawing $z_0^i\sim\mu_0$ independently,
setting $w_0^i=1/N$, $m_{0|0}^i=m_0(z_0^i)$, and
$P_{0|0}^i=P_0(z_0^i)$. For each $t=1,\ldots,T$:

1. Draw ancestor indices $J_t^1,\ldots,J_t^N$ independently with
   probabilities $(w_{t-1}^1,\ldots,w_{t-1}^N)$ and append independent
   transitions $z_t^i\sim M_t(z_{t-1}^{J_t^i},\cdot)$.
2. For each particle, abbreviate its appended path by $z_{0:t}^i$ and compute
   $$m_{t|t-1}^i=A_t(z_{0:t}^i)m_{t-1|t-1}^{J_t^i},\qquad P_{t|t-1}^i=A_t(z_{0:t}^i)P_{t-1|t-1}^{J_t^i}A_t(z_{0:t}^i)^T+Q_t(z_{0:t}^i).$$
   Form $v_t^i=y_t-C_t(z_{0:t}^i)m_{t|t-1}^i$ and
   $S_t^i=C_t(z_{0:t}^i)P_{t|t-1}^iC_t(z_{0:t}^i)^T+R_t(z_{0:t}^i)$.
3. Set the incremental weight to the finite positive Gaussian density
   $$a_t^i=(2\pi)^{-p_t/2}\det(S_t^i)^{-1/2}\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12(v_t^i)^T(S_t^i)^{-1}v_t^i\right),$$
   and normalize $w_t^i=a_t^i/\sum_j a_t^j$.
4. Update the conditional Gaussian moments by
   $$K_t^i=P_{t|t-1}^iC_t(z_{0:t}^i)^T(S_t^i)^{-1},\qquad m_{t|t}^i=m_{t|t-1}^i+K_t^iv_t^i,\qquad P_{t|t}^i=P_{t|t-1}^i-K_t^iS_t^i(K_t^i)^T.$$

The procedure returns the weighted nonlinear particle paths and the Gaussian
mixture
$$\widehat\pi_t^N(du_t,dz_{0:t})=\sum_{i=1}^Nw_t^iN(du_t;m_{t|t}^i,P_{t|t}^i)\,\delta_{z_{0:t}^i}(dz_{0:t}).$$
Its conditional mean is $\sum_iw_t^im_{t|t}^i$ and its conditional covariance
is $\sum_iw_t^i[P_{t|t}^i+(m_{t|t}^i-\bar m_t)(m_{t|t}^i-\bar m_t)^T]$,
where $\bar m_t=\sum_iw_t^im_{t|t}^i$. These are moments of the finite
mixture approximation; for finite $N$ the algorithm remains an approximation
to the full nonlinear filter. It makes no general variance-reduction,
convergence-rate, or long-time accuracy claim.

## Remarks

For every particle, $P_{t|t-1}^i$ is positive semidefinite and
$R_t(z_{0:t}^i)$ is positive definite, so $S_t^i$ is positive definite and
the Kalman update and Gaussian density are defined. Conditional exactness of
each Gaussian component is supplied by
[[lem-kalman-filter-is-exact-conditional-on-nonlinear-path]]. More precisely,
each stored component is the conditional law of $U_t$ given its prefix
$Z_{0:t}=z_{0:t}^i$ and $Y_{1:t}=y_{1:t}$. This follows by induction: at time
zero it is the specified Gaussian law; at each later step the appended
nonlinear transition is $M_t(z_{t-1},\cdot)$ and does not depend on
$U_{t-1}$, so conditioning on the appended state does not change the parent
Gaussian component, after which the Kalman prediction and observation update
give the stated component. Equivalently, the Kalman lemma's conditional law
given the full nonlinear path depends through time $t$ only on $z_{0:t}$;
future nonlinear transitions are independent of $U_{0:t}$ and $Y_{1:t}$
given the prefix, depend only on the current nonlinear state, and integrate
to one. Integrating over $z_{t+1:T}$ therefore leaves that same Gaussian
law given the prefix. The mixture over nonlinear paths is still represented
by finitely many particles.
