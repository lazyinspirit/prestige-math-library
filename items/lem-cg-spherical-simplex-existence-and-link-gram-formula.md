## Statement

Let $C=(c_{ij})$ be a real symmetric positive-definite $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrix with diagonal $1$, and let $L$, $u_0,\dots,u_n$, $K(C)$, $\Sigma(C)$ be as in [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]].

**(i) Existence and uniqueness of the Gram realisation.** The vectors $u_i$ are unit vectors with $u_i\cdot u_j=c_{ij}$, they are linearly independent and span $\mathbb R^{n+1}$, and every other family $u_i'$ of unit vectors in a Euclidean space with $u_i'\cdot u_j'=c_{ij}$ is the image of $(u_0,\dots,u_n)$ under a linear isometry of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ onto the span of the $u_i'$. Hence $\Sigma(C)$ is well-defined up to an isometry of $S^n$ mapping vertices to vertices.

**(ii) Hemisphere, radial coordinates and bi-Lipschitz comparison.** The linear functional $\varphi$ determined by $\varphi(u_i)=1$ for all $i$ satisfies $\varphi\bigl(\sum_i\lambda_iu_i\bigr)=\sum_i\lambda_i$; consequently $\varphi>0$ on $K(C)\setminus\{0\}$ and $\Sigma(C)$ is contained in the open hemisphere $\{x\in S^n:\varphi(x)>0\}$. Radial normalisation $\rho(x):=x/|x|$ restricts to a bijection $\Delta(C)\to\Sigma(C)$, where $\Delta(C):=\{\sum_i\lambda_iu_i:\lambda_i\ge0,\ \sum_i\lambda_i=1\}$, with inverse $y\mapsto y/\varphi(y)$; every point of $\Sigma(C)$ therefore has unique barycentric ray coordinates $\lambda_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. Moreover $\rho$ and $\rho^{-1}$ satisfy the explicit Lipschitz estimates $|\rho(x)-\rho(y)|\le\frac{2}{\min_{z\in\Delta(C)}|z|}|x-y|$ and $|\rho^{-1}(y)-\rho^{-1}(y')|\le(1+\lVert\varphi\rVert)|y-y'|$, proved directly from the formulas without differentiability; here $\lVert\varphi\rVert=|w|$ for the unique coordinate vector $w$ with $\varphi(x)=w\cdot x$. Hence the round metric $d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)=\arccos\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$ on $\Sigma(C)$ is bi-Lipschitz equivalent to the Euclidean metric of the compact convex cell $\Delta(C)$ carried by $\rho$ (on a subset of the unit sphere the round distance and the chord distance satisfy $\text{chord}\le\text{round}\le\frac{\pi}{2}\,\text{chord}$).

**(iii) Finite spherical complexes.** Let $K$ be a finite abstract simplicial complex ([[def-abstract-simplicial-complex]]) whose nonempty simplices $\sigma$ carry positive-definite Gram matrices $C_\sigma$ of diagonal $1$, compatible with faces. Form $X:=|K|_C$ by gluing the spherical simplices $\Sigma(C_\sigma)$ along their vertex-wise face identifications. On each component define the **chain metric** $d$ as the infimum of $\sum_i d_{p_i}(x_{i-1},x_i)$ over finite chains in common cells, with $d_p$ the angular metric of that cell; equivalently it is the infimum of lengths of continuous paths. Between different components this infimum is $+\infty$, auxiliary notation rather than a metric value ([[def-metric-space]]). The inverse cellwise radial maps descend to a homeomorphism $X\to Y$, where $Y$ is the Euclidean gluing with cells $\Delta(C_\sigma)$; it is bi-Lipschitz on every component. Each component of $X$ is a compact proper complete length space whose metric topology is the weak topology, and $X$ is a finite disjoint union of these components. For the empty complex, $X=Y=\emptyset$. *Under the Axiom of Choice* ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), every two points in a component are joined by a minimizing geodesic of length $d(x,y)$, in particular every pair at distance $<\pi$. The proper-target argument is written locally below, using [[lem-cg-metric-target-length-reparametrization-and-lower-semicontinuity]] and [[cor-arzela-ascoli-subsequence-theorem-for-proper-metric-targets]]; compare [[thm-cg-proper-polyhedral-spaces-have-minimizing-geodesics]].

**(iv) Vertex and face links by Schur complements.** The normal link of a nonempty spherical face spanned by vertices indexed by $A$ consists of the unit tangent directions perpendicular to $P_A:=\operatorname{span}\{u_a:a\in A\}$ that point into $\Sigma(C)$ at a relative interior point of the face. For $n\ge1$ and $1\le i\le n$ put $v_i:=(u_i-c_{i0}u_0)/\sqrt{1-c_{i0}^{2}}\in u_0^\perp\cap S^n$. The link of the vertex $u_0$ in $\Sigma(C)$ is the spherical simplex with vertices $v_1,\dots,v_n$, and its Gram matrix is
$$c^{\mathrm{lk}}_{ij}=\frac{c_{ij}-c_{i0}c_{j0}}{\sqrt{(1-c_{i0}^{2})(1-c_{j0}^{2})}}\qquad(1\le i,j\le n),$$
which is positive definite: the projection formula follows by expanding $v_i\cdot v_j$, and positivity follows from the positive definiteness of the unnormalised Schur complement $(c_{ij}-c_{i0}c_{j0})_{i,j\ge1}$, whose quadratic form is $w^{T}Cw>0$ for the nonzero vector $w=(-x_1c_{10}-\cdots-x_nc_{n0},\,x_1,\dots,x_n)$. Iterating the formula over the vertices of a face of $\Sigma(C)$ gives the link of that face; the result is independent of the order of iteration, and the link of a face of a link is the link of a higher face. If no vertices remain, the link is empty; in particular the vertex link for $n=0$ is empty. More generally, for a nonempty proper vertex set $A$, let $C_A=(c_{ab})_{a,b\in A}$, $b_i=(c_{ai})_{a\in A}$ and $w_i:=u_i-\sum_{a\in A}(C_A^{-1}b_i)_a u_a$ for $i\notin A$. These are the orthogonal projections onto $P_A^\perp$, with $z_{ij}:=\langle w_i,w_j\rangle=c_{ij}-b_i^TC_A^{-1}b_j$; the face-link Gram matrix is $z_{ij}/\sqrt{z_{ii}z_{jj}}$.

**(v) Angular links of Euclidean faces.** For a compact convex polyhedral cell $C$ and a face $F$ as in [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]], with direction space $U(F)$ and normal cone $N_FC=T_FC\cap U(F)^{\perp}$, after constant defining inequalities are discarded, the two descriptions of the tangent cone agree: $T_FC=\overline{\{\lambda(x-p):x\in C,\ \lambda\ge0\}}$ for every $p$ in the relative interior of $F$, and $T_FC=\{\xi:\langle\xi,n_j\rangle\ge0\ (j\in I(F))\}$ for the inward unit normals of the active defining hyperplanes; the tangent cone and the normal cone depend only on $F$. The cone $N_FC$ is pointed, and is closed under nonnegative combinations, so for $\xi,\eta\in N_FC\cap S(V)$ the shorter great-circle arc between them stays in $N_FC\cap S(V)$ and realises $\arccos\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$ as an intrinsic path in the link; conversely every path in $N_FC\cap S(V)$ has length at least the round distance of its endpoints. Hence the angular distance of [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]] is the intrinsic path metric of the round sphere restricted to $\operatorname{Lk}_C(F)$, it is a metric of diameter at most $\pi$, and the links $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_p}(F)$ of the cells containing a face $F$ of an isometric polyhedral gluing depend only on $F$, so their quotient $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is well defined up to canonical isometry.

**(vi) Disconnected components.** If the angular link of a face of a complex is disconnected, the componentwise path distance is $+\infty$ between distinct components; this value is auxiliary notation only and is truncated in the cone formulas of [[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A real symmetric positive-definite $(n+1)\times(n+1)$ matrix $C$ with diagonal $1$, its Cholesky factor $L$, the vectors $u_i$, the cone $K(C)$ and simplex $\Sigma(C)$ of [[def-cg-spherical-gram-simplex-and-angular-link]]; in clauses (iii) and (vi) additionally a finite abstract simplicial complex $K$ with compatible Gram matrices $C_\sigma$ as in the Statement.

[F1] A real symmetric positive-definite matrix has a unique Cholesky factorisation $C=LL^{T}$ with lower triangular $L$ and positive diagonal; then $L$ is invertible and $C=LL^{T}$ is its own real case ([[thm-cholesky-factorisation-exists-iff-hermitian-positive-definite-and-is-unique]], [[def-cholesky-factorisation-with-positive-diagonal]]).

[F2] In a real inner-product space, $\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle$ is bilinear and symmetric, $|v|=\sqrt{\langle v,v\rangle}$ is a norm, $|\langle u,v\rangle|\le|u||v|$ with equality exactly for linearly dependent $u,v$, every finite-dimensional subspace has an orthogonal complement and orthogonal projection by [[thm-finite-dimensional-orthogonal-decomposition]] and [[def-orthogonal-projection]], the unit sphere is $S^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^{n+1}:|x|=1\}$ ([[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]]), and $\arccos:[ -1,1]\to[0,\pi]$ is the inverse of the strictly decreasing restriction of $\cos$ to $[0,\pi]$, continuous on $[-1,1]$ ([[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]], [[cor-inner-product-induces-a-norm]], [[def-real-and-complex-inner-product-space]], [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-in-an-inner-product-space]]).

[F3] $\cos(x+y)=\cos x\cos y-\sin x\sin y$ and $\sin(x+y)=\sin x\cos y+\cos x\sin y$ for all real $x,y$ ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]]); $\sin'=\cos$ and $\cos'=-\sin$ ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]); $\sin(\pi/2)=1$, $\cos(\pi/2)=0$, $\sin\pi=0$ and $\cos\pi=-1$ ([[thm-quarter-turn-values-and-shift-formulas]]); $\cos^{2}t+\sin^{2}t=1$ and $\sin(-t)=-\sin t$ ([[cor-trigonometric-parity-and-pythagorean-identity]]); $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$, and $\sin$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\pi/2]$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]]); for $0<x\le2$ one has $\sin x\ge x-x^{3}/6\ge x/3>0$, and $\cos2\le-1/3$, so $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,2]$ and $\pi/2<2$ ([[lem-sine-positive-and-cosine-decreasing-on-zero-two]]); $\lim_{x\to0}\sin x/x=1$ ([[cor-sin-x-over-x-limit]]); and $\sin u\le u$ for $u\ge0$, since for $u>0$ the mean value theorem gives $\sin u=u\cos\xi$ for some $\xi\in(0,u)$ with $\cos\xi\le1$ ([[cor-mean-value-theorem]]).

[F4] A continuous real function on an interval assumes every value between its endpoint values ([[thm-intermediate-value]]).

[F5] A compact convex polyhedral cell is given by finitely many affine inequalities $\ell_j\ge0$; its nonempty faces arise by turning some of them into equalities, and each nonempty face is the cell itself or its intersection with a supporting hyperplane ([[def-finite-convex-cell-complex-and-linear-subdivision]]).

[F6] A finite abstract simplicial complex has a compact Hausdorff geometric realization, and a closed subspace of a compact space is compact ([[prop-a-finite-simplicial-complex-has-compact-hausdorff-realization]], [[lem-closed-subset-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], [[def-abstract-simplicial-complex]]).

[F7] For an isometric polyhedral gluing: the cells are compact convex polyhedral cells, the face relation and gluing isometries $h_{p,q}$ satisfy the cocycle and intersection conditions, the topology is the weak topology, and the chain metric is the infimum of chain lengths over points lying in common cells; its metric axioms, the agreement of its topology with the weak topology and properness and completeness under (H1)-(H3) are the conclusions of [[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]], the proper-target geodesic argument is recorded in [[thm-cg-proper-polyhedral-spaces-have-minimizing-geodesics]], and arc-length reparametrisation together with lower semicontinuity of length under uniform convergence is [[lem-cg-metric-target-length-reparametrization-and-lower-semicontinuity]] ([[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]).

[F8] Assume the Axiom of Choice; the proper-target Ascoli theorem states that a pointwise bounded equicontinuous sequence of continuous maps from a nonempty compact metric space into a proper metric space has a uniformly convergent subsequence, and a compact metric space is proper because its closed balls are closed, hence compact ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[cor-arzela-ascoli-subsequence-theorem-for-proper-metric-targets]], [[def-metric-compactness]], [[def-metric-space]], [[def-pointwise-uniform-and-uniformly-cauchy-convergence]], [[def-bilipschitz-embedding-and-bilipschitz-equivalence]], [[def-complete-metric-space]], [[def-geodesic-and-geodesic-metric-space]], [[def-upper-bound]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]]).

[F9] A continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact metric space attains its minimum and maximum, without Choice ([[thm-extreme-value-metric]]).

## Proof

1.1 Gram realisation. By [F1], $C=LL^{T}$ with $L$ invertible, so the rows $u_i$ of $L$ satisfy $u_i\cdot u_j=(LL^{T})_{ij}=c_{ij}$, in particular $|u_i|^{2}=c_{ii}=1$, and they are linearly independent because $\det L\neq0$. If $(u_i')$ is any family of unit vectors of a Euclidean space with $u_i'\cdot u_j'=c_{ij}$ and $\sum_i\lambda_iu_i=0$, then $\bigl|\sum_i\lambda_iu_i'\bigr|^{2}=\lambda^{T}C\lambda=\bigl|\sum_i\lambda_iu_i\bigr|^{2}=0$, so $\sum_i\lambda_iu_i'=0$; the two families therefore obey the same linear relations, the map $T$ sending $u_i$ to $u_i'$ is a well-defined linear isomorphism of their spans, and it preserves inner products because these are given by the Gram matrices on both sides; $T$ extends to a linear isometry of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ onto the span of the $u_i'$, which carries $K(C)$ onto the cone generated by the $u_i'$ and $\Sigma(C)$ onto its unit section. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 The two tangent-cone descriptions. Let $C$ be given by the nonconstant inequalities $\ell_j\ge0$ after the discarding prescribed in the definition, and let $p$ lie in the relative interior of the face $F$, with $I(F)$ the set of $j$ with $\ell_j(p)=0$ and $n_j=\nabla\ell_j/|\nabla\ell_j|$ inward. If $\xi$ satisfies $\langle\xi,n_j\rangle\ge0$ for all $j\in I(F)$, then for small $\varepsilon>0$ the point $p+\varepsilon\xi$ satisfies $\ell_j(p+\varepsilon\xi)=\ell_j(p)+\varepsilon\langle\nabla\ell_j,\xi\rangle=\varepsilon|\nabla\ell_j|\langle n_j,\xi\rangle\ge0$ for $j\in I(F)$ and $\ell_j(p+\varepsilon\xi)>0$ for $j\notin I(F)$ by continuity and $\ell_j(p)>0$, so $p+\varepsilon\xi\in C$ and $\xi$ is a limit of vectors $\lambda(x-p)$ with $x\in C$. Conversely, for $x\in C$ and $j\in I(F)$ the affine identity $\ell_j(x)=\ell_j(p)+\langle x-p,\nabla\ell_j\rangle$ gives $\langle x-p,n_j\rangle=\ell_j(x)/|\nabla\ell_j|\ge0$, so every $\lambda(x-p)$ with $\lambda\ge0$ lies in the closed cone $\{\xi:\langle\xi,n_j\rangle\ge0\ (j\in I(F))\}$; the two cones agree, and since an affine function nonnegative on $F$ vanishes at a relative interior point exactly when it vanishes throughout $F$, the active set does not depend on $p$, and the tangent cone $T_FC$ does not depend on the chosen point of the relative interior. The common kernel of the active gradients is $U(F)$: it contains $U(F)$ because the active functions vanish on $F$; conversely, for a vector in that kernel, sufficiently small displacements $p\pm\varepsilon\xi$ remain in $C$ and satisfy all active equalities, hence belong to $F$ by [F5], so $\xi\in U(F)$. [given, F5, F2, algebra]

1.3 The angular distance is a metric. For unit vectors $\xi,\eta$ the number $d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)=\arccos\langle\xi,\eta\rangle\in[0,\pi]$ is well defined by [F2], is symmetric, vanishes exactly when $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=1$, i.e. $\xi=\eta$, and is at most $\pi$. If $\xi,\zeta,\eta$ are unit, $\alpha:=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\zeta)$ and $\beta:=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\zeta,\eta)$ satisfy $\alpha+\beta\le\pi$, write (trivially if $\alpha=0$ or $\beta=0$) $\xi=\cos\alpha\,\zeta+\sin\alpha\,a$ and $\eta=\cos\beta\,\zeta+\sin\beta\,b$ with $a,b$ unit and orthogonal to $\zeta$ by [F2]; then $\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=\cos\alpha\cos\beta+\sin\alpha\sin\beta\langle a,b\rangle\ge\cos\alpha\cos\beta-\sin\alpha\sin\beta=\cos(\alpha+\beta)$ by [F2] and [F3], so $d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)\le\alpha+\beta$ because $\arccos$ is the inverse of the strictly decreasing $\cos$ on $[0,\pi]$; if $\alpha+\beta>\pi$ the same inequality is trivial. So $d_{\mathrm{ang}}$ is a metric of diameter at most $\pi$ on every set of unit vectors. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 The hemisphere functional. Since $(u_i)$ is a basis of $\mathbb R^{n+1}$ by step 1.1, there is a unique linear functional $\varphi$ with $\varphi(u_i)=1$ for all $i$, and $\varphi\bigl(\sum_i\lambda_iu_i\bigr)=\sum_i\lambda_i$ for all $\lambda$. If $x=\sum_i\lambda_iu_i\in K(C)\setminus\{0\}$ with $\lambda_i\ge0$, then some $\lambda_i>0$, so $\varphi(x)=\sum_i\lambda_i>0$; hence $\Sigma(C)\subseteq\{x\in S^n:\varphi(x)>0\}$ and $0\notin\Delta(C):=\{x\in K(C):\varphi(x)=1\}$, which is the set of convex combinations $\sum_i\lambda_iu_i$ with $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. In standard coordinates $\varphi(x)=w\cdot x$ for a unique nonzero vector $w$; [F2] gives $|\varphi(x)|\le|w||x|$, with equality at $x=w/|w|$, so $\lVert\varphi\rVert:=|w|$ is the finite operator norm. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

2.2 Schur complements. Since $|c_{i0}|<1$ for $i\ge1$ by [F2] and the linear independence of $u_i,u_0$ of step 1.1, the vectors $v_i:=(u_i-c_{i0}u_0)/\sqrt{1-c_{i0}^{2}}$ are well defined; they satisfy $v_i\cdot u_0=0$ and $|v_i|=1$, and expanding $v_i\cdot v_j$ gives $v_i\cdot v_j=\bigl(c_{ij}-c_{i0}c_{j0}\bigr)/\sqrt{(1-c_{i0}^{2})(1-c_{j0}^{2})}=c^{\mathrm{lk}}_{ij}$, in particular $c^{\mathrm{lk}}_{ii}=1$. Writing $w:=(-\sum_jc_{0j}x_j,\ x_1,\dots,x_n)$ for $x\in\mathbb R^{n}\setminus\{0\}$, the vector $w$ is nonzero and, with $c_{00}=1$, $w^{T}Cw=\sum_{i,j\ge1}c_{ij}x_ix_j-\bigl(\sum_jc_{0j}x_j\bigr)^{2}>0$; the quadratic form on the right is that of the unnormalised Schur complement $Z:=(c_{ij}-c_{i0}c_{j0})_{i,j\ge1}$, so $Z$ is positive definite, and $C^{\mathrm{lk}}=S^{-1/2}ZS^{-1/2}$ with the positive diagonal $S=\operatorname{diag}(1-c_{i0}^{2})$ is positive definite as well. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

2.3 The intrinsic path metric of a link. For a continuous path $\gamma$ in $V$ write $\ell(\gamma)$ for the supremum of $\sum_i|\gamma(t_{i+1})-\gamma(t_i)|$ over finite partitions of its parameter interval, and for $\xi,\eta\in\operatorname{Lk}_C(F)$ let $d_{\mathrm{int}}(\xi,\eta)$ be the infimum of $\ell(\gamma)$ over continuous paths in $\operatorname{Lk}_C(F)$ from $\xi$ to $\eta$. Let $\theta=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)$. The cone $N_FC$ is pointed: a vector and its negative lying in it satisfy every active facet equality, hence lie in the direction space of the affine hull of $F$, namely $U(F)$; orthogonality to $U(F)$ then forces the vector to be zero. Thus distinct unit directions are never antipodal and $0<\theta<\pi$. If $\theta>0$, pick a unit $w\in\xi^{\perp}$ with $\eta=\cos\theta\,\xi+\sin\theta\,w$ and put $\gamma(t):=\cos t\,\xi+\sin t\,w$ for $t\in[0,\theta]$; then $\gamma([0,\theta])\subseteq N_FC\cap S(V)$ because $\gamma(t)=\frac{\sin(\theta-t)}{\sin\theta}\xi+\frac{\sin t}{\sin\theta}\eta$ is a nonnegative combination of $\xi,\eta\in N_FC$ for $0\le t\le\theta$, and for every partition $\sum_i|\gamma(t_{i+1})-\gamma(t_i)|=\sum_i2\sin\frac{t_{i+1}-t_i}{2}\le\sum_i(t_{i+1}-t_i)=\theta$ while the uniform partition into $m$ parts has sum $2m\sin\frac{\theta}{2m}\to\theta$ as $m\to\infty$ by [F3]; hence $\ell(\gamma)=\theta$ and $d_{\mathrm{int}}(\xi,\eta)\le\theta$. Conversely let $\gamma:[a,b]\to N_FC\cap S(V)$ be any continuous path from $\xi$ to $\eta$ and let $f(t):=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\gamma(t))$, a continuous function with $f(a)=0$ and $f(b)=\theta$; for each $m\ge1$ successively applying [F4] on $[t_{i-1},b]$ gives $a=t_0\le t_1\le\cdots\le t_m=b$ with $f(t_i)=i\theta/m$, and then $\sum_i|\gamma(t_i)-\gamma(t_{i-1})|=\sum_i2\sin\frac{\varphi_i}{2}$ with $\varphi_i:=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_i))\ge|f(t_i)-f(t_{i-1})|=\theta/m$ by step 1.3 and [F2]; since $\varphi_i\in[0,\pi]$ and $\sin$ is strictly increasing on $[0,\pi/2]$, each term is at least $2\sin\frac{\theta}{2m}$, so $\ell(\gamma)\ge2m\sin\frac{\theta}{2m}\to\theta$ and $d_{\mathrm{int}}(\xi,\eta)\ge\theta$; for $\theta=0$ both numbers are $0$. Hence $d_{\mathrm{int}}=d_{\mathrm{ang}}$ on $\operatorname{Lk}_C(F)$, the link has diameter at most $\pi$, and $d_{\mathrm{ang}}$ is the intrinsic path metric of the round unit sphere restricted to the link. [step 1.2, step 1.3, F2, F3, F4, algebra]

2.4 Links of gluings. Let $X$ be an isometric polyhedral gluing with cells $C_p$ and let $F$ be a face. By step 1.2 the tangent cone $T_FC_p$ and the angular link $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_p}(F)$ depend only on $F$, and for $p\le q$ the isometry $h_{p,q}:C_p\to C_q$ maps $F$ onto $F$, so its linear part maps $T_FC_p$ isometrically into $T_FC_q$ and maps its normal section onto the corresponding link face that is compatible with the cocycle condition; the intersection condition makes the intersections of the resulting images correspond to the common faces, so the cell links glue to a well-defined quotient $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$, and replacing the point of $F$ changes nothing by step 1.2. The normal sections in cofaces $G>F$ inherit their polyhedral face incidences; when $X$ is simplicial these correspond to the simplices $G\setminus F$ of its combinatorial link. A general polyhedral face link need not itself be simplicial. [step 1.2, F7]

3.1 Radial coordinates. For $x\in\Delta(C)$ the vector $\rho(x)=x/|x|$ lies in $K(C)\cap S^n=\Sigma(C)$, and for $y\in\Sigma(C)$, written $y=\sum_i\lambda_iu_i$ with $\lambda_i\ge0$, the number $\varphi(y)=\sum_i\lambda_i>0$ is positive, the point $y/\varphi(y)=\sum_i(\lambda_i/\varphi(y))u_i$ lies in $\Delta(C)$ and normalises back to $y$; so $y\mapsto y/\varphi(y)$ is an inverse of $\rho$ and $\rho$ is a bijection $\Delta(C)\to\Sigma(C)$. It is injective because $x/|x|=x'/|x'|$ gives $x=ax'$ with $a=|x|/|x'|=\varphi(x)/\varphi(x')=1$. The coefficients $\lambda_i$ of a point of $\Delta(C)$ in the basis $(u_i)$ are unique, so every point of $\Sigma(C)$ has unique barycentric ray coordinates $\lambda_i\ge0$ with $\sum_i\lambda_i=1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, F2, algebra]

3.2 Iterated links and face compatibility. Let $A$ be a nonempty proper set of vertices (if it is the full set, the normal link is empty), put $P_A:=\operatorname{span}\{u_a:a\in A\}$, and write $b_i=(c_{ai})_{a\in A}$. The orthogonal projection of $u_i$ onto $P_A^\perp$ is $w_i=u_i-\sum_{a\in A}(C_A^{-1}b_i)_a u_a$: pairing with each $u_a$ gives zero, while the subtracted vector lies in $P_A$. Consequently $\langle w_i,w_j\rangle=c_{ij}-b_i^TC_A^{-1}b_j$, the Schur complement $Z$ of $C_A$, and $Z$ is positive definite since for nonzero $x$ on the complementary indices, $( -C_A^{-1}Bx,x)^TC(-C_A^{-1}Bx,x)=x^TZx>0$, where $B=(c_{ai})$. In particular all $w_i$ are nonzero; normalising them gives the diagonal-$1$ Gram matrix $z_{ij}/\sqrt{z_{ii}z_{jj}}$. These projected rays describe the normal link: at an interior point of the face, coefficients of the face vertices can vary in both directions, whereas coefficients of the remaining vertices must be nonnegative; quotienting out the face span therefore leaves exactly the cone generated by the $w_i$. For iteration, after removing a vertex, project the remaining vectors onto the perpendicular of its residual projected direction, not onto the perpendicular of the original vector. For a relative interior point $p$ of the spherical face, $p$ is a positive combination of its face vertices. A tangent vector $v$ with $v\perp p$ points into the simplex exactly when its coefficients outside $A$ are nonnegative: for small $\varepsilon>0$, the vector $p+\varepsilon v$ retains positive coefficients in $A$, and its normalisation has initial direction $v$, as $(p+\varepsilon v)/|p+\varepsilon v|=p+\varepsilon v+O(\varepsilon^2)$. Removing the tangent face directions $P_A\cap p^\perp$ leaves $P_A^\perp$ and the projected rays $w_i$, proving the asserted geometric link identification. Those residual directions obtained from an ordering of $A$ are orthogonal and span $P_A$ (each new residual is orthogonal to the preceding span and is nonzero by independence); their successive perpendicular projections therefore equal the single orthogonal projection onto $P_A^\perp$. Intermediate normalisations only rescale rays by positive factors, so the final normalised Gram matrix is the same in every order. Restricting to a subface retains the corresponding subset of remaining rays, while linking a further face enlarges $A$ and repeats this operation. [step 1.1, step 2.2, F2, algebra]

4.1 Lipschitz estimates and the chord bound. $\Delta(C)$ is a nonempty compact convex cell and avoids $0$ by step 2.1 and [F5]. The reverse norm triangle inequality makes $z\mapsto|z|$ continuous, so [F9] gives an attained minimum $m_0:=\min_{z\in\Delta(C)}|z|>0$, and for $x,y\in\Delta(C)$ the estimate $\bigl|\frac{x}{|x|}-\frac{y}{|y|}\bigr|\le\frac{|x-y|}{|x|}+\frac{\bigl||x|-|y|\bigr|}{|x|}\le\frac{2|x-y|}{m_0}$ holds. On $\Sigma(C)$ one has $|y|=1$ and $\varphi(y)\ge1$ (because $y=z/|z|$ with $z\in\Delta(C)$ and $|z|\le1$), so writing $y/{\varphi(y)}-y'/{\varphi(y')}=\frac{(y-y')}{\varphi(y)}+\frac{y'(\varphi(y')-\varphi(y))}{\varphi(y)\varphi(y')}$ gives $\bigl|\rho^{-1}(y)-\rho^{-1}(y')\bigr|\le|y-y'|+\lVert\varphi\rVert|y-y'|$. For unit vectors $\xi,\eta$ put $\theta:=d_{\mathrm{ang}}(\xi,\eta)=\arccos\langle\xi,\eta\rangle$; then $|\xi-\eta|^{2}=2-2\langle\xi,\eta\rangle=2-2\cos\theta=4\sin^{2}(\theta/2)$ by [F3], so the chord length is $\text{chord}=2\sin(\theta/2)\le\theta=\text{round}$, the last step because $\sin u\le u$ for $u\ge0$ by [F3]. For the reverse comparison note first that $\pi>2$: for $0<u\le\pi/2$ the mean value theorem gives $\sin u=u\cos\xi$ with $\xi\in(0,u)$, and $\cos\xi<\cos0=1$ because $\cos$ is strictly decreasing on $[0,\pi]$ [F3], so $\sin u<u$ and $1=\sin(\pi/2)<\pi/2$; the restriction of $\cos$ to $[0,\pi]$ is strictly decreasing with $\cos0=1$ and $\cos(\pi/2)=0$ by [F2] and [F3], so $2/\pi\in(0,1)$ and $u_{*}:=\arccos(2/\pi)$ is the unique point of $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos u_{*}=2/\pi$, lying in $(0,\pi/2)$; the function $f(u):=\sin u-2u/\pi$ therefore has $f'(u)=\cos u-2/\pi$ by [F3], positive on $[0,u_{*})$ and negative on $(u_{*},\pi/2]$. The mean value theorem [F3] gives $f(u)\ge f(0)=0$ on $[0,u_{*}]$ and $f(u)\ge f(\pi/2)=0$ on $[u_{*},\pi/2]$ (with $f(\pi/2)=\sin(\pi/2)-1=0$), that is, $\sin u\ge2u/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$. Applied to $u=\theta/2\le\pi/2$ this gives $\theta\le(\pi/2)\,2\sin(\theta/2)=(\pi/2)\,\text{chord}$, so the chord and round distances satisfy $\text{chord}\le\text{round}\le\frac{\pi}{2}\,\text{chord}$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, F2, F3, F5, F9, algebra]

4.2 The finite spherical complex and the descent map. By steps 1.1 and 3.2 the identifications $\Sigma(C_\tau)\to\Sigma(C_\sigma)$ for $\tau\subseteq\sigma$ are well-defined isometries onto faces, so $X=|K|_C$ is a well-defined quotient of the finite disjoint union of the compact simplices $\Sigma(C_\sigma)$ by finitely many face identifications. Let $Y$ be the corresponding isometric polyhedral gluing with cells $\Delta(C_\sigma)$; the cell maps $\Phi_\sigma:\Sigma(C_\sigma)\to\Delta(C_\sigma)$, $\Phi_\sigma(y):=y/\varphi_\sigma(y)$, agree on identified faces, because $\varphi_\sigma$ takes the value $1$ on all vertices in $\sigma$ and hence restricts to $\varphi_\tau$; so the $\Phi_\sigma$ descend to a bijection $\Phi:X\to Y$. [step 1.1, step 3.2]

4.3 The link of a face of a finite spherical complex. Let $X=|K|_C$ be a finite spherical complex and let $F$ be the face corresponding to a nonempty simplex $\sigma$. The cells of $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ are the links $\operatorname{Lk}_{C_\tau}(F)$ of the spherical simplices containing $F$; by step 3.2 each is again a spherical simplex, with Gram matrix obtained by iterating the Schur complements of step 2.2 over the vertices of $\sigma$, and these matrices are positive definite of diagonal $1$ and compatible with faces, because passing to a smaller coface retains a subset of the projected rays, while linking a further face enlarges the projected-out span; the identifications are those induced by $K$ and are isometric by step 2.4. Hence $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is again presented as a finite spherical complex, and clauses (i)-(v) apply to it. [step 2.2, step 3.2, step 2.4]

5.1 Bi-Lipschitz equivalence of the model and its spherical simplex. With $m_0=\min_{z\in\Delta(C)}|z|>0$ and the operator norm $\lVert\varphi\rVert$, steps 3.1 and 4.1 give for $y=\rho(x)$, $y'=\rho(x')$ the bounds $d_{\mathrm{ang}}(y,y')\le\frac{\pi}{2}|y-y'|\le\frac{\pi}{m_0}|x-x'|$ and $|x-x'|\le(1+\lVert\varphi\rVert)|y-y'|\le(1+\lVert\varphi\rVert)d_{\mathrm{ang}}(y,y')$, so $\rho$ is a bi-Lipschitz equivalence between the Euclidean metric of $\Delta(C)$ and the round metric of $\Sigma(C)$ with the constant $L:=\max\{\pi/m_0,\,1+\lVert\varphi\rVert\}$. [step 3.1, step 4.1, F8]

6.1 The radial map is bi-Lipschitz for the chain metrics. If $K$ has no nonempty simplex then $X=Y=\emptyset$ and there are no distance estimates to check. Otherwise, by step 5.1 each $\Phi_\sigma$ is bi-Lipschitz with constant $L_\sigma=\max\{\pi/m_0^\sigma,1+\lVert\varphi_\sigma\rVert\}$, and the finitely many simplices of $K$ give finite maxima $L':=\max_\sigma(1+\lVert\varphi_\sigma\rVert)$ and $L'':=\max_\sigma\pi/m_0^\sigma$. Every chain in $X$ maps under $\Phi$ to a chain in $Y$ whose one-step Euclidean distances are at most $L'$ times the angular distances of the original chain, so $d_Y(\Phi x,\Phi x')\le L'\,d_X(x,x')$; likewise every chain in $Y$ is the image of a chain in $X$ with angular steps at most $L''$ times the Euclidean steps, so $d_X(x,x')\le L''\,d_Y(\Phi x,\Phi x')$. Hence $\Phi$ is a bi-Lipschitz equivalence of the chain metrics. [step 5.1, step 4.2]

7.1 Component metrics and topology. The classes under being joined by a chain are finite in number. Each is a union of whole cells: any two points of one cell are joined by a single chain step. The trace of a class on every cell is therefore the entire cell or empty, so every class is weakly open and closed; each class is path connected by cell arcs, hence is a connected component. The corresponding component of $Y$ satisfies (H1)-(H3), so [F7] supplies its metric, weak topology, properness and completeness; step 6.1 transfers these properties to the component of $X$. Each component is compact as a quotient of finitely many compact cells. Its chain metric is a length metric: a chain can be replaced by cell arcs, whose $d$-length is at most its chain length because $d\le d_{\mathrm{ang}}$ on a cell, while every path has length at least the distance of its endpoints. Cellwise radial homeomorphisms descend to a homeomorphism for the quotient (weak) topologies, and the components are open and closed, so $X$ and $Y$ are compact finite disjoint unions of the component metric spaces. Distances between distinct components remain auxiliary infinities; no real-valued global chain metric is asserted. The empty case is vacuous. [step 4.2, step 6.1, F6, F7]

8.1 Minimizing geodesics (AC). Assume AC, fix $x,y$ in one component and put $R=d(x,y)$. AC selects, for each $n\ge1$, a chain of length $L_n<R+1/n$; the infimum definition gives $R\le L_n$. Replace its steps by cell arcs, traversed at angular speed $L_n$ on $[0,1]$ (use the constant path for $L_n=0$). Each arc lies in its cell by the nonnegative-combination argument of step 2.3 applied to $K(C_\sigma)$, and the triangle inequality gives $d(\gamma_n(s),\gamma_n(t))\le L_n|s-t|$, hence $R\le\ell(\gamma_n)\le L_n$. The paths are equicontinuous and pointwise bounded in the compact proper component of step 7.1. The domain $[0,1]$ is compact as a nonempty bounded one-dimensional polyhedral cell by [F5]; with AC it satisfies [F8], giving a uniformly convergent subsequence with continuous limit $\gamma$ and endpoints $x,y$. Since $R\le\ell(\gamma_n)\le L_n\to R$, lower semicontinuity [F7] gives $\ell(\gamma)\le R$, while the endpoint partition gives the reverse bound. Reparametrise by arc length using [F7]. For $0\le u\le v\le R$, the resulting path $\bar\gamma$ is $1$-Lipschitz, and $R=d(x,y)\le u+d(\bar\gamma(u),\bar\gamma(v))+R-v$ forces $d(\bar\gamma(u),\bar\gamma(v))\ge v-u$; thus equality holds and $\bar\gamma$ is a minimizing geodesic. AC is used for the sequence selection and for Ascoli. [step 2.3, step 7.1, F5, F7, F8]

9.1 The $+\infty$ convention. Let $F$ be a face of a finite spherical complex $X$. By step 4.3 the angular link $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$ is a finite spherical complex, so step 7.1 applies to it: being joined by a chain of directions inside common cells is reflexive (singleton chains), symmetric (reverse a chain) and transitive (concatenate chains), hence an equivalence relation on $\operatorname{Lk}_X(F)$; the infimum $d_{\mathrm{path}}(x,y)$ is finite whenever a chain from $x$ to $y$ exists and is a metric on each class by step 7.1, while for $x,y$ in different classes no chain joins them and $d_{\mathrm{path}}(x,y)=+\infty$. This infinite value is auxiliary notation, not an ordinary distance of the metric-space definition, and it is replaced by $\pi$ in the truncated metric of [[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]]. [step 4.3, step 7.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Choice.** In step 8.1 the Axiom of Choice selects the sequence of near-minimizing chains and supplies the hypothesis of the proper-target Ascoli theorem; clauses (i), (ii), (iv), (v) and (vi), and the metric/topology conclusions of (iii), are choice-free; the geodesic conclusion of (iii) assumes AC.
- **The two model metrics.** Steps 4.1 and 5.1 compare the round metric of $\Sigma(C)$ with the Euclidean metric of $\Delta(C)$ through the chord metric; the constants are explicit ($\pi/2$ for round versus chord, $\max\{\pi/m_0,1+\lVert\varphi\rVert\}$ in step 5.1) and are not asserted to be optimal.
- **No curvature claim.** The link metric is proved to be a metric and the intrinsic metric of the round sphere; no CAT(1) assertion is made here, and the $D_\pi$-geodesic convention remains a hypothesis of [[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]] and [[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]].
