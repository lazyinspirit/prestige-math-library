## Example
Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC), Countable Choice (CC), and Dependent Choice (DC). 
For every integer $m\ge1$, give $\mathbb Q$ the modulus consisting of the finite ideal $(m)$ and its real place. Then the ray class group is canonically $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$, and its ray class field is $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$. For every rational prime $\ell\nmid m$, the Artin image of the prime ideal $(\ell)$ is the automorphism $\sigma_\ell(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^\ell$. For $m=1$ or $m=2$, both the ray class group and the Galois group are trivial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Axiom of Choice (AC), Countable Choice (CC), and Dependent Choice (DC); An integer $m\ge1$, a primitive $m$-th root of unity $\zeta_m$, and the modulus $(m,\{\infty\})$.

[F1] The ray class group is fractional ideals prime to $(m)$ modulo principal ideals generated by positive rationals congruent to $1$ modulo $m$. [[def-ray-class-group]]

[F2] $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)/\mathbb Q$ has Galois group canonically isomorphic to $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$ by $\sigma_a(\zeta_m)=\zeta_m^a$. [[cor-the-galois-group-of-a-rational-cyclotomic-field]]

[F3] For $\ell\nmid m$, the prime $\ell$ is unramified in $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$ and its arithmetic Frobenius is $\sigma_\ell$. [[cor-unramified-prime-decomposition-in-a-cyclotomic-field]] [[lem-arithmetic-frobenius-on-a-cyclotomic-field]]

[F4] The ray class field is the unique finite abelian extension corresponding to the modulus subgroup, and its Artin map is an isomorphism. [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]]


[A1] The ray class field and reciprocity input [F4] inherits AC, Countable Choice, and DC from global class field theory; it is used in step 2.1. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]] [[thm-number-field-ray-class-field-artin-isomorphism]]
## Proof

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Every nonzero fractional ideal of $\mathbb Q$ is $(q)$ for a unique positive rational $q$. If it is prime to $m$, its residue $q\bmod m$ is a unit. The map $[(q)]\mapsto q\bmod m$ is a homomorphism; it is well-defined because the positive generator is unique. It is surjective since every unit class modulo $m$ has a positive integer representative coprime to $m$. Its kernel consists exactly of positive rational generators congruent to $1$ modulo $m$, so [F1] identifies the ray class group with $(\mathbb Z/m\mathbb Z)^\times$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 By [F2], the cyclotomic Galois group has the same order and is identified with the same unit group. For every prime $\ell\nmid m$, [F3] says its Frobenius is $\sigma_\ell$, exactly the image of $[(\ell)]$ under the isomorphism in step 1.1. Such prime classes generate the ray class group because every positive rational prime factor of a numerator or denominator outside $m$ supplies a prime ideal generator. Thus the Artin correspondence for $\mathbb Q(\zeta_m)$ has kernel the ray congruence subgroup, and [F4] identifies it as the ray class field. The cases $m=1,2$ have a trivial unit group and give $\mathbb Q$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra, A1]

3.1 For a finite check at $m=5$, the group is $(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^\times=\{1,2,3,4\}$, the extension has degree $4$, and the prime class $(2)$ maps to $\sigma_2$ with $\sigma_2(\zeta_5)=\zeta_5^2$. The order of $2$ modulo $5$ is $4$, agreeing with the residue degree and Frobenius order. [F2, F3, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
