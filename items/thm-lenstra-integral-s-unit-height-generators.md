## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $K$ be a number field of degree $n$, signature $(r_1,r_2)$, and discriminant $\Delta=d_K$, and put $d=(2/\pi)^{r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}$. Let $T$ be a finite set of nonzero prime ideals of $\mathcal O_K$ containing every prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ with $N\mathfrak p\le d$. Put $m_T=\max(\{1\}\cup\{N\mathfrak p:\mathfrak p\in T\})$ and $U_T=\mathcal O_{K,T}^{\times}$, where $T$ contains only finite prime ideals.

For $x\in K^\times$, define $\operatorname{den}(x)=\{y\in K:y(\mathcal O_K+\mathcal O_Kx)\subseteq\mathcal O_K\}$ and the unrooted relative height
$$H_K([x:1])=N\operatorname{den}(x)\prod_{\sigma\ \mathrm{real}}\max\{1,|\sigma(x)|\}\prod_{\tau\ \mathrm{complex\ up\ to\ conjugation}}\max\{1,|\tau(x)|\}^{2}.$$
The denominator is a nonzero integral ideal: $\mathcal O_K+\mathcal O_Kx$ is a fractional ideal containing $\mathcal O_K$, and its inverse is a nonzero ideal contained in $\mathcal O_K$. Then $U_T$ is generated, as a multiplicative group, by the elements $x\in\mathcal O_K\cap U_T$ satisfying $H_K([x:1])\le d^2m_T$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, $K$, its signature and discriminant, $T$, and the definitions of $U_T$, $\operatorname{den}(x)$, and $H_K([x:1])$ in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed both by the Minkowski convex-body suppliers and by the compact-coset transversal lemma ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[lem-compact-fundamental-domain-generates-discrete-subgroup]]).

[F1] For finite $T$ of finite prime ideals, $U_T$ consists of the nonzero $x\in K$ whose principal fractional ideals have valuation zero outside $T$ ([[def-s-integers-and-s-units-of-a-number-field]]).

[F2] The unscaled Minkowski embedding sends a number field to $\mathbb R^{r_1}\times\mathbb C^{r_2}$; the covolume of an integral ideal lattice is $2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}\,N\mathfrak a$ ([[def-minkowski-embedding-of-a-number-field]], [[thm-covolume-of-an-ideal-lattice]]).

[F3] The norm of a nonzero principal integral ideal is the absolute field norm, and ideal norms multiply ([[thm-principal-ideal-norm-is-absolute-field-norm]], [[thm-ideal-norm-is-multiplicative]]).

[F4] A nonzero ideal factors uniquely into finitely many prime ideals; every integral ideal of norm at most a fixed real bound belongs to a finite set ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]], [[lem-finitely-many-number-field-ideals-of-bounded-norm]]).

[F5] The archimedean product-body lemma gives a nonzero lattice point with all real absolute values and complex moduli at most $t$ when $t^n=(4/\pi)^{r_2}$ times the lattice covolume ([[lem-archimedean-product-body-minkowski-bound]]).

[F6] A compact fundamental domain and a discrete subgroup yield generators in $H\cap F^{-1}PF$ when $G$ is generated by $P$ and its identity component ([[lem-compact-fundamental-domain-generates-discrete-subgroup]]).

[F7] The field norm is the product over embeddings in the separable number-field case ([[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]]).

[F8] The denominator ideal is nonzero and integral, with $v_{\mathfrak q}(\operatorname{den}(x))=\max(0,-v_{\mathfrak q}(x))$ at every finite prime ([[lem-denominator-ideal-valuation-in-a-number-field]]).

[F9] The relative height formula is invariant under common nonzero scaling of the projective coordinate tuple, so it defines a value on $[x:1]$ ([[lem-relative-projective-height-coordinate-invariance]]).

## Proof



1.1 Apply [F5] to the full lattice $\sigma(\mathcal O_K)$, whose covolume is $2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}$. It gives a nonzero integral $\alpha$ with $|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(\alpha)|=\prod_{\sigma\ \mathrm{real}}|\sigma(\alpha)|\prod_{\tau\ \mathrm{complex}}|\tau(\alpha)|^2\le d$. The left side is a positive integer by [F3], so $d\ge1$. [A1, F2, F3, F5, F7, given]

1.2 Let $K_\infty=K\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\mathbb R\cong\mathbb R^{r_1}\times\mathbb C^{r_2}$ with $N_\infty(a)=\prod_i|a_i|^{n_i}$, where $n_i=1$ at real places and $2$ at complex places. Let $I_T$ be the group of fractional ideals generated by primes in $T$, with norm extended multiplicatively from integral ideals. Give the ambient product $\widetilde G=K_\infty^\times\times I_T$ its product topology, with $I_T$ discrete, and set $G=V_T=\{(a,\mathfrak a)\in\widetilde G:N_\infty(a)=N\mathfrak a\}$. Define $i_T(x)=(\sigma_\infty(x),(x))$ for $x\in U_T$. Then $G$ is a subgroup of the Hausdorff product and $i_T(U_T)\subseteq G$, since $(x)$ is supported on $T$ and its norm equals the absolute field norm. [F1, F3, F7, given]

1.3 The subgroup $i_T(U_T)$ is discrete. The ambient product is locally compact, and $G$ is closed in it because the norm equality is closed on each discrete ideal-coordinate slice; hence $G$ is locally compact. A compact subset of $G$ meets only finitely many ideal coordinates because $I_T$ is discrete. For a fixed principal ideal coordinate $(x)$, all its generators $x$ lie in that fractional ideal; its image is a full lattice by [F2] and the full-lattice theorem, so a compact archimedean set meets it finitely. Thus every compact neighborhood in $G$ meets $i_T(U_T)$ finitely, which isolates each subgroup point. [F1, F2, given]

1.4 For a fractional ideal $I$, choose $0\ne u\in\mathcal O_K$ with $uI$ integral. The integral covolume formula [F2], together with $\sigma(ux)=L_u\sigma(x)$ and $|\det L_u|=|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(u)|$, gives $\operatorname{covol}(\sigma(I))=2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}\,NI$, where $NI=N(uI)/|N_{K/\mathbb Q}(u)|$. In particular $N(\mathfrak a^{-1})=1/N\mathfrak a$. For $(a,\mathfrak a)\in G$, scaling $\sigma(\mathfrak a^{-1})$ by the archimedean coordinates of $a$ therefore gives covolume $N_\infty(a)\,2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}/N\mathfrak a=2^{-r_2}\sqrt{|\Delta|}$. [F2, F3, F7, given, algebra]

1.5 Let $P$ consist of the pairs $((\epsilon_i),\mathcal O_K)$ with each real or complex coordinate $\epsilon_i\in\{1,-1\}$, together with $(((N\mathfrak p)^{1/n},\ldots,(N\mathfrak p)^{1/n}),\mathfrak p)$ for $\mathfrak p\in T$. This finite set contains the identity. The prime-coordinate elements and their inverses generate every ideal coordinate in $I_T$. After removing those factors, an element has ideal coordinate $\mathcal O_K$ and archimedean norm one. Its real signs are removed by one of the sign-coordinate elements. The remaining subgroup has positive real coordinates and nonzero complex coordinates with product norm one; taking logarithms of the positive real coordinates and complex moduli identifies the modulus part with a real hyperplane, while the complex arguments form a product of circles. Both are connected, so this subgroup is connected and lies in $G^\circ$. Hence $G$ is generated by $P\cup G^\circ$. [given, algebra]

1.6 Apply [F6] with $H=i_T(U_T)$ and $F=F_T$. Every generator obtained from $H\cap F_T^{-1}PF_T$ has a representation [given]
$$ (a,(a))=(b,\mathfrak b)^{-1}(y,\mathfrak y)(c,\mathfrak c),$$
where both outer pairs lie in $F_T$ and the middle pair lies in $P$. Let $E$ be the set of archimedean coordinates where $|a_i|>1$, and put $n_E=\sum_{i\in E}n_i$. The bounds in $F_T$ give
$$\prod_{i\in E}|c_i|^{n_i}\le d^{n_E/n},\quad
\prod_{i\in E}|b_i|^{-n_i}=N\mathfrak b^{-1}\prod_{i\notin E}|b_i|^{n_i}\le N\mathfrak b^{-1}d^{1-n_E/n},\quad
\prod_{i\in E}|y_i|^{n_i}\le m_T^{n_E/n}.$$
At each finite prime $\mathfrak q$, [F8] gives the exponent of $\operatorname{den}(a)$ as $\max(0,-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q}(a))$. The displayed ideal identity gives $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q}(a)=-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q}(\mathfrak b)+\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q}(\mathfrak y)+\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak q}(\mathfrak c)$, with the last two exponents nonnegative, so $\operatorname{den}(a)$ divides $\mathfrak b$ and $N\operatorname{den}(a)\le N\mathfrak b$. Using the height formula in the Statement and the preceding archimedean product bound yields $H_K([a:1])\le d m_T$. [F3, F4, F6, F8, given, algebra]

2.1 Define $F_T$ to consist of $(b,\mathfrak b)\in V_T$ with $\mathfrak b\subseteq\mathcal O_K$, $N\mathfrak b\le d$, and every archimedean coordinate of $b$ of absolute value at most $d^{1/n}$. By [F4], only finitely many ideals can occur. For each fixed ideal, the coordinate conditions are closed and bounded; the product equality $N_\infty(b)=N\mathfrak b\ge1$, together with the upper coordinate bounds and $d\ge1$, bounds every coordinate away from zero. Thus $F_T$ is a finite union of compact sets and is compact. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Put $\mathfrak d=\operatorname{den}(a)$. It is an integral ideal supported on $T$ by [F1] and [F8]. Apply [F5] to its ideal lattice to obtain $0\ne\beta\in\mathfrak d$ with all archimedean coordinates at most $(dN\mathfrak d)^{1/n}$. The ideal $(\beta)\mathfrak d^{-1}$ is integral and has norm at most $d$, by [F3], [F5], and the principal-ideal norm formula; its prime factors therefore lie in $T$. Thus $\beta$ is an integral $T$-unit. Since $\beta\in\operatorname{den}(a)$, also $\beta a\in\mathcal O_K$, and it is a $T$-unit because both $a$ and $\beta$ have no prime support outside $T$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 1.6]

3.1 Apply [F5] to that lattice. It gives a nonzero point $b=a c$ with $c\in\mathfrak a^{-1}$ and every coordinate of $b$ bounded by $d^{1/n}$. The ideal $\mathfrak b=c\mathfrak a$ is integral, has $N\mathfrak b=N_\infty(b)\le d$, and is supported on primes of norm at most $d$, hence on $T$. Therefore $(c)=\mathfrak b\mathfrak a^{-1}$ is supported on $T$, so $c\in U_T$, $(b,\mathfrak b)\in F_T$, and $(a,\mathfrak a)=(b,\mathfrak b)i_T(c^{-1})$. Thus $G=F_Ti_T(U_T)$. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.4]

4.1 Since $\beta$ and $\beta a$ are integral, their denominator ideals are $\mathcal O_K$. The height formula and $dN\mathfrak d\ge1$ give $H_K([\beta:1])\le dN\mathfrak d\le d^2$. For each archimedean coordinate, $\max(1,|\beta_i a_i|)\le\max(1,|\beta_i|)\max(1,|a_i|)$; multiplying with the real/complex weights gives $\prod_i\max(1,|\beta_i a_i|)^{n_i}\le(dN\mathfrak d)H_K([a:1])/N\mathfrak d$. Therefore $H_K([\beta a:1])\le dH_K([a:1])\le d^2m_T$. Since $a=(\beta a)/\beta$, every generator from step 1.6 is a quotient of integral $T$-units within the claimed height bound. Those integral $T$-units therefore generate $U_T$. [F3, F7, step 1.1, step 1.6, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
