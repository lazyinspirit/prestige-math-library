## Statement

Let $A$ be a Noetherian local domain with maximal ideal $(a,b)$, and suppose $b$ is a nonzerodivisor on $A/(a)$. Let $e\ge1$, and suppose $A$ has a $\mathbb Z/e\mathbb Z$ grading in which $a,b$ have weights $1,-1$. Put $B=A_0$, $s=ab$ and $R=B[s^{-1}]$. Then every invertible $R$-module is free of rank one; equivalently $\operatorname{Pic}(R)=0$. No invertibility of $e$ and no Axiom of Choice are assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian local domain, the two parameters and the finite congruence grading in the Statement, and an invertible $R$-module.

## Proof

1.1 The degree-zero subring $B$ is a domain. Embed the given invertible $R$-module into its one-dimensional generic fibre, represent it by an invertible fractional ideal $I\subset\operatorname{Frac}(B)$, and choose finitely many generators $f_1,\ldots,f_m$ with $f_1\ne0$. A nonzero common denominator $d\in B$ puts all $df_i$ in $B$. Set $J=(df_1,\ldots,df_m)A$; it is a finitely generated graded ideal generated in degree zero. [given, choose, construct, algebra]

2.1 For a nonzero fractional ideal $M$ of $A$ write $M^{-1}=\{x\in\operatorname{Frac}(A):xM\subset A\}$ and put $N=(J^{-1})^{-1}$. Both inverse modules are finite: for instance, multiplication by a nonzero generator embeds $J^{-1}$ into the Noetherian module $A$. They are graded because the denominator may be taken to be the nonzero weight-zero element $df_1$, and homogeneous components of a series in $A$ remain in $A$. Moreover $N$ is reflexive: $J\subseteq N$ gives $N^{-1}\subseteq J^{-1}$, while the definition of $N$ gives the reverse inclusion; hence $N=(N^{-1})^{-1}$. As a fractional ideal, $N$ is torsion-free of rank one. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The sequence $a,b$ is regular on $N$. Multiplication by $a$ is injective because $A$ is a domain and $N$ is torsion-free. If $bz\in aN$ for $z\in N$, then for every $\phi\in N^{-1}=\operatorname{Hom}_A(N,A)$ one has $b\phi(z)\in aA$. The hypothesis on $A/(a)$ implies $\phi(z)\in aA$. Thus $z/a$ pairs integrally with every $\phi\in N^{-1}$, so $z/a\in(N^{-1})^{-1}=N$ and $z\in aN$. [given, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The Koszul complex of $a,b$ is a free resolution of the residue field $A/(a,b)$. Tensoring it with $N$, the positive first homology vanishes: if $ax+by=0$, reduction modulo $aN$ and step 3.1 give $y=az$, whence $x=-bz$. Thus $\operatorname{Tor}_1^A(N,A/(a,b))=0$. Choose finitely many lifts of a basis of $N/(a,b)N$, giving a surjection $A^r\to N$ with finite kernel $K$. Tensoring $0\to K\to A^r\to N\to0$ with the residue field identifies $K/(a,b)K$ with that vanishing Tor group. Nakayama's finite determinant argument gives $K=0$, so $N$ is free of rank one. [step 2.1, step 3.1, choose, algebra]

5.1 Since $N$ is graded and principal as a fractional ideal, it has a homogeneous generator. To see this, take a generator $g$, multiply it by a nonzero degree-zero element to make it integral, and decompose $g=\sum_r g_r$ into the finitely many weights. Each $g_r=gc_r$ lies in $N$, and $\sum_r c_r=1$. The local ring $A$ has a unit among the $c_r$, so one $g_r$ is a homogeneous generator. Localization commutes with the two inverse operations for the finitely generated ideals involved. After inverting $s$, the extension $J A[s^{-1}]=dI A[s^{-1}]$ is invertible and hence equals its double dual, so it is generated by this homogeneous element. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 In $A[s^{-1}]$ the element $a$ is a unit of weight one, since $a^{-1}=b/s$. Multiplying the homogeneous generator by a suitable power of $a$ makes it degree zero. The degree-zero subring of $A[s^{-1}]$ is $R$, since the denominator $s$ has weight zero; the degree-zero part of $dI A[s^{-1}]$ is $dI$ because its generators have degree zero. Therefore $dI$ is generated over $R$ by the degree-zero generator, and $I$ is principal. The original module is free. All choices are finite for the given module, and the grading argument works even if the characteristic divides $e$. [step 1.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
