## Example

Take $W=\{1\}$ and $\dim\mathfrak h=1$. Then $H_{1,0}(W,\mathfrak h)$ is the first Weyl algebra, with polynomial representation $\mathbb C[x]$ on which $x$ acts by multiplication and $y$ acts by $d/dx$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The one-dimensional reflection datum with trivial group.

[F1] [[def-rational-cherednik-algebra]]: $H_{t,c}$ is generated by $x,y$ with $[y,x]=t x(y)-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)\alpha_s^\vee(x)s$.

[F2] [[thm-rational-cherednik-pbw-theorem]]: the ordered monomials $x^a w y^b$ form a basis of $H_{t,c}$.

[F3] [[def-rational-cherednik-polynomial-representation]]: $P_c=\Delta_c(\mathbf 1)$ has underlying vector space $\mathbb C[x]$.

[F4] [[lem-rational-cherednik-polynomial-representation-dunkl-formula]]: on $P_c$, $y$ acts by $\partial_y-\sum_s c_s\alpha_s(y)\alpha_s^{-1}(1-s)$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** [F1]–[F4] and a basis $x\in\mathfrak h^*$ with dual $y\in\mathfrak h$.

1.1 Since $S=\varnothing$ for $W=\{1\}$ and $x(y)=1$, [F1] reduces to $[y,x]=1$ with no other generators. This is the defining presentation of the first Weyl algebra; [F2] also gives its basis $\{x^a y^b:a,b\ge0\}$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.2 In [F4] the reflection sum is empty, so $y=\partial_y=d/dx$ and $x$ is multiplication by $x$ on [F3]. Directly, $(d/dx)(xf)-x(d/dx)f=f$ for every polynomial $f$, verifying the Weyl relation in this representation. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 Thus the trivial-group rational Cherednik algebra is the first Weyl algebra and its polynomial standard module is the usual polynomial representation. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
