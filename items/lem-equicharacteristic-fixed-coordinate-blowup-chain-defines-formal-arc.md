## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Dependent Choice
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]]). Let
$(B,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$ be an equicharacteristic Noetherian local domain, and
let $(B_n,\mathfrak m_n)$, $n\ge0$, be the local rings at a successive
infinite chain of point blowups, $B_0=B$, all with residue field $\kappa$: for
every $n$ the ring $B_{n+1}$ is the local ring at a point of the blowup
$\operatorname{Bl}_{\mathfrak m_n}(B_n)$ of the closed point, the structural
maps $B_n\to B_{n+1}$ are local, and $B_{n+1}/\mathfrak m_{n+1}=\kappa$, compatibly with the specified residue-field maps.
Suppose that one element $t\in\mathfrak m$ generates the pullback of every
centre ideal on the next local ring:

$$\mathfrak m_nB_{n+1}=tB_{n+1}\qquad(n\ge0).$$

Then there is a nonsingular formal arc $B\to V$, extended from a surjection
$\widehat B\to V$, whose point-blowup centres are the given ones. Here $V$ is a
complete discrete valuation ring with residue field $\kappa$ and uniformizer
the image of $t$; no chosen coefficient-field identification is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An equicharacteristic Noetherian local domain $(B,\mathfrak m,\kappa)$, the chain of local rings $(B_n,\mathfrak m_n)$ of successive point blowups with all residue fields $\kappa$, and $t\in\mathfrak m$ with $\mathfrak m_nB_{n+1}=tB_{n+1}$ for every $n$.

[F1] *Standard charts of an affine blowup.* The blowup of $\operatorname{Spec}A$ along $I=(f_0,\dots,f_r)$ is covered by the affine charts $\operatorname{Spec}A[I/f_i]$, so the local rings of a blowup are localizations of these chart rings; different generating families give the same blowup. ([[thm-affine-blowup-standard-charts]])

[F2] *Universal property of the blowup.* If $f\colon Y\to X$ is an $X$-scheme and $f^{-1}(Z)$ is an effective Cartier divisor on $Y$, where $Z$ is the zero scheme of the blown-up ideal, then $f$ factors uniquely through $\operatorname{Bl}_ZX\to X$. ([[thm-blowup-universal-property]])

[F3] *Completions of Noetherian local rings.* The $\mathfrak m$-adic completion $\widehat R$ of a Noetherian local ring is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m\widehat R$, the same residue field, and the completion map is faithfully flat. ([[thm-completion-of-a-noetherian-local-ring]])

[F4] *Dimension one regular equals DVR.* A nonzero Noetherian local ring of dimension one is regular if and only if it is a discrete valuation ring. ([[thm-one-dimensional-regular-local-rings-are-dvrs]])

[F5] The Axiom of Choice and the Axiom of Dependent Choice are assumed; they also supply the successive choices of residue lifts in step 8.1. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 *The directed union.* By [F1], each $B_{n+1}$ is a localization of a chart $B_n[\mathfrak m_n/f]$ for a nonzero $f\in\mathfrak m_n$. Such a chart is a subring of $\operatorname{Frac}(B_n)$, so its localization is a domain containing $B_n$ and with the same fraction field. Thus all $B_n$ embed compatibly in $\operatorname{Frac}(B)$, and locality gives $\mathfrak m_{n+1}\cap B_n=\mathfrak m_n$. The union $U=\bigcup_nB_n$ is a local domain with maximal ideal $\mathfrak n=\bigcup_n\mathfrak m_n$: this union is a proper ideal, and every element outside it is already a unit in a ring $B_n$. The compatible residue-field identifications give $U/\mathfrak n=\kappa$. Moreover $\mathfrak m\ne0$, since blowing up the zero ideal has no points by [F1]; injectivity and $\mathfrak mB_1=tB_1$ therefore imply $t\ne0$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 *The maximal ideal is generated by $t$.* For $k>n$ the hypothesis gives $\mathfrak m_nB_k=tB_k$, hence $\mathfrak m_nU=tU$. Since extending each $\mathfrak m_n$ to $U$ stays inside $\mathfrak n$, their union is still $\mathfrak n$, and consequently $\mathfrak n=tU$. The element $t$ is a nonzero nonunit in the domain $U$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 *Successive quotients.* For $j\ge0$, cancellation in $U$ identifies $U/tU$ with $t^jU/t^{j+1}U$ by multiplication by $t^j$. In particular $t^j\notin t^{j+1}U$, since otherwise cancellation would make $t$ a unit. Thus $U/t^NU$ has a filtration of length $N$ whose factors are $\kappa$; it is a local Artinian ring for every $N\ge1$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 *The inverse limit.* Put $V=\varprojlim_{j\ge1}U/t^jU$, with projections $\pi_N:V\to U/t^NU$. Each $\pi_N$ is surjective: a representative $u\in U$ of any prescribed class gives the compatible system of its residues. This alone does not identify the kernel; that identification follows from regularity of $t$ in $U$. [step 3.1, construct]

5.1 *Compatible division and kernels.* Fix $N\ge1$. Multiplication by $t^N$ identifies $U/t^jU$ with $t^NU/t^{N+j}U$ for every $j\ge1$, by cancellation in $U$. If $v=(v_j)_j\in\ker\pi_N$, then $v_{N+j}$ lies in $t^NU/t^{N+j}U$; its unique quotient by $t^N$ defines $w_j\in U/t^jU$. These quotients are compatible, so they define $w\in V$ with $t^Nw=v$. Conversely $t^NV\subseteq\ker\pi_N$, proving $\ker\pi_N=t^NV$. Multiplication by $t^N$ is also injective on $V$: if $t^Nw=0$, the component at $N+j$ says $w_{N+j}\in t^jU/t^{N+j}U$, whose reduction gives $w_j=0$ for every $j$. Thus division in $V$ is unique whenever it is defined, without having assumed that $V$ is a domain. [step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 *Completeness and locality.* Steps 4.1 and 5.1 give $V/t^NV\cong U/t^NU$ for every $N$, so $V\cong\varprojlim_N V/t^NV$ is $t$-adically complete and $\bigcap_Nt^NV=\bigcap_N\ker\pi_N=0$. Also $V/tV=\kappa$, making $tV$ a proper ideal. If $v\notin tV$, choose a unit $u\in U$ lifting its nonzero residue; its image in $V$ is a unit, and $v=u(1+\varepsilon)$ with $\varepsilon\in tV$. The series $\sum_{j\ge0}(-\varepsilon)^j$ converges in $V$ and inverts $1+\varepsilon$. Thus every element outside $tV$ is a unit, and $V$ is local with maximal ideal $tV$ and residue field $\kappa$. [step 1.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra]

7.1 *Orders and the domain property.* For $0\ne v\in V$, separatedness gives a largest $N\ge0$ with $v\in t^NV$. Write $v=t^Nu$; maximality makes $u\notin tV$, hence a unit. For two such elements, regularity of powers of $t$ proved in step 5.1 shows that their product is nonzero and has order the sum of their orders: any further divisibility by $t$ would, after cancellation, put a product of units in $tV$. Therefore $V$ is a domain. [step 5.1, step 6.1, algebra]

8.1 *A complete DVR.* Every nonzero ideal $J\subseteq V$ has an element of least order $N$. Writing it as $t^Nu$ with $u$ a unit shows that $t^N\in J$, and all other elements have order at least $N$, so $J=(t^N)$. Hence every ideal is principal and $V$ is Noetherian. Its only prime ideals are $0$ and $tV$: powers $(t^N)$ with $N>1$ are not prime. Thus $V$ has dimension one, and its maximal ideal is generated by $t$, so it is regular and is a DVR by [F4]. Completeness and its residue field were established in step 6.1. [F4, step 6.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 *The arc and its centres.* The maps $B_n\to U\to V$ are local and induce the given residue-field identifications. Since $\mathfrak m_nU=tU$, one has $\mathfrak m_nV=tV$. The map $B\to V$ is therefore a formal arc, nonsingular because the image of $t\in\mathfrak m$ generates $tV/t^2V$. For every $n$, the pullback of the closed-point ideal of $\operatorname{Spec}B_n$ is the effective Cartier divisor cut out by $t$. The universal property [F2] gives a unique factorization through its blowup. The given local map $B_{n+1}\to V$, viewed through its chart in [F1], gives such a factorization; locality places its closed point at the prescribed centre. Uniqueness therefore identifies the successive centres of the arc with the given chain. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 6.1, step 8.1]

10.1 *Completion and surjectivity.* Since $\mathfrak mV=tV$, there are compatible maps $B/\mathfrak m^N\to V/t^NV$. Passing to inverse limits extends $B\to V$ uniquely to $\widehat B\to V$, using [F3] and step 6.1. Given $v\in V$, set $r_0=v$ and successively choose $c_i\in B$ with residue equal to that of $r_i$, then define $r_{i+1}=(r_i-c_i)/t$. Existence and uniqueness of this division follow from step 5.1; [F5] supplies the sequence of residue lifts. The partial sums $s_N=\sum_{i<N}c_it^i$ satisfy $v-s_N=t^Nr_N$ in $V$. They are also Cauchy in $B$ for its $\mathfrak m$-adic topology, since $s_M-s_N\in\mathfrak m^N$ whenever $M\ge N$, as $t\in\mathfrak m$. Hence they define an element of $\widehat B$ whose image agrees with $v$ modulo every $t^NV$, and separatedness gives equality. Thus $\widehat B\to V$ is surjective, without a coefficient-field choice. [F3, F5, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The equicharacteristic hypothesis on $B$ enters through the standing setup of this page; the construction of $V$ uses only that $U$ is a domain, so that the successive quotients $t^jU/t^{j+1}U$ are copies of $\kappa$ and division by $t$ is unique.
- No coefficient field of $V$ is chosen: the residue-field identification is transported along the local maps $B_n\to V$, and the surjectivity of $\widehat B\to V$ uses only residue lifts in $B$.
- The hypothesis that $t$ generates every pullback centre ideal is exactly what forces $\mathfrak n=tU$; without it the union $U$ need not have a principal maximal ideal and the argument does not apply.
