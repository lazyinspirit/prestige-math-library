## Statement

Let $(X,\mathcal A)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B)$ be measurable spaces. If
$E \in \mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$, then $E_x \in \mathcal B$ for every
$x \in X$ and $E^y \in \mathcal A$ for every $y \in Y$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Measurable spaces $(X,\mathcal A)$ and $(Y,\mathcal B)$, and a set $E \in \mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$.

[L1] The product sigma-algebra $\mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$ is generated by the measurable rectangles. ([[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]])

[L2] The generated sigma-algebra is the smallest sigma-algebra containing the generating family. ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]])

[A1] For fixed $x \in X$, $$(A \times B)_x = \begin{cases} B,& x \in A,\\ \varnothing,& x \notin A,\end{cases}$$ and for fixed $y \in Y$, $$(A \times B)^y = \begin{cases} A,& y \in B,\\ \varnothing,& y \notin B.\end{cases}$$

[A2] For fixed $x$, $$((X \times Y) \setminus E)_x = Y \setminus E_x,\qquad \left(\bigcup_{n \ge 1} E_n\right)_x = \bigcup_{n \ge 1} (E_n)_x,$$ and the analogous formulas hold for vertical sections.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $x \in X$ and let $\mathcal M_x$ be the family of sets $F \subseteq X \times Y$ such that $F_x \in \mathcal B$. By [A1], every measurable rectangle belongs to $\mathcal M_x$. By [A2], $\mathcal M_x$ is a sigma-algebra on $X \times Y$. Since [L1] says $\mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$ is generated by the measurable rectangles, [L2] gives $\mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B \subseteq \mathcal M_x$. Therefore $E_x \in \mathcal B$. [A1, A2, L1, L2]

2.1 Fix $y \in Y$ and let $\mathcal N_y$ be the family of sets $F \subseteq X \times Y$ such that $F^y \in \mathcal A$. The same argument shows that $\mathcal N_y$ is a sigma-algebra containing all measurable rectangles, hence all of $\mathcal A \otimes \mathcal B$. Therefore $E^y \in \mathcal A$. Since $x$ and $y$ were arbitrary, every horizontal and vertical section of $E$ is measurable. [step 1.1, L1, L2, A1, A2] ∎
