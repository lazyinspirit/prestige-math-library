## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $d\ge1$ and $m\ge0$ be finite integers, and let $(Y_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ be a centered strictly stationary sequence of $\mathbb R^d$-valued random vectors with $\mathbb E\|Y_0\|^2<\infty$. Strict stationarity means that every finite-dimensional law is invariant under integer shifts. Assume $m$-dependence: for each $a\in\mathbb Z$, the sigma-algebras generated by $(Y_t)_{t\le a}$ and $(Y_t)_{t\ge a+m+1}$ are independent, in the sense of [[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]. Set
$$\Sigma_m=\sum_{|j|\le m}\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_j),$$
where $\operatorname{Cov}(U,V)=\mathbb E[(U-\mathbb EU)(V-\mathbb EV)^T]$ ([[def-moments-variance-and-covariance]]). Then $\Sigma_m$ is symmetric positive semidefinite and
$$\frac1{\sqrt n}\sum_{t=1}^nY_t\ \Rightarrow\ N_d(0,\Sigma_m).$$
The conclusion allows singular $\Sigma_m$, including $\Sigma_m=0$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume AC. Let $(Y_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ be centered, strictly stationary, $m$-dependent $\mathbb R^d$-valued random vectors with finite second moment, as in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] A centered row-wise independent triangular array with finite second moments and the Lindeberg condition has a standard normal normalized row-sum limit ([[thm-lindeberg-feller-central-limit-theorem-sufficiency]]); the row and Lindeberg conventions are those of [[def-row-wise-independent-centered-triangular-array]] and [[def-total-row-variance-and-lindeberg-condition]].

[F2] Slutsky's theorem permits multiplication of a convergent real random variable by a sequence converging in probability to a constant ([[thm-slutsky-for-real-random-variables]]).

[F3] If two sequences on a common metric space differ by a quantity tending to zero in probability, they have the same weak limit ([[thm-converging-together-lemma]]).

[F4] Under AC, pointwise convergence of characteristic functions to a specified characteristic function implies weak convergence of the corresponding real laws ([[cor-characteristic-function-criterion-for-weak-convergence]]).

[F5] Under AC, a positive semidefinite covariance matrix, including a singular one, defines $N_d(0,\Sigma)$ ([[def-multivariate-normal-law]]); convergence of all one-dimensional linear projections to the corresponding projections of a specified law implies vector weak convergence ([[thm-cramer-wold-device]]).

[F6] A square-integrable real random variable satisfies Chebyshev's inequality, so a sequence whose second moments tend to zero converges in probability to zero ([[cor-chebyshev-inequality-for-random-variables]]).

[F7] Dominated convergence removes the tails of a square-integrable random variable when it is clipped at levels $K\to\infty$ ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F8] Cauchy--Schwarz for square-integrable real random variables bounds expectations of products by the product of their $L^2$ norms ([[cor-cauchy-schwarz-for-random-variables]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The objects and assumptions in the Statement.

1.1 For $h\in\mathbb Z$ put $C_h=\operatorname{Cov}(Y_0,Y_h)$. These covariance terms are finite by [F8]. Strict stationarity gives $C_{-h}=C_h^T$, and $m$-dependence gives $C_h=0$ when $|h|>m$. Expanding the finite variance of the sum yields $$\frac1n\operatorname{Cov}\!\left(\sum_{t=1}^nY_t\right)=\sum_{|h|\le m}\left(1-\frac{|h|}{n}\right)_+C_h\longrightarrow\Sigma_m.$$ Thus $\Sigma_m$ is symmetric, and for every $a\in\mathbb R^d$, $a^T\Sigma_ma$ is the limit of nonnegative variances divided by $n$, so it is nonnegative. Hence $N_d(0,\Sigma_m)$ exists by [F5]. [A1, given, F5, F8, algebra]

2.1 Fix $a\in\mathbb R^d$ and let $Z_t=a^TY_t$ and $\sigma^2=a^T\Sigma_ma$. If $\sigma^2=0$, step 1.1 gives $\mathbb E|n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^nZ_t|^2\to0$; [F6] and then [F3] give convergence in distribution to zero. Suppose $\sigma^2>0$. For $K\ge1$ define $Z_t^{(K)}=\max(-K,\min(K,Z_t))-\mathbb E[\max(-K,\min(K,Z_0))]$. This is centered, stationary, $m$-dependent and bounded by $2K$; [F7] gives $\|Z_0-Z_0^{(K)}\|_2\to0$. Write $\sigma_K^2=\lim_n n^{-1}\operatorname{Var}(\sum_{t=1}^n Z_t^{(K)})=\sum_{|h|\le m}\operatorname{Cov}(Z_0^{(K)},Z_h^{(K)})$. Finite summation and [F8] give $\sigma_K^2\to\sigma^2$, so $\sigma_K^2>0$ for all sufficiently large $K$. [given, F3, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Fix such a $K$, put $\ell_n=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor$, and retain $q_n=\lfloor n/(\ell_n+m)\rfloor$ blocks of length $\ell_n$, with $m$ omitted indices between successive blocks. Write $b$ for the last index in a retained block. The next block starts at $b+m+1$, so $m$-dependence makes its sigma-algebra independent of the sigma-algebra generated by all earlier blocks; induction gives row-wise independence of the block sums $B_{n,r}$. If $\gamma_h^{(K)}=\operatorname{Cov}(Z_0^{(K)},Z_h^{(K)})$, stationarity gives $$\operatorname{Var}(B_{n,r})=\ell_n\gamma_0^{(K)}+2\sum_{h=1}^m(\ell_n-h)\gamma_h^{(K)},\qquad \frac{s_n^2}{n}:=\frac{q_n\operatorname{Var}(B_{n,1})}{n}\longrightarrow\sigma_K^2.$$ Here $q_n\ell_n/n\to1$ and $q_n/n\to0$. Also $|B_{n,r}|\le2K\ell_n$ and $s_n/\sqrt n\to\sigma_K>0$. Thus $q_n\ge1$ and $s_n>0$ for all sufficiently large $n$; on this tail, for each $\varepsilon>0$ every block is eventually bounded by $\varepsilon s_n$, so the Lindeberg sum is zero. Applying [F1] to this tail of rows, $s_n^{-1}\sum_rB_{n,r}\Rightarrow N(0,1)$, and [F2] gives $n^{-1/2}\sum_rB_{n,r}\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_K^2)$. [A1, F1, F2, given, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 The omitted gaps and terminal remainder contain $r_n\le m q_n+\ell_n+m=O(n/\ell_n+\ell_n)=o(n)$ indices. For a centered $m$-dependent sequence bounded by $2K$, at most $(2m+1)r_n$ ordered pairs of omitted terms can have nonzero covariance, each covariance having absolute value at most $4K^2$. Therefore the omitted sum divided by $\sqrt n$ tends to zero in $L^2$, hence in probability by [F6]. By [F3], $n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^n Z_t^{(K)}\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma_K^2)$. [F3, F6, given, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The centered $m$-dependent truncation errors $W_t^{(K)}=Z_t-Z_t^{(K)}$ satisfy $$\mathbb E\left|n^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^nW_t^{(K)}\right|^2\le(2m+1)\mathbb E|W_0^{(K)}|^2\longrightarrow0\quad(K\to\infty),$$ by expanding the variance and bounding each of its at most $2m+1$ nonzero lag terms with [F8]. For fixed real $u$, $|e^{iu x}-e^{iu y}|\le|u||x-y|$; thus the characteristic function of $n^{-1/2}\sum Z_t$ differs from that of its $K$-truncation by at most $|u|\sqrt{(2m+1)\mathbb E|W_0^{(K)}|^2}$. First taking $n\to\infty$ using step 4.1 and then $K\to\infty$ using $\sigma_K^2\to\sigma^2$ proves convergence to $e^{-u^2\sigma^2/2}$. By [F4], $n^{-1/2}\sum Z_t\Rightarrow N(0,\sigma^2)$. This also covers every projection with positive variance; the zero-variance case was handled in step 2.1. [A1, F4, F8, step 2.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Steps 2.1 and 5.1 show for every $a\in\mathbb R^d$ that $a^Tn^{-1/2}\sum_{t=1}^nY_t\Rightarrow N(0,a^T\Sigma_ma)$. These are precisely the projections of the law $N_d(0,\Sigma_m)$ established in step 1.1, so [F5] gives the asserted vector convergence, including singular and zero covariance. [A1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 5.1] ∎
