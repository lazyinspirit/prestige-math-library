## Statement

For $n\ge2$, $[S_n,S_n]=A_n$. For $n\ge5$,
$[A_n,A_n]=A_n$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The groups $S_n$ and $A_n$ in the stated ranges of $n$.

[F1] $[G,G]$ is generated by the commutators $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F2] The commutator subgroup is normal ([[lem-commutator-subgroup-is-normal]]).

[F3] For $N\trianglelefteq G$, the quotient $G/N$ is abelian exactly when $[G,G]\subseteq N$ ([[thm-quotient-abelian-iff-contains-commutator-subgroup]]).

[F4] $A_n$ is the kernel of sign ([[def-alternating-group]]) and is generated by $3$-cycles for $n\ge3$ ([[thm-alternating-group-is-generated-by-three-cycles]]).

[F5] $A_n$ is simple for $n\ge5$ ([[thm-alternating-group-is-simple-for-n-at-least-five]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Since $S_n/A_n$ is the abelian sign image, [F3] gives $[S_n,S_n]\subseteq A_n$. [F3, F4, algebra]

1.2 Every $3$-cycle satisfies $(a\ b\ c)=[(b\ c),(a\ b)]$ under the convention in [F1], so [F4] gives $A_n\subseteq[S_n,S_n]$ for $n\ge3$. [F1, F4, algebra]

1.3 For $n\ge5$, the $3$-cycles $(1\,2\,3)$ and $(3\,4\,5)$ do not commute, so $[A_n,A_n]$ is nontrivial by [F1]. [F1, algebra]

2.1 At $n=2$, both $A_2$ and the commutator subgroup of the abelian group $S_2$ are trivial. Thus steps 1.1--1.2 prove the first formula for all $n\ge2$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 This subgroup is normal by [F2], and simplicity [F5] therefore forces $[A_n,A_n]=A_n$. [F2, F5, step 1.3] ∎
