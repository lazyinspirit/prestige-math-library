## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $(R,\mathfrak m)$ be a Noetherian local ring, let $M\ne0$ be a finite
$R$-module, and put $d=\dim\operatorname{Supp}(M)$. Assume $d\ge1$ and let
$x,x_2,\ldots,x_d$ be a system of parameters for $M$. Put
$Q=(x,x_2,\ldots,x_d)$ and $Q'=(x_2,\ldots,x_d)$. For a finite module $N$ on
which $J$ is an ideal of definition, and for an integer $r\ge0$ with
either $N=0$ or $r\ge\deg P_{J,N}$, write
$$e_J^{[r]}(N):=r!\,[n^r]P_{J,N}(n),$$
with value $0$ when $N=0$ or $\deg P_{J,N}<r$. Then
$$ e_Q^{[d]}(M)=e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(M/xM)-e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(0:_M x). $$
In particular, if $x$ is $M$-regular, then
$$ e_Q(M)=e_{Q'}(M/xM). $$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]), a Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m)$, a nonzero finite $R$-module $M$ of support dimension $d\ge1$, and a system of parameters $x,x_2,\ldots,x_d$ as above.

[L1] The degree of a nonzero finite module's Hilbert-Samuel polynomial equals its support dimension under AC ([[thm-hilbert-samuel-dimension-theorem]]).

[L2] For a finite module and an ideal of definition generated by $r$ elements, its degree-$r$ coefficient is the Euler characteristic of the Koszul complex; in particular its polynomial has degree at most $r$ ([[thm-hilbert-samuel-multiplicity-as-koszul-euler-characteristic]]).

[L3] When $M/(x,Q')M$ has finite length, the quotient $C=M/xM$ and annihilator $K=0:_M x$ each have $Q'$-quotients of finite length, and their Koszul Euler characteristics satisfy the first-element subtraction formula ([[lem-koszul-euler-characteristic-first-element-reduction]]).

[L4] Degree-indexed multiplicity and Koszul Euler characteristic use the displayed polynomial and homological sign conventions ([[def-koszul-euler-characteristic-and-degree-indexed-multiplicity]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.


1.1 Put $K=(0:_M x)$ and $C=M/xM$. The parameter hypothesis gives finite length of $M/QM$, so [L3] makes $Q'$ a module-relative ideal of definition for both $C$ and $K$ (including the zero-module case). The $(d-1)$-generator degree bound in [L2] gives $\deg P_{Q',C},\deg P_{Q',K}\le d-1$ whenever the polynomials are nonzero. [L2, L3, given]

2.1 By [L2], $e_Q^{[d]}(M)=\chi(K(x,x_2,\ldots,x_d;M))$. The exact first-element reduction [L3] makes this $\chi(K(x_2,\ldots,x_d;C))-\chi(K(x_2,\ldots,x_d;K))$. Apply [L2] to each $Q'$-complex, with the degree-indexed convention [L4], to obtain $$e_Q^{[d]}(M)=e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(C)-e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(K).$$ [L2, L3, L4, step 1.1]


3.1 If $x$ is $M$-regular, then $K=0$. By [L1], $\deg P_{Q,M}=d$, so $e_Q^{[d]}(M)=e_Q(M)\ne0$. Step 2.1 therefore makes $e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(C)$ nonzero. The degree bound in step 1.1 forces $\deg P_{Q',C}=d-1$, and hence $e_{Q'}^{[d-1]}(C)=e_{Q'}(C)$. This proves $$e_Q(M)=e_{Q'}(M/xM).$$ [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]


4.1 Therefore the parameter-reduction formula holds. [step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
