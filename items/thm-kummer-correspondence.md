## Statement

Let $F$ be a field, let $n\ge1$, assume $\operatorname{char}F\nmid n$, and
assume $\mu_n\subseteq F$. Then the assignment

$$B\longmapsto F(B^{1/n})$$

is an inclusion-preserving bijection between

1. subgroups $B$ with $(F^\times)^n\subseteq B\subseteq F^\times$ and finite quotient $B/(F^\times)^n$, and
2. finite abelian extensions $K/F$ whose Galois group has exponent dividing $n$.

For corresponding objects one has

$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\cong \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(B/(F^\times)^n,\mu_n\bigr).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $F$, the integer $n$, a subgroup $B$ as above, and a finite abelian extension $K/F$ of exponent dividing $n$.

[F1] A Kummer extension is a finite Galois extension of the form $F(B^{1/n})$ with $\mu_n\subseteq F$ ([[def-kummer-extension]]).

[L1] The Kummer pairing for $K=F(B^{1/n})$ is perfect, in particular bilinear and nondegenerate in both variables ([[thm-kummer-pairing-is-perfect]]).

[L2] Cyclic degree-$d$ extensions over a base containing $\mu_d$ are exactly the extensions generated by a $d$-th root whose defining polynomial $x^d-a$ is irreducible ([[thm-cyclic-degree-n-extensions-with-roots-of-unity-are-kummer]]).

[L3] Artin's fixed-field theorem computes degrees from finite automorphism groups ([[thm-artin-fixed-field-degree-theorem]]).

[L4] In a finite Galois extension, normal intermediate fields correspond to quotient Galois groups ([[thm-normal-subgroups-and-quotients-in-the-galois-correspondence]]).

[L5] Every finite abelian group is a finite direct product $C_{n_1}\times\cdots\times C_{n_r}$ with $1<n_1\mid\cdots\mid n_r$ ([[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-abelian-groups-invariant-factor-form]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $B$ be a subgroup with finite quotient. Because $B/(F^\times)^n$ is finite, choose elements $a_1,\dots,a_r\in B$ whose classes generate it. Then $$F(B^{1/n})=F(a_1^{1/n},\dots,a_r^{1/n}).$$ For each $j$, the field $F(a_j^{1/n})$ contains all roots of $x^n-a_j$ because $\mu_n\subseteq F$, so it is a finite Galois extension whose automorphism group embeds in $\mu_n$ by the rule $\sigma\mapsto \sigma(a_j^{1/n})/a_j^{1/n}$. Thus each one-generator step is cyclic of degree dividing $n$. The compositum $K:=F(B^{1/n})$ is therefore finite Galois and abelian, and every automorphism acts on every chosen $n$-th root by multiplication with an element of $\mu_n$. Hence the exponent of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ divides $n$. [F1, choose, algebra]

1.2 For this extension, the Kummer pairing of [L1] is perfect, so the natural maps $$\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(B/(F^\times)^n,\mu_n\bigr),\qquad B/(F^\times)^n\longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\mu_n\bigr)$$ are isomorphisms because both groups are finite and the pairing is nondegenerate in both variables. In particular, $$\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)\cong \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(B/(F^\times)^n,\mu_n\bigr).$$ [L1]

1.3 Conversely, let $K/F$ be finite abelian of exponent dividing $n$ and define $$B(K):=\{\,b\in F^\times : \text{some }n\text{-th root of }b\text{ lies in }K\,\}.$$ This contains $(F^\times)^n$. For $b\in B(K)$ choose $\beta\in K$ with $\beta^n=b$ and define $$\chi_b(\sigma):=\frac{\sigma(\beta)}{\beta}\in\mu_n\qquad(\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)).$$ As in the Kummer-pairing proof, this depends only on the class of $b$ modulo $(F^\times)^n$, and if $\chi_b=1$ then every $\sigma$ fixes $\beta$, so $\beta\in F$ and $b\in(F^\times)^n$. Therefore $$B(K)/(F^\times)^n\hookrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\mu_n\bigr),$$ so the quotient is finite. [algebra]

1.4 Let $L:=F(B(K)^{1/n})\subseteq K$ and put $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$. If $\sigma\in G$ is nontrivial, use [L5] to write $G\cong C_{n_1}\times\cdots\times C_{n_r}$. Each $n_j$ divides $n$ because the exponent of $G$ divides $n$. Some coordinate of $\sigma$ is nonzero, say in $C_{n_j}$. If $\zeta\in F$ is a primitive $n$-th root of unity, then $\eta=\zeta^{n/n_j}$ has order $n_j$, so projection to this coordinate followed by a generator-preserving map $C_{n_j}\to\langle\eta\rangle$ gives a character $\chi:G\to\mu_n$ with $\chi(\sigma)\ne1$. Put $H=\ker\chi$ and $d=|\operatorname{im}\chi|$. Then $H\trianglelefteq G$, $d\mid n$, and [L4] makes $E:=K^H/F$ cyclic of degree $d$. Since $\operatorname{char}F\nmid d$ and $\mu_d\subseteq F$, [L2] gives $E=F(\gamma)$ with $\gamma^d\in F$. Hence $\gamma^n=(\gamma^d)^{n/d}\in F$, so $\gamma\in L$. But $\chi(\sigma)\ne1$ means $\sigma\notin H=\operatorname{Gal}(K/E)$, so $\sigma$ acts nontrivially on $E=F(\gamma)$ and therefore does not fix $L$. Thus $\operatorname{Gal}(K/L)=1$, and [L3] gives $[K:L]=1$. Therefore $K=F(B(K)^{1/n})$. [L2, L3, L4, L5, algebra, choose]

2.1 Starting from a finite abelian extension $K/F$, step 1.4 shows that the radicals coming from $B(K)$ generate $K$, so the field-side composite is the identity. [step 1.4]

2.2 Starting from a subgroup $B$ and putting $K=F(B^{1/n})$, one has $B\subseteq B(K)$ by definition. For the reverse inclusion, let $c\in B(K)$ and choose $\beta_c\in K$ with $\beta_c^n=c$. Step 1.2 identifies the perfect pairing with an isomorphism $$B/(F^\times)^n\cong \operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\operatorname{Gal}(K/F),\mu_n\bigr),$$ so the character $\chi_c(\sigma)=\sigma(\beta_c)/\beta_c$ equals $\chi_b$ for some class $\bar b\in B/(F^\times)^n$ represented by $\beta_b^n=b$ with $b\in B$. Then for every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(K/F)$ one has $$\frac{\sigma(\beta_c/\beta_b)}{\beta_c/\beta_b}=\frac{\chi_c(\sigma)}{\chi_b(\sigma)}=1,$$ so $\beta_c/\beta_b\in F$. Hence $c/b=(\beta_c/\beta_b)^n\in(F^\times)^n\subseteq B$, and therefore $c\in B$. Thus $B(K)=B$. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Inclusion preservation is immediate from the definitions, and the displayed Galois-group formula is exactly the isomorphism of step 1.2. [step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
