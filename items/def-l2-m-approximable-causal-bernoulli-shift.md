## Definition

Let $\mathcal B$ be the Borel sigma-algebra of $[0,1]$, generated by intervals with rational endpoints; let $dt$ be Lebesgue measure restricted to $\mathcal B$, and set $H=L^2([0,1],\mathcal B,dt;\mathbb R)$, with inner product $\langle x,y\rangle_H=\int_0^1x(u)y(u)\,du$. We use the quotient-by-null-functions convention for $L^2$ ([[def-l-p-space-as-a-quotient-by-null-functions]]). The measure space is finite and countably generated, so under Countable Choice $H$ is separable ([[thm-l-p-of-a-sigma-finite-countably-generated-measure-space-is-separable]], [[def-countable-choice]], [[def-separable-space]]); [[lem-l-two-with-the-integral-pairing-is-a-hilbert-space]] verifies that this real pairing makes the quotient a Hilbert space.

Let $(S,\mathcal S,\nu)$ be a probability space and $(\delta_t)_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ an independent identically distributed family of $S$-valued random elements with law $\nu$ ([[def-random-element-and-real-random-variable]], [[def-law-or-distribution-of-a-random-element]], [[def-independent-random-elements]]). A **causal Bernoulli shift** with innovation law $\nu$ is a process
$$X_t=f(\delta_t,\delta_{t-1},\delta_{t-2},\ldots),\qquad t\in\mathbb Z,$$
where $f:S^{\mathbb N_0}\to H$ is measurable from the product sigma-algebra $\mathcal S^{\otimes\mathbb N_0}$ to the Borel sigma-algebra of $H$. This product-measurability formulation is meaningful for a merely measurable innovation space $S$.

On an independent product extension, take replacement innovations $\delta'_{t,m,k}$, indexed by $t\in\mathbb Z$, $m\in\mathbb N_0$, and integers $k\le t-m$, all with law $\nu$, mutually independent, and independent of the original innovations. Such a countable family and its product with the original probability space exist under Countable Choice and Dependent Choice ([[def-countable-choice]], [[def-dependent-choice]], [[cor-countable-independent-copies-exist]], [[thm-sigma-finite-product-measure-exists-is-rectangular-and-is-unique]]). For $m\ge1$ define
$$X_{t,m}=f(\delta_t,\ldots,\delta_{t-m+1},\delta'_{t,m,t-m},\delta'_{t,m,t-m-1},\ldots),$$
and define the full replacement $X_{t,0}=f(\delta'_{t,0,t},\delta'_{t,0,t-1},\ldots)$. Each $X_{t,m}$ has the same marginal law as $X_t$; for fixed $m\ge1$ the sequence $(X_{t,m})_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ is strictly stationary and $m$-dependent because its original-innovation windows have length $m$ and its replacement arrays are independent across $t$.

For every fixed $m\ge0$, the joint process $(X_t,X_{t,m})_{t\in\mathbb Z}$ is strictly stationary: shifting all indices relabels a family of mutually independent innovations with the same law and commutes with the defining measurable maps. Consequently $D_{t,m}:=X_t-X_{t,m}$ is stationary and $\|D_{t,m}\|_{L^2(H)}=\delta_m$ for every $t$.

Write $\|X\|_{L^2(H)}=(\mathbb E\|X\|_H^2)^{1/2}$ and
$$\delta_m=\|X_0-X_{0,m}\|_{L^2(H)},\qquad m\ge0.$$
The process is **$L^2$-$m$-approximable** when
$$\mathbb E\|X_0\|_H^2<\infty\qquad\hbox{and}\qquad\sum_{m\ge1}\delta_m<\infty.$$
The same marginal law gives $\delta_0\le2\|X_0\|_{L^2(H)}<\infty$. For a Bochner-integrable $H$-valued random element $Y$, write $\mathbb E_B Y=\int Y\,d\mathbb P$; the moment condition makes $X_t$ and every $X_{t,m}$ Bochner integrable ([[def-bochner-integrable-function]], [[thm-bochner-integrability-criterion]]).

Here **strict stationarity** means invariance of every finite-dimensional law under integer shifts. A process is **$m$-dependent** if, for every $a\in\mathbb Z$, the sigma-algebras generated by $(X_s)_{s\le a}$ and $(X_s)_{s\ge a+m+1}$ are independent ([[def-independent-sigma-algebras-and-events]]). The stated input windows for $X_{t,m}$ are disjoint across these two index ranges, so they satisfy this definition.

## Remarks

Every Borel $H$-valued random element here is strongly measurable. Indeed, fix a dense sequence $(h_j)$ in the separable space $H$. For each $N$, send $x$ to the first point among $h_0,\ldots,h_N$ minimizing $\|x-h_j\|_H$. Its finitely many tie-broken cells are Borel because the distance functions are continuous, so this is a finite-valued measurable map; its distance from $x$ tends to zero since the sequence is dense. Composition with any Borel $H$-valued random element gives the simple approximants in [[def-strongly-measurable-banach-valued-function]]. The phrase “Borel measurable” in the design is thus applied to the target $H$; the innovation domain uses its stated product sigma-algebra.
