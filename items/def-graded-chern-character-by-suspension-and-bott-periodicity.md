## Definition

Assume AC. Let $X$ be a based finite CW complex. The reduced Chern character
$$\widetilde{\operatorname{ch}}:\widetilde K^0(X)\longrightarrow \bigoplus_k\widetilde H^{2k}(X;\mathbb Q)$$
is defined by subtracting ranks: on a virtual class $[E]-[\operatorname{rank}E]$
with $E$ a complex bundle, it is the image of $\operatorname{ch}(E)-\operatorname{rank}E$
in reduced cohomology, using
[[thm-chern-character-is-a-natural-ring-homomorphism-on-k-zero]] and
[[def-chern-character-of-a-complex-vector-bundle]].

In degree one use the suspension identification of the *based* theory
$\widetilde K^{-1}(X)=\widetilde K^0(\Sigma X)$ together with the reduced
cohomology suspension
$\widetilde H^{2k}(\Sigma X;\mathbb Q)\cong\widetilde H^{2k-1}(X;\mathbb Q)$
from [[prop-reduced-and-unreduced-generalized-cohomology-theories-correspond]],
and define
$$\operatorname{ch}:\widetilde K^{-1}(X)\longrightarrow \bigoplus_k\widetilde H^{2k-1}(X;\mathbb Q)$$
by first applying $\widetilde{\operatorname{ch}}$ on $\Sigma X$ and then the
inverse of the cohomological suspension map
$\widetilde H^{2k-1}(X;\mathbb Q)\xrightarrow{\cong}
\widetilde H^{2k}(\Sigma X;\mathbb Q)$. Bott periodicity
[[thm-complex-bott-periodicity]] supplies natural isomorphisms
$K^{q}(X)\cong K^{q-2}(X)$, and the two-periodic theory
[[thm-complex-k-theory-is-a-two-periodic-generalized-cohomology-theory]] makes
the resulting maps independent of the chosen even shift: for every $j\in\mathbb Z$
there is a natural additive map
$$\operatorname{ch}:K^j(X)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_kH^{j+2k}(X;\mathbb Q).$$

The normalization is fixed on the Bott class: $\widetilde K^0(S^2)=
\widetilde K^{-2}(*)$ is generated by $\beta$, and $\operatorname{ch}(\beta)$
is the rational suspension generator of $H^2(S^2;\mathbb Q)\cong\mathbb Q$.
Because every degree is reached from degree zero by suspending $X$, this
single normalization determines all the maps; the compatibility with external
products reduces to the degree-zero external formula of
[[thm-chern-character-is-a-natural-ring-homomorphism-on-k-zero]] and the
compatibility of the suspension isomorphisms with products, and it is used in
that form in the next two items.
