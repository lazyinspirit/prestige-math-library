## Example

Take $M=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$. The canonical free cover on its underlying set $\{0,\bar1\}$ is $$\varepsilon_0:\mathbb Ze_0\oplus\mathbb Ze_1\to\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z,$$ with $\varepsilon_0(e_0)=0$ and $\varepsilon_0(e_1)=\bar1$. Its kernel is generated by $e_0$ and $2e_1$, so the next stage of the canonical construction is the free abelian group on that kernel as a set.
## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The module $M=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$.

[L1] The iterated free-cover construction gives a canonical exact free resolution ([[prop-the-iterated-free-module-resolution-is-canonical-in-zf]]).
## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 A vector $ae_0+be_1$ maps to $\bar b$, so it lies in $\ker(\varepsilon_0)$ exactly when $b$ is even. Therefore $$\ker(\varepsilon_0)=\mathbb Ze_0\oplus\mathbb Z(2e_1).$$ [given, algebra]

2.1 The next free object in the canonical construction is therefore $F_1=\mathbb Z^{(\ker(\varepsilon_0))}$ together with its canonical surjection $F_1\twoheadrightarrow\ker(\varepsilon_0)$. This makes concrete what [L1] does: the construction simply repeats the free-on-the-underlying-set cover on the current kernel, with no arbitrary choices. The same pattern starts even for the zero module. [L1, step 1.1, discharge-construct] ∎
