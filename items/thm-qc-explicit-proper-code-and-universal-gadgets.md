## Statement

There is an explicit fixed quantum code and finite logical gate set whose generated subgroup, including adjoints, is dense in the unitary group on its logical space in operator norm, and whose encoder, error-correction, initialization, readout, and gate gadgets satisfy the proper-code contract of [[def-qc-ft-gadget-and-proper-code-contract]]. A Steane $t=1$ construction must be paired with a separately proved extended-rectangle correctness and overlap analysis. A route using the concatenated threshold theorem with $d\ge2\ell$ must exhibit a code correcting at least $2\ell$ located errors and prove every required gadget contract.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite gate and instrument model of the proper-code contract,
including encoded-input noisy contracts and ideal unknown-input encoding.

[F1] [[lem-qc-concatenated-steane-distance-and-transversal-cliffords]]
gives an explicit $[\![49,1,9]\!]$ encoding $W$, a fixed exact decoded recovery
through weight four, and exact transversal $H,S,$ CNOT circuits.

[F2] [[lem-qc-four-round-verified-shor-correction]] gives a fixed finite
correction instrument $C$: with zero faults it maps arbitrary inputs into
the code and corrects weight-four errors; with one fault it corrects
weight-two inputs up to a weight-one residual.

[F3] [[lem-qc-verified-steane-magic-state-and-injection]] gives the exact
finite bare encoder, a verified encoded $T|+\rangle$ factory, and injection
which takes input error weight one and one fault to output error weight
at most two. With no fault its local incoming errors remain local.

[F4] [[lem-qc-verified-steane-initialization-and-readout]] gives protected
zero, plus, encoded-classical readout and reset. Its readout emits a new
code block in logical $|m\rangle$, not an unprotected classical bit.

[F5] [[lem-qc-scalar-phase-completes-clifford-t-universality]] proves full
$U(2)$ density for $H,T$ and $P=e^{2\pi i\sqrt2}I$, including adjoints,
and exact one-layer physical gadgets for $P,P^*$.

[F6] [[def-qc-ft-gadget-and-proper-code-contract]] imposes the computation
and correction budgets; [[def-qc-code-deviation-and-spread]] specifies
the joint encoded-instrument sectors and retained external references.

[F7] [[lem-qc-sparse-fault-path-implies-code-deviation]] states the additional finite port-substitution hypothesis and proves hierarchical sparse correctness from it. Its finite hypothesis, rather than an assumed all-level conclusion, is verified below.

[F8] [[lem-qc-encoded-error-instrument-and-cptp-distinction]] proves scalar recovery identities from a normalized mixed Pauli channel, including memory-valued coefficients. [[thm-complex-spectral-theorem-for-normal-endomorphisms]] supplies diagonalization of the finite positive Gram matrix.

## Proof


1.1 Take $m=49$, the code $Q=\operatorname{im}W$, effective spread budget $\ell=1$, and declared capacity $d=2$. Choose the decoded recovery to be the zero-fault circuit $C$ of [F2], followed by the inverse of [F3]'s bare Clifford encoder and discard of its other $48$ coordinates. This is an actual finite circuit: $C$ is code valued, and the inverse encoder extracts its one logical input with all other encoder coordinates reset to zero. By [F1] and [F2] it corrects the entire weight-four error span and hence every located weight-two channel required by the contract. The same recovery tensorized across live blocks corrects the whole joint error span: expand each joint Kraus operator as a sum of tensor products of local error operators and apply the one-block scalar recovery identities to every pair of terms. The resulting coefficients are scalars on every logical factor, although their fault-memory actions may be correlated. Thus the external reference is unchanged. The numerical conditions $49\ge2d+1$ and $d\ge2\ell$ hold, and the integers $q\ge1$ with $2q\ell\le d$ consist exactly of $q=1$. This is a nonvacuous sparse-fault contract. [F1, F2, F3, F6, construct, algebra]

1.2 Declare the finite physical alphabet to consist of $H,S,S^*,T,T^*,X,P,P^*$, CNOT, Toffoli, zero preparation, computational-basis measurement with a classical output register, reset, discard and idle. Classical registers are computational-basis registers; their reversible Boolean gates are the restrictions of $X,$ CNOT and Toffoli. No Boolean computation or control signal is noiseless. Every operation in the supplier circuits is in this alphabet: their controlled $X$ is CNOT, their doubly controlled $X$ is Toffoli, their controlled $Z$ is $H$–Toffoli–$H$ with the indicated two controls, their controlled $S$ has the explicit five-gate Clifford+$T$ decomposition in [F3], and their conditional swaps use two CNOTs and one Toffoli. Source locations include all preparations, measured raw bits, scratch workspaces and idles. [F2, F3, F4, F5, construct]

1.3 Define protected elementary unitary templates as follows. Begin every template by applying the finite circuit $C$ separately to every input block, before that block interacts with another input. For $H,S,S^*$ use their $49$-coordinate transversal core. For $X$ use the explicit weight-nine logical Pauli core, with idles on the other coordinates. For $P,P^*$ use [F5]'s scalar core; for CNOT use the transversal two-block core. Append one copy of $C$ to every output block. For $T$ use [F3]'s injection core between that input refresh and output correction. For $T^*$ use that same injection, then transversal $S^*$, then output correction, since $S^*T=T^*$. These occurrences of $C$ are its explicitly expanded finite physical circuit, not an in-place recursive call. All templates are exact on fault-free encoded inputs. If the one fault occurs in an input correction, [F2] leaves at most one residual on that block while other input corrections are ideal; the fault-free core propagates this within its supported error budget and the output corrections are ideal. If the fault occurs in a one-block transversal, scalar or Pauli core, the input weight one plus that location gives at most two errors, corrected by the fault-free suffix. If it occurs in the suffix, its input has weight at most one and [F2] leaves at most one residual. [F1, F2, F3, F5, construct, algebra]

1.4 Here is an exact finite Toffoli core over the other gates. For computational bits $x,y,z$ the integer identity $4xyz=x+y+z-(x\mathbin\oplus y)-(x\mathbin\oplus z)-(y\mathbin\oplus z)+(x\mathbin\oplus y\mathbin\oplus z)$ follows by expanding $a\mathbin\oplus b=a+b-2ab$. Apply $T$ to the three original bits; compute their three pair parities into fresh zero registers, apply $T^*$ to each and uncompute; compute their triple parity into a fourth zero register, apply $T$ and uncompute. The net basis phase is $(-1)^{xyz}$ and scratch returns to zero. Conjugate by $H$ on the third original coordinate to obtain exact Toffoli, without a scalar-phase discrepancy. There are $27$ unitary elementary gates, four scratch preparations and four scratch discards. [F3, algebra, construct]

2.1 For CNOT, two input blocks with one error each can give the union of two coordinate labels in either output. A faulty core location adds at most one more label per block, for a total of three. In that case every suffix correction is fault free and [F2] corrects through weight four. If the fault is in a suffix correction, the core is fault free and produces at most two errors per output; the faulty suffix therefore satisfies precisely [F2]'s weight-two input contract. Other suffixes are ideal. Hence the protected CNOT template has at most one residual per block. For $T$ and $T^*$, [F3] gives at most two errors when the fault is in the core, so ideal suffix correction removes them. If the fault is in suffix correction, the injection is noiseless and propagates an incoming one-coordinate error to one-coordinate error; transversal $S^*$ preserves that support. Again [F2] applies. Every protected elementary unitary therefore takes joint weight-one inputs and one fault to joint weight-one outputs, stronger than the required weight-two computation bound. These are joint Kraus-sector assertions, so correlated faults and arbitrary finite references are covered by the suppliers' termwise proofs. [F1, F2, F3, F6, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 Use [F4]'s verified zero and plus preparations, reset and encoded-classical readout, and [F3]'s verified magic preparation. Append $C$ to their live encoded output blocks. A fault in a preparation core leaves weight one, corrected by an ideal suffix; a suffix fault has a clean admitted input and leaves weight one. Prepend $C$ to readout's encoded input as well. A fault there leaves at most one error for the ideal readout core; a fault in the core or suffix has the stated bounds by [F4] and [F2]. Readout thus preserves the logical measurement instrument and emits an encoded classical block with at most one error. In particular the final correction/control bit is protected: recursive replacement of a computational-basis measurement emits $W|m\rangle$ in a new block. Subsequent classical NOT, CNOT and Toffoli use the same coherent encoded unitary templates on these basis states. Discard is the ordinary trace channel; a trace-preserving fault confined to discarded registers cannot change the retained external reference. An encoded idle template is input $C$, a layer of $49$ physical idles, and output $C$. Besides its weight-one invariant on admitted inputs, this template attracts arbitrary inputs into the weight-one code sector with an unspecified resulting logical map: use [F2]'s arbitrary-input attraction for the faulty correction, and its ideal arbitrary-input action for the others. This local input refresh is included even for classical registers. [F2, F3, F4, F6, step 1.3, algebra]

2.3 For completeness specify the recursive domains, rather than treating an arbitrary bad child's output as encoded. Put $V_0=I$, $V_r=V_{r-1}^{\otimes49}W$. Let $\mathcal E_{1;s}$ be the usual weight-$s$ operator span. For $r\ge2$, let $\mathcal E_{r;s}$ be the span of tensor operators on the $49$ child blocks for which at most $s$ child factors are unrestricted and every other factor belongs to $\mathcal E_{r-1;1}$. This is the hierarchical error sector; all formulas use its joint tensor version across live blocks. For the base scalar identities, list all phase-free weight-at-most-four Pauli errors $P_a$. Each is unitary, so $P_a/\sqrt M$, where $M$ is their number, form a trace-preserving Kraus family. The fixed recovery corrects that actual channel by [F1]. By [F8], each recovery Kraus map satisfies $R_lP_aW=c_{la}I$, and linearity extends this to the entire error span. A fixed recursive decoded recovery $D_r$ corrects $\mathcal E_{r;s}$ for $s\le4$: take the declared $D_1$, apply $D_{r-1}$ in every child while retaining its private recovery-syndrome register, and then apply $D_1$ to the $49$ logical outputs. By induction a good child factor becomes scalar identity on its logical factor, with its syndrome coefficient retained. An unrestricted factor becomes an arbitrary one-qubit map, hence a Pauli linear combination at that outer coordinate. The outer recovery therefore sees at most $s$ erroneous coordinates and corrects them by [F1]. Expanding every pair of Kraus terms proves the same assertion for coherent sums and correlations, not merely tensor product channels. [F1, F2, F6, F8, step 1.1, induction, algebra]

3.1 Implement step 1.4 on encoded blocks using the protected elementary templates and verified zero scratch preparations, first refreshing the three original inputs separately by $C$. After each elementary stage apply $C$ to every live block of this at-most-seven-block construction, including untouched scratch and input blocks. This supplies a fixed finite Toffoli template. To verify its one-fault branch, induct over its finitely many stages. A fault in the active core produces at most three errors per block by step 2.1; all following corrections in that stage are ideal and correct them. A fault in a correction sees at most two errors from the fault-free core and leaves at most one residual. An idle fault on another live block occurs either before its ideal correction, when at most three errors are removed, or after that correction, when its now-clean state receives one error. A fault in scratch preparation has [F4]'s one-coordinate bound. Thus every stage ends in the correct ideal joint logical state with at most one error per block, and later stages obey the same invariant. The Pauli-basis argument in [F2] and [F3] applies jointly, retaining fault memory and the external reference. This proves the Toffoli contract without assuming a fault-tolerant Toffoli supplier in its own proof. Its native physical Toffoli locations occur only inside finite lower-level templates, not as an unproved logical gate. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 2.1, step 1.4, induction, algebra]

3.2 The arbitrary unknown-input encoder is the exact finite linear encoder of [F3]; its zero-fault action is $W$ with the reference retained. The quantitative noisy-input contract starts on already encoded sectors or protected prescribed preparations, as stipulated in [F6]. This boundary is necessary: a circuit encoding every unknown $\sigma$ as $W\sigma W^*$ receives exactly the same physical input in a fault-free run on $X\rho X$ as in a run on $\rho$ with an initial $X$ fault. It cannot output both $W\rho W^*$ and $WX\rho XW^*$; taking $\rho=|0\rangle\langle0|$ contradicts orthogonality of the isometry's two encoded basis states. Every use of the bare encoder inside a prescribed-state factory is nevertheless an ordinary counted faulty location, and [F3] and [F4] verify all its arbitrary faulty outputs by the subsequent rank-one checks and fallback construction. The inverse quantum-code conversion in the declared decoded recovery is likewise an ideal decoding boundary; physical readout inside a noisy computation instead uses step 2.2's protected encoded-classical instrument. Error correction itself is the unmodified $C$, whose fault-free action on every arbitrary input is code valued by [F2]. Its finite syndrome-measurement family consists of the $48$ explicit check circuits of [F2]: on weight-one input, one fault adds at most one data coordinate error, so each has the required weight-two computation output sector while revealing only an error syndrome, not the logical state. [F2, F3, F4, F6, construct, algebra]

5.2 The locality certificate for substitution is as follows. At a fixed physical fault pattern, each elementary location acts on its declared data ports and any fault memory allowed by [F6]. Here is an explicit factorization of a correctable block edge with encoding $V$. For a finite error basis $E_a$, step 2.3 gives scalar Gram equations $V^*E_a^*E_bV=c_{ab}I$: applying each recovery Kraus operator to $E_aV$ gives a scalar multiple of identity, and summing their pairwise products gives this equation. Diagonalize the finite positive matrix $(c_{ab})$ and discard its zero eigenvalues. The normalized transformed errors $F_jV$ then satisfy $(F_jV)^*(F_kV)=\delta_{jk}I$, so $J(|\psi\rangle\otimes|j\rangle)=F_jV|\psi\rangle$ is an isometry identifying the whole correctable range with logical register times a syndrome register. The termwise sector identities make each good-child Kraus map on this range $J_{\rm out}(g\otimes B)J_{\rm in}^*$; for a measurement retain its logical outcome Kraus operator and public outcome port in place of $g$. Trace preservation holds on this entire admitted domain. This is the required CP factorization, not an asserted full-space extension. Private cat, verifier, selector and truth-table registers are freshly prepared and discarded at their listed boundaries. Only listed data ports and public classical-result ports cross a template boundary. Recovery-syndrome registers introduced for comparison in step 2.3 remain attached to their corresponding physical block edge; they are not an additional globally shared register coupling unrelated locations. A good lower child, on its admitted encoded input, consequently factors its ideal logical channel or instrument and its attached syndrome maps by the termwise identities already proved. Public measurement records remain explicit encoded classical ports. The coefficient $B$ may act on shared fault memory; it is not assumed to commute with another location's coefficient. [F6, F8, step 2.3, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Keep that memory order by expanding the finite Kraus products before making a substitution. Fix a complete instrument outcome and Kraus index at every location, and choose bases on the attached syndrome and private registers. In the logical/syndrome coordinates of step 5.2, each good map on admitted inputs has its ideal logical Kraus operator as data factor.
