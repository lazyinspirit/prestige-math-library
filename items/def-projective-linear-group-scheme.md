## Definition

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the supplied projective-bundle formalism. Let $S$ be a scheme and $n\ge1$. The **projective linear group scheme** $\operatorname{PGL}_{n,S}$ is the open subscheme of the projective space of $n\times n$ matrices on which the homogeneous determinant is nonzero:
$$\operatorname{PGL}_{n,S}=D_+(\det)\subseteq\mathbb P_S^{n^2-1}.$$
Here the projective space uses the quotient convention of [[def-projective-bundle-scheme]], with the standard coordinate basis giving the displayed matrix coordinates. Multiplication is induced by matrix multiplication, identity by the identity matrix, and inversion by the adjugate matrix. For every $S$-scheme $T$, the group $\operatorname{PGL}_{n,S}(T)$ consists of matrices locally on $T$ up to multiplication by units, with local representatives required to be invertible and identified on overlaps by unit scalars. Equivalently it is the sheaf quotient $\operatorname{GL}_{n,S}/\mathbb G_{m,S}$ for the Zariski, étale, or fppf topology. In general it is not asserted to equal the naive quotient $\operatorname{GL}_n(\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T))/\Gamma(T,\mathcal O_T)^\times$.

The construction commutes with base change. The structure map $\operatorname{PGL}_{n,S}\to S$ is affine, smooth, and of finite presentation, with relative dimension $n^2-1$. In particular over a field it is a smooth affine algebraic group. These properties supply the exact interface needed in [[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]], [[def-affine-morphism-schemes]], and [[def-smooth-morphism-schemes]].

## Construction and verification

The determinant is homogeneous of degree $n$, so its nonvanishing is independent of the local scalar chosen to represent a projective matrix. The standard Proj calculation identifies $D_+(\det)$ over an affine base $\operatorname{Spec}R$ with the affine scheme whose coordinate ring is the degree-zero part of $R[x_{ij},\det(x)^{-1}]$; this is finite type over $R$, generated by $x_{i_1j_1}\cdots x_{i_nj_n}/\det(x)$. Its defining equations are the finitely many relations among these degree-zero generators, and this presentation is preserved by base change.

On the product of two determinant opens the product matrix has invertible determinant, and is well-defined under either scalar rescaling. The entries of the adjugate have common degree $n-1$ and define an inverse on the determinant open: $M\operatorname{adj}(M)=\det(M)I$. For $n=1$ the adjugate is the constant identity and the construction gives the trivial group. Matrix associativity, the identity and the adjugate identities prove the group axioms after trivializing the representing line bundles, hence globally.

For a $T$-point the quotient-bundle presentation of projective space gives an invertible quotient of the rank-$n^2$ free module whose coordinate sections have determinant nowhere vanishing. On a trivializing open, these sections give an invertible matrix, and two choices differ by a unit. Conversely such matrices and scalar transition functions glue to the projective point. The map $\operatorname{GL}_n\to\operatorname{PGL}_n$ therefore has local sections and its two local lifts differ by a unique scalar; this proves the stated sheaf quotient and avoids the false naive quotient assertion.

To verify smoothness, use the opens where a chosen matrix entry is invertible. On each such chart normalize that entry to $1$; the remaining $n^2-1$ entries are affine coordinates and the determinant condition is the principal open given by one polynomial. These opens cover the determinant open because a projective point has locally a unit coordinate. Each is an open subscheme of affine $(n^2-1)$-space over $S$, proving smoothness and the relative dimension. The affine degree-zero description proves affineness; base change and finite presentation follow from its finite homogeneous coordinate construction.
