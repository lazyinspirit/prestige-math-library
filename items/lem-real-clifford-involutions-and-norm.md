## Statement

Let $V$ be finite-dimensional over $\mathbb R$, let $B$ be symmetric, put $Q(v)=B(v,v)$, and use the Clifford convention $v^2=Q(v)1$ in $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ from [[def-clifford-algebra-of-a-quadratic-space]]. The assignments $\alpha(v)=-v$ and $t(v)=v$, extended respectively as an algebra automorphism and an order-reversing anti-automorphism, descend to the quotient. Put $\bar x=t(\alpha(x))$ and $N(x)=x\bar x$. For anisotropic $u_1,\ldots,u_k$,

$$N(u_1\cdots u_k)=\prod_{j=1}^k(-Q(u_j))\,1.$$

No nondegeneracy assumption is needed for the involution and norm-of-product assertions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The tensor-algebra quotient and relation $v^2=Q(v)1$ of [[def-clifford-algebra-of-a-quadratic-space]].

[F1] The Clifford algebra is the quotient of the tensor algebra by the two-sided ideal generated by $v^2-Q(v)1$ for all $v\in V$ ([[def-clifford-algebra-of-a-quadratic-space]]).

## Proof


**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 On the tensor algebra, send a word $v_1\cdots v_m$ to $(-1)^m v_1\cdots v_m$ for $\alpha$, and to $v_m\cdots v_1$ for $t$. The first is an algebra automorphism and the second reverses products. Each defining relation $v^2-Q(v)1$ is fixed by both maps. The ideal generated by these relations is therefore invariant under each map: applying either map to a generator of the ideal gives another generator, with left and right factors exchanged for $t$. Thus both maps descend to the quotient and square to the identity. [F1, construct]

2.1 Their composite $\bar x=t(\alpha(x))$ is an order-reversing involution and satisfies $\bar u=-u$ for $u\in V$. Hence $N(u)=u\bar u=-u^2=-Q(u)1$. [step 1.1, F1, algebra]

3.1 For $k=0$, the empty product is $1$ and $N(1)=1$. If $N(y)$ is scalar, then $N(xy)=xy\overline{xy}=xy\bar y\bar x=xN(y)\bar x=N(y)N(x)$. Induction on $k\ge1$, starting with step 2.1, therefore gives $N(u_1\cdots u_k)=\prod_{j=1}^k(-Q(u_j))1$. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

This is $x\bar x$ with $\bar v=-v$. Meinrenken's text often writes the reversion norm $t(x)x$; for a homogeneous word of parity $k$ that norm differs by $(-1)^k$, so the conventions agree on even products only.
