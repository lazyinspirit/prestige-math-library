## Statement

For every finite field $k$ and every $n\ge2$, one has $K_n^M(k)=0$.

[[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

[[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The objects in the Statement.

[F1] The Milnor groups are generated by multilinear Steinberg symbols. [[def-milnor-k-group-of-a-field]]

[F2] Symbols anticommute and $\{a,a\}=\{a,-1\}$, which is killed by $2$. [[lem-milnor-symbol-anticommutativity-and-repeated-entry-identities]]

[F3] The multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic. [[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data and conventions above.

1.1 Let $|k|=q$ and take a generator $\theta$ of $k^\times$. In degree two every symbol is an integer multiple of $c=\{\theta,\theta\}$. By [F2], $2c=0$, while $(q-1)c=0$ by multilinearity. If $q$ is even, these two integers are coprime and $c=0$. This includes $q=2$, where $\theta=1$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 If $q$ is odd, let $S,N$ be the nonzero squares and nonsquares. Each has size $(q-1)/2$. The map $a\mapsto1-a$ sends $N$ injectively into $k^\times\setminus\{1\}$, since neither $0$ nor $1$ belongs to $N$. It cannot have its whole image in $S$, because $1\in S$ is missing and the image has size $|S|$. Thus some $a\in N$ has $1-a\in N$. Write $a=\theta^i$, $1-a=\theta^j$ with $i,j$ odd. The Steinberg relation gives $ijc=0$; since $ij$ is odd and $2c=0$, one gets $c=0$. Hence $K_2^M(k)=0$ in all cases. Every higher generating symbol is a product with a degree-two symbol, so vanishes as well. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
