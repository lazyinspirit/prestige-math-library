## Definition

For $n\ge1$ and $1\le m\le n$ let $Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$ be the centre of the
complex group algebra $\mathbb C[S_m]$
([[def-center-of-the-group-algebra]], [[def-group-ring]]). We use the standard
chain of symmetric groups
$$S_1\subset S_2\subset\cdots\subset S_n,\qquad S_m=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,m\}),$$
in which $S_m$ is the subgroup of $S_n$ fixing every element of
$\{m+1,\dots,n\}$ pointwise; thus $\mathbb C[S_m]$ is the subalgebra of
$\mathbb C[S_n]$ spanned by $S_m$, and $Z(\mathbb C[S_1])=\mathbb C\cdot1$.
Each centre $Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$ is thereby regarded as a subalgebra of
$\mathbb C[S_n]$.

The **Gelfand-Tsetlin algebra** of the chain is
$$\mathrm{GZ}(n):=\bigl\langle Z(\mathbb C[S_1]),Z(\mathbb C[S_2]),\dots, Z(\mathbb C[S_n])\bigr\rangle\subseteq\mathbb C[S_n],$$
the unital subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ generated by all these centres.

## Remarks

- **Well-definedness.** Each $Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$ is a $\mathbb C$-subalgebra
  of $\mathbb C[S_m]$, hence, via the inclusion $\mathbb C[S_m]\subseteq
  \mathbb C[S_n]$, a subset of $\mathbb C[S_n]$ closed under addition,
  multiplication and scalar multiplication; the generated subalgebra is
  therefore a unital subalgebra of $\mathbb C[S_n]$. It is finite-dimensional
  because $\mathbb C[S_n]$ is: each $\mathbb C[S_m]$ is a finite-dimensional
  semisimple algebra ([[cor-group-algebra-is-semisimple-when-char-k-does-not-divide-group-order]]),
  so its centre is finite-dimensional as well.

- **Commutativity.** Every element of $Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$ is central in
  $\mathbb C[S_m]$. If $m\le k$ and $z\in Z(\mathbb C[S_m])$,
  $w\in Z(\mathbb C[S_k])$, then $\mathbb C[S_m]\subseteq\mathbb C[S_k]$ and
  $w$ commutes with every element of $\mathbb C[S_k]$, in particular with
  $z$; hence $zw=wz$. Thus all the generating centres commute with one
  another, and $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is a commutative subalgebra of
  $\mathbb C[S_n]$. Only this centrality, not semisimplicity, is used.

- **The top centre is included.** The term $m=n$ shows
  $Z(\mathbb C[S_n])\subseteq\mathrm{GZ}(n)$; in fact the centre of
  $\mathbb C[S_n]$ is the algebra generated by its class sums, and the other
  centres are generated by the class sums of the subgroups, so
  $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is generated by class sums of the chain together with
  $\mathbb C[S_1]=\mathbb C$.

- **The algebra used below.** $\mathrm{GZ}(n)$ is the diagonal algebra of the
  Young basis: [[thm-gelfand-tsetlin-algebra-is-the-diagonal-algebra-in-the-young-basis]]
  identifies it with $\bigoplus_T\mathbb C P_T$ over the standard tableaux
  $T$ of size $n$, and
  [[thm-jucys-murphy-elements-generate-the-gelfand-tsetlin-algebra]] shows
  that it is also the algebra generated by the Jucys-Murphy elements.
