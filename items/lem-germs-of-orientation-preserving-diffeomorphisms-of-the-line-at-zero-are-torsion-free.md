## Statement

Every finite subgroup of the group $\operatorname{Diff}^+_0(\mathbb R,0)$ of
germs at $0$ of orientation-preserving local diffeomorphisms of $\mathbb R$
fixing $0$ is trivial: if $h$ is such a germ and $h^n=\mathrm{id}$ in the group
of germs for some $n\ge1$, then $h=\mathrm{id}$. Equivalently, the group of
germs of orientation-preserving local diffeomorphisms fixing a point of a
one-dimensional manifold is torsion-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A germ $h\in\operatorname{Diff}^+_0(\mathbb R,0)$ and a positive integer $n$ with $h^n=\mathrm{id}$ in the group of germs.

[F1] A germ of local diffeomorphisms of $\mathbb R$ at $0$ fixing $0$ is an equivalence class of local diffeomorphisms $f:U\to V$ with $0\in U$, $f(0)=0$, two representatives being equivalent when they agree on a neighbourhood of $0$; the product is represented by the composite and the group structure is as in [[lem-germs-of-local-diffeomorphisms-form-a-group]] ([[def-germ-of-a-local-diffeomorphism-at-a-point]]).

[F2] Composition of representatives induces a well-defined associative operation with identity and inverses on $\operatorname{Diff}_0(\mathbb R)$, so it is a group, and an element is the identity germ exactly when one (equivalently every) representative equals the identity on a neighbourhood of $0$ ([[lem-germs-of-local-diffeomorphisms-form-a-group]]).

[F3] A local diffeomorphism of $\mathbb R$ is a $C^\infty$ map with $C^\infty$ local inverse; an orientation-preserving one fixing $0$ has positive derivative at $0$ and is therefore strictly increasing on a neighbourhood of $0$ ([[def-diffeomorphism-and-local-diffeomorphism-of-manifolds]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (A representative with controlled iterates.) By [F1, F2] choose a representative $h:I\to\mathbb R$ defined on an open interval $I$ containing $0$, with $h(0)=0$ and $h$ orientation-preserving; by [F3] $h$ has positive derivative at $0$ and is strictly increasing on a neighbourhood of $0$. Since $h^n$ is the identity germ, some neighbourhood of $0$ is mapped identically by $h^n$ [F2]. First shrink $I$ so $h$ is strictly increasing throughout $I$. Continuity at the fixed point then gives an open interval $J\ni0$ so that $h^n=\mathrm{id}$ on $J$ and every iterate $h^k(J)$, $0\le k\le n$, lies in $I$ [F1, F2, F3]. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 (No displacement.) Suppose $h$ is not the identity germ. Then, by [F2], for every neighbourhood of $0$ there is a point $t$ of that neighbourhood with $h(t)\neq t$. Choose such a point $t\in J$. If $h(t)>t$, then strict increase of $h$ on $I$ gives $h^{k+1}(t)>h^k(t)$ for every $k<n$, hence $h^n(t)>t$, contradicting $h^n(t)=t$; if $h(t)<t$, the same monotonicity gives $h^{k+1}(t)<h^k(t)$ and $h^n(t)<t$, again a contradiction. Hence no such $t$ exists and $h$ agrees with the identity on a neighbourhood of $0$, that is, $h=\mathrm{id}$ as a germ. [F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 (Finite subgroups.) Let $H\le\operatorname{Diff}^+_0(\mathbb R,0)$ be a finite subgroup and let $h\in H$. The cyclic subgroup generated by $h$ is contained in $H$, hence finite, so $h^k=\mathrm{id}$ for some $k\ge1$; step 2.1 applied with that $k$ gives $h=\mathrm{id}$. Therefore every element of $H$ is the identity germ and $H$ is the trivial subgroup: the group of germs is torsion-free. [step 2.1]

4.1 For a one-dimensional manifold $T$ and $x\in T$, choose a chart at $x$; a germ of an orientation-preserving local diffeomorphism of $T$ at $x$ is represented in this chart by a germ of an orientation-preserving local diffeomorphism of $\mathbb R$ at $0$, and composition and the identity are preserved by the chart change. Hence the same argument shows that the group of germs at $x$ is torsion-free. [step 3.1] ∎
