## Statement

Let $B$ and $A$ be unital rings and let $P$ be a $(B,A)$-bimodule that is finitely generated and projective as a left $B$-module. Then $P^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)$ is an $(A,B)$-bimodule under $(a\varphi)(p)=\varphi(pa)$ and $(\varphi b)(p)=\varphi(p)b$, and the evaluation map
$$\operatorname{ev}:\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)\otimes_BY\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y),\qquad \varphi\otimes y\longmapsto\bigl(p\mapsto\varphi(p)y\bigr),$$
is an isomorphism of abelian groups. It is natural in the left $B$-module $Y$ and is an isomorphism of left $A$-modules when both sides carry the actions induced by $(a\varphi)(p)=\varphi(pa)$ and $(a\psi)(p)=\psi(pa)$. If $p_i\in P$ and $\varphi_i\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)$ form a dual basis with $p=\sum_i\varphi_i(p)p_i$ for all $p$, then the inverse is $h\mapsto\sum_i\varphi_i\otimes h(p_i)$. In particular, if $P$ is a left $B$-module and $Y$ ranges over left $B$-modules, then $\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,-)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)\otimes_B-$ naturally. No commutativity is assumed and no choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Unital rings $B$ and $A$, a $(B,A)$-bimodule $P$ that is finitely generated and projective as a left $B$-module, and $P^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)$.

[F1] A $(B,A)$-bimodule is a left $B$-module and a right $A$-module whose actions commute: $(bp)a=b(pa)$ ([[def-bimodule]]).

[F2] For left $R$-modules $M,N$ the set $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an abelian group under pointwise addition, and pre- and postcomposition with module maps are additive ([[def-hom-groups-and-induced-hom-maps]]).

[F3] A left $B$-module is finitely generated when it is generated by a finite subset, and the generated submodule of a subset is the set of its finite $B$-linear combinations ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[lem-generated-submodule-as-finite-linear-combinations]]).

[F4] A projective module $P$ has the lifting property for epimorphisms: every surjective module map onto $P$ admits a section ([[def-projective-module]]; the lifting property applied to the identity of $P$ produces the section).

[F5] A family of maps out of the summands of a direct sum extends uniquely to a map out of the direct sum, and an element of a direct sum is the finite sum of its coordinate inclusions ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]], [[thm-universal-property-of-module-direct-sums]]).

[F6] A balanced pairing on $M\times N$ into an abelian group induces a unique group homomorphism out of the tensor product, and an elementary-tensor formula descends exactly when its pairing is balanced ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-tensor-products]], [[prop-elementary-tensor-formulas-descend-exactly-when-balanced]]).

[F7] If ${}_SM_R$ is an $(S,R)$-bimodule and $N$ is a left $R$-module, then $M\otimes_RN$ carries a unique left $S$-module structure with $s(m\otimes n)=(sm)\otimes n$ ([[thm-bimodule-actions-induced-on-tensor-products]]).

[F8] Module maps $g:N\to N'$ induce $1\otimes g$ on tensor products, compatibly with identities and composition ([[prop-functoriality-of-module-tensor-products]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 ($P^{\vee}$ is an $(A,B)$-bimodule.) For $a\in A$ and $\varphi\in P^{\vee}$, the prescription $(a\varphi)(p):=\varphi(pa)$ is additive in $p$ because the right $A$-action and $\varphi$ are additive, and it is left $B$-linear because $P$ is a bimodule and $\varphi$ is left $B$-linear: $(a\varphi)(bp)=\varphi((bp)a)=\varphi(b(pa))=b\varphi(pa)=b(a\varphi)(p)$. For $b\in B$ the prescription $(\varphi b)(p):=\varphi(p)b$ is additive and left $B$-linear because $(\varphi b)(b'p)=\varphi(b'p)b=(b'\varphi(p))b=b'(\varphi(p)b)$. The module laws for $a\mapsto a\varphi$ and $b\mapsto\varphi b$ follow from the ring laws of $A$ and $B$ and the module laws of $P$, and the two actions commute, $(a\varphi)b=a(\varphi b)$, because both sides send $p$ to $\varphi(pa)b$; hence $P^{\vee}$ is an $(A,B)$-bimodule. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

1.2 (Existence of a dual basis.) Since $P$ is finitely generated, it has a finite generating set $x_1,\dots,x_n$ by [F3]; the maps $\beta_i:B\to P$, $b\mapsto bx_i$, are left $B$-linear, so the universal property of the direct sum $B^n$ gives a unique left $B$-linear $q:B^n\to P$ with $q\,\jmath_i=\beta_i$. This $q$ is surjective: every $p\in P$ is a finite $B$-linear combination $\sum_ir_ix_i$ by [F3], and $\sum_ir_ix_i=q(\sum_i\jmath_i(r_i))$. By [F4] the epimorphism $q$ onto the projective module $P$ has a section $s:P\to B^n$ with $qs=1_P$; write $\pi_i:B^n\to B$ for the coordinate projections. Setting $p_i:=x_i$ and $\varphi_i:=\pi_i\circ s$ gives left $B$-linear maps $\varphi_i:P\to B$, and for every $p$ the description of elements of a direct sum [F5] gives $s(p)=\sum_i\jmath_i(\pi_i(s(p)))$, so $p=qs(p)=\sum_i\pi_i(s(p))\,q\jmath_i(1)=\sum_i\varphi_i(p)\,p_i$. Only finitely many objects are selected inside the given finite generating set, so no choice principle is used. [F3, F4, F5, given, construct]

1.3 (The evaluation pairing is balanced.) For fixed $y\in Y$ the map $\varphi\mapsto(p\mapsto\varphi(p)y)$ is additive by [F2], and for fixed $\varphi$ the map $y\mapsto(p\mapsto\varphi(p)y)$ is additive; the pairing $(\varphi,y)\mapsto(p\mapsto\varphi(p)y)$ from $P^{\vee}\times Y$ to $\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y)$ is therefore additive in each variable. It is $B$-balanced because $(\varphi b)(p)y=\varphi(p)by=\varphi(p)(by)$ for $b\in B$: forming $(\varphi b,y)$ and $(\varphi,by)$ gives the same map $P\to Y$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 (The evaluation map exists and is natural.) By the universal property of the tensor product [F6], the balanced pairing of step 1.3 induces a unique group homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}:P^{\vee}\otimes_BY\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y)$ with $\operatorname{ev}(\varphi\otimes y)=(p\mapsto\varphi(p)y)$. For a left $B$-module map $g:Y\to Y'$, the composites $\operatorname{ev}_{Y'}\circ(1\otimes g)$ and $g_*\circ\operatorname{ev}_Y$ are group homomorphisms that agree on every elementary tensor $\varphi\otimes y$, both sending it to $(p\mapsto\varphi(p)g(y))$ by [F8] and [F2]; since the elementary tensors generate the tensor product additively, the two maps are equal, which is naturality in $Y$. [F6, F8, F2, step 1.3]

2.2 (A candidate inverse from the dual basis.) Fix the dual basis $p_i,\varphi_i$ of step 1.2 and define $\delta:\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y)\to P^{\vee}\otimes_BY$ by $\delta(h):=\sum_i\varphi_i\otimes h(p_i)$; this is a finite sum, and it is additive in $h$ because each evaluation $h\mapsto h(p_i)$ and the tensor product are additive. [F2, step 1.2, given, construct]

3.1 (The two composites are identities.) For $h\in\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y)$, applying $\operatorname{ev}$ to $\delta(h)$ gives the map $p\mapsto\sum_i\varphi_i(p)h(p_i)$, which equals $h(p)$ because $h$ is left $B$-linear and $p=\sum_i\varphi_i(p)p_i$; hence $\operatorname{ev}\circ\delta=1$. For an elementary tensor, $\delta(\operatorname{ev}(\varphi\otimes y))=\sum_i\varphi_i\otimes\varphi(p_i)y$. The element $\sum_i\varphi_i\varphi(p_i)$ of $P^{\vee}$ equals $\varphi$, since for every $p$ one has $\bigl(\sum_i\varphi_i\varphi(p_i)\bigr)(p)=\sum_i\varphi_i(p)\varphi(p_i)=\varphi\bigl(\sum_i\varphi_i(p)p_i\bigr)=\varphi(p)$ by left $B$-linearity of $\varphi$ and the dual-basis formula; hence $\sum_i\varphi_i\otimes\varphi(p_i)y=\bigl(\sum_i\varphi_i\varphi(p_i)\bigr)\otimes y=\varphi\otimes y$, so $\delta\circ\operatorname{ev}$ is the identity on elementary tensors and therefore on all of $P^{\vee}\otimes_BY$. Thus $\operatorname{ev}$ is a bijection with inverse $\delta$, and it is in particular an isomorphism of abelian groups. [F2, F6, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

3.2 ($A$-linearity.) On $P^{\vee}\otimes_BY$ the left $A$-action $a(\varphi\otimes y)=(a\varphi)\otimes y$ is the one of [F7] for the $(A,B)$-bimodule $P^{\vee}$ of step 1.1, and on $\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,Y)$ we use $(a\psi)(p)=\psi(pa)$, which is again a left $B$-linear map by the same bimodule computation as in step 1.1. For $a\in A$ and $\varphi\otimes y$, $\operatorname{ev}(a(\varphi\otimes y))$ sends $p$ to $(a\varphi)(p)y=\varphi(pa)y$, and $a\operatorname{ev}(\varphi\otimes y)$ sends $p$ to $\operatorname{ev}(\varphi\otimes y)(pa)=\varphi(pa)y$; the two sides agree on elementary tensors and hence everywhere, so $\operatorname{ev}$ is left $A$-linear. [F7, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 1.1-1.3 and 2.1 construct the $(A,B)$-bimodule $P^{\vee}$ and the natural evaluation map, steps 2.2 and 3.1 show it is an isomorphism with the stated inverse built from any dual basis, and step 3.2 upgrades it to a left $A$-module isomorphism. Retaining only the group structures, steps 2.1 and 3.1 give the natural isomorphism $\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,-)\cong\operatorname{Hom}_B(P,B)\otimes_B-$ of the final sentence: neither the existence of the dual basis nor the two composite computations uses the $A$-action, so for a left $B$-module $P$ finitely generated and projective the result applies with any right $A$-structure on $P$ or with none. The only selections made lie inside the finite data of a generating set and a section, so no choice principle is used. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
