## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $q$ be a prime power, put $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ and let $V=\mathbb F_q^n$ carry the standard flag $V_0\subsetneq V_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V_n=V$ ([[def-standard-subgroups-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]]). Let $\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ be compositions of $n$ with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$ and $J_1,\dots,J_s$ and partial sums $d_i=a_1+\cdots+a_i$, $e_j=b_1+\cdots+b_j$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]]), let $P_\alpha\ge B$ and $P_\beta\ge B$ be the corresponding standard parabolics, let $W_\alpha,W_\beta\le W\cong S_n$ be the parabolic Weyl subgroups and let $\ell$ be the inversion length ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]). For $\tau\in S_n$ put $$M(\tau)_{ij}:=\#\{\,v\in J_j:\tau(v)\in I_i\,\}\qquad(1\le i\le r,\ 1\le j\le s),$$ the **block-count matrix** of $\tau$, a matrix with nonnegative integer entries, and call $\tau$ **$(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing** when $\tau$ is increasing on each $\beta$-block ($v<v'$ in a common $J_j$ implies $\tau(v)<\tau(v')$) and $\tau^{-1}$ is increasing on each $\alpha$-block ($u<u'$ in a common $I_i$ implies $\tau^{-1}(u)<\tau^{-1}(u')$). For $x\in G$ let $\sigma(x)\in S_n$ be its Bruhat index, the unique permutation with $x\in BP_{\sigma(x)}B$ ([[thm-bruhat-decomposition-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]]). Then:

1. **Double cosets.** The assignment $\Psi\bigl(W_\alpha w_\tau W_\beta\bigr):=P_\alpha P_\tau P_\beta$ is a well-defined bijection from the set of $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double cosets of $W$ onto the set of $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double cosets of $G$.
2. **Block counts are a complete invariant.** For all $x,y\in G$ one has $M(\sigma(x))=M(\sigma(y))$ if and only if $P_\alpha xP_\beta=P_\alpha yP_\beta$, if and only if $W_\alpha\sigma(x)W_\beta=W_\alpha\sigma(y)W_\beta$; and $\tau\mapsto M(\tau)$ induces a bijection from $W_\alpha\backslash W/W_\beta$ onto the set of all matrices with nonnegative integer entries whose row sums are $a_1,\dots,a_r$ and whose column sums are $b_1,\dots,b_s$.
3. **Unique minimal representative.** Every $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset contains exactly one $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing permutation, and this permutation is the unique element of the coset of minimal inversion length; every other element of the coset has strictly larger length. It is the **monotone matching** determined by $M$: as the elements of a fixed $\beta$-block $J_j$ are listed in increasing order, their images run in increasing order through the first $M_{1j}$ elements of $I_1$ not already allocated to $J_1,\dots,J_{j-1}$, then through the first $M_{2j}$ such elements of $I_2$, and so on, the blocks $J_1,J_2,\dots,J_s$ being processed in this order.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, a prime power $q$, the group $G=\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$, the space $V=\mathbb F_q^n$ with standard flag $V_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq V_n=V$, compositions $\alpha=(a_1,\dots,a_r)$ and $\beta=(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ of $n$ with blocks $I_1,\dots,I_r$ and $J_1,\dots,J_s$, partial sums $d_i,e_j$, standard parabolics $P_\alpha,P_\beta$, parabolic Weyl subgroups $W_\alpha,W_\beta$ and inversion length $\ell$.

[L1] The blocks are the intervals $I_i=\{\,d_{i-1}+1,\dots,d_i\,\}$ with $|I_i|=a_i$ and $J_j=\{\,e_{j-1}+1,\dots,e_j\,\}$ with $|J_j|=b_j$, the standard partial flag of type $\alpha$ has members $V_{d_i}=\langle e_1,\dots,e_{d_i}\rangle$, and $P_\alpha$ is the stabiliser in $G$ of that flag; moreover $B\le P_\alpha\le G$ for every composition $\alpha$ ([[def-compositions-partial-flags-and-standard-parabolics]], [[def-standard-subgroups-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L2] $W=N/T\cong S_n$ with $w_\tau=P_\tau T$, the permutation matrices satisfy $P_\sigma P_\tau=P_{\sigma\circ\tau}$, a monomial matrix lies in $P_\alpha$ exactly when its permutation matrix has $\tau(I_i)=I_i$ for all $i$, the parabolic Weyl subgroup is $W_\alpha=\{\,\tau\in S_n:\tau(I_i)=I_i\text{ for every }i\,\}$, and $\ell(\tau)$ counts the pairs $i<j$ with $\tau(i)>\tau(j)$, so that $\ell(\tau^{-1})=\ell(\tau)$ ([[def-weyl-group-and-length-for-finite-gl-n]]).

[L3] $G=\bigsqcup_{\tau\in S_n}BP_\tau B$, and for every $x\in G$ the double coset $BxB$ equals $BP_\sigma B$ for exactly one $\sigma\in S_n$; writing this $\sigma=\sigma(x)$ gives $x=b_1P_{\sigma(x)}b_2$ with $b_1,b_2\in B$ ([[thm-bruhat-decomposition-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L4] For $x\in G$ and $1\le i,j\le n$ one has $\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(V_i\cap xV_j)=j-r_{i+1,j}(x)$, where $r_{k,l}(x)$ is the rank of the submatrix on the rows $k,\dots,n$ and the columns $1,\dots,l$, with $r_{n+1,j}(x):=0$ ([[thm-relative-position-classifies-pairs-of-complete-flags]]).

[L5] If $x\in BP_\sigma B$ then $r_{i,j}(x)=\#\{\,k\le j:\sigma(k)\ge i\,\}$ for all $i,j$ ([[lem-rank-matrices-determine-the-pivot-permutation]]).

[L6] $S_n=\operatorname{Sym}(\{1,\dots,n\})$ is the group of bijections of $\{1,\dots,n\}$ under composition $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$, with identity $\mathrm{id}$; in particular every $\tau$ has an inverse $\tau^{-1}$, $(\tau^{-1})^{-1}=\tau$, and composition is associative ([[def-symmetric-group]], [[lem-symmetric-group-is-a-group]]).

[L7] A linear subspace $U\le V$ is a vector space over $\mathbb F_q$ in its own right, and a linear map $T:V\to V$ restricts to a linear map $U\to V$, the restriction being $T$ with domain shrunk ([[def-linear-subspace]], [[def-linear-map]]).

[L8] For $A\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb F_q)$ the map $v\mapsto Av$ is a linear isomorphism of $V$ ([[thm-invertible-matrix-theorem]]), hence injective; therefore for every linear subspace $U\le V$ the restriction of this map to $U$ is an injective linear map $U\to V$ with image $A[U]$, and a linear map whose domain $U$ is finite-dimensional satisfies $\dim_{\mathbb F_q}U=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\ker+\dim_{\mathbb F_q}\operatorname{im}$, so that $\dim_{\mathbb F_q}A[U]=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}U$; a linear isomorphism of finite-dimensional spaces preserves dimension ([[thm-rank-nullity]], [[cor-finite-dimensional-vector-spaces-are-isomorphic-iff-equal-dimension]], [[def-injection-surjection-bijection]]).

[L9] The intersection of two linear subspaces of $V$ is a linear subspace, and a linear subspace of the finite-dimensional space $V_{d_i}$ is again finite-dimensional ([[def-linear-subspace]], [[thm-dimension-of-a-linear-subspace]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **The Bruhat index.** For every $x\in G$ there is exactly one $\sigma(x)\in S_n$ with $x\in BP_{\sigma(x)}B$, and then $x=b_1P_{\sigma(x)}b_2$ with $b_1,b_2\in B$; in particular $\sigma(P_\tau)=\tau$ for every $\tau\in S_n$, because $P_\tau\in BP_\tau B$ while $P_\tau\notin BP_\sigma B$ for $\sigma\ne\tau$. [L3]

1.2 **The dimension identity.** Fix $x\in G$ and put $C_{ij}(x):=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(V_{d_i}\cap xV_{e_j})$ for $1\le i\le r$ and $1\le j\le s$; these intersections are finite-dimensional by [L9], and computing with [L4] and [L5] for $\sigma=\sigma(x)$ gives $$C_{ij}(x)=e_j-r_{d_i+1,e_j}(x)=e_j-\#\{\,k\le e_j:\sigma(k)\ge d_i+1\,\}=\#\{\,k\le e_j:\sigma(k)\le d_i\,\}=\sum_{i'\le i}\ \sum_{j'\le j}M(\sigma(x))_{i'j'},$$ where the third equality uses $\sigma(k)\le n$ for all $k$ and the last one splits $k\le e_j$ according to the $\beta$-blocks $J_1,\dots,J_j$ and the $\alpha$-blocks $I_{i'}$ containing $\sigma(k)$. [L1, L4, L5, L9]

1.3 **The margins of a block-count matrix.** For every $\tau\in S_n$ the block-count matrix has row sums $\sum_jM(\tau)_{ij}=\#\{\,v:\tau(v)\in I_i\,\}=|I_i|=a_i$ and column sums $\sum_iM(\tau)_{ij}=|J_j|=b_j$; in particular $M(\tau)$ is a matrix with nonnegative integer entries, row sums $a_1,\dots,a_r$ and column sums $b_1,\dots,b_s$. [L1]

1.4 **Invariance of block counts under the two parabolic actions.** One has $M(u\tau v)=M(\tau)$ for all $u\in W_\alpha$, $v\in W_\beta$ and $\tau\in S_n$: for $u$ this is $M(u\tau)_{ij}=\#\{\,v\in J_j:u(\tau(v))\in I_i\,\}=M(\tau)_{ij}$, because $u(I_i)=I_i$ for every $i$; for $v$ it is $M(\tau v)_{ij}=\#\{\,x\in J_j:\tau(v(x))\in I_i\,\}=M(\tau)_{ij}$, because $v$ permutes each block $J_j$. [L2]

1.5 **The monotone matching.** Let $m=(m_{ij})$ be any matrix with nonnegative integer entries, row sums $a_i$ and column sums $b_j$. For all $i,j$ let $$A_{ij}:=\Bigl\{\,t\in I_i:\ \sum_{j'<j}m_{ij'}<t-d_{i-1}\le\sum_{j'\le j}m_{ij'}\,\Bigr\},$$ the set of the next $m_{ij}$ elements of $I_i$ after those already allocated to $J_1,\dots,J_{j-1}$; the $A_{ij}$ for fixed $i$ partition $I_i$ into consecutive pieces of sizes $m_{ij}$, and every element of $A_{i'j}$ is smaller than every element of $A_{ij}$ whenever $i'<i$. Define $\sigma_m$ by requiring, for each $j$, that $\sigma_m$ maps $J_j$ increasingly onto the increasing list of $\bigcup_{i\le r}A_{ij}$; this is a well-defined bijection because each $J_j$ has $\sum_im_{ij}=b_j$ elements, the target has the same number, and the targets for distinct $j$ are disjoint with union $\bigcup_iI_i=\{1,\dots,n\}$. By construction $\sigma_m(J_j)\cap I_i=A_{ij}$, so $M(\sigma_m)_{ij}=|A_{ij}|=m_{ij}$; and $\sigma_m$ is increasing on each $\beta$-block, while $\sigma_m^{-1}$ is increasing on each $\alpha$-block because for fixed $i$ the sets $A_{i1},A_{i2},\dots,A_{is}$, each listed increasingly, occur in this order in $I_i$ and are the images of $J_1,\dots,J_s$. Thus $\sigma_m$ is $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing with $M(\sigma_m)=m$. [construct, L1]

1.6 **The normal form of an arbitrary permutation.** Let $\sigma\in S_n$ and put $m:=M(\sigma)$. For all $i,j$ the sets $S_{ij}:=\{\,x\in J_j:\sigma(x)\in I_i\,\}$ and $T_{ij}:=\{\,x\in J_j:\sigma_m(x)\in I_i\,\}$ satisfy $|S_{ij}|=|T_{ij}|=m_{ij}$, and $S_{1j},\dots,S_{rj}$ as well as $T_{1j},\dots,T_{rj}$ partition $J_j$. Let $v\in S_n$ be the permutation which maps each $S_{ij}$ increasingly onto $T_{ij}$; it is well defined, and $v\in W_\beta$ because $v^{-1}(J_j)=J_j$ for every $j$. Let $u:=\sigma\circ v^{-1}\circ\sigma_m^{-1}$. Then $u\circ\sigma_m\circ v=\sigma$, and $u\in W_\alpha$: for $y\in I_i$ write $y=\sigma_m(w)$ with $w\in J_j$, so that $w\in T_{ij}$, hence $v^{-1}(w)\in S_{ij}$, hence $u(y)=\sigma(v^{-1}(w))\in I_i$. Therefore every $\sigma\in S_n$ satisfies $\sigma=u\,\sigma_{M(\sigma)}\,v$ with $u\in W_\alpha$ and $v\in W_\beta$. [construct, L2, L6]

1.7 **A length formula for products.** For a two-element subset $P=\{x,y\}\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}$ with $x<y$ and a permutation $w$ write $\delta_w(P):=1$ if $w(x)>w(y)$ and $\delta_w(P):=0$ otherwise, so that $\ell(w)=\sum_P\delta_w(P)$ over all two-element subsets. For $\rho,\rho'\in S_n$ and such a $P$ put $Q:=\rho'(P)=\{\rho'(x),\rho'(y)\}$. If $\delta_{\rho'}(P)=0$ then $(\rho'(x),\rho'(y))$ is the natural labelling of $Q$, so $\delta_{\rho\rho'}(P)=1$ exactly when $\delta_\rho(Q)=1$; if $\delta_{\rho'}(P)=1$ then $\rho'(y)<\rho'(x)$, so $\delta_{\rho\rho'}(P)=1$ exactly when $\delta_\rho(Q)=0$. Since $\delta$-values are $0$ or $1$ and $a+b-2ab$ is $1$ exactly when $a\ne b$ for such $a,b$, this gives $$\delta_{\rho\rho'}(P)=\delta_{\rho'}(P)+\delta_\rho(\rho'(P))-2\,\delta_{\rho'}(P)\,\delta_\rho(\rho'(P)),$$ and summing over all $P$, using that $P\mapsto\rho'(P)$ is a bijection of the set of two-element subsets, yields $$\ell(\rho\rho')=\ell(\rho)+\ell(\rho')-2N(\rho,\rho'),\qquad N(\rho,\rho'):=\#\{\,P:\delta_{\rho'}(P)=\delta_\rho(\rho'(P))=1\,\}.$$ In particular $\ell(\rho\rho')\le\ell(\rho)+\ell(\rho')$. [L2, L6]

1.8 **The map $\Psi$ is well defined.** Let $u\in W_\alpha$ and $v\in W_\beta$. By [L2], $P_u$ and $P_v$ are monomial matrices with $u(I_i)=I_i$ for all $i$ and $v(J_j)=J_j$ for all $j$, hence $P_u\in P_\alpha$ and $P_v\in P_\beta$ by [L1]; therefore $P_\alpha P_uP_\sigma P_vP_\beta=P_\alpha P_\sigma P_\beta$. Since $P_uP_\sigma P_v=P_{u\sigma v}$ by [L2] and [L6], the value $\Psi(W_\alpha w_\sigma W_\beta)=P_\alpha P_\sigma P_\beta$ does not depend on the chosen representative of the double coset $W_\alpha w_\sigma W_\beta$. [L1, L2, L6]

2.1 **Second differences.** Taking second differences of the identity of step 1.2, with the conventions $C_{0,j}=C_{i,0}=0$ coming from $V_0=\{0\}$ and $d_0=e_0=0$, gives $M(\sigma(x))_{ij}=C_{ij}(x)-C_{i-1,j}(x)-C_{i,j-1}(x)+C_{i-1,j-1}(x)$ for all $i,j$; hence the dimension matrix $C(x)=\bigl(C_{ij}(x)\bigr)$ alone determines $M(\sigma(x))$. [step 1.2]

2.2 **Invariance of the dimension matrix.** Let $p\in P_\alpha$ and $q\in P_\beta$. By [L1], $p$ stabilises the standard partial flag of type $\alpha$ and $q$ that of type $\beta$, so $p[V_{d_i}]=V_{d_i}$ and $q[V_{e_j}]=V_{e_j}$. By [L7] the restriction of the linear map $v\mapsto pv$ to a linear subspace $A\le V$ is a linear map $A\to V$, and by [L8] this map $v\mapsto pv$ is injective; an injective map satisfies $p[A\cap B]=p[A]\cap p[B]$ for any two subsets $A,B$: the inclusion $\subseteq$ is clear, and $p(a)=p(b)$ with $a\in A$ and $b\in B$ forces $a=b\in A\cap B$. Therefore $$V_{d_i}\cap pxqV_{e_j}=V_{d_i}\cap p[xV_{e_j}]=p[V_{d_i}]\cap p[xV_{e_j}]=p[V_{d_i}\cap xV_{e_j}],$$ using $q[V_{e_j}]=V_{e_j}$ in the first step and $p[V_{d_i}]=V_{d_i}$ in the second, and applying $\dim_{\mathbb F_q}p[U]=\dim_{\mathbb F_q}U$ from [L8] to $U=V_{d_i}\cap xV_{e_j}$ gives $C_{ij}(pxq)=C_{ij}(x)$ for all $i,j$. [step 1.2, L1, L7, L8]

2.3 **The double coset of $x$.** For every $x\in G$ one has $P_\alpha xP_\beta=P_\alpha P_{\sigma(x)}P_\beta$. One inclusion holds because $x=b_1P_{\sigma(x)}b_2$ with $b_1\in B\le P_\alpha$ and $b_2\in B\le P_\beta$ by [L1] and [L3], so $P_\alpha xP_\beta\subseteq P_\alpha P_{\sigma(x)}P_\beta$; conversely $P_{\sigma(x)}=b_1^{-1}xb_2^{-1}\in P_\alpha xP_\beta$, so $P_\alpha P_{\sigma(x)}P_\beta\subseteq P_\alpha xP_\beta$. [step 1.1, L1, L3]

2.4 **Block counts classify double cosets.** If $\sigma,\sigma'\in S_n$ satisfy $M(\sigma)=M(\sigma')$, then step 1.6 applied to both gives $\sigma,\sigma'\in W_\alpha\sigma_{M(\sigma)}W_\beta$, so $\sigma'$ lies in the same $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset as $\sigma$; conversely step 1.4 shows that all elements of one double coset share one block-count matrix. Since step 1.5 produces for every matrix $m$ with the prescribed margins a permutation $\sigma_m$ with $M(\sigma_m)=m$, the assignment $\tau\mapsto M(\tau)$ induces a bijection from $W_\alpha\backslash W/W_\beta$ onto the set of all matrices with nonnegative integer entries whose row sums are $a_1,\dots,a_r$ and whose column sums are $b_1,\dots,b_s$. [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 1.5, step 1.6]

2.5 **Length is additive along $\beta$-block permutations.** If $\rho$ is increasing on each $\beta$-block and $\rho'\in W_\beta$, then $N(\rho,\rho')=0$ in step 1.7 and hence $\ell(\rho\rho')=\ell(\rho)+\ell(\rho')$. Indeed, if $\delta_{\rho'}(P)=1$ for $P=\{x<y\}$, then $\rho'(x)>\rho'(y)$; since $\rho'$ maps each block $J_j$ onto itself and maps the blocks in increasing order, $x$ and $y$ lie in a common block $J_j$, so also $\rho'(x),\rho'(y)\in J_j$; as $\rho$ is increasing on $J_j$ and $\rho'(y)<\rho'(x)$, we get $\rho(\rho'(y))<\rho(\rho'(x))$, that is $\delta_\rho(\rho'(P))=0$, so no $P$ contributes to $N(\rho,\rho')$. [step 1.7, L2]

2.6 **Simple reflections.** For every $\rho\in S_n$ and every $1\le i<n$, where $s_i$ is the adjacent transposition of $i$ and $i+1$, one has $\ell(\rho s_i)=\ell(\rho)+1-2\,[\rho(i)>\rho(i+1)]$ and $\ell(s_i\rho)=\ell(\rho)+1-2\,[\rho^{-1}(i)>\rho^{-1}(i+1)]$. For the first identity, $s_i$ reverses exactly one two-element set, namely $\{i,i+1\}$, and fixes it, so in step 1.7 the sum defining $N(\rho,s_i)$ has the single term $\delta_{s_i}(\{i,i+1\})\,\delta_\rho(s_i(\{i,i+1\}))=[\rho(i)>\rho(i+1)]$, while $\ell(s_i)=1$. The second identity follows from the first applied to $\rho^{-1}$: using $\ell(s_i\rho)=\ell((s_i\rho)^{-1})$ and $(s_i\rho)^{-1}=\rho^{-1}s_i^{-1}=\rho^{-1}s_i$ together with $\ell(\rho^{-1})=\ell(\rho)$ gives $\ell(s_i\rho)=\ell(\rho)+1-2\,[\rho^{-1}(i)>\rho^{-1}(i+1)]$. [step 1.7, L2, L6]

2.7 **Uniqueness of the block-increasing element with given block counts.** Suppose $\sigma,\sigma'\in S_n$ are both $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing with $M(\sigma)=M(\sigma')=m$. For fixed $i$ put $A_{ij}:=\{\,t\in I_i:\sigma^{-1}(t)\in J_j\,\}$; then $|A_{ij}|=m_{ij}$ and the $A_{ij}$ partition $I_i$. If $t\in A_{ij}$ and $t'\in A_{ij'}$ with $j<j'$, then $\sigma^{-1}(t)\in J_j$ and $\sigma^{-1}(t')\in J_{j'}$, and every element of $J_j$ is smaller than every element of $J_{j'}$, so $\sigma^{-1}(t)<\sigma^{-1}(t')$, and $\sigma^{-1}$ increasing on $I_i$ gives $t<t'$; so the sets $A_{i1},\dots,A_{is}$ occur in $I_i$ in this increasing order and, having sizes $m_{ij}$, they are exactly the sets of step 1.5. Fix now $j$: the restriction of $\sigma$ to $J_j$ is increasing and has image $\bigcup_{i\le r}A_{ij}$, so that restriction is the increasing bijection of $J_j$ onto that union, and listing the union increasingly presents it as $A_{1j},A_{2j},\dots,A_{rj}$ with each $A_{ij}$ increasing. This is exactly the prescription defining $\sigma_m$ in step 1.5; the same computation applies to $\sigma'$, so $\sigma=\sigma'=\sigma_m$. [step 1.5]

3.1 **Block counts are constant on $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double cosets.** For all $p\in P_\alpha$, $q\in P_\beta$ and $x\in G$ one has $M(\sigma(pxq))=M(\sigma(x))$ by steps 2.2 and 2.1; equivalently $M\circ\sigma$ is constant on $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double cosets. [step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 **Minimal elements are block-increasing.** Let $\rho\in S_n$ be an element of minimal length in its $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset. If $\rho$ is not increasing on some $\beta$-block, there are $i,i+1$ in a common block $J_j$ with $\rho(i)>\rho(i+1)$; then $s_i\in W_\beta$, so $\rho s_i$ lies in the same double coset, and $\ell(\rho s_i)=\ell(\rho)-1$ by step 2.6, contradicting minimality. If $\rho$ is increasing on every $\beta$-block but $\rho^{-1}$ is not increasing on some $\alpha$-block, there are $i,i+1$ in a common block $I_{i'}$ with $\rho^{-1}(i)>\rho^{-1}(i+1)$; then $s_i\in W_\alpha$, so $s_i\rho$ lies in the same double coset, and $\ell(s_i\rho)=\ell(\rho)-1$ by step 2.6, again contradicting minimality. Hence every minimal-length element is $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing. [step 2.6, L2]

3.3 **$\Psi$ is surjective.** For $x\in G$ step 1.1 gives $\sigma(P_{\sigma(x)})=\sigma(x)$, so $\Psi(W_\alpha w_{\sigma(x)}W_\beta)=P_\alpha P_{\sigma(x)}P_\beta=P_\alpha xP_\beta$ by step 2.3; every $(P_\alpha,P_\beta)$-double coset therefore lies in the image of $\Psi$. [step 1.1, step 2.3]

3.4 **$\Psi$ is injective.** Suppose $P_\alpha P_\sigma P_\beta=P_\alpha P_\tau P_\beta$ for $\sigma,\tau\in S_n$. Then $P_\tau\in P_\alpha P_\sigma P_\beta$, say $P_\tau=pP_\sigma q$ with $p\in P_\alpha$ and $q\in P_\beta$; steps 2.2 and 2.1 applied to the pair $pP_\sigma q$ give $M(\sigma(P_\tau))=M(\sigma(P_\sigma))$, that is $M(\tau)=M(\sigma)$ because $\sigma(P_\rho)=\rho$ for every $\rho$ by step 1.1. Step 2.4 then gives $W_\alpha\sigma W_\beta=W_\alpha\tau W_\beta$: distinct $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double cosets have distinct images under $\Psi$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.4]

4.1 **The unique minimal representative in a double coset.** Let $D=W_\alpha\sigma W_\beta$ be a $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset and put $m:=M(\sigma)$. By step 1.6, $\sigma\in W_\alpha\sigma_mW_\beta$, so $\sigma_m\in D$ and $D=W_\alpha\sigma_mW_\beta$; by step 1.4 every element $\rho\in D$ has $M(\rho)=m$; by step 2.7 every $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing element of $D$ equals $\sigma_m$; and by step 3.2 every element of $D$ of minimal length is $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing. Hence $\sigma_m$ is the unique element of $D$ of minimal length, every other element of $D$ has strictly larger length, and $\sigma_m$ is the monotone matching of step 1.5. [step 1.4, step 1.5, step 1.6, step 2.7, step 3.2]

5.1 **Claim 3.** By step 4.1 every $(W_\alpha,W_\beta)$-double coset contains exactly one $(\alpha,\beta)$-block-increasing permutation, it is the unique element of the coset of minimal length, and it is the monotone matching $\sigma_M$ determined by the block-count matrix $M$ through the allocation rule of step 1.5; this is claim 3 of the statement. [step 1.5, step 4.1]

5.2 **Boundary cases.** For $n=1$ we have $\alpha=\beta=(1)$, $r=s=1$, $W_\alpha=W_\beta=\{1\}$, $\ell(\mathrm{id})=0$ and $M(\mathrm{id})=(1)$: there is one double coset and its unique block-increasing element is $\mathrm{id}$, the monotone matching. For $r=1$, that is $\alpha=(n)$ and $P_\alpha=G$, the parabolic Weyl subgroup is $W_\alpha=S_n$ by [L2], since the single block is preserved by every permutation; every $\tau$ then has the same block-count matrix, namely the row $(b_1,\dots,b_s)$ of column sums, the double-coset set $W_\alpha\backslash W/W_\beta$ consists of one class, and step 1.5 assigns to that row the permutation $\mathrm{id}$, which is increasing on every $\beta$-block with $\mathrm{id}^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ increasing on $I_1=\{1,\dots,n\}$. The case $s=1$, that is $\beta=(n)$ and $P_\beta=G$, is analogous: every $\tau$ has the single-column block-count matrix of row sums, there is one double coset, and its unique block-increasing representative is $\mathrm{id}$. All formulas above are stated for arbitrary compositions and require no separate treatment of these extremes. [step 1.3, step 1.5, step 4.1, L1, L2]

6.1 **Claims 1 and 2, and conclusion.** Steps 1.8, 3.3 and 3.4 exhibit $\Psi$ as a well-defined bijection, which is claim 1, and step 5.1 is claim 3. For claim 2 let $x,y\in G$: if $M(\sigma(x))=M(\sigma(y))$, then step 2.4 gives $W_\alpha\sigma(x)W_\beta=W_\alpha\sigma(y)W_\beta$ and step 2.3 gives $P_\alpha xP_\beta=P_\alpha P_{\sigma(x)}P_\beta=P_\alpha P_{\sigma(y)}P_\beta=P_\alpha yP_\beta$; conversely if $P_\alpha xP_\beta=P_\alpha yP_\beta$, then $y=pxq$ with $p\in P_\alpha$ and $q\in P_\beta$, so $M(\sigma(y))=M(\sigma(x))$ by step 3.1; and $W_\alpha\sigma(x)W_\beta=W_\alpha\sigma(y)W_\beta$ is equivalent to $M(\sigma(x))=M(\sigma(y))$ by step 2.4 together with step 1.4. The bijection onto matrices with the prescribed margins is step 2.4, and the boundary cases are step 5.2. ∎ [step 1.4, step 1.8, step 2.3, step 2.4, step 3.1, step 3.3, step 3.4, step 5.1, step 5.2]

## Remark

The argument uses only the Bruhat decomposition of [[thm-bruhat-decomposition-of-gl-n-over-a-finite-field]] and the intersection-dimension formula of [[thm-relative-position-classifies-pairs-of-complete-flags]]: no multiplication rule for Bruhat cells, and no Coxeter-theoretic exchange condition, appears anywhere. The combinatorial core of steps 1.5-1.7, 2.5-2.7 and 4.1 is self-contained, and it shows in addition that the block-count matrix may be replaced by the dimension matrix $\bigl(\dim_{\mathbb F_q}(V_{d_i}\cap xV_{e_j})\bigr)$ of step 1.2, which is the form of the classification used for partial flags on this page. This is the finite-field case of Dudas-Michel Lemma 9.9 (printed p. 40 of the Beijing lectures cited under Sources); their proof also uses minimal coset representatives, and over $\mathbb F_q$ no connectedness input is needed, every set occurring above being finite.
