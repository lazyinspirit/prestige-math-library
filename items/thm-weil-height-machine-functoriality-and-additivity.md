## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and let $K$ be a number field with algebraic closure $\overline K$. For a projective $K$-scheme $X$, let $X(\overline K)$ denote its algebraic points, let $\operatorname{Pic}(X)$ be the group of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves under tensor product, and let $B(X(\overline K))$ be the subgroup of bounded real-valued functions. There is a unique group homomorphism
$$
h_X:\operatorname{Pic}(X)\longrightarrow
\mathbb R^{X(\overline K)}/B(X(\overline K))
$$
such that if an invertible sheaf $\mathcal L$ is generated by finitely many global sections, then $h_X(\mathcal L)$ is represented by the function sending $P$ to the absolute logarithmic height of the projective point defined by those sections. For every $K$-morphism $f:X\to Y$ between projective $K$-schemes and every invertible sheaf $\mathcal L$ on $Y$, the homomorphisms satisfy
$$
h_X(f^*\mathcal L)=h_Y(\mathcal L)\circ f,
\qquad
h_X(\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal M)=h_X(\mathcal L)+h_X(\mathcal M).
$$
Thus the values are defined up to a bounded function, while pullback and tensor product give exact identities in the quotient group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a number field $K$; its algebraic closure $\overline K$; projective $K$-schemes $X,Y$; a $K$-morphism $f:X\to Y$; and invertible sheaves on these schemes.

[A1] AC is the premise of the projective-section and height suppliers used in the construction. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-projective-morphism-pre-proj]], [[def-absolute-logarithmic-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]])

[F1] Every invertible sheaf on a projective scheme is a tensor quotient of two globally generated invertible sheaves. ([[lem-invertible-sheaf-is-difference-of-globally-generated-sheaves]])

[F2] The invertible-sheaf and projective-morphism interfaces define the section maps; for a fixed generating system, its projective height is computed in any finite field of definition and is independent of enlarging that field. ([[def-invertible-sheaf]], [[def-projective-morphism-pre-proj]], [[thm-line-bundle-sections-define-projective-map]], [[def-absolute-logarithmic-weil-height-on-projective-space-over-a-number-field]], [[thm-absolute-height-is-invariant-under-field-extension]])

[F3] For fixed generating systems, the product system gives exact additivity of the corresponding projective height functions. ([[lem-height-additivity-for-globally-generated-line-bundles]])

[F4] The quotient target identifies two real-valued functions precisely when their difference is bounded; the Picard group consists of isomorphism classes of invertible sheaves with tensor product. ([[def-height-functions-modulo-bounded-functions]], [[def-invertible-sheaf]])

[L1] The generating-system comparison proves bounded difference on all algebraic points, uniformly over finite fields of definition, for any two finite generating systems of the same invertible sheaf. ([[lem-projective-height-class-is-independent-of-generating-system]])

## Proof

1.1 For a globally generated invertible sheaf $\mathcal M$, choose a finite generating system, possible since $X$ is quasi-compact. Let $g_X(\mathcal M)$ be the class of the projective height of its section map. It is independent of the system by [L1]. Isomorphic sheaves give the same class: transport the sections across an isomorphism and apply [L1]; transport changes no projective point since it multiplies all fibre coordinates by one common nonzero scalar. Product systems generate tensor products by [F3], which gives $g_X(\mathcal M\otimes\mathcal N)=g_X(\mathcal M)+g_X(\mathcal N)$. [given, A1, F2, F3, F4, L1, choose, algebra]

1.2 For any invertible $\mathcal L$, choose $\mathcal L\cong\mathcal M\otimes\mathcal N^{-1}$ as in [F1], and set $h_X(\mathcal L)=g_X(\mathcal M)-g_X(\mathcal N)$. If also $\mathcal L\cong\mathcal M'\otimes(\mathcal N')^{-1}$, then $\mathcal M\otimes\mathcal N'\cong\mathcal M'\otimes\mathcal N$. Applying step 1.1 to this isomorphism and using its additivity yields $g_X(\mathcal M)+g_X(\mathcal N')=g_X(\mathcal M')+g_X(\mathcal N)$. Thus the two differences agree exactly in the quotient by bounded functions. [given, F1, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

2.1 Tensoring two decompositions and using step 1.1 shows that $h_X(\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal L')=h_X(\mathcal L)+h_X(\mathcal L')$. The structure sheaf has the generating section $1$ and constant section map to $\mathbb P^0$, of height zero, so its class is zero and inverse sheaves give negatives. If $\mathcal L$ itself is globally generated, use the decomposition $\mathcal L\otimes\mathcal O_X^{-1}$ to obtain the required normalization. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Let $\mathcal M$ be globally generated on $Y$, with finite generating system $(s_i)$. Their pullbacks generate $f^*\mathcal M$: at each point, a section nonzero at its image remains nonzero after extending the residue field. By the chart description in [F2], the section map of the pulled-back system is exactly the original section map composed with $f$. Its height at $P$ is therefore the original height at $f(P)$, using a common finite field of definition. This proves $h_X(f^*\mathcal M)=h_Y(\mathcal M)\circ f$ for globally generated sheaves. Pullback of a bounded function is bounded, so this is an identity in the stated quotient. [given, F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Decompose arbitrary $\mathcal L$ on $Y$ as in [F1]. Pullback respects tensor products and inverses, and both globally generated factors pull back to globally generated sheaves. Subtract the two identities of step 3.1 and use step 1.2 to obtain functoriality for $\mathcal L$. Finally any group homomorphism with the prescribed normalization agrees with $g_X$ on globally generated sheaves and hence, by [F1], agrees with the difference formula for every invertible sheaf. This proves uniqueness. The empty scheme causes no exception: its function quotient is the zero group, and every assertion above is vacuous or zero. [given, F1, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
