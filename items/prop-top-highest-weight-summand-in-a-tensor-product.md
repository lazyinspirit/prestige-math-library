## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a fixed
positive system, and let $\lambda,\mu$ be dominant integral weights
([[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]). Then the tensor
product $V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)$
([[prop-direct-sum-dual-hom-and-tensor-representations]]) contains $V(\lambda+\mu)$
as a summand with multiplicity one, and every other irreducible summand has
highest weight strictly below $\lambda+\mu$ in the root order
([[def-partial-order-on-weights]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a fixed positive system, dominant integral $\lambda,\mu$, and the modules $V(\lambda)$, $V(\mu)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory used by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] $V(\lambda)$ and $V(\mu)$ are finite-dimensional irreducible highest weight modules with top lines $\mathbb Cv_\lambda$ and $\mathbb Cv_\mu$ and with all weights bounded above by $\lambda$ and $\mu$ respectively; the tensor product carries the action $x\cdot(v\otimes w)=x\cdot v\otimes w+v\otimes x\cdot w$ ([[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]], [[prop-the-highest-weight-space-of-an-irreducible-module-is-one-dimensional]], [[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]], [[prop-direct-sum-dual-hom-and-tensor-representations]]).

[L2] The tensor product is a finite direct sum of irreducibles $L(\nu)$ with $\nu$ dominant integral, and $L(\nu)$ has all weights $\le\nu$ and a unique highest weight $\nu$ ([[cor-every-finite-dimensional-representation-is-a-direct-sum-of-highest-weight-modules]], [[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]]).

[L3] The root order is a partial order with $Q_+$ as its positive cone: if $\nu_1\le\lambda$ and $\nu_2\le\mu$ then $\nu_1+\nu_2\le\lambda+\mu$, and if in addition $\nu_1+\nu_2=\lambda+\mu$ then $\nu_1=\lambda$, $\nu_2=\mu$ ([[def-partial-order-on-weights]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The vector $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ is nonzero and is a highest weight vector of weight $\lambda+\mu$: $\mathfrak n^+$ acts by $x\cdot(v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu)=x\cdot v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu+v_\lambda\otimes x\cdot v_\mu=0$ by [L1], and $H\cdot(v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu)=(\lambda+\mu)(H)v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$. [A1, L1]

1.2 Every weight of the tensor product is a sum $\nu_1+\nu_2$ of a weight of $V(\lambda)$ and a weight of $V(\mu)$ (the direct sum decomposition of the tensor product into weight spaces has components of this form), so by [L1] and [L3] every weight satisfies $\nu_1+\nu_2\le\lambda+\mu$. [L1, L3]

1.3 Let $U$ be the submodule of the tensor product generated by $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$; it is nonzero, and since the tensor product is a direct sum of irreducibles as in [L2], $U$ is the direct sum of some of the summands $W_r$. [L1, L2]

2.1 The $(\lambda+\mu)$-weight space of the tensor product is the direct sum of the spaces $V(\lambda)_{\nu_1}\otimes V(\mu)_{\nu_2}$ over pairs with $\nu_1+\nu_2=\lambda+\mu$; by [L3] the only pair with $\nu_1\le\lambda$, $\nu_2\le\mu$ and $\nu_1+\nu_2=\lambda+\mu$ is $(\lambda,\mu)$, so this weight space is $V(\lambda)_\lambda\otimes V(\mu)_\mu=\mathbb C(v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu)$ and has dimension one by [L1]. [L1, L3, step 1.2]

3.1 Decompose $V(\lambda)\otimes V(\mu)=\bigoplus_rW_r$ into irreducibles as in [L2], with $W_r\cong L(\nu_r)$; the top weight $\nu_r$ of each summand is a weight of the tensor product, so $\nu_r\le\lambda+\mu$ by step 1.2, and $(W_r)_{\lambda+\mu}=0$ unless $\nu_r=\lambda+\mu$ because all weights of $L(\nu_r)$ are $\le\nu_r$ by [L2]; comparing with step 2.1 shows that exactly one summand has highest weight $\lambda+\mu$. [L2, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 The $(\lambda+\mu)$-weight space of $U$ is one-dimensional: it contains the nonzero vector $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ and lies in the one-dimensional space of step 2.1; hence $U$ has exactly one summand isomorphic to $L(\lambda+\mu)$ and every other summand of $U$ has strictly smaller highest weight; but $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$ lies in that $L(\lambda+\mu)$ summand, which is therefore stable under the generation by $v_\lambda\otimes v_\mu$, so $U$ equals that summand and is isomorphic to $V(\lambda+\mu)$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.3]

5.1 By steps 3.1 and 4.1 the tensor product contains $V(\lambda+\mu)$ with multiplicity one and every other irreducible summand has highest weight below $\lambda+\mu$, as asserted. [step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
