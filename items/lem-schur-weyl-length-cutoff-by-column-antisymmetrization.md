## Statement

Let $V$ be a finite-dimensional complex vector space of dimension $d$, let
$n\ge0$, and let $E:=V^{\otimes n}$ with the left place action of $S_n$ of
[[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]. For every
$\lambda\vdash n$,
$$\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}\bigl(S^\lambda,E\bigr)\ne0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\ell(\lambda)\le d,$$
where $\ell(\lambda)$ is the number of nonzero rows of $\lambda$ and
$S^\lambda\subseteq M^\lambda$ is the complex Specht module
([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]]).
Equivalently, the complex irreducible $S_n$-module $S^\lambda$ occurs in
$V^{\otimes n}$ exactly for the partitions of $n$ with at most $d$ rows.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a finite-dimensional complex vector space $V$ of dimension $d$, an integer $n\ge0$, a partition $\lambda\vdash n$, and the module $E=V^{\otimes n}$ with its left $S_n$-action.

[F1] $\sigma\cdot(v_1\otimes\cdots\otimes v_n)=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$ defines a left $S_n$-action on $E$ with $E=V^{\otimes n}$ finite-dimensional and $E=\mathbb C$ for $n=0$ ([[def-commuting-symmetric-and-linear-actions-on-tensor-power]]).

[F2] $M^\lambda$ is free with the $\lambda$-tabloids as basis, $e_t=\kappa_t\cdot\{t\}$ with $\kappa_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\gamma$, $S^\lambda$ is the span of the polytabloids, $C_t\cap R_t=\{1\}$, and $e_{\sigma\cdot t}=\sigma\cdot e_t$, $\gamma\cdot e_t=\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)e_t$ for $\gamma\in C_t$ ([[def-column-antisymmetrizer-polytabloid-and-specht-module]], [[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F3] $S^\lambda$ is a nonzero irreducible $\mathbb C[S_n]$-module, and $S^\lambda$ is generated by $e_t$ for any single $\lambda$-tableau $t$ ([[thm-complex-specht-modules-are-irreducible]], [[lem-polytabloid-covariance-and-column-sign]]).

[F4] $R_t$ and $C_t$ preserve each row set and each column set of $t$ respectively, and the row stabilizer of the tabloid $\{t\}$ is $R_t$; every $\lambda$-tabloid is $\sigma\cdot\{t\}$ for some $\sigma\in S_n$, and $C_t=\prod_jS_{B_j}$ over the disjoint column label sets $B_j$ ([[def-row-and-column-stabilizers-of-a-tableau]], [[def-young-subgroup-tabloid-and-permutation-module]]).

[F5] If $e_1,\dots,e_d$ is a basis of $V$, the elementary tensors $e_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_n}$ form a basis of $E$, so distinct such tensors are linearly independent; $\ell(\lambda)=\lambda'_1$ is the height of the first column of $[\lambda]$ ([[thm-tensor-product-basis-from-bases]], [[def-linear-basis]], [[def-partition-young-diagram-and-conjugate-partition]]).

[F6] $\operatorname{sgn}$ is multiplicative and $\operatorname{sgn}((ab))=-1$ for a transposition ([[thm-sign-is-a-homomorphism]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 [construct] Assume $\ell(\lambda)\le d$ and fix a $\lambda$-tableau $t$ and a basis $e_1,\dots,e_d$ of $V$. Let $w_t\in E$ be the tensor with factor $e_i$ in every place labelled by an entry of row $i$ of $t$, that is, the place carrying label $a$ holds $e_{r(a)}$, where $r(a)$ is the row of the box of $t$ containing $a$; define $\Phi(\sigma\cdot\{t\}):=\sigma\cdot w_t$ for $\sigma\in S_n$ and extend linearly. If $\rho\in R_t$, then $\rho$ permutes only the places inside each row of $t$, all of which carry the same basis vector, so $\rho\cdot w_t=w_t$; hence, since the stabilizer of $\{t\}$ is $R_t$ and every tabloid is $\sigma\cdot\{t\}$ by [F4], $\Phi$ is a well-defined $\mathbb C$-linear map $M^\lambda\to E$, and it is $S_n$-linear by construction. [given, F1, F4, F5, construct, algebra]

1.2 Conversely, assume $\ell(\lambda)>d$, and let $Z$ be the set of labels in the first column of a $\lambda$-tableau $t$, so $|Z|=\lambda'_1=\ell(\lambda)>d$ by [F4, F5]. Let $A_Z:=\sum_{z\in S_Z}\operatorname{sgn}(z)z$, acting on $E$ through place permutations. Then $A_Z$ annihilates $E$: it suffices by linearity and [F5] to check this on a basis tensor $w=e_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{a_n}$, where the place carrying label $a$ holds $e_{a_a}$ for basis indices $a_a\in\{1,\dots,d\}$. Since $|Z|>d$, two labels $a\ne b$ of $Z$ carry the same basis vector, so the transposition $\tau=(ab)$ fixes $w$. Choose representatives $k$ for the right cosets $k\langle\tau\rangle$ in $S_Z$. By [F6], $$A_Z=\sum_k\operatorname{sgn}(k)k(1-\tau),$$ and therefore $A_Zw=0$. [given, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

2.1 For $\gamma\in C_t$, the tensor $\gamma\cdot w_t$ has at the place carrying label $a$ the factor $e_{r(\gamma^{-1}(a))}$, so $\gamma\cdot w_t=w_t$ holds exactly when $\gamma^{-1}$ maps every row set of $t$ to itself, that is, exactly when $\gamma\in R_t$; since $C_t\cap R_t=\{1\}$ by [F2], the tensors $\gamma\cdot w_t$, $\gamma\in C_t$, are pairwise distinct, and the coefficient of $w_t$ in $\kappa_t\cdot w_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\,\gamma\cdot w_t$ is the coefficient of the single term $\gamma=1$, namely $1$. By [F5] and [F2], $\Phi(e_t)=\Phi(\kappa_t\cdot\{t\})=\kappa_t\cdot w_t\ne0$. [given, F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Write $B_1,\dots,B_r$ for the column label sets of $t$. By [F4], $C_t=\prod_jS_{B_j}$ is the direct product over disjoint supports, so with $A_{B_j}:=\sum_{z\in S_{B_j}}\operatorname{sgn}(z)z$ the multiplicativity of the sign [F6] gives $\kappa_t=A_{B_1}A_{B_2}\cdots A_{B_r}$ in $\mathbb C[S_n]$; here $B_1=Z$. Since $A_{B_1}$ annihilates $E$ by step 1.2 and the operators commute, $\kappa_t$ acts as the zero operator on $E$. Also $\kappa_t\cdot e_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}\operatorname{sgn}(\gamma)\,\gamma\cdot e_t=\sum_{\gamma\in C_t}e_t=|C_t|e_t$ by [F2], so $e_t=|C_t|^{-1}\kappa_t\cdot e_t$ with $|C_t|\ne0$ in $\mathbb C$. If $f:S^\lambda\to E$ is $S_n$-linear, then $f(e_t)=|C_t|^{-1}f(\kappa_t\cdot e_t)=|C_t|^{-1}\kappa_t\cdot f(e_t)=0$; since $e_t$ generates $S^\lambda$ by [F3], $f=0$. Hence $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S^\lambda,E)=0$ when $\ell(\lambda)>d$. [given, F2, F3, F4, F6, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The restriction $\Phi|_{S^\lambda}:S^\lambda\to E$ is a map of $S_n$-modules, because $S^\lambda\subseteq M^\lambda$ is an $S_n$-submodule and $\Phi$ is $S_n$-linear by step 1.1; it is nonzero at $e_t$ by step 2.1. Its kernel is a proper $S_n$-submodule of $S^\lambda$, hence zero because $S^\lambda$ is irreducible by [F3]; therefore $\Phi|_{S^\lambda}$ is injective and $\operatorname{Hom}_{S_n}(S^\lambda,E)\ne0$ when $\ell(\lambda)\le d$. [given, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Steps 3.1 and 2.2 prove the equivalence for $n\ge1$; for $n=0$ we have $\lambda=\varnothing$, $\ell(\lambda)=0\le d$, $S^\varnothing=\mathbb C=E$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb C,\mathbb C)\ne0$, in agreement. If $d=0$ and $n\ge1$ then $E=0$, so every homomorphism into $E$ is zero, and indeed $\ell(\lambda)\ge1>0=d$; if $d=0$ and $n=0$ the previous case applies. This proves the claimed equivalence in all cases. [given, F1, F3, step 2.2, step 3.1, discharge-construct] ∎

## Remarks

- **Where irreducibility and nonvanishing are used.** The forward direction uses irreducibility of $S^\lambda$ only to convert a nonzero map into an injection, and uses the nonvanishing of $\kappa_tw_t$ to produce that map; the reverse direction uses $|C_t|\ne0$ in $\mathbb C$, so it does **not** survive in characteristic $p\le n$, where the corresponding multiplicity question is a modular branching question treated elsewhere.

- **Interpretation.** For $\lambda=(n)$ and $\lambda=(1^n)$ the cutoff requires $1\le d$ and $n\le d$; the corresponding multiplicity factors below are the symmetric and exterior powers of $V$, of dimensions $\binom{n+d-1}{n}$ and $\binom{d}{n}$, and the second vanishes exactly when $n>d$.

- **No choice.** The basis of $V$, the tableau $t$ and the tensor $w_t$ are fixed explicitly, and $Z$ is a finite set; no selection principle is used.
