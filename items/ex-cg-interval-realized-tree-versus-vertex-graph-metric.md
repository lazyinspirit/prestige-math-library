## Example

Let $\Gamma$ be a finite tree with vertex set $V$ and at least one edge, carrying its graph path metric $d_\Gamma$ ([[def-graph-path-metric]], [[thm-the-path-metric-of-a-connected-simple-graph-is-a-metric]]). Give each edge the length $1$ and form the **interval realization** $X_\Gamma$: a compact convex $1$-cell $[0,1]$ for each edge, glued at endpoints according to the incidence of $\Gamma$, with the chain metric $d$ of [[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]]. Then $X_\Gamma$ is connected, locally finite and has finitely many shapes, so $(X_\Gamma,d)$ is complete by [[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]] and is geodesic by the verification below; moreover:

**(i)** on the vertex set $V$, the chain metric restricts to the graph metric: $d(v,w)=d_\Gamma(v,w)$ for all $v,w\in V$, both equal to the number of edges of the unique $\Gamma$-path from $v$ to $w$;

**(ii)** every two points of $X_\Gamma$ are joined by exactly one geodesic segment ([[def-geodesic-and-geodesic-metric-space]]), and the midpoints of edges are points of $X_\Gamma$ at distance $1/2$ from their endpoints, so the intrinsic metric takes non-integer values on points that the discrete metric does not see;

**(iii)** the vertex set with its graph metric is **not** geodesic when $\Gamma$ has an edge: $d_\Gamma$ is integer-valued on distinct vertices, so no point lies at distance $1/2$ from a vertex;

**(iv)** if the edges are given prescribed lengths $\ell_e>0$ instead of $1$, then the chain metric still restricts on $V$ to the weighted path length $\sum_{e\in\mathrm{path}(v,w)}\ell_e$, which equals the unweighted graph distance $d_\Gamma$ only when every $\ell_e=1$; for instance on a single edge of length $2$ the two distances are $2$ and $1$.

Thus the discrete vertex metric is not a substitute for the interval-realized intrinsic metric: it is defined on a different set, it ignores edge lengths, and it is not geodesic.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite tree $\Gamma$ with vertex set $V$, edge set $E\ne\emptyset$ and $m:=|E|$; for every edge $e=\{a,b\}\in E$ a positive real $\ell_e$ and a cell $C_e:=[0,\ell_e]\subset\mathbb R$ whose endpoint $0$ is labelled by one endpoint of $e$ and whose endpoint $\ell_e$ by the other; the one-point cells $C_u:=\{0\}$ for $u\in V$; the isometric polyhedral gluing $X_\Gamma$ of shape $P=\{\varnothing\}\cup V\cup E$, where the vertex labels, edge labels and empty face are disjointly tagged, with $u<e$ exactly when $u$ is an endpoint of $e$, with the face isometries given by the labellings, the weak topology with its quotient maps $\iota_p\colon C_p\to X_\Gamma$, and the chain metric $d$ of [[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]].

[F1] An isometric polyhedral gluing and its chain metric candidate: cells, face isometries, the intersection condition, the weak topology, chains as finite sequences with consecutive pairs in a common cell, their lengths as sums of Euclidean distances, and $d$ as the infimum. ([[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]])

[F2] Under (H1)-(H3) the chain metric candidate is a metric inducing the weak topology, and $(X,d)$ is proper and complete. ([[thm-cg-polyhedral-chain-metric-topology-and-properness]], [[def-cg-abstract-isometric-polyhedral-gluing-and-chain-metric]])

[F3] A geodesic segment from $x$ to $y$ is a map $\gamma\colon[0,L]\to X$ with $\gamma(0)=x$, $\gamma(L)=y$ and $d(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|s-t|$ for all $s,t$; such a map is $1$-Lipschitz, hence continuous, and necessarily $L=d(x,y)$. ([[def-geodesic-and-geodesic-metric-space]], [[thm-metric-regularity-hierarchy]], [[def-metric-continuity]])

[F4] $\mathbb R$ with $d_{\mathbb R}(x,y)=|x-y|$ is a metric space, so its triangle inequality holds ([[lem-real-line-is-a-metric-space]]). If $u\le v$, then $|u-z|+|z-v|$ equals $v-u$ for $u\le z\le v$, equals $(v-u)+2(u-z)>v-u$ for $z<u$, and equals $(v-u)+2(z-v)>v-u$ for $z>v$. Symmetry covers $v<u$. Hence equality holds exactly when $z$ lies between the endpoints.

[F5] The path metric of a connected simple graph is a metric assigning to two vertices the least number of edges of a path joining them; a nonempty simple graph is a tree exactly when each pair of vertices is joined by exactly one path, and a tree is connected and acyclic. ([[def-graph-path-metric]], [[thm-the-path-metric-of-a-connected-simple-graph-is-a-metric]], [[def-cycles-trees-and-forests-in-a-simple-graph]], [[thm-a-simple-graph-is-a-tree-exactly-when-every-two-vertices-are-joined-by-a-unique-path]])

[F6] A closed interval $[a,b]\subset\mathbb R$ is order-convex, hence connected ([[cor-connected-subsets-of-the-line]], [[def-interval]], [[def-connected-space]]); a continuous image of a connected space is connected, and a union of connected subsets all meeting a fixed connected subset is connected ([[thm-continuous-image-of-a-connected-space]], [[thm-unions-of-connected-sets]]).

## Verification

1.1 (The gluing and (H1)-(H3).) Every principal down-set of $P$ is finite and isomorphic to the face poset of the corresponding cell: $P_{\le u}=\{\varnothing,u\}$ for the one-point cell $C_u$, and $P_{\le e}=\{\varnothing,a,b,e\}$ for the edge $e=\{a,b\}$, which is the face poset of the interval $C_e$ with its two vertices. Meets are $u\wedge e=u$ for an incident pair, $e\wedge f=u$ for distinct edges sharing the vertex $u$, and $\varnothing$ for disjoint edges, distinct vertices or nonincident vertex-edge pairs; also $p\wedge p=p$ and $p\wedge\varnothing=\varnothing$. The face isometries satisfy the cocycle condition vacuously (the only nonempty strict chains are $u<e$), the assignment $p\mapsto F_{p,e}$ is the poset isomorphism from $\{a,b,e\}$ onto the nonempty faces of $C_e$, and the intersection condition holds: the cell images are injective and two of them meet exactly in the image of the meet, which for distinct edges sharing a vertex is that vertex and otherwise is empty. Hence $X_\Gamma$ is an isometric polyhedral gluing. Local finiteness (H2): a point of $\iota_e(C_e)$ that is not a vertex point lies in that cell alone, and a vertex point lies in its point cell and exactly the cells of the finitely many edges at $u$. Finite shapes (H3): every cell is a point or an interval of one of the finitely many lengths $\{\ell_e:e\in E\}$. Connectedness (H1): order the edges $e_1,\dots,e_m$ so that each $e_i$ with $i\ge2$ shares a vertex with $\bigcup_{j<i}e_j$, which is possible because $\Gamma$ is connected; each cell inclusion is continuous by the weak-open trace criterion of [F1], so each $\iota_{e_i}(C_{e_i})$ is connected [F6], and $Y_i:=\bigcup_{j\le i}\iota_{e_j}(C_{e_j})$ is connected by induction on $i$, since $Y_{i-1}$ and $\iota_{e_i}(C_{e_i})$ are connected and meet in the shared vertex [F6]; finally $X_\Gamma=\bigcup_i\iota_{e_i}(C_{e_i})$, because every vertex of $\Gamma$ is an endpoint of an edge (an isolated vertex would contradict connectedness of $\Gamma$ with $E\ne\emptyset$), so its point cell is a face of an edge cell and its image is already covered. Thus (H1)-(H3) hold, and [F2] gives that $d$ is a metric inducing the weak topology and that $(X_\Gamma,d)$ is proper and complete. [F1, F2, F5, F6]

1.2 (Cutting a leaf edge: the induction claim.) Let $Z$ be the interval realization of a finite tree $\Gamma_0$ with $m_0\ge1$ edges and positive lengths, with chain metric $d_0$. We prove: there is a function $\lambda_0$ on pairs of points of $Z$, defined recursively below, such that (a) for all $x,y$ there is a chain from $x$ to $y$ of length $\lambda_0(x,y)$ and every chain from $x$ to $y$ has length at least $\lambda_0(x,y)$; (b) for vertices $u,w$ of $\Gamma_0$, $\lambda_0(u,w)$ is the sum of the lengths of the edges of the unique reduced path from $u$ to $w$; (c) exactly one geodesic segment joins any two points of $Z$. Base: if $m_0=1$ with edge $e$, then $Z=\iota_e(C_e)$, all points have coordinates in $[0,\ell_e]$ and the chains are sequences of such coordinates; a chain of $k$ steps has length $\sum_i|t_{i-1}-t_i|\ge|t_x-t_y|$ [F4], with equality for the one-step chain, so (a) holds with $\lambda_0(x,y):=|t_x-t_y|$; (b) is the case of two vertices, where $\lambda_0$ is $\ell_e$ for the two distinct vertices; and for (c), an isometric map $\gamma$ with $\gamma(0)=x$, $\gamma(L)=y$, $L=|x-y|$, satisfies $|\gamma(s)-x|=s$ and $|\gamma(s)-y|=L-s$ at every $s$ [F3], and in the real interval the unique point with these distances is $x$ if $L=0$, and otherwise the one at fraction $s/L$ from $x$ to $y$ [F4], so $\gamma$ is unique. Step: let $m_0\ge2$; take a simple path of maximal length in $\Gamma_0$. An endpoint $v$ has no neighbour outside this path (otherwise it extends), and no neighbour on the path except its next vertex $w$ (otherwise there is a cycle); hence $v$ is a leaf. Removing $v$ and $e=\{v,w\}$ leaves a connected acyclic graph $\Gamma_1$ with $m_0-1\ge1$ edges, since paths between remaining vertices cannot pass through the leaf; the induction hypothesis supplies $\lambda_1$ and (a)-(c) for $Z_1:=X_{\Gamma_1}$ with its own chain metric $d_1$; we will prove that $d_1=d_0|_{Z_1\times Z_1}$. Label the cell $C_e$ so that $w$ corresponds to $\ell_e$, and write $t_x$ for the coordinate in $[0,\ell_e]$ of $x\in\iota_e(C_e)$. Define $\lambda_0(x,y):=|t_x-t_y|$ if $x,y\in\iota_e(C_e)$; $\lambda_0(x,y):=(\ell_e-t_x)+\lambda_1(w,y)$ if $x\in\iota_e(C_e)$, $y\in Z_1$; use the symmetric cross formula when $x\in Z_1$, $y\in\iota_e(C_e)$, and put $\lambda_0(x,y):=\lambda_1(x,y)$ if $x,y\in Z_1$. The two formulas agree for $x=w\in\iota_e(C_e)\cap Z_1$, so $\lambda_0$ is well defined on all pairs. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, base, IH]

2.1 (The induction step, continued.) $Z=\iota_e(C_e)\cup Z_1$ and $\iota_e(C_e)\cap Z_1=\iota_w(C_w)=\{w\}$: the cell $C_v$ is a face of $C_e$ alone (as $e$ is the only edge at the leaf $v$), and every cell of $\Gamma_1$ meets $C_e$ in the image of the common face, which by the intersection condition is a face of the vertex cell $C_w$, hence contained in $\{w\}$. A chain passing from the leaf interval to $Z_1$ must visit $w$: at its first step leaving that interval the common cell is a cell of $\Gamma_1$, so the departing point lies in both pieces. Reversing a chain gives the same assertion for entry. In a chain with endpoints in $Z_1$, every maximal excursion into the leaf interval therefore starts and ends at $w$; delete these excursions. The remaining chain is in $Z_1$ and has no greater length, so every original chain has length at least $d_1(x,y)=\lambda_1(x,y)$, and an attaining chain in $Z_1$ gives the reverse bound. Thus $d_0=d_1$ on $Z_1\times Z_1$. For endpoints in the leaf interval, chains staying there have length at least $|t_x-t_y|$ by [F4]. A chain leaving it has an initial portion from $x$ to $w$ and a final portion from $w$ to $y$, both in the interval, of total length at least $(\ell_e-t_x)+(\ell_e-t_y)\ge|t_x-t_y|$; all intervening lengths are nonnegative. The one-step chain attains $|t_x-t_y|$, hence $d_0(x,y)=|t_x-t_y|$ and $d_0(x,w)=\ell_e-t_x$. For $x$ in the leaf interval and $y\in Z_1$, split at the first visit to $w$ preceding departure; the initial portion has length at least $\ell_e-t_x$, and the rest has length at least $d_0(w,y)=d_1(w,y)=\lambda_1(w,y)$. Concatenating the interval segment with an attaining $Z_1$ chain gives equality. The symmetric case follows by reversing chains. This proves (a) and $d_0=\lambda_0$ without presupposing any restriction identity. For (b): the unique reduced path from the leaf $v$ to a vertex $y$ of $\Gamma_1$ begins with the edge $e$, so the weighted path length from $v$ to $y$ is $\ell_e$ plus the weighted path length from $w$ to $y$, and the remaining vertex pairs lie in $\Gamma_1$; this is exactly the recursion defining $\lambda_0$. [F1, F2, F4, F5, step 1.2]

3.1 (The interval between two points, and geodesic uniqueness.) With the notation of steps 1.2 and 2.1, put $I(x,y):=\{z\in Z:d_0(x,z)+d_0(z,y)=d_0(x,y)\}$. We show by the same induction that $I(x,y)$ is a set on which $z\mapsto d_0(x,z)$ is injective. Base $m_0=1$: all points have coordinates in the interval and $|x-z|+|z-y|=|x-y|$ holds exactly for $z$ between $x$ and $y$ [F4], so $I(x,y)$ is the coordinate segment between $t_x$ and $t_y$, on which $z\mapsto d_0(x,z)=|t_x-t_z|$ is injective. Step, case $x,y\in Z_1$: if $z\in\iota_e(C_e)$, then by the additivity of step 2.1, $d_0(x,z)+d_0(z,y)=(d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,z))+(d_0(z,w)+d_0(w,y))\ge d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,y)\ge d_0(x,y)$, with equality only if $z=w$ (which is a point of $Z_1$) and $d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,y)=d_0(x,y)$; thus $I(x,y)$ is computed in $Z_1$, where the induction hypothesis applies. Step, case $x,y\in\iota_e(C_e)$: for $z\in\iota_e(C_e)$ the sum is $|t_x-t_z|+|t_z-t_y|$, equal to $|t_x-t_y|$ exactly for $t_z$ between $t_x$ and $t_y$ [F4]; for $z\in Z_1$ with $z\ne w$ the sum is $(d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,z))+(d_0(w,z)+d_0(w,y))>d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,y)\ge|t_x-t_y|$ by step 2.1; and $z=w$ is a point of $\iota_e(C_e)$ with coordinate $\ell_e$. Hence $I(x,y)$ is the set of points of $\iota_e(C_e)$ whose coordinate lies between $t_x$ and $t_y$, on which $z\mapsto d_0(x,z)=|t_x-t_z|$ is injective. Step, case $x\in\iota_e(C_e)$, $y\in Z_1$: for $z\in\iota_e(C_e)$ the sum is $d_0(x,z)+d_0(z,w)+d_0(w,y)\ge d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,y)=d_0(x,y)$, with equality exactly when $d_0(x,z)+d_0(z,w)=d_0(x,w)$, that is, when $t_z$ lies between $t_x$ and $\ell_e$; for $z\in Z_1$ the sum is $d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,z)+d_0(z,y)$, equal to $d_0(x,y)$ exactly when $d_0(w,z)+d_0(z,y)=d_0(w,y)$, that is, when $z\in I_{Z_1}(w,y)$. On the first part $z\mapsto d_0(x,z)=|t_x-t_z|$ is injective, on the second it is $d_0(x,w)+d_0(w,z)$, injective by the induction hypothesis, the distance values on the first part lie in $[0,d_0(x,w)]$ and those on the second lie in $[d_0(x,w),d_0(x,y)]$, with the shared boundary value attained only at $w$. Thus injectivity holds across the two parts as well. For the opposite ordering $x\in Z_1$, $y\in\iota_e(C_e)$, apply this case to $I(y,x)=I(x,y)$: injectivity of $z\mapsto d_0(y,z)$ and the identity $d_0(x,z)=d_0(x,y)-d_0(y,z)$ on $I(x,y)$ give injectivity from $x$ as well. Consequently, if $\gamma$ is a geodesic segment from $x$ to $y$ with $R=d_0(x,y)$, then for every $s$ the point $\gamma(s)$ satisfies $d_0(x,\gamma(s))+d_0(\gamma(s),y)=s+(R-s)=R$, so $\gamma(s)\in I(x,y)$ and $d_0(x,\gamma(s))=s$; injectivity of $z\mapsto d_0(x,z)$ on $I(x,y)$ determines $\gamma(s)$ uniquely for every $s$. Hence the geodesic segment is unique, proving (c); existence follows recursively: use the straight unit-speed interval segment for two points in the leaf interval, the inductively supplied geodesic for two points in $Z_1$, and their concatenation at $w$ for the cross case. Step 2.1 gives the isometry equality also for parameter pairs on opposite sides of $w$; when $x=y$ use the constant map on $[0,0]$. [F3, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, IH]

4.1 (Application to $\Gamma$.) Steps 1.2, 2.1 and 3.1 apply to the finite tree $\Gamma$ with its edge lengths; in the unit case $\ell_e=1$ all cells are intervals of length $1$, so there are two shapes (points and unit intervals), and the recursively defined $\lambda$ is the length of the tree path. [F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 (Clause (i).) Let $v,w\in V$. By clause (b) of the induction claim, $d(v,w)$ is the sum of the edge lengths over the unique reduced path of $\Gamma$ from $v$ to $w$, which for $\ell_e=1$ all is the number of its edges; by [F5] the graph path metric $d_\Gamma(v,w)$ is the least length of a path joining $v$ and $w$, and by the uniqueness of the reduced path in a tree the only reduced walk is that path, so $d_\Gamma(v,w)$ is the same number. Hence $d(v,w)=d_\Gamma(v,w)$ on $V$. [F5, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 (Clause (ii).) Existence and uniqueness of the geodesic segment joining any two points of $X_\Gamma$ are clauses (c) of the induction claim, verified in steps 1.2 and 3.1. For an edge $e$ and $u$ an endpoint of $e$, the midpoint $m$ of $e$ is the point of $\iota_e(C_e)$ at coordinate $\ell_e/2$, so $d(m,u)=|\ell_e/2-0|=\ell_e/2$ by the same-cell case of step 2.1, which in the unit case is $1/2$; in particular $d$ takes the non-integer value $1/2$ between two points of $X_\Gamma$, while the graph metric takes only integer values on distinct vertices by clause (i). [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.2]

5.2 (Clause (iii).) If $\Gamma$ has an edge, let $v,w$ be its endpoints; by clause (i) and [F5], $d_\Gamma(v,w)=d(v,w)=1$. If the metric space $(V,d_\Gamma)$ were geodesic, a geodesic segment from $v$ to $w$ would have length $1$ and its point at parameter $1/2$ would satisfy $d_\Gamma(v,p)=d_\Gamma(p,w)=1/2$ [F3]; but on the vertex set $d_\Gamma$ counts edges of paths [F5], so all its nonzero values are at least $1$ and $1/2$ is impossible. Hence $(V,d_\Gamma)$ is not geodesic when $\Gamma$ has an edge. [F3, F5, step 4.2]

6.1 (Clause (iv).) The general positive edge lengths were carried through steps 1.2, 2.1, 3.1 and 4.1 without change, so by clause (b) $d(v,w)=\sum_{e\in\operatorname{path}(v,w)}\ell_e$ on vertices. If some $\ell_{e_0}\ne1$, then for the two endpoints $v,w$ of $e_0$ one has $d(v,w)=\ell_{e_0}\ne1=d_\Gamma(v,w)$, so the weighted path length and the unweighted graph distance differ; conversely, if every $\ell_e=1$ they agree by clause (i). For the single edge of length $2$ the two values are $d(v,w)=2$ and $d_\Gamma(v,w)=1$. [F5, step 4.2, discharge-induction: step 1.2] ∎
