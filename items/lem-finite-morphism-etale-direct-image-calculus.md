## Statement

Let f:Y→X be finite, with no flatness, finite-presentation, reducedness or Noetherian hypothesis. Its direct image on small etale abelian sheaves is exact for all coefficients. For every g:X'→X, the actual pullback-counit map g^*f_*F→f'_*g'^*F is an isomorphism. At each GIVEN geometric point its stalk is the finite product of the coefficient stalks over the underlying finite geometric fibre, with the empty product zero. These identities give exactness of finite big-etale direct image and compatibility of canonical small-sheaf extension on every arbitrary big object. No family of geometric points, choice axiom or proper-basechange theorem is assumed.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let (R,m) be henselian local and B finite as an R-module. First, every element 1−z with z∈mB is a unit. Choose finite module generators for B. Multiplication by z has a matrix all of whose entries belong to m; its determinant relation gives (1+a)·1=(1−z)c for some a∈m and c∈B. Since 1+a is an R-unit, 1−z is a B-unit. Thus mB lies in the Jacobson radical, by the defining unit criterion, without selecting maximal ideals of an arbitrary ring. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Idempotents lift uniquely B/mB→B. For a given idempotent ε in the finite-dimensional k-algebra B/mB, choose finite vector-space generators for its ε and (1−ε) summands and lift them to B. They generate B as an R-module: their finite cokernel is zero modulo m, so the determinant-form Nakayama lemma kills it. Lift ε to b∈B. With the chosen ordered generator list, multiplication by b can be represented by a matrix whose reduction is diag(I_r,0_s): each error is in mB and is expressed with coefficients in m using that generating list. Its monic characteristic polynomial P annihilates b by the adjugate determinant identity, and P mod m=(T−1)^r T^s. If r=0 or s=0 the residue idempotent is 0 or 1 and those are the lifts. Otherwise henselianity factors P=GH with G mod m=(T−1)^r and H mod m=T^s. The resultant is a unit, so G,H are comaximal in R[T]. The resulting Chinese-remainder idempotent in R[T]/(P), equal to 1 modulo G and 0 modulo H, maps to an idempotent e∈B reducing to ε. If e,e' lift the same ε, the orthogonal idempotents e(1−e') and e'(1−e) lie in mB. Any such idempotent is zero because its complement is a unit by 1.1. Hence e=e'. This proves the lift for an arbitrary module-finite B, without assuming B is free or finitely presented. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 The finite-dimensional k-algebra B/mB is a finite product of local Artinian algebras. One finite-dimensional proof selects a proper ideal of maximal vector-space dimension; for the resulting maximal ideal n, choose N with n^N=n^{N+1}. The determinant trick for the finite-dimensional ideal n^N=(n^N)^2 produces an idempotent generating n^N. Its complement isolates the local factor with nilpotent maximal ideal and quotient the given residue field, exactly as the finite-localization lemma §33.1. Repeat on the other factor; dimensions decrease, so the process is finite. Lift these orthogonal idempotents by 2.1; their orthogonality and sum-one lift by uniqueness. Thus B=∏_{i=1}^r B_i. Each B_i is local: an element outside the inverse image of the unique maximal ideal of B_i/mB_i is invertible modulo mB_i, hence invertible by 1.1. The residue field k_i is finite over k. If k is separably closed, k_i/k is purely inseparable and k_i is itself separably closed (a finite separable extension of k_i would yield a finite separable extension of k after taking the finitely many needed inseparable powers). [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra]

4.1 Each local B_i is henselian. For every finite B_i-algebra C, it is also finite over R; hence idempotents lift uniquely from C/mC by 2.1. Writing n_i for the maximal ideal of B_i, n_i/mB_i is nilpotent, since B_i/mB_i is local Artinian. Thus n_iC/mC is nilpotent. Idempotents lift uniquely through a nilpotent ideal: for a square-zero error t=e²−e, replace e by e−t(2e−1)^{-1}, and iterate through finitely many square-zero stages; uniqueness is the orthogonal-idempotent argument. Consequently idempotents of C/n_iC lift uniquely to C. To recover the defining henselian factorization, apply this to C=B_i[T]/(P) for monic P and to the residue idempotent from any prescribed coprime factorization P̄=GH. The two lifted direct factors of C are finite projective B_i-modules, hence finite free over the local ring B_i. For completeness, choose a basis of the residue space, lift it, and use Nakayama for surjectivity; split the surjection by projectivity and apply Nakayama to its finite-projective complementary summand. Their multiplication-by-T characteristic polynomials are monic, multiply to P (the companion matrix has characteristic polynomial P), and reduce to G,H. Uniqueness follows from uniqueness of the idempotent, or monic division. Therefore each B_i is henselian; for strictly henselian R, §3.1 makes each B_i **strictly henselian**. This proves the needed decomposition, including nilpotents. B=0 has the empty decomposition. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 The finite-ring arguments in the finite-algebra preparation require no choice: for a henselian local ring H, every module-finite H-algebra is a finite product of henselian local rings, whose residue fields are finite over the residue field of H. Idempotent lifting is proved there using a determinant polynomial with residue T^r(T−1)^s and the **defining** monic coprime-factor Hensel property. Its proof does not suppose the algebra is free or finitely presented. The zero algebra gives the empty product. The finite-dimensional local decompositions use a proper ideal of greatest possible vector-space dimension and finitely descending dimensions, not arbitrary maximal-ideal existence. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra]

6.1 We need the following pointwise consequence of the actual geometric definition of étale, without its AC-qualified alternative-characterization supplier. If V→T is étale and v lies over t, then its fibre local ring at v is a regular zero-dimensional local ring, hence a field. In an affine fibre chart D all prime localizations are fields, so all its primes are minimal. For the **given** prime q, apply the unqualified finite-type Noether normalization to D/q. If its injected polynomial ring K[z_1,…,z_d], K=κ(t), had d>0, the explicit ideal (z_1,…,z_d) would have a nonzero finite-dimensional fibre of D/q: localization followed by the determinant Nakayama lemma proves nonzeroness. A proper ideal of greatest vector-space dimension in that fibre gives a nonzero prime of D/q, contradicting minimality of all primes of D. Thus d=0, D/q is a finite-dimensional domain and hence a field, and κ(v)/κ(t) is finite. It is separable: for a finite inseparable residue extension, construct a finite splitting field of its finitely many minimal polynomials by finite irreducible adjunctions. The repeated-root factor gives a nonreduced local ring after that field base change, contradicting the geometrically regular zero-dimensional fibre condition. This finite construction, not an algebraic closure, is sufficient. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 5.1, given, construct, algebra]

7.1 Consequently étale morphisms have zero relative differentials: after tensoring the finitely generated local differential module with κ(v), it is the differential module of the finite separable fibre field, hence zero. The determinant Nakayama argument makes its localization zero. Thus the differential sheaf is zero. The actual unqualified formally-unramified/differentials criterion and unramified-diagonal-open clause give the open diagonal. This use does not drop AC from the generic flat/unramified equivalence. In particular two maps from a local scheme to an étale T-scheme that agree at its closed residue point agree everywhere: their equalizer is open and contains the closed point, hence is the whole local scheme. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 6.1, given, construct, algebra]

8.1 The site operations needed below follow pointwise at this scope. Base change preserves finite presentation, flatness and the defining geometric-fibre condition. For composition, after any field base change the intermediate zero-dimensional fibre has local finite separable fields by 6.1; localization identifies the composite local fibre with the fibre of the second map over such a field. Separable towers and finite splitting fields give geometric regularity of dimension zero. Thus composition is étale. A jointly surjective étale cover remains surjective after base change: for a **given** point of the new base, choose a covering point above its image. The tensor of its finite separable residue field with the new residue field is a nonzero finite-dimensional algebra; a proper ideal of greatest vector-space dimension supplies a point of that tensor. This proves the cover base-change axiom without a prime-existence theorem for arbitrary rings or a family of closures. Identity and composite cover axioms are pointwise. These arguments also show that affine restrictions about a specified point may be taken finitely presented over an affine base. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 7.1, given, construct, algebra]

9.1 We use the actual inverse-image construction, not a geometric-stalk substitute. For h:T→S and a sheaf F on S_et, h^{-1}F is the sheafification of the presheaf whose sections on W are the colimit of F(V) over diagrams W→T×S V with V étale over S. Two precise elementary consequences will be used: [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 8.1, given, construct, algebra]

10.1 (i) a section of h^{-1}F is locally the image of a section s∈F(V) with such a diagram; [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 9.1, given, construct, algebra]

11.1 (ii) if the pullback of a section s∈F(V) is zero, this is locally witnessed by a further étale V_1→V on which s=0, together with a diagram into T×S V_1. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 10.1, given, construct, algebra]

12.1 These follow directly from the covering-sieve plus construction: a representative or equality is witnessed on a covering refinement. For presheaf-colimit equalities, a finite zigzag is refined using the fibre products of the finitely many V's and the equalizer opens from 7.1; equality is then one ordinary restriction equality. No representatives for an arbitrary indexed family are selected. At a **specified ordinary point**, choose one member and one point of the local witnessing cover. Its residue extension is finite separable by 6.1. This produces exactly the finite data used below. Kernels of sheaf maps are sectionwise, and sheaf epimorphisms are locally surjective, by the same associated-sheaf construction for cokernels. The assertion concerns all abelian groups, not the finite-coefficient Statement of an existing carrier. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 11.1, given, construct, algebra]

13.1 Fix an ordinary point x of an affine Spec A, with residue field k=κ(x). Consider **all** affine elementary pointed étale neighborhoods (U_λ,u_λ) with κ(u_λ)=k and the specified residue identification. They form a filtered system. Fibre products have the canonical point with residue k; equalizer opens from 7.1 equalize parallel arrows, followed by an affine restriction. Set H=colim Γ(U_λ,O). Evaluation in k makes H local: any element with nonzero evaluation becomes a unit by a pointed principal localization. Its residue is exactly k, since fractions of A at x already give κ(x). Every specified coprime monic residue factorization lifts: descend its polynomial's finite coefficient list to a stage; the finite-localization lemma's explicit monic factor/resultant chart is étale, and its specified factor coefficients define an elementary point with residue k. This is another member of the system, and its universal coefficients lift the factorization in H. Uniqueness follows from the idempotent/determinant argument in the finite-algebra preparation the coprime-factor uniqueness argument. Thus H is henselian. No separable closure was constructed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 12.1, given, construct, algebra]

14.1 If T=Spec C is henselian local and an étale W→T has a specified **rational** point over its closed point, then it has a T-section through that point. Apply the finite-localization lemma at that quasi-finite point. Its elementary U is built by two monic factor/resultant charts and principal localizations. The defining Hensel factor property gives a T-section of this particular U: lift its specified factors; each inverted resultant and denominator is a unit because its residue is nonzero. Pull the finite-localization lemma's finite open back along that section. It is finite étale over C, with the specified unique residue point and residue field κ(C). Its closed-fibre algebra is κ(C). Its element 1 therefore generates it by finite-module Nakayama, making it C/I; finite presentation makes I finitely generated. Flatness gives I/I²=0, and I⊂m_C gives I=0 by Nakayama. Hence this finite open is T itself and yields the required section. This is the the finite-algebra preparation §2 proof with a **given rational residue point**; separable closedness was only needed there to create rational points automatically. Uniqueness is 7.1. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 13.1, given, construct, algebra]

15.1 For a specified finite separable extension E/k, construct a finite étale henselian local H-algebra H_E with residue E. Choose a finite field-generator tower for E/k. At each step lift its monic separable irreducible polynomial to the preceding henselian local ring. Its derivative is a unit in the resulting monic finite free algebra: it is invertible modulo the closed ideal and that ideal is in the Jacobson radical by the finite-algebra preparation 1.1. All field fibres are reduced zero-dimensional (a finite splitting-field calculation with this derivative), so the algebra is étale by the actual definition. Its closed fibre is the prescribed field, making it local; it is henselian by 5.1. Continue finitely. This avoids a primitive-element theorem or an infinite compatible-root choice. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 14.1, given, construct, algebra]

16.1 These constructions have the precise finite-witness universality needed later. If A→H' is local at x, H' is henselian local with residue K, and k→K is the induced embedding, every elementary pointed U_λ admits a unique H'-section after base change: 14.1 applies to its rational closed point. Uniqueness makes these sections compatible, so they define H→H' without choosing a family of lifts. If additionally a specified embedding E→K is given, the finite monic tower in 15.1 lifts its specified simple residue roots uniquely, giving H_E→H'. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 15.1, given, construct, algebra]

17.1 Both H and H_E are limits of the actual étale neighborhoods just described. For H_E, its finitely many monic polynomials and unit/derivative certificates descend to one stage in the H system; thereafter they give a compatible system of finite étale neighborhoods with the specified E-point. Maps from an affine finitely presented algebra into these limits descend by finitely many generator images and relation equations; equalities of such maps descend too. In particular a module-finite B over A need not be finitely presented: B⊗A H_E is a filtered colimit of the B⊗A A_λ, and component idempotents, maps into **finitely presented étale B-algebras**, and all their finite compatibility identities descend. This is algebraic finite-equation descent, not continuity of arbitrary sheaf sections. Sections below descend because they are explicitly pullbacks from finitely many such étale algebras, glued on finitely many clopen components. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 16.1, given, construct, algebra]

18.1 Let k_i/k be a finite list of finite field extensions, and let L_{ij}/k_i be a finite list of finite **separable** extensions. There exists a single finite **normal separable** extension E/k such that each k_i⊗k E is a finite product of fields K_{iα} purely inseparable over E, and each L_{ij}⊗k_i K_{iα} is a product of copies of K_{iα}. This is an existence statement involving finitely many fields and generators, with no algebraic closure. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 17.1, given, construct, algebra]

19.1 In characteristic zero take the finite normal closure of the compositum of the finitely many k_i and L_{ij}, constructed by finite polynomial splitting. In characteristic p, let k_i^s be the subfield of elements separable over k. For every element a∈k_i, its irreducible polynomial has form Q(T^{p^r}) with Q separable, so a^{p^r}∈k_i^s. A common r for a finite generator list gives k_i^{p^r}⊂k_i^s. Thus k_i^s/k is finite separable and k_i/k_i^s purely inseparable. Choose finite separable generator lists for L_{ij}/k_i and their separable minimal polynomials P. Raise their coefficients to a sufficiently large common p-power so all lie in k_i^s; the resulting coefficient-Frobenius polynomial is still separable. Adjoin its splitting roots and the k_i^s to a finite separable field over k, then take a finite normal splitting field over k for the minimal polynomials of its finitely many generators. All these operations are finite irreducible adjunctions and finite factorization choices. Call the resulting field E. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 18.1, given, construct, algebra]

20.1 Normality ensures that **every** embedding k_i^s→E has the same splitting property for the transported polynomials. The tensor k_i^s⊗k E splits into copies of E; adjoining the purely inseparable k_i gives fields K_{iα} purely inseparable over E (finite separable and purely inseparable extensions are linearly disjoint; this also follows by successive p-power irreducibility tests). Over any K_{iα}, a root of P has a p-power in E because the coefficient-Frobenius polynomial splits there. It is therefore purely inseparable over K_{iα}, whereas P is separable. Each field factor is consequently K_{iα}; equivalently its inverse p-power is polynomially expressible using separability. This proves 18.1. The argument includes nonprimitive mixed extensions, inseparability of k_i/k, repeated component indices and an empty list (E=k). [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 19.1, given, construct, algebra]

21.1 Over H_E of 15.1, any finite A-algebra B consequently decomposes into henselian local components whose residue fields are purely inseparable over E, once E contains normal closures of the separable parts of the finitely many residue fields of B⊗A k. After any further henselian local base change H_E→H' with a field embedding E→κ(H'), **each such component has exactly one closed point and none disappears**. Indeed its residue field K_i is finite purely inseparable over E, and K_i⊗E κ(H') is a nonzero local finite-dimensional algebra with one prime; possible nilpotents are allowed. A direct p-power calculation proves this: in a finite pure tower each element has a p-power in the base field, so the radical quotient has a unique possible field embedding and the kernel is nilpotent. The finite-dimensional algebra then has one prime. Section 5.1 gives the corresponding single henselian local component upstairs. This controls the component matching used in base change. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 20.1, given, construct, algebra]

22.1 Kernels are preserved sectionwise, so prove preservation of epimorphisms. Let F→G be a sheaf epimorphism on Y_et, let U→X be étale, let s∈G(Y_U), and fix an **ordinary** x∈U. Restrict U to an affine neighborhood Spec A and write Y_U=Spec B, B module-finite over A. The finite-dimensional algebra B⊗A κ(x) has a finite list of points y_i, with finite residue fields k_i/κ(x), by finite-dimensional algebra. Local surjectivity gives for each y_i an étale V_i→Y_U, a point v_i over y_i with finite separable residue L_i/k_i, and a section t_i∈F(V_i) lifting s|V_i. Choose affine finitely presented V_i around these finitely many specified points. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 21.1, given, construct, algebra]

23.1 Form H and choose E/k=κ(x) by §3 splitting all k_i and L_i as specified. Over H_E, B decomposes into finitely many henselian local components. Every component has one of the prescribed V_i with a rational closed point: its residue field is one of K_{iα}, and L_i⊗k_i K_{iα} is split. Section 14.1 lifts that rational point to a section from the **whole component** to V_i. Pull t_i back to that component. Its image is the restriction of s, and these lifts glue across the finite clopen decomposition. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 22.1, given, construct, algebra]

24.1 Descend the component idempotents, the finitely many maps to V_i and their finite defining equations by 17.1 to one actual étale neighborhood U'→U of x (its chosen residue is E, not necessarily k). Pulling the original t_i back and gluing gives t∈F(Y_{U'}) whose image is literally s|Y_{U'}: the identity already holds on the original V_i. No arbitrary sheaf-section continuity is being asserted. Taking the family of **all** such witness neighborhoods for **all** x∈U gives a jointly surjective étale covering of U; this does not select one witness for each x. Thus f_*F→f_*G is locally surjective and is an epimorphism. This proves exactness on all abelian sheaves for every finite f. The empty fibre needs no V_i; its zero algebra equality descends at one stage and gives the unique lift on the empty inverse image. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 23.1, given, construct, algebra]

25.1 Fix an arbitrary square with g:X'→X and finite f. β is the adjoint of the pulled-back counit f^{-1}f_*F→F, or equivalently the natural restriction of a represented section of F(Y_U). We prove β locally surjective and injective on the **small étale site of X'**, using ordinary points. This suffices for an isomorphism of abelian sheaves; no family of geometric stalks is used. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 24.1, given, construct, algebra]

26.1 Let U'→X' be étale, fix x'∈U', and put x=g(x'), k=κ(x), k'=κ(x'). Shrink to affine charts about x and x'; all the finite data used below are on these charts. Let B be the finite algebra of Y over the first chart. Each residue field of B⊗A k is a finite extension of k. Each residue field of the finite fibre over x' is a finite extension of k'. Nothing requires k'/k to be algebraic. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 25.1, given, construct, algebra]

27.1 Suppose t∈(g'^{-1}F)(Y'_{U'}). For each point y'_l of its **finite** fibre over x', apply 9.1(i). This gives an affine finitely presented étale W_l→Y'_{U'} through a point w_l over y'_l, an affine finitely presented étale V_l→Y over its image, a map W_l→Y'×Y V_l, and s_l∈F(V_l) whose pullback equals t|W_l. The residue extension κ(w_l)/κ(y'_l) is finite separable. The image v_l in V_l has residue finite separable over the appropriate original fibre residue field k_i. These are finitely many original and target residue extensions, not fields of fractions of arbitrary big objects. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 26.1, given, construct, algebra]

28.1 Choose E/k normal separable by §3 splitting all original k_i and all κ(v_l)/k_i. Separately choose D_0/k' normal separable splitting the target residues κ(y'_l)/k' and κ(w_l)/κ(y'_l). To arrange an embedding of E as well, take a field factor D of the nonzero finite étale algebra D_0⊗k E. Such a factor exists by finite-dimensional algebra; it is finite separable over k' and has specified embeddings of E and D_0. All prior splitting properties persist after this extension. Construct the ordinary henselization H' at x' and its finite étale henselian extension H'_D. The map A→H'_D gives the canonical map H→H'_D from 16.1, and the specified E→D lifts uniquely to H_E→H'_D. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 27.1, given, construct, algebra]

29.1 The original components of B⊗A H_E have residue fields purely inseparable over E. By 21.1 they correspond **bijectively** to the components after base change to H'_D; each stays one local component, though its fibre can acquire nilpotents. In particular no original component is lost or silently merged. For each new component choose its original W_l (according to the point y'_l below it) and one rational closed point of that W_l, available by the D splitting. Section 14.1 gives a section of W_l on the entire new component. Composing gives a map ψ_l from this component to the original V_l, and t equals the pullback of s_l there. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 28.1, given, construct, algebra]

30.1 Let T_i be the corresponding **original** component over H_E, with residue K_i. The fibre point v_l of V_l lies over the original residue k_i. The algebra κ(v_l)⊗k_i K_i is a product of copies of K_i by choice of E. Therefore the residue map of ψ_l is the base change of **one** of its K_i-rational points: a homomorphism from a product K_i^n to the local residue field of the new component selects exactly one factor. Apply 14.1 to that rational point to get a map T_i→V_l. After pulling it to the new component, this map and ψ_l agree on its closed residue point. Their equalizer is open by 7.1 and contains that closed point, hence they agree on the **whole** local component. This is why splitting all conjugates and the purely inseparable component control are necessary; merely choosing an arbitrary original residue embedding would not justify equality with t. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 29.1, given, construct, algebra]

31.1 Pull s_l back along T_i→V_l from 30.1 and glue over the finite original decomposition of B⊗A H_E. As in 24.1, it is represented by a genuine section u∈F(Y_V) at one actual étale neighborhood V→X of x, with an E-valued point. Here “represented” is proved by descending these finite component idempotents and maps to the finitely presented V_l; it is not a general continuity assertion for F. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 30.1, given, construct, algebra]

32.1 The map H_E→H'_D gives a map from Spec H'_D to this V. Since V→X is finitely presented on the affine chart, this map descends, by finite equations, to a single actual étale neighborhood V'→U' of x' with the chosen finite residue extension D. Thus u represents a section a∈g^{-1}f_*F on V'. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 31.1, given, construct, algebra]

33.1 Its image under β equals t after a further étale refinement of V', with actual finite witnesses. Indeed descend to a common stage the finite new-component idempotents, the maps of those components to the original W_l, the maps to V_l, and their equality from 30.1. Equality of the latter maps is equality on finitely many algebra generators, because V_l is finitely presented over the affine original Y. On each descended clopen component t is the pullback of s_l by the **original** representative identity t|W_l=s_l, while βa is the pullback by the equal map to V_l. Hence the two sections are literally equal on those components and glue to equality on all Y' over that neighborhood. This does not descend an unstructured assertion that two arbitrary sheaf sections happen to agree at a limit. There are finitely many explicit representative identities and morphism equations witnessing it. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 32.1, given, construct, algebra]

34.1 We have obtained a local preimage near each ordinary x'. The family of all these witness étale neighborhoods covers U', so β is an epimorphism. No embeddings or representative choices over an infinite family of points were made. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 33.1, given, construct, algebra]

35.1 Let a∈g^{-1}f_*F(U') with βa=0 and fix x'∈U'. By 9.1(i), after an étale neighborhood through a point over x', a is represented by one u∈F(Y_V) and a map from that neighborhood to V étale over X. Replace X by V and X' by this neighborhood. The problem is now a section u∈F(Y) whose inverse-image section on Y' is zero. This replacement changes no finiteness hypothesis and uses no rational-residue-point assumption. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 34.1, given, construct, algebra]

36.1 For each point of the finite fibre of Y' over the chosen ordinary point, apply 9.1(ii). We obtain finitely many affine finitely presented étale W_l→Y' with points over that fibre, maps W_l→Y'×Y V_l with V_l étale over Y, and the actual identity u|V_l=0. Choose E/k and D/k' exactly as in 28.1, now using the residue extensions of these zero witnesses. Components correspond exactly as in 29.1, and for each original H_E component the chosen zero-witness V_l has a rational point matching a point of the corresponding new component, by 30.1. Section 14.1 therefore gives a map of the whole original component to that V_l. It follows that u vanishes on every original H_E component. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 35.1, given, construct, algebra]

37.1 Descend those finite component maps and idempotents to an actual étale neighborhood V→X; on its Y inverse image u is **literally** zero because u|V_l=0 already held on each original witness. Descend the compatible map from Spec H'_D to V to an actual étale neighborhood of x', as in 32.1. The represented section a is zero there. All such witnesses form a covering, so a=0 by the sheaf condition. Thus β is a monomorphism, and §§6–7 prove that it is an isomorphism. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 36.1, given, construct, algebra]

38.1 The constructions are the actual natural restriction/counit maps. Identity and iterated-base-change compatibilities follow on their local representatives and hence as sheaf maps; there is no isomorphism chosen merely by matching cardinalities. Empty sources/fibres are handled by the zero algebra and empty clopen gluing; nonreduced and radicial components are retained by 21.1. Arbitrary g can have transcendental residue fields: only the finitely many **separable** witness extensions over k and k' are split, and D_0⊗k E is finite étale over D_0 even in this case. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 37.1, given, construct, algebra]

39.1 Exactness applies on an arbitrary big-site object T by applying §4 to the finite base change Y_T→T. For a big étale sheaf G on Y, its restriction to the actual small site of each Y_T is an abelian sheaf and epimorphisms stay locally surjective there, since an étale cover of an object étale over Y_T remains such an object. Thus the same local-lift proof establishes exactness of finite big-étale direct image, not only small-site exactness. For F from the small site, the arbitrary-base-change identity just proved identifies its finite direct image with the canonical small-sheaf extension on **every** T→X. This is precisely the previously missing arbitrary-T interface in the topology-comparison induction, not a restriction to finite k-algebras. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 38.1, given, construct, algebra]

40.1 For a GIVEN geometric point with algebraically closed field Omega, form its pointed-etale neighborhood colimit exactly as the ordinary henselization construction, allowing its finite separable residue subfields in Omega. Every finite separable residue element occurs by a monic separable polynomial chart with derivative inverted at that specified root. The colimit residue field is therefore the separable closure in Omega, and the same factor-chart argument makes the colimit henselian. Every finite algebra over it splits into the finitely many henselian local components already proved; their residue fields are purely inseparable over that separably closed field. Hence they correspond exactly to the finite points of the geometric fibre. For any germ at one component choose one affine finitely presented pointed-etale representative. The rational section construction gives a map from the entire henselian component into that representative. Descend these finitely many maps and idempotents at one pointed-etale stage and glue their sections. This proves surjectivity of the finite-product stalk map. If a section has zero germ on every component, choose finitely many zero-representative neighborhoods, descend the same maps and idempotents, and obtain zero at one stage. This proves injectivity. The empty fibre is the zero algebra, whose equality1=0 descends to a stage. Thus the stalk formula holds for each given geometric point; no selection of points or global stalk-conservativity is used to prove exactness or base change. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 39.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
