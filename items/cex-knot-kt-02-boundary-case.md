## Statement refuted

Assume countable choice. For every smooth oriented knot $K$ with a chosen product tubular neighbourhood $N(K)\cong S^1\times D^2$ using its product Euclidean fibre norm, define its exterior by $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ and its boundary torus by $T_K=\partial E_K$. Let $i:T_K\hookrightarrow E_K$ be inclusion and let $p:T_K\to S^1$ be the product projection to the oriented core. Every primitive class $c\in H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$ with $p_*c=1\in H_1(S^1;\mathbb Z)$ is in $\ker i_*$. Here primitive means that $c$ is not an integer multiple $m d$ of another class with $|m|>1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable choice, a smooth oriented knot with a chosen product tubular neighbourhood, and its oriented boundary torus.

[A1] Countable choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, is [[def-countable-choice]].

[F1] [[def-knot-exterior]] defines $E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}N(K)$ and retains its boundary torus $T_K$.

[F2] [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]] orients the fibre meridian and defines the preferred longitude by degree one over the oriented core and zero image in $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)$.

[F3] [[lem-knot-exterior-has-peripheral-torus]] identifies $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$ with the product meridian and section basis $([\mu],[\sigma])$.

[F4] [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] gives $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, generated by $[\mu]$, with $[\lambda]$ a degree-one kernel class, and says $([\mu],[\lambda])$ is a basis of $H_1(T_K;\mathbb Z)$.

## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], the exterior inclusion and the meridian and longitude classes have the stated meanings. In the basis from [F3], the degree-one property in [F4] writes $[\lambda]=[\sigma]+q[\mu]$ for a unique integer $q$. The curve $\gamma(t)=(e^{2\pi it},e^{2\pi i(q+1)t})$ is embedded because its first coordinate is injective on $S^1$, and it represents $[\sigma]+(q+1)[\mu]=[\lambda]+[\mu]$. Its projection to the oriented core has degree $+1$. [A1, F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct]

2.1 In the basis $([\mu],[\lambda])$ from [F4], the class $[\lambda]+[\mu]$ has coordinates $(1,1)$. If it were $m d$ for an integer $|m|>1$, both coordinates would be divisible by $m$, which is impossible; hence it is primitive. [F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The inclusion sends $[\lambda]+[\mu]$ to $[\mu]$ because $[\lambda]\in\ker i_*$. The latter is a generator of $H_1(E_K;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$, so this image is nonzero. The primitive degree-one class constructed above therefore contradicts the refuted assertion. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
