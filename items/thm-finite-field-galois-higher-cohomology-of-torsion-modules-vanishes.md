## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a finite field, let $G_k$ be its
absolute Galois group, and let $M$ be a discrete torsion abelian group with a
continuous $G_k$-action. Then
$$H^r_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_k,M)=0\qquad(r\ge2).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a finite field $k$, and a discrete torsion $G_k$-module $M$.

[A1] AC is the premise of the supplied absolute-Galois-group description.
([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[A2] AC implies DC, as required for the supplied derived-functor
interpretation of continuous cohomology.
([[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]],
[[def-dependent-choice]])

[F1] $G_k\cong\widehat{\mathbb Z}$ with finite cyclic quotients generated by
arithmetic Frobenius. ([[thm-absolute-galois-group-of-finite-field-is-procyclic]])

[F2] Continuous cohomology is the filtered direct limit of finite-quotient
cohomology with fixed coefficients. ([[thm-continuous-profinite-cohomology-as-direct-limit-of-finite-quotients]])

[F3] For a finite cyclic quotient, inflation from $C_n$ to $C_{na}$ in degree
$r=2j$ or $2j+1$ multiplies by $a^j$ for $j\ge1$.
([[lem-cyclic-group-cohomology-norm-and-inflation]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** kill each finite-level class by a sufficiently large
cyclic inflation.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 Let $\alpha\in H^r_{\mathrm{cont}}(G_k,M)$ with $r\ge2$. By [A2] and [F2], it is represented at some quotient $C_n$ with coefficient module $M^{U_n}$. A representing cochain has finitely many values; since $M$ is torsion, their $C_n$-stable span is a finite subgroup $M_0$ of exponent $d\ge1$. Thus $\alpha$ is represented in $H^r(C_n,M_0)$. [A1, A2, F1, F2, given]

2.1 Inflate from $C_n$ to $C_{nd}$. By [F3], the class becomes a multiple $d^j$ of a class computed with coefficients in $M_0$, for $r=2j$ or $r=2j+1$, $j\ge1$. Every cohomology group with coefficients in $M_0$ is annihilated by its exponent $d$, so this inflated class is zero. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Every class in the direct limit is killed at a later finite quotient by step 2.1. Therefore the direct limit in [F2] is zero in every degree $r\ge2$, which is the Statement. AC and DC are used through [A1]–[A2]. [A1, A2, F2, step 2.1] ∎
