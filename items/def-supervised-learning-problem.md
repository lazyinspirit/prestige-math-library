## Definition

A **supervised prediction problem** consists of nonempty measurable spaces $(\mathsf X,\Sigma_X)$ of instances, $(\mathsf Y,\Sigma_Y)$ of labels, and $(\mathsf A,\Sigma_A)$ of prediction actions; the product measurable space $(\mathsf X\times\mathsf Y,\Sigma_X\otimes\Sigma_Y)$; a probability measure $P$ on that product; a nonempty class $\mathcal F$ of measurable predictors $f:\mathsf X\to\mathsf A$; and a loss $\ell:\mathsf A\times\mathsf Y\to[0,+\infty]$ that is measurable with respect to $\Sigma_A\otimes\Sigma_Y$. The law $P$ need not be supported on a deterministic labeling function, and this definition imposes no assumption that sample observations be independent or share a common law.

## Remarks

The joint law $P$ can be used at a fixed member of a statistical experiment. If it is selected from a parametric family, this prediction definition makes no parameter-recovery claim, and the family of laws need not distinguish parameter labels.

## Remarks references

[[def-parametric-statistical-model]]: the parameter-to-law map need not be injective.
