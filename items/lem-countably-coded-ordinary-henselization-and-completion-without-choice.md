## Statement

Let $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ be a Noetherian local ring equipped with a specified surjection $\mathbb N\to R$. In ZF, its ordinary henselization $H$ has a specified surjection $\mathbb N\to H$, is Noetherian, has residue field $k$, and has maximal-adic completion canonically isomorphic to $\widehat R$. The map $H\to\widehat R$ is faithfully flat.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian local ring and its specified enumeration.

[F1] A finite monic simple-root equation with specified residue root may be adjoined by a pointed localization; equality of two such roots follows from the invertible difference quotient. [[def-henselian-pair-and-henselian-local-ring]]

[F2] The unqualified simple-root criterion is equivalent to the henselian factor-lifting property. [[cor-henselian-local-simple-root-criterion]]

[F3] Completion of a countably coded Noetherian ring is Noetherian and flat, and is faithfully flat for its maximal ideal. [[lem-countably-coded-noetherian-completion-without-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** all finite pointed root towers and faithful completion descent.

1.1 Use all FINITE TOWERS of the following local simple-root extensions. From a countably coded local ring B with residue k, take a monic f∈B[T] and a specified a∈k with fbar(a)=0 and fbar'(a)≠0. Form B[T]/f and localize at the kernel of its evaluation map to k, T↦a. This kernel is specified, not selected by a prime-existence principle. The resulting ring is Noetherian. It has a canonical enumeration from finite polynomial strings and numerator/denominator codes. All finite towers and their finite root data are uniformly coded by natural numbers, because R and k have specified enumerations. This does not use the AC-qualified general standard-étale neighbourhood theorem. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Each such extension B' has residue k and maximal ideal m_B B'. Its local fibre is the localization of k[T]/fbar at T−a. Since a is a simple root, that localization is k: write fbar=(T−a)g with g(a)≠0, so g is a unit in that local ring and T−a vanishes. Thus at every finite tower stage the maximal ideal is generated by the original m, and the closed fibre remains k. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Two local R-maps between these tower stages preserving the specified residue point are equal. Induct on the tower. Once maps agree on the previous ring, the two candidate images x,y of the next root satisfy the same f and x−y is in the target maximal ideal. The identity f(x)−f(y)=(x−y)(f'(y)+(x−y)Q(x,y)) has a unit second factor, so x=y. The same argument applies to any local target, not just to other stages. Finite towers have common successors: first perform one tower, then base change the successive simple-root equations of the second tower and localize at their specified k-roots. Each new derivative is still nonzero at that root. This receives both original stages. Hence the diagram is filtered, with unique parallel local maps, using only finite common-successor constructions. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 Let H be its ring colimit, formed as the disjoint union of uniformly coded elements modulo eventual equality. This disjoint union is uniformly countably coded, giving a specified surjection ω→H. There is no inference that an arbitrary countable union of countable sets is countable. The local transition maps make H local with maximal ideal mH and residue k. [F1, step 3.1, construct]

5.1 Every monic polynomial over H with a specified simple root in k descends to one finite tower stage, since its coefficients are finite. Adjoin that root by one more allowed tower step. Thus the specified residue root lifts in H. Uniqueness is the polynomial-difference identity in3.1. The published UNQUALIFIED `cor-henselian-local-simple-root-criterion` states precisely that this unique monic simple-root property is equivalent to the coprime monic factor-lifting convention for henselian local rings. Its literal Statement has no AC premise. Consequently H is henselian. No multivariable finite-factor theorem is smuggled into this step. [F1, F2, step 4.1, construct]

6.1 A local map R→D into a henselian local ring, with its given residue extension k→κ(D), extends uniquely along every finite tower. At each stage the unique specified simple root in D exists by that same unqualified criterion, and all elements inverted in the local simple-root ring map to D-units because their residues are nonzero. Induction over this FINITE tower gives the map. Uniqueness in3.1 makes all tower maps compatible without a choice of one map per object; they are uniquely defined by the original map. The colimit gives the unique local H→D extending R→D. Thus H is the ordinary henselization, with the actual universal property. [F1, F2, step 3.1, step 5.1, construct]

7.1 For every n≥1 there is a unique local R-map B→R/m^n extending the specified residue point. For each step of its finite tower, lift the specified simple monic root by Newton correction across the nilpotent filtration of m/m^n; the derivative remains a unit. Finitely many corrections and finitely many tower steps suffice. Uniqueness follows from the polynomial-difference identity in3.1. The natural R/m^n→B/m^nB is an isomorphism: the constructed map is a left inverse, and the other composite equals the identity by uniqueness of the finite tower of simple nilpotent lifts. This argument is finite for each n, and uniqueness makes all n-maps compatible. Hence Bhat≅Rhat canonically. [F1, step 3.1, construct, algebra, step 6.1]

8.1 Each B is a countably coded Noetherian local ring. Apply the ZF completion package to B and its finitely generated maximal ideal mB. Its completion Rhat is B-flat. The finite equational criterion then makes Rhat H-flat: a finite relation with H-coefficients descends to a common B, and its finite flatness witnesses over B are also witnesses over H. Rhat is local with residue k, so the cyclic-submodule argument in the completion package makes it faithfully flat over H. [F1, F3, step 4.1, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 The completion package also makes Rhat Noetherian. Noetherianity descends to H by a finite argument. For an ideal J⊂H, the ideal J Rhat has finitely many generators; express each as a finite sum of elements of J times Rhat-coefficients. The finitely many J-elements appearing generate an ideal J0⊂J with J0 Rhat=J Rhat. Flatness and faithfulness imply (J/J0)⊗_H Rhat=0 and hence J=J0. Thus every ideal of H is finitely generated. This does not choose generators simultaneously for all ideals. [F3, step 8.1, construct, algebra]

10.1 Passing the unique isomorphisms in7.1 to the colimit of pointed charts gives H/m^nH=R/m^n for every n. Their inverse limits identify Hhat with Rhat, with its ACTUAL maximal-adic map H→Hhat. Together with4.1,5.1,8.1 and9.1 this proves the Statement in ZF. [F3, step 5.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, step 9.1, algebra] ∎
