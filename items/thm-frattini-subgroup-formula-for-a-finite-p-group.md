## Statement

For every finite $p$-group $P$, the subgroup $P^p$ is characteristic and

$$\Phi(P)=P'P^p,$$

where $P'=[P,P]$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]) and $P^p$ is the subgroup generated by the $p$th powers.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite $p$-group $P$.

[F1] For a group $G$ and a prime $p$, the $p$th-power subgroup is $G^p=\langle g^p:g\in G\rangle$ ([[def-pth-power-subgroup-of-a-group]]).

[L1] For a finite $p$-group $P$, $P/\Phi(P)$ is elementary abelian, and $P/N$ is elementary abelian exactly when $\Phi(P)\le N$ ([[thm-frattini-quotient-is-the-largest-elementary-abelian-quotient]]).

[L2] The commutator subgroup $P'$ is normal in $P$ ([[lem-commutator-subgroup-is-normal]]).

[L3] If $H\le G$ and $N\trianglelefteq G$, then $HN$ is a subgroup ([[lem-product-with-normal-subgroup]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every automorphism sends $g^p$ to $\alpha(g)^p$, so it preserves the generating set in [F1] and hence $P^p$ is characteristic; conjugation by an element of $P$ is an automorphism, so $P^p$ is normal. By [L2], $P'$ is normal as well, so [L3] makes $K:=P'P^p$ a subgroup, and $gKg^{-1}=(gP'g^{-1})(gP^pg^{-1})=P'P^p=K$ for every $g\in P$ makes it normal. [given, F1, L2, L3, algebra]

2.1 The elementary abelian quotient $P/\Phi(P)$ is abelian and has exponent $p$ by [L1], so it kills every commutator and every $p$th power. Thus $K=P'P^p\le\Phi(P)$. [step 1.1, F1, L1, algebra]

3.1 The quotient $P/K$ is abelian because it kills $P'$, and every element has $p$th power one because it kills $P^p$. It is therefore elementary abelian, so the kernel criterion in [L1] gives $\Phi(P)\le K$. Together with step 2.1 this proves equality. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L1, algebra] ∎
