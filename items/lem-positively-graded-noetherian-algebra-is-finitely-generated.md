## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let
$A=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}A_n$ be a graded commutative ring with $A_0$ a field, and
suppose $A$ is Noetherian ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]],
[[def-noetherian-ring]]). Then $A$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-algebra
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a nonnegatively graded commutative ring $A=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}A_n$ with $A_nA_m\subseteq A_{n+m}$, whose degree-zero part $A_0$ is a field, and which is Noetherian.

[F1] *Graded rings.* A nonnegatively graded ring is a commutative ring $S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n$ with $S_nS_m\subseteq S_{n+m}$ for all $m,n\ge0$; an element of $S_n$ is homogeneous of degree $n$ ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]).

[F2] *Noetherian ideals are finitely generated.* Every ideal of a Noetherian commutative ring is finitely generated ([[thm-noetherian-ring-ideal-characterisations]]).

[F3] *Finite generation over the base.* A commutative $R$-algebra $A$ is of finite type over $R$, equivalently a finitely generated $R$-algebra, when $A=R[a_1,\dots,a_n]$ for some $n\in\mathbb N$ and some $a_1,\dots,a_n\in A$; for $n=0$ this is the image of $R$ ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $A_+=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}A_n$. Then $A=A_0\oplus A_+$ as abelian groups, and $A_+$ is an ideal of $A$: for $a\in A_m$ with $m\ge1$ and $b\in A_n$ one has $ab\in A_{m+n}$ with $m+n\ge1$, and $A_+$ is closed under sums by the direct-sum decomposition. Since $A$ is Noetherian, the ideal $A_+$ is finitely generated. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 Fix a finite generating list $h_1,\dots,h_r$ of $A_+$. Each $h_j$ is the finite sum of its homogeneous components, and because $h_j$ lies in $A_+=\bigoplus_{n\ge1}A_n$ and this is a direct sum decomposition, the component of $h_j$ in $A_d$ vanishes for $d=0$ and lies in $A_d\subseteq A_+$ for $d\ge1$; each $h_j$ is therefore the sum of its homogeneous components of positive degree. Discarding zero components and relabelling, we obtain finitely many homogeneous elements $g_1,\dots,g_m\in A_+$ of positive degrees $e_1,\dots,e_m\ge1$ that still generate $A_+$: every $h_j$ is an $A$-linear combination of the $g_i$ while each $g_i$ lies in $A_+=(h_1,\dots,h_r)$, so $(h_1,\dots,h_r)\subseteq(g_1,\dots,g_m)\subseteq A_+$ and the two lists generate the same ideal. [F1, step 1.1]

3.1 Every homogeneous element $f\in A_d$ lies in the $A_0$-subalgebra $A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$. This is proved by induction on $d$. For $d=0$ one has $f\in A_0\subseteq A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$. For $d\ge1$, step 2.1 gives $f\in A_+=(g_1,\dots,g_m)$, so $f=\sum_i r_ig_i$ with $r_i\in A$; taking the homogeneous component of degree $d$ of this identity, and using that $g_i$ is homogeneous of degree $e_i$, we may assume each $r_i$ lies in $A_{d-e_i}$, which is the zero group when $d-e_i<0$ because the grading is nonnegative. In the nonzero cases $d-e_i<d$, so the induction hypothesis gives $r_i\in A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$ and hence $f=\sum_ir_ig_i\in A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $f\in A$ be arbitrary. Since $A$ is the direct sum of the graded pieces $A_d$, the element $f$ is a finite sum $f=\sum_d f_d$ of homogeneous elements $f_d\in A_d$; by step 3.1 each $f_d$ lies in $A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$, hence so does $f$. Therefore $A=A_0[g_1,\dots,g_m]$ is generated as an $A_0$-algebra by the finitely many elements $g_1,\dots,g_m$, that is, $A$ is a finitely generated $A_0$-algebra. [F1, F3, step 3.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The argument is the graded form of Nakayama: $A_+$ is a homogeneous ideal, so it can be generated by homogeneous elements, and the top-degree part of a relation lowers the degree. This is the argument used by Brion in the proof of his Theorem 1.24(i) and by Popov–Vinberg in Theorem 3.6.
- No choice is used beyond the finite generation supplied by the Noetherian hypothesis; with an empty generating list the conclusion reads $A=A_0=A_0[\varnothing]$.
