## Statement

**(i) Spherical radius estimate.** Let $Z$ be a CAT(1) space ([[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]) and let $\gamma$ be a closed rectifiable curve of length $2r$ with $0<2r<2\pi$. Split $\gamma$ at points $y,z$ into two subarcs of length $r$, and let $a$ be the midpoint of the geodesic $[y,z]$ (which exists because $d(y,z)\le r<\pi$). Then for every point $x$ of $\gamma$,
$$\cos d(a,x)\ \ge\ \frac{\cos d(x,y)+\cos d(x,z)}{2\cos(d(y,z)/2)}\ \ge\ \cos(r/2),$$
and the image of $\gamma$ is contained in the closed ball $\bar B(a,r/2)$; with $\eta:=\tfrac12(\pi-r)>0$ one has $r/2=\pi/2-\eta$.

**(ii) Quadrilateral separation.** There is a function $\delta$ on $\{(\eta,\mu):0<\mu<2\eta<\pi\}$ with values $\delta(\eta,\mu)>0$, continuous on every compact subset of its domain, such that: if $a,x,y,z$ are points of a CAT(1) space with $d(a,x)\le\pi/2-\eta$, $d(x,y)=d(x,z)=\mu$ and $d(a,y),d(a,z)\le d(a,x)$, then
$$d(y,z)\ \le\ 2\mu-\delta(\eta,\mu).$$
The estimate is intrinsic: the two spherical comparison triangles of $(a,x,y)$ and $(a,x,z)$ are glued along their common side $[a,x]$, and a shortest path in the resulting quadrilateral $Q$ either stays in one triangle or crosses the common side; the ambient spherical chord need not lie in $Q$ and is not used. The bound is uniform on the compact family of configurations with $0<\mu<2\eta$, including degenerate comparison triangles by continuity; equality of either radial distance with $d(a,x)$ is also allowed; the equality corner is excluded by $\mu<2\eta$, and the degenerate case $d(a,x)=0$ is impossible for a nonconstant configuration.

**(iii) The midpoint-operation disk (assuming the Axiom of Choice).** Let $x\in C^0_h(n)$ and choose $m\ge1$ with $L(f^m x)<2l$ ([[def-cg-cyclic-small-mesh-polygon-and-midpoint-energy]]). Assume that every comparison triangle used below is nondegenerate, i.e. its three side lengths satisfy the strict triangle inequalities. Build the finite cellulation $G(m,n)$ from the nested midpoint polygons of a regular Euclidean $n$-gon: its ears have principal vertices $v(i,j),v(i+1,j-1),v(i+1,j)$ for $0\le i<m$, and its final fan has triangles $v(m,1),v(m,j-1),v(m,j)$ for $3\le j\le n$. Row-$i$ edges are subdivided at their row-$(i+1)$ midpoints. Assign $v(i,j)$ the point $(f^ix)_j$ of $X$ and give each face the spherical comparison metric of its assigned triple ([[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]]). The ambient space need only have the uniform CAT(1) radius $l$; its compactness is not used in this finite construction. Then:

(a) every face has perimeter $<2l<\pi$ and is nondegenerate;

(b) the vertexwise map extends along each edge to a map $g\colon K_1(G)\to X$ of the $1$-skeleton, and $\rho(u,v)\ge d(gu,gv)$ for all skeleton points $u,v$, where $\rho$ is the disk's intrinsic polyhedral metric;

(c) every interior vertex of the disk has cone angle at least $2\pi$;

(d) the boundary vertices are exactly $V(0)\cup V(1)$; those in $V(1)$ have boundary angle at least $\pi$, and those in $V(0)$ have boundary angle $<\pi$;

(e) the disk contains no simple closed local geodesic: successive ear removal forces such a curve into the final fan, where the spherical radial maximum argument excludes it;

(f) consequently the disk, with the polyhedral metric, is a CAT(1) space ([[thm-cg-compact-local-cat-one-short-circle-criterion]], [[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]]).

**(iv) Quantitative output (assuming the Axiom of Choice).** In the situation of (iii), assume also $0<L(x)<2\pi$ and put $r=L(x)/2$, $\xi_i=d(x_i,x_{i+1})$, $\zeta_i=d(fx_i,fx_{i+1})$, $\eta=(\pi-r)/2$ and $\mu=\min\{r/(2n),\eta\}$. If $\xi_i\ge r/n$ for all $i$ (the case produced by the variance estimate used later on this page), then there is an index $k$ with
$$\zeta_k\ \le\ \frac{\xi_k+\xi_{k+1}}{2}-\delta(\eta,\mu).$$
The index $k$ is adjacent to a first-row boundary vertex $b$ of maximally possible distance from the radius center $a$ supplied by (i); the two outgoing subarcs at $b$ are cut at distance $\mu$, perimeters satisfy $2R+\mu<\pi$ with $R=\pi/2-\eta$, and the intrinsic quadrilateral estimate (ii) applied in the disk, together with $\rho\ge d\circ(g\times g)$ of (b), gives the displayed deficit for equal cut segments; longer unequal midpoint segments are handled by taking initial equal $\mu$-pieces and adding the remaining lengths.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A CAT(1) space $Z$ and a closed rectifiable curve $\gamma$ of length $2r$, $0<2r<2\pi$, split at $y,z$ into two subarcs of length $r$; configurations $a,x,y,z$ as in (ii); a compact locally CAT(1) space $X$, a uniform radius $l<\pi/2$, a fixed $n\ge3$ and a tuple $x\in C^0_h(n)$ as in (iii) with all $\xi_i\ge r/n$.

[F1] [[def-cg-cat-zero-cat-one-and-local-geodesic]]: CAT(1) and locally CAT(1) spaces; $d_S(x,y)=\arccos(x\cdot y)$ on $S^2$; local geodesics; the CAT(1) inequality for all pairs of points of a triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$.

[F2] [[lem-cg-comparison-convexity-and-model-spaces]]: comparison triangles of perimeter $<2\pi$ exist and are unique up to isometry and the comparison map on them is distance-nonincreasing; the spherical cosine rule and the midpoint identity $\cos d_S(x,a)=(\cos d_S(x,y)+\cos d_S(x,z))/(2\cos(d_S(y,z)/2))$; balls of radius $<\pi/2$ in a CAT(1) space are convex with unique geodesics.

[F3] [[lem-cg-cat-one-short-and-closed-local-geodesics]]: unique short geodesics with continuous dependence on endpoints; local geodesics of length $\le\pi$ are geodesics.

[F4] [[def-cg-cyclic-small-mesh-polygon-and-midpoint-energy]] and [[lem-cg-polygon-midpoint-drop-and-equality]]: mesh, $L$, $E$, the midpoint operation $f$ and its continuity, the zero-limit basin $C^0_h(n)$, the invariance $\operatorname{mesh}(fx)\le\operatorname{mesh}(x)$, and the pointwise bound $d(fx_i,fx_{i+1})\le(\xi_i+\xi_{i+1})/2$.

[F5] [[def-metric-space]], [[def-metric-ball]]: metric axioms, the triangle inequality, and balls.

[F6] [[def-metric-continuity]], [[def-metric-compactness]], [[thm-continuous-image-of-a-compact-space-is-compact]], [[thm-extreme-value-metric]], [[thm-heine-borel-rn]]: continuity, compactness of closed bounded subsets of $\mathbb R^k$, images of compact sets, and attained extrema.

[F7] [[lem-metrics-on-rn]], [[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]]: Euclidean and spherical geometry as used in the model arguments.

[F8] [[def-principal-inverse-sine-and-cosine]], [[thm-sine-and-cosine-addition-formulas]], [[thm-sine-cosine-signs-monotonicity-and-ranges]], [[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]]: monotonicity of $\cos$ on $[0,\pi]$, the addition formulas, and $\sin>0$ on $(0,\pi)$.

[F9] Finite comparison-cell construction: take the spherical comparison triangles supplied by [F2] and identify their prescribed corresponding edges by length-preserving maps. This is the definition of the quotient cellulation used in (iii); an arbitrary edge identification is not an application of the triangle-gluing or patchwork lemma, and no global angle comparison follows from the identification alone.

[F10] [[thm-cg-compact-local-cat-one-short-circle-criterion]]: a compact, geodesic, locally CAT(1) space is CAT(1) if and only if it contains no isometrically embedded circle of length $<2\pi$.

[F11] [[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[cor-arzela-ascoli-subsequence-theorem-for-proper-metric-targets]]: the compact short-circle criterion of [F10] consumes AC through its Arzelà–Ascoli path; the radius estimate, the separation constant, the disk construction are choice-free; clauses (iii)(f) and (iv) consume that criterion and hence assume AC.

[F12] [[def-upper-bound]] defines upper bounds and suprema. By [[thm-extreme-value-metric]], a continuous real-valued function on a nonempty compact metric space is bounded and attains its supremum and infimum; nonemptiness and continuity must be checked for each application.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **Radius estimate (i).** Let $x$ be a point of $\gamma$; it lies on one of the two subarcs between $y$ and $z$, so writing $b:=d(x,y)$, $d:=d(x,z)$, $c:=d(y,z)$ we have $b+d\le r$ (the two pieces of that subarc dominate the two distances), $|b-d|\le c$ (triangle inequality) and $c\le r<\pi$. The triangle $(x,y,z)$ of $Z$ has perimeter $b+d+c\le2r<2\pi$, so a comparison triangle exists in the round sphere $S^2$ of [F7] and the CAT(1) inequality of [F1] applied to the pair $(a,x)$ gives $d(a,x)\le d_S(\bar a,\bar x)$, where $\bar a$ is the comparison point of the midpoint $a$ of $[y,z]$. The midpoint identity of [F2] gives $\cos d_S(\bar a,\bar x)=(\cos b+\cos d)/(2\cos(c/2))$, and the addition formula ([F8]) turns this into $\cos((b+d)/2)\cos((b-d)/2)/\cos(c/2)$; since $|b-d|\le c$ and $\cos$ decreases on $[0,\pi]$ with $\cos(c/2)>0$, this is at least $\cos((b+d)/2)$, which is at least $\cos(r/2)$ because $b+d\le r$ and $r/2<\pi$. Hence $\cos d(a,x)\ge\cos(r/2)$ with both arguments in $[0,\pi]$, so $d(a,x)\le r/2$: the image of $\gamma$ is contained in the closed ball of radius $r/2$ about $a$, and $r/2=\pi/2-\eta$ for $\eta=(\pi-r)/2$. [F1, F2, F5, F7, F8, algebra]

1.2 **Quadrilateral separation (ii).** Let $a,x,y,z$ satisfy the hypotheses, and consider the two comparison triangles $\bar T_y=(\bar a,\bar x,\bar y)$ and $\bar T_z=(\bar a,\bar x,\bar z)$ of the triangles $(a,x,y)$ and $(a,x,z)$ in $S^2$; both are admissible because their perimeters are at most $2d(a,x)+\mu\le 2(\pi/2-\eta)+\mu<2\pi$. Glue them along the common side $[\bar a,\bar x]$ on opposite sides, obtaining an abstract spherical quadrilateral $Q$ (two triangles joined along the isometric side $[\bar a,\bar x]$) with intrinsic distance $d_Q$; the corresponding boundary maps agree on the common side because the geodesic $[a,x]$ is unique ($d(a,x)<\pi$, [F3]); comparison on each triangle boundary, followed by the triangle inequality at the crossings of the common side, gives $d(y,z)\le d_Q(\bar y,\bar z)$. Write $\vartheta$ for the angle at $\bar x$ between $[\bar x,\bar a]$ and $[\bar x,\bar y]$ inside $\bar T_y$ and $\vartheta'$ analogously in $\bar T_z$; by the cosine rule and $d(a,y)\le d(a,x)$, $\cos\vartheta=(\cos d(a,y)-\cos d(a,x)\cos\mu)/(\sin d(a,x)\sin\mu)\ge(\cos d(a,x)-\cos d(a,x)\cos\mu)/(\sin d(a,x)\sin\mu)=\cot d(a,x)\tan(\mu/2)$, and since $d(a,x)\le\pi/2-\eta$ gives $\cot d(a,x)\ge\tan\eta$, we get $\cos\vartheta\ge\tan\eta\tan(\mu/2)$ and likewise $\cos\vartheta'\ge\tan\eta\tan(\mu/2)$; as the right-hand side is positive and $\mu>0$, both $\vartheta,\vartheta'$ are strictly less than $\pi/2$, so with $\psi:=\arcsin(\tan\eta\tan(\mu/2))>0$ the angle $\Phi:=\vartheta+\vartheta'$ satisfies $\Phi\le\pi-2\psi<\pi$. Put $q:=\min\{1/2,\tan\eta\tan(\mu/2)\}$ and $\psi:=\arcsin q$, so the preceding angle bounds still give $\Phi\le\pi-2\psi$. The triangle inequality gives $\mu=d(x,y)\le d(a,x)+d(a,y)\le2d(a,x)$, hence the common side has length at least $\mu/2$. Cut both outgoing edges at distance $e:=\mu/4$ from $\bar x$. The short spherical chord joining the cutpoints lies in the wedge of angle $\Phi<\pi$ and in the convex closed ball $\bar B(\bar x,e)$, since $e<\pi/4$. The other two edges are outside this small ball: for $p\in[\bar a,\bar y]$, writing $t=d(a,x)$, the triangle inequality gives $d_S(\bar x,p)\ge\max\{\mu-d_S(p,\bar y),t-d_S(p,\bar a)\}\ge(\mu+t-d(a,y))/2\ge\mu/2$, and likewise for $[\bar a,\bar z]$. It meets the common radial side before its far endpoint (at distance at most $e<\mu/2$); each half lies in the corresponding convex spherical triangle. Thus this chord really is a path in $Q$, irrespective of whether the chord joining $\bar y,\bar z$ lies in $Q$. Its length is at most $\ell_e:=\arccos(\cos^2e+\sin^2e\cos(\pi-2\psi))<2e$. Adding the two remaining edge pieces gives $d_Q(\bar y,\bar z)\le2(\mu-e)+\ell_e=2\mu-\delta(\eta,\mu)$, with $\delta(\eta,\mu):=2e-\ell_e>0$. This formula is continuous on the whole stated parameter domain, including parameter values for which no configuration exists; the clamp in $q$ keeps the inverse sine defined there. Degenerate comparison triangles, with a zero angle at $\bar x$, give the same chord shortcut directly; equality of radial distances was already included in the cosine bound; $d(a,x)=0$ would give $\mu=d(x,y)\le d(x,a)+d(a,y)\le0$, contradicting $\mu>0$. [F1, F2, F5, F8, F9, F12, algebra]

1.3 **The finite disk and its face bounds (iii)(a).** In the Euclidean plane take a regular $n$-gon $P_0$, and let $P_{i+1}$ be its midpoint polygon; it is a regular $n$-gon, rotated through $\pi/n$ and scaled by $\cos(\pi/n)>0$. The difference between $P_i$ and $P_{i+1}$ consists of the $n$ ears $T(i,j)$ with principal vertices $v(i,j),v(i+1,j-1),v(i+1,j)$; their radial sides meet at the row-$i$ vertices, while each row-$i$ side is subdivided at $v(i+1,j)$. The ears for $0\le i<m$ and the $n-2$ triangles $T(m,j)=(v(m,1),v(m,j-1),v(m,j))$, $3\le j\le n$, of a diagonal fan triangulate the closed disk $P_0$. Thus the prescribed edge identifications yield a topological disk, with boundary vertices $V(0)\cup V(1)$ and interior vertices $V(2)\cup\cdots\cup V(m)$; the row-$i$ polygon is the boundary of the remaining disk after the first $i$ ear layers are removed. Assigning spherical metrics leaves this topology unchanged, since the strict triangle inequalities make each face a nondegenerate closed triangle. The ear sides are $\xi^{(i)}_{j-1}/2$, $\xi^{(i)}_j/2$ and $d(v(i+1,j-1),v(i+1,j))$ in $X$, so by [F4] their perimeter is at most $\xi^{(i)}_{j-1}+\xi^{(i)}_j\le2h<2l<\pi$. For a cap triangle, the three consecutive subarcs of the last-row loop between its three assigned vertices have total length $L(f^mx)<2l$ and dominate its three distances; hence its perimeter is also $<2l<\pi$. Every face is therefore contained in an open spherical hemisphere and has angles $<\pi$. [F2, F4, F5, F7, F8, F9, algebra, construct]

1.4 **The skeleton map dominates the ambient distance (iii)(b).** Map each edge at constant speed to the corresponding unique short geodesic of $X$. This is consistent with subdivision: the row-$(i+1)$ vertex on a row-$i$ side is its assigned midpoint by [F4]. Each ear has all assigned vertex distances $\le h<l$, and each cap face has all such distances $\le L(f^mx)/2<l$. Thus each assigned triple lies in a radius-$l$ CAT(1) ball; its three geodesic sides lie there by comparison convexity. The CAT(1) inequality gives $d(gu,gv)\le d_T(u,v)$ for any two points of the boundary of its spherical comparison face $T$. The intrinsic path metric of the finite complex is the infimum of lengths of chains of face segments; for skeleton endpoints, each segment of such a chain begins and ends on the boundary of its face (split at successive face crossings). Applying the boundary comparison to these segments and the triangle inequality in $X$ gives $d(gu,gv)\le$ the chain length; the infimum gives $d(gu,gv)\le\rho(u,v)$. No extension of $g$ to face interiors is needed. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F9, construct]

1.5 **Shortest paths through a vertex span at least $\pi$ on each side (cone lemma).** Let $W$ be a polyhedral surface with an interior vertex $v$ at which the total cone angle is $\Theta$, let $u_1,u_2$ be points on two edges issuing from $v$ at positive distance from $v$, and suppose the concatenation of the two segments $[u_1,v]$, $[v,u_2]$ is a shortest path from $u_1$ to $u_2$ in $W$. Then the two sectors of $W$ at $v$ cut out by the segments have angles at least $\pi$, so $\Theta\ge2\pi$. Indeed, if a sector had angle $\varphi<\pi$, then in that sector (a sufficiently small spherical wedge of angle $<\pi$, hence convex) the two points at equal small distance $\varepsilon$ from $v$ on the two edges would be joined by a path of length strictly less than $2\varepsilon$: the spherical cosine rule gives $\cos\ell=\cos^2\varepsilon+\sin^2\varepsilon\cos\varphi>\cos(2\varepsilon)$ for $\varphi<\pi$, so $\ell<2\varepsilon$, and adding the remaining pieces gives a strictly shorter path, contradicting minimality. At a boundary vertex the same shortcut applies to its one available disk-side sector and forces that sector to have angle at least $\pi$; no second sector or $2\pi$ bound is asserted there. [F2, F8, algebra]

2.1 **Ear removal.** Let $D_i$ be the closed disk remaining after removal of ear layers $0,\ldots,i-1$, with $D_0=D$. Suppose a simple closed local geodesic $\gamma$ of $D$ lies in $D_i$, $i<m$. It is also locally shortest among paths in $D_i$. At a tip $v(i,j)$ the only incident face of $D_i$ is the ear $T(i,j)$, whose angle is $<\pi$; the shortcut argument of step 1.5 excludes passage through that tip. At an interior point of a boundary side of $D_i$, the local space is a spherical half-disk and a local geodesic meeting that boundary must follow its great-circle side. Continuing toward the adjacent tip would reach $v(i,j)$ before any other vertex, which is impossible. Therefore $\gamma$ avoids all boundary side interiors of $D_i$. A component of its intersection with an ear interior must consequently enter and leave through that ear's base, possibly at its endpoints: the other two sides are boundary sides of $D_i$. Inside the ear the arc is a great-circle arc contained in an open hemisphere; its length is $<\pi$, so it cannot meet the same short great-circle base twice unless it coincides with that base. Nor can a whole closed great circle lie in the ear's hemisphere. Thus $\gamma$ misses the ear interiors and lies in $D_{i+1}$. Induction forces $\gamma$ into the final fan $D_m$. [step 1.3, step 1.5, F2, F7, F8]

2.2 **Interior cone angles (iii)(c).** Let $u=v(i,j)$ with $2\le i\le m$ and let $w_1:=v(i-1,j)$, $w_2:=v(i-1,j+1)$ be the endpoints of the row-$(i-1)$ edge whose midpoint is $u$; the two radial edges $[w_1,u]$ and $[u,w_2]$ are sides of the ears $T(i-1,j)$ and $T(i-1,j+1)$ and have $\rho$-length equal to the $X$-distances $\xi^{(i-1)}_j/2=d(gw_1,gu)=d(gu,gw_2)$. Since $gu$ is the midpoint of the geodesic $[gw_1,gw_2]$, $d(gw_1,gw_2)=d(gw_1,gu)+d(gu,gw_2)=\rho(w_1,u)+\rho(u,w_2)$, while $\rho(w_1,w_2)\ge d(gw_1,gw_2)$ by (iii)(b) and $\rho(w_1,w_2)\le\rho(w_1,u)+\rho(u,w_2)$ by the triangle inequality; hence equality holds and the concatenation $[w_1,u]\cup[u,w_2]$ is a shortest path. By the cone lemma of step 1.5 the cone angle of $D$ at $u$ is at least $2\pi$. (The cap supplies the inner sector when $i=m$.) [step 1.3, step 1.4, step 1.5, F1, F4, algebra]

3.1 **Boundary angles (iii)(d).** The boundary of $D$ is the subdivided row-$0$ polygon. At a first-row vertex $v(0,j)$ exactly the ear $T(0,j)$ is incident, so the boundary angle is the angle of its spherical comparison triangle at $v(0,j)$; the comparison triangle is nondegenerate with perimeter $<2h<2\pi$, and all angles of a nondegenerate spherical triangle of perimeter $<2\pi$ are strictly less than $\pi$ (an angle $\ge\pi$ would force the opposite side to be at least the sum of the other two by the cosine rule, contradicting strictness), so $\angle(\partial D,v(0,j))<\pi$. At the boundary vertex $v(1,j)$ of the last row of the first strip, the incident faces are $T(0,j)$, $T(0,j+1)$ and the faces on the remaining-disk side; the concatenation $[v(0,j),v(1,j)]\cup[v(1,j),v(0,j+1)]$ is a shortest path by the argument of step 2.2 with $w_1=v(0,j)$, $w_2=v(0,j+1)$, using only the disk-side sector of the shortcut argument, so by step 1.5 the sector of $D$ at $v(1,j)$ that lies on the disk side of the path -- which is exactly the disk's sector at its boundary vertex -- has angle at least $\pi$, i.e. $\angle(\partial D,v(1,j))\ge\pi$; equivalently the row-$0$ polygon is locally geodesic at $v(1,j)$. [step 1.3, step 1.5, step 2.2, F1, F2, F8, algebra]

3.2 **The final fan excludes a closed local geodesic (iii)(e).** Write $c=v(m,1)$. On each cap face define $R(p)$ as its spherical distance to $c$. These functions agree on fan diagonals, hence define a continuous function on $D_m$; each face lies in the convex spherical ball of radius $L(f^mx)/2<l<\pi/2$ about $c$, so $R<\pi/2$. Suppose the curve forced into $D_m$ by step 2.1 exists, and let $p$ maximize $R$ on it. Its maximum is positive, so $p\ne c$. If $p$ is in a face interior or a fan diagonal interior, unfold the adjacent faces into $S^2$: the common radial segment to $c$ agrees in the unfolding, and the local geodesic is a great-circle segment. At its radial maximum its two outgoing directions are perpendicular to the radial direction, so the cosine rule gives $\cos R(\gamma(t))=\cos R(p)\cos t<\cos R(p)$ for small nonzero $t$, contradicting maximality. The same argument applies at a boundary side interior if the curve follows that side. At a remaining boundary vertex $p\ne c$, the one or two incident cap faces form a sector, split by the radial diagonal to $c$ when there are two. If an outgoing direction has angle $\alpha$ to the radial direction, the cosine rule reads $\cos R(\gamma(t))=\cos R(p)\cos t+\sin R(p)\sin t\cos\alpha$. Maximality implies $\cos\alpha\ge0$, hence both outgoing directions make angles at most $\pi/2$ with the radial direction. The sector between them consequently has angle at most $\pi$; local minimality requires angle at least $\pi$ by step 1.5. Equality forces both angles to be $\pi/2$, and the same displayed cosine rule again increases $R$ for small positive $t$, a contradiction. Thus no simple closed local geodesic exists in $D$. [step 1.3, step 1.5, step 2.1, F2, F6, F7, F8, algebra]

4.1 **Local CAT(1) via a spherical cone chart.** At a vertex let $J$ be its angular link with metric truncated at $\pi$: an interior link is a circle of circumference $\Theta\ge2\pi$, and a boundary link is an interval of its finite boundary angle. Both are CAT(1). For the circle use [F2](vi); truncation changes neither short segments nor triangles of perimeter $<2\pi$. For the interval every tested triangle lies in a subinterval of length $<\pi$ and is degenerate. Let $p$ be a one-point space. The Euclidean cone criterion [[thm-cg-cone-cat-equivalence-and-polyhedral-link-criterion]] makes $C(J)$ CAT(0). The product $[0,\infty)\times C(J)$ is CAT(0), by adding the squared vertex-to-side inequalities of [F2](iv). The join–product isometry [[thm-cg-cone-join-metric-and-local-product-chart]] (3) identifies it with $C(\{p\}*J)$; the same cone criterion now makes $\{p\}*J$ CAT(1). Its polar metric about $p$ is $\cos d=\cos t\cos t'+\sin t\sin t'\cos d_J(u,u')$, by [[def-cg-euclidean-cone-and-spherical-join-metrics]]. On each angular subinterval of length $<\pi$ this is exactly the spherical sector metric, and these sectors glue in the same order as the incident faces of $D$. Thus their polar charts identify a sufficiently small neighborhood of the vertex with a neighborhood of $p$ in the join, preserving lengths. This also identifies the induced distances on smaller balls: choose an outer chart radius $\sigma<\pi/2$ within every incident face; a path leaving it between points of radii at most $\sigma/4$ has length at least $3\sigma/2$, whereas the path through the vertex has length at most $\sigma/2$. Shorter paths remain in the chart. The join's smaller closed balls are convex and CAT(1), so their corresponding balls in $D$ are CAT(1). Face and edge interiors have spherical disk or half-disk charts, which have convex small CAT(1) balls. Hence $D$ is locally CAT(1), including boundary angles greater than $\pi$. 5.1 **Global CAT(1) (iii)(f).** The intrinsic finite spherical complex is compact, being a quotient of finitely many compact model triangles, and geodesic: a minimizing sequence of constant-speed paths has uniformly bounded speed, and compact Arzelà–Ascoli gives a uniformly convergent subsequence whose limit has length at most the infimum, by the finite-partition definition of length. This is the AC use of [F11]. Step 4.1 supplies local CAT(1), and step 3.2 excludes every simple closed local geodesic, hence every isometrically embedded circle of length $<2\pi$. The compact short-circle criterion [F10] now proves that $D$ is CAT(1). [step 1.3, step 3.2, step 4.1, F6, F10, F11]

6.1 **Quantitative output (iv).** Assume the situation of (iii) and (iv), and use (iii)(f), so that $(D,\rho)$ is CAT(1); put $2r=L(x)$, $\eta=(\pi-r)/2$, $\mu=\min\{r/(2n),\eta\}$ and $R=r/2=\pi/2-\eta$. Apply the radius estimate (i) to the boundary curve $\partial D$, which has $\rho$-length $L(x)=2r<2\pi$; this gives a point $a\in D$ with $\partial D\subseteq\bar B(a,\pi/2-\eta)$. Choose a maximum $b$ of $\rho(a,\cdot)$ on $\partial D$. The boundary lies in a ball of radius $R<\pi/2$; on a short boundary segment a maximum cannot occur in its interior. Indeed, if its interior midpoint $b$ maximized the distance, choose equal small offsets $y,z$ on that segment. Its triangle with $a$ is admissible, and the midpoint cosine inequality gives $\cos\rho(a,b)\ge(\cos\rho(a,y)+\cos\rho(a,z))/(2\cos(\rho(y,z)/2))>\cos\rho(a,b)$, since all three radial distances are at most $R<\pi/2$. Thus a maximum occurs at a boundary vertex. It cannot occur at a midpoint vertex of $V(1)$: there the two boundary edges concatenate to a local geodesic (step 3.1), and the same cosine comparison on a short segment through that vertex excludes a maximum there. Thus $b=v(0,k+1)$ for some $k$, and $\rho(a,b)\le\pi/2-\eta$ and $\rho(a,y)\le\rho(a,b)$ for every point $y$ of $\partial D$. Let $y_1$ and $y_2$ be the points of the boundary edges $[b,v(1,k)]$ and $[b,v(1,k+1)]$ at distance exactly $\mu$ from $b$; these exist because those edges have $\rho$-length $\xi_k/2$ and $\xi_{k+1}/2$, and $\mu\le r/(2n)\le\xi/2$ by hypothesis. Then $\rho(b,y_1)=\rho(b,y_2)=\mu$, $\rho(a,y_i)\le\rho(a,b)$ since $y_i\in\partial D$, and $\mu<2\eta$; the separation estimate (ii) in the CAT(1) space $(D,\rho)$ gives $\rho(y_1,y_2)\le2\mu-\delta(\eta,\mu)$. Also $\rho(v(1,k),y_1)=\xi_k/2-\mu$ and $\rho(y_2,v(1,k+1))=\xi_{k+1}/2-\mu$, because on the edge $[b,v(1,k)]$ the two points $y_1$ and $v(1,k)$ lie on the same side of $b$ at the stated distances.
