## Statement

For every field $K$ and every finite $d\ge0$, the ring $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is
Noetherian: each ideal of it has a finite generating list. The proof uses only
finite selections and is choice-free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a field $K$ and an integer $d\ge0$.

[L1] A ring $R$ is left Noetherian when its left regular module ${}_RR$ is Noetherian; unqualified "Noetherian ring" means left Noetherian, and a commutative ring carries no side ambiguity ([[def-noetherian-ring]]).

[L2] A module is Noetherian when every one of its submodules is finitely generated, and a submodule is finitely generated when it is generated by a finite set ([[def-noetherian-module]], [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[L3] The regular left module ${}_RR$ has scalar action $r\cdot x=rx$. Ring multiplication satisfies the module axioms; by the submodule and left-ideal definitions, a subset $I\subseteq R$ is a submodule of ${}_RR$ exactly when it is an additive subgroup closed under $ri$, that is, a left ideal. In a commutative ring left, right and two-sided ideals coincide ([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-left-and-right-modules]], [[def-submodule]], [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]).

[L4] For $S\subseteq R$ the ideal $(S)$ is the smallest ideal containing $S$, and in a commutative ring it consists of the finite sums $\sum_i r_is_i$ with $r_i\in R$, $s_i\in S$; for $a\in R$ the principal ideal is $(a)=Ra$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L5] In $R[x]$ addition is coefficientwise and the coefficient of $x^n$ in a product is $\sum_{i+j=n}a_ib_j$, while $x$ is the coefficient sequence with the single value $1_R$ at index $1$ ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L6] For nonzero $f,g$ over a commutative ring, $\deg(f+g)\le\max\{\deg f,\deg g\}$ when $f+g\ne0$, and the coefficient of $x^{\deg f+\deg g}$ in $fg$ is $\operatorname{lc}(f)\operatorname{lc}(g)$; the degree and leading coefficient are as in [[def-polynomial-degree-leading-coefficient-and-monic]] ([[prop-polynomial-degree-laws-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L7] $R[x_1,\ldots,x_0]:=R$ and $R[x_1,\ldots,x_{n+1}]:=R[x_1,\ldots,x_n][x_{n+1}]$ ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]).

[L8] A field is a commutative ring in which every nonzero element is a unit ([[def-field]], [[lem-field-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Every field $F$ is Noetherian. Let $I\subseteq F$ be an ideal of the commutative ring $F$; if $I$ contains some $a\ne0$, then $1_F=a^{-1}a\in I$ by [L8] and [L4], so $I=F=(1_F)$; otherwise $I=\{0\}=(0_F)$, which is generated by the empty list. In both cases $I$ is finitely generated, so every submodule of the regular module ${}_FF$ is finitely generated by [L3], and [L1] and [L2] make $F$ Noetherian. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L8]

1.2 Let $C$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let $I\subseteq C[x]$ be an ideal. Then $I$ is finitely generated. In this step, a polynomial is said to have support bounded by $n$ when all coefficients above index $n$ vanish; this includes the zero polynomial without assigning it a degree. For $n\in\mathbb N$ put $J_n:=\{a\in C:\text{the coefficient of }x^n\text{ in some }f\in I\text{ with }\text{support bounded by }n\text{ equals }a\}$, a nonempty set containing $0$. Each $J_n$ is an ideal of $C$: it is closed under addition because coefficients of $x^n$ add, and under multiplication by $r\in C$ because $rf\in I$ again has support bounded by $n$ with coefficient of $x^n$ equal to $r a$. By [L5] the coefficient of $x^{n+1}$ in $xf$ is the coefficient of $x^n$ in $f$, and $xf$ has support bounded by $n+1$ by [L5]; hence $J_n\subseteq J_{n+1}$. The union $J:=\bigcup_n J_n$ is an ideal of $C$: for $a\in J_m$ and $b\in J_n$, both lie in $J_{\max(m,n)}$, which is an ideal, so $a+b\in J_{\max(m,n)}\subseteq J$; also $ra\in J_m\subseteq J$ for $r\in C$. Since $C$ is Noetherian, every ideal of $C$ is finitely generated by [L1], [L2] and [L3]; fix a finite generating list $J=(b_1,\ldots,b_r)$ as in [L4]. If $r=0$, then $J=0$, hence $I=0$ because the leading coefficient of any nonzero $f\in I$ would belong to $J_{\deg f}\subseteq J$; in this case the empty list generates $I$. Otherwise each $b_i$ lies in some $J_{N_i}$; put $N:=\max_iN_i$, which exists because the list is finite and nonempty. Then $J_N$ is an ideal containing every $b_i$, so $J\subseteq J_N$ by [L4], and $J_N\subseteq J$ by definition of $J$; hence $J_n=J_N$ for every $n\ge N$. For each of the finitely many $n\le N$ the ideal $J_n$ has a finite generating list $J_n=(c_{n,1},\ldots,c_{n,s_n})$ by [L1], [L2] and [L3], and for each pair $(n,k)$ we choose $f_{n,k}\in I$ with support bounded by $n$ whose coefficient of $x^n$ is $c_{n,k}$, which exists by the definition of $J_n$ (take $f_{n,k}=0$ when $c_{n,k}=0$). We claim that the finite set $W:=\{f_{n,k}:0\le n\le N,\ 1\le k\le s_n\}$ generates $I$; by [L4] this means $(W)=I$, and $(W)\subseteq I$ holds because $W\subseteq I$. The zero polynomial is already in $(W)$. Let $0\ne f\in I$ have degree $d$, and put $n:=\min\{d,N\}$; the leading coefficient $\operatorname{lc}(f)$ is the coefficient of $x^d$ in $f$, so $\operatorname{lc}(f)\in J_d$ if $d\le N$ and $\operatorname{lc}(f)\in J_d=J_N$ if $d>N$, that is $\operatorname{lc}(f)\in J_n$ in both cases. By [L4] there are $\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_{s_n}\in C$ with $\operatorname{lc}(f)=\sum_k\mu_kc_{n,k}$, and each $x^{d-n}f_{n,k}$ lies in $I$ and has support bounded by $d$ with coefficient of $x^d$ equal to $c_{n,k}$; therefore $g:=f-\sum_k\mu_kx^{d-n}f_{n,k}$ lies in $I$ and is either zero or has degree strictly smaller than $d$ by [L5] and [L6]. Induction on $d$, in the form of repeated descent of the degree, expresses every element of $I$ as a combination of elements of $W$ with coefficients in $C[x]$; hence $I=(W)$ is finitely generated. [L1, L2, L3, L4, L5, L6, induction, discharge-induction: degree descent to the case of the zero polynomial]

2.1 We prove by induction on $d$ that $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is Noetherian. For $d=0$ the ring is $K$ by [L7], Noetherian by step 1.1. For the step, $K[x_1,\ldots,x_{d+1}]=K[x_1,\ldots,x_d][x_{d+1}]$ by [L7]; if $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is Noetherian, then every ideal of $K[x_1,\ldots,x_{d+1}]$ is finitely generated by step 1.2, so $K[x_1,\ldots,x_{d+1}]$ is Noetherian by [L1], [L2] and [L3]. [L1, L2, L3, L7, step 1.1, step 1.2, base, discharge-induction: step 1.1]

3.1 Thus for every field $K$ and every $d\ge0$ the ring $K[x_1,\ldots,x_d]$ is Noetherian, that is, each of its ideals has a finite generating list: by step 2.1 its regular module is Noetherian, and its ideals are exactly the submodules of that module by [L3]. Every selection made above was from a finite list — the generating lists of the finitely many ideals $J_n$ with $n\le N$, the finitely many witnesses $f_{n,k}$, and the finitely many indices $N_i$ — and the degree descent is an induction on $\mathbb N$, so no choice principle is used. [L1, L2, L3, step 2.1] ∎
