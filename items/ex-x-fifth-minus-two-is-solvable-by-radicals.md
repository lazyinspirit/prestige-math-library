## Example

The quintic polynomial $x^5-2$ over $\mathbb Q$ is solvable by radicals. Its
splitting field is contained in

$$\mathbb Q(\zeta_5,\sqrt[5]{2}),$$

so it lies in a radical extension of $\mathbb Q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial $f(x)=x^5-2$.

[F1] A polynomial is solvable by radicals when its splitting field lies in a radical extension ([[def-solvable-by-radicals]]).

[L1] A splitting field is generated by all roots of the polynomial ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The five roots of $x^5-2$ are $$\sqrt[5]{2},\ \zeta_5\sqrt[5]{2},\ \zeta_5^2\sqrt[5]{2},\ \zeta_5^3\sqrt[5]{2},\ \zeta_5^4\sqrt[5]{2}.$$ Therefore [L1] makes the splitting field a subfield of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5,\sqrt[5]{2})$. [L1]

2.1 The field $\mathbb Q(\zeta_5,\sqrt[5]{2})$ is radical over $\mathbb Q$: first adjoin $\zeta_5$, a root of $x^5-1$, and then adjoin $\sqrt[5]{2}$, a root of $x^5-2$. Hence [F1] says that $x^5-2$ is solvable by radicals. [F1, step 1.1] ∎
