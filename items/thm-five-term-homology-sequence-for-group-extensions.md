## Statement

Assume AC. For every group extension
$$1\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow G\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1,$$
the maps induced by inclusion, quotient, and transgression form an exact sequence
$$H_2(G;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_2(Q;\mathbb Z) \longrightarrow N/[G,N]\longrightarrow H_1(G;\mathbb Z) \longrightarrow H_1(Q;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow 0.$$
Here $[G,N]$ is the normal subgroup generated by the commutators
$gng^{-1}n^{-1}$ with $g\in G$ and $n\in N$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the group extension.

[A1] Full AC, written AC, is [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

[L1] AC implies DC by choosing one successor at each stage of a serial relation. [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The extension has a strongly convergent homological spectral sequence with $E^2_{p,q}=H_p(Q;H_q(N;\mathbb Z))$ and abutment $H_{p+q}(G;\mathbb Z)$. [[thm-homological-lhs-spectral-sequence-for-group-extensions]]

[F2] Every first-quadrant homological spectral sequence with finite abutment filtration has the exact five-term sequence $H_2\to E^2_{2,0}\to E^2_{0,1}\to H_1\to E^2_{1,0}\to0$. [[thm-five-term-exact-sequence-of-a-first-quadrant-homological-spectral-sequence]]

[F3] The bar model computes group homology; in degree one, $H_1(N;\mathbb Z)$ is generated by symbols $[n]$ with relations $[n_1n_2]=[n_1]+[n_2]$. [[lem-diagonal-bar-coinvariants-compute-group-homology]]

## Proof

**Given:** The extension and [A1].

1.1 By [A1] and [L1], the DC condition of [F3] holds. Apply [F2] to the spectral sequence in [F1]. Since $H_0(N;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ with trivial $Q$-action, its terms identify as $E^2_{2,0}=H_2(Q;\mathbb Z)$, $E^2_{0,1}=H_0(Q;H_1(N;\mathbb Z))$, $H_1=H_1(G;\mathbb Z)$, and $E^2_{1,0}=H_1(Q;\mathbb Z)$. This gives an exact sequence with $H_0(Q;H_1(N;\mathbb Z))$ in the middle. [A1, L1, F1, F2, F3]

1.2 By [F3], $H_1(N;\mathbb Z)=N/[N,N]$ under the map sending $n$ to its class. The $Q$-coinvariants impose the relations $[gng^{-1}]=[n]$ for every lift $g\in G$ and $n\in N$. Their subgroup in $N/[N,N]$ is exactly $[G,N]/[N,N]$, because $[N,N]\subseteq[G,N]$. Therefore $H_0(Q;H_1(N;\mathbb Z))\cong N/[G,N]$. [A1, F1, F3, algebra]

2.1 Substituting this natural identification in the exact sequence from step 1.1 gives the displayed five-term sequence. The identification commutes with the map induced by any homomorphism of group extensions, since it is defined by the meridian-class map $n\mapsto[n]$ and the conjugation action. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
