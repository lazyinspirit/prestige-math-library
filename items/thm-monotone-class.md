## Statement

For every algebra $\mathcal A$ of subsets of $X$,

$$m_X(\mathcal A)=\sigma_X(\mathcal A).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An algebra $\mathcal A$ on $X$ and $\mathcal M:=m_X(\mathcal A)$.

[L1] The family $\mathcal M$ is the smallest monotone class containing $\mathcal A$ ([[lem-generated-monotone-class-exists-and-is-minimal]]).

[L2] The family $\mathcal M$ is closed under complements ([[lem-generated-monotone-class-closed-under-complements]]) and binary intersections ([[lem-generated-monotone-class-closed-under-intersections]]).

[L3] An algebra closed under increasing countable unions is a sigma-algebra ([[lem-algebra-with-increasing-unions-is-sigma]]).

[L4] The family $\sigma_X(\mathcal A)$ is the smallest sigma-algebra containing $\mathcal A$ ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L2], $\mathcal M$ contains $X$ and is closed under complements and binary intersections, hence under finite unions; it is therefore an algebra. [L1, L2, algebra]

1.2 Every sigma-algebra is closed under increasing unions and, by De Morgan's law, decreasing intersections. Thus $\sigma_X(\mathcal A)$ is a monotone class containing $\mathcal A$, and minimality in [L1] gives $\mathcal M\subseteq\sigma_X(\mathcal A)$. [L1, L4, algebra]

2.1 Since $\mathcal M$ is a monotone class by [L1], it is closed under increasing countable unions. Step 1.1 and [L3] make it a sigma-algebra containing $\mathcal A$, so [L4] gives $\sigma_X(\mathcal A)\subseteq\mathcal M$. [step 1.1, L1, L3, L4]

3.1 The inclusions of steps 2.1 and 1.2 prove the equality. [step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎
