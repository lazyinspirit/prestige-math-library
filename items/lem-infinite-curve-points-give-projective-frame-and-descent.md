## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be any field, $N\ge1$, and let
$C\subseteq\mathbb P^N_k$ be an integral Noetherian closed curve not
contained in any $k$-hyperplane. If $S\subseteq C(k)$ is infinite, there
are distinct points $P_0,\ldots,P_{N+1}\in S$ such that every $N+1$ of
them are linearly independent in homogeneous coordinates. Such an ordered
tuple is called a projective frame.

For any two ordered projective frames in $\mathbb P^N(k)$ there is a
unique element of $\operatorname{PGL}_{N+1}(k)$ taking the first to the
second. If a projective transformation over an arbitrary extension $L/k$
takes one such frame to the other in the given order, it is the base
extension of this $k$-transformation. Neither smoothness nor perfectness is
required.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, the field, integer, integral Noetherian curve, embedding and
infinite set of rational points in the Statement. Homogeneous independence
means independence of any nonzero vector representatives; rescaling does not
affect it. A projective transformation is an invertible linear map modulo
nonzero scalar matrices.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] A Noetherian space and each of its closed subspaces have finitely many
irreducible components. [[lem-noetherian-space-has-finitely-many-irreducible-components]]

[F2] A curve has chain dimension one: no chain of three distinct nonempty
irreducible closed subsets occurs. [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]]
[[def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space]]

[F3] Projective space has standard affine charts with coordinates the ratios
of homogeneous coordinates. Consequently its rational points are the lines
in $k^{N+1}$, and a $k$-rational point is closed: after taking a chart with
one coordinate nonzero its ideal is generated by its coordinate ratios minus
their values. [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited through the finite irreducible-component assertion [F1]. The
selection of the frame is finite; the descent argument is explicit linear
algebra.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; avoid finitely many hyperplane sections, then
normalize the frame by a basis matrix and a diagonal matrix.

1.1 A proper closed subset $Z\subsetneq C$ contains only finitely many closed points. Indeed [F1] writes it as a finite union of irreducible closed subsets $Z_j$. If some $Z_j$ contained two distinct closed points, either of them would be a nonempty irreducible closed subset strictly smaller than $Z_j$, giving a chain $\{P\}\subsetneq Z_j\subsetneq C$ prohibited by [F2]. Thus each $Z_j$ contains at most one closed point. In particular, the rational points on the intersection of $C$ with any $k$-hyperplane form a finite set, since that intersection is proper by hypothesis and rational points are closed by [F3]. [A1, F1, F2, F3, given]

2.1 Choose $P_0\in S$. If $P_0,\ldots,P_{r-1}$ have independent representatives with $r<N+1$, their span is contained in some $k$-hyperplane: extend their independent vectors to a basis of $k^{N+1}$ and use one of the unused coordinate linear forms. Step 1.1 leaves a point of $S$ outside that hyperplane. Choose it as $P_r$. After finitely many steps obtain $P_0,\ldots,P_N$ with basis representatives $v_0,\ldots,v_N$. For each $i$, the span of the other $N$ vectors is a hyperplane. Avoid the finite union of their intersections with $S$, using step 1.1, to choose $P_{N+1}$. Its representative has the form $\sum_i a_i v_i$ with every $a_i\ne0$. Replacing any $v_i$ by this last vector has determinant $a_i$ times the basis determinant, up to sign, so every $N+1$ of the selected points are independent. [given, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 Given an ordered frame with basis matrix $V=(v_0\ \cdots\ v_N)$ and last point represented by $V(a_0,\ldots,a_N)^T$, the invertible matrix $\operatorname{diag}(a_0^{-1},\ldots,a_N^{-1})V^{-1}$ takes it to the standard frame $[e_0],\ldots,[e_N],[1:\cdots:1]$. All its entries lie in $k$. A projective transformation fixing that standard frame has a diagonal representative, since it fixes each coordinate line. Fixing the last point forces all diagonal entries equal; hence it is the identity in PGL. Composing the normalizing maps proves existence and uniqueness between any two ordered frames. The same uniqueness holds over $L$, and the matrix just constructed over $k$ already has the required effect, so any given transformation over $L$ equals its scalar extension. This includes $N=1$, where a frame consists of three distinct points. [F3, given, algebra, step 2.1] ∎
