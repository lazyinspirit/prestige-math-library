## Statement

Consider a finite recursively closed distance-three stabilizer-code simulation encoding one logical qubit per block, with the following **checked local gadget conditions**. The finite logical alphabet consists of unitary gates, prescribed one-block pure-state preparations (in particular the usual $|0\rangle$, $|+\rangle$ and any verified pure magic-state preparation), and destructive rank-one basis measurements, together with explicitly retained classical records and their declared controls. Each template is a finite directed circuit with declared input/output quantum and logical classical ports. Syndrome-processing and ancilla records of an EC are internal to that EC; the checked channel or instrument identity includes every logical classical port, with decoded recovery acting as identity on ports it does not decode. Its internal ancillas are freshly allocated or reset for that invocation; every wire crossing invocations is an explicit template port. Recursive replacement refines these ported vertices and wires, rather than introducing an undeclared shared scratch register. These are hypotheses about elementary gadgets, rather than a hypothesis of exRec correctness or of a witness reduction. Use a complete stabilizer Pauli recovery, with one chosen Pauli correction for every syndrome, including uncorrectable syndromes. Let $D$ be this recovery followed by ideal decoding; require the zero-fault EC $E_0$ to obey $D E_0=D$ on every input. Let $P_s$, $s=0,1$, project onto the span of weight-at-most-$s$ Pauli errors applied to codewords. Equalities below hold as channel equalities on the indicated subspaces, with arbitrary finite reference systems. They must also hold when a faulty location interacts with an auxiliary memory; the memory is untouched at nonfaulty locations.

For multiple blocks, the indicated error sector is the joint linear span of tensor Pauli errors with the indicated weight in each block, tensored with an unchanged external reference. Arbitrary coherent errors and correlations between data blocks are allowed; no product noise or product state is required.

An EC with $f\le1$ faults maps every input into $P_f$. For $s+f\le1$, it preserves decoded recovery on $P_s$: $D E_f P_s=D P_s$, where a projection in a channel identity denotes restriction to its range. A gate gadget $G_f$, with ideal logical unitary $g$, on inputs in $\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}$, where $f+\sum_i s_i\le1$, has each output in $P_{f+\sum_i s_i}$ and satisfies $D^{\otimes\mathrm{out}}G_f=gD^{\otimes\mathrm{in}}$ on those inputs. A one-block preparation of a prescribed pure state with $f\le1$ faults has output in $P_f$ and decoded output its prescribed ideal state, independent of a reference or memory. A destructive measurement on $P_s$ with $s+f\le1$ has precisely the ideal logical instrument after $D$, including its retained classical outcome. Classical processing of the final outcomes is reliable. Idle gadgets satisfy the gate conditions. All scheduled operations, ancilla preparations, syndrome processing and measurements belong to these finite gadgets. Unknown unencoded inputs are not covered: initial inputs are encoded or are the prescribed preparations.

A rectangle is a gate gadget followed by output ECs; its extended rectangle (exRec) also includes its input ECs. Preparations have no input EC; destructive measurements have no output EC. Shared ECs are the only overlaps at a fixed scale. Use the following truncation convention recursively: when a later bad exRec claims an input EC, erase that shared EC from the earlier exRec, **including every elementary location belonging to the recursively expanded later exRec**. Here the expanded support is defined by the recursive input-collar construction in the proof: start with the ordinary recursively replaced gadget cores, and adjoin the leading lower-scale ECs of opening child roots, descending one scale at a time. Thus an erased EC also erases those opening lower-level ECs. Complete erased output ECs by ideal recovery ECs, starting at the lowest scale. Assume $b\ge2$ uniformly bounds the number of child-location roots in any complete one-scale exRec template, including its leading ECs; erased roots retain their template labels. Every recursive path has the declared full depth.

Scan roots in reverse circuit order. At scale zero a retained location is bad exactly when faulty. At scale $r\ge1$, perform the scale-$(r-1)$ scan inside the retained exRec, completing erased ECs ideally. Call the retained exRec bad if at least two bad child roots survive this scan, and good otherwise. A bad root claims its entire retained exRec; an earlier root loses any locations so claimed. A good root removes only its rectangle during decoding, leaving its leading ECs for the earlier scan. Use any fixed reverse topological order when roots are incomparable.

Then every good complete or truncated exRec is correct: its retained rectangle followed by decoded recovery equals decoded recovery at its input followed by the ideal logical operation, on the state produced by its retained leading ECs. The scan reduces the circuit exactly to its ideal circuit with faults only at selected bad roots. Each selected bad level-$r$ root has a full binary witness tree of depth $r$, with distinct faulty elementary leaves; trees of jointly selected bad roots have pairwise disjoint leaves. A fixed root has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ possible labelled witness trees, where $A=\binom b2$. Consequently, for any specified $k$ roots which are required to be jointly selected bad at level $r$, the independent elementary fault model gives
$$
\mathbb P(\text{all specified roots are selected bad})\le \bigl(A^{2^r-1}\eta^{2^r}\bigr)^k.
$$
This bound includes the dependence of truncation on the fault pattern; it does not assert independence of the selected bad-root events.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite recursive schedule, checked local gadget identities, decoder, truncation rule, full-depth hierarchy, bound $b$, and reverse scan in the statement. Prove correctness, disjoint witnesses, and the probability bound.

[F1] [[def-qc-noise-channel-model]]: For any fixed set of $h$ distinct elementary locations, the probability they are all faulty is $\eta^h$; other locations are unrestricted. Conditional on the pattern, each replacement is a fixed CPTP map on its own declared support; there is no additional joint cross-slot operation or undeclared persistent fault-memory wire.

[F2] [[thm-finite-union-bound]] bounds the probability of a finite union by the sum of the probabilities.

[A1] The elementary EC, gate, preparation and measurement identities in the statement hold for every allowed fault map, with references and auxiliary memory retained.

[A2] The recursive templates, full-depth replacement, shared-EC overlap rule and bound $b$ are those in the statement. Truncation erases the full elementary support of a claimed later exRec, at every nested scale.

## Exact syndrome-preserving boundary identity

The following local identity explains the syndrome-preserving alternative to a lossy recovery insertion. It applies at an **actual adjacent input boundary** of a later bad region; it does not place that boundary or justify crossing any retained gate.

For a level-$j$ block let $U_j:H_{P,j}\to H_L\otimes H_{S,j}$ be the invertible syndrome decoder, fix its zero syndrome $|0\rangle$, and define the code isometry $V_j\psi=U_j^{-1}(\psi\otimes|0\rangle)$. The coherent recovery isometry and its inverse on its range are
$$
C_j=(V_j\otimes I_{S,j})U_j:H_{P,j}\longrightarrow Q_j\otimes H_{S,j},\qquad K_j=U_j^{-1}(V_j^*\otimes I_{S,j}):Q_j\otimes H_{S,j}\longrightarrow H_{P,j}.
$$
Since $V_j^*V_j=I_L$, $K_jC_j=I_{P,j}$ exactly, including after tensoring any other blocks, classical records or memory. Tracing the stored syndrome after $C_j$ gives the code-valued recovery $\operatorname{Ad}_{V_j}D_j$; that trace is not made in the boundary identity.

If $B:H_{P,j}\otimes M\to H_{\rm out}\otimes M'$ is the actual retained channel of the selected later bad region, put $B'=B\circ(\operatorname{Ad}_{K_j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_M)$ on $Q_j\otimes H_{S,j}\otimes M$. Then
$$
B=B'\circ(\operatorname{Ad}_{C_j}\otimes\operatorname{id}_M).
$$
This is a typed channel equality on every input, not merely on the logical marginal. Both operations act only on that input block and its new syndrome register; all other logical blocks are unchanged references. Thus the reconstruction adds neither a logical data support nor an elementary witness address. It is included in the already selected bad region's effective operation, and no new bad region is introduced. The same equality is valid for a bad quantum comb when $K_j$ is inserted at its actual first input port: all its later memory ports and their chronological order remain as originally supplied. For disjoint blocks use tensor products of $C_j,K_j$. For nested cuts use their actual temporal composition and reconstruct in the inverse order, so that adjacent $K_jC_j$ pairs cancel. This last assertion is an identity of composed isometries; it does not allow any pair to cancel through an intervening retained gate.

The proof below does not insert these recovery isometries: it changes coordinates at actual owned block boundaries. The identity records why a syndrome-preserving reconstruction, if used, would have to remain local and adjacent; it cannot justify crossing a retained gate.

## Proof


1.1 Choose a complete stabilizer syndrome decoder $U:H_P\to H_L\otimes H_S$, using the stipulated Pauli recovery representative for each syndrome. The stabilizer eigenspaces are mutually orthogonal copies of the code, so this map is unitary. Write $\mathcal U=\operatorname{Ad}_U$ and $D=\operatorname{Tr}_S\mathcal U$. Recursively, $U_j$ first applies $U_{j-1}$ to each subblock and then $U$ to the resulting logical coordinates, retaining every syndrome register. Thus $H_{P,j}=H_L\otimes H_{S,j}$ after applying $U_j$. A weight-filter sector becomes $H_L\otimes S_0$ or $H_L\otimes S_1$, where $S_s$ is the span of syndrome basis vectors of errors of weight at most $s$. For a degenerate code, errors with the same syndrome give the same copy of the logical space, so this assertion remains true. All tensor products below include the external reference unchanged. [given, A1, construct]

1.2 Here are the actual circuit boundaries used below. An ordinary gadget core is its recursive replacement circuit, before adjoining leading ECs of its opening child roots. Give every child slot its schedule address and every shared EC one physical address. To expand an exRec, adjoin those opening leading EC cores, then their opening leading EC cores at the next lower scale, and continue downwards. An added leading $j$-EC is the output $j$-EC of the previous $j$-rectangle on precisely that wire; it does not include the previous gate. Thus after a later bad root claims its expanded support, its earlier neighbors lose only output ECs at the outer scale and at successively lower scales. Each affected lower rectangle still has the full input and output registers of its own gate. Its missing EC wires are connected directly from the last retained operation to the first operation owned by the later root. Register identity along that connection does not assert equality of their states after any recovery. This is a boundary in the actual retained directed circuit, not a factor of a remotely placed final decoder. Gates on other wires may lie between its endpoints in a linear schedule; none lies on that wire between these endpoints, by the definition of the preceding rectangle. [given, A2, construct, induction]

2.1 At scale one let $s_i$ count faults in the leading ECs, $f$ those in the gate, and $t_a$ those in the output ECs which are present. If their total is at most one, the leading ECs put the input in $\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}$. The gate takes this entire sector into $P_{s+f}$ on each output, where $s=\sum_i s_i$, and has decoded logical action $g$. Every present output EC preserves that action because $s+f+t_a\le1$. If an output EC is absent, the decoded gate identity already gives precisely the same conclusion on that output; no EC is inserted. This proves, simultaneously for every subset $J$ of output ECs retained, $D_{\rm out}E_JG_f|_{\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}}=gD_{\rm in}|_{\bigotimes_i P_{s_i}}$. The corresponding preparation and destructive-measurement identities are exactly those in [A1]. This establishes the base assertion for complete and truncated rectangles together. [given, A1, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 First remove transparent classical bypasses from the EC ownership graph. A retained logical classical record on which $D$ acts as identity cannot have a last incident location inside an elementary EC: replacing that location by its ideal operation followed by resetting that outgoing bit to zero is an allowed one-location CPTP fault. No later EC location could restore it. On an encoded quantum input with that logical bit equal to one, this would violate $D E_1 P_0=D P_0$, including its classical port. The same argument excludes a newly prepared retained logical bit. Thus these external record wires pass untouched through the EC; route their fan-out directly from their original incoming source, outside the EC owner. Recursive refinement preserves these bypasses because it replaces locations, and there is no EC location on such a wire to replace. Records obtained by decoding an encoded block instead belong to that block bundle until its declared measurement. Now contract ordinary recursively replaced cores. By the port discipline this recovers the parent circuit DAG, with the transparent wires bypassed: all internal ancillas close within their invocation, and every remaining external quantum or classical wire is a parent edge. Moving a shared EC from an earlier rectangle to a following selected bad exRec transfers an output-terminal subcircuit forward along that edge. No output of the moved piece returns to a retained operation of the earlier owner. To check the last assertion at nested boundaries, take the first lower EC core adjoined across the boundary: it is the trailing EC immediately before the first opening child in the already transferred piece. A retained gate on that wire between it and that child would contradict their being consecutive. Any other non-bypass output of that EC is an internal ancilla, closed within the same EC, or another output in that same block bundle. Repeat at smaller scales. Thus every collar piece transferred forward is terminal on all its non-bypass external output wires in the retained earlier owner. All such outputs of the transferred EC feed the claiming successor: syndrome-processing records are internal, transparent classical fan-out has already been bypassed, and the quantum output is precisely that successor's input bundle. Thus each new quotient edge into the successor replaces an old forward path through the preceding owner and that EC; there is no new outgoing edge to a third owner. Such a transfer cannot create a quotient cycle, since replacing every new edge by its old forward path would give a cycle in the previous DAG. Repeating these finite transfers, in the prescribed reverse root order and descending collar scale, gives disjoint owners and a causal quotient DAG. The owner's interface can be staggered in elementary time; at a stagger use its complete child block bundles, descending until each bundle is exposed. Every primitive multi-register operation belongs to one owner and is never split. This also proves that the quotient's root labels and the addresses erased at its boundaries are exactly those in the stated scan. [given, A1, A2, step 1.2, construct, induction]

3.1 We will retain the syndromes in that identity. If a CPTP map $M$ on $H_L\otimes K$ has logical marginal $g\operatorname{Tr}_K$ for every input, its conjugation by $g^{-1}$ has identity logical marginal. Its Choi matrix has marginal supported on the one-dimensional maximally entangled logical subspace. Positivity forces the entire Choi matrix to have support in that subspace tensored with the remaining registers; hence it factors as the identity-channel Choi matrix times the Choi matrix of a CPTP map on $K$. Consequently $M=\operatorname{Ad}_g\otimes T$. Apply this to the entire filter sector in step 2.1, which by step 1.1 is the full logical space tensored with an allowed syndrome subspace. It gives an exact syndrome-retaining identity on that sector, including all input correlations. Extend $T$ to the remaining syndrome space by first projecting onto this sector and its orthogonal complement and using any fixed channel on the complement. Only the restriction is used on reachable states. For a prescribed pure preparation, its pure logical marginal forces its joint logical/syndrome output to be that state tensored with a syndrome state; this follows by the same positivity-support argument. For a good destructive measurement there is no syndrome output: its full quantum-to-classical channel on the admitted sector is exactly the ideal measurement following $\operatorname{Tr}_S$, with all other-block syndromes included in the unchanged external reference. Retain its classical output as a logical wire. No tensor-factor theorem for a general instrument is being assumed. [given, A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Fix the elementary pattern. Its replacement maps are fixed local CPTP maps by [F1]; they have no unlisted persistent memory port. Contract each actual physical owner in the DAG of step 2.2, before introducing any syndrome coordinates. It is a channel $B$ on its declared block bundles and classical ports; other live blocks are references. Now change coordinates at its actual input and output bundles: $\widehat B=\mathcal U_{\rm out}B\mathcal U_{\rm in}^{-1}$, so $\mathcal U_{\rm out}B=\widehat B\mathcal U_{\rm in}$ exactly. Insert $\mathcal U^{-1}\mathcal U$ on each internal block edge of that DAG; the adjacent pairs cancel. At a staggered interface use the lower bundles of step 2.2 and keep the upper decoder on their logical outputs. No decoder crosses an intervening retained gate. In this construction a syndrome register is the coordinate of its particular physical block edge, not a new global bath: a multi-block operation can process syndromes only on its declared block ports, and a destructive measurement has no outgoing quantum or syndrome port. Thus the coordinate change introduces no extra cross-owner memory edge or cycle. In particular, for a bad owner $\widehat B$ is the conjugation of the ORIGINAL retained physical channel, with its original incoming syndromes. Its logical support is only its declared blocks and classical ports, and its elementary support has not enlarged. Neither a physical ideal recovery nor the false identity $U_j\mathcal E_j=U_j$ has been inserted. [given, F1, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, construct, algebra]

3.3 To compare this exact network to the scan, distinguish a restored address from its original physical address: the former is a virtual ideal copy at the same template slot; the original, if claimed by a later bad owner, stays with that owner. Restore the erased cores and collars from the smallest scale upwards. We prove by address induction that every opening EC address of a virtual child is also virtual. Within its restored core this is immediate; at the input boundary the address is one of the leading ECs adjoined by the expanded-support rule of step 1.2, hence was claimed and erased together with that core, and is restored as a fresh ideal copy. Repeat down to elementary addresses. Therefore every wholly added child's entire expanded support is ideal, not merely its gate core. It is good at scale zero and, inductively, at every scale, so it makes no claim on a retained physical address. A retained child's new output EC pieces have just this property; its original physical faults and original leading addresses are unchanged. Induct on its scale: the added child roots are good and make no erasure, and the original child roots have unchanged bad flags and unchanged claims on original addresses. Hence the selected bad children and their template labels agree with the scan in which those output EC pieces are absent. This proves the needed scan mapping without replacing any retained real context by an ideal channel. [given, A2, step 1.2, step 2.2, construct, induction]

4.1 Specify the induction domain at one actual INPUT boundary, rather than intersecting constraints at different circuit times. Fix the fault pattern, root and truncation. At every exposed opening lower block port record the filter required by that opening component's leading EC; impose no filter at an opening bad component. Also record the outer one-scale filter $P_{s_i}$ on the decoded subblock coordinates of each parent input block, where $s_i$ counts the effective faults in its leading EC. These are all conditions on the same input registers. Under the recursive decoder each lower-port filter constrains the syndrome register of its decoder-tree node; the outer filter constrains the outer syndrome register. Each acts as identity on the final logical coordinates by step 1.1. Thus their joint allowed space is $U_{j,\rm in}^{-1}(H_{L,\rm in}\otimes K_\partial)$ for a syndrome subspace $K_\partial$ (use a direct sum of allowed syndrome tuples if several boundary cases are allowed). No filter at a later intermediate time is included in this definition. We prove simultaneously at scale $j$: a good retained rectangle has ideal logical action on EVERY state in this full input space, with arbitrary reference; its syndrome-retaining form is the factor in step 3.1; a bad owner is its original exact conjugation; and a leading-EC subnetwork with $f\le1$ effective child faults puts its decoded subblock coordinates in $P_f$, preserving their decoded logical state on $P_s$ when $s+f\le1$. The EC assertion is on those decoded coordinates, not a claim of weight-at-most-one elementary error at every concatenation level. At scale one these assertions are exactly [A1] and step 2.1 and step 3.1. [given, A1, A2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, induction]

5.1 Suppose the assertion holds at every scale at most $j$. Decompose a retained level-$(j+1)$ exRec using the actual level-$j$ block interfaces of step 2.2, descending through staggered collars. All components used here have scale at most $j$. Start with an arbitrary state in the full input sector of step 4.1, and process this DAG in topological order. For each next good component its leading EC is either at the initial boundary, where the required filter is part of $K_\partial$, or is already in the processed prefix, where the lower-scale EC assertion forces that filter on EVERY incoming state. A bad component needs no input filter. This forward support induction proves eligibility of each lower-scale identity for every state in the parent input sector; it does not infer eligibility merely from one particular image of the parent leading EC. Contract their original channels and change coordinates as in step 3.2. The induction hypothesis replaces a good unitary component by its ideal logical gate and a syndrome-only channel; a good preparation by its prescribed pure logical state and a syndrome state; and a good destructive measurement by its ideal quantum-to-classical channel, discarding that measured block's syndrome. Classical outcomes remain explicit logical wires. On other blocks all syndrome registers remain references. The tensor syndrome channels on the surviving block edges commute with ideal logical gates on those edges, and with a measurement on its logical coordinate before its syndrome is discarded. If the parent is good there is at most one bad component by step 3.3. Keep each syndrome-edge channel in causal order. Its causal past supplies an arbitrary finite syndrome-memory input to that one fault; its causal future has no further access to logical data through a fault. After tracing the final syndromes, those future trace-preserving memory channels have no effect on the logical instrument and can be removed, even when conditioned on retained classical outcomes. With no bad component the whole syndrome network is of this harmless kind. Thus only the memory already arriving at the bad component is included in its effective fault map; no future outcome is pulled backwards and no other logical block is accessed. Classical controls are handled on each retained classical history, keeping the corresponding instrument branch and summing those branches at the end. This is the primitive one-scale template with at most one effective faulty location, the same classical ports, some output ECs omitted, and arbitrary finite memory only at that fault. It has not contracted multiple bad owners into a common channel or reordered memory interactions between them. [given, A1, A2, step 3.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.1, induction, construct]

6.1 Apply [A1] to the resulting one-scale network. If $s_i$ effective faults are in leading EC $i$, the EC condition puts its coarse output coordinates in $P_{s_i}$ for every coarse input, with the smaller syndrome registers retained as reference or allowed fault memory. On a coarse $P_s$ input it also gives the asserted decoded preservation whenever $s+s_i\le1$. This proves the auxiliary EC assertion at the new scale. The gate filter condition similarly propagates the coarse input filters, and its decoded identity, followed by any present output ECs, gives step 2.1 for the parent. Its absent output ECs remain absent. Step 3.1 now supplies the parent's syndrome-retaining factor, pure preparation factor, or destructive measurement identity, as applicable. It remains to verify the ENTIRE boundary sector for the actual parent leading-EC output. Use the following cover of the actual exposed ports. A retained good opening level-$a$ component $p$ has its immediately preceding level-$a$ EC $e$ and the descending input collar of $e$ available as physical context, possibly owned by an earlier rectangle. Here availability is relative to the network under consideration: if $p$ is inside a larger claimed owner, that owner contains this context. If a different later owner had erased $e$ from this context, its only external quantum outlet would lead to $p$. By step 1.2 and step 2.2 the claim must then include that immediately following opening region; it cannot pass through or jump across a retained gate. Encoded inputs or the prescribed pure preparations close the initial circuit boundary, and final destructive instruments give the ideal output law when all selected top roots are good. [given, A1, A2, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.2, step 3.3, step 4.1, step 5.1, induction]

7.1 A selected bad level-$r$ root, $r\ge1$, contains at least two selected bad child roots in its retained scan. A later selected bad child claimed every location of its retained exRec before the earlier child was processed. Hence the two child supports are disjoint. This remains true at every nested boundary because [A2] erases all overlapping elementary locations, not just the nominal outer EC. Induction down the tree gives $2^r$ distinct faulty elementary leaves. The same argument gives disjoint leaves for trees from jointly selected roots. Ideal completions create no faulty leaves. [given, A2, step 3.3, step 6.1, construct, induction]

8.1 Count the trees before considering probabilities. At each internal vertex its two child roots have labels in its fixed complete exRec template, with at most $b$ possibilities; hence their unordered pair has at most $A=\binom b2$ possibilities. Order that pair by the fixed template order. A full depth-$r$ binary tree has $2^r-1$ internal vertices, so it has at most $A^{2^r-1}$ labelings. A label sequence fixes every elementary leaf's physical location in the original schedule, regardless of which ECs the scan erased: truncation deletes locations and never changes a root's template address. Thus no extra count of truncation patterns is needed. Some labelings cannot survive a scan or repeat a leaf; discard them. Every actual witness is among the remaining predetermined labelings, by step 7.1. [given, A2, step 7.1, algebra]

9.1 For $k$ specified selected bad roots, choose one of these labelled witness trees for each root. There are at most $A^{k(2^r-1)}$ such tuples. For a fixed retained tuple the probability all its leaves fault is $\eta^{k2^r}$ by [F1]; no restriction on other locations, erasure decisions, or additional faults is imposed. ## Remarks

Broader primitive classes such as generic mixed multioutput preparations and arbitrary POVMs are not included in this source-derived helper and would require their own instrument/environment interface proof.
