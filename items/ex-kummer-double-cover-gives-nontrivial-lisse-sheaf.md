## Example

Assume AC. Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic different from $2$, let $X=\operatorname{Spec}k[t,t^{-1}]$, and let $Y=\operatorname{Spec}k[u,u^{-1}]$ with $t=u^2$. For a geometric basepoint over $t=1$, the cover $Y\to X$ has fiber $\{1,-1\}$. The permutation module $\mathbb F_2^{\{1,-1\}}$ with the resulting continuous left fundamental-group action defines a rank-two lisse $\mathbb F_2$-sheaf, and its monodromy action is nontrivial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields, schemes, morphism, and coefficient module in the Example.

[F1] [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]]: étale is the scheme-theoretic notion of a smooth morphism of relative dimension zero.

[F2] [[thm-etale-equivalent-flat-unramified-fp]]: under AC, a finitely presented flat morphism with zero relative differentials is étale.

[F3] [[thm-finite-etale-covers-equivalent-to-finite-continuous-pi1-sets]]: connected nonempty finite étale covers correspond to transitive finite continuous left fundamental-group actions.

[F4] [[cor-lisse-etale-sheaves-and-finite-pi1-modules]]: finite modules with continuous action correspond to lisse étale module sheaves.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is inherited through [F2]–[F4].

## Verification

**Given:** $A=k[t,t^{-1}]$, $B=k[u,u^{-1}]$ with $t\mapsto u^2$, and $\operatorname{char}(k)\ne2$.

1.1 The relation $u^2=t$ and the invertibility of $t$ give $B\cong A[U]/(U^2-t)$; the image of $U$ is invertible, so this quotient is also $A[U,U^{-1}]/(U^2-t)$. Division by the monic quadratic shows that $B$ is free over $A$ on $1,u$. The relative differentials are generated by $du$ with relation $2u\,du=0$; both $2$ and $u$ are units in $B$, so $\Omega_{B/A}=0$. Thus $Y\to X$ is finite, finitely presented and flat, and [F2] makes it finite étale. The ring $B=k[u,u^{-1}]$ is a domain, so $Y$ is connected. [given, F1, F2, A1, algebra]

2.1 Pulling back to the geometric point over $t=1$ gives $k[U]/(U^2-1)=k[U]/((U-1)(U+1))$. The map to $k\times k$ sends $g(U)$ to $(g(1),g(-1))$ and has inverse $(a,b)\mapsto (a+b)/2+(a-b)U/2$, so it is an isomorphism because $2$ is invertible. Its two geometric points are the two square roots $1$ and $-1$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The connected nonempty cover $Y\to X$ gives a transitive action on this two-element fiber by [F3]. A transitive action on two points contains the transposition, so the action is nontrivial. Its permutation action on the two basis vectors of $\mathbb F_2^{\{1,-1\}}$ is continuous and $\mathbb F_2$-linear; by [F4] it corresponds to a lisse sheaf. [F3, F4, step 2.1, construct]

4.1 The fiber, its permutation module, and the continuous action are therefore exactly the rank-two lisse sheaf described in the Example, with nontrivial monodromy as claimed. AC enters only through [F2]–[F4]. [F2, F3, F4, A1, step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
