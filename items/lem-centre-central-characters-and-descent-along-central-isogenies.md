## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a
split semisimple group over $k$ with root system $\Phi$ and root lattice
$Q=\mathbb Z\Phi$ ([[def-root-datum-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). Then: (a)
the scheme-theoretic centre $Z(G)$ equals
$\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi}\ker\alpha$, and restriction of characters identifies
$X(Z(G))$ with $X(T)/Q$
([[lem-reductive-center-radical-and-semisimple-quotient]],
[[lem-diagonalizable-character-antiequivalence]]); (b) if $V$ is a simple
rational representation of $G$ of highest weight $\lambda$, then $Z(G)$ acts on
$V$ through the character $\lambda+Q\in X(Z(G))$
([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]]). Now let
$\pi:(G',T')\to(G,T)$ be a central isogeny of split reductive groups, so that
$X(T)\subseteq X(T')$ is a subgroup of finite index. Let $\lambda\in X(T')$ and
let $V'$ be a simple $G'$-module of highest weight $\lambda$; then $V'$ factors
through $G$ if and only if $\lambda\in X(T)$, and the descended $G$-module is
simple. For identification of highest weights, choose Borel subgroups
$B'\supseteq T'$ and $B\supseteq T$ with $\pi(B')=B$; relative to these
compatible source and target Borel pairs, the descended module has highest
weight $\lambda$. Equivalently, for the finite central
subgroup scheme $N=\ker\pi$
([[def-quotient-sheaf-and-representable-quotient]]), a simple $G'$-module whose
highest weight lies in $X(T)$ has $N$ acting trivially and hence descends along
the fppf quotient $G'/N=G$
([[thm-fppf-quotient-for-affine-finite-locally-free-equivalence-relation]]).

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a split semisimple group $(G,T)$ with root system $\Phi$ and root lattice $Q=\mathbb Z\Phi$; a simple rational representation $(V,r)$ of $G$ of highest weight $\lambda$; and a central isogeny $\pi:(G',T')\to(G,T)$ of split reductive groups with kernel $N$. For highest-weight identification choose $B'\supseteq T'$ and $B\supseteq T$ with $\pi(B')=B$.

[F1] *Centre inside the torus.* For every reductive algebraic group $G$ and maximal torus $T$ one has $Z(G)\subseteq T$, and $Z(G)$ is of multiplicative type ([[lem-reductive-center-radical-and-semisimple-quotient]], [[def-group-of-multiplicative-type-and-torus]]).

[F2] *Root groups generate, and the conjugation formula.* $G$ is generated by $T$ and the root groups $U_\alpha$ ($\alpha\in\Phi$), each $u_\alpha:\mathbf G_a\to U_\alpha$ is an isomorphism of group schemes. A smooth $T$-stable subgroup contains $U_\alpha$ precisely when its Lie algebra contains $\mathfrak g_\alpha$. Also, $t\,u_\alpha(c)\,t^{-1}=u_\alpha(\alpha(t)c)$ for all $t\in T(R)$, $c\in R$, as derived universally over the torus coordinate domain in [F3] of [[lem-root-group-expansion-of-a-weight-vector]] ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], [[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F3] *Weights of a simple representation.* If $W$ is a simple rational representation of a split reductive group $(H,S)$ of highest weight $\nu$, then $\nu$ is dominant, $W_\nu$ is one-dimensional, and every weight of $W$ has the form $\nu-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$ ([[thm-simple-rational-representations-have-a-highest-weight]]).

[F4] *Diagonalizable duality.* For split diagonalizable groups, $X$ is an exact contravariant equivalence with finitely generated abelian groups ([[lem-diagonalizable-character-antiequivalence]]). Consequently, for a morphism $\psi:T\to H$ of split diagonalizable groups with kernel $K$, one has $X(K)=\operatorname{coker}(\psi^*:X(H)\to X(T))$, the character lattice of $K$ being identified with the quotient of $X(T)$ by the image of $\psi^*$ ([[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]]).

[F5] *Quotients and their universal property.* The fppf quotient of an affine finite-type group scheme by a finite locally free equivalence relation exists and represents the quotient sheaf, with the usual universal property: a morphism constant on the equivalence classes factors uniquely through the quotient ([[def-quotient-sheaf-and-representable-quotient]], [[thm-fppf-quotient-for-affine-finite-locally-free-equivalence-relation]]).

[F6] The kernel $N$ of the central isogeny is finite and lies in $Z(G')\subseteq T'$. Its restriction to the split tori is an isogeny $T'\to T$ with kernel $N$; thus pullback identifies $X(T)$ with a finite-index subgroup of $X(T')$. This torus assertion does not require the total Lie differential to be an isomorphism. ([[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]], [[lem-diagonalizable-character-antiequivalence]])

[F7] A subgroup scheme that is both unipotent and diagonalizable is trivial. The scheme image of a group homomorphism is its exact kernel quotient; in particular a homomorphism with trivial scheme kernel is an isomorphism onto its closed image. ([[lem-unipotent-and-diagonalizable-intersection-is-trivial]], [[lem-nonaffine-group-image-exact-quotient-properties]])

[F8] The root big cell $U^-\times T\times U\to G$ is an open immersion with dense image; $G$ is smooth and geometrically connected, hence geometrically integral. ([[thm-bruhat-decomposition-for-split-reductive-group]], [[def-split-reductive-algebraic-group]])

## Proof

**Given:** AC; a split semisimple group $(G,T)$ with root system $\Phi$ and root lattice $Q=\mathbb Z\Phi$; a simple rational representation $(V,r)$ of $G$ of highest weight $\lambda$; and a central isogeny $\pi:(G',T')\to(G,T)$ of split reductive groups with kernel $N$. For highest-weight identification choose $B'\supseteq T'$ and $B\supseteq T$ with $\pi(B')=B$.

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For (a), first inclusion: let $R$ be a $k$-algebra and let $z\in Z(G)(R)$. By [F1] $z\in T(R)$. Since $z$ is central, $z\,u_\alpha(c)\,z^{-1}=u_\alpha(c)$ for every $\alpha\in\Phi$ and every $c\in R$, while the conjugation formula of [F2] gives $z\,u_\alpha(c)\,z^{-1}=u_\alpha(\alpha(z)c)$. As $u_\alpha$ is an isomorphism of group schemes, $u_\alpha(R)$ is injective, so $\alpha(z)c=c$ for all $c$; setting $c=1$ gives $\alpha(z)=1$. Hence $z\in(\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi}\ker\alpha)(R)$ and $Z(G)\subseteq\bigcap_{\alpha\in\Phi}\ker\alpha$. [F1, F2, given]

1.2 Conversely let $t\in(\bigcap_\alpha\ker\alpha)(R)$. By [F2], conjugation by $t$ is the identity on $T_R$ and each $U_{\alpha,R}$, so it is the identity on the base-changed root big cell of [F8]. Restriction $O(G)\to O(U^-\times T\times U)$ is injective because this is a dense open in an integral affine scheme. Tensoring that injection with the $k$-vector space $R$ preserves injectivity: each finite tensor expression can be checked in a finite-dimensional subspace of $R$, where the map is a finite direct sum of the original injection. Thus restriction remains injective even for nonreduced $R$, and the two endomorphisms $c_t$ and $\operatorname{id}$ of $G_R$ have equal maps on coordinate rings. Hence $t$ is central. This proves the scheme equality in (a); the same argument works for split reductive groups. [F2, F8, algebra]

1.3 The restriction $\pi|_{T'}:T'\to T$ is, by [F6], a finite locally free surjection of split tori with kernel $N$, so it identifies $T$ with the fppf quotient $T'/N$ [F5]. Hence a character $\chi\in X(T')$ vanishes on $N$ if and only if it factors through $T'/N=T$, i.e. if and only if $\chi\in X(T)\subseteq X(T')$: a character trivial on $N$ is constant on $N$-orbits and factors through the quotient by its universal property, while a character pulled back from $T$ is trivial on $N$. [F5, F6]

2.1 The root groups, rather than the total Lie differential, give the root correspondence. For $\alpha'\in\Phi'$, the kernel of $\pi|_{U_{\alpha'}}$ is $N\cap U_{\alpha'}$. It is unipotent as a subgroup of $U_{\alpha'}\simeq\mathbf G_a$ and diagonalizable as a subgroup of $T'$, so it is trivial by [F7]. The restriction is therefore an isomorphism onto a smooth connected closed image $H\simeq\mathbf G_a$. Centrality of $N$ and the root conjugation formula imply $\alpha'|_N=1$, so step 1.3 gives a unique character $\alpha\in X(T)$ whose pullback is $\alpha'$. The image $H$ is $T$-stable and its nonzero tangent line has weight $\alpha$, so $\alpha$ is a root of $G$. The root-subgroup containment criterion of [F2] then gives $U_\alpha\subseteq H$; both are smooth connected curves, hence $H=U_\alpha$. Distinct $\alpha'$ give distinct $\alpha$ by injectivity of torus pullback. Since the isogeny preserves dimension and torus rank, and $\dim G=\dim T+|\Phi|$, this injection of roots is bijective. For compatible Borels, it identifies the positive roots: the image of $B'$ is the Borel generated by $T$ and the images of its positive root groups. Rank-one normalizer representatives map to the corresponding target reflections, so torus pullback intertwines the two reflections. Comparing their formulas gives $\langle\pi^*x,\alpha'{}^\vee\rangle=\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle$ for every $x\in X(T)$, and hence $\pi_*\alpha'{}^\vee=\alpha^\vee$. This includes inseparable isogenies and nonreduced $N$. [F2, F6, F7, step 1.3, algebra]

2.2 For the second assertion of (a), apply [F4] to the morphism $\psi=(\alpha)_{\alpha\in\Phi}:T\to\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbf G_m$. Its kernel is $\bigcap_\alpha\ker\alpha=Z(G)$ by steps 1.1 and 1.2, and its dual $\psi^*:\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathbb Z e_\alpha\to X(T)$ sends $e_\alpha$ to $\alpha$, so its image is exactly the root lattice $Q$. Therefore $X(Z(G))=\operatorname{coker}\psi^*=X(T)/Q$, and the restriction map $X(T)\to X(Z(G))$ is the quotient map with kernel $Q$. [F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 For (b): let $\mu$ be a weight of $V$. By [F3] applied to the split reductive group $(G,T)$, one has $\mu=\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$; hence for $z\in Z(G)(R)$ we get $\alpha(z)=1$ for all roots $\alpha$ by step 1.1, so $\mu(z)=\lambda(z)\prod_\alpha\alpha(z)^{-m_\alpha}=\lambda(z)$. The action of $z$ on the spanning weight spaces $V_\mu\subseteq V_R$ is therefore the scalar $\lambda(z)$, so $z$ acts on $V_R$ as $\lambda(z)\operatorname{id}$. This is the character of $Z(G)$ obtained by restricting $\lambda\in X(T)$, which under step 2.2 corresponds to $\lambda+Q\in X(T)/Q=X(Z(G))$. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The argument of steps 1.1, 1.2, 2.2 and 3.1 used only that the group is split reductive (generation by $T$ and the root groups, the conjugation formula, the weight description of simple modules, and [F1]), so it applies verbatim to the split reductive group $(G',T')$: $Z(G')=\bigcap_{\alpha'\in\Phi'}\ker\alpha'$ with $X(Z(G'))=X(T')/Q'$, and $Z(G')$ acts on a simple $G'$-module of highest weight $\lambda$ through the character $\lambda+Q'$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.2, step 3.1]

5.1 Now let $\lambda\in X(T')$ and let $V'$ be a simple $G'$-module of highest weight $\lambda$. By step 4.1 the centre $Z(G')$ acts on $V'$ through $\lambda+Q'$; since $N\subseteq Z(G')$ by [F6] and $Q'$ vanishes on $Z(G')$, the subgroup $N$ acts on $V'$ through the character $\lambda|_N$. Hence $N$ acts trivially if and only if $\lambda|_N=1$, which by step 1.3 is exactly $\lambda\in X(T)$. If $N$ acts trivially, the $G'$-action factors through the fppf quotient $G'/N=G$ [F5], and the resulting $G$-module is simple because every $G$-submodule is a $G'$-submodule; conversely, if $V'$ factors through $G$, then $N$ acts trivially, so $\lambda\in X(T)$. For the compatible Borel pairs chosen above, it remains to identify the highest weight: by [F3] applied to $(G',T')$, the $T'$-weights of $V'$ are $\lambda-\sum_{\alpha'\in\Delta'}m_{\alpha'}\alpha'$ with $m_{\alpha'}\ge0$ and $\lambda$ has multiplicity one, and by the root correspondence of step 2.1 the roots $\alpha'$ are pullbacks of the roots $\alpha$ of $(G,T)$ with the same positive systems; hence the $T$-weights of the descended module are $\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$, again with $\lambda$ of multiplicity one. So the descended $G$-module is simple with highest weight $\lambda$. [F3, F5, F6, step 1.3, step 2.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

- The two inclusions of steps 1.1 and 1.2 are Milne's Corollary 21.8; the computation of the root lattice is the exact sequence $0\to Q\to X(T)\to X(Z(G))\to0$ dual to $Z(G)\hookrightarrow T\xrightarrow{(\alpha)}\prod\mathbf G_m$.
- Part (b) is Milne 22.11: all weights of $V$ are congruent to $\lambda$ modulo the root lattice, and $Z(G)$ is precisely the part of $T$ on which every root is trivial.
- The descent statement is Milne 22.12; the hypothesis that $\lambda$ lies in $X(T)$ is exactly the condition that the central subgroup $N=\ker\pi$ acts trivially, so that the fppf quotient $G=G'/N$ receives the action.
