## Statement

Assume AC and Countable Choice. Let $N(Y)$ count isomorphism classes of nonzero-discriminant cubic $\mathbb Z$-orders of absolute discriminant less than $Y$, and suppose $N(Y)\le CY$ for all $Y>0$. Let $W_p(X)$ count those orders of absolute discriminant less than $X$ that are not maximal at the rational prime $p$. Then for every prime $p$ and $X>0$,
$$W_p(X)\le3N(X/p^2)+(p+1)N(X/p^6)+N(X/p^4)\le4CX/p^2.$$
The same inequalities hold when $N$ and $W_p$ are restricted to orders in cubic number fields. This does not assert either linear bound itself or a bound for maximal orders totally ramified at $p$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, Countable Choice, the positive constant $C$, the stated linear bound for $N$, a prime $p$ and $X>0$.

[F1] Delone–Faddeev associates a cubic order to a binary cubic, with multiplication laws and discriminant preserved by the correspondence; the form in a normal basis is the map $r\mapsto r\wedge r^2$ on the rank-two quotient by the scalars. [[thm-delone-faddeev-cubic-rings-and-binary-cubic-forms]]

[F2] Smith normal form exists for finite free modules over a PID. For a finite-index suborder, the change-of-basis matrix $T$ sends the trace Gram matrix $G$ to $T^{\mathsf T}GT$, so determinant multiplicativity makes the discriminant scale by $[R\prime:R]^2$. [[thm-smith-normal-form-existence-over-a-pid]] [[thm-determinant-multiplicative]] [[thm-determinant-of-transpose]]

[F3] The local cubic maximality calculation reduces a nonmaximal order to an overorder obtained by dividing one normal-basis vector by $p$ or both by $p$; its explicit multiplication-table proof is given in the cited local-density lemma. [[lem-cubic-maximal-nontotal-ramification-local-density]]

[A1] AC and Countable Choice are the explicit hypotheses of [F3]. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $R$ be counted by $W_p(X)$. By [F3], after restricting an overorder to its $p$-primary enlargement and applying Smith normal form, either it has an overorder $R'$ of index $p$, or it has an overorder $R''$ with $R=\mathbb Z+pR''$ and index $p^2$. The local overorder globalizes by intersection with $R\otimes\mathbb Z[1/p]$. In the second case [F2] gives $|\operatorname{Disc}(R'')|=|\operatorname{Disc}(R)|/p^4$, and $R''$ determines $R$. Thus these orders contribute at most $N(X/p^4)$. [A1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For an index-$p$ suborder $R\subset R'$, the quotient $R'/\mathbb Z$ is free of rank two, and the intermediate lattice $R/\mathbb Z$ corresponds to a line in $(R'/\mathbb Z)/p$. A line generated by $\bar\theta$ is closed under multiplication exactly when $\theta^2$ lies in the span of $1$ and $\theta$ modulo $p$; by [F1] this is exactly a projective zero of the associated binary cubic modulo $p$. If the cubic is nonzero modulo $p$, it has at most three projective zeros, so a $p$-primitive order $R'$ has at most three index-$p$ suborders. By [F2], this case contributes at most $3N(X/p^2)$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 If $R'$ is not $p$-primitive, all four normal-form coefficients are divisible by $p$. In $R'/pR'$ their two-dimensional non-scalar span is the unique nilradical. Let $J$ be its inverse image in $R'$. The multiplication table [F1] gives $J^2\subseteq pJ$ and $p\in J$, so $B=p^{-1}J$ is a cubic order and $R'=\mathbb Z+pB$. It is canonical from $R'$, and $[B:R']=p^2$. Thus [F2] gives $|\operatorname{Disc}(B)|=|\operatorname{Disc}(R)|/p^6$. Every $R'$ has at most $p+1$ index-$p$ sublattices containing $1$, the number of lines in $\mathbb F_p^2$, so this case contributes at most $(p+1)N(X/p^6)$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The three cases may overlap, which only increases their sum. Therefore $W_p(X)\le3N(X/p^2)+(p+1)N(X/p^6)+N(X/p^4)$. Apply the stated bound to all three arguments. For $p\ge2$, $(p+1)/p^4+1/p^2\le1$, so the right side is at most $4CX/p^2$. If $R$ is an order in a cubic field, every overorder $R\prime$, $R\prime\prime$ and $B$ above lies in the same field, so the identical argument proves the domain-restricted version. The argument makes no claim about maximal orders that are totally ramified. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
