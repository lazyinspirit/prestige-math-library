## Statement

Let $R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}R_n$ be a nonnegatively graded ring
([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]) and let $t\in R_\delta$ be a homogeneous
element of degree $\delta\ge1$. Write $R_t=S_t^{-1}R$ for the principal
localisation of $R$ at $t$ ([[def-principal-localisation]],
[[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]) and $\lambda_t:R\to R_t$,
$r\mapsto r/1$, for the localisation map. Then:

1. With $(R_t)_n:=\{r/t^m:m\ge0,\ r\in R_{n+m\delta}\}$ for $n\in\mathbb Z$,
   one has $R_t=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}(R_t)_n$; this is a graded $R$-module
   structure on $R_t$ in the sense of [[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]],
   the multiplication satisfies $(R_t)_i(R_t)_j\subseteq(R_t)_{i+j}$, and
   $\lambda_t(R_n)\subseteq(R_t)_n$ for every $n$.
2. If $\phi:R\to T$ is a unital ring homomorphism into a graded ring $T$ with
   $\phi(R_n)\subseteq T_n$ for every $n$, then
   $\ker\phi=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}(\ker\phi\cap R_n)$: an element of $R$ lies in
   $\ker\phi$ if and only if all of its homogeneous components do.
3. If $J\subseteq R$ is generated by homogeneous elements $a_1,\ldots,a_k$,
   then every homogeneous component of every element of $J$ lies in $J$, and
   $R/J$ is nonnegatively graded by $(R/J)_n=(R_n+J)/J$, the quotient map
   being degree-preserving.
4. Assume $\delta=1$ and let $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ be as in 3, with
   $d_i=\deg a_i$. Then the degree-zero part $(JR_t)_0$ of the extended ideal
   $JR_t$ is the ideal of $(R_t)_0$ generated by
   $a_1/t^{d_1},\ldots,a_k/t^{d_k}$.
5. Assume $\delta=1$ and $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$ as in 3. Then
   $((R/J)_t)_0\cong(R_t)_0/(a_1/t^{d_1},\ldots,a_k/t^{d_k})$ as rings.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nonnegatively graded ring $R=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}R_n$, a homogeneous
element $t\in R_\delta$ of degree $\delta\ge1$, the principal localisation
$R_t$ with localisation map $\lambda_t$, and, where stated, homogeneous elements
$a_1,\ldots,a_k\in R$ of degrees $d_i$ generating the ideal
$J=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$.

[L1] A nonnegatively graded ring is a commutative ring
$S=\bigoplus_{n\ge0}S_n$ with $S_iS_j\subseteq S_{i+j}$, and an element of
$S_n$ is homogeneous of degree $n$; a graded $S$-module is an $S$-module
$M=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb Z}M_n$ with $S_iM_j\subseteq M_{i+j}$, and elements of
$M_j$ are homogeneous of degree $j$ ([[def-graded-ring-and-graded-module]]).

[L2] For $f\in R$ the powers $S_f=\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}$ form a multiplicative
subset, $R_f=S_f^{-1}R$, and its elements may be written $r/f^n$; the fraction
$r/s$ is zero exactly when $ur=0$ for some $u\in S$, so
$r/s=r'/s'$ exactly when $u(rs'-r's)=0$ for some $u\in S$
([[def-principal-localisation]],
[[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]).

[L3] In a commutative ring the ideal $(S)$ generated by a subset $S$ consists
of the finite sums $\sum_ir_is_i$ (the empty sum included and equal to $0$)
([[thm-generated-ideal-description-in-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L4] Localisation is exact and has the universal property: the quotient map
$R\to R/J$ and $\lambda_t$ induce a unique ring homomorphism
$R_t\to(R/J)_t$ with $r/t^m\mapsto\bar r/\bar t^{\,m}$, it is surjective, and its
kernel is $JR_t$, so $R_t/JR_t\cong(R/J)_t$; the induced map is the localisation
of the quotient data ([[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]],
[[cor-localisation-commutes-with-kernels-images-and-cokernels]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For $r\in R$ written in its finite decomposition $r=\sum_nr_n$ with $r_n\in R_n$, and any $m\ge0$, one has $r/t^m=\sum_nr_n/t^m$ with $r_n/t^m\in(R_t)_{n-m\delta}$, so every element of $R_t$ is a finite sum of elements of the sets $(R_t)_n$; each $(R_t)_n$ is an additive subgroup, because a sum of two fractions with numerators in $R_{n+m\delta}$ can be written over the common denominator $t^m$ with numerator again in $R_{n+m\delta}$, and likewise for additive inverses. [L1, L2]

1.2 Let $j\in J$; by [L3] $j=\sum_ic_ia_i$ for finitely many $c_i\in R$, and decomposing $c_i=\sum_nc_{i,n}$ with $c_{i,n}\in R_n$, the $N$-th homogeneous component of $j$ is $\sum_ic_{i,N-d_i}a_i$ (over those $i$ with $N\ge d_i$), a combination of the generators and hence an element of $J$; so every homogeneous component of every element of $J$ lies in $J$. [L1, L3]

2.1 If $z_n=r_n/t^m\in(R_t)_n$ are finitely many elements with pairwise distinct degrees $n$ and $\sum_nz_n=0$, then $\bigl(\sum_nr_n\bigr)/t^m=0$, so $t^N\sum_nr_n=0$ for some $N\ge0$ by [L2]; the elements $t^Nr_n\in R_{n+m\delta+N\delta}$ are homogeneous of pairwise distinct degrees, so each $t^Nr_n=0$ by [L1], whence $z_n=r_nt^N/t^{m+N}=0$; therefore the sum of the $(R_t)_n$ is direct and every element of $R_t$ has a unique decomposition into homogeneous pieces. [L1, L2, step 1.1]

2.2 Hence $R/J$ is nonnegatively graded with $(R/J)_n:=(R_n+J)/J$: every class $r+J$ is the finite sum $\sum_n(r_n+J)$ of such classes, and if $\sum_n(r_n+J)=0$ with $r_n\in R_n$ then $\sum_nr_n=j\in J$ and step 1.2 gives $r_n\in J$ for every $n$, so each $r_n+J=0$; moreover $(R_i+J)/J\cdot(R_j+J)/J\subseteq(R_{i+j}+J)/J$, and the quotient map carries $R_n$ into $(R/J)_n$. [L1, step 1.2]

2.3 The elements of the extended ideal $JR_t$ are exactly the fractions $j/t^m$ with $j\in J$ and $m\ge0$: one inclusion is $j/t^m=(1/t^m)(j/1)\in JR_t$, and conversely every element of $JR_t$ is $\sum_iy_i(a_i/1)$ by [L3], which after writing the finitely many $y_i\in R_t$ over a common denominator $t^m$ becomes $\bigl(\sum_ic_ia_i\bigr)/t^m$ with $\sum_ic_ia_i\in J$. [L3, step 1.2]

3.1 The grading is multiplicative and $\lambda_t$ is degree-preserving: $(r/t^m)(s/t^{m'})=rs/t^{m+m'}$ with $rs\in R_{(i+m\delta)+(j+m'\delta)}$ whenever $r\in R_{i+m\delta}$ and $s\in R_{j+m'\delta}$, so $(R_t)_i(R_t)_j\subseteq(R_t)_{i+j}$, and $\lambda_t(r)=r/1=r/t^0\in(R_t)_n$ for $r\in R_n$; together with steps 1.1 and 2.1 this gives the graded $R$-module structure and the direct sum decomposition of 1. [L1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 Let $\phi:R\to T$ satisfy $\phi(R_n)\subseteq T_n$ and let $r=\sum_nr_n$ with $r_n\in R_n$; then $\phi(r)=\sum_n\phi(r_n)$ with $\phi(r_n)\in T_n$ of pairwise distinct degrees $n$, so $\phi(r)=0$ if and only if $\phi(r_n)=0$ for every $n$, i.e. if and only if every homogeneous component of $r$ lies in $\ker\phi$; hence $\ker\phi=\sum_n(\ker\phi\cap R_n)$, a direct sum because the $R_n$ are independent in $R$, which is claim 2. [L1, step 2.1]

3.3 Assume $\delta=1$ and let $x\in(JR_t)_0$; by step 2.3 write $x=j/t^m$ with $j\in J$, and replacing $j$ by its homogeneous component of degree $m$, which lies in $J$ by step 1.2 and contributes exactly the degree-zero part of $j/t^m$ by step 2.1, we may suppose $j$ homogeneous of degree $m$; by [L3] write $j=\sum_ic_ia_i$ with $c_i\in R_{m-d_i}$ (the degree-$(m-d_i)$ components of arbitrary coefficients), so $x=\sum_i(c_i/t^{m-d_i})(a_i/t^{d_i})$ with $c_i/t^{m-d_i}\in(R_t)_0$ and $a_i/t^{d_i}\in(R_t)_0$; conversely $a_i/t^{d_i}\in(JR_t)_0$ because $a_i\in J$ and $\deg(a_i/t^{d_i})=d_i-d_i=0$. Hence $(JR_t)_0=(a_1/t^{d_1},\ldots,a_k/t^{d_k})$ as ideals of $(R_t)_0$. [L3, step 2.1, step 1.2, step 2.3]

4.1 Assume $\delta=1$ and $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_k)$; by [L4] the surjection $R_t\to(R/J)_t$ has kernel $JR_t$, and it carries homogeneous elements to homogeneous elements of the same degree, because a degree-$n$ element of $R_t$ can be written $r/t^m$ with $r\in R_{n+m\delta}$ homogeneous and the quotient map is degree-preserving by step 2.2; hence the induced map $(R_t)_0\to((R/J)_t)_0$ of degree-zero parts is surjective with kernel $(JR_t)_0$, and step 3.3 identifies this kernel, giving $((R/J)_t)_0\cong(R_t)_0/(a_1/t^{d_1},\ldots,a_k/t^{d_k})$. [L4, step 2.2, step 3.3]

5.1 Claims 1 to 5 are proved: the localisation at a homogeneous element is graded with the displayed degree pieces, degree-preserving ring maps have graded kernels, the quotient by an ideal generated by homogeneous elements is graded, and for a degree-one $t$ the degree-zero parts of $JR_t$ and of $(R/J)_t$ are the displayed dehomogenised ideals and quotients; no choice principle is used, the argument working with the explicit fraction calculus of $R_t$. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 2.2, step 3.3, step 4.1] ∎
