## Statement

Assume full AC and the constructed earring data. The real determinant line of the cylindrical ASD operator is orientable on every fixed-endpoint trajectory configuration component. One can choose rank-one integral orientation modules for the critical points and compatible determinant identifications for every relative trajectory class. They are compatible with concatenation, gauge equivalence and the nonlinear broken-trajectory collars. With the translation orientation removed, the signed boundary of an index-two compactification is the signed sum of its ordered index-one broken pairs. Consequently the integral signed differential defined by those pairs has square zero. Choices of bases in the critical orientation modules change the resulting complex by the corresponding diagonal signs.

## Proof

**Given:** The real determinant family of the gauge-fixed cylindrical operator with fixed nondegenerate critical ends, and its determinant line defined using finite-dimensional target augmentations.

1.1 **Determinant excision, including its signs.** Enlarge the target of a Fredholm operator by a finite-dimensional space spanning its cokernel. The augmented kernel gives the line $\det\ker\widehat D\otimes\det V^*$, and common enlargements give its exact-sequence transitions as in [F2]. For two operators with identical collar symbols, use the finite block rotation exchanging their equal collars from [F3]. Stabilize before rotating; the inverse on the complementary infinite-dimensional subspaces varies continuously, leaving a finite-dimensional exact sequence of augmented kernels and targets. Its exterior powers give the determinant excision isomorphism. A third summand gives the same isomorphism in either association, because both are obtained by ordered exterior products of the same stabilized bases; the only exchange sign is $(-1)^{ab}$ for determinant factors of indices $a,b$. The same construction on long cylindrical critical collars uses [F1]'s critical inverse; a constant cylinder contributes its canonically oriented invertible line. Thus this is the determinant isomorphism used by [F5]'s normal inverse, not merely a numerical index equality. Gauge conjugation and continuous compact changes of lower-order terms commute with these stabilized exact sequences. [F1, F2, F3, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Local complex orientations without a singular $SU(3)$ lift.** On an oriented Euclidean four-ball choose an orthogonal complex structure, with its singular plane, if present, a complex line. On its punctured conformal completion use Euclidean radial weighted spaces and framed boundary conditions. The gauge-fixed ASD operator has the Kähler expression $\bar\partial_A^*\oplus\bar\partial_A$ from adjoint-valued $(0,1)$-forms to $(0,0)\oplus(0,2)$-forms: combine $d_A^*$ and the Kähler-form component of $d_A^+$ into one complex component; the remaining component is $(d_Aa)^{0,2}$. This identity follows by writing $d_A=\partial_A+\bar\partial_A$ in the two constant complex coordinates, and uses no integrability assumption on $A$. Hence this real operator is complex linear. Radial weights commute with these complex structures. Its framed Fredholm realization follows by cutting off the compact interior and separating the constant end operator on the logarithmic radial cylinder as in [F1]; choose a weight between its integer indicial roots. Finite-dimensional constant gauge values are separated as the framing factors. The order-two action preserves both complex coordinates and commutes with the complex structures on the form spaces, so its invariant operator is complex linear as well. Kernel and cokernel therefore have their actual complex orientations, independently of their dimensions. This argument constructs the needed local orientations directly; a global $SU(3)$ orbifold extension, which need not exist, is not asserted. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The elementary marked local changes and their relations.** An equivariant clutch on $S^3\subset\mathbb H$ satisfies $g(iq(-i))=i g(q)(-i)$. On its fixed circle it has values in $S^1_i$ with winding $n$. Its ordinary degree $d$ satisfies $d\equiv n\pmod2$: a regular value on that fixed circle has its fixed preimages counted by $n$, and all other preimages occur in two-element orbits. Conversely first homotope its fixed-circle restriction to the degree-$n$ circle map, extend this homotopy over a small invariant tubular neighborhood by the logarithm and then by the homotopy extension on its finite cells. Two such maps agreeing there differ only on the free part. Quotienting that free part, its first and second extension obstructions vanish because $\pi_1SU(2)=\pi_2SU(2)=0$; its remaining three-cell degrees add to $(d-n)/2$. Thus ordinary insertions away from the plane, with pair degree two and winding zero, and the quaternion clutch $g(q)=q$, with $(d,n)=(1,1)$, generate all these local classes. Reversing both oriented plane coordinates changes the latter to $(1,-1)$. The sole degree relation is $(1,1)+(1,-1)=(2,0)$; any residual homotopy of such cell constructions is generated on four-cells by a supported $\pi_4SU(2)$ transformation. The first model has ordinary framed index eight; the quaternion model has invariant framed index four. Indeed [F3]'s entire ordinary framed kernel is its four center directions, scale and three framing directions, with no cokernel; invariance leaves two center directions tangent to the fixed plane, scale, and the one axis framing direction. The reversed model has the same index. Excision then gives relative real index $4d$ and complex index $2d$ for every local change, including inverses. Consequently reversing the two complex coordinates changes its complex determinant orientation by $(-1)^{2d}=+1$. Orientations survive a reversal of the local plane orientation, and permutations of such changes have no determinant sign. The relation above preserves orientation: choose both insertions in one flat complex chart; the cut-and-paste determinant comparison is complex linear. The residual four-cell transformations preserve orientation by [F2]'s ordinary supported $SU(3)$ argument and step 1.1's localization. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **The actual gauge-loop generators and their monodromy.** For a loop of cylindrical configurations, lift it to connections over the compactified cylinder, with a closing determinant-one gauge. At both temporal ends this gauge has central values. Its restrictions on a core are sections of the axis circle with longitudinal monodromy conjugating $z$ to $z^{-1}$. The sections $1$ and $-1$ lie in different components: a lift of their angle has $a(2\pi)=-a(0)+2\pi m$, whose parity distinguishes them. Thus the closing gauge has the same central sign at its two ends; remove that common sign. The resulting based family on each core contracts to one. Explicitly its temporal loop winding is an integer equal to its own negative after longitudinal transport, hence zero; its angle has a unique lift zero on the initial temporal section, anti-periodic along the core, and scalar contraction contracts it relative that section and both ends. Extend this contraction to a tubular neighborhood by the gauge logarithm and a cutoff, as in [F4]. The remaining gauge is identity near the singular surface and near the temporal ends. On the finite four-dimensional relative cell model, a section with fiber $SU(2)=S^3$ has no obstruction on cells of dimension below three. Its three-cell degrees give an integral relative three-cocycle; differences with zero such cocycle can be removed over the three-skeleton, leaving transformations supported in four-balls. Geometrically its three-cocycle is dual to a finite oriented curve in the ordinary region, with normal identified with the oriented adjoint three-plane. This curve description can also be obtained by taking a regular inverse image of the central fiber value $-1$ after a relative smooth perturbation; local conjugation fixes that value and acts orientation-preservingly on its normal space. The inverse-image tubular map is precisely the degree-one instanton insertion transported around that curve. Collapsing its complement reconstructs the section, with the remaining four-cell ambiguity already described. Along an ordinary instanton transport, step 1.1 compares the determinant with the framed local line $\det(TX\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\operatorname{ad}P)$. Both $TX$ and $\operatorname{ad}P$ are oriented, so this line returns with positive sign. Supported four-cell gauges have positive sign by [F2]. This exhausts the closing gauges, proving trivial determinant monodromy on the actual fixed-endpoint configuration component. No unproved simply connectedness of that singular configuration space was used. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 **Comparisons between classes and their coherence.** Fix a reference connection and paths to each critical point. Changes in those paths are represented by the actual gauge components of [F4]. Their finite-cell construction changes a path by ordinary ball insertions and by local singular insertions on either core; equivalently, cut out their disjoint marked balls and compare the equal complements. Use step 1.1 and the complex orientations of steps 2.1–3.1 to identify the resulting determinant orientation sets. Moving a ball in the ordinary region has positive monodromy by step 4.1. Moving a singular insertion along the core gives the line $\det(T\Sigma\oplus\mathbb R\oplus\mathbb R_Q)$ of its two center, scale and axis framing directions. Its orientation local system is the axis orientation line, since $\Sigma=\mathbb R\times K$ is oriented. An insertion which returns with the same charge has trivial axis transport and hence positive monodromy. If transport reverses that axis, its local label changes from $(1,1)$ to $(1,-1)$; step 3.1 gives the same comparison orientation and the ordinary-insertion relation accounts for the changed marking. These are all relations by the cell construction; its possible four-cell ambiguities have positive sign. Thus the identifications are independent of decomposition. With ordered excision, they are associative under concatenation; no odd insertion-index exchange sign occurs. Define the critical orientation modules as the free rank-one modules on the two orientations of the reference-to-critical determinant, imposing that the two choices are negatives. These comparisons and step 1.1 now give, for every trajectory class, its determinant orientation line as the tensor of its endpoint modules, in one fixed incoming/outgoing order. This proves the claimed coherence across classes, not just orientability of separate components. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 **Boundary signs and the integral differential.** At a regular index-one solution its determinant is its one-dimensional tangent line; divide by its positively oriented translation vector to obtain the signed point map between the endpoint orientation modules. At a broken index-two pair, step 1.1 and [F5]'s normal inverse identify its determinant with the ordered two level determinants. In the two core phase coordinates the common translation and increasing separation form an invertible change of basis with one fixed sign, independent of the pair. On quotienting common translation, the outward collar direction is the negative of increasing $r=1/T$. Thus the induced boundary sign is this same fixed convention sign times the composite of the two signed point maps. The middle endpoint orientation module cancels with its dual. All index-two compactifications have compact oriented one-manifold interiors and these coherently oriented collars; their algebraic boundary sum is zero, since each compact interval has opposite endpoint signs and circles have no boundary. Multiplying by the one common convention sign gives exactly $\sum_\beta d_{\beta\gamma}d_{\alpha\beta}=0$. Sums are finite by [F5]. Changing a chosen critical orientation basis changes each incident point map by that endpoint sign, giving the asserted diagonal chain isomorphism. [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra] ∎
