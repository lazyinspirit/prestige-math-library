## Statement

Let $p$ be a prime, let $G$ be a finite abelian group, and let $R=\mathbb Z_p[\![\mathbb Z_p^{\mathbb N}]\!]$ and $A=R[G]$ be as in the definition of the infinite-rank completed $p$-adic Iwasawa algebra. Then $R$ and $A$ are not Noetherian.

## Statement references

[[def-infinite-rank-p-adic-iwasawa-algebra]]

[Burns, Daoud, and Liang, §3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.02946)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The prime $p$, a finite abelian group $G$, and the inverse-limit presentation of $R$ in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-infinite-rank-p-adic-iwasawa-algebra]]: $R$ is the inverse limit of the formal power-series rings in the first $n$ coordinate variables, with projections setting the last variable to zero, and every finite set of coordinates has the stated retraction.

## Proof

1.1 Let $I=(X_i:i\ge1)$ be the ideal of $R$ generated algebraically by all coordinate variables. Suppose that $I$ were generated by finitely many elements $r_1,\ldots,r_m$. Since each $r_j\in I$, each is a finite sum of variables times coefficients in $R$; the union $S$ of the variables appearing in these finitely many expressions is finite. Thus $I=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)\subseteq(X_i:i\in S)$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 Choose $j\notin S$. The retraction $\rho_{\{j\}}:R\to\mathbb Z_p[\![X_j]\!]$ kills every $X_i$ with $i\in S$ but sends $X_j$ to the nonzero variable $X_j$. Hence $X_j\notin(X_i:i\in S)$, contradicting $X_j\in I=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$. Therefore $I$ is not finitely generated. [F1, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

3.1 A Noetherian ring has every ideal finitely generated, so $R$ is not Noetherian. The augmentation homomorphism $A=R[G]\to R$, $\sum_{g\in G}r_g g\mapsto\sum_{g\in G}r_g$, is surjective. If $A$ were Noetherian, its quotient $R$ would be Noetherian, a contradiction. Thus $A$ is not Noetherian. [F1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
