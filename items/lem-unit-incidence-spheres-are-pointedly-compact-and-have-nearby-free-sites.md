## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $X=\operatorname{Sym}^m(\Sigma,j)$ for a compact connected Riemann surface, $m\ge1$. Fix the compact-bubble nearly symmetric geometry and finitely many chosen incidence divisors. A unit-incidence sphere means a smooth holomorphic map $u:S^2\to X$ whose incidence degree at every surface point is one. Fix a source point $p$ and a node value $x$ outside the diagonal and chosen divisors, in a fixed exact holomorphic chart. If smooth autonomous nearly symmetric structures $J_n\to J_\infty$ have common geometry and spheres $u_n$ have unit incidence degree and $u_n(p)=x$, then after a subsequence there are holomorphic sphere automorphisms $g_n$ fixing $p$ for which $u_n\circ g_n$ converges globally smoothly to a nonconstant $J_\infty$-sphere of the same degree and node value.

For the standard integrable structure every such sphere has open dense globally unique interior immersion points. Choose a compact protected neighborhood $D$ of the diagonal and chosen divisors, disjoint from finitely many pairwise disjoint exact node-chart neighborhoods $A_x$. Optionally fix finitely many compact totally real submanifolds $L_a$ disjoint from $D$, for the standard structure. Suppose $x$ lies in one $A_x$. There is a smooth neighborhood of the standard structure, among structures fixed on these protected sets and on the $L_a$, in which EVERY unit-incidence sphere through that $x$ has a nonempty open set of globally unique immersion points with image outside $D\cup\bigcup A_x\cup\bigcup L_a$.

Both assertions hold uniformly over an actual smooth compact interval of integrable surface backgrounds, using adapted relative symmetric-product charts and common fixed node charts and node values, provided the optional $L_a$ remain totally real for every background. The compactness assertion does not assume any regularity or index. No transversality, sphere avoidance or disk count is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] Compact bubble positivity gives a nonnegative integer sphere degree common to all surface points, domain-controlled energy, and constancy when this degree is zero. Finite holomorphic ordered lifts exist for an actual integrable symmetric-product curve, including higher collisions and repeated constant roots. ## Proof

1.1 The degree-energy identity in [F1] gives one finite energy bound for the sequence. Cover the closed source sphere by finitely many interior conformal disks. [F2]'s countable energy-basis extraction, small-energy estimates and higher bootstrap give a smooth base limit off finitely many positive energy atoms. Removability fills each base puncture. At each atom use [F2]'s actual isolated sphere rescaling. Its child masses lie between the common positive quantum $h$ and the parent mass minus $h$, and its total mass is the sum of the sphere energy and its child masses. Induction on the integer part of mass divided by $h$ therefore resolves all children in finitely many steps. Each constant bubble sphere has at least two children and its parent node, so at least three nodes. Finitely many atoms and finite branching give a finite tree of smooth spheres, with the exact energy identity and vanishing annular energy and diameter from [F2]. This is the same actual interior induction with no source boundary; no new global compactness theorem is imported. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Incidence degrees add over this tree. Choose a unitary connection on the actual incidence line as in [F3] and integrate its curvature on the finitely many converging sphere cores. A remaining annulus has pullback curvature bounded by $C|du_n|^2$, so its integral tends to zero with its energy. To define the integer degree on each capped core, choose a small circle in each shrinking source annulus whose angular derivative has squared integral tending to zero; Fubini applied to its annular energy supplies such a circle. In the common coordinate ball about the limiting node the image loop can be capped by radial contraction. The contraction's area is bounded by its diameter times its angular length, and hence by Cauchy--Schwarz and those small derivative integrals tends to zero. The connection curvature integral on that cap tends to zero. This proves convergence of the sum of the capped degrees to the original degree; because these are integers it gives eventual exact equality, separately for every chosen incidence point. All component degrees are nonnegative by [F1], and their sum is one. Exactly one component is therefore nonconstant and has degree one. Every other component is constant; a stable constant bubble with at least three nodes would require at least two descendant branches each ending in a nonconstant leaf, impossible with only one nonconstant component. Thus either the original base is that component, or the base is constant and one sphere bubble is attached directly to it. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Normalize to retain the marked value, including when the positive bubble forms at the mark. Put $p=\infty$ in a global holomorphic coordinate $z$ on the original sphere. If the base is nonconstant, no reparametrization is needed. If the bubble is at a finite point, the actual rescaling can be performed in this global coordinate and is $z=c_n+r_n\zeta$, $c_n\in\mathbb C$, $r_n>0$. It already fixes infinity. If it forms at $p$, use $w=1/z$ and the rescaling $w=c_n+r_n\zeta$. The bubble-coordinate preimage of $p$ is $\xi_n=-c_n/r_n\in\mathbb C$. Extract a subsequence converging in $\mathbb{CP}^1$. For a finite limit take $T_n(\zeta)=\xi_n+1/\zeta$, a genuine complex Möbius map taking infinity to $\xi_n$ and converging to a genuine such map. If $\xi_n\to\infty$, take $T_n(\zeta)=\zeta/(1+\zeta/\xi_n)$ after discarding finitely many zeros; these converge to the identity in the Möbius group and also take infinity to $\xi_n$. Then $g_n(\zeta)=1/(c_n+r_nT_n(\zeta))$ is a genuine holomorphic sphere automorphism fixing the original infinity exactly. In both cases the rescaled maps converge smoothly away from one possible point to the degree-one component precomposed with a limiting genuine Möbius map. These maps fix the marked point throughout; the limiting marked value will follow from global convergence, not from discarding that point. [F2, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 The convergence after this normalization is GLOBAL smooth. Its limit is a smooth sphere with the entire limiting energy by step2.1. Away from its possible exceptional point convergence is already smooth. The energy in a small fixed disk about that point is at most its limiting sphere energy there plus a term tending to zero: subtract the energy on its compact complement from the total energy identity. Choose the disk small enough that this bound is below the common small-energy threshold. [F2]'s interior estimates give a uniform gradient bound on a smaller disk, and its higher bootstrap gives every derivative bound there. Countable diagonal extraction yields smooth convergence on that smaller disk too; its limit equals the already known limit on their overlap. The same argument covers the marked point if it is exceptional. Consequently its value is $x$, since every normalized map has value $x$ there. Incidence degrees are unchanged by conformal reparametrization and pass to the global smooth limit, so its degree is one. These arguments only use smooth convergence and the uniform bounds in [F1] and [F2], so they hold also for a converging relative integrable background family by [F4]. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 For a standard integrable sphere construct the individual-root incidence surface using the actual root continuation in [F1]: away from its finite exceptional set let a sheet record ONE distinct support root, retaining its positive integer multiplicity. Complete each finite monodromy cycle by its power-coordinate disk exactly as in that proof; bounded root functions extend there. This gives finitely many compact incidence components $S_\nu$, finite base maps $p_\nu:S_\nu\to S^2$, holomorphic surface maps $f_\nu:S_\nu\to\Sigma$, and fixed positive root multiplicities $r_\nu$. For a surface point $q$ avoiding the finite branch images and the constant root values, the zeros of the incidence divisor's local defining product are exactly the points with $f_\nu=q$, counted with $r_\nu$ and their local holomorphic multiplicities. Consequently $1=\deg u(E_q)=\sum_{f_\nu\text{ nonconstant}}r_\nu\deg f_\nu$. This is the actual individual-root cover, not the possibly much larger covering of every ordering. Every summand is a positive integer. Thus precisely one is nonconstant and its surface projection has degree one; every other incidence component maps to a constant surface point. A nonconstant degree-one holomorphic map between compact curves is a biholomorphism: at each target point the sum of its local positive multiplicities is one, so it has exactly one preimage and nonzero derivative; the complex inverse theorem gives local inverses and compactness gives the global inverse. Remove the finite set of constant incidence values and the finite branch values of the active component's projection to the base sphere. An active root then determines its unique incidence point by the surface biholomorphism and hence determines its unique base point. Therefore if the same unordered tuple were attained at two base points, this active root would force them to coincide. Such points occur on an open dense base set: the excluded incidence points and branch values are finite, and a nonconstant holomorphic map cannot have derivative zero on an open set. In target coefficient coordinates its derivative's common zero set is discrete unless it vanishes identically, by the local holomorphic power series identity principle. Compactness makes that zero set finite. This proves open dense globally unique immersion points, including uniqueness against the whole sphere. Repeated constant roots cause no difficulty; a sphere through the stipulated off-diagonal $x$ in fact cannot have a repeated constant root. [F1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 There is a free image point outside the protected union. If the whole connected image lay in $D\cup\bigcup A_x\cup\bigcup L_a$, its value $x$ and the positive separation of $D$ from the other compact sets would put it in $\bigcup A_x\cup\bigcup L_a$. An interior point outside the $A_x$ has a source open neighborhood mapping into the finite union of totally real $L_a$. Their closed preimages cover that neighborhood; a finite union of closed sets with empty interior cannot contain an open disk, since the complements are dense open and their finite intersection is dense open by successively shrinking nonempty disks. Thus one preimage has interior. On it the complex-linear differential has image in a totally real tangent space, so vanishes; holomorphic continuation makes the sphere constant. If there is no point outside the $A_x$, connectedness and their pairwise separation put the entire image in one exact holomorphic chart. Its coordinates are holomorphic functions on the compact sphere; their affine-plane restrictions are bounded entire functions, hence constant by [F4]. Both alternatives contradict degree one. Hence the free preimage is open and nonempty. It meets the dense unique-immersion set from step5.1. [F1, F4, step 5.1, construct, contradiction]

7.1 This site persists under global $C^1$ convergence. Choose two real target components whose derivative at that source point is invertible. On a small convex source disk their projected derivative is uniformly close to that invertible derivative, giving a lower Lipschitz bound by integrating along segments; the bound remains valid for every sufficiently close map. The compact source complement has image separated from the site's value by global uniqueness, and uniform convergence preserves that separation at the nearby value. Its positive distance from the closed protected set persists too. Thus the nearby maps have the same globally unique free immersion site; varying the source point slightly proves openness. If the asserted neighborhood of the standard family failed, Countable Choice would give structures converging to one standard background parameter and pointed degree-one spheres without such a site. Steps1.1--4.1 give global smooth convergence after node-fixing Möbius reparametrizations to a standard sphere. Step6.1 and the just proved stability contradict the selection. Finite node sets and compact background parameters give the uniform assertion by the same subsequence argument. This supplies compactness and a genuine open free site, while leaving every operator-surjectivity and avoidance claim separate. [F1, F2, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
