## Statement

Let $K$ be a field, $\delta\in K$, $n\ge0$, and $A=\mathrm{TL}_n(K,\delta)$. Let $\varepsilon:A\to K$ send the identity diagram to $1$ and every nonidentity basis diagram to $0$. Let $K_\varepsilon$ be the one-dimensional left module defined by $\varepsilon$. Then $K_\varepsilon$ is projective if and only if there exists a nonzero idempotent $e\in A$ with $d_i e=0$ for every $1\le i<n$. Every such idempotent automatically satisfies $\varepsilon(e)=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The diagram algebra over a field, its elementary generators, and their spanning property.

[F1] The diagram algebra is generated by $d_1,\ldots,d_{n-1}$; every basis diagram is a product of these generators, with the empty product giving the identity ([[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-generators-span]]).

[F2] Stacking is associative and diagram composition cannot increase the number of propagating strands ([[def-temperley-lieb-diagram-category]], [[thm-temperley-lieb-diagram-category-laws]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** Split the augmentation and use the generator-word kernel.

1.1 A basis diagram has $n$ propagating strands exactly when it is the identity. If either factor in a product has fewer than $n$ propagating strands, every exterior through path in the product must pass through a distinct propagating path in that factor; hence the product also has fewer than $n$. Thus the span of nonidentity diagrams is a two-sided ideal and $\varepsilon$ is an algebra homomorphism. Its kernel is spanned by nonempty generator words by [F1]. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 If $K_\varepsilon$ is projective, the surjection $\varepsilon:A\to K_\varepsilon$ splits as a left $A$-module map $s$. Put $e=s(1)$. Then $\varepsilon(e)=1$ and, for every $a\in A$, $ae=s(\varepsilon(a))=\varepsilon(a)e$. Taking $a=e$ gives $e^2=e$, and taking $a=d_i$ gives $d_i e=0$. [given, step 1.1, construct]

2.2 Conversely suppose $e\ne0$, $e^2=e$, and $d_i e=0$ for every generator. Every nonempty generator word kills $e$ by its rightmost letter, so [F1] gives $ae=\varepsilon(a)e$ for all $a\in A$. If $\varepsilon(e)=0$, then $e\in\ker\varepsilon$ is a linear combination of nonempty generator words, whence $e^2=0$, contradicting $e^2=e\ne0$. Thus $\varepsilon(e)=1$, and $s:K_\varepsilon\to A$, $s(\lambda)=\lambda e$, is an $A$-linear section of $\varepsilon$. Hence $K_\varepsilon$ is a direct summand of the free left regular module and is projective. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 prove both implications and the normalization $\varepsilon(e)=1$. No uniqueness assertion is needed for this projectivity equivalence. [step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
