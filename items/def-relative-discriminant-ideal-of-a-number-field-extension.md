## Definition

Let $L/K$ be a finite separable extension of number fields, put $n=[L:K]$, and let $\mathcal O_L$ and $\mathcal O_K$ be their rings of integers. For each ordered $K$-basis $x_1,\ldots,x_n$ of $L$ with every $x_i\in\mathcal O_L$, form
$$\Delta(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=\det\bigl(\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x_i x_j)\bigr)_{1\le i,j\le n}.$$
The **relative discriminant ideal** $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is the integral ideal of $\mathcal O_K$ generated by all such determinants.

## Facts & Assumptions


**Given:** A finite separable extension $L/K$ of number fields and $n=[L:K]$.

[F1] The field trace is defined as the trace of multiplication, and in a finite separable extension it is the sum over the $K$-embeddings ([[def-field-norm-and-trace]], [[thm-field-norm-and-trace-by-embeddings]]).

[F2] Integral elements over a nonzero ring are closed under products and finite sums ([[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]]).

[F3] The ring of integers consists of elements integral over $\mathbb Z$ ([[def-ring-of-integers-of-a-number-field]]).

[F4] The trace pairing $(x,y)\mapsto\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(xy)$ is nondegenerate for a finite separable extension ([[lem-trace-pairing-for-a-finite-separable-extension]]).

[F5] Every element of a number field has a nonzero integer multiple that is an algebraic integer ([[thm-clearing-denominators-for-an-algebraic-number]]).

[F6] The ideal generated by a subset $S$ is the intersection of all two-sided ideals containing $S$, and such intersections are ideals ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[lem-ideal-criterion-and-intersections]]).

[F7] A number field is finite over $\mathbb Q$, and for a finite extension $[L:K]$ is its finite vector-space dimension ([[def-number-field]], [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]).

[F8] A finite separable extension is an extension in which every minimal polynomial has distinct roots ([[def-separable-elements-and-separable-extensions]]).

[F9] For a nonzero integral ideal $\mathfrak a\subseteq\mathcal O_K$, its absolute norm is $|\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak a|$, and this quotient is finite ([[def-absolute-norm-of-an-ideal]], [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]]).

[F10] A fractional ideal is a nonzero $\mathcal O_K$-submodule of $K$ that becomes an integral ideal after multiplication by some nonzero element of $\mathcal O_K$ ([[def-fractional-ideal]]).

## Verification

1.1 If $x_i,x_j\in\mathcal O_L$, then $x_ix_j$ is integral over $\mathbb Z$ by [F2] and [F3]. Every $K$-embedding of $L$ sends it to an algebraic integer, so [F1] and [F2] show that $\operatorname{Tr}_{L/K}(x_ix_j)$ is integral over $\mathbb Z$. It lies in $K$, hence it belongs to $\mathcal O_K$ by [F3]. The determinant therefore lies in $\mathcal O_K$, and [F6] makes the generated subset an ideal of $\mathcal O_K$. [F1, F2, F3, F6, given, algebra]

1.2 Choose a $K$-basis $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ of $L$. By [F5], choose a nonzero integer $c_i$ with $c_i y_i\in\mathcal O_L$ for each $i$. Since each $c_i$ is a nonzero element of $K$, the elements $c_i y_i$ remain a $K$-basis; thus the generating set in the Definition is nonempty. [F5, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 The form in [F4] is nondegenerate, so for the integral $K$-basis chosen in step 1.2 its Gram matrix is nonsingular. Its determinant is one of the generators and is nonzero. Thus $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ is a nonzero integral ideal. It is also a fractional ideal in $K$, with denominator $1$, by [F10]. By [F9], its absolute norm is a finite positive integer. [F4, F9, F10, step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
