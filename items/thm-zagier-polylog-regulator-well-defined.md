## Statement

Let $F$ be a number field, $m\ge2$, and let $e_m=r_2$ for even $m$ and $e_m=r_1+r_2$ for odd $m$. For a formal sum $u=\sum_i n_i[x_i]\in\mathcal A_m(F)$, the branchwise formula in the preceding definition gives a vector

$$\rho^{\mathrm{pre}}_{m,F}(u):=\left(\sum_i n_i\mathcal L_m(\sigma_j(x_i))\right)_{1\le j\le e_m},$$

where the selected embeddings are one representative from each complex-conjugate pair when $m$ is even, and all real embeddings together with one representative from each complex pair when $m$ is odd. This expression is independent of branch, extends continuously to projective endpoints, and vanishes on $\mathcal C_m(F)$. Therefore it factors uniquely through $\mathcal A_m(F)/\mathcal C_m(F)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A number field $F$, an integer $m\ge2$, and $u\in\mathcal A_m(F)$.

[F1] [[def-zagier-higher-bloch-prequotient-and-polylogarithm]]: $\mathcal A_m(F)$ is the kernel of the tensor-symbol map, and $\mathcal C_m(F)$ is generated by differences of projective specializations from $\mathcal A_m(\mathbb Q(t))$.


[F2] $B_j$ are the coefficients in $v/(e^v-1)=\sum B_jv^j/j!$. [[def-bernoulli-numbers-by-their-generating-function]]

[F3] A complex power series can be differentiated termwise inside its disc. [[thm-termwise-differentiation-of-complex-power-series]]

[F4] Analytic continuation depends only on the endpoint-fixed homotopy class of the path, and standard meridians freely generate a finitely punctured disk's fundamental group. [[thm-monodromy-theorem]] [[thm-the-punctured-disk-fundamental-group-is-free-on-standard-meridians]]

[F5] Every nonconstant complex polynomial has a complex root. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-algebra-minimum-modulus-proof]]

[F6] Wirtinger derivatives obey the real-differentiable chain rule. [[thm-wirtinger-chain-rule-for-real-differentiable-maps]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** explicit monodromy cancellation, inversion, and all-weight tensor-contraction induction.

1.1 Write $\beta_j=2^jB_j/j!$ and $B(v)=\sum\beta_jv^j=2v/(e^{2v}-1)$. Thus $B(-v)=e^{2v}B(v)$, $\beta_1=-1$, and $\beta_j=0$ for odd $j>1$, since $B(v)+v=v\coth v$ is even. For $l=\log|z|$ put $A_m(z)=\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\beta_jl^j\operatorname{Li}_{m-j}(z)$, so $\mathcal L_m=\operatorname{Re}_m A_m$. Branches here mean continuation of this expression from the defining power-series branches along one path, equivalently compatible local branches of the polylogarithm hierarchy; independently changing one summand while fixing all other summands is not continuation of the branchwise expression. From the series and [F3], $z\operatorname{Li}'_n=\operatorname{Li}_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$ and $\operatorname{Li}_1=-\log(1-z)$, $\operatorname{Li}'_1=(1-z)^{-1}$. These identities persist under continuation. [F1, F2, F3, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The series branches are holomorphic near zero. A positive meridian around $1$ changes $\operatorname{Li}_n(z)$ by $-2\pi i\,w^{n-1}/(n-1)!$, where $w=\log z$ on the continued branch. For $n=1$ this is the logarithm increment. Inductively its derivative is the preceding increment divided by $z$; the integration constant is zero at $z=1$, since the local primitives of $\log(1-z)$ multiplied by a function holomorphic at1 have a finite limit and their monodromy tends to zero there. This proves the formula for every $n$, not just its derivative. Consequently the increment of $A_m$ is $-2\pi i$ times $[v^{m-1}]e^{wv}B(lv)$. The conjugate of $e^{wv}B(lv)$ is $e^{\bar wv}B(lv)=e^{-wv}B(-lv)$ because $w+\bar w=2l$. Thus that coefficient is real when $m$ is odd and purely imaginary when $m$ is even. Its increment therefore has zero selected real or imaginary part. The meridian at0 fixes the initial series germ. Any loop is contained in a large twice-punctured disk, whose meridians generate by [F4]. Since each meridian fixes the resulting real-analytic germ, every word does, and homotopic paths have the same continuation by applying [F4] to the holomorphic summands. Hence $\mathcal L_m$ is a single-valued real-analytic function on $\mathbb C\setminus\{0,1\}$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 We prove the inversion identity without importing a zeta-value or polylogarithm inversion formula. Work off the positive real axis, choose $w=\log(-z)$ and compatible branches, and set $R_n(z)=\operatorname{Li}_n(z)+(-1)^n\operatorname{Li}_n(1/z)$. Directly $R_1=-w$, and differentiation gives $dR_n/dw=R_{n-1}$ for $n\ge2$. As a formal power series in $v$, $R(v,w)=\sum_{n\ge1}R_n(z)v^n$ therefore satisfies $\partial_wR=vR-v$, so $R=1+C(v)e^{wv}$. At $z=-1$, $w=0$, each odd $R_n$ vanishes and each even $R_n$ is real (use the convergent real series at $-1$). Thus $C$ is a real even formal series, with constant term $-1$. The difference $\mathcal L_m(z)-(-1)^{m-1}\mathcal L_m(1/z)$ is the selected part of $[v^m]B(lv)R(v,w)$. The contribution of $B(lv)$ has selected part zero, because its odd coefficient vanishes for odd $m>1$ and its even coefficient is real. For the other contribution, conjugation of $B(lv)C(v)e^{wv}$ equals its value at $-v$, using $C(-v)=C(v)$ and $w+\bar w=2l$. Its degree-$m$ coefficient is consequently purely imaginary for odd $m$ and real for even $m$, again with selected part zero. This proves inversion on that cut domain and hence everywhere by the single-valued real-analytic continuation of step 2.1. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Near0, $\operatorname{Li}_r(z)=O(z)$, so every term $l^j\operatorname{Li}_{m-j}(z)$ tends to zero. Inversion gives the limit zero at infinity. Near1, the only unbounded elementary term is $\operatorname{Li}_1=-\log(1-z)$; all higher $\operatorname{Li}_r$ have a finite limit there by integrating this logarithmic singularity recursively with $1/z$ holomorphic near1. That limit is $\sum_{n\ge1}n^{-r}$ for $r\ge2$, by the absolutely convergent series approached radially. Since $l=O(|z-1|)$, the factor $l^{m-1}\log(1-z)$ tends to zero and all terms with $j\ge1$ disappear. Thus the continuous value at1 is zero for even $m$ and $\sum n^{-m}$ for odd $m$. This proves continuity at all three projective endpoints and fixes their values for the remaining induction. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 The all-weight differential needs all its lower-weight terms. Put $Q_m=A_m-(-1)^m\overline{A_m}$, so $Q_m=2\mathcal L_m$ for odd $m$ and $Q_m=2i\mathcal L_m$ for even $m$, and put $Q_1=-2\log|1-z|$. In the following formal series the variable $v$ is independent of $z$: let $L(z,v)=\sum_{n\ge1}\operatorname{Li}_n(z)v^n$ and $A(z,v)=B(lv)L(z,v)$. Then $\sum Q_mv^m=A(z,v)-\overline{A(z,-v)}$. The derivative identities of step 1.1 give $z\partial_zL=vL+vz/(1-z)$, and $\partial_zl=1/(2z)$. Logarithmic differentiation of $B(-v)=e^{2v}B(v)$ therefore gives $$z\partial_z\sum_{m\ge1}Q_mv^m=vC(lv)\sum_{m\ge1}Q_mv^m+vB(lv)\frac{z}{1-z},\qquad C(v)=1+\frac{B'(v)}{2B(v)}=\frac{1-B(v)}{2v}.$$ The last equality follows directly from $B(v)=2v/(e^{2v}-1)$. Thus $[v^r]C(v)=-\beta_{r+1}/2$. Extracting degree $m$ and separating its $Q_1$ term proves the exact recurrence $$\partial_zQ_m=-\frac1{2z}\sum_{j=1}^{m-2}\beta_jl^{j-1}Q_{m-j}+\beta_{m-1}l^{m-2}\left(\frac{\log|1-z|}{z}+\frac{l}{1-z}\right).$$ For $m=2$ the lower-weight sum is empty. All equalities are coefficientwise finite differentiations, so no convergence in the formal variable or interchange of infinite analytic sums is assumed. [F2, F3, F6, step 1.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 Strengthen the functional-equation assertion to finite real coefficient sums of rational functions $x_i(t)\in\mathbb C(t)$: if $\sum_i n_i x_i^{\odot(m-2)}\otimes(x_i\wedge(1-x_i))=0$ in the symmetric/exterior tensor space on $V=\mathbb C(t)^\times\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R$, then $\sum_i n_iQ_m(x_i(t))$ is constant. Constant functions0,1 or infinity have prescribed constant values and can be set aside; every other function is considered off its finitely many preimages of0,1,infinity. For each fixed allowed $t$, $\lambda_t(f)=\log|f(t)|$ and $\delta_t(f)=f'(t)/f(t)$ are real-linear functionals on $V$ (with complex codomain for $\delta_t$). Evaluating the tensor relation by $\lambda_t^{m-2}\otimes(\delta_t\wedge\lambda_t)$ gives $$\sum_i n_i l_i^{m-2}\left(\frac{x_i'}{x_i}\log|1-x_i|+\frac{x_i'}{1-x_i}l_i\right)=0.$$ This is precisely the sum of the residual terms in step 5.1 after the chain rule. It already proves that the derivative of the asserted functional equation vanishes for $m=2$, its base case. [F1, F6, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 The lower-weight contractions used in the induction are legitimate in the stated symmetric tensor space. For any real-linear functional $\ell:V\to\mathbb R$, the derivation on its symmetric algebra sends $x^{\odot N}$ to $N\ell(x)x^{\odot(N-1)}$. Contracting $j\le m-2$ slots and dividing by the nonzero falling factorial gives, for any fixed functionals $\ell_1,\ldots,\ell_j$, the relation $$\sum_i n_i\Bigl(\prod_{r=1}^j\ell_r(x_i)\Bigr)x_i^{\odot(m-j-2)}\otimes(x_i\wedge(1-x_i))=0.$$ It is a weight-$(m-j)$ relation with constant real coefficients. This explains why real rather than only integer coefficients were included in the strengthened induction; no inference from scalar multiplication of individual relations is being made. [step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Factor each rational function as $x_i(t)=c_i\prod_{a\in S}(t-a)^{\nu_a(x_i)}$ with one finite set $S$ and integer orders $\nu_a$, using [F5] and finite polynomial division. Its leading constant $c_i\ne0$ is multiplicative in $x_i$, so $\lambda_0(x)=\log|c(x)|$ is a real-linear functional on $V$, as is each order $\nu_a$. Consequently $x_i'/x_i=\sum_a\nu_a(x_i)/(t-a)$ and $l_i=\lambda_0(x_i)+\sum_a\nu_a(x_i)\log|t-a|$. Assume functional constancy through weight $m-1$. For each $1\le j\le m-2$, each $a\in S$, and every fixed list $\ell_2,\ldots,\ell_j$ drawn from $\lambda_0,\nu_b$, apply step 7.1 with $\ell_1=\nu_a$. The induction hypothesis makes $\sum_i n_i\nu_a(x_i)\prod_{r=2}^j\ell_r(x_i)Q_{m-j}(x_i(t))$ constant. At $t=a$, every nonzero-coefficient term has $\nu_a(x_i)\ne0$ and hence $x_i(a)=0$ or infinity, where $Q_{m-j}$ is zero by step 4.1; all other coefficients are zero. The constant is therefore zero. [F5, step 4.1, step 7.1, induction, algebra]

9.1 Expand $l_i^{j-1}$ using its finite expression in step 8.1. Each coefficient is a product of fixed functionals $\lambda_0,\nu_b$, multiplied by a scalar product of the numbers $\log|t-b|$. Step8.1 annihilates each resulting sum with its extra $\nu_a(x_i)$ factor. Multiplying by $1/(t-a)$ and summing therefore proves $$\sum_i n_i\frac{x_i'}{x_i}l_i^{j-1}Q_{m-j}(x_i(t))=0\qquad(1\le j\le m-2).$$ By step 6.1 the residual sum also vanishes. Step5.1 and the Wirtinger chain rule [F6] now give $\partial_t\sum_i n_iQ_m(x_i(t))=0$. Since its conjugate is $(-1)^{m+1}$ times itself, its $\partial_{\bar t}$ derivative vanishes as well. Thus its real gradient is zero. The complement of a finite set in $\mathbb C$ is connected by polygonal paths, so it is constant there; continuity from step 4.1 extends that same value across all omitted points and to infinity. This completes the strengthened induction for every $m\ge2$. [F6, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 8.1, induction, algebra]

10.1 Return to the exact integral tensor kernel of [F1]. A relation in $\mathcal A_m(\mathbb Q(t))$ maps to a real tensor relation of step 6.1, so step 9.1 proves $\sum_i n_i\mathcal L_m(x_i(t))$ constant on $\mathbb P^1(\mathbb C)$. Each embedding $\sigma:F\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ sends evaluation at $a\in\mathbb P^1(F)$ to evaluation at $\sigma(a)$, since the rational functions have rational coefficients. Thus the two projective specializations $\phi_a(u),\phi_b(u)$ have identical regulator values in every selected embedding. Every generator of $\mathcal C_m(F)$ is killed, and hence so is the whole subgroup. Finally the sum defining $\rho^{\rm pre}$ is a homomorphism by additivity of finite formal sums; branch independence and endpoint continuity hold termwise by steps 2.1–4.1. The elementary quotient universal property gives the unique factor through $\mathcal A_m(F)/\mathcal C_m(F)$. The parity-selected list has exactly $e_m$ components by its definition, and requires no Borel-rank or regulator comparison theorem. This proves all original assertions and all weights, including the empty sum and endpoint symbols. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 9.1, algebra] ∎
