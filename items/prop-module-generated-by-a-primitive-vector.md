## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(V,r)$ be a
rational representation of a split reductive group $(G,T)$ (not necessarily
finite-dimensional) that is generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector $v$
of weight $\lambda$
([[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]). Then: (a) $V$ is
generated as a module over the opposite unipotent group $U^-$ by $v$; (b)
$V=kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V_\mu$, where every weight $\mu$ of $V$ has
the form $\mu=\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ with
$m_\alpha\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, and $V_\lambda=kv$ has dimension one; (c)
$\lambda$ is dominant; (d) the sum of all proper $G$-stable subspaces of $V$ is
proper, so $V$ has a largest proper $G$-submodule and the quotient by it is a
simple $G$-module generated by the image of $v$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, unipotent radical $U=B_u$, opposite unipotent group $U^-$, a rational representation $(V,r)$ generated as a $G$-module by a primitive vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$.

[F1] *Big cell.* The multiplication map $U^-\times B\to G$ is an open immersion onto a dense open subscheme of $G$ ([[thm-bruhat-decomposition-for-split-reductive-group]]).

[F2] *Morphisms equal on a dense open.* Since the split reductive group $G$ is smooth, hence reduced, two morphisms from $G$ to a separated target that agree on a dense open subscheme are equal ([[cor-morphisms-equal-on-dense-open-reduced-source]]).

[F3] *Root expansion.* For $\alpha\in\Phi$ and a weight vector $w\in V_\mu$ one has $u_\alpha(c)\cdot w=w+\sum_{i\ge1}c^iw_i$ with $w_i\in V_{\mu+i\alpha}$, only finitely many nonzero ([[lem-root-group-expansion-of-a-weight-vector]]).

[F4] *Primitive vectors.* $v\ne0$ is fixed by $U$ and satisfies $t\cdot v=\lambda(t)v$, and $B$ acts on the line $kv$ through $\lambda$ ([[def-primitive-vector-of-a-rational-representation]]).

[F5] *Weights and the order.* The weight spaces are the eigenspaces of $T$, the order on $X$ is generated by the positive simple roots, and an expression of an element of the root lattice as $\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ is unique ([[def-weight-and-dominant-weight-of-a-rational-representation]], [[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

[F6] *Simple reflections.* For a simple root $\alpha$ the reflection acts on weights by $s_\alpha(\mu)=\mu-\langle\mu,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$, and it is realized by an element of the normalizer of $T$ ([[lem-normalizer-action-permutes-weight-spaces]], [[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]], [[lem-sl2-structure-and-root-coordinates]]).

[F7] Every finite subset of a rational representation lies in a finite-dimensional subrepresentation. ([[lem-finite-dimensional-subcomodules-contain-elements]])

[F8] Ordered products of the negative root groups give $U^-$. ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *(a).* By [F7], a finite-dimensional $G$-submodule contains $v$; since $v$ generates $V$, that submodule is $V$. Let $Z$ be the $U^-$-submodule generated by $v$, defined as the span of the coefficients of its $U^-$-coaction. Coassociativity makes this span a subcomodule; equivalently it is the smallest subcomodule containing $v$. If a linear functional $f\in V^*$ vanishes on $Z$, the regular function $g\mapsto f(gv)$ restricts to zero on $U^-B$: universally over every base algebra, $ubv$ is the scalar by which $b$ acts on $kv$ times $uv$, and the latter has all its coefficients in $Z$. By [F1] and [F2] this regular function is zero on $G$. Thus $f$ vanishes on the coefficients of the $G$-coaction of $v$, whose span is $V$. In finite dimension the annihilator of $Z$ being zero implies $Z=V$, proving (a). No vector over a general base algebra is treated as an element of $V$. [F1, F2, F4, F7, given, algebra]

2.1 *(b), weights.* By (a), $V$ is the coefficient span of the $U^-$-coaction of $v$. The ordered product of the negative root groups is $U^-$ by the root-group supplier. Applying [F3] successively to this universal product shows that all these coefficients lie in $kv+\sum_{\mu<\lambda}V_\mu$: each application of $u_{-\alpha}(c)$ replaces a weight $\mu$ by $\mu$ or by $\mu-i\alpha$ with $i\ge1$, and $\lambda-\sum m_\alpha\alpha$ with $m_\alpha\ge0$ is the corresponding form, the simple roots generating the positive roots. Distinct weights give independent eigenspaces, so the sum $kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V_\mu$ is direct, and the $V_\lambda$-component of every element of $V$ is a multiple of $v$; since $v\ne0$ this gives $V=kv\oplus\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V_\mu$ and $V_\lambda=kv$ of dimension one. [F3, F4, F5, F8, step 1.1]

3.1 *(c).* Let $\alpha\in\Delta$ be a simple root and let $n$ represent the reflection $s_\alpha$. Since $v$ has weight $\lambda$, the vector $n\cdot v$ has weight $s_\alpha(\lambda)$ by [F6]; it is nonzero, so $s_\alpha(\lambda)$ is a weight of $V$, hence by step 2.1 of the form $\lambda-\sum_{\beta\in\Delta}m_\beta\beta$ with $m_\beta\ge0$. Writing $s_\alpha(\lambda)=\lambda-\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ and comparing the expansions of $s_\alpha(\lambda)-\lambda$ in the basis $\Delta$ of the root lattice, uniqueness of the coefficients [F5] gives $\langle\lambda,\alpha^\vee\rangle=m_\alpha\ge0$. Hence $\lambda$ is dominant. [F5, F6, step 2.1]

3.2 *(d).* Every proper $G$-stable subspace $W\subsetneq V$ has $W\cap V_\lambda=0$: if $W$ contained a nonzero element of $V_\lambda=kv$, then it would contain $v$, hence by (a) the whole $U^-$-span of $v$, which is $V$, a contradiction. Since $W$ is $T$-stable, its weight components lie in $W$: applying the coordinate functional of each character basis element to its coaction extracts that component in $W$. Thus the vanishing of $W\cap V_\lambda$ forces every proper $G$-stable subspace to be contained in $\bigoplus_{\mu<\lambda}V_\mu$, and so is their sum $M$; since $v\notin M$ the sum is proper and is therefore the largest proper $G$-stable subspace. For any nonzero $G$-submodule $Q\subseteq V/M$, its preimage in $V$ is a $G$-stable subspace strictly containing $M$, hence equal to $V$; so $V/M$ is simple, and it is generated by the image of $v$. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 Steps 1.1, 2.1, 3.1 and 3.2 prove (a), (b), (c) and (d). [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎

## Remarks

- The density step in (a) is the scheme-theoretic form of Milne's "as $U^-B$ is dense in $G$, $V$ is spanned by $U^-Bv$"; the hypothesis that one vector generates the rational module already forces that module to be finite-dimensional, by the finite-subcomodule theorem.
- The weights of $V$ below $\lambda$ are exactly the elements of the form $\lambda-\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta}m_\alpha\alpha$ that occur; the order is the dominance order generated by the positive simple roots.
