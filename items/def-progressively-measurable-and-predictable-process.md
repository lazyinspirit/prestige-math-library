## Definition

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,P)$ be a probability space with a continuous-time
filtration $(\mathcal F_t)_{t\ge0}$
[[def-continuous-time-filtration-and-all-pairs-martingale]]. All processes here
are real-valued and indexed by $[0,\infty)$. This definition uses no choice
principle: only generated sigma-algebras, product rectangles and the given
stopping times occur, and every object mentioned is constructed from them.

1. **Progressively measurable.** A process $X=(X_t)_{t\ge0}$ is
   **progressively measurable** relative to $(\mathcal F_t)$ when for every
   $t\ge0$ the restricted map
   $[0,t]\times\Omega\to\mathbb R$, $(s,\omega)\mapsto X_s(\omega)$, is
   measurable for the product sigma-algebra
   $\mathcal B([0,t])\otimes\mathcal F_t$
   [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]].

2. **Time-zero and interval generators.** On the product space
   $[0,\infty)\times\Omega$, let $\mathcal P$ be the sigma-algebra generated by
   the family
   $$\mathcal G:=\bigl\{(s,u]\times A:\ 0\le s<u<\infty,\ A\in\mathcal F_s\bigr\}\ \cup\ \bigl\{\{0\}\times A:\ A\in\mathcal F_0\bigr\}.$$
   The interval generators with $s=0$ are included, so $(0,u]\times A$ with
   $A\in\mathcal F_0$ is a generator. The sigma-algebra $\mathcal P$ exists by
   [[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-exists-and-is-minimal]] and is called the
   **predictable sigma-algebra**. A process $H=(H_t)_{t\ge0}$ is
   **predictable** when the map $(s,\omega)\mapsto H_s(\omega)$ is
   $\mathcal P$-measurable. For a finite horizon $T>0$ we write
   $\mathcal P_T$ for the sigma-algebra on $[0,T]\times\Omega$ generated by the
   same family restricted to $u\le T$ together with the time-zero generators;
   the two conventions agree on predictable processes stopped at $T$, and every
   statement below names its horizon.

3. **Sections, adaptedness, and null sets.** Two structural conventions are
   used repeatedly and are part of the definition.
   (a) Every generator is a measurable rectangle of
   $\mathcal B([0,\infty))\otimes\mathcal F$, hence
   $\mathcal P\subseteq\mathcal B([0,\infty))\otimes\mathcal F$; the time-zero
   generator $\{0\}\times A$ has $dt\otimes P$-measure zero for every $A$. A
   set in $\mathcal P$ has each time section in $\mathcal F_s$ whenever it is
   measurable, by the monotone-class computation from the generators.
   (b) If a process is predictable then it is progressively measurable: for
   $s\le t$ the section $\omega\mapsto H_s(\omega)$ is $\mathcal F_s$-measurable
   $\subseteq\mathcal F_t$-measurable, and the progressive-measurability claim
   is the restriction of the joint map to $[0,t]\times\Omega$; the restriction
   of a $\mathcal P$-measurable map to $[0,t]\times\Omega$ is measurable for
   $\mathcal B([0,t])\otimes\mathcal F_t$ because every generator with $u\le t$
   lies in that product sigma-algebra and the generators with $u>t$ have
   intersection with $[0,t]$ of the form $(s,t]\times A$ with $A\in\mathcal F_s$
   or $[0,t]\times\Omega$, both of which are in
   $\mathcal B([0,t])\otimes\mathcal F_t$.

The family $\mathcal G$ is not assumed to be a pi-system: a finite intersection
of generators is empty or again a generator, or a time-zero generator, because
$(s,u]\cap(s',u']=(s\vee s',u\wedge u']$ and $A\cap A'\in\mathcal F_{s\vee s'}$
whenever $A\in\mathcal F_s$, $A'\in\mathcal F_{s'}$, while
$\{0\}\times A$ meets an interval generator only if that interval begins at
$0$, in which case their product is $\{0\}\times(A\cap A')$ with
$A\cap A'\in\mathcal F_0$. This observation, and the fact that the family of
finite unions of disjoint generators is an algebra generating $\mathcal P$, is
used by the density theorem below.

Because predictability is a measurability requirement for the joint map, it is
preserved by pointwise limits, products and linear combinations of predictable
processes, and a deterministic Borel function $h$ of the time variable defines
a predictable process exactly when $h$ is Borel measurable. In particular the
indicators $1_{[0,u]}$ and $1_{(0,u]}$ are predictable for every deterministic
$u\ge0$, and the process $s\mapsto 1_{s\le\tau(\omega)}$ for a stopping time
$\tau$ [[def-continuous-time-stopping-time]] is predictable, since
$$\{(s,\omega):s>\tau(\omega)\}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}}(q,\infty)\times\{\tau<q\}=\bigcup_{q\in\mathbb Q_{>0}}\bigcup_{n>q}(q,n]\times\{\tau<q\}$$
is a countable union of generators: for rational $q>0$ one has
$\{\tau<q\}=\bigcup_{q'<q,\ q'\in\mathbb Q}\{\tau\le q'\}\in\mathcal F_q$. The
complement of that set inside $[0,\infty)\times\Omega$ is the event of the
indicator, so $1_{[0,\tau]}$ is predictable; its left-continuity in the time
variable is the visual form of the same computation.

No completeness of the filtration and no right continuity of $(\mathcal F_t)$
is used or assumed.
