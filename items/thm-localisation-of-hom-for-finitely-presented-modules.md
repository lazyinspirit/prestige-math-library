## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring, let $S \subseteq R$ be multiplicative, and let $M,N$ be left $R$-modules. The natural map
$$
\theta_{M,N}:S^{-1}\!\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N) \longrightarrow \operatorname{Hom}_{S^{-1}R}(S^{-1}M,S^{-1}N)
$$
is injective when $M$ is finitely generated, and it is an isomorphism when $M$ is finitely presented.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring $R$, a multiplicative subset $S \subseteq R$, and left $R$-modules $M,N$.

[L1] The natural localisation map on Hom satisfies $\theta_{M,N}(f/s)(m/u)=f(m)/(su)$ ([[lem-localisation-of-hom-natural-map]]).

[L2] A finitely generated module has a finite generating set, and a finitely presented module admits a finite presentation by finite free modules ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], [[def-finitely-presented-module-and-algebra]]).

[L3] A localised fraction is zero exactly when one denominator kills its numerator ([[lem-zero-in-a-localised-module]]).

[L4] The group $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an $R$-module ([[lem-hom-module-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[L5] The natural localisation map on Hom is an isomorphism for finite free sources ([[lem-localised-hom-finite-free-case]]).

[L6] A finite presentation reduces the Hom-localisation comparison to the finite free case ([[lem-localised-hom-finite-presentation-kernel-step]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Suppose that $M$ is generated by $m_1,\dots,m_r$ and that $\theta_{M,N}(f/s)=0$. Then $f(m_i)/s=0$ for each $i$ by [L1], so [L3] gives $u_i \in S$ with $u_i f(m_i)=0$. Let $u=u_1 \cdots u_r$. Then $uf(m_i)=0$ for every generator, hence $uf=0$ as a homomorphism $M \to N$. Since $\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,N)$ is an $R$-module by [L4], [L3] applied there gives $f/s=0$. Therefore $\theta_{M,N}$ is injective whenever $M$ is finitely generated. [L1, L2, L3, L4, choose]

1.2 If $M$ is finitely presented, [L2] supplies a finite presentation $R^m \to R^n \to M \to 0$. The free modules $R^m$ and $R^n$ satisfy the isomorphism hypothesis of [L6] by [L5], so [L6] makes $\theta_{M,N}$ an isomorphism. [L2, L5, L6]

2.1 Steps 1.1 and 1.2 prove the injective and finitely presented claims. [step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
