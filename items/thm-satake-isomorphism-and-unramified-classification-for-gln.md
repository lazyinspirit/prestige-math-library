## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and the local data in the spherical Hecke definition. The normalized Satake transform is an isomorphism
$$
\mathcal S:H(G,K)\longrightarrow\mathbb C[X_1^{\pm1},\ldots,X_n^{\pm1}]^{S_n}.
$$
Every irreducible smooth admissible spherical complex representation $V$ of $G$ has $\dim V^K=1$. Its Hecke character, transported through $\mathcal S$, corresponds to a unique unordered tuple $\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\}\subset\mathbb C^\times$, with multiplicities. This is a bijection between isomorphism classes of such representations and unordered nonzero complex $n$-tuples. For an ordered tuple, let $\chi_i$ be the unramified character with $\chi_i(\varpi)=\alpha_i$. The normalized principal series $I(\chi)$ has a spherical irreducible admissible subquotient, unique up to isomorphism, with exactly that tuple. No full local Langlands correspondence or classification of ramified representations is asserted.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The local data of [F1] and AC [A1]. All representation spaces are algebraic smooth complex representations.

[A1] AC is assumed for the Haar-existence and normalization context carried by the local suppliers. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The field, groups, smoothness, admissibility, transform and normalized principal series are defined in [[def-spherical-hecke-algebra-and-normalized-satake-transform-for-gln]].

[F2] Iwasawa decomposition and its exact integration formula are proved in [[lem-gln-local-iwasawa-and-cartan-decompositions]].

[F3] Fixed vectors are exact, and irreducible spherical smooth representations correspond to simple unital $H$-modules. [[lem-compact-open-averaging-and-spherical-hecke-modules]]

[F4] The normalized transform descends to Laurent polynomials and preserves convolution. [[lem-normalized-satake-transform-is-convolution-homomorphism-for-gln]]

[F5] The transform is injective; $H$ is generated by $h_1,\ldots,h_n,h_n^{-1}$, with $\mathcal S(h_r)=q^{r(n-r)/2}e_r$ and $\mathcal S(h_n^{-1})=e_n^{-1}$. [[lem-minuscule-gln-hecke-transforms-and-triangularity]]

[F6] Every symmetric polynomial is uniquely a polynomial in the elementary symmetric polynomials. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-symmetric-polynomials]]

[F7] Every nonconstant complex polynomial splits into linear factors. [[thm-the-complex-numbers-are-algebraically-closed]]

## Proof

**Given:** The local data and the Axiom of Choice inherited through [F1].

1.1 By [F4] and [F5], the image of $\mathcal S$ is exactly the algebra generated by $e_1,\ldots,e_n,e_n^{-1}$, which is contained in the symmetric Laurent polynomials. Conversely, for any symmetric Laurent polynomial $P$, choose $M\ge0$ large enough that $e_n^M P$ has no negative exponents. It is a symmetric ordinary polynomial, so [F6] writes it as a polynomial in $e_1,\ldots,e_n$; multiplying back by $e_n^{-M}$ shows that $P$ lies in the image. Injectivity is [F5], proving the isomorphism. [A1, F4, F5, F6, algebra]

1.2 Fix an ordered tuple $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)\in(\mathbb C^\times)^n$ and define $\chi_i(\varpi^a u)=\alpha_i^a$ for $a\in\mathbb Z$ and $u\in\mathcal O^\times$. The decomposition $F^\times=\varpi^{\mathbb Z}\mathcal O^\times$ makes these well-defined unramified characters. Restriction to $K$ identifies $I(\chi)$ with locally constant functions on $(B\cap K)\backslash K$: for $b=tu\in B\cap K$, $t\in T\cap K$ and $u\in U\cap K$, so $\delta(b)^{1/2}\chi(b)=1$; every such function extends by the required $B$-equivariance using $G=BK$, and two decompositions differ by an element of $B\cap K$. Define $K_m=\{k\in K:k\equiv I_n\pmod{\varpi^mM_n(\mathcal O)}\}$. These compact open subgroups form a neighborhood basis at $1$. Compactness of $K$ and local constancy of the restricted function $\phi$ give a finite cover by right cosets $k_iK_{m_i}$ on which $\phi$ is constant. Taking $m=\max_i m_i$ gives $K_m\subseteq K_{m_i}$ for every $i$, hence $\phi(kj)=\phi(k)$ for every $k\in K$ and $j\in K_m$. For $g=bk$, $j\in K_m$, we have $s(gj)=\delta(b)^{1/2}\chi(b)\phi(kj)=\delta(b)^{1/2}\chi(b)\phi(k)=s(g)$, so the extension is smooth. The constant function $1$ on $K$ extends to a nonzero right-$K$-fixed section $s_0$, and every $K$-fixed section is determined by its value at $1$ using $G=BK$. Hence $I(\chi)^K=\mathbb C s_0$. [A1, F1, F2, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $V$ be irreducible, smooth, admissible and spherical. By [F3], $V^K$ is a simple $H$-module; admissibility makes it finite-dimensional. Step 1.1 identifies $H$ with a commutative algebra generated by $h_1,\ldots,h_n,h_n^{-1}$. These finitely many commuting operators on $V^K$ have a common eigenvector: take an eigenspace of the first operator, which is nonzero by [F7], restrict the next commuting operator to it, and continue; each restriction has an eigenvector by [F7]. The span of the resulting vector is an $H$-submodule, so simplicity gives $\dim V^K=1$. Transport its Hecke character through the isomorphism of step 1.1, and let $c_r$ be the transported character's value on $e_r$; $c_n\ne0$ because $e_n$ is invertible. By [F7], the polynomial $Z^n-c_1Z^{n-1}+c_2Z^{n-2}-\cdots+(-1)^nc_n$ has roots $\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n$, all nonzero. Their multiset is uniquely determined by repeated polynomial division by its linear factors. Evaluation at this multiset has the same values on $e_1,\ldots,e_n,e_n^{-1}$, hence is the same Hecke character. [A1, F3, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For $h\in H$, evaluate its action on that line at the identity. The function $g\mapsto h(g)s_0(g)$ is right-$K$-invariant and compactly supported, so [F2] gives $(\pi(h)s_0)(1)=\int_G h(g)s_0(g)\,dg=\int_T\int_U h(tu)\delta(t)^{1/2}\chi(t)\,du\,dt=\int_T(\mathcal Sh)(t)\chi(t)\,dt$. The last integral is a finite sum over $T/(T\cap K)$ by [F4]; each coset has $dt$-mass one and $\chi$ is trivial on $T\cap K$, so it is the Laurent polynomial $\mathcal Sh$ evaluated at $(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n)$. Thus this is the Hecke character on $I(\chi)^K$. Let $W$ be the $G$-submodule generated by $s_0$. Then $W^K=\mathbb C s_0$ and this line generates $W$. Let $N$ be the sum of all $G$-submodules of $W$ with zero $K$-fixed space. Every vector of $N$ lies in a finite sum of such submodules. Averaging its summands over $K$ with normalized Haar probability gives a sum of vectors in their zero fixed spaces, so $N^K=0$; in particular, $N\ne W$. In $W/N$, the fixed line survives. Any nonzero submodule has nonzero fixed space: if its preimage in $W$ has zero $K$-fixed space, then it is one of the submodules in the sum defining $N$ and its image is zero. Hence a nonzero submodule has the full fixed line and, since that line generates $W/N$, equals $W/N$. Thus $W/N$ is irreducible with the computed Hecke character, so it is a spherical irreducible subquotient of $I(\chi)$. [A1, F1, F2, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The representation $I(\chi)$ is admissible. For every compact open $J\subseteq G$, restriction to $K$ is injective on $I(\chi)^J$ by $G=BK$, and its image consists of functions on the finite set $K/(J\cap K)$; the index is finite because $J\cap K$ is open in compact $K$. Thus $I(\chi)^J$ is finite-dimensional. Subrepresentations have no larger fixed spaces, and [F3] makes fixed vectors exact, so every subquotient, including $W/N$, is admissible. Conversely, every spherical irreducible subquotient has a nonzero fixed line which is a quotient of a subspace of $I(\chi)^K=\mathbb C s_0$ by exactness, so it has the same character. [A1, F2, F3, step 1.2, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Step 2.2 realizes every unordered nonzero tuple by an admissible irreducible spherical representation, and step 2.1 assigns a unique tuple to every such representation. Two representations with the same tuple have the same simple $H$-module of fixed vectors, so [F3] makes them isomorphic. These constructions are inverse bijections and prove all claims. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎

## Notes

This is the GL(n) spherical classification from the local Hecke algebra and principal series only. No general Bernstein admissibility theorem, parabolic classification, LLC, or ramified representation theorem is used.
