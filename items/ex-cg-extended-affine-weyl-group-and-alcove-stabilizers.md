## Example

Let $\Phi\subseteq E$ be a reduced crystallographic root system spanning the finite-dimensional real inner-product space $(E,B)$, with affine walls and affine reflection group $W_a$ as in [[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]]. Let $A$ be the componentwise fundamental alcove from [[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]], and let $Q^\vee$ and $P^\vee$ be the coroot and coweight lattices from [[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]. Define the **extended affine Weyl group**
$$W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}:=P^\vee\rtimes W,$$
using the natural Weyl action on $P^\vee$.

The affine group is a normal subgroup, and
$$W_a=Q^\vee\rtimes W\trianglelefteq W_a^{\mathrm{ext}},\qquad W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}/W_a\cong P^\vee/Q^\vee,$$
where $P^\vee/Q^\vee$ is finite abelian. The extended group acts transitively on alcoves. If $\Omega:=\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}}(A)$, then the quotient map restricts to an isomorphism $\Omega\cong P^\vee/Q^\vee$; through this isomorphism the quotient acts faithfully on the vertices of $\overline A$. Thus every alcove stabilizer in the extended group is conjugate to $\Omega$, while the affine stabilizer of $A$ is trivial by [[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]] (2).

For a concrete non-trivial case, in $E=\mathbb R^2$ take $\Phi_{A_2}=\{\pm\alpha_1,\pm\alpha_2,\pm\theta\}$ with $\alpha_1=(1,0)$, $\alpha_2=(-1/2,\sqrt3/2)$, and $\theta=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, with positive roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\theta$. Then $\alpha_i^\vee=2\alpha_i$ and $\theta^\vee=2\theta$. The coweight quotient $P^\vee/Q^\vee$ is cyclic of order $3$. The closure of the fundamental alcove has vertices $0$, $v_1=(1,1/\sqrt3)$, and $v_2=(0,2/\sqrt3)$. The element
$$g:=t_{v_1}s_{\alpha_1}s_{\alpha_2}\in W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}$$
cyclically permutes these three vertices and the three facet walls, so $\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}}(A)=\langle g\rangle\cong C_3$. Consequently $W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}$ does not act freely on alcoves and, in this A2 case, is a strict extension of the Coxeter group generated by the affine facet reflections. More generally, whenever $P^\vee/Q^\vee$ is non-trivial, the extended group is strictly larger than the reflection-generated affine Coxeter group. No axiom of choice is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The root-system, affine-wall, coroot, coweight-lattice, and componentwise-fundamental-alcove conventions above.

[F1] $Q^\vee$ is generated by the simple coroots; $P^\vee$ consists of the vectors pairing integrally with every root; both are full-rank lattices, and $Q^\vee\subseteq P^\vee$. The simple-coroot basis and its integral generation of all coroots are proved in [[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]], proof step 1.5 ([[def-root-lattice-coroot-lattice-weight-lattice-and-coweight-lattice]]).

[F2] The external semidirect product has multiplication $(\lambda,w)(\mu,v)=(\lambda+w\mu,wv)$ for the natural action ([[def-external-semidirect-product]]).

[F3] $W$ is generated by the orthogonal root reflections, permutes $\Phi$, and $\alpha^\vee=2\alpha/B(\alpha,\alpha)$ ([[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]], [[def-coroot-and-dual-root-system]]).

[F4] The affine reflections are $r_{\alpha,k}=t_{k\alpha^\vee}s_\alpha$ and $W_a=Q^\vee\rtimes W$; the affine walls and their arrangement are preserved by these reflections ([[def-cg-affine-root-hyperplane-reflection-and-alcove]], [[lem-cg-affine-reflection-identities-and-local-finiteness]]).

[F5] The fundamental alcove is a finite product of bounded geometric simplices; in the irreducible A2 model its region is $B(x,\alpha_1)>0$, $B(x,\alpha_2)>0$, $B(x,\theta)<1$ ([[lem-cg-highest-root-and-fundamental-alcove]]).

[F6] The stabilizer of $A$ in $W_a$ is trivial ([[lem-cg-affine-alcove-separation-and-facet-types]]).

[F7] The fundamental affine facet reflections generate $W_a$, and $W_a$ acts simply transitively on alcoves; in particular the presentation makes $W_a$ the Coxeter group with those simple generators ([[thm-cg-affine-alcove-transitivity-presentation-and-length]]).

[F8] If an integer matrix $C$ has nonzero determinant, then $\mathbb Z^r/C\mathbb Z^r$ is finite of order $|\det C|$ ([[cor-index-of-a-full-rank-integer-sublattice-is-the-absolute-determinant]]).

[F9] A reduced crystallographic root system is finite and spanning, invariant under root reflections, crystallographic, and reduced ([[def-reduced-crystallographic-euclidean-root-system]]).

[F10] The simple roots form a real basis and every root has integral coordinates of one sign in that basis ([[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]]).

## Verification

**Given:** The root-system data, the affine action, and the lattice definitions above.

1.1 Suppose $\Phi\ne\varnothing$, let $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_r\}$ be a simple-root basis, and let $\omega_1^\vee,\ldots,\omega_r^\vee$ be the dual basis for $B(\alpha_i,\omega_j^\vee)=\delta_{ij}$. By [F10], every root is an integer combination of the simple roots, so the condition defining $P^\vee$ is equivalent to $B(\alpha_i,\lambda)\in\mathbb Z$ for every $i$; hence $P^\vee=\bigoplus_i\mathbb Z\omega_i^\vee$. By [F1], the simple coroots form a real basis and generate $Q^\vee$. Their coordinate matrix in the $\omega_i^\vee$ basis has entries $C_{ij}=B(\alpha_i,\alpha_j^\vee)\in\mathbb Z$ and is invertible, since its columns are a real basis. Thus $Q^\vee$ corresponds to $C\mathbb Z^r\subseteq\mathbb Z^r$, and [F8] shows $P^\vee/Q^\vee$ is finite of order $|\det C|$. It is abelian because both lattices are additive groups. [F1, F8, F10, algebra]

1.2 For the displayed $A_2$ set, the roots span $\mathbb R^2$, have squared norm $1$, and occupy the six directions that are multiples of $\pi/3$. The mirror of a root reflection is perpendicular to its root, so if the root direction is $j\pi/3$, reflection sends a root direction $k\pi/3$ to $(2j+3-k)\pi/3$ and preserves the six roots. The possible Cartan numbers are $2B(\beta,\alpha)\in\{2,1,-1,-2\}$, and each root line contains only the two displayed signs. Thus [F9] verifies the reduced crystallographic root-system axioms. Choose $\Phi^+=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\theta\}$; these are exactly the roots positive on $\theta$, since $B(\theta,\alpha_1)=B(\theta,\alpha_2)=1/2$ and $B(\theta,\theta)=1$. Then $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ are simple and $\theta$ is highest. The coroots are $2\alpha_1,2\alpha_2,2\theta$ by [F3]. The vectors $v_1=(1,1/\sqrt3)$ and $v_2=(0,2/\sqrt3)$ satisfy $B(\alpha_i,v_j)=\delta_{ij}$, so they are the dual coweight basis. In this basis the simple coroots have columns $(2,-1)$ and $(-1,2)$, giving the matrix $\begin{pmatrix}2&-1\\-1&2\end{pmatrix}$ of determinant $3$. By [F8], $P^\vee/Q^\vee$ has order $3$. The class of $v_1$ is nonzero because solving this matrix equation for $(1,0)$ gives $(2/3,1/3)$, not an integer vector; also $3v_1=2\alpha_1^\vee+\alpha_2^\vee$. Hence $[v_1]$ generates the quotient. [F1, F3, F8, F9, F10, algebra]

2.1 The Weyl group preserves $P^\vee$, since $B(\alpha,w\lambda)=B(w^{-1}\alpha,\lambda)\in\mathbb Z$ for every root $\alpha$, and it preserves $Q^\vee$ because it permutes coroots. For a root reflection, $s_\alpha(\lambda)-\lambda=-B(\lambda,\alpha^\vee)\alpha=-B(\lambda,\alpha)\alpha^\vee$. If $\lambda\in P^\vee$, the last coefficient is integral, so every root reflection, and hence all of $W$, acts trivially on $P^\vee/Q^\vee$. By [F2], $\pi((\lambda,w)(\mu,v))=\lambda+w\mu+Q^\vee=\lambda+\mu+Q^\vee=\pi(\lambda,w)+\pi(\mu,v)$, so $\pi:W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}\to P^\vee/Q^\vee$, $\pi(\lambda,w)=\lambda+Q^\vee$, is a homomorphism; it is onto and has kernel $Q^\vee\rtimes W=W_a$ by [F4]. Thus $W_a\trianglelefteq W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}$ with the stated quotient. Translations by $P^\vee$ shift each wall level by the integer $B(\lambda,\alpha)$, while $W$ permutes roots, so $W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}$ preserves the wall arrangement and its alcoves. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $\Omega=\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}}(A)$. If $g\in\Omega$ and $\pi(g)=0$, then $g\in W_a$ and [F6] gives $g=1$; hence $\pi|_\Omega$ is injective. For any $\lambda\in P^\vee$, $t_\lambda(A)$ is an alcove by step 2.1. By [F7] choose $h\in W_a$ carrying $t_\lambda(A)$ to $A$. Then $h t_\lambda\in\Omega$, and its quotient class is $\pi(h t_\lambda)=\lambda+Q^\vee$, so $\pi|_\Omega$ is onto. This representative is unique by injectivity. The closure of $A$ is a full-dimensional product of simplices by [F5], so its vertices affinely span $E$. An affine isometry fixing all those vertices is the identity; therefore $\Omega$, and hence the quotient via $\pi|_\Omega$, acts faithfully on the vertex set of $\overline A$. The action of $W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}$ on alcoves is transitive because its subgroup $W_a$ is transitive by [F7]; if $C=q(A)$, then $\operatorname{Stab}_{W_a^{\mathrm{ext}}}(C)=q\Omega q^{-1}$. [F2, F5, F6, F7, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 By [F5], the fundamental $A_2$ alcove is the triangle interior cut out by $B(x,\alpha_1)>0$, $B(x,\alpha_2)>0$, and $B(x,\theta)<1$. The simple-root walls meet at $0$; solving $B(x,\alpha_2)=0$, $B(x,\theta)=1$ gives $v_1$, and solving $B(x,\alpha_1)=0$, $B(x,\theta)=1$ gives $v_2$. From the root-reflection formula in [F3], $s_{\alpha_1}(x_1,x_2)=(-x_1,x_2)$ and $s_{\alpha_2}(x_1,x_2)=(x_1/2+\sqrt3 x_2/2,\sqrt3 x_1/2-x_2/2)$. Thus $w=s_{\alpha_1}s_{\alpha_2}$ sends $v_1$ to $v_2-v_1$ and $v_2$ to $-v_1$. With $\eta=v_1\in P^\vee$, the element $g=t_\eta w$ therefore sends $0\mapsto v_1\mapsto v_2\mapsto0$. It fixes the triangle's centroid and cyclically permutes its vertices and facet walls, so it is a 120-degree rotation. In particular $g\in\Omega$ and $g\ne1$. Since $\Omega\cong P^\vee/Q^\vee\cong C_3$ by steps 3.1 and 1.2, $\Omega=\langle g\rangle$ and has order $3$. Thus the extended action is not free on alcoves, and this $A_2$ extended group is strictly larger than the Coxeter group $W_a$ generated by its affine facet reflections. [F2, F3, F5, F7, step 3.1, step 1.2, algebra]

5.1 If $\Phi=\varnothing$, spanning forces $E=0$, so $P^\vee=Q^\vee=0$ and both affine groups are trivial. In rank one, with $\Phi=\{\pm\alpha\}$, the coweight generator $\omega=\alpha/B(\alpha,\alpha)$ satisfies $P^\vee=\mathbb Z\omega$ and $\alpha^\vee=2\omega$, so the quotient is $\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$; $t_\omega s_\alpha$ swaps the two vertices of the fundamental interval and stabilizes it. For reducible $\Phi$, the simple-root and simple-coroot bases, the lattices, their quotient, the affine groups, and the fundamental alcove split over the orthogonal components, so the preceding proof applies factorwise. The only choice in step 3.1 is the element whose existence follows from transitivity; injectivity makes it unique for each coset, and no axiom of choice is used. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
