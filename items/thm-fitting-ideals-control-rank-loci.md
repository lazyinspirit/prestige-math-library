## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from the Fitting ideal sheaf definition
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-fitting-ideal-sheaf]]). Let $X$ be a scheme and let $\mathcal F$ be a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module
of finite type ([[def-finite-type-finite-presentation-module-sheaf]]). For an
ideal sheaf $\mathcal I\subseteq\mathcal O_X$ put
$$V(\mathcal I)=\{x\in X:\mathcal I_x\subseteq\mathfrak m_x\}=\{x\in X:\mathcal I_x\neq\mathcal O_{X,x}\},\qquad D(\mathcal I)=X\setminus V(\mathcal I).$$
Let $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)\subseteq\mathcal O_X$, $k\ge0$, be the
Fitting ideal sheaves of $\mathcal F$ ([[def-fitting-ideal-sheaf]]), with the
convention $\operatorname{Fitt}_{-1}(\mathcal F)=0$ recorded there, and let
$\mathcal F(x)=\mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x)$ be the fibre at
$x$ ([[def-fibre-of-module-at-point]]).

Then:

1. For every $r\ge0$,
   $$V(\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F))=\{x\in X:\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)>r\},\qquad D(\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F))=\{x\in X:\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)\le r\}.$$
2. For $r=0$ this reads
   $V(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal F))=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)=\{x:\mathcal F_x\neq0\}$;
   in particular $\operatorname{Fitt}_0$ cuts out the support, which is closed
   ([[thm-support-finite-type-qc-closed]]).
3. If $\mathcal F$ is finite locally free of rank $r\ge0$
   ([[def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]]), then
   $\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}(\mathcal F)=0$ and
   $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$.
4. Conversely, if $U\subseteq X$ is open,
   $\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}(\mathcal F)|_U=0$ and
   $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)|_U=\mathcal O_U$, then $\mathcal F|_U$ is
   locally free of rank $r$: every point of $U$ has an open neighbourhood on
   which $\mathcal F$ is free of rank $r$.
5. The equalities in (4) cannot be replaced by pointwise fibre dimension: for
   $A=k[t]/(t^2)$ with $k$ a field and $M=A/(t)$, the sheaf
   $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$ on $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$ has fibre of dimension
   $1$ at the unique point of $X$, while $\mathcal F$ is not locally free of
   rank $1$.

No Noetherian hypothesis on $X$ or on its affine charts is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a scheme $X$, a quasi-coherent $\mathcal O_X$-module $\mathcal F$ of
finite type, and an integer $r\ge0$.

[F1] The Fitting ideal sheaf: on an affine open $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with
$\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ and a presentation
$A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A^n\to M\to0$ with $n$ finite,
$\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U=\widetilde{I_{n-k}(\varphi)}$ where
$I_m(\varphi)$ is the ideal generated by the $m\times m$ minors of a matrix of
$\varphi$, with $I_m(\varphi)=A$ for $m\le0$ and $I_m(\varphi)=0$ when $m$
exceeds the number of rows (or the finitely many available columns);
$I_{n-k}(\varphi)$ depends only on $M$ and $k$ and commutes with localisation;
the ideals are compatible with restriction to open subschemes
([[def-fitting-ideal-sheaf]], [[lem-fitting-ideals-presentation-independent]]).

[F2] Finite type and local freeness are local conditions: $X$ is covered by
affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$, $M$
a finitely generated $A$-module; $\mathcal F$ is locally free of rank $r$ on
$U$ if $U$ is covered by opens on which $\mathcal F$ is isomorphic to
$\mathcal O^r$ ([[def-finite-type-finite-presentation-module-sheaf]],
[[def-locally-free-sheaf-finite-rank]], [[def-quasi-coherent-module-scheme]]).

[F3] The fibre is $\mathcal F(x)=\mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\kappa(x)=\mathcal F_x/\mathfrak m_x\mathcal F_x$
([[def-fibre-of-module-at-point]]).

[F4] For finite type $\mathcal F$: $\mathcal F(x)=0$ if and only if $\mathcal F$
vanishes on an open neighbourhood of $x$
([[lem-sheaf-nakayama-fibre-detects-generation]]).

[F5] The support is $\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)=\{x:\mathcal F_x\neq0\}$
([[def-support-module-sheaf]]), and for finite type $\mathcal F$ it is closed and
on an affine chart $\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)\cap U=V(\operatorname{Ann}_A(M))$
([[thm-support-finite-type-qc-closed]]).

[F6] Localisation is exact: localising the presentation of $M$ at a prime
$\mathfrak p$ gives an exact sequence
$A_{\mathfrak p}^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{\mathfrak p}}A_{\mathfrak p}^n\to M_{\mathfrak p}\to0$,
and $M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)=M_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak p}}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$
([[thm-localisation-of-modules-is-exact]]).

[F7] The tensor product is right exact, so from the presentation one obtains
the exact sequence
$\kappa(\mathfrak p)^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak p)^n\to M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to0$;
the matrix of $\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is the reduction modulo
$\mathfrak p$ of the matrix of $\varphi$
([[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]).

[F8] Rank-nullity over a field, applied to the inclusion of the image of
$\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ into the finite-dimensional codomain
$\kappa(\mathfrak p)^n$, gives
$\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\operatorname{coker}(\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p))=n-\operatorname{rank}(\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p))$,
even when the source has infinitely many basis vectors; the image is a
subspace of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)^n$ and hence finite-dimensional.
([[thm-rank-nullity]])

[F9] Elementary linear algebra over a field $F$: for a linear map
$L:F^{(J)}\to F^n$ one has $\operatorname{rank}(L)<s$ if and only if every
$s\times s$ minor of a matrix of $L$ vanishes; consequently, if an $s\times s$
minor is a unit after base change to a ring, row and column operations by
invertible matrices put the matrix in block form with an identity block. This
is used over the residue fields $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ and over the localisations
$A_\delta$ in step 4.2. [algebra]

[F10] Quotient rings and modules: $k[t]/(t^2)$ is the quotient of the
polynomial ring by the ideal $(t^2)$, and $A/(t)$ is the quotient module
([[def-quotient-ring]], [[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]],
[[def-quotient-module]]).



**Proof technique:** direct; compute the fibre of the cokernel of a presentation matrix by rank-nullity, identify the rank drop with the vanishing of the relevant minors, and handle the converse by inverting a unit minor and block-reducing the matrix.

## Proof

1.1 The fibre of a presented module: let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be an affine open with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$ for a finitely generated $A$-module $M$, and fix a presentation $A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A^n\to M\to0$ with $n$ finite; for $\mathfrak p\in U$ the localisation and the tensor product with $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$ give the exact sequence $\kappa(\mathfrak p)^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak p)^n\to M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)\to0$, and $\mathcal F(\mathfrak p)=M_{\mathfrak p}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak p}}\kappa(\mathfrak p)=M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)$; hence, with $\varphi$ also denoting its reduced matrix, $\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\mathcal F(\mathfrak p)=n-\operatorname{rank}(\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p))$. [F3, F6, F7, F8]

2.1 Minors detect the rank drop: for a given $r\ge0$ and $s=n-r$, the inequality $\operatorname{rank}(\varphi\otimes\kappa(\mathfrak p))<s$ holds if and only if all $s\times s$ minors of the reduced matrix vanish, which happens if and only if every $s\times s$ minor of the matrix of $\varphi$ lies in $\mathfrak p$, that is, $I_{n-r}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathfrak p$; the conventions of [F1] give $I_{n-r}(\varphi)=A$ when $s\le0$, and $\operatorname{rank}<s$ is then impossible, while for $s>n$ one has $I_{n-r}(\varphi)=0$ and $\operatorname{rank}<s$ holds always. [F1, F9, step 1.1]

3.1 The rank locus on a chart: combining steps 1.1 and 2.1, for $\mathfrak p\in U$ one has $\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\mathcal F(\mathfrak p)>r$ if and only if $I_{n-r}(\varphi)\subseteq\mathfrak p$; by [F1] the stalk of $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)$ at $\mathfrak p$ is $I_{n-r}(\varphi)\cdot A_{\mathfrak p}$, and $I\cdot A_{\mathfrak p}\subseteq\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}$ if and only if $I\subseteq\mathfrak p$; hence $\mathfrak p\in V(\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F))$ if and only if $\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\mathcal F(\mathfrak p)>r$. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 Converse, the case $n=r$: let $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ be affine with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\widetilde M$, presentation $A^{(J)}\xrightarrow{\varphi}A^n\to M\to0$, and assume $I_{n-r}(\varphi)=A$, $I_{n-r+1}(\varphi)=0$ for some $r\ge1$. If $A=0$ the conclusion is vacuous, so assume $A\neq0$; then $n\ge r$, since $n-r+1\le0$ would force $A=I_{n-r+1}(\varphi)=0$. If $n=r$, then $I_1(\varphi)=I_{n-r+1}(\varphi)=0$ says that every entry of the matrix of $\varphi$ is zero, so $\varphi=0$ and $M=\operatorname{coker}\varphi\cong A^n=A^r$ is free of rank $r$. [F1, step 2.1]

4.1 Claim 1 globally: the affine charts of step 3.1 cover $X$ by [F2] and the assertion is pointwise in each chart, so $V(\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F))=\{x:\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)>r\}$ and $D(\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F))$ is its complement $\{x:\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)\le r\}$; this proves claim 1. [F2, step 3.1]

4.2 Converse, the case $n>r$: keep the hypotheses of step 3.2 with $s=n-r\ge1$. The $s\times s$ minors of $\varphi$ generate $I_s(\varphi)=A$, so the distinguished opens $D(\delta)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}A$, as $\delta$ runs over the $s\times s$ minors, cover $\operatorname{Spec}A$; fix such a minor $\delta$ and invert it. Over $A_\delta$ the selected minor is a unit, so after permuting the $s$ chosen rows and columns into the top-left corner, row operations by invertible $n\times n$ matrices and column operations by an automorphism of $A_\delta^{(J)}$ put the matrix of $\varphi\otimes A_\delta$ into block form $\begin{pmatrix}I_s&0\\ C&\psi\end{pmatrix}$ with $\psi$ of size $r$ rows; the $(s+1)\times(s+1)$ minors formed from $s$ rows and $s$ columns of the identity block together with one further row and column are, up to sign, exactly the entries of $\psi$, and they vanish because $I_{s+1}(\varphi)\otimes A_\delta=I_{n-r+1}(\varphi)\otimes A_\delta=0$; hence $\psi=0$ and $M_\delta\cong\operatorname{coker}\begin{pmatrix}I_s&0\\ C&0\end{pmatrix}\cong A_\delta^{\,r}$, because the image is the graph $\{(x,Cx)\}$, a direct summand of $A_\delta^n$ of rank $s$. Thus $\mathcal F$ is free of rank $r$ on each member $D(\delta)$ of a cover of $U$, so $\mathcal F|_U$ is locally free of rank $r$. [F1, F9, step 3.2]

5.1 Claim 2, the support: taking $r=0$ in claim 1 gives $V(\operatorname{Fitt}_0(\mathcal F))=\{x:\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)>0\}=\{x:\mathcal F(x)\neq0\}$. For finite type $\mathcal F$ one has $\mathcal F(x)=0$ only if $\mathcal F$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of $x$, hence only if $\mathcal F_x=0$, and conversely $\mathcal F_x=0$ gives $\mathcal F(x)=0$; therefore $\{x:\mathcal F(x)\neq0\}=\{x:\mathcal F_x\neq0\}=\operatorname{Supp}(\mathcal F)$, and this agrees with the closedness of the support in [F5]. [F4, F5, step 4.1]

5.2 Claim 3, the locally free case: suppose $\mathcal F$ is finite locally free of rank $r$, so that by [F2] $X$ is covered by affine opens $U=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $\mathcal F|_U\cong\mathcal O_U^r=\widetilde{A^r}$; for the presentation $A^r=A^r$ with $n=r$ generators and no relations the matrix has $r$ rows and $0$ columns, so by the conventions of [F1] $I_{r-k}(\varphi)=A$ for $k\ge r$ and $I_{r-k}(\varphi)=0$ for $0\le k<r$, whence $\operatorname{Fitt}_k(\mathcal F)|_U=\mathcal O_U$ for $k\ge r$ and $=0$ for $k<r$; in particular $\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}(\mathcal F)|_U=0$ and $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)|_U=\mathcal O_U$ for every member of a cover of $X$, and since membership in an ideal subsheaf and the equality with $\mathcal O_X$ are checked on a cover, $\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}(\mathcal F)=0$ and $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)=\mathcal O_X$. [F1, F2, step 4.1]

5.3 Claim 4 globally: let $U\subseteq X$ be open with $\operatorname{Fitt}_{r-1}(\mathcal F)|_U=0$ and $\operatorname{Fitt}_r(\mathcal F)|_U=\mathcal O_U$. If $r=0$, claim 1 gives $\dim_{\kappa(x)}\mathcal F(x)\le0$, hence $\mathcal F(x)=0$, for every $x\in U$, and [F4] then shows that $\mathcal F$ vanishes on a neighbourhood of every point of $U$, so that $\mathcal F|_U=0$ is locally free of rank $0$. If $r\ge1$, cover $U$ by affine charts $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $\mathcal F|_V\cong\widetilde M$; on each chart the restriction of the hypotheses reads $I_{n-r}(\varphi)=A$ and $I_{n-r+1}(\varphi)=0$ by [F1], so steps 3.2 and 4.2 show that $\mathcal F|_V$ is free of rank $r$ on the members of a cover of $V$; these covers for all charts combine to a cover of $U$ by opens on which $\mathcal F$ is free of rank $r$. This proves claim 4. [F1, F2, F4, step 3.2, step 4.2]

6.1 Claim 5, sharpness: let $k$ be a field, $A=k[t]/(t^2)$ with class $\varepsilon$ of $t$, so that $\varepsilon^2=0$ and $\varepsilon\neq0$, and let $M=A/(\varepsilon)=k$; then $M$ is generated by one element with the single relation $\varepsilon\cdot1=0$, giving a presentation $A\xrightarrow{\ \varepsilon\ }A\to M\to0$, and $\mathcal F=\widetilde M$ is a finite type quasi-coherent module on $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, whose underlying space is the single point $\mathfrak p=(\varepsilon)$. The fibre there is $\mathcal F(\mathfrak p)=M\otimes_A\kappa(\mathfrak p)=M/\varepsilon M=k$, of dimension $1$ over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=k$; the Fitting ideals of this presentation are $\operatorname{Fitt}_0(M)=I_1(\varphi)=(\varepsilon)\neq0$ and $\operatorname{Fitt}_1(M)=I_0(\varphi)=A$. By the contrapositive of claim 3 proved in step 5.2, a locally free module of rank $1$ would have $\operatorname{Fitt}_0=0$, so $\mathcal F$ is not locally free of rank $1$; directly, $\mathcal F\cong\mathcal O_X$ would give $M\cong\Gamma(X,\mathcal O_X)=A$, impossible because $\dim_kM=1$ while $\dim_kA=2$. This proves claim 5 and shows that the two Fitting equalities, not merely the pointwise fibre dimension, are needed for local freeness. [F1, F10, step 5.2]

7.1 Conclusion and choice accounting: claims 1 and 2 are steps 4.1 and 5.1, claim 3 is step 5.2, claim 4 is step 5.3 and claim 5 is step 6.1. All constructions are local and finite: a presentation with finitely many generators is used on one affine chart at a time, the minors of a matrix are indexed by finite subsets of that presentation, and the cover $\{D(\delta)\}$ in step 4.2 is the set of all distinguished opens defined by the minors, so no selection of points, presentations or minors is made; the Axiom of Choice is inherited from the associated-sheaf existence theorem used by the Fitting definition [F1], and no Noetherian hypothesis is used. [F1, step 4.1, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 5.3, step 6.1] ∎
