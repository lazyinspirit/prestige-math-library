## Statement

Let $(S,m)$, $W$, $\ell$ be as in [[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]], and let $R$, $v_s$, $H$ and the generators $T_s$ be as in [[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]].

1. **Well-defined reduced products.** If $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ and $w=s'_1\cdots s'_k$ are reduced expressions of $w\in W$ (so $k=\ell(w)$), then
$$T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}=T_{s'_1}\cdots T_{s'_k}\quad\text{in }H.$$
Hence $T_w:=T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ is a well-defined element of $H$ depending only on $w$; in particular $T_1=1$ (the empty product).

2. **Length-multiplication rules.** For all $s\in S$ and $w\in W$,
$$T_sT_w=\begin{cases}T_{sw},&\ell(sw)=\ell(w)+1,\\[2pt] T_{sw}+(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_w,&\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1,\end{cases}\qquad T_wT_s=\begin{cases}T_{ws},&\ell(ws)=\ell(w)+1,\\[2pt] T_{ws}+(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_w,&\ell(ws)=\ell(w)-1.\end{cases}$$
(Exactly one case occurs, by [[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]], part 1.)

3. **Spanning.** Every product $T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ of generators is a finite $R$-linear combination of the elements $T_w$, and consequently $\{T_w:w\in W\}$ spans $H$ as an $R$-module: every element of $H$ is a finite sum $\sum_wa_wT_w$ with $a_w\in R$.

4. **Scope.** No independence or freeness of $\{T_w\}$ is asserted here; that is [[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the group $W$ with length $\ell$, and the presented algebra $H$ with generators $T_s$ and coefficient ring $R$.

[F1] Any two reduced expressions of the same $w\in W$ are braid-equivalent: one is obtained from the other by finitely many replacements of an alternating subword $s\,t\,s\,t\cdots$ of length $m(s,t)<\infty$ by the alternating word $t\,s\,t\,s\cdots$ of the same length. ([[thm-hh-matsumoto-reduced-word-theorem]])

[F2] $H$ is the quotient of the free associative $R$-algebra $F=R\langle T_s:s\in S\rangle$ by the two-sided ideal generated by the relations $(T_s-v_s)(T_s+v_s^{-1})=0$ and, for $s\ne t$ with $m(s,t)<\infty$, the equality of the two alternating products of $m(s,t)$ factors; in particular those two products are equal in $H$, and $T_s^2=(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s+1$ in $H$. ([[def-hh-universal-coxeter-hecke-parameters-and-presentation]])

[F3] For all $x\in W$ and $s\in S$ one has $\ell(sx)=\ell(x)\pm1$ and $\ell(xs)=\ell(x)\pm1$. ([[thm-hh-coxeter-exchange-deletion-and-faithfulness]])

[F4] $\ell(w)$ is the least length of a word in $S$ representing $w$, so a word of length $\ell(w)$ representing $w$ is a reduced expression, and a multiplicative identity $w=w'\cdot s$ with $\ell(w)=\ell(w')+1$ together with a reduced expression of $w'$ yields a reduced expression of $w$ by concatenation. ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]])

[F5] $F$ is free as an $R$-module on the finite words in the generators, so every element of $F$, and hence every element of the quotient $H$, is a finite $R$-linear combination of images of words. ([[lem-hh-free-associative-ring-and-relations-descent]])

[F6] The induction principle holds: a property of natural numbers holding at $0$ and stable under successors holds for all natural numbers. ([[thm-induction-principle]], [[def-natural-numbers]])

## Proof

**Given:** A finite Coxeter matrix $(S,m)$, the group $W$ with length $\ell$, and the presented algebra $H$ over $R$.

1.1 If $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ and $w=s'_1\cdots s'_k$ are reduced expressions, then by [F1] they are connected by finitely many braid moves. A braid move replaces a consecutive block $s,t,s,\dots$ of $m(s,t)$ alternating letters by $t,s,t,\dots$, and the corresponding block $T_sT_tT_s\cdots$ of the product is replaced by $T_tT_sT_t\cdots$, which equals it in $H$ by the braid relation ([F2]); letters outside the block are untouched. Reading the moves one at a time, the two products are equal, so $T_w:=T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ is a well-defined element of $H$; for $w=1$ the empty product equals $1$. [F1, F2]

2.1 Suppose $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)+1$ and let $w=s_1\cdots s_k$ be a reduced expression, so $k=\ell(w)$. Then $ss_1\cdots s_k$ is a word of length $\ell(w)+1=\ell(sw)$ representing $sw$, hence a reduced expression of $sw$ ([F4]), and the definition of $T_{sw}$ from 1.1 gives $T_{sw}=T_sT_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}=T_sT_w$. Similarly, if $\ell(ws)=\ell(w)+1$, then appending $s$ to a reduced expression of $w$ gives a reduced expression of $ws$, so $T_{ws}=T_wT_s$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Suppose $\ell(sw)=\ell(w)-1$ and put $w':=sw$, so that $sw'=w$ and $\ell(sw')=\ell(w)=\ell(w')+1$ ([F3]). By 2.1, $T_w=T_{sw'}=T_sT_{w'}$. Multiplying the quadratic relation $T_s^2=(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_s+1$ of [F2] on the right by $T_{w'}$ gives $T_s^2T_{w'}=(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_sT_{w'}+T_{w'}$, that is $T_sT_w=T_{sw}+(v_s-v_s^{-1})T_w$. The right-handed rule follows by multiplying the same relation on the left by $T_{w'}$ with $w'=ws$. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 By [F5] every element of $H$ is a finite $R$-linear combination of images of words in the $T_s$, so it suffices to show that every word product $T_{s_1}\cdots T_{s_k}$ is a finite $R$-linear combination of the $T_w$. Argue by induction on $k$ ([F6]): for $k=0$ the empty product is $T_1$ by 1.1, and for $k\ge1$ the induction hypothesis writes $T_{s_2}\cdots T_{s_k}$ as a finite $R$-linear combination of the $T_w$, after which left multiplication by $T_{s_1}$ distributes and step 2.1 or step 3.1 expresses each $T_{s_1}T_w$ as an $R$-linear combination of $T_{s_1w}$ and $T_w$ (with $s_1w$ a group element, so $T_{s_1w}$ is among the $T$'s). This gives the spanning claim. [F2, F5, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Combining the steps: 1.1 gives the well-definedness of $T_w$ including $T_1=1$, steps 2.1 and 3.1 give the two multiplication rules in both hands, and step 4.1 gives spanning. Nothing here asserts independence or freeness of $\{T_w\}$; that is the content of [[thm-hh-generic-coxeter-hecke-standard-basis]]. No choice is used and $W$ need not be finite: reduced expressions are supplied by the minimum in the definition of $\ell$ ([F4]) and the induction of step 4.1 runs over finite words. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1] ∎
