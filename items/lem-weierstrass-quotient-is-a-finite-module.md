## Statement

Let $W$ be a Weierstrass polynomial of degree $d$ in $z_m$. Then the quotient
$\mathcal O_{m,0}/(W)$ is a finitely generated $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$-module,
where $\mathcal O_{0,0}=\mathbb C$ when $m=1$, generated by the residue classes
of

$$1,\ z_m,\ \dots,\ z_m^{d-1}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A degree-$d$ Weierstrass polynomial $W$.

[L1] A Weierstrass polynomial is the monic degree-$d$ polynomial in the last variable from [[def-weierstrass-polynomial]].

[L2] Weierstrass division gives unique quotient and remainder of degree $<d$ upon division by $W$ ([[thm-weierstrass-division-theorem]]).

[L3] Noetherian-module language is that of [[def-noetherian-ring-and-module]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1] and [L2], every germ $f\in\mathcal O_{m,0}$ can be written uniquely as $$f=qW+r_0+r_1z_m+\cdots+r_{d-1}z_m^{d-1}$$ with $r_j\in\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$. Modulo $(W)$ this becomes $$[f]=r_0[1]+r_1[z_m]+\cdots+r_{d-1}[z_m^{d-1}],$$ so the listed residue classes generate the quotient as an $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$-module. [L1, L2, L3]

2.1 The same division theorem [L2] makes the remainder unique, so those generators give a canonical normal form for every class in the quotient. Since there are only $d$ generators, the quotient is a finite $\mathcal O_{m-1,0}$-module. [step 1.1, L2] ∎
