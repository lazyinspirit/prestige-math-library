## Statement

Let $n\in\mathbb N$ and let $Q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$ be the base configuration of
[[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]], with $h=\frac{1}{4(n+1)}$. Let
$i,j$ be indices with $1\le i,j\le n-1$ and $|i-j|>1$; then the two pairs
$\{i,i+1\}$ and $\{j,j+1\}$ are disjoint, and such indices exist only for
$n\ge4$, so for $n\le3$ the assertions below are vacuous. Write $m_i=q_i+(h,0)$
and let $\rho$ be the diamond path, so that the half twists $\sigma_i$,
$\sigma_j$ and their supports $U_i$, $U_j$ are as in
[[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]], with $U_i\cap U_j=\varnothing$.

**(a) The simultaneous braid.** Define $\Sigma_{ij}$, the **simultaneous**
execution of the two half twists, by

$$(\Sigma_{ij})_i:=m_i+\rho,\quad (\Sigma_{ij})_{i+1}:=m_i-\rho,\quad (\Sigma_{ij})_j:=m_j+\rho,\quad (\Sigma_{ij})_{j+1}:=m_j-\rho,$$

and $(\Sigma_{ij})_k(t):=q_k$ for the remaining labels $k$. Then $\Sigma_{ij}$
is a braid based at $Q$, and both stackings of the two half twists are
braid-isotopic to it. Here $\sim$ denotes braid isotopy
([[def-braid-isotopy-relative-top-and-bottom]]) and $\star$ the stacking of
[[prop-stacking-of-geometric-braids-is-well-defined]]:

$$\sigma_i\star\sigma_j\ \sim\ \Sigma_{ij}\ \sim\ \sigma_j\star\sigma_i .$$

**(b) Far commutativity.** Consequently
$[\sigma_i][\sigma_j]=[\sigma_j][\sigma_i]$ in the group $G_n$ of
[[thm-geometric-braids-form-a-group]]: far-away half twists commute.

The isotopy is explicit and no choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n$, the base configuration $Q=(q_1,\dots,q_n)$, indices $i,j$ with $1\le i,j\le n-1$ and $|i-j|>1$, and the half twists $\sigma_i,\sigma_j$ based at $Q$.

[F1] A braid based at $Q$ is a tuple $(u_1,\dots,u_n)$ of continuous maps $u_j\colon I\to D^\circ$ with $u_i(t)\ne u_j(t)$ for $i\ne j$, $u_j(0)=q_j$, and $\{u_1(1),\dots,u_n(1)\}=\{q_1,\dots,q_n\}$; the endpoint permutation $\pi(u)$ is the unique permutation with $u_j(1)=q_{\pi(u)(j)}$ ([[def-geometric-braid-with-setwise-endpoints]]).

[F2] The half twist at $i$ is $(\sigma_i)_i=m_i+\rho$, $(\sigma_i)_{i+1}=m_i-\rho$ and $(\sigma_i)_k=q_k$ for $k\notin\{i,i+1\}$, where $m_i=q_i+(h,0)$ and $\rho(0)=(-h,0)$, $\rho(\tfrac12)=(0,-h)$, $\rho(1)=(h,0)$ with $\lVert\rho(t)\rVert_2\le h$ and $\rho(t)\ne0$ for every $t$; $\sigma_i$ is a braid based at $Q$ with endpoint permutation the transposition of $i$ and $i+1$; its support disc $U_i$ contains $q_i,q_{i+1}$ and no other base point, satisfies $U_i\subseteq D^\circ$, and $U_i\cap U_j=\varnothing$ whenever $|i-j|>1$ ([[def-elementary-geometric-half-twist]]).

[F3] Stacking is $(\gamma\star\beta)_j(t)=z_j(2t)$ for $t\le\tfrac12$ and $(\gamma\star\beta)_j(t)=w_{\pi(\beta)(j)}(2t-1)$ for $t\ge\tfrac12$, with $\pi(\gamma\star\beta)=\pi(\gamma)\circ\pi(\beta)$; it descends to isotopy classes and is associative, and braid isotopy is the equivalence relation generated by the jointly continuous families of [[def-braid-isotopy-relative-top-and-bottom]] ([[prop-stacking-of-geometric-braids-is-well-defined]]).

[F4] A braid isotopy from $\beta$ to $\beta'$ is a tuple $Z=(Z_1,\dots,Z_n)$ of jointly continuous maps $Z_j\colon I\times I\to D^\circ$ such that every slice $Z(s,\cdot)$ is a braid based at $Q$ and $Z_j(0,t)=z_j(t)$, $Z_j(1,t)=z'_j(t)$ for all $j,t$ ([[def-braid-isotopy-relative-top-and-bottom]]).

[F5] $G_n$ is a group whose operation is induced by stacking, so $[\gamma][\beta]=[\gamma\star\beta]$, and $[\beta]=[\beta']$ whenever $\beta\sim\beta'$ ([[thm-geometric-braids-form-a-group]], [[prop-stacking-of-geometric-braids-is-well-defined]]).

[L6] Composites of continuous maps are continuous and continuity on the two closed halves of a square pastes, the interval $I=[0,1]$ and its two closed halves carrying the subspace topology ([[lem-continuity-is-local-and-pastes]], [[def-continuous-map-top]], [[def-interval]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **(a), $\Sigma_{ij}$ is a braid.** Each motion of $\Sigma_{ij}$ is either the constant $q_k$ or one of $m_i\pm\rho$, $m_j\pm\rho$, hence continuous with values in $U_i$ respectively $U_j$, and $U_i\cup U_j\subseteq D^\circ$ by [F2]; the only nonconstant pairs are $\{i,i+1\}$, which are antipodal about $m_i$ and therefore distinct because $\rho(t)\neq0$ for every $t$ by [F2], and $\{j,j+1\}$, likewise antipodal about $m_j$; the two supports are disjoint and the remaining motions are the constant $q_k$ with $q_k\notin U_i\cup U_j$, so all $n$ motions are pairwise distinct at every height; the bottom values are $m_i+\rho(0)=m_i+(-h,0)=q_i$, $m_i-\rho(0)=q_{i+1}$ and likewise for $j$, the remaining values being the base points themselves; the top values are $m_i+\rho(1)=q_{i+1}$, $m_i-\rho(1)=q_i$ and likewise for $j$, so the top values run through $\{q_1,\dots,q_n\}$ and the endpoint permutation of $\Sigma_{ij}$ is the product of the transpositions of $i,i+1$ and of $j,j+1$. [F1, F2]

1.2 **The interpolation of the two time windows.** For $s\in I$ put $\alpha_s(t):=0$ for $t\le\frac{1-s}{2}$ and $\alpha_s(t):=\frac{t-(1-s)/2}{(1+s)/2}$ for $t\ge\frac{1-s}{2}$, and $\beta_s(t):=\frac{t}{(1+s)/2}$ for $t\le\frac{1+s}{2}$, $\beta_s(t):=1$ for $t\ge\frac{1+s}{2}$; the two branches of each definition agree at the switch point, so $\alpha_s,\beta_s$ are continuous, nondecreasing and map $I$ onto $I$ with $\alpha_s(0)=\beta_s(0)=0$ and $\alpha_s(1)=\beta_s(1)=1$, the map $(s,t)\mapsto(\alpha_s(t),\beta_s(t))$ is jointly continuous because the switch points depend continuously on $s$ and the two branches agree there, and $\alpha_0(t)=\max(0,2t-1)$, $\beta_0(t)=\min(2t,1)$, $\alpha_1=\beta_1=\operatorname{id}_I$. [F2, L6]

1.3 **Pasting two isotopies.** If $Z$ is a braid isotopy from $\beta$ to $\beta'$ and $W$ one from $\beta'$ to $\beta''$, then $U_j(s,t):=Z_j(2s,t)$ for $s\le\tfrac12$ and $U_j(s,t):=W_j(2s-1,t)$ for $s\ge\tfrac12$ is jointly continuous by [L6] because the branches agree at $s=\tfrac12$ and the two closed halves of the square cover it, every slice of $U$ is a slice of $Z$ or of $W$ and hence a braid based at $Q$ by [F4], and its boundary slices are $\beta$ and $\beta''$; so braid isotopy is transitive. [F4, L6]

2.1 **The isotopy.** Define $Z_k(s,t):=m_i+\rho(\alpha_s(t))$ for $k=i$, $Z_k(s,t):=m_i-\rho(\alpha_s(t))$ for $k=i+1$, $Z_k(s,t):=m_j+\rho(\beta_s(t))$ for $k=j$, $Z_k(s,t):=m_j-\rho(\beta_s(t))$ for $k=j+1$, and $Z_k(s,t):=q_k$ otherwise. Every $Z_k$ is a composite of jointly continuous maps by step 1.2 and [F2], hence jointly continuous; for fixed $s$ the tuple $Z(s,\cdot)$ is collision-free and takes the base values at $t=0$ and the setwise base values at $t=1$ by the same computations as in step 1.1, with $\alpha_s,\beta_s$ in place of the identity: at $t=0$ one has $\alpha_s(0)=\beta_s(0)=0$ so the four moving labels sit at $q_i,q_{i+1},q_j,q_{j+1}$, while at $t=1$ one has $\alpha_s(1)=\beta_s(1)=1$ so they sit at the same four points with the two neighbouring labels interchanged. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 **The two ends of the isotopy.** At $s=0$ the formulas of step 1.2 give $Z_k(0,t)=m_i+\rho(\max(0,2t-1))$ for $k=i$ and $Z_k(0,t)=m_i-\rho(\max(0,2t-1))$ for $k=i+1$, which is the motion of $\sigma_i$ run during the second half of the height interval and held at $q_i$ respectively $q_{i+1}$ during the first half, while $Z_k(0,t)=m_j\pm\rho(\min(2t,1))$ for the labels $k=j,j+1$ runs $\sigma_j$ during the first half and holds it at the swapped base points during the second half, and all other labels are constant; comparing with the stacking formula $(\gamma\star\beta)_k(t)=z_k(2t)$ for $t\le\tfrac12$, $=w_{\pi(\beta)(k)}(2t-1)$ for $t\ge\tfrac12$ of [F3], with $\beta=\sigma_j$, $\gamma=\sigma_i$ and $\pi(\sigma_j)$ the transposition of $j,j+1$, shows $Z(0,\cdot)=\sigma_i\star\sigma_j$. At $s=1$ one has $\alpha_1=\beta_1=\operatorname{id}_I$ by step 1.2 and step 2.1, so $Z(1,\cdot)=\Sigma_{ij}$ by the definitions of step 1.1. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1]

4.1 **(a), first stacking.** Step 2.1 exhibits $Z$ as a tuple of jointly continuous maps whose every slice is a braid based at $Q$, and step 3.1 identifies its boundary slices as $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j$ and $\Sigma_{ij}$; hence $Z$ is a braid isotopy from $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j$ to $\Sigma_{ij}$ in the sense of [F4], that is $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j\sim\Sigma_{ij}$. [F4, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 **(a), second stacking.** Interchanging the roles of the two pairs, that is replacing $(\alpha_s,i,U_i)$ by $(\beta_s,j,U_j)$ and conversely throughout steps 1.2, 2.1 and 3.1, yields in the same way a braid isotopy whose first boundary slice is $\sigma_j\star\sigma_i$, the pair $\{i,i+1\}$ now executing its half twist during the second half of the height interval and the pair $\{j,j+1\}$ during the first, and whose second boundary slice is again $\Sigma_{ij}$; hence $\sigma_j\star\sigma_i\sim\Sigma_{ij}$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Steps 4.1 and 4.2 give $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j\sim\Sigma_{ij}\sim\sigma_j\star\sigma_i$, so transitivity of braid isotopy in step 1.3 yields $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j\sim\sigma_j\star\sigma_i$; passing to isotopy classes with [F5] gives $[\sigma_i][\sigma_j]=[\sigma_i\star\sigma_j]=[\sigma_j\star\sigma_i]=[\sigma_j][\sigma_i]$, which is (b), while (a) is steps 1.1, 4.1 and 4.2. ∎ [F5, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 1.3, step 1.1]

## Remarks

- The only geometric input is that the two supports are disjoint: the pairs of moving labels are distinct, so the two half turns never see each other, and the two time windows can be slid past one another.
- The isotopy of step 2.1 is *not* a reparametrisation of the height in the sense of items 1 to 4: the two pairs are reparametrised by different functions $\alpha_s$ and $\beta_s$, and this is legitimate because each pair is unaffected by the other.
- Assertion (b) is a statement in the group $G_n$ of isotopy classes, not an equality of the braids $\sigma_i\star\sigma_j$ and $\sigma_j\star\sigma_i$ themselves; the two stackings are distinct parametrised tuples whenever $n\ge4$, since their height windows differ.
