## Statement

Let $m\ge1$ and let $A_m$ be the Khovanov–Seidel type A algebra of
[[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]]. As a graded abelian group $A_m$
is free of rank $4m+1$, with basis the images of the $4m+1$ paths
$$(0),\dots,(m);\qquad (0|1),\dots,(m-1|m);\qquad (1|0),\dots,(m|m-1);\qquad (1|0|1),\dots,(m|m-1|m),$$
that is: the $m+1$ vertices, the $2m$ arrows, and one degree-one length-two
**return** at each vertex $i=1,\dots,m$. Every path of length at least three has
class $0$ in $A_m$, the monotone length-two path $(i-1|i|i+1)$ and its reverse
$(i+1|i|i-1)$ have class $0$ for $0<i<m$, the return $(0|1|0)$ has class $0$,
and at an interior vertex $0<i<m$ the two returns $(i|i+1|i)$ and $(i|i-1|i)$
have the same class. In particular $A_m$ is a finitely generated free abelian
group, so its underlying $\mathbb Z$-module is free of finite rank $4m+1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, the doubled line quiver $\Gamma_m$, its path ring $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$, the ideal $I_m$ generated by the four families of relations, and the algebra $A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$.

[F1] $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ is the free abelian group on the directed paths of $\Gamma_m$; a product of composable paths is their left-to-right concatenation and a product of non-composable paths is $0$; the unit is $\sum_{i=0}^m(i)$, and $(u)p=p$ for paths $p$ with source $u$, respectively $p(v)=p$ for paths with target $v$, these products being $0$ otherwise ([[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]]).

[F2] $A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$, where $I_m$ is the two-sided ideal generated by $(i-1|i|i+1)$ and $(i+1|i|i-1)$ for $0<i<m$, by the differences $(i|i+1|i)-(i|i-1|i)$ for $0<i<m$, and by $(0|1|0)$; the internal degree is additive over concatenation, with $\deg(i)=\deg(i|i+1)=0$ and $\deg(i+1|i)=1$ ([[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]]).

[L3] Every element of a free abelian group on a set $X$ is a finite $\mathbb Z$-linear combination of basis elements, and a $\mathbb Z$-linear map out of it may be specified by arbitrary values on the basis ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[L4] In the quotient $A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$ two elements have equal classes exactly when their difference lies in $I_m$; every generator of $I_m$ has class $0$; and since $I_m$ is a two-sided ideal, multiplying any element of $I_m$ on either side by any element of $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ again gives an element of $I_m$ ([[def-quotient-ring]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *The relations in path notation.* Write $u_j:=(j|j+1)$ and $d_j:=(j+1|j)$ for $0\le j\le m-1$. The generators of $I_m$ read, in this notation,
$$u_ju_{j+1}\quad(0\le j\le m-2),\qquad d_{j+1}d_j\quad(0\le j\le m-2),\qquad u_id_i-d_{i-1}u_{i-1}\quad(0<i<m),\qquad u_0d_0 .$$
Indeed $(j|j+1|j+2)=u_ju_{j+1}$, $(j+2|j+1|j)=d_{j+1}d_j$,
$(i|i+1|i)=u_id_i$ and $(i|i-1|i)=d_{i-1}u_{i-1}$. Each of these elements has class $0$ in $A_m$ by [L4]; in particular $u_id_i$ and $d_{i-1}u_{i-1}$ have the same class for $0<i<m$, and $u_0d_0$ has class $0$. [F1, F2, L4]

1.2 *A separating functional on the path ring.* Let $F$ be the free abelian group with basis
$$e_0,\dots,e_m,\qquad u_0,\dots,u_{m-1},\qquad d_0,\dots,d_{m-1},\qquad r_1,\dots,r_m,$$
a total of $4m+1$ basis elements. By [L3] there is a unique $\mathbb Z$-linear map $\rho:\mathbb Z\Gamma_m\to F$ with
$$\rho((i))=e_i,\qquad \rho((i|i+1))=u_i,\qquad \rho((i+1|i))=d_i,$$
$$\rho((i|i-1|i))=r_i\quad(1\le i\le m),\qquad \rho((i|i+1|i))=r_i\quad(1\le i<m),\qquad \rho(p)=0\text{ for every other path }p .$$
Here "other" covers $(0|1|0)$, the monotone length-two paths not of return form, and all paths of length at least three; the two returns at an interior vertex are both sent to $r_i$, and $(m|m-1|m)$, the only return at $m$, is sent to $r_m$. [F1, L3]

2.1 *Length-two paths in $A_m$.* A length-two path $(i_1|i_2|i_3)$ has consecutive differences $\pm1$ and is of one of four forms. If both steps go up it is $(j|j+1|j+2)=u_ju_{j+1}$ with $0\le j\le m-2$, and if both go down it is $(j+2|j+1|j)=d_{j+1}d_j$ with $0\le j\le m-2$; both have class $0$ in $A_m$ by step 1.1. If the steps are up-then-down it is the return $(j|j+1|j)=u_jd_j$ at $j$, for $0\le j\le m-1$: its class is $0$ when $j=0$ by step 1.1 and otherwise equals the class of $d_{j-1}u_{j-1}=(j|j-1|j)$. If the steps are down-then-up it is the return $(j+1|j|j+1)=d_ju_j$ at $j+1$, for $0\le j\le m-1$, whose class equals that of $u_{j+1}d_{j+1}$ when $j+1<m$ by step 1.1 and is the class of the unique return at $m$ when $j+1=m$. Consequently the classes of length-two paths are $0$ or one of $r_1,\dots,r_m$, and each of the two returns at an interior vertex represents the same class. [step 1.1, F1]

2.2 *No path of length three survives.* Let $w=(v_0|v_1|v_2|v_3)$ be a path of length three and put $s_j:=v_j-v_{j-1}\in\{\pm1\}$ for $j=1,2,3$, so that $w$ is the product of its three arrows by [F1]. If $s_1=s_2$, then the subpath $(v_0|v_1|v_2)$ is monotone with interior vertex $v_1$ satisfying $0<v_1<m$, hence is one of the generators of $I_m$ listed in step 1.1 and
$$w=(v_0|v_1|v_2)\,(v_2|v_3)=0$$
in $A_m$ by [L4]. If $s_2=s_3$, the same argument applies to $(v_1|v_2|v_3)$ and
$w=(v_0|v_1)\,(v_1|v_2|v_3)=0$. Otherwise $s_1\ne s_2$ and $s_2\ne s_3$, so
$v_0=v_2$ and $v_1=v_3$: the path oscillates on the edge $\{j,j+1\}$ with
$j=\min(v_0,v_1)$, and there are two cases. If $v_0=j$, then
$w=u_jd_ju_j=(u_jd_j)u_j$; for $j=0$ the factor $u_0d_0$ is the zero return at
$0$ by step 1.1, while for $1\le j\le m-1$ the relation of step 1.1 rewrites
$u_jd_j$ as $d_{j-1}u_{j-1}$ and
$$w=d_{j-1}u_{j-1}u_j=d_{j-1}\bigl(u_{j-1}u_j\bigr)=0,$$
the inner product being the generator $(j-1|j|j+1)$ of $I_m$ with $0<j<m$. If
$v_0=j+1$, then $w=d_ju_jd_j$ and $j+1\ge1$. If $m=1$ then $j=0$ and
$w=d_0\bigl(u_0d_0\bigr)=0$, the factor $u_0d_0$ being the zero return at $0$ by
step 1.1. If $m\ge2$, the two possibilities are exhaustive: for $j+1<m$ the
relation at the interior vertex $j+1$ writes the middle return as
$d_ju_j=u_{j+1}d_{j+1}$, so $w=u_{j+1}\bigl(d_{j+1}d_j\bigr)=0$, the inner
product being the generator $(j+2|j+1|j)$ with $0<j+1<m$; and for $j+1=m$ the
relation at $m-1$, an interior vertex because $m\ge2$, writes
$u_{m-1}d_{m-1}=d_{m-2}u_{m-2}$, so
$w=d_{m-1}\bigl(d_{m-2}u_{m-2}\bigr)=\bigl(d_{m-1}d_{m-2}\bigr)u_{m-2}=0$,
the inner product now being the generator $(m|m-1|m-2)$ with $0<m-1<m$. All
cases are exhausted, so $w$ has class $0$ in $A_m$. [step 1.1, F1, L4]

2.3 *$\rho$ vanishes on the relation ideal.* The two-sided ideal $I_m$ consists of finite sums $a s b$, where $s$ is a relation generator from step 1.1 and $a,b\in\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$: such sums form an ideal containing the generators and are contained in every such ideal. By bilinearity it suffices to consider basis paths $a,b$. Every path term in $a s b$, if nonzero, has length $|a|+2+|b|$. If this length is at least three, each term has $\rho$-image zero by step 1.2. Otherwise $a,b$ are vertex paths. For a monotone generator, $a s b$ is either zero or the same monotone path, which $\rho$ sends to zero. The same holds for $s=u_0d_0$. For $s=u_id_i-d_{i-1}u_{i-1}$, both summands start and end at $i$: multiplication by vertex paths either kills both summands or retains both, and in the latter case their images are $r_i-r_i=0$. Thus $\rho(a s b)=0$ for every relation generator, and linearity gives $\rho(I_m)=0$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, F2]

3.1 *All paths of length at least three have class $0$.* We prove by induction on $l\ge3$ that every path of length $l$ has class $0$ in $A_m$. The case $l=3$ is step 2.2. For $l\ge4$, write $w=a\,w'$ where $a$ is the first arrow of $w$ and $w'$ is the suffix of length $l-1$; by the induction hypothesis $w'$ has class $0$, hence $w=a\,w'$ has class $0$ by [L4], the product of the class of $a$ with the class of $w'$ being the class of their concatenation by the ring structure of the quotient. [step 2.2, F1, L4]

3.2 *The functional descends.* By step 2.3 the $\mathbb Z$-linear map $\rho$ vanishes on the additive subgroup $I_m\subseteq\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$. The quotient map $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m\to A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$ is a surjective homomorphism of abelian groups with kernel $I_m$ by [L4], so $\rho$ factors through it: there is a $\mathbb Z$-linear map $\bar\rho:A_m\to F$ with $\bar\rho(x+I_m)=\rho(x)$ for every $x\in\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$. It is surjective, because each basis element $e_i$, $u_i$, $d_i$, $r_i$ of $F$ is the image of the class of the corresponding displayed path by step 1.2. [step 1.2, step 2.3, L3, L4]

4.1 *The class map $\pi$ is surjective.* By step 3.1 and step 2.1, the images in $A_m$ of the $4m+1$ displayed paths span $A_m$ as an abelian group: every path of length at least three is $0$, every length-two path is $0$ or one of the returns, and length-zero and length-one paths are the vertices and arrows. Let $\pi:\mathbb Z^{4m+1}\to A_m$ be the $\mathbb Z$-linear map sending the standard basis elements to these $4m+1$ classes in the displayed order; it is surjective. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L3]

5.1 *The composite of $\pi$ and $\bar\rho$.* Let $F=\mathbb Z^{4m+1}$ be identified with the free group of step 1.2 in the displayed basis. For each displayed path $p$ one has $\rho(p)$ equal to the corresponding basis element of $F$; hence, for the standard basis element $\epsilon_p$ attached to $p$, the composite $\bar\rho\circ\pi$ satisfies $\bar\rho\pi(\epsilon_p)=\bar\rho(\text{class of }p)=\rho(p)=\epsilon_p$. Since both sides are $\mathbb Z$-linear and agree on a basis, $\bar\rho\circ\pi=\mathrm{id}_F$ by [L3]. [step 1.2, step 3.2, step 4.1, L3]

6.1 *Conclusion: a basis.* From $\bar\rho\circ\pi=\mathrm{id}_F$ of step 5.1, $\pi$ is injective: if $\pi(x)=0$ then $x=\bar\rho\pi(x)=0$. Since $\pi$ is surjective by step 4.1 it is an isomorphism, so $A_m\cong\mathbb Z^{4m+1}$ and the images under $\pi$ of the standard basis, namely the classes of the $4m+1$ displayed paths, form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $A_m$. In particular those classes are $\mathbb Z$-linearly independent, the rank is $4m+1$, and the vanishing and identification statements for paths of length at least two are exactly those of steps 2.1 and 3.1. The internal degrees of the basis elements are $0$ for the vertices and ascending arrows and $1$ for the descending arrows and the returns, by the degree convention of [F2], so the basis is graded as displayed. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, F2] ∎
