## Statement

Let $F$ be a $C^1$ codimension-one foliation of a smooth Hausdorff manifold
$M$ ([[def-smooth-manifold]], [[def-hausdorff-space]]) and let $L$ be a leaf of
$F$ that is compact in its intrinsic leaf-manifold topology
([[def-compact-space]]). Then the inclusion $L\hookrightarrow M$ is a $C^1$
embedding, so $L$ is a compact embedded $C^1$ hypersurface of $M$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A $C^1$ codimension-one foliation $F$ of a smooth Hausdorff manifold $M$ and a compact leaf $L$.

[F1] A $C^1$ foliation atlas has charts $(x_\alpha,t_\alpha)$ with $C^1$ inverse in which plaques are the connected components of the level sets $t_\alpha=c$, and leaves are generated by intersecting plaques; each leaf carries the structure of a one-dimensional-transverse $C^1$ manifold of dimension $n-1$, with the inclusions of plaques as charts ([[def-c1-regular-codimension-one-foliation-and-transverse-orientation]], [[lem-c1-foliated-atlas-preserves-plaque-equivalence-and-transverse-orientation]]).

[F2] A space is compact when every open cover has a finite subcover ([[def-compact-space]]).

[F4] A subspace is compact if and only if every cover by ambient open sets has a finite subcover; the indexed form also holds without a choice axiom ([[lem-compactness-of-a-subspace-is-ambient]]).

[F3] The ambient manifold is Hausdorff ([[def-hausdorff-space]]).

[F6] A subset $S$ of an $m$-manifold is an embedded $k$-submanifold when near each of its points there is a smooth chart carrying $S$ onto $\mathbb R^k\times\{0\}$ ([[def-embedded-submanifold-and-slice-chart]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Compact-to-Hausdorff without metrization.) The inclusion $j:L\to M$ is continuous and injective. For any closed subset $C$ of compact $L$, $C$ is compact: add $L\setminus C$ to an open cover of $C$, take a finite subcover of $L$, then discard the added set. Pulling back any ambient open cover of $j(C)$ gives a finite subcover by compactness of $C$; F4 then makes $j(C)$ compact in its subspace topology. A compact subset $K$ of Hausdorff $M$ is closed: for $z\notin K$, consider all pairs $(U,V)$ of ambient open sets with $z\in V$ and $U\cap V=\varnothing$. Hausdorffness makes their first entries cover $K$; the indexed form of F4 gives finitely many such pairs covering $K$. Intersect their second entries, which are neighborhoods of $z$. This intersection misses $K$. Thus $j$ takes closed subsets of $L$ to closed subsets of $j(L)$ and has continuous inverse onto its image. No metric or full-AC theorem is invoked. [F2, F3, F4, given]

1.2 (Immersion in plaque coordinates.) By F1 and its atlas certificate, the compact intrinsic leaf has a finite $C^1$ plaque atlas. In a foliation chart its plaque inclusion is $y\mapsto\varphi^{-1}(y,c)$. Differentiating the identity $\varphi\circ\varphi^{-1}=\mathrm{id}$ shows that $D\varphi^{-1}$ is invertible, so this inclusion has rank $n-1$. Thus $j$ is an injective $C^1$ immersion, including the zero-dimensional case $n=1$. [F1]

2.1 (Exclude other branches.) Fix $p\in L$ and an intrinsic plaque-chart neighborhood $V_L$ of $p$. Step 1.1 gives an ambient open neighborhood $O$ with $p\in O$ and $O\cap L\subseteq V_L$. Shrink the foliation chart to a product box inside $O$. Its intersection with $L$ is the single slice through $p$, with no other branch of $L$ entering the box. The foliation chart itself is a $C^1$ slice chart, and step 1.2 supplies the immersion. Hence $j$ is a $C^1$ embedding. F6 is a smooth slice-chart definition; here its explicit analogue in the $C^1$ category is used, with no claim that a merely $C^1$ leaf is smooth. [F1, F6, step 1.1, step 1.2] ∎
