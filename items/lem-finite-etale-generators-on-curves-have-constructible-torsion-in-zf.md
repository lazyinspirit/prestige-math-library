## Statement

In ZF, let $X$ be a finite-type scheme of dimension at most one over a field, let $n\ge1$, and let $U_i\to X$ be a finite family of quasi-compact separated étale maps. The sheaves $\mathbb Z/n[U_i]$ freely generated by the representable étale sheaves have finite constructible fibres. Any cokernel of a specified morphism between two finite direct sums of such generators is a finite constructible $\mathbb Z/n$-module sheaf. The statement concerns the displayed finite presentation; it does not claim every torsion sheaf admits one presentation.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite-type curve, integer and the specified finite diagram of quasi-compact separated étale objects.

[F1] Étale maps admit local standard-étale equations and are universally open in ZF. [[lem-etale-morphisms-are-locally-standard-and-universally-open-in-zf]]

[F2] A finite constructible module sheaf is locally constant finite on a finite locally closed stratification. [[def-constructible-etale-sheaf-of-finite-modules]]

[F3] A morphism from a proper scheme over a base to a separated scheme over that base is proper. [[lem-projective-properness-and-finite-type-surjectivity-without-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** make each étale generator finite on a dense open of each curve component, then use finite quotient tables.

1.1 The finite-type $X$ is Noetherian by finite Hilbert-basis induction. Its reduction has finitely many irreducible components. Removing their finitely many intersections and any zero-dimensional components gives finitely many disjoint integral one-dimensional locally closed pieces, together with finitely many closed points. Work on one affine chart $D$ of such an integral piece and one affine étale chart $B$ of a specified $U_i$ above it. The generic fibre $B\otimes_D\operatorname{Frac}D$ is a finite product of finite separable fields by the standard chart [F1]. If it is zero, one nonzero element of $D$ annihilates $1$ in $B$ and the map is empty after inverting it. Otherwise, each of the finitely many generators of $B$ satisfies a monic polynomial over the generic field. Clear finitely many denominators and the resulting equalities, and invert one common nonzero $s\in D$; then $B_s$ is finite over $D_s$. It remains étale, hence finite étale there. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Each specified $U_i$ has a finite affine cover, and each overlap with the finitely many base charts is quasi-compact because the map is separated. Apply step 1.1 to the finite resulting list and shrink by the finite product of the chosen nonzero elements on each integral piece. The complement is a proper closed subset of a one-dimensional finite-type integral scheme, hence a finite set of closed points, each with finite residue-field extension; finite Noether normalization proves the latter without choosing an algebraic closure. The same finite argument applies to the finitely many affine overlaps needed to glue the charts. On the resulting dense open, each affine chart is finite over the base. It is proper by a finite-module argument: after any base change and for any closed subset, a missing fibre over a specified prime is a finite-dimensional field algebra without primes and hence zero; determinant Nakayama then gives an open neighbourhood missing the image. Finite maps are separated and of finite type. Its open immersion into the separated $U_i$ is therefore proper by [F3], and is also closed. The finite chart images are clopen; taking their finite disjoint differences expresses $U_i$ as a finite disjoint union of schemes finite over the base. Thus each $U_i$ is finite étale on each stratum of the resulting finite stratification. [F1, F3, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 On a stratum, a finite étale generator has a finite locally constant fibre set. Its free $\mathbb Z/n$-module is finite locally constant, and finite direct sums remain so. To treat the specified morphism and its cokernel, take a common finite étale splitting cover of the finitely many finite étale fibre sets: adjoining roots of their finitely many separable equations gives a finite common splitting algebra, and the descent automorphism groups and relation matrices are finite. On the splitting cover the morphism is a locally constant matrix of finite $\mathbb Z/n$-modules. Its finitely many possible matrices have open-and-closed loci, so refine by those finitely many loci. On each refined piece the cokernel is the cokernel of one homomorphism of finite $\mathbb Z/n$-modules and is finite and constant; finite descent supplies the locally constant structure on the stratum. Thus the cokernel is finite constructible by [F2]. Every selection and quotient here is finite; no presentation of an arbitrary torsion sheaf has been assumed. [F1, F2, step 2.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎
