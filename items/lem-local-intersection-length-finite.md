## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $k$ be a field, let $p\in\mathbf A^2(k)$, put $R=k[x,y]$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_p$, and $O=R_{\mathfrak m}$, and let $f,g\in\mathfrak m$ be nonzero. Then the following are equivalent: (1) $(f,g)O$ is $\mathfrak m$-primary; (2) $O/(f,g)O$ has finite length as an $O$-module; (3) $f$ and $g$ have no common irreducible factor $h\in k[x,y]$ with $h(p)=0$, equivalently no height-one prime of $R$ contained in $\mathfrak m$ contains $(f,g)$. If the conditions fail the length is infinite, and if they hold $(f,g)O$ is a parameter ideal of the two-dimensional regular local ring $O$, so $O/(f,g)O$ is a zero-dimensional local ring of finite length. Moreover $O$ is a UFD. Every ideal $I\subseteq O$ with radical $\mathfrak m O$ contains a power of $\mathfrak m O$, and its quotient satisfies $\ell_O(O/I)=\dim_k(O/I)$. For a surjective ring map $O\to V$ and a $V$-module $M$, the submodules over $O$ and $V$ coincide, so its composition length is unchanged.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, an algebraically closed or arbitrary field $k$, a point $p\in\mathbf A^2(k)$, $R=k[x,y]$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_p$, $O=R_{\mathfrak m}$, and nonzero $f,g\in\mathfrak m$; write $k$ for the field and note $\dim R=2$.

[F1] $R=k[x,y]$ is Noetherian [[cor-finite-variable-polynomial-ring-noetherian]], an integral domain of Krull dimension $2$ [[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]], and a unique factorisation domain in which every irreducible is prime and every height-one prime is generated by an irreducible element [[lem-finite-variable-polynomial-rings-over-fields-are-ufds]], [[def-unique-factorisation-domain]], [[def-irreducible-and-prime-elements-in-a-domain]], [[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[def-krull-dimension-of-a-ring]].

[F2] $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak m_p$ is a maximal ideal, $R/\mathfrak m\cong k$, and $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=\dim R=2$ [[lem-evaluation-ideal-is-maximal]], [[cor-affine-domain-maximal-ideal-height-equals-dimension]], [[def-prime-and-maximal-ideals]], [[def-field]]. AC is used here through the height formula.

[F3] $O=R_{\mathfrak m}$ is a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m O$, of dimension $\dim O=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=2$; it is regular: after translation $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$, the classes of $x,y$ span $\mathfrak m O/(\mathfrak m O)^2$ and are independent, since clearing a denominator with nonzero constant term cannot kill a nonzero linear part. Thus its embedding dimension is $2=\dim O$ [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]], [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]], [[def-local-ring]], [[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]], [[cor-localisation-dimension-does-not-increase]].

[F4] Contraction gives inclusion-preserving bijections between the primes of $O$ and the primes of $R$ contained in $\mathfrak m$ (inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak p O$), and between the primes of a quotient $O/I$ and the primes of $O$ containing $I$ [[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]], [[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]].

[F5] For an ideal $I$ of the local ring $(O,\mathfrak m O)$ one has: $I$ is $\mathfrak m O$-primary exactly when $\sqrt I=\mathfrak m O$; and a tuple $(x_1,\ldots,x_d)\in(\mathfrak m O)^d$ with $d=\dim O$ is a system of parameters exactly when $\sqrt{(x_1,\ldots,x_d)}=\mathfrak m O$, in which case $(x_1,\ldots,x_d)$ is a parameter ideal [[def-radical-of-an-ideal]], [[def-system-of-parameters-and-parameter-ideal]], [[lem-parameter-ideal-equivalent-m-primary]].

[F6] A commutative Noetherian ring is Artinian if and only if every prime ideal of it is maximal; a commutative ring is Artinian if and only if it has finite length as a module over itself; and every prime of an Artinian ring is maximal [[thm-artinian-ring-characterisation-by-primes]], [[thm-artinian-ring-has-finite-length]], [[thm-artinian-ring-primes-are-maximal]], [[def-artinian-ring]], [[def-composition-series-and-length-of-a-module]]. AC is used through these characterisations.

[F7] AC is assumed throughout [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters only through the height, prime-existence and Artinian characterisations of [F2], [F5] and [F6]. No further choice is made.

## Proof

1.1 Translating the coordinates by $p$ replaces $\mathfrak m_p$ by $(x,y)$ and induces a $k$-algebra automorphism of $R$ carrying $f,g$ to nonzero elements of $(x,y)$; lengths and primary-ness are unchanged, so assume $p=0$ and $\mathfrak m=(x,y)$. Then $R$ is a Noetherian UFD of dimension $2$, $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=2$, and $O$ is a two-dimensional regular local ring with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m O$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 Every prime $\mathfrak q$ of $R$ with $(f,g)\subseteq\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak m$ has height $1$ or $2$: height $0$ is impossible because $f\ne0$, and height at most $2$ since $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak m$ and $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=2$. A height-two prime contained in $\mathfrak m$ equals $\mathfrak m$, and a height-one prime is generated by an irreducible element $h$, which lies in $\mathfrak m$ exactly when $h(p)=0$. [F1, F2, algebra]

1.3 The ring $O$ is a UFD: factor a numerator in $R$ and discard the irreducible factors outside $\mathfrak m$, which become units. Each remaining factor $h$ stays prime and nonunit, since $O/(h)O$ is a localisation of the domain $R/(h)$ at denominators disjoint from $(h)$. Clearing denominators and using factorisation in $R$ proves uniqueness. Also, if $I\subseteq O$ has radical $\mathfrak m O=(x,y)O$, then $x^a,y^b\in I$ for some positive $a,b$, and every monomial of degree $a+b-1$ is divisible by $x^a$ or $y^b$; hence $(\mathfrak m O)^{a+b-1}\subseteq I$. For a quotient $Q=O/I$ with this containment, the finite filtration by powers of $\mathfrak m O$ has finite-dimensional $k$-vector space factors, each killed by $\mathfrak m O$. Refine each factor by a finite vector-space flag to obtain simple factors $k=O/\mathfrak m O$. Therefore $\ell_O(Q)=\dim_k Q$. Finally, for any quotient map $O\twoheadrightarrow V$ and a $V$-module $M$, the $O$-submodules and $V$-submodules coincide, so $\ell_O(M)=\ell_V(M)$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, algebra, construct]

2.1 By [F4], the primes of $O$ containing $(f,g)O$ are exactly the primes $\mathfrak q O$ with $(f,g)\subseteq\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak m$, and $\mathfrak q O=\mathfrak m O$ exactly for $\mathfrak q=\mathfrak m$. Hence $(f,g)O$ is $\mathfrak m O$-primary, equivalently $\sqrt{(f,g)O}=\mathfrak m O$ by [F5], if and only if $\mathfrak m$ is the only prime of $R$ with $(f,g)\subseteq\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak m$: here the radical is the intersection of the primes containing the ideal [[thm-radical-as-intersection-of-primes]]. [step 1.1, F4, F5, algebra]



3.1 By step 1.2, the condition of step 2.1 fails exactly when there is a height-one prime $(h)\subseteq\mathfrak m$ containing $(f,g)$, that is, exactly when $f$ and $g$ have a common irreducible factor $h$ with $h(p)=0$. This proves the equivalence of condition (3) with condition (1). [step 1.2, step 2.1, F1, algebra]

3.2 Assume the conditions hold, so $(f,g)O$ is $\mathfrak m O$-primary and $\sqrt{(f,g)O}=\mathfrak m O$ with $f,g\in\mathfrak m O$ and $\dim O=2$. Then $(f,g)$ is a system of parameters and $(f,g)O$ is a parameter ideal of the regular local ring $O$ [F5]. The quotient $O/(f,g)O$ is Noetherian [F3], it is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m O/(f,g)O$, and by [F4] its only prime is that maximal ideal; hence every prime of it is maximal, so it is Artinian and therefore of finite length as an $O$-module [F6]. In particular (1) implies (2), and the described quotient is zero-dimensional of finite length. [step 2.1, F3, F4, F5, F6]

4.1 Conversely assume there is a common irreducible factor $h$ with $h(p)=0$, so that $(f,g)\subseteq(h)\subseteq\mathfrak m$ and $(h)$ has height one. Then $(h)O$ is a prime of $O$ containing $(f,g)O$ and different from $\mathfrak m O$ because $\dim O=2>\operatorname{ht}((h))=1$ [F1, F3]. Its image in $O/(f,g)O$ is prime and not maximal [F4], so $O/(f,g)O$ is not Artinian; by [F6] it cannot have finite length, so its length is infinite. Hence (2) implies (1), the length is infinite whenever the conditions fail, and the equivalence of (1), (2) and (3) is established. [step 3.1, F1, F3, F4, F6, given, F7] ∎
