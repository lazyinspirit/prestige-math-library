## Statement

On the Specht module $S_\lambda$ of
[[def-arbitrary-level-cyclotomic-hecke-murphy-cell-and-schur-modules]], $X_r$
acts dominance-triangularly with diagonal $U_az^{j-i}$ at the node $(i,j,a)$
carrying $r$. Over a field with $z=q_H$ of finite order $e\ge2$ and
$U_a=q_H^{c_a}$, every simultaneous generalized eigenword of $S_\lambda$ is
among the standard-tableau residue words $c_a+j-i$ modulo $e$; every such word
has the fixed node-residue multiplicities of $\lambda$. The universal
triangularity also specializes to the LM deformation DVR and the generic
fibre, with no semisimplicity or affine-center premise.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The arbitrary-level Murphy basis and higher ideals of [[thm-arbitrary-level-cyclotomic-hecke-murphy-basis-is-cellular]], and the ordinary Jucys–Murphy triangularity ([[thm-hecke-jucys-murphy-action-is-dominance-triangular]]).

[F1] The Murphy elements $m_{st}$ form a dominance-cellular basis with higher two-sided ideals $N^{>\lambda}$, and $m_\lambda(X_{a+1}-Q_k)=x_\lambda u_b\in N^{>\lambda}$ for $a=a_k$ and $\lambda^{(k)}\ne\varnothing$ ([[thm-arbitrary-level-cyclotomic-hecke-murphy-basis-is-cellular]]); the generators satisfy $T_iX_i=X_{i+1}T_i-(q_H-1)X_{i+1}$ and $T_iX_{i+1}=X_iT_i+(q_H-1)X_{i+1}$, with distant commutation ([[def-arbitrary-level-cyclotomic-hecke-murphy-cell-and-schur-modules]]).

[F2] The ordinary multiplicative Jucys–Murphy elements $M_i=q_H^{1-i}T_{i-1}\cdots T_1^2\cdots T_{i-1}=1+(q_H-1)L_i$ act on ordinary standard tableaux with diagonal $q_H^{\mathrm{content}}$ ([[thm-hecke-jucys-murphy-action-is-dominance-triangular]]).

[F3] Bruhat order on permutations has both the prefix-rank and reduced-subword characterizations, is invariant under inversion, and strictly lowers length on strict intervals ([[lem-bruhat-order-basic-properties-for-permutations]]). Shift the zero-based convention by one to use labels $1,\ldots,n$.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The multipartition $\lambda$, the Murphy module $S_\lambda$ with its

basis $m_t$, and the data of [F1]–[F2].

1.1 Initial-row eigenvalues. Consider only a nonempty component $k$ and put $a=a_k$; empty components contribute no nodes or JM eigenvalues. Then [F1] gives $m_\lambda(X_{a+1}-Q_k)=x_\lambda u_b\in N^{>\lambda}$. The internal finite Hecke submodule generated by $m_\lambda$ modulo higher global shapes is spanned by standard internal component permutations; each such permutation fixes the smallest internal label $a+1$ (that label occupies the first node of its component), so its Hecke element has a reduced word in the internal generators with indices at least $a+2$ and commutes with $X_{a+1}$. Hence $X_{a+1}$ acts as the scalar $Q_k$ on that whole submodule, with errors in the two-sided ideal $N^{>\lambda}$. The $X$-recurrence then identifies $X_{a+i}$ on this submodule with $Q_k$ times the ordinary multiplicative Jucys–Murphy element $M_i$ of [F2], by induction from the finite quadratic relation and the additive-JM recurrence (a polynomial identity also at $q_H=1$). Ordinary JM triangularity gives diagonal $Q_kq_H^{c-r}$ at the node $(r,c,k)$ occupied by local entry $i$: the internal label $i$ is not a row index, and no extra subtraction of $i-1$ occurs. For the initial component tableau, which is maximal in tableau dominance, there are no strictly more dominant same-shape terms. Thus for the node $(r,c,k)$ carrying global entry $t$, $m_\lambda X_t\equiv Q_kq_H^{c-r}m_\lambda\pmod{N^{>\lambda}}$.  [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 JM triangularity for every standard multitableau. Inducting through a reduced word of $w$, the relations [F1] give $T_wX_r=X_{w(r)}T_w+\sum_{v<w}\sum_ba_{(v,b)}X_bT_v,$ where the corrections are supported on proper Bruhat subwords (at the first correction a generator is deleted, moving its linear $X$-coefficient through preceding generators deletes further generators, and finite quadratic multiplication preserves Bruhat support). Applying this with $w=d(t)$ and multiplying by $m_\lambda$ on the left, step 1.1 evaluates every surviving $X$ coefficient modulo higher shapes; a correction $m_\lambda T_v$ reduces first to a row-standard representative $d(u)$ of its Young-subgroup coset, the removed row subgroup contributing its row eigenvalue, with $d(u)\le v<d(t)$; the Bruhat/tableau-prefix relation on the ordered rows of all components then makes $u$ strictly dominate $t$, and componentwise straightening of $u$ keeps the shape or produces same-shape tableaux at least $u$ in prefix dominance, hence strictly dominating $t$. The leading eigenvalue is $Q_kq_H^{j-i}$ at the node carrying $r$, because $w(r)$ is its initial-row label under $t=t^\lambda\cdot w=w^{-1}\cdot t^\lambda$. Therefore $m_{(t^\lambda,t)}X_r\equiv Q_kq_H^{j-i}m_{(t^\lambda,t)} +\sum_{v\lhd t}a_vm_{(t^\lambda,v)}\pmod{N^{>\lambda}},$ the exact arbitrary-level JM content statement, independent of an affine center theorem, semisimplicity or block classification. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Residue words and boundary cases. On the resulting Specht module a total ordering extending tableau dominance triangularizes all the $X_r$ simultaneously; the one-dimensional successive quotients have exactly the content characters of step 2.1, so every simultaneous generalized eigenword is among the standard-tableau residue words $c_a+j-i\pmod e$ ([[thm-hecke-jucys-murphy-action-is-dominance-triangular]] for the ordinary part); the residue multiplicities of every such word equal the node-residue multiplicities of $\lambda$, since permuting the labels of a standard tableau does not change the multiset of node residues. For rank zero, $H=F$, the sole shape and tableau are empty and all assertions have their one-dimensional interpretation. The triangularity is polynomial in the parameters, so it specializes to the LM deformation DVR and to the generic fibre. [step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The lemma supplies the content-triangularity input of the Jantzen and core-reduction theory over the original parameters, with no semisimplicity or affine-center hypothesis; the diagonal is the node content and the corrections are strictly dominant standard tableaux.
