## Statement

Let $(X,\mathcal O_X)$ be a ringed space, let $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$ be
$\mathcal O_X$-modules, and let $x\in X$. Then there is a canonical
isomorphism of $\mathcal O_{X,x}$-modules
$$(\mathcal F\otimes_{\mathcal O_X}\mathcal G)_x \cong \mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal G_x.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A ringed space $(X,\mathcal O_X)$, two $\mathcal O_X$-modules $\mathcal F,\mathcal G$, and a point $x\in X$.

[F1] The sheaf tensor product is the sheafification of the presheaf $U\mapsto\mathcal F(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X(U)}\mathcal G(U)$ ([[def-sheaf-tensor-product]]).

[F2] Stalks are germs of sections over neighbourhoods of the point ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]]).

[L1] Sheafification preserves stalks ([[thm-sheafification-preserves-stalks]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [L1], it is enough to identify the stalk of the tensor-product presheaf $$U\longmapsto \mathcal F(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X(U)}\mathcal G(U).$$ [F1, L1, given]

1.2 For every neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, the germ maps $\mathcal F(U)\to\mathcal F_x$, $\mathcal G(U)\to\mathcal G_x$, and $\mathcal O_X(U)\to\mathcal O_{X,x}$ induce an $\mathcal O_X(U)$-balanced pairing into $\mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal G_x$. Hence there is a homomorphism $$\mathcal F(U)\otimes_{\mathcal O_X(U)}\mathcal G(U)\longrightarrow \mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal G_x,$$ and these maps are compatible with restriction. Therefore they induce a map $$\Phi:(\mathcal F\otimes_{p,\mathcal O_X}\mathcal G)_x\longrightarrow \mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal G_x.$$ [F2, construct]

1.3 Conversely, if $s_x\in\mathcal F_x$ and $t_x\in\mathcal G_x$ are represented by sections $s\in\mathcal F(U)$ and $t\in\mathcal G(U)$ on the same neighbourhood $U$ of $x$, define $$\Psi(s_x\otimes t_x):=(s\otimes t)_x.$$ If the representatives are changed on a smaller neighbourhood, the represented germ of $s\otimes t$ is unchanged there, so $\Psi$ is well defined on simple tensors and extends linearly to a homomorphism $$\Psi:\mathcal F_x\otimes_{\mathcal O_{X,x}}\mathcal G_x\longrightarrow (\mathcal F\otimes_{p,\mathcal O_X}\mathcal G)_x.$$ [F2, construct]

2.1 On a simple tensor represented on one neighbourhood, $\Phi$ and $\Psi$ plainly undo each other. Since both sides are generated by simple tensors, they are inverse isomorphisms. Combining this with step 1.1 gives the stated isomorphism for the sheaf tensor product. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3] ∎
