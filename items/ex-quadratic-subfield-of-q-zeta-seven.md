## Example

The unique intermediate field $\mathbb Q\subseteq F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)$
with $[F:\mathbb Q]=2$ is
$$F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7}),$$
namely the field generated by the quadratic Gauss sum $\tau_7$ attached to a
chosen primitive seventh root of unity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The odd prime $p=7$, a fixed primitive seventh root of unity
$\zeta=\zeta_7$, the Gauss sum
$\tau=\tau_7=\sum_{a\bmod7}(a/7)\zeta^{a}$ attached to it, and
$p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p$
([[def-quadratic-gauss-sum-in-a-cyclotomic-field]],
[[def-cyclotomic-extension]]).

[F1] For every odd prime $p$, the unique intermediate field
$\mathbb Q\subseteq F\subseteq\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)$ with $[F:\mathbb Q]=2$ is
$\mathbb Q(\tau_p)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p^{*}})$
([[thm-quadratic-subfield-of-a-prime-cyclotomic-field]]).

[F2] For every odd prime $p$, $\tau_p^{2}=p^{*}$; here in particular
$\tau^{2}=p^{*}$ ([[thm-quadratic-gauss-sum-square]]).

[F3] $(7-1)/2=3$, so $p^{*}=(-1)^{3}\cdot7=-7$. [arithmetic]

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Substituting $p=7$ into $p^{*}=(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p$ gives $p^{*}=(-1)^{3}\cdot7=-7$. [F3]

2.1 By [F1] with $p=7$, the unique degree-two intermediate field of $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)/\mathbb Q$ is $\mathbb Q(\tau_7)=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{p^{*}})=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, where $\tau_7$ is the Gauss sum attached to the chosen primitive root $\zeta_7$. [step 1.1, F1]

3.1 By [F2] with $p=7$, $\tau^{2}=-7$, so $\tau=\pm\sqrt{-7}$ and the generator $\mathbb Q(\tau)$ is indeed $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, in agreement with the identification of step 2.1. [F2, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **The field is canonical, the generator is not.** Replacing $\zeta_7$ by
  another primitive seventh root multiplies $\tau_7$ by a sign
  $\pm1$, so the element $\tau_7$ is not canonical; the field
  $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ it generates is, by the uniqueness clause of [F1].
  This is the phenomenon recorded in the companion counterexample on the
  Gauss-sum sign.
- **Discriminant form.** $-7\equiv1\pmod4$, so $-7$ is a fundamental
  discriminant. Since $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)/\mathbb Q)
  \cong(\mathbb Z/7)^{\times}$ is cyclic of order $6$, the field
  $\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ is the fixed field of its unique subgroup of order
  $3$, and $\mathbb Q(\zeta_7)/\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$ is a cyclic cubic
  extension.
