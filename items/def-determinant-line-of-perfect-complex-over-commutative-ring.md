## Definition

Let $R$ be a commutative ring and $P^\bullet$ a bounded complex of finitely generated projective $R$-modules. On each open subset where a term $P^i$ is free of rank $r_i$, its local determinant line is generated by wedges $p_1\wedge\cdots\wedge p_{r_i}$; on overlaps the generators are identified by the determinant of the change-of-basis matrix. The determinant line of the complex is the alternating tensor product of these term determinant lines,
$$\operatorname{Det}_R(P^\bullet)=\bigotimes_i\operatorname{Det}_R(P^i)^{(-1)^i},$$
where exponent $-1$ denotes the dual. This is the general determinant-functor convention for a bounded finite-projective representative; invariance under quasi-isomorphism is a separate theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A commutative ring and a bounded complex of finitely generated projective modules.

[F1] [[def-commutative-ring]]: multiplication in $R$ is commutative.

[F2] [[def-projective-module]]: a finite projective module is a direct summand of a finite free module.

[F3] [[def-perfect-complex-over-a-ring]]: a perfect complex has a bounded finite-projective representative.

[F4] [[def-determinant-line-of-a-perfect-complex]]: over a domain with constant-rank terms, the determinant is the alternating tensor product of top exterior powers.

[F5] [[thm-determinant-multiplicative]]: determinants multiply for square matrices over every commutative ring.

[F6] [[def-principal-localisation]] and [[def-localisation-of-a-module]] fix localization and equality after multiplication by a denominator.

## Construction and well-definedness

This construction uses finite algebra over the actual ring; it does not replace the ring by a product of DVRs, assume constant rank, or invoke a prime-ideal existence principle. Write a finite projective module as the image of an idempotent matrix $E$ on $R^n$. There is a finite principal cover on which its image is free, constructed as follows. At a node put $a=E_{11}$ and pass to the two localizations at $a$ and $1-a$. They cover algebraically because $a+(1-a)=1$. On the first, $v=Ee_1$ is a unimodular element of the image: the functional $x\mapsto a^{-1}(Ex)_1$ takes value one on $v$ and is unchanged by $E$. The basis $v,e_j-a^{-1}(Ee_j)_1v$ for $2\le j\le n$ makes $E$ the block sum of $1$ and an idempotent of size $n-1$. The same construction for $1-E$ on the second localization makes $E$ the block sum of $0$ and an idempotent of size $n-1$. Repeat this finite binary procedure until size zero. Clearing the finitely many denominators gives a finite list $f_j\in R$ generating the unit ideal, with explicit bases of $P_{f_j}$, whose ranks may differ. A localization which is the zero ring is harmless. To verify the cover after clearing, an equation $1=\sum u_i g_i$ in $R_f$ becomes $f^N=\sum v_i g_i$ after multiplication by a power of $f$; induction through the finite tree proves the unit-ideal assertion.

On an overlap where the two displayed ranks differ, the overlap ring is zero: an isomorphism $A^r\cong A^s$ with $r<s$ makes $A=\bigwedge^s A^s\cong\bigwedge^s A^r=0$. Otherwise use the top exterior power and the determinant of the change-of-basis matrix. Its inverse has inverse determinant, and the cocycle identity follows from multiplicativity of the determinant over commutative rings. Thus the local free rank-one modules have an actual cocycle, also on disconnected rings.

For clarity the finite gluing here is algebraic. For a finite principal cover $(f_j)=R$ and a module cocycle, the glued module consists of the matching tuples in the product of its localized chart modules. A local element extends after multiplication by a sufficiently large power of its chart denominator: clear that denominator in the finitely many other charts, and multiply by a further power to kill the finitely many overlap discrepancies. This proves that localization of the matching module is the specified chart module. In the rank-one case do this both for each chart frame and its dual. It gives global elements $x_j$ and functionals $l_j$ with $x_j l_j=f_j^N\operatorname{id}$, after increasing one common $N$; equality is checked on the finite charts and denominator discrepancies are killed as just described. Powers of the $f_j$ still generate one (expand a sufficiently large power of an expression $1=\sum a_j f_j$), so a finite linear combination gives $\sum b_j x_j l_j=\operatorname{id}$. This finite dual basis proves the matching module is finitely generated projective. Evaluation against its dual is an isomorphism on every chart and hence globally. Indeed, an element killed after all the finitely many localizations is killed by powers of all $f_j$, and the same unit-ideal identity kills it. This proves both existence of the invertible module and uniqueness of the glued module and maps, without an arbitrary open gluing or choice theorem.

Apply this to each of the finitely many terms and their common finite refinement. Tensor products and duals of these invertible modules give the displayed alternating determinant. Refining the cover or changing bases gives the same module by the matching-tuple uniqueness, because the transition matrices compose. The construction commutes with every homomorphism $R\to S$: the same finite unit-ideal cover pulls back, exterior powers are defined by the alternating-tensor quotient and commute with tensoring, and duals of rank-one projectives commute with base change by their finite dual basis. No flatness of $S$ is required. This establishes the definition for a chosen representative; the next theorem constructs the canonical transport between representatives.
