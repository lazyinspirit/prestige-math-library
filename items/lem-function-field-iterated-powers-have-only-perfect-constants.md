## Statement

Let $K/k$ be a finitely generated field extension of characteristic $p>0$
with $k$ relatively algebraically closed in $K$. For a field $E$, write
$E^{p^n}=\{b^{p^n}:b\in E\}$. Then
$$\bigcap_{n\ge0}K^{p^n}=\bigcap_{n\ge0}k^{p^n}.$$
In particular, if $k$ is perfect this intersection equals $k$. The extension
may have any finite transcendence degree, including zero, and need not be
separably generated.

## Statement references

[Samuel, Chapter III, §2, Lemma 4, printed p. 74](https://mathweb.tifr.res.in/Documents/Publications/Lectures/tifr36.pdf)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The fields, finite generation, characteristic and exact-constant
hypotheses of the Statement.

[F1] Exact constants mean that every element of $K$ algebraic over $k$ is in
$k$. [[def-function-field-with-exact-constant-field]]

[F2] A finite field extension has degree its vector-space dimension; degrees
multiply in a finite tower. [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]
[[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; a nonconstant element cannot have roots of
unbounded purely inseparable degree in one fixed finite extension.

1.1 Fix $f\in\bigcap_{n\ge0}K^{p^n}$ and suppose first that $f$ is transcendental over $k$. Start with the independent singleton $\{f\}$ and examine a finite field-generating tuple for $K/k$, adjoining a generator precisely when it is transcendental over the field generated by the preceding independent elements. The resulting list $f,t_2,\ldots,t_d$ is algebraically independent and each omitted generator is algebraic over its generated field $E=k(f,t_2,\ldots,t_d)$. Thus $K/E$ is generated by finitely many algebraic elements and is finite, of some degree $M$. No maximality or choice over an infinite family is used. [given, construct, F2]

2.1 Put $B=k(t_2,\ldots,t_d)$, interpreting $B=k$ when $d=1$. For $q=p^n$ choose the unique $z\in K$ with $z^q=f$, which exists by the choice of $f$. This $z$ is transcendental over $B$. The powers $1,z,\ldots,z^{q-1}$ are linearly independent over $B(f)$: after clearing denominators, any putative relation becomes $\sum_{i=0}^{q-1} A_i(z^q)z^i=0$ with $A_i\in B[T]$; its distinct monomials have exponents in distinct residue classes modulo $q$, so transcendence of $z$ forces every $A_i=0$. The relation $z^q=f$ also shows that these powers span $B(f)[z]$, which is a field because it is a finite-dimensional domain over a field: multiplication by each nonzero element is injective and hence surjective. Consequently $[E(z):E]=q$. [given, algebra, step 1.1, F2]

3.1 The tower $E\subseteq E(z)\subseteq K$ gives $q\le M$ by [F2]. Choosing $n$ with $p^n>M$ contradicts step 2.1. Hence $f$ is algebraic over $k$, and [F1] gives $f\in k$. For every $n$, its root $z\in K$ is algebraic over $k$, since $z^{p^n}=f\in k$; thus [F1] gives $z\in k$. It follows that $f\in\bigcap_n k^{p^n}$. The opposite containment is immediate from $k\subseteq K$. If $k$ is perfect, all its iterated Frobenius maps are surjective, so this last intersection is $k$. For transcendence degree zero, [F1] already gives $K=k$ and the equality is immediate. [F1, F2, algebra, step 1.1, step 2.1] ∎

## Remarks

For imperfect $k$ the answer is its perfect core, not necessarily all of
$k$. In particular this lemma cannot justify silently replacing imperfect
constants by algebraically closed constants in a smoothness claim.
