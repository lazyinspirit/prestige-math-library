## Statement

Let $n\geq2$, let $p$ be a prime, $G=\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb Q_p)$, and $K=\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\mathbb Z_p)$ with Haar measure normalized by $\operatorname{Vol}(K)=1$. For $0\leq\ell\leq n+1$ define

$$T_{p,\ell}=p^{-n\ell/2}\mathbf1_{K\operatorname{diag}(p^\ell,I_n)K},\qquad T^*_{p,\ell}(g)=T_{p,\ell}(g^{-1}),$$

and for $0\leq i\leq\ell$ define

$$U_{p,i}=p^{-ni}\mathbf1_{K\operatorname{diag}(p^i,I_{n-1},p^{-i})K}.$$

Use convolution $h_1*h_2(g)=\int_Gh_1(gg'^{-1})h_2(g')\,dg'$. Then there are scalars $c_{p,\ell,i}$ with $|c_{p,\ell,i}|\leq C_n$, where $C_n$ depends only on $n$, such that

$$T_{p,\ell}*T^*_{p,\ell}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}c_{p,\ell,i}U_{p,i}.$$

## Statement references

[Liyang Yang, *Relative Trace Formula, Subconvexity and Quantitative Nonvanishing of Rankin–Selberg L-Functions for GL(n+1) × GL(n)*, equation (3.11), §3.7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.07534v2)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the prime, rank, double cosets, normalized Haar measure and convolution in the Statement. Put $r=n+1$, $O=\mathbb Z_p$, $V=\mathbb Q_p^r$, $L_0=O^r$, and $A_\ell=K\operatorname{diag}(p^\ell,1,\ldots,1)K$. A lattice means the $O$-span of a basis of $V$. Only finite partitions of the given Haar measure are used; its existence is not asserted here.

[F1] Every nonzero $p$-adic number has a unique digit expansion $\sum_{j\ge N}a_jp^j$, with $a_N\ne0$ and $0\le a_j<p$. [[thm-p-adic-digit-expansion]]

[F2] Every matrix over a PID is equivalent, under invertible row and column operations, to a Smith diagonal matrix whose nonzero entries form a divisibility chain. [[thm-smith-normal-form-existence-over-a-pid]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** finite lattice and cyclic-quotient counting.

1.1 By [F1], every nonzero element of $O$ is $p^a$ times a unit, $O/p^mO$ has the canonical representatives $0,\ldots,p^m-1$, and its unit group has size $p^m-p^{m-1}$ for $m\ge1$. Every nonzero ideal of $O$ is generated by an element of its least occurring valuation, so $O$ is a PID. Apply [F2] after multiplying a matrix over $\mathbb Q_p$ by a sufficiently large power of $p$. It follows that every $g\in G$ belongs to $K\operatorname{diag}(p^{a_1},\ldots,p^{a_r})K$ for integers $a_j$. The same argument gives a basis of any sublattice of $L_0$ in which its inclusion is diagonal. In particular, $A_\ell/K$ is in bijection with the sublattices $M\subset L_0$ such that $L_0/M\simeq O/p^\ell O$, by $xK\mapsto xL_0$; equality of lattices means the change of basis lies in $K$. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 All left and right $K$-coset sets of a double coset $D=KaK$ are finite. Indeed, for $a$ diagonal as in step 1.1 a sufficiently deep principal congruence subgroup $K_s=1+p^sM_r(O)$ is contained in both $K\cap aKa^{-1}$ and $K\cap a^{-1}Ka$, while $K/K_s$ is finite by reduction modulo $p^s$. Right translation by an element $k\in K$ preserves the measure of any left $K_t$-coset: $xK_tk=xkK_t$ because $K_t$ is normal in $K$, and both cosets have measure $1/[K:K_t]$. This applies to $Ka$, since it is a finite union of such left cosets for sufficiently large $t$. Thus every left $K$-coset in $KaK$ has the same measure $\operatorname{vol}(Ka)$. Transposition gives a bijection between its left and right coset sets, since $a$ is diagonal and $K^t=K$; write their common size as $N$. Partitioning $KaK$ into its $N$ right cosets gives volume $N$ by left invariance, while its $N$ left cosets give $N\operatorname{vol}(Ka)$. Hence $\operatorname{vol}(Ka)=1$. The diagonal decomposition from step 1.1 and right-$K$ invariance just established show that every left $K$-coset in every double coset has measure1. Now partition the convolution integral over the left cosets $Kg'$ in $A_\ell^{-1}$. The integrand $\mathbf1_{A_\ell}(gg'^{-1})\mathbf1_{A_\ell^{-1}}(g')$ is constant on each such coset. The map $Kg'\mapsto gg'^{-1}K$ is a bijection onto the right cosets $xK$ with $x\in A_\ell\cap gA_\ell$. Consequently $T_{p,\ell}*T^*_{p,\ell}(g)=p^{-n\ell}$ times the number of lattices $M\subset L_0\cap gL_0$ for which both $L_0/M$ and $gL_0/M$ are cyclic of order $p^\ell$. This counting argument needs neither a general inversion theorem for Haar measure nor any choice principle. [given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If such an $M$ exists, both lattices have the same index over $M$, so $v_p(\det g)=0$. Further, $(L_0+gL_0)/gL_0$ is a quotient of the cyclic module $L_0/M$, and $(L_0+gL_0)/L_0$ is a quotient of the cyclic module $gL_0/M$. In a diagonal relative basis from step 1.1 these modules are respectively the direct sums of $O/p^{a_j}O$ over the positive $a_j$ and of $O/p^{-a_j}O$ over the negative $a_j$. Such a sum is cyclic only if it has at most one nonzero summand: its reduction modulo $p$ has dimension equal to the number of summands, whereas a nonzero cyclic module has dimension1. The zero determinant valuation therefore forces either all $a_j=0$, or one exponent $i>0$, one exponent $-i$, and the rest zero. Each cyclic quotient above has order at most $p^\ell$, so $i\le\ell$. Thus the convolution is supported on precisely the possible double cosets $D_i=K\operatorname{diag}(p^i,I_{n-1},p^{-i})K$, $0\le i\le\ell$. Its values are constant on each $D_i$ by the lattice count, since changing $g$ on either side by $K$ gives a bijection of the counted lattices. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Suppose $\ell\ge1$ and $g=1$. A lattice in the count is the kernel of a surjective row map $O^r\to O/p^\ell O$. A row is surjective exactly when some coordinate is a unit, so there are $p^{r\ell}-p^{r(\ell-1)}$ such rows. Two surjective rows have the same kernel exactly when they differ by an automorphism of their cyclic target, that is, multiplication by a unit modulo $p^\ell$; this action is free. Hence the number of kernels is $p^{n(\ell-1)}(p^{n+1}-1)/(p-1)$. Step 2.1 gives the convolution value at1 as $p^{-n}(p^{n+1}-1)/(p-1)=\sum_{j=0}^np^{-j}$. This is the coefficient of $U_{p,0}=\mathbf1_K$. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Fix $1\le i\le\ell$, put $g_i=\operatorname{diag}(p^i,I_{r-2},p^{-i})$, and set $J=L_0\cap g_iL_0$. Its displayed basis is $f_1=p^ie_1$, $f_j=e_j$ for $2\le j\le r$. Both $L_0/J$ and $g_iL_0/J$ are cyclic of order $p^i$. Set $m=\ell-i$. For a counted $M\subset J$, the subgroup $J/M$ of the cyclic group $L_0/M$ is cyclic of order $p^m$. Since $i>0$, $e_1$ maps to a generator of $L_0/J$ and therefore to a generator of the cyclic group $L_0/M$; hence $f_1=p^ie_1$ generates $J/M$. Applying the same reasoning to the generator $p^{-i}e_r$ of $g_iL_0/J$ shows that $f_r=e_r$ also generates $J/M$. Conversely, if $J/M$ is cyclic of order $p^m$ and both $f_1,f_r$ generate it, then $L_0/M$ is generated by $e_1$: every $f_j$ is a multiple of $f_1=p^ie_1$ modulo $M$. Its order is $p^{i+m}=p^\ell$ by the index formula. The identical argument with $p^{-i}e_r$ proves the other cyclic-quotient condition. If $m=0$, these conditions give the unique lattice $M=J$. If $m\ge1$, such kernels are represented uniquely by rows $J\to O/p^mO$ with value1 on $f_1$, arbitrary values on $f_2,\ldots,f_{r-1}$, and a unit value on $f_r$. Thus their number is $p^{(r-2)m}(p^m-p^{m-1})=p^{nm}(1-p^{-1})$. All parameters here run over explicitly finite residue sets. [F1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 At $D_\ell$ step 4.1 gives convolution value $p^{-n\ell}$; since $U_{p,\ell}$ has that same value there, its coefficient is1. At $D_i$ for $1\le i<\ell$, the value is $p^{-n\ell}p^{n(\ell-i)}(1-p^{-1})=p^{-ni}(1-p^{-1})$, so its coefficient is $1-p^{-1}$. Together with step 3.2 the explicit coefficients for $\ell\ge1$ are $c_{p,\ell,0}=\sum_{j=0}^np^{-j}$, $c_{p,\ell,i}=1-p^{-1}$ for $0<i<\ell$, and $c_{p,\ell,\ell}=1$. Equality holds on every support double coset, and both sides vanish elsewhere by step 3.1. Every coefficient is nonnegative and at most $n+1$, uniformly in the prime. [step 3.1, step 3.2, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 When $\ell=0$, both $T_{p,0}$ and its adjoint are $\mathbf1_K$. Their convolution is $\mathbf1_K$ because $K$ has volume1, so take $c_{p,0,0}=1$. This includes every allowed $0\le\ell\le n+1$ and proves the original Statement with $C_n=n+1$. No analytic spectral, test-vector or trace-formula input has been used. [given, step 5.1, algebra] ∎
