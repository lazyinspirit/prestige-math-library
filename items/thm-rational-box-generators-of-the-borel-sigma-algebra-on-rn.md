## Statement

Let $n\in\mathbb N$ with $n\ge1$. In the product topology on $\mathbb R^n$,
each of the following families generates $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$: all open
sets; all closed sets; all compact sets; all Euclidean open balls; all open
boxes; all rational open boxes; and all rational half-open boxes
$\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i]$ with rational endpoints $a_i<b_i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n\ge1$ and the product topology on $\mathbb R^n$.

[L1] The rational open boxes form a countable basis for the product topology on $\mathbb R^n$, and $\mathbb Q^n$ is countable and dense ([[thm-rational-points-and-boxes-in-rn]]).

[L2] In a metric topology, every point of an open set has an open ball contained in that set ([[def-metric-topology]]).

[L3] The product topology on $\mathbb R^n$ is the Euclidean metric topology, and a subset is compact if and only if it is closed and bounded ([[cor-heine-borel-in-the-product-topology]]).

[L4] The real field is a complete ordered field ([[cor-cauchy-reals-lub-complete]]), so every real number is below some positive natural number ([[thm-of-archimedean]]).

[L5] For every positive real $\varepsilon$, some positive natural number $m$ satisfies $1/m<\varepsilon$ ([[cor-archimedean-reciprocal]]).

[L6] Families that lie in each other's generated sigma-algebras generate the same sigma-algebra ([[thm-generated-sigma-algebra-comparison-criterion]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L1], every open set is the union of the subfamily of rational open boxes it contains, and that subfamily is countable. Thus the rational open boxes generate the open sets and hence $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$; all open boxes generate the same sigma-algebra. [L1]

1.2 By rational density in [L1], every rational open box is the union of the rational half-open boxes contained in it. Conversely, $\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i]=\bigcap_{m\ge1}\prod_{i<n}(a_i,b_i+1/m)$ by [L5], so rational open and rational half-open boxes lie in each other's generated sigma-algebras. [L1, L5, algebra]

1.3 Open and closed sets generate the same sigma-algebra by complementation. Euclidean balls with centres in $\mathbb Q^n$ and radii $1/m$ form a countable basis: for a ball of radius $r$ about $x$, use [L5] to choose $m$ with $2/m<r$, then use the density in [L1] to choose $q\in\mathbb Q^n$ with $d(x,q)<1/m$. Thus $x\in B(q,1/m)\subseteq B(x,r)$. By [L2] and [L3], every open set is therefore a countable union of open balls, while every open ball is open. [L1, L2, L3, L5]

2.1 By [L6], step 1.2 and step 1.1 identify the sigma-algebra generated by rational half-open boxes with $\mathcal B(\mathbb R^n)$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, L6]

3.1 By [L4], every closed $F\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ is $\bigcup_{m\ge1}(F\cap[-m,m]^n)$. Each term is closed and bounded, hence compact by [L3], while every compact set is closed. Therefore compact sets and closed sets generate the same sigma-algebra. Combining this with steps 1.1, 2.1, and 1.3 proves the claim for all displayed families. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.3, L3, L4, L6] ∎
