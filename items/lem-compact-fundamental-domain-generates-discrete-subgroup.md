## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $G$ be a Hausdorff topological group, let $G^\circ$ be the connected component of its identity, let $H\le G$ be a subgroup whose subspace topology is discrete, and let $F\subseteq G$ be compact with $G=FH$. Let $P\subseteq G$ contain the identity and suppose that $G$ is generated as a group by $P\cup G^\circ$. Then $H$ is generated by $H\cap F^{-1}PF$.

Here $F^{-1}PF=\{x^{-1}yz:x,z\in F,\ y\in P\}$, and “generated” means the subgroup generated by the indicated set.

## Statement references

[Lenstra, Lemma 6.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9204234)

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice and the Hausdorff topological group, discrete subgroup, compact set, and generation hypotheses in the Statement.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice supplies a representative for each member of the set of left cosets $G/H$ when the proof selects a transversal ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof



1.1 Choose an identity neighborhood $U_0$ with $U_0\cap H=\{1\}$. For any $g\in\overline H$, continuity gives a neighborhood $V$ of $1$ with $V^{-1}V\subseteq U_0$. Any two elements of $gV\cap H$ have quotient in $V^{-1}V\cap H$, so $gV\cap H$ has at most one element. If $g\notin H$, let $h$ be that possible element; Hausdorffness gives a smaller neighborhood of $g$ inside $gV$ that avoids $h$. It then misses $H$, contradicting $g\in\overline H$. Hence $H$ is closed. [given, algebra]

2.1 The set $F^{-1}F$ is compact, so its intersection with the closed discrete set $H$ is finite. Set $O=(G\setminus H)\cup(H\cap F^{-1}F)$. This is open: $G\setminus H$ is open by step 1.1, and for each of the finitely many added points $h\in H$, discreteness gives a neighborhood $W_h$ with $W_h\cap H=\{h\}$; then $W_h\subseteq O$. The set $O$ contains $F^{-1}F$. [step 1.1, given]

3.1 For each $(x,z)\in F\times F$, the point $x^{-1}z$ lies in $O$ by step 2.1. Continuity of $(x,u,z)\mapsto x^{-1}uz$ gives neighborhoods $V_{x,z}$ of $x$, $W_{x,z}$ of $z$, and $U_{x,z}$ of $1$ such that $V_{x,z}^{-1}U_{x,z}W_{x,z}\subseteq O$. Compactness of $F\times F$ gives finitely many rectangles $V_{x,z}\times W_{x,z}$ covering it; intersect their corresponding identity neighborhoods and their inverses to obtain a symmetric identity neighborhood $U$ with $F^{-1}UF\subseteq O$. Since $1\in U$, $F^{-1}F\subseteq F^{-1}UF$, and the definition of $O$ then gives $H\cap F^{-1}UF=H\cap F^{-1}F$. [step 2.1, given]

4.1 The subgroup generated by the symmetric open identity neighborhood $U$ is open, hence also closed, so it contains the connected component $G^\circ$. Thus $G$ is generated by $P\cup U$. Using [A1], choose a subset $F'\subseteq F$ containing exactly one representative of each right coset $gH$; write $f_H$ for its representative of the coset $H$. [A1, step 3.1, given, choose]

5.1 Let $J$ be generated by $H\cap F^{-1}(P\cup U)F$. For $y\in P\cup U$ and $z\in F'$, let $x\in F'$ represent $yzH$ and write $yz=xh$. Then $h=x^{-1}yz\in J$, so $yF'\subseteq F'J$. For $y\in P$, the map $zH\mapsto yzH$ is a bijection of the right cosets. Therefore, for every $z\in F'$, there is $z'\in F'$ whose image is $zH$; writing $yz'=zh$ with $h\in J$ gives $y^{-1}z=z'h^{-1}\in F'J$. The same inverse stability holds for $U$ because $U=U^{-1}$. Since $J$ is a subgroup, $F'J$ is stable under left multiplication by every generator and inverse in $P\cup U$, and it is nonempty. As these elements generate $G$, this implies $F'J=G$. [step 4.1, given, algebra]

6.1 If $h\in H$, write $h=xj$ with $x\in F'$ and $j\in J\subseteq H$. Then $xH=hH=H$, so $x=f_H$ and $h=f_Hj$. Because $f_H\in H$, left multiplication by $f_H^{-1}$ maps $H$ onto itself; hence every element of $H$ lies in $J$, and $H=J$. By step 3.1, $H\cap F^{-1}UF=H\cap F^{-1}F\subseteq H\cap F^{-1}PF$ because $1\in P$. Therefore $J$ is generated already by $H\cap F^{-1}PF$, proving the claim. [step 3.1, step 5.1, given] ∎
