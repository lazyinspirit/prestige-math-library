## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $R\to B$ be a ring map, let $A\subseteq B$
be the integral closure of the image of $R$ in $B$, and let $E$ be an étale
$R$-algebra. The natural map $E\otimes_R A\to E\otimes_R B$ is injective,
and its image is exactly the integral closure of the image of $E$ in
$E\otimes_R B$. No reducedness, normality, or Noetherian hypothesis is
required.

The standard-étale chart calculation below and its passage to arbitrary
étale algebras use the locally standard-étale theorem
[[thm-etale-locally-standard-etale]].

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** The ring maps and integral closure in the Statement.

[F1] Étale algebras are flat; tensoring an injection with a flat module
preserves injectivity ([[thm-flatness-criteria-by-injections-and-ideals]],
[[lem-ag-standard-smooth-flatness]]). Integral elements form a subring, and
an algebra generated by finitely many integral elements is module-finite
([[cor-integral-elements-form-a-subring]],
[[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

[F2] On an open cover of $\operatorname{Spec}E$, an étale map is standard
étale: after localizing the base and source it has a presentation
$C=(R[T]/(P))_g$ with $P$ monic and $P'$ invertible in $C$
([[thm-etale-locally-standard-etale]], [[def-standard-etale-algebra]]).
The local monic presentation is proved in the cited theorem.

[F3] Integral closure commutes with localization, so localization of $A$
is the integral closure of the localized base in the localized $B$
([[thm-integrality-commutes-with-localisation]]).

[F4] AC is the choice-function axiom ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).



## Proof

**Proof technique:** a standard-étale coefficient calculation followed by
localization and descent from a principal open cover.

1.1 Because $E$ is flat over $R$, [F1] makes $E\otimes_R A\to E\otimes_R B$ injective, and we identify its source with its image. Every element of $A$ is integral over the image of $R$, so every element of the algebra $E\otimes_R A$ is integral over $E$: each is a finite sum of products of elements $1\otimes a$ satisfying monic equations over $E$, and integral elements form a subring by [F1]. This proves one inclusion. [F1]

1.2 For the converse first take a monic polynomial $P\in R[T]$ of degree $d\ge1$, put $C=R[T]/(P)$ and $D=B[T]/(P)$, and let $c\in D$ be integral over $C$. As $C$ is finite free over $R$ with basis $1,T,\ldots,T^{d-1}$, it is integral over $R$; transitivity of integrality makes $c$ integral over $R$. Write uniquely $c=\sum_{j<d}b_jT^j$ with $b_j\in B$. The claim needed for étale charts is that every coefficient of $P'(T)c$ reduced modulo $P$ belongs to $A$. [F1, F2]

2.1 Construct the splitting algebra $B'$ of $P$ by adjoining one formal root at a time: quotient successively by the current monic polynomial, divide by $(T-\alpha_i)$, and continue. Each quotient is free over its predecessor with a basis containing $1$, so the composite $B\to B'$ is injective. In $B'[T]$ one has $P(T)=\prod_{i=1}^d(T-\alpha_i)$, with each $\alpha_i$ integral over $R$. For every polynomial $h(T)$ of degree less than $d$ the identity $$P'(T)h(T)\equiv\sum_{i=1}^d h(\alpha_i)\prod_{j\ne i}(T-\alpha_j)\pmod{P(T)}$$ holds without distinct-root or denominator assumptions: it is an identity over the polynomial ring in independent formal roots, where it follows by checking the degree-less-than-$d$ interpolation basis, and polynomial specialization preserves it. Apply it to the representative of $c$. Since $c$ is integral over $R$, every evaluation $c(\alpha_i)$ is integral over $R$; each coefficient on the right is a sum of products of these evaluations and the integral roots $\alpha_i$, hence integral over $R$ by [F1]. The left side has its reduced coefficients in the injected subring $B\subseteq B'$, so those coefficients lie in $A$ by the definition of $A$. Thus $P'c\in A[T]/(P)$ inside $D$. [F1, step 1.2]

3.1 Now let $C_g=(R[T]/(P))_g$ be a standard étale algebra, so $P'$ is a unit in $C_g$ by [F2], and let $b\in D_g$ be integral over $C_g$. Clearing the finitely many denominators in a monic equation for $b$ gives an integer $N$ and an element $c=g^N b\in D$ integral over $C$; this is the localization property [F3] applied to the relative integral closure. Step 2.1 gives $P'c\in A[T]/(P)$. Since $g$ and $P'$ are units in $C_g$, both $g^{-N}$ and $(P')^{-1}$ belong to $C_g$, so $b=g^{-N}(P')^{-1}(P'c)$ lies in the image of $C_g\otimes_R A\to C_g\otimes_R B$. Thus the converse inclusion holds for every standard étale chart. [F2, F3, step 2.1]

4.1 For general $E$, use the standard-étale affine neighbourhoods of [F2] and the base localization compatibility [F3]. On each such neighbourhood, step 3.1 proves that any element integral over $E$ lies in the localized image of $E\otimes_R A$. For each integral element $b$, its class in the quotient module $(E\otimes_R B)/(E\otimes_R A)$ vanishes after localization at every prime on that cover, so that class is zero globally. Together with step 1.1 this proves equality. If $E=0$ or $B=0$, both sides are zero and the same argument is vacuous. AC is inherited only through [F2] and [F3]; the finite splitting construction uses no further choice. The cited local standard-étale theorem supplies [F2], completing the general case. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
