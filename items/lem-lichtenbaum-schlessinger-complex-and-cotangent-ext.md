## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the resolution comparisons
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $A\to B$ be a map of commutative unital rings,
choose a polynomial presentation $R=A[x]\to B$ with kernel $I$
([[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]]), a free $R$-module $F$
surjecting onto $I$ with kernel $Q$, and let $F_0\subseteq Q$ be the submodule
generated by the Koszul relations $j(a)b-j(b)a$. Then the
Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger complex
$$L_\bullet\colon Q/F_0\xrightarrow{d_2}F\otimes_RB\xrightarrow{d_1}\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB$$
is canonically quasi-isomorphic to the truncation $\tau_{\ge-2}L_{B/A}$ of the
cotangent complex ([[def-cotangent-complex-of-a-ring-map]],
[[def-canonical-truncation-of-a-complex]], [[def-quasi-isomorphism]]).
Consequently, for every $B$-module $M$ and $i=0,1,2$, the groups
$T^i(B/A,M)=H^i(\operatorname{Hom}_B(L_\bullet,M))$ of
Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger are canonically isomorphic to
$\operatorname{Ext}^i_B(L_{B/A},M)$
([[def-ext-groups-of-the-cotangent-complex]]), with the explicit presentations
$$T^1=\operatorname{coker}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_B(\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(I/I^2,M)\bigr),\qquad T^2=\operatorname{coker}\bigl(\operatorname{Hom}_B(F\otimes_RB,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(Q/F_0,M)\bigr),$$
and the connecting maps for a short exact sequence of coefficients agree. In
particular first-order deformations and obstructions computed from a
presentation are invariantly the $\operatorname{Ext}^1$ and
$\operatorname{Ext}^2$ of $L_{B/A}$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** a ring map $A\to B$, a polynomial presentation $R=A[x]\to B$ with kernel $I$, a free $R$-module $F$ surjecting onto $I$ with kernel $Q$, the Koszul submodule $F_0\subseteq Q$, a $B$-module $M$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[F1] $Q/F_0$ is a $B$-module under the natural action, $F\otimes_RB$ and $\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB$ are $B$-modules with the latter two free, and $d_1,d_2$ are well defined with $d_1d_2=0$; the construction is that of Hartshorne Construction 3.1. ([[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]], [[def-derivation-algebra]], [[def-polynomial-ring-on-a-family-of-indeterminates]], [[thm-kahler-differentials-existence-presentation]])

[F2] The Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger complex is canonically independent of the choices of $R$ and $F$, and admits a canonical map $L_{B/A}\to L_\bullet$ in $D(B)$ inducing an isomorphism $\tau_{\ge-2}L_{B/A}\to L_\bullet$. ([[def-cotangent-complex-of-a-ring-map]], [[lem-cotangent-complex-resolution-independence]], [[def-standard-resolution-of-a-ring-map]], [[def-canonical-truncation-of-a-complex]])

[F3] $\operatorname{Ext}^i_B(L_{B/A},M)=H^i(\mathbf R\operatorname{Hom}_B(L_{B/A},M))$, and for a complex concentrated in degrees $\le0$ the groups $\operatorname{Ext}^0,\operatorname{Ext}^1,\operatorname{Ext}^2$ depend only on the truncation $\tau_{\ge-2}$. ([[def-ext-groups-of-the-cotangent-complex]], [[def-canonical-truncation-of-a-complex]])

[F4] The conormal sequence of the presentation identifies $I/I^2$ with the cokernel of $Q\otimes_RB\to F\otimes_RB$, and $\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB$ is the degree-$0$ term of the truncated complex. ([[thm-conormal-exact-sequence-algebra]], [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** build the Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger complex, compare it with the truncation of the cotangent complex, and compute its derived Hom in degrees at most two using that its degree $0$ and $-1$ terms are free.

1.1 The complex $L_\bullet$ is well defined: $F_0\subseteq Q$ because each Koszul relation maps to zero in $I$. Write $F=\bigoplus_tRe_t$ with $j(e_t)=f_t$. For $q=\sum_sq_se_s\in Q$ one has $\sum_sq_sf_s=0$, hence $f_tq=\sum_sq_s(f_te_s-f_se_t)\in F_0$. Since the $f_t$ generate $I$, the ideal $I$ annihilates $Q/F_0$, making it a $B$-module; the map $d_2$ is induced by the inclusion $Q\hookrightarrow F$, and $d_1$ is the composite $F\otimes_RB\to I/I^2\to\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB$ of the conormal presentation with the universal derivation; $d_1d_2=0$ because the first arrow lands in $I/I^2$ and the second vanishes on the image of $Q$ by [F4]. All three terms are $B$-modules and the last two are free, as recalled in [F1]. This is Hartshorne's construction of the Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger complex. [F1, F4, given]

1.2 The comparison with the cotangent complex: by [F2] there is a canonical map $L_{B/A}\to L_\bullet$ inducing an isomorphism $\tau_{\ge-2}L_{B/A}\to L_\bullet$ in $D(B)$; this is the content of the Lichtenbaum-Schlessinger comparison theorem (Stacks, *The Cotangent Complex*, tag 09CG, and Hartshorne Chapter 1 Section 3 for the independence of choices). The exact comparison theorem is applied from the cited full chapter, Section 13 (tag 09CG); its hypotheses are precisely the polynomial presentation and free relation presentation of the Statement. [F2, given]

2.1 Computing $\operatorname{Ext}$. Put $V=\Omega_{R/A}\otimes_RB$, $U=F\otimes_RB$, and $W=Q/F_0$. The complex $L_\bullet$ has terms $W,U,V$ in degrees $-2,-1,0$. Its ordinary Hom complex has the direction $\operatorname{Hom}_B(V,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(W,M)$. To justify its use although $W$ need not be projective, resolve $M$ injectively by $J^\bullet$ and form $\operatorname{Hom}_B(L_\bullet,J^\bullet)$. The vertical higher cohomology for $U$ and $V$ vanishes because they are free; for $W$ it can contribute only in total degree at least $3$. Thus in total degrees $0,1,2$ this derived Hom has the cohomology of the displayed ordinary Hom complex. The cokernel of $W\to U$ is $I/I^2$ by tensoring the presentation $Q\to F\to I\to0$ with $B$: the image of $Q$ agrees with the image of $W$. Consequently the kernel of $\operatorname{Hom}_B(U,M)\to\operatorname{Hom}_B(W,M)$ is $\operatorname{Hom}_B(I/I^2,M)$, giving the displayed $T^1$ formula; the degree-$2$ cokernel is the displayed $T^2$ formula. Truncation [F3] and step 1.2 identify these groups with $\operatorname{Ext}^i_B(L_{B/A},M)$ for $i\le2$. This identification is natural in $M$, and the connecting maps are those from the derived Hom construction with injective resolutions, so they agree in the stated degrees. [F1, F3, F4, step 1.2, algebra] ∎

**Source application.** Step 1.2 uses the exact cited Stacks comparison, tag 09CG, whose construction and cohomology comparison were read in full. The final computation proves separately why ordinary Hom computes the three lowest Ext groups despite the possibly nonprojective degree $-2$ term.
