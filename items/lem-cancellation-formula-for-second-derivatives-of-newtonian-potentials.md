## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $n\ge2$ and let $\Phi$ be the fundamental solution normalised by $-\Delta\Phi=\delta_0$ on $\mathbb R^n$ ([[def-laplace-fundamental-solution-with-positive-minus-laplacian-sign]]); for $z\ne0$ put $k_{ij}(z):=\partial_i\partial_j\Phi(z)$ and write $V_{ij}f:=\mathrm{p.v.}(k_{ij}*f)$ as in [[def-calderon-zygmund-kernel-and-principal-value-operator]]. Then:

(i) $k_{ij}$ is smooth off $0$, homogeneous of degree $-n$, satisfies $|k_{ij}(z)|\le C_n|z|^{-n}$ and $|\nabla k_{ij}(z)|\le C_n|z|^{-n-1}$, and hence $|k_{ij}(z-h)-k_{ij}(z)|\le C_n|h||z|^{-n-1}$ whenever $|z|\ge2|h|$; its mean over every centred sphere is zero, so it is a standard Hölder Calderón–Zygmund kernel in the sense of [[def-standard-holder-calderon-zygmund-kernel]].

(ii) For every $f\in C^{0,\alpha}_c(\mathbb R^n)$ with $0<\alpha\le1$, $Nf$ has classical second derivatives and, for every $x\in\mathbb R^n$,
$$\partial_i\partial_jNf(x)=\mathrm{p.v.}\!\int_{\mathbb R^n} k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\,dy-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}f(x)=\int_{|x-y|<1}k_{ij}(x-y)(f(y)-f(x))\,dy+\int_{|x-y|\ge1}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\,dy-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}f(x).$$
Both displayed ordinary integrals converge absolutely: the first by Hölder continuity at the singularity, the second because it avoids the singularity and $f$ is compactly supported. The local subtraction is only over the unit ball; the globally subtracted integrand $k_{ij}(x-y)(f(y)-f(x))$ is not absolutely integrable over $\mathbb R^n$ when $f(x)\ne0$, since $k_{ij}(r\theta)=r^{-n}k_{ij}(\theta)$, its nonzero continuous angular factor has positive spherical $L^1$ norm, and $\int_1^\infty r^{-1}dr=\infty$, so no global Lebesgue integral replaces the principal value.

(iii) As a tempered distribution, $D_{ij}\Phi=\mathrm{p.v.}\,k_{ij}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\delta_0$; consequently, for every $f\in L^\infty_c(\mathbb R^n)$, the distributional second derivative of $Nf$ is given by the truncated pairings,
$$\partial_i\partial_jNf=\mathrm{p.v.}(k_{ij}*f)-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}f\qquad\text{in }\mathcal D'(\mathbb R^n),$$
that is, $\langle\partial_i\partial_jNf,\varphi\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\iint_{|x-y|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\varphi(x)\,dy\,dx-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\int f\varphi$ for every $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, an integer $n\ge2$, the fundamental solution $\Phi$ with the profiles below, the kernel $k_{ij}=\partial_i\partial_j\Phi$ on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$, and a continuous compactly supported $f$ with finite global Hölder seminorm $[f]_{0,\alpha;\mathbb R^n}<\infty$ for a fixed $0<\alpha\le1$.

[A1] The only choice assumption is Countable Choice $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$; it enters through the choice-qualified measure, polar, surface, divergence, potential and distribution interfaces cited below. No full Axiom of Choice is used. ([[def-countable-choice]])

[F1] For $n\ge3$, $\Phi(x)=|x|^{2-n}/((n-2)\omega_{n-1})$; for $n=2$, $\Phi(x)=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|x|$, $x\ne0$; $\Phi$ is locally integrable on $\mathbb R^n$ with the pole assigned arbitrarily. Here $\omega_{n-1}=|S^{n-1}|$. ([[def-laplace-fundamental-solution-with-positive-minus-laplacian-sign]], [[def-euclidean-spheres-and-closed-balls]])

[F2] On $(0,\infty)$, $(s^\gamma)'=\gamma s^{\gamma-1}$ for real $\gamma$ and $(\log s)'=1/s$; the chain rule and the algebra of derivatives compute derivatives of the radial profiles and of products; and the mean value theorem bounds the increment of a differentiable function on a segment by the supremum of its derivative times the segment length. ([[def-real-power]], [[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]], [[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-total-derivatives]], [[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]], [[cor-mean-value-theorem]], [[def-ck-and-multi-index-notation-in-several-variables]])

[F3] Polar coordinates: $\int_{\mathbb R^n}g\,d\lambda_n=\int_0^\infty\int_{S^{n-1}}g(r\theta)r^{n-1}\,d\sigma(\theta)\,dr$; the surface measure $\sigma$ is preserved by orthogonal transformations and the map $\theta\mapsto a+R\theta$ multiplies it by $R^{n-1}$; $\sigma(S^{n-1})=\omega_{n-1}=n|B_1|$. ([[thm-polar-coordinates-formula-for-lebesgue-measure]], [[lem-euclidean-chart-measure-agrees-with-polar-surface-measure]])

[F4] The divergence theorem $\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}F\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}F\cdot\nu\,dS$ holds for bounded $C^1$ Euclidean domains and $F\in C^1(\overline\Omega;\mathbb R^n)$, with $\nu$ the outward normal. ([[thm-divergence-theorem-for-bounded-c-one-euclidean-domains]])

[F5] A distribution on an open set with support contained in $\{0\}$ equals a finite combination $\sum_{|\gamma|\le m}c_\gamma\partial^\gamma\delta_0$; in particular the functional $\varphi\mapsto\varphi(0)$ generates the one-dimensional space of distributions with support $\{0\}$ that are invariant under all such combinations of order zero, so a distribution pairing $\varphi$ to exactly $\pm(\delta_{ij}/n)\varphi(0)$ is $\pm(\delta_{ij}/n)\delta_0$. ([[thm-distributions-supported-at-one-point]])

[F6] For bounded compactly supported data the Newtonian integral $Nf(x)=\int\Phi(x-y)f(y)\,dy$ is absolutely finite at every $x$ and $Nf$ is locally bounded. ([[def-newtonian-potential]], [[lem-newtonian-potential-is-well-defined-for-compactly-supported-bounded-data]])

[F7] If $0<\beta<1$, $g\in C_c^{0,\beta}(\mathbb R^n)$ and $Ng$ is its Newtonian potential, then $Ng\in C^2(\mathbb R^n)$, $-\Delta Ng=g$ pointwise, and for every $x$ and every $r>0$ with $\operatorname{supp}g\subset B_r(x)$, $$\partial_i\partial_jNg(x)=\int_{B_r(x)}\partial_i\partial_j\Phi(x-y)\bigl(g(y)-g(x)\bigr)\,dy-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}g(x).$$ ([[thm-newtonian-potential-for-holder-data-is-classical]])

[F8] Here, for $0<\alpha\le1$, $C^{0,\alpha}_c(\mathbb R^n)$ denotes the continuous compactly supported functions with finite global seminorm $[f]_{0,\alpha;\mathbb R^n}:=\sup_{x\ne y}|f(x)-f(y)|/|x-y|^{\alpha}$, and $\|f\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}:=\sup|f|+[f]_{0,\alpha;\mathbb R^n}$. For $0<\alpha<1$ this is the convention in [[thm-newtonian-potential-for-holder-data-is-classical]]; $\alpha=1$ means Lipschitz continuity.

[F9] A Calderón–Zygmund kernel with constants $A_1,A_2$ satisfies the annular size condition $\sup_{R>0}\int_{R\le|x|\le2R}|k|\le A_1$ and Hörmander's condition $\sup_{y\ne0}\int_{|x|\ge2|y|}|k(x-y)-k(x)|\,dx\le A_2$; it is standard $\delta$-Hölder with constant $A_2'$ when additionally $|k(x-y)-k(x)|\le A_2'|y|^{\delta}/|x|^{n+\delta}$ for $|x|\ge2|y|>0$. A principal-value distribution for $k$ is a tempered distribution agreeing with $k$ off the origin and obtained as a truncation limit over some sequence $\delta_j\downarrow0$. ([[def-calderon-zygmund-kernel-and-principal-value-operator]], [[def-standard-holder-calderon-zygmund-kernel]])

[F10] For a function on a product of $\sigma$-finite measure spaces that is integrable with respect to the product measure, the iterated integrals exist and agree (Fubini). ([[thm-fubini-theorem-for-l-one-on-sigma-finite-product-spaces]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $r=|z|$ for $z\ne0$. Differentiating the profiles of [F1] with the rules of [F2] gives, in both cases $n\ge3$ and $n=2$, $$\partial_i\Phi(z)=-\frac{z_i}{\omega_{n-1}r^n},\qquad k_{ij}(z)=\partial_i\partial_j\Phi(z)=-\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{r^n}\Bigl(\delta_{ij}-n\frac{z_iz_j}{r^2}\Bigr).$$ Both formulas are smooth in $z\ne0$, and $k_{ij}(\lambda z)=\lambda^{-n}k_{ij}(z)$ for every $\lambda>0$, so $k_{ij}$ is homogeneous of degree $-n$. [given, F1, F2, algebra, A1]

2.1 Estimates. Directly from step 1.1, $|k_{ij}(z)|\le (1+n)\omega_{n-1}^{-1}r^{-n}$. Since $k_{ij}$ is smooth and homogeneous of degree $-n$ on $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$, each partial derivative $\partial_lk_{ij}$ is homogeneous of degree $-n-1$, and its supremum over the compact unit sphere is a finite constant $C_{n,j,l}$, so $|\nabla k_{ij}(z)|\le C_n r^{-n-1}$. If $|z|\ge2|h|$, every point of the segment from $z-h$ to $z$ has norm at least $r/2$, so the mean value theorem bound in [F2], applied along that segment, gives $|k_{ij}(z-h)-k_{ij}(z)|\le C_n|h|(r/2)^{-n-1}$, which is the stated pointwise estimate with a constant doubled to the power $n+1$. [step 1.1, F2, algebra]

2.2 Zero spherical means. On the unit sphere $k_{ij}(\theta)=-\omega_{n-1}^{-1}(\delta_{ij}-n\theta_i\theta_j)$. By [F3] the measure $\sigma$ is invariant under coordinate reflections, so $\int\theta_i\theta_j\,d\sigma=0$ for $i\ne j$; it is also invariant under coordinate permutations, so all $n$ numbers $\int\theta_i^2d\sigma$ are equal, and $\sum_i\theta_i^2=1$ gives $\int\theta_i^2d\sigma=\omega_{n-1}/n$. Hence $\int_{S^{n-1}}\theta_i\theta_j\,d\sigma=\delta_{ij}\omega_{n-1}/n$ and therefore $\int_{S^{n-1}}k_{ij}(\theta)\,d\sigma(\theta)=-\omega_{n-1}^{-1}(\delta_{ij}\omega_{n-1}-n\delta_{ij}\omega_{n-1}/n)=0$. By the homogeneity of step 1.1 the mean over every centred sphere vanishes: $\int_{S(0,\rho)}k_{ij}\,dS=\rho^{n-1}\rho^{-n}\int_{S^{n-1}}k_{ij}(\theta)d\sigma=0$. [step 1.1, F3, algebra]

3.1 $k_{ij}$ is a standard Hölder Calderón–Zygmund kernel. The annular size condition follows from step 2.1 and [F3]: $\int_{R\le|z|\le2R}|k_{ij}|\,dz=\int_R^{2R}\rho^{-1}d\rho\int_{S^{n-1}}|k_{ij}(\theta)|\,d\sigma\le (1+n)\log 2=:A_1$. Hörmander's condition follows from the difference estimate of step 2.1 and polar integration: for $y\ne0$, $\int_{|x|\ge2|y|}|k_{ij}(x-y)-k_{ij}(x)|\,dx\le C_n|y|\int_{2|y|}^\infty\rho^{-n-1}\omega_{n-1}\rho^{n-1}d\rho=C_n\omega_{n-1}/2=:A_2$. The pointwise estimate of step 2.1 is exactly condition (1) of [F9] with $\delta=1$ and constant $C_n2^{n+1}$, so $k_{ij}$ is standard $1$-Hölder in the sense of [[def-standard-holder-calderon-zygmund-kernel]]. [step 2.1, step 2.2, F3, F9, algebra]

3.2 The principal value exists. Let $\varphi\in\mathcal S(\mathbb R^n)$ and $0<\varepsilon<1$. Using the vanishing spherical means of step 2.2 on the annulus, $$\int_{|z|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(z)\varphi(z)\,dz=\int_{\varepsilon\le|z|<1}k_{ij}(z)\bigl(\varphi(z)-\varphi(0)\bigr)\,dz+\int_{|z|\ge1}k_{ij}(z)\varphi(z)\,dz.$$ The second integral is absolutely convergent because $|k_{ij}(z)\varphi(z)|\le C_n|z|^{-n}|\varphi(z)|$ and $\varphi$ is Schwartz, and the first integrand is dominated by $C_n\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty|z|^{1-n}$, which is integrable on $|z|<1$ for $n\ge2$; hence the first integral converges as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ by dominated convergence. Therefore $W_{ij}(\varphi):=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\int_{|z|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(z)\varphi(z)\,dz$ exists for every Schwartz $\varphi$; the bound $|W_{ij}(\varphi)|\le C_n(\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty+\sup_z(1+|z|)^2|\varphi(z)|)$ proves continuity in Schwartz seminorms, so the map is a tempered distribution agreeing with $k_{ij}$ off the origin, and taking $\delta_j=1/j$ exhibits it as a principal-value distribution for $k_{ij}$ in the sense of [F9]. [step 2.2, F3, F9, algebra]

3.3 Classical formula for Hölder data. If $0<\alpha<1$ put $\beta:=\alpha$, and if $\alpha=1$ put $\beta:=1/2$; $f\in C_c^{0,\beta}(\mathbb R^n)$ in either case: for $\alpha=1$, if $|x-y|\le1$ then $|f(x)-f(y)|/|x-y|^\beta\le[f]_{0,1}$, while if $|x-y|>1$ it is at most $2\|f\|_\infty$. Thus $[f]_{0,\beta}\le\max\{[f]_{0,1},2\|f\|_\infty\}<\infty$, so [F7] gives $Nf\in C^2(\mathbb R^n)$ together with the ball formula. Fix $x\in\mathbb R^n$ and choose $r>1$ with $\operatorname{supp}f\subset B_r(x)$; substituting $z=x-y$ and splitting $\{|z|<r\}$ into $\{|z|<1\}$ and $\{1\le|z|<r\}$ gives $$\int_{B_r(x)}k_{ij}(x-y)\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)dy=\int_{|x-y|<1}k_{ij}(x-y)\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)dy+\int_{|x-y|\ge1}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\,dy,$$ because the pure multiple term $-f(x)\int_{1\le|z|<r}k_{ij}(z)dz$ vanishes by the annular zero-mean property of step 2.2, while the remaining $\{1\le|z|<r\}$-integral equals the integral over $\{|z|\ge1\}$: indeed $f(x+z)=0$ for $|z|\ge r$, so the two extended integrands agree almost everywhere. Hence $\partial_i\partial_jNf(x)=\int_{|x-y|<1}k_{ij}(x-y)(f(y)-f(x))dy+\int_{|x-y|\ge1}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)dy-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}f(x)$ for the fixed $x$. [step 2.2, F7, F8, algebra]

3.4 The principal value and the two integrals. With $0<\varepsilon<1$, the vanishing of $\int_{\varepsilon\le|x-y|<1}k_{ij}(x-y)dy$ by step 2.2 gives $$\int_{|x-y|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)dy=\int_{\varepsilon\le|x-y|<1}k_{ij}(x-y)\bigl(f(y)-f(x)\bigr)dy+\int_{|x-y|\ge1}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\,dy.$$ The first integrand is bounded by $C_n[f]_{0,\alpha;\mathbb R^n}|x-y|^{\alpha-n}$, whose integral over $|x-y|<1$ is finite because $\alpha-n>-n$, so dominated convergence lets $\varepsilon\downarrow0$ pass to the absolutely convergent first integral of the statement; the other integral is absolutely convergent because $|k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)|\le C_n|f(y)|$ on $|x-y|\ge1$ and $f$ is integrable. Hence the principal value $\mathrm{p.v.}\int k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)dy$ exists for every $x$ and equals the sum of the two integrals of the displayed formula. [step 2.2, F3, F8, algebra]

4.1 Distributional identity. Let $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$ and choose $R$ with $\operatorname{supp}\varphi\subset B_{R/2}(0)$. For $0<\varepsilon<R/2$ apply the divergence theorem [F4] on the bounded $C^1$ domain $\Omega_\varepsilon:=\{\varepsilon<|x|<R\}$ to the field $F:=\Phi\partial_j\varphi\,e_i-\varphi\,\partial_i\Phi\,e_j$. Since $\operatorname{div}F=\partial_i(\Phi\partial_j\varphi)-\partial_j(\varphi\partial_i\Phi)=\Phi\partial_i\partial_j\varphi-k_{ij}\varphi$ and $F=0$ near $|x|=R$, while $\nu=-x/|x|$ on $|x|=\varepsilon$, $$\int_{\Omega_\varepsilon}\Phi\,\partial_i\partial_j\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega_\varepsilon}k_{ij}\varphi\,dx+\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}\Bigl(\Phi\,\partial_j\varphi\,\nu_i-\varphi\,\partial_i\Phi\,\nu_j\Bigr)dS.$$ On $|x|=\varepsilon$ the first boundary term tends to $0$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$, because $\Phi=O(\varepsilon^{2-n})$ or $O(\log(1/\varepsilon))$ and $dS$ contributes $\varepsilon^{n-1}$; the second boundary term is $-\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}\varphi\,\partial_i\Phi\,\nu_j\,dS$, and by step 1.1, the outward normal $\nu=-x/\varepsilon$ and the scaling rule of [F3], $$\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}\varphi\,\partial_i\Phi\,\nu_j\,dS=\frac{1}{\omega_{n-1}\varepsilon^{n+1}}\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}\varphi(x)\,x_ix_j\,dS(x)\longrightarrow\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\varphi(0),$$ because $\int_{|x|=\varepsilon}\varphi\,x_ix_j\,dS=\varepsilon^{n+1}\int_{S^{n-1}}\varphi(\varepsilon\theta)\theta_i\theta_j\,d\sigma(\theta)$ and $\varphi(\varepsilon\theta)=\varphi(0)+O(\varepsilon)$. Hence the boundary integral in the identity tends to $-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\varphi(0)$. Passing to the limit, using local integrability of $\Phi$ on the left and step 3.2 on the right, gives $\langle\partial_i\partial_j\Phi,\varphi\rangle=\langle\Phi,\partial_i\partial_j\varphi\rangle=W_{ij}(\varphi)-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\varphi(0)$; by [F5] this is the distributional identity $D_{ij}\Phi=\mathrm{p.v.}\,k_{ij}-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\delta_0$. [step 1.1, step 2.2, step 3.2, F3, F4, F5, algebra]

5.1 Assembly. Combining steps 3.3 and 3.4 gives, for every $x\in\mathbb R^n$, $\partial_i\partial_jNf(x)=\mathrm{p.v.}\int k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)dy-(\delta_{ij}/n)f(x)$, and the two ordinary integrals in the equivalent displayed form converge absolutely by step 3.4; step 3.3 supplies the classical second derivatives. The subtraction of $f(x)$ is made only on the unit ball: the globally subtracted integrand obeys $|k_{ij}(x-y)(f(y)-f(x))|\ge\frac12|f(x)|\,|k_{ij}(x-y)|$ for all $y$ outside a large ball when $f(x)\ne0$, and $\int_{|z|\ge1}|k_{ij}(z)|dz=\int_1^\infty\rho^{-1}d\rho\int_{S^{n-1}}|k_{ij}(\theta)|d\sigma=\infty$ by [F3] and step 1.1, so no absolutely convergent Lebesgue integral over $\mathbb R^n$ can replace the principal value; the annular cancellation of step 2.2 is what makes the truncations converge. The case $\alpha=1$ was reduced to $\beta=1/2<\alpha$ in step 3.3, where higher Hölder regularity than assumed is irrelevant to the formula; the positivity of $\alpha$ makes $|z|^{\alpha-n}$ integrable at the origin in step 3.4. All constructions are pointwise in $x$ and use only Countable Choice in the cited measure, polar, potential and distribution interfaces. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 3.3, step 3.4, F3, given]

6.1 Distributional form for bounded compact data. Let $f\in L^\infty_c(\mathbb R^n)$ and $\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R^n)$. The double integral $\iint\Phi(x-y)f(y)\partial_i\partial_j\varphi(x)\,dy\,dx$ is absolutely convergent: the integrand is supported in a bounded subset of $\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^n$ and is dominated there by a constant times $|\Phi(x-y)|$, and $\Phi$ is locally integrable. Fubini [F10] and the substitution $z=x-y$ therefore give $\langle D_{ij}Nf,\varphi\rangle=\int f(y)\langle\Phi,D_{ij}\varphi(\cdot+y)\rangle\,dy=\int f(y)\langle D_{ij}\Phi,\varphi(\cdot+y)\rangle\,dy$, the last equality being the definition of the distributional derivative. Inserting the kernel identity proved in step 4.1, $\langle D_{ij}Nf,\varphi\rangle=\int f(y)\bigl[W_{ij}(\varphi(\cdot+y))-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\varphi(y)\bigr]dy$. For $y$ in the compact support of $f$, subtract $\varphi(y)$ on $\varepsilon\le|z|<1$ using step 2.2. The resulting near integrand is bounded by $C_n\|\nabla\varphi\|_\infty|z|^{1-n}$; the far integral is bounded by $C_n\|\varphi\|_\infty\int_{1\le|z|\le R_0}|z|^{-n}dz$, where one $R_0>1$ contains all differences of the two supports. These bounds are uniform in $y$ and $\varepsilon$; multiplying by $|f(y)|$ gives an integrable majorant on its compact support. Dominated convergence now passes the truncation limit through the $y$ integral; another application of Fubini [F10] (the truncated double integral is absolutely convergent on the bounded region) gives $\int f(y)W_{ij}(\varphi(\cdot+y))\,dy=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\iint_{|x-y|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\varphi(x)\,dy\,dx$. Hence $\langle\partial_i\partial_jNf,\varphi\rangle=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow0}\iint_{|x-y|\ge\varepsilon}k_{ij}(x-y)f(y)\varphi(x)\,dy\,dx-\frac{\delta_{ij}}{n}\int f\varphi$ for every test function, which is clause (iii). [step 4.1, F5, F6, F10, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- The sign of the correction term is fixed by step 4.1 and checked against the trace: summing the identity over $i=j$ gives $\Delta\Phi=\mathrm{p.v.}(\Delta\Phi)-\delta_0=-\delta_0$ off the origin, that is $-\Delta\Phi=\delta_0$, the normalisation of [[def-laplace-fundamental-solution-with-positive-minus-laplacian-sign]]. This is a consistency check, not a substitute for step 4.1.
- Nothing here asserts a global subtracted identity, and no smoothness of $f$ beyond continuity and the stated Hölder modulus is used; the formula for $Nf$ is the same for real- or complex-valued data after applying the real case to real and imaginary parts.
