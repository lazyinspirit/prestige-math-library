## Statement

Let $\mathcal C,\mathcal C'$ be essentially small abelian categories and let
$\mathcal D,\mathcal D'$ be essentially small additive categories. For any
abelian group $H$, every function
$\chi\colon\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)\to H$ satisfying
$\chi(Y)=\chi(X)+\chi(Z)$ for every short exact sequence
$0\to X\to Y\to Z\to0$ factors uniquely as a homomorphism
$\overline\chi\colon G_0(\mathcal C)\to H$ with
$\overline\chi([X])=\chi(X)$. Every function
$\psi\colon\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal D)\to H$ satisfying
$\psi(X\oplus Y)=\psi(X)+\psi(Y)$ factors uniquely as a homomorphism
$\overline\psi\colon K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathcal D)\to H$ with
$\overline\psi([X])=\psi(X)$. Here $\operatorname{Iso}$ is the set of
isomorphism classes, so these are class functions.

An exact functor between essentially small abelian categories induces a
homomorphism on $G_0$, and an additive functor between essentially small
additive categories induces a homomorphism on split $K_0$. These assignments
preserve identities and composition. Naturally isomorphic exact functors, or
naturally isomorphic additive functors, induce equal homomorphisms.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The categories and abelian group $H$ in the Statement. The free
abelian groups are formed on the sets of isomorphism classes, and all quotient
relations are the ones in the respective $G_0$ and split-$K_0$ definitions. No
choice principle is used.

[F1] $G_0(\mathcal C)$ is the free abelian group on
$\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$ modulo the relations
$e_Y-e_X-e_Z$ from short exact sequences
([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]]).

[F2] $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathcal D)$ is the free abelian group on
$\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal D)$ modulo $e_{X\oplus Y}-e_X-e_Y$
([[def-split-grothendieck-group-of-an-additive-category]]).

[F3] A function from a set to an abelian group extends uniquely to a
homomorphism from the free abelian group on that set
([[def-free-abelian-group]]).

[F4] A homomorphism that kills a normal subgroup factors uniquely through the
quotient group ([[thm-quotient-group-universal-property]]).

[F5] In an abelian category, a composable sequence
$0\to X\xrightarrow{i}Y\xrightarrow{p}Z\to0$ is short exact exactly when
$i$ is a kernel of $p$ and $p$ is a cokernel of $i$
([[thm-a-short-exact-sequence-is-a-kernel-cokernel-pair]]).

[F6] An exact functor is additive and preserves the finite limits and finite
colimits that exist in its source category
([[def-exact-functor-between-abelian-categories]]).

[F7] An additive functor induces additive homomorphisms on the morphism
abelian groups ([[def-additive-functor]]).

[F8] An additive category is a preadditive category with all finite biproducts
([[def-additive-category]]).

[F9] A functor preserves identity morphisms and composition
([[def-functor-and-contravariant-functor]]).

[F10] A natural isomorphism has an inverse natural transformation
([[def-natural-isomorphism]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $F_{\mathcal C}$ be the free abelian group on $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$ and let $R_{\mathcal C}$ be the subgroup generated by $e_Y-e_X-e_Z$ for the short exact sequences of [F1]. By [F3], $\chi$ extends uniquely to $\widetilde\chi\colon F_{\mathcal C}\to H$. For each generator of $R_{\mathcal C}$, $\widetilde\chi(e_Y-e_X-e_Z)=\chi(Y)-\chi(X)-\chi(Z)=0$, so $R_{\mathcal C}\subseteq\ker\widetilde\chi$. Since $H$ is abelian, $R_{\mathcal C}$ is normal; [F4] gives a unique homomorphism from $F_{\mathcal C}/R_{\mathcal C}=G_0(\mathcal C)$ with the required values. The zero sequence gives $\chi(0)=\chi(0)+\chi(0)$, hence $\chi(0)=0$. [F1, F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 Let $F_{\mathcal D}$ be the free abelian group on $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal D)$ and let $R_{\mathcal D}$ be generated by $e_{X\oplus Y}-e_X-e_Y$. By [F3], $\psi$ extends uniquely to $\widetilde\psi\colon F_{\mathcal D}\to H$, and the additivity hypothesis makes every generator of $R_{\mathcal D}$ lie in its kernel. The subgroup $R_{\mathcal D}$ is normal because $F_{\mathcal D}$ is abelian, so [F4] gives a unique homomorphism $F_{\mathcal D}/R_{\mathcal D}=K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathcal D)\to H$. Taking $X=Y=0$ gives $\psi(0)=0$. [F2, F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $F\colon\mathcal C\to\mathcal C'$ be exact. If $f$ is an isomorphism, applying [F9] to $ff^{-1}=1$ and $f^{-1}f=1$ shows $Ff$ is an isomorphism, so $[X]\mapsto[FX]$ is a function on $\operatorname{Iso}(\mathcal C)$. In a short exact sequence, [F5] identifies $i$ as $\ker p$ and $p$ as $\operatorname{coker}i$. Exactness [F6] preserves these finite limit and colimit diagrams, so $0\to FX\to FY\to FZ\to0$ is short exact in $\mathcal C'$. Thus the image class function is additive, and step 1.1 gives the induced homomorphism $G_0(F)\colon G_0(\mathcal C)\to G_0(\mathcal C')$ with $G_0(F)([X])=[FX]$. [F1, F5, F6, F9, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Let $F\colon\mathcal D\to\mathcal D'$ be additive. If $f$ is an isomorphism, applying [F9] to $ff^{-1}=1$ and $f^{-1}f=1$ shows $Ff$ is an isomorphism, so $[X]\mapsto[FX]$ is defined on isomorphism classes. For a biproduct $X\oplus Y$, let $i_X,i_Y$ and $p_X,p_Y$ be its inclusions and projections. The equations $p_a i_b=\delta_{ab}$ and $i_Xp_X+i_Yp_Y=1$ characterize this biproduct. By [F7], $F$ preserves zero morphisms and addition; by [F9], it preserves identities and composition. The four image maps therefore exhibit $F(X\oplus Y)$ as a biproduct of $FX$ and $FY$, so $F(X\oplus Y)\cong FX\oplus FY$. The image class function is additive, and step 1.2 gives the induced homomorphism $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(F)\colon K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathcal D)\to K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(\mathcal D')$ with $K_0^{\mathrm{split}}(F)([X])=[FX]$. [F2, F7, F8, F9, step 1.2, construct, algebra]

3.1 For identity functors the formulas in steps 2.1–2.2 fix every generator. For composable exact functors $F,G$, and separately for composable additive functors, the composite is again of the same type. On every generator, $[X]\mapsto[FX]\mapsto[G(FX)]=[(GF)X]$; hence the induced homomorphism of $GF$ is the composite of the induced homomorphisms of $F$ and $G$. Equality on generators proves identity and composition laws for both assignments. [F6, F7, F9, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 If $\eta\colon F\Rightarrow G$ is a natural isomorphism, [F10] supplies a natural inverse, so each component $\eta_X\colon FX\to GX$ is an isomorphism. Thus $[FX]=[GX]$ in the target isomorphism-class set, and the induced homomorphisms agree on every generator of the corresponding $G_0$ or split-$K_0$ group. They are therefore equal. [F9, F10, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
