## Definition

Work in $\mathrm{ZFC}$, and write $\mathfrak c=2^{\aleph_0}$
([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[def-cardinal]]). For a cardinal $\lambda$, put
$2^\lambda=\{x:x:\lambda\to\{0,1\}\}$.

For finite $F\subseteq\lambda$ and $s:F\to\{0,1\}$, let
$C(F,s)=\{x\in2^\lambda:x|_F=s\}$. The **finite-cylinder sigma-algebra**
$\mathcal C_\lambda$ is generated by these sets. It equals the cylinder
sigma-algebra of [[def-coordinate-maps-and-cylinder-sigma-algebra]], since
any subset of a finite binary coordinate space is a finite union of singleton
assignments. In particular $C(\varnothing,\varnothing)=2^\lambda$.

Give $\{0,1\}$ its full power set and the probability assigning each point
mass $1/2$. This is standard Borel: the discrete metric is complete and the
finite space itself is a countable dense set, and its Borel sets are all its
subsets ([[def-standard-borel-space]]). Thus
[[cor-arbitrary-product-measure-for-standard-borel-probability-spaces]],
with index set $\lambda$ and these factors, supplies a unique probability
$\mu_\lambda$ on $\mathcal C_\lambda$ with
$\mu_\lambda(C(F,s))=2^{-|F|}$. Indeed its finite marginals are the finite
coin products; conversely these cylinder masses determine every such marginal
by finite disjoint unions. This is the **fair-coin product measure**. For
finite $\lambda$ it is normalized counting measure on $2^\lambda$; for
$\lambda=0$ it is the unit mass on the singleton empty function.

A **full extension** is a probability measure
$\nu:\mathcal P(2^\lambda)\to[0,1]$ restricting to $\mu_\lambda$ on
$\mathcal C_\lambda$. Here a probability measure has total mass $1$ and is
countably additive in the sense of [[def-measure]]. Its full domain makes it
complete ([[def-complete-measure-space]]), but completeness alone does not
mean that every subset of the underlying space is measurable.

A full extension is **$\mathfrak c$-additive** if for every pairwise disjoint
family $(A_j)_{j\in J}$ with $|J|<\mathfrak c$,
$$\nu\left(\bigcup_{j\in J}A_j\right)=\sum_{j\in J}\nu(A_j),$$
where the sum denotes the supremum of the finite subsums. Equivalently its
null ideal is closed under unions of fewer than $\mathfrak c$ sets. To verify
this equivalence, well-order a null family and disjointify it by subtracting
earlier members; the resulting sets remain null, so the displayed identity
makes their union null. Conversely, for a disjoint family in a probability
space, at most $n$ members have mass at least $1/n$ for each integer $n\ge1$.
Thus only countably many members have positive mass. Countable additivity
sums those members, and the remaining fewer than $\mathfrak c$ null members
have null union by the assumed null-ideal closure. This proves the identity.
The cardinal bound is strictly less than $\mathfrak c$; no condition on
increasing families of length $\mathfrak c$ is intended.

**PMEA** (the *product measure extension axiom*) asserts that for every cardinal
$\lambda$, $\mu_\lambda$ has a $\mathfrak c$-additive full extension.

**PMEA-$\sigma$** asserts that for every cardinal $\lambda$, $\mu_\lambda$
has a countably additive full extension.

## Remarks

PMEA implies PMEA-$\sigma$. The additional null-ideal closure in PMEA is the
clause available to later consumers dealing with fewer than continuum many
null sets. The product-measure corollary constructs $\mu_\lambda$ on
$\mathcal C_\lambda$; it does not construct either full extension asserted
by these axioms. Neither axiom asks for a two-valued measure on the index
set $\lambda$.
