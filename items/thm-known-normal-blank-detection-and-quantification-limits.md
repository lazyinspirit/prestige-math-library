## Statement

Assume AC, $\sigma_0>0$, $0<\alpha,\beta<1/2$, and $k_Q>0$. Consider the
response-only experiment with concentration $c\in[0,\infty)$ and
$X\sim N(c,\sigma_0^2)$ under each $c$. Reject the blank when
$X>L_C$. For the coefficient-of-variation criterion
$\operatorname{CV}(c)\le k_Q$, the exact critical level, detection limit
for false-negative target $\beta$, and quantification limit are
$$L_C=z_{1-\alpha}\sigma_0,\qquad L_D=(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\beta})\sigma_0,\qquad L_Q=\frac{\sigma_0}{k_Q}.$$
where $z_q$ is the unique standard normal $q$-quantile from
[[lem-standard-normal-cdf-quantiles-and-symmetry]]. These formulas are
conditional on the known-blank, equal-variance normal model.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; $\sigma_0>0$; $0<\alpha,\beta<1/2$; $k_Q>0$; and the experiment
$X\sim N(c,\sigma_0^2)$ for each concentration $c\ge0$.

[A1] AC is required by the standard normal-law construction. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws]]

[F1] The response laws are location-scale images of the standard normal law: $N(c,\sigma_0^2)$ is the law of $c+\sigma_0 Z$. [[def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws]]

[F2] The standard normal CDF is continuous and strictly increasing, its $q$-quantiles satisfy $\Phi(z_q)=q$, and $z_{1-q}=-z_q$; in particular $z_{1/2}=0$. [[lem-standard-normal-cdf-quantiles-and-symmetry]]

[F3] For a location-scale normal law, $E_c[X]=c$ and $\operatorname{Var}_c(X)=\sigma_0^2$. [[lem-normal-location-scale-mean-and-variance]]

[F4] For a simple null $\{0\}$, the size is $E_0[\varphi(X)]$ for the test function $\varphi$. [[def-test-size-and-power]]

[F5] Every measurable $\varphi:\mathbb R\to[0,1]$ defines a randomized test under the binary-action convention. [[lem-measurable-rejection-function-defines-randomized-test]] [[def-randomized-test]]

[F6] The Borel sigma-algebra of $\mathbb R$ is generated by its open sets. [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]

[F7] Measurability means inverse images of measurable target sets are measurable. [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]]

[F8] An exact critical level and the least-concentration definitions of $L_D$ and $L_Q$ are those of [[def-critical-detection-and-quantification-levels]].

[F9] A statistical experiment is a measurable data space with a parameter-indexed family of probability laws. [[def-statistical-experiment]]

[F10] For every measurable event $A$, $E[\mathbf 1_A]=P(A)$. [[cor-expectation-of-an-indicator-is-probability]]

[F11] For a probability measure, $P(A^c)=1-P(A)$. [[lem-probability-measure-basic-identities]]

[F12] The CDF of a real random variable is $F_X(x)=P(X\le x)$. [[def-cumulative-distribution-function-of-a-random-variable]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses in the Statement.

1.1 Set $L_C=z_{1-\alpha}\sigma_0$ and $\varphi(x)=\mathbf 1_{\{x>L_C\}}$. For every Borel $B\subseteq[0,1]$, $\varphi^{-1}(B)$ is one of $\varnothing,\mathbb R,(L_C,\infty),(-\infty,L_C]$; these are Borel, so [F6] and [F7] make $\varphi$ measurable. By [F5] it defines a test in the experiment [F9]. Under the blank, $X=\sigma_0 Z$ in law by [F1], hence $P_0(X>L_C)=1-\Phi(z_{1-\alpha})=\alpha$ by [F2] and [F11]. By [F4], the simple-null size is $E_0[\varphi(X)]$; [F10] identifies it with $P_0(X>L_C)$. Thus $L_C$ is an exact critical level by [F8]. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, F10, F11, algebra]

2.1 For any $c\ge0$, the affine law in [F1] and CDF definition [F12] give $P_c(X\le L_C)=\Phi((L_C-c)/\sigma_0)$. By strict increase and uniqueness in [F2], this equals $\beta$ exactly when $(L_C-c)/\sigma_0=z_\beta$. Hence the unique solution is $c=\sigma_0(z_{1-\alpha}-z_\beta) =\sigma_0(z_{1-\alpha}+z_{1-\beta})$, using the symmetry in [F2]. Since $z_{1/2}=0$ and $\Phi$ is strictly increasing by [F2], while $1-\alpha,1-\beta>1/2$, both quantiles in the sum are positive. This solution is therefore the least positive concentration and is $L_D$ by [F8]. [A1, F1, F2, F8, F12, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $c>0$, [F3] gives $\operatorname{CV}(c)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}_c(X)}/E_c[X] =\sigma_0/c$. Since $\sigma_0,k_Q>0$, the inequality $\sigma_0/c\le k_Q$ is equivalent to $c\ge\sigma_0/k_Q$. Its least positive solution is therefore $L_Q=\sigma_0/k_Q$ by [F8]. [A1, F3, F8, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

The exact formulas are derived from the stated normal law and the local
quantile and moment lemmas. NIST SP 958 reports rounded known-blank values
under $\alpha=\beta=0.05$, constant $\sigma_0$, and 10% relative standard
deviation; Eurachem's normal-tail discussion uses the corresponding
approximate $1.65+1.65$ factor. Neither source is used in place of the local
derivation.
