## Example

In the setting of [[thm-kostant-nilradical-cohomology-theorem]],
$$H^1(\mathfrak n^+,V)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb C_{s_i\cdot\lambda},$$
one line for each simple reflection $s_i$, generated by the classes of the
extremal cochains $\gamma_{s_i}$ of
[[lem-extremal-weight-cochain-for-a-weyl-element-is-closed]]. This identifies
the common simple-reflection indexing of degree-one Kostant cohomology and the first term
$\bigoplus_iM(s_i\cdot\lambda)$ of the BGG resolution, without identifying the cohomology lines with the Verma modules, and shows that distinct
simple roots give distinct weights, because $\lambda+\rho$ is regular.

## Verification

**Given:** The setting of [[thm-kostant-nilradical-cohomology-theorem]], with simple reflections $s_1,\dots,s_r$ attached to a base $\Delta=\{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_r\}$ of $\Phi^+$.

[L1] $H^k(\mathfrak n^+,V)\cong\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=k}\mathbb C_{w\cdot\lambda}$ as $\mathfrak h$-modules, and consequently $H^1(\mathfrak n^+,V)=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=1}\mathbb C_{w\cdot\lambda}$ ([[thm-kostant-nilradical-cohomology-theorem]]).

[L2] An element of $W$ has length $1$ exactly when it is a simple reflection: the length is the minimum number of simple reflections in an expression, so $\ell(w)=1$ means $w=s_i$ for some $i$, and conversely each $s_i$ has $\ell(s_i)=1$ with inversion set $\{\alpha_i\}$ ([[def-length-and-longest-element-of-a-finite-weyl-group]], [[lem-finite-weyl-positive-roots-and-simple-reflections]], [[thm-simple-roots-form-a-basis-and-every-root-has-one-sign-of-integral-coordinates]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]).

[L3] The classes of the extremal cochains are nonzero and the cochain space in the extremal weight is one-dimensional: $C^{\ell(w)}(\mathfrak n^+,V)_{w\cdot\lambda}=\mathbb C\gamma_w$ and $[\gamma_w]\neq0$ ([[lem-extremal-weight-cochain-for-a-weyl-element-is-closed]]).

[L4] Distinct simple roots give distinct reflections and distinct dot weights: $s_i\neq s_j$ for $i\neq j$, and $\lambda+\rho$ has trivial stabilizer, so $s_i\cdot\lambda=s_j\cdot\lambda$ forces $s_i=s_j$ ([[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

[L5] The degree-one term of the BGG complex is $C_1(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=1}M(w\cdot\lambda)=\bigoplus_{i=1}^rM(s_i\cdot\lambda)$ ([[def-bgg-bruhat-verma-sum-in-degree-k]], [[def-bgg-category-o]]).

1.1 By [L1] and [L2], $H^1(\mathfrak n^+,V)=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=1}\mathbb C_{w\cdot\lambda}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb C_{s_i\cdot\lambda}$, and each summand is generated by the class of $\gamma_{s_i}$ by [L3]. [L1, L2, L3]

2.1 By [L4] the weights $s_i\cdot\lambda$ are pairwise distinct, so the displayed sum is direct with one line per simple reflection; and by [L5] the same index set labels the first BGG term, giving $H^1(\mathfrak n^+,V)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^r\mathbb C_{s_i\cdot\lambda}$ against $C_1(\lambda)=\bigoplus_iM(s_i\cdot\lambda)$ as $\mathfrak h$-module statements. [L4, L5, step 1.1] ∎
