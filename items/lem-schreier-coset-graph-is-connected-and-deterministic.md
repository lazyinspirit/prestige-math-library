## Statement

Let $F(X)$ be a free group on $X$, let $H\le F(X)$, and let
$\operatorname{Sch}_X(H)$ be the labeled Schreier coset graph. Then:

1. $\operatorname{Sch}_X(H)$ is connected.
2. For every vertex $Hg$ and every $x\in X$, there is exactly one outgoing
   $x$-edge from $Hg$ and exactly one incoming $x$-edge into $Hg$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A free group $F(X)$, a subgroup $H\le F(X)$, and its labeled
Schreier coset graph.

[L1] A free group on a set $X$ is a group $F(X)$ equipped with the universal
property for maps out of $X$ ([[def-free-group]]).

[L2] Words, elementary cancellations, and reduced words on $X\sqcup X^{-1}$
are defined as in [[def-alphabet-words-and-reduction]].

[L3] The Schreier graph has vertices the right cosets $Hg$ and an $x$-labeled
edge $Hg\to Hgx$ for each $x\in X$ ([[def-labeled-schreier-coset-graph]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $K\le F(X)$ be the subgroup generated by the image of $X$. The inclusion $X\hookrightarrow K$ extends, by [L1], to a homomorphism $\phi:F(X)\to K$, and the inclusion $K\hookrightarrow F(X)$ composed with $\phi$ agrees with the identity of $F(X)$ on $X$. Uniqueness in [L1] therefore forces $(K\hookrightarrow F(X))\circ\phi=\operatorname{id}_{F(X)}$, so $K=F(X)$. Thus every element of $F(X)$ is represented by a word on $X\sqcup X^{-1}$. [L1, given, construct]

2.1 Let $Hg$ be any vertex. Choose a word $a_1\cdots a_n$ on $X\sqcup X^{-1}$ that represents $g$, and delete adjacent inverse pairs until the word is reduced. Reading the remaining letters from the base vertex $H$ follows the edges of [L3] forward for letters in $X$ and backward for letters in $X^{-1}$, and after the first $j$ letters one is at the coset $Ha_1\cdots a_j$. The final vertex is therefore $Hg$, so the graph is connected. [L2, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 For fixed $Hg$ and $x\in X$, [L3] gives exactly one outgoing $x$-edge, namely $Hg\to Hgx$. The same edge starts at $Hgx^{-1}$ and ends at $Hg$, so it is also the unique incoming $x$-edge into $Hg$. This is the required determinism. [L3] ∎
