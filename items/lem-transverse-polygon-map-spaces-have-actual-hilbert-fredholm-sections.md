## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Let $X$ be a compact smooth almost complex manifold and $L_0,\ldots,L_{n-1}$ compact oriented totally real submanifolds, $n\ge3$. Fix transverse corner points $x_i\in L_{i-1}\cap L_i$, cyclically, and actual target charts near each corner in which these two boundary submanifolds are $\mathbb R^m$ and $\prod_j e^{i\theta_j}\mathbb R$, with arbitrary fixed $0<\theta_j<\pi$. No right-angle condition is imposed. Take a smooth finite-dimensional family of the actual $n$-punctured sources in the finite-tree conformal charts, with all neck lengths finite. Work locally in its parameter space $B$; compact subsets of $B$ may be used for uniform bounds. Let $J_{b,z}$ be smooth source-dependent structures for which the assigned $L_i$ is totally real on its labelled boundary edge, equal in each far strip end, on the given corner chart, to its constant product structure. The source strip coordinates are fixed on those ends. No simultaneous target chart for three distinct boundary submanifolds is assumed.

For a smooth reference map with these boundary labels and limits, whose difference from each corner point and its derivatives through order three have finite weighted norms, choose a smooth weight $e^{\delta s}$ on every end, where $0<\delta$ is smaller than every transverse angular gap. Nearby maps with the given boundary values and fixed corners form an actual separable Hilbert manifold modelled on $H^3_\delta$ sections with ONLY the zeroth totally real trace condition. The Cauchy–Riemann equation defines a smooth section to the actual $H^2_\delta$ target bundle. Its fixed-source linearization is Fredholm; its real index is the actual $H^1\to L^2$ index $\mu$. Adding $d$ finite conformal or auxiliary family parameters gives Fredholm index $\mu+d$. At a surjective solution the zero set has an actual smooth finite-dimensional chart of that dimension. There is no translation quotient for the $n\ge3$ marked source.

These map charts and sections are second-countable across fixed corners and finitely many labels, and are smooth in all fixed higher Sobolev topologies near smooth solutions when the coefficients and reference maps have the corresponding higher bounds. Source and parameter derivatives are included in the section. This statement supplies no transversality, compactness or neck-uniform inverse estimate; a degenerating conformal parameter is not silently treated as a compact part of $B$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The actual targets, boundary submanifolds, corner charts, source family, coefficient family, reference map and weights in the Statement. Its numerical index is defined from the actual fixed-source Fredholm operator, not an unproved Euler-domain formula.

[F1] Finite-tree source charts supply the finite conformal parameters, actual end coordinates and smooth coordinate changes for every finite neck length. ([[lem-boundary-marked-disks-have-finite-tree-conformal-neck-charts]])

[F2] Actual finite-ended disk operators with transverse product end frames are Fredholm under Countable Choice; their $H^3\to H^2$ realizations impose only the zeroth real trace, have the same kernel and cokernel, and have the same index as $H^1\to L^2$. ([[lem-finite-transverse-strip-ended-disk-cauchy-riemann-fredholm]], [[lem-transverse-strip-ended-disk-fredholm-realizations-in-higher-sobolev-spaces]])

[F3] Weighted two-dimensional Sobolev extension, $H^3\subset C^1$, the $H^2$ algebra and smooth coefficient composition are proved locally, including real boundary patches. Actual strip configuration charts are built with finite-dimensional target local additions and these estimates. ([[lem-countable-choice-weighted-strip-sobolev-algebra-and-composition]], [[lem-higher-sobolev-strip-map-charts-and-cauchy-riemann-section]])

[F4] A smooth Hilbert section with split surjective derivative has actual finite-dimensional zero charts by an explicit Countable Choice Newton construction. ([[lem-countable-choice-quantitative-newton-correction-and-smooth-zero-chart]])

## Proof

1.1 Fix a parameter value and trivialize a small source-family neighborhood by actual smooth diffeomorphisms, equal to the given holomorphic identifications on its far ends and preserving each boundary label. Such diffeomorphisms can be constructed directly: move the finitely many boundary punctures by smooth real boundary vector fields supported in disjoint boundary intervals, extend those fields into a boundary collar, and use their finite-time flows. On each still smaller puncture chart replace the flow by the specified affine or fractional-linear holomorphic chart identification, and interpolate their infinitesimal fields on an intermediate collar. The fields and flows depend smoothly on the finitely many point and neck coordinates; reducing the parameter neighborhood keeps their derivatives invertible. The end charts agree exactly where the interpolation is already constant. On the pulled-back reference source the complex structure varies smoothly on a compact core and is fixed on the ends. In a fixed end trivialization the equation is consequently the stated strip equation. This construction is local in finite neck length and claims no bound as a neck becomes infinite. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Construct a source-dependent target local addition preserving the required boundary label. On an interior source patch use any smooth connection on $TX$, constructed from a finite coordinate cover and smooth partition. Along the interior of a labelled boundary edge use a connection for which that ONE $L_i$ is totally geodesic: in an adapted submanifold chart take a product connection along $L_i$, then patch such connections by an affine partition; its normal covariant derivative on two tangent vectors remains zero. Near a far corner end use the flat target connection on the specified corner chart, which preserves both transverse product planes. These choices are compatible on source overlaps by a source partition of unity; on the boundary every nonzero summand preserves the same assigned $L_i$, and on the ends retain exactly the flat connection. A global connection on all of $X$ preserving every $L_i$ simultaneously is unnecessary. Finite-dimensional geodesic existence and the inverse theorem, as proved in [F3]'s local-addition construction, give a uniform small fiber neighborhood over the compact core and the finitely many fixed end charts on which $\xi\mapsto\operatorname{Exp}_{b,z,u_*(z)}\xi$ is a diffeomorphism onto its image. If $\xi$ has the real boundary trace, its boundary values stay on the required $L_i$. Conversely a sufficiently close map has a unique such section, with that trace, by the inverse addition. This is a genuine boundary-preserving chart, even if several target boundary submanifolds meet at a point away from the specified ends. [F3, given, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Let $E=u_*^*TX$ and give it the corresponding totally real boundary subbundle. The model is the closed subspace of $H^3_\delta(E)$ whose image in the actual $H^1_\delta$ boundary domain has that zeroth trace. Closedness follows by continuity into $H^1$ and its closed real trace domain; no higher normal trace or closure of smooth sections in the $H^3$ norm is asserted. Its weighted $H^3$ norm controls its global $C^1$ norm: use [F3] on the finite compact cover and on translated unit end windows, on which the smooth weight and its logarithmic derivatives have uniform bounds. Consequently a small norm ball lies in the addition neighborhood of step2.1 and converges uniformly to the fixed corner on every end. The weighted algebra and Taylor estimates in [F3] make addition, its inverse and overlapping chart transitions smooth on these Hilbert balls. The same estimates apply to smooth source and target bundle changes from step1.1. This proves the actual Hilbert manifold structure with exactly the asserted trace. [F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 In a local target chart the section is $du+J_{b,z}(u)\,du\circ j_b$, regarded as a target-valued anti-complex-linear one-form, with the fixed harmless factor giving end coefficient $\partial_s+i\partial_t$. Bundle-trivialize its target near the reference graph by parallel transport along the addition paths. First derivatives of a map belong to $H^2_\delta$, and the target coefficient compositions obey [F3]'s Taylor bounds. Multiplying one such derivative by a bounded smooth coefficient, or two section variations by the required bounded coefficient derivatives, therefore defines the actual smooth $H^3_\delta\to H^2_\delta$ section. The parameter derivatives of $j_b$, $J_{b,z}$ and the source trivialization enter this same formula; none is omitted. On an end every nearby map is eventually in its fixed corner chart, so there the section is EXACTLY the constant linear strip equation. Thus every parameter or nonlinear term outside that linear equation is supported on a compact portion for each fixed chart, or has the required weighted decay. The section consequently has the asserted weighted target, rather than a nonintegrable constant error at infinity. [F1, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

4.2 The spaces are separable and second-countable, not merely formally modelled on unspecified Banach spaces. Weighted scalar Sobolev spaces on this finite core and finitely many ends are separable: truncate, use finite chart partitions, and approximate smooth coefficients and their finitely many derivatives by rational polynomial or rational convolution data on rational rectangles, exactly as in [F3]. Closed Hilbert trace subspaces are separable by the orthogonal-projection argument used in [F2]. To obtain a countable base for all map charts, embed the compact target into a finite-dimensional Euclidean space by the finite target-chart embedding in [F3]. Subtract a fixed smooth end-offset function taking the prescribed corner values on the ends. The resulting maps lie in one separable weighted Euclidean $H^3$ space; local composition estimates for the embedding and its smooth tubular inverse identify its relative topology with the chart topology in step3.1. A subspace of a second-countable metric space has a countable base. A countable cover of the finite-dimensional source-parameter space from [F1] gives the same conclusion with parameters. Different fixed corners give only finitely many choices here. No assertion of a finite or countable number of Whitney classes is needed. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 At a smooth solution its map derivative is the actual real-linear Cauchy–Riemann operator on $u^*TX$, with the oriented real boundary bundle $u^*TL_i$. Smooth complex frames convert its principal symbol to $2\bar\partial$ on each source chart. On a far end it has precisely the product planes and $D_0$ equation from step4.1; in particular its zeroth-order coefficient and all derivatives vanish there. Product metrics there and any finite core extension give exactly [F2]'s hypotheses. Apply its actual Fredholm and higher-realization theorems: the derivative on the model in step3.1 is Fredholm, has the same index $\mu$, kernel and cokernel as its actual $H^1\to L^2$ realization. A nearby coefficient realization remains Fredholm by the same finite Schur-block or parametrix proof in those suppliers. Appending a $d$-dimensional parameter space adds $d$ to the index: first append the zero map from $\mathbb R^d$, which adds $d$ kernel dimensions, then add its finite-rank derivative contribution; a direct finite kernel/cokernel Schur block gives the same index. This is an actual Fredholm dimension computation and requires no triangle translation action. [F2, F3, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 At a surjective solution choose the Hilbert orthogonal kernel complement. The bounded range inverse of [F2], and a finite lift of any parameter directions used to remove a finite cokernel, give a bounded right inverse for the full derivative. [F4] provides an actual smooth zero chart on its finite-dimensional kernel, of dimension $\mu+d$. Higher weak elliptic regularity on a finite core uses the real-boundary estimates in [F2]; on every end the exact $D_0$ cutoff inverse gives the same higher estimate. Differentiating the smooth nonlinear equation and applying these local estimates yields smooth solutions and parameter families in every fixed higher topology for which the coefficient bounds were imposed. Equivalently apply the higher-level zero chart and its finite-dimensional inverse to the lower zero chart; identical smooth kernels and the lower uniqueness identify the two. This is a statement near a smooth surjective solution and assumes neither global regularity nor a neck-uniform right inverse. [F2, F3, F4, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Finally, the sources have their boundary labels and at least three marked ends fixed. Their automorphism group is trivial by [F1]. Hence the local dimension in step6.1 is the actual count dimension; subtracting a strip translation would be incorrect. When a separate two-ended strip occurs in a later broken configuration, its own translation quotient must instead be taken, as already in the disk theory. The present proof treats every finite-neck configuration and includes every conformal and auxiliary parameter exactly once, but establishes neither regularity of all solutions nor compactification or gluing across an infinite neck. Those require additional arguments. [F1, given, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 4.2, algebra] ∎
