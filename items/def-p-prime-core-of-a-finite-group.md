## Definition

Let $G$ be a finite group ([[def-group]], [[def-finite-cardinality]]) and let $p$
be a prime ([[def-prime]]).

**$p$-element terminology.** An element $g\in G$ of finite order is a
**$p$-element** when $\operatorname{ord}(g)$ is a power of $p$, and a
**$p'$-element** when $p\nmid\operatorname{ord}(g)$
([[def-order-in-a-group]], [[def-finite-p-group]]). A subgroup $K\le G$ is a
**$p$-subgroup** when $|K|$ is a power of $p$, and a **$p'$-subgroup** when
$p\nmid|K|$; the trivial subgroup is both. The terminology is used without
further comment throughout the normal-complement material of this page.

**The $p'$-core.** Call a subgroup $N\le G$ of $G$ a *normal $p'$-subgroup* when
$N\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$ and $p\nmid|N|$
([[def-normal-subgroup]]). The **$p'$-core** of $G$ is

$$O_{p'}(G):=\Bigl\langle\,\bigcup\{N\le G: N\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G,\ p\nmid |N|\}\,\Bigr\rangle ,$$

the subgroup generated by all normal $p'$-subgroups of $G$
([[def-generated-subgroup]]). It is the unique largest normal $p'$-subgroup of
$G$: it is normal in $G$, its order is prime to $p$, and every normal
$p'$-subgroup of $G$ is contained in it.

**Why the definition is well posed.** The family
$\mathcal S=\{N\le G:N\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G,\ p\nmid|N|\}$ is nonempty
($\{1\}\in\mathcal S$, since $p\nmid1$) and finite: every member is a subset of
the finite set $G$, and $G$ has finitely many subsets
([[def-finite-cardinality]], [[cor-cardinality-of-the-power-set]]). Write
$\mathcal S=\{N_1,\dots,N_r\}$.

*Products stay in the family.* If $M,N\in\mathcal S$, then $MN$ is a subgroup of
$G$ ([[lem-product-with-normal-subgroup]]), it is normal because
$g(MN)g^{-1}=gMg^{-1}\,gNg^{-1}=MN$ for every $g\in G$
([[def-normal-subgroup]], [[thm-conjugation-is-an-automorphism]]), and its order
divides $|M|\,|N|$: by the second isomorphism theorem $M/(M\cap N)\cong MN/N$, so
$|MN|=|M|\,|N|/|M\cap N|$ ([[thm-second-isomorphism-theorem-groups]],
[[thm-lagrange]]). A divisor of the $p'$-number $|M|\,|N|$ is again prime to $p$:
if the prime $p$ divided $|MN|$ it would divide $|M|\,|N|$ and hence, by Euclid's
lemma, one of $|M|$, $|N|$ ([[thm-euclids-lemma]],
[[lem-divisibility-basic]]). So $MN\in\mathcal S$.

*The generated subgroup is a member.* By the product closure just proved,
$T:=N_1\cdots N_r$ belongs to $\mathcal S$, by finite induction. This subgroup
contains each $N_i$ (insert identities in all other factors), hence contains
$\langle\bigcup\mathcal S\rangle$ by the defining minimality of the generated
subgroup ([[def-generated-subgroup]]). Conversely every factor $N_i$ lies in
that generated subgroup, so their product $T$ does too. Thus
$O_{p'}(G)=T\in\mathcal S$; in particular $O_{p'}(G)=\bigcup\mathcal S$,
because $T$ is itself a member of the family and contains every member.

*Largest and unique.* As a member of $\mathcal S$, $O_{p'}(G)$ is a normal
$p'$-subgroup of $G$, and it contains every $N\in\mathcal S$ by construction; a
normal $p'$-subgroup is by definition a member of $\mathcal S$. Hence $O_{p'}(G)$
is the largest normal $p'$-subgroup, and it is the only one with that property,
since two normal $p'$-subgroups each contain the other.
