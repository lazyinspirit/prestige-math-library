## Example

Let $K:=\mathbb F_2[t]/(t^{3}+t+1)$ and let $\alpha$ be the class of $t$. Then
$K$ is a field of order $8$, the squaring map $\sigma(x)=x^{2}$ generates

$$\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb F_2)=\{\mathrm{id},\sigma,\sigma^{2}\},$$

cyclic of order three, its two orbits on $K\setminus\mathbb F_2$ are

$$\{\alpha,\ \alpha^{2},\ \alpha^{2}+\alpha\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\alpha+1,\ \alpha^{2}+1,\ \alpha^{2}+\alpha+1\},$$

and $K/\mathbb F_2$ has no intermediate field other than $\mathbb F_2$ and $K$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial $\pi:=t^{3}+t+1\in\mathbb F_2[t]$, the ring $K=\mathbb F_2[t]/(\pi)$ and the class $\alpha$ of $t$, so that $\alpha^{3}=\alpha+1$ because $\alpha^{3}+\alpha+1=0$ and $-1=1$ in characteristic two.

[L1] A polynomial of degree $2$ or $3$ over a field is irreducible if and only if it has no root in that field ([[thm-quadratic-and-cubic-irreducibility-test]]).

[L2] For a field $F$ and nonconstant $p\in F[x]$, $p$ is irreducible if and only if $F[x]/(p)$ is a field ([[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]).

[L3] If $a$ is algebraic over $F$ with minimal polynomial $m_a$ of degree $n$, then $F(a)$ has power basis $1,a,\dots,a^{n-1}$ and $[F(a):F]=n$ ([[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]], [[def-extension-degree-and-finite-extension]]); a monic irreducible vanishing at $a$ is that minimal polynomial ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[L4] An extension $E/\mathbb F_q$ of finite fields of degree $n$ is Galois with $\operatorname{Gal}(E/\mathbb F_q)=\langle\sigma_q\rangle$ cyclic of order $n$, where $\sigma_q(x)=x^{q}$, and $|E|=q^{n}$ ([[thm-extensions-of-finite-fields-are-galois-with-cyclic-frobenius-group]], [[def-relative-frobenius-of-a-finite-field-extension]], [[lem-order-of-the-relative-frobenius]], [[def-finite-field-and-its-order]]).

[L5] The intermediate fields of $\mathbb F_{q^{n}}/\mathbb F_q$ are the $\mathbb F_{q^{d}}$ for the positive divisors $d$ of $n$, one for each divisor ([[thm-intermediate-fields-of-an-extension-of-finite-fields]], [[def-divides-in-z]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 $\pi$ has no root in $\mathbb F_2$: $\pi(0)=1$ and $\pi(1)=1+1+1=1$. By [L1] it is irreducible, so $K$ is a field by [L2]. [L1, L2, given]

2.1 $\pi$ is monic irreducible with $\pi(\alpha)=0$, so it is the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb F_2$ and $[K:\mathbb F_2]=3$ with power basis $1,\alpha,\alpha^{2}$ by [L3]; hence $|K|=2^{3}=8$ by [L4]. [step 1.1, L3, L4]

3.1 By [L4] the extension $K/\mathbb F_2$ is Galois with Galois group generated by $\sigma(x)=x^{2}$ and of order three. [step 2.1, L4]

4.1 The orbit of $\alpha$: $\alpha^{2}$ is $\alpha^{2}$; $\alpha^{4}=\alpha\cdot\alpha^{3}=\alpha(\alpha+1)=\alpha^{2}+\alpha$; and $(\alpha^{2}+\alpha)^{2}=\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{2}=(\alpha^{2}+\alpha)+\alpha^{2}=\alpha$, using that squaring is additive in characteristic two. So $\{\alpha,\alpha^{2},\alpha^{2}+\alpha\}$ is one orbit of size three. [step 2.1, step 3.1, given]

5.1 The orbit of $\alpha+1$: $(\alpha+1)^{2}=\alpha^{2}+1$, $(\alpha^{2}+1)^{2}=\alpha^{4}+1=\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1$, and $(\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1)^{2}=\alpha^{4}+\alpha^{2}+1=\alpha+1$. So $\{\alpha+1,\alpha^{2}+1,\alpha^{2}+\alpha+1\}$ is the other orbit of size three, and together with $\{0\}$ and $\{1\}$ these account for all eight elements. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, given]

6.1 The positive divisors of three are $1$ and $3$, so by [L5] the intermediate fields are exactly two: $\mathbb F_2$ and $K$ itself. There is no field strictly between them. [step 2.1, step 3.1, L5] ∎

## Remarks

- **Why the two nontrivial orbits have the same size.** Each is an orbit of a group of prime order acting without fixed points outside $\mathbb F_2$: a fixed point of $\sigma$ is an element with $x^{2}=x$, and those are exactly the two elements of $\mathbb F_2$ ([[lem-fixed-field-of-the-relative-frobenius]]).
