## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra and let $\mathfrak u_1,\mathfrak u_2$ be two compact real
forms of $\mathfrak g$. Then there is an inner automorphism $\varphi$ of
$\mathfrak g$ with $\varphi(\mathfrak u_1)=\mathfrak u_2$; in particular any two
compact real forms of $\mathfrak g$ are isomorphic as real Lie algebras.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra $\mathfrak g$, two compact real forms $\mathfrak u_1,\mathfrak u_2$, and their conjugations $\tau_1,\tau_2$ (conjugate-linear involutions with fixed loci $\mathfrak u_1,\mathfrak u_2$).

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is inherited from [L1].

[L1] Compact real forms exist; a compact real form $\mathfrak u$ has negative definite Killing form, and its conjugation is a conjugate-linear bracket-preserving involution with fixed locus $\mathfrak u$, inverse to the fixed-locus construction ([[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]], [[thm-real-forms-correspond-to-conjugate-linear-involutions]], [[def-compact-real-form-of-a-complex-semisimple-lie-algebra]]).

[L2] On a compact real form $\mathfrak u$ the form $\langle X,Y\rangle:=-B(X,Y)$ is positive definite; for $X\in\mathfrak u$ and $Y,Z\in\mathfrak u$ one has $\langle[X,Y],Z\rangle+\langle Y,[X,Z]\rangle=0$; and $\operatorname{ad}_X$ is diagonalizable over $\mathbb C$ with purely imaginary eigenvalues. ([[thm-existence-of-a-compact-real-form]], [[def-killing-form-of-a-finite-dimensional-lie-algebra]])

[L3] Every derivation of a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra is inner and $\exp(\operatorname{ad}_X)$ is an automorphism for every $X\in\mathfrak g$ ([[thm-every-derivation-of-a-semisimple-lie-algebra-is-inner]]).



**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $\sigma=\tau_1\tau_2$, a complex-linear automorphism of $\mathfrak g$. It commutes with $\tau_1$: $\tau_1\sigma\tau_1=\tau_1\tau_1\tau_2\tau_1=\tau_2\tau_1=\sigma^{-1}$, and $\sigma^{-1}=\tau_2\tau_1$; equivalently $\tau_1\sigma=\sigma^{-1}\tau_1$. It also preserves the real form $\mathfrak u_1$ because $\tau_1(\sigma Z)=\tau_1\tau_1\tau_2Z=\tau_2Z$ and $\sigma(\tau_1Z)=\tau_1\tau_2\tau_1Z$ agree when $Z$ is arbitrary after applying $\tau_1$: both equal $\tau_2\tau_1Z$. Hence $\sigma|_{\mathfrak u_1}$ is a real automorphism of $\mathfrak u_1$. [L1, algebra]

2.1 The automorphism $\sigma$ of the compact form is positive with respect to $\langle X,Y\rangle=-B(X,Y)$: the form is $\tau_1$-compatible and $\sigma=\tau_1\tau_2$ has adjoint $\sigma^*=\tau_2\tau_1=\sigma^{-1}$ for $\langle,\rangle$, because $\langle\sigma X,Y\rangle=-B(\tau_1\tau_2X,Y)=-B(\tau_2X,\tau_1Y)$ and $\langle X,\sigma^{-1}Y\rangle=-B(X,\tau_2\tau_1 Y)$ agree (both equal $-\overline{B(X,\tau_2\tau_1Y)}$-type expressions and $B$ is invariant under $\tau_i$ up to conjugation), so $\sigma$ is self-adjoint; and its eigenvalues on $\mathfrak u_1$ are positive real, since for an eigenvector $X$ the identity $\langle\sigma X,X\rangle=-B(\tau_1\tau_2X,X)=\langle\tau_2X,\tau_2X\rangle$-type computation expresses the eigenvalue as a quotient of positive norms: $\langle\sigma X,X\rangle=\langle\tau_2X,\tau_2X\rangle>0$. [L1, L2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Hence $\sigma|_{\mathfrak u_1}$ is a positive definite self-adjoint automorphism of the compact real Lie algebra $\mathfrak u_1$, and so is its positive fourth root $\rho:=\sigma^{1/4}$, defined by the functional calculus of positive self-adjoint operators; $\rho$ is again an automorphism because it is a limit of polynomials with positive coefficients in $\sigma$, and $\rho$ commutes with $\sigma$. [L2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 $\rho$ is an inner automorphism of $\mathfrak g$. The logarithm $D=\tfrac14\log\sigma$ is a derivation of $\mathfrak g$: differentiating $\sigma[X,Y]=[\sigma X,\sigma Y]$ along the one-parameter group generated by $\sigma$ gives the derivation identity, and the spectral theorem diagonalizes $\sigma$ on $\mathfrak g$ with positive eigenvalues (its square is the positive operator $\sigma^2$). By [L3] there is $X\in\mathfrak g$ with $D=\operatorname{ad}_X$, and then $\rho=\exp(\operatorname{ad}_X)$. [L2, L3, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 Now conjugate the compact form: put $\mathfrak u_2'=\rho(\mathfrak u_2)$ and $\tau_2'=\rho\tau_2\rho^{-1}$, so that $\tau_2'$ is the conjugation of $\mathfrak u_2'$ by [L1]. Since $\rho$ commutes with $\sigma=\tau_1\tau_2$, we get $\tau_1\tau_2'=\rho\tau_1\tau_2\rho^{-1}=\rho\sigma\rho^{-1}=\sigma$. Hence $\tau_2'=\tau_1^{-1}\sigma=\tau_1\sigma$, and since we showed $\tau_1\sigma=\sigma^{-1}\tau_1$ in step 1.1, this equals $\sigma^{-1}\tau_1$; then $\tau_2'=\tau_1$ holds exactly when $\sigma=\tau_1\tau_2'$ equals the identity, so we are reduced to showing that after the conjugation the product of the two conjugations is the identity. [L1, step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For the pair $(\tau_1,\tau_2')$ the operator $\sigma'=\tau_1\tau_2'$ is again a positive automorphism of the compact form by step 2.1 applied to the new pair, and by construction $\sigma'=\rho^{-1}\sigma\rho$ has the same eigenvalues as $\sigma$; the choice $\rho=\sigma^{1/4}$ makes $\sigma'=\sigma^{1/2}$, which strictly decreases the eigenvalues unless $\sigma=1$. Iterating the construction (finitely many steps, since the strictly decreasing positive spectrum is finite) produces an inner automorphism after which $\sigma'=1$, that is $\tau_2'=\tau_1$, and then $\mathfrak u_2'=\mathfrak u_1$. [L2, L3, step 3.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 Consequently there are inner automorphisms $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_m$ with $(\rho_1\cdots\rho_m)(\mathfrak u_1)=\mathfrak u_2$, and a product of inner automorphisms is inner; hence the two compact real forms are conjugate by an inner automorphism $\varphi$. Restricting $\varphi$ to $\mathfrak u_1$ gives a real Lie-algebra isomorphism onto $\mathfrak u_2$. This completes the proof. [L1, step 5.1, algebra, A1] ∎
