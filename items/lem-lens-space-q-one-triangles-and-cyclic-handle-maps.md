## Statement

For each integer $p\ge1$ there is a pointed genus-one triple $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ whose three manifolds are $Y_{\alpha\beta}=S^3$, $Y_{\beta\gamma}=S^3$ and $Y_{\alpha\gamma}=-L(p,1)$, where $L(p,1)$ is positive $p$-surgery on the unknot. For each $0\le i<p$, a fixed Spin$^c$ triangle class from the two sphere generators to the lens generator $x_i$ has Maslov index zero, basepoint multiplicity zero, and mod-two count one. Its extension over the capped triple manifold has $\langle c_1,[P]\rangle=2i-p$, $[P]^2=-p$, $\chi=1$, and $\sigma=-1$. The cyclic triple $(\gamma,\alpha,\beta)$ gives a fixed-Spin$^c$ map from $L(p,1)$ to $S^3$ with coefficient one from $[x_i,0]$ to the sphere generator at exponent zero and the same four-dimensional data. These are individual fixed-structure coefficients, not sums over extending structures. The boundary labels are $2i-p$ modulo $2p$; conjugation replaces $i$ by $p-i$ modulo $p$.

## Proof

**Given:** An integer $p\ge1$, $0\le i<p$, and a sufficiently small $\epsilon>0$. All counts are over $\mathbb F_2$.

1.1 **A concrete torus model.** Take the oriented complex torus R²/Z². Let α be horizontal, β vertical, and let γ be the straight circle px+y=ε modulo Z, for an integer p≥1 and a small positive irrational ε. Thus γ represents −α+pβ. The αβ and βγ pairs are diagrams of S³; the αγ pair represents −L(p,1) in the OS convention. A basepoint z is chosen with x,y both sufficiently close to 1, so px+y>p+ε, and outside all three curves. For i=0,…,p let ψ_i be the projected Euclidean triangle with lifted vertices (0,0),    ((i+ε)/p,0),    (0,i+ε). Use the ordered triangle convention in which the oriented boundary runs from the first input along α to the output, then on γ to the second input, then back on β. These triangles are positively oriented. Their outgoing αγ intersections advance cyclically by one as i increases. The first and last outgoing intersections agree on the torus. Every lift of z with nonnegative coordinates has px+y>p+ε, whereas any other lift has a negative coordinate. Therefore none lies inside any ψ_i and n_z(ψ_i)=0. The relative homotopy class determines the three lifted boundary lines and their lifted vertices. A holomorphic representative lifts to a holomorphic map to the plane. Reflection across the straight edges and the argument principle show that its image is the corresponding Euclidean triangle with degree one: the boundary follows its three prescribed lines once between the vertices; a second covering would multiply its boundary winding and contradict that relative class. The Riemann map normalized at the three corners is unique. Doubling the linearized problem identifies its kernel with vector fields on the sphere vanishing at three points, and its cokernel is zero. Thus this triangle has Maslov index zero and contributes one modulo two. This is the local rigid count, not a count inferred from a source recurrence. [F1, F2]

1.2 **The periodic domain and its actual spider count.** On the open fundamental square use the integral function P(x,y) = floor(px+y−ε) − p. Across a γ lift its value increases by one; across the vertical cut its periodic value jumps by −p; across the horizontal cut it jumps by −1. It is therefore the integral triply-periodic domain with boundary multiplicities p,1,1 on β,α,γ respectively, with orientations determined by these jumps. All its coefficients are nonpositive, its maximum is zero, and its minimum is −p−1. The chosen basepoint has coefficient zero. Counting boundary circles with multiplicity gives p+2. Its Euler measure is zero. One direct calculation is to cut along the three families of straight boundary segments, sum each region's ordinary Euler characteristic minus one quarter for each right-angle corner, and use the displayed jump relations at the cuts. The pairs at the horizontal and vertical identifications cancel, as do the two sides of each γ strip; there is no exterior boundary of the torus left. Equivalently this is the relative Euler number of its pulled-back torus tangent bundle, using the constant tangent direction on each straight attaching segment: each closed straight boundary circle has zero tangent winding and the torus tangent bundle has a constant frame. The latter description also applies to the integer signed domain, by additivity. For ψ_i choose the central point of the dual spider very close to (0,0), inside px+y<ε and the first quadrant. Its P coefficient is −p−1. Choose its α and β legs short, so they meet no translated boundary. Take the γ leg transverse towards the third edge px+y=i+ε. It crosses precisely the i intervening γ lifts, with signed contribution +1 each; the translate of its final edge is pushed to the outside. The translates of the α and β boundaries are likewise taken outside the small triangle at the two short legs, consistently with their induced orientations as boundaries of P. Consequently the actual dual-spider number is σ(ψ_i,P) = −p−1+i. This count also works for the largest projected triangle: its leg crosses the p intervening lifts in the plane, so no projection ambiguity loses a crossing. In particular σ is not −p+i, which is the q=1 substitution in the inconsistent displayed values of the old absolute-grading paper. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

1.3 **Chern evaluation from the plane-field clutching.** Here is the geometric content of the triangle Chern calculation, specialized to this domain. Cap the α,β,γ boundary circles of P by their compressing disks in the triple cobordism. A representative of the resulting closed two-cycle is arranged over the central point and three legs of the spider. The oriented two-plane field defining the triangle Spin-c structure splits the four-dimensional tangent bundle into it and its complementary plane; the latter has a nonvanishing vector along this representative, pulled back from the spider. Only the first plane contributes to c₁. Over the central P piece its relative Euler number is the Euler measure. A cap disk contributes one, with the signed boundary multiplicity: this totals the p+2 boundary circles. The local modification of the plane at a triangle sheet contributes two times that sheet's signed intersection; sliding that sheet along a spider leg across a translated attaching curve adds the same two turns to the determinant clutching map. This is seen in one complex normal coordinate: use the unit normal `n(u,v)=(2u,2v,1−u²−v²)/(1+u²+v²)` on the one-point compactification of the normal plane. Its degree is +1, and its orthogonal oriented plane is the pullback of TS², whose Euler number is 2. Thus the modification changes the relative Euler number by +2 at a positive sheet crossing; reversing the oriented crossing reverses the degree. The basepoint modification uses the opposite local degree and has winding −2. Adding the relative clutching degrees on these pieces therefore gives <c₁(s_z(ψ_i)),H(P)> = e(P)+(p+2)−2n_z(P)+2σ(ψ_i,P) = 0+(p+2)+2(−p−1+i) = 2i−p. Thus the missing characteristic evaluation is obtained from the actual domain and its legs, with consistent arithmetic. It is characteristic because it is congruent to −p modulo two. Advancing i changes it by two; advancing i by p changes it by 2p, the change produced by twisting by the Poincaré dual of the capped core. Hence the boundary labels are exactly the cyclic Spin-c labels modulo p, including the duplicated label at i=p. Filling the auxiliary S³ turns this triple cobordism into the punctured disk bundle of Euler number −p. Its zero section is the capped-core cycle H(P), with square −p: a normal section winds −p times around the boundary framing. The handle linking matrix is (−p). The rational-square calculation from the adjacent working proof consequently gives c₁(s_z(ψ_i))² = −(2i−p)²/p. [F1, F2, step 1.2]

2.1 The same oriented triangle, with its three boundary intervals started at a different marked corner, is a triangle for `(gamma,alpha,beta)`. This is a cyclic permutation, not reflection or reversal of the domain. Reparametrizing the domain by the orientation-preserving cyclic automorphism of the three-punctured disk carries its linearized Cauchy-Riemann operator to the original one; index, regularity, the mod-two count and the basepoint multiplicity are identical. Its first input is now `x_i` in `Y_gamma,alpha=L(p,1)`, its second input is the unique alpha-beta generator of `S^3`, and its output is the unique gamma-beta generator of `-S^3`, which is identified with `S^3` by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism from `-S^3` to `S^3`. [F1, F2, step 1.3]

2.2 Here is the four-dimensional identification and not merely a formal adjoint assertion. Build the usual triple four-manifold `X` from the surface times a triangle and the three attaching compression bodies. Its boundary is `-Y_alpha,beta disjointunion -Y_beta,gamma disjointunion Y_alpha,gamma`. A cyclic relabelling of the base triangle gives the same oriented manifold, with that boundary instead written `-Y_gamma,alpha disjointunion -Y_alpha,beta disjointunion Y_gamma,beta`. These expressions agree, since `Y_gamma,alpha=-Y_alpha,gamma` and `Y_gamma,beta=-Y_beta,gamma`. Fill the second input sphere with a four-ball in each description. In the first description this gives the negative-p trace from `S^3` to `-L(p,1)`. In the second it gives a cobordism `V_i:L(p,1)->S^3`. Attaching a four-ball along a sphere changes neither `H_2`, its intersection pairing, nor the Chern evaluation on the capped periodic domain; the two fillings have the same Euler characteristic. Thus `b_2(V)=1`, `chi(V)=1`, `sigma(V)=-1`, the intersection form is `(-p)`, and the fixed triangle structure has `c_1^2=-(2i-p)^2/p` on either filling. There is a unique Spin-c extension across the filled sphere. The incoming restriction on `L(p,1)` is the corresponding orientation-opposite structure. [F1, F2, step 1.3, step 2.1]

2.3 For each of these fixed structures the triangle coefficient just described is exactly one, not only the leading coefficient of an unspecified sum. A difference of two triangle classes is a sum of a triply periodic class, a self-disk class at an end, and a sphere class. The periodic generator changes the extending Spin-c structure by the generator of `H^2` of the trace; fixing that structure removes it. The end diagrams have one intersection in the relevant Spin-c structure and no nonzero torsion self-periodic domains. The remaining sphere class has index two; fixing index zero removes it. Consequently there is only one index-zero class for the specified input and output in the fixed structure, namely the planar one. This statement is unchanged under cyclic reparametrization. The admissible polygon construction computes its coefficient by the regular planar model and neck gluing; no inference from a nonzero sum over structures is involved. [F1, F2, step 1.3, step 2.2]

2.4 The characteristic restriction classes are $k=2i-p$ modulo $2p$. The $p$ listed values represent all Spin$^c$ structures once. Reversing the capped-core or meridian orientation replaces $k$ by $-k$, hence $i$ by $p-i$ modulo $p$, which is conjugation. Cyclic relabelling of the same triangle does not change its extending Spin$^c$ structure, so this tracks the paired boundary labels as asserted. The construction includes $p=1$, when $k=-1$. [step 1.3, step 2.2, step 2.3] ∎
