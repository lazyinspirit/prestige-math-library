## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice for the path-law, conditional-expectation, and martingale suppliers. Let a finite MDP have $S\ge1$ states and at most $A\ge1$ actions per state, and let $T\ge1$ be an integer and $\delta\in(0,1)$. Consider any length-$T$ interaction generated by a nonanticipating policy whose action distributions are measurable functions of the revealed history and whose randomization is independent of unexposed MDP samples. For each state-action pair $(s,a)$, whenever it has received $n$ visits by time $T$, let $\widehat r_n(s,a)$ be the mean of those first $n$ rewards and let $\widehat P_n(\cdot\mid s,a)$ be the empirical law of those next states. With probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously for all such pairs and all $1\le n\le T$,
$$|\widehat r_n(s,a)-r(s,a)|\le\sqrt{\frac{7\log(4SAT/\delta)}{2n}},\qquad \|\widehat P_n(\cdot\mid s,a)-P(\cdot\mid s,a)\|_1\le\sqrt{\frac{14S\log(4AT/\delta)}{n}},$$
where $\log$ is the natural logarithm.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The finite bounded-reward MDP, its fixed conditional law for each state-action pair, and the parameters in the Statement.

[F1] [[def-bounded-reward-finite-mdp]]: conditional on the pre-observation history and the selected pair, the reward and next state have the fixed law $Q(\cdot\mid s,a)$.

[F2] [[lem-ucrl2-adaptive-sample-tape-representation]]: adaptive visits reveal prefixes of fixed i.i.d. sample tapes without changing the interaction law.

[F3] [[thm-azuma-hoeffding-inequality]]: for a martingale with increments of deterministic widths $c_k$, each one-sided tail is at most $\exp(-2t^2/\sum_k c_k^2)$.

[F4] [[def-martingale-difference-sequence]]: centered independent tape coordinates form a martingale-difference sequence in their natural filtration.

[F5] [[thm-martingales-and-martingale-differences-correspond]]: partial sums of martingale differences are martingales relative to the same filtration.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is used by [F2]'s path-space construction, [F3]'s conditional expectations, and [F4]–[F5]'s martingale interfaces; the finite union bounds and subset enumeration use no additional choice.

## Proof

1.1 By [F2], each pair's observed samples are prefixes of a fixed i.i.d. tape with law $Q(\cdot\mid s,a)$. By [F1], its reward coordinate has mean $r(s,a)$ and its next-state coordinate has law $P(\cdot\mid s,a)$. Rewards and indicators of next-state subsets lie in $[0,1]$. The finite tape construction uses AC exactly as stated in [A1]. [given, F1, F2, A1]

2.1 By [F4] and [F5], the centered reward tape prefix has martingale partial sums with width-one increments; applying [F3] to that sum and to its negative gives the two-sided prefix tail $2e^{-2n\varepsilon^2}$. For a fixed pair and $n$, set $\varepsilon_r=\sqrt{7\log(4SAT/\delta)/(2n)}$. Step 1.1 bounds the failure probability by $2(\delta/(4SAT))^7$. Union over at most $SA$ pairs and $T$ possible prefix lengths gives at most $2SAT(\delta/(4SAT))^7\le\delta/2$. [given, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For transition observations, fix a subset $B\subseteq S$. By step 1.1, the indicators $\mathbf1_{\{S'\in B\}}$ have mean $P(B\mid s,a)$ and range $[0,1]$. Their centered tape coordinates satisfy [F4]; by [F5] their partial sums are martingales, so [F3] gives $2e^{-2n u^2}$ for a deviation larger than $u$. For two probability vectors $p,q$ on a finite set, $\|p-q\|_1=2\max_{B\subseteq S}|p(B)-q(B)|$: the positive coordinates of $p-q$ sum to half its $\ell^1$ norm, and choosing exactly those coordinates attains the maximum. Therefore an $\ell^1$ deviation larger than $\varepsilon$ implies that some subset indicator deviates by more than $\varepsilon/2$. A union bound over the $2^S$ subsets gives $2^{S+1}e^{-n\varepsilon^2/2}$. [given, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Set $L=\log(4AT/\delta)$ and $\varepsilon_p=\sqrt{14S L/n}$. Step 2.2 gives per-pair, per-prefix failure probability at most $2^{S+1}e^{-7SL}$. Union over at most $SAT$ pairs and prefixes is at most $SAT2^{S+1}e^{-7SL}\le\delta/2$: after dividing by $\delta/2$, its logarithm is $\log S+L+S\log2-7SL\le(2S)L-7SL\le0$, using $S\le2^{S-1}$ and $L\ge\log2$. Combining the reward failure bound from step 2.1 with the transition bound just obtained proves the simultaneous claim. [given, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra] ∎
