## Statement

Let $n>2$, let $B_n$ be the braid group of
[[def-braid-group-by-the-artin-presentation]] with its generators
$\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$ and its half twist $\Delta$
([[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]]). Call
$$\Delta^{2}$$
the **full twist** of $B_n$. Then the center of $B_n$ is
$$Z(B_n):=\{x\in B_n:xg=gx\text{ for all }g\in B_n\}=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle=\{\Delta^{2k}:k\in\mathbb Z\},$$
and it is infinite cyclic: $\Delta^{2}\ne1$ and the map $\mathbb Z\to Z(B_n)$,
$k\mapsto\Delta^{2k}$, is a group isomorphism.

The statement is false for $n=2$, where the center is all of
$B_2=\langle\Delta\rangle$: that exception is
[[prop-the-center-of-b-two-is-all-of-b-two]]. The proof is choice free.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A natural number $n>2$, the braid group $B_n$ with generators $\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_{n-1}$, the positive braid monoid $B_n^{+}$ with half twist $\Delta$ of length $N=n(n-1)/2$, and a group element $x\in B_n$.

[F1] **The square of the half twist is central.** $\sigma_i\Delta^{2}=\Delta^{2}\sigma_i$ for every $i$; moreover $\sigma_i\Delta=\Delta\sigma_{n-i}$ ([[lem-conjugation-by-delta-reverses-artin-generators]]).

[F2] **Central positive braids.** If $z\in B_n^{+}$ is central in $B_n$, then $z=\Delta^{2s}$ for some $s\ge0$ ([[lem-a-central-positive-braid-is-a-power-of-delta-squared-for-n-greater-than-two]]).

[F3] **$\Delta$-power divisibility.** Every positive braid $b\in B_n^{+}$ is a right divisor of some power of $\Delta$: there is $k\ge0$ with $\Delta^{k}=cb$ for some $c\in B_n^{+}$; exponents may be enlarged, so an exponent of the form $2r$ may be chosen ([[lem-every-positive-braid-divides-a-power-of-delta-on-both-sides]]).

[F4] **Description by fractions.** Every element of $B_n$ has the form $ab^{-1}$ with $a,b\in B_n^{+}$, the positive monoid being regarded as a submonoid of $B_n$ through its embedding ([[thm-the-ore-fraction-group-of-positive-braids-is-the-artin-braid-group]]).

[F5] **Length and torsion freeness.** On $B_n^{+}$ the length is additive, $\ell(w)=0$ only for $w=1$, and $\ell(\Delta^{m})=mN$ for $m\ge0$; the group $B_n$ is torsion free ([[lem-positive-artin-relations-preserve-homogeneous-length]], [[thm-braid-groups-are-torsion-free-by-the-garside-lattice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 **$\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle\subseteq Z(B_n)$.** By [F1] $\Delta^{2}$ commutes with every generator $\sigma_i$; multiplying the identity $\sigma_i\Delta^{2}=\Delta^{2}\sigma_i$ on both sides by $\sigma_i^{-1}$ shows that $\Delta^{2}$ commutes with $\sigma_i^{-1}$ as well. Every element of $B_n$ is a product of generators and their inverses (it is a class of a word in the $\sigma_i^{\pm1}$), so induction on the number of letters of such a word shows that $\Delta^{2}g=g\Delta^{2}$ for every $g\in B_n$; hence $\Delta^{2}\in Z(B_n)$ and every power $\Delta^{2k}$, $k\in\mathbb Z$, is central. [F1, F4]

1.2 **Enlarging the exponent to an even one.** Let $b\in B_n^{+}$. By [F3] there is $k\ge0$ and $c\in B_n^{+}$ with $\Delta^{k}=cb$; choose $r$ with $2r\ge k$ (and $2r\ge0$). Then $\Delta^{2r}=\Delta^{2r-k}\Delta^{k}=(\Delta^{2r-k}c)b$ with $\Delta^{2r-k}c\in B_n^{+}$, so $\Delta^{2r}=c'b$ for some $c'\in B_n^{+}$: $b$ is a right divisor of an *even* power of $\Delta$. [F3, F5]

2.1 **Every central element is a power of $\Delta^{2}$.** Let $x\in Z(B_n)$. By [F4] write $x=ab^{-1}$ with $a,b\in B_n^{+}$, and by step 1.2 choose $r\ge0$ and $c\in B_n^{+}$ with $\Delta^{2r}=cb$; thus $b^{-1}=\Delta^{-2r}c$ and, since $\Delta^{2r}$ is central by step 1.1, $z:=\Delta^{2r}x=\Delta^{2r}ab^{-1}=a\Delta^{2r}b^{-1}=acbb^{-1}=ac\in B_n^{+}$. The element $z$ is central: it is the product of the central elements $\Delta^{2r}$ and $x$. Hence by [F2] $z=\Delta^{2s}$ with $s\ge0$, and therefore $x=\Delta^{-2r}z=\Delta^{2(s-r)}=( \Delta^{2})^{s-r}\in\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle$. [F2, F4, step 1.1, step 1.2]

3.1 **Infinite cyclic order.** By steps 1.1 and 2.1, $Z(B_n)=\langle\Delta^{2}\rangle$, and $\mathbb Z\to Z(B_n)$, $k\mapsto\Delta^{2k}$, is a surjective homomorphism. It is injective: if $\Delta^{2k}=1$ with $k>0$ then $\Delta^{2}$ is a nonidentity torsion element, because $\Delta^{2}\ne1$ — indeed $\ell(\Delta^{2})=2N>0=\ell(1)$ and $\ell(w)=0$ forces $w=1$ in $B_n^{+}$ — contradicting the torsion freeness of $B_n$. Hence $Z(B_n)\cong\mathbb Z$ is infinite cyclic, generated by the full twist $\Delta^{2}$. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

4.1 **Assembly.** The inclusion is step 1.1, the reverse inclusion is step 2.1 and the cyclic description is step 3.1. The key use of the hypothesis $n>2$ is in [F2], where an odd exponent of $\Delta$ is excluded by the distinct atoms $\sigma_1\ne\sigma_{n-1}$; for $n=2$ the argument fails exactly because $\sigma_1=\sigma_{n-1}$, and the center is larger. All steps are algebraic; no geometric model of braids is used and no choice principle is used. ∎ [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

## Remarks

- **The full twist.** The generator of the center is the square of the half
  twist, $\Delta^{2}=(\sigma_1\sigma_2\cdots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}$ in the classical
  notation for type $A$; here only the description $\Delta^{2}$ in terms of the
  triangular word of
  [[def-garside-half-twist-and-simple-positive-braid]] is used, so the identity
  with $(\sigma_1\cdots\sigma_{n-1})^{n}$ is not needed.
- **Why centrality of squares helps.** The two ingredients are structural: an
  arbitrary group element can be shifted into the positive monoid by a
  *central even* power of $\Delta$ (step 1.2, applied in step 2.1), and central positive braids are
  even $\Delta$-powers (the preceding lemma). The same two ingredients give
  Garside's theorem in J. González-Meneses, *Basic results on braid groups*,
  Theorem 4.2, printed pp. 30--31.
- **Comparison with $n=2$.** For $n=2$ the conclusion is false: $B_2$ is
  abelian, so $Z(B_2)=B_2=\langle\Delta\rangle$
  ([[prop-the-center-of-b-two-is-all-of-b-two]]). Both statements together give
  the complete description of the center of $B_n$ for every $n\ge2$.
- Nothing here uses the Axiom of Choice or any weaker choice principle.
