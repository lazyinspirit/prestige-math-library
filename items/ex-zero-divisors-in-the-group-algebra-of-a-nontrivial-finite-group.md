## Example

Let $G$ be a nontrivial finite group, let $k$ be a field, and choose
$g \in G \setminus \{e\}$. Then $k[G]$ has nonzero zero divisors:
$$([e]-[g])([e]+[g]+\cdots+[g^{n-1}])=0,$$
where $n \ge 2$ is the order of $g$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A nontrivial finite group $G$, a field $k$, and $g \in G \setminus \{e\}$.

[L1] The group algebra has basis vectors $[x]$ indexed by the elements of $G$, and they multiply by $[x][y]=[xy]$ ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]).

[L2] The powers $g^m$ are defined for all integers, with $g^0=e$ ([[def-group-power]]).

[L3] The cyclic subgroup generated by $g$ is denoted $\langle g\rangle$ ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The subgroup $\langle g\rangle$ of [L3] is finite because it is a subset of the finite group $G$, so some least integer $n \ge 1$ satisfies $g^n=e$. Because $g \ne e$, this least $n$ is at least $2$. The vectors $[e]-[g]$ and $[e]+[g]+\cdots+[g^{n-1}]$ are both nonzero, since they are sums of distinct basis vectors from [L1]. [L1, L2, L3, given]

2.1 Using [L1] and [L2], $$([e]-[g])([e]+[g]+\cdots+[g^{n-1}])=([e]+[g]+\cdots+[g^{n-1}])-([g]+[g^2]+\cdots+[g^n])=[e]-[g^n]=0.$$ So $k[G]$ has nonzero zero divisors. [step 1.1, L1, L2, algebra] ∎
