## Example

Over $\mathbb F_2$ one has

$$\Phi_7(t)=t^{6}+t^{5}+t^{4}+t^{3}+t^{2}+t+1=(t^{3}+t+1)(t^{3}+t^{2}+1).$$

These are the two monic irreducible cubic factors, and over a splitting field the
two factors correspond to the Frobenius orbits

$$\{\zeta,\zeta^{2},\zeta^{4}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{\zeta^{3},\zeta^{6},\zeta^{5}\}$$

of a primitive seventh root of unity $\zeta$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $\mathbb F_2$ and a primitive seventh root of unity $\zeta$ in a splitting field of $\Phi_7$.

[L1] If $\gcd(n,q)=1$, the reduction of $\Phi_n$ in $\mathbb F_q[t]$ is a product of distinct monic irreducibles, each of degree the order of $[q]$ modulo $n$ ([[thm-factorisation-of-the-cyclotomic-polynomial-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L2] For $\gcd(n,q)=1$, the image of $\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb F_q(\mu_n)/\mathbb F_q)$ in $(\mathbb Z/n)^\times$ is generated by $[q]$ ([[thm-the-cyclotomic-galois-group-over-a-finite-field]]).

[L3] The roots of a monic irreducible polynomial of degree $d$ over $\mathbb F_q$ are one Frobenius orbit $\alpha,\alpha^q,\ldots,\alpha^{q^{d-1}}$ ([[thm-conjugates-over-a-finite-field-are-the-frobenius-powers]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 In $(\mathbb Z/7)^\times$ the class $[2]$ has order $3$, since $2^{3}=8\equiv1\pmod 7$ and $2\not\equiv1$, $2^{2}=4\not\equiv1\pmod 7$. So [L1] and [L2] say every irreducible factor of $\Phi_7$ over $\mathbb F_2$ is a distinct cubic. [L1, L2, algebra]

2.1 The product of the two monic cubics is $$ (t^{3}+t+1)(t^{3}+t^{2}+1)=t^{6}+t^{5}+t^{4}+t^{3}+t^{2}+t+1=\Phi_7(t) $$ in $\mathbb F_2[t]$. Since both factors are monic of degree $3$, step 1.1 makes them the two irreducible factors of $\Phi_7$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Frobenius acts by $\zeta\mapsto\zeta^{2}$, so the orbit of $\zeta$ is $\{\zeta,\zeta^{2},\zeta^{4}\}$ because $\zeta^{8}=\zeta$, and the orbit of $\zeta^{3}$ is $\{\zeta^{3},\zeta^{6},\zeta^{5}\}$ because $(\zeta^{3})^{2}=\zeta^{6}$, $(\zeta^{6})^{2}=\zeta^{12}=\zeta^{5}$ and $(\zeta^{5})^{2}=\zeta^{10}=\zeta^{3}$. These are the two size-three Frobenius orbits of primitive seventh roots, and [L3] identifies them as the respective root sets of the two irreducible cubic factors from step 2.1. [step 1.1, step 2.1, L3, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

- **This is the degree-three case of the theorem, not a coincidence of cubics.** The orbit size and the factor degree are both the order of $[2]$ modulo $7$.
