## Statement

For every group $G$, its commutator subgroup $[G,G]$ is normal in $G$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group $G$, its commutator subgroup $D=[G,G]$, and an element $x\in G$.

[F1] The subgroup $D$ is generated by all elements $[g,h]=ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$ with $g,h\in G$ ([[def-commutator-and-commutator-subgroup]]).

[L1] The subgroup generated by a set is contained in every subgroup that contains that set ([[def-generated-subgroup]]).

[F2] Conjugating a subgroup by a fixed group element produces a subgroup ([[def-subgroup]]).

[L2] A subgroup $D\le G$ is normal if $xDx^{-1}\subseteq D$ for every $x\in G$ ([[thm-normal-subgroup-characterisations]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Direct multiplication gives $x[g,h]x^{-1}=[xgx^{-1},xhx^{-1}]$ for all $g,h\in G$. [F1, algebra]

1.2 The conjugate $x^{-1}Dx=\{x^{-1}dx:d\in D\}$ is a subgroup of $G$. [F2, algebra]

2.1 For every commutator $c=[g,h]$, step 1.1 gives $xcx^{-1}\in D$, so $c\in x^{-1}Dx$. Thus the subgroup $x^{-1}Dx$ contains every generator of $D$, and [L1] gives $D\subseteq x^{-1}Dx$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F1, L1]

3.1 Conjugating the containment in step 2.1 by $x$ gives $xDx^{-1}\subseteq D$. Since $x$ was arbitrary, [L2] gives $D\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}G$. [step 2.1, L2, algebra] ∎
