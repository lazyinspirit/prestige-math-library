## Definition

Let $(I,A,D,P,P^{\vee},q)$ be a symmetrizable Cartan datum with $a_{ii}=2$ for
all $i$ ([[def-symmetrizable-cartan-datum-for-a-quantum-group]]) and let
$U_q(\mathfrak g)$ be its Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra
([[def-drinfeld-jimbo-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]) with Borel subalgebra
$U_q^{\ge0}=U_q^0U_q^+$ and negative half $U_q^-$
([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]). Fix
$\lambda\in P$ and let $\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda=\mathbb Q(q)$ be the
**Borel character module** on which $K_h$ acts by the scalar
$q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}$ and every $E_i$ acts as $0$ (the character is a
well-defined $\mathbb Q(q)$-algebra homomorphism
$\chi_\lambda\colon U_q^{\ge0}\to\mathbb Q(q)$, Verification, step 1.1). The
**quantum Verma module** is the induced module

$$M(\lambda):=U_q(\mathfrak g)\otimes_{U_q^{\ge0}}\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda,$$

a left $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module, with distinguished vector
$v_\lambda:=1\otimes1$ ([[def-quotient-ring]] for the quotient/induction
conventions).

Then (Verification, steps 2.1 and 3.1):

(i) $M(\lambda)$ carries the **canonical $P$-label grading**
$M(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\mu\in P}M(\lambda)_\mu$ with
$M(\lambda)_{\lambda-\nu}=U_q^-[-\nu]v_\lambda$ for $\nu\in Q_+$ and
$M(\lambda)_\mu=0$ otherwise; each $M(\lambda)_\mu$ is finite dimensional
(there are only finitely many words in the $F_i$ of a given root degree), and
$M(\lambda)_\lambda=\mathbb Q(q)v_\lambda$ is the highest line. With this
grading $M(\lambda)$ is a $P$-graded type-one quantum weight module
([[def-quantum-weight-module-and-integrability]]) and $v_\lambda$ is a highest
weight vector: $K_hv_\lambda=q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}v_\lambda$,
$E_iv_\lambda=0$, $M(\lambda)=U_q(\mathfrak g)v_\lambda$.

(ii) (Universal property) For every left $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module $V$ and every
$v\in V$ with $K_hv=q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}v$ and $E_iv=0$ for all $h,i$
there is a unique $U_q(\mathfrak g)$-module map $M(\lambda)\to V$ with
$v_\lambda\mapsto v$; if $V=U_q(\mathfrak g)v$ is cyclic, this map is
surjective. When $V$ is a $P$-graded type-one module and $v\in V_\lambda$, the
map is grading-preserving.

(iii) The sum $N(\lambda)$ of all proper graded submodules of $M(\lambda)$ is a
proper graded submodule, and

$$L(\lambda):=M(\lambda)/N(\lambda)$$

is the **simple highest weight quotient**: it has no nonzero proper graded
submodule, and it is the unique simple graded quotient of $M(\lambda)$ up to
isomorphism ([[def-submodule]]). Thus the maximal proper submodule occurring
here is the sum of *all* proper graded submodules, not an arbitrarily chosen
maximal one; no choice is used.

If the pairing separates $P$, then every submodule of $M(\lambda)$ is graded,
$N(\lambda)$ is the unique maximal proper submodule, and $L(\lambda)$ is the
ordinary simple (not only graded-simple) quotient: this is the usual simple
weight-module quotient of the Verma module over a separating torus
(Verification, step 4.1).

The contravariant Cartan-projection lemma
`lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form`
identifies $N(\lambda)$ with the radical of the normalized Verma contravariant
form and gives the canonical normalized form on $L(\lambda)$; for $\lambda$
dominant integral, the existence theorem
`thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique` proves the
ordinary simplicity and the universal integrable conclusions. Both are
declared obligations of those later items, not assumptions here.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A symmetrizable Cartan datum with $a_{ii}=2$, its Drinfeld–Jimbo algebra, the subalgebras $U_q^\pm,U_q^0,U_q^{\ge0}$, and a weight $\lambda\in P$.

[F1] Triangular decomposition: the multiplication map $U_q^-\otimes U_q^0\otimes U_q^+\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is a vector-space isomorphism; consequently the multiplication map $U_q^-\otimes U_q^{\ge0}\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is also a vector-space isomorphism, and $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is a free right $U_q^{\ge0}$-module with basis any basis of $U_q^-$; $U_q^{\ge0}=U_q^0U_q^+$ is a subalgebra, and $U_q^-\cap U_q^{\ge0}$ and $U_q^0\cap U_q^+$ are both the scalars $\mathbb Q(q)\cdot1$ ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]]).

[F2] $U_q^0\cong\mathbb Q(q)[P^{\vee}]$, the group algebra of $P^{\vee}$, via $K_h\leftrightarrow X_h$; in particular $K_0=1$, $K_hK_{h'}=K_{h+h'}$, and evaluation $X_h\mapsto q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}$ is a well-defined algebra homomorphism $\varepsilon_\lambda\colon U_q^0\to\mathbb Q(q)$ ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]] (ii), [[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F3] $U_q^+$ is generated by the $E_i$ and presented by the positive Serre relations; hence the augmentation $\varepsilon_+\colon U_q^+\to\mathbb Q(q)$ with $\varepsilon_+(E_i)=0$ and $\varepsilon_+(1)=1$ is a well-defined algebra homomorphism. The root grading makes $U_q^\pm,U_q^0,U_q^{\ge0}$ graded subalgebras with $U_q^+[0]=\mathbb Q(q)1$ and $U_q^{\ge0}=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_+}U_q^{\ge0}[\nu]$, where $U_q^{\ge0}[0]=U_q^0$ ([[thm-triangular-decomposition-of-a-quantized-enveloping-algebra]] (ii)-(iii), [[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).

[F4] For $u\in U_q^-[-\nu]$, the commutator construction gives $E_iu=uE_i+(K_ie_i''(u)-K_i^{-1}e_i'(u))/(q_i-q_i^{-1})$, where both skew coefficients have degree $-(\nu-\alpha_i)$. Moving their toral factors to the right shows that $E_iu\in U_q^-[-\nu]E_i+U_q^-[-(\nu-\alpha_i)]U_q^0$, with the second summand zero if $\nu-\alpha_i\notin Q_+$ ([[def-kashiwara-skew-derivations-on-the-negative-quantum-half]]).

[F5] Root components of the negative half are finite dimensional: for $\nu=\sum_in_i\alpha_i$, a word of degree $-\nu$ has exactly $n_i$ letters $F_i$, so only finitely many words occur; they span $U_q^-[-\nu]$ ([[def-positive-negative-and-toral-quantum-subalgebras]]).



## Verification

**Proof technique:** build the Borel character by keeping the degree-zero part of the graded Borel and killing the positive part; identify the induced module with $U_q^-$ by right-freeness; then analyse graded submodules and quotients.

1.1 $\chi_\lambda$ is well defined: $U_q^{\ge0}=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_+}U_q^{\ge0}[\nu]$ is graded with $U_q^{\ge0}[0]=U_q^0$ by [F3], so every $b\in U_q^{\ge0}$ has a unique decomposition $b=b_0+b_+$ with $b_0\in U_q^0$ and $b_+\in\bigoplus_{\nu>0}U_q^{\ge0}[\nu]$. Define $\chi_\lambda(b):=\varepsilon_\lambda(b_0)$ with $\varepsilon_\lambda$ as in [F2] and $\chi_\lambda(b_+):=0$; this is $\mathbb Q(q)$-linear and well defined by the uniqueness of the decomposition. It is multiplicative: for $\nu,\nu'\in Q_+$, $U_q^{\ge0}[\nu]U_q^{\ge0}[\nu']\subseteq U_q^{\ge0}[\nu+\nu']$; if $\nu=\nu'=0$ both factors lie in $U_q^0$ and $\varepsilon_\lambda$ is multiplicative; if $\nu>0$ then $\chi_\lambda(bb')=0=\chi_\lambda(b)\chi_\lambda(b')$, and symmetrically for $\nu'>0$. Also $\chi_\lambda(1)=1$. Hence $\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda$ is a well-defined left $U_q^{\ge0}$-module. [given, F2, F3, algebra]

2.1 By [F1] the map $U_q^-\otimes U_q^{\ge0}\to U_q(\mathfrak g)$, $u\otimes b\mapsto ub$, is a vector-space isomorphism compatible with right multiplication by $U_q^{\ge0}$, so $U_q(\mathfrak g)$ is a free right $U_q^{\ge0}$-module on any basis of $U_q^-$; tensoring over $U_q^{\ge0}$ with $\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda$ gives a linear isomorphism $U_q^-\to M(\lambda)$, $u\mapsto u\otimes1=uv_\lambda$. Hence $M(\lambda)=\bigoplus_{\nu\in Q_+}U_q^-[-\nu]v_\lambda$ with the component $M(\lambda)_{\lambda-\nu}:=U_q^-[-\nu]v_\lambda$ and $M(\lambda)_\mu=0$ unless $\mu=\lambda-\nu$ with $\nu\in Q_+$; each component is finite dimensional and the top component is $\mathbb Q(q)v_\lambda$ by [F5]. The scalar conditions hold: $K_hv_\lambda=q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}v_\lambda$ because $K_h$ acts on $\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda$ by $\chi_\lambda(K_h)=q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}$ and $v_\lambda=1\otimes1$; $E_iv_\lambda=\chi_\lambda(E_i)v_\lambda=0$; and $M(\lambda)=U_qv_\lambda$. For the containment $E_iM(\lambda)_\mu\subseteq M(\lambda)_{\mu+\alpha_i}$ use [F4]: $E_i(u v_\lambda)=u'E_iv_\lambda+(u''b'')v_\lambda=u''v_\lambda$-terms with $u''\in U_q^-[ -(\nu-\alpha_i)]$ when $u\in U_q^-[-\nu]$, and $F_iu\in U_q^-[ -(\nu+\alpha_i)]$; combined with the $K_h$-eigenvalue computation $K_h(u v_\lambda)=q^{\langle\lambda-\nu,h\rangle}uv_\lambda$ this realizes $M(\lambda)$ as a $P$-graded type-one module with the stated grading. [step 1.1, F1, F4, F5, algebra]

3.1 Universal property: the $U_q^{\ge0}$-module map $\mathbb Q(q)_\lambda\to V$, $1\mapsto v$, is well defined exactly because $K_hv=q^{\langle\lambda,h\rangle}v$ and $E_iv=0$, i.e. because $\chi_\lambda$ acts as prescribed; the map $u\otimes c\mapsto cuv$ is balanced because $bv=\chi_\lambda(b)v$ for $b\in U_q^{\ge0}$, and therefore defines a $U_q$-module map $M(\lambda)\to V$. Any such map is determined by $v_\lambda$, so it is unique, and it is surjective when $V=U_qv$. If $V$ is $P$-graded and $v\in V_\lambda$, the map sends $U_q^-[-\nu]v_\lambda$ into $V_{\lambda-\nu}$ because $U_q^-[-\nu]$ lowers labels by $\nu$, so it is grading-preserving. [step 2.1, F1, algebra]

3.2 Proper graded submodules have zero top component: if $N\subseteq M(\lambda)$ is graded and $N\cap M(\lambda)_\lambda\ne0$, then $v_\lambda\in N$ (as $M(\lambda)_\lambda=\mathbb Q(q)v_\lambda$) and $M(\lambda)=U_qv_\lambda\subseteq N$, so $N=M(\lambda)$. Hence every proper graded submodule $N$ has $N_\lambda=0$, and then $(\sum_jN_j)_\lambda=\sum_j(N_j)_\lambda=0$ for any family of proper graded submodules, because the $\lambda$-component of a finite sum of vectors is the sum of components. Thus $N(\lambda)$, the sum of all proper graded submodules, is proper, graded, and contains every proper graded submodule. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The quotient $L(\lambda)=M(\lambda)/N(\lambda)$ is nonzero and inherits the $P$-grading. If $\bar P\subseteq L(\lambda)$ is a graded submodule, its preimage $P\subseteq M(\lambda)$ is graded and contains $N(\lambda)$; if $P\ne M(\lambda)$ then $P$ is proper graded, so $P\subseteq N(\lambda)$ and $\bar P=0$. Hence $L(\lambda)$ has no nonzero proper graded submodule. If $M(\lambda)/Q$ is any simple graded quotient with $Q$ graded, then $Q$ is proper graded (else the quotient is $0$), so $Q\subseteq N(\lambda)$, and $N(\lambda)/Q$ is a graded submodule of $M(\lambda)/Q$; if $Q\ne N(\lambda)$ it is nonzero, and it is proper because $N(\lambda)\ne M(\lambda)$, contradicting simplicity. Hence $Q=N(\lambda)$ and the graded-simple quotient is unique. Finally assume the pairing separates $P$: then any submodule $N$ is graded, because the $K_h$ act semisimply on $M(\lambda)$ (by the label grading of step 2.1 and the eigenvalue prescription) and $N=\bigoplus_\mu(N\cap M(\lambda)_\mu)$ — for a finite sum $x=\sum_jx_{\mu_j}\in N$, fix $j$ and for each $l\ne j$ choose $h_{jl}$ distinguishing their characters. The product $\prod_{l\ne j}(K_{h_{jl}}-q^{\langle\mu_l,h_{jl}\rangle})/(q^{\langle\mu_j,h_{jl}\rangle}-q^{\langle\mu_l,h_{jl}\rangle})$ sends $x$ to $x_{\mu_j}$ and preserves $N$, so every component lies in $N$; here the label spaces coincide with the simultaneous eigenspaces by separation, so this is the usual weight decomposition. Therefore all proper submodules are proper graded, $N(\lambda)$ is the unique maximal proper submodule, and $L(\lambda)$ is the ordinary simple quotient of the Verma module. The identifications of $N(\lambda)$ with the radical of the contravariant form and the dominant-integral simplicity statements are the declared content of `lem-cartan-projection-constructs-the-quantum-verma-contravariant-form` and `thm-integrable-quantum-highest-weight-module-exists-and-is-unique`, both of which are proved on this page. [step 3.1, step 3.2, F2, algebra] ∎
