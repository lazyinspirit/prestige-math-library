## Definition

Let $(E,\Phi)$ be a CM pair and let $L/\mathbb Q$ be a finite Galois closure of all factors of $E$ in $\mathbb C$. Set $\widetilde\Phi=\{\tau\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb Q}(L,\mathbb C):\tau|_E\in\Phi\}$. The **reflex field** $E^r$ is the subfield of $L$ generated by the type traces $\operatorname{Tr}_{\Phi}(a)=\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi(a)$ for $a\in E$. Equivalently, $E^r=L^{H_\Phi}$, where $H_\Phi=\{\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q):\{\sigma\circ\tau:\tau\in\widetilde\Phi\}=\widetilde\Phi\}$. The **reflex type** $\Phi^r$ is the CM type on the smallest CM subfield $K\subseteq L$ such that the restrictions of $\widetilde\Phi^{-1}=\{\tau^{-1}:\tau\in\widetilde\Phi\}$ to $K$ form a CM type and their full pullback to $L$ is $\widetilde\Phi^{-1}$; this subfield is $E^r$. For a product CM algebra, take the component reflex fields and their compositum, with the induced component types.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A CM pair $(E,\Phi)$ and a finite Galois closure $L/\mathbb Q$.

[F1] Distinct embeddings of a finite extension into a field are linearly independent as multiplicative characters of the source field's nonzero elements. [[thm-dedekind-linear-independence-of-characters]]

[F2] A finite separable extension has a finite Galois closure containing every conjugate embedding. [[def-galois-closure-of-a-finite-separable-extension]]

## Proof

1.1 Every type trace is fixed by $H_\Phi$, because each $\sigma\in H_\Phi$ permutes the summands $\varphi(a)$. Hence the trace-generated field is contained in $L^{H_\Phi}$. [given, algebra]

1.2 If $\sigma$ fixes every type trace, then $\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi}\sigma\varphi(a)-\sum_{\varphi\in\Phi}\varphi(a)=0$ for every $a\in E$. By [F1], equal sums of distinct embeddings have equal supports, so $\sigma\widetilde\Phi=\widetilde\Phi$. Thus the trace-generated field and $L^{H_\Phi}$ have the same fixing subgroup and are equal. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.1 For any CM type $\Psi$ on the Galois closure $L$, regard its embeddings as automorphisms of $L$ and put $H=\{h:\Psi h=\Psi\}$, using right composition. The field $L$ is the finite compositum of the conjugate CM fields. Its involution $\iota_L$ restricts to complex conjugation on each factor; conjugating $\iota_L$ by any $g\in\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)$ again restricts to complex conjugation on each conjugate factor, so the two automorphisms agree on generators of $L$ and are equal. Thus $\iota_L$ is central. Complex conjugation does not preserve $\Psi$, so $\iota_L\notin H$; centrality makes $H$ stable under conjugation by $\iota_L$. Therefore $L^H$ is stable under $\iota_L$, and the induced nontrivial involution makes $L^H$ a CM field. If two distinct $\psi,\psi'\in\Psi$ became conjugates on $L^H$, then $\psi'^{-1}\iota_L\psi$ would fix $L^H$, so it would lie in $H$; thus $\iota_L\psi=\psi'h$ for some $h\in H$, placing a conjugate of $\psi$ in $\Psi$, contrary to $\Psi\cap\iota_L\Psi=\varnothing$. Hence the restriction of $\Psi$ to $L^H$ is a CM type. Conversely, if $K\subset L$ is a CM subfield on which $\Psi$ restricts to a CM type, $\Psi$ is the pullback of that type; every $h$ fixing $K$ preserves $\Psi$, so $\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)\subseteq H$ and $K\supseteq L^H$. Thus $L^H$ is the unique smallest CM subfield to which $\Psi$ descends. [given, algebra]

2.2 Apply step 2.1 to $\Psi=\widetilde\Phi^{-1}$. Its right stabilizer is $H^r=\{g:\widetilde\Phi^{-1}g=\widetilde\Phi^{-1}\}$. Taking inverses shows $g\in H^r$ exactly when $g^{-1}\widetilde\Phi=\widetilde\Phi$, which is equivalent to $g\in H_\Phi$ because $H_\Phi$ is a subgroup. Thus its smallest field of descent is $L^{H_\Phi}=E^r$. Applying the argument factorwise gives the product convention. [F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
