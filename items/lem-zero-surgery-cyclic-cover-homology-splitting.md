## Statement

Assume AC. For every smooth oriented knot $K$ and $q\ge2$, the first homology of the $q$-fold cyclic cover $M_{0,q}$ of zero surgery has an isomorphism of abelian groups
$$H_1(M_{0,q};\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(\Sigma_q(K);\mathbb Z)\oplus\mathbb Z,$$
where the free summand is generated by $\widetilde\mu$, the closed lift of the $q$-fold meridian loop $\mu^q$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Full AC, a smooth oriented knot $K$, the integer $q\ge2$, the exterior cover $E_q$, its meridional filling $\Sigma_q(K)$, and its zero-surgery filling $M_{0,q}$.

[A1] Full AC is assumed wherever the cover and orientation conventions use the choice axiom. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] The zero-surgery cover is obtained by filling the common exterior cover $E_q$ along a lift of the preferred longitude. Its filling solid torus has core represented by the closed lift $\widetilde\mu$ of $\mu^q$. [[def-cyclic-zero-surgery-cover]]

[F2] The branched cover $\Sigma_q(K)$ is obtained by filling the same $E_q$ along the closed lift of $\mu^q$. [[def-cyclic-branched-cover-of-a-knot]]

[F3] The exterior homology is generated by the meridian; the preferred longitude is a degree-one peripheral curve nullhomologous in the exterior. The induced homomorphism $\ell_K:\pi_1(E_K)\to\mathbb Z$ sends $\mu$ to $1$ and is the meridian-coordinate map. [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]] [[def-knot-meridian-and-preferred-longitude]]

[F4] Every smooth oriented knot bounds a connected compact smooth oriented Seifert surface. Full AC supplies the countable-choice hypothesis of this supplier. [[thm-every-oriented-knot-bounds-a-seifert-surface]] [[def-countable-choice]] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F5] For a one-component knot, the total-linking homomorphism is the meridian-coordinate map $\ell_K$ from [F3]; it equals signed intersection with a Seifert surface. [[lem-total-linking-equals-intersection-with-link-seifert-surface]] [[thm-knot-exterior-homology-and-longitude]]

[F6] Mayer–Vietoris computes the first homology of each filling from the common boundary torus, and the H0 map is injective because all three spaces are connected. [[thm-mayer-vietoris-sequence-in-singular-homology]]

[F7] The unbranched exterior cover is classified by the kernel of $\ell_K\bmod q$; a connected, locally path-connected surface whose fundamental-group image lies in this subgroup lifts to the cover, for each prescribed point above its basepoint. [[def-cyclic-branched-cover-of-a-knot]] [[thm-classification-of-connected-covering-spaces]] [[thm-covering-space-lifting-criterion]]

[L1] Conway's Lemma 2.31 states the same splitting and gives a Mayer–Vietoris proof. The complete proof in the retrieved §2.6 was read; the explicit meridian retraction below makes the splitting step transparent. [Anthony Conway, *Algebraic Concordance and Casson–Gordon Invariants*, §2.6, Lemma 2.31, pp. 23–24](https://web.archive.org/web/20240101000000id_/https://www.unige.ch/math/folks/conway/Notes/AlgebraicConcordanceCassonGordon.pdf)

## Proof



1.1 Choose a Seifert surface for $K$ by [F4] and remove its boundary collar to obtain a properly embedded surface $F_0$ in the knot exterior. Its boundary is a degree-one longitude curve, hence represents the preferred longitude by [F3]. Each element of $\pi_1(F_0)$ has a smooth loop representative. Push that loop slightly to one side of the Seifert surface; the push is homotopic in the exterior and disjoint from the surface, so [F5] gives meridian coordinate zero. Thus $\ell_K\bmod q$ vanishes on $\pi_1(F_0)$, and [F7] lifts $F_0$ to each sheet of $E_q$. Each lifted copy has one boundary component over the preferred longitude, proving that every lifted longitude is nullhomologous in $E_q$. [A1, F3, F4, F5, F7, given, construct]

1.2 In the branched filling, $\widetilde\mu$ bounds the attached solid torus's meridian disk and a longitude lift maps to its core generator. The same Mayer–Vietoris cokernel calculation [F6] gives $H_1(\Sigma_q(K);\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(E_q;\mathbb Z)/\langle[\widetilde\mu]\rangle$. [F2, F6, algebra]

2.1 In the zero-surgery filling, the boundary-torus basis consists of the closed meridian lift $\widetilde\mu$ and a longitude lift $\widetilde\lambda$. The latter bounds a meridian disk in the attached solid torus and the former maps to its core generator. The Mayer–Vietoris cokernel in [F6] therefore eliminates the core generator and gives $H_1(M_{0,q};\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(E_q;\mathbb Z)/\langle[\widetilde\lambda]\rangle$. Step 1.1 makes this quotient $H_1(E_q;\mathbb Z)$. [F1, F6, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The cover subgroup is $\ker(\ell_K\bmod q)$ by [F7]. Thus $\ell_K(g)$ is divisible by $q$ for every $g\in\pi_1(E_q)$, and $g\mapsto\ell_K(g)/q$ is a homomorphism $\pi_1(E_q)\to\mathbb Z$. It descends to $r:H_1(E_q;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z$ and satisfies $r([\widetilde\mu])=1$, since $\widetilde\mu$ projects to $\mu^q$. Therefore $\mathbb Z[\widetilde\mu]$ is a direct summand and $H_1(E_q)\cong H_1(E_q)/\langle[\widetilde\mu]\rangle\oplus\mathbb Z[\widetilde\mu]$. Using step 1.2 and then step 2.1 proves the statement. [F3, F7, step 2.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
