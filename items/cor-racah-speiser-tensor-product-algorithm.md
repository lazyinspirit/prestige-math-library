## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda^+$. For every weight
$\varphi$ of $L(\mu)$ with multiplicity $m_\mu(\varphi)>0$ put
$\psi=\varphi+\lambda$ and say that $\varphi$ is **regular relative to
$\lambda$** when $\psi+\rho$ is fixed by no reflection of $W$, equivalently
$\langle\psi+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle\ne0$ for every root $\alpha$. If
$\varphi$ is regular relative to $\lambda$, there is a unique $u\in W$ with
$u(\psi+\rho)$ strictly dominant
([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]]), and then
$\nu(\varphi):=u(\psi+\rho)-\rho=u\cdot\psi$ is a dominant integral weight
(the shifted action being $u\cdot\xi=u(\xi+\rho)-\rho$). The Racah--Speiser
algorithm computes the multiplicities of
[[thm-steinberg-tensor-product-multiplicity-formula]] as
$$c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}=\sum_{\substack{\varphi\text{ weight of }L(\mu)\\ \varphi\text{ regular relative to }\lambda,\ \nu(\varphi)=\nu}}(-1)^{\ell(u(\varphi))}\,m_\mu(\varphi),$$
the sum being finite; a weight $\varphi$ for which $\psi+\rho$ is not regular
is discarded, and every $\nu\in\Lambda^+$ with $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$ occurs
as $\nu(\varphi)$ for some regular weight $\varphi$ of $L(\mu)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, dominant integral weights $\lambda,\mu,\nu$, the weight set of $L(\mu)$ with multiplicities $m_\mu(\varphi)=\dim L(\mu)_\varphi$ ([[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]), and the Weyl group $W$ acting on $\mathfrak h^*$ by the reflection action ([[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]]).

[F1] Steinberg's formula:
$$c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}=\sum_{w\in W}(-1)^{\ell(w)}m_\mu\bigl(w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)-(\lambda+\rho)\bigr),$$
the sum being finite
([[thm-steinberg-tensor-product-multiplicity-formula]]).

[F2] The shifted dominant weight $\nu+\rho$ is strictly dominant, and $\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$ ([[lem-positive-root-pairings-of-a-dominant-integral-weight]]). Every orbit in the real root span $E$ has exactly one point in the closed dominant chamber. Its stabilizer is generated by reflections in the simple walls through that point. Consequently a regular $\xi\in E$ has a strictly dominant representative and a unique element $u$ sending it there; uniqueness of the element is asserted only for regular points ([[lem-finite-weyl-closed-chambers-and-stabilizers]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]], [[def-finite-weyl-root-system-lattice-and-chamber-conventions]]). In particular $\xi=\varphi+\lambda+\rho$ is integral; if regular, all simple-coroot pairings of $u\xi$ are positive integers, so subtracting $\rho$ leaves nonnegative integral pairings and $u\xi-\rho\in\Lambda^+$.

[F3] Length parity: $\ell(ws)\equiv\ell(w)+1\pmod 2$ for every reflection $s\in W$ (the sign $\varepsilon(w)=(-1)^{\ell(w)}$ is a homomorphism), so $(-1)^{\ell(ws)}=-(-1)^{\ell(w)}$ ([[lem-weyl-length-parity-is-multiplicative]]).

## Proof

1.1 Rewrite the Steinberg sum by the second argument. For $w\in W$ put $\varphi_w:=w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)-(\lambda+\rho)$ and $\psi_w+\rho:=w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)=\varphi_w+\lambda+\rho$, so that $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}=\sum_w(-1)^{\ell(w)}m_\mu(\varphi_w)$ by [F1], the sum being finite. Fix a weight $\varphi$ of $L(\mu)$ and let $W(\varphi)=\{w\in W:\varphi_w=\varphi\}=\{w:w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)=\psi+\rho\}$ where $\psi=\varphi+\lambda$. This set is nonempty exactly when $\psi+\rho$ is $W$-conjugate to $\nu+\rho$. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Regular weights contribute one term each. Suppose $\varphi$ is regular, so that $\psi+\rho$ has trivial stabilizer. If $W(\varphi)\ne\emptyset$, then $\nu+\rho=w(\psi+\rho)$ is strictly dominant for any $w\in W(\varphi)$, so by the uniqueness in [F2] there is exactly one such $w$, namely $w=u(\varphi)$ where $u(\varphi)$ is the unique element with $u(\varphi)(\psi+\rho)$ strictly dominant; in that case $\nu(\varphi)=u(\varphi)(\psi+\rho)-\rho=\nu$, and the contribution of $\varphi$ to $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}$ is $(-1)^{\ell(u(\varphi))}m_\mu(\varphi)$. If $W(\varphi)=\emptyset$, or if $\nu(\varphi)\ne\nu$, the weight $\varphi$ contributes nothing to $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}$. [F1, F2, given, algebra]

2.1 Irregular weights cancel. Suppose $\varphi$ is not regular: $\psi+\rho$ is fixed by some reflection $s$. Then $W(\varphi)$ is stable under right multiplication by $s$, because $(ws)^{-1}(\nu+\rho)=s\,w^{-1}(\nu+\rho)=s(\psi+\rho)=\psi+\rho$ for every $w\in W(\varphi)$; the map $w\mapsto ws$ is a fixed-point-free involution of $W(\varphi)$, and by [F3] the signs of paired terms are opposite. Since the multiplicity $m_\mu(\varphi)$ is the same for paired terms, the total contribution of the group $W(\varphi)$ to the sum of step 1.1 is $0$. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Combining step 1.2 and 2.1, the value of $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}$ is the sum of $(-1)^{\ell(u(\varphi))}m_\mu(\varphi)$ over the regular weights $\varphi$ of $L(\mu)$ with $\nu(\varphi)=\nu$, which is the displayed Racah--Speiser formula; the sum is finite because $L(\mu)$ has finitely many weights. If $c^\nu_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$, the displayed sum is nonzero, so at least one regular weight $\varphi$ satisfies $\nu(\varphi)=\nu$; this proves the final assertion. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
