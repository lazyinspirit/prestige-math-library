## Example

Rotate the region

$$\{(x,y):0\le x\le\pi,\ 0\le y\le\sin x\}$$

about the $x$-axis. The resulting solid is compact and Jordan measurable, and
its volume is

$$V=\frac{\pi^2}{2}.$$

The vanishing endpoint radii are included in the solid and require no separate
measurability argument.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The profile $f(x)=\sin x$ on $[0,\pi]$ and its solid of revolution about the $x$-axis.

[F1] If $a\le b$ and $f:[a,b]\to[0,\infty)$ is continuous, then its solid of revolution about the $x$-axis is compact and Jordan measurable and has volume $\pi\int_a^b f(x)^2\,dx$ ([[thm-volume-of-a-solid-of-revolution-by-discs]]).

[L1] $\sin\pi=0$, and $\sin x>0$ for every $x$ with $0<x<\pi$; thus $\pi$ is the first positive zero of sine, in particular $\pi>0$ ([[cor-pi-is-the-first-positive-sine-zero]]).

[L2] The functions $\sin$ and $\cos$ are differentiable on $\mathbb R$, with $(\sin x)'=\cos x$ and $(\cos x)'=-\sin x$; also $\sin0=0$ and $\cos0=1$ ([[thm-sine-and-cosine-derivatives]]).

[L3] For a real-valued function on $A\subseteq\mathbb R$, differentiability at a limit point $c\in A$ implies continuity there ([[cor-differentiable-implies-continuous]]).

[L4] For every real $x$, $\sin^2x=(1-\cos2x)/2$ ([[thm-double-angle-and-power-reduction-identities]]).

[L5] Every continuous real-valued function on a nondegenerate closed interval is Riemann integrable there ([[thm-continuous-implies-integrable]]).

[L6] The integral is linear on integrable functions ([[thm-linearity-of-the-integral]]).

[L7] If $a<b$, every constant function on $[a,b]$ is integrable and $\int_a^b c=c(b-a)$ ([[lem-integral-elementary-bounds]]).

[L8] If $a<b$, $G:[a,b]\to\mathbb R$ is differentiable at every point of $[a,b]$, and $G'$ is integrable, then $\int_a^bG'=G(b)-G(a)$ ([[thm-ftc-second-part]]).

[L9] If real functions $g$ and $h$ are differentiable at the relevant points, then $(g\circ h)'=(g'\circ h)h'$ ([[thm-chain-rule]]).

[L10] Sums and scalar multiples obey the usual derivative rules ([[thm-algebra-of-derivatives]]).

[L11] Both sine and cosine have period $2\pi$ ([[thm-sine-cosine-zero-sets-and-fundamental-period]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Facts [L1], [L2], and [L3] show that $f$ is continuous and nonnegative on $[0,\pi]$, so [F1] makes the rotated solid compact and Jordan measurable and gives $V=\pi\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx$. [F1, L1, L2, L3]

1.2 Since $\pi>0$ by [L1], the interval $[0,\pi]$ is nondegenerate. The function $G(x)=\tfrac12\sin(2x)$ is differentiable with $G'(x)=\cos(2x)$, and this derivative is continuous and therefore integrable on $[0,\pi]$. [L1, L2, L3, L5, L9, L10]

1.3 On the same nondegenerate interval, the constant function $1$ is integrable and has integral $\pi$. [L1, L7, algebra]

2.1 By power reduction and linearity, followed by the fundamental theorem applied to step 1.2, $\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\tfrac12\int_0^\pi1\,dx-\tfrac12\int_0^\pi\cos(2x)\,dx=\pi/2-\tfrac12(G(\pi)-G(0))=\pi/2$, because periodicity and [L2] give $G(\pi)=G(0)=0$. [step 1.2, step 1.3, L2, L4, L6, L8, L11, algebra]

3.1 Substituting step 2.1 into the disc formula of step 1.1 gives $V=\pi(\pi/2)=\pi^2/2$. [step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The profile radius vanishes at both endpoints, but [F1] permits nonnegative continuous profiles and explicitly includes zero-radius sections. No division by the profile occurs.

Its radius is $\sin t$ and its area is $\pi\sin^2t$, as used in step 1.1. The filled dots at $x=0$ and $x=\pi$ represent the included zero-radius sections.
