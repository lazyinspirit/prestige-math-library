## Statement

Let $R$ be a PID, let $M$ be a nonzero finite free $R$-module, and let $0\ne N\le M$. A nonzero submodule of a finite free PID module admits a primitive pivot splitting both the ambient module and submodule. More precisely, there are $\psi\in M^*$, $e_1\in M$, a nonzero $a\in R$, and $v=ae_1\in N$ such that $\psi(e_1)=1$, the ideal $\psi(N)=(a)$ is maximal among value ideals $\varphi(N)$ containing a fixed nonzero value ideal, and

$$M=Re_1\oplus\ker\psi,\qquad N=Rae_1\oplus(N\cap\ker\psi).$$

Moreover $e_1$ belongs to a basis of $M$, and $\ker\psi$ is free of rank one less than the rank of $M$.

The maximality is taken among all functional value ideals, so it remains available after the pivot is split off.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The dual module $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}_R(M,R)$ of [[def-internal-hom-module-over-a-commutative-ring]] and coordinate functionals from a finite free basis ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]).

[L1] Every principal ideal domain is a unique factorisation domain ([[thm-principal-ideal-domains-are-unique-factorisation-domains]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Some coordinate functional has a nonzero value on $N$. Fix one such nonzero value ideal $I_0$. By [L1], a nonzero generator of $I_0$ has only finitely many divisor classes, so only finitely many principal ideals can contain $I_0$. Choose a maximal value ideal among them, write it as $\psi(N)=(a)$ with $a\ne0$, and choose $v\in N$ with $\psi(v)=a$. [L1, choose]

2.1 For any coordinate functional $\varphi$, let $d$ generate $(a,\varphi(v))$ and choose $r,s$ with $d=ra+s\varphi(v)$. The functional $r\psi+s\varphi$ takes $v$ to $d$, so its value ideal contains $(d)\supseteq(a)$; maximality in step 1.1 forces $(d)=(a)$, hence $a\mid\varphi(v)$. [step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Divisibility of every coordinate of $v$ gives $v=ae_1$ for some $e_1\in M$. Since $a=\psi(v)=a\psi(e_1)$ and $a\ne0$, cancellation gives $\psi(e_1)=1$, so $e_1$ is primitive. [step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The ideal generated by the coordinates of $e_1$ in a basis of $M$ is $\{\varphi(e_1):\varphi\in M^*\}$, so it does not depend on the basis, and $\psi(e_1)=1$ makes it all of $R$. Fix a basis $f_1,\ldots,f_n$ of $M$ and let $b_1,\ldots,b_n$ be the coordinates of $e_1$ in it. For $j\ge2$ let $d$ generate $(b_1,b_j)$. If $d=0$ then $b_1=b_j=0$ and nothing is done; otherwise write $d=rb_1+sb_j$ and put $A=\begin{pmatrix}r&s\\-b_j/d&b_1/d\end{pmatrix}$, whose entries lie in $R$ and whose determinant is $(rb_1+sb_j)/d=1$, so $A^{-1}$ has entries in $R$ as well. Replacing the basis pair $(f_1,f_j)$ by the pair whose coordinates are the columns of $A^{-1}$ again gives a basis of $M$, in which the coordinates of $e_1$ at $1$ and $j$ are the entries of $A(b_1,b_j)^{\mathsf T}=(d,0)^{\mathsf T}$ and the other coordinates are unchanged. Doing this for $j=2,\ldots,n$ in turn clears the coordinates $2,\ldots,n$, so the resulting basis $g_1,\ldots,g_n$ has $e_1=cg_1$ with $(c)$ the coordinate ideal, which is $R$. Hence $c$ is a unit and $e_1,g_2,\ldots,g_n$ is a basis of $M$. [step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Put $u_j=g_j-\psi(g_j)e_1$ for $2\le j\le n$. The passage from $e_1,g_2,\ldots,g_n$ to $e_1,u_2,\ldots,u_n$ is triangular with $1$ on the diagonal, so the latter is again a basis of $M$, and each $u_j$ lies in $\ker\psi$. If $m=\lambda_1e_1+\sum_{j\ge2}\lambda_ju_j$ lies in $\ker\psi$, applying $\psi$ gives $\lambda_1=0$; hence $u_2,\ldots,u_n$ is a basis of $\ker\psi$, which is therefore free of rank $n-1$, and zero when $n=1$. Every $m\in M$ is $\psi(m)e_1+(m-\psi(m)e_1)$ with the second summand in $\ker\psi$, and $Re_1\cap\ker\psi=0$ because $\psi(re_1)=r$, so $M=Re_1\oplus\ker\psi$. For $w\in N$ one has $\psi(w)\in\psi(N)=(a)$, say $\psi(w)=ra$, and then $w=rv+(w-rv)$ with $w-rv\in N\cap\ker\psi$; also $Rv\cap\ker\psi=0$, since $\psi(rv)=ra$ and $a\ne0$ in the domain $R$. Hence $N=Rae_1\oplus(N\cap\ker\psi)$. When $a$ is a unit, $v$ and $e_1$ generate the same submodule and the second decomposition reads $N=Re_1\oplus(N\cap\ker\psi)$. [step 4.1, step 3.1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
