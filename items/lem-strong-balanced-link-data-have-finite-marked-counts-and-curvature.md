## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Supply a complete balanced Heegaard diagram of a nonempty oriented based link in a connected closed oriented three-manifold, with one w and one z mark in every alpha and beta cut ball. Fix a Morse Spin-c sector and impose the all-w-normalized strict Chern coefficient condition, including strict positivity at Chern evaluation zero. In the actual nearly-symmetric symmetric-product setup with transverse product-torus corners there exist regular finite polynomial full-marked disk counts D over the ring with all marked variables independent. Their square is the scalar sum of U_w V_z over the adjacent marked link intervals. Uniform identification of all w variables and of all z variables makes D square to zero. No torsion implication from the w structure to the z structure is assumed. The local marked pair insertion used in the proof has finite low-index positive domains without an assumption that it preserves the full strong-admissibility criterion.

## Proof

1.1 On each actual strip use integer Sobolev map charts with exponential end weight below the finitely many end spectral gaps, with the torus boundary conditions imposed by their tubular charts. The linearization is $\partial_s+J_t\partial_t$ plus bounded zeroth-order terms. On an end its fixed operator has Fourier modes with nonzero rates: if the two boundary lines have angle $\vartheta_j$, the rates are $\vartheta_j+\pi k$. The transverse angles are nonzero modulo $\pi$. Integrate each mode from the end on which it decays. The resulting end inverses, together with the reflected interior/boundary Cauchy estimates on a compact middle piece, give a parametrix; cutoff commutators have compact support and are compact after the higher-to-lower Sobolev inclusion. Hence the operator is Fredholm. Its integer index is the actual capped determinant winding, with the given corner conventions, and its kernels and cokernels are smooth by elliptic bootstrap. This constructs the two-ended Fredholm problem rather than applying a knot-label theorem to it. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.2 Here is the injective-point argument needed for universal regularity. Regard a nonconstant solution as the map $(s,t)\mapsto(u(s,t),t)$. On the open set $u_s\ne0$ it is an immersion. Work in a relatively compact regular chart avoiding the two limit generators. Finite energy and end convergence make all of its other preimages lie in a compact s interval. Critical points have isolated images by the same continuation argument applied to $u_s$; first remove those finitely many images on a compact piece. Local injectivity separates every other preimage from the diagonal by a positive source distance on a smaller chart. Cover that remaining interval by countably many small immersion charts disjoint from this diagonal neighborhood. If the given chart had no point without another preimage at the same t, Baire's theorem applied to their closed smaller images would give an open coincident sheet with one of the other charts. The sheet relation has the form $u(s,t)=u(\sigma(s,t),t)$. The two Cauchy–Riemann equations imply $(\sigma_s-1)u_s(\sigma,t)+\sigma_tJ_tu_s(\sigma,t)=0.$ The displayed two vectors are real-linearly independent, so $\sigma_s=1$ and $\sigma_t=0$. Thus the sheet shift is a constant $c\ne0$. Unique continuation extends $u(s+c,t)=u(s,t)$ throughout the strip, which contradicts finite energy unless the solution is constant. The same Baire argument in every regular chart proves the needed density of points with no other preimage at the same t. This also supplies such a point in the free tensor-variation region: a nonconstant strip cannot be confined to a small exact transverse generator chart, where its energy would be zero; leaving that chart crosses a free annulus before reaching the protected divisors. A nonconstant solution has $u_s\ne0$ on an open dense set. The relevant unique-continuation statement is obtained by writing the difference of two local solutions in complex frames as $\bar\partial v+Bv+C\bar v=0$ with bounded coefficients. Absorb the antilinear term into a bounded measurable complex matrix along v, solve its small-disk Cauchy-integral gauge equation and shrink the disk until the gauge is invertible. The gauged vector is holomorphic. Its zeros are isolated unless it vanishes identically, which gives the continuation assertion used above. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.3 Let $\eta$ be a nonzero cokernel section. It solves the adjoint equation and by the same continuation argument cannot vanish on the free regular set. At an injective point in that set the allowed anti-complex-linear tensor variation $Y_t(u)$ makes $Y_t(u)u_t$ an arbitrary tangent vector: prescribe its value on the nonzero vector $u_t$ and its negative J-conjugate on $J_tu_t$. A supported variation near this point pairs nontrivially with $\eta$; the injective-point property prevents cancellation by another sheet at the same t. Therefore the universal linearization has no cokernel. Take a countable dense collection of smooth allowed variations and weight their coefficients sufficiently rapidly that an $\ell^2$ sequence converges in every required derivative order. This is an actual separable Hilbert parameter space of admissible smooth tensors, and the same cokernel argument applies by density. Fix source translation by a compactly supported smooth linear functional nonzero on the actual smooth translation vector of the reference solution. This supplies a genuine phase hypersurface in the Sobolev chart. It does not assume a differentiable translation or general composition action on a fixed-order Sobolev map space. On the bootstrapped solution set the actual translations recover the orbit quotient. On each such map-space chart the universal zero set is a Banach manifold and the projection to these parameters is Fredholm. Sard–Smale and the Baire intersection over a countable atlas and countably many homotopy components give regular data for every needed strip index at once. Homotopy components are countable without a domain/class-bijection assumption: nearby maps are homotopic through their boundary tubular charts, so components are open in the separable Sobolev map space. Only countable selections are needed in this particular regularity construction. The imported compactification, index-budget and gluing facts retain their declared full-Choice assumption; this step does not lower that assumption. Constant strips have their fixed invertible end model; a regular nonconstant strip of index at most zero is impossible, because its nonzero translation vector belongs to its kernel. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.4 The finite nonnegative domain list bounds the physical energy by the supplied degree-energy estimate. Apply fixed-target elliptic extraction to the actual maps, retaining all independent marked intersection degrees and both torus boundary labels. This gives strips, one-system boundary disks and interior sphere pieces, with the actual fixed-corner determinant index. Every nonconstant principal strip has positive index by step 1.3. Every nonconstant positive one-system boundary disk has index at least two, and at index two is a unit cut-component domain. A nonconstant sphere has a positive integer full-surface degree and index $2n$ times that degree. For $n>1$ a sphere therefore cannot occur in a total-index-at-most-two limit. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.5 At index one there can be no bubble, so the regular zero-dimensional translation quotient is compact. A compact discrete space is finite, proving that the index-one matrix is an actual finite polynomial matrix, even if there were infinitely many abstract classes with a common domain. When $n>1$, at index two its one-dimensional quotient has just two types of ends: two index-one strips, or a unit one-system degeneration attached to the constant strip. Zero-domain ghosts cannot introduce a second positive vertex at this budget; the distinguished end normalization and the actual unit-degeneration matching retain their automorphism parameters. Broken regular strips have their actual collar from the long-strip construction: separate their translations, retain the relative neck length, patch their normal inverses and correct the exponentially small error. The common transverse end has no zero mode. The unit-bubble collar instead uses the NEW one-system inverse and its onto real boundary-attachment map, together with the invertible constant-strip operator. Those are exactly the matching modes required here. Extraction followed by the same phase/root slices and contraction uniqueness exhausts these collars. Thus for $n>1$ the boundary of the compactified one-manifold is precisely the actual weighted ends being counted. For $n=1$ only the proved compactness and regularity of the index-one counts are used below; no separate assertion about the parity or emptiness of its unit boundary-degeneration moduli is required. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.6 Construct the actual pair insertion as follows. In a small old marked region take coordinates with the surface at height zero and the oriented old knot segment $(s,0,-s)$ near its w crossing. Replace it by the embedded graph $(s,0,-s h(s))$, where $h(s)=(s-a)(s-b)$ near $0<a<b$ and h is one outside the chosen interval, positive on its other portions. The interpolation is an isotopy through graphs, since their first coordinate is s. The crossings are the old w at zero, new z at a, and new w at b; the sides alternate beta, alpha, beta, alpha. Draw the new alpha disk around the old w and new z, and the new beta disk around the new z and new w, with their boundaries crossing twice and missing every old curve. Each is an actual compression disk splitting the corresponding old cut ball along the two boundary-parallel subarcs; each resulting cut ball has one w, one z and one bridge. Other bridges and all other marks are retained. Thus this is an actual balanced diagram of the same oriented link, with one additional marked pair, and generators are literally old generators times the two local intersection points. The remote old z is not discarded or moved into a local bigon. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.7 Pair insertion need not be asserted to preserve the full strong-admissibility condition. The following direct bound is what its low-index compactness requires. For fixed old corners extend the index to formal integral region chains by $\ell(B)=e(B)+n_x(B)+n_y(B)$, with the fixed corner-cap constant if those caps use another normalization. This is the actual determinant index on realized classes. On a formal periodic chain P its difference is $\ell(P)=\langle c_1(\mathfrak t),H(P)\rangle+2\sum_i n_{w_i}(P)$: cap its alpha and beta boundary components by the supplied compression disks and compare the two corner sections; the boundary winding gives the Chern term, and crossing a w divisor gives two. This difference formula concerns formal capped region chains and requires no realization of every chain by a holomorphic or topological strip. Let $A_i$ be the alpha cut-ball region chain containing $w_i$. It has one w multiplicity, cap class zero and $\ell(A_i)=2$; this follows by capping its boundary by that ball's compression disks. Suppose a nonzero nonnegative periodic P had $\ell(P)\le0$. Put $m_i=n_{w_i}(P)$, $M=\sum_i m_i$ and $Q=P-\sum_i m_iA_i$. All w multiplicities of Q vanish. The normalized chain $-Q$ has Chern evaluation $2N=2M-\ell(P)\ge2M$. Its every coefficient is at most M, since the cut components partition the regions and P is nonnegative. Moreover Q cannot be zero: otherwise $\ell(P)=2M$, forcing $M=0$ and P zero. This contradicts the supplied STRICT coefficient-greater-than-N criterion, including N zero. Consequently there is no nonzero nonnegative periodic direction with $\ell\le0$. For each fixed corner pair, the real polyhedron of nonnegative formal domains with $\ell\le C$ is bounded: an unbounded polyhedron defined by these integral boundary equations and rational index inequality has a nonzero rational recession direction; clearing denominators gives precisely the excluded integral P. Its lattice points are therefore finite. This proves the old formal-domain bound for every fixed C, including formal domains that are not realized at low symmetric-product rank. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.8 In the actual small-disk insertion, denote the three bounded face coefficients by r, s, t in cyclic order, with s the new z lens, and the surrounding coefficient by k. All four are nonnegative. Remove the new circles and give their old region the coefficient k. The resulting old formal domain B has the same old corner pair. The boundary jump at the new pair of intersections is $\kappa=r+t-s-k\in\{-1,0,1\}$, determined by its two new corners; equal corners give zero. The three bounded bigons each have Euler measure $1/2$, and the old surrounding region loses $3/2$. Thus their change in Euler measure is $(r+s+t-3k)/2$. The sum of the two new corner averages is $(r+s+t+k)/2$. All old corner terms are unchanged. The actual index formula therefore reads $\mu_{\rm new}=\ell(B)+r+s+t-k=\ell(B)+2s+\kappa$, up to the already fixed old cap constant. For an actual nonnegative domain with $\mu_{\rm new}\le2$, this bounds $\ell(B)\le3$ with that same fixed constant. Step1.7 bounds B, hence bounds k and gives a lower bound for $\ell(B)$. The displayed identity then bounds s, and $r+t=s+k+\kappa$ bounds r and t separately. There are only finitely many old and new corner pairs. This proves a finite nonnegative domain list and hence a physical energy bound for the actual inserted diagram at indices one and two. The argument includes the old rank-one torus case without assuming a domain/class bijection. In old rank zero the entire insertion is the explicitly counted four-marked sphere, so its actual finite matrix supplies the bootstrap directly. No assertion of full strong-admissibility transfer has been used. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.9 Define $D$ by these finite index-one counts over the ring with every w and z variable independent. For $n>1$, broken ends of step 1.5 contribute $D^2$. Unit alpha and beta ends occur only on the diagonal. For $n>1$ their normalized counts are one, and a unit cut component containing $w_i,z_j$ contributes precisely $U_{w_i}V_{z_j}$. Its alpha or beta bridge is the corresponding adjacent interval of the actual link. Hence the boundary count gives $D^2=\left(\sum_{\text{adjacent }w,z\text{ intervals}}U_wV_z\right)I.$ The independent-variable identity is the input for the explicit basepoint derivative/Hasse homotopies; it has been obtained before coloring. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.10 The stretched chosen $D^+$ can be made index-two regular without changing its finite rigid count matrix: its index-one representatives are regular and form a finite compact quotient. Their inverse-function neighborhoods persist under small perturbations. If new rigid representatives escaped all those neighborhoods along a sequence of smaller perturbations, the same finite-domain energy bound and index-one compactness would give a limiting representative in that finite set, a contradiction. Choose the generic index-two perturbation inside this neighborhood and retain the one-system unit normalization. Thus this actual count matrix satisfies the n-greater-than-one boundary identity even if its initial split tensors were not index-two generic. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct]

1.11 For $n=1$, start with the ACTUAL finite regular index-one matrix $D$ already constructed in steps 1.1–1.5, without assuming any curvature identity. Perform the explicit local pair insertion of step 1.6. The resulting actual diagram has $n=2$. Steps1.7–1.8 supply its actual finite low-index domain and energy bounds; steps 1.1–1.5 then supply its regularity and compactification, so the new unit normalization and step 1.9 apply. Choose the sufficiently stretched count data in the newly authored colored-neck operation; its actual count-matrix conclusion uses only the old good finite counts, fixed-end analytic compactness, the verified low-index admissibility bounds and the new unit normalization, not an assumed old scalar curvature. With $a=U_w+U_{w'}$ and $b=V_z+V_{z'}$, it gives $D^+=\begin{pmatrix}D&aI\\bI&D\end{pmatrix}.$ The auxiliary variables are independent. The original one-pair link has two adjacent intervals, and insertion replaces them by the four intervals joining $w',z,w,z'$ in their alternating order. Their curvature is exactly $ab$. Therefore the already proved $n=2$ curvature identity gives $(D^+)^2=abI$. Direct multiplication of its ACTUAL matrix gives diagonal blocks $D^2+abI$, hence $D^2=0$ over the polynomial extension. The extension of the old variable ring is injective, so this is the identity over the old ring itself. This bootstrap constructs the one-pair chain complex without proving, or presupposing, that its individual alpha or beta degeneration moduli have zero count or are empty.   Finally color all w variables by U and all z variables by V. For $n>1$ the curvature becomes $2n\,UV=0$, and for $n=1$ it already vanishes by the bootstrap. This gives actual finite regular two-color complexes for every supplied strong diagram, with every marked degree retained and with no connected-knot assumption. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F5, F6, F7, F8, A1, construct] ∎
