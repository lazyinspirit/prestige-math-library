## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the named suppliers. Let $(G,T)$ be a split reductive group with Borel $B\supseteq T$, base $\Delta$ and $U=B_u$ ([[def-roots-and-root-groups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]). (a) $G$ is generated by $T$ and the subgroups $G_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in\Delta$, hence by $T$ and the $U_{\pm\alpha}$ with $\alpha\in\Delta$; if $G$ is semisimple it is generated by the $U_\alpha$, $\alpha\in\Phi$. (b) For $w\in W(G,T)$ let $U_w$ (resp. $U^w$) be the subgroup generated by the $U_\alpha$ with $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w(\Phi^+)$ (resp. $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w(\Phi^-)$). These subgroups are smooth, connected and $T$-stable, equal to the products of their root groups in any order; the multiplication map $U_w\times U^w\to U$ is an isomorphism; and $U_\alpha\subseteq U_w$ iff $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^+$, while $U_\alpha\subseteq U^w$ iff $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^-$. (c) The isotropy group of $wB/B$ in $U$ equals $U_w$, the orbit map $U^w\to UwB/B$ is an isomorphism, and $\dim(UwB/B)=n(w)=|\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^-|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a split reductive group $(G,T)$ with Borel $B\supseteq T$, base $\Delta$, $U=B_u$, and an element $w\in W(G,T)$.

[F1] $G$ is generated by $T$ and the root groups; $W\cdot\Delta=\Phi$, $n_wU_\alpha n_w^{-1}=U_{w\alpha}$, and $W(G,T)\cong W(R)$ with simple reflections generating $W$ ([[thm-weyl-group-borel-chambers]], [[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], [[lem-root-datum-combinatorics]]).

[F2] Products of root groups: for a Borel $B$ with positive system $\Phi^+$, the multiplication map $\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}U_\alpha\to U$ is a $T$-equivariant isomorphism for any ordering; every smooth $T$-stable subgroup of $U$ is the product of the $U_\alpha$ it contains, and the weight set of such a subgroup is quasi-closed; ordinary root closure is sufficient in every characteristic and is necessary in characteristic $0$ or $p>3$ ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]]).

[F3] The Lie functor detects generation for smooth connected groups, and containment of a root group is equivalent to containment of its Lie algebra for smooth $T$-stable subgroups ([[lem-lie-functor-exactness-fixed-points-and-generation]], [[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F4] For $K=U\cap n_wBn_w^{-1}$, the Lie intersection is $\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^+}\mathfrak g_\alpha$. The reduced geometric identity subgroup of a $T$-stable subgroup of $U$ is the product of the root groups it contains, as in the ordered-root-coordinate theorem. The smooth limit subgroup $U_G(\lambda)$ has positive Lie weights, and its points are precisely those contracted to $1$ by $\lambda$. ([[thm-root-subgroups-of-a-split-reductive-group]], [[thm-cocharacter-limit-subgroups]]) These are the precise inputs for the intersection and orbit argument of Milne, Propositions 21.77–21.79; arbitrary smooth $T$-stable intersections need not be smooth.

## Proof

1.1 Let $H$ be the subgroup generated by $T$ and the $G_\alpha$ for $\alpha\in\Delta$. Each $G_\alpha$ contains its two root groups and a representative of $s_\alpha$, by the rank-one results in [F1]. As the simple reflections generate $W$, products of these representatives in $H$ represent every $w\in W$. Conjugation by them and $W\cdot\Delta=\Phi$ show that $H$ contains every root group. It therefore equals $G$ by [F1]. Each $G_\alpha$ is generated by $T$ and its two root groups, so this also proves generation by $T$ and the simple positive and negative root groups. If $G$ is semisimple, let $K$ be generated by all root groups. The standard $\mathrm{SL}_2$ root-group generation in each rank-one derived subgroup puts every coroot image in $K$. The coroots span $X_*(T)\otimes\mathbb Q$ for semisimple $G$, so these images generate $T$. Thus $K=G$. If $\Delta$ is empty, $G=T$ and the first assertion still holds; a semisimple group with empty root system is trivial. [F1, F3, given, algebra]

2.1 The subgroups $U_w$ and $U^w$ are smooth connected $T$-stable by [F2], being generated by root groups with weight sets $\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^+$ and $\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^-$ respectively; these two subsets of $\Phi^+$ are disjoint and their union is $\Phi^+$ (a positive root is either in $w\Phi^+$ or in $w\Phi^-$), and each is ordinarily closed: a positive root sum of members remains positive and its inverse image under $w$ retains the same positive or negative sign. Ordinary closure is sufficient by [F2], so each is the weight set of a smooth $T$-stable subgroup of $U$ and the product decomposition of $U$ from [F2] gives a $T$-equivariant isomorphism $U_w\times U^w\to U$. The membership criterion for root groups follows because $U_\alpha\subseteq U_w$ iff the weight $\alpha$ belongs to the weight set of $U_w$ (uniqueness of the smooth subgroup with a given weight semigroup in [F2]). This proves (b). [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The stabilizer of $n_wB$ in $U$ is $K=U\cap n_wBn_w^{-1}$. Work geometrically and take $K_{\mathrm{red}}^\circ$, which is smooth connected over the algebraically closed field. By the root-subgroup containment criterion its root groups are exactly those with $\alpha\in\Phi^+\cap w\Phi^+$, since its Lie algebra is contained in the Lie intersection of [F4], and all these shared root groups lie in $K$. The ordered-coordinate theorem therefore identifies $K_{\mathrm{red}}^\circ$ with $U_w$. Its dimension is the dimension of $\operatorname{Lie}K$, so $K$ is regular at the identity and translation makes it smooth. To identify all components, choose a regular cocharacter $\lambda$ with $n_wBn_w^{-1}=P_G(\lambda)$. Every root coordinate of $U$ has nonzero $\lambda$-weight. An element of $U\cap P_G(\lambda)$ has a limit under conjugation, so its negative coordinates vanish and the positive coordinates tend to zero. Thus its limit is $1$, and it lies in $U_G(\lambda)$. This also contracts $K$ to $1$, showing its components all meet the identity component, hence $K$ is connected. Therefore $K=U_w$, and the equality descends to $k$. Order the complementary root groups first: $U^w\times U_w\simeq U$ identifies the fppf quotient $U/U_w$ with $U^w$, since right multiplication by $U_w$ changes only the second factor. The homogeneous orbit map identifies this quotient with $Un_wB/B$, and its dimension is $\dim U^w=n(w)$. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
