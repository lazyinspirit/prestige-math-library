## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $R\to S$ be a ring
map of finite type ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]). Then the
set of primes

$$ U=\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S):R\to S \text{ is quasi-finite at }\mathfrak q\} $$

is open in $\operatorname{Spec}(S)$
([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]],
[[def-principal-distinguished-subset-of-spectrum]],
[[lem-zariski-closed-set-axioms]]).

At every point of $U$ the local theorem
[[thm-algebraic-zariski-main-localization]] supplies an element of the relative
integral closure at which the algebra becomes a principal localisation of that
closure. Because the algebra is of finite type, the closure may be replaced
there by the finite subalgebra it generates, and such a finite algebra is
quasi-finite over the base at every one of its primes; this is the content of
the proof below, which is where the Axiom of Choice enters, through the local
theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A unital ring map $R\to S$ of finite type, the relative integral closure $S'=\operatorname{Int}_R(S)\subseteq S$ of the image of $R$ in $S$, and the Axiom of Choice, assumed throughout.

[L1] The map $R\to S$ is **quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$** when the $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, that is, finitely generated as a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-module, equivalently finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ ([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]).

[L2] An $R$-algebra $A$ is of finite type over $R$ when $A=R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for finitely many elements, equivalently a quotient of a polynomial ring, and it is module-finite over $R$ when it is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] Assume the Axiom of Choice. For a finite type map $R\to S$ that is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ there is $g\in S'\setminus\mathfrak q$, where $S'$ is the integral closure of the image of $R$ in $S$, such that the inclusion induces an isomorphism $S'_g\cong S_g$ of localisations ([[thm-algebraic-zariski-main-localization]]).

[L4] For a unital ring map $R\to S$ the relative integral closure $\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ is the set of elements of $S$ integral over the map; it is a subring of $S$ containing the image of $R$, hence an $R$-subalgebra, and it is exactly the integral closure of the image of $R$ in $S$ ([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]).

[L5] If $A\subseteq B$ is a subring and $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ are integral over $A$, then the $A$-subalgebra $A[b_1,\ldots,b_n]$ is module-finite over $A$ ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).

[L6] For multiplicative subsets $S,T\subseteq R$ with image $\bar T$ in $S^{-1}R$ and $U$ the multiplicative subset generated by $S\cup T$ there is a unique $R$-algebra isomorphism $\bar T^{-1}(S^{-1}R)\cong U^{-1}R$; in particular $(R_f)_g\cong R_{fg}$ ([[prop-iterated-localisation]]).

[L7] For an ideal $I$ of a commutative ring $R$ and a multiplicative subset $S\subseteq R$ with image $\bar S$ in $R/I$ there is a canonical isomorphism $(S^{-1}R)/(S^{-1}I)\cong\bar S^{-1}(R/I)$ ([[thm-localisation-commutes-with-quotients]]).

[L8] The localisation $S^{-1}R$ consists of the classes $r/s$ with $r\in R$, $s\in S$, and every $s\in S$ maps to a unit, with $(s/1)^{-1}=1/s$ ([[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]]).

[L9] For a prime ideal $\mathfrak p$ of a commutative ring $R$ there is a canonical field isomorphism $R_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pR_{\mathfrak p}\cong\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p)$, the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ ([[cor-residue-field-of-a-localisation-at-a-prime]]).

[L10] The **Axiom of Choice** (AC) is the statement that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L11] For a multiplicative subset $S\subseteq R$ contraction along the localisation map induces an inclusion-preserving bijection $\operatorname{Spec}(S^{-1}R)\to\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(R):\mathfrak p\cap S=\varnothing\}$, with inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto S^{-1}\mathfrak p$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]).

[L12] For $f$ in a commutative ring $R$ the principal localisation is $R_f=S_f^{-1}R$ with $S_f=\{1,f,f^2,\ldots\}$, and its elements may be written $r/f^n$; in particular $R_1$ is canonically isomorphic to $R$ ([[def-principal-localisation]]).

[L13] For $f\in R$ the principal distinguished subset is $D(f)=\{\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(R):f\notin\mathfrak p\}$, the complement of $V((f))$ inside $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ ([[def-principal-distinguished-subset-of-spectrum]]).

[L14] The subsets $V(I)$, as $I$ ranges over the ideals of $R$, contain $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ and $\varnothing$, are closed under arbitrary intersections and finite unions, and define a topology on $\operatorname{Spec}(R)$ ([[lem-zariski-closed-set-axioms]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 We assume the Axiom of Choice as recorded in [L10]. By [L2] the finite type $R$-algebra $S$ has the form $S=R[s_1,\ldots,s_n]$ for some $n\ge0$ and elements $s_i\in S$, and $S'=\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ is an $R$-subalgebra of $S$ containing the image of $R$ by [L4]. We must show that the set $U$ of primes $\mathfrak q$ at which $R\to S$ is quasi-finite, in the sense of [L1], is open in the topology of [L14]. [given, L1, L2, L4, L10, L14]

1.2 We record a general fact about module-finite algebras. Let $B$ be module-finite over a ring $C$ and let $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(C)$; choose $b_1,\ldots,b_m\in B$ generating $B$ as a $C$-module. Then the images of $b_1,\ldots,b_m$ generate $B/\mathfrak pB$ as a $C/\mathfrak p$-module, and therefore generate the $C/\mathfrak p$-algebra $(B/\mathfrak pB)\otimes_{C/\mathfrak p}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ as a module over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)=\operatorname{Frac}(C/\mathfrak p)$ by [L9]: localising a module at a multiplicative set multiplies its generating set by scalars and never enlarges it. Hence $(B/\mathfrak pB)\otimes_{C/\mathfrak p}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is a finite-dimensional $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra. [given, L2, L9]

1.3 We also record a general fact about localising finite-dimensional algebras. Let $C$ be a finite-dimensional algebra over a field $K$ and let $W\subseteq C$ be a multiplicative subset. For every $u\in W$ the ideals $(u)\supseteq(u^2)\supseteq(u^3)\supseteq\cdots$ of $C$ are $K$-subspaces of the finite-dimensional $K$-vector space $C$, so the chain stabilises: there is $N\ge1$ with $(u^N)=(u^{N+1})$, hence $u^N=u^{N+1}x$ for some $x\in C$ and therefore $u^N(1-ux)=0$ in $C$. Since $u$ is a unit of $W^{-1}C$ with inverse $1/u$ by [L8], the image of $u^N$ is invertible in $W^{-1}C$, so the image of $1-ux$ is zero there, that is $x=1/u$ in $W^{-1}C$. Consequently every element of $W^{-1}C$ is of the form $c\cdot x$ with $c\in C$ and hence lies in the image of the localisation map $C\to W^{-1}C$, which is therefore surjective as a $K$-linear map; so $\dim_KW^{-1}C\le\dim_KC$ and $W^{-1}C$ is finite-dimensional over $K$. [given, L8]

2.1 Let $\mathfrak q\in U$ and put $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$. By [L3] there is $g\in S'\setminus\mathfrak q$ such that the inclusion $S'\to S$ induces an isomorphism $S'_g\to S_g$ of localisations; we fix such a $g$ and view $S'_g=S_g$ as subrings of the common ring $S_g$. [given, step 1.1, L3]

2.2 The localisation $S_g$ is a finitely generated $R$-algebra: its elements are the fractions with numerator in $S$ and denominator a power of $g$ by [L12], so the images of $s_1,\ldots,s_n$ together with $1/g$ generate $S_g$ over $R$ by [L2]. Hence there are finitely many elements $z_1,\ldots,z_N\in S_g$ generating $S_g$ as an $R$-algebra; taking them to be the explicit generators just listed. [given, step 1.1, L2, L12]

3.1 Each $z_j$ lies in $S'_g=S_g$, so by [L12] and [L8] it can be written $z_j=y_j/g^{m_j}$ with $y_j\in S'$ and some exponent $m_j\ge0$. Put $T:=R[g,y_1,\ldots,y_N]\subseteq S'$. Then $T_g=S_g$: the inclusion $T\subseteq S'$ gives $T_g\subseteq S'_g=S_g$, while each generator $z_j=y_j/g^{m_j}$ of $S_g$ over $R$ lies in $T_g$, so $S_g\subseteq T_g$. Also $T$ is an $R$-subalgebra of $S$ containing the image of $R$. [given, step 2.1, step 2.2, L2, L8, L12]

4.1 Every generator $g,y_1,\ldots,y_N$ of $T$ lies in $S'$ and is therefore integral over $R$ by [L4]. Writing $A$ for the image of $R$ in $S$, the elements $g,y_1,\ldots,y_N$ are integral over the subring $A\subseteq S$, so [L5] shows that $T=A[g,y_1,\ldots,y_N]$ is module-finite over $A$; the same finite list generates $T$ as an $R$-module, so $T$ is module-finite over $R$, and $T$ is of finite type over $R$ by [L2]. [given, step 3.1, L2, L4, L5]

4.2 Now let $\mathfrak q'\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ with $g\notin\mathfrak q'$, and put $\mathfrak q'':=\mathfrak q'\cap T$, $\mathfrak p':=\mathfrak q'\cap R=\mathfrak q''\cap R$ and $T_g$-prime $\mathfrak q''T_g\subseteq T_g$, $\mathfrak q'S_g\subseteq S_g$. The primes $\mathfrak q''T_g$ and $\mathfrak q'S_g$ correspond under the $R$-algebra isomorphism $T_g\cong S_g$ of step 3.1, because contraction along $T\to T_g$ and $S\to S_g$ recovers $\mathfrak q''$ and $\mathfrak q'$ by [L11] and $\mathfrak q'\cap T=\mathfrak q''$. [given, step 3.1, L11]

5.1 Consequently, for the module-finite $R$-algebra $T$ of step 4.1 and any prime $\mathfrak r\in\operatorname{Spec}(T)$ with contraction $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak r\cap R$, the algebra $T_{\mathfrak r}/\mathfrak pT_{\mathfrak r}$ is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Indeed, $\mathfrak pT\subseteq\mathfrak r$, so [L7] gives $T_{\mathfrak r}/\mathfrak pT_{\mathfrak r}\cong(T/\mathfrak pT)_{\bar{\mathfrak r}}$ for the prime $\bar{\mathfrak r}=\mathfrak r/\mathfrak pT$; put $C:=(T/\mathfrak pT)\otimes_{R/\mathfrak p}\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, which is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ by step 1.2, and let $S_0$ be the image of $(R/\mathfrak p)\setminus\{0\}$ in $T/\mathfrak pT$ and $\bar U$ the image of $(T/\mathfrak pT)\setminus\bar{\mathfrak r}$. Since $\bar{\mathfrak r}\cap(R/\mathfrak p)=0$, we have $S_0\subseteq(T/\mathfrak pT)\setminus\bar{\mathfrak r}$; applying [L6] to the multiplicative subsets $S_0$ and $(T/\mathfrak pT)\setminus\bar{\mathfrak r}$ of the ring $T/\mathfrak pT$ shows that $(T/\mathfrak pT)_{\bar{\mathfrak r}}\cong\bar U^{-1}C$, a localisation of the finite-dimensional $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-algebra $C$ at a multiplicative subset, which is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ by step 1.3. [given, step 4.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, L6, L7, L9]

5.2 Hence the canonical $R$-algebra homomorphism $T_{\mathfrak q''}\to S_{\mathfrak q'}$ is an isomorphism: both rings are localisations of the common ring $T_g=S_g$ at the primes $\mathfrak q''T_g$ and $\mathfrak q'S_g$ of step 4.2, and [L6] exhibits each of them as a localisation at the multiplicative subset of the common ring generated by $\{g^n\}$ together with the elements outside that prime. [given, step 4.2, L6, L12]

6.1 Therefore $T_{\mathfrak q''}/\mathfrak p'T_{\mathfrak q''}\cong S_{\mathfrak q'}/\mathfrak p'S_{\mathfrak q'}$ is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p')$ by step 5.1, and since $S$ is of finite type over $R$ by step 1.1 and $\mathfrak p'=\mathfrak q'\cap R$, the map $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q'$ by [L1]. Thus $D_S(g)\subseteq U$. [given, step 5.1, step 5.2, L1]

7.1 It remains to draw the topological conclusion. By [L13] and [L14] each set $D_S(g)=\operatorname{Spec}(S)\setminus V((g))$ is the complement of a closed subset, hence open in the topology of [L14]. Step 2.1 attaches to every $\mathfrak q\in U$ an element $g\in S$ with $\mathfrak q\in D_S(g)$, and step 6.1 shows $D_S(g)\subseteq U$; thus every point of $U$ has an open neighbourhood contained in $U$, which is the defining property of an open subset, so $U$ is open (no selection from infinitely many points is needed, the argument being applied to one prime at a time). [given, step 2.1, step 6.1, L13, L14]

8.1 The proof used the Axiom of Choice only in step 2.1, through [L3]; every other step selected finitely many elements (the generators $s_i$, the generators $z_j$, the numerators $y_j$, the module generators $b_i$, the exponent $N$ and the elements $u,x$ of step 1.3). ∎ [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, L10]
