## Definition

Let $p:\tilde X\to X$ be the Burau infinite cyclic cover of
[[def-burau-infinite-cyclic-cover]] with deck generator $t$, and let
$\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$ be the Laurent polynomial ring of
[[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]. The **reduced Burau module** is the
absolute singular homology
$$M_{\mathrm{red}}:=H_1(\tilde X;\mathbb Z)$$
([[def-singular-chain-complex-and-singular-homology]]) equipped with the left
$\Lambda_1$-module structure on which $t^k$ acts by the induced automorphism
$(T_{t^k})_*$ of the deck transformation $T_{t^k}$, extended uniquely to a
unital ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(M_{\mathrm{red}})$
([[def-deck-transformation-and-deck-group]]).

**Conventions.** Integral coefficients and ordinary absolute singular homology
are used. Deck transformations act on the left. The module structure is
*defined* by the deck action through the universal property below and is not an
extra choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Burau infinite cyclic cover $p:\tilde X\to X$, its deck group $\operatorname{Deck}(\tilde X/X)=\{T_{t^k}:k\in\mathbb Z\}$ generated by $T_t=t$, and the abelian group $M_{\mathrm{red}}=H_1(\tilde X;\mathbb Z)$.

[F1] $\Lambda_1$ has the universal property that every unital ring homomorphism $\mathbb Z[t]\to A$ carrying $t$ to a unit of $A$ extends uniquely to a unital ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to A$; equivalently, each unit $u$ of a unital ring $A$ determines a unique unital ring homomorphism $\Lambda_1\to A$ with $t\mapsto u$ ([[def-the-laurent-polynomial-ring]]).

[F2] A continuous map $f:\tilde X\to\tilde X$ induces chain maps $f_{\#,n}$ and homomorphisms $H_n(f_\#)$, functorially: $H_n(g\circ f)=H_n(g)\circ H_n(f)$ and $H_n(\operatorname{id})=\operatorname{id}$ ([[def-induced-singular-chain-map]], [[prop-singular-chains-and-homology-are-covariantly-functorial]]).

[F3] Each deck transformation $T_{t^k}$ is a homeomorphism of $\tilde X$ over $X$, the deck group is a group under composition with $T_{t^k}\circ T_{t^l}=T_{t^{k+l}}$ and $T_{t^k}^{-1}=T_{t^{-k}}$, and it acts on $\tilde X$ on the left ([[def-deck-transformation-and-deck-group]], [[def-burau-infinite-cyclic-cover]]).

[F4] $M_{\mathrm{red}}=H_1(\tilde X;\mathbb Z)$ is the degree-one homology of the singular chain complex with integral coefficients ([[def-singular-chain-complex-and-singular-homology]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Each deck transformation induces an automorphism.* By [F3] each $T_{t^k}$ is a homeomorphism, so by [F2] it induces $(T_{t^k})_*:=H_1((T_{t^k})_\#)$, an endomorphism of $M_{\mathrm{red}}$. Since $T_{t^k}\circ T_{t^{-k}}=\operatorname{id}=T_{t^{-k}}\circ T_{t^k}$ by [F3], functoriality in [F2] gives $(T_{t^k})_*\circ(T_{t^{-k}})_*=\operatorname{id}$ and $(T_{t^{-k}})_*\circ(T_{t^k})_*=\operatorname{id}$; hence each $(T_{t^k})_*$ is an automorphism of $M_{\mathrm{red}}$, and $t_*:=(T_t)_*$ is a unit of the ring $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(M_{\mathrm{red}})$ with inverse $(T_{t^{-1}})_*$. [F2, F3, F4]

2.1 *The $\Lambda_1$-module structure.* By [F1] applied directly to the unital ring $\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(M_{\mathrm{red}})$, where the inverse of $t_*$ was proved in step 1.1, the unit $t_*$ determines a unique unital ring homomorphism $$\varphi:\Lambda_1\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb Z}(M_{\mathrm{red}}),\qquad t\longmapsto t_*,$$ and we let $\Lambda_1$ act on $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ through $\varphi$. This gives $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ the structure of a left $\Lambda_1$-module, since $\Lambda_1$ is commutative; it is well defined because $\varphi$ is unique, so no further choice enters. [F1, step 1.1]

3.1 *The action of $t^k$ is the induced automorphism.* Since $\varphi$ is a unital ring homomorphism, $\varphi(t^k)=\varphi(t)^k=(t_*)^k$ for every $k\in\mathbb Z$, and by functoriality of [F2] applied to the composition of $T_t$ with itself, $(t_*)^k=(T_{t^k})_*$. Hence $t^k$ acts on $M_{\mathrm{red}}$ exactly by $(T_{t^k})_*$, as asserted, and in particular the action is the deck action and not an additional datum. No choice principle is used. [F2, F3, step 2.1] ∎
