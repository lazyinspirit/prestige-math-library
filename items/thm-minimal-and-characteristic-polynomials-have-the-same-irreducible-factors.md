## Statement

Let $T$ be an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional vector space over $F$. A monic irreducible polynomial in $F[x]$ divides $\mu_T$ if and only if it divides $\chi_T$. Thus $\mu_T$ and $\chi_T$ have exactly the same monic irreducible factors, though generally with different exponents.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite-dimensional endomorphism $T$ and a monic irreducible polynomial $q\in F[x]$.

[L1] The minimal polynomial divides the characteristic polynomial ([[cor-minimal-polynomial-divides-characteristic-polynomial]]).

[L2] For monic irreducible $q$, the quotient $K=F[x]/(q)$ is a field extension containing $a=x+(q)$ with $q(a)=0$ ([[thm-irreducible-quotient-adjoins-a-root]]).

[L3] Extending scalars from $F$ to $K$ leaves the minimal polynomial unchanged ([[lem-minimal-polynomial-is-invariant-under-field-extension]]).

[L4] A scalar is an eigenvalue exactly when it is a root of the characteristic polynomial ([[thm-spectrum-is-the-root-set-of-the-characteristic-polynomial]]).

[L5] If $a$ is algebraic over $F$, the kernel of evaluation at $a$ is generated by its unique monic irreducible minimal polynomial, and $f(a)=0$ exactly when that polynomial divides $f$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]]).

[L6] Polynomial evaluation at an endomorphism is $p(T)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kT^k$ for $p(x)=\sum_{k\ge0}a_kx^k$ ([[def-polynomial-evaluation-at-an-endomorphism]]).

[L7] An endomorphism and its matrix in any ordered basis have the same minimal polynomial; in particular, this polynomial is invariant under changing the basis ([[thm-minimal-polynomial-is-matrix-representation-and-similarity-invariant]]).

[L8] In any ordered basis, the characteristic polynomial of an endomorphism is the characteristic polynomial of its representing matrix, independently of the chosen basis ([[def-characteristic-polynomial-of-an-operator]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 If $q\mid\mu_T$, then [L1] immediately gives $q\mid\chi_T$. [L1]

1.2 Conversely suppose $q\mid\chi_T$. Choose an ordered basis of $V$, let $A$ be the matrix of $T$, and form $K$ and $a$ as in [L2]. Since $q(a)=0$, also $\chi_T(a)=0$; by [L8] this is the characteristic polynomial of $A$, whose determinant formula is unchanged after scalar extension. Thus [L4] applied to the resulting endomorphism of $K^n$ gives a nonzero $K$-eigenvector with eigenvalue $a$. [L2, L4, L8, choose]

2.1 By [L7], $A$ has minimal polynomial $\mu_T$, and [L3] says its scalar extension has the same minimal polynomial. Applying [L6] to the eigenvector from step 1.2 gives $\mu_T(a)=0$. [step 1.2, L3, L6, L7]

3.1 The monic irreducible minimal polynomial of $a$ divides $q$ because $q(a)=0$, and it is nonconstant; irreducibility of $q$ makes it equal to $q$. Now [L5] and step 2.1 give $q\mid\mu_T$. [step 2.1, L2, L5]

4.1 Steps 1.1 and 3.1 prove both directions. In the zero-dimensional case $\mu_T=\chi_T=1$, so neither has an irreducible factor. [step 1.1, step 3.1] ∎
