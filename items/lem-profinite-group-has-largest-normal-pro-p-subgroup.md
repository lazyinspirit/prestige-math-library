## Statement

Let $G$ be a profinite group and let $p$ be a prime. The family of all closed normal pro-$p$ subgroups of $G$ has a largest member, denoted $O_p(G)$. It is the closure of the subgroup generated by that family and is itself a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A profinite group $G$ and a prime $p$.

[F1] [[def-profinite-group-by-inverse-limit]]: a profinite group is an inverse limit of finite discrete groups and is compact and Hausdorff.

[F2] [[def-pro-p-group]]: a pro-$p$ group is an inverse limit of finite $p$-groups.

[F3] [[def-normal-subgroup]]: normality means invariance under conjugation by every element of the ambient group.

## Proof

1.1 Let $O$ be the closure of the subgroup generated by all closed normal pro-$p$ subgroups of $G$. It is normal by [F3]. Fix an open normal subgroup $U\triangleleft G$. The finite quotient $G/U$ has only finitely many subgroups. Each image of a closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup is a normal $p$-subgroup by [F2]. Their product is a normal subgroup; the product of two normal $p$-subgroups is a $p$-group because its order is $|P||Q|/|P\cap Q|$. Repeating this over the finitely many distinct images shows that $O$ maps to a $p$-group in $G/U$. [F1, F2, F3, given, algebra]

1.2 The inverse-limit topology on $G$ has the open normal subgroups as a neighborhood basis by [F1]. Thus $O$ is the inverse limit of its finite images $O/(O\cap U)$, each a $p$-group by step 1.1, so $O$ is pro-$p$ by [F2]. It contains every closed normal pro-$p$ subgroup used to generate it and is itself such a subgroup, hence is the largest one and equals $O_p(G)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
