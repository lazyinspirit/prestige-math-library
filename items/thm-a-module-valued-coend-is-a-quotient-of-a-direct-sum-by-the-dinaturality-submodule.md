## Statement

Let $R$ be a unital ring, let $\mathcal C$ be a small category
([[def-small-locally-small-and-large-category]]) and let
$T:\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C\to R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ be a
functor ([[prop-modules-and-homomorphisms-form-category-rmod]]). Write
$\jmath_c:T(c,c)\to\bigoplus_{c}T(c,c)$ for the coordinate inclusions of the
direct sum ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]) and let

$$S:=\Bigl\langle\;\jmath_c\bigl(T(f,1_c)(x)\bigr)-\jmath_{c'}\bigl(T(1_{c'},f)(x)\bigr)\;:\;f:c\to c'\text{ in }\mathcal C,\ x\in T(c',c)\;\Bigr\rangle_R$$

be the submodule generated by those elements
([[def-submodule]],
[[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

Then $T$ has a coend, and it is the direct sum of the diagonal values modulo
the dinaturality submodule ([[def-end-and-coend]],
[[def-quotient-module]]):

$$\int^{c}T(c,c)=\Bigl(\bigoplus_{c}T(c,c)\Bigr)\Big/S,\qquad \rho_c=\pi\circ\jmath_c,$$

where $\pi$ is the quotient homomorphism. As in the set-valued case, the
generating element $x$ lies in the off-diagonal value $T(c',c)$, and the two
terms of the generator sit in the summands at $c$ and at $c'$ respectively.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A unital ring $R$, a small category $\mathcal C$, and a functor $T$ from $\mathcal C^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal C$ to left $R$-modules.

[F5] For a fixed ring $R$, left $R$-modules and module homomorphisms form a large locally small category $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$ ([[prop-modules-and-homomorphisms-form-category-rmod]]).

[F8] A category is **small** when both $\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal C)$ and $\operatorname{Mor}(\mathcal C)$ are sets. ([[def-small-locally-small-and-large-category]]).

[F1] The direct sum $\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i$ is the submodule of the direct product consisting of the families of finite support, with coordinate inclusions $\jmath_i$; If $I=\varnothing$, both product and direct sum are the zero module. ([[def-direct-sum-of-a-family-of-modules]]).

[F3] A subset $N\subseteq M$ is a **submodule** when it is a subgroup of the additive group of $M$ and is closed under scalars: $r\in R,\ n\in N\Rightarrow rn\in N$ ([[def-submodule]]).

[F2] The submodule generated by $S\subseteq M$ is $\langle S\rangle_R:=\bigcap\{N\le M:S\subseteq N\}$, the smallest submodule of $M$ containing $S$ ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[F4] For $N\le M$ the quotient module $M/N$ has the cosets $m+N$ as elements and scalar action $r(m+N):=rm+N$ ([[def-quotient-module]]).

[L1] For every family of homomorphisms $f_i:M_i\to N$ there is a unique homomorphism $f:\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_i\to N$ such that $f\circ\jmath_i=f_i$ for every $i$. It is given by the finite sum of the $f_i(m_i)$ over the support ([[thm-universal-property-of-module-direct-sums]]).

[L2] If $f:M\to P$ is a homomorphism and $N\le M$ satisfies $N\subseteq\ker f$, there is a unique homomorphism $\bar f:M/N\to P$ such that $\bar f(m+N)=f(m)$, equivalently $f=\bar f\circ\pi$ ([[thm-quotient-module-universal-property]]).

[F7] A cowedge from $T$ to $d$ is a dinatural transformation from $T$ to a constant functor: a family $\rho_c:T(c,c)\to d$ with $\rho_c\circ T(f,1_c)=\rho_{c'}\circ T(1_{c'},f)$ for every $f:c\to c'$ ([[def-wedge-and-cowedge]]).

[F6] An end of $T$ is a terminal object of the category of wedges over $T$ and a coend an initial object of the category of cowedges under $T$; in short, an end is a terminal wedge and a coend an initial cowedge ([[def-end-and-coend]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** constructive.

1.1 Since $\mathcal C$ is small its objects form a set, so $(T(c,c))_{c\in\operatorname{Ob}(\mathcal C)}$ is a set-indexed family of left $R$-modules and the direct sum $\bigoplus_cT(c,c)$ with its coordinate inclusions $\jmath_c$ is formed. [F1, F5, F8, given, construct]

2.1 The displayed generating elements lie in $\bigoplus_cT(c,c)$, so $S$ is the smallest submodule containing them, and $Q:=(\bigoplus_cT(c,c))/S$ with quotient homomorphism $\pi$ is a left $R$-module. The construction is carried out here rather than obtained from a general cocompleteness theorem, which would assert that the coend exists without exhibiting it. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, construct]

3.1 The family $\rho_c:=\pi\circ\jmath_c$ is a cowedge from $T$ to $Q$: for $f:c\to c'$ and $x\in T(c',c)$ the difference $\rho_c(T(f,1_c)(x))-\rho_{c'}(T(1_{c'},f)(x))$ is $\pi$ applied to a generator of $S$, hence zero, and each $\rho_c$ is a homomorphism because the coordinate inclusion $\jmath_c$ and the quotient map $\pi$ are. [F1, F4, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 Let $\lambda_c:T(c,c)\to N$ be any cowedge. By [L1] there is a unique homomorphism $\lambda:\bigoplus_cT(c,c)\to N$ with $\lambda\jmath_c=\lambda_c$; the cowedge equations for the family $\lambda_c$ make every displayed generator lie in $\ker\lambda$, and $\ker\lambda$ is a submodule, so $S\subseteq\ker\lambda$ by [F2]. By [L2] there is a unique $\bar\lambda:Q\to N$ with $\bar\lambda\pi=\lambda$, hence with $\bar\lambda\rho_c=\lambda_c$ for every $c$; and any homomorphism $\mu:Q\to N$ with $\mu\rho_c=\lambda_c$ satisfies $\mu\pi\jmath_c=\lambda_c$, so $\mu\pi=\lambda$ by the uniqueness in [L1] and $\mu=\bar\lambda$ by the uniqueness in [L2]. So $(Q,\rho)$ is an initial cowedge, that is a coend of $T$. [F6, F7, L1, L2, step 3.1]

5.1 If $\mathcal C$ is empty the index set is empty, so by [F1] the direct sum is the zero module, the generating set is empty, $S$ is the zero submodule and $Q$ is the zero module; step 4.1 then says the zero module is the initial cowedge, which is correct because a cowedge under the empty family is just an object and the zero module is initial in $R\text{-}\mathbf{Mod}$. This is the asserted presentation of the coend in every case. [F1, step 4.1, discharge-construct] ∎

## Remarks

The route is deliberately through the direct-sum and quotient universal properties rather than through a cocompleteness theorem for $R$-modules: such a theorem says a colimit exists, and what is wanted here is the coequalizer itself, in a form in which an element of the coend can be named as a class of a finite sum.

At $f=1_c$ the generator is $\jmath_c(x)-\jmath_c(x)=0$, so identity morphisms enlarge $S$ by nothing; and if $\mathcal C$ is discrete there are no non-identity morphisms at all, $S$ is the zero submodule and the coend is the direct sum of the diagonal values.
