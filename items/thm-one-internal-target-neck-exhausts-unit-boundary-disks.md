## Statement

Assume Countable Choice. Use the actual target plumbing and proper planar cut data of the local internal-torus plumbing theorem. Suppose the COMPLETE set of old integrable unit-domain disks with the fixed old boundary node has finitely many positive-affine orbits, all satisfying that theorem's geometric hypotheses and its verified two-dimensional scalar-kernel condition. This is an inductive premise about the old smooth target, not a conclusion from local gluing. Choose $q$ outside the finite union of their incidence projection branch fibers, and choose one target phase nondegenerate for every old orbit. The empty old set is allowed. The old proper cut component has at least one external boundary copy. All inactive circles lie outside its closed active region.

For every sufficiently small positive neck parameter $\rho$, EVERY integrable enlarged unit-domain disk is, modulo positive affine maps fixing the node, exactly one of the local coupled solutions produced from an old orbit. It has all internal circles active, with regular marked banks, and its actual node-fixed $H^3\to H^2$ operator is onto of index two. The correspondence with old orbits is bijective. In particular the number of integrable orbits is finite and unchanged, not just its parity.

More precisely every sequence in the degenerating targets has, after the explicit positive-affine normalization and a subsequence, one old unit disk, a constant torus projection and one collapsed NEW slit. Its old and disk responses converge in every fixed trace order on the fixed matching circles. The exact torus residue obstruction then gives $\lambda/\epsilon\to-A_0$, excluding an inactive new circle or a node-critical slit wall. Hence the sequence enters the actual coupled response neighborhood. No compactness theorem for a fixed smooth target is used in place of this degeneration argument.

This proves one inductive target-neck step under a complete old-target hypothesis. It does not itself supply that induction's base, a surface-model identification for an arbitrary attaching system, arbitrary-background transport, or a Floer chain identity.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The complete old orbit set and its verified properties, actual old cut component, marked points, flat torus, target plumbing and common nondegenerate phase. All coordinates at the two torus punctures are translations of the same flat coordinate. The new tuple size is $m+1$.

[F1] Every integrable unit disk has one active interior-univalent incidence component. An external circle is a full active boundary; an internal circle is either inactive with one constant root, or active with one proper arc, two banks and two quadratic tips. Other labels give constant roots. Its projection covers the physical disk with one boundary pole per active label, and the full fiber count including constants is exactly its tuple size. ([[lem-integrable-planar-unit-domain-boundary-disks-have-index-two]])

[F2] Positive half-plane maps have actual compact normal subsequences, reflected estimates away from poles, at most simple isolated real boundary poles, removable full interior punctures, and EXACT matching of Laurent constants through an interior target neck. These conclusions are proved by circle kernels and Laurent sine estimates. ([[lem-positive-half-plane-maps-have-controlled-boundary-and-plumbing-limits]])

[F3] The rational slit model works for EVERY nonzero complex $\lambda$, including a marked tip when $\operatorname{Re}\lambda=0$. Its pole coordinate $\tau_\lambda=\lambda/h_\lambda$ has a uniform disk at $i$ for all phases. The actual old nonlinear response and coupled target-gluing uniqueness are the named proved constructions. ([[lem-real-boundary-simple-pole-model-parametrizes-a-constant-sheet-slit]], [[lem-regular-unit-disks-have-nonlinear-localized-incidence-responses-with-moving-slits]], [[thm-internal-torus-target-plumbing-locally-produces-a-regular-unit-disk]])

[F4] Circle Cauchy formulas, Laurent/Stokes identities, and the actual local inverse theorem identify nearby regular preimages and smooth symmetric fiber coefficients. The torus inverse uses the exact complex integral obstruction, not a presumed transverse split operator. ([[thm-cauchy-integral-formula-circle]], [[thm-euclidean-inverse-function-theorem]], [[lem-flat-complex-torus-cauchy-riemann-mean-inverse-and-normalized-dipoles]])

## Proof

1.1 Take any sequence $u_n$ with target parameter $\epsilon_n\to0$ along the chosen phase. Apply [F1] to its actual active incidence projection $p_n$, viewed as a half-plane function on the active target image. On the old OPEN cut component, away from its interior plumbing point, this is defined at every point, independent of its possible slit choices: that open cut component is always covered exactly once. Choose one fixed point $b_*$ there, distinct from $q$, and use the unique positive affine change of the physical projection making $p_n(b_*)=i$. Every old internal active arc has a subsequence whose endpoints and arc lengths converge on its fixed analytic circle; retain also whether each label was inactive. Finite extraction classifies each limiting internal label as a proper nondegenerate arc, a collapsed arc at its marked point, an inactive filled circle, or a full-circle wall whose complementary gap shrinks to a point. A collapsed active arc MUST shrink at its marked point because that point belongs to every arc. Apply [F2] on the connected fixed old cut component minus $q$. It gives a holomorphic half-plane limit there and smooth convergence on all compact subsets. The full interior puncture $q$ is removable with value $c\in\mathbb H$. The limit is nonconstant: there is an external boundary copy; on every smaller analytic edge interval away from its marked point [F2]'s reflected bound gives real boundary values, which exclude a constant nonreal limit. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Assemble the OLD limiting active surface explicitly, before assuming it is an old disk. For a limiting proper arc separate its two banks and use square-root charts at its two distinct endpoints. For a collapsed or inactive internal circle fill its paired copies. For a full-circle wall keep its two boundary copies separate. Away from the finitely many endpoints and marked points these are fixed target charts. The positive limit extends across every filled-circle puncture by [F2]'s full-disk removal; across stable edges it has the real reflection from that lemma. At every remaining isolated boundary singularity, including an endpoint which approaches a mark or the meeting point of a full-circle wall, its real reflection has at most a simple pole by [F2]'s Laurent sine proof. Thus it is a meromorphic half-plane function $p_O$ on this compact connected bordered normalization $S_O$. It has no interior pole. On each boundary circle it is nonconstant with positive boundary derivative away from its poles, by [F2]. Hence each such circle has at least one pole: a real smooth function on a compact circle cannot have strictly positive derivative everywhere. These poles are simple with the positive half-plane sign. If $e$ is the old number of external copies, $\ell$ the old number of internal labels, $a$ the number of proper limiting arcs and $f$ the number of full-circle walls, the active boundary count is $e+a+2f$. Therefore $p_O$ is proper with degree at least $e+a+2f$. Indeed near every pole a large generic imaginary value has one inverse point, and compactness of the remainder accounts for no additional escaping preimages; the usual finite local inverse continuation makes degree constant. This surface is connected because the original cut component is connected before any of these boundary pairs are rejoined. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 Record carefully the sheet contributed by a COLLAPSED active slit; this also applies later to the new torus circle. Let its target point be $x_j$ and choose a fixed analytic real coordinate $w$ there. The arc is small, so [F3] expresses its two banks exactly by $w=h_{\lambda_n}(z)$, with the sign chosen for the actual marked bank; $\lambda_n\to0$, and purely imaginary $\lambda_n$ are allowed. Pull the actual projection back to the rational disk piece, $P_n(z)=p_n(h_{\lambda_n}(z))$. It is a positive half-plane map on the disk with a shrinking interior hole at $i$, real on its remaining boundary, with its ONLY boundary pole the fixed mark $\infty$. Put $\tau=\lambda_n/h_{\lambda_n}(z)$ near $i$. The exact relation $w\tau=\lambda_n$ identifies their Laurent constants. On a fixed old-side $w$ circle the limit extends across $x_j$ with value $c_j\in\mathbb H$, so that constant tends to $c_j$. By [F2] the disk functions have a nonreal compact limit away from $i$, and its interior puncture is removable. On every finite boundary interval the reflected bounds give real values. At infinity the Laurent sine estimate permits only a simple pole. Thus the limit on the full disk is $az+b$, $a>0,b\in\mathbb R$, with $ai+b=c_j$: subtract that affine principal part and use the zero-boundary harmonic identity. A nonreal constant would violate its real boundary values. For any fixed physical value $z_*$ other than $c_j$, its unique affine inverse is away from $i$. The actual local inverse theorem then supplies one nearby solution of $P_n(z)=z_*$, whose TARGET root $h_{\lambda_n}(z)$ tends to $x_j$. This is one actual constant root SHEET in the limit, for every generic $z_*$. An already inactive label supplies the same sheet exactly, without rescaling. Different labels have disjoint target neighborhoods. No fixed-target disk compactness or assumption of a regular marked bank entered this argument. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 There are $s=\ell-a-f$ collapsed or inactive old internal labels, contributing $s$ sheets by step3.1. The remaining old labels outside this cut component contribute their unchanged constant roots. Their number is $m-(e+\ell)$. Thus the OLD side supplies at least $$e+a+2f+s+m-(e+\ell)=m+f$$ preimages of a generic physical value, counting both active and constant sheets. This is a literal lower bound on the fibers of the original $u_n$. To verify that no sheet has been counted twice, choose $z_*=iR$ with $R$ larger than the absolute values of the finite critical values, the values at filled-circle marked points and $q$, and the maxima on the finitely many compact filled circles. Those circle images are bounded because they lie in the interior where $p_O$ is holomorphic. Increase $R$ further when the torus preimage list is included below; only finitely many additional values and compact sets must be avoided. Each active limiting inverse point then lies in a fixed target patch off all circles and the node and has an actual nearby inverse by [F4]. The collapsed and inactive sheets lie near their distinct circle marks and are disjoint from those patches. This proves the asserted count for one fixed $z_*$ and all large $n$. It remains valid when $f>0$; no old-unit-disk hypothesis has yet been applied to $p_O$. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, construct, algebra]

5.1 Now analyze the torus side using the actual TARGET neck. The Laurent constant of $p_n$ on a fixed $\xi$ circle about $q$ tends to $c=p_O(q)\in\mathbb H$. Through $\xi\eta_a=\epsilon_n$ the constant is EXACTLY the same on a fixed torus circle about $a$. The positive circle-average version of [F2] therefore gives a nonreal half-plane limit $p_T$ on the connected fixed torus cut along $\gamma$, extending across $a$. If the new circle was inactive, this extension is on the full closed torus, so it is constant by the maximum principle. If its active arc has a proper nondegenerate limit, reflection and removal as in step2.1 give a proper half-plane map on a torus with one slit, of genus one and one boundary. Its sole possible boundary pole is the marked $x$, including when it is an endpoint: nodeless stable edges and separated tip charts are uniformly bounded by [F2], so no other pole appears. The pole is simple, hence this map has degree one. A proper holomorphic degree-one map is a biholomorphism: any branch would give more than one nearby inverse, and local inverses plus properness give a one-sheeted cover. Its source cannot then have genus one; its Euler characteristic is that of a torus minus an open disk, namely minus one, while the disk has Euler characteristic one. Thus this case is impossible. If the new slit tends to the whole circle, the limit is instead a nonconstant proper map on the cut annulus, with TWO real boundary circles. Each requires a positive simple pole, so it supplies at least two active sheets. Together with the old lower bound $m+f$ this exceeds the EXACT total $m+1$ from [F1], a contradiction. The same generic physical $z_*$ can be chosen for both finite preimage lists. In the remaining collapsed-arc case the limit extends across the filled circle and its one puncture by [F2], hence is a holomorphic half-plane map on the CLOSED torus and is constant. Consequently $p_T$ is constant in $\mathbb H$, and the new label is either inactive or a collapsed slit at $x$; any nondegenerate real boundary arc would otherwise force real boundary values of a nonreal constant. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The constant torus case contributes exactly the lower bound of ONE new sheet: it is the exact inactive root, or the collapsed-slit sheet constructed in step3.1, with $c_j=p_T(x)=c$. Hence $m+f+1\le m+1$, and $f=0$. There are NO old full-circle walls. For each remaining active old boundary the only possible pole is its marked point: stable nodeless edges and tip charts away from that point have the uniform bounds of [F2]. If the mark reaches a tip, its square-root boundary chart still has at most one simple pole. A proper arc has just one marked-bank pole; its other bank is nodeless and bounded there. Each active boundary requires a pole by step2.1, so has exactly this one. Therefore $p_O$ has degree $e+a$. Add the $s=\ell-a$ old internal constant sheets and the $m-(e+\ell)$ outside constants. Its inverse fibers form an ACTUAL old degree-$m$ unit-domain disk: the active root map is the inclusion of the normalized old region, its fibers are holomorphic by fixed coefficient contours even at branches or active/constant collisions, its boundary roots are distinct labelled circle points, and its infinity tuple is exactly the old marked tuple. It is smooth there because every boundary pole is simple and the only root tips are quadratic. Its degree on the old open cut component is one and on the others zero, by construction. This is now an old unit disk, so the COMPLETE old-target hypothesis applies. It belongs to one of the stated finite orbits, has every old internal label active, and all marked banks are regular. In particular $s=0$ in the limit, every old slit is proper nondegenerate and every old mark stays away from its tips. This recognition uses the old hypothesis only AFTER constructing an actual old disk. [F1, F2, F4, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 5.1, construct, algebra]

7.1 Convergence to this old disk is strong enough for actual response uniqueness. Away from $q$, use the reflected edge estimates and the square-root coordinates with their converging endpoints. All coefficients and derivatives converge on smaller finite patches. At an old marked point, a fixed small reflected target circle avoids every other singularity. Its Cauchy integrals give convergent simple-pole and regular coefficients, so convergence holds in the compactified source chart too. The inverse fiber coefficients on interior projection-branch patches are fixed contour integrals of the nearby graph functions, giving all derivatives even when a branch splits. Thus no bounded derivative of individual ramified roots is assumed. On a fixed $\xi$ circle about $q$, its negative Laurent trace $G_{O,n}$ tends to zero in every fixed Hardy order. Subtract its cutoff negative extension and fill $q$ by its regular part. This changes the projection only in the fixed simple-root patch, is orientation preserving there for large $n$, and is uniformly close to $p_O$; its imaginary part stays positive there and it has the same positive boundary poles elsewhere. Its inverse fibers consequently give an actual old node-fixed $H^3$ map solving the forced inverse-root section of [F3]. Normalize its regular constant at $q$ to $i$ by the unique positive affine change; these changes converge to the one for the old limit. The regularized maps converge strongly in $H^3$, by the just established coefficient and boundary convergence. Hence the ACTUAL implicit uniqueness in [F3] identifies them with the old nonlinear response, not with a postulated meromorphic operator. The torus functions converge to $i$ on each fixed puncture circle after this normalization. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 6.1, construct, algebra]

8.1 Exclude an inactive new circle without assuming it away. If it is inactive, the actual torus projection is holomorphic on the torus with just the one plumbing hole at $a$ and extends across $x$. Its boundary Laurent residue at $a$ is zero by Stokes. But the EXACT first-mode transfer across $\xi\eta_a=\epsilon_n$ says that residue is $\epsilon_n(A_0+o(1))$, where $A_0$ is the derivative of the old regular part at $q$. By the choice of $q$ outside the branch fibers of EVERY old orbit, $A_0\ne0$. This contradicts zero residue for large $n$. Thus the new circle is active. Its collapsed arc has its exact rational parameter $\lambda_n\to0$, possibly on a node-critical bank initially. Step3.1 gives its pulled-back disk projection converging to $z$ away from $i$, with the same conclusion at infinity from the real simple-pole coefficient estimates. The uniform coordinate $\tau_{\lambda_n}$ allows all phases, so its incoming negative trace tends to zero in every fixed order. The torus and old traces do likewise by step7.1. On these fixed component patches the actual functions are precisely their responses plus the constants: on the old part this is the actual forced-section uniqueness, on the disk it is the real simple-boundary-pole inverse, and on the torus it is the zero-integral inverse plus a complex constant. Thus their constants, nonzero traces and first residues satisfy EXACTLY the coupled equations of the local plumbing theorem. Its residue equation gives, before any rate is assumed, $$0=\epsilon_n(A_0+o(1))+\lambda_n(1+o(1)).$$ Therefore $\lambda_n/\epsilon_n\to-A_0$. The fixed target phase now makes $\operatorname{Re}\lambda_n$ a nonzero fixed fraction of $|\epsilon_n|$, excluding the node-critical slit wall. All parameters lie in the actual response neighborhood of [F3]. This is target exhaustion, obtained from the positive functions, real boundary analysis and sheet budget, rather than a compactness theorem for a fixed target. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 3.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1, construct, algebra]

9.1 By local coupled uniqueness each such sequence is eventually the solution produced from its old orbit, up to the normalized positive affine map. The assertion is uniform for all sufficiently small $\rho$: if it failed, for each integer $N$ the nonempty set of counterexamples with $0<\rho<1/N$ would allow one choice by Countable Choice. Those independent choices form a sequence to which steps1.1--8.1 apply, a contradiction. There are finitely many old orbits. Choose disjoint neighborhoods of their unique representatives normalized at $q$; the local solutions from different old orbits are distinct, since their old parts converge to those different representatives. The local theorem constructs one from each and proves its actual onto index-two operator. Exhaustion shows there is no other orbit. If the old set was empty, step6.1 itself contradicts any countersequence, so the new set is empty for small $\rho$ as well. This proves the complete bijection and regularity. It uses a complete OLD-target hypothesis as an induction input, but assumes neither a component-response axiom nor degeneration exhaustion on the new target. The base and arbitrary-system identification remain separate obligations stated explicitly. [F1, F2, F3, F4, step 6.1, step 7.1, step 8.1, construct, contradiction] ∎
