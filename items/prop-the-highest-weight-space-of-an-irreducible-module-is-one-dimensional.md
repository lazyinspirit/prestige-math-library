## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $\mathfrak g$ be a finite-dimensional complex
semisimple Lie algebra with Cartan subalgebra $\mathfrak h$ and a chosen
positive system, and let $V$ be a finite-dimensional irreducible highest
weight module of highest weight $\lambda$
([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]]). Then the
$\lambda$-weight space of $V$ is one-dimensional:
$$\dim V_\lambda=1 .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, such $\mathfrak g,\mathfrak h$, a chosen positive system, and a finite-dimensional irreducible highest weight module $V$ of highest weight $\lambda$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the root-space theory used by the cited suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L1] There is a highest weight vector $v\in V$ of weight $\lambda$ generating $V$ as a $\mathfrak g$-module, and $V$ is irreducible ([[def-highest-weight-vector-and-highest-weight-module]], [[def-weight-and-weight-space-of-a-lie-algebra-representation]]).

[L2] The subrepresentation of an irreducible module generated by any highest weight vector is the whole module: here $U(\mathfrak g)v=V$ ([[prop-a-finite-dimensional-irreducible-module-is-generated-by-any-highest-weight-vector]]).

[L3] A module generated by a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$ satisfies $V=U(\mathfrak n^-)\mathbb Cv$ and has $\lambda$-weight space exactly $\mathbb Cv$ ([[lem-highest-weight-modules-have-weights-below-the-top-weight]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Take a highest weight vector $v$ of weight $\lambda$ generating $V$, as [L1] provides. [A1, L1]

1.2 By [L2], $U(\mathfrak g)v=V$, and by the definition of a highest weight module this is the statement that $v$ generates $V$; hence the pair $(V,v)$ satisfies the hypothesis of the weight bound [L3]. [L1, L2]

2.1 Applying [L3] to $(V,v)$ gives $V_\lambda=\mathbb Cv$. [L3, step 1.2]

3.1 Therefore $\dim V_\lambda=1$, which is the assertion. [step 2.1] ∎
