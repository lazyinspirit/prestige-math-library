## Statement

Let $F/\mathbb Q_p$ be a GIVEN valued algebraic closure. For each integer $d\ge1$ there are only finitely many intermediate fields $K\subset F$ with $[K:\mathbb Q_p]\le d$. Their compositum $L_d$ is a finite Galois extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, the fields $L_d$ form an increasing sequence, and $F=\bigcup_{d\ge1}L_d$. All assertions use no Choice axiom and the tower is uniquely defined from the given closure.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The valued algebraic closure $F$ and one positive integer $d$.

[F1] Finite intermediate fields are complete, and their finite $\mathbb Q_p$-embeddings into $F$ are isometric. [[lem-choice-free-finite-ax-sen-approximation]]

[F2] Finite valuation rings are finite free; different transitivity and the monogenic derivative formula hold; and a totally ramified finite extension is generated integrally by any uniformizer with Eisenstein polynomial. [[lem-choice-free-finite-padic-different-and-base-change-calculus]]

[F3] Finite-field Witt rings give the corresponding unramified finite extension of $\mathbb Q_p$, and the Witt Frobenius has the indicated finite order. [[def-p-typical-witt-vectors-of-a-perfect-field]]

[F4] The finite Galois correspondence applies to finite normal separable extensions and their automorphism groups. [[thm-fundamental-theorem-of-finite-galois-theory]]

## Proof

1.1 Let $K\subset F$ be finite of degree $n\le d$, with residue degree $f$ and ramification index $e=n/f$. Choose one residue-field generator $\bar\theta$ over $\mathbb F_p$ and one lift in $\mathcal O_K$. Newton lifting for $X^{p^f}-X$ gives its unique Teichmüller lift $\theta\in\mathcal O_K$. The irreducible residue polynomial shows $K_0=\mathbb Q_p(\theta)$ is unramified of degree $f$, so $K/K_0$ is totally ramified. Choose one uniformizer $\pi$ and set $a=\theta+\pi$. The order $A=\mathbb Z_p[a]$ is finite free over $\mathbb Z_p$ and hence $p$-adically complete. Its minimal polynomial reduces modulo $p$ to a positive power of the irreducible polynomial of $\bar\theta$: all conjugate residues are Frobenius conjugates by [F1]. Thus $A/pA$ is local with residue field generated by $\bar\theta$. The simple-root Newton iteration for $X^{p^f}-X$ in this finite complete order lifts $\bar\theta$ uniquely to $\theta\in A$. Hence $\pi=a-\theta\in A$, and $\mathcal O_K=\mathcal O_{K_0}[\pi]=A$ by [F2]–[F3]. In particular $K=\mathbb Q_p(a)$; its monic minimal polynomial $g$ has integral coefficients and $g'(a)$ generates the different of $K/\mathbb Q_p$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $h(X)=X^e+\sum_{j<e}c_jX^j$ be the Eisenstein polynomial of $\pi$ over $K_0$. The nonzero terms $j c_j\pi^{j-1}$ of $h'(\pi)$ have pairwise distinct valuation residues modulo $1$, since their fractional parts are $(j-1)/e$. Thus no cancellation raises their minimum and $v(h'(\pi))\le v_p(e)+(e-1)/e$. The unramified different is a unit, so different transitivity in [F2] yields $v(g'(a))\le B_d:=1+v_p(d!)$. Every conjugate of $a$ is integral, and for $n=1$ the maximum below is understood as $-\infty$. The product formula $g'(a)=\prod_{a'\ne a}(a-a')$ gives $\max_{a'\ne a}v(a-a')\le B_d$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Set $N=2B_d+2$. Replace the coefficients of $g$ modulo $p^N$ by their unique integer representatives in $\{0,\ldots,p^N-1\}$, producing a monic integer polynomial $g_0$ of the same degree. At $a$, $v(g_0(a))\ge N$ and $v(g_0'(a))=v(g'(a))\le B_d$. The deterministic Newton iteration in the complete field $K$ therefore produces a root $b\in K$ of $g_0$ with $v(b-a)>B_d$. If $\mathbb Q_p(a)$ were not contained in $\mathbb Q_p(b)$, a finite Galois normal closure and [F4] would supply an automorphism fixing $b$ and moving $a$; by [F1] it is isometric, so the moved conjugate would satisfy $v(a-\sigma(a))\ge v(a-b)>B_d$, contradicting step 2.1. Hence $K=\mathbb Q_p(b)$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 For each degree $n\le d$ there are only finitely many monic integer polynomials of degree $n$ whose lower coefficients are in $\{0,\ldots,p^N-1\}$, and each has finitely many roots in $F$. The set of all fields generated by these finitely many roots is finite and contains every $K$ of degree at most $d$ by step 3.1. This proves finiteness of the actual subfields, not merely their isomorphism classes. [step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Define $L_d$ as the compositum of all fields in that finite intrinsic set. It is finite. Every $\mathbb Q_p$-conjugate of each constituent is again a subfield of degree at most $d$, so every embedding of $L_d$ into $F$ preserves $L_d$; separability in characteristic zero makes $L_d/\mathbb Q_p$ finite Galois. The intrinsic field sets increase with $d$, so the $L_d$ increase. Every element of $F$ is algebraic of some finite degree, hence belongs to one $L_d$. No sequence of roots, bases or automorphisms is selected: $L_d$ is defined by the entire finite set at level $d$. [F4, step 4.1, given, construct, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

Milne's local bounded-extension discussion was read. The argument above supplies a uniform discriminant bound and a finite coefficient list directly; it does not invoke compactness of an infinite product or a countable family of root choices. The canonical cofinal tower does not by itself choose compatible automorphism lifts, and finite Galois lifting to the whole closure is not asserted here.
