## Statement

Let $x$ be an invertible indeterminate and put $p=x-x^{-1}$. Every Laurent
polynomial $P(x)\in\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]$ has a unique expression
$$P(x)=a(p)+b(p)x$$
with $a,b\in\mathbb Z[p]$, where $\mathbb Z[p]$ is the ring of finite
integer-coefficient polynomials in the indeterminate $p$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The commutative polynomial ring $R=\mathbb Z[p]$, the monic polynomial $G(X)=X^2-pX-1\in R[X]$, and the Laurent polynomial ring $\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]$.

[F1] The polynomial ring $R[X]$ consists of finite-support coefficient sequences with coefficientwise addition and finite convolution multiplication ([[def-polynomial-ring-over-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F2] If $R$ is commutative, then $R[X]$ with these operations is a commutative ring ([[thm-polynomial-ring-is-a-commutative-ring]]).

[F3] The ideal generated by $G$ is a two-sided ideal; the quotient ring consists of its additive cosets with multiplication induced from $R[X]$ ([[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]], [[def-quotient-ring]]).

[F4] Division by the monic degree-two polynomial $G$ gives each polynomial in $R[X]$ a unique remainder of degree less than two ([[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]).

## Proof

1.1 Let $Q=R[X]/(G)$. By [F1] and [F2], $R[X]$ is a commutative polynomial ring, and [F3] makes $(G)$ a two-sided ideal and $Q$ a quotient ring. Since $G$ is monic of degree two, [F4] gives every class in $Q$ a representative $a(p)+b(p)X$ of degree at most one in $X$. If two such representatives define the same class, their difference is a multiple of $G$ and has degree less than two; uniqueness in [F4] forces the difference to be zero. Thus this representative is unique. [given, F1, F2, F3, F4, construct]

2.1 In $Q$, the relation $X^2-pX-1=0$ gives $X(X-p)=1$, so $X$ is invertible with inverse $X-p$. The homomorphism $Q\to\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]$ sending $p\mapsto x-x^{-1}$ and $X\mapsto x$ respects $G=0$. Conversely, sending $x\mapsto X$ defines a homomorphism $\mathbb Z[x,x^{-1}]\to Q$ because $X^{-1}=X-p$. The composites fix the generators $p,X$ and $x$, respectively, so these homomorphisms are inverse. The unique representatives from step 1.1 therefore give the asserted unique Laurent decomposition. [given, F1, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
