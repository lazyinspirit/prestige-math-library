## Example

Let $S=\{s,t\}$ with $m(s,t)=\infty$, so that $W=\langle s,t\rangle$ is the
infinite dihedral group with length $\ell$
([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]); put $u:=st$, so that $u$ has
infinite order, $W=\{u^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}\cup\{u^ks:k\in\mathbb Z\}$,
$sus^{-1}=u^{-1}$, and $\ell(u^k)=2|k|$ for every $k$, while $\ell(u^ks)=2|k|+1$
for $k\ge0$ and $\ell(u^ks)=2|k|-1$ for $k\le-1$
([[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]] (3)(c), (4) and (7): the
alternating words representing these elements are reduced). Let $I=\{s\}$ and
$J=\{t\}$, so that $W_I=\{1,s\}$, $W_J=\{1,t\}$ and
$W_I\cap W_J=W_\emptyset=\{1\}$
([[thm-cg-parabolic-intersections-and-coset-factorization]] (1)); these are the
*standard* parabolics of rank $1$.

**(i) All parabolics of $W$.** The parabolic subgroups of $W$ are exactly
$\{1\}$, the order-two subgroups $\{1,r\}$ generated by a reflection $r\in T$,
and $W$; in particular $W_I$ and $W_J$ are distinct, non-conjugate standard
parabolics with trivial intersection.

**(ii) The quotients.**
${}^IW=\{w:\ell(sw)>\ell(w)\}=\{u^{-k}:k\ge0\}\cup\{u^{-k}s:k\ge1\}=\{1,t,ts,tst,\dots\}$,
$W^J=\{w:\ell(wt)>\ell(w)\}=\{u^{k}:k\le0\}\cup\{u^{k}s:k\ge0\}=\{1,s,ts,sts,tsts,\dots\}$,
and ${}^IW^J=(ts)^{\mathbb N}=\{1,ts,tsts,tststs,\dots\}=\{u^{-k}:k\ge0\}$: the
two-sided descent-free elements are exactly the powers of the translation $ts$.

**(iii) The double cosets.** For every $k\ge0$ the set $W_I(ts)^kW_J$ is a
double coset with exactly four elements,

$$W_I(ts)^kW_J=\{(ts)^k,\ s(ts)^k,\ (ts)^kt,\ s(ts)^kt\}=\{u^{-k},\ u^{k}s,\ u^{-(k+1)}s,\ u^{k+1}\},$$

of lengths $2k,2k+1,2k+1,2k+2$, and its minimum is $d_k=(ts)^k$ with length
$2k$ ([[thm-cg-double-coset-unique-minimum-and-normal-form]] (2)). Here
$K=\{s'\in I:d_k^{-1}s'd_k\in J\}$ is empty for every $k\ge0$, so $W_I^K=W_I$,
and $|W_I(ts)^kW_J|=4=|W_I|\cdot|W_J|/|W_K|$ in agreement with (3) of the same
theorem. Finally $W$ is the disjoint union of the double cosets
$W_I(ts)^kW_J$, $k\ge0$; the first one is

$$W_IW_J=\{1,s,t,st\},$$

and as $k$ runs over $\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$ the elements
$u^{-k},u^ks,u^{-(k+1)}s,u^{k+1}$ of these blocks exhaust the lists $u^m$ and
$u^ms$ ($m\in\mathbb Z$) without repetition, so the decomposition is exhaustive
and the parts are disjoint.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** the Coxeter matrix with $S=\{s,t\}$, $m(s,t)=\infty$, the presented group $W$ with length $\ell$, $u=st$, and the subsets $I=\{s\}$, $J=\{t\}$.

[F1] $st$ has infinite order in $W$ and $s\ne t$; alternating words in $s,t$ are reduced in the ambient group, so the value of an alternating word of length $q\ge1$ beginning with $s$ or with $t$ has length exactly $q$ ([[lem-hh-dihedral-root-recurrence-and-root-sign]]).

[F2] $W_I=\langle s:s\in I\rangle$, ${}^IW=\{w:\ell(sw)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in I\}$, $W^J=\{w:\ell(ws)>\ell(w)\text{ for all }s\in J\}$, ${}^IW^J={}^IW\cap W^J$, $W_\emptyset=\{1\}$, and the parabolic subgroups are the conjugates $wW_Iw^{-1}$ ([[def-cg-parabolic-quotient-and-two-sided-minima]]).

[F3] $T=\{wsw^{-1}:w\in W,\ s\in S\}$ is the set of reflections, so it contains $s$, $t$ and every conjugate of a simple reflection ([[def-cg-canonical-reflection-homomorphism]]).

[F4] Every double coset $W_IwW_J$ contains exactly one element of ${}^IW^J$, its unique minimum; that element $d$ satisfies $\ell(d)\le\ell(x)$, with equality only for $x=d$, and $|W_IdW_J|=|W_I^K|\cdot|W_J|$ where $K=I\cap dJd^{-1}$, so that for finite $W_I$ also $|W_IdW_J|=|W_I||W_J|/|W_K|$ ([[thm-cg-double-coset-unique-minimum-and-normal-form]]).

[F5] $W_I=\{w\in W:S(w)\subseteq I\}$ and $W_I\cap S=I$; in particular the two-element subgroup $\{1,s\}$ is $W_I$ and $\{1,t\}$ is $W_J$ ([[thm-hh-parabolic-minimal-representatives-and-length-additivity]]).

[F6] The presented group $W$ has the universal property: an assignment of $s,t$ to elements of a group satisfying $f(s)^2=f(t)^2=1$ extends to a homomorphism $W\to G$ ([[def-hh-coxeter-matrix-word-group-and-length]]).

[F7] A group homomorphism $\varphi:G\to G'$ satisfies $\varphi(xy)=\varphi(x)\varphi(y)$ and $\varphi(x^{-1})=\varphi(x)^{-1}$, hence $\varphi(ghg^{-1})=\varphi(g)\varphi(h)\varphi(g)^{-1}$ for all $g,h\in G$ ([[def-group-homomorphism]]).

[F8] $W_I\cap W_J=W_{I\cap J}=W_\emptyset=\{1\}$ ([[thm-cg-parabolic-intersections-and-coset-factorization]]).

## Verification

**Proof technique:** direct reduced-word computations in the infinite dihedral group.

1.1 *The parabolic subgroups.* By [F2] the parabolic subgroups are the conjugates of $\{1\}$, $W_I=\{1,s\}$, $W_J=\{1,t\}$ and $W_S=W$; here $\{1\}$ and $W$ are their own conjugates, and the conjugates of $\{1,s\}$ or of $\{1,t\}$ are the two-element subgroups $\{1,r\}$ with $r\in T$, while conversely every $r\in T$ is a conjugate of $s$ or of $t$ by [F3]. Hence the parabolic subgroups are exactly $\{1\}$, the $\{1,r\}$ with $r\in T$, and $W$. By [F8], $W_I\cap W_J=\{1\}$, and the two standard parabolics are not conjugate to each other: the assignment $s\mapsto0$, $t\mapsto1$ sends the relators $s^2,t^2$ to $0$, so by [F6] it defines a homomorphism $\varphi\colon W\to\mathbb Z/2$, and if $w\{1,s\}w^{-1}=\{1,t\}$, then $wsw^{-1}=t$ and [F7] gives $0=\varphi(s)=\varphi(wsw^{-1})=\varphi(t)=1$, a contradiction. [F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F8]

1.2 *The one-sided quotients.* First $sus^{-1}=s(st)s=ts=u^{-1}$, so $su^ms=u^{-m}$ for every integer $m$. Every element of $W$ is $u^m$ or $u^ms$ with $m\in\mathbb Z$: any word in $s,t$ can be shortened by cancelling adjacent equal letters until it alternates, and an alternating word is $(st)^m$, $(ts)^m$, $(st)^ms$ or $(ts)^ms$, which is $u^m$, $u^{-m}$, $u^ms$ or $u^{-m}s$. The two families are disjoint: if $u^m=u^{m'}s$ then $s=u^{m-m'}\in\langle u\rangle$, and $s=u^k$ implies $u^{-k}=su^ks=s\cdot s\cdot s=s=u^k$, where the first equality uses $sus^{-1}=u^{-1}$ and $s=s^{-1}$, so $u^{2k}=1$ and $k=0$ by the infinite order of $u$, whence $s=1$ and $st=t$ of order $2$, contradicting [F1]. Using the reduced alternating forms of [F1]: for $m\ge1$ one has $s\,u^m=s(st)^m=t(st)^{m-1}$ of length $2m-1<2m$, so $u^m\notin{}^IW$; for $m<0$ the element $s\,u^m=(st)^{|m|}s$ is alternating of length $2|m|+1>2|m|=\ell(u^m)$, and for $m=0$ one has $s\cdot1=s$ of length $1>0$, so no left descent occurs for $m\le0$; for $m\ge0$ one has $s(u^ms)=u^{-m}$ of length $2m<2m+1=\ell(u^ms)$, so $u^ms\notin{}^IW$; and for $m\le-1$ the element $s(u^ms)=u^{-m}$ has length $2|m|>2|m|-1=\ell(u^ms)$, so no left descent occurs. Hence ${}^IW=\{u^m:m\le0\}\cup\{u^ms:m\le-1\}=\{u^{-k}:k\ge0\}\cup\{u^{-k}s:k\ge1\}$. Dually, for $m\ge1$ one has $u^mt=(st)^{m-1}s$ of length $2m-1<2m$, so $u^m\notin W^J$; for $m\le0$ the element $u^mt=(ts)^{|m|}t$ is alternating of length $2|m|+1>2|m|=\ell(u^m)$, so no right descent occurs; for $m\ge0$ one has $u^mst=u^{m+1}$ of length $2m+2>2m+1=\ell(u^ms)$, so $u^ms\in W^J$; and for $m\le-1$ the element $u^mst=u^{m+1}$ has length $2|m|-2<2|m|-1=\ell(u^ms)$, so $u^ms\notin W^J$. Hence $W^J=\{u^m:m\le0\}\cup\{u^ms:m\ge0\}$. [F1, F2]

2.1 *The blocks of the decomposition.* For $k\ge0$ put $d_k:=(ts)^k=u^{-k}$. Then $s(ts)^k=(st)^ks=u^ks$, since $s\,t\,s=sts$ may be rewritten as $(st)s$; further $(ts)^kt=(ts)^{k+1}s=u^{-(k+1)}s$, and $s(ts)^kt=(st)^{k+1}=u^{k+1}$. Hence $W_I(ts)^kW_J=\{u^{-k},u^ks,u^{-(k+1)}s,u^{k+1}\}$, and by the reduced alternating forms of [F1] these four elements have lengths $2k$, $2k+1$, $2(k+1)-1=2k+1$ and $2k+2$; they are pairwise distinct, the two translations having different lengths and the two $s$-multiples being equal only if $u^{2k+1}=1$, impossible by [F1]. The minimum length among them is $2k=\ell(d_k)$, and $d_k=u^{-k}\in{}^IW^J$ by the description of step 1.2, so [F4] shows that $d_k$ is the unique minimum of the double coset $W_I(ts)^kW_J$. [F1, F2, F4, step 1.2]

2.2 *The two-sided quotient.* By step 1.2, ${}^IW^J={}^IW\cap W^J$ consists of the elements of the list $\{u^m:m\le0\}\cup\{u^ms:m\le-1\}$ that also lie in the list $\{u^m:m\le0\}\cup\{u^ms:m\ge0\}$. Since the two families are disjoint, an element of the first family lies in the second list precisely when its index already satisfies $m\le0$, and an element of the second family would have to satisfy $m\le-1$ (for ${}^IW$) and $m\ge0$ (for $W^J$) simultaneously, which is impossible. Hence ${}^IW^J=\{u^m:m\le0\}=\{u^{-k}:k\ge0\}=\{(ts)^k:k\ge0\}$. [F1, F2, step 1.2]

3.1 *The intersection, the sizes and the decomposition.* For $k\ge0$ one has $d_k^{-1}sd_k=u^ksu^{-k}=u^{2k}s$ because $su^{-k}s=u^{k}$; the only element of $I$ is $s$, and $u^{2k}s\in J=\{t\}$ would force $u^{2k}s=t=u^{-1}s$, hence $u^{2k}=u^{-1}$ and $u^{2k+1}=1$, impossible since $u$ has infinite order by [F1]. Hence $K=\emptyset$, $W_K=\{1\}$ and $W_I^K=W_I$; the size formula of [F4] gives $|W_I(ts)^kW_J|=2\cdot2=4=2\cdot2/1$. Finally each element of $W$ is $u^m$ or $u^ms$ with a unique $m\in\mathbb Z$, and the four families $u^{-k}(k\ge0)$, $u^{k+1}(k\ge0)$, $u^ks(k\ge0)$ and $u^{-(k+1)}s(k\ge0)$ list exactly $u^m$ for $m\le0$, $u^m$ for $m\ge1$, $u^ms$ for $m\ge0$ and $u^ms$ for $m\le-1$, without repetition by the disjointness of the two families in step 1.2. Hence $W$ is the disjoint union of the double cosets $W_I(ts)^kW_J$, $k\ge0$, the first block being $W_IW_J=\{1,s,t,st\}$; this completes the example. [F1, F4, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
