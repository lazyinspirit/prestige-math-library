## Definition

Let $\mathcal O_D$ be the quadratic order of discriminant $D$ in
$K_D=\mathbb Q(\sqrt D)$. A fractional $\mathcal O_D$-ideal is a nonzero
$\mathcal O_D$-submodule $I\subseteq K_D$ for which $dI\subseteq\mathcal O_D$
for some $0\ne d\in\mathcal O_D$; the product $IJ$ consists of finite sums
$\sum_j x_jy_j$ with $x_j\in I$ and $y_j\in J$, and the principal fractional
ideal generated by $0\ne\lambda\in K_D$ is $\lambda\mathcal O_D$. A nonzero
fractional $\mathcal O_D$-ideal $I$ is
**proper** if its multiplier ring
$(I:I)=\{x\in K_D:xI\subseteq I\}$ equals $\mathcal O_D$, and it is
**invertible** if $I(\mathcal O_D:I)=\mathcal O_D$. For $D<0$, the ordinary
proper ideal class group $\operatorname{Pic}(\mathcal O_D)$ is the quotient of
the proper invertible fractional ideals by the principal fractional ideals
$(\lambda)=\lambda\mathcal O_D$ for all $0\ne\lambda\in K_D$. For $D>0$, the
narrow proper ideal class group $\operatorname{Pic}^+(\mathcal O_D)$ is the
quotient by those $(\lambda)$ for which both real embeddings of $\lambda$ are
positive. In both cases multiplication is induced by fractional-ideal product.

For $D>0$, the two real embeddings send $\sqrt D$ to $\sqrt D$ and
$-\sqrt D$, respectively; a scalar is **totally positive** when both images
are positive.
