## Statement

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic zero and let $\rho:\Gamma\to SL_2(F)$ be a representation of a finitely generated group which is absolutely irreducible, meaning that no line is invariant under all its matrices after extension to any field containing $F$. Let $K\subseteq F$ be the subfield generated by the prime field and all traces $\operatorname{tr}\rho(g)$, $g\in\Gamma$. There are words $A=\rho(a)$ and $B=\rho(b)$ and an element $\xi$ satisfying $\xi^2-(\operatorname{tr}A)\xi+1=0$, with $\xi\ne\xi^{-1}$, such that over $F(\xi)$ the representation is conjugate by a matrix in $GL_2(F(\xi))$ to one whose generator matrices all lie in $SL_2(K(\xi))$. Thus the required model field has degree at most two over $K$. The entries are obtained by explicit finite formulas from $\xi$ and the traces of $A,B,AB,C,AC,BC,ABC$ for each generator matrix $C$. If $K(\xi)\ne K$, its nonidentity automorphism is realized on this model by simultaneous conjugation with one explicit matrix in $GL_2(K(\xi))$. In particular, suppose a projective point $z\in\mathbb P^r(K(\xi))$ is obtained from the model by a construction commuting with field automorphisms and invariant under simultaneous $GL_2$ conjugation. Then $z$ is defined over $K$. This last clause requires the stated compatibility and invariance; it asserts no analytic, homology-rank or quotient-existence theorem.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, finitely generated group, absolutely irreducible representation and trace subfield in the Statement.

[F1] For a two-by-two matrix $T$, direct multiplication gives $T^2-\operatorname{tr}(T)T+\det(T)I=0$. A matrix with two distinct eigenvalues has the corresponding eigenvectors as a basis. A quadratic polynomial with distinct roots gives either the trivial extension or a separable quadratic extension, whose nonidentity automorphism exchanges the roots.

## Proof

1.1 **Find a noncentral semisimple word and a word meeting both eigenspaces.** Some group matrix has trace different from $2,-2$. Otherwise, if all matrices were scalar the representation would be reducible; choose a nonscalar $D=\epsilon I+N$ with $\epsilon=\pm1$, $N\ne0$ and $N^2=0$ by [F1]. For every group matrix $C$ and integer $m\ge0$, $$\operatorname{tr}(D^mC)=\epsilon^m\bigl(\operatorname{tr}C+m\epsilon^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(NC)\bigr).$$ All these traces belong to $\{2,-2\}$ by the supposition. In characteristic zero a nonconstant affine function on the nonnegative integers takes infinitely many values, so $\operatorname{tr}(NC)=0$ for every $C$. In a basis over $F$ with $N=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\0&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ this forces the lower-left entry of every $C$ to be zero. Its first coordinate line would be invariant, a contradiction. Thus choose a word $A$ with trace different from $\pm2$, and adjoin one root $\xi$ of its characteristic polynomial. Its two distinct eigenvalues are $\xi,\xi^{-1}$, so it is diagonalizable already over $F(\xi)$. In that eigenbasis some group word $B$ has both off-diagonal entries nonzero. Indeed the elements preserving the first and second eigenlines form two proper subgroups of $\Gamma$ by absolute irreducibility. A group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups: for elements in their respective differences, their product belongs to neither. Choose $B$ outside that union. These are finitely many existential selections, not an infinite choice principle. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 **Normalize and recover every generator by trace equations.** In the chosen $A$ eigenbasis write $B_{12}=u\ne0$ and conjugate by $\operatorname{diag}(u^{-1},1)$ to make this entry one. The lower-left entry becomes the nonzero product $q=B_{12}B_{21}$. Put $a=\operatorname{tr}A$, $b=\operatorname{tr}B$, $c=\operatorname{tr}(AB)$. In the normalized basis $$A=\begin{pmatrix}\xi&0\\0&\xi^{-1}\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}d_1&1\\q&d_2\end{pmatrix},\quad d_1=\frac{c-\xi^{-1}b}{\xi-\xi^{-1}},\quad d_2=b-d_1,\quad q=d_1d_2-1\ne0.$$ All these entries lie in $K'=K(\xi)$. For each generator $C$ put $e=\operatorname{tr}C$, $f=\operatorname{tr}(AC)$, $g=\operatorname{tr}(BC)$ and $h=\operatorname{tr}(ABC)$, which belong to $K$ by its definition. Its diagonal entries are $$c_{11}=\frac{f-\xi^{-1}e}{\xi-\xi^{-1}},\qquad c_{22}=e-c_{11}.$$ Its off-diagonal entries are uniquely solved from $$c_{21}+q c_{12}=g-d_1c_{11}-d_2c_{22},\qquad \xi c_{21}+\xi^{-1}q c_{12}=h-\xi d_1c_{11}-\xi^{-1}d_2c_{22}.$$ The determinant of this two-by-two linear system is $q(\xi^{-1}-\xi)\ne0$. Thus every normalized generator matrix has entries in $K'$. These are the actual conjugated original matrices in $F(\xi)$, not merely formal matrices with the correct traces: the normalization was performed first and the displayed linear equations uniquely recover their entries. Therefore their determinant-one equations and every group relation hold in $K'$, by its injection into $F(\xi)$. The conjugated representation is an actual model over $K'$, with no new relator-existence assumption. [F1, step 1.1, given, construct, algebra]

3.1 **The quadratic swap is explicit conjugation.** If $K'=K$ there is nothing to prove. Otherwise its automorphism $\tau$ exchanges $\xi$ and $\xi^{-1}$ and fixes every word trace. The formulas of step 2.1 give $\tau(d_1)=d_2$, $\tau(q)=q$. Put $$P=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\q&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad P^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&q^{-1}\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.$$ Direct multiplication gives $PAP^{-1}=\tau A$ and $PBP^{-1}=\tau B$. For each generator $C$, both $PCP^{-1}$ and $\tau C$ have traces $e,f,g,h$ when paired respectively with $I,\tau A,\tau B,(\tau A)(\tau B)$: conjugation preserves trace, and $\tau$ fixes the four word traces. The diagonal/off-diagonal recovery in step 2.1 applies to this swapped pair as well; its determinant is still nonzero. Uniqueness therefore gives $PCP^{-1}=\tau C$ for every generator, hence for all group words. This proves simultaneous conjugacy over $K'$ itself, without an appeal to pointwise complex-character classification. [F1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 **Projective descent for compatible invariant constructions.** Under the hypotheses on $z$, applying $\tau$ to its construction yields the point constructed from $\tau\rho$, while conjugation invariance and step 3.1 identify that point with $z$. Hence $\tau z=z$. Choose one nonzero homogeneous coordinate $z_j$ and normalize it to $1$. Equality of projective points implies that each ratio $z_i/z_j$ is fixed by $\tau$. Every element of the quadratic field has the unique form $u+v\xi$ with $u,v\in K$; being fixed gives $v(\xi-\xi^{-1})=0$, so $v=0$. Thus every normalized coordinate lies in $K$, proving that $z$ is a $K$-point. In the trivial-extension case this is immediate. The argument uses only finite matrix and field operations; it neither asserts that an arbitrary merely pointwise invariant assignment is rational nor supplies any analytic or chain-complex prerequisite for constructing $z$. [step 2.1, step 3.1, given, algebra] ∎
