## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $R\to S$ be a homomorphism of commutative rings with $S$ a nonzero $\mathbb Q$-algebra, and let $f\in S$. Assume that there is an $S$-linear map $\theta:\Omega_{S/R}\to S$ with $\theta(\mathrm df)=1$; equivalently, $S\,\mathrm df$ is freely generated by $\mathrm df$ and $\Omega_{S/R}=S\,\mathrm df\oplus C$ for an $S$-submodule $C$ (then $\theta$ is projection onto this free summand and $C=\ker\theta$). Then $f$ is not nilpotent, and if $S$ is a Noetherian local ring then $f$ is a nonzerodivisor. No finiteness of $S$ over $R$ is assumed. The Axiom of Choice is inherited through the local-ring unit characterization and the Krull intersection theorem in the nonzerodivisor statement.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A homomorphism $R\to S$ of commutative rings with $S$ a nonzero $\mathbb Q$-algebra, an element $f\in S$, and an $S$-linear map $\theta:\Omega_{S/R}\to S$ with $\theta(\mathrm df)=1$.

[F1] [[def-kahler-differentials-algebra]]: $\Omega_{S/R}$ is an $S$-module with universal $R$-derivation $\mathrm d:S\to\Omega_{S/R}$; $\mathrm d$ is additive, satisfies $\mathrm d(ab)=a\,\mathrm db+b\,\mathrm da$ and kills the image of $R$, and composition $g\mapsto g\circ\mathrm d$ is a bijection $\operatorname{Hom}_S(\Omega_{S/R},M)\to\operatorname{Der}_R(S,M)$ for every $S$-module $M$.

[F2] [[def-derivation-algebra]]: an $R$-derivation $D:S\to S$ is additive, kills the image of $R$, and satisfies the Leibniz rule $D(ab)=a\,D(b)+b\,D(a)$; sums and scalar multiples of derivations are derivations.

[F3] [[thm-local-ring-unit-characterisations]]: assuming Choice, in a nonzero local ring the nonunits are exactly the elements of the unique maximal ideal.

[F4] [[def-jacobson-radical-of-a-ring]]: the Jacobson radical $J(S)$ is the intersection of the maximal ideals; combined with [F3], in a local ring $J(S)$ is the unique maximal ideal.

[F5] [[thm-krull-intersection-theorem]]: for a Noetherian ring $S$, an ideal $I$ and a finite $S$-module $M$, if $I\subseteq J(S)$ then $\bigcap_{n\ge0}I^nM=0$.

## Proof

1.1 The two hypotheses are equivalent: if $\theta(\mathrm df)=1$ then $S\,\mathrm df\cap\ker\theta=0$, because $\theta(s\,\mathrm df)=s$ vanishes only for $s=0$, and every element of $\Omega_{S/R}$ is the sum of its $S\,\mathrm df$-component $(\theta(\omega))\,\mathrm df$ and its $\ker\theta$-component; conversely, such a decomposition with $S\,\mathrm df$ freely generated by $\mathrm df$ defines an $S$-linear $\theta$ by $\theta(\mathrm df)=1$ and $\theta|_C=0$, so the displayed hypothesis and its reformulation agree. [F1, given, algebra]

1.2 Define $D:S\to S$ by $D(a)=\theta(\mathrm da)$. Then $D$ is an $R$-derivation in the sense of [F2]: it is additive because $\mathrm d$ and $\theta$ are additive; it kills the image of $R$ because $\mathrm d$ does and $\theta$ is $S$-linear; and it satisfies the Leibniz rule because $D(ab)=\theta(a\,\mathrm db+b\,\mathrm da)=a\theta(\mathrm db)+b\theta(\mathrm da)=aD(b)+bD(a)$. In particular $D(f)=\theta(\mathrm df)=1$. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 The element $f$ is not nilpotent. Suppose $f^n=0$ and let $n\ge1$ be minimal with this property. If $n=1$, then $f=0$ and $D(f)=D(0)=0$, contradicting $D(f)=1$. If $n\ge2$, then the Leibniz rule and induction on $n$ give $D(f^n)=n f^{n-1}D(f)=n f^{n-1}$, while $D(f^n)=D(0)=0$; since $n$ is invertible in the $\mathbb Q$-algebra $S$, this forces $f^{n-1}=0$, contradicting the minimality of $n$. So no such $n$ exists. [F2, step 1.2, given, algebra]

2.2 Assume now that $S$ is a Noetherian local ring and let $a\in S$ with $fa=0$. If $f$ is a unit then $a=0$; otherwise $f$ is a nonunit, so $f$ lies in the unique maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ of $S$ by [F3], and $J(S)=\mathfrak m$ by [F4]. We prove $a\in(f^n)$ for all $n\ge1$ by induction. For $n=1$ the Leibniz rule gives $0=D(fa)=fD(a)+aD(f)=fD(a)+a$, so $a=-fD(a)\in(f)$. If $a=f^n b$, then $0=D(f^{n+1}b)=(n+1)f^n b+f^{n+1}D(b)$, so $f^n b=-(n+1)^{-1}f^{n+1}D(b)$ and $a=f^{n+1}\bigl(-(n+1)^{-1}D(b)\bigr)\in(f^{n+1})$, using that $n+1$ is invertible in $S$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 By step 2.2, $a\in\bigcap_{n\ge1}(f^n)$, an intersection inside the finite $S$-module $M=S$; since $(f)\subseteq\mathfrak m=J(S)$ and $S$ is Noetherian, the Krull intersection theorem [F5] gives $\bigcap_{n\ge0}(f^n)=0$. Hence $a=0$, so $fa=0$ implies $a=0$: the element $f$ is a nonzerodivisor. [F5, step 2.2] ∎
