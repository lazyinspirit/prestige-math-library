## Statement

Let $\pi$ be a group and $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$. For every
$u\in\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ there are $c\ge1$ and an invertible matrix
$A\in\mathrm{GL}_c(R)$ whose class in $K_1(R)$ maps to $u$ under the quotient
$K_1(R)\to\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$. Conversely every invertible matrix over $R$
defines an element of $\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ by this quotient. Stabilizing a
representative by $A\mapsto A\oplus I_b$ does not change the class.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group $\pi$, its integral group ring $R=\mathbb Z[\pi]$, and an element $u\in\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$.

[F1] The stable general linear group is the union $\mathrm{GL}(R)=\bigcup_{n\ge0}\mathrm{GL}_n(R)$ along the stabilizations $A\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(A,1)$, so every element of $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ is represented by an invertible $n\times n$ matrix for some finite $n$, and a matrix is invertible when it has a two-sided inverse; the class $[A]\in K_1(R)$ of an invertible matrix is unchanged by stabilization, that is $[\operatorname{diag}(A,I_b)]=[A]$, and the group law satisfies $[AB]=[A]+[B]$ and $[I_n]=0$ ([[def-stable-general-linear-group-and-elementary-subgroup-of-a-ring]], [[def-k-one-of-a-ring-and-the-whitehead-group-of-a-discrete-group]], [[def-invertible-matrix-and-general-linear-group]]).

[F2] $K_1(R)=\mathrm{GL}(R)/\mathrm E(R)$ is the quotient of the stable general linear group by the stable elementary subgroup, and $\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)=K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])/\langle[\pm g]:g\in\pi\rangle$ is the further quotient by the subgroup generated by the classes of the $1\times1$ units $\pm g$; the quotient map $K_1(\mathbb Z[\pi])\to\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ is the canonical projection ([[def-k-one-of-a-ring-and-the-whitehead-group-of-a-discrete-group]], [[def-group-ring]]).

## Proof

1.1 By [F2] the group $K_1(R)$ is the quotient of $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ by $\mathrm E(R)$, so the canonical projection $\mathrm{GL}(R)\to K_1(R)$ is surjective. By [F1] every element of $\mathrm{GL}(R)$ is represented by an invertible $c\times c$ matrix over $R$ for some finite $c\ge0$, and $K_1(R)$ is the quotient of that group, so every class in $K_1(R)$ is the class $[A]$ of such a matrix. [F1, F2]

2.1 By [F2] the group $\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ is the quotient of $K_1(R)$ by the subgroup generated by the classes of the units $\pm g$, so the projection $q:K_1(R)\to\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ is surjective: for the given $u\in\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$ there is a class $[A]\in K_1(R)$ with $q([A])=u$. Combining this with step 1.1 exhibits an invertible matrix $A$ of some size $c\ge1$ whose class in $K_1(R)$ maps to $u$; if $u=0$ one may take $A=I_1$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

2.2 Conversely, if $A\in\mathrm{GL}_c(R)$ is invertible, then by [F1] it represents the class $[A]\in K_1(R)$ of the stabilized matrix $\operatorname{diag}(A,I_b)$ for every $b\ge0$, and [F2] turns this class into the element $q([A])\in\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$. Stabilization does not change the underlying stable class, because $\operatorname{diag}(A,I_b)$ is by definition the image of $A$ in $\mathrm{GL}_{c+b}(R)$ under the iterated stabilization, and hence $[\operatorname{diag}(A,I_b)]=[A]$ in $K_1(R)$ and $q([\operatorname{diag}(A,I_b)])=q([A])$ in $\operatorname{Wh}(\pi)$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 give the two asserted directions, and step 2.2 also gives the stabilization statement; the argument is a direct reading of the definitions of $K_1$ and of $\operatorname{Wh}$ and uses no choice principle and no property of the group $\pi$ beyond the definition of its group ring. [step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎
