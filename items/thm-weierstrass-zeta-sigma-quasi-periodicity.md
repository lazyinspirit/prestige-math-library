## Statement

Let $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2\subseteq\mathbb C$ be a full
complex lattice with oriented basis $(\omega_1,\omega_2)$
([[def-complex-lattice-and-complex-torus]]), let
$\wp=\wp_\Lambda$, $\zeta=\zeta_\Lambda$ and $\sigma=\sigma_\Lambda$ be its
Weierstrass functions
([[def-weierstrass-elliptic-p-function]],
[[def-weierstrass-zeta-and-sigma-functions]]), and put
$\eta_j:=2\zeta(\omega_j/2)$ for $j=1,2$, so that $\omega_1,\omega_2$ are
primitive and $\eta_j=\eta_{\omega_j}$. Then:

1. the finite-subset net defining $\zeta$ converges normally on
   $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ (uniformly on every compact subset), the
   unordered limit being independent of the exhaustion of
   $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ by finite subsets; the function $\zeta$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb C$,
   holomorphic exactly on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, odd, and has at each
   lattice point $\lambda\in\Lambda$ a simple pole with principal part
   $(z-\lambda)^{-1}$ and residue $1$, with no other poles; moreover
   $$\zeta_\Lambda'(z)=-\wp_\Lambda(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda);$$
2. the finite-subset net defining the product of the elementary factors
   $E_2(z/\omega)$ over $\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ converges normally on
   $\mathbb C$, independently of the exhaustion, so that $\sigma$ is entire; $\sigma$ is odd, its zero set is
   exactly $\Lambda$ and every zero is simple, $\sigma'(0)=1$, and
   $$\frac{\sigma_\Lambda'(z)}{\sigma_\Lambda(z)}=\zeta_\Lambda(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda);$$
3. the **quasi-period laws** hold, for $j=1,2$ and all
   $z\in\mathbb C$ (with poles matched),
   $$\zeta_\Lambda(z+\omega_j)=\zeta_\Lambda(z)+\eta_j,\qquad \sigma_\Lambda(z+\omega_j)=-\exp\!\left(\eta_j\Bigl(z+\frac{\omega_j}{2}\Bigr)\right)\sigma_\Lambda(z),$$
   and the **Legendre relation** holds:
   $$\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i .$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A full complex lattice $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2$ with oriented basis $(\omega_1,\omega_2)$, the summands $g_\omega(z):=1/(z-\omega)+1/\omega+z/\omega^2$ and $h_\omega(z):=(z-\omega)^{-2}-\omega^{-2}$ for $\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$, the factors $F_\omega(z):=E_2(z/\omega)=(1-z/\omega)\exp(z/\omega+z^2/2\omega^2)$ for $\omega\ne0$, and the functions $\wp=\wp_\Lambda$, $\zeta=\zeta_\Lambda$, $\sigma=\sigma_\Lambda$ defined by the unordered finite-subset nets $\wp(z)=z^{-2}+\sum_{\omega\ne0}h_\omega(z)$, $\zeta(z)=1/z+\sum_{\omega\ne0}g_\omega(z)$ and $\sigma(z)=z\prod_{\omega\ne0}F_\omega(z)$ of [[def-weierstrass-elliptic-p-function]] and [[def-weierstrass-zeta-and-sigma-functions]]; also $\eta_j:=2\zeta(\omega_j/2)$.

[F1] $\Lambda=\mathbb Z\omega_1+\mathbb Z\omega_2$ is a subgroup of $\mathbb C$ with $\omega_1,\omega_2$ real-linearly independent and $\operatorname{Im}(\omega_2/\omega_1)>0$ in an oriented basis ([[def-complex-lattice-and-complex-torus]]); $\mathbb C$ is a real vector space spanned by $\{1,i\}$ and an independent set is no larger than a finite spanning set, so $\omega_1,\omega_2$ are a real basis of $\mathbb C$: every $z\in\mathbb C$ has unique real coordinates $z=s\omega_1+t\omega_2$ ([[thm-complex-numbers-are-the-real-coordinate-plane]], [[cor-independent-set-is-no-larger-than-a-finite-spanning-set]]).

[F2] For all $z,w\in\mathbb C$: $|z|\ge0$ with $|z|=0$ exactly for $z=0$, $|zw|=|z|\,|w|$ and $|z+w|\le|z|+|w|$ ([[lem-complex-conjugation-and-modulus-laws]]); continuity and convergence on $\mathbb C$ are the metric notions for $d_{\mathbb C}(z,w)=|z-w|$ ([[def-complex-metric-convergence-and-continuity]]); a complex differentiable function is continuous ([[cor-complex-differentiability-implies-continuity]]).

[F3] $\wp_\Lambda(z)=z^{-2}+\sum_{\omega\ne0}h_\omega(z)$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ and the defining sums of $\zeta_\Lambda,\sigma_\Lambda$ are the unordered finite-subset limits over $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ given by the displayed formulas $1/z+\sum_{\omega\ne0}g_\omega$ and $z\prod_{\omega\ne0}F_\omega$; $\eta_\omega=2\zeta_\Lambda(\omega/2)$ for primitive $\omega$, the laws stated here are the ones promised by the definition, and the elementary factor is $E_2(w)=(1-w)e^{w+w^2/2}$ ([[def-weierstrass-elliptic-p-function]], [[def-weierstrass-zeta-and-sigma-functions]], [[def-weierstrass-elementary-factor]]).

[F4] The $\wp$-series converges absolutely at every $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, uniformly on every compact subset of $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, independently of any enumeration; $\wp$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, even and $\Lambda$-periodic, with a double pole of principal part $(z-\lambda)^{-2}$ at each $\lambda\in\Lambda$ and no other poles; and $\wp'(z)=-2\sum_{\omega\in\Lambda}(z-\omega)^{-3}$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ with that series normally convergent, $\wp'$ being odd and $\Lambda$-elliptic ([[thm-weierstrass-p-normal-convergence-and-periodicity]]).

[F5] The complex exponential is entire with $\exp'=\exp$ ([[thm-complex-exponential-is-entire-with-derivative-itself]]) and satisfies $\exp(z+w)=\exp z\exp w$ for all $z,w$, with $\exp 0=1$ ([[thm-complex-exponential-addition-and-real-extension]], [[def-complex-exponential]]); hence $\exp$ never vanishes. For $|w|\le1$ and every integer $p\ge0$ one has $|1-E_p(w)|\le|w|^{p+1}$, so in particular $|1-E_2(w)|\le|w|^3$ ([[lem-unit-disc-estimate-for-weierstrass-elementary-factors]]).

[F6] Complex derivatives are linear, satisfy the product and reciprocal rules, and the derivative of the identity is $1$; the chain rule holds for composable complex differentiable maps ([[thm-algebra-of-complex-derivatives]], [[thm-chain-rule-for-complex-derivatives]]). A holomorphic function on a domain whose derivative vanishes identically is constant ([[thm-zero-complex-derivative-on-a-domain-implies-constant]]). A function holomorphic on a punctured disc whose principal part there is zero, or which has a finite limit at the centre, extends holomorphically across the centre ([[thm-removable-singularity-characterizations]], [[thm-laurent-regular-principal-decomposition]]).

[F7] If $f$ is holomorphic on a punctured disc around $a$ and $(z-a)^mf(z)$ extends holomorphically to $a$ with a nonzero value, then $a$ is a pole of order $m$, with principal part $c_{-m}(z-a)^{-m}+\cdots+c_{-1}(z-a)^{-1}$; a pole of order $1$ is a simple pole, and the residue is the Laurent coefficient $c_{-1}$ ([[def-isolated-singularity-types]], [[def-simple-pole]], [[thm-pole-characterizations]], [[def-residue-isolated-singularity]], [[thm-laurent-regular-principal-decomposition]]). A holomorphic function has a zero of order $m$ at $a$ exactly when it equals $(z-a)^mg(z)$ near $a$ with $g$ holomorphic and $g(a)\ne0$ ([[thm-zero-order-factorization-holomorphic-function]]).

[F8] A function $f:\Omega\setminus P\to\mathbb C$ is meromorphic on a connected open set $\Omega$ exactly when it is holomorphic on $\Omega\setminus P$ and every point of $P$ is a pole of $f$ ([[def-meromorphic-function-complex-domain]]).

[F9] Let $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb C$ be open, let $f$ be meromorphic on $\Omega$ with pole set $S$, and let $\Gamma$ be admissible for the residue theorem in $\Omega$; then $\int_\Gamma f(z)\,dz=2\pi i\sum_{a\in S}n(\Gamma,a)\operatorname{Res}(f,a)$, only finitely many terms being nonzero. A cycle $\Gamma$ is admissible in $\Omega$ when $\Gamma^\ast\subseteq\Omega\setminus S$ and $\Gamma$ is null-homologous in $\Omega$, that is $n(\Gamma,p)=0$ for every $p\in\mathbb C\setminus\Omega$ ([[thm-residue-theorem-null-homologous-cycle]], [[def-admissible-cycle-for-residue-theorem]], [[def-null-homologous-and-homologous-complex-cycles]]).

[F10] Let $w<w'$, let $\alpha,\beta:[w,w']\to\mathbb R$ be continuous with $\alpha\le\beta$, real-analytic on $(w,w')$ with Puiseux-analytic graphs, put $T=\{x+iy:w\le y\le w',\ \alpha(y)\le x\le\beta(y)\}$, and let $\gamma$ be the positively oriented boundary contour of $T$. Then $n(\gamma,q)=1$ for every $q\in T^\circ$ and $n(\gamma,q)=0$ for every $q\in\mathbb C\setminus T$; the same two index assertions hold for the region $\sigma(T)$ with boundary contour $\sigma\circ\gamma$, for every orientation-preserving similarity $\sigma(z)=cz+d$, $c\in\mathbb C^\times$ ([[lem-index-of-graph-bounded-region-boundary]]).

[F11] For a piecewise-$C^1$ contour $\gamma$ and a function $f$ continuous on its trace, $\int_\gamma f(z)\,dz=\sum_j\int f(\gamma_j(t))\gamma_j'(t)\,dt$ over the smooth pieces ([[thm-riemann-stieltjes-and-parametric-contour-integrals-agree]], [[def-complex-line-integral-over-a-rectifiable-path]]); the reversal satisfies $\int_{\gamma^-}f\,dz=-\int_\gamma f\,dz$ and concatenation is additive, the concatenation and reversal of paths being those of [[def-complex-contours-reversal-concatenation-and-closedness]] ([[prop-reversal-and-concatenation-of-complex-line-integrals]]); and $\int_{\gamma\circ\phi}f\,dz=\int_\gamma f\,dz$ for a strictly increasing continuous bijection $\phi$ ([[thm-invariance-of-complex-line-integrals-under-increasing-reparametrization]]).

[F12] If holomorphic functions on an open set $\Omega$ converge locally uniformly to $g$, then $g$ is holomorphic and the derivatives converge locally uniformly to $g'$ ([[cor-locally-uniformly-convergent-holomorphic-series]], [[thm-weierstrass-convergence-holomorphic-functions]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 (Uniform gap and finiteness in discs.) Put $A:=|\omega_1|^2$, $B:=\operatorname{Re}(\omega_1\overline{\omega_2})$, $C:=|\omega_2|^2$ and $M:=\max(A,C)$. Expanding with [F2] gives $|s\omega_1+t\omega_2|^2=As^2+2Bst+Ct^2$ for real $s,t$, and $AC-B^2=(\operatorname{Im}(\omega_1\overline{\omega_2}))^2>0$ because real-linear independence forbids $\operatorname{Im}(\omega_1\overline{\omega_2})=0$. If $|s|\ge|t|$, write $x:=|s|$ and $\tau:=|t|\le x$: then $As^2-2|B||s||t|+Ct^2$ is a convex quadratic in $\tau$ whose values at $\tau=0$, at its vertex $\tau=|B|x/C$ and at $\tau=x$ are $Ax^2$, $x^2(AC-B^2)/C$ and $(A-2|B|+C)x^2$, all at least $x^2(AC-B^2)/C\ge x^2(AC-B^2)/M$; the case $|t|\ge|s|$ is symmetric with $A$ and $C$ interchanged. Hence $|s\omega_1+t\omega_2|\ge\delta\max(|s|,|t|)$ with $\delta:=\sqrt{(AC-B^2)/M}>0$: every nonzero lattice point has modulus at least $\delta$, while a lattice point $\lambda=m\omega_1+n\omega_2$ with $|\lambda|\le R$ has $|m|,|n|\le R/\delta$ by [F1] and the displayed inequality. Consequently $\Lambda\cap\overline{D}(0,R)$ has at most $(2R/\delta+1)^2$ elements, so every bounded set meets $\Lambda$ in finitely many points; in particular $F_n:=\{\omega\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}:|\omega|\le n\}$ is finite for every $n$. [F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 (The tail of $|\omega|^{-3}$ and the pointwise bounds.) For $|z|\le R$ and $|\omega|\ge2R$ one has $|z-\omega|\ge|\omega|-|z|\ge\tfrac12|\omega|$, so $|g_\omega(z)|=|z|^2/(|z-\omega|\,|\omega|^2)\le2R^2|\omega|^{-3}$ and, by [F5] applied with $p=2$ and $|z/\omega|\le\tfrac12\le1$, $|F_\omega(z)-1|=|1-E_2(z/\omega)|\le|z/\omega|^3\le R^3|\omega|^{-3}$. By step 1.1, the nonzero lattice points with $|\omega|<1$ are finite, and for $k\ge0$ the shell $2^k\le|\omega|<2^{k+1}$ has at most $(2^{k+2}/\delta+1)^2$ points. Hence $\sum_{\omega\ne0}|\omega|^{-3}$ is bounded by the finite contribution from $|\omega|<1$ plus the convergent series $\sum_{k\ge0}(2^{k+2}/\delta+1)^2 2^{-3k}$, so it converges and its finite-subset tails are arbitrarily small. [F2, F4, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 (The region $\mathbb C\setminus(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})$ is a domain.) Every point of $\Lambda$ is isolated in $\Lambda$ by the gap $\delta$ of step 1.1, so $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ is open; and $\mathbb C\setminus(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})$ contains the ball $B(0,\delta/2)$ by the gap, hence is open as well. For path-connectedness, let $x,y\in\mathbb C\setminus(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})$. By step 1.1 the segment $[x,y]$ meets $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ in finitely many points $p_1,\dots,p_N$. If $N=0$, the segment is already a path in the region. Otherwise choose $r>0$ smaller than $\delta/3$ and than every distance from a $p_j$ to either endpoint. The discs $B(p_j,r)$ are disjoint, contain no other lattice point, and neither endpoint lies in them; they also exclude $0$ because each nonzero lattice point is at least $\delta$ from $0$. Replace the subsegment through each $p_j$ by one of the two arcs on $\partial B(p_j,r)$ joining its endpoints. These arcs and the remaining straight pieces avoid $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$, so they form a path in the region from $x$ to $y$. [F2, given, step 1.1]

3.1 (Normal convergence away from the lattice.) Let $K\subseteq\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ be compact and choose $R\ge\sup_{z\in K}|z|$. The finite set $F_0:=\{\omega\ne0:|\omega|<2R\}$ contains every possible pole of a summand in $\overline D(0,R)$. For finite $F''\supseteq F'\supseteq F_0$, step 2.1 gives $\sup_K|\sum_{\omega\in F''\setminus F'}g_\omega|\le2R^2\sum_{\omega\in F''\setminus F'}|\omega|^{-3}\to0$ as $F'$ grows. Thus the finite-subset net converges uniformly on $K$, independently of its exhaustion, to $E:=\sum_{\omega\ne0}g_\omega$; its cofinal partial sums $S_n$ are holomorphic on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, so [F12] makes $E$ holomorphic there. For each fixed $\lambda\in\Lambda$, omit the term $g_\lambda$ if $\lambda\ne0$; every remaining summand is holomorphic on $B(\lambda,\delta/2)$ by the gap of step 1.1, and the same tail estimate gives locally uniform convergence on that ball. In particular $E$ is holomorphic near $0$, while near $\lambda\ne0$ only $g_\lambda$ has a pole. [F12, given, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 (The factors $F_\omega$: holomorphy, zeros, and the product lower bound.) By [F3] and [F5] each $F_\omega$ is holomorphic on $\mathbb C$ with $F_\omega(z)=0$ exactly when $z=\omega$, the zero at $\omega$ being simple because $F_\omega(z)=(1-z/\omega)e^{z/\omega+z^2/2\omega^2}$ has a simple factor $1-z/\omega$ and a nonvanishing exponential factor; in particular $F_\omega(0)=1$. Fix $z_0\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ and put $F_0:=\{\omega\ne0:|\omega|<2|z_0|\}$, a finite set by step 1.1; every factor $F_\omega(z_0)$ with $\omega\in F_0$ is nonzero because $z_0\ne\omega$, so $c_0:=\prod_{\omega\in F_0}\min(1,|F_\omega(z_0)|)>0$. For every finite $F$ one has $|\prod_{\omega\in F}F_\omega(z_0)|\ge c_0\exp(-2|z_0|^3T)>0$ where $T<\infty$ is the total supremum of the finite sums $\sum_{\omega\in G}|\omega|^{-3}$ from step 2.1: indeed $\prod_{\omega\in F\cap F_0}|F_\omega(z_0)|\ge c_0$ because $|F_\omega(z_0)|\ge\min(1,|F_\omega(z_0)|)$ for every $\omega$, while for $\omega\notin F_0$ one has $|F_\omega(z_0)|\ge1-|F_\omega(z_0)-1|\ge1-|z_0|^3|\omega|^{-3}\ge\tfrac12$ and hence $\log|F_\omega(z_0)|\ge-2|z_0|^3|\omega|^{-3}$ by $\log(1-t)\ge-2t$ on $[0,\tfrac12]$, so $\prod_{\omega\in F\setminus F_0}|F_\omega(z_0)|\ge\exp(-2|z_0|^3\sum_{\omega\in F\setminus F_0}|\omega|^{-3})\ge\exp(-2|z_0|^3T)$. [F3, F5, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 (Principal parts and residues of $\zeta$.) Let $\lambda\in\Lambda$. For $\lambda=0$, $\zeta(z)-z^{-1}=E(z)$ is holomorphic near $0$ by step 3.1. For $\lambda\ne0$, split off $g_\lambda$: $$\zeta(z)-\frac{1}{z-\lambda}=\frac1z+\frac1\lambda+\frac{z}{\lambda^2}+\sum_{\omega\ne0,\lambda}g_\omega(z).$$ The right side is holomorphic near $\lambda$ by step 3.1. Thus every lattice point is a simple pole of residue $1$; elsewhere $\zeta=1/z+E$ is holomorphic. The lattice is discrete by step 1.1, so $\zeta$ is meromorphic on $\mathbb C$ with precisely these poles. [F7, F8, given, step 1.1, step 3.1]

4.2 ($\zeta'=-\wp$.) The cofinal partial sums $S_n$ converge locally uniformly to $E$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ by step 3.1 and are holomorphic there. Thus [F12] gives $E'=\lim_nS_n'$ on that domain. Since $g_\omega'=-h_\omega$ by [F6] and the $h_\omega$-net converges normally there by [F4], $E'(z)=-\sum_{\omega\ne0}h_\omega(z)=z^{-2}-\wp(z)$. Differentiating $\zeta=1/z+E$ yields $\zeta'=-\wp$ on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$. [F4, F6, F12, given, step 3.1]

4.3 (Oddness of $\zeta$.) For $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ one has $\zeta(-z)=-1/z+\sum_{\omega\ne0}g_\omega(-z)$ in the net sense of [F3], and $g_\omega(-z)=z^2/(\omega^2(-z-\omega))=-z^2/((-\omega)^2(z-(-\omega)))=-g_{-\omega}(z)$ by [F2]; since $\omega\mapsto-\omega$ is a bijection of $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ preserving inclusion of finite sets, the reindexed net converges to $-E(z)$ by step 3.1. Therefore $\zeta(-z)=-1/z-E(z)=-\zeta(z)$ for all $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, which extends to $\mathbb C$ with poles matched. [F2, F3, given, step 3.1]

4.4 (The product net converges: $\sigma$ is entire with $\sigma(0)=0$ and $\sigma'(0)=1$.) Fix $R>0$ and put $F_0:=\{\omega\ne0:|\omega|<2R\}$; writing $u_\omega:=F_\omega-1$, step 2.1 gives $\sup_{|z|\le R}|u_\omega|\le R^3|\omega|^{-3}$ for $\omega\notin F_0$, so the finite sums $\sum_{\omega\in G}\sup_{|z|\le R}|u_\omega|$ are bounded over all finite $G$ and shrink to $0$ outside large finite sets by step 2.1; put $M_R$ for their supremum. For finite $F_0\subseteq F_1\subseteq F_2$ and $|z|\le R$ one has $$\Bigl|\prod_{\omega\in F_2}F_\omega(z)-\prod_{\omega\in F_1}F_\omega(z)\Bigr|\le e^{M_R}\Bigl(\exp\Bigl(\sum_{\omega\in F_2\setminus F_1}\sup_R|u_\omega|\Bigr)-1\Bigr)$$ because $|\prod_{\omega\in E}(1+u_\omega(z))-1|\le\exp(\sum_{\omega\in E}|u_\omega(z)|)-1$ for finite $E$ and $|\prod_{\omega\in F_1}F_\omega(z)|\le\exp(\sum_{\omega\in F_1}\sup_R|u_\omega|)\le e^{M_R}$; the right-hand side is arbitrarily small for $F_1$ large. Thus the finite-subset net of the products, indexed by the directed set of finite subsets of $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$, is uniformly Cauchy on every closed disc; along the cofinal sequence $F_n$ of step 1.1 the partial products $Q_n=\prod_{\omega\in F_n}F_\omega$ are holomorphic and converge uniformly on each closed disc to a limit $P$, which is holomorphic on $\mathbb C$ by [F12], and the net limit equals $P$ by cofinality and is independent of the exhaustion. Moreover $P(0)=1$, because every finite product equals $1$ at $0$ by $F_\omega(0)=1$ of step 3.2. Hence $\sigma(z)=zP(z)$ is entire with $\sigma(0)=0$, and the product rule of [F6] gives $\sigma'(0)=P(0)+0\cdot P'(0)=1$. [F6, F12, given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, choose]

5.1 (Nonvanishing off $\Lambda$ and the simple zeros at $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$.) For $z_0\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, step 3.2 shows that all finite products $\prod_{\omega\in F}F_\omega(z_0)$ have modulus at least $c_0\exp(-2|z_0|^3T)>0$, and step 4.4 makes $P(z_0)$ the limit of that net of numbers, so $|P(z_0)|\ge c_0\exp(-2|z_0|^3T)>0$; in particular $P$ has no zero on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$. Fix $\omega_0\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ and let $P^{\omega_0}$ be the limit of the net of finite products over finite subsets of $\Lambda\setminus\{0,\omega_0\}$: by the estimates of step 4.4 that sub-net converges uniformly on each closed disc as well, and $\prod_{\omega\in F}F_\omega=F_{\omega_0}(z)\prod_{\omega\in F\setminus\{\omega_0\}}F_\omega(z)$ for every finite $F\ni\omega_0$ gives, passing to the limit, $P(z)=F_{\omega_0}(z)P^{\omega_0}(z)$ for all $z$. Applying step 3.2 to the family $\Lambda\setminus\{0,\omega_0\}$ with $z_0=\omega_0$ shows $P^{\omega_0}(\omega_0)\ne0$, and $F_{\omega_0}(z)=(1-z/\omega_0)e^{z/\omega_0+z^2/2\omega_0^2}$ has the simple zero at $\omega_0$ by step 3.2 and [F5]; since the exponential and $P^{\omega_0}$ are nonzero at $\omega_0$, [F7] makes $\omega_0$ a simple zero of $P$, hence of $\sigma=zP$. Finally $0$ is a simple zero of $\sigma$ because $\sigma(z)=zP(z)$ with $P(0)=1\ne0$, and there are no other zeros: outside $\Lambda$ both $z$ and $P(z)$ are nonzero, and on $\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ the zeros just located are simple. [F5, F7, given, step 3.2, step 4.4]

5.2 (Oddness of $\sigma$.) For every finite $F\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\{0\}$ one has $\prod_{\omega\in F}F_\omega(-z)=\prod_{\omega\in F}E_2(-z/\omega)=\prod_{\eta\in-F}E_2(z/\eta)$ by [F2], and $F\mapsto-F$ is a bijection of the directed set of finite subsets preserving inclusion, so the two nets have the same limit by step 4.4: $P(-z)=P(z)$. Hence $\sigma(-z)=-zP(-z)=-zP(z)=-\sigma(z)$ for all $z\in\mathbb C$. [F2, given, step 4.4]

5.3 (Quasi-periodicity of $\zeta$.) Fix $j\in\{1,2\}$ and put $G_j(z):=\zeta(z+\omega_j)-\zeta(z)$ for $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, a holomorphic function there. Near any $\lambda\in\Lambda$, step 4.1 writes $\zeta(z)=1/(z-\lambda)+\psi_\lambda(z)$ and $\zeta(z+\omega_j)=1/(z-\lambda)+\psi_{\lambda+\omega_j}(z+\omega_j)$ with $\psi_\lambda,\psi_{\lambda+\omega_j}$ holomorphic near $\lambda$ and $\lambda+\omega_j$ respectively, so $G_j$ extends holomorphically across $\lambda$; thus $G_j$ is entire. On $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ one has $G_j'(z)=\zeta'(z+\omega_j)-\zeta'(z)=-\wp(z+\omega_j)+\wp(z)=0$ by step 4.2 and the periodicity of $\wp$ in [F4]. The complement of $\Lambda$ is dense in $\mathbb C$: for $k\ge2$ and $\lambda\in\Lambda$ the points $\lambda+\omega_1/k$ lie outside $\Lambda$, since $\lambda+\omega_1/k\in\Lambda$ would give $\omega_1/k\in\Lambda$, say $\omega_1=k(m\omega_1+n\omega_2)$ with integers $m,n$, whence $1=km$ and $0=kn$ by the uniqueness of the real coordinates in [F1], contradicting $k\ge2$. Hence the entire function $G_j'$, which vanishes on the dense set $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, vanishes identically by continuity; so $G_j$ is constant on the domain $\mathbb C$ by [F6]. Evaluating at $-\omega_j/2$, a point of $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ because $\omega_j$ is a primitive basis vector, and using the oddness of step 4.3 gives $G_j(-\omega_j/2)=\zeta(\omega_j/2)-\zeta(-\omega_j/2)=2\zeta(\omega_j/2)=\eta_j$. Therefore $\zeta(z+\omega_j)=\zeta(z)+\eta_j$ for all $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, with poles matched. [F1, F4, F6, given, step 4.1, step 4.2, step 4.3]

5.4 (A translated parallelogram with one pole.) Put $\tau:=\omega_2/\omega_1$, which has $\operatorname{Im}\tau>0$ by [F1], and define the closed parallelogram $T:=\{s+t\tau:0\le s,t\le1\}$ of the boundary lemma with $\alpha(y):=(\operatorname{Re}\tau/\operatorname{Im}\tau)y$ and $\beta(y):=\alpha(y)+1$ on $[0,\operatorname{Im}\tau]$; define also $a:=-\tfrac12(\omega_1+\omega_2)$, the orientation-preserving similarity $\sigma(w):=a+\omega_1w$, and $P:=\sigma(T)=\{a+s\omega_1+t\omega_2:0\le s,t\le1\}$ with interior $P^\circ$. The boundary contour $\gamma_T$ of $T$ is the concatenation of the bottom segment, the graph of $\beta$ with $y$ increasing, the top segment and the graph of $\alpha$ with $y$ decreasing; its image under $\sigma$ traverses the four sides of $P$ as the paths $\gamma_1(t):=a+t\omega_1$, $\gamma_2(t):=a+\omega_1+t\omega_2$, $\gamma_3(t):=a+\omega_1+\omega_2-t\omega_1$, $\gamma_4(t):=a+\omega_2-t\omega_2$ for $t\in[0,1]$, up to strictly increasing reparametrizations of the two graph pieces. By [F10] applied to $\sigma$, the index of the contour $\Gamma:=\sigma\circ\gamma_T=\gamma_1*\gamma_2*\gamma_3*\gamma_4$ (with the reparametrizations of [F11]) is $n(\Gamma,q)=1$ for $q\in P^\circ$ and $0$ for $q\notin P$. A lattice point $\lambda=m\omega_1+n\omega_2$ lies in $P$ iff $s=m+\tfrac12\in[0,1]$ and $t=n+\tfrac12\in[0,1]$, that is iff $m=n=0$; hence $0\in P^\circ$ is the only lattice point in $P$ and every other lattice point lies outside $P$, with $n(\Gamma,\lambda)=0$ for $\lambda\ne0$. Consequently $\Gamma^\ast$ avoids $\Lambda$; since $\zeta$ is holomorphic on $\Omega\setminus\{0\}=\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ and $0$ is a pole of $\zeta$ by step 4.1, $\zeta$ is meromorphic on the domain $\Omega:=\mathbb C\setminus(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})$ of step 2.2 with pole set $\{0\}$, and $\Gamma$ is admissible for the residue theorem in $\Omega$ because $n(\Gamma,p)=0$ for every $p\notin\Omega$ by the preceding index computation. [F1, F8, F10, F11, given, step 4.1, step 2.2]

6.1 ($P'=PE$ and $\sigma'/\sigma=\zeta$.) For $z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ the finite product rule of [F6] and the identity $F_\omega'(z)/F_\omega(z)=g_\omega(z)$, obtained by differentiating the displayed factor with [F5] and [F6], give $Q_n'(z)=Q_n(z)S_n(z)$ with $S_n=\sum_{\omega\in F_n}g_\omega$, and $S_n\to E$ uniformly on compact subsets of $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ by step 3.1; on a compact $K\subseteq\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ the factors $Q_n$ are uniformly bounded by $e^{M_K}$ as in step 4.4, so $Q_n'\to PE$ uniformly on $K$. The convergence $Q_n\to P$ is locally uniform on the open set $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, so [F12] gives $P'=\lim_nQ_n'=PE$ there. Since $P$ is zero-free on $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ by step 5.1, the product and reciprocal rules of [F6] applied to $\sigma=zP$ give $$\frac{\sigma'(z)}{\sigma(z)}=\frac1z+\frac{P'(z)}{P(z)}=\frac1z+E(z)=\zeta(z)\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda).$$ [F3, F6, F12, given, step 3.1, step 4.4, step 5.1]

6.2 (The four sides and the Legendre relation.) With $\Omega$ as in step 5.4, the residue theorem [F9] applied to $\zeta$, whose only pole in $\Omega$ is $0$ with $n(\Gamma,0)=1$ and residue $1$ by step 4.1, gives $\int_\Gamma\zeta(z)\,dz=2\pi i$. On the other hand the parametric formula and additivity of [F11] give $\int_\Gamma\zeta=\sum_{k=1}^4\int_{\gamma_k}\zeta$, and $\gamma_3$ is the reversal of the translated path $\delta_1(t):=\gamma_1(t)+\omega_2$ while $\gamma_4$ is the reversal of $\delta_2(t):=\gamma_2(t)-\omega_1$. By step 5.3, $\zeta(\delta_1(t))=\zeta(\gamma_1(t))+\eta_2$ and, using $\zeta(w-\omega_1)=\zeta(w)-\eta_1$ for $w=\gamma_2(t)\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$, also $\zeta(\delta_2(t))=\zeta(\gamma_2(t))-\eta_1$; the parametric formula applied to each translated path (with derivative $\omega_1$, respectively $\omega_2$) yields $\int_{\delta_1}\zeta=\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\eta_2\omega_1$ and $\int_{\delta_2}\zeta=\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\eta_1\omega_2$. Since reversal negates integrals, $$\int_\Gamma\zeta=\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\Bigl(\int_{\gamma_1}\zeta+\eta_2\omega_1\Bigr)-\Bigl(\int_{\gamma_2}\zeta-\eta_1\omega_2\Bigr)=\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1 .$$ Comparing with $\int_\Gamma\zeta=2\pi i$ gives $\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i$. [F9, F11, given, step 4.1, step 5.3, step 5.4]

7.1 (Quasi-periodicity of $\sigma$.) Fix $j$ and put $H_j(z):=\sigma(z+\omega_j)/\sigma(z)$, meromorphic on $\mathbb C$; by step 5.1 the only zeros of $\sigma$ are the lattice points, all simple, and $\sigma(z)=0$ exactly when $z\in\Lambda=\Lambda-\omega_j$, so $\sigma(z+\omega_j)$ has exactly the same simple zeros. At $\lambda\in\Lambda$ write $\sigma(z)=(z-\lambda)u(z)$ and $\sigma(z+\omega_j)=(z-\lambda)v(z)$ with $u,v$ holomorphic and nonzero at $\lambda$ ([F7] applied to the simple zeros); then $H_j=v/u$ is holomorphic near $\lambda$ with $H_j(\lambda)=v(\lambda)/u(\lambda)\ne0$. On $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ both $\sigma$ and $\sigma(\cdot+\omega_j)$ are zero-free, so $H_j$ is holomorphic and zero-free there as well; hence $H_j$ is entire and zero-free. The quotient rule of [F6] together with step 6.1 gives $$\frac{H_j'(z)}{H_j(z)}=\frac{\sigma'(z+\omega_j)}{\sigma(z+\omega_j)}-\frac{\sigma'(z)}{\sigma(z)}=\zeta(z+\omega_j)-\zeta(z)=\eta_j\qquad(z\in\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda),$$ an identity between entire functions, valid on the dense set $\mathbb C\setminus\Lambda$ by step 5.3 and hence everywhere by continuity. It follows that $\bigl(H_j(z)e^{-\eta_jz}\bigr)'=e^{-\eta_jz}\bigl(H_j'(z)-\eta_jH_j(z)\bigr)=0$ on $\mathbb C$ by [F5] and [F6], so $H_j(z)=C_je^{\eta_jz}$ for a constant $C_j$ by [F6]. Evaluating at $-\omega_j/2$ and using the oddness of $\sigma$ from step 5.2 and $\sigma(\omega_j/2)\ne0$: $$-1=\frac{\sigma(\omega_j/2)}{\sigma(-\omega_j/2)}=H_j(-\omega_j/2)=C_je^{-\eta_j\omega_j/2},\qquad\text{so}\qquad C_j=-e^{\eta_j\omega_j/2},$$ and therefore $\sigma(z+\omega_j)=-e^{\eta_j(z+\omega_j/2)}\sigma(z)$ for all $z\in\mathbb C$; both sides vanish at lattice points. [F5, F6, F7, given, step 5.1, step 5.2, step 6.1, step 5.3]

8.1 (Assembly.) Step 3.1 gives the normal convergence of the $\zeta$-series and step 4.1 that $\zeta$ is meromorphic with exactly the simple lattice poles of residue $1$; step 4.2 gives $\zeta'=-\wp$ and step 4.3 the oddness; step 4.4 gives the normal convergence of the product and that $\sigma$ is entire, step 5.1 the simple lattice zeros, step 6.1 the identities $\sigma'(0)=1$ and $\sigma'/\sigma=\zeta$, and step 5.2 the oddness of $\sigma$. Steps 5.3 and 7.1 prove the two quasi-period laws for $j=1,2$, and step 6.2 proves the Legendre relation $\eta_1\omega_2-\eta_2\omega_1=2\pi i$. This proves all three clauses. ∎

## Remarks

The two corrections in the summands of $\zeta$ and in the exponential factors of $\sigma$ are exactly what makes the derivative series of $\zeta$ equal to the series of $-\wp$: the derivative of $1/(z-\omega)+1/\omega+z/\omega^2$ is $-h_\omega$, so no divergent series $\sum\omega^{-2}$ ever appears, and likewise $F_\omega'/F_\omega=g_\omega$. The Legendre relation is the residue theorem applied to the translated parallelogram $P$ that contains the single pole $0$: the two pairs of opposite sides contribute $-\eta_2\omega_1$ and $\eta_1\omega_2$, and the orientation of the basis makes $P$ the positively oriented boundary, so the sign is fixed by $\operatorname{Im}(\omega_2/\omega_1)>0$. For $\omega=m\omega_1+n\omega_2$ define $\eta_\omega:=m\eta_1+n\eta_2$. Iteration of the zeta law in clause (3) gives $\zeta(z+\omega)=\zeta(z)+\eta_\omega$. Iterating the two sigma laws, first by $m\omega_1$ and then by $n\omega_2$, gives the exponent $$m\eta_1\left(z+\frac{m\omega_1}{2}\right)+n\eta_2\left(z+m\omega_1+\frac{n\omega_2}{2}\right).$$ By the Legendre relation of clause (3), this differs from $\eta_\omega(z+\omega/2)$ by $-mn\pi i$. Thus the general law is $$\sigma(z+\omega)=(-1)^{m+n+mn}\exp\!\left(\eta_\omega\left(z+\frac{\omega}{2}\right)\right)\sigma(z).$$ If $\omega$ is primitive, $\gcd(m,n)=1$, so $m,n$ are not both even and $m+n+mn$ is odd; the sign is then $-1$. Also $\omega/2\notin\Lambda$, and applying the extended zeta law at $z=-\omega/2$ with oddness gives $\eta_\omega=2\zeta(\omega/2)$. This recovers the primitive-period form in [[def-weierstrass-zeta-and-sigma-functions]]; for nonprimitive periods the parity sign above is required. The proof selects nothing beyond finite subsets of the lattice and the finitely many lattice points of step 5.4; in particular no countable or dependent choice is invoked.
