## Statement

Let $k$ be a commutative field and let $H\trianglelefteq G$ be a normal subgroup with torsion-free abelian quotient $G/H$. If $k[H]$ has no zero divisors, then $k[G]$ has no zero divisors. The group extension need not split; neither finite generation of $G/H$ nor an order on all of $G$ is assumed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field $k$, normal subgroup $H\trianglelefteq G$, and torsion-free abelian quotient $A=G/H$.

[F1] Group-ring elements have unique finite expansions, multiplication is $k$-bilinear with $[g][h]=[gh]$, and each $[g]$ is a unit ([[def-group-ring]], [[thm-group-ring-is-a-unital-algebra-with-basis-g]]).

[F2] Every finitely generated torsion-free abelian group is isomorphic to $\mathbb Z^r$ for some finite $r$ ([[lem-finitely-generated-torsion-free-abelian-freeness-without-choice]]).

[A1] The ring $k[H]$ has no zero divisors.

## Proof

**Given:** Two nonzero elements $f,g\in k[G]$ and [A1].

1.1 Let $B$ be the subgroup of $A$ generated by the images of the finite supports of $f$ and $g$. It is finitely generated, torsion-free and abelian, so [F2] identifies it with $\mathbb Z^r$. Transport the lexicographic order from $\mathbb Z^r$ to $B$. This is a total order invariant under addition. No order on the whole quotient or on $G$ is needed. Let $X,Y\subseteq B$ be the finite quotient supports of $f,g$. Choose lifts $t_x\in G$ for the finitely many elements of $X\cup Y\cup(X+Y)$, using the same lift for any repeated element. By grouping terms in each coset, [F1] gives unique expressions $f=\sum_{x\in X}r_xt_x$ and $g=\sum_{y\in Y}s_yt_y$ with $0\ne r_x,s_y\in k[H]$. Distinct cosets have disjoint group bases, so these grouped coefficients are nonzero. [F1, F2, given, construct]

2.1 For these lifts put $\alpha_x(s)=t_xst_x^{-1}$ on $k[H]$ and $h_{x,y}=t_xt_yt_{x+y}^{-1}\in H$. Normality makes $\alpha_x$ a ring automorphism, with inverse conjugation by $t_x^{-1}$. Direct multiplication gives $(r_xt_x)(s_yt_y)=r_x\alpha_x(s_y)h_{x,y}t_{x+y}$. This is the crossed-product multiplication on the finite supports. The factor $h_{x,y}$ is retained and is a unit; it is not assumed to be $1$. Its compatibility with iterated multiplication is inherited from associativity in $G$. Thus the calculation applies to a nonsplit extension. [F1, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Let $x_0=\max X$ and $y_0=\max Y$. Every other pair $(x,y)$ satisfies $x+y<x_0+y_0$: if $x<x_0$, then $x+y\le x+y_0<x_0+y_0$, and if $x=x_0$ but $y<y_0$, addition invariance gives the same strict inequality. Hence the coefficient of $t_{x_0+y_0}$ in $fg$ is the single term $r_{x_0}\alpha_{x_0}(s_{y_0})h_{x_0,y_0}$. It is nonzero by [A1], invertibility of $h_{x_0,y_0}$ and injectivity of $\alpha_{x_0}$. By disjointness of coset bases, $fg\ne0$. Since $f,g$ were arbitrary, $k[G]$ has no zero divisors. Only finitely many lifts were chosen, so the proof introduces no choice axiom. [A1, F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra] ∎
