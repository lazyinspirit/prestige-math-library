## Example

Construct the common free open, coordinate Koszul zero locus, hyperbolic normal complex and Gysin identity; calculate double Euler comparison zero via nowhere-zero tautological section.

## Setup


For a rank-one check take $V_1=V_2=\mathbb C$. Then $\mathbb P(V_1\oplus V_2)=\mathbb P^1$ with coordinates $[x:y]$. The zero-component loci are $J_1=[1:0]$ and $J_2=[0:1]$; in a simple old-object model the common free locus is all of $\mathbb P^1$. The sections are $y,x\in H^0(\mathbb P^1,\mathcal O(1))$, each with a regular coordinate zero. For every point $[x:y]$, at least one component is nonzero. If a scalar automorphism of the old one-dimensional object and a scalar automorphism of the source line preserve this framing, their scalars must agree; quotienting by the source-line scalar therefore leaves only the identity. Hence the common free locus is all of $\mathbb P^1$. The normal obstruction pair is ordered $\mathcal O(1)^\vee\oplus\mathcal O(1)[2]$.

## Verification

1.1 Near $J_1$ in the chart $x\ne0$, the zero section $y$ is the coordinate $y/x$ in a trivialization of $\mathcal O(1)$, so its zero ideal is generated by a nonzerodivisor and its normal is $\mathcal O(1)|_{J_1}$. The chart $y\ne0$ gives the same result for $x$ at $J_2$. [given, F1, algebra]

2.1 The two normal halves pair by evaluation. Their determinant $\det(N^\vee)\otimes\det(N)$ has the canonical trivialization in the displayed order, so the zero-section Gysin uses the hyperbolic orientation required by [F2]. [given, F2, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Since $c_1(\mathcal O(1))$ has degree one, a single cap gives $c_1(\mathcal O(1))\cap[\mathbb P^1]=[J_i]$. The double cap is $c_1(\mathcal O(1))^2\cap[\mathbb P^1]=0$ because $H^4(\mathbb P^1)=0$. Equivalently, each own component section is nowhere zero when restricted to its pair locus, so that top Chern class restricts to zero. [given, F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 This finite model checks regular zero loci, orientation, and the supported single-cap formula. The zero double cap is not injective and says nothing about mixed fixed properness. [given, F2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
