## Statement

Let $K$ be a field ([[def-field]]). Then the only ideals of $K$ are the zero
ideal $(0)$ and $K$ itself
([[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]],
[[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]); consequently every ideal of $K$ is
finitely generated, and $K$ is a Noetherian ring
([[def-noetherian-ring]], [[def-noetherian-module]]). No choice principle is used.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $K$ and an ideal $I\subseteq K$.

[F1] [[def-field]]: in a field every nonzero element $a$ has a multiplicative inverse $a^{-1}$ with $a\,a^{-1}=1$, and $1\ne0$.

[F2] [[def-left-right-and-two-sided-ideal]]: an ideal $I\subseteq K$ is an additive subgroup closed under multiplication by elements of $K$, so $ra\in I$ for all $r\in K$, $a\in I$; hence $I=K$ as soon as $1\in I$.

[F3] [[def-generated-and-principal-ideals]]: for $a\in K$ the ideal $(a)$ is the intersection of all ideals containing $a$; in particular $(0)=\{0\}$ is generated by $0$ and $K=(1)$ is generated by $1$, so both are generated by a single element.

[F4] [[def-noetherian-ring]] and [[def-noetherian-module]]: a ring is Noetherian when its left regular module is Noetherian, meaning that every left submodule is finitely generated. For the commutative field $K$, these submodules are precisely its ideals.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 A nonzero ideal is everything: if $I\ne(0)$ choose $a\in I$ with $a\ne0$; by [F1] $a$ is invertible with inverse $a^{-1}\in K$, and since $I$ is closed under multiplication by elements of $K$, $1=a^{-1}a\in I$ by [F2]; then $x=x\cdot1\in I$ for every $x\in K$ by [F2] again, so $I=K$. [F1, F2, given]

2.1 The ideal list: by step 1.1 every ideal of $K$ is either $(0)$ or $K$; the zero ideal is generated by the single element $0$ and $K=(1)$ is generated by the single element $1$, so every ideal of $K$ is finitely generated. [step 1.1, F3]

3.1 Conclusion: by step 2.1 every ideal of $K$ is finitely generated, so [F4] makes $K$ a Noetherian ring. The argument used only the field axioms, the ideal axioms and the two-element list of ideals, so it invokes no choice principle. [step 2.1, F4, given] ∎
