## Statement

Let $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ be a number field with $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, and let $F$ be the monic minimal polynomial of $\alpha$. For a rational prime $p$, factor the image $\bar F$ of $F$ in $\mathbb F_p[t]$ as $\prod_i g_i^{a_i}$ into distinct monic irreducibles. Let $\widetilde g_i\in\mathbb Z[t]$ be the coefficientwise lift of $g_i$ with coefficients in $\{0,\ldots,p-1\}$. Then

$$p\mathcal O_K=\prod_i P_i^{a_i},\qquad P_i=(p,\,\widetilde g_i(\alpha)),$$

The ideal $(p,\widetilde g_i(\alpha))$ is independent of the integer lift, since two lifts differ by a polynomial in $p\mathbb Z[t]$. The $P_i$ are distinct primes of $\mathcal O_K$ with residue degrees $\deg g_i$. This proof uses no Axiom of Choice.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A number field $K=\mathbb Q(\alpha)$ with $\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$, its monic minimal polynomial $F\in\mathbb Z[t]$, a rational prime $p$, and a factorisation $\bar F=\prod_{i=1}^{k}g_i^{a_i}$ in $\mathbb F_p[t]$ into distinct monic irreducibles $g_i$, where $d_i:=\deg g_i$ and $a_i\ge1$, and their coefficientwise integer lifts $\widetilde g_i$ as in the Statement.

[F1] Evaluation at $\alpha$ identifies $\mathbb Z[t]/(F)$ with $\mathbb Z[\alpha]=\mathcal O_K$ ([[thm-evaluation-kernel-and-minimal-polynomial]], [[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-rings]], [[def-quotient-ring]]). Reducing this presentation modulo $p$ gives $\mathcal O_K/p\mathcal O_K\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/(\bar F)$.

[F2] Since $p$ is prime, $\mathbb F_p$ is a field ([[thm-z-mod-p-is-a-field]]). The powers $(g_i^{a_i})$ are pairwise comaximal in $\mathbb F_p[t]$, so the Chinese remainder theorem gives $$\mathbb F_p[t]/(\bar F)\cong\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb F_p[t]/(g_i^{a_i}).$$ Each factor has unique prime ideal generated by the image of $g_i$, and its residue field is $\mathbb F_p[t]/(g_i)$ ([[thm-bezout-identity-for-polynomials]], [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]], [[thm-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-ufd]]).

[F3] Every nonzero integral ideal of a number field's ring of integers has a unique finite factorisation into powers of distinct nonzero prime ideals; the finite construction uses no Choice ([[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]]).

[F4] If $P$ is a prime ideal, $\mathcal O_{K,P}$ is the localisation at the multiplicative set $\mathcal O_K\setminus P$, and its maximal ideal is $P\mathcal O_{K,P}$ ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]], [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]]). Localisation commutes with quotient rings ([[thm-localisation-commutes-with-quotients]]).

[F5] A prime $P\subseteq\mathcal O_K$ lies above $(p)$ when $P\cap\mathbb Z=(p)$, and its residue degree is $[\mathcal O_K/P:\mathbb F_p]$ ([[def-prime-above-and-residue-degree]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $A:=\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\alpha]$. By [F1], $$A/pA\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/(\bar F).$$ [F1, given]

1.2 Since $pA\ne0$, apply [F3] to write $$pA=\prod_{j=1}^{r}Q_j^{e_j}$$ with distinct nonzero primes $Q_j$ and positive exponents $e_j$. [F3, given]

2.1 Under this isomorphism, [F2] decomposes $A/pA$ as the product of the local rings $B_i:=\mathbb F_p[t]/(g_i^{a_i})$. The unique prime of $B_i$ is generated by $g_i$ and has residue field $\mathbb F_p[t]/(g_i)$; therefore the primes of $A$ containing $pA$ are exactly the distinct inverse images $P_i=(p,\widetilde g_i(\alpha))$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 It follows that $A/P_i\cong\mathbb F_p[t]/(g_i)$, a field of degree $d_i$ over $\mathbb F_p$. Thus each $P_i$ is a nonzero prime above $(p)$ with residue degree $d_i$ by [F5]. [F5, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 The finite ring $A/pA$ is the product in [F2], so every prime containing $pA$ is maximal. Each $Q_j$ contains $pA$ and hence equals one of the $P_i$ by step 2.1. Conversely, each $P_i$ contains $pA=\prod_jQ_j^{e_j}$; its primality implies $Q_j\subseteq P_i$ for some $j$, and maximality makes $Q_j=P_i$. Hence the list $Q_1,\ldots,Q_r$ is exactly the list $P_1,\ldots,P_k$, with one exponent $e_i$ attached to each $P_i$. [step 2.1, step 1.2]

4.1 Fix $i$ and put $S:=A_{P_i}$ and $\mathfrak m:=P_iS$. Each other $P_j$ contains an element outside $P_i$, which becomes a unit, so $$pS=\mathfrak m^{e_i}.$$ By [F4], $S$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$; it is a domain because it is a localisation of the domain $A$. Also $\mathfrak m=(p,\widetilde g_i(\alpha))S$, so each power of $\mathfrak m$ is finitely generated by the monomials in these two generators. [F4, step 2.1, step 3.2]

5.1 The ideal $J:=\mathfrak m^{e_i-1}$ is nonzero: it contains $p^{e_i-1}\ne0$. If $J\subseteq\mathfrak m^{e_i}$, then $J=\mathfrak mJ$. Among finite generating lists of $J$, take one of minimum length $n\ge1$, say $v_1,\ldots,v_n$. The equality $J=\mathfrak mJ$ gives $v_n=\sum_{j=1}^n c_jv_j$ with $c_j\in\mathfrak m$. Since $1-c_n$ is a unit in the local ring $S$, this expresses $v_n$ in terms of the first $n-1$ generators, contradicting minimality. Hence $\mathfrak m^{e_i-1}\not\subseteq\mathfrak m^{e_i}$. In $S/pS=S/\mathfrak m^{e_i}$ the maximal ideal therefore has nilpotency index exactly $e_i$, including $e_i=1$. [F4, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 By [F1] and [F4], $$A_{P_i}/pA_{P_i}\cong\mathbb F_p[t]_{(g_i)}/(\bar F).$$ Writing $\bar F=g_i^{a_i}u_i$ with $u_i=\prod_{j\ne i}g_j^{a_j}$, each factor of $u_i$ is a unit in $\mathbb F_p[t]_{(g_i)}$. Thus the displayed local ring is $\mathbb F_p[t]_{(g_i)}/(g_i^{a_i})$. Its maximal ideal is generated by $g_i$ and has nilpotency index exactly $a_i$: its $a_i$-th power vanishes, while $g_i^{a_i-1}\notin(g_i^{a_i})$, since cancellation in the polynomial localisation domain would otherwise make the nonunit $g_i$ a unit. Comparing its nilpotency index with step 5.1 gives $e_i=a_i$. [F1, F2, F4, step 5.1]

7.1 Substituting $e_i=a_i$ into the factorisation of step 1.2 yields $$p\mathcal O_K=\prod_{i=1}^{k}P_i^{a_i},\qquad P_i=(p,\widetilde g_i(\alpha)).$$ The residue degrees are those proved in step 3.1, and $\sum_i a_i d_i=\deg\bar F$. The polynomial factorisation and its CRT decomposition are finite; the only ideal-factorisation input [F3] explicitly uses finite least-coded choices and no Choice. [F3, step 3.1, step 1.2, step 6.1] ∎

## Remarks

- **Repeated factors are retained.** The multiplicities $a_i$ are recovered by localising the polynomial quotient at $(g_i)$ and comparing its nilpotency index with the local exponent in the ideal factorisation.
- **Supplier route.** This proof uses the published choice-free ideal-factorisation theorem [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]] for existence of the ideal factorisation. The exact local nilpotency index is proved here using the explicit finite generators and a minimal generating list. It therefore no longer claims to avoid general ideal-factorisation theory.
- **The monogenic hypothesis is essential.** It identifies $\mathcal O_K$ with the explicit quotient $\mathbb Z[t]/(F)$; for a non-monogenic order, reduction of a minimal polynomial does not by itself describe the primes of $\mathcal O_K$.
