## Statement

Let $T>0$ and let $t\mapsto\mu_t$ be a narrowly continuous curve of Borel probability measures on $\mathbb R^d$, where narrow continuity means convergence against every bounded continuous real test function. For a product-Borel set $E\subseteq[0,T]\times\mathbb R^d$, write $E_t=\{x:(t,x)\in E\}$ and define $M(E)=\int_0^T\mu_t(E_t)dt$. Then $t\mapsto\mu_t$ is a probability kernel, $M$ is a finite Borel measure of total mass $T$, and for every nonnegative product-Borel $f$, $\int f\,dM=\int_0^T\int f(t,x)d\mu_t(x)dt$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $T>0$ and a narrowly continuous curve $(\mu_t)_{t\in[0,T]}$ of Borel probability measures on $\mathbb R^d$.

[F1] A Borel probability law on a Polish space is a countably additive measure on its Borel sigma-algebra with total mass one. [[def-borel-probability-law-on-a-polish-space]]

[F2] Narrow convergence of Borel probabilities implies the open-set lower bound $\mu(G)\le\liminf_n\mu_n(G)$ for every open $G$. [[thm-portmanteau-theorem]]

[F3] A lambda-system containing a pi-system contains the sigma-algebra it generates. [[thm-dynkin-pi-lambda]]

[F4] A probability kernel has a probability measure in every section and measurable evaluations in its source variable. [[def-measure-kernel-and-probability-kernel]]

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra on a product of second-countable spaces equals the product sigma-algebra, which is generated by measurable rectangles. [[lem-borel-product-of-second-countable-spaces]] [[def-product-sigma-algebra-and-finite-product-sigma-algebras]]

[F6] Sections of product-measurable sets are measurable. [[thm-sections-of-product-measurable-sets-are-measurable]]

[F7] For a probability kernel $K$ and a nonnegative product-measurable function $f$, $s\mapsto\int f(s,t)K(s,dt)$ is measurable. [[thm-measurability-of-integration-against-a-kernel]]

[F8] Increasing limits pass through nonnegative Lebesgue integrals. [[thm-monotone-convergence-for-the-integral]]

[F9] Every nonnegative measurable function has an increasing simple approximation; nonnegative simple integrals are finite sums of their level-set measures; increasing limits of measurable functions are measurable. [[thm-increasing-simple-approximation-of-a-nonnegative-measurable-function]] [[def-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]] [[def-integral-of-a-nonnegative-simple-function]] [[cor-additivity-of-the-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]] [[prop-closure-properties-of-measurable-functions-used-by-the-integral]]

[F10] A measure is zero on the empty set and countably additive on disjoint measurable unions. [[def-measure]]


## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $G\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ be open and let $t_n\to t$. Narrow continuity gives $\mu_{t_n}\Rightarrow\mu_t$, so [F2] gives $\mu_t(G)\le\liminf_n\mu_{t_n}(G)$. Thus $t\mapsto\mu_t(G)$ is lower semicontinuous and hence Borel measurable. [given, F2]

2.1 Let $\mathcal D$ be the Borel sets $A\subseteq\mathbb R^d$ for which $t\mapsto\mu_t(A)$ is measurable. It contains the whole space because each section is a probability. If $A\in\mathcal D$, then $\mu_t(A^c)=1-\mu_t(A)$, so $A^c\in\mathcal D$. For pairwise disjoint $A_j\in\mathcal D$, countable additivity gives $\mu_t(\bigcup_jA_j)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^N\mu_t(A_j)$, a measurable increasing limit. Hence $\mathcal D$ is a lambda-system. The open sets form a pi-system generating the Borel sigma-algebra and belong to $\mathcal D$ by step 1.1, so [F3] and [F9] give $\mathcal D=\mathcal B(\mathbb R^d)$. Together with [F4], this proves that $t\mapsto\mu_t$ is a probability kernel. [given, F1, F3, F4, F9, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F5], a product-Borel set $E$ is measurable for $\mathcal B([0,T])\otimes\mathcal B(\mathbb R^d)$. Its sections $E_t$ are Borel by [F6]. Since $t\mapsto\mu_t$ is a probability kernel by step 2.1, [F7] applied to $\mathbf1_E$ shows that $t\mapsto\mu_t(E_t)$ is measurable. Thus $M(E)=\int_0^T\mu_t(E_t)dt$ is defined; $M(\varnothing)=0$ and $M([0,T]\times\mathbb R^d)=\int_0^T1dt=T$. [F5, F6, F7, step 2.1]

4.1 If $E_j$ are disjoint product-Borel sets, their sections are disjoint and $\mu_t((\bigcup_jE_j)_t)=\sum_j\mu_t((E_j)_t)$. Integrating the increasing partial sums and using [F8] gives $M(\bigcup_jE_j)=\sum_jM(E_j)$; with step 2.1 this makes $M$ a finite Borel measure. For an indicator, the iterated-integral formula is the definition of $M$; finite nonnegative simple combinations give it for simple functions. For arbitrary nonnegative product-Borel $f$, use [F9] to choose $s_n\uparrow f$ and apply [F8] to both $M$ and the time-section integrals. This proves $\int f\,dM=\int_0^T\int f(t,x)d\mu_t(x)dt$. [F1, F8, F9, F10, step 3.1] ∎
