## Statement refuted

Let $A_5$ be the alternating group on the five letters $0,1,2,3,4$ and let
$p=5$ ([[def-alternating-group]],
[[def-finite-symmetric-group-and-permutation-notation]]). Then:

1. $|A_5|=60$ and every Sylow $5$-subgroup $P$ of $A_5$ is cyclic of order $5$
   ([[def-sylow-p-subgroup]], [[cor-prime-order-group-is-cyclic]]);
2. $A_5$ has **no** normal $5$-complement
   ([[def-normal-p-complement-and-p-nilpotent-group]]);
3. for such a Sylow $P$ the automizer is
   $N_{A_5}(P)/C_{A_5}(P)\cong C_2$, so it is not a $5$-group and the automizer
   criterion of [[cor-frobenius-automizer-criterion-for-p-nilpotence]] fails.

Thus a cyclic Sylow $p$-subgroup does not by itself imply the existence of a
normal $p$-complement: the defect is detected by the automizer, not by the
isomorphism type of the Sylow subgroup.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The alternating group $A_5$ and the prime $p=5$.

[F1] $A_5$ has order $60$: indeed $|S_5|=120$ and $2|A_5|=5!$ ([[cor-alternating-group-is-normal-and-has-half-the-elements]], [[def-alternating-group]]).

[F2] $A_5$ is simple, and $A_5\ne\{1\}$ because $|A_5|=60$ ([[thm-alternating-group-is-simple-for-n-at-least-five]], [F1]).

[F3] Sylow counting: $n_5(A_5)\equiv1\pmod5$ and $n_5(A_5)\mid 12$, so $n_5(A_5)\in\{1,6\}$ because the divisors of $12$ are $1,2,3,4,6,12$; and if $n_5(A_5)=1$ then the unique Sylow $5$-subgroup is fixed by conjugation by every element of $A_5$, hence is normal, of order $5$, so nontrivial and proper, contradicting [F2]; therefore $n_5(A_5)=6$ and, whenever $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_5(A_5)$, $[A_5:N_{A_5}(P)]=6$ so $|N_{A_5}(P)|=10$ ([[thm-sylow-third-theorem]], [[thm-sylow-second-theorem]], [[thm-sylow-number-as-normalizer-index]], [[def-number-of-sylow-p-subgroups]], [[def-normal-subgroup]], [[thm-lagrange]], [F1], [F2]).

[F4] Let $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_5(A_5)$. Then $|P|=5$ by [F3] and [F1], so $P$ is cyclic ([[cor-prime-order-group-is-cyclic]]), and in particular abelian; a subgroup of order $5$ is generated by any of its nonidentity elements ([[lem-cyclic-subgroup-is-the-set-of-powers]], [[def-generated-subgroup]], [[def-order-in-a-group]], [[def-subgroup]], [[lem-subgroup-criterion]]).

[F5] Automorphism group of a cyclic group of prime order: $\operatorname{Aut}(P)\cong(\mathbb Z/5\mathbb Z)^{\times}$ has order $4$ ([[thm-automorphisms-of-a-finite-cyclic-group]], [[def-group-isomorphism-and-automorphism]]).

[F6] Conjugation action of the normalizer: for $P\le G$, the map $\alpha:N_G(P)\to\operatorname{Aut}(P)$, $\alpha(n)(q)=nqn^{-1}$, is a group homomorphism, because conjugation is an automorphism of $P$ for $n\in N_G(P)$ and composition of conjugations is conjugation by the product; its kernel is $\{n\in N_G(P):nqn^{-1}=q$ for all $q\in P\}=N_G(P)\cap C_G(P)=C_G(P)$; by the first isomorphism theorem $N_G(P)/C_G(P)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$ ([[def-normalizer-of-a-subgroup]], [[def-centralizer-of-a-subgroup]], [[lem-centralizers-and-normalizers-are-subgroups]], [[thm-conjugation-is-an-automorphism]], [[def-group-homomorphism]], [[thm-image-subgroup-and-kernel-normal]], [[thm-first-isomorphism-theorem-groups]], [[def-quotient-group]], [[cor-order-of-a-quotient-group]], [[thm-lagrange]], [[lem-group-inverse-laws]]).

[F7] Cycle signs and conjugations: a cycle of length $k$ has sign $(-1)^{k-1}$, and the sign is a homomorphism, so a $5$-cycle and a product of two transpositions are even and lie in $A_5=\ker(\operatorname{sgn})$; and for a cycle $\sigma=(a_0\,a_1\,a_2\,a_3\,a_4)$ and a permutation $g$ one has $g\sigma g^{-1}=(g(a_0)\,g(a_1)\,g(a_2)\,g(a_3)\,g(a_4))$ ([[cor-sign-from-disjoint-cycle-structure]], [[thm-sign-is-a-homomorphism]], [[def-alternating-group]], [[lem-conjugating-a-cycle-relabels-its-entries]]).

[F8] Automizer criterion: for a finite group $G$, a prime $p$ and $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_p(G)$, the group $G$ has a normal $p$-complement if and only if $N_G(Q)/C_G(Q)$ is a $p$-group for every $Q$ with $1\ne Q\le P$ ([[cor-frobenius-automizer-criterion-for-p-nilpotence]], [[def-normal-p-complement-and-p-nilpotent-group]]).



## Counterexample

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F1] and [F3], $|A_5|=60=5\cdot12$ and the exact power of $5$ dividing $|A_5|$ is $5$; so a Sylow $5$-subgroup has order $5$, and a group of order $5$ is cyclic by [F4]. Moreover $n_5(A_5)=6$. This is assertion 1. [F1, F3, F4]

2.1 Put $\sigma:=(0\,1\,2\,3\,4)$ and $P:=\langle\sigma\rangle\le A_5$. By [F7] the $5$-cycle $\sigma$ is even, so $\sigma\in A_5$; $P$ has order $5$ because $\sigma$ has order $5$; hence $P\in\operatorname{Syl}_5(A_5)$ by step 1.1, and $P=\{\operatorname{id},\sigma,\sigma^{2},\sigma^{3},\sigma^{4}\}$ is abelian. [F4, F7, step 1.1]

3.1 Put $t:=(1\,4)(2\,3)$. By [F7] the sign of each transposition is $-1$, so $t$ has sign $(-1)^{2}=1$ and $t\in A_5$. By the conjugation formula of [F7], $t\sigma t^{-1}=(t(0)\,t(1)\,t(2)\,t(3)\,t(4))=(0\,4\,3\,2\,1)=\sigma^{-1}$, which lies in $P$; hence $t\in N_{A_5}(P)$. [F7, step 2.1]

4.1 Moreover $t\notin C_{A_5}(P)$: if $t$ centralized $P$ then in particular $t\sigma t^{-1}=\sigma$, but step 3.1 gives $t\sigma t^{-1}=\sigma^{-1}$, and $\sigma^{-1}\ne\sigma$ because $\sigma$ has order $5\ne2$ so $\sigma^{2}\ne\operatorname{id}$, whereas $\sigma^{-1}=\sigma$ would give $\sigma^{2}=\operatorname{id}$. Hence $N_{A_5}(P)\ne C_{A_5}(P)$. [F4, step 3.1]

5.1 By [F6] the quotient $N_{A_5}(P)/C_{A_5}(P)$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $\operatorname{Aut}(P)$, so its order divides $4$ by [F5]; it also divides $|N_{A_5}(P)|=10$ by [F3] and [F6]. Since it is not $1$ by step 4.1, its order divides $\gcd(4,10)=2$ and is $>1$; hence $|N_{A_5}(P)/C_{A_5}(P)|=2$, and this group is cyclic of order $2$, that is $N_{A_5}(P)/C_{A_5}(P)\cong C_2$. This is assertion 3. [F3, F5, F6, step 4.1]

6.1 The group $C_2$ has order $2$, which is not a power of the prime $5$; so the automizer of the nontrivial $5$-subgroup $P\le P$ is not a $5$-group, and the criterion [F8] fails in its second condition; therefore $A_5$ has no normal $5$-complement. This is assertion 2. [F4, F8, step 5.1]

7.1 The same conclusion follows directly from simplicity: a normal $5$-complement would be a normal subgroup of index $5$, hence a proper nontrivial normal subgroup of $A_5$, contradicting [F2]. [F1, F2, step 6.1]

8.1 Combining: the Sylow $5$-subgroup $P$ is cyclic by assertion 1, yet $A_5$ has no normal $5$-complement by steps 6.1 and 7.1, while its automizer $N_{A_5}(P)/C_{A_5}(P)\cong C_2$ is not a $5$-group by assertion 3. This is the announced counterexample, and it shows that cyclicity of the Sylow subgroup alone carries no normal $p$-complement. ∎ [step 1.1, step 5.1, step 6.1, step 7.1]
