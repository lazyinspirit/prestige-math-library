## Statement

In a short exact sequence $0\to N\to M\to Q\to0$, the module $M$ is Noetherian if and only if $N$ and $Q$ are Noetherian; the same equivalence holds with “Artinian” in place of “Noetherian”. See [[def-noetherian-module]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A short exact sequence $0\to N\xrightarrow{i}M\xrightarrow{p}Q\to0$ of left $R$-modules.

[F1] Noetherian means that every submodule is finitely generated ([[def-noetherian-module]]). Artinian means that every descending sequence of submodules stabilizes ([[def-artinian-module]]).

[F2] The submodule generated by a set is the smallest submodule containing it; finitely generated means generated by a finite set ([[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]]).

[F3] Exactness gives injective $i$, surjective $p$, and $i(N)=\ker p$ ([[def-exact-and-short-exact-sequences-of-modules]]).

[L1] Finitely many nonempty fibres admit a choice of representatives in ZF ([[lem-finite-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Identify $N$ with $i(N)\subseteq M$ using the injective homomorphism in [F3]. The generated submodule of a finite set is precisely its finite linear combinations: these combinations form a submodule containing the set, and every submodule containing it contains all such combinations. This follows from [F2] and the module laws. Thus homomorphisms carry finite generating sets to generating sets of their images. [F2, F3, given]

2.1 Suppose $M$ is Noetherian. Every submodule of $N$ is a submodule of $M$, hence finitely generated. If $V\le Q$, its preimage $p^{-1}(V)$ is a submodule of $M$ and has finite generators; their images generate $V$ because $p$ is surjective. Consequently $N$ and $Q$ are Noetherian. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 Conversely suppose $N$ and $Q$ are Noetherian, and let $L\le M$. Choose finite generators $a_1,\ldots,a_r$ of $L\cap N$ and $q_1,\ldots,q_s$ of $p(L)$. By [L1], lift the finitely many $q_j$ to $b_j\in L$. For $x\in L$, write $p(x)=\sum_j c_jq_j$. Then $x-\sum_j c_jb_j$ belongs to $L\cap\ker p=L\cap N$, so is a linear combination of the $a_i$. Thus the $a_i$ and $b_j$ generate $L$. Empty generating lists cause no change to this argument. Since $L$ was arbitrary, $M$ is Noetherian, without any countable or dependent choice. [F1, F3, L1, step 1.1, choose]

2.3 Suppose $M$ is Artinian. A descending chain of submodules of $N$ is one in $M$, hence stabilizes. A descending chain $(V_j)$ in $Q$ lifts to the descending chain $(p^{-1}(V_j))$ in $M$; after it stabilizes, its images $V_j$ stabilize by surjectivity. Thus $N$ and $Q$ are Artinian. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

2.4 Conversely suppose $N$ and $Q$ are Artinian and $L_0\supseteq L_1\supseteq\cdots$ is a descending chain in $M$. The chains $L_j\cap N$ and $p(L_j)$ stabilize. Take an index $j_0$ beyond both stabilization indices. For $j\ge j_0$ and $x\in L_{j_0}$, equality of images gives $y\in L_j$ with $p(y)=p(x)$. Then $x-y\in L_{j_0}\cap N=L_j\cap N$, so $x\in L_j$. Hence $L_j=L_{j_0}$ for all $j\ge j_0$, and $M$ is Artinian. [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 prove the Noetherian equivalence, and steps 2.3 and 2.4 prove the Artinian equivalence. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 2.3, step 2.4] ∎
