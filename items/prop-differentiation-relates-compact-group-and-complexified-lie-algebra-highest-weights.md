## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Differentiating a compact-group highest weight
gives the same highest weight for the complexified derived Lie algebra: if
$\pi$ is an irreducible finite-dimensional representation of $G$ with highest
weight $\lambda\in X^*(T)$, then the associated $\mathfrak g'_{\mathbb C}$-module
has highest weight the restriction of the differential of $\lambda$ to the
derived Cartan algebra. Conversely, a module for $\mathfrak g'_{\mathbb C}$
alone contains no action of the connected centre and therefore does not by
itself determine a representation of $G$. After choosing a representation of
$Z(G)^0$ commuting with the integrated $G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}$-action,
the resulting representation of
$Z(G)^0\times G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}$ descends to $G$ exactly when the
finite central covering kernel acts trivially, equivalently when its weights
on the preimage of $T$ descend to characters in $X^*(T)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume the Axiom of Choice, a compact connected $G$ with maximal torus $T$, and the covering $Z(G)^0\times G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}\to G$ with finite central kernel.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it enters through [L1] and [L2].

[L1] Irreducible representations of $G$ are classified by dominant elements of $X^*(T)$, and the representation restricts on the derived factor to the module of the differential of its highest weight ([[thm-highest-weight-classification-for-a-compact-connected-lie-group]]).

[L2] Characters of $T$ differentiate to integral functionals on $\mathfrak t$, and a character is determined by its differential because $T$ is connected with surjective exponential ([[prop-differentiation-identifies-characters-with-the-integral-weight-lattice-of-t]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\pi$ have highest weight $\lambda\in X^*(T)$ and let $v$ be a highest-weight vector. Differentiating the group action on the line $\mathbb Cv$ gives the Lie-algebra weight $d\lambda$, and for every positive root and every $X\in\mathfrak g_\alpha$ one has $X\cdot v=0$ while the Borel subalgebra acts on $v$ by the restriction of $d\lambda$; hence the $\mathfrak g'_{\mathbb C}$-module generated by $v$ has highest weight $d\lambda|_{\mathfrak t\cap\mathfrak g'}$. [L1, L2]

1.2 Conversely, let $M$ be a finite-dimensional $\mathfrak g'_{\mathbb C}$-module. Its restriction to the compact real form integrates to the simply connected compact group $G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}$ by [L1]. No action of $Z(G)^0$ is determined by this derived-algebra module: one must separately choose a representation $\zeta:Z(G)^0\to\operatorname{GL}(M)$ commuting with the derived-group action. Such a choice gives a representation of the product $Z(G)^0\times G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}$. [L1]

2.1 Let $K$ be the finite central kernel of $Z(G)^0\times G_{\mathrm{der}}^{\mathrm{sc}}\to G$. The product representation from step 1.2 descends precisely when it is constant on the fibers of this quotient, that is, precisely when every element of $K$ acts trivially. On the preimage of $T$, [L2] identifies the characters trivial on $K$ with the characters that descend to $T$, namely the elements of $X^*(T)$. Thus kernel-triviality is equivalent to the stated condition that all product-torus weights descend to $X^*(T)$. Together with step 1.1 this proves both directions, while explicitly recording the central action that the derived module alone lacks. [A1, L1, L2, step 1.1, step 1.2]∎
