## Statement

Let $n\ge1$ and $a\in\mathbb R^n$. The linear map
$$\ell_a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R,\qquad \ell_a(y)=a^Ty,$$
is measurable from the Euclidean Borel space $\mathbb R^n$ to the Borel space
$\mathbb R$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $n\ge1$, a vector $a\in\mathbb R^n$, and the standard
Euclidean metrics and their Borel sigma-algebras on $\mathbb R^n$ and
$\mathbb R$.

[F1] Every linear map $L:\mathbb R^m\to\mathbb R^k$, with $m,k\ge1$, is bounded:
for some $K\ge0$, $\|Lh\|_2\le K\|h\|_2$ for every $h$.
Linearity means preservation of linear combinations by
[[def-euclidean-linear-map]], and the bound is
[[lem-euclidean-linear-maps-have-matrices-and-are-bounded]].

[F2] On $\mathbb R^m$ for $m\ge1$, the norm from the Euclidean inner product
agrees with $d_2$. The inner product is bilinear, and $\mathbb R^m$ consists
of functions on $\{0,\ldots,m-1\}$ with pointwise vector operations.
[[def-euclidean-inner-product]], [[lem-metrics-on-rn]].

[F3] For every $m\ge1$, $d_2$ is a metric on $\mathbb R^m$ by
[[lem-metrics-on-rn]]. For any metric space, its metric-open sets form a
topology: the empty set and the whole space are open by
[[def-metric-topology]], and arbitrary unions and
finite intersections are open by [[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]]. The balls
$B(x,r)$ used below are as defined by [[def-metric-ball]]. Thus
the metric topology gives a topological space by
[[def-topological-space]], and its Borel sigma-algebra is generated by those
open sets by [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]].

[F4] A map of topological spaces is continuous when, for each point and each
open target neighborhood of its image, it maps some open source neighborhood
of the point into that target neighborhood.
[[def-continuous-map-top]].

[F5] A continuous map between topological spaces has Borel preimages of Borel
sets. [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]].

[F6] A map between measurable spaces is measurable when the preimage of every
measurable target set is measurable in the source.
[[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]].

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Define $J:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R^1$ by $J(t)(0)=t$. By [F2], $J$ is a bijection with inverse $x\mapsto x_0$, and preserves linear combinations. The one-coordinate metric formula gives $d_2(J(s),J(t))=\sqrt{(s-t)^2}=|s-t|$. Thus the usual scalar distance $d_{\mathbb R}(s,t):=|s-t|$ inherits the metric axioms from $d_2$ on $\mathbb R^1$. By [F3], both this scalar metric and the source metric $d_2$ give the required topologies and Borel sigma-algebras. [F2, F3, construct, algebra]

2.1 Put $L:=J\circ\ell_a:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R^1$. Since $a^Ty=\langle a,y\rangle$, bilinearity in [F2] and step 1.1 show that $L$ preserves linear combinations. By [F1], choose $K\ge0$ with $\|Lh\|_2\le K\|h\|_2$ for every $h$. Hence $|\ell_a(y)-\ell_a(z)|=\|L(y-z)\|_2\le Kd_2(y,z)$ for all $y,z\in\mathbb R^n$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, choose, algebra]

3.1 Let $y\in\mathbb R^n$ and let $V$ be open in the scalar target topology with $\ell_a(y)\in V$. By [F3], choose $\varepsilon>0$ with $B_{d_{\mathbb R}}(\ell_a(y),\varepsilon)\subseteq V$. Put $\delta:=\varepsilon/(K+1)>0$, where $K$ is from step 2.1. If $z\in B_{d_2}(y,\delta)$, then step 2.1 gives $|\ell_a(z)-\ell_a(y)|\le Kd_2(z,y)\le (K+1)d_2(z,y)<\varepsilon$. Thus $\ell_a[B_{d_2}(y,\delta)]\subseteq B_{d_{\mathbb R}}(\ell_a(y),\varepsilon)\subseteq V$. The source ball is open by [F3], so [F4] shows that $\ell_a$ is continuous between the topological spaces established in step 1.1. [F3, F4, step 1.1, step 2.1, choose, algebra]

4.1 By [F5], every Borel subset of the target has a Borel preimage in the source; [F6] therefore makes $\ell_a$ measurable. [step 3.1, F5, F6] ∎
