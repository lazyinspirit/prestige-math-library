## Statement

Let $k$ be a field of characteristic not $2$, let $V$ be finite-dimensional of dimension $n$, and let $B$ be any symmetric bilinear form on $V$, possibly degenerate; put $Q(v)=B(v,v)$. Fix an ordered basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$. Then

$$1,\qquad \bar e_{i_1}\cdots\bar e_{i_r}\quad(1\le i_1<\cdots<i_r\le n)$$

is a $k$-basis of $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$, so $\dim_k\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)=2^n$. Give $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ the word-length filtration $F_{-1}=0$ and, for $m\ge0$, let $F_m$ be the span of products of at most $m$ canonical generators. The natural graded algebra map

$$\Lambda V\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}_F\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)=\bigoplus_{m\ge0}F_m/F_{m-1}$$

that sends $v\in V$ to the degree-one class of $\bar v$ is an isomorphism, where the graded product is induced by $F_pF_q\subseteq F_{p+q}$. No nondegeneracy hypothesis is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $k$ of characteristic not $2$, a finite-dimensional $k$-vector space $V$ with ordered basis $e_1,\ldots,e_n$, a symmetric bilinear form $B$ (possibly degenerate), and $Q(v)=B(v,v)$. In the proof, $\operatorname{End}_k(W)$ denotes the unital $k$-algebra of $k$-linear maps $W\to W$, with composition as multiplication.

[F1] In $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$ the canonical image satisfies $\bar v^{\,2}=Q(v)1$ ([[def-clifford-algebra-of-a-quadratic-space]]).

[F2] The polarized relation is $\bar v\bar w+\bar w\bar v=2B(v,w)1$ ([[lem-clifford-polarized-relations]]).

[F3] A linear map $V\to A$ satisfying $f(v)^2=Q(v)1_A$ extends uniquely to an algebra homomorphism $\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)\to A$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-clifford-algebra]]).

[F4] $\Lambda V$ is the tensor algebra quotient by the two-sided ideal generated by $v^2$ for $v\in V$ ([[def-exterior-algebra-of-a-finite-free-module]]).

[F5] For an ordered basis of $V$, the increasing square-free wedges form a basis of $\Lambda V$ ([[lem-exterior-algebra-basis-monomials]]).

[F6] The operators $c(v)=\epsilon_v+\iota_v$ on $\Lambda V$ are well defined and satisfy $c(v)^2=Q(v)I$ ([[lem-clifford-exterior-wedge-contraction-relations]]).

[F7] A linear map $V\to A$ into a unital associative $k$-algebra extends uniquely to a unital algebra homomorphism $T_k(V)\to A$ ([[thm-universal-property-of-the-tensor-algebra]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [F6], $c:V\to\operatorname{End}_k(\Lambda V)$ is linear and satisfies the quadratic relation. With endomorphism multiplication given by composition, [F3] gives a representation $\rho:\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)\to\operatorname{End}_k(\Lambda V)$. For $I=(i_1<\cdots<i_r)$, the degree-$r$ part of $\rho(\bar e_{i_1}\cdots\bar e_{i_r})(1)$ is exactly $e_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e_{i_r}$, since it comes from choosing every exterior-multiplication term; any contraction lowers the resulting degree. [F3, F6, construct]

1.2 The defining relation [F1] gives $\bar e_i^2=Q(e_i)1$, while [F2] gives $\bar e_j\bar e_i=-\bar e_i\bar e_j+2B(e_i,e_j)1$ whenever $j>i$. [F1, F2]

2.1 Use well-founded induction on the lexicographically ordered pair (word length, inversion count), where the inversion count of $\bar e_{i_1}\cdots\bar e_{i_r}$ is the number of pairs $a<b$ with $i_a>i_b$. Swapping an adjacent inversion lowers the inversion count in the same-length term and creates only a scalar correction of length two less; a sorted word with a repeated index has an adjacent square, which [F1] replaces by a scalar. Both resulting measures are smaller, so induction reduces every word to a linear combination of the displayed words and proves spanning. [step 1.2, algebra]

2.2 If a linear relation among the spanning words were nonzero, choose its greatest word length $r$ with a nonzero coefficient and apply $\rho$ to $1$. Shorter words have exterior degree at most their word length, so the degree-$r$ component is a relation among distinct basis wedges from [F5]; their linear independence forces all coefficients of length $r$ to vanish, a contradiction. Descending over the finitely many lengths proves independence. [step 1.1, F5, algebra]

3.1 For each $m$, the relations in [F1] and [F2] never increase word length, so the words of length at most $m$ span $F_m$. Steps 2.1 and 2.2 show that the ordered square-free words of length at most $m$ are a basis of $F_m$. [F1, F2, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 The filtration is multiplicative because concatenating words of lengths at most $p$ and $q$ has length at most $p+q$. Send $v$ to the class of $\bar v$ in $F_1/F_0$ and extend to $T_k(V)$ by [F7]. The square of each degree-one class is zero, since $\bar v^2=Q(v)1\in F_0\subseteq F_1$; hence this tensor-algebra map kills each exterior relation in [F4]. Its kernel is a two-sided ideal because the map preserves sums and products, so it contains the ideal generated by those relations and induces a graded algebra map $\Phi:\Lambda V\to\operatorname{gr}_F\mathrm{Cl}(V,Q)$. [F1, F4, F7, step 3.1]

5.1 In degree $m$, [F5] gives the basis of $\Lambda^mV$ and step 3.1 gives the corresponding basis of $F_m/F_{m-1}$. The map $\Phi$ sends each basis wedge to its matching Clifford-word class, so it is a vector-space isomorphism in every degree and therefore a graded algebra isomorphism. [F5, step 3.1, step 4.1]

6.1 Steps 2.1 and 2.2 prove the ordered-word basis; its words are indexed by subsets of the $n$ basis vectors, and partitioning those subsets by size gives $\sum_{r=0}^n\binom nr=2^n$. Steps 4.1 and 5.1 prove the associated-graded assertion, including the boundary case $n=0$. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 4.1, step 5.1, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

No choice axiom is used. The ordered basis is part of the theorem's hypotheses, and the contraction, reduction, and exterior-action constructions are explicit.
