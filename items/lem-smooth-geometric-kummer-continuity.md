## Statement

Let $k$ be an algebraically closed field of characteristic $p>0$, $n\ge1$ prime to $p$, $S=\operatorname{Spec}k\llbracket t\rrbracket$, $K=k((t))$, and let a separable closure $K^s/K$ be specified. Let $H$ be the strict henselization at $(t,x)$ of $k\llbracket t\rrbracket[x]_{(t,x)}$. For each finite subextension $K'/K$, let $S'$ be its valuation ring with uniformizer $s$, and let $H'$ be the henselization at the closed point of the base-changed local model. Assume explicitly that $R'=H'[s^{-1}]$ is a domain with $\operatorname{Pic}(R')=0$ and $R'^\times/(R'^\times)^n$ generated by the class of $s$. Then
$$H^1_{\acute et}\!\left(\operatorname{Spec}H\times_S\operatorname{Spec}K^s,\mu_n\right)=0.$$
Under the stalk definition of nearby cycles, the first $\mu_n$ nearby-cycle group is therefore zero at this smooth point. The finite-level hypotheses are part of this Statement; this lemma does not assert a global proper-base-change comparison.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The field, smooth local model, specified separable closure, and the explicit finite-level Picard, unit and domain hypotheses.

## Proof

1.1 Every finite subextension $K'/K$ has complete valuation ring $S'$ with residue field $k$ and $t=s^e a(s)$ for a unit $a(s)$. The finite $H$-algebra $H\otimes S'$ has unique closed point, since its closed fibre is $k[s]/(s^e)$, and it is henselian local by finite-algebra idempotent lifting. By the universal property of henselization it is canonically isomorphic to $H'$. Thus the geometric generic coordinate ring is the filtered colimit of the domains $R'$. [given, algebra]

2.1 Étale $\mu_n$-torsors and their isomorphisms descend through this filtered affine colimit. A torsor is represented by a finite projective coordinate module, an idempotent matrix, multiplication, unit and coaction matrices, and a finite inverse matrix expressing the torsor isomorphism. The finitely many entries and equations descend to a common finite stage; any isomorphism similarly descends after enlarging the stage. Thus the torsor group over the colimit is the colimit of the finite-level torsor groups. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 At each finite stage, the character eigenspaces of a $\mu_n$-torsor form invertible modules. The stated Picard hypothesis trivializes the weight-one eigenspace, making every torsor $z^n=f$ for a unit $f$; two such torsors agree exactly when their units differ by an $n$th power. By the stated unit hypothesis its class is a multiple of $[s]$. Adjoining an $n$th root of $s$ is a finite separable extension, so that class becomes zero at a later stage. Hence the colimit torsor group is zero. [given, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Étale $H^1$ of $\mu_n$ classifies its torsors by local trivialization, cocycles and gluing, so step 3.1 proves the displayed vanishing. The stalk construction of nearby cycles identifies this $H^1$ with the first nearby-cycle group at the smooth point. No global proper-base-change or monodromy statement is used. [step 1.1, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
