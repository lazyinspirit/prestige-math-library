## Statement

Let $X$ be a Noetherian scheme and $\mathcal J$ a coherent ideal sheaf. Let $\beta:X'\to X$ be the blowup constructed from its finite affine Rees-algebra charts. Then $\beta$ is proper. The extended ideal $\mathcal J\mathcal O_{X'}$ is invertible, and for every morphism $f:T\to X$ for which $\mathcal J\mathcal O_T$ is an invertible ideal, there is a unique $X$-morphism $T\to X'$. Here an invertible ideal is locally generated by one nonzerodivisor.

The morphism $\beta$ is an isomorphism over $U=X\setminus V(\mathcal J)$, and this copy of $U$ is schematically dense, and hence topologically dense, in $X'$. In particular, if the center is disjoint from a given open $V\subseteq X$, the morphism is an isomorphism over $V$. Zero and unit ideals and empty schemes are included.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Noetherian scheme, coherent ideal and finite affine-chart blowup; for the universal assertion, a morphism $f:T\to X$ with invertible image ideal.

[F1] A nonzero finite-type algebra over a field is module-finite over a polynomial subalgebra generated by algebraically independent elements. If a finite module $M$ satisfies $IM=M$, some $a\in I$ satisfies $(1-a)M=0$. Neither of these source Statements assumes a choice axiom. [[cor-noether-normalisation-module-finiteness]] [[lem-determinant-trick-for-nakayama]]

[F2] Over a Noetherian base the affine blowup charts glue canonically, give a separated finite-type morphism, and are unchanged by base localization. On the chart $A[J/a]$, the ideal $J$ becomes $(a)$ with $a$ a nonzerodivisor. The chart algebra is generated by $a_j/a$; its presentation by the relations $aT_j-a_j$ has only $a$-power torsion in the kernel. [[lem-nagata-finite-affine-blowup-chart-gluing]] [[lem-affine-blowup-algebra-properties]]

[F3] Projective space is constructed by its finitely many polynomial affine charts with the usual ratio overlaps, and these charts commute with base change. The closed sets of the projective prime spectrum are the sets $V_+(I)$ for homogeneous ideals. [[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]] [[def-proj-graded-ring-points]]

[F4] Proper means separated, of finite type and universally closed. Separatedness can be checked by affine intersections and surjectivity of the tensor-to-overlap coordinate maps. [[def-proper-morphism]] [[thm-separatedness-gluing-overlap-criterion]]

[F5] An invertible ideal is locally a free rank-one submodule of the structure sheaf; its generator is a nonzerodivisor. Localization and quotient ring arithmetic include the zero ring. [[def-invertible-sheaf]] [[def-multiplicative-subset-and-localisation]] [[def-quotient-ring]]

[F6] A finite-type algebra over a Noetherian commutative ring is Noetherian. The finite principal-cover argument in [F2] shows directly that Noetherian affine charts and all their open subsets are quasi-compact, without using arbitrary-affine compactness. [[cor-finite-type-algebra-over-noetherian-ring-is-noetherian]] [[lem-nagata-finite-affine-blowup-chart-gluing]]

## Proof

1.1 We first prove the finite prime-existence calculation needed for projective closedness. Let $D$ be a nonzero finite-type algebra over a field $k$. By [F1], it is finite as a module over an injectively embedded polynomial ring $P=k[z_1,\ldots,z_d]$. Put $\mathfrak m=(z_1,\ldots,z_d)$. If $\mathfrak mD=D$, the determinant trick [F1] gives $a\in\mathfrak m$ with $(1-a)D=0$. Applying this to $1_D$ and using $P\hookrightarrow D$ gives $1-a=0$ in $P$, impossible after evaluating all $z_i$ at zero. Hence $E=D/\mathfrak mD$ is a nonzero finite-dimensional $k$-algebra. Among its proper ideals choose one of maximum $k$-dimension, possible because the dimensions belong to the finite set $\{0,\ldots,\dim_kE-1\}$ and zero is a proper ideal. A strictly larger proper ideal would have larger dimension; consequently every nonzero element of the quotient generates the unit ideal and is invertible. That quotient is a field finite over $k$. Thus every nonzero finite-type field algebra has a field quotient finite over its base, using only the supplied finite algebra statements and one maximum in a finite set. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra]

1.2 The extended ideal is invertible because [F2] gives its regular generator $a_i$ on each source chart. For a morphism $f:T\to X$ as in the Statement, work locally where $X=\operatorname{Spec}A$, $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$ and its image ideal on $T$ is generated by a nonzerodivisor $t$. Locally write $f^*a_i=tu_i$ and $t=\sum_i v_i f^*a_i$. Cancelling the regular $t$ gives $\sum_i v_iu_i=1$. Hence the opens where $u_i$ is a unit cover $T$, and on each of them $f^*a_i$ is a regular generator of the image ideal. Send each fraction $a_j/a_i$ in $A[J/a_i]$ to the unique local quotient $f^*a_j/f^*a_i$. The relations $a_iT_j-a_j$ vanish, and the additional $a_i$-power torsion relations in [F2] vanish because $f^*a_i$ is a nonzerodivisor. This defines a morphism to source chart $i$. On overlaps the fraction identities are precisely those of [F2], so these local morphisms glue, first on $T$ and then over affine opens of $X$, to an $X$-morphism $T\to X'$. Any such factorization is unique: on an open where $a_i$ generates the image ideal, its image lies in chart $i$ (the ratio to any other local chart generator is a unit), and its coordinate map must send every $a_j/a_i$ to that unique quotient. [F2, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $R=k[T_0,\ldots,T_n]/I$ be a homogeneous quotient over a field. Its projective prime spectrum is empty if and only if $R_m=0$ for some $m\ge1$. For the nontrivial implication, if some $T_i$ is not nilpotent, then $(R[1/T_i])_0$ is a nonzero finite-type $k$-algebra, generated by the ratios $T_j/T_i$. Step 1.1 gives it a field quotient and hence a prime ideal. The inverse image of that prime in the homogeneous localization gives a homogeneous prime of $R$ avoiding $T_i$: explicitly send a homogeneous element $r$ of degree $e$ to $t^e$ times the image of $r/T_i^e$ in the Laurent polynomial ring over that quotient field. The kernel is homogeneous and prime and avoids $T_i$. This is a projective point, a contradiction. Therefore every $T_i$ is nilpotent. Finitely many exponents give an $m$ such that every monomial of degree $m$ vanishes, hence $R_m=0$. Conversely, if $R_m=0$, every $T_i^m=0$, so no homogeneous prime avoids the irrelevant ideal and the projective spectrum is empty. [F1, F3, F5, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 We now prove universal closedness of the standard projective space directly. Its affine charts in [F3] identify its underlying topology over a ring $B$ with the projective prime spectrum of $B[T_0,\ldots,T_n]$. The point correspondence needs no prime-existence theorem: for a prime of the $i$th polynomial ratio chart, map each homogeneous polynomial $H$ of degree $e$ to $t^eH(1,T_j/T_i)$ in the Laurent polynomial ring over that prime's residue field; its kernel is the required homogeneous prime avoiding $T_i$. Conversely homogeneous localization gives the chart prime. These maps are inverse, carry equations to their dehomogenizations and agree on ratio overlaps. Therefore a closed subset has the form $V_+(I)$ for a homogeneous ideal $I$, with the fibre over $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}B$ given by its homogeneous quotient after tensoring with $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. Put $M_m=(B[T]/I)_m$, a finite $B$-module generated by the finitely many degree-$m$ monomials. By step 2.1 the fibre is empty exactly when $M_m\otimes_B\kappa(\mathfrak p)=0$ for some $m\ge1$. Then $(M_m)_{\mathfrak p}=\mathfrak p(M_m)_{\mathfrak p}$, so [F1]'s determinant trick gives $a\in\mathfrak pB_{\mathfrak p}$ with $(1-a)(M_m)_{\mathfrak p}=0$. The element $1-a$ is a unit in that local ring, so $(M_m)_{\mathfrak p}=0$. Clearing the denominators of a finite generating list gives $b\notin\mathfrak p$ with $(M_m)_b=0$. Every fibre over $D(b)$ is therefore empty by step 2.1. Hence the complement of the image of $V_+(I)$ is open. This proves closedness of every closed-set image over every affine base ring $B$, including arbitrary base changes; restriction to an affine cover proves universal closedness over every base scheme. [F1, F3, F5, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Over a Noetherian base, the projection of projective space is of finite type: its finitely many affine charts are polynomial algebras in finitely many variables, and on affine base opens these rings are Noetherian by [F6], so their open subsets are quasi-compact by the explicit finite principal-cover argument in [F6]. It is separated by [F4]: the overlap of charts $i,j$ is the localization at $T_j/T_i$, and its coordinate ring is generated by the two chart rings, since the inverse ratio $T_i/T_j$ is in the second one. Thus the tensor-to-overlap map is surjective. Together with step 3.1 and [F4], this proves properness of the projective-space projection over a Noetherian base without invoking the AC-qualified projective-properness theorem. [F3, F4, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Work over an affine open $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq X$, with $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_m)$. The graded surjection $A[T_1,\ldots,T_m]\to\bigoplus_{d\ge0}J^dt^d$ given by $T_i\mapsto a_it$ yields a closed immersion of the chart construction into $\mathbb P^{m-1}_A$ by finite affine gluing: on the $i$th projective chart its coordinate map to $A[J/a_i]$ sends $T_j/T_i$ to $a_j/a_i$ and is surjective by [F2]. On the $j$th source chart the inverse image of the $i$th target chart is $D(a_i/a_j)$, exactly their source overlap; hence these closed chart maps glue and the full inverse image of target chart $i$ is source chart $i$. After any base change they remain quotient-ring closed immersions. Therefore the image of any closed subset of the base-changed blowup is closed in the base-changed projective space, whose image in the base is closed by step 3.1. This proves universal closedness of the blowup over this affine open. Separatedness and finite type are given by [F2]; all three properties are local on the base, as follows by restricting the diagonal, finite affine covers and closed-set images to base opens. Thus $\beta$ is proper globally by [F4]. [F2, F3, F4, F5, step 3.1, step 4.1, construct, algebra]

6.1 The isomorphism over $U$ is [F2]. On chart $A[J/a_i]$, the corresponding part of $U$ is $D(a_i)$, and the ring map to its localization is injective because $a_i$ is a nonzerodivisor. These are exactly the local kernel tests for the map $\mathcal O_{X'}\to j_*\mathcal O_U$, so it is injective and $U$ is schematically dense. This implies topological density: on an open disjoint from $U$, the target sheaf is zero, so the structure sheaf would be zero, which is possible only for the empty scheme. A center disjoint from $V$ has $V\subseteq U$, giving the final isomorphism assertion. The zero ideal gives the empty blowup; an invertible pullback of the zero ideal forces $T$ empty, so its universal property holds too. The unit ideal gives the identity blowup. These observations also include empty base schemes and finish the proof. [F2, F5, step 5.1, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
