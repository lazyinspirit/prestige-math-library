## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $R$ be a Dedekind domain, put $S=\operatorname{Spec}R$, and let $X\to S$ be a smooth finite-type morphism of pure relative dimension $d\ge0$. Its relative diagonal $\Delta_{X/S}:X\to X\times_SX$ is a regular immersion of length $d$: near each point of its image it is a closed immersion whose ideal in the ambient local ring is generated by a regular sequence of length $d$. If $X\to S$ is separated, the diagonal is closed globally. For $d=0$ the diagonal is an open immersion, with the empty regular sequence. The assertion for empty $X$ is vacuous.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Dedekind domain $R$, and the smooth finite-type morphism $X\to\operatorname{Spec}R$ of pure relative dimension $d$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is used by the smooth local-standard-form and étale comparison suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] A smooth morphism of relative dimension $d$ is locally on its source étale over relative affine $d$-space on an affine open of the base ([[thm-smooth-local-standard-form]]).

[F2] The diagonal represents the pairs of equal morphisms, and fibre products exist ([[def-diagonal-morphism-scheme]], [[thm-fibre-products-of-schemes-exist]]).

[F3] Polynomial evaluation at an assigned family of elements defines a unique homomorphism of commutative rings ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring-on-a-family]]).

[F4] A regular sequence acts injectively on each successive nonzero quotient and has nonzero terminal quotient ([[def-regular-sequence-on-a-module]]).

[F5] Étale means smooth of relative dimension zero. Étale morphisms are stable under base change and composition; they are flat at every point and unramified ([[lem-etale-stable-base-change-composition]], [[def-etale-morphism-schemes]], [[def-flat-morphism-schemes]], [[thm-etale-equivalent-flat-unramified-fp]]).

[F6] Flatness of a module means that tensor product preserves exact sequences; for a flat morphism this applies to the induced local-ring maps ([[def-flat-and-faithfully-flat-modules-and-ring-maps]], [[def-flat-morphism-schemes]]).

[F7] The diagonal of an unramified morphism is an open immersion ([[thm-unramified-diagonal-open-immersion]]).

[F8] A morphism is separated precisely when its diagonal is a closed immersion ([[def-separated-morphism-schemes]]).

## Proof

1.1 Fix $x\in X$. By [A1] and [F1], after restricting the base to an affine open $V=\operatorname{Spec}A$ and $X$ to an open $U$ containing $x$, there is an étale morphism $u:U\to\mathbb A^d_V$ over $V$. The diagonal near $\Delta(x)$ lies in the open $U\times_VU$ of $X\times_SX$, so it suffices to treat this chart. [given, A1, F1, F2]

2.1 The diagonal of $\mathbb A^d_A$ is defined in $B=A[t_1,\ldots,t_d,s_1,\ldots,s_d]$ by $t_i-s_i$ for $1\le i\le d$. Indeed, evaluation $s_i\mapsto t_i$ gives the quotient $A[t_1,\ldots,t_d]$, with the inverse on the remaining generators, by [F3]. After quotienting by the first $i-1$ differences, the ring is the polynomial ring in the remaining variables. Regard it as $C[t_i]$ with $s_i\in C$. If a polynomial $f\ne0$ has leading coefficient $c\ne0$, then $(t_i-s_i)f$ has the same nonzero leading coefficient and degree one larger, even if $A$ has zero divisors. Thus multiplication by each next difference is injective. The terminal quotient is nonzero whenever $A\ne0$; in this chart $V$ contains the image of $x$, so $A\ne0$. This proves regularity by [F4], including the empty sequence for $d=0$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The map $u\times u:U\times_VU\to\mathbb A^d_V\times_V\mathbb A^d_V$ is a composite of two base changes of $u$, and is therefore étale and flat by [F5]. Put $W=U\times_{\mathbb A^d_V}U$, the pullback of the affine-space diagonal. At a point of $W$, tensoring the successive injective multiplication maps in step 2.1 with the flat ambient local ring preserves injectivity by [F6]. The successive quotients are the quotients by the pulled-back differences. Their terminal quotient is the nonzero local ring of $W$ at that point; hence every preceding quotient is nonzero too. Localizing the injections from step 2.1 preserves their injectivity, since a fraction annihilated after localization already has a denominator multiple annihilated before localization. Thus the pulled-back differences form a regular sequence of length $d$ at every point of $W$. [F2, F4, F5, F6, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The map $U\to W$ is the diagonal of the étale morphism $u$, so it is an open immersion by [F5]–[F7]. Its image is open in the closed subscheme $W\subset U\times_VU$. Choose an ambient open whose intersection with $W$ is that image. There the map $U\to U\times_VU$, which is the original relative diagonal on this chart by [F2], is closed and its local ideal is the ideal of $W$. Step 3.1 supplies the required regular sequence of length $d$ at each image point. [F2, F5, F7, step 3.1, choose]

5.1 The charts in step 1.1 cover all diagonal points, so step 4.1 proves the local regular-immersion assertion. If $X\to S$ is separated, [F8] makes this same diagonal closed globally. If $d=0$, $X\to S$ is étale by [F5], and [F7] makes its diagonal open, consistently with the empty sequence. If $X$ is empty there are no image points to check. [given, F5, F7, F8, step 1.1, step 4.1] ∎

## Remarks

For the smooth arithmetic situation of pure total dimension $n=d+1$, the regular-sequence length is $n-1$, as required by Gillet–Soulé §4.5.1. This lemma supplies the diagonal calculation only. The full integral supported Gysin calculus, exterior-product descent and arithmetic Green-current product remain separate proof obligations.
