## Definition

Assume AC for the site-level sheafification construction. Let $f:A\to S$ be an abelian scheme ([[def-abelian-scheme]]) with unit section $e:S\to A$. For an $S$-scheme $T$ write $A_T=A\times_ST$ and $e_T=e\times_S\operatorname{id}_T$; a **rigidified line bundle** on $A_T$ is a pair $(\mathcal L,\alpha)$ consisting of an invertible sheaf $\mathcal L$ on $A_T$ ([[def-invertible-sheaf]]) and a trivialisation $\alpha:\mathcal O_T\to e_T^*\mathcal L$ along the unit section.

The **rigidified relative Picard functor** $\mathcal P_{A/S,e}$ is the fppf sheaf on the category of $S$-schemes associated ([[def-fppf-sheaf-and-sheafification]], [[lem-fppf-sheafification-exists]]) with the functor
$$T\longmapsto\{\text{isomorphism classes of rigidified line bundles on }A_T\},$$
with group law given by tensor product of rigidified line bundles, identity the trivially rigidified structure sheaf and inverse by the dual pairing. An $S$-scheme representing $\mathcal P_{A/S,e}$ is called a **relative Picard scheme** of $A/S$; its identity component is written $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{A/S}$, and when this exists and is an abelian scheme over $S$ it is called the **dual abelian variety** $\hat A$ of $A$. A **Poincare sheaf** is the universal rigidified invertible sheaf $\mathcal P$ on $A\times_S\hat A$.

The **algebraically trivial subfunctor** of $\mathcal P_{A/S,e}$ consists of the classes whose geometric-fibre restrictions are algebraically equivalent to zero, where algebraic equivalence is the equivalence relation generated by differences of fibres in connected finite-type families of line bundles; this definition of the subfunctor does not presuppose a representing scheme, and once a Picard scheme exists the appropriate identity-component theorem identifies the subfunctor with $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{A/S}$. The notation "degree zero" here refers to this subfunctor and is not numerical degree on $A$ when $\dim A\ge2$. For $A$ over a field $k$ the dual $\hat A$ means a representing abelian variety for this subfunctor; representability of $\mathcal P_{A/S,e}$, the existence of $\operatorname{Pic}^0$ and the Poincare sheaf remain claims of the commissioned theorem, and no link from this definition to that theorem is a dependency. The functor is considered with the fppf topology; all test objects are arbitrary $S$-schemes, and flatness and local finite presentation enter only through the definitions of abelian schemes and invertible sheaves used above ([[def-flat-morphism-schemes]], [[def-locally-finite-presentation-morphism]], [[def-group-scheme-over-a-scheme]]).

Sheafification is taken on a fixed set-sized big fppf site containing the test schemes in use, as in the cited construction; the convention imposes no finite-type or reducedness restriction on those tests. AC selects representatives and equality refinements in the plus construction, not line bundles or a representing Picard scheme.
