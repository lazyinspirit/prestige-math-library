## Definition

Assume Countable Choice and let $H$ be a nonzero complex Hilbert space with $T\in\mathcal B(H)$ ([[def-space-of-bounded-linear-operators]]). The **unital $\ast$-algebra generated by $T$** is the smallest subset $P(T)\subseteq\mathcal B(H)$ containing the identity $I$ and $T$ that is closed under addition, scalar multiplication, multiplication and the adjoint. The **C\*-algebra generated by $T$** is its norm closure

$$C^*(I,T):=\overline{P(T)}^{\ \|\cdot\|}\subseteq\mathcal B(H).$$

For an arbitrary $T$, the concrete elements of $P(T)$ are finite linear combinations
of finite words in the two letters $T$ and $T^*$. When $T$ is normal, the two
letters commute and every such word can be reordered, so in that case

$$P(T)=\left\{\sum_{j,k}c_{jk}T^j(T^*)^k:\text{only finitely many }c_{jk}\ne0\right\}.$$

**Well-definedness and the algebra structure.** The set $P(T)$ is a complex $\ast$-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(H)$ containing $I$, and the closure of a $\ast$-subalgebra of a C\*-algebra is again a unital $\ast$-subalgebra: sums, products and adjoints of limits are the limits of the corresponding sums, products and adjoints, because the algebra operations and the adjoint are continuous ([[thm-hilbert-adjoint-properties]], [[lem-bounded-hilbert-operators-form-a-c-star-algebra]], [[def-c-star-algebra]]). A closed subset of the complete space $\mathcal B(H)$ is complete, so $C^*(I,T)$ with the inherited norm, multiplication, unit and adjoint is itself a unital complex C\*-algebra, a **unital C\*-subalgebra** of $\mathcal B(H)$ with the same identity $I$.

**Commutativity is exactly normality.** If $T$ is normal ([[def-self-adjoint-positive-unitary-and-normal-operator]]), then $T$ commutes with $T^*$, hence any two words in $T$ and $T^*$ commute, hence any two $\ast$-polynomials commute, so $P(T)$ is commutative; commutativity passes to the closure because if $A_nB_n=B_nA_n$ and $A_n\to A$, $B_n\to B$, then $AB=BA$ by continuity of multiplication. Conversely, if $C^*(I,T)$ is commutative, then the elements $T$ and $T^*$ of it commute, that is $TT^*=T^*T$ and $T$ is normal. In particular $C^*(I,T)$ is a nonzero commutative unital C\*-algebra exactly when $T$ is normal, and only that case is fed to the Gelfand theory later on this page.

**Minimality convention.** $C^*(I,T)$ is the smallest closed unital $\ast$-subalgebra of $\mathcal B(H)$ containing $T$: every closed unital $\ast$-subalgebra contains $P(T)$ and hence its closure. No generator other than $I$ and $T$ is adjoined, and the definition does not presuppose any particular representation of the generated algebra ([[def-operator-norm]] for the norm used in the closure).
