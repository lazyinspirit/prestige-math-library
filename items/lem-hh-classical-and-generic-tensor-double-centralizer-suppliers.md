## Statement

Let $N\ge2$, $n\ge1$, $V_F=\mathbb Q(q)^N$, and $M_F=V_F^{\otimes n}$. Let $A_F$ and $B_F$ be respectively the images of the quantum algebra from [[def-hh-type-a-quantum-tensor-action-for-centralizers]] and the normalized Hecke algebra action from [[thm-hh-hecke-symmetry-tensor-power-action]]. Let $A_0$ be the unital algebra generated by the classical operators $\rho_n(a)$ of [[lem-hh-classical-tensor-centralizer-by-polarization]], and let $B_0$ be the place-permutation image of $\mathbb Q[S_n]$. The regular quantum and Hecke matrix families below specialize to generating families for these two rational algebras. Then
$$\dim_{\mathbb Q}A_0\le\dim_F A_F,\qquad \dim_{\mathbb Q}B_0\le\dim_F B_F,$$
$$\dim_F\operatorname{End}_{B_F}(M_F)\le\dim_{\mathbb Q}A_0,\qquad \dim_F\operatorname{End}_{A_F}(M_F)\le\dim_{\mathbb Q}B_0.$$
These are separate image lower bounds and commutant upper bounds; the commuting inclusion needed to turn them into equalities is proved in [[lem-hh-quantum-and-hecke-tensor-actions-commute]].

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The quantum and Hecke actions above, their local relation-descent proofs, and the three local rank/centralizer suppliers below.

[F1] The specialized classical quantum image satisfies $A_0=\operatorname{End}_{B_0}(M_0)$ ([[lem-hh-classical-tensor-centralizer-by-polarization]]).

[F2] Over $\mathbb Q$, the finite permutation image satisfies $B_0=\operatorname{End}_{A_0}(M_0)$ ([[lem-hh-finite-semisimple-matrix-double-commutant]]).

[F3] A finite matrix over $\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]_{(q-1)}$ has generic rank at least its rank at $q=1$; this gives both word-image lower bounds and commutant-kernel upper bounds ([[lem-hh-finite-matrix-rank-under-specialization]]).

## Proof

**Given:** The data in [F1]–[F3].

1.1 Put $R=\mathbb Q[q,q^{-1}]_{(q-1)}$ and $M_R=(R^N)^{\otimes n}$, a finite free module with fraction-field fiber $M_F$ and residue-field fiber $M_0$. All Hecke matrices and all iterated quantum matrices are regular over $R$ by their explicit formulas. For the quantum family, also use $H_j=(L_j-I)/(q-1)$. On a basis word with $m_j$ occurrences of $j$, $L_j$ has eigenvalue $q^{m_j}$, so $H_j$ is the diagonal matrix with entry $1+q+\cdots+q^{m_j-1}$ if $m_j>0$ and $0$ if $m_j=0$. Thus $H_j$ is regular and specializes to the classical diagonal count operator $\rho_n(E_{jj})$. The generators $E_i,F_i,H_j$ specialize to the adjacent matrix Lie operators and all diagonal count operators, which generate $A_0$ by the last conclusion of [[lem-hh-classical-tensor-centralizer-by-polarization]]; $L_j^{\pm1}$ specialize to the identity. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 In a basis of $M_R$, vectorize $X\in\operatorname{End}(M_R)$. The simultaneous commutator system for the finite family $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}$ is a finite matrix over $R$, and its kernel over $F$ is $\operatorname{End}_{B_F}(M_F)$ while its kernel at $q=1$ is $\operatorname{End}_{B_0}(M_0)$. By [F3] its generic kernel dimension is at most the specialized one, which equals $\dim_{\mathbb Q}A_0$ by [F1]. The same construction for the finite family $E_i,F_i,L_j^{\pm1},H_j$ has generic kernel $\operatorname{End}_{A_F}(M_F)$; at $q=1$ the family generates $A_0$, so its specialized kernel is $\operatorname{End}_{A_0}(M_0)$, of dimension $\dim_{\mathbb Q}B_0$ by [F2]. This proves both commutant upper bounds independently. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The algebra $A_0$ is finite-dimensional and generated by the specializations at $q=1$ of the regular quantum family in step 1.1. Words in that finite family span it, so select a finite word basis. The same words in the regular generic matrices have a vectorized-column matrix over $R$ whose specialization has full column rank; [F3] gives their $F$-linear independence. Hence $\dim_F A_F\ge\dim_{\mathbb Q}A_0$. Words in the finite family $T_1,\ldots,T_{n-1}$ similarly specialize to a spanning family of $B_0$; selecting a basis and applying [F3] gives $\dim_F B_F\ge\dim_{\mathbb Q}B_0$. Only these chosen regular words are specialized; no arbitrary rational denominator is evaluated at $q=1$. [given, F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
