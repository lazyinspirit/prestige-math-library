## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $B$ be a Boolean
algebra ([[def-boolean-algebra-and-boolean-ultrafilter-for-stone-duality]]) and
let $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ be its ultrafilter space with the topology generated
by the sets $[b] = \{U : b \in U\}$
([[def-stone-space-and-clopen-algebra]]). Then:

1. the map $b \mapsto [b]$ is an isomorphism of Boolean algebras from $B$ onto
   $\operatorname{Clop}(\operatorname{Ult}(B))$, the algebra of clopen subsets
   of the ultrafilter space;
2. $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is a Stone space: compact, Hausdorff, with a basis of
   clopen sets.

For the trivial Boolean algebra both sides are the trivial Boolean algebra
$\{\varnothing\}$ and the empty space. The proof of compactness is a direct
filter argument and does not use Tychonoff's theorem for products.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An assumed Axiom of Choice, a Boolean algebra $B$, and its ultrafilter space $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ with the basic open sets $[b]$.

[L1] $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is the set of Boolean ultrafilters, the topology is generated by the sets $[b] = \{U : b \in U\}$, and these are clopen because $[b] \cap [\neg b] = \emptyset$ and $[b] \cup [\neg b] = \operatorname{Ult}(B)$; the Boolean operations on $\operatorname{Clop}$ are set-theoretic ([[def-stone-space-and-clopen-algebra]], [[def-boolean-algebra-and-boolean-ultrafilter-for-stone-duality]]).

[L2] Every proper filter of $B$ extends to an ultrafilter, and ultrafilters separate elements: for $a \ne b$ there is an ultrafilter containing exactly one of them ([[lem-boolean-ultrafilter-extension-from-compact-products]], [[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[L3] An ultrafilter decides every element: exactly one of $a \in U$, $\neg a \in U$ ([[lem-boolean-ultrafilter-extension-from-compact-products]]).

[L4] A closed subset of a compact space is compact, and a family of closed subsets of a compact space with the finite intersection property has nonempty intersection. [algebra]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The map $b \mapsto [b]$ preserves the Boolean operations: $[a\wedge b] = [a]\cap[b]$ and $[a\vee b] = [a]\cup[b]$ because an ultrafilter is closed under $\wedge$ and upward closed, $[\neg a] = \operatorname{Ult}(B)\setminus[a]$ by [L3], and $[0] = \emptyset$, $[1] = \operatorname{Ult}(B)$ by the definition of a proper filter; hence each $[b]$ is clopen and $b \mapsto [b]$ is a Boolean homomorphism. [L1, L3, algebra]

1.2 The map $b \mapsto [b]$ is injective by the separation property of [L2]: if $a \ne b$ then some ultrafilter contains exactly one of them, so $[a] \ne [b]$. [L2, algebra]

1.3 $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is Hausdorff: if $U \ne V$ are ultrafilters, then $U \not\subseteq V$, so there is $b \in U \setminus V$; then $\neg b \in V$ by [L3], so $U \in [b]$, $V \in [\neg b]$ and the two basic clopens are disjoint. [L1, L3, algebra]

1.4 $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is compact. It suffices to show that every cover by basic clopens has a finite subcover, since the $[b]$ form a basis: for a basic cover $\{[b_i]\}_{i \in I}$ with no finite subcover, every finite set of indices has $[b_{i_1}]\cup\cdots\cup[b_{i_n}] \ne \operatorname{Ult}(B)$, so there is an ultrafilter $U$ omitting all $b_{i_k}$, and then $\neg b_{i_1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\neg b_{i_n} \ne 0$; consequently the filter generated by $\{\neg b_i : i \in I\}$ is proper, and by [L2] it is contained in an ultrafilter $U$, which then contains every $\neg b_i$ and so lies in no $[b_i]$, contradicting that the $[b_i]$ cover. [L1, L2, L3, algebra]

2.1 The map $b \mapsto [b]$ is surjective onto the clopen subsets: if $C \subseteq \operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is clopen, then $C$ is closed in the compact space $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ by [step 1.4], hence compact. Since $C$ is open and the $[b]$ form a basis, the basic clopens satisfying $[b]\subseteq C$ cover $C$; finitely many of them cover $C$, and their containment in $C$ gives $C = [b_1]\cup\cdots\cup[b_n] = [b_1\vee\cdots\vee b_n]$ by [step 1.1]. [step 1.1, step 1.4, L1, L4]

3.1 By [step 1.1], [step 1.2] and [step 2.1] the map $b \mapsto [b]$ is a bijective Boolean homomorphism onto $\operatorname{Clop}(\operatorname{Ult}(B))$, that is, an isomorphism; and by [step 1.3], [step 1.4] and [L1] the space $\operatorname{Ult}(B)$ is compact, Hausdorff and has a basis of clopen sets, that is, a Stone space. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 2.1, L1] ∎

## Remarks

- **No Tychonoff product is used.** Compactness is proved from the ultrafilter extension lemma directly, which is why the argument works uniformly for the trivial algebra: there the empty cover has the empty finite subcover.
- **The two halves of the theorem are separate improvements.** Injectivity is the separation property, surjectivity is compactness plus the basis, and the topological half uses only the definition of the topology.
