## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice inherited from the cited smoothness, quotient and reduction suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

Let $k$ be a field and let $G$ be an affine algebraic group over $k$ ([[def-affine-scheme]], [[def-group-scheme-over-a-field]]) with derived subgroup $DG$ as in [[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]]. Then:

(a) $DG$ is a closed normal characteristic subgroup scheme of $G$, and $G/DG$ is commutative;

(b) if $G$ is smooth then $DG$ is smooth, and if $G$ is connected then $DG$ is connected;

(c) every closed subgroup scheme $H\subseteq G$ containing $DG$ is normal in $G$;

(d) for every $k$-algebra $R$ the abstract derived subgroup $[G(R),G(R)]$ is contained in $(DG)(R)$, and if $G$ is smooth over an algebraically closed field $k$ then $(DG)(k)=[G(k),G(k)]$;

(e) if $G$ is smooth, connected and solvable with $G\ne1$, then $DG\ne G$, so $\dim DG<\dim G$.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC, a field $k$ and an affine algebraic group $G$ over $k$.

[F1] $DG$ is the smallest closed subgroup scheme through which the commutator morphism $c:G\times_kG\to G$, $(g,h)\mapsto ghg^{-1}h^{-1}$, factors; equivalently $DG$ is the closed subgroup scheme generated by the image of $c$. A subgroup scheme $N$ is normal when conjugation $G\times_kN\to G$ factors through $N$. ([[def-derived-subgroup-and-solvable-algebraic-group]])

[F2] Assume AC. Connected finite-type group schemes are geometrically connected; smooth connected groups are geometrically integral; geometrically reduced finite-type group schemes are smooth. ([[lem-nonaffine-connected-group-geometrically-connected]])

[F3] For a smooth affine group over an algebraically closed field, the abstract commutator subgroup of its rational points equals the rational points of its derived subgroup. Milne Proposition 6.20 proves this using iterated commutator images, constructibility, and a dense open subset of the generated group (printed pp. 130-131). This source statement is used only for clause (d), not to infer smoothness of an image of the commutator morphism.

[F4] Dimension is measured by chains of irreducible closed subsets. Appending a nonempty irreducible ambient finite-dimensional space to a chain in a proper closed subset proves the strict dimension inequality. ([[def-dimension-noetherian-topological-space]])

[F5] Assume AC. The quotient by a closed normal subgroup is a separated finite-type group scheme with faithfully flat projection and the given subgroup as its scheme-theoretic kernel. It represents the fppf coset sheaf. ([[thm-nonaffine-group-scheme-normal-subgroup-quotient]])

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is inherited through the cited suppliers and is the axiom of [[def-axiom-of-choice]].

## Proof

**Given:** AC, a field $k$ and an affine algebraic group $G$ over $k$.

1.1 Write $A=O(G)$ and let $c_n:G^{2n}\to G$ send its arguments to a product of $n$ commutators. Put $I=\bigcap_{n\ge1}\ker(c_n^*:A\to A^{\otimes 2n})$ and $B=A/I$. The induced maps from $B$ to the target rings are jointly injective. Their pairwise tensor products are jointly injective on $B\otimes_kB$: for a finite tensor expression, restrict to the finite-dimensional spans of its factors; joint injectivity supplies finitely many separating coefficient functionals on each span. For $f\in I$, every such tensor map kills $\Delta(f)$ because composing multiplication with $c_n\times c_m$ is $c_{n+m}$. Hence $\Delta(I)\subseteq I\otimes A+A\otimes I$. Inversion reverses a commutator product and replaces each commutator by the one with its two arguments exchanged, so $S(I)\subseteq I$; evaluation at the identity gives $\varepsilon(I)=0$. Thus $I$ is a Hopf ideal. The group $\operatorname{Spec}B$ contains the commutator morphism, and any closed subgroup containing it contains every $c_n$, so its defining Hopf ideal lies in $I$. Therefore $DG=\operatorname{Spec}B$. [F1, given, algebra]

2.1 For any field extension $K/k$, express an element of $A\otimes_kK$ as a finite sum $\sum_i a_i\otimes\lambda_i$ with the $\lambda_i$ linearly independent over $k$. All $c_n^*$ kill this element exactly when they kill every $a_i$, so the joint kernel after extension is $I\otimes_kK$. If $G$ is smooth, every target $(A^{\otimes 2n})\otimes_kK$ is reduced, and their jointly injected subalgebra $B\otimes_kK$ is reduced. Thus $DG$ is geometrically reduced and smooth by [F2]. If $G$ is connected, its geometric connectedness in [F2] makes each finite product $G^{2n}$ connected. For an idempotent $b\in B$, every $c_n^*(b)$ is an idempotent on the connected affine scheme $G^{2n}$, hence a scalar $0$ or $1$. Evaluation at the identity shows that all these scalars equal $\varepsilon(b)$. Joint injectivity of the maps from $B$ then gives $b=\varepsilon(b)$, so $B$ has no nontrivial idempotents and $DG$ is connected. This proves (b), without treating $c$ as a group homomorphism or identifying a scheme-generated closure with a union of underlying images. [F2, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 For every $k$-algebra $R$ the commutator of any two $G(R)$-points lies in $(DG)(R)$, since $c$ factors through $DG$; hence $[G(R),G(R)]\subseteq(DG)(R)$. Under the additional hypotheses of (d), [F3] gives equality. If $DG\subseteq H\subseteq G$, the identity $ghg^{-1}=[g,h]h$ for $g\in G(R),h\in H(R)$ proves $H(R)$ stable under conjugation for every $R$, which is scheme-theoretic normality. In particular $DG$ is normal. This proves (c) and (d). [F1, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 The quotient $G/DG$ exists by [F5]. Its commutator is trivial after pullback along the faithfully flat product cover $G\times G\to(G/DG)\times(G/DG)$, because the commutator of $G$ lands in its kernel; faithfully flat descent therefore makes the quotient commutative. The independent-coefficient argument of step 2.1 also works with an arbitrary $k$-algebra in place of $K$, considered as a $k$-vector space. Thus the description by the $c_n$ commutes with such base changes. Every automorphism of $G_R$ preserves commutator products and their generated closed subgroup, so it preserves $(DG)_R$. Hence $DG$ is characteristic as well as closed and normal, proving (a). [F1, F5, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 If smooth connected solvable $G\ne1$ satisfied $DG=G$, every term of its derived series would equal $G$, contradicting termination at $1$. Therefore $DG\ne G$. The group $G$ is geometrically integral by [F2], and $DG$ is a nonempty proper closed subgroup; [F4] gives $\dim DG<\dim G$. This proves (e) and all clauses. [A1, F1, F2, F4, step 2.1] ∎
