## Statement

Let $p\in\mathbb Q[t]$ be monic, irreducible, and self-reciprocal, with $p(0)\ne0$ and $p\ne t-1$. Put $E=\mathbb Q[t]/(p)$, write $\alpha=t+(p)$, and give $E$ the involution $\iota(\alpha)=\alpha^{-1}$. If $p\ne t+1$, the primary component $G_p$ of the rational isometric Witt group is isomorphic to the hermitian Witt group of $E$ with this involution. Here the latter group is generated by finite-dimensional nonsingular hermitian forms, with orthogonal direct sum as addition and forms having an isotropic half-dimensional $E$-subspace set to zero. If $p=t+1$, the same conclusion holds with the group generated by even-dimensional nonsingular symmetric forms over $\mathbb Q$, modulo metabolic forms. For a semisimple primary isometry, the comparison sends a hermitian form $h$ to $(\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}h,\alpha)$ on its underlying rational vector space. These comparisons require no choice principle.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial $p$, the finite field extension $E$, and a rational isometric structure $(W,B,T)$ whose minimal polynomial is a power $p^a$.

[F1] Invariant isotropic reduction $L^\perp/L$ preserves the Witt class and detects metabolicity; a rational isometric Witt class is zero exactly when its structure is metabolic. [[lem-isotropic-reduction-of-rational-isometric-structures]]

[F2] Restriction gives the reciprocal-primary direct-sum decomposition and identifies $G_p$ with the Witt group of its primary structures. Self-reciprocal $p\ne t\pm1$ has even degree. [[thm-rational-isometric-witt-group-has-reciprocal-primary-decomposition]]

[F3] The field trace pairing of a finite separable extension is nondegenerate. [[lem-trace-pairing-for-a-finite-separable-extension]]

[A1] Every algebra, polynomial factorization, iteration, and choice of basis in this proof is finite. No algebraic closure or infinite choice is used.

## Proof

**Given:** The primary structure above; the zero-dimensional case is understood as the zero object.

1.1 Suppose $W\ne0$. The algebra $A=\mathbb Q[T]$ is $\mathbb Q[t]/(p^a)$ and its ideal $I=(p(T))$ is nilpotent, with $A/I=E$. Since $p$ is irreducible in characteristic zero, $p'$ is nonzero and relatively prime to $p$. Starting with $s_0=T$, form the finite Newton iteration $s_{j+1}=s_j-p(s_j)p'(s_j)^{-1}$. All inverses exist in $A$: modulo $I$, $s_j$ equals $\alpha$ and $p'(\alpha)\ne0$, so $p'(s_j)$ is invertible modulo $I$; lift its inverse by the finite geometric series for a nilpotent error. The polynomial Taylor identity shows that $p(s_{j+1})$ belongs to the square of the ideal generated by $p(s_j)$. Since $I^a=0$, finitely many iterations give $S\in A$ with $p(S)=0$ and $S-T\in I$. This lift is unique: for two such roots $S,S'$, the factor $H$ in $p(S)-p(S')=(S-S')H$ reduces modulo $I$ to $p'(\alpha)\ne0$, so $H$ is a unit and $S=S'$. The minimal polynomial of $S$ is $p$, hence $S$ is semisimple; it and $I-S$ are invertible since neither $0$ nor $1$ is a root of $p$. [A1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $X\mapsto X^*$ denote the adjoint for $B$. The isometry condition gives $T^*=T^{-1}$, so adjunction preserves the commutative algebra $A$ and its ideal $I$; the latter follows from $p(T^{-1})=p(0)T^{-\deg p}p(T)$. Modulo $I$, adjunction induces the well-defined field involution $\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{-1}$. Both $S^*$ and $S^{-1}$ are roots of $p$ reducing to $\alpha^{-1}$ modulo $I$. The same uniqueness argument from step 1.1, with $\alpha^{-1}$ in place of $\alpha$, gives $S^*=S^{-1}$. Thus $S$ is a $B$-isometry. Put $U=S^{-1}T$. It commutes with $S$, satisfies $U-I\in I$, and has $U^*=U^{-1}$. The finite nilpotent logarithm $Z=\log U=\sum_{j\ge1}(-1)^{j+1}(U-I)^j/j$ therefore satisfies $Z^*=-Z$, by the polynomial identity $\log(U^{-1})=-\log U$ in the nilpotent algebra. Moreover $Z=0$ exactly when $U=I$, since $Z=(U-I)$ times a polynomial with constant term one, which is a unit. [step 1.1, construct, algebra]

3.1 If $Z\ne0$ has nilpotence index $m\ge2$, set $k=\lceil m/2\rceil$ and $L=\operatorname{im}Z^k$. It is nonzero since $k<m$, is invariant under both $S$ and $T$, and is isotropic since $B(Z^kx,Z^ky)=(-1)^kB(x,Z^{2k}y)=0$. Reduce by $L$. By [F1] this preserves the Witt class of $(W,B,T)$ and also that of $(W,B,S)$. The quotient retains the descended commuting operators $S,T,U,Z$, with $S$ semisimple, $T=SU$, $Z^*=-Z$, and $Z=\log U$. Its dimension has decreased by $2\dim L>0$. Repeating while $Z\ne0$ terminates in finite dimension at a common quotient with $T=S$. Applying [F1] at every step proves $[W,B,T]=[W,B,S]$. This proves removal of the unipotent part without invoking a classification of forms. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 The semisimple operator $S$ makes $W$ an $E$-vector space by $\alpha x=Sx$, and its adjoint is the field involution $\iota$. The extension $E/\mathbb Q$ is separable because $p$ is irreducible in characteristic zero, so [F3] gives, for every $x,y\in W$, a unique $h(x,y)\in E$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(a h(x,y))=B(ax,y)$ for every $a\in E$. Uniqueness proves $h(bx,y)=b h(x,y)$ and $h(x,by)=\iota(b)h(x,y)$: apply the defining trace identity and the adjoint relation $B(ax,by)=B(\iota(b)ax,y)$. Symmetry of $B$ and invariance of field trace under $\iota$ give $h(y,x)=\iota(h(x,y))$ by the same test against all $a$. Thus $h$ is hermitian, and $B=\operatorname{Tr}h$ by taking $a=1$. It is nonsingular: an $h$-radical vector would be a $B$-radical vector. Conversely, starting with nonsingular $h$, the trace form $B$ is nonsingular: if $h(x,y)=c\ne0$, choose $a\in E$ with $\operatorname{Tr}(ac)\ne0$ by [F3], so $B(ax,y)\ne0$; equivalently a nonzero $x$ cannot lie in the $B$-radical, since that radical is stable under all $a$ by the adjoint relation. Multiplication by $\alpha$ is a $B$-isometry, and both it and $I-\alpha$ are invertible. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

4.1 A rational subspace invariant under $S$ is exactly an $E$-subspace, because $E=\mathbb Q[S]$. On such a subspace $L$, vanishing of $B$ is equivalent to vanishing of $h$: if $B|_L=0$, then $B(ax,y)=0$ for every $a\in E$ and $x,y\in L$, so the nondegenerate trace pairing forces $h(x,y)=0$; the reverse implication follows from $B=\operatorname{Tr}h$. Half-dimension over $\mathbb Q$ is equivalent to half-dimension over $E$. Thus the trace correspondence preserves metabolicity and direct sums. If $T$ is not semisimple but is metabolic, its metabolizer is invariant under $S$ because $S$ is polynomial in $T$; its image under this correspondence is consequently a hermitian metabolizer. The finite Newton lift is natural under isometries and direct sums by its uniqueness, so the assignment to the hermitian Witt class is well-defined on the primary Witt relations. Conversely the trace construction is well-defined on hermitian Witt relations. It recovers the same hermitian form from a semisimple structure by step 3.2; in the other direction it recovers $[W,B,T]$ by step 3.1. These maps are inverse group isomorphisms. For $p\ne t+1$, its even degree from [F2] makes every finite $E$-dimension admissible in the rational even-dimensional group. For $p=t+1$, $E=\mathbb Q$, $\iota$ is the identity, and the required rational even dimension is exactly the even-dimensional symmetric-form restriction stated above. This proves all cases. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra] ∎

## Remarks

The theorem reduces rational primary components to arithmetic hermitian Witt groups. It does not compute those arithmetic groups, prove Landherr's theorem, or identify the integral algebraic concordance group.
