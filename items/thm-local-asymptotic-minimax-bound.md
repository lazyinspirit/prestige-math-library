## Statement

Assume AC and let $d\ge1$ be finite. Let $\{P_{n,h}:h\in\mathbb R^d\}$ be a finite-dimensional
experiment that is LAN at $h=0$ with positive-definite information $K$ and
central sequence $\Delta_n\Rightarrow N_d(0,K)$ as in
[[def-lan-experiment]]. Let $M\ge0$ be finite and
$L:\mathbb R^d\to[0,M]$ be Borel measurable,
symmetric ($L(-x)=L(x)$), and quasi-convex (each sublevel set is convex).
Then
$$ \liminf_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty} \inf_{\widehat h_n}\sup_{\|h\|_2\le c} E_{P_{n,h}}L(\widehat h_n-h) \ge E L(Z),\qquad Z\sim N_d(0,K^{-1}). $$
The infimum ranges over measurable, possibly randomized estimators. The
experiment is pointwise LAN in each fixed $h$; no uniform likelihood-field
assumption is added.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the pointwise LAN experiment with $K\succ0$; and the stated
bounded, Borel, symmetric quasi-convex loss.

[A1] [[def-axiom-of-choice]]: AC is assumed by the Gaussian and LAN
prerequisites used here.

[F1] [[def-lan-experiment]]: For each fixed $h$, there are mean-one likelihood
proxies with pointwise log-likelihood expansion and total-variation
approximation to the actual local law.

[F2] [[lem-finite-prior-lan-bayes-envelope-convergence]]: For a finite prior
and a bounded Lipschitz loss, pointwise LAN makes the actual finite-prior
Bayes values converge to those in the Gaussian shift.

[F3] [[lem-finite-priors-approximate-gaussian-shift-bayes-risk]]: In the
Gaussian shift, the Bayes value under any prior can be approximated
arbitrarily closely by a finite-support prior, uniformly over decision rules.

[F4] [[lem-bowl-shaped-loss-has-centered-lipschitz-minorants]]:
For every $N\ge2$ and $m\ge1$ it constructs a bounded Lipschitz minorant
$g_{N,m}\le L$ and proves its Gaussian shift risk is minimized at the center;
it also gives $\lim_{N\to\infty}\lim_{m\to\infty}E g_{N,m}(Z)=E L(Z)$.

[F5] [[def-loss-function-and-risk]] and [[def-bayes-and-minimax-risk]]: A
rule's risk is its expected loss and its prior risk is $r_\pi(\delta)$. In
this proof write $B_{\mathcal E}(\pi,g):=\inf_\delta r_\pi(\delta)$ for the
Bayes value in experiment $\mathcal E$, with the infimum over measurable
randomized estimators; this is local notation, since the supplier defines
$r_\pi$ and the minimax value but does not name a Bayes-value infimum. For any
prior supported in a parameter set, every rule's worst-case risk on that set
is at least its prior risk. Write $B_n$ and $B_G$ for this value in the
$n$th experiment and the Gaussian shift, respectively.

[F6] [[def-multivariate-normal-law]],
[[def-standard-normal-and-normal-laws]],
[[lem-normal-density-has-total-mass-one]], and
[[thm-linear-change-of-variables-for-lebesgue-measure]]: A nondegenerate
Gaussian has its usual Lebesgue density and the stated affine transforms.

[F7] [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]]: The finite Gaussian
first moment is bounded by its finite second moment.

[F8] [[def-euclidean-inner-product]]: $\|\cdot\|_2$ is the Euclidean norm.

[F9] [[def-borel-and-lebesgue-measurable-function-on-rn]]: Borel
measurability on $\mathbb R^d$ uses the sigma-algebra generated by its open
sets.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** compare finite-prior Bayes risks, compute a Gaussian
prior Bayes value, then approximate the loss from below.

1.1 If $M=0$, then $L$ is identically zero and the claimed bound is equality. Assume $M>0$ for the rest of the proof. Fix arbitrary integers $N\ge2$ and $m\ge1$, and set $g=g_{N,m}$ from [F4]; it is bounded Lipschitz and $g\le L$. For each $\rho>0$, set $\Gamma=\rho^{-1}K^{-1}$, and let $\pi_\Gamma=N_d(0,\Gamma)$. Define $V_{n,c}(g)=\inf_{\widehat h_n}\sup_{\|h\|_2\le c} E_{P_{n,h}}g(\widehat h_n-h)$ and $V(g)=\liminf_{c\to\infty}\liminf_{n\to\infty}V_{n,c}(g)$. If a finite prior $\pi_f$ is supported in the radius-$c$ ball, then every worst-case risk is at least its prior average by [F5]. Taking infima gives $V_{n,c}(g)\ge B_n(\pi_f,g)$, the finite-prior Bayes value. [F4, F5, F6, F8, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 In the Gaussian shift, $Y\mid h\sim N_d(h,K^{-1})$. Multiplying its likelihood by the prior density $N_d(0,\Gamma)$ and completing the square gives $H\mid Y\sim N_d(SK Y,S),\qquad S=(K+\Gamma^{-1})^{-1}.$ For the fixed constructed loss $g=g_{N,m}$, [F4] gives that the posterior risk is minimized at $SKY$, with value $E g(N_d(0,S))$ independent of $Y$. The action $Y\mapsto SKY$ is measurable and attains this value, so $B_G(\pi_\Gamma,g)=E g(N_d(0,S))$. [A1, F4, F6, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 Let $C_f=\max\{\|h\|_2:h\in\operatorname{supp}(\pi_f)\}$. For every $c\ge C_f$, [F2] gives $B_n(\pi_f,g)\to B_G(\pi_f,g)$, where $B_G$ is the Bayes value in the Gaussian shift. Hence $V(g)\ge B_G(\pi_f,g)$. Since this holds for every finite prior, the bound also holds for any value approximated by finite-prior Bayes values. [F1, F2, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 For every $\eta>0$, [F3] supplies a finite prior $\pi_f$ whose Gaussian-shift Bayes value differs from $B_G(\pi_\Gamma,g)$ by less than $\eta$. Step 2.2 therefore gives $V(g)\ge B_G(\pi_\Gamma,g)-\eta$. Let $\eta\downarrow0$ to obtain $V(g)\ge B_G(\pi_\Gamma,g)$. [F3, step 1.1, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 Here $S=(1+\rho)^{-1}K^{-1}$. Couple $N_d(0,S)$ as $(1+\rho)^{-1/2}Z$ for $Z\sim N_d(0,K^{-1})$. If $B_g$ is a Lipschitz constant for $g$, then $\left|E g((1+\rho)^{-1/2}Z)-E g(Z)\right| \le B_g\left(1-(1+\rho)^{-1/2}\right)E\|Z\|_2\to0$ as $\rho\downarrow0$ by [F7]. By steps 3.1 and 2.1, for each $\rho>0$ we have $V(g)\ge E g((1+\rho)^{-1/2}Z)$. Since the construction applies for every $\rho>0$, taking $\rho\downarrow0$ yields $V(g)\ge E g(Z)$. [F6, F7, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Since $g\le L$, every estimator's risk under $L$ dominates its risk under this fixed $g$, so the local minimax value for $L$ is at least $V(g)$. Step 4.1 gives the lower bound $E g_{N,m}(Z)$. The choices $N,m$ in step 1.1 were arbitrary; first let $m\to\infty$ and then $N\to\infty$. By [F4] these lower bounds converge to $E L(Z)$. This is the stated inequality. [F4, F9, step 1.1, step 4.1, given, algebra] ∎
