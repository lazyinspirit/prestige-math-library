## Definition

A **root datum** is a quadruple $(X,\Phi,X^\vee,\Phi^\vee)$ consisting of free $\mathbb Z$-modules $X,X^\vee$ of finite rank in perfect duality $\langle\ ,\ \rangle:X\times X^\vee\to\mathbb Z$, finite subsets $\Phi\subseteq X$ and $\Phi^\vee\subseteq X^\vee$, and a bijection $\alpha\mapsto\alpha^\vee$ from $\Phi$ to $\Phi^\vee$, subject to (rd1) $\langle\alpha,\alpha^\vee\rangle=2$ for all $\alpha\in\Phi$; (rd2) the reflections $s_\alpha(x)=x-\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle\alpha$ and $s_{\alpha^\vee}(y)=y-\langle\alpha,y\rangle\alpha^\vee$ satisfy $s_\alpha(\Phi)\subseteq\Phi$ and $s_{\alpha^\vee}(\Phi^\vee)\subseteq\Phi^\vee$; (rd3) the group $W(R)$ generated by the $s_\alpha$ acting on $X$ is finite. The root datum is **reduced** if $\mathbb Q\alpha\cap\Phi=\{\pm\alpha\}$ for every $\alpha\in\Phi$. The group $W(R)$ is the **Weyl group**, $\Phi$ the root system and $\Phi^\vee$ the coroot system; $W(R)$ acts dually on $X^\vee$ by the $s_{\alpha^\vee}$, and $s_\alpha$ fixes the hyperplane $\langle\ ,\alpha^\vee\rangle=0$. For $V_{\mathbb R}=X\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb R$, the **Weyl chambers** are the connected components of $V_{\mathbb R}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{\alpha\in\Phi}\{x:\langle x,\alpha^\vee\rangle=0\}$, and a **base** is a linearly independent subset $\Delta\subseteq\Phi$ such that every root is a $\mathbb Z$-combination of $\Delta$ with all coefficients of one sign. A root datum is **semisimple** if $Z\Phi$ has finite index in $X$.

The reflection $s_\alpha$ depends only on the pair $(\alpha,\alpha^\vee)$: it fixes the hyperplane $\langle\ ,\alpha^\vee\rangle=0$, sends $\alpha$ to $-\alpha$ by (rd1), and is determined by those two conditions on the rational direct sum $\mathbb Q\alpha\oplus\ker(\langle\ ,\alpha^\vee\rangle:X\otimes\mathbb Q\to\mathbb Q)$. The lattice form above is related to the Euclidean root-system theory of [[def-weyl-group-of-a-root-system]] and [[def-positive-system-and-base-of-simple-roots]] through a $W$-invariant form on $V_{\mathbb R}$, but no choice of invariant inner product belongs to the definition. The perfect character–cocharacter pairing for a split torus is supplied by [[lem-character-and-cocharacter-lattices-of-a-split-torus]]; roots and coroots are additional data, not supplied by that lattice lemma.

An **isomorphism of root data** $R=(X,\Phi,X^\vee,\Phi^\vee)\to R_1=(X_1,\Phi_1,X_1^\vee,\Phi_1^\vee)$ is a pair of $\mathbb Z$-linear isomorphisms $f:X\to X_1$ and $f^\vee:X_1^\vee\to X^\vee$ that are transpose to one another, $\langle f(x),y_1\rangle=\langle x,f^\vee(y_1)\rangle$ for all $x\in X$, $y_1\in X_1^\vee$, and that carry $\Phi$ bijectively onto $\Phi_1$ with $f(\alpha)^\vee=f^\vee{} ^{-1}(\alpha^\vee)$ for every $\alpha\in\Phi$. Composites of isomorphisms are isomorphisms, with the evident inverses, so root data form a category whose isomorphisms are these pairs; this is the notion of isomorphism used when separating the root data of SL_2 and PGL_2.
