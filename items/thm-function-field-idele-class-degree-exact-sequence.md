## Statement

Assume AC.

Let $F=\mathbb F_q(C)$ be the function field of a smooth proper geometrically
integral curve with exact constant field $\mathbb F_q$. Normalize each
valuation $\operatorname{ord}_v$ to have value group $\mathbb Z$, put
$\deg(v)=[\kappa(v):\mathbb F_q]$, and define
$\deg(a)=\sum_v\deg(v)\operatorname{ord}_v(a_v)$ for an idele
$a=(a_v)_v$. This is well-defined on the idele class group
$C_F=\mathbb A_F^\times/F^\times$, is surjective, and has compact kernel
$C_F^0$. Consequently
$$0\longrightarrow C_F^0\longrightarrow C_F
\xrightarrow{\deg}\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0$$
is exact and admits a continuous splitting.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the function field, curve, valuations, and ideles specified
in the Statement.

[F1] The product formula gives
$\sum_v\deg(v)\operatorname{ord}_v(x)=0$ for every $x\in F^\times$.
([[thm-product-formula-for-function-fields]])

[F2] The subgroup generated by the closed-place degrees is $\mathbb Z$.
([[thm-closed-place-degrees-have-gcd-one-for-full-constant-function-field]])

[F3] The degree-zero divisor class group of $C/\mathbb F_q$ is finite.
([[thm-finiteness-of-degree-zero-divisor-class-group-over-finite-field]])

[F4] Each local unit group $\mathcal O_v^\times$ is compact, and the restricted
product of local multiplicative groups defines the idele group and its class
group. ([[thm-local-valuation-ring-compact-open]],
[[def-idele-group-of-a-global-field]],
[[def-idele-class-group-of-a-global-field]])

[F5] The product of any family of compact spaces is compact under AC.
([[thm-tychonoff]])

[F6] A global function field has a full finite constant field, its places
are the closed points of the proper curve, and the place degree is the
residue-field degree. ([[def-global-field]],
[[def-place-of-a-global-field]], [[def-finite-field-and-its-order]])

[F7] Divisors are finite integral sums of closed points, their degree is the
weighted residue-degree sum, and the divisor class group is the quotient by
principal divisors. ([[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]],
[[def-divisor-smooth-proper-curve]],
[[def-principal-weil-divisor-and-class-group]])

[F8] The diagonal field is closed in the idele group and the idele group is
locally compact with its restricted-product topology.
([[thm-global-field-idelic-diagonal-discrete-and-closed]],
[[thm-global-field-adeles-and-ideles-locally-compact]])

[A1] AC is assumed for [F5]. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data in the Statement and Facts & Assumptions.

1.1 An idele has unit components at all but finitely many places, so the
degree sum is finite. [F4, given]

1.2 Multiplying an idele by $x\in F^\times$ changes the sum by zero by [F1].
Thus degree descends to $C_F$. By [F2], choose a finite divisor
$D=\sum_v n_v[v]$ of degree one and form the idele with component
$\pi_v^{n_v}$ at each of its finitely many places and component $1$ elsewhere.
Its class has degree one, so degree is onto $\mathbb Z$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, choose]

2.1 Let $U_F=\prod_v\mathcal O_v^\times$. By [F4] and [F5], $U_F$ is
compact. The valuation map gives an exact sequence
$$1\to U_F/\mathbb F_q^\times\to C_F^0\to\operatorname{Cl}^0(C)\to0.$$
The kernel consists of unit ideles modulo the global units
$H^0(C,\mathcal O_C^\times)=\mathbb F_q^\times$, and every degree-zero
divisor class has an idele representative. [A1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.2]

2.2 The left group in step 2.1 is compact, and the right group is finite by
[F3]. Therefore $C_F^0$ is a finite union of compact cosets and is compact.
The kernel and surjectivity already proved give exactness. Lifting the
degree-one idele from step 1.2 defines the continuous section
$n\mapsto[a]^n$ from the discrete group $\mathbb Z$. [F3, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
