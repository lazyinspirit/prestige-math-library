## Definition

Let $F$ be an infinite field and write $F^\times_{\mathbb Q}=F^\times\otimes_{\mathbb Z}\mathbb Q$. Set $B_1(F)=F^\times_{\mathbb Q}$. Recursively, for $n\ge2$, let $V_n(F)=\mathbb Q[\mathbb P^1(F)]$ and define

$$\delta_n:V_n(F)\longrightarrow\begin{cases}\Lambda^2_{\mathbb Q}F^\times_{\mathbb Q},&n=2,\\B_{n-1}(F)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}F^\times_{\mathbb Q},&n>2\end{cases}$$

on generators by $\delta_2([x])=(1-x)\wedge x$ and $\delta_n([x])=[x]_{n-1}\otimes x$ for $n>2$, with $[0]=[\infty]=0$ and $\delta_n([1])=0$. For $a\in\mathbb P^1(F)$, let $\phi_a:V_n(F(t))\to V_n(F)$ be projective evaluation. Let $R_n(F)$ be generated by $[0]$ and all $\phi_a(u)-\phi_b(u)$ with $u\in\ker\delta_n$ over $F(t)$ and $a,b\in\mathbb P^1(F)$. Define

$$B_n^{\mathrm{GR}}(F):=V_n(F)/R_n(F).$$

The natural map $\delta_n$ descends to the quotient. Its weight-four complex is

$$B_4^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)\xrightarrow{\delta_4}B_3^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}F^\times_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\delta_3\otimes1}B_2^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)\otimes_{\mathbb Q}\Lambda^2_{\mathbb Q}F^\times_{\mathbb Q}\xrightarrow{\delta_2\otimes1}\Lambda^4_{\mathbb Q}F^\times_{\mathbb Q}.$$

The degree-one cohomology of this complex is $\ker\delta_4$.

This is the rationalized convention used in the weight-four formula below; it is kept distinct from Zagier's $\mathcal B_m(F)$ until an explicit comparison is proved.

## Proof

1.1 By induction on $n$, the displayed generator assignment has its target in the previously defined group $B_{n-1}(F)\otimes F^\times_{\mathbb Q}$ for $n>2$, and in $\Lambda^2F^\times_{\mathbb Q}$ for $n=2$. The stated values at $0,1,\infty$ make the assignment compatible with the projective-symbol convention, so it extends linearly to $V_n(F)$. [F1, given, construct]

2.1 Projective evaluation commutes with each generator formula for $\delta_n$. Thus if $u\in\ker\delta_n$ over $F(t)$, both $\phi_a(u)$ and $\phi_b(u)$ lie in $\ker\delta_n$ over $F$, so their difference does too. The additional generator $[0]$ also lies in the kernel by the displayed convention. Therefore $R_n(F)\subseteq\ker\delta_n$, and $\delta_n$ descends to $B_n^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)$. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Substituting $n=4$ into the recursive differential gives the first map $B_4^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)\to B_3^{\mathrm{GR}}(F)\otimes F^\times_{\mathbb Q}$; the next two maps are induced by $\delta_3$ and $\delta_2$. Since the complex has no incoming differential in degree one, its degree-one cohomology is exactly $\ker\delta_4$. [given, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
