## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Take
$\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_3$ with simple roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, positive
roots $\Phi^+=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_2\}$, fundamental weight
$\omega_1$ dual to $\alpha_1^\vee$ and Weyl vector
$\rho=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$. The Weyl dimension formula
([[thm-weyl-dimension-formula]]) gives
$$\dim L(\omega_1)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\frac{(\omega_1+\rho,\alpha)}{(\rho,\alpha)}=\frac{\langle\omega_1+\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle}\cdot\frac{\langle\omega_1+\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle}\cdot\frac{\langle\omega_1+\rho,\theta^\vee\rangle}{\langle\rho,\theta^\vee\rangle}=\frac{2}{1}\cdot\frac{1}{1}\cdot\frac{3}{2}=3,$$
matching the three-dimensional defining representation of
$\mathfrak{sl}_3$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, the realization of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ with
simple roots $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$, positive roots
$\Phi^+=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\theta\}$ with $\theta=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, the
fundamental weight $\omega_1$, the Weyl vector $\rho$ and the coroots
$\alpha_1^\vee,\alpha_2^\vee,\theta^\vee$.

[A1] The Axiom of Choice is assumed; it enters through the dimension formula
and the highest-weight suppliers below ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] In this realization the positive roots are $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ and
$\theta=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, whose coroots in the simply-laced system add:
$\theta^\vee=\alpha_1^\vee+\alpha_2^\vee$
([[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]],
[[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]],
[[thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]]).

[F2] The fundamental weight $\omega_1$ is dual to $\alpha_1^\vee$:
$\langle\omega_1,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle=1$ and
$\langle\omega_1,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=0$, and the Weyl vector satisfies
$\langle\rho,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle=1$ and
$\rho=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$; moreover
$\langle\rho,\theta^\vee\rangle=2$
([[def-fundamental-weights]],
[[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]],
[[thm-the-root-set-is-a-reduced-crystallographic-root-system]]).

[F3] The Weyl dimension formula states
$\dim L(\lambda)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)/(\rho,\alpha)=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^+}\langle\lambda+\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle/\langle\rho,\alpha^\vee\rangle$
([[thm-weyl-dimension-formula]]).

[F4] The defining representation $\mathbb C^3$ of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ has
weights $\varepsilon_1,\varepsilon_2,\varepsilon_3$, with $\varepsilon_1$
the highest weight $\omega_1$; it is irreducible, because for a nonzero
$v=\sum_ic_ie_i$ with $c_j\ne0$ the matrix units $E_{ij}$ with $i\ne j$ and
the diagonal elements of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ produce all three basis vectors
$e_1,e_2,e_3$, so the submodule generated by $v$ is everything; hence
$L(\omega_1)\cong\mathbb C^3$ has dimension $3$
([[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]],
[[thm-highest-weight-classification-of-finite-dimensional-irreducible-representations]]).

## Verification

1.1 By [F2] the three coroot pairings of the numerator are $\langle\omega_1+\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle=1+1=2$, $\langle\omega_1+\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=0+1=1$ and, using $\theta^\vee=\alpha_1^\vee+\alpha_2^\vee$ from [F1], $\langle\omega_1+\rho,\theta^\vee\rangle=2+1=3$, while the denominators are $\langle\rho,\alpha_1^\vee\rangle=\langle\rho,\alpha_2^\vee\rangle=1$ and $\langle\rho,\theta^\vee\rangle=2$. [F1, F2, A1]

2.1 Substituting these six values into the product of [F3] gives $\dim L(\omega_1)=(2/1)(1/1)(3/2)=3$. [F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The defining module $\mathbb C^3$ is irreducible of highest weight $\omega_1$ by [F4], so $L(\omega_1)$ has dimension $3$, in agreement with the product computed in step 2.1; this checks the normalisation of the product. [F4, step 2.1] ∎
