## Statement

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let
$$ P_0\subsetneq P_1\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_n $$
be a strict prime chain in $R[x]$. Then the length $n$ is at most $\dim R+1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$ and a strict prime chain $P_0\subsetneq\cdots\subsetneq P_n=P$ in $R[x]$.

[L1] In the local ring $R_{\mathfrak q}$, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$ can be made minimal over $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)$ generators, where $\mathfrak q=P\cap R$ ([[thm-krull-height-theorem-converse]], [[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]).

[L2] A prime minimal over an ideal generated by $r$ elements has height at most $r$ ([[thm-krull-height-theorem]]).

[L3] If $\mathfrak q$ is prime, then $(R/\mathfrak q)[x]$ is a polynomial ring over the domain $R/\mathfrak q$, and after localizing at the nonzero elements of that domain its nonzero prime ideals become nonzero prime ideals of a PID and hence are minimal over one generator ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]], [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Let $\mathfrak q=P\cap R$. Localizing the given chain at $\mathfrak q$ preserves its length, so it is enough to bound the height of the prime $P_{\mathfrak q}\subset R_{\mathfrak q}[x]$. By [L1], after relabelling $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)=r$ and choosing $a_1,\ldots,a_r\in\mathfrak q$, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$ is minimal over $J=(a_1/1,\ldots,a_r/1)$. [L1, given]

2.1 If $P_{\mathfrak q}=\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$, then $P_{\mathfrak q}$ is minimal over $JR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$: any prime of $R_{\mathfrak q}[x]$ containing $J$ contracts to a prime of $R_{\mathfrak q}$ containing $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$, hence to $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$ itself. Therefore [L2] gives $\operatorname{ht}(P_{\mathfrak q})\le r$. [L1, L2, step 1.1]

2.2 Suppose now that $P_{\mathfrak q}\neq\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$. Then $P_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$ is a nonzero prime ideal of $\kappa(\mathfrak q)[x]$, where $\kappa(\mathfrak q)=\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak q)$. By [L3], choose a lift $f\in R_{\mathfrak q}[x]$ whose image generates that nonzero prime of $\kappa(\mathfrak q)[x]$. Any prime $Q\subseteq P_{\mathfrak q}$ containing $J$ and $f$ has contraction containing $J$, hence equal to $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$ by step 1.1; modulo $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$, the prime $Q/\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$ contains the generator of $P_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}[x]$, so it equals that prime. Therefore $Q=P_{\mathfrak q}$, and $P_{\mathfrak q}$ is minimal over the ideal $(J,f)$ generated by $r+1$ elements. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 By [L2], step 2.2 gives $\operatorname{ht}(P_{\mathfrak q})\le r+1=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)+1\le \dim R+1$. Since the localized chain still has length $n$, we have $n\le \operatorname{ht}(P_{\mathfrak q})\le \dim R+1$. Steps 2.1 and 3.1 cover both cases. [L2, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Therefore every strict prime chain in $R[x]$ has length at most $\dim R+1$. [step 3.1] ∎
