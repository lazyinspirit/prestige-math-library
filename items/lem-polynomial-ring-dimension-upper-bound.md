## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring and let
$P\in\operatorname{Spec}(R[x])$ contract to $\mathfrak q=P\cap R$ of finite
height $r$. Then
$$\operatorname{ht}(P)=\begin{cases}r,&P=\mathfrak qR[x],\\r+1,&P\ne\mathfrak qR[x].\end{cases}$$
If $R\ne0$, consequently every strict prime chain in $R[x]$ has length at most
$\dim R+1$, with the usual extended-integer interpretation if $\dim R$ is
infinite.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice, a Noetherian commutative ring $R$, and a prime $P$ of $R[x]$ over $\mathfrak q$ of finite height $r$. The Axiom of Choice is [[def-axiom-of-choice]]; it is spent in [L1] and [L2].

[L1] Assuming the Axiom of Choice, the maximal ideal $\mathfrak qR_{\mathfrak q}$ in the Noetherian local ring $R_{\mathfrak q}$ is minimal over an ideal generated by $r=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q)$ elements ([[thm-krull-height-theorem-converse]], [[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]], [[thm-localisation-at-a-prime-is-local]], [[thm-noetherian-ring-quotients-and-localisations]]).

[L2] Assuming the Axiom of Choice, a prime minimal over an ideal generated by $m\ge1$ elements in a Noetherian ring has height at most $m$ ([[thm-krull-height-theorem]]). The polynomial ring $B[x]$ is Noetherian when $B$ is Noetherian ([[thm-hilbert-basis-theorem]]).

[L3] If $\mathfrak q$ is prime, then $(R/\mathfrak q)[x]$ is a polynomial ring over the domain $R/\mathfrak q$, and after localizing at the nonzero elements of that domain its nonzero prime ideals become nonzero prime ideals of a PID and hence are minimal over one generator ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]], [[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Put $S=R\setminus\mathfrak q$, $B=R_{\mathfrak q}$, $\mathfrak m=\mathfrak qB$, and $P'=S^{-1}P\subset B[x]$. Every prime below $P$ contracts into $\mathfrak q$ and is disjoint from $S$, so localization preserves all chains below $P$ and $\operatorname{ht}(P)=\operatorname{ht}(P')$. Contraction from $B[x]$ also shows that $P'=\mathfrak mB[x]$ exactly when $P=\mathfrak qR[x]$. Likewise $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak m)=r$. Under the assumed Choice hypothesis, [L1] supplies $a_1,\ldots,a_r\in\mathfrak m$ such that $\mathfrak m$ is minimal over $J=(a_1,\ldots,a_r)$ in $B$. Because $B$ is local with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$, it is the only prime of $B$ containing $J$. [L1, given]

2.1 If $P'=\mathfrak mB[x]$, any prime $Q\subseteq P'$ containing $JB[x]$ contracts to a prime of $B$ containing $J$, hence to $\mathfrak m$ by step 1.1. Thus $Q$ contains $\mathfrak mB[x]=P'$ and equals it, proving that $P'$ is minimal over $JB[x]$. For $r\ge1$, [L2] gives $\operatorname{ht}(P')\le r$; for $r=0$, $P'$ is a minimal prime of $B[x]$ because $\mathfrak m$ is the only prime of $B$, so its height is zero. Conversely a chain of length $r$ below $\mathfrak q$ extends coefficientwise to a chain of prime ideals below $\mathfrak qR[x]$, giving $\operatorname{ht}(P')\ge r$. Therefore this case has height exactly $r$. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 If $P'\ne\mathfrak mB[x]$, its image $P'/\mathfrak mB[x]$ is a nonzero prime of the PID $(B/\mathfrak m)[x]$. By [L3], choose $f\in P'$ lifting a generator of that prime. Any prime $Q\subseteq P'$ containing $J$ and $f$ contracts to $\mathfrak m$ by step 1.1, hence contains $\mathfrak mB[x]$. Its image modulo $\mathfrak mB[x]$ contains the generator of $P'/\mathfrak mB[x]$, and containment in $P'$ forces equality. So $Q=P'$ and $P'$ is minimal over $(J,f)$, which has $r+1\ge1$ generators. By [L2], $\operatorname{ht}(P')\le r+1$. The extended coefficientwise chain below $\mathfrak qR[x]$, followed by the strict inclusion $\mathfrak qR[x]\subsetneq P$, gives the reverse inequality. Hence $\operatorname{ht}(P)=r+1$. [L1, L2, L3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 For $R\ne0$, consider any strict chain ending at a prime $P$ of $R[x]$. If $\operatorname{ht}(P\cap R)$ is finite, steps 2.1–2.2 bound its length by $\operatorname{ht}(P\cap R)+1\le\dim R+1$. If the contraction has infinite height, then $\dim R=\infty$ and the claimed extended-integer bound is automatic. [step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Thus the exact relative height formula and the original chain bound both hold under the stated Choice hypothesis. [step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1] ∎
