## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice and let $L$ be a nonempty oriented $r$-component link with $r\ge1$ and maximal-abelian-cover module $A_L$ over $\Lambda_r=\mathbb Z[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_r^{\pm1}]$. Then $A_L$ is finitely presented, every elementary/Fitting ideal $E_j(A_L)$ is independent of the chosen finite triangulation and of the chosen lifts of its cells, and the order $\operatorname{ord}(A_L)$—the greatest common divisor of the generators of $E_q(A_L)$, where $q=\dim_{\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda_r)}(A_L\otimes_{\Lambda_r}\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda_r))$—is defined up to a unit $\pm t_1^{k_1}\cdots t_r^{k_r}$. The diagonal ring map $\eta:\Lambda_r\to\Lambda_1=\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]$, $t_i\mapsto t$, specializes the lifted chain complex by coefficient extension to the total-linking cyclic-cover chain complex. This statement concerns the chain complex only; it does not identify $A_L\otimes_{\Lambda_r,\eta}\Lambda_1$ with the first homology of the specialized complex. For $r\ge2$, the diagonal map is nonflat and such an identification need not hold because homology need not commute with nonflat base change.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; a nonempty oriented $r$-component link $L$ with $r\ge1$; its maximal-abelian-cover module $A_L$; $\Lambda_r=\mathbb Z[t_1^{\pm1},\ldots,t_r^{\pm1}]$; a basepoint $b\in E_L$, and the first Hurewicz map $\phi_E:\pi_1(E_L,b)\to H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)$.

[A1] AC, as assumed by the link-complement and one-variable-cover suppliers ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F1] The maximal-abelian-cover module is $A_L=H_1(X_L^{\mathrm{ab}};\mathbb Z)$ with its $\Lambda_r$ deck action ([[def-maximal-abelian-cover-alexander-module]]).

[F2] The ring $\Lambda_r$ is Noetherian and a UFD, with units exactly signed Laurent monomials ([[lem-multivariable-laurent-ring-noetherian-ufd]]).

[F3] For a finitely presented module, the $j$th elementary ideal is the ideal generated by all minors of the prescribed size in a finite presentation matrix ([[def-elementary-ideals-of-a-finitely-presented-module]]).

[F4] For every commutative ring and finitely presented module, each elementary ideal is independent of the finite presentation and invariant under module isomorphism ([[lem-elementary-ideals-are-independent-of-the-presentation]]).

[F5] A finite CW structure on $E_L$ lifts to a finite-rank free $\Lambda_r$ cellular chain complex whose first homology is $A_L$ ([[lem-maximal-abelian-cover-cellular-chain-model]]).

[F6] The one-variable Alexander module is first homology of the cover classified by total linking, whose defining map sums the Alexander-dual meridian coordinates ([[def-one-variable-alexander-module-of-an-oriented-link]]).

[F7] Connected covers are classified by their induced fundamental-group subgroups ([[thm-classification-of-connected-covering-spaces]]).

[F8] The maximal abelian cover deformation retracts equivariantly onto the restricted compact-exterior cover ([[lem-maximal-abelian-cover-link-exterior-retraction]]).

[F9] A connected regular cover has deck group the quotient of its fundamental group by the induced subgroup ([[thm-regular-covering-characterizations]], [[cor-deck-group-of-a-regular-covering]]).

## Proof

1.1 Write the lifted finite free complex from [F5] as $C_2\xrightarrow{d_2}C_1\xrightarrow{d_1}C_0$. Since $\Lambda_r$ is Noetherian by [F2], every submodule of a finite free module is finitely generated: induct on the rank $n$ of $\Lambda_r^n$; project a submodule $N$ to the first coordinate, choose lifts of finitely many generators of its image ideal, and apply the induction hypothesis to the kernel in $\Lambda_r^{n-1}$. Thus $Z_1=\ker d_1$ is finitely generated, and $B_1=\operatorname{im}d_2$ is generated by the finitely many columns of $d_2$. The quotient $A_L=Z_1/B_1$ is finitely generated. Choose a surjection $\Lambda_r^m\to A_L$ with finite $m$; its kernel is a submodule of $\Lambda_r^m$ and hence finitely generated by the same argument. This is a finite presentation of $A_L$. [A1, F1, F2, F5, given, construct]

1.2 Let $\bar\eta:H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)\to\mathbb Z$ be the total-linking map. By [F1] the positive meridians are the coordinate basis, so the coordinate-sum definition [F6] sends each one to $1$. Define $\eta:\Lambda_r\to\Lambda_1$ to be its induced ring map $t_i\mapsto t$. By [F7], quotienting the maximal abelian cover of $E_L$ by $\ker\bar\eta$ gives the connected cover classified by $\ker(\bar\eta\circ\phi_E)$; this quotient cover is regular with deck group $H_1(E_L;\mathbb Z)/\ker\bar\eta\cong\mathbb Z$ by [F9]. The equivariant deformation retraction [F8] descends to a deformation retraction of this quotient cover onto the corresponding quotient of the open-complement cover; by [F6] the latter is the one-variable total-linking cover. For every lifted cell orbit, tensoring its free rank-one $\Lambda_r$ summand with $\Lambda_1$ along $\eta$ identifies the quotient-orbit generator with a free rank-one $\Lambda_1$ generator, and sends each boundary coefficient to its image under $\eta$. Therefore $C_*^{\mathrm{cell}}(E_L^{\mathrm{ab}};\mathbb Z)\otimes_{\Lambda_r,\eta}\Lambda_1$ is the cellular chain complex of the total-linking cover of $E_L$. [F1, F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, construct]

2.1 Use the resulting finite presentation to define each $E_j(A_L)$ as in [F3]. Any other triangulation of the same marked exterior lifts to another CW structure on the same topological cover; [F5] identifies the homology of either lifted cellular complex with the same singular module $A_L$, and the deck action in both identifications is the same. A different lift of a cell multiplies its basis element by a deck monomial, also leaving the resulting singular module unchanged. Hence the modules computed from the two choices are isomorphic. By [F4] all their elementary ideals agree. [F1, F3, F4, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 Put $K=\operatorname{Frac}(\Lambda_r)$ and $q=\dim_K(A_L\otimes_{\Lambda_r}K)$. For a finite presentation matrix with $n$ target generators, tensoring with $K$ shows its rank is $n-q$. Therefore at least one $(n-q)\times(n-q)$ minor is nonzero (with the empty minor equal to $1$ when $n=q$), so $E_q(A_L)\ne0$. This ideal has finitely many generators by its finite presentation, and in the UFD [F2] their gcd exists and is unique up to a unit. Since [F4] makes the ideal presentation-independent, the gcd is well-defined up to a unit of $\Lambda_r$, which by [F2] is $\pm t_1^{k_1}\cdots t_r^{k_r}$. [F2, F3, F4, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Homology does not in general commute with this coefficient extension. For $r=1$, $\eta$ is the identity; for $r\ge2$, consider the finite free chain complex $C_1=\Lambda_r\xrightarrow{\,t_1-t_2\,}C_0=\Lambda_r$ with all other chain groups zero. The ring $\Lambda_r$ is a domain and $t_1-t_2\ne0$, so $H_1(C_*)=0$. After diagonal base change $\eta$, the differential is multiplication by $t-t=0$, hence $H_1(C_*\otimes_{\Lambda_r,\eta}\Lambda_1)=\Lambda_1$, while $H_1(C_*)\otimes_{\Lambda_r,\eta}\Lambda_1=0$. The multiplication map $\Lambda_r\xrightarrow{t_1-t_2}\Lambda_r$ is injective before tensoring and becomes the zero map afterward, so $\Lambda_1$ is nonflat over $\Lambda_r$ for $r\ge2$. Thus for $r\ge2$ the chain specialization does not in general identify the specialized chain-complex homology with the tensor product of $A_L$, and no equality of specialized homology orders follows without additional hypotheses. [F2, step 1.2, algebra] ∎
