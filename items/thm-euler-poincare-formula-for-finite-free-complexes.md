## Statement

Let $C_\bullet$ be a bounded chain complex of finite-rank free abelian groups.
Assume that every homology group $H_n(C)$ is free of finite rank. Then
$$\chi(C)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb Z}(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(H_n(C)).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A bounded chain complex $C_\bullet$ of finite-rank free abelian groups whose homology groups are free of finite rank.

[L1] The Euler characteristic is the finite alternating sum $\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(C_n)$ ([[def-euler-characteristic-of-a-finite-complex-of-finite-rank-free-abelian-groups]]).

[L2] Homology is the quotient $Z_n(C)/B_n(C)$ ([[def-homology-object-of-a-chain-complex]]).

[L3] Abelian groups and $\mathbb Z$-modules are the same objects and morphisms ([[prop-abelian-groups-are-z-modules]]).

[L4] The ring $\mathbb Z$ is a principal ideal domain: it is a commutative ring ([[thm-int-comm-ring]]), it has no zero divisors ([[lem-int-cancellation]]), and every ideal is generated by one integer because every subgroup of $(\mathbb Z,+)$ is cyclic ([[lem-subgroups-of-z-are-cyclic]], [[def-principal-ideal-domain]]).

[L5] A submodule of a finite-rank free module over a PID is again finite free ([[cor-submodules-of-finite-free-pid-modules-are-free]]).

[L6] A finite free module over a commutative ring is projective ([[cor-free-modules-are-projective-and-flat]]).

[L7] A short exact sequence ending in a projective module splits ([[thm-projective-module-characterizations]]).

[L8] A free module of rank $r$ has a basis of $r$ elements ([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]], [[def-invariant-basis-number-and-rank-of-a-free-module]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [L3] and [L4], each $C_n$ is a finite-rank free $\mathbb Z$-module over a PID. Therefore [L5] makes every cycle group $Z_n(C)\le C_n$ and every boundary group $B_n(C)\le C_n$ finite free. Since the homology groups are finite free by the hypothesis, all ranks appearing below are defined. [L3, L4, L5, given]

2.1 By [L2], the short exact sequence $$0\to Z_n(C)\to C_n\to B_{n-1}(C)\to0$$ has projective quotient $B_{n-1}(C)$ by steps 1.1 and [L6], so [L7] splits it. Likewise $$0\to B_n(C)\to Z_n(C)\to H_n(C)\to0$$ splits because $H_n(C)$ is finite free by hypothesis and hence projective by [L6]. Using [L8], split exactness gives $$\operatorname{rank}(C_n)=\operatorname{rank}(Z_n(C))+\operatorname{rank}(B_{n-1}(C))$$ and $$\operatorname{rank}(Z_n(C))=\operatorname{rank}(B_n(C))+\operatorname{rank}(H_n(C)).$$ [L2, L6, L7, L8, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Substitute the second identity of step 2.1 into the first: $$\operatorname{rank}(C_n)=\operatorname{rank}(B_n(C))+\operatorname{rank}(H_n(C))+\operatorname{rank}(B_{n-1}(C)).$$ Multiply by $(-1)^n$ and sum over all $n$. The boundedness in [L1] makes this a finite sum, and the two boundary sums cancel after the index shift $m=n-1$: $$\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(B_{n-1}(C)) =-\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(B_n(C)).$$ Hence only the homology contribution remains. [L1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The resulting identity is $$\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(C_n) =\sum_n(-1)^n\operatorname{rank}(H_n(C)),$$ which is exactly the formula in the statement by [L1]. [L1, step 3.1] ∎
