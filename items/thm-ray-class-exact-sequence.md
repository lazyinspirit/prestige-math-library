## Statement

Assume AC and AC_omega. Let $K$ be a number field and let $\mathfrak m=(\mathfrak m_0,S_\infty)$ be a modulus. Set
$$R_{\mathfrak m}=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m_0}(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}})^\times\right)\times\{\pm1\}^{S_\infty},$$
where an empty product is the trivial group. Define
$$\rho(u)=\bigl((u\bmod\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}})_{\mathfrak p},(\operatorname{sgn}(\sigma(u)))_{\sigma\in S_\infty}\bigr)$$
for $u\in\mathcal O_K^\times$. For $r=((\bar a_{\mathfrak p})_{\mathfrak p},(\epsilon_\sigma)_\sigma)\in R_{\mathfrak m}$, choose $\alpha\in K^\times$ with residues $\bar a_{\mathfrak p}$ and signs $\epsilon_\sigma$, and set $\phi(r)=[(\alpha)]_{\mathfrak m}$. Define $\psi([\mathfrak a]_{\mathfrak m})=[\mathfrak a]$ in the ordinary ideal class group. Weak approximation gives the required $\alpha$, and the exact sequence is
$$\mathcal O_K^\times\longrightarrow R_{\mathfrak m}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)\longrightarrow1.$$
Exactness means that at each middle group the kernel of the outgoing map is the image of the incoming map, and the final map is surjective. The image of $\mathcal O_K^\times$ is retained as the kernel; it need not be trivial.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and AC_omega; a number field $K$; a modulus $\mathfrak m$; its selected finite-prime powers and real embeddings.

[F1] The ray class group is the group of fractional ideals prime to $\mathfrak m_0$ modulo the subgroup of principal ideals generated by elements congruent to $1$ at every selected finite prime power and positive at every selected real embedding. ([[def-ray-class-group]], [[lem-ray-class-congruence-subgroup-well-defined]])

[F2] The ordinary ideal class group is the group of nonzero fractional ideals modulo principal fractional ideals, and equality of two principal fractional ideals means their generators differ by a unit of $\mathcal O_K$. ([[def-ideal-class-group-of-a-domain]], [[lem-ideal-class-group-well-defined]], [[def-fractional-ideal]])

[F3] Weak approximation supplies one $\alpha\in K$ satisfying finitely many prescribed open conditions in the completions at distinct finite and real places. ([[thm-weak-approximation-for-global-fields]])

[F4] If $a,b$ are local units and $v_{\mathfrak p}(a-b)\ge n_{\mathfrak p}$, then $a/b\equiv1$ modulo the $n_{\mathfrak p}$-th local maximal-ideal power; real numbers with the same prescribed sign have positive ratio. ([[def-place-of-a-global-field]], [[def-completion-of-a-global-field-at-a-place]], [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]])

[F5] The principal-ideal map is multiplicative and the valuation of a product of fractional ideals is the sum of valuations. ([[def-fractional-ideal]], [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]])

[F6] At a finite number-field place, $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)=v_{\mathfrak p}((x))$ is a valuation on $K$ and $|x|_{\mathfrak p}=(N\mathfrak p)^{-\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)}$. ([[def-place-of-a-global-field]], [[def-prime-ideal-valuations-on-fractional-ideals]], [[def-valuation-on-a-field]])

[F7] For a Dedekind domain with fraction field $K$, each nonzero prime-ideal valuation on principal fractional ideals attains every integer. ([[lem-prime-ideal-valuation-attains-every-integer]])

[F8] The ring of integers $\mathcal O_K$ of a number field is a Dedekind domain under AC. ([[cor-ring-of-integers-is-a-dedekind-domain]])

## Proof

**Given:** The data and assumptions in the Statement.

1.1 Define $\rho:\mathcal O_K^\times\to R_{\mathfrak m}$ by reducing a unit modulo each $\mathfrak p^{n_{\mathfrak p}}$ and recording its sign at each $\sigma\in S_\infty$. This is a homomorphism because reduction and each real embedding preserve multiplication. [F4, given, construct]

1.2 For $u=((\bar a_{\mathfrak p})_{\mathfrak p},(\epsilon_\sigma)_\sigma)\in R_{\mathfrak m}$, if all factors are empty choose $\alpha=1$. Otherwise choose representatives $a_{\mathfrak p}\in\mathcal O_K$ of the unit residue classes. At each finite place require $\alpha-a_{\mathfrak p}$ to have valuation at least $n_{\mathfrak p}$; at each selected real place require $\alpha$ to have sign $\epsilon_\sigma$. These are finitely many open conditions, so [F3] gives $\alpha\in K$ satisfying them. The finite conditions make $\alpha$ a local unit at each prime dividing $\mathfrak m_0$, hence $(\alpha)$ is prime to $\mathfrak m_0$. Define $\phi(u)=[(\alpha)]_{\mathfrak m}$. [F1, F3, F4, given, choose]

2.1 If $\alpha$ and $\beta$ both realize $u$, their difference has valuation at least $n_{\mathfrak p}$ at each selected finite prime and their real signs agree. Since $\beta$ is a local unit, $\alpha/\beta-1=(\alpha-\beta)/\beta$ satisfies the required finite congruences; the ratio is positive at every selected real place. Thus $\alpha/\beta\in K_{\mathfrak m,1}$, so [F1] gives $[(\alpha)]_{\mathfrak m}=[(\beta)]_{\mathfrak m}$. Therefore $\phi$ is well-defined. [F1, F4, step 1.2]

3.1 If $u,u'$ are represented by $\alpha,\beta$, then $\alpha\beta$ represents $uu'$ by multiplication of residues and signs, and $(\alpha\beta)=(\alpha)(\beta)$. Hence $\phi(uu')=\phi(u)\phi(u')$, so $\phi$ is a homomorphism. [F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Every unit maps to the identity ray class because its principal ideal is $\mathcal O_K$, so $\operatorname{im}\rho\subseteq\ker\phi$. Conversely, if $u\in\ker\phi$, choose a realizing $\alpha$ as in step 1.2. Then $(\alpha)=(\gamma)$ for some $\gamma\in K_{\mathfrak m,1}$ by [F1]. By [F2], $v=\alpha/\gamma$ is a unit of $\mathcal O_K$. Its residues and signs are those of $\alpha$, since $\gamma$ is congruent to $1$ and positive at the prescribed places. Thus $u=\rho(v)$ and $\ker\phi=\operatorname{im}\rho$. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

4.2 The map $\psi:\operatorname{Cl}_{\mathfrak m}(K)\to\operatorname{Cl}(\mathcal O_K)$ sends a ray class represented by $\mathfrak a$ to its ordinary ideal class. It is well-defined because the ray-principal subgroup is principal, and it is a homomorphism by [F5]. If $\psi([\mathfrak a]_{\mathfrak m})=1$, then $\mathfrak a=(\alpha)$ for some $\alpha\in K^\times$; as $\mathfrak a$ is prime to $\mathfrak m_0$, $\alpha$ is a local unit at each selected finite place. Its residues and signs give $u\in R_{\mathfrak m}$, and step 1.2 shows $\phi(u)=[\mathfrak a]_{\mathfrak m}$. Conversely every class in $\operatorname{im}\phi$ maps to the identity ordinary class. Hence $\ker\psi=\operatorname{im}\phi$. [F1, F2, F5, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1]

5.1 Let $[\mathfrak a]$ be any ordinary ideal class, represented by a nonzero fractional ideal $\mathfrak a$. If $\mathfrak m_0=\mathcal O_K$, choose $x=1$. Otherwise [F8] makes $\mathcal O_K$ a Dedekind domain. For each prime $\mathfrak p\mid\mathfrak m_0$, [F7] supplies $c_{\mathfrak p}\in K^\times$ with $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_{\mathfrak p})=-v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)$. Apply [F3] at these distinct finite places with centers $c_{\mathfrak p}$ and positive errors $|c_{\mathfrak p}|_{\mathfrak p}/2$; the resulting $x\in K$ satisfies $|x-c_{\mathfrak p}|_{\mathfrak p}<|c_{\mathfrak p}|_{\mathfrak p}$, so $x\ne0$. By [F6], $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x-c_{\mathfrak p})>\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_{\mathfrak p})$. The valuation inequality applied to $x=(x-c_{\mathfrak p})+c_{\mathfrak p}$ gives $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)\ge\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_{\mathfrak p})$. If this inequality were strict, applying it to $c_{\mathfrak p}=(c_{\mathfrak p}-x)+x$ would force $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_{\mathfrak p})>\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(c_{\mathfrak p})$, impossible. Hence $\operatorname{ord}_{\mathfrak p}(x)= -v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)$. By [F5], $v_{\mathfrak p}((x)\mathfrak a)=v_{\mathfrak p}((x))+v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak a)=0$, so $(x)\mathfrak a$ is prime to $\mathfrak m_0$ and represents the same ordinary class as $\mathfrak a$. Thus $\psi$ is surjective; the kernel computations in steps 4.1 and 4.2 prove exactness. [F3, F5, F6, F7, F8, step 4.1, step 4.2, choose] ∎
