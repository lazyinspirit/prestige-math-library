## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For the cusp $k[t^2,t^3]\subseteq k[t]$, the conductor is the ideal
$(t^2,t^3)$ of $k[t^2,t^3]$: the least exponent $m$ such that
$t^n\in k[t^2,t^3]$ for every $n\ge m$ is $m=2$, and $t^2$ does not generate
the conductor alone since $t^3$ is not a multiple of $t^2$ inside the semigroup
ring.

## Facts & Assumptions
**Given:** AC, an algebraically closed field $k$ of characteristic not two, the semigroup ring $A=k[t^2,t^3]\subseteq k[t]$, its maximal ideal $\mathfrak m=(t^2,t^3)$, and the conductor $\mathfrak c=\operatorname{Ann}_A(k[t]/A)=\{a\in A:a\,k[t]\subseteq A\}$.

[F1] The conductor of the normalization of the cusp is $\mathfrak c$, an ideal of both $A$ and $k[t]$, and the normalization of the cusp is $k[t^2,t^3]\hookrightarrow k[t]$ with conductor $(t^2,t^3)$ ([[def-conductor-normalization]], [[lem-conductor-ideal-common-ideal]], [[ex-normalization-cusp]]).

[F2] The normalization of the cusp realizes the extension as $A=k[t^{2},t^{3}]\subseteq k[t]$, with $k[t]=A+At$ a finite $A$-module and fraction field $k(t)$, and its conductor is the maximal ideal $(t^{2},t^{3})$ ([[ex-normalization-cusp]]).

[F7] AC is inherited through the classical localization, normalization, or finite-morphism suppliers cited above ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

1.1 $t^2\,k[t]\subseteq A$: for $n\ge0$ the monomial $t^{n+2}$ has exponent at least $2$, and every exponent $\ge2$ lies in the numerical semigroup generated by $2$ and $3$, so $t^{n+2}\in A$; the same argument gives $t^3\,k[t]\subseteq A$. Hence $t^2,t^3\in\mathfrak c$, and since $\mathfrak c$ is an ideal, $(t^2,t^3)\subseteq\mathfrak c$. [F1, algebra, given, F7]

1.2 The semigroup $\langle2,3\rangle=\{0,2,3,4,\dots\}$ contains every integer $n\ge2$ and misses $1$, so the least exponent $m$ with $t^n\in A$ for all $n\ge m$ is $m=2$. The element $t^3$ is not a multiple of $t^2$ inside $A$: an equation $t^3=t^2a$ with $a\in A\subseteq k[t]$ would force $a=t\notin A$ since $k[t]$ is a domain. Hence $t^2$ alone does not generate the conductor, and the two monomials $t^2,t^3$ are both needed. [F2, algebra, given]

2.1 Conversely let $a\in A$ satisfy $a\,k[t]\subseteq A$. If $a\notin(t^2,t^3)$, it has a nonzero constant coefficient $c$ and all remaining terms have exponent at least two. Thus $at$ has nonzero coefficient $c$ at exponent one, so $at\notin A$, a contradiction; $a=0$ lies in $(t^2,t^3)$. Hence $a\in(t^2,t^3)$ and $\mathfrak c\subseteq(t^2,t^3)$; with step 1.1, $\mathfrak c=(t^2,t^3)$. [F1, given]

3.1 Therefore the conductor of the cusp extension $k[t^2,t^3]\subseteq k[t]$ is the maximal ideal $(t^2,t^3)$ of the semigroup ring, whose generators are the first two positive elements of the semigroup: the least exponent from which all monomials lie in $k[t^2,t^3]$ is $2$, and neither generator alone suffices. [F1, F2, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎
