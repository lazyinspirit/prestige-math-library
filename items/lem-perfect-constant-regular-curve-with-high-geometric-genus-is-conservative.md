## Statement

Assume AC. Let $k$ be perfect of characteristic $p>0$, let $K/k$ be
finitely generated of positive transcendence degree with $k$ relatively
algebraically closed in $K$, and let $C/K$ be a regular projective
geometrically integral curve of arithmetic genus $g$. Suppose $C(K)$ is
infinite and the geometric normalization has genus $h\ge2$. Then $C$ is
smooth over $K$, $h=g$, and for some finite extension $N/K$ it is
isomorphic over $N$ to a smooth proper geometrically integral curve over
the exact constant field $k_N$ of $N/k$. The geometric-genus-zero and
geometric-genus-one cases are not assumed or concluded in this lemma.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and the perfect exact constants, regular curve, infinite
point set and geometric genus hypothesis of the Statement.

[A1] AC is assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F1] After a finite purely inseparable field extension, a geometrically
integral projective curve has a smooth projective geometrically integral
normalization of its geometric genus $h$. One has $h\le g$, with equality
if and only if the original curve is smooth.
[[lem-geometric-normalization-descends-to-finite-purely-inseparable-extension]]

[F2] A smooth proper geometrically integral high-genus curve with
infinitely many points over any finitely generated exact-constant field
becomes constant over the exact constants of some finite extension, allowing
inseparability. For a constant high-genus curve over perfect constants,
there are only finitely many points outside the points over any fixed
iterated $p$th-power subfield.
[[lem-smooth-function-field-curves-descend-after-finite-constant-extension]]
[[lem-constant-high-genus-curve-has-finite-Frobenius-nondescent-over-perfect-constants]]

[F3] A nonconstant map from a smooth proper geometrically integral curve
to a regular proper geometrically integral curve forces the target smooth,
without a separability assumption on that map. A curve morphism over a
finite purely inseparable extension descends if infinitely many source
points and their infinitely many image points are rational over the base.
[[lem-smooth-proper-dominator-forces-regular-curve-smooth]]
[[lem-infinitely-many-rational-point-values-descend-a-purely-inseparable-curve-map]]

[F4] Purely inseparable elements have a $p$-power in the base. Every
irreducible polynomial in characteristic $p$ has a separable core in a
$p$-power of its variable. An extension generated by separable elements is
separable; algebraic extensions of perfect fields are separable. Field
degrees and finite transcendence degrees satisfy their tower formulas,
and perfect characteristic-$p$ fields are their $p$th-power fields.
[[thm-purely-inseparable-extension-characterizations]]
[[thm-irreducible-polynomial-in-positive-characteristic-has-a-unique-separable-core]]
[[thm-extension-generated-by-separable-elements-is-separable]]
[[cor-algebraic-extensions-of-perfect-fields-are-separable]]
[[thm-tower-law-for-finite-field-extensions]]
[[thm-perfect-field-characterizations]]
[[cor-transcendence-degree-tower-additivity]]

[F5] Finite separable extensions are simple; their minimal polynomials
are coprime to their derivatives. Polynomials factor into irreducibles,
and the quotient by an irreducible polynomial is a field. Flat local
regularity ascends when both base and closed fibre are regular, while
smoothness descends under field extension.
[[thm-primitive-element-theorem-for-finite-separable-extensions]]
[[thm-polynomial-is-separable-iff-coprime-to-its-derivative]]
[[lem-polynomial-factorisation-into-irreducibles]]
[[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]]

[[lem-ag-geometric-regularity-field-tests]]
[[thm-ag-standard-smooth-geometric-regularity]]

[F6] A proper closed subset of an integral curve is finite. A finite
map is affine over each affine target open.
[[lem-curve-closed-subsets-finite]] [[lem-finite-morphism-affine]]

## Axiom use

AC is inherited from the stated normalization, constant-curve and
map-descent suppliers. Only finite field generators, exponents and
exceptional point sets are chosen.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct; descend a smooth constant dominator to a
separable intermediate field and use regularity of that target.

1.1 Apply [F1] to obtain a finite purely inseparable $K'/K$ and a smooth projective normalization $\nu:Y\to C_{K'}$ of genus $h\ge2$. The exact constants of $K'/k$ are still $k$. If $a\in K'$ is algebraic over $k$, some $a^{p^r}$ lies in $K$ by [F4], hence in $k$ by the given exactness; perfection of $k$ then puts its unique $p^r$th root $a$ in $k$. Infinitely many $K$-points lift to $Y(K')$. To see this, the finite birational map is an isomorphism on a nonempty open: on an affine chart its finite algebra lies in the same fraction field, so clearing the finitely many module-generator denominators makes the two rings equal. The complementary target subset is finite by [F6]. All but finitely many original points therefore lie in this isomorphism locus after extension and lift uniquely. Apply [F2] to $Y/K'$ with these exact constants. There are a finite $M/K'$, its finite exact constants $c/k$, and a smooth proper geometrically integral curve $D/c$ with an $M$-isomorphism $u:Y_M\to D_M$. The composite $f=\nu_M\circ u^{-1}:D_M\to C_M$ is nonconstant and sends infinitely many distinct points $Q_i\in D(M)$ to distinct original points $P_i\in C(K)$. [F1, F2, F4, F6, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Let $L$ be the elements of $M$ separable over $K$. They form a subfield: field operations on finitely many separable elements take place in their finite separable generated extension by [F4]. As a $K$-subspace of finite $M/K$, it is finite over $K$, and is separable by definition. Every element $a\in M$ has a $p$-power separable over $K$, by the separable core in [F4]. Hence $M/L$ is purely inseparable. Choose a common exponent $q=p^r$ for a finite field generating list, so $M^q\subseteq L$. Also $c\subseteq L$: every element of finite $c/k$ is separable over perfect $k$, hence separable over $K$ because its relative minimal polynomial divides that separable polynomial. The finite extension $c/k$ is itself perfect. Indeed Frobenius gives $[c^p:k]=[c:k]$ since $k^p=k$, and the tower formula for $k\subseteq c^p\subseteq c$ forces $c^p=c$. [F4, construct, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 Apply the constant-curve assertion of [F2] over $M/c$. This is a finitely generated field extension of positive transcendence degree by [F4]. Only finitely many $D(M)$-points lie outside $D(M^q)$. Thus, after discarding finitely many pairs from step 1.1, all $Q_i$ lie in $D(M^q)\subseteq D(L)$, while their distinct images $P_i$ lie in $C(K)\subseteq C(L)$. The map-descent assertion of [F3] now applies to $M/L$, the smooth curve $D_L$ (defined since $c\subseteq L$) and the projective curve $C_L$. It descends the actual nonconstant morphism $f$ to a nonconstant $L$-morphism $D_L\to C_L$. [F2, F3, F4, algebra, step 1.1, step 2.1]

3.2 The curve $C_L$ is regular. Here is the required separable-base-change argument. Write $L=K(a)$ with separable minimal polynomial $P$ by [F5]. On a regular affine chart $\operatorname{Spec}A\subseteq C$, the ring $A\otimes_K L$ is finite free over $A$, hence flat. At any prime over $\mathfrak p\subseteq A$ its local map from $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is flat. The closed fibre is a localization of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)[T]/(P)$. The Bezout identity for $P,P'$ over $K$ remains valid over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so $P$ has distinct irreducible factors there by [F5]. Localizing the quotient at a prime leaves the quotient by exactly one irreducible factor, a field. The closed fibre is regular, and the base local ring is regular; flat local regularity ascent in [F5] makes the target local ring regular. This holds at every prime, proving the assertion without passing to an inseparable extension. [F5, given, algebra, step 2.1]

4.1 The descended map of step 3.1 has smooth proper geometrically integral source $D_L$ and regular proper geometrically integral target $C_L$. By the dominator assertion of [F3], the target is smooth over $L$. Field descent in [F5] makes $C$ smooth over $K$. Now [F1] gives $h=g$. Finally apply the smooth constant-model assertion of [F2] directly to $C/K$: it gives the stated finite extension $N/K$ and smooth constant model over its exact constants $k_N$. The geometric-genus-zero and geometric-genus-one branches were not used. [F1, F2, F3, F5, given, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
