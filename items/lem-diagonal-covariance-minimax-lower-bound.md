## Statement

Assume AC, as inherited through [[thm-fano-packing]]. For every $n\ge1$,
$p\ge2$, $\tau>0$, $0\le q<1$ and $c\ge0$, the minimax squared operator
risk of [[def-minimax-squared-operator-risk]] over
$\mathcal P_q(\tau,c)$ of [[def-weak-lq-sparse-covariance-class]] satisfies
$$\mathfrak R_n(\mathcal P_q(\tau,c))\ge b\tau^2\min\{1,\log(p)/n\},$$
for an absolute constant $b>0$. In particular, the lower bound applies when
$c=0$ and when an effective sparse-row count rounds to zero.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The class, dimensions and tail constant in the statement; all constructed laws are centered independent-coordinate Gaussian laws.

[F1] A centered multivariate normal vector is a linear image of a standard normal vector. [[def-multivariate-normal-law]].

[F2] Diagonal covariance matrices satisfy every stated off-diagonal weak-sparsity radius, including zero. [[def-weak-lq-sparse-covariance-class]].

[F3] For $N$ separated targets and reference-law average KL at most $K$, Fano bounds squared metric minimax risk below by $\Delta^2(1-(K+\log2)/\log N)/4$. [[thm-fano-packing]].

[F4] The operator norm is the attained maximum of $\|Av\|_2$ over unit vectors; the Frobenius norm is the Euclidean norm of the entry list. [[def-operator-norm-on-a-finite-dimensional-inner-product-space]], [[thm-operator-norm-is-the-largest-singular-value]], [[def-frobenius-matrix-norm]].

[F5] Euclidean Cauchy–Schwarz and the Euclidean norm triangle inequality hold in every finite dimension. A metric requires separation, symmetry and the triangle inequality; its Borel sigma-algebra is generated by its open sets. [[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]], [[def-metric-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]].

[F6] Every measurable statistic induces the point-mass probability kernel $K_T(x,B)=\mathbf1_B(T(x))$. [[lem-statistic-induces-deterministic-procedure]].

[F7] The positive-definite Gaussian covariance KL formula and finite independent-product KL additivity hold under the stated AC assumption. [[lem-gaussian-kullback-leibler-covariance-formula]], [[thm-product-kl-additivity]].

## Proof



1.1 Put $s=\tau/4$ and $\delta=\tfrac12\min\{1,\sqrt{\log(p+1)/n}\}$. Use $p+1$ Gaussian laws $P_0=N(0,sI_p)$ and $P_j=N(0,s(I_p+\delta e_je_j^{\mathsf T}))$, $1\le j\le p$. Each covariance has operator norm at most $3\tau/8<\tau$. If $Z$ is standard normal, $P(|Z|>t)=e^{-t^2/2}\sqrt{2/\pi}\int_0^\infty e^{-w^2/2-tw}dw\le e^{-t^2/2}$. Hence every unit-direction projection of these laws obeys the required tail with parameter $\tau$. Their covariance matrices are diagonal, so [F2] puts every law in every class in the statement. [given, F1, F2, algebra]

2.1 For $j\ge1$, the Gaussian covariance KL formula with $\Sigma_j=s(I_p+\delta e_je_j^{\mathsf T})$ and $\Sigma_0=sI_p$ gives $$D(P_j\|P_0)=\tfrac12(\delta-\log(1+\delta))\le\delta^2/4,$$ where $\delta-\log(1+\delta)=\int_0^\delta t/(1+t)\,dt\le\delta^2/2$. By finite-product KL additivity, $$D(P_j^{\otimes n}\|P_0^{\otimes n})=nD(P_j\|P_0)\le n\delta^2/4\le\log(p+1)/16.$$ The $j=0$ divergence is zero, so the reference-law average KL has the same bound. [given, F1, F7, step 1.1, algebra]

2.2 Use the full matrix action space $\mathcal T=\mathbb R^{p\times p}$ and set $\rho(A,B)=\|A-B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. For a unit vector $v$, rowwise Cauchy–Schwarz [F5] gives $\|Av\|_2^2\le\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^2=\|A\|_F^2$, so [F4] gives $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_F$. Conversely each coordinate unit vector $e_j$ has norm one, so $\|A\|_F^2=\sum_j\|Ae_j\|_2^2\le p\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2$. Thus $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_F\le\sqrt p\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. The maximum definition also gives homogeneity, symmetry under $A\mapsto-A$, and $\|A+B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}$ by the Euclidean triangle inequality on each unit vector. If $\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}=0$, all columns $Ae_j$ vanish, so $A=0$. Hence $\rho$ is a finite metric by [F5]. The two norm inequalities imply each ball for either metric contains a ball for the other about the same center, so their open sets and therefore their Borel sigma-algebras coincide. Under the entry-list identification $\mathbb R^{p\times p}=\mathbb R^{p^2}$ this is exactly the Euclidean Borel action space in the minimax-risk definition. In particular the diagonal embedding $a\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(a)$ is continuous: its Frobenius difference norm is $\|a-b\|_2$, and its operator difference norm is at most that. All targets in step 1.1 are thus legitimate Borel metric-space points, and every estimator in the minimax definition is measurable into this metric Borel space and induces an admissible deterministic kernel by [F6]. No new choice axiom is used. [given, F4, F5, F6, algebra, step 1.1]

3.1 For a diagonal matrix $D=\operatorname{diag}(a)$ and a unit vector $v$, $\|Dv\|_2^2=\sum_j a_j^2v_j^2\le\max_j|a_j|^2$; equality holds at a coordinate unit vector attaining the finite maximum. Hence $\|D\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_j|a_j|$. A target difference from the base has one diagonal entry $s\delta$; a difference between two nonbase targets has two entries $s\delta,-s\delta$. Every distinct pair therefore has metric separation exactly $s\delta$. [F4, step 1.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Apply [F3] to these $p+1$ targets, with the base sample law as reference. Its bracket is at least $1-1/16-\log2/\log3>1/4$. Thus the risk is at least $s^2\delta^2/16\ge(\tau^2/1024)\min\{1,\log p/n\}$. These finite experiments are subsets of the full class, so the full minimax risk is no smaller. [given, F2, F3, F6, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1, algebra] ∎
