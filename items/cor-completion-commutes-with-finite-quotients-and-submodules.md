## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice.

Let $R$ be a Noetherian commutative ring, let $I \subseteq R$ be an ideal, and let
$N \subseteq M$ be finitely generated $R$-modules.

1. The natural map
   $$
   \widehat M/\widehat N \longrightarrow \widehat{M/N}
   $$
   is an isomorphism.
2. Under the natural map $\widehat N \to \widehat M$, the image of
   $\widehat N$ is the $\widehat R$-submodule $N\widehat R \subseteq \widehat M$.
   In particular, for every ideal $J \subseteq R$,
   $$
   \widehat{JM}\cong J\widehat M.
   $$
3. For every $n \ge 0$,
   $$
   \widehat M/I^n\widehat M \cong M/I^nM.
   $$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Noetherian commutative ring $R$, an ideal $I \subseteq R$, and finite $R$-modules $N \subseteq M$.

[L1] Completion is exact on finite modules ([[thm-completion-is-exact-on-finite-modules]]).

[L2] For a finite module $X$, one has $\widehat X \cong X \otimes_R \widehat R$ ([[thm-completion-as-extension-of-scalars]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Apply [L1] to the short exact sequence $$0 \to N \to M \to M/N \to 0.$$ This gives an exact sequence $$0 \to \widehat N \to \widehat M \to \widehat{M/N} \to 0.$$ Therefore $$\widehat M/\widehat N \cong \widehat{M/N},$$ proving part 1. [L1]

1.2 By [L2], the map $\widehat N \to \widehat M$ identifies with $$N \otimes_R \widehat R \longrightarrow M \otimes_R \widehat R.$$ Its image is, by definition, the $\widehat R$-submodule generated by the image of $N$, namely $N\widehat R$. Hence the image of $\widehat N$ in $\widehat M$ is $N\widehat R$. Taking $N=JM$ yields $$\widehat{JM}\cong J\widehat M.$$ [L2, algebra]

2.1 Apply part 1 to the submodule $I^nM \subseteq M$. Then $$\widehat M/\widehat{I^nM}\cong \widehat{M/I^nM}.$$ By step 1.2, $\widehat{I^nM}$ identifies with $I^n\widehat M$. Also $M/I^nM$ is annihilated by $I^n$, so its $I$-adic filtration reaches $0$ after stage $n$ and its completion is canonically itself. Hence $$\widehat{M/I^nM}\cong M/I^nM,$$ which proves part 3. [step 1.1, step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Parts 1, 2, and 3 are exactly the three displayed conclusions above. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
