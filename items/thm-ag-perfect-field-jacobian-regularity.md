## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $k$ be a perfect field
([[def-perfect-field]]), let $P=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$ with $n\ge0$, let $I\subseteq P$
be an ideal and put $A:=P/I$. Fix elements $f_1,\dots,f_r\in P$ generating $I$
and let $J=(\partial f_j/\partial x_i)$ be the Jacobian matrix
([[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]]), of size $r\times n$, with entries
viewed in $A$.

1. **Closed-point criterion.** Let $\mathfrak m\subseteq A$ be a maximal ideal
   whose residue field $\kappa=A/\mathfrak m$ is a finite separable extension of
   $k$, and let $J(\mathfrak m)$ be the matrix over $\kappa$ obtained by
   evaluating the entries of $J$. Then $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is a regular local ring
   if and only if
   $$\operatorname{rank}_\kappa J(\mathfrak m)=n-\dim A_{\mathfrak m}.$$
   In particular the rank of $J(\mathfrak m)$ does not depend on the chosen
   generating set of $I$.
2. **Local charts.** If $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}A$ and
   $A_{\mathfrak q}$ is regular, then there are
   $g_1,\dots,g_c\in I$ and $t\in A\smallsetminus\mathfrak q$ such that $I$ is
   generated by $g_1,\dots,g_c$ after inverting $t$, some $c\times c$ minor of
   the Jacobian matrix $(\partial g_j/\partial x_i)$ is a unit of $A_t$, and
   therefore $A_t$ is a standard smooth $k$-algebra
   ([[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]]). Here
   $c=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak q\cap P)-\dim A_{\mathfrak q}$
   ([[def-height-of-a-prime-ideal]]).
3. **Openness and density.** The regular locus
   $\{\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}A:A_{\mathfrak q}\text{ regular}\}$ is open
   in $\operatorname{Spec}A$. If $\mathfrak p$ is a minimal prime of $A$ with
   $A_{\mathfrak p}$ reduced, then the regular locus contains a dense open subset
   of $V(\mathfrak p)$; when $A$ is reduced this holds for every irreducible
   component.

The remaining relations of $I$ beyond the $c$ chosen ones are killed by a
Nakayama argument, so no appeal to a later local-presentation theorem is needed.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A perfect field $k$, the polynomial ring $P=k[x_1,\dots,x_n]$, an ideal $I\subseteq P$ generated by $f_1,\dots,f_r$, the algebra $A=P/I$, and the Axiom of Choice.

[F1] [[lem-ag-polynomial-quotient-differentials]]: $\Omega_{P/k}$ is free on $\mathrm dx_1,\dots,\mathrm dx_n$, and for $I=(f_1,\dots,f_r)$ the module $\Omega_{P/I/k}$ is the cokernel of the Jacobian matrix $(\partial f_j/\partial x_i)$ acting from $A^r$ to $A^n$.

[F2] [[lem-ag-separable-residue-cotangent-sequence]]: for a Noetherian local $k$-algebra $R$ with maximal ideal $\mathfrak m$ and residue field $\kappa$ finitely generated and separably generated over $k$, the sequence $0\to\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\to\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa\to\Omega_{\kappa/k}\to0$ is exact; if $\kappa/k$ is finite separable, then $\Omega_{\kappa/k}=0$ and the first map is an isomorphism.

[F3] [[thm-ag-separating-transcendence-basis-perfect-field]]: every finitely generated field extension of a perfect field is separably generated.

[F4] [[thm-right-exactness-of-tensor-products]]: tensoring is right exact, so the cokernel of the Jacobian matrix base changes to the cokernel of the base-changed matrix.

[F5] [[lem-regular-system-of-parameters-equivalent-basis]]: under the Axiom of Choice, in a Noetherian local ring the classes of a regular system of parameters form a basis of $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2$, and conversely a lift of any basis generates the maximal ideal as a system of parameters.

[F6] [[lem-regular-local-regular-quotient-ideal-is-parameter-generated]]: under the Axiom of Choice, for a regular local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ of dimension $d$ and an ideal $I\subseteq\mathfrak m$, the quotient $R/I$ is regular if and only if $I$ is generated by an initial part of a regular system of parameters, if and only if $\dim_k((I+\mathfrak m^2)/\mathfrak m^2)=d-\dim(R/I)$.

[F7] [[thm-localisation-and-polynomial-extension-of-regular-rings]]: under the Axiom of Choice, localisations and finite polynomial extensions of a regular Noetherian ring are regular; in particular every prime localisation $P_{\mathfrak p}$ of the polynomial ring $P$ is a regular local ring of dimension $\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)$.

[F8] [[def-embedding-dimension-and-regular-local-ring]]: for a nonzero Noetherian local ring $(R,\mathfrak m,k)$ one has $\operatorname{edim}R=\dim_k(\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2)$, and $R$ is regular if and only if $\operatorname{edim}R=\dim R$.

[F9] [[lem-ag-standard-smooth-regular-geometric-fibres]]: under the Axiom of Choice, for a standard smooth presentation with $n$ variables and $c$ equations over a commutative ring $R$, every local ring of the base change to any field extension of the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ of any $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}R$ is regular; over the field $R=k$ and the extension $k/k$ this says that every local ring of a standard smooth $k$-algebra is regular.

[F10] [[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-localisation-bijection]]: contraction is a bijection from the primes of a localisation onto the primes of the base ring avoiding the multiplicative set, so an element outside a prime stays outside every prime of the localisation at that prime and is a unit after further inverting it.

[F11] [[cor-minimal-prime-has-height-zero]]: a minimal prime ideal has height zero, that is, $\dim A_{\mathfrak p}=0$ for a minimal prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$.

[F12] [[thm-artinian-ring-characterisation-by-primes]] and [[thm-artinian-local-ring-has-nilpotent-maximal-ideal]]: under the Axiom of Choice, a Noetherian ring is Artinian exactly when all its primes are maximal, and the maximal ideal of an Artinian local ring is nilpotent.

[F13] [[def-irreducible-topological-space-and-subset]]: a nonempty open subset of an irreducible topological space is dense.

[F14] [[def-perfect-field]]: a field is perfect when every algebraic extension of it is separable, equivalently when its Frobenius endomorphism is surjective in characteristic $p$.



## Proof

1.1 The closed-point criterion. Let $\mathfrak m\subseteq A$ be maximal with $\kappa=A/\mathfrak m$ finite separable over $k$. The local ring $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is a Noetherian local $k$-algebra with residue field $\kappa$, which is finitely generated and separably generated over the perfect field $k$ by [F3]; since $\kappa/k$ is finite separable, [F2] gives $\mathfrak m/\mathfrak m^2\cong\Omega_{A_{\mathfrak m}/k}\otimes_{A_{\mathfrak m}}\kappa$, and $\Omega_{A_{\mathfrak m}/k}\otimes\kappa\cong\Omega_{A/k}\otimes_A\kappa$. By [F1] and [F4] the latter is the cokernel of the matrix $J(\mathfrak m)$ acting from $\kappa^r$ to $\kappa^n$, so its dimension is $n-\operatorname{rank}_\kappa J(\mathfrak m)$. Hence $\operatorname{edim}A_{\mathfrak m}=n-\operatorname{rank}_\kappa J(\mathfrak m)$ by [F8], and by [F8] again $A_{\mathfrak m}$ is regular if and only if $\operatorname{edim}A_{\mathfrak m}=\dim A_{\mathfrak m}$, that is, if and only if $\operatorname{rank}J(\mathfrak m)=n-\dim A_{\mathfrak m}$; both $\operatorname{edim}$ and $\dim$ are intrinsic, so the rank is independent of the generating set of $I$. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F8, F14]

1.2 Local charts at regular points. Let $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}A$ with $A_{\mathfrak q}$ regular, let $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap P$ correspond to $\mathfrak q$, and put $R:=P_{\mathfrak p}$, a regular local ring of dimension $d:=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak p)$ by [F7], with ideal $I':=IR$ and quotient $R/I'=A_{\mathfrak q}$ of dimension $\dim A_{\mathfrak q}$. By [F6] there are $y_1,\dots,y_c\in I'$ forming an initial part of a regular system of parameters of $R$, with $c=d-\dim A_{\mathfrak q}$, and generating $I'$. Put $\mathfrak n:=\mathfrak pR$. The classes of the $y_j$ form a basis of $(I'+\mathfrak n^2)/\mathfrak n^2$: they span this subspace because they generate $I'$, and are linearly independent as part of a cotangent basis by [F5], with dimension $c$ by [F6]. Write $y_j=g_j/u_j$ with $g_j\in I$ and $u_j\in P\smallsetminus\mathfrak p$. Since each $u_j$ is a unit of $R$, the $g_j$ still generate $I'$ and their classes are nonzero scalar multiples of the corresponding $y_j$ classes, hence linearly independent. The map $\mathfrak n/\mathfrak n^2\to\Omega_{R/k}\otimes_R\kappa(\mathfrak p)\cong\kappa(\mathfrak p)^n$ is injective by [F2], the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ being finitely generated and separably generated over the perfect field $k$ by [F3], and the images of the classes of the $g_j$ are the vectors $(\partial g_j/\partial x_i\bmod\mathfrak p)_i$ by [F1]; these vectors are therefore linearly independent over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so some $c\times c$ minor $h$ of the Jacobian matrix of $g_1,\dots,g_c$ satisfies $h\notin\mathfrak p$. Since $I$ is finitely generated and $I'=(g_1,\dots,g_c)R$, there is $s\notin\mathfrak p$ with $sI\subseteq(g_1,\dots,g_c)$; put $t:=sh\in A$. By [F10] the element $t$ is not in $\mathfrak q$ and becomes a unit in $A_t$, and in $A_t$ the ideal $I$ is generated by $g_1,\dots,g_c$ while $h$ is a unit, so $A_t\cong(P/(g_1,\dots,g_c))_t$ is a standard smooth $k$-algebra with $n$ variables and $c$ equations. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F7, F10]

2.1 Openness of the regular locus. In the situation of step 1.2, [F9] applied over the field $k$ shows that every local ring of the standard smooth $k$-algebra $A_t$ is regular; hence the whole distinguished open $D(t)$ consists of regular points and is a neighbourhood of $\mathfrak q$. As $\mathfrak q$ was an arbitrary regular point, the regular locus is open in $\operatorname{Spec}A$. [F9, step 1.2]

3.1 Density along a generically reduced component. Let $\mathfrak p$ be a minimal prime of $A$ with $A_{\mathfrak p}$ reduced. By [F11] the local ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is Noetherian local of dimension $0$, so all its primes are maximal and it is Artinian by [F12]; its maximal ideal is therefore nilpotent by [F12], and reducedness forces it to be zero, so $A_{\mathfrak p}$ is a field, in particular regular. Step 2.1 then provides $t\notin\mathfrak p$ with $D(t)$ contained in the regular locus, and $D(t)\cap V(\mathfrak p)$ is a nonempty open subset of the irreducible space $V(\mathfrak p)$, hence dense in $V(\mathfrak p)$ by [F13]. When $A$ is reduced, every localisation $A_{\mathfrak p}$ at a minimal prime is reduced, so this applies to every irreducible component. [F11, F12, F13, step 2.1]

4.1 The three assertions are steps 1.1, 1.2 and 2.1 with the density statement of step 3.1; all of them use only the perfectness of $k$ through [F3] and [F2], and no later local-presentation theorem. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 3.1] ∎
