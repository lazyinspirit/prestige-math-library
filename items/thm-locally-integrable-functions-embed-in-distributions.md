## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice. The map $f\mapsto u_f$, $u_f(\varphi)=\int_\Omega f\varphi$, is a complex-linear injection from $L^1_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega)$ modulo almost-everywhere equality into $\mathcal D'(\Omega)$. It is continuous from the local $L^1$ topology, generated by $f\mapsto\int_K|f|$ for compact $K\subseteq\Omega$, to the strong distribution topology. In particular local $L^1$ convergence implies strong distribution convergence. The continuity estimate itself is choice-free; Countable Choice is used in the cited $L^1$ approximate-identity theorem proving injectivity.

## Facts & Assumptions

[F1] The regular functional is well-defined modulo almost-everywhere equality and satisfies $|u_f(\varphi)|\le\int_K|f|\,p_0(\varphi)$ on $\mathcal D_K$ ([[def-regular-distribution-from-a-locally-integrable-function]]).

[F2] Compactwise finite-order bounds characterize distributions ([[thm-local-finite-order-characterization-of-distributions]]).

[F3] A unit-mass smooth bump has rescalings $\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)$ ([[def-mollifier-family-generated-by-a-unit-mass-smooth-bump]]); these form an $L^1$ approximate identity under Countable Choice ([[prop-mollifier-families-are-l-one-approximate-identities]]).

[F4] Under Countable Choice, convolution by such an approximate identity converges to each $g\in L^1(\mathbb R^n)$ in $L^1$ ([[thm-l-one-approximate-identities-converge-in-l-p]], with $p=1$).

[F5] Smooth nonnegative compact cutoffs equal to one near a compact set exist ([[lem-test-function-cutoffs-and-euclidean-localization]]).

[F6] Strong seminorms are uniform pairings over bounded test sets, which have common compact support and bounded derivative suprema ([[def-weak-and-strong-topologies-on-distributions]]).

[F7] Dominated convergence gives $L^1$ convergence for almost-everywhere convergent functions under one integrable majorant ([[thm-dominated-convergence]]).

[F8] The assumed choice principle is [[def-countable-choice]].

## Proof

**Given:** Countable Choice and a locally integrable function $f$ on $\Omega$.

1.1 F1 and F2 show $u_f$ is a distribution, with compactwise order at most zero; linearity of the integral gives linearity in the equivalence class of $f$. For a bounded test set $B$, take its common compact $K$ and finite $M_B=\sup_{\varphi\in B}p_0(\varphi)$. Then $p_B(u_f)\le M_B\int_K|f|$. Thus each strong seminorm of the image is bounded by a constant times a defining local $L^1$ seminorm, proving continuity, including for nets. If $M_B=0$, the left side is zero. [given, F1, F2, F6]

2.1 Suppose $u_f=0$, and fix a closed ball $H$ compactly inside $\Omega$. Take $\chi$ from F5 equal to one near $H$ and compactly supported in $\Omega$, and extend $g=\chi f$ by zero to $\mathbb R^n$; it is in $L^1$ by local integrability. Also use F5 to obtain a nonnegative smooth compact bump equal to one on a ball, and divide it by its positive finite integral to obtain $\rho$ of mass one. Fix $R$ containing its support. For sufficiently small $\varepsilon$, every $x\in H$ has $x-\operatorname{supp}\rho_\varepsilon$ inside the neighborhood where $\chi=1$: the compact $H$ has positive distance from that neighborhood's closed complement. Consequently [step 1.1, given, F3, F5]
$$g*\rho_\varepsilon(x)=\int_\Omega f(y)\rho_\varepsilon(x-y)\,dy=u_f(\rho_\varepsilon(x-\cdot))=0.$$
The integrals are absolutely finite because $g\in L^1$ and the kernel is bounded; the reflected kernel is a test compactly supported in $\Omega$. [step 1.1, given, F3, F5]

3.1 By F3, F4 and F8, $\|g*\rho_\varepsilon-g\|_1\to0$. Since $g=f$ on $H$ and step 2.1 makes the convolution zero there, $\int_H|f|\le\|g*\rho_\varepsilon-g\|_1\to0$. Hence $f=0$ almost everywhere on $H$. The closed rational balls compactly inside $\Omega$ are countable and their interiors cover $\Omega$; applying this conclusion to each and taking the countable union of the null sets $\{x\in H:f(x)\ne0\}$ gives $f=0$ almost everywhere on $\Omega$. No family of cutoffs was selected simultaneously. Apply the same argument to $f-h$ for injectivity of the map on equivalence classes. [step 2.1, F3, F4, F8]

4.1 As a useful convergence consequence, if $f_j\to f$ almost everywhere and for every compact $K$ there is an integrable majorant of all $|f_j|$ on $K$, F7 gives $\int_K|f_j-f|\to0$. Step 1.1 then gives strong convergence $u_{f_j}\to u_f$. On the empty domain there is only the zero class and zero distribution, so injection and continuity still hold. $\square$ [step 3.1, step 1.1, F7]
