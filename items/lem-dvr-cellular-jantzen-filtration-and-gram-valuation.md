## Statement

Let $O$ be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizer $\pi$ and residue field
$F=O/\pi O$ ([[def-discrete-valuation-ring]]), and let $\nu_\pi$ denote its
valuation. Let $M$ be a finite free $O$-module with an $O$-valued bilinear
pairing $b$ ([[def-left-and-right-modules]]) whose scalar extension to
$\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ is nondegenerate. For $s\ge0$ put
$$M(s):=\{x\in M:b(x,M)\subseteq\pi^sO\},\qquad M_F^s:=(M(s)+\pi M)/\pi M .$$
Then:

1. $M_F^0=M/\pi M$ and $M_F^1=\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$, where $b_F$ is the
   reduced pairing on $M_F$;
2. the filtration terminates ($M_F^s=0$ for all sufficiently large $s$), and
   for any Gram matrix $G$ of $b$ one has the exact identity
   $$\sum_{s>0}\dim_FM_F^s=\nu_\pi(\det G);$$
3. if an $O$-algebra $A$ acts on $M$ with an anti-involution $*$ satisfying
   $b(ax,y)=b(x,a^*y)$ for all $a\in A$, $x,y\in M$, then every $M(s)$, and
   hence every $M_F^s$, is an $A$-submodule;
4. if $M_F$ is a cellular standard module and $b_F$ is its nonzero cellular form
   ([[def-cellular-algebra-basis-and-cell-module]]), then
   $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$ is its unique maximal submodule and therefore
   coincides with its module radical
   ([[def-module-radical-socle-head-and-loewy-series]]).

The Smith reduction used in the proof works over $O$ without any
characteristic restriction, in particular in characteristic $2$; it uses
independent bases of $M$ and of $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}_O(M,O)$, never a
diagonalization of a symmetric form.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A discrete valuation ring $O$ with uniformizer $\pi$, residue field $F$, valuation $\nu_\pi$; a finite free $O$-module $M$ of rank $n$ with bilinear pairing $b$, Gram matrix $G$ with respect to an $O$-basis, and the submodules $M(s)$ of the Statement.

[F1] Every nonzero element of $O$ is a unit times a unique power of $\pi$, and $\nu_\pi$ is additive on products; a square matrix over $O$ is invertible if and only if its determinant is a unit, and $\nu_\pi(\det(UV))=\nu_\pi(\det U)+\nu_\pi(\det V)$ ([[def-discrete-valuation-ring]], [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]).

[F2] $M^*=\operatorname{Hom}_O(M,O)$ is free of rank $n$, with dual basis $\varepsilon_1,\dots,\varepsilon_n$; an $O$-linear map $\varphi\colon M\to M^*$ has matrix $G^{\mathsf T}$ in column coordinates, where $G=(b(u_i,u_j))$, and $\det G\ne0$ makes $\varphi$ injective with finitely generated torsion cokernel: the adjugate identity makes $\det G$ annihilate the cokernel, and the cokernel is generated by the images of a finite basis ([[def-left-and-right-modules]], [[def-determinant-of-a-square-matrix]]).

[F3] $M_F=M/\pi M$ carries the reduced pairing $b_F(\bar x,\bar y):=b(x,y)\bmod\pi$; $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)=\{x\in M_F:b_F(x,M_F)=0\}$. The identification with the module radical in the cellular standard case is proved in step 1.5 ([[def-module-radical-socle-head-and-loewy-series]]).

[F4] The fixed-first-index cell law and its starred fixed-last-index counterpart are the cellular axioms ([[def-cellular-algebra-basis-and-cell-module]]). Their rank-one form action is derived in step 1.5.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data of [F1]–[F4]: the DVR $O$ with uniformizer $\pi$, the free

module $M$ with pairing $b$ and Gram matrix $G$, the submodules $M(s)$, and the

reduced pairing $b_F$.

1.1 The rank-zero case. If $n=0$, then $M=0$ and $M^*=0$, the pairing is generically nondegenerate on the zero space, and $M(s)=M_F^s=0$ for every $s$. The empty Gram determinant is defined here by the empty Leibniz product to be $1$, whose valuation is $0$; the empty sum of elementary-divisor exponents is $0$. Thus the Gram valuation identity is $0=0$, the radical and stability assertions hold, and the conditional nonzero-cellular-form assertion has no rank-zero instance. For the remaining steps assume $n\ge1$. [given, F1, algebra]

1.2 Smith reduction of the form. View $b$ as the $O$-linear map $\varphi\colon M\to M^*$, $\varphi(x)=b(x,-)$, with matrix $G^{\mathsf T}$ in column coordinates by [F2]; nondegeneracy over $\operatorname{Frac}(O)$ gives $\det G\ne0$, so $\varphi$ has full rank. Choose a nonzero matrix entry of $\varphi$ of least valuation $\nu_\pi$ and move it to position $(1,1)$ by permuting the bases, then multiply its row by a unit of $O$ to make it $\pi^{a_1}$; every entry of the matrix is divisible by $\pi^{a_1}$ by minimality (including zero entries). Subtracting suitable $O$-multiples of the first row from the other rows and of the first column from the other columns (all coefficients in $O$, all operations unimodular by [F1]) kills the first row and column outside position $(1,1)$, leaving a smaller matrix over $O$. Iterating over the remaining rows and columns produces bases $u_1,\dots,u_n$ of $M$ and $v_1,\dots,v_n$ of $M^*$ with $\varphi(u_j)=\pi^{a_j}v_j$ and $0\le a_1\le\dots\le a_n$. [F1, F2, construct]

1.3 Reduction of the radical. For $\bar x\in M_F$ one has $b_F(\bar x,M_F)=0$ if and only if $b(x,M)\subseteq\pi O$, i.e. $x\in M(1)$; hence $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)=(M(1)+\pi M)/\pi M=M_F^1$ by [F3], the containment $\pi M\subseteq M(1)$ being immediate from $b(\pi x,M)\subseteq\pi O$. [F3, algebra]

1.4 Submodule stability. Let $a\in A$ and $x\in M(s)$; by the hypothesis $b(ax,y)=b(x,a^*y)\in\pi^sO$ for every $y\in M$, since $a^*y\in M$. Hence $ax\in M(s)$ and $M(s)$ is an $A$-submodule; consequently $M_F^s=(M(s)+\pi M)/\pi M$ is an $A$-submodule of $M_F$, the $\pi M$-cosets of $M(s)$ being stable because $\pi M$ is stable. [F3, algebra]

1.5 The cellular standard module. In a cell layer, applying both axioms [F4] to $c_{us}c_{tv}$ shows that the product is $b_F(s,t)c_{uv}$ modulo higher shapes: right multiplication retains $u$, left multiplication retains $v$, and comparison makes the scalar independent of $u,v$. This is the cellular form; the cell action therefore sends a vector $w$ to $b_F(v,w)u$ in the left convention, with indices transposed in the right convention. Assume $M_F$ is a cellular standard module and $b_F$ is its nonzero cellular form, and let $N\subseteq M_F$ be a proper submodule. If some $w\in N$ lies outside $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$, choose a label $v$ with $b_F(v,w)\ne0$; by the rank-one action [F4] every basis vector $u$ equals $b_F(v,w)^{-1}$ times the image of $w$ under the cell element of the pair $(u,v)$, so $u\in N$ for every basis label $u$ and $N=M_F$, a contradiction. Thus every proper submodule is contained in $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$, which is proper because the form is nonzero, and the same rank-one argument shows that the quotient $M_F/\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$ is simple (it has no nonzero proper submodule). Hence $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$ is the unique maximal submodule of $M_F$, i.e. its module radical by [F3]. [F3, F4, algebra]

2.1 The filtration in the Smith bases. In the bases of step 1.2 one computes for $s\ge0$ that $b(u_j,M)=\pi^{a_j}O$, so $M(s)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{n}\pi^{\max(0,s-a_j)}Ou_j,\qquad \dim_FM_F^s=\#\{j:a_j\ge s\}.$ In particular $M(0)=M$, so $M_F^0=M/\pi M$; the reduced filtration terminates because every summand of $M(s)$ is contained in $\pi M$ for $s>\max_ja_j$, so $M_F^s=0$; and each $M_F^s$ is a subspace of the finite-dimensional $F$-space $M_F$. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 The Gram valuation identity. By the matrix form of step 1.2, the determinant of $G$ equals that of $G^{\mathsf T}$ and hence $\det(\pi^{a_j}\delta_{jk})$ times a unit, since both change-of-basis matrices are invertible over $O$; hence, using additivity of $\nu_\pi$ by [F1], $\nu_\pi(\det G)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_j =\sum_{s>0}\#\{j:a_j\ge s\} =\sum_{s>0}\dim_FM_F^s,$ the middle equality counting each $a_j$ as the number of positive integers $s\le a_j$. [F1, F2, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Conclusion. The rank-zero argument of step 1.1 and steps 1.3–1.5 and 2.1–3.1 establish all four assertions of the Statement: the identification of $M_F^0$ and $M_F^1=\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$, the termination together with the exact identity $\sum_{s>0}\dim_FM_F^s=\nu_\pi(\det G)$, the submodule stability under an anti-involution action, and the equality of $\operatorname{rad}(b_F)$ with the module radical in the cellular standard case. The Smith reduction of step 1.2 is valid over any discrete valuation ring, including residue characteristic $2$, because it uses independent domain and codomain bases and no symmetric diagonalization. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 1.3, step 1.4, step 1.5] ∎



## Remarks

The exactness of the sum formula is the point of the lemma: the multiplicities $\dim_FM_F^s$ of the Jantzen filtration are read off from the $\pi$-adic valuation of the Gram determinant, and in positive residue characteristic the valuation is the full $p$-power of the corresponding binomial coefficient, not merely an indicator. No choice principle is used: all bases are obtained by finitely many unimodular operations on finite matrices.
