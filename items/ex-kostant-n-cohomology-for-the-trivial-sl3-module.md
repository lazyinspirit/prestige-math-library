## Example

Assume the Axiom of Choice, inherited from Kostant's theorem ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$, $\lambda=0$ and $V=\mathbb C$ the trivial module. Here $W\cong S_3$ has elements $1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,w_0$ of lengths $0,1,1,2,2,3$, and the dot action is $w\cdot0=w\rho-\rho$. Kostant's theorem therefore gives
$$H^k(\mathfrak n^+,\mathbb C)=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=k}\mathbb C_{w\cdot0},$$
of dimensions $1,2,2,1$ in degrees $k=0,1,2,3$ with pairwise distinct weights $0,-\alpha_1,-\alpha_2,-2\alpha_1-\alpha_2,-\alpha_1-2\alpha_2,-2\alpha_1-2\alpha_2$; in particular $\dim H^1(\mathfrak n^+,\mathbb C)=2$, one class for each simple reflection, and the total dimension is $|W|=6$. This is the first rank-two check of the multiplicity-free formula, and it shows that a trivial coefficient module does not force all cohomology weights to vanish.

## Verification

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; $\mathfrak g=\mathfrak{sl}_3(\mathbb C)$ with its diagonal Cartan subalgebra, positive system $\Phi^+=\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2,\alpha_1+\alpha_2\}$, simple reflections $s_1,s_2$, longest element $w_0$, and the trivial module $\mathbb C$.

[L1] The root system of $\mathfrak{sl}_3$ is type $A_2$, with three positive roots and base $\{\alpha_1,\alpha_2\}$; the Weyl group is generated by $s_1,s_2$ subject to $s_1^2=s_2^2=1$ and $s_1s_2s_1=s_2s_1s_2$, so $W=\{1,s_1,s_2,s_1s_2,s_2s_1,w_0\}$ with lengths $0,1,1,2,2,3$ and $w_0=s_1s_2s_1$ ([[thm-rank-two-root-system-classification]], [[prop-root-systems-of-the-classical-complex-lie-algebras]], [[def-classical-complex-matrix-lie-algebras]], [[def-root-reflections-and-the-weyl-group-action]], [[def-length-and-longest-element-of-a-finite-weyl-group]], [[prop-weyl-length-equals-positive-root-inversion-number]]).

[L2] $\rho=\frac12(\alpha_1+\alpha_2+(\alpha_1+\alpha_2))=\alpha_1+\alpha_2$, and $\lambda+\rho=\rho$ is regular; the dot action is $w\cdot0=w\rho-\rho$ ([[def-weyl-vector-rho-for-a-chosen-positive-system]], [[def-integral-dominant-and-strictly-dominant-weights]]).

[L3] $H^k(\mathfrak n^+,L(0))=\bigoplus_{\ell(w)=k}\mathbb C_{w\cdot0}$ for every $k$, and the weights $w\cdot0$ for distinct $w$ are distinct ([[thm-kostant-nilradical-cohomology-theorem]], [[prop-weyl-length-equals-positive-root-inversion-number]]).

1.1 With $s_1\rho=\rho-\alpha_1$ and $s_2\rho=\rho-\alpha_2$, direct evaluation gives $s_1\cdot0=-\alpha_1$, $s_2\cdot0=-\alpha_2$, $s_1s_2\cdot0=s_1(\rho-\alpha_2)-\rho=s_1(\alpha_1)-\rho=-2\alpha_1-\alpha_2$, $s_2s_1\cdot0=-2\alpha_2-\alpha_1$, and $w_0\cdot0=-\rho-\rho=-2\rho=-2\alpha_1-2\alpha_2$, while $1\cdot0=0$. [L1, L2]

2.1 By [L3] the $k$-th cohomology is the sum of one line for each Weyl element of length $k$, so its dimension is the number of such elements and its weights are precisely those computed in step 1.1 for the length-$k$ elements. For $k>3$ the sum is empty, so $H^k(\mathfrak n^+,\mathbb C)=0$. [L1, L3, step 1.1]

3.1 Counting the elements listed in [L1] gives dimensions $1,2,2,1$ in degrees $0,1,2,3$ and total dimension $6=|W|$; in degree one the two classes have the distinct weights $-\alpha_1,-\alpha_2$ attached to the two simple roots, and both are nonzero because $\alpha_1,\alpha_2$ are linearly independent. [L1, step 2.1] ∎
