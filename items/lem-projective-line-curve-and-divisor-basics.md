## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. For every field $k$, $\mathbb P^1_k$ is a smooth proper geometrically integral curve of genus zero. Write $t=x_1/x_0$ and $\infty=[0:1]$. Every closed point in the $t$-chart is $p=V(g)$ for a monic irreducible $g\in k[t]$; if $d=\deg g$, then $[\kappa(p):k]=d$ and $\operatorname{div}(g)=[p]-d[\infty]$. Every divisor $D$ is linearly equivalent to $\deg_k(D)[\infty]$, and $\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O(\infty)$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC, a field $k$, and the projective line with coordinate $t=x_1/x_0$ and $u=t^{-1}$ on its two standard charts.

[F1] The standard charts are $\operatorname{Spec}k[t]$ and $\operatorname{Spec}k[u]$, glued where $tu=1$; these charts commute with field extension. Projective space is proper and finite type. ([[def-relative-projective-space-standard-charts]], [[thm-projective-space-proper-over-base]], [[lem-projective-space-finite-type-over-base]])

[F2] Polynomial algebras in one variable are standard smooth; their dimension is one and their coordinate rings are domains. Finite-chart chain dimension is the supremum of chart dimensions. ([[def-ag-standard-smooth-algebra]], [[def-smooth-morphism-classical]], [[cor-dimension-of-a-finite-polynomial-ring-over-a-field]], [[lem-chain-dimension-open-cover]], [[lem-irreducibility-criteria-and-open-subspaces]], [[def-integral-affine-scheme]], [[def-algebraic-curve-over-field]])

[F3] $k[t]$ is a PID. Its maximal ideals are generated by monic irreducible polynomials $g$, and $k[t]/(g)$ has $k$-dimension $\deg g$. ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-field-is-a-pid]], [[thm-polynomial-quotient-is-a-field-iff-irreducible]], [[thm-simple-algebraic-extension-quotient-power-basis-and-degree]], [[def-residue-field-scheme-point]])

[F4] At closed points of a smooth curve the local rings are DVRs. Divisor degree is the finite residue-weighted sum; under AC, curve Cartier and Weil divisors agree and the rational-section dictionary identifies their invertible sheaves. ([[thm-local-ring-smooth-curve-dvr]], [[def-divisor-smooth-proper-curve]], [[def-degree-divisor-proper-curve]], [[thm-cartier-weil-divisors-curves-agree]], [[thm-line-bundle-rational-section-cartier-divisor]])

[F5] The coordinate forms $x_0,x_1$ are global sections of $\mathcal O(1)$ and are its local frames on their nonvanishing charts. $H^1(\mathbb P^1_k,\mathcal O)=0$ by the direct twisting-sheaf calculation. On a smooth proper geometrically integral curve the genus is $h^1(\mathcal O)$. ([[def-very-ample-invertible-sheaf-relative]], [[cor-h0-projective-space-o-d-homogeneous-polynomials]], [[cor-top-cohomology-projective-space-o-d]], [[def-arithmetic-genus-proper-curve]])

[A1] AC is inherited from projective space, local DVRs, cohomology and the Cartier/Weil dictionary; it supplies DC for the cycle map. ([[def-axiom-of-choice]], [[thm-choice-implies-dependent-implies-countable-choice]])

## Proof

1.1 Each chart is integral and the overlap is nonempty and dense in each. Thus every nonempty open of either chart meets the overlap, and any two nonempty opens of the glued space meet. The scheme is irreducible and reduced. The same argument over an algebraic closure proves geometric integrality. The chart rings are Noetherian because they are PIDs by [F3], so the finite affine cover makes the scheme Noetherian and licenses the chart-dimension computation in [F2]. The charts are smooth of dimension one; [F1] gives properness, separatedness and finite type. Hence it is a smooth proper geometrically integral curve. [F1, F2, F3]

2.1 Its genus is zero by the $H^1(\mathcal O)=0$ calculation in [F5]. Finite points and their residue degrees are those of [F3], and infinity is $u=0$ with residue field $k$. [F3, F5, step 1.1]

2.2 At $p=V(g)$, $g$ generates the maximal ideal of $k[t]_{(g)}$, so its order is one. At every other finite point it is a unit. At infinity, $g(t)=u^{-d}h(u)$ with $h(0)=1$, so its order is $-d$. Thus $\operatorname{div}(g)=[p]-d[\infty]$. [F3, F4, step 1.1]

3.1 Write a divisor as $D=\sum_i n_i[p_i]+m[\infty]$ with $p_i=V(g_i)$ and $d_i=\deg g_i$. Then $\deg_kD=\sum_i n_id_i+m$, and the finite product $f=\prod_i g_i^{n_i}$, allowing negative exponents, satisfies $\operatorname{div}(f)=D-\deg_k(D)[\infty]$. This is the required linear equivalence, also for $D=0$ with empty product $f=1$. [F4, step 2.1, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 The section $x_0$ has coefficient $1$ on its own chart and coefficient $u$ in the $x_1$-frame on the other chart, so its divisor is exactly $[\infty]$. The rational-section dictionary gives $\mathcal O(1)\cong\mathcal O(\infty)$. Steps 1.1–2.2 establish the other assertions. No choices beyond the supplier AC premises are made. ∎ [F4, F5, A1, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2, step 3.1]
