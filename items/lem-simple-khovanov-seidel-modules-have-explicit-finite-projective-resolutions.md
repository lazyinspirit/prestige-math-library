## Statement

Fix $m\ge1$ and $0\le i\le m$, let $A_m$ be the type A algebra of
[[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]], let $A_m\text{-mod}$ be the
category of [[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]] with
vertex projectives $P_j=A_me_j$ and internal shifts $\{p\}$, and let $S_i$ and
$S_i/pS_i$ be the vertex modules of [[def-vertex-khovanov-seidel-modules]].

Write $j(p,q):=i+p-q$ and put
$$C_{p,q}:=\begin{cases}P_{j(p,q)}\{p\},& p\ge0,\ q\ge0,\ p\le m-i,\ q\le i+p,\\ 0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}$$
so that the nonzero entries form the staircase
$$0\le p\le m-i,\qquad 0\le q\le i+p,$$
on which the projective index $j=i+p-q$ runs from $j=i+p$ down to $j=0$. Let
$$h_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p-1,q},\quad x\mapsto x\cdot(j(p,q)\,\big|\,j(p,q)-1),$$
$$v_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p,q-1},\quad x\mapsto(-1)^p\,x\cdot(j(p,q)\,\big|\,j(p,q)+1),$$
where a symbol $(j|j-1)$ with $j=0$, a symbol $(j|j+1)$ with $j=m$, and any
symbol whose source or target module is zero denote the zero map, and where right multiplication
by a path $\alpha$ means $x\mapsto x\alpha$ in the ring $A_m$. Then:

1. $(C,h,v)$ is a first-quadrant homological double complex of graded
   $A_m$-modules, and its direct-sum total complex
   $\operatorname{Tot}_\bullet$, with differential $d=h+v$ and
   $\operatorname{Tot}_n=\bigoplus_{p+q=n}C_{p,q}$, is a finite graded
   projective resolution of $S_i$: every term is a finite direct sum of internal
   shifts of the $P_j$, only finitely many terms are nonzero, $H_0$ is
   $C_{0,0}=P_i$ modulo the image of $d_1$ and $H_n(\operatorname{Tot})=0$
   for $n\ne0$, the augmentation being the quotient map
   $P_i\to P_i/A_m\{u_{i-1},d_i,r_i\}\cong S_i$, with the endpoint omissions of [[def-vertex-khovanov-seidel-modules]].
2. For every prime $p$, the mapping cone of multiplication by $p$ on
   $\operatorname{Tot}_\bullet$ is a finite graded projective resolution of
   $S_i/pS_i$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** An integer $m\ge1$, an index $0\le i\le m$, the algebra $A_m$ with its internal grading, the vertex projectives $P_j=A_me_j$, the internal shifts $\{p\}$, and the vertex modules $S_i$ and $S_i/pS_i$.

[F1] $A_m=\mathbb Z\Gamma_m/I_m$ is the quotient of the path ring of the doubled line by the two-sided ideal generated by $(j-1|j|j+1)$, $(j+1|j|j-1)$, $(j|j+1|j)-(j|j-1|j)$ for $0<j<m$ and $(0|1|0)$; the internal degree is additive over concatenation with $\deg(j)=\deg(j|j+1)=0$ and $\deg(j+1|j)=1$; write $u_j=(j|j+1)$ and $d_j=(j+1|j)$ ([[def-khovanov-seidel-type-a-quiver-algebra]]).

[F2] In $\mathbb Z\Gamma_m$ the product of paths is their left-to-right concatenation, $0$ when the first does not end where the second starts; the vertex paths $(j)$ are orthogonal idempotents with $\sum_j(j)=1$, and $(j)p=p$ for paths $p$ with source $j$ while $p(j)=p$ for paths with target $j$, the products being $0$ otherwise; these identities hold in the quotient $A_m$ ([[def-path-ring-of-a-finite-quiver-over-the-integers]]).

[L3] $A_m$ is free of rank $4m+1$ on the classes of the vertices $(j)$, the arrows $(j|j\pm1)$ and the returns $r_j=(j|j-1|j)$ for $1\le j\le m$; every path of length at least three has class $0$; the classes of the two length-two returns at an interior vertex $0<j<m$ coincide, and $(0|1|0)$ has class $0$ ([[lem-the-khovanov-seidel-algebra-has-the-four-m-plus-one-path-basis]]).

[L4] $A_m\text{-mod}$ is the abelian category of finitely generated graded left $A_m$-modules and degree-zero maps; $A_m=\bigoplus_jP_j$ with each $P_j=A_me_j$ a finite graded projective left module generated by $e_j$; the internal shift $M\{p\}$ with $(M\{p\})_d=M_{d-p}$ is an automorphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$ carrying finite graded projectives to finite graded projectives; kernels and cokernels are computed degreewise; and multiplication by an element of $A_m$ on the right is a degree-zero $A_m$-linear map $P_j\{a\}\to P_{j'}\{b\}$ exactly when the element is homogeneous of the degree that matches the shifts ([[def-graded-khovanov-seidel-module-category-and-projectives]]).

[L5] $S_i$ is the graded left $A_m$-module whose underlying graded abelian group is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $0$ and $0$ elsewhere, with $e_i$ acting as the identity and every other vertex idempotent and every positive-length path acting as zero; for a prime $p$, $S_i/pS_i$ is the corresponding $\mathbb Z/p$ vertex module, and $0\to S_i\xrightarrow{p}S_i\to S_i/pS_i\to0$ is degreewise exact ([[def-vertex-khovanov-seidel-modules]]).

[L6] A projective resolution of an object $X$ of an abelian category is an augmented chain complex $\cdots\to Q_1\to Q_0\xrightarrow{\varepsilon}X\to0$ with every $Q_n$ projective that is exact at every displayed term ([[def-projective-resolution-in-an-abelian-category]]).

[L7] A homological double complex has morphisms $h_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p-1,q}$ and $v_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p,q-1}$ with $h^2=0$, $v^2=0$ and $h_{p,q-1}v_{p,q}+v_{p-1,q}h_{p,q}=0$, and is first quadrant when $C_{p,q}=0$ for $p<0$ or $q<0$; its direct-sum total complex has $\operatorname{Tot}_n=\coprod_{p+q=n}C_{p,q}$ and the unique differential $d$ with $d\iota_{p,q}=\iota_{p-1,q}h_{p,q}+\iota_{p,q-1}v_{p,q}$, which satisfies $d^2=0$ ([[def-homological-double-complex]], [[def-direct-sum-total-complex-of-a-double-complex]], [[lem-the-total-differential-squares-to-zero]]).

[L8] If a first-quadrant homological double complex $C$ has $H^v_q(C_{p,*})=0$ for every $p$ and every $q>0$, then with $B_p=H^v_0(C_{p,*})$ and the differential induced by $h$, the natural projection $\operatorname{Tot}(C)\to B$ that is the quotient map on $C_{n,0}$ and zero on the other summands of degree $n$ is a quasi-isomorphism ([[thm-acyclic-assembly-lemma-for-a-first-quadrant-double-complex]]).

[L9] For a chain map $f:C_\bullet\to D_\bullet$ in an additive category, $\operatorname{Cone}(f)_n=D_n\oplus C_{n-1}$ with $d(y,x)=(d^Dy+f(x),-d^Cx)$; for a chain map in an abelian category the canonical sequence $0\to D_\bullet\to\operatorname{Cone}(f)_\bullet\to C[1]_\bullet\to0$ is degreewise split short exact, and a short exact sequence of complexes induces a long exact homology sequence $\cdots\to H_n(D)\to H_n(\operatorname{Cone}(f))\to H_{n-1}(C)\xrightarrow{\partial}H_{n-1}(D)\to\cdots$ ([[def-mapping-cone-of-a-chain-map]], [[thm-the-canonical-mapping-cone-sequence-is-degreewise-split-short-exact]], [[thm-long-exact-sequence-in-homology]]).

[L10] An object $P$ of an abelian category is projective when every epimorphism $q:E\twoheadrightarrow M$ and every morphism $f:P\to M$ admit a lift $\widetilde f$ with $q\widetilde f=f$ ([[def-projective-object]]); a quasi-isomorphism is a chain map inducing an isomorphism on every homology object ([[def-quasi-isomorphism]]).





## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 *Basis and products in the vertex projectives.* By [L3] the $4m+1$ displayed classes form a $\mathbb Z$-basis of $A_m$, and each basis path has a source and a target, so by [F2] the module $P_j=A_me_j$ has the $\mathbb Z$-basis consisting of $e_j$ together with the basis paths ending at $j$, namely $e_j$; $u_{j-1}=(j-1|j)$; $d_j=(j+1|j)$; $r_j$, a symbol being omitted when the arrow has no source or target in $\{0,\dots,m\}$. Thus $P_j$ has basis $\{e_j,u_{j-1},d_j,r_j\}$ for $0<j<m$, basis $\{e_0,d_0\}$ for $j=0$ (since $(0|1|0)$ has class $0$ and $u_{-1}$ does not exist) and basis $\{e_m,u_{m-1},r_m\}$ for $j=m$ (since $d_m$ does not exist). For the multiplication we use [F1], [F2] and [L3]: $xe_j=x$ for $x\in P_j$, while $e_je_j=e_j$; $u_{j-1}u_j$ is a monotone length-two path, hence $0$ by [F1], while $r_ju_j$ and $r_jd_{j-1}$ are classes of paths of length three, hence $0$; and $d_ju_j=(j+1|j|j+1)$ is the return at $j+1$, which is exactly the basis return $r_{j+1}=(j+1|j|j+1)$. [F1, F2, L3]

2.1 *The staircase is a first-quadrant double complex in $A_m\text{-mod}$.* The entries $C_{p,q}=P_{j(p,q)}\{p\}$ are graded left $A_m$-modules, vanish for $p<0$ or $q<0$, and are nonzero exactly on $0\le p\le m-i$, $0\le q\le i+p$; the staircase condition $q\le i+p$ means $j(p,q)\ge0$, and $p\le m-i$ means $j(p,q)=i+p-q\le i+p\le m$, so the projective indices are in range. When both source and target entries are nonzero, right multiplication by $\alpha=(j|j-1)$ carries $P_j$ into $P_j\alpha\subseteq P_{j-1}$, and by [L4] it is a degree-zero module map $P_j\{p\}\to P_{j-1}\{p-1\}$ because $\deg\alpha=1$ exactly compensates the drop of the shift; right multiplication by $\beta=(j|j+1)$ carries $P_j$ into $P_{j+1}$ and is degree-zero as a map $P_j\{p\}\to P_{j+1}\{p\}$ because $\deg\beta=0$. Hence $h_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p-1,q}$ and $v_{p,q}:C_{p,q}\to C_{p,q-1}$ are degree-zero module maps, the sign $(-1)^p$ being a unit of the ground ring. On $C_{p,q}$, $h^2$ is right multiplication by the monotone length-two path $(j|j-1|j-2)$, hence is $0$ by [F1] (and $h^2=0$ at the boundary, where one of the two symbols does not exist); similarly $v^2$ is right multiplication by the monotone length-two path $(j|j+1|j+2)=0$ by [F1], and $hv+vh$ is right multiplication by $(-1)^{p-1}(j|j-1|j)+(-1)^p(j|j+1|j)=(-1)^{p-1}\bigl[(j|j-1|j)-(j|j+1|j)\bigr]=0$ by the relation of [F1]. For the mixed relation at the staircase boundary, if $p=0$ or $q=0$ both composites have zero target. If $p,q>0$ and $j=0$, the horizontal-first composite vanishes and the vertical-first composite multiplies by $(0|1|0)=0$. In all other nonzero mixed squares $0<j<m$, so the displayed return relation applies. Maps with zero source or target are zero by convention; the same convention handles the boundary cases of $h^2$ and $v^2$. Thus $(C,h,v)$ satisfies [L7]. [step 1.1, F1, L3, L4, L7]

2.2 *The columns are exact above their top entry.* Fix $0\le j\le m-1$ and $x=\alpha e_j+\beta u_{j-1}+\gamma d_j+\delta r_j\in P_j$. By the product rules of step 1.1, $xu_j=\alpha u_j+\beta u_{j-1}u_j+\gamma d_ju_j+\delta r_ju_j=\alpha u_j+\gamma r_{j+1}$, so by basis independence $xu_j=0$ if and only if $\alpha=\gamma=0$, that is, if and only if $x\in\mathbb Zu_{j-1}\oplus\mathbb Zr_j$. On the other hand right multiplication by $u_{j-1}$ on $P_{j-1}$ has image $\{x'u_{j-1}\}$ spanned by $e_{j-1}u_{j-1}=u_{j-1}$ and $d_{j-1}u_{j-1}=r_j$, the other two basis products being $0$ by step 1.1; so the image is $\mathbb Zu_{j-1}\oplus\mathbb Zr_j$ and $\ker(x\mapsto xu_j)=\operatorname{im}(x'\mapsto x'u_{j-1})$. For $j=0$ the same computation gives $xu_0=\alpha u_0+\gamma r_1$ (using $r_0=0$ by [L3]); the two images are independent basis classes, so the kernel is $0=\operatorname{im}(x'\mapsto x'u_{-1})$, with $P_{-1}=0$. In the column $C_{p,*}$ the maps $v$ multiply by $u_{i+p-q}$ and the statement just proved identifies, for every $q\ge1$, the kernel of $v:C_{p,q}\to C_{p,q-1}$ with the image of $v:C_{p,q+1}\to C_{p,q}$, since $C_{p,q+1}=P_{j-1}\{p\}$ and $C_{p,q-1}=P_{j+1}\{p\}$; hence $H^v_q(C_{p,*})=0$ for every $p$ and every $q>0$. [step 1.1, L3, L4]

3.1 *The bottom row of the assembled complex.* By step 2.2 and [L7], $B_p:=H^v_0(C_{p,*})=C_{p,0}/\operatorname{im}(v:C_{p,1}\to C_{p,0})$ for $0\le p\le m-i$, where $C_{p,0}=P_{i+p}\{p\}$ and the image is $P_{i+p-1}u_{i+p-1}=\mathbb Zu_{i+p-1}\oplus\mathbb Zr_{i+p}$ for $i+p\ge1$ and $0$ for $i=p=0$; the differential $h$ descends to $B$ by the anticommutation proved in step 2.1. So $B_p$ is the quotient of $P_{i+p}$ by the $\mathbb Z$-span of $u_{i+p-1}$ and $r_{i+p}$, with $\mathbb Z$-basis the classes $[e_{i+p}]$ and $[d_{i+p}]$ when $i+p<m$, and basis $[e_m]$ when $i+p=m$. On these classes $h:B_p\to B_{p-1}$ is induced by right multiplication by $d_{i+p-1}$, and by step 1.1 it sends $[e_{i+p}]\mapsto[d_{i+p-1}]$ and $[d_{i+p}]\mapsto[d_{i+p}d_{i+p-1}]=0$. [step 1.1, step 2.2, L4, L7]

4.1 *The homology of the bottom row is $S_i$.* Since $h_1:B_1\to B_0$ has image $\mathbb Z[d_i]$ (and is $0$ when $m=i$), $H_0(B)=B_0/\mathbb Z[d_i]=\mathbb Z[e_i]$; the quotient $C_{0,0}=P_i$ modulo the span of $u_{i-1},d_i,r_i$ is the quotient of $P_i$ by $A_mu_{i-1}+A_md_i$, which by step 1.1 equals the submodule generated by all positive-length paths ending at $i$, so $H_0(B)$ is $\mathbb Z$ in degree $0$ with $e_i$ acting as the identity and every other vertex idempotent and every positive-length path acting as zero: by [L5] this is exactly $S_i$. For $1\le p<m-i$, step 3.1 gives $\ker h_p=\mathbb Z[d_{i+p}]=\operatorname{im}h_{p+1}$, while at $p=m-i\ge1$ the group $B_p=\mathbb Z[e_m]$ maps injectively to $B_{p-1}$, so its kernel is $0=\operatorname{im}h_{p+1}$; when $i=m$ there are no positive-degree $B_p$ at all. Hence $H_p(B)=\ker h_p/\operatorname{im}h_{p+1}=0$ for every $p\ge1$. [step 1.1, step 3.1, L4, L5]

5.1 *Totalisation is a finite graded projective resolution of $S_i$.* Step 2.2 verifies the hypothesis of [L8], so the projection $\operatorname{Tot}(C)\to B$ is a quasi-isomorphism; by step 4.1 and [L10] therefore $H_0(\operatorname{Tot})\cong S_i$ and $H_n(\operatorname{Tot})=0$ for $n\ne0$. Each $\operatorname{Tot}_n$ is the finite direct sum of the $C_{p,q}=P_{j(p,q)}\{p\}$ with $p+q=n$, a finite direct sum of internal shifts of vertex projectives; each $P_j$ is finite graded projective by [L4], its shift is finite graded projective because $\{p\}$ is an automorphism of $A_m\text{-mod}$ by [L4], and a finite direct sum of projective objects is projective by the lifting property applied componentwise with the universal property of the coproduct, so every term of $\operatorname{Tot}$ is finite graded projective. Only the finitely many degrees $0\le n\le m+(m-i)$ can be nonzero, and the differential satisfies $d^2=0$ by [L7]. The augmentation $\varepsilon:\operatorname{Tot}_0=C_{0,0}=P_i\to S_i$ is the quotient map of step 4.1; since the projection of [L8], followed by the augmentation $B_0\to S_i$, restricts to this quotient on $C_{0,0}$, the augmented complex is exact at $P_i$ and at every other term, so by [L6] $\operatorname{Tot}$ is a finite graded projective resolution of $S_i$. [step 2.2, step 4.1, L4, L6, L7, L8, L10]

6.1 *The cone of multiplication by $p$ resolves $S_i/pS_i$.* Multiplication by the integer $p$ is an $A_m$-linear endomorphism of each $P_j$ preserving degrees, so it defines a degree-zero chain map $\mu_p:\operatorname{Tot}_\bullet\to\operatorname{Tot}_\bullet$. By [L9] the cone has $\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p)_n=\operatorname{Tot}_n\oplus\operatorname{Tot}_{n-1}$, a finite direct sum of finite graded projectives, and there is a degreewise split short exact sequence of complexes $0\to\operatorname{Tot}\to\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p)\to\operatorname{Tot}[1]\to0$ whose long exact homology sequence reads $H_n(\operatorname{Tot})\xrightarrow{\mu_p}H_n(\operatorname{Tot})\to H_n(\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p))\to H_{n-1}(\operatorname{Tot})\xrightarrow{\mu_p}H_{n-1}(\operatorname{Tot})$. By step 5.1 the only nonzero homology of $\operatorname{Tot}$ is $H_0=S_i$; hence $H_0(\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p))=\operatorname{coker}(p:S_i\to S_i)=S_i/pS_i$, $H_1(\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p))=\ker(p:S_i\to S_i)=0$ because $S_i=\mathbb Z$ is torsion free and $p\ne0$, and $H_n(\operatorname{Cone}(\mu_p))=0$ for $n\ge2$ and for $n\le-1$. So the cone is a finite complex of finite graded projectives whose only homology is $S_i/pS_i$ in degree $0$, that is, a finite graded projective resolution of $S_i/pS_i$ in the sense of [L6]. [step 5.1, L5, L6, L9]

7.1 *Conclusion.* The double complex $(C,h,v)$ of step 2.1 is first quadrant, its columns are exact above the top entry by step 2.2, and its total complex is the finite graded projective resolution of $S_i$ described in claim 1 (steps 4.1 and 5.1); the mapping cone of multiplication by $p$ on it is the finite graded projective resolution of $S_i/pS_i$ described in claim 2 (step 6.1). The word "simple" in the identifier of this item asserts nothing beyond the explicit vertex modules $S_i$ of [L5]. [step 2.1, step 5.1, step 6.1] ∎
