## Statement

Let $F$ be a field with two normalized discrete valuations $v_1,v_2$, each
making $F$ complete with finite residue field. Then $v_1=v_2$. Consequently
every field automorphism of a nonarchimedean local field with finite residue
field preserves its normalized valuation and its valuation ring. These
assertions require no Choice axiom.

Assume Countable Choice for the following additional consequence. If $L/K$
is any finite field extension and $K,L$ are equipped with complete normalized
discrete valuations with finite residue fields, then the given valuation on
$L$ is the normalized extending valuation constructed from that on $K$.
In particular, there is a positive integer $e$ such that
$$v_L(x)=e\,v_K(x)\quad(x\in K^\times),$$
and the given valuation rings, residue extension and ramification index are
those used by the local degree formula $[L:K]=e[k_L:k_K]$. No compatibility
of the two given valuations is an additional hypothesis.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** $F$ and the two complete normalized discrete valuations with finite
residue cardinalities $q_1,q_2\ge2$. Only the final finite-extension clause
additionally assumes Countable Choice and its displayed $L/K$ data.

[F1] [[def-local-field-valuation-ring-uniformizer-and-residue-field]]: each
valuation ring $\mathcal O_i$ is a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal
$\mathfrak m_i$ generated by a uniformizer, and its field is complete.

[F2] [[thm-multiplicative-group-of-a-finite-field-is-cyclic]]: the
multiplicative group of a finite field of cardinality $q_i$ has order
$q_i-1$ and is cyclic.

[F3] [[lem-hensel-factor-lifting-over-a-complete-valued-field]]: a simple
residue root of a monic integral polynomial lifts uniquely over a complete
nonarchimedean field. Its actual simple-root proof uses deterministic
successive corrections, without a Choice assumption.

[A1] [[def-countable-choice]]: a countable family of nonempty sets has a
choice function; this is assumed only in step 5.1.

[F4] [[thm-finite-extension-local-degree-equals-ramification-index-times-residue-degree]]:
under [A1], the unique extending absolute value on a finite extension is
complete, has discrete normalized valuation $v'_L=e w_L$ with
$w_L|_K=v_K$, has finite residue degree $f$, and satisfies $[L:K]=ef$.

## Proof

1.1 Let $Q=1+\operatorname{lcm}(q_1-1,q_2-1)$, so $Q>1$ and $Q-1$ is divisible by both residue multiplicative group orders. By [F2], every residue element $t$ in either field satisfies $t^Q=t$, including $t=0$. Set $f(T)=T^Q-T-1$ and $V=\{f(x)^{-1}:x\in F\}$. For either valuation, if $x$ is integral then $f(x)$ reduces to $-1$ and is a unit. If its value is negative, $x^Q$ is the unique term of smallest valuation in $f(x)$, so $v_i(f(x))=Qv_i(x)<0$. Thus $f$ has no zero in $F$ and $V\subseteq\mathcal O_i$ for both $i$. The set $V$ and the unital subring $R$ generated by $V-V$ are the same algebraic sets for the two valuations; in particular $R\subseteq\mathcal O_1\cap\mathcal O_2$. [F1, F2, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 For $y\in\mathfrak m_i$, the element $y-1$ is a unit. The monic integral polynomial $h(T)=f(T)-(y-1)^{-1}$ has residue polynomial $T^Q-T$, with simple root $0$: because $Q>1$, $h'(0)=-1$ in every residue characteristic, independently of $Q$ modulo that characteristic. By [F3], there is an $x\in\mathfrak m_i$ with $f(x)=(y-1)^{-1}$. Since $f(0)=-1$, we have $y=f(x)^{-1}-f(0)^{-1}\in V-V$. Therefore $\mathfrak m_i\subseteq R\subseteq\mathcal O_i$ for each $i$. This lifts one specified residue root at a time and makes no infinite selection. [F1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The integral closure of $R$ in $F$ is exactly $\mathcal O_i$, for each $i$. Indeed if $z\in\mathcal O_i$, then $c=z^Q-z\in\mathfrak m_i\subseteq R$ by step 1.1, and $z$ satisfies the monic polynomial $T^Q-T-c$ over $R$. If instead $v_i(z)<0$, then in any purported monic equation $z^d+\sum_{j<d}r_jz^j=0$ with $r_j\in R$, the leading term has value $dv_i(z)$, strictly smaller than all other nonzero terms because $r_j\in\mathcal O_i$. It cannot cancel. Thus such $z$ is not integral over $R$. The same integral closure consequently equals both valuation rings. [F1, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Equal discrete valuation rings have equal unit groups, maximal ideals and all ideal powers. For an integral nonzero element, membership in $\mathfrak m^j\setminus\mathfrak m^{j+1}$ determines the normalized value $j$; negative values follow by inversion. Therefore $v_1=v_2$, including the common value $+\infty$ at zero. If $\sigma$ is a field automorphism, $x\mapsto v_1(\sigma x)$ is again a complete normalized discrete valuation with isomorphic finite residue field: $\sigma$ is by construction an isometry from that pulled-back valuation to $v_1$. The uniqueness just proved gives $v_1(\sigma x)=v_1(x)$. This proves automorphism invariance without assuming continuity beforehand. [F1, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 For the finite-extension clause assume [A1] and apply [F4] to the given valued field $K$ and abstract finite extension $L/K$. Its constructed normalized valuation $v'_L$ is complete, discrete and has finite residue field, since its residue degree is finite over the finite $k_K$. The given $v_L$ has these properties too. Steps 1.1–4.1 applied on the same underlying field $L$ give $v_L=v'_L$. Since $v'_L=e w_L$ and $w_L|_K=v_K$, we get $v_L|_{K^\times}=e v_K$. Equality of valuations identifies their rings, maximal ideals and residue fields; the compatible residue inclusion follows because base integral elements remain integral and base units remain units. Thus the given data have exactly the index $e$ and residue degree $f$ of [F4], proving the stated degree formula for them. The only use of Countable Choice is this construction [F4]. [A1, F1, F4, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Source adaptation

This is an explicit intrinsic-ring reconstruction for the standard local
valuation setting, not the attribution of this whole two-clause statement to
a numbered theorem in the references. The comparison polynomial works for
two different residue cardinalities simultaneously. The same polynomial/Hensel
method also supplies automorphism invariance in the local normality proof;
no source citation substitutes for any step above.
