## Statement

Assume Countable Choice (CC). Let $L/K$ be a finite abelian extension of number fields, let $G=\operatorname{Gal}(L/K)$, and let $\widehat G=\operatorname{Hom}(G,\mathbb C^\times)$. For each $\chi\in\widehat G$, let $\mathfrak f(\chi)$ be its finite Artin conductor ideal and let $\mathfrak d_{L/K}$ be the relative discriminant ideal. Then

$$\mathfrak d_{L/K}=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat G}\mathfrak f(\chi).$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Countable Choice (CC); a finite abelian extension $L/K$ of number fields. All completions used below are constructed by the CC completion theorem, at the explicit prime valuations. The stronger hypotheses of the former two global localization suppliers are not assumed.

[F1] Upper ramification breaks of a finite abelian local extension are integers. [[def-herbrand-function-and-upper-ramification-numbering]] [[thm-hasse-arftheorem-for-finite-abelian-local-extensions]]

[F2] The different exponent of a finite local Galois extension is $d=\sum_{i\ge0}(|D_i|-1)$. [[thm-local-different-exponent-as-ramification-group-sum]]

[F3] The relative discriminant ideal is generated by determinants of relative trace Gram matrices of integral $K$-bases. [[def-relative-discriminant-ideal-of-a-number-field-extension]]

[F4] The Artin conductor exponent is the stated lower-filtration codimension sum. [[def-artin-conductor-of-a-global-ray-class-character]]

[F5] A finite abelian group has exactly its order many irreducible complex characters, all of degree one. [[thm-a-finite-group-is-abelian-iff-all-its-irreducible-complex-characters-have-degree-one]] [[thm-number-of-irreducible-representations-equals-the-number-of-conjugacy-classes-when-k-is-algebraically-closed-and-char-k-does-not-divide-group-order]]

[F6] The regular character, fixed-space dimension and multiplicity inner-product formulas have their usual finite-dimensional meanings. [[def-standard-inner-product-on-complex-class-functions]] [[def-character-of-a-complex-representation]] [[def-trivial-regular-and-permutation-representations]] [[thm-character-of-the-regular-representation]] [[cor-multiplicity-of-an-irreducible-summand-is-a-character-inner-product]]

[F7] For compatible finite local extensions, the local degree is $ef$; in a finite local Galois extension the inertia order is $e$. Every automorphism of a complete discretely valued field with finite residue field preserves its normalized valuation, without an extra continuity assumption. [[thm-local-inertia-fixed-field-is-maximal-unramified-extension]] [[thm-finite-extension-local-degree-equals-ramification-index-times-residue-degree]] [[lem-nonarchimedean-local-valuations-are-intrinsic]]

[F8] Only finitely many rational primes ramify in the number field $L$. The finite relative-prime consequence is derived in step 6.1. [[cor-only-finitely-many-primes-ramify]]

[F9] Under CC an absolutely valued field has a complete valued-field completion, with dense isometric embedding, unchanged nonarchimedean value group and residue field, and the completion universal property. [[thm-completion-of-an-absolutely-valued-field]]

[F10] Rings of integers have finite integral $\mathbb Z$-bases. Their nonzero ideals have finite quotients and unique finite prime factorizations in ZF; the proof of the latter explicitly constructs the DVR $\mathcal O_{K,\mathfrak p}$ in steps 2.3–6.1. Finite CRT applies to comaximal prime powers. [[thm-ring-of-integers-free-of-rank-degree]] [[lem-nonzero-number-field-ideal-has-finite-quotient]] [[thm-number-field-integral-ideal-factorisation-in-zf]] [[thm-chinese-remainder-theorem-for-comaximal-ideals]]

[F11] A separable finite extension has a nondegenerate trace pairing. Trace is the trace of its finite multiplication matrix, and a finite Galois extension is a finite separable splitting field with automorphism-group order equal to its degree. [[thm-trace-form-is-nondegenerate-iff-separable]] [[def-field-norm-and-trace]] [[thm-finite-galois-extension-characterizations]] The adjugate identity holds over every commutative ring. [[thm-adjugate-identity-over-a-commutative-ring]]

[F12] The local codifferent is the trace-dual set; when it is a nonzero fractional ideal its inverse is the different. [[def-different-ideal-of-a-finite-local-extension]]

[A1] CC is used only for the completion and local-field suppliers [F1], [F2], [F7], [F9]. Every selection below is finite, or uses least natural-number codes from a single fixed finite integral basis. [[def-countable-choice]]

## Proof

**Given:** The data above. A prime is fixed one at a time; no simultaneous choice of completions or bases indexed by primes is made.

1.1 Fix a nonzero prime $\mathfrak p$ of $R=\mathcal O_K$, put $T=\mathcal O_L$, and let $R_0=R_{\mathfrak p}$. This is a DVR by the exact ZF construction in [F10]; its fraction field is $K$. The same applies to $T_{\mathfrak P}$ at every prime $\mathfrak P$ above $\mathfrak p$, with finite residue fields by [F10]. Thus their normalized discrete valuations define nonarchimedean absolute values. Apply [F9] to construct $F=K_{\mathfrak p}$ and $E_{\mathfrak P}=L_{\mathfrak P}$, and put $A=\mathcal O_F$, $B_{\mathfrak P}=\mathcal O_{E_{\mathfrak P}}$. Their integer rings are complete DVRs, with the original value groups and residue fields. They identify with the corresponding adic inverse limits: an integral Cauchy class gives its eventual residues; conversely compatible residues have representatives in the original DVR forming a Cauchy sequence. Representatives may be chosen by least codes in the countable DVR, using one fixed integral $\mathbb Z$-basis, or by CC. The limits and these identifications preserve addition and multiplication. No AC-qualified number-field completion definition or polynomial-factor theorem is used. [F9, F10, A1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The $R_0$-module $M=T\otimes_RR_0$ is finite and torsion-free: a finite $\mathbb Z$-basis of $T$ also generates it over $R_0$, and it embeds in $L$. It is free of rank $n=[L:K]$ by the following finite DVR argument. Choose a $K$-basis in $M$, and clear the finitely many coordinates of a generating list of $M$ to embed $M$ in a scaled $R_0^n$. Every submodule of $R_0^n$ is free of rank at most $n$: project to the first coordinate, take a generator of the image ideal of least valuation, lift it once, and split off that vector; the kernel is a submodule of $R_0^{n-1}$ and induction completes the basis. Only finitely many choices occur. Factor $\mathfrak pT=\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}\mathfrak P^{e_{\mathfrak P}}$ by [F10]. This finite list is nonempty: $M$ is free of positive rank, so $\mathfrak pM\ne M$ and therefore $\mathfrak pT\ne T$. Finite CRT gives $M/\mathfrak p^rM\cong\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}T/\mathfrak P^{r e_{\mathfrak P}}$; elements of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$ are units in every quotient, so localization does not change it. Taking inverse limits in a finite basis of $M$, and using cofinality of the powers $r e_{\mathfrak P}$, gives the ring isomorphisms $C:=M\otimes_{R_0}A\cong\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}B_{\mathfrak P}$ and $L\otimes_KF\cong\prod_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}E_{\mathfrak P}$. In the second isomorphism we invert a uniformizer of $R_0$, whose value in each factor is the positive integer $e_{\mathfrak P}$. In particular each $B_{\mathfrak P}$ is a finite direct summand of a finite free $A$-module; the same DVR-submodule induction makes it free over $A$. Its rank is $[E_{\mathfrak P}:F]$. Traces become sums of the factor traces, since multiplication matrices become block diagonal after this scalar extension. This proves the required integral and field completion decomposition directly under CC, with no global Dedekind-module decomposition or choice of extensions of valuations. [F9, F10, F11, step 1.1, A1, construct, algebra]

3.1 For the fixed $\mathfrak P$, its decomposition subgroup $D$ preserves the prime valuation, because automorphisms permute the finite prime factorizations of principal ideals. Hence each element of $D$ extends uniquely and isometrically to $E_{\mathfrak P}$, fixing $F$, and restriction is injective by density. The factor surjection in step 2.1 shows $E_{\mathfrak P}=F[L]$. Since $L/K$ is a finite separable splitting field, all roots of one defining separable polynomial already lie in its embedded copy of $L$; they generate $F[L]$. Thus $E_{\mathfrak P}/F$ is a finite Galois extension. Any $F$-automorphism of it permutes those roots, restricts to a $K$-automorphism of $L$, and preserves its local valuation by [F7]; its restriction therefore stabilizes $\mathfrak P$. Consequently $D\cong\operatorname{Gal}(E_{\mathfrak P}/F)$ and $|D|=[E_{\mathfrak P}:F]=ef$, with $|D_0|=e$ by [F7]. The primes above $\mathfrak p$ form one $G$-orbit: otherwise finite CRT gives $a\in T$ congruent to $0$ on one orbit and $1$ on all others. The product $c=\prod_{\sigma\in G}\sigma(a)$ is $G$-fixed and integral, hence in $R$; it is in $\mathfrak p$ using the first orbit and congruent to $1$ at a prime in another, a contradiction. Thus their number is $g=|G|/|D|$. These automorphisms also give isometries between the completion factors, preserving residue degrees, traces, differents and ramification filtrations; the values $e,f,d$ are common to the factors. [F7, F10, F11, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

3.2 Fix one factor $B=B_{\mathfrak P}$ with fraction field $E$. In an $A$-basis of $B$, its trace Gram matrix $Q$ has entries in $A$, because multiplication by any element of the finite free $A$-algebra $B$ has an integral matrix. It is nonsingular by separability and [F11]. The trace-dual set is $B^\vee=Q^{-1}A^r$ in this basis. It contains $B$, is stable under multiplication by $B$, and is bounded inside $(\det Q)^{-1}B$ by the adjugate identity. It is therefore a nonzero fractional ideal of the DVR $B$, of the form $\pi_B^{-d}B$ for some $d\ge0$; [F12] identifies $d$ with the different exponent. Multiplication by $Q$ identifies $B^\vee/B$ with $A^r/QA^r$. Diagonalize $Q$ over the DVR by choosing an entry of least valuation, clearing its row and column, and iterating on the nonsingular remaining block. Invertible row and column operations preserve the cokernel. If its diagonal valuations are $a_i$, its cardinality is $\#k_F^{\sum_i a_i}=\#k_F^{v_F(\det Q)}$. The filtration of $\pi_B^{-d}B/B$ has $d$ successive quotients isomorphic to $k_E$, so its cardinality is also $\#k_F^{fd}$. Hence $v_F(\det Q)=fd$, including $d=0$. [F7, F11, F12, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 An $R_0$-basis of $M$ computes the localization of the relative discriminant ideal: every integral tuple has coordinates in $R_0$, so its Gram determinant is the basis determinant times the square of an integral coordinate determinant. Conversely, clearing the finitely many denominators of that local basis by one element of $R\setminus\mathfrak p$ produces an integral $K$-basis in $T$ without changing the $\mathfrak p$-valuation. Passing from $R_0$ to its complete DVR $A$ preserves that valuation. In the decomposition of step 2.1 the product of the factor bases is an $A$-basis of $C$; changing an $A$-basis multiplies a Gram determinant by the square of a unit. The trace matrix is block diagonal by step 2.1. Thus [F3] and step 3.2 give $v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak d_{L/K})=\sum_{\mathfrak P\mid\mathfrak p}f_{\mathfrak P/\mathfrak p}d_{\mathfrak P}=gfd$. This proves the required discriminant localization at the original CC scope rather than using the AC-qualified supplier. [F3, F10, step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2, algebra]

4.2 Restriction $\widehat G\to\widehat D$ is surjective: extend a character through a finite chain of cyclic quotients, choosing a complex root of the specified value at each finite step. The kernel is $\widehat{G/D}$, so every character of $D$ has exactly $|G|/|D|$ extensions by [F5]. By [F5] and [F6], the regular representation of $D$ is the direct sum of its one-dimensional characters once each. Its $D_i$-fixed space consists of coefficient functions constant on the $|D|/|D_i|$ left orbits. Therefore $\sum_{\psi\in\widehat D}\dim(V_\psi/V_\psi^{D_i})=|D|-|D|/|D_i|$. Applying [F4] gives $\sum_{\psi\in\widehat D}a_{\mathfrak p}(\psi)=\frac{|D|}{|D_0|}\sum_{i\ge0}(|D_i|-1)$. These are finite sums because the lower groups eventually become trivial. [F4, F5, F6, step 3.1, algebra]

5.1 Multiply step 4.2 by $|G|/|D|$, the number of extensions of each character, to get $\sum_{\chi\in\widehat G}a_{\mathfrak p}(\chi)=\frac{|G|}{|D_0|}\sum_{i\ge0}(|D_i|-1)=gfd$. Here [F2] gives the different exponent, while step 3.1 supplies $|D_0|=e$, $|D|=ef$, and $|G|=g|D|$. By step 4.1 this is precisely $v_{\mathfrak p}(\mathfrak d_{L/K})$. [F2, F4, step 3.1, step 4.1, step 4.2, algebra]

6.1 This equality was proved for each finite prime separately. Only finitely many contribute: [F8] gives finitely many rational primes ramifying in $L/\mathbb Q$; above each, [F10] gives finitely many primes of $K$. Outside these, multiplication of ramification indices in the valuation tower makes $e(L_{\mathfrak P}/K_{\mathfrak p})=1$, so $D_0$ and every $D_i$ are trivial by step 3.1. Hasse–Arf [F1] gives the integral exponents of [F4]. Equality of all prime exponents and unique ideal factorization [F10] imply $\mathfrak d_{L/K}=\prod_{\chi\in\widehat G}\mathfrak f(\chi)$. All completion and local-field uses have exactly the assumed CC scope; every global localization step was established in the finite constructions above. [F1, F4, F8, F10, step 5.1, A1, algebra] ∎
