## Statement

Let a group $G$ act without inversions on a simplicial tree $T$. Let
$\mathcal G$ be the quotient graph of groups built from this action, and let
$T_0$ be a maximal subtree of the quotient graph. Then

$$G\cong \pi_1(\mathcal G,T_0).$$

Moreover the Bass-Serre tree of $(\mathcal G,T_0)$ is $G$-equivariantly
isomorphic to the original tree $T$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A group $G$ acting without inversions on a simplicial tree $T$, its quotient graph of groups $\mathcal G$, and a maximal subtree $T_0$ of the quotient graph.

[L1] The quotient graph-of-groups construction records vertex and edge stabilizers together with the boundary monomorphisms induced by chosen lifts, with connecting elements satisfying $g_{\bar e}=g_e^{-1}$. ([[def-quotient-graph-of-groups-from-a-tree-action]])

[L2] The path group is generated by the vertex groups and oriented edges, subject to the reversal and edge-group conjugacy relations. ([[def-path-group-of-a-graph-of-groups]])

[L3] The relative fundamental group is obtained by killing the edges of the chosen maximal subtree. ([[def-fundamental-group-of-a-graph-of-groups-relative-to-a-maximal-tree]])

[L4] The Bass-Serre tree has vertices and edges given by cosets of the vertex and edge groups. ([[def-bass-serre-tree-of-a-graph-of-groups]])

[L5] Every element of the graph-of-groups fundamental group has a reduced representative, and a reduced word is trivial only in the edge-length-$0$ identity case. ([[thm-normal-form-for-fundamental-groups-of-graphs-of-groups]])

[L6] The Bass-Serre coset graph is a tree. ([[lem-the-bass-serre-coset-graph-is-a-tree]])

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Choose the lift data from [L1] successively along the maximal subtree $T_0$ so that every tree edge has connecting element $g_e=1$. Send each chosen vertex stabilizer element to itself in $G$ and each oriented edge symbol to the corresponding $g_e$. The identity $g_{\bar e}=g_e^{-1}$ from [L1] respects the reversal relation, and the definition of the boundary map respects the conjugacy relation of [L2], so this defines a homomorphism from the path group to $G$. Because every tree edge maps to $1$, [L3] yields a homomorphism $$ \Phi:\pi_1(\mathcal G,T_0)\longrightarrow G. $$ [L1, L2, L3, given, construct]

2.1 Fix a chosen lift $\widetilde v_0$ of some quotient vertex $v_0$. Let $g\in G$. The unique path in the tree $T$ from $\widetilde v_0$ to $g\widetilde v_0$ projects to an edge path $e_1,\dots,e_n$ in the quotient graph. Inductively along that path, choose stabilizer elements $h_0,\dots,h_n$ at the intermediate chosen lifts so that the $j$th lifted edge is $\Phi(h_0e_1h_1\cdots e_j)\widetilde e_j$ and its terminal vertex is $\Phi(h_0e_1h_1\cdots e_jh_j)\widetilde{t(e_j)}$. At the end one obtains $g=\Phi(h_0e_1h_1\cdots e_nh_n)$. Hence $\Phi$ is surjective. [L1, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 Let $\widetilde X$ be the Bass-Serre tree of $(\mathcal G,T_0)$. Using [L4], define $$ \Psi(\gamma G_v):=\Phi(\gamma)\widetilde v,\qquad \Psi(\gamma G_e):=\Phi(\gamma)\widetilde e. $$ This is well defined because the vertex and edge groups in [L1] are actual stabilizer subgroups of the chosen lifts, and the incidence formulas of [L4] match the connecting elements $g_e$ by construction. [L1, L4, step 1.1]

3.1 The map $\Psi$ is $\Phi$-equivariant by definition. At a chosen vertex coset $G_v$, the incident edges of $\widetilde X$ above an oriented quotient edge $e$ are the cosets $hG_e$ with $h\in G_v$, and $\Psi$ sends them bijectively to the incident edges $h\widetilde e$ at the chosen lift $\widetilde v$. By equivariance the same holds at every vertex. Since [L6] says $\widetilde X$ is a tree and step 2.1 shows that every translate of every chosen lift lies in the image, $\Psi$ is a covering map from a connected tree onto the tree $T$, hence an isomorphism of graphs. [L6, step 2.1, step 2.2]

3.2 Let $\gamma\in\pi_1(\mathcal G,T_0)$ satisfy $\Phi(\gamma)=1$. By [L5], choose a reduced closed graph-of-groups word representing $\gamma$. Under the map $\Psi$ of step 2.2, its closed edge path begins and ends over the same quotient vertex, and the endpoint is the $\Phi(\gamma)$-translate of the starting point. Thus it traces a closed path in $T$. If the word had positive edge length, this would be a nontrivial reduced closed path in the tree $T$, impossible. Therefore the reduced representative has edge length $0$, so by the length-$0$ clause of [L5] it is just one coefficient from a chosen vertex stabilizer. But $\Phi$ restricts on each vertex group to the actual inclusion into $G$, so $\Phi(\gamma)=1$ forces that coefficient to be the identity. Hence $\gamma=1$, and $\Phi$ is injective. [L5, step 2.2, algebra]

4.1 Steps 2.1 and 3.2 show that $\Phi$ is an isomorphism, and step 3.1 gives the $G$-equivariant identification of the Bass-Serre tree with the original tree. This proves both claims. [step 2.1, step 3.1, step 3.2] ∎
