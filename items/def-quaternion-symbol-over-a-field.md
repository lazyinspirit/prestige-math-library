## Definition

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic different from $2$, and let
$a,b\in F^\times$. The **quaternion algebra** $Q_F(a,b)$ is the associative
$F$-algebra generated by $i,j$ with relations
$$i^2=a,\qquad j^2=b,\qquad ij=-ji.$$
Its **quaternion symbol** $(a,b)_F$ is the similarity class of
$Q_F(a,b)$ in the classical Brauer group
$\operatorname{Br}_{\mathrm{alg}}(F)$ of finite-dimensional central simple
$F$-algebras. The group law is tensor product, and the inverse of a class is
represented by the opposite algebra. The symbol is killed by $2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A field $F$ with $\operatorname{char}(F)\ne2$ and nonzero
$a,b\in F$.

[F1] The classical Brauer group is the abelian group of similarity classes
of finite-dimensional central simple algebras over $F$.
[[def-brauer-group-as-galois-cohomology]]

[F2] Similarity classes use tensor product for addition and the opposite
algebra for inversion. [[def-brauer-group-as-galois-cohomology]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** split the algebra over a separable closure, then use
the quaternion conjugation to identify it with its opposite.

**Given:** The data above.

1.1 In a separable closure $F_s$, choose $r,s\in F_s^\times$ with
$r^2=a$ and $s^2=b$. The assignments
$$i\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}r&0\\0&-r\end{pmatrix},\qquad
j\longmapsto\begin{pmatrix}0&s\\s&0\end{pmatrix}$$
satisfy the defining relations. The images of $1,i,j,ij$ are linearly
independent because $2rs\ne0$, so they span $M_2(F_s)$. Hence
$Q_F(a,b)\otimes_FF_s\cong M_2(F_s)$ and $Q_F(a,b)$ has dimension four.
If its center were larger than $F$, scalar extension would give a center
larger than $F_s$; if it had a nonzero proper two-sided ideal, faithful scalar
extension would give such an ideal in $M_2(F_s)$. Both are impossible.
Thus it is central simple and [F1] assigns it a Brauer class.
[F1, given, algebra]

1.2 The map fixing $F$ and sending $i\mapsto-i$, $j\mapsto-j$ reverses
products, since it preserves the relations and sends $ij$ to $-ij=ji$.
It therefore identifies $Q_F(a,b)$ with its opposite algebra. By [F2], the
opposite class is the inverse, so $(a,b)_F=-(a,b)_F$ and
$2(a,b)_F=0$. [F2, step 1.1, algebra] ∎
