## Statement

Over any field $F$, at any level $\ell\ge1$, with $q_H\ne1$ of finite order
$e\ge2$ and charges $Q_a=q_H^{c_a}$, use the actual multipartition-summand
Schur cover and standards of
[[thm-cyclotomic-schur-standards-and-schur-functor-control-block-linkage]] and
the integer $J_{\lambda\mu}$ of
[[thm-cyclotomic-schur-jantzen-sum-formula-from-rim-hook-valuations]]. Let
$\sim_J$ be the equivalence relation on multipartitions generated by
$J_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$ or $J_{\mu\lambda}\ne0$. Then
$$\lambda\sim_J\mu\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \Delta^\lambda,\Delta^\mu\text{ lie in one Schur block}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad S^\lambda,S^\mu\text{ lie in one Hecke block}.$$
For rank $0$ there is the single empty class. No residue-content block
assertion is used as an input.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Schur/Specht block correspondence and standard $K_0$ basis [F1],
the terminating radical-first Jantzen formula [F2], the block convention [F3]
and the Grothendieck basis convention [F4].

[F1] Standard block purity, shared-simple-factor linkage, the unitriangular
standard $K_0$ basis, and the Schur/Specht block correspondence: two standards
$\Delta^\lambda,\Delta^\mu$ share a block exactly when the corresponding
Murphy Specht modules $S^\lambda,S^\mu$ share a block, and cell modules share
a block exactly when linked by a finite chain of cell modules whose
neighbouring terms share a simple factor
([[thm-cyclotomic-schur-standards-and-schur-functor-control-block-linkage]]).

[F2] The Jantzen filtration $(\Delta_F(\lambda)^s)$ is radical-first and
terminating, with
$\sum_{s>0}[\Delta_F(\lambda)^s]=\sum_\mu J_{\lambda\mu}[\Delta_F(\mu)]$ in
the integer Grothendieck group
([[thm-cyclotomic-schur-jantzen-sum-formula-from-rim-hook-valuations]]).

[F3] Blocks are the primitive-central-idempotent blocks
([[def-blocks-of-a-finite-dimensional-algebra-by-primitive-central-idempotents]]).

[F4] The Grothendieck group has the standard classes as a $\mathbb Z$-basis
because the decomposition matrix to simple classes is triangular with
diagonal $1$
([[def-grothendieck-group-of-an-essentially-small-abelian-category]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

**Given:** The data and suppliers of [F1]–[F4].

1.1 Standard classes form a $\mathbb Z$-basis. The matrix of standard classes to simple classes is triangular with diagonal one by [F1], hence [F4] makes the classes $[\Delta(\lambda)]$ a $\mathbb Z$-basis of the integer Grothendieck group of the Schur cover, and the classes of the block projections are independent as well. [F1, F4]

2.1 Nonzero $J$-coefficients link the same block. Suppose $J_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$ and project the Jantzen sum identity of [F2] to all blocks other than the block of $\Delta(\lambda)$. Its left side is zero because each $\Delta_F(\lambda)^i$ is a submodule of the block-pure standard $\Delta(\lambda)$; its right side is a $\mathbb Z$-linear combination of standard classes lying in those other blocks, and the coefficient of $[\Delta(\mu)]$ in it is exactly $J_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$. Basis independence from step 1.1 forces that coefficient to vanish, a contradiction unless $\Delta(\mu)$ lies in the block of $\Delta(\lambda)$. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

2.2 Nonzero decomposition numbers force a $J$-chain. Put $d_{\lambda\mu}=[\Delta(\lambda):L(\mu)]$. For $\lambda\ne\mu$, the radical $\Delta(\lambda)^1=\operatorname{rad}\Delta(\lambda)$ contains every composition factor $L(\mu)$ of $\Delta(\lambda)$, and all higher filtration terms lie in that radical, so the coefficient of $[L(\mu)]$ in the left side of the Jantzen identity is nonzero exactly when $d_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$; on the right it is $\sum_\nu J_{\lambda\nu}d_{\nu\mu}$. If $d_{\lambda\mu}\ne0$, this integer is nonzero, so some $\nu$ satisfies $J_{\lambda\nu}\ne0$ and $d_{\nu\mu}\ne0$, with $\lambda\rhd\nu\unrhd\mu$. Repeating strictly decreases the index in the finite dominance poset until $\nu=\mu$, so $\lambda\sim_J\mu$. Consequently any two standards sharing a simple factor are both $J$-connected to that factor's index, and therefore to each other. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1]

3.1 Blocks and Hecke linkage. By [F1], two standards share a Schur block exactly when they are linked by a finite chain of standards whose neighbouring terms share a simple factor. Step 2.2 converts each such shared factor into a $J$-edge to the common index, and step 2.1 shows every $J$-edge lies inside one block; hence sharing a Schur block is equivalent to $\sim_J$-connectivity. Applying the established Schur/Specht block correspondence of [F1] transfers this equivalence to the Murphy Specht modules and the Hecke algebra. In rank $0$ there is one empty standard, all positive filtration terms vanish, and the single empty class is its own Jantzen class; the argument never reduces $J$ coefficients modulo the field characteristic. [F1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎

## Remarks

The lemma proves the Lyle–Mathas block-linkage description of the Jantzen
equivalence from the local Jantzen sum formula rather than citing it: nonzero
decomposition numbers propagate along strictly decreasing dominance chains,
and the cellular block purity of the standards supplies the converse. No
choice principle is used: all chains, coefficient sums and block projections
are finite.
