## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $F=\langle a,b\rangle$ be the free group on
$a,b$, given the discrete topology, and let $A=\langle a\rangle$ and
$B=\langle b\rangle$. Then $F=A*B$ is the free product of two infinite cyclic
groups,
$\operatorname{Comm}_F(A)=A$ and $\operatorname{Comm}_F(B)=B$, and for every
$g\in F$,
$$g^{-1}Bg\cap A=\{e\}\quad\text{and}\quad g^{-1}Ag\cap B=\{e\}.$$

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC; the free group $F$ on the basis $\{a,b\}$; its subgroups
$A=\langle a\rangle$ and $B=\langle b\rangle$, with the discrete topology.

[F1] A free group on a set has the universal property that each map from its
basis to a group extends uniquely to a homomorphism
([[def-free-group]]).

[F2] A free product has the universal property for homomorphisms from each
factor into a common group ([[def-free-product-of-a-family-of-groups]]).

[F3] Every element of a free product has a unique reduced syllable expression;
the identity has the empty word and no nonempty reduced word is the identity
([[thm-normal-form-for-free-products]]).

[F4] For an open subgroup $H$ of a topological group $G$,
$\operatorname{Comm}_G(H)$ consists of those $g$ for which
$H\cap g^{-1}Hg$ has finite index in both $H$ and $g^{-1}Hg$
([[def-commensurator-unitary-character-and-monomial-induced-representation]]).

[F5] A free product of infinite cyclic groups is a free group on one generator
from each factor ([[cor-free-product-of-infinite-cyclic-groups-is-free]]).

[F6] In a free group with a free basis, the word length is the length of the
reduced word ([[cor-word-length-in-a-free-group-with-respect-to-a-free-basis-is-reduced-word-length]]).

[F7] AC says every family of nonempty sets has a choice function
([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

[F8] Integer powers in a group satisfy $a^{m+n}=a^m a^n$ and
$a^{-m}=(a^m)^{-1}$ for integers $m,n$
([[def-group-power]], [[lem-group-power-laws]]).

[F9] The cyclic subgroup generated by $g$ is exactly
$\{g^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$, and every cyclic subgroup is abelian
([[lem-cyclic-subgroup-is-the-set-of-powers]]).

[F10] The discrete topology on a set consists of all its subsets, so every
subset is open ([[def-standard-topologies]]).

[F11] In the binary product topology, products of open sets are basic open
sets ([[def-product-topology]]).

[F12] A map $f:X\to Y$ is continuous at $x$ iff, for every open
$V\subseteq Y$ with $f(x)\in V$, some open $U\subseteq X$ contains $x$ and
satisfies $f[U]\subseteq V$ ([[def-continuous-map-top]]).

[F13] A topological group is a group whose multiplication and inversion are
continuous ([[def-topological-group]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 For nonzero $k\in\mathbb Z$, the reduced word for $a^k$ has length $|k|$, so [F6] gives $a^k\ne e$; the same holds for $b^k$. If $a^m=a^n$, then [F8] gives $a^{m-n}=e$, forcing $m=n$ by the preceding fact; likewise the powers of $b$ are distinct. By [F9], the power maps $\mathbb Z\to A$ and $\mathbb Z\to B$ are surjective, and they are injective by these distinctness arguments; [F8] makes them homomorphisms. Thus $A$ and $B$ are infinite cyclic. By [F5], their free product is free on the canonical copies of $a,b$. Let $\phi:A*B\to F$ be induced by the factor inclusions using [F2], and let $\psi:F\to A*B$ send the free basis $a,b$ to those copies using [F1]. The composite $\phi\psi$ fixes $a,b$, so it is $\mathrm{id}_F$ by [F1]; the composite $\psi\phi$ restricts to the identity on each factor, so it is $\mathrm{id}_{A*B}$ by [F2]. Hence $\phi$ is an isomorphism and we identify $F=A*B$. The factor maps are injective because each nonidentity factor element is a nonempty reduced word by [F3], which also gives the reduced syllable normal form. [F1, F2, F3, F5, F6, F8, F9]

2.1 Let $g\notin A$. Its reduced syllable form, after removing an initial and terminal $A$-syllable when present, is $g=a_0wa_1$ with $a_0,a_1\in A$ and a nonempty reduced word $w$ beginning and ending in nonidentity $B$-syllables. For $x\in A\setminus\{e\}$, cyclicity of $A$ gives $g^{-1}xg=a_1^{-1}w^{-1}a_0^{-1}xa_0wa_1=a_1^{-1}w^{-1}xwa_1$. The middle word $w^{-1}xw$ is reduced and contains $B$-syllables on both ends; multiplication by the outer $A$-elements cannot cancel those syllables. By [F3] this element is not in $A$. Therefore $g^{-1}Ag\cap A=\{e\}$ for every $g\notin A$. Interchanging $A$ and $B$ gives $g^{-1}Bg\cap B=\{e\}$ for every $g\notin B$. [F3, step 1.1]

2.2 In the identification of step 1.1, let $\rho_A:F=A*B\to A$ be the retraction which is the identity on $A$ and trivial on $B$, supplied by the universal property [F2]. If $g^{-1}b^kg=a^\ell$ lies in $g^{-1}Bg\cap A$, then applying $\rho_A$ gives $e=a^\ell$, so the intersection element is $e$. Thus $g^{-1}Bg\cap A=\{e\}$ for every $g$. The retraction $\rho_B:F\to B$ proves $g^{-1}Ag\cap B=\{e\}$ for every $g$. [F2, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F10], every singleton in $F$ is open; by [F11], each singleton rectangle in $F\times F$ is open, so the product topology on $F\times F$ is discrete. For either multiplication or inversion, every open set containing the image of a point has an open preimage containing that point, because the domain is discrete; [F12] therefore gives continuity. Thus [F13] makes $F$ a topological group. Every subgroup of $F$ is open by [F10], so the commensurator definition [F4] applies to both $A$ and $B$. If $g\in A$, then $g^{-1}Ag=A$, so both indices in [F4] are $1$. If $g\notin A$, step 2.1 gives $A\cap g^{-1}Ag=\{e\}$, whose index in the infinite cyclic group $A$ is infinite; hence $g\notin\operatorname{Comm}_F(A)$. Therefore $\operatorname{Comm}_F(A)=A$. The same argument with $B$ gives $\operatorname{Comm}_F(B)=B$, and step 2.2 gives the two cross-factor intersections in the statement. AC is the stated inherited assumption [F7]; the proof steps use no further choice. [F4, F7, F10, F11, F12, F13, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.2] ∎

## Remarks

- Bekka–de la Harpe, Example 1.F.14(1), states the self-commensurator conclusion
  but leaves its verification implicit. The normal-form argument above proves
  the required same-factor malnormality, while the two cross-factor claims use
  separate retractions.
- The general monomial representation criterion in Theorem 1.F.16 is context;
  it does not establish the free-group normal-form or cross-factor claims.
