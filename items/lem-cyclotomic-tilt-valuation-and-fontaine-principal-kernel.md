## Statement

Let $p$ be prime. Let $C$ be a characteristic-zero field with a real-valued nonarchimedean valuation $v$, normalized by $v(p)=1$, and write $A=\{a\in C:v(a)\ge0\}$. Assume that the canonical map $A\to\varprojlim_{m\ge1}A/p^mA$ is an isomorphism. Suppose a compatible sequence of primitive roots $\zeta_{p^m}\in C$ is given, with $\zeta_{p^{m+1}}^p=\zeta_{p^m}$ and $\zeta_{p^0}=1$. Put $B=A^\flat$, $W=W_p(B)$ and let $\theta:W\to A$ and $x^\sharp$ be the canonical Fontaine map and sharp map. Then $B$ is a perfect valuation domain, with valuation
$$v_B(x)=v(x^\sharp),\qquad v_B(0)=+\infty.$$

Define $\epsilon=(\zeta_{p^n}\bmod p)_{n\ge0}\in B$, $u=\varphi^{-1}\epsilon$, $b=u-1$, and
$$\tau=b^{p-1},\qquad \xi=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}[u]^j\in W.$$
Then $v_B(b)=1/(p-1)$, $v_B(\tau)=1$, $\xi\bmod p=\tau$, and
$$\ker\theta=\xi W.$$
The element $\xi$ is a nonzerodivisor. Moreover $W[1/p]$ is separated for powers of $\xi$, so its canonical map to $\varprojlim_n W[1/p]/\xi^nW[1/p]$ is injective. The subring of $W[1/p]$ generated by all $\alpha^m/m!$ with $\alpha\in\ker\theta$ and $m\ge0$ is exactly $W[\xi^m/m!:m\ge0]$. These assertions require no Choice axiom. They do not assert that $\theta$ is surjective.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The valued field, the displayed canonical completion isomorphism, and the specified compatible primitive roots. A compatible root sequence is input data, not selected from a family of nonempty root sets.

[F1] For a ring with this canonical completion isomorphism, $A^\flat$ is perfect, the canonical Fontaine map exists, and reduction identifies its elements multiplicatively with actual tuples $a_{n+1}^p=a_n$ in $A$. Its sharp map satisfies $x^\sharp\equiv\widetilde{x_n}^{p^n}\bmod p^{n+1}A$. [[lem-fontaine-map-from-finite-witt-ghost-congruences]]

[F2] For a perfect characteristic-$p$ ring, multiplication by $p$ in its Witt ring is injective, reduction modulo $p$ is the base ring, and the Witt ring is canonically $p$-adically separated and complete. [[lem-perfect-base-witt-frobenius-and-p-adic-completeness]]

[F3] Teichmüller representatives are multiplicative, and Witt vectors have unique convergent expansions $\sum_{i\ge0}p^i[c_i]$. [[lem-perfect-base-witt-teichmuller-expansions-and-units]]

[F4] Division by a monic polynomial is valid over a commutative ring; in particular a root permits division by its monic linear factor. [[thm-monic-polynomial-division]]

[F5] A subring of a field is a valuation ring when, for each nonzero field element, it or its inverse belongs to the subring. The adic completion map has kernel the intersection of all ideal powers. [[def-valuation-ring]] [[def-adic-completion-of-a-module]]

## Proof

1.1 By [F1], write $x\in B$ as its uniquely determined actual tuple $(a_n)$, so $a_n^{p^n}=x^\sharp$. Thus $x^\sharp=0$ implies every $a_n=0$ and $x=0$. Multiplicativity of sharp proves $v_B(xy)=v_B(x)+v_B(y)$ and shows that $B$ is a domain. To check addition, suppose $x,y$ are not both zero, with actual tuples $(a_n),(c_n)$, and set $h=\min(v_B(x),v_B(y))<\infty$. For any one integer $n$ with $n+1>h$, the element $a_n+c_n$ lifts the $n$th coordinate of $x+y$. Hence [F1] gives $(x+y)^\sharp\equiv(a_n+c_n)^{p^n}\bmod p^{n+1}A$. The right-hand power has valuation at least $h$, and the error has valuation at least $n+1$, proving $v_B(x+y)\ge h$. The zero case is immediate. For nonzero $x,y$ with $v_B(x)\ge v_B(y)$, every quotient $a_n/c_n$ belongs to $A$ and these quotients form an actual compatible tuple. By [F1] they define $z\in B$, and the monoid identification gives $x=yz$. It follows that $B$ is the nonnegative subring for the valuation extended to its fraction field; this is the valuation-ring property in [F5]. [F1, F5, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 The residue field of $A$ has characteristic $p$. Each $\zeta_{p^m}$ reduces to $1$, since $T^{p^m}-1=(T-1)^{p^m}$ in that residue field. For $1\le a\le p^m$ with $p\nmid a$, the quotient $(1-\zeta_{p^m}^a)/(1-\zeta_{p^m})$ is the finite geometric sum whose residue is $a\ne0$, and is consequently a unit of $A$. The monic polynomial $1+T^{p^{m-1}}+\cdots+T^{(p-1)p^{m-1}}$ has as roots exactly the $p^{m-1}(p-1)$ distinct primitive $p^m$th roots. Repeated linear division by [F4], with matching monic degrees, gives its factorization by these roots. Evaluation at $1$ gives $p=\prod_{p\nmid a}(1-\zeta_{p^m}^a)$, and therefore $v(1-\zeta_{p^m})=1/(p^{m-1}(p-1))$. The $n$th coordinate of $b$ is lifted by $\zeta_{p^{n+1}}-1$. Its $p^n$th power has valuation $1/(p-1)$. Taking one $n$ with $n+1>1/(p-1)$ in the sharp congruence of [F1] proves $v_B(b)=1/(p-1)$ and $v_B(\tau)=1$. This also covers $p=2$. [F1, F4, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 The actual tuple for $u$ is $(\zeta_{p^{n+1}})_{n\ge0}$, so [F1] gives $u^\sharp=\zeta_p$. Hence $\theta(\xi)=\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\zeta_p^j=0$. Reduction modulo $p$ and the characteristic-$p$ identity $\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}T^j=(T-1)^{p-1}$ give $\overline\xi=\tau\ne0$. The reduction of $\theta$ modulo $p$ sends $x\in B$ to $x^\sharp\bmod p$. Its kernel consists exactly of the $x$ with $v_B(x)\ge1$: in the valuation ring $A$, the ideal $pA$ consists of the elements of valuation at least $1$. By step 1.1 and $v_B(\tau)=1$, this kernel is $\tau B$. No surjectivity statement is used. [F1, F2, F3, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 Let $z\in\ker\theta$. By step 3.1 its residue has a unique quotient $q_0\in B$ by $\tau$, since $B$ is a domain. By [F2] there is a unique $z_1\in W$ with $pz_1=z-\xi[q_0]$. Applying $\theta$ shows $p\theta(z_1)=0$, so $\theta(z_1)=0$ because $A$ is a subring of a characteristic-zero field. Repeat this uniquely defined operation: $q_i$ is the unique quotient of $\overline{z_i}$ by $\tau$, and $pz_{i+1}=z_i-\xi[q_i]$. Recursion of a function with unique values gives all $z_i,q_i$ without a Choice axiom. It yields $z=\xi\sum_{i<N}p^i[q_i]+p^Nz_N$. The partial sums have a unique limit in $W$ by [F2]; their products with $\xi$ converge to $z$, because the displayed error is in $p^NW$. Thus $z\in\xi W$, and the reverse inclusion follows from step 3.1. If $\xi w=0$, reduction gives $\tau\bar w=0$, hence $w=pw_1$ uniquely. Cancel $p$ by [F2] and repeat. Then $w\in\bigcap_m p^mW=0$. This proves both the principal-kernel assertion and regularity. [F2, F3, step 3.1, algebra, construct]

5.1 A nonzero Witt vector has a least nonzero standard coordinate, equivalently a finite $p$-adic order. Because $\tau$ is regular, reduction modulo $p$ shows that multiplying a vector not divisible by $p$ by $\xi$ still gives a vector not divisible by $p$. Thus multiplication by $\xi$ preserves $p$-adic order in $W$, and in $D=W[1/p]$ after scaling by a power of $p$. Let $a\in\bigcap_n\xi^nD$. Scale once so that $a\in W$. If $a\ne0$ and $a=\xi^ny_n$, order preservation shows $y_n\in W$ for each $n$; for $a=0$ nothing is required. Reduction gives $\bar a\in\bigcap_n\tau^nB=0$, since an element of finite real valuation cannot have valuation at least every integer. Write $a=pa_1$. Each equation $a=\xi^ny_n$ now implies $\tau^n\bar y_n=0$, so $y_n=py'_n$; cancellation gives $a_1=\xi^ny'_n$. Consequently $a_1$ again belongs to every $\xi^nW$. Iterating the unique division by $p$ gives $a\in\bigcap_m p^mW=0$. Thus $\bigcap_n\xi^nD=0$, which proves the asserted injectivity by [F5]. [F2, F3, F5, step 1.1, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra]

6.1 Finally, step 4.1 writes each $\alpha\in\ker\theta$ as $\xi c$ with $c\in W$. In $D$ one has $\alpha^m/m!=c^m\xi^m/m!$; all factorials are invertible in $D$ because their factors other than $p$ are units of $W$, by [F3] and their nonzero residues in the characteristic-$p$ field inside $B$. Hence all the stated divided powers lie in $W[\xi^m/m!:m\ge0]$. The converse inclusion follows by taking $\alpha=\xi$. This proves the equality of subrings and completes the proof. [F3, step 4.1, algebra] ∎

## Source notes

The proof reconstructs the cyclotomic instance of the principal-kernel mechanism in BMS §3.1. It uses the already constructed Fontaine map, finite cyclotomic polynomial identities and unique successive divisions. It does not import BMS Lemma3.9's repeated selection of Frobenius preimages. Only the compatible primitive root system displayed in the hypotheses is used.

For period rings this supplies a principal-kernel and separatedness leaf once the specified valuation-ring completeness and cyclotomic data are established. It does not construct $C_p$, prove its original unqualified completeness obligations, prove surjectivity of $\theta$, identify the residue of the completed characteristic-zero period ring, or prove that the completed crystalline ring embeds in it. General perfectoid fields need not contain this cyclotomic system; their full BMS comparison is not narrowed to this lemma.
