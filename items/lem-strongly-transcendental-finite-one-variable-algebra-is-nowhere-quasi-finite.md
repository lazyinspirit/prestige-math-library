## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]). Let $R\subseteq S$ be an
inclusion of reduced commutative rings
([[def-nilradical-and-reduced-ring]]), let $x\in S$ be strongly transcendental
over $R$ ([[def-strongly-transcendental-element]]), and suppose that $S$ is
module-finite over the $R$-subalgebra $R[x]\subseteq S$ generated by $x$
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]). Then $R\to S$ is a ring map of
finite type that is quasi-finite
([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]) at no prime of $S$.

The hypotheses are exactly those of the one-variable case of Zariski's main
theorem with the roles of the variable and the finite extension separated: $x$
contributes a transcendental direction, and the finiteness of $S$ over $R[x]$
prevents a local fibre from being finite-dimensional over its base residue field. A local fibre can nevertheless have Krull dimension zero: for $R=k$, $S=k[x]$ and $\mathfrak q=(0)$ it is $k(x)$, which has infinite dimension over $k$. In the normal-domain part of the proof, finite residue degree forces a strict prime chain in the local fibre; normalization and minimal-prime descent then give the general result. The Axiom of Choice is used exactly through
going down, lying over, the minimal-prime descent of strong transcendence and
the existence of a minimal prime below a given prime.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The Axiom of Choice; an inclusion of reduced commutative rings $R\subseteq S$; an element $x\in S$ strongly transcendental over $R$; and the hypothesis that $S$ is module-finite over $R[x]$.

[L1] The map $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ when $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ for $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$, that is, finitely generated as a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-module, equivalently finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ ([[def-quasi-finite-at-a-prime-for-finite-type-algebras]]).

[L2] An $R$-algebra $A$ is of finite type over $R$ when $A=R[a_1,\ldots,a_n]$ for some $n\ge0$ and some $a_i\in A$, and $A$ is module-finite over $R$ when $A$ is finitely generated as an $R$-module ([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]]).

[L3] For a prime $\mathfrak p$ of a commutative ring $A$ there is a canonical field isomorphism $A_{\mathfrak p}/\mathfrak pA_{\mathfrak p}\cong\operatorname{Frac}(A/\mathfrak p)$; this quotient is the residue field of the local ring $A_{\mathfrak p}$ ([[cor-residue-field-of-a-localisation-at-a-prime]]).

[L4] For a commutative ring $A$ and an ideal $P\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}A$, the quotient $A/P$ is an integral domain if and only if $P$ is a prime ideal ([[thm-quotient-is-domain-iff-ideal-prime]]).

[L5] For $R\subseteq S$ and $x\in S$, strong transcendence of $x$ over $R$ is the annihilator-sensitive condition that $u(a_0+a_1x+\cdots+a_kx^k)=0$ with $u\in S$ and $a_i\in R$ implies $ua_i=0$ for all $i$; when $S$ is a domain this is the same as saying that $x$ is transcendental over the fraction field of $R$ ([[def-strongly-transcendental-element]]).

[L6] The evaluation homomorphism $\operatorname{ev}_{\varphi,s}:R[x]\to S$ extending a unital ring homomorphism $\varphi:R\to S$ and sending $x$ to $s$ is unique; in particular the evaluation of a polynomial of $R[x]$ at the indeterminate $x$ is that same polynomial ([[thm-universal-property-of-a-polynomial-ring]]).

[L7] If $R$ is an integral domain then $R[x]$ is an integral domain ([[cor-polynomial-ring-over-a-domain-is-a-domain]]).

[L8] For an integrally closed domain $R$, the polynomial ring $R[x]$ is integrally closed, with no Noetherian hypothesis ([[lem-zmt-polynomial-rings-over-normal-domains-are-normal]]).

[L9] For a domain $A$ and a homomorphism $A\to B$, the integral closure of $A$ in $B$ is the set of elements of $B$ integral over $A$; and $A$ is integrally closed when every element of $\operatorname{Frac}(A)$ integral over $A$ already lies in $A$. The integral closure of a domain in a field extension of its fraction field is itself an integrally closed domain ([[def-integral-closure-and-integrally-closed-domain]], [[thm-integral-closure-is-integrally-closed]]).

[L10] Let $A\subseteq B$ be commutative rings with $A\ne0$ and $b\in B$. Then $b$ is integral over $A$ if and only if $A[b]$ is finitely generated as an $A$-module; in particular a module-finite extension is integral ([[thm-integrality-and-finite-module-equivalences]]).

[L11] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $A\subseteq B$ be an integral extension of domains with $A$ integrally closed; if $\mathfrak p_0\subseteq\mathfrak p_1$ are primes of $A$ and $\mathfrak q_1$ is a prime of $B$ with $\mathfrak q_1\cap A=\mathfrak p_1$, then there is a prime $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q_1$ of $B$ with $\mathfrak q_0\cap A=\mathfrak p_0$ ([[thm-going-down-over-normal-domains]]).

[L12] If $f:A\to B$ is a unital homomorphism and $f(t)$ is a unit of $B$ for every $t\in T$, then there is a unique unital homomorphism $T^{-1}A\to B$ with $\widetilde f\circ\lambda_T=f$; in particular an injection of domains extends canonically to their fraction fields ([[thm-universal-property-of-localisation]]).

[L13] For a unital ring map $R\to S$, the set $\operatorname{Int}_R(S)$ of elements of $S$ integral over the map is a subring of $S$ containing the image of $R$, hence an $R$-subalgebra ([[def-integral-subalgebra-of-an-arbitrary-ring-map]]).

[L14] Let $A\subseteq B$ be commutative rings and let $b_1,\ldots,b_n\in B$ be integral over $A$; then the $A$-subalgebra $A[b_1,\ldots,b_n]$ is module-finite over $A$ ([[lem-algebra-generated-by-finitely-many-integral-elements-is-module-finite]]).


[L17] Assume the Axiom of Choice. Let $f:A\to B$ be an integral ring map and let $\mathfrak p\in\operatorname{Spec}(A)$ with $\ker f\subseteq\mathfrak p$. Then there is $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(B)$ with $f^{-1}(\mathfrak q)=\mathfrak p$ ([[thm-lying-over]]).

[L18] Contraction along $A\to A/I$ is an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(A/I)$ onto the primes of $A$ containing $I$, with inverse $\mathfrak p\mapsto\mathfrak p/I$ ([[thm-prime-spectrum-of-a-quotient-bijection]]).

[L19] For a unital ring $A$, a right $A$-module $M$ and a left $A$-module $N$, the tensor product $M\otimes_AN$ is the quotient of the free abelian group on $M\times N$ by the balanced relations, with elementary tensors $m\otimes n$ ([[def-tensor-product-of-modules-by-generators-and-relations]]).

[L20] Quasi-finiteness transfers: for a finite-type map $R\to S$ quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$ and any ring map $R\to R'$, the base-changed map $R'\to S\otimes_RR'$ is quasi-finite at every prime of $S\otimes_RR'$ lying over $\mathfrak q$; and for an ideal $J\subseteq\mathfrak q$ the quotient map $R\to S/J$ is quasi-finite at the image of $\mathfrak q$ ([[lem-zmt-quasi-finite-transfer-through-intermediate-rings]]).

[L21] Assume the Axiom of Choice. If $R\subseteq S$ are reduced rings, $x\in S$ is strongly transcendental over $R$, $\mathfrak q$ is a minimal prime of $S$ and $\mathfrak p=R\cap\mathfrak q$, then the image of $x$ in $S/\mathfrak q$ is strongly transcendental over $R/\mathfrak p$ ([[lem-strong-transcendence-descends-to-minimal-prime-quotients]]).

[L22] Contraction along $A\to A_{\mathfrak p}$ induces an inclusion-preserving bijection from $\operatorname{Spec}(A_{\mathfrak p})$ onto the set of primes $\mathfrak q\subseteq\mathfrak p$ of $A$ ([[cor-primes-of-a-prime-local-ring]]).

[L23] Assume the Axiom of Choice. For a commutative ring $A$ and a proper ideal $I\mathrel{\trianglelefteq}A$ there is a prime ideal of $A$ containing $I$ that is minimal among the primes containing $I$ ([[lem-minimal-prime-over-an-ideal-exists]]).

[L24] For a multiplicative subset $T$ of a commutative ring $A$ and $r\in A$ one has $r/s=0$ in $T^{-1}A$ if and only if $ur=0$ for some $u\in T$; moreover $T^{-1}A$ is the zero ring if and only if $0\in T$ ([[prop-localisation-zero-equality-and-kernel-criteria]]).

[L25] For a prime $\mathfrak p$ of $A$ the localisation is $A_{\mathfrak p}=(A\setminus\mathfrak p)^{-1}A$, with elements fractions $r/s$ for $s\notin\mathfrak p$ ([[def-localisation-at-a-prime-ideal]]).

[L26] The Axiom of Choice is the assertion that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function; it is assumed here and used exactly through [L11], [L17], [L21] and [L23] ([[def-axiom-of-choice]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By the hypothesis of module-finiteness there are $t_1,\ldots,t_m\in S$ with $S=R[x]t_1+\cdots+R[x]t_m$. Hence $S=R[x,t_1,\ldots,t_m]$ is generated as an $R$-algebra by the finitely many elements $x,t_1,\ldots,t_m$ and is of finite type over $R$ by [L2]; consequently the notion of quasi-finiteness of [L1] is defined for $R\to S$, and it remains to show that no prime of $S$ satisfies it. [given, L2]

1.2 We first treat the case in which $R$ and $S$ are domains; here, by [L5], the strong transcendence of $x$ over $R$ says exactly that $x$ is transcendental over $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Let $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$, put $\mathfrak p=\mathfrak q\cap R$ and $\mathfrak r=\mathfrak q\cap R[x]$, and suppose for contradiction that $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$. [given, L5]

1.3 Now let $R\subseteq S$ be reduced and let $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$. By [L25] the localisation $S_{\mathfrak q}=(S\setminus\mathfrak q)^{-1}S$ is nonzero, because $0\in\mathfrak q$ excludes $0\in S\setminus\mathfrak q$ and $S_{\mathfrak q}=0$ would force $0\in S\setminus\mathfrak q$ by [L24]; so the zero ideal is a proper ideal of $S_{\mathfrak q}$, and by [L23] there is a prime $P$ of $S_{\mathfrak q}$ minimal over $(0)$. By [L22] every prime of $S_{\mathfrak q}$ is the extension of a prime of $S$ contained in $\mathfrak q$, so $P=\mathfrak q_0S_{\mathfrak q}$ for the prime $\mathfrak q_0=P\cap S\subseteq\mathfrak q$, and the inclusion-preserving bijection shows that $\mathfrak q_0$ is a minimal prime of $S$. [given, L22, L23, L24, L25]

2.1 First consider the normal-domain case, so assume here that $R$ is a domain and integrally closed in $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$. Then $R[x]$ is an integral domain by [L7] and is integrally closed by [L8] and [L9]. Moreover $R[x]\subseteq S$ is an integral extension: every element of the module-finite $R[x]$-algebra $S$ is integral over $R[x]$ by [L10]. [given, step 1.1, L7, L8, L9, L10]

2.2 By [L1] the algebra $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, and by [L3] the residue field $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ is $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak qS_{\mathfrak q}$, which is a quotient of $S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ because $\mathfrak p\subseteq\mathfrak q$. Hence $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. [given, step 1.2, L1, L3]

2.3 For the general domain case drop the normality of $R$: let $\overline R$ be the integral closure of $R$ in $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ as in [L9], which is a subring of $K$ by [L13] and is integrally closed by [L9]; put $L=\operatorname{Frac}(S)$ and let $\overline S=\overline R[S]$ be the subring of $L$ generated by $\overline R$ and $S$, a domain because it is a subring of the field $L$. [given, step 1.2, L9, L13]

2.4 By [L21] the image $\bar x$ of $x$ in $S/\mathfrak q_0$ is strongly transcendental over $R/\mathfrak p_0$, where $\mathfrak p_0=\mathfrak q_0\cap R$. Both $R/\mathfrak p_0$ and $S/\mathfrak q_0$ are domains by [L4], so by [L5] the element $\bar x$ is transcendental over $\kappa(\mathfrak p_0)=\operatorname{Frac}(R/\mathfrak p_0)$. [given, step 1.3, L4, L5, L21]

2.5 The quotient $S/\mathfrak q_0$ is module-finite over $(R/\mathfrak p_0)[\bar x]$: it is the image of the module-finite $R[x]$-algebra $S$ under the quotient map, and the image of $R[x]$ is the subalgebra generated by $\bar x$ over $R/\mathfrak p_0$. [given, step 1.1, step 1.3]

3.1 The kernel of $R[x]\to S/\mathfrak q$ is exactly $\mathfrak r$, so $R[x]/\mathfrak r$ is a subring of the domain $S/\mathfrak q$ by [L4]; by [L3] the residue fields are $\kappa(\mathfrak r)=\operatorname{Frac}(R[x]/\mathfrak r)$ and $\kappa(\mathfrak q)=\operatorname{Frac}(S/\mathfrak q)$, and by [L12] the inclusion of domains extends to an injection $\kappa(\mathfrak r)\to\kappa(\mathfrak q)$ of fields carrying the image of $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ onto a subfield contained in $\kappa(\mathfrak r)$. Since a $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linearly independent subset of $\kappa(\mathfrak r)$ is also $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linearly independent in $\kappa(\mathfrak q)$, step 2.2 shows that $\kappa(\mathfrak r)$ is finite over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$. [given, step 1.2, step 2.2, L3, L4, L12]

3.2 Since $R\subseteq\overline R\subseteq K$ and $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$, their fraction fields satisfy $K\subseteq\operatorname{Frac}(\overline R)\subseteq K$, hence $\operatorname{Frac}(\overline R)=K$. The element $x$ is transcendental over $K$ by [L5]: if a nonzero polynomial over $K$ vanished at $x$, clearing its finitely many denominators would give a nonzero $h\in R[X]$ with $h(x)=0$, and strong transcendence with multiplier $1$ would force every coefficient of $h$ to be zero, a contradiction. Hence $x$ is transcendental over $\operatorname{Frac}(\overline R)$ as well. [given, step 2.3, L5, algebra]

3.3 The algebra $\overline S$ is integral over $S$: by [L13] the set $\operatorname{Int}_S(\overline S)$ is a subring of $\overline S$ containing $S$, and it contains every element of $\overline R$, which is integral over $R$ by [L9] and hence over $S$ by the same monic equation; being a subring containing $S$ and $\overline R$, it contains the subring they generate, namely $\overline S$, so $\overline S=\operatorname{Int}_S(\overline S)$ is integral over $S$. [given, step 2.3, L9, L13, algebra]

3.4 The algebra $\overline S$ is module-finite over $\overline R[x]$: from $S=R[x]t_1+\cdots+R[x]t_m$ of step 1.1 we get $\overline S=\overline R[x][t_1,\ldots,t_m]$, and each $t_j$ is integral over $R[x]$ by module-finiteness and [L10], hence over $\overline R[x]\supseteq R[x]$ by the same monic equation, so [L14] makes $\overline S$ module-finite over $\overline R[x]$. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 2.3, L14, algebra]

4.1 Consequently $\mathfrak r\ne\mathfrak pR[x]$: if equality held, then $R[x]/\mathfrak r=(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ and $\kappa(\mathfrak r)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)(\bar x)$ where $\bar x$ is the class of $x$. Then $\bar x$ is transcendental over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$: if $0\ne h\in\kappa(\mathfrak p)[X]$ satisfied $h(\bar x)=0$, then writing $h=d^{-1}g$ with $0\ne d\in R/\mathfrak p$ and $g\in(R/\mathfrak p)[X]$ would give $g(\bar x)=0$, that is $g=0$ because evaluation at the indeterminate is the identity by [L6], contradicting $g\ne0$ in the domain $(R/\mathfrak p)[x]$ by [L7]. But then the powers $1,\bar x,\ldots,\bar x^{d}$ for $d=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak p)}\kappa(\mathfrak r)<\infty$ would be $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linearly dependent, yielding a nonzero polynomial relation over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ of degree $\le d$ satisfied by $\bar x$, a contradiction. [given, step 3.1, L6, L7]

5.1 Since $\mathfrak pR[x]\subsetneq\mathfrak r$ and $\mathfrak q$ lies over $\mathfrak r$ along the integral extension $R[x]\subseteq S$ of step 2.1, going down [L11] provides a prime $\mathfrak q'\subseteq\mathfrak q$ of $S$ with $\mathfrak q'\cap R[x]=\mathfrak pR[x]$; then $\mathfrak q'\ne\mathfrak q$, and $\mathfrak q'\cap R=(\mathfrak q'\cap R[x])\cap R=\mathfrak pR[x]\cap R=\mathfrak p$. [given, step 2.1, step 4.1, L11]

6.1 The prime ideals $\mathfrak q'S_{\mathfrak q}\subseteq\mathfrak qS_{\mathfrak q}$ of $S_{\mathfrak q}$ are distinct and both contain $\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ by step 5.1, so $A:=S_{\mathfrak q}/\mathfrak pS_{\mathfrak q}$ carries a strict chain of primes; $A$ is finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ by [L1]. But every prime $P$ of a finite-dimensional algebra $A$ over a field $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$ is maximal: $A/P$ is a domain by [L4], finite-dimensional over $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so for $0\ne u\in A/P$ the $\kappa(\mathfrak p)$-linear map $a\mapsto ua$ is injective by [L4] and hence surjective by finite dimension, which makes $u$ a unit and $A/P$ a field. This contradiction shows that, when $R,S$ are domains and $R$ is integrally closed, $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at no prime of $S$, and then also when $R,S$ are any domains, as the next steps show. [given, step 5.1, L1, L4]

7.1 Applying the result of step 6.1 to the domains $\overline R\subseteq\overline S$, where $\overline R$ is integrally closed by step 2.3 and $x$ is transcendental over $\operatorname{Frac}(\overline R)$ by step 3.2 while $\overline S$ is module-finite over $\overline R[x]$ by step 3.4, we obtain: $\overline R\to\overline S$ is quasi-finite at no prime of $\overline S$. [step 6.1, step 2.3, step 3.2, step 3.4]

8.1 Now suppose that $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at a prime $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$. By [L17] applied to the integral extension $S\subseteq\overline S$ of step 3.3 there is $\overline{\mathfrak q}\in\operatorname{Spec}(\overline S)$ with $\overline{\mathfrak q}\cap S=\mathfrak q$. The $R$-algebra homomorphism $S\otimes_R\overline R\to\overline S$ of [L19] sending $s\otimes r$ to $sr$ is surjective because its image is a subring of $\overline S$ containing the images of $S$ and of $\overline R$; with $J$ its kernel we have $\overline S\cong(S\otimes_R\overline R)/J$, and by [L18] the prime $\overline{\mathfrak q}$ corresponds to a prime $\widetilde{\mathfrak q}$ of $S\otimes_R\overline R$ mapping to $\mathfrak q$ in $S$. By [L20] the base change $\overline R\to S\otimes_R\overline R$ is quasi-finite at $\widetilde{\mathfrak q}$, and by [L20] again, applied to the quotient $\overline S$, the map $\overline R\to\overline S$ is quasi-finite at $\overline{\mathfrak q}$, contradicting step 7.1. Hence in the domain case $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at no prime of $S$. [given, step 3.3, step 7.1, L17, L18, L19, L20]

9.1 By step 8.1 applied to the domains $R/\mathfrak p_0\subseteq S/\mathfrak q_0$ with the element $\bar x$ of step 2.4, the map $R/\mathfrak p_0\to S/\mathfrak q_0$ is quasi-finite at no prime of $S/\mathfrak q_0$; in particular it is not quasi-finite at the prime $\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q_0$ of $S/\mathfrak q_0$, which exists by [L18] because $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q$ and $\mathfrak q_0$ is prime. [given, step 8.1, step 2.4, step 2.5, L18]

10.1 If $R\to S$ were quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$, then by [L20] applied to the ideal $\mathfrak q_0\subseteq\mathfrak q$ the quotient map $R\to S/\mathfrak q_0$ would be quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q/\mathfrak q_0$, contradicting step 9.1: the actions factor through $R/\mathfrak p_0$, and $\kappa(\mathfrak p/\mathfrak p_0)=\kappa(\mathfrak p)$, so these two base-ring descriptions give the same local fibre. Hence $R\to S$ is not quasi-finite at $\mathfrak q$; since $\mathfrak q\in\operatorname{Spec}(S)$ was arbitrary, $R\to S$ is quasi-finite at no prime of $S$. [given, step 9.1, L20]

11.1 Steps 1.1 and 10.1 prove the Statement: $R\to S$ is of finite type whenever $S$ is module-finite over $R[x]$, and it is quasi-finite at no prime of $S$ for reduced $R\subseteq S$ and strongly transcendental $x$. The Axiom of Choice was assumed in the Statement and used exactly through [L11] in step 5.1, [L17] in step 8.1, [L21] in step 2.4 and [L23] in step 1.3; all other steps are choice-free. ∎ [step 1.1, step 10.1, L11, L17, L21, L23, L26]
