## Definition

Let $F$ be a number field. Its adele ring is the restricted product $\mathbb A_F=\prod'_v F_v$ of its completions, restricted with respect to the ring of integers $\mathcal O_v$ at each finite place $v$. For $n\ge1$, write $G_n=\mathrm{GL}_n$ and let $Z_n$ be its scalar-matrix center. A central character is a continuous character $\omega:Z_n(F)\backslash Z_n(\mathbb A_F)\to\mathbb C^\times$.

An automorphic form with central character $\omega$ is a function $\phi:G_n(F)\backslash G_n(\mathbb A_F)\to\mathbb C$ that is smooth in the archimedean variables, locally constant in the finite variables, satisfies $\phi(zg)=\omega(z)\phi(g)$, and has moderate growth: for some constants $C,N\ge0$, $|\phi(g)|\le C H(g)^N$, where

$$H(g)=\prod_v\max\left(1,\max_{i,j}|(g_v)_{ij}|_v,\max_{i,j}|(g_v^{-1})_{ij}|_v\right)$$

uses normalized local absolute values. This product has only finitely many non-unit factors at finite places. An adelic automorphic representation of $G_n$ is an irreducible subquotient of the right-regular representation on this space of automorphic forms for some $\omega$; irreducible means that there is no proper nonzero invariant subspace, a subquotient is a quotient of an invariant subspace, and the right action is $R(h)\phi(g)=\phi(gh)$. We write it as a restricted tensor product $\pi=\bigotimes'_v\pi_v$ of local representations $\pi_v$ of $G_n(F_v)$, using a $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal O_v)$-fixed vector at all but finitely many finite places. For smooth realizations, the archimedean local spaces carry their Casselman–Wallach Fréchet topologies and are combined by the completed projective tensor product; the finite-place restricted tensor product is algebraic, with its smooth inductive-limit topology. The global smooth tensor realization carries the resulting inductive-limit topology. Finite sums of pure tensors are dense; partial insertion of a fixed tensor into all other slots is continuous. Here the projective tensor topology is generated by the seminorms $u\mapsto\inf_{u=\sum_i x_i\otimes y_i}\sum_i p(x_i)q(y_i)$ for continuous seminorms $p,q$, followed by completion, and iterated over the finitely many archimedean places. A finite-place component is unramified when it has such a nonzero fixed vector.
