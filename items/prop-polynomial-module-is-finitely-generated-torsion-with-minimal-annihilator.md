## Statement

Let $T$ be an endomorphism of a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space $V$. The $F[x]$-module $V_T$ is finitely generated and torsion. The annihilator of $V_T$ is generated by the minimal polynomial $\mu_T$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The polynomial action of [[def-polynomial-module-of-an-endomorphism]], the module finite-generation convention of [[def-generated-cyclic-finitely-generated-and-free-modules]], and the operator-annihilator definition of [[def-annihilator-ideal-and-minimal-polynomial-of-an-endomorphism]].

[L1] For every endomorphism $T$ of a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space, $\operatorname{Ann}(T)$ is a nonzero ideal of $F[x]$ and has a unique monic generator $\mu_T$; moreover $p(T)=0$ exactly when $\mu_T\mid p$ ([[thm-minimal-polynomial-is-well-defined-and-controls-annihilators]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Any finite $F$-basis of $V$ generates $V_T$ over $F[x]$, because constant polynomials already give every $F$-linear combination. On the zero space the empty basis generates. [given]

1.2 By [L1], $\mu_T(T)=0$, so $\mu_T$ kills every vector. Thus every vector is torsion and $V_T$ is a torsion $F[x]$-module. [L1]

2.1 A polynomial $p$ annihilates the whole module exactly when $p(T)v=0$ for every $v$, exactly when $p(T)=0$. By [L1], this is equivalent to $p\in(\mu_T)$, so $\operatorname{Ann}_{F[x]}(V_T)=(\mu_T)$. For $V=0$, both ideals are $(1)$. [L1, given] ∎
