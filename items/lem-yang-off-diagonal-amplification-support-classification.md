## Statement

Let $n\geq2$, let $p$ be a prime, put $G'=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Q_p)$, $K'_p=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb Z_p)$, let $B'$ be the upper-triangular subgroup, let $N'$ be the upper-unitriangular subgroup, and let $W'$ be the permutation-matrix Weyl group. For $t=\operatorname{diag}(t_1,\ldots,t_n)$ write $\delta_{B'}(t)=\prod_{i<j}|t_i/t_j|_p$. Let $\ell=\ell_p\geq1$, and let $f_p$ be the $p$-factor of the off-diagonal test function $f^{\mathrm{off}}_{p_1,p_2}$ with $p=p_2$. Given $y\in G'$ and $x\in\mathbb Q_p^n$ as a column vector, suppose the minimum valuations of the entries of $y$ and $x$ are $-e$ and $-e'$, respectively, for integers $e,e'\geq0$. Choose $w\in W'$, $u\in N'(\mathbb Q_p)$, $k\in K'_p$, and integers $r_1\geq\cdots\geq r_{n-1}\geq0$ in a decomposition

$$wy=tuk,\qquad t=\operatorname{diag}(p^{r_1-e},\ldots,p^{r_{n-1}-e},p^{-e}),$$

with the usual unipotent factor included as $wy=tuk$; write $wx=\binom{x'}{\alpha}$ with $x'\in\mathbb Q_p^{n-1}$ and $\alpha\in\mathbb Q_p$. If

$$f_p\!\left(\begin{pmatrix}y&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)\ne0,$$

then $\delta_{B'}(t)^{-1}\leq p^{(n-1)\ell}$ and exactly one of the following alternatives applies.

**(A)** If $e\geq e'$, then $e=\ell$ and there are $u''\in p^{-\ell}\mathbb Z_p^{n-1}$ and $k\in K'_p$ such that

$$wy=\operatorname{diag}(I_{n-1},p^{-\ell})\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&u''\\0&1\end{pmatrix}k,$$

with $x'-\alpha p^\ell u''\in\mathbb Z_p^{n-1}$ and $\alpha\in p^{-\ell}\mathbb Z_p$.

**(B)** If $e<e'$, then $2e'-e=\ell$, $x'\in p^{-e'}\mathbb Z_p^{n-1}$, and $\alpha\in p^{-e'}\mathbb Z_p^\times$. There are $u''\in\mathbb Q_p^{n-1}$ and $k\in K'_p$ such that

$$wy=\operatorname{diag}(p^{(\ell-e)/2}I_{n-1},p^{-e})\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&u''\\0&1\end{pmatrix}k,$$

with $u''-p^{-e'}\alpha^{-1}x'\in\mathbb Z_p^{n-1}$. In this case $\ell>e'>e\geq0$.

For the displayed decompositions, $x'$ denotes the first $n-1$ coordinates in the chosen expression $wx=\binom{x'}{\alpha}$; all valuations are normalized by $v_p(p)=1$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Exactly the original prime, rank, off-diagonal local test factor, valuation minima, and chosen decomposition in the Statement, with the corrected column-vector placement. No additional choice, regularity or Cartan-refinement hypothesis is assumed. Put $O=\mathbb Z_p$ and $K=\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(O)$.

[F1] At the second amplification prime the local factor is the adjoint of the central projection of $p^{-n\ell/2}\mathbf1_{K\operatorname{diag}(p^\ell,I_n)K}$. Thus nonzero value implies membership in the central multiple of the inverse double coset. This follows directly from the displayed local factor and involution formulas in [[def-yang-2026-amplified-test-function-data]].

[F2] A matrix over a PID has a Smith diagonal form under invertible row/column operations. [[thm-smith-normal-form-existence-over-a-pid]]

[F3] Unique $p$-adic digits imply that each nonzero element of $O$ is a unit times a nonnegative power of $p$; in particular $O$ is a DVR and a PID. [[thm-p-adic-digit-expansion]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** isolate the unique negative elementary divisor.

1.1 By [F3], a nonzero ideal of $O$ is generated by an element of its least valuation, so [F2] applies. Smith form, after clearing a common denominator, shows that $A\in K\operatorname{diag}(I_n,p^{-\ell})K$ has determinant valuation $-\ell$, minimum entry valuation $-\ell$, all its $2\times2$ minors of valuation at least $-\ell$, and integral inverse. Conversely, isolating any entry of valuation $-\ell$ by integral elementary operations leaves a complement in $\mathrm{GL}_n(O)$ for exactly this Smith type. By [F1], a nonzero value supplies a central scalar. Its unit part is absorbed in $K$, leaving some $j\in\mathbb Z$ for which $p^j\begin{pmatrix}y&x\\0&1\end{pmatrix}\in K\operatorname{diag}(I_n,p^{-\ell})K$. Multiply by the integral permutation $\operatorname{diag}(w,1)$ on the left and by $\operatorname{diag}(k^{-1},1)$ on the right. Set $D=\operatorname{diag}(p^{r_1-e},\ldots,p^{r_{n-1}-e})$, $c=p^{-e}$, and write the chosen unipotent as $u=\begin{pmatrix}u_0&b\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$. With $v=x'$ the matrix to study is $p^jH$, where $H=\begin{pmatrix}Du_0&Db&v\\0&c&\alpha\\0&0&1\end{pmatrix}$. Since $w$ only permutes coordinates, every entry of $Db$ has valuation at least $-e$, and every coordinate of $(v,\alpha)$ has valuation at least $-e'$, with at least one attaining that minimum. [given, F1, F2, F3, construct]

2.1 The minimum entry of the original block matrix has valuation $-\max(e,e')$, so $j=\max(e,e')-\ell$. Its bottom-right entry and the bottom-right entry of its inverse give $-\ell\le j\le0$. Its determinant gives $\sum_{a=1}^{n-1}r_a-ne+(n+1)j=-\ell$. Further $p^{-j}y^{-1}$ is integral as a submatrix of the inverse of the scaled original block matrix. Since $y^{-1}=k^{-1}u^{-1}t^{-1}w$ and multiplication by $K'_p$ preserves entrywise integrality, $p^{-j}u^{-1}t^{-1}$ is integral. Its diagonal entries have valuations $e-r_a-j$, hence $r_1\le e-j$. These assertions use the selected triangular decomposition only, and do not assume that $tut^{-1}$ is integral. [given, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 Suppose $e\ge e'$. Then $j-e=-\ell$. Both $Db/c$ and $\alpha/c$ are integral. Subtract $Db/c$ times row $n$ from the top rows and $\alpha/c$ times column $n$ from the last column. This isolates the entry $p^jc$ of valuation $-\ell$ and leaves the complement $p^j\begin{pmatrix}Du_0&v-(Db/c)\alpha\\0&1\end{pmatrix}$ in $\mathrm{GL}_n(O)$ by step 1.1. Its bottom-right entry forces $j\ge0$; step 2.1 forces $j=0$, hence $e=\ell$. Its upper-left triangular block $Du_0$ is integral with unit determinant, so each diagonal entry is a unit, giving $r_a=e$ for every $a$ and $D=I_{n-1}$. Hence $u_0\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(O)$. Absorb $\operatorname{diag}(u_0,1)$ into the right factor $k$, put $u''=b$, and obtain $wy=\operatorname{diag}(I_{n-1},p^{-\ell})\begin{pmatrix}I_{n-1}&u''\\0&1\end{pmatrix}k$. The bounds $Db/c\in O^{n-1}$, $\alpha\in p^{-e}O$, and integrality of the complement's last column give respectively $u''\in p^{-\ell}O^{n-1}$, $\alpha\in p^{-\ell}O$, and $x'-\alpha p^\ell u''\in O^{n-1}$. This proves (A). [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, construct, algebra]

3.2 Suppose $e<e'$. Then $j=e'-\ell$. We first prove $v_p(\alpha)=-e'$. If not, some coordinate $v_a$ has valuation $-e'$ and $v_p(\alpha)>-e'$. In $H$, the minor on rows $a,n$ and columns $n,n+1$ is $(Db)_a\alpha-cv_a$. The first term has valuation strictly greater than $-e-e'$ and the second has valuation exactly $-e-e'$, so their difference has that valuation. The scaled minor bound of step 1.1 gives $2j-e-e'\ge-\ell$, equivalently $\ell\le e'-e$. Together with $j\le0$, hence $\ell\ge e'$, this forces $e=0$, $\ell=e'$ and $j=0$. The inverse diagonal bound $r_1\le e-j$ from step 2.1 then makes every $r_a=0$. Its determinant equation becomes $0=-\ell$, impossible because $\ell\ge1$. Thus $\alpha\in p^{-e'}O^\times$, and $x'=v\in p^{-e'}O^{n-1}$. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 The entry $p^j\alpha$ has valuation $-\ell$. The ratios $v/\alpha$, $1/\alpha$, and $c/\alpha$ are integral. Subtract $v/\alpha$ times row $n$ from the top rows and $1/\alpha$ times row $n$ from the last row, then subtract $c/\alpha$ times the last column from column $n$. After permuting the pivot into its own block, these integral operations leave the complement $p^j\begin{pmatrix}Du_0&Db-(c/\alpha)v\\0&-c/\alpha\end{pmatrix}\in\mathrm{GL}_n(O)$. Every diagonal entry is integral and their product is a unit; hence all are units. The last has valuation $j-e+e'=2e'-e-\ell$, giving $\ell=2e'-e$ and $j=e-e'$. The other diagonal entries give $r_a=e'$ and $D=p^{e'-e}I_{n-1}$, while the upper-left block gives $u_0\in\mathrm{GL}_{n-1}(O)$. Absorbing it into $k$ again, put $u''=b$. The last column of the complement gives $u''-p^{-e'}\alpha^{-1}x'\in O^{n-1}$. The top exponent is $e'-e=(\ell-e)/2$, proving the displayed factorization (B). Finally $\ell-e'=e'-e>0$, so $\ell>e'>e\ge0$. [given, step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.2, construct, algebra]

5.1 In (A), the originally chosen $t$ has top exponents zero and last exponent $-\ell$, so $\delta_{B'}(t)^{-1}=p^{(n-1)\ell}$. In (B), its top exponents are $e'-e$ and last exponent $-e$, so $\delta_{B'}(t)^{-1}=p^{(n-1)e'}\le p^{(n-1)\ell}$. The two cases are disjoint by $e\ge e'$ versus $e<e'$, and exhaust the hypotheses. Every operation is finite over the DVR; the same block argument applies when $n=2$ and there are no size-$n-2$ blocks. All primes and every allowed $\ell\ge1$ are included without an added refined-Cartan premise. [given, step 3.1, step 4.1, algebra] ∎
