## Statement

Assume AC and DC. Let $k$ be algebraically closed and $X$ an integral smooth projective finite-type $k$-scheme. Define $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ as the subgroup generated by differences of fibres in connected finite-type families of line bundles, and put $N=\operatorname{Pic}(X)/\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$. Let $\ell\ne\operatorname{char}k$ be prime. Then for every $r\geq1$ the Kummer boundary induces an injection
$$N/\ell^rN\hookrightarrow H^2_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\mu_{\ell^r}),$$
and $N[\ell^r]$ is a quotient of $H^1_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\mu_{\ell^r})$. Both groups are finite. The inverse limit $\widehat N_\ell=\varprojlim_r N/\ell^rN$ is a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module and injects into $\varprojlim_rH^2_{\mathrm{et}}(X,\mu_{\ell^r})$. The kernel of $N\to\widehat N_\ell$ is $\bigcap_r\ell^rN$. No finite-generation claim for $N$ itself is made.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The algebraically closed field, smooth projective integral scheme, family-equivalence subgroup and prime in the Statement.

[A1] AC and DC are assumed. [[def-axiom-of-choice]] [[def-dependent-choice]]

[F1] The reduced identity component of the Picard scheme is an abelian variety whose $k$-points are precisely $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$. [[thm-picard-variety-representability-for-smooth-projective-varieties]]

[F2] Multiplication by every positive integer on an abelian variety is finite flat and surjective. [[lem-multiplication-by-n-on-abelian-scheme]]

[F3] Higher direct images of finite constructible torsion sheaves under proper maps of Noetherian schemes are finite constructible. [[lem-proper-torsion-constructibility-under-choice]]

## Proof

**Proof technique:** use divisibility of the Picard identity component and the étale Kummer sequence.

1.1 Choose $e\in X(k)$; the nonempty finite-type scheme has a closed rational point. By [F1], $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ is the group of $k$-points of an abelian variety. By [F2], multiplication by any positive integer is surjective on that variety; every nonempty finite fibre has a $k$-point because $k$ is algebraically closed. Hence $\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ is divisible. For $n=\ell^r$, the quotient map induces an isomorphism $\operatorname{Pic}(X)/n\operatorname{Pic}(X)\cong N/nN$. Also, if $x\in N[n]$ is represented by $L\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)$, divisibility gives $P\in\operatorname{Pic}^0_{\mathrm{alg}}(X)$ with $nP=nL$, so $L-P\in\operatorname{Pic}(X)[n]$ maps to $x$. [A1, F1, F2, given, choose, algebra]

2.1 The étale Kummer sequence $0\to\mu_n\to\mathbb G_m\xrightarrow{n}\mathbb G_m\to0$ is exact because $n$ is invertible. Its long exact sequence injects $\operatorname{Pic}(X)/n$ into $H^2(X,\mu_n)$ and maps $H^1(X,\mu_n)$ onto $\operatorname{Pic}(X)[n]$. Step 1.1 gives the asserted injection and torsion quotient. By [F3] applied to the proper map $X\to\operatorname{Spec}k$, the two cohomology groups are finite: $\mu_n$ is finite locally constant, and constructible sheaves on an algebraically closed point have finite stalks. [A1, F3, step 1.1, algebra]

3.1 The Kummer injections in step 2.1 commute with the transition maps: on roots of unity the map is raising to $\ell$, and on Picard quotients it is reduction. Passing to inverse limits gives an injection of $\widehat N_\ell$ into the inverse limit of the cohomology groups. The kernel of $N\to\widehat N_\ell$ is exactly the intersection of the subgroups $\ell^rN$ by definition. Choose finitely many elements of $N$ mapping onto the finite group $N/\ell N$. Repeated reduction shows that their images generate every $N/\ell^rN$ as a $\mathbb Z/\ell^r$-module. The resulting compatible surjections from $(\mathbb Z/\ell^r)^d$ pass to a surjection $\mathbb Z_\ell^d\to\widehat N_\ell$: compatible lifts exist by compactness of the inverse system of nonempty finite lift sets. Thus the completion is finitely generated over $\mathbb Z_\ell$. [A1, step 2.1, choose, construct, algebra] ∎

## Scope

This controls prime-to-characteristic torsion and the completed group, not the discrete group itself. In particular, a subgroup of a finitely generated $\mathbb Z_\ell$-module need not be finitely generated over $\mathbb Z$: for a prime $q\ne\ell$, the countable subgroup $\mathbb Z[1/q]\subset\mathbb Z_\ell$ is not finitely generated. The remaining proof must control both $\bigcap_r\ell^rN$ and the integral lattice nature of the image.
