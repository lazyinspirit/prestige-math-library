## Statement

Let $R$ be a commutative ring with identity. Write $\operatorname{Fitt}_R$ for the zeroth Fitting ideal. A quadratic presentation means an exact sequence $R^n\xrightarrow{D}R^n\to Q\to0$ with $n$ finite; injectivity of $D$ is not required.

If $0\to U\to V\to Q\to0$ is exact, $U$ is finitely presented, and $Q$ has a quadratic presentation, then $V$ is finitely presented and
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V)=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U)\operatorname{Fitt}_R(Q).$$
If $U$ also has a quadratic presentation, so does $V$.

Consequently, if $U,U'$ are finitely presented and there are exact sequences $0\to U\to V\to W\to0$ and $0\to U'\to V'\to W\to0$ with $V,V'$ quadratically presented, then
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U)\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V')=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U')\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V).$$

In particular, let $t\geq0$ be finite, let $a_i,b_i,c_i\in R$ for $1\leq i\leq t$, and suppose the following are exact, with first local arrow multiplication by $c_i$ and second local arrow projection:
$$0\to M\to E\to\bigoplus_{i=1}^t R/(c_i,b_i)\to0,\qquad 0\to R/(a_i,b_i)\xrightarrow{c_i}R/(b_i)\to R/(c_i,b_i)\to0.$$
If $M$ is finitely presented and $E$ is quadratically presented, then
$$\operatorname{Fitt}_R(M)\prod_{i=1}^t(b_i)=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(E)\prod_{i=1}^t(a_i,b_i).$$
All products here are products of ideals of $R$; the empty product is $R$. No non-zero-divisor or Noetherian hypothesis, and no choice axiom, is required for these conclusions.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The commutative ring, finite presentations, and exact sequences specified in the Statement. In an exact sequence we identify the left module with its image.

[F1] The zeroth Fitting ideal is generated by maximal minors of a finite presentation matrix, with determinant of a $0\times0$ matrix equal to $1$. It is independent of the presentation. [[def-fitting-ideal-of-a-finitely-presented-module]] [[lem-fitting-ideals-presentation-independent]]

## Proof

1.1 Fix a presentation $R^k\xrightarrow{P}R^m\xrightarrow{\alpha}U\to0$ and a quadratic presentation $R^n\xrightarrow{D}R^n\xrightarrow{\beta}Q\to0$. Lift the images under $\beta$ of the $n$ standard basis elements to $V$ and extend linearly to $s:R^n\to V$. For each standard basis vector $e_j$, the element $s(De_j)$ maps to zero in $Q$, hence is the image of a unique element of $U$. Choose a lift of that element under $\alpha$; these $n$ lifts define a matrix $L:R^n\to R^m$ with $sD=\iota\alpha L$, where $\iota:U\to V$ is the injection. These are only finitely many choices. [given, choose, construct]

1.2 Take the finite direct sum of the given local sequences. Its middle module $V'=\bigoplus_i R/(b_i)$ has the diagonal quadratic presentation with entries $b_i$, so its Fitting ideal is $\prod_i(b_i)$. Its kernel $U'=\bigoplus_i R/(a_i,b_i)$ has a presentation with $t$ rows and $2t$ columns, with the two entries $a_i,b_i$ supported on row $i$. A maximal minor vanishes unless it selects one column supported on each row; the remaining minors, up to sign, are precisely the products choosing one of $a_i,b_i$ for each $i$. Thus its Fitting ideal is $\prod_i(a_i,b_i)$. Both modules are finitely presented, including when $t=0$. [F1, given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Define $h:R^m\oplus R^n\to V$ by $h(x,y)=\iota\alpha(x)+s(y)$. It is surjective: first lift the image in $Q$ using $\beta$, and then lift the difference using $\alpha$. The columns of the block matrix $T=\begin{pmatrix}P&-L\\0&D\end{pmatrix}$ map to zero under $h$. Conversely, if $h(x,y)=0$, then $\beta(y)=0$, so $y=Dz$ for some $z\in R^n$. Subtracting the column combination $(-Lz,Dz)$ leaves $(x+Lz,0)$, and injectivity of $\iota$ gives $\alpha(x+Lz)=0$. This vector is therefore a column combination of $(P,0)$. Thus $T$ is a finite presentation matrix for $V$. This reasoning did not require $D$ to be injective. [step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Every maximal minor of $T$ uses $m+n$ columns. Unless all $n$ columns of its second block are selected, its bottom $n$ rows have fewer than $n$ potentially nonzero columns, so its determinant is zero. Selecting all of them leaves exactly $m$ columns from $P$, and the block triangular determinant is the corresponding $m\times m$ minor of $P$ multiplied by $\det D$. Conversely each such minor of $P$ occurs in this way. Hence [F1] gives $\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V)=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U)(\det D)=\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U)\operatorname{Fitt}_R(Q)$. If $k<m$, there are no nonzero maximal minors on either side. The same determinant argument covers $m=0$ or $n=0$ with the empty-minor convention. [F1, step 2.1, algebra]

4.1 If $U$ has a quadratic presentation, take $k=m$ in step 1.1. Then $T$ in step 2.1 is square of size $m+n$, and is a quadratic presentation for $V$. This proves the first assertions. [step 1.1, step 2.1, step 3.1, algebra]

4.2 For the two sequences over $W$, form $X=\{(v,v')\in V\oplus V':v\text{ and }v'\text{ have the same image in }W\}$. Projection gives exact sequences $0\to U'\to X\to V\to0$ and $0\to U\to X\to V'\to0$. Surjectivity of each projection follows by lifting the common image using the other given surjection. Its kernel is the other given kernel. The finite-presentation result of step 2.1 applies to both sequences. Step 3.1 therefore computes $\operatorname{Fitt}_R(X)$ both as $\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U')\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V)$ and as $\operatorname{Fitt}_R(U)\operatorname{Fitt}_R(V')$, proving the comparison. [step 2.1, step 3.1, given, construct, algebra]

5.1 Apply step 4.2 with $U=M$, $V=E$, $W=\bigoplus_i R/(c_i,b_i)$ and the modules of step 1.2. The two computed Fitting ideals give the final displayed identity. Finite lifts, explicit kernels, and finite determinant expansions were the only constructions; no choice axiom was used. This proves all assertions. [step 4.2, step 1.2, given, algebra] ∎
