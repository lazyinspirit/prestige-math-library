## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Choice (AC). For proper morphisms of schemes
$X\xrightarrow{f}Y\xrightarrow{g}S$, the composition
$g\circ f:X\to S$ is proper.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** AC and composable proper morphisms $X\xrightarrow{f}Y$
$\xrightarrow{g}S$ of schemes.

[F1] A morphism is proper exactly when it is separated, of finite type, and
universally closed. ([[def-proper-morphism]])

[F2] A morphism is of finite type exactly when it is locally of finite type
and quasi-compact. ([[def-locally-finite-type-and-finite-type-morphism]])

[F3] Locally finite type is affine-local on source and target; the local
finite-type ring-map condition survives affine open restriction.
([[lem-finite-type-local-on-source-and-target]])

[F4] A finite-type algebra is generated by a finite list over its base ring.
([[def-finite-type-and-module-finite-algebras]])

[F5] A morphism is quasi-compact when inverse images of quasi-compact target
opens are quasi-compact. ([[def-quasi-compact-and-quasi-separated-morphism]])

[F6] Every affine scheme is quasi-compact.
([[cor-affine-scheme-quasi-compact]])

[F7] Every point of a scheme has an affine open neighbourhood.
([[def-scheme]])

[F8] A morphism is separated exactly when its diagonal is a closed immersion.
([[def-separated-morphism-schemes]])

[F9] The diagonal is the unique morphism whose two projections are the
identity. ([[def-diagonal-morphism-scheme]])

[F10] AC states that every family of nonempty sets has a choice function.
([[def-axiom-of-choice]])

[F11] Under AC, every base change of a closed immersion is a closed immersion.
([[lem-closed-immersion-affine-quotient-and-base-change]])

[F12] A closed immersion is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset with
surjective structure-sheaf map. ([[def-closed-immersion-schemes]])

[F13] For a continuous map $j:Y\to T$ and sheaf $\mathcal F$,
$(j_*\mathcal F)(U)=\mathcal F(j^{-1}(U))$.
([[def-direct-image-sheaf]])

[F14] The stalk of a sheaf at a point is the filtered colimit of its sections
over neighbourhoods of that point. ([[def-stalk-of-presheaf]])

[F15] A sequence of sheaves of abelian groups is exact exactly when its
sequence on every stalk is exact. We apply this to the underlying additive
sheaves. ([[thm-exactness-of-sheaves-stalkwise]])

[F16] Base change is the pullback of a morphism along a map to its target.
([[def-base-change-morphism-schemes]])

[F17] Fibre products of schemes exist and are characterized by their
compatible projection maps. ([[thm-fibre-products-of-schemes-exist]])

[F18] Fibre products reassociate by the canonical projection-compatible
isomorphisms. ([[lem-fibre-product-associativity-and-symmetry]])

[F19] A morphism is universally closed exactly when each of its base changes
is a closed map. ([[def-universally-closed-morphism]])

**Exact AC use:** AC is used only in [F11], to make the pullback
$X\times_Y X\to X\times_S X$ of the closed diagonal of $Y\to S$ a closed
immersion. The finite-type, closed-immersion-composition, and
universally-closedness arguments below use no choice principle.

## Proof

1.1 By [F1] and [F2], both $f$ and $g$ are locally of finite type. For any $x\in X$, refine affine neighbourhoods around $x$, $f(x)$, and $g(f(x))$ so that their coordinate-ring maps are finite type, using [F3]. If the two maps are $A\to B$ and $B\to C$, finite generating lists for $B/A$ and $C/B$ give a finite generating list for $C/A$ by [F4]. Thus $g\circ f$ is locally of finite type. [F1, F2, F3, F4, F7]

1.2 Let $W\subseteq S$ be any quasi-compact open. Then $g^{-1}(W)$ is quasi-compact by [F5]. Its affine open cover from [F7] has a finite subcover $V_1,\ldots,V_n$; each $V_i$ is quasi-compact by [F6], so $f^{-1}(V_i)$ is quasi-compact by [F5]. Their finite union is $(g\circ f)^{-1}(W)$, which is therefore quasi-compact. Since $W$ was arbitrary, $g\circ f$ is quasi-compact, and [F2] makes it of finite type. If $g^{-1}(W)$ is empty, the finite subcover is empty and the same conclusion holds. [F2, F5, F6, F7]

1.3 Let $i:Z\to Y$ and $j:Y\to T$ be closed immersions. Their composite is a homeomorphism onto a closed subset: $j(Y)$ is closed in $T$, and $j(i(Z))$ is closed in the subspace $j(Y)$. By [F13], $j_*i_*\mathcal O_Z$ equals $(j\circ i)_*\mathcal O_Z$. For any sheaf $\mathcal G$ on $Y$, [F13]--[F14] show that $(j_*\mathcal G)_t$ is $\mathcal G_y$ at $t=j(y)$ and is zero outside $j(Y)$: at an image point, open neighbourhoods restrict to a neighbourhood basis in $Y$; outside the closed image, some neighbourhood has empty inverse image. The map $j_*\mathcal O_Y\to j_*i_*\mathcal O_Z$ is therefore surjective on every stalk, since the stalk map $\mathcal O_{Y,y}\to(i_*\mathcal O_Z)_y$ is surjective by [F12]. By [F15] it is a surjection of sheaves; composing with the surjection $\mathcal O_T\to j_*\mathcal O_Y$ proves that $\mathcal O_T\to(j\circ i)_*\mathcal O_Z$ is surjective. Thus the composite is a closed immersion by [F12]. [F12, F13, F14, F15]

1.4 Let $T\to S$ be arbitrary and put $Y_T=Y\times_S T$. The maps $Y_T\to T$ and $X\times_Y Y_T\to Y_T$ are base changes of $g$ and $f$, respectively; they are closed because both original maps are universally closed by [F1] and [F19]. By [F17]--[F18], $X\times_Y(Y\times_S T)\cong X\times_S T$ compatibly with the maps to $T$. Thus the base change of $g\circ f$ to $T$ is the composite of these two closed maps, hence is closed. Since $T$ was arbitrary, [F19] makes $g\circ f$ universally closed. [F1, F16, F17, F18, F19]

2.1 By [F1] and [F8], both diagonals $\Delta_{X/Y}$ and $\Delta_{Y/S}$ are closed immersions. The map $u:X\times_Y X\to X\times_S X$ is the pullback of $\Delta_{Y/S}$ along $f\times_S f$: a test pair $(a,b)$ into $X\times_S X$ factors through that pullback exactly when $f\circ a$ equals $f\circ b$. Hence $u$ is a closed immersion by [F10]--[F11]. The composite $u\circ\Delta_{X/Y}$ has identity composites with both projections, so equals $\Delta_{X/S}$ by [F9]. Step 1.3 makes this diagonal a closed immersion, and [F8] makes $g\circ f$ separated. [F1, F8, F9, F10, F11, F16, F17, step 1.3]

3.1 Steps 1.1--1.2, 2.1, and 1.4 prove that $g\circ f$ is of finite type, separated, and universally closed, so it is proper by [F1]. If $X$ is empty, the composite is empty and these conditions hold; if $Y$ is empty then $X$ is empty. Empty and zero-ring affine charts cause no exception, and the identity morphism is covered by empty generator lists and singleton affine covers. The statement has no endpoint or iff cases. AC is used only in step 2.1 as declared above; the finite chart and generator arguments are pointwise or finite and do not use arbitrary-index choice. [F1, step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1, step 1.4] ∎
