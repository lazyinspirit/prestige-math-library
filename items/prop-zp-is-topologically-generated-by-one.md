## Statement

In the additive topological group $\mathbb Z_p$, the closure of
$\mathbb Z\cdot 1$ is all of $\mathbb Z_p$. However, the additive group
$\mathbb Z_p$ is not cyclic as an abstract group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** The additive group of $\mathbb Z_p$ and the element $1\in\mathbb Z_p$.

[L1] The canonical image of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathbb Z_p$ is dense ([[thm-the-integers-map-injectively-and-densely-into-zp]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 The subgroup generated by $1$ consists exactly of the integer multiples $n\cdot1$, so it is the canonical image of $\mathbb Z$ in $\mathbb Z_p$. Therefore [L1] says that its closure is all of $\mathbb Z_p$. [L1, given, algebra]

1.2 For every binary sequence $\epsilon=(\epsilon_i)_{i\ge0}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$, define a compatible tuple $x(\epsilon)$ by $$x(\epsilon)_n:=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\epsilon_i p^i\pmod{p^n}.$$ If two binary sequences first differ at index $j$, their $(j+1)$-st coordinates differ because their difference is divisible by $p^j$ but not by $p^{j+1}$. Hence $\epsilon\mapsto x(\epsilon)$ is injective. [given, construct, algebra]

2.1 Cantor's diagonal argument shows that $\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}$ is uncountable, so step 1.2 makes $\mathbb Z_p$ uncountable. Every abstract cyclic group is the image of the countable group $\mathbb Z$ under $n\mapsto n\gamma$ for one generator $\gamma$, and is therefore countable. Hence the additive group of $\mathbb Z_p$ cannot be cyclic. [step 1.2, algebra]

3.1 Step 1.1 proves topological generation by $1$, while steps 1.2 and 2.1 show that no element can generate the additive group abstractly. [step 1.1, step 1.2, step 2.1] ∎
