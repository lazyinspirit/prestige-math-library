## Statement

Let $G$ be a finite group. Then $\operatorname{soc}(G)$ is a characteristic
subgroup of $G$. Moreover, for some integer $r \ge 0$ there are pairwise
distinct minimal normal subgroups $N_1,\dots,N_r \trianglelefteq G$ such that

$$\operatorname{soc}(G)=N_1 \times \cdots \times N_r.$$

Here the case $r=0$ means the empty direct product, namely the trivial group.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A finite group $G$.

[L1] Distinct minimal normal subgroups of $G$ centralize one another ([[lem-distinct-minimal-normal-subgroups-centralize-one-another]]).

[L2] Every nontrivial finite characteristically simple group is a direct product of isomorphic simple groups ([[thm-finite-characteristically-simple-groups-are-direct-products-of-isomorphic-simple-groups]]).

[L3] Every minimal normal subgroup of a finite group is characteristically simple ([[lem-minimal-normal-subgroups-of-finite-groups-are-characteristically-simple]]).

[A1] Automorphisms of $G$ permute its minimal normal subgroups.

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 By [A1], the subgroup generated by all minimal normal subgroups of $G$ is stable under every automorphism of $G$. By definition this subgroup is $\operatorname{soc}(G)$, so $\operatorname{soc}(G)$ is characteristic in $G$. [given, A1]

1.2 Let $N_1,\dots,N_r$ be a maximal family of pairwise distinct minimal normal subgroups of $G$ chosen so that none is contained in the product of the preceding ones; when $G=1$, this family is empty. For $i \ge 1$, [L1] shows that $N_i$ centralizes $N_1 \cdots N_{i-1}$, and minimality gives $N_i \cap (N_1 \cdots N_{i-1}) = 1$ because the intersection is a normal subgroup of $G$ contained in $N_i$ but $N_i$ was chosen outside the preceding product. Hence $N_1 \cdots N_r$ is an internal direct product, with the case $r=0$ giving the trivial group. [L1, choose]

2.1 The product $N_1 \cdots N_r$ is generated by minimal normal subgroups, so it lies in $\operatorname{soc}(G)$. Conversely, if $M$ is any minimal normal subgroup of $G$ not contained in $N_1 \cdots N_r$, then adjoining $M$ would contradict maximality of the chosen family; therefore every minimal normal subgroup of $G$ lies in the displayed product. Thus $\operatorname{soc}(G)=N_1 \times \cdots \times N_r$. By [L3], each nontrivial factor $N_i$ is characteristically simple, and [L2] then makes it a direct product of isomorphic simple groups. [step 1.2, L2, L3] ∎
