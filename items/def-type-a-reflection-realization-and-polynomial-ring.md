## Definition

**The ring and its grading.** Fix $k=\mathbb Q$ and $n\ge2$, and let
$R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ be the polynomial ring in $n$ commuting
indeterminates ([[def-multivariate-polynomial-ring-by-iteration]]). Grade it by
$\deg x_i=2$: $R_d$ is the $\mathbb Q$-span of the monomials of total degree
$d/2$ when $d$ is even, and $R_d=0$ when $d$ is odd. Thus $R$ is a nonnegatively
graded commutative $\mathbb Q$-algebra with $R_0=\mathbb Q$, and every element of
$R$ is a finite sum of homogeneous elements.

**The reflection representation.** Put $\mathfrak h:=R_2=\bigoplus_{i=1}^n\mathbb Q x_i$,
so $\dim_{\mathbb Q}\mathfrak h=n$, and let $S_n$ act on $\mathfrak h$ by place
permutations,
$$w\cdot x_i:=x_{w(i)},$$
extended to a $\mathbb Q$-algebra automorphism of $R$; on polynomial functions
this is the substitution
$$(w\cdot f)(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=f(x_{w(1)},\ldots,x_{w(n)}).$$
This is a left action: $v\cdot(w\cdot x_i)=v\cdot x_{w(i)}=x_{v(w(i))}=x_{(vw)(i)}$,
and the substitution formula is the same statement read on functions, since
$x_i\mapsto x_{w(i)}$ for all $i$ is the substitution by the tuple
$(x_{w(1)},\ldots,x_{w(n)})$; the linear extension of $x_i\mapsto x_{w(i)}$ to
$R$ is an automorphism because it merely permutes indeterminates. By [[lem-reduced-adjacent-transposition-words-have-well-defined-positive-lifts]], $S_n$
is generated by the adjacent transpositions $s_i=(i\ i+1)$.

**Two normalizations of the simple roots.** Equip $\mathfrak h$ with the
symmetric bilinear form $(x_a,x_b)=\delta_{ab}$ and use it to identify
$\mathfrak h^*$ with $\mathfrak h$.

*(a) Coordinate roots.* For $1\le i\le n-1$ put
$\beta_i:=x_i-x_{i+1}\in\mathfrak h^*$ and
$\beta_i^\vee:=\beta_i\in\mathfrak h$, with
$$\langle\beta_i^\vee,\beta_j\rangle:=(\beta_i,\beta_j)=2\delta_{ij}-\delta_{|i-j|,1}.$$
Then
$$s_i(v):=v-\langle v,\beta_i\rangle\beta_i^\vee$$
is the place permutation of $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$ on the basis:
$s_i(x_a)=x_a-(\delta_{ai}-\delta_{a,i+1})(x_i-x_{i+1})$ exchanges $x_i$ and
$x_{i+1}$ and fixes the remaining $x_a$. We write $\Phi$ for the root system
$\{\pm(x_a-x_b):a\ne b\}$, $\Phi^+$ for the roots $\beta_{ab}:=x_a-x_b$ with
$a<b$, and $\Phi^+_{st}:=\Phi^+\cap(\mathbb Q\beta_s+\mathbb Q\beta_t)$ for the
positive roots of the rank-two parabolic subsystem generated by $s,t$. With this
choice the published length criterion of
[[lem-finite-weyl-strong-exchange-and-deletion]] applies verbatim:
$\ell(xs_i)=\ell(x)-1$ if and only if $x\beta_i<0$ in the positive system
$\Phi^+$. We call $(\mathfrak h,\{\beta_i^\vee\},\{\beta_i\})$ the **length
normalization** of the realization.

*(b) Balanced roots.* Put $\varepsilon_i:=(-1)^{i-1}$ for $1\le i\le n-1$ and
$$\alpha_i:=\varepsilon_i\beta_i\in\mathfrak h^*,\qquad \alpha_i^\vee:=\varepsilon_i\beta_i^\vee\in\mathfrak h,$$
so that the Cartan matrix of the realization is
$$\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle=\varepsilon_i\varepsilon_j(\beta_i,\beta_j)=2\delta_{ij}+\delta_{|i-j|,1}:$$
adjacent pairings are $+1$ and distant pairings are $0$. Because
$\varepsilon_i^2=1$, the reflection
$$s_i(v)=v-\langle v,\alpha_i\rangle\alpha_i^\vee=v-\langle v,\beta_i\rangle\beta_i^\vee$$
coincides with the transposition computed in (a); hence the two choices of simple
roots define the *same* reflection representation, the same reflecting
hyperplanes, the same invariant rings $R^{s_i}$ and the same root system $\Phi$
as a set of elements of $\mathfrak h^*$. We call
$(\mathfrak h,\{\alpha_i^\vee\},\{\alpha_i\})$ the **balanced normalization**; it
is the one attached to the diagrammatic calculus of
[[def-type-a-diagrammatic-soergel-category-and-its-bimodule-functor]]. The two
normalizations are related by the dictionary
$$\alpha_i=\varepsilon_i\beta_i,\qquad \alpha_i^\vee=\varepsilon_i\beta_i^\vee,\qquad \partial_i=\varepsilon_i\,\partial_i^{\beta},\qquad \partial_i^{\beta}:=\frac{f-s_i(f)}{\beta_i},\qquad \partial_i:=\frac{f-s_i(f)}{\alpha_i},$$
and every identity below is read in the normalization displayed in it; in
particular the descent criterion in the balanced labeling reads
$\ell(xs_i)=\ell(x)-1$ if and only if $\varepsilon_i\,x\alpha_i<0$.

**Invariants and the Demazure operator.** Let $R^{s_i}$ denote the subring of
$s_i$-invariants and, in the balanced normalization, define
$$\partial_i(f):=\frac{f-s_i(f)}{\alpha_i}\qquad(f\in R),$$
so that $\partial_i=\varepsilon_i\partial_i^\beta$ with
$\partial_i^\beta(f)=(f-s_i(f))/\beta_i$; the kernel and the surjectivity below
are the same for both operators, and the algebraic items of this page may use
either.
This is well defined and lands in $R^{s_i}$: $R$ is free over $R^{s_i}$ with
basis $\{1,\alpha_i\}$, because $2$ is invertible in $\mathbb Q$ and every
polynomial can be written uniquely as $f=g+\alpha_i h$ with
$g=\tfrac12(f+s_i(f))$ and $h=\tfrac12\partial_i(f)$; the two summands are
independent over $R^{s_i}$ since $\alpha_i$ is not a zero divisor. Hence
$s_i(\alpha_i h)=-\alpha_i s_i(h)$ forces the $\alpha_i$-part of $f-s_i(f)$ to be
$2\alpha_i h$, so $\partial_i(g)=0$ for $g\in R^{s_i}$ and
$$\partial_i(\alpha_ih)=2h\qquad(h\in R^{s_i}).$$
The factor $2$ is the one displayed: applying $\partial_i$ to
$\delta_i h$ with $\delta_i:=\alpha_i/2$ returns $h$, so $\partial_i$ is
$R^{s_i}$-linear and surjective onto $R^{s_i}$ (and the same operator annihilates
$R^{s_i}$, so its kernel is exactly $R^{s_i}$).

**The stated properties of the realization.** For this data:

1. it is **faithful**: if $w\in S_n$ fixes every element of $\mathfrak h$ then
   $x_{w(i)}=w\cdot x_i=x_i$ for all $i$, so $w(i)=i$ for all $i$ and $w=e$;
2. it is **reflection faithful**: every reflection of $S_n$ is a transposition
   $(a\ b)$, and its fixed space on $\mathfrak h$ is the hyperplane $x_a-x_b=0$;
   distinct transpositions have distinct fixed hyperplanes, since the hyperplane
   determines the unordered pair $\{a,b\}$;
3. it satisfies **Demazure surjectivity** (Elias–Williamson, Assumption 3.7):
   $\alpha_s\in\mathfrak h^*$ is a nonzero functional on $\mathfrak h$, hence
   surjective onto $\mathbb Q$, evaluation at $\alpha_s^\vee\ne0$ is surjective
   on $\mathfrak h^*$, and $\partial_s$ is surjective onto $R^{s_s}$ because
   $\partial_s(\delta_s h)=h$ for $h\in R^{s_s}$ with $\delta_s=\alpha_s/2$;
4. it is **balanced** in the sense of Elias–Williamson, Definition 3.6. Recall
   from Definition 3.5 that the two-colored quantum numbers are determined by
   $[0]_x=[0]_y=0$, $[1]_x=[1]_y=1$, $[2]_x=x$, $[2]_y=y$ together with the
   recursions $[2]_x[k]_y=[k+1]_x+[k-1]_x$ and
   $[2]_y[k]_x=[k+1]_y+[k-1]_y$. Fix $s\ne t$ and put
   $x:=\langle\alpha_s^\vee,\alpha_t\rangle$,
   $y:=\langle\alpha_t^\vee,\alpha_s\rangle$. If $|s-t|>1$ then $m_{st}=2$ and
   $x=y=0$, so the technical condition (3.3) reads
   $[m_{st}]_x=[m_{st}]_y=[2]_0=0$ and
   $[m_{st}-1]_x=[m_{st}-1]_y=[1]=1$. If $|s-t|=1$ then $m_{st}=3$ and
   $x=y=1$; the recursion at $k=2$ gives
   $[3]_x=x[2]_y-[1]_x=1\cdot1-1=0$ and likewise $[3]_y=0$, so (3.3) holds,
   while $[m_{st}-1]_x=[2]_1=1=[m_{st}-1]_y$. Thus
   $[m_{st}-1]_x=[m_{st}-1]_y=1$ for every pair $s,t$, which is balancedness,
   and the technical condition (3.3) also holds throughout. This is the
   hypothesis under which Elias–Williamson §§5.1–5.3 present the diagrammatic
   category with the simple relation families used on this page, and it is why
   the balanced normalization (b), rather than the coordinate roots of (a),
   carries the diagrammatic data. Every finite dihedral integer $2m_{st}$ is $4$
   or $6$ here, hence invertible in $\mathbb Q$, and so is $2$.

**Remark on the length normalization.** In the coordinate-root normalization
(a) the same realization has adjacent Cartan entries
$\langle\beta_s^\vee,\beta_t\rangle=-1$, so $[m_{st}-1]_x=[2]_{-1}=-1\ne1$ for
adjacent $s,t$: that normalization is odd-unbalanced, and Elias–Williamson's
balanced presentation does not apply to it as written. Since the reflections,
the invariant rings, the root hyperplanes and the bimodules $B_i$ of this page
depend only on the reflection representation and not on which linear forms
represent the simple roots, the page uses the coordinate roots (a) for the
length-theoretic and rank-two algebraic statements, where the published length
criterion is stated in that notation, and the balanced roots (b) for the
diagrammatic statements of Elias–Williamson §§5–7.

**Small $n$.** For $n=0$ or $n=1$, take $R=\mathbb Q[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$
(with $R=\mathbb Q$ when $n=0$). The group $S_n$ is trivial and there are no
simple reflections. The empty-word bimodule is $R$; the additive graded
Bott–Samelson category consists of finite direct sums of shifts of $R$, with
degree-zero bimodule maps. Its Soergel category is its idempotent completion.
In particular it contains the zero object, all shifts and all scalar maps;
$\operatorname{End}^0(R)=\mathbb Q$. Statements involving a simple reflection
have no instances in these cases.

**Shift dictionary.** Following Elias–Williamson we write $M(1)$ for the graded
shift with $M(1)_d=M_{d+1}$. In the library convention for graded modules and
their internal shifts, in which the shift $\{r\}$ has $(M\{r\})_d=M_{d-r}$, this
is the internal shift $M\{-1\}$, and $M(r)_d=M_{d+r}=M\{-r\}_d$ for all
$r\in\mathbb Z$. This dictionary is used without further comment in every shifted
formula on this page.
