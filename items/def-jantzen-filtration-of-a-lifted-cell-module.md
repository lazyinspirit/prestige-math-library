## Definition

Let $R$ be a principal ideal domain ([[def-principal-ideal-domain]]) with a
chosen prime element $\pi$, let $F:=R/\pi R$ be the residue field, and let $M$
be a free $R$-module of finite rank
([[def-free-module-on-a-set-and-standard-basis]]) equipped with a symmetric
$R$-bilinear form $\langle\ ,\ \rangle:M\times M\to R$ whose extension to the
fraction field $K=\operatorname{Frac}(R)$ is non-degenerate (equivalently
$\det G\ne0$ for a Gram matrix $G$ of the form). For $i\ge1$ put
$$M(i):=\{x\in M:\langle x,y\rangle\in\pi^iR\ \text{for all }y\in M\},\qquad M_F(i):=(M(i)+\pi M)/\pi M\subseteq M_F:=M\otimes_RF.$$
Then $M(0)=M$, the $M(i)$ are $R$-submodules with $M(i+1)\subseteq M(i)$, the
descending chain
$$M_F=M_F(0)\supseteq M_F(1)\supseteq M_F(2)\supseteq\cdots$$
stabilises at $0$ (that is, $M_F(z)=0$ for all sufficiently large $z$, because
$\det G\ne0$), and $M_F(1)=\mathrm{rad}\,M_F$, the radical of the reduced
form. This finite descending filtration is the **Jantzen filtration** of $M$
with respect to $(R,\pi,\langle\ ,\ \rangle)$. The hypotheses are exactly: $R$
a PID, $\pi$ a prime element, the form non-degenerate over
$\operatorname{Frac}(R)$; no valuation-ring or DVR hypothesis is substituted,
and the prime element $\pi$, its ideal $\pi R$ and the fraction field $K$ are
kept distinct. Each $M(i)$ is a submodule: for the cell forms on this page the
associativity identity $\langle xa,y\rangle=\langle x,ya^*\rangle$ shows
closedness under the action. For $0\ne r\in R$, define $\nu_\pi(r)=\max\{k\ge0:\pi^k\mid r\}$. This maximum is finite: otherwise the uniquely determined elements $r/\pi^k$ give an ascending chain of ideals whose union is principal, say generated by an element in its $N$-th ideal. The chain then stabilizes at $N$, whereas $(r/\pi^N)=(r/\pi^{N+1})$ and cancellation would make $\pi$ a unit. For termination, put $a=\nu_\pi(\det G)$. If $x\in M(i)$, its coordinate column $v$ satisfies $Gv\in\pi^iR^d$, so the adjugate identity gives $(\det G)v\in\pi^iR^d$. For $i>a$, primality of $\pi$ forces every coordinate of $v$ into $\pi R$. Thus $M(i)\subseteq\pi M$ and $M_F(i)=0$. In rank zero the filtration is already zero. Finally a reduced vector belongs to the reduced form radical exactly when any lift pairs with every vector of $M$ into $\pi R$, which is exactly membership in $M(1)$. No choice principle is used.
