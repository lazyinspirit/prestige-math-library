## Statement

Let $X$ be a separable real Banach space, let $D\subseteq X$ be Borel, and let $\mu_0$ be a Borel probability measure on $X$ with $\mu_0(D)=1$ and positive mass on some bounded Borel subset of $D$. Give $D$ the relative norm topology and trace Borel sigma-algebra $\mathcal B(D)=\{D\cap A:A\in\mathcal B(X)\}$. Let $\Phi,\Phi_N:D\to\mathbb R$ be finite continuous potentials indexed by integers $N\ge1$, let $M_1,M_2:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)$ be Borel and nondecreasing, and let $\psi_N\ge0$ satisfy $\psi_N\to0$. Suppose for every $u\in D$ and every $N$,
$$\Phi(u)\ge-M_1(\|u\|_X),\qquad \Phi_N(u)\ge-M_1(\|u\|_X),\qquad |\Phi_N(u)-\Phi(u)|\le M_2(\|u\|_X)\psi_N,$$
and
$$J:=\int_D e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}\bigl(1+M_2(\|u\|_X)^2\bigr)\,\mu_0(du)<\infty.$$
Define the pointwise fixed-data evidences and posterior updates by
$$Z:=\int_D e^{-\Phi(u)}\,\mu_0(du),\quad \Pi(du):=Z^{-1}e^{-\Phi(u)}\mu_0(du),\qquad Z_N:=\int_D e^{-\Phi_N(u)}\,\mu_0(du),\quad \Pi_N(du):=Z_N^{-1}e^{-\Phi_N(u)}\mu_0(du).$$
Then $Z,Z_N$ are positive and finite, and there are $C<\infty$ and $N_0$ such that
$$h(\Pi,\Pi_N)\le C\psi_N\qquad(N\ge N_0),$$
where $h$ is the library root-L2 Hellinger distance. This is a fixed-data assertion and makes no sampling-law or conditional-law version claim. In a separate branch, if AC is assumed for the library density/total-variation comparison convention, then $\|\Pi-\Pi_N\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le h(\Pi,\Pi_N)\le C\psi_N$, where $\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}:=\sup_{A\in\mathcal B(D)}|P(A)-Q(A)|$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A Borel prior concentrated on the forward domain, finite continuous potentials $\Phi,\Phi_N$, a shared lower envelope, a pointwise error envelope $M_2\psi_N$, and the weighted integrability condition in the Statement.

[F1] Each finite continuous potential has a continuous section; under a common integrable lower envelope on a Borel full-measure forward domain in a separable Banach space, its fixed-data evidence is positive and finite and the normalized update is a probability measure. [[def-continuous-map-top]], [[def-banach-space]], [[def-separable-space]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-probability-measure]], [[thm-ip-function-space-posterior-propriety]]

[F2] For a finite potential $\Phi$, the fixed-data evidence is $Z=\int_D e^{-\Phi}\,d\mu_0$ and the posterior density relative to $\mu_0$ is $e^{-\Phi}/Z$ when $0<Z<\infty$. [[def-ip-prior-on-a-forward-domain]], [[def-ip-misfit-evidence-and-posterior-law]]

[F3] For supplied densities $p,p_N$ relative to $\mu_0$, the library distance is $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)=\|\sqrt p-\sqrt{p_N}\|_{L^2(\mu_0)}$, with no factor $1/\sqrt2$. [[def-hellinger-distance-for-dominated-laws]]

[A1] Assume the Axiom of Choice only for the optional generic density/TV comparison branch. [[def-axiom-of-choice]]

[F4] Under [A1], for two probability laws with a common sigma-finite dominator, $\|P-Q\|_{\mathrm{TV}}=\sup_A|P(A)-Q(A)|\le h(P,Q)$ in the library conventions. [[thm-hellinger-tv-comparison-under-library-convention]], [[def-total-variation-distance-for-probability-laws]]

[F5] The derivatives of $t\mapsto e^{-t/2}$ and $t\mapsto e^{-t}$ are $-\tfrac12e^{-t/2}$ and $-e^{-t}$; the real mean value theorem bounds their increments by derivative magnitude times interval length. [[thm-derivative-of-exponential]], [[thm-chain-rule]], [[lem-mean-value-theorem-on-closed-interval]]

[F6] The nonnegative integral is monotone and positively homogeneous. [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]

[F7] Continuous finite potentials on the Borel subspace $D$ are Borel; via the real-line inclusion they are measurable as extended-real-valued potentials. The norm map and real exponential are continuous, so composing Borel radius envelopes with them gives measurable integrands. [[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra]], [[def-extended-reals]], [[def-borel-sigma-algebra-on-the-extended-real-line]], [[def-measurable-function-between-measurable-spaces]], [[def-norm-and-normed-space]], [[lem-metric-reverse-triangle]], [[thm-exponential-is-strictly-increasing]], [[thm-composition-with-borel-functions-preserves-measurability]]

[F8] Sums, differences, and absolute values of measurable real-valued functions are measurable. [[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]

[F9] The nonnegative integral is additive, monotone, and positively homogeneous. [[cor-additivity-of-the-nonnegative-lebesgue-integral]], [[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]

[F10] For square-integrable real functions, the root integral of the square is the $L^2$ norm and satisfies the triangle inequality. [[def-calligraphic-l-p-on-a-measure-space]], [[thm-minkowski-inequality-for-integrals]]

## Proof

**Given:** The hypotheses and pointwise posterior convention in the Statement.

1.1 Since $M_2\ge0$, $e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}\le e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}(1+M_2(\|u\|_X)^2)$, so [F6] gives $\int_D e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}d\mu_0\le J<\infty$. Apply [F1] to $\Phi$ and, separately for each fixed $N$, to $\Phi_N$; this gives $0<Z<\infty$ and $0<Z_N<\infty$ for every $N$, and defines the displayed probability updates. [F1, F2, F6, F7, F8, given, algebra]

2.1 Fix $u\in D$ and $N$. Both potential values are at least $-M_1(\|u\|_X)$, so every real between them has that lower bound. By [F5] and the potential-error assumption, $|e^{-\Phi_N(u)}-e^{-\Phi(u)}|\le e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)}M_2(\|u\|_X)\psi_N.$ Integrating and using $M_2\le1+M_2^2$ gives $|Z_N-Z|\le J\psi_N.$ The absolute difference of the integrals is bounded by the integral of the absolute difference: apply monotonicity and additivity to $e^{-\Phi_N}\le e^{-\Phi}+|e^{-\Phi_N}-e^{-\Phi}|$ and its reverse. Since $Z>0$ and $\psi_N\to0$, there is $N_0$ such that $J\psi_N\le Z/2$ for every $N\ge N_0$, and then $Z_N\ge Z/2$. [F5, F6, F7, F8, F9, step 1.1, given, algebra]

2.2 The same mean-value argument for $t\mapsto e^{-t/2}$ gives $|e^{-\Phi_N(u)/2}-e^{-\Phi(u)/2}|\le\tfrac12 e^{M_1(\|u\|_X)/2}M_2(\|u\|_X)\psi_N.$ Squaring and integrating yields $\|e^{-\Phi_N/2}-e^{-\Phi/2}\|_{L^2(\mu_0)}\le\tfrac12\sqrt{J}\,\psi_N.$ [F5, F6, F7, F8, step 1.1, given, algebra]

3.1 Put $a=e^{-\Phi/2}$ and $a_N=e^{-\Phi_N/2}$. The square roots of the posterior densities are $q=a/\sqrt Z$ and $q_N=a_N/\sqrt{Z_N}$ by [F2]. Add and subtract $a_N/\sqrt Z$ and use the triangle inequality in $L^2$ to obtain $\|q-q_N\|_2\le\frac{\|a-a_N\|_2}{\sqrt Z}+\frac{|\sqrt{Z_N}-\sqrt Z|}{\sqrt Z}.$ By [F3], the left side is $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)$. Also $\|a_N\|_2^2=\int_D e^{-\Phi_N}\,d\mu_0=Z_N$, which gives the displayed second term after applying [F10]. The first term is at most $\sqrt{J}\psi_N/(2\sqrt Z)$ by step 2.2. The second equals $|Z_N-Z|/[\sqrt Z(\sqrt{Z_N}+\sqrt Z)]$, so by step 2.1 and $Z_N\ge Z/2$ it is at most $J\psi_N/Z$. Therefore $h(\Pi,\Pi_N)\le\left(\frac{\sqrt J}{2\sqrt Z}+\frac{J}{Z}\right)\psi_N,$ which is the claimed bound with a finite constant independent of $N\ge N_0$. [F2, F3, F10, step 2.1, step 2.2, given, algebra]

4.1 Under the separate assumption [A1], apply [F4] to the two explicit posterior densities relative to $\mu_0$. This gives $\|\Pi-\Pi_N\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\le h(\Pi,\Pi_N)$, and step 3.1 gives the optional TV consequence without adding an axiom to the Hellinger estimate. [A1, F4, step 3.1, given] ∎
