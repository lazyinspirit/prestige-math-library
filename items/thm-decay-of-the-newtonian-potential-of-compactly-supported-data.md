## Statement

Assume the Axiom of Countable Choice and let $n\ge2$. Fix $R>0$, and let
$f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ be Lebesgue measurable, integrable, and compactly
supported, with $\|f\|_1:=\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f(y)|\,dy<\infty$, and zero outside the open
Euclidean ball $B_R(0)$. Put
$M:=\int_{\mathbb R^n}f(y)\,dy$ and let $\omega_{n-1}$ be the sphere-area
normalization in the definition of $\Phi$.

For every $x\in\mathbb R^n$ with $r:=|x|>2R$, the Newtonian integral
$Nf(x)=\int_{\mathbb R^n}\Phi(x-y)f(y)\,dy$ is absolutely finite. If $n\ge3$,
then
$$
\left|Nf(x)-\frac{M r^{2-n}}{(n-2)\omega_{n-1}}\right| \le \frac{2^{n-1}R}{\omega_{n-1}}\|f\|_1 r^{1-n}.
$$
If $n=2$, then
$$
\left|Nf(x)+\frac{M}{2\pi}\log r\right| \le \frac{R}{\pi}\|f\|_1 r^{-1}.
$$
The constants are independent of the direction of $x$. In particular, when
$M=0$ these are the respective improved orders
$Nf(x)=O(r^{1-n})$ for $n\ge3$ and $Nf(x)=O(r^{-1})$ for $n=2$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** Assume $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, let $n\ge2$, let $R>0$, and let
$f:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$ be Lebesgue measurable with
$\int_{\mathbb R^n}|f|<\infty$ and $f=0$ off $B_R(0)$.

[A1] The Axiom of Countable Choice, written $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, says every
sequence of nonempty sets has a choice function.
([[def-countable-choice]]).

[F1] The Euclidean norm satisfies the triangle inequality and hence the reverse
triangle inequality. ([[thm-cauchy-schwarz-and-the-euclidean-norm]],
[[def-euclidean-inner-product]]).

[F2] Away from its pole the kernel is
$\Phi(z)=|z|^{2-n}/((n-2)\omega_{n-1})$ for $n\ge3$ and
$\Phi(z)=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log|z|$ for $n=2$; the value at zero is assigned as in
the kernel convention.
([[def-laplace-fundamental-solution-with-positive-minus-laplacian-sign]]).

[F3] A real measurable function is integrable exactly when
$\int|f|<\infty$, and its integral is the difference of the finite integrals
of its positive and negative parts.
([[def-integrable-real-and-complex-functions-and-their-integrals]]).

[F4] The open ball is $B(0,R)=\{y:\|y\|_2<R\}$ for $R>0$.
([[def-metric-ball]]).

[F5] The Borel sigma-algebra is generated by the open sets.
([[def-borel-sigma-algebra]]).

[F17] Every open ball of positive radius in a metric space is open.
([[thm-metric-open-set-algebra]]).

[F6] Continuous maps have Borel preimages of Borel sets.
([[thm-continuous-preimages-of-borel-sets-are-borel]]).

[F7] Under $\mathrm{AC}_\omega$, every Borel subset of $\mathbb R^n$ is
Lebesgue measurable. ([[thm-borel-sets-are-lebesgue-measurable]]).

[F8] Sums, differences, products and absolute values of measurable real
functions are measurable. ([[thm-arithmetic-and-lattice-operations-preserve-measurability]]).

[F9] The nonnegative integral is monotone and homogeneous for nonnegative
scalars. ([[prop-order-and-scalar-rules-for-the-nonnegative-integral]]).

[F10] The integral is linear on $L^1$.
([[thm-linearity-of-the-lebesgue-integral-on-l-one]]).

[F11] For an integrable function,
$|\int h|\le\int|h|$.
([[thm-integral-triangle-inequality]]).

[F12] If a real function is continuous on a closed interval and differentiable
on its interior, the mean value theorem gives its difference as an interior
derivative times the interval length.
([[cor-mean-value-theorem]]).

[F13] For $\alpha\in\mathbb R$, $(t^\alpha)'=\alpha t^{\alpha-1}$ on
$t>0$, and $t^\alpha$ is continuous there.
([[thm-real-power-continuity-and-derivatives]]).

[F14] For $t>0$, $(\log t)'=1/t$.
([[thm-logarithm-derivative-and-integral]]).

[F15] Differentiability of a real function at every point of an open interval
implies continuity there. ([[cor-differentiable-implies-continuous]]).

[F16] The Newtonian potential is the pointwise integral wherever it is
absolutely finite. ([[def-newtonian-potential]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct.

1.1 Fix $x$ with $r:=|x|>2R$ and $y\in B_R(0)$, and set $s:=|x-y|$. By [F1], $s\ge r-|y|>r-R>r/2>0$ and $|s-r|\le|y|<R$. Every number between $r$ and $s$ is therefore greater than $r/2$, so neither kernel evaluation below is at the pole. [given, F1, F2]

1.2 For $t>0$, write $q_n(t)=t^{2-n}/((n-2)\omega_{n-1})$ when $n\ge3$ and $q_2(t)=-(2\pi)^{-1}\log t$ when $n=2$. By [F2], [F13], and [F14], $q_n'(t)=-1/(\omega_{n-1}t^{n-1})$ for $n\ge3$ and $q_2'(t)=-1/(2\pi t)$. These profiles are continuous on every closed interval in $(0,\infty)$ and differentiable in its interior; for the logarithm, continuity follows from [F15]. [F2, F13, F14, F15, algebra]

2.1 If $s=r$, then $\Phi(x-y)-\Phi(x)=0$. Otherwise apply [F12] between $r$ and $s$; the derivative bounds in step 1.2 and the lower bound in step 1.1 give $|\Phi(x-y)-\Phi(x)|\le C_n|y|r^{1-n}$, where $C_n=2^{n-1}/\omega_{n-1}$ for $n\ge3$ and $C_2=1/\pi$. The same bound holds when $s=r$. [step 1.1, step 1.2, F12, algebra, cases]

2.2 For this fixed $x$, $y\mapsto\Phi(x-y)$ is continuous on $B_R(0)$ by step 1.1 and [F2], [F13]–[F15], and the Euclidean norm is continuous by [F1]. Extend this function by zero outside the open ball to obtain $g_x$, and extend $y\mapsto\Phi(x-y)-\Phi(x)$ the same way to obtain $h_x$. For any open $O\subseteq\mathbb R$, continuity makes each preimage inside $B_R(0)$ relatively open and [F6] makes it Borel; since $B_R(0)$ is open by [F17], this relative preimage is open in $\mathbb R^n$. The extension's preimage is that set, with $B_R(0)^c$ adjoined when $0\in O$, so [F4], [F5], and [F17] show that $g_x$ and $h_x$ are Borel. Thus [F7] makes both functions Lebesgue measurable under [A1]. [A1, F1, F2, F4, F5, F6, F7, F13, F14, F15, F17, step 1.1]

3.1 By [F8], $f g_x=f\Phi(x-\cdot)$ and $f h_x$ are measurable. Step 2.1 gives $|f(y)h_x(y)|\le C_nRr^{1-n}|f(y)|$, while $|g_x(y)|\le|\Phi(x)|+C_nRr^{1-n}$ gives $|f(y)\Phi(x-y)|=|f(y)g_x(y)|\le(|\Phi(x)|+C_nRr^{1-n})|f(y)|$. Since $f\in L^1$, [F3] and [F9] make both products integrable. In particular the Newtonian integral is absolutely finite at $x$, so [F16] defines $Nf(x)$. [F3, F8, F9, F16, step 2.1, step 2.2]

4.1 Since $f$ vanishes outside $B_R(0)$, $f(y)\Phi(x-y)=\Phi(x)f(y)+f(y)h_x(y)$ for every $y$. Integrability from step 3.1 and [F10] give $Nf(x)=M\Phi(x)+\int f(y)h_x(y)\,dy$. By [F11], then the pointwise bound of step 3.1 and [F9], $|Nf(x)-M\Phi(x)|\le\int|f(y)h_x(y)|\,dy\le C_nR\|f\|_1r^{1-n}$. Substituting the profiles [F2] yields the stated two estimates, with constants independent of the direction of $x$. [F2, F9, F10, F11, step 3.1]

5.1 If $M=0$, the leading term in step 4.1 vanishes, giving the two improved orders; if $f=0$, then $M=Nf=0$ and both bounds hold with equality. The assumptions $n\ge2$ and $r>2R$ exclude dimensions zero and one and the endpoint $r=2R$. Countable Choice is used only through the kernel and potential conventions [F2], [F16] and the Borel-to-Lebesgue interface [F7]; no full Axiom of Choice is invoked. [A1, F2, F7, F16, step 4.1, cases] ∎
## Source notes

Hunter, §2.7 equation (2.24) and the following exterior-asymptotic passage,
printed pp.36–37, defines the Newtonian potential and, for $n\ge3$, rewrites
it as the total-charge leading term times a kernel ratio, then uses dominated
convergence to obtain the leading asymptotic. For $n=2$ Hunter states only
that the potential generally grows logarithmically. That passage does not
prove the explicit $O(r^{1-n})$ and $O(r^{-1})$ remainders or the zero-mass
improvements; steps 1.1–4.1 derive those quantitatively from the radial
derivatives. Oh, §4.2 Theorem 4.4 and Corollary 4.7, printed pp.59–60, give
the harmonic-derivative and growth-class context for applications of these
estimates; they are not used to prove the far-field bounds here.
