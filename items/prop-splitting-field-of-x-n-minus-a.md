## Statement

Let $n\ge1$, let $F$ be a field, and let $a\in F$. Suppose an extension $E/F$ contains a nonzero root $\alpha$ of $x^n-a$. Then $a\ne0$, and for $\beta\in E$,
$$ \beta^n=a\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \beta=\zeta\alpha\text{ for some }\zeta\in E\text{ with }\zeta^n=1. $$
Consequently, if $\mu_n(E)=\{\zeta\in E:\zeta^n=1\}$ and $E$ contains every root of $x^n-a$, its splitting field inside $E$ is $F(\alpha,\mu_n(E))$.

## Facts & Assumptions

**Given:** A positive integer $n$, a field extension $E/F$, and a nonzero $\alpha\in E$ satisfying $\alpha^n=a\in F$.

[F1] Every nonzero field element is a unit ([[def-field]]).

[F2] In a commutative group, $(xy)^n=x^ny^n$ and $(x^{-1})^n=(x^n)^{-1}$ ([[def-group-power]], [[lem-group-power-laws]]).

[F3] A splitting field is generated over the base by all roots of the polynomial ([[def-polynomials-that-split-and-splitting-fields]]).

## Proof

**Proof technique:** direct, both directions.

1.1 Since $\alpha\ne0$, [F1] gives $\alpha^{-1}$. Also $a=\alpha^n\ne0$, because a product of nonzero field elements is nonzero. [F1]

1.2 Conversely, if $\beta=\zeta\alpha$ and $\zeta^n=1$, then [F2] gives $\beta^n=\zeta^n\alpha^n=a$. [F2]

2.1 If $\beta^n=a$, put $\zeta=\beta\alpha^{-1}$. By [F2], $\zeta^n=\beta^n(\alpha^n)^{-1}=aa^{-1}=1$, and $\beta=\zeta\alpha$. [F1, F2, step 1.1]

3.1 Thus the root set in $E$ is exactly $\alpha\mu_n(E)$. The field generated by that set equals $F(\alpha,\mu_n(E))$: it contains $\alpha=1\alpha$, and from any root $\zeta\alpha$ it recovers $\zeta=(\zeta\alpha)\alpha^{-1}$; the reverse containment follows because each $\zeta\alpha$ is a root. Now [F3] gives the splitting-field assertion. [F1, F3, step 2.1, step 1.2] ∎
